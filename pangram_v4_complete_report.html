Not every countable group is MF in the operator norm approximation sense. We prove a one-sided compression criterion that places normal property (T) subgroups in the MF radical. Normal generation can make that radical equal the whole group. An elementary group over the binary Leavitt algebra satisfies the criterion, giving a simple property (T) group that is not MF. We also prove exact pullback formulas showing that a kernel invisible to MF preserves the complete family of MF quotients above it.

Introduction

A countable group is MF if it embeds in the unitary group of a norm matrix corona 𝒬_(d) = ∏_(n)M_(d_(n))(ℂ)/⨁_(n)M_(d_(n))(ℂ). Here ⨁_(n)M_(d_(n))(ℂ) = {(x_(n)) ∈ ∏_(n)M_(d_(n))(ℂ): ∥x_(n)∥ → 0} is the c₀-direct sum. Equivalently, an MF group admits finite-dimensional unitary models whose multiplicative defects tend to zero in operator norm. These models asymptotically separate its nonidentity elements: for each g ≠ 1, the quantity limsup_(n)∥V_(n)(g) − 1∥ is positive, with a lower bound that may depend on g. This is the operator norm notion of MF group introduced by Carrión–Dadarlat–Eckhardt [citation omitted].

For a countable group G, define its MF radical by Rad_(MF)(G) = ⋂_(d)⋂_(π: G → 𝒰(𝒬_(d)))ker π. Proposition 1 shows that G is MF precisely when Rad_(MF)(G) is trivial. If Rad_(MF)(G) = G, every homomorphism from G to an MF group is trivial.

More generally, for N ⊴ G define its MF kernel closure by cl_(MF)^(G)(N) = ⋂{ker f: N ≤ ker f, f: G → M, M MF}. The image of a corona homomorphism from G is countable and is itself MF; conversely, every MF target embeds in a norm matrix corona. Thus Rad_(MF)(G) = cl_(MF)^(G)(1), and G/N is MF precisely when cl_(MF)^(G)(N) = N. This is a closure operation on the normal subgroups of one fixed group; it is unrelated to topological closure of classes of marked groups.
