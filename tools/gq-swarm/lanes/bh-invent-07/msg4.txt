Splitting categories are connected exactly when root graphs are; condition (G) reduces to atom pairs

Split trees of one dissection are coherent: any two are joined by local moves at minimal common
upper bounds of two atoms, and such a move disconnects the splitting category only when it is the
whole dissection. Hence, for every dissection with at least three pieces, the splitting category
is connected if and only if the root graph (atoms below it, joined when it is not their minimal
common upper bound) is connected. Only first-level spine elements can split disconnectedly.

So condition (G) for finite presentation holds when pairs of atoms have finitely many minimal
common upper bounds. For the closed-MCG edge-split host, finite presentation now needs Ore plus
finitely many two-sided minimal common upper bounds of two edge splits, not a finite spine.
Calibrated on twisted Brin-Thompson grids and on the flop computation. Also corrects the
connectivity indices in the proof of thumann-finiteness-without-finite-type; its statement is
unchanged.
