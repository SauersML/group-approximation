Ask for an elementary proof that piecewise-canonical permutations of Z are products of class transpositions

This is the only non-elementary input to the answer to Kourovka 17.59: the
k-graph node supplies it from the generation theorem for topological full
groups of purely infinite minimal groupoids.

Proved here, elementary: piecewise-canonical permutations form a group, and
boxes pull back to boxes; for proper boxes A, B a product of at most two class
transpositions restricts to the canonical map A -> B, because two meeting boxes
cover Z only if one of them is Z; transpositions of two pieces of one partition
generate a copy of Sym(M) that changes neither partition, so the bijection
between the two partitions may be chosen freely.

Open: the induction on the number of pieces. Composing with the helper element
splits the pieces of the target partition that meet its support without lying
inside it, so the piece count can grow. Three variants are recorded with the
reason each fails. This is the same coherence gap already recorded on the
transposition-presentation node.
