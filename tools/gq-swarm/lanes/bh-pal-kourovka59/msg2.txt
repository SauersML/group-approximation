Prove that piecewise-canonical permutations of Z are products of class transpositions

The proof is elementary: the Chinese remainder theorem and the cocycle identity
for canonical maps, with no Matui or Li input. Natural (tree) partitions carry a
type: the root prime and the multiset of the other node primes.

- Two trees of the same type are related by swapping disjoint subtrees, which are
  class transpositions.
- The p-split and the 2-split commute.
- Equivalence cancels a common refinement: if u^x ~ v^x, then u ~ v. Leaves are
  permuted inside each x, and canonical maps are unique.

With x = b(a, b), where a is the p-split and b the 2-split, one root commutation
and two inner commutations take the p-split refined by x to the type of a binary
p-leaf tree refined by x. So the p-split is equivalent to a binary tree. Every
g in PC maps a natural partition onto a natural one, and the result follows.
Only the moduli 2 and p occur, so the same argument works in CT_P(Z).

This replaces the failed piece-count induction of the first version. It also
corrects that version's remark: generation does not settle the
transposition-presentation node, which is about relations. A remark on the
Kourovka 17.59 node records the elementary route.
