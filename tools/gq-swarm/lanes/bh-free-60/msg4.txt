Free-product boundary groupoids carry the vertex-group homology; Reid's d = 0 Higman-Thompson variant has abelianization Z + Z/2 (MO 339541)

For D = Z/k * B with B infinite, D acting on its Bass-Serre tree compactified by the
B-vertices is purely infinite minimal on a Cantor set. Its groupoid homology is Z in
degree 0 (a signed Euler measure, [1_X] = k) and H_n(B; Z) for n >= 1: Mayer-Vietoris
over the tree, finite vertex groups induced, and a descent argument kills the
connecting kernel. With Matui, Nekrashevych and Li: D(F) is simple, f.g. for f.g. B,
contains every perfect subgroup of B, and F_ab sits in H_2(B) -> Z/2 -> F_ab -> B_ab -> 0.
For k = 2, B = Z this answers Colin Reid's MathOverflow 339541 on the d = 0 member:
homology Z, Z, 0, ...; abelianization Z + Z/2 with an explicit index map; G' simple,
f.g., rationally acyclic. Finite presentation stays open.

Lesson for general BH: infinite vertex groups survive as isotropy and as homology, so
Z/2 * B is an isotropy host for any B. Finite presentation of such hosts cannot be
read from isotropy or homology (unsolvable-WP perfect B gives non-fp simple D(F)).
