Tree domination: the least-damage cut-and-synchronize recursion terminates on every test, while following the deepest fragment runs away

Tree domination follows from termination of a top-down recursion: choose a first split, cut the fragments that straddle it, synchronize fragments that do not descend from their child, and recurse. One msismall core, calibrated in rank 2 (it returns every split tree unchanged). With the least-damage rule, all named instances and all 93 random non-tree instances in ranks 3 and 4 were dominated with at most 7 extra cuts and no synchronization. That includes the four full-operad primes and the 7-piece restriction of 227db96ee (two cuts, matching its hand repair). Following one fragment's descent path instead diverges (up to 179549 cuts). Recorded as Attempt 4 with the conjecture that the least-damage recursion always terminates.
