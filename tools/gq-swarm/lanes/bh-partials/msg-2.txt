Tanner's question: Stein groups whose slope is commensurable with an (F) unit are finitely presented

If β lies in Z[c] and β^b = c^a for an (F) unit c, then V(Z[c],⟨β⟩,ℓ) is a full contracting RSG on the a-block
recoding of the c-shift, so it is finitely presented. The proof runs the Pisot finite-nucleus argument with real slope
exponents; block recoding keeps the boundary set and the finite nucleus. Example: β = θ+1 = θ³ with θ plastic. Its
β-shift is not of finite type (d_β(1) = 201^∞), yet V(Z[θ],⟨θ+1⟩,ℓ) is perfect, hence a finitely presented simple group.
The (F) node gets a pointer.

Lesson for general BH: finite presentation of RSG hosts sees the numeration, not the slope's greedy shift. The gate is
the existence of one finite numeration for the pair (breakpoint module, slope group), and block recoding moves it along
commensurability.
