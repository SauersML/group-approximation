## Setting

- **The space.** `K = ∏_(i ∈ I) C_i` with `I` countable. Each `C_i` is a Cantor space with a rooted tree coding
  whose vertex sets are called cylinders. A *box* is a product of cylinders in finitely many coordinates.
- **Coordinatewise cellular maps.** A homeomorphism `f` of `K` is *coordinatewise cellular* if there are a finite
  box partition of `K` and, on each piece `P`, a bijection `j_P` of `I` with the following property: `f(k)_i` is
  obtained from `k_(j_P(i))` by a map induced by a rooted-subtree isomorphism (a cylinder map), and it is the
  identity for all but finitely many `i`.
  - Compositions of coordinatewise cellular maps are coordinatewise cellular. So every element of `nV`, of a
    twisted Brin–Thompson group `SV_G`, of `2V_τ` (generated by `2V` and the odometer `τ × id`, a tree
    automorphism of coordinate 1), and of products of Röver–Nekrashevych groups is coordinatewise cellular.
- **The register.** Fix a coordinate `r ∈ I` and a continuous onto map `π_0 : C_r → R/Z` with two properties:
  - `π_0` is injective off a countable set;
  - every cylinder has a shadow `π_0(Z)` that is a finite union of nondegenerate closed arcs.

  Put `π = π_0 ∘ pr_r`.
  - Example: the mixed-radix register `D` (the circle Cantorized at `Q/Z`) of `rationals-embed-in-brin-thompson-group-2v`
    and `twisted-bt-point-fixing-actor-elements-are-divisible`, in any tree coding of that coordinate.
- **Register copies.** A *register copy* of a subgroup `A <= Q` is an injective homomorphism `a ↦ q_a` into
  coordinatewise cellular homeomorphisms such that:
  - `π ∘ q_a = π + a` (mod 1);
  - **(R) locality:** every nonempty open `U` contains a nonempty open `U'` and an `ε > 0` such that, for
    `|a| < ε`, `q_a` changes only coordinate `r` on `U'`.

  Registers with overflow into other coordinates satisfy (R), since `U'` can be taken away from the overflow
  point of the register.

## Theorem

Let `q` be a register copy of `A`, let `d >= 2`, and let `t` be coordinatewise cellular with
`t q_a t^-1 = q_(da)` for all `a ∈ A`. Then:

1. **The fibration is preserved up to rotations.** On every piece `P` of `t` we have `j_P(r) = r`. The twist
   `f := π ∘ t − d·π` takes finitely many values and is constant on the pieces of a refinement of the
   partition of `t`. So `t` reads the register only through the register coordinate, up to finitely many
   rotations.
2. **Bounded denominators.** There is `M` such that `A ⊆ (1/M) Z[1/d]`. In particular `A` is not `Q`, and not
   `Z[1/(dp_1 p_2 ⋯)]` for infinitely many primes `p_i ∤ d`.

So `Q x| <d>` never arises by dilating a one-coordinate register copy of `Q`.
- This holds in `nV` (all `n`), in every `SV_G` (register coordinate `*`), in `2V_τ`, and in any other
  coordinatewise cellular host.
- It holds whatever the dilation does to the other coordinates: permuting them, moving digits between them
  (baker-type), or twisting them by the actor.

## What is left

The proof uses one-coordinate registers and coordinatewise germs in exactly two places (see the proof's
remarks), so these are the two escapes left for `Q x| <d>` in Cantor hosts:
- a register **spread over at least two coordinates**, so that a box of small shadow can be long and thin and
  the dilation's pieces cut its shadow;
- **genuinely two-dimensional germs**, in which the register coordinate of `t(k)` depends continuously on
  another coordinate, for example an odometer adding that coordinate's value into the register.

These are the "two-coordinate hyperbolic mixing" design of `board/major-gln.md` R3, now in exact form.

## Lesson for general BH

**Equivariance forces locality, and locality forces arithmetic.**
- A dilation that normalizes a register copy must commute with the register's small rotations up to the twist
  `f = π∘t − dπ`, and `f` is invariant under the copy.
- In a coordinatewise host, invariance under the local rotations makes `f` constant on each piece, and makes
  the dilation read the register only through the register coordinate.
- The boundary-arithmetic lemma then bounds the denominators by one finitely generated group, `Z`-spanned by
  finitely many shadow endpoints and twist values.

So a one-coordinate divisible register survives a dilation only in a host that can twist it by a continuum
of values, or cut its shadows across coordinates. The first needs germs that are continuous but not locally constant functions of a second coordinate, such as odometer or
adding-machine germs across coordinates. For GL_n(Q) hosts: put the rational arithmetic where the twist can
be continuous, meaning a register carried jointly by several coordinates or by an acting group, never a
register in one coordinate of a coordinatewise host.
