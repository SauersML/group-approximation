Lane proof (bh-free-16, 2026-09-18). Notation as in the claim. `D_d(x) = dx` on `R/Z`. `μ(B) = Leb(π(B))`
for a box `B`. The *register depth* of a box is the depth of its cylinder in coordinate `r` (`0` if
coordinate `r` is unconstrained). The shadow `π(B)` of a box depends only on its `r`-cylinder `Z_B`.

## Step 1. The twist is constant on pieces

If `A` is cyclic there is nothing to prove, so let `A` be non-cyclic. Then its elements accumulate at `0`.
Call `x ∈ C_r` *simple* if `π_0^-1(π_0(x)) = {x}`. All but countably many points are simple, so simple
points are dense in every cylinder.

- **The twist is invariant.** `f = π∘t − dπ` is continuous, and
  `f(q_a k) = π(q_(da) t k) − dπ(k) − da = f(k)`.
- **Small rotations move simple points slightly.** Take `k ∈ U'` as in (R), with `k_r` simple. For small
  `a ∈ A`, `q_a(k)` differs from `k` only in coordinate `r`, and `π_0(q_a(k)_r) = π_0(k_r) + a`. Since `k_r` is
  simple and `C_r` is compact, `q_a(k)_r → k_r` as `a → 0`.
- **`f` does not depend on coordinate `r`.**
  - Let `y` be simple and close to `k_r`, inside a cylinder around `k_r` that `(R)` controls.
  - Choose `a_n ∈ A` with `π_0(k_r) + a_n → π_0(y)`. The limit points of `q_(a_n)(k)_r` lie in `π_0^-1(π_0(y)) = {y}`.
    So `q_(a_n)(k) → k^(y)`, the point `k` with its `r`-coordinate replaced by `y`.
  - Invariance and continuity give `f(k^(y)) = f(k)`. Such `k` and `y` are dense, so `f` is locally
    independent of coordinate `r` everywhere.
- **`j_P(r) = r` on every piece `P` of `t`.**
  - On `P`, `π(t k) = π_0(w(k_j))` with `j = j_P(r)`. Suppose `j ≠ r`.
  - Take `k ∈ P ∩ U'` with `k_r` simple, and `a ∈ A` small and nonzero. Then `q_a(k) ∈ P` by the previous bullet,
    and it has the same `j`-coordinate as `k`.
  - So `f(q_a k) − f(k) = −d a`, which is nonzero mod 1 for `0 < |a| < 1/d`. This contradicts invariance.
- **`f` is constant on deep cylinders of `P`.**
  - With `j = r`, `f(k) = π_0(w(k_r)) − dπ_0(k_r)` depends only on `k_r`, and it is locally independent of
    `k_r`. So it is locally constant on the compact `r`-cylinder of `P`.
  - So `f` takes finitely many values, and it is constant on every `r`-cylinder of depth `>= n_f`, for some
    `n_f`.

This is item 1 of the claim, with "constant on `P`" meaning constant on each piece after refining the pieces
of `t` to register depth `n_f`. Refine them so from now on.

## Step 2. Chains

- **Constants.** Let `n_t` be the largest register depth of a piece of `t`. By uniform continuity of `π_0`,
  choose `n_0 >= n_t` such that every `r`-cylinder of depth `>= n_0` has a shadow of diameter `< 1/(2d)`.
  Let `F ⊆ R/Z` be the finite set of values of `f`, and let `E` be the finite set of endpoints of the shadows
  of `r`-cylinders of depth `< n_0`.
- **The group.** Let `L ⊆ R` be the subgroup generated by `1`, by lifts of `E` and by lifts of `F`. It is
  finitely generated, so `L ∩ Q` is finitely generated, hence cyclic: `L ∩ Q = (1/M') Z`.

**Claim.** If `B` is a box with `μ(B) > 0` and `y ∈ ∂π(B)`, then `d^k ỹ ∈ L` for some `k >= 0` and any lift `ỹ`
of `y`, modulo `Z ⊆ L`.

*Proof.*
- **Shallow boxes.** If the register depth of `B` is `< n_0`, then `y ∈ E`.
- **One step of the chain.** Otherwise pick a piece `P` of `t` meeting `B`. Since `depth(Z_B) >= n_t`, the box
  `B ∩ P` has the same `r`-cylinder, hence the same shadow. `B' = t(B ∩ P)` is a box, since `t` is
  coordinatewise cellular.
- **Its shadow.** By Step 1, `π(B') = D_d(π(B)) + f_P`. `π(B)` lies in an arc of length `< 1/(2d)`, on which `D_d`
  is a homeomorphism onto its image. So `μ(B') = d μ(B)` and `∂π(B') = d·∂π(B) + f_P`.
- **The chain stops.** Repeat. Masses multiply by `d`. While the register depth is `>= n_0`, the mass is
  `< 1/(2d)`. So after some `k` steps the register depth drops below `n_0`.
- **Conclusion.** Then `d^k y + Σ_(i<k) d^(k−1−i) f_i ∈ E` with `f_i ∈ F`, so `d^k ỹ ∈ L`. ∎

Boxes with `μ = 0` are never used. There are none here, but the argument does not need that.

## Step 3. Rotations and conclusion

- **A small box.** Let `a ∈ A`, write `P'` for the pieces of `q_a`, and take a box `B` inside one piece with
  `0 < μ(B) < 1`. This is possible: `π` is onto, so some deep `r`-cylinder has a shadow of positive measure
  and diameter `< 1/2`. Take the other coordinates deep enough.
- **Its image.** `q_a(B)` is a box with `π(q_a B) = π(B) + a`. `π(B)` is a proper nonempty closed subset
  of the circle with finite boundary, so it has a boundary point `y`, and `y + a ∈ ∂π(q_a B)`.
- **Apply the Claim to both boxes.** `d^k ỹ ∈ L` and `d^(k')(ỹ + ã) ∈ L`, so `d^(max(k,k')) a ∈ L ∩ Q ⊆ (1/M') Z`.
  Hence `a ∈ (1/M') Z[1/d]`.
- **Conclusion.** This holds for every `a ∈ A`. `M = M'` depends only on `t` and `π`, and gives item 2. ∎

## Remarks

- **Arithmetic.** The arithmetic step is the finitely generated form of the lemma in
  `t-check-contains-no-q-rtimes-2-z`. A finitely generated subgroup of `R` meets `Q` in a cyclic group.
  This covers irrational shadow endpoints and irrational twists without any case analysis.
- **Where the proof breaks** (the two escapes in the claim).
  - Step 1 uses only that each coordinate of `t(k)` is a function of one coordinate of `k` on each piece. With
    genuinely two-dimensional germs, `f` can vary continuously with another coordinate, and `F` becomes
    infinite.
  - Step 2 uses that `B ∩ P` has the same shadow as `B`. With a register spread over several coordinates, a
    thin box can be cut by the pieces of `t` in a direction that changes its shadow, and masses need not grow
    along the chain.
- **Calibration.** A binary register (`π_0` the binary expansion) carries `Z[1/2] x| <2>` by the inverse baker
  map `(x, y) ↦ (y_1 x, σ y)` in `2V`. Here `f = 0`, `E ⊆ Z[1/2]/Z` and `M = 1`, as the theorem allows.
