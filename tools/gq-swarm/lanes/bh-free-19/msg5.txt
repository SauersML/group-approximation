Transcendental-slope Thompson groups have arbitrarily hard word problems; their BH case implies the Birget root

transcendental-slope-thompson-groups-have-hard-word-problems (lane proof, elementary,
not reviewed): inside D(V_lambda) = D(V(Z[lambda^{+-1}], <lambda>, 1)), the commutator
[u', rho^n w' rho^{-n}] is trivial iff floor((n+1)lambda) = floor(n lambda). Here rho is
a rotation of [0, lambda^j) doubled with its inverse, and u', w' are doubled PL bumps; no
dyadic F is needed. So the word problem computes the Sturmian word of lambda with
length-linear words. A computable Liouville lambda whose digits encode a diagonal set
then makes the 2-generated simple group D(V_lambda) beat any given recursive bound.

The route arbitrarily-complex-fp-simple-via-transcendental-slope-thompson shows that
transcendental-slope-thompson-groups-satisfy-boone-higman, for all computable lambda,
implies fp-simple-groups-with-arbitrarily-complex-word-problem. Calibration sections are
added to both nodes (Attempt 9 on the root).
