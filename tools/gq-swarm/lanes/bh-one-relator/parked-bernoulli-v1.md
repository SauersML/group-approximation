## Setting

- `C = {0,1}^N` with Thompson's `V`, and `G_V` its groupoid of germs.
- `Λ` is a finitely generated group, and `X` is any nonempty compact, zero-dimensional, metrizable `Λ`-space. The action
  need not be free, minimal or expansive.
- `T_(Λ,X) = G_V × (Λ ⋉ X)` on `Ω = C × X`, and `F(T)` is its group of full compact open bisections. When `T` is
  effective (for instance when the action is topologically free), this is the homeomorphism group used in
  `v-times-sft-full-groups-split-into-two-wreath-pieces`.
- The two pieces and the gluing group are the ones of that node, read as bisections:
  - `M = V(Λ) = C(C, Λ) ⋊ V`;
  - `N_X = LC(X, V) ⋊ Λ`;
  - `I = V × Λ`, which is `M ∩ N_X` (at the bisection level this needs no freeness);
  - `Γ~_(Λ,X) = M *_I N_X / ⟨⟨[M_0, N_1]⟩⟩`, with `π : Γ~ → F(T)` and `K_(Λ,X) = ker π`.
- `π` is onto for every `(Λ, X)`:
  - `F(T)` is generated by transpositions of product pieces (`v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions`,
    whose proof works with full bisections of any ample `H`);
  - each such transposition is a product of `M`- and `N_X`-elements, by the lift formulas of
    `v-times-full-groups-have-local-transposition-presentations`. Neither step uses freeness.

## Statement

1. **Subsystems.** Let `X ⊆ Y` be closed and `Λ`-invariant, and let
   `J = {f ∈ LC(Y, V) : f|_X = 1} ⊴ N_Y`.
   - Restriction `r : F(T_Y) → F(T_X)` is onto, with kernel the normal closure of `J`.
   - `Γ~_X = Γ~_Y / ⟨⟨J⟩⟩`.

   Hence **`K_X` is the image of `K_Y`**.
2. **Quotient groups.** Let `Λ = Λ~ / R`, with `R` the normal closure of a set `R_0`, and view a `Λ`-space `X` as a
   `Λ~`-space.
   - The groupoid quotient induces a surjection `p : F(T_(Λ~,X)) → F(T_(Λ,X))`. Its kernel is the normal closure
     of `{r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`. Here `r|_([0])` is the label `r` on the column `[0] × X`.
   - `Γ~_(Λ,X) = Γ~_(Λ~,X) / ⟨⟨r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0⟩⟩`.

   Hence **`K_(Λ,X)` is the image of `K_(Λ~,X)`**.
3. **Universality.** Let `Λ` be generated by a finite set `S`, and let `X` be any nonempty compact,
   zero-dimensional, metrizable `Λ`-space. Then `K_(Λ,X)` is a quotient of the single group `K_(F_S, C^(F_S))`, the gluing kernel of
   the Bernoulli shift of the free group `F_S` with Cantor alphabet. It is the image under a map of gluing groups
   that sends `M` onto `M` and `N` onto `N`.

## Consequences

- **One statement per rank.** Suppose the Bernoulli kernel `K_(F_n, C^(F_n))` is trivial (exactness), or the normal
  closure of finitely many elements (P2′b). Then the same holds for every free subshift over every group with
  `n` generators, and for every topologically free one. By item 3 this covers every other zero-dimensional
  action too. For exactness this is an equivalence: `K_(Λ,X) = 1` for every `n`-generated `Λ` and every `X` iff
  `K_(F_n, C^(F_n)) = 1`, since the Bernoulli shift is one of the instances. For P2′b it is a sufficient
  condition. P2′b and its exactness thus become a single problem, posed on the freest dynamics there is. Once
  that problem is solved, which `X` is used matters only in `N_X`, that is, in P2′a.
- **For the master route.** Take any instance over a finitely presented acting group generated by `n` elements.
  This covers `Λ = Λ₁ × Λ₁` of v4, which is `2n`-generated when `Λ₁` is `n`-generated, and `Λ₀` of v5. The
  gluing half of P2′ for that instance needs only the Bernoulli shift of `F_n`. The standard two-generator
  embeddings would make the acting group of v5 2-generated. They preserve finite presentation; decidability of
  the word problem is not checked here. If that holds, the instance needs the single space `C^(F_2)`, and `C^(F_4)`
  in the v4 form.
- **Failures propagate upward.** Suppose exactness fails for some `(Λ, X)`, even for a finite group acting on a
  finite set. Then it fails for the Bernoulli shift of the free group of that rank. So finite models are free
  necessary tests of the universal statement.
- **Robust to enlarging the relators.** Items 1–3 hold verbatim for any gluing group obtained from `Γ~` by adding
  a fixed set of relators written in `M ∪ N_(C^(F_S))`, since their images are the corresponding relators
  downstairs. If exactness needs more relators than disjoint-cone commutation, the right gluing presentation can
  be designed once, at the Bernoulli shift, and then holds for every `X`.

## The first finite test

Take `Λ = Z/2` acting on two points `{x_1, x_2}`, a quotient of `F_1 = Z` acting on the period-two orbit.
- `F(T) = V_(2,2) ≅ V` acts on `C × {x_1, x_2}`, and `s` swaps the copies.
- `M = V(Z/2)` is exactly the centralizer of the fixed-point-free involution `s`: an element commuting with `s`
  has the form `(c, x) ↦ (v(c), f(c)·x)`.
- `N = (V × V) ⋊ ⟨s⟩` is the stabilizer of the partition into the two copies.

So universal exactness in rank one implies the following statement about Thompson's group alone:

> `V` is the amalgam of the centralizer of a fixed-point-free involution `s` and the stabilizer of a two-piece
> partition swapped by `s`, over their intersection, modulo commutation of `M_0` with `N_1`.

All groups here are finitely presented, so this kernel is always finitely normally generated. Only exactness is
tested.

**The first relation to check** is an entangled braid. Let:
- `A = (1, w) ∈ N`, where `w` swaps the cones `[u']` and `[u]` on the copy of `x_2` only;
- `B = s|_([u']) ∈ M`, the local swap of copies over `[u']`.

In `F(T)` their supports share the one piece `[u'] × {x_2}`, so `(AB)^3 = 1`. Neither acts on the other's support by
a single label or a single `V`-move. Relations (R1) and (R2) of `v-times-sft-gluing-kernel-is-relatively-perfect`
fix how an `M`-element and an `N`-element interact in only two cases: when their supports are disjoint, or when
one acts on the other's support by a single label. The pair `(A, B)` is in neither case. The manipulations tried
here only move the question around:
- conjugating by `V × Z/2`;
- rewriting `A` as `(w, w)(w, 1)`;
- splitting through a third cone.

Each turns the braid for `(A, B)` into the braid for another entangled pair. Splitting `[u], [u']` into halves
shows that `x = (AB)^3 ∈ K` satisfies `x = x_0 x_1`, with `x_0, x_1` commuting `V`-conjugates of `x`. This is
consistent with relative perfectness, and it is not a proof that `x = 1`.

The lane tried two families of candidate representations of `Γ~` that might separate `x` from `1`:
- `M` and `N` acting on `C × X^` through an equivariant map `X^ → X`;
- doubled actions, in which the `C_0`-half follows one equivariant map `X^ → X` and the `C_1`-half another.

Both families factor through `F(T)`, through an explicit homomorphism `F(T_X) → F(T_(X^))`. This is weak evidence
for exactness, and nothing more.

## Lesson for general BH

The gluing half of the group-side finiteness gate is **universal**:
- the kernel for any action is a quotient of the kernel for the Bernoulli shift of the free group;
- exactness passes to closed invariant subsets and to quotient groups;
- so it is decided once, at the freest dynamics, where the clopen algebra is free and no subshift structure is
  left.

If the universal statement holds, no property of `X` (SFT, minimality, rigidity) is needed for the gluing. The
whole `X`-dependence of the group-side gate then sits in `N_X`, that is, in P2′a. This would give P2′a/P2′b exactly
the "rigidity ⊕ combinatorics" shape the synthesis assumes.

In the other direction, finite models are free tests: an obstruction to gluing in any finite model is an
obstruction to the universal statement. The first test is a question about Thompson's `V` alone: is `V` glued from
the centralizer of a fixed-point-free involution and the stabilizer of a halving swapped by it?

## Proof

**A lemma.** Let `π : A → B` be onto with kernel `K`. Let `E ⊆ A` and `F ⊆ B` be subsets with `π(E) = F`, and write
`A' = A/⟨⟨E⟩⟩`, `B' = B/⟨⟨F⟩⟩` and `π' : A' → B'`. Then `ker π'` is the image of `K`.

Since `π` is onto, `π(⟨⟨E⟩⟩) = ⟨⟨F⟩⟩`. If `π'(a⟨⟨E⟩⟩) = 1`, then `π(a) = π(e)` for some `e ∈ ⟨⟨E⟩⟩`, so `a e^-1 ∈ K`
maps to the class of `a`. ∎

**Item 1, the gluing groups.** Restriction `q : N_Y → N_X` is onto:
- every clopen partition of the closed set `X` extends to one of `Y`, so `LC(Y, V) → LC(X, V)` is onto;
- the label group is the same.

Its kernel is `J`, and `J ∩ I = 1`. So `M *_I N_X = (M *_I N_Y) / ⟨⟨J⟩⟩`, by the universal property of the pushout.
Also `q(N_(Y,1)) = N_(X,1)`, so the commutation relators map onto the commutation relators. Hence
`Γ~_X = Γ~_Y / ⟨⟨J⟩⟩`.

**Item 1, the full groups.** `r` is a homomorphism, because `C × X` is invariant.
- *Onto.* `F(T_X)` is generated by `M` and `N_X`, and both lift.
- *Kernel, first step.* Let `r(g) = 1`, and write `g` in normal form: pieces
  `B_(u → u') × (λ, P)`, with source rectangles `[u] × P` partitioning `Ω`. If `P ∩ X ≠ ∅`, the restricted
  arrows `(uw ↦ u'w, (λ, x))` with `x ∈ P ∩ X` are units, so `u = u'` and `λ = 1`, and the piece is a unit piece.
- So `g` fixes the union `U` of those pieces with unit arrows, and it maps the complement `W` onto itself. `W` is
  a finite union of rectangles `[u_i] × P_i` with `P_i ∩ X = ∅`. Put `Z = ⋃ P_i`, a clopen set disjoint from `X`.
  Then `g ∈ F(G_V × (Λ ⋉ Y)|_Z)`, which is generated by its transpositions
  (`v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions` for the ample groupoid `(Λ ⋉ Y)|_Z`).
- *Kernel, second step.* After splitting in the `C` direction, each such transposition is a commuting product of
  elementary ones `t`, which exchange `[u] × P` and `[u'] × λP` with `P, λP ⊆ Z`, `u, u' ≠ ∅`.
  - If `u, u'` are comparable, the rectangles are disjoint only if `P ∩ λP = ∅`. Then `n_1 = v^[λP]` fixes
    `[u] × P`, where `v ∈ V` maps `[u']` by prefix onto a cone `[u'']` with `u'' ⊥ u` (such a cone exists since
    `u ≠ ∅`). So `n_1 t n_1^-1` is elementary with incomparable addresses.
  - If `u ⊥ u'`, then `t = m^-1 n m`, with `m = λ|_([u]) ∈ M` and `n = (u u')^[λP] ∈ LC(Y, V)`. Evaluate on the six
    regions `[u]` or `[u']` or elsewhere, times `P` or `λP` or elsewhere. Since `λP ⊆ Z` misses `X`, `n ∈ J`.
- Hence `ker r ⊆ ⟨⟨J⟩⟩_(F(T_Y))`. The reverse inclusion is clear.

**Item 1, conclusion.** `π_Y` is onto (Setting). Apply the lemma with `E = J`.

**Item 2, the gluing groups.** `V(Λ~) → V(Λ)` is onto, with kernel `C(C, R)`. This kernel is the normal closure in
`V(Λ~)` of `{r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`:
- `v r|_([0]) v^-1 = r|_([w])` for `v ∈ V` mapping `[0]` onto a proper cone `[w]` by prefix;
- `s|_([w]) r|_([w]) s|_([w])^-1 = (s r s^-1)|_([w])` for `s ∈ Λ~`;
- products give every `ρ|_([w])` with `ρ ∈ R`, and `ρ|_(C) = ρ|_([0]) ρ|_([1])`;
- finite products of these give `C(C, R)`.

`LC(X, V) ⋊ Λ~ → LC(X, V) ⋊ Λ` has kernel `R`, acting as global labels. `R` acts trivially on `X`, so it is
normal there, and `R ⊆ C(C, R)` as constants. Also `I_(Λ~) ∩ C(C, R) = R`. So killing `C(C, R)` in `M` kills
exactly `R` in `I` and in `N`, and the commutation relators map onto the commutation relators. Hence
`Γ~_(Λ,X) = Γ~_(Λ~,X) / ⟨⟨r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0⟩⟩`.

**Item 2, the full groups.** The quotient map `T_(Λ~,X) → T_(Λ,X)` is the identity on units, and on arrows it is
`(γ, (s, x)) ↦ (γ, (s R, x))`.
- *Onto.* Pieces lift through any choice of preimages of labels.
- *Kernel.* A full bisection lies in the kernel iff every arrow has unit `G_V`-part and label in `R`. So the
  kernel consists of products of commuting label-only bisections `r|_([u] × P)`, with `r ∈ R`.
- *Each `r|_([u] × P)` lies in the normal closure `𝒩` of `{r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`.*
  - `𝒩` contains every `r|_([w])` with `r ∈ R`, by the computation above, done inside `M ≤ F(T)`.
  - Let `[u], [w]` be disjoint cones, `v ∈ V` the prefix swap of `[u]` and `[w]`, and `n = v^[P] ∈ N`. Then
    `n r|_([u]) n^-1 = r|_(([w] × P) ∪ ([u] × P^c))`, by evaluating arrows on the four regions. So
    `r|_([u])^-1 · n r|_([u]) n^-1 = r^-1|_([u] × P) · r|_([w] × P)` lies in `𝒩`.
  - Modulo `𝒩`, therefore, `r|_([u] × P) ≡ r|_([w] × P)` whenever `u ⊥ w`.
  - For `u ≠ ∅`, pick `w ⊥ u`. Then `u0 ⊥ w` and `u1 ⊥ w`, so
    `r|_([w] × P) ≡ r|_([u] × P) = r|_([u0] × P) · r|_([u1] × P) ≡ r|_([w] × P)^2`.
    So both sides are trivial modulo `𝒩`.
  - For `u = ∅`, split into `[0]` and `[1]`.

**Item 2, conclusion.** `π_(Λ~)` is onto. Apply the lemma with `E = {r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`.

**Item 3.** Let `Λ = F_S / R`.
- Choose an embedding `f : X → C`. Then `x ↦ (t ↦ f(t̄^-1 x))` is an `F_S`-equivariant embedding of `X` into
  `C^(F_S)`, where `t̄` is the image of `t` in `Λ`. The shift convention is `(t·y)(g) = y(t^-1 g)`.
- Item 1, with `Y = C^(F_S)` and `X` as an `F_S`-space, makes `K_(F_S,X)` a quotient of `K_(F_S, C^(F_S))`.
- Item 2 makes `K_(Λ,X)` a quotient of `K_(F_S,X)`.

Both maps are induced by maps of gluing groups that are onto on `M` and on `N`. ∎
