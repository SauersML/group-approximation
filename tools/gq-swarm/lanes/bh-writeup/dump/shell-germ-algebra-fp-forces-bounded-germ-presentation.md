**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, not reviewed; no priority claimed).

## Setting

`P` is a countably infinite group generated by a finite symmetric set `S`, `nu : P -> N` is a
bijection, `rho : P -> Sym(N)` and `E_nu = <V, hat(P)> <= Homeo(C)` are as in
`shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`, `p = 0^∞`, and
`A = A_(F_2)(G_nu)` is the Steinberg algebra of the germ groupoid. Let `Q` be the isotropy
group of `G_nu` at `p`, i.e. the germ group of `E_nu` at `p`. Let `τ ∈ Q` be the germ at `p`
of `z ↦ 0z` (the cofinite shift on shells), and `r_g ∈ Q` the germ at `p` of `hat(g)`. Then
`Q = <τ, r_g : g ∈ S>` (same node). The generators of `A` are `s_0, s_1, t_0, t_1` and
`u_g = 1_(hat(g))`, `g ∈ S` (`germ-steinberg-algebras-over-v-are-fg-central-simple`).

For a word `W` in `τ^(±1)` and `S`, its *input length* is the number of letters from `S`.

## Statement

Suppose `A` is finitely presented, with relators of length at most `ℓ` in these generators.
Let `Q'` be the quotient of the free group `F(τ, S)` by the normal closure of all words of
input length at most `2ℓ` that are trivial in `Q`. Then the natural map `Q' -> Q` is an
isomorphism. So `Q` has a presentation on `τ, S` in which every relator contains at most
`2ℓ` input letters, with no bound on the exponents of `τ`.

## Proof

**1. A matrix model over the germ group.** Let `Ω = {w 0^∞}` be the `G_nu`-orbit of `p`. For
`x = u 0^∞ ∈ Ω`, `u` the shortest such word, let `γ_x` be the germ at `p` of
`0^(|u|) z ↦ u z`; so `γ_p = 1`. The source fibre `F_2[G_p]` is a free right
`F_2[Q]`-module on `{γ_x}`, and left multiplication by `A` commutes with the right action of
`Q`. So each `a ∈ A` has a column-finite matrix `(a_yx)` over `F_2[Q]` indexed by `Ω`, with
`a · γ_x = Σ_y γ_y a_yx`. Since this is a representation, every relator of `A` has the zero
matrix.

**2. The generator matrices are monomial.** For a bisection `B` and `x ∈ Ω`, `1_B · γ_x` is
`β γ_x` for the unique `β ∈ B` with source `x`, or `0`. So each column has at most one
nonzero entry, `γ_y^(-1) β γ_x ∈ Q` with `y = r(β)`. A direct computation gives:
- `s_0`, `s_1` have entries `τ`, and `t_0`, `t_1` have entries `τ^(-1)`, at every point where
  they are defined, including `p` (`s_0`, `t_0` fix `p`; `s_1` sends `p` to `1 0^∞`; `t_1`
  kills `p`);
- `u_g` at `x = 0^n 1 w' 0^∞` has entry `τ^(rho_g(n) - n)`, because
  `0^(|u|) z ↦ 0^n 1 w' z ↦ 0^(rho_g(n)) 1 w' z ↦ 0^(|u| + rho_g(n) - n) z`;
- `u_g` at `p` has entry `r_g`, and `u_g` fixes `p`.

So the entry of a monomial `m` of length `≤ ℓ` in column `x` is a word
`τ^(a_0) r_(g_1)^(±1) τ^(a_1) ... r_(g_k)^(±1) τ^(a_k)` with `k ≤ ℓ`. An input letter
appears exactly when the path of `m` from `x` passes through `p` at a `u`-step.

**3. The relators survive in `Q'`.** A relator `r = Σ_i m_i` over `F_2` has zero matrix
over `F_2[Q]`. So in each entry the words `w_i` contributed by the monomials cancel in pairs:
there is a perfect matching `i ↔ i'` with `w_i = w_(i')` in `Q`. Each `w_i w_(i')^(-1)` has
input length at most `2ℓ` and is trivial in `Q`, hence trivial in `Q'`. Now define matrices
over `F_2[Q']` for the generators by the same formulas, with `r_g` read in `Q'`. The same
matching shows that every relator has the zero matrix over `F_2[Q']`. By finite presentation
this defines a homomorphism `ψ : A -> M_Ω(F_2[Q'])`.

**4. Every relation of `Q` holds in `Q'`.** Let `W` be a word in `τ^(±1)`, `S` that is
trivial in `Q`. Let `W~ ∈ A` be the monomial obtained by `τ ↦ s_0`, `τ^(-1) ↦ t_0`,
`g ↦ u_g`. Each factor fixes `p`, and its `(p,p)` entry is the corresponding letter, so the
`p`-column of `ψ(W~)` is the single entry `W`, read in `Q'`, at `(p,p)`. In `A`, `W~` is the
indicator of a compact open bisection containing the unit germ at `p`. Since `G_nu` is
étale, that bisection contains every unit over some `[0^m]`, so `W~ e_m = e_m` with
`e_m = s_0^m t_0^m`. Its `p`-column is `τ^m τ^(-m) = 1` at `(p,p)`. Applying `ψ` and reading
the `(p,p)` entry gives `W = 1` in `F_2[Q']`, hence in `Q'`. So `Q' -> Q` is injective. `∎`

## What it shows about the algebra gate

- **It is the germ-group half of the group gate, with the shift exponents freed.**
  Finite presentation of the group `E_nu` forces a *finite* presentation of `Q`
  (`shell-envelope-fp-forces-fp-germ-group`). Finite presentation of the algebra forces only
  a presentation with boundedly many input letters per relator. The reason is visible in
  step 2: one algebra relator, read along shell `n`, yields a group relation with shift
  exponents `rho_g(n) - n`, which are unbounded for every input that is not virtually cyclic
  (`shell-germ-algebra-fp-forces-fp-germ-group`, item 2).
- **The two conditions differ in general.** `Z ≀ Z = <τ, r | [r, τ^k r τ^(-k)] (k ∈ Z)>` has a
  presentation with four input letters per relator and is not finitely presented. That is
  the Bieri--Strebel phenomenon, and in the shell setting it is the ascending-HNN clause of
  `shell-germ-fp-is-an-ascending-hnn-gate`. So the necessary condition proved here does not
  include that clause.
- **Not claimed.** No shell example is given where `Q` is boundedly presented in this sense
  but not finitely presented, and no proof that bounded presentation of `Q` suffices for
  finite presentation of `A`. The implication `A` f.p. ⇒ `Q` f.p. is neither proved nor
  refuted; this is the part of it that holds.
