All citations are to Lodha, arXiv:1710.06220v2, TeX source `FPSimple.tex`, read on 2026-09-18.

1. **S is finitely generated.** `S` is generated by `T` together with the map `s` (l.109–117). `T` is
   finitely generated, being `<F, l>` with `F = <a, b>` (l.141–157).
2. **S is simple.** This is the Proposition in the section "Simplicity of S" (l.1480–1505). Its proof uses
   that `G_0'` is simple (Burillo–Lodha–Reeves, quoted as Theorem `simplegroup`, l.507–509).
3. **S contains Thompson's T in a standard action.**
   - Here `T` is "the group of piecewise `PSL_2(Z)` homeomorphisms of `S^1 = R ∪ {∞}` with breakpoints in
     `Q ∪ {∞}`" (l.108, l.155–157).
   - Lodha's map `Φ : 2^N → R ∪ {∞}` (l.327–340) semiconjugates the Cantor model of `T` (prefix replacements
     and the maps `p_n`, l.275–290) to this action. The dyadic model, binary expansion `2^N → R/Z`,
     semiconjugates the same Cantor model to the piecewise-linear action of `T`.
   - The two quotient maps identify exactly the same pairs of sequences, `w01^∞ ~ w10^∞` and `0^∞ ~ 1^∞`:
     - `φ(01^∞) = 1 = φ(10^∞)`, by the recursion at l.331–332;
     - `Φ(0^∞) = −∞ = ∞ = Φ(1^∞)`;
     - both maps are injective on all other sequences.
   - So they induce a homeomorphism `R/Z → R ∪ {∞}` that conjugates the piecewise-linear `T` onto Lodha's `T`.
     This is the classical Minkowski question-mark conjugacy.
   - Hence `S` contains a conjugate of the standard `T`, as the hypothesis of
     `fg-simple-circle-groups-with-t-are-two-generated` requires.
4. **Conclusion.** By items 1–3, and items 1–2 of that theorem, `S` is generated by two elements. One of them
   is a rotation of finite order in (the conjugate of) `T`.

*Check on hypothesis (G).* It is used only through item 2 of the general theorem: `S` is simple, and the
rigid stabilizer of a proper closed arc contains a copy of `F`, whose commutator subgroup is nontrivial.
Concretely, `s = w_(10,110) = y_10 y_110^(-1)` (l.402–405) is itself a commutator of two elements supported
in the compact interval `[−2, 3] ⊂ R`:
- Let `u = y_10 y_001^(-1) = w_(10,001)`. It lies in `S`, because the relations `w_(σ,τ) f = f w_(σ·f, τ·f)`
  (l.1403) carry `w` along the `T`-action on pairs:
  - `(10, 001)` and `(10, 1110)` are independent and not cyclically consecutive, so Lemma `Taction`(2)
    (l.301–313) makes `u` a `T`-conjugate of `w_(10,1110)`;
  - `w_(10,1110) = w_(10,110) w_(110,1110)`, and `(10, 110)` and `(110, 1110)` are consecutive, so by Lemma
    `Taction`(1) both factors are `T`-conjugates of `s`.
- Let `x' ∈ F` have the tree pair `(00, 010, 011, 10, 110, 1110, 1111) ↦ (00, 01, 10, 110, 11100, 11101, 1111)`.
- `x'` fixes the cones `[00]` and `[1111]` pointwise, so it is supported in `Φ([01] ∪ [10] ∪ [110] ∪ [1110]) =
  [−1, 3]`. It maps the cone `[10]` onto `[110]` by the prefix replacement `10 ↦ 110`.
- So conjugation by `x'` carries `y_10` to `y_110` and fixes `y_001`. Hence `s = u · x'^(-1) u^(-1) x'` in
  Lodha's right-action convention.

This explicit form is not needed for the proof, but it confirms directly that (G) holds with the single extra
generator `s`.
