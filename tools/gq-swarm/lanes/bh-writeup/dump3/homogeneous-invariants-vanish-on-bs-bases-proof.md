**Step 1 (the lemma).** Let `s u s^-1 = u^n`, `|n| >= 2`, and let `nu` be
homogeneous and conjugation invariant. Then
`nu(u) = nu(s u s^-1) = nu(u^n) = |n| nu(u)`. If `nu(u)` is finite this forces
`(|n| - 1) nu(u) = 0`, so `nu(u) = 0`; otherwise `nu(u) = infinity`. []

**Step 2 (translation length).** For an isometric action on a metric space `X`
and `x in X`, the limit `l(g) = lim_m d(x, g^m x) / m` exists by subadditivity
of `m -> d(x, g^m x)` (Fekete), is independent of `x` because
`|d(x, g^m x) - d(y, g^m y)| <= 2 d(x, y)`, satisfies `l(g^k) = |k| l(g)`
(for `k < 0` use `d(x, g^-m x) = d(g^m x, x)`), and is conjugation invariant
because `d(x, (h g h^-1)^m x) = d(h^-1 x, g^m h^-1 x)`. It is finite since
`d(x, g^m x) <= m d(x, gx)`. Step 1 gives `l(u) = 0`. An element with `l = 0`
is not loxodromic, by definition of loxodromic in the CAT(0) and Gromov
hyperbolic settings. []

**Step 3 (scl and entropy).** `scl` is homogeneous and conjugation invariant on
`[H, H]`, and finite there because commutator length is; Step 1 applies.
Topological entropy of a homeomorphism of a compact metric space satisfies
`h(f^k) = |k| h(f)` and is a conjugacy invariant, so Step 1 gives
`h(u) in {0, infinity}`. []

**Step 4 (CAT(0), first proof).** Let `Gamma` act properly and cocompactly by
isometries on a complete CAT(0) space, let `u in Gamma` be conjugate to `u^n`
with `|n| >= 2` by `s in Gamma`, and suppose `u` has infinite order. Put
`K = <u, s>`. Since the only defining relation of `BS(1,n)` holds in `K`, there
is a surjection `BS(1,n) -> K` sending the base generator to `u` and the stable
letter to `s`. The image `N` of the base `Z[1/n]` is the normal closure of `u`
in `K`; it is abelian and `K/N` is cyclic, so `K` is metabelian, hence solvable,
and it is generated by two elements.

`K` is not virtually abelian. Suppose it were. Then `K` is finitely generated
and virtually abelian, so it is polycyclic and every subgroup of it is finitely
generated; in particular `N` is a finitely generated abelian group, written
additively. Conjugation by `s` is an automorphism `phi` of `N` with
`phi(u) = n u`, so `u = n v` for `v = s^-1 u s in N`, and iterating,
`u` is divisible by `n^k` in `N` for every `k >= 1`. Writing
`N = Z^r (+) T` with `T` finite, an element divisible by all powers of `n` has
zero free part, so `u in T` is a torsion element, contradicting that `u` has
infinite order.

So `K` is a finitely generated solvable subgroup of `Gamma` that is not
virtually abelian, contradicting
`cat0-group-solvable-subgroups-are-virtually-abelian`. []
**Step 5 (CAT(0), second proof, independent of Step 4).** By Step 2,
`l(u) = 0`. In a proper cocompact isometric action on a complete CAT(0) space
every element is semisimple, that is, it attains its displacement infimum
(Bridson--Haefliger, Part II, Chapter 6; cited, not re-read in this lane). An
element with `l(u) = 0` that attains its infimum has a fixed point, and
properness makes point stabilizers finite, so `u` has finite order. []

**Step 6 (the host exclusions).** Every nontrivial image of `H4` contains
`BS(1,2)`, and `BG` contains `BS(1,2)`. Both contain an element of infinite
order conjugate to its square, so Steps 4 and 5 exclude every proper cocompact
CAT(0) host. The Burger--Mozes groups and Wise's lattices act properly and
cocompactly on a product of two trees, which is a complete CAT(0) square
complex, so they contain no copy of `H4` or of `BG`. The same applies to any
group acting properly and cocompactly on a CAT(0) cube complex. []
