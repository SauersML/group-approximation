**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed). No priority is claimed.

This generalizes `out-ct-z-is-c2` (the case `P` = all odd primes, Kourovka 17.57)
to every nonempty `P`. There are two new points:
- the integers must be located inside `X_P ∩ Q`, which is larger than `Z` when `P` is
  not all primes;
- all moduli produced along the way must be `P'`-smooth.

The external inputs are the same as before and were read at source:
- Matui arXiv:1210.5800, Theorem `spatial`, Proposition `tfg=F`, and the lemma before
  `spatial>iso`;
- Bell's Theorem 1.4 (`bell-generalized-cobham-theorem`), or independently
  Adamczewski–Bell arXiv:1303.2019, Theorem `thm: main`, via `out-ct-z-is-c2`.

## Statement

Let `P` be a nonempty set of odd primes, `P' = P ∪ {2}`, and `X = X_P = ∏_{p∈P'} Z_p`.
Then `Out(CT_P(Z)) = ⟨σ⟩ ≅ C_2`, where `σ(n) = −n − 1`.

## Setup

As in `ct-p-z-groups-are-pairwise-nonisomorphic` (Setup), `CT_P(Z) = F := [[G_P]]`,
the full group of the germ groupoid of canonical box maps on `X`.

- `N` is the normalizer of `F` in `Homeo(X)`.
- `T_m(x) = (x − r)/m`, where `r ≡ x mod m` and `0 ≤ r < m`, for `P'`-smooth `m`.
  Note `T_{ab} = T_b ∘ T_a`.

## Step 1: Out(F) ≅ N/F, with σ ∈ N ∖ F

Matui's `tfg=F` and `spatial` make every automorphism conjugation by some `φ ∈ N`. The
centralizer of `F` in `Homeo(X)` is trivial, by the argument of item 1 of the reduction
node. Moreover:
- `σ` conjugates the canonical map `B(r', m') → B(r, m)` to the canonical map
  `B(m' − 1 − r', m') → B(m − 1 − r, m)`, with the same moduli, so `σ ∈ N`;
- `σ(N_0) = Z_{<0}`, so `σ ∉ F`.

## Step 2: φ(Z) = Z, and φ(N_0) ∈ {N_0, Z_{<0}}, for every φ ∈ N

By Step 2 of `ct-p-z-groups-are-pairwise-nonisomorphic`, the points with nontrivial
germ group are exactly the rational points `X ∩ Q`, and `φ` permutes them. At a
rational point `y`, the germ group is `Γ_y = {m/m' : T_m(y) = T_{m'}(y)}`.

- **Integers have the full group.** If `y ∈ Z`, then `T_m(y) ∈ {0, −1}` for all large
  `m`, so `Γ_y = Γ_P`, the group of all positive `P'`-smooth rationals.
- **2 in the germ group forces an integer.**
  - `Γ_y ∩ 2^Z = 2^{t Z}` for some `t = t_2(y) ≥ 1`, by Step 2 of the 17.60 node.
  - If `t_2(y) = 1`, pick `m'` with `T_{2m'}(y) = T_{m'}(y)`. Then
    `z = T_{m'}(y)` satisfies `T_2(z) = z`.
  - The only fixed points of `T_2` are `0` and `−1`, so `y = (y mod m') + m'z ∈ Z`.
- **An intrinsic count.**
  - By Steps 3–4 of the 17.60 node, the axis `A_2` at `y` and its label "2" are
    intrinsic, and so are the neutral subgroup
    `H_2(y) = {γ ∈ Γ_y : v_2(γ) = 0}` and the contracting generator `2^{t_2(y)}` of
    `⋂_{p≠2} H_p`. These use `|P'| ≥ 2`, i.e. `P ≠ ∅`.
  - Hence the `H_2(y)`-space `(A_2 ∖ {y})/⟨2^{t_2(y)}⟩` is intrinsic up to
    equivariant homeomorphism.
  - It is `t_2(y)` shells `2^j Z_2^×`, with `H_2(y)` acting by multiplication.
  - Let `K_y` be the closure of `H_2(y)` in `Z_2^×`. It is infinite, since `p^{t_p}`
    lies in it for `p ∈ P`, and so it is open.
  - Its minimal closed invariant subsets are the `K_y`-cosets, so their number is
    `c(y) = t_2(y) · [Z_2^× : K_y]`, and `φ` preserves `c`.
- **The count characterizes the integers.**
  - `H_2(y) ⊆ ⟨P⟩ = H_2(n)` for `n ∈ Z`, so `K_y ⊆ K_Z`.
  - Hence `c(y) ≥ t_2(y) · c(n)`, and `c(y) > c(n)` whenever `y ∉ Z`.
  - So `Z = {y ∈ X ∩ Q : c(y) is minimal}`, which gives `φ(Z) = Z`.
- **Orbits.** The `G_P`-orbits in `Z` are `N_0` and `Z_{<0}`: canonical maps keep the
  sign of `t` in `r + tm`, and class transpositions of large 2-power modulus act
  transitively on each. Orbits are permuted by `φ`.

## Step 3: φ is affine on a box

Replace `φ` by `σφ` if needed, so that `φ(N_0) = N_0`, and put `Φ = φ|_{N_0}`. Fix
`p ∈ P`. For `b ∈ {2, p}`:
- the dilation elements `z ↦ bz + e` on `B(r, b) → B(br + e, b²)` have `P'`-smooth
  moduli;
- they extend to elements of `F`, since the complements are nonempty clopen sets.

Items 1–2 of `ct-z-normalizers-are-2-and-3-regular` then apply verbatim, with base 3
replaced by `p`. So `Φ` is an integer-valued bijection `N_0 → N_0` that is 2-regular
and `p`-regular over `Q`, with polynomial growth.

`2` and `p` are multiplicatively independent. By Bell's Theorem 1.4 (or
Adamczewski–Bell, following steps 2–6 of `out-ct-z-is-c2`), `Φ` is eventually a
quasi-polynomial. Step 7 there gives a class `i mod N` and integers `c_1 > 0`, `c_0`
with `Φ(i + Nt) = c_0 + c_1 t` for `t ≥ t_0`.

**New point: `N` need not be `P'`-smooth.** Write `N = N_1 N_2`, with `N_1` `P'`-smooth
and `N_2` prime to `P'`. Then `N_2` is a unit of `X`.
- The set `{i + Nt : t ≥ t_0}` is dense in the box `B(i, N_1)`, because
  `t ↦ N_2 t` is a homeomorphism of `X` and `{t ≥ t_0}` is dense in `X`.
- `ℓ(z) = c_0 + c_1 N_2^{-1} (z − i)/N_1` is continuous on `B(i, N_1)`, affine with
  rational coefficients, and agrees with `φ` on that dense set.
- So `φ = ℓ` on `B(i, N_1)`.

## Step 4: an affine-on-a-box normalizer lies in F

1. **Locally affine everywhere.** Take `φ ∈ N` with `φ(N_0) = N_0`, affine on a box
   `B`. For `x ∈ X`, minimality and Matui's lemma give `g ∈ F` with `g(x) ∈ B`. Near
   `x`, `φ = (φgφ^{-1})^{-1} ∘ φ ∘ g` is a composite of affine maps. By compactness,
   `φ` is affine on each piece of a finite partition of `X` into boxes `B(r_i, m)`
   with `m` `P'`-smooth.
2. **The image moduli are `P'`-smooth.**
   - On `Z`, the piece `r_i + tm` maps to the progression `s_i + t n_i`, with
     `n_i > 0` and zero shift, by the argument of
     `ct-z-is-the-rcwa-group-fixing-the-nonnegative-integers` (`φ(N_0) = N_0`).
   - `φ(B(r_i, m))` is the closure of `s_i + n_i Z`, which is `B(s_i, (n_i)_{P'})`,
     where `(n_i)_{P'}` is the `P'`-part of `n_i`.
   - Its trace on `Z` is `s_i + (n_i)_{P'} Z`, but it also equals
     `φ(B(r_i, m) ∩ Z) = s_i + n_i Z`, because `φ` is bijective on `Z`. So
     `n_i = (n_i)_{P'}`.
3. **Conclusion.**
   - By item 3 of `ct-p-z-is-a-one-vertex-k-graph-full-group` (for finite `P`), or by
     the Setup of the 17.60 node (for any `P`), `φ|_Z ∈ CT_P(Z)`.
   - `Z` is dense, so `φ ∈ F`.
   - Hence `N = F ⊔ σF`, and `Out(CT_P(Z)) = ⟨σ⟩ ≅ C_2`. ∎

## Remarks

- **The boundary case.** `P = ∅` gives `V`, where Step 2's labeling and Step 3's second
  base both fail. There `Out(V) ≅ O_2` is infinite
  (`out-v-contains-the-root-problem-for-the-full-2-shift`). So the answer is `C_2`
  exactly when `P ≠ ∅`.
- **Consequences.** For `P ≠ ∅`, `Aut(CT_P(Z)) ≅ CT_P(Z) ⋊ ⟨σ⟩`. Together with
  `ct-p-z-groups-are-pairwise-nonisomorphic`, the family `{CT_P(Z)}` is
  2^{ℵ_0} pairwise non-isomorphic simple groups, each with outer automorphism group
  `C_2`. For finite `P` they are also `F_∞`.
