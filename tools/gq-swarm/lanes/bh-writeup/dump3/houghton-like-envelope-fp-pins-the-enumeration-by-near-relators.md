**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-free-48, not independently reviewed, no priority claimed). The proof is below.

**Setting.** As in `free-shift-houghton-like-envelopes-are-not-fp`:
- `P` is infinite with a finite generating set `A`, and `nu : P -> N` is a bijection.
- `Γ_nu = H_n(E_nu(P))` is generated by a finite generating set of `K = H_n(E_2)` together with the ray copies
  `rho^(k)_g`, for `g ∈ A` and `k ∈ [n]`.
- `F = P * <ŝ>`. The **near relators** are the reduced words of `N_nu = ker(F -> R_nu)`.
- For a bijection `nu'`, write `lambda'_g(nu'(h)) = nu'(gh)`. The **realization** of a word `u ∈ F` at `m ∈ N`
  applies the letters of `u` right to left: `λ̂_g` acts as `lambda'_g` and `ŝ^c` as `m -> m + c`. It is defined when
  every step stays in `N`.
- `u` **closes at `m` far out** (for `nu'`, above `M`) if, whenever its realization path from `m` is defined and
  stays in `[M, ∞)`, it returns to `m`.

**Statement.**

1. **Pinning by near relators.** Suppose `Γ_nu` is finitely presented. Then there are:
   - a finite set `S ⊂ P`,
   - a finite set `U` of nonempty near relators,
   - and a bound `M` with `nu^-1([0, M)) ⊆ S`,

   such that `nu` is the **only** bijection `nu' : P -> N` with both of these properties:
   - `nu' = nu` on `S`;
   - every `u ∈ U` closes far out for `nu'`, above `M`, at every `m`.
2. **Flexibility kill.** Suppose that for every finite `S` and `U` and every `M` as in item 1, some `nu' ≠ nu`
   has both properties. Then `H_n(E_nu(P))` is finitely presented for no `n >= 1`. The free-shift case `N_nu = 1`
   has `U` empty, so every `nu'` qualifies (bh-map's item 2).
3. **Countability.** For fixed `P` and `n`, at most countably many `nu` make `H_n(E_nu(P))` finitely presented.
4. **Translation relators need a virtually cyclic input.** If `λ̂_g ŝ^(-a) ∈ N_nu` with `g ≠ 1`, then `a ≠ 0` and
   `[P : <g>] < ∞`.
   - So if `P` is not virtually cyclic, every cyclically reduced nontrivial near relator has at least two
     `λ̂`-syllables.
   - Even for `Z`, the zigzag enumeration of `zigzag-cyclic-shift-envelope-is-finitely-presented` uses the
     two-syllable relator `ŝ λ̂_1 ŝ^-1 λ̂_1`: near infinity, `s` conjugates `lambda_1` to its inverse.
5. **Coset flexibility.** Let `Q ≤ P` have infinite index, and suppose every word of `U` lies in `Q * <ŝ>`. Then for
   every finite `S` there is `nu' ≠ nu`, equal to `nu` on `S`, with `lambda'_q = lambda_q` for every `q ∈ Q`.
   - Such a `nu'` has the same realizations of every word of `Q * <ŝ>`.
   - Hence, in item 1, the `P`-syllables of the words of `U` generate a **finite-index** subgroup of `P`.
   - In particular, the mixed relation `[λ̂_t ŝ^4 λ̂_t^-1, ŝ^8]` of `infinite-order-inputs-admit-mixed-near-relations`,
     or any family of near relators supported on one infinite-index subgroup, cannot pin `nu` by itself.

## Proof

**Item 1.** Fix a finite presentation `<Y | r_1, ..., r_m>` of `Γ_nu` on the generating set `Y` of the setting. Run
the trajectory analysis of `free-shift-houghton-envelopes-local-stability-proof`. Strand coordinates put the even
strand of ray `k` at `m <-> 2m` (source convention `rho(g)(2 nu(h)) = 2 nu(gh)`).

- Let `E_0 ⊂ X = [n] x N` be a finite set outside which every K-letter of every `r_i` acts by its strand map.
- A trajectory of `r_i` that avoids `E_0` has a strand sequence fixed by `r_i` and its starting strand `j`. Recording
  `λ̂_g` for each `rho^(k)_g` applied on the even strand of ray `k`, and `ŝ^c` for each strand map, gives a word
  `w_{i,j}` in `F`.
- Since `r_i(nu) = 1`, the image of `w_{i,j}` in `R_nu` is trivial. Let `u_{i,j}` be its reduced form, drop the empty
  ones, and let `U` be the rest.
- Each `u ∈ U` is near-trivial for `nu`. So there is `M_u` such that, for `nu`, `u` closes at every `m >= M_u` where
  its realization is defined. Choose `M >= max(M_u)`.
- Put `E = E_0 ∪ {points of X with strand coordinate < M}`, which is finite. Let `S` be the finite set of elements
  of `P` at which `nu` is used by the trajectories through `E`, together with `nu^-1([0, M))`.

Two facts about free reduction in `F`. Both hold because `lambda'` is a genuine action and `ŝ^a ŝ^b` acts as
`ŝ^(a+b)` wherever defined:
- where the literal realization of `w_{i,j}` is defined, it equals that of `u_{i,j}`;
- the reduced path visits a subset of the points of the literal path.

Now let `nu'` have both properties. Fix `r = r_i` and `x ∈ X`.
- If the `nu'`-trajectory of `x` meets `E`, it is exceptional. As in the source's item 1, it coincides with the
  `nu`-trajectory because `nu' = nu` on `S`. So `r(nu')(x) = x`.
- Otherwise every K-letter acts by its strand map along it, and all strand coordinates are `>= M`. So its strand
  coordinates trace the literal realization of `w_{i,j}` in `[M, ∞)`. By the two facts, the reduced path of `u_{i,j}`
  is defined, stays in `[M, ∞)`, and has the same endpoint. The second property gives `r(nu')(x) = x`.

So every `r_i` holds for `nu'`, and `y -> y(nu')` is a homomorphism `phi : Γ_nu -> Sym(X)`.
- By item 3 of `free-shift-houghton-like-envelopes-are-not-fp`, `nu' = nu ∘ R_c` for some `c ∈ P`.
- `S` is nonempty, since it contains `nu^-1([0, M))` and `M >= 1` after enlarging `M`. Pick `h ∈ S`. Then
  `nu(hc) = nu(h)`, so `c = 1` and `nu' = nu`.
- Finally, `nu` itself has both properties: a path in `[M, ∞)` starts at `m >= M >= M_u`. ∎

**Item 2.** This is the contrapositive of item 1. If `N_nu = 1`, every reduced near relator is empty, so `U` is
empty, and the swap in the source's item 2 gives `nu' ≠ nu` on any cylinder. ∎

**Item 3.** The tuple `(S, nu|_S, U, M)` is finite data. By item 1 it determines `nu`, because the two properties
mention `nu` only through `nu|_S`. There are countably many tuples. ∎

**Item 4.** The relation says `nu(gh) = nu(h) + a` whenever `nu(h)` is large.
- If `a = 0`, then `gh = h` for some `h`, so `g = 1`.
- Otherwise, after replacing `g` by `g^-1`, `a > 0`. For large `m`, `m` and `m + a` lie in the same right coset
  `<g>h`, because `nu^-1(m + a) = g nu^-1(m)`. So the tail of `N` is covered by at most `a` right cosets of `<g>`,
  and `[P : <g>] < ∞`.
- A cyclically reduced near relator with at most one `λ̂`-syllable is `ŝ^b` (trivial in `R_nu` only if `b = 0`),
  `λ̂_g`, or `λ̂_g ŝ^(-a)`. The first bullet rules out `λ̂_g` with `g ≠ 1`. ∎

**Item 5.** Since `[P : Q] = ∞` and `S` is finite, pick two right cosets `Qh_1 ≠ Qh_2` that miss `S`. Define
`psi : P -> P` by `psi(q h_1) = q h_2`, `psi(q h_2) = q h_1`, and `psi = id` elsewhere.
- `psi` is a bijection, `psi ≠ id`, and `psi = id` on `S`.
- `psi(qh) = q psi(h)` for all `q ∈ Q` and `h ∈ P`.

Put `nu' = nu ∘ psi`. Then `nu'(qh) = nu(q psi(h)) = lambda_q(nu'(h))`, so `lambda'_q = lambda_q` on all of `N`.
So every word of `Q * <ŝ>` has identical realizations, and the same paths, for `nu` and `nu'`.

If every word of `U` lies in `Q * <ŝ>`, then `nu'` has both properties of item 1 and differs from `nu`,
contradicting item 1. Apply this with `Q` the subgroup generated by the `P`-syllables of `U`. ∎

## What this says for the Houghton-like route

A witness enumeration for `decidable-inputs-have-fp-houghton-like-shift-envelopes` must be **self-assembling**. It
must be the unique extension of a finite seed `nu|_S` under finitely many local closing rules, which are near
relators of `R_nu` read far out. Items 4 and 5 constrain those rules:
- they must involve `P`-letters generating a finite-index subgroup;
- for `P` not virtually cyclic, each must have at least two `P`-syllables.

For accessible `P` that is not virtually cyclic, `accessible-shell-inputs-admit-no-shift-normalizing-enumeration`
also rules out the normalizing form `ŝ^m λ̂_g ŝ^-m λ̂_(phi(g))^-1` used by the zigzag, holding for every generator `g`
at once.

The following are dead:
- generic (free-shift) enumerations, by item 2;
- enumerations whose near relations are all supported on one infinite-index subgroup, by item 5. This covers the
  single mixed relation along `<t>` constructed for route-form and infinite-order inputs.

**Open.** Does some one-ended `P` admit an enumeration that is rigid in the sense of item 1? A "no" for all one-ended
`P` kills this route for every input needed by `boone-higman-iff-simple-kazhdan-decidable-inputs`, since infinite
Kazhdan groups are one-ended. The coset argument of item 5 does not extend to finite-index `Q`: an equivariant `psi`
fixing a point of every coset is the identity. So an answer needs a genuinely new flexibility mechanism, or a
rigid construction.
