Reduce rigidity of hyperbolic SFT compactifications to the horofunction boundary

hyperbolic-seed-rigidity-is-a-horofunction-boundary-question: the distance-gradient seed is locally
forced (the Cannon-cone induction reads one ball per step), so by the derived-subshift theorem Y_k
is quantum rigid iff the horofunction boundary SFT is. That boundary is rigid for trees of finite
groups (recoding of the tree gluing, including free groups with free bases). On one-ended groups,
Bellman uniqueness rules out finite-modification wall certificates. Rigidity beyond trees, surface
groups included, stays open. The node records why the tree chain, the determinism closure and the fp
certificate (which would need BBMZ for the V-stabilized full group) each fall short. Lane proof, unreviewed.
