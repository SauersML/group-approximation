Polynomial-activity automata: finite presentation of V_d(G) reduces to cycling relators

For an automaton in which every state has at most one section in its own strongly connected component (implied by subexponential activity), order letters by component and count them as a multiset. A section keeps this complexity only when the word is special: every letter is a cycle state receiving its cycle letter. Induction then shows the relator kernel is the lift ideal of a finite set R as soon as every cycling relator (special under every power of the cycle successor map) lies in it. So V_d(G) is finitely presented iff the cycling relators lie in a finitely generated lift ideal; this recovers Grigorchuk's group, explains the spine failure, and gives N = K_inf when no nontrivial relator is cycling.
