Seed evaluation is bounded by the forcing radius; hard seeds must force non-locally

An isolated point of an SFT is computed on a ball by searching admissible patterns up to its
forcing radius R(n), the least radius at which all admissible extensions of the seed agree. So
evaluation costs at most exponential in |B_R(n)|, and over polynomial-growth groups a seed that
beats exponential time needs super-polynomial forcing radius. Locally forced seeds, which include
forward-deterministic machine seeds and the recorded finite-state seeds, have linear R and are
capped. The open hard-seed premise records the necessary condition, the backward-signal candidate
shape, and the ordered-field design rule for exact orbit closure.
