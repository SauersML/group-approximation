Rigid anticipation needs scale-bounded fault detection; decoding fix for the seed orbit-problem nodes

New claim: with injective walks, a quantum rigid anticipation layer admits no two separated
non-terminating chains. By compactness, long walks of the seed are isolated at every scale. For
walks that run a reversible machine with local tape edits, this forces every cell at a fixed offset
to be approached within bounded time. Ordinary deciders sweeping growing workspaces violate that,
so the raw halting rung is not rigid. A rigid rung needs sparse hard inputs and fault detection at
every scale (Gacs/DRS-type self-checking).

  polynomial-time Turing reduction. The title and Lesson are adjusted.
