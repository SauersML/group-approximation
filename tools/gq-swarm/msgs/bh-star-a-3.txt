Seed compactifications dominate the end compactification

Gluing a seed point to a translate across any cut that avoids their difference set would create a
second origin. So the difference set coarsely connects the two seeds, and every seed compactification
maps onto the Freudenthal end compactification. Over infinitely ended groups the seed boundary has a
perfect kernel mapping onto the Cantor set of ends, and scattered seeds exist only over one- or
two-ended groups.

With bh-invent-10's derived-subshift theorem, the quantum condition of CAP is live only on perfect
kernels. Over one-ended groups CAP has a rigidity-free part: scattered, locally forced seeds, which are
rigid automatically.
