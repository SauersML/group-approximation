Branching squares are the only operadic escape from the complexity cap

Follow-up to the bounded-propagation cap. Unary squares (at most one move per piece in some common multiple of each generator pair) give bounded propagation with P = generators. So they make Ore a local consequence of the squares and cap the word problem at 2^2^O(n). More generally, any class whose split-refinement functions are dominated by one recursive function of the data is capped, hence not universal (nor are the full groups it presents). An operadic not-CAP host must therefore have branching squares. There reconciliation is not a well-founded recursion, and Ore is a global theorem, as for the closed-MCG edge-split host. Records the open machine-operad candidate with test rungs R-a to R-c, shared with the CAP lanes.
