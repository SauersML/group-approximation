**Even-branching design notes (not landed, hand analysis 09-18):** in a fresh-cell hierarchy (interior 0^k at start/end, extents read off the tape) every zero-start-cost first-chain starts in ONE state at ONE cell, so its types are indistinguishable until the first junction, which sees only a fresh 0 (plus an arbitrary boundary). A return's first crossing must stop at an unmarked far cell, locally identical to the top of an E-extension chain => returns cannot occur in first-chains => (with the parity lemma) branching is odd. So an even design must break freshness: parent-left marks inside the domain, a head-carried stack whose content distinguishes chain depth, or a non-restoring (Gray/Hanoi-type) tape. Side question found: Hamiltonian paths of Q_k whose flipped-bit positions move by exactly ±1 each step (exist for k = 2, 3: 1,2,1 and 2,1,2,3,2,1,2) would be a 'non-restoring SMART' skeleton; existence for all k unknown to me.
**Binary design pass 2 (hand, 09-18, not landed).** Per-tape type calculus E (zero start cost, extra cell at end), S (extra cell at start, zero end cost), R (return, far extra cell): costs + parity force E -> E R, S -> R S, R -> E S (unique). A peek rule (E ending on fresh 0 looks one cell further: 0 = extend, home-letter = turn; R homes written w_E / w_S by the parent) resolves the first-junction collision with 3 letters and no walks. What kills it is REVERSIBILITY: "S starts on a 0 cell" arises both from a turn (cell was 0) and from the reset of an S-parent's counter (cell was w_S). Separating them needs a new state, which needs a new counter letter, which needs a new state: a pigeonhole regress (r+1 variants into r letters). Lesson: a return's turn creates one bit of history that a finite reversible head cannot erase. Even branching needs a place where that bit is absorbed: a second tape (4V), a counter whose last value is reached only through turns, or letters that the turn writes and a later crossing of the same cell erases. Next: look for the third option inside SMART_3-style counters (odd sub-counts per level, combined into branching 2 through a two-level period).
