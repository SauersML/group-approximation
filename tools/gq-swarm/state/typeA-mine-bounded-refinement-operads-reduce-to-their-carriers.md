## Setting

`𝒪` is an operad with transformations as in `oligomorphic-operads-have-f-infinity-groups`, with
Thumann's conventions (`thumann-operad-groups-need-only-f-infinity-transformations`). Fix a color
`c` and write:
- `ℐ_c = Aut_ℐ(c)`;
- `E_c` for the set of one-sided classes `[θ]` of elementary operations with output `c`.

`ℐ_c` acts on `E_c` and on all one-sided classes with output `c` through `[θ]·h = [θ*h]`. The
stabilizer of `[θ]` is the pull-through subgroup `H_θ` (Step 2 of the Thumann node).

A class `[θ]` **divides** `[μ]` if `μ = θ∘ψ` for some morphism `ψ` on the inputs of `θ`, up to input
transformations.

**Hypotheses.** Fix `D ≥ 1`.
- **(O1_D)** Finitely many `ℐ_c`-orbits of one-sided classes of degree at most `D` with output `c`.
  This is the degree-`D` part of (O1).
- **(BR_D), bounded refinement.** Any two classes in `E_c` have a common multiple of degree at most
  `D`.
- **(LF), local finiteness.** Every class has finitely many elementary divisors.
  - It holds when an elementary operation is determined, up to input transformations, by the
    pieces it produces. Then a divisor is fixed by which pieces of the multiple it merges. This is
    the case in the twisted cube operad, where a split is determined by its coordinate.
  - It also holds whenever `E_c` is finite.
  - In a general topological operad it is a genuine hypothesis. Two elementary operations can have
    the same pieces and differ by homeomorphisms of the pieces that are not transformations.

## Statement

1. Under (O1_D), (BR_D) and (LF), `ℐ_c` has finitely many orbits on `E_c × E_c`, hence finitely
   many orbits of two-element subsets of `E_c`.
2. Suppose moreover that:
   - `ℐ_c` is finitely presented;
   - each `H_θ` is finitely generated (for instance `F_∞`, as in (O2));
   - `ℐ_c` acts faithfully on `E_c`.

   Then `ℐ_c ↷ E_c` is of type (A) in Zaremsky's sense
   (`type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`). Every subgroup of `ℐ_c`, in particular every
   input placed among the transformations, lies in `B_A` through `SV_{ℐ_c}`, **without using the
   operad**.
3. If `ℐ_c` is not faithful on `E_c`, then its action is `[A_2]` in the abstract sense, and the input
   meets exactly the kernel-removal gap recorded in `bbmz-oligomorphic-and-twisted-questions-are-pbh-forms`.

## Proof

- **Divisibility is equivariant.** Composition at the output commutes with composition at the
  inputs. So `[θ]` divides `[μ]` iff `[θ]·h` divides `[μ]·h`.
- **Counting.** Let `μ_1, …, μ_r` represent the orbits of classes of degree at most `D`, finitely
  many by (O1_D).
  - Given `(e_1, e_2) ∈ E_c^2`, (BR_D) gives a common multiple `[μ]` of degree at most `D`. Then
    `[μ] = [μ_j]·h` for some `j` and some `h ∈ ℐ_c`.
  - By equivariance, `(e_1·h^{-1}, e_2·h^{-1})` is a pair of elementary divisors of `μ_j`.
  - So every orbit on `E_c^2` meets the finite set `⋃_j Div(μ_j)^2` given by (LF), and the number
    of orbits is at most `Σ_j |Div(μ_j)|^2`.
  - Unordered pairs are images of ordered ones.
- **Part 2.**
  - Point stabilizers are the `H_θ`, which are finitely generated by assumption.
  - With faithfulness and finite presentation of `ℐ_c`, these are the four conditions of type (A).
  - Zaremsky's Corollary B, quoted there, gives the conclusion.
- **Part 3.** Without faithfulness, the same counts are the abstract `[A_2]` condition of
  `abstract-twisted-bt-type-fn-criterion`. ∎

## Instances and where the regime changes

- **Twisted Brin--Thompson.** Two splits `θ_s` and `θ_t` refine in degree 3 (split along `s`, then
  both halves along `t`). So (BR_3) holds, and part 1 recovers the pair-orbit half of type (A) from
  (O1_3).
- **Every finite-type operad.** `E_c` is finite, so the lemma says nothing new: the carrier is finite
  on moves. In the finite-type case, `finite-type-operad-groups-split-over-a-label-kernel` is the
  binding constraint.
- **Oligomorphic operads with bounded refinement**, for instance moves whose pairwise relations
  come from finitely many shapes of common refinement: `ℐ_c` is itself an actor on its moves. The
  operad is then at most a twisted Brin--Thompson group in different clothes, and the input gains
  nothing.
- **Unbounded refinement (non-LCM regime).** Here pairs of moves need common refinements of
  unboundedly many pieces, and `ℐ_c` may have infinitely many orbits on pairs. The fp half of
  `interval-spherical-brown-criterion` counts only intervals of bounded length `L` whose open part
  is disconnected. It asks for the union `Y_L` of such intervals to be simply connected, that is,
  for every long pair relation to be a consequence of short ones.
  - In the transformation channel, this is the only place where the operadic language can produce
    an actor from a carrier that is not an actor.
  - The non-LCM dissection operads (`non-lcm-dissection-operad-groups-are-f-infinity`) and the
    closed-MCG hosts are infinite-spine operads, but they carry their inputs in the other channel.

## Scope correction (2026-09-18, later the same day)

This node concerns inputs placed **among the transformations** `ℐ_c`. An operad host can also carry
a group `Λ` **through its dynamics**, when `Λ` acts by fractions rather than by transformations.
In that channel the host is the full group `[[Λ]]`
(`symmetric-ore-operads-containing-their-carrier-are-full-groups`). `Λ` may then have infinitely
many orbits on pairs of tiles even when refinement is bounded.

The example is the edge-split operad. It is finitely generated with transformation group `S_{m+1}`,
and its spine is conjectured finite. Its carrier `GL_{m+1}(Z)` has infinitely many orbits on pairs
of unimodular cones.

So the first version's title phrase, "operadic hosts can only beat twisted Brin--Thompson through
unbounded refinement depth", was too broad. It holds for the transformation channel only, and the
title now says so.

## Lesson for general BH

**Refinement depth is the dial.**
- **Bounded refinement.** Common refinements of bounded size turn any orbit-finiteness of an operad
  back into Zaremsky's pair-orbit condition on the carrier. Operadic, Garside-type (LCM) and
  cube-type move languages are then no more powerful than twisted Brin--Thompson: whatever they
  certify through their transformations, the transformation group already certifies alone.
- **The sharpened operadic actor problem, transformation channel.** This is the only regime of
  that channel that can beat twisted Brin--Thompson. Find a finitely presented `ℐ ⊇ G` and moves
  such that:
  - `ℐ` has infinitely many orbits on pairs of moves;
  - the pair relations of bounded length lie in finitely many orbits;
  - they generate all longer relations (simple connectivity of `Y_L`).

  Through the transformations, an input with no Zaremsky actor can be carried only this way.
- **The other channel.** The dynamics channel needs no pair-orbit condition at all. There the
  carrier acts by fractions, and the host is its full group
  (`symmetric-ore-operads-containing-their-carrier-are-full-groups`). The closed-MCG hosts use
  this channel.
