\title[Coordinate-block factorization and \texorpdfstring{\((TT)/T\)}{(TT)/T}]
{Coordinate-Block Factorization under Single-Sandwich Division
and Applications to Property \texorpdfstring{\((TT)/T\)}{(TT)/T}}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{August 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{20J06, 20F65, 16S50, 16S88, 19B99}
\keywords{property (TT)/T, quasi-cocycles, elementary groups,
Leavitt algebras, purely infinite rings, bounded generation}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We prove a coordinate-block factorization theorem for general linear
groups over rings satisfying the single-sandwich condition
\[
  a\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  xay=1\ \text{for some }x,y.
\]
Every element of \(\GL_n(R)\) is a product of at most \(2n+2\)
factors, each an elementary transvection or an element of one fixed
coordinate copy of \(\GL_{n-1}(R)\).  Thus \(\GL_n(R)\) has width at
most \(2n+2\) relative to the elementary root subgroups and that
fixed coordinate block.

The algebraic factorization is the main input developed here.  We
combine it with a quantitative root-subgroup estimate in Mimura's
relative quasi-cocycle framework.  The estimate is proved here by an
exhausting finite-word filtration whose boundary terms vanish by an
exact telescoping identity.  If \(R\) is a nontrivial finite-type
\(\F_2\)-algebra carrying a binary Leavitt family, satisfying the
single-sandwich condition, and satisfying
\(\diag(u,1)\in\EL_2(R)\) for every \(u\in R^\times\), then
\(\EL_n(R)\) has property \((TT)/T\) for every \(n\ge2\).
For the binary Leavitt algebra, the structural identifications
\(\GL_n(L)=\EL_n(L)\cong L^\times\) are known from the work of
Khanh--Thanh.  Combining them with the rigidity theorem gives the
conclusion
\[
  \EL_n\bigl(L_{\F_2}(1,2)\bigr)
  \cong L_{\F_2}(1,2)^\times,
  \qquad n\ge2,
\]
with property \((TT)/T\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

All rings and ring homomorphisms in this paper are unital.

Property \((TT)/T\), introduced by Mimura, asks that Hilbert-space
quasi-cocycles be bounded after the invariant-vector part of the
representation has been removed.  It is stronger than Kazhdan's
property~\((T)\) and is designed to control bounded cohomology and
homomorphisms into groups admitting unbounded
quasi-cocycles~\cite{MimuraTT}.  Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain proved property~\((T)\)
for elementary groups of rank at least three over arbitrary finitely
generated associative rings~\cite{ErshovJaikin}.  The corresponding quasi-cocycle problem
is subtler: the bounded error in the cocycle identity accumulates
under a long word, so finite generation alone gives no global bound.

The present paper supplies a globalization mechanism for a class of
purely infinite rings.  Its algebraic input is an explicit
matrix factorization.  The hypothesis is the following strong
two-sided division property:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sandwich}
  a\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \exists x,y\in R,\qquad xay=1.
\end{equation}
We call~\eqref{eq:sandwich} the single-sandwich property.  Ara, Goodearl, and
Pardo proved that, for a nondivision simple ring, this is equivalent
to pure infiniteness~\cite[Theorem~1.6]{AraGoodearlPardo}.

\begin{mainthm}[Coordinate-block factorization]\label{thm:A}
Let \(R\) be a nontrivial ring satisfying~\eqref{eq:sandwich}, let
\(n\ge2\), and fix a coordinate \(j<n\).  Every \(A\in\GL_n(R)\)
admits a factorization of length at most \(2n+2\) in which every
factor is either an elementary transvection or belongs to the
coordinate copy
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.coordinateBlock_factorization}
\[
  \GL_{n-1}^{(j)}(R)
  =\{B\in\GL_n(R):Be_j=e_j,\ e_j^{\,t}B=e_j^{\,t}\}.
\]
\end{mainthm}

Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is a relative bounded-width statement rather
than bounded elementary generation: the bounded factors come from
the elementary root subgroups together with one fixed coordinate
block.  Fixing the block lets the quasi-cocycle argument use one
relative subgroup throughout the factorization.  A version in which
the coordinate depends on the matrix could also be used after a finite
permutation argument, but it would be a weaker width statement with
additional bookkeeping.  By the characterization just cited, the conclusion applies
in particular to every unital purely infinite simple ring.

For the synthesis theorem, a \emph{binary Leavitt family} in \(R\)
means elements \(x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1\) satisfying
\[
  y_i x_j=\delta_{ij},\qquad x_0y_0+x_1y_1=1.
\]
It supplies the matrix self-similarity used to move between ranks.
We say that an \(\F_2\)-algebra is \emph{finite type} when it is
generated by a finite set as an \(\F_2\)-algebra, equivalently when
it is a quotient of \(\F_2\langle X\rangle\) for some finite set
\(X\).

\begin{mainthm}[Rigidity synthesis]\label{thm:B}
Let \(R\) be a nontrivial finite-type \(\F_2\)-algebra.  Assume that
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(R\) contains a binary Leavitt family;
\item \(R\) satisfies the single-sandwich
  property~\eqref{eq:sandwich};
\item \(\diag(u,1)\in\EL_2(R)\) for every \(u\in R^\times\).
\end{enumerate}
Then \(\EL_n(R)\) has property \((TT)/T\) for every \(n\ge2\).
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.finiteType_elementaryGroup_hasTTmodT}
\end{mainthm}

The assumptions play separate roles: finite type presents the
ring as a quotient of a finite free algebra, the Leavitt family moves
between matrix ranks, the single-sandwich property supplies bounded
matrix reduction, and the diagonal condition identifies elementary
and general linear groups.

There is a useful structural interpretation.  Under the first two
ring-theoretic assumptions, \(R\) is simple, is not a division ring,
and is therefore purely infinite simple by
Ara--Goodearl--Pardo's characterization.  Their description
\(V(R)\cong\{0\}\sqcup K_0(R)\) identifies the existence of a binary
Leavitt family with the module identity \(R\cong R^2\), equivalently
\([1_R]=0\) in \(K_0(R)\)~\cite{AraGoodearlPardo}.  Their isomorphism
\(R^\times_{\mathrm{ab}}\cong K_1(R)\), together with the GE property,
shows that \(K_1(R)=0\) implies the diagonal hypothesis: every unit is
a product of commutators, and the elementary identity in
Khanh--Thanh's Lemma~4.1 puts
\(\diag(u,1)\) in \(\EL_2(R)\).  Thus the two concrete
self-similarity and diagonal assumptions are the visible forms of
\([1_R]=0\) and \(K_1(R)=0\) in the purely infinite simple setting.

\begin{mainthm}[Binary Leavitt algebra]\label{thm:C}
The unit group of the binary Leavitt algebra has property \((TT)/T\):
\[
  L_{\F_2}(1,2)^\times
  \quad\text{has property }(TT)/T.
\]
For every \(n\ge2\), matrix self-similarity and the elementary
diagonal reduction give
\[
 \EL_n\bigl(L_{\F_2}(1,2)\bigr)
 =\GL_n\bigl(L_{\F_2}(1,2)\bigr)
 \cong L_{\F_2}(1,2)^\times.
\]
In particular, every displayed elementary group has property
\((TT)/T\).
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.binaryLeavittUnits_hasTTmodT}
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.binaryLeavitt_elementaryEquivUnits}
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.binaryLeavitt_elementaryGroup_hasTTmodT}
\end{mainthm}

The rank-two case is not a low-rank analogue of the universal-lattice
argument.  The analytic estimate is proved in rank four; the binary
Leavitt self-similarity then identifies \(\EL_2(L)\) with
\(\EL_4(L)\).  This is why a rank-two elementary group can inherit
property \((TT)/T\) here.

The algebraic equalities in Theorem~\ref{thm:C} are not new.
Khanh and Thanh prove \(K_1(L_k(1,2))=0\), perfection of its unit
group, and
\(\GL_n(L_k(1,2))=\EL_n(L_k(1,2))\) for every \(n\ge2\)
~\cite[Proposition~4.2]{KhanhThanh}.  Their complete-prefix-code
matrix isomorphisms \(M_n(L_k(1,2))\cong L_k(1,2)\) send the
elementary group onto the unit group
~\cite[Corollary~4.4]{KhanhThanh}.  The contribution of
Theorem~\ref{thm:C} is the property \((TT)/T\) conclusion for this
already identified unit group.

\subsection*{Relation to previous work}

The argument combines several established theories.  Mimura
introduced property \((TT)/T\), developed the relative-to-global
strategy for quasi-cocycles~\cite{MimuraTT}.  The quantitative argument below gives
an explicit root bound and also controls the invariant-vector summand.
Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain proved property~\((T)\) for elementary
groups of rank at least three over finitely generated associative
rings~\cite{ErshovJaikin}.
On the ring-theoretic side, Ara, Goodearl, and Pardo obtained the GE
consequence for purely infinite simple rings, using the earlier
Menal--Moncasi reduction, and identified \(K_1\) with the
abelianization of the unit group~\cite{MenalMoncasi,AraGoodearlPardo}.
Ara, Brustenga, and Corti\~nas
computed the stable algebraic \(K\)-theory of Leavitt path
algebras~\cite{AraBrustengaCortinas}.

The pivot in Section~\ref{sec:factorization} belongs to the same
bounded-reduction circle as Shalom's property~\((T)\) criterion and
the GE reduction used by Ara--Goodearl--Pardo; compare the discussion
in Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain~\cite{ErshovJaikin}.  This is a setting in
which stable-range elimination is unavailable: purely infinite simple
rings have infinite Bass stable rank
~\cite[Theorem~2.8(2)]{AraGoodearlPardo}.
The fixed-coordinate organization is the additional feature used here:
the coordinate is chosen before the matrix, exactly one block factor
occurs, and the remaining word has length at most \(2n+1\).
Thus the full factorization has width \(2n+2\).  The rigidity theorem
uses this factorization to globalize relative quasi-cocycle bounds.
Section~\ref{sec:quasicocycles}
gives a quantitative proof of the root estimate, and
Section~\ref{sec:leavitt} verifies the remaining structural
hypotheses for the binary Leavitt algebra.

Each numbered theorem and lemma proved in the formal companion carries
a margin tag naming its public Lean declaration, written relative to
the root namespace \nolinkurl{PropertyTTPaper}.  Appendix~\ref{app:formal}
gives a proof-order reading path through the implementation and states
the definitions and trust surface used there.

Section~\ref{sec:quasicocycles} develops the relative root estimate.

\section{Quasi-cocycles and relative control}\label{sec:quasicocycles}

Let \(G\) be a group and let
\(\pi:G\to\mathcal U(\mathcal H)\) be a unitary representation on a
complex Hilbert space.  A map \(b:G\to\mathcal H\) is a
\emph{quasi-cocycle of defect at most \(D\ge0\)} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quasicocycle}
  \norm{b(gh)-b(g)-\pi(g)b(h)}\le D
  \qquad(g,h\in G).
\end{equation}
An exact cocycle has \(D=0\); it is the translation part of an
affine isometric action.  A quasi-cocycle permits uniformly bounded
failure of the affine action law.

\begin{definition}\label{def:tt}
A group \(G\) has property \((TT)/T\) if, for every unitary
representation \(\pi\) with no nonzero invariant vector, every
Hilbert-space quasi-cocycle into \(\pi\) is bounded on \(G\).
For a subgroup \(H\le G\), the pair \((G,H)\) has relative property
\((TT)/T\) if the same hypotheses imply boundedness on \(H\).  We
write relative \((TT)\) when the conclusion holds without excluding
invariant vectors.
\end{definition}

We first isolate the quantitative rank-three estimate used in the
direct proof.  For distinct \(i,j,k\), write
\(H_{ijk}=\langle X_{ik},X_{jk}\rangle\le\EL_3(A)\), and identify
the elements of \(X\) with the corresponding free generators of
\(A\).

The proof uses the word-length filtration
\(A_{\le d}\subseteq A\).  Put
\(V_d=A_{\le d}\oplus A_{\le d}\), viewed as the finite elementary
abelian subgroup
\[
 (a,c)\longmapsto x_{ik}(a)x_{jk}(c)
 \quad\text{of }H_{ijk}.
\]
For an orthogonal representation \(\sigma\) on a real Hilbert space,
let \(P_{d,\chi}\) be the spectral projection for
\(\chi\in\widehat V_d\), and put \(z_{d,\chi}=P_{d,\chi}z\).
The projections are mutually orthogonal and their squared norms sum to
\(\norm z^2\).  Write a character as \((\chi_1,\chi_2)\).  For a
character \(\chi\) of \(A_{\le d}\), define
\[
 \nu(\chi)=\min\{\lvert w\rvert:\chi(w)=-1\},
\]
where a word is identified with its monomial in \(A_{\le d}\), and
put \(\nu(\chi)=d+1\) when \(\chi\) is trivial.  In particular,
\(\nu(\chi)=0\) exactly when \(\chi(1)=-1\): degree zero is the
one-dimensional constant subspace \(\F_2\cdot1\).  The nontrivial
pairs split into
\[
 \begin{array}{c@{\quad}l}
 A&0<\nu(\chi_2)<\nu(\chi_1)\\
 B&0<\nu(\chi_1)=\nu(\chi_2)\\
 C&0<\nu(\chi_1)<\nu(\chi_2)\\
 D&\nu(\chi_1)=0\ \text{or}\ \nu(\chi_2)=0.
 \end{array}
\]
Let \(m_d^A,m_d^B,m_d^C,m_d^D\) be the corresponding Fourier
masses.  Let \(\partial_{r,d}\) be the mass for which the \(r\)-th
coordinate character is trivial on \(A_{\le d-1}\) and becomes
nontrivial on \(A_{\le d}\).  Thus every index below is a genuine
character of the finite elementary abelian group \(V_d\).

\begin{lemma}[Finite-plane energy estimate]\label{lem:plane-energy}
Let \(\sigma:\EL_3(A)\to\mathcal O(\mathcal H)\) be an orthogonal
representation on a complete real Hilbert space.  If \(P\) is the
orthogonal projection onto
\(\mathcal H^{H_{ijk}}\) and \(w=z-Pz\), then
\begin{align}\label{eq:plane-energy}
 \norm w^2\le{}&d_{ik,1}^2+d_{jk,1}^2
 +\frac32\sum_{s\in X}2\norm z
       (d_{ij,s}+d_{ji,s})\notag\\
 &+2\norm z(d_{ij,1}+d_{ji,1}),
\end{align}
where \(d_{ab,t}=\norm{\sigma(x_{ab}(t))z-z}\).
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.freeCharacteristicTwo_planeEnergyEstimate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 U_{ab}=2\norm z\,d_{ab,1},\qquad
 G_{ab}=\sum_{s\in X}2\norm z\,d_{ab,s}.
\]
Conjugation by \(x_{ji}(1)\) shears region \(A\) into region \(B\),
and conjugation by \(x_{ij}(1)\) does the same from \(C\) to
\(B\).  More explicitly, on the plane coordinates these conjugations
act by
\[
 (a,c)\longmapsto(a,c+a),
 \qquad (a,c)\longmapsto(a+c,c),
\]
with the choice of formula determined by the adjacent root.  Their
dual maps are
\[
 (\chi_1,\chi_2)\longmapsto(\chi_1\chi_2,\chi_2),
 \qquad
 (\chi_1,\chi_2)\longmapsto(\chi_1,\chi_1\chi_2).
\]
Suppose, for example, that \((\chi_1,\chi_2)\) lies in \(A\), so
\(0<\nu(\chi_2)<\nu(\chi_1)\).  At the first degree detected by
\(\chi_2\), the character \(\chi_1\) is still trivial.  Hence
\(\nu(\chi_1\chi_2)=\nu(\chi_2)\), while the second character is
unchanged, and the image lies in \(B\).  Interchanging the
coordinates gives the transport from \(C\) to \(B\).

For either shear \(g\), spectral covariance is the identity
\[
 \sigma(g)P_{d,\chi}\sigma(g)^{-1}=P_{d,\chi\circ g^{-1}}.
\]
Together with orthogonality and
\[
 \left|\sum_{\chi\in\Omega}\norm{z_{d,\chi}}^2-
 \sum_{\chi\in\Omega}\norm{z'_{d,\chi}}^2\right|
 \le(\norm z+\norm{z'})\norm{z-z'}
\]
for \(z'=\sigma(g)z\), these two transports give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ABCB}
 m_d^A\le m_d^B+U_{ji},\qquad
 m_d^C\le m_d^B+U_{ij}.
\end{equation}
On region \(D\), one coordinate character detects the unit
coefficient.  For an involution \(r\), the projection onto its
\((-1)\)-eigenspace is \((I-\sigma(r))/2\); hence its squared norm is
one quarter of the squared \(r\)-displacement.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dmass}
 m_d^D\le\tfrac14(d_{ik,1}^2+d_{jk,1}^2).
\end{equation}

We next record the cross-stage inequalities controlling the
equal-valuation region.  Write
\(\operatorname{res}_d:\widehat V_{d+1}\to\widehat V_d\) for
restriction.  For each \(\chi\in\widehat V_d\), its Fourier component
is the orthogonal sum of the components indexed by
\(\operatorname{res}_d^{-1}(\chi)\).  Consequently its squared norm is
the sum of their squared norms.  A character whose two valuations are
not newly exposed in degree \(d+1\) remains in the same valuation
region after restriction.  Therefore, if \(m_d^S\) denotes the mass
in a union \(S\) of valuation regions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stage-monotonicity}
 m_d^S\le m_{d+1}^S+\partial_{1,d+1}+\partial_{2,d+1}.
\end{equation}
The two generator shears give the sharper descents
\begin{align}
 m_{d+2}^{A\cup B}
 &\le m_{d+1}^{C\cup D}+G_{ji}+\partial_{2,d+2},
 \label{eq:AB-descent}\\
 m_{d+2}^{C\cup B}
 &\le m_{d+1}^{A\cup D}+G_{ij}+\partial_{1,d+2}.
 \label{eq:CB-descent}
\end{align}
We prove the first; the second follows after interchanging the two
coordinates.  Fix an enumeration of \(X\).  For \(s\in X\), the
relevant shear is the homomorphism
\[
 \psi_s:V_{d+1}\longrightarrow V_{d+2},
 \qquad (a,c)\longmapsto(a,c+sa).
\]
It is induced by conjugation with \(x_{ji}(s)\).  Notice that its
domain and codomain are different: multiplication by \(s\) raises
degree, so no invariance of a finite truncation is being asserted.
Pullback along \(\psi_s\) sends a character pair
\((\chi_1,\chi_2)\in\widehat V_{d+2}\) to
\[
 \psi_s^*(\chi_1,\chi_2)=
 \bigl(\chi_1\cdot\partial_s\chi_2,\chi_2\bigr)
\]
where \((\partial_s\chi_2)(a)=\chi_2(sa)\).  If the leading
homogeneous part of \(\chi_2\) detects a word beginning with \(s\),
then
\[
 \nu(\partial_s\chi_2)=\nu(\chi_2)-1.
\]
No equality is asserted for the other generators.  At least one such
generator exists: a word detected by the leading homogeneous part has
a first letter.  Call such a generator \emph{eligible}.  Fix an
ordering of \(X\) and assign the character to its least eligible
generator.

Here is the projection bookkeeping for~\eqref{eq:AB-descent}.  Let
\(F_s\subseteq\widehat V_{d+2}\) be the characters in \(A\cup B\)
assigned to \(s\).  Split
\[
 F_s=F_s^{\mathrm{int}}\sqcup F_s^{\mathrm{top}},
\]
where \(F_s^{\mathrm{top}}\) consists of characters whose second
coordinate becomes visible only in degree \(d+2\).  The sets
\(F_s^{\mathrm{top}}\) are disjoint and their total mass is at most
\(\partial_{2,d+2}\).  For \(\chi\in F_s^{\mathrm{int}}\), the
leading part of \(\chi_2\) survives restriction to
\(A_{\le d+1}\), and
\[
 \operatorname{res}_{d+1}(\psi_s^*\chi)\in C\cup D.
\]
Moreover, the surviving leading part recovers the least eligible
letter.  Concretely, the second character of
\(\operatorname{res}_{d+1}(\psi_s^*\chi)\) is the restriction of
\(\chi_2\).  Below the top boundary its leading homogeneous part is
unchanged, so its least eligible generator is still \(s\).  If a
character belonged to both \(R_s\) and \(R_t\), its second coordinate
would therefore have least eligible generator both \(s\) and \(t\),
forcing \(s=t\).  Thus the receiving sets
\[
 R_s=\{\operatorname{res}_{d+1}(\psi_s^*\chi):
          \chi\in F_s^{\mathrm{int}}\}
 \subseteq C\cup D
\]
are pairwise disjoint.

Let \(P_d(\Omega)=\sum_{\chi\in\Omega}P_{d,\chi}\) and put
\(g_s=x_{ji}(s)\).  Spectral covariance says that
\(\sigma(g_s)P_{d+2}(F_s^{\mathrm{int}})z\) belongs to the range of
\(P_{d+1}(R_s)\).  Apply the variation inequality to
\(z_s=\sigma(g_s)z\):
\[
 \norm{P_{d+2}(F_s^{\mathrm{int}})z}^2
 \le \norm{P_{d+1}(R_s)z}^2
      +2\norm z\,d_{ji,s}.
\]
Summing over \(s\), pairwise disjointness gives
\(\sum_s\norm{P_{d+1}(R_s)z}^2\le m_{d+1}^{C\cup D}\), so every
target component is charged at most once.  Adding the top-boundary
mass proves~\eqref{eq:AB-descent}.  The forward shear
\((a,c)\mapsto(a+sc,c)\), whose character pullback is
\((\chi_1,\chi_2\cdot\partial_s\chi_1)\), proves
~\eqref{eq:CB-descent}.

It remains to collect the coefficients.  Use
~\eqref{eq:stage-monotonicity} to move the two coarse target masses in
~\eqref{eq:AB-descent} and~\eqref{eq:CB-descent} to stage \(d+2\).
Suppressing that common stage and writing
\(\partial_r=\partial_{r,d+2}\), the resulting inequalities are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A+B&\le C+D+G_{ji}+\partial_1+2\partial_2,\\
 C+B&\le A+D+G_{ij}+2\partial_1+\partial_2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
After addition and cancellation of \(A+C\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bmass}
 B\le D+\tfrac12(G_{ji}+G_{ij})
       +\tfrac32(\partial_1+\partial_2).
\end{equation}
On the other hand,~\eqref{eq:ABCB} gives
\[
 A+B+C+D\le 3B+D+U_{ji}+U_{ij}.
\]
Substitute~\eqref{eq:Bmass}, and then use~\eqref{eq:Dmass}.  This
gives the completely collected finite-stage inequality
\begin{align}\label{eq:finite-mass}
 m_{d+2}^A+m_{d+2}^B+m_{d+2}^C+m_{d+2}^D
 \le{}&d_{ik,1}^2+d_{jk,1}^2
   +\tfrac32(G_{ji}+G_{ij})+U_{ji}+U_{ij}\notag\\
 &+\tfrac92(\partial_{1,d+2}+\partial_{2,d+2}).
\end{align}
This is the finite-stage estimate whose limit is
~\eqref{eq:plane-energy}; it also accounts for the coefficient
\(3/2\) there.

The boundary limit follows from an exact operator identity.  Let
\(Q_{r,d}\) be the orthogonal projection onto the vectors fixed by
the \(r\)-th coordinate copy of \(A_{\le d}\), and put
\(T_{r,d}=\norm{Q_{r,d}z}^2\).  Orthogonal refinement gives
\[
 T_{r,d}=\partial_{r,d+1}+T_{r,d+1}.
\]
The nonnegative sequence \(T_{r,d}\) decreases, so
\(\partial_{r,d+1}=T_{r,d}-T_{r,d+1}\to0\).  Finally, the subgroups
\(V_d\) increase to \(H_{ijk}\).  The orthogonal projections onto
their moving subspaces converge strongly to the moving projection for
\(H_{ijk}\); equivalently, the decreasing fixed spaces have
intersection \(\mathcal H^{H_{ijk}}\).  Hence the left side
of~\eqref{eq:finite-mass} converges to \(\norm{z-Pz}^2\).  Passing to
the limit proves~\eqref{eq:plane-energy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Localized plane estimate]\label{lem:localized-plane}
Let \(\sigma:\EL_3(A)\to\mathcal U(\mathcal H)\) be a unitary
representation and let \(z\in\mathcal H\).  Suppose \(B\ge0\) and
the displacement of \(z\) is at most \(B\) under
\[
 x_{ik}(1),\ x_{jk}(1),\ x_{ij}(1),\ x_{ji}(1),
 \quad x_{ij}(s),\ x_{ji}(s)\quad(s\in X).
\]
Then, for every \(g\in H_{ijk}\),
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.freeCharacteristicTwo_localizedPlaneEstimate}
\[
  \norm{\sigma(g)z-z}
  \le 2(6\abs X+6)B.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Regard the complex Hilbert space as real and the unitary operators as
orthogonal operators; norms, fixed spaces, and displacements are
unchanged.  Let \(P\) be the orthogonal projection onto
\(\mathcal H^{H_{ijk}}\), and set \(w=z-Pz\).  The adjacent root
groups \(X_{ij}\) and \(X_{ji}\) normalize \(H_{ijk}\), while
\(X_{ik},X_{jk}\le H_{ijk}\).  Orthogonal projection therefore
passes each of the displayed displacement bounds from \(z\) to
\(w\), with the same bound \(B\).
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:plane-energy} to \(w\).  Since \(Pw=0\), it
gives
\[
  \norm w^2
  \le 2B^2+(6\abs X+4)\norm w\,B.
\]
If \(B=0\), this forces \(w=0\).  If \(B>0\) and
\(\norm w\le B\), the desired bound on \(w\) is immediate.
Otherwise \(B<\norm w\), and the last inequality gives
\(\norm w\le(6\abs X+6)B\).  Finally, every \(g\in H_{ijk}\)
fixes \(Pz\), so
\(\norm{\sigma(g)z-z}\le2\norm w\).
The formal declaration obtains the same non-strict bound from its
strict-bound helper by applying it at
\(B+\varepsilon/(6\lvert X\rvert+7)\) and then letting
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Row-plane symmetry]\label{lem:row-symmetry}
Let \(\overline{\phantom a}:A\to A\) reverse every word and fix the
free generators.  The inverse-transpose map
\[
 \Theta(g)=\overline{g}^{\,-t}
\]
is an automorphism of \(\EL_3(A)\), and
\[
 \Theta(x_{ij}(a))=x_{ji}(-\overline a)
 \qquad(a\in A).
\]
In particular, \(\Theta(x_{ij}(t))=x_{ji}(t)\) for
\(t\in\{1\}\cup X\).
Consequently Lemma~\ref{lem:localized-plane} applies with the two
root directions reversed.
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.freeCharacteristicTwo_inverseTranspose_root}
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.freeCharacteristicTwo_rowEmbedding_controlRoot}
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.freeCharacteristicTwo_rowEmbedding_elementaryRoot}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Word reversal followed by transpose identifies the matrix ring with
its opposite ring.  On units, inversion identifies the opposite
group with the original group, so \(\Theta\) is an automorphism.
Since \(E_{ij}^2=0\),
\(x_{ij}(a)^{-1}=x_{ij}(-a)\).  In characteristic two the sign
vanishes, while word reversal fixes \(1\) and every \(s\in X\),
which gives the displayed formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Root quasi-cocycle estimate]\label{thm:root}
Let \(X\) be finite, put \(A=\F_2\langle X\rangle\), and let
\[
  G=\EL_4(A),\qquad
  X_{03}=\{I+aE_{03}:a\in A\}.
\]
For every unitary representation \(\pi\), every \(D\ge0\), and every
quasi-cocycle \(b:G\to\mathcal H\) of defect at most \(D\), the set
\(b(X_{03})\) is bounded.
Equivalently, the pair \((G,X_{03})\) has relative property \((TT)\),
with no invariant-vector hypothesis.
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.freeCharacteristicTwo_root_hasRelativeTT}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(N\) be the last-column subgroup generated by
\(X_{03},X_{13},X_{23}\).  Decompose
\[
  \mathcal H=\mathcal H^N\oplus(\mathcal H^N)^\perp,
  \qquad b(r)=p+w
\]
for \(r=I+aE_{03}\).  Since the coefficient ring has
characteristic two, \(r^2=1\).
First, \(\norm{b(1)}\le D\), by applying~\eqref{eq:quasicocycle}
to \(1\cdot1\).  Apply the same inequality to \(r^2\) and project
orthogonally to \(\mathcal H^N\).  Since \(r\in N\), it acts as the
identity on this subspace.  If \(q\) denotes the projection of
\(b(1)\), contractivity of the projection gives
\[
  \norm{q-p-p}\le D,
  \qquad \norm q\le D.
\]
Thus \(\norm{2p}\le2D\), and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed}
  \norm p\le D.
\end{equation}

For the moving component, let \(Q_X\subseteq\EL_3(A)\) consist of
all elementary roots with coefficient \(1\) or one of the free
generators.  Embed it on the coordinates \(\{1,2,3\}\), and also on
the coordinates \(\{0,1,2\}\) using the transpose--word-reversal
symmetry of Lemma~\ref{lem:row-symmetry}; the geometry is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:root-geometry}.  Put
\[
 M=\sum_{s\in Q_X}
 \bigl(\norm{b(\iota_{123}(s))}+\norm{b(\iota_{012}(s))}\bigr),
 \quad B=2M+2D,
\]
\[
 K=2(6\abs X+6)B.
\]
Only the following members of these two finite embedded sets are
used:
\[
 \begin{array}{c}
 x_{13}(1),\ x_{23}(1),\ x_{12}(t),\ x_{21}(t),\\
 x_{01}(1),\ x_{02}(1),\ x_{21}(t),\ x_{12}(t),
 \end{array}
 \qquad t\in\{1\}\cup X.
\]
Here the first row comes from the \(123\)-embedding and the second
from the \(012\)-embedding.  Every displayed element commutes with
\(r=x_{03}(a)\) and normalizes \(N\).  Comparing the
quasi-cocycle equations for \(sr\) and \(rs\), then projecting to
the moving subspace, gives for each displayed control element
\[
  \norm{\pi(s)w-w}
  \le \norm{\pi(s)b(r)-b(r)}
  \le 2\norm{b(s)}+2D\le B.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:localized-plane} propagates these finitely many
inequalities to the complete root planes in the two embeddings.
In particular, for every \(c\in A\),
\[
 \norm{\pi(x_{13}(c))w-w},\quad
 \norm{\pi(x_{23}(c))w-w},\quad
 \norm{\pi(x_{01}(c))w-w}
 \le K.
\]
The Steinberg
relation
\[
  [\,I+cE_{01},\,I+E_{13}\,]=I+cE_{03}
\]
and two applications of the product displacement inequality give
\[
 \norm{\pi([g,h])w-w}
 \le2\norm{\pi(g)w-w}+2\norm{\pi(h)w-w}.
\]
With \(g=x_{01}(c)\) and \(h=x_{13}(1)\), the right side is at most
\(4K\).  Every element of \(N\) is a product of
one element from each of \(X_{03},X_{13},X_{23}\), so its
displacement of \(w\) is at most \(6K\).  Since
\(w\perp\mathcal H^N\), this already bounds \(w\).  Indeed, the
closed convex hull of the \(N\)-orbit of \(w\) has a unique vector
of minimal norm; that vector is \(N\)-fixed.  The convex hull stays
inside \((\mathcal H^N)^\perp\), so its minimal vector is zero.
On the other hand, the whole orbit, and hence its closed convex
hull, lies in the closed ball of radius \(6K\) about \(w\).
Consequently \(\norm w\le6K\).  Together with~\eqref{eq:fixed}, this gives
\[
  \norm{b(I+aE_{03})}
  \le
  D+12(6\abs X+6)(2M+2D),
\]
uniformly in \(a\in A\).
\end{proof}

\section{Coordinate-block factorization}\label{sec:factorization}

We now prove the main algebraic input.  Write
\[
  e_{ij}(a)=I+aE_{ij}\qquad(i\ne j)
\]
for an elementary transvection.

\begin{lemma}[Three-move diagonal pivot]\label{lem:pivot}
Let \(R\) satisfy~\eqref{eq:sandwich}, let \(A\in\GL_n(R)\), and
fix distinct coordinates \(i,j\).  At most three elementary row and
column operations in total produce a matrix \(B\) satisfying
\[
  B_{jj}=1.
\]
More precisely, one left factor and two right factors suffice.
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.threeMove_diagonalPivot}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We give the calculation, since it is the source of the uniform
constant.  Choose two distinct indices \(i,j\).  Put \(F_0=I\) if
\(A_{ii}\ne0\).  If \(A_{ii}=0\), invertibility of \(A\) gives \(k\)
with \(A_{ik}\ne0\); put \(F_0=e_{ki}(1)\).  In either case set
\(A^{(0)}=AF_0\) and \(a=A^{(0)}_{ii}\).  Then \(a\ne0\), and \(F_0\)
is a product of at most one elementary column operation.

Write \(b=A^{(0)}_{ij}\), and choose \(p,q\in R\) with \(paq=1\).  Put
\[
  \rho=q(1-pb),\qquad
  F_1=e_{ij}(\rho),\qquad A_1=A^{(0)}F_1,\qquad b_1=(A_1)_{ij}.
\]
Then
\[
  b_1=a\rho+b,\qquad
  pb_1=pa\,q(1-pb)+pb=1.
\]
Set
\[
  d_1=(A_1)_{jj},\qquad
  w=(1-d_1)p,\qquad
  E_1=e_{ji}(w),\qquad A_2=E_1A_1.
\]
The \((j,j)\)-entry is now
\[
  (A_2)_{jj}=d_1+wb_1
  =d_1+(1-d_1)pb_1=1.
\]
Thus \(B=A_2=E_1A(F_0F_1)\) has \(B_{jj}=1\).  This uses one left
factor and at most two right factors.  The calculation applies to
arbitrary rings satisfying~\eqref{eq:sandwich}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Choose \(i\ne j\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:pivot} gives elementary products
\(E,F\), represented by lists \(\ell,r\), such that
\[
  B=EAF,\qquad B_{jj}=1,
\]
with
\[
  \abs\ell\le1,\qquad \abs r\le2.
\]

For every \(k\ne j\), right multiply by
\[
  e_{jk}(-B_{jk}).
\]
These transvections have a common source column and distinct target
columns, so each previously cleared entry stays zero.  For the factor
indexed by \(k\), the entry being cleared is
\[
 (Be_{jk}(-B_{jk}))_{jk}
 =B_{jk}+B_{jj}(-B_{jk})=0.
\]
If \(T\) is
their product and \(C=BT\), then
\[
  C_{jc}=\begin{cases}1,&c=j,\\0,&c\ne j.\end{cases}
\]
The list \(t\) of right-clearing factors has length exactly \(n-1\).

For every \(k\ne j\), left multiply by
\[
  e_{kj}(-C_{kj}).
\]
Again the operations have distinct target rows and preserve the
previously cleared entries.
If \(S\) is their product and \(D=SC\), then
\[
  D_{rc}=
  \begin{cases}
    1,&r=c=j,\\
    0,&r=j,\ c\ne j,\\
    0,&r\ne j,\ c=j,\\
    C_{rc},&r,c\ne j.
  \end{cases}
\]
Hence \(D\in\GL_{n-1}^{(j)}(R)\).  The list \(s\) of
left-clearing factors also has length exactly \(n-1\).

Solving \(D=SEAF T\) for \(A\) gives
\[
  A=E^{-1}S^{-1}DT^{-1}F^{-1}.
\]
At the word level, inverse products are represented by reversing
the factor lists and inverting every transvection.  The inverse of
\(e_{ab}(z)\) is \(e_{ab}(-z)\), so all these factors remain
elementary.  The resulting word has the exact form
\[
  \ell^{-1}_{\mathrm{rev}}\,
  s^{-1}_{\mathrm{rev}}\,
  [D]\,
  t^{-1}_{\mathrm{rev}}\,
  r^{-1}_{\mathrm{rev}},
\]
and therefore length at most
\[
  1+(n-1)+1+(n-1)+2=2n+2.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The fixed coordinate \(j\) is chosen before \(A\).  Allowing the
coordinate block to depend on \(A\) gives a weaker width statement.
Because there are only finitely many coordinate blocks, that weaker
form could still be used after permutation transport; the fixed form
keeps one relative subgroup throughout and is the sharper reusable
statement.
\end{remark}

\section{From relative control to global rigidity}\label{sec:globalization}

Let \(R\) be a finite-type \(\F_2\)-algebra.  Choose a finite set
\(X\) and a surjection
\[
  \F_2\langle X\rangle\twoheadrightarrow R.
\]
Elementary matrices are functorial in the coefficient ring, so the
root estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:root} descends to \(R\).  Coordinate
permutations then give the same estimate for every root subgroup in
rank four.

Two larger pieces are used in the assembly: a last-column subgroup
and a last-row subgroup.  The quasi-cocycle is bounded on both.
The stabilized rank-three coordinate group normalizes these pieces.
Kazhdan control for the rank-three group converts boundedness on the
normalized root system into boundedness on the coordinate group,
provided the ambient representation has no invariant vectors.  This
is the only place where the quotient by the trivial representation
enters.

This is the normalized-set step in Mimura's relative-to-global
mechanism~\cite[Theorem~5.1]{MimuraTT}; the following Kazhdan-pair
form records the quantitative estimate used here.  For a subset
\(S\subseteq G\), say that quasi-cocycles are \emph{bounded on
\(S\)} if every quasi-cocycle into a unitary representation without
invariant vectors has bounded restriction to \(S\).

\begin{lemma}[Kazhdan globalization on a normalized set]
\label{lem:kazhdan-globalization}
Let \(G\) have property~\((T)\), let \(S\subseteq G\) generate
\(G\), and let \(H\le G\).  Suppose
\[
  h^{-1}Sh\subseteq S\qquad(h\in H)
\]
and quasi-cocycles are bounded on \(S\).  Then \((G,H)\) has
relative property \((TT)/T\).
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.kazhdan_normalizedSet_globalization}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a unitary representation \(\pi\) of \(G\) without nonzero
invariant vectors and a quasi-cocycle \(b\) of defect at most \(D\).
Choose a Kazhdan pair \((K,\varepsilon)\), and choose once and for
all an \(S\cup S^{-1}\)-word for each \(k\in K\).  Let \(L\) be
the sum of their lengths, so \(L\) bounds each chosen length.  If
\(\norm{b(s)}\le C\) for \(s\in S\), where \(C\) is the bound for
this fixed quasi-cocycle,
comparison of the quasi-cocycle identities for \(sh\) and
\(h(h^{-1}sh)\) gives
\[
  \norm{\pi(s)b(h)-b(h)}\le 2C+2D
  \qquad(s\in S,\ h\in H).
\]
For an inverse letter \(s^{-1}\), apply this estimate to \(s\in S\).
The
identity
\[
  \norm{\pi(s^{-1})v-v}=\norm{\pi(s)v-v}
\]
holds for every vector \(v\), so with \(v=b(h)\) the constant remains
\(2C+2D\).  No bound on \(b(s^{-1})\) is used.
The displacement estimate for a product of unitaries therefore gives
\[
  \norm{\pi(k)b(h)-b(h)}
  \le L(2C+2D)\qquad(k\in K).
\]
Since \(\pi\) has no invariant vectors, the Kazhdan inequality
supplies some \(k\in K\) with
\(
  \varepsilon\norm{b(h)}
  \le\norm{\pi(k)b(h)-b(h)}.
\)
Thus \(\norm{b(h)}\le L(2C+2D)/\varepsilon\), uniformly in
\(h\in H\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Leavitt rank equivalence]
\label{lem:rank-equivalence}
If \(R\) contains a binary Leavitt family, then for all
\(m,n\ge2\) there is a group isomorphism
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.leavitt_elementaryRankEquivalence}
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.elementaryBlockFlattening}
\[
  \EL_m(R)\cong\EL_n(R).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every \(q\ge2\), the complete left-comb binary prefix code
\[
 \{\,1^r0:0\le r\le q-2\,\}\cup\{1^{q-1}\}
\]
has \(q\) words.  Its Leavitt matrix units give a ring isomorphism
\(M_q(R)\cong R\).  Functoriality in the
coefficient ring and block flattening give
\[
 \EL_m(R)
 \cong \EL_m(M_n(R))
 \cong \EL_{mn}(R)
 \cong \EL_n(M_m(R))
 \cong \EL_n(R).
\]
The middle identifications are bidirectional on elementary groups.
A block transvection decomposes into commuting scalar
transvections after flattening.  Conversely, a scalar transvection
whose indices lie in different outer blocks is already a block
transvection.  If both indices lie in one outer block, choose a
second outer block and use the Steinberg identity
\[
 e_{(i,k),(i,l)}(a)
 =\bigl[e_{(i,k),(j,k)}(a),e_{(j,k),(i,l)}(1)\bigr].
\]
Thus block flattening identifies the full elementary subgroups in
both directions, and the displayed chain restricts to the claimed
group isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary equals general linear]\label{lem:gl-el}
Assume that \(R\) has a binary Leavitt family, satisfies~\eqref{eq:sandwich},
and satisfies
\(\diag(u,1)\in\EL_2(R)\) for every \(u\in R^\times\).  Then
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.elementaryGroup_eq_generalLinear}
\[
  \EL_n(R)=\GL_n(R)\qquad(n\ge2).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the pivot and the row and column clearing in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with \(n=2\).  They reduce an arbitrary element
of \(\GL_2(R)\), by elementary operations, to the remaining
one-by-one coordinate block \(\diag(u,1)\) for some
\(u\in R^\times\).  The diagonal hypothesis
therefore gives \(\GL_2(R)=\EL_2(R)\).

The binary Leavitt family gives a coefficient isomorphism
\(M_n(R)\cong R\) for every \(n\ge2\).  Transporting the two
ring-theoretic hypotheses across this isomorphism gives the same
rank-two equality over \(M_n(R)\).  The bidirectional block
flattening proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-equivalence} then gives
\[
  \EL_2(M_n(R))=\GL_2(M_n(R))
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \EL_{2n}(R)=\GL_{2n}(R).
\]
Reindexing \(\operatorname{Fin}(2n)\) by
\(\operatorname{Fin}(n)\times\operatorname{Fin}(2)\), rather than
\(\operatorname{Fin}(2)\times\operatorname{Fin}(n)\), gives the same
bidirectional flattening in the form
\[
  \EL_n(M_2(R))=\GL_n(M_2(R)).
\]
Transport through \(M_2(R)\cong R\) now gives
\(\EL_n(R)=\GL_n(R)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rank-four assembly]\label{prop:rank-four}
Let \(X\) be finite and let
\(f:\F_2\langle X\rangle\twoheadrightarrow R\) be a surjective ring
homomorphism.  Suppose that \(R\) is nontrivial, contains a binary
Leavitt family, satisfies~\eqref{eq:sandwich}, and satisfies
\(\diag(u,1)\in\EL_2(R)\) for every \(u\in R^\times\).  Then
\(\EL_4(R)\) has property \((TT)/T\).
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.finiteFreeQuotient_rankFour_hasTTmodT}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:root} descends through \(f\) and gives relative
\((TT)/T\) for \(X_{03}(R)\).  Coordinate permutations give it for
the six roots
\[
  X_{03},X_{13},X_{23},X_{30},X_{31},X_{32}.
\]

The assembly follows the chain
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
   \text{six root bounds}
   \Longrightarrow \text{last row and last column are bounded}\\
   \Longrightarrow \text{the rank-three core is bounded}
   \Longrightarrow \text{coordinate blocks are bounded}\\
   \overset{\text{Theorem A}}{\Longrightarrow}
   \text{the whole rank-four group is bounded}.
 \end{gathered}}
\]
Here are the details.  Put
\[
  U^+=X_{03}X_{13}X_{23},
  \qquad U^-=X_{30}X_{31}X_{32}.
\]
The three roots in each product commute, so the quasi-cocycle
identity makes \(b\) bounded on \(U^+\) and on \(U^-\).  The set
\(S=U^+\cup U^-\) generates \(\EL_4(R)\): roots meeting the last
coordinate lie in \(S\), and every remaining root is a Steinberg
commutator of one last-column and one last-row root.

For the Kazhdan input, first apply the
Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain theorem to
\(\EL_3(\F_2\langle X\rangle)\)~\cite{ErshovJaikin}.  Property~\((T)\)
descends along the surjection induced by \(f\), so \(\EL_3(R)\) has
property~\((T)\).

The formal proof establishes the same input internally rather than
postulating the cited theorem.  The declaration
\nolinkurl{A2MagicHilbert.elementary_exists_rootSet_isKazhdan}
provides a Kazhdan constant \(\kappa\) for the union of the six
rank-three root subgroups.  The plane estimate proves that the finite
unit-and-generator control set controls that union with factor
\(C=12\lvert X\rvert+13\).  The standard controlled-subset argument
then makes this finite set a Kazhdan pair with constant
\(\kappa/(2C)\).  The relevant declaration is
\begin{center}
\nolinkurl{FreeElementaryPropertyT.controlSet_isKazhdanPair}.
\end{center}
This is the additional step between Lemma~\ref{lem:localized-plane} and a
rank-three spectral gap.  The resulting pair descends through \(f\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-equivalence} transports property
\((T)\) to \(\EL_4(R)\).  The stabilized rank-three subgroup
\(H\) is the upper-left copy of \(\EL_3(R)\), fixing the last basis
vector and covector.  Conjugation acts on a last-column vector by the
upper-left elementary matrix and on a last-row vector by its inverse.
Thus \(H\) normalizes both \(U^+\) and \(U^-\), and hence \(S\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:kazhdan-globalization} now proves that
\((\EL_4(R),H)\) has relative property \((TT)/T\).  Notice that the
no-invariant-vectors hypothesis is used precisely in the Kazhdan
inequality inside that lemma.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:gl-el}, the elementary groups in ranks three and
four are the full general linear groups.  Under these identifications,
the upper-left stabilized subgroup is
\[
 \{\diag(A,1):A\in\GL_3(R)\}=\GL_3^{(3)}(R),
\]
the coordinate block fixing the last basis vector and covector.
Finally, coordinate permutations carry this
relative statement to \(\GL_3^{(j)}(R)\) for every
\(j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\).

Each elementary transvection lies in a coordinate block avoiding its
two indices.  Hence the union of one fixed coordinate block and all
root subgroups is bounded for the quasi-cocycle.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A}
writes every element of \(\GL_4(R)\) as a product of at most \(10\)
elements of that union.  Lemma~\ref{lem:products} below, or directly
the corresponding bounded-product lemma, makes the quasi-cocycle
bounded on \(\GL_4(R)\).  Transporting through
\(\EL_4(R)=\GL_4(R)\) proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

The passage from a bounded generating subset to the whole group
uses the following elementary estimate.

\begin{lemma}[Products of controlled pieces]\label{lem:products}
Let \(b\) be a quasi-cocycle of defect at most \(D\), and suppose
\(\norm{b(g_i)}\le C\) for a list \((g_1,\ldots,g_m)\).  Then
\leanverified{PropertyTT/PaperStatements}{PropertyTTPaper.quasiCocycle_list_product_bound}
\[
  \norm{b(g_1\cdots g_m)}
  \le mC+(m+1)D.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induct on the list.  The empty-list case is
\(\norm{b(1)}\le D\).  For a nonempty list, apply~\eqref{eq:quasicocycle}
to the head and the product of the tail, use
that the representation is isometric, and invoke the inductive
bound.  The displayed estimate is a slightly loose uniform form that
also covers the empty list.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
Finite type supplies a finite set \(X\) and a surjection
\(\F_2\langle X\rangle\twoheadrightarrow R\), so
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-four} gives the result in rank four.  For
arbitrary \(n\ge2\), Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-equivalence} supplies an
explicit group isomorphism \(\EL_n(R)\cong\EL_4(R)\).
Property \((TT)/T\) is invariant under group isomorphism, so the
rank-four conclusion transports to rank \(n\).
\end{proof}

\section{The binary Leavitt specialization}\label{sec:leavitt}

Let \(L\) be the binary Leavitt algebra~\cite{Leavitt},
\[
  L=L_{\F_2}(1,2)
  =\F_2\langle x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1\rangle/
  (y_i x_j-\delta_{ij},\,x_0y_0+x_1y_1-1).
\]
The defining generators themselves form a binary Leavitt family,
and the presentation makes \(L\) a finite-type \(\F_2\)-algebra.

For a binary word \(\alpha=i_1\cdots i_r\), write
\(x_\alpha=x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}\) and
\(y_\alpha=y_{i_r}\cdots y_{i_1}\).  Then
\(y_\alpha x_\beta\) is determined by prefix comparison, and at every
depth \(r\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinder-partition}
 \sum_{\abs\delta=r}x_\delta y_\delta=1.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Single-sandwich division for \(L_k(1,2)\)]
\label{lem:leavitt-sandwich}
For every field \(k\) and every nonzero \(z\in L_k(1,2)\), there are
\(a,b\in L_k(1,2)\) such that \(azb=1\).
\leanverified{Leavitt/LeavittSimplicity}{BinaryLeavitt.exists_mul_mul_eq_one}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(z\) as a finite sum
\(\sum_i c_i x_{\alpha_i}y_{\beta_i}\), and let
\(M=\max_i\abs{\beta_i}\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:cylinder-partition}
shows that \(zx_\delta\ne0\) for some word \(\delta\) of length
\(M\); otherwise
\(z=\sum_{\abs\delta=M}zx_\delta y_\delta=0\).
Prefix cancellation expresses \(zx_\delta\) as a finite linear
combination of pure \(x\)-words.  After collecting equal words, write
\[
 zx_\delta=\sum_{\gamma\in G}c_\gamma x_\gamma,
 \qquad c_\gamma\ne0,
\]
and choose \(\gamma_0\in G\) of minimal length.  Multiplication on the
left by \(y_{\gamma_0}\) turns the \(\gamma_0\)-term into
\(c_{\gamma_0}\); every other surviving term is a proper
\(x_\varepsilon\), with \(1\le\abs\varepsilon\le m\) for
\(m=\max_{\gamma\in G}\abs\gamma\).

Let \(w=0^m1\).  For every such \(\varepsilon\), the words \(w\)
and \(\varepsilon w\) are incomparable.  Indeed,
\(\varepsilon w\) is longer than \(w\).  If \(w\) were its prefix,
the first \(m\) letters of \(\varepsilon w\) would force
\(\varepsilon=0^{\lvert\varepsilon\rvert}\), but in position \(m+1\)
the word \(w\) has \(1\) and \(\varepsilon w\) still has \(0\).
Hence
\(y_w{}x_\varepsilon x_w=0\).  It follows that
\[
 y_w{}y_{\gamma_0}zx_\delta x_w=c_{\gamma_0}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \bigl(c_{\gamma_0}^{-1}y_w{}y_{\gamma_0}\bigr)
 z\bigl(x_\delta x_w\bigr)=1.
\]
\end{proof}

This direct argument proves the single-sandwich form of pure
infiniteness used below.
Alternatively, the graph criterion of
Abrams--Aranda Pino gives pure infiniteness and simplicity, and
Ara--Goodearl--Pardo's characterization gives the same conclusion
~\cite[Theorem~11]{AbramsArandaPino}
~\cite[Theorem~1.6]{AraGoodearlPardo}.
It remains to establish the unstable diagonal condition.

\begin{proposition}[Elementary matrices over the binary Leavitt algebra]
\label{prop:diag}
For every field \(k\), write \(L_k=L_k(1,2)\).  For every
\(u\in L_k^\times\),
\[
  \diag(u,1)\in\EL_2(L_k).
\]
More generally,
\leanverified{KOne/PaperStatements}{KOnePaper.diagUnit_mem_elementary}
\leanverified{KOne/PaperStatements}{KOnePaper.elementaryGroup_two_eq_top}
\leanverified{KOne/AllRanksElementaryCore}{BinaryLeavitt.elementaryGroup_eq_top}
\[
  \GL_n(L_k)=\EL_n(L_k)\qquad(n\ge2).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Leavitt-path-algebra exact sequence of
Ara--Brustenga--Corti\~nas applies because \(k\) is regular
supercoherent.  The binary rose has adjacency matrix \((2)\), so the
relevant segment is
\[
 K_1(k)\xrightarrow{-1}K_1(k)\longrightarrow K_1(L_k)
 \longrightarrow K_0(k)\xrightarrow{-1}K_0(k).
\]
This is the specialization of
~\cite[Theorem~7.6]{AraBrustengaCortinas}.
The two outer maps are automorphisms: under
\(K_1(k)=k^\times\) the first is inversion, and under
\(K_0(k)=\mathbb Z\) the second is multiplication by \(-1\).
Exactness therefore gives
\[
 K_1(L_k)=0.
\]
The ring \(L_k\) is purely infinite simple.  Ara--Goodearl--Pardo
show that it is a GE-ring and that the canonical map induces
\[
 (L_k^\times)_{\mathrm{ab}}\cong K_1(L_k).
\]
See~\cite[Theorem~2.4 and its proof]{AraGoodearlPardo}.
Thus \(L_k^\times\) is perfect.

It remains only to recall the unstable elementary calculation.
For a unit \(c\), put
\[
 w(c)=e_{12}(c)e_{21}(-c^{-1})e_{12}(c).
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 w(c)w(-1)=\diag(c,c^{-1})\in\EL_2(L_k).
\]
If \([a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}\), then
\[
 \diag([a,b],1)
 =\diag(a,a^{-1})\diag(b,b^{-1})
  \diag(a^{-1}b^{-1},ba)\in\EL_2(L_k).
\]
Since every \(u\in L_k^\times\) is a product of commutators, this
proves \(\diag(u,1)\in\EL_2(L_k)\).  Embedding the calculation in
larger matrices shows that every invertible diagonal matrix is
elementary.  The GE property now gives
\(\GL_n(L_k)=\EL_n(L_k)\) for all \(n\ge2\).  This is also the argument
recorded explicitly by Khanh--Thanh
~\cite[Lemma~4.1 and Proposition~4.2]{KhanhThanh}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Constructive formal route]\label{rem:constructive-diagonal}
The formal companion also proves Proposition~\ref{prop:diag} by a
constructive route, without formalizing algebraic \(K\)-theory.  Its
reduction gives
every unit a representative in the degree window \([-1,1]\), expands
that representative relative to complete binary prefix codes into
the pencil
\[
 a_0y_0+a_1y_1+c+b_0x_0+b_1x_1,
\]
and applies the scalar-matrix dichotomy to the stacked pair
\(\binom{B_0}{B_1}\).  If the row and column codes have cardinalities
\(r\) and \(c\), then \(B_0,B_1\in M_{r\times c}(k)\), so this stack
is the linear map \(k^c\to k^{2r}\).  It either has a nonzero kernel,
or it is injective and hence has a left inverse.  A kernel vector
produces a \(B\)-free column;
splitting its code word increases the column-code cardinality by one.
A left inverse instead forces all entries of the inverse pencil into
strictly negative degree.  The two branches terminate respectively
at the deep-column and inverse deep-row nonpositive-window lemmas.
The decreasing counter is \(2^{r+1}-\lvert Q\rvert\), with the row
code fixed at cardinality \(2^r\).  This is the exact route through
\nolinkurl{exists_narrow_representative},
\nolinkurl{exists_pencil_form}, \nolinkurl{pencil_free_exit},
\nolinkurl{pencil_full_exit}, and \nolinkurl{pencil_unit_mem_pow}
listed in the formal-companion reading guide.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
The binary Leavitt algebra is a nontrivial finite-type
\(\F_2\)-algebra, its canonical generators form a binary Leavitt
family, Lemma~\ref{lem:leavitt-sandwich} supplies
~\eqref{eq:sandwich}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:diag} supplies the elementary diagonal
condition.  Theorem~\ref{thm:B} gives property \((TT)/T\) for
\(\EL_n(L)\) in every rank \(n\ge2\), while
Lemma~\ref{lem:gl-el} identifies it with \(\GL_n(L)\).

For \(n=1\), take the one-word code consisting of the empty word.
For each \(n\ge2\), the left-comb prefix code
\[
 C_n=\{\,1^r0:0\le r\le n-2\,\}\cup\{1^{n-1}\}
\]
is a complete binary prefix code with \(n\) words.  Its Leavitt
matrix units give a ring isomorphism \(M_n(L)\cong L\), hence a group
isomorphism \(\GL_n(L)\cong L^\times\).  For \(n\ge2\) this yields
\(\EL_n(L)\cong L^\times\).  Transporting the rank-two conclusion
across this isomorphism gives property \((TT)/T\) for \(L^\times\).
\end{proof}

\section{Further directions}\label{sec:directions}

The characteristic-two hypothesis enters most visibly
in~\eqref{eq:fixed}, where every root element has order two.  Over a
field of characteristic \(p\), the corresponding root has order
\(p\), and iterating the quasi-cocycle identity gives
\[
  b(1)\approx
  b(r)+\pi(r)b(r)+\cdots+\pi(r)^{p-1}b(r).
\]
On the root-fixed space this is \(p\) times the fixed projection of
\(b(r)\), with error at most \((p-1)D\).  Thus the fixed-space
argument has a plausible finite-characteristic analogue.  Extending
the theorem requires uniform replacements for the
characteristic-two rank-three control and the sign-free coordinate
symmetries.

The algebraic factorization theorem suggests a separate question.
For which natural classes of simple or purely infinite rings does
the single-sandwich condition hold, and when can the coordinate
block in Theorem~\ref{thm:A} itself be controlled by elementary
subgroups?  A satisfactory answer would extend the present
synthesis from the Leavitt setting to a broader rigidity criterion.
The binary-family hypothesis is restrictive: it gives an isomorphism
of right modules \(R\cong R^2\), hence \([R]=2[R]\) in \(K_0(R)\).
For \(L_k(1,d)\), the class of the unit generates
\(K_0\cong\mathbb Z/(d-1)\)~\cite{AraBrustengaCortinas}, so no binary Leavitt family can exist
when \(d>2\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:B} therefore does not extend to the
higher-arity Leavitt algebras without a different self-similarity
input.
