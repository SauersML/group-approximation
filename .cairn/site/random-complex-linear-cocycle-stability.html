Statement

Show that in a middle triangle-density range, random 2-dimensional Linial--Meshulam complexes typically have a linear cocycle stability rate in permutations.

The cocycle stability rate measures how far a cochain with small coboundary must be from an actual cocycle. "Linear" is the strongest possible rate, and it is the hypothesis that Chapman--Peled convert into a nonsofic hyperbolic group.

Explicitly conjectural: this is a probabilistic estimate that Chapman--Peled propose and do not prove. Their analogy is the known non-local-testability of Sipser--Spielman expander codes, which is what makes a linear rate plausible in the middle density regime rather than a weaker one.

Source: Chapman--Peled, Cocycle stability in permutations of random simplicial complexes, arXiv:2509.21566 (2025-09-25, rev. 2025-12-04).
