Powers–Størmer into the Hilbert–Schmidt representation, then property (T)

Direct establishment; the complete argument is Proposition 3 of the artifact. requires: [] is a proof commitment, not a placeholder.

Four standard ingredients, none specific to this problem: weak-\ density of normal states in the state space of B(H); Day's convexity trick, upgrading weak-\ almost invariance to ‖·‖₁ almost invariance; the Powers–Størmer inequality ‖a^{1/2} − b^{1/2}‖₂² ≤ ‖a − b‖₁, which converts almost invariant density operators into almost invariant unit vectors of Ad π on HS(H) ≅ H ⊗ H̄; and the spectral theorem for trace-class operators, which makes a nonzero spectral projection of T^*T finite rank.

Property (T) is used once, in its defining form — almost invariant vectors give a nonzero invariant vector — applied to the Hilbert–Schmidt representation rather than to π itself. That is the whole trick, and it is why the conclusion is finite-dimensionality rather than the existence of an invariant vector in π.
