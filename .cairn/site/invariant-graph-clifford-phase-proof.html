Statement

Direct proof

1. Free-lamp normal form. Let L=ker(H ->> G) for the retraction killing k (which exists because H = G *_Gamma (Gamma x C_2) maps onto G *_Gamma Gamma = G). Then L semidirect G = H, since G <= H splits the retraction and L cap G = 1. Act on the Bass--Serre tree of the amalgam. L is normal, so L cap g G g^(-1)=1 and L cap g Gamma g^(-1)=1 for all g, while L cap (Gamma x C_2) = <k>. Thus L acts on the tree with trivial edge stabilizers, trivial stabilizers at the G-type vertices, and stabilizers of order two at the (Gamma x C_2)-type vertices. Since H=LG, the quotient graph L\T has one G-type vertex, |G/Gamma| vertices of the other type and |G/Gamma| edges: a star, hence a tree. Bass--Serre therefore gives L = *_X C_2 with the factors indexed by X=G/Gamma, generated by k_(g Gamma)=g k g^(-1) (well defined because k commutes with Gamma).

With the convention [a,b]=aba^(-1)b^(-1),

r=[k,h]=k_o (h k h^(-1))^(-1)=k_o k_y,
k_o r k_o^(-1)=k_o(k_o k_y)k_o=k_y k_o=r^(-1),
x=[k_o,r]=(k_o r k_o^(-1)) r^(-1)=r^(-2).

In a free product of copies of C_2 the product of two distinct generators has infinite order, so x!=1 and x has infinite order. (The presentation identity is Lean-checked as commutator_lamp_lamp_mul_lamp.)

2. The invariant-graph quotients. Fix S and b_S. G-invariance of S makes every g in G an automorphism of the presentation of Vtilde_S, so Wtilde_S=Vtilde_S semidirect G is defined. The normal forms z^e prod_(u in F) c_u over finite ordered F are pairwise distinct, because the tracial representation of part 3 realizes them as linearly independent operators; hence Vtilde_S is a central extension of V by <z> with commutator pairing exactly b_S.

The assignment k |-> c_o, g |-> g respects k^2=1 and, because Gamma fixes o, respects [k,Gamma]=1; so it defines a homomorphism from H, surjective whenever S is nonempty (the images generate all c_u by transitivity and then z as one edge commutator). Its lamp part is the map k_u |-> c_u. Then r |-> c_o c_y and, all commutators being central,

x |-> [c_o,c_o c_y]=[c_o,c_y]=z^(b_S(o,y)).

For nonsoficity: x in Rad_sof(H) by the established Kun--Thom phase claim, its image is nontrivial when {o,y} in S, and by the established soficization statement the image of the radical lies in Rad_sof(Wtilde_S), which is therefore nontrivial, so Wtilde_S is not sofic.

3. Tracial model. For a finite F <= X the algebra generated by {C_u}_(u in F) is the twisted group algebra of F_2^F at b_S|_F, of dimension 2^|F|; A_S is the trace-closure of their union, hence approximately finite dimensional, and the monomials C_F=prod_(u in F)C_u form an orthonormal basis of L^2(A_S,tau). Permutations of X preserving S preserve the relations and the trace, so M_S=A_S rtimes G is a finite tracial crossed product. Vtilde_S -> U(A_S) is injective (distinct monomials are orthogonal, and z |-> -1 != 1) and remains injective after adjoining G, since Fourier coefficients in the crossed product separate different group coordinates. Finally x |-> (-1)^(b_S(o,y)), which is -1 on the marked edge.

4. Centres, factors and the monolith. C_F is central in A_S iff b_S(sum_F e_u, e_w)=0 for all w, i.e. iff sum_F e_u in rad b_S; so Z(A_S)=L(rad b_S) and likewise Z(Vtilde_S) is the preimage of rad b_S. Hence factoriality, Z(Vtilde_S)=<z> and nondegeneracy of b_S are the same condition, and in the nondegenerate case A_S is an approximately finite dimensional II_1 factor with separable predual, hence the hyperfinite one.

Suppose b_S nondegenerate and let N <| Wtilde_S with z notin N. If 1!=n in N cap Vtilde_S, write n=z^e c_F; choosing w with b_S(sum_F e_u,e_w)=1 gives [n,c_w]=z in N, a contradiction. So N cap Vtilde_S=1, whence [N,Vtilde_S] <= N cap Vtilde_S=1 (both are normal) and N centralizes Vtilde_S. An element c_v g centralizing all c_w forces g w=w for every w and v in rad b_S=0; so N injects into the kernel of G acting on X. If that action is faithful, N=1. Thus every nontrivial normal subgroup of Wtilde_S contains z, i.e. <z> is the monolith, and Wtilde_S is minimal among quotients of H retaining x.

For the complete graph, b_S(sum_F e_u,e_w)=|F|-[w in F] mod 2; taking one w inside and one outside a finite nonempty F forces F empty, so the form is nondegenerate and A_S=Cl(X).

5. Phase rigidity. Part 1 applies verbatim with C_2 replaced by any K, giving H_K=(*_X K) semidirect G and, in any central extension E of directSum_X K with pairing b and lifts c_u of e_u,

r |-> c_o c_y^(-1),   x |-> [c_o,c_o c_y^(-1)]=[c_o,c_y^(-1)]=b(e_o,e_y)^(-1),

using centrality of commutators. G-equivariance of the extension makes b a G-invariant alternating pairing. If g in G satisfies g o=y and g y=o then

b(e_o,e_y)=b(g e_o,g e_y)=b(e_y,e_o)=b(e_o,e_y)^(-1),

so b(e_o,e_y)^2=1. Such a g exists iff g in h Gamma and gh in Gamma, i.e. iff h gamma_1 h in Gamma for some gamma_1 in Gamma, which is equivalent to Gamma h Gamma = Gamma h^(-1) Gamma. A detector needs b(e_o,e_y)!=1, so on a symmetric marked double coset the value is an element of order two.

Scope

Parts 1--5 are group theory and elementary twisted-group-algebra structure; nothing here asserts Connes embeddability of any M_S. What the classification buys is that the analytic gate may be attacked at any nondegenerate invariant graph containing the marked edge, not only at the complete one.
