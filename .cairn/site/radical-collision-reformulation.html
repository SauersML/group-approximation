Statement

Let H be countable, x in Rad_sof(H) with x!=1.

1. Automatic nonsoficity. For every homomorphism q:H->Q with q(x)!=1, the image q(H) is not sofic, and q(x) in Rad_sof(q(H))\{1}. The nonsoficity of a detecting range is never an extra thing to prove — it is free — but neither can it be avoided. 2. Equivalence with the program target. The following are equivalent.

  • There is a countable hyperlinear nonsofic group.
  • There are a countable H, an x in Rad_sof(H)\{1}, and a

    • homomorphism from H to a hyperlinear group not killing x.

So the collision criterion is a restatement of Question 3.4 at full strength; it does not reduce it to a strictly easier problem. 3. No soficity-closure certificate. Because of 1, the hyperlinearity of the detecting range can never be certified by placing it in a class built from sofic groups by operations that preserve soficity. In particular no quotient of H in which x survives is LEF, LEA, residually finite, residually amenable, residually sofic, linear over any field, sofic-by- amenable, an amalgam of sofic groups over an amenable subgroup, a free product of sofic groups, a countable direct product or increasing union of sofic groups, or a generalized wreath product of sofic groups over a sofic underlying action. Each such certificate would prove the target sofic and hence kill x. 4. What remains admissible. The one certificate not of that shape: if M is a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state, Phi:H->U(M) is a homomorphism with Phi(x)!=1, and the von Neumann subalgebra generated by Phi(H) is Connes embeddable, then Phi(H) is a countable hyperlinear group and Phi discharges the hypothesis of 2. Equivalently, in finite coordinates: normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic representations rho_n:H->U(d_n) with liminf ||rho_n(x)-1||_2 > 0.

5. Where the criterion has leverage. Its whole gain over "prove the candidate hyperlinear" is the freedom to pass to a proper quotient retaining x, i.e. the poset {N normal in H : x notin N}. Its maximal elements give quotients that are monolithic with x in the monolith. The poset is trivial — only N=1 — whenever H is just infinite, since then every nontrivial normal subgroup has finite index and every finite quotient is sofic; there the criterion says exactly "H is hyperlinear". If H is torsion-free hyperbolic, every nontrivial member of the poset is nonelementary and contains a free subgroup of rank two, so no finite or elementary collapse is available. The leverage is therefore proportional to the richness of the normal subgroup lattice below the radical, which is exactly what the lamp constructions supply and what a hyperbolic candidate does not.

The practical content of the criterion is therefore exactly one thing, and it is worth keeping: the model may be non-faithful. One matrix-visible word is enough; no faithful hyperlinear representation of any nonsofic group has to be built.
