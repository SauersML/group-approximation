Statement

Why sufficient

Complete written argument in the artifact. The established form is the extreme one: not finite index, but <<pi(S)>>^G = G, so property (T) of the normal closure is property (T) of G and no inheritance step is needed.

The one new ingredient is a substitution inside Hull's own proof of [Hul16, Corollary 7.4]. That proof picks the two infinite-order elements h_1, h_2 that generate the suitable subgroup by applying [Hul16, Corollary 5.7] to the free factor G_2. Pick them instead from a prescribed infinite normal subgroup D of G_2. The picking is licensed by

  • Lemma A: an infinite normal subgroup of a torsion-free G in AH is a

    • suitable subgroup. Proof: it is s-normal (D^g = D is infinite), so it acts non-elementarily by the published [Osi16, Lemma 7.1]; the finite-subgroup clause of Hull's Definition 1.4 is vacuous in a torsion-free group.

and nothing else in Hull's proof uses any property of h_1, h_2 beyond "infinite order, in G_2, non-commensurable, with {<h_1>, <h_2>} ,->_h G_2", all of which [Hul16, Corollary 5.7] delivers for D once D is suitable. The output is Theorem B of the artifact: a common quotient Q of G_1 and G_2, injective on prescribed finite sets, torsion-free if the inputs are, finitely presented if the inputs are, with the extra clause alpha_2(D) = Q.

Feeding G_1 = H, G_2 = E, D = <<S>>^E gives G = pi(<<S>>^E), hence G = <<pi(S)>>^G. The artifact also records defectNormal = <<pi(S)>>^G exactly (Lemma 0: [t c t^{-1}, -] generates the simple factor because pi(S) is non-abelian simple and t_1 c t_1^{-1} is a nontrivial element of it), so the defect is the whole group and K = G satisfies every hypothesis of normal-kazhdan-defect-non-mf.
