Let `A` and `B` be finitely presented torsion-free acylindrically hyperbolic
groups, let `1 != u in B`, put `D = <<u>>^B`, and let `F_A subset A` and
`F_B subset B` be finite.  Then there are a group `Q` and surjections
`q_A : A ->> Q`, `q_B : B ->> Q` with

1. `Q` acylindrically hyperbolic;
2. `Q` torsion-free;
3. `Q` finitely presented;
4. **`Q` generated by two elements**;
5. `q_A` injective on `F_A` and `q_B` injective on `F_B`;
6. `q_B(D) = Q`, equivalently `<<q_B(u)>>^Q = Q`.

Two things are new relative to `common-quotient-onto-normal-subgroup`.

**The reservoir may be a single element.**  Nothing is asked of `u` beyond
being nontrivial.  Torsion-freeness makes `u` of infinite order, so `D` is
infinite; normality makes it s-normal; Osin's s-normality lemma makes it act
non-elementarily; and torsion-freeness kills Hull's finite-radical clause.  So
the normal closure of one arbitrary nonzero element is automatically a suitable
reservoir, and no distinguished subgroup — simple, normal, or otherwise — has
to be identified first.

**Two-generation comes from the second stage, not from the partner.**  Clause 4
holds even when `A` is not two-generated, because the second application of
Hull's Theorem 7.1 is run through the two-generated suitable subgroup
`<gamma(f_1), gamma(f_2)>` rather than through the whole image `gamma(A)`.
That is the clause every routing argument in this program has been quietly
consuming; before this node it was stated only for the Fournier--Facio instance.

The reusable sentence is: *in a finitely presented torsion-free acylindrically
hyperbolic group, the normal closure of one nonzero element can be made to fill
a two-generated acylindrically hyperbolic common quotient.*

**Trust boundary.**  Paper-level, not formalized, and deliberately so: the
repository's formalization boundary for this machinery is the routing datum,
not Hull's proof.  A formalizable substitute with no acylindrical hyperbolicity
in it is tracked separately as `bespoke-routing-lemma`, which this claim does
not discharge.
