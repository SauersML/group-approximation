**Source facts** (KMS, Bull. Math. Sci. 7 (2017), read 2026-09-13 from the PDF text;
superscripts and primes are partly lost in extraction, and the readings below
follow the surrounding definitions):
- **(S1) Generators and (G1).** p. 334: `L_2 = {a_i, a'_i, ã_i, ã'_i}`, and
  (G1) says "H2 is an Abelian group", where `H_2 = <L_2>`.
- **(S2) BR-conjoints.** Lemma 4.1(2), p. 334: "For every a ∈ F and every x ∈ X we
  have x f(a) = x a' for some monic polynomial f of a which has at least two terms
  (everywhere below f (t) = t − 1 and hence x f(a) = a −1 xax −1 = [a, x −1 ])".
- **(S3) The relation used.** Remark 4.2, p. 335: "the letters {a'_i | i ∈ I} are
  BR-conjoints of {a_i | i ∈ I} with respect to the set of all x(u)'s where u does
  not contain letters A_i, i ∈ I". This is (G5) b) together with (G1) and (G7).
- **(S4) The derived operation.** (G8), p. 335: `f ∗ a_i` is a product of conjugates
  of `f^(±1)` by `a_i` and `a'_i`.
- **(S5) Distinctness.** Theorem 4.3(c), pp. 335--336: the equality
  `x(q_i) ∗ a_1^(m_1) ∗ ... ∗ a_k^(m_k) ∗ A_1^(α_1) ∗ ... ∗ A_k^(α_k) = x(q_j) ∗ a_1^(n_1) ∗ ...`
  "is true in G(MM k ) if and only if the equality qi a1m1 . . . akmk Aα1 1 . . . Aαk k
  = q j a1n1 . . . aknk A1β1 . . . Akβk is true in the semigroup Ŝ (i.e., these words
  coincide ...)".
- **(S6) The module.** Lemma 4.5, p. 336: "The normal subgroup T of G generated as a
  normal subgroup by all the elements x(u), u ∈ U, is Abelian of exponent p."

Write `T` additively, as a vector space over `F_p` on which `G` acts by
conjugation. Fix a command `j` and put `x = x(q_j)`. The word `q_j` contains no
letter `A_1`. Let `a = a_1` and `a' = a'_1`.

**Step 1: `a' = a - 1` on the submodule generated by `x`.** Let `V` be the
`<a, a'>`-submodule of `T` generated by `x`: the span of all `g . x` with
`g in <a, a'>`, which is abelian by (S1).
- By (S2) and (S3), `a' . x = (a - 1) . x`.
- For `y = g . x` with `g in <a, a'>`, commutativity gives
  `a' . y = g . (a' . x) = g . ((a - 1) . x) = (a - 1) . y`.
- So `a' = a - 1` on `V`. Since `a'` is invertible and `V` is invariant under
  `a'^(-1)`, the map `a - 1` sends `V` onto `V`, with inverse the action of `a'^(-1)`.

**Step 2: the `a`-orbit of `x` spans an infinite-dimensional space.**
- By (S4), each `x ∗ a^(n)` lies in `V`.
- By (S5), with `q_i = q_j`, all `m_i = n_i = 0` except `m_1 = n`, and `α = β = 0`,
  the elements `x ∗ a^(n)` (`n = 0, 1, 2, ...`) are pairwise distinct, because the
  words `q_j a_1^n` are. So `V` is infinite.
- Let `U` be the span of `{a^m . x : m in Z}`, an `a`-invariant subspace of `V`.
  If `U` were finite-dimensional, `a - 1` would be injective on `U` (it is injective
  on `V` by Step 1), hence bijective on `U`, so `U` would be invariant under
  `(a - 1)^(-1) = a'^(-1)` and under `a' = a - 1`. Then `U` would contain every
  `g . x` with `g in <a, a'>`, so `U = V`. But `V` is infinite and `U` would be a
  finite-dimensional space over a finite field. So `U` is infinite-dimensional.

**Step 3: the base is a permutation module.** Suppose `iota(x)` lies in the base
`B^(Omega)`.
- The base is normal and `V` is generated by conjugates of `x`, so `iota(V)` lies
  in the base.
- `iota(V)` has exponent `p`, so it lies in `B[p]^(Omega)`. Choose an `F_p`-basis
  `E` of `B[p]`; then `B[p]^(Omega)` is `F_p[Omega x E]`.
- Write `iota(a) = (f, q)`. Conjugation by `(f, q)` acts on the abelian base as
  the permutation `q` of the coordinates: the lamp part `f` commutes with the base.
  So on `F_p[Omega x E]` it is the permutation `alpha = q x id_E`. Likewise
  conjugation by `iota(a')` is a permutation.

**Step 4: contradiction.** `iota` is injective and equivariant for the two
conjugation actions. So `iota(V)` is an `alpha`-invariant subspace of the
restricted permutation module `F_p[Omega x E]`, and `alpha - 1` maps it onto
itself (Step 1, transported). By `invertible-difference-modules-are-not-permutation-submodules`,
the span of `{alpha^m iota(x)}` is finite-dimensional. Pulling back along `iota`,
the span of `{a^m . x}` is finite-dimensional, contradicting Step 2. So `iota(x)`
is not in the base. `∎`

**Not used:** residual finiteness, sym-universal halting, or the command table
(G8) beyond the fact that `∗` is built from conjugations by `a_1, a'_1`.
