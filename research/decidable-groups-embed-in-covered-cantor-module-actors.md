**OPEN.** Let `M = C(C, F_2)` with Thompson's `V` acting by `v·f = f ∘ v^(-1)`. Call
`W <= Aut(M)` *covered* if `V <= W`, `W = <V, x_1, ..., x_r>`, and for each `j` there are a
subgroup `C_j <= V` commuting with `x_j` and finitely many `m ∈ M` whose `C_j`-translates
span `M`. Then every finitely generated group with solvable word problem embeds in a
finitely presented covered `W`.

## Why it matters

By route `affine-actors-via-covered-cantor-module-amalgams` it gives
`decidable-groups-embed-in-orbit-finite-affine-actors`, hence permutational
Boone--Higman.

## What is known

- **A member.** The binary Leavitt unit group `U` is covered, with one transvection
  `x = 1 + s_(00) t_(01)` and `C_x = {c(h,k)}`: its translates of six cone indicators span
  `M` (`leavitt-cantor-module-fp-via-thompson-amalgam`, Step 1 and item 2 there).
- **Generators are pinned by finite data.** Since `x_j` commutes with `C_j`,
  `x_j·(c·m) = c·(x_j·m)`, so `x_j` is determined by the finitely many images `x_j·m`.
- **The commutant of all of `V` is `{0, id}`.** A linear map `T` commuting with `V` sends
  `χ_[0]` to a function invariant under `V_[0] × V_[1]`, i.e. `a χ_[0] + b χ_[1]`, hence
  `T χ_A = a χ_A + b χ_(C∖A)` for proper nonempty clopen `A`; comparing `T χ_[00] + T χ_[01]`
  with `T χ_[0]` gives `b = 0`. So each `C_j` must be a proper subgroup of `V`.
- **Walls.** No shared recursive word-problem bound for covered groups is known, so
  `complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal` does not apply yet.

## Attempts

1. **Diagonal cone covering subgroups.** *Dies*. By
   `diagonal-cone-covered-actors-lie-in-binary-leavitt-unit-group`, every generator
   commuting with a clopen-rigid diagonal cone subgroup is a Leavitt unit. So `W <= U`, which
   has one word-problem bound.
2. **Punctured covering subgroups (2026-09-17, c-pbh1, operator-algebras).** *Dies* for the
   whole class. Examples are `Stab_V(0^∞) ∩ Stab_V(1^∞)`, and generally any subgroup
   containing a conjugate of a diagonal cone subgroup built from tame groups such as
   `V^(P)` for a finite puncture set `P`. The result is
   `punctured-cone-covered-actors-are-locally-finite-by-leavitt`.
   - **Invariant.** The commutant lies in `B = Φ(R) ⊕ J_0`, where `J_0` is the ideal of
     finite-rank evaluation operators `f ↦ f(y) g`. The quotient `π : B -> R` maps `W` onto
     a finitely generated subgroup of `U`, with locally finite kernel `W ∩ (1 + J_0)`.
   - **Where every member dies.** Every member dies when it tries to contain a torsion-free
     graph-shift group `G_A` whose word problem beats that of `U`. `G_A` has trivial
     intersection with the kernel, so it would embed in `U`.
   - **Why the complexity principle alone does not suffice.** These `W` share no
     word-problem bound, since the punctures can be arbitrarily complex computable points.
     The bound lives on the quotient.
   - **Fixed points.** A covering subgroup spanned by `n` translates has at most `n` global
     fixed points. So finite puncture sets are the only global singular data.
   - **What survives.** A proof needs covering subgroups containing no tame diagonal cone
     subgroup. Two known candidates remain:
     - twisted diagonals `{s_0 h t_0 + s_1 σhσ^(-1) t_1}` with `σ ∈ N_Homeo(V)∖V`. They are
       covering, and for the bit flip their commutant leaves `B`, as proved there.
     - non-locally-moving covering subgroups such as Thompson's `F`.

     For twisted diagonals the next obstruction to test is a uniform exponential
     word-problem bound for operator groups generated by `Φ(U)`, rational homeomorphisms and
     `J_0`.
