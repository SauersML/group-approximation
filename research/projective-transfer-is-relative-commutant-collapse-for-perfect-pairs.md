Let `C<=A`, suppose

```text
A=<C,h>,
C is perfect,
A is perfect.                                             (PTR1)
```

Fix a finite generating set `S` of `C`.  For unitaries in a tracial
ultraproduct write

```text
e(u,v)=1-|tau([u,v])|^2.                                 (PTR2)
```

Then the following matrix-ultraproduct statements are equivalent.

1. **Projective transfer.**  For every homomorphism
   `pi:A->U(M)` into a tracial matrix ultraproduct and every `u in U(M)`,

   ```text
   e(u,pi(s))=0 for every s in S
   implies e(u,pi(h))=0.                                 (PTR3)
   ```

2. **Ordinary relative-commutant collapse.**  For every such `pi`,

   ```text
   pi(C)' cap M = pi(A)' cap M.                          (PTR4)
   ```

In particular this applies to

```text
C=SL_3(Z),
A=SL_3(Z[1/2]),
h=diag(2,1,1/2).                                        (PTR5)
```

Thus the projective trace-square route has a useful exact
finite-dimensional theorem, but its genuinely approximate limiting problem
is not weaker than the original relative-commutant-collapse problem.

## Scalar commutators are characters

In a finite tracial von Neumann algebra, a unitary `w` satisfies

```text
|tau(w)|=1
```

if and only if `w` is scalar.  This is equality in Cauchy--Schwarz for the
vectors `w` and `1` in `L^2(M,tau)`.  Hence `e(u,pi(s))=0` means

```text
u pi(s) u^* = lambda_s pi(s)                            (PTR6)
```

for a scalar `lambda_s in T`.

More generally, if `(PTR6)` holds on a generating set of a group `H`, the
scalars extend to a character `lambda:H->T`.  Indeed conjugation by `u` and
`pi` are both representations; evaluating a relator shows that the free
character determined by the generator phases kills every relator.  If `H`
is perfect, the character is trivial.

Apply this first to `C`.  The hypothesis in `(PTR3)` therefore says that
`u` actually commutes with `pi(C)`.  If its conclusion holds, then
`[u,pi(h)]` is scalar.  Since `A=<C,h>`, conjugation by `u` differs from
`pi` on the generators of `A` by scalars, hence by a character of `A`.
Perfectness of `A` kills that character, so `u` commutes with `pi(h)` as
well.  This proves `(PTR3)=>(PTR4)`.  The converse is immediate.

For `(PTR5)`, `SL_3(Z)` is generated by elementary matrices and is perfect,
and the same elementary-commutator identities over `Z[1/2]` make
`SL_3(Z[1/2])` perfect.  The displayed `h` together with `SL_3(Z)` generates
the localized elementary matrices, hence all of `A`.

## Sequential form

Fix a finite presentation of `A`.  Statement `(PTR3)` is equivalent to the
absence of a sequence of finite-dimensional assignments `rho_n` and
unitaries `U_n` for which

```text
presentation_defect(rho_n) -> 0,
sum_(s in S) e(U_n,rho_n(s)) -> 0,
liminf_n e(U_n,rho_n(h)) > 0.                            (PTR7)
```

One direction follows by taking the tracial ultraproduct.  For the other,
representatives of any ultraproduct homomorphism give such a sequence if
`(PTR3)` fails.  Consequently `(PTR3)` is also equivalent to the existence
of some joint modulus `omega(t)->0` such that

```text
e(U,rho(h))
 <= omega(presentation_defect(rho)
          + sum_(s in S)e(U,rho(s))).                   (PTR8)
```

The compactness argument gives no linear or affine shape for `omega`.
Therefore `(PTR8)` is weaker than the proposed estimate

```text
e_h <= K e_C + eta(delta),                              (PTR9)
```

but `(PTR8)` already suffices for the HNN regular-trace contradiction: the
HNN relators make both inputs of `omega` tend to zero.

## Why the exact theorem does not pass automatically

`exact-projective-codensity-has-a-uniform-trace-square-gap` proves `(PTR9)`
with `delta=0` on every exact finite-dimensional representation.  If
`(PTR8)` failed, however, `(PTR7)` would produce an exact representation of
`A` only in a tracial matrix ultraproduct, not an exact finite-dimensional
representation.  Finite-dimensional co-density says nothing by itself about
the new relative commutant in that ultraproduct.  Passing from the exact
theorem to `(PTR8)` is precisely the missing matrix-stability/relative-
commutant theorem; it is not a formal compactness consequence.

## Exact falsification object and the sofic-action gate

Let

```text
W=(direct_sum_(A/C) Z/2) semidirect A                   (PTR10)
```

be the restricted permutational wreath product, and let `v` be the lamp at
the base coset `C`.  In `L(W)`, the canonical copy of `A` has regular trace,

```text
v in L(C)' cap L(W),
[v,h] is the product of the distinct lamps at C and hC. (PTR11)
```

The last word is nontrivial and has canonical trace zero.  Therefore any
trace-preserving embedding

```text
L(W) -> product_omega M_(d_n)                           (PTR12)
```

produces `(PTR7)` with limiting `h`-energy equal to one.  Equivalently,
Connes embeddability of `L(W)` refutes both projective transfer and ordinary
relative-commutant collapse.

There is no automatic soficity shortcut.  The group `A` is residually
finite and hence sofic, but `(PTR10)` is a generalized wreath product over
the action `A curvearrowright A/C`.  The relevant permanence theorem assumes
that this **action** is sofic; whether arbitrary actions of sofic groups are
sofic is open.  If this particular coset action is sofic, the generalized
wreath-product theorem makes `W` sofic, hence `(PTR12)` exists and transfer
fails.  Contrapositively, projective transfer for `(PTR5)` would prove both

```text
A curvearrowright A/C is not a sofic action,
W is not hyperlinear.                                  (PTR13)
```

This is the sharp falsification boundary.  Finite quotient actions cannot
approximate the distinguished coset: finite-dimensional co-density makes
the images of `C` and `A` equal in every finite quotient, so a point fixed
by `C` is then fixed by `A`.

## Claim boundary

This note does **not** prove or refute `(PTR3)` for `(PTR5)`.  It proves:

- projectivization removes scalar cocycle noise in exact finite models, but
  gives no qualitative weakening at the ultraproduct frontier;
- the affine quantitative estimate `(PTR9)` is stronger than the HNN route
  needs;
- the minimal remaining theorem is exactly `(PTR4)` (or `(PTR8)`);
- the coset permutational wreath product is the exact producer-side
  countermodel, conditional on its group factor being Connes embeddable.

The arithmetic lane should therefore not spend further effort optimizing
the exact constant `K`.  Its load-bearing question is whether the coset
Bernoulli/wreath representation can occur in a tracial matrix ultraproduct.

## Literature check

The generalized-wreath implication used above is the action-sensitive one:
for sofic groups acting through a sofic action, the restricted generalized
wreath product is sofic.  Recent work on sofic actions explicitly retains
the action hypothesis and notes that arbitrary actions of sofic groups are
not known to be sofic.  This is exactly why residual finiteness of `A` does
not settle `(PTR12)`.

Precisely, Gao--Kunnawalkam Elayavalli--Patchell, *Soficity for group
actions on sets and applications*, Research in the Mathematical Sciences 12
(2025), Article 48, Theorem 3.6, proves the generalized-wreath implication;
their Theorem 3.8 gives its hyperlinear/Connes-embeddable analogue.  Their
introduction explicitly records as open whether every action of a sofic
group is sofic.  Their positive coset theorem assumes a locally finite
stabilizer, which does not apply to `C=SL_3(Z)`.
