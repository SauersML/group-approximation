**ESTABLISHED.** Route: `unique-games-holonomy-shift-reduction-proof`.

**Setting.** `U` is a weighted unique game with alphabet `[k]` on a connected
graph `G = (V, E, w)`. A constraint on the directed edge `e = (u, v)` reads
`sigma_v = pi_e(sigma_u)` with `pi_e in S_k`, and the reversed edge carries
`pi_e^(-1)`. A **gauge** is a map `g : V -> S_k`. It acts by
`pi_e^g = g_v pi_e g_u^(-1)`. For a root `r`, the **holonomy group**
`Mon_r(U) <= S_k` is the set of products `pi_(e_m) ... pi_(e_1)` over closed
walks `r = v_0, v_1, ..., v_m = r`.

**Theorem.**

1. *(Gauge.)* `sigma -> (g_v(sigma_v))_v` is a bijection of labelings that
   satisfies the same edges, so `val(U^g) = val(U)`, and similarly for every
   SDP or SoS relaxation that is invariant under relabeling each vertex's
   alphabet. The graph and its weights do not change.
   Also, `Mon_r(U^g) = g_r Mon_r(U) g_r^(-1)`, and a change of root conjugates
   `Mon_r` by the product along a path.
2. *(Tree gauge.)* Let `T` be a spanning tree. There is a unique gauge `g`
   with `g_r = id` that makes every tree constraint the identity, and it is
   computed by breadth-first search. Then `Mon_r(U)` is generated by the
   gauged non-tree constraints `pi_e^g`, `e not in T`, and it contains every
   `pi_e^g`.
3. *(Subgroup criterion.)* For `Gamma <= S_k`, `U` is gauge-equivalent to an
   instance with every constraint in `Gamma` if and only if `Mon_r(U)` is
   conjugate in `S_k` into `Gamma`.
4. *(Orbit split.)* Let `O_1, ..., O_t` be the orbits of `H = Mon_r(U)` on
   `[k]`. In the tree gauge, every constraint preserves each `O_i`, so `U^g`
   restricts to a unique game `U_i` with alphabet `O_i` on the same graph, and
   ```text
   max_i val(U_i)  <=  val(U)  <=  sum_i val(U_i)  <=  t max_i val(U_i).
   ```
5. *(Shift form.)* Suppose `H` acts regularly on `O_i` through
   `A_i = H|_(O_i)`, that is, `|A_i| = |O_i|`. This holds automatically when
   `A_i` is abelian. For any `o_i in O_i`, the bijection `A_i -> O_i`,
   `a -> a(o_i)`, turns `U_i` into the left-shift game `x_v = gamma_e x_u` over
   the group `A_i`, where `gamma_e = pi_e^g|_(O_i)`. If `A_i` is cyclic, this is
   Affine-UG over `Z_(|O_i|)` in the sense of Bafna--Minzer Definition 1.4.
   Conversely, a transitive `A_i` that is not regular has a nontrivial point
   stabilizer, and the orbit game is then a coset game `A_i / Stab`, not a shift
   game. General UG over `[k]` is the extreme case `S_k / S_(k-1)`.
6. *(Polynomial time.)* The gauge, the orbits and, when the `A_i` are
   regular, a multiplication table and the identification in (5) are computed
   in time `poly(|E|, k)`. The closure of the generators restricted to `O_i`
   stops at `|O_i| <= k` elements or certifies non-regularity. Commutativity
   and cyclicity are checked on this table.

**Corollary T (host-preserving transfer).** Fix a class `H` of weighted graphs.
Suppose an algorithm, on every Affine-UG instance over `Z_q` whose constraint
graph lies in `H` and whose value is at least `c`, finds a labeling of value at
least `delta(c, q)`. Now take any unique game `U` with alphabet `k` on a host in
`H` whose holonomy acts on every orbit through a cyclic group. If
`val(U) >= c`, the same algorithm, run on every `U_i` (these live on the same
host), finds a labeling of `U` of value at least
`min_(q <= k) delta(c/k, q)`.

**Corollary BM (the Bafna--Minzer hosts).** By Theorems 1.6--1.8 of
`affine-ug-easy-on-globally-hypercontractive-graphs`, for every `c > 0` and
alphabet `k` there is `delta > 0` such that, on `Grass(n, l)`, on
`J(n, l, alpha l)` and on the depth-`alpha` HDX walks of Theorem 1.8, every
unique game with cyclic holonomy on every orbit and value `>= c` has a labeling
of value `>= delta` found in time `n^(D(l, k, c))`. So Gap-UG[`c`, `s`] with
`s < delta` is in P on these instances at fixed `l, k`.

## What it kills (impact type 3)

**Class.** Every reduction that aims to make unique games hard on a
Bafna--Minzer host (Grassmann, noisy Johnson or HDX walks at fixed `l` and
alphabet), at any constant completeness `c > 0`, using constraints that are
shifts in a fixed cyclic group *up to any per-vertex relabeling*. This covers:

* literal `Z_q`-linear encodings;
* instances disguised by per-vertex gauges;
* instances whose constraints generate a cyclic group of permutations with
  several orbits (for example sign or parity games embedded in larger
  alphabets);
* any "rotation" encoding whose monodromy around every cycle is a power of one
  permutation.

**Invariant.** `Mon_r(U)` up to conjugacy, a host-independent and
reduction-independent quantity computable in polynomial time.

**Step where every member dies.** The NO case. BM rounding, applied through (5)
to the best orbit, produces value `delta(c/k)` on every YES instance. So no
soundness below `delta(c/k)` is possible.

**What it does not kill (the exact survivor).** Instances whose holonomy is not
cyclic on some orbit. These are either regular but non-cyclic (shift games over
a non-cyclic group, abelian or not) or non-regular (coset games). Bafna--Minzer
footnote 2 asks for general UG on these graphs; after this theorem, that
question is exactly the rungs R2--R4 of
`general-unique-games-easy-on-globally-hypercontractive-graphs`. By
`ugc-is-equivalent-to-affine-ugc` item (3), rung R2 (elementary abelian
`F_2^N` holonomy) already carries the full hardness of UGC on hosts that the
reduction may choose.

**Artifact.** `check_holonomy_reduction.py` runs in under 1 s. It checks
items 1, 2, 4 and 5 by brute force on 300 disguised shift games over `Z_2`,
`Z_3`, `Z_4`, `Z_6` and the non-cyclic `Z_2 x Z_2`, with one or two regular
orbits.
