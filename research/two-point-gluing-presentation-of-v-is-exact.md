## Statement

Let `Ω = C × {1, 2}`, so `F(T) = V_(2,2) ≅ V`. Put:
- `N = (V × V) ⋊ ⟨s⟩`, where `(a, b)` acts copywise and `s` swaps the copies. This is the stabilizer of the
  halving `{C × 1, C × 2}`.
- `M = V(Z/2) = C(C, Z/2) ⋊ V`, generated by the diagonal `V` and the local swaps `σ_U`. This is the centralizer
  of `s`.
- `Γ~ = M *_(V×Z/2) N / ⟨⟨[M_0, N_1]⟩⟩`, with `π : Γ~ → V_(2,2)`.

**Theorem.** `π` is an isomorphism.

So the smallest gluing test posed in `gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-free-group-full-shift` (`Λ = Z/2` on two
points, an image of `K_(Z, 2^Z)`) is exact. The finite models of P2′b-exactness show no obstruction at the first
level.

## Tools

- (R1) `[M_U, N_(U')] = 1` for disjoint clopen `U, U' ⊆ C`.
- (R2) `σ_U n σ_U = s n s` for `n ∈ N_U`.

  Both are from `v-times-sft-gluing-kernel-is-relatively-perfect`. Here `M_U` and `N_U` are the elements
  supported over `U`.
- (Sh) For every partition `Q` of `C` into at least two cones, the piece permutations of `Q × {1, 2}` lift to a
  subgroup of `Γ~` containing `M ∩ Sym(Q)` and `N ∩ Sym(Q)`. This is item 3 of
  `splitting-kills-the-finite-shadow-parity-of-the-gluing-group`.
- *Pieces* are `(i, γ)`, the cone `[γ]` in copy `i`. `w_(γδ) ∈ V` swaps `[γ], [δ]` by prefix (for `γ ⊥ δ`), and
  `ι_1(h) = (h, 1)`, `ι_2(h) = (1, h)`.

## Proof

**Step 1: canonical lifts.** For disjoint pieces `a, b`, define `L(a, b) = L(b, a) ∈ Γ~` with
`π L(a, b) = t_(a,b)`, the prefix swap of `a` and `b`.
- (L1) Same copy: `L((i,γ), (i,δ)) = ι_i(w_(γδ)) ∈ N`.
- (L2) `L((1,γ), (2,γ)) = σ_[γ] ∈ M`. In particular `σ_[∅] = s`.
- (L3) `γ ⊥ δ`: `L((1,γ), (2,δ)) = σ_[γ] (1, w_(γδ)) σ_[γ] = σ_[δ] (w_(γδ), 1) σ_[δ]`. The two forms agree by (R2)
  for `σ_[γ] σ_[δ]` and `(w_(γδ), 1) ∈ N_([γ]∪[δ])`. By (Sh), this is also the image of the transposition in
  every symmetric shadow containing `γ` and `δ`. Braids give `ABA = BAB` there.
- (L4) `γ, δ` nonempty, comparable and distinct: `L((1,γ), (2,δ)) = (1, h) L((1,γ), (2,η)) (1, h)^-1`, for any
  `η ⊥ γ` and any `h ∈ V` with `h|_([η])` the prefix map `η → δ`.
- (L5) If one of the cones is empty, split: for example
  `L((1,γ), (2,∅)) = L((1,γ0), (2,0)) · L((1,γ1), (2,1))` for `γ ≠ ∅`.

**Step 2: (S) for (L1)–(L3).**
- (L1) and (L2): these are relations of `N` and `M`.
- (L3): `σ_[γ] = σ_[γ0] σ_[γ1]` and `(1, w_(γδ)) = (1, w_(γ0,δ0)) (1, w_(γ1,δ1))`. Also `σ_[γ1]` commutes with
  `(1, w_(γ0,δ0))`, and `σ_[γ0]` with `(1, w_(γ1,δ1))`, by (R1). Rearranging gives
  `L((1,γ),(2,δ)) = L((1,γ0),(2,δ0)) L((1,γ1),(2,δ1))`.

**Step 3: moves between incomparable cross lifts.**
- **Lemma A.** Let `γ ≠ ∅` and `η, η' ⊥ γ`, and let `h|_([η])` be the prefix map `η → η'`. Then
  `(1, h) L((1,γ),(2,η)) (1, h)^-1 = L((1,γ),(2,η'))`.
  - (i) If `h` is the identity on `[η]`, then `(1, h) ∈ N_(C∖[η])` commutes with `σ_[η]` (R1) and with
    `(w_(γη), 1)`. So it commutes with `L` in the second form.
  - (ii) If `γ, η, η'` are pairwise incomparable, write `h = w_(ηη') h'` with `h'` the identity on `[η]`. By (i),
    conjugating by `(1, w_(ηη'))` is conjugation inside the symmetric shadow on a partition containing `γ, η, η'`.
    That gives the claim, by (Sh) and (L3).
  - (iii) Otherwise, pick `ζ ⊥ γ, η, η'` and factor `h = (h w_(ηζ)) · w_(ηζ)`. Then apply (ii) twice.
  - (iv) Such a `ζ` exists unless `[γ] ∪ [η] ∪ [η'] = C`. In that case split once by Step 2, apply (iii) to both
    halves, and recombine.
- **Lemma A′** is the mirror statement: copy-1 moves `(f, 1)` between `L((1,κ),(2,δ))` and `L((1,κ'),(2,δ))` for
  `κ, κ' ⊥ δ`. It has the same proof, using the first form of (L3).

**Step 4: (L4) is well defined, and (S) holds for all lifts.**
- *Independence of the choice.* Two choices `(η, h)` and `(η', h')` differ by `(1, h'^-1 h)`, which maps `η → η'`
  by prefix. By Lemma A they give the same lift.
- *Agreement with (L3).* If `γ ⊥ δ`, take `η = δ` and `h = 1`.
- *(S) for (L4).* Split `L((1,γ),(2,η))` by Step 2 and conjugate each half. Each half is `L((1,γ w),(2,δ w))`,
  either by definition (L4) or by Lemma A, whichever type the half has.
- *(S) for (L5).* This is the definition.

Hence the **splitting principle**: every identity below between products of lifts holds as soon as it holds after
replacing each pair `(a, b)` by all `(a w, b w)` with `|w| = k`. So pieces may be taken as deep as needed, and
"room" (an auxiliary incomparable cone, or a nonempty complement) is always available.

**Lemma B (copy-1 description of (L4)).** Let `κ ⊥ δ`, and let `f|_([κ])` be the prefix map `κ → γ`. Then
`L((1,γ),(2,δ)) = (f, 1) L((1,κ),(2,δ)) (f, 1)^-1`.
- With room, pick `η ⊥ γ, κ` and `g` with `g|_([η])` the prefix map `η → δ`.
- By Lemma A, the right side is `(f, 1)(1, g) L((1,κ),(2,η)) (1, g)^-1 (f, 1)^-1`.
- `(f, 1)` and `(1, g)` commute, and Lemma A′ turns `(f, 1) L((1,κ),(2,η)) (f, 1)^-1` into `L((1,γ),(2,η))`.
- This gives the left side.

**Step 5: naturality under generators.** Let `x` be `(1, h)`, `(h, 1)`, `s` or `σ_[κ]`, mapping disjoint pieces
`a, b` by prefix onto `a', b'`. Then `x L(a, b) x^-1 = L(a', b')`. By the splitting principle, assume all cones
are nonempty and have room.

*Case `x = (1, h)`.*
- (L1): this is computed in `N`.
- (L2), with `hγ = γ`: commute by (R1).
- (L2), with `hγ ⊥ γ`: write `h = w_(γ,hγ) h'`, then use (R1) and (Sh).
- (L2), with `hγ` comparable to `γ`: write `h = (h w_(γζ)) w_(γζ)` for some `ζ ⊥ γ, hγ`, then use (Sh) and the
  definition (L4).
- (L3): use Lemma A when `γ ⊥ hδ`, and (L4) when they are comparable. When `hδ = γ`, write
  `h = w_(δγ) h'` with `h'` the identity on `[δ]`. Then use (R1) and (Sh): the swap `(2γ)(2δ)` conjugates
  `(1γ)(2δ)` to `(1γ)(2γ)`.
- (L4): `(1, h)(1, g) = (1, hg)`, and the result follows by the definition (L4), by Lemma A, or by the
  `hδ = γ` case of (L3).

*Case `x = (h, 1)`.* This is the mirror of the previous case, using Lemmas A′ and B.

*Case `x = s`.*
- `s ι_2(w) s = ι_1(w)`, and `s σ_[γ] s = σ_[γ]`.
- (L3): `s L((1,γ),(2,δ)) s = σ_[γ] (w_(γδ), 1) σ_[γ] = L((1,δ),(2,γ))`, by the second form.
- (L4): `s (1, g) = (g, 1) s`, and Lemma B.

*Case `x = σ_[κ]`.* Each of `[γ]` and `[δ]` lies inside `[κ]` or is disjoint from it.
- (L1), both inside: this is (R2).
- (L1), both outside: this is (R1).
- (L1), `γ` inside and `δ` outside: `σ_[κ] = σ_[γ] σ_([κ]∖[γ])`, and the second factor commutes by (R1).
  What remains is the definition (L3).
- (L2): the two elements commute in `M`.
- (L3), both inside: `σ_[κ] (1, w) σ_[κ] = (w, 1)` by (R2), so the conjugate is `σ_[γ] (w, 1) σ_[γ] = L((1,δ),(2,γ))`.
- (L3), mixed: split off `σ_[γ]` or `σ_[δ]` as for (L1). This gives `ι_2(w_(γδ))` or `ι_1(w_(γδ))`.
- (L4): comparable cones are both inside or both outside `[κ]`.
  - Outside: choose `η` and `g` supported off `[κ]`. Everything commutes by (R1) and the (L3) case.
  - Inside: choose `η` and `g` inside `[κ]`. By (R2), `σ_[κ] (1, g) σ_[κ] = (g, 1)`, and the (L3) case turns
    the inner lift into `L((1,η),(2,γ))`. So the conjugate is `(g, 1) L((1,η),(2,γ)) (g, 1)^-1 = L((1,δ),(2,γ))`,
    by Lemma B.

These generate `Γ~`: `N = ⟨ι_1 V, ι_2 V, s⟩`, and `M = ⟨V_diag, σ_[κ]⟩` with `V_diag ⊆ ι_1 V · ι_2 V`.

**Step 6: strong naturality (multiplicativity).** Let `g ∈ Γ~`, and suppose `π(g)` maps disjoint pieces `a, b` by
prefix. Then `g L(a, b) g^-1 = L(π(g) a, π(g) b)`.
- Write `g = x_1 ⋯ x_r` in the generators of Step 5.
- Split `(a, b)` into `(a w, b w)`, `|w| = k`, with `k` so large that every suffix `x_j ⋯ x_r` maps every `a w`,
  `b w` by prefix (finite tables).
- Apply Step 5 `r` times to each factor, and recombine by (S). Here `π(g)(a w) = (π(g) a) w`.

**Step 7: Bleak–Quick.** Identify `V_(2,2) ≅ V` by `(c, i) ↦ (i−1) c`. Pieces become nonempty words.
Bleak–Quick (arXiv:1511.02123, Theorem 1.1, read from the arXiv HTML) present `V` by generators `s_(α,β)`
(`α ⊥ β`) and three kinds of relations:
- (O) `s_(α,β)^2 = 1`;
- (C) `s_(γ,δ) s_(α,β) s_(γ,δ) = s_(α·(γδ), β·(γδ))`, whenever the right side is defined;
- (S) `s_(α,β) = s_(α0,β0) s_(α1,β1)`.

The lifts satisfy them:
- (C) is Step 6 with `g = L(c, d)`, since "defined" means `t_(c,d)` maps both pieces by prefix;
- (S) is Step 4;
- (O) holds for (L1)–(L4), which are conjugates of involutions. For (L5) it follows from (C): the two factors
  have disjoint supports, so they commute.

So `s_(a,b) ↦ L(a, b)` defines `Φ : V_(2,2) → Γ~`, with `π Φ = id` on generators.
- The image of `Φ` contains every `ι_i(w_(γδ))`, hence `ι_1 V` and `ι_2 V` (`V` is generated by transpositions).
- It also contains `s = L((1,∅),(2,∅))` and every `σ_[γ]`.
- So `Φ` is onto, and `π` is injective. `∎`

## Lesson for general BH

A gluing presentation is exact once every transposition has a **canonical lift that is natural** under the
generators of both pieces. Naturality is checked one generator at a time, which is a finite case analysis.
Two tools make it go through:
- disjoint-support commutation (R1), and local-label conjugation (R2);
- the braid theorem inside finite shadows, which is where Thompson splitting kills the parity.

After that, Bleak–Quick's presentation does the rest. This is the "multiplicativity lemma" of
`v-times-gluing-exactness-is-commutation-with-one-transposition`, proved here in its simplest instance.

Only three features of this model were used:
- the labels form a finite group acting on a finite set;
- local labels over cones exist;
- `N` contains all of `V` in each copy.

For `Λ` acting on a Cantor space (FSG(Z, 2), EX(Z) for bh-invent-16), the same scheme needs lifts of
**conditioned** transpositions. Their naturality under local labels `λ|_[κ]` with `λ` of infinite order is the new
case, since there is no involution to braid with. That is the precise next step on the P2′b route.
