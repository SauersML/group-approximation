# Compute the independent-set affine hull and glue the private contextual absorbers

## 1. The exact source language

Fix the independent-set game \(I\) extracted in
perfect-qc-separation-yields-finite-bcs-with-no-matrix-model.
It has a finite question set \(Q\) and, for every question \(i\), the same
finite outcome set \(Y\). Its projection algebra is generated by

\[
 p_{i,v}=p_{i,v}^*=p_{i,v}^2,\qquad
 \sum_{v\in Y}p_{i,v}=1,                                \tag{1}
\]

together with

\[
 p_{i,u}p_{j,v}=0
 \quad\hbox{for every losing pair }(i,j;u,v).            \tag{2}
\]

This includes \(i=j\) and \(u\ne v\), so (2) contains the
within-question orthogonality relations.

Put \(z_{i,v}=1-2p_{i,v}\). Paddock's SynchBCS construction is exact:
the Boolean algebra generated by the \(z_{i,v}\), with the exact-one
constraints and the forbidden-overlap constraints, is star-isomorphic to
the algebra in (1)--(2). For scalar assignments write

\[
 \xi_{i,v}=\frac{1-z_{i,v}}2\in\mathbf F_2.              \tag{3}
\]

The support on one question is

\[
 E_m=\{e_1,\ldots,e_m\}\subseteq\mathbf F_2^m,
 \qquad m=|Y|,                                           \tag{4}
\]

and every forbidden pair has support

\[
 N=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}\subseteq\mathbf F_2^2.          \tag{5}
\]

One can equivalently present (4) as the odd-parity equation together with
the within-question instances of (5). This distinction will not affect the
argument.

The chosen source has

\[
 \omega^{\mathrm{co}}(I)=1,\qquad \omega^*(I)<1.         \tag{6}
\]

For a finite game the payoff is continuous and \(C_{qa}\) is the closure of
the finite-dimensional correlation set. Therefore taking the supremum does
not change the value:

\[
 \omega^{qa}(I)=\omega^*(I)<1.                           \tag{7}
\]

Thus preserving a strict qa gap, rather than merely excluding an exact
finite-dimensional perfect strategy, is load-bearing.

## 2. Scalar solutions of an LCS are affine

Let an output binary LCS have reflection variables \(X_s\) and central
reflection \(J\). In a one-dimensional representation write

\[
 X_s=(-1)^{x_s},\qquad J=(-1)^j,
 \qquad x_s,j\in\mathbf F_2.                             \tag{8}
\]

Every LCS equation becomes an affine equation

\[
 \sum_{s\in L}x_s=bj
 \quad\hbox{in }\mathbf F_2.                             \tag{9}
\]

Scalar commutation relations are automatic. After fixing the negative
sector \(j=1\), the scalar solution set of any finite LCS gadget is
therefore an affine subspace, possibly empty.

Suppose a gadget has visible coordinates \(V\) and auxiliary coordinates
\(A\). Projection of its affine solution set to \(V\) is affine: in linear
algebra terms it is the image of an affine space under a linear coordinate
map. If that projection contains a set \(S\), it contains its affine hull:

\[
 S\subseteq\operatorname{pr}_V\operatorname{Sol}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Aff}(S)
 \subseteq\operatorname{pr}_V\operatorname{Sol}.         \tag{10}
\]

This is also an immediate specialization of
lcs-parity-gadgets-preserve-odd-dictator-affine-hull.

## 3. The two hulls can be computed exactly

Every vector in \(E_m\) has odd coordinate sum, so

\[
 \operatorname{Aff}(E_m)
 \subseteq H_{\mathrm{odd}}
 :=\left\{x:\sum_{r=1}^m x_r=1\right\}.                  \tag{11}
\]

Fix \(e_1\). The differences

\[
 e_r-e_1=e_r+e_1,\qquad 2\le r\le m,                    \tag{12}
\]

form a basis of the even-parity linear hyperplane. Hence the affine span of
\(E_m\) is \(e_1\) plus that hyperplane, giving

\[
 \operatorname{Aff}(E_m)=H_{\mathrm{odd}}.              \tag{13}
\]

This also covers \(m=1\).

The set \(N\) contains the origin and the two standard basis vectors.
Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{Aff}(N)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0),(0,1)\}
 =\mathbf F_2^2.                                        \tag{14}
\]

Equation (14) is the decisive source-specific collapse. A local parity
gadget which is complete on the three allowed NAND atoms must also accept
the forbidden atom \((1,1)\) in its scalar negative-\(J\) sector.

## 4. A global scalar cheat

Assume that each source predicate \(C\) is replaced by a finite LCS gadget
whose auxiliary set \(A_C\) is private to that predicate, and that every
allowed scalar atom of \(C\) extends in the sector \(J=-1\).

Choose an arbitrary outcome \(v_i\in Y\) for every question \(i\), and put

\[
 \xi_{i,v}=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&v=v_i,\\
  0,&v\ne v_i.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{15}
\]

On an exact-one context, (15) belongs to \(E_m\), so completeness gives an
auxiliary extension. More generally, (13) would suffice.

On a forbidden-pair context, the restriction of (15) is some element of
\(\mathbf F_2^2\). It can be \((1,1)\) when the independently selected
outcomes form an edge of the game graph. By (10) and (14), even this profile
has a scalar satisfying extension through that local gadget.

All these extensions agree on shared source ports because they were chosen
from the single global assignment (15). They also agree on \(J=-1\).
Since the auxiliary sets \(A_C\) are pairwise disjoint and every output
equation belongs to one local gadget, their union is a scalar solution of
the entire output LCS.

If exact-one is presented instead as one odd-parity equation plus
within-question NAND cells, (15) satisfies the parity equation and the same
argument extends every NAND cell. Therefore either canonical presentation
has the same cheat.

Mapping the scalar bits back by (8) gives a one-dimensional unitary
representation of the solution group in which \(J=-1\). The usual classical
LCS strategy is perfect. Hence

\[
 \omega^{\mathrm{cl}}
 =\omega^q
 =\omega^{qa}
 =\omega^{\mathrm{co}}
 =1.                                                      \tag{16}
\]

In particular, a decoder with source loss tending to zero as the target
LCS defect tends to zero cannot exist for this compiler class: applying it
at target defect zero to (16) would produce a perfect finite-dimensional
strategy for the source, contradicting (7).

## 5. The audited Magic-Square cells do not repair the cheat

There are three exact boundary classifications already proved in the
repository.

### 5.1 One port

A one-port Magic Square extends every representation of its port after
tensoring with \(\mathbf C^4\). In particular, both scalar signs extend.
Thus a one-port square imposes no restriction on the scalar boundary
profile.

### 5.2 A common full row

There are positive- and negative-port four-dimensional Magic-Square
solutions agreeing on one entire row. Spectral control therefore extends
an arbitrary tuple of source involutions through any number of squares
sharing that row, in one \(\mathbf C^4\) amplification. The ports need not
commute. A fortiori every scalar tuple extends.

### 5.3 Two transverse ports

A Magic Square with two transverse line-product ports extends exactly when
the two port involutions commute. Every pair of scalars commutes. Hence all
four scalar sign profiles extend after a four-dimensional amplification.

Now start with the scalar assignment (15). Any group word in its visible
ports is again a scalar sign, so changing the port word used by a cell does
not help. Replace a NAND constraint by one of the three boundary blocks
above, or append such blocks to a local LCS lowering. The forbidden
\((1,1)\) profile still extends.

For a finite collection of private one-port and transverse blocks, place
the new cell operators of distinct blocks on distinct tensor factors. A
row-glued family uses one common four-dimensional factor for the entire
family. Distinct row-glued families with disjoint new interfaces can again
use separate factors. Every old visible port acts by its scalar from (15)
tensored with the identity. Thus, if there are \(r\) independent block
families, dimension \(4^r\) is enough for a global perfect representation.

This tensoring statement deliberately excludes identifications of new cell
operators between distinct families, except for the common full row inside
one audited row-glued family. Such cross-family identifications are exactly
where a source-dependent cyclic holonomy could arise, and are not covered
by the absorber theorems.

Closed degree-two Magic-Square incidence networks do not provide a shortcut
around this boundary. By Arkhipov's marked-sign theorem, recorded in
two-magic-square-incidence-overlaps-never-separate, their central sign
survives in a commuting-operator model exactly when it survives in a
finite-dimensional model. They may have infinite universal groups, but
they cannot create the desired perfect model separation.

## 6. Why exact finite-dimensional exclusion is not enough

The target needed for the nonhyperlinear-group endpoint must obey

\[
 \omega^{\mathrm{co}}=1,\qquad \omega^{qa}<1,            \tag{17}
\]

not only

\[
 \omega^{\mathrm{co}}=1
 \quad\hbox{and no exact finite-dimensional perfect model}.             \tag{18}
\]

Slofstra constructed a binary linear-system game which is playable
perfectly by a limit of finite-dimensional strategies but not by any
finite-dimensional or spatial tensor-product perfect strategy. Thus (18)
can coexist with \(\omega^{qa}=1\) already inside the LCS class.

At the group level the same quantifier failure is visible in
property-t-actor-keeps-fd-invisible-center-in-lef-models. Its central
involution is killed by every exact finite-dimensional representation, yet
finite spin quotients give asymptotically faithful marked models in which
that involution remains at normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance
\(\sqrt2\) from the identity. Exact representation theory does not control
a moving finite window.

Accordingly, feeding only an exact no-matrix statement through a
Slofstra-style group-to-solution-group embedding is insufficient. The input
mark must already be in the normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt approximate radical,
or the compiler must supply a dimension-independent reverse decoder that
transfers the strict source gap (7).

## 7. Exact residual interface

The argument used both of the following hypotheses essentially:

1. every allowed scalar atom of a full source predicate, rather than only
   atoms in the support of one chosen non-CE trace, has its own extension;
2. auxiliary coordinates belonging to different predicates do not meet
   around a new cycle.

Dropping the first hypothesis leads to the trace-support affine relaxation:
a successful trace could in principle have smaller local support whose
affine hull avoids forbidden atoms. Dropping the second leads to
source-dependent shared cyclic ancillas, higher-occurrence ports, or
non-incidence cross-block equations. A live construction must then prove
that its global perfect commuting representation extends the chosen source
trace and that every approximate matrix representation decodes back to a
near-perfect source strategy.

The local affine and private Magic-Square routes do neither. They have the
explicit zero-defect finite-dimensional countermodel (15), so the perfect
SynchBCS source cannot be transformed through them into an LCS with a
strict qa gap. The unrestricted compiler, and hence the nonhyperlinear
solution group, remains open.
