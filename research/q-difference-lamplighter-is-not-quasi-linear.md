## Lemma

Let `G` be a group with a nontrivial torsion-free abelian normal subgroup `V`, and let
`x, y ∈ G` and an integer `m` with `|m| >= 2` satisfy `[x,y] v [x,y]^-1 = v^m` for all
`v ∈ V`. Then no homomorphism `ρ: G → GL_(n_1)(F_1) × … × GL_(n_r)(F_r)`, with the `F_i`
fields, is injective. The same holds for `GL_n(R)` with `R` a reduced commutative
Noetherian ring, since `R` embeds in the finite product of the fraction fields of its
quotients by minimal primes.

**Proof.** Fix one factor `ρ_i: G → GL_n(F)`, with `F` algebraically closed. Let `U` be
the Zariski closure of `ρ_i(V)`. It is a commutative algebraic group normalized by
`ρ_i(x)` and `ρ_i(y)`. Conjugation by `c = ρ_i([x,y])` is an automorphism of `U` that
agrees with `u ↦ u^m` on the dense subgroup `ρ_i(V)`, hence on `U`. Write
`U = U_s × U_u`.
- The identity component of `U_s` is a torus `G_m^k`. On it `u ↦ u^m` is an isogeny of
  degree `|m|^k`, so it is an automorphism only if `k = 0`. So `U_s` is finite.
- In characteristic `0`, `U_u` is a vector group with Lie algebra `L ⊆ gl_n`. `Ad(c)` acts
  on `L` as `m·Id`. `L` is invariant under `Ad ρ_i(x)` and `Ad ρ_i(y)`, and
  `Ad(c) = [Ad ρ_i(x), Ad ρ_i(y)]` on `L`, so `m^(dim L) = det = 1`. Hence `L = 0`.
- In characteristic `p`, `U_u` has exponent a power of `p`.

So `ρ_i(V)` has bounded exponent, and so does `ρ(V) ⊆ ∏ ρ_i(V)`. Since `V` is torsion-free
and nontrivial, `ρ` is not injective. ∎

## The q-difference lamplighter

Let `q >= 2` and `R = Z[1/q][x^(±1)]`. Let `K_q` be the group of affine maps of `R`
generated by:
- `X: f ↦ x·f`;
- `T: f(x) ↦ f(qx)`, the `q`-difference substitution, a ring automorphism;
- `e: f ↦ f + 1`.

Equivalently `K_q = V ⋊ ⟨X, T⟩` with `V = ⊕_(i∈Z) Z[1/q]·e_i`, where `X e_i = e_(i+1)` and
`T e_i = q^i e_i`.

1. **Structure.** `[X,T] = XTX^-1T^-1` acts on `V` as multiplication by `q^-1`, so `⟨X,T⟩` is the
   Heisenberg group `H_3(Z)`. `K_q` is 3-generated: conjugating `e` by `X^i` and `T^k`, and
   `e_0` by `X T X^-1`, gives all of `V`. It has derived length exactly 3. Indeed
   `K_q/V ≅ H_3(Z)`, and `[[X,T], e_1 − e_0] = (q^-1 − 1)(e_1 − e_0) ≠ 0` lies in `K_q''`.
2. **Not quasi-linear.** Apply the lemma with `x = T`, `y = X` and `m = q`, since
   `[T,X] = [X,T]^-1` acts on `V` as multiplication by `q`.
3. **Residually finite, and residually a finite set of primes.** Let `ℓ` be a prime not
   dividing `q`, and let `n` be a multiple of `ord_(ℓ^j)(q)`. Reducing lamps mod `ℓ^j` and positions mod `n`
   gives an `H_3(Z)`-equivariant finite quotient of `V`, because `q^n ≡ 1`. This separates
   any given element of `V` for `j` and `n` large. Elements outside `V` are separated through `K_q → H_3(Z)`. For `q = 2` and `ℓ = 3`, take `n = 2·3^J` with `J >= j−1`, so
   `K_2` is residually a finite `{2,3}`-group. This passes the bounded-composition-factor
   test (N1) of `every-fp-rf-group-embeds-in-fp-self-similar-group`.
4. **Word problem.** It is solvable, via the normal form `(v, X^a T^b [X,T]^c)`.

## Consequence

`K_q` is a finitely generated, residually finite, abelian-by-nilpotent group with solvable
word problem, and no quasi-linear route reaches it. Its Boone--Higman status is recorded in
`q-difference-lamplighter-satisfies-boone-higman`.
