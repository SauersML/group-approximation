## Statement

Let `V` be Thompson's group acting on the Cantor set `C = {0,1}^N`. Let `G` be a
finite germ extension of `V` in the sense of Belk--Hyde--Matucci
(arXiv:2407.03149; conditions quoted in `germ-complex-and-bux-gonzalez-morse-inputs`).
For `p ∈ C` put

    S_p = SingFix_G({p},{p}) = {g ∈ G : sing(g) ⊆ {p}, g(p) = p},
    K_p = RStab_V(p) = {v ∈ V : v is the identity on a neighbourhood of p}.

1. The germ map `S_p -> (G)_p`, `g ↦ (g)_p`, is onto, and its kernel is `K_p`.
2. `K_p` is a nontrivial simple group. So for every `1 ≠ k ∈ K_p`,
   `(G)_p ≅ S_p / ⟨⟨k⟩⟩`.
3. If `S_p` is finitely generated, so is `(G)_p`. If `S_p = ⟨X | R⟩` is a finite
   presentation, then `(G)_p = ⟨X | R, k⟩` for any word `k` representing a
   nontrivial element of `K_p`.

## Consequence for Theorem 2.1

Theorem 2.1 of Belk--Hyde--Matucci (label `thm:SingFixFiniteness`, quoted verbatim
in the artifact) asks, for type `F_n`, that `SingFix_G(M,M')` have type `F_n` for
all `M ⊆ M' ⊆ sing(G)` with `|M'| <= n`. For `n >= 1` this includes
`(M,M') = ({p},{p})` for every `p ∈ sing(G)`. So:
- whenever Theorem 2.1 is used to prove `G` finitely generated (`n = 1`), every
  singular germ group `(G)_p` is finitely generated;
- whenever it is used to prove `G` finitely presented (`n = 2`), every singular
  germ group `(G)_p` is **finitely presented**.

The paper states no such requirement, and none is needed for its Corollary 2.10
(there `(G)_p` is virtually `(B)_p`, or `(G)_p/(B)_p` is `F_n` with `(B)_p ≅ Z`
normal). It matters when one hopes, as in the germ implant U2 of
`research/artifacts/gq-gq-critic-pass-3.md`, that infinitely many relations of a germ
group can be absorbed by the locally trivial part `K_p`. They cannot: `K_p` is
normally generated by a single element, so it accounts for exactly one relator.

## Scope

- Nothing here says that `(G)_p` finitely presented makes `S_p` finitely presented.
  `K_p` is not finitely generated, so the lift is a separate problem.
- Nothing here constrains finite presentation of `G` proved by other means.
- **Remark (not proved here).** The same argument works for `V_{d,r}` with `d` even,
  where `V_{d,r}` and the groups `V[U]` below are simple (Higman). For `d` odd the
  kernel `K_p` has simple commutator subgroup of index at most `2`, so it is normally
  generated by at most two elements, and item 3 holds with two extra relators.
