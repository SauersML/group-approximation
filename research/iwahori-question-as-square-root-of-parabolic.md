Write `PSL_2(Z) = C_2 * C_3 = <x, y | x^2, y^3>` with `x = S`, `y = ST`, so that
the parabolic is `T = xy` and `V = [[1,0],[1,1]] = x y^2`.  Let
`t = diag(2,1)`, `V_1 = PSL_2(Z)`, `V_2 = t^(-1) V_1 t`, `B = V_1 cap V_2 =
Gamma^0(2)/+- = <T^2, V>`.  Then

```text
PSL_2(Z[1/2]) = < x, y, x', y' |  x^2 = y^3 = x'^2 = y'^3 = 1,
                                  (x y)^2 = x' y',            (T^2 = T')
                                  x y^2 = (x' y'^2)^2 >       (V  = V'^2)     (SR1)
```

(the involution relation `T^2 V^(-1) = T' V'^(-2)` is a consequence), and
equivalently, eliminating `x, x'`,

```text
PSL_2(Z[1/2]) = < T', V | V T'^(-4) V = T'^4,  (T'^(-2) V)^3 = 1,
                          V^2 T'^(-2) V^2 = T'^2,  (T'^(-1) V^2)^3 = 1 >,   (SR2)
```

with `T' = [[1, 1/2],[0,1]]`.  Swapping the two copies gives the mirror
form `T = T'^2`, `V' = V^2`.

**Square-root form of the gluing.**  Given a representation `(x, y)` of
`C_2 * C_3` with parabolic `T = xy` and Iwahori involution
`eps = x y^2 x y x = T V^(-2)`, a second copy `(x', y')` with `T = T'^2` and
`eps = eps'` exists iff `T` has a square root `s` with

```text
(eps s)^3 = 1,                                                         (SR3)
```

and then `x' = s^(-1) eps s`, `y' = s^(-1) eps T`.  In a congruence
representation `T` has odd order `m` and `s = T^((m+1)/2)` works.  So
Dogon--Vigdorovich's question asks whether, for an exact representation
`(x, y)` of `PSL_2(Z)` and an approximate square root `s` of `T` with
`(eps s)^3` approximately `1`, the data is close to a congruence
representation with its canonical root.

*Proof of `(SR1)`.*  `B` is generated by `T^2` and `V` (together with
`-I`), and `t^(-1) T^2 t = T`, `t^(-1) V t = V^2`, so the amalgam
`V_1 *_B V_2` is presented by the two copies and the identifications of
these two generators; `x = T^(-1) V T^(-1)` and `y = T^(-1) V` recover the
generators from `(T, V)` (and `x^2 = 1`, `y^3 = 1` become the two relations
of `(SR2)` for each copy).  The square-root form: from `T = T'^2` and
`eps = T V^(-2) = T'^2 V'^(-1)` one solves `V' = eps^(-1) T = eps T`,
`x' = T'^(-1) V' T'^(-1) = s^(-1) eps s`, `y' = T'^(-1) V' = s^(-1) eps T`,
and `y'^3 = 1` is `(eps T s^(-1))^3 = (eps s)^3 = 1` since `T s^(-1) = s`;
`x'^2 = 1` is automatic. ∎

**Sanity.**  `experiments/psl2_half_exotic_torch.py` verifies `(SR1)`
numerically on the permutation representation of `PSL_2(F_p)` on `P^1`,
and an autograd search from a random start finds exact solutions of
`(SR1)` with `T` of finite odd order in dimension `12`.
