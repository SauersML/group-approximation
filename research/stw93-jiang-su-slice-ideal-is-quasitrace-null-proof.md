Because `ι(E)⊆J_τ∩D=I` and `I` is an ideal of `D`, the ideal
`K_𝒵` generated by `ι(E)` is contained in `I`.

Fix a normalized `2`-quasitrace `q` on `D`.  Tensor-factor rigidity says that
`q∘ι` is the canonical limit quasitrace on `F(𝒵)`.  Hence, for every
`x∈E`,

```text
q(ι(x)*ι(x))=τ_ω(x*x)=0.
```

For a bounded `2`-quasitrace the null space

```text
N_q={y∈D:q(y*y)=0}
```

is a closed two-sided ideal.  Therefore `ι(E)⊆N_q` implies
`K_𝒵⊆N_q`.  If `k∈(K_𝒵)_+`, hereditary closure gives
`k^(1/2)∈N_q`, and thus `q(k)=0`.  This proves the common annihilation
statement.

Every normalized quasitrace on `D` therefore factors uniquely through
`π:D→D/K_𝒵`; conversely, pullback of a normalized quasitrace on the
quotient is a normalized quasitrace on `D`.  These operations are inverse, so
pullback is the asserted bijection.  Finally `K_𝒵⊆I` gives
`π(I)=I/K_𝒵`, and the bijection preserves vanishing on these two
ideals.  This proves the residual-obstruction equivalence and the
unique-quasitrace reduction.
