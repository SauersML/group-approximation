**Problem LXX of Schafhauser--Tikuisis--White, arXiv:2506.10902.**
Characterise abstractly the C\*-algebras stably isomorphic to graph
algebras of graphs with finitely many vertices.

## Attempts

* The classification side is complete (Eilers--Restorff--Ruiz--
  Sorensen geometric classification of unital graph algebras by
  filtered K-theory), so this is a pure RANGE recognition problem,
  and the invariant-side necessary conditions are known and finite:
  finitely many ideals in a linear-ish lattice with gauge-simple
  subquotients each AF or Kirchberg-like, `K_1` free of finite rank,
  `K_0` finitely generated with the unit-class and cokernel
  conditions of the adjacency presentation, real rank zero.  The
  honest gap is a REALIZATION theorem: which filtered K-theories with
  those constraints arise from finite integer matrices — a purely
  arithmetic Smith-normal-form-with-positivity question (prescribing
  `coker(I − Aᵗ)`, `ker(I − Aᵗ)` across the ideal filtration).  The
  abstract characterisation would then read: RR0 + finite ideal
  lattice with AF/Kirchberg gauge subquotients + realizable filtered
  K-data.  Flagged: the arithmetic realization step is elementary
  number theory over the ideal poset and looks closable with known
  Eilers-school technology; nothing conceptual blocks this problem.
