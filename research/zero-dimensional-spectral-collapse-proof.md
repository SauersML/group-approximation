## Why sufficient

`C*(v) ~= C(sigma(v))` by continuous functional calculus.  A compact
zero-dimensional Hausdorff space has a basis of clopen sets, so
`C(sigma(v))` is generated by the characteristic functions of clopen sets:
`C*(v)` is generated by its spectral projections `1_U(v)`.

Fix a clopen `U <= sigma(v)` and put `p = 1_U(v)`.  Functional calculus
commutes with unitary conjugation, so hypothesis 1 makes `p` commute with
`pi(s L s^{-1})`, and hypothesis 2 makes the `L`-conjugates of `p` — the
same functional-calculus function applied to the pairwise commuting
conjugates of `v`, all lying in one commutative C-star algebra — commute
pairwise.  `corona-projection-collapse` gives `[p, pi(L)] = 0`.

The commutant `pi(L)'` is a closed subalgebra containing every generator
`1_U(v)` of `C*(v)`, hence contains `C*(v)`, in particular `v`.
