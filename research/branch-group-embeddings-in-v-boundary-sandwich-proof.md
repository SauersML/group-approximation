## (a) Only boundary actions reach V

Let `G ↪ V` and fix a finite generating set `𝒜`.

**Step 1 (finitely many context-free orbits).** By (V1), `G` acts faithfully on a finite union `X_1 ⊔ … ⊔ X_k` of orbits, and each orbit has a context-free Schreier graph.

**Step 2 (one orbit is already faithful).** Let `N_i = ker(G ↷ X_i)`, a normal subgroup; then `∩ N_i = 1`. Suppose every `N_i ≠ 1`.
- **Each kernel contains a derived rigid stabilizer.** By the double commutator lemma (`l-double-comm`), a nontrivial `g ∈ N_i` moves some vertex `u`, so `N_i ⊇ rist(u)'`. By level-transitivity it contains every `rist(w)'` with `|w| = |u|`, hence `rist(n_i)'` for `n_i = |u|`.
- **The rigid stabilizers are nested.** Since `rist(n+1) ≤ rist(n)`, we get `∩ N_i ⊇ rist(max n_i)'`.
- **That group is nontrivial.** This is fact (F1) of `multi-ended-weakly-branch-actions-sandwich-proof`, valid for weakly branch groups.

This is a contradiction, so some `X_i =: G/H` carries a faithful action. Its Schreier graph `Γ = Γ_{G/H}` is context-free, and `G` equals the transition group `𝒢(Γ)`.

**Step 3 (exponential growth).** `Γ` is infinite, since `G` is infinite.
- If `Γ` had polynomial growth, (V3) would make `G = 𝒢(Γ)` elementary amenable. That contradicts (J) in case (B), and the hypothesis in case (W).
- So by (V2), `Γ` has exponential growth, and `𝒟(Γ)` has a type `t` lying on two distinct closed walks.
- End-cones of a type lying on a cycle are infinite, and distinct end-cones at the same depth are disjoint. So an end-cone of type `t` contains two disjoint infinite end-cones at some greater depth, and iterating gives infinitely many ends.
- In particular `e(Γ) ≥ 2`.

**Step 4 (sandwich).** Apply `multi-ended-branch-actions-are-boundary-sandwiched` to `H`. It gives `ξ` with `⊕_{v∈N(ξ)} K_v ≤ H ≤ G_ξ`.

## (b) Converse

Let `H ≤ G_ξ` have a context-free Schreier graph.
- `G` is level-transitive, hence minimal on `∂T`, so the orbit `ξG` is dense.
- An element acting trivially on a dense subset of `∂T` is trivial, because `G ≤ Aut(T)` acts faithfully on `∂T`.
- Hence `core_G(H) ≤ core_G(G_ξ) = 1`, and `G ↷ G/H` is a faithful context-free action.
- By `faithful-context-free-action-groups-embed-in-v` (BDPR Theorem A), `G ↪ V`.

## (c) Finite germs

In case (B), suppose the groups of germs are finite, and take `H` from Step 2.
- **Finite index.** The action on `G/H` is faithful and `e(Γ_{G/H}) = ∞` by Step 3. So (M) gives `[G_ξ : H] = m < ∞`, and `e(Γ̃_ξ) = ∞`.
- **Growth transfers down the cover.** The covering `p : Γ_{G/H} → Σ_ξ` has fibres of size `m` and maps `r`-balls into `r`-balls, so `|B_{Σ_ξ}(ξ, r)| ≥ |B_Γ(H, r)|/m`. Hence `Σ_ξ` has exponential growth.
- **The equivalence.** "`⟸`" is (b). "`⟹`" is the finite-index `H` just found.
- **The exclusion** is the contrapositive of the growth transfer.

## (d) Named consequences

- **Bounded automata.** A finitely generated branch group generated by a bounded automaton is contracting (N), so its groups of germs are finite (M, introduction). Bounded activity is polynomial activity, so every `Σ_ξ` has subexponential growth (Bo). Apply (c).
- **Polynomial activity with finite germs.** Same argument, with finite germs assumed.
- **Hanoi towers and Skipper's groups.** `H^(m)` and Skipper's `G_n` are branch and generated by bounded automata, as BDPR recall in §7. This recovers BDPR's corollary there without Manning's bottleneck computation.

## Calibration (Basilica, case (W))

- **Hypotheses hold.** Every normal subgroup of the Basilica group is finitely generated (BDPR `prop:Dominik_Basilica`), so (R) holds. The group is not elementary amenable (J, Basilica corollary).
- **What (a) gives.** A context-free, hence quasi-tree, graph `Γ_{G/H}` covering `Σ_ξ`.
- **The contradiction.** `Σ_ξ` is not a quasi-tree (BDPR's citation of the Basilica Schreier graph study), so (V4) forces `F_2 ≤ G`. But the Basilica group is contracting and has no free subgroups.
- **Conclusion.** The Basilica group does not embed in `V`, as BDPR prove.

## Relation to BDPR's closing remark

BDPR (`sec7_branch.tex`, the remark after the Basilica corollary) note that MNZ's Theorem B excludes contracting self-replicating branch groups. The argument above differs in two ways:
- **Different second input.** It uses MNZ's `t-multi-ended-graphs` together with growth of boundary orbits. It does not use the bound on ends via limit-space cut points.
- **No self-replicating hypothesis.** It needs no self-replicating assumption, and it also gives the positive direction (b).
