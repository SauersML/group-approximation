Direct proof. Not independently reviewed. Notation as in the target. Let `𝒟` be the class of
distortion-free groups.

**Step 1 (V ∈ 𝒟).**
- `V` is finitely generated.
- By `thompson-v-cyclic-subgroups-are-undistorted` (Bleak–Bowman–Gordon–Graham–Hughes–Matucci–
  Sapir, arXiv:1107.0672v3, Theorem 1.3), every infinite-order `α ∈ V` has
  `|α^m|_V ≥ κ|m| − C`.
- For a finitely generated `Λ = ⟨S⟩ ≤ V`, we have `|·|_V ≤ M_S |·|_S` with
  `M_S = max_{s∈S} |s|_V`. So `|α^m|_S ≥ (κ|m| − C)/M_S`.

**Step 2 (closure).**
- `𝒟` is closed under subgroups. A finitely generated subgroup of a subgroup is a finitely
  generated subgroup of the whole group.
- `𝒟` is closed under finite direct products and continuous wreaths, by Corollary 1 of
  `cantor-wreath-distortion-descends-to-fibre-or-base`.
- So `𝒟` is a class that contains the subgroups of `V` and has the three closure properties that
  define `𝒯`. By minimality, `𝒯 ⊆ 𝒟`.

**Step 3 (towers).**
- By induction, `T_j ∈ 𝒯`: we have `T_1 = V`, and `T_{j+1} = C(C,T_j) ⋊ V` is a continuous wreath
  with `T_j, V ∈ 𝒯` and `C` compact.
- `T_j ≤ jV` follows by induction from `cantor-maps-by-v-embed-in-next-brin-thompson-group`,
  applied with `Γ = T_j ≤ jV`.

**Step 4 (excluded subgroups).** For a distortion-free group, every finitely generated subgroup
has only linearly growing powers of infinite-order elements.
1. Let `N` be finitely generated, torsion-free, nilpotent of class `k ≥ 2`.
   - Then `γ_k(N) ≠ 1` is central and generated by the commutators `[x,y]` with `x ∈ N` and
     `y ∈ γ_{k−1}(N)`. Choose one of these, `c = [x,y] ≠ 1`.
   - `c` has infinite order, because `N` is torsion-free, and it commutes with `x` and `y`.
   - So `[x^n, y^n] = c^{n^2}` and `|c^{n^2}|_{\{x^{±1},y^{±1}\}} ≤ 4n`, which is not linear
     in `n^2`.
   - `H_3(Z)` is the case `N = H_3`.
2. In `BS(1,k) = ⟨s,u | usu^{-1} = s^k⟩`, `s` has infinite order and `|s^{k^n}| ≤ 2n+1`.
3. `|f^N| = o(N)` is excluded by definition.

**Step 5 (T_j ≠ jV).**
- For `j ≥ 2`, `jV` contains the Callard–Salo element (`brin-thompson-mv-contains-a-distortion-element`).
  Its distortion holds in a finitely generated subgroup of `jV`, and that subgroup is not
  distortion-free.
- If `ΦT_jΦ^{-1}` contained that element, then `T_j` would contain a conjugate of it.
- Distortion inside the conjugated finitely generated subgroup is the same function, so this
  contradicts `T_j ∈ 𝒟`.

**Where the descent stops.**
- Unwind Corollary 2 of the transfer claim along a tower. Each level passes a `√`-distorted
  element either to its base `V` or to its fibre `T_{j−1}`.
- After at most `j` levels the element sits in a finitely generated subgroup of `V`. This
  contradicts Step 1.
