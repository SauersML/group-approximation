Let `B` be a unital star algebra generated by projections and assume that
`B` has no Connes-embeddable tracial state.  Let `G` have a nontrivial
central involution `J`, put

```text
q=(1-J)/2,
```

and assume that `H=G/<J>` is torsion-free.  If there is a unital star
homomorphism

```text
Phi:B -> q C[G]q,                  Phi(1)=q,              (SAC1)
```

then `G` does **not** satisfy the Strong Atiyah conjecture over `C`.

Equivalently, whenever `G` satisfies Strong Atiyah and `G/<J>` is
torsion-free, the authenticated negative corner has no such model.  In fact
it has no proper algebraic projection at all:

```text
p=p*=p^2 in q C[G]q       implies       p=0 or p=q.        (SAC2)
```

This applies in particular to the exact gate
`nonhyperlinear-bloop-central-sign-corner`.  A Rips--Segev-type
non-unique-product quotient does not evade the obstruction merely by
failing unique product: any successful central double cover over that
torsion-free quotient must also fail Strong Atiyah.  For the Promislow
(Hantzsche--Wendt) group, every central double cover is excluded
unconditionally for the separate reason that it is amenable, so its group
von Neumann algebra and every central corner are Connes embeddable.

## Proof

Every finite subgroup `F<=G` maps to a finite subgroup of the torsion-free
quotient `H`.  Its image is therefore trivial, so

```text
F<=<J>.
```

Since `J` is nontrivial, the least common multiple of the orders of finite
subgroups of `G` is exactly

```text
lcm(G)=2.                                                 (SAC3)
```

Assume first that `G` satisfies the Strong Atiyah conjecture over `C`, and
let `p` be a projection in `q C[G]q`.  Regard `1-p` as the left-convolution
operator on `ell^2(G)`.  Its kernel is the range of `p`, so its von Neumann
dimension is the canonical group trace of `p`:

```text
dim_(N(G)) ker(1-p)=tau_G(p).                             (SAC4)
```

The Strong Atiyah conjecture and `(SAC3)` give

```text
tau_G(p) in (1/2) Z.                                     (SAC5)
```

Because `p=qpq`, it is a subprojection of `q`, while

```text
tau_G(q)=1/2.
```

Thus `(SAC5)` leaves only `tau_G(p)=0` or `tau_G(p)=1/2`.
Faithfulness of the canonical trace gives `p=0` in the first case.  In the
second case `q-p` is a projection of trace zero, so `p=q`.  This proves
`(SAC2)`.

Now suppose `(SAC1)` holds.  If every projection in one projection-generating
set of `B` were sent to `0` or `q`, the image of `Phi` would lie in `Cq`.
Identifying `q` with `1` would give a character `B->C`, hence a
one-dimensional Connes-embeddable tracial state on `B`, contrary to the
hypothesis.  Therefore some generator has image

```text
0<p<q.                                                    (SAC6)
```

The canonical trace is faithful, so `(SAC6)` gives

```text
0<tau_G(p)<1/2,                                          (SAC7)
```

contradicting `(SAC5)`.  Hence `G` fails Strong Atiyah.

For the Promislow application, that group is torsion-free and virtually
abelian.  A central extension by the finite group `<J>` is amenable.  If
`(SAC1)` existed, the normalized canonical trace on `qL(G)q`, pulled back
along `Phi`, would be a Connes-embeddable trace on `B`, because `L(G)` is
hyperfinite.  This is impossible.  Notice that this last argument does not
invoke Strong Atiyah: the established Cairn import
`elementary-amenable-torsion-free-strong-atiyah` is deliberately stated only
for torsion-free groups and therefore does not by itself certify the
2-torsion central cover `G`.  End proof.

## Exact boundary

This result does not assert that a central extension failing Strong Atiyah
contains the BCS corner; failure is only necessary.  It does show that the
familiar explicit non-unique-product groups are not a neutral supply of
support-cancellation patterns.  Over a torsion-free quotient, a successful
Bloop projection would itself be an explicit counterexample to Strong
Atiyah, with forbidden trace lying strictly between `0` and `1/2`.  Thus the
twisted-idempotent step is already a major open algebraic obstruction before
cross-context compatibility is considered.
