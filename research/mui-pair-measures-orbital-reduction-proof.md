Notation of the claim. Limits are along `U`. `B_n(g,h)` is the set of
`a in Y_n` with `s_n(gh)a != s_n(g)s_n(h)a`; soficity gives
`u_n(B_n(g,h)) -> 0`. TV is `sup_A |.|`.

## Lemma 0 (MUI measures ignore null sets)

If `lambda` is MUI and `B_n subset Y_n` with `u_n(B_n) -> 0`, then
`lambda_n((B_n x Y_n) cup (Y_n x B_n)) <= mu^1_n(B_n) + mu^2_n(B_n) -> 0`.
This is the definition of uniform absolute continuity of the marginals. ∎

## Proof of (2)

For `w = (a,b)` with `a, b notin B_n(g,h)`,
`psi_n(gh)w = psi_n(g)psi_n(h)w`. So the two push-forwards of `lambda_n`
agree off the image of a set of `lambda_n`-mass at most
`lambda_n((B x Y) cup (Y x B))`, and
`||psi_n(gh)_* lambda_n - psi_n(g)_* psi_n(h)_* lambda_n||_TV <= 2 lambda_n((B x Y) cup (Y x B)) -> 0`
by Lemma 0. ∎

## Proof of (2')

Call `lambda` *admissible* if it is MUI and internally `Gamma`-invariant.
Push-forward by `psi_n(g)` preserves MUI (the marginals are pushed by the
permutations `s_n(g)`, which preserve `u_n`). If `lambda` is admissible and
`t in P_Gamma`, then `psi_n(t^-1)_* lambda` is admissible: for `gamma in
Gamma`, `t gamma t^-1 in Gamma`, and by (2)
`psi(gamma) psi(t^-1)_* lambda ≈ psi(t^-1)_* psi(t gamma t^-1)_* lambda ≈ psi(t^-1)_* lambda`
in TV. Also `psi(gamma)_*` preserves admissibility (by (2),
`psi(gamma')psi(gamma) ≈ psi(gamma)psi(gamma^-1 gamma' gamma)`). Assume
the finite form: `psi(t)_*` preserves admissibility for `t in T`. Then
`psi(g)_*` preserves admissibility for every `g` in the group generated by
`Gamma` and `T`, which is `G` (write `g` as a word, apply (2) to compare
`psi(g)` with the product of the letters). So for admissible `lambda` and
any `g in G`, `psi(g)_* lambda` is internally `Gamma`-invariant, i.e.
`lambda` is internally `g^-1 Gamma g`-invariant (by (2) again). These
conjugates generate `N`, and invariance passes to words by (2); so `lambda`
is internally `N`-invariant. Conversely, if every admissible `lambda` is
internally `N`-invariant, then for `t in T` and `gamma in Gamma`,
`t^-1 gamma t in N`, so `psi(gamma) psi(t)_* lambda ≈ psi(t)_* psi(t^-1 gamma t)_* lambda ≈ psi(t)_* lambda`. ∎

## Proof of (1)

Fix `i`, write `mu_n = mu^i_n`, `f_n = f^i_n`. Since the coordinate
projection intertwines `psi_n(g)` with `s_n(g)`,
`||s_n(gamma)_* mu_n - mu_n||_TV <= ||psi_n(gamma)_* lambda_n - lambda_n||_TV -> 0`
for `gamma in Gamma`. In density form,
`||f_n o s_n(gamma)^-1 - f_n||_(L^1(u_n)) -> 0`.

*Truncation.* For `M > 0` put `g^M_n = min(f_n, M)`. The map `x -> min(x,M)`
is 1-Lipschitz and commutes with composition by a permutation, so
`||g^M_n o s_n(gamma)^-1 - g^M_n||_1 -> 0`. Uniform integrability gives
`eps(M) := lim_U ||f_n - g^M_n||_1 -> 0` as `M -> infinity`.

*Standard part.* Let `X` be the Loeb space of `(Y_n, u_n)` with the Loeb
action of `G` induced by `s_n` (a p.m.p. action, since `sigma` is a
homomorphism modulo Hamming-null sequences; it is sofic in Păunescu's sense,
the embedding `L^∞(X) ⋊ G -> prod_U M_(Y_n)` being the tautological one,
as used in `planted-lifts-defeat-hilbert-level-gaussian-soficity`). For a
uniformly bounded internal function `h = [h_n]`,
`||st(h)||_(L^1(X)) = lim_U ||h_n||_(L^1(u_n))` and
`st(h o s(g)^-1) = g . st(h)`. Hence `F_M := st(g^M) in L^∞(X)` satisfies
`gamma . F_M = F_M` for `gamma in Gamma`: `F_M in L^∞(X)^Gamma`.

*Theorem C.* `L^∞(X)^Gamma` is `G`-invariant. So for `g in G`,
`g^-1 . F_M` is `Gamma`-invariant, i.e. `F_M` is fixed by `g Gamma g^-1`.
The stabilizer of `F_M` is a subgroup, so it contains `N`.

*Back to the internal level.* For `u in N`,
`lim_U ||g^M_n o s_n(u)^-1 - g^M_n||_1 = ||u . F_M - F_M||_(L^1(X)) = 0`, and
therefore
`lim_U ||f_n o s_n(u)^-1 - f_n||_1 <= 2 eps(M)`. Letting `M -> infinity`,
`||s_n(u)_* mu_n - mu_n||_TV -> 0`. ∎

*Remark.* This is exactly where the one-point statement differs from the
pair statement. The density of a marginal is absolutely continuous with UI
density relative to `u_n`, so its truncations are bounded internal
functions and live in `L^∞(X)`. The pair measure `lambda_n` has no such
density relative to `u_n x u_n` (a permutation graph has density
`|Y_n|` on a set of density `1/|Y_n|`), which is why its standard part loses
the information (`mui-commutant-reduces-to-internal-joining-invariance`
(2)), and why (1) does not extend to pairs by the same argument.

## Proof of (3)

Assume `s_n|_Gamma` is a homomorphism. Then `gamma -> psi_n(gamma)` is a
genuine permutation representation of `Gamma` on `W_n`, and hence a unitary
representation on `l^2(W_n)`. Let `(S, kappa)` be a Kazhdan pair for
`Gamma` and `P_n` the orthogonal projection onto `psi_n(Gamma)`-invariant
vectors. `P_n` is averaging over `psi_n(Gamma)`-orbits (the image of
`Gamma` in `Sym(W_n)` is a finite group), so it preserves nonnegativity.

Let `xi_n = lambda_n^(1/2)` (unit vector in `l^2(W_n)` with counting
measure). By Lemma 2.1 of
`mui-commutant-reduces-to-internal-joining-invariance-proof` (Hellinger vs
TV), `eps_n := max_(s in S) ||psi_n(s) xi_n - xi_n|| <= (2||psi_n(s)_* lambda_n - lambda_n||_TV)^(1/2) -> 0`.
The Kazhdan inequality applied to `xi_n - P_n xi_n` (orthogonal to the
invariants, and `P_n` commutes with `psi_n(Gamma)`) gives
`||xi_n - P_n xi_n|| <= eps_n / kappa -> 0`. Put
`nu_n = (P_n xi_n)^2 / ||P_n xi_n||^2`. It is constant on
`psi_n(Gamma)`-orbits, i.e. orbital. Its Hellinger distance to `lambda_n`
tends to `0`, hence so does its TV distance.

TV-perturbations by `o(1)` preserve MUI: the marginals of `nu_n` differ from
those of `lambda_n` by `o(1)` in TV, and uniform absolute continuity along
`U` is insensitive to `o(1)` mass. Internal `u`-invariance is also
insensitive to `o(1)` TV changes, by the triangle inequality and
`||psi_n(u)_* (lambda_n - nu_n)||_TV = ||lambda_n - nu_n||_TV`. So `lambda`
is internally `N`-invariant iff `nu` is. Conversely every MUI orbital
measure is internally `Gamma`-invariant. This proves the equivalence with
(MUI-O) for all `u in N`.

Finally, `N` is generated by the conjugates `g^-1 gamma g`
(`g in G`, `gamma in S`). If a measure is internally invariant under
finitely many elements `g_1, ..., g_k`, it is internally invariant under
every word in them, by (2) and the triangle inequality. So internal
`N`-invariance is equivalent to internal invariance under every
`g^-1 gamma g`. ∎

**Orbit structure.** For a finite `Gamma`-set, orbits of the diagonal action
on `Gamma/H x Gamma/H'` are in bijection with `H \ Gamma / H'` via
`Gamma (xH, yH') -> H x^-1 y H'`. The pair orbit of a double coset `D` has
size `|Gamma/H| . |D/H'|`, and its uniform measure has marginals uniform on
`Gamma/H` and on `Gamma/H'`. A double coset with `|D/H'| = |H\D| = 1`
is the graph of an equivariant bijection `Gamma/H -> Gamma/H'`: these give
the permutation-graph measures of Theorem 4.1.

## Proof of (4)

If `s_n` is a homomorphism on `G`, the image `Q_n = s_n(G)` is a finite
group, and Lemma 4.2 of `mui-commutant-reduces-to-internal-joining-invariance-proof`
(finite collapse, which uses only that `Q_n` is a finite quotient of `G`)
gives `s_n(N) = s_n(Gamma)`. Every orbital measure is then exactly
`psi_n(N)`-invariant. ∎

## Where a proof of (MUI-O) must work

By (2') it suffices to show: for MUI orbital `nu` and `t in T`,
`psi_n(t)_* nu` is internally `Gamma`-invariant. It is already internally
`Lambda_t`-invariant: for `lambda' = t gamma t^-1`,
`psi(lambda') psi(t) = psi(t) psi(gamma)` off `(B x Y) cup (Y x B)` with
`u_n(B) -> 0`, which costs `o(1)` of `nu_n`-mass by Lemma 0. Its marginals
`s_n(t)_* mu^i_n` are internally `N`-invariant, by (1) applied to `nu` and
normality of `N`. So the push and its `Gamma`-average have the same
marginals up to `o(1)`; they differ, if at all, only in how they distribute
mass over the `Lambda_t`-orbits inside each `Gamma`-orbit of pairs. That is
the residual statement.
