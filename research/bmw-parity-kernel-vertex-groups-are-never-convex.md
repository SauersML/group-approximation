**Setting.** Take a one-vertex complete VH square complex with `m` horizontal letters `h_i`, `n`
vertical letters `v_j` (each letter's inverse is a letter) and one square `h v = v' h'` for each pair
`(h, v)`. Let `Γ` be its BMW group, `A = ⟨h_i⟩`, `B = ⟨v_j⟩`. Every `g ∈ Γ` is uniquely `g = a b`
(and uniquely `g = b' a'`) with `a, a' ∈ A` and `b, b' ∈ B` freely reduced. Let `Γ^+` be the parity
kernel (even `h`-length and even `v`-length), and `A^+ = A ∩ Γ^+`, `B^+ = B ∩ Γ^+`.

`B` acts on the set of reduced `A`-words, which form the rooted `m`-regular tree `T_A`. The action is
`b · a = a'`, where `b a = a' b'`. `A` acts on `T_B` in the same way. These are the two automaton
actions whose finiteness is the census reducibility certificate.

**Lemma (convex commensurated subgroups).** Let `G` be a left-ordered group, `H` a convex subgroup
and `g ∈ G` with `[H : H ∩ gHg^{-1}] < ∞`. Then `g^{-1} H g ⊆ H`. If `g^{-1}` also has this property,
then `gHg^{-1} = H`. So a convex subgroup that `G` commensurates is normal.

**Theorem.** Let `<` be a left order on `Γ^+`.
1. If `B^+` is convex, then `B^+` acts trivially on `T_A`, so the image of `B` in `Aut(T_A)` has
   order at most 2.
2. If `A^+` is convex, then the image of `A` in `Aut(T_B)` has order at most 2.

The same holds for every `Γ`-conjugate of `A^+` and `B^+` that lies in `Γ^+`. These conjugates are the
`Γ^+`-stabilizers of the vertices of the two trees.

**Corollary.** Suppose `Γ` is irreducible, meaning it has no finite-index subgroup that splits as a
direct product of two free groups. Then both automaton images are infinite (Burger–Mozes). So in any
left order of `Γ^+`, if one exists, no vertex stabilizer of either tree is convex.

In particular, no left order of `Γ^+` is lexicographic along the Zappa–Szép factorization
`Γ^+ = A^+ B^+`. That is, it cannot be obtained by ordering `Γ^+/B^+ ≅ A^+` invariantly and then `B^+`,
or the same with the roles swapped. This kills that construction for every irreducible class in every
degree. For reducible classes whose `B` acts trivially on `T_A`, the construction does apply.

**Theorem 2 (normal closures).** Let `C` be a convex subgroup of a left order on `Γ^+` with
`[B^+ : B^+ ∩ C] < ∞`. Then `C` contains `N_B`, the normal closure of `B^+` in `Γ^+`. Moreover
`Γ^+/N_B ≅ Q_B := A^+/M`, where `M` is normally generated by the relations `w = w'` for `w, w'` in one
`B^+`-orbit on the sphere `S_2(T_A)`. If `Q_B` is finite, then `C = Γ^+`. `Q_B = 1` whenever `B^+` is
transitive on `S_2(T_A)` and `m ≥ 3`. The same holds with `A` and `B` exchanged, and for conjugates.

So when `Q_A` and `Q_B` are both finite, every proper convex subgroup of every left order meets every
vertex stabilizer of both trees in infinite index. When `Q_B` is infinite, `Γ^+` maps onto the infinite
group `Q_B` with kernel `N_B`, and the obstruction stops there. If `N_B` and `Q_B` are both
left-orderable, the lexicographic order makes `N_B` convex, so Theorem 2 cannot exclude `C = N_B`.

**Computation of `Q_A`, `Q_B` over the census.** This covers the 1201 census classes that are not
certified reducible (`experiments/bmw-beyond-census-sampling-2026-09-19/qsphere.jsonl`, `qtally.py`).
- Both finite: 141 classes.
- Exactly one infinite: 902 classes.
- Both infinite: 158 classes. In every infinite case the abelian invariants contain `0`.

All three 40_40 survivors #193, #281 and #332 have both `Q_A` and `Q_B` infinite (`Q_A^{ab} ≅ Q_B^{ab} ≅ ℤ`). Their parity kernels are nevertheless not left-orderable, by the
subgroup-cone certificates of `bmw-40-40-193-281-332-parity-kernels-not-left-orderable`. So an
infinite `Q` does not indicate orderability.

**What this answers for the census program.** It gives a uniform restriction in all degrees. Any left
order of an irreducible parity kernel must have its maximal proper convex subgroup `C` (which exists,
since `Γ^+` is finitely generated) distinct from every vertex stabilizer. More strongly, `C` is not
commensurated by any `g ∉ N(C)`. The statement is proved in
`bmw-parity-kernel-vertex-groups-are-never-convex-proof`.

**Beyond the census degrees.** `experiments/bmw-beyond-census-sampling-2026-09-19/` holds 64 random classes in
the patterns 50_40, 60_30, 50_50, 51_41, 61_31, 52_42, 63_31 and 52_52. All are settled, solver-free:
- 27 are certified reducible.
- 33 are refuted by checked cone trees and 3 by RUP-checked cone lemmas.
- One, 50_40 seed 107, is irreducible by Thompson–Wielandt and SAT at radius 8. It is refuted by a four-term
  Klein chain of pure words, checked by `kverify.py`.

No counterexample appeared. Of the four certificate types, cone trees and cone lemmas are the universal ones,
by compactness. Klein and Klein-power chains are not: many refuted classes have no Klein pair. But the
universal types carry no radius bound, and on 50_40 seed 107 only a chain succeeded at a feasible size.
