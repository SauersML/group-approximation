# Intermediate-ring truncations preserve the Kun--Thom named return

## 1. The exact intermediate actor

Work in characteristic two and put

~~~text
P=F_2[x_1,x_2,x_3],
L=F_2[x_1^(+-1),x_2^(+-1),x_3^(+-1)],
Gamma=EL_3(P),
y=x_2/x_1,
h=e_12(y).
~~~

The map

~~~text
F_2[x_1,y,x_3] -> L,
(x_1,y,x_3) |-> (x_1,x_2/x_1,x_3)
~~~

is injective. Indeed, the monomial `x_1^a y^b x_3^c` maps to the Laurent
monomial `x_1^(a-b)x_2^b x_3^c`, and the exponent triple determines
`(a,b,c)`. We therefore identify its image with

~~~text
S=P[y]=F_2[x_1,y,x_3] subset L.
~~~

Let `H=<Gamma,h>`. We prove

~~~text
H=EL_3(S).                                                 (1)
~~~

The inclusion from left to right is immediate. For the reverse inclusion,
let `R_0` be the set of coefficients `a in S` such that `e_ij(a) in H`
for every ordered pair `i!=j`. Constant Weyl elements in
`EL_3(F_2) subset Gamma` move `e_12(a)` to every root, so `y in R_0`,
and plainly `P subset R_0`. Root additivity and the Steinberg commutator
identity give, for distinct `i,j,k`,

~~~text
e_ij(a+b)=e_ij(a)e_ij(b),
[e_ik(a),e_kj(b)]=e_ij(ab).                               (2)
~~~

Hence `R_0` is a subring of `S` containing `P` and `y`. It is all of
`S`, proving (1). This step is important: the models below satisfy the
relations of the entire group generated by `Gamma` and `h`, not just a
chosen list of pairwise covariance rows.

## 2. Finite truncations keep the return outside the positive core

In the polynomial coordinates `(x_1,y,x_3)`, the positive subring is

~~~text
P=span_F2 {x_1^a y^b x_3^c : a>=b}.                       (3)
~~~

One inclusion follows from `x_2=x_1y`; the reverse inclusion follows from

~~~text
x_1^a y^b x_3^c=x_1^(a-b)x_2^b x_3^c when a>=b.
~~~

For `N>=1`, set

~~~text
m=(x_1,y,x_3),
T_N=S/m^(N+1),
P_N=image(P->T_N),
Q_N=EL_3(T_N),
Gamma_N=EL_3(P_N).                                        (4)
~~~

The ring `T_N` is finite. The quotient on coefficients induces a
surjection

~~~text
q_N:H=EL_3(S) -> Q_N,                                     (5)
~~~

and `q_N(Gamma)=Gamma_N`. By (3), `P_N` is spanned by the surviving
monomials of total degree at most `N` whose exponents satisfy `a>=b`.
The degree-one monomial `y` violates this inequality, so

~~~text
ybar notin P_N.                                           (6)
~~~

Every entry of a matrix in `EL_3(P_N)` belongs to `P_N`. Consequently

~~~text
q_N(h)=e_12(ybar) notin Gamma_N                           (7)
~~~

for every `N>=1`.

## 3. The coset action is locally profinite

We need more than (7): canonical moments require eventual separation of
every fixed finite actor and coset table.

First, the maps `q_N` separate points of `H`. If `g!=g'`, some entry of
`g-g'` is a nonzero polynomial in `S`; for every `N` at least its total
degree, that entry survives unchanged in `T_N`.

Second, they separate `Gamma`-cosets. Suppose
`gGamma!=g'Gamma` and put `a=g'^(-1)g`. Then `a notin Gamma`. If every
matrix entry of `a` belonged to `P`, then `a in SL_3(P)`. Suslin's
elementary-generation theorem for polynomial rings over a field says

~~~text
SL_3(F_2[x_1,x_2,x_3])=EL_3(F_2[x_1,x_2,x_3]),             (8)
~~~

which would put `a` in `Gamma`, a contradiction. Thus an entry `f` of
`a` lies in `S minus P`. By (3), `f` contains a monomial
`x_1^a y^b x_3^c` with `a<b`. Choose `N` at least the total degree of
every monomial of `f`. Its image in `T_N` is still `f`, and its forbidden
monomial shows that it is not in `P_N`. Therefore `q_N(a)` has an entry
outside `P_N`, so

~~~text
q_N(a) notin Gamma_N,
q_N(g)Gamma_N != q_N(g')Gamma_N.                          (9)
~~~

The same conclusion holds for every larger `N`. Taking the maximum of
the finitely many degree bounds proves simultaneous injectivity on any
prescribed finite subset of `H` and on any prescribed finite subset of
`H/Gamma`.

## 4. Residually finite coherent wreath models

Let

~~~text
X=H/Gamma,
X_N=Q_N/Gamma_N,
W_H=(direct_sum_X C_2) rtimes H,
W_N=(direct_sum_(X_N) C_2) rtimes Q_N.                    (10)
~~~

The equivariant map

~~~text
gGamma |-> q_N(g)Gamma_N
~~~

induces a surjective homomorphism `pi_N:W_H->W_N`. On the Boolean lamp
group it sends a finitely supported function to the push-forward function;
coefficients at colliding sites are added modulo two.

These homomorphisms separate points. For an element `(f,g)` with `g!=1`,
choose `N` so that `q_N(g)!=1`. For `(f,1)` with nonempty finite support,
choose `N` so that all support cosets remain distinct by (9); then its
push-forward is nonzero. Hence `W_H` is residually finite through this
particular sequence.

Let `lambda_N` be the left regular representation of `W_N`. For every
fixed nonidentity `w in W_H`, the element `pi_N(w)` is nonidentity for all
sufficiently large `N`, and therefore

~~~text
tr(lambda_N(pi_N(w)))=0.                                  (11)
~~~

The identity has trace one. Thus the normalized regular characters converge
pointwise to the canonical character of `W_H`. By linearity, every fixed
mixed moment of actor words and lamp words also converges to its canonical
value. This proves the advertised canonical-moment audit, including any
finite packet of stabilizer relations and any finite list of later words
that already lie in `H`.

## 5. The exact half-projection wall

Write `o_N=Gamma_N in X_N`, let `a_z` be the order-two lamp at a site
`z`, and define

~~~text
V_N=lambda_N(a_(o_N)),
p_N=(1+V_N)/2,
U_N=lambda_N(q_N(h)),
r_N=U_N p_N U_N^*.
~~~

Every element of `Gamma_N` fixes `o_N`, so `p_N` commutes with the entire
image of `Gamma`, not merely a Kazhdan generating packet. On the other
hand, (7) says that `q_N(h)o_N!=o_N`. Hence

~~~text
r_N=(1+lambda_N(a_(q_N(h)o_N)))/2.
~~~

The two lamp elements at these distinct sites and their product are all
nonidentity. The regular trace therefore gives

~~~text
tr(V_N)=0,
tr(U_N V_N U_N^*)=0,
tr(V_N U_N V_N U_N^*)=0.                                 (12)
~~~

It follows that

~~~text
tr(p_N)=tr(r_N)=1/2,
tr(p_N r_N)=1/4,
||[p_N,U_N]||_2^2
 =||p_N-r_N||_2^2
 =tr(p_N)+tr(r_N)-2tr(p_Nr_N)
 =1/2.                                                     (13)
~~~

This is the exact `1/sqrt(2)` named-return wall.

## 6. Padding and perturbation

Let `D_N=|W_N|`, the dimension of `lambda_N`, and append arbitrary blocks
`p_N'` and `U_N'` of common dimension `s_N`. Orthogonality of direct-sum
blocks and (13) imply

~~~text
||[p_N direct_sum p_N',U_N direct_sum U_N']||_2^2
 = (D_N/(D_N+s_N))||[p_N,U_N]||_(2,D_N)^2
   +(s_N/(D_N+s_N))||[p_N',U_N']||_(2,s_N)^2
 >= D_N/(2(D_N+s_N)).                                     (14)
~~~

In particular, `s_N=o(D_N)` leaves the limit `1/2` for the squared wall.

For the perturbation estimate, let `p_tilde` be a contraction and
`U_tilde` a unitary. Expanding the difference of the two commutators and
using the operator-norm bounds gives

~~~text
||[p_tilde,U_tilde]-[p_N,U_N]||_2
 <=2||p_tilde-p_N||_2+2||U_tilde-U_N||_2.                 (15)
~~~

Combining (13) and (15) proves the claimed robust lower bound.

## 7. Exact boundary of the result

There is no conflict with
`kt-finite-ring-specializations-kill-the-named-laurent-return`. A quotient
of the full Laurent ring must send `x_1` to a unit, and finiteness then
expresses its inverse as a positive power. The map `S->T_N` used here does
not extend to the Laurent ring: `x_1` is nilpotent in `T_N`, while `y`
remains an independent degree-one variable. This is precisely why (7)
survives.

There is likewise no conflict with
`kt-full-kazhdan-majorana-return-collapses-in-fd`. That theorem uses a
genuine finite-dimensional representation of the full Laurent elementary
actor. The present quotients are genuine representations only of

~~~text
H=<Gamma,h>=EL_3(F_2[x_1,x_2/x_1,x_3]).
~~~

They prove a sharp negative result at the requested seam: exactifying every
relation in the smallest coherent packet `Gamma union {h}`, allowing
sublinear padding, and matching all of its canonical moments still cannot
place the named return on the rounded Gamma commutant. The missing
coherence consists of later Laurent words that force `x_1` to be invertible.
Those relations are absent from `H` and are indispensable to any global
Kun--Thom collapse argument. The non-hyperlinear-group root remains open.
