Sources: BZ = arXiv:2001.04579 (TeX on MSI, `gqsrc/bh-openq/2001.04579/`); FFWZ =
arXiv:2603.24687v2 (text on MSI, `/scratch.global/sauer354/bh-reviewer/2603.24687.txt`).

**Step 1. Ψ is well defined.** FFWZ define `SV_G^{abs}` (lines 437–455) as the groupoid
generated by the Brin--Thompson groupoid `SV` (colors `S`) and copies `G(n)` of `G`,
subject to a set of relations closed under composition and direct sum and containing, in
their words (N4): "x_{g.s} ∘ g = (g ⊕ g) ∘ x_s for all s ∈ S and g ∈ G" and
"(g_1 ⊕ ··· ⊕ g_n) ∘ p_σ = p_σ ∘ (g_{σ(1)} ⊕ ··· ⊕ g_{σ(n)}) for all σ ∈ Σ_n and
g_1, ..., g_n ∈ G". In `S'V_G`:
- the `S`-colored part of the groupoid `S'V` is a copy of `SV`, as the subgroupoid acting on
  the `C^S` factor and trivially on `C^G` (BZ §2, "Subsets of S and spectrum");
- twists compose (BZ Lemma `lem:Relations`(3));
- the two displayed relations are `lem:Relations`(7) and (6), valid for all `s ∈ S ⊆ S'`.

So the generators map compatibly, and `Ψ` exists.

**Step 2. Ψ is injective.** By FFWZ line 476, every element of `SV_G^{abs}` is
`h = F_−^{-1} ∘ p_σ ∘ (g_1, ..., g_n) ∘ F_+` with `F_±` forests in the colors `S`. Its image
maps the `i`-th leaf brick `B_i` of `F_+` onto the `σ(i)`-th leaf brick `B'_{σ(i)}` of `F_−`
by `canon_{B'_{σ(i)}} ∘ τ_{g_i} ∘ canon_{B_i}^{-1}`. The bricks are cut only in
`S`-coordinates.

Suppose `Ψ(h)` is the identity. Then:
- `B'_{σ(i)} = B_i` for each `i`, so the two canonical maps agree;
- hence `τ_{g_i}` is the identity of `C^{S'}`, so `g_i` fixes `S'` pointwise;
- since `G` acts freely on the summand `G ⊆ S'`, `g_i = 1`.

So `h = F_−^{-1} p_σ F_+` lies in the image of `SV`. Its image in `S'V_G` is the
`SV`-element `F_−^{-1} p_σ F_+`, acting on the `C^S` factor. That is a homeomorphism, and it
is the identity, so the `SV`-element is the identity of `SV`, and `h = 1`.

**Step 3. Image and kernel.** The image is generated by `S`-colored forests, permutations and
twists. By `lem:swap` (with the relation above) every such composite has the form
`f_2^{-1} t f_1` with `f_i` `S`-colored, which gives item 2. For `u = [T, id, (k_i), T]` with
`k_i ∈ K`: each `τ_{k_i}` fixes all `S`-coordinates, since `k_i` acts trivially on `S`, and
permutes the `C^G` coordinates by left translation. Forgetting `C^G` gives the formula
defining `SV_G → SV_(G/K)` (FFWZ Remark 2.13), which gives item 3.
