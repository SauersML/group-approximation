**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-algebra; elementary given the cited facts; not reviewed; no priority claimed).
The C*-analogue, proper infiniteness of `C(βP) ⋊_r P` iff `P` is non-amenable, is due to Rørdam–Sierakowski
(recalled, not re-read). Also recalled, not re-read:
- Whyte / Deuber–Simonovits–Sós: a finitely generated group is non-amenable iff it has an exactly 2-to-1 map
  to itself of bounded displacement;
- Elek–Szabó: group algebras of sofic groups are stably finite over every field;
- that `EL_3(F_2[t])` and `EL_3(F_2[t, t^(-1)])` are infinite Kazhdan groups (lattices in higher-rank groups
  over local fields; also Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain).

**Literature (read at source 09-18; priority).** Ara–Li–Lledó–Wu, arXiv:1607.00328 (*Amenability of coarse
spaces and K-algebras*, Bull. Math. Sci. 2018), Theorem 6.3: for a locally finite extended metric space `X`, `X`
is amenable iff its translation algebra `K_u(X)` is not properly infinite, iff it contains no unital
`L_K(1,n)`. Their proof of (4) ⇒ (1) is the partial-translation construction of Theorem 1(a) below, and
their (1) ⇒ (4) implies Theorem 1(b) through orbit representations.
Theorem 1 refines it. The paradoxical decomposition is read from a finite-alphabet SFT, so it already lives
in `LC(Z, k) ⋊ P` for every nonempty closed invariant `Z ⊆ X_par × Y`. That is what Track B needs.

`k` is a field. `L = L_k(1,2)` is generated by `x_1, x_2, y_1, y_2` with `y_i x_j = δ_ij` and
`x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2 = 1`. `LC(Z, k) ⋊ P` is the Steinberg algebra of the transformation groupoid `P ⋉ Z`, so
indicator functions of compact open bisections multiply as bisections: `1_U 1_V = 1_(UV)`, and `1_U^* := 1_(U^(-1))`.

## Theorem 1 (Leavitt dichotomy)

Let `P` be finitely generated.
- **(a)** If `P` is non-amenable, there is an SFT `X_par` over `P` with this property. For every compact
  totally disconnected `P`-space `Y` and every nonempty closed invariant `Z ⊆ X_par × Y`, there is a unital
  embedding `L → LC(Z, k) ⋊ P`. Taking `Y` a free subshift and `Z` minimal gives free minimal examples.
- **(b)** If `P` is amenable, then for no nonempty compact metrizable totally disconnected `P`-space `Z`
  does `LC(Z, k) ⋊ P` contain a unital copy of `L`.

**Proof of (a).**
1. Fix an exactly 2-to-1 map `φ : P → P` with displacement at most `R`. The alphabet is `B(R)`. Let `X_par` be
   the set of `x ∈ B(R)^P` such that `v ↦ v·x(v)` is exactly 2-to-1.
2. The preimages of `u` lie in `u·B(R)`, so the condition is checked on balls of radius `2R`, and `X_par` is
   an SFT. It is shift invariant: `φ_(λx) = λ ∘ φ_x ∘ λ^(-1)`. It is nonempty, since `x(v) = v^(-1)φ(v)` lies in it.
3. Order `B(R)`. Then `u` has a first and a second preimage `f_1^x(u)` and `f_2^x(u)`, both read from `x` on
   `u·B(R)`.
4. On `Z` this gives, for `i = 1, 2`, a locally constant cocycle `c_i : Z → B(R)` and a compact open bisection
   `U_i = {(c_i(z)^(-1), z)}` of `P ⋉ Z`. Its source map is bijective onto `Z`, and its range map is a
   homeomorphism onto a clopen set `D_i`: in each orbit, `U_i` acts as `f_i` does on `P`. Here
   `D_1 ⊔ D_2 = Z`, because every point has exactly two preimages.
5. Hence `U_i^(-1)U_i = Z`, `U_i^(-1)U_j = ∅` for `i ≠ j`, and `U_iU_i^(-1) = D_i`. So `x_i ↦ 1_(U_i)` and
   `y_i ↦ 1_(U_i^(-1))` satisfy the Leavitt relations, with `1_(D_1) + 1_(D_2) = 1`.
6. `L` is simple, so this unital homomorphism is injective. Nothing about `Y` or `Z` was used beyond
   `Z ≠ ∅`. ∎

**Proof of (b).**
1. Suppose `ι : L → LC(Z, k) ⋊ P` is unital. Choose a minimal `Z' ⊆ Z`. Restriction to `Z'` is a unital ring
   homomorphism, and `L` is simple, so the composite `L → LC(Z', k) ⋊ P` is still injective.
2. If `Z'` is infinite, `LC(Z', k) ⋊ P` embeds in a rank ultraproduct
   (`amenable-minimal-crossed-products-have-faithful-rank-models`), so it is directly finite.
3. If `Z' = P/H` is finite, `LC(Z', k) ⋊ P ≅ M_[P:H](k[H])` (imprimitivity, recalled). This is directly
   finite by Elek–Szabó, since `H` is amenable, hence sofic.
4. But `y_1 x_1 = 1 ≠ x_1 y_1` in `L` (`binary-leavitt-algebra-not-directly-finite`). ∎

## Theorem 2 (the Leavitt tensor is absorbed by the scaffold)

For every compact totally disconnected `Λ`-space `Y`, Theorem 1(a) for `F_2`, tensored with the identity,
gives a unital embedding
`(LC(Y, k) ⋊ Λ) ⊗ L ↪ (LC(Y, k) ⋊ Λ) ⊗ (LC(X_par, k) ⋊ F_2) ≅ LC(Y × X_par, k) ⋊ (Λ × F_2)`.
Tensoring over a field preserves injectivity. `Y × X_par` is effective when `Y` is. Multiplying by a free
subshift makes the action free.

- **The oracle host becomes Leavitt-free.** The host `(LC(Y) ⋊ Z) ⊗ L` of
  `decidable-algebras-embed-in-oracle-leavitt-crossed-products` lies unitally in `LC(W) ⋊ (Z × F_2)` with
  `W = Y × X_par` effective.
- **The oracle gets more room there.** A general effective `W ⊆ X_par × A^(Z × F_2)` may label the Leavitt
  coordinate as well. In the tensor product the oracle is read only along `Z`, and every Leavitt word sees the
  same tape. Over `Z × F_2` the tape is indexed by the stack too, so Leavitt moves can read data attached to
  the current Leavitt word. This is the interleaving that `amenable-crossed-product-units-are-linear-sofic`
  asked for, for non-linear-sofic inputs.
- **Track B takes the shape of Track A.** Its host becomes a crossed product over one fixed non-amenable
  scaffold, `Λ × F_2`. This scaffold is torsion-free and Farrell–Jones when `Λ` is, since Farrell–Jones is
  stable under products (recalled). So M1, E1 and E2 are the same gates in both tracks, posed over one
  scaffold of the form `Λ' × Z²`.

## Theorem 3 (the Kazhdan and soficity filters are permutational)

Let `k = F_2`.
1. **Amenable scaffold, `Λ = Z`.** For every nonempty compact totally disconnected `Z`-space `W`, `t ↦ u`
   embeds `F_2[t, t^(-1)]` in `LC(W) ⋊ Z`, since the powers `u^n` are a basis over `LC(W)`. So `GL_3(LC(W) ⋊ Z)`
   contains the infinite Kazhdan group `EL_3(F_2[t, t^(-1)])`, and faithfully so on every minimal subsystem.
   - By contrast, `[[Z ⋉ W]]` has no infinite Kazhdan subgroup when `W` is free
     (`kazhdan-groups-avoid-full-groups-of-subexponential-actions`).
   - Every subgroup of `GL_n` is linear sofic when `W` is minimal
     (`amenable-crossed-product-units-are-linear-sofic`).
2. **Virtually free scaffold, `Λ = F_2`.** Take `W ⊆ X_par × Y_free` nonempty, closed and invariant. By
   Theorem 1(a):
   - `GL_1(LC(W) ⋊ F_2)` contains `L^x`, which is finitely presented, simple and non-sofic
     (`openai-leavitt-unit-nonsofic`), and contains Thompson's `V` (recalled);
   - `GL_3` contains `EL_3(F_2[t])`, which is infinite and Kazhdan, via `t ↦ x_1`. This is injective because
     `x_1^n e_η = e_(1^n η)` separates the powers on the orbit of `η = 2^∞`.

   On the other hand, every Kazhdan subgroup `K` of the full group `[[F_2 ⋉ W]]` is finite:
   - `K` acts on each orbit `≅ F_2` by a bounded-displacement realization;
   - items 5–6 of `kazhdan-inputs-need-one-ended-nonamenable-wobbling-scaffolds` leave only finite orbits,
     of uniformly bounded size, because `sep_(F_2)` is bounded;
   - `K` is finitely generated and acts faithfully, so it embeds in finitely many `Sym(≤ N)`.

So over the tree the gap is maximal. The full group has only finite Kazhdan subgroups, while `GL_3` of the
same ring has infinite ones and `GL_1` has a non-sofic one.

## What this changes for gate U

- The caps found so far all apply to realizations by permutation matrices on scaffold orbits:
  - growth and separation (`translation-like-hosts-are-capped-by-separation-profiles`);
  - ends, finite cuts and Følner sets (the wobbling-scaffold node).
  The open test `infinite-kazhdan-group-in-wobbling-group-of-product-of-trees` tests only the permutational
  gate.
- For the **linear gate**, `Γ ↪ GL_n(LC(W, F_2) ⋊ Λ)` with `W` effective:
  - the only known filter is linear soficity over amenable `Λ`;
  - Theorem 1 says that filter disappears exactly when `Λ` is non-amenable;
  - over `Λ = F_2`, the smallest non-amenable scaffold, it is not capped by Kazhdan, expanders or soficity.
- What gate U asks for in linear form: for each decidable `Γ`, say for each canonical input `H_Γ`, a faithful
  module with a basis of bounded support whose support graph lies on the scaffold, with effectively coded
  coefficients. This is a *linear move table*. The support graph is not a Schreier graph. For
  `EL_3(F_2[t]) ⊆ GL_3(L)` it is three rays, even though the group's finite quotients are expanders.

## Lesson for general BH

A Følner set or a finite cut in an orbit gives a commensurated subset. Property FW collapses a commensurated
subset to a trivial one, and that is the whole mechanism behind the Kazhdan, ends and separation kills of
gate U. The linear analogue, a commensurated subspace, can be a nontrivial submodule: `t^n F_2[t]^3` for
`SL_3(F_2[t])`. Nothing collapses it.

So the caps on gate U are caps on permutation matrices. For invertible matrices over the same crossed
product, the scaffold matters only through amenability. Exactly at non-amenability, the Leavitt algebra, and
with it non-soficity, becomes internal: a paradoxical decomposition is an SFT.

Gate U should therefore be posed linearly, over one fixed non-amenable Farrell–Jones scaffold with oracles
indexed by the whole scaffold. Posed that way, Track B is a crossed product over one scaffold, like Track A,
and the two tracks share their dynamical gates.
