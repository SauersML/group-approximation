Choose orthogonal equivalent projections `p,w in N` of trace `t>0`, and a
projection `f` orthogonal to `p+w`, of trace `s-t`, where

```text
2t<=s,       s+t<1.
```

Put `E_0=p+f`.  In the `2`-by-`2` corner generated by `p,w`, let `u_theta`
be the usual rotation through angle `theta`, fixing `f`, and set

```text
p_1(theta)=p,       p_2(theta)=u_theta p u_theta^*,
F_theta=u_theta E_0u_theta^*=p_2(theta)+f.
```

Then `E_0` lies in the first carrier branch and `F_theta` lies in the
second, while

```text
||E_0-F_theta||_2 ->0       as theta->0.               (HC1)
```

For every `theta>0`, however,

```text
p join p_2(theta)=p+w.
```

Hence every trace-`s` projection `G` in the intersection of the two
branches contains `w`.  Since `w` is orthogonal to `E_0`,

```text
||G-E_0||_2>=||(G-E_0)w||_2=sqrt(t).                  (HC2)
```

The two branches are closed.  A path in their union from `E_0` (which is
not in the second branch for `theta>0`) to `F_theta` (which is not in the
first) must meet their intersection: otherwise its two closed preimages
would disconnect the interval.  Equation `(HC2)` says every such path
leaves the `sqrt(t)`-ball about `E_0`, whereas `(HC1)` makes its endpoints
arbitrarily close.  This disproves equi-`LC^0`, hence also equi-`LC^1`.

For the simplex-coordinate variant, at `theta=0` the point

```text
((1/2,1/2),E_0)
```

is admissible because the two carriers coincide.  If `theta>0` and both
coordinates remain positive, admissibility requires a projection in the
intersection, so `(HC2)` prevents convergence to that point.  The
multifunction is therefore not lower semicontinuous.
