## Statement

Let `S = ⟨T, s⟩ ≤ Homeo^+(S^1)` be Lodha's group, with `S^1 = R ∪ {∞}`.
- `T` is the group of piecewise `PSL_2(Z)` homeomorphisms with breakpoints in
  `Q ∪ {∞}`.
- `s` is the map of l.114–120: `t ↦ 2t/(1+t)` on `[0,1]`, `t ↦ 2/(3−t)` on `[1,2]`,
  and the identity elsewhere.

Then `S` preserves `X = P^1(Q) = Q ∪ {∞}`, and the action of `S` on `X` is of type (A)
in the sense of `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`:
1. the action is faithful;
2. `S` is finitely presented;
3. `Stab_S(∞)` is finitely generated, hence so is every point stabilizer;
4. there is exactly **one** orbit of two-element subsets. In fact `S` is transitive
   on ordered pairs of distinct points.

**Consequences.**
- By Zaremsky's Corollary B, the twisted Brin–Thompson group `SV_S` of this action
  is finitely presented and simple, and it contains `S`.
- So `S` satisfies permutational Boone–Higman. It is a positive instance of
  Zaremsky's Question 5.13(i), "Does every finitely presented simple group admit an
  action of type (A)?", and of BFFHZ Question 3.4
  (`fp-simple-groups-embed-in-fp-simple-mif-groups`), through
  `bh-implies-pbh-iff-fp-simple-groups-satisfy-pbh`.
- It is also a new line for the Q3.4 census, which had Lodha's group unchecked.

## Proof

**Inputs from the source.**
- (L1) `S` is finitely presented and simple (Theorem 1, l.89–104; Theorem `main`,
  l.1341; the simplicity section, l.1438ff).
- (L2) *Cantor model* (l.320–415). There is a continuous surjection
  `Φ: 2^N → R ∪ {∞}` that semiconjugates the Cantor action to the circle action.
  - `F, T, S` act on `2^N` through tree diagrams and the percolating maps `y_s`
    (localizations of `y`).
  - `Φ(ξ)·s = Φ(ξ·y_{10} y_{110}^{-1})`, and `S` is generated by `x, x_1, p_0` and
    `y_{10} y_{110}^{-1}`.
- (L3) *Generators and standard forms* (l.622–760).
  - For independent `σ, τ ∈ 2^{<N}` put `w_{σ,τ} = y_σ y_τ^{-1}`. Every `w_{σ,τ}`
    lies in `S` (lemma at l.631).
  - Every element of `S` is a standard form `f w_{s_1,t_1}^{l_1} ⋯ w_{s_n,t_n}^{l_n}`
    with `f ∈ T` (Lemma `stdform`, l.739).
  - Relation (4): `w_{σ,τ} x_u = x_u w_{σ·x_u, τ·x_u}` whenever `x_u` acts on `σ, τ`.
- (L4) `F` is the stabilizer of `∞` in `T`, and `T` acts 2-transitively on `P^1(Q)`.
  This is standard. `PSL_2(Z) ≤ T` is transitive on `P^1(Q)`, and `Stab_T(∞) = F`, the
  piecewise `PSL_2(Z)` homeomorphisms of `R` with rational breakpoints (l.140–155). In
  this model `F` acts transitively on `Q`: Minkowski's `?` conjugates it to the
  piecewise-linear `F` acting on dyadic rationals.

**(0) `S` preserves `X`.** The pieces of `s` are the matrices
`[[2,0],[1,1]]` and `[[0,2],[−1,3]]` of `PGL_2(Q)`, with breakpoints `0, 1, 2`. So `s`
maps `Q ∪ {∞}` onto itself, and so does `T`.

**(1) Faithful.** `S ≤ Homeo(S^1)` and `X` is dense in `S^1`.

**(2) Finitely presented.** This is (L1).

**(4) One orbit on pairs.** `T ≤ S` is transitive on ordered pairs of distinct points
of `X`, by (L4).

**(3) The stabilizer of `∞` is `⟨F, w_{σ,τ}⟩`.** We have `Φ^{-1}(∞) = {0^∞, 1^∞}`:
- `φ(1ξ) = 1 + φ(ξ)` and `φ(0ξ) = 1/(1 + 1/φ(ξ)) ≤ 1` force `φ(ξ) = ∞` only for
  `ξ = 1^∞`;
- `Φ(0ξ) = −φ(ξ̃)` then gives `0^∞`.

Every `y_u` fixes `0^∞` and `1^∞`:
- if `u` is not constant, the cone `u` contains neither point;
- `y(1η) = 11 y(η)` forces `y(1^∞) = 1^∞`;
- `y(00η) = 0 y(η)` forces `y(0^∞) = 0^∞`.

So every `w_{σ,τ}` fixes `∞`. Independence excludes `σ = ∅` unless `σ = τ`.

Now write `g ∈ S` as a standard form `f w⋯w` with `f ∈ T`, by (L3). Then
`∞·g = ∞` exactly when `∞·f = ∞`, i.e. when `f ∈ F`. Hence

    Stab_S(∞) = ⟨F ∪ {w_{σ,τ} : σ, τ independent}⟩.

**(3′) Finitely many `w` suffice.** Take `f ∈ F` that acts by prefix replacement on
both `σ` and `τ`. The same computation as relation (4) gives
`f^{-1} w_{σ,τ} f = w_{σ·f, τ·f}`, as homeomorphisms.

Order the pair so that `σ < τ` in the tree order; `w_{τ,σ} = w_{σ,τ}^{-1}` covers the
other order. Record which of three regions is empty:
- the region before `σ`, empty iff `σ = 0^k`;
- the region between `σ` and `τ`, empty iff they are consecutive;
- the region after `τ`, empty iff `τ = 1^k`.

Two pairs with the same emptiness pattern are `F`-related. Put `σ, τ` into a finite
prefix set `L` and `σ', τ'` into a prefix set `R`, then subdivide leaves other than
`σ, τ` (resp. `σ', τ'`) until the numbers of leaves before, between and after agree.
The tree diagram `(L, R) ∈ F` then maps `σ ↦ σ'` and `τ ↦ τ'`.

So there are at most `2^3 = 8` classes of `w_{σ,τ}` up to conjugation by `F`, and

    Stab_S(∞) = ⟨x, x_1, w_{r_1}, …, w_{r_8}⟩,

with `r_i` one representative pair per class. It is finitely generated. `S` is
transitive on `X` (item (4)), so every point stabilizer is conjugate to this one. ∎

## Remarks

- **A projective dilation.** The class with all three regions empty is `(σ,τ) = (0,1)`.
  Here `y_1` is `t ↦ 2t` on `[0,∞]` and `y_0` is `t ↦ t/2` on `(−∞,0]`. This uses
  `φ y φ^{-1}(u) = 2u`, read off from `Φ·c = Φ·y_{10}` with `c(t) = 2t/(1+t)`, and
  `φ(ξ̃) = 1/φ(ξ)`. So `w_{1,0} = y_1 y_0^{-1}` is the dilation `t ↦ 2t` of the whole
  circle, and `S ∋ diag(2,1)`.
- **Not highly transitive.** `S` preserves the cyclic order, so the action on `X` is
  not 3-transitive. PBH comes from type (A) directly, not through high transitivity.
- **Same mechanism for other circle groups.** It applies to any group
  `⟨T, W⟩ ≤ Homeo^+(S^1)` with
  (a) a normal form `T·⟨W⟩` in which `W` fixes `∞`, and
  (b) the conjugation action of `F` on `W` having finitely many orbits.
  For such a group, finite presentation is the only remaining clause of type (A).
