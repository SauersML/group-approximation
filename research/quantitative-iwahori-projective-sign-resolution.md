Fix a finite presentation

```text
C=<S | R>
```

and a subgroup `B<C` generated by fixed words `T_B` in `S`.  Assume that
restriction of sign characters is injective:

```text
Hom(C,{+1,-1}) -> Hom(B,{+1,-1}).                       (QIS1)
```

Let `L_R` be the maximum relator length and `L_B` the maximum length of the
chosen words in `T_B`.  For a unitary assignment `rho:S->U(d)`, extend `rho`
to words multiplicatively, and put

```text
delta=max_(r in R)||rho(r)-I||_2.
```

For a projection `P`, set `V=2P-I` and

```text
e_s=1-|tr(V rho(s) V rho(s)^*)|^2,
a=max_(s in S) sqrt(2 e_s),
beta=max_(t in T_B)||[P,rho(t)]||_2.                    (QIS2)
```

If

```text
L_R a+2 delta<2,              L_B a+2 beta<2,           (QIS3)
```

then

```text
max_(s in S)||[P,rho(s)]||_2 <= a/2.                    (QIS4)
```

All norms and constants are normalized and dimension independent.
Consequently, for any sequence of approximate representations with relator
defect tending to zero, projective trace-square energy tending to zero on
`S`, and ordinary commutators tending to zero on `T_B`, the projection
commutes asymptotically with the whole vertex group.

For `C=SL_2(Z)` and either index-three Iwahori subgroup `B`, `(QIS1)` holds.
Indeed `SL_2(Z)^ab` is cyclic of order twelve, so there is one nontrivial sign
character; it is sign after reduction to `SL_2(F_2)=S_3`, and its restriction
to the order-two point stabilizer, hence to its Iwahori inverse image, is
nontrivial.  Therefore `(QIS4)` applies to the approximate second vertex in
the canonical Iwahori amalgam microstates.

This is the finite-level orientation theorem needed after projective
commutant transfer.  No exactification of the vertex representation and no
coordinate-Pauli orientation gadget are required.
