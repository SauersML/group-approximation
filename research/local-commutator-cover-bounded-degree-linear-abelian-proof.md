The notation is that of the target.  Fix `n >= 1`.

## Cited inputs

- **(C1)** From `local-commutator-cover-derived-series-stops-at-one`,
  verbatim: "There are an integer `L_1` and words `t_1, ..., t_d` in
  `[F,F]`, with `d <= r + s + r^2`, such that for every `L >= L_1` the
  following hold.  1. **Finite generation.**  The commutator subgroup
  `Gamma_L'` is generated by the images of `t_1, ..., t_d`.
  2. **Perfectness.**  `Gamma_L'` is perfect."
- **(C2)** From `abelianized-cover-mf-radical-is-union-of-local-cover-radicals`,
  verbatim: "Then `N_L` is finitely normally generated, `U_L N_L = [R,R]`".
  The `N_L` increase with `L`.
- **(C3)** From `abelianized-cover-commutator-is-finitely-generated-perfect`,
  item 4, verbatim: "every homomorphism `T -> U(d)`, for every finite `d`,
  is **trivial**; that is, `Rad_fd(T) = T`."
- **(A1) Standard commutative algebra.**  A finitely generated commutative
  ring `A_0` is residually finite: for every `a != 0` in `A_0` there is an
  ideal `I` of finite index with `a` not in `I`.

  Proof from textbook facts.
  - `A_0` is Noetherian, by the Hilbert basis theorem.
  - Given `a != 0`, the ideal `Ann(a)` is proper.  Choose a maximal ideal
    `m` containing it.  Then `a/1 != 0` in the localization `(A_0)_m`.
  - By the Krull intersection theorem in the Noetherian local ring
    `(A_0)_m`, `a/1` does not lie in `m^k (A_0)_m` for some `k`.  Hence `a`
    does not lie in `m^k`.
  - The field `A_0/m` is finitely generated as a ring, hence finite.  This
    is Zariski's lemma over `Z`: a field finitely generated as a
    `Z`-algebra is finite.
  - Each `m^i/m^(i+1)` is a finitely generated module over the finite field
    `A_0/m`, hence finite.  So `A_0/m^k` is finite, and `I = m^k` works.

## Step 1: T has no nontrivial finite quotient

If `T ->> K` with `K` finite and nontrivial, then composing with the left
regular representation `K -> U(|K|)` gives a nontrivial homomorphism
`T -> U(|K|)`.  This contradicts (C3).

## Step 2: every homomorphism T -> GL_n(A) is trivial

Let `A` be a commutative ring and `rho : T -> GL_n(A)` a homomorphism.

- `T` is finitely generated by (C1).  Its images under the surjection
  `Gamma_L' ->> T` are generated by the images of the `t_j`, because a
  surjection of groups maps commutator subgroup onto commutator subgroup.
- Let `A_0` be the subring generated by the entries of the matrices
  `rho(t_j)^(+-1)`.  It is finitely generated, and `rho(T) <= GL_n(A_0)`.
- Suppose `g = rho(t) != 1`.  Some entry `a` of `g - 1` is nonzero.  By
  (A1) there is a finite-index ideal `I` of `A_0` with `a` not in `I`.
- Reduction mod `I` is a group homomorphism `GL_n(A_0) -> GL_n(A_0/I)`
  into a finite group.  It does not kill `g`.
- So the composite `T -> GL_n(A_0/I)` has nontrivial finite image,
  contradicting Step 1.

Hence `rho` is trivial.

## Step 3: the representation ring of the free group on the t_j

Let `Phi` be free on `tau_1, ..., tau_d`, and let `theta : Phi -> F` send
`tau_j` to `t_j`.  Put

```text
K_L = theta^(-1)(N_L),      K_inf = theta^(-1)([R,R]).
```

- By (C2) the `K_L` increase with `L` and their union is `K_inf`.
- For `L >= L_1`, the composite `Phi -> F -> Gamma_L` has image
  `Gamma_L'`.  The image contains the generators of `Gamma_L'` by (C1), and
  is contained in `Gamma_L'` because each `t_j` lies in `[F,F]`.  So
  `Phi/K_L = Gamma_L'`.
- In the same way `Phi -> E` has image `T` and kernel `K_inf`, so
  `Phi/K_inf = T`.

Let `O = Z[y^(j)_ab : 1 <= j <= d, 1 <= a,b <= n][delta^(-1)]`, where
`delta` is the product of the determinants of the generic matrices
`Y_j = (y^(j)_ab)`.  So each `Y_j` lies in `GL_n(O)`, and every `w` in `Phi`
has an evaluation `w(Y)` in `GL_n(O)`.  Let `I_L` be the ideal of `O`
generated by the entries of `w(Y) - 1` for `w` in `K_L`.

**(U) Universal property.**  Let `A` be a commutative ring.  Sending a ring
homomorphism `f : O -> A` to the homomorphism `tau_j |-> f(Y_j)` is a
bijection:

```text
{ ring homs O -> A }  <->  Hom(Phi, GL_n(A)).
```

Under it, `f` kills `I_L` iff the corresponding homomorphism kills `K_L`.

- It is a bijection because a ring homomorphism out of `O` is exactly a
  choice of `d` matrices over `A` with invertible determinants.
- `f` induces a group homomorphism `GL_n(O) -> GL_n(A)`, so
  `f(w(Y)) = w(f(Y))`.
- So `w(f(Y)) = 1` iff `f` kills every entry of `w(Y) - 1`.

## Step 4: the certificate lives at one finite level

Let `I_inf` be the union of the increasing chain `I_L`.  It is an ideal,
and it contains the entries of `w(Y) - 1` for every `w` in
`K_inf = U K_L`.

Let `A_inf = O/I_inf` and let `f_inf` be the quotient map.
- By (U), the homomorphism `tau_j |-> f_inf(Y_j)` kills `K_inf`.  So it
  descends to a homomorphism `T = Phi/K_inf -> GL_n(A_inf)`.
- By Step 2 that homomorphism is trivial, so `f_inf(Y_j) = 1` for every
  `j`.
- That is, the `n^2 d` elements `y^(j)_ab - delta_ab` of `O` all lie in
  `I_inf`.  Here `delta_ab` is the Kronecker delta.

Each of these finitely many elements lies in some `I_L`.  Let `L(n)` be
the largest of those levels, or `L_1` if that is larger.  The chain is
increasing, so all of them lie in `I_L` for every `L >= L(n)`.

## Step 5: items 1 and 2

Let `L >= L(n)` and let `rho : Gamma_L' -> GL_n(A)` be a homomorphism.

- The composite `Phi ->> Gamma_L' -> GL_n(A)` kills `K_L`.  So the
  corresponding `f` kills `I_L`, by (U).
- Hence `f` kills every `y^(j)_ab - delta_ab`, which says `f(Y_j) = 1`.
- So `rho` kills the images of all the `t_j`.  These generate `Gamma_L'`
  by (C1), so `rho` is trivial.  This is item 1.

For item 2, let `rho : Gamma_L -> GL_n(A)`.
- Then `rho(Gamma_L)' = rho(Gamma_L') = 1`, so the image is abelian.
- `N_L <= [R,R] <= [F,F]`, so `Gamma_L^ab = F/[F,F] = Z^r`.
- `Gamma_L'` maps onto `T`, which is nontrivial.  The source claim of (C3)
  states verbatim "Then `T` is nontrivial".  So a faithful `rho` would be
  nontrivial on `Gamma_L'`, which is impossible.

## Step 6: item 3

Let `phi : Gamma_L ->> P` with `P` finite, so `phi(Gamma_L') = P'`.

- Let `q : P' ->> Q` and `eta : Q -> GL_n(A)`.  Then
  `eta o q o phi` restricted to `Gamma_L'` is trivial by item 1.  Since
  `q o phi` maps `Gamma_L'` onto `Q`, `eta` is trivial.
- `P'` is perfect, being an image of the perfect group `Gamma_L'` (C1).
  So every simple quotient `S` of `P'` is perfect, hence nonabelian.
- Suppose `S` had a nontrivial representation `S -> GL_m(K)` over a field
  `K` with `m <= n`.  Adding an identity block of size `n - m` gives a
  nontrivial homomorphism `S -> GL_n(K)`, which contradicts the first
  bullet.  So `m(S) > n`.

## Step 7: item 4

Let `P` be a nonabelian finite quotient of `Gamma_L` all of whose proper
quotients are abelian.  Let `N` be a minimal normal subgroup.

- **`P' = N`, and it is the unique minimal normal subgroup.**  `P/N` is a
  proper quotient, so it is abelian and `P' <= N`.  Since `P' != 1` is
  normal, minimality gives `P' = N`.  Any minimal normal subgroup therefore
  equals `P'`.
- **`N = S^k`.**  `N = P'` is perfect by Step 6, so `N` is nonabelian.  A
  minimal normal subgroup of a finite group is a direct product of
  isomorphic simple groups.  So `N = S^k` with `S` nonabelian simple.
- **`C_P(N) = 1`.**  `C = C_P(N)` is normal, and `C cap N = Z(N) = 1`.  If
  `C != 1`, it contains a minimal normal subgroup of `P`, which must be
  `N`.  That is a contradiction.
- **`P <= Aut(S) wr Sym(k)`.**  Conjugation embeds `P` in `Aut(N)`, and
  `Aut(S^k) = Aut(S) wr Sym(k)` for nonabelian simple `S`.
- **Abelian regular action on the factors.**
  - The `k` simple factors are exactly the minimal normal subgroups of `N`,
    so `P` permutes them.
  - The action is transitive.  The product of the factors in one orbit is
    a nontrivial normal subgroup of `P` inside `N`, so it is `N`.
  - The kernel `P_1` of the action contains `N`, since each factor is
    normal in `N`.  So `P/P_1` is a quotient of the abelian group `P/N`.
  - A transitive abelian permutation group is regular.  A point stabilizer
    is normal, so it fixes every point of the orbit, and it is trivial in
    the faithful action of `P/P_1`.
- **`m(S) > n`.**  Projection to one factor makes `S` a simple quotient of
  `P'`, so Step 6 applies.

**Reduction to the minimal case.**  Let `P` be any nonabelian finite
quotient of `Gamma_L`.  Choose `K` normal in `P`, maximal among normal
subgroups with `P/K` nonabelian; `K = 1` qualifies, so such a `K` exists.
Then `P/K` is a quotient of `Gamma_L` of the kind just treated.  Its
commutator subgroup `S^k` is the image of `P'`.

## Step 8: item 5

- The left regular representation embeds `A_0` in `GL_(|A_0|)(Z)`.  So
  `A_0^X` embeds in `GL_(|A_0|)(Z)^X = GL_(|A_0|)(Z^X)`, and `Z^X` is a
  commutative ring.  Padding by an identity block raises the degree to `n`.
- For `phi : Gamma_L -> A_0^X x| B` with `B` abelian, `phi(Gamma_L')` lies
  in the base `A_0^X`.  So item 1 gives `phi(Gamma_L') = 1`, and the image
  is abelian.

## Remarks on the variants in the statement

- **Unitary.**  `U(n) <= GL_n(C)`.
- **Projective.**  `PGL_n(K)` acts on `M_n(K)` by conjugation.  The kernel
  is trivial, because only scalars commute with all of `M_n(K)`.  So a
  projective representation of degree `n` over a field is covered by
  item 2 in degree `n^2`, that is, for `L >= L(n^2)`.

The route uses no property (T), no analytic input and no word problem of
`G`.  The only inputs are (C1)--(C3) and textbook commutative algebra.
