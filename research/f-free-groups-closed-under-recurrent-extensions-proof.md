Throughout, `F` is generated by `x_0, x_1`, so `F` is finitely generated.

## T1 (no copy of F)

- **Subgroups.** Trivial.
- **Extensions.** Let `N ◁ G` with `N, G/N ∈ 𝓕`, and suppose `F_0 ≤ G` with
  `F_0 ≅ F`. By the consequence recorded in
  `nontrivial-normal-subgroups-of-thompson-f-contain-a-copy-of-f`, either
  `F_0 ∩ N = 1`, so `F_0` embeds in `G/N`, or `F_0 ∩ N ≤ N` contains a copy of `F`.
  Both contradict the hypothesis.
- **Directed unions.** If `F_0 ≤ ∪ G_α`, the finitely many generators of `F_0` lie in
  one `G_α`, so `F_0 ≤ G_α`.
- **Recurrent extensions.** Let `G` be finitely generated, `G·x` recurrent,
  `G_x ∈ 𝓕`, and `F_0 ≤ G` with `F_0 ≅ F`. Theorem R
  (`thompson-f-recurrent-orbits-have-stabilizers-containing-f`) gives a copy of `F` in
  `G_x ∩ F_0 ≤ G_x`, a contradiction.

## T2 (no section F)

Sections of sections are sections, and a subgroup of a section is a section.

- **Subgroups and quotients.** A section of a subgroup or of a quotient of `G` is a
  section of `G`.
- **Directed unions.** Suppose `F ≅ K/M` with `K ≤ ∪ G_α`. Lift `x_0, x_1` to
  `k_0, k_1 ∈ K`, and let `K_0 = ⟨k_0, k_1⟩`. Then `K_0 / (K_0 ∩ M) ≅ F`, and `K_0`
  lies in some `G_α`.
- **Extensions.** Let `N ◁ G` with `N, G/N ∈ 𝓓`, and suppose `F ≅ K/M` with `K ≤ G`.
  The image `P = (K ∩ N)M/M` is normal in `K/M ≅ F`.
  - If `P = 1`, then `K ∩ N ≤ M`. So `F ≅ K/M` is a quotient of
    `K/(K ∩ N) ≅ KN/N ≤ G/N`, a section of `G/N`.
  - Otherwise `P` contains a copy of `F`, by
    `nontrivial-normal-subgroups-of-thompson-f-contain-a-copy-of-f`. And
    `P ≅ (K ∩ N)/(K ∩ N ∩ M)` is a section of `N`, so `N` has a section `F`.
- **Recurrent extensions.** Let `G` be finitely generated, `G·x` recurrent and
  `G_x ∈ 𝓓`, and suppose `F ≅ K/M` with `K ≤ G`. Choose `K_0 = ⟨k_0, k_1⟩` as above
  and put `M_0 = K_0 ∩ M`, so `K_0/M_0 ≅ F`.
  1. *Recurrence passes to `K_0`.* By part 1 of
     `recurrent-orbits-force-every-z3-to-meet-the-stabilizer`, `Y = K_0·x` is
     recurrent for `T = (k_0^{±1}, k_1^{±1})`.
  2. *The quotient set.* `M_0 ◁ K_0`, so `K_0` permutes the `M_0`-orbits:
     `k(M_0 y) = M_0 ky`. So `F ≅ K_0/M_0` acts transitively on `Z = M_0\Y`. The
     stabilizer of `z = M_0 x` is `M_0 (K_0)_x / M_0 ≅ (K_0)_x / ((K_0)_x ∩ M_0)`,
     which is a section of `G_x`.
  3. *Recurrence passes to the quotient.* Let `Y_n = t_n ⋯ t_1 x`, with `t_i`
     independent and uniform in `T`; this is the Schreier walk on `Y`. Its projection
     `Z_n = M_0 Y_n = t̄_n ⋯ t̄_1 z` is the Schreier walk on `Z` for the image multiset
     `T̄`. If `Y_n = x` infinitely often almost surely, then `Z_n = z` infinitely often
     almost surely. So `F·z = Z` is recurrent.
  4. *Conclusion.* Theorem R, applied to `G = F_0 = F` acting on `Z`, shows that the
     stabilizer of `z` contains a copy of `F`. That is a section of a section of `G_x`,
     contradicting `G_x ∈ 𝓓`.

## Bases

- **Residually finite.** This class is closed under subgroups. `F` is not residually
  finite (`thompson-f-is-not-residually-finite`), so it lies in `𝓕`.
- **LEF.** A subgroup of an LEF group is LEF: restrict the partial embeddings to finite
  subsets of the subgroup. `F` is finitely presented (`thompson-f-is-of-type-f-infinity`)
  and not residually finite. By `finitely-presented-lef-groups-are-residually-finite`,
  `F` is not LEF. So LEF lies in `𝓕`.
- **Locally subexponential growth.** This class is closed under subgroups and
  quotients: a finitely generated subgroup of a subgroup is a finitely generated
  subgroup; a finitely generated subgroup of `G/N` is the image of a finitely
  generated subgroup of `G`; and word growth does not increase under quotients. So it
  suffices to find a finitely generated subgroup of `F` of exponential growth.
  - Let `t = b_{0,1}^{-1}` (notation of `nontrivial-normal-subgroups-of-f-contain-f-proof`),
    so `t(s) > s` on `(0,1)`.
  - Pick a dyadic `p ∈ (0,1)`. Then `t(p)` is dyadic, and `J_0 = (p, t(p))` satisfies
    `t^n(J_0) ∩ J_0 = ∅` for `n ≠ 0`.
  - Let `e = b_{c,d}` for a dyadic subinterval `(c,d) ⊆ J_0` of length a power of 2.
  - For `ε ∈ {0,1}^n` put `w_ε = e^{ε_1} t e^{ε_2} t ⋯ e^{ε_n} t`, a word of length at
    most `2n` in `{t, e}`. Then
    `w_ε t^{-n} = ∏_{i=1}^{n} t^{i-1} e^{ε_i} t^{-(i-1)}`.
  - The factors have pairwise disjoint supports `t^{i-1}(c,d)` or are trivial. On
    `t^{i-1}(c,d)` the product agrees with `t^{i-1} e^{ε_i} t^{-(i-1)}`, which is
    nontrivial iff `ε_i = 1`.
  - So the `2^n` elements `w_ε` are distinct, and `⟨t, e⟩` has exponential growth.

## Elementary amenable groups

The smallest class containing finite and abelian groups and closed under extensions
and directed unions (and, if desired, subgroups and quotients) is contained in the
closure of locally subexponential groups under the five operations. That closure lies
in `𝓓` by T2. ∎
