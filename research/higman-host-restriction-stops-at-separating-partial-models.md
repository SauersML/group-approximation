Let `T_F` be the Fibonacci minimal subshift, generated by the computable
substitution

```text
0 -> 01,             1 -> 0,
```

and put `H=[[T_F]]'`.  Grigorchuk--Medynets give recursive generators and
relations for this derived topological full group and prove that the word
problem is decidable exactly when the subshift language is recursive.  Thus
`H` is a concrete finitely generated recursively presented FFGS group and
can be fed either to the explicit Higman algorithm or, with a time bound for
its word problem, to `quantitative-higman-embedding-with-dehn-control`.

Fix any embedding `i:H->G=<A|R>` into a finitely presented group, source
generators `B`, and words over `A` representing `i(B)`.  If an `A`-tuple of
unitaries has maximum normalized-HS relator defect `delta`, then every source
relator `u` satisfies

```text
||u(U_B)-1||_2 <= Area_G(i(u)) delta.                  (FHD1)
```

Consequently, for a sequence with `delta_n->0`, diagonalizing over source
balls gives an approximate representation of `H`.  FFGS local HS stability
rounds it to a **partial homomorphism**, not to a representation of `H`.

That conclusion is sharp.  Since `H` is LEF, choose local embeddings of
increasing balls into finite groups `K_n`.  Compose with the left regular
representations of `K_n` (and amplify if desired).  This gives partial
unitary homomorphisms `rho_n` such that for every fixed `1!=h in H`, eventually

```text
||rho_n(h)-1||_2=sqrt(2).                              (FHD2)
```

Indeed the local embedding keeps `h` nonidentity and every nonidentity left
translation has normalized trace zero.  The sequence `rho_n` already has
zero local-rounding error.  Hence no argument using only `(FHD1)`, source
local HS stability, subgroup distortion, or the existence of a controlled
relative Dehn function can force the finite-dimensionally invisible mark
`h` toward the identity.

This remains true for the strongest currently recorded quantitative Higman
host: bounded distortion controls word length and relative Dehn control gives
the factor in `(FHD1)`, but neither changes the partial target in `(FHD2)`.
For source relators of length at most `k`, the exact loss is

```text
Delta_(H,G)(k) delta,
```

so one may choose `k=k(n)->infinity` slowly enough to obtain partial
multiplicativity.  There is no defect-independent passage to all source
relations.

Finally, a presentation-level return map strong enough to avoid this loss is
impossible for these hosts.  If words in `B` are assigned to the generators
`A` so that every host relator maps to a true source relator and each embedded
`b in B` maps back to `b`, the assignment induces a homomorphism `G->H` left
inverse to `i`.  Thus `H` would be a retract of the finitely presented `G`,
contradicting `ffgs-local-hs-examples-resist-split-finite-presentation-wrappers`.
A coarse or quasi-isometric retraction that is not multiplicative cannot be
evaluated on a unitary tuple and supplies no replacement inequality.

Therefore the surviving Higman/Clapham route must exploit the **joint stable-
letter relations of the wrapper** to rule out the separating partial models
`(FHD2)`.  Restriction, distortion, quasi-isometry, and source-only van
Kampen bounds cannot provide local-HS stability of the host.
