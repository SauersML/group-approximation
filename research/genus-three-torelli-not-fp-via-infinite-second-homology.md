A finitely presented group has finitely generated second homology. Write
`G = F/R` with `F` free of finite rank and `R` the normal closure of finitely many
relators. By Hopf's formula, `H_2(G; Z) = (R ∩ [F,F])/[F,R]`, which is a subgroup of
the abelian group `R/[F,R]`. That group is generated by the images of the relators,
because `[F,R]` contains every `f r f^{-1} r^{-1}`. So `H_2(G; Z)` is a subgroup of a
finitely generated abelian group, hence finitely generated.

Apply this to `G = I_3`: infinitely generated `H_2(I_3; Z)` excludes a finite
presentation.

This is a genuine reduction, not a restatement: finite generation of `H_2` is
necessary but not sufficient for finite presentability, so the prerequisite is
strictly stronger than the target.
