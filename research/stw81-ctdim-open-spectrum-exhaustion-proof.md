Write `s=sup_n ctdim(B_n)`.  Let `K_n` be the ideal of `A` generated by
`B_n`.  The hereditary algebra `B_n` is full in `K_n`, so Morita invariance
and ideal monotonicity give

```text
ctdim(B_n) = ctdim(K_n) <= ctdim(A).
```

Taking the supremum gives

```text
ctdim(A) >= s.                                      (1)
```

For the reverse inequality, fix a continuous-trace subquotient

```text
D=J/I
```

of `A`, and put

```text
H_n=(J intersect B_n)/(I intersect B_n).
```

The canonical map identifies `H_n` with the image of `J intersect B_n` in
`J/I`.  This image is hereditary in `D`: if `x,y` lie in `J intersect B_n`
and `a in J`, then `xay` lies in `J intersect B_n`, since
`B_n A B_n subset B_n`.  Thus every nonzero `H_n` has continuous trace and
is one of the subquotients tested by `ctdim(B_n)`.

The union of the `H_n` is dense in `D`.  Indeed, first

```text
J = closure(union_n (J intersect B_n)).             (2)
```

It is enough to prove (2) for `a` positive.  Approximate `a^(1/2)` by
`b in B_n`, and choose a positive contraction `e` from an approximate
identity for `J` such that `a^(1/2) e a^(1/2)` is close to `a`.  Then

```text
b* e b belongs to J intersect B_n
```

and is as close to `a` as desired.  Here membership in `B_n` uses again
`B_n A B_n subset B_n`.  Passing (2) through the quotient map proves the
asserted density.

Let `L_n` be the ideal of `D` generated by `H_n`.  These are increasing
ideals with dense union.  Since `H_n` is full hereditary in `L_n`, their
primitive spectra are homeomorphic.  Thus the `L_n` correspond to
increasing open subsets `U_n` of `X=Prim(D)` such that

```text
X = union_n U_n.                                    (3)
```

Moreover,

```text
dim(U_n) = dim(Prim(H_n)) <= ctdim(B_n) <= s.       (4)
```

If `s` is infinite there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise, `X` is a
second-countable locally compact Hausdorff space, hence metrizable.  Each
open set `U_n` is an `F_sigma` subset of `X`; write it as a countable union
of subsets closed in `X`.  Every such closed subset has dimension at most
`dim(U_n)`.  Equations (3)--(4) and the countable closed-sum theorem for
covering dimension therefore give

```text
dim(X) <= s.
```

Taking the supremum over all continuous-trace subquotients `D` of `A`
proves `ctdim(A)<=s`, which together with (1) proves equality.

For the inductive-limit formulation, use injectivity to identify the
stages with their images.  Heredity is transitive, and each stage remains
hereditary in the limit: the characterization `B A B subset B` follows by
approximating the middle element of `A` from later stages.  The union of the
stage images is dense, so the first formulation applies.

Finally, `stw81-continuous-trace-shadow-invariant` identifies `ctdim<=1`
with the LXXXI shadow hypothesis, yielding the stated permanence and
reflection.

**Trust boundary.**  The hereditary-image hypothesis is used twice: Morita
equivalence transports the lower bound from each stage to an ideal of the
limit, and the relation `B_n A B_n subset B_n` makes the cut-down pieces
`H_n` hereditary, hence continuous trace.  The proof does not assert
continuity of `ctdim` for arbitrary injective inductive systems.  Its only
topological input is the countable closed-sum theorem for covering dimension
of metric spaces.
