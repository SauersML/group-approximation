**ESTABLISHED.**  Put

```text
J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>,       E=EL_5(J),
I={1,2,3},               Omega={4,5}.
```

Let `P_+` be the subgroup generated by

* all coefficient roots `x_(i alpha)(r)` with `i in I`,
  `alpha in Omega`, and `r in J`;
* the constant Weyl groups on the active coordinates `I` and on the target
  coordinates `Omega`; and
* any desired balanced active-row shifts.

Thus `P_+` is contained in the amenable marked upper parabolic of
`binary-jacobson-two-target-parabolic-is-marked-mf`.  Then

```text
<P_+,x_41(1)>=E.                                         (JREV1)
```

The same conclusion holds with any reverse constant root
`x_(alpha i)(1)`, `alpha in Omega`, `i in I`, in place of `x_41(1)`.

Hence the full upper parabolic has a sharp generation boundary.  Before a
reverse root it is amenable and has a faithful marked operator-MF model;
after one reverse root it is the complete Jacobson elementary group.  There
is no proper full-parabolic-plus-reverse-root subsystem on which a separate
Hall deficit can be proved first.  A useful smaller breaker would have to use
only a selected finite part of the upper coefficient module and authenticate
that selection in each matrix coordinate.
