**ESTABLISHED (lane proof, bh-g1-universal-point, 2026-09-18; elementary, not independently reviewed).** SEED TOWER
team, CAP place "Mikhailova fibre product".

**Setting.**
- `Q = ⟨X | R⟩` is finitely presented, `π : F = F(X) → Q`, and `N = ker π`.
- The fibre product `M_Q = {(u,v) : π(u) = π(v)} ≤ F × F` is generated by the elements `(x,x)` and `(r,1)`.
  Membership in `M_Q` is `WP(Q)`.
- Relative seeds are as in `relative-seeds-glue-graphs-of-groups-into-class-c`, with conditions (RS1)–(RS3).
  The action is `(g·y)(h) = y(g⁻¹h)`, and rules read `y` at `h·u` for `u` in a finite window.
- The diagonal `ΔQ ≤ Q × Q` has orbit `(Q×Q)/ΔQ ≅ Q`, on which `Q × Q` acts by `(α,β)·q = αqβ⁻¹`.
- A (rigid) relative seed for `(Q×Q, ΔQ)` is called a **(rigid) bi-seed of `Q`**. It is a seed on the
  two-sided Cayley graph of `Q`.

## Theorem

1. **Push-down.** A relative seed for `(F×F, M_Q)` gives a relative seed for `(Q×Q, ΔQ)`. Each of (RS1), (RS2)
   and (RS3) transfers separately.
2. **Diagonal trick.** A seed of `Q` (trivial stabilizer) gives a relative seed for `(Q×Q, ΔQ)` satisfying (RS1).
3. **Consequence.** If `F×F *_{M_Q}` (any HNN or amalgam along `M_Q`) is put in `𝒞` by gluing, then `Q` has a
   rigid bi-seed.
   - Its word problem is certified by that bi-seed.
   - So a Mikhailova edge carries `WP(Q)` into `𝒞` only if `Q` already carries a rigid two-sided seed.

## Proof

**1. Push-down.**
- **The seed point factors.** Let `(Y, π_0, y_*)` be a relative seed for `(F×F, M_Q)`. Since
  `N × N ⊆ M_Q = Stab(y_*)`, and `N × N` is normal, `y_*` factors as `z ∘ ρ` with `ρ = π × π`.
- **The pushed-down SFT.** Put `Y_Q = {z' : z' ∘ ρ ∈ Y}`.
  - The pattern of `z'∘ρ` on a window `hU` is read off `z'` on `ρ(h)ρ(U)`. So each rule of `Y` is a rule on
    `Q×Q` with window `ρ(U)`, and `Y_Q` is an SFT.
- **(RS1).**
  - If `z' ∈ Y_Q` has the pushed seed at `1`, then `z'∘ρ ∈ [π_0] ∩ Y = {y_*}`. Since `ρ` is onto, `z' = z`.
  - The pullback is equivariant: `g·(z∘ρ) = (ρ(g)·z)∘ρ`. So `Stab(z) = ρ(M_Q) = ΔQ`.
- **(RS2).** If `z' ∈ Y_Q`, then `z'∘ρ = lim g_n·y_* = lim (ρ(g_n)·z)∘ρ`. Pullback is a homeomorphism onto its
  image, so `z' = lim ρ(g_n)·z`.
- **(RS3).** Let `(E_ℓ(k))` be a `D`-family for `Y_Q`. Put `E_ℓ(h) := E_ℓ(ρ(h))`.
  - Partition of unity and near commutation are inherited, since `ρ` is 1-Lipschitz.
  - A non-fibre-constant pattern is killed, by orthogonality at a repeated site.
  - A fibre-constant pattern missing from `L(Y)` comes from a pattern missing from `L(Y_Q)`, so it is killed too.
  - Hence this is a `D`-family for `Y`, and by (RS3) for `Y` it is commutative. These are the same operators.

**2. Diagonal trick.** Let `(Z_Q, p_Q, z_*)` be a seed of `Q`. Define `Y ⊆ Σ^{Q×Q}` by two kinds of rules.
- **(R1) Rows.** For each `α`, the row `β ↦ y(α, β)` obeys the rules of `Z_Q`. Windows are `(α, β b)`, i.e.
  right multiplication by `(1, b)`.
- **(R2) Seed propagation.** For each generator `s`, "`p` at `h`" holds iff "`p` at `h(s,s)`" holds. Here "`p` at
  `h`" means `y(h(1,b)) = p_Q(b)` on the seed ball. This is a local rule.

The candidate point is `y_*(α,β) = z_*(α⁻¹β)`.
- **It lies in `Y`.** Row `α` is `α·z_*`. Also `p` occurs at `(α,β)` iff `α⁻¹β = 1`, because `p_Q` occurs in `z_*`
  only at `1`, and this condition is invariant under `(s,s)`.
- **It is isolated.** Let `y ∈ Y` have `p` at `(1,1)`.
  - By (R2) in both directions, `p` occurs at every `(α,α)`.
  - So `α⁻¹·row_α ∈ [p_Q] ∩ Z_Q = {z_*}`, i.e. `row_α = α·z_*` and `y = y_*`.
- **Stabilizer.** Since `[p] = {y_*}`, `Stab(y_*) = {g : p at g⁻¹} = ΔQ`. ∎

**Cautions.**
- (RS2) and (RS3) are not claimed for the diagonal trick. Rows without a seed are points of `∂Z_Q`, and (R2) does
  not couple them. So `Y` has free row directions, and rigidity would need a cross-row coupling beyond seeds.
- The converse of the push-down, i.e. lifting a bi-seed of `Q` to `F × F`, needs the pulled-back rows to factor
  through `π`.
  - The rows of the lift have stabilizer `N`, which is infinitely generated.
  - By `relative-seeds-on-free-groups-have-fg-stabilizers`, rows can never be seeded by second-factor rules alone.
  - The factorization must come from the cross-row seeds at the whole coset `uN`, which the diagonal and
    `(r,1)` propagation supply. For transport-type seeds, such as the product `C_Z` seed of `Z²`, this works by
    hand. It is not claimed in general.

## Consequence for CAP

- **The fibre product itself carries no hardness.** Its role is to put a hard membership problem next to an easy,
  seeded vertex group `F×F`.
- **The hardness must come from `Q`.** By 1, the edge's relative seed pushes down to a rigid bi-seed of `Q`, whose
  forced point computes `WP(Q)`.
- **So the route needs rigid bi-seeds.** Mikhailova refutes CAP only if some `Q` whose word problem is not
  elementary has a rigid bi-seed that lifts. By 2, that is formally weaker than a seed of `Q`: two-sided rules
  also see left multiplication, and so conjugation.
- **The next test.** Does some `Q` with a non-elementary word problem have a rigid bi-seed, or at least a
  relative seed for `ΔQ`, while it has no known seed? A natural candidate is a Higman–Clapham group, where
  conjugation carries the computation (bh-invent-14's `(B_2, A_±)`).

## Lesson for general BH

- **Edges that encode another group's word problem transfer complexity; they do not create it.** A relative seed
  for a fibre product `M_Q ≤ F×F` descends to a rigid seed for the diagonal of `Q×Q`, i.e. a two-sided seed of
  `Q`.
- **So Mikhailova edges reduce CAP to the same question for `Q`,** with two-sided local rules in place of
  one-sided ones.
- **The one-sided case.** Every one-sided seed yields a two-sided one, by the diagonal trick: rows forced by seeds
  propagated along the diagonal.
- **The two-sided case.** Whether two-sided rules can force what one-sided rules cannot, for example conjugation
  structure, is the whole remaining content of this CAP place.
