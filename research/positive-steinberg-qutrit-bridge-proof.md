Write

```text
X_alpha=r_alpha E_(i_alpha,j_alpha),      i_alpha<j_alpha,
```

so that the selected elementary generator is `1+X_alpha`.  Conjugation
by the finite qutrit packet has the explicit form

```text
D(f) X_alpha D(f)^(-1)
 = (f r_alpha)E_(1,j_alpha),   if i_alpha=1,
 = X_alpha,                    if i_alpha>1.             (PSQ4)
```

(The missing case `j_alpha=1` cannot occur for a positive root.)  Hence

```text
mathcal X={D(f)X_alpha D(f)^(-1): f in F, alpha selected}
```

is finite.

Let `mathcal B` be the `F_2`-linear span of all nonempty products

```text
Y_1 ... Y_k,        Y_l in mathcal X,       1<=k<n.      (PSQ5)
```

Every `Y_l` is strictly upper triangular.  A product of `n` strictly
upper-triangular `n`-by-`n` matrices is zero, even when the coefficient
ring is noncommutative: a possibly nonzero entry would require a strictly
increasing chain of `n+1` indices in `{1,...,n}`.  It follows that
`mathcal B` is a nonunital algebra and

```text
mathcal B^n=0.                                           (PSQ6)
```

There are only finitely many words in `mathcal X` of length less than
`n`, so `mathcal B` is finite-dimensional over `F_2`, and therefore
finite as a set.  Formula `(PSQ4)` also shows that `D(F)` normalizes
`mathcal X), hence normalizes `mathcal B`.

Nilpotence makes

```text
1+mathcal B={1+b:b in mathcal B}
```

a group: multiplication stays in `1+mathcal B), and the inverse of
`1+b` is the terminating geometric series
`1-b+b^2-...+(-b)^(n-1)`.  Thus `1+mathcal B` is a finite
unipotent group (indeed a finite `2`-group).  It contains every selected
root generator and every one of its `D(F)`-conjugates.  Since `D(F)`
normalizes it, word collection gives

```text
H <= (1+mathcal B) D(F).                                 (PSQ7)
```

The right-hand side is a finite set, proving that `H` is finite.  Notice
that no finite-dimensionality assertion about the whole Leavitt algebra was
used: bounded positive-root length, forced by triangularity, is exactly what
closes the coefficient orbit.

For the Steinberg statement, let `Gamma` be generated by the lifted
packet and the chosen standard root lifts.  Its image under `pi` is the
finite subgroup `H'` of `H).  Therefore

```text
1 -> Gamma intersect ker(pi) -> Gamma -> H' -> 1.        (PSQ8)
```

For `n>=5), the established Steinberg lift has central kernel, so
`Gamma intersect ker(pi)` is central in `Gamma).  It is abelian, and
`H'` is finite.  Hence `Gamma` is central-by-finite and amenable; in
particular it is hyperlinear.  This also explains why an uncomputed central
`K_2` contribution cannot be the missing obstruction.

Finally, arbitrary additive overlap coefficients formed from this packet and
these roots remain in `C[H]` downstairs or `C[Gamma]` upstairs.  If a
finite family of them completed a no-CE atlas, finite-support localization
would produce a finitely generated nonhyperlinear subgroup of a finite or
amenable group, which is impossible.  The proof never replaces an overlap by
a conjugacy, Morita transport, or same-prefix-code permutation.  What fails
is the whole positive-root support basin.  A surviving bridge must introduce
an opposite-root or Weyl return, or otherwise defeat the bounded-length
nilpotent algebra `mathcal B`.
