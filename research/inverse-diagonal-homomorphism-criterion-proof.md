The second coordinate of i(g)i(h) is g⁻¹h⁻¹, whereas that of
i(gh) is h⁻¹g⁻¹. Equality for every pair is equivalent, after inversion,
to hg=gh for every pair. In the abelian case both coordinates multiply
correctly and i(1)=(1,1), giving a homomorphism. The first coordinate
always proves set-theoretic injectivity. This elementary proof uses no
external theorem. It corrects the error identified in the user-supplied
September 20 Cairn audit.
