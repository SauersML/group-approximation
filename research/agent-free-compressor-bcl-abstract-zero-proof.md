Let `R` be the coefficient ring defined by `(BCL0)--(BCL2)`, and suppose the
image of a primitive forbidden block idempotent `P` is nonzero.  The kernel of
the map

```text
P k[B_f] -> R
```

is a two-sided ideal in the simple algebra `P k[B_f]=M_(2D)(k)`.  It is not
the whole algebra because `P` survives, so this map is injective.

For every free word `w`, the commutation and identification rows in `(BCL0)`
give by a fixed-word induction

```text
u_w P=theta(w)P.                                        (1)
```

For a free basis generator `x_i`, use `[P,t]=0`, the HNN row, and `(1)` twice:

```text
t (b_iP)t^(-1)
 =t(u_(x_i)P)t^(-1)
 =u_(alpha(x_i))P
 =a_iP.                                                 (2)
```

The `b_i` generate `B_f`, so the algebra generated by the left side of `(2)`
is the conjugate `t(Pk[B_f])t^(-1)`.  The `a_i` generate `A_f`, so the algebra
generated by the right side is exactly `Pk[A_f]`.  Thus conjugation by the
invertible coefficient `t` restricts to an injective algebra map

```text
M_(2D)(k)=Pk[B_f] -> Pk[A_f]=M_D(k) tensor kI_2.        (3)
```

But the source and target dimensions are `4D^2` and `D^2`, respectively.
This contradicts injectivity.  Hence `P=0`, proving `(BAZ2)`.

If the selected idempotent is a sum of forbidden primitive blocks, apply the
same argument after multiplying the rows by each central primitive summand.
Root additivity sends the zero coefficient to the identity, proving `(BAZ3)`.
Because this is an identity in a finitely presented coefficient quotient,
every fixed finite wordization has a finite derivation and therefore a
dimension-independent normalized-HS telescoping bound.
