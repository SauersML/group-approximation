Notation as in the target.

**Imports (recalled, standard).**
- (PW) Peter–Weyl: the finite-dimensional unitary representations of a Hausdorff compact group separate its points.
- (M) Malcev: a finitely generated subgroup of `GL_k(C)` is residually finite.
- (UCT) For a group `G` and a trivial module `C`, `H^n(G; C) = Hom(H_n(G), C) ⊕ Ext(H_(n-1)(G), C)`, and
  `Ext(-, C) = 0` since `C` is divisible.

**In-graph inputs.**
- `binary-leavitt-elementary-group-is-simple`, with `leavitt-steinberg-map-iso-from-rank-three` (`St_20(R) = GL_20(R)`):
  `Delta` is simple.
- `Delta` is finitely generated (it is `EL_20` of the finitely generated ring `R`) and infinite (the `x_12(s_1^k)` are

- `leavitt-cone-cover-kernel-is-free-and-gamma-perfect` (2),(3): `K = [K, Gamma]`, `H_1(Gamma) = 0`,

- `leavitt-cuntz-cone-halves-have-rf-amalgam-cover` (3): `Gamma` is residually finite, `K` is normally generated by

## Step 1. `Delta` has no nontrivial compact image

Let `psi : Delta -> C` be a homomorphism into a Hausdorff compact group. Suppose `psi` is nontrivial. Its kernel is a
proper normal subgroup, so it is `1` by simplicity, and `psi` is injective. Take any `g ≠ 1` in `Delta`. By (PW) there
is a finite-dimensional unitary representation `sigma` of `C` with `sigma(psi(g)) ≠ 1`. Then `sigma psi(Delta)` is a
finitely generated linear group. By (M) it has a finite quotient in which the image of `g` is nontrivial. Composing
gives a homomorphism from `Delta` onto a finite group that does not kill `g`. Its kernel is proper and normal, so it is
`1`, and `Delta` is finite. That is a contradiction. So `psi` is trivial.

## Step 2. Compact density (statement 1)

Let `rho : Gamma -> G` with `G` compact Hausdorff, and put `H = closure rho(Gamma)` and `N = closure rho(K)`.
- `rho(K)` is normalized by `rho(Gamma)`, so by continuity of conjugation `N` is normalized by `H`.
- `N` is closed, so `H/N` is a Hausdorff compact group.
- `gamma ↦ rho(gamma) N` kills `K`, so it induces a homomorphism `psi : Delta = Gamma/K -> H/N`.
- By Step 1, `psi` is trivial. So `rho(Gamma) ⊆ N`, and taking closures gives `H = N`.

## Step 3. Statements 2, 3 and 4

- *(2)* A finite group is compact and discrete, so closures are the sets themselves: `pi(K) = F`. With
  `N = ker pi`, this says `K N = Gamma`. `K` is proper, since `Gamma/K = Delta ≠ 1`. The profinite completion is the
  inverse limit of the `Gamma/N`, and `K` maps onto each of them, so the image of `K` is dense there. Its closure is
  therefore the whole completion.
- *(3)* If `rho` is finite-dimensional unitary (so `G = U(k)` is compact) or finite, and `rho(k_i) = 1` for all `i`,
  then `rho(K) = 1`, since `K` is the normal closure of the `k_i`. By (1), `rho(Gamma) ⊆ closure rho(K) = 1`.
- *(4)* `z ∉ K` because `p(z) = z ≠ 1` in `Delta`. For finite-index normal `N`, (2) gives `Gamma = K N`, so
  `z ∈ K N`. The image of `K` in `Gamma/N` is the normal closure of the images of the `k_i`, so the image of `z` is a
  product of conjugates of the images of the `k_i^(±1)`.

## Step 4. Finite-dimensional coefficients (statement 5)

- Let `M` be a finite-dimensional unitary `Delta`-module, i.e. a homomorphism `Delta -> U(M)` into a compact group. By
  Step 1 it is trivial.
- For trivial `M = C^k`: `H^1(Delta; M) = Hom(H_1(Delta), M) = 0`, `H^1(Gamma; M) = Hom(H_1(Gamma), M) = 0`, and by
  (UCT) `H^2(Delta; M) = Hom(H_2(Delta), M) ⊕ Ext(H_1(Delta), M) = 0`.
- `Hom_Delta(K^ab, M)`, for `M` with trivial action, is the group of `Gamma`-invariant homomorphisms `K -> M`. By
  `leavitt-cone-cover-kernel-is-free-and-gamma-perfect` (3) it is `0`. The five-term sequence
  `0 -> H^1(Delta;M) -> H^1(Gamma;M) -> Hom_Gamma(K,M) -> H^2(Delta;M)` gives the same answer from the vanishing of
  both ends.
- In particular both pieces named in "What is and is not claimed" of
  `leavitt-cone-cover-kernel-free-gamma-perfect-proof` vanish: `M^(Delta_0) = M^(Delta_+) = M^(Delta_-) = M`, so the
  quotient is `0`, and `H^2(Delta; M) = 0`.

## What is and is not claimed

- Established: (1)–(5) of the target, from the imports and in-graph inputs above.
- Not claimed: anything about approximate models. For a sequence of finite-dimensional unitary representations
  `rho_n` of `Gamma` with `||rho_n(k_i) - 1||_2 -> 0`, (1) says only that each `rho_n(K)` is dense in its image.
  Whether `||rho_n(z) - 1||_2 -> 0` is forced is the hyperlinearity question for `Delta` and is untouched.
- Not claimed: finite normal generation of `K` by Steinberg relators, beyond what
  `leavitt-cuntz-cone-halves-have-rf-amalgam-cover` already states.
