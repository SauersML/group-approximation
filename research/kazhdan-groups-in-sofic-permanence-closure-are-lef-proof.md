The full proof is in `research/artifacts/sk-sofic-host-hamming-2026-09-13-part1.md` §3. It follows the induction of
`lea-permanence-closure-fp-kazhdan-rf-proof`, with LEF in place of residual finiteness and openness of property (T) for
the limit step.

**Stages.** Put `𝒫_0` = amenable ∪ LEF groups. `𝒫_(α+1)` adds the groups obtained from `𝒫_α` by one of (O1)–(O6), and
limit stages are unions. `P(α)`: every finitely generated Kazhdan subgroup `G` of a member `H` of `𝒫_α` is LEF.

**Facts used:**
- quotients and finite-index subgroups of Kazhdan groups are Kazhdan;
- countable amenable Kazhdan groups are finite;
- property (T) implies property FA (Watatani);
- Kazhdan marked groups form an open set (`fpbs-shalom-property-t-is-open`);
- LEF is closed under subgroups, finite products and marked limits;
- **finite extensions of LEF groups are LEF.** If `K ⊲ G` has finite index, `G ≤ K ≀ (G/K)`, and a partial embedding of
  a ball of `K` into a finite `F`, applied coordinatewise, is a partial embedding into `F ≀ (G/K)`.

**Induction.**
- **`α = 0`:** a Kazhdan subgroup of an amenable group is finite, and a subgroup of a LEF group is LEF.
- **(O1):** `G` lies in a member of `𝒫_α`.
- **(O2):** `G` is finitely generated, so it lies in one member of the union.
- **(O3):** the projections `p_i(G)` are finitely generated Kazhdan subgroups of the factors, so they are LEF, and
  `G ≤ ∏ p_i(G)`.
- **(O4)** (`N ⊲ H`, `H/N` amenable):
  - `G/(G∩N)` embeds in `H/N`, so it is amenable and Kazhdan, hence finite;
  - `G∩N` has finite index in `G`, so it is finitely generated and Kazhdan, and lies in `N`; so it is LEF;
  - `G` is a finite extension of `G∩N`, hence LEF.
- **(O5):** `G` has property FA, so it fixes a vertex of the Bass–Serre tree and lies in a conjugate of a vertex group.
- **(O6)** (`H = lim H_k`):
  - with `G` generated by words `w_i` in the generators of `H`, the groups `G_k = ⟨w_i⟩ ≤ H_k` converge to `G` as marked
    groups;
  - by openness `G_k` is Kazhdan for large `k`, hence LEF by `P(α)`;
  - so `G` is a marked limit of LEF groups, hence LEF. ∎
