**Setting.**
- `Λ` is a finitely generated group, and `X ⊆ A^Λ` is a nonempty subshift on which `Λ` acts freely.
- `C = {0,1}^N`, and `T = G_V × (Λ ⋉ X)` on `Ω = C × X`.
- The pieces are subgroups of `F(T)`:
  - `M = V(Λ) = C(C, Λ) ⋊ V`, acting by `(c, x) -> (v(c), f(c)·x)`. Its Λ-labels depend only on the `C`
    coordinate.
  - `N_X = LC(X, V) ⋊ Λ`, acting by `(c, x) -> (u_x(c), λ·x)`. Its `V`-part depends only on the `X` coordinate, and
    its label is global.
- `v^(a)` is `v ∈ V` applied to the `C` coordinate when `x(1) = a`, and the identity otherwise.

**Statement.**
1. **Two pieces.** `F(T) = < M, N_X >`, and `M ∩ N_X = V × Λ`.
2. **The labelled piece is always fp.** If `Λ` is finitely presented, then `M` is finitely presented
   (`cantor-integer-maps-by-v-are-of-type-fp2` item 1), whatever `X` is.
3. **The Boolean-power piece is finitely generated.** `N_X = < Λ, v^(a) : v ∈ S_V, a ∈ A >` for a finite
   generating set `S_V` of `V`.
4. **The Boolean-power piece sees rigidity.** `N_X` contains every translation `t_s`, every site-conditioned
   3-cycle `e_a`, and `V × {1}`. So if `N_X` is finitely presented, then `X` is a subshift of finite type that is
   `D`-quantum rigid over every field for all large `D`
   (`fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`, both parts).
5. **Self-similar gluing group.** Let `M_0 ≤ M` be the elements supported in `C_0 × X`, and `N_1 = LC(X, V_[1])`
   the elements of `N_X` with trivial label supported in `C_1 × X`. Put
   `Γ~ = M *_(V×Λ) N_X / <<[M_0, N_1]>>`.
   - `[M_0, N_1] = 1` follows from finitely many relations. So `Γ~` is finitely presented if `M` and `N_X` are.
   - `Γ~` maps onto `F(T)`.
   - The copy maps `F(T) -> F(T)`, onto the elements supported in `C_i × X` (`i = 0, 1`), lift to commuting
     endomorphisms `φ_0`, `φ_1` of `Γ~` with `[φ_0(Γ~), φ_1(Γ~)] = 1`.

**Consequence: P2′ splits.** For a free SFT `X` over a finitely presented `Λ`, `F(T)` is finitely presented if
- (P2′a) `N_X` is finitely presented (`rigid-sfts-give-fp-v-boolean-power-crossed-products`), and
- (P2′b) the kernel of `Γ~ -> F(T)` is finitely normally generated
  (`v-times-sft-full-groups-glue-from-two-wreath-pieces`).

By item 4, the premise of (P2′a) cannot hold unless `X` is quantum rigid. (P2′b) mentions no dynamics at all.

**Lesson for general BH.**
- On the group side of the master route, rigidity lives in exactly one place: the Boolean power of `V` over `X`,
  crossed by `Λ`. That is the group-theoretic twin of the crossed-product ring `LC(X, k) ⋊ Λ`. The necessity
  theorem already sees it, and item 4 says it sees nothing more.
- Everything else in `F(T)` comes from the labelled Thompson group `V(Λ)`, which is finitely presented for free.
  The two are glued along `V × Λ`, by commutation of pieces supported on disjoint cones.
- The glue is self-similar (item 5). So a proof of P2′ has two parts:
  - one "ring-type" theorem, rigid ⇒ `N_X` fp;
  - one dynamics-free gluing theorem, of the kind that holds for Brin–Thompson `2V`, where both pieces are `V(V)`.

  This is the same two-layer shape as Houghton-like groups. There the finitary layer carries the obstruction
  (`fsym-by-odometer-group-on-the-integers-is-fp`), and the room comes from a finitely presented rigid piece.
