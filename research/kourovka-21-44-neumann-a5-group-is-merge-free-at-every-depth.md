growth of `H` stay **OPEN**.

## Setting

Notation as in `kourovka-21-44-spinal-groups-over-a5-dense-regime` (section "Length-reduction
attempt"): `H = ⟨A_5, d_s : s ∈ A_5⟩ ≤ Aut(T_5)` with `d_s = (s, 1, 1, 1, d_s)`. A reduced word is
`w = r_0 d_{s_1} r_1 d_{s_2} ⋯`. With `π_i = r_0 ⋯ r_{i−1}`, the letter `d_{s_i}` puts the rooted
letter `s_i` into coordinate `c_i = π_i(1)` and the directed letter `d_{s_i}` into `e_i = π_i(5)`.
A **merge** is two letters of the same type adjacent in one section stream; "merge-free at depth m"
means no merge in any stream at depths `1, …, m`.

Let `S* = {σ ∈ A_5 : σ(1) = 5}` in this convention; it is a coset of a point stabilizer, so
`|S*| = 12`, and `1 ∉ S*`. (In the right-action convention the condition reads `5σ = 1`.)

## Theorem

Let `Z = {r d_s : r, s ∈ S*}` (144 elements). Every positive word `z_1 ⋯ z_k` in `Z` is a reduced
word in `A_5 * D` that is **merge-free at every depth**. This remains true if the first rooted letter
`r_1` is replaced by any nontrivial element of `A_5`. There are `144^k` such words with `k` directed letters, so for every `m` the set of
merge-free-to-depth-`m` reduced words has exponential growth rate at least `12` per letter.

## Proof

**Lemma (the a = 1 regime reproduces itself).** Let `w = x d_{t_1} u_1 d_{t_2} u_2 ⋯ d_{t_k} [u_k]`
be an alternating word with `x` an optional rooted prefix and all `u_i, t_i ∈ S*`. Then:
1. `c_{i+1} = e_i` for every `i`, since `c_{i+1} = π_i(u_i(1)) = π_i(5) = e_i`.
2. So stream `j` receives exactly: `R(t_1)` if `j = c_1`, and for each visit `i` (`e_i = j`) the
   pair `D(t_i)` at step `i`, then `R(t_{i+1})` at step `i+1`. A stream `c_{i'}` receives an `R`
   only when `e_{i'−1} = j`, so nothing else arrives in between. Since `c_i ≠ e_i`, no step puts
   two letters into one stream.
3. Each stream's letters therefore alternate in type: **no merge at depth 1**.
4. Each section is `[t_1] · d_{t_{i_1}} t_{i_1+1} d_{t_{i_2}} t_{i_2+1} ⋯`: an alternating word of
   the same form, whose connectors `t_{i+1}` and labels `t_i` all lie in `S*`.

Applying the lemma to each section, then to each section of a section, and so on, gives
merge-freeness at every depth by induction. A positive word in `Z` is exactly such a `w` with
`x = r_1` (arbitrary) and `u_i = r_{i+1} ∈ S*`, `t_i = s_i ∈ S*`. All letters are nontrivial, so the
word is reduced, and distinct letter sequences are distinct reduced words. ∎

## Consequences

- **The depth-3 finite computation is moot.** The mean-payoff check proposed in the 21.44 node
  (minimal merge density at depth 3 or 4) has value **0**, and it has it at every depth, attained by
  a family of positive entropy. No acn112 run is needed. The a = 2 regime studied there is not
  the adversary's best: in the a = 1 regime the pulled-back directed-last set of every stream is
  always `{5}`, whatever the stream prefix. So all the depth constraints on a label coincide
  (`s(1) = 5`) instead of intersecting.
- **Calibration against Fabrykowski–Gupta.** For rooted `ℤ/3` on 3 points with `b = (a, 1, b)`, the
  analogous coset `{σ : σ(1) = 3}` has one element. The family then has exactly one word per length,
  the odometer family (powers of `ba`) that the Fabrykowski–Gupta argument treats separately. This
  matches the count in the 21.44 node.
- **Principle.** For a group generated by a rooted group `A` acting transitively on `d` points and
  directed elements of this spinal shape, the a = 1 family has `(|A|/d)²` choices per step. So
  merge-counting length reduction can only succeed when `A` acts **regularly** (`|A_x| = 1`), as in
  Fabrykowski–Gupta and GGS groups. For `A_5` on 5 points, `|A_5|/5 = 12`.

## What this does not show

- The words in `Z⁺` need not be geodesic, and their sections need not be geodesic. The actual
  length-reduction method bounds geodesic lengths `|w_v|`, which can be smaller than letter counts.
  So this rules out **merge-only** arguments, not every length-reduction argument:
  - a proof of subexponential growth of `H` must use relations of `H` beyond merges, i.e. show
    that `Z⁺` words are far from geodesic;
  - a proof of exponential growth could show that `Z⁺`, or a subsemigroup over a smaller
    alphabet `Z_T = {u d_t : u, t ∈ T}` with `T ⊆ S*`, maps injectively to `H`.
- **Next target:** is `Z_T⁺ → H` injective for some `T ⊆ S*` with `|T| ≥ 2`? `Z_T⁺` is closed under
  taking sections, up to one boundary letter at each end (Lemma, item 4). Given the prefix, the map
  `u ↦ π_i(u(5))` recovers the connector `u` from the visited stream whenever `u ↦ u(5)` is
  injective on `T`. The remaining difficulty is recovering the order of visits from the section
  words.

## Attribution (the Segal check; corrected per bh-ref-d, f2e0b60e78)

- **H is a group of Neumann–Segal type.** Juschenko–Nekrashevych–de la Salle (arXiv:1305.2637,
  §4.3.2) describe their construction as "a general version of Segal's construction", and say
  only that P. Neumann's examples are "similar". `H` is their group for the constant sequence
  `G_i = A_5` on `X = {1, …, 5}`, with chosen points `y = 1` and `x = 5`, rooted generators `α = a`
  and directed generators `β = (a at 1, β at 5)`. Since `s ↦ d_s` is a homomorphism, `d_s` for `s`
  in a generating set of `A_5` already give all of `D`.
- **H is amenable.** This is JNS Proposition 4.12 (pr:neumansegaltype): such a group is amenable
  iff the group generated by the sequences `(a_{1,j}, a_{2,j}, …)` is. For constant sequences that
  group is `A_5`. This matches the state artifact's recalled bounded-automaton argument.
- **Where the density of H comes from.**
  - Segal's main examples take `G_i = PSL(2, p_i)` for increasing primes `p_i` and are non-amenable
    (JNS).
  - Bondarenko (arXiv:1002.0320, introduction) describes Segal's result as finite generation of
    iterated wreath products of perfect groups, using "basically the same construction used by
    P. Neumann, L. Bartholdi, D. Segal".
  - Whether Proposition D's density appears in Segal (PLMS 2001) or Neumann (Illinois J. Math. 30,
    1986) is not settled here; neither paper was read at source.
- **Related:** Brieussel (arXiv:1207.2820, Theorem 3.3) shows the alternate mother group `G_d`, with
  rooted `A_d`, is amenable for `d ≥ 5`.

No priority is claimed for anything here beyond the explicit family and the principle above.
