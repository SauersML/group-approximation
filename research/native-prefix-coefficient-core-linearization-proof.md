Put `a=s_1t_0` and `b=s_00t_1`.  Prefix cancellation gives

```text
a^2=b^2=0.                                             (1)
```

Every nonzero nonempty monomial in `a,b` is therefore alternating.  Direct
cancellation gives, for `r>=1`,

```text
(ba)^r=s_(0^(r+1))t_0,          (ab)^r=s_(10^r)t_1,    (2)
```

and, for `r>=0`,

```text
a(ba)^r=s_(10^r)t_0,            b(ab)^r=s_(0^(r+2))t_1. (3)
```

The elements in `(2)--(3)`, together with `1`, are linearly independent.
Here is a direct check which does not invoke a normal-form theorem for the
whole Leavitt ring.  Let the Leavitt generators act on the vector space with
basis the infinite binary strings, with `s_i` adjoining the prefix `i` and
`t_i` deleting it when present.  For a finite proposed dependence, choose a
tail `xi` for which the finitely many strings obtained by adjoining the
listed zero-prefixes are distinct.  Evaluation on `0xi` separates `1`, the
`(ba)^r`, and the `a(ba)^r`; evaluation on `1xi` then separates the
`(ab)^r` and `b(ab)^r`.  A zero element of the ring acts as zero in every
representation, so the proposed dependence was trivial.

Let

```text
I={ [[f,tg],[h,k]] in M_2(F_2[t]) : f(0)=k(0) }.        (4)
```

This is the unital subring of `M_2(F_2[t])` generated by `E_21` and
`tE_12`.  The assignment

```text
a |-> E_21,                         b |-> tE_12         (5)
```

matches multiplication because

```text
ba |-> tE_11,                       ab |-> tE_22.       (6)
```

The standard vector-space basis of `(4)` is

```text
I_2,
t^rE_11, t^rE_22                    (r>=1),
t^rE_21, t^(r+1)E_12                (r>=0).             (7)
```

Equations `(2)--(3)` and their linear independence show that `(5)` extends
to an isomorphism from the unital subring `S=<1,a,b>` onto `I`.

All four generators `n,m,A,B` are elementary matrices with coefficients in
`S`.  Applying `(5)` entrywise and flattening `M_3(M_2(F_2[t]))` gives an
injective homomorphism

```text
H=<n,m,A,B> -> GL_6(F_2[t]).                            (8)
```

If `g!=1` in `H`, some entry of the polynomial matrix `(8)(g)-I_6` is a
nonzero polynomial `F`.  Choose `N>deg(F)`.  Reduction modulo `t^N` keeps
`F` nonzero and maps `(8)(g)` to an invertible matrix over the finite ring
`F_2[t]/(t^N)`.  Thus the finite quotients

```text
H -> GL_6(F_2[t]/(t^N))                               (9)
```

separate points.  This proves residual finiteness.  A finite product of
such quotients injects any prescribed finite subset, proving LEF directly;
`lef-implies-operator-mf` gives operator-MF.  Since every subgroup of a
residually finite group is residually finite, `(8)` also rules out an
embedded copy of the binary Jacobson elementary hard core: its nontrivial
head root is killed by every finite quotient.

It remains to determine amenability.  The prerequisite constructs
`x_87(c^r)` for every `r>=1`, where `c=ba`.  Put `w=nmn`, the constant Weyl
swap of coordinates `7,8`, and

```text
u_r=w x_87(c^r) w^(-1)=x_78(c^r).                      (10)
```

In `(5)`, `c^r` is `t^rE_11`.  On the rank-two direct summand consisting of
the first polynomial component in coordinates `7,8`, the elements in
`(10)` and `w` restrict to

```text
U_r=[[1,t^r],[0,1]],               W=[[0,1],[1,0]].     (11)
```

The subgroup `V=<U_1,U_2>` is `C_2 x C_2`: the two matrices commute, have
order two, and their three nonidentity coefficients are `t`, `t^2`, and
`t+t^2`.

The natural map

```text
C_2 * (C_2 x C_2) -> <W,U_1,U_2>                      (12)
```

is injective.  Indeed a reduced word alternates `W` with matrices `U(f)`,
where `f` is one of `t,t^2,t+t^2`.  After harmless multiplication by `W`
at an endpoint, its essential matrix is a product

```text
M(f_1)...M(f_r),             M(f)=U(f)W=[[f,1],[1,0]]. (13)
```

Inductively the upper-left entry in `(13)` has degree
`sum_i deg(f_i)` and leading term `prod_i LT(f_i)`: in the recurrence the
other summand has strictly smaller degree.  Thus no nonempty reduced word
is the identity.  This proves `(12)`.

The free product `C_2*(C_2 x C_2)` has a finite-index free subgroup of rank
three (the kernel of its map to the direct product, equivalently its
standard reduced-word tree action), and is nonamenable.  Therefore `H` is
nonamenable as well.
