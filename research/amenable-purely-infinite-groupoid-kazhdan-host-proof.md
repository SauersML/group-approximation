## Definitions used

The groupoid vocabulary follows H. Matui, *Topological full groups of one-sided shifts
of finite type*, J. reine angew. Math. 705 (2015), arXiv:1210.5800 (source tex, fetched
2026-09-17). Verbatim:

- l.362–365: "The isotropy bundle is $G'=\{g\in G\mid r(g)=s(g)\}$. We say that $G$ is
  principal if $G'=G^{(0)}$. When the interior of $G'$ is $G^{(0)}$, we say that $G$ is
  essentially principal."
- l.356–357: "When every $G$-orbit is dense in $G^{(0)}$, $G$ is said to be minimal."
- l.366: "A subset $U\subset G$ is called a $G$-set if $r|U,s|U$ are injective."
- l.376–378: "For an open $G$-set $U$, we let $\pi_U$ denote the homeomorphism
  $r\circ(s|U)^{-1}$ from $s(U)$ to $r(U)$."
- l.404–406: "The set of all $\alpha\in\Homeo(G^{(0)})$ for which there exists a compact
  open $G$-set $U$ satisfying $\alpha=\pi_U$ is called the topological full group of $G$
  and denoted by $[[G]]$."
- Definition `pi`, l.1196–1201: "A clopen set $A\subset G^{(0)}$ is said to be properly
  infinite if there exist compact open $G$-sets $U,V\subset G$ such that $s(U)=s(V)=A$,
  $r(U)\cup r(V)\subset A$ and $r(U)\cap r(V)=\emptyset$. […] We say that $G$ is purely
  infinite if every clopen set $A\subset G^{(0)}$ is properly infinite."
- l.1748–1749 and l.1757: "$G=\{(x,n,y)\in X\times\Z\times X\mid \exists k,l\in\N,\

  (x,n,y)^{-1}=(y,-n,x)".
- l.1783–1789: "we define a compact open $G$-set $U_{\mu,\nu}$ by
  $U_{\mu,\nu}=\{(x,\lvert\mu\rvert-\lvert\nu\rvert,y)\in G\mid
  \sigma^{\lvert\mu\rvert}(x)=\sigma^{\lvert\nu\rvert}(y),\ x\in C_\mu,\ y\in C_\nu\}$.
  The subsets $U_{\mu,\nu}$ form a base for the topology of $G$."
- Lemma `SFT>pim`, l.1791–1793: "For any non-empty clopen set $Y\subset X$, the \'etale
  groupoid $G|Y$ is purely infinite and minimal."

Write `G_2` for Matui's groupoid of the full shift on two symbols (unit space `C`).

**Topological amenability.** An étale groupoid `𝒢` is *topologically amenable* if there
are systems `x ↦ m_n^x ∈ Prob(𝒢^x)` (with `𝒢^x = r^{-1}(x)`), continuous in `x`
(`x ↦ Σ_g f(g) m_n^x(g)` is continuous for every `f ∈ C_c(𝒢)`), such that for every
compact `K ⊆ 𝒢`

```text
sup_{g ∈ K} || g·m_n^{s(g)} − m_n^{r(g)} ||_1 → 0,                         (A)
```

where `g·m` is the pushforward of `m ∈ Prob(𝒢^{s(g)})` under `h ↦ gh`. This is the
standard definition (approximately invariant continuous systems of probability measures).
Everything below is proved against (A).

## Step 0. Transformation groupoids

Let `Γ` act continuously on a compact space `X`, and let `X ⋊ Γ` have arrows
`(γx, γ, x)`, topology `X × Γ`, `r = γx`, `s = x`. A group-level amenability witness is a
family of continuous `μ_n: X → Prob(Γ)` with `sup_x ||γ μ_n(x) − μ_n(γx)||_1 → 0` for
each `γ` (this is (R1) of `stw40-compact-amenable-model-and-reiter-obstruction-proof`).
Put `m_n^y` = the image of `μ_n(y)` under `η ↦ (y, η, η^{-1}y)`. For `g = (γx, γ, x)`,
`g·m_n^x` is the image of `γμ_n(x)` under `η' ↦ (γx, η', η'^{-1}γx)`, and `m_n^{γx}` is the
image of `μ_n(γx)` under the same injective map. So the error in (A) at `g` equals
`||γμ_n(x) − μ_n(γx)||_1`. A compact `K` meets only finitely many slices `X × {γ}`, so (A)
holds. Hence an amenable action gives an amenable transformation groupoid, and an
amenable action pulls back along any continuous equivariant map `p: X' → X` (use
`μ_n ∘ p`).

## Step 1. Facts about `Γ`

- `Γ` has property (T): `affine-semidirect-sl3-source-has-property-t`.
- `Γ` is infinite and not amenable: it contains `SL_2(Z) ≤ SL_3(Z)`, which contains the
  free subgroup generated by `[[1,2],[0,1]]` and `[[1,0],[2,1]]` (Sanov), and subgroups of
  amenable groups are amenable.
- `Γ` is a countable subgroup of `GL_4(Z)` via `(v, A) ↦ [[A, v],[0, 1]]`, so it has
  property A by `commutative-ring-linear-groups-have-property-a` (`R = Z`).
- For finitely generated groups, property A is exactness. Verbatim from D. Osajda,
  *Residually finite non-exact groups*, GAFA 28 (2018), arXiv:1703.03791, l.119–120, as
  recorded in `residually-finite-non-exact-groups-exist-citation`: "A finitely generated
  group is \emph{non-exact} if its reduced $C^{\ast}$--algebra is non-exact. Equivalently,
  it has no Guoliang Yu's property A".
- `Γ` is exact and nonamenable, so `stw40-unital-nuclear-no-trace-iff-exact`
  ((1) ⇒ (2)) gives a compact metrizable `Y` with a topologically amenable `Γ`-action.

## Step 2. A totally disconnected amenable model

Every compact metrizable `Y` is a continuous image `π: T → Y` of a closed subset `T` of a
product `∏_k [n_k]` of finite sets. Choose finite covers `𝒰_k` of `Y` by closed sets of
diameter `≤ 2^{-k}`. Let `T` be the set of sequences `(U_1, U_2, …)` with `U_k ∈ 𝒰_k` and
`U_1 ∩ … ∩ U_k ≠ ∅` for all `k`. `T` is closed by compactness. Put
`π(U_•) = ` the unique point of `∩_k U_k`. `π` is surjective, and continuous because
sequences agreeing up to `k` map within `2^{1-k}`.

Let `X' = {c ∈ T^Γ : π(c_γ) = γ·π(c_e) for all γ ∈ Γ}` with `(δ·c)_γ = c_{γδ}`.

- `X'` is closed in the compact metrizable, totally disconnected space `T^Γ`.
- `X'` is invariant: `π((δc)_γ) = π(c_{γδ}) = γδ·π(c_e) = γ·π((δc)_e)`.
- The formula is an action: `(δ'·(δ·c))_γ = c_{γδ'δ} = ((δ'δ)·c)_γ`.
- `p(c) = π(c_e)` is continuous and equivariant: `p(δc) = π(c_δ) = δ·p(c)`.
- `p` is surjective: for `y ∈ Y` choose `c_γ ∈ π^{-1}(γy)`.

By Step 0, `Γ ↷ X'` is topologically amenable.

## Step 3. A free minimal amenable Cantor model `M`

`elek-free-minimal-cantor-actions-with-invariant-measures` gives a free continuous
`Γ`-action on a Cantor set `Z`. Let `Γ` act diagonally on `W = X' × Z`.

- `W` is compact, metrizable and totally disconnected.
- The action on `W` is free, since it is free on `Z`.
- The action is amenable by Step 0, pulled back along the projection to `X'`.

By Zorn's lemma, `W` contains a minimal nonempty closed invariant subset `M`.

- `M` is compact, metrizable, totally disconnected, and the action on `M` is free, minimal
  and amenable.
- `M` has no isolated points. The set `I` of isolated points is open and invariant, so
  `M \ I` is closed and invariant. By minimality, `I = ∅` or `I = M`. If `I = M`, then `M`
  is compact and discrete, hence finite. A point of `M` then has a finite orbit, so a
  finite-index (hence nontrivial, as `Γ` is infinite) stabilizer, which contradicts freeness.
- So `M` is a Cantor set.

Put `H = M ⋊ Γ`. By Step 0 it is topologically amenable. It is principal because the
action is free. It is minimal because orbits are `Γ`-orbits. It is ample with Cantor unit
space.

## Step 4. `G_2`

- **Minimal and purely infinite:** Lemma `SFT>pim` with `Y = X = C`.
- **Essentially principal.** Suppose an open `W ⊆ G_2'` contains `g = (x, n, x)`.
  - If `n = 0`, then `g` is a unit.
  - If `n ≠ 0`, say `n > 0` (else invert), the basic sets give some `U_{α,β} ⊆ W`
    containing `g`, with `|α| − |β| = n`. Every arrow `(αz, n, βz)` of `U_{α,β}` lies in
    the isotropy, so `αz = βz` for all `z ∈ C`.
  - Both `α` and `β` are prefixes of `x`, so `α = βρ` with `|ρ| = n ≥ 1`. Then `ρz = z`
    for all `z ∈ C`, which is false for any `z` whose first letter differs from that of
    `ρ`.
  - Hence `int G_2' = C`.
- **Topologically amenable.** Put `m_N^x = (1/N) Σ_{0 ≤ j < N} δ_{(x, j, σ^j x)}`.
  - `(x, j, σ^j x) ∈ G_2` (take `k = j`, `l = 0`), and `x ↦ (x, j, σ^j x)` is continuous
    (on `C_μ` with `|μ| = j` it is the inverse of `s` on `U_{μ,∅}`). So `x ↦ m_N^x` is
    continuous.
  - Let `g = (μz, a − b, νz) ∈ U_{μ,ν}` with `a = |μ|`, `b = |ν|`. Then `g·m_N^{νz}` is
    the average of `δ_{(μz, a−b+j, σ^j νz)}` over `j < N`, and `σ^j(νz) = σ^{j−b} z` for
    `j ≥ b`. The average `m_N^{μz}` is over `δ_{(μz, i, σ^i μz)}` with
    `σ^i(μz) = σ^{i−a} z` for `i ≥ a`.
  - The terms with `j ∈ [b, N)` and `i = j − b + a ∈ [a, N − b + a)` coincide. At most
    `a + b` terms on each side are unmatched, so
    `||g·m_N^{s(g)} − m_N^{r(g)}||_1 ≤ 2(a+b)/N`.
  - A compact `K` is covered by finitely many `U_{μ,ν}`, so (A) holds.
- **Topological full group:** `[[G_2]] ≅ V` (l.130–132). This is not used in the proof.

## Step 5. The product `𝒢 = H × G_2`

`𝒢` has unit space `M × C`, a Cantor set. It is a second countable, Hausdorff, ample
étale groupoid, with bisections spanned by products of compact open bisections.

1. **Amenable.** Put `m_n^{(u,x)} = m_n^u ⊗ m_n^x`. For `(h, g)` in `K_1 × K_2`,
   `||(h,g)·(μ⊗ν) − μ'⊗ν'||_1 ≤ ||h·μ − μ'||_1 + ||g·ν − ν'||_1`. Every compact set lies
   in a product of compacts, so (A) follows from Steps 3 and 4.
2. **Minimal.** The orbit of `(u, x)` is `Γu × G_2(x)`, a product of dense sets.
3. **Essentially principal.** `𝒢' = H' × G_2'`. An open subset of `𝒢'` around `(h, g)`
   contains a basic `O × P` with `O ⊆ H'` and `P ⊆ G_2'` open. So `O ⊆ int H' = M` and
   `P ⊆ int G_2' = C`. Hence `int 𝒢' = M × C`.
4. **Purely infinite.** A clopen `A ⊆ M × C` is a finite disjoint union of rectangles
   `B_i × D_i` with `B_i ⊆ M` and `D_i ⊆ C` clopen (compactness plus clopen bases).
   - By Step 4, each `D_i` is properly infinite in `G_2`, via `U_i, V_i`.
   - Put `U = ⊔_i B_i × U_i` and `V = ⊔_i B_i × V_i` (with `B_i` viewed as units of `H`).
     These are compact open `𝒢`-sets, because the sources `B_i × D_i` and the ranges
     `B_i × r(U_i)` are pairwise disjoint.
   - `s(U) = s(V) = A`, `r(U) ∪ r(V) ⊆ A`, and `r(U) ∩ r(V) = ⊔_i B_i × (r(U_i) ∩ r(V_i)) = ∅`.
5. **`Γ ≤ [[𝒢]]`.** For `γ ∈ Γ` put `U_γ = {(γu, γ, u) : u ∈ M} × C`, which is
   `(M × {γ}) × C`.
   - `U_γ` is a compact open `𝒢`-set with `s(U_γ) = r(U_γ) = M × C`, and
     `π_{U_γ}(u, x) = (γu, x)`.
   - `γ ↦ π_{U_γ}` is a homomorphism into `[[𝒢]]` (l.404–406). It is injective because
     the action on `M` is free and `M ≠ ∅`.

## Conclusion

`[[𝒢]]` contains the infinite property (T) group `Γ`. The Haagerup property passes to
subgroups, and a countable group with property (T) and the Haagerup property is finite
(Cherix–Cowling–Jolissaint–Julg–Valette, as recorded in `thompson-v-has-haagerup-property`).
So `[[𝒢]]` is not a-T-menable and has an infinite Kazhdan subgroup. ∎

## Application to `nV`

Brin's `nV` consists of the homeomorphisms of `C^n` that, on the pieces of a finite
partition into bricks `C_{μ_1} × … × C_{μ_n}`, replace the prefixes coordinatewise
by `ν_1, …, ν_n`.

- Such a piece is `π` of the product bisection `U_{ν_1,μ_1} × … × U_{ν_n,μ_n}` of `G_2^n`.
- The disjoint union over the partition is a compact open `G_2^n`-set with source and
  range `C^n`.
- So `nV ≤ [[G_2^n]]`.
- Steps 4 and 5 (items 1–4, with `H` replaced by `G_2^{n−1}`) show that `G_2^n` is also
  amenable, minimal, essentially principal and purely infinite.

So `G_2^n` and `𝒢` lie in the same class, and no proof of the Haagerup property for `nV`,
or of finiteness of its Kazhdan subgroups, can use only membership in that class.
