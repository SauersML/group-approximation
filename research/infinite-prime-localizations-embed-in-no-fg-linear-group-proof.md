Let `Γ <= GL_N(F)` be generated by `γ_1, ..., γ_r`. Let `A <= F` be the subring
generated by the entries of the `γ_i` and the `γ_i^-1`. Then `A` is a finitely
generated domain and `Γ <= GL_N(A)`. Suppose `ι: Z[1/P] -> Γ` is injective. Put
`g = ι(1)`; for every `q in P` and `j >= 1`, `h_j = ι(1/q^j) in GL_N(A)` satisfies
`h_j^(q^j) = g`, and `g` has infinite order.

## Characteristic 0

**(a) Only finitely many primes are units of `A`.** `A ⊗ Q` is a finitely
generated `Q`-domain. By Noether normalization it is finite over a polynomial
ring `Q[x_1, ..., x_d]`, and after scaling we may take `x_i in A`. Clearing
denominators in monic equations for the generators of `A` gives an integer
`D >= 1` such that `A[1/D]` is integral over `R = Z[1/D][x_1, ..., x_d]`. If a
prime `q ∤ D` were a unit of `A`, then `1/q in Frac(R)` would be integral over the
integrally closed ring `R` (a UFD), hence in `R`, which it is not.

**(b) A valuation ring for each other prime.** Fix a prime `q ∤ D`.
- The Gauss valuation `v_q` on `Q(x_1, ..., x_d)` has valuation ring `R_(q)`,
  contains `R`, and has residue field `F_q(x_1, ..., x_d)`.
- `K = Frac(A)` is a finite, separable (characteristic 0) extension of
  `Q(x_1, ..., x_d)`. So the integral closure of `R_(q)` in `K` is a Dedekind
  domain, and localizing it at a maximal ideal over `q` gives a DVR `O ⊆ K`.
- `O` contains every element of `K` integral over `R_(q)`, in particular `A`.
- `q` lies in its maximal ideal, and its residue field is finite over
  `F_q(x_1, ..., x_d)`, hence finitely generated over `F_q`.

**(c) Conclusion.** `P` is infinite, so some `q in P` does not divide `D`. Take
`O` as in (b). Then `g, h_j in GL_N(O)`, and
`infinitely-q-divisible-dvr-matrices-are-torsion` makes `g` torsion, a
contradiction.

## Characteristic p > 0

Let `q in P` be any prime. Choose `e_0` with `p^(e_0) >= N`. Unipotent elements
of `GL_N` in characteristic `p` have order dividing `p^(e_0)`.
- So `g' = g^(p^(e_0))` is semisimple and of infinite order.
- `h'_j = h_j^(p^(e_0))` satisfies `h'_j^(q^j) = g'`.
- Since `g'` is semisimple of infinite order, some eigenvalue `λ` of `g'` is not
  a root of unity.
- `λ` is algebraic of degree `<= N` over `K = Frac(A)`, a finitely generated
  field; put `F_0 = K(λ)`.
- `λ` is not algebraic over `F_p`: the nonzero elements algebraic over `F_p` are
  roots of unity.

Extend `λ` to a transcendence basis `λ, t_2, ..., t_d` of `F_0` over `F_p`. The
`λ`-adic valuation of `F_p(t_2, ..., t_d)(λ)` is discrete with value 1 at `λ`.
Extend it to a valuation `w` of the finite extension `F_0`: it is discrete, and
`w(λ) = c` for some integer `c >= 1` once the value group is normalized to `Z`.

For each `j`, some eigenvalue `β_j` of `h'_j` satisfies `β_j^(q^j) = λ`, and
`[F_0(β_j) : F_0] <= [K(β_j) : K] <= N`. Extend `w` to `w_j` on `F_0(β_j)`. The
value group of `w_j` contains `Z` with index at most `N`, so it lies in
`(1/N!) Z`. Then `c = w_j(λ) = q^j w_j(β_j) in q^j (1/N!) Z`, i.e. `q^j` divides
`c · N!` for every `j`, which is impossible.

So no eigenvalue of `g'` can fail to be a root of unity. Then `g'`, being
semisimple with all eigenvalues roots of unity, has finite order, contradicting
the choice of `g'`. The argument used only one prime `q`, which gives the
stronger remark in the target. ∎
