Let `t` be the stable word supplied by the private sixfold equivariant edge,
and let `L,L'` be its old and fresh reset subgroups.  A direct attachment of
the two binary endpoint words would impose

```text
t r_0 t^(-1)=Z_X,             t r_1 t^(-1)=Z_Y,         (EGC1)
```

with both `Z_X,Z_Y` in the fresh multiplicity commutant of `L'`.  Then for
every `ell in L`, conjugating

```text
[Z_j,t ell t^(-1)]=1
```

back by `t` gives `[r_j,ell]=1`.  Thus both `r_0,r_1` would have to
centralize `L`.

This is impossible in the crossed chart.  Its reset commutant meets the raw
radical plane in exactly

```text
<r_0+r_1>,                                               (EGC2)
```

so neither independent basis word centralizes the reset.  Consequently a
single synchronized stable letter cannot attach both endpoint events by
ordinary outside-word conjugacy.  Equivariance does not alter this
commutator obstruction.

Nor can the construction merge the six private residual packets and then
install one common endpoint algebra.  The exact regular-edge audit shows
that the literal residual-sharing quotient has a `150`-dimensional kernel.
It destroys the injective edge before any endpoint law is read.

The remaining possibility is to keep the residual packets private and use
fresh global BCS words only after compression to the common forbidden or
off-capacity multiplicity source `Q`.  An unconditional covariance relator

```text
v a v^(-1) approximately Z
```

does not supply this compressed attachment, because

```text
a v Q-v Q Z=(a v-v Z)Q+v(ZQ-QZ).                       (EGC3)
```

The ordinary conjugacy defect pays only the first term; the second is exactly
the unknown source commutator.  It can be nonzero when the global relator is
exact.  Therefore a valid semantic attachment must control a capacity-gated
mixed row on one common source projection, including its commutators with the
original shared BCS tuple.  Context-private sources or uncompressed global
conjugacy are insufficient.

This is a necessity theorem, not a construction of that cell.  It leaves
`s3-equivariant-endpoint-conventions-use-global-bcs-words` open, but reduces
its surviving content to the same matrix-only common-source interface that
appears in `capacity-gated-common-source-gram-word-cell`.
