## Proof

Let `rho` be a corona homomorphism of `E_K`; homomorphisms to MF groups
reduce to this case.

`F` is a finite subgroup normalized by `Gamma`.  The coordinate of `F` at
the point `alpha(Gamma) in Omega` is centralized by the stabilizer of that
point in `Gamma`, which is `alpha(Gamma) = t Gamma t^(-1)`.

Apply the last assertion of the finite-dimensional transport theorem of
`non_mf_groups_exist.tex` with `L = Gamma`: a finite subgroup normalized by
`L`, one of whose elements centralizes `t L t^(-1)`, has that element sent
to something centralizing `rho(L)`.  So `rho(k_(alpha(Gamma)))` commutes
with `rho(Gamma)`.

For `g in Gamma`, conjugation carries the coordinate at `alpha(Gamma)` to
the one at `g alpha(Gamma)`:

```text
g k_(alpha(Gamma)) g^(-1) = k_(g alpha(Gamma)).
```

Applying `rho` and using that `rho(g)` commutes with
`rho(k_(alpha(Gamma)))` gives `rho(k_(g alpha(Gamma))) =
rho(k_(alpha(Gamma)))`.  `Gamma` acts transitively on `Omega =
Gamma/alpha(Gamma)`, so this covers every coordinate.

## The two corollaries

For **perfect** `K` and `m > 1`, two distinct coordinates of `F = K^Omega`
commute, so the common image is a homomorphic image of the abelianization
of `K`, which is trivial.

For `K = C_2` and `m >= 2`, take `omega != omega'`.  The element
`z_omega z_(omega')` is nontrivial in `C_2^Omega`, and the identification
sends it to `1` under every `rho`.  So it lies in `Rad_MF(E_(C_2))`, which
is therefore nontrivial.

## Where perfection is genuinely needed

A separate, purely group-theoretic remark explains why the compression
criterion alone cannot reach `E_K` for perfect `K`.  If `F` is generated by
subgroups `A_omega` whose images in `F/Z` form a direct product with `Z`
central, then for `x in A_omega`, `y in A_eta` with `omega != eta` the
commutator `[x,y]` lies in `Z`; centrality makes `(x,y) -> [x,y]` a
homomorphism in each variable into an abelian group, so it factors through
`A_omega^(ab) x A_eta^(ab)`.  **Perfect blocks therefore commute pairwise in
every central extension**, and the defect the construction supplies is a
product `c_0 c_1^(-1)` of elements of two distinct blocks lying in the block
kernel `B_K` — a free product of finite groups, whose property-(T) subgroups
are finite and which has no nontrivial finite normal subgroup.  So the
criterion has no normal Kazhdan subgroup to act on, and the
finite-dimensional route is the only one.
