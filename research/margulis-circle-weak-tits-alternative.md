**ESTABLISHED by citation.** Let `Γ ≤ Homeo_+(S^1)` be any subgroup. Then either `S^1` carries
a `Γ`-invariant Borel probability measure, or `Γ` contains a non-abelian free subgroup.

Source: G. Margulis, *Free subgroups of the homeomorphism group of the circle*, C. R. Acad.
Sci. Paris Sér. I Math. 331 (2000) 669–674. The statement was conjectured by Ghys. It is
quoted verbatim below from Navas's monograph, which also gives Ghys's later proof.

Verbatim, A. Navas, *Groups of circle diffeomorphisms*, arXiv:math/0607481, TeX source
`navas.tex`, fetched 2026-09-17:
- l.5567–5569: "The following result was conjectured by Ghys [...] and proved by Margulis in
  \cite{Ma1}."
- l.5575–5578: "\begin{thm} {\em If \esp $\Gamma$ is a subgroup of
  $\mathrm{Homeo}_+(\clo)$, then either there exists a probability measure on
  $\mathrm{S}^1$ which is invariant by $\Gamma$, or $\Gamma$ contains a free subgroup on two
  generators.}"
- l.18858–18859: "\bibitem{Ma1} {\sc G. Margulis.} Free subgroups of the homeomorphism group
  of the circle. {\em C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris S\'{e}r. I Math.} {\bf 331} (2000), 669-674."

(`\clo` is Navas's macro for `\mathrm{S}^1`, and `[...]` marks index commands left out.)

**Not a dichotomy.** Navas notes (l.5587–5590) that `F_2` acts faithfully on the interval, so
a circle group can have both an invariant measure and a free subgroup. Only the direction
"no free subgroup ⟹ invariant measure" is used on this graph.
