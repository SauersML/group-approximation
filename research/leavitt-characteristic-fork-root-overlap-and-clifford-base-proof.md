# The characteristic fork, root-overlap modulus, and Clifford-base locality proof

Let \(\mathcal A_I\) denote the fixed finite complex SynchBCS algebra in the
target claim. We use only the following established properties:

1. \(\mathcal A_I\) is a unital complex star algebra generated by finitely
   many projections;
2. it has no unital finite-dimensional star representation;
3. every tracial state on it is non-Connes-embeddable.

The first two properties prove the local firewalls. The third authenticates
any eventual map into a complex group-ring corner.

## 1. A complex unital algebra cannot enter a characteristic-two corner

Let \(S\) be any ring of characteristic two and let
\(0\ne e=e^2\in S\). Suppose that

\[
 \Phi:\mathcal A_I\longrightarrow eSe,\qquad \Phi(1)=e
 \tag{1}
\]

were a unital ring homomorphism. Its restriction to the scalar copy of
\(\mathbf C\) would be a unital ring homomorphism. In \(S\),

\[
 \Phi(2\cdot 1)=2e=0.
\]

But \(2\cdot 1\) is invertible in \(\mathcal A_I\), with inverse
\((1/2)\cdot 1\). Therefore

\[
 e=\Phi(1)=\Phi((1/2)\cdot 1)\Phi(2\cdot 1)=0,
\]

a contradiction. This rules out (1) even without complex linearity or star
preservation.

In particular it rules out every coefficient-level map

\[
 \mathcal A_I\longrightarrow eL_{\mathbf F_2}(E)e.
 \tag{2}
\]

The characteristic-two choice in the binary Jacobson/Leavitt route is not a
cosmetic choice: it is what turns each elementary root into an involution.

## 2. Characteristic zero destroys the root spectral corner

For \(i\ne j\), the standard elementary root in \(EL_n(R)\) is

\[
 x_{ij}(a)=I+aE_{ij}.
\]

Since \(E_{ij}^2=0\),

\[
 x_{ij}(a)x_{ij}(b)=x_{ij}(a+b),\qquad
 x_{ij}(a)^m=x_{ij}(ma).                                  \tag{3}
\]

The root parametrization is injective in the elementary matrix group. If
\(R\) is an algebra over a characteristic-zero field and \(a\ne0\), then
\(ma\ne0\) for every \(m\ge1\). Thus \(x_{ij}(a)\) has infinite order.

For a group element \(u\), direct calculation in the complex group algebra
gives

\[
 \left(\frac{1-u}{2}\right)^2-\frac{1-u}{2}
 =\frac{u^2-1}{4}.                                        \tag{4}
\]

Consequently the standard negative sign cut is a projection exactly when
\(u^2=1\). Equation (3) shows that no nonzero elementary root over a complex
Leavitt coefficient algebra has this property. More generally, the usual
finite Fourier spectral idempotents require a finite-order root, which (3)
also excludes.

Hence the two desired features lie on opposite sides of an exact
characteristic fork:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{coefficient characteristic}&
\text{unital complex source map}&
\text{nonzero finite root spectrum}\\ \hline
2&\text{impossible by Section 1}&\text{available}\\
0&\text{scalar-compatible}&\text{absent by (3)}
\end{array}                                                \tag{5}
\]

This does not rule out finite torsion elsewhere in a characteristic-zero
elementary group. It rules out the proposed coefficient-root sign.

## 3. The canonical root trace sees XOR, not an orthogonal partition

The scalar mismatch already blocks a coefficient map over \(\mathbf F_2\),
but it is useful to compute what the root-sign rule itself does. Take

\[
 R=\mathbf F_2\times\mathbf F_2,\qquad
 e=(1,0),\qquad f=(0,1).
\]

For a fixed root position put

\[
 U=x_{ij}(e),\qquad V=x_{ij}(f),\qquad
 Q_e=\frac{1-U}{2},\qquad Q_f=\frac{1-V}{2}.               \tag{6}
\]

The elements \(U\) and \(V\) are commuting involutions and

\[
 UV=x_{ij}(e+f).
\]

Therefore \(Q_e,Q_f\) are commuting projections and

\[
 \frac{1-UV}{2}=Q_e+Q_f-2Q_eQ_f.                           \tag{7}
\]

This is the Boolean XOR identity.

Now use the canonical group trace on \(EL_n(R)\). The three group elements
\(U,V,UV\) are distinct and nonidentity: under

\[
 EL_n(R)=EL_n(\mathbf F_2)\times EL_n(\mathbf F_2)
\]

they occupy the first factor, the second factor, and both factors,
respectively. Expanding (6) gives

\[
 Q_eQ_f=\frac{1-U-V+UV}{4},
 \qquad
 \tau(Q_eQ_f)=\frac14.                                    \tag{8}
\]

Because \(Q_eQ_f\) is a projection,

\[
 \|Q_eQ_f\|_2^2=\tau(Q_eQ_f)=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \|Q_eQ_f\|_2=\frac12.                                    \tag{9}
\]

Thus the canonical group trace detects each individual root cut, but its
joint mass is the wrong one for orthogonality.

There is also a dimension-independent perturbative wall. For arbitrary
projections \(P_e,P_f\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_eP_f-Q_eQ_f
  &=(P_e-Q_e)P_f+Q_e(P_f-Q_f),\\
 \|P_eP_f-Q_eQ_f\|_2
  &\leq \|P_e-Q_e\|_2+\|P_f-Q_f\|_2.                      \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]

Combining (9), (10), and the reverse triangle inequality proves

\[
 \|P_eP_f\|_2
 \geq \frac12-\|P_e-Q_e\|_2-\|P_f-Q_f\|_2.                \tag{11}
\]

In particular, if \(P_eP_f=0\), the two corrections have total normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt size at least \(1/2\). Any successful mixed occurrence
identity must therefore make a macroscopic correction; it cannot be a
small perturbation of the individual root spectral cuts.

Notice what is and is not used. Orthogonality of the coefficient idempotents
does imply the Steinberg word relation

\[
 [x_{ij}(e),x_{jk}(f)]=x_{ik}(ef)=1,                      \tag{12}
\]

in suitable root positions. But (12) is a homogeneous group-word relation.
It does not imply the nonhomogeneous group-ring identity
\((1-U)(1-V)=0\) in (8). This is the exact occurrence seam.

## 4. The Leavitt corner literature does not supply a root promotion

Abrams--Nam prove that every nonzero corner of a Leavitt path algebra of a
finite graph is isomorphic to a Leavitt path algebra. More generally,
Abrams--Dokuchaev--Nam prove that the endomorphism algebra of a nonzero
finitely generated projective module over an arbitrary-graph Leavitt path
algebra is a Steinberg algebra (Theorem 3.6), and deduce the corner statement
in Corollary 3.8.

Here a Steinberg algebra is the convolution algebra of an ample groupoid.
It is not a group algebra of a Steinberg group. Neither theorem constructs
a map from a coefficient corner to a group-ring corner, and the elementary
root map cannot fill that gap: (3) sends coefficient addition to group
multiplication, while (7) shows that its spectral cut sends addition to XOR.

The finite-dimensional representation classification of Koc--Ozaydin does
not create a matrix absorber in the purely infinite simple case. Indeed, if
\(L\) is a unital simple infinite-dimensional algebra, every unital
homomorphism \(L\to M_d(\mathbf C)\) has ideal kernel. It is nonzero, hence
injective, which is impossible by vector-space dimension. Thus a purely
infinite simple complex Leavitt path algebra has no unital finite-dimensional
representation. The lack of a matrix model for \(\mathcal A_I\) therefore
does not by itself rule out a direct unital map

\[
 \mathcal A_I\longrightarrow eL_{\mathbf C}(E)e.           \tag{13}
\]

We leave (13) open.

What fails is the proposed second arrow. Let
\(0\ne q=q^*=q^2\in\mathbf C[\Gamma]\). The canonical coefficient trace is
faithful on the algebraic group ring, so

\[
 \tau_q(a)=\frac{\tau_\Gamma(a)}{\tau_\Gamma(q)}
 \quad(a\in q\mathbf C[\Gamma]q)                           \tag{14}
\]

is a tracial state. If a complex Leavitt corner \(L\) has properly infinite
unit, choose two isometries \(s_0,s_1\in L\) with orthogonal range
projections. A tracial state \(\sigma\) would satisfy

\[
 \sigma(s_i s_i^*)=\sigma(s_i^*s_i)=1\quad(i=0,1),
\]

whereas positivity and
\(s_0s_0^*+s_1s_1^*\leq1\) give

\[
 2=\sigma(s_0s_0^*+s_1s_1^*)\leq\sigma(1)=1.              \tag{15}
\]

Thus no such trace exists. This applies to the binary Leavitt algebra and to
the properly infinite unit corners in the purely infinite simple
finite-graph route. Composing a hypothetical unital star homomorphism

\[
 L\longrightarrow q\mathbf C[\Gamma]q
\]

with (14) would contradict (15). Therefore the Leavitt coefficient corner
cannot itself be placed unitally in the desired algebraic group corner.

This is a direction-sensitive statement. It does not rule out (13), and a
nonunital or merely linear map is useless for the SynchBCS unit and PVM
relations.

Primary-source audit:

- Abrams--Nam, main theorem:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03641
- Abrams--Dokuchaev--Nam, Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 3.8:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03964
- Koc--Ozaydin, finite-dimensional module classification:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04622
- Abrams--Aranda Pino, row-finite purely infinite simplicity criterion:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0509496

None of these statements asserts (13), the group-corner arrow following it,
or preservation of the canonical group trace through elementary roots.

## 5. The shared-center Clifford base is locally matricial

Let \(X\) be infinite and let \(E(X)\) be the shared-center extraspecial
group, with common central involution \(J\). Put

\[
 p_-=\frac{1-J}{2}.
\]

For every finite \(F\subset X\), the negative central-character block of the
finite extraspecial group is the unique spin matrix algebra:

\[
 p_-\mathbf C[E(F)]p_-\cong M_{2^{|F|}}(\mathbf C).        \tag{16}
\]

The inclusions for nested site windows are unital, with common unit \(p_-\),
and

\[
 p_-\mathbf C[E(X)]p_-=
 \bigcup_{F\Subset X}p_-\mathbf C[E(F)]p_-.                \tag{17}
\]

Suppose a unital star homomorphism

\[
 \Phi:\mathcal A_I\longrightarrow p_-\mathbf C[E(X)]p_-   \tag{18}
\]

existed. Choose a finite generating set \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\) for
\(\mathcal A_I\). Each \(\Phi(a_k)\) is an algebraic group-ring expression,
so it has finite group support; every element of that support uses only
finitely many sites. The union over \(k\) is contained in some finite
\(F\Subset X\). Hence all generator images, and therefore the whole image of
\(\Phi\), lie in the single algebra in (16). Equation (18) is then a unital
finite-dimensional star representation of \(\mathcal A_I\), a contradiction.

This proof uses algebraic finite support. It would not apply to arbitrary
elements of the norm-completed UHF algebra, but the target nonhyperlinearity
criterion requires finite algebraic group-ring expressions.

## 6. Trace audit in the full actor corner

Now put

\[
 \Pi=E(X)\rtimes G.
\]

Since \(J\ne1\), the canonical group trace satisfies

\[
 \tau_\Pi(p_-)=\frac12.                                   \tag{19}
\]

The normalized canonical trace on
\(p_-\mathbf C[\Pi]p_-\) is therefore a genuine tracial state. By Section 5,
a unital image of \(\mathcal A_I\) in this full corner cannot be contained in
the zero-actor Fourier coefficient. At least one source generator must have
a nonidentity actor coefficient.

If such an algebraic unital map is constructed, its pullback of the
normalized trace is a tracial state on \(\mathcal A_I\). Injectivity and
faithfulness of the pullback are irrelevant: every trace on
\(\mathcal A_I\) is non-CE. If \(\Pi\) were hyperlinear, its canonical trace,
its nonzero corner, and the tracial algebra generated by the source image
would all be Connes embeddable. This is impossible. Thus the mixed actor map
would already prove that \(\Pi\) is nonhyperlinear, exactly as in
`nonce-bcs-unital-algebraic-corner-map-forces-nonhyperlinear`.

No such mixed map is constructed here. The conclusion is the sharp division
of labor:

- a complex Leavitt coefficient corner may still be large enough for the
  source, but cannot be promoted by standard roots or embedded as a unital
  traceless corner in a group ring;
- the Clifford base has the correct fixed-density group spectral corner, but
  is locally matricial and therefore too small for the source;
- the first live construction must use genuinely mixed root/constant
  occurrence terms or genuinely mixed base/actor Fourier coefficients.

That is the exact residual gate, and Cairn remains OPEN.
