By `lef-implies-operator-mf`, the LEF hypothesis supplies an injective
homomorphism

```text
iota : G -> U((prod_k M_(d_k)) / (oplus_k M_(d_k)))
```

for a sequence of finite dimensions.  Recall that its construction is
elementary: embed an exhausting finite window of `G` into a finite group,
apply the finite group's left regular permutation representation, and pass
to the corona.  Multiplication becomes eventually exact on each fixed pair.
If `g != 1`, then eventually its local image is nonidentity; left translation
by that image is fixed-point free, and its regular permutation matrix stays
at distance at least `sqrt(3)` from the identity.  Thus `iota(g) != 1`, which
is the required injectivity.  No finite-group classification or restricted
Burnside theorem enters.

The image is infinite because `iota` is injective.  It is generated by the
images of any fixed finite generating set of `G`.  Finally, for every `g in G`,

```text
iota(g)^N = iota(g^N) = iota(1) = 1,
```

so the image has exponent dividing `N`.  It is therefore a direct
counterexample to `operator-norm-restricted-burnside`.  The same embedding
shows that `G` is MF, directly contradicting the conclusion of
`infinite-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-non-mf` for this `G`.

Notice what is and is not proved.  The implication above is unconditional
and internal.  Producing its premise -- one infinite finitely generated
bounded-exponent LEF group -- is a separate group-theoretic problem and is
not supplied here.
