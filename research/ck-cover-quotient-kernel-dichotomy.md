Let S be infinite simple, let Z[S] embed in a unital ring U with
1-s_0 full for some s_0!=1, and write Q=EL_n(U), n>=3, with
D={diag(s,1,...,1):s in S} contained in Q.

Suppose rho:Gamma->Q is surjective, Gamma is finitely generated and
contains a marked copy of S mapping to D by the diagonal isomorphism,
and Gamma is normally generated by s_0. Put K=ker(rho).

For any proper normal subgroup M of Gamma, set L=Gamma/M,
N=rho(M), and let S_M be the image of S in L. Then S_M is isomorphic
to S and:

1. If N is proper in Q, S_M has infinitely many double cosets in L.
2. If S_M has finitely many double cosets in L, then N=Q, Gamma=MK,
   and the restriction K->L is surjective with kernel K intersect M.
3. If M is maximal proper normal, exactly one of the following holds:
   K is contained in M and L is a simple quotient of Q; or N=Q and
   L is a simple quotient of K.
4. If K is solvable or locally finite, the second case of (3) is
   impossible, and no proper quotient of Gamma has S_M of finite bi-index.

The theorem gives a necessary condition for repairing the canonical coset
action by a quotient whose map does not descend to a nontrivial matrix
quotient. It does not establish a suitable quotient of K, does not prove
that this particular K is solvable or locally finite, and does not rule
out finitely presented simple quotients with infinite core bi-index.
