**ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** Let `H` be a countable group with an infinite subgroup `H_0` that has
property (T). Assume `H_0` is **wq-normal** in `H`: there are subgroups `H_β <= H`, indexed by the ordinals
`β <= α`, with `H_α = H`. Each `H_(β+1)` is generated by the `a ∈ H` with `H_β ∩ aH_βa^(-1)` infinite, and
`H_λ = ∪_(β<λ) H_β` at limit ordinals `λ`.

Let `(Y,ν)` be a nontrivial standard probability space, and let `H ↷ (Z,ζ)` be an essentially free factor of
the Bernoulli shift `H ↷ (Y,ν)^H`, for instance the shift itself. Let `G ↷ (X,μ)` be an essentially free
p.m.p. action of a countable group, and `A ⊆ X` measurable with `μ(A) > 0`. Let `θ : (Z,ζ) → (A,μ_A)` be a
measure-space isomorphism with `θ(h·z) ∈ G·θ(z)` for every `h ∈ H` and a.e. `z`. Then there is a morphism
`ρ : H → G` with finite kernel.

## Consequences

1. **No sofic host.** Suppose `G` is sofic and no quotient of `H` by a finite normal subgroup is sofic. Then
   no such `θ` exists, since `H/ker ρ` embeds in `G` (`sofic-passes-to-subgroups`).
2. **Overgroups.** Let `H' >= H` be countable. The restriction of the Bernoulli shift of `H'` to `H` is the
   Bernoulli shift of `H` over the base `(Y,ν)^(H\H')`, and its orbit relation is a subrelation. So a containment
   for `H'` gives a finite-kernel morphism `H → G`.
3. **Kun--Thom wreaths.** Take `W = (Z/2Z) wr_(G_1/Γ) G_1` for an infranormal, non-normal pair `Γ < G_1`
   with both groups Kazhdan (`kun-thom-nonsofic-wreath`, Theorem A). The hypothesis holds with `H_0 = G_1`
   and `H_1 = W`, and every quotient of `W` by a finite normal subgroup is again such a wreath, so it is not
   sofic. So Bernoulli seeds of `W`, and of every countable group containing `W`, have no sofic host. The
   binary Leavitt unit group is the simple case, already recorded in
   `leavitt-unit-bernoulli-relations-have-no-sofic-host`.

## What it leaves

The statement covers factors of Bernoulli shifts only. The proof uses only two facts: the extension is
mixing, and its restriction to `H_0` meets Popa's Theorem 0.1. It says nothing about free actions of these
groups that are not of this kind. Examples are algebraic, homogeneous or co-induced actions, and products
`Bernoulli × τ` with `τ` arbitrary, for which Theorem 0.1 untwists the cocycle only to a cocycle of `τ`.
