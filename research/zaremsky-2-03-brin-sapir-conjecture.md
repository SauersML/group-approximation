M. C. B. Zaremsky, *Some open problems* (version of July 12, 2026,
https://zaremsky.github.io/open_problems.pdf), Section 2, Problem 3, verbatim:
"Is every subgroup of F either elementary amenable or else contains a copy of F?
(Brin–Sapir Conjecture)".

This claim is the question. It is established only through an answer route:

- **Yes**: `zaremsky-2-03-by-yes` requires
  `every-subgroup-of-thompson-f-is-ea-or-contains-f`.
- **No**: `zaremsky-2-03-by-no` requires
  `some-subgroup-of-thompson-f-is-neither-ea-nor-contains-f`.

Never write a `requires: []` route into this claim.

**Precise reading.** `F` is Richard Thompson's group, realized as the group of
orientation-preserving piecewise linear homeomorphisms of `[0,1]` with dyadic
breakpoints and slopes in `2^Z`. The elementary amenable groups form the smallest
class of groups containing all finite groups and all abelian groups and closed
under subgroups, quotients, extensions and directed unions. "Contains a copy of
F" means "has a subgroup isomorphic to F". Since `F` is not elementary amenable
(`thompson-f-has-no-free-subgroups`), the two alternatives exclude each other, and
the question asks whether "elementary amenable" and "contains no copy of F"
coincide for subgroups of `F`.

Elementary amenable groups are closed under directed unions, and a group contains
a copy of `F` exactly when one of its finitely generated subgroups does (`F` is
finitely generated). So the question is equivalent to its restriction to finitely
generated subgroups of `F`.

**History and variants.** Brin, arXiv:math/0501085, Conjecture 3, states the
version for the ambient group: "Every subgroup of PL_o(I) is either elementary
amenable or it contains a subgroup that is isomorphic to F." Bleak, Brin and Moore,
arXiv:1711.10998, Conjecture 1: "If G is a subgroup of PL_+(I), then either G is
elementarily amenable or else G contains a copy of F", attributed there to Brin and
Sapir, with "While we do not settle Conjecture 1". A bounded web search on 2026-09-13
(arXiv abstracts, general web) found no answer, before or after the list's date.

**Known partial results** (from the papers' abstracts and first pages):

- Brin–Squier, Invent. Math. 79 (1985): `PL_o(I)` has no non-abelian free subgroup.
- Brin, J. London Math. Soc. (2) 60 (1999) 449–460, arXiv:math/9705205, Theorem 1:
  a subgroup of `PL_o(I)` with an orbital `(a,b)` in which some element approaches
  one end but not the other contains a copy of `F`
  (`brin-plo-i-one-sided-approach-gives-a-copy-of-f`).
- Brin, arXiv:math/0501085, Theorem 1: for each non-limit ordinal `α ≤ ω²+1` there
  is a subgroup of `F` of elementary class `α`.
- Bleak, arXiv:math/0602038 (J. Algebra 319 (2008)): algebraic classification of
  the solvable subgroups of `PL_o(I)`; Bleak, arXiv:math/0608182 (Groups Geom. Dyn.
  3 (2009)): one non-solvable group `W` embeds in every non-solvable subgroup of
  `PL_o(I)`.
- Kim–Koberda–Lodha, arXiv:1610.04099, Theorem 1.1: large powers of homeomorphisms
  supported on a chain of intervals generate a chain group; for two overlapping
  bumps this is a copy of `F` (`kim-koberda-lodha-2-prechain-powers-generate-f`).
- Bleak–Brin–Kassabov–Moore–Zaremsky, arXiv:1701.08321: groups generated by "fast"
  families of homeomorphisms have marked isomorphism type determined by finite
  dynamical data, and embed in `F`.
- Taylor, arXiv:1708.01290: locally solvable subgroups of `PL_o(I)` are countable,
  and `F` has uncountably many non-isomorphic locally solvable subgroups.
- Bleak–Brin–Moore, arXiv:1711.10998, Theorem 1: finitely generated elementary
  amenable subgroups of `F` realize EA-class `α+2` for every `α < ε_0`, and their
  Theorem 4 gives two such subgroups, neither embedding in the other
  (`f-has-ea-subgroups-of-every-class-below-epsilon-zero`).

## Attempts

- 2026-09-13, lane z2-03-brin-sapir. Established here (unreviewed):
  - `plo-i-group-is-ea-iff-its-orbital-germ-kernels-are`: a finitely generated
    subgroup of `PL_o(I)` is elementary amenable iff the germ-trivial kernels on its
    finitely many orbitals are; a non-EA one carries an infinite compactly nested
    descent of non-EA germ-trivial kernels.
  - `f-free-plo-i-groups-have-equal-end-germ-kernels`: in an `F`-free subgroup of
    `PL_o(I)`, on every orbital of every subgroup, an element is the identity near
    one end iff it is the identity near the other.
  - `two-one-bump-pl-maps-unequal-supports-ea-or-contain-f`: two one-bump generators
    with different supports generate `ℤ²`, `ℤ ≀ ℤ`, or a group containing `F`.
- Open crux, affirmative side: a rank on pairs (subgroup, orbital) of `F`-free groups
  that drops along germ-kernel descent steps. Any such rank bounds EA-class, so by
  Bleak–Brin–Moore its values exceed every ordinal below `ε_0`.
- Open crux, both sides: two one-bump generators with the same support, and the
  conjectured laminarity of orbitals of subgroups of `F`-free groups. Details in the
  Attempts of the two answer claims.
- 2026-09-17, lane sw-105 (inverter). Established here (unreviewed):
  `brin-sapir-is-equivalent-to-equal-support-one-bump-pairs`. The equal-support crux
  above is not a sub-case: it is the whole conjecture. A counterexample exists iff one
  exists of the form `⟨x_0, u x_0 v⟩` with `u, v` supported in `[1/8, 3/16]`, and `u`
  can be fixed to one conjugate of `x_0^{-1}` into that interval. The construction
  places the generators of an arbitrary `H` in `x_0`-translated blocks along a Sidon
  set of positions and multiplies them into `x_0^{-1}`. Block width 1 gives only
  `ℤ ≀ ℤ`; width 2 is already universal. So arguments that see only supports, germs,
  or one-sided approach of two generators are exhausted.
- Dead end, same lane. A minimal counterexample under embeddability (Bleak–Brin–Moore
  Conjecture 3) embeds in its own compactly supported germ kernel. This yields no
  contradiction: an ascending HNN extension along a PL conjugacy keeps both the EA and
  the `F`-containment status, and EA-class counting gives no bound.
