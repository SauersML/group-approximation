# Use one constant root in each Steinberg commutator

## 1. Constant roots survive every unital compression

For \(i\ne j\) and \(f\in S\), write
\[
 e_{ij}(f)=I_r+fE_{ij}.
\]
The induced endomorphism on the elementary group is
\[
 \beta_*(e_{ij}(f))=e_{ij}(\beta(f)).
\]
Because \(\beta\) is unital,
\[
 \beta(1)=1.
\]
Therefore every constant elementary root belongs to the compressed copy:
\[
 e_{ij}(1)=\beta_*(e_{ij}(1))\in\Lambda.                 \tag{1}
\]

For the Kun--Thom monomial compressor this is literal: monomial
substitution changes exponent vectors but fixes the coefficient \(1\).
No injectivity, strictness, or cone calculation is needed for (1).

## 2. One Steinberg commutator reaches every root

Let
\[
 N=\langle\!\langle\Lambda\rangle\!\rangle_\Gamma.
\]
Fix \(i\ne j\), choose \(k\) distinct from both, and let \(f\in S\).
The Steinberg commutator relation in \(EL_r(S)\) is
\[
 [e_{ik}(f),e_{kj}(1)]=e_{ij}(f).                        \tag{2}
\]
The second entry in the commutator lies in \(\Lambda\) by (1). Since
\(N\) is normal in \(\Gamma\), every commutator
\[
 [g,\lambda]=g\lambda g^{-1}\lambda^{-1}
\]
with \(g\in\Gamma\) and \(\lambda\in\Lambda\) belongs to \(N\).
Equation (2) therefore gives
\[
 e_{ij}(f)\in N                                           \tag{3}
\]
for every root position and every coefficient.

By definition \(EL_r(S)\) is generated by these elementary roots. Hence
\[
 N=\Gamma.                                                \tag{4}
\]
This proves (KTN1). The use of a third index is the only reason for
\(r\ge3\).

## 3. Every finite-selector action collapses before the amalgam is formed

Let
\[
 \alpha:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aut}(K)
\]
be a homomorphism and suppose that \(\alpha(\lambda)=\mathrm{id}_K\)
for all \(\lambda\in\Lambda\). Then
\[
 \Lambda\le\ker\alpha.
\]
The kernel is normal in \(\Gamma\), so (4) implies
\[
 \ker\alpha=\Gamma.
\]
Thus \(\alpha\) is trivial, and every difference
\(\alpha(\gamma)(k)k^{-1}\) equals \(1\). This proves (KTN2).

The conclusion applies in particular when \(K\) is finite and
\(\operatorname{Aut}(K)\) is finite. It is not a statement about the size
of the automorphism group: there is no nontrivial action into any target
group once the compressed copy is in the kernel.

For the Q8 selector, the desired action would send some element of
\(\Gamma\) to
\[
 x\longmapsto y,\qquad y\longmapsto x,\qquad z\longmapsto z.
\]
If \(\Lambda\) is required to centralize Q8, this action is impossible.
The coinvariant identity \(x=y\), which would imply \(z=1\), therefore
cannot coexist with an embedded semidirect Q8 vertex.

## 4. Why this kills the direct nonamenable-edge design

Assume \(z\in Z(K)\) is fixed by \(\alpha\). In
\[
 B=K\rtimes_\alpha\Gamma
\]
the subgroup generated by \(\Gamma\) and \(z\) is
\(\Gamma\times\langle z\rangle\). If the same subgroup embeds into
\(D=G\times\langle z\rangle\), the amalgam
\[
 B*_{\Gamma\times\langle z\rangle}D                      \tag{5}
\]
embeds \(B\), and hence \(K\), by Bass--Serre normal form. This is the
cleanest possible nonamenable-edge analogue of the one-word Q8
construction.

To activate finite-dimensional image collapse, however, the copy
\(\Lambda\) must commute with \(K\) in \(B\). In a semidirect product this
condition is exactly
\[
 \alpha|_\Lambda=1.                                      \tag{6}
\]
Sections 1--3 turn (6) into \(\alpha=1\). The putative coinvariant subgroup
is then trivial, so (5) preserves \(z\) but has no mechanism which kills it
in finite-dimensional images. The two desired exact gates cannot be met
simultaneously in this architecture.

Notice that property \((T)\) is downstream of the obstruction. A relative
Kazhdan constant cannot create a nontrivial action which (4) forbids
algebraically. Likewise, adding a Kazhdan direct-product spectator to an
edge only produces the neutral direct-product identity recorded in the
two-BS firewall.

## 5. The precise escape routes

There are two ways not covered by the argument.

First, a partial HNN relation can identify selected subgroups of \(K\)
without making all of \(\Gamma\) normalize \(K\). Such a construction is
not described by a homomorphism
\(\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aut}(K)\). Its survival must be proved by a
separate normal-form or small-cancellation theorem. The doubled
\(BS(2,3)\) calculation warns that finitely many independent partial
attachments can expose a transverse amenable splitting.

Second, the actor can move a packet at one site to genuinely different
packets at other sites. Then the finite packet is normalized only by a
stabilizer, not by all of \(\Gamma\). The shared-center extraspecial wreath
has this form. The common sign survives at fixed trace mass and is killed
in every exact finite-dimensional representation, but a fixed site window
leaves an arbitrary multiplicity unitary. Closing that moving-window
normalized-HS gate remains necessary.

Thus normal generation removes the most direct finite-selector
nonamenable-edge proposal, while leaving the infinite-packet and partial-HNN
fronts accurately open.
