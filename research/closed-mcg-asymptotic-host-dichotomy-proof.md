**Standard inputs, not re-proved.**
- **(I) Inclusion.** Let `S ⊂ Σ_h` be a compact connected subsurface of genus
  `h`, each of whose complementary components is noncompact with exactly one
  boundary circle. Extending by the identity gives a homomorphism
  `Mod(S) → PMod_c(Σ_h)`, and it is injective.
  - The image lies in `PMod_c` because every representative is the identity
    outside `S`.
  - Injectivity is how we read AF Lemma 2.1, "For g ≥ 0 and n ≥ 1,
    Mod(S_{g,n}) < Mod(Σ_g)", an "immediate observation" whose statement does
    not name the embedding.
  - For finite-type surfaces this is Farb--Margalit, *Primer*, Theorem 3.18;
    the number was not re-checked. The kernel of an inclusion homomorphism is
    generated by twists about boundary curves bounding a disk or once-punctured
    disk in the complement, and by `T_{c_i}T_{c_j}^{-1}` for boundary curves
    cobounding an annulus in the complement. Here no complementary component is
    compact.
- **(P) Capping.** Gluing a twice-punctured disk to each boundary circle of
  `S_{h,n}` induces an injective homomorphism `Mod(S_{h,n}) → Mod(S_h^{2n})`,
  by the same theorem. A class fixing a nonempty boundary pointwise preserves
  orientation.
- **Genus.** The genus of `Σ_h` is `h`, the largest genus of a compact
  subsurface.
- **Dehn--Lickorish.** `Mod(Σ)` is finitely generated.

**Step 1. Supports.** Let `k_1, …, k_s ∈ PMod_c(Σ_h)` be represented by
homeomorphisms equal to the identity outside compact subsurfaces `Z_1, …, Z_s`.
Choose `S` as in (I) containing every `Z_i` in its interior:
1. Take a compact connected subsurface containing, in its interior, every `Z_i`
   and a compact subsurface of genus `h`.
2. Add every compact component of its complement. The result `S` is compact,
   connected, of genus `h`, and every complementary component is noncompact.
3. No complementary component `P` has `k ≥ 2` boundary circles. Otherwise, gluing
   to `S` a compact connected subsurface of `P` containing them would give a
   compact subsurface of `Σ_h` of genus at least `h + k − 1 > h`.

Each `k_i` restricts to a homeomorphism of `S` fixing `∂S` pointwise, so
`⟨k_1, …, k_s⟩` lies in the image of `Mod(S)`. `Σ_h` is noncompact, so
`S ≅ S_{h,n}` with `n ≥ 1`. By (I), every finitely generated subgroup of
`PMod_c(Σ_h)` embeds in `Mod(S_{h,n})` for some `n ≥ 1`.

**Step 2. Part (a).**
1. If `π∘ρ(L)` is finite, then `K` has finite index in `L`, hence in `Mod(Σ)`,
   and `K` is finitely generated.
2. `ρ(K) ≤ ker π = PMod_c(Σ_h)`, by AF Propositions 4.2 and 4.6.
3. By Step 1, `K ≅ ρ(K)` embeds in `Mod(S_{h,n})` with `n ≥ 1`. By (P) it embeds
   in `Mod(S_h^{2n})`, which lies in the extended mapping class group.

This is the premise, with finite-index subgroup `K` and `Σ′` the genus-`h`
surface with `2n` punctures.

**Step 3. Part (b).** If `K = 1`, then `π∘ρ` embeds `L` in `V`, contradicting
`closed-mcg-finite-index-subgroups-do-not-embed-in-v`. `K` is normal in `L`, and
it has infinite index when the image is infinite. Its finitely generated
subgroups embed in bordered groups by Step 1, since `ρ(K) ≤ PMod_c(Σ_h)`.

**Step 4. Part (c).** `V` is simple, by `thompson-v-finitely-presented-infinite-simple`,
and `V₂[Z₂] ≅ V`.
1. **Normal subgroups.** For `N ⊴ G`, the image of `N` in `G/PMod_c ≅ V` is
   normal, so it is `1` or `V`. So `N ≤ PMod_c` or `N·PMod_c = G`.
2. **Not simple.** `PMod_c ≠ G` because `V ≠ 1`. For `PMod_c ≠ 1`:
   - Split the end space into two nonempty clopen sets.
   - Choose `S` as in (I) with one noncompact complementary component for each
     set, so `n = 2`.
   - By (I), `Mod(S_{h,2})` embeds in `PMod_c`, and the twist about a boundary
     circle has infinite order there.
3. **Quotients through `V`.** If `N ≥ PMod_c`, then `G/N` is a quotient of the
   simple group `V`, so it is `1` or `V`. Neither contains the infinite group
   `L`, by `closed-mcg-finite-index-subgroups-do-not-embed-in-v`.
4. **Simple quotients.** If `G/N` is simple and contains a copy of `L`, then the
   image of `PMod_c` is normal in `G/N`. It is nontrivial by item 3, so it is
   `G/N`, and `N·PMod_c = G`.
5. **Perfection.** AF Corollary 1.5: "B_g and H_g are perfect for every g ≥ 3."

**Step 5. Part (d).**
1. Use the identification scoped in
   `cantor-punctured-birman-sections-have-no-finite-end-orbit`. The ends of
   `Σ_g` are the points of the Cantor set `C`, and `G ≤ Mod(Σ_g)`.
2. `ρ` is a section of the forgetful map over `L`, so it is injective, and by
   that claim `ρ(L)` has no finite orbit on `C`.
3. Suppose `π∘ρ(L)` is finite. Then `K` has finite index in `L`. Elements of
   `ρ(K) ≤ PMod_c(Σ_g) ≤ PMod(Σ_g)` fix every end. So the orbit of a point
   `x ∈ C` is `{ρ(ℓ)x}`, with `ℓ` running over representatives of `L/K`, and it
   has at most `[L : K]` points. This is a contradiction.
4. Hence `π∘ρ(L)` is infinite, and `ρ` is in case (b). `∎`
