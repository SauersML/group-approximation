**Status: complete proof (unreviewed).** It uses only elementary topology: the complement of an essential simple
closed curve on a torus is an open annulus, the van Kampen lemma for disk diagrams, and Jordan's theorem for
polyominoes.

## 0. Conventions

- `T = Z^2/Lambda'` is a grid torus. `Lambda'` has rank 2, and `lambda_1 = min{|lambda|_1 : 0 != lambda in Lambda'}`.
  We assume `lambda_1 >= 2`, so every edge borders exactly two distinct faces.
- The cell structure has vertices `Z^2/Lambda'`, edges `x -> x+e_j`, and unit faces. `pi_1(T) = Lambda'`, and a
  closed edge path is sent to its displacement in the universal cover `R^2`.
- A connection `A` gives each oriented edge a value in `F`; the reversed edge gets the inverse value. The holonomy
  of an edge path `p = (d_1, ..., d_r)` is `hol_A(p) = A(d_r) ... A(d_1)`. The face at `x` has holonomy
  `hol(x) = gamma(x)^-1 beta(x+e2)^-1 gamma(x+e1) beta(x)`, which is the holonomy of its boundary loop based at `x`.
- `A` is *flat on* a 2-subcomplex `Y` if every face of `Y` has trivial holonomy. On a connected `Y` that is flat,
  `hol_A` induces a homomorphism `pi_1(Y, y_0) -> F`: an edge loop is null-homotopic in `Y` iff its word is a
  product of conjugates of face boundaries of `Y` (combinatorial van Kampen).
- A *gauge* is a map `g: V -> F`, and `(g.A)(x -> y) = g(y) A(x -> y) g(x)^-1`. Gauges preserve flatness.

**Lemma 0 (gauge rigidity).** Let `Y` be a connected 2-subcomplex that contains every vertex of `T`, and let `A`,
`A'` be flat on `Y` with `hol_A = hol_A'` on `pi_1(Y, y_0)`. Then there is a gauge `g` with `A = g.A'` on every
edge of `Y`.

*Proof.* For a vertex `v`, choose an edge path `p` in `Y` from `y_0` to `v` and set
`g(v) = hol_A(p) hol_A'(p)^-1`.
- *Well defined.* For a second path `p'`, the loop `p'^-1 p` gives
  `hol_A(p')^-1 hol_A(p) = hol_A'(p')^-1 hol_A'(p)`, so `g(v)` does not depend on the choice.
- *Gauge equation.* For an edge `d: v -> u` of `Y`, extend `p` by `d`. Then
  `g(u) = A(d) hol_A(p) hol_A'(p)^-1 A'(d)^-1 = A(d) g(v) A'(d)^-1`, that is, `A(d) = g(u) A'(d) g(v)^-1`. QED.

## 1. Bands and the complement annulus

A **band** consists of distinct faces `f_0, ..., f_(m-1)` and distinct edges `e_0, ..., e_(m-1)` such that `e_i`
is a side of `f_i` and of `f_(i+1)` (indices mod `m`). Its dual curve `gamma` is the concatenation of arcs
`alpha_i`. The arc `alpha_i` runs inside the open face `f_i` from the midpoint of `e_(i-1)` to the midpoint of
`e_i`. We require `[gamma] = w` with `w` primitive in `Lambda'`.

Since `e_i` borders only `f_i` and `f_(i+1)`, the only rungs that are sides of `f_i` are `e_(i-1)` and `e_i`.
The arcs lie in distinct open faces and meet only at their endpoints, so `gamma` is a simple closed curve.

**Lemma 1.** Let `X` be `T` with the open faces `f_i` and the open rungs `e_i` removed. Then `X` is a connected
2-subcomplex that contains every vertex. Its fundamental group is `Z`, generated by an edge loop `c` whose class in
`pi_1(T)` is `w`.

*Proof.* `X` is a deformation retract of `T \ gamma`. The retraction is built in two steps.
- *Faces.* Inside the closed face `f_i`, the arc `alpha_i` cuts the square into two closed disks. Each disk is
  bounded by a sub-arc of `alpha_i` and a boundary path `b` of `f_i` joining the two midpoints. A closed disk minus
  a closed boundary arc deformation retracts onto the rest of its boundary. (A model is `[0,1] x [0,1)` retracting
  onto its three closed sides.) So each half of `f_i \ alpha_i` retracts onto `b` minus the two midpoints, fixing
  `b` pointwise.
- *Rungs.* Each half-open half-rung `e_i \ {midpoint}` then retracts onto its endpoint.

All these retractions fix `X` pointwise, and they are defined on pieces whose closures meet only in `X`, so they
glue. Hence `X ~ T \ gamma`.

Now `gamma` is a simple closed curve and is essential, because `w != 0`. The complement of an essential simple
closed curve on the torus is an open annulus, whose core is a parallel push-off of `gamma`. So `X` is connected
and `pi_1(X) = Z`. Its generator is represented by an edge loop `c` (any 2-complex), freely homotopic in `T` to
`gamma`, so its class is `w`. Removing open faces and open edges keeps every vertex. QED.

## 2. Theorem 1 (rung lemma)

**Theorem 1.** Suppose every charged face of `A` lies in the band `B`. Then there is a flat connection `A'` on `T`
that agrees with `A` on every edge other than the rungs `e_0, ..., e_(m-1)`.

*Proof.* The faces of `X` are exactly the faces outside `B`, so `A` is flat on `X`. Take a base vertex `x_0` on
`c` and let `a = hol_A(c)`. By Lemma 1, `hol_A` on `pi_1(X, x_0) = <c>` is determined by `a`.

*A flat model with the same holonomy.* Extend `w` to a basis `(w, w')` of `Lambda'`. Let `l: R^2 -> R` be the
linear form with `l(w) = 1` and `l(w') = 0`; it is integral on `Lambda'`. Put `n(z) = floor(l(z))` for `z` in
`Z^2`, and let `psi(z -> z+e_j) = a^(n(z+e_j) - n(z))`.
- *Well defined on `T`.* `n(z + lambda) = n(z) + l(lambda)` for `lambda` in `Lambda'`, so `psi` descends to `T`.
- *Flat.* On `Z^2`, `psi` is the coboundary of `z -> a^(n(z))` with values in the abelian group `<a>`, so every
  face is flat.
- *Holonomy.* An edge path from `z` to `z + lambda` has holonomy `a^(n(z+lambda) - n(z)) = a^(l(lambda))`, with
  no conjugation. In particular `hol_psi(c) = a^(l(w)) = a`.

*Gauge.* `A` and `psi` are both flat on `X` and agree on the generator `c` of `pi_1(X, x_0)`, so their holonomies
agree on all of `pi_1(X, x_0)`. Lemma 0 gives a gauge `g` with `A = g.psi` on every edge of `X`. Put `A' = g.psi`
on all of `T`. It is flat, and it agrees with `A` on every edge of `X`, that is, on every edge except the
rungs. QED.

**Remark (what the rungs pay for).** Assume the closure of `B` is an embedded closed annulus, as it is for the
digital band. Cut `B` open along the rung `e_0`. The result is a strip of faces `D`, and
`hol(∂D)` is the ordered product of the charges in `B` (van Kampen, suitably conjugated). Let `c_1` and `c_2` be
the two sides of the strip. Then `∂D ~ c_1 r c_2^-1 r^-1`, where `r` crosses the strip along `e_0`.
- Both `c_j` lie in the flat annulus `X` and are parallel to its core. So `hol(c_2) = k hol(c_1) k^-1`, where `k`
  is the holonomy of a path in `X` from `c_1` to `c_2`.
- Hence the ordered product equals `[hol(c_1), h k]`, with `h = hol(r)`. This is a commutator of the holonomies
  of a `w`-loop and a `w'`-loop.

For abelian `F` the product is 1. This is Stokes, and it is why a spanning tree of `B` suffices there. For
non-abelian `F` it is generally `!= 1`, and Theorem 2 shows that no disk-supported change can then help. Changing
the last rung `e_0` as well lets the `w'`-holonomy change, and the commutator becomes trivial.

## 3. Corollary (charges next to the band)

**Corollary.** Let `Q` be the set of charged faces. Suppose every face of `Q` lies in `B` or shares an edge with a
face of `B`. Then `A` can be made flat by changing at most `m + |Q \ B|` edges.

*Proof.* For each `q` in `Q \ B`, choose a side `e_q` of `q` that is also a side of a band face. `e_q` is not a
rung, since the faces bordering a rung both lie in `B`. The `e_q` are distinct, because an edge borders only two
faces and one of them is in `B`.

The value on `e_q` occurs exactly once in the boundary word of `q`, so it can be chosen to make `q` flat. This
changes the holonomy only of `q` and of the band face across `e_q`. Doing this for every `q` in turn leaves all
charges in `B`, and each fixed `q` stays fixed because no later change touches its sides. Theorem 1 then changes
only the rungs, which are not sides of any `q`. The total is `|Q \ B| + m`. QED.

## 4. Theorem 2 (disk-supported changes cannot cancel a nontrivial product)

Let `A'` be flat on `T` and agree with `A` off an edge set `S`. Let `Gamma` be the graph described next.
- *Vertices:* the charged faces of `A`, together with both faces bordering each edge of `S`.
- *Edges:* one for each `s` in `S`, joining the two faces that `s` borders.

A closed walk in `Gamma` (a dual walk) has a class in `Lambda'`: lift it face by face to `R^2` and take its
displacement. A component `K` is *inessential* if every closed walk in it has class 0.

**Lemma 2 (length).** A closed dual walk of class `lambda != 0` has at least `|lambda|_1 >= lambda_1` edges.
In particular, if `|S| < lambda_1` then every component of `Gamma` is inessential.

*Proof.* Each dual step moves the lifted face centre by `±e_1` or `±e_2`, so the length is at least
`|lambda|_1`.

For the second part, the cycle space of a component is spanned by simple cycles, and these use distinct edges of
`S`. If some closed walk has class `!= 0`, then so does some simple cycle, and by the first part it uses at least
`lambda_1` edges of `S`. QED.

**Lemma 3 (filled lift).** Let `K` be inessential.
- Choose a lift of one face and lift `K` along walks. The result `K~` is a copy of `K` in the grid of `R^2`,
  holding exactly one lift of each face of `K`.
- Let `U` be the union of the closed faces of `K~`, and let `D` be `U` together with the bounded components of
  `R^2 \ U`.

Then:
- (i) `D` is a closed disk;
- (ii) no edge of `∂D` lies in the lifted edge set `S~`;
- (iii) for `0 != lambda` in `Lambda'`, no face of `U + lambda` lies in `D`.

*Proof.*
- *The lift.* It is well defined because closed walks have class 0. It is injective on faces: two lifts
  `f~, f~ + lambda` in `K~` would be joined by a walk projecting to a closed walk of class `lambda`.
- *`U` is edge-connected.* Adjacent vertices of `K~` share the edge of `S~` between them.

(ii) Let `s` in `S~` be a side of a face of `U`. The two faces bordering `s` are adjacent in the lifted graph, so
both lie in `K~`. Hence both sides of `s` lie in `D`, and `s` is not on `∂D`.

(iii) The sets `U` and `U + lambda` share no face, since `K~` holds one lift per face. Two faces outside `U` that
share an edge are in the same component of `R^2 \ U`: their shared edge is not a side of any face of `U`, because
an edge borders only two faces. So the edge-connected set `U + lambda` lies either entirely in one bounded
component of `R^2 \ U` or entirely in the unbounded one.
- *Bounded case.* Then `R^2 \ D` is connected, unbounded and disjoint from `U + lambda`. So it lies in the
  unbounded component of `R^2 \ (U + lambda)`, which gives `D + lambda ⊆ D`. Equal areas force
  `D + lambda = D`, and then `D` is invariant under the translation by `lambda`. That is impossible for a compact
  nonempty set.
- *Unbounded case.* Then `U + lambda` has no face in `D`.

(i) `D` is a finite union of closed unit squares, with connected interior (`U` is edge-connected, and each hole is
attached along edges) and connected complement.
- *No pinch vertex.* Suppose a vertex `p` had two diagonal squares in `D` and the other two, `s` and `s'`,
  outside `D`. Join the diagonal squares by a path through the interior of `D` and close the path through `p`. The
  result is a closed curve in `D` that crosses the diagonal at `p`, so it separates `s` from `s'`. One of them would
  then lie in a bounded component of `R^2 \ D`, which contradicts connectedness of the complement.
- *Conclusion.* So `D` is a compact connected surface with boundary in the plane. Its complement is connected, so
  by Jordan's theorem `∂D` is one simple closed curve and `D` is a closed disk. QED.

**Theorem 2.**
- (a) Let `K` be an inessential component and `D` its filled lift. Then `hol_A(∂D) = 1`, with `A` lifted
  periodically. That is, the ordered product (van Kampen) of the charges of `A` in `D` is trivial.
- (b) Suppose `A` has exactly one charged face `q`. Then every flat `A'` differs from `A` on at least
  `lambda_1` edges. Theorem 1 with the digital band through `q` (`m = lambda_1`) attains this.
- (c) If `|S| < lambda_1`, then each component `K` of `Gamma` has trivial ordered product in its filled
  lift. In other words, fewer than `lambda_1` changes can cancel charges only in disk clusters whose ordered
  product is 1.

*Proof.*
- (a) By Lemma 3(ii), `A~` and `A'~` agree on `∂D`. `A'~` is flat on all of `R^2`, which is simply connected,
  so `hol_A(∂D) = hol_A'(∂D) = 1`. By the van Kampen lemma for the disk `D`, `hol_A(∂D)` is a product of
  conjugates of `hol_A(f)^(±1)`, one for each face `f` of `D`. Only the charged faces contribute.
- (b) If `|S| < lambda_1`, then by Lemma 2 the component `K` of `q` is inessential. Every charged face of the
  lift is a lift `q + lambda` of `q`.
  - `D` contains the lift in `K~`.
  - By Lemma 3(iii), `D` contains no other lift of `q`, because `q` is a face of `K`.

  So `hol_A(∂D)` is a conjugate of `hol(q)^(±1) != 1`, contradicting (a).
- (c) This is Lemma 2 followed by (a). QED.

**Consequence for the strip cut.** In a piece of the abelian strip cut, the spanning-tree cancellation keeps one
rung of the band. Its changed edges form an inessential component whose filled lift is the cut-open band strip.
So by (a) the tree cancellation succeeds only if the ordered product of the piece's charges is 1. By the Remark
in section 2, that product is a commutator of the piece's holonomies. Theorem 1 removes the obstruction at the
cost of the one remaining rung per piece.

## 5. Computation

`experiments/nonabelian-rectangle-profile-2026-09-17/rungs.py` builds regular `F`-covers for
`F = S_3, Q_8, A_4` with one or two charged faces, on random HNF tori and thin tori. It then:
- closes the digital band, first with the tree-only choice (keeping the original closing rung), then with every
  value on the last rung;
- runs the two-charge cases of `nonabelian-two-charge-torus-covers-rank-distance-linear`;
- asserts commutation of the output, the site bounds, `dist <= |F| sites`, and `dist <= 36 rho_lower`.

Summary in `rungs-summary.txt`: 6900 covers and 5056 closed bands. On 3641 bands the tree-only choice fails,
including every single-charge cover (as (b) predicts). Some closing value exists on every band, as Theorem 1 says.
The largest `dist / rho_lower` is 7.10.
