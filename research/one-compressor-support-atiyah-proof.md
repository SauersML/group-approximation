Throughout, `K` is a subfield of `C` closed under complex conjugation. "Strong Atiyah over `K`"
for a torsion-free group `L` means that `dim_(N(L)) ker r_A` is an integer for every finite matrix
`A` over `K[L]`.

**(F1) Subgroups and directed unions.** For `L <= L'` and `A` over `K[L]`,

```text
dim_(N(L')) ker r_A = dim_(N(L)) ker r_A
```

(Step 1 of `algebraic-atiyah-determinant-colimit-proof`). So Strong Atiyah over `K` passes to
subgroups and to isomorphic groups. A group satisfies it iff all its finitely generated subgroups
do, because a matrix involves finitely many group elements. Hence it passes to directed unions.

**(F2) Extensions.** `atiyah-passes-to-torsion-free-elementary-amenable-extensions` (Schick,
Corollary 3.2): if `L_0` is torsion-free and satisfies Strong Atiyah over `K`, and `L/L_0` is
torsion-free elementary amenable, then `L` satisfies Strong Atiyah over `K`.

## Step 1: the normal union

Clause 1 of `compressor-subgroup-is-directed-union-by-cyclic` says `N` is an increasing union of
conjugates of `Gamma`, normal in `H`, with `H/N` cyclic. The check is short and is repeated here
because Step 2 uses the notation.

Let `N_n = t^-n Gamma t^n` for `n >= 0`, and `N = union_n N_n`.

* **Increasing.** Conjugating `t Gamma t^-1 <= Gamma` by `t^-(n+1)` gives `N_n <= N_(n+1)`.
  So `N` is a subgroup, and a directed union of copies of `Gamma`.
* **`t` normalizes `N`.** `t^-1 N_n t = N_(n+1)`. Also `t N_n t^-1 = N_(n-1)` for `n >= 1`, and
  `t N_0 t^-1 <= N_0`. So `t^(+-1) N t^(-+1) <= N`.
* **`Gamma` normalizes `N`.** `Gamma = N_0 <= N_n` for every `n`, so each `gamma in Gamma`
  normalizes every `N_n`.
* **Structure.** `N` is normal in `H = <Gamma, t>`, and `H = N <t>`. Hence `H/N` is cyclic,
  generated by `tN`.

## Step 2: the quotient is infinite cyclic

Suppose `t^m in N` for some `m >= 1`. Then `t^m in N_n` for some `n`, that is,
`t^m = t^-n gamma t^n` with `gamma in Gamma`, so `gamma = t^m in Gamma`. Therefore
`t^m Gamma t^-m = Gamma`.

On the other hand, conjugating the proper inclusion (OC1) by the injective map `Ad(t^(j-1))`
gives a proper inclusion

```text
t^j Gamma t^-j  <  t^(j-1) Gamma t^-(j-1)          for every j >= 1,
```

so `t^m Gamma t^-m` is a proper subgroup of `Gamma`, a contradiction. Negative powers reduce to
positive ones. So `H/N` is infinite cyclic, hence torsion-free elementary amenable.

## Step 3: Strong Atiyah

* **Torsion-freeness.** `N` is a directed union of copies of the torsion-free group `Gamma`, so
  it is torsion-free.
* **If `Gamma` satisfies Strong Atiyah over `K`.** By (F1), so does `N`, and by (F2) with
  `L_0 = N`, `L = H`, so does `H`.
* **Conversely.** `Gamma <= H`, so (F1) gives Strong Atiyah over `K` for `Gamma`.

This proves (OC2) and (OC3).

## Step 4: Ore compressor families

Let `s Gamma s^-1 <= Gamma` for every `s in T`, let the monoid `M` generated by `T` be left Ore,
and put `N_T = union_(m in M) m^-1 Gamma m`.

* **Monotone.** For `m, a in M`, `a Gamma a^-1 <= Gamma` gives
  `m^-1 Gamma m <= (am)^-1 Gamma (am)`.
* **Directed.** Given `m_1, m_2`, a common left multiple `c = a m_1 = b m_2` contains both
  members inside `c^-1 Gamma c`. So `N_T` is a directed union of conjugates of `Gamma`, and it is
  torsion-free.
* **Normality.**
  - `Gamma <= m^-1 Gamma m` for every `m`, so `Gamma` normalizes each member.
  - For `s in T`, `s^-1 (m^-1 Gamma m) s = (ms)^-1 Gamma (ms)`.
  - For `s in T`, choose `a, b in M` with `a m = b s`, so that `s m^-1 = b^-1 a`. Then
    `s (m^-1 Gamma m) s^-1 = b^-1 (a Gamma a^-1) b <= b^-1 Gamma b`.

  So `N_T` is normal in `H_T = <Gamma, T>`, and the quotient is generated by the images of `T`.
  When `T` pairwise commutes, `M` is commutative, hence left Ore, and the quotient is abelian.
* **Conclusion.** If `H_T/N_T` is torsion-free elementary amenable and `Gamma` satisfies Strong
  Atiyah over `K`, then (F1) gives it for `N_T` and (F2) gives it for `H_T`. The converse is (F1).

## Step 5: the Fournier--Facio applications

* **Strictness.** (TL1) of `fournier-facio-compression-tree-lamp-group` says `t_i Gamma t_i^-1`
  commutes with `pi(S) <= Gamma`, where `S` is infinite simple, hence nonabelian. If
  `t_i Gamma t_i^-1 = Gamma`, then `pi(S)` would be central in itself, which is impossible.
  For a positive word `w = w_1 w'`,
  `t_w Gamma t_w^-1 = t_(w_1) (t_(w') Gamma t_(w')^-1) t_(w_1)^-1 <= t_(w_1) Gamma t_(w_1)^-1`,
  a proper subgroup.
* **No common left multiples.** If `a t_1 = b t_2` for positive words `a, b`, then (TL5) makes
  the slot conjugates `S_(a1)` and `S_(b2)` equal. These are distinct words, and (TL3)-(TL4)
  make distinct slots commuting independent copies of the nonabelian group `S`, a
  contradiction. So the positive monoid on `t_1, t_2` is not left Ore.

End proof.
