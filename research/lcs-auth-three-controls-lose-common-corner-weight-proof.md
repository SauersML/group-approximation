The artifact supplies the complete symbolic proof and independent review.

1. The products of the first controlled reflection with the other two
   give irrational source rotations about two axes on each of the
   ancilla X=-1 and Z=-1 planes. Their Lie brackets, followed by the
   controlled-Z conjugation `XX -> YY`, span all fifteen su(4) Pauli
   directions. Hence the actual port group's image is dense in PU(4).
   Its quotient by scalar matrices is ICC: a finite-index centralizer
   would be dense in the connected group PU(4), whose center is trivial.
2. With chi the normalized character of rho, the explicit amplification
   has character `chi(h)[(1+16|chi(h)|^2)/17]^m`. It converges to the
   canonical scalar-character trace, which is lambda on lambda I and
   zero on nonscalars. Its GNS algebra is the twisted group factor of
   the ICC scalar quotient. All ordinary relators and scalar marks
   remain exact along the amplification.
3. X,U_0,XU_0 are distinct nonscalar projective classes. Therefore the
   group-algebra projection b has canonical trace 1/4, although its
   original honest four-dimensional matrix value is zero. If good
   asymptotically reducing corners retained positive weight, their
   compressed traces would converge to a nonzero tracial functional
   dominated by this factorial trace. Its density must be scalar, so
   it cannot annihilate b. This contradiction proves vanishing weight.
   The literal controlled identity on a reducing corner annihilates b
   for every choice of the decoded projection.
4. Pairwise disjoint conjugates of {X,U_0,XU_0} give centered average
   variance 3/(16N) and coefficient-l1 square bound 9/16. The exact
   character formula yields the stated finite-test estimate. The
   conjugation action on l2 of the nonidentity scalar quotient has
   no invariant vectors; its orbit convex hull supplies each required
   finite-support displacement. Rational complex entries make the
   corresponding word search exact and terminating for fixed N.

The one-atom calculation in the same artifact retains a corner of weight
at least 1/4, making clear why the common three-port reduction hypothesis
is essential. No computation or external universality theorem is used
in the proof.
