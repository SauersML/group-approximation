Lane proof (bh-g2-fixedpoint-a, 2026-09-18). Not independently reviewed.

Notation is that of the target. An FLEF `P` on `W` is as in
`fixed-point-wang-tile-rigidity-is-edge-family-commutation`, and item 1 there turns every `D`-family (`D >= 1`)
into an FLEF that commutes iff the family does. So items 2–3 of the target follow from item 1, and it is enough
to show that **every FLEF `P` commutes**.

*Algebra of a set of edges:* the subalgebra generated by their idempotents. *Face rule:* since the four edges of
a face commute, a colour of one edge that the tile rule fixes as a function `f` of the other three gives
`P_c(e) = Σ_{f(u) = c} Π_u`, where `Π_u` is the product over the other three edges. Non-tiles vanish by (F2).

## Step 1: coordinates are central

- A tile's coordinates are fixed by the coordinate part of any one of its edges (L0). So `C_p(z)`
  ("face `z` has coordinates `p`") lies in the algebra of each single edge of `z`.
- The right edge of `z` is the left edge of `z + e_1`. Hence `C_p(z) = C_(p + e_1)(z + e_1)`, and similarly
  vertically, so `C_p(z) = C_(p + z − z')(z')` for all `z, z'`.
- `C(z')` commutes with the edges of `z'`, so every `C_p(z)` commutes with every edge.

The `N^2` projections `C_p(0)` are central and sum to `1`. On each summand the level-0 grid and all
coordinate-determined data are scalars: blanks, program bits, the start head and the layout roles.

## Step 2: one block (fixed phase)

Let `Z` be a level-1 block and `β` its `4k` bit edges.
- **(a) The inputs commute.** By (L2), all edges along a wire (including turns and fan-outs) carry one PVM.
  By (L3), for bit edges `e, e'` some crossing face has a copy of `e` and a copy of `e'` among its four
  edges, which commute by (F1). So the PVMs of `β` pairwise commute. Let `A(Z)` be their commutative algebra.
- **(b) The wires.** Every wire edge lies in `A(Z)`, and every blank edge is a scalar.
- **(c) The zone.** By induction on rows, every zone edge lies in `A(Z)`.
  - *Row 0:* input copies lie in `A(Z)`, and the program bits and head are scalars.
  - *Crossing edges.* Let `v` be the vertical edge between cells `j − 1` and `j` in row `t`. The
    projections "head leaves `j − 1` to the right in state `q`" are sums of bottom-edge projections of cell
    `j − 1`. This is the face rule in face `(t, j − 1)`: only head tiles emit a head to the right. Likewise
    "head leaves `j` to the left" uses cell `j`, and "none" is the complement. So `v ∈ A(Z)` once row `t`
    is in `A(Z)`.
  - *Top edges:* `h_(t+1, j)` is a function of the other three edges of face `(t, j)` (L4), so the face
    rule puts it in `A(Z)`.
- **(d) Margins.** A face in the margin of side `s` (not a corner) has only wire copies of `s`'s bits and
  blanks (L1), so its edges lie in `A(s)`, the algebra of `s`'s bit edges. Corner faces have scalar edges.

## Step 3: the level-1 edge family

For a side `s` of `Z` and `c ∈ C`, let `P^1_c(s)` be the joint projection of `s`'s bit edges onto the encoding
of `c`. The bit edges of `s` are shared by the two blocks on either side of `s`, so `P^1_c(s)` is well defined.
- **(F1).** The four sides of `Z` commute, by Step 2(a).
- **(F2) and partition of unity.** Let `Π` be a joint spectral projection of `β` with values `u`. On `ΠW` every
  edge of `Z` acts by a scalar colour (Steps 2(b)–(c)), namely the deterministic evaluation of the layout on
  `u`. If `u` encodes no colour, or encodes colours `(l, r, b, t) ∉ τ`, that evaluation contains a
  non-accepting tile. By (F2) at level 0, the product of the four edge projections of that face vanishes,
  and it acts as `1` on `ΠW`. So `Π = 0`.

Hence `P^1` is an FLEF of `τ` on the level-1 grid, and every edge of `Z` lies in the commutative algebra
`A_1(Z)` of the four `P^1` sides.

Two further facts complete the step:
- **Centrality.** Step 1 applied to `P^1` gives level-1 coordinate projections that commute with all `P^1`
  edges, hence with every `A_1(Z)`, hence with all of `P`. So they are central for `P`.
- **Margins.** Step 2(d) says margin faces of side `s` have edges in the algebra of `P^1(s)`, and corner
  faces are scalar.

## Step 4: iteration

For `K >= 1`, split `W` along the finitely many central phase projections of levels `0, …, K − 1`. On each
summand, induction on `K` (Steps 2–3 applied to `P^(K−1)`, which is an FLEF of `τ`) gives:
- **(I1)** every level-0 edge inside a level-`K` block `B` lies in the commutative algebra `A_K(B)` of
  `B`'s four `P^K` sides;
- **(I2)** if a level-0 face of `B` is within `m N^(K−1)` of exactly one side `s`, its edges lie in the
  algebra of `P^K(s)`; if it is within that distance of two sides, its edges are scalars.

*Proof of (I2).* Such a face lies in a level-`(K−1)` block `B'` at position `q` of `B` with `q_1 >= N − m`
and `m <= q_2 < N − m` (or in a corner position). This is the margin computation of item 2 in the reduction
node. So `B'` is a margin (or corner) face of `B` at level `K − 1`. By Step 2(d) for `P^(K−1)`, the four
sides of `B'` lie in the algebra of `P^K(s)` (or are scalars). By (I1) at level `K − 1`, so does every
level-0 edge of `B'`.

## Step 5: any two edges commute

Take edges `e, e'` and faces `f ∋ e`, `f' ∋ e'` at `ℓ¹` distance `d`. Choose `K` with `m N^(K−1) > d + 2`, and
work on one summand of Step 4.
- *Same level-`K` block `B`:* both lie in the commutative `A_K(B)`.
- *Blocks `B, B'` sharing a side `s`:* `f` and `f'` are within `d < m N^(K−1)` of `s`. By (I2), each is in
  the algebra of `P^K(s)` or is scalar.
- *Blocks sharing only a corner:* `f` and `f'` are within that distance of two sides, so both are scalars.

The blocks cannot be farther apart, since `d < N^K`. So `e` and `e'` commute on every summand, hence on `W`.
QED

## Checks

- **Classical shadow.** The same induction shows that two tilings of `τ` never have an edge wall (item 4 of
  the reduction node), consistent with item 1 of the target.
- **The colour-product set.** It is all crossings, with rows crossing columns but never rows, and it is not
  rigid. Here (L3) is used in full: every pair of inputs meets.
- **Field independence.** The proof uses only idempotents, sums and finite central decompositions.
