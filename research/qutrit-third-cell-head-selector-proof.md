## The binary branch deletion

Use

```text
H=x_(2,1)(Q_0),        Q_0=1-s_0t_0=s_1t_1,
T_r=x_(0,2)(t_r).
```

The root positions are composable, so

```text
[T_r,H]=x_(0,1)(t_rQ_0).                                (QTS8)
```

The Leavitt relations give

```text
t_0Q_0=t_0-t_0s_0t_0=0,
t_1Q_0=t_1-t_1s_0t_0=t_1.
```

This proves `(QTS1)`. Put `B=x_(0,1)(t_1)`. The roots `H,T_1,B`
are involutions, `B` commutes with the other two, and
`[T_1,H]=B`. Their three displayed matrix entries recover the exponents
in the class-two normal form

```text
H^epsilon T_1^delta B^gamma.
```

Thus they generate `D_8`.

## The exact head polar

Let `E=(1-H)/2` in the complex group algebra. Since `H` is an
involution, `E` is a projection. The commutator identity gives

```text
T_1 H T_1=HB.
```

Therefore

```text
T_1ET_1=(1-HB)/2
```

and, using `HE=-E`,

```text
(E T_1 E)^2
 =E(T_1ET_1)E
 =E(1-HB)E/2
 =E(1+B)/2
 =F_+.                                                   (QTS9)
```

The operator `E T_1 E` is self-adjoint. Equation `(QTS9)` says its
polar is a self-adjoint partial isometry with both support projections
`F_+`. The complementary projection is `F_-=E(1-B)/2`. In contrast,
`[T_0,H]=1`, so

```text
E T_0 E=T_0E
```

is unitary on the full `E` corner. This is the exact full-versus-half
binary dichotomy.

## The nine-atom table

Every active coordinate is fixed by `h=J^2Z`. Hence `H,T_0,T_1,B`
centralize `h`. The head `H` joins corresponding copies of the same
nontrivial `C=<J,Z>` module and commutes with all of `C`.
Each of `T_0,T_1,B` joins a trivial and a nontrivial copy. It does not
normalize `C`, and

```text
C intersect vCv^(-1)=<h>,        v=T_0,T_1,B.            (QTS10)
```

Index the characters of `C` by `(rho,a) in F_3^2` and write
`z_(rho,a)` for their Fourier projections. Restriction to `<h>` is the
linear form

```text
ell(rho,a)=2rho+a.
```

The standard finite-subgroup Fourier calculation now gives

```text
||z_(rho',a') v z_(rho,a)||_2^2
 =1/27 if ell(rho',a')=ell(rho,a),
 =0 otherwise,                                        (QTS11)
```

for `v=T_0,T_1,B`. For each source there are exactly three compatible
targets.

The head cut commutes with every Fourier atom. Expanding
`E=(1-H)/2` in `(QTS11)` halves each nonzero squared norm:

```text
||z_(rho',a') T_r E z_(rho,a)||_2^2=1/54.               (QTS12)
```

For the two-sided compression, expand

```text
E T_1 E=(T_1-HT_1-T_1H+HT_1H)/4.
```

The four D8 words have distinct root normal forms and remain in distinct
`C` double-coset components; multiplying two different terms cannot
produce the identity after inserting `C` Fourier words. Thus their
canonical squared norms add. Each term has the support in `(QTS11)`,
and consequently

```text
||z_(rho',a') E T_1 E z_(rho,a)||_2^2
 =4(1/16)(1/27)=1/108                                  (QTS13)
```

on a compatible pair, and is zero otherwise. Summing the three targets
gives `1/36`. Directly, the same number is

```text
tau(z_(rho,a)F_+)
 ={1/4}tau(z_(rho,a)(1-H)(1+B))
 =1/36,                                                 (QTS14)
```

because every term containing `H` or `B` has zero identity
coefficient. The same calculation applies to `F_-`. This proves every
entry of `(QTS4)` and shows that the half-polar loss is uniform over all
nine atoms.

## Why the proper qutrit arrow is zero

In the selected central row put

```text
e_a=z_(1,a).
```

The three `h` characters are `ell(1,a)=2+a` and are pairwise distinct.
Every `v in G_3` centralizes `h`, not only the four words just audited:
`G_3` is generated by matrices supported on the `h`-fixed coordinate
block. Therefore spectral orthogonality gives

```text
e_b v e_a=0                       whenever a!=b.         (QTS15)
```

With `R=e_0+e_1` and `q-R=e_2`, equation `(QTS15)` is exactly

```text
(q-R)vR=0.
```

Thus the binary identity `t_1Q_0=t_1` does not identify the binary branch
with the qutrit complement. It only produces the D8 half-polar inside
each fixed `h` sector.

The finite-head calculation gives

```text
tau(ER)=1/9,                tau(E(q-R))=1/18.
```

If a partial isometry `V` satisfied

```text
V^*V=ER,                    VV^*<=E(q-R),
```

traciality would give `1/9<=1/18`, impossible. Quantitatively at least
`1/18` of source projection trace must be lost. But the literal filtered
third-cell operator is zero by `(QTS15)`, while the unfiltered D8 polar
has source `F_+` with

```text
tau(F_+R)=1/18.
```

It fits the one-complement capacity exactly. This proves the claimed
Hall no-go.

## Finite-presentation status

The coefficient ring has the finite presentation

```text
L=F_2<s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1 |
      t_i s_j=delta_(ij),
      s_0t_0+s_1t_1=1>.
```

The Krstic--McCool theorem makes `EL_n(R)` finitely presented for
`n>=4` when the unital associative ring `R` is finitely presented.
Hence `G_3=EL_4(L)` is finitely presented.

Let `F_q` be the finite transitive qutrit packet. The two-cell generators
already contain a matched opposite-root chart. The one-chart theorem says
that `F_q` and that chart generate `GL_28(F_2)`. Scalar conjugation of
the `s_r,t_s` roots in `G_3` then gives every root position, so

```text
<F_q,G_3>=EL_28(L).
```

This qutrit extension is finitely presented by the same theorem. It is
also minimally almost periodic and nonsofic by the established Leavitt
results. Its hyperlinearity is not decided here.

The next selector must use a scalar element outside `C_K(h)`. Without
that extra actor, the exact vanishing `(QTS15)` rules out the intended
proper-marginal arrow before any analytic estimate begins.
