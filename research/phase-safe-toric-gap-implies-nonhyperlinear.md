ESTABLISHED, conditional reduction proved in
`notes/TRUE_NORMAL_TORIC_GAP_IMPLIES_NONHYPERLINEAR.md`.

Let `Gcal` be a finite two-player game that is *torically determined* in the
sense of Watts--Helton--Klep (arXiv:2111.14928): determined by finitely many
relations `h_i - 1` with `h_i = beta_i g_i`, `g_i` in the universal game group
`G` and `beta_i` a scalar phase.  Let `B` be the (assumed finite) group
generated by the phases, `Gtilde = B x G`, `H = <h_i>` and `N` its normal
closure in `Gtilde`.

> **Theorem.**  If `N ∩ B = {1}` and `omega_qa(Gcal) < 1`, then the finitely
> presented group `Q = Gtilde/N` is **not hyperlinear**.

The proof does not use stability or any approximate-representation decoder.
`N ∩ B = {1}` embeds the finite central `B` in `Q`, so the defining character
`chi : B -> T` has a central spectral projection `p_chi` in `L(Q)` with
`tau_Q(p_chi) = 1/|B| > 0`.  If `Q` were hyperlinear, `L(Q)` and hence the
corner `p_chi L(Q)` would be Connes embeddable; on that corner every clause
reads `beta_i rho(g_i) = p_chi`, so the corner trace is a *perfect* tracial
strategy.  Lifting the finitely many PVMs into a matrix ultraproduct and
using left/right multiplication on `M_{d_n} = C^{d_n} ⊗ conj(C^{d_n})` turns
it into an ordinary finite-dimensional tensor strategy, giving
`omega_qa(Gcal) = 1` and contradicting the gap.

## Why the phase-safe hypothesis is the whole difficulty

Watts--Helton--Klep's Theorem 5.12 characterizes perfect commuting-operator
strategies by the *subgroup* condition `H ∩ B = {1}`.  The reduction needs the
*normal closure* condition `N ∩ B = {1}`, and the doc gives a finite model
showing the gap between them is real: in `C_2 x A_5` with `b` central and `a`
an involution, `H = <ba>` meets `<b>` trivially while `b` lies in the normal
closure of `H`.  Normal closure can kill exactly the scalar phase that
supplied the perfect vector.

A sufficient certificate is a **perfect tracial model of the full toric
universal game algebra**: its kernel is normal and contains `N`, and
faithfulness of `chi` on `B <= T` then forces `N ∩ B = {1}`.  A synchronous
trace on a one-player/opposite-algebra presentation does *not* qualify --
passing to the one-player trace reverses word order on the second player's
copy and does not identify the two normal closures.

Two exact restrictions on any witness, also proved there: a toric
`qc = 1 > qa` gap forces `H` infinite and `proj_G(H)` of infinite index in
`G`, because finite or finite-index clause subgroups admit an induced
finite-dimensional representation whose distinguished vector satisfies every
clause exactly.
