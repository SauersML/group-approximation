title: Every hyperbolic group embeds in some Brin-Thompson group nV
---

For every finitely generated word-hyperbolic group `G` there is `n ≥ 1` and an
injective homomorphism `G → nV`. This is the affirmative answer to Zaremsky
Problem 2.19(a) (`zaremsky-2-19a-every-hyperbolic-group-in-some-nv-resolved`).

## Known cases

- **Virtually special groups.** Every virtually special group embeds in some nV
  (Belk–Bleak–Matucci, arXiv:1602.08635, Corollary 1.5). This includes every
  hyperbolic group acting properly and cocompactly on a CAT(0) cube complex, by
  Agol's theorem. Examples: finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold groups, finite
  `C'(1/6)` small cancellation groups, one-relator groups with torsion and
  hyperbolic Coxeter groups.
- **Virtual subgroups of RAAGs.** Every group that virtually embeds in a
  finitely generated RAAG embeds in some nV (same source, p. 2). This includes
  hyperbolic subgroups of such groups that are not themselves cubulated.

## Consequence if true

Infinite hyperbolic groups with property (T) exist
(`torsion-free-hyperbolic-kazhdan-partner-exists`). So this claim would put an
infinite Kazhdan group inside some mV, and then kV lacks the Haagerup property
for every k ≥ m. That is a negative answer to the Haagerup part of Zaremsky
Problem 2.7.

## Attempts

- 2026-09-13 (lane z2-19-hyperbolic-nv): the cubulated case is imported. Every
  hyperbolic group that virtually embeds in a RAAG has the Haagerup property, so
  the known method cannot reach infinite hyperbolic Kazhdan groups. A proof must
  build faithful actions of Kazhdan hyperbolic groups on the Cantor cube by
  piecewise prefix replacements.
  - The natural candidates fail, because their local maps have nontrivial
    states: the boundary action is rational but not piecewise canonical
    (Belk–Bleak–Matucci, arXiv:1711.08369), and so are actions on
    Bruhat–Tits flag varieties.
  - No construction is known to this lane. Such a construction would also
    decide the Haagerup part of Problem 2.7 negatively, so expect it to be hard.
- 2026-09-17 (lane c-hyp-nv, transplanter via symbolic-dynamics, unreviewed):
  **a slope-lattice class kill, and the two slope shapes a Kazhdan embedding must
  realize.** The claim stays OPEN.
  - **Invariant.** For an embedding `ι: G -> nV`, the slope lattice
    `L(ι G) <= Z^n` is generated by the germ prefix-length changes
    `s(g,x) = (|u'_j| - |u_j|)_j`.
  - **Established (unreviewed):**
    `nv-subgroups-with-sign-definite-rank-one-slopes-embed-in-v`, route
    `nv-sign-definite-slope-block-coding-proof`.
    - Suppose `L(K) <= Z v`, where the nonzero entries of `v` all have one sign.
      Then the finitely generated `K <= nV` embeds in Thompson's `V`, so it is
      a-T-menable and its Kazhdan and FW subgroups are finite.
    - More generally, `k` disjointly supported sign-definite blocks give `K -> kV`.
    - The proof freezes the zero-slope coordinates at a uniform depth, refines
      every piece so that the prefixes in each block are aligned with the slope
      vector, and interleaves each block into one binary coordinate.
  - **Class killed.** This covers every construction of an embedding of the
    Kazhdan partner (`torsion-free-hyperbolic-kazhdan-partner-exists`) into
    `nV` whose pieces all expand or all contract by one common scaling vector,
    with extra coordinates carried only as bounded labels. Diagonal
    self-similar codings and multi-coordinate copies of a one-dimensional
    similarity structure are examples. Every such construction dies at the
    recoding step: it lands in `V`, and Farley's theorem forbids an infinite
    Kazhdan subgroup there.
  - **Decomposition.** Any embedding of the partner must realize one of two
    shapes.
    - **(S1) Rank at least two**, with two non-proportional slope directions.
      The Robertson–Steger calibration
      (`rank-two-prefix-replacement-groups-contain-infinite-kazhdan`) shows that
      rank-two word systems host infinite Kazhdan groups. Coding them into the
      product word system of `nV` is the open step, expected to fail at the
      (H1) squares.
    - **(S2) A mixed-sign line** `Z(a_+, -a_-)`, baker type.
      - When the block sums are equal, `A = B`, Lebesgue measure is invariant.
        So by `kazhdan-subgroups-of-nv-finite-iff-invariant-sets-carry-measures`
        part 1, an infinite Kazhdan subgroup of this shape lives only on a closed,
        nowhere dense, null invariant set.
      - When `A ≠ B`, the ambient orbits are non-amenable Diestel–Leader graphs
        and no measure is available.

    Each shape can fail on its own. Neither is excluded here.
  - **Dead end, with the step.** Coding the unimodular mixed-sign subgroup
    `S_{(1,-1)}` into the two-sided full-shift full group, in order to reuse the
    amenable-orbit argument, dies at the first step. Pieces rewrite
    a finite window as well as shifting, so the group is the full group of
    "shift plus finite modification". Its orbits are `DL(2,2)`-like rather than
    `Z`-like. Kazhdan Schreier graphs on them are bounded-distance subgraphs of
    an amenable graph, and they need not be amenable.
  - **Side remark on host classes (unrefereed import).** Kojima–Sheng,
    arXiv:2603.18410, abstract: "We prove that the Brin-Thompson group $nV$ is
    torsion locally finite for $ n \geq 1$". If this is correct, no host group
    containing a finitely generated infinite torsion group embeds in any `nV`.
    So a route "hyperbolic group -> universal host class -> nV" must use the
    specific host of each `G`, not a class closed under adding groups such as
    the Grigorchuk group.
