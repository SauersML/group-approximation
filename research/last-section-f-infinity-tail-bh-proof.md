Lane proof (bh-free-20, 2026-09-18); not reviewed.

**Notation.**
- `X = {0,…,L}`, `C = X^N`; maps act on the left. `V = V_d`, and `B := V_d S` is the base group.
- `O` is the `V`-orbit (tail class) of `p = 0^∞`.
- Standing hypothesis (H0): every state `q` of the automaton generating `S` satisfies
  `q|_{0^n} = 1` for large `n`. It holds for Grigorchuk, the supergroup, Fabrykowski–Gupta and
  Basilica, whose directed rays avoid `0^∞`. By induction on word length, every `f ∈ S` then
  maps `O` into `O` with eventually trivial sections there. So **no point of `O` is singular
  for `B`**, and the germ group of `B` at each point of `O` is the cyclic group of prefix
  replacements.

## 1. The host `W`

The hats are `ĝ` (`g ∈ G`) and `s ∈ V` is the ray shift, both from the embedding lemma
(d-ary form in `bounded-last-section-ascending-hnns-bh-proof`, §2–§3, which uses neither
finiteness hypothesis). So `G*_σ ↪ W_0 := ⟨B, ĝ, s⟩`. Let `W` be the full closure of `W_0`
(all homeomorphisms that locally agree with `W_0`).

- **Singular points.** `ĝ` is locally in `B` away from `p`. On `LC` it acts by `g`, and on
  `0^{k+1}yC` by `ψ_{y−1}σ^k(g) ∈ S`. So each generator of `W_0` has at most the singular point
  `p`, and every element of `W` has finitely many singular points, all in `O`.
- **Finite germ extension of `B`.** `B` is full, so the elements of `W` with no singular point
  form exactly `B`. For `f ∈ W` and `p' ∈ sing(f)`, BHM's cone-swap construction (proof of
  `prop:BoundedAutomataGermExtension`) gives `h ∈ W` with `sing(h) = {p'}` agreeing with `f`
  near `p'`. That uses the normal form of §2 for the germ at `p'`: it maps small cones at `p'`
  onto cones.
- **Singular set.** `sing(W) = O`, one `V`-orbit.

## 2. Local structure at `p`

- **The dilation.** Let `A = 0C` and let `t ∈ V` be any element with `t(0w) = 00w`.
- **The tail lift.** Let `ε_g` be `ĝ` on `A` and the identity elsewhere. Then
  `ε_g = ĝ·(ĝ|_{C∖A})^{-1} ∈ W`. `ε: G → W` is a homomorphism, and
  `ε(G) ≅ E(G)`, because `ε_g` is determined by the values `ψ_zσ^k(g)`.
- **Equivariance.** For `w ∈ C`, using `E_g(0w) = 0E_{σ(g)}(w)`:

  ```text
  t^{-1} ε_g t (0w) = t^{-1}(0 E_g(0w)) = t^{-1}(00 E_{σ(g)}(w)) = 0 E_{σ(g)}(w) = ε_{σ(g)}(0w).
  ```

  So `t^{-1}ε_g t = ε_{σ(g)}·c_g`. Here `c_g := t^{-1}(ε_g|_{A∖t(A)})t ∈ B` is supported in
  `t^{-1}(A∖t(A)) ⊆ C∖A`.
- **Germ group.** Germs of `W` at points of `O` are products of `V`-germs between points of `O`
  and germs at `p` of `ĝ` (equal to that of `ε_g`) and of `s` (equal to `(t)_p^{-1}`). So
  `(W)_p = ⟨(t)_p, (ε(G))_p⟩`, with `(t)_p^{-1}(ε_g)_p(t)_p = (ε_{σ(g)})_p`. Every element has
  the form `(t)_p^i (ε_x)_p (t)_p^{-j}` with `i, j ≥ 0` (move `t^{±1}` past `ε` using the
  relation).
- **Slope.** Each germ in `(W)_p` maps the cones `0^nC` (`n` large) onto cones `0^{n+j}C`. The
  shift `j` is a homomorphism `(W)_p → Z`, with `t ↦ 1` and `ε(G) ↦ 0`.

The same holds at every `p' = α0^∞ ∈ O` after conjugating by the prefix replacement
`w ↦ αw` on `C_α`.

(Basilica: `(W)_p ≅ Z[1/2]² ⋊ Z`, and the dilation germ generates a non-normal infinite-index
subgroup. So BHM Corollary `cor:MainFinitenessCorollary` does not apply. Its condition (3)
fails, which is why §3 is needed.)

## 3. SingFix groups are iterated ascending HNN extensions

For a nonempty clopen `U ⊆ C`, let `W_U = {f ∈ W : f = id off U}`. Let `M' ⊆ U` be a finite set
of rational points not in `sing(B)`, and `M ⊆ M' ∩ O`. Put

```text
SF_U(M, M') = {f ∈ W_U : sing(f) ⊆ M,  f|_{M'} = id}.
```

**Claim.** `SF_U(M, M')` has type `F_∞`. The proof is by induction on `|M|`.

**Base `M = ∅`.** Then `SF_U(∅, M') = Fix_{B_U}(M')`, where `B_U` is the group of elements of
`B` supported in `U`. This is `≅ V_{d,m}S` if `U` is a union of `m` cones. Run BHM's proof of
`thm:Stabilizers` verbatim with `V_{d,m}S` in place of `V_{d,m}`. It needs only two facts:
- at each `s ∈ M'` the germ group of the stabilizer is generated by the germ of a prefix
  dilation (true: `s` is rational and not singular for `B`);
- elements supported on a union of cones form a copy of some `V_{d,m'}S` (true: `B` is full).

So `Fix_{B_U}(M')` is an iterated ascending HNN extension of some `V_{d,n}S`. That group is
`F_∞` by BHM `thm:RoverNek`, and ascending HNN extensions preserve `F_∞`.

**Step.** Pick `p' ∈ M`, `p' = α0^∞`, and put `A = C_α ⊆ U` with `A ∩ M' = {p'}`. Let
`E = U ∖ A`.
- Choose `t ∈ V`, supported in `U`, with `t(αw) = α0w` on `A`, `t = id` near `M'∖{p'}`, and
  `t(E) = U ∖ t(A)`. This exists: `E` and `U∖t(A)` differ by the `L` cones of `A∖t(A)`, so
  cone counts agree mod `L`.
- Let `Y = {ε^α_g}`, the transport of `ε(G)` to `A`. `Y ≅ E(G)` is `F_∞` by hypothesis.
- Let `H = SF_E(M∖{p'}, M'∖{p'})`, which is `F_∞` by induction.
- `H` and `Y` have disjoint supports, so `H_1 := H × Y ≤ G_1 := SF_U(M, M')`, and `H_1` is `F_∞`.

`G_1` is an ascending HNN extension of `H_1` with stable letter `t`. We check BHM's three
conditions (Appendix A):
1. **No positive power of `t` lies in `H_1`.** Germs of `H_1` at `p'` have slope 0, and
   `(t^i)_{p'}` has slope `i`.
2. **`t^{-1}H_1t ≤ H_1`.**
   - For `h ∈ H`: `t^{-1}ht` is supported in `t^{-1}(E) ⊆ E`, and it fixes `M'∖{p'}`. Its
     singular set is `t^{-1}(sing h) ⊆ M∖{p'}`. So it lies in `H`.
   - For `y ∈ Y`: §2 gives `t^{-1}ε^α_g t = ε^α_{σ(g)}·c_g`. Here `c_g ∈ B` is supported in
     `t^{-1}(A∖t(A)) ⊆ E`, which avoids `M'` because `t` fixes `M'∖{p'} ⊆ E`. So `c_g ∈ H`.
3. **`⋃_{i,j} t^i H_1 t^{-j} = G_1`.** Take `f ∈ G_1`.
   - By §2, `(f)_{p'} = (t)^i(ε^α_x)(t)^{-j}`. So `f' = t^{-i}ft^j ∈ G_1` has germ
     `(ε^α_x)_{p'}`.
   - Then `f'' = (ε^α_x)^{-1}f'` is the identity near `p'`. For large `k`, `t^{-k}f''t^k` is the
     identity on `A`, so it lies in `H`.
   - By condition 2, `t^{-k}ε^α_x t^k ∈ Y·H`. Hence `t^{-k}f't^k ∈ H_1` and
     `f ∈ t^{i+k}H_1t^{-(j+k)}`.

So `G_1 ≅ H_1 *_t` is `F_∞`. ∎(Claim)

## 4. `W` has type `F_∞`

Apply BHM Theorem `thm:SingFixFiniteness` for every `n`, with base `B` and `sing(W) = O`:
1. `V ≤ B` acts on `O^n` with finitely many orbits. It is highly transitive on a tail class,
   with support control.
2. `SingFix_W(M, M') = SF_C(M, M')` is `F_∞` by §3.

## 5. Boone–Higman and permutational Boone–Higman

- **The point `q`.** `sing(B)` is a finite union of `V`-orbits (BHM, proof of `thm:RoverNek`).
  Pick a rational `q` whose tail class `O_q` avoids `sing(B)` and `O`; for example
  `(0^j1)^∞` with `j` large.
- **`W` preserves `O_q`.** Every element of `W` is locally in `V` at the points of `O_q`, so `W`
  preserves `O_q` and is transitive on it.
- **High transitivity.** `W` is full, so it acts highly transitively on `O_q` (BBMZ
  `prop:HighlyTransitiveAction`). The action is faithful because `O_q` is dense.
- **Stabilizers.** Let `M_q ⊆ O_q` be finite. `Fix_W(M_q)` is a finite germ extension of
  `Fix_B(M_q)`: the cone swaps of §1 can avoid `M_q`. Its SingFix groups are
  `SF_C(M, M' ∪ M_q)`, which are `F_∞` by §3. `Fix_V(M_q)` still acts on `O^n` with finitely
  many orbits: use elements supported off a neighbourhood of `M_q`. So `Fix_W(M_q)` is `F_∞` by
  `thm:SingFixFiniteness`, in particular finitely generated.
- **Conclusion.** `W` is finitely presented and oligomorphic on `O_q`, with finitely generated
  stabilizers of finite subsets.
  - Zaremsky's theorem (BBMZ `thrm:action_to_simple`) gives a finitely presented simple
    `SV_W ⊇ W ⊇ G*_σ`.
  - The action on `O_q` is of type (A), so `G*_σ ∈ B_A`. ∎
