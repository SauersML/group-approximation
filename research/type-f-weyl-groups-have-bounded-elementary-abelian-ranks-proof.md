Verification tier: proposed-established. The ingredients are standard:
- Serre's theorem on `cd` of finite-index overgroups (Brown, *Cohomology of
  Groups*, VIII.3.1);
- Tate cohomology of a finite group vanishes on projective modules (Brown VI);
- the Künneth formula and the universal coefficient theorem;
- Minkowski's lemma: the kernel of `GL_b(Z) -> GL_b(Z/3)` is torsion-free.

The core step is Browder's exponent theorem (Invent. Math. 71 (1983)
599--607). It is reproved here in the algebraic form needed, so nothing is
cited without proof except the standard facts above. The dependence on
`normalized-benign-witnesses-give-the-semidirect-join` is only for the
translation in Corollary 2.

## Step 0: the complex

Let `G = E/Σ ≅ (Z/p)^r`.
- **Dimension.** `E` is torsion-free and contains `Σ` with finite index, and
  `cd Σ = n < ∞`. By Serre's theorem, `cd E = n`.
- **The complex `C`.** Let `P_* -> Z` be a projective resolution over `ZE` of
  length `n`. Put `C = Z ⊗_{ZΣ} P_* = ZG ⊗_{ZE} P_*`.
  - Each `C_j = ZG ⊗_{ZE} P_j` is a projective `ZG`-module, and `C_j = 0`
    for `j ∉ [0, n]`.
- **Its homology.** `ZE` is free over `ZΣ`, so `P_*` restricted to `Σ` is a
  projective resolution of `Z` over `ZΣ`. Hence `H_j(C) = H_j(Σ; Z)`. The
  induced `G`-action on it is the conjugation action of `E/Σ`.
- **Notation.** `H_0 = Z` with the trivial action. Write `H_j = H_j(Σ; Z)`.

## Step 1: reduction to trivial action on homology

- `G` acts on each `H_i`, `1 ≤ i ≤ n`, through a homomorphism
  `G -> ∏_i Aut(H_i)`.
- Its image is an elementary abelian `p`-group. The image of a subgroup of a
  product has rank at most the sum of the ranks of its projections, so the
  rank of the image is at most `Σ_i rk_p Aut(H_i)`.
- Let `G_0` be the kernel, of rank `r_0 ≥ r - Σ_i rk_p Aut(H_i)`, and let
  `E_0` be its preimage in `E`. Then `E_0` satisfies every hypothesis, with
  trivial action.
- So it suffices to prove the following: **if `G` acts trivially on `H_{≥1}`,
  then `r ≤ 2·#{1 ≤ i ≤ n : H_i ≠ 0}`.** More precisely, `p^r` divides
  `∏_{i=1}^{n} e_i` with `e_i = exp H^{i+1}(G; H_i)`.

**Finiteness of `rk_p Aut H`.** Let `H = Z^b ⊕ T` with `T` finite. There is
an exact sequence `1 -> K -> Aut(H) -> GL_b(Z) × Aut(T)`, where
`K ≅ Hom(Z^b, T) = T^b` (the proof of item 3 of
`u-weyl-pairs-need-out-sigma-to-contain-all-finite-simples`). Let
`P ≤ Aut(H)` be elementary abelian.
- `P ∩ K` has order at most `|T|^b`.
- Finite subgroups of `GL_b(Z)` embed in `GL_b(Z/3)` (Minkowski).
- So `|P| ≤ |T|^b |GL_b(Z/3)| |Aut T|`.
- If `p ∤ |T|·|Aut T|` and `p > b + 1`, then `P = 1`. An element of order `p`
  in `GL_b(Z)` needs `p - 1 ≤ b`.

This gives the stated vanishing of `c_p(Σ)`.

## Step 2: Tate hypercohomology

Fix a complete resolution `F̂` of `Z` over `ZG`. This is an acyclic complex of
projective `ZG`-modules that agrees with a projective resolution in
nonnegative degrees. For a bounded complex `X` of `ZG`-modules (homological
grading), let `T^k(X) = H^k(Hom_G(F̂, X))`, where a degree-`k` cochain is a
family of maps `F̂_q -> X_{q-k}`. Since `X` is bounded, each total degree is a
finite product. Four properties are used:
- **(a) Long exact sequences.** A short exact sequence of bounded complexes
  gives a long exact sequence in `T^*`, since each `Hom_G(F̂_q, -)` is exact.
  - A quasi-isomorphism of bounded complexes induces an isomorphism on `T^*`.
    The cone is bounded and acyclic, and a double complex with finitely many
    exact rows has acyclic total complex.
- **(b) Modules.** For a module `M` placed in homological degree `j`,
  `T^k = Ĥ^{j+k}(G; M)`. In particular `T^0(Z) = Ĥ^0(G; Z) = Z/|G|`,
  generated by `1`.
- **(c) Projectives vanish.** `T^*(C) = 0`.
  - For a projective `ZG`-module `Q`, `Ĥ^*(G; Q) = 0`.
  - `C` is a finite iterated extension of its terms `C_j` in degree `j`,
    through its brutal truncations. Apply (a).
- **(d) Linearity.** All maps in (a) are homomorphisms of abelian groups.

## Step 3: climbing the truncations (Browder's argument)

**The truncations.** For `0 ≤ i ≤ n`, let `X_i` be the quotient complex of
`C` given by:
- `C_j` in degrees `j < i`;
- `C_i/B_i` in degree `i`, where `B_i = d(C_{i+1})`;
- `0` above `i`.

Then:
- `X_n = C`, because `B_n = 0`;
- `X_0 = C_0/B_0 = H_0 = Z`;
- the natural surjection `X_i -> X_{i-1}` has kernel
  `K_i = (C_i/B_i -> B_{i-1})`, in degrees `i` and `i - 1`;
- the differential of `K_i` is onto, so `K_i` has homology `H_i` in
  degree `i` and nothing else;
- the inclusion of `Z_i/B_i = H_i` into degree `i` of `K_i` is a
  quasi-isomorphism.

**The exact sequence.** By (a) and (b), the short exact sequence
`0 -> K_i -> X_i -> X_{i-1} -> 0` gives an exact sequence

`T^0(X_i) -> T^0(X_{i-1}) --δ_i--> T^1(K_i) = Ĥ^{i+1}(G; H_i)`.

**Induction.** Put `e_i = exp Ĥ^{i+1}(G; H_i)`. This is finite, since
`|G|` kills Tate cohomology.
- **Start.** `y_0 = 1 ∈ T^0(X_0) = Z/|G|`.
- **Step.** Suppose `y_{i-1} ∈ T^0(X_{i-1})` maps to
  `(e_1 ⋯ e_{i-1})·1` in `T^0(X_0)` under the composite of the maps
  `T^0(X_{i-1}) -> ... -> T^0(X_0)`.
  - `δ_i(e_i y_{i-1}) = e_i δ_i(y_{i-1}) = 0`.
  - So `e_i y_{i-1}` lifts to some `y_i ∈ T^0(X_i)`.
  - `y_i` maps to `(e_1 ⋯ e_i)·1` in `T^0(X_0)`.
- **End.** `y_n ∈ T^0(X_n) = T^0(C) = 0` by (c). So `(e_1 ⋯ e_n)·1 = 0`
  in `Z/|G|`, that is, **`|G| = p^r` divides `e_1 ⋯ e_n`.**

**Calibration.** Take `E = Z`, `Σ = pZ`, `G = Z/p`. Then
`C = (ZG --(t-1)--> ZG)`, with `H_1 = Z` (spanned by the norm element) and
trivial action. The theorem gives `p | e_1 = exp Ĥ^2(Z/p; Z) = p`, which is
correct and sharp.

## Step 4: the exponents for `G = (Z/p)^r` with trivial coefficients

**`p·H^k(G; Z) = 0` for `k ≥ 1`.**
- By Künneth, `H_j(G; Z)` for `j ≥ 1` is a finite sum of tensor products and
  `Tor` terms of copies of `Z/p` and `Z`, each involving at least one `Z/p`.
  So it is killed by `p`.
- By the universal coefficient theorem, `H^k(G; Z) = Ext(H_{k-1}(G; Z), Z)`
  for `k ≥ 2`, which is killed by `p`.
- Also `H^1(G; Z) = Hom(G, Z) = 0`.

**Finite cyclic coefficients.** Consider the sequence
`0 -> Z -m-> Z -> Z/m -> 0` with trivial action. It gives
`H^k(G; Z) -> H^k(G; Z/m) -> H^{k+1}(G; Z)`, so `H^k(G; Z/m)` is an
extension of a subgroup of `H^{k+1}(G; Z)` by a quotient of `H^k(G; Z)`.
For `k ≥ 1`, both are killed by `p`, so `p^2` kills `H^k(G; Z/m)`.

**Conclusion.** For a finitely generated trivial module `A` and `k = i + 1 ≥ 2`:
- `e_i` divides `p^2`;
- `e_i` divides `p` if `A` is torsion-free;
- `e_i = 1` if `A = 0`.

So `r ≤ Σ_i log_p e_i ≤ 2·#{i ≥ 1 : H_i ≠ 0} ≤ 2n`. With Step 1, this gives
the theorem. ∎

## Corollaries

1. **Weyl groups of torsion-free pairs.** Let `Q ≤ W = N_Λ(Σ)/Σ` be finite,
   and let `P ≤ Q` be elementary abelian of rank `r`.
   - Let `E ≤ N_Λ(Σ)` be the preimage of `P`. Then `Σ ⊴ E` and
     `E/Σ ≅ P`.
   - `E ≤ Λ` is torsion-free.
   - The theorem gives `r ≤ 2 cd Σ + c_p(Σ)`.
   - For a type `F` Weyl pair, `Λ` of type `F` is torsion-free (a finite
     `K(Λ, 1)`), and `Σ` of type `F` has finite `cd` and finitely generated
     homology.
   - For `Γ/M` with `Γ` and `M ⊴ Γ` of type `F`, take `Λ = Γ`, `Σ = M`.
2. **`U` is not type `F` Weyl.** `U` contains every finitely presented group,
   in particular `(Z/2)^r` for all `r`. If `U ≤ W` for a type `F` Weyl pair,
   then `r ≤ 2 cd Σ + c_2(Σ)` for all `r`, which is absurd.
   - By item 2 of `normalized-benign-witnesses-give-the-semidirect-join`,
     this says exactly that `N_U` has no `F(B)`-normalized type `F` witness.
   - So the sufficient criterion in its item 4 fails in type `F`.
3. **Other groups excluded.** The same argument applies to every group with
   elementary abelian `p`-subgroups of unbounded rank for a single `p`:
   - Thompson's `V`, which contains every finite group;
   - `Z/2 ≀ Z ⊇ ⊕_{k<r} Z/2`;
   - Grigorchuk's group, which is regular branch over a subgroup `K` with
     `K × K ≤ K`, so `K^{2^k} ≤ K` for every `k`, and `K` is an infinite
     `2`-group;
   - `⊕_∞ Z/p`. ∎

## What is not claimed

- **The `Out` question.** Whether some type `F` group `Σ` has `A_n ≤ T(Σ)`
  for infinitely many `n` is left open. Those `A_n` would have to be realized
  only by extensions `Σ.A_n` with torsion, and a type `F` Weyl pair never
  supplies such extensions.
- **The root.** The root (normal subgroups, arbitrary witnesses) is not
  touched. Only its normalized sufficient route is killed.
- **Where the argument stops.** It does not apply to `F_m`-Weyl pairs whose
  `Λ` has torsion (item 1 fails), or whose `Σ` has infinite cohomological
  dimension (Step 0 fails).
