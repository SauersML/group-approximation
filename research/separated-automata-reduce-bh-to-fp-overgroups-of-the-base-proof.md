Notation of the claim. `F = F(S_0)` with formal inverses, and `N` is its relator kernel. Put `F_B = F(S_B)`.

## 1. Separated criterion

**Letter rules.**

| letter | receives `y in Y` | receives `z in Z` |
|---|---|---|
| base letter `b` | outputs `b(y) in Y`, section `b|_y` (base) | outputs `z`, section `1` |
| loop letter `t` | outputs `t(y) in Y`, section in `S_B ∪ {1}` | at `ℓ(t)`: outputs `t(ℓ(t))`, section `t`; at other `z`: outputs `t(z) in Z`, section `1` |

Every letter preserves the type (`Y` or `Z`) of the letter it receives.

**Complexity.** Let `β(w)` be the number of loop letters of a reduced word `w`. Then `β(red(w|_x)) <= β(w)`, with
equality iff every loop letter receives its loop letter and no loop letters cancel. In the equality case:
- `x in Z`, since loop letters are in `Z` and types propagate;
- every base letter receives a `Z`-letter and dies;
- `x = ℓ(`rightmost loop letter`)` is unique;
- `w|_x = w_L`, the loop letters of `w` in order, a word following composable arrows of the loop groupoid of
  `nekrashevych-fp-iff-cycle-germ-groups-fp-proof` §0 (objects `Z`).

If `w` is a relator, `w_L` is a closed path at `x` that is a relator.

**Induction.**
- Choose finite `R_B` with `N ∩ F_B = ⟨⟨R_B⟩⟩_lift` in `F_B`, for `B`'s action on `Y^*`.
- Choose finite `R_C` normally generating the germ groupoid relators, using that every `G_(z^ω)` is fp
  (loc. cit. (Φ5)).
- Put `M = ⟨⟨R_B ∪ ŵ(R_C)⟩⟩_lift` in `F`.
- **Base words.** `M ∩ F_B` is a lift ideal of `F_B` for `B`'s action on `Y^*`. A base word with trivial root
  permutation on `Y` and all `Y`-sections in `M` also has trivial root permutation on `Z` and empty `Z`-sections.
  So it contains `R_B`, hence `N ∩ F_B`.
- **Loop words.** By induction on `β` over relators `w` with `β(w) >= 1`: all sections `w|_{x'}` are relators, of
  smaller `β` except possibly at the one letter `x`, where `w|_x = w_L in ⟨⟨ŵ(R_C)⟩⟩_F`, since `ŵ` is a groupoid
  morphism. So all sections lie in `M`, and one lift step gives `w in M`.

So `N = M`. The converse is loc. cit. §1: `Z` is a closed family of stable letters, and the base dies at `Z`. ∎(1)

## 2. Germ saturation

For a closed path `w` at `z`, and `u ≠ z`:
- if `u in Z`, every letter dies and outputs a `Z`-letter;
- if `u = y in Y`, the sections are products of base letters.

So `w ↦ (w|_{Z∖z}; w|_Y, (w|_y)_{y in Y})` embeds `G_(z^ω)` in `Q_z = Sym(Z∖{z}) × (B ≀ Sym(Y))`. Fix `y_0 in Y`.
The spinal loop states
- `ŝ_σ` (`σ in Sym(Z∖z) × Sym(Y)`, loop at `z`, all other sections trivial), and
- `ŝ_{b}` (`b in S_B`, loop at `z`, section `b` at `y_0`, other sections trivial)

are separated, and their germs generate `Q_z`, since `Sym(Y)` is transitive on `Y`. Adding them for every
`z in Z` gives `G^+`. Every vertex group of its germ groupoid then equals `Q_z`, which is finitely presented when
`B` is. A finite presentation `R` of `B` makes its action lift-presented for any finite generating set, directly:
`⟨⟨R⟩⟩ ⊆ ⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift ⊆ N_B`, with no use of Theorem A. So `G^+` is
lift-presented by 1. ∎(2)

## 3. Germ transplant

Every point of `X^ω` with a `Y`-letter is uniquely `u v r`:
- `u in Z^*` is the maximal `Z`-prefix;
- `v` is the maximal `Y`-block (nonempty, finite or infinite);
- `r` is the rest.

By the letter rules, every generator acts as `(u, v, r) ↦ (h(u), f(u)(v), r)`:
- a base letter `b` has `h = 1`, `f(∅) = b` and `f(u) = 1` for `u ≠ ∅`;
- a loop `t` has `h = t_Z` (its action on `Z^*`) and `f_t(u) = q_t` if `u in ℓ(t)^*`, and `1` otherwise.

The rest `r` is fixed: after the `Y`-block every letter is in a base state or trivial, and these fix the next
`Z`-letter with trivial section. These are elements of `W = B̂^{Z^*} ⋊ H` (unrestricted), where `H = ⟨t_Z⟩ <= Aut(Z^*)`. `W` acts faithfully on
`Ξ = {(u, v)}`, because `B̂` acts faithfully on `Y`-blocks. `G` acts faithfully on the dense invariant set of
points with a `Y`-letter. So `G` is isomorphic to the subgroup of `W` generated by these elements.

Let `φ: B̂ ↪ P`, with `P = ⟨S_P⟩` an automaton group on `Y'`. Let `S_P'` be `S_P` together with all sections of
`φ(b)` (`b in S_B`) and `φ(q_t)` (`t in L`), and their inverses. It is finite, because sections of a word over `S_P` are words of no
greater length. Define `G'` on `(Y' ⊔ Z)^*`:
- base letters `φ(b)`, fixing `Z` with trivial sections;
- loops `t'` with `t'_Z = t_Z`, which act at a `Y'`-letter `y'` by outputting `φ(q_t)(y')` with section
  `φ(q_t)|_{y'}`.

`G'` is a subgroup of the separated automaton group `⟨S_P' ∪ L'⟩` over `P`. By the same decomposition it is the
subgroup of `P^{Z^*} ⋊ H` generated by the `φ`-images of the generators of `G`. `φ` induces an injective
homomorphism `W -> P^{Z^*} ⋊ H` mapping generators to generators. So `G ≅ G'`. ∎(3)

## 4. Reduction

**`B̂` embeds in `B ≀ Sym(Y)`.** The sections of `b in B` and of `q_t` lie in `B`, and sections of products are
products of sections. So the wreath recursion restricted to `B̂` is injective with image in `B ≀ Sym(Y)`.

**The host.** If `B ↪ P`, then `B̂ ↪ P ≀ Sym(Y)`. That is the automaton group `⟨ι_j(S_P), Sym(Y)⟩` of
`lift-presentable-groups-closed-under-products-and-wreaths` (proof of 1). There `Δ(s) = ∏_j ι_j(s)` (disjoint supports, equal action on each cone; only this identity and injectivity of the
`ι_j` are used from that unrefereed node), so it is exactly
`P ≀ Sym(Y)`, and it is finitely presented when `P` is.

**Conclusion.** By 3, `G` is isomorphic to a separated automaton group over `P ≀ Sym(Y)`. By 2 that group lies in a
lift-presented `G^+`. Then `lift-presented-automaton-groups-satisfy-boone-higman` gives the finitely presented
simple host. That last step uses Theorem A of `rover-nekrashevych-fp-iff-finite-lift-presentation`, sufficiency
half. ∎
