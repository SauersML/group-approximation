**ESTABLISHED** through `fpbs-sandwich-cost-fiber-formula-proof`.

Notation: `p^Δ_K(b) = inf{ C(E) : E_{b|K} ⊆ E ⊆ E_b }`. For `N ≤ Δ` of
index `d`, write `b_N = b × (Δ ↷ Δ/N)`.

**(A) Fiber formula.** Let `Δ` be a countable group, `K ≤ Δ`, and `N ≤ Δ`
of finite index `d` with `K N = Δ`. Let `b` be any p.m.p. action of `Δ`.
Then

```text
p^Δ_K(b_N) = 1 + ( p^N_{K∩N}(b|N) − 1 ) / d        (in [0, ∞]).
```

Restriction to `X × {N}` is a cost-rescaling bijection between the two
sandwich lattices. For Bernoulli, `s_Δ|N ≅ s_N`.

**(B) Consequence for RBL.** Suppose RBL
(`fpbs-relative-sandwich-cost-bernoulli-lower-bound`) holds for `(Δ, K)`
with `K` finitely generated. Then for every such `N`,

```text
p^Δ_K(s_Δ) ≤ 1 + ( p^N_{K∩N}(s_N) − 1 ) / d.
```

The reverse inequality holds by the easy direction (Proposition 2.5 of the
RBL artifact, not a graph node). With it, RBL predicts exact index
multiplicativity:

```text
p^N_{K∩N}(s_N) − 1 = d · (p^Δ_K(s_Δ) − 1).
```

**(C) Hanna Neumann seal.** Let `Δ` be free, `K ≤ Δ` of rank 2, `N` as in
(A), and `M'` finitely generated with `K∩N ≤ M' ≤ N`. Then

```text
rk M' ≥ 1 + d.
```

So for every free action `b` of `Δ`, the sandwich of `(b_N, K)` induced
from `E_{b|M'}` has cost `1 + (rk M' − 1)/d ≥ 2`. For `Δ = F_2`, every
sandwich of `(b, K)` generated by a subgroup `K ≤ M ≤ F_2` also has cost
`rk M ≥ 2`, and `E_b` has cost 2. So finite-cover test actions combined
with virtually group-generated sandwiches give exactly the value 2, in
Bernoulli and in every other free action. They cannot separate `s_{F_2}`
from any free action.

Both a counterexample to RBL on `F_2` and a proof of it must therefore go
through sandwiches that are not virtually group-generated. The target
statement is `fpbs-free-group-sandwich-cost-is-two`, a measured Hanna
Neumann statement, which implies the `F_2` slice of RBL.

**Computation.** An exhaustive and random census
(`experiments/fpbs-sandwich-fiber-rank-drop-2026-09-17/`) found no virtual
rank drop `mrk_N(K∩N) < 1 + d(rmin(K) − 1)`. It covered 839 exhaustive
and 400 random rank-2 pairs and 328 rank-3 pairs in `F_2` with `d ≤ 3`,
and 150 rank-3 pairs in `F_3` with `rmin(K) = 3` and `d ≤ 3`. Here `rmin` is the least rank of an overgroup, and `mrk_N` is the
least rank of an intermediate subgroup of `N`. This confirms (C) in `F_2`.
In `F_r` with `r ≥ 3`, a drop is not excluded by (C), and none was found.
