## Setting

- **The lattice.** Take an interlacing pair `({ζ_z}_{z ∈ Z}, {δ_D}_{D ∈ V})` on a connected graph `L`
  with vertex set `V`, `|V| >= 2` (Amir–Lazarovich, AL, arXiv:2605.09493, Prop. "the lattice Lambda").
  Then `Λ' = ⟨Z ∪ V | z², D², [D,D'] ({D,D'} ∈ E(L)), z·D·δ_D(z)·ζ_z(D)⟩`.
  - `Λ'` acts on `T × X_L`, where `T = X_Z` is the c-regular tree, simply transitively on vertices.
  - `W = ⟨V⟩` is the stabilizer of the base tree vertex `t_0`.
- **The subgroups.** `χ: Λ' → (Z/2)²` sends `z ↦ (1,0)` and `D ↦ (0,1)`, and `Λ'^+ = ker χ`.
  Also `φ: W → F_2^V` is the abelianization, and `W^+ = W ∩ Λ'^+`, the even words.
- **Legal states.** `L(D) = {z : δ_D(z) = z}`. By (D5), `z ∈ L(D)` iff `z ∈ L(ζ_z(D))`.

## Statement

1. **Transducer formula.** Let `ψ: Λ'^+ → F_2` be a homomorphism. Then there are `f_1, f_2: V → F_2`,
   `c ∈ F_2` and `h: Z → F_2` with
   `ψ(w) = f_1·φ(w)` and `ψ(z_0 w z_0) = f_2·φ(w)` for `w ∈ W^+`, and
   ```text
   (*)   f_1(D) + f_2(ζ_z(D)) + c = h(z) + h(δ_D(z))     for all z ∈ Z, D ∈ V.
   ```
   Here `⟨δ_D⟩` is assumed transitive on Z. Conversely, `ψ = 0` whenever `f_1` and `f_2` are
   constant. Also `H_1(Λ'^+; Z)` is an elementary abelian 2-group.
2. **Perfectness.** Assume in addition:
   - **(P1)** there is `Y ⊆ Z` such that the even-length words in `{ζ_y : y ∈ Y}` form a group that
     is transitive on V;
   - **(P2)** if `y ∈ Y \ L(D)`, then `ζ_y(D) = D`;
   - **(P3)** if `Y ⊄ L(D)`, then `ζ_w(D) = D` for some `w ∈ L(D)`;
   - **(P4)** every `δ_D` has a fixed point in Z.

   Then `H_1(Λ'^+; Z) = 0`. So `Λ'^+ = [Λ', Λ']` is perfect, and `H_1(Λ') = (Z/2)²`.
3. **AL lattices.** (P1), (P2) and (P4) hold for every lattice of AL's proof of Theorem A, with
   `Y = 𝒴`, for every involutive BMW seed Γ. (P3) holds at B always. It holds at `C_i` whenever some
   `υ_m` with `m ∉ {i, …, i+3}` preserves `C_i`; that is true for AL's printed `C_i` once `d >= 9`.
   So in all these lattices `Λ'^+` is perfect.
4. **What is left for Kourovka 21.46 in AL-type data.** With item 3 of
   `amir-lazarovich-simple-lattice-has-torsion-iff-index-four`, the following are equivalent:
   - AL's simple lattice Λ is torsion-free, so of cd 3;
   - the perfect group `Λ'^+` has a nonabelian finite simple quotient.

   No abelian or solvable quotient of any finite-index subgroup containing `Λ'^+` can certify this.

## Proof

**Tree amalgam.**
- The relator gives `D·δ_D(z) = z·ζ_z(D)`. So D maps the tree edge `e_{δ_D(z)} = [t_0, δ_D(z)t_0]` to
  `e_z`, and `z` inverts `e_z`.
- `Λ'^+` preserves the bipartition of T, since `χ_1` is the parity of the T-displacement. It is
  transitive on edges: the stabilizer of `e_{z_0}` in `Λ'` contains `z_0` and any D with
  `z_0 ∈ L(D)`, so it meets every coset of `Λ'^+`.
- Bass–Serre then gives `Λ'^+ = W^+ *_{E^+} z_0W^+z_0`, with
  `E^+ = W^+ ∩ z_0W^+z_0 = {w ∈ W^+ : w z_0t_0 = z_0t_0}`.

**`W^+`.**
- `W^+` is generated by the `s_D = DD_0`. An even word `D_{i_1}⋯D_{i_{2m}}` equals
  `s_{i_1}s_{i_2}^{-1}s_{i_3}⋯`. So every `ψ: W^+ → F_2` is `f_1·φ` with `f_1(D) = ψ(s_D)`.
- For an edge `{D,D'}`, `(DD')² = 1` gives `2(s_D − s_{D'}) = 0` in `H_1(W^+)`. Since L is connected,
  every `s_D` has order at most 2 there.
- `H_1(Λ'^+)` is generated by the images of the two vertex groups, so it is elementary abelian.

**Transducer identity.**
- For `w = D_1⋯D_m ∈ W`, iterate `zD = ζ_z(D)·δ_D(z)`:
  `z_0 w = D_1'⋯D_m' z_m`, where `z_i = δ_{D_i}(z_{i−1})` and `D_i' = ζ_{z_{i−1}}(D_i)`.
- So `w ∈ E^+` iff m is even and the state walk `z_0 → z_1 → ⋯` returns to `z_0`. In that case
  `z_0 w z_0 = D_1'⋯D_m'`.
- This is the wreath recursion of the transducer with states Z, letters V, output `ζ_z` and
  transition `δ_D`.

**Item 1.**
- Put `g(D, z) = f_1(D) + f_2(ζ_z(D))`. Evaluating `ψ` on `e ∈ E^+` in both vertex groups gives
  `Σ_i g(D_i, z_{i−1}) = 0` along every closed state walk of even length at `z_0`.
- Every closed walk in the state graph (vertices Z, an edge `z — δ_D(z)` for each `(z, D)`) is read
  by an element of W. By (D4), `g(D, z) = g(D, δ_D(z))`, so g lives on undirected edges.
- Let c be the g-sum of any closed odd walk. Then `g + c` sums to 0 on all closed walks. The state
  graph is connected, so `g + c = δh` for some `h: Z → F_2`, which is (*).
- If `f_1` and `f_2` are constant, `ψ` vanishes on the vertex groups, since their φ-images have even
  weight. So `ψ = 0`.

**Item 2.**
- **`f_2` on a star.** At `z ∈ L(G)`, (*) reads `f_1(G) + f_2(ζ_z(G)) + c = 0`. So `f_2` is constant
  on the star `{ζ_u(G) : u ∈ L(G)}`.
- **Invariance under pairs.** We show `f_2(X) = f_2(ζ_{y'}ζ_y(X))` for all X and `y, y' ∈ Y`.
  Put `G = ζ_y(X)`.
  - If `y ∈ L(X)` and `y' ∈ L(G)`: X and `ζ_{y'}(G)` both lie in the star of G, since
    `y ∈ L(G)` by (D5).
  - If `y ∉ L(X)`: `G = X` by (P2), and we need `f_2(X) = f_2(ζ_{y'}(X))`. This is trivial if
    `y' ∉ L(X)`, by (P2). Otherwise (P3) gives `w ∈ L(X)` with `ζ_w(X) = X`. Then X and `ζ_{y'}(X)`
    both lie in the star of X.
  - If `y ∈ L(X)` and `y' ∉ L(G)`: `ζ_{y'}(G) = G`, and we need `f_2(X) = f_2(G)`. (P3) at G gives
    `w ∈ L(G)` with `ζ_w(G) = G`. Then `G = ζ_w(G)` and `X = ζ_y(G)` both lie in the star of G.
- **`f_2` is constant.** By (P1), `f_2` is constant on the transitive group of even words, hence on V.
- **`f_1` is constant.** For each D, (P4) gives `z ∈ L(D)`, and then (*) gives `f_1(D) = c + f_2`.
- So `ψ = 0`. Since `H_1(Λ'^+; Z)` is an elementary abelian 2-group, it is 0.
- Every commutator lies in `Λ'^+`, and `[Λ'^+, Λ'^+] = Λ'^+`. So `[Λ', Λ'] = Λ'^+`.

**Item 3.** The data are recorded in `gq-bh-bh-free-42-al-scaffolding-reading.md`, §A.
- **(P1).** `⟨υ_j⟩ = Alt_{2d−1}` by (E3). The even-word subgroup is normal of index at most 2, so it
  is `Alt_{2d−1}`, which is transitive on `(d−1)`-sets.
- **(P2).** A y is moved only by `δ_B` (just `y_1`) and by `δ_{C_i}` (just `y_i..y_{i+3}`). The
  corresponding `υ` preserve B or `C_i` by (E3) or (E4).
- **(P4).** The `α_j` fix 𝒴 pointwise, and `β`, `γ_i` move 4 points each. Also
  `⟨δ_D⟩ = Alt(Z)` (AL, Claim "the local actions of Λ").
- **(P3) at B.** `w = x_4`: β fixes it, and `supp ξ_4 ⊆ B` (AL, l.2224).
- **(P3) at the printed `C_i` with `d >= 9`.** The complement is an arc of `d+1` consecutive points
  minus at most one point, so it contains a window `{m, …, m+3}`. Then `υ_m` preserves `C_i`, and
  `m ∉ {i, …, i+6} ⊆ C_i`.

**Item 4.** If `Λ ≠ Λ'^+`, then `Λ'^+/Λ` is a nontrivial finite perfect group, so it has a nonabelian
simple quotient. The converse is item 3 of the required node. Every M between `Λ'^+` and `Λ'` has
`[M, M] ⊇ [Λ'^+, Λ'^+] = Λ'^+`.

## Scope

- **Relation to bh-free-extensions.** `amir-lazarovich-simple-lattices-have-torsion` settles the AL
  route for seeds satisfying (H). There `Λ'^+` is simple, which is stronger than item 2.
- **What this adds for seeds violating (H).** A torsion-free simple lattice would need a nonabelian
  finite simple quotient of `Λ'^+`. By that node, the quotient of `Λ'` would have to separate all
  the `A_j`.
- **Hypotheses.** Items 1–2 need neither the Odd graph nor Alt local actions beyond (P1)–(P4). So they
  apply to other one-vertex product lattices, for instance to scaffoldings with other `υ`'s.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Lattices are transducers.** A lattice acting simply transitively on the vertices of `T × X` is a
  transducer: the relator `zD = ζ_z(D) δ_D(z)` says that state z reads letter D, outputs `ζ_z(D)`
  and moves to `δ_D(z)`. So its abelian invariants are cocycles (*) of that transducer. For
  `Λ'^+` this is a linear system with `|V| + |Z|` unknowns.
- **Transitivity kills abelian quotients.** Whenever the legal moves generate a perfect transitive
  group, only constants survive. The simple group produced by the normal subgroup theorem is then
  never cut down by abelian or solvable quotients. Whether it is the obvious index-4 subgroup is a
  question about nonabelian finite simple quotients alone.
- **For BH hosts built by NST.** The host is the finite residual Λ, and an embedded seed Γ keeps
  only `Γ ∩ Λ`. The colour characters are exactly what forces `Γ ∩ Λ` to be a proper subgroup of
  Γ, since Γ is generated by involutions. To embed all of Γ, the seed has to sit in the
  colour-preserving part.
- **The same formula elsewhere.** It computes `H_1` of the type-preserving subgroup of any BMW or
  one-vertex cube-complex lattice.
