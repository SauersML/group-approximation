Notation as in the target. All groups `G, H, S, mu_3 H` are closed subgroups of `U(d)`, hence compact Lie
groups. `H` is normal in `G` (closure of the normal subgroup `rho(K)` of the dense subgroup `rho(M)`),
and `S` is characteristic in `H` (closed subgroup generated by commutators), hence normal in `G`.
Scalars `mu_3 I` are central, so `S = cl[mu_3 H, mu_3 H]` as well. Standard input used: **(PW)** a
compact Lie group has a faithful finite-dimensional unitary representation, and every irreducible
unitary representation of a compact `L <= U(d)` occurs in some `V_ab` restricted to `L`.

## Step 1: (CD1)

Let `N = cl rho(ker c)`, normal in `G`. The map `M -> G -> G/N` kills `ker c`, so it factors through
`M / ker c = E_3` ([ST3] of `deligne-torelli-character-counts-separating-twists`), and under this
identification `t_s` maps to `z^(epsilon)`, `epsilon` a unit mod 3. `G/N` is a compact Lie group; let
`pi` be a faithful finite-dimensional unitary representation of it (PW). Then `pi` composed with
`E_3 -> G/N` is a finite-dimensional unitary representation of `E_3`, so it kills `z` by (DTC2) of
`deligne-triple-cover-fd-central-invisibility`. Faithfulness of `pi` gives `rho(t_s) in N`. Since `N` is
normal in `G` and `K` is the normal closure of `t_s` in `M` ([ST1]), `rho(K) <= N`, so `H <= N <= H`.

For the reformulation: `K` is generated by the separating twists ([ST1]), and a word `k` in them maps
under `x -> g_x` to `omega^(-e(k)) rho(k)`, `e` the exponent sum. By [ST2], `e(k) = 0 mod 3` on
`ker c`, so the closed group `N_1` generated by the `g_x` contains `cl rho(ker c) = H`. It contains
`g_s` and `rho(t_s) in H`, hence `omega^-1 I`, hence `mu_3 H`; and it lies in the closed set `mu_3 H`.
Each `||g_x - I|| = ||rho(t_x) - omega I|| = eps`, as the `t_x` are conjugate to `t_s`.

## Step 2: (CD2)

On `V_ab` the scalar `omega I in U(d)` acts, through the tensor word extended to `U(d)`, by
`omega^(a-b)`.

*(a) excludes (b).* If `omega I in S` and `a - b` is not `0 mod 3`, then `omega I` acts on `V_ab^S` both
trivially and by the scalar `omega^(a-b) != 1`, so `V_ab^S = 0`.

*Not (a) implies (b).* Let `L = mu_3 H`, compact, with `S` normal in `L`, and suppose `omega I notin S`.
Then the image of `omega I` in the compact Lie group `L/S` is non-trivial, so by (PW) some irreducible
unitary representation `tau` of `L/S` has `tau(omega I) != 1`. Inflate `tau` to `L`; by (PW) it occurs in
some `V_ab |_L`. Its image there is `S`-fixed, so `V_ab^S != 0`, and `omega I` acts on it by the scalar
`omega^(a-b)`, which equals `tau(omega I) != 1`. So `a - b` is not `0 mod 3`.

## Step 3: (CD3)

`V_ab^S` is `G`-invariant because `S` is normal in `G`, so `sigma = rho_ab |_(V_ab^S)` is an honest
representation of `M`. On it `S` acts trivially, so `H` acts through the abelian group `H/S`
(`S >= [H,H]`). So `sigma(K)` lies in the image of `H` in `U(V_ab^S)`, a quotient of `H/S`, and is
abelian: `sigma` is Torelli-abelian.

For the norm: `rho(t_s)` is unitary with eigenvalues `lambda_i = omega exp(2 pi i delta_i)`, where
`2 |sin(pi delta_i)| <= eps`. The operator `rho_ab(t_s)` is unitary with eigenvalues
`lambda_(i_1)...lambda_(i_a) conj(lambda_(j_1)...lambda_(j_b)) = omega^(a-b) exp(2 pi i Delta)`, where
`Delta` is a signed sum of `a + b` of the `delta`'s. `V_ab^S` reduces `rho_ab(t_s)`, so the spectrum of
`sigma(t_s)` lies among these. Using `|sin(x + y)| <= |sin x| + |sin y|`,

```text
| omega^(a-b) exp(2 pi i Delta) - omega^(a-b) | = 2 |sin(pi Delta)| <= sum 2 |sin(pi delta)| <= (a + b) eps .
```

For a normal operator the operator norm is the largest eigenvalue modulus, so
`||sigma(t_s) - omega^(a-b) I|| <= (a + b) eps`. If `omega^(a-b) = omega^-1`, the complex conjugate
`conj(sigma)` is honest, Torelli-abelian, and within `(a + b) eps` of `omega I`.

## Step 4: (CD4)

Let `W <= C^d` be an irreducible `H`-subrepresentation, `n = dim W`. The line `Lambda^n W` lies in
`Lambda^n C^d <= V_(n,0)`, and `h in H` acts on it by `det(h |_W)`. This character of `H` kills
commutators, hence by continuity `S`, so `V_(n,0)^S != 0`. If `3 not | n`, then `n - 0` is not `0 mod 3`
and `D(rho) <= n`. In case (a), `D(rho) = infinity` by (CD2), so every such `n` is divisible by 3;
`C^d` is a direct sum of irreducible `H`-subrepresentations (compactness), so `3 | d`.

## Step 5: (CD5)

If `rho` is outside case (a), (CD2) gives `(a,b)` with `a + b = D(rho)` and `V_ab^S != 0`, and (CD3)
gives an honest Torelli-abelian `sigma'` (namely `sigma` or `conj(sigma)`) with
`c_ab <= ||sigma'(t_s) - omega I|| <= D(rho) eps`. The sequence statement is this bound applied to a
sequence witnessing `1/3 in Z_hon` (definition in
`mod-s2-exact-representations-have-an-opnorm-torelli-scalar-gap`), together with (CD4). And
`1/3 notin Z_ab` iff `c_ab > 0` is the definition of `Z_ab` in
`mod-s2-torelli-abelian-scalars-are-shifted-maslov-multipliers`. QED.

## Remarks (not part of the claim)

- The loss `D(rho)` is not dimension-free. (CD4) bounds it by the least prime-to-3 dimension of an
  irreducible Torelli constituent. When `rho(K)` is abelian, `S = 1` and `D = 1` (no loss), which
  recovers the Torelli-abelian case trivially. In the finite-image case the target of
  `mod-s2-finite-image-near-scalar-reps-are-torelli-abelian` gives `S = 1` directly.
- Case (a) is not an empty condition on closed subgroups: if `H >= SU(d)` and `3 | d`, then
  `omega I in SU(d) = [SU(d), SU(d)] <= S`. Nothing here says such an `H` arises from a near-`omega`
  honest `rho`; whether it can is exactly residual (R-a).
- (CD1) says the `eps`-near-identity generators `g_x` of the w10 Frobenius attempt topologically generate
  a group containing `omega I`, at distance `sqrt 3` from `I`; (R-a) asks whether `omega I` can moreover
  lie in the closed derived subgroup of that group.
