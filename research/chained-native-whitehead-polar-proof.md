The coefficient products in `(CNW1)` follow directly from
`t_u s_v=delta_(u,v)`.  They also give

```text
y_1x_1=s_0t_0,       x_1y_1=s_(00)t_(00),
y_2x_2=s_(00)t_(00), x_2y_2=s_(000)t_(000).           (CNP1)
```

The standard characteristic-two three-elementary calculation applied on
the index pairs `(8,7)` and `(9,8)` proves `(CNW2)` and `J_i^2=1`.

For `J_1`, its `2 by 2` block on ordered indices `(8,7)` is

```text
[[1-e_(00),x_1],[y_1,1-e_0]].                         (CNP2)
```

Conjugating a root whose terminal index is `7` or `8` by `(CNP2)` and using

```text
a_1y_1=a_2,       a_2x_1=a_1,
a_1(1-e_0)=0,     a_2(1-e_(00))=0                    (CNP3)
```

shows that `J_1` swaps `x_i7(a_1)` with `x_i8(a_2)` for `i=5,6`.
The analogous block for `J_2`, together with
`a_2y_2=a_3` and `a_3x_2=a_2`, swaps `x_i8(a_2)` with
`x_i9(a_3)`.  This proves `(CNW4)`.  The Whitehead indices are disjoint
from those of `H`, so both words centralize `H` and `e`.

For a finite elementary-abelian group `D`, write
`z_D=|D|^(-1)sum_(d in D)d`.  All groups occurring in `(CNW3)--(CNW7)` lie
in the common abelian root group generated by the six displayed roots, and

```text
z_D z_E=z_<D,E>.                                      (CNP4)
```

Consequently

```text
p_2(J_i p_2J_i)p_2=e z_<W_0,J_iW_0>,
W_0=<v,w,s>,                                           (CNP5)
```

which is `q_i`.  Each generated subgroup in `(CNP5)` is `J_i`-invariant,
so `p_2J_ip_2=J_iq_i` is a self-adjoint partial isometry with support
`q_i`.  The subgroups have order `16`, and their intersection with `H` is
trivial, proving `(CNW5)--(CNW6)`.

The group generated by the two swaps in `(CNW4)` is `S_3`, acting
transitively on each of the two triples

```text
{v,r,t},                     {w,s,l}.                  (CNP6)
```

The sum of the `S_3`-translates of `W_0` is therefore all of `N`.  By
`(CNP4)`, the meet of the commuting projections
`sigma p_2 sigma^(-1)`, `sigma in S_3`, is `ez_N=p_*`.  If `Q<=p_2`
reduces both `J_i`, then `Q<=sigma p_2sigma^(-1)` for every `sigma`, hence
`Q<=p_*`.  This proves maximality in `(CNW7)`.  The trace calculation is
again the identity coefficient and gives `(CNW8)`.

Since `A_2=r in N`, it acts as the identity on `z_N`.  Also `p_*<=p_2`,
and `p_2` reduces `A_1,A_2` by
`center-chain-hecke-polar-supports-are-nested-pauli-flags`; all extra roots
in `N` commute with `A_1`.  Hence `p_*` reduces `A_1` and `(CNW9)` follows.
The same argument gives the equality on every `Q<=p_*`.

The first pair in `(CNW10)` reduces `p_*` because it reduces `p_2` and its
actions preserve the enlarged root average.  Conjugating it by `J_1,J_2`
gives the other two reducing pairs.  The coefficient products in `(CNP1)`
make every own commutator `x_42(q)`, while different pairs cross-commute.
Since `x_42(q)e=-e`, the standard three-Pauli tensor decomposition gives
`(CNW11)`, exactly as in
`same-center-prefix-whiteheads-are-uncontrolled-adjacent-swaps`.

For finiteness, the six-root group `N`, the three Pauli pairs, `H`, and the
two Whiteheads form a finite collected root group extended by the finite
permutation group `S_3`; the elementary image separates its collected
forms.  In its left regular representation, `p_*` is the character atom of
the finite subgroup generated by `H,N`, and `J_1J_2` represents a nontrivial
order-three normalizer coset.  Therefore its group-basis trace on `p_*` is
zero.  Expanding the squared norm gives `(CNW12)`, completing the exact
finite countermodel and the proof.
