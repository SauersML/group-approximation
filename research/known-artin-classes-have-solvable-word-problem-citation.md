This is a citation import. Sources were read on 2026-09-13 through ar5iv or arXiv
abstract renderings with a summarizing fetch tool; the primary PDFs were not read.

- **Right-angled and spherical types:** Belk–Bleak–Matucci–Zaremsky, arXiv:2306.16356,
  footnote 10. The survey says the word problem "is known for several large classes,
  such as right-angled Artin groups ... and Artin groups of spherical or Euclidean
  type." Both classes are also covered by type FC below.
- **Irreducible euclidean type:** McCammond–Sulway, *Artin groups of euclidean type*,
  arXiv:1312.7770 (Invent. Math.), Theorem D: "Every irreducible euclidean Artin group
  Art(X̃_n) is a torsion-free centerless group with a solvable word problem and a
  finite-dimensional classifying space."
- **Type FC:**
  - Huang–Osajda, *Helly meets Garside and Artin*, arXiv:1904.09060, Main Theorem:
    "Weak Garside groups of finite type and Artin groups of type FC are Helly." Item
    (6) of their Corollary says such groups are biautomatic.
  - Chalopin–Chepoi–Genevois–Hirai–Osajda, *Helly groups*, arXiv:2002.06895, Geom.
    Topol. 29 (2025), Theorem 1.5(1): Helly groups are biautomatic.
  - A biautomatic group has solvable word problem.
- **Large type:** Huang–Osajda, *Large-type Artin groups are systolic*,
  arXiv:1706.05473 (Proc. LMS), Theorem 5.8: "Every Artin group of almost large type
  is systolic." Item (1) of their Corollary gives biautomaticity, through
  Januszkiewicz–Świątkowski.
- **Dimension at most 2:** Huang–Osajda, *Metric systolicity and two-dimensional Artin
  groups*, arXiv:1710.05157, Corollary 1.3(2): finitely presented subgroups of
  2-dimensional Artin groups have solvable conjugacy problem. The group itself is one,
  and a solvable conjugacy problem gives a solvable word problem. Their dimension
  criterion, credited to Charney–Davis, is `1/p + 1/q + 1/r ≤ 1` on every triangle.
- **Rank three:** Delucchi–Paolini–Salvetti, *Dual structures on Coxeter and Artin
  groups of rank three*, arXiv:2206.14518, Geom. Topol. 28 (2024) 4295–4336, Theorem
  7.4: "The word problem for G_W is solvable."
- **Direct products** (case 8): an elementary closure property.
