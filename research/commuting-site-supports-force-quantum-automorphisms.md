**ESTABLISHED** by `commuting-site-supports-force-quantum-automorphisms-proof`.

**Setting.** Let `G` be a group and `d >= 2`, with `A = (x)_G M_d`, shifts `alpha_g`, and a quantum automaton
`Phi` (unital *-endomorphism, `alpha_g Phi = Phi alpha_g`, `Phi(A_e) <= A_N` for a finite `N`). For `h in G` and
`k in hN`, the *site support* `S(h,k) <= A_k = M_d` is the *-algebra generated by the slices
`(omega (x) id)(x)` of all `x in Phi(A_h)` at site `k`, with `omega` ranging over functionals on `A_(hN \ k)`.

**Hypothesis (CS).** For every site `k` and all `h != h'` with `k in hN cap h'N`, the algebras `S(h,k)` and
`S(h',k)` commute.

(CS) holds whenever `N` is *left Sidon*: `|N cap gN| <= 1` for every `g != e`.

**Theorem.** Under (CS), `Phi` is an automorphism. Moreover, for each `m in N` the algebra `S(e,m)` is a full matrix
algebra `M_(c_m)`, `prod_m c_m = d`, and:
- every site splits as `A_k = (x)_(m in N) S(k m^-1, k)`;
- `Phi(A_h) = (x)_(m in N) S(h, hm)`.

So `Phi` is an on-site isomorphism `M_d -> (x)_m M_(c_m)` followed by a wiring of tensor slots, and
`Phi^-1(A_e) <= A_(N^-1)`.

**Key count.** Let `k_min(B)` be the smallest dimension of an irreducible representation of a finite-dimensional
C*-algebra `B`.
- (a) The unital embedding `M_d = Phi(A_e) <= (x)_m S(e,m)` gives `prod_m k_min(S(e,m)) >= d`.
- (b) At a site, the commuting supports `S(k m^-1, k)`, `m in N`, act on `C^d` through their tensor product, so
  `prod_m k_min(S(e,m)) <= d`.

The two products agree term by term because `G` indexes both through `N`. The equality case forces every
representation involved to be irreducible, which gives the slot splitting.

**Consequences.**
1. *Where a strict quantum automaton can live.* A counterexample to `quantum-surjunctivity-conjecture`, on any group,
   must fail (CS). In particular its minimal neighbourhood `{m : S(e,m) != C}` is not left Sidon: some `g != e`
   has `|N cap gN| >= 2`.
   - Over torsion-free groups every two-point set is Sidon, so the neighbourhood has at least three points with a
     relation `m_1 m_2^-1 = m_3 m_4^-1` between distinct pairs, e.g. `{e, a, a^2}`.
2. *Belief corrected.* The Attempts of `quantum-surjunctivity-conjecture` say the Schumacher–Werner
   support-commutation step "uses the linear order of Z and fails for more than one generator, which is why the sofic
   count is needed".
   - Support commutation needs no order and no soficity. It needs one-site overlaps, and the per-site count then
     closes on every group.
   - What fails beyond `Z` is commutation itself, at overlaps of size at least two (artifact E2).
3. *Census explained.* On `F_2`, with `N = {1, a, b}` (left Sidon) and `d = p` prime, `prod c_m = p` forces one slot
   to carry `M_p`. This proves the remark of step 3 in `quantum-surjunctivity-conjecture`, for every `d` and every
   host.
   - In the Clifford sector it gives exactly the `3 |Sp_2(F_p)|` wirings-with-gates found by `clifford_star_f2.py`.
4. *Calibration off groups.* The strict tree automaton of `sofic-groups-are-qca-surjunctive` (part 4) has commuting
   supports, `S(v,c) = S(v,c') = M_p`. There count (a) runs over two child slots (`p . p >= p`) and count (b) over
   one parent slot (`p <= p`). They cannot be compared, which shows the matching of in-slots and out-slots given by
   the group is essential.
5. *Decidable criterion.* (CS) is a finite-dimensional check on the local rule, so it is a sufficient test for
   reversibility with an explicit inverse of neighbourhood `N^-1`.

**Scope.** This does not touch the Clifford/Kaplansky core. By the theorem, the strict Clifford automaton
`diag(A, B^*)` built from `AB = 1 != BA` over `F_p[G]` has a non-Sidon neighbourhood. Sidon-supported matrices are
a regime where direct finiteness is expected to be elementary, so no Kaplansky instance is decided here. The theorem
marks the exact boundary of per-site counting: noncommuting supports at a two-site overlap.
