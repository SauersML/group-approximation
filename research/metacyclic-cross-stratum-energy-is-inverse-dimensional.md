Let `N_m=4^m-1`, `m>=2`, and let

```text
b_m=(R_m,S_m),             c_m=(R_m,S_m^2)            (MCE1)
```

be the two length-`m` exact metacyclic cores from
`bs-root-strata-have-no-energy-paid-invariant-reconciliation`.  Put
`A=A_(N_m)`, `B=B_(N_m)`, and take the left regular endpoint of `A`, of
dimension `D_m=|A|`, in the square-free coordinates

```text
<x,r,t | x^2,(xr)^2,(xt)^3,rtr^(-1)=t^4,
         (xt^2r)^3,t^N>.                              (MCE2)
```

The reduction of `B` modulo `N_m` has a metacyclic quotient generated by the
unipotent and the fourth-power actor.  Concretely, the reduced actor has
order divisible by `m`, its `m`th power centralizes the unipotent because
`4^m=1 mod N_m`, and quotienting by that central power gives
`C_(N_m) semidirect C_m`.  Hence the restriction of the
regular representation to the identity `B`-coset contains a reducing copy
`P` of `c_m`.  On this one copy replace `c_m` by `b_m`, leaving `R_m`, every
other core summand, and the regular endpoint involution `X` unchanged.  Call
the resulting exact supplied core `(R,T')`, and write `(R,T)` for the original
regular core.

Then the first endpoint cubic has the exact energy identity

```text
||(XT')^3-1||_(2,D_m)^2
   = (3/D_m) ||S_m^(-1)-1||_F^2
   = (3/D_m) ||S_m-S_m^2||_F^2.                       (MCE3)
```

In particular, for absolute constants `0<c<C<infinity`,

```text
c/D_m <= E_end(X,(R,T')) <= C/D_m.                    (MCE4)
```

Thus the endpoint involution has genuine three-branch off-diagonal mass on
this substitution.  Its two-cubic energy is `Theta(1/D_m)`, not
`o(1/D_m)`.

## Orthogonal branching calculation

Let `a=xt in A`.  Reduction modulo `N_m` sends `B` into the upper-triangular
subgroup, whereas `a` has nonzero lower-left matrix entry.  Thus `a notin B`.
Since `a^3=1`, the three left cosets

```text
B,                    aB,                    a^2B      (MCE5)
```

are distinct.  Consequently `P`, `APA^*`, and `A^2P(A^*)^2` are pairwise
orthogonal for `A=lambda_A(a)`.

On `P`, the old root is `S_m^2` and the new root is `S_m`; hence

```text
T'=Tq,       q|_P=S_m^(-1),       q|_(P^perp)=1.       (MCE6)
```

Since `A^3=1`,

```text
(Aq)^3=(AqA^(-1))(A^2qA^(-2))q.                       (MCE7)
```

The three nonidentity corners in `(MCE7)` have the orthogonal supports in
`(MCE5)`.  The squared Frobenius norm of `(Aq)^3-1` is therefore exactly the
sum of the three corner norms, which proves `(MCE3)`.

Writing `zeta=exp(2 pi i/N_m)`, one has

```text
alpha_m:=||S_m^(-1)-1||_F^2
       =sum_(j=0)^(m-1) |zeta^(4^j)-1|^2.             (MCE8)
```

The last summand is at least `2`, because
`1/4 < 4^(m-1)/(4^m-1) <= 4/15`.  Also

```text
alpha_m
 <= (4 pi^2/N_m^2) sum_(j=0)^(m-1)16^j
 = (4 pi^2/15)(4^m+1)/(4^m-1)
 <= 68 pi^2/225.                                     (MCE9)
```

This gives the lower bound in `(MCE4)`.  For the upper bound, the involution
and `(XR)^2` row are unchanged.  The first cubic is bounded by `(MCE3)`, and
unitary word telescoping gives

```text
||(XT'^2R)^3-(XT^2R)^3||_F
 <=3||T'^2-T^2||_F
 <=6||T'-T||_F.                                      (MCE10)
```

The original endpoint is exact and
`||T'-T||_F^2=alpha_m`, so `(MCE9)--(MCE10)` give the upper bound in
`(MCE4)`.

## Consequence for the optimized-energy route

Choosing the original regular core as comparator gives a competitor of cost
`alpha_m/D_m`.  By `(MCE3)`, the first cubic alone is exactly three times
that competitor cost.  In particular the optimized infimum is at most one
third of the endpoint energy.  Therefore this minimal cross-root-multiplicity family supports,
rather than refutes, the desired gauge-optimized inequality at the correct
scale.  It does not prove the global inequality: a counterexample, if one
exists, must use a coupled substitution not confined to a single regular
`B`-coset packet, or arrange endpoint branching outside the three distinct
cosets in `(MCE5)`.
