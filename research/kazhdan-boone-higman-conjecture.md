**OPEN.** For every finitely generated group `G` with solvable word problem
there is a finitely presented simple group `T` with property (T) and an
embedding `G <= T`.

It implies `boone-higman-conjecture` trivially.

## Why the graph carries it

This is what the double-coset hole
`ck-envelope-simple-core-has-finite-bi-index` really asks. By
`bi-index-core-makes-normals-core-class-unions`, a Kazhdan
envelope with a normally generating simple core of finite bi-index has a
maximal quotient that is finitely presented, simple, Kazhdan and contains
the input. See `ck-finite-bi-index-hole-is-kazhdan-boone-higman` for the
two directions.

Finitely presented simple Kazhdan groups exist, for example among the
simple Kac--Moody lattices of Caprace--Rémy. This node records no embedding
theorem into them. Note that the hosts produced by the twisted
Brin--Thompson technology contain Thompson's group `V`. That does not
prevent property (T), because (T) does not pass to subgroups, but no
twisted Brin--Thompson group is known to have (T).

## Attempts

1. **Through the finite bi-index envelope.** *Reduces to itself.* A proof of
   `ck-envelope-simple-core-has-finite-bi-index` would give this conjecture,
   but by `ck-finite-bi-index-hole-is-kazhdan-boone-higman` that hole is
   equivalent to this conjecture when the core is free. So this is not an
   independent attack.
2. **Twisted Brin--Thompson hosts.** *Deferred.* They are the only known
   universal finitely presented simple hosts, and no one of them is known to
   have (T). No method for proving (T) for them is recorded.
3. **Embedding into Kac--Moody lattices.** *Untried.* There is no recorded
   embedding theorem for decidable groups into simple Kazhdan lattices.
