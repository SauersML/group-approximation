**ESTABLISHED.**  Let `R` be any algebra over `F_2` (more generally over a
finite field) and `n>=3`.  Let `D` be the diagram of all finite subgroups
of `St_n(R)` that are generated by root elements `x_ij(a)`, with the
inclusion maps.  Then the canonical map

```text
colim D  ->  St_n(R)                                                     (SFC1)
```

is an isomorphism: `St_n(R)` is presented by the multiplication tables of
its root-generated finite subgroups, glued along their intersections.

Consequently, for `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)` and a corona representation `Theta` of
`St_n(R)` with coordinate lifts, for every finite family `F_1,...,F_s` of
root-generated finite subgroups and every `epsilon>0`, all large
coordinates carry exact unitary representations `rho_i` of the `F_i`,
lifting `Theta`, that agree up to `epsilon` on every intersection
`F_i cap F_j`.  If `St_n(R)` is finitely presented (the graph records this
for the rank-twenty binary Leavitt Steinberg group), there is a finite
family `F_1,...,F_s` inside a ball of radius `r_0` such that `St_n(R)` is
already the colimit of that finite subdiagram, and then:

```text
exact agreement on all intersections  ==>  an exact finite-dimensional
representation of St_n(R)  ==>  the compression defect and the marked root
die (compression-defect-dies-in-finite-dimensions).                      (SFC2)
```

Hence the Property-`(T)`-free full MF radical of `St_n(R)` is equivalent
to the following statement about one explicit finite complex of finite
groups: **every family of exact representations of the `F_i` that agrees
up to `epsilon` on intersections can be conjugated, vertex by vertex, to a
family that agrees exactly, once `epsilon` is small** -- with a modulus
that may depend on the complex but not on the matrix dimension.  For a
tree of groups this is automatic (conjugate along the tree), which is why
virtually free groups and all one-relator-free sub-configurations are MF;
the obstruction lives in the two-cells of the root-system complex, i.e. in
the holonomy of compatibility unitaries around triangles of finite
subgroups, and the only known way to kill such holonomy is the spectral
gap of the finite links (Garland's method), which is how Property `(T)`
enters.

## What is finite here

The vertex groups are: the unipotent finite `2`-groups generated by finitely
many positive roots (arbitrary gauge degree); the degree-zero finite
simple groups `SL_(n*2^m)(F_2)`; and the mixed opposite-root finite
groups such as `<x_01(s_0),x_10(t_0)> ~= S_3` and their extensions by the
commuting complementary blocks.  Every Steinberg relator lies in one of
the first kind.  Two-cells arise exactly from Weyl-mixed relations between
nonzero-degree roots, matching
`leavitt-one-compressor-defect-lies-in-amenable-subgroups`.

No Property `(T)`, spectral gap, or literature input is used.
