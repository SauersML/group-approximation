Let `F` be a nonzero finite-dimensional C-star subalgebra of `C(X)`.  Its
unit is a projection in `C(X)`.  Connectedness makes every projection in
`C(X)` equal to zero or one, so `F` is unital.  A finite-dimensional
commutative C-star algebra is generated by its minimal projections, and
these again must be zero or one.  Hence `F=C1`.

Choose a nonconstant `f in C(X)` and `0!=b in B`.  Its distance from
`C1 tensor B` is positive after applying a norm-one functional on `B`
that is nonzero on `b`: the resulting scalar function is a nonzero
multiple of `f` and has positive distance from the constants.  Therefore
no directed union of subalgebras `F tensor B` with finite-dimensional
`F subset C(X)` is dense in `C(X) tensor B`.

This is the exact topological break in the zero-dimensional argument.
For a locally compact second-countable space, `C_0(X)` is AF exactly when
`X` is zero-dimensional.  Once a connected one-dimensional component is
present, clopen partitions cannot provide the finite-stage models, so a
different theorem must collapse the base and fibre colours.
