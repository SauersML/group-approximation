The finite inputs are exact: integer tables, exhaustive enumeration, the normal form in an
amalgamated product, and coset enumeration. Everything else is written out here.

## 0. Data

`aut.py` lists the four automata, with `g(xw) = out_g(x) · g|_x(w)`. `checkaut.log` confirms they are
the output of `selfrep.build`. In each automaton:
- three letters `D` have root permutation fixing a point or swapping two points, and sections in
  `D`;
- the fourth letter `τ` is `(01)(τ,τ,τ)` (letter 3, except letter 2 on 193H).

For an involution letter `s`, the sections `s|_x` and `s|_{s(x)}` coincide for every `x` (read off the
table). So `ss` has trivial sections, as a word.

## 1. The surjection π and agreement to length 12

**The relations of A hold in K.** `amnf.py` checks with `AG.trivial` that each defining relation of
`A` holds in `K`:
- the letters are involutions;
- the D-letters commute pairwise;
- `a_0a_1a_2 = 1` (332V), or `(a_0a_1a_2τ)^3 = 1` (the others; `(x_0x_1x_3x_2)^3` on 193H).

`AG.trivial` explores the finite set of sections of a word and is exact. So `π : A → K` exists, and it
is onto.

**Agreement.** `amalg.py` enumerates every cyclically reduced word of length `≤ 12` up to rotation and
reversal, and decides exactly which are trivial in `K`: 603 for 332V, 242 for the others.
`amnf.Amal.trivial` is the standard normal form in a free product with amalgamation (reduced sequences
of syllables from the two factors, with the amalgamated subgroup pushed into the neighbour), and it
shows that all of these words are trivial in `A` (`amnf.log`: "not trivial in A: 0").

Let `1 ≠ g ∈ ker π`. Take any word for `g` and cyclically reduce it freely. This conjugates `g` in `A`,
so the new word is still trivial in `K` and nontrivial in `A`. Hence it has length `≥ 13`.

**A_12 = A.** The defining relators of `A` are cyclically reduced of length `≤ 12`, so they appear in
the list. The list holds in `A`. Therefore the GAP group `A_12 = ⟨letters | list⟩` of `A12_nm.g` is `A`.

## 2. The recursion on A

For a word `w` and a letter `x`, the word `w|_x` is defined letter by letter as in `agrp.py`. Changing
`w` by an elementary move changes the sections as follows:
- inserting `ss`: by §0, `ss` has trivial sections;
- inserting a defining relator `r` of `A`: `r` fixes level 1, and `amnf.relsec` checks that each
  section of `r` and of its reverse is trivial in `A` (`amnf.log`, "all sections trivial in A: True").

In both cases the sections change only by elements trivial in `A`. Hence for `g ∈ A` and `x ∈ X`:
- `x^g` is well defined, since the level-1 action is that of `π(g)`;
- `g|_x ∈ A` is well defined;
- `(gh)|_x = g|_{h(x)} h|_x`.

So `φ_0^A : St_A(0) → A` is a homomorphism, where `St_A(0) = π^{-1}(St_K(0))`, and
`π ∘ φ_0^A = φ_0 ∘ π`.

For `G ≤ A` we have `π(G) ∩ St_K(0) = π(G ∩ St_A(0))`. By induction, the section chain of `K`,
`G^K_{k+1} = φ_0(St_{G^K_k}(0))` with `G^K_0 = K`, is `π` of the chain `G_k` computed in `A`.

## 3. The index-3 subgroup

`amnf.rho` defines `ρ` on letters, with values in `S_3` on points `{0,1,2}`:
- 332V: `a_0, a_2 ↦ (12)`, `a_1 ↦ 1`, `τ ↦ (01)`.
- The other three: `D[1] ↦ (12)`, the other two D-letters `↦ 1`, `τ ↦ (01)`.

`amnf.py` checks that `ρ` kills every defining relator of `A`, so `ρ` is a homomorphism on `A`. The
products `(12)(01)` have order 3, as `(τc)^3` requires.

**The generators of G_1.** `St_A(0)` is generated by the Schreier generators with respect to the
level-1 transversal, because the level-1 action of `A` is that of `K`. Their `0`-sections are the
words `G1w` in `A12_nm.g`, for example `0,1,2,303,323,31013,31213,31313` for 332H.

`amnf.py` evaluates `ρ` on each of them: they all fix `p` (`p = 0` for `#332`, `p = 2` for `#193`),
and `ρ(ℓ)` moves `p`. So `G_1^A := φ_0^A(St_A(0)) ≤ M = ρ^{-1}(Stab p)`, which has index 3 in `A`,
and `ℓ ∉ M`.

`lowidx.log` gives more: `LowIndexSubgroupsFpGroup(A, G_1, 9)` returns exactly `A` and `M`.

**ρ is not a congruence quotient (332H).** `fac332H.log` lists `|⟨(π_n(x), ρ(x)) : x letter⟩| / |K_n|`
for `n = 1..5`. Here `π_n` is the level-`n` action. The ratios are `6, 3, 3, 3, 3`, none equal to 1. So
no map `K_n → S_3` composes with the letters to give `ρ`, for `n ≤ 5`.

## 4. Consequences

**(a) Self-replication.**
- `G_1 = φ_0(St_K(0)) = π(G_1^A)` contains the three letters other than `ℓ`. So `K` is
  self-replicating at `0` iff `ℓ ∈ π(G_1^A)`.
- The level-1 action is transitive, and conjugation by an element moving `0` to `v` carries
  `φ_0(St(0))` to a conjugate of `φ_v(St(v))`. So the same holds at every level-1 vertex.
- Suppose `ker π ≤ ker ρ`. Then `ker ρ` is the core of `M`, so `π^{-1}(G_1) = G_1^A · ker π ≤ M`,
  and `ℓ ∉ π^{-1}(G_1)`.
- Hence an exact word `ℓ = u` with `u ∈ G_1`, verified by `trivial()`, produces a relation of `K` that
  is not killed by `ρ`. That relation is a nontrivial element of `ker π`, so by §1 some cyclically
  reduced word of length `≥ 13` is trivial in `K` but not in `A`.

**(b) Branching.**
- Suppose `N ≤ K` with `N ≠ 1` and `N × N × 1 ≤ ψ(N)`. Then `N` contains a copy of `N × N`.
- If `N` were finite, this would give `|N|^2 ≤ |N|`. So `N` is infinite.
- If `K ≅ A`, then `K` is virtually free, so the infinite subgroup `N` contains `Z`, and `N × N`
  contains `Z^2`. A virtually free group has no such subgroup. So `K ≇ A`, and §1 applies.

**(c) Chain closure.**
- The weak-cycle certificate (header of `kltc.py`) needs `(G^K_j)^g ≤ G^K_k` for some `j < k` and
  `g ∈ K`.
- `achain.g` computes `G_k` in `A` by coset enumeration and finds `[A : G_k] = 3^k` for `k ≤ 6` on
  332H and `k ≤ 5` on the others (`achain_*.log`). If `K ≅ A`, a closure with `k` in that range
  would give `3^j = [A : G_j] ≥ [A : G_k] = 3^k`, which is false.
- The same computation shows that every `G_k` is transitive on level 1. So the chain never stops for
  the trivial reason, and it is not closed by any inclusion `G_j ≤ G_k`.

**(d) The dichotomy.**
- `A` is infinite. If `K ≅ A`, then `K` is infinite, `H_x` is infinite, and `Γ` is irreducible
  (Burger–Mozes, Prop. 1.2, as in `bmw-44-sat-survivor-parity-kernels-not-left-orderable`). By
  (a)–(c), no exact self-replication, branching or chain-closure certificate exists in that case.
- Otherwise `ker π ≠ 1`, witnessed by a cyclically reduced relation of length `≥ 13`.
- Either way, a certificate of the kind asked for would first have to find such a relation. ∎
