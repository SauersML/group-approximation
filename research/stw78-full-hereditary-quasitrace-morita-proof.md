title: Use fullness and Morita invariance to transfer bounded quasitraces

Let `c` be full and positive in unital `B`, and put `H=closure(cBc)`.

If `tau` is a normalized `2`-quasitrace on `B`, then its restriction to `H`
is bounded.  It is nonzero: if it vanished on `H`, its trace-kernel ideal
would contain `c`, hence the ideal generated by `c`, which is all of `B`;
this would contradict `tau(1)=1`.

Conversely, let `sigma` be a nonzero bounded `2`-quasitrace on `H`.
Morita invariance of lower semicontinuous `2`-quasitraces extends `sigma` to
a nonzero lower semicontinuous `2`-quasitrace `sigma_tilde` on the ideal of
`B` generated by `H`, which is `B`.

This extension is finite at the unit.  Indeed, fullness of `c` and
compactness of `[1_B]` in `Cu(B)` give a positive contraction `h in H` and
an integer `m` such that

```text
[1_B] <= m[h].
```

Cuntz monotonicity of the associated dimension function and boundedness of
`sigma` give

```text
sigma_tilde(1_B)
 = d_(sigma_tilde)([1_B])
 <= m d_(sigma_tilde)([h])
 = m sup_n sigma(h^(1/n))
 <= m ||sigma|| < infinity.
```

The value is nonzero because `sigma_tilde` is nonzero on `H`.  Normalizing
`sigma_tilde` therefore gives a normalized `2`-quasitrace on `B`.  This
proves the equivalence.

Finally, if `a` is nonzero in the simple unital algebra `A`, then `a` is
full.  Applying `phi` to a finite approximate fullness relation for `a`
shows that `phi(a)` is full in `B`; unitality of `phi` is used here.  The
equivalence and quasitracelessness of `B` now exclude every nonzero bounded
quasitrace on `closure(phi(a)Bphi(a))`.
