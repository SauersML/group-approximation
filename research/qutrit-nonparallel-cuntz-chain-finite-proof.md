## The selector identities

Use the coefficient spaces

```text
S=span{s_0,s_1},           T=span{t_0,t_1},
P=span{s_rt_s:r,s in {0,1}}.
```

The Leavitt relations give

```text
TS=F_2,        t_ss_a=delta_(sa),
PS=S,          (s_rt_s)s_a=delta_(sa)s_r.              (QNC7)
```

The Steinberg commutator along the first two edges of the coordinate
chain is

```text
[U_r,V_s]=x_(ik)(s_rt_s)=Z_(rs).
```

A second commutator along `k->ell` and `(QNC7)` give

```text
[Z_(rs),W_a]
 =x_(i,ell)((s_rt_s)s_a)
 =delta_(sa)x_(i,ell)(s_r).                             (QNC8)
```

On the other hand

```text
[V_s,W_a]=x_(j,ell)(t_ss_a)
         =delta_(sa)x_(j,ell)(1),
```

and commuting that root with `U_r` gives the same right side of
`(QNC8)`. This proves `(QNC2)` and `(QNC3)` directly; no analytic
polar or completion is involved.

## The nilpotent coefficient algebra

Let `mathcal n` be the subspace of strictly upper-triangular matrices on
`(i,j,k,ell)` with entries constrained by

```text
(i,j):S,        (j,k):T,        (k,ell):S,
(i,k):P,        (j,ell):F_2,    (i,ell):S.              (QNC9)
```

It is an algebra. The only nonzero products of its displayed coefficient
spaces are precisely

```text
S T subset P,       T S=F_2,
P S subset S,       S F_2 subset S,                    (QNC10)
```

corresponding to concatenated directed paths. Every product of four
elements is zero, because there is no directed path of length four on
four coordinates. Thus `mathcal n` is nilpotent.

The dimensions in `(QNC9)` are

```text
2+2+2+4+1+2=13.
```

The six root spaces are all generated by `U_r,V_s,W_a` and their
commutators: `P` comes from `[U_r,V_s]`, the scalar `(j,ell)` root
comes from `[V_s,W_s]`, and the final `S` root comes from `(QNC8)`.
Standard ordered root collection in a unitriangular group therefore gives

```text
M=<U_r,V_s,W_a>=1+mathcal n.                            (QNC11)
```

The map `x |->1+x` is a bijection from the `13`-dimensional vector
space `mathcal n` onto this algebra group. Hence

```text
|M|=2^13=8192.
```

## Scalar intersection and qutrit types

If `1+x in M` is a scalar matrix, the entries in the four spaces
`S,T,S,S` of `(QNC9)` must vanish. The `(i,k)` coefficient in `P`
can then be scalar only as `0` or `1=s_0t_0+s_1t_1`. The
`(j,ell)` coefficient is already in `F_2`. Thus

```text
M intersect K=<A,B>,
A=x_(ik)(1),             B=x_(j,ell)(1),                (QNC12)
```

an elementary four-group.

Choose `j` and `ell` as corresponding basis coordinates in two copies
of the same nontrivial `C`-module. Then `B` is a multiplicity
transvection and commutes with `C`. The root `A` acts between two
trivial `C` coordinates and also commutes with `C`. All of `M`
centralizes `h` because every active coordinate is in its fixed block.

A root between a trivial and a nontrivial coordinate does not normalize
`C`: conjugating `J` creates a nonzero homogeneous cross-block
coefficient. Its intersection with its conjugate of `C` is therefore
exactly `<h>`. This applies to `U_r,V_s,W_a` and to the selected output
`x_(i,ell)(s_r)`. Consequently every one of those roots has compatibility

```text
2alpha'+beta'=2alpha+beta
```

on joint `C` characters, with squared compatible-block norm `1/27`.

## Finite-amalgam realization

Let

```text
H=<h> times M,             E=<h,A,B>.
```

Equations `(QNC12)` and the scalar module decomposition give

```text
H intersect K=E,             C intersect E=<h>.
```

There is a canonical surjection

```text
K *_E H -> <K,M>=EL_28(L).                               (QNC13)
```

The equality on the right holds because `K`-conjugates of the first two
edge families already place `s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1` in every root position.
The group on the left has two finite vertices and a finite edge. It is
virtually free and residually finite. Its canonical trace restricts to
the regular trace on `C` and realizes all selector identities
`(QNC7)--(QNC8)` exactly, while every character atom has weight `1/9`.
Thus the coefficient deletion does not produce a matrix defect.

## Why every acyclic finite continuation has the same fate

More generally, fix finitely many elementary roots whose coordinate
incidence graph admits an acyclic ordering. Conjugate them into strictly
upper-triangular positions. In a matrix product, a coefficient can then
be a product of at most `n-1` of the finitely many original coefficients,
where `n` is the number of coordinates. There are only finitely many
such words, so their `F_2`-span is finite dimensional. The generated
unitriangular group is contained in `1+mathcal a` for a finite-dimensional
nilpotent `F_2`-algebra `mathcal a`, and is finite.

Therefore no longer acyclic Cuntz chain can escape by depth alone. A
directed coordinate cycle or opposite root is necessary for unbounded
coefficient iteration. This proves the stated finite selector firewall.
