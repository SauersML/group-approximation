- *Rigorous part:* Parts 1–2, which are elementary.
- *Sketch:* the conditional theorem in Part 3 has a proof sketch whose hierarchical step is not

No finitely presented host over `Z²` is claimed.

## 1. What remains after exact gluing and sliding

Let `F = F(G_V × (Z² ⋉ Ω))` for the crossing-wire shift `Ω`.
- **Exact gluing.** `v-times-gluing-presentations-are-exact` (referee PASS, bd5481ba2) presents
  `F` as `M *_{V×Λ} N_Ω` modulo finitely many relators, with `M` finitely presented.
- **Sliding.** `v-cone-sliding-localizes-disjoint-support-relations` shows that every commutation
  between elements with disjoint V-supports follows from bounded relators.

So the only relations that may fail to follow from bounded ones are the **same-support far
conjunctions**. For `v, w ∈ V` with overlapping supports and sites `p, q` far apart:

  `[v^[a@p], w^[b@q]] = [v,w]^[a@p ∧ b@q]`.

By `one-scale-filling-decides-fp-of-v-times-subshift-hosts`, `F` is finitely presented iff these
identities follow from the relations of one bounded radius.

## 2. The step with no group analog

The linear proof (`crossing-wire-fixed-point-tile-sets-are-quantum-rigid`) has three steps:
- crossing tiles make two far bits commute;
- determinism makes every edge a Boolean function of the input bits;
- commuting idempotents then commute with every Boolean function of them.

The last step is associativity: `E_P E_{c'} E_c = E_P (E_{c'} E_c) = 0` when `c ≠ c'` at one site.

In a group the conjunction of conditioned subgroups is a commutator subgroup,
`H_{P∧Q} = [H_P, H_Q]`, and commutators are not associative. Take `x ∈ H_P`, `y ∈ H_{c'@r}` and
`z ∈ H_{c@r}` with `c ≠ c'`, so `[y, z] = 1` locally. Hall–Witt,

  `[[x,y⁻¹],z]^y · [[y,z⁻¹],x]^z · [[z,x⁻¹],y]^x = 1`,

leaves only

  `[H_{P∧c'@r}, H_{c@r}] = 1  ⟺  [H_{P∧c@r}, H_{c'@r}] = 1`.

So the two triple relations are equivalent to each other. No derivation of either from pairwise
data is known; in the linear case the analogue is immediate. In the linear case they hold for free. In the group case each needs its own local
certificate, involving three conditions at once. Pairwise crossings (L3) give only two co-located
bits. This is the precise sense in which "group rigidity = ring rigidity + something": the
something is 3-way joint structure.

## 3. Conditional theorem

**(a) Triple crossings (a design change).**
- Every triple of input bits of a macrotile is co-located in one tile. For example, bundle two
  wires on the horizontal edges and cross a third vertically.
- The edge alphabet grows only by a constant factor. The `O(k³)` crossings still fit in
  `poly(log N)` geometry, so the Durand–Romashchenko–Shen fixed-point argument is unchanged.
- The linear crossing-wire proof applies verbatim, since triple crossing implies pairwise.

**(b) 3-locality of Boolean powers (conjecture; a Curtis–Tits statement for `V`).**
- Let `B` be a finite Boolean algebra generated by subalgebras `B_1, …, B_m`, with `m ≥ 3`.
- Conjecture: `V[B] = LC(Stone(B), V)` is the colimit of the groups `V[B_S]`, over the subsets
  `S ⊆ {1, …, m}` with `|S| ≤ 3` and `B_S = ⋁_{i∈S} B_i`.
- Group presentations are 2-dimensional, so the question is whether the 4- and higher-way joins
  add relations. The linear analogue (commuting idempotents) is 2-local.

**Claim (sketch).** Under (a) and (b), `F` is finitely presented.
- *One level.* Use (b) inside each macrotile, and (a) for its input triples. Every relation among
  level-1 macrotile-conditioned subgroups at level-1 distance at most 1 then follows from
  tile-level relations of radius at most `2N`. Here `N` is the zoom.
  - Via the self-similarity `𝒢 ≅ 𝒢 × Pair(N²)` (unique macrotile decomposition), this says:
    `ψ(R) ⊆ ⟨⟨R⟩⟩`, where `R` is all relations of radius at most `R_* ≥ 2N + c` and `ψ` lifts
    the desubstitution.
- *All levels.* By (b), every relation is a consequence of relations among at most three sites.
  Three sites at mutual distance `d` lie in adjacent level-`K` macrotiles once `N^K > d`. Their
  relation is then `ψ^K` of a bounded relation, modulo `ψ^j`-images of the finitely many
  "tile = union of level-1 conditions" relations. Iterating the one-level closure puts
  everything in `⟨⟨R⟩⟩`.
- So `F = ⟨S | R⟩` is finitely presented. P2′b exactness then gives nothing more to check.

**What each hypothesis buys.**
- Without (a), the triple relations of Part 2 are not co-located at any tile, and the one-level
  step fails exactly as Part 2 describes.
- Without (b), 4-way and higher joint structure would need co-location of every `m`-tuple of
  bits. A fixed-point tile set cannot provide that, since its alphabet is bounded by the
  self-simulation.

## 4. Finite certificate

Given (a), hypothesis (b) is needed only for the finitely many Boolean algebras generated by one
macrotile's input sites and their neighbours. So:

> For a fixed triple-crossing tile set, it suffices to check (b) for finitely many finite `B`.

That is a finite, if large, computation: coset enumeration in finitely presented groups built
from `V`-powers. It is the group-side counterpart of the scale-≤5 rigidity certificates. It is
not run here.

## Lesson for general BH

The whole gap between the ring side (rigidity ⇒ finite presentation, proved) and the group side
(open) is **higher joint structure**. Commuting idempotents form a Boolean algebra as soon as they
commute pairwise, because multiplication is associative. Conditioned subgroups of `V` combine by
commutators, which are not associative, so pairwise co-location leaves the triple relations
undetermined. Hall–Witt shows that they come only in equivalent pairs.

The group-side analogue of the crossing-wire design therefore needs:
- **triple co-location**, which is cheap in the tile set;
- a **Curtis–Tits theorem for Boolean powers of V**, stating that they are presented by their
  triple joins.

That theorem is a clean, host-independent group-theory target. If it holds, it converts every
pairwise-rigidity proof built by co-location into a finite-presentation proof, including over
`Z²`.
