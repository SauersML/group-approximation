Only textbook facts about `Λ_n(R)` and `Sp_{2n}(Z)` are used. No priority is claimed.

## Setting

- `(R^{2n}, ω)` is standard symplectic space, `Λ = Λ_n(R)` its Lagrangian Grassmannian,
  `Γ = Sp_{2n}(Z)`, and `n ≥ 1`.
- **Frames.** A *unimodular frame* is a symplectic basis `(e, f)` of `Z^{2n}`, that is,
  `ω(e_i, f_j) = δ_ij` and `ω(e_i, e_j) = ω(f_i, f_j) = 0`.
  - Frames are the column systems of elements of `Γ`, so `Γ` acts simply transitively on
    them.
  - Write `L_e = span(e)` and `L_f = span(f)`.
- **Maslov cycles.** `Σ(M) = {L : L ∩ M ≠ 0}`.
- **Chart.** `φ_{e,f}(S) = span{e_i + Σ_j S_ij f_j}`, for `S ∈ Sym_n(R)`, is a diffeomorphism
  onto `Λ ∖ Σ(L_f)`. Moreover `φ_{e,f}(S) ∈ Σ(L_e)` iff `det S = 0`.
- **Cells.** `C_k(e,f) = φ_{e,f}{S : det S ≠ 0, S has exactly k negative eigenvalues}`,
  for `k = 0, …, n`.
  - Each cell is open and connected.
  - The `n+1` cells of one frame partition `Λ ∖ (Σ(L_e) ∪ Σ(L_f))`.
  - `C_k(e,f)` is the set of `L` transverse to both `L_e` and `L_f` whose Maslov
    triple index against `(L_e, L_f)` takes a fixed value.
- **Generic part.** `Λ^0` is `Λ` minus all rational Maslov cycles `Σ(M)`,
  `M ∈ Λ_n(Q)`. It is comeager, hence dense.
  - Every rational Lagrangian is `L_e` for some frame, since `Γ` is transitive on
    primitive Lagrangian sublattices (textbook).
  - So `Λ^0` is `Λ` minus all `Σ(L_e) ∪ Σ(L_f)`, over all frames.
- **The space.** `𝔅_u` is the Boolean algebra of subsets of `Λ^0` generated by all sets
  `C_k(e,f) ∩ Λ^0`, and `Y_u` is its Stone space of ultrafilters.
  - `γ C_k(e,f) = C_k(γe, γf)`, so `Γ` acts on `𝔅_u`, and hence on `Y_u`.
  - `[A] ⊆ Y_u` denotes the clopen set of `A ∈ 𝔅_u`.
- **The case n = 1.** Frames are oriented Farey edges, and the two cells are the two
  arcs cut out by the edge. So `Y_u` is the Farey model of the Cantor set on which
  piecewise-`PSL_2(Z)` maps form Thompson's `V` (textbook).

## Statement

1. **Cantor model.**
   - `Y_u` is a Cantor set.
   - For every `y ∈ Y_u`, the intersection `∩_{B ∈ y} cl_Λ(B)` is a single point
     `π(y)`.
   - `π : Y_u → Λ` is a continuous, `Γ`-equivariant surjection, and it is injective over
     `Λ^0`.
2. **One frame separates.** For `L ≠ L'` in `Λ` there is a frame with `L ∈ C_k(e,f)` and
   `L' ∈ C_{k'}(e,f)`, where `k ≠ k'`.
3. **Expansive.** The partition of `Y_u` into the clopen sets `[C_k(e_0,f_0)]` of the
   standard frame has `Γ`-translates that separate points. So the action is conjugate
   to a subshift of `{0,…,n}^Γ`.
4. **Local contraction.**
   - Fix `y ∈ Y_u`, a nonempty clopen set `[A]` and a finite-index subgroup `Δ ≤ Γ`.
   - There are a clopen neighborhood `N` of `y` and a unipotent `u ∈ Δ` with
     `u N ⊆ [A]`.
5. **Minimal.** `Γ`, and every finite-index subgroup of `Γ`, acts minimally on `Y_u`.
6. **Topologically free.**
   - The kernel of the action is `{±I}`.
   - Every `γ ∉ {±I}` has a nowhere dense fixed set in `Y_u`.
7. **Decidable.**
   - Equality in `𝔅_u` of two Boolean words in cells is decidable. Cells are given by
     integer frame matrices and indices `k`.
   - The action of a given `γ` on words is computable.
8. **Scope of the proofs.**
   - Items 4–6 use only three facts: each element of the clopen algebra has the form
     `W ∩ Λ^0` with `W` open in `Λ`; the algebra separates points of `Λ`; and `Γ`
     preserves `Λ^0`.
   - So items 4–6 hold verbatim for the finer Cantorization `Y_Λ` of
     `lagrangian-thompson-group-contains-deligne-lattice`.
   - Item 3 is specific to `Y_u`. Expansivity of `Y_Λ` is not known here.

## Consequences

- **Faithful version for even n.** `−I` acts trivially on `Y_u`. Put
  `F = F_3^{2n} ∖ {0}`, with `Γ` acting by reduction mod 3.
  - `Γ` is transitive on `F`, since it is transitive on primitive vectors of `Z^{2n}`.
  - `−I` has no fixed point on `F`.
  - Items 4 and 5, applied to the stabilizers `Γ_v ⊇ Γ(3)`, make `Γ ↷ Y_u × F` minimal.
  - The action on `Y_u × F` is faithful, expansive and topologically free.
- **Hypotheses supplied.** These properties are what
  `matui-purely-infinite-minimal-full-groups-have-simple-d` needs, together with a
  purely infinite factor. They are also what Theorems 4.1 and 5.6 of
  `nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups` need. The consumer is
  `deligne-lattice-in-fg-simple-lagrangian-brin-thompson-group`.
