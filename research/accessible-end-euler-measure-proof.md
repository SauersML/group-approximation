**Audit note (2026-09-17).** Referee lenses 1 and 2 refuted step 2
(coarse connectivity) when some vertex group is infinite. Steps 2(a) and 2(b)
assume that the Bass-Serre tree is locally finite. Step 3 is therefore
unsupported, and the route now takes step 3 as the open prerequisite
`accessible-half-tree-pieces-generate-end-clopen-algebra`. Steps 0-2 below are
kept as the attempt. The referees found steps 4-6 and the one-ended case sound.

Standard facts used (not re-proved here):

- **(F1)** Dunwoody: finitely presented groups are accessible.
- **(F2)** Bass–Serre theory. `P` acts on the tree `T` of the graph of
  groups, with finite edge stabilizers and finitely many orbits of vertices
  and edges. The vertex groups are finitely generated, and each vertex has
  finitely many `G_v`-orbits of incident edges. One may reduce the graph of
  groups so that no non-loop edge has `G_e = G_v` at an endpoint. Then `T`
  has no vertex of valence one, and every half-tree is infinite.
- **(F3)** A two-ended group is virtually infinite cyclic. An infinite
  virtually free group has `chi = 0` iff it is virtually Z, by
  multiplicativity of `chi` under finite index and `chi(F_r) = 1 - r`.

The one-ended case is immediate, because the clopen algebra consists of
finite and cofinite sets. A non-virtually-cyclic accessible group with more
than one end has infinitely many ends (F3), so assume that from now on.

**0. Set-up.**
- Let `P` act on `T` on the right. Subdivide one edge orbit and take `v0`
  to be a midpoint, so that its stabilizer `G0` is finite. The subdivided
  tree still has no leaves.
- Put `pi(y) = v0.y`, and write `pi(yd) = pi(y).d`. The fibres of `pi` have
  `|G0|` elements.
- For an oriented edge `e`, let `T_e` be the half-tree it points into, and
  put `P_e = pi^-1(T_e)`.
- For a vertex `v`, put `P_v = pi^-1(v)`. This has `|G0|` points on the
  orbit of `v0` and is empty otherwise.
- Right multiplication by `d` maps `P_e` to `P_(e.d)`.

**1. `P_e` has finite coboundary.**
- The distance `d_T(v0, v0.g)` is at most `L0` for `g` in `S`.
- If the edge `{y, gy}` crosses the coboundary, then `e` lies on
  `[v0, v0.g].y`, so `e.y^-1` lies in a finite set of edges. The set of such
  `y` is a finite union of cosets of the finite edge stabilizer.
- Hence the closure of `P_e` is clopen. Its complement is `P_(ebar)`.

**2. Coarse connectivity.** There is a constant `L` such that:

- **(a)** every `P_e` is `L`-chain connected in `Gamma`. Consecutive points
  are joined by paths of length at most `L`.
- **(b)** for every vertex `v` with infinite `G_v`, the set
  `N_v = pi^-1(B_T(v, R1))` is `L`-chain connected and coarsely one-ended.
  That is, for each finite set `B`, all but finitely many points of `N_v`
  lie in one `L`-chain component of `N_v` minus `B_L(B)`.

For (a):
- Every vertex is within `R0` of the orbit `v0.P`, and `T_e` is infinite, so
  `T_e` meets that orbit.
- Fibres over the vertices of `v0.P` inside `T_e` are joined as follows.
  Base vertices at tree distance at most `2R0+1` differ by one of finitely
  many `G_(v0)`-orbit types. So some point of one fibre is within bounded
  word distance of a point of the other: choose a short representative
  `g_i` with `pi(g_i y)` equal to the target.

For (b):
- `N_v` is invariant under right multiplication by `G_v`, with finitely
  many orbits `y_i G_v`.
- Each `y_i G_v = (y_i G_v y_i^-1) y_i` is a coset of a finitely generated
  one-ended subgroup, so it is coarsely connected and coarsely one-ended.
- Different orbits are at distance at most `|y_j y_i^-1|`, because
  `d(y_i h, y_j h) = |y_j y_i^-1|`.

Choose `R1` so that every branch `T_e` at `v` meets `v0.P` within `R1` of
`v`. There are finitely many `G_v`-orbits of branches, and each branch is
infinite.

**3. Every clopen set lies in the algebra `A` generated by the `P_e` and the
finite sets.**
- Let `U` have finite coboundary with endpoint set `D`, and let `K` be the
  finite subtree spanned by `pi(B_L(D))`.
- If an edge `e` leaves `K`, then `P_e` is at distance more than `L` from
  `D`. By 2(a), `P_e` lies in `U` or is disjoint from it.
- For each infinite vertex `v` of `K`, 2(b) gives a side (in `U` or not)
  containing all but finitely many points of `N_v`.
- Each branch `e` at `v` that leaves `K` contains a point of `N_v`, and
  distinct branches contain distinct points. So all but finitely many such
  branches follow the side of `N_v`.

Collect the finitely many exceptional branches in a set `X`, and put

    Omega_v^(K,X) = P minus union of P_e over edges e at v that lie in K or in X, oriented away from v.

- This is the union of `P_v` and the non-exceptional branches.
- Then `U` agrees, up to a finite set, with a union of pieces of three
  kinds: points of `P_w` for `w` in `K`; sets `P_e` for `e` leaving `K` at a
  finite vertex, or `e` in `X`; and sets `Omega_v^(K,X)`.
- Every piece of these kinds is infinite except the points.

**4. Definition and consistency.**
- Unknowns are `T` and `x_e` for oriented edge orbits, with
  `x_e + x_ebar = T`.
- Put `mu(point) = 1`, `mu(P_e) = x_e`, and
  `mu(Omega_v^(K,X)) = T - sum of x_e` over its removed branches.
- Refining `(K, X)` by making a branch exceptional, or by adding a leaf `w`
  through `e`, preserves the sum.
  - If `w` is one-ended, this needs only `x_e = T - x_ebar`.
  - If `w` is finite, it needs the **vertex equation**
    `T = n_w + sum over edges f at w of x_f`, with `n_w = |P_w|`.
- Any two partitions have a common refinement by such moves.
- Two unions of pieces that differ by a finite set differ, in the common
  refinement, only by point pieces, because all other pieces are infinite.
- So `mu` is well defined and finitely additive on `A`.
- It is right-invariant, because right multiplication permutes pieces of
  the same types.
- By step 3 the clopen algebra of `Pbar` is `A`, taken modulo the closure
  operation.

**5. Solvability.**
- Write `x_e = T/2 + z_e` with `z` antisymmetric (a flow on the quotient
  graph `Y`). Dividing the equation at a finite vertex `v` by `|G_v|` gives

      sum over oriented edges e of Y out of v of z_e/|G_e| = T*kappa_v - [v = v0],
      kappa_v = 1/|G_v| - sum over edges of Y at v of 1/(2|G_e|)   (a loop counted twice).

- If `Y` has a vertex with an infinite group, there is no equation there.
  The weighted divergence can then be prescribed arbitrarily at all finite
  vertices, by routing flow to that vertex along a spanning tree. So a
  solution exists for every `T`.
- If all vertex groups are finite, the divergences must sum to zero:
  `T*chi(P) - 1 = 0`, since `sum kappa_v = chi(P)` for the subdivided graph,
  which has the same `chi`. This is solvable iff `chi(P) != 0`, that is iff
  `P` is not virtually cyclic (F3). Then `T = 1/chi(P)`.

**6. The total in the virtually free case is forced.**
- Any end Euler measure satisfies the vertex equations. The partition at a
  finite vertex is a finite disjoint union, and sets `P_e` in one edge
  orbit are right translates of each other.
- Summing as in step 5 gives `mu(Pbar) * chi(P) = 1`.
- No uniqueness of `mu` beyond its total is claimed.

**Computation.** `experiments/window-zero-enumerations-2026-09-17/end_measure_check.py`
solves the equations exactly for eleven graphs of groups. It finds them
unsolvable for `Z`, `D_inf`, `Z x Z/2` and `Z/4 *_(Z/2) Z/4`, and solvable for
`F_2`, `Z/2*Z/3`, `(Z/2)^(*3)`, `Z/4 *_(Z/2) Z/6`, `Z^2*Z`, `Z^2*Z/2` and
`Z^2*Z^2`.
