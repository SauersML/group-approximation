# Nest twice through spare indices and let the dead bracket propagate

Fix a root `(i,j)` and monomials `u, v`; choose `k ∉ {i,j}` and
`l ∉ {i,j,k}` — possible exactly when `n ≥ 4`.  The Steinberg relation
gives `x_kj(ρv) = [x_kl(ρ), x_lj(v)]`, and `x_kl(ρ) = 1` by hypothesis,
so `x_kj(ρv) = 1`.  Then `x_ij(uρv) = [x_ik(u), x_kj(ρv)] = [x_ik(u), 1]
= 1`.  For a ring relation given as a sum of monomials rather than a
single monomial, additivity of the root subgroups splits `x_ij` over the
sum first, and the argument applies to each monomial context separately;
degenerate cases `u = 1` or `v = 1` need only the single nesting.  ∎

The two-line brevity is the point: what looked like infinitely many
imposed relations (`x_ij(uρv) = 1` over all contexts) is generated by
finitely many, at the price of two spare indices — and that price is the
[KrM] rank threshold, verbatim.
