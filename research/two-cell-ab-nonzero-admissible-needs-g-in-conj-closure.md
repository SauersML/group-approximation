## Setting

The setting is that of `two-cell-core-deletions-need-a-unit-fox-coefficient`:
- `G = ⟨S | w⟩`, `H ≤ G` and a two-cell core `C ⊂ X_H`;
- the basis `B` of `π_1(C^1)`, with `g = t_1 t_0^{-1} ∉ H` and `ε = ε_0 ε_1`;
- the cylinder row `c_x = ε g ∂_x u_0 - ∂_x u_1`.

Write `α = [u_0]` and `β = [u_1]` in `Z^B`. Put

`P = ⟨ g^k H g^{-k} : k ∈ Z ⟩`,

the smallest subgroup that contains `H` and is normalized by `g`.

## Statement

**(N1) General criterion.** Let `Q = ⟨g⟩ / (⟨g⟩ ∩ P)`, a cyclic group generated by `ḡ`. If `c` is
unimodular over `ZG`, then the binomials `ε α_x ḡ - β_x` (`x ∈ B`) generate the unit ideal of `ZQ`.
This holds for either side convention of unimodularity.

**(N2) Degree-one normal form.** Suppose `α = a p` and `β = b p` with `p` primitive and `ab ≠ 0`. This
is the (E5) normal form of a degree-one admissible base, with `ab ≠ 0`. If `c` is unimodular (that
is, if `pd_{ZG} I_{G/H} ≤ 1`), then `g ∈ P`.

Consequences:
- **Normal closure.** `g ∈ ⟨⟨H⟩⟩_G`. So every homomorphism `ψ: G → K` with `ψ(H) = 1` has
  `ψ(g) = 1`.
- **Normalizer.** `g ∉ N_G(H)`, since otherwise `P = H ∌ g`. More generally, `g` normalizes no
  subgroup `P' ⊇ H` with `g ∉ P'`.
- **Quotients.** For every quotient `π: G → Q'`, `π(g) ∈ ⟨π(g)^k π(H) π(g)^{-k}⟩`. In particular
  `[g]` lies in the image of `H_1(H) → H_1(G)`.

**(N3) Representation test.** If `c` is unimodular, then for every field `k` and every
representation `ρ: G → GL_n(k)`, the block row `(ρ(c_x))_{x∈B}` of size `n × rn` has rank `n`.

For a character `χ: G → k^*` this says `c^χ ≠ 0`. Equivalently, `e_1 - εχ(g) e_0` is not a
`χ`-twisted 2-cycle of `C`.

## Scope

(N2) turns the open step ("every two-cell core of a degree-one admissible base has `ab = 0`") into a
question about the position of `g`. An `ab ≠ 0` counterexample needs all of the following:
1. `g ∈ P`, while `g ∉ H` by (U1);
2. no character or finite-dimensional representation killing `c`, by (N3);
3. unimodularity of `c` itself.

The core of `degree-one-two-cell-cores-with-ab-nonzero-exist` passes the abelian shadow of (1) but
fails (2). So do all 132 fiber-product configurations of `finchar.py 3`: each fails (2) through an
`F_3`-character.
