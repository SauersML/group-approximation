The polar-gauge proof has error parameter
`Delta=tau(p)-tau(R^*R)` and becomes informative only when `Delta` is small.
The atlas capacity and spectral-corner estimates bound `tau(R^*R)` from
below but provide no upper bound on `Delta`.  The dilution construction
realizes exactly this logical gap with arbitrary commutant gauge.

The exact common quarter projection improves range identification, not the
cross-Gram source deficit or the fivefold reduced-block labeling.  Hence none
of these established overlap statements supplies the hypothesis missing from
the polar lemma, and the extension-authentication conclusion does not follow.
