**ESTABLISHED, and kernel-checked.**  Let `A` be a unital ring generated as a
ring by a finite set `X`, and let `n >= 3`.  Then

```text
EL_n(A) = < e_ij(y) : i != j, y in X union {1} >,                     (EFG1)
```

so `EL_n(A)` is finitely generated, by at most `n(n-1)(|X|+1)` elementary
matrices.

The engine is that the coefficient set

```text
C = { a in A : e_ij(a) in H for every i != j }                        (EFG2)
```

is a subring of `A` for any subgroup `H` containing every `e_ij(1)`: it is
closed under addition because `e_ij(a)e_ij(b) = e_ij(a+b)` lives inside one
root subgroup, and closed under multiplication because for a third index `k`

```text
[e_ij(a), e_jk(b)] = e_ik(ab),                                        (EFG3)
```

which needs `n >= 3` to supply that third index.  Taking `H` to be the
subgroup generated by the right-hand side of `(EFG1)` gives `X subset C`,
hence `C = A`, hence `H = EL_n(A)`.

In the development this is `elementaryGroup_finitelyGenerated` for a
finite-type `ZMod 2`-algebra and `elementaryGroup_finitelyGenerated_finiteField`
for a finite-type algebra over any finite coefficient field, both in
`GroupApproximation/Leavitt/ElementaryGroup.lean`, with
`elementaryCoefficientSubalgebra` as the subalgebra of `(EFG2)`.

## Why it is worth a node

The printed manuscript obtains finite generation of `H = EL_12(L_(F_2)(1,2))`
as a by-product of the Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain property-(T) theorem for
elementary groups over finitely generated rings -- the same citation that
supplies the spectral gap.  `(EFG1)` removes that dependency outright: finite
generation of every elementary group in this program costs one subring
argument and no rigidity theorem at all.

This matters for the property-(T)-free lane, where the finite presentation is
the object being spent.  Every collapse theorem in that lane -- the
operator-norm support row of `authenticated-coarse-fine-return-forces-mf-collapse`
and the atomic coefficient return of
`atomic-morita-return-kills-finite-order-mf-mark` -- needs the relator defect
of a **finite** presentation to tend to zero, and neither may quietly recover
finiteness of the generating set from the theorem it is trying to delete.

`(EFG1)` is generation only.  It gives no finite presentation, and nothing
here bears on the finite presentability questions in the Hilbert-hotel cover
lane.
