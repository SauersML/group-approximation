Citation import, secondary. Read on 2026-09-13 from Grigorchuk, *Milnor's problem on the growth
of groups and its consequences*, arXiv:1111.0512, TeX `Grigorchuk10.tex` on MSI.

l.866–868: "A useful fact about groups of intermediate growth is due to S.~Rosset
\cite{rosset:76}. \begin{thm} If $G$ is a finitely generated group which does not grow
exponentially and $H$ is a normal subgroup such that $G/H$ is solvable, then $H$ is finitely
generated. \end{thm}"

l.884–886: "\begin{thm} Let $G$ be a finitely generated group with no free subsemigroups on two
generators and let the quotient $G/N$ be an elementary amenable group. Then the kernel $N$ is a
finitely generated group. \end{thm}"

l.888–890: "The proof of this fact is based on the use of a version of Milnor's lemma (Lem.~1
from \cite{rhemtulla_long:freesemig95}) and transfinite induction on the ``complexity'' of
elementary amenable groups".

Bibliography: [Ros76] S. Rosset, *A property of groups of non-exponential growth*, Proc. Amer.
Math. Soc. 54 (1976), 24–26; [LMR95] P. Longobardi, M. Maj, A. H. Rhemtulla, *Groups with no free
subsemigroups*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 347 (1995). The primary papers were not re-read.

The consequence in the claim: subexponential growth excludes free subsemigroups (free generators
of length at most `c` put `2^n` elements in the ball of radius `c n`), `Z` is solvable, and a
normal subgroup with infinite cyclic quotient is complemented by any lift of a generator.
