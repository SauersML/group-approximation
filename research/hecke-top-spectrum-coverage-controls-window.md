Use the regular finite-core notation

\[
D=(\lambda_G(B))',\qquad X_0=\lambda_G(x),\qquad X=X^*=X^{-1},
\]

and let \(E_D\) be the trace-preserving expectation.  Define

\[
T_X:L^2(D)\to L^2(D),\qquad T_X(A)=E_D(X_0AX).          \tag{HTS1}
\]

Then \(T_X\) is a selfadjoint contraction and its top eigenspace is exactly

\[
\ker(1-T_X)
 =\{A\in D:X_0A=AX\}.                                  \tag{HTS2}
\]

Moreover the following are equivalent.

1. \(X\) belongs to the relative Hecke--Kraus hull
   \({\cal K}_{G,B}(X_0)\).
2. There are \(A_1,\ldots,A_q\in\ker(1-T_X)\) such that

   \[
   \sum_jA_j^*A_j=1.                                   \tag{HTS3}
   \]

Equivalently, the right-support ideal generated by the top eigenspace is
all of \(D\).  Thus exact relative-window membership is a top-singular-space
**coverage** condition, not merely the assertion
\(\lambda_{\max}(T_X)=1\).

There is a quantitative version.  Let

\[
{\cal H}_\epsilon
 =1_{[1-\epsilon,1]}(T_X)L^2(D).                       \tag{HTS4}
\]

If there are \(A_1,\ldots,A_q\in{\cal H}_\epsilon\) satisfying (HTS3),
then

\[
\Delta^{\rm reg}_{G,B}(X)\leq\sqrt{2\epsilon}.          \tag{HTS5}
\]

To prove (HTS2), note that left and right multiplication by the involutions
\(X_0,X\) are isometries on Hilbert--Schmidt space and \(E_D\) is the
orthogonal projection onto \(D\).  If \(T_XA=A\), equality of norms in that
projection forces

\[
X_0AX=A,
\]

which is equivalent to \(X_0A=AX\).  The converse is immediate.

For exact membership, a frame of intertwiners gives

\[
\sum_jA_j^*X_0A_j
 =\sum_jA_j^*A_jX=X.
\]

Conversely, if a Kraus frame has
\(\sum_jA_j^*X_0A_j=X\), the associated ucp map sends the unitary \(X_0\)
to the unitary \(X\).  The multiplicative-domain equality in its
Stinespring column gives \(X_0A_j=A_jX\) for every \(j\).  If the top
eigenspace merely has full right support, choose finitely many elements
whose \(P=\sum_jA_j^*A_j\) is invertible.  Each \(A_j^*A_j\), hence \(P\),
commutes with \(X\); replacing \(A_j\) by \(A_jP^{-1/2}\) produces (HTS3).

For (HTS5), put \(E_j=X_0A_j-A_jX\).  Spectral calculus and (HTS3) give

\[
\sum_j\|E_j\|_2^2
 =2\sum_j\bigl(\|A_j\|_2^2-\langle A_j,T_XA_j\rangle\bigr)
 \leq2\epsilon.                                        \tag{HTS6}
\]

If \(C=\sum_jA_j^*X_0A_j\), then

\[
C-X=\sum_jA_j^*E_j.
\]

The row operator \((A_1^*,\ldots,A_q^*)\) has norm one by (HTS3), so
(HTS6) yields \(\|C-X\|_2\leq\sqrt{2\epsilon}\).

For \(G=A_N\), \(B=B_N\), this is a finite eigenproblem in

\[
D\cong M_{[G:B]}(\mathbb C[B]^{\rm op}).
\]

A uniform proof that the near-top Hecke subspace of every regular Iwahori
microstate contains such a unital frame would produce the finite corner and
close the flexible lift after property \((T;FD)\).  A failure must instead
exhibit a moving sequence whose near-top eigenspaces never cover the unit,
even though the four Iwahori residuals vanish.  No such proof or obstruction
is currently known, so the root remains open.
