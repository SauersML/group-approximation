**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-invent-17, 2026-09-18; elementary modulo the two necessity
theorems in `requires`, which are lane proofs that have not been independently reviewed; no
priority claimed). It settles the V-stabilized half of gate (IE) of SYNTHESIS v5, negatively.

## Setting

- `A` and `S` are nontrivial finitely generated groups, `S` infinite, and `D = A * S`, generated by
  `Σ = Σ_A ⊔ Σ_S`. Word length is additive along normal forms, and `|s|_Σ = |s|_{Σ_S}` for `s ∈ S`.
- `T` is the Bass–Serre tree: vertices `gA`, `gS`, and one edge `e_g` from `gA` to `gS` for each
  `g ∈ D`, so `D` acts on edges by left multiplication and preserves this orientation.
- Deleting the open edge `e_g` leaves `T_A(g) ∋ gA` and `T_S(g) ∋ gS`.
- `X = Ω` is the compactified boundary: the ends of `T` together with one point for every vertex
  of infinite valence (every `gS`, and every `gA` if `A` is infinite). A point lies on one side of
  each edge. The half-spaces are clopen and form a basis; this is the space `Ω^{S⁺}` of
  `stabilizer-engines-embed-in-every-branch-compactification`, whose vertex points `v = gS` are
  the infinite emitters with isotropy `gSg^{-1}`.

## Statement

1. **Coding.** The map `ξ ↦ c_ξ ∈ {0,1}^D`, with `c_ξ(g) = 1` iff `ξ` lies on the `T_S(g)` side, is
   an equivariant embedding for `(g·c)(h) = c(g^{-1}h)`. Its image is a subshift. `D` acts on it
   topologically freely: `Fix(g)` is a single vertex point, two ends or empty, and never open.
2. **Not of finite type.** The subshift `X` is not an SFT, and so is not conjugate to one.
3. **Groups.** Let `T_X = G_V × (D ⋉ X)`. No finitely presented `Γ ≤ F(T_X)` contains `V × {1}`,
   the translations `t_σ` (`σ ∈ Σ`) and the site-conditioned 3-cycles `e_a`. In particular
   `F(T_X) = A(T_X)` is not finitely presented, and neither is any simple group arising this way.

For `A = Z/2` this covers every Cuntz-stabilized stabilizer engine `F(G_V × (D ⋉ Ω^{S⁺}))`,
including the stabilized form of Reid's group (`S = Z`, MathOverflow 339541).

## Proof

**Part 1.** Two distinct points of `X` are separated by an edge, so the map is injective. A
cylinder pulls back to a finite Boolean combination of half-spaces, so the map is continuous, and
`X` is compact. Equivariance: `gξ` lies on the `T_S(h)` side iff `ξ` lies on the `T_S(g^{-1}h)`
side, since `g^{-1}e_h = e_{g^{-1}h}`. Freeness: edge stabilizers are trivial. So an elliptic
`g ≠ 1` fixes one vertex and no end, and a hyperbolic `g` fixes the two ends of its axis. A single
point is not open, because every vertex point and every end is a limit of other ends.

**The local rule.** At `v = gS` the edges are `e_{gs}`, `s ∈ S`, and `e_{gs}` points away from
`v` exactly when `c(gs) = 0`. A point of `X` lies beyond at most one edge at each vertex. So every
`c ∈ X` satisfies

  **(L1)** for all `g ∈ D` and `s ≠ s'` in `S`, not both `c(gs) = 0` and `c(gs') = 0`.

(L1) is a family of forbidden pairs, one pair of sites `{1, s}` for every `s ∈ S \ {1}`, up to
translation.

**Part 2: a two-sink configuration.** Suppose `X` is the SFT of all colourings whose patterns on
the translates of the ball `B_R` occur in `X`. Pick `a ∈ A \ {1}` and `b ∈ S` with `|b|_Σ > 2R`;
`S` is infinite and finitely generated. Let `v = S`.
- Let `ξ_1` be the vertex point of `aS`, which lies in `T_A(1)`. Let `ξ_b` be the vertex point of
  `baS`, which lies in `T_A(b)`.
- Let `E_b = {b} ∪ edges(T_A(b))`, and define `y = c_{ξ_b}` on `E_b` and `y = c_{ξ_1}` elsewhere.

*`y ∉ X`.* We have `y(1) = c_{ξ_1}(1) = 0` and `y(b) = c_{ξ_b}(b) = 0`. So both edges `e_1` and `e_b`
at `v` point away from `v`, which violates (L1).

*Every `B_R`-pattern of `y` occurs in `X`.*
- For an edge `e ∈ E_b`, both `v` and `ξ_1` lie on the same side of `e`. So `c_{ξ_1}` and `c_{ξ_b}`
  differ on `e` iff `e` separates `v` from `ξ_b`, that is, iff `e ∈ D_b = {b, ba}`.
- For an edge `e ∉ E_b`, both `v` and `ξ_b` lie on the same side of `e`. So they differ iff
  `e ∈ D_1 = {1, a}`.
- Hence `y = c_{ξ_1}` off `D_b`, and `y = c_{ξ_b}` off `D_1`.
- By additivity of normal forms, `d(D_1, D_b) = min(|b|, |b|+|a|, |a|+|b|, 2|a|+|b|) = |b|_Σ > 2R`.
- A translate of `B_R` has diameter at most `2R`, so it misses `D_1` or `D_b`. On it, `y` agrees
  with `c_{ξ_b}` or with `c_{ξ_1}`.

So `y` avoids every forbidden pattern but is not in `X`, which is a contradiction for every `R`.
Finite type is a conjugacy invariant, which gives the second clause. `∎`

**Part 3.** `D` is finitely generated, and `X ⊆ {0,1}^D` is a topologically free subshift with the
action convention of `fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`. So part 2 of that
theorem, in the topologically free form of `topologically-free-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`,
applies. A finitely presented `Γ` containing `V × {1}`, every `t_σ` and every `e_a` would force `X`
to be of finite type, which part 2 excludes. `F(T_X)` contains all of these, and
`F(T_X) = A(T_X)` (`v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions`). `∎`

**Calibration.**
- If `S` is finite, every vertex has finite valence, (L1) and the valence rule at finite vertices
  are finitely many patterns, and `X = ∂T` is an SFT. The V-stabilized full group is then a
  Cuntz-stabilized SFT full group, and finite presentation is not excluded. The obstruction is
  exactly an infinite vertex group.
- Blow-ups escape. For `S = Z`, the two-point blow-up `Z ⊔ {±∞}` of
  `stabilizer-engines-embed-in-every-branch-compactification` (part 3b) replaces (L1) at `v` by a
  radius-one arrow rule along `S`. For `A = Z/2` the result is `RP^1` cut at all cusps of
  `Γ_θ ≅ Z/2 * Z`, the boundary of the `PSL_2(Z)` tree. That identification is sketched, not proved
  here.

## The mechanism, and why the unstabilized question survives

- **What the V-coordinate detects.** Take noncommuting `h, h' ∈ V`. For each `s ∈ S \ {1}`, apply
  `h` to the Cantor coordinate where `c(1) = 0` and `h'` where `c(s) = 0`. The two elements commute
  in `F(T_X)`, because by (L1) their supports are disjoint. On a two-sink colouring they do not
  commute.
- These are infinitely many independent relations, one per element of `S`. A finite presentation
  has relators of bounded radius, and those relators still hold on two-sink colourings. This is the
  content of the necessity theorem, and the reason finite presentation forces finite type once `V`
  is present: `V` supplies **inert** site-conditioned elements, which act without moving the point.
- **Why this says nothing about Reid's group itself.** Without `V`, every nontrivial element of
  `[[D ⋉ X]]` moves points by `D`-translations. Near an emitter those translations lie in `S` and
  shift the far sites, so no element fixes the conjunction pattern while acting on it. The
  unstabilized necessity theorem (`fp-alternating-full-groups-of-free-subshifts-force-sft`) needs a
  uniform separation `x ≠ ux` on a fixed ball, and that separation fails at the fixed point `v`.
  So finite presentation of `F_S` or `D(F_S)` is not decided here.
- The calibration is Thompson's `T`. Every cusp of `PSL_2(Z)` on the circle is an emitter with
  parabolic isotropy, and `T` is `F_∞`. It is not an ample full group, and it allows breakpoints at
  the cusps.

## Consequences for the gates (SYNTHESIS v5)

- **(IE) splits in two.** The Cuntz-stabilized form is refuted for every infinite vertex group. The
  unstabilized form (Reid, `D(F_S)`) is untouched and remains gate 1.
- **Track A′ cannot put the input at an emitter.**
  - Route 1 (ring) needs `(★′)`, which is an SFT.
  - Route 2 (group) needs `F(G_V × (Λ₀ ⋉ X))` to be finitely presented, which forces an SFT by
    part 3.
  - So over `Λ₀ = Λ₁ * Z`, or any free product with an infinite factor, the compactified tree is
    excluded as `X` on both routes.
  - The input must instead be a subgroup of `Λ₀` acting on a finite-type blow-up (gate (IE′) of
    `stabilizer-engines-embed-in-every-branch-compactification`), or a germ at a one-ended cusp.
    It cannot be isotropy at an emitter.
- **Track C must stay unstabilized.** Cuntz stabilization is the standard way to buy merge packing
  (`cuntz-stabilized-garside-full-groups-are-f-infinity`), and it is unavailable at emitters. A
  finiteness proof for `D(F_S)` must work for the unstabilized full group, where the natural cone complex has
  infinitely many cone types at every emitter (the removed-branch sets modulo `S`). It must
  therefore be an infinite-type Brown argument, like the one for `T`, not a finite-type one.

## Lesson for general BH

Finite presentation of a Cuntz-stabilized host forces finite type. An infinite emitter is the
opposite of finite type: it is an infinite family of forbidden pairs, one for each element of the
vertex group. The `V`-coordinate supplies inert site-conditioned elements whose commutators detect
each pair.

So in any stabilized host the input can be a subgroup of the acting group, or a germ at a
blown-up, one-ended cusp, but never isotropy at an emitter. Emitters can live only in unstabilized
full groups. There the necessity mechanism is blind at the fixed point, because nothing inert acts
there. The question "is Reid's group finitely presented?" is therefore a question about
unstabilized, infinite-type finiteness, of the kind Thompson's `T` answers positively.
