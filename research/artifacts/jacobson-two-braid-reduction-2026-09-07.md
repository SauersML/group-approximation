# The joint Jacobson candidate as a two-braid matrix problem

2026-09-07. The reductions and restrictions below have complete elementary
proofs. The number `gamma` defined in Section 4 is not proved positive.
Consequently this note does not yet prove a non-MF existence theorem.
It uses no property-(T) input and claims neither literature priority nor
Lean verification.

## 1. An explicit finitely presented marked group

Let `D=GL_3(F_2)`, let `F=D_Q x D_P`, and write `g_Q=(g,1)` and
`g_P=(1,g)`. The subscripts specify the head and tail factors. Let

```
u=(w_12,w_12),       b=(w_23,w_23),       rho=u b,
d=u b u=(w_13,w_13)=d_Q d_P,             c=(w_23)_Q.
```

Here `w_ij` is the coordinate swap. Let `K` be the copy of
`GL(<e_1,e_3>)` in the head factor, acting identically on `e_2`.
Define

```
Gamma = < F,h | h^2=1, [h,k]=1 (k in K),
                    (h b)^3=1, (h d_P)^3=1 >,              (1)
w=(I+E_13)_Q.
```

This is an explicit finite presentation: one can use the multiplication
table of the finite group `F` as its finite presentation in (1).

The word `w` is nontrivial. For a direct verification, act linearly on the
vector space over `F_2` with basis `e_(i,n)`, `1<=i<=3`, `n>=0`, consisting
of finite-support vectors. Let `g_Q` act as `g` on level zero and let
`g_P` act as `g` on every positive level. Define `h` to exchange

```
e_(2,n) <-> e_(1,n+1)       for every n>=0
```

and to fix `e_(1,0)` and every `e_(3,n)`. This defines an invertible linear
map, is an involution, and commutes with `K`. The permutation `h b` has the
three-cycles

```
(e_(1,n+1), e_(2,n), e_(3,n))       (n>=0)
```

and fixes `e_(1,0)`. The permutation `h d_P` has the three-cycles

```
(e_(1,n), e_(3,n), e_(2,n-1))       (n>=1)
```

and fixes `e_(1,0),e_(3,0)`. Thus both braid relations hold. Finally `w`
sends `e_(3,0)` to `e_(3,0)+e_(1,0)`, so its image is not the identity.
In particular the marked group in (1) is a concrete candidate, independent
of any unproved assertion about its finite or unitary representations.

## 2. Exact equivalence with the two shortest cells

The previous joint presentation uses the affine braid `(h b)^3=1` and
the controlled cell `W=c`, where

```
W=(rho^2 h u rho^(-2))(rho h u rho^(-1))(h u).
```

In the envelope generated by `F,h` with `[h,K]=1`, the controlled relator
is a conjugate of the second braid relator. Indeed `rho^3=1`,
`u rho^2=d`, `d u=rho`, and `d_Q` commutes with `h,d_P`. Hence

```
W c^(-1)
 = rho^2 (h d)^3 rho c^(-1)
 = rho^2 d_Q (h d_P)^3 rho c^(-1)
 = (c rho^2) (h d_P)^3 (c rho^2)^(-1).                  (2)
```

The last step uses `rho^2 d_Q rho=c`. Thus (1) is exactly the joint
quotient already studied, with the same packet and mark. No additional
relation or quotient has been introduced.

There is also an exact metric conclusion. For an exact unitary
representation `pi` of `F` and any unitary `H` commuting with `pi(K)`,
equation (2) gives

```
|| W(pi,H)-pi(c) || = || (H pi(d_P))^3-I ||.             (3)
```

This equality has no dimension-dependent constant and does not require
either braid to hold approximately or exactly.

## 3. A sharp restriction on finite matrix types

Suppose `pi:F->U(V)` is exact, `H=H*=H^(-1)` commutes with `pi(K)`, and

```
max( ||(H pi(b))^3-I||, ||(H pi(d_P))^3-I|| ) < 2.       (4)
```

For self-adjoint unitaries `A,B`, direct multiplication gives

```
(AB) A (AB)^(-1)-B = ((AB)^3-I) B.                      (5)
```

If the right side has norm less than two, the negative spectral
projections of the left conjugate of `A` and of `B` are less than one
apart. They have equal ranks: a projection of larger rank has a unit
vector in its range and in the kernel of the smaller projection, making
their distance at least one. Applying (5) twice proves

```
rank_-(pi(b)) = rank_-(H) = rank_-(pi(d_P)).              (6)
```

Since `d_P` and `(w_23)_P` are conjugate inside `D_P`, and the two packet
factors commute, (6) is equivalently

```
Tr(pi(t_Q s_P)) = Tr(pi(s_P))                            (7)
```

for every transvection `t` and `s` in `D`. To see that all transvections
are conjugate, write one as `I+v f`, where `v!=0`, `f!=0`, and `f(v)=0`,
then choose a basis starting with `v` and a vector on which `f` equals
one. A coordinate swap in characteristic two has the same form and
rank one after subtracting the identity. Thus conjugation separately
inside the two factors propagates the trace equality to (7).

For `e=(I-pi(w))/2` and any tail transvection `s_P`, (7) implies

```
Tr(e pi(s_P))=0,
rank(e (I-pi(s_P))/2)=rank(e)/2.                         (8)
```

So exactly half of the marked negative space must be negative for each
tail transvection. This conclusion is exact even though (4) allows
nonzero errors.

There is a finer integral restriction. In each factor take the four-group

```
A={ I, I+E_12, I+E_13, I+E_12+E_13 } ~= C_2 x C_2.
```

All three nonidentity elements are transvections. For a character
`alpha` of `A_Q` and `beta` of `A_P`, let `n_(alpha,beta)` be their joint
eigenspace dimension. If `alpha` is nontrivial, (7) and Fourier averaging
give, for every `s!=1` in `A_P`,

```
Tr(P_alpha pi(s_P))
 = (1/4) sum_(a in A_Q) alpha(a) Tr(pi(a s_P)) = 0.
```

Fourier inversion on `A_P` therefore makes `n_(alpha,beta)` independent
of `beta`. The subgroup acting on coordinates `2,3` in `D_Q` normalizes
`A_Q`, permutes its three nontrivial characters transitively, and
commutes with the tail. Consequently there is an integer `k>=0` such
that

```
n_(alpha,beta)=k  for all alpha!=1 and all beta,
rank(e)=8k.                                              (9)
```

In particular a surviving mark in (4) requires `k>=1`. Three quarters
of its negative space is outside the `A_P`-fixed space. These constraints
do not force `k=0`; Section 6 gives an exact packet satisfying them.

One class is excluded completely. Suppose that

```
V=V_Q directSum V_P directSum V_0,
```

where the tail acts trivially on `V_Q`, the head acts trivially on `V_P`,
and both act trivially on `V_0`. If `pi(w)!=I`, every choice of `H` as
above has maximum braid defect exactly two. Otherwise (8), applied to
the nonzero marked space contained in `V_Q`, would assert that a
trivial tail involution has a nonzero negative space. Defects are always
at most two, proving the sharp assertion. No assumption that `H`
preserves the displayed decomposition is needed.

An exact algebraic version works over any field: with the same separated
factor decomposition and both braids exact, `h` is conjugate to both
`d_P` and `b`. Equality of `rank(h-I)` with the other two ranks forces
`rank((w_23)_Q-I)=0`, since the head and tail ranks add on the direct
sum. Conjugacy to the mark then kills `w`. This includes arbitrary
numbers of natural head and tail copies, arbitrary spectators, and an
otherwise unrestricted mixing involution `h`.

## 4. The precise remaining number

For the explicit packet above define

```
gamma = inf max( ||(H pi(b))^3-I||,
                ||(H pi(d_P))^3-I|| ),                  (10)
```

where the infimum ranges over all positive dimensions, all exact
`pi:F->U(d)` with `pi(w)!=I`, and all self-adjoint unitary `H` commuting
with `pi(K)`. The set is nonempty: take a faithful finite permutation
representation of `F` and `H=I`. Thus `0<=gamma<=2`.

**Theorem.** `gamma>0` if and only if every homomorphism from `Gamma`
to every norm matrix corona kills `w`. In particular `gamma>0`, together
with Section 1, would prove the requested property-(T)-free existence
theorem.

If `gamma=0`, choose marked exact packets in dimensions `d_n` whose two
defects tend to zero. Their classes in

```
Q_d=product_n M_(d_n) / { (a_n): ||a_n|| -> 0 }
```

satisfy the presentation (1). Each `pi_n(w)` is a nonidentity involution,
so `||pi_n(w)-I||=2`. The resulting homomorphism keeps `w` nontrivial.

Conversely, suppose a corona homomorphism keeps `w`. Lift its finitely
many packet values and `h` to unitary matrix sequences. Such lifts exist
by polar decomposition: a lift of a unitary is asymptotically unitary
on both sides, hence is invertible on a tail, and its polar unitary
differs from it by a norm-null sequence. Section 5 corrects the finite
packet to exact representations `pi_n` and the other lift to exact
self-adjoint unitaries `H_n` commuting with `pi_n(K)`, without changing
their corona classes. Both braid defects still tend to zero. Infinitely
many `pi_n(w)` are nonidentity, since otherwise the corona image would
be the identity. Restricting to those coordinates gives `gamma=0`.

This proves the equivalence. It does not determine which alternative
holds. In particular the theorem concerns invisibility of this mark;
it does not assert that visibility of the mark makes the joint group MF.

## 5. Elementary simultaneous packet correction

Here are the finite-dimensional correction facts used above, including
their dimension independence. Let `L` be a finite group and let
`v_g in U(d)` satisfy

```
max_(g,h in L) ||v_(gh)-v_g v_h|| <= epsilon.
```

Define an isometry into `ell^2(L) tensor C^d` by

```
W xi = |L|^(-1/2) sum_g delta_g tensor v_g^* xi.
```

For the left regular representation `lambda`, each component gives
`||lambda(h)W-Wv_h||<=epsilon`. With `P=WW*`, it follows that
`||lambda(h)P lambda(h)*-P||<=2 epsilon`. Average these conjugates to
obtain a positive contraction `B` commuting with `lambda(L)` and
satisfying `||B-P||<=2 epsilon`. For `epsilon<1/4`, its spectrum is in
the `2 epsilon` neighbourhood of `{0,1}`. Its cut
`Q=1_((1/2),infinity)(B)` therefore satisfies `||Q-P||<=4 epsilon`.

For `epsilon<1/4`, put `alpha=||Q-P||<1`. These two finite-rank
projections have equal ranks by the elementary projection argument in
Section 3. The map `R=QW` has `R*R>= (1-alpha)I`. Its polar isometry
`Z=R(R*R)^(-1/2)` is onto the range of `Q`. Moreover

```
||Z-W|| <= ||Z-R||+||R-W|| <= 2 alpha <= 8 epsilon.
```

Indeed the singular values of `R` are in `[sqrt(1-alpha),1]`, so
`||Z-R||<=1-sqrt(1-alpha)<=alpha`. Since `Q` commutes with `lambda(L)`,

```
pi(h)=Z* lambda(h) Z
```

is an exact representation on the original `C^d`, and

```
||pi(h)-v_h|| <= 2||Z-W||+epsilon <= 17 epsilon.          (11)
```

Apply this to the finite packet `F` on a tail of the sequence. Next
replace the lift `v` of `h` by an involution: the self-adjoint part
`(v+v*)/2` is asymptotically close to `v`, because `v^2-I` tends to
zero, and its spectral sign `T` is likewise asymptotically close.
Average `T` over the exact `pi(K)` to obtain

```
B_K=|K|^(-1) sum_(k in K) pi(k) T pi(k)*.
```

If `kappa=max_k ||[pi(k),T]||<1`, then
`||B_K-T||<=kappa`; its spectrum is within `kappa` of `{+1,-1}` and
avoids zero. Its sign `H` is an exact involution commuting with
`pi(K)`, with `||H-T||<=2 kappa`. Along the sequence `kappa` tends
to zero, completing the simultaneous correction.

All constructions preserve the original dimension. They establish a
normal form for the approximation problem, not positivity in (10).

## 6. What the finite checks can settle

Run the [exact audit](../../experiments/jacobson_two_braid_audit.py) with
`python3 experiments/jacobson_two_braid_audit.py`. Its committed output is
[the replay record](jacobson-two-braid-replay-2026-09-07.json).

The audit enumerates all 316 involutions on the
four-dimensional `K`-trivial space in the natural six-dimensional
head-plus-tail module over `F_2`. Exactly 12 satisfy the affine braid
and 33 satisfy the tail braid; none satisfies both. The relator identity
(2) is checked on all 316 involutions. Section 3 proves the stronger exclusion
without a bound on the separated module dimensions.

The same audit constructs a 56-point permutation representation of `F`
which satisfies the necessary rank balances (6)--(9) with `k=2`:
use the seven nonzero vectors for the head and the eight cosets of a
subgroup of order 21 in `GL_3(F_2)` for the tail, then take the product
action. A transvection has no fixed tail coset, because an odd-order
stabilizer cannot contain an involution. Direct counting gives marked
negative rank 16, tail negative rank 28, product negative rank 28, and
joint negative rank 8. All 28,224 products in the tail action table are
also checked. This packet is not asserted to extend to an `H`
satisfying the two braids. It proves that the rank restrictions alone
do not establish a positive value of (10).

The unresolved step is therefore an all-dimensions positive lower bound
in (10), allowing representations with both factors acting on the same
summands. A finite unsuccessful search cannot establish that bound.

A subsequent [exact cyclotomic construction](jacobson-mixed-packet-certificate-2026-09-07.md)
improves the mixed-type check: it supplies an actual admissible involution
on dimension twelve with both defects strictly below two and marked rank
eight. Thus `0<=gamma<2`, and the necessary rank divisibility is sharp.
It does not determine whether `gamma` is positive or zero.
