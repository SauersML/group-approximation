# Parallel order-three nonlinear gates: exact collisions

Date: 2026-09-08. The specified scalar parallel encoder is not
injective. Its collision is finite and exact; no calculation was run.

For an element t of order three define

    q_t(x)(g)=x(g)x(gt)+x(gt)x(gt^2),
    Phi_t(x)=x+q_t(x).

On each coset g<t>, Phi_t fixes weights 0,1,3 and cyclically
rotates the weight-two patterns. Hence Phi_t itself is reversible.
The candidate considered here applies the four atlas corrections
in parallel:

    F(x)=x+q_(a_1)(x)+q_(b_1)(x)+q_(a_2)(x)+q_(b_2)(x).

All four actors have order three. This formula is a simultaneous
sum of corrections, not the composition of the reversible Phi_t.

## Two active corrections produce the collision

Put a=a_1, b=b_1. Their order-three subgroups are distinct: a fixes
the last standard chart vector while b takes it to the third one.
Thus <a> intersects <b> only in 1. Define the finite sets

    S={1,a,b},
    T=<a> union <b>={1,a,a^2,b,b^2}.

For x=1_S, the only coset g<a> with two occupied sites is
<a> itself. Its pattern in the order (1,a,a^2) is (1,1,0), so

    supp q_a(1_S)={1,a^2}.

The corresponding assertion for b gives

    supp q_b(1_S)={1,b^2}.

The common toggle at 1 cancels. Therefore

    1_S+q_a(1_S)+q_b(1_S)=1_T.                    (1)

For input 1_T, the subgroup <a> is fully occupied. Every other
coset g<a> meets T in at most one point: two points of <b>
in such a coset would put b or b^2 in <a>. Thus q_a(1_T)=0.
Similarly q_b(1_T)=0, and

    1_T+q_a(1_T)+q_b(1_T)=1_T.                    (2)

These arguments hold for any two distinct order-three subgroups
in any group. No freeness of the subgroup generated by a,b is used.

## The other chart corrections vanish everywhere

Both S and T lie in the first finite chart group K_1 with leaves
(01,1,000,001). The other chart has leaves (00,1,010,011).
The two second-chart actors lie outside K_1, by the following
independent exact separations:

    t_1 a_2 s_010=1,
    t_1 h s_010 belongs to {0,s_0} for every h in K_1;

    t_01 b_2 s_1=s_0,
    t_01 h s_1 belongs to {0,1} for every h in K_1.

The first line follows from column 2 of the native a matrix;
the second follows from column 1 of the native b matrix. The
contrasts are strict because s_0 differs from both 0 and 1.

If an order-three element u is outside a subgroup K, each coset
g<u> meets K in at most one point. Two intersection points
would put u or u^2 in K, and either inclusion puts u in K. Hence
q_u(x)=0 globally whenever x is supported in K.

Apply this with K=K_1 and u=a_2,b_2. The second-chart corrections
vanish for both 1_S and 1_T. Equations (1) and (2) now give the
literal full-shift collision

    F(1_S)=1_T=F(1_T),       |S|=3, |T|=5.

Thus this scalar parallel rule has no left inverse. In particular
its complement-pair linear equation need not be decided in order
to reject it: the finite chart restriction already fails injectivity.
That separate linear equation is not claimed solved by this note.

## The design consequence

Parallel application of individually reversible, conservative
order-three gates can create particles. Here two gates independently
remove the same occupied site; their removal toggles cancel while
both newly occupied sites remain. The resulting five-point pattern
is fixed. A replacement must address this simultaneous activation
mechanism, as well as any dual-boundary functional obstruction.

This excludes the specified candidate. It does not settle the
surjunctivity of the Leavitt unit group.

## The first higher-degree conflict correction also collides

A concrete repair is suggested by the exact reversible composition

    Phi_b(Phi_a(x))
      =x+q_a(x)+q_b(x)+Dq_b(x)[q_a(x)]+q_b(q_a(x)).

Here the polarization is literal Boolean composition:

    Dq_b(x)[d](g)
      =d(g)x(gb)+x(g)d(gb)
       +d(gb)x(gb^2)+x(gb)d(gb^2).

Retain the cubic correction but omit the final quartic term:

    J(x)=x+q_a(x)+q_b(x)+Dq_b(x)[q_a(x)].

This repairs the preceding three-point collision. Nevertheless,
for the native first-chart actors a,b, it has a different exact
collision. Define

    S_4={1,a,b,ba},
    S_6={1,a,b,ba,ba^2,b^2},
    T_6={1,a,a^2,ba,ba^2,b^2}.

Then

    J(1_(S_4))=1_(T_6)=J(1_(S_6)).

To check it, first note that a,a^2,ba,ba^2 lie in four distinct
cosets g<b>, all disjoint from <b>. Conjugation comparisons
that could identify two of these cosets would force a to normalize
<b> or b to normalize <a>. An order-three element acts trivially
on Aut(C_3), so those comparisons would force a,b to commute;
they do not. The two remaining comparisons are
a^2 b a^2=b^2 and a b a=b^2. On the last standard basis vector,
their left sides give e_1+e_2 and e_0+e_1+e_2, while b^2 gives
e_2+e_3. Thus neither holds.

For S_4 the a-correction has support {1,a^2,b,ba^2}, and

    Phi_a(1_(S_4))=1_{a,a^2,ba,ba^2}.

The b-correction of this last configuration is zero. The
b-correction of q_a(1_(S_4)) has support {1,b^2}. Using
J=Phi_b Phi_a+q_b(q_a) therefore gives J(1_(S_4))=1_(T_6).

For S_6, the a-correction has support {1,a^2}. Its b-correction
is zero. Applying Phi_a and then Phi_b directly gives T_6, so
J(1_(S_6))=1_(T_6) as well.

Thus keeping the cubic overlap correction alone is insufficient.
Keeping the omitted quartic term recovers the explicitly reversible
composition. Both alternatives have been decided for this proposed
repair; neither gives a strict encoder.

## Parallel nonconstant-triple complementation: twelve versus sixteen

Date: 2026-09-08. This specified particle-creating scalar encoder
is not injective. The proof supplies finite inputs of sizes 12 and
16 with identical outputs. No computation was run.

### Local reversible control and proposed parallel rule

For t of order three, let Psi_t fix constant patterns on each
coset g<t> and complement its three bits on every nonconstant
pattern. It is an involution and exchanges weights one and two.
Its quadratic formula is

    Psi_t(x)(g)=x(gt)+x(gt^2)
                +x(g)x(gt)+x(g)x(gt^2)+x(gt)x(gt^2).

Write p_t=Psi_t+Id. Its value is constant on each coset g<t>:

    p_t(x)(g)=x(g)+x(gt)+x(gt^2)
               +x(g)x(gt)+x(g)x(gt^2)+x(gt)x(gt^2).

The candidate applies all four atlas corrections in parallel:

    F(x)=x+p_a(x)+p_b(x)+p_c(x)+p_d(x),
    a=a_1, b=b_1, c=a_2, d=b_2.

This differs from the weight-two rotation gate in the preceding
order-three audit. Here singleton orbits activate, so another
chart can create particles outside an input's supporting chart.
Also p_t(1+x)=p_t(x), hence F(1+x)=1+F(x); complementary inputs
cannot collide. For distinct order-three subgroups there is no
single-bit collision on any background: its correction differences
are scalar multiples of the subgroup indicators, and their exterior
sites force all coefficients to vanish, leaving the original bit.

### Exact quadratic difference formula

On one t-orbit write its three input bits as x_0,x_1,x_2. If a
difference configuration occupies exactly one site, then

    p_t(x+delta)+p_t(x)=1+sum of the other two background bits.

If it occupies exactly two sites, the difference is

    1+sum of the background bits at those two sites.

These are scalar values on the entire orbit. They follow by
expanding the displayed quadratic polynomial; no derivative
approximation is used.

### The two finite inputs

Define

    Delta={a,a^2,b,b^2},

    E={ba,b^2 a,ab,a^2 b}
       union {r c:r in Delta}
       union {r d:r in Delta}.

The exact address separations proved below imply |E|=12 and
E intersects Delta trivially. Put

    x=1_E,       y=1_(E union Delta)=x+1_Delta.

The a-orbit <a> meets Delta at a,a^2, where both background bits
are zero. Thus its correction difference is one. The two other
a-orbits meeting Delta are b<a> and b^2<a>; each meets Delta
once, and its other two background bits have sum one, by the
choices ba and b^2 a in E. Their correction differences vanish.
Consequently

    p_a(y)+p_a(x)=1_(<a>).

The choices ab,a^2 b similarly give

    p_b(y)+p_b(x)=1_(<b>).

Each c-orbit meeting Delta meets it at exactly one point r. Its
other sites are rc,rc^2, with background values one and zero.
Thus p_c(y)+p_c(x)=0. The same argument applies to d. Since
<a> and <b> intersect only in 1,

    F(y)+F(x)
      =1_Delta+1_(<a>)+1_(<b>)=0.

This is the required literal collision, with |supp x|=12 and
|supp y|=16. An explicit common output is unnecessary to verify
equality: the difference calculation determines every output bit.

### Complete address separation

The native a,b matrices have order three and generate distinct
cyclic subgroups. The four words b^k a^l, k,l in {1,2}, are distinct;
equality of two would put a nontrivial power of a in <b>. The four
words a^k b^l are distinct for the same reason.

No word of the first group equals one of the second. Apply each
matrix to e_3, using zero-based indices. Every a^k b^l e_3 has
coefficient one at e_1; every b^k a^l e_3 has coefficient zero
there, because a fixes e_3. None of these eight words belongs to
Delta or to {1}. This proves all the needed first-chart endpoints
are distinct.

For the second-chart endpoints, the five cosets

    K_1, K_1 c, K_1 c^2, K_1 d, K_1 d^2

are pairwise disjoint. Individual c,d lie outside K_1 by the
separations in
`research/artifacts/gottschalk-parallel-order-three-five-point-collision-2026-09-08.md`;
their squares are outside as well because they have order three.
For the four cross comparisons, direct second-chart calculations
give

    t_1(cd)s_010=1,
    t_1(c^2 d)s_010=1,
    t_1(cd^2)s_011=1,
    t_01(c^2 d^2)s_010=s_1.

For h in K_1, the first two expressions would belong to {0,s_0},
the third to {0,s_1}, and the fourth to {0,s_0}. Thus each of
cd,c^2 d,cd^2,c^2 d^2 lies outside K_1. These are exactly the
cross-coset membership tests c^k(d^l)^-1, k,l in {1,2}.

It follows that all selected rc and rd endpoints are distinct,
outside K_1, and different from every unselected rc^2 or rd^2
endpoint. It also follows that each c- or d-orbit meets Delta
at most once. This completes every address check used above.

The candidate passes the complementary-input and single-bit tests
but fails this four-bit difference test. No left inverse exists.
The surjunctivity of the Leavitt unit group remains undecided.
