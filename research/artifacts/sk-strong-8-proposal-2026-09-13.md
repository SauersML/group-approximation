## What changes and why
- Rev3's §2 proves that LEF groups are exactly the subgroups of infinite simple Kazhdan LEF groups. Its host is LEF through models on `E_n = F_2^{Q'_n} × Q'_n`.
- Those models commute with translations of the base point, so they give no finite simple approximants.
- Letting L act on configurations seen from the base point, `Y_n = F_2^{Q_n}`, the letters generate the full matrix algebra `M_{2^{|Q_n|}}(F_2)`.
- The §2 host is then, like G_X in Theorem 1, a marked limit of finite simple groups `SL_N(F_2)` whose Cayley graphs form expanders.
- So the corollary can state the strongest form: LEF groups are exactly the subgroups of infinite simple Kazhdan groups that are limits of finite simple expanders. That matches the title on main, "Infinite simple Kazhdan groups that are limits of finite simple groups", and makes §2 the title's theorem in general.

## (a) Corollary statement
Current (rev3.tex l.256–259):
```latex
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lef}
A finitely generated group is LEF if and only if it is a subgroup of an
infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan LEF group.
\end{corollary}
```
Replacement:
```latex
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lef}
A finitely generated group is LEF if and only if it is a subgroup of an
infinite finitely generated simple group with property~\textup{(T)} that is
a limit of finite simple groups $\SL_N(\F_2)$ in the space of marked
groups, whose Cayley graphs form a family of expanders.
\end{corollary}
```

## (b) Model paragraph (complete proof text as it would appear)
Current (rev3.tex l.297–313), verbatim:
```latex
For finite models $\psi_n\colon B_n\to Q'_n$ of $\Delta$ put
$E_n=\F_2^{Q'_n}\times Q'_n$, and attach to $(c,q)\in E_n$ the
configuration $h\mapsto c(q\psi_n(h))$ on $B_n$. Let $x\mapsto\delta x+f$
act on $E_n$ by
$(c,q)\mapsto\bigl(c+\sum_{h\in\operatorname{supp}f}1_{q'\psi_n(h)},q'\bigr)$
with $q'=q\psi_n(\delta^{-1})$. For fixed elements of $L$ and large $n$,
these permutations multiply as in $L$ and move the attached
configurations near $e$ as $L$ moves points of $Z$. Every finite pattern
occurs in $Z$, so a function depending on coordinates in a ball defines a
diagonal matrix $D_n(f)$ by its values at the attached configurations,
and $\sum_\ell f_\ell u_\ell\mapsto\sum_\ell D_n(f_\ell)P_\ell$, with
$P_\ell$ the permutation matrices, is a unital ring homomorphism from $R$
to the algebraic ultraproduct $\prod_\omega M_{|E_n|}(\F_2)$. It maps $G$
to the ultraproduct of the groups $\GL_{3|E_n|}(\F_2)$. The kernel is a
normal subgroup of the simple group $G$ that does not contain
$e_{12}(1)$, so $G$ embeds there and is LEF.
```
Replacement:
```latex
Let $\psi_n\colon\Delta\to Q_n$ be finite models of $\Delta$ with
$\psi_n(e)=e$ and $Q_n$ generated by the images of the generators. Let
$L$ act on $Y_n=\F_2^{Q_n}$ as it acts on $Z$: $\delta$ by
$y\mapsto\psi_n(\delta)\cdot y$ and the generator $a$ by adding $1_e$.
Each $y\in Y_n$ gives the point $y\circ\psi_n$ of $Z$. Put
$D_n(f)\delta_y=f(y\circ\psi_n)\delta_y$ on $\F_2^{Y_n}$, let $P_\ell$ be
the permutation matrix of $\ell$, and put
$\Phi_n(\sum_\ell f_\ell u_\ell)=\sum_\ell D_n(f_\ell)P_\ell$. For fixed
$r,s\in R$ and large $n$, $\Phi_n$ is additive and multiplicative on $r,s$,
since the permutations multiply as in $L$ and move $y\circ\psi_n$ near $e$
as $L$ moves points of $Z$. If $r\ne0$, then $\Phi_n(r)\ne0$ for large $n$:
choose a cylinder on which $f_{\ell_0}\circ\ell_0=1$ for some coefficient
$f_{\ell_0}\ne0$, and on which the points $\ell x$, for $\ell$ in the
support of $r$, differ pairwise at prescribed coordinates, which is
possible as $\Delta$ is infinite, and choose $y$ with $y\circ\psi_n$ in it.
The conjugates of $D_n(e_{\{x:x(e)=1\}})$ by $Q_n$ are the coordinate
functions of $Y_n$, and the conjugates of $P_a$ are all translations of
$Y_n$. So the letters generate $M_{2^{|Q_n|}}(\F_2)$, and as in
Section~1, $G$ and $\SL_{3\cdot2^{|Q_n|}}(\F_2)$ are marked quotients of
$\EL_3$ of a free algebra, these finite simple groups converge to $G$,
their Cayley graphs form expanders, and $G$ is LEF.
```
