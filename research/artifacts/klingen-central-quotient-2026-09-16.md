## 0. The Klingen subgroup `E` of `Sp_4(Z)`

Take `Z^4` with basis `(e1, e2, f1, f2)` and `J = [[0, I], [-I, 0]]`, so
`omega(e_i, f_j) = delta_ij` and `omega(e_i, e_j) = omega(f_i, f_j) = 0`.  For
`v = a e2 + b f2` (`a, b in Z`), `c in Z` and `g in SL_2(Z)`, put

```text
X_v(x) = omega(v,x) e1 + omega(e1,x) v,      Z(x) = omega(e1,x) e1,
N(v,c) = I + X_v + c Z,
L(g)   = identity on e1, f1, and g on span(e2, f2)  (in the basis (e2, f2)).
```

**(F1)–(F4).**

- (F1) `N(v,c)` and `L(g)` lie in `Sp_4(Z)`.
- (F2) `N(v,c) N(w,d) = N(v+w, c+d+omega(v,w))`, where
  `omega(a e2 + b f2, a' e2 + b' f2) = a b' - b a'`.
- (F3) `L(g) N(v,c) L(g)^{-1} = N(g v, c)`.
- (F4) `L` is a homomorphism.

Hand proof of (F2):
`omega(v, e1) = omega(e1, e1) = 0` gives `X_v^2 = 0`, `X_v Z = Z X_v = Z^2 = 0`
and `X_v X_w = omega(v,w) Z`.  Hand proof of (F3): `L(g)` fixes `e1` and preserves
`omega`, so `L(g) X_v L(g)^{-1} = X_{gv}` and `L(g) Z L(g)^{-1} = Z`.  All four
identities are also machine-checked by
`experiments/sp2g-z-full-c-star-algebra-has-llp-2026-09-16/klingen_structure.py`.

Let `Lambda = Z^2 x| SL_2(Z)`, with `(v,g)(w,h) = (v + g w, g h)`, and let

```text
c((v,g),(w,h)) = omega(v, g w)   in Z.                               (0.1)
```

This is exactly the `Z`-valued 2-cocycle of ISW's proof of Corollary B (source
line 661: `c((x,y),(z,t)) = xt - yz`, extended by `c((a,g),(b,h)) = c(a, g.b)`).

**(F5).** `E := { N(v,c) L(g) }` is a subgroup of `Sp_4(Z)`.  The map
`(c, (v,g)) -> N(v,c) L(g)` is an isomorphism from the central extension
`Z x_c Lambda`, with law `(m,x)(n,y) = (m + n + c(x,y), x y)`, onto `E`.

Proof of (F5).  By (F2)–(F4),
`N(v,c)L(g) N(w,d)L(h) = N(v + g w, c + d + omega(v, g w)) L(g h)`.  The map is
injective: the image of `f1` is `f1 + v + c e1`, and the `(e2, f2)`-block is `g`.

Write `z = (1, e) = N(0,1)`.  It is central and generates the kernel of
`E -> Lambda`, and every element is `(m,x) = z^m (0,x)`.

**(F6) (correction of a label; used nowhere below).**  The radical
`U = {N(v,c)}` has `[N(e2,0), N(f2,0)] = N(0,2)`.  Its centre is `{N(0,c)}` and
`[U,U] = {N(0,2c)}`, so `Z(U)/[U,U] = Z/2`.  Therefore `U` is not isomorphic to
the integer Heisenberg group `H_3(Z)`, where that quotient is trivial.  Write
`H_omega` for `U`.  The map `(v,c) -> (v, c + ab)` turns its law into
`c + d + 2ab'`, so `H_omega` is an index-two overgroup of `H_3(Z)` (put
`c -> 2c`).  This is the
Eichler–Zagier Jacobi–Heisenberg law.  The nodes
`klingen-center-kills-isw-condition-one` and
`isw-scalar-cocycle-obstruction-dies-on-sp4-parabolic-radicals` call this radical
`H_3(Z)`.  Their stated conclusions do not visibly depend on the difference (not
re-verified here).

## 1. The central quotients

For `t in R` put `sigma_t = exp(2 pi i t c) in Z^2(Lambda, T)`, and let
`A_t = C^*(Lambda, sigma_t)`.  This is the full twisted group C*-algebra, universal
for unitary `W` with `W(x) W(y) = sigma_t(x,y) W(xy)`.  Then:

- `A_0 = C^*(Lambda)`;
- `sigma_{1/k}` is ISW's `c_k` (source line 663);
- `c_k|_{Z^2}` is not a coboundary for `k >= 3` (same line).

**(1.1).** There is a surjective *-homomorphism

```text
q_t : C^*(E) -> A_t,    u_{(m,x)} -> e^{2 pi i t m} W_t(x),
```

whose kernel is `I_t`.  Here `I_t` is the closed ideal generated by
`u_z - e^{2 pi i t}`, which equals the closure of `(u_z - e^{2 pi i t}) C^*(E)`
because `u_z` is central.

Proof of (1.1).  If `W` is a `sigma_t`-representation, then
`V(m,x) := e^{2 pi i t m} W(x)` is a unitary representation of `E`, since
`V((m,x)(n,y)) = e^{2 pi i t (m+n+c(x,y))} W(xy) = V(m,x) V(n,y)`.  Conversely, a
representation `V` of `E` with `V(z) = e^{2 pi i t}` gives the
`sigma_t`-representation `W(x) := V(0,x)`.  Both `A_t` and `C^*(E)/I_t` are
universal for the same class, so `q_t` is well defined and onto, and
`ker q_t = I_t`.

This is the fibre picture of FFW Proposition `c ext struct` (Packer–Raeburn):
`C^*(E)` is a `C(T)`-algebra through `C^*(<z>)`, with fibre `A_t` at `e^{2 pi i t}`.

## 2. Two permanence lemmas

Write `B(l^2)` for the bounded operators on a separable infinite-dimensional
Hilbert space.

We use four standard facts, cited without theorem numbers:

- (K) **Kirchberg's criterion.**  A unital C*-algebra `A` has the LLP iff
  `A (x)_max B(l^2) = A (x)_min B(l^2)`.  ISW source line 728 cites [Ki93] and
  [Oz04b, Corollary 3.17]; this was not re-fetched.
- (MX) **The max tensor product is exact.**  If `I` is an ideal of `A`, then
  `I (x)_max C -> A (x)_max C` is injective, and the kernel of
  `A (x)_max C -> (A/I) (x)_max C` is the closure of `I (.) C`.
- (MN) **The min tensor product is injective** (also for operator systems).  A min
  tensor product of completely positive maps is completely positive, with norm the
  product of the norms.
- (CE) **LLP passes to the range of a ucp conditional expectation.**  If
  `P : A -> C` is a ucp idempotent onto `C`, then every finite-dimensional operator
  system in `C` lies in `A`, and a ucp map `phi` on `C` restricts along `phi o P`.

**Lemma A (quotient permanence under min-exactness).**  Let `A` have the LLP, and
let `I` be an ideal of `A` with

```text
ker( q (x) id : A (x)_min B(l^2) -> (A/I) (x)_min B(l^2) ) = I (x)_min B(l^2).   (EX)
```

Then `A/I` has the LLP.

*Proof.*  Let `B = B(l^2)`.  By (K), the canonical map is an isomorphism
`A (x)_max B = A (x)_min B`.  By (MX), `(A/I) (x)_max B` is the quotient of
`A (x)_max B` by the closure of `I (.) B`.  Under the isomorphism, that closure is
`I (x)_min B` (by (MN)).  Hence
`(A/I) (x)_max B = (A (x)_min B)/(I (x)_min B)`, and by (EX) this is
`(A/I) (x)_min B`.  Every map here is the identity on algebraic tensors, so
max = min for `A/I`, and (K) applies.  QED

Without (EX) the lemma is false: `C^*(F_infinity)` has the LLP, and every
separable unital C*-algebra is a quotient of it.

**Lemma B (local splitting forces exactness).**  Let `q : A -> A/I` be a quotient
map.  Assume that for some operator system `S` in `A/I` there is a ucp
`s : S -> A` with `q o s = id_S`.  Let `y` be in `S (.) C`, and let `w` in
`A (.) C` satisfy `(q (x) id) w = y`.  Then

```text
dist_{A (x)_min C}( w, I (x)_min C ) <= || y ||_{(A/I) (x)_min C}.
```

If this holds for every finite-dimensional `S` (q "locally split"), then
`ker(q (x) id_C) = I (x)_min C` for every C*-algebra `C`.

*Proof.*  By (MN), `s (x) id : S (x)_min C -> A (x)_min C` is completely
contractive, and `S (x)_min C` carries the norm of `(A/I) (x)_min C`.  So
`||(s (x) id) y|| <= ||y||`.  Now `w - (s (x) id) y` lies in `A (.) C` and is
killed by `q (x) id`.  On algebraic tensor products, `ker(q (x) id) = I (.) C` by
linear algebra, so `w - (s (x) id) y` lies in `I (x)_min C`, which proves the
inequality.

For the second statement, apply the inequality with `S` spanned by `1` and the
first legs of `y` and their adjoints.  The induced map
`(A (x)_min C)/(I (x)_min C) -> (A/I) (x)_min C` is then isometric on the dense
image of `A (.) C`, so it is injective.  QED

## 3. Theorem 1: the central quotient at `t = 0` is min-inexact

**Theorem 1.**  Let `q_0 : C^*(E) -> C^*(Lambda)` with kernel `I_0` (§1).  Then

```text
ker( q_0 (x) id : C^*(E) (x)_min B(l^2) -> C^*(Lambda) (x)_min B(l^2) )
      is strictly larger than  I_0 (x)_min B(l^2).                         (T1)
```

Moreover, there are `g_1, ..., g_m in Lambda` such that
`S_F = span{1, u_{g_i}, u_{g_i}^*}` admits no ucp map `s : S_F -> C^*(E)` with
`q_0 o s = id`.

*Proof.*

**Step 1 (finite-dimensional genuine representations of `E`).**  Fix `k >= 3`.

- `c mod k` factors through `Gamma_k = (Z/k)^2 x| SL_2(Z/k)` (ISW line 664), so
  `E_k := Z/k x_{c mod k} Gamma_k` is a finite group.
- Reduction `theta_k : E -> E_k` is a surjective homomorphism, and
  `theta_k(z) = (1,e)` is central of order `k`.
- Let `V_k` be the `e^{2 pi i/k}`-eigenspace of `lambda(theta_k(z))` on
  `l^2(E_k)`.  It is `E_k`-invariant because `theta_k(z)` is central.  It is
  nonzero because `l^2(E_k)`, restricted to the cyclic group `<theta_k(z)>`, is a
  multiple of that group's regular representation, which contains every
  character.
- Put `d_k = dim V_k` and `rho_k = lambda(theta_k(.))|_{V_k}`.  This is a unitary
  representation of `E` with `rho_k(z) = e^{2 pi i/k}`.
- Set `pi_k(x) := rho_k(0,x)` for `x in Lambda`.  Since
  `(0,x)(0,y) = z^{c(x,y)} (0,xy)`, we get
  `pi_k(x) pi_k(y) = c_k(x,y) pi_k(xy)`.  So `pi_k` is a `c_k`-projective
  representation, as in hypothesis (3) of ISW Theorem A.

**Step 2 (the ISW gap).**  The pair `(Lambda, Z^2)` has relative property (T)
(ISW line 236, citing [Ka67, Ma82]).  In the second proof of Theorem A (lines
695–704, Claim `gap`), ISW use Theorem `NPS` ([NPS07, Lemma 1.1]) to obtain
`g_1, ..., g_m in Lambda` and `D = sqrt(m^2 - delta^2/2) < m` such that

```text
|| sum_i pi(g_i) || <= D  for EVERY projective unitary representation pi of Lambda
                          whose 2-cocycle restricted to Z^2 is not a coboundary.   (G)
```

For `k >= 3`, `c_k|_{Z^2}` is not a coboundary (line 663), and neither is its
complex conjugate.

**Step 3 (the element).**

- Let `B = prod_{k>=3} M_{d_k}(C)`, acting block-diagonally on
  `H = (+)_k C^{d_k}`, a copy of `l^2`.  Fix orthonormal bases of the `V_k`.
- Let `w_i = ( conj(pi_k(g_i)) )_k in B`, with entrywise complex conjugation.
- Set

  ```text
  t~ = sum_i u_{(0,g_i)} (x) w_i  in C^*(E) (.) B,
  t  = (q_0 (x) id)(t~) = sum_i u_{g_i} (x) w_i  in C^*(Lambda) (.) B.
  ```

  Up to `conj(B) = B`, `t` is ISW's element (line 708) built from the `pi_k`.

**Step 4 (upper bound: `||t||_min <= D`).**  Represent `C^*(Lambda)` faithfully
by its universal representation `pi_u`.  Then `C^*(Lambda) (x)_min B` acts
block-diagonally on `(+)_k (H_u (x) C^{d_k})`, so

```text
||t||_min = sup_k || sum_i pi_u(g_i) (x) conj(pi_k(g_i)) ||.
```

The map `x -> pi_u(x) (x) conj(pi_k(x))` is a projective representation with
2-cocycle `conj(c_k)`, because `pi_u` is genuine.  By (G), every block is at most
`D`.  This is ISW's estimate (min), line 713.

**Step 5 (lower bound: `dist(t~, I_0 (x)_min B) >= m`).**

Let `Q = prod_k (M_{d_k} (x) M_{d_k}) / (+)_{c_0} (M_{d_k} (x) M_{d_k})`.  Define
`Theta : C^*(E) (x)_min B -> Q` as the corona quotient of
`(rho_k (x) ev_k)_k`.  Each `rho_k (x) ev_k` is a *-homomorphism by functoriality
of `(x)_min`, so `Theta` is a *-homomorphism.

(a) `Theta` kills `I_0 (x)_min B`.

- For a finite sum `a = sum_j x_j (u_z - 1) y_j`,
  `||rho_k(a)|| <= |e^{2 pi i/k} - 1| sum_j ||x_j|| ||y_j|| -> 0`.
- By density and `||rho_k|| <= 1`, `||rho_k(a)|| -> 0` for every `a in I_0`.
- Hence `(rho_k (x) ev_k)(a (x) b) = rho_k(a) (x) b_k` lies in the `c_0`-sum.
- By linearity and continuity, `Theta(I_0 (x)_min B) = 0`.

(b) `||Theta(t~)|| = m`.

- `Theta(t~)` is the class of `(T_k)_k`, where
  `T_k = sum_i pi_k(g_i) (x) conj(pi_k(g_i))`.
- Identify `C^d (x) C^d` with `M_d` via `e_i (x) e_j -> e_i e_j^T`.  Then
  `U (x) conj(U)` acts as `X -> U X U^*`, so it fixes
  `xi_d = d^{-1/2} sum_j e_j (x) e_j` whenever `U` is unitary.
- Hence `T_k xi = m xi`, so `||T_k|| = m` for all `k`.
- The norm in `Q` is `limsup_k ||T_k|| = m`.

(c) For every `kappa` in `I_0 (x)_min B`,
`m = ||Theta(t~ - kappa)|| <= ||t~ - kappa||`.

**Step 6 (inexactness against `B`, and no local lift).**

- If `ker(q_0 (x) id_B) = I_0 (x)_min B`, the induced injective *-homomorphism
  `(C^*(E) (x)_min B)/(I_0 (x)_min B) -> C^*(Lambda) (x)_min B` would be
  isometric.  Then `m <= dist(t~, I_0 (x)_min B) = ||t||_min <= D < m`, which is
  impossible.
- If `s : S_F -> C^*(E)` were a ucp lift, Lemma B with `y = t` and `w = t~` would
  give `dist(t~, I_0 (x)_min B) <= D`, which contradicts Step 5.

**Step 7 (transfer to `B(l^2)`).**

- `P(x) = sum_k p_k x p_k` is a ucp conditional expectation from `B(H) = B(l^2)`
  onto `B`.
- By (MN), `id (x) P : C^*(E) (x)_min B(l^2) -> C^*(E) (x)_min B` is contractive
  and is the identity on `C^*(E) (x)_min B`.
- It maps `I_0 (.) B(l^2)` into `I_0 (.) B`, hence `I_0 (x)_min B(l^2)` into
  `I_0 (x)_min B`.
- So for `kappa in I_0 (x)_min B(l^2)`:
  `||t~ - kappa|| >= ||t~ - (id (x) P)(kappa)|| >= m`.
- By (MN), `||(q_0 (x) id)(t~)||_{C^*(Lambda) (x)_min B(l^2)} = ||t||_min <= D`.
- The argument of Step 6 gives (T1).  QED

**Corollary 1.1 (the quotient-permanence route is closed).**

- Hypothesis (EX) of Lemma A fails for `(C^*(E), I_0)`, and `q_0` is not locally
  split.
- So ISW's non-LLP of `C^*(Lambda)` (Corollary B) cannot be pushed up to
  `C^*(E)` by quotient permanence.  Neither can it be pushed to `C^*(Sp_4(Z))`,
  which contains `C^*(E)` with a conditional expectation (ISW Remark
  `hereditary`).
- Theorem 1 is unconditional, so this holds whether or not `C^*(E)` has the LLP.

**Corollary 1.2 (the lifted witness does not separate max and min).**
`||t~||_{C^*(E) (x)_max B} = ||t~||_{C^*(E) (x)_min B} = m`.

- The upper bound holds because `t~` is a sum of `m` unitaries.
- The lower bound is `||(rho_k (x) ev_k)(t~)|| = ||T_k|| = m`.
- At the fibre `t = 0`, ISW show `||t||_max >= m > D >= ||t||_min` (lines
  716–726).  In the `C(T)`-algebra `C^*(E)`, that defect is compensated by the
  representations `rho_k`, which live over the fibres `e^{2 pi i/k} -> 1`.
- The same compensation occurs for every ISW-type witness whose matrices are
  `sigma_{t_n}`-projective representations.  Such a representation `pi` extends
  to the genuine representation `(m,x) -> e^{2 pi i t_n m} pi(x)` of `E`, and the
  maximally entangled vector gives norm `m` in the min tensor product.
- So a proof that `C^*(E)` fails the LLP needs a witness that is not a lift of
  this kind, for example one built from cocycles on `Lambda` that are not of the
  form `sigma_t`.

## 3A. Theorem 1': every central quotient is min-inexact

**Theorem 1'.**  Let `t in R`, `zeta = e^{2 pi i t}`, and `q_t : C^*(E) -> A_t`
with kernel `I_t` (§1).  Then `ker(q_t (x) id)` on `C^*(E) (x)_min B(l^2)` is
strictly larger than `I_t (x)_min B(l^2)`.  Moreover, for ISW's `g_1, ..., g_m`,
the operator system `span{1, W_t(g_i), W_t(g_i)^*}` of `A_t` admits no ucp lift
to `C^*(E)`.

*Proof.*  Only Steps 1, 4 and 5(a) of Theorem 1 change.

**Step 1'.**  Choose rationals `s_n = p_n / k_n` with `k_n >= 1`, `s_n -> t`, and
`0 < |s_n - t| < 1/2`.

- Reduce `E` modulo `k_n` as in Step 1.  The central element `theta_{k_n}(z)` has
  order `k_n`.
- Its `e^{2 pi i s_n}`-eigenspace `V_n` in `l^2(E_{k_n})` is nonzero and invariant
  (the regular representation of `Z/k_n` contains every character).
- This gives a finite-dimensional unitary representation `rho_n` of `E` with
  `rho_n(z) = e^{2 pi i s_n}`.  Put `d_n = dim V_n`.
- `pi_n := rho_n(0, .)` satisfies `pi_n(x) pi_n(y) = sigma_{s_n}(x,y) pi_n(xy)`,
  as in Step 1.

**Step 3'.**  Let `B = prod_n M_{d_n}`, `w_i = (conj(pi_n(g_i)))_n`, and put:

```text
t~ = sum_i u_{(0,g_i)} (x) w_i,     t_t := (q_t (x) id)(t~) = sum_i W_t(g_i) (x) w_i.
```

**Step 4' (upper bound: `||t_t||_{A_t (x)_min B} <= D`).**

- Let `W_u` be the universal `sigma_t`-representation, which is faithful on
  `A_t`.  As in Step 4,
  `||t_t||_min = sup_n || sum_i W_u(g_i) (x) conj(pi_n(g_i)) ||`.
- The map `x -> W_u(x) (x) conj(pi_n(x))` is a projective representation of
  `Lambda` with 2-cocycle `sigma_t conj(sigma_{s_n}) = sigma_{t - s_n}`.
- On `Z^2`, `x = (e2, e)` and `y = (f2, e)` give `c(x,y) = 1` and `c(y,x) = -1`.
  So `sigma_{t - s_n}(x,y) / sigma_{t - s_n}(y,x) = e^{4 pi i (t - s_n)}`, which is
  not `1` because `0 < |2(t - s_n)| < 1`.
- A 2-coboundary on the abelian group `Z^2` is symmetric, so this cocycle is not a
  coboundary on `Z^2`.
- ISW Claim `gap` (lines 697–704) holds for every projective representation on
  any Hilbert space with this property.  Its proof uses only Theorem `NPS` (ISW
  line 552, [NPS07, Lemma 1.1], stated there for arbitrary projective
  representations) and `pi(e) = 1`, which holds because `c(e, .) = c(., e) = 0`.
  Hence every block is at most `D`.

**Step 5'(a).**  `Theta = corona o (rho_n (x) ev_n)_n` kills `I_t (x)_min B`,
because for `a = (u_z - zeta) x`,
`||rho_n(a)|| <= |e^{2 pi i s_n} - e^{2 pi i t}| ||x|| -> 0`.  The density
argument of Step 5(a) is unchanged.

Steps 5(b), 5(c), 6 and 7 are verbatim, with `rho_n` in place of `rho_k`.  This
gives `dist(t~, I_t (x)_min B(l^2)) >= m > D >= ||t_t||_min`, so (EX) fails for
`I_t`, and Lemma B excludes a ucp lift.  QED

At `t = 0` with `s_n = 1/n` (`n >= 3`), this is Theorem 1.

## 4. Theorem 2: every rational fibre fails the LLP

**Theorem 2.**  Let `t = p/q` be rational (`p in Z`, `q >= 1`).  The fibre
`A_t = C^*(Lambda, sigma_t)` contains a copy of `C^*(Lambda_q) = C^*(Lambda)` as
the range of a ucp conditional expectation.  In particular `A_t` does not have
the LLP.

*Proof.*

**Step 1 (a subgroup where the twist dies).**

- Let `Lambda_q = qZ^2 x| SL_2(Z) <= Lambda`, a subgroup of index `q^2`.
- `(v,g) -> (qv, g)` is an isomorphism `Lambda -> Lambda_q`, because
  `(qv,g)(qw,h) = (q(v + g w), g h)`.
- On `Lambda_q`, `c((qv,g),(qw,h)) = q^2 omega(v, g w)`, so
  `t c = p q omega(v, g w)` is an integer.  Hence

  ```text
  sigma_t |_{Lambda_q x Lambda_q} = 1  identically.                      (4.1)
  ```

- Let `E_q = {(m,x) : x in Lambda_q} <= E`.  It contains `z`, and it is the
  central extension of `Lambda_q` by `<z>` with cocycle `c|_{Lambda_q}`.

**Step 2 (descend the expectation).**

- By ISW Remark `hereditary` (line 327, citing [Pi20, Proposition 3.5]),
  `C^*(E_q)` sits canonically in `C^*(E)`.
- There is a ucp conditional expectation `Phi : C^*(E) -> C^*(E_q)` with
  `Phi(u_gamma) = 1_{E_q}(gamma) u_gamma`.
- `Phi` is `C^*(E_q)`-bimodular.  On group elements this holds because, for
  `gamma_1, gamma_2 in E_q`, `gamma_1 gamma gamma_2 in E_q` iff `gamma in E_q`.
  It extends by linearity and continuity.
- Let `zeta = e^{2 pi i t}`.  Let `I^E` and `I^{E_q}` be the closures of
  `(u_z - zeta) C^*(E)` and `(u_z - zeta) C^*(E_q)`.  These are the ideals of
  (1.1), because `u_z` is central in both algebras.
- Bimodularity gives `Phi(I^E) <= I^{E_q}`, and clearly `I^{E_q} <= I^E`.
- So `Phi` descends to a unital map `Phi- : C^*(E)/I^E -> C^*(E_q)/I^{E_q}`, and
  the inclusion descends to a *-homomorphism
  `iota- : C^*(E_q)/I^{E_q} -> C^*(E)/I^E`, with `Phi- o iota- = id`.
- `Phi-` is completely positive, because positive elements of
  `M_n(A/I) = M_n(A)/M_n(I)` lift to positive elements of `M_n(A)`.
- Hence `iota-` is injective, and `iota- o Phi-` is a ucp conditional expectation
  of `C^*(E)/I^E` onto a copy of `C^*(E_q)/I^{E_q}`.

**Step 3 (identify the two algebras).**

- By (1.1), `C^*(E)/I^E = A_t`.
- By (1.1) applied to `E_q`, `C^*(E_q)/I^{E_q} = C^*(Lambda_q, sigma_t|)`.  By
  (4.1) this is `C^*(Lambda_q)`, which is isomorphic to `C^*(Lambda)`.
- Directly: for a representation `V` of `E_q` with `V(z) = zeta`,
  `V(0,x) V(0,y) = zeta^{c(x,y)} V(0,xy) = V(0,xy)`.

**Step 4.**  `C^*(Lambda)` does not have the LLP (ISW Corollary B, line 243).
By (CE), `A_t` does not have the LLP.  QED

At `t = 1/k` this says the ISW twisted algebras `C^*(Lambda, c_k)` fail the LLP.
They contain `C^*(Lambda)` as the range of a conditional expectation.

**Corollary 2.1 (FFW's fibrewise route is closed on `E`).**

- FFW Corollary `c ext` says: if every fibre `C^*(Lambda; sigma_omega)` has the
  LLP, then so does `C^*Gamma`.
- For the extension `Z -> E -> Lambda`, the fibres at the dense set
  `{e^{2 pi i t} : t in Q}` fail the LLP.  By Theorem 3, every fibre does.
- So that corollary can never certify the LLP for `C^*(E)`, and no LLP proof for
  `C^*(Sp_4(Z))` can pass through `E` by fibres.  Recall that `C^*(E)` having the
  LLP is necessary for `C^*(Sp_4(Z))` to have it, by Remark `hereditary`.

**Corollary 2.2 (what an LLP proof must see).**  Suppose `C^*(Sp_4(Z))` has the
LLP, and hence so does `C^*(E)`.

- By Lemma A and Theorem 2, every rational central quotient
  `0 -> I_t -> C^*(E) -> A_t -> 0` is min-inexact against `B(l^2)`.
- At `t = 0` this is Theorem 1 unconditionally.  So there is no contradiction,
  only a constraint.
- The LLP of `C^*(E)` would have to be proved without fibrewise permanence.
  At each rational `t` there is some `y` in `C^*(E) (.) B(l^2)` with
  `||y_t||_max > ||y_t||_{A_t (x)_min B}`.
- By the criterion (R1) of §5,
  `limsup_{x -> t} ||y_x||_{A_x (x)_min B} >= ||y_t||_max`.  So
  `x -> ||y_x||_{A_x (x)_min B}` fails to be upper semicontinuous at `t`, and the
  missing norm must flow in from neighbouring fibres, exactly as in
  Corollary 1.2.
- By Theorem 3 below, the same holds at every `t in R`, not only at rational `t`.

## 4A. Theorem 3: every fibre fails the LLP

**Theorem 3.**  For every `t in R`, `A_t = C^*(Lambda, sigma_t)` does not have the
LLP.

This is the twisted form of ISW's second proof of Theorem A: the approximating
cocycles converge to `sigma_t` instead of to `1`.

*Proof.*  Take `s_n`, `rho_n`, `pi_n`, `d_n`, `B = prod_n M_{d_n}` and
`w_i = (conj(pi_n(g_i)))_n` as in Theorem 1', Steps 1' and 3'.  Put

```text
y = sum_{i=1}^m W_t(g_i) (x) w_i   in  A_t (.) B.
```

**(i) `||y||_{A_t (x)_min B} <= D`.**  This is Step 4' of Theorem 1'.

**(ii) `||y||_{A_t (x)_max B} >= m`.**

- Let `Q = prod_n (M_{d_n} (x) M_{d_n}) / (+)_{c_0} (M_{d_n} (x) M_{d_n})`, with
  quotient map `eta`.
- For `x in Lambda` put `alpha_0(x) = eta((pi_n(x) (x) 1)_n)`, a unitary in `Q`.
- For `x, y in Lambda`,
  `(pi_n(x) (x) 1)(pi_n(y) (x) 1) - sigma_t(x,y) (pi_n(xy) (x) 1)` has norm
  `|sigma_{s_n}(x,y) - sigma_t(x,y)|`.  This tends to `0`, because
  `c(x,y) in Z` is fixed and `s_n -> t`.
- So `alpha_0(x) alpha_0(y) = sigma_t(x,y) alpha_0(xy)` exactly in `Q`.
- By the universal property of the full twisted group C*-algebra, `alpha_0`
  extends to a *-homomorphism `alpha : A_t -> Q` with `alpha(W_t(x)) = alpha_0(x)`.
  Represent `Q` faithfully on a Hilbert space if needed.
- `beta : B -> Q`, `beta(b) = eta((1 (x) b_n)_n)`, is a *-homomorphism whose range
  commutes with the range of `alpha`.
- By the universal property of `(x)_max`, `alpha x beta` is a *-homomorphism on
  `A_t (x)_max B`, and
  `(alpha x beta)(y) = eta((T_n)_n)` with
  `T_n = sum_i pi_n(g_i) (x) conj(pi_n(g_i))`.
- As in Step 5(b) of Theorem 1, `T_n xi_{d_n} = m xi_{d_n}` for the maximally
  entangled unit vector.  So `||T_n|| = m`, and
  `||y||_max >= ||eta((T_n)_n)|| = limsup_n ||T_n|| = m`.

**(iii) Transfer to `B(l^2)`.**  Let `P : B(l^2) -> B` be the block-diagonal
conditional expectation (Theorem 1, Step 7).

- The max tensor product of ucp maps is ucp, so `id (x) P` is a contraction from
  `A_t (x)_max B(l^2)` to `A_t (x)_max B`.  It fixes `y`, so
  `||y||_{A_t (x)_max B(l^2)} >= m`.
- By (MN), `||y||_{A_t (x)_min B(l^2)} = ||y||_{A_t (x)_min B} <= D < m`.
- So `A_t (x)_max B(l^2) != A_t (x)_min B(l^2)`, and by (K) `A_t` fails the LLP.
  QED

**Remarks on Theorem 3.**

- At `t = 0` with `s_n = 1/n`, this is ISW's proof of Corollary B for
  `Z^2 x| SL_2(Z)`.
- At rational `t`, Theorem 2 gives more: `A_t` contains `C^*(Lambda)` as the range
  of a ucp conditional expectation.
- **Crossed-product form.**  We have `c((0,g),(0,h)) = 0` and
  `c((v,e),(0,g)) = 0`.  So `W_t(v,g) = W_t(v,e) W_t(0,g)`, `g -> W_t(0,g)` is a
  genuine representation, and
  `W_t(0,g) W_t(w,e) W_t(0,g)^* = W_t(gw, e)`.
  - The Weyl-normalized unitaries `W_t(v,e)` satisfy
    `W_t(v,e) W_t(w,e) = e^{pi i (2t) omega(v,w)} W_t(v+w, e)`.  They generate the
    rotation algebra `A_{2t}`, with `W_t(e2) W_t(f2) = e^{4 pi i t} W_t(f2) W_t(e2)`.
  - The action `W(v) -> W(gv)` is well defined, because `omega` is
    `SL_2(Z)`-invariant.
  - A covariant pair `(U, V)` gives the `sigma_t`-representation
    `W(v,g) = U(v) V(g)`.  Check:
    `U(v)V(g)U(w)V(h) = U(v)U(gw)V(gh) = sigma_t((v,g),(w,h)) W(v+gw, gh)`.
    Conversely, a `sigma_t`-representation restricts to a covariant pair.
    So covariant pairs correspond exactly to `sigma_t`-representations of
    `Lambda`.
  - So `A_t` is the full crossed product of the rotation algebra `A_{2t}` by
    `SL_2(Z)`.
  - Theorem 3 therefore says: the full crossed product of every rotation algebra
    by the canonical `SL_2(Z)`-action fails the LLP, including irrational
    rotation algebras.
  - This identification is only a reformulation; the proof above does not use
    it.  No literature search on this form was possible this session (see the
    notes file), so no novelty is claimed.
- **Obstruction to the root.**  Every fibre of the `C(T)`-algebra `C^*(E)` fails
  the LLP.  So no fibrewise-permanence argument (FFW Corollary `c ext` or any
  variant with a dense set of good fibres) can give the LLP for `C^*(E)`, and
  hence none can give it for `C^*(Sp_4(Z))`.
  - If `C^*(E)` has the LLP, then by Lemma A and Theorem 3 every central quotient
    is min-inexact.  This is consistent with Theorem 1' and does not refute it.
  - Also by R1, norm inflow from neighbouring fibres is then needed at every
    point of `T`.

## 5. Remarks

**(R1) Fibrewise LLP criterion.**  Let `A` be a unital separable `C(X)`-algebra
with fibres `A_x`, and let `B = B(l^2)`.  Then `A` has the LLP iff, for every `y`
in `A (.) B` and every `x0 in X`,

```text
|| y_{x0} ||_{A_{x0} (x)_max B}  <=  inf_{U ni x0} sup_{x in U} || y_x ||_{A_x (x)_min B}.
```

*Proof.*

1. `A (x)_max B` is a `C(X)`-algebra with fibres `A_x (x)_max B`, by (MX), since
   `C_0(X \ x) A` is an ideal.
2. In any `C(X)`-algebra the norm is the supremum of the fibre norms.
3. `||y||_min = sup_x ||y_x||_{A_x (x)_min B}`, since irreducible representations
   of `A` factor through fibres and the min norm is a supremum over pairs of
   representations.
4. (=>) Take `f in C(X)`, `0 <= f <= 1`, `f(x0) = 1`, supported in `U`.  Then
   `||y_{x0}||_max <= ||f y||_max = ||f y||_min <= sup_{x in U} ||y_x||_min`.
5. (<=) Take the maximum over `x0` of the left side.  This gives
   `||y||_max <= ||y||_min`.  Now apply (K).  QED

For `A = C^*(E)` and `X = T`, Theorem 2 gives, at every rational point `x0`,
some `y` whose left side exceeds `||y_{x0}||_{A_{x0} (x)_min B}`.  So at rational
points the LLP of `C^*(E)` amounts to norm inflow from neighbouring fibres.
ISW-type witnesses do have this inflow (Corollary 1.2).

**(R2) What is not proved.**

- The LLP status of `C^*(E)` and of `C^*(Sp_4(Z))`.
