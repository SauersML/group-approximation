# Amenable Ore compressor slices of the Kun--Thom Clifford crossed product are Connes embeddable (2026-09-16)

Lane: swarm attack on `kun-thom-clifford-crossed-product-ce`.  This artifact
holds the complete proofs for

- `separable-stabilizer-clifford-crossed-products-are-ce` (Theorem A,
  Section 1);
- `kt-amenable-compressor-clifford-crossed-products-are-ce` (Theorem B,
  Sections 2--4), with its localization corollary (Section 5).

Nothing here decides the target claim.  What is proved is this.  Take the
Kun--Thom Clifford crossed product `M = Cl(G/Gamma) rtimes G` with the exact
anticommutation relations and the canonical trace.  Its subalgebra
generated by `Cl(G/Gamma)` and the unitaries of `<Gamma, S>` is Connes
embeddable for every left Ore monoid `S` of positive compressors whose
generated group is amenable.  This covers one compressor, any commuting
family of compressors, and the Heisenberg pair `I+E_12, I+E_23`.  The
subalgebra generated by `Cl(G/Gamma)` and the actor group `SL_d(Z)` is Connes
embeddable as well.  All of these are statements about the **Clifford
crossed product** itself, not about permutation or Pauli models.  The
permutation-level, Pauli-level and finitary analogues were already in the
graph (see Section 5 of the notes file for the audit).

## 0. Conventions and standard facts

**Kun--Thom data.**  Let `k` be a finite field, `r >= 3` and `d >= 1`.  Put

```text
R_0 = k[N^d] = k[x_1,...,x_d],     R = k[Z^d] = k[x_1^(+-1),...,x_d^(+-1)],
Gamma = EL_r(R_0),   E = EL_r(R),   G = E rtimes SL_d(Z),   X = G/Gamma.
```

The group `SL_d(Z)` acts on `R` by the ring automorphisms
`phi_L(x^z) = x^(Lz)`, and on `E` entrywise.  Elements of `G` are written
`(e, L)`, with `(e,L)(e',L') = (e phi_L(e'), LL')`.  We identify `e` with
`(e,1)` and `L` with `(1,L)`.  Then

```text
L e L^(-1) = phi_L(e),        g Gamma g^(-1) = Stab_G(g Gamma).
```

Put `L_+ = SL_d(Z) intersect M_d(N)`.  For `s in L_+` we have
`s N^d subset N^d`, so

```text
s Gamma s^(-1) = phi_s(EL_r(R_0)) = EL_r(k[s N^d]) <= Gamma,
s^(-1) Gamma s = EL_r(k[s^(-1) N^d]) >= Gamma.
```

The target instance is `k = F_2`, `r = d = 3`.  Every proof below works for
the general parameters.

For `z in Z^d` let `kappa_z : R -> k` be the coefficient of `x^z`.  The
support of `f in R` is `{z : kappa_z(f) != 0}`.

**Suslin's theorem.**  For a field `k`, `r >= 3` and a polynomial ring `A`
over `k` in finitely many variables, `SL_r(A) = EL_r(A)`.  Source: A. A.
Suslin, *On the structure of the special linear group over polynomial
rings*, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR 1977.  It is quoted as standard and was not
re-fetched in this session.  It is used only through the following
consequence.  For every `L in SL_d(Z)` the monoid `L N^d` is freely
generated by the columns of `L`, so `k[L N^d]` is a polynomial ring.  Hence

```text
EL_r(k[L N^d]) = { A in SL_r(R) : every entry of A lies in k[L N^d] }.   (0.1)
```

**Clifford algebras.**  For a countable set `X`, `Cl(X)` denotes the tracial
von Neumann algebra generated by self-adjoint unitaries `c_x` (`x in X`)
subject to

```text
c_x^2 = 1,        c_x c_y = - c_y c_x   (x != y).
```

Its canonical trace satisfies `tau(c_{x_1} ... c_{x_k}) = 0` for pairwise
distinct `x_1,...,x_k` with `k >= 1`.  When `X` is infinite this is the
hyperfinite `II_1` factor.  A permutation action `H action X` gives
trace-preserving Bogoliubov automorphisms `alpha_h(c_x) = c_{hx}`.  The
crossed product `Cl(X) rtimes H` carries the canonical trace
`tau(a u_h) = tau(a) delta_{h,e}`.

For `H <= G` and `X = G/Gamma` (or any `G`-set), write

```text
M_H = ( Cl(X) union { u_h : h in H } )''   inside   M = Cl(X) rtimes G,
```

with the restricted trace.  All trace computations below are done in `M`.
No identification of `M_H` with an abstract crossed product is needed.

For a finite set `Y`, `Cl(Y)` is the finite-dimensional complex Clifford
algebra with the same relations.  It is realized on `(C^2)^(tensor |Y|)` by
Jordan--Wigner matrices, and it has the linear basis `c_T` (`T subset Y`),
the ordered products for a fixed ordering of `Y`.  The functional
`tau_Y(c_T) = delta_{T,empty}` is tracial.  It is also faithful, because
`tau_Y(c_T^* c_{T'}) = delta_{T,T'}`.

**(S1) Moment criterion.**  Let `(M,tau)` be a tracial von Neumann algebra
generated by a countable set `S` of contractions.  Then `M` is Connes
embeddable, meaning it embeds trace-preservingly in `R^omega`, if and only if
the following holds.  For every finite `S_0 subset S`, every `n`, and every
`eps > 0`, there are `D` and contractions `(b_s)_{s in S_0}` in `M_D(C)` with

```text
| tr_D(w(b)) - tau(w(s)) | < eps
```

for every `*`-monomial `w` of length at most `n`.  In the "only if"
direction the same holds for any finite set of contractions of a Connes
embeddable algebra, whether or not it generates.  This is the standard
microstate form of Connes embeddability.  Textbook references are
Brown--Ozawa, *C\*-algebras and finite-dimensional approximations* (GSM 88),
and Capraro--Lupini, *Introduction to sofic and hyperlinear groups and
Connes' embedding conjecture* (LNM 2136).  Neither book was re-fetched in
this session.

**(S1')**  In (S1) the matrices may be replaced by contractions in any Connes
embeddable tracial algebra `(N,tau_N)`.  To see this, apply the "only if"
direction of (S1) inside `N` to the finitely many chosen contractions, with
`eps/2`, and compose.  Examples of such `N`: finite-dimensional C\*-algebras
with a faithful tracial state (S3), and `M_m(C) tensor N_0` with trace
`tr_m tensor tau_0` for Connes embeddable `N_0`, since
`M_m(R^omega) = (M_m(R))^omega` and `M_m(R)` is isomorphic to `R`.

**(S2) Heredity.**  A von Neumann subalgebra of a Connes embeddable algebra,
with the restricted trace, is Connes embeddable.  An isomorphism of tracial
algebras preserves Connes embeddability.  In particular, for `g in G`,
`Ad u_g` maps `M_H` onto `M_{gHg^(-1)}`, because `Ad u_g (Cl(X)) = Cl(X)`
and `Ad u_g (u_h) = u_{ghg^(-1)}`.  Hence `M_H` is Connes embeddable if and
only if `M_{gHg^(-1)}` is.  Also, `M_K` is Connes embeddable whenever
`K <= H` and `M_H` is.

**(S3)**  A finite-dimensional C\*-algebra `B = direct_sum_i M_{n_i}(C)`,
with a faithful tracial state of weights `lambda_i`, embeds
trace-preservingly in the hyperfinite factor `R`.  Choose orthogonal
projections `p_i in R` with `tau(p_i) = lambda_i` and `sum p_i = 1`.  Embed
`M_{n_i}(C)` unitally in `p_i R p_i`, which is isomorphic to `R`.  The traces
agree by uniqueness of the tracial state on `M_{n_i}(C)`.  So `B` is Connes
embeddable.

**(S4) Word reduction in Clifford systems.**  Let `y_1,...,y_m` be labels,
and consider `P = c_{y_1} ... c_{y_m}` in any algebra satisfying the Clifford
relations.  The **equality pattern** of the word is the set
`{(i,j) : y_i = y_j}`.

- *Odd case.*  Suppose some label occurs an odd number of times.  Sort the
  word by adjacent transpositions and cancel squares.  This gives
  `P = +- c_T`, where `T != empty` is the set of labels of odd multiplicity.
  Then `tau(P) = 0` in `Cl(X)`, and `tau_Y(P) = 0` in `Cl(Y)`.
- *Even case.*  Suppose every label occurs an even number of times.  Let `j`
  be the next position with `y_j = y_1`.  Move `c_{y_1}` rightward past
  `c_{y_2},...,c_{y_{j-1}}`.  Each of these letters is different from `y_1`,
  so the move costs `(-1)^(j-2)`.  Cancel `c_{y_1}^2 = 1`, and recurse on the
  remaining word of length `m-2` with its inherited pattern.  The result is
  `P = sigma 1` with `sigma in {+-1}`, and `sigma` is computed from the
  equality pattern alone.

Whether the odd case or the even case holds is itself read off from the
pattern.  Consequently `tau(c_{y_1} ... c_{y_m})` is a function of the
equality pattern only.  It is the same function in `Cl(X)` for every `X` and
in `Cl(Y)` for every finite `Y`.

## 1. Theorem A: separable stabilizers give Connes embeddable Clifford crossed products

**Theorem A.**  Let `H` be a countable residually finite group, and let `X`
be a countable `H`-set in which every point stabilizer `H_x` is separable in
`H`, i.e.

```text
H_x = intersection over finite-index normal N <| H of  H_x N.
```

Then `Cl(X) rtimes H`, with its canonical trace, is Connes embeddable.

The hypotheses pass to subgroups.  If `K <= H`, then `K` is residually
finite and `K_x = K intersect H_x` is separable in `K`: intersect with `K`
the finite-index normal subgroups that separate `H_x`.

The proof uses only the relations `u_h c_x u_h^* = c_{hx}` and the trace
formula `tau(a u_h) = tau(a) delta_{h,e}`.  So it applies verbatim to
`M_H`, for `H <= G` acting on `X = G/Gamma`, with the trace computed in `M`.

*Proof.*  The algebra is generated by the countable set of contractions
`{c_x : x in X} union {u_h : h in H}`.  Fix a finite set `X_0 subset X`, a
finite set `F_0 subset H`, a length `n`, and `eps > 0`.  We apply (S1') with
a finite-dimensional model and obtain **exact** equality of all moments of
length at most `n`.

*Normal form.*  A `*`-monomial of length at most `n` in the letters `c_x`
(`x in X_0`) and `u_h, u_h^* = u_{h^(-1)}` (`h in F_0`) can be rewritten in
`M` by pushing every group unitary to the right, using
`u_h c_x = c_{hx} u_h`.  This gives

```text
w = c_{y_1} c_{y_2} ... c_{y_m} u_q,    y_j = a_j x_{i_j},              (1.1)
```

where `x_{i_j} in X_0` is the `j`-th Clifford letter and `a_j` is the
product of the group letters standing to its left.  Also `q` is the product
of all group letters, and `m <= n`.  Therefore

```text
tau(w) = delta_{q,e} tau(c_{y_1} ... c_{y_m}).                          (1.2)
```

Choose representatives `x_o` of the finitely many `H`-orbits that meet
`X_0`, and write each `x in X_0` as `x = b_x x_{o(x)}`.  Then
`y_j = (a_j b_{x_{i_j}}) x_{o(x_{i_j})}`.  For two positions `j,k` with the
same orbit label `o`, we have `y_j = y_k` if and only if

```text
d_{jk} := (a_k b_{x_{i_k}})^(-1) (a_j b_{x_{i_j}})  in  H_{x_o}.
```

Only finitely many words `w` occur.  So only finitely many elements `q` and
`d_{jk}` occur.

*Choice of the finite quotient.*  Let `D_bad` be the finite set of the
occurring elements `d_{jk}` that lie outside the relevant `H_{x_o}`.  Let
`Q_bad` be the finite set of the occurring `q != e`.  By separability,
for each `d in D_bad` there is a finite-index normal `N_d` with
`d notin H_{x_o} N_d`.  By residual finiteness, for each `q in Q_bad` there
is a finite-index normal `N_q` with `q notin N_q`.  Let `N` be the
intersection of all of them.  Then `N` is a finite-index normal subgroup,
`H_{x_o} N subset H_{x_o} N_d`, and so

```text
d notin H_{x_o} N   (d in D_bad),        q notin N   (q in Q_bad).       (1.3)
```

*The model.*  Put `Q = H/N` and let `pi : H -> Q` be the quotient map.  Let
`Y` be the disjoint union, over the chosen orbit representatives, of the
finite sets `H/(H_{x_o} N)`.  Since `N` is normal, `N` acts trivially on
`Y`, so `Q` acts on `Y`.  The map

```text
pi_X : h x_o |-> h H_{x_o} N
```

is well defined on the orbits meeting `X_0` and `H`-equivariant.  Let
`B = Cl(Y) rtimes Q` be the algebraic crossed product for the Bogoliubov
action, with the functional `tau_B(sum_q a_q u_q) = tau_Y(a_e)`.  Then `B`
is a finite-dimensional algebra with involution, and `tau_B` is tracial.
It is faithful because

```text
tau_B(x^* x) = sum_q tau_Y(a_q^* a_q)   for   x = sum_q a_q u_q.
```

The left regular representation on `L^2(B, tau_B)` makes `B` a
finite-dimensional C\*-algebra with a faithful tracial state.  By (S3) it is
Connes embeddable.  Send `c_x |-> c_{pi_X(x)}` and `u_h |-> u_{pi(h)}`.
These are contractions in `B`.

*Moments agree exactly.*  The relation
`u_{pi(h)} c_{pi_X(x)} = c_{pi_X(hx)} u_{pi(h)}` holds in `B`.  So the model
word is

```text
c_{pi_X(y_1)} ... c_{pi_X(y_m)} u_{pi(q)},
tau_B = delta_{pi(q),e} tau_Y(c_{pi_X(y_1)} ... c_{pi_X(y_m)}).
```

We compare the two factors.

- By (1.3), `pi(q) = e` if and only if `q = e`.
- Take positions `j,k`.  If they carry different orbit labels, then
  `y_j != y_k` and `pi_X(y_j) != pi_X(y_k)`, since the two points lie in
  different pieces of `Y`.  If they carry the same label, equivariance gives
  `y_j = y_k => pi_X(y_j) = pi_X(y_k)`.  Conversely, if `y_j != y_k`, then
  `d_{jk} in D_bad`, and (1.3) gives `pi_X(y_j) != pi_X(y_k)`.

So the labels `pi_X(y_j)` have the same equality pattern as the `y_j`.  By
(S4) the Clifford factors have the same trace.  Hence `tau_B(model word)`
equals `tau(w)` for every `*`-monomial of length at most `n`, and (S1')
finishes the proof.  QED.

**Remark 1.1 (free actions).**  If `H` is residually finite and acts freely,
the stabilizers are trivial and hence separable.  So `Cl(X) rtimes H` is
Connes embeddable.  This will be applied to the actor group
`SL_d(Z) <= G` (Section 4.5).

**Remark 1.2 (why not directly `G`).**  Theorem A cannot be applied to
`H = G` acting on `G/Gamma`.  In every compact image of `G` the closure of
`Gamma` is normal (`infranormal-compact-closure-normal`), while `Gamma` is
not normal in `G`.  So `Gamma` is not separable in `G`.

## 2. Lemma B: the base group has separable stabilizers

**Lemma B.**

1. `Gamma` is residually finite.
2. For every `g in G`, the stabilizer `Gamma intersect g Gamma g^(-1)` of
   the point `g Gamma` is separable in `Gamma`.

Consequently, by Theorem A, `M_Gamma` is Connes embeddable.

This is the `j = 0` level of the stabilizer separation lemma of
`kt-one-compressor-full-coset-action-is-sofic`.  That route uses total-degree
truncation.  We repeat the argument here with coordinate truncation so that
this artifact is self-contained.

*Truncation ideals.*  For `M >= 1` let `I_M subset R_0` be the ideal
spanned by the monomials `x^w`, `w in N^d`, with `max_i w_i >= M`.  The
quotient `R_0/I_M` has `k`-basis `x^w` with `0 <= w_i < M`, so it is finite.
Hence

```text
Gamma(I_M) = ker( Gamma -> SL_r(R_0/I_M) )
```

is a finite-index normal subgroup of `Gamma`.

*Proof of 1.*  If `gamma != I`, some entry of `gamma - I` has a nonzero
coefficient `kappa_w` with `w in N^d`.  For `M > max_i w_i` that coefficient
survives in `R_0/I_M`, so `gamma notin Gamma(I_M)`.

*Proof of 2.*  Write `g = (e, L) = e L` with `e in E` and `L in SL_d(Z)`.
For `gamma in Gamma` we have `g^(-1) gamma g = phi_{L^(-1)}(e^(-1) gamma e)`,
an element of `E`.  It lies in `Gamma = EL_r(R_0)` if and only if
`e^(-1) gamma e in EL_r(k[L N^d])`.  By (0.1), which is where Suslin's
theorem enters, this holds if and only if every entry of `e^(-1) gamma e`
lies in `k[L N^d]`.  Thus

```text
gamma in Stab := Gamma intersect g Gamma g^(-1)
  <=>  kappa_z( (e^(-1) gamma e)_{ab} ) = 0   for all a,b and all z notin L N^d.   (2.1)
```

Without Suslin's theorem, the right-hand side of (2.1) would only describe
membership in `SL_r(R_0)`, which could a priori be larger than `Gamma`.

Let `U` be the union of the supports of the entries of `e^(-1)`, and `V`
the union of the supports of the entries of `e`.  Both are finite.  For any
matrix `A in M_r(R)`,

```text
kappa_z( (e^(-1) A e)_{ab} )
  = sum_{c,d} sum_{u in U, v in V} kappa_u((e^(-1))_{ac}) kappa_{z-u-v}(A_{cd}) kappa_v(e_{db}).   (2.2)
```

So this coefficient is a linear function of the finitely many coefficients
`kappa_w(A_{cd})` with `w in W_z := z - U - V`.

Now let `gamma_0 in Gamma minus Stab`.  By (2.1) there are `a,b` and
`z_0 notin L N^d` with `kappa_{z_0}((e^(-1) gamma_0 e)_{ab}) != 0`.  Choose
`M` larger than every coordinate of every `w in W_{z_0} intersect N^d`.
Suppose, for contradiction, that `gamma_0 in Stab * Gamma(I_M)`, say
`gamma_0 = s n` with `s in Stab` and `n in Gamma(I_M)`.  Then
`gamma_0 - s = s (n - I)` has all entries in `I_M`, because `s` has entries
in `R_0` and `I_M` is an ideal.  Every entry of `gamma_0 - s` lies in `R_0`,
so its coefficients vanish off `N^d`.  The coefficients at
`w in W_{z_0} intersect N^d` vanish because `max_i w_i < M` and the entry
lies in `I_M`.  By (2.2) with `A = gamma_0 - s`,

```text
kappa_{z_0}((e^(-1) gamma_0 e)_{ab}) = kappa_{z_0}((e^(-1) s e)_{ab}) = 0,
```

the last equality by (2.1), since `s in Stab`.  This is a contradiction.  So
`gamma_0 notin Stab * Gamma(I_M)`, and the subgroup `Stab * Gamma(I_M)` has
finite index.  Hence

```text
Stab = intersection over M of  Stab * Gamma(I_M),
```

and `Stab` is separable.  QED.

## 3. Two permanence lemmas

**Lemma C (directed unions).**  Let `H = union_{i in I} H_i` be an upward
directed union of subgroups of `G`.  If every `M_{H_i}` is Connes embeddable,
then so is `M_H`.

*Proof.*  The algebra `M_H` is generated by the countable set of contractions
`S = {c_x : x in X} union {u_h : h in H}`.  A finite `S_0 subset S` involves
finitely many `h`, and by directedness they all lie in one `H_i`.  So
`S_0 subset M_{H_i}`.  The "only if" direction of (S1) for the Connes
embeddable algebra `M_{H_i}` gives microstates for `S_0`.  The traces agree,
because `M_{H_i}` carries the restricted trace.  By the "if" direction of
(S1), `M_H` is Connes embeddable.  QED.

**Lemma D (amenable extensions).**  Let `(M,tau)` be a tracial von Neumann
algebra and `N subset M` a von Neumann subalgebra, generated by a countable
set `S_N` of contractions.  Let `P` be a countable amenable group and
`p |-> u_p` a unitary representation of `P` in `M` such that

```text
u_p N u_p^* = N,        tau(x u_p) = 0    (x in N, p != e).             (3.1)
```

If `N` is Connes embeddable, then `N_P := (N union {u_p : p in P})''` is
Connes embeddable.

*Proof.*  Write `alpha_p = Ad u_p`.  It restricts to a trace-preserving
automorphism of `N`.  The algebra `N_P` is generated by the countable set
`S_N union {u_p}`.

Fix finite `S_0 subset S_N`, a finite set `F_0 subset P`, a length `n`, and
`eps > 0`.  Let `K_n` be the finite set of products of at most `n` elements
of `F_0 union F_0^(-1)`, and put `delta = eps/(n+1)`.  By amenability (the
Folner condition) there is a finite nonempty `F subset P` with

```text
|kF triangle F| <= delta |F|        for all k in K_n.                    (3.2)
```

*The model.*  Let `N' = M_F(C) tensor N`, with trace `tr_F tensor tau`,
where `tr_F` is the normalized trace.  By (S1') `N'` is a legitimate Connes
embeddable target.  Define

```text
D(b) = sum_{f in F} e_{ff} tensor alpha_{f^(-1)}(b)        (b in N),
```

a unital trace-preserving `*`-homomorphism `N -> N'`.  For each
`p in F_0`, choose a permutation `sigma_p` of `F` that agrees with
`f |-> pf` on `F intersect p^(-1)F`, and put

```text
V_p = sum_{f in F} e_{sigma_p(f), f} tensor 1.
```

Then `V_p^* = V_p^(-1)` is the permutation `sigma_p^(-1)`.  It agrees with
`f |-> p^(-1) f` on `F intersect pF`, which is the image of
`F intersect p^(-1)F` under `f |-> pf`.  Put `V_e = 1`.  The model sends
`b |-> D(b)` for `b in S_0` and `u_p |-> V_p` for `p in F_0`, and hence the
adjoint letter `u_p^*` to `V_p^*`.

The only property of the model permutations used below is this: the
permutation attached to a letter with group element `q` agrees with
`f |-> qf` on `F intersect q^(-1)F`.  If `F_0` contains an element together
with its inverse, or an involution, then two different letters can carry the
same group element and different permutations.  The analysis below runs
letter by letter, so this causes no ambiguity.

*Normal form.*  Any `*`-monomial of length at most `n` in these letters can
be written as

```text
w = b_1 u_{q_1} b_2 u_{q_2} ... b_m u_{q_m},
```

with the following data.

- Each `b_i` is a product of letters from `S_0 union S_0^*`, possibly empty,
  in which case `b_i = 1`.
- Each `q_i in F_0 union F_0^(-1) union {e}`, and at most `n` of the `q_i`
  are different from `e`.

Every group letter is kept as its own `q_i`.  Consecutive group letters are
separated by `b_i = 1`, and a trailing Clifford block is closed off with
`q_m = e`.  The model word is then exactly

```text
W = D(b_1) V_{q_1} D(b_2) V_{q_2} ... D(b_m) V_{q_m},
```

because `D` is multiplicative and `*`-preserving, with `V_{p^(-1)} := V_p^*`.
Using (3.1),

```text
tau(w) = tau( b_1 alpha_{q_1}(b_2) alpha_{q_1 q_2}(b_3) ... alpha_{q_1...q_(m-1)}(b_m) u_{q_1...q_m} )
       = delta_{q_1...q_m, e} tau( b_1 alpha_{q_1}(b_2) ... alpha_{q_1...q_(m-1)}(b_m) ).      (3.3)
```

*Computing the model trace.*  Write `sigma_{q_i}` for the permutation
underlying the model letter at position `i`, with `sigma_{q_i} = id` when
`q_i = e`.  Fix `f in F` and define the trajectory

```text
g_m = f,        g_(i-1) = sigma_{q_i}(g_i)        (i = m, ..., 1).
```

A direct multiplication gives

```text
W = sum_{f in F} e_{g_0(f), f} tensor alpha_{g_0^(-1)}(b_1) alpha_{g_1^(-1)}(b_2) ... alpha_{g_(m-1)^(-1)}(b_m),
```

so

```text
(tr_F tensor tau)(W) = (1/|F|) sum_{f : g_0(f) = f} tau( alpha_{g_0^(-1)}(b_1) ... alpha_{g_(m-1)^(-1)}(b_m) ).
```

Call `f` *good* if every step is a genuine left multiplication, i.e.
`g_(i-1) = q_i g_i` for all `i`.  Otherwise call `f` *bad*.

- *Good `f`.*  We have `g_i = q_(i+1) ... q_m f` and `g_0 = q_1 ... q_m f`.
  Since `P` acts freely on itself, `g_0 = f` if and only if
  `q_1 ... q_m = e`.  In that case
  `(q_i ... q_m)^(-1) = q_1 ... q_(i-1)`, so

  ```text
  g_(i-1)^(-1) = f^(-1) q_1 ... q_(i-1),
  ```

  and the summand is
  `tau(alpha_{f^(-1)}(b_1 alpha_{q_1}(b_2) ...)) = tau(b_1 alpha_{q_1}(b_2) ...)`.
  Comparing with (3.3), every good `f` contributes exactly `tau(w)/|F|`,
  whether or not `q_1 ... q_m = e`.
- *Bad `f`.*  Let `i` be the largest index at which the step fails.  Then
  `q_i != e`, `g_i = q_(i+1) ... q_m f in F`, and `q_i g_i notin F`.  So
  `k f notin F` for `k = q_i q_(i+1) ... q_m`, a product of at most `n`
  letters, i.e. `k in K_n`.  Hence, by (3.2),

  ```text
  #bad <= sum_{i : q_i != e} |{f in F : q_i...q_m f notin F}| <= n max_{k in K_n} |kF minus F| <= n delta |F| / 2,
  ```

  using `|kF minus F| = |kF triangle F|/2`.  Each bad summand has absolute
  value at most `1`, because the `b_i` are contractions.

Therefore

```text
| (tr_F tensor tau)(W) - tau(w) | <= (1/|F|) sum_{f bad} ( 1 + |tau(w)| ) <= 2 #bad/|F| <= n delta < eps.
```

The model elements are contractions in the Connes embeddable algebra `N'`.
By (S1'), `N_P` is Connes embeddable.  QED.

Lemma D is presumably folklore (Connes embeddability passes to crossed
products by amenable groups).  No source was fetched for it, so a
self-contained proof is given instead of a citation.

## 4. Theorem B: amenable Ore compressor slices

Throughout this section, `S subset L_+` is a submonoid, so `I in S`.  It is
**left Ore** if `Ss intersect Ss' != empty` for all `s,s' in S`.  Put

```text
P = <S> <= SL_d(Z),      N_S = union_{s in S} s^(-1) Gamma s <= E,      H_S = N_S P.
```

### 4.1 The Ore telescope

**Lemma 4.1.**  Let `S` be left Ore.

1. The family `(s^(-1) Gamma s)_{s in S}` is upward directed, so `N_S` is a
   subgroup of `E`.
2. `P` normalizes `N_S`.  Hence `H_S = N_S rtimes P` is a subgroup of `G`
   and `H_S intersect E = N_S`.
3. `H_S = <Gamma, S>`.

*Proof.*

1. Given `s,s' in S`, choose `a,b in S` with `t := as = bs'`.  Since
   `a in L_+`, we have `a^(-1) Gamma a >= Gamma`.  Hence
   `t^(-1) Gamma t = s^(-1)(a^(-1) Gamma a) s >= s^(-1) Gamma s`, and in the
   same way `t^(-1) Gamma t >= s'^(-1) Gamma s'`.  A directed union of
   subgroups is a subgroup.
2. Fix `s in S`.  For `s' in S` we have
   `s^(-1)(s'^(-1) Gamma s') s = (s's)^(-1) Gamma (s's) subset N_S`.  So
   `s^(-1) N_S s subset N_S`.  For the reverse inclusion, choose
   `a,b in S` with `a s' = b s`, so that `s s'^(-1) = b^(-1) a`.  Then
   `s (s'^(-1) Gamma s') s^(-1) = b^(-1)(a Gamma a^(-1)) b subset b^(-1) Gamma b subset N_S`,
   because `a Gamma a^(-1) <= Gamma`.  So `s N_S s^(-1) subset N_S`.  The two
   inclusions give `s N_S s^(-1) = N_S`.  Hence every element of `P`
   normalizes `N_S`.  Since `N_S <= E`, `P <= SL_d(Z)` and
   `E intersect SL_d(Z) = 1`, the product `N_S P` is the internal semidirect
   product, and `(n p in E) <=> p = 1`.
3. The group `<Gamma, S>` contains every `s^(-1) Gamma s` and `P`, so it
   contains `H_S`.  Conversely, `H_S` contains `Gamma = I^(-1) Gamma I` and
   `S`.  QED.

### 4.2 The theorem

**Theorem B.**  Let `S subset L_+` be a left Ore submonoid such that
`P = <S>` is amenable.  Then

```text
M_{<Gamma,S>} = ( Cl(G/Gamma) union { u_h : h in <Gamma,S> } )''
```

is Connes embeddable with the restricted canonical trace.  Hence `M_K` is
Connes embeddable for every subgroup `K` of every conjugate
`g <Gamma,S> g^(-1)`.

*Proof.*

- By Lemma B and Theorem A, `M_Gamma` is Connes embeddable.
- By (S2), each `M_{s^(-1) Gamma s} = Ad u_{s^(-1)}(M_Gamma)` is Connes
  embeddable.
- By Lemma 4.1(1) and Lemma C, `M_{N_S}` is Connes embeddable.
- Apply Lemma D with `N = M_{N_S}` and the representation `p |-> u_p` of
  `P`.  Here `N` is generated by the countable set of contractions
  `{c_x} union {u_n : n in N_S}`.  We check (3.1).  First,
  `u_p M_{N_S} u_p^* = M_{p N_S p^(-1)} = M_{N_S}` by Lemma 4.1(2).
  Second, for `p != e`, the functional `x |-> tau(x u_p)` is normal.  On the
  weakly dense `*`-subalgebra spanned by the `a u_n`
  (`a in Cl(X)`, `n in N_S`) it takes the values
  `tau(a u_{np}) = tau(a) delta_{np,e} = 0`, because `np = e` would force
  `p = n^(-1) in E intersect SL_d(Z) = 1`.  So it vanishes on `M_{N_S}`.
- Since `H_S = N_S P`, the algebra generated by `M_{N_S}` and `u_P` is
  `M_{H_S}`.  By Lemma D and Lemma 4.1(3), `M_{<Gamma,S>}` is Connes
  embeddable.
- The last sentence of the theorem follows from (S2).  QED.

### 4.3 A growth criterion for the Ore condition

**Lemma 4.2.**  Let `S` be a submonoid of a group, and suppose that for all
`s,s' in S` there is an `n` such that the `2^n` positive words of length `n`
in `s,s'` represent fewer than `2^n` distinct elements.  Then `S` is left
Ore.

*Proof.*  If `s = s'`, then `s in Ss intersect Ss'`.  Otherwise take two
different words `u = u_1...u_n` and `v = v_1...v_n` with the same value.
Let `j` be the largest position with `u_j != v_j`.  Cancel the common
suffix in the group.  This gives `u_1...u_j = v_1...v_j` with
`{u_j, v_j} = {s, s'}`, so `(u_1...u_(j-1)) s = (v_1...v_(j-1)) s'`, up to
renaming.  The prefixes lie in `S` (an empty prefix is `I`).  QED.

### 4.4 Instances

**(i) One compressor.**  Take `A in L_+`, for example `I + E_ij` with
`i != j`, and `S = {A^j : j >= 0}`.  This monoid is commutative, hence left
Ore, and `P = <A>` is cyclic.  So `M_{<Gamma,A>}` is Connes embeddable.

Now suppose `A N^d != N^d`.  Then `A` has infinite order, because `A^m = I`
would give `N^d = A^m N^d subset A N^d`.  In this case `<Gamma, A>` is the
ascending HNN extension
`V_A = <Gamma, t | t gamma t^(-1) = phi_A(gamma)>`.  To see this, the
natural map `V_A -> <Gamma,A>` is compatible with the exponent maps to `Z`.
On the kernels it is the canonical map from the direct limit of
`Gamma --phi_A--> Gamma --phi_A--> ...` onto
`union_j A^(-j) Gamma A^j`.  That map is injective, because each map
`Gamma -> A^(-j) Gamma A^j`, `gamma |-> A^(-j) gamma A^j`, is injective.
This is the group `V_T` of `monomial-kazhdan-hnn-is-rf-but-non-rfd-and-not-vfhs-stable`
and the one-compressor subgroup of `one-kun-thom-compressor-is-relatively-embeddable`,
`kun-thom-single-compressor-sofic-joint-nonsofic` and
`kt-one-compressor-full-coset-action-is-sofic`.

**(ii) Commuting compressors.**  Take pairwise commuting
`A_1, ..., A_m in L_+`, and let `S` be the monoid they generate.  It is
commutative, hence left Ore, and `P` is abelian.  So
`M_{<Gamma,A_1,...,A_m>}` is Connes embeddable.

**(iii) The Heisenberg pair (`d >= 3`).**  Take `A = I + E_12`,
`B = I + E_23` and `S = <A,B>_+`.  Every element of `S` has the form

```text
(x,y,z) := I + x E_12 + y E_23 + z E_13,     x,y,z in N,
(x,y,z)(x',y',z') = (x+x', y+y', z+z'+x y').
```

Let `s,s' in S` and let `C_0` be the largest coordinate of `s` and `s'`.  A
product of `n` factors from `{s,s'}` has `x,y in [0, n C_0]` and
`z <= n C_0 + n^2 C_0^2`.  So these products take at most
`(nC_0+1)^2 (n^2 C_0^2 + n C_0 + 1) <= (n C_0 + 1)^4` values, which is less
than `2^n` for `n` large.  By Lemma 4.2, `S` is left Ore.  The group
`P = <A,B>` is the integral Heisenberg group.  It is nilpotent, hence
amenable (standard).  So `M_{<Gamma, I+E_12, I+E_23>}` is Connes embeddable.
The graph node `heisenberg-kun-thom-slice-is-relatively-embeddable` uses the
larger monoid `<A,B,[A,B]>_+`.  Both monoids give the same group `H_S`,
because both generate the Heisenberg group.

**(iv) Subexponential growth.**  Suppose `S subset L_+` generates a group
`P` of subexponential growth.  Then every two-generated subgroup `<s,s'>`
also has subexponential growth, so its ball of radius `n` has fewer than
`2^n` elements for `n` large.  By Lemma 4.2, `S` is left Ore.  Groups of
subexponential growth are amenable (standard, not re-fetched).  So Theorem B
applies.  By the Tits alternative the amenable subgroups of `SL_d(Z)` are
virtually solvable.  Not every solvable case is covered: a solvable group can
contain a free submonoid of rank two, which is not left Ore, and Theorem B
says nothing about such monoids.

### 4.5 The actor group

For `g in G`, `g Gamma g^(-1) <= E`, because `Gamma <= E` and `E` is normal.
So the stabilizer of `g Gamma` in any `K <= G` with `K intersect E = 1` is
trivial.  Such a `K` is isomorphic to its image in `SL_d(Z)`, so it is
residually finite.  By Remark 1.1, `M_K` is Connes embeddable.  In
particular `M_{SL_d(Z)}` is Connes embeddable.

## 5. Localization, and what the target claim can still use

**Corollary 5.1.**  Let `C = { K <= G : M_K is Connes embeddable }`.

1. `C` is closed under subgroups, conjugation and directed unions, by (S2)
   and Lemma C.
2. `C` contains the following subgroups:
   - every conjugate of every `<Gamma,S>` with `S subset L_+` left Ore and
     `<S>` amenable, by Theorem B;
   - every `K` with `K intersect E = 1`, by Section 4.5;
   - every residually finite `K <= G` whose stabilizers
     `K intersect g Gamma g^(-1)` are separable in `K`, by Theorem A.
3. The target claim `kun-thom-clifford-crossed-product-ce` is the statement
   `G in C`.  Any refutation must therefore exhibit a subgroup outside `C`,
   and in particular a subgroup contained in no group listed in 2.

**Lemma 5.2 (the Laurent group is not covered).**  `E` is not contained in
any conjugate of any `H_S` with `S` left Ore.

*Proof.*  If `E <= g H_S g^(-1)`, then `E = g^(-1) E g <= H_S`, so
`E <= H_S intersect E = N_S`.  Let `z = (1,...,1)` and consider
`I + x^(-z) E_12 in E`.  If it lay in `N_S`, it would lie in some
`t^(-1) Gamma t = EL_r(k[t^(-1) N^d])`.  Then `-z in t^(-1) N^d`.  Also
`z in N^d subset t^(-1) N^d`, because `t N^d subset N^d`.  So both `tz` and
`-tz` lie in `N^d`.  This forces `tz = 0` and hence `z = 0`, a
contradiction.  QED.

**Lemma 5.3 (congruence quotients cannot separate `Gamma` in `E`).**  Let
`J subset R` be an ideal with `R/J` finite.  Then `Gamma` and `E` have the
same image `EL_r(R/J)` in `SL_r(R/J)`.

*Proof.*  The image of `x_i` in the finite ring `R/J` is a unit of finite
multiplicative order `m_i`.  So `x_i^(-1) = x_i^(m_i - 1)` modulo `J`.  Hence
`R_0 -> R/J` is surjective.  Therefore every elementary matrix over `R/J`
lifts to `Gamma`.  QED.

So Theorem A cannot be applied to `E` through congruence quotients.  Whether
`Gamma` is separable in `E` for the full profinite topology is not decided
here.  By Lemma 5.2, Theorem B says nothing about `M_E`.  Even if `M_E`
were Connes embeddable, Lemma D could not pass to `M_G = M_{E rtimes SL_d(Z)}`,
because `SL_d(Z)` is not amenable for `d >= 2`.

- The Ore hypothesis is used only in Lemma 4.1.  Amenable but non-Ore
  compressor monoids are not treated.
- Theorem A and Lemma D are general permanence statements with complete
  proofs here.  Their novelty outside this graph was not assessed.
  Theorem A is a finite-quotient argument in the spirit of residually finite
