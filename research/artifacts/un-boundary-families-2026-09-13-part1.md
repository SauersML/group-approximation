# Paradoxical boundary families of simple Kazhdan groups with no MF quotient (part 1)

Lane `un-boundary-families`, 2026-09-13. Unreviewed. Builds on the reviewed simplicity theorem
`steinberg-elementary-groups-are-simple-mod-centre`, the rank-two full-defect theorem
`full-defect-ring-non-mf-at-rank-two` (the printed Theorem [full complementary idempotents], tex l.918), and
Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain. It generalizes un-paradox's headline `boundary-action-elementary-simple-kazhdan-not-mf`
(the free group on its boundary) to every minimal, topologically free Cantor action with one north–south element and
a finite generating family of clopens, and gives two new explicit families.

## 0. Statements

Throughout, `q` is a prime power, `N >= 3`, `Γ` is a countable group acting by homeomorphisms on a Cantor space `X`,
and `R = LC(X, F_q) ⋊ Γ` is the algebraic crossed product (`u_g f u_g^(-1) = f ∘ g^(-1)`).

**(H-gen)** `Γ` is generated by a finite set `S`, and there is a finite family `𝒫` of clopen sets whose `Γ`-translates
separate the points of `X`.

**(H-par)** There are clopen partitions `X = A_1 ⊔ ... ⊔ A_m = B_1 ⊔ ... ⊔ B_l` and `g_i, h_j ∈ Γ` such that the sets
`g_i A_i` and `h_j B_j` (all `i`, `j`) are pairwise disjoint.

**(NS)** Some `g ∈ Γ` is *north–south*: there are points `ξ^+ != ξ^-` such that for every compact `K ⊆ X \ {ξ^-}` and
open `V ∋ ξ^+`, `g^n K ⊆ V` for all large `n`, and the same holds for `g^(-1)` with `ξ^+` and `ξ^-` exchanged.

**Theorem B.** Suppose the action is minimal and topologically free and satisfies (H-gen) and (H-par). Put
`S_N = EL_N(R) / Z(EL_N(R))`. Then:
1. `S_N` is an infinite, finitely generated, simple group with property (T), and `Z(EL_N(R)) ⊆ F_q^× I_N`;
2. every homomorphism from `EL_n(R)` to an MF group is trivial for every `n >= 2`, so every homomorphism from `S_N` to
   an MF group is trivial.

**Lemma NS.** If the action is minimal and some element is north–south, then (H-par) holds with `m = l = 2`.

**Family T (free products of finite groups).** Let `A`, `B` be finite groups with `|A| >= 2` and `|B| >= 3`, and let
`Γ = A * B` act on `Ω`, the space of infinite reduced alternating words (the ends of its Bass–Serre tree). Theorem B
applies. Example: `PSL_2(Z) = Z/2 * Z/3`.

**Family P (the p-adic projective line).** Let `p` be a prime and `Γ_p ≤ PSL_2(Q)` the subgroup generated by the images
of `e_12(1)`, `e_21(1)` and `d_p = diag(p, p^(-1))`, acting on `X = P^1(Q_p)` by Möbius transformations. Theorem B
applies. So `EL_N(LC(P^1(Q_p), F_q) ⋊ Γ_p) / Z` is an infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan group with no
nontrivial MF quotient, for every prime `p`, prime power `q` and `N >= 3`.

## 1. The ring is a Steinberg algebra, and finite generation

**Lemma D.** Let `𝒢 = Γ ⋉ X` (arrows `(g, x)`, `s(g,x) = x`, `r(g,x) = gx`, `(g, hx)(h, x) = (gh, x)`). Then `𝒢` is a
Hausdorff ample groupoid with compact unit space `X`, and `R ≅ A_(F_q)(𝒢)` via `χ_(gU) u_g ↦ 1_({g}×U)`. The groupoid
is minimal iff every orbit is dense, and effective if the action is topologically free.

*Proof.* Compact open bisections are finite disjoint unions of sets `{g} × U` with `U` clopen, so their indicators span
`A_(F_q)(𝒢)`. Multiplication matches:
- in `R`, `χ_(gU) u_g χ_(hV) u_h = χ_(gU ∩ ghV) u_(gh)`;
- in `A_(F_q)(𝒢)`, `1_({g}×U) * 1_({h}×V) = 1_({gh} × (V ∩ h^(-1)U))`, and `gh(V ∩ h^(-1)U) = gU ∩ ghV`.

The unit space is `{e} × X`. The isotropy is `{(g, x) : gx = x}`, and an open subset of it meets `{g} × X` in
`{g} × W_g` with `W_g` open and `W_g ⊆ Fix(g)`. Topological freeness forces `W_g = ∅` for `g != e`, so the interior of
the isotropy is the unit space (effective). Minimality is invariance-plus-density of orbits. QED

**Lemma F.** Under (H-gen), `R` is generated as a ring by `u_s^(±1)` (`s ∈ S`), `χ_P` (`P ∈ 𝒫`) and one generator of
`F_q` over `F_p`.

*Proof.* Let `R_0` be the subring they generate and `𝔅` the set of clopens `U` with `χ_U ∈ R_0`.
- `𝔅` contains every translate `gP`, since `χ_(gP) = u_g χ_P u_g^(-1)` and `u_g` is a word in the `u_s^(±1)`.
- `𝔅` is a Boolean algebra, since `χ_(U∩V) = χ_U χ_V` and `χ_(X \ U) = 1 - χ_U`.
- `𝔅` contains every clopen `U`. For `x ∈ U` and `y ∉ U` some translate `gP` or its complement contains `x` and not
  `y`. For fixed `x`, finitely many such sets `B_1, ..., B_r ∈ 𝔅` have complements covering the compact `X \ U`, so
  `B_x = ∩ B_i ∈ 𝔅` and `x ∈ B_x ⊆ U`. Finitely many `B_x` cover `U`, so `U ∈ 𝔅`.

Hence `LC(X, F_q) ⊆ R_0` (with `F_q = F_p[α]`), and so is every `f u_g`. QED

## 2. Paradox gives a full complementary idempotent

**Lemma P.** Under (H-par) put `s = Σ_i u_(g_i) χ_(A_i)` and `t = Σ_i χ_(A_i) u_(g_i)^(-1)`. Then `ts = 1` and
`R(1 - st)R = R`.

*Proof.* Using `u_g χ_A = χ_(gA) u_g`:
- `ts = Σ_(i,i') u_(g_i)^(-1) χ_(g_i A_i) χ_(g_(i') A_(i')) u_(g_(i'))`. The images are disjoint for `i != i'`, so
  `ts = Σ_i u_(g_i)^(-1) χ_(g_i A_i) u_(g_i) = Σ_i χ_(A_i) = 1`.
- `st = Σ_i u_(g_i) χ_(A_i) u_(g_i)^(-1) = χ_E` with `E = ⊔_i g_i A_i`, so `1 - st = χ_(X \ E)`.
- For `a_j = χ_(B_j) u_(h_j)^(-1)` and `b_j = u_(h_j) χ_(B_j)`, `a_j (1 - st) b_j = χ_(B_j ∩ h_j^(-1)(X \ E)) = χ_(B_j)`,
  since `h_j B_j ⊆ X \ E`. Summing over `j` gives `Σ_j a_j (1 - st) b_j = 1`. QED

## 3. Proof of Theorem B

- By Lemma D, `R = A_(F_q)(𝒢)` with `𝒢` Hausdorff, ample, effective and minimal, and the unit space `X` is compact and
  infinite. By Lemma F, `R` is finitely generated. The reviewed theorem `steinberg-elementary-groups-are-simple-mod-centre`
  gives: normal subgroups of `EL_N(R)` are central or everything, `Z ⊆ F_q^× I_N`, and `S_N` is an infinite finitely
  generated simple group with (T). Its (T) clause is Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain for the finitely generated ring `R` over the
  finite field `F_q`.
- By Lemma P, `R` satisfies the hypothesis of `full-defect-ring-non-mf-at-rank-two`, so every homomorphism from
  `EL_n(R)` to an MF group is trivial for `n >= 2`. A homomorphism from `S_N` to an MF group composes with the
  quotient map to such a homomorphism, so it is trivial. QED

## 4. North–south elements give paradox

**Lemma N (B. H. Neumann).** If a group is a union of finitely many left cosets `c_1 S_1 ∪ ... ∪ c_n S_n`, some `S_i`
has finite index.

*Proof.* Induct on the number `r` of distinct subgroups among the `S_i`.
- For `r = 1`, the group is a finite union of cosets of one subgroup, which then has finite index.
- For `r > 1`, let `S = S_1`, and suppose `[Γ : S]` is infinite. Some coset `dS` is none of the finitely many `c_i S`
  in the list. So it is disjoint from them, and `dS ⊆ ∪_(j : S_j != S) c_j S_j`.
- Each `c_i S` in the list equals `c_i d^(-1) (dS) ⊆ ∪_(j : S_j != S) c_i d^(-1) c_j S_j`.
- Replacing them gives a finite cover by cosets of `r - 1` distinct subgroups, and induction finishes. QED

*Proof of Lemma NS.*
1. **Fixed points.** `g^n ξ^+ -> ξ^+`, so `g ξ^+ = ξ^+` by continuity. Likewise `g ξ^- = ξ^-`. Any other fixed point
   `x` satisfies `x = g^n x ∈ V` for every neighbourhood `V` of `ξ^+`, so `x = ξ^+`.
2. **A conjugate with four distinct fixed points.** A minimal action on an infinite compact space has no finite orbit
   (a finite orbit is closed and invariant). So the stabilizers `St(ξ^±)` have infinite index. The `c ∈ Γ` with
   `cξ^+ ∈ {ξ^+, ξ^-}` or `cξ^- ∈ {ξ^+, ξ^-}` form at most four left cosets of `St(ξ^+)` and `St(ξ^-)`, which by
   Lemma N do not cover `Γ`. Pick `c` outside them and put `h = cgc^(-1)`. Then `h` is north–south with points
   `η^± = cξ^±` (use the compact `c^(-1)K` and the open `c^(-1)V`), and `ξ^+, ξ^-, η^+, η^-` are pairwise distinct.
3. **The pieces.** Choose pairwise disjoint clopen neighbourhoods `V_1 ∋ ξ^+`, `V_2 ∋ η^+`, `V_3 ∋ ξ^-`, `V_4 ∋ η^-`,
   a clopen `C` with `ξ^- ∈ C`, `η^- ∉ C`, and a clopen `D` with `ξ^+ ∈ D`, `η^+ ∉ D`. Put `A_1 = X \ C` (avoids
   `ξ^-`), `A_2 = C` (avoids `η^-`), `B_1 = X \ D` (avoids `ξ^+`) and `B_2 = D` (avoids `η^+`). For `n` large,
   `g^n A_1 ⊆ V_1`, `h^n A_2 ⊆ V_2`, `g^(-n) B_1 ⊆ V_3` and `h^(-n) B_2 ⊆ V_4`, and these four sets are pairwise
   disjoint. QED

## 5. Family T: `A * B` on its boundary

Let `Ω` be the set of infinite words `x_1 x_2 ...` with letters in `A^× ∪ B^×` (with `A^× = A \ {1}`) and consecutive
letters in different factors, with the product topology.
- **Cantor.** `Ω` is closed in a product of finite sets, so compact, metrizable and totally disconnected. After a
  letter of `A^×` there are `|B| - 1 >= 2` choices, so every cylinder `C(w)` (words beginning with `w`) is uncountable,
  and `Ω` is perfect.
- **Action.** `γ = y_1 ... y_k` in normal form acts by concatenation followed by reduction at the junction: merge
  letters from the same factor, delete a trivial product and continue. At most `k` letters of `ω` are consumed and at
  most one more is modified. This is the left action on the ends of the Bass–Serre tree, and each `γ` is a
  homeomorphism with inverse `γ^(-1)`.
- **(H-gen).** `𝒫 = {C(x) : x ∈ A^× ∪ B^×}`, and `S = A^× ∪ B^×` generates `Γ`. Since consecutive letters of a reduced
  word lie in different factors, `C(x_1 ... x_m) = (x_1 ... x_(m-1)) · C(x_m)`. Cylinders separate points.
- **Minimal.** Given a cylinder `C(w)` and `ω`, put `γ = w y`. Take `y` to be one letter from the factor different from
  both the last letter of `w` and `x_1(ω)`, when those two lie in the same factor. Otherwise take two letters
  `y = y_1 y_2`, with `y_1` in the factor of `x_1(ω)` and `y_2` in the factor of the last letter of `w`. Then
  `w y ω` is reduced, so `γω ∈ C(w)`.
- **Topologically free.** Suppose `γ != 1` has normal-form length `k` and fixes a cylinder pointwise. Extend it to
  `C(w')` with `|w'| >= k + 2`. For `ω = w' ω''` the reduction touches only the first `k + 1` letters, so
  `γω = (γw') ω''`, where `γw'` is a reduced word ending in the last letter of `w'`. Choose `ω''` not eventually
  periodic. Then `(γw') ω'' = w' ω''` forces `|γw'| = |w'|`, so `γw' = w'` as words and `γ = 1`, a contradiction.
- **(NS).** Take `g = ab` with `a ∈ A^×` and `b ∈ B^×`, and put `ξ^+ = abab...` and `ξ^- = b^(-1)a^(-1)b^(-1)...`.
  A compact `K ∌ ξ^-` misses some `C((b^(-1)a^(-1))^M)`, so the reduction of `(ab)^n ω` (`ω ∈ K`) consumes fewer than
  `2M + 2` letters and `g^n K ⊆ C((ab)^(n-M-1))`. The inverse is symmetric.

Lemma NS and Theorem B apply.

## 6. Family P: `Γ_p` on `P^1(Q_p)`

- **Cantor.** `P^1(Q_p)` is the one-point compactification of `Q_p`: compact, metrizable, totally disconnected and
  perfect.
- **`Γ_p` contains `e_12(b)` and `e_21(b)` for all `b ∈ Z[1/p]`.** Conjugating by `d_p^k` gives `e_12(p^(2k))`, and
  every `b ∈ Z[1/p]` is `n p^(2k)` with `n ∈ Z` and `k <= 0`. The same holds for `e_21`.
- **Minimal.** `Z[1/p]` is dense in `Q_p`, so the closure of `Γ_p` in `PSL_2(Q_p)` contains all `e_12(Q_p)` and
  `e_21(Q_p)`, which generate `SL_2(Q_p)`. Hence `Γ_p` is dense. `PSL_2(Q_p)` is transitive on `P^1(Q_p)`
  (translations are transitive on `Q_p`, and `z ↦ z/(z+1)` sends `∞` to `1`). So for `x ∈ X` and open `U != ∅` the
  set `{g : gx ∈ U}` is open and nonempty and meets `Γ_p`. Every orbit is dense.
- **Topologically free.** An element fixing an open set pointwise fixes at least three points, and a Möbius
  transformation over a field fixing three points is trivial in `PGL_2`.
- **(H-gen) with `𝒫 = {Z_p, pZ_p}`.**
  - Let `a + p^m Z_p` be a ball. Pick `b ∈ Z[1/p]` with `a - b ∈ p^m Z_p`, and write `m = 2k + ε` with `ε ∈ {0,1}`.
    Then `φ = e_12(b) d_p^k : z ↦ p^(2k) z + b` lies in `Γ_p` and `φ(p^ε Z_p) = b + p^m Z_p`.
  - Balls separate points of `Q_p`, and a ball contains its centre but not `∞`.
- **(NS).** `g = d_p : z ↦ p^2 z`, with `ξ^+ = 0` and `ξ^- = ∞`. A compact `K ⊆ Q_p` has `|z|_p <= p^c`, so
  `|g^n z|_p <= p^(c-2n) -> 0` uniformly. A compact `K ∌ 0` has `|z|_p >= p^(-c)`, so `g^(-n)K` leaves every ball.

Lemma NS and Theorem B apply. Recalled, not used: `Γ_p = PSL_2(Z[1/p])`, since `E_2(Z[1/p]) = SL_2(Z[1/p])`.

## 7. Model tests and firewalls

- **Pestov ring.** A minimal `Z`-subshift carries an invariant measure `μ`, and (H-par) would give
  `μ(X) >= 2μ(X)`. So (H-par) fails, as it must: that group is LEF. A north–south element would push `μ` to an atom
  at `ξ^+`, which a minimal infinite `X` cannot carry.
- **Free group.** `g = a` is north–south on `∂F_d`, which recovers `boundary-action-elementary-simple-kazhdan-not-mf`.
- **Degenerate cases.**
  - `D_∞ = Z/2 * Z/2` is excluded by `|B| >= 3`: its boundary has two points.
  - Finite `X` has no Cantor structure.
- **Connected boundaries (firewall).** For a one-ended hyperbolic group (e.g. a surface group, `∂Γ = S^1`),
  `LC(∂Γ, k) = k`, so the construction degenerates. A zero-dimensional model with a minimal, topologically free
  Cantor action and a finite generating family is needed; see the open claim for hyperbolic groups.
- **Separating the families.** No invariant separating the groups `S_N` across families is known. All contain
  `SL_(Nn)(F_q)` for every `n`, hence every finite group, and all are simple with no MF quotient. This is open (part 2).
