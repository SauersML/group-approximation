# Port-generated square completions force groups and cosets

Lane `ex-kac-quantum-compiler`, 2026-09-13, part 3.  Node:
`port-generated-square-completions-force-coset-support` with its `-proof`
route.

## 0. The question

The colored rung needs a *bisynchronous* game, one with a square magic
unitary, carrying a closure-safe trace
(`closure-safe-support-is-exactly-the-colored-iso-rung`).  A synchronous
verifier gives only rows `e_(q,·)`.  The most direct squarification adds
padding rows whose entries are sums of the port projections `e_(q,a)`.
Harris's circulant completion is one example.
`harris-circulant-one-matrix-compiler-forces-affine-support` shows that it
sees only affine support under an exact colour-equality presentation.  Here
every such completion is classified under closure-safety alone.  The groups
may be nonabelian and the Latin squares arbitrary.

## 1. Setting

- `G` is a finite synchronous game with questions `I`, answer sets `O_q`, and
  rule `W_(qq')` on `O_q x O_(q')`.
- `τ` is a perfect tracial state on `A(G)`, with supports
  `S_(qq') = {(a,b) : τ(e_(qa) e_(q'b)) > 0}`.
- **Full support**: `τ(e_(qa)) > 0` for all `q, a`.  This is no loss for the
  rung.  Imposing `e_(qa) = 0` on zero-weight answers keeps the trace, and it
  keeps the absence of `R^U` models, because it is a quotient
  (as in `oracular-commutation-quotient-preserves-tracial-nonru`).
- **Port-generated (Latin) completion.**  For each `q`, a Latin square
  `L^q : O_q x O_q -> O_q`, whose rows `L^q_x` are permutations, with a port row
  `p_q` such that `L^q_(p_q) = id`.
  - The completed game `H_L` has questions and answers `(q,x)`, `(q,y)`, and
    magic unitary blocks `u_((q,x),(q,y)) = e_(q, (L^q_x)^(-1) y)`.
  - Its rule decodes answers through the rows and applies `W`.
  - `A(H_L) ≅ A(G)`, since `e_(qa) = u_((q,p_q),(q,a))` and the Latin
    property gives row and column sums.
  - Every completion whose entries lie in the span of `{e_(q,a)}_a` has this
    form: for fixed `a` the map `x |-> y` is a bijection.
- **Closure-safe** as in the closure-safe node: every connected component of
  the support graph between question pairs and answer pairs is accepted
  throughout by `H_L`'s rule.

## 2. Theorem L

Under full support, the trace of a port-generated completion is closure-safe
iff:

- **(L1)** each `{L^q_x}_x` is a regular permutation group `K_q` on `O_q`;
- **(L2)** for each `q != q'`, fix `s_0 in S_(qq')` and let
  `M_(qq') <= K_q x K_(q')` be generated by `{g : g s_0 in S_(qq')}`.  Then
  `M_(qq') s_0 subseteq W_(qq')`.

`M_(qq') s_0` is the smallest orbit of a subgroup containing `S_(qq')`: the
*coset hull*.  Under the identification `O_q x O_(q') ≅ K_q x K_(q')` through
`s_0` it is a left coset of a subgroup.

**Proof of (L1).**  Within block `q`, the question pair `((q,x),(q,x'))`
decodes a single PVM.
- `u_(xy) u_(x'y') = δ e_(qa)` with `a = L_x^(-1) y = L_(x')^(-1) y'`.
- By full support its neighbourhood is the graph of `L_(x') L_x^(-1)`.
- The rule accepts exactly when the decoded answers agree.

Fix `x, x'` and `y_0`.  Choose `z` with
`L_z(y_0) = L_(x') L_x^(-1)(y_0)`.
- The neighbourhood of `((q,p_q),(q,z))` is the graph of `L_z`.  It meets the
  graph of `L_(x') L_x^(-1)` at `y_0`, so both lie in one component.
- Acceptance at `((q,p_q),(q,z))` on the pair `(y, L_(x') L_x^(-1) y)`
  forces `L_z^(-1) L_(x') L_x^(-1) y = y` for all `y`.  So
  `L_(x') L_x^(-1) = L_z`.

So the row set contains `id` and is closed under `(x,x') |-> L_(x') L_x^(-1)`.
It is therefore a group, and it is regular because it is sharply transitive.

Conversely, for a regular group the graphs of distinct elements are disjoint.
Each component is one element's graph, and it decodes to equal answers.

**Proof of (L2).**  For `q != q'`, identify `O_q x O_(q')` with
`K = K_q x K_(q')` by `g |-> g s_0`, and put `Σ = {g : g s_0 in S}`, so `e in Σ`.
- The question pair `g = (L_x, L_(x'))` has neighbourhood `g Σ`.
- Let `C` be the answer side of the component of the port pair `e`.
  - **`C subseteq <Σ>`.**  By induction, if `g Σ` meets `C subseteq <Σ>`, then
    `g in <Σ>` and `g Σ subseteq <Σ>`.
  - **`C ⊇ <Σ>`.**  If `w in C` is a word in `Σ`, then `w Σ ∋ w` meets `C`, so
    `w Σ subseteq C`.  Finite semigroups generated by `Σ ∋ e` are groups.
- So `C = M s_0` with `M = <Σ>`.
- At the port pair the rule decodes identically, so it needs `M s_0 subseteq W`.
- At any `g in M`, decoding gives `g^(-1) M s_0 = M s_0`: the same condition.
- The components of `g ∉ M` translate this one.

## 3. Consequences

**Boolean answers.**  `|O_q| = 2` forces `K_q = Z_2`, so (L2) is affine
safety of each pair support.  The
`jnvwy-active-output-has-no-affine-safe-perfect-model` firewall then applies
unchanged.

**Oracularized games.**  Let `c` be a context question and `i` a variable
question, with a functional consistency support `graph(π_i)`.
- Under (L2) the hull `M s_0` equals the graph.
- Full support and `|M s_0| = |O_c|` make the projection `M -> K_c` a
  bijection.  So `M = {(k, φ_i(k))}` for a homomorphism
  `φ_i : K_c -> K_i`, and `π_i` is `φ_i`-equivariant.
- Hence the context's support, embedded through `(π_i)_i`, is the orbit of
  one point under the subgroup `{(φ_i(k))_i}` of `prod_i K_i`.  That is a
  left coset: the local relations of torsor CFI syntax
  (`torsor-cfi-isomorphism-algebra-is-coset-constraint-algebra`).
- So port-generated squarification of an oracularized verifier lands in coset
  syntax.  Atom-by-atom gadgets there realize only cosets
  (`torsor-cfi-port-profiles-are-cosets`).

**What escapes: padding outside the port algebra.**  Let `S_3` act on
`Z = B ⊔ B'`, with `B = {0,1,2}` and `B'` the three 2-subsets.  Colour
`Z x Z` by the orbitals of `S_3`.
- `Aut Z = S_3`: the orbital structure is two triangles joined by the
  complement matching, and the symmetric group on three points is 2-closed.
- The uniform measure on `S_3` gives a classical, hence closure-safe, trace.
- Take the port pair `(0, {0,1})`.  Its support is the incidence
  `{(i,P) : i in P}`.
- The incidence is not rectangular: it contains `(0,{0,1})`, `(1,{0,1})` and
  `(0,{0,2})` but not `(1,{0,2})`.  Every coset of a subgroup of a product
  group is rectangular, since `(a',b) (a,b)^(-1) (a,b')` stays in the coset.
- The padding rows `u_(1,·)` and `u_(2,·)` record `g(1)` and `g(2)`.  They are
  not functions of the port answer `g(0)`.

So Theorem L is sharp in its hypothesis.  Supports outside cosets require
padding operators outside the algebra of the port measurement, i.e.
auxiliary randomness or genuinely new noncommuting operators.  The
simulated perfect correlation of the PZK tableau
(`pzk-tableau-private-s5-gauge-normal-form`) is auxiliary randomness of
exactly this kind, but only at the trace level.

## 4. Scope

- Only completions inside the span of the port PVM of the same question are
  classified.
- Nothing here excludes completions with auxiliary operators, classical or
  noncommuting.
- For answer alphabets of size above 2, (L2) is coset safety for some regular
  groups, possibly nonabelian.  Whether the JNVWY output admits a
  coset-safe perfect model for nonabelian regular structures on its
  multi-bit alphabets is not settled here.  The affine firewall is stated for
  Boolean coordinates.
