# The Fibonacci simple Kazhdan group: sharp finite models from Fibonacci words (part 1)

Lane sk-open-3, 2026-09-13. Written proofs plus small exact computations on MSI. Not reviewed (queued with
sk-referee-2). Conventions are those of the note `simple_kazhdan_sofic_group.tex` (disk version l.71-77, 131-144):
- `(Tx)_n = x_{n+1}`; `R = LC(X,F_2) ⋊_T Z`, `u f u^{-1} = f∘T^{-1}`;
- `P δ_n = δ_{n+1}` on `F_2^{Z/N}`;
- `φ_z(Σ f_j u^j) = Σ D_z(f_j) P^j`, entrywise on matrices.

## 0. The object
- σ is the substitution `a ↦ ab, b ↦ a`; its fixed point is `abaababaabaab…`; X = X_Fib is its subshift (minimal,
  Sturmian, `|L_m(X)| = m+1`).
- Fibonacci words: `f_1 = a`, `f_2 = ab`, `f_{k+1} = f_k f_{k-1}`. So `f_3 = aba`, `f_4 = abaab`, `f_5 = abaababa`, with
  lengths `1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, …`. Each `f_k` is a prefix of the fixed point.
- `p = χ_{[x_0 = a]}`, so `1 - p = χ_{[x_0 = b]}`. R is generated by `u, u^{-1}, p`.
- G = G_Fib = EL_3(R) is generated by the 24 involutions `e_ij(s)`, `i ≠ j`, `s ∈ {1, u, u^{-1}, p}`.
- `B_r` is the ball of radius r for this generating set.

## 1. Lemma 1 (circular Fibonacci words)
Let `k ≥ 2`, `z = f_k`, `N = |f_k|`, and write `z^∞` for the bi-infinite periodic sequence.
1. `z^∞` has least period N, so it has at least `L+1` words of length L for every `L ≤ N-1`.
2. Every word of `z^∞` of length `≤ N+1` occurs in X. So `z^∞` and X have exactly the same words of every length
   `L ≤ N-1`.
3. (Sharp form.) Every word of `z^∞` of length `≤ |f_{k+2}| - 1 = 2N + |f_{k-1}| - 1` occurs in X, and the bound is
   attained.

**Proof of 1.**
- The numbers of letters are `|f_k|_a = |f_{k-1}|` and `|f_k|_b = |f_{k-2}|` (induction).
- `gcd(|f_k|, |f_{k-2}|) = gcd(|f_{k-1}|, |f_{k-2}|) = 1`, so `f_k` is not a proper power, and `z^∞` has least period N.
- Suppose `p(L) ≤ L` for some `L < N`. Then `p(L'+1) = p(L')` for some `L' ≤ L`, so every word of length L' has a unique
  right extension.
- Among the windows at positions `0, …, p(L')`, two coincide, at distance `t ≤ p(L') < N`. Right determination makes
  `t` a period, a contradiction (Morse–Hedlund). ∎

**Proof of 2.**
- `f_{k+3} = f_{k+2} f_{k+1} = f_{k+1} f_k f_k f_{k-1}`, so `zz` occurs in X.
- A word of `z^∞` of length `M ≤ N+1` starting at a position in `[0, N)` lies inside `zz`.
- For `L ≤ N-1`, part 1 gives at least `L+1` distinct words, all in `L_L(X)`, which has exactly `L+1` words. ∎

**Proof of 3.**
- Standard identity (induction from `f_2 f_1 = aba`, `f_1 f_2 = aab`): `f_j f_{j-1}` and `f_{j-1} f_j` differ exactly
  in their last two letters, for `j ≥ 2`.
- `f_{k+4} = f_{k+1} f_k f_k f_{k-1} f_k f_{k-1} f_k`, so `zz f_{k-1} f_k = zz f_{k-1} f_{k-1} f_{k-2}` occurs in X.
- The sequence `z^∞` continues after `zz` with `f_{k-1} f_{k-2} f_{k-1} f_{k-2}…`.
- The words `f_{k-1} f_{k-1} f_{k-2}` and `f_{k-1} f_{k-2} f_{k-1}` agree on their first `|f_{k-1}| + |f_{k-1}| + |f_{k-2}| - 2 = N + |f_{k-1}| - 2` letters.
- So the prefix of `z^∞` of length `3N + |f_{k-1}| - 2` occurs in X.
- Words of length M starting in `[0, N)` lie in that prefix when `N + M - 1 ≤ 3N + |f_{k-1}| - 2`, i.e. when
  `M ≤ 2N + |f_{k-1}| - 1`.
- Attainment is computed (§3, table 1). ∎

## 2. Proposition 2 (sharp models of balls)
Let `z = f_k`, `N = |f_k|`, and `r ≥ 1` with `N ≥ 2r+2`. Then `φ_z` is defined on `B_r ∪ B_r B_r` and satisfies
`φ_z(gh) = φ_z(g) φ_z(h)` for all `g, h ∈ B_r`. It is injective on `B_r`, with values in `SL_{3N}(F_2) = GL_{3N}(F_2)`.
With the easy Lemma 1.2 alone, the same holds when `N ≥ 4r`.

**Proof.**
1. **Monomials.**
   - A product `s_1⋯s_m` with `s_t ∈ {1, u, u^{-1}, p}` equals `∏_t χ_{[x_{-a_t} = a]} · u^{a_m}`. Here `a_t` is the sum of
     the exponents before position t, and the product runs over the t with `s_t = p`. This uses
     `u^a p u^{-a} = p∘T^{-a} = χ_{[x_{-a} = a]}`.
   - So its coefficient depends only on coordinates in `[-m, m]`, and its exponent satisfies `|a_m| ≤ m`.
   - Entries of an element of `B_r` are F_2-sums of monomials of length `≤ r`. Entries of a product of two elements of
     `B_r` are sums of monomials of length `≤ 2r`.
2. **The model.**
   - Let `A_{2r}` be the functions depending on `[-2r, 2r]`. For `f ∈ A_{2r}` put `D_z(f)_{nn} = f(x)` for any `x ∈ X`
     with `x_{[-2r,2r]} = (z^∞)_{[n-2r, n+2r]}`.
   - Such x exists by Lemma 1.3, because `4r+1 ≤ 2N-3 ≤ 2N + |f_{k-1}| - 1`. With Lemma 1.2 instead, it exists when
     `4r+1 ≤ N+1`.
   - D_z is well defined, additive and multiplicative on `A_{2r}`.
   - `P^i D_z(g) P^{-i} = D_z(g∘T^{-i})` whenever `g` and `g∘T^{-i}` both lie in `A_{2r}`. Indeed both diagonals at n are
     `g` evaluated on `(z^∞)_{n-i+W}`, where W is the window of g.
   - Multiplying two monomials of total length `≤ 2r` only uses such pairs:
     `(f u^i)(g u^j) = f (g∘T^{-i}) u^{i+j}`, with `f` and `g∘T^{-i}` coefficients of partial products.
   - So `φ_z(gh) = φ_z(g) φ_z(h)` for `g, h ∈ B_r`. Taking `h = g^{-1} ∈ B_r` shows the images are invertible.
3. **Injectivity.**
   - If `g ≠ h ∈ B_r`, some entry of `g - h` is `Σ_j f_j u^j ≠ 0` with `|j| ≤ r` and `f_j ∈ A_r`.
   - Since `N ≥ 2r+1`, the matrices `D_z(f_j) P^j` have disjoint supports.
   - A nonzero `f_j` equals 1 on the cylinder of some `v ∈ L_{2r+1}(X)`. By Lemma 1.2 (`2r+1 ≤ N-1`), v occurs in `z^∞`,
     so `D_z(f_j) ≠ 0`. ∎

**Remark (the bound on N).**
- Proposition 2 applies to circular words whose `(2r+1)`-words are all of `L_{2r+1}(X)`. Such a word must have length
  `N ≥ |L_{2r+1}(X)| = 2r+2`.
- So among Fibonacci words, the least admissible N is the least Fibonacci number `≥ 2r+2`.
- §3, table 2 shows that no circular word of any length does better under these conditions, for `r ≤ 8`.

**First models.**
- `B_1 → SL_15(F_2)` (`z = abaab`, matching main's Remark [the Fibonacci subshift]);
- `B_2, B_3 → SL_24(F_2)`;
- `B_4, B_5 → SL_39(F_2)`;
- `B_6, B_7, B_8 → SL_63(F_2)`;
- in general `B_r → SL_{3N}(F_2)` with `N ≤ φ(2r+2)`, where φ is the golden ratio.

## 4. True faithfulness thresholds on the exact balls (MSI, `fibball.py`)
- Setup:
  - the balls `B_r` of G_Fib are built exactly as sets of 3×3 matrices over R;
  - coefficients are stored as window tables on `L(X)`, in canonical minimal windows;
  - multiplication follows the note's conventions.
- For each Fibonacci word `z`, `φ_z` is applied with `D_z(f)_{nn}` = the table of f on `z^∞` at n, which is undefined if
  the word is not in L(X).
- Sizes: `|B_1| = 25`, `|B_2| = 445`, `|B_3| = 6925`.

| r | N=2 | N=3 (`aba`) | N=5 (`abaab`) | N=8 | N=13 | N=21 |
|---|-----|-------------|---------------|-----|------|------|
| 1 | not injective | faithful (all 625 pairs multiplicative) | faithful | faithful | faithful | faithful |
| 2 | not injective | faithful (all 198,025 pairs) | faithful | faithful | faithful | faithful |
| 3 | not injective | not injective | faithful (60,000 random pairs) | faithful | faithful | faithful |

- "Faithful" means: defined on `B_r` and on every tested product, injective on `B_r`, and multiplicative on every tested pair.
- The true first models are smaller than Proposition 2 guarantees:
  - `B_1` and `B_2` embed partially into `SL_9(F_2)` via `aba`;
  - `B_3` embeds into `SL_15(F_2)` via `abaab`.
- Proposition 2 guarantees N = 5, 8, 8 for r = 1, 2, 3. So its bound `N ≥ 2r+2` is sufficient with a written proof, and
  not optimal for actual faithfulness.
- These thresholds are computational evidence only, not proved statements.
