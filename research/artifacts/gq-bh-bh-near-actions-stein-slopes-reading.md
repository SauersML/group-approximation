## Tanner, "Studying Stein's groups as topological full groups", arXiv:2312.07375 (main.tex)
- Definition "Stein's Groups" (l. 338):
  - `Λ ⊆ (0,∞)` is a multiplicative subgroup and `Γ` a `Z·Λ`-submodule with `ΛΓ = Γ`, `1 ≤ ℓ ∈ Γ`.
  - `V(Γ,Λ,ℓ)` = right-continuous PL bijections of `[0,ℓ]` with slopes in `Λ` and finitely many
    discontinuities in `Γ`.
- Theorem `fg when fg by alg` / intro Theorem (l. ~110):
  - `D(V(Γ,Λ,ℓ))` is simple for all data.
  - The following are equivalent: `Γ ⋊ Λ` f.g.; `D(V)` f.g. for all `ℓ`; `D(V)` 2-generated.
- l. 498–507: `IE(Γ,ℓ)` = PL bijections of `[0,ℓ]` with finitely many "angles" `ft − t`, all in `Γ`, and
  the Zappa--Szép decomposition `V(Γ,Λ,ℓ) = F(Γ,Λ,ℓ) ⋈ IE(Γ,ℓ)`.
- Remark "Juschenko-Monod groups in Clearys group" (l. 510–512): `IE(Z ⊕ τZ) ↪ V_τ`, and `D(IE(Z⊕τZ))` has
  solvable word problem "since D(V_τ) is simple and finitely presented".
  - This is the priority source for the golden case of
    `norm-minus-one-sturmian-jm-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`.
  - Tanner's `τ` is `(1+√5)/2` (l. 366); ours is the root in `(0,1)`. They give the same `Z[τ^{±1}]`.
- l. 914–917, Question: "Under what conditions is D(V(Γ,Λ,ℓ)) finitely presented?"
  `stein-derived-groups-need-computable-modules` gives a necessary condition.
- Theorem `ah conj` (l. ~989), AH exact sequence (from Li, Corollary E):
  `H_2(D(V)) → H_2 → H_0⊗Z_2 → V^ab → H_1 → 0`, with groupoid homology `H_k = H_k(Γ⋊Λ, C_c(R_Γ,Z))`.
- Lemma (Li, Prop 5.5; l. 1040): for one algebraic `λ`, `H_0 ≅ Γ/(1−λ)Γ` and `H_k ≅ H_{k+1}(Γ⋊Λ)` for
  `k ≥ 1`.
- Corollary `abelianisation single lambda` (l. 1055–1070). **Possible sign issue.** The bullet "d=2,
  a_0 ≠ 1" gives `Z/(1+a_0)` summands. For the golden polynomial (`a_0 = −1`) that would be `Z`, but Tanner and
  BNR give `V_τ^ab = Z/2`.
  - The direct computation in `norm-minus-one-sturmian-jm-groups-satisfy-boone-higman-proof` step 2 is `H_1 = H_2(Z²⋊_A Z) = Z/(1 − det A)`.
    It gives `Z/2` for norm −1 and `Z` for norm +1, consistent with BNR.
  - I did not rely on the corollary as printed.
- l. 1080 and 1101, rational homology: for transcendental `λ`,
  `H_*(D(V_λ);Q) ≅ ⊕_{i≥1} Q` for `* > 1`.

## Matui, "Classifying Stein's groups", arXiv:2412.05492 (csg250728.tex)
- Remark, l. 845–862: Brown proved `V_{n,r}` is `F_∞`. Stein proved `F_∞` for integral slope groups.
  "In [Cl95Rocky, Cl00Illinois], S. Cleary proved that V(Z[λ],⟨λ⟩,ℓ) is of type F_∞, when λ is an algebraic
  integer of degree two, such as √2−1 or (√5−1)/2."
  - This is input (C) of the metallic node. The primary papers are Cleary, RMJM 25 (1995) 935–955, and
    Illinois J. Math. 44 (2000) 453–464. They were not obtained: Project Euclid is walled from MSI.
- Proposition `S2<Sbeta`: `V_{2,1} ↪ V_{β,1}` for the golden `β`.

## Burillo--Nucinkis--Reeves, arXiv:2006.02401 (ttau.tex)
- Intro, l. 97–100: `V_τ` = left-continuous PL maps of `(0,1]` with breaks in `Z[τ]` and slopes powers of `τ`.
  "These groups are of type F_∞ ... The extension of the result to T_τ and V_τ follows by directly applying
  the methods of Stein ... we will not present them here."
- Theorem `thethm` (l. 429): the index-2 kernel `V_{xz}` is simple.
