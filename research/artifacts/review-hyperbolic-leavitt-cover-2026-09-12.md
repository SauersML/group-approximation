## 1. Belegradek–Osin, read from the PDF

The PDF was fetched on MSI on 2026-09-12. The arXiv listing shows v1–v4, and the PDF header reads "arXiv:math/0605553v4 [math.GR] 14 Sep 2007". Text was extracted with ghostscript. Ligatures are restored below.

- **Corollary 1.2.** "For any nonelementary hyperbolic group H and any finitely presented group Q, there exists a short exact sequence (1) [1 → N → G → Q → 1], where G is hyperbolic and N is a quotient group of H. Moreover, G is torsion-free whenever H is."
- **Theorem 1.1**, of which 1.2 is the case `S = {1}`, adds: (c) a splitting condition; (d) "If H and Q are torsion free, then so is G."; (e) "The canonical map Q → Out(N) is injective and |Out(N) : ψ(Q)| < ∞."

**Check of the import.** The claim's statement is exactly Corollary 1.2 minus the torsion-free rider. The only hypotheses are that `H` is non-elementary hyperbolic and `Q` is finitely presented. Nothing else is required: no torsion-freeness of `Q`, no finite normal subgroups condition (the proof quotients `H` by its maximal finite normal subgroup, and `N` stays a quotient of `H`). "Hyperbolic" is word-hyperbolic, since `S = {1}`.

**Permanence facts.** Quotients of Kazhdan groups are Kazhdan, and Kazhdan-by-Kazhdan extensions are Kazhdan (Bekka–de la Harpe–Valette, Chapter 1). Both are correctly stated.

**Bonus, available from the same corollary.** With `H` torsion-free, the cover `G` of Theorem 1 is **torsion-free**.

## 2. `hyperbolic-kazhdan-cover-of-leavitt-unit-group`

1. **Source group.** A torsion-free cocompact lattice in `Sp(2,1)` is non-elementary, word-hyperbolic and Kazhdan (`torsion-free-hyperbolic-kazhdan-partner-exists`, established by citation).
2. **`R^×` is finitely presented.** This is Khanh Theorem 6.1, verified by lit-groups against the v1 PDF with the proof chain read (`research/artifacts/khanh-2609-08428-leavitt-gl-verified-2026-09-11.md`).
3. **`R^×` is Kazhdan.** This was the requested weak spot, and it holds by a route shorter than the one the node states.
   - `elementary-group-property-t-over-free-algebras` is internal Lean. It proves (T) for `EL_3` over free algebras on a finite field and descends to every finite-type `ZMod 2`-algebra, which includes `R` (generated by `s_0, s_1, t_0, t_1`).
   - The three-leaf code gives `M_3(R) ≅ R`, so `R^× ≅ GL_3(R) = EL_3(R)` (`leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group`). So `R^×` is Kazhdan with no rank-nine statement needed.
   - The node's stated route also holds: `EL_D(R)` is the image of `EL_9(R)` under `M_9(R) ≅ R`, and `GL_9 = EL_9` makes it all of `R^×`.
   - Consequence: `Ĝ = π⁻¹(G_L) = G` in Theorem 3.
4. **The Rips sequence.** `N` is Kazhdan as a quotient of `H`, and `G` is Kazhdan as an extension. Correct.
5. **`N` is infinite.** `G` is finitely generated. A surjection with finite kernel is a quasi-isometry, and hyperbolicity is a quasi-isometry invariant. `R^×` contains `J ≅ V`, `V` contains Thompson's `F`, and `F` contains `Z²`, which no hyperbolic group contains. Correct.
6. **Finite images.** `φ(G)/φ(N)` is a finite quotient of `G/N ≅ R^×`, which is infinite and simple, so it is trivial. Correct.

**Adversarial tests.**
- **Does the construction secretly make `G` sofic or residually finite?** No. Theorem 1.1(e) gives an injective `R^× → Out(N)` with finite-index image, which is consistent with everything claimed. And `φ(N) = φ(G)` for finite images says residual finiteness of `G` would be invisible on `R^×`, as the artifact notes.
- **Could `N` be finite anyway, e.g. `H → N` killing everything?** Excluded by step 5, independently of the construction.

## 3. `leavitt-unit-group-carries-nontrivial-rigid-defect`

- **The rigid pair.** `Γ = EL_α(R)` and `G_L = EL_D(R)` are Kazhdan, by the same Lean theorem, since `Γ ≅ EL_3(R)` and `G_L = R^×`. `P_Γ ⊇ Γ ∪ {u, v}`, and `G_L = <Γ, u, v>`, so `Γ` is infranormal. Correct.
- **The defect.** `J ⊆ C_{R^×}(Γ)` and `u ∈ G_L`. If every `[u j u⁻¹, γ]` vanished, then `uJu⁻¹ = V_(0001) ⊆ Γ ∩ C(Γ) = Z(Γ)`.
  - The node's contradiction ("`V` is nonabelian") is correct.
  - It is even stronger: `Z(Γ) = 1`, because `Γ ≅ EL_3(R)` is simple (`binary-leavitt-elementary-group-is-simple`). So all commutators vanishing would force `J = 1`.
- **Not normal.** If `Γ` were normal, `G_L` would normalize `C(Γ)` and the defect would vanish. Correct.
- **Consistency check.** Combined with `sofic-groups-kill-rigid-compression-defects` (reviewed PASS in `review-rigid-defect-criterion-2026-09-12.md`), this gives a second proof that `R^×` is nonsofic, through Kun–Thom Theorem 4.1. That agrees with OpenAI's theorem.

## 4. `hyperbolic-kazhdan-group-has-infranormal-kazhdan-subgroup`

Put `Γ̂ = π⁻¹(Γ)` and `Ĝ = π⁻¹(G_L) = G`.

- **(T).** `1 → N → Γ̂ → Γ → 1`, and both ends are Kazhdan. Correct.
- **Infranormality** (the requested weak spot).
  - For a lift `û` of `u`, `ûΓ̂û⁻¹` is a subgroup containing `ûNû⁻¹ = N` with image `uΓu⁻¹`, so it equals `π⁻¹(uΓu⁻¹) ⊆ π⁻¹(Γ) = Γ̂`.
  - `P_Γ̂` contains `Γ̂` (hence `N`), `û` and `v̂`.
  - The group they generate contains `N` and maps onto `<Γ, u, v> = G_L`, so it is `π⁻¹(G_L) = Ĝ`. Correct.
- **Strictness.** `uΓu⁻¹ = vΓv⁻¹`, and `G_L = <Γ, u, v>` with `Γ` not normal, so the common image is a proper subgroup of `Γ`. Hence `π⁻¹(uΓu⁻¹) ⊊ π⁻¹(Γ)`. Not normal: a normal `Γ̂` would have normal image `Γ`. Correct.
- **Non-elementarity** (the requested weak spot). `Γ̂ ⊇ N` is infinite and Kazhdan. An infinite elementary subgroup is virtually `Z`, hence amenable and not Kazhdan (rider of `hyperbolic-nonelementary-centralizer-finite`). Correct.
- **(1), the absolute defect is trivial.** `hyperbolic-compression-centralizer-rigidity` gives `t C_G(Γ̂) t⁻¹ = C_G(Γ̂)` for every compressor `t`. So the group `Ĝ` generated by `P_Γ̂` normalizes `C_G(Γ̂)`, and `[g z g⁻¹, γ] = 1`. Correct.
- **(2), the relative defect.** For a lift `z` of `j`, `π([z, γ']) = [j, π(γ')] = 1`, so `[z, Γ̂] ⊆ N`. And `π([ûzû⁻¹, γ̂]) = [uju⁻¹, γ] ≠ 1`, so the commutator is not in `N`. Correct.
- **(d), not quasiconvex.**
  - `[G_L : Γ] = ∞` (`infranormal-nonnormal-has-infinite-compression-orbit`), so `[G : Γ̂] = ∞`. That gives infinitely many essentially distinct conjugates, all containing the infinite group `N`.
  - Finite height of quasiconvex subgroups (Gitik–Mitra–Rips–Sageev, Trans. AMS 350 (1998)) then excludes quasiconvexity. Correct.
  - An alternative with the same conclusion: `N` is an infinite normal subgroup, so its limit set is all of `∂G`, and a quasiconvex subgroup with full limit set has finite index.
  - GMRS is not imported as a node and was not re-read. Nothing in the graph consumes (d) as a requirement.

**`leavitt-cover-finite-quotients-split-over-the-kernel`.**
1. **Outer action.**
   - `N` is infinite, normal and Kazhdan, hence non-elementary, so `C_G(N)` is finite (`hyperbolic-nonelementary-centralizer-finite`).
   - `G` is torsion-free (Belegradek–Osin Corollary 1.2 with `H` torsion-free), so `C_G(N) = 1`.
   - Hence `G ↪ Aut(N)` and `R^× ↪ Out(N)`: if `g` acts as the inner automorphism by `n ∈ N`, then `n⁻¹g ∈ C_G(N) = 1`.
   - `Out(N)` contains the infinite simple group `R^×`, so it is not residually finite. Correct, and consistent with Theorem 1.1(e) of Belegradek–Osin.
2. **Splitting.**
   - `R^× → Out(N/L)` has finite image, so it is trivial because `R^×` is simple and infinite. Every `g` then acts on `N/L` as an inner automorphism, and `G/L = (N/L)·C` with `C = C_(G/L)(N/L)`, `C ∩ N/L = Z(N/L)` and `C/Z(N/L) ≅ G/N ≅ R^×`.
   - The extension is central with trivial action, so `H² = 0` splits it: `C = Z(N/L) × s(R^×)`.
   - `s(R^×)` is perfect and `Z` is central, so `[C,C] = s(R^×) =: K_L`. `K_L` is characteristic in the normal subgroup `C`, hence normal. `K_L ∩ N/L ⊆ K_L ∩ Z = 1`, and `K_L` centralizes `N/L`. So `G/L = N/L × K_L`.
   - **Uniqueness, stronger than stated.**
     - A normal complement `K'` commutes with `N/L`, since the intersection is trivial, so `K' ⊆ C`. It is perfect, so `K' ⊆ [C,C] = K_L`, and the Dedekind law gives `K' = K_L`.
     - An arbitrary complement is the graph of a homomorphism `R^× → N/L`, which is trivial, so even non-normal complements are unique.
3. **All finite quotients.**
   - For a normal finite-index `K ⊴ G`, `NK = G` (every finite image of `G` is the image of `N`), and `L = K ∩ N` satisfies item 2.
   - `K/L` is a normal complement to `N/L`, so `K/L = K_L`, and `G/K ≅ N/L`. Conversely `L ↦ π⁻¹(K_L)` inverts the map. So it is a bijection, and `Ĝ = N̂_G`.
   - Residual finiteness of `G` is injectivity of `G → N̂_G`. Correct.
