# Finite presentation of subshift crossed products, and the death of free acting groups

Worker: swarm-0917-w4-bh-cantor-crossed. Target: `decidable-group-algebras-have-fp-cantor-crossed-hosts`.

## 0. Setting and conventions

- `P` is a group generated by a finite symmetric set `S`, and `F(S)` is the free group on `S`
  with quotient map `F(S) -> P` and kernel `N`. `B_n` is the ball of radius `n` in `P` for the word
  metric of `S`.
- `A` is a finite alphabet and `X ⊆ A^P` a nonempty subshift, with shift `(g x)(h) = x(g^(-1) h)`.
  For a finite `F ⊆ P`, `L_F(X) = {x|_F : x ∈ X}`.
- `k` is a field and `B_X = LC(X, k) ⋊ P`: the free left `LC(X,k)`-module on `{u_g : g ∈ P}` with
  `u_g f u_g^(-1) = f∘g^(-1)` and `u_g u_h = u_(gh)`. Write `e_a = χ[x(1) = a]`. Then
  `u_g e_a u_g^(-1) = χ[x(g) = a]`, and every cylinder `χ[x|_F = π]` is the product over `h ∈ F`
  of `u_h e_(π(h)) u_h^(-1)`. So `B_X` is generated by the finite set `{e_a} ∪ {u_s : s ∈ S}`
  (note `u_s^(-1) = u_(s^(-1))`).
- Finite presentation of a finitely generated algebra does not depend on the finite generating set
  (Tietze moves). So if `B_X` is finitely presented, it has a presentation with generators
  `E_a (a ∈ A)`, `U_s (s ∈ S)` mapping to `e_a`, `u_s`, and finitely many relators
  `r_1, ..., r_m`, which include `U_s U_(s^(-1)) - 1`. Let `n` bound the lengths of all monomials
  occurring in the `r_j`.

**Lemma 0 (monomials).** Let `Q` be any group generated by the image of `S`, `Z ⊆ A^Q` any
`Q`-invariant closed set, and `W` a monomial of length `<= n` in the letters `E_a, U_s`. Let
`g_W ∈ F(S)` be the product of the `U`-letters of `W`. Under `E_a -> e_a`, `U_s -> u_s`, `W` maps in
`LC(Z,k) ⋊ Q` to `c_W u_([g_W])`, where `c_W(z) = ∏ χ[z(p_i) = a_i]`. Here the product runs over the
`E`-letters `E_(a_i)` of `W`, and `p_i ∈ F(S)` is the product of the `U`-letters to the left of
that `E`-letter, evaluated in `Q`. Every `p_i` and `g_W` has length `<= n`.

*Proof.* Move each `u` to the right with `u_p e_a = (u_p e_a u_p^(-1)) u_p = χ[z(p) = a] u_p`. `∎`

So the image of `W` is determined by formal data in `F(S)`: the words `p_i` and `g_W`, all of
length `<= n`. It is evaluated in `Q` only at the end.

## 1. The local models

Fix the presentation and `n` of Section 0. For an integer `M >= 2n`, let `N_M` be the normal
closure in `F(S)` of `N ∩ (ball of radius M in F(S))`, and `P_M = F(S)/N_M`. Also put
`N_∞ = N` and `P_∞ = P`. The quotient `π_M : P_M -> P` is a bijection from the `n`-ball of `P_M` to
`B_n`: if words `u, v` of length `<= n` agree in `P`, then `u^(-1) v ∈ N` has length `<= 2n <= M`,
so it lies in `N_M`. Use this bijection to identify patterns on the two `n`-balls.

Define
`Z_M = { z ∈ A^(P_M) : for every q ∈ P_M, (q^(-1) z)|_(B_n) ∈ L_(B_n)(X) }`.
It is closed and `P_M`-invariant.

**Lemma 1 (nonempty).** For `x ∈ X`, `x∘π_M ∈ Z_M`.

*Proof.* `(q^(-1)(x∘π_M))(h) = x(π_M(q) π_M(h)) = (π_M(q)^(-1) x)(π_M(h))`. After the
identification of balls, the `n`-window of `q^(-1)(x∘π_M)` is the `n`-window of
`π_M(q)^(-1) x ∈ X`. `∎`

**Lemma 2 (the relators survive).** If `B_X` is finitely presented as in Section 0, then for every
`M ∈ {2n, 2n+1, ..., ∞}` the assignment `E_a -> e_a`, `U_s -> u_([s])` induces a unital algebra
homomorphism `ψ_M : B_X -> B_(Z_M) = LC(Z_M, k) ⋊ P_M`.

*Proof.* Take a relator `r = Σ λ_W W`, with monomials `W` of length `<= n`. By Lemma 0 its image in
`B_X` is `Σ_(g ∈ B_n) (Σ_([g_W]_P = g) λ_W c^X_W) u_g`, and it is zero. Since `{u_g}` is a basis
over `LC(X,k)`, for each `g`, `Σ_([g_W]_P = g) λ_W c^X_W = 0` on `X`. Now `c^X_W(x) = φ_W(x|_(B_n))`
for the function `φ_W(π) = ∏_i χ[π(p_i) = a_i]` on `A^(B_n)`. Since every pattern of `L_(B_n)(X)`
occurs in some `x`, the function `Φ_g = Σ_([g_W]_P = g) λ_W φ_W` vanishes on `L_(B_n)(X)`.

In `B_(Z_M)` the image of `r` is `Σ_W λ_W c^(Z)_W u_([g_W]_M)`, and `c^(Z)_W(z) = φ_W(z|_(B_n))`
through the identification. The words `g_W` have length `<= n`, so `[g_W]_M = [g_(W')]_M` iff
`[g_W]_P = [g_(W')]_P`. The grouping is the same, and the image is `Σ_g (Φ_g∘res_(B_n)) u_g`. For
`z ∈ Z_M`, `z|_(B_n) ∈ L_(B_n)(X)`, so every coefficient vanishes. The free algebra on the `E, U`
therefore maps to `B_(Z_M)` killing every relator, which gives `ψ_M`. `∎`

By construction `ψ_M(u_w) = u_([w]_M)` for every `w ∈ F(S)`, and `ψ_M` sends the cylinder
`χ[x|_F = π]`, written as `∏_(h ∈ F) u_h e_(π(h)) u_h^(-1)` with chosen words for `h`, to the
corresponding product in `B_(Z_M)`.

## 2. Theorem A: finite presentation forces an SFT, over every group

**Theorem A.** Let `P` be finitely generated, `X ⊆ A^P` a nonempty subshift and `k` a field. If
`LC(X,k) ⋊ P` is finitely presented, then `X` is a subshift of finite type. More precisely,
`X = Y := {y : every n-window of y lies in L_(B_n)(X)}`, with `n` the maximal relator degree.

*Proof.* Take `M = ∞`, so `Z_∞ = Y` and `ψ = ψ_∞ : B_X -> B_Y` satisfies `ψ(u_h) = u_h` for all
`h ∈ P`. Let `π ∈ A^F` be a pattern on a finite set `F` that does not occur in `X`. Then
`χ[x|_F = π] = 0` in `LC(X,k)`, so the element `∏_(h∈F) u_h e_(π(h)) u_h^(-1)` of `B_X` is `0`.
Its image under `ψ` is the cylinder `χ[y|_F = π]` of `LC(Y,k)`, which must also be `0`. So no
`y ∈ Y` contains `π` at `F`, and by shift invariance nowhere. Hence `Y ⊆ X`. The inclusion `X ⊆ Y`
is clear, so `X = Y`, which is defined by forbidding the finitely many `n`-ball patterns outside
`L_(B_n)(X)`. `∎`

This generalizes `fp-subshift-crossed-products-are-sfts` (the case `P = Z^2`) with the same idea:
a diagonal model on locally admissible configurations. The only new point is that the monomial
bookkeeping of Lemma 0 needs no commutation relations of `P`.

## 3. Theorem B: finite presentation forces a finitely presented acting group

**Theorem B.** Let `P`, `X`, `k` be as in Theorem A. If `LC(X,k) ⋊ P` is finitely presented, then
`P` is finitely presented.

*Proof.* Suppose not. Then `N` is not the normal closure of any finite set, so `N_M ≠ N` for every
finite `M`. Fix `M = 2n` and `w ∈ N \ N_M`. In the free algebra on the `E, U`, the element
`U_w - 1` (with `U_w` the product of the `U_s` along `w`) maps to `u_([w]_P) - 1 = u_1 - 1 = 0` in
`B_X`. So it lies in the ideal generated by the relators, and Lemma 2 gives `ψ_M(u_w - 1) = 0`, that
is, `u_([w]_M) = u_1` in `B_(Z_M)`. By Lemma 1, `Z_M ≠ ∅`, so `LC(Z_M,k) ≠ 0` and the `u_q`,
`q ∈ P_M`, are linearly independent over it. Hence `[w]_M = 1`, that is, `w ∈ N_M`, a
contradiction. `∎`

So the necessary shape of a host in the target now also includes: `P` is finitely presented and `X`
is an SFT. In particular every torsion-free Farrell–Jones group that is finitely generated but not
finitely presented is excluded.

## 4. Theorem C: units of tree-SFT crossed products have doubly exponential word problem

**Theorem C.** Let `d >= 0`, let `F_d` be free on `a_1, ..., a_d` with `S = {a_i^(±1)}`, let
`X ⊆ A^(F_d)` be a nonempty SFT and `k` a finite field. Then every finitely generated subgroup `G` of
the unit group of `B = LC(X,k) ⋊ F_d` has, for any finite generating set, a word-problem algorithm
running in time at most `C * 2^(2^(C*l))` on words of length `l`, for a constant `C` depending on
`G`, its generating set, `X` and `k`.

The algorithm need not be uniform in `(X, G)`; only its existence with this time bound matters for
`complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal`.

*Step 1 (nearest-neighbour recoding).* Let `X` be defined by forbidden patterns on `B_r`. The block
map `β(x)(g) = (g^(-1) x)|_(B_r)` is a conjugacy from `X` onto the nearest-neighbour SFT `X'` over
the alphabet `L_(B_r)(X)`: letters are required to be legal, and neighbours `α` at `g` and `α'` at
`gs` must agree on overlaps, `α(s h) = α'(h)` whenever `|h| <= r` and `|s h| <= r`. From `y ∈ X'`
recover `x(g) = y(g)(1)`, and by induction along geodesics `y(g) = (g^(-1) x)|_(B_r)`, so all
`r`-windows of `x` are legal and `x ∈ X`. A conjugacy commutes with the shifts, so it induces an
isomorphism `LC(X,k) ⋊ F_d ≅ LC(X',k) ⋊ F_d` fixing every `u_g`. The alphabet `L_(B_r)(X)` is a
fixed finite set, hard-coded in the algorithm. So assume `X` is nearest-neighbour: there are
relations `R_s ⊆ A × A` with `R_(s^(-1))` the transpose of `R_s`, and
`X = {x : (x(g), x(gs)) ∈ R_s for all g, s}`.

*Step 2 (pruning).* Let `A_∞` be the largest subset `A_0 ⊆ A` such that every `a ∈ A_0` and every
`s ∈ S` admit some `b ∈ A_0` with `(a, b) ∈ R_s`. It is the union of all such subsets, since the
condition is preserved by unions. The set of letters occurring in points of `X` has this property,
so every point of `X` uses only letters of `A_∞`.

**Lemma 3 (extension on the tree).** Let `F ⊆ F_d` be finite and connected in the Cayley tree, and
let `π ∈ (A_∞)^F` satisfy `(π(g), π(gs)) ∈ R_s` whenever `g, gs ∈ F`. Then `π ∈ L_F(X)`.

*Proof.* List the vertices outside `F` as `v_1, v_2, ...` in nondecreasing distance to `F`. Every
`v_j` has exactly one neighbour in `F ∪ {v_1, ..., v_(j-1)}`: at least one on a geodesic to `F`,
and at most one, since two would close a cycle through the connected set `F ∪ {v_i : i < j}` in a
tree. Put `x|_F = π`, and if `v_j = u s` for its unique earlier neighbour `u`, choose
`x(v_j) ∈ A_∞` with `(x(u), x(v_j)) ∈ R_s`, which pruning allows. Every edge of the tree either
lies in `F`, where `π` is legal, or was checked when its later endpoint was defined. So `x ∈ X`. `∎`

Hence `L_(B_M)(X)` is the set of locally legal `A_∞`-patterns on `B_M`. Brute force decides it in
time `O(|A|^(|B_M|) * |B_M| * d)`, with `|B_M| <= (2d+1)^M`.

*Step 3 (tables).* Every `f ∈ LC(X,k)` is a finite combination of cylinder functions, so it has the
form `f(x) = φ(x|_(B_M))` for some `M` and some `φ : L_(B_M)(X) -> k`. Let `γ_1, ..., γ_m` be the
generators of `G` together with their inverses. Fix `R >= 1` such that every `γ_i` is
`Σ_(g ∈ B_R) φ_(i,g)(x|_(B_R)) u_g`. These finitely many tables are hard-coded. A **table of radius
`(K, M)`** is a map `T : B_K × L_(B_M)(X) -> k`, representing `Σ_(g ∈ B_K) T(g, x|_(B_M)) u_g`.
Two tables with `M` at least the needed radius represent the same element iff they agree
everywhere on `L_(B_M)(X)`. Indeed each pattern of the language occurs in some point, and the `u_g`
are a basis. Elements of `F_d` are stored as reduced words.

*Step 4 (multiplication by a generator).* `(f u_g)(f' u_h) = f · (f'∘g^(-1)) u_(gh)`, and
`(f'∘g^(-1))(x) = φ'((g^(-1) x)|_(B_R))` with `(g^(-1)x)(h') = x(g h')`, which depends only on
`x|_(B_(|g|+R))`. So if `w = γ_(i_1) ... γ_(i_j)` is represented by a table of radius `(Rj, Rj)`,
then `w γ_(i_(j+1))` is represented by a table of radius `(R(j+1), R(j+1))`. For each pattern
`p ∈ L_(B_(R(j+1)))(X)`, each `g ∈ B_(Rj)` and each `h ∈ B_R`, add
`T_w(g, p|_(B_(Rj))) * φ_(i,h)((g^(-1) p)|_(B_R))` to the entry `(gh, p)`. Here `(g^(-1)p)|_(B_R)`
is read off `p` at the vertices `g h'`, `|h'| <= R`. The time per step is polynomial in
`|B_(R(j+1))|` times `|L_(B_(R(j+1)))(X)|`, times constants for field arithmetic.

*Step 5 (decision).* A word `w` of length `l` is trivial in `G` iff `w = 1` in `B`, iff its table
`T_w` of radius `(Rl, Rl)` has `T_w(1, p) = 1` and `T_w(g, p) = 0` for `g ≠ 1`, for all
`p ∈ L_(B_(Rl))(X)`. Computing `L_(B_(Rl))(X)` (Lemma 3), running `l` steps of Step 4 and checking
takes time at most `poly((2d+1)^(Rl)) * |A|^((2d+1)^(Rl)) * l`. This is at most `C * 2^(2^(C l))`
for a suitable `C`, since `|A|^((2d+1)^(Rl)) = 2^(log_2|A| * 2^(Rl log_2(2d+1)))`. Changing the
finite generating set changes only `C`. `∎`

**Remark (where the tree is used).** Only Lemma 3 uses that `F_d` is free. It makes the language of
the SFT computable by local checking, with a bound that is uniform across SFTs up to constants. For
`Z^2`, languages of SFTs can be non-recursive, since Robinson-type tilings encode the halting
problem into extendability of patterns, and the proof gives nothing.

## 5. The kill: free acting groups never serve every decidable group

`complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal` (ESTABLISHED), verbatim: "Let `T: N -> N` be
recursive and let `K` be a class of finitely generated groups such that every `S in K` has, for
some finite generating set, a word-problem algorithm running in time
`C_S*T(C_S*l) + C_S*l + C_S`. Then some two-generated group with solvable word problem embeds in no
member of `K`."

**Corollary D.** There is a two-generated group `H` with solvable word problem such that, for no
`d >= 0`, no nonempty compact totally disconnected `F_d`-space `X` and no finite field `k`, is there
a finitely presented algebra `B = LC(X,k) ⋊ F_d` with a unital embedding `k[H] -> B`. Neither
simplicity nor centre enters. In particular the target claim fails for every host with `P` free
(including `P = Z` and `P = 1`), whatever `X` is.

*Proof.* Take `T(l) = 2^(2^l)`, and let `K` be the class of finitely generated subgroups of unit
groups `(LC(X,k) ⋊ F_d)^x`, over all `d`, all nonempty SFTs `X` and all finite fields `k`. By
Theorem C every member satisfies the hypothesis, so there is a two-generated decidable `H` that
embeds in no member of `K`. Suppose `k[H] -> B` is a unital embedding with `B` finitely presented.
Then `B` is finitely generated, so `X` is conjugate to an `F_d`-subshift. This is the necessary
shape of the target claim, artifact `fjc-crossed-product-leavitt-hosts-2026-09-16`, Section 6; the
conjugacy induces an isomorphism of crossed products. By Theorem A the subshift is an SFT. The
embedding restricts to an injective group homomorphism `H -> B^x`, since distinct group elements are
distinct basis vectors of `k[H]`. Its image is a finitely generated subgroup, a member of `K`. This
contradicts the choice of `H`. `∎`

**Named invariant and failing step.** The invariant is the *local extension property of tree SFTs*
(Lemma 3). Every locally legal pattern extends, so the ball languages of every SFT over a free group
are decidable in time `2^(O(|B_M|))`. That gives every unit group of every host a uniformly doubly
exponential word problem. Every member of the family "`P` free, `X` any, `B` finitely presented"
dies at the same step: the unital embedding `F_2[G] -> B` for the hard input `G = H` of
`complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal`. Finite presentation enters only through
Theorem A, to make `X` an SFT.

This retires Attempt 4 of the target claim as a *family*, not only the single boundary host.
The Leavitt path algebra `LC(∂F_d, F_2) ⋊ F_d` is finitely presented, simple, has centre `F_2`, and
contains `F_2[F_d]`. Yet no host of this dynamical shape can ever contain `F_2[H]`.

## 6. Calibration: the obstruction is not dynamical

The boundary action `∂F_d` is a nearest-neighbour SFT: letters `s ∈ S`, with the rule that along
each edge `{g, gs}` exactly one of `x(g) = s` and `x(gs) = s^(-1)` holds. Every vertex then has out
degree one and every edge is oriented, so all edges point towards one end. The action is minimal,
topologically free and infinite, with a finitely presented simple crossed product with centre
`F_2` (`boundary-crossed-product-is-a-leavitt-path-algebra`). So free groups do carry SFT hosts of
exactly the shape the target asks for: minimality, freeness, finite presentation and simplicity are
all available. No invariant built from ends, periodic points or minimality can kill the free-group
family. The death in Corollary D is purely a complexity phenomenon, which is why it had to come from
`complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal`.

(An earlier guess in this session, "minimal SFTs over `F_d` are finite", is false, by this example.)

## 7. The sharpened necessary shape, and a new prerequisite

Combining Sections 2–5 with Section 6 of `fjc-crossed-product-leavitt-hosts-2026-09-16`: any family
of hosts witnessing the target claim uses pairs `(P, X)` with

1. `P` finitely presented, torsion-free and Farrell–Jones (Theorem B);
2. `X` a minimal, topologically free SFT over `P` (Theorem A plus the old Section 6);
3. `P` not free (Corollary D);
4. if `P` is amenable, then every finitely generated group with solvable word problem is
   `F_2`-linear sofic, by `amenable-minimal-crossed-products-have-faithful-rank-models`
   (ESTABLISHED, unreviewed). That result needs only minimality, so this upgrades the heuristic
   Attempt 3 of the target;
5. **(prerequisite, open, can fail on its own)** the hosts used must have no common recursive
   word-problem bound for their unit subgroups.

A sketch of what item 5 demands (not claimed as a theorem). Steps 3–5 of Theorem C work over any
finitely generated `P` with solvable word problem, given an algorithm for the ball languages
`L_(B_M)(X)`. The resulting bound is recursive in `l`, built from the word-problem time of `P` and
the language time of `X`. So if some recursive `T` bounds, up to constants, both the word problem of
`P` and the ball languages of every SFT used over `P`, then the family dies exactly as in
Corollary D. A viable `P` must therefore carry *minimal, topologically free, finitely presented
(quantum-rigid)* SFTs whose ball languages have no uniform recursive time bound. For `Z^d` all three
parts are open (`free-minimal-z2-sft-is-quantum-rigid`). For free groups the language part fails
outright (Lemma 3). This isolates a symbolic-dynamics prerequisite: *hard minimal SFTs with
finitely presented crossed products*. It can fail independently of the Farrell–Jones and
simplicity parts.
