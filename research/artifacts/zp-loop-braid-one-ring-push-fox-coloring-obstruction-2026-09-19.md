# One-ring pushes in the loop braid descending link: the mutual link is not a family member

Lane swarm-0917-w14-w14-z-last1, written 2026-09-19. These notes are unreviewed.
- Target hole: `loop-braid-forbidden-ring-coning-statement` (CS).
- This answers the open sub-question in §7 of `zp-loop-braid-forbidden-ring-push-reduction-2026-09-17.md`
  (w8): for the one-ring push `b' = A_vc·b0`, is `lk(b0) ∩ lk(b')` the family member
  `L_c ≅ X_{n−1}(F ∪ {f, c})`?
- The answer is **no**. The mutual link is a proper subcomplex of L_c. It is cut out by a condition
  that the abelian invariant e cannot see and D4 cannot see, but Fox 3-colorings do see.
- The same answer applies to the §0.5 repair in
  `zp-loop-braid-cx-antisymmetric-clasp-obstruction-2026-09-18.md` (w11): pushing `u_0 = (a,b)` by
  `A_av` is the same configuration after renaming rings.

## 0. Conventions

These are the conventions of the w8 artifact and of `mutual_link.py`.
- `P = PLB_n = PΣAut_n`, with the group law being composition.
- `A_ij : x_i ↦ x_j x_i x_j^{-1}`.
- `X_n(F) ⊆ X_n = L_n(LB_*)`, with strict fundamental domain `Q ≅ OM(K_n)`
  (`pure-loop-braid-group-is-colimit-of-cabling-stabilizers`).
- A vertex is `p[τ] = p P_τ`, where τ is an ordered pair of free rings.
- The cabling subgroup `P_(a,b)` is generated by three families:
  - `A_ij` for `i, j ∉ {a, b}`;
  - `θ_k : x_k ↦ (x_a x_b) x_k (x_a x_b)^{-1}`;
  - `ζ_q : x_a, x_b ↦ x_q(·)x_q^{-1}`.
- Since Q is a strict fundamental domain and the stabilizers fix it pointwise:
  - `p[τ] ∈ lk(b0)` iff `p ∈ P_b0 P_τ`;
  - `p[τ] ∈ lk(g b0)` iff `g^{-1} p ∈ P_b0 P_τ`.
- Fix distinct free rings `v, w, c`, and put `b0 = [(v,w)]` and `b' = A_vc^{ε} b0` with `ε = ±1`.
  - `b' ≠ b0`, because `A_vc ∉ P_b0`: the λ of §1 vanishes on `P_b0` and `λ(A_vc) = 1`.
  - By the Link Lemma (w8 Lemma 2.1), `lk(b0) ≅ X_{n−1}(F' ∪ {f})`.
  - Its c-avoiding part is `L_c ≅ X_{n−1}(F' ∪ {f, c})`, where f is the fused ring.

## 1. Theorem A

**Theorem A.** Let `n ≥ 7`, let F be a set of rings, and let `v, w, c` be distinct free rings. Let
`b0 = [(v,w)]` and `b' = A_vc^{ε} b0` with `ε = ±1`, and write `I = lk(b0) ∩ lk(b')` in `X_n(F)`.
- (i) No vertex of I has a type meeting c. Hence `I ⊆ L_c`.
- (ii) If there are free rings `a, d ∉ {v, w, c}`, i.e. free count `N ≥ 5`, then the vertex
  `u = A_ca[(a,d)]` lies in `L_c` but not in `lk(b')`. Hence `I ⊊ L_c`.

The bound `n ≥ 7` comes only from the cited prerequisite. The argument uses nothing beyond the
generator description of `P_τ` and the membership criterion of §0.

**Proof of (i): the clasp homomorphism.**
- Let `e : P → Z^{n(n−1)}` be the conjugator exponent homomorphism of
  `zp-loop-braid-descending-links-model-2026-09-13.md` §3.
- Consider a vertex of `lk(b0)` whose type meets c. Its type avoids v and w, so it is `z = (c,d)` or
  `(d,c)` with d free. Write the vertex as `s[z]` with `s ∈ P_b0`.
- If it lay in `lk(b')`, then `A_vc^{−ε} s ∈ P_b0 P_z`, so `A_vc^{ε} ∈ P_b0 P_z P_b0`.
- Put `λ = e_vc − e_vd − e_wc + e_wd`. λ is a homomorphism, and it vanishes on both subgroups:
  - On the generators of `P_b0`, `e_vj = e_wj` for every `j ∉ {v, w}`. The only nonzero values
    are `ζ_q`, with `e_vq = e_wq = 1`, and `θ_k`, whose values lie in rows `k ∉ {v, w}`.
    So `λ(P_b0) = 0`.
  - On the generators of `P_z`, `e_ic = e_id` for every `i ∉ {c, d}`. Every `θ_k` has
    `e_kc = e_kd = 1`, and the other generators have no entries in columns c or d outside rows c
    and d. So `λ(P_z) = 0`.
- But `λ(A_vc^{ε}) = ε ≠ 0`. This contradiction proves (i). □

**Proof of (ii): a Fox 3-coloring certificate.**
- **Membership in `L_c`.** `A_ca ∈ P_b0`, because `c, a ∉ {v, w}`, and `[(a,d)] ∈ lk_Q(b0)`. So
  `u ∈ lk(b0)`, and its type `(a,d)` avoids c.
- **Criterion.** `u ∈ lk(b')` iff `p := A_vc^{−ε} ∘ A_ca ∈ H K`, where `H = P_b0` and
  `K = P_(a,d)`.
- **The P-set Ω.** Let Ω be the set of homomorphisms `ρ : F_n → S_3` that send every `x_i` to a
  transposition. Identify ρ with its color vector `c ∈ F_3^n`, where `x_i ↦ r_{c_i}` and r is the
  reflection of the triangle, with `r_α r_β r_α = r_{2α−β}`.
  - P acts on Ω on the right by `ρ·φ = ρ ∘ φ`, since `φ(x_i)` is conjugate to `x_i`.
  - `(ρ·φ)·ψ = ρ·(φ∘ψ)`.
- **Action of the generators on colors.**
  - `A_ij^{±1} : c_i ↦ 2c_j − c_i`.
  - `θ_k^{±1}` (for the cable `(a,b)`): `c_k ↦ c_k ± (2c_a − 2c_b)`.
  - `ζ_q^{±1}`: `c_a ↦ 2c_q − c_a` and `c_b ↦ 2c_q − c_b`.
- **Two invariant sets.**
  - (H) `D = {c_v ≠ c_w}` is invariant under the generators of `P_b0`:
    - `A_ij` and `θ_k` do not change `c_v` or `c_w`;
    - `ζ_q` negates `c_v − c_w`.
  - (K) `S = {c_x = 1 for x ∉ {a,d}, c_a = c_d}` is invariant under the generators of `P_(a,d)`:
    - `A_ij` sends `1 ↦ 2 − 1 = 1`;
    - `θ_k` adds `±2(c_a − c_d) = 0`;
    - `ζ_q` sends `c_a, c_d ↦ 2 − c_a, 2 − c_d`, which stay equal.

    Each generator permutes the finite set Ω, so an invariant subset is also invariant under the
    inverse. Hence S is K-invariant, and likewise D is H-invariant.
- **The certificate.** Take `ρ = (c_v, c_w, c_c, c_a, c_d; others) = (0, 1, 2, 0, 0; 1, …, 1) ∈ D`.
  - `ρ·A_vc^{−ε}` changes only `c_v`, to `2·2 − 0 = 1`.
  - Applying `A_ca` then changes only `c_c`, to `2·0 − 2 = 1`.
  - So `ρ·p = (1, 1, 1, 0, 0; 1, …, 1) ∈ S`.
- **Contradiction.** Suppose `p = hk` with `h ∈ H` and `k ∈ K`.
  - Then `ρ·h = (ρ·p)·k^{−1} ∈ S`, so `c_v = c_w = 1`.
  - But also `ρ·h ∈ D`.
  - This is a contradiction, so `u ∉ lk(b')`. □

**Remark (which obstructions see it).**
- The abelian test `e(p) ∈ e(H) + e(K)` passes, so (ii) is invisible to e and to every clasp
  homomorphism.
- It is also invisible to D4-representations, which are class-2 nilpotent: in the scan of §2,
  D4 flags nothing.
- It is detected by Fox 3-colorings, and by A4 with the same pattern. This matches the extra
  3-torsion in `H_1` found at N = 7 in w8 §6.

## 2. Machine checks (experiments/loop-braid-one-ring-push-2026-09-17/)

- **`fox_certificate.py`, output in `fox_certificate_output.txt`.** For `n = 5..10` it recomputes
  `ρ·p` by breadth-first search over all generators and inverses. It confirms three things:
  - the K-orbit of `ρ·p` has size 2 and satisfies `c_v = c_w`;
  - the H-orbit of ρ has size `2·3^{n−1}` and satisfies `c_v ≠ c_w`;
  - the conclusion holds for both signs ε.
- **`orbit_test.py`, output in `orbit_test_output.txt`.** This is a general finite-orbit
  non-membership test on `Hom(F_n, G)` for G ∈ {S3, D4, A4}, with n = 5, 6, 7 for S3 and n = 5 for
  D4 and A4. It checks:
  - (0) Sanity: no element of `P_b0 P_τ` is ever certified.
  - (0′) `A_vc` is certified outside `P_b0 P_τ` for every τ that meets c, consistent with (i).
  - (1) Among the vertices `s[τ]` of `L_c` with s a generator of `P_b0` (or its inverse), the
    certified non-members of `lk(b')`, for every τ = (x,y) avoiding c and both signs, are exactly
    `A_cx^{±1}, A_cy^{±1}, θ_x^{±1}, θ_y^{±1}, ζ_x^{±1}, ζ_y^{±1}`.
    - With S3 and A4 this is the same list for every n tested.
    - With D4 the list is empty.
  - (2) Control against the ESTABLISHED `loop-braid-pair-twist-push-mutual-link`. For the push
    `τ_wc`, no vertex `t[τ]` with t in the triple-cable subgroup is ever certified. This passes for
    every configuration.
- **Not certified.** The cases `A_xc, A_yc, A_xy, A_yx, θ_c, ζ_c` are not certified. All but `θ_c`
  are provably in I. Each such s lies in `P_b0` and commutes with `g = A_vc^{ε}`:
  - `[A_vc, A_xc] = 1` by McCool;
  - `A_xy` and `A_vc` have disjoint support;
  - `ζ_c = A_vc A_wc`.

  Also `g ∈ P_τ` for τ avoiding v and c. Hence `g^{−1} s[τ] = s g^{−1}[τ] = s[τ]`, which shows
  `s[τ] ∈ lk(b')`. These tests do not decide `θ_c[τ]`.

## 3. What dies, and what stays open

**Dead (precise obstruction).** The w8 §7 hope that a one-ring push has mutual link a
forbidden-ring family member is refuted, specifically its natural candidate `I = L_c`.
- The same holds for the w11 §0.5 repair in the `A_av` form.
- Any nerve argument that treats `lk(b0) ∩ lk(A_vc^{±1} b0)` as a copy of `X_{n−1}(F ∪ {f,c})`
  to feed the CS induction is invalid.
- By (i), `I ⊆ L_c`, and `L_c` is highly connected by induction. But `I ↪ L_c` is not the identity,
  and nothing yet computes the connectivity of I.

**Open.**
- (Q1) Is I the `(P_b0 ∩ P_b')`-orbit of the part of Q avoiding v, w, c and F?
  - The easy inclusion "⊇" holds, and `P_b0 ∩ P_b'` contains `A_xc`, `A_xy` and `ζ_c` by §2.
  - The reverse inclusion is unproved.
- (Q2) What is the connectivity of I? Here the obstruction is 3-local, which parallels the N = 7
  3-torsion of w8 §6.
- **CS stays OPEN.**
  - The pair-twist push is still the only push with a proved family-member mutual link
    (`loop-braid-pair-twist-push-mutual-link`, of free count N−3).
  - Its degree loss is recorded in `loop-braid-pair-twist-push-complexity-fails`.

**Literature (web search, 2026-09-19).** No resolution of Zaremsky Problem 2.12 was found. The only
related statement is Witzel–Zaremsky Conjecture 10.3.
