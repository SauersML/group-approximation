## 9.1 Re-derivation of Theorem 6

- **Reduction.**
  - A matrix has finitely many entries, so `F` may be finitely generated. For `trdeg <= 1` this is Theorem 4.
  - For transcendence degree 2, `F = Qbar(V)`, where `V` is the `Qbar`-Zariski closure of a point `P_0`
    generated by the entries: an irreducible surface with generic point `P_0`.
  - A common denominator `h in Qbar[V]` with `h(P_0) != 0` scales rows and leaves kernels unchanged.
- **Normalization.**
  - Noether normalization gives a finite surjective `π: V -> A^2` over `Qbar`.
  - Generic freeness gives `g != 0` with `Qbar[V]_g` free of rank `e = [Qbar(V) : Qbar(x)]` over
    `Qbar[x]_g`.
  - In characteristic 0, `π` is étale over a dense open set, which is absorbed into `g`.
- **Step 1 (fibre sums).** Take a basis `ω_1..ω_e` and form `ρ(M)`, made polynomial by a power of `g`.
  - At `w in U(C)` the fibre algebra is `C^e` with `e` distinct points. So `(ω_i(P_j))` is an invertible
    scalar matrix, which commutes with `G`, and it conjugates `ρ(M)(w)` to `(+)_j M(P_j)`.
  - Algebraic `w` off `Y`: `K`, by Lemma J (part 5 PASS).
  - Algebraically independent `w`: `K`, by Theorem 5, item 1 (part 5 PASS).
  - **`trdeg w = 1`, justification made explicit.**
    - `w` is a generic point of an irreducible `Qbar`-curve `W ⊄ Y`.
    - The statement of Theorem 4 gives integrality and `Aut(C/Qbar)`-invariance. Invariance moves `w` only
      among generic points of `W`, so it cannot compare `w` with algebraic points.
    - What is needed is constancy along the curve. That is what the *proof* of Theorem 4 gives (§8 of the
      transcendence-degree-one artifact, Steps 1–2; groups part 10 §10.2 PASS), and it is stated there for
      smooth curves.
    - So pull `ρ(M)` back to the normalization `W~ -> W`, a smooth affine curve over `Qbar`. The kernel
      dimension is constant on `W~` off finitely many algebraic points, and its value at a point of `W`
      equals the value at any preimage.
    - Infinitely many algebraic points of `W` lie off `Y` and off that finite set, and Lemma J gives `K`
      there. So the value at `w` is `K`.
- **Step 2 (continuity).**
  - `k(P) = τ(χ_{0}(|M(P)|))` is upper semicontinuous under norm-continuous families: weak convergence of
    spectral measures plus portmanteau on closed sets.
  - Étale fibres move continuously. So for `P'` near `P`,
    `k(P') = K - sum_(i>=2) k(P'_i) >= k(P) - (e-1) ε`. With upper semicontinuity at `P`, `k` is continuous
    on `V°(C)`.
- **Step 3 (constancy and invariance).**
  - `k` takes integer values at algebraic points, by Strong Atiyah over `Qbar`, since `M(P)` is over
    `Qbar[G]`. These points are dense, so the continuous `k` is integer-valued, hence locally constant.
  - `V° = V \ π^-1(V(g) ∪ Y)` is `V` minus a proper `Qbar`-closed set, so `V°(C)` is connected, and `k` is
    constant there.
  - `P_0` and every `σ(P_0)`, `σ in Aut(C/Qbar)`, are generic points, so they lie in `V°`. Also
    `σ(M(P_0)) = M(σ P_0)`, because `M` has coefficients in `Qbar[V][G]`. So all of these carry the same
    integer.
