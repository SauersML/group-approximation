## Directive step 1: can nonabelian lamps realize the difference operator?

**No, for every finite lamp group.**
- Any element of `B wr_Omega Q` that normalizes a subgroup `V` of the base conjugates it
  as a twisted permutation: it moves fibres, and applies an inner automorphism of `B`
  inside each fibre.
- Lamp commutators `y |-> [y, f]` are not automorphisms of `V`. The conjoint `a'_1`
  must be an automorphism equal to `a_1 - 1` on `V`, and it too acts as a twisted
  permutation.

**The mechanism, in the proof of lemma 1:**
- `V` embeds in `⊕_omega V_omega`, the sum over `V`-orbits of finite elementary abelian
  groups, and conjugation moves the summands.
- On an infinite orbit of `a` on summands, the sum is a free `F_p[t^(±1)]`-module of finite
  rank, where `(t - 1)`-divisible elements vanish.
- On a finite orbit the sum is a finite group, so `a` has finite order there.
- But `a_1 - 1` is onto `V` and the `a_1`-orbit of `x(q_j)` is infinite.

No GAP computation was needed: the obstruction is uniform in `B`.

## Sharpness: the finiteness hypotheses cannot be dropped

**The letter's own subgroup is Baumslag's group.** Let `x = x(q_j)`, `a = a_1`,
`a' = a'_1` and `R = F_p[t^(±1), (t-1)^(-1)]`.
- The submodule `V_j` generated by `x` is a cyclic module over `R`, since `a'` acts as
  `a - 1` and is invertible.
- `R` is a localization of `F_p[t]`, hence a PID, and `V_j` is infinite. So `V_j ≅ R`,
  free of rank one.
- `t` and `t - 1` are multiplicatively independent units, so `<a, a'> ≅ Z^2` acts
  faithfully.
- `T ∩ <H_1, H_2> = 1`, because `gamma` is an isomorphism onto `T_1 x| M` and sends the
  letters into `M`.
- So `<x, a, a'> ≅ R x| Z^2`. With `x = t - 1` this is Baumslag's group
  `F_p[x^(±1), (1+x)^(-1)] x| Z^2`, which `char-p-linear-groups-satisfy-permutational-boone-higman`
  already places in `B_A`.

**Each hypothesis alone is satisfiable** (proof route of lemma 1):
- `L = R x| Z^2` acts faithfully on the disjoint union of the coset spaces `L/K`, over
  finite-index `K <= R`, with finite `R`-orbits. This is (H1) without (H2).
- The affine action has a single orbit. This is (H2) without (H1).

So the lemma cannot be pushed to all placements. What it closes is exactly the finite
lamp and finitary placements.

## The natural affine actor

- **The isomorphism.** `gamma` is an isomorphism, by Lemmas 4.10, 4.11 and 4.13:
  injective on `T`, and the kernel lies in `T`.
- **Shapes of the letters.** Each letter acts on the vectors at a word `w` by one of four
  shapes: (P) identity; (N) triangular plus a shift to `w a_j`; (N-1), its conjoint; or
  (A), plus a shift to `w A_j` or to `psi(w)`.
- **Recomputed inverses.** They are listed in `kms-natural-affine-actor-orbit-count-proof`.
  The extracted PDF text displays the inverse of `a_j` at `i_j = 3` as
  `z(i - e_j, w)^(-1) z(i, w)^(-1) z(i, w a_j)^(-1)`. The value checked here is
  `e_1 - e_2 - 2 e_3 - S e_3`, so the extraction (or the printed formula) differs.
  Nothing downstream uses the displayed form.
- **Invariant subspaces.** The letters never shorten words, so the spans `U_n` over words
  with at least `n` a-letters, together with all words of `W_0`, are invariant and
  strictly decreasing.
- **General invariant.** An orbit-finite affine actor has only finitely many
  submodules: each orbit spans its own cyclic submodule, and the cyclic submodules
  determine every submodule. So any affine host for `G(MM_k)` needs a module with
  finitely many `W`-submodules, which `T_1` under `M` does not have.
