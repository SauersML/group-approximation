# The cyclic fiber-product route to finite rope synchronization

This note isolates the positive profinite geometry of the three-generator
bidirectional bridge and shows how it supplies synchronized rope quotients for
the graph benign witness.

## 1. The positive bridge is a cyclic fiber product

Put

```text
P=F(x_1,y) times F(x_2,t)
```

and let `epsilon_1,epsilon_2` be exponent sums in `x_1,x_2`, respectively.
For

```text
chi(g_1,g_2)=epsilon_1(g_1)-epsilon_2(g_2),
```

the embedding of `B3(1)` is

```text
x |-> (x_1,x_2),       y |-> (y,1),       t |-> (1,t).         (1)
```

The normal closure of `y` in the first free factor has free basis
`x_1^n y x_1^-n`, and the normal closure of `t` in the second has free basis
`x_2^m t x_2^-m`.  Every element of `ker(chi)` has equal `x_1`- and
`x_2`-exponent, so after removing a power of `(x_1,x_2)` it lies in the
product of those two normal closures.  These are exactly the three families
generated by `(1)`.  Hence

```text
H=B3(1)=ker(chi),              P/H=Z.                           (2)
```

## 2. Every finite congruence of `H` refines through `P`

Let `J` be a finite-index normal subgroup of the three-generated group `H`.
Choose `z=(x_1,1)`, whose image generates `P/H`.  Conjugation by `z` permutes
the subgroups of `H` having index `[H:J]`.  There are only finitely many such
subgroups because `H` is finitely generated.  Therefore

```text
M=intersection_(n in Z) z^n J z^-n
```

is a finite intersection, is finite-index normal in `H`, is invariant under
`z`, and satisfies `M<=J`.  Since `P=H semidirect <z>`, it is normal in `P`.

Conjugation by `z` has some finite order `d` on the finite group `H/M`.
Thus

```text
(H/M) semidirect C_d
```

is a finite quotient of `P` whose kernel intersects `H` in exactly `M`.  For
`J=ker(beta)`, this proves

```text
ker(r|H)=M <= ker(beta).                                      (3)
```

The proof is effective.  A finite quotient of the finitely generated `H`
gives a finite coset action; iterate the computable conjugation action until
the finite-index subgroup repeats, take the finite product action for the
intersection, compute the induced automorphism of the resulting finite
group, and take its finite order.

## 3. The graph benign witness

Let `F->Q=F/N` be any finite-rank recursive presentation with an embedding
`j:Q->P` satisfying the refining finite CEP `(3)`.  Define

```text
K=F times P,
e(f)=(f,1),
L=graph(jq)={(f,j(q(f))):f in F}.                              (4)
```

Then `K` is finitely presented, `L` is finitely generated, and
`L intersect e(F)=e(N)`, so `(4)` is a benign witness.

It also has the right profinite geometry.  The graph is closed in `K`: for
`(f,p)` off the graph, residual finiteness of `P` gives a finite quotient
`r` with `r(p) != r(j(q(f)))`, and

```text
(f',p') |-> (r(j(q(f'))),r(p'))
```

sends the graph diagonally while keeping the chosen point off the diagonal.
For any Britton-reduced word in

```text
Gamma=<K,v | [v,L]=1>,
```

finitely many such quotients preserve all non-pinching coefficients.  The
corresponding central HNN over a finite base is virtually free and residually
finite, so another finite quotient separates the word.  Hence `Gamma` is
effectively residually finite.

## 4. The synchronized finite quotient

Given `beta:Q->B`, choose `r:P->C` as in `(3)` and map

```text
K -> C times C,       (f,p) |-> (r(j(q(f))),r(p)).              (5)
```

The graph `L` maps to the diagonal.  Therefore sending the central HNN letter
`v` to the coordinate swap extends `(5)` to

```text
lambda_beta:Gamma -> (C times C) semidirect C_2.                (6)
```

For `S=<F,F^v>` the image lies in `C times C`, and first-coordinate
projection is `r j tau`.  Thus

```text
ker(lambda_beta|S) <= ker(beta tau).                            (7)
```

Products with independent cofinal finite quotients of `Gamma` and `Q` give
the equality of graph-edge kernels required by `(RPS1)`.

## 5. The sole remaining uniformity problem

For the positive group `B3(1)`, Sections 1--4 give the complete profinite
rope construction.  The finite-presentation reduction, however, must output
one benign witness uniformly before knowing whether the c.e. set is finite
or infinite.  The remaining target is a relative Higman compiler whose
benign data specialize on `INF` to `(4)` while retaining ordinary benignness
on `FIN`.  Merely multiplying an arbitrary Higman witness by `(4)` is not
enough: the arbitrary factor remains in the positive vertex group and can be
non-MF.
