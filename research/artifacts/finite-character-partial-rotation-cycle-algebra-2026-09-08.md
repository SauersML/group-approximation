# Exact cycle criteria for finite-character partial rotations

2026-09-08. A finite network of literal partial transports may leave the
normalizer of the subgroup defining an augmentation corner. Nevertheless,
if its vertices are orthogonal finite-character atoms, its complete algebra,
including arbitrary additive products of the associated partial rotations,
has an exact matrix decomposition over a finite-character loop algebra.

This gives a coefficient-level criterion for closing cycles. In particular,
a triangular comparison of two rotated projections closes exactly when its
literal return word belongs to a specified character kernel. Neither local
transport identities nor the existence of all local matrix packets imply
that additional group relation. This is a criterion for a particular
construction mechanism, not a construction of the full `B_loop` map.

## 1. The network and its exact algebra

Let `G` be a group. Let `f_0,...,f_(m-1)` be nonzero, pairwise orthogonal
projections of the form

```text
f_i=e_(H_i,chi_i)
   =|H_i|^-1 sum_(h in H_i) conjugate(chi_i(h)) h,
```

where `H_i<=G` is finite and `chi_i` is a one-dimensional unitary
character. Suppose a finite connected directed graph on these vertices
has group-labelled edges `e:i->j` with

```text
g_e f_i g_e^-1=f_j,
v_e=g_e f_i.
```

Reverse edges use `g_e^-1` and `v_e^*`. Put `p=sum_i f_i`.
Equality of the Fourier sums shows that every edge conjugates the pair
`(H_i,chi_i)` to `(H_j,chi_j)`. In particular all `H_i` have the same
order `h`.

Choose a spanning tree rooted at zero. Let `h_i` be the ordered group word
along its path from zero to `i`, with `h_0=1`, and put

```text
u_i=h_i f_0,
u_i^*u_i=f_0,          u_i u_i^*=f_i,
u_i^*u_j=0            when i!=j.
```

For an edge `e:i->j`, define its return word

```text
ell_e=h_j^-1 g_e h_i.
```

It satisfies `ell_e f_0 ell_e^-1=f_0`. Because every Fourier coefficient
of `f_0` on `H_0` is nonzero, comparison of supports and coefficients gives

```text
ell_e H_0 ell_e^-1=H_0,
chi_0(ell_e h ell_e^-1)=chi_0(h)       for h in H_0.
```

Let

```text
L=<H_0, ell_e for all edges e>,
D=f_0 C[L] f_0.
```

Then `H_0` is finite and normal in `L`, and `f_0` is central in `C[L]`.
The algebra `A` generated by the `f_i,v_e,v_e^*`, with unit `p`, is exactly

```text
A ~= M_m(D).                                               (1)
```

The map sends a matrix `(a_ij)` to `sum_(i,j) u_i a_ij u_j^*`.
The displayed identities for the `u_i` prove multiplicativity and show
injectivity by multiplying on the left by `u_i^*` and on the right by
`u_j`. Tree paths put every `u_i` in `A`, while

```text
u_j^* v_e u_i=f_0 ell_e,
v_e=u_j (f_0 ell_e) u_i^*.
```

These identities prove both inclusions in (1). No assumption that a
single finite subgroup contains all the `H_i` or all edge supports is used.

Let `Gamma=L/H_0` and choose representatives `s_gamma`, with `s_1=1`.
The elements

```text
b_gamma=f_0 s_gamma
```

are a basis of `D`: they have disjoint, nonzero supports in distinct
`H_0`-cosets. Their multiplication is

```text
b_gamma b_delta=omega(gamma,delta) b_(gamma delta),
omega(gamma,delta)=chi_0(s_gamma s_delta s_(gamma delta)^-1).
```

Thus `D` is the twisted group algebra `C_omega[Gamma]`. The cocycle
values lie in the finite image of `chi_0`; this conclusion here follows
directly from the explicit one-dimensional character formula.
In particular,

```text
dim_C A=m^2 |Gamma|             when Gamma is finite,
A is infinite-dimensional      when Gamma is infinite.      (2)
```

The normalized canonical trace on `A` is
`tr_m tensor tau_omega`: `tau_G(f_i)=1/h`, and distinct nonidentity
`H_0`-cosets have zero identity coefficient.

## 2. Additive rotations and an exact coefficient test

Assume `p<=q`, where `q=1-e_K` is the full augmentation projection
of a finite subgroup `K` of order `k>=2`, and set `S=q-p`.
There is an exact finite-subgroup test for this containment. For any
one-dimensional character projection `f=e_(H,chi)`, put `D=K intersect H`.
The coefficient of `k_0 h_0` in `e_K f` is

```text
conjugate(chi(h_0))/(|K||H|) sum_(d in D) conjugate(chi(d)).
```

Every decomposition is obtained from `k_0 h_0` by inserting one
element of `D`; if an element is outside `KH`, its coefficient is
zero. The character sum vanishes exactly when the restriction of
`chi` to `D` is nontrivial. Since `f<=1-e_K` is equivalent to
`e_K f=0`, this proves

```text
e_(H,chi)<=1-e_K
 iff chi restricted to K intersect H is nontrivial.        (2a)
```

In particular, for `K=<J>` of order two, every such atom in
`(1-J)/2` must have `J in H` and `chi(J)=-1`. The converse
also holds. The atoms may have different subgroups, and no
normality of `K` is being inferred.

Because the connected network has `tau(f_i)=1/h`, the already
assumed orthogonality and containment `p<=q` give the exact test

```text
p=q iff m/h=(k-1)/k,
in which case k divides h and m=(k-1)h/k.                  (2b)
```

For sufficiency, `q-p` is a projection with zero canonical trace,
so faithfulness of the regular trace makes it zero. For an
involution this is `h=2m`. Raw integer data alone do not construct
orthogonal contained atoms; once those hypotheses are proved,
the trace equality does certify exhaustion.

Each edge between distinct vertices gives the corner unitary

```text
R_e=(q-f_i-f_j)+c_e(f_i+f_j)+s_e(v_e-v_e^*),
c_e,s_e real,             c_e^2+s_e^2=1.                    (3)
```

The orthogonal-support identities prove unitarity, exactly as for the
two-context cell. Every finite sum, product, and adjoint made from these
rotations and the vertex projections belongs to

```text
A direct-sum C S.                                          (4)
```

Consequently additive interference does not invalidate (1). It creates
matrix entries which are finite sums in the loop algebra `D`.

For a concrete entry, write

```text
T=sum_a c_a f_0 w_a,       w_a in L,
w_a=s_(gamma_a) k_a,       k_a in H_0.
```

Because `k_a f_0=chi_0(k_a)f_0`, the exact equality `T=f_0` is equivalent
to the finite coefficient conditions

```text
sum_(a: gamma_a=gamma) c_a chi_0(k_a)
    =1 if gamma=1, and 0 otherwise.                        (5)
```

Apply (5) to every matrix entry of a proposed product of rotations.
For example, equality of the network compression of that product to `p`
requires the right side
to be one only in diagonal matrix entries and the identity coset.
The full product of the `q`-unitaries in (3) also has the unchanged
scalar coefficient on `S`; its full identity is `q=p+S`.
All scalar cancellations and all word identifications are explicit in
this test. No further linear independence or representation assumption
is being supplied by the word "holonomy."

For a single closed literal path with return word `w`, the criterion
simplifies further:

```text
w f_0=f_0  iff  w in H_0 and chi_0(w)=1.                    (6)
```

Indeed the supports first force `wH_0=H_0`, and then the character
coefficient gives (6). An ordinary group relation identifying `w` with
a named element of `ker chi_0` is sufficient. Conversely, exact closure
in the group algebra forces precisely such membership. To use this in
a group presentation one must still prove that the quotient retains the
embedded finite subgroup and its character; the calculation does not
supply that authentication.

## 3. A triangular interference test with an exact trace value

Consider three vertices and edges

```text
v_01=g_01 f_0,       v_12=g_12 f_1,       v_20=g_20 f_2.
```

There are two partial transports from `f_0` to `f_2`:

```text
a=v_20^*,
b=v_12 v_01.
```

Both have initial projection `f_0` and final projection `f_2`.
Their two rotated source projections are

```text
P_a=(f_0+f_2+a+a^*)/2,
P_b=(f_0+f_2+b+b^*)/2.
```

They are exact projections with complements in `f_0+f_2`. Multiplying
on the left by `f_2` and on the right by `f_0` recovers `a/2` and `b/2`,
respectively. Therefore

```text
P_a=P_b
 iff a=b
 iff w f_0=f_0
 iff w in ker chi_0,

w=g_20 g_12 g_01.                                         (7)
```

Here `w in ker chi_0` includes membership in `H_0`. The second
equivalence follows from `a^* b=w f_0`; partial transports with the same
initial and final projections agree exactly when this product is `f_0`.
This tests the triangular return through equality of actual projections,
not merely covariance of separately chosen context copies.

There is also an exact canonical-trace cost. With
`||x||_(2,tau)^2=tau_G(x^*x)`, orthogonality of the initial and final
corners gives

```text
||P_a-P_b||_(2,tau)^2
 = (1/2)||a-b||_(2,tau)^2
 = tau_G(f_0)-Re tau_G(w f_0)
 = 1/h                         if w not in H_0,
 = (1-Re chi_0(w))/h            if w in H_0.                (8)
```

Thus an unclosed group word outside the finite packet has a fixed,
nonzero coefficient-level defect in this test. This is an exact statement
in the group algebra with its canonical trace; it is not a lower bound
for arbitrary finite-dimensional representations of the group.

## 4. The resulting construction boundary

All BCS maps in this section are unital star homomorphisms.

If every fundamental return word satisfies (6), then `L=H_0` and (1)
reduces to `M_m(C)`. Finite scalar return phases give the same conclusion.
More generally, a finite quotient `L/H_0` gives a finite-dimensional
algebra by (2). None of these can contain a unital map of the fixed
no-CE-trace BCS algebra.

There is an additional obstruction when `S!=0`: the algebra in (4)
has a unital scalar quotient. A unital BCS map into it would therefore
give a scalar, hence finite-dimensional, BCS representation. Thus a
construction using only this finite network and identity on its leftover
corner must exhaust `q`, or introduce operations which couple that
leftover corner to the network.

When `L/H_0` is infinite, the reduction does not assert a finite model.
It identifies exactly where the infinite algebra lives: the finite-normal
character corner of the return subgroup `L`. If `L` is hyperlinear,
its normalized canonical trace on `f_0 L(L) f_0` is Connes embeddable;
finite matrix amplification preserves this property. A unital no-CE BCS
map into `A` would contradict that trace. Hence a successful construction
entirely in this network algebra would already require a nonhyperlinear
return subgroup `L`.

The theorem therefore covers additive partial rotations, including the
outside-normalizer cell, but only when they use finitely many orthogonal
finite-character vertices and literal transports between them. It does
not cover arbitrary overlapping noncommuting cuts or arbitrary elements
of the full augmentation corner. Such additional operations would need
their own exact identities. The criterion (5), and its concrete triangular
form (7), specify what the present mechanism can enforce without leaving
an unproved group-ring equality.
