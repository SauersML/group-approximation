# A finitely presented torsion-free nonhyperlinear double with cd at most three

Date: 2026-09-20. This verifies a downstream application, with the
literature and existing algebraic inputs kept explicit. It does not
claim cohomological dimension exactly three or a new analytic theorem.

## 1. Exact Ollivier–Wise input and the dimension convention

For a countable group Q, Ollivier–Wise Theorem 1.1 gives a short exact
sequence

```text
1 -> N -> G -> Q -> 1
```

with G torsion-free, a graphical 1/6 presentation, and N with property
(T). The theorem additionally says that G is finitely presented when Q
is finitely presented. Their construction makes N generated by two
elements. For the present application Q is finitely presented; no claim
of finite presentability of N is needed.

Section 2.1 states the dimension-two consequence of its graphical
condition. For an explicit asphericity formulation see Dominik Gruber,
[*Groups with graphical C(6) and C(7) small cancellation presentations*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.0178),
Theorem 2.18 and Corollary 2.19: a graphical C(6) presentation has a
classifying space of dimension at most two. There is a notation issue
worth recording: Ollivier–Wise define a piece using two distinct lifts,
not merely two lifts inequivalent under a graph automorphism. Their
Gr'(1/6) is therefore the stronger distinct-lift condition relevant to
this asphericity conclusion; it is not an arbitrary modern essential-
piece Gr' presentation. We use the resulting `cd_Z G<=2` and no
stronger dimension assertion.

## 2. Make the cover's entire ambient group Kazhdan

Let `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)` and `Q=EL_4(R)≅R^x`. Use the native pair

```text
Gamma=diag(EL_3(R),1),
Q=<Gamma,u,k_+,k_->,
u Gamma u^-1 < Gamma,
k_+ Gamma k_+^-1 = k_- Gamma k_-^-1 = Gamma.
```

Both Gamma and Q have property (T). The explicit strictness witness
is x_12(e), with e=ab=1-st: e cannot be in sRt because multiplying
by t and s would give r=0. The direct artifact verifies the inverse
matrices and generation. Thus the ambient pair is the **whole**
finitely presented unit group after rank identification; no separate
nine-leaf identification is needed. This equality matters because a
subgroup preimage in a finitely presented group need not be finitely
presented.

The named finite-presentation input supplies a finitely presented Q.
Apply Ollivier–Wise to obtain the finitely presented torsion-free G
of cohomological dimension at most two, with quotient map `pi:G->Q`
and Kazhdan kernel N. Put `H=pi^-1(Gamma)`.

Both G and H have property (T), because property (T) is closed under
extensions: respectively their quotients by N are Q and Gamma.
For clarity, the extension argument uses the invariant subspace of N
in a unitary representation. It is G-invariant because N is normal;
the orthogonal projection onto it commutes with G. A Kazhdan pair
for N forces almost G-invariant vectors asymptotically into this
subspace. On it the representation factors through the quotient,
whose property (T) supplies a nonzero invariant vector. This applies
to H as well as G. No relative property (T) substitute is being used.

Discrete Kazhdan groups are finitely generated. A finite Kazhdan set
must generate the group: otherwise the quasi-regular representation
on its subgroup's cosets, with constants removed in the finite-index
case, has a nonzero vector fixed by that set but no invariant vector.
In particular H is finitely generated, though not assumed finitely
presented.

## 3. Compressors and nonnormality lift through the kernel

Choose lifts of u,k_+,k_-. Since N is normal and contained
in H, surjectivity gives the exact equality, for every lift t of s,

```text
t H t^-1 = pi^-1(s Gamma s^-1).
```

Thus all three lifts compress H and generate G with H: this subgroup
contains N and maps onto Q. The lift t of u is strict. Choose

```text
h in H \ tHt^-1.
```

Then `t notin H` and `t^-1 h t notin H`. This is the group-theoretic
witness needed by the double obstruction; nonhyperlinearity of Q by
itself would not suffice, since hyperlinearity is not being assumed
closed under arbitrary quotients.

## 4. The double is not hyperlinear — actual killed nontrivial word

Form `P=G *_H G`, with first and second copies denoted by subscripts
1 and 2. Let `sigma:P->U(M)` be any homomorphism into a specified
tracial matrix ultraproduct. The Liu–Thom normalization input applies
to its restriction to the first vertex G, because H and G are
finitely generated Kazhdan groups and G is generated by H and the
three compressors. Hence `sigma(G_1)` normalizes

```text
A=sigma(H)' cap M.
```

The element `c=t_1^-1 t_2` centralizes H in P: for each `k in H`,
`tkt^-1 in H`, so the copies agree on this conjugate and
`t_1^-1 t_2 k t_2^-1 t_1=k`. Therefore `sigma(c) in A`.
Normalization by `sigma(t_1)` gives

```text
sigma(t_2 t_1^-1)=sigma(t_1 c t_1^-1) in A.
```

Thus every such homomorphism kills

```text
w=[h,t_2 t_1^-1].
```

This is not the identity in P. Write it as the alternating word

```text
(h t)_2 (t^-1 h^-1 t)_1 (t^-1)_2.
```

Its outer syllables lie outside H because `t notin H`; its middle
syllable lies outside H by the choice of h. The normal-form theorem
for amalgamated free products makes this a reduced nonempty word.
Consequently no such homomorphism can be injective. In particular P
is not hyperlinear. This is Thom's double argument specialized to the
lifted Leavitt pair, with Liu supplying the universal analytic input.
It is not the inference “nonsofic, therefore nonhyperlinear.”

## 5. Finite presentation, torsion-freeness, and the upper dimension bound

Let `G=<X|R_0>` be a finite presentation and let `s_1,...,s_k` be a
finite generating set of H, expressed as words in X. A presentation
of P consists of two copies of X and R_0 together with the finitely
many relations identifying the two words for each s_i. Identifying
these generators identifies every element of H, so this is exactly
the universal amalgamated product. Finite presentation of H is not
required.

The Bass–Serre tree of this amalgam has vertex stabilizers conjugate
to G and edge stabilizers conjugate to H. Its action has no
inversions. A finite-order element generates a finite subgroup that
fixes a vertex, hence is conjugate into G. Since G is torsion-free,
so is P.

Restriction of a length-two projective resolution over `ZG` to
`ZH` stays projective, because `ZG` is free over `ZH` on coset
representatives. Thus `cd_Z H<=cd_Z G<=2`.

The cellular chain sequence of the Bass–Serre tree is

```text
0 -> Z[P/H] -> Z[P/G] direct-sum Z[P/G] -> Z -> 0.
```

Induction from H and G is exact and sends their projective modules
to projective `ZP`-modules. The first two nontrivial modules in the
sequence therefore have projective dimension at most two. The
standard mapping-cone construction on their projective resolutions
gives a projective resolution of Z of length at most three. Hence

```text
cd_Z P <= max(cd_Z G, cd_Z H+1) <=3.
```

Equivalently, the cohomological Mayer–Vietoris sequence vanishes in
degree at least four for every coefficient module. Neither version
requires H to be finitely presented or of type F_2.

This completes the claimed existence proof. There is **no claim here
that cd_Z P=3**. Establishing a nonzero third cohomology group is a
separate lower-bound problem, not part of this consequence.

## 6. Positive Baum–Connes, independent of normalization

The finite Ollivier–Wise construction gives word-hyperbolic G.
Vincent Lafforgue's [coefficient theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4653)
therefore gives Baum–Connes with coefficients for G. In the Bass–Serre
tree of P, every vertex stabilizer is a conjugate of G. Apply
[Oyono-Oyono's tree theorem](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1012786413219),
recorded as `bcc-passes-from-tree-vertex-stabilizers`, to obtain
Baum–Connes with coefficients for P. No amenability, finite index,
quasiconvexity or local-finiteness hypothesis on H is required.

This proves `rips-doubles-satisfy-bc-with-coefficients` for every
subgroup preimage of every finite Ollivier–Wise cover. It also covers
the earlier nine-leaf double. The proof does not use Liu–Thom
normalization and remains valid independently of that fresh input.
Combining it with Sections 1–5 for the very same native double gives
`fp-torsion-free-nonhyperlinear-bcc-group-exists`.

Since P is torsion-free, ordinary assembly surjectivity and the
assembly trace theorem give τ_P(K_0(C*_r P))=Z. A scalar projection
other than 0 or 1 would have faithful trace strictly between 0 and 1,
a contradiction. This is not a claim that all matrix K-classes are
generated by the identity. It rules out using this double to refute
ordinary assembly or to produce a forbidden higher-Kazhdan trace.
