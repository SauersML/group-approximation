**PASS**, with two wording amendments (W1, W2) and one citation note (C1).

- Every classical input is used within its hypotheses once W1 is made.
- The use of `char-zero-representable-rings-embed-in-fp-simple-rings` matches its statement. I passed that node in

## Checks

1. **Prime radical.**
   - For any ring, the prime radical is the intersection of all prime ideals.
   - Semiprime means it is `0`. So `A ↪ Π_P A/P`. **Correct.**
2. **Posner's theorem in the Formanek–Rowen form.**
   - For a prime PI ring `R` with centre `Z`: `Z` is a domain, its nonzero elements are regular, and every nonzero
     ideal meets `Z` (Rowen).
   - So `R ⊗_Z Frac(Z)` is central simple and finite-dimensional, of dimension `n^2`, and `R` injects into it.
   - This is the standard statement, as in Rowen's *Polynomial identities in ring theory* or McConnell–Robson §13.6.
     I did not re-read it. **Used within its hypotheses.**
3. **The bound `n ≤ m`.**
   - Amitsur–Levitzki gives the minimal identity degree of `M_n`: `M_n(F)` satisfies no identity of degree
     `< 2n`, for any field `F`.
   - **W1.** The proof needs the identity of `A/P` to hold in `Q_P ⊗ F̄_P ≅ M_{n_P}(F̄_P)`. That is automatic only
     for a multilinear identity.
     - Add one line: `A` satisfies a multilinear identity of degree `<= 2m` (multilinearization). Multilinear
       identities pass to quotients, to central localizations and to scalar extensions.
     - Then `2n_P <= 2m`.
   - With W1 the step is **correct**.
4. **One matrix size and finitely many entries. Correct.**
   - `n_P ≤ m` implies `n_P | m! = N`.
   - Block diagonal maps are unital embeddings.
   - `Π_P M_N(F̄_P) = M_N(Π_P F̄_P)`.
   - Each `F̄_P` has characteristic 0, since `A/P ≠ 0` is a `Q`-algebra.
   - `C_0` is generated by the finitely many entries of the images of the generators, so `A ⊆ M_N(C_0)`.
5. **The host.**
   - The node states: "every subring of `M_D(C ⊗_Q Q⟨x_1..x_m⟩)` embeds in … `M_e(R_k)`, for `C` a commutative
     `Q`-algebra embedding in some `M_e(Q(t))`, including every finitely generated commutative `Q`-algebra (its
     §2)".
   - Taking `C = C_0`, `D = N`, `m = 1`, and `A ⊆ M_N(C_0) ⊆ M_N(C_0 ⊗ Q⟨x_1⟩)` **matches**.
6. **GK dimension at most one.**
   - Small–Stafford–Warfield, *Affine algebras of Gelfand–Kirillov dimension one are PI*, Math. Proc. Cambridge
     Philos. Soc. 97 (1985) 407–414. Title, venue and pages were confirmed on Cambridge Core. Its opening
     hypothesis, from the search excerpt, is: "Let R be an affine (finitely generated) algebra over a field k and of
     Gelfand–Kirillov dimension one."
   - The semiprime case is re-proved in Pappacena–Small–Wald, arXiv:math/0211330. Abstract, verbatim: "an affine
     semiprime algebra R of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension 1 satisfies a polynomial identity". It is an accessible
     citation for exactly the case used here.
   - **C1.** Cite both, and mark Small–Stafford–Warfield "title and hypotheses confirmed, full text not read".
   - **W2.** "At most one" means GK dimension `0` or `1` only because of Bergman's gap theorem: no finitely
     generated algebra has GK dimension strictly between 0 and 1, or strictly between 1 and 2. Cite it. GK dimension
     `0` for a finitely generated algebra means finite-dimensional. **Correct.**
7. **The Anan'in lead.** It is correctly marked as a lead and not used.
8. **The example `Q⟨x,y⟩/(yx, y^2)`.**
   - The basis is `x^i` and `x^i y`.
   - The images are `I`, `diag(t^i,0)` (`i >= 1`) and `t^i e_12`. These are linearly independent, and the relations
     hold (`e_12 diag(t,0) = 0`).
   - So it is representable, as stated. **Correct.**
