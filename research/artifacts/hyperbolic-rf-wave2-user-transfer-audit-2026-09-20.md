## Finite-support subgroup theorem

Let A be finite, G residually finite, X a G-set, L the restricted direct
sum of A over X, and W=L semidirect G. If H<=W and F=H intersect L is
finite, then H is residually finite.

Write Q for the image of H in G, and S for the union of the supports of
F. For a lift (l,q) in H, conjugation sends the support of f in F to
q supp(f): coordinatewise inner conjugation does not change whether an
entry is the identity. Normality of F in H therefore implies qS=S.
Restriction to S defines L semidirect Q -> A^S semidirect Q. Its
restriction to H is injective: a kernel element lies in F and is supported
in S, so a trivial restriction makes it trivial. If B is the finite image
of Q in Aut(A^S), the map

    (a,q) -> ((a, action(q)), q)

embeds A^S semidirect Q into (A^S semidirect B) times Q. The latter is
residually finite. This proves the theorem, including nonabelian lamps,
arbitrary actions, and subgroups H that are not finitely generated.

A fixed word-hyperbolic group has bounded orders of finite subgroups.
Thus its locally finite subgroups are finite: M+1 distinct elements in
one would generate a finite subgroup exceeding an order bound M.
Since L is locally finite, every word-hyperbolic H<=W meets the theorem's
hypothesis and is residually finite. No quasiconvexity is required.

Geometric source checked directly: Meintrup--Schick, *A Model for the
Universal Space for Proper Actions of a Hyperbolic Group*, Corollary 8,
<https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0209163>. The corollary gives the stronger
finiteness of conjugacy classes of finite subgroups. Its only role here
is the finite-subgroup order bound.

## Finite residual equals the word-hyperbolic residual

More generally let E=L semidirect G with L locally finite and G residually
finite. Write Res_whyp(E) for the intersection of kernels of all maps to
word-hyperbolic groups. This notation deliberately avoids Rad_hyp, which
some existing Cairn nodes use for the HYPERLINEAR radical.

Projection to G puts Res_fin(E) inside L. For f:E->K with K hyperbolic,
F=f(L) is finite. Conjugation gives alpha:G->Aut(F), and

    (l,g) -> (f(l), alpha(g)) in F semidirect alpha(G)

is a homomorphism to a finite group with exactly the same kernel as f
on L. It kills Res_fin(E), so f does too. Therefore
Res_fin(E)<=Res_whyp(E). Finite groups themselves are hyperbolic, giving
the reverse inclusion. The splitting is essential to this argument.

This does not say every hyperbolic quotient of E is residually finite.
It says every such quotient kills the existing finite residual of E.

## Application to the supplied Liu wreath

For R=F2[x1^(+-1),x2^(+-1),x3^(+-1)], the actor
G=EL3(R) semidirect SL3(Z) is residually finite. Here is a direct check
independent of Liu's analytic theorem. EL3(R) is finitely generated by
elementary matrices with coefficients 1 and xi^(+-1): elementary
commutators multiply coefficients and elementary products add them.
It is residually finite by evaluating a nonzero Laurent entry at a
nonvanishing tuple in a finite extension of F2. SL3(Z) is residually
finite by reduction modulo primes. A split extension of a finitely
generated residually finite N by a residually finite Q is residually
finite: for n!=1 choose a finite-index characteristic subgroup C of N
missing n (intersect all subgroups of one separating index), then use
(N/C) semidirect image(Q->Aut(N/C)); elements with nontrivial Q-coordinate
are separated in Q.

Thus the supplied binary permutational wreath has no non-residually-finite
word-hyperbolic subgroup. Every map from it to a word-hyperbolic group
kills its entire finite residual. These facts need neither Liu's
internality theorem nor a conjecture that hyperbolic groups are hyperlinear.
