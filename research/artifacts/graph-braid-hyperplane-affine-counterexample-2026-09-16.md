# Graph braid groups have characters that are not hyperplane-affine

Lane swarm-zaremsky-1-08, 2026-09-16. Root:
`zaremsky-1-08-bnsr-invariants-of-graph-braid-groups`. This refutes
`graph-braid-characters-are-hyperplane-affine`, the input (A) of
`research/artifacts/zp-graph-braid-sigma-2026-09-13-part2.md`, §3.
Notation follows part 2.

**Theorem.** Let `Γ` be either:

- any subdivision of the complete graph `K_4`, including `K_4` itself; or
- any subdivision of the theta graph `Θ_3` (two vertices joined by three
  internally disjoint arcs) in which every arc has at least two edges.

Then the linear map from antisymmetric functions on oriented hyperplane
classes of `UD_2Γ` to `H^1(UD_2Γ; ℝ)` is not surjective. Its image has
dimension at most `β_1(Γ)` (3 for `K_4`, 2 for `Θ_3`). But
`dim H^1(UD_2Γ; ℝ) ≥ 1 − e(UD_2Γ)`, which is 4 for `K_4` and 3 for `Θ_3`.

§§1–6 are a complete proof and use no outside results. §7 covers
interpretation and evidence, and nothing proved here depends on it.

## 1. Conventions

`Γ` is a finite simple graph (no loops, no multiple edges) with `N` vertices
and `M` edges. `ē = {e^−, e^+}` is the vertex set of an edge `e`, and each
edge carries a fixed orientation `e^− → e^+`. Write
`Γ_e := Γ − {e^−, e^+}` for the induced subgraph on `V(Γ) ∖ ē`. For
connected `Γ`, `β_1(Γ) = M − N + 1 = dim H_1(Γ; ℝ)`.

`X = UD_nΓ` is the cube complex whose cells are the sets of `n` closed cells
of `Γ` with pairwise disjoint closures; a set with `j` edges is a `j`-cube.
Only the 2-skeleton matters for `H^1`, and for `n = 2` there are no
3-cubes.

- 0-cells are the `n`-subsets `S ⊆ V(Γ)`.
- 1-cells are `{e} ∪ P`, where `P` is an `(n−1)`-subset of `V(Γ_e)`.
  **Orientation:** from `P ∪ {e^−}` to `P ∪ {e^+}`. Such a 1-cell is an
  *X-edge over `e`*.
- 2-cells are `Q = {e, f} ∪ R`, where `R` is an `(n−2)`-subset of vertices,
  and `e`, `f` and the vertices of `R` have pairwise disjoint closures.
  Write `e = (a → b)`, `f = (c → d)`. Going round the square,
  `R+a+c → R+b+c → R+b+d` and `R+a+c → R+a+d → R+b+d`, so
  `∂Q = {e}∪R∪{c} + {f}∪R∪{b} − {e}∪R∪{d} − {f}∪R∪{a}`.

**Hyperplane classes** are the classes of the equivalence relation on
X-edges generated by "opposite in a square". In the square `Q` above, the
opposite pairs are `{e}∪R∪{c}`, `{e}∪R∪{d}` (both over `e`, both oriented
`a → b`) and `{f}∪R∪{a}`, `{f}∪R∪{b}` (both over `f`, both oriented
`c → d`). So a class consists of X-edges over a single edge `e` of `Γ`, all
carrying the orientation induced by `e`.

An antisymmetric function on oriented classes is therefore the same as a
real function `c` on the set of classes. It defines the 1-cochain whose
value on the X-edge `ε`, oriented as above, is `c(class of ε)`, and minus
that on the reverse orientation. These are the **affine cochains**, as in
part 2, §1.

**Lemma 0.** Every affine cochain `c` is a cellular cocycle, so the map
`A: c ↦ [c] ∈ H^1(X; ℝ)` is defined.

*Proof.* In the formula for `∂Q`, the two X-edges over `e` get the same
value of `c` with opposite signs, and so do the two over `f`. So
`c(∂Q) = 0`. ∎

## 2. Connectivity of configuration graphs

**Lemma 1.** Let `Δ` be a finite connected graph with at least `m ≥ 0`
vertices. Build the graph `G_m(Δ)`:

- vertices are the `m`-subsets of `V(Δ)`;
- `S` and `S'` are adjacent iff `S' = (S ∖ {z}) ∪ {z'}` for an edge `[z,z']`
  of `Δ` with `z ∈ S`, `z' ∉ S`.

Then `G_m(Δ)` is connected.

*Proof.* Moves in a spanning tree are moves in `Δ`, so we may assume `Δ` is
a tree `T`. Induct on `N = |V(T)|`, for all `m ≤ N` at once.

- If `m = N`, or `m = 0`, there is only one vertex.
- Otherwise `N ≥ 2` and `T` has a leaf `ℓ`.

**Step 1: every `S` reaches a set containing `ℓ`.** If `ℓ ∉ S`, let `s` be
the element of `S` nearest to `ℓ` in `T`. By minimality, no vertex of the
`T`-path from `s` to `ℓ` other than `s` lies in `S`. Sliding `s` along this
path gives adjacent moves ending at a set that contains `ℓ`.

**Step 2: all sets containing `ℓ` are connected.** Such a set is
`{ℓ} ∪ S'` with `S'` an `(m−1)`-subset of the tree `T − ℓ`, which has
`N − 1 ≥ m − 1` vertices. By induction, `G_{m−1}(T − ℓ)` is connected. Its
moves never involve `ℓ`, so they are moves of `G_m(T)` that keep `ℓ` in the
set.

Steps 1 and 2 together show that `G_m(T)` is connected. ∎

Remarks.

- `G_m(Δ)` is exactly the 1-skeleton of `UD_mΔ`, so `UD_mΔ` is connected.
- For `m = 1`, `G_1(Δ) = Δ`.

## 3. One hyperplane class per edge

**Lemma 2.** Let `n ≥ 2` and let `e` be an edge of `Γ` such that `Γ_e` is
connected and has at least `n − 1` vertices. Then all X-edges of `UD_nΓ`
over `e` lie in one hyperplane class.

*Proof.* Take `P' = (P ∖ {z}) ∪ {z'}`, where `f = [z,z']` is an edge of
`Γ_e`, `z ∈ P` and `z' ∉ P`.

- `f̄ = {z,z'} ⊆ V(Γ_e)` is disjoint from `ē`.
- The vertices of `R := P ∖ {z}` lie in `V(Γ_e) ∖ {z, z'}`.

So `{e, f} ∪ R` is a square. Its two X-edges over `e` are
`{e} ∪ R ∪ {z} = {e} ∪ P` and `{e} ∪ R ∪ {z'} = {e} ∪ P'`, which are
opposite. So `{e} ∪ P` and `{e} ∪ P'` are in the same class. These moves are
the edges of `G_{n−1}(Γ_e)`, which is connected by Lemma 1. ∎

## 4. Affine classes factor through `H^1(Γ)`

**Lemma 3.** Let `Γ` be connected and `n ≥ 2`, and suppose that for every
edge `e`, `Γ_e` is connected with at least `n − 1` vertices. Then the image
of `A` has dimension at most `β_1(Γ)`.

*Proof.* Define linear maps on cellular chains with real coefficients:

- `σ: C_0(X) → C_0(Γ)` by `σ(S) = Σ_{x∈S} x`;
- `τ: C_1(X) → C_1(Γ)` by `τ({e} ∪ P) = e`, with the orientations of §1.

1. **`∂τ = σ∂` on `C_1(X)`.** We have
   `∂({e} ∪ P) = (P ∪ {e^+}) − (P ∪ {e^−})`. Applying `σ` gives
   `e^+ − e^− = ∂e = ∂τ({e} ∪ P)`.
2. **`τ∂ = 0` on `C_2(X)`.** By the formula for `∂Q` in §1,
   `τ(∂Q) = e + f − e − f = 0`.
3. **`τ` descends to homology.** By step 1, `τ` maps 1-cycles of `X` to
   1-cycles of `Γ`. By step 2, it kills 1-boundaries. So it induces
   `τ_*: H_1(X; ℝ) → H_1(Γ; ℝ)`.
4. **Affine cochains factor through `τ`.** Let `c` be affine. By Lemma 2,
   `c` takes one value `a(e)` on all X-edges over `e` oriented along `e`.
   So `c = a ∘ τ` as a functional on `C_1(X)`, where `a ∈ C^1(Γ; ℝ)`.
5. **Pairing with cycles.** Over the field `ℝ`, the universal coefficient
   theorem gives an isomorphism `H^1(X; ℝ) ≅ Hom_ℝ(H_1(X; ℝ), ℝ)`, sending
   `[c]` to `[z] ↦ c(z)`. For affine `c` and a 1-cycle `z`,
   `c(z) = a(τz) = (a|_{Z_1(Γ)} ∘ τ_*)([z])`.
6. **Conclusion.** `A(c)` lies in the image of the dual map
   `τ_*^∨: Hom(H_1(Γ; ℝ), ℝ) → Hom(H_1(X; ℝ), ℝ)`. That image has dimension
   at most `dim H_1(Γ; ℝ) = β_1(Γ)`. ∎

Conversely, every `a ∈ C^1(Γ)` gives the affine cochain `a ∘ τ`. So under
these hypotheses the affine classes are exactly the circulation characters
of part 2, §1.

## 5. Euler characteristic of `UD_2Γ`

For a finite simple graph `Γ`:

- `UD_2Γ` has `C(N,2)` vertices;
- it has `M(N−2)` edges: an edge `e` and a vertex off `ē`;
- it has `C(M,2) − Σ_v C(deg v, 2)` squares. These are the unordered pairs
  of edges with disjoint closures; in a simple graph two distinct edges share
  at most one vertex, and `Σ_v C(deg v, 2)` counts the pairs that share one.

Hence

`e(UD_2Γ) = C(N,2) − M(N−2) + C(M,2) − Σ_v C(deg v, 2)`.

**`K_4` with `s` subdivision vertices.** `N = 4 + s` and `M = 6 + s`. Four
vertices have degree 3 and `s` have degree 2, so `Σ_v C(deg v,2) = 12 + s`.

- `C(N,2) + C(M,2) = [(s²+7s+12) + (s²+11s+30)]/2 = s² + 9s + 21`.
- `M(N−2) = s² + 8s + 12`.

So `e = s² + 9s + 21 − (s² + 8s + 12) − 12 − s = −3`.

**`Θ_3` with arc lengths `k_1, k_2, k_3`, `K = k_1 + k_2 + k_3`.** `N = K − 1`
and `M = K`. Two vertices have degree 3 and `K − 3` have degree 2, so
`Σ_v C(deg v,2) = K + 3`.

- `C(K−1,2) + C(K,2) = (K−1)(K−2+K)/2 = (K−1)²`.
- So `e = (K−1)² − K(K−3) − (K+3) = −2`.

Checks:

- `K_4`: `6 − 12 + 3 = −3`.
- `Θ_3` with `k_i = 2`: `10 − 18 + 6 = −2`.
- Gal's product formula (part 3, §1) gives `[t²](1−2t)^4/(1−t)^6 = −3` and
  `[t²](1−2t)^2/(1−t)^3 = −2`. This is a check only; nothing depends on it.

**Betti number bound.** `X = UD_2Γ` is a finite complex of dimension at most
2, so with real coefficients `e(X) = b_0 − b_1 + b_2`. Hence
`b_1 = b_0 + b_2 − e ≥ 1 − e`. This needs no connectivity; in fact `X` is
connected by Lemma 1 with `m = 2`.

- `K_4`: `b_1(UD_2Γ) ≥ 4`.
- `Θ_3`: `b_1(UD_2Γ) ≥ 3`.

## 6. Every `Γ_e` is connected

**`K_4`, any subdivision.** Let the original vertices be `a, b, c, d`, and
let `P_{xy}` be the path replacing the edge `xy`. Let `e = [v,w]` lie on
`P_{ab}`. Then `c, d ∉ {v, w}`.

- `P_{cd}` is untouched and joins `c` to `d`.
- The vertices of `P_{ac}`, `P_{bc}` other than `a, b` form paths ending
  at `c`. Those of `P_{ad}`, `P_{bd}` form paths ending at `d`.
- If `a` survives, it lies on the untouched `P_{ac}`. Likewise `b` on
  `P_{bc}`.
- `P_{ab} − {v, w}` has at most two pieces. A nonempty piece contains `a`
  or `b`, which survives.

So `Γ_e` is connected, and it has at least 2 vertices (`c` and `d`), which is
at least `n − 1 = 1`.

**`Θ_3`, every arc of length at least 2.** Let the arcs be `A_1, A_2, A_3`
from `p` to `q`, and let `e = [v,w]` lie on `A_1`.

- *Case `{v,w} ∩ {p,q} = ∅`.* `A_2 ∪ A_3` is untouched, connected, and
  contains `p` and `q`. Each nonempty piece of `A_1 − {v,w}` contains `p` or
  `q`.
- *Case `p ∈ {v,w}`, say `v = p`.* Since `A_1` has at least two edges,
  `w ≠ q`. The vertex `q` survives, and `A_2 − p`, `A_3 − p` and
  `A_1 − {p,w}` are paths containing `q`. (`A_1 − {p,w}` is nonempty
  because `w ≠ q`.)
- *Case `q ∈ {v,w}`.* Symmetric.

These cases are exhaustive: `{v,w}` cannot contain both `p` and `q`,
because `A_1` has at least two edges.

So `Γ_e` is connected, with `N − 2 ≥ 3` vertices.

(If some arc is a single edge `[p,q]`, then `Γ_{[p,q]}` has two components.
Lemma 2 does not apply, and indeed the computation in §7 finds all
characters affine for arc lengths `(1,2,2)` and `(1,3,3)`.)

**Proof of the Theorem.** By §6 the hypotheses of Lemma 3 hold with
`n = 2`. So `dim A(affine cochains) ≤ β_1(Γ)`:

- `K_4`: `β_1 = 6 − 4 + 1 = 3`;
- `Θ_3`: `β_1 = 3 − 2 + 1 = 2`.

By §5, `dim H^1(UD_2Γ; ℝ) = b_1 ≥ 4`, respectively `≥ 3`. So `A` is not
surjective. ∎

**Why this refutes the claim under any reading of "sufficiently
subdivided".**

- The claim asserts surjectivity onto `H^1(UD_nΓ; ℝ)`. That fails for every
  subdivision of `K_4` at `n = 2`, however fine, and there is no other model.
- `UD_2Γ` is connected (Lemma 1). So `H^1(UD_2Γ; ℝ) = Hom(π_1 UD_2Γ, ℝ)`.
- For subdivisions fine enough to meet the subdivision hypotheses cited in
  part 1 (Abrams; Kim–Ko–Park; Prue–Scrimshaw), `π_1 UD_2Γ = B_2Γ`.

So some real character of `B_2K_4` is not induced by any function on
hyperplane classes. The same holds for `B_2Θ_3` with every arc of length at
least 2.

## 7. Interpretation and evidence (not used above)

**Exact Betti numbers.** Ko–Park, *Characteristics of graph braid groups*,
arXiv:1101.2648. The e-print TeX source was read on 2026-09-16; the theorem
is labelled `thm:H1Bn` and is Theorem 3.16 by the source's numbering. Its
statement:

`H_1(B_nΓ) = ℤ^{N_1(n,Γ)+N_2(Γ)+N_3(Γ)+β_1(Γ)} ⊕ ℤ_2^{N'_3(Γ)}`.

Here `N_3` (resp. `N'_3`) is the number of planar (resp. nonplanar)
triconnected components, and
`N_2 = Σ_{{x,y}∈V_2} (μ({x,y})−1)(μ({x,y})−2)/2`. In the source's proof, a
triconnected `Γ` has `H_1(B_2Γ) ≅ ℤ^{β_1(Γ)} ⊕ ⟨[A_k(a)]⟩`, and the extra
class is torsion-free iff `Γ` is planar.

- `K_4` is planar and triconnected, so `b_1 = 3 + 1 = 4`.
- `Θ_3` has the 2-cut `{p,q}` with `μ = 3`, so `N_2 = 1` and `b_1 = 3`.
- In both cases the lower bounds of §5 are exact and `b_2 = 0`.
- For `K_5` and `K_{3,3}` the extra class is 2-torsion. It is invisible to
  real characters, and all characters there are affine.

So the missing real character is the planar-triconnected class for `K_4`,
and the three-arc exchange class at the 2-cut for `Θ_3`.

**Conjecture (evidence only).** Suppose every topological edge is cut into
at least 2 edges and `Γ` meets the subdivision hypothesis for `n`. Then the
affine image has dimension `β_1(Γ) + N_1(n,Γ)`. In particular, all real
characters are affine iff `N_2(Γ) = N_3(Γ) = 0`.

The `Θ_3` rows with an arc of length 1 show that the affine image is *not*
invariant under subdivision.

**General `n`.** Lemma 3 holds for every `n ≥ 2`. Take `Θ_3` with every arc
of length at least 2, or any subdivision of `K_4`, with
`n − 1 ≤ |V(Γ_e)|` for every edge. Then the affine classes of `UD_nΓ` are
only the circulation classes, of dimension at most `β_1(Γ)`. Once `Γ` meets
the subdivision hypothesis for `n`, Ko–Park gives `b_1(B_nΓ) = β_1(Γ) + 1`
(here `N_1 = 0`). So for every `n ≥ 2` the affine characters miss a real
character.

**Cube-affine heights are affine cochains.** A `χ`-equivariant height on
`X̃` that is affine on every cube has equal increments on opposite edges of
each square, and deck-invariant increments. So it descends to an affine
cochain. Theorem M of part 2 therefore applies only to affine characters,
and cannot see the missing directions.

**Cubical subdivision of `X` does not help.** Hyperplanes of the cubical
subdivision `X'` come in pairs `H_−`, `H_+` of pushoffs of hyperplanes `H` of
`X`. Loops in `X^1` generate `H_1(X') = H_1(X)`. On such a loop, an affine
cochain `c'` of `X'` evaluates like the affine cochain
`c(H) = c'(H_−) + c'(H_+)` of `X`. So the affine image does not grow.

**What this means for Σ.**

*The counterexample groups are free.* In `UD_2K_4`, the only edge of `K_4`
disjoint from `[a,c]` is `[b,d]`. So each of the 12 X-edges lies in exactly
one of the 3 squares, and the squares meet only in vertices; each vertex
lies in two squares. Replace each square, rel its corners, by a cone on its 4
corners. This gives a connected graph with 9 vertices and 12 edges, so
`B_2K_4 ≅ F_4`.

For `Θ(2,2,2)`, each of the 6 squares has a free edge. For example
`{[p,m_1], m_2}` lies only in `{[p,m_1],[m_2,q]}`. So `UD_2Θ_3` collapses
to a graph and `B_2Θ_3 ≅ F_3`.

`B_2Γ = π_1 UConf_2Γ` does not change under subdivision, and `K_4` and
`Θ(2,2,2)` already meet the `n = 2` subdivision hypothesis (Kim–Ko–Park;
Prue–Scrimshaw; see the root claim). So the same holds for every
subdivision. `sigma1_probe.py`, replayed by `run_probe.sh` with output in
`sigma1-probe-2026-09-16.txt`, also confirms by free-face collapses that the
`k = 1, 2` models of `K_4` and the `k = 2, 3` models of `Θ_3` collapse to
graphs. Hence `Σ^m(B_2Γ) = ∅` for these `Γ`, and the missing
characters do not change the Σ-invariants of these particular groups.

*Non-affine characters also occur in non-free groups.* This is evidence
only. `K_4` with a triangle glued at a vertex, and `K_4` with a triangle
attached by a bridge, both at `n = 2`, `k = 2` (`run_all.sh`), give:

| graph | `b_1` | affine image | `e(UD_2Γ)` | `b_2` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| triangle at a vertex | 8 | 7 | `−6` | 1 |
| triangle on a bridge | 8 | 7 | `−4` | 3 |

Both graphs contain two disjoint cycles, so `B_2Γ ⊇ ℤ^2` and neither group is
free.

*Openness.* `Σ^1(G)` is open in the character sphere (Bieri–Neumann–Strebel
1987; theorem number not re-checked), and `Σ^m ⊆ Σ^1`. Let `A` be the space
of affine characters. If `A ≠ Hom(B_nΓ, ℝ)`, then `S(A)` has empty interior.
So:

- **(i)** If every class in `Σ^m(B_nΓ)` is affine, as the open claim
  `graph-braid-ascending-matching-complex-criterion-is-exact` asserts, then
  `Σ^m(B_nΓ) = ∅` for every such `Γ`.
- **(ii)** If Theorem M produces a class in `Σ^m(B_nΓ)` for such a `Γ`, that
  claim is false.

The probe `sigma1_probe.py` tests (ii) with `m = 1` and `n = 2` over all
sign patterns on hyperplane classes:

- Calibration cases, as expected:
  - the cycle and the triod (`B_2 = ℤ`) have 2 passing patterns each;
  - free cases of rank at least 2 have none. These are the cases whose
    complex collapses to a graph: `K_{1,4}`, `K_4`, `Θ_3` (including
    `Θ(1,2,2)`), figure-eight, lollipop, `Θ_4`, triangle with legs.
- The non-free cases have none: the dumbbell and the two
  `K_4`-plus-triangle graphs. Each contains two disjoint cycles, and none of
  their complexes collapses to a graph.

So Theorem M gives no contradiction in any case tested.
