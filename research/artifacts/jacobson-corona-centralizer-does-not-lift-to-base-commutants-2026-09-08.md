# An exact coherent base whose corona centralizer has no coordinate commutant lift

2026-09-08. There are finite-dimensional representations of the
entire coherent Jacobson base, together with unitary matrices
`T_n`, such that the constant finite group commutes with `T_n`
exactly and every fixed base element commutes with it asymptotically
in operator norm. Nevertheless, the distance of `T_n` from the
commutant of the entire finite base image is exactly one at every
stage.

All base representations stay exact in this construction. In the
four-error presentation, the finite packet tables, the full finite
intertwining, and the three base word relators are exact. Only the
remaining stable-letter word has an error, which tends to zero.
The head is killed throughout. This therefore excludes a correction
of the stable letter into the exact base commutant while keeping
the base fixed; it does not exclude joint correction of base and
stable letter or decide the marked non-MF question.

The slow-phase method itself already appears in
[the corona commutant-growth proof](../corona-commutant-growth-witness-proof.md).
That argument separates the commutants of two compressed infinite
subgroups. The addition here is its explicit realization on the
coherent Jacobson base, with the whole prescribed constant-group
intertwining exact, and the distance calculation for the full
coordinate commutant. No new general slow-phase principle is
claimed.

## 1. Explicit finite images of the coherent base

Put

```text
R=F_2[z,z^(-1)],             Q=SL_3(R).
```

The previous
[Laurent generation calculation](jacobson-stable-letter-laurent-quotient-and-local-inner-action-2026-09-08.md)
proves that `Q` is generated by its constant subgroup

```text
G=SL_3(F_2),       |G|=168,
```

and the involution

```text
       [ 0      z  0 ]
h(z) = [ z^(-1) 0  0 ].
       [ 0      0  1 ]
```

For each integer `n>=2`, choose `alpha_n` of degree `n`
over `F_2` in `F_(2^n)`, and evaluate `z->alpha_n`.
This is a surjective ring homomorphism `R->F_(2^n)`:
`alpha_n` is nonzero and generates that field. It induces
a surjective group homomorphism

```text
q_n:Q -> Q_n=SL_3(F_(2^n)).                            (1)
```

Surjectivity in (1) follows by lifting the coefficients of all
elementary matrices, which generate the special linear group over
a field. Consequently the constant copy of `G` and

```text
h_n=h(alpha_n)
```

generate `Q_n`. The constant copy remains faithful, and
`h_n` is an involution. Let

```text
lambda_n:Q_n->U(ell^2(Q_n))
```

be the complex left regular representation, and abbreviate
`lambda_n(q_n(g))` by `lambda_n(g)` for `g in Q`.

The coherent base is `M=EL_3(J)`, where
`J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>`. Its surjective positive-symbol map
`M->Q` was established in
[the ascending-corner description](jacobson-coherent-group-is-an-ascending-corner-extension-2026-09-08.md).
Composing with (1) and `lambda_n` gives an exact representation
of every element and every relation of that base. In particular,
this simultaneously represents the complete finite packets
`mathcal B`, `mathcal C`, and the third `GL_3(F_2)`.

## 2. The finite coset graph and its unbounded radius

Let `X_n=G\Q_n` be the set of left cosets. Define an
undirected graph on `X_n` by putting an edge between

```text
Gx and G h_n x        for every x in Q_n.              (2)
```

The phrase "for every x" includes every representative of every
coset. Thus the neighbors of `Gx` are among

```text
{G h_n g x : g in G},
```

so the degree is at most `168`. Changing a representative is
allowed to give another neighbor; no ill-defined single action of
`h_n` on left cosets is being asserted. Since `h_n^2=1`,
each edge reverses by the same rule.

The graph is connected. Starting at `G`, left multiplication
by a constant element of `G` leaves the coset unchanged,
while left multiplication by `h_n` is an edge of (2).
A word in `G` and `h_n` therefore gives a path to its
left coset, and these elements generate all of `Q_n`.

Let

```text
f_n(Gx)=distance_(X_n)(G,Gx),
R_n=max_(Gx in X_n) f_n(Gx).
```

Here `R_n>=1`, because `alpha_n` has degree at least two
and `Q_n` is larger than its constant subgroup. Also

```text
R_n -> infinity.                                     (3)
```

Indeed `|X_n|=|Q_n|/168` tends to infinity: for example,
the upper unitriangular subgroup of `Q_n` already has
`2^(3n)` elements. A graph of degree at most `168` has
at most `1+168+...+168^r` vertices at distance at most
`r` from one vertex. Connectivity and unbounded vertex counts
force (3).

By definition,

```text
f_n(G g x)=f_n(Gx)                    for g in G,
|f_n(G h_n x)-f_n(Gx)|<=1.                            (4)
```

## 3. The stable-letter unitaries and fixed-word estimates

Define a diagonal unitary on `ell^2(Q_n)` by

```text
T_n delta_x
 =exp(i pi f_n(Gx)/R_n) delta_x.                      (5)
```

It is an operator on the regular-representation space, not
necessarily an element of the represented left group algebra.
The first equality in (4) gives

```text
[T_n,lambda_n(g)]=0                 for every g in G. (6)
```

The second and the elementary bound
`|exp(i a)-exp(i b)|<=|a-b|` give

```text
||[T_n,lambda_n(h)]||_op<=pi/R_n.                    (7)
```

Use the fixed symmetric alphabet `G union {h}` of `Q`.
For a fixed word `g` of length `ell(g)` in this alphabet,
telescoping its factors yields

```text
||[T_n,lambda_n(g)]||_op<=pi ell(g)/R_n.              (8)
```

Any chosen word representing `g` may be used in this estimate;
no uniform bound over all elements of `Q` is inferred.
For an integer `k`, telescoping powers gives

```text
||[T_n^k,lambda_n(g)]||_op
 <=|k| pi ell(g)/R_n.                                (9)
```

Thus the maps

```text
V_n:Q x Z -> U(ell^2(Q_n)),
V_n(g,k)=lambda_n(g) T_n^k                           (10)
```

form an actual asymptotic representation of the direct product.
For example, for fixed `g,g' in Q` and `k,k' in Z`,

```text
||V_n(g,k)V_n(g',k')-V_n(gg',k+k')||_op
 <=|k| pi ell(g')/R_n ->0.                           (11)
```

They send the identity to the identity; (11) also handles each
fixed inverse relation. Hence their classes define a group
homomorphism into the operator-norm matrix corona.

In fact this homomorphism is faithful on `Q x Z`. If
`g!=1`, choose a nonzero Laurent entry of `g-I` and clear
its negative powers. The resulting nonzero polynomial cannot
vanish at `alpha_n` once `n` exceeds its degree. Thus
`q_n(g)!=1` eventually. Acting on `delta_1` then gives
two orthogonal basis vectors for `V_n(g,k)` and the identity,
so

```text
||V_n(g,k)-I||_op>=sqrt(2)
```

eventually. If `g=1` and `k!=0`, a geodesic to a vertex
at distance `R_n` includes vertices at every integer distance
from zero to `R_n`. Choosing a distance nearest
`R_n/|k|` shows that an eigenvalue of `T_n^k` tends to
`-1`, and consequently

```text
||T_n^k-I||_op ->2.
```

This is a faithful model of the arithmetic quotient, whose MF
status was already available from residual finiteness. It is not
a faithful model of the coherent Jacobson group.

## 4. Exact coherent packets and exact finite intertwining

Pull (10) back through the established quotient

```text
Gamma_triangle -> Gamma_triangle/L ~= Q x Z.
```

Its restriction to the base `M` is the exact representation
from Section 1. Therefore both complete finite packet tables,
their full intersection, the third finite packet, and the three
base relators

```text
(h b)^3=1,             W=1,             E=1
```

are exact at every stage.

The quotient kills all finitary subgroups. Thus `G_Q` and
`tau(G)` both have identity image, whereas `G_P` and
`delta(G)` both have the same constant image `G`. Equation
(6) proves the entire prescribed finite intertwining

```text
T_n rho_n(f) T_n^*=rho_n(alpha_F(f))    for every f in F
```

exactly, including its noncentral finite-group matrix units.
The local factor `h_0` has identity image, so `h_1` and
`h` have the same image `lambda_n(h)`. Consequently the
only remaining stable-letter error is bounded by (7):

```text
||(H_n rho_n(b))^3-I||=0,
||W_n-I||=0,
||T_n H_n T_n^*-H_(1,n)||<=pi/R_n,
||E_n-I||=0.                                         (12)
```

At the same time,

```text
rho_n(w)=I
```

for the head mark at every stage. These tuples therefore fail
the surviving-head requirement of the domain defining
`gamma_triangle`. Equation (12) supplies no upper bound
on that marked infimum.

## 5. Distance exactly one from every full coordinate commutant

Let

```text
A_n=lambda_n(Q_n)',
```

the commutant algebra of the entire finite base image in
`B(ell^2(Q_n))`. It is also the commutant of the image of
`M`, because the symbol and specialization maps are surjective.
Every `X in A_n` has a constant diagonal in the group basis:
commutation with left translations gives

```text
<X delta_(g x),delta_(g x)>=<X delta_x,delta_x>
```

for all `g,x in Q_n`, and that action is transitive.

The diagonal of `T_n` takes value `1` on the base coset
and `-1` on a farthest coset. If `c` is the constant
diagonal value of `X`, then

```text
||T_n-X||_op>=max(|1-c|,|-1-c|)>=1.                  (13)
```

Conversely `0 in A_n` and `||T_n||=1`. Thus

```text
dist_op(T_n,A_n)=1                  for every n>=2.   (14)
```

The equality is to the entire commutant algebra. For its unitary
group, (13) gives a lower bound of one; the upper bound using
zero does not apply to that smaller set.

Let `rho:Q->U(Q_d)` be the corona homomorphism furnished by
the exact base representations and let `t_0=[(T_n)]`.
Equation (8) proves that `t_0` commutes with every `rho(g)`.
But (14) proves that no sequence `X_n in A_n` represents
`t_0` in the same norm corona: such a lift would require
`||T_n-X_n||->0`. In particular there is no unitary lift
in the coordinate commutants.

## 6. Whole matrix-unit averaging does not repair this example

For each finite packet `K`, its group average

```text
E_K(X)=|K|^(-1) sum_(a in K) rho_n(a) X rho_n(a)^*
```

is the conditional expectation onto its full commutant. This
already averages over noncentral matrix units. More explicitly,
if the occurring irreducible blocks have matrix units
`e_ij^sigma`, it equals

```text
E_K(X)=sum_sigma (1/d_sigma)
                    sum_(i,j) e_ij^sigma X e_ji^sigma.
```

In the present example, the image of `mathcal B` is the
constant `G`, so `E_(mathcal B)(T_n)=T_n` exactly.
The image of `mathcal C` is the finite shifted group generated
by `c,k,h`, and the image of the third packet is generated by
`h,q,r`. Each has order `168`. In its connected Cayley
graph on these involutive generators, every element has a word
of length at most `167`; each letter other than `h` lies
in the constant `G`. Hence (8) gives, for either of these
two packets,

```text
||E_K(T_n)-T_n||_op<=167 pi/R_n.                     (15)
```

Every such expectation is contractive in operator norm. A fixed
composition of `a` of these packet expectations therefore moves
`T_n` by at most `167 a pi/R_n`, by telescoping the
composition. Its output has distance at least

```text
1-167 a pi/R_n
```

from the full base commutant, by (14). Thus any fixed number
of these full matrix-unit averaging rounds still leaves the
distance tending to one. This statement concerns the specified
averaging operations, not all nonlinear matrix procedures.

## 7. Exact limitation of the counterexample

There is no dimension-independent modulus tending to zero that,
for all these exact coherent base representations, corrects
`T_n` into their full commutants while leaving the base fixed.
The assertion fails even with exact constant-group intertwining
and the error pattern (12).

The examples kill the entire finitary group and the head. A
correction result on a specifically retained head sector is not
refuted. Nor is a repair which also changes the base generators.
The marked non-MF gap and any decoder exploiting such additional
information remain undecided here.
