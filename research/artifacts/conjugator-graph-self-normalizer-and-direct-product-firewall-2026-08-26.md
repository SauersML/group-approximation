# The conjugator graph is self-normalizing: no direct-product benign witness exists

## Status

This is a Cairn-only strengthening of the common-envelope audit for
`Higman.Star.ConjugatorGraph.graph_benign`.

The exact result is

\[
  \boxed{N_{F_3\times F_3}(\Gamma)=\Gamma,}
\]

where

\[
  \Gamma=\langle(a^h,h):h\in\langle b,c\rangle\rangle.
\]

It has an immediate construction-level consequence stronger than the earlier
kernel and coordinate-projection obstructions:

> There is no group \(Q\) and no finitely generated subgroup
> \(L\leq (F_3\times F_3)\times Q\) whose intersection with the distinguished
> first factor is \(\Gamma\).

Thus **every** auxiliary-direct-product benign witness is impossible, not just
the obvious graph-of-a-homomorphism witness.  Any successful witness must make
the original double nonnormal in the ambient group.

No external theorem is used.  The proof is free-product normal forms plus one
evaluation at the basis index \(1\).

## 1. The two free groups inside the first factor

Put

\[
  A=\mathbb F(a,b,c)=\langle a\rangle *K,
  \qquad K=\mathbb F(b,c).
\]

Let

\[
  r:A\longrightarrow K
\]

kill \(a\) and fix \(b,c\).  Its kernel \(N\) is freely generated by

\[
  a^h=h^{-1}ah\qquad(h\in K).
\]

For the argument below, only the following concrete consequences are needed.

1. Every \(x\in A\) has a unique expression
   \[
     x=nq,\qquad n\in N,\ q=r(x)\in K.
   \]
   Existence follows from \(n=xr(x)^{-1}\in\ker r\), and uniqueness follows by
   applying \(r\).
2. If
   \[
     U=\mathbb F\{u_h:h\in K\},
   \]
   then
   \[
     \operatorname{cb}:U\longrightarrow N,\qquad
     \operatorname{cb}(u_h)=a^h
   \]
   is an isomorphism.  Injectivity is the conjugate-basis normal form; its
   image contains all conjugates of \(a\) by \(K\), hence is \(N\).

Define also

\[
  \varepsilon:U\longrightarrow K,\qquad \varepsilon(u_h)=h.
\]

The subgroup under audit is the graph

\[
  \Gamma=
  \{(\operatorname{cb}(w),\varepsilon(w)):w\in U\}
  \leq A\times A.
\]

## 2. The free factor \(K\) is self-normalizing in \(A\)

We first record the one free-product fact needed in the second coordinate.

### Lemma 2.1

\[
  N_A(K)=K.
\]

### Proof

Certainly \(K\) normalizes itself.  Let \(y\notin K\).  Isolate the boundary
\(K\)-syllables in its reduced normal form:

\[
  y=k_0y_0k_1,
\]

where \(k_0,k_1\in K\) and the nonempty word \(y_0\) begins and ends with
syllables in \(\langle a\rangle\).  For the nontrivial element \(b\in K\),
the conjugate is

\[
  y^{-1}by=k_1^{-1}y_0^{-1}(k_0^{-1}bk_0)y_0k_1.
\]

The central \(k_0^{-1}bk_0\) is a nontrivial \(K\)-syllable adjacent on both
sides to non-\(K\) syllables, so none of the non-\(K\) syllables in \(y_0\)
can disappear.  The outer \(k_1^{\pm1}\) may merge with boundary
\(K\)-syllables but cannot remove those non-\(K\) syllables.  Hence the
conjugate does not belong to \(K\), and \(y\) does not normalize \(K\).
\(\square\)

The center of \(K=\mathbb F(b,c)\) is trivial by the same kind of direct
reduction.  If a reduced word commutes with \(b\), strip its maximal initial
and terminal powers of \(b\).  Any remaining letter makes one of \(wb\) and
\(bw\) begin with different reduced syllables, so no remainder is possible;
thus \(w=b^n\).  If it also commutes with \(c\), the reduced words
\(b^{-n}cb^n\) and \(c\) agree only when \(n=0\).

## 3. Right shifts of the conjugate basis

For \(q\in K\), let

\[
  \rho_q:U\longrightarrow U,
  \qquad \rho_q(u_h)=u_{hq}.
\]

This is an automorphism because right multiplication by \(q\) permutes the
basis index set.  Let

\[
  \delta_q=\varepsilon\rho_q:U\longrightarrow K.
\]

On basis elements,

\[
  \delta_q(u_h)=hq.
\]

Conjugation by \(q\) in \(A\) implements \(\rho_q\) on \(N\):

\[
  q^{-1}\operatorname{cb}(w)q
  =\operatorname{cb}(\rho_q(w)).
\]

This follows on \(u_h\) from

\[
  q^{-1}a^hq=a^{hq}
\]

and then on every word by homomorphicity.

## 4. Exact normalizer computation

### Theorem 4.1

\[
  \boxed{N_{A\times A}(\Gamma)=\Gamma.}
\]

### Proof

The inclusion \(\Gamma\leq N_{A\times A}(\Gamma)\) holds because every subgroup
normalizes itself.

For the reverse inclusion, let \((x,y)\in A\times A\) normalize \(\Gamma\).
The second projection of \(\Gamma\) is all of \(K\), since
\(\varepsilon(u_h)=h\).  Therefore \(y\) normalizes \(K\), and Lemma 2.1 gives

\[
  y\in K.
\]

Write

\[
  x=\operatorname{cb}(v)q
\]

with \(v\in U\) and \(q=r(x)\in K\), using Section 1.

Fix \(h\in K\).  Since \((x,y)\) normalizes \(\Gamma\), conjugating the graph
basis element \((\operatorname{cb}(u_h),h)\) gives another graph element.  Its
first coordinate is

\[
\begin{aligned}
  x^{-1}\operatorname{cb}(u_h)x
    &=q^{-1}\operatorname{cb}(v)^{-1}
      \operatorname{cb}(u_h)\operatorname{cb}(v)q\\
    &=\operatorname{cb}
      \bigl(\rho_q(v^{-1}u_hv)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Because \(\operatorname{cb}\) is injective, the unique graph word producing
this first coordinate is

\[
  \rho_q(v^{-1}u_hv).
\]

Equality of second coordinates therefore forces

\[
  y^{-1}hy
    =\varepsilon\bigl(\rho_q(v^{-1}u_hv)\bigr)
    =\delta_q(v)^{-1}(hq)\delta_q(v).
\]

Put \(d=\delta_q(v)\in K\).  At the single index \(h=1\), this equation says

\[
  1=d^{-1}qd,
\]

so \(q=1\).  Consequently \(d=\delta_1(v)=\varepsilon(v)\), and the equation
for arbitrary \(h\) becomes

\[
  y^{-1}hy=d^{-1}hd.
\]

Thus \(yd^{-1}\) centralizes every element of \(K\).  The center of \(K\) is
trivial, so \(y=d=\varepsilon(v)\).  Finally

\[
  (x,y)=(\operatorname{cb}(v),\varepsilon(v))\in\Gamma.
\]

This proves the reverse inclusion. \(\square\)

## 5. No direct-product cutter

### Theorem 5.1

For no group \(Q\) does there exist a finitely generated subgroup

\[
  L\leq (A\times A)\times Q
\]

such that

\[
  L\cap((A\times A)\times\{1\})
  =\Gamma\times\{1\}.
\]

### Proof

Assume such \(L\) exists.  The intersection with the first direct factor is
the kernel of the projection \(L\to Q\), so \(\Gamma\times\{1\}\) is normal
in \(L\).

Let \(p:L\to A\times A\) be the first projection.  For every \(\ell\in L\),
normality gives

\[
  p(\ell)\Gamma p(\ell)^{-1}=\Gamma.
\]

Theorem 4.1 implies \(p(\ell)\in\Gamma\).  Hence

\[
  p(L)\leq\Gamma.
\]

On the other hand \(\Gamma\times\{1\}\leq L\), so \(\Gamma\leq p(L)\).  Thus

\[
  p(L)=\Gamma.
\]

But \(p(L)\) is finitely generated as a homomorphic image of the finitely
generated group \(L\), while \(\Gamma\) is not finitely generated.  To see the
latter directly, identify it with the free group on the basis
\(\{u_h:h\in K\}\).  A finite collection of words uses only finitely many
basis letters; killing those letters and sending one omitted \(u_h\) to the
generator of \(\mathbb Z\) proves that the omitted basis element is not in the
subgroup they generate.  Contradiction. \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2

The “hand-built direct-product witness, with nothing glued” proposed in the
older audit cannot exist in any form.  The obstruction is not merely that a
particular graph homomorphism fails or that \(\Gamma\) is nonnormal in the
original double.  It is the combination

\[
  \Gamma\triangleleft L
  \Longrightarrow p(L)\leq N_{A\times A}(\Gamma)=\Gamma
  \Longrightarrow \Gamma=p(L)\text{ is finitely generated},
\]

which is impossible.

## 6. Common-envelope consequence

The target benign witness must therefore satisfy a stronger structural demand
than previously recorded:

\[
  \boxed{
  \text{the embedded original }A\times A\text{ must be nonnormal in the
  ambient finitely presented group}.}
\]

Indeed, when the original double is a direct factor, the cutter intersection
is a normal subgroup of the cutter and the projection-normalizer argument
applies.  Together with the separate retraction firewall, this removes both
natural ways to keep coordinate control:

* the ambient group cannot retract to the original double once Nielsen walkers
  are identified with the graph;
* the ambient group cannot place the original double as a direct factor and
  recover the graph by intersecting with any finitely generated cutter.

A genuinely nonretractive, nonnormal graph-of-groups attachment remains
possible in principle.  No such attachment is constructed here, so
`ConjugatorGraph` remains uninhabited.
