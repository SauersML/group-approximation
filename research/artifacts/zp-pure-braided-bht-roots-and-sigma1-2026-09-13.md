# bF_{n,r}: every number of roots, and Sigma^1 of braid characters

## 1. F_{n,r} is F_{n,1}, preserving the endpoint characters

**Lemma 6.** For every `r >= 1` there is a homeomorphism `h: [0,1] -> [0,r]` such that
`f -> h f h^{-1}` is an isomorphism `F_{n,1} -> F_{n,r}` preserving slopes at the left endpoint and at the right
endpoint. So it carries `chi_0, chi_1` of `F_{n,1}` to `chi_0, chi_1` of `F_{n,r}`, and `Sigma^m(F_{n,r})` has the
same endpoint holes as `Sigma^m(F_{n,1})`.

*Proof.* Let `A = Z[1/n]`, `P = <n>`. The intervals `[0, 1 - 1/n]` and `[0, r - r/n]` have lengths `(n-1)/n` and
`r(n-1)/n`, both of residue `0` mod `n - 1` (part 1, Lemma 1). By part 1 Lemma 2 there is a PL homeomorphism
`h_0` between them with slopes in `P` and breakpoints in `A`. Likewise `h_1: [1 - 1/n, 1 - 1/n^2] ->
[r - r/n, r - r/n^2]`. Define `h = h_0` on `[0, 1 - 1/n]` and, for `j >= 1` and `x` in `[1 - 1/n, 1 - 1/n^2]`,

`h(1 - n^{-(j-1)} (1 - x)) = r - n^{-(j-1)} (r - h_1(x))`,

and `h(1) = r`. Consecutive pieces agree at `1 - n^{-j}` (both give `r - r n^{-j}`), so `h` is a homeomorphism,
PL on `[0,1)` with slopes in `P` and breakpoints in `A`, with countably many breakpoints accumulating only at `1`.

Let `f` be in `F_{n,1}`. Near `1`, `f(x) = 1 - n^k (1 - x)`. For `y = h(1 - n^{-(j-1)}(1 - x))` with `j` large,
`h f h^{-1}(y) = h(1 - n^{-(j-1-k)}(1 - x)) = r - n^{-(j-1-k)}(r - h_1(x)) = r - n^k (r - y)`. So `h f h^{-1}` is
linear near `r` with slope `n^k`. Away from `r` it has finitely many breakpoints, all in `A`, and slopes in `P`,
since `h` does on compact subsets of `[0,1)`. Near `0` it has slope `f'(0)`. So `h f h^{-1}` is in `F_{n,r}`,
and the same argument applied to `h^{-1}` gives the inverse map. ∎

**Consequence.** Proposition 4 of part 2 holds for every `r`: for `m >= 2` and `a, b >= 0` not both zero,
`[(a chi_0 + b chi_1) o pi]` is not in `Sigma^m(bF_{n,r})`. `Sigma^m` is an isomorphism invariant, so Zaremsky's
Theorem A (arXiv:1502.02620) for `F_{n,1}` gives the holes of `F_{n,r}`, and Meinert's split-quotient criterion
pulls them back.

## 2. Sigma^1 for every character with a nonzero braid part

(T6) Koban–McCammond–Meier, arXiv:1306.4046, Theorem A (as extracted): "The BNS-invariant for the pure braid
group P_n is the complement of a union of P_3-circles and the P_4-circles in its character sphere." A character
lies on a P_3-circle only if every generator `S_ij` with `chi(S_ij) != 0` has both indices in one 3-element set
(and `chi(S_ijk) = 0`); on a P_4-circle only if they all lie in one 4-element set (plus linear relations). So a
character of `PB_k` whose support graph (edges `ij` with `chi(S_ij) != 0`) spans at least 5 strands lies in
`Sigma^1(PB_k)`.

**Theorem 7.** Every character `chi = pi^* psi + phi` of `bF_{n,r}` with `phi != 0` in `W` lies in
`Sigma^1(bF_{n,r})`.

*Proof.* If `phi` vanishes on all interior types (`PPP`, `PEP`) and on `EPP`, `PPE`, then by Theorem 1 only
`EPE` is free and the derived values give `EEP = PEE = 0`, so `phi` is supported on the first–last pair,
`tau_phi ≡ EPE != 0`, and Theorem 3 gives `Sigma^infinity`. Otherwise `phi` is nonzero on some type among
`PPP, PEP, EPP, PPE`, and every such type occurs at index pairs `(i, j)` for at least three pairs spanning at
least 5 strands once `k` is large. Examples: `PEP(alpha)` at `(i, i+1)` for all `i ≡ alpha + 1 mod d`,
`2 <= i <= k-2`; `EPP(gamma)` at `(1, j)` for all `j ≡ gamma + 2 mod d`. So for large `k` the restriction
`Phi_k` spans at least 5 strands and lies in `Sigma^1(PB_k)` by (T6). Run Meinert's criterion (T1) with `m = 1`
on the connected, cocompact band `X^{p <= f <= q}` of Theorem 3. Cell stabilizers are conjugates of
`PB_{f(x)}`, the restrictions are nonzero, and vertex restrictions lie in `Sigma^1`. ∎

## 3. Sigma^1 for pulled-back characters off the endpoint plane

**Lemma 8 (extensions).** Let `G = N ⋊ Q` be finitely generated, `chi = psi o pi`, and `[psi]` in `Sigma^1(Q)`.
Suppose some finite `E ⊆ N` has the property that `N` is generated by `{h e h^{-1} : e in E, h in Q,
psi(h) >= 0}`. Then `[chi]` is in `Sigma^1(G)`.

*Proof.* Take the finite generating set `S_Q ∪ E` of `G`. Let `g = s(f) n` with `chi(g) = psi(f) >= 0`. Since
`[psi]` is in `Sigma^1(Q)`, the full subgraph of the Cayley graph of `Q` on `{psi >= 0}` is connected, so there
is a path `1 -> s(f)` through `chi >= 0`. Write `n` as a product of elements `h e^{±1} h^{-1}` with `psi(h) >= 0`.
Since `[psi]` is in `Sigma^1(Q)`, each such `h` has a word whose prefixes all have `psi >= 0`. From `s(f)`, for
each factor in turn follow that word, then `e^{±1}`, then the word backwards. Every vertex on the way is
`s(f) · (earlier factors, in N) · (prefix of h) · (element of N)`, so its `chi`-value is
`psi(f) + psi(prefix) >= 0`, because `chi(N) = 0`. ∎

**Theorem 9.** Let `psi` in `Hom(F_{n,r}, R)` not lie in the span of `chi_0, chi_1`. Then `[psi o pi]` lies in
`Sigma^1(bF_{n,r})`.

*Proof.* `[psi]` is in `Sigma^2(F_{n,r}) ⊆ Sigma^1(F_{n,r})`, by Zaremsky's Theorem A (some `c_i != 0`) and
Lemma 6. `N` is generated by the windings `alpha(I, J) = (T, A_{ij}, T)` over all forests and leaf pairs. Fix one
representative `alpha_t` for each of the finitely many types `t` other than `EEE`. A winding of type `EEE`
(two leaves making up `[0, r]`) is, after cabling one leaf, a product of windings of other types, so it is not
needed. For a winding of type `t`, Lemma 3 gives `g` with `g alpha_t g^{-1} = alpha(I, J)`. Any `c` supported in
a positive gap of `alpha_t` (every type but `EEE` has one) centralizes `alpha_t`. It remains to find such `c`
with `psi(c)` of either sign; then `psi(gc) >= 0` for a suitable power.

Restricted to elements supported inside a gap, `chi_0 = chi_1 = 0`. Writing
`psi = a chi_0 + sum_c e_c rho_c`, where `rho_c` is the sum of log slope jumps at breakpoints of residue `c`
(`sum rho_c = chi_1 - chi_0`; `psi` is off the plane iff `e` is not constant), the element that trades the
subdivision "split `Q`, then split child 1" for "split `Q`, then split child 2" on a standard interval `Q` of
residue `q` inside the gap has slope jumps `+1, -2, +1` at residues `q, q+1, q+2`. So `psi` takes the value
`e_q - 2 e_{q+1} + e_{q+2}` on it. These second differences all vanish only for affine, hence constant, periodic
`e`. So for some `q` the element has `psi != 0`. ∎

**Status of the Sigma^1 formula.** By Theorems 7 and 9, Lemma 6 and Proposition 4, `Sigma^1(bF_{n,r})`
contains every character outside the circle `span(chi_0, chi_1) o pi`, and misses `[chi_0 o pi]`, `[chi_1 o pi]`
if `F_{n,r}` misses them (Bieri–Neumann–Strebel for PL groups; to be cited precisely). Open for `Sigma^1`: the
rest of that circle.
