# Beyond polynomial germs: compact cores and observed mapping tori

**Submitted research draft, 2026-09-20. Independent mathematical review pending;
not Lean certified; no priority claim.** This edited integration records the
third manuscript supplied in the conversation, especially §§0–5 and 13. It is
not a verbatim source copy. Its substantive construction and finiteness lemmas

## 1. Proposed mapping-torus theorem

Let `k≥2` or `k=∞`. Suppose `D` has type `F_k`, `φ∈Aut(D)`, and
`ρ:D→V` is a homomorphism satisfying both conditions

`⋂_{j≥0}ker(ρ∘φ^j)=1`, and `⋂_{j≥0}ker(ρ∘φ^{-j})=1`.

Then the proposed theorem embeds
`T=<D,t | t^-1 g t=φ(g)>` in a simple group of type `F_k` generated by
two finite-order elements. In fact the same construction contains the larger
twisted restricted wreath group `V^(Z)⋊T` with the action in §5 below.
This larger source need not itself be finitely presented.

An embedding `D→V` is sufficient for the observation hypothesis. There is no
requirement that `φ` extend to an automorphism of `V`, be spatially realized
on the Cantor set, or admit a finite-state realization. More generally `ρ`
may have a kernel: its forward and backward iterates must jointly distinguish
every nonidentity element. Since `φ` is an automorphism, either separation
condition also holds after discarding any finite initial segment. Apply the
same condition to `φ^N(g)` or `φ^{-N}(g)` to verify this tail assertion.

The intended contribution is the uniform observed mapping-torus and host
construction. No priority claim for individual free-by-cyclic, automorphism
group, or other previously studied Boone–Higman examples is made.

## 2. Explicit classical dependencies

* Belk–Hyde–Matucci, *Finite Germ Extensions*,
  [arXiv:2407.03149v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03149v1), **Theorem 2.1**:
  for a finite germ extension `B≤Γ`, use the prescribed finite orbit
  condition on tuples of singular points and type `F_k` for the groups
  `SingFix_Γ(M,P)`, where `M⊆P` and `P` is finite with `|P|≤k`.
  Here `SingFix_Γ(M,P)` means singularities are contained in `M` and
  every point of `P` is fixed. For `F_∞`, verify every finite marked set.
  The compact-core proof below aims at this stronger SingFix hypothesis;
  it does **not** use the convenient normal-germ criterion of Theorem 1.1.
* Belk–Zaremsky, *Twisted Brin–Thompson groups*,
  [arXiv:2001.04579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04579), Theorem D and
  the manuscript's cited Theorem 3.4: a faithful oligomorphic action of
  a type `F_k` actor with all finite-subset stabilizers of type `F_k`
  yields a simple type `F_k` host containing the actor; the cited generation
  theorem supplies two finite-order generators under its hypotheses.
* The standard type `F_k` closure properties for finite products, finite
  extensions and ascending HNN extensions, plus the finiteness properties
  of finite-forest Higman–Thompson groups, are used explicitly.

The citations and numbering are the manuscript's supplied classical inputs.
This integration is not an independent source re-audit and does not turn
them into assumption-free Lean proofs. In particular, finite presentation
of a full group does not by itself imply finite presentation of its simple
commutator subgroup; the proposed simple host is furnished by BZ instead.

## 3. The compact-core SingFix mechanism (OPEN)

Start with a base group `B` on the boundary of a regular rooted forest,
with all its relevant finite-forest variants of type `F_k`. The countable
marked set `Q` has finitely many base orbits, called species, and the base
action on finite marked tuples is oligomorphic. Maps must preserve species.
At each marked point choose shrinking balls and their annuli. Base zooms
supported in arbitrarily small buffers advance one annular level.
For the clopen-actor conclusion, explicitly require enough base prefix
maps for the marked-tuple and Boolean-atom matches, and require the
full restriction group on any nonempty clopen atom to be exactly the
corresponding finite-forest construction, with compatible charts and
germ data. These must be verified in each application; an arbitrary
regular-tree base does not supply them automatically.

For each species specify a group `L` and a subgroup `H≤L` representing
**all** depth-zero base isotropy. The required hypotheses are substantive:

1. `L` and `H` have type `F_k` and faithful realized depth-preserving
   ball actions fixing the center and preserving each annulus. In the
   allowed-singularity case the relevant profile group is `L`; at a
   marked point where singularity is forbidden it is `H`.
   Type `F_k` for `H` is a separate requirement: it does not follow
   merely from `H≤L` and type `F_k` for `L`.
2. Each annular restriction is locally base; restrictions to finite
   annular bands, with identity elsewhere, belong to the base group.
3. An injective endomorphism `σ:L→L` describes advance: conjugation by
   inverse zoom agrees on the inner ball with the shifted `σ` profile,
   and differs globally by a base element supported in a finite outer
   band. The corresponding base-only data for `H` must also satisfy
   the required properties.
4. The allowed isotropy germ group is the actual ascending HNN extension
   of `L` under `σ`, with a depth character to `Z` detecting nonzero
   powers of the zoom. This is a normal-form assertion, not only a
   presentation surjecting onto the germs.

The proposed full group `Γ` consists of finitely singular maps locally
base away from `Q`, with specified germs transported by base source/target
charts of the same species. Its closure under composition and inversion,
chart independence, countability, fullness, and the three finite-germ-extension
axioms must be proved, including localization of any one prescribed singular
germ. These are not consequences of just writing a list of allowed germs.

### The actual core at finitely many points

Fix `M⊆P={p_1,…,p_s}`. Choose disjoint buffers and inner balls `U_i`
leaving a nonempty complement `C`. The proposed core is the actual
disjoint-support product

`K=B_C × ∏_{p_i∈M}L_i × ∏_{p_i∈P\M}H_i`.

There is **one** factor at each marked point. It is incorrect to insert
`L_i×H_i` at a point with allowed singularity, because `H_i≤L_i`.
One must identify the complement base group with the appropriate
finite-forest version and prove faithfulness of every realized factor.

Choose commuting contractions `z_i`, supported in their disjoint buffers,
with `z_i U_i⊊U_i`. Conjugation by `z_i^-1` maps `K` into itself.
Indeed complement-supported elements still fix `U_i`, the inner profile
becomes its `σ_i` shift, and the discrepancy belongs to `B_C` as a finite
outer-band correction. The other profile factors are unchanged.

The claim is
`SingFix_Γ(M,P)=<K,z_1,…,z_s>`.
For surjectivity first match the finitely many germs by localized profile
and zoom words. The remainder is a base element which is the identity
near every point of `P`. Sufficiently deep simultaneous contraction
expresses that remainder as a conjugate of `B_C`.

### Why the proposed HNN descriptions must be injective

Adjoin the `z_i` successively. Actual conjugation preserves the previous
stage: it preserves `K` and fixes the earlier zooms because their supports
are disjoint. The abstract ascending HNN normal form is
`z_i^a h z_i^-b` with `a,b≥0`. Every element of the preceding stage has
depth zero at `p_i`. If this normal form maps to the identity in the
actual homeomorphism group, the depth character forces `a=b`; conjugating
then gives `h=1`, using the established faithfulness of the previous stage.
Thus each actual stage is an ascending HNN extension, not an uncontrolled
quotient of one. Products and ascending HNN extensions now give `F_k`.

This argument must work for **all** finite sets needed for the intended
finiteness degree, in every relevant finite forest. BHM Theorem 2.1 would
then give type `F_k` for each `Γ`. For the action on proper nonempty
clopens, finite tuples are classified by Boolean-atom patterns and, in
nonbinary branching, the cone-count residues modulo `b-1`. Base prefix
maps must actually realize the matching. The pointwise stabilizer of a
finite clopen family is proposed to equal a product of finite-forest
restriction groups; setwise stabilizers are finite extensions. Faithfulness,
oligomorphicity, and these stabilizers supply the BZ actor.

The OPEN claim `compact-core-singfix-finiteness` records this mechanism.
Its important remaining checks are the actual localized products, all
base isotropy, the zoom correction's support, successive-stage injection,
and equality with the whole restriction groups on clopen atoms.

A bounded independent internal reading on 2026-09-20 found no immediate
algebraic contradiction in the HNN depth argument, the single local
factor `L` containing `H`, or the use of two genuinely distinct marked
species. It did not certify the full-core equality, localization, or
finite-forest restriction constructions. All new gates remain OPEN.

## 4. Two species for bilateral observations (OPEN)

On the binary Cantor set let `Q_0` be the eventually-zero sequences and
`Q_1` the eventually-one sequences. They are distinct `V`-orbits,
preserved by prefix replacements. At `0^∞`, realize `g∈D` on the
annulus `0^j1C` by the suffix action `ρ(φ^j(g))`. At `1^∞`, use the
annulus `1^j0C` with suffix action `ρ(φ^{-j}(g))`.

Each profile fixes its center, preserves its annuli and both marked
species, has inverse the profile of `g^-1`, and is locally `V` off the
center. Tail separation proves faithfulness at the level of germs.
Depth-zero `V` isotropy is trivial, so here `L=D` and `H=1`.
The advance maps are `φ` at `Q_0` and `φ^-1` at `Q_1`; their ascending
HNN germs are the corresponding semidirect products with `Z`.

Define `Γ(D,φ,ρ)` using these two species and allowed transported germs.
The proposed chart-independence proof observes that a change of base
chart shifts annular depth by an integer, hence only replaces a profile
by an iterate of `φ` or `φ^-1`. Products have finitely many singularities
by the usual containment for singular sets; compact finite covers give
fullness, and an everywhere nonsingular map belongs to `V`.

To isolate one germ, cut off a profile and a supported zoom in a small
buffer, then supply any required source/target map by a base chart within
the same species. Prove the cutoff formulas agree with the prescribed
germs and leave no unintended singularities. Countability follows from
the countable labels and finite descriptions; type `F_k`, `k≥2`, ensures
`D` is finitely generated. The base action on finite marked tuples has
orbits determined by coincidence and species: disjoint cones can be
matched and their complements completed by prefix maps.

The two-species choice is essential. Placing two eventually-zero points
in different forest roots does **not** separate the germ species: they
are in the same `V`-orbit. Transport would put both `φ` and `φ^-1`
profile families at one point, and their generated mixture has not been
shown to have type `F_k`. The proposed construction avoids that unsupported
identification by using the genuinely distinct eventually-zero and
eventually-one orbits. All finite-forest versions still require verification.

## 5. Exact bridge, mapping torus, and larger lamp group (OPEN)

Identify the complement of the endpoints `0^∞,1^∞` with integer-indexed
shells via

`(n,w)=0^{n+1}1w` for `n≥0`, and `(n,w)=1^{-n}0w` for `n<0`.

Let `X∈V` have prefix table `0w→00w`, `10w→01w`, `11w→1w`.
It fixes the two endpoints and maps `(n,w)` to `(n+1,w)`. Define
`ĝ(n,w)=(n,ρ(φ^n(g))(w))`. At the negative endpoint the shell index
introduces a harmless integer offset in the allowed backward profile.
The exact relation is

`X^-1 ĝ X=widehat{φ(g)}`.

This identity must hold across the bridge between the two halves,
not merely as germs. A mapping-torus normal form `ĝ X^j` acting
trivially has `j=0` because it preserves all shell indices; then every
`ρ(φ^n(g))` is trivial, and separation gives `g=1`. Thus the intended
map `T→Γ` is injective.

For each integer `n`, embed a copy of `V` supported on shell `n`,
writing its elements as `e_n(v)`. These are actual base elements with
disjoint supports. The action of `T` is

`X e_n(v) X^-1=e_{n+1}(v)`,

`ĝ e_n(v) ĝ^-1=e_n(ρ(φ^n(g)) v ρ(φ^n(g))^-1)`.

Consequently the proposed subgroup is `V^(Z)⋊T`. To prove injectivity
of this larger semidirect product, an identity first has zero shift.
If a profile equals a finitely supported lamp configuration, it is
trivial on both tails. Tail separation gives `g=1`, then every lamp
is trivial. This is stronger than just verifying the displayed conjugacy
relations. The source need not be finitely presented to embed in the
proposed finitely presented simple host.

The OPEN construction claim `two-species-bilateral-mapping-torus-hosts`
must verify all compact-core hypotheses, exact bridge, and both injections.
Together with the OPEN compact-core lemma, it feeds the root theorem
`observed-mapping-tori-have-simple-fk-hosts` and its larger-lamp companion.

## 6. A noninjective observation family (OPEN application)

Let `r,s≥1` and
`D=Z^r*F_s=<a_1,…,a_r | [a_i,a_j]=1>*<c_1,…,c_s>`.
Define `φ(a_1)=a_1`, `φ(a_i)=a_{i-1}a_i` for `i>1`, and
`φ(c_j)=c_j`. This is an automorphism. Choose a free subgroup
`<α,γ_1,…,γ_s>` of `V`, and define `ρ(a_1)=α`, `ρ(a_i)=1`
for `i>1`, and `ρ(c_j)=γ_j`.

The Jordan-binomial formula, for all integers `n`, observes an abelian
syllable with exponent vector `v` as

`α^{Σ_{i=1}^r v_i C(n,i-1)}`.

For a nonzero vector this polynomial is not identically zero and has
only finitely many integer roots. A reduced free-product word has
finitely many nontrivial abelian syllables. Outside the finite union
of their root sets all survive, and their images alternate with
nontrivial words in the free `γ_j`, so the image remains reduced and
nontrivial. This proves both tail separation conditions, provided the
free subgroup and all representation formulas are verified.

The group `D` has a finite classifying space, the wedge of the `r`-torus
and `s` circles, so is of type `F_∞`. Its mapping torus can also be
written

`(Z^r⋊_φ Z) *_Z (F_s×Z)`,

amalgamating the common stable-letter subgroup with the stated conjugacy
convention. The proposed observed-mapping-torus theorem supplies a simple
`F_∞` host, as well as a host for its larger twisted `V` lamp group.

For `r≥2`, `ρ` is noninjective and `D` contains `Z²*Z`. The manuscript
attributes the nonembedding of this latter group in `V` to
Bleak–Salazar-Díaz, [arXiv:0911.0979](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.0979).
That is an explicit additional literature input, not a fresh priority
claim or independently audited theorem here. A prefix ping-pong proof
or the corresponding classical free-subgroup result supplies the chosen
free subgroup of `V`. This example is intended to show why noninjective
observations matter, not to assert new BH priority for the individual groups.

## 7. Antecedents and limits

The existing `dynamically-v-separated-groups-satisfy-boone-higman` and
its route `dynamically-v-separated-host-singfix-proof` treat a one-sided
injective endomorphism with infinitely many nontrivial observations.
Their finite-presentation argument checks one- and two-point SingFix
groups. Its higher-finiteness remark is explicitly not written out.
The present proposal requires all relevant finite marked sets and
finite-forest restriction groups, and additionally realizes the entire
bilateral mapping torus and the larger lamp group. Earlier status is
retained; it is not upgraded by this new unverified manuscript.

`zoom-tower-germ-groups-give-f-infinity-germ-extensions` provides a related
ascending-germ mechanism. The present compact core must accommodate
nontrivial depth-zero base isotropy and must prove its whole local
profile group has type `F_k`; citing a zoom tower alone does not do this.

The negative `annular-free-factor-germ-extensions-fail-bhm-singfix`
remains intact. Unrelated free germ factors lack the finite transport
relations supplied here by the observed automorphism. An observation
with finite image cannot satisfy the bilateral conditions for an
infinite finitely generated `D`: there are only finitely many homomorphisms
to that fixed finite image, so the intersection of all iterated kernels
has finite index. This does not contradict the negative result about
finite annular realizations.

The statement does not cover arbitrary linear mapping tori: constants
in the depth-zero base isotropy cannot be omitted, and adjoining the
observed profiles may generate a larger local group whose finiteness is
unknown. Nor does it cover an injective nonsurjective endomorphism HNN:
the global negative iterates and exact bridge use an automorphism.
It gives no blanket theorem for all residually finite or solvable
groups, no quasi-retraction, and no assertion of failure of `F_{k+1}`.
The main review gates remain chart invariance, genuine species separation,
actual HNN injection, all base isotropy, and full clopen restrictions.
