## 1. Scalar germ criterion: definitions

Let `f₁,…,f_s` be finitely many one-sided profiles that are integer valued
for all sufficiently large indices. Work modulo eventual equality. Let
`σf(n)=f(n+1)`, which is invertible on germs. Let `M` be the additive group
generated by `1`, the `f_i`, and all their positive and negative shifts.
Set `J=M⋊_σℤ`, with stable letter conjugating `m` to `σm`.

The proposed exact criterion is the equivalence of:

1. Each `f_i` eventually satisfies a homogeneous constant-coefficient
   recurrence over `ℚ`.
2. `dim_ℚ(M⊗ℚ)<∞`.
3. `J` is finitely presented.
4. `J` is of type `F_∞`.

There is **no unit-endpoint hypothesis**. The forward map on the integral
lattice need only be injective, not surjective. For example `1,a^n`, `a≥2`,
gives `σ=diag(1,a)` and `J≅ℤ×BS(1,a)`. This is nonnilpotent and is not a
polynomial-degree induction with nilpotent polynomial germ quotients.

## 2. The finite forward lattice

Choose a common cutoff after which all profiles are integral and satisfy a
common recurrence. Include the constant profile. Remove the zero-root
transient, so both extreme coefficients of the common recurrence are
nonzero. Let its order be `d`. Every forward shift of the chosen tails is
again an integral solution of that recurrence.

The first `d` values inject the space of one-sided rational solutions into
`ℚ^d`: the nonzero leading coefficient determines subsequent values. Let
`L` be the **ℤ-span of the chosen forward shifts** and `1`, as actual
tails from this fixed cutoff. The evaluation map sends it injectively into
`ℤ^d`. Subgroups of finite-rank free abelian groups are finitely generated
free abelian, so `L` is such a group. It is `σ`-invariant.

The nonzero constant coefficient permits backward propagation of a zero
tail. Thus an element of `L` that is eventually zero is already zero as
a tail; passage to germs loses nothing, and `σ:L→L` is injective. By its
definition from all shifts,

```text
M = ⋃_(j≥0) σ^(-j)L.
```

The group `J` is the actual ascending HNN extension of `L` under this
injective map, hence has type `F_∞`. An integer matrix for `σ` on `L`
also supplies a monic integer recurrence by Cayley–Hamilton.

This is **not** the assertion that all eventually integral solution germs
form a finitely generated abelian group. Already the germs `2^(n−j)`, for
all `j≥0`, generate `ℤ[1/2]`. The selected forward lattice is finitely
generated; its inverse-shift union need not be. This distinction is part
of `integer-recurrence-tails-have-finite-forward-lattices`.

Finite rational rank conversely gives a recurrence: the invertible shift
acts on the finite-dimensional rational span and satisfies its
characteristic polynomial. Thus conditions 1 and 2 are equivalent, and
the forward-lattice argument gives 1 ⇒ 4 ⇒ 3.

## 3. Finite presentation forces finite rational rank

This is the submitted finite-window proof of 3 ⇒ 2. More generally let
`M` be an abelian group generated by finitely many `u_i` as a
`ℤ[t,t⁻¹]`-module, with `t` acting by `σ`. For `N≥1` set

```text
B_N = ⟨σ^j u_i : 0≤j≤N⟩,
A_N = ⟨σ^j u_i : 0≤j<N⟩,       C_N=σA_N,
P_N = HNN(B_N,t | t a t⁻¹=σa for a∈A_N).
```

The vertex and edge groups are finitely generated abelian, hence finitely
presented, and the displayed HNN group is finitely presented. Eliminating
the shifted generators gives the fixed generating tuple `(u_i,t)`.
There are natural **surjections** `P_N→P_(N+1)→J`; they are not asserted
to be embeddings. Their direct limit is `J`: every relation in the
abelian kernel involves finitely many shifted generators, and conjugating
the finite collection makes all their shift indices nonnegative, putting
that relation in one sufficiently large window.

If `J` is finitely presented, the kernel of the map from the free group on
the fixed tuple to `J` is finitely normally generated. Each of these
finitely many relators dies at some window, so all die at one `P_N`.
The maps to and from the same presentation then identify `P_N≅J`.

If both `A_N` and `C_N` were proper in `B_N`, choose
`a∈B_N\A_N` and `b∈B_N\C_N`. The word

```text
[t a t⁻¹,b] = t a t⁻¹ b t a⁻¹ t⁻¹ b⁻¹
```

is Britton reduced and nontrivial in `P_N`. But its two commutator factors
belong to the abelian kernel of `J→ℤ`, so it is trivial in `J`, a
contradiction. One edge group therefore equals the whole vertex group.
The HNN extension is ascending in one orientation; its kernel is an
increasing union of conjugates of the finitely generated abelian group
`B_N`. All those groups have the same finite rational rank, which bounds
the rational rank of `M`. This proves 3 ⇒ 2 if the finite-window
stabilization and exact HNN identifications hold as written.

`finite-window-metabelian-hnn-stabilizes` isolates this mathematical
obligation. Together with the forward-lattice claim it feeds
`recurrence-scalar-germ-fp-iff-recurrent`. The older node
`block-power-germ-schedules-with-fp-germ-group-are-exponential` proves only
a necessary recurrence/growth condition in its setting; it does not by
itself prove this equivalence.

## 4. Exact arithmetic scalar germs and scope of the obstruction

For `q≥2`, a prime `p∤q`, and the p-adic valuation `ν`, realize a scalar
profile near zero by

```text
R_f(y)=q^(f(ν(y)))y,       R_f(0)=0.
```

Only a tail of `f` is needed for a germ. Powers of `q` are p-adic units,
so this map preserves depth and is continuous at zero. On each annulus
it is an ordinary arithmetic base map. If `z(y)=py` locally, then
`z⁻¹R_fz=R_(σf)`. A profile germ is trivial exactly when its exponent is
eventually zero, since `q^c=1` for an integer `c` forces `c=0`. Depth
detects the zoom exponent separately. Thus the germ group is the exact
semidirect product `J`, not a quotient assumed to have that shape.

The profiles `n!` and `2^(n²)` cannot satisfy a constant-coefficient
recurrence: such recurrences have at most exponential growth. Their
scalar germ groups are therefore not finitely presented, conditional on
the criterion above. This is an obstruction to this finite-type germ
construction only. It is not a nonembedding theorem for simple finitely
presented groups and does not refute Boone–Higman for those source groups.

## 5. Nilpotent arithmetic base, independently of the polynomial tower

Let `m,q≥2`, `p∤q`,
`N=UT_m(ℤ[1/q])`, `𝒩=UT_m(ℤ_p)`, and `N₀=UT_m(ℤ)`.
The grading dilation `δ_a` multiplies entry `(i,j)` by `a^(j−i)`.
It is a homomorphism; it is an automorphism **only when `a` is a unit
in the coefficient ring**. In particular `δ_q` is an automorphism of
both `N` and `𝒩`, whereas `δ_p` contracts `𝒩` injectively into an open
subgroup. Put `H_j=δ_p^j(𝒩)`. These subgroups need not be normal.

The rooted tree of left cosets has constant branching
`b=p^((m³−m)/6)`, and its boundary is `𝒩`. The identity
`N∩H_j=δ_p^j(N)` gives the sections of
`A=N⋊⟨δ_q⟩`: if the input digit is an integral transversal element `t`
and the output digit is `t′`, the section of `x↦aδ_q^ℓ(x)` is

```text
x ↦ δ_p⁻¹(t′⁻¹ a δ_q^ℓ(t)) δ_q^ℓ(x).
```

The translated coefficient remains in `N`. The action is faithful by
evaluation at the identity and then on the first superdiagonal. Moreover
`N=⋃δ_q^(-j)(N₀)`, so `A` is the actual ascending HNN extension of the
finitely generated nilpotent group `N₀` under `δ_q`; normal forms must
give injectivity as well as generation. It is consequently `F_∞`.

The finite-forest RN group `B_r` is proposed to be `F_∞` for every finite
number of roots `r`. The SZ proof must be applied to the r-headed split
poset: stabilizers are finite extensions of finite products of `A`,
cocompact height sublevels are available, and descending-link connectivity
depends on the number of leaves along `ℓ≡r mod(b−1)`. This finite-root
verification is retained as an explicit part of
`arithmetic-recurrence-germs-realize-compact-core-data`.

Rational marked points are the copies of `N`. Matching sufficiently
small rational balls, and then complements of matching cone-count type,
gives the proposed high transitivity and supported zooms. Base isotropy
germs at a rational point have p-scale and q-scale coordinates
`δ_p^jδ_q^ℓ`; their entire depth-zero subgroup is `H=⟨δ_q⟩≅ℤ`.
The recurrence lattice must include the constant profile `1` to contain
**all** of this group. Omitting it would invalidate the compact-core input.

For a tail exponent use
`R_f(y)=δ_q^(f(ν(y)))(y)`. It preserves depth, is locally base off the
marked point, and every cutoff on a finite annular band is a base map.
Forward shift is conjugation by the inverse p-zoom. First-superdiagonal
values detect the exponent germ. These assertions provide faithful
annular data for the finite forward lattice, not a polynomial induction.

## 6. Bilateral recurrence nilpotent lamps

Let `f₁,…,f_s:ℤ→ℤ` have eventually recurrent positive tails `f_i(n)`
and negative tails `f_i(−n−1)`, independently; allow arbitrary finite
exceptions. Let `𝔽` be the additive span of all translates of these
profiles and the constant profile `1`. Define

```text
W = N^(ℤ) ⋊ (𝔽 ⋊ ℤ),
T_f acts on lamp n by δ_q^(f(n)),
X moves n to n+1,              X T_f X⁻¹=T_(f(·−1)).
```

The sum is restricted. Combine the finite forward lattices for both ends
in one abelian observation lattice; this combination is valid because
the profile groups here commute. The compact-core theorem is applied to
two singular endpoints, with the full depth-zero subgroup present at
both. Global translate modules are not assumed to have finite rank.

Use two copies of `𝒩`, with `U=𝒩\H₁`, and shell coordinates

```text
(n,u) ↦ δ_p^n(u)          for n≥0 in the first copy,
(n,u) ↦ δ_p^(-n−1)(u)    for n<0 in the second copy.
```

The shift is a finite-prefix map with the prescribed opposite zooms at
the endpoints. Define `T_f(n,u)=(n,δ_q^(f(n))(u))`. A lamp `a∈N` at
zero acts by left multiplication by `δ_p(a)`. Since `δ_p(a)∈H₁`, this
preserves `U`: multiplication by an element of any subgroup preserves
the subgroup and its complement, without normality. Shifting defines
all other lamps. The commuting grading dilations give exact covariance.

Faithfulness must also detect finitely supported profiles. First shell
displacement forces the shift exponent to be zero. If on one shell
`δ_q^c` agrees with a left translation on all of `U`, fix `u∈U` and
compare its values on `u` and `uh`, `h∈H₁`. Cancellation forces
`δ_q^c(h)=h` for every `h∈H₁`. Testing an `(1,2)` coordinate equal to
`p` gives `q^c=1`, hence `c=0`, and the translation is trivial. Thus
every profile value and every lamp is detected; the proof does not
rely only on infinitely many empty lamp positions.

Adjacent elementary unit lamps at zero, the shift, the finitely many
prescribed profile twists, and the constant twist generate `W`.
Conjugation by the constant twist supplies q-denominators; elementary
matrix commutators supply higher superdiagonal entries. Under the
arithmetic data gate and the shared compact-core theorem this embeds
`W` in a finite-germ actor of type `F_∞`, whose clopen action satisfies
the BZ hypotheses. The resulting simple `F_∞` host has two finite-order
generators. This is
`recurrence-nilpotent-lamps-have-simple-f-infinity-hosts`.

Polynomial profiles are a special case. A new conditional route from
this theorem feeds the earlier polynomial unitriangular host claim,
and hence its already-recorded q-difference lamplighter specialization.

## 7. An explicit three-generator group nonlinear over every field

Fix `q,a≥2`, take scalar lamps `ℤ[1/q]^(ℤ)`, and let
`f(n)=a^|n|`. Let `A` be the unit lamp at zero, `X` the shift, and `T`
the twist scaling lamp `n` by `q^(f(n))`. Set `G_(q,a)=⟨A,X,T⟩`.
The source need not contain the constant profile: it is a subgroup of
the group in Section 6. Its positive tail is `a^n`; its negative tail
under the coordinate `−n−1` is `a^(n+1)`. Both are recurrent.

Because `TAT⁻¹=A^q`, conjugating by `T⁻j` supplies q-denominators at
zero, and shifts supply every lamp. The group is exactly
`ℤ[1/q]^(ℤ)⋊(M_a⋊ℤ)`, where `M_a` is the span of translates of `f`.
All factors are torsion-free, so the group is torsion-free. Its derived
length is at most three. For the opposite inequality use

```text
H=[X,T],              H has profile f(n−1)−f(n),
B=[T,A]=A^(q−1),
[H,B]=A^((q^(a−1)−1)(q−1)) ≠ 1.
```

Both `H` and `B` are in the derived subgroup, so the second derived
subgroup is nontrivial. The derived length is exactly three.

Suppose there were a faithful finite-dimensional representation over a
field. Pass to an algebraic closure for eigenvalues. Since `A` and `A^q`
are conjugate, raising to the q-th power permutes the finite set of
eigenvalues of `A`. Each is therefore a root of unity, and for some
positive integer `h`, `A^h` is unipotent. In positive characteristic it
has finite p-power order, contradicting faithfulness and infinite order
of `A`. In characteristic zero, `N=log(A^h)` is a well-defined finite
nilpotent polynomial in `A^h−I` and is nonzero. Put
`N_n=X^n N X⁻n`. Each is nonzero, and

```text
Ad(T)(N_n)=q^(a^|n|) N_n.
```

For `n≥0` these are infinitely many distinct eigenvalues of one linear
operator on the finite-dimensional matrix space, impossible. Thus
`G_(q,a)` is nonlinear over every field. The proposed simple `F_∞` host
comes from Section 6, not from a theorem for linear or metabelian groups.

The global profile module has infinite rank. With `σf(n)=f(n+1)`,

```text
(aσ²−(a²+1)σ+a)f = (a²−1)δ_(-1).
```

Translating gives infinitely many linearly independent point masses.
This does not contradict the finite-rank endpoint lattices, because
finite-support exceptions vanish in each endpoint germ. The new claim
`three-generator-recurrence-lamp-is-nonlinear` keeps these algebraic
arguments as a substantive open gate, separate from host finiteness.

## 8. Weighted matrix profile bases

Fix `r≥1`. Consider finitely many matrices
`P_i(n)=q^(a_i(n))U_i(n)`, with `U_i(n)∈UT_r(ℤ)`, such that each
exponent and matrix entry has eventually recurrent tails at both ends.
Let `𝒫` be the group generated pointwise by all translates of the
`P_i`, the constant matrices `UT_r(ℤ)`, and `qI`. The target source is

```text
W_mat=(ℤ[1/q]^r)^(ℤ) ⋊ (𝒫⋊ℤ).
```

Unlike Section 6, these profiles need not commute. Choose a finite
forward integer lattice `E` containing the coefficient tails at both
ends and `1`, regarding all the tails as functions of the same forward
index. Each endpoint uses this actual profile lattice, not a potentially
nonfaithful projection from a lattice of pairs. For each `j≥0`, let `C_j` be the
ℤ-span of products of at most `j` members of `E`, with `C₀=ℤ·1`.
Finite bases give finitely many generating monomials, so each `C_j` is
finitely generated. They satisfy

```text
C_i C_j⊂C_(i+j),             σC_j⊂C_j.
```

To see there are no hidden eventually-zero nonzero sequences, represent
the rational shift space of `E` by an invertible finite-dimensional
matrix. Products of at most `j` coordinates are controlled by its tensor
powers up to `j`; their common recurrence has nonzero constant
coefficient. Backward propagation therefore detects zero germs exactly.
In particular, shift is injective on the selected product lattices.

Let `𝒰` consist of upper unitriangular profile matrices with entry
`(i,j)` in `C_(j−i)`. Multiplication and inversion preserve these
weights, because products along a path have total weight `j−i`.
Elementary matrices for additive bases of the `C_(j−i)` generate
`𝒰`, by eliminating successive superdiagonals. It is finitely
generated, torsion-free and nilpotent, hence `F_∞`. Shift is an
injective endomorphism and all constant `UT_r(ℤ)` matrices are present.
The central scalar profiles `q^a I`, `a∈E`, meet the unipotent factor
trivially, so the depth-zero observation group is
`L≅E×𝒰`, also `F_∞`.

For example

```text
[I+2^n E₁₂, I+3^n E₂₃] = I+6^n E₁₃.
```

The product profile belongs to `C₂`, as required. Bounding the weights
by `r−1` is essential: the unrestricted ring generated by recurrence
profiles need not have finite rank (the powers `2^(kn)` are already an
obstruction). `weighted-recurrence-matrix-germs-form-finite-type-bases`
records this finite-weight theorem as an open mathematical prerequisite.

## 9. Vector arithmetic realization and matrix-lamp embedding

Use the vector p-adic tree and

```text
A_r=ℤ[1/q]^r ⋊ (UT_r(ℤ)×⟨qI⟩).
```

Sections subtract the output residue and divide the translation by
`p`, leaving the linear part unchanged. Thus the action is faithful
and self-similar. The integer group
`ℤ^r⋊UT_r(ℤ)` is a finitely generated nilpotent block-unitriangular
group. Scalar q-dilation expands its translations and fixes its
unipotent linear factor; the actual ascending HNN extension is `A_r`,
so it is `F_∞`. Apply the finite-root SZ construction to this base.

The full depth-zero isotropy is
`H=UT_r(ℤ)×⟨qI⟩`. It is not merely the cyclic scalar subgroup.
The observation group `L` of Section 8 contains **all** of `H`.
Its radial action is

```text
R_(a,U)(y)=q^(a(ν(y))) U(ν(y))y.
```

These matrices lie in `GL_r(ℤ_p)`, so they preserve depth. The map is
locally affine away from zero, has base cutoffs on finite bands, and
inverse p-zoom realizes shift. Testing primitive vectors detects the
scalar through diagonal entries, then the unipotent entries; the
no-eventually-zero property of Section 8 makes this a faithful germ
realization. These are the vector cases of the arithmetic data gate.

In two-ended shell coordinates with `U=ℤ_p^r\pℤ_p^r`, the lamp at
zero translates by `pv`, while profile `P(n)` acts linearly. Since p
is scalar, the covariance is exact. If an affine map is the identity
on all primitive vectors, vary one fixed primitive vector by
`pℤ_p^r`. The linear part fixes that open lattice and is therefore
the identity, after which the translation is zero. This detects
finite-support profiles as well as tails and proves the claimed
faithfulness. Shift, finitely many prescribed profiles, finite constant
matrix generators, and standard vector lamps at zero generate the
source; `qI` supplies denominators.

The shared compact-core theorem, with the whole `H` at forbidden
points, gives an `F_∞` actor and its finite-subset clopen stabilizers.
The BZ application then gives a simple `F_∞` host with two finite-order
generators. This is
`recurrence-matrix-lamps-have-simple-f-infinity-hosts`, conditional on
the weighted observation-base and arithmetic/compact-core gates.

## 10. RN realization without a finiteness assumption on the state group

The scalar and matrix radial maps are rooted-tree automorphisms:
they preserve depth, and within each annulus their arithmetic maps
preserve the corresponding coset structure. Let `K` be generated by
the arithmetic self-similar base and radial maps for finitely many
generators of `L`. The zero/identity branch section of a radial is
the shifted radial `σL⊂L`; other sections lie in the arithmetic
base. Thus `K` is finitely generated and self-similar.

For every finite root number, the proposed identification is

```text
V_(b,r)(K)=Γ_r,
```

where `Γ_r` is the full finite-germ group of the recurrence data.
One inclusion follows from the specified germs of the generators.
For the other, match finitely many exceptional germs with localized
words in `K` and prefix maps; the remaining map is in the base full
group. This requires actual localization and full-group membership,
not just equality of formal germs. The open gate
`recurrence-radials-realize-rn-full-groups` isolates those assertions.

Once that identification and the arithmetic/compact-core gates hold,
these RN groups are `F_∞` without assuming that the state group `K`
itself is `F_∞`. The actions can be noncontracting: in these models
the constant q-dilations have arbitrarily large powers whose sections
along the identity ray remain those same powers. A common finite
nucleus would have to contain all of them. This is a statement about
these displayed actions, not a general contracting-group theorem.

The recurrence RN result includes the earlier polynomial radial
family by taking Newton profiles, but its proof route uses the new
compact-core criterion rather than treating the old proposed tower
as established. All host, finiteness and exact-identification claims
remain conditional in Cairn until their listed mathematical gates are
proved. Finite formula checks in the companion script do not discharge
those gates.
