### (a1) Corollary statement, rev3 draft l.256–259
Current:
```latex
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lef}
A finitely generated group is LEF if and only if it is a subgroup of an
infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan LEF group.
\end{corollary}
```
Replacement:
```latex
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lef}
A finitely generated group is LEF if and only if it is a subgroup of an
infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan group that is the limit, in the
space of marked groups, of finite simple groups $\SL_{3N}(\F_2)$ whose
Cayley graphs form a family of expanders.
\end{corollary}
```

### (a2) Finite models, rev3 draft l.297–312
Current:
```latex
For finite models $\psi_n\colon B_n\to Q'_n$ of $\Delta$ put
$E_n=\F_2^{Q'_n}\times Q'_n$, and attach to $(c,q)\in E_n$ the
configuration $h\mapsto c(q\psi_n(h))$ on $B_n$. Let $x\mapsto\delta x+f$
act on $E_n$ by
$(c,q)\mapsto\bigl(c+\sum_{h\in\operatorname{supp}f}1_{q'\psi_n(h)},q'\bigr)$
with $q'=q\psi_n(\delta^{-1})$. For fixed elements of $L$ and large $n$,
these permutations multiply as in $L$ and move the attached
configurations near $e$ as $L$ moves points of $Z$. Every finite pattern
occurs in $Z$, so a function depending on coordinates in a ball defines a
diagonal matrix $D_n(f)$ by its values at the attached configurations,
and $\sum_\ell f_\ell u_\ell\mapsto\sum_\ell D_n(f_\ell)P_\ell$, with
$P_\ell$ the permutation matrices, is a unital ring homomorphism from $R$
to the algebraic ultraproduct $\prod_\omega M_{|E_n|}(\F_2)$. It maps $G$
to the ultraproduct of the groups $\GL_{3|E_n|}(\F_2)$. The kernel is a
normal subgroup of the simple group $G$ that does not contain
$e_{12}(1)$, so $G$ embeds there and is LEF.
```
Replacement:
```latex
For finite models $\psi_n\colon B_n\to Q'_n$ of $\Delta$, with $Q'_n$
generated by the images of the generators, let $x\mapsto\delta x+f$ act on
$Y_n=\F_2^{Q'_n}$ by
$y\mapsto\bigl(h\mapsto y(\psi_n(\delta)^{-1}h)\bigr)
+\sum_{h\in\operatorname{supp}f}1_{\psi_n(h)}$, and attach to $y$ the
configuration $h\mapsto y(\psi_n(h))$ on $B_n$. For fixed elements of $L$
and large $n$, these permutations multiply as in $L$ and move the attached
configurations as $L$ moves points of $Z$. Every finite pattern occurs in
$Z$, so a function depending on coordinates in a ball defines a diagonal
matrix $D_n(f)$ by its values at the attached configurations. With $P_\ell$
the permutation matrices, $\varphi_n(\sum_\ell f_\ell u_\ell)
=\sum_\ell D_n(f_\ell)P_\ell$ is additive and multiplicative on fixed pairs
for large $n$. If $f_{\ell_0}\ne0$, topological freeness gives $x$ with
$f_{\ell_0}(\ell_0x)=1$ and $\ell x\ne\ell'x$ for distinct $\ell,\ell'$ in
the support of $r=\sum_\ell f_\ell u_\ell$. A configuration agreeing with
$x$ on a large ball then gives a nonzero column, so $\varphi_n(r)\ne0$ for
large $n$. The images of the generators generate $M_{|Y_n|}(\F_2)$:
conjugating the indicator of $y(e)=1$ by the shifts gives the indicators of
$y(t)=1$, and so every diagonal matrix unit, and the flips act simply
transitively on $Y_n$. So the argument of Section~1 shows that the finite
simple groups $\SL_{3|Y_n|}(\F_2)$ converge to $G$ in the space of marked
groups and form a family of expanders, and $G$ is LEF.
```

### (d) Reader trade-off
- **Stronger statement:** the LEF characterization becomes a characterization by limits of finite simple expanders,
  the same object as Theorem 1 and the title.
- **No new machinery:** the proof reuses the free-algebra argument of Section 1, and the ultraproduct and kernel
  sentences go.
- **Cost:** about ten lines.
