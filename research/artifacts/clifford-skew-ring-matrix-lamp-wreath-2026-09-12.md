# Clifford skew rings over the Kun--Thom pair: the matrix-lamp wreath, and two gates

Lane `w4-clifford-df`, 2026-09-12. Target: `clifford-cover-anti-half-skew-ring-not-directly-finite`.

## 0. Setting

- `k` is a field with `char k != 2`; `F_3` is the first case of interest.
- `G` is a group and `X` is a `G`-set.
- `S` is a `G`-invariant graph on `X` with no loops, `b_S(x,y) = [x ~ y]`, extended bilinearly to
  `V = directSum_X F_2`.
- `q : X -> F_2` is `G`-invariant.

`C_S` is the `k`-algebra with generators `c_x` and relations

```text
c_x^2 = (-1)^q(x),     c_x c_y = (-1)^b_S(x,y) c_y c_x   (x != y).
```

It has a basis of ordered monomials `c_T`, one for each finite `T` in `X`. `G` acts by `c_x -> c_(gx)`, and
`A_S = C_S semidirect G`. For the Kun--Thom pair `X = G/Gamma` this is the anti-central factor of
`F_3[E_S]` (`clifford-cover-group-algebra-splits-into-wreath-and-skew-ring`).

**The matrix-lamp wreath.** `P_X` is the restricted tensor product of copies of `M_2(k)` indexed by `X`. It
is generated by `s_x, p_x` with `s_x^2 = p_x^2 = 1` and `s_x p_x = -p_x s_x`, and generators at different
sites commute. `G` permutes sites. Put

```text
B_X = P_X semidirect G.
```

**Orientation.** A `G`-invariant finite out-degree orientation of `S` is a `G`-invariant set `D` of ordered
pairs such that:
- each edge `{x,y}` of `S` has exactly one of `(x,y), (y,x)` in `D`;
- every `N+(x) = {y : (x,y) in D}` is finite.

## 1. Oriented Clifford skew rings live in the matrix-lamp wreath

**Proposition 1.1 (spin embedding).** Suppose `S` has a `G`-invariant finite out-degree orientation `D`. Put

```text
iota(c_x) = p_x  s_x^q(x)  prod_(y in N+(x)) s_y .
```

Then `iota` extends to an injective, unital, `G`-equivariant algebra map `C_S -> P_X`, and so to an
injective unital map `A_S -> B_X`.

*Proof.*
- **Squares.** `x` is not in `N+(x)`, so `p_x` commutes with every `s_y` in the product.
  - If `q(x) = 0`, `iota(c_x)^2 = p_x^2 (prod s_y)^2 = 1`.
  - If `q(x) = 1`, `(p_x s_x)^2 = -1`, and `p_x s_x` commutes with `s_y` for `y != x`, so
    `iota(c_x)^2 = -1`.
- **Commutation.** For `x != x'`, the only anticommuting pairs of factors in `iota(c_x)` and `iota(c_x')` are
  `p_x` against an `s_x` in `iota(c_x')`, and `p_x'` against an `s_x'` in `iota(c_x)`. The factor
  `s_x^q(x)` sits at site `x` and anticommutes only with `p_x`, which is absent from `iota(c_x')`. So the
  sign is `(-1)^([x in N+(x')] + [x' in N+(x)]) = (-1)^b_S(x,x')`, because exactly one orientation of each
  edge lies in `D`.
- **Equivariance.** `N+(gx) = g N+(x)` and `q` is invariant, so `g iota(c_x) g^-1 = iota(c_(gx))`.
- **So `iota` is a homomorphism.** `C_S` is presented by exactly these relations.
- **Injectivity.** `iota(c_T)` is `+-` a Pauli string whose `p`-support is exactly `T`. Distinct `T` give
  linearly independent strings.
- **Crossed products.** `B_X` is a free left `P_X`-module on the tags `u_g`, so the equivariant injection
  extends to an injective map of crossed products. QED

**Example 1.2 (the compressor graph).** Take the Kun--Thom pair, with `u Gamma u^-1 < Gamma` strict.
- `psi(g Gamma) = g u^-1 Gamma` is well defined and `G`-equivariant, since `u Gamma u^-1` lies in `Gamma`.
  Its fibres `psi^-1(o) = Gamma u Gamma / Gamma` are infinite.
- `psi(x) != x`, because `u` is not in `Gamma`.
- `psi^2(x) != x`, because `u^2 Gamma u^-2 <= u Gamma u^-1 < Gamma`, so `u^2` is not in `Gamma`.
- So `S_psi = {{x, psi(x)}}` is a `G`-invariant graph with `b(o, psi o) = 1`, and `D = {(x, psi x)}` has
  out-degree one. Proposition 1.1 embeds `A_(S_psi)` into `B_X`.
- `b_(S_psi)` is nondegenerate.
  - Let `v` be a nonempty finite set in the radical and `y` in `v`.
  - Every child `z` of `y` (so `psi z = y`) needs `|v cap N(z)| = 1 + |v cap psi^-1(z)|` to be even.
    So `z` has a child in `v`.
  - `y` has infinitely many children, and their child sets are disjoint, which contradicts finiteness
    of `v`.

**Proposition 1.3 (matching and complete graphs).**
- (a) `B_X = A_(S')`, where `S'` is the perfect matching `{x_s, x_p}` on `X x {s,p}` and `q = 0`.
- (b) Suppose the quaternion algebra `(-1,-1)_k` splits, as over every finite field of odd characteristic.
  Then `A_(S')` is isomorphic to its `q = 1` version, site by site and equivariantly. Also
  `s_x -> c_(x,1) c_(x,2)`, `p_x -> c_(x,2) c_(x,3)` embeds the `q = 1` version unitally and equivariantly
  into `A_K`, for `K` the complete graph on `X x {1,2,3}` (any constant `q`).

*Proof.*
- (a) is the definition.
- For (b), any two anticommuting elements of `M_2(k)` with nonzero scalar squares generate it, which gives
  the site isomorphism.
- For the embedding: on even vectors the complete-graph form is `|v cap w| mod 2`. The vectors
  `{x1,x2}` and `{x2,x3}` meet in one point, and vectors at different `x` are disjoint. A product of two
  anticommuting generators squares to `-1`.
- The linear map `F_2[X x {s,p}] -> F_2[X x 3]` is injective, so distinct monomials go to `+-` distinct
  `c_T`. QED

**Proposition 1.4 (commutative form).** Fourier transform on the `s`-lamps gives

```text
B_X  =  C_lc({+-1}^X, k)  semidirect  W,     W = F_2 wr_X G.
```

Here `G` permutes coordinates and the lamp `p_x` flips coordinate `x`.

*Proof.*
- The `s_x` generate `k[directSum_X F_2] = C_lc({+-1}^X, k)`, since `char k != 2`.
- The `p_x` commute and square to `1`.
- Conjugation by `p_x` sends `s_x` to `-s_x` and fixes `s_y`, which is the flip of coordinate `x`.
- The monomials `s_T p_v u_g` form a basis. QED

**Consequences.**
- **Largest search space.** For every oriented `S` over the Kun--Thom pair,
  `A_S <= B_X <= A_K(X x 3)`, unitally. A direct-finiteness failure for an oriented cover shows up in
  `B_X` and in the complete-graph skew ring over three copies of `X`, so that is the widest search space.
- **Positive side.** Stable finiteness of the single algebra `B_X` settles every oriented cover at once,
  the compressor graph included (`matrix-lamp-wreath-algebras-are-stably-finite`).
- **Remark 1.5 (why the monomial theorem does not apply).**
  - `residual-lamp-quotients-handle-monomial-actions` twists lamp values by group automorphisms of the lamp
    group `A`. On `k[A] = k^(dual A)` those fix the trivial character.
  - The flip of `{+-1}` fixes no point. So Proposition 1.4 is a monomial action twisted by characters, not
    by automorphisms, and is not an instance of that theorem.
  - A unital equivariant copy of `k^{+-1}` with the flip inside some `k[A]` with automorphism twists would
    need a fixed-point-free block partition of the dual group. The trivial character rules that out.
