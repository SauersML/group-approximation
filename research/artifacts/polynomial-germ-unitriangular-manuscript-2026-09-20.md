# Polynomial valuation germs and unitriangular lamps: edited manuscript integration

**Submitted argument; independent mathematical review pending. Not Lean certified.
No priority claim.** This is an edited mathematical integration of the manuscript
supplied in the user conversation of 2026-09-20, especially its Sections 3–13.
The original sandbox source was unavailable; this is not a verbatim or byte-for-byte
copy. Formula checks in the separately preserved script are finite consistency
checks, not proofs of the finiteness or embedding theorems below.
The preserved script, supplied results, fresh MSI results, and replay
notes are in [the arithmetic artifact directory](polynomial-germs/REPLAY.md).

The proposed headline is: for every `m ≥ 2`, `q ≥ 2`, and `d ≥ 0`, the polynomially
twisted unitriangular lamp group `W_{m,q,d}` embeds in a two-generated simple group
of type `F_∞`. The two host generators are proposed to have finite order. This
would settle the repository's q-difference lamplighter question (`m=2,d=1`), but
that question remains OPEN until the substantive proof gates below are discharged.
This is not a proof of the full Boone–Higman conjecture.

## 1. Definitions and exact target

Let `P_d` be the additive group of integer-valued rational polynomials of degree
at most `d`, with Newton basis `C(n,0),…,C(n,d)`. Put
`H_d=P_d ⋊ Z`, where the generator `X` shifts the argument, and set

`W_{m,q,d}=UT_m(Z[1/q])^(Z) ⋊ H_d`.

Here `^(Z)` means the restricted direct sum. The grading dilation `δ_a`
multiplies entry `(i,j)` by `a^(j-i)`; it is a homomorphism and is an
automorphism when `a` is a unit of the coefficient ring. The element `T_P` acts on the lamp
at position `n` by `δ_q^(P(n))`; `X` moves position `n` to `n+1`. Thus
`X T_P X^-1 = T_{P(·-1)}`. Write `T_j=T_{C(n,j)}`. The identity
`X^-1 T_j X T_j^-1=T_{j-1}` for `j≥1` recovers all lower Newton twists.
Adjacent elementary unit lamps at position zero, together with `X,T_d`, are
the proposed `m+1` generators. Conjugation by `T_0` generates the allowed
denominators; elementary commutators generate the other unitriangular entries.
This includes `d=0`, with no finite-difference relation required.

For `m=2,d=1`, the lamp group is the additive group `Z[1/q][x^±1]`, and the
acting group is the discrete Heisenberg group generated by shift and q-difference.
The commutator acts as uniform multiplication by `q`, up to the convention for
the order of commutators. All source groups are torsion-free and solvable; their
derived lengths are unbounded as `m` grows. These adjectives are not priority claims.

## 3. The affine nilpotent self-similar group (manuscript §4)

Choose a prime `p` not dividing `q`. Put `N=UT_m(Z_p)`,
`N_q=UT_m(Z[1/q])`, `N_0=UT_m(Z)`, and `H_j=δ_p^j(N)` for `j≥0`.
The `H_j` need not be normal. They are open, have trivial intersection, and
`[H_j:H_{j+1}]=b=p^D`, where `D=Σ_{i<j}(j-i)=(m^3-m)/6`.
The boundary of the rooted left-coset tree is `N`.

Let `A=N_q ⋊ <δ_q>` act by `x↦a δ_q^ℓ(x)`. Evaluation at the identity
recovers `a`; the `(1,2)` entry then detects `ℓ`, giving faithfulness.
Choose an integral transversal `T` for `H_1` in `N`. For an input digit `t`,
choose the output digit `t'` so that `a δ_q^ℓ(t)∈t'H_1`. The section has formula

`x ↦ δ_p^-1(t'^-1 a δ_q^ℓ(t)) δ_q^ℓ(x)`.

Entrywise denominators give `N_q∩H_j=δ_p^j(N_q)`, so this translation is
again in `N_q`. Thus the action is self-similar. Moreover
`N_q=⋃_{j≥0}δ_q^-j(N_0)`. The intended normal-form argument identifies `A`
with the ascending HNN extension of `N_0` under `δ_q`; injection, not only
surjection, must be checked. Nilpotence of `N_0` then supplies `F_∞` for `A`.

## 4. Finite roots and rational marked points (manuscript §5)

Let `B_{0,r}` be the RN group of `A` on the forest with `r≥1` roots and
branching degree `b`. Its rational marked set `R_r` consists of `r` copies
of `N_q`. The proposed finite-root SZ adaptation uses the `r`-headed dangling
split poset: directedness gives contractibility; the Stein subcomplex has
stabilizers of finite index in finite wreath products of `A`; leaf-height
sublevels are cocompact. Descending links depend on the feet, not the number
of heads. The original connectivity estimate must therefore be verified on
the allowable leaf numbers `ℓ≡r mod(b-1)` as `ℓ→∞`.

Affine maps between small marked balls extend to prefix tables on their
complements when the complements have the same cone-count type. Taking tiny
disjoint balls around finite tuples leaves nonempty complements and is intended
to prove high transitivity on `R_r`. This extension assertion requires the
cone-count argument, not arbitrary extension of partial homeomorphisms.

At a marked point `a`, a germ fixing `a` has centered form
`a y↦a δ_p^k δ_q^ℓ(y)`. Testing the `(1,2)` entry and using `p∤q`
gives unique `(k,ℓ)∈Z²`. The germ group is consequently proposed to be `Z²`.

## 5. Pivotal base stabilizer lemma (manuscript §6; OPEN)

Fix finite `F={a_1,…,a_s}⊂R_r`. Choose disjoint buffers, and small balls
`U_i=a_i H_L` inside them, with nonempty complement. Construct `t_i` supported
in buffer `i` which contracts `U_i` by `δ_p`, and `d_i` which equals the local
`δ_q` on `U_i` and is the identity elsewhere. The `t_i` commute. Let
`L=B_{0,u}×Z^s`, acting on the complement of the `U_i` and by the `d_i`.
The complement is represented by a finite nonempty forest, with some number
`u` of roots.

The claim is that `α_i(h)=t_i^-1 h t_i` defines commuting injective
endomorphisms of `L`, and that the actual pointwise fixer has presentation

`Fix_{B_{0,r}}(F) ≅ <L,t_1,…,t_s | t_i^-1 h t_i=α_i(h), [t_i,t_j]=1>`.

The support argument uses `t_i U_i⊂U_i`: conjugating a complement-supported
element by `t_i^-1` still fixes every `U_j`. Conjugating `d_i` agrees with
`d_i` on `U_i`; its discrepancy is supported in the complement. This must
be checked for the actual chosen finite-prefix contractions.

For surjectivity, remove the `(p,q)` germ exponent at each point using the
`t_i,d_i`. The remainder is the identity on some neighborhoods of `F`.
Conjugation by sufficiently deep simultaneous contractions moves it into
the complement subgroup. For injectivity, use a normal form
`t^a h t^-b`, `a,b∈N^s`. If it is the identity in the actual fixer, its
vector of `p`-germ exponents forces `a=b`; conjugating then gives `h=1`.
One must establish this normal form in the proposed abstract presentation.

Finally, realize this as successive ascending HNN extensions: `α_i`
extends to earlier stages fixing the earlier stable letters; commutation
and normal forms prove injectivity of these extended maps. Then the fixer
has type `F_∞`, and its setwise stabilizer is a finite extension. The case
`F=∅` is the finite-root RN group from the preceding section.

This is a substantive unverified lemma. Finite generation or finite
presentation of the ambient group alone cannot substitute for it. The
existing zoom-tower result is a neighboring mechanism, not an automatic
certificate for these affine rational fixers.

## 6. Polynomial valuation germs (manuscript §7)

For `y≠1`, let `ν(y)=max{j≥0:y∈H_j}`. Locally, whenever defined,
`ν(δ_p^k y)=ν(y)+k` and `ν(δ_q y)=ν(y)`. For `P∈P_d`, consider the germ
at the identity of `f_{k,P}(y)=δ_p^k δ_q^{P(ν(y))}(y)`, fixing the identity.
Shell depths prove continuity there. Direct composition gives

`f_{k,P} f_{ℓ,Q}=f_{k+ℓ,Q+P(·+ℓ)}`,

with inverse `f_{-k,-P(·-k)}`. Testing `1+p^n E_12` for large `n` proves
uniqueness. Hence the germ group `J_d` is isomorphic, with this convention,
to `P_d⋊Z` and has nilpotency class `d+1`. For `d≥1`, the top Newton
coefficient defines `J_d→Z` with kernel `J_{d-1}`. In particular
`J_{d-1}◁J_d`; it is not legitimate to assert `J_0◁J_d` for arbitrary `d`.
The tower uses these successive normal inclusions only.

## 7. Global groups and simultaneous induction (manuscript §§8–9; OPEN)

Define `B_{d,r}` as the full group of homeomorphisms locally in `B_{0,r}`
except at finitely many marked points, where centered germs have form
`a y↦c f_{k,P}(y)`. Composition and inversion preserve the condition.
Compactness and finite covers are used to show countability on the original
compact forest boundary. Pure radial maps (`k=0`) can be supported on an
arbitrarily small centered ball. Affine prefix maps supply source, target,
and the `k` exponent. Thus the intended germ group is exactly `J_d`.

For each `d≥1`, verify all three finite-germ-extension axioms for
`B_{d-1,r}≤B_{d,r}`: finite relative singular sets; absence of relative
singularities characterizes the lower full group; and each individual
singular germ can be isolated by a globally defined element. Its singular
set is `R_r`, on which the base has high transitivity. Together with
`J_{d-1}◁J_d` and quotient `Z`, BHM should establish type `F_∞` for `B_{d,r}`
provided the lower finite marked stabilizers have already been established.

The induction must prove simultaneously that **all** finite marked fixers
are of type `F_∞`. For finite `F⊂R_r`, put `P=Fix_{B_{d,r}}(F)` and

`C={g∈P: the singularities of g relative to B_{d-1,r} lie in F}`.

The top coefficients at `F` are proposed to give an exact sequence

`1→Fix_{B_{d-1,r}}(F)→C→Z^F→1`.

Disjoint supported radials prove surjectivity. For each finite
`M⊂R_r\F`, the analogous sequence for `Fix_C(M)` has kernel
`Fix_{B_{d-1,r}}(F∪M)` and the same quotient `Z^F`, because the chosen
radial supports can avoid `M`. This proves the required finiteness of `C`
and its finite singular-subset stabilizers, conditional on the induction.

Now act on the **punctured Hausdorff space** `C_r\F`. The proposed finite
germ extension is `C≤P`, with singular set `R_r\F`. A map with no
singularities in this punctured space belongs to `C` because it is already
an element of the global `B_{d,r}`. New singularities can be isolated away
from `F`. The subgroup `Fix_{B_{0,r}}(F)≤C` acts highly transitively on the
remaining marked points. Germ inclusion away from `F` is again
`J_{d-1}◁J_d`. Applying BHM would prove `P` is of type `F_∞`.

This punctured-space argument is the second pivotal unverified step. The
punctured space is generally **not compact**, and no compactness assertion
is being made for it. One must check the actual theorem covers this space,
the action is faithful there, the singular set is exactly the stated one,
and every localization and stabilizer assertion survives puncturing.
In particular, for `x∉F`, establish equality of the local germ groupoids
of `C` and `B_{d-1,r}`, including source/target germs and not just isotropy,
by extending lower-group local maps with support avoiding `F`. Otherwise
global relative singularities need not equal singularities for `C≤P`
on the punctured space. The restriction action is faithful because the
punctured set is dense in the original boundary.

A bounded independent internal reading on 2026-09-20 found no algebraic
contradiction in the proposed HNN orientation or normal-form injection,
and identified this local germ-groupoid equality as a proof obligation.
It was not a complete referee pass: the actual local constructions and
BHM hypotheses remain unverified, so the claims stay OPEN.

## 8. The clopen actor and simple host (manuscript §10; OPEN)

Let `Ω_r` be the countable set of proper nonempty clopen subsets of the
forest boundary. The action of `B_{d,r}` is faithful. The proposed finite
orbit classification for tuples uses the emptiness of their Boolean atoms
**and** each nonempty atom's cone-count type modulo `b-1`.
Emptiness alone is insufficient for general branching degree.

The pointwise stabilizer of a finite clopen family should be the product
of its full restriction groups on nonempty atoms, each isomorphic to some
`B_{d,u}`. This needs equality with the full restriction group, not just
an inclusion: rational affine charts preserve the rational set by
`N_q∩H_j=δ_p^j(N_q)`, change `ν` by an integer, and preserve polynomial
degree and the integral scale exponent. Fullness then glues the pieces.
The stabilizer is of type `F_∞` if the preceding induction holds; setwise
stabilizers are finite extensions. BZ would yield the claimed simple
`F_∞` host and, subject to its generation hypotheses, two finite-order
generators. These actor verifications remain an explicit proof gate.

## 9. The two-root lamp embedding (manuscript §11; OPEN)

Put `U=N\H_1`. Identify shells in two copies of `N` by

`(n,u)↦(+,δ_p^n u)` for `n≥0`, and
`(n,u)↦(-,δ_p^{-n-1} u)` for `n<0`, with two added identity endpoints.

Define `X(n,u)=(n+1,u)`. Its finite prefix table maps the whole positive
root onto its first zero cone, the negative zero cone onto the whole
negative root, and each other first-level negative cone to the matching
positive cone. Define `T_P(n,u)=(n,δ_q^{P(n)}u)`. Its endpoint germs have
profiles `P(ν)` and `P(-ν-1)`; elsewhere it is locally affine.

For `a∈N_q`, the position-zero lamp is `u↦δ_p(a)u` on `U`, extended by
the identity elsewhere. Since `δ_p(a)∈H_1`, this preserves the complement
`U` of the subgroup `H_1`, even though `H_1` need not be normal. The map
is a finite locally affine base-group element; left translations are
faithful. Shifting gives disjointly supported copies at every integer.
The required covariance is
`T_P a_n T_P^-1=(δ_q^{P(n)}a)_n`.

For faithfulness of the whole semidirect product, the displacement detects
the shift. Outside finitely many lamp positions, an identity action forces
`δ_q^{P(n)}` to act trivially on `U`; test the `(1,2)` coordinate to get
`q^{P(n)}=1`. Infinitely many such `n` force `P=0`, and then each lamp is
trivial. This gives the proposed embedding `W_{m,q,d}→B_{d,2}`. Every
membership, covariance, and injectivity assertion is retained as a proof
obligation, independently of the finiteness theorem.

## 10. Extensions in the submitted argument (manuscript §12)

### Several inverted primes

For finite `S` use `Z[S^-1]`, choose `p∉S`, and replace the affine factor
by the commuting scalings `Z^S`. Its finiteness is proposed via commuting
ascending HNN extensions of the integral unitriangular group. The germ
tower is `P_d^S⋊Z`, with successive quotient `Z^S`; the local base-fixer
factor becomes `Z^{S×F}`. The same intended arguments cover finite
multiplicatively independent integer scalings and their localization.
Commuting endomorphisms must be checked on successive HNN stages, as above.
The separate OPEN claim
`multiscaling-polynomial-germ-towers-have-f-infinity-actors` includes the
entire affine base, every vector polynomial layer, all rational marked
fixers, and all clopen finite-subset stabilizers. Together with the
OPEN vector embedding/finite-actor extension lemma it feeds the root
`multiscaling-lamps-have-simple-f-infinity-hosts`.
That target explicitly covers `UT_m(Z[S^-1])^(Z)⋊(E_{d,h}^S⋊Z)`, its
global polynomial subfamily, finite products, and finite extensions.
It is a conditional graph route, not an assumption that the scalar
tower already proves the multiscaling tower.

### Eventual polynomial profiles and their finite exceptions

Let `E_d` consist of integer sequences which agree, independently on the
two tails, with integer-valued polynomials of degree at most `d`, with
finitely many exceptions. The proposed larger source is
`N_q^(Z) ⋊ (E_d⋊Z)`. Its `T_f` lies in `B_{d,2}`: the endpoints supply
the two polynomial germs and the exceptional shells are finite local
affine pieces. The shift module `E_d` is finitely generated by the
position-zero delta sequence and `2(d+1)` half-line Newton sequences;
the combined source is consequently proposed to be finitely generated.

Faithfulness needs a stronger argument here: an eventual polynomial may
have finite support, so empty lamp shells no longer detect the entire
profile. On each shell, if left translation composed with `δ_q^e` is the
identity, compare the `(1,2)` coordinates at `1+cE_12` and `1+c'E_12`
for two distinct `p`-adic units `c,c'`. This gives `q^e=1`, hence `e=0`,
and then the translation is trivial. This step covers finite exceptions.

### Finite products and finite extensions

Finitely many of the constructed actors act on the disjoint union of
their countable sets. Faithfulness, oligomorphicity, and finite-subset
stabilizer finiteness pass to this product action, so BZ applies again.

For `1→H→E→Q→1` with `Q` finite and `H` embedded in an actor `B`, use
Kaloujnine–Krasner `E→H wr Q→B wr Q`. For a section `s:Q→E`, the
coordinates can be written `s(a)e s(a ē)^-1`; verify the action convention
and cocycle identity. The kernel is detected at the identity coordinate.
Restricted and unrestricted wreath products coincide because `Q` is
finite. The actor `B wr Q` is a finite extension of `B^Q` and has a
faithful oligomorphic block action with the required stabilizers. Passing
to a normal core reduces arbitrary finite-index overgroups to this case.
This is a construction for these actors, not closure of arbitrary simple
`F_∞` groups under finite extension.

### Fixed-period eventual quasipolynomials

For fixed period `h`, let `E_{d,h}` be integer profiles which are eventually
polynomial of degree at most `d` on each residue class at each end. The
index-`h` subgroup allowing shifts divisible by `h` embeds in a product
of `h` copies of the eventual-polynomial construction using `n=hk+r`
and a diagonal shift. The finite-extension argument restores all shifts.
No single fixed host is asserted for unbounded degree or unbounded period.

### Graph products

Only a conditional application of the existing permutational BH graph
product closure is proposed. Its Lean frontier is not closed here. This
manuscript does not prove that graph products preserve the stronger
simple-`F_∞` envelope conclusion.

### Noncontracting RN models

Let `ρ_j(y)=δ_q^{C(ν(y),j)}(y)` for `j≥1`, fixing the identity, and
`ρ_0=δ_q`. Put `G=<A,ρ_1,…,ρ_d>`. The proposed recursion for `ρ_j`
has trivial first-level permutation, zero-digit section `ρ_jρ_{j-1}`,
and identity sections at all other digits. The affine sections are already
in `A`. Thus `G` is finitely generated and self-similar. For `d≥1`, the
sections of `ρ_1` along `0^k` are `δ_q^kρ_1`, all distinct. This would
prove noncontraction of this specific action.

The claimed equality `V_{b,r}(G)=B_{d,r}` needs both inclusions. Generator
germs give the first. For the second, first remove a base map matching the
finitely many exceptional image points and the `p` exponents; then use
localized products of the `ρ_j` in centered charts to match the remaining
polynomial germs. Chart shifts preserve degree. The residue is everywhere
locally base and hence belongs to the base full group. Conditional on
this equality and the finiteness induction, these give explicit
noncontracting RN groups of type `F_∞`. They do not settle the existing
general question about contracting RN groups.

## 11. Scope and negative neighbors (manuscript §13)

The existing non-quasi-linearity obstruction for `K_q` is not a new
result of this manuscript. A torsion-free abelian normal subgroup on
which a commutator acts by an integer `q≥2` cannot be faithfully
represented in a finite product of linear groups over fields. The
repository records the algebraic-closure proof: a torus cannot admit
this power automorphism, the characteristic-zero unipotent Lie algebra
contradicts the determinant of a commutator, and positive-characteristic
unipotent images have bounded exponent. Every `W_{m,q,d}` with `m≥2,d≥1`
contains this `K_q`. For `q=2`, its lamps lie in every lower central term.

For `m≥3`, the proposed source is not residually finite: in any finite
image let `h` be the order of the shift. Positions zero and `h` have
the same image and commute because they are distinct source lamps.
Thus that lamp's image is abelian, killing the nontrivial commutator
`[1+E_12,1+E_23]=1+E_13` in every finite image. This does not obstruct
an embedding in a finitely presented simple group.

The negative nodes `shell-prefix-germ-is-not-normal-or-finite-index` and
`normal-germ-extensions-of-thompson-v-contain-no-sl3z` remain in force in
their stated regimes. The proposed construction uses successive normal
`J_{d-1}≤J_d` inclusions after an affine base; it does not claim the
original prefix cyclic germ is normal in the whole polynomial germ group.
The aperiodic stabilizer obstruction assumes local prefix replacement at
the fixed point. The current fixer lemma concerns rational marked points
and affine germs, so it neither refutes nor automatically bypasses every
stabilizer obstruction. The finitely singular tree-host obstruction is
about its particular edge-free core/tree action, not all Cantor actors.

No conclusions about full BH, `Out(F_n)`, closed surfaces of genus at least
three, the LISW complex quasi-retraction, a universal `FP_2` group, general
contracting RN finiteness, or FFWZ Question 5.7 follow from this submission.
