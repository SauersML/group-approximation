## 0. Summary

Seward's weak containment equality turns the Rokhlin entropy of a Bernoulli shift
`L^G` into a relative entropy of a product `Y × L^G` over `B(Y)`, where `Y` is any
free action weakly contained in all free actions. For a free action, `Y × L^G` is
the *Bernoulli extension of the orbit relation* `R_Y`: an iid `L`-field over each
orbit, with the group moving only the base point. The relative entropy of that
extension depends only on the relation, not on the group. So:

* `h^Rok_G(L^G) = ε_{R}(L)` for the orbit relation `R` of any weakly minimal free
  ergodic action of `G` (Section 3).
* `ε` is anti-monotone: `S ⊆ R` implies `ε_R ≤ ε_S` (Section 4).
* Hence maximality of `h^Rok` flows from `G` to every group `H` with a free ergodic
  action whose orbit relation lies inside `R_Y` (Section 5). This contains subgroup
  permanence and gives orbit-equivalence invariance for weakly minimal actions.
* Obstruction: on the relation of any free ergodic action of an amenable group,
  `ε` is full. Lower bounds therefore never climb from a subrelation to an ambient
  relation. Every "subrelation lifting" principle is as strong as INF for all groups
  (Section 6).
* Reframing of the U-target: INF(U) at `q` holds iff `ε_R(L) = log |L|` for one
  single relation `R` (the Bernoulli relation of `U`), with any generating group of
  `[R]`. Lower bounds must come from a *super*-relation realized by a certified
  group, and that seed step is where the U-transfer dies (Section 7).

## 1. Imported statements (verbatim where quoted)

* Seward, *Weak containment and Rokhlin entropy*, arXiv:1602.06680v2, Theorem 1.2:
  "Let G be a countably infinite group, let G ↷ (X, μ) be a free p.m.p. action, and
  let F be a G-invariant sub-σ-algebra. If G ↷ (Y, ν) is a p.m.p. action which is
  weakly contained in all free p.m.p. actions of G then
  h_G(X, μ | F) = h_G(X × Y, μ × ν | F ∨ B(Y))."
  Graph node: `seward-weak-containment-relative-entropy-equality`, where
  `h_G(X, μ | F) = inf { H(α | F ∨ I_G) : σ-alg_G(α) ∨ F ∨ I_G = B(X) }`.
* Abért–Weiss, arXiv:1103.1063v2, Theorem 1 (`abert-weiss-free-actions-weakly-contain-bernoulli`):
  every free p.m.p. action of a countably infinite group weakly contains every Bernoulli action.
* Burton–Kechris, arXiv:1611.07921, Section 3 (`amenable-free-actions-weakly-equivalent-to-bernoulli`):
  for countably infinite amenable `Γ`, every p.m.p. action of `Γ` on a non-atomic
  standard space is weakly contained in `s_Γ`.
* Elek–Szabó Theorem 1(3) with `N = 1` (`elek-szabo-sofic-permanence`): amenable groups are sofic.
* Seward per-group computation, item 4 (`seward-per-group-rokhlin-entropy-of-bernoulli-shifts`):
  if `G` is sofic, `h^Rok_G(L^G, λ^G) = H(L, λ)`.

Standard facts used inline (textbook level, Kechris *Global aspects of ergodic group
actions* and Kechris–Miller *Topics in orbit equivalence*): Feldman–Moore (every
countable Borel equivalence relation is generated by a countable group of Borel
automorphisms in its full group), weak containment is transitive, a free Bernoulli
shift with nontrivial base is essentially free and mixing, mixing × ergodic is
ergodic, and an aperiodic relation has an aperiodic element of its full group.

## 2. The Bernoulli extension of a relation and the invariant ε

Let `R` be an ergodic aperiodic p.m.p. countable Borel equivalence relation on a
standard probability space `(Y, ν)`, and let `(L, λ)` be a finite probability space.

**Extension.** `Ỹ = { (y, c) : y ∈ Y, c : [y]_R → L }`. Its Borel structure and measure
`ν̃` are defined on cylinders: for `B ⊆ Y` Borel and `φ_1, ..., φ_n ∈ [R]` with
`φ_i(y)` pairwise distinct on `B`,
`ν̃{(y, c) : y ∈ B, c(φ_i y) = l_i} = ν(B) ∏ λ(l_i)`.
Concretely, fix a Feldman–Moore enumeration and transport to a subset of `Y × L^N`.
Each `φ ∈ [R]` acts by `φ̃(y, c) = (φ y, c)`. The label field is the same function on
the same class, so `ν̃` is `[R]`-invariant. Write `R̃` for the extension.

**Definition.** For a countable group `Γ ⊆ [R]` generating `R`,
`ε_R(L, λ) = inf { H(α | B(Y)) : α finite or countable partition of Ỹ, σ-alg_Γ(α) ∨ B(Y) = B(Ỹ) }`.

**Lemma 2.1 (independent of Γ).** Let `Γ, Γ' ⊆ [R]` both generate `R`. Then for every
partition `α`, `σ-alg_Γ(α) ∨ B(Y) = σ-alg_{Γ'}(α) ∨ B(Y)`. So `ε_R` does not depend on
`Γ`, and one can equivalently require the smallest `[R]`-invariant σ-algebra
containing `α` and `B(Y)` to be `B(Ỹ)`.

*Proof.* Put `M = σ-alg_Γ(α) ∨ B(Y)` and let `φ ∈ [R]`. Enumerate `Γ = {γ_0, γ_1, ...}`
and let `Y_k = { y : φ y = γ_k y, φ y ≠ γ_j y for j < k }`. These sets are Borel and
partition `Y`, because `Γ y = [y]_R`. Their preimages `Ỹ_k` lie in `B(Y) ⊆ M`, and on
`Ỹ_k` we have `φ̃ = γ̃_k`. For `A ∈ M`, `φ̃^{-1} A = ⊔_k (Ỹ_k ∩ γ̃_k^{-1} A) ∈ M`, and the
same holds for `φ^{-1}`. So `M` is `[R]`-invariant, hence `Γ'`-invariant, and it
contains `σ-alg_{Γ'}(α) ∨ B(Y)`. By symmetry the two are equal. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (the extension is ergodic).** If `R` is ergodic and aperiodic, then
`[R] ↷ (Ỹ, ν̃)` is ergodic. In particular `I_Γ` is trivial for every generating `Γ`.

*Proof.* Let `E ⊆ Ỹ` be invariant and `f_E(y) = ν̃(E | y)`. For `φ ∈ [R]`, the
conditional law of `c` given `y` is the iid field on `[y]_R = [φ y]_R`, and
`(y, c) ∈ E ⟺ (φ y, c) ∈ E`. So `f_E(φ y) = f_E(y)`. Then `f_E` is `R`-invariant, hence
constant, and equal to `ν̃(E)`.

Fix `δ > 0`. Choose a cylinder `C` that depends on `y` and on the labels at
`φ_1 y, ..., φ_n y` (with `φ_i ∈ [R]`) such that `ν̃(E Δ C) < δ`. Let `T ∈ [R]` be
aperiodic. For a fixed `y`, the map `m ↦ T^m y` is injective, so for each pair
`(i, j)` there is at most one `m` with `φ_i(T^m y) = φ_j(y)`. Averaging over
`1 ≤ m ≤ M`, some `m` has a bad set `B_m = { y : {φ_i T^m y} ∩ {φ_j y} ≠ ∅ }` of measure
at most `n²/M`. Off `B_m`, the events `C` and `T̃^{-m} C` depend on disjoint labels
given `y`, so they are conditionally independent. Hence
`ν̃(C ∩ T̃^{-m} C) = ∫ f_C(y) f_C(T^m y) dν + O(n²/M)`. Also `‖f_C − f_E‖_1 < δ` and
`f_E ≡ ν̃(E)`, so the integral is `ν̃(E)² + O(δ)`. Finally,
`ν̃(E) = ν̃(E ∩ T̃^{-m}E) = ν̃(C ∩ T̃^{-m} C) + O(δ)`. Let `M → ∞` and `δ → 0` to get
`ν̃(E) = ν̃(E)²`. ∎

So in the definition of `ε_R` the `I_Γ` term of Seward's relative entropy is absent,
and `ε_R = h_Γ(Ỹ, ν̃ | B(Y))` for every generating `Γ` (Seward's definition, applied
to the possibly non-free `Γ`-action).

## 3. Groups: `h^Rok_G(L^G) = ε_{R_Y}` for weakly minimal free actions

**Proposition 3.1.** Let `G` be countably infinite and let `G ↷ (Y, ν)` be a free ergodic
p.m.p. action with orbit relation `R_Y`. Then
`h_G(Y × L^G, ν × λ^G | B(Y)) = ε_{R_Y}(L)` and `ε_{R_Y}(L) ≤ h^Rok_G(L^G)`.

*Proof.* Define `Φ(y, a) = (y, c)` with `c(h·y) = a(h^{-1})`. This is well defined by
freeness. For `g ∈ G`:
`g·(y, a) = (g y, g a)` with `(g a)(k) = a(g^{-1} k)`. The image field `c'` satisfies
`c'(h g y) = (g a)(h^{-1}) = a((h g)^{-1}) = c(h g y)`, so `c' = c` and
`Φ(g·(y, a)) = (g y, c) = g̃ Φ(y, a)`. Labels at distinct orbit points are the
coordinates `a(h^{-1})` at distinct `h`, which are iid `λ`. So `Φ` is a measure-
preserving isomorphism that fixes the `Y`-coordinate, and `Φ^{-1} B(Y) = B(Y)`. Also
`Y × L^G` is ergodic (ergodic × mixing), so `I_G` is trivial. Thus
`h_G(Y × L^G | B(Y)) = ε_{R_Y}` with `Γ = G`.

For the inequality, let `α` generate `L^G` and let `p : Y × L^G → L^G` be the
projection. `p` is `G`-equivariant, so `σ-alg_G(p^{-1}α) ⊇ p^{-1} B(L^G)`, and joined
with `B(Y)` this is everything. By independence, `H(p^{-1}α | B(Y)) = H(α)`. Take the
infimum. ∎

**Theorem 3.2.** If moreover `G ↷ Y` is weakly contained in all free p.m.p. actions of
`G` (for example `Y = [0,1]^G` by Abért–Weiss, or any free action weakly contained
in `s_G`), then `h^Rok_G(L^G, λ^G) = ε_{R_Y}(L, λ)`.

*Proof.* If `λ` is a point mass, both sides are 0. Otherwise `X = L^G` is free and
ergodic. Seward Theorem 1.2 with `F` trivial gives
`h^Rok_G(L^G) = h_G(L^G | trivial) = h_G(L^G × Y | B(Y))`. Proposition 3.1 identifies
the right side with `ε_{R_Y}`. ∎

## 4. Anti-monotonicity in the relation

**Theorem 4.1.** Let `S ⊆ R` be ergodic aperiodic p.m.p. relations on the same
`(Y, ν)`. Then `ε_R(L) ≤ ε_S(L)`.

*Proof.* Choose `Γ_S ⊆ [S]` generating `S`, and let `Γ_R ⊆ [R]` be the group generated
by `Γ_S` and a Feldman–Moore generating set of `R`. By Lemma 2.1 this choice is
harmless.

Let `π : R̃ → S̃` be `(y, c) ↦ (y, c|_{[y]_S})`. It is measure preserving (a restriction
of an iid field), `Γ_S`-equivariant, and `π^{-1} B(Y) = B(Y)`. Let `α` be a partition of
`S̃` with `σ-alg_{Γ_S}(α) ∨ B(Y) = B(S̃)`, and put `α' = π^{-1} α`. Then
`M' = σ-alg_{Γ_R}(α') ∨ B(Y) ⊇ π^{-1}(σ-alg_{Γ_S}(α) ∨ B(Y)) = π^{-1} B(S̃)`, so the base
label `(y, c) ↦ c(y)` is `M'`-measurable. `M'` is `Γ_R`-invariant, so `(y, c) ↦ c(δ y)`
is `M'`-measurable for every `δ ∈ Γ_R`. Since `Γ_R y = [y]_R`, `M'` contains every label
and `B(Y)`, so `M' = B(R̃)`. Finally `H(α' | B(Y)) = H(α | B(Y))`. Take the infimum. ∎

## 5. Consequences

**Corollary 5.1 (relation permanence).** Let `G ↷ (Y, ν)` be free, ergodic, and weakly
contained in all free actions of `G`, with orbit relation `R_Y`. Let `H` be a countably
infinite group with a free ergodic p.m.p. action `H ↷ (Y, ν)` whose orbit relation `S`
satisfies `S ⊆ R_Y`. Then `h^Rok_G(L^G) ≤ h^Rok_H(L^H)` for every finite `(L, λ)`.
In particular, if `G` is Rokhlin-maximal at `q`, so is `H`.

*Proof.* `h^Rok_G(L^G) = ε_{R_Y} ≤ ε_S ≤ h^Rok_H(L^H)`, by Theorem 3.2, Theorem 4.1 and
Proposition 3.1. ∎

*Checks.*
* **Subgroups.** For infinite `H ≤ G` and `Y = s_G`, `s_G|_H` is free and mixing, and
  `R_H ⊆ R_G`. So this recovers the infinite-subgroup case of
  `bernoulli-rokhlin-maximality-passes-to-subgroups`.
* **Orbit equivalence.** If `G ↷ Y` and `H ↷ Y` are free ergodic with the same orbit
  relation and each is weakly contained in all free actions of its own group, then
  `h^Rok_G(L^G) = h^Rok_H(L^H)` (apply 5.1 in both directions). Example: `G`, `H`
  amenable, where both sides equal `H(λ)`. The content is for nonamenable pairs,
  e.g. orbit-equivalent Bernoulli shifts.

**Corollary 5.2 (amenable subrelations are always full).** Let `A` be a countably
infinite amenable group and `A ↷ (Z, ζ)` free ergodic with relation `S`. Then
`ε_S(L, λ) = H(L, λ)`.

*Proof.* By Burton–Kechris, `Z ≺ s_A`. By Abért–Weiss, `s_A ≺` every free action.
By transitivity, `Z` is weakly contained in all free actions. Theorem 3.2 gives
`ε_S = h^Rok_A(L^A)`. `A` is sofic (Elek–Szabó), so Seward item 4 gives `H(λ)`. ∎

## 6. Obstruction: subrelation lower bounds never lift (class killed)

**Class.** Approaches that certify `h^Rok_G(L^G) = log |L|` for a group `G` by exhibiting
a subrelation `S ⊆ R_Y` (of a weakly minimal free action of `G`) on which the
Bernoulli-extension entropy, or any quantity that bounds it from below, is known to be
full, and then lifting that lower bound to `R_Y`. Examples are the "free subrelations"
idea in the Attempts of `every-group-has-positive-rokhlin-entropy-action` (Gaboriau–Lyons
`F_2` subrelations) and the "subrelations generated by the partition" idea in
`kun-thom-wreath-bernoulli-rokhlin-maximal`.

**Invariant.** `ε` on the lattice of ergodic subrelations of `R_Y`.

**Where every member dies: the lifting step.** Theorem 4.1 gives `ε_R ≤ ε_S`. Upper
bounds pass *down* to subrelations, and lower bounds pass *up* from ambient to
sub. Corollary 5.2 says `ε_S = H(λ)` whenever `S` is generated by a free ergodic
action of an infinite amenable group. Such an `S` exists inside `R_{s_G}` for every
`G` with an infinite amenable subgroup `A` (take `s_G|_A`). By the standard existence
of ergodic elements in the full group of an ergodic aperiodic relation, it exists
inside every ergodic aperiodic relation. So any lifting principle of the form
"`ε_S` full ⇒ `ε_R` full" for `S ⊆ R` would, with Theorem 3.2, prove Rokhlin-maximality
at every alphabet for every countably infinite group containing an infinite amenable
subgroup (and, with the standard fact, for every group), including `U`. That is
INF for all such groups, so the principle is equivalent in strength to the flagship,
not a reduction of it. The same holds for any sub-invariant that is full on amenable
relations, e.g. sofic entropy of the extension relative to the base on hyperfinite `S`.

*Not landed, remark only.* Seward Corollary 1.8 (arXiv:1602.06680v2, for residually
finite groups with property MD and the subgroup formula) extends the fullness in 5.2
to free `F_2`-subrelations generated by suitable actions. That would cover the
Gaboriau–Lyons subrelations too. It is not imported here.

## 7. Reframing of the U-target, and where the U-transfer dies

**Reframed statement.** By Theorem 3.2 with `Y = s_U`, for finite `(L, λ)`:
`h^Rok_U(L^U) = ε_{R_U}(L)`, where `R_U` is the orbit relation of `U ↷ [0,1]^U`. By
Lemma 2.1, `ε_{R_U}` can be computed with *any* countable generating group of
`[R_U]`. So the target `bernoulli-rokhlin-entropy-maximal-on-fixed-tester` at alphabet
`A` is the single relational statement

> `ε_{R_U}(A, uniform) = log |A|`.

It fails on its own when some generating group `Γ ⊆ [R_U]` (not necessarily free, not
necessarily isomorphic to `U`) admits a cheap relative generator.

**Admissible lower-bound source.** By Section 6, a lower bound on `ε_{R_U}` can only
come from a *super*-relation `R_U ⊆ R'` (up to conjugation of `R_U` inside the ambient
space) with `ε_{R'}` full. Corollary 5.1 then transfers maximality from `G` to `U` if
`R' = R_Y` for a weakly minimal free action of a group `G` already certified
Rokhlin-maximal.

**Where it dies for U (the seed step).** Every group the graph certifies maximal is
certified through sofic groups. This is Seward item 4 plus the six permanence
operations of `rokhlin-permanence-toolkit-certifies-only-sofic-groups`. Assume
Elek–Lippner (Bernoulli actions of sofic groups have sofic orbit relations) and the
folklore fact that an action weakly contained in a sofic action is sofic. Neither is
imported, so this paragraph is a remark. Then:
* `R_Y` is sofic for such `G` and weakly minimal `Y ≺ s_G`.
* A subrelation of a sofic relation is sofic.
* A free action of a nonsofic group has a nonsofic relation (Păunescu Prop 1.15; compare
  `free-action-containing-nonsofic-group-relation-is-nonsofic`).

So if `U` is nonsofic, no certified seed `G` has a weakly minimal free action whose
relation contains a free ergodic `U`-relation. The relation-permanence operation of
Corollary 5.1 is a seventh operation that stays inside the sofic class. It does not
break the barrier. Approach "transfer maximality to U through orbit relations of
certified groups" is recorded as dead at the seed step.

**What survives.** Section 7's reframed statement puts no group in front. A lower bound
on `ε_{R_U}` could still come from a super-relation realized by a group that is
certified maximal by a *non-permanence* argument, or directly from relation-level
tools: cost, `ℓ²`-Betti numbers of `R_U`, or a relational version of the finitary
witness. Spark: under restriction to a positive-measure set `B`, the extension over
`R|_B` versus `R` should scale `ε` by `ν(B)` (compression formula). That would make `ε`
a measure-equivalence invariant up to index and would give `h^Rok`-maximality
permanence along ME couplings of weakly minimal actions.
