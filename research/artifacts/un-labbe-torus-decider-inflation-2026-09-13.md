# Torus tiling modules: descent in scale, constant-shape inflation, and why Labbé's ω does not inflate sitewise

Lane `un-labbe-torus-decider`, 2026-09-13. Status: Theorems A, B and Corollary C ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).
Row 7 of the UN calibration table (is `LC(Ω_U, F_q) ⋊ Z^2` exactly matricial?) is still UNDECIDED.

## 0. Target and outcome

Target: decide whether the torus tiling algebras `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω_U, F_q)` have nonzero finite-dimensional modules at
every scale `D` (the exact form of row 7, `torus-quantum-tilings-decide-matriciality-of-sft-rings`, review PASS).

Outcome: undecided in both polarities. What is proved:
- **Theorem A (descent).** A module at scale `D` is a module at every smaller scale on the same torus.
- **Theorem B (inflation).** An `Ω`-preserving substitution of constant shape `S` (both sides `>= 2`) turns a
  module at scale `D` on `Z^2/Λ` into a module at scale `s_min(D-1)` on `Z^2/SΛ`, on the same vector space.
- **Corollary C.** For a free minimal `Z^2`-SFT with such a substitution and a finite field `k`, exact matriciality
  of `LC(Ω,k) ⋊ Z^2` holds iff ONE torus tiling algebra at scale `3` has a nonzero finite-dimensional module.

Firewall: Labbé's `ω` is not of constant shape, so Theorem B does not apply to `Ω_U`; §5 records what a
renormalization for `Ω_U` must handle instead. No searches were run (§5.4).

## 1. Setting

As in `research/artifacts/un-labbe-ring-torus-tilings-2026-09-13.md` §1: `Ω ⊆ A^(Z^2)` is an SFT, `L_(B_m)(Ω)` its
`B_m`-language (sup norm), `k` a field, and `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω,k)` is generated by `e_a(t)` (`a ∈ A`, `t ∈ Z^2/Λ`) with
- (T1) `sum_a e_a(t) = 1`, `e_a(t) e_b(t) = δ_ab e_a(t)`;
- (T2) `[e_a(t), e_b(t')] = 0` whenever `t' - t` has a representative in `B_(2D)`;
- (T3) `prod_(h ∈ B_D) e_(π(h))(t + h) = 0` for every `π ∉ L_(B_D)(Ω)`.

A **substitution of constant shape** `S = diag(s_1, s_2)` is a map `σ : A -> A^(Q_S)`, `Q_S = [0,s_1) × [0,s_2)`,
extended to configurations by `σ(x)(Sn + j) = σ(x(n))_j` (`n ∈ Z^2`, `j ∈ Q_S`). It is **`Ω`-preserving** if
`σ(Ω) ⊆ Ω`. Put `s_min = min(s_1, s_2)`.

## 2. Theorem A (descent in scale)

**Statement.** If `D' <= D`, every `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω,k)`-module is a `𝒯_(D',Λ)(Ω,k)`-module through the same generators.

*Proof.* (T1) is the same relation, and (T2) at `2D'` is part of (T2) at `2D`. For (T3), let `π ∉ L_(B_(D'))(Ω)` be
placed at `t`. By (T1), inserting `sum_a e_a(t+h) = 1` for `h ∈ B_D \ B_(D')`,
`prod_(h ∈ B_(D')) e_(π(h))(t+h) = sum_(π̃) prod_(h ∈ B_D) e_(π̃(h))(t+h)`,
the sum over all extensions `π̃` of `π` to `B_D`. All factors commute by (T2) at scale `D`. Every `π̃` restricts to
`π`, so `π̃ ∉ L_(B_D)(Ω)`, and (T3) at scale `D` kills each term. ∎

**Consequence.** In Theorem 1(d) of the torus criterion it suffices to have modules at unboundedly many scales.

## 3. Theorem B (constant-shape inflation)

**Statement.** Let `σ` be an `Ω`-preserving substitution of constant shape `S` with `s_min >= 2`, let `D >= 2`,
`D' = s_min (D - 1)`, and `Λ' = SΛ`. If `V` is a finite-dimensional `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω,k)`-module, then `V` is a
`𝒯_(D',Λ')(Ω,k)`-module through

`f_b(t') = sum_(a ∈ A : σ(a)_j = b) e_a(p̄)`, where `t' = Sp + j` with `j ∈ Q_S`, and `p̄ = p mod Λ`.

*Proof.*
0. **Well defined.** Changing the representative of `t'` by `Sλ` changes `p` by `λ` and keeps `j`.
1. **(T1).** `sum_b f_b(t') = sum_a e_a(p̄) = 1`. Since the `e_a(p̄)` are orthogonal idempotents,
   `f_b(t') f_(b')(t') = sum_(a : σ(a)_j = b = b') e_a(p̄) = δ_(bb') f_b(t')`.
2. **(T2).** Generators with the same parent site commute, being polynomials in the commuting `e_a(p̄)`. Let
   `t'_2 - t'_1` have a representative `w'` with `|w'| <= 2D'`, and lift so that `t'_2 = t'_1 + w'`. With parents
   `p_l = ⌊t'_l / S⌋` (coordinatewise floor), `|(p_2 - p_1)_i| <= ⌈|w'_i| / s_i⌉ <= ⌈2 s_min (D-1) / s_i⌉ <= 2(D-1)`.
   So the parents are within `2D`, and their generators commute by (T2) at scale `D`.
3. **(T3).** Let `π ∉ L_(B_(D'))(Ω)`, lift the ball to `t'_0 + B_(D') ⊆ Z^2`, and let
   `P = {⌊(t'_0 + h)/S⌋ : h ∈ B_(D')} ⊆ Z^2`. In coordinate `i`, `P` takes at most
   `⌈(2D' + 1)/s_i⌉ <= ⌈(2 s_i (D-1) + 1)/s_i⌉ = 2D - 1` consecutive values, so `P ⊆ c + B_(D-1)` for some `c`.
   - All factors commute by step 2. Grouping by parents,
     `prod_(h ∈ B_(D')) f_(π(h))(t'_0 + h) = prod_(p ∈ P) sum_(a ∈ A_p) e_a(p̄)`,
     with `A_p = {a : σ(a)_j = π(h) whenever t'_0 + h = Sp + j}`.
   - Expanding, and inserting (T1) at the points of `c + B_D` outside `P`, the product equals the sum over all
     `ρ̃ : c + B_D -> A` with `ρ̃(p) ∈ A_p` (`p ∈ P`) of `prod_(z ∈ c + B_D) e_(ρ̃(z))(z̄)`. Two lifts of one torus site
     carrying different letters give a zero term by orthogonality, so the identity holds as a sum over lifted
     assignments and no embedding of the ball in the torus is needed.
   - If `ρ̃ ∉ L_(B_D)(Ω)`, the term vanishes by (T3).
   - If `ρ̃ ∈ L_(B_D)(Ω)`, choose `x ∈ Ω` extending it. Then `σ(x) ∈ Ω`, and for every `h ∈ B_(D')`, writing
     `t'_0 + h = Sp + j`, `σ(x)(t'_0 + h) = σ(x(p))_j = σ(ρ̃(p))_j = π(h)`. So `π ∈ L_(B_(D'))(Ω)`, a contradiction.
   Every term vanishes. ∎

**Scale growth.** For `s_min >= 2` and `D >= 3`, `D' = s_min(D-1) >= 2D - 2 >= D + 1`.

## 4. Corollary C (one base module decides)

**Statement.** Let `Ω` be a free minimal `Z^2`-SFT, `k` a finite field, and `σ` an `Ω`-preserving substitution of
constant shape with `s_min >= 2`. Then `LC(Ω,k) ⋊ Z^2` is exactly matricial iff there is `Λ` such that
`𝒯_(3,Λ)(Ω,k)` has a nonzero finite-dimensional module.

*Proof.* (⇒) Theorem 1(d) of `torus-quantum-tilings-decide-matriciality-of-sft-rings` at `D = 3`. (⇐) Put `D_0 = 3`
and `D_(m+1) = s_min(D_m - 1)`. By Theorem B there are nonzero modules at scale `D_m` on `Z^2/S^mΛ`, and
`D_(m+1) >= D_m + 1`. By Theorem A there are modules at every scale. Theorem 1(d) gives the embedding. ∎

**Calibration.**
- **Periodic tile set.** For `A = {a}`, `Ω = {a^(Z^2)}` and `σ(a) = a^(Q_S)`, every torus carries the one-dimensional
  module `e_a = 1`, and Theorem B returns the same module on `SΛ`. (This `Ω` is not free; freeness enters only
  through Theorem 1(d).)
- **Aperiodic `Ω`.** For `D >= r`, no nonzero torus module is commutative: a common eigenvector reads off a
  `Λ`-periodic legal point (proof of Theorem 2(a) of the torus criterion). So the base module in Corollary C is
  necessarily contextual. Corollary C reduces "every scale" to "one scale"; it does not produce the base.

## 5. Labbé's shift: why this does not decide row 7

1. **`ω` is not of constant shape.** By Labbé's `thm:exist-homeo` (import
   `labbe-substitution-is-primitive-and-recognizable`), `α` and `β` send each letter to a letter or a domino, and `γ`
   is a letter bijection, so `ω = αβγ` has letter images of shapes in `{(1,1),(2,1),(1,2),(2,2)}`, some letter giving
   `(2,2)`. Theorem B needs one shape for all letters.
   - With letter-dependent shapes, the concatenation rule gives a common width per column and a common height per
     row for legal words (`research/artifacts/un-labbe-ring-substitution-complexity-2026-09-13.md` §3 Step 2). In a
     module, "which columns widen" becomes the projections `W(t) = sum_(a : width(ω(a)) = 2) e_a(t)`, and the inflated
     torus is not a fixed set unless these projections are scalar.
   - **Attempt.** If every width and height projection is central, a simple module has scalar skeleton and inflates
     by the non-uniform rule. The next step's width projections are new combinations of generators and need not be
     central, so this does not iterate. Not established. I did not check from the source that single-letter and
     domino images both occur in each direction.
2. **No recognizable constant-shape self-similarity (recalled, not imported).** A minimal `Z^2`-subshift generated by a
   recognizable constant-shape substitution factors onto the `S`-adic odometer, hence has eigenvalues in
   `S^(-m)Z^2 mod Z^2`. `Ω_U` is measure-isomorphic to the rotation by `φ^(-2)` (`labbe-shift-is-a-toral-rotation-coding`),
   whose eigenvalues `Z + Zφ^(-2)` (per coordinate) contain no non-integer rationals. So Corollary C cannot be applied
   to `Ω_U` through a recognizable constant-shape substitution. Non-recognizable `Ω_U`-preserving constant-shape maps
   are not excluded by this argument.
3. **Renormalization for `Ω_U` must superpose geometry (heuristic).** A witness built by renormalizing Labbé's `ω`
   must keep some skeleton projections (which rows or columns carry dominoes) non-scalar at some level. If all of them
   were scalar at every level, the witness would be a hierarchical configuration on a finite torus, i.e. a periodic
   legal point. Making this precise needs module desubstitution through recognizability. Not established.
4. **Searches: not run.** A meaningful search needs `D >= max(r, D_0)` (the wall-rigidity constant `D_0` of Theorem W
   is not explicit), simple factors of dimension `>= 3` (wall rigidity with Corollary 3.3 of
   `research/artifacts/quantum-rigidity-walls-and-commutator-ideal-2026-09-13.md`), and tori of sup-diameter `> 2D`,
   e.g. at least `14 × 14` at `D = 3`: `196` sites with `19` idempotent generators each. Noncommutative Gröbner bases
   or representation searches at that size are beyond the RULES §3 budget. A periodic-tile calibration is trivial
   (§4). So no MSI job was run.

## 6. What remains

- **"No".** At one scale, a certificate uniform in `Λ` that `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω_U, F_q)` has no nonzero finite-dimensional
  module. One-dimensional skeletons alone cannot give it: one-dimensional Sturmian torus modules exist, since
  Sturmian crossed products are matricial.
- **"Yes".** A contextual base module together with a renormalization whose skeleton is quantum, since no
  constant-shape self-similarity of `Ω_U` is available (§5.2).
