# Does S_X ≅ S_Y force orbit equivalence? Part 1: what the isomorphism theorems actually give

Lane `un-orbit-equivalence`, 2026-09-13. Status:
- §1 is a verbatim import of Zel'manov 1985, read from the scanned Springer PDF on MSI.
- §2 (Theorem G) is proved here and UNREVIEWED.
- §3 records why the projective group `S_X` is not reached; §4 plans the `K_0` and Giordano–Putnam–Skau steps. Both
  stay OPEN.

## 0. Setting and target

`q` is a prime power, `X, Y` are infinite minimal subshifts, `R_X = LC(X,F_q) ⋊_T Z`, `G_X = EL_3(R_X)` and
`S_X = G_X / Z(G_X)`. The open claim `subshift-elementary-group-isomorphism-forces-orbit-equivalence` asks whether
`S_X ≅ S_Y` forces strong orbit equivalence. The route in its Attempts has three steps:
- (a) group isomorphism ⇒ ring isomorphism or anti-isomorphism of `M_3(R_X)` and `M_3(R_Y)`;
- (b) the ring (anti-)isomorphism gives an isomorphism of unital ordered `K_0`, identified with `K^0(X,T)`;
- (c) Giordano–Putnam–Skau.

> E_n(R) the elementary subgroup of GL_n(R) generated by transvections t_ij(λ) = 1 + λe_ij, i ≠ j, 1 ≤ i, j ≤ n,
> λ ∈ R [...]"
>
> "It follows from the results of Secs. 1 and 2 that the isomorphisms φ: GL_n(R) → GL_m(S), φ: O_n(R, π) → O_m(S, π'),
> where n ≥ 3, m ≥ 2; R, S ∋ 1/2, are standard on the elementary subgroup."

(Z1), p. 520, §2, the orthogonal closure:
> "Let E = {e_1, ..., e_m} be a finite set of commuting idempotents of the ring R. The orthogonal closure of the set E
> is the set of idempotents E_R = {δ_1...δ_m | δ_i ∈ {e_i, 1 − e_i}}. If Σ = {σ_1, ..., σ_m} is a finite set of
> commuting involutions, their orthogonal closure is the subgroup Σ_R generated by the involutions 1 − 2e where
> e ∈ {((1 − σ_i)/2) | 1 ≤ i ≤ m}."

(Z2), p. 527, Theorem 3:
> "THEOREM 3. Let R be an associative ring with 1/2 and assume that either E_n(R) ⊆ G ⊆ GL_n(R), Σ = Σ_1 or
> EO_n(R, π) ⊆ G ⊆ O_n(R, π), Σ = Σ_2; φ: G → S* is a monomorphism into the group of invertible elements of an
> associative ring S ∋ 1/2 such that φ(G) ⊇ φ(Σ)_S. Then ψ = (ψ_α | α ∈ M*), ψ_α: J_α ∋ x → φ(1 + x) − 1 ∈ S is a
> specialization of the Jordan system (J_α | α ∈ M*) into S."

Here (p. 523) "Σ_1 = {σ = diag[±1, ..., ±1] ∈ E_n(R) | det σ = 1}".

(Z3), p. 528, §3, standing assumptions:
> "In this section we point out some corollaries of Theorems 1-3. We start with two associative rings R, S with 1/2;
> the group G lies either between E_n(R) and GL_n(R) or between EO_n(R, π) and O_n(R, π), n ≥ 3; φ: G → S* is a
> monomorphism such that φ(G) ⊇ φ(Σ)_S. We shall assume that the ring S is generated by the set φ(G)."

(Z4), p. 528, Corollary 1:
> "COROLLARY 1. Assume that E_n(R) ⊆ G ⊆ GL_n(R). Then there exist 1) a decomposition of the ring S into a direct sum
> of ideals S = S_0 ⊕ S_1 ⊕ S', 2) an epimorphism φ_0: R_n → S_0, 3) an antiepimorphism φ_1: R_n → S_1, such that for
> every element g ∈ E_n(R) we have φ(g) = φ_0(g) + φ_1(g^{-1}) + f, where f is the identity of the ideal S'."

(Z5), p. 529, Corollary 4:
> "COROLLARY 4. If S = A_m is the matrix ring of order m ≥ 2 over a ring A ∋ 1/2, φ(G) = GL_m(A) = S* then
> R_n = I_0 ⊕ I_1, S = S_0 ⊕ S_1, φ_0: I_0 → S_0 an isomorphism, φ_1: I_1 → S_1 an antiisomorphism, and for every
> element g = g_0 + g_1 ∈ E_n(R), g_i ∈ I_i, i = 1, 2 we have φ(g) = φ_0(g_0) + φ_1(g_1^{-1})."

Scope notes:
- **1/2 is needed.** Every statement assumes `R, S ∋ 1/2`, so characteristic 2 (`q` even) is excluded.
- **Groups between E_n and GL_n.** The theorems concern groups `G` with `E_n(R) ⊆ G ⊆ GL_n(R)` and monomorphisms
  into a ring's unit group. They do not treat quotients `E_n(R)/Z` by the centre.

## 2. Theorem G (q odd): GL_3 remembers M_3(R) up to anti-isomorphism

**Theorem G.** Let `q` be odd and `X, Y` infinite minimal subshifts. If `GL_3(R_X) ≅ GL_3(R_Y)` as abstract groups,
then `M_3(R_X) ≅ M_3(R_Y)` or `M_3(R_X) ≅ M_3(R_Y)^op` as rings.

Two lemmas come first.

**Lemma G1.** For any unital ring `A` and `m ≥ 2`, `M_m(A)` is generated as a ring by `GL_m(A)`.
*Proof.* For `i ≠ j` and `r ∈ A`, `r E_ij = e_ij(r) − 1` is a difference of two units. For the diagonal,
`r E_ii = (r E_ij)(E_ji)` with `j ≠ i` and `E_ji = e_ji(1) − 1`. These matrix-unit multiples span `M_m(A)` additively. ∎

**Lemma G2.** If `R` is a simple ring, then `M_n(R)` is simple.
*Proof.* Every two-sided ideal of `M_n(R)` has the form `M_n(I)` for a two-sided ideal `I` of `R`: `I` is the set of
`(1,1)` entries of the ideal, and conjugating by matrix units moves any entry to position `(1,1)`. ∎

*Proof of Theorem G.* Let `φ_iso: GL_3(R_X) → GL_3(R_Y)` be an isomorphism. Set:
- `R = R_X`, `n = 3`, `G = GL_3(R_X)`, so `E_3(R) ⊆ G ⊆ GL_3(R)`;
- `S = M_3(R_Y)` and `A = R_Y`, `m = 3`;
- `φ = φ_iso` followed by the inclusion `GL_3(R_Y) = S* ⊆ S`.

Check the standing hypotheses (Z3) and those of Corollary 4 (Z5):
1. **1/2.** `R_X` and `R_Y` contain `F_q` with `q` odd, so `2` is invertible.
2. **Monomorphism.** `φ` is injective and `φ(G) = GL_3(R_Y) = S*`.
3. **Closure condition.** `φ(Σ)_S` is a subgroup of `S*` by (Z1), and `φ(G) = S*`, so `φ(G) ⊇ φ(Σ)_S`.
4. **S is generated by φ(G) = S*.** This is Lemma G1.

Corollary 4 then gives `M_3(R_X) = I_0 ⊕ I_1` and `M_3(R_Y) = S_0 ⊕ S_1`, with `φ_0: I_0 → S_0` a ring isomorphism
and `φ_1: I_1 → S_1` an anti-isomorphism.

By `minimal-subshift-algebra-is-simple-lef-ring` (item 1), `R_X` and `R_Y` are simple. By Lemma G2 so are
`M_3(R_X)` and `M_3(R_Y)`. A simple ring has no nontrivial direct sum decomposition into two ideals.
- If `I_1 = 0`, then `I_0 = M_3(R_X)` and `S_0 ≅ M_3(R_X) ≠ 0`. Hence `S_1 = 0` and `M_3(R_X) ≅ M_3(R_Y)`.
- If `I_0 = 0`, then `I_1 = M_3(R_X)`, `S_1 ≠ 0`, `S_0 = 0`, and `M_3(R_X) ≅ M_3(R_Y)^op`. ∎

Model tests and scope:
- **Calibration.** Take `Y = X` and the transpose-inverse automorphism `g ↦ (g^t)^{-1}`. Transposition over a
  noncommutative ring is an anti-automorphism of `M_3` only after composing with an anti-automorphism of `R_X`. The
  theorem correctly allows the op case.
- **Hypotheses used.** Simplicity is used only through Lemma G2. For a non-simple ring (a periodic subshift) the
  decomposition case of Corollary 4 genuinely occurs, and the theorem makes no claim there.
- **q even.** Not covered: (Z0)–(Z5) all assume `1/2`.

## 3. Why the projective group S_X is not reached (firewall F-PE)

The open claim concerns `S_X = E_3(R_X)/Z`, not `GL_3`. An isomorphism `S_X ≅ S_Y` does not supply the setting of
(Z3), for two reasons.

**F-PE1: the lift stops at central quotients.** `S_X` is perfect. `E_3(R_X)` and `E_3(R_Y)` are perfect central
extensions of `S_X ≅ S_Y` with finite kernels inside `F_q^× I_3`. Lifting to the universal central extension `U`
gives `E_3(R_X) = U/C_X` and `E_3(R_Y) = U/C_Y`. The isomorphism descends to `E_3(R_X) ≅ E_3(R_Y)` only when
`C_X = C_Y`. In general one gets only a common central quotient `E_3(R_X)/Z_0 ≅ E_3(R_Y)/Z_0'`, which is not a group
between `E_3` and `GL_3`.

The centre itself can depend on `X`. `λ I_3 ∈ E_3(R_X)` needs `3[λ] = 0` in `K_1(R_X)`. The class of the constant
`λ` in `coker(1 − T_*)` on `LC(X,F_q^×)` vanishes when `λ^{-1}` is a continuous eigenvalue of `T`, which happens
iff `X` has a cyclic factor of order `ord(λ)`. So `C_X = C_Y` is not automatic.

**F-PE2: Corollary 1 at G = E_3 needs the closure condition.** For `G = E_3(R_X)`,
`S = M_3(R_Y)` is generated by `φ(G) = E_3(R_Y)` (Lemma G1 uses only elementary matrices). But (Z3) also asks
`φ(Σ_1)_S ⊆ φ(G)`. The orthogonal closure contains sign involutions `1 − 2e` for single atoms `e` of the image
idempotents. Their `K_1` class is `[−1]` on a corner, which need not lie in `E_3(R_Y)`. So an isomorphism
`E_3(R_X) ≅ E_3(R_Y)` is not covered by Zel'manov as quoted.

**What would close step (a) for S_X:** an isomorphism theorem for projective elementary groups
`PE_3(R) = E_3(R)/Z` over simple rings with `1/2`, or a group-theoretic recognition of root subgroups of `S_X`.
Golubchik, *Isomorphisms of GL_n(R), n ≥ 4* (Contemp. Math. 131, 1992) treats `n ≥ 4` and was not obtained (AMS
paywall). No `PE_3` theorem was read.

## 4. Steps (b) and (c): plan, with what is verified

**(b1) The K_0 group.** ABC (arXiv:0903.0056v2, text on MSI):
- Section 1 recalls: "for unital A, the K-theory of the twisted Laurent polynomials was computed in [18] and [34]".
  The references are Farrell–Hsiang and Yao.
- Lemma 7.2, verbatim: "Let R be a regular supercoherent ring and let α be an automorphism of R. Extend α to an
  automorphism of R[t_1, t_1^{-1}, ..., t_p, t_p^{-1}] by α(t_i) = t_i. Then NK_n(R[t_1, t_1^{-1}, ..., t_p,
  t_p^{-1}]; α) = 0 for every p ≥ 0 and every n ∈ Z."

`A = LC(X,F_q)` is regular supercoherent. It is a directed union of finite products of `F_q`, with flat connecting
maps, since every module over a von Neumann regular ring is flat. Polynomial rings over `A` are directed unions of
finite products of polynomial rings over `F_q`, which are Noetherian regular. Coherence and finite projective
dimension of finitely presented modules then pass to the colimit, because finitely presented modules descend to a
finite stage and base change is exact.

With `NK = 0`, the twisted Laurent sequence reads
`K_0(A) →(1 − T_*) K_0(A) → K_0(R_X) → K_{−1}(A) = 0`. That gives `K_0(R_X) ≅ C(X,Z)/(1 − T_*)C(X,Z) = K^0(X,T)`,
with `[1] ↦ [χ_X]`. The verbatim statement of the twisted Laurent sequence from [18] or [34] is still owed, so this
step is not yet landed as established.

**(b2) Order.** Every nonzero idempotent `e ∈ M_k(R_X)` has `rk_μ(e) > 0` for every full ergodic invariant `μ`: by
`cantor-z-crossed-product-embeds-in-continuous-factor` (Ara–Claramunt), `rk_μ` is faithful with `rk_μ(χ_U) = μ(U)`.
Minimal systems have only full-support invariant measures.

Every state of `K^0(X,T)` is a combination of ergodic `μ`. So `K_0^+(R_X) ⊆ {0} ∪ {a : μ(a) > 0 for all μ}`, and
classes of nonnegative functions are sums of idempotent classes `[χ_U]`. Equality of the two cones needs the
Herman–Putnam–Skau description of `K^0(X,T)^+`: a strictly positive class has a nonnegative representative. That is
not yet quoted.

**(c) Unit scaling.** A ring isomorphism `M_3(R_X) ≅ M_3(R_Y)` preserves `(K_0, K_0^+, 3[1])`. Giordano–Putnam–Skau
classify `(K^0, K^0^+, [1])` up to strong orbit equivalence. With the unit `3[1]`, the conclusion is strong orbit
equivalence of the height-3 Kakutani towers over `X` and `Y`: `(X × {0,1,2}, T')` climbs the floors and applies `T`
on the top floor, and has `K^0` with unit `3[1]`. It is not strong orbit equivalence of `X` and `Y` themselves.

For the anti-isomorphism case, `R_X^op ≅ R_(X,T^{-1})`, and `K^0(X,T^{-1}) = K^0(X,T)` with the same cone.

**Corrected statement to aim at, for q odd:** `GL_3(R_X) ≅ GL_3(R_Y)` ⇒ the height-3 towers over `X` and `Y` are
strongly orbit equivalent. Proving this needs (b1) verbatim, the HPS cone, and GPS verbatim. The projective version
for `S_X` additionally needs F-PE closed.
