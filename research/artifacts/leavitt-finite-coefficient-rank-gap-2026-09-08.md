# A rank gap for literal finite coefficient realizations

2026-09-08. Handwritten block and nullity proofs, independently audited.
Sections 5--7 transfer the obstruction to arbitrary complex unitary
and projective representations of the full finite coefficient groups. No numerical
experiment is used. An unrestricted complex unitary-model gap is not
proved.

A subsequent [explicit full-coefficient theorem](leavitt-all-finite-coefficient-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md)
gives scalar gap `sqrt(2)/192` for projective representations of
`EL_3(A)` over every nonzero finite unital characteristic-two ring,
including nonzero radicals, and more generally over semilocal rings
of characteristic two. It uses elementary coefficient extraction in
place of the covering argument below. The full-group extension
requirement remains.

For marked subgroups with invertible ts, the later
[internal-ring proof](leavitt-unit-product-marked-subgroup-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md)
removes that requirement and gives gap sqrt(2)/97. It still assumes
literal coefficients in a finite ring.

## 1. Retain the coefficient defect

Let `K` have characteristic two, let `s,t,v in M_d(K)`, and put
`Z=ts`. Take the coordinate cycle `c` with `e_1 -> e_2 -> e_3 -> e_1`
and `b_1=x_12(s)`, `b_2=x_12(t)`, `b_3=x_12(v)`.
All three `b_i` commute and square to the identity, whether or not
their coefficients are independent. Use the packet's words

```text
gamma=Ad(c),                 d_word=[b_2,gamma(b_1)],
h=gamma^2(d_word),           a=[d_word,h],
e=gamma(d_word),             j=a e a^(-1),
u=[b_2,gamma(b_3)],          w=[gamma^2(b_1),a],
r_*=j c j c,                R_v=[b_3,gamma(b_3)]d_word^(-1),
R_0=[u,w].
```

The distinct-index commutator formula gives

```text
d_word=x_13(Z), h=x_32(Z), a=x_12(Z^2), e=x_21(Z),
u=x_13(tv), w=x_32(sZ^2),
R_v=x_13(v^2+Z), R_0=x_12(tvsZ^2).                 (RG1)
```

No use of `ts=1` has been made. Since `a^(-1)=a`, direct block
multiplication gives

```text
j = [ I+Z^3  Z^5    0 ]       jc = [ Z^5    0  I+Z^3 ]
    [ Z      I+Z^3  0 ]            [ I+Z^3  0  Z     ]
    [ 0      0      I ]            [ 0      I  0     ].
```

In particular

```text
(r_*-I)_(3,3)=((jc)^2-I)_(3,3)=Z-I.                (RG2)
```

Every entry in the multiplication is a power of the same `Z`, so
no commutativity of the separate coefficients is assumed.

## 2. The quantitative rank obstruction

Set

```text
r=rank(Z-I), q=rank(v^2+Z), p=rank(tvsZ^2).
```

First, `rank Z>=d-r`, and `rank t>=rank(ts)=rank Z`, so
`nullity t<=r`. Also, in characteristic two,

```text
rank(v^2-I)<=rank(v^2+Z)+rank(Z-I)=q+r,
rank v>=rank(v^2)>=d-q-r.                           (RG3)
```

On the subspace `E=ker(Z-I)`, the map `sZ^2` is injective.
Indeed, for `x in E`, `Z^2x=x`. If `sZ^2x=0`, then `sx=0`
and hence `x=Zx=tsx=0`. Thus

```text
rank(sZ^2)>=dim E=d-r,
nullity(sZ^2)<=r.                                  (RG4)
```

For square matrices of size `d`, the rank inequality
`rank(ABC)>=rank B-nullity A-nullity C` follows by applying
`rank(XY)>=rank X+rank Y-d` twice. Apply it with
`A=t`, `B=v`, `C=sZ^2`. Equations (RG3)--(RG4) give

```text
p>=d-q-3r,                 p+q+3r>=d.              (RG5)
```

Taking a block cannot increase rank, so (RG2) gives
`r<=rank(r_*-I)`. Equation (RG1) identifies the full ranks
of `R_v-I` and `R_0-I` with `q` and `p`. Consequently

```text
rank(R_0-I)+rank(R_v-I)+3 rank(r_*-I)>=d.
```

Divide by the ambient dimension `3d`. If the maximum of the
three normalized ranks is `M`, then `5M>=1/3`, proving
`M>=1/15`. The fourth packet word `R_sum` is unnecessary for
this coefficient-class obstruction.

## 3. Exact directly finite coefficient rings

The block identities also hold for `s,t,v` in an arbitrary unital
ring of characteristic two. Recall that direct finiteness means
`xy=1` implies `yx=1`. If all three words are identity, (RG2)
gives `ts=1`; direct finiteness then gives `st=1`.
Equation (RG1) gives `v^2=1` and `tvs=0`. But

```text
s(tvs)t=(st)v(st)=v,
```

so `v=0`, contradicting `v^2=1` in any nonzero ring.
This covers finite coefficient rings as well: on a finite ring,
`ts=1` makes left multiplication by `s` injective and hence
surjective, giving a right inverse for `s`, which must equal `t`.

## 4. What this does and does not transfer

Sections 1--3 apply to literal elementary matrices over coefficients
with one common finite-dimensional vector space, and the exact
claim applies to directly finite coefficient rings. They do not
identify arbitrary abstract word values with such coefficients.
Section 5 supplies a rank-to-HS comparison when the complex
representation extends to the full finite special linear group;
that additional hypothesis is not automatic for a representation
of the subgroup generated by the four marked elements.

The missing issue can also be expressed on a finite elementary
abelian receiver `A`: if globally defined endomorphisms `S,T,V`
satisfy `TS=id`, `V^2=id`, and `TVS=0` on all of `A`, then
`S,T,V` are bijective and `TVS` cannot be zero unless `A=0`.
One must first construct this common additive receiver and prove
the identities on all of it. The finite group-word packet alone
does not provide that construction or propagation argument.

The unrestricted `24k` normalized-HS gap and the opposite
vanishing branch both remain open.

## 5. A uniform scalar-HS gap in full finite-linear-group representations

There is an absolute integer `M` with the following property. Let
`q` be any power of two, `s,t,v in M_d(F_q)`, and `n=3d`. Evaluate
the three native words `r_*,R_v,R_0` in `SL_n(F_q)` using the
coordinate cycle and elementary matrices of Section 1. For every
exact unitary representation

```text
rho:SL_n(F_q) -> U(N),
eta=max_(r in {r_*,R_v,R_0}) min_(|zeta|=1)
                                      ||rho(r)-zeta I||_(2,N),
```

one has

```text
||rho(c)-I||_(2,N) <= 2M eta.                        (FH1)
```

Consequently the frozen condition `tr_N(rho(c))=0` forces

```text
eta >= sqrt(2)/(2M).                                (FH2)
```

The bound is independent of `q`, the coefficient dimension `d`,
the complex representation dimension `N`, and its multiplicities.
The representation need not be irreducible or monomial. This
therefore rules out replacing the regular finite-field models by
arbitrary complex representations of the full finite linear group
as a way of making these three scalar relator defects vanish.

### Projective support strengthens the coefficient calculation

For an invertible `n`-by-`n` matrix `g`, define

```text
nu(g)=min_(lambda in algebraic_closure(F_q), lambda!=0)
                                             rank(g-lambda I).
```

All ranks are unchanged by extension of the coefficient field.
Keep `Z=ts`, and put

```text
p=rank(tvsZ^2),        q_0=rank(v^2+Z),
r_lambda=rank(r_*-lambda I).
```

The `(3,3)` block calculation in `(RG2)` gives
`rank(Z-lambda I)<=r_lambda`. Hence

```text
rank Z >= d-r_lambda,
nullity t <= r_lambda,
rank v >= rank(v^2) >= d-q_0-r_lambda.
```

On `E_lambda=ker(Z-lambda I)`, the map `sZ^2` is injective:
if `sZ^2 x=lambda^2 sx=0`, then `sx=0`, so
`lambda x=Zx=tsx=0` and `x=0`. Thus
`nullity(sZ^2)<=r_lambda`. The same product-rank inequality as
before now gives

```text
p+q_0+3r_lambda >= d.
```

Minimize over nonzero `lambda`. Both `R_v` and `R_0` are
unipotent roots with square-zero difference from the identity.
For such a matrix every shift by `lambda!=1` is invertible,
so its projective support equals its rank difference from `I`.
Therefore

```text
nu(R_0)+nu(R_v)+3nu(r_*) >= d.                       (FH3)
```

At least one of these three words, call it `g`, consequently has
`nu(g)>=d/5=n/15`. In particular its image in `PSL_n(F_q)` is
nonidentity. Using ordinary rank distance from `I` alone would
not justify this last conclusion; `(FH3)` excludes proximity to
every scalar over the algebraic closure.

### The conjugacy-class size has a dimension-independent exponent

Put `r=nu(g)`. Over the algebraic closure, write the column lengths
of the Jordan partition for eigenvalue `lambda` as `m_(lambda,j)`.
The dimension of the matrix centralizer is

```text
sum_(lambda,j) m_(lambda,j)^2 <= (n-r)n,
```

because every column length is at most the largest eigenspace
dimension `n-r`, and the sum of all column lengths is `n`.
The formula follows by summing `min(a,b)` for homomorphisms
between Jordan blocks of sizes `a,b`. The centralizer equations
are linear over `F_q`, so their solution-space dimension is
unchanged by extending the field. In particular

```text
|C_(SL_n(F_q))(g)| <= q^(n(n-r)).
```

Also `|SL_n(F_q)|>=q^(n^2-1)/4`. For example, the product
formula for `GL_n(F_q)` has factor
`product_(j=1)^n(1-q^(-j))>1/4`: at `q=2`, the first two
factors have product `3/8`, and the remaining product is at
least `1-sum_(j>=3)2^(-j)=3/4`.

Let `G=PSL_n(F_q)` and let `C` be the conjugacy class of the
image of `g`. The preimage of its centralizer in `SL_n(F_q)`
is a union of at most `|Z(SL_n(F_q))|<=n` cosets of the
ordinary centralizer of `g`: each such coset corresponds to an
equality `hgh^(-1)=z g` with `z` central. It follows that

```text
|C| >= q^(nr-1)/(4n),       |G| <= q^(n^2).          (FH4)
```

Here is one deliberately loose uniform numerical bound:

```text
log|G|/log|C| <= 6300.                              (FH5)
```

For `n>=30`, use `r>=n/15` and
`1+log_2(4n)<=n^2/30` in `(FH4)`; the ratio is at most `30`.
For `3<=n<30` and `q>=4n`, `(FH4)` gives
`|C|>=q^(n-2)`, so the ratio is at most
`n^2/(n-2)<=3n<90`. In the remaining cases `q<4n<120<128`
and `n^2<900`; since the nonidentity class in the simple group
has size at least two, the ratio is less than `6300`.

### Bounded normal generation and removal of the central ambiguity

Use the finite-simple-group covering theorem: there is an absolute
constant `c_cov` such that, for a nontrivial conjugacy class `C`
in a nonabelian finite simple group `G`,

```text
C^k=G whenever k>=c_cov log|G|/log|C|.
```

This is [Liebeck--Shalev, Theorem 1.1](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/11021).
Its exact statement is also recorded in the introduction of
[Maroti--Pyber](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.14270v2#S1), whose
Theorem 1.2 proves the stronger version for products of different
normal subsets. Take `M=ceil(6300 c_cov)`, increasing it to one
if necessary. Then `(FH5)` gives `C^M=G`.

Let `a` and `b` be the constant block-coordinate transpositions
`(23)` and `(12)`, respectively. Characteristic two puts both
in `SL_n(F_q)`, and with the stated commutator convention

```text
[a,b]=c.
```

Lift a length-`M` conjugacy-class expression for the image of `a`
from `PSL_n(F_q)` to obtain

```text
a=z product_(j=1)^M h_j g h_j^(-1),
z in Z(SL_n(F_q)),       h_j in SL_n(F_q).
```

Choose a scalar `zeta` minimizing the defect of `rho(g)`.
Unitary telescoping gives

```text
||rho(a)-zeta^M rho(z)||_2 <= M eta.
```

The matrix `rho(z)` need not be scalar when `rho` is reducible.
It does, however, commute with `rho(b)`. Taking the commutator
therefore removes this central ambiguity and yields

```text
||rho(c)-I||_2
 =||rho(a)rho(b)-rho(b)rho(a)||_2
 <=2||rho(a)-zeta^M rho(z)||_2
 <=2M eta.
```

This proves `(FH1)` and `(FH2)`.

### Exact scope of the new obstruction

The conjugators `h_j` above belong to the full special linear
group, and can lie outside the subgroup generated by the four
marked elements. A unitary representation of that marked subgroup
does not automatically extend to the full group. In particular,
the proof cannot be applied to arbitrary frozen `24k` tuples.
Nor does it construct coefficient matrices from such tuples.
Its new conclusion is a uniform scalar-HS obstruction for the
specified full finite-linear-group representation construction,
including nonmonomial complex representations. The unrestricted
hyperlinearity question is unchanged. This proof used handwritten
matrix arguments and the cited covering theorem; no code or
Lean was run.

## 6. Finite products of matrix coefficient algebras

The obstruction also covers arbitrary complex representations of the
full groups associated with finite semisimple coefficient algebras.
Explicitly, take

```text
A=product_(j=1)^J M_(d_j)(F_(q_j)),      q_j powers of two,
G_j=SL_(3d_j)(F_(q_j)),                 G=product_j G_j,
s,t,v in A.
```

Evaluate the marked cycle c, the three elementary generators, and
the three words `r_1=r_*`, `r_2=R_v`, `r_3=R_0` componentwise.
For every exact unitary representation `rho:G->U(N)`, put

```text
eta_l=min_(|zeta|=1)||rho(r_l)-zeta I||_2.
```

With the same absolute M as in Section 5,

```text
||rho(c)-I||_2 <= 2M(eta_1+eta_2+eta_3)
               <=6M max_l eta_l.                   (FP1)
```

In particular `tr(rho(c))=0` forces
`max_l eta_l>=sqrt(2)/(6M)`, independently of the number of
simple coefficient factors and of the representation dimension.
The representation may mix those factors through tensor products
and arbitrary direct sums; no decomposition of rho is assumed.

For a proof, assign each component j a label l(j) for which
`nu((r_(l(j)))_j)>=3d_j/15`, using `(FH3)`. Let a_j and b_j
be the two constant coordinate transpositions from Section 5.
For each label l, construct elements `h_(l,1),...,h_(l,M)` in G
so that

```text
W_l=product_(a=1)^M h_(l,a) r_l h_(l,a)^(-1)
```

has component `(W_l)_j=z_j a_j`, with z_j central in G_j,
whenever l(j)=l. Such conjugators exist by the covering theorem
in Section 5. Its conclusion holds for every length above the
threshold, so the same exact length M works in all components.
On the other components choose arbitrary conjugators, for example
the identity; no restriction on `(W_l)_j` is needed there.

Define b_l in G to have component b_j when l(j)=l and the
identity otherwise. Central factors disappear in the commutator,
giving

```text
[W_l,b_l]_j = c_j  if l(j)=l,
[W_l,b_l]_j = I    otherwise,
c=product_(l=1)^3 [W_l,b_l].                         (FP2)
```

Choose a minimizing scalar zeta_l for each eta_l. Because W_l
is a product of M conjugates of r_l, unitary telescoping gives

```text
||rho(W_l)-zeta_l^M I||_2 <= M eta_l,
||rho([W_l,b_l])-I||_2 <=2M eta_l.
```

Apply the triangle inequality to (FP2) to prove (FP1). This also
explains why unrelated choices of scalar phase for the three
relators cause no difficulty.

There is a slightly stronger bound for direct sums of separate
single-factor constructions. If their physical summand weights
are w_j, apply `(FH1)` on each summand, square, and sum to get

```text
||rho(c)-I||_2^2 <=4M^2 sum_(l=1)^3 eta_l^2.         (FP3)
```

Here the weighted sum of the individually minimized squared scalar
defects is at most the globally minimized squared scalar defect,
since using one phase for all summands is a more restrictive
minimization. Thus a
trace-zero direct sum satisfies
`max_l eta_l>=sqrt(2)/(2 sqrt(3) M)`.

As in Section 5, the exact full-group representation is essential.
The constructed conjugators and the elements b_l need not belong
to the marked subgroup. This proof neither supplies such an
extension for arbitrary unitary tuples nor, by itself, handles
coefficient rings with a nonzero nilpotent radical. The subsequent
[five-term extraction proof](leavitt-all-finite-coefficient-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md)
does handle the radical with an explicit constant. Both results still
require full-group representations and do not exclude every possible
matrix model of H.

## 7. Projective full-group representations obey the same scalar gap

For purposes of the scalar-relator criterion, linear extension to the
full finite group can be weakened to projective extension. Write

```text
d_sc(U,V)=min_(|zeta|=1)||U-zeta V||_2.
```

This defines a bi-invariant metric on `PU(N)=U(N)/(T I)`.
Let `rho:G->PU(N)` be a homomorphism for either the single group
of Section 5 or the product group of Section 6. Choose arbitrary
unitary representatives when writing traces or distances. Then

```text
d_sc(rho(c),I) <=2M max_l d_sc(rho(r_l),I)       (single factor),
d_sc(rho(c),I) <=6M max_l d_sc(rho(r_l),I)       (finite product).
                                                               (PR1)
```

All quantities in (PR1) are independent of those representatives.
Evaluating a word in representatives of the marked generators changes
its representative of rho(word) only by a scalar phase. Thus the
right side is exactly the scalar defect of the literal native words.

The proofs above work in this quotient metric. In the single-factor
case the central correction z need not have a scalar representative,
and its representatives need not commute literally with those of b.
Its image does commute with rho(b) in PU(N), which is enough:
in any group with a bi-invariant metric,

```text
d([x,y],[z,y])<=2d(x,z).
```

Apply this with the length-M conjugacy-class expression for a.
The reference commutator is the identity in PU(N), and
`[rho(a),rho(b)]=rho(c)`. For products, use the same identities
`c=product_l[W_l,b_l]` from `(FP2)` and the triangle inequality
in PU(N). This proves (PR1).

Finally, a unitary C with `tr(C)=0` has

```text
d_sc(C,I)^2=2-2|tr(C)|=2.
```

Hence the scalar gaps `(FH2)` and `(FP1)`'s consequence remain
unchanged for projective representations. In particular, an exact
frozen-factor tuple cannot have vanishing scalar defects for these
three words if its projective marked representation extends to the
specified full finite linear group or full product. No such
projective extension is provided for arbitrary frozen tuples.
