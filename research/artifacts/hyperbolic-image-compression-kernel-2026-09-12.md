# Hyperbolic images kill the Kazhdan compression defect

Date: 2026-09-12. This result addresses a concrete proposed construction of a
non-MF hyperbolic group: take a known non-MF compression source and pass to a
hyperbolic quotient while protecting its marked obstruction. The conclusion
is stronger than the existing obstruction to embedding the whole source.
Every homomorphism into a word-hyperbolic group kills the entire defect.

The proof also identifies why a Rips extension or a relative filling cannot
carry this particular witness through unchanged. It does not settle the
existence of a non-residually-finite or non-MF hyperbolic group.

## 1. The image theorem

Let `E` be a group, let `i:Gamma -> E` be a homomorphism from a group with
property (T), and suppose `t,c in E` satisfy

```text
t i(Gamma) t^-1 <= i(Gamma),
c in C_E(i(Gamma)).
```

Put

```text
D = normal_closure_E { [t c t^-1, i(gamma)] : gamma in Gamma }.
```

Then every homomorphism `f:E -> H` to a word-hyperbolic group has
`D <= ker(f)`.

**Proof.** Write `A=f(i(Gamma))`, `s=f(t)`, and `b=f(c)`. Property (T)
passes to quotients, so `A` is Kazhdan. We have

```text
s A s^-1 <= A,
b in C_H(A).
```

There are two cases.

If `A` is finite, its conjugate has the same cardinality. The inclusion is
therefore equality. For `a in A`, write `a=s a' s^-1` with `a' in A`; then
`s b s^-1` commutes with `a`, because `b` commutes with `a'`.

If `A` is infinite, it cannot be virtually cyclic. Otherwise a subgroup of
finite index would be infinite cyclic; property (T) passes to subgroups of
finite index, whereas an infinite cyclic group does not have property (T).
Thus `A` is a non-elementary subgroup of the hyperbolic group `H`.
Its centralizer `C_H(A)` is finite. Centralizers reverse inclusions, giving

```text
C_H(A) <= C_H(s A s^-1) = s C_H(A) s^-1.
```

Both sides have the same finite cardinality, so equality holds. Hence
`s b s^-1` belongs to `C_H(A)` in this case as well.

In both cases all displayed generators of `D` map to the identity; therefore
their normal closure maps to the identity. This proves the theorem.

The hyperbolic input is precisely
`hyperbolic-nonelementary-centralizer-finite`; the infinite-image case is
the existing `hyperbolic-compression-centralizer-rigidity` argument applied
after taking the image. The finite-image case closes the possibility that
a quotient could escape that argument by collapsing the Kazhdan subgroup.
The map `i` need not be injective, and `f` need not be surjective.

## 2. Consequences for the actual quotient constructions

**Protecting one defect element is impossible.** If `d in D` is nontrivial,
there is no homomorphism of `E` to a word-hyperbolic group that is injective
on `{1,d}`. Thus adjusting small-cancellation parameters cannot both
preserve `d` and produce a word-hyperbolic quotient of this source.

**The conclusion survives any preliminary quotient.** Let `q:E -> P` be
an epimorphism. For every `f:P -> H` with `H` word-hyperbolic,
`f(q(D))=1`. If `q(D)=P`, then every homomorphism from `P` to a
word-hyperbolic group is trivial. In particular, a nontrivial such `P`
cannot itself be word-hyperbolic and cannot embed in a word-hyperbolic
group. This applies to a defect-saturation construction whenever its
normal-generation statement identifies the whole output with `q(D)`.

**A relative-hyperbolic intermediate with residually finite peripherals
does not fix this source.** Suppose `E` is finitely generated and
hyperbolic relative to finitely many residually finite subgroups
`P_1,...,P_r`, and suppose `d in D` is nontrivial. Osin filling provides a
finite forbidden set in the peripherals and permits injectivity on the
prescribed finite set `{1,d}`. Residual finiteness lets us choose, in each
peripheral, a normal subgroup of finite index avoiding its finite forbidden
set: intersect finitely many kernels of finite separating homomorphisms.
All peripheral quotients are then finite. The filled quotient is
word-hyperbolic and preserves `d`, contradicting Section 1. Thus such an
`E`, with `D != 1`, admits no relative-hyperbolic structure of this form.

This deduction uses only the filling theorem already imported as
`relatively-hyperbolic-dehn-filling`. Its source is D. Osin,
[*Peripheral fillings of relatively hyperbolic groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0510195).
It is a restriction on this compression source, not a general restriction
on relatively hyperbolic non-MF groups.

## 3. A separate finite-lamp image calculation

Let `W=L semidirect G`, where `L` is locally finite. Let `f:W -> H` be a
homomorphism to a word-hyperbolic group, and put `F=f(L)`. Then `F` is
finite. Indeed, finite subgroups of a fixed word-hyperbolic group have a
uniform bound on their orders. If a locally finite subgroup had more than
that many elements, the subgroup generated by those elements would be a
finite subgroup exceeding the bound.

Conjugation by `f(G)` gives a homomorphism
`alpha:G -> Aut(F)`. There is a finite group `F semidirect alpha(G)` and
a homomorphism

```text
v:W -> F semidirect alpha(G),
v(l,g) = (f(l), alpha(g)).
```

The semidirect-product multiplication verifies this formula directly; no
injectivity of `f` on `G` or disjointness of `f(G)` and `F` is needed.
The restrictions `v|_L` and `f|_L` have exactly the same kernel.
Consequently

```text
L intersect finite_residual(W) <= ker(f).
```

Thus a finite-residual lamp element in a split locally finite wreath source
cannot survive in any hyperbolic image, independently of property (T).
This calculation must not be applied to an arbitrary nonsplit extension:
the displayed finite homomorphism uses the given semidirect-product
decomposition. In particular it does not assert that every finite normal
subgroup of every hyperbolic group is separable.

## 4. Rips and the fixed quaternionic lattice

The Rips construction reverses the direction of the needed map. A
surjection `G -> E` with hyperbolic `G` and non-MF `E` need not make `G`
non-MF. A marked element of `E` has lifts in `G`, but the defining
compression relations in `E` need not hold on those lifts.

The version of Belegradek--Osin controls a kernel by starting with a
**non-elementary hyperbolic** group `K` and making the Rips kernel a
quotient of `K`. It cannot accept a defect-saturated group `P` from
Section 2 as a replacement for `K`: any nontrivial quotient of `P`
embedding as a subgroup of a hyperbolic total group would contradict that
section. The source is
[*Rips construction and Kazhdan property (T)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605553),
Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2. Its Section 3 explicitly retains finite
images when using quaternionic-lattice quotients to restrict linear
representations; it supplies no finite-quotient-free kernel from a known
residually finite hyperbolic input.

There is a more precise algebraic restriction on trying to make the base
group eliminate the kernel's finite simple images.

**Finite simple quotient theorem.** Suppose
`1 -> N -> G -> Q -> 1` is exact, `N` is finitely generated, and `Q` has
no nontrivial finite quotient. Every centerless finite quotient of `N`
extends to a quotient of `G`. In particular, `G` and `N` have exactly the
same nonabelian finite simple quotients.

**Proof.** Let `p:N -> K` be an epimorphism with `K` finite and centerless,
and let `L=ker(p)`. A finitely generated group has finitely many subgroups
of each fixed finite index: each such subgroup is a point stabilizer in a
homomorphism to a fixed finite symmetric group, and there are only finitely
many homomorphisms from a finitely generated group to that symmetric
group. Hence the conjugacy orbit of `L` under `G` is finite. Since `L` is
normal in `N`, the action on this orbit factors through `Q`. It is trivial
because `Q` has no nontrivial finite quotient. Thus `L` is normal in `G`.

Conjugation gives `alpha:G -> Aut(K)`. Its restriction to `N` is the
surjection onto `Inn(K)` induced by `p`. Therefore `alpha(G)` contains
`Inn(K)`, and the finite quotient `alpha(G)/Inn(K)` is a quotient of `Q`.
It is trivial. Thus `alpha(G)=Inn(K)`. Since `K` is centerless,
`K -> Inn(K)` is an isomorphism. Composing its inverse with `alpha`
gives an epimorphism `G -> K` restricting exactly to `p` on `N`.

Conversely, for any epimorphism `G -> F` with `F` finite, the quotient
`F/image(N)` is a finite quotient of `Q`, so `image(N)=F`. In particular
every nonabelian finite simple quotient of `G` is one of `N`. This proves
both assertions. No classification of finite simple groups is used.

**Perfect-kernel consequence.** If `N` is also perfect, then

```text
G has a nontrivial finite quotient
    iff N has a nontrivial finite quotient.
```

The forward implication is restriction. For the reverse, a nontrivial
finite quotient of a perfect group is perfect; it has a nonabelian finite
simple quotient, which extends by the theorem. Thus making all finite
images of a perfect Rips kernel inside finite quotients of `G` cyclic,
solvable, or trivial is not a weaker construction problem than eliminating
the finite quotients of the kernel itself. Every surviving finite simple
image of that kernel is automatically compatible with the total group.

This result leaves a possible role for central finite images when the
kernel is not perfect: the centerless hypothesis cannot be suppressed.
It also does not claim that arbitrary finite homomorphisms of the kernel
extend, only the stated centerless epimorphisms. These are enough for the
perfect-kernel conclusion.

For the fixed torsion-free cocompact lattice `Gamma < Sp(2,1)`, a finite
relator construction of a quotient `Gamma/M` without finite quotients
requires a normal subgroup `M` whose closure in the full profinite
completion is the whole completion. Checking all congruence quotients
only checks its image in the congruence completion. That is an actual
remaining condition on the construction, not a consequence of choosing
relators long enough for hyperbolicity. No relators satisfying both
requirements have been obtained in this lane.

## 5. What a successful transfer must change

The proved image theorem rules out preserving a marked compression defect
through any homomorphism to a hyperbolic group. A successful use of the
known non-MF source would therefore have to produce a new matrix
obstruction in a hyperbolic extension, with a proof that does not assume
the lifted compression relations hold. Alternatively, one must find an
independent hyperbolic source with a nontrivial MF-residual element. Neither
step follows from Rips surjectivity, Olshanskii's common-quotient theorem,
or the currently supplied property-(T) hypothesis.
