## 1. What is proved

For `T = G_V × H`, with `H` ample Hausdorff on a compact totally disconnected
`Q`: `F(T) = S(T) = A(T)`, and the group is perfect. The complete argument is in
the route body, Steps 1–6. This artifact records:

- a worked example showing the mechanism is not vacuous;
- the relation that the abstract Step 5 unwinds to;
- the independent finite-model check;
- the dependency audit;
- where the lemma enters Problem 1.20.

## 2. Worked example: the shift on half the space is a product of transpositions

Take `H = Z ⋉ Σ` (the full shift, unrestricted) and `y ∈ F(T)` acting by `σ` on
`[0] × Σ` and by the identity on `[1] × Σ`. Inside `F(Z ⋉ Σ)` the shift has
index `1` and is not a product of transpositions. In `F(T)`, however, it is.

- **Normal form of `y`.** Pieces `B_{0→0} × σ` and `B_{1→1} × Σ`. So
  `ρ = ρ' = r`, the `V`-element `00 ↦ 00`, `01 ↦ 10`, `10 ↦ 01`, `11 ↦ 11`
  (prefix replacements), times `id_Σ`.
- **Rotation identity.** `r y r^{-1}` acts by `σ` on `([00] ⊔ [10]) × Σ` and
  trivially elsewhere. This is `y^(0) y^(1)`.
- **Copies.** Write `s = id × σ ∈ F(T)`, so that `y = s^(0)`. (Referee fix:
  the copies below are copies of `s`, not of `y`; in the route's notation
  `y^(00)` would be `σ` on `[000] × Σ`.)
  - With `g_0 = (0w ↦ 00w, 10w ↦ 01w, 11w ↦ 1w)`: `g_0 y g_0^{-1} = s^(00)` is
    `σ` on `[00] × Σ`.
  - With `g_1 = (0w ↦ 10w, 10w ↦ 0w, 11w ↦ 11w)`: `g_1 y g_1^{-1} = s^(10)` is
    `σ` on `[10] × Σ`.

  So `y^(0) y^(1) = s^(00) s^(10)`, and the relation is

  `r y r^{-1} = (g_0 y g_0^{-1}) (g_1 y g_1^{-1})`.
- **Unwinding.** Let `N` be the normal closure of `r`, `g_0` and `g_1` in
  `F(T)`. Modulo `N` the relation reads `x = x·x` for the class `x` of `y`, so
  `y ∈ N`. Explicitly, writing `c_g = g y g^{-1} y^{-1} ∈ N`, the relation is
  `c_r y = c_{g_0} y c_{g_1} y`, so `y^{-1} c_{g_0}^{-1} c_r y = c_{g_1} y` and
  `y = c_{g_1}^{-1} · y^{-1}(c_{g_0}^{-1} c_r) y`, a product of an element of `N`
  and a conjugate of one. Every element of `V × id` is a
  product of transpositions of `T` (Step 2), so `y ∈ S(T)`.
- **Check of the relation by hand.**
  - On `[10] × Σ`: `r^{-1}` goes to `[01] × Σ`, `y` acts by `σ` there, and `r`
    returns, so `σ`.
  - On `[00] × Σ`: `r` fixes it and `y = σ`.
  - On `[01] × Σ`: `r^{-1}` goes to `[10] × Σ`, where `y` is the identity.
  - On `[11] × Σ`: identity.

This is the group-level shadow of `[C] = [C_0] + [C_1] = 2[C]` in `H_0(G_V)`.
Through Künneth it kills `H_1(G_V × H)`, but no homology is used.

## 3. Finite-model check

`experiments/v-times-ample-full-group-swindle-2026-09-16/check_rotation_identity.py`
tests the rotation identity of Step 4.

- **Model.** `H` is the pair groupoid on a finite set `Q`. Random normal-form
  elements `h` are built from independent random complete prefix codes for sources
  and ranges, matched by a random bijection.
- **Test.** It builds `ρ` and `ρ'` exactly as in Step 4 and compares
  `ρ' h ρ^{-1}` with `h^(0) h^(1)` on random points, using 40-letter words as
  stand-ins for infinite words.
- **Validity checks.** Each evaluation asserts that the sources of `h`, `ρ'` and
  `ρ` and the ranges of `ρ` partition the space at the sampled point.
- **Result** (`output.txt`): 300 elements, 200 points each, no failure. This is
  evidence for the bookkeeping only; the certificate is the proof.

## 4. Dependency audit

- **`V` is generated by transpositions of `G_V`.** This is the only external
  theorem. It comes from Higman's simplicity of `V` (1974), since the
  transpositions generate a nontrivial normal subgroup. Independently, it is Matui,
  arXiv:1210.5800, Lemma 6.10 with §6.7, imported and reviewed in
  `matui-sft-full-groups-generated-by-transpositions`. That node identifies
  `V_{2,1}` with the full group of the full-shift groupoid, which is `G_V`.
- **Definitions of multisections, `S` and `A_3`.** Nekrashevych,
  arXiv:1511.08241, Definitions 3.1–3.2, as imported in
  `nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups`. No theorem of that paper
  is used. In particular the claim that `S/A` is a quotient of `H_0(G, Z/2)` is not
  used; Step 6 proves `S ⊆ A` directly by halving.
- **Hausdorffness of `H`.** It is used only so that products of basic bisections
  form a basis of compact open bisections, which the normal form of Step 1 uses.
- **Effectiveness.** Not used in the lemma, and not used in the kernel route
  either. There `ρ_Y` restricts bisections (Step 2 of
  `v-times-shift-alternating-group-restrictions-are-simple-proof`), so
  `ρ_Y(h) = id` already means that `h` consists of unit germs over `C × Y`.

## 5. Where this enters Problem 1.20

`v-times-shift-restriction-kernels-are-perfect-proof` combines three facts.

1. **Localization.** A kernel element is the identity on `C × U` for a clopen
   `U ⊇ Y`.
2. **This lemma**, for `H = (Z ⋉ Σ)|_{Σ∖U}`. The element lies in
   `A(G|_{C×(Σ∖U)})`.
3. **`halvable-groupoid-alternating-full-groups-are-perfect`** (or Step 6 here).
   That group is perfect and contained in the kernel.

So `K_Y` is a union of perfect subgroups of itself, hence perfect. With
`fp2-iff-quotient-of-fp-group-by-perfect-normal-subgroup`, the route
`uncountably-many-simple-fp2-via-v-times-shift-restrictions` now needs only
`v-times-shift-alternating-group-is-of-type-fp2`.

## 6. Novelty

No source stating `F(G_V × H) = S(G_V × H)` for non-minimal `H` was found in this
lane's checks. The problem-status check is recorded in the notes file. The
statement is close in spirit to Matui's index-map results and to Li's AH theorem
(arXiv:2209.08087 v2, 25 Nov 2024, which assumes minimality and comparison). It is
not claimed as new; treat priority as unverified.
