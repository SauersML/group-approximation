## Verbatim proof skeleton (pp. 407–414)

- **Lemma 1** (`A = K[X]/I`, Taitslin in the ring case). Given `φ : A → A` a `K`-module endomorphism that is an algebra homomorphism on `A[a_1,…,a_n]` with `φ(a_i) = b_i`, there is an overalgebra where the system
  `x a_i z y = b_i`, `a_i z = z β_i`, `x z β_i β_j = b_i x z β_j` (`i,j = 1,…,n`)
  is solvable. Proof: adjoin `x,y,z,β_1,…,β_n`, `I_1` generated by `I` together with `{x w z β y − φ[wβ(ā)]}` (all words `w ∈ L[X]∪{∅}`, `β ∈ L[β_i]∪{∅}`, `wβ≠∅`), `{a_i z − z β_i}`, `{x z β_i β_j − b_i x z β_j}`; show `I_1 ∩ K[X] = I` by induction on parenthesis-depth rank.
- **Remark 2**. The version actually used: `A ≤ B`, `A` a `K`-module direct summand `A ⊕ C = B`, `φ : A → B` a homomorphism; then some algebra containing `B` solves `Hom_n(ā,b̄,θ̄)`.
- **Lemma 2**. Input algebra `A` with generators `x,y,z` and relations `{x y^i z + x y^j z = x y^{f(i,j)} z : i≠j} ∪ {x y^i z = x y^j z : ⟨i,j⟩∈Y}`. Output `B ⊇ A`, `A` a `K`-module direct summand, generators `x,y,z,u,α,β,γ`, relations
  `{x y^i z = x u^{n_i(j)} ε_{ij} z} ∪ {x y^{f(i,j)} z = x u^{s(i,j)} y z} ∪ {x y^i z = x y^j z : ⟨i,j⟩∈Y} ∪ {α+β=γ}`,
  `ε_{ij} = α` (i+j even) / `β` (i+j odd). `n_i, s` are the injective functions from the line-intersection diagram: each natural `n` is `s(i,j)=s(j,i)` for a unique `i<j`, and `n = n_i(j−1) = n_i(...)`; the `n_i` are one-to-one.
- **Lemma 3** (Mal'tsev modification). Countable `A={a_1,a_2,…}` embeds in `B` with `a,b,c`, `a_i = a b^i c`. Proof: `A_1 = A × K[x]`, `φ(x^n)=a_n`, apply Lemma 1.
- **Theorem**. Enumerate `A` (each element ≥ twice); Lemma 3 → `a_i = ab^i c`; `Y = {⟨i,j⟩: ab^i c = ab^j c}`; recursive `f` with `ab^i c + ab^j c = ab^{f(i,j)} c`; form `S` (gens `x,y,z`, the two relation families); Lemma 2 → `S_1` (relations `Σ ∪ {α+β=γ}`, `Σ` word equalities); Lemma 1 solves system (2) in `S_2 ⊇ B×S_1`; **Murskii** on the semigroup `G` (gens `x',…,γ'`, relations `Σ`) → finite `G_1`; Lemma 1 solves (4) in `S_3 ⊇ S_2 × K[G_1]`; a recursive `g` with `ab^i c ab^j c = ab^{g(i,j)} c`; **Murskii** on `H` (gens `ρ,q,τ`, relations `ρ q^i τ = ρ q^{g(i,j)} τ`) → finite `H_1`; Lemma 1 solves (6) in `S_4 ⊇ S_3 × K[H_1]`; relations (7) tie in the `K`-generators. Σ* = union (1)–(7) is finite; the algebra it presents, on the elements `a_i = ab^i c`, "satisfies all equalities of `A` and only these."
- **Corollary**. Envelope 2-generated; for `K=Z` the equalities (7) are superfluous.

## Where decidability is preserved, and where it is not

Decidable field `K` (e.g. `F_p`, `Q`); input `A` with solvable word problem.

- **Coordinating data is computable — proved** (`belyaev-embedding-relations-are-recursive-for-decidable-input`). Enumeration `(a_i)`, `Y`, `f`, `g`, `n_i`, `s` all computable; so `S`, `G`, `H` have *recursive* relation sets.
- **Semigroup algebra inherits decidability — proved** (`semigroup-algebra-of-decidable-semigroup-is-decidable`). `K[G_1]`, `K[H_1]` decidable once `G_1, H_1` are.
- **Gap 1 — the two Murskii semigroups** (`belyaev-murskii-semigroups-have-solvable-word-problem`, OPEN). Murskii tolerates recursively enumerable relations; its replacement `birget-semigroup-embedding-preserves-word-problem` needs *solvable word problem* of `G` and `H`. Recursive relations ⇏ solvable semigroup word problem (Markov–Post). The relations of `G` and `H` have a rigid single-power-block shape (`x [y|u]^k [ε] z`, `ρ q^k τ`) with injective exponent functions, which makes a normal form plausible, but confluence/termination was not proved.
- **Gap 2 — Lemma 1 preserves the word problem** (`belyaev-lemma-one-preserves-word-problem`, OPEN). The overalgebra `A_1` has the infinite relation family `{x w z β y − φ[wβ(ā)]}`; Belyaev's rank induction reads like a confluence argument for the rewriting `x w z β y → φ[wβ(ā)]`, `a_i z → z β_i`, `x z β_i β_j → b_i x z β_j`, and `φ[wβ(ā)]` is computable, so a normal form is expected — but a full termination+confluence proof, including the three-rule overlaps, is not supplied.

## Net

`decidable-clapham-via-effective-belyaev` wires the reduction: (C) follows from Gap 1 + Gap 2 (plus the three proved ingredients and the elementary "finite products of decidable algebras are decidable"). So statement (C) — hence the algebra-Clapham half of algebra Boone–Higman — is reduced from "audit the whole construction" to two concrete, self-contained decidability lemmas. Neither is settled here.

The alternative route through contracted monoid algebras (attempt 2 on the (C) node) covers monomial algebras only; a general algebra is not a contracted monoid algebra, so it does not reach (C). This Belyaev route is the one that could.
