# Nonexistence side of Zaremsky Problem 1.10, part 2: finite presentation forces finite type over every finitely generated group

Lane `z1-10-simple-amen-block`, 2026-09-13. **Unreviewed.** Part 1 is
`research/artifacts/zp-fp-simple-amenable-obstructions-2026-09-13-part1.md`.

## 0. Outcome

**Theorem G.** Let `Λ` be an infinite finitely generated group and `X ⊆ A^Λ` a subshift on which `Λ` acts freely. If
some finitely presented subgroup of `[[Λ ~ X]]` contains the rule 3-cycles of one radius (Section 1), then `X` is a
subshift of finite type. In particular, if `D([[Λ ~ X]])` or the alternating full group `A([[Λ ~ X]])` is finitely
presented, then `X` is of finite type.

- This generalizes `zd-derived-full-group-fp-forces-finite-type` from free minimal `Z^d`-subshifts to free subshifts
  over any infinite finitely generated group, with no minimality.
- The earlier proof uses ChJN's shapes `(0, e, -e)` and a perpendicular direction, which needs commutativity. Here the
  shapes are arbitrary triples in a fixed ball, chosen at each step of the recursion to avoid a bounded set.
- The commutator identity of Chornyi–Juschenko–Nekrashevych is re-verified here with an explicit convention (Lemma A).
- Consequence for Problem 1.10: a derived or alternating full group of a free subshift can answer 1.10 only if the
  subshift is a nonempty strongly aperiodic SFT.

## 1. Setting and notation

- `Λ` is infinite, generated by a finite symmetric set `D ∌ 1`. `|μ|` is word length and `B(r) = {μ : |μ| <= r}`.
  Since `Λ` is infinite, `B(k) ⊊ B(k+1)` for all `k`, so `|B(5)| >= 6`.
- `Λ` acts on `A^Λ` by `(λ·x)(μ) = x(μλ)`. `X ⊆ A^Λ` is closed, invariant, and the action on `X` is free.
- A *colouring* is any `c : Λ -> A`. A pattern `π` with finite support `P ⊆ Λ` *occurs at the site* `w` of `c` if
  `c(pw) = π(p)` for all `p ∈ P`. `Occ_c(π)` is the set of such sites. For `x ∈ X`, `π` occurs at `w` iff `w·x`
  lies in the cylinder `[π]`.
- `c` is *`N`-legal* if at every site its `B(N)`-pattern occurs in some point of `X`. Legal colourings form a
  `Λ`-invariant SFT containing `X`.
- `[[Λ ~ X]]` is the group of homeomorphisms `g` of `X` with a continuous `κ_g : X -> Λ` and `g(x) = κ_g(x)·x`. By
  compactness `κ_g(x)` depends only on `x|B(R_g)`, through a *local rule* `k_g`. On the orbit `λ -> λ·x`, `g` acts
  by `λ -> k_g(pattern of x at λ) λ`. The *rule action* of `g` on a colouring `c` is given by the same formula.
- **Constants.**
  - `ρ = 5`, and `F` is the set of shapes `σ = (1, s, t)` with `s, t ∈ B(ρ)` and `1, s, t` pairwise distinct.
  - `R_1` is chosen so that `x|B(R_1) ≠ (u·x)|B(R_1)` for every `x ∈ X` and every `u ∈ B(2ρ + 2) \ {1}`. It exists
    because `u·x ≠ x` by freeness, by compactness, and because there are finitely many `u`.
  - `R >= R_1 + 1`.
- **Rule 3-cycles.** For a pattern `π` on `B(R)` that occurs in `X` and `σ = (1, s, t) ∈ F`, `τ(π; σ)` is the
  homeomorphism of `X` with `x -> s·x` on `[π]`, `x -> ts^(-1)·x` on `s·[π]`, `x -> t^(-1)·x` on `t·[π]`, and the
  identity elsewhere.
  - The three clopen sets are pairwise disjoint. For example, `s·y ∈ [π]` with `y ∈ [π]` would give
    `y|B(R_1) = (s·y)|B(R_1)`.
  - On orbits it sends sites `w -> sw -> tw -> w` for `w ∈ Occ(π)`.
  - It is a 3-cycle of disjoint clopen sets through bisections, so it lies in `A([[Λ ~ X]])`. It is the commutator
    of the involutions swapping `[π] ↔ s·[π]` and `[π] ↔ t·[π]` (`amenable-full-group-forces-invariant-measure`,
    item 1), so it lies in `D([[Λ ~ X]])`.
  - `S_R` is the finite set of all `τ(π; σ)`.
- For a colouring `c` and any pattern `q` whose support contains `B(R_1)`, `τ_c(q; σ)` is the permutation of `Λ`
  sending `w -> sw -> tw -> w` for `w ∈ Occ_c(q)`. It is well defined whenever the three blocks are disjoint
  (Lemma 0).

## 2. Two lemmas

**Lemma 0 (disjointness).** Let `c` be `(R_1 + 2ρ + 2)`-legal. Let `π, π'` be patterns whose supports contain `B(R_1)`
and which agree on `B(R_1)`. If `a ≠ b` lie in `B(ρ + 1)`, then `a·Occ_c(π) ∩ b·Occ_c(π') = ∅`.

*Proof.*
1. Suppose `aw = bw'` with `w ∈ Occ_c(π)` and `w' ∈ Occ_c(π')`. Then `w' = uw` with `u = b^(-1)a ∈ B(2ρ + 2) \ {1}`.
2. The `B(R_1 + 2ρ + 2)`-pattern of `c` at `w` occurs in X: some `x ∈ X` has `x(p) = c(pw)` for `|p| <= R_1 + 2ρ + 2`.
3. For `p ∈ B(R_1)`: `x(p) = c(pw) = π(p)`, and `(u·x)(p) = x(pu) = c(puw) = c(pw') = π'(p) = π(p)`.
4. So `x|B(R_1) = (u·x)|B(R_1)`, which contradicts the choice of `R_1`. ∎

**Lemma A (ChJN, arXiv:1602.04255; re-verified here).**
- **Convention.** Products compose as functions, `(xy)(p) = x(y(p))`, and `[x, y] = x^(-1) y^(-1) x y`.
- **Hypotheses.**
  - `a` permutes a set by cycling pairwise disjoint subsets `A_1 -> A_2 -> A_3 -> A_1` bijectively, and fixes
    everything else.
  - `b` does the same for pairwise disjoint `B_1 -> B_2 -> B_3 -> B_1`.
  - `A_i ∩ B_j = ∅` unless `i = j = 1`.
- **Conclusion.** `[[b^(-1), a^(-1)], [b, a]]` equals `a` on `C ∪ aC ∪ a^2 C`, where `C = A_1 ∩ B_1`, and is the
  identity elsewhere.

*Proof.*
1. **Local model.** For `p ∈ C` the five points `p, ap, a^2 p, bp, b^2 p` are distinct, the sets for different `p` are
   disjoint, and they are invariant under `a` and `b`. On one such set write `a = (p a_1 a_2)` and `b = (p b_1 b_2)`.
2. **Direct computation.**
   - `[b, a] = b^(-1) a^(-1) b a = (p b_2 a_2)`.
   - `[b^(-1), a^(-1)] = b a b^(-1) a^(-1) = (p b_1 a_1)`.
   - With `X = (p b_1 a_1)` and `Y = (p b_2 a_2)`, `[X, Y] = X^(-1) Y^(-1) X Y` sends `p -> a_1 -> a_2 -> p` and fixes
     `b_1` and `b_2`. That is `a` on this set.
3. **Remaining points.** A point of `A_1 \ B_1` has an `a`-orbit disjoint from every `B_j`, where `b` is trivial. So
   all four commutators fix it, and the same holds for `B_1 \ A_1`. Points in neither support are fixed. ∎

## 3. The words and Lemma B

**Enumeration.** Enumerate `D = {h_1, ..., h_m}`. Then `B(r+1) = B(r) ∪ ⋃_j B(r) h_j`, since a word of length `r + 1`
is a word of length `r` times one letter on the right.

**Patterns at translated supports.**
- Let `q` be a pattern on `B(r+1)` with `r >= R`.
- Put `Q_0 = q|B(r)` and `Q_j = q|(B(r) ∪ B(r)h_1 ∪ ... ∪ B(r)h_j)`, so `Q_m = q`.
- Put `π'_j = q|(B(r) h_j)` and `q_(h_j)(p) = q(p h_j)` for `p ∈ B(r)`.
- **Relations.** `Occ_c(Q_j) = Occ_c(Q_(j-1)) ∩ Occ_c(π'_j)` and `Occ_c(q_(h_j)) = h_j · Occ_c(π'_j)`. The second holds
  because `q_h` occurs at `v` iff `c(pv) = q(ph)` for `p ∈ B(r)`, i.e. iff `π'_j` occurs at `h^(-1) v`.
- **Cores.** `Q_j` and `π'_j` both contain `q|B(R_1)` at the anchor, since `B(R_1) ⊆ B(r) h_j` for `r >= R_1 + 1`.

**Words `W(q; σ)`,** for patterns `q` on `B(r)`, `r >= R`, on the auxiliary supports, and for `σ = (1, s, t) ∈ F`.
- **Base, `r = R`.** `W(q; σ) = τ(q; σ)` if `q` occurs in `X`, and the empty word otherwise.
- **Step.** `W(Q_0; σ) = W(q|B(r); σ)`. For `j = 1..m`, put `h = h_j`, `a = W(Q_(j-1); σ)`, `b = W(q_h; σ')`, and
  `W(Q_j; σ) = [[b^(-1), a^(-1)], [b, a]]`. Then `W(q; σ) = W(Q_m; σ)`. The shape `σ'` is a fixed choice:
  - **(i) `h ∉ {s, t}`.** `σ' = (1, h^(-1), y)` with `y ∈ B(ρ) \ {1, h^(-1), s h^(-1), t h^(-1)}`.
  - **(ii) `h = s`.** `σ' = (1, x, y)` with distinct `x, y ∈ B(ρ) \ {1, s^(-1), t s^(-1)}`.
  - **(iii) `h = t`.** `σ' = (1, x, y)` with distinct `x, y ∈ B(ρ) \ {1, t^(-1), s t^(-1)}`.
  - The choices exist because `|B(5)| >= 6`.

**Lemma B.** Let `N >= max(R + ρ, R_1 + 2ρ + 2)` and let `c` be `N`-legal. For every `r >= R`, every pattern `q` on
`B(r)`, every auxiliary `Q_j`, and every `σ ∈ F`, the word `W(q; σ)` evaluated through the rule action on `c` equals
`τ_c(q; σ)`, and likewise for `Q_j`.

*Proof.* Induction on `r`, then on `j`.
1. **Base.** The rule of `τ(π; σ)` reads `B(R + ρ)`-windows. These are legal, so it acts as `τ_c(π; σ)`. For an
   illegal `q` on `B(R)` there are no occurrences in `c`, since `N >= R`, so `τ_c(q; σ) = id`, matching the empty word.
2. **Step, blocks of `a`.** By induction `a` acts as `τ_c(Q'; σ)` with `Q' = Q_(j-1)`. Its blocks are
   `{1, s, t} · Occ(Q')`, pairwise disjoint by Lemma 0.
3. **Step, blocks of `b`.** By induction `b` acts as `τ_c(q_h; σ')`. Its blocks are `{1, σ'_2, σ'_3} · h · Occ(π'_j)`.
4. **Case (i).** The `b`-blocks are `{h, 1, yh} · Occ(π'_j)`.
   - Take `A_1 = Occ(Q')` and `B_1 = Occ(π'_j)`.
   - A coincidence `a' = b'` with `a' ∈ {1, s, t}` and `b' ∈ {h, 1, yh}` occurs only for `a' = b' = 1`, because
     `h ∉ {1, s, t}` and `yh ∉ {1, s, t}`.
   - Every other pair has `a' ≠ b'` in `B(ρ + 1)` and cores that agree, so Lemma 0 makes it disjoint.
   - Lemma A gives `a` on `C = Occ(Q_j)` and its images, which is `τ_c(Q_j; σ)`.
5. **Case (ii).** The `b`-blocks are `{s, xs, ys} · Occ(π'_j)`.
   - Take `A_1 = s·Occ(Q')` and `B_1 = s·Occ(π'_j)`. The only coincidence is `a' = b' = s`, since
     `xs, ys ∉ {1, s, t}`.
   - `C = s·Occ(Q_j)`. Since `a` sends `sw -> tw -> w -> sw`, `a` on `C ∪ aC ∪ a^2 C` is `τ_c(Q_j; σ)`.
6. **Case (iii).** Symmetric, with `t` in place of `s`. ∎

**Corollary C.** For every pattern `q` on `B(r)`, `r >= R`, that occurs in no point of `X`, and every `σ ∈ F`, the word
`W(q; σ)` is the identity of `[[Λ ~ X]]`.

*Proof.*
1. Take `c = x ∈ X`, which is legal at every radius. Each generator's rule action on `x` is its action on the orbit
   of `x`.
2. Lemma B gives `τ_x(q; σ) = id`, since `Occ_x(q) = ∅`.
3. So `W(q; σ)` fixes every orbit pointwise. ∎

## 4. Proof of Theorem G

**Setup.**
- Let `Γ ≤ [[Λ ~ X]]` be finitely presented with `S_R ⊆ Γ`.
- Choose a finite generating set `S ⊇ S_R` of `Γ`, a local rule for each generator, and the natural rules for `S_R`.
- Let `R_S` bound the rule radii, `D_S` bound the displacements `|κ_g|`, and let `Γ = <S | ℛ>` with relators of
  length `<= L`, `L >= 2`.
- Put `N = max(L D_S + R_S, R + ρ, R_1 + 2ρ + 2)` and `Y = {N-legal colourings}`. So `X ⊆ Y`.

**Steps.**
1. **Rule actions are permutations.** On `c ∈ Y`, the rule action of `g^(-1) g`, evaluated at `v`, reads `c` on
   `B(2D_S + R_S) v`. That window occurs in `X`. Along a point of `X` with that window, `g^(-1) g` acts trivially, so
   by freeness the displacement is `1`. Hence each `g_c` is a bijection, and `F(S) -> Sym(Λ)` is a homomorphism.
2. **Relators act trivially.** Evaluating a relator `ℓ` at `v` reads `c` only on `B(L D_S + R_S) v`. Take `x ∈ X`
   with the same window at site `1`.
   - The two trajectories agree step by step, so `ℓ_c(v) v^(-1) = κ_ℓ(x)`.
   - `ℓ(x) = x` in `Γ`, and by freeness `κ_ℓ(x) = 1`.
   - So the rule action factors through `Γ`.
3. **No illegal patterns.** Suppose `c ∈ Y \ X`.
   - Some pattern of `c` occurs in no point of `X`. Shifting by `λ`, with `(λ·c)(μ) = c(μλ)` and `Y` invariant, and
     enlarging the support, gives an illegal `q` on `B(M)`, `M >= N`, occurring at site `1` of a colouring in `Y`.
   - By Lemma B, `W(q; σ)` acts as `τ_c(q; σ)`, which moves `1` to `s`.
   - By Corollary C, `W(q; σ) = 1` in `Γ`, so by step 2 it acts trivially. Contradiction.
4. **Conclusion.** `X = Y` is the SFT whose forbidden patterns are the illegal patterns on `B(N)`. ∎

**Corollary G1.** If `D([[Λ ~ X]])` or `A([[Λ ~ X]])` is finitely presented, then `X` is of finite type. Both groups
contain `S_R` (Section 1).

## 5. Consequences and scope

- **Recovered cases.**
  - For `Λ = Z`, every nonempty `Z`-SFT has a periodic point, so no nonempty free `Z`-subshift is of finite type. So
    `D([[Z ~ X]])` is never finitely presented for any nonempty free `Z`-subshift, minimal or not. Matui's theorem is
    the minimal case.
  - `zd-derived-full-group-fp-forces-finite-type` is the free minimal `Z^d` case.
- **For Problem 1.10.** A finitely presented infinite simple amenable group that is the derived or alternating full
  group of a free subshift over `Λ` needs a nonempty strongly aperiodic SFT over `Λ`. By Jeandel's theorem (part 1,
  Section 4, abstract only), `Λ` then has decidable word problem if it is finitely presented.
- **Not covered.**
  - Non-free actions: freeness is used in Lemma 0, in the choice of `R_1`, and in steps 1–2.
  - Groupoids that are not transformation groupoids of subshifts, e.g. one-sided SFT groupoids, which are compressible
    and not amenable anyway.
  - Subgroups of `[[Λ ~ X]]` that do not contain the rule 3-cycles.
- **Model tests.**
  - Labbé's `Omega_U` is a free minimal SFT, so Theorem G does not fire. That matches the open status of
    `labbe-shift-derived-full-group-is-finitely-presented`.
  - Matui's finitely presented groups come from one-sided shift groupoids, which are not free `Λ`-subshift actions,
    so they are outside the setting.
