# Steinberg ring rigidity in the normalized rank metric

Lane `gk-l-gate-neg`, 2026-09-12. Supports the open Cairn claim
`el3-rank-models-factor-through-ring-rank-models` and the route
`non-linear-sofic-via-el3-ring-rank-rigidity`.

## 0. The mechanism, and why it avoids property (T)

The Hamming and Hilbert--Schmidt proofs that the Leavitt unit group is nonsofic, or has no
MF image, round the Kazhdan subgroup's approximate objects into exact ones. In the rank
metric over a finite field that rounding is unavailable
(`kazhdan-group-rank-models-admit-no-expander-decomposition`).

This artifact records a different mechanism. It uses only the Steinberg relations of
`EL_3(R)` and the ring structure of `R`:

* **Extraction.** From a nontrivial rank model of `EL_3(R)`, build a unital ring
  homomorphism from `R` into some rank ultraproduct.
* **Contradiction.** `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)` has no such homomorphism
  (`leavitt-algebra-has-no-unital-rank-model`).

The additive special case is already established
(`block-unipotent-rank-models-of-leavitt-el3-are-trivial`). Section 5 states the general
claim. Sections 1--4 record what is proved toward it and where each attempt stops. No step
uses property (T) or compression.

Setting: `k` is a field of characteristic `p` and `M = prod_omega M_(n_i)(k) / d_omega` is
a rank ultraproduct with rank function `rk`. `sigma : EL_3(R) -> M^x` is a homomorphism,
where `R` is a unital algebra over a field of characteristic `p` and `x_ij(a)` are the
elementary matrices.

## 1. Exact unipotence in characteristic p

**Lemma 1.** For `i != j` and `a` in `R`, put `n_ij(a) = sigma(x_ij(a)) - 1` in `M`. Then:

1. `n_ij(a)^p = 0`;
2. the `n_ij(a)`, `a` in `R`, commute pairwise;
3. `n_ij(a + b) = n_ij(a) + n_ij(b) + n_ij(a) n_ij(b)` when `p = 2`, and in general
   `1 + n_ij(a + b) = (1 + n_ij(a))(1 + n_ij(b))`.

*Proof.* `x_ij(a)^p = x_ij(pa) = 1`, so `sigma(x_ij(a))^p = 1` exactly in `M`. In
characteristic `p`, `(1 + n)^p = 1 + n^p`, which gives 1. Root subgroups are abelian, so 2
holds exactly. 3 is multiplicativity. QED

So every model acts on each root subgroup by commuting unipotents, with no rounding. The
root subgroup `U_ij = (R, +)` is an elementary abelian `p`-group. After Lemma 1 the only
non-linear feature is the product term in 3.

## 2. The additive case extracts a ring homomorphism

If the product terms vanish and `n_ij(a)` lies in a corner `e_i M e_j`, the relations
`x_ij(a) x_ij(b) = x_ij(a+b)` and `[x_ij(a), x_jk(b)] = x_ik(ab)` read
`psi_ij(a+b) = psi_ij(a) + psi_ij(b)` and `psi_ij(a) psi_jk(b) = psi_ik(ab)`. The matrix
units `u_ij = psi_ij(1)` then identify the corners, and `a -> psi_12(a) u_21` is a unital
ring homomorphism `R -> e_1 M e_1`. That corner is a rank ultraproduct after
renormalization. For the Leavitt algebra that forces `e_1 = 0`
(`block-unipotent-rank-models-of-leavitt-el3-are-trivial`, lane `gk-l-gate-pos`).

The general claim asks for the same extraction with no additivity and no block
hypothesis.

## 3. Associated-graded linearization, and where it stops

Let `P` be a finite subgroup of the upper unitriangular group `UT_3(R)`, for instance the
group generated by `x_12(a_s), x_23(b_t), x_13(c_u)` for finitely many coefficients. It is a
finite `p`-group, so the augmentation ideal `J` of `F_p[P]` is nilpotent.

* **Filtration.** `V_m = sigma(J^m) M` is a finite sum of principal right ideals of the
  regular ring `M`, hence principal. The pieces `gr_m = V_m / V_(m+1)` have ranks summing
  to `1`.
* **First order.** For `g, h` in `P`, `gh - 1 = (g-1) + (h-1) + (g-1)(h-1)`, so on `gr`
  the degree-raising map `g -> (g - 1)` is additive modulo `J^2`. The product term of
  Lemma 1(3) disappears.
* **Second order.** `ghg^-1h^-1 - 1` lies in `J^2`, with leading term
  `(g-1)(h-1) - (h-1)(g-1)` modulo `J^3`. So `gr` carries a representation of the Lazard
  Lie ring of `P`. For `P` inside `UT_3(R)` there are operators `X(a)` and `Y(b)` of degree
  `+1` and `Z(c)` of degree `+2`, additive in their arguments, with
  `[X(a), Y(b)] = Z(ab)`. This is a Heisenberg Lie ring representation over the additive
  groups of `R`.

**Where it stops.**

* **No composition law.** The bilinear map `(a, b) -> [X(a), Y(b)]` depends only on `ab`,
  but multiplication of `R` is not realized as composition of operators. The additive case
  of Section 2 gets composition from `x_12(1)` acting as a matrix unit between equal-rank
  corners. On `gr` the operators are nilpotent and raise degree, so no piece is mapped
  isomorphically onto another.
* **No Weyl symmetry.** The Weyl elements `w_ij = x_ij(1) x_ji(1) x_ij(1)` permute root
  subgroups but do not preserve the filtration of `UT_3(R)`, so `gr` sees only the Borel
  part and none of the lower root subgroups.
* **No limit.** The filtration depends on the finite subgroup `P`. As `P` grows, the
  graded pieces are not compatible, so no single graded object carries all of `R`.

The open step is to find, inside the model, equal-rank pieces on which some root element
acts as a matrix unit. That is exactly the block structure the additive case assumes.

## 4. Finite-level calibration: counting on finite subgroups cannot finish

Let `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)` and `m = 2^k`. The level-`k` matrix units `s_a t_b` with
`|a| = |b| = k` span a unital copy of `M_m(F_2)` in `R`, so
`H_k = EL_3(M_m(F_2)) = SL_(3m)(F_2)` is a finite subgroup of `EL_3(R) = R^x`.

* **Doubling is an identity.** `s_a t_b = s_(a0) t_(b0) + s_(a1) t_(b1)`, so under the
  level-`(k+1)` identification every `g` in `H_k` equals `g (x) I_2` in `H_(k+1)`.
* **The compressor gives a corner.** The compressor of the nine-leaf configuration acts on
  coefficients by `a -> s_0 a t_0`, which sends `g` in `H_k` to `g (+) I_(3m)` in
  `H_(k+1)`, the copy of `g` on the prefix-`0` coordinates. So `g (+) I` is conjugate to
  `g` inside `R^x`.
* **Involution classes.** In `SL_N(F_2)` the involutions `1 + N` with `N^2 = 0` and
  `rank N = r` form one conjugacy class. Put `f_(k+1)(r) = rk(sigma(g_r) - 1)` for such an
  element `g_r` of `H_(k+1)`. It is well defined because conjugation inside `H_(k+1)` is
  exact in `M`.

**Proposition 2.** For every rank model `sigma` of `R^x` over any field of characteristic
`2`, every `k` and every `1 <= r <= 3m/2`: `f_(k+1)(2r) = f_(k+1)(r)`.

*Proof.* Take `g` in `H_k` of type `r`.
* Its double `g (x) I_2` is `g` itself, and has type `2r` in `H_(k+1)`, so
  `f_(k+1)(2r) = rk(sigma(g) - 1)`.
* Its compressed copy `g (+) I` has type `r` in `H_(k+1)` and is conjugate to `g` in
  `R^x`, so `f_(k+1)(r) = rk(sigma(g) - 1)`.
QED

**Calibration.**
* **Consistent:** the regular module of `H_(k+1)`, where every involution acts freely with
  `rank(g - 1) = n/2`, and the trivial module. Both satisfy Proposition 2.
* **Excluded:** only models dominated by small natural-type modules, such as the natural
  module, where `f(r) = r/N`.

So counting ranks on finite subgroups, even with the exact identities that come from
`R ~= R (+) R` and from the compressor, is consistent with free behavior. It yields no
contradiction by itself. Any extraction has to use the Steinberg relations across all of
`R`, including elements of infinite order.

## 4b. Uniform displacement bounds and root self-similarity

**Lemma 3 (commutator images).** Let `p = 2`, `x = n_12(a)` and `y = n_23(1)`. Then

```text
n_13(a) = xy + yx + xyx + yxy + xyxy
        = y (x + xy)  +  x y (1 + x + xy),
Im n_13(a)  is contained in  Im n_23(1) + n_12(a) (Im n_23(1)).
```

*Proof.* `x_13(a) = [x_12(a), x_23(1)]`. With `g = 1 + x` and `h = 1 + y`, `x^2 = y^2 = 0`
and characteristic two give `g^-1 = g` and `h^-1 = h`. So
`ghg^-1h^-1 = ((1+x)(1+y))^2 = 1 + (x + y + xy)^2`. Expanding with `x^2 = y^2 = 0` gives the
five terms, and the factorization is direct. QED

**Corollary 4.** Put `rho = rk(n_12(1))`. Weyl elements of `EL_3(F_2)` conjugate all
`x_ij(1)` to one another, so every `n_ij(1)` has rank `rho`, and `rk(n_ij(a)) <= 2 rho` for
every `i != j` and every `a` in `R`. A model is trivial on the root subgroups exactly when
`rho = 0`.

**Proposition 5 (self-similarity of root elements over `L_(F_2)(1,2)`).** For every `k` and
every word `w` of length `k`, the elements `x_12(1)` and `x_12(s_w t_w)` are conjugate in
`GL_3(R) = R^x`. Also

```text
x_12(1) = product over |w| = k of x_12(s_w t_w),
```

a product of `2^k` commuting conjugates of `x_12(1)`.

*Proof.* The product is additivity of the root subgroup together with
`sum_(|w|=k) s_w t_w = 1`. For conjugacy, write `e = s_w t_w`, `N = E_12` and `N' = e E_12`
on `R^3`.
* `N` maps `C = e_2 R` isomorphically onto `A = e_1 R` and kills `B = e_1 R + e_3 R`
  modulo `A`. More precisely, `R^3 = A (+) B (+) C` with `B = e_3 R`.
* `N'` maps `C' = e_2 e R` onto `A' = e_1 e R` and kills
  `B' = e_1 (1-e) R (+) e_2 (1-e) R (+) e_3 R`.
* Left multiplication by `s_w` gives `R ~= eR`, hence `C ~= C'` and `A ~= A'` compatibly
  with `N` and `N'`.
* `B ~= B'`, because every nonzero finitely generated free right `R`-module is isomorphic
  to `R`.
The resulting automorphism `g` of `R^3` satisfies `g N g^-1 = N'`. QED

**Counting.**
* **Additive models die at once.** When root actions are additive with orthogonal images,
  `rho = 2^k rho` forces `rho = 0`. This recovers the rank form of
  `block-unipotent-rank-models-of-leavitt-el3-are-trivial`.
* **Free-type modules survive.** Take `P_k = < x_12(s_w t_w) : |w| = k > = (Z/2)^(2^k)`.
  Every nontrivial element is `x_12(e_S)` for an idempotent `e_S = sum_(w in S) s_w t_w`, and
  `e_S R ~= R`, so every nontrivial element of `P_k` is conjugate to `x_12(1)`. So every model
  has all nontrivial elements of `P_k` at the same displacement rank `rho`. Two families meet
  that: free modules over `F_2[P_k]`, with `rho = 1/2` on the free part, and the uniform sum
  `(+)_H F_2[P_k / H]` over all hyperplanes `H`, with `rho ~ 1/4`.

So root self-similarity rules out every additive model and constrains the rest only to
uniform displacement. The extraction step must still use the Steinberg relations between
different root subgroups, which is where Lemma 3 enters.

## 4c. Block structure on the unit root elements propagates to every root element

This section kills the hybrid shape of
`research/artifacts/leavitt-unit-rank-model-construction-audit-2026-09-12.md` Section 7.
There, a model is block unipotent on a polynomial subring's root subgroups and arbitrary on
the rest. The only hypothesis used is that the six unit root elements act by matrix units.

**Proposition 6 (propagation).** Let `R` be a unital ring of characteristic two, `M` a ring
of characteristic two, and `sigma : EL_3(R) -> M^x` a homomorphism. Suppose `M` has pairwise
orthogonal idempotents `p_1, p_2, p_3`, and put `f = 1 - p_1 - p_2 - p_3`. Suppose also that
for `i != j`

```text
sigma(x_ij(1)) = 1 + u_ij,    u_ij in p_i M p_j,
u_ij u_jk = u_ik,  u_ij u_ji = p_i        (i, j, k distinct).
```

Then for every `a` in `R` there is `C(a)` in `p_1 M p_1` with
`n_ij(a) = sigma(x_ij(a)) - 1 = C(a) E_ij` for all `i != j`. Here `E_ij` means `u_ij`
composed with the identifications `p_k M p_l ~= p_1 M p_1` through the matrix units. The map
`a -> C(a)` is a unital ring homomorphism `R -> p_1 M p_1`.

*Proof.* Write elements of `M` as `4 x 4` block matrices over `(p_1, p_2, p_3, f)`, and
identify the three corners through the `u`'s, so that `u_ij = E_ij`. By Lemma 1 every
`n_ij(a)` squares to zero, and Lemma 3's expansion
`n([g,h]) = xy + yx + xyx + yxy + xyxy` holds for root elements `g = 1 + x`, `h = 1 + y`.

1. **`Z = n_13(a)`.** It commutes with `E_12` and `E_23`, because `x_13(a)` commutes with
   `x_12(1)` and `x_23(1)`. Comparing `E_12 Z` with `Z E_12`, and `E_23 Z` with `Z E_23`,
   entry by entry gives
   `Z = D(E_11 + E_22 + E_33) + C E_13 + P E_14 + Q E_43 + S E_44`.
2. **`X = n_12(a)`.** `x_12(a) = [x_13(a), x_32(1)]`. The five-term expansion with `W = E_32`
   gives `X = C(1+D) E_12 + Q(1+D) E_42 + D^2 E_32`.
3. **Back to `n_13(a)`.** `x_13(a) = [x_12(a), x_23(1)]`. Expanding with `X` from step 2 and
   `Y = E_23` gives `AE_13 + BE_43 + D^2(E_22 + E_33 + E_23) + (higher terms in D^2)`, with
   `A = C(1+D)` and `B = Q(1+D)`. Comparing with step 1: the `E_11` entry forces `D = 0`,
   then `P = 0`, `S = 0`, `A = C` and `B = Q`. So `n_13(a) = C E_13 + Q E_43` and
   `n_12(a) = C E_12 + Q E_42`.
4. **`Z' = n_23(a)`.** It commutes with `E_13` and `E_21`, because `x_23(a)` commutes with
   `x_13(1)` and `x_21(1)`. The same comparison gives
   `Z' = D'(E_11 + E_22 + E_33) + C' E_23 + P' E_24 + Q' E_43 + S' E_44`.
5. **The other expansion of `n_13(a)`.** `x_13(a) = [x_12(1), x_23(a)]`. Expanding with
   `E_12` and `Z'` gives `n_13(a) = (1+D')C' E_13 + (1+D')P' E_14 + D'^2 E_12` (coefficient order corrected by
   `gk-vf-linear`; not load-bearing), which has no
   `E_43` entry. Comparing with step 3 gives `Q = 0`. So `n_13(a) = C E_13` and
   `n_12(a) = C E_12`.
6. **The remaining roots.** `x_23(a) = [x_21(1), x_13(a)]` gives `n_23(a) = C E_23`, and
   `x_21(a) = [x_23(a), x_31(1)]` gives `n_21(a) = C E_21`. In each case only the `XY` term
   survives. The other roots follow the same way.
7. **Ring homomorphism.**
   * *Additive.* `n_12(a+b) = n_12(a) + n_12(b) + n_12(a) n_12(b)`, and the last term is
     `C(a) C(b) E_12 E_12 = 0`.
   * *Multiplicative.* `x_13(ab) = [x_12(a), x_23(b)]` with `X = C(a) E_12` and
     `Y = C(b) E_23` leaves only `XY = C(a) C(b) E_13`, so `C(ab) = C(a) C(b)`.
   * *Unital.* `C(1)` is the identity of the corner, since `u_12 = E_12`.
QED

Every relation used is an exact Steinberg relation of `EL_3(R)`:
* commutation of `U_13` with `U_12` and `U_23`, and of `U_23` with `U_13` and `U_21`;
* the commutator identities `[x_12(a), x_23(b)] = x_13(ab)` and their index permutations.

**Corollary 7.** Let `R` be a simple ring of characteristic two that is not directly finite,
for instance `L_K(1,n)` with `char K = 2` and `n >= 2`. Let `M` be a rank ultraproduct over a
field of characteristic two. Every homomorphism `sigma : EL_3(R) -> M^x` whose unit root
elements act by a `3 x 3` system of matrix units, as in Proposition 6, is trivial.

*Proof.* `C : R -> p_1 M p_1` is a unital ring homomorphism into a corner of a faithful rank
ultraproduct, which is directly finite. If `xy = p_1` there, then `yx` is an idempotent below
`p_1` of the same rank, hence equal to `p_1`.
* `t s = 1 != st` in `R` gives `C(s) C(t) = p_1`, so `C(1 - st) = 0`.
* Simplicity makes the kernel of `C` all of `R`, so `p_1 = 0`.
* Then every `u_ij = 0`, so `rho = 0`, and Corollary 4 kills every root element.
* `EL_3(R)` is generated by root elements, so `sigma` is trivial.
QED

## 4d. Odd characteristic: the torus propagates block structure

Proposition 6 uses `n_ij(a)^2 = 0`, which holds only in characteristic two. In odd
characteristic `p`, root actions satisfy only `n^p = 0` (Lemma 1). The diagonal torus of
`SL_3(F_p)` replaces the five-term expansions. This covers the ternary hosts
`L_(F_3)(1,2)^x` and `PG`.

**Proposition 8.** Let `p` be odd, `R` a unital algebra over a field of characteristic `p`,
`M` a rank ultraproduct over a field of characteristic `p`, and `sigma : EL_3(R) -> M^x` a
homomorphism. Suppose the matrix-unit hypothesis of Proposition 6 holds: pairwise orthogonal
idempotents `p_1, p_2, p_3`, `f = 1 - p_1 - p_2 - p_3`, and
`sigma(x_ij(1)) = 1 + u_ij` with `u_ij` in `p_i M p_j`, `u_ij u_jk = u_ik` and
`u_ij u_ji = p_i`. Then `n_ij(a) = sigma(x_ij(a)) - 1` lies in `p_i M p_j` for every `i != j`
and every `a` in `R`.

*Proof.*

1. **`sigma` on `SL_3(F_p)`.** `Phi(E_ij) = u_ij` and `Phi(E_ii) = p_i` define an algebra
   homomorphism `M_3(F_p) -> M` with `Phi(1) = p_1 + p_2 + p_3`, so `g -> Phi(g) + f` is a
   group homomorphism `GL_3(F_p) -> M^x`. Since `u_ij^2 = 0`,
   `sigma(x_ij(lambda)) = (1 + u_ij)^lambda = 1 + lambda u_ij = Phi(x_ij(lambda)) + f`. The
   elementary matrices generate `SL_3(F_p)`, so `sigma(g) = Phi(g) + f` there.
2. **Block form (characteristic free).** Write `n = n_13(a)` in `4 x 4` blocks over
   `(p_1, p_2, p_3, f)`, with corners identified through the `u`'s. Commutation with `u_12`
   and `u_23` gives, exactly as in Proposition 6,
   `n = D(E_11 + E_22 + E_33) + C E_13 + P E_14 + Q E_43 + S E_44`.
   * `p_2` is invariant, and `n` acts on it by `D`.
   * On `p_1 + f + p_3`, in that order, `n` is upper triangular with diagonal `(D, S, D)`.
   * So `n^p = 0` gives `D^p = 0` and `S^p = 0`.
3. **First torus element.** `h = diag(-1, -1, 1)` lies in `SL_3(F_p)` and
   `h x_13(a) h^-1 = x_13(-a) = x_13(a)^-1`.
   * By step 1, conjugation by `sigma(h)` multiplies the `(i,j)` block entry by
     `eps_i eps_j` with `eps = (-1, -1, 1, 1)`. So `(1 + n)(1 + n') = 1` with
     `n' = D(E_11 + E_22 + E_33) - C E_13 - P E_14 + Q E_43 + S E_44`.
   * Reading entries: `(1,1)` gives `2D + D^2 = 0`, `(f,f)` gives `2S + S^2 = 0`, and `(f,3)`
     gives `2Q + QD + SQ = 0`.
   * `2` is invertible and `D`, `S` are nilpotent, so `D + 2` and `S + 2` are invertible in
     their corners. So `D = S = 0`, and then `Q = 0`.
4. **Second torus element.** `h' = diag(1, -1, -1)` also inverts `x_13(a)`, with
   `eps = (1, -1, -1, 1)`. Now `n = C E_13 + P E_14` and `n' = -C E_13 + P E_14`, so
   `(1 + n)(1 + n') = 1 + 2P E_14`. So `P = 0`, and `n_13(a) = C(a) E_13` lies in
   `p_1 M p_3`.
5. **Weyl transport.** Every ordered pair `(k,l)` is `(pi(1), pi(3))` for some permutation
   `pi`, realized by a monomial `w` in `SL_3(F_p)` with `w x_13(b) w^-1 = x_kl(+-b)`. By
   step 1, `sigma(w)` permutes the corners with signs and fixes `f`, so `n_kl(a)` lies in
   `p_k M p_l` for every `a`.
QED

**Corollary 9.** Let `p` be odd, `R` a simple algebra of characteristic `p` that is not
directly finite, for instance `L_K(1,n)` with `char K = p` and `n >= 2`, and `M` a rank
ultraproduct over a field of characteristic `p`. Every homomorphism
`sigma : EL_3(R) -> M^x` meeting the matrix-unit hypothesis is trivial.

*Proof.* By Proposition 8, `sigma(x_ij(a)) = 1 + psi_ij(a)` with `psi_ij(a)` in `p_i M p_j`.
* *Additive.* `psi_ij(a) psi_ij(b)` lies in `p_i M p_j p_i M p_j = 0`, so `psi_ij` is
  additive.
* *Commutators.* For `X` in `p_i M p_j` and `Y` in `p_j M p_k` with `i, j, k` distinct,
  `X^2 = Y^2 = YX = 0`, so `[1 + X, 1 + Y] = 1 + XY` in every characteristic. The Steinberg
  relations give
  `psi_13(ab) = psi_12(a) psi_23(b)`, `psi_12(c) = psi_13(c) u_32`,
  `psi_13(b) = psi_12(b) u_23` and `psi_23(b) = u_21 psi_13(b)`.
* *Ring homomorphism.* Put `C(a) = psi_12(a) u_21` in `p_1 M p_1`. Then `C(1) = p_1`, `C` is
  additive, and
  `C(ab) = psi_13(ab) u_31 = psi_12(a) u_21 psi_13(b) u_31 = psi_12(a) u_21 psi_12(b) u_21 = C(a) C(b)`.
* *Collapse.* `p_1 M p_1` is directly finite, since the rank function is faithful and an
  idempotent below `p_1` of the same rank equals `p_1`. So `ts = 1` gives `C(s) C(t) = p_1`
  and `C(1 - st) = 0`. Simplicity gives `C = 0`, hence `p_1 = 0`. Then every `u_ij` and every
  `psi_ij(a)` vanishes, and root elements generate `EL_3(R)`.
QED

**For the ternary hosts.** Put `R_3 = L_(F_3)(1,2)`. Since `R_3 ~= M_3(R_3)`,
`EL_3(R_3) <= GL_3(R_3) ~= R_3^x = G_3`, and `G_3` embeds in `PG` through
`leavitt-units-embed-in-their-scalar-quotient`. An `F_3`-linear sofic approximation of `G_3`
or of `PG` restricts to a separating model of the infinite group `EL_3(R_3)`. By Corollary 9,
its restriction to `SL_3(F_3)` is not `(natural (x) W) (+) trivial`. So the construction
shapes excluded in characteristic two are excluded for the `F_3` gate too.

**A tool for general complements in odd characteristic (open).**
* *Exact logarithm.* `L_ij(a) = log sigma(x_ij(a)) = sum_(k<p) (-1)^(k+1) n^k / k` is exact,
  because `n^p = 0`, and `exp L_ij(a) = sigma(x_ij(a))`.
* *Weight vectors.* `(1 + n)^t = exp(tL)`, so `Ad sigma(h) L_ij(a) = chi_ij(h) L_ij(a)` for
  `h` in the diagonal torus `T` of `SL_3(F_p)`, with `chi_ij(h) = t_i t_j^-1`.
* *Weight splitting.* `p` does not divide `|T| = (p-1)^2`, so `sigma(T)` splits `M` into
  weight idempotents `q_lambda`, and `L_ij(a) q_lambda = q_(lambda + alpha_ij) L_ij(a)`.
* *Where it stops.* `L_ij` is not additive in `a` (mixed terms of degree at least `p`
  survive), and for `p = 3` the root weight `alpha_13` equals the natural weight `eps_2`. So
  weights alone do not separate corners from a complement. Proposition 8 also needed
  commutation and unipotence.

## 4e. Dual natural shapes, free content, and the Leavitt instance

**Dual natural shapes (`gk-vf-linear`, verification artifact Section 29, eda113e5ce).**
* Transpose is an anti-isomorphism `M_3(R) -> M_3(R^op)`, so `theta(g) = (g^T)^-1` is a group
  isomorphism `EL_3(R) -> EL_3(R^op)` with `theta(x_ij(a)) = x_ji(-a)`.
* `R^op` is simple and not directly finite.
* If `sigma(x_ij(1)) = 1 - u'_ji` with a system of matrix units `u'`, then
  `sigma o theta^-1` sends `x_kl(1)` to `1 + u'_kl`, which is the matrix-unit form over `R^op`.
* So `(dual natural (x) W) (+) trivial` models are trivial, in every characteristic, by
  Corollaries 7 and 9.

**Proposition 10 (free content blocks block support).** Let `p` be a prime, `R` a unital
`F_p`-algebra, `H_0 = SL_3(F_p)` inside `EL_3(R)`, and `sigma : EL_3(R) -> M^x` a rank model
over `k` of characteristic `p`. Suppose there are representatives `sigma_i` that are honest
representations on `H_0` and contain `k[H_0]^(m_i)` as a direct summand, with
`m_i / n_i -> mu > 0`. Then `rk((sigma(x_23(1)) - 1)(sigma(x_12(1)) - 1)) >= mu`, and the six
unit root elements admit no block support.

*Proof.*
* `beta = (h - 1)(g - 1) = hg - h - g + 1`, with `h = x_23(1)` and `g = x_12(1)`, combines
  four distinct group elements, so it is nonzero in `k[H_0]` and left multiplication by it on
  `k[H_0]` has rank at least `1`.
* The free summand is `sigma_i(H_0)`-invariant with an invariant complement, so
  `rank sigma_i(beta) >= m_i`.
* Block support puts `v_23 v_12` in `p_2 M p_3 · p_1 M p_2 = 0`.
QED

**Example 11 (free models over a ring with a ring rank model).**
* `S_inf = lim M_(p^k)(F_p)`, under `a -> a (x) I_p`, is simple. Left multiplication on the
  finite stages, with normalized rank, is a ring rank model.
* `EL_3(S_inf)` is the union of the level groups `SL_(3 p^k)(F_p)`.
* Their regular representations give a separating rank model, since every nontrivial element
  moves with normalized rank at least `1/2`.
* That model is free on `H_0`, with `mu = 1/|H_0|`.
* So nontrivial models of `EL_3` over a simple ring can be free on `SL_3(F_p)` even when the
  ring has a ring rank model. Block support at the level of summands cannot be the general
  mechanism of an extraction.

**Where free content sits for the Leavitt instance.**
* A free `k[H]`-module has a permutation basis. So on each finite subgroup `H` of the model,
  free content is monomial in a basis of its own.
* For a nonsofic host no global monomial basis exists
  (`monomial-rank-models-are-hamming-models`). A surviving free-like model must glue its local
  permutation bases non-monomially along infinite subgroups.
* For `L_(F_2)(1,2)` those are generated by level groups together with level-changing units:
  `s_0`-type coefficients and Thompson `V`. There Kraft's inequality breaks every trace on the
  locally matricial part, and Proposition 5 already makes root displacement trace-blind.

**The Leavitt instance.** Following `gk-vf-linear`'s recommendation, the payoff is carried by
the instance claim `leavitt-el3-rank-models-over-finite-fields-are-trivial`, with its own route
`non-linear-sofic-via-leavitt-el3-rank-triviality`. The class statement of Section 5 implies it
through `leavitt-el3-rank-triviality-from-ring-rank-rigidity`, but a linear sofic `EL_3` over
some other simple ring would refute only the class statement.

## 5. The claim

**Claim `el3-rank-models-factor-through-ring-rank-models` (open).** Let `p` be a prime and
`R` a simple unital algebra over a field of characteristic `p`. Suppose `R` admits no unital
ring homomorphism into any rank ultraproduct of matrix algebras over a field of
characteristic `p`. Then every homomorphism `sigma : EL_3(R) -> M^x`, with `M` a rank
ultraproduct over a field of characteristic `p`, is trivial.

**Payoff.** `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)` satisfies the hypothesis
(`leavitt-algebra-has-no-unital-rank-model`), `EL_3(R) = R^x`
(`leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group`), and `R^x` is simple and infinite. So `R^x`
has no nontrivial rank model over any field of characteristic `2`. In particular it is not
`F_2`-linear sofic, which settles `non-linear-sofic-group`.

**Why the characteristic must match.** Over other characteristics the root elements are
semisimple and Lemma 1 has no content. For the payoff group, nothing on this page decides
complex or `F_3` models.

**How it can fail.** If some simple characteristic-`p` ring with no rank model of its own
has a linear sofic `EL_3`, the claim is false. The prime candidates are purely infinite
simple rings that are not finitely generated, where property (T) and the known nonsoficity
arguments are unavailable. A sofic `EL_3` over such a ring would already refute the claim,
through monomial rank models (`monomial-rank-models-are-hamming-models`).

**Relation to the Hamming row.** The permutation analogue of this claim, that a sofic
approximation of `EL_3(R)` yields a rank model of `R`, would reprove nonsoficity of `R^x`
with no property (T). It is open as well.
