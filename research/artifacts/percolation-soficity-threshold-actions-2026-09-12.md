# Percolation soficity thresholds, part 2: nonsofic actions and nonsofic subgroups

Lane `ex-free-bridges`, 2026-09-12. This continues `research/artifacts/percolation-soficity-threshold-2026-09-12.md`
(part 1), whose notation, definitions and Lemmas 1.1--1.5 are used throughout. Handwritten proofs, no computation.

**Summary.**
- Part 1 needed the group itself to be nonsofic.
  - Here the same threshold is proved for every free ergodic nonsofic *action*. That covers residually
    finite Kazhdan groups such as the Kun--Thom groups, which are sofic.
  - So the reduction "one sofic supercritical cluster relation gives `p_c < p_u`" now applies to those
    groups as well.
- A nonsofic subgroup generated by a subset of the generators forces nonsoficity of the whole cluster
  relation as soon as its own clusters are in their uniqueness phase. That bounds the threshold by the
  subgroup's `p_u`.

## 5. Nonsofic actions

**Setting.** `G`, `S`, `E`, `Omega = [0,1]^E`, `omega_p` and `C_p` as in part 1.
- Let `a: G ↷ (X, nu)` be a free ergodic p.m.p. action whose orbit relation `R_a` is not sofic.
- Put `Omega_a = X x Omega` with `nu x mu` and the diagonal action. It is free, because the second factor
  is free. It is ergodic, as the product of an ergodic action with a mixing one.
- Define `R^a_p = { ((x,xi), g^-1 . (x,xi)) : g in C_p(xi) }`, a subrelation of the orbit relation
  `R_(a x B)` of `Omega_a`.
- Put `A^a_p = X x A_p`.

**Lemma 5.1 (the action is a class-bijective factor).** If `R_(a x B)` is sofic, then `R_a` is sofic.

*Proof.*
- `L^inf(X) ⊆ L^inf(X x Omega)`, and `u_g -> u_g` generates a trace-preserving copy of
  `L(R_a) = L^inf(X) ⋊ G` inside `L(R_(a x B))`. For a free action these crossed products are the relation
  algebras.
- An element `phi in [R_a]` has the form `x -> c(x).x` for a measurable `c: X -> G`. It lifts to
  `phi~(x, xi) = c(x).(x, xi) in [R_(a x B)]`, and `u_phi` maps to `u_(phi~)`.
- So an embedding `Theta` for `R_(a x B)` restricts to an embedding for `R_a` with the Cartan subalgebra in
  the diagonal and the full group in permutations. QED

**Theorem 5.2.** Let `Sof(a,S) = { p : R^a_p is sofic }`.
1. It is a down-set containing `[0, p_c)`, and it contains `p_c` if `Cay(G,S)` is nonamenable.
2. It misses `(p_u, 1]`.

So every free ergodic nonsofic action has a threshold `p_c <= p_sof(a,S) <= p_u`. The same holds with
"hyperlinear" in place of "sofic".

*Proof.*
1. Exactly as part 1, Theorem 3.1(1)--(2). The relations increase with `p` (Lemma 1.1), and below `p_c`
   the classes are finite (Lemma 1.5).
2. **Equality.** Let `p > p_u`. If `(x,xi)` and `g^-1.(x,xi)` both lie in `A^a_p`, then `1` and `g` lie in
   infinite clusters of `omega_p(xi)`. By `fpbs-cluster-count-trichotomy` they lie in the same one. So
   `R^a_p|_(A^a_p) = R_(a x B)|_(A^a_p)`, with `(nu x mu)(A^a_p) = theta(p) > 0`.
3. **Nonsoficity.** If `R^a_p` were sofic, its restriction would be sofic (Lemma 1.2). Then
   `R_(a x B)` would be sofic (Lemma 1.3, by ergodicity), and `R_a` would be sofic (Lemma 5.1).
   Contradiction.

The hyperlinear case uses the same lemmas with `R^omega`. QED

**Corollary 5.3 (Benjamini--Schramm from one nonsofic action).** Suppose `G` has a free ergodic nonsofic
action `a`, and `R^a_p` is sofic for some `p > p_c`. Then `p_c < p_u` on `Cay(G,S)`.

*Proof.* `p <= p_u` by Theorem 5.2(2). QED

**Instances.** The residually finite Kazhdan groups of `kun-thom-free-nonsofic-action` qualify, as do the
groups carrying Kun--Thom pairs recorded in `groups-containing-kun-thom-pair-have-nonsofic-actions`. For
them the Bernoulli cluster relations are sofic at every `p`, because Bernoulli shifts of sofic groups are
sofic. So part 1 says nothing about them, and Theorem 5.2 is the relevant statement.

**Comparison.** Part 1 is the case where `a` is the Bernoulli shift of a nonsofic group. Its relation is
nonsofic by Lemma 1.4.

## 6. Nonsofic subgroups

**Theorem 6.1.** Let `H <= G` be nonsofic and generated by `S_H ⊆ S`. Then the cluster relation `R_p` of
part 1 is nonsofic for every `p > p_u(Cay(H, S_H))`. So for every finite symmetric `S ⊇ S_H`:

    p_sof(G, S) <= p_u(Cay(H, S_H)).

The same holds for hyperlinearity.

*Proof.*
- **The subgroup relation.** Let `E_H ⊆ E` be the edges with labels in `S_H`. Let `R^H_p` be the relation
  on `Omega` generated by moves along open `S_H`-edges, `xi ~ h^-1 . xi` for `h` in the open `S_H`-cluster
  of `1`. It is contained in `R_p`.
- **Freeness and ergodicity for `H`.** `H` acts freely on `Omega`: for `h != 1` some edge is moved (part 1,
  Section 0) and the base is nonatomic. The `H`-orbits on `E` are infinite, since edge stabilizers are
  finite. So `H ↷ Omega` is mixing, hence ergodic.
- **Equality.** The open `S_H`-edges inside the coset `H` form Bernoulli bond percolation on
  `Cay(H, S_H)`. Let `A^H_p` be the event that `1` lies in an infinite `S_H`-cluster. For
  `p > p_u(Cay(H,S_H))`, `fpbs-cluster-count-trichotomy` gives a unique infinite cluster in that coset. So
  `R^H_p|_(A^H_p) = R_(H ↷ Omega)|_(A^H_p)`, with positive measure.
- **Conclusion.** `R_(H ↷ Omega)` is nonsofic by Lemma 1.4, since `H` is nonsofic. By Lemmas 1.2--1.3
  (ergodicity of `H ↷ Omega`), `R^H_p` is nonsofic. A relation containing a nonsofic subrelation is
  nonsofic (Lemma 1.1). QED

**Remark (the threshold need not be `p_u`).**
- Take `G = H * Z` with `H` nonsofic and `S = S_H ⊔ {t^(±1)}`.
- A Cayley graph with infinitely many ends has `p_u = 1`.
- If `p_u(Cay(H,S_H)) < 1`, Theorem 6.1 gives `p_sof(G,S) < 1 = p_u(G,S)`. For one-ended finitely presented
  `H` (for instance the binary Leavitt unit group, which is Kazhdan and so one-ended), `p_u < 1` is the
  Babson--Benjamini theorem.
- Neither the `p_u = 1` statement nor Babson--Benjamini is imported into Cairn or verified from source here.
  So this remark is not claimed. Part 1's question (Q2), whether `p_sof = p_u`, should be read for one-ended
  groups.

## 7. Scope of the open route

- **Treeability.** If, for some `p > p_c`, every cluster is a tree of finite 2-connected blocks, choose a
  Borel spanning tree inside each finite block. That gives a treeing of `R_p`, and treeable relations are
  sofic (Elek--Lippner).
  - Such a block structure is a purely probabilistic statement.
  - It would make the soficity route to Benjamini--Schramm a consequence of a statement close to
    Benjamini--Schramm itself. The route is not vacuous, because soficity can hold without treeability, but
    its natural proof is probabilistic.
- **Certificates.** A sofic model of `R_p` supplies partial permutations along open paths.
  - For a nonsoficity certificate `(F, eps_0)` of word length `L`, all word paths of `F` from a point are
    open with probability at least `p^(3L)` (FKG). So extending the partial permutations arbitrarily gives
    `(F, 1 - p^(3L))`-approximations.
  - Hence `R_p` is nonsofic once `p^(3L) > 1 - eps_0`. This is a certificate bound
    `p_sof <= (1 - eps_0)^(1/(3L))`, independent of `p_u`. It is recorded as a sketch, not claimed.
