## 2. P2: sharpness over Z

**All four steps re-derived by hand.**
1. (T) ⇒ finitely generated: the import, verified below.
2. The entries of finitely many generators and their inverses generate R, since e_12(r) has the entry r.
3. Let B be the T-invariant unital subalgebra generated by all f_j∘T^t.
   - (f u^a)(f′ u^b) = f (f′∘T^{−a}) u^{a+b}, which follows from u f u^{−1} = f∘T^{−1}.
   - So the sums with coefficients in B form a unital subring containing the generators, and LC(X,F_2) = B.
   - That is, the translates of the supports of the f_j generate the clopen algebra.
4. The itinerary map ι(x) = (atom of T^t x)_t has four properties.
   - It is continuous.
   - It is equivariant: ι(Tx)_t = ι(x)_{t+1}.
   - It is injective: ι(x) = ι(y) puts x and y in the same members of B, and clopen sets separate points.
   - X is compact, so ι is a conjugacy onto a subshift.

**Subsumption.** This is the F_2, n = 3 case of 3⇒2⇒1⇒4 in Cairn `crossed-product-el-kazhdan-iff-finite-field-and-subshift` (PASS, sk-verify-5). No new mathematics.

**Optional, re-derived here: simplicity also forces minimality.**
- Let Y ⊊ X be a nonempty closed invariant set.

- So that kernel is a proper nontrivial normal subgroup.

## 3. P3: finite models give the maps φ_k over any group

**Re-derived.**
1. **Generation.** u_λ e_a u_λ^{−1} = e_{λU} with U = {x : x(e) = a}, and λU = {z : z(λ) = a} because (λ^{−1}z)(e) = z(λ). Products give the cylinders. ✓
2. **One orbit.** ✓
3. **Models.** P_ξ is a representation. D(f) is well defined for f depending only on F, because y|_F is a pattern of X. ✓
4. **Conjugation rule.** See X1.
5. **Matrix units.**
   - For y ≠ y′ pick λ with y(λ) ≠ y′(λ). Then P_λ D(e_a) P_λ^{−1} = diag([y″(λ) = a])_{y″}, with a = y(λ), is 1 at y and 0 at y′.
   - The product over y′ ≠ y is E_{yy}.
   - By transitivity, E_{yy} P_ξ E_{y′y′} = E_{yy′} when ξy′ = y.
   - All of these lie in the ring generated by φ_F(S): φ_F(u_s^{±1}) = P_{s^{±1}}, and φ_F(e_a) = D(e_a) once e ∈ F. ✓
6. **Growth.** N_k → ∞ follows from the infinitude of EL_n(R) through Theorem 2's ultraproduct step. ✓

**X1, justification gap in step 4.** The condition "whenever f and f∘ξ^{−1} both depend on F" does not justify P_ξ D(f) P_ξ^{−1} = D(f∘ξ^{−1}) as written.
- At y, the left side evaluates f through the F-pattern of ξ^{−1}y, that is, through y|_{ξF}. The right side evaluates f∘ξ^{−1} through y|_F.
- On X these agree. For y ∈ Y they agree only if some point of X has y's pattern on F ∪ ξF, and the hypothesis does not provide that.
- I constructed no counterexample, so this is a gap, not a refutation.

Fix: "whenever $f$ depends only on coordinates in a finite $W$ with $W\cup\xi W\subseteq F$". The rule then holds:
- For x ∈ X with x|_F = y|_F, we have (ξ^{−1}x)|_W = x|_{ξW} = y|_{ξW}.
- For x″ ∈ X with x″|_F = (ξ^{−1}y)|_F, we have x″|_W = y|_{ξW}.
- So both sides evaluate f at points with the same W-pattern.

For fixed r, s, choose F containing W ∪ ξW for every coefficient window W of s and every ξ in the support of r, together with the windows of the coefficients of r, s, r+s and rs. Then φ_F is additive and multiplicative on r and s. This is exactly l.264–265 of the paper, where [−ℓ+1, ℓ−1] sits inside the window [−ℓ, ℓ].

**X2, notation.** Step 5 writes D(δ_y) for a diagonal matrix unit. Write E_{yy} P_ξ E_{y′y′} instead.

**X3, printed sentence.** Add "infinite": "for every infinite minimal topologically free subshift". Optional: "The argument in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives ...".

**Model tests, by hand.**
- **Z, Sturmian or any infinite minimal subshift:** Y is the orbit of y_ℓ, whose words of length 2ℓ+1 are words of X (l.255–257). It reduces to §2. ✓
- **Z^2, positive.** Take X = X_1 × X_2 for infinite minimal Z-subshifts X_i, as the Z^2-subshift z(m,n) = (x_1(m), x_2(n)) over A_1×A_2.
  - It is minimal, since orbits are products of dense orbits.
  - It is free: (m,n) ≠ 0 moves every point, because the T_i have no periodic points.
  - Finite models are the orbits of pairs of periodic approximants, whose patterns on [−ℓ,ℓ]^2 are pairs of words of X_1 and X_2.
  - The lemma applies. ✓
- **Z^2, negative:** a minimal subsystem of an aperiodic SFT has no finite models once F contains the forbidden shapes. A point with a finite orbit has a finite-index stabilizer, so it would be a periodic point of the SFT. The lemma does not apply, but P1's first part does. ✓
- **Lamplighter hosts (l.356–395):** the affine action of Λ on Ω = F_2^Δ is not the shift on A^Λ, so the lemma does not apply. The paper's models there are almost-actions on Y_n = F_2^{Q′_n}, multiplicative for fixed elements at large n. This agrees with the artifact. ✓

**Subsumption.** Cairn `finite-model-subshift-elementary-groups-simple-kazhdan-lef` (PASS, sk-verify-4) assumes a free action and equal pattern sets. P3 needs only topological freeness and inclusion of patterns to produce the φ_k. So they are close but not duplicates. Print P3 without a novelty claim.
