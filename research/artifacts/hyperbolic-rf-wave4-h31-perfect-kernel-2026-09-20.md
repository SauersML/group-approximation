# H31: a perfect kernel beyond the first finite quotient

20 September 2026. This proves a restriction on finite refinements of a
specified quotient. It does not classify every finite quotient of H31.

Let H=H31, Q=PSL2(31), and let pi:H->Q kill c and identify the vertex
subgroup <a,b> with Q. Write K=ker(pi). Let

    theta:H -> Q x C5,  a |-> (a,0), b |-> (b,0), c |-> (1,1),
    N=ker(theta).

Then K_ab=C5 and **N is perfect**. Consequently any finite image of H
in which the image of N is solvable factors through Q x C5. In particular
there is no nontrivial solvable-kernel finite refinement over theta.

The actual H31 group and its triangle of finite groups are those in
Caprace--Conder--Kaluba--Witzel, Theorem 1.2 and Section 6,
[primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09276). The three vertex groups
are Q, C5^2, and a Sylow-five subgroup of Sp4(5). Their embeddings and the
hyperbolic development are inputs already recorded in Cairn.

## The exact module for the first kernel

The retraction splits over Q, so H=K semidirect Q. Its kernel K is normally
generated by c. Thus its abelianization is an F5[Q]-module generated by the
class v of c. The relation [a,c]=1 gives (a-1)v=0. The relation [b,c,b]=1
gives (b-1)^2v=0, up to multiplication by an invertible power of b.
The other two mixed b,c commutator relators contain at least two letters
from K in an iterated commutator and vanish automatically in K_ab.

These are all the module relations, not only necessary ones. Indeed the
semidirect product of Q with the cyclic module

    V = F5[Q]v / <(a-1)v, (b-1)^2v>

satisfies every defining relation of H upon sending c to v. Its kernel
over Q is generated as a module by v, so this realizes the reverse
surjection. Therefore K_ab=V.

First impose (a-1)v=0, giving the permutation module on Q/<a> with 2976
basis elements. The remaining relations, indexed by q in Q, are

    [q<a>] - 2[qb<a>] + [qb^2<a>]=0.

The exact script `experiments/hrf-wave4-h31-2026-09-20/retraction_module.py`
checks the three mixed Q presentation relators on the matrices

    a=[[8,14],[4,11]], b=[[23,0],[14,27]] over F31,

enumerates all 14880 projective matrices, enumerates the 2976 cosets, and
constructs all 14880 rows of this relation matrix over F5. Its rank is
2975. The upper bound also follows because every row has coordinate sum
zero. Thus V has dimension one. Sending c to a generator of C5 gives a
nonzero quotient and identifies K_ab with C5.

## Why the next kernel is perfect

Adding [b,c]=1 to the presentation makes c central and leaves exactly
Q x C5. Thus N is the normal closure of [b,c] in H. The commutator belongs
to the Sylow-five vertex subgroup of Sp4(5), whose exponent is five: it
embeds in a four-dimensional unitriangular group over F5, where
(I+U)^5=I since U^4=0. Consequently N_ab has exponent five, and is an
F5-vector space M.

The restriction K->C5 splits over <c>, so K=N semidirect C5. The elementary
abelianization formula for a split extension gives

    K_ab = M_C5 direct-sum C5.

Since K_ab=C5, the coinvariants M_C5 vanish. If T denotes conjugation by
c on M, this means M=(T-1)M. But T^5=1, and in characteristic five
(T-1)^5=0. Iterating M=(T-1)M five times gives M=0. Hence N_ab=0.
This argument does not require M to have finite dimension.

Every image of a perfect group is perfect. A group both solvable and
perfect is trivial. This proves the finite-refinement assertion.

## The corresponding five-cone geometry

For completeness the orbit complex N\X also has a simple description.
Let B be the connected bipartite coset graph with vertices Q/<a> and
Q/<b> and edges Q. It is connected because a,b generate Q. Then N\X
is the union of five cones on the same B, with distinct apex vertices.
Faces correspond to (q,i), with q in Q and i in C5. The a,b edges keep
the index i; the c edge is independent of i. The Q-type vertices are
the five cone apices and the other vertices are those of B.

Thus B has 5952 vertices and 14880 edges, so its first Betti number is
8929. The union of five cones has H1=0 and H2=Z^(4*8929)=Z^35716,
by successive Mayer--Vietoris sequences (equivalently, suspension of B
after collapsing one cone). N-cell stabilizers are five-groups, of orders
1,1,25 at vertices and trivial on edges and faces. For any prime ell!=5,
averaging kills their higher homology. The equivariant spectral sequence
therefore gives H1(N;Fell)=0 and H2(N;Fell)=Fell^35716.

The rank computation and split-extension argument above are necessary
to settle characteristic five. The five-cone geometry alone does not
prove perfectness, and it does not prove vanishing of H2.

## Scope

No finite quotient is forced to be a refinement of theta. For any other
finite quotient, its product with theta is such a refinement, but its
kernel (the image of N) may be nontrivial perfect and nonsolvable. These
refinements remain possible. Neither perfectness nor the large second
homology proves that N, H, or a central extension is non-residually finite.
