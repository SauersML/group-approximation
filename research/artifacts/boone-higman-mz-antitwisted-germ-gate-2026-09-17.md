## 1. Three shared assumptions and their denials

These are the unstated assumptions shared by the earlier attempts: algorithmically periodic enumerations, bounded
jumps, square spirals and finite-prefix census. Each is denied in turn.

- **A1. The shell germ analysis transfers to the permutation envelope.** The graph records that the shell and
  permutation problems are "not identified". In practice the shell fiber product `R x_Z R` has served as the
  germ-level model of both.
  - *Denial.* The germ group of `E_nu(P)` is not the symmetric fiber product. Its parity-preserving part is the
    *antitwisted* product `F^- = {eta(a)+eta(b)=0}`, generated by `(s,s^-1)` rather than `(s,s)`, because a
    permutation of `N` has total near index zero.
  - Node: `mz-envelope-germ-group-is-an-antitwisted-fiber-product`.
- **A2. Finite presentation is attacked by constructing presentations.** Every attempt tries to build finite
  relator systems for a cleverly chosen `nu`.
  - *Denial.* Use coarse invariants that detect non-finite-presentation uniformly over all `nu`: BNSR invariants of
    `R_nu x R_nu` evaluated on the one character `eta+eta`. The Bieri–Geoghegan product formula over `Z` in
    dimension 2 turns this into a Σ^1 condition on `R_nu` alone.
- **A3. Decidability of `P` is consumed only in choosing `nu`, and the obstruction is algorithmic.**
  - *Denial.* The first gate is purely geometric: `ker eta` must be finitely generated, a two-sided window.
    Decidability plays no role in it.
  - Node: `mz-envelope-fp-forces-two-sided-fg-near-index-kernel`.

## 2. The theorem

For infinite finitely generated `P` and any bijection `nu`:

    E_nu(P) f.p.  ==>  E_nu(P)/FSym f.p.  <==>  F^- f.p.  <==>  R_nu f.p. and ker(eta) f.g.

Compare the shell side: the shell germ group is f.p. iff `F^+ = R x_Z R` is f.p., iff `R_nu` is f.p. and some
one-sided ascending union works (`shell-germ-fp-is-an-ascending-hnn-gate`). In Σ-language the shell condition is
`Σ^1(R) ∩ {[eta],[-eta]} ≠ ∅`, whereas the permutation envelope needs `{[eta],[-eta]} ⊆ Σ^1(R)`.

The reason is geometric. The character cutting out `F^+` is `eta + (-eta)`. It lies in the join
`Σ^1(R)^c * Σ^1(R)^c` only if *both* `[eta]` and `[-eta]` are bad. The character cutting out `F^-` is
`eta + eta`, which lies in that join as soon as *one* of them is bad. This is the Stallings–Bieri mechanism: for
free `R`, `F^-` is `SB_2`-like and is not finitely presented.

## 3. Proof sketch

The full proofs are `mz-antitwisted-germ-fiber-product-proof` and `mz-two-sided-kernel-gate-sigma-proof`.

1. `FSym(N)` is the normal closure of `(0 1)`, so an f.p. `E` gives an f.p. germ group.
2. Near parity is a homomorphism `E -> Z/2`. On its kernel, the pair of germs on evens and odds identifies
   `E^0/FSym` with a subgroup of `R x R` of index-sum zero. It contains `(lambda_g,1)`, `(1,lambda_g)` (conjugate by
   `omega = prod (2k 2k+1)`) and `(s,s^-1)` from `E_2`. Since `ker eta` is the normal closure of `lambda(P)`, it is
   all of `F^-`.
3. `a -> (a, s^-eta(a))` makes `R` a retract of `F^-`, and retracts of f.p. groups are f.p.
4. `F^- = ker(eta+eta)`. By Renz Satz C, `[±(eta+eta)] ∈ Σ^2(R x R)`. By MMVW (Bieri–Geoghegan Theorem 1.1),
   these points lie in `Σ^2(R x R; Z)`. If some `[ε eta] ∉ Σ^1(R)`, then `[ε(eta+eta)]` lies in the join, and
   Bieri–Geoghegan Theorem 1.5 (product formula over `Z`, `n ≤ 3`) puts it outside `Σ^2(R x R; Z)`. This is a
   contradiction.
5. Converse: Meinert's inequality together with Satz C.

## 4. Calibrations

These are recalled and not used in any proof.

- **`BS(1,2) = Z[1/2] ⋊ Z`, `eta` the projection.** Only one of `[±eta]` is in `Σ^1`.
  - `F^+ = Z[1/2]^2 ⋊ diag(2,2)` is an ascending HNN extension of `Z^2` and is finitely presented.
  - `F^- = Z[1/2]^2 ⋊ diag(2,1/2)` has both directions bad and is not tame, so it is not finitely presented
    (Bieri–Strebel).
  - This matches the one-sided / two-sided dichotomy.
- **`F_2 = <x, s>`, `eta` the exponent sum in `s`.** Both `[±eta]` are bad. `F^+` and `F^-` are not finitely
  presented.
- **`E_2` itself.** `E_2^0 = FSym(N) ⋊ <(s,s^-1)> ≅ H_2` has index 2, and `(s,s^-1)` is a translation in zigzag
  coordinates.
  - The germ group `Z` is f.p., but `H_2` is not.
  - So the lifting gate G3 is not formal. This is the content of the open node
    `mz-germ-finite-presentation-lifts-to-the-envelope`.

## 6. Side idea (spark, unverified)

The "diamond spiral" enumeration of `Z^2` rotates the square spiral by 45 degrees:
- the open quadrants flow in directions `(1,-1), (-1,-1), (-1,1), (1,1)`;
- the axis rays carry the transitions.

Its slide looks like an axis-orthant piecewise translation. The heuristic prediction is that `R_nu` is metabelian,
with a row/column line module `Z[t^{±1}]` on which one generator acts by `t` and the shift by `t^-1`. That would make
the invariant two-sided and non-tame, so `R_nu` would not be f.p.

If the pattern is general, every piecewise-translation enumeration of `Z^2` dies at (G2) through a two-sided Laurent
boundary-line module. None of this is proved here.
