# The coproduct maximality question at a Kazhdan edge: what the tree gives, and what it cannot

Lane `atiyah-coproduct-inner-rank`, 2026-09-12. Directive: decide
`kazhdan-edge-coproduct-rank-is-inner-rank`, i.e. whether
`rk_G(phi(Y)) = rho_R(Y)` for every matrix `Y` over `R = D_A *_(D_C) D_B`, when
`G = A *_C B` has an infinite property (T) edge.

**Outcome: not decided.** No proof and no counterexample. What is established is
a clean split of the Hughes condition into a half that is unconditional and a
half that is not, plus a proof that the natural third method -- Bass--Serre
coset and length arguments on `l^2(G)` -- cannot reach the coproduct at all.

## 0. What the claim says

By Linnell's criterion the factors' Strong Atiyah hypothesis makes `D_A`, `D_B`,
`D_C` skew fields, so `R` is a coproduct of skew fields over a common skew
subfield. For such an `R`, Cohn's theory gives a universal field of fractions
whose rank function is the inner rank `rho_R`, and `rho_R` is the *largest*
Sylvester rank function on `R`: any `R`-field `E` has `rho_E <= rho_R`, because
`Y = PQ` through `r` columns forces rank at most `r` in every homomorphic image.

So the claim is exactly:

```text
D_G is the universal field of fractions of R,
```

equivalently every full matrix over `R` is invertible over `D_G`. It is
strictly stronger than Strong Atiyah for `G`, which needs only that `phi^* rk_G`
be the rank function of *some* epic `R`-field.

## 1. The freeness half is unconditional

**Lemma 1** (`subgroup-cosets-are-independent-over-division-closure`). For any
`H <= G`, elements `a_i in U(H)` and `x_i in G` in distinct right cosets `H x_i`,
`sum_i a_i x_i = 0` forces every `a_i = 0`. Hence
`span_(D_H)(G) = (+)_(Hg) D_H g` is free.

*Proof.* `N(H)` is finite, so `U(H)` is its Ore localization at
non-zero-divisors and the `a_i` have a common left denominator: `a_i = b^(-1) c_i`
with `b, c_i in N(H)` and `b` injective. Applying `b` gives
`sum c_i x_i = 0` with bounded coefficients. Evaluating at `delta_e`, the vector
`c_i lambda(x_i) delta_e = (c_i delta_e) * delta_(x_i)` is supported in `H x_i`,
and distinct cosets give orthogonal subspaces of `l^2(G)`. So each
`c_i delta_(x_i) = 0`, hence `c_i delta_e = 0`, hence `c_i = 0` because
`delta_e` is separating. QED

The common-denominator step is what makes this work for unbounded coefficients:
an element of `U(H)` need not have `delta_e` in its domain. (Example: `1 + z`
vanishes at one point of the circle, so `(1+s)^(-1)` is affiliated but its
formal Fourier series is not in `l^2`.)

This is the *freeness* half of the Hughes condition, and it needs nothing:
no amenability, no indicability, no Strong Atiyah. Property (T) of the edge
blocks the Hughes-free framework through the *uniqueness* half only
(`atiyah-kazhdan-edge-blocks-both-permanence-routes`: finite abelianization, so
`G` is not locally indicable and Hughes' uniqueness theorem does not apply).
The lemma is standard; it is recorded because the two halves are elsewhere
treated as one.

## 2. The coproduct escapes the coset decomposition

**Lemma 2** (`vertex-division-closure-escapes-edge-coset-span`). Let `C <= A`
and let `s in A` normalize `C` with infinite order in `N_A(C)/C`. Then
`(1+s)^(-1) in D_A` but `(1+s)^(-1) not in span_(D_C)(A)`.

*Proof.* Fourier transform on `<s>` turns `1 + lambda(s)` into multiplication by
`1 + z`, whose zero set is null, so it is invertible in `U(A)` and its inverse
lies in `D_A`. By Lemma 1 the span of `<C,s>` is the skew Laurent ring
`D_C[s^(+-1); sigma]`, where `sigma` is conjugation by `s` (a unitary, so it
preserves `U(C)` and `D_C`). Degrees add on products of nonzero elements, so the
units of that ring are the monomials `d s^k`; `1 + s` is not one. QED

**Consequence.** `R` is generated by `D_A` and `D_B`, and by Lemma 2 a
generating set of `R` already lies outside `span_(D_C)(G)`. Every method the
Bass--Serre tree supplies -- orthogonality of coset subspaces, translation
length, finite-support normal forms in `K[G]` -- sees only `span_(D_C)(G)`. So
the tree cannot decide injectivity of `phi`, let alone maximality. The escape is
not a property (T) phenomenon: it happens already for `C` amenable, where the
conjecture is known by other means.

This is the cost of the reduction `atiyah-amalgam-rank-from-cohn-coproduct`. It
buys an elementary statement about one ring homomorphism, and it pays by
handing over a ring whose elements have no `l^2`-geometric meaning.

## 3. Scope: the hypotheses are not known to be satisfiable

The claim assumes `A`, `B`, `C` satisfy Strong Atiyah with `C` an infinite
property (T) group. No infinite torsion-free property (T) group is currently
known to satisfy Strong Atiyah, and two of the standard positive classes
provably exclude them:

- **Locally indicable / Hughes-free (Jaikin-Zapirain).** A finitely generated
  property (T) group has `H^1(G; R) = 0`, hence finite abelianization, hence no
  surjection onto `Z`. So it is not locally indicable.
- **RFRS (Jaikin-Zapirain, virtually special groups).** In an RFRS chain
  `G = G_0 >= G_1 >= ...` with trivial intersection, if some `G_i` had finite
  abelianization then `G_(i+1) = G_i` and the chain would stabilize at a
  nontrivial group. So an infinite RFRS group has a finite-index subgroup with
  infinite abelianization, which property (T) forbids since (T) passes to
  finite-index subgroups.

Linnell's class `C` is not addressed here.

Consequence for the lane: the claim can be neither tested against a known
example nor attacked by exhibiting a counterexample matrix over a known host.
It remains meaningful as a conditional, which is how the Fournier--Facio
application uses it (`fournier-facio-one-compressor-subgroup-reduces-to-gamma`,
`one-compressor-support-atiyah-reduces-to-base`: Strong Atiyah for the host
reduces to the open case of `Gamma`).

## 4. What a proof would now have to do

- Supply a uniqueness theorem for division rings of fractions that are free over
  the edge closure, without local indicability. Freeness itself is free
  (Lemma 1); uniqueness is the whole content.
- Work inside `R` rather than inside `l^2(G)` (Lemma 2), so the input has to be
  ring-theoretic: fullness of matrices over a coproduct of skew fields, and a
  reason why `phi` keeps full matrices full.
- Or retreat from maximality to the weaker sufficient statement that
  `phi^* rk_G` is the rank function of some epic `R`-field, which is all that
  `atiyah-amalgam-rank-from-cohn-coproduct` (3) needs.

## 5. Attempts that were tried and are not recorded as claims

- **Free-product sanity check.** For `C = 1` the claim is Lewin's theorem, and
  it holds; the fir structure of the coproduct, not any property of the edge, is
  what drives it. This says the target is not absurd, but gives no mechanism at
  a nontrivial edge.
- **Inheritance.** Maximality for `(A, B)` ought to descend to `A' *_C B'` for
  intermediate `C <= A' <= A`, which would show the claim implies Strong Atiyah
  for a whole family. The step needs fullness to be preserved by the coproduct
  embedding `D_(A') *_(D_C) D_(B') -> R`, which is a Cohn inertia statement not
  verified here, so nothing is claimed.
- **`L^2`-homology of the edge.** Property (T) gives `b_1^(2)(C) = 0`, i.e.
  vanishing of `Tor_1` against the trivial module only; the vertex-mixing
  computation needs `Tor_1` against restricted modules. Already recorded on
  `kazhdan-edge-coproduct-rank-is-inner-rank`.
