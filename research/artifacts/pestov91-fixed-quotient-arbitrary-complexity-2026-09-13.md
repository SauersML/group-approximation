# Arbitrary word-problem degree with the same kernel and maximal MF quotient

2026-09-13. Written proof, not Lean-verified.

Fix the computable irrational `alpha=sqrt(2)-1` and its binary Sturmian
subshift `Y`. Put

`R_Y=LC(Y,F_2) semidirect Z`, `Q=EL_6(R_Y)`,
`L=SL_infinity(F_2)` with ordinary identity-block inclusions.

The group `Q` is infinite simple Kazhdan LEF and has decidable word
problem. It is fixed throughout this note. The abstract kernel `L` is
also fixed. Varying the joining point, rather than the endpoint systems,
produces every Turing degree without changing either of these groups.

## Theorem

For every Turing degree `d` there is a Kazhdan group `H_d` generated by
three elements of orders `7,2,2` such that

`1 -> L -> H_d -> Q x Q -> 1`,
`Rad_MF(H_d)=L`, `deg_T WP(H_d)=d`.

Every `H_d` has exactly five normal subgroups, has no nontrivial finite
quotient, and is just non-MF with every proper quotient LEF. The
isomorphism types of all its proper quotients are the same fixed list
`Q x Q`, `Q`, and `1`. It is centerless, directly indecomposable and
Hopfian, but is not finitely presented.

In particular there are continuum many pairwise nonisomorphic such
extensions with the same abstract kernel, the same maximal MF quotient,
and the same normal lattice. Both kernel and quotient have decidable
word problems in their usual effective descriptions; all arbitrary
complexity is carried by the extension. This does not give a uniform
computable presentation of the extensions.

## 1. Keep the slope fixed and vary only the intercept

For `beta in (0,1)` avoiding the countable set

`D={-n alpha mod 1:n in Z}`,

define the two-sided mechanical sequence

`y^beta_j=floor((j+1)alpha+beta)-floor(j alpha+beta)`.

All these sequences belong to the same fixed subshift `Y`. The standard
rotation description, also used in the existing Sturmian word-problem
proof, makes `Y` infinite and minimal. Its language is computable:
for length `n`, sort the finitely many distinct computable circle cuts
`-j alpha mod 1`, `0<=j<=n`, and read the coding on each intervening
interval. There are no singleton words to add at cuts, since the
half-open coding there agrees with a neighboring interval.

For completeness the nonnegative itinerary has exactly the degree of
the intercept:

`(y^beta_j)_(j>=0) equivalent_T beta`.                   (1)

The forward computation uses strict comparisons with the computable
cuts; `beta notin D` makes each comparison terminate. Conversely, set

`S_n=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) y^beta_j=floor(n alpha+beta)`.

The first `n` bits give the constraint

`S_n-n alpha <= beta < S_n+1-n alpha`.

Intersect these intervals and `[0,1]` for `1<=n<=N`. Density of the
irrational rotation implies that the endpoints approach `beta` from
both sides: the threshold values `1-{n alpha}` are dense. Thus the
intersection widths tend to zero. Using computable enclosing rational
bounds for the finitely many endpoints, search for an enclosure of
width less than `2^(-m)` to compute `beta` to precision `2^(-m)`.
This proves (1), without any recurrence-rate assumption on `beta`.

Every Turing degree is represented by some allowed `beta`. For the
computable degree take `beta=1/2`, which is outside `D`. For any
noncomputable degree take a real in `(0,1)` of that degree; every point
of `D` is computable, so it is automatically outside `D`.

## 2. The seam language records exactly that itinerary

Fix `y^-=y^(1/2)` once and for all. Define the tagged seam

`z^beta_j=(y^-_j,0)` for `j<0`,
`z^beta_j=(y^beta_j,1)` for `j>=0`,

and let `X_beta` be its orbit closure. Its two minimal components are
the same copies of `Y`, with constant phase zero and one. Only the
isolated joining orbit varies.

A finite tagged word belongs to `L(X_beta)` exactly as follows.

- If its phase is constant, its payload must belong to the fixed
  computable language `L(Y)`.
- If its phase has a `10` transition, it is forbidden.
- Otherwise its phase is `0^a 1^b`, with `a,b>0`. Its payload must be
  exactly `y^-_(-a)...y^-_(-1) y^beta_0...y^beta_(b-1)`.

These cases prove `L(X_beta) <=_T (y^beta_j)_(j>=0)`. For the reverse
reduction, let `a=y^-_(-1)`, a fixed computable bit. Query the language
on all words

`(a,0)(v_0,1)...(v_(n-1),1)`, `v in {0,1}^n`.

Exactly one is allowed, because the phase transition locates the seam
uniquely. Its payload `v` is the length-`n` nonnegative itinerary. Hence

`L(X_beta) equivalent_T (y^beta_j)_(j>=0) equivalent_T beta`. (2)

Notice that a phase-constant word near the seam still belongs to one
of its endpoint sequences. Thus the first case neither misses nor
adds any words from the isolated orbit.

## 3. Binary interleaving preserves the language degree effectively

For `x_j=(b_j,p_j)`, put `J(x)_(2j)=b_j` and `J(x)_(2j+1)=p_j`, and
let `Z_beta=J(X_beta) union T J(X_beta)`. This is the recognizable
binary coding from the prescribed-endpoint theorem. Here its parity
can be recognized with one computable bound independent of `beta`.

Minimality supplies `B` such that every length-`B` word of `Y` contains
`10`. Such a `B` is computable: test the finitely many allowed words
using the decidable language until all contain `10`. The word `10`
occurs in `Y`, and its returns are uniformly recurrent, so this search
terminates. Every length-`2B` payload block in any `X_beta` contains
`10`: a block crossing the seam has at least `B` letters on one side.
Every phase block is nondecreasing and contains no `10`.

Consequently, in a length-`4B` binary block, exactly one parity
subsequence contains `10`: it is the payload parity. This identifies
the pairing effectively and uniformly in `beta`.

Here are explicit language reductions. To query a binary word of
length `ell`, enumerate tagged words of length `ceil((ell+1)/2)` and
ask `L(X_beta)`; test the requested word as the length-`ell` prefix of
their interleaving, with either zero or one initial bit discarded.
This decides `L(Z_beta)` from `L(X_beta)`.

Conversely, to query a tagged word `v` of length `n`, enumerate its
right extensions `w` of length `m=max(n,2B)` whose payload contains
`10` and whose phase is nondecreasing. Ask whether any `J(w)` belongs
to `L(Z_beta)`. An allowed extension of `v` necessarily passes these
filters. A positive answer cannot occur in the wrong parity, since
the even subsequence of `J(w)` contains `10`, whereas the phase
subsequence never does. Thus a positive answer decodes to an actual
occurrence of `w` in `X_beta`. This proves

`L(Z_beta) equivalent_T L(X_beta)`.                     (3)

## 4. Transfer the degree without changing the quotient

The two clopen parity cells give

`R_(Z_beta) ~= M_2(R_(X_beta))`,
`H_beta=EL_3(R_(Z_beta)) ~= EL_6(R_(X_beta))`.

Use the fixed three torsion-generator formulas `(c,t,w)` on the binary
side. The quadratic cylinder witnesses and finite-window normal-form
algorithm apply to every nonempty binary subshift, including these
nonminimal ones. They give

`WP(H_beta) equivalent_T L(Z_beta) equivalent_T beta`.   (4)

The prescribed-endpoint and normal-lattice theorems apply to `X_beta`.
Its endpoint systems are always `Y,Y`, so they give the same `L` and
`Q x Q`, the same five normal subgroups and all the stated group
properties. The normal-form algorithm over the computable language
`L(Y)` also proves that `Q=EL_6(R_Y)` has decidable word problem. The
finitary matrix description makes the word problem of `L` decidable
in its standard countable effective generating family.

Isomorphic finitely generated groups have Turing-equivalent word
problems: express each finite generating set as fixed words in the
other and substitute. Distinct degrees in (4) therefore give distinct
abstract isomorphism types. Every Turing degree is countable as a set
of subsets of the integers, so there are continuum many degrees and
hence continuum many isomorphism types here.

## 5. No member is finitely presented

For `beta notin D`, any fixed finite prefix of its nonnegative
itinerary is unchanged throughout a sufficiently small open interval
of intercepts. Section 2 shows that this fixes the seam language
through the same length; section 3 gives agreement of binary languages
through any prescribed length after increasing the prefix length.
The finite-window relation theorem then gives agreement of all group
relations up to any prescribed finite length. One can always choose
an allowed `gamma!=beta` in that interval.

If `H_beta` were finitely presented in its three marked generators,
choose a finite defining relator list and its maximum length. A
distinct sufficiently close `gamma` satisfies all those relators, so
there is a marked epimorphism `H_beta -> H_gamma`. Both groups are
non-MF. Since every proper quotient of `H_beta` is MF, this epimorphism
must be injective and hence a marked isomorphism. The cylinder
witnesses then give `L(Z_beta)=L(Z_gamma)`. The effective parity
decoder gives `L(X_beta)=L(X_gamma)`, and the unique-seam queries give
identical nonnegative itineraries. Equation (1) forces `beta=gamma`,
a contradiction. This proves that no `H_beta` is finitely presented.

The same proper-quotient argument proves Hopficity: a surjective
endomorphism with nontrivial kernel would make the non-MF group `H_beta`
isomorphic to an MF proper quotient.

## Scope and verification

The word-problem degrees are invariants of finite markings; they are
used here only up to Turing equivalence, not as time-complexity bounds.
The fixed quotient does not determine the extension, even when its
kernel, full normal lattice and torsion marking orders are also fixed.
No claim that all different intercepts give different unmarked groups
is needed; distinct degrees suffice for the cardinality assertion.

This is a written mathematical deduction from the seam, normal-lattice
and binary cylinder-witness results. No Lean formalization or global
priority assertion is claimed.
