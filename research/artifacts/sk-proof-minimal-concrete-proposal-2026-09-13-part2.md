\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

\subsection*{The ring and property (T)}
Write $e_U$ for the indicator of a clopen set $U\subseteq X$. Our
conventions are $(Tx)_n=x_{n+1}$ and
\[
 R=\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes_T\Z
   =\left\{\sum_j f_j u^j:\text{finite sums}\right\},
 \qquad ufu^{-1}=f\circ T^{-1},
\]
where $\LC$ denotes locally constant functions. Thus $ue_Uu^{-1}=e_{TU}$,
and $R$ is generated by $u^{\pm1}$ and the letter indicators
$e_a=e_{\{x:x_0=a\}}$, since $u^{-t}e_au^t=e_{\{x:x_t=a\}}$.
Put $G=\EL_3(R)$, $e_{ij}(r)=I_3+rE_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$, and
$[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$. The identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elementary}
 e_{ij}(r)e_{ij}(s)=e_{ij}(r+s),\qquad
 [e_{ik}(r),e_{kj}(s)]=e_{ij}(rs)\quad(i,j,k\text{ distinct})
\end{equation}
show that $G$ is generated by the $e_{ij}(s)$ with
$s\in\{u,u^{-1}\}\cup\{e_a:a\in A\}$, because $\sum_ae_a=1$.
Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain proved that $\EL_n$ of every finitely
generated unital associative ring has property~(T) for
$n\ge3$~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}. So $G$ has property~(T). It is infinite
because $e_{12}(\LC(X,\F_2))$ is infinite.

\subsection*{Finite models}
A group is LEF if every finite subset embeds injectively into a finite
group, preserving all products that stay in that subset.

Fix $\ell$. By minimality every word of $X$ of length $2\ell+1$ occurs in
every long enough word of $X$. So a point of $X$ contains two occurrences
of a word $v$ of length $2\ell$ at a distance $L$ that can be taken as
large as we like. Let $w$ be the word of length $L$ that begins at the
first occurrence. Then $wv$ occurs in $X$ and $w$ contains every word of
$X$ of length $2\ell+1$, so the periodic sequence $y=w^\infty$ has exactly
the same words of length $2\ell+1$ as $X$.

Let $P\delta_n=\delta_{n+1}$ on $\F_2^{\Z/L\Z}$. If $f\in\LC(X,\F_2)$
depends only on the coordinates in $[-\rho,\rho]$, where $\rho\le\ell$,
let $D(f)$ be the diagonal matrix whose $n$-th entry is the value of $f$
on the word $y_{n-\rho}\cdots y_{n+\rho}$. Send
\[
  \sum_j f_ju^j\ \longmapsto\ \sum_j D(f_j)P^j.
\]
Since $P^iD(f)P^{-i}=D(f\circ T^{-i})$, this map preserves the sums and
products among finitely many elements of $R$ once $\ell$ exceeds the
windows of all coefficients involved, including the shifted ones
$f\circ T^{-i}$. Every allowed window occurs in $y$, so nonzero
coefficients have nonzero images. If $L$ exceeds twice every exponent,
nonzero elements also have nonzero images.

For a finite set $F\subseteq G$, choose $\ell$ and $L$ large enough for
the entries of $F\cup F^{-1}$, their differences, and the sums and
products used in multiplying these matrices. Applying the map entrywise
gives an injective map $F\to\GL_{3L}(\F_2)$ preserving the products that
stay in $F$. So $G$ is LEF, hence sofic and hyperlinear~\cite{Pestov}.

\subsection*{Towers}
Let $C\subseteq X$ be clopen with $C\cap T^jC=\varnothing$ for
$0<|j|\le2m$. For $|a|,|b|\le m$ and clopen $W\subseteq C$, put
$E_{ab}(W)=e_{T^aW}u^{a-b}$. Then
$E_{ab}(W)E_{cd}(W')=\delta_{bc}E_{ad}(W\cap W')$, so these elements span
a subring $B_m(C)\cong M_{2m+1}(\LC(C,\F_2))$. Multiplying $E_{ab}(W)$ on
the left by $fu^i$ and on the right by $f'u^j$ gives $E_{a+i,b-j}(W')$ for
a clopen $W'\subseteq W$. So if $r,r'\in R$ involve only powers $u^j$ with
$|j|\le w$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:absorb}
  rB_{m-w}(C)\,r'\subseteq B_m(C).
\end{equation}
Put $d=3(2m+1)$ and identify $M_3(B_m(C))$ with $\LC(C,M_d(\F_2))$. The
$k\in\GL_3(R)$ such that $k-I_3$ and $k^{-1}-I_3$ lie in $M_3(B_m(C))$
form a group, and $k\mapsto I_d+(k-I_3)$ identifies it with
$\LC(C,\GL_d(\F_2))$. For clopen $W\subseteq C$, the functions equal to
$I_d$ off $W$ and constant on $W$ form a copy $H_W$ of $\GL_d(\F_2)$.
The transvections $I_d+E_{xy}$ generate $\GL_d(\F_2)$. Index the
coordinates by $(p,a)$, where $1\le p\le3$ and $|a|\le m$. For $p\ne q$ the
transvection of $H_W$ between $(p,a)$ and $(q,b)$ is $e_{pq}(E_{ab}(W))$,
and the others are commutators of these, by
$[I_d+E_{xy},I_d+E_{yz}]=I_d+E_{xz}$. So $H_W\subseteq G$.

\subsection*{Simplicity}
The center of $R$ is $\F_2$~\cite{BCFS,ClarkEdie,Steinberg}. Indeed, if
$c=\sum_jf_ju^j$ is central and $j\ne0$, the coefficient
$f_j(e_{T^jV}-e_V)$ of $u^j$ in $ce_V-e_Vc$ vanishes for every clopen $V$,
and a clopen $V$ that contains $x$ but not $T^{-j}x$ gives $f_j(x)=0$.
Then $cu=uc$ makes $f_0$ invariant, hence constant by minimality.

Let $1\ne N\trianglelefteq G$ and choose $1\ne g\in N$. Let $w$ bound the
absolute values of the exponents in the entries of $g$ and $g^{-1}$, and
put $m=w+1$. Since $X$ is minimal and infinite, $T$ has no periodic
points, so every point has a clopen neighborhood $V$ with
$V\cap T^jV=\varnothing$ for $0<|j|\le2m$. By compactness there is a
finite clopen partition $\mathcal P$ refining the letter partition whose
pieces $C$ have this property. Some $h=e_{ij}(s)$ with
$s\in\{e_C,e_Cu:C\in\mathcal P\}$ does not commute with $g$. Otherwise
$g$ commutes with every $e_{ij}(1)=\prod_Ce_{ij}(e_C)$, so $g=cI_3$ with
$c\in R$. Then $c$ commutes with every $e_C$ and $e_Cu$, hence with
$u^{\pm1}$ and every $e_a$. So $c\in Z(R)^\times=\{1\}$, a contradiction.

Put $k=[g,h]\in N\setminus\{1\}$ and $S=sE_{ij}$, so $h^{\pm1}=I_3+S$ and
\[
 k-I_3=(gSg^{-1}-S)(I_3+S),\qquad k^{-1}-I_3=(I_3+S)(gSg^{-1}-S).
\]
Since $s=E_{00}(C)$ or $s=E_{0,-1}(C)$ lies in $B_1(C)$, \eqref{eq:absorb}
puts both matrices in $M_3(B_m(C))$. So $k$ corresponds to a locally
constant $\kappa:C\to\GL_d(\F_2)$ with $\kappa\ne I_d$, and $\kappa$ is a
constant $\kappa_0\ne I_d$ on some nonempty clopen $W\subseteq C$.
Conjugation by $k$ acts on $H_W$ as conjugation by $\kappa_0$. The group
$\GL_d(\F_2)=\PSL_d(\F_2)$ is simple and centerless, as $d\ge9$. So
$[k,H_W]$ is a nontrivial subset of $N\cap H_W$, the normal subgroup
$N\cap H_W$ of $H_W$ equals $H_W$, and $e_{ij}(e_W)\in N$ for all $i\ne j$.

Finally, $I_N=\{r\in R:e_{ij}(r)\in N\text{ for all }i\ne j\}$ is a
two-sided ideal by~\eqref{eq:elementary}, and it contains $e_W$. So it
contains every $e_{T^nW}=u^ne_Wu^{-n}$. By minimality and compactness
finitely many of these sets cover $X$, so
$1=1-\prod_i(1-e_{T^{n_i}W})\in I_N$. Thus $N$ contains every $e_{ij}(r)$,
and $N=G$.\hfill$\square$
