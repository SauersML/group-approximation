# Mixing nonsofic actions from a nonsofic lattice in a product of two tree groups

Lane `ex-nonsofic-action-entropy`, 2026-09-12. Handwritten proofs, nothing run.
Supports `product-of-tree-groups-has-factorwise-howe-moore` and
`tree-product-lattice-nonsofic-gives-mixing-nonsofic-action`. Builds on
`paunescu-class-measure-equivalence-2026-09-12.md`. Status: proved here, unreviewed.

The peer theorem `howe-moore-lattice-nonsofic-action-transfers-to-mixing` needs a
Howe--Moore envelope. Irreducible lattices in products of trees have envelopes
`G_1 × G_2`, which are not Howe--Moore: the factor `G_1` acts trivially on
`G/G_1`. What survives is a factorwise version, and it is enough.

## 0. Source: C. Ciobotaru, *A unified proof of the Howe--Moore property*, arXiv:1403.0223v2

Read from the PDF, pages 1 to 9 and 16 to 19.

* p. 5, **Lemma 2.9.** *"Let G be a second countable, locally compact group admitting a
  decomposition G = K_1 A K_2, where K_1, K_2 are compact subset and A is any subset of
  G. Let (π, ℋ) be a unitary representation of G. If for every sequence
  {a_n}_{n>0} ⊂ A, with {a_n}_{n>0} → ∞, the corresponding matrix coefficients are C_0,
  then all matrix coefficients of G, with respect to (π, ℋ), vanish at ∞."*
* p. 6, **Definition 2.11.** For a sequence `α = {g_n}`,
  `U_α^+ := {g ∈ G | lim g_n^{-1} g g_n = e}`, and `U_α^-` with `g_n g g_n^{-1}`;
  `N_α^± = closure(U_α^±)`.
* p. 7, **Lemma 2.13.** A sequence of pairwise commuting unitaries on a separable Hilbert
  space has a weakly convergent subsequence, and every such weak limit is normal.
* p. 8, **Lemma 3.1.** *"Let G be a second countable, locally compact group and (π, ℋ) be
  a unitary representation of G, without G-invariant vectors. Assume there exists a
  sequence α = {g_n}_{n>0} of pairwise commuting elements of G such that g_n → ∞ and
  G = closure⟨U_α^+, U_α^-⟩. Then all matrix coefficients of (π, ℋ) corresponding to
  {g_n}_{n>0} are C_0."* The proof: if `|⟨π(g_n)v, w⟩| ↛ 0`, pass to a weak limit
  `E ≠ 0` of `π(g_n)`; `E` is normal (2.13); `π(u_+) E π(u_-) = E` for
  `u_± ∈ U_α^±`; so `E(ℋ)` is `U_α^+`-invariant and `E^*(ℋ)` is `U_α^-`-invariant, and a
  nonzero `ψ ∈ closure E(ℋ) ∩ closure E^*(ℋ)` is `⟨N_α^+, N_α^-⟩`-invariant.
* p. 9, **Theorem 3.2** (criterion): a `K_1 A^+ K_2` decomposition with `A^+` an abelian
  semigroup, and every sequence in `A^+` tending to `∞` having a subsequence `β` with
  `G = closure⟨U_β^+, U_β^-⟩`, imply the Howe--Moore property.
* p. 16, **Corollary 4.18.** For `G` closed, strongly transitive and type preserving, and
  `a` hyperbolic, `closure⟨U_a^+, U_a^-⟩` is normal in `G`.
* p. 19, **proof of Theorem 4.19** for `G` a closed, topologically simple subgroup of
  `Aut(T)` acting 2-transitively on `∂T`, `T` bi-regular of valence `>= 3`: condition (i)
  of Theorem 3.2 by Lemma 4.7; *"in the case of a bi-regular tree, the corresponding group
  A^+ of G, given by the polar decomposition of G, is generated by a single hyperbolic
  element a ∈ G (see Example 4.10). Therefore, the second condition follows immediately
  from Corollary 4.18 applied to G."*

Call such `G` a **boundary-2-transitive simple tree group**. `Aut(T_d)^+`, the
type-preserving subgroup of index two, is one (Tits simplicity; its action on `∂T_d` is
2-transitive).

## 1. Factorwise Howe--Moore for products

**Proposition 1.1.** Let `G_1`, `G_2` be boundary-2-transitive simple tree groups and
`G = G_1 × G_2`. Let `(π, ℋ)` be a unitary representation of `G` with no nonzero
`G_1`-invariant vector and no nonzero `G_2`-invariant vector. Then every matrix
coefficient of `π` vanishes at infinity on `G`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2.4 of the source we may take `ℋ` separable.
1. *Decomposition.* Lemma 4.7 gives `G_i = K_i A_i^+ K_i'` with `K_i, K_i'` compact and
   `A_i^+ = {a_i^m : m >= 0}` for a hyperbolic `a_i` (Example 4.10). So
   `G = (K_1 × K_2)(A_1^+ × A_2^+)(K_1' × K_2')`, with `A_1^+ × A_2^+` abelian. By
   Lemma 2.9 it suffices to treat sequences `g_n = (a_1^{m_n}, a_2^{l_n}) → ∞`.
2. *One coordinate escapes.* Passing to a subsequence, `m_n → ∞` or `l_n → ∞`. Say
   `m_n → ∞`. Then `U_α^± ⊇ U_{a_1}^± × {e}`: for `u ∈ U_{a_1}^+`,
   `g_n^{-1}(u, e)g_n = (a_1^{-m_n} u a_1^{m_n}, e) → e`, and similarly for `-`.
3. *Generation.* `closure⟨U_{a_1}^+, U_{a_1}^-⟩` is a nontrivial closed normal subgroup of
   `G_1` (Corollary 4.18), hence equal to `G_1` by topological simplicity. So
   `closure⟨U_α^+, U_α^-⟩ ⊇ G_1 × {e}`.
4. *Lemma 3.1's argument.* The `g_n` commute pairwise. If some coefficient does not tend
   to `0` along `g_n`, the proof of Lemma 3.1 produces a nonzero vector invariant under
   `⟨N_α^+, N_α^-⟩ ⊇ G_1 × {e}`, contrary to the hypothesis. The generation hypothesis
   `G = closure⟨U_α^+, U_α^-⟩` of Lemma 3.1 is used there only to conclude invariance
   under the whole group; here invariance under `G_1 × {e}` already contradicts. ∎

**Corollary 1.2.** If a p.m.p. action of `G_1 × G_2` is ergodic for `G_1` and for `G_2`
separately, it is mixing for `G_1 × G_2`, and so is its restriction to every discrete
subgroup.

## 2. The transfer theorem

**Theorem 2.1.** Let `L = G_1 × G_2` with `G_i` boundary-2-transitive simple tree groups.
Let `Γ <= L` be a lattice whose projections `p_i(Γ)` are dense in `G_i`, and assume `Γ`
is not sofic. Put `B = ({0,1}, fair)^Γ`, `W = L ×_Γ B = (L × B)/Γ` with
`(l, b)·γ = (lγ, γ^{-1}b)`, and let `L` act on `W` by left multiplication. Then for every
lattice `Λ <= L` the action `Λ ↷ W` is essentially free, mixing and not sofic.

*Proof.*
1. *Coupling.* `Ω = L × B` with `Λ` acting on the left of `L` and `Γ` acting by
   `(l, b) ↦ (lγ, γ^{-1}b)` is a measure equivalence coupling: the actions commute,
   preserve `Haar ⊗ fair`, are free, and have fundamental domains `D_Λ × B`, `D_Γ × B` of
   finite measure. Its quotients are `Λ ↷ Ω/Γ = W` and `Γ ↷ Λ\Ω = (Λ\L) × B`.
2. *Freeness.* `Γ ↷ (Λ\L) × B` is free because `Γ ↷ B` is. If `λ[l, b] = [l, b]` then
   `λl = lγ` and `γ^{-1}b = b` for some `γ`; off a null set of `b` this forces `γ = e`,
   hence `λ = e`.
3. *Nonsoficity.* A nonsofic group has no sofic p.m.p. action, so `Γ ↷ (Λ\L) × B` is not
   sofic. Both quotient orbit relations are restrictions of the orbit relation of
   `Λ × Γ` on `Ω` to fundamental domains meeting every class (proof of Lemma 3.1(4) of
   the measure-equivalence artifact). By `sofic-free-actions-are-soe-invariant`,
   `Λ ↷ W` is not sofic.
4. *Ergodicity of each factor.* A `G_1`-invariant function on `W` lifts to `F` on
   `L × B` with `F(g_1 l γ, γ^{-1}b) = F(l, b)`, i.e. to a function on
   `G_1\L × B = G_2 × B` invariant under `(x, b) ↦ (x p_2(γ), γ^{-1} b)`. Write
   `F = F_0 + F_1` with `F_0(x) = ∫ F(x, b) db`. `F_0` is invariant under right
   translation by the dense subgroup `p_2(Γ)`, hence under `G_2` (continuity of
   translation in `L^1_loc`), hence constant. `φ(x) = F_1(x, ·) ∈ L^2_0(B)` is bounded,
   measurable, and satisfies `φ(x p_2(γ)) = κ(γ) φ(x)` with `κ` the Koopman
   representation of `Γ` on `L^2_0(B)`, which is mixing. Choose distinct `γ_n ∈ Γ`
   with `p_2(γ_n) → e` (density, `G_2` non-discrete); then `γ_n → ∞` in `Γ`. Continuity
   of translation gives a subsequence with `φ(x p_2(γ_n)) → φ(x)` in norm for almost
   every `x`, while `κ(γ_n) φ(x) → 0` weakly. So `φ = 0` and `F` is constant. The same
   for `G_2`.
5. *Mixing.* By step 4, `L^2_0(W)` has no nonzero `G_1`- or `G_2`-invariant vector, so
   `L ↷ W` is mixing by Proposition 1.1. A sequence `λ_n → ∞` in the discrete group `Λ`
   tends to infinity in `L`. ∎

## 3. Instances

* `L^+ = Aut(T_p)^+ × Aut(T_q)^+`, `p, q >= 3`, contains the lattice
  `K_p × K_q` of `product-tree-lattices-paunescu-class-proof`: `K_p` consists of words
  with an even number of each involution, so of even length, and preserves the type of
  vertices. So: **if some lattice of `Aut(T_p)^+ × Aut(T_q)^+` with dense projections is
  not sofic, the product of two free groups `K_p × K_q` has an essentially free mixing
  nonsofic action.** `K_p × K_q` is residually finite, which answers
  `mixing-free-nonsofic-action-of-sofic-group`.
* For Radu's lattice `Γ_R <= Aut(T_3) × Aut(T_3)`, the finite-index subgroup
  `Γ_R^+ = Γ_R ∩ L^+` is a lattice of `L^+`, nonsofic if `Γ_R` is. Density of its
  projections in `Aut(T_3)^+` is not established here.
* The theorem needs `Γ` nonsofic, not merely outside `𝒮`: with a nontrivial ergodic seed
  `Z`, step 4 would need ergodicity of `Γ ↷ G_2 × Z × B`, which the argument does not give.
