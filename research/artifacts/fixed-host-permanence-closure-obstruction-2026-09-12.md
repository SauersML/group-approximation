# Simple nonsofic FA atoms: where the surjunctivity permanence machinery cannot reach

Lane `gk-fixed-host`, 2026-09-12. Supports the Cairn claims
`sofic-permanence-closure-omits-simple-fa-nonsofic-groups`,
`fixed-host-outside-sofic-permanence-closure` and `sofic-groups-are-surjunctive`, and
the dead route `fixed-host-surjunctive-via-sofic-permanence`.

## 1. How the tester host U is built

`whitehead-universal-container-via-enumeration-and-rope` builds `U` in four steps.

1. Enumerate every countably generated recursively enumerable presentation `P_i`, and
   form `Q = *_i P_i`, a countably generated recursively presented group.
2. Two-generator recursive compression: `Q -> E`, with `E` two-generated and
   recursively presented.
3. A torsion-order-preserving Higman embedding: `E -> U_0` with `U_0` finitely presented.
4. The finite-presentation compression: `U_0 -> U`, with `U` two-generated and
   finitely presented.

Every arrow is an embedding. So `U` contains a copy of every countably generated
recursively presented group, in particular of every finitely presented group. Only this
universality enters `gottschalk-surjunctivity-fixed-two-generator-tester`. The Whitehead
and torsion bookkeeping of the construction plays no role for surjunctivity.

## 2. The tester reduction holds, one alphabet at a time

I re-derived the four steps of `gottschalk-surjunctivity-fixed-tester-proof`.

* **Locality and subgroups.** Take memory `M` inside `H = <M>`. The rule
  `tau(x)(g) = mu((x(gm))_(m in M))` reads only inside the left coset `gH`. So
  `A^G = prod_(gH) A^(gH)`, and `tau` acts on each coset as a copy of `tau_H`, through
  `h -> g_0 h` for a chosen representative `g_0`. Injectivity and surjectivity transfer
  in both directions. The step is correct.
* **Marked limits.** Compactness gives a finite injectivity detector `N`, and closedness
  of the image gives a missing cylinder `F`. Both transport once a finite ball holds
  `M`, `N`, `F` and the products needed to evaluate the rule on `N` and `F`. The step is
  correct.
* **Directed colimits with noninjective maps.** An equality of the colimit appears at a
  finite stage, and an inequality holds at every stage. So the marked subgroups
  generated by lifted generators converge to the colimit's subgroup. The step is correct.
* **Universality.** Every group is the directed colimit of the finitely presented
  groups `<S | R>` on finite sets of its elements and relations true in it. Each of these
  embeds in `U`. The step is correct.

No step changes the alphabet. So, for every finite alphabet `A`, `U` is `A`-surjunctive
if and only if every group is `A`-surjunctive.

Universality forces nothing in either direction. A strict automaton passes up to
overgroups through the coset product, and surjunctivity passes down to subgroups. So "U
carries a strict automaton" says exactly "some group does".

## 3. Atoms that no permanence step reaches

**Theorem 3.1.** Let `Sigma` be a finitely presented simple group with Serre's property
FA that is not sofic. Let `D_Sigma` be the class of groups with no subgroup isomorphic to
`Sigma`. Then:

* **(a)** every sofic group lies in `D_Sigma`;
* **(b)** `D_Sigma` is closed under subgroups;
* **(c)** `D_Sigma` is closed under directed colimits with arbitrary structure maps;
* **(d)** `D_Sigma` is closed under extensions: if `N` is normal in `E` and both `N` and
  `E/N` lie in `D_Sigma`, then `E` lies in `D_Sigma`;
* **(e)** `D_Sigma` is closed under fundamental groups of graphs of groups whose vertex
  groups lie in `D_Sigma`, for any graph, any edge groups and any edge maps;
* **(f)** `D_Sigma` is closed under limits in the space of finitely generated marked
  groups.

*Proof.*

(a) A subgroup of a sofic group is sofic, and `Sigma` is not.

(b) This is immediate.

(c) Let `G = colim G_i` with maps `psi_i: G_i -> G`, and let `j: Sigma -> G` be an
embedding. Write `Sigma = <s_1,...,s_d | r_1,...,r_k>`. Choose a stage `i_0` and elements
`h_1,...,h_d` of `G_(i_0)` with `psi_(i_0)(h_a) = j(s_a)`. Each `r_b(h)` maps to
`r_b(j(s)) = 1` in `G`. An element that dies in a directed colimit dies at some stage, so
the finitely many `r_b(h)` die together at one later stage `i`. Then `s_a -> image of h_a`
defines `phi: Sigma -> G_i` with `psi_i o phi = j`. Since `j` is injective, `phi` is
injective, and `G_i` is not in `D_Sigma`. This step uses only finite presentation.

(d) Let `Sigma <= E`. The intersection `Sigma ∩ N` is normal in `Sigma`, so it is `1` or
`Sigma`. If it is `Sigma`, then `Sigma <= N`. If it is `1`, then `Sigma` maps injectively
into `E/N`. Either way a hypothesis fails. This step uses only simplicity.

(e) Let `P` be the fundamental group of a graph of groups with vertex groups `P_v` in
`D_Sigma`. It acts on its Bass--Serre tree without inversions, and the vertex stabilizers
are the conjugates of the `P_v` (Serre, *Trees*, Section I.5). If `Sigma <= P`, property FA
gives a vertex fixed by `Sigma` (Serre, Section I.6). So `Sigma` lies in a conjugate of
some `P_v`, a contradiction. This step uses only FA.

(f) Let marked groups `(H_n, h^(n))` in `D_Sigma` converge to `(G, g)`, and suppose
`Sigma <= G` is generated by words `w_1(g),...,w_d(g)`, in the generator order of a
finite presentation of `Sigma`. Put `K_n = <w_1(h^(n)),...,w_d(h^(n))> <= H_n`. For
large `n` the finitely many relators of `Sigma` hold for the `w_a(h^(n))`, so `K_n` is a
quotient of `Sigma`. Since `w_1(g) != 1` in `G`, also `w_1(h^(n)) != 1` for large `n`, so
`K_n != 1`. Simplicity gives `K_n ~= Sigma`, a contradiction. This step uses finite
presentation and simplicity. QED

*Remark.* In (a) the sofic groups can be replaced by any base class none of whose members
contains `Sigma`.

**Corollary 3.2 (the landed permanence theorems).** Let `S_0` be the class generated from
sofic groups by any transfinite sequence of the following:

* subgroups and directed colimits;
* finitary split extensions `N ⋊ G` over a member `G`
  (`finitary-split-extension-surjunctivity-permanence`);
* graph folds into a member (`graph-folds-over-surjunctive-groups-are-surjunctive`);
* LEF-lamp graph wreaths over a member (`lef-lamp-graph-wreaths-are-surjunctive`);
* their special cases: permutational wreath products with residually finite lamps, and
  group doubles.

By Gromov--Weiss and those theorems, every member of `S_0` is surjunctive. Every member
also lies in `D_Sigma`, by induction over the construction:

* A finitary split extension is an extension of `N` by `G`. Axiom (A1), with residually
  finite `N_S`, makes `N` residually finite: `p_S(n) != 1` survives in a finite quotient of
  `N_S`. So `N` is sofic, and (a) and (d) apply.
* A graph fold is a graph of groups whose vertex groups embed in a member. By (b) they
  lie in `D_Sigma`, and (e) applies.
* A LEF-lamp graph wreath is an extension of `Lambda(A)` by a member. `Lambda(A)` is the
  directed union of the `Lambda_V(A)` over finite vertex sets `V`. If `V` spans a complete
  graph, `Lambda_V(A) = prod_(x in V) A_x` is an iterated extension of sofic groups.
  Otherwise choose `v` whose star `st(v)` is not all of `V`. Then
  `Lambda_V(A) = Lambda_(V\v)(A) *_(Lambda_(lk v)(A)) Lambda_(st v)(A)` with
  `Lambda_(st v)(A) = A_v x Lambda_(lk v)(A)` (Green, *Graph products of groups*, thesis,
  Leeds 1990). Induction on `|V|`, using (c), (d) and (e), puts `Lambda(A)` in `D_Sigma`.
  `A` is LEF, hence sofic.

Hence `S_0 ⊆ D_Sigma`.

## 4. The instance: the Leavitt unit group and the host U

Put `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)` and `Sigma = R^x`.

* **Finitely presented:** `leavitt-unit-group-finitely-presented` (Khanh, arXiv:2609.08428,
  Theorem 6.1).
* **Simple:** `binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-simple`.
* **Not sofic:** `openai-leavitt-unit-nonsofic`.
* **Property FA:** a complete binary prefix code with twelve leaves identifies `R` with
  `M_12(R)`. So `R^x ~= GL_12(R) = EL_12(R)` by
  `leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group`, and `EL_12(R)` is Kazhdan by
  `rank-twelve-leavitt-simple-non-mf`. Property (T) implies FA (Watatani, Math. Japon. 27
  (1982); Bekka--de la Harpe--Valette, *Kazhdan's Property (T)*, Theorem 2.12.4).

Consequences:

1. `R^x` is not in `S_0`. Iterating the landed permanence theorems from sofic groups, in
   any order, never proves `R^x` surjunctive.
2. `R^x` is finitely presented, hence recursively presented, so it embeds in `U` by
   `whitehead-universal-finitely-presented-container`. So `U` is not in `D_(R^x)`, which
   contains `S_0`. The route "U is surjunctive because it lies in the permanence
   closure" is dead: `fixed-host-surjunctive-via-sofic-permanence`.
3. The exclusion survives any future permanence theorem. Suppose surjunctivity were
   proved closed under all extensions, all graphs of groups (amalgams and HNN extensions
   included), directed colimits and marked limits. That still would not reach `U` or
   `R^x` from sofic groups. A positive proof must handle a finitely presented simple
   nonsofic Kazhdan group directly, through automata on that group (guard subgroups,
   decoder fibers, observables, entropy), not by decomposing it.
4. One operation does break the invariant: quotients. Closure of surjunctivity under
   quotients would already prove Gottschalk's conjecture, since every group is a quotient
   of a free group and free groups are residually finite.

This settles no surjunctivity question for `U`, `R^x` or any other group. It is a
statement about which proof architectures can succeed.
