# Boone--Higman gate audit, 2026-08-30

## Result

No complete proof of the Boone--Higman conjecture, of
`ck-envelope-has-a-finitely-normally-generated-maximal-kernel`, or of
`bffhz-action-image-is-finitely-presented` survives this audit.

There are two concrete outputs:

1. a material correction to the repository's 2026-08-17 citation audit; and
2. two new elementary level results for the direct CK matrix envelope,
   drafted separately as `ck-envelope-level-ideal-screen.md` and

The correction should be committed.  The level lemma is safe to commit as an

## 1. Material correction: FFWZ v2 contains the two allegedly absent items

The current file
`research/artifacts/boone-higman-audit-2026-08-17.md`, section A.3, says that
arXiv:2603.24687v2 contains neither the term "bi-index" nor a Question 5.9
about finitely normally generated kernels.  That statement is false for the
primary source currently served by arXiv as v2 (2 April 2026).

Fournier-Facio--Wu--Zaremsky, *Abstract twisted Brin--Thompson groups*, has:

* **Lemma 5.5:** relative PBH is equivalent to the existence of a finitely
  presented group `G` and a finitely generated subgroup `H <= G` of finite
  bi-index such that every nontrivial element of the embedded group can be
  conjugated out of `H`.
* **Question 5.9, first part:** for an action `G -> Sym(S)` of type `[A_2]`
  with kernel `K`, can one sharply embed the pair into another such pair whose
  kernel is finitely normally generated?
* **Question 5.9, second part:** can every finitely presented relatively
  simple pair be sharply embedded in one whose largest normal subgroup is
  finitely normally generated?

Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24687v2, Lemma 5.5 and Question 5.9.

This restores the paper as an independent confirmation that finite normal
generation is the exact obstruction when one passes from a finitely
presented actor/relatively simple envelope to a finitely presented simple
quotient.  It does **not** prove the repository's CK leaf: FFWZ pose the
upgrade as a question, and their normal pairs are not automatically the
specific elementary matrix envelope used here.

### Proposed replacement for section A.3's conclusion

Replace the claim that the citation failed with:

> The citation verifies after all.  Lemma 5.5 gives the finite-bi-index
> characterization of relative PBH, and Question 5.9 explicitly asks whether
> one can upgrade the relevant action kernel or largest normal subgroup to a
> finitely normally generated one.  This independently confirms the shape of
> the repository's obstruction, but it is an open question in the source and
> does not establish the CK-specific maximal-kernel claim.

The graph's local definition of bi-index agrees with FFWZ's usage, so the
word should no longer be described as merely repository-local.

### 2.2 BFFHZ

Belk--Fournier-Facio--Hyde--Zaremsky, arXiv:2503.21882v2, proves finite
presentability of `Aut_G(G*F_n)` when `G` is **finitely presented** and

by the action kernel is finitely presented when `G` is finitely presented
simple.  Question 3.3 asks the corresponding finite-normal-generation
question for the `G`-Jacobson radical.

Thus Cairn's leaf for a merely computably presented simple `S` remains
strictly stronger than the published question.  Neither Proposition 1.1 nor
the highly transitive action analysis removes the missing adjective.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21882v2, Proposition 1.1 and
Questions 3.2--3.3.

### 2.3 The new continuous-automatic preprint

Belk--Bleak--Chatterji--Matucci--Perego, *Rational embeddings of continuous
automatic groups* (preliminary 2026), is now publicly readable.  It proves

## 3. Exact strength of the CK maximal-kernel leaf

Fix one direct CK envelope `Gamma = EL_4(U)` containing the simple core `S`.
For a maximal proper normal subgroup `M`, the following are equivalent:

1. `M` is finitely normally generated in `Gamma`;
2. `Gamma/M` is finitely presented;
3. `Gamma/M` is a finitely presented simple property-(T) group containing
   `S` via the given quotient map.

Proof: (1) iff (2) is the Tietze kernel criterion already formalized in the
repository.  Maximality gives simplicity; property (T) passes to quotients;
and the normal-generator/simple-core lemma gives `M cap S = 1`.  Conversely,
(3) implies (2), hence (1).

This exposes a hidden strengthening: the CK lane does not merely seek an
arbitrary finitely presented simple envelope.  It seeks one that is a
property-(T) quotient of this particular matrix envelope.  That observation
does not refute the lane--CK already shows arbitrary recursively presented
groups embed in property-(T) groups--but it prevents treating the leaf as a
routine normal-lattice compactness statement.

## 4. New matrix-level screen

For every normal `M <| EL_n(U)`, define

    lev(M) = {a in U : e_ij(a) in M for every i != j}.

The Steinberg commutator identities prove that `lev(M)` is a two-sided ideal.
If `M` is proper, the fullness of every `1-s` (`1 != s in S`) proves that
`lev(M)` contains none of them.  Hence `S` embeds in the unit group of
`U/lev(M)`.

The full proof is in `ck-envelope-level-ideal-screen.md`.

This is the honest surviving sharp statement: any candidate maximal kernel
has a canonical proper, core-separating ring level.  A complete proof still
needs a theorem controlling the subgroup above that level--in particular,
finite generation of the level ideal and finite normal generation of the
possible noncongruence/central part.  No such theorem was found for the
noncommutative universal sandwich ring.

There is an exact finiteness theorem at the bottom of this sandwich.  For any
two-sided ideal `I <| U`, the relative elementary subgroup `E_n(U,I)` is
finitely normally generated in `E_n(U)` if and only if `I` is finitely
generated as a two-sided ideal.  The proof is a direct application of the
Steinberg commutator identities in one direction and reduction modulo the
finite entry ideal in the other; see
`relative-elementary-fng-iff-level-ideal-fg.md`.  Consequently, if a normal
structure theorem identified a candidate maximal `M` with
`E_n(U,lev(M))`, the group leaf would reduce exactly to finite generation of
its level ideal.  The still-missing part is precisely that identification
(or control of the layer above it) plus existence of a finitely generated
maximal level.

## 5. Failed completion attempts (do not rerun as proofs)

* **Property (T) implies Max-n:** false as a method; no normal-lattice
  Noetherianity follows from the fixed-point property, and the 2026 "just
  finite presentation" result concerns deletion of chosen relators.
* **Take a maximal ring ideal:** gives a simple coefficient quotient, but its
  finite presentation is equivalent to finite generation of that maximal
  ideal.  This is the same obstruction one category down.
* **Identify a maximal group kernel with its level ideal:** unjustified for
  arbitrary noncommutative `U`.  Normal-structure theorems can leave central
  or noncongruence layers, and no finiteness control was verified here.
* **Use abstract twisted Brin--Thompson relative simplicity:** FFWZ
  deliberately leave the canonical kernel potentially not finitely normally
  generated; Question 5.9 is precisely the requested upgrade.
* **Use the continuous-automatic preprint:** the authors explicitly do not
  prove finite presentation of the relevant full groups.
