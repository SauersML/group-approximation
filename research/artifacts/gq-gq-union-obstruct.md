# Necessary conditions for overgroups of ⋃_m GL_n(Z[1/m!]) (lane gq-union-obstruct, 2026-09-17)

Target: `gl-n-q-embeds-in-fp-simple-group`. Role: obstruction miner for family E (unions, Scott/Röver–Nekrashevych,
adelic, tree hosts). All four nodes below are elementary, established, and not independently reviewed. No
priority is claimed.

## 0. The union is the group

`GL_n(Q) = ⋃_m GL_n(Z[1/m!])`, since every rational number has a denominator dividing some `m!`. So "a host for the
union" means a host for `GL_n(Q)`. A f.g. group is never the union of a strictly increasing chain of subgroups, so
a chain of hosts `S_1 <= S_2 <= ...` for the stages never gives a finitely generated host. A compatible chain of
simple hosts gives a countable simple group `S_∞ ⊇ GL_n(Q)`, and embedding `S_∞` is the same problem again. So any
union route has to produce one finitely presented group directly. The constraints below apply to it.

## 1. Profinite side: `sl-n-q-lies-in-finite-residual-of-every-overgroup` (landed c7caf5b8c)

For `n >= 2` and any `H ⊇ GL_n(Q)`, every finite-index subgroup of `H` contains `SL_n(Q)`. The finite residual of
`GL_n(Q)` is exactly `SL_n(Q)`, and the image of `GL_n(Q)` in `Ĥ` is a quotient of `Q^x`.

- Kills: residually finite intermediate hosts (O1, sharpened), and certificates built from finite quotients of the
  stages (congruence subgroups, level stabilizers).
- Leaves: separation of elements with different determinants by finite quotients.

## 2. Tree side: `divisible-elements-act-elliptically-on-trees`

In any action on a tree, an element with roots of infinitely many orders is elliptic. A copy of `(Q,+)` fixes a
vertex or exactly one end. When vertex stabilizers are residually finite, each root group `U_ij(Q)` is
parabolic: it fixes exactly one end and no vertex, as an ascending union of vertex-group pieces along a ray.

- Constrains every Bass–Serre host (graphs of RF groups, Bux–Llosa Isenrich–Wu-type tree actions). Divisibility
  must enter "at infinity", the way `Z[1/m]` does in `BS(1,m)`.

## 3. The parabolic case exists: `gl-n-q-acts-faithfully-on-p-adic-bruhat-tits-buildings`

For each prime `p`, `GL_n(Q) <= PGL_(n+1)(Q)` acts faithfully on the vertices of the Bruhat–Tits building of
`PGL_(n+1)(Q_p)`. `PGL_2(Q)` and `Aff(Q)` act faithfully on the `(p+1)`-regular tree, with residually finite
vertex stabilizers and the unipotent `(Q,+)` parabolic at `[e_1]`. The action is transitive, with infinitely many
pair orbits and non-f.g. stabilizers, so it is not of type (A).

- O1's tree clause is exactly about rooted trees. Unrooted locally finite trees and buildings carry `GL_n(Q)`.
- **One prime suffices for all divisibility**, since `Q <= Q_p`. Divisibility by `q != p` happens inside the compact
  stabilizers; divisibility by `p` comes from the end. So the "all-primes / varying branching" direction of family
  E (`gq-rn-varying-degree`) is not forced by divisibility. A single `p`-adic tree already works for `n = 1`
  (`Aff(Q)`) and for `PGL_2(Q)`, and a single `p`-adic building works for `GL_n(Q)`.

## 4. Kill: `no-q-in-tree-product-actions-with-finite-stabilizers`

A group acting on a finite product of trees with finite vertex stabilizers (in particular any discrete subgroup
of `Aut(T_1) x ... x Aut(T_r)`, `T_i` locally finite) contains no `(Q,+)`.

- Excludes Burger–Mozes finitely presented simple lattices and all lattices in products of two trees as hosts for
  `GL_n(Q)`, `Aff(Q)`, `U_3(Q)`. For those three groups this was already known on main by distortion
  (`proper-semisimple-cat0-groups-contain-no-gl-2-q`, which also covers Caprace–Rémy). What is new here is
  `(Q,+)` itself and every group containing it, with no properness or local finiteness.

## 5. What a family-E host must look like (combining 1–4 with root O1–O4)

A finitely presented `H ⊇ GL_n(Q)` built from tree-like data must

- (a) not be residually finite, with `SL_n(Q)` in its finite residual (§1);
- (b) have exponentially distorted unipotents, since `BS(1,m) <= GL_2(Z[1/m]) <= H`. Root O4 uses this to
  exclude `V` and `VA`. CAT(0) and hyperbolic groups are excluded as well, since their abelian subgroups are
  finitely generated. That is a standard fact (Bridson–Haefliger, Ch. III.Γ.1), not checked at the source here;
- (c) if it acts on a tree or a product of trees: have infinite vertex stabilizers (§4); and if those are
  residually finite, root groups parabolic at ends (§2);
- (d) sit, if its tree action is continuous for a locally compact topology, in a *non-discrete* ambient such as
  `Aut(T_(p+1))` or an almost-automorphism group, as the countable group `PGL_2(Q)` sits in `PGL_2(Q_p)` (§3).

## 6. Sparks (free to adopt)

- **S1.** Countable finitely generated subgroups of the Neretin group of `T_(p+1)` containing `PGL_2(Q)` (for
  `Aff(Q)`, `SL_2(Q)`), e.g. generated by `PGL_2(Z[1/p])` (a lattice in `PGL_2(R) x PGL_2(Q_p)`), a Higman–Thompson
  type group of `∂T_(p+1) = P^1(Q_p)`, and elements realizing divisibility by primes `q != p`. Candidate link between
  the Bruhat–Tits picture and V-type simplicity. Suggested for gq-solenoid, gq-adelic, gq-rn-varying-degree.
- **S2.** (Superseded for the root's groups by `proper-semisimple-cat0-groups-contain-no-gl-2-q`.) A divisibility
  version for CAT(0) complexes would only add `(Q,+)`-containing groups without distorted elements, such as `Q^n`.
- **S3.** Is there a *uniform* statement for the chain: an f.g. `H ⊇ GL_n(Q)` in which `|e_12(1/m)|_H` grows slower
  than `log m`? Every such `H` has `|e_12(1/m^k)|_H <= 2k |diag(m,1,...)|_H + |e_12(1)|_H`, but the dilations
  `diag(p,1,...)` over all primes `p` need not have bounded length.

## 7. The adelic median graph `X = ∏'_p T_p` (2026-09-18, with gq-sl2q; critic pass 2, item 1)

Split with gq-sl2q. They took the construction side and landed the automorphism group and the orbit count
(`adelic-median-graph-automorphisms-are-restricted-products`, `cocompact-fg-stabilizer-actions-force-finite-generation`,
and the permanence schema (P) inside `sl2-q-in-permutational-boone-higman-class`). The obstruction side follows.

**Q1. Does `divisible-elements-act-elliptically-on-trees` force the root group into one end in each factor?** Yes
(`sl-2-q-root-group-is-parabolic-in-every-prime-factor`):
- `U = u(Q)` fixes no vertex of any `T_p` and exactly one end `ξ_p = [Q_p e_1]` of each;
- in `∏_p (T_p ∪ ∂T_p)` it fixes exactly `ξ = (ξ_p)_p`, whose every coordinate is at infinity;
- `Stab(ξ) = B(Q)`, and the joint Busemann map `B(Q) -> ⊕_p Z` has image `2 ⊕_p Z` and kernel `±U`. The divisible
  group is the joint kernel of infinitely many independent Busemann characters.

**Q2. Does that kill the permanence theorem?** Not by itself. `SL_2(Q)` realizes this configuration with residually
finite vertex groups `SL_2(Z)`, so no contradiction arises from ends alone. What kills a class of hosts is finite
generation (`fg-restricted-product-actions-have-finite-support`):
- a finitely generated group of *coordinatewise* bijections of any restricted product of pointed sets fixes the
  base coordinate outside a finite set `S`;
- so no finitely generated coordinatewise host, meaning any thickening `Ω_p ⊇ T_p^0`, arbitrary bijections,
  BLIW-style per-factor rigid permutations, or finite products of per-prime hosts, contains the natural
  `SL_2(Q)`, `GL_n(Q)` or `Aff(Q)`;
- a finitely generated *monomial* host (factor-permuting) must put all prime factors into finitely many orbits of
  its factor permutation. The prime shift `σ` of `gl-n-q-lies-in-prime-shift-permutation-group` merges primes in the
  same spirit, on `Q^n` rather than on `X`.

The schema (P) itself is a membership statement, and neither result refutes it. They show that (P) cannot be
proved by building a host that acts coordinatewise on `X` or on a thickening. They also show that `SL_2(Q)`'s
instance of (P) needs a prime-mixing actor, which is gq-sl2q's construction side.

**Q3. Which MT-C invariant along `u(1/m!)` must an fp host satisfy?** Two lower bounds on word length, both forced
by the complex-of-groups geometry. Here `Ω(m!) = Σ_(p≤m) v_p(m!) >= m - 1`, which is `~ m log log m`.
- **Depth.** If the host `H = <F>` acts isometrically on a space containing `X^0` equivariantly, then
  `|u(1/m!)|_F >= 2 Ω(m!)/C`, since `u(1/m!)` displaces `o` by exactly `2 Ω(m!)`. The fixed vertex `x_m` of `U_m`
  nearest to `o` is `[Z e_1 ⊕ m! Z e_2]`. The `x_m` lie on one geodesic ray, with `U ∩ Stab(x_m) = U_m`, and step
  `m` has length `Ω(m)` in the prime directions of `m`.
- **Prime support.** For a monomial host, `|u(1/m!)|_F >= π(m)/s`, where `s` is the largest support of a
  generator.

The depth bound applies only to hosts acting on a space that contains `X` equivariantly, and the prime-support
bound only to monomial hosts. The prime-shift groups `K_n` are neither, so these are calibration tests, not kills
of `K_n`. For comparison, the natural word for `u(1/m!)` in `K_2` (conjugate `u(1)` by the product of prime
dilations, each written as a `σ`-conjugate of `diag(2,1)`) has length `O(Σ_(p≤m) (m/p)(1 + index of p))`, which
exceeds both bounds anyway.

**A route to refuting (P), not taken.** If `G` lies in `B_A`, it lies in one fp group `S` with solvable word problem
(`SV_Γ`). Then the finitely presented groups embeddable in `G` have uniformly solvable word problems. Given a
presentation `Π` and a word `w`, search for a homomorphism `Π -> S` with `w -> ≠ 1` (checkable in `S`) in parallel
with a derivation of `w = 1`. This is the Boone–Rogers argument quoted in the survey arXiv:2306.16356v3, §4
footnote. So (P) fails if some group acting on `X` has every vertex stabilizer in `B_A`, while its finitely
presented subgroups admit no uniform word-problem algorithm. Every automorphism of `X` is coordinatewise, and
finitely generated subgroups act on finite products `∏_(p∈S) T_p`. So such a group needs infinitely many vertex
orbits, with finitely presented pieces of non-uniform complexity spread over infinitely many prime directions.
No such example is known to this lane. The needed input, a non-uniform family of fp groups each lying in `B_A`,
is not on main.
