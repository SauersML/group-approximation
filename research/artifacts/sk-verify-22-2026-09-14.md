## 1. `dyadic-derived-full-groups-no-uniform-semifree-diameter`
Route `dyadic-derived-full-groups-no-uniform-semifree-diameter-proof`; artifact `research/artifacts/sk-rauzy-intermediate-2026-09-14-part1.md` §1.

**Re-derived.**
1. **Growth rate.** For a finite generating set `S` of `G = [[T]]′`, `γ_S(ℓ+ℓ′) ≤ γ_S(ℓ)γ_S(ℓ′)`. So `ω_S = lim_ℓ γ_S(ℓ)^(1/ℓ)` exists (Fekete) and equals the limit along `ℓ = Nk`.
2. **A pair forces growth.** Suppose `a, b` lie in the `N`-ball of `S` and freely generate a free semigroup.
   - The `2^k` positive words of length `k` in `a, b` are pairwise distinct and lie in the `Nk`-ball.
   - So `γ_S(Nk) ≥ 2^k`, then `γ_S(Nk)^(1/(Nk)) ≥ 2^(1/N)`, so `ω_S ≥ 2^(1/N)`.
   - Only distinctness among words of one length is used.
3. **Conclusion.** `dyadic-tower-subshift-full-group-non-uniform-growth` (sk-verify-19 PASS-WITH-FIXES) gives `ω(G) = inf_S ω_S = 1`. With `ε = 2^(1/N) − 1 > 0`, some `S` has `ω_S < 2^(1/N)`, and by step 2 its `N`-ball has no free-semigroup pair.
4. **Remark in the route.** In any finitely generated group a uniform semi-free diameter `N` gives `ω ≥ 2^(1/N) > 1`, which is uniform exponential growth. Correct.

## 2. `subshift-gl-exactness-reduces-to-limit-subsystem`
Route `subshift-gl-exactness-reduces-to-limit-subsystem-proof`; artifact `research/artifacts/sk-exact-banded-2026-09-14-part2.md`.

**Re-derived.**
1. **Step 1.**
   - `Z` is closed in the Cantor set `Y`, so a clopen subset of `Z` is a finite union of cylinders intersected with `Z`, the trace of a clopen subset of `Y`.
   - Restriction is equivariant, so `Σ f_j u^j ↦ Σ f_j|_Z u^j` is a surjective ring homomorphism. By uniqueness of the expansion its kernel is `I_Z`.
   - On `GL_m` its kernel is the congruence subgroup `GL_m(R_Y, I_Z)`.
2. **Step 2.**
   - The limit-set hypothesis is that of `biasymptotic-subshift-complement-ideal-is-ultramatricial` (sk-verify-5 PASS), with `(X,Y) := (Y,Z)`. Also `f_j|_Z = 0` iff `supp f_j ∩ Z = ∅`.
   - `ultramatricial-ideals-have-elementary-congruence-kernels` (sk-verify-5 PASS) gives local finiteness.
   - Direct check: if every finite subset of `I` lies in a finite subring `B ⊆ I`, write `g_i = 1 + x_i` and `g_i^(−1) = 1 + y_i` with `x_i, y_i ∈ M_n(B)`. These generate a subgroup of the finite set `1 + M_n(B)`, which is closed under products because `(1+x)(1+x′) = 1 + x + x′ + xx′`.
   - This works for every `n ≥ 1`, so the `m = 1` embedding `diag(g,1)` is correct but unnecessary.
3. **Step 3.** `N = Γ ∩ GL_m(R_Y,I_Z) = ker(ρ|_Γ)`, so `Γ/N ≅ ρ(Γ)`.
   - Locally finite groups are amenable, hence exact.
   - Kirchberg–Wassermann then gives that `Γ` is exact. Its abstract and introduction were read at source and recorded in `exact-groups-are-closed-under-extensions-citation`.
4. **Step 4, base case.** Let `e = e_y` for `y` of least period `p`, and `E_ij = u^i e u^(−j)` for `0 ≤ i,j < p`.
   - `e u^d e = e · e_(T^(±d)y) · u^d = 0` for `0 < |d| < p`, so `E_ij E_kl = δ_jk E_il`, and `Σ_i E_ii = 1`.
   - `u^p` commutes with `e`, so `eRe ≅ F_2[t^(±1)]` with `t = e u^p`. Hence `R ≅ M_p(F_2[t^(±1)])`.
   - Then `R_(Y_d) ≅ ∏_i M_(p_i)(F_2[t^(±1)])`, and padding by identity blocks embeds `∏_i GL_(mp_i)(F_2[t^(±1)])` in `GL_(mP)(∏_i F_2[t^(±1)])`.
   - Guentner–Tessera–Yu applies to this commutative ring. Independently, each factor is linear over the field `F_2(t)`, and exactness passes to finite products and subgroups.
5. **Step 4, induction.** Step 3 applied to `(Y_i, Y_(i+1))`. Correct.
6. **Step 5.**
   - If `y` agrees with periodic sequences at `±∞`, then `cl O(y) = O(y) ∪ P_+ ∪ P_−`. For `x ∈ O(y) ∖ (P_+ ∪ P_−)`, `α(x) ⊆ P_−` and `ω(x) ⊆ P_+`. So `d = 1`, or `d = 0` if `y` is itself periodic.
   - The banded sentence rests on `banded-matrix-groups-are-subshift-crossed-product-groups`, queue line 25, which sk-verify-18 is reviewing.
7. **Model tests.**
   - Full shift: a point whose forward orbit contains every word has `ω(x) = Y`, so it lies in `Z`, and then `Z = Y`. ✓
   - Infinite minimal `Y` ✓; Sturmian closure plus one asymptotic point ✓.
   - The lamplighter test is wrong as displayed; see Fix X1.

**Strengthening S1 (from main; recommended).** The limit-set hypothesis can be weakened to
> `Z` contains every minimal subset of `Y`.

- **The proof carries over.** Step 2 then cites `minimal-set-complement-ideal-is-ultramatricial` (sk-verify-7 PASS). Its bounded-chain step is `subshift-visit-chains-are-bounded-off-every-minimal-subset` (sk-verify-7 PASS). Steps 1 and 3 are unchanged.
- **The limit-set hypothesis implies it.** A minimal `M ⊄ Z` is disjoint from `Z`, because `M ∩ Z` is closed and invariant. Then `x ∈ M` has `∅ ≠ ω(x) ⊆ M`, so `ω(x) ⊄ Z`.
- **Hand check of bounded chains.**
  - Arbitrarily long `J`-chains of visits to a clopen `V` disjoint from `Z` give a limit point that visits `V` in every window of length `J`.
  - That condition is closed, so it passes to the orbit closure. A minimal set inside that closure then meets `V`, a contradiction.
- **Corollary (a) becomes one step**, with `Z = Y_d`; by the argument above, every minimal subset of `Y` lies in each `Y_i`. So every finitely generated subgroup of `GL_m(R_Y)` is exact when `Y` has finitely many minimal subsets, all of them periodic orbits.
