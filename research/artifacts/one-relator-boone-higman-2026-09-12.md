## 1. Where the problem is asked

- **BBMZ, arXiv:2306.16356v3, Problem 5.3:** "Prove the Boone–Higman conjecture
  for: ... (9) One-relator groups (without torsion)." Theorem 5.1 lists
  "(9) One-relator groups with torsion." among known classes.
- **Linton–Nyberg-Brodda, §1.8 (p. 72–73):** "by combining this with the B. B.
  Newman Spelling Theorem (§1.6.1) we have that every one-relator group with
  torsion embeds into a finitely presented simple group. Whether this can be
  extended to all one-relator groups seems a next natural step for the
  Boone–Higman Conjecture." Then: "Problem 1.8.6. Can every one-relator group be
  embedded into a finitely presented simple group?"

**BBMZ Remark 5.4, read in full.** It records the resolution of items (4), (8)
and (1), and partial progress on items (2), (3) and (13). It does not mention
item (9).

## 2. Classical inputs (Linton–Nyberg-Brodda)

The original papers, as listed in the survey's bibliography; none of them was
read:
- [Mag30] W. Magnus, *Über diskontinuierliche Gruppen mit einer definierenden
  Relation (Der Freiheitssatz)*, J. Reine Angew. Math. 163 (1930), 141–165.
- [Mag32] W. Magnus, *Das Identitätsproblem für Gruppen mit einer definierenden
  Relation*, Math. Ann. 106 (1932), no. 1, 295–307.
- [Mol67] D. I. Moldavanskiĭ, *Certain subgroups of groups with one defining
  relation*, Sibirsk. Mat. Ž. 8 (1967), 1370–1384.
- [MS73] J. McCool and P. E. Schupp, *On one relator groups and HNN extensions*,
  J. Austral. Math. Soc. 16 (1973), 249–256.

**Freiheitssatz,** Theorem 1.3.1: "Let G = ⟨A | r = 1⟩ be a one-relator group
such that the word r is cyclically reduced, and such that r involves every
letter of A. Let A_0 ⊊ A. Then the subgroup of G generated by A_0 is free, and
furthermore is free with A_0 as a basis." The text continues that the second
condition "is also of little consequence, since one can simply pull out the
unused letters of A into a free factor".

**Word problem,** Theorem 1.3.6 (Magnus 1932): "Every one-relator group has
decidable word problem." §1.3.3: "the generalized word problem asks for deciding
membership in Magnus subgroups of one-relator groups", and "This solves the
generalized word problem in all one-relator groups, and hence solves the word
problem in all one-relator groups." Footnote 18 separates this from the general
subgroup membership problem, "which remains open for one-relator groups".

**HNN form,** §1.5.4: "if G = ⟨A | r = 1⟩ is a one-relator group in which some
generator t, actually appearing in r, has exponent sum zero in r, then it is not
hard to see that G is an HNN-extension of a one-relator group G_1, where the
associated subgroups are Magnus subgroups of G_1 and t is the stable letter (for
a proof see [MS73, Theorem 1])."

**Moldavanskii embedding,** Theorem 1.5.8 (Moldavanskii 1967): "Every one-relator
group G_1 = ⟨A_1 | r_1 = 1⟩ can be embedded in a one-relator group G_1' which is
the HNN-extension of a one-relator group G_2 = ⟨A_2 | r_2 = 1⟩, in which the
associated subgroups are Magnus subgroups and |r_2| < |r_1|."

**Moldavanskii's criterion,** Theorem 1.5.11. For a non-abelian
`G = ⟨a, b | r⟩` with `σ_a(r) = 0`, rewritten over `b_i = a^i b a^-i` with least
and greatest indices `m, M`, the following are equivalent:
- G is (finitely generated free)-by-cyclic;
- the commutator subgroup G' is finitely generated;
- "m < M, and b_m resp. b_M occur exactly once in the rewritten form of r".

The survey prints `a_m`, `a_M` in that item; it means `b_m`, `b_M`.

**Magnus subgroups,** §1.7.1:
- **Newman:** "Any Magnus subgroup of a one-relator group with torsion is
  malnormal."
- **Bagherzadeh 1976:** "for any one-relator group G, if H < G is a Magnus
  subgroup, then H ∩ g^-1 H g is cyclic for g ∉ H."

**Virtual fibring,** Theorem 2.5.34: "If G is a torsion-free two-generator
one-relator group that is virtually RFRS, then G is virtually {finitely generated
free}-by-cyclic."

## 3. Hyperbolicity (Linton, arXiv:2202.11324v3)

- **Characterization of negative immersions,** Introduction, item (3): "X has
  negative immersions if and only if every two-generator subgroup of π_1(X) is
  free [LW22]."
- **Theorem 7.2:** "One-relator groups with negative immersions are hyperbolic
  and virtually special."
- **Theorem 7.1, the equivalence of (1) and (3):** a one-relator hierarchy is
  "a quasi-convex hierarchy and π_1(X) is hyperbolic" iff it "is a Z-stable
  hierarchy and π_1(X) contains no Baumslag–Solitar subgroups".

## 4. Locally finite trees (Bux–Llosa Isenrich–Wu, arXiv:2408.05673v2)

- **Theorem B [10.5]:** "Let G be a group acting faithfully and cocompactly on a
  locally finite tree. If all edge stabilizers for the action are finitely
  presented, then G embeds in a finitely presented simple group."
- **Theorem 10.5:** "Let G be a graph of groups such that all vertex groups are
  of type F_n and all edge groups have finite index in the vertex groups. Assume
  that G acts faithfully on its Bass–Serre tree. Then G embeds in a simple group
  of type F_n."
- **Theorem 11.1:** for G of type F_n with n ≥ 2, if some nontrivial H ∈ BS_G
  acts faithfully on its Bass–Serre tree, "then every K ∈ BS_G embeds in a simple
  group of type F_n". Here BS_G consists of finite graphs of groups whose vertex
  and edge groups are abstractly commensurable with G, with finite-index edge
  inclusions.
- **Theorem 12.5:** every group in BS_{F_k} for k ≥ 2 embeds in a simple group of
  type F_∞.
- **Remark 12.7:** "Our proof of Theorem 12.5 shows that for every group in
  BS_{F_k} the permutational Boone–Higman Conjecture holds, that is, every such
  group embeds in a finitely presented group that admits an action of type (A)
  on a countable set".
- **The method** (§8): G embeds in the rigid permutation group RPG(T), whose
  action on V(T) is oligomorphic (Lemma 8.3), and then in the twisted
  Brin–Thompson group SV_{RPG(T)} (Theorem 8.2, Belk–Zaremsky).

## 5. The conditional theorem

`magnus-hnn-permanence-forces-one-relator-boone-higman` (established) proves
that (M) implies every one-relator group embeds in a finitely presented simple
group. Here (M) is `bh-embeddability-survives-magnus-subgroup-hnn`.

- **Method:** induction on relator length.
- **Bottom:** `Z/k * F`, handled by `hyperbolic-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`.
- **Inputs used:** the Freiheitssatz, to make the HNN steps well defined, and
  (M) on shift data and on empty associated subgroups.

The induction step is the explicit Magnus rewriting, with a Tietze check, and
Moldavanskii's trick. Nothing is taken from Theorem 1.5.8 beyond what the proof
route rederives.

(M) follows from the general decidable-edge permanence
`bh-embeddability-survives-decidable-edge-hnn`, because Magnus edges have
decidable membership (§2).

**Honest cost.** On shift data, (M) is equivalent to the target: the HNN
extension of shift data is again a one-relator group. The route isolates the
induction step and adds one hypothesis, that the base embeds. It does not shrink
the problem.

## 6. What the known theorems reach, and where they stop

- **Torsion:** hyperbolic, so covered by BBMZ.
- **Two-free (negative immersions):** hyperbolic by Linton Theorem 7.2, so
  covered by BBMZ.
- **Finitely generated free-by-cyclic, and BS(m,n):** covered by BLIW
  Theorem A.
- **Locally finite tree methods at a Magnus step.** A Magnus step H*_β has free
  associated subgroups of equal rank. Finite index of both in H makes H virtually
  free and torsion-free, hence free (Stallings–Swan). So Theorem 10.5 reaches
  only steps over free bases.
- **Hyperbolic combination.** By Linton Theorem 7.1, it cannot reach any group
  containing a Baumslag–Solitar subgroup.

**Test case:** the Baumslag–Gersten group
`BG = ⟨a, t | (t a t^-1) a (t a^-1 t^-1) = a^2⟩`.
- **Structure:** rewriting over `a_i = t^i a t^-i` gives
  `H = ⟨a_0, a_1 | a_1 a_0 a_1^-1 = a_0^2⟩ = BS(1,2)`, with `Y_1 = {a_0}`,
  `Y_2 = {a_1}`. So `BG = BS(1,2)*_β` over cyclic edges of infinite index.
- **Not residually finite,** so no residually finite host applies.
- **Tree methods:** the base BS(1,2) is not free, so the BLIW tree methods do not
  apply to this step.
- **Hyperbolicity:** BG contains BS(1,2), so hyperbolic methods do not apply.

No source read here states BH for BG.
