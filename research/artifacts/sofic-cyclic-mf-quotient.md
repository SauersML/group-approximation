# A finitely presented sofic Powers group with exactly cyclic MF quotients

Date: 2026-09-07.

This is a complete written proof relative to Theorem F of
`non_mf_groups_exist.tex` and the classical results cited below. The new
arguments are not claimed to have been checked by Lean. Cairn checks the
dependency graph, not the mathematics in this file. No priority claim is made.

## 1. Statement and construction

Use the explicit group of Theorem F:

    Gamma = Z^3 semidirect SL_3(Z),
    alpha(v,A) = (2v,A),
    V = <Gamma,t | t gamma t^-1 = alpha(gamma)>,
    Gamma+ = t^-1 Gamma t,
    X = V/Gamma,  Y = V/Gamma+,
    B = *_(y in Y) product_(x in p^-1(y)) (A_5)_x,
    E = B semidirect V,                         (1)

where p(vGamma)=vGamma+. Theorem F proves that E is finitely presented
and sofic, that B=Rad_MF(E)=Res_fin(E), and that any nonidentity element
of a coordinate A_5 normally generates B in E. Each block is (A_5)^8.

Let x in SL_3(Z) be the matrix

    x = [[0,1,0], [0,0,1], [1,0,0]],

and, in the coordinate (A_5)_Gamma, put

    a = (12)(34),  b = (123),
    r = t x^-1,  d = a t b t^-1.

Define the finite presentation obtained from that of E by adding one
generator and one relator:

    P = <E,s | s r s^-1 = d>.                   (2)

**Theorem.** Both cyclic edge groups in (2) embed and intersect trivially
in E. The group P is finitely presented, sofic, acylindrically hyperbolic,
and a Powers group. In particular C*_r(P) is simple with a unique tracial
state. The split epimorphism

    chi:P -> Z,  chi(s)=1, chi(E)=0

has nontrivial kernel N satisfying

    N = normalClosure_P(d)
      = Rad_MF(P) = Res_fin(P),
    Rad_MF(N) = N.                              (3)

The word d has infinite order. The kernel N is perfect and is not finitely
generated. For every normal subgroup R of P,

    Rad_MF(P/R) = NR/R,
    P/R is MF  iff  d in R  iff  P/R is cyclic. (4)

Every homomorphism from P to an MF group factors uniquely through chi.
The finite quotients of P are precisely its cyclic quotients; chi induces
an isomorphism of profinite completions P-hat ~= Z-hat.

## 2. A weight-one element of the affine quotient

Let h:V->Z be the height map h(t)=1, h(Gamma)=0. Then h(r)=1, so r has
infinite order. We prove that r normally generates V.

In the quotient V/normalClosure(r), the equality t=x holds. The element t
commutes with SL_3(Z), so the image of x is central in the image of that
linear group. Write e_ij=I+E_ij for the elementary integral matrices.
Conjugation by x cycles e_12, e_31, and e_23. Their images in this quotient
are consequently equal. The identity

    [e_12,e_23] = e_13

therefore kills e_13. Conjugating by determinant-one signed permutation
matrices kills every e_ij (a possible minus sign is handled by inversion).
These matrices generate SL_3(Z), by integral elementary row reduction.
Thus the whole linear group dies. Now t=x dies as well. Each translation
generator v_i satisfies t v_i t^-1=v_i^2, so v_i=v_i^2 and v_i dies.
The displayed generators of V have all died, proving

    V/normalClosure_V(r) = 1.                   (5)

This uses the faithful affine description, not an unverified proposed
presentation of SL_3(Z).

## 3. One infinite-order generator of the lamp normal subgroup

The factors containing a and tbt^-1 lie in different blocks: their block
labels are Gamma+ and tGamma+, distinguished by h. The word
d=a(tbt^-1) is cyclically reduced of free-product length two in B. Its
positive powers have reduced length 2n, so d has infinite order.

In E/normalClosure_E(d), the images of a and tbt^-1 are inverse. The
first has order dividing 2 and the second order dividing 3. Both images
must therefore be the identity. Since a normally generates B in E,
normalClosure_E(d) contains B. The reverse containment follows from
d in B and normality of B. Hence

    normalClosure_E(d) = B.                     (6)

Extend h to E by h(B)=0. If r^k=d^l in E, height gives k=0, and the
infinite order of d then gives l=0. Thus

    <r> intersect <d> = {1}.                    (7)

Both subgroups are proper in E: E contains a nonabelian finite subgroup
A_5 and is not cyclic. Equation (2) is therefore a genuine nonascending
HNN extension of E over two infinite cyclic subgroups. Britton's lemma
embeds E in P. In particular d remains an element of infinite order.

## 4. The absorption lemma and the intrinsic radical

Here is the general mechanism, which does not require a lamp construction.
Let E be countable and B normal in E. Suppose every homomorphism E->M
to an MF group kills B; let d in B and r in E have infinite order; and
suppose the image of r normally generates E/B. Form P as in (2).

The map chi is well defined and split by k |-> s^k. Its kernel N is
generated by the subgroups

    E_i = s^i E s^-i   (i in Z).                 (8)

For clarity, any word in E and s with total s-exponent zero can be
rewritten as a product of conjugates s^i e s^-i, proving (8) directly.
Write B_i=s^i B s^-i, and use the analogous notation for r_i,d_i.
Conjugating the defining HNN relation gives

    r_(i+1) = d_i   for every i in Z.           (9)

Let f:N->M be any homomorphism to an MF group. Its restriction to E_i
kills B_i, because E_i is isomorphic to E. Therefore f(d_i)=1 for every
i. Equation (9) gives f(r_(i+1))=1. As r normally generates E/B, the
restriction of f to E_(i+1) is trivial. This holds for every integer i,
so (8) gives f=1. We have proved the intrinsic statement

    Rad_MF(N)=N.                                (10)

In particular every MF homomorphism out of P kills N and factors uniquely
through chi. Conversely Z is MF and residually finite, so chi and its
finite cyclic reductions separate every element outside N. Finite groups
are MF; it follows that

    Rad_MF(P)=Res_fin(P)=N.                      (11)

If, additionally, normalClosure_E(d)=B, quotienting P by d kills B and
then, by the HNN relation, r. It kills E/B by normal generation. The
resulting presentation has just s and no relators, and is Z. Thus
normalClosure_P(d)=ker(chi)=N. Equations (5) and (6) verify precisely
these hypotheses for the concrete example.

This also proves N is perfect: every countable abelian group is MF, since
characters into the circle separate its elements and countably many such
characters give a diagonal matrix-corona embedding. A nonzero
abelianization of N would contradict (10).

## 5. All normal quotients, not just the original group

Let R be any normal subgroup of P, with no finite generation assumption.
The image NR/R of N in P/R has no nontrivial homomorphism to an MF group,
by (10). Hence it is contained in Rad_MF(P/R). The quotient

    (P/R)/(NR/R) ~= P/NR

is a quotient of Z and is therefore cyclic and MF. Its quotient map
proves the reverse containment. This establishes the first equality of
(4). Because N is normally generated by d, NR/R is trivial precisely
when d belongs to R. This gives the MF equivalence in (4). If P/R is
cyclic it is MF, and if d belongs to R then P/R is cyclic; thus the final
equivalence is also exact.

Every finite quotient is an MF quotient, hence factors through chi.
Conversely all finite cyclic quotients of Z occur. In particular the
finite-index subgroups of P are exactly chi^-1(mZ), m>=1: for a
finite-index subgroup, the finite permutation action on its cosets kills
N, so it contains N. The inverse systems of finite quotients are therefore
identical, which proves the profinite-completion assertion. No statement
that arbitrary quotients of a sofic group remain sofic is being used.

## 6. Soficity and the size of the kernel

The HNN extension is sofic by Collins--Dykema, Corollary 3.6: the base E
is sofic and the associated subgroup <r> is infinite cyclic, hence
monotileably amenable. Concretely, intervals tile Z and give its required
Folner sets. Their result applies to arbitrary injective embeddings of
this subgroup, so it applies to r^k |-> d^k.

The kernel description also makes the permanence argument transparent.
For each finite interval [p,q] in Z, let N_[p,q] be the subgroup generated
by E_i, p<=i<=q. HNN normal form identifies it with the iterated amalgam
of those vertex groups along the cyclic relations r_(i+1)=d_i. These
amalgams are sofic by Collins--Dykema, Theorem 3.4. The directed union
N=union_n N_[-n,n] is sofic, and P=N semidirect Z is sofic by the
sofic-by-amenable extension theorem of Elek--Szabo. This is also the
proof of the cited HNN corollary in this special case.

Every finite subset of N lies in N_[p,q] for some finite interval.
Amalgam normal form gives

    N_[p,q+1] ~= N_[p,q] *_(<d_q>=<r_(q+1)>) E_(q+1).

The cyclic subgroup is proper in E_(q+1), so an element of that vertex
group outside the edge subgroup is outside N_[p,q]. Thus each such
finite interval is a proper subgroup of N. No finite subset generates N.
In particular N is not a property-(T) group. It also contains A_5. This
construction does not close the separate Cairn target asking for a
torsion-free sofic group with a property-(T) radical over Z.

## 7. Reduced C*-simplicity and acylindrical hyperbolicity

Equation (7) supplies both geometric conclusions, without an assumption
about the reduced C*-algebra of E. The kernel of the action of P on its
Bass--Serre tree is contained in every edge stabilizer, in particular
in <r> intersect <d>. It is therefore trivial. The HNN extension is
nonascending and the intersection of its associated subgroups is finite.
Bryder--Ivanov--Omland, Proposition 4.12, now implies that P is a Powers
group and that C*_r(P) is simple with the unique trace property.

Also <r> is weakly malnormal in P: conjugation by s sends it to <d>,
and their intersection is trivial. Minasyan--Osin, Corollary 2.3, applies
because both associated subgroups are proper, and proves that P is
acylindrically hyperbolic. Their erratum concerns commensurability and
3-manifold consequences, not this HNN criterion.

Since P is not MF, its reduced C*-algebra cannot be MF: an embedding
of that algebra in a norm matrix corona would restrict to an MF embedding
of its canonical group unitaries. Its faithful canonical trace makes it
stably finite. These conclusions are also integrated into Theorem G and
Section 6 of the manuscript.
