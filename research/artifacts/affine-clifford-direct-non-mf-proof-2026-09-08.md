# An explicit non-MF group from a rational matrix certificate

**Scope correction (2026-09-08).** This is a property-T argument: the
certificate proves a uniform representation estimate equivalent to property
T for the affine subgroup, and the construction uses that estimate.
Reproving the property explicitly does not avoid using it. This proof does
not fulfill the requested proof without property T.

## Statement

There is an explicit nine-generated countable group \(W\), with a specified
central element \(z\) of order two, such that every homomorphism

\[
W\longrightarrow U\left(\prod_n M_{d_n}(\mathbb C)\big/
                \bigoplus_n M_{d_n}(\mathbb C)\right)
\]

sends \(z\) to the identity. Here the product consists of norm-bounded
sequences and the direct sum consists of norm-null sequences. Consequently
\(W\) is not MF, with MF meaning embeddability in one of these unitary groups.

The finite numerical input is the integer table
`experiments/sl3-sos-radius0-certificate.npz`. Its complete coefficient check
is specified in [the certificate appendix](p13-rational-certificate-audit-2026-09-08.md).
The [affine estimate appendix](affine-seven-generator-distance-estimate-2026-09-08.md)
derives the representation estimate used below. Both appendices are parts of
this proof; the numerical table is proof data, not a hypothesis.

## 1. The explicit group and its nontrivial central sign

Write \(A=\mathbb Z[1/2]^3\), and let

\[
V=A\rtimes (\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb Z),
\qquad (M,k)v=2^kMv.
\]

Thus \((v,M,k)(v',M',k')=(v+2^kMv',MM',k+k')\).
Let \(B=\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)\le V\), with \(k=0\),
and let

\[
t=(0,I,1),\qquad a=(e_1,I,0),\qquad X=V/B,\qquad o=B.
\]

These definitions use left cosets. Direct multiplication gives

\[
tBt^{-1}=(2\mathbb Z^3)\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)\le B,
\qquad t^{-1}at=(e_1/2,I,0)\notin B.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular \(to\ne ato\).

For completeness, construct the real Clifford algebra on the countable set
\(X\) as follows. Order \(X\), take the real vector space with basis \(e_F\)
for finite subsets \(F\subset X\), and define

\[
e_Fe_H=(-1)^{N(F,H)}e_{F\triangle H},\qquad
N(F,H)=|\{(x,y)\in F\times H:x>y\}|.
\]

The exponent modulo two is bilinear on finite subsets under symmetric
difference. This proves associativity. The unit is \(e_\varnothing\);
for \(e_x=e_{\{x\}}\) one has

\[
e_x^2=1,\qquad e_xe_y=-e_ye_x\quad(x\ne y).
\tag{2}
\]

Distinct basis vectors are linearly independent and the scalar field is
real, so \(-1\ne1\). Let \(C(X)\) be the group of units generated by all
\(e_x\) and the scalar \(-1\). Every permutation of \(X\) induces an
algebra automorphism sending \(e_x\) to its permuted generator: the relations
(2) reduce every word to a signed ordered monomial, and these monomials are
exactly the displayed basis. The inverse permutation gives its inverse.

The left action of \(V\) on \(X\) therefore defines the explicit group

\[
W=C(X)\rtimes V.
\]

Identify \(B,V,C(X)\) with their copies in \(W\), and put \(c=e_o\), \(z=-1\).
Every \(b\in B\) fixes \(o\), hence commutes with \(c\). With commutators
defined by \([u,v]=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}\), equations (1) and (2) give

\[
d=tct^{-1}=e_{to},\qquad ada^{-1}=e_{ato},\qquad
[d,ada^{-1}]=-1=z.
\tag{3}
\]

The element \(z\) is central in \(W\) and has order two: all automorphisms
above fix real scalars. Thus (3) supplies a concrete nontrivial marked word.

The group is countable. It is generated by the six matrices \(I+E_{ij}\)
(\(i\ne j\)), the translation \(a\), the dilation \(t\), and the lamp \(c\).
Indeed, integer row operations show that these six matrices generate
\(\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)\); their conjugates of \(a\) generate integer
translations; conjugating by powers of \(t^{-1}\) supplies all dyadic
translations; and transitivity on \(V/B\) supplies every lamp. Equation (3)
also supplies the scalar sign. There are nine generators in total.

## 2. The concrete estimate for the affine subgroup

Let \(S\subset B\) consist of the six elementary matrices and \(a\). For
every unitary representation \(\pi\) of \(B\) on any complex Hilbert space,
let \(P_B\) be orthogonal projection onto its invariant vectors. The two
appendices prove

\[
\|\xi-P_B\xi\|
 \le C\max_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\xi-\xi\|,
\qquad C=2+5\sqrt{3000}.
\tag{4}
\]

Here is the connection between the finite certificate and (4). Its exact
rational factorization for the six elementary generators gives

\[
H=DD^*+F^*F=\frac1{250}I+Q^*Q+R,
\qquad \|\pi(R)\|\le
 \frac{11670886519714}{10^{16}}<\frac1{500}.
\]

The column \(D\) has entries \(u_{ij}-1\). The rows of \(F\) are the Fox
derivatives of thirteen explicit relations, so \(FD=0\). Evaluation on
\(D\xi\) gives, for \(L=D^*D\),

\[
L^2\ge \frac1{500}L.
\]

Consequently the distance \(d\) from \(\xi\) to the rotation-invariant
subspace is at most \(\sqrt{3000}\delta\), where
\(\delta=\max_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\xi-\xi\|\). If \(p\) is its projection,
then \(a\) moves \(p\) by at most \(\delta+2d\). Every integer vector is a
sum of two primitive vectors, and every primitive vector is an
\(\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)\)-translate of \(e_1\). Thus every translation moves \(p\) by at most
\(2\delta+4d\). The closed convex hull of the translation orbit contains a
vector invariant under both translations and rotations within that distance
of \(p\). This proves (4). The appendices justify the factorization, the
orbit assertion, and the convex-hull argument explicitly.

Put \(\gamma=C^{-2}>0\). In particular, for

\[
L_B=\sum_{s\in S}(1-\pi(s))^*(1-\pi(s)),
\]

equation (4) implies

\[
\operatorname{spec}(L_B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\gamma,28],
\qquad \ker L_B=\operatorname{Fix}\pi(B).
\tag{5}
\]

The estimate applies to every Hilbert-space representation, including
faithful representations of C*-algebras. It is not an assertion limited to
exact representations on finite-dimensional spaces.

## 3. A finite-algebra fact about the norm matrix quotient

For any positive integers \(k_n\), write

\[
\mathcal Q_k=\prod_n M_{k_n}(\mathbb C)/\bigoplus_nM_{k_n}(\mathbb C).
\]

This algebra is finite: \(v^*v=1\) implies \(vv^*=1\). To see this, lift \(v\)
to bounded matrices \(v_n\). Then \(v_n^*v_n\to I\) in norm, so for all
sufficiently large \(n\), polar decomposition gives a square unitary \(u_n\)
with \(v_n-u_n\to0\). Thus \(v\) is unitary.

It follows that unitarily conjugate projections in \(\mathcal Q_k\) cannot
be properly ordered. Indeed, if \(p\le q=upu^*\), the element
\(v=up+1-q\) satisfies \(vv^*=1\) and \(v^*v=p+1-q\). Apply finiteness
to \(v^*\) to obtain \(q=p\). The vanishing cross terms follow from
\(p\le q\) and \(up=qu\).

Every projection \(p\in\mathcal Q_k\) lifts to projections \(p_n\): first
choose a self-adjoint lift, then cut its spectrum at \(1/2\), since its
idempotence defect tends to zero. The corner \(p\mathcal Q_kp\) identifies
with the quotient of the matrix corners \(p_nM_{k_n}p_n\). For \(p\ne0\),
discard the zero-rank coordinates, leaving infinitely many positive ranks.
After orthonormal bases are chosen in their ranges, this is a norm matrix
quotient of the same form. Its unit is \(p\).

## 4. Corner a hypothetical surviving sign

Suppose a homomorphism \(\rho:W\to U(\mathcal Q_k)\) satisfies
\(\rho(z)\ne1\). Since \(z^2=1\), its image is self-adjoint, and

\[
p=\frac{1-\rho(z)}2
\]

is a nonzero projection. Centrality of \(z\) implies that \(p\) commutes
with every \(\rho(g)\). Passing to the corner as in Section 3 gives a
homomorphism into some \(\mathcal Q_r\), with \(r_n\ge1\), on which \(z=-1\).

Choose unitary lifts \(U_n(g)\in U(r_n)\) for each \(g\in W\). Such lifts
exist by the same polar argument. Choose \(U_n(1)=I\) and \(U_n(z)=-I\).
The homomorphism identities in the quotient say, for every fixed pair
\(g,h\),

\[
\|U_n(g)U_n(h)-U_n(gh)\|_{\rm op}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]

Use the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
\(\|x\|_{2,n}^2=r_n^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(x^*x)\), and let
\(H_n=M_{r_n}(\mathbb C)\) with this Hilbert structure. Thus
\(\|x\|_{2,n}\le\|x\|_{\rm op}\) and every unitary has \(2\)-norm one.
Conjugation \(\operatorname{Ad}(U)\) is unitary on \(H_n\), and

\[
\|\operatorname{Ad}(U)-\operatorname{Ad}(V)\|_{B(H_n)}
\le2\|U-V\|_{\rm op}.
\tag{7}
\]

Expanding \(UxU^*-VxV^*=(U-V)xU^*+Vx(U^*-V^*)\) proves (7).
Equations (6) and (7) therefore define an exact homomorphism

\[
\sigma:W\to U(\mathcal Q_{r^2}),\qquad
\sigma(g)=[\operatorname{Ad}(U_n(g))].
\tag{8}
\]

## 5. Transport the invariant projection inside the finite quotient

In \(\mathcal Q_{r^2}\), set

\[
L=\sum_{s\in S}(1-\sigma(s))^*(1-\sigma(s)).
\]

Represent this C*-algebra faithfully on a Hilbert space and apply (5) to
\(\sigma|_B\). Continuous functional calculus then gives a projection
\(P=f(L)\in\mathcal Q_{r^2}\), where \(f(0)=1\) and
\(f=0\) on \([\gamma,28]\). Its range in the faithful representation is
exactly the \(B\)-invariant subspace. In particular

\[
\sigma(b)P=P\quad(b\in B).
\tag{9}
\]

Let \(T=\sigma(t)\). The inclusion \(tBt^{-1}\le B\) implies

\[
T^*PT\le P.
\tag{10}
\]

Indeed, for \(b\in B\),
\(\sigma(b)T^*P=T^*\sigma(tbt^{-1})P=T^*P\).
Section 3, applied in \(\mathcal Q_{r^2}\), upgrades (10) to equality.
Thus \(TP=PT\).

Here is a precise way to let these quotient operators act on the varying
matrix spaces. Form the normed vector space

\[
\mathcal H=\{(x_n):\sup_n\|x_n\|_{2,n}<\infty\}/
                 \{(x_n):\|x_n\|_{2,n}\to0\}.
\]

Uniformly bounded operators on \(H_n\) act on this quotient; operator-norm
null sequences act as zero. This defines an action of \(\mathcal Q_{r^2}\)
on \(\mathcal H\). No Hilbert inner product on \(\mathcal H\) is needed.

Take \(\xi=[U_n(c)]\in\mathcal H\). Since \(c\) commutes with every
\(b\in B\), (6) gives \(\sigma(b)\xi=\xi\), so \(L\xi=0\). On the spectrum
of \(L\), the function \(h(0)=0\), \(h(\lambda)=1/\lambda\) for
\(\lambda\ge\gamma\), is continuous. Hence
\(1-P=h(L)L\), which proves \(P\xi=\xi\).

Now \(\eta=T\xi\) also satisfies \(P\eta=\eta\), because \(TP=PT\).
By (9), \(\sigma(a)\eta=\eta\). In matrix notation, put

\[
D_n=U_n(t)U_n(c)U_n(t)^*,\qquad
E_n=U_n(a)D_nU_n(a)^*.
\]

The last equality in \(\mathcal H\) says

\[
\|E_n-D_n\|_{2,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]

## 6. The contradiction

For any two unitaries \(D,E\), unitary invariance of the Hilbert--Schmidt
norm and the triangle inequality give

\[
\|DED^*E^*-I\|_2
=\|DE-ED\|_2
\le2\|E-D\|_2.
\]

Therefore (11) gives

\[
\|[D_n,E_n]-I\|_{2,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]

On the other hand, the fixed-word identity (3) and repeated application of
(6) imply

\[
\|[D_n,E_n]-U_n(z)\|_{\rm op}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Since \(U_n(z)=-I\), this also tends to zero in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
norm. Together with (12) it would give

\[
2=\|I-(-I)\|_{2,n}\longrightarrow0,
\]

a contradiction. Every such homomorphism kills the nontrivial \(z\), proving
the statement.
