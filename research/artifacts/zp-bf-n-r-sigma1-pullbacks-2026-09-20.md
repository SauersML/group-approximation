# Sigma^1 of bF_{n,r} on pulled-back characters: the endpoint circle

Lane swarm-0917 (w23-z-last1), 2026-09-20. Unreviewed. Part 4. Notation from parts 1–3:
`zp-pure-braided-bht-characters-2026-09-13.md` (forests, residues, Lemmas 1–3, types),
`zp-pure-braided-bht-sigma-2026-09-13.md` (T1–T5, Proposition 4),
`zp-pure-braided-bht-roots-and-sigma1-2026-09-13.md` (Lemma 6, Lemma 8, Theorem 9).

**Theorem 12.** For `n >= 2`, `r >= 1` and nonzero `psi` in `Hom(F_{n,r}, R)`:
`[psi o pi]` is not in `Sigma^1(bF_{n,r})` iff `[psi]` is `[chi_0]` or `[chi_1]`.

With Theorem 9 (off the plane `span(chi_0, chi_1)`), it remains to treat
`psi = a chi_0 + b chi_1`: Theorem 11 (a, b both nonzero), Proposition 10 (`[-chi_0]`, `[-chi_1]`),
Lemma H (the two holes).

## 1. Block windings and their centralizers

Fix a convention for the generators `A_ij` of `PB_k` in which strand `i` (say) travels to the right
*behind* strands `i+1, ..., j-1`, goes once around strand `j`, and returns the same way.

Let `T` be a forest with `k` leaves and let `P = {p, ..., p'}`, `Q = {q, ..., q'}` be blocks of consecutive
leaves with `p' < q`. Let `beta_T(P, Q)` in `PB_k` be the braid in which the strands of `P`, held parallel
as one ribbon, do what strand `i` does in `A_ij`: they pass behind the strands strictly between the blocks,
go once around the ribbon formed by the strands of `Q`, and return. For one-element blocks this is `A_pq`.
Let `I`, `J` be the intervals formed by the leaves in `P`, `Q`, and put `alpha(I, J) := (T, beta_T(P, Q), T)`.

**Lemma W.**
(a) `alpha(I, J)` depends only on the intervals `I < J`, not on `T`.
(b) If `I`, `J` are single leaves of `T`, then `alpha(I, J)` is the winding `(T, A_ij, T)` of parts 1–3.
(c) Let `f` in `F_{n,r}` be represented by a forest pair `(T_-, T_+)`, where `f` carries leaf `t` of `T_+`
affinely onto leaf `t` of `T_-`. If `I`, `J` are unions of leaves of `T_+`, then
`s(f) alpha(I, J) s(f)^{-1} = alpha(f(I), f(J))`.
(d) If `f(I) = I` and `f(J) = J`, then `s(f)` commutes with `alpha(I, J)`. This holds whether or not `f`
is affine on `I` or `J`.

*Proof.* (a) Two forests in which `I`, `J` are unions of leaves have a common expansion with the same
property, so it suffices to expand one leaf `l`. The expansion relation replaces `(T, beta, T)` by
`(T', c_l(beta), T')`, where `c_l` cables strand `l` into `n` parallel strands. If `l` lies in `P` or in
`Q`, cabling thickens that ribbon. If `l` lies strictly between the blocks, the `P`-ribbon passes behind
all `n` copies. If `l` lies outside, the braid never touches it. In every case
`c_l(beta_T(P, Q)) = beta_{T'}(P', Q')`, where `P'` and `Q'` are the blocks of `T'` making up `I` and `J`.
(b) By definition.
(c) Part 1, §3: conjugation by `s(T_-, T_+)` sends `(T_+, b, T_+)` to `(T_-, b, T_-)`. Because `f` is
order-preserving and carries leaves to leaves, `f(I)` and `f(J)` are unions of the leaves of `T_-` with
the same indices `P`, `Q`. So the conjugate is `(T_-, beta(P, Q), T_-) = alpha(f(I), f(J))` by (a).
(d) Expand `T_+` until `I` and `J` are unions of its leaves, then apply (c).

With the opposite composition convention, replace `f` by `f^{-1}` in (c). Nothing below depends on this,
and (d) is symmetric.

`N = ker pi` is the union of the `PB(T) = {(T, b, T)}`, and `PB_k` is generated by the `A_ij`. So `N` is
generated by the windings `alpha(I, J)` with `I`, `J` leaves. By Lemma 3 of part 1, a winding of type `t`
is `s(g) alpha_t s(g)^{-1}` for some `g` in `F_{n,r}`, where `alpha_t = alpha(I_t, J_t)` is a fixed
representative and there are finitely many types.

**Endpoint germs.** Fix the representatives `(I_t, J_t)` for all types. Choose `e >= 2` such that
`eps = n^{-e}` satisfies these bounds for every `t`:
- `eps <= |I_t|`;
- `eps <= left(I_t)` when `left(I_t) > 0`;
- `eps <= |J_t|`;
- `eps <= r - right(J_t)` when that is positive.

Let `y_0` be the identity on `[eps, r]`, affine of slope `1/n` from `[0, eps/n]` onto `[0, eps/n^2]`, and
any PL map of `[eps/n, eps]` onto `[eps/n^2, eps]` with slopes in `<n>` and breakpoints in `A`. Such a map
exists by part 1, Lemma 2: the two lengths are `(n-1) n^{-e-1}` and `(n^2-1) n^{-e-2}`, both of residue
`0` mod `d`. Let `y_1 = rho y_0 rho` with `rho(x) = r - x`; conjugation by `rho` preserves `F_{n,r}`.
By (C1):
- `chi_0(y_0) = -1` and `chi_1(y_0) = 0`;
- `chi_1(y_1) = -1` and `chi_0(y_1) = 0`.

The supports `[0, eps]` and `[r - eps, r]` are disjoint, because `eps <= 1/4`. So `y_0` and `y_1` commute.

**Lemma E.** For every type `t`, `s(y_0)` and `s(y_1)` commute with `alpha_t`.

*Proof.* Take `y_0`. If `left(I_t) >= eps`, then `y_0` fixes `I_t` and `J_t` pointwise. Otherwise
`left(I_t) = 0`, so `I_t = [0, |I_t|]` contains `[0, eps]`. Then `y_0` maps `I_t` onto itself and fixes
`J_t` (which lies in `[eps, r]`) pointwise. Apply Lemma W(d). The argument for `y_1` is the mirror image,
using `J_t`. ∎

## 2. The quarter-arcs: both endpoint coefficients nonzero

**Theorem 11.** If `psi = a chi_0 + b chi_1` with `a != 0` and `b != 0`, then `[psi o pi]` is in
`Sigma^1(bF_{n,r})`.

*Proof.* Apply (C3) to `G = bF_{n,r}` (finitely generated) and `chi = psi o pi`, with
`J = {s(y_0), s(y_1)}` and `I = Z_0 ∪ Z_1`, where `Z_i` is the centralizer of `s(y_i)` in `bF_{n,r}`.

- `J` survives: `chi(s(y_0)) = psi(y_0) = -a != 0` and `chi(s(y_1)) = -b != 0`.
- `C(J)` is one edge, since `s` is a homomorphism and `y_0`, `y_1` commute.
- `J` dominates `I` by definition.

It remains to show that `H := <Z_0 ∪ Z_1>` is all of `bF_{n,r}`.

(i) If `f` in `F_{n,r}` is the identity on `[0, eps]`, then `s(f)` is in `Z_0`: disjointly supported
elements commute, and `s` is a homomorphism. If `f` is the identity on `[r - eps, r]`, then `s(f)` is in
`Z_1`. In particular every power of `y_0` has `s(y_0^j)` in `Z_1`.

(ii) `s(F_{n,r}) ⊆ H`. Let `f` be in `F_{n,r}`. Near `0`, `f(x) = n^m x` for some `m`, and near `0`,
`y_0^{-m}(x) = n^m x`. So `f_1 = y_0^m f` is the identity on some `[0, delta]`. Pick `e' >= e` with
`delta' = n^{-e'} <= delta`. Let `v` be affine from `[0, delta']` onto `[0, eps]` (slope `n^{e'-e}`), any
PL map from `[delta', r - eps]` onto `[eps, r - eps]` (by Lemma 2: the lengths `r - eps - delta'` and
`r - 2 eps` are positive, since `eps <= 1/4`, and both have residue `res(r) - 2`), and the identity on
`[r - eps, r]`. Then:
- `s(v)` is in `Z_1` by (i);
- `f_2 = v f_1 v^{-1}` is the identity on `v([0, delta]) ⊇ [0, eps]`, so `s(f_2)` is in `Z_0`.

Hence `s(f) = s(y_0)^{-m} s(v)^{-1} s(f_2) s(v)` is in `H`.

(iii) `N ⊆ H`. By Lemma E, `alpha_t` is in `Z_0` for every type `t`. A winding `alpha(I, J)` of type
`t` is `s(g) alpha_t s(g)^{-1}` with `g` in `F_{n,r}`, and `s(g)` is in `H` by (ii). The windings
generate `N`.

Since `bF_{n,r} = N s(F_{n,r})`, `H = bF_{n,r}`, and (C3) gives the claim. ∎

**Corollary 11'.** By (T3), `[a chi_0 + b chi_1]` lies in `Sigma^1(F_{n,r})` whenever `a, b != 0`. The
same proof also works directly in `F_{n,r}`, with `J = {y_0, y_1}`. This recovers the Bieri–Neumann–Strebel
computation on the endpoint circle without citing it.

## 3. The antipodes of the holes

**Proposition 10.** `[-chi_0 o pi]` and `[-chi_1 o pi]` lie in `Sigma^1(bF_{n,r})`.

*Proof.* Take `psi = -chi_0`. By (C2), `[-chi_0]` is in `Sigma^infinity(F_{n,1}) ⊆ Sigma^1(F_{n,1})`,
and Lemma 6 transports this to `F_{n,r}`, preserving `chi_0` by (C1). Apply Lemma 8 of part 3 with
`E = {alpha_t}` (all types, including `EEE`). Let `alpha(I, J)` be a winding of type `t`, so
`alpha(I, J) = s(g) alpha_t s(g)^{-1}`. By Lemma E, `s(g y_0^k) alpha_t s(g y_0^k)^{-1} = alpha(I, J)` for
every integer `k`. Since `psi(g y_0^k) = psi(g) + k`, some `k` gives `psi(g y_0^k) >= 0`. The windings
generate `N`, so Lemma 8 applies. For `-chi_1`, use `y_1`: `-chi_1(y_1) = 1`. ∎

## 4. The holes

**Lemma H.** `[chi_0]` and `[chi_1]` are not in `Sigma^1(F_{n,r})`. Hence, by (T3),
`[chi_0 o pi]` and `[chi_1 o pi]` are not in `Sigma^1(bF_{n,r})`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 6 and (C1), it suffices to take `r = 1`. By (C2), `F_n = B * x_0` with `B = F_n(1)`,
which is finitely generated because it is isomorphic to `F_n`. The character `chi := -chi_0` has
`chi(B) = 0` and `chi(x_0) = 1`.

The extension is strictly ascending. Otherwise `x_0` normalizes `B`, so `F_n = B ⋊ <x_0>`, and
`B = ker chi_0`. By (C1), `ker chi_0` consists of the `f` with `f'(0+) = 1`, that is, the `f` that are the
identity on some `[0, delta]`. A finite subset lies in `K_delta = {f : f|[0, delta] = id}` for one
`delta`. But `K_delta` is a proper subgroup of `ker chi_0`: a copy of the `y_0` construction on a standard
interval inside `(0, delta)` is a nontrivial element of `ker chi_0` that is not the identity on
`[0, delta]`. So `ker chi_0` is not finitely generated, a contradiction.

(C4) now gives `[-chi] = [chi_0]` in `Sigma^1(F_n)^c`. For `chi_1`, conjugation by `x -> 1 - x` is an
automorphism of `F_n` that exchanges `chi_0` and `chi_1`. ∎

## 5. Proof of Theorem 12

Write `psi = a chi_0 + sum_i c_i psi_i + b chi_1`, using Zaremsky's basis on `F_{n,1}` transported by
Lemma 6.
- Some `c_i != 0`: Theorem 9 of part 3 puts `[psi o pi]` in `Sigma^1`.
- Otherwise `psi = a chi_0 + b chi_1` with `(a, b) != 0`:
  - `a, b != 0`: Theorem 11;
  - `b = 0, a < 0` or `a = 0, b < 0`: Proposition 10;
  - `b = 0, a > 0` or `a = 0, b > 0`: holes, by Lemma H. ∎

## 6. What this does not reach: Sigma^m, m >= 2, on pulled-back characters

Still open: `[psi o pi]` in `Sigma^m(bF_{n,r})` for `m >= 2` and `psi` outside the endpoint hull. Notes
from this attempt:

- (C3) is a `Sigma^1` statement, and there is no higher connected-and-dominating criterion to cite.
- **Morse route.** This would lift Zaremsky's Proposition 5.6 (arXiv:1502.02620) to the Skipper–Wu Stein
  space. Its key Lemma 5.5 fails verbatim: in the linear-disk complexes `LD`, a braided disk can meet
  every standard disk, so the "far from the endpoints" simplices no longer form a cone.
  Skipper–Wu Theorem 4.13 (`LMA_d(S, Z)` is `(floor((|Z|-1)/(3d-2)) - 1)`-connected for every set `Z` of
  allowed initial points) does handle restricted foot sets. So the missing step is only the ascending /
  descending bookkeeping in `(chi, f)`, not the connectivity input.
- **Deferred-subgroup route.** This generalizes Case 3 of arXiv:1803.02717 Theorem 5.1:
  `bF_{n,r} = bF(w) * s(x)`, then BGK Theorem 2.3 (Citation 1.11). The MMV Theorem 2.4 argument on the
  Bass–Serre tree gives the `Sigma^m` version from `F_m` of the base together with
  `[chi|base] in Sigma^m(base)`. It needs `F_infinity` for the `n`-ary deferred subgroups. Zaremsky
  obtains that from CAT(0) cube complexes `X(n)` (Theorem 4.6, Corollaries 4.7, 4.8). Skipper–Wu have no
  CAT(0) statement for `n >= 3`, so contractibility of the restricted Stein complex is the gap.
