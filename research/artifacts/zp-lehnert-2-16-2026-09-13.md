# Zaremsky Problem 2.16 (Lehnert's conjecture; cloning-system groups) — analysis

Lane z2-16-lehnert, 2026-09-13. Sources read this date; page/theorem numbers
verified from the PDFs unless marked "abstract only".

## The question (verbatim)

Zaremsky, *Some open problems* (July 12, 2026), §2 Problem 16: "Lehnert's
conjecture: Every group with context-free co-word problem embeds in Thompson's
group V. So, question, given a cloning system on a family of finite groups Gn,
is the Thompson-like group T(G∗) coCF and/or does it embed into V?"

Two sub-questions on cloning systems (Witzel–Zaremsky, arXiv:1405.5491; guide
Zaremsky arXiv:1606.08762) with every `G_n` finite:
- **Q1** coCF: is `T(G_*)` co-context-free? Read as *locally coCF* (every f.g.
  subgroup is coCF), since `T(G_*)` need not be f.g.; for f.g. `T(G_*)` this is
  literally coCF, as coCF passes to f.g. subgroups.
- **Q2** embed: does `T(G_*)` embed in `V`?

## Verified inputs

- coCF is generating-set independent and the class is closed under finite direct
  products, restricted wreath products with virtually free top, f.g. subgroups,
  and finite-index overgroups (Holt–Rees–Röver–Thomas, J. LMS 71 (2005) 643–657;
  restated in arXiv:1407.7745 p. 1). `V` is coCF (Lehnert–Schweitzer,
  arXiv:math/0507090).
- **Berns-Zieve–Fry–Gillings–Hoganson–Mathews, arXiv:1407.7745, Thm 4.3.**
  For every finite group `G` and homomorphism `θ: G → G`, the group `V_(G,θ)`
  is coCF. `V_(G,θ)` is the Thompson-like group of the twisted direct-power
  cloning system: `G_n = S_n ⋉ G^n` (finite), `ρ_n` the projection to `S_n`,
  cloning duplicates the split leaf's `G`-label with the second copy twisted by
  `θ`. Proof (p. 11–12): a pushdown automaton whose accepted language `𝓛_P`
  has `CoWP(V_(G,θ)) = (𝓛_P)°` (cyclic shift), and coCF is closed under cyclic
  shift (Maslov 1973). So Q1 = YES for the twisted direct-power / labeled-`V`
  family. These are also Berns-Zieve et al.'s proposed potential counterexamples
  to Lehnert for `θ ≠ id` (no obvious faithful Cantor action).
- Farley, arXiv:1406.4590: groups with a finite similarity structure (FSS /
  local similarity groups, Hughes) are coCF under a mild hypothesis; a subfamily
  is proposed as potential Lehnert counterexamples. (abstract + intro.)
- **Bishop–Bodart–Issini–Perego, arXiv:2601.13058 (Jan 21, 2026), Thm 2.7
  (obstruction tool).** Every coCF group `G` has period growth
  `p_G(n) ⪯ exp(n^4)` (and `p_G^{D_k}(n) ⪯ exp(n^2)`), where
  `p_G(n) = max{ord(g) : ‖g‖_S ≤ n}`. Period growth is monotone under
  embedding (Lemma 1.1). Also `p_V(n) ≍ exp(n^2)` (Thm 6.2), and the
  Brin–Thompson group `2V` has period growth not bounded by any recursive
  function, so `2V` is not coCF.
  ⇒ **A cloning system on finite groups whose `T(G_*)` has an element of order
  `> exp(n^4)` reachable by a word of length `n` gives Q1 = NO** (a f.g.
  `T(G_*)` of that kind is not coCF), refuting the general Q1 and, if that
  `T(G_*)` still embeds in `V`, contradicting nothing — but if not coCF it also
  cannot embed in `V`, so Q2 = NO there too.
- Bodart–D'Angeli–Perego–Rodaro, arXiv:2608.02111 (Aug 3, 2026): a f.g. group
  embeds in `V` iff it admits a faithful context-free action (class CF-TR of
  transition groups of context-free graphs); "all known examples of groups with
  co-context-free Word Problem do embed in V" (abstract). Every f.g. subgroup of
  `V` is virtually abelian or contains a free non-abelian sub-semigroup, so
  intermediate-growth, Basilica and Hanoï-Tower groups are not in `V`.
- Jaspars, arXiv:2608.01168 (Aug 2026): `G` embeds in `V` iff `G` is the
  transition group of a finite union of context-free automata, iff finitely many
  context-free subgroups of `G` have trivially intersecting cores. (abstract.)
- Bleak–Matucci–Neunhöffer, arXiv:1312.1855 (J. LMS 94 (2016)):
  `QAut(T_{2,c})` embeds in `V`, giving Lehnert's conjecture its current form.

## Logic between the parts (used to wire the root routes)

- `V` coCF + coCF closed under f.g. subgroups ⇒ **Q2 yes ⇒ Q1 yes**.
- Contrapositive: **Q1 no ⇒ Q2 no**.
- A f.g. coCF `T(G_*)` that embeds in no `V` refutes Lehnert (that is exactly
  why Zaremsky pairs the two): the `V_(G,θ)`, `θ ≠ id`, are the standing
  candidates. Settling Lehnert's conjecture in general settles neither Q1 nor Q2
  by itself.

## Program (Attempts recorded on the answer claims)

- Q1 (general, `finite-cloning-system-groups-are-locally-cocf`): positive for
  twisted direct powers (Thm 4.3) and FSS groups (Farley). To refute in general,
  build a finite-group cloning system with period growth `≻ exp(n^4)` (Thm 2.7).
  Open question this raises: can any cloning system on finite groups produce
  element orders growing faster than `V`'s `exp(n^2)`? Bounded-exponent families
  (`G_n = A^n`, signed permutations) cannot; a growing-exponent compatible
  cloning family is the thing to find or forbid.
- Q2 (`some-finite-cloning-system-group-does-not-embed-in-v`): the `V_(G,θ)`,
  `θ ≠ id`, candidate. But arXiv:2608.02111 says all currently known coCF groups
  embed in `V`; check whether their CF-TR machinery already places `V_(G,θ)` in
  `V` (that would push Q2 toward yes and make Lehnert's conjecture more likely).
