# Finite presentation of SFT crossed-product algebras is quantum rigidity

Lane `ex-fp-kazhdan-ring`, 2026-09-13. Unreviewed. It supports:
- `sft-crossed-product-fp-iff-quantum-rigid`;
- `positive-entropy-sft-crossed-products-are-not-fp`;
- `labbe-wang-shift-crossed-product-is-finitely-presented` (open);
- gate (a) of `sofic-fp-simple-kazhdan-group-from-fp-simple-ring`.

## 0. Setting and summary

`k` is a field. `Ω ⊆ A^(Z^2)` is a subshift of finite type with forbidden patterns supported in
`B_r = {h : |h| <= r}` (sup norm). `(σ^g x)(z) = x(z + g)`. Put

```text
R_Ω = LC(Ω, k) ⋊ Z^2,     u^g f u^(-g) = f o σ^g,
e_a = chi[x(0) = a],      P_h^a = u^h e_a u^(-h) = chi[x(h) = a].
```

`R_Ω` is generated as a `k`-algebra by the `e_a` and `u_1^(±1)`, `u_2^(±1)`: products of the
`P_h^a` give every cylinder. Write `L_F(Ω)` for the patterns on `F` that occur in `Ω`.

Summary.
1. (Theorem 1) `R_Ω` is finitely presented iff `Ω` is *quantum rigid*: every family of locally
   commuting, locally admissible idempotent partitions indexed by `Z^2` commutes globally.
2. (Theorem 2) Two configurations that agree on a thick wall and differ on both sides of it
   refute rigidity.
3. (Corollary 3) If `Ω` has positive entropy, such walls exist at every scale, so `R_Ω` is not
   finitely presented.
4. (Proposition 4) For a minimal zero-entropy SFT, both sides of every wall are infinite. This
   applies to Labbé's shift, so no finite-interior certificate exists there.
5. (Proposition 5, calibration) Ledrappier's shift has zero entropy and is rigid, so its crossed
   product is finitely presented. Zero entropy does not, by itself, prevent finite presentation.

## 1. Local presentations

For `D >= 1` let `A_D` be the `k`-algebra with generators `e_a` (`a ∈ A`) and `u_1^(±1)`,
`u_2^(±1)`, with `P_h^a := u^h e_a u^(-h)` as an abbreviation, and relations:
- (R1) `u_i u_i^(-1) = u_i^(-1) u_i = 1` and `u_1 u_2 = u_2 u_1`;
- (R2) `e_a e_b = delta_ab e_a` and `sum_a e_a = 1`;
- (R3) `e_a P_h^b = P_h^b e_a` for `|h| <= 2D`;
- (R4) `prod_(h ∈ B_D) P_h^(pi(h)) = 0` for every `pi : B_D -> A` not in `L_(B_D)(Ω)`, in a fixed
  order.

Conjugating (R3) by `u^g` gives `[P_g^a, P_(g')^b] = 0` whenever `|g - g'| <= 2D`. In particular
the order in (R4) is irrelevant.

**Lemma 1.**
1. `A_D` is finitely presented, and all its relations hold in `R_Ω`. So there is a surjection
   `q_D : A_D -> R_Ω`.
2. Let `xi` be a linear combination of words of length `<= D` in the generators that vanishes in
   `R_Ω`. Then `xi = 0` in `A_D`.
3. `R_Ω` is finitely presented iff `q_D` is an isomorphism for some `D`.

*Proof.*
1. The relation set is finite, and each relation holds for cylinder indicators.
2. **Normal form.** A word `x_1 ... x_m` with `m <= D` equals `P_(g_1)^(a_1) ... P_(g_s)^(a_s) u^g`
   in `A_D`, where the `g_j` are prefix sums of the `u`-letters, so `|g_j| <= D`. These `P` commute
   by (R3). So `xi = sum_g Q_g u^g` with `Q_g` in the commutative algebra generated by
   `{P_h^a : h ∈ B_D}`.
   - Inserting `1 = prod_(h ∈ B_D) sum_a P_h^a` writes `Q_g = sum_pi c_(g,pi) p_pi`, with
     `p_pi = prod_h P_h^(pi(h))`.
   - By (R4) only `pi ∈ L_(B_D)(Ω)` survive.
   - In `R_Ω` the elements `p_pi u^g` with `pi ∈ L_(B_D)(Ω)` are nonzero indicators of disjoint
     cylinders times distinct group elements, so they are linearly independent.
   - Hence `xi = 0` in `R_Ω` forces every `c_(g,pi) = 0`, and `xi = 0` in `A_D`.
3. If `R_Ω` is finitely presented, the kernel of `k<gens> -> R_Ω` is generated by finitely many
   combinations of words of length `<= D_0`. For `D >= D_0` part 2 puts them in the kernel of
   `k<gens> -> A_D`, so `q_D` is injective. The converse is part 1. QED

## 2. Quantum rigidity (Theorem 1)

**Definition.** `Ω` is *`D`-quantum rigid* if the following holds for every `k`-vector space `W`
and every family of idempotents `E_a(z) ∈ End_k(W)` (`a ∈ A`, `z ∈ Z^2`) such that:
- (Q1) `E_a(z) E_b(z) = delta_ab E_a(z)` and `sum_a E_a(z) = 1`;
- (Q2) `[E_a(z), E_b(z')] = 0` whenever `|z - z'| <= 2D`;
- (Q3) `prod_(h ∈ B_D) E_(pi(h))(z + h) = 0` for every `z` and every `pi ∉ L_(B_D)(Ω)`.

The conclusion is that all the `E_a(z)` commute pairwise. There is no translation invariance in
the hypothesis.

**Theorem 1.** `R_Ω` is finitely presented iff `Ω` is `D`-quantum rigid for some `D >= r`.

*Proof of "only if".*
1. By Lemma 1.3, `q_D` is an isomorphism for some `D`, and we may take `D >= r`. Fix a family on `W`.
2. **The representation.** Let `V` be the finitely supported functions `Z^2 -> W`, and put
   `(rho(e_a) xi)(w) = E_a(w) xi(w)` and `(rho(u^g) xi)(w) = xi(w + g)`.
3. **Covariance.** Then `(rho(P_h^a) xi)(w) = E_a(w + h) xi(w)`. So (R1) holds, and (Q1), (Q2), (Q3)
   give (R2), (R3), (R4) sitewise.
4. **Conclusion.** `rho` is a representation of `A_D = R_Ω`. In `R_Ω` the elements `e_a` and `P_h^b`
   commute for every `h`, so `[E_a(w), E_b(w + h)] = 0` for all `w` and `h`.

*Proof of "if".*
1. **The family on `A_D`.** Take `W = A_D` and let `E_a(z)` be left multiplication by `P_z^a`. (R2),
   (R3) and (R4), conjugated by `u^z`, give (Q1)–(Q3). Rigidity makes all `P_z^a` commute in `A_D`.
2. **The commutative part.** Let `C` be the subalgebra generated by the `P_z^a`. It is commutative,
   generated by idempotent partitions, and kills every pattern outside `L_(B_D)(Ω)` on every
   translated `D`-ball. The universal such algebra is `LC(Y_D, k)`, the locally constant functions
   on the Stone space
   `Y_D = {x : every D-ball pattern of x lies in L(Ω)}`,
   by Stone duality for the Boolean algebra of its idempotents. Since `D >= r`, `Y_D = Ω`. So there
   is a unital homomorphism `LC(Ω, k) -> C` with `chi[x(z)=a] -> P_z^a`.
3. **Covariance.** It is covariant for `u^g`.
4. **Universal property.** The algebraic crossed product is universal for covariant pairs:
   `sum f_g u^g -> sum pi(f_g) U^g` is multiplicative by covariance. This gives `R_Ω -> A_D`,
   identity on generators.
5. **Conclusion.** Both composites with `q_D` are identities on generators, so `q_D` is an
   isomorphism, and `R_Ω` is finitely presented by Lemma 1. QED

The proof uses no simplicity, entropy or minimality. The same argument runs for any finitely
presented group acting on its own SFTs, with word-metric balls and the group's relators in (R1).
That version is not written out here.

## 3. Wall certificates (Theorem 2)

**Theorem 2.** Suppose there are `T_1, T_2 ∈ Ω` and a partition `Z^2 = I ⊔ M ⊔ O` with:
- `d(I, O) > 2D`;
- `T_1 = T_2` on `M`;
- points `z_I ∈ I` and `z_O ∈ O` where `T_1` and `T_2` differ.

Then `Ω` is not `D`-quantum rigid.

*Proof.*
1. **The family.** Let `W = k^2` with basis `w_1, w_2`, and put `w_1' = w_1 + w_2`, `w_2' = w_2`.
   Define `E_a(z) = diag([T_1(z) = a], [T_2(z) = a])`, in the basis `(w_1, w_2)` for `z ∈ I ∪ M`
   and in the basis `(w_1', w_2')` for `z ∈ O`. On `M` the two diagonal entries agree, so `E_a(z)`
   is `0` or `1` there, the same in both bases.
2. **(Q1).** It is clear.
3. **(Q2).** Two points at distance `<= 2D` cannot lie in `I` and `O` respectively. So both lie in
   `I ∪ M` (diagonal in one basis), or both in `O ∪ M` (diagonal in the other, scalars on `M`).
4. **(Q3).** A ball `z + B_D` has diameter `2D`, so it lies in `I ∪ M` or in `O ∪ M`. There the
   product is `diag(prod_h [T_1(z+h) = pi(h)], prod_h [T_2(z+h) = pi(h)])`, which is `0` for
   `pi ∉ L(Ω)` because `T_1, T_2 ∈ Ω`.
5. **Noncommutation.** Put `a = T_1(z_I)` and `c = T_1(z_O)`. Then `P = E_a(z_I)` fixes `w_1` and
   kills `w_2`, while `Q = E_c(z_O)` fixes `w_1 + w_2` and kills `w_2`, so `Q w_1 = w_1 + w_2`.
   Hence `PQ w_1 = P(w_1 + w_2) = w_1 != w_1 + w_2 = QP w_1`. QED

## 4. Positive entropy (Corollary 3)

**Corollary 3.** If `h_top(Ω) > 0`, then for every `D` there is a wall certificate. So `Ω` is not
quantum rigid, and `R_Ω` is not finitely presented.

*Proof.* Fix `D` and put `w = max(2D, 2r) + 1`.
1. **A recurrent support.** By the variational principle there is an ergodic `σ`-invariant
   measure `mu` with `h_mu > 0`. Put `X = supp mu`, so `h_top(X) > 0`.
2. **Pigeonhole.** `|L_(B_(n+w))(X)| >= exp(c n^2)` for some `c > 0` and all large `n`. The annulus
   `M_n = B_(n+w) \ B_n` carries at most `|A|^(O(w n))` patterns. So for large `n` some annulus
   pattern `p ∈ L(X)` has two fillings `f != f'` of `B_n` with `f ∪ p` and `f' ∪ p` in `L(X)`.
3. **Two occurrences.** The cylinder `[p]` meets `X = supp mu`, so `mu([p]) > 0`. Poincaré
   recurrence gives `x ∈ X` with `x|_(M_n) = p` and a `g` with `|g| > 2(n + w) + 2D` and
   `(σ^g x)|_(M_n) = p`.
4. **The configurations.** Let `T_1` be `x` with `f` written on `B_n` and on `B_n + g`, and `T_2` be
   `x` with `f'` on both. Every `r`-ball meeting a rewritten box lies inside `B_(n+2r) + (0 or g)`,
   which is inside `B_(n+w)` or its translate. There `T_i` reads a subpattern of `f ∪ p` or `f' ∪ p`,
   which is admissible, and elsewhere `T_i = x`. So `T_1, T_2 ∈ Ω`.
5. **The wall.** Take `I = B_n`, `M = M_n`, and `O` the rest. Then `d(I, O) = w + 1 > 2D`,
   `T_1 = T_2` on `M`, they differ in `B_n`, and they differ in `B_n + g ⊆ O`. Apply Theorem 2
   and Theorem 1. QED

**Examples.** Full shifts, and every SFT with positive entropy. The ring route needs zero entropy.

## 5. Minimal zero-entropy SFTs (Proposition 4)

**Proposition 4.** Let `Ω` be a minimal SFT and `D >= r`. If a wall certificate has `I` finite,
then `h_top(Ω) > 0`. The same holds with `O` finite, by exchanging the roles.

*Proof.*
1. **The flip pattern.** Points within distance `2D` of `I` lie in `I ∪ M`. So `T_1 = T_2` on the
   annulus `N = N_(2D)(I) \ I`, with `p = T_1|_N`. The fillings `f_i = T_i|_I` differ.
2. **Syndetic occurrences.** By minimality `p` occurs syndetically in every `x ∈ Ω`. Take
   occurrences at `g_1, ..., g_m` inside `B_n`, pairwise far apart, with `m >= c n^2`.
3. **Independent flips.** Writing `f_1` or `f_2` independently at each occurrence gives a locally
   admissible configuration. Any `r`-ball meeting `I + g_j` lies in `N_(2r)(I) + g_j ⊆ N_(2D)(I) + g_j`,
   where it reads a pattern of `T_1` or `T_2`. So the result is in `Ω`.
4. **Counting.** This gives `2^m` distinct patterns on a box of side `O(n)`, so `h_top > 0`. QED

**Self-similar subshifts have zero entropy.** Suppose `X = φ(X)` up to shift, for a substitution
`φ` sending letters to blocks with expansion `λ > 1` in both directions. Every `n`-box pattern is
then a subpattern of `φ` applied to a `(n/λ + c)`-box pattern, at `O(n^2)` offsets. So
`P(n) <= C n^2 P(n/λ + c)`, and `log P(n) = O((log n)^2)`, which is `o(n^2)`.

Labbé's `Ω_U` is self-similar and minimal (Labbé, arXiv:1802.03265, Theorem 1, quoted in
`research/artifacts/fp-simple-amenable-z2-sft-2026-09-12.md`). By Proposition 4, every wall
certificate for `Ω_U` has two infinite sides.

## 6. Calibration: Ledrappier's shift is rigid (Proposition 5)

Let `p` be a prime and `X_L = {x ∈ F_p^(Z^2) : x(z + e_2) = x(z) + x(z + e_1)}`. It is an SFT with
`r = 1` and entropy `0`: the bottom row and left column of a box determine it.

**Proposition 5.** If `k` contains a primitive `p`-th root of unity `zeta` and `p != char k`, then
`X_L` is `1`-quantum rigid, so `R_(X_L)` is finitely presented.

*Proof.*
1. **Observables.** Put `X(z) = sum_a zeta^a E_a(z)`. It is invertible, and the `E_a(z)` are
   polynomials in it, since `p` is invertible in `k`. `X(z)`, `X(z + e_1)` and `X(z + e_2)` commute
   by (Q2), and by (Q3) their joint eigenpatterns obey the rule. So
   `X(z + e_2) = X(z) X(z + e_1)`.
2. **Rows commute.** Claim: `[X(w), X(w + j e_1)] = 1` for all `w` and all `j`, by induction on `j`.
   The cases `j = 0, 1` are (Q2). For `k >= 2`, put `a = X(z)`, `b = X(z + e_1)`,
   `c = X(z + (k-1)e_1)`, `d = X(z + k e_1)`.
   - By induction, `a` commutes with `b` and `c`, `b` with `c` and `d`, and `c` with `d`.
   - The row above gives `[ab, cd] = 1`, from the case `j = k - 1` at `w = z + e_2`.
   - So `abcd = cdab`. The left side is `acdb`, so `acd = cda`, that is `cad = cda`, and `ad = da`.
3. **Everything commutes.** By step 1, `X(z + (j, m))` with `m >= 0` is a product of `X`'s in row
   `z_2` at positions `z_1 + j, ..., z_1 + j + m`. So it commutes with `X(z)` by step 2. The case
   `m < 0` is symmetric. QED

This is Baumslag's lamp computation. Fourier transform identifies `R_(X_L)` with the group algebra
of `F_p[t^(±1), (1+t)^(-1)] ⋊ Z^2`, which is recalled, not re-read, as finitely presented
(Baumslag 1972, Baumslag–Remeslennikov). That is consistent with Proposition 5.

**Lesson.** Rigidity comes from permutive propagation of commutation from short range to long
range. Ledrappier's shift has fixed points, so its crossed product is not simple. The open
question is whether an aperiodic minimal SFT can propagate commutation in the same way.

## 7. Consequences for the ring route

- By Theorem 1, gate (a) for `R_U = LC(Ω_U, F_q) ⋊ Z^2` is exactly quantum rigidity of Labbé's shift.
- **Dead certificates.**
  - Finite-interior walls (Proposition 4).
  - The two-basis construction of Theorem 2 needs `T_1 != T_2` agreeing on a wall of width `> 2D`
    that separates two infinite regions where they differ.
  - In hierarchical tilings the known ambiguities are fault lines. On each side of a fault the
    tiling is a single infinite supertile, and no pair differing along two parallel faults is
    known. So the natural walls look one-sided. This is heuristic and has not been checked for
    `Ω_U`.
- **Open.** Families that are not piecewise diagonal. Constraint systems of this type can have
  operator solutions with no classical counterpart, as the magic square does, so wall rigidity
  need not imply quantum rigidity.
- **If `Ω_U` is rigid.** `R_U` is a finitely presented infinite-dimensional simple algebra over a
  finite field with a unital rank model. Simplicity and the rank model are to be written in the
  next artifact.
- **If `Ω_U` is not rigid.** Some non-classical local tiling of Labbé's tiles exists, and the
  route has to move to another minimal zero-entropy SFT.
