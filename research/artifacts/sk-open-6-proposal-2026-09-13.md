## (a) Statement: abstract, theorem, and one remark

**Current, l.28-34 (abstract):**
```
For every infinite minimal subshift $X$, the group
$\EL_3(\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes\Z)$ is infinite, simple, finitely generated,
and has property~\textup{(T)}. It is locally embeddable into finite
groups, hence sofic and hyperlinear. This answers the questions of
Brown, Ozawa, and Pestov. The proof combines periodic approximation
with a commutator supported on a finite tower. Every Turing degree
occurs as the word-problem degree of one of these groups.
```
**Replacement:**
```

is not periodic, and let $R_x$ be the ring of linear maps of
$\F_2[\Z]$ generated by the shift and by multiplication with the

finite groups, hence sofic and hyperlinear. This answers the questions
of Brown, Ozawa, and Pestov. Every Turing degree occurs as the

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $X\subseteq A^{\Z}$ be an infinite minimal subshift over a finite
alphabet, with shift $T$. Then
\[
  G_X=\EL_3\bigl(\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes_T\Z\bigr)
\]

\end{theorem}
```
**Replacement:**
```
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $x\colon\Z\to A$ be a uniformly recurrent sequence over a finite
alphabet that is not periodic. Let $R_x$ be the ring of linear maps of
$V=\F_2[\Z]$ generated by $\sigma^{\pm1}$, where $\sigma\delta_n=\delta_{n+1}$,
and by the maps $D_a\delta_n=[x_n=a]\,\delta_n$ for $a\in A$. Then
$G_x=\EL_3(R_x)$ is an infinite finitely generated simple group with
Kazhdan's property~\textup{(T)} that is locally embeddable into finite
groups \textup{(LEF)}. In particular, it is sofic and hyperlinear.
\end{theorem}

Here $x$ is uniformly recurrent if every word occurring in $x$ occurs in
every sufficiently long window of $x$. The Fibonacci word is an example.
Equivalently, the orbit closure $X$ of $x$ is an infinite minimal
subshift. Evaluating along the orbit of $x$ gives an isomorphism
$\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes\Z\cong R_x$, so $G_x$ depends only on the words of $x$.
Both hypotheses are needed. If $x$ has period $N$, then
$R_x\cong M_N(\F_2[t,t^{-1}])$ and $G_x=\SL_{3N}(\F_2[t,t^{-1}])$ is
residually finite. If some word of $x$ does not recur boundedly, some
sequence $y$ in the orbit closure omits it, and the operators
$\sum_jD_{P_j}\sigma^j$ whose windows $P_j$ contain no word of $y$ form a
proper nonzero ideal.
```

## (b1) Proof, first part: the ring, (T), separated occurrences, simplicity and centre

**Current, l.69-116** (from `\subsection*{The ring and property (T)}` through `Thus $Z(G)=\{1\}$.`). **Replacement:**
```
\subsection*{The ring and property (T)}
For $P\subseteq A^{2k+1}$ let $D_P\delta_n=[x_{[n-k,n+k]}\in P]\,\delta_n$,
and write $D_v=D_{\{v\}}$. Products of the operators
$\sigma^{-t}D_a\sigma^{t}$, which multiply $\delta_n$ by $[x_{n+t}=a]$, give
every $D_P$, and $\sigma D_P\sigma^{-1}$ is again of this form. So $R_x$ is
spanned by the operators $D_P\sigma^j$. A sum $\sum_jD_{P_j}\sigma^j$ vanishes
only if no $P_j$ contains a word of $x$.

Put $G=\EL_3(R_x)$ and $e_{ij}(r)=I_3+rE_{ij}$. The elementary identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elementary}
 \begin{aligned}
 e_{ij}(r+s)&=e_{ij}(r)e_{ij}(s),\qquad i\ne j,\\
 [e_{ik}(r),e_{kj}(s)]&=e_{ij}(rs),\qquad i,j,k\text{ distinct},
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
show that $G$ is generated by the matrices $e_{ij}(s)$ with
$s\in\{\sigma,\sigma^{-1}\}\cup\{D_a:a\in A\}$. Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain's
theorem says that $\EL_n(R)$ has property~(T) for every finitely generated
unital associative ring $R$ and $n\ge3$~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}. So $G$ has
property~(T). It is infinite because the $e_{12}(\sigma^j)$ are distinct.

\emph{Separated occurrences.} For every $L$ there is $k$ such that any two
occurrences in $x$ of a word of length $2k+1$ are more than $L$ apart.
Indeed, $x$ is not eventually periodic, so it has more than $L$ words of
length $L$~\cite{MorseHedlund}, and all of them occur in every window of
some length $M$. A word of length $M+L$ occurring at two positions at
distance $d\le L$ spans a segment of period $d$ containing all these words,
and a word of period $d$ has at most $d$ subwords of length $L$.

\emph{$R_x$ is simple and $Z(R_x)=\F_2$.} After multiplying by a power of
$\sigma$, a nonzero ideal contains $r=\sum_{|j|\le w}D_{P_j}\sigma^j$ with
$D_{P_0}\ne0$. Extend a word of $x$ in $P_0$, on both sides, to a word $v$
of $x$ whose occurrences are more than $w$ apart. Then
$D_v\sigma^jD_v=0$ for $0<|j|\le w$, so $D_vrD_v=D_v$. By uniform recurrence
$v$ occurs in every window of some length $M$, so the ideal contains
$1=1-\prod_{0\le i<M}(1-\sigma^iD_v\sigma^{-i})$.
If $r$ is central, comparing the coefficients of $\delta_{m+j}$ in
$D_vr\delta_m$ and $rD_v\delta_m$ shows that $[x_{[m+j-k,m+j+k]}\in P_j]=0$
whenever a word $v$ occurs at $m+j$ and not at $m$; by separated
occurrences this forces $D_{P_j}=0$ for $j\ne0$. Commuting with $\sigma$
makes $[x_{[n-k,n+k]}\in P_0]$ independent of $n$, so $r\in\{0,1\}$.
Finally, commuting with every $e_{ij}(1)$ forces a central element of
$G$ to be $cI_3$; commuting with all $e_{ij}(r)$ gives
$c\in Z(R_x)^\times=\{1\}$. Thus $Z(G)=\{1\}$.
```
Notes for the verifier:
- "Occurs at n" means centered at n.
- Every occurrence of v has its central (2k+1)-window in P_0, so D_vD_{P_0}D_v = D_v.
- σ^jD_vσ^{-j}δ_n = [v at n−j]δ_n, so D_v σ^j D_v = D_v(σ^jD_vσ^{-j})σ^j = 0 when occurrences are more than |j| apart.
- A uniformly recurrent sequence that is eventually periodic is periodic, so "not periodic" gives "not eventually periodic", and Morse–Hedlund applies.
