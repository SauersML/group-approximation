# Central C(X)-fibers and quasitrace linearity

Swarm artifact, 2026-09-16.  Target: `stw99-problem-i-quasitraces-are-traces`.
Angle: special case / strengthening.

## 0. Summary and what is new

`QL(A)` means that every bounded 2-quasitrace on `A` is a trace.

**Theorem 4.1 (central fiber principle).**  Let `A` be a unital C\*-algebra, `X` a
compact Hausdorff space and `theta: C(X) -> Z(A)` a unital star homomorphism.  For
`x in X`, let `J_x` be the closed two-sided ideal of `A` generated by
`theta(C_0(X \ {x}))`, and put `A_x = A/J_x`.  Then

```text
QL(A)   <=>   QL(A_x) for every x in X.
```

More precisely, every extreme normalized quasitrace on `A` factors through exactly
one fiber `A_p` (Proposition 4.2).

Consequences proved here:

- **Corollary 5.1.**  `QL(C(X,B)) <=> QL(B)` for unital `B` and nonempty `X`.
- **Corollary 5.2 (strengthening of Haagerup's theorem).**  A unital C(X)-algebra
  all of whose central fibers are exact has `QL`.  The algebra itself need not be
  exact.
- **Corollary 5.3.**  For unital `A_i`, `i in I`:
  `QL(prod_i A_i) <=> QL(A_i)` for all `i` and `QL(prod_omega A_i)` for every free
  ultrafilter `omega` on `I`.
- **Corollary 5.4 (Glimm form).**  `QL(A)` holds iff `QL(A / closure(m A))` holds
  for every maximal ideal `m` of `Z(A)`.
- **Proposition 6.2.**  A direct, self-contained proof that every bounded
  2-quasitrace on `W = prod_i M_(n_i)`, or on any quotient of `W`, is a trace.  It
  closes the open hole `stw01-matrix-product-algebras-have-qt-linearity`.  The
  proof uses only axioms (i)-(iii) and shows `tau = tau o T` on `W_sa`, where `T` is
  the center-valued trace.
- **Corollary 6.3.**  `C(X, W/J)` has `QL` for every compact `X` and every quotient
  `W/J` of a product of matrix algebras.  More generally this holds for every unital
  C(X)-algebra whose central fibers are exact or quotients of matrix products.

What is new, relative to the graph:

1. The graph's permanence theorems are:
   - extensions (`stw01-extensions-preserve-qt-linearity`);
   - transfinite filtrations;
   - directed limits;
   - central-projection ideal extensions.

   All of them glue along *ideals*.  Theorem 4.1 glues along *points of a central
   spectrum*.  Over a connected base such as `X = [0,1]` there are no nontrivial
   central projections.  The ideals `C_0(U)A` are again C_0(U)-algebras whose
   linearity is the same kind of question.  So none of those theorems yields, for
   example, `QL(C([0,1], prod_n M_n))`, which follows from Corollary 6.3.
2. Corollary 5.2 strengthens Haagerup's exact theorem (Theorem 5.11 in the 2014
   paper) from exact algebras to fiberwise exact C(X)-algebras.
3. Corollary 5.3 reduces linearity for arbitrary `l^infinity` products to
   ultraproducts.  This is the direction not available from quotient pullback.
4. Proposition 6.2 supplies the missing direct route for the matrix-product hole.
   The algorithmic core, the balancing lemma, is already in
   `research/artifacts/stw01-certificates-products-universal-tests-2026-09-11.md`
   §5.  The version here avoids the additivity-on-positives criterion.

**Novelty caveat.**  The central-cut device goes back to Haagerup: his Proposition
4.6 cuts a quasitrace by a central projection of the AW\*-completion.  The
localization to fibers of an arbitrary central `C(X)` is a short consequence of
Haagerup's extreme-point architecture.  For linear traces it is standard that
extreme traces live on fibers.  We did not find the quasitrace statement in the
sources listed in §9, but it may be folklore.

No explicit example is certified to lie outside every class the graph has already
established *and* outside the classes obtained by combining those theorems with
exactness.  The examples `C([0,1], prod_n M_n)` of Corollary 6.3 are nonexact.  They
are not obtained from the graph's ideal-gluing theorems alone.

## 1. Conventions and standing inputs

All algebras in the main results are unital.  Haagerup's Definition 3.1 (read from
the PDF on 2026-09-16) defines a quasitrace on `A` as a function `tau: A -> C` with
four properties:

```text
(i)   tau(x*x) = tau(xx*) >= 0                     for all x in A;
(ii)  tau(a + ib) = tau(a) + i tau(b)               for a, b in A_sa;
(iii) tau is linear on every abelian C*-subalgebra B of A;
(iv)  there is tau_2: M_2(A) -> C satisfying (i)-(iii) with
      tau(x) = tau_2(x (x) e_11).
```

`QT(A)` is the set of normalized (`tau(1) = 1`) quasitraces, and `T(A)` is the
tracial state space.  These are the bounded 2-quasitraces of the graph.

**(F0) Pullback.**  If `pi: B -> C` is a star homomorphism and `sigma` is a quasitrace
on `C`, then `sigma o pi` is a quasitrace on `B`, witnessed in (iv) by
`sigma_2 o (pi (x) id_(M_2))`.  The reasons:

- `pi` preserves adjoints, products and self-adjointness;
- `pi` maps an abelian C\*-subalgebra onto an abelian C\*-subalgebra, since images of
  C\*-algebras are closed.

If `pi` is surjective and `sigma o pi` is linear, then `sigma` is linear.  Hence
quotients of `QL` algebras are `QL`.

**(F1) Positivity and vanishing.**  Let `tau` be a quasitrace on unital `A`.

- For `a in A_sa`, `tau` restricted to the abelian algebra `C*(1,a)` is linear by
  (iii).
- It is positive by (i), because `b >= 0` is `(b^(1/2))* b^(1/2)`.
- So it is a positive functional of norm `tau(1)`, and `|tau(a)| <= tau(1) ||a||`.
- By (ii), `|tau(x)| <= 2 tau(1) ||x||` for all `x`.

In particular `tau(1) = 0` forces `tau = 0`.

**(F2) Unitary invariance on self-adjoints.**

- If `y >= 0` and `u` is unitary, put `z = u y^(1/2)`.  Then `z*z = y` and
  `zz* = uyu*`, so `tau(uyu*) = tau(y)` by (i).
- For `y in A_sa`, write `y = y_+ - y_-` inside `C*(y)`.  Then `uyu*` equals
  `uy_+u* - uy_-u*` inside `C*(uyu*)`, and linearity there gives
  `tau(uyu*) = tau(y)`.

**(F3) Compact convex state spaces.**  `QT(A)` is convex: if `tau, sigma` are in
`QT(A)` with witnesses `tau_2, sigma_2`, then `t tau + (1-t) sigma` satisfies
(i)-(iii), witnessed by `t tau_2 + (1-t) sigma_2`.

For normalized `tau`:

- Put `v = 1 (x) e_12`.  Axiom (i) for `tau_2` gives
  `tau_2(1 (x) e_22) = tau_2(1 (x) e_11) = 1`.
- The two corners commute, so `tau_2(1_2) = 2`.
- By (F1) for `tau_2`, `|tau_2(y)| <= 4||y||`.

Consider the set `P` of pairs `(tau, tau_2)` in the compact product

```text
prod_(x in A) D(0, 2||x||)  x  prod_(y in M_2(A)) D(0, 4||y||)
```

that satisfy (i)-(iii) for both coordinates, `tau(1) = 1` and
`tau_2(x (x) e_11) = tau(x)`.  Condition (iii) says that
`tau(x + y) = tau(x) + tau(y)` and `tau(lambda x) = lambda tau(x)` whenever
`x, x*, y, y*` pairwise commute.  Every defining condition involves finitely many
coordinates and is closed, so `P` is compact.  `QT(A)` is its image under the
continuous first-coordinate projection, hence compact.

`T(A)` is a closed convex subset of `QT(A)`.  A tracial state `tau` belongs to
`QT(A)` with witness `tau_2((x_jk)) = tau(x_11) + tau(x_22)`.  Haagerup uses exactly
this compact convex set and the Krein--Milman theorem in his proof of Theorem 5.11.

**(F4) Descent (Haagerup, Proposition 3.2, verified text).**  If `tau` is a
quasitrace on `A`, then `N_tau = {x in A : tau(x*x) = 0}` is a closed two-sided
ideal.  There is a unique quasitrace `bar_tau` on `A/N_tau` with
`tau = bar_tau o rho`, where `rho` is the quotient map.

**(F4') Descent through a smaller ideal.**  Let `J` be a closed ideal with
`J subset N_tau`, and let `pi: A/J -> A/N_tau` be the induced map.  Then

```text
tau = tau_J o q_J,        tau_J = bar_tau o pi,
```

and `tau_J` is a quasitrace on `A/J` by (F0).  The graph route
`stw01-qt-invisible-ideal-extension-proof` uses the same lemma.

## 2. Central cuts

**Lemma 2.1.**  Let `tau` be a quasitrace on `A`, let `z in Z(A)` with `z >= 0`, and
define `tau_z(x) = tau(zx)`.

1. `tau_z` is a quasitrace on `A`.
2. If moreover `0 <= z <= 1`, then `tau = tau_z + tau_(1-z)`.

*Proof.*  The map `x -> zx` is not a homomorphism.  The proof only uses two facts:
it maps commuting families to commuting families, and it relates `x*x` and `xx*`
correctly.

(i) Put `w = z^(1/2) x`.  Since `z^(1/2)` is central,

```text
w*w = x* z x = z x*x,        ww* = z^(1/2) x x* z^(1/2) = z xx*.
```

Axiom (i) for `tau` gives `tau_z(x*x) = tau(w*w) = tau(ww*) = tau_z(xx*) >= 0`.

(ii) For `a, b in A_sa`, the elements `za` and `zb` are self-adjoint because `z` is
central and self-adjoint.  So `tau(z(a + ib)) = tau(za) + i tau(zb)`.

(iii) If `B` is an abelian C\*-subalgebra, then `C*(B, z)` is abelian because `z` is
central.  It contains `zx` for every `x in B`.  The map `x -> zx` is linear, and
`tau` is linear on `C*(B, z)`, so `tau_z` is linear on `B`.

(iv) `z (x) 1_2` is a positive central element of `M_2(A)`.  Applying (i)-(iii) to
`tau_2` and `z (x) 1_2` shows that `(tau_2)_(z (x) 1_2)` satisfies (i)-(iii).  Also

```text
(tau_2)_(z (x) 1_2)(x (x) e_11) = tau_2(zx (x) e_11) = tau(zx).
```

For part 2, let `a in A_sa`.  The algebra `C*(1, a, z)` is abelian and contains
`a`, `za` and `(1-z)a`.  Hence `tau(a) = tau(za) + tau((1-z)a)`.  The general case
follows from (ii).  QED.

## 3. Extreme quasitraces are multiplicative on the center

**Proposition 3.1.**  Let `tau` be an extreme point of `QT(A)`.  Then
`tau(zx) = tau(z) tau(x)` for every self-adjoint `z in Z(A)` and every `x in A`.  In
particular `tau` restricts to a character of `Z(A)`.

*Proof.*  First let `0 <= z <= 1` be central and put `t = tau(z)`.  This lies in
`[0,1]` by (F1).

- **Case `t in (0,1)`.**  By Lemma 2.1,
  `tau = t (tau_z / t) + (1-t)(tau_(1-z) / (1-t))`.  Both normalized cuts lie in
  `QT(A)`, so extremality gives `tau_z = t tau`.
- **Case `t = 0`.**  `tau_z` is a quasitrace with `tau_z(1) = 0`, hence `tau_z = 0`
  by (F1).
- **Case `t = 1`.**  `tau_(1-z)(1) = 0`, so `tau_(1-z) = 0` and `tau_z = tau`.

In every case `tau(zx) = t tau(x)`.

Now let `z` be any nonzero central self-adjoint element and put
`u = (z + ||z||)/(2||z||)`, so that `0 <= u <= 1` is central.  Take `x in A_sa`.
The algebra `C*(1, z, x)` is abelian, so `tau` is linear on the relevant
combinations of `x`, `zx` and `ux`.  From `tau(ux) = tau(u) tau(x)` we get
`tau(zx) = tau(z) tau(x)`.

In detail: linearity on `C*(1, z, x)` gives
`tau(ux) = (tau(zx) + ||z|| tau(x)) / (2||z||)` and
`tau(u) = (tau(z) + ||z||) / (2||z||)`.  For a general `x`, apply (ii) to
`zx = z Re(x) + i z Im(x)`, whose two summands are self-adjoint.

For the character statement, `tau` is linear on the abelian algebra `Z(A)`.  Write
central elements as `z = z_1 + i z_2` and `w = w_1 + i w_2` with `z_j, w_k` central
and self-adjoint.  Then

```text
zw = z_1 w_1 - z_2 w_2 + i (z_1 w_2 + z_2 w_1),
```

and the self-adjoint case gives `tau(zw) = tau(z) tau(w)`.  Together with
`tau(1) = 1`, this makes `tau` restricted to `Z(A)` a character.

We do not claim `tau(zx) = tau(z) tau(x)` for non-self-adjoint central `z` and
arbitrary `x`; it is not needed.  QED.

**Remark 3.2.**  This is the C\*-level shadow of Haagerup's Proposition 4.6.  There
an extreme faithful quasitrace has a factorial AW\*-completion, and the proof splits
`tau` along a central projection `p` of the completion as
`tau_1 = bar_tau(p pi(.))` and `tau_2 = bar_tau((1-p) pi(.))`.  Lemma 2.1 replaces
the central projection of the completion by an arbitrary central positive element of
`A` itself.  No AW\*-completion is needed.

## 4. The central fiber principle

Throughout this section:

- `theta: C(X) -> Z(A)` is a unital star homomorphism;
- `J_x` is the closed ideal generated by `theta(C_0(X \ {x}))`, which is the closure
  of `theta(C_0(X \ {x})) A` because the image is central;
- `A_x = A/J_x` and `q_x: A -> A_x` is the quotient map.

**Theorem 4.1.**  `QL(A)` holds iff `QL(A_x)` holds for all `x in X`.

*Proof.*  The "only if" direction is (F0), since each `A_x` is a quotient of `A`.

For "if", let `tau` be extreme in `QT(A)`.

1. By Proposition 3.1, `tau o theta` is a unital multiplicative linear functional on
   `C(X)`.  So `tau o theta = ev_p` for a unique `p in X`.
2. Let `f in C_0(X \ {p})` be real.  Then `theta(f)` is self-adjoint and
   `tau(theta(f)* theta(f)) = tau(theta(f^2)) = f(p)^2 = 0`.  Hence
   `theta(f) in N_tau`.
3. For complex `f`, the real and imaginary parts also vanish at `p`, so
   `theta(f) in N_tau`.
4. `N_tau` is a closed two-sided ideal by (F4), so `J_p subset N_tau`.
5. By (F4'), `tau = tau_p o q_p` for a quasitrace `tau_p` on `A_p`.  By hypothesis
   `tau_p` is linear, so `tau` is linear.

Thus `ext QT(A) subset T(A)`.  By (F3) and the Krein--Milman theorem, `QT(A)` is the
closed convex hull of its extreme points.  So `QT(A) subset T(A)`, because `T(A)` is
closed and convex.

Finally, a bounded quasitrace `tau` with `tau(1) = 0` is zero by (F1).  Otherwise
`tau/tau(1)` lies in `QT(A)`.  QED.

**Proposition 4.2 (extreme points).**  Write `q_x^* sigma = sigma o q_x`.  Then

```text
ext QT(A) = disjoint union over x in X of q_x^*( ext QT(A_x) ).
```

*Proof.*  First, the union is disjoint.  For `sigma in QT(A_x)` and `f in C(X)`,
`q_x(theta(f)) = f(x) 1`, because `f - f(x)` lies in `C_0(X \ {x})`.  Hence
`(q_x^* sigma) o theta = ev_x`, and the point `x` is determined by the quasitrace.

**Inclusion "subset".**  Let `tau` be extreme.  The proof of Theorem 4.1 gives
`tau = tau_p o q_p`.  Suppose `tau_p = t s_1 + (1-t) s_2` with `s_j in QT(A_p)` and
`0 < t < 1`.  Then `tau = t q_p^* s_1 + (1-t) q_p^* s_2`, so `q_p^* s_j = tau`.
Since `q_p` is surjective, `s_j = tau_p`.

**Inclusion "superset".**  Let `sigma` be extreme in `QT(A_p)` and put
`tau = q_p^* sigma`.  Suppose `tau = t s_1 + (1-t) s_2` with `s_j in QT(A)` and
`0 < t < 1`.

- For `0 <= f in C_0(X \ {p})`, `tau(theta(f)) = f(p) = 0` and `s_j(theta(f)) >= 0`.
  Hence `s_j(theta(f)) = 0`.
- Applying this to `f^2` for real `f` gives `theta(f) in N_(s_j)`, so
  `J_p subset N_(s_j)`.
- By (F4'), `s_j = q_p^* s_j'` for some `s_j' in QT(A_p)`.
- Then `sigma = t s_1' + (1-t) s_2'`, so `s_j' = sigma` and `s_j = tau`.

QED.

## 5. Corollaries

**Corollary 5.1 (trivial fields).**  Let `B` be unital and `X` a nonempty compact
Hausdorff space.  Then `QL(C(X,B)) <=> QL(B)`.

*Proof.*  Put `theta(f) = f (x) 1_B`, which is central.  We claim
`J_x = ker(ev_x) = C_0(X \ {x}, B)`.

- The inclusion `J_x subset ker(ev_x)` is clear.
- Conversely, let `g(x) = 0` and put `h(y) = ||g(y)||`.  Let
  `f_eps = min(1, h/eps)`, which lies in `C_0(X \ {x})`.
- Then `||theta(f_eps) g - g|| = sup_y (1 - f_eps(y)) h(y) <= eps`, so `g in J_x`.

Hence `A_x ≅ B` through `ev_x`.  Theorem 4.1 gives "<=", and (F0) gives "=>", since
`B` is a quotient of `C(X,B)`.  QED.

**Corollary 5.2 (fiberwise exact C(X)-algebras; strengthening Haagerup).**  Let `A`
be unital and `theta: C(X) -> Z(A)` a unital star homomorphism.  If every fiber
`A_x` is exact, then every bounded quasitrace on `A` is a trace.

*Proof.*  Each `A_x` is zero or a unital exact C\*-algebra.  By Haagerup's Theorem
5.11 it has `QL`, and Theorem 4.1 applies.  QED.

*Remark on exactness of `A`.*  The total algebra need not be exact.  A standard
sketch follows; it is not load-bearing, and its references were not re-read (§9).

- Choose `k_n -> infinity` and pairs of unitaries in `M_(k_n)` that converge
  strongly to a free Haar pair.
- Let `E` be the C\*-subalgebra of `prod_n M_(k_n)` generated by
  `direct_sum_n M_(k_n)` and the two sequences.
- Strong convergence gives `E / direct_sum M_(k_n) ≅ C*_r(F_2)`.
- `theta(f) = (f(n) 1_(k_n))_n` defines a central `C(N ∪ {infinity})`-structure.
  Its fibers are `M_(k_n)`, and `C*_r(F_2)` at infinity.
- If `E` were exact, the extension would be locally split by Effros--Haagerup.
  Then `C*_r(F_2)` would be quasidiagonal, contradicting Rosenberg's theorem.

This particular `E` is also an extension of an exact algebra by a nuclear ideal.
It is therefore not claimed as a new example.

**Corollary 5.3 (products versus ultraproducts).**  Let `(A_i)_(i in I)` be unital
and `A = prod_i A_i`.  Put `theta: l^infinity(I) = C(beta I) -> Z(A)` with
`theta(f) = (f_i 1_(A_i))_i`.

(a) For `i in I`, the fiber is `A_i`.

(b) For a free ultrafilter `omega in beta I \ I`, we have
`J_omega = N_omega = {a in A : lim_omega ||a_i|| = 0}`.  So the fiber is the norm
ultraproduct `prod_omega A_i`.

(c) Consequently,

```text
QL(prod_i A_i)  <=>  QL(A_i) for all i  and  QL(prod_omega A_i) for all free omega.
```

*Proof.*  The Gelfand transform of `f in l^infinity(I)` at `omega in beta I` is
`lim_omega f_i`.  Hence

```text
C_0(beta I \ {omega}) = c_omega = {f : lim_omega f_i = 0}.
```

(a) For a principal point `i`, `C_0(beta I \ {i}) = {f : f_i = 0}`.  So
`J_i = {a : a_i = 0}`, because such an `a` equals `theta(1 - delta_i) a`.

(b) Two inclusions.

- `J_omega subset N_omega`: for `f in c_omega` and `b in A`,
  `||f_i b_i|| <= |f_i| ||b||` tends to `0` along `omega`.  And `N_omega` is a closed
  ideal.
- `N_omega subset J_omega`: let `a in N_omega`.  Put `f_i = ||a_i||^(1/2)` and
  `b_i = ||a_i||^(-1/2) a_i`, with `b_i = 0` when `a_i = 0`.  Then
  `||b_i|| <= ||a||^(1/2)`, `f in c_omega` and `a = theta(f) b`.

(c) Theorem 4.1.  QED.

*Why this is not a formal consequence of ideal gluing.*

- `stw01-extensions-preserve-qt-linearity` reduces `QL(prod A_i)` to the ideal
  `direct_sum A_i` and the corona.
- `stw01-central-projection-ideal-extensions-preserve-qt-linearity`, applied to
  `J_omega` with its central projections `theta(chi_S)` for `S not in omega`, needs
  `QL` of the corners `prod_(i in S) A_i`.  These are again infinite products.
- The graph's gluing theorems therefore return products over smaller index sets and
  never reach the ultraproducts.

The same computation shows that the sequence algebra
`A_infinity = l^infinity(N, A) / c_0(N, A)` is a `C(beta N \ N)`-algebra with
fibers `A_omega`.  So `QL(A_infinity) <=> QL(A_omega)` for all free `omega`.

**Corollary 5.4 (Glimm form).**  Take `theta` to be the inclusion
`Z(A) = C(X) -> A`, where `X` is the maximal ideal space of `Z(A)`.  Then

```text
QL(A)  <=>  QL(A / closure(mA))    for every maximal ideal m of Z(A).
```

The quotients `A / closure(mA)` may have a larger center.  Iterating is possible,
but it yields nothing beyond the graph's existing reduction to separable simple
algebras (`stw01-separable-simple-reduction`).

**Remark 5.5 (radical-central structures).**  Let `J_b` be the intersection of the
ideals `N_tau` over `tau in QT(A)`, the universal bounded-quasitrace radical.
Suppose `theta: C(X) -> A` is a unital star homomorphism with
`[theta(C(X)), A] subset J_b`, which is weaker than centrality.  Then

```text
QL(A/J_x) for all x   =>   QL(A),
```

where `J_x` is the closed ideal of `A` generated by `theta(C_0(X \ {x}))`.

*Proof.*  By (F4'), every quasitrace on `A` factors through `A~ = A/J_b`, so
`QL(A) <=> QL(A~)`.  The image of `theta` in `A~` is central.  The ideal it
generates at `x` is `(J_x + J_b)/J_b`.  Theorem 4.1 for `A~` reduces the question
to `QL(A/(J_x + J_b))`, which follows from `QL(A/J_x)` by (F0).  QED.

The same radical-centrality idea underlies
`stw01-qt-central-projection-extensions-preserve-linearity`.

## 6. Products of matrix algebras: a direct proof

This section gives the direct route into
`stw01-matrix-product-algebras-have-qt-linearity`.  That claim was committed in
`765e91a7e` without a route file.  Its algorithmic core, the balancing lemma, is
Lemma 5.1 of the 2026-09-11 certificates artifact.

**Lemma 6.1 (balancing).**  Let `c in R^n` with `sum_j c_j = 0` and `|c_j| <= M`.
Then there are a diagonal matrix `w in M_n` with real entries and `||w|| <= M`, and
a permutation unitary `v in M_n`, such that `diag(c) = w - v w v*`.

*Proof.*  First order the entries greedily.

- While the running sum is `<= 0`, take a remaining entry `>= 0`; otherwise take a
  remaining entry `< 0`.
- Since the total is zero, an entry of the required sign remains whenever entries
  remain.
- A partial sum in `[-M, 0]` plus an entry in `[0, M]` lies in `[-M, M]`.  A partial
  sum in `(0, M]` plus an entry in `[-M, 0)` lies in `(-M, M]`.

Let `rho` be the resulting ordering, so the entries are taken in the order
`c_(rho(1)), ..., c_(rho(n))`.  Let `w_j` be the partial sums, with `w_0 = w_n = 0`,
so `|w_j| <= M` and `c_(rho(j)) = w_j - w_(j-1)`.

Now pass to matrices.

- Let `S` be the cyclic shift, `S e_j = e_(j+1)` with indices mod `n`, and put
  `D_w = diag(w_1, ..., w_n)`.
- Then `S D_w S* = diag(w_0, w_1, ..., w_(n-1))`, so
  `D_w - S D_w S* = diag(c_(rho(1)), ..., c_(rho(n)))`.
- Let `P` be the permutation unitary with `P e_j = e_(rho(j))`.  Conjugating by `P`
  gives `diag(c) = w - v w v*` with `w = P D_w P*` and `v = P S P*`.

QED.

**Proposition 6.2.**  Let `W = prod_(i in I) M_(n_i)` for an arbitrary family.
Every function `tau: W -> C` satisfying (i)-(iii) is linear.  In particular every
bounded 2-quasitrace on `W` is a trace, and by (F0) so is every bounded 2-quasitrace
on every quotient `W/J`.

*Proof.*  Let `D = prod_i D_(n_i)` be the diagonal subalgebra, which is an abelian
C\*-subalgebra.  Let `T: W -> Z(W)` be the center-valued trace
`T(x) = (tr_(n_i)(x_i) 1_(n_i))_i`, with `tr` normalized.  `T` is linear and
`T(uxu*) = T(x)` for unitaries `u`.

*Step 1: diagonal elements.*  Let `d in D_sa` and put `c = d - T(d) in D_sa`.
Each block of `c` has trace zero and entries bounded by `2||d||`.

- Apply Lemma 6.1 in each block.  This gives `w = (w_i)` in `D` with
  `||w|| <= 2||d||` and a unitary `v = (v_i)` in `W` with `c = w - v w v*`.
  Both families are bounded, so both elements lie in `W`.
- The elements `d`, `T(d)`, `w` and `v w v*` all lie in `D`.
- Linearity on `D` and (F2) give
  `tau(d) = tau(T(d)) + tau(w) - tau(v w v*) = tau(T(d))`.

*Step 2: self-adjoint elements.*  Let `x in W_sa`.

- Diagonalize blockwise: `x = u d u*` with `u in W` unitary and `d in D_sa`, where
  `||d_i|| = ||x_i||`.
- By (F2) and Step 1, `tau(x) = tau(d) = tau(T(d)) = tau(T(x))`.

*Step 3: linearity.*  Let `x, y in W_sa` and `t in R`.

- `T(x)` and `T(y)` lie in the abelian algebra `Z(W)`, so
  `tau(x + y) = tau(T(x) + T(y)) = tau(T(x)) + tau(T(y)) = tau(x) + tau(y)`.
- Real homogeneity holds on `C*(x)`.
- For `lambda = lambda_1 + i lambda_2` and `a, b in W_sa`,

  ```text
  tau(lambda (a + ib)) = tau(lambda_1 a - lambda_2 b) + i tau(lambda_2 a + lambda_1 b)
                       = lambda (tau(a) + i tau(b))
  ```

  by (ii) and real-linearity on `W_sa`.

So `tau` is complex-linear.  Axiom (iv) was not used.  QED.

**Corollary 6.3.**  Let `X` be compact Hausdorff and `J` a closed ideal of
`W = prod_i M_(n_i)`.  Then `C(X, W/J)` has `QL`.  More generally, a unital
C(X)-algebra has `QL` whenever each of its central fibers is exact or a quotient of
a product of matrix algebras.

*Proof.*  Combine Theorem 4.1 with Corollary 5.1, Haagerup's Theorem 5.11 and
Proposition 6.2.  QED.

*Examples.*  `C([0,1], prod_n M_n)` and `C([0,1], prod_omega M_n)` have `QL`.  They
contain the nonexact algebras `prod_n M_n` and `prod_omega M_n` as constants.  The
graph claim states that `prod_omega M_(n_k)` is not exact when `n_k -> infinity`;
this was not re-verified here.  Hence these examples are not exact.

## 8. Limits and dead ends

1. **Non-central `theta`.**  For a non-central commutative subalgebra, `tau o theta`
   need not be a character, even for extreme `tau`.  Example: `A = M_2`, `theta` the
   diagonal copy of `C^2`, `tau = tr`; then `tau o theta = (ev_1 + ev_2)/2`.  The cut
   `x -> zx` then fails axiom (i).  Only the radical-central weakening of Remark 5.5
   survives.
2. **No reduction beyond simple algebras.**  For `Z(A) = C` the principle is empty.
   Since the graph already reduces Problem I to separable simple algebras, Theorem
   4.1 is a permanence theorem that produces positive classes.  It does not narrow
   the minimal counterexample class.
3. **Subalgebras.**  `QL` does not pass to subalgebras by any known argument.
   Consider `prod_i C(X_i, M_(n_i))` with `dim X_i` unbounded.
   - Its center is `C(beta(disjoint union X_i))`.
   - A fiber at a remainder point `p` is the quotient by the ideal where
     `lim_(t -> p) ||x(t)|| = 0`.
   - Choosing an ultrafilter on the underlying set of `disjoint union X_i` that
     converges to `p`, the fiber embeds isometrically into an ultraproduct of matrix
     algebras.
   - It is not a quotient of one.

   So Proposition 6.2 does not transfer, and this attempt dies at the wrong direction
   of the arrow.
4. **Direct balancing over `C(X)`.**  The balancing proof of Proposition 6.2 needs a
   diagonalization of each block, which does not exist continuously in `t in X`.
   Theorem 4.1 replaces the missing continuity by fiberwise evaluation, but only
   after passing to extreme points.  There is no pointwise identity
   `tau = tau o T` on `C(X, W)` from this argument.
5. **Explicit examples outside all known classes.**  The nonexact fiberwise exact
   example of §5 is also an extension of an exact algebra by a nuclear ideal.  An
   example needs a C(X)-algebra over connected `X` with exact (or matrix-product
   quotient) fibers such that no nonzero ideal `C_0(U)A` is exact.  None was
   constructed or certified.
6. **Non-unital algebras.**  Not treated.  The proof uses `tau(1)`, compactness of
   `QT(A)` and Krein--Milman.
7. **Ideas considered earlier in this session and rejected.**
   - Tensor products and finite crossed products: these lack the needed permanence
     lemma.
   - Real rank zero, one-relator groups and full group algebras: no mechanism beyond
     exactness was found.
   - A `sqrt 2` additivity constant and Dixmier averaging: one-sided inequalities are
     restatements (certificates artifact §4).
   - Directed unions of exact groups: already exact.
   - A trivial-center reduction: already in the graph.
