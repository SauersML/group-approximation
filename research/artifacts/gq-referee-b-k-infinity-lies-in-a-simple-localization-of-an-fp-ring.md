## Mathematics (checked)

- **`V` and `P`.** `V = Z[s^(±1), (1+s)^(-1)]` is free abelian with basis `s^k` (`k ∈ Z`) and `(1+s)^(-n)` (`n ≥ 1`), by
  partial fractions over `Z`, using `1 = (1+s) − s`. So `P = Sym(V)` is a polynomial ring in countably many
  variables, `t = t_1` is one of them, and `Frac P ≅ K_∞`.
- **Item 1, the presented ring `T'`.**
  - The unit group `⟨x, y⟩` is `Z^2`.
  - `L` is a quotient of `Z[x^(±1), y^(±1)]/(y−1−x) ≅ V` and retracts onto `V`, so `L ≅ V`.
  - *The commutator induction.* Conjugating `[t, t_1] = 0` by `y^k` gives
    `Σ C(k,i)C(k,j)[t_i, t_(j+1)] = 0`. The only gap of `k+1` is at `(i,j) = (0,k)`.
  - *Negative powers.* These are cleared by conjugating with `x^M y^N`, which is an automorphism.
  - *Conclusion.* `P' ≅ P` through the universal property of `Sym`, and `T' = ⊕ P' g` maps injectively to `T`.
- **Item 2.** The action is faithful because `s^a(1+s)^b = 1` only for `a = b = 0`. The minimal-support argument is
  correct.
- **Item 3.**
  - Coefficients of products lie in the ring `L_0`, generated by translates. `L_0` sits inside a localization of the
    UFD `P` at the primes `h(e_j)`, whose units are scalars times products of those primes.
  - Degree preservation then bounds the degrees of inverted irreducibles.
  - `t^d + 2` is irreducible in `P`, by Eisenstein in `Q[t]`; a factorization in more variables would have factors in
    `Q[t]`. So it gives a contradiction.

## (b) Priority

I know of no source for this ring presentation of `Sym(V) ⋊ Z^2`, and I did not search the literature exhaustively.
There is an evident precursor that the node should record.

- **The integral group ring `Z[Γ]` of Baumslag's group.** It is a finitely presented ring, because `Γ` is a finitely
  presented group.
- **Its structure.** `Z[Γ] = Z[V] ⋊ Z^2`, where `Z[V]` is the Laurent polynomial ring in countably many variables
  `u_v^(±1)`, with `u_(v+w) = u_v u_w`.
- **Its localization.** Localizing at `Z[V] \ 0` gives `Q(u_v) ⋊ Z^2 ≅ K_∞ ⋊ Z^2`, which is simple by the node's
  item 2. The action is faithful for the same reason.
- **What this means for the node.**
  - The conclusion "`K_∞` lies in a simple localization of a finitely presented ring" already follows from Baumslag
    1972 in one line.
  - The node's `T` is the additive, symmetric-algebra analogue, `t_(v+w) = t_v + t_w`.
  - "No priority is claimed" is right. Add the group-ring remark, so that item 1 is read as a variant of that
    precursor, not as its first instance.

## (d) The simplicity criterion, pinned

- **Source.** J. Öinert, *Simple group graded rings and maximal commutativity*, arXiv:0904.4661v1 (29 Apr 2009). I read
  pp. 1–4.
  - The abstract, verbatim: "A skew group ring R_e ⋊_σ G, where R_e is commutative, is shown to be a simple ring if
    and only if R_e is G-simple and maximal commutative in R_e ⋊_σ G."
  - The introduction, p. 3, numbers it Theorem 6.13. It also states Proposition 1.6: "if `R_e` is maximal
    commutative in `R` and `R_e` is a simple ring, then `R` is simple".
  - I did not read the text of Theorem 6.13 itself.
- **Applied to a field.** For a field `K` with faithful `G`, `K` is `G`-simple. It is also maximal commutative:
  if `Σ f_g g` commutes with every `λ`, then `(λ − g(λ)) f_g = 0` for all `λ`, so `f_g = 0` for `g ≠ 1`.
- **Recommendation.** Cite Öinert Theorem 6.13, or Proposition 1.6. The inline proof is also correct and can stay.
- **Not read at source.** McConnell–Robson and Passman were not reachable.
