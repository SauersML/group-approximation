relative Lemma B (`bk-relative-lemma-b`).

## 0. What Schafhauser's tool does and does not give

Moutzouris Theorem 4.1 is Schafhauser Corollary 5.4.  Schafhauser's realisation
(Theorem D) takes: source `A` separable, unital, exact, UCT, with a **faithful**
amenable trace; target `B` a **simple, unital AF algebra with a unique trace**
and divisible `K_0`.  It realises a positive unital `K_0`-map preserving the
(unique) trace by a faithful embedding.  The paper states (p. 4) that the
non-simple / general-codomain refinement is deferred to forthcoming work with
Carrión, Gabe, Tikuisis and White.  So the realisation needed for a genuine
ideal-relative Lemma B -- a faithful map into a **non-simple** AF target with a
distinguished ideal carrying the trace -- is not in Schafhauser, and Moutzouris'
own frontier remark (p. 22) records that his class `G` (every `K_0`-state
induced by a **faithful** trace) has no known interesting escapees.

## 1. Descent lemma (new; a corollary of Theorem 7)

**Proposition 1.**  Let `I ⊴ A` be any ideal and `π : A → A/I` the quotient.
Then `(A/I, π_*(G))` again has the *surviving property*: **no nonzero densely
finite lower semicontinuous trace on `A/I` annihilates `π_*(G)`.**

*Proof.*  Let `σ` be a densely finite l.s.c. trace on `A/I` with
`σ̂(π_*(G)) = 0`.  Then `τ_0 := σ ∘ π ∘ q_J` is an l.s.c. trace on `R`.  It is
densely finite: quotient maps carry `Ped(R)` onto `Ped(A/I)`
(Pedersen ideal is functorial and surjective under surjections), and for
`x ∈ Ped(R)_+`, `τ_0(x) = σ((π∘q_J)(x))` with `(π∘q_J)(x) ∈ Ped(A/I)_+`, finite.
Its pairing with `H` is `τ_0^ = σ̂ ∘ π_* ∘ (q_J)_* ` on `H`, i.e.
`σ̂(π_*(G)) = 0`.  By `bk-radical-traces-vanishing-on-boundary-are-zero`
(Theorem 7), a densely finite l.s.c. trace on `R` annihilating `H` is zero (as
`R ≠ 0`).  So `τ_0 = 0`, and since `π ∘ q_J` is surjective, `σ = 0`.  QED

Consequence: the surviving obstruction is *hereditary down every quotient*.  In
particular one cannot escape it by passing to a quotient where some finite trace
reappears -- every such trace already sees the boundary.

## 2. The finiteness ideal, and the dichotomy

The relative Lemma B hypothesis gives one l.s.c. trace `τ` on `A`, faithful and
densely finite on a proper ideal `I ⊊ A`, `+∞` on `A ∖ I`, with `τ̂(G_I)=0` for
`G_I = G ∩ im(K_0(I) → K_0(A))`, and (up to scale) the only annihilating trace.
`I` is the *finiteness ideal* of `τ` (the ideal generated by `{a ≥ 0 : τ(a)<∞}`).

Consider the six-term exactness `K_0(I) →^{ι} K_0(A) →^{π_*} K_0(A/I)`, so
`ker π_* = im ι`.  Exactly one of the following holds.

**(a) Ideal-captured: `π_*(G) = 0`.**  Then `G ⊆ im ι`.  Lift each generator to
`K_0(I)` (well defined modulo `im(∂ : K_1(A/I) → K_0(I))`), obtaining
`G̃ ⊆ K_0(I)` with `ι(G̃) = G` and `τ|_I^(G̃) = τ̂(G) = 0`.  Now `τ|_I` is a
**faithful densely finite** trace on `I` annihilating the singular `G̃`.  So the
entire killing problem is thrown onto the ideal `I`, where a genuine faithful
trace is available.  This is the good case: relative Lemma B reduces to Lemma B
(the faithful-trace killing of parts 1--3) applied to `I`, plus the single
Brown--Dadarlat gluing step `0 → I → A → A/I → 0` (Theorem 3.4), whose only
hypothesis beyond `A/I` nuclear QD UCT is that the boundary index into `K_0(I)`
is killed by the embedding.  When `I` has an approximate unit of projections and
`K_0(A)` has cancellation, `ι` is injective (Brown--Dadarlat Lemma 4.5), and
the lift is unique.

**(b) Escaping: `π_*(G) ≠ 0`.**  Then a nonzero part of the boundary lives in
`K_0(A/I)`.  By Proposition 1, `(A/I, π_*(G))` is again surviving: no densely
finite trace on `A/I` annihilates `π_*(G)`.  Two sub-cases:

- **(b1) `π_*(G)` singular in `K_0(A/I)`.**  The obstruction genuinely descends
  to the strictly smaller `A/I`, and one repeats the analysis there.  The
  finiteness ideal of the (new) annihilating trace on `A/I` gives the next layer
  of a filtration `I = I_1 ⊊ I_2 ⊊ … ` of `A`.  Termination is a transfinite
  induction on the (separable, hence countable-length) primitive-ideal filtration.
- **(b2) `π_*(G) ∩ K_0^+(A/I) ≠ {0}`.**  The descended boundary is detected
  **positively** by the quotient.  Let `K ⊆ R` be the ideal of `E` with
  `K/J = I`, so `A/I = R/K`; the boundary image is `π_*(G) = (q_K)_*(H)`.  By
  Spielberg (Brown--Dadarlat Prop 4.1), `π_*(G) ∩ K_0^+(R/K) ≠ {0}` forces `E/K`
  **not stably finite** -- exactly Theorem 9
  (`bk-radical-projection-quotients-are-toeplitz-infinite`).  Consequences,
  stated carefully:
  - By the Brown--Dadarlat remark after Def 4.4, `A/I` (being non-singular for
    `π_*(G)`) has **no** faithful embedding into a stably finite QD algebra
    killing `π_*(G)`.  So the *peeling / gluing route*, which factors the killing
    of `G` through such an embedding of the quotient `A/I`, is **blocked** here.
  - This is *not* an absolute obstruction to killing `G` in `A`: a general
    faithful embedding of `A` need not respect the ideal `I`, so positivity in
    `K_0^+(A/I)` does not by itself put `G` into `K_0^+(A)`.  The absolute
    obstruction (§2, item 2) is only `G ∩ K_0^+(A) ≠ {0}`; for the radical
    (`J = 0`) `H` is singular in `K_0(R)`, so none is present at the top.
  - What (b2) shows is that the boundary cannot be peeled off *through the
    quotient side*: an ideal-equivariant embedding that does not factor through
    the infinite quotients `E/K` is the only route left.

## 3. The obstruction is forced, so relative Lemma B is conditional

Case (b2) is not hypothetical.  Corollary 10 of
`bk-radical-projection-quotients-are-toeplitz-infinite` proves that in a **type I**
counterexample with UCT quotients, some `h ∈ H` has strictly positive rank
`r_{P_i}(h) > 0` at every elementary closed point `P_i` of `Prim(R)`, i.e.
`(q_{P_i})_*(H) ∩ K_0^+(R/P_i) ≠ {0}`.  So the escaping-with-positive-detection
case (b2) occurs at the elementary points of any type I counterexample.

**Conclusion.**  The *filtration / peeling route* to relative Lemma B -- kill `H`
by descending through quotients -- is blocked wherever a quotient detects the
boundary positively, which Cor 10 / Thm 9 force at the elementary points of a
type I counterexample.  The route works only in the **ideal-captured** case (a):
when the boundary is carried by the finiteness ideal `I` (`π_*(G)=0`), a faithful
densely finite trace is available on `I` and Lemma B applies there, leaving only
the one Brown--Dadarlat gluing step.  This is not an absolute impossibility of
killing `H` (which would need `H ∩ K_0^+(R) ≠ {0}`, and `H` is singular): it says
the boundary cannot be removed through the infinite quotients, so a single
ideal-equivariant embedding (CGSTW-type, not factoring through those quotients)
is the only remaining route.  The residual open content is therefore sharpened to
two independent questions:

1. **(gluing)** In case (a), does the single index map
   `∂ : K_1(A/I) → K_0(I)` die under the Lemma-B embedding of `I`?  (This is one
   Brown--Dadarlat step, not a transfinite tower.)
2. **(the shape to avoid)** Can a glued UCT `E` be **stably finite** while some
   quotient `R/K` detects the singular boundary positively -- a class singular in
   `K_0(R)` yet positive-with-a-projection in `K_0(R/K)`, `E/K` forced infinite by
   Theorem 9?  Nothing here excludes it: the descent (Prop 1) leaves no trace
   obstruction, and `E` stably finite does not force its quotients `E/K` stably
   finite.  Deciding this is Problem VII for the glued UCT radical itself, now
   reduced to the single ideal-equivariant realisation question of §0 -- realise
   a killing embedding of `R` that does not factor through the infinite quotients.

## 4. Model tests

- **Direct-sum germ dissolves (consistency of case (a)).**  Moutzouris
  Example 4.6, `A = C(S²) ⊕ C`, `x = (0,1,-1)`, `I = C(S²)`:
  `π : A → A/I = C`, and `π_*(x) = -[1_C] ≠ 0` lies in `-K_0^+(C)`, so
  `x` is *not* ideal-captured for this `I`.  But swapping the ideal to `I' = C`
  gives `π'_*(x) = (0,1) ∈ K_0(C(S²))`, the infinitesimal Bott class, which is
  singular, so `x` is captured after one descent into `C(S²)` where the Lebesgue
  trace is faithful.  Consistent with Prop 5.6: `C(S²)⊕C ∈ O` is killed, because
  the split lets one choose the ideal that captures `x`.  A non-split extension
  removes that freedom -- one is stuck with the finiteness ideal `I`.
- **Toeplitz, failing the surviving hypothesis.**  `E = T`, `R = K`, `H = Z[e]`.
  Here `Tr` is densely finite on `R` and `Tr^(H) = Z ≠ 0`, so `R` is not
  surviving and `T` is not stably finite.  Prop 1 is vacuous.  Consistent.
- **Real object satisfying case (a).**  A stably finite extension of `C(T)` by
  `K ⊗ (C ⊕ C)` with index `(1,-1)`: `I = K⊗(C⊕C)` carries `Tr⊕Tr` faithful
  densely finite annihilating `(1,-1)`, `π_*(H)=0`, and `E` is QD (`R_qd=0`),
  matching case (a).
- **Where (b2) would bite.**  A hypothetical `E` with `R` having an ideal `I`
  (say `I = C(S²)⊗K`, Bott class infinitesimal, Lebesgue trace faithful densely
  finite) glued non-split to a quotient `R/K` in which the lifted boundary is a
  genuine positive projection class: this is exactly the type I Toeplitz shape
  of Cor 10.  No trivial model realises it while stably finite -- that is the
  open counterexample question (§3.2).
