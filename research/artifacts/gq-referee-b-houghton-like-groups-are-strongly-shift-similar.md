## 2. The route: Zaremsky's Proposition 5.3 applied to `H_n(G)` (the lane's question)

**The two definitions differ.**
- Z Definition 5.2 (`def:shift_similar`, l.474–480) calls `G` strongly shift-similar if "each `ψ_j` restricted to `G`
  is surjective". It uses the same `ψ_j` as MZ l.437: `ψ_j(g) = s_(g(j))^(−1) ∘ g|_(N∖{j}) ∘ s_j`.
- MZ Definition 3.17 asks only that `ψ_∞` be surjective on germs, for infinite `G`.

**They are equivalent for infinite `G`.**
- MZ Lemma 3.19 gives MZ-strong ⇒ Z-strong.
- Conversely, `ψ_∞(g Symfin) = ψ_j(g) Symfin` (MZ, lemma before Definition 3.17). So `ψ_j` onto implies `ψ_∞` onto.

**The route's step 4.**
- MZ Proposition 5.5 gives an MZ-strong representation of `H_n(G)`. That representation is infinite, since it
  contains `Symfin(N)`. So it is Z-strong.
- Z Proposition 5.3 (`prop:shift_similar`, l.484), read at source: "Every finitely presented, strongly
  shift-similar group admits an action of type (A)". Its proof uses [mallery, Theorem 3.12] (containing `Symfin`) and
  [mallery, Lemma 3.19] (the point-stabilizer isomorphism). Both apply to the representation of `H_n(G)`.
- So step 4 is correct once `H_n(E_ν(P))` is finitely presented, which is the OPEN step 2 of the route, as the route
  says.
- Item 4 of the claim records exactly this implication. ✓
- **A minor observation (no warning).** Z's proof of Proposition 5.3 invokes MZ Lemma 3.19, which is stated for
  MZ-strong groups, while its own hypothesis is Z-strong. The equivalence above closes that gap in either direction.
