# Separativity of real rank zero algebras reduces to prime algebras under Zhang's conjecture

Artifact for route `rr0-separativity-from-prime-case-and-k1-bijectivity`
(swarm session on `stw99-problem-lx-rr0-weak-cancellation`, 2026-09-16).

**Theorem.**  Assume

* (K) every unital C\*-algebra of real rank zero is K1-bijective
  (`rr0-algebras-are-k1-bijective`), and
* (Pr) every unital prime C\*-algebra of real rank zero is separative
  (`prime-rr0-algebras-are-separative`).

Then every C\*-algebra of real rank zero is separative
(`rr0-c-star-algebras-are-separative`).

More precisely, (K) alone shows the following.  If some real rank zero algebra
is not separative, then there is a unital prime real rank zero algebra `B'`
with orthogonal projections `P, Q`, `P + Q = 1`, such that

```text
P (+) P ~ Q (+) P ~ Q (+) Q  in M_2(B'),   P not ~ Q,   P ~ Q modulo every nonzero closed ideal.
```

Throughout, ideals are closed and two-sided, `~` is Murray--von Neumann
equivalence, and `(+)` is the block diagonal sum.

## 0. Standard facts

**(F1) Permanence of real rank zero.**  Real rank zero passes to quotients,
to matrix algebras and to corners `pAp` (Brown--Pedersen, *C\*-algebras of real
rank zero*, J. Funct. Anal. 99 (1991); theorem numbers not re-verified here).
The quotient case is elementary: self-adjoint elements of `A/J` lift to
self-adjoint elements of `A`, and a finite-spectrum approximant maps to a
finite-spectrum approximant.  The matrix and corner cases are used as known.

**(F2) Equivalence descends to corners.**  If `e, f <= p` are projections in a
C\*-algebra `N` and `w` in `N` satisfies `w*w = e`, `ww* = f`, then
`w = f w e` lies in `pNp`.  In particular equivalence of projections of
`M_n(N)` inside some `M_m(N)`, `m >= n`, is equivalence in `M_n(N)`.

**(F3) Closed ideal arithmetic.**  If `K_1, K_2` are ideals of a C\*-algebra,
then `K_1 + K_2` is closed and `K_1 ∩ K_2` is the closure of `K_1 K_2`.  So an
algebra is prime (nonzero ideals intersect nontrivially) if and only if the
product of two nonzero ideals is nonzero.

**K1 conventions.**  For a unital C\*-algebra `N`, `K_1(N)` is the group of
homotopy classes of unitaries in the matrix algebras `M_k(N)`, with
`[u (+) 1] = [u]`, `[u][v] = [uv] = [u (+) v]`, abelian, and invariant under
conjugation by unitaries: `[R y R*] = [R][y][R]^(-1) = [y]`.
The natural map `U(N)/U_0(N) -> K_1(N)` sends `u` to `[u]`.  A unital
\*-homomorphism `rho` induces `rho_*[u] = [rho(u)]`.

**(F4) Lifting the identity component.**  Let `rho: E -> F` be a surjective
unital \*-homomorphism of unital C\*-algebras.  Then `rho(U_0(E)) = U_0(F)`.

*Proof.*  If `u` is in `U(F)` and `||u - 1|| < 2`, then `-1` is not in the
spectrum of `u`, and `u = exp(i h)` with `h = -i log(u)` self-adjoint (principal
branch).  So the subgroup `G` of `U(F)` generated by `exp(i F_sa)` contains a
neighbourhood of `1`, hence is open, hence also closed (its complement is a
union of open cosets).  Each `exp(i t h)`, `t` in `[0,1]`, is a path to `1`,
so `G` lies in `U_0(F)`, and connectedness of `U_0(F)` gives `G = U_0(F)`.  Each
self-adjoint `h` in `F` lifts to a self-adjoint `k` in `E` (lift, then take the
real part), and `rho(exp(i k)) = exp(i h)`.  So every element of `U_0(F)` is
`rho` of a finite product of exponentials, which lies in `U_0(E)`.  The
inclusion `rho(U_0(E))` in `U_0(F)` is clear.

**(F5) Rotation lemma.**  Let `N` be unital and let `V` in `N` be a partial
isometry with `V*V = p`, `VV* = q`.  For every unitary `u` of the corner `pNp`
(unit `p`),

```text
[ V u V* + (1 - q) ] = [ u + (1 - p) ]   in K_1(N).
```

*Proof.*  Put `R = [[V, 1-q], [1-p, V*]]` in `M_2(N)`.  Using `V(1-p) = 0`,
`(1-q)V = 0`, `V*(1-q) = 0` and `(1-p)V* = 0`, one checks `RR* = R*R = 1`.
With `x = u + 1 - p`, and `(1-p)u = u(1-p) = 0`,

```text
R (x (+) 1) R* = [[V u V* + (1-q), 0], [0, (1-p) + V*V]] = (V u V* + 1 - q) (+) 1 .
```

Conjugation invariance and `[y (+) 1] = [y]` give the claim.  Applied to
`M_k(N)`, the same holds for partial isometries and unitaries in matrix
algebras.

**(F6) Full corners.**  Let `N` be unital and `e` a projection in `N` whose
ideal is all of `N`.  Then

```text
kappa_e : K_1(eNe) -> K_1(N),   [u] |-> [u + (1 - e)]      (u unitary in M_k(eNe), 1 - e read as 1_k (x) (1 - e))
```

is a group isomorphism.  For a unital \*-homomorphism `rho: N -> N'`,
`kappa_(rho(e)) o (rho|eNe)_* = rho_* o kappa_e`.

*Proof.*  The map `u |-> u + (1-e)` is a continuous group homomorphism
`U(M_k(eNe)) -> U(M_k(N))` compatible with `(+) 1`, so `kappa_e` is a
well-defined homomorphism, and naturality is immediate.  It suffices to treat
unitaries of `eNe` and `N` themselves; the matrix levels follow by applying the
argument to `M_k(N)` and the full projection `1_k (x) e`.

(a) *`1 <~ n e`.*  The algebraic ideal `span(N e N)` is dense in `N`, so it
contains some `c` with `||1 - c|| < 1`, which is invertible, and
`1 = c^(-1) c = sum_(j=1)^n a_j e b_j`.  Let `X` be the column with entries
`e b_j` and `Y` the row with entries `a_j e`.  Then `Y X = 1`, so in a faithful
representation `||xi|| <= ||Y|| ||X xi||`, i.e. `T = X*X >= ||Y||^(-2) 1` is
invertible.  `S = X T^(-1/2)` is a column over `N` with `S*S = 1` and entries in
`eN`.  So `f = S S*` is a projection in `M_n(eNe)` with `f <= 1_n (x) e`.

(b) *An inverse.*  Define `psi : K_1(N) -> K_1(eNe)` by
`psi[w] = [S w S* + (1_n (x) e - f)]`.  Here `S w S*` has entries in `eNe`, and
`S w S*` is a unitary of `f M_n(eNe) f`, so the argument is a unitary of
`M_n(eNe)`; `psi` is a well-defined homomorphism for the same reason as
`kappa_e`.

(c) *`kappa_e o psi = id`.*  `kappa_e psi [w] = [S w S* + 1_n - f]`.  Let `V`
in `M_n(N)` be the matrix whose first column is `S` and whose other columns are
zero.  Then `V*V = 1 (+) 0_(n-1)`, `VV* = f`, and `V (w (+) 0) V* = S w S*`.  By
(F5), `[S w S* + 1_n - f] = [w (+) 1_(n-1)] = [w]`.

(d) *`psi o kappa_e = id`.*  For `u` unitary in `eNe`, write `u + 1 - e` and
split `S (u + 1 - e) S* = S e u e S* + (S S* - S e S*)`.  Let `S' = S e`, a
column with entries in `eNe`, with `S'* S' = e` and `f' = S' S'* <= f`.  Then

```text
psi kappa_e [u] = [ S' u S'* + (f - f') + (1_n (x) e) - f ] = [ S' u S'* + (1_n (x) e) - f' ] .
```

Let `V'` in `M_n(eNe)` have first column `S'` and zero elsewhere, so
`V'*V' = e (+) 0_(n-1)` and `V'V'* = f'`.  Apply (F5) in the unital algebra
`M_n(eNe)` (unit `1_n (x) e`): the class equals `[u (+) (1_(n-1) (x) e)] = [u]`
in `K_1(eNe)`.

So `kappa_e` is bijective.

## 1. Normal form of a separativity failure

Let `A` have real rank zero and fail separativity: there are projections `p, q`
in `M_n(A)` with `p (+) p ~ p (+) q ~ q (+) q` in `M_inf(A)` and `p` not
equivalent to `q`.  In `M_(2n)(A)` put `P = p (+) 0`, `Q = 0 (+) q` and

```text
B = (P + Q) M_(2n)(A) (P + Q),   1_B = P + Q .
```

`B` is a unital real rank zero algebra by (F1).  `P (+) P` corresponds to
`p (+) p`, `Q (+) Q` to `q (+) q`, and `Q (+) P` to `q (+) p ~ p (+) q`, all as
projections of `M_2(B)`, which is a corner of `M_(4n)(A)`.  By (F2) the
equivalences hold inside `M_2(B)`, and `P` is not equivalent to `Q` in `B`
(an equivalence in `B` is one in `M_(2n)(A)`, and `P ~ p`, `Q ~ q`).  Fix

```text
W in M_2(B):  W*W = P (+) P,  WW* = Q (+) P;      X in M_2(B):  X*X = P (+) P,  XX* = Q (+) Q .
```

**Fullness.**  `X = (Q (+) Q) X`, so the `(1,1)` entry of `X*X` is
`P = x_11* Q x_11 + x_21* Q x_21`, and `P` lies in the ideal generated by `Q`.
Hence `Q` is full in `B` (its ideal contains `P + Q = 1`).  All of this passes
to every quotient `B/J`, by applying the quotient map to `W` and `X`.

## 2. A critical quotient

Let `S` be the set of ideals `J` of `B` such that the images of `P` and `Q` in
`B/J` are not equivalent.  `0` is in `S`, and `B` is not (in the zero algebra
`0 ~ 0`).

**Chains.**  Let `(J_a)` be a chain in `S` and `J` the closure of its union.
Suppose the images of `P, Q` in `B/J` are equivalent, by some `pi_J(x)`; we may
take `x = Q x P`.  For every `b` in `B`, `||pi_J(b)|| = dist(b, J)`, and since
the union of the `J_a` is dense in `J` and increasing,
`dist(b, J) = inf_a dist(b, J_a) = lim_a ||pi_(J_a)(b)||`.  Choose `a` with

```text
|| y*y - P' || < 1/4   and   || y y* - Q' || < 1/4   in B/J_a,
```

where `y`, `P'`, `Q'` are the images of `x`, `P`, `Q`.  Then `y = Q' y P'`,
`y*y` is invertible in the corner `P' (B/J_a) P'` with
`|| (y*y)^(-1) - P' || <= (1/4)/(3/4) = 1/3`, and `v = y (y*y)^(-1/2)` satisfies
`v*v = P'` and `v v* <= Q'`.  Moreover

```text
|| v v* - Q' || <= || y [(y*y)^(-1) - P'] y* || + || y y* - Q' || < (5/4)(1/3) + 1/4 < 1,
```

using `v v* = y (y*y)^(-1) y*` and `||y||^2 < 5/4`.  So `Q' - v v*` is a
projection of norm less than `1`, hence zero, and `P' ~ Q'` in `B/J_a`,
contradicting `J_a` in `S`.  So `J` is in `S`.

By Zorn's lemma `S` has a maximal element `J_0`.  Put `B' = B/J_0` and keep the
names `P, Q, W, X` for the images.  Then `B'` is unital (it is nonzero), of real
rank zero (F1), `Q` is full in `B'`,

```text
W*W = P (+) P,  WW* = Q (+) P,   X*X = P (+) P,  XX* = Q (+) Q   in M_2(B'),   P not ~ Q in B',
```

and for every nonzero ideal `K` of `B'`, `P ~ Q` in `B'/K`: the preimage of `K`
in `B` strictly contains `J_0`, so it is not in `S`, and `B'/K` is the
quotient of `B` by that preimage.  If `B` is separable, so is `B'`.

## 3. Under (K), the critical quotient is prime

Suppose `K_1, K_2` are nonzero ideals of `B'` with `K_1 ∩ K_2 = 0`.  We derive
`P ~ Q` in `B'`, a contradiction.  By (F3), `K_1 + K_2` is an ideal.  Put

```text
B_i = B'/K_i,   pi_i : B' -> B_i,   D = B'/(K_1 + K_2),   rho_i : B_i -> D,   rho_1 pi_1 = rho_2 pi_2 .
```

**The pullback.**  `theta(b) = (pi_1(b), pi_2(b))` is an isomorphism of `B'`
onto `{(b_1, b_2) : rho_1(b_1) = rho_2(b_2)}`.  It is injective because
`ker theta = K_1 ∩ K_2 = 0`.  For surjectivity, lift `b_i = pi_i(c_i)`.  Then
`c_1 - c_2` lies in `K_1 + K_2`, say `c_1 - c_2 = k_1 + k_2`, and
`c = c_1 - k_1 = c_2 + k_2` satisfies `theta(c) = (b_1, b_2)`.

Write `P_i, Q_i, W_i` for the images in `B_i` (and `M_2(B_i)`), and
`P~, Q~, W~` for the images in `D`.

**Case `D = 0`.**  Then `theta` is an isomorphism onto `B_1 (+) B_2`.  By
criticality there are `v_i` in `B_i` with `v_i* v_i = P_i`, `v_i v_i* = Q_i`, and
`V = theta^(-1)(v_1, v_2)` gives `P ~ Q` in `B'`.

**Case `D != 0`.**  By criticality pick `v_i` in `B_i` with
`v_i* v_i = P_i` and `v_i v_i* = Q_i`.  Put `v~_i = rho_i(v_i)` and

```text
w~ = v~_2 v~_1*,   a unitary of the corner F = Q~ D Q~,   with w~ v~_1 = v~_2 .
```

(Indeed `w~* w~ = v~_1 P~ v~_1* = Q~ = w~ w~*`, and `w~ v~_1 = v~_2 P~ = v~_2`.)
Let `E_i = Q_i B_i Q_i`.  The restrictions `rho_i : E_i -> F` are surjective
unital \*-homomorphisms.  `E_1, E_2, F` are unital of real rank zero by (F1).

*Goal.*  Find unitaries `u_1` in `E_1` and `u_2` in `E_2` with
`rho_2(u_2)* rho_1(u_1) = w~`.  Then `V_i = u_i v_i` satisfy
`V_i* V_i = P_i`, `V_i V_i* = Q_i`, and

```text
rho_2(V_2) = rho_2(u_2) v~_2 = rho_2(u_2) w~ v~_1 = rho_1(u_1) v~_1 = rho_1(V_1),
```

so `V = theta^(-1)(V_1, V_2)` exists.  Since `theta` is injective,
`theta(V*V) = (P_1, P_2) = theta(P)` gives `V*V = P`, and likewise `VV* = Q`.
So `P ~ Q` in `B'`, the desired contradiction.

*Step 1: a K1 identity.*  Put

```text
G_i = (Q_i (+) P_i) M_2(B_i) (Q_i (+) P_i),     H = (Q~ (+) P~) M_2(D) (Q~ (+) P~),
Z_i = W_i (v_i* (+) P_i)   in M_2(B_i).
```

From `W_i* W_i = P_i (+) P_i` and `W_i W_i* = Q_i (+) P_i`:

```text
Z_i* Z_i = (v_i (+) P_i)(P_i (+) P_i)(v_i* (+) P_i) = Q_i (+) P_i,
Z_i Z_i* = W_i (v_i* v_i (+) P_i) W_i* = W_i (P_i (+) P_i) W_i* = Q_i (+) P_i,
```

so `Z_i` is a unitary of `G_i`.  Both `rho_i(W_i)` equal `W~`, the image of `W`
from `B'`.  Hence

```text
rho_2(Z_2)* rho_1(Z_1) = (v~_2 (+) P~) W~* W~ (v~_1* (+) P~) = v~_2 v~_1* (+) P~ = w~ (+) P~ .
```

The projection `Q_i (+) 0` is full in `G_i`.  Indeed, its ideal in `M_2(B_i)`
contains `Q_i (+) 0`, `0 (+) Q_i` (conjugate by the flip), hence also
`P_i (+) 0` and `0 (+) P_i` (fullness of `Q_i`, Section 1), hence `1`.  For a
projection `f <= e`, `e m f m' e = (e m e) f (e m' e)`, so the ideal of `eMe`
generated by `f` contains `e (closure of span M f M) e`, which contains `e`.
Similarly `Q~ (+) 0` is full in `H`.  So (F6) gives isomorphisms

```text
kappa_i : K_1(E_i) -> K_1(G_i),  [u] |-> [u (+) P_i],       kappa_F : K_1(F) -> K_1(H),  [u] |-> [u (+) P~],
```

with `kappa_F o (rho_i)_* = (rho_i)_* o kappa_i`.  Put `x_i = kappa_i^(-1)[Z_i]`.
Then

```text
kappa_F( rho_1* x_1 - rho_2* x_2 ) = [rho_1(Z_1)] - [rho_2(Z_2)] = [rho_2(Z_2)* rho_1(Z_1)] = [w~ (+) P~] = kappa_F [w~],
```

so `[w~] = rho_1* x_1 - rho_2* x_2` in `K_1(F)`.

*Step 2: apply (K).*  `E_i` is K1-surjective, so `x_i = [u_i]` for unitaries
`u_i` of `E_i`.  Put `g = rho_2(u_2)* rho_1(u_1) w~*`, a unitary of `F` with
`[g] = -rho_2* x_2 + rho_1* x_1 - [w~] = 0`.  `F` is K1-injective, so `g` is in
`U_0(F)`.  By (F4) there is `g'` in `U_0(E_2)` with `rho_2(g') = g`.  Replace
`u_2` by `u_2 g'`:

```text
rho_2(u_2 g')* rho_1(u_1) = g* rho_2(u_2)* rho_1(u_1) = g* g w~ = w~ .
```

This is the goal, so `P ~ Q` in `B'`, a contradiction.  Hence any two nonzero
ideals of `B'` intersect nontrivially: `B'` is prime.

## 4. Conclusion

`B'` is a unital prime real rank zero algebra.  The classes `x = [P]` and
`y = [Q]` in `V(B')` satisfy `2x = x + y = 2y` (by `W` and `X`), and `x != y`,
since `P ~ Q` in `M_inf(B')` would give `P ~ Q` in `B'` by (F2).  So `B'` is
not separative, contradicting (Pr).  This proves the Theorem.

**Where the hypotheses enter.**  (K) is used only in Section 3, and only as
K1-surjectivity of the corners `E_i = Q_i B_i Q_i` of the two quotients and
K1-injectivity of the corner `F` of their common quotient.  (Pr) is used only
in Section 4.  Real rank zero is used only through (F1), to keep the corners and
quotients inside the class where (K) and (Pr) apply.  If the original
counterexample `A` is separable, so are `B` and `B'`, so (Pr) for separable
algebras suffices for separable `A`.

**Remarks.**

1. K1-injectivity of real rank zero algebras is attributed to Lin (AGOR cite
   Lin, Pacific J. Math., Lemma 2.2; STW cite Lemma 2.1).  That paper was not
   read here, so (K) is assumed as bijectivity rather than split into a cited
   and an open half.
2. Without (Pr), Sections 1--3 show under (K): every non-separative real rank
   zero algebra `A` has a matrix corner `B` with a prime quotient `B'` of the
   special form displayed after the Theorem.  Such a `B'` is not a direct sum,
   and for every nonzero ideal `K` of `B'`, `K` or `B'/K` is non-separative if
   AGOP Theorem 7.5 holds as cited in AGOR Remark 3.2 (extensions of separative
   real rank zero algebras are separative; AGOP not re-read).
3. Gluing needs `K_1 ∩ K_2 = 0`.  In a prime algebra gluing over
   `B'/(K_1 ∩ K_2)` only gives equivalence modulo `K_1 ∩ K_2`, which criticality
   already provides.  This is the wall for (Pr).
