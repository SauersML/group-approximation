# The Hilbert-hotel radical of a ring, and the MF radical of its elementary groups

Lane `un-open-1`, 2026-09-13. Plan: `research/artifacts/un-open-1-plan-2026-09-13.md` (`e321f55692`).
Everything here is **unreviewed** except the two manuscript propositions quoted verbatim in §2.

## 0. What this does

The manuscript computes `Rad_MF(EL_n(R))` one family at a time, and each time the answer is the relative
elementary subgroup of a two-sided ideal: the unit ideal for simple non-directly-finite rings
(`thm:full-defect-ring`, `cor:simple-infinite-ring`), the zero ideal for the Pestov 9.1 ring, the finitary
ideal `JeJ` for the Toeplitz-Jacobson algebra (`prop:torsion-defect-ring` and the paragraph after it), and
the restriction kernel to the chain-recurrent core for zero-dimensional systems (`thm:core-mf-radical`).
This artifact isolates a single ideal, defined without dynamics, groupoids or a choice of defect, that
produces all of them, and proves the containment `EL_n(R, hh(R)) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))` in one theorem.

## 1. The radical

`R` is a unital associative ring. Call `(s,t)` a **one-sided pair** when `ts = 1`, and
`e = 1 - st` its **defect**; `R` is directly finite exactly when every defect is `0`. Put

```text
d(R) = the two-sided ideal generated by { 1 - st : ts = 1 in R }.
```

Define an increasing chain of ideals by `hh_0 = 0`, `hh_(a+1)/hh_a = d(R/hh_a)`, and `hh_l = U_(a<l) hh_a`
at limits. The chain is increasing, so it stabilises; write `hh(R)` for its value, the **Hilbert-hotel
radical** of `R`.

**Theorem 1.** `hh(R)` is the smallest two-sided ideal of `R` with directly finite quotient. Moreover:
1. `R/hh(R)` is directly finite, and `hh(R) = 0` exactly when `R` is directly finite;
2. `hh(R) <= I` for every ideal `I` with `R/I` directly finite;
3. if `f : R ->> R'` is surjective then `f(hh(R)) <= hh(R')`;
4. `hh(R) = R` exactly when `R` has no nonzero directly finite quotient;
5. the same construction with defects taken in every `M_m(R)` gives `shh(R)`, the smallest ideal with
   stably finite quotient, and `hh(R) <= shh(R)`.

*Proof.* (1) At the stable stage `d(R/hh(R)) = 0`, i.e. every one-sided pair of `R/hh(R)` has zero defect.
(2) Transfinite induction. `hh_0 = 0 <= I`. If `hh_a <= I`, a one-sided pair of `R/hh_a` maps to a one-sided
pair of `R/I`, whose defect is `0` by direct finiteness; so the generators of `d(R/hh_a)` map into `I` and
`hh_(a+1) <= I`. Unions at limits.
(3) `R/f^(-1)(hh(R')) = R'/hh(R')` is directly finite, so (2) gives `hh(R) <= f^(-1)(hh(R'))`.
(4) Immediate from (2), the zero ring being directly finite.
(5) The proof of (2) verbatim, with defects of `M_m(R/hh_a)`; ideals of `M_m(R)` are `M_m(I)` and
`M_m(R)/M_m(I) = M_m(R/I)`. `[]`

`hh` is **not** functorial for injections: a directly finite ring embeds in rings that are not.

## 2. The two manuscript inputs, verbatim

From `non_mf_groups_exist.tex` at main's tip.

> **Proposition (torsion complementary idempotents), `prop:torsion-defect-ring`.** Let `R` be a countable
> unital ring, let `s,t in R` satisfy `ts=1`, and suppose that `e=1-st` has finite additive order. Then for
> every `n>=4`, `EL_n(R,ReR) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))`, where `EL_n(R,ReR)` is the normal closure of the
> `e_ij(a)` with `a in ReR`. In particular `EL_n(R)` is not MF when `e != 0`, and if `R` has positive
> characteristic, then `EL_4(R)` MF implies that `R` is directly finite.

> **Theorem (one-sided compression criterion), `thm:compression-criterion`.** Let `G` be countable and let
> `L <= G` have property (T). If `K` normal in `G` has property (T) and `K <= D_G(L)`, then every
> homomorphism from `G` to an MF group is trivial on `K`.

**What the proof of the proposition consumes.** Reading it line by line, the only ring facts used are

```text
(R1) ts = 1;            (R2) es = te = 0;              (R3) f_ij f_kl = delta_jk f_il, f_ij = s^i e t^j;
(R4) m e = 0 for some m >= 1;                          (R5) e != 0, witnessed by t f_12 s^2 = e.
```

(R2) and (R3) are consequences of (R1). Everything else is Steinberg calculus in `EL_4`, property (T) of
`EL_3(S)` for the unital subring `S = <s,t>` (EJZ Theorem 1.1, which needs only that `S` is finitely
generated), and `thm:compression-criterion`. In particular the proof never uses that (R1)-(R5) hold in `R`
rather than in a quotient of `R`.

## 3. The relative step

**Lemma 2 (radical descent).** Let `G` be a group and `N <= Rad_MF(G)` a normal subgroup. Then
`Rad_MF(G)` is the preimage of `Rad_MF(G/N)` under `G ->> G/N`.

*Proof.* If `g in Rad_MF(G)` and `th : G/N -> H` with `H` MF, then `th . pi` kills `g`, so `pi(g) in
Rad_MF(G/N)`. Conversely let `pi(g) in Rad_MF(G/N)` and `th : G -> H` with `H` MF. Then `N <= Rad_MF(G) <=
ker th`, so `th = th' . pi` for some `th' : G/N -> H`, and `th'` kills `Rad_MF(G/N)`, so `th(g) = 1`. `[]`

**Theorem 3 (relative torsion-defect step).** Let `R` be a countable unital ring, `J` a two-sided ideal,
`n >= 4`, and suppose `EL_n(R,J) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))`. Let `s,t in R` satisfy `ts - 1 in J`, put
`e = 1 - st`, and suppose `m e in J` for some `m >= 1`. Then

```text
EL_n(R, ReR + J) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R)).
```

*Proof.* Write `G = EL_n(R)`, `N = EL_n(R,J)`, `Gbar = G/N`, and `q : Gbar ->> EL_n(R/J)` for the map
induced by reduction. By Lemma 2 it suffices to put the image of `EL_n(R, ReR+J)` inside `Rad_MF(Gbar)`.

In `R/J` the pair `(sbar, tbar)` is a genuine one-sided pair and `ebar = 1 - sbar tbar` satisfies
`m ebar = 0`, so (R1)-(R4) of §2 hold in `R/J`. Their elementary-matrix consequences therefore hold in
`Gbar`: if two ring expressions agree modulo `J`, the corresponding elementary matrices differ by a factor
`e_ij(a)` with `a in J`, which is trivial in `Gbar`. Run the construction in the proof of
`prop:torsion-defect-ring` inside `Gbar`, with `S` the unital subring of `R` generated by `s,t`, with
`Lbar` the image of `EL_3(S)`, and with `vbar, cbar, ybar, zbar` the images of the displayed elements.

- `Lbar` is a quotient of `EL_3(S)`, and `S` is finitely generated, so `Lbar` has property (T).
- The compressor, centralizer and commutator identities are the elementary-matrix consequences of
  (R1)-(R3) modulo `J`, so they hold in `Gbar`, and `zbar in D_Bbar(Lbar)` for `Bbar = <Lbar, vbar, cbar,
  ybar>`.
- `zbar^m = 1` because `z^m = D(1 + m f_12)` and `m f_12 in J`.
- `zbar != 1`: `q(zbar)` is the corresponding element over `R/J`, nontrivial there by (R5), since
  `tbar fbar_12 sbar^2 = ebar != 0` whenever `ebar != 0`. (If `ebar = 0` then `ReR <= J` and there is
  nothing to prove.)
- `<zbar>` is central in `Bbar`, again by (R3) modulo `J`.

`thm:compression-criterion` applied to `Bbar` kills `zbar` under every homomorphism from `Bbar` to an MF
group, hence under every homomorphism from `Gbar`, because MF passes to subgroups. The printed propagation
`[z, e_41(f_20)] = e_41(f_10)` and `[e_24(t), e_41(f_10)] = e_21(e)`, valid in `Gbar` modulo `J`, then puts
every `e_ij(aeb)` in `Rad_MF(Gbar)`, and Steinberg relations with signed permutations give the image of
`EL_n(R, ReR+J)`. `[]`

## 4. The containment theorem

**Theorem 4.** Let `R` be a countable unital ring whose additive group is torsion (for instance any
algebra over a field of positive characteristic) and let `n >= 4`. Then

```text
EL_n(R, hh(R)) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R)).
```

*Proof.* Transfinite induction on the chain of §1, the statement at stage `a` being
`EL_n(R, hh_a) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))`. Stage `0` is trivial. At a successor, let `(sbar,tbar)` be a one-sided
pair of `R/hh_a` with defect `ebar`, and lift `s,t` to `R`. Then `ts - 1 in hh_a` and `m e in hh_a` for the
additive order `m` of `ebar`, so Theorem 3 gives `EL_n(R, ReR + hh_a) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))`. The ideal
`hh_(a+1)` is the sum of the ideals `ReR + hh_a` over all such pairs, and `e_ij(a_1 + ... + a_r) =
e_ij(a_1) ... e_ij(a_r)`, so the normal closures multiply: `EL_n(R, hh_(a+1)) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))`. At a
limit every entry lies at some earlier stage, so `EL_n(R, hh_l) = U_(a<l) EL_n(R, hh_a)`. `[]`

The torsion hypothesis is used only through (R4); it is exactly the hypothesis of
`prop:torsion-defect-ring`, and the manuscript's own remark after `cor:one-sided-ring-maximal` shows the
characteristic-zero case needs a different argument.

## 5. The sandwich, and what it reduces to

Write `GL_n(R,I)` for the congruence subgroup, the kernel of `GL_n(R) -> GL_n(R/I)`.

**Theorem 5.** Let `R` be as in Theorem 4, `n >= 4`, `hh = hh(R)`. If `EL_n(R/hh)` is MF, then

```text
EL_n(R, hh) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R)) <= EL_n(R) n GL_n(R, hh),
```

and the maximal MF quotient of `EL_n(R)` sits between `EL_n(R/hh)` and `EL_n(R)/EL_n(R,hh)`. The two ends
coincide exactly when the relative group `(EL_n(R) n GL_n(R,hh)) / EL_n(R,hh)` vanishes, and then
`Rad_MF(EL_n(R)) = EL_n(R,hh)` with maximal MF quotient `EL_n(R/hh)`.

*Proof.* The left containment is Theorem 4. For the right, `EL_n(R) ->> EL_n(R/hh)` is a homomorphism onto
an MF group, so `Rad_MF(EL_n(R))` lies in its kernel, which is `EL_n(R) n GL_n(R,hh)`. `[]`

This is the unification the two calibrating papers point at. The **paradox side supplies the radical**
(Theorem 4, a consequence of one-sided Kazhdan transport), the **measure side supplies the hypothesis**
that `EL_n(R/hh)` is MF (LEF towers, residually finite actions, exact matricial models), and they meet at
`hh(R)`. The residual ambiguity is a relative `K_1`-type group, which is the same correction term that
appears in the manuscript's `thm:mf-quotient-units`, where `Rad_MF(GL_n R) = [GL_n R, GL_n R]` with
quotient `K_1(R)`.

## 6. Model tests

| `R` | `hh(R)` | Theorem 5 gives | agrees with |
|---|---|---|---|
| simple, not directly finite (e.g. `L_k(1,m)`) | `R`, by Theorem 1(2) and simplicity | `Rad_MF = EL_n(R)`; quotient hypothesis is vacuous, `EL_n(0) = 1` | `cor:simple-infinite-ring` |
| `LC(X,F_q) ⋊ Z`, `X` infinite minimal subshift | `0` (the ring is stably finite) | `Rad_MF = 1` iff `EL_n(R)` is MF, which the Pestov towers supply | Pestov 9.1: `EL_3(R)/Z` is LEF |
| `J = F_2<s,t \| ts=1>` | `JeJ`, the finitary ideal: `J/JeJ = F_2[z,z^(-1)]` is directly finite | `Rad_MF = EL_n(J,JeJ)`, quotient `EL_n(F_2[z,z^(-1)])`, which is residually finite | the manuscript paragraph after `prop:torsion-defect-ring`, which proves the equality directly |
| `C(X,F_2) ⋊ Z`, `X` compact zero-dimensional | conjecturally the ideal of functions vanishing on the chain-recurrent core | the printed core formula | `thm:core-mf-radical`, `zero-dimensional-system-mf-radical-is-core-restriction` |

A field, or `F_q[x]`, is directly finite, so `hh = 0` and the theorem says nothing beyond "no defect".
This is the correct behaviour: a trivial ring rung must give a trivial group rung.

## 7. Two questions this raises

1. **Is the chain ever longer than one step?** Equivalently: if `R/d(R)` has a one-sided pair, does `R`
   have one whose defect generates more? A one-sided pair of `R/d(R)` need not lift to one in `R`, so
   nothing formal forces `hh(R) = d(R)`, but no example separating them is known here. Landed as
   `hilbert-hotel-radical-is-reached-in-one-step` with its attempts.
2. **Is `hh` the chain-recurrent core?** For `C(X,k) ⋊ Z` the defects should be exactly the wandering part,
   which would make the printed core theorem a corollary of Theorem 5 and give the radical a purely
   ring-theoretic reading. Landed as `zero-dimensional-hilbert-hotel-radical-is-core-ideal`.

A third, larger question: Theorem 5 predicts a finitely generated Kazhdan group whose MF radical is
**proper and nontrivial** and whose maximal MF quotient is an infinite simple LEF Kazhdan group, namely
`EL_n` of an algebraic Toeplitz extension of a minimal subshift crossed product. Nothing on main builds
that ring yet.
