# Rokhlin entropy dichotomy for groups with centralized self-copies

Date: 2026-09-12. Fork `gottschalk-positive-host`. Handwritten proofs, no computation.

**Sources.**
- B. Seward, *Krieger's finite generator theorem for actions of countable groups II*, arXiv:1501.03367v4 ("Seward II"). The quoted statements were read from the PDF text, extracted with ghostscript `txtwrite` on MSI on 2026-09-12.
- A. Alpeev and B. Seward, Part III, arXiv:1705.09707v3. Its abstract and introduction were read; they do not settle whether POS implies INF.

This note proves an internal form of Seward's Theorem 6.7. It then applies it to the binary Leavitt unit group and to a restricted sum of copies of the Gottschalk tester host. It proves no positive entropy lower bound for any nonsofic group.

## 0. Definitions and imported statements

Throughout, `G` is a countably infinite group. For an ergodic p.m.p. action on a standard probability space, the Rokhlin entropy is

    h^Rok_G(X, mu) = inf { H(alpha) : alpha a countable generating partition }.

`h^Rok_sup(G)` is the supremum of `h^Rok_G(X, mu)` over free ergodic p.m.p. actions with `h^Rok_G(X, mu) < infinity`. Seward's statements:

    POS(G): some free ergodic p.m.p. action has h^Rok_G(X, mu) > 0.
    INF(G): h^Rok_sup(G) = infinity.
    RBS(G): h^Rok_G(L^G, lambda^G) = H(L, lambda) for every standard probability space (L, lambda).

The following are imported from Seward II.

- **(S1) Theorem 2.6 (Seward–Tucker-Drob).** "Let G be a countably infinite group and let G y (X;µ) be a free p.m.p. action. Then for every ǫ > 0 there is a factor G y (Y;ν) of (X;µ) such that hRok(Y;ν) < ǫ and G acts freely on Y."
- **(S2) Sub-additivity, Corollary 2.5.** It is used here in the form it takes in the proof of Theorem 6.7. If `Y` is a factor of an ergodic `X` with invariant sigma-algebra `F`, then `h^Rok(X) <= h^Rok(Y) + h^Rok(X | F)`. Moreover `h^Rok(X | F) <= H(alpha | F)` whenever `sigma-alg_G(alpha) v F = B(X)`.
- **(S3) Theorem 1.1, from Part I.** If `G` acts ergodically on a non-atomic space and `h^Rok_G(X, mu) < H(p)` for a probability vector `p`, then there is a generating partition with distribution `p`. In particular `h^Rok < log k` gives a generator with `k` pieces.
- **(S4) Theorem 6.6.** If `H(L, lambda) < infinity`, then `h^Rok_G(L^G, lambda^G) = min{ H(L, lambda), h^Rok_sup(G) }`.
- **(S5) Theorem 7.6.** If `H(L, lambda) = infinity`, then `h^Rok_G(L^G, lambda^G) = infinity` iff POS(G).
- **(S6) Corollary 7.7.** The following are equivalent:
  - `h^Rok_sup(G) > 0`;
  - some free ergodic action has `0 < h^Rok < infinity`;
  - some free ergodic action has `h^Rok = infinity`.

  The first paragraph of the proof of Theorem 7.6 shows that POS(G) implies `h^Rok_sup(G) > 0`.
- **(S7) Corollary 4.1.** "Assume that hRok(kG;uG) = log(k) for every k ∈ N. Then G satisﬁes Gottschalk’s surjunctivity conjecture and Kaplansky’s direct ﬁniteness conjecture." The Kaplansky part is deduced from the Gottschalk part through [8, Section I.5] of Seward's bibliography.
- **(S8) Theorem 6.7.** "Let P be a countable group containing arbitrarily large ﬁnite subgroups. If G is any countably inﬁnite group with hRok_sup(G) < ∞ then hRok_sup(P × G) = 0." Seward concludes "(∀G POS) ⇒ (∀G INF)" and writes "We do not know whether POS implies INF".

Seward's own summary is `INF => RBS => INV + CPE + GOT + KAP`.

## 1. Theorem A: an internal commuting finite subgroup suffices

**Theorem A.** Let Gamma be a countably infinite group. Suppose Gamma contains
- an infinite subgroup G with `h^Rok_sup(G) < infinity`, and
- finite subgroups T_n with `|T_n| -> infinity`, such that each T_n commutes elementwise with G and `T_n ∩ G = {1}`.

Then `h^Rok_sup(Gamma) = 0`.

*Proof.* This is Seward's proof of Theorem 6.7, with `P x G` replaced by a commuting pair `T_n, G` inside Gamma.

Fix a finite set L with `|L| >= 2` and uniform measure lambda. By (S4) for Gamma, it suffices to show `h^Rok_Gamma(L^Gamma, lambda^Gamma) = 0`. That forces `min{log|L|, h^Rok_sup(Gamma)} = 0`, and hence `h^Rok_sup(Gamma) = 0`.

Write `X = L^Gamma` and `mu = lambda^Gamma`, with shift `(g.x)(h) = x(g^-1 h)`. The Bernoulli action is free and ergodic, and its restriction to every infinite subgroup is mixing.

Fix eps > 0. Choose k with `h^Rok_sup(G) < log k`, then `T = T_n` with `log(k)/|T| < eps`.

**Step 1: a free G-Bernoulli factor.** For `pi in L^T` put `D_pi = {x : x|_T = pi}`. T acts on `L^T` by `(u.pi)(s) = pi(u^-1 s)`, and `u.D_pi = D_(u.pi)`. Let Q be the partition whose atoms are the unions of `D_pi` over T-orbits of pi.
- The partition `g.Q` is measurable with respect to the coordinates in `gT`.
- If `g != g'` in G, then `gT ∩ g'T` is empty: `gt = g't'` would give `g'^-1 g = t' t^-1` in `G ∩ T = {1}`.
- So the G-translates of Q are independent, and the factor `G y (Z, eta)` generated by Q is a G-Bernoulli shift with base Q.
- Q has at least two atoms, because the constant patterns lie in distinct orbits. So G acts freely on Z.

**Step 2: a small free factor and a transversal.** By (S1) there is a factor `Gamma y (Y, nu)` with `h^Rok_Gamma(Y, nu) < eps` on which Gamma acts freely. T is finite and acts freely, so there is a Borel `M' ⊆ Y` meeting every T-orbit exactly once. Let F be the Gamma-invariant sigma-algebra of Y, and M in F the preimage of M'. The sets `t.M`, `t in T`, partition X, so `mu(M) = 1/|T|`.

**Step 3: a T-invariant generator along G.** Put `C_pi = union over s in T of s.(D_pi ∩ M)` and `xi = {C_pi : pi in L^T}`. This is a partition, and each `C_pi` is T-invariant.
- **xi generates modulo F.** Suppose `x(1) = l` and `x in s.M`. Then `s^-1.x` lies in `D_pi ∩ M` with `pi = (s^-1.x)|_T`, and `pi(s^-1) = x(1) = l`. Hence

      R_l = {x : x(1) = l} = union over s in T and pi with pi(s^-1) = l of (C_pi ∩ s.M),

  so `sigma-alg_Gamma(xi) v F = B(X)`.
- **xi refines Q.** Each atom of Q is a union of atoms `C_(t.pi)`.

Let `G y (W, omega)` be the factor generated by xi along G.
- W factors onto Z, so G acts freely on W.
- W is ergodic, being a factor of the mixing restriction to G. It is non-atomic.
- `h^Rok_G(W) <= H(xi) <= log|L^T| < infinity`. By the definition of the supremum, `h^Rok_G(W) <= h^Rok_sup(G) < log k`.

By (S3), W has a generator beta' with k pieces. Let beta be its preimage in X. Then `sigma-alg_G(beta)` contains xi, and

    B(X) = sigma-alg_Gamma(xi) v F ⊆ sigma-alg_Gamma(beta) v F.

Every set in `sigma-alg_G(xi)` is T-invariant, because each `C_pi` is and G commutes with T. In particular every atom of beta is T-invariant.

**Step 4: recording beta on the transversal.** Put `alpha = {X \ M} ∪ {B ∩ M : B in beta}`. For B in beta, T-invariance gives `B = union over s in T of s.(B ∩ M)`, and `s.M` lies in F. So `beta ⊆ sigma-alg_Gamma(alpha) v F`, and therefore `B(X) = sigma-alg_Gamma(alpha) v F`.

**Step 5: the estimate.** By (S2),

    h^Rok_Gamma(X, mu) <= h^Rok_Gamma(Y, nu) + h^Rok_Gamma(X, mu | F) < eps + H(alpha | F).

M lies in F, alpha is trivial off M, and alpha has at most k atoms on M. So `H(alpha | F) <= mu(M) log k = log(k)/|T| < eps`, and `h^Rok_Gamma(X, mu) < 2 eps`. QED.

**Corollary A1 (self-copies).** If Gamma contains a subgroup isomorphic to `Gamma x Gamma` and has finite subgroups of unbounded order, then `h^Rok_sup(Gamma)` is 0 or infinity.

*Proof.* Let `Gamma_1 x Gamma_2 <= Gamma` be the embedded square.
- Take `G = Gamma_1`. It is isomorphic to Gamma, so `h^Rok_sup(G) = h^Rok_sup(Gamma)`.
- Take `T_n <= Gamma_2` finite of unbounded order. These commute with G and meet it trivially.

If `h^Rok_sup(Gamma) < infinity`, Theorem A gives 0. QED.

**Corollary A2.** Under the hypotheses of A1, POS(Gamma), INF(Gamma) and RBS(Gamma) are equivalent. They imply that Gamma is surjunctive and that `K[Gamma]` is directly finite for every field K.

*Proof.*
- POS => INF: POS gives `h^Rok_sup > 0` by (S6), and A1 then gives infinity.
- INF => RBS: for finite-entropy bases this is (S4). For infinite-entropy bases it is (S5), since INF implies POS.
- RBS => POS: the Bernoulli 2-shift is free and ergodic with Rokhlin entropy log 2.
- RBS gives surjunctivity and direct finiteness by (S7).

QED.

## 2. The binary Leavitt unit group

`L = L_(F_2)(1,2)` has generators `s_0, s_1, t_0, t_1` with `t_i s_j = delta_ij` and `s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1 = 1`. Put `U = L^x`.

**Lemma 2.1 (corners).** The maps `iota_0(a) = s_0 a t_0 + s_1 t_1` and `iota_1(b) = s_0 t_0 + s_1 b t_1` are injective homomorphisms `U -> U`. Their images commute and meet trivially.

*Proof.*
- **Homomorphisms.** Since `t_0 s_1 = t_1 s_0 = 0`, `iota_0(a) iota_0(a') = s_0 a a' t_0 + s_1 t_1` and `iota_0(1) = 1`. So `iota_0` is multiplicative and maps units to units.
- **Injective.** `t_0 iota_0(a) s_0 = a`.
- **Commuting.** `iota_0(a) iota_1(b) = s_0 a t_0 + s_1 b t_1 = iota_1(b) iota_0(a)`.
- **Trivial intersection.** If `iota_0(a) = iota_1(b)`, then `t_0 (.) s_0` gives `a = 1`, and `t_1 (.) s_1` gives `b = 1`.

The same computations apply to `iota_1`. QED.

**Lemma 2.2 (finite subgroups).** `GL_(2^n)(F_2)` embeds in U for every `n >= 1`.

*Proof.* For words `u = u_1...u_n` put `s_u = s_(u_1)...s_(u_n)` and `t_u = t_(u_n)...t_(u_1)`.
- From `t_i s_j = delta_ij`, induction on n gives `t_v s_w = delta_(v,w)` for `|v| = |w| = n`.
- From `s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1 = 1`, induction gives `sum over |u| = n of s_u t_u = 1`.
- So the elements `e_(u,v) = s_u t_v` are `2^n x 2^n` matrix units summing to the identity. They define a unital algebra homomorphism `M_(2^n)(F_2) -> L`.
- It is injective, because `M_(2^n)(F_2)` is simple and `t_u e_(u,u) s_u = 1 != 0`. Units map to units.

QED.

**Proposition 2.3.** U satisfies the hypotheses of Corollary A1. So `h^Rok_sup(U)` is 0 or infinity, and POS(U), INF(U) and RBS(U) are equivalent. Each makes U surjunctive and `K[U]` directly finite for every field K.

**Corollary 2.4 (entropy collapse under a counterexample).** Suppose an injective nonsurjective cellular automaton over U exists, or some `K[U]` has a one-sided inverse that is not two-sided. Then every free ergodic p.m.p. action of U has Rokhlin entropy zero, including every Bernoulli shift with finite or infinite base. So U would fail POS. Seward lists POS for all groups as an open problem, and we know of no group proved to fail POS.

**Remark 2.5 (Thompson's V).** The elements of V supported in the cylinder `[0]` form a copy of V. The permutations of the `2^n` cylinders of length `n+1` inside `[1]` give `Sym(2^n)`. These commute with that copy and meet it trivially, so the same dichotomy holds for V.

## 3. One countable host for Gottschalk's conjecture

Let `U_*` be the two-generator host of `gottschalk-surjunctivity-fixed-two-generator-tester`: `U_*` is surjunctive iff every group is surjunctive. Let `S = Sym_fin(N)`, and put

    Gamma_U = restricted direct sum over i in N of (U_* x S).

Splitting N into even and odd indices embeds `Gamma_U x Gamma_U` in `Gamma_U`, and S contains `Sym(n)` for every n. So Corollaries A1 and A2 apply.

**Proposition 3.1.** Suppose `Gamma_U` admits a free ergodic p.m.p. action with positive Rokhlin entropy. Then every group is surjunctive, and Kaplansky's direct finiteness conjecture holds for all groups and fields.

*Proof.*
1. By A2, POS(`Gamma_U`) makes `Gamma_U` surjunctive.
2. Subgroups of surjunctive groups are surjunctive, so `U_*` is surjunctive.
3. By the tester equivalence, every group is surjunctive.
4. Gottschalk's conjecture implies Kaplansky's direct finiteness conjecture, as recorded under (S7).

QED.

Seward's Corollary 7.8 needs POS for every countably infinite group; Proposition 3.1 needs it for one explicit countable group. `Gamma_U` is not finitely generated, but any countable group containing `U_*` and satisfying A1 serves equally well.

## 4. What this does not do

- **No lower bound.** No lower bound for Rokhlin entropy is proved here; POS(U) and POS(`Gamma_U`) stay open. Every known lower bound runs through sofic entropy (Seward II, introduction), and U is not sofic.
- **Restriction and co-induction.** If `H <= U` is sofic, a generator for the H-action generates for U, so restriction gives only `h^Rok_U(X) <= h^Rok_H(X)`. Co-inducing a positive-entropy action from a sofic subgroup gives a U-action with a generator of the base's entropy, which is again an upper bound.
- **No existence mechanism.** The dichotomy decides neither side. Surjunctive nonsofic groups exist (`surjunctive-nonsofic-group-exists`), and whether they satisfy POS is not decided here.
- **A counterexample proves more.** By Corollary 2.4, a strict automaton over U would also exhibit a group failing POS.
