## 0. Gate: what `prop:torsion-defect-ring` consumes

un-open-1 (`un-open-1-hilbert-hotel-radical-2026-09-13.md` §2) claims the printed proof uses only
(R1) `ts=1`, (R2) `es=te=0`, (R3) `f_ij f_kl = delta_jk f_il`, (R4) `me=0`, (R5) `e!=0`.

- **Consumption: PASS.** `lem:ring-compression-cell` assumes only `ts=1`. Its compressor `u` and the element
  `c=[e_41(e),e_14(t)]=diag(1,1,1,1+et)` are computed from `te=0` and `es=0`. The proposition adds the matrix-unit
  identities for `f_ij=s^i e t^j`, `me=0` (for `z^m=1`) and `t f_12 s^2=e` (for `z!=1`). Nothing else about `R` is used.
- **Transfer to a quotient: GAP**, found independently by un-verify-open, whose review owns the formal verdict.
  `relative-torsion-defect-mf-radical-step` runs the proof in `Gbar = EL_n(R)/EL_n(R,J)` when `ts-1 in J`. The printed
  identities (eq. `intertwine`, the diagonal form of `c`, `vcv^{-1}=e_14(f_02)`, `[y,d]=z^{-1}`, `z^m=1`) are MATRIX
  identities. With `ts=1+j`, the lifted words satisfy them only modulo `EL_n(R) n GL_n(R,J)`, not modulo `EL_n(R,J)`.
  The discrepancy is the relative group `(EL_n(R) n GL_n(R,J))/EL_n(R,J)`. So the relative step, and the transfinite
  containment built on it, stand only at stage one: `EL_n(R,d(R)) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))` for torsion `R` follows from the
  absolute proposition alone. **The construction below uses a genuine one-sided pair and never the relative step.**

## 1. Crossed products cannot give the predicted object

The sandwich predicts an extension `0 -> J -> R -> Pestov ring -> 0` whose group has nontrivial proper MF radical
and simple maximal MF quotient. The manuscript's zero-dimensional crossed products `R_X = LC(X,F_2) x| Z` have exact
radical `EL_n(R_X,I)` with quotient `EL_n(R_Y)`, `Y = CR(T)` (`thm:core-mf-radical`). They never produce it.

**Lemma C (Conley).** Let `T` be a homeomorphism of a compact metric space `X`. If `CR(T)` is chain transitive
(any two of its points are joined by `eps`-chains in `X` for every `eps>0`), then `CR(T) = X`.

*Proof.* Let `x in X` and `eps>0`, and put `delta = eps/2`. The limit sets `omega(x)` and `alpha(x)` are nonempty and
lie in `CR(T)`. Pick `N` with `d(T^N x, q) < delta` for some `q in omega(x)`, and `M` with `d(T^{-M} x, r) < delta`
for some `r in alpha(x)`. Take a `delta`-chain `q = y_0, ..., y_k = r`. Then
`x, Tx, ..., T^{N-1}x, y_0, ..., y_{k-1}, T^{-M}x, ..., T^{-1}x, x` is an `eps`-chain from `x` to `x`. The two junctions
are `d(T(T^{N-1}x), y_0) < delta` and `d(T y_{k-1}, T^{-M}x) <= d(T y_{k-1}, r) + d(r, T^{-M}x) < eps`. `[]`

**Firewall.** If `X != CR(T)`, then `CR(T)` is not chain transitive, hence not minimal, since a minimal set is chain
transitive through its dense orbits. It contains a nonempty closed invariant `Z` properly. The functions vanishing
on `Z` give a nonzero proper ideal `I_Z` of `R_Y`: a clopen piece of `Y\Z` supplies a nonzero element, and `1` is not
in `I_Z`. For `n>=3`, `EL_n(R_Y, I_Z)` is normal and noncentral (`[e_12(a), e_23(1)] = e_13(a)`), and its product
with the centre is proper, because `EL_n(R_Y/I_Z)` is perfect and nontrivial. So **for every transient
zero-dimensional system the maximal MF quotient `EL_n(R_Y)` is not simple modulo its centre**. The predicted object
must leave the crossed-product world.

## 2. The ring

Fix an infinite minimal subshift `Y` of `A^Z`, `A` finite, with shift `(Tx)_n = x_{n+1}` and `alpha(f) = f o T^{-1}`.
Let `k` be a field, eventually `F_2`. Put

```text
V = direct sum over i >= 0 of LC(Y,k) b_i,
M_f(g b_i) = (fg) b_i,     s(g b_i) = alpha(g) b_{i+1},     t(g b_{i+1}) = alpha^{-1}(g) b_i,  t(g b_0) = 0.
```

Let `R` be the unital subalgebra of `End_k(V)` generated by `s`, `t` and the letter indicators
`M_{chi_a}`, `chi_a = [x_0 = a]`, `a in A`. So `R` is finitely generated.

**Relations** (direct evaluation on `g b_i`):
- `ts = 1`;
- `st = 1 - e`, with `e` the projection onto the `b_0` summand;
- `es = te = 0`;
- `e` commutes with every `M_f`;
- `s M_f = M_{alpha(f)} s`, `M_f s = s M_{alpha^{-1}(f)}`;
- `t M_f = M_{alpha^{-1}(f)} t`, `M_f t = t M_{alpha(f)}`;
- `t^m M_f s^m = M_{alpha^{-m}(f)}`.

Let `A_+` be the unital subalgebra of `LC(Y,k)` generated by the `alpha^{-m}(chi_a)`, `m>=0`, the cylinder functions
on coordinates `x_m`, `m >= 0`. Over a finite field it is locally finite: every finitely generated subalgebra is
some `k^r`.

**Lemma 1 (normal form).** `R = span{ s^i M_a t^j : i, j >= 0, a in A_+ }`.

*Proof.* The generators lie in the span, and it is closed under products. If `k >= j`,
`(s^i M_a t^j)(s^k M_b t^l) = s^{i+k-j} M_{alpha^{-(k-j)}(a) b} t^l`. If `j > k`, it is
`s^i M_{a alpha^{-(j-k)}(b)} t^{j-k+l}`. Both use `t^j s^k = s^{k-j}` or `t^{j-k}` and the commutations above, and
negative translates stay in `A_+`. Conversely `alpha^{-m}(chi_a) = t^m M_{chi_a} s^m`, so the span is generated. `[]`

**Lemma 2 (the defect ideal).** `J := ReR = span{ s^i M_a e t^j }`, and `a E_ij |-> s^i M_a e t^j` is a ring
isomorphism `M_inf(A_+) -> J`. Over a finite field `J` is locally matricial, with local units
`p_N = sum_{i<N} s^i e t^i`.

*Proof.* `(s^i M_a t^j) e (s^k M_b t^l)` vanishes unless `j = k = 0`, since `t e = 0 = e s`, and then it equals
`s^i M_{ab} e t^l`. Products: `(s^i M_a e t^j)(s^k M_b e t^l) = delta_jk s^i M_{ab} e t^l`, because `t^j s^k` is a
positive power of `s` killed by `e` on its left, or a positive power of `t` killed by `e` on its right, unless
`j = k`. Injectivity: `s^i M_a e t^j` sends `g b_j` to `alpha^i(a alpha^{-j}(g)) b_i` and kills the other summands. `[]`

**Lemma 3 (the quotient).** There is a surjective ring map `pi : R -> LC(Y,k) x|_alpha Z`, with `s |-> u` and
`t |-> u^{-1}`, whose kernel is `J`.

*Proof.* For `x = sum c_{ij} s^i M_{a_ij} t^j` and `m >= max j`,
`x(g b_m) = sum_d F_d alpha^d(g) b_{m+d}` with `F_d = sum_{i-j=d} alpha^i(a_ij)`.
- **Well defined.** The `F_d` are determined by the operator (evaluate at `g=1`, `m` large). Put
  `pi(x) = sum_d F_d u^d`.
- **Multiplicative.** On the tail, composition is the crossed-product rule `(F u^{d'})(F' u^d) = F alpha^{d'}(F') u^{d'+d}`.
- **Surjective.** The image contains `u^{+-1}` and `alpha^i(A_+)` for all `i`, which generate `LC(Y,k)`.
- **Kernel.** `J` lies in it (`e |-> 0`). Conversely, `s^{i+1} M_{alpha^{-1}(a)} t^{j+1} = s^i M_a (st) t^j`, so
  `s^i M_a t^j - s^{i+1} M_{alpha^{-1}(a)} t^{j+1} = s^i M_a e t^j in J`. Modulo `J` every term with `i - j = d`
  becomes `s^N M_{alpha^{i-N}(a_ij)} t^{N-d}` for one large `N`. Their sum is `s^N M_{alpha^{-N}(F_d)} t^{N-d}`, which
  is `0` when `F_d = 0`. `[]`

## 3. The Hilbert-hotel radical is reached in one step

**Proposition 4.** `hh(R) = d(R) = J`.

*Proof.* `e` is the defect of the genuine one-sided pair `(s,t)`, so `J = ReR <= d(R) <= hh(R)`.
`R/J ~= LC(Y,k) x| Z` is directly finite: minimal subshifts satisfy the cycle condition, so the ring is LEF
(`subshift-ring-lef-iff-word-edges-lie-on-cycles`), and LEF rings are stably finite. By
`hilbert-hotel-radical-is-smallest-directly-finite-quotient`, `hh(R) <= J`. `[]`

This is a positive instance of the open `hilbert-hotel-radical-is-reached-in-one-step`.

## 4. The group

**Theorem 5.** Let `k = F_2`, `Y` an infinite minimal subshift, and `R, J` as above.
1. For `n >= 3`, `EL_n(R)` is finitely generated with property (T).
2. For `n >= 4`: `Rad_MF(EL_n(R)) = EL_n(R,J) = ker(EL_n(R) -> EL_n(R/J))`. This subgroup is locally finite,
   nontrivial and proper, so `EL_n(R)` is not MF.
3. The maximal MF quotient of `EL_n(R)`, `n >= 4`, is `EL_n(LC(Y,F_2) x| Z)`. That group is infinite, finitely
   generated and simple (the centre is `F_2^x I_n = 1`), with property (T), and it is LEF. At `n = 3`, modulo its
   centre, it is Pestov's group `S`.

*Proof.*
- **(1)** `R` is finitely generated (§2), so EJZ applies (`elementary-groups-over-fg-rings-have-property-t`). For
  `n >= 3`, `EL_n(R)` is generated by the `e_ij(r)` with `r` a generator, via `e_ij(ab) = [e_ik(a), e_kj(b)]`.
- **(2), lower bound.** `J = ReR` and `2e = 0`, so `prop:torsion-defect-ring`
  (`finite-additive-order-one-sided-defects-are-mf-invisible`) gives `EL_n(R,J) <= Rad_MF(EL_n(R))` for `n >= 4`.
- **(2), kernel.** Let `g in EL_n(R)` with `g == I` modulo `J`. The entries of `g - I` and `g^{-1} - I` lie in a
  finite `B = M_N(A_0) ~= prod M_N(F_2)` inside `J`, with unit `p = p_N` (Lemma 2). Then `h = pI + (g - I)` is invertible
  in `M_n(B)` with inverse `pI + (g^{-1} - I)`, and `g = (I - pI) + h`. Over `F_2`,
  `GL_n(B) ~= prod GL_{nN}(F_2) = prod SL_{nN}(F_2)` is generated by transvections. Under block flattening these are
  roots `e_ij(b)`, `b in B`, or commutators `[e_ik(x), e_ki(y)]` through another coordinate. The map
  `h |-> (I - pI) + h` is a homomorphism sending `pI + bE_ij` to `e_ij(b)`, so `g in EL_n(R,J)`. Finitely many such `g`
  lie in one finite group, which gives local finiteness. This is the argument of `thm:core-mf-radical`, transported to
  `J ~= M_inf(A_+)`.
- **(2), upper bound.** Restriction `EL_n(R) -> EL_n(R/J)` is surjective, since elementary coefficients lift, and its
  target is LEF, hence MF. So `Rad_MF` lies in the kernel.
- **(2), nontrivial and proper.** `e_21(e) != I` in `EL_n(R,J)`, and the quotient is infinite.
- **(3)** `R/J` is the Steinberg algebra of the free minimal transformation groupoid
  (`crossed-products-are-transformation-steinberg-algebras`). The reviewed
  `steinberg-elementary-groups-are-simple-mod-centre` gives simplicity modulo the centre, which lies in the scalars
  `F_2^x = 1`. `R/J` is finitely generated (by `u^{+-1}` and the letter indicators), so EJZ gives (T). LEF holds as in
  Proposition 4. `[]`

**Reading.** One finitely generated Kazhdan group holds both calibrating mechanisms:
- the **paradox mechanism**: the defect `e` of a genuine one-sided pair makes its locally finite radical invisible to
  every MF target;
- the **measure mechanism**: the Pestov towers make the quotient LEF.

The sandwich of un-open-1 is exact here, with no relative `K_1` slack: `hh = d = J`, and `Rad_MF` equals the congruence
kernel. Compare the calibrating rings:
- Leavitt: `Rad_MF` is everything;
- Pestov ring: `Rad_MF = 1`;
- Toeplitz–Jacobson `F_2<s,t|ts=1>`: quotient `EL_n(F_2[z^{+-1}])`, residually finite, not simple;
- transient crossed products: quotient not simple (§1).

## 5. Limits and checks

- **Model tests.**
  - Trivial alphabet: `A = {a}` gives `Y` a single fixed point, excluded (`Y` infinite). There the same construction
    over `LC({pt}) = k` is the Toeplitz–Jacobson algebra, and Lemma 3 recovers `J/JeJ = k[z^{+-1}]`, matching the
    manuscript.
  - Two directions: `A_+` uses only nonnegative coordinates. That is why the quotient needs the tail argument of
    Lemma 3 rather than a presentation.
- **Characteristic.** Theorem 5(2) uses `F_2` for `GL = SL`. Over `F_q`, `q > 2`, the kernel contains
  `EL_n(R,J)` with at most a determinant-type abelian slack. That case is not claimed.
- **Novelty.** Not checked beyond main's graph. The Toeplitz–Pimsner construction is classical at the C*-level
  (Pimsner–Voiculescu extensions). The algebraic packaging and the group consequence are, as far as the graph shows, new.
- **Unreviewed:** Lemmas 1–3, Proposition 4, Theorem 5, Lemma C and the firewall.
