## 1. Type rigidity

**Theorem D.**
- Setting: N ≥ 4; R a unital ring generated by x_1,…,x_s; F = Z⟨t_1,…,t_s⟩ the free ring, with π: F → R, t_i ↦ x_i. G = EL_N(R) is marked by T = {e_ij(1), e_ij(x_m)}.
- Hypothesis: (Q_k, T_k) → (G, T) with Q_k finite nonabelian simple.
- Conclusion: for all large k there are:
  - a finite field F_{q_k};
  - an integer d_k ≥ 1;
  - a surjective ring homomorphism ρ_k: F → M_{d_k}(F_{q_k});
  - an isomorphism Q_k ≅ PSL_{Nd_k}(F_{q_k}) carrying T_k to the images of e_ij(1), e_ij(ρ_k(t_m)).
- Moreover, for each finite set P ⊆ ker π, ρ_k(P) = 0 for all large k.
- If G is infinite and simple, then d_k → ∞, by Proposition B3 of part 1. In general d_k may stay bounded: for R = F_q[t], G is linear and bounded ranks occur.

*Proof.*
1. **Cover.** St_N(F) is finitely presented on S = {x_ij(1), x_ij(t_m)} ([KrM]; f.p. does not depend on the finite generating set). G is its marked quotient through x_ij(a) ↦ e_ij(π a). The finitely many relators are relators of G, so for large k they hold in Q_k, and there is an epimorphism ψ_k: St_N(F) → Q_k with x_ij(t) ↦ t_k.
2. **Level ideal.** K_ij = {a ∈ F : ψ_k(x_ij(a)) = 1} is additive.
   - [x_ij(a), x_jl(1)] = x_il(a) and [x_li(1), x_ij(a)] = x_lj(a) give the inclusion cycle of `lef-elementary-groups-force-lef-rings-proof` step 4, so all K_ij equal one K.
   - [x_ij(a), x_jl(b)] = x_il(ab) makes K a two-sided ideal.
   - A_k = F/K injects additively into Q_k through x_12, so A_k is finite.
   - The relations x_ij(K) = 1 hold, so ψ_k factors through St_N(F)/⟨⟨x_ij(K)⟩⟩ = St_N(A_k) (generators and relations lift).
3. **Central kernel.** A_k is finite, hence semilocal. By [Vor], K_2(N,A_k) is central in St_N(A_k). Its image in the nonabelian simple Q_k is central, hence trivial. So Q_k is a quotient of E_N(A_k).
4. **Radical.** J = J(A_k) is nilpotent. The kernel of E_N(A_k) → E_N(A_k/J) lies in GL_N(A_k,J) = I + M_N(J), a nilpotent group (it has the central series I + M_N(J^i)). A nilpotent normal subgroup of a nonabelian simple group is trivial, so Q_k is a quotient of E_N(A_k/J).
5. **Semisimple part.**
   - A_k/J = ∏_i M_{d_i}(F_{q_i}) (Wedderburn; finite division rings are fields).
   - E_N(∏_i M_{d_i}(F_{q_i})) = ∏_i E_{Nd_i}(F_{q_i}) = ∏_i SL_{Nd_i}(F_{q_i}), a product of perfect quasisimple groups (Nd_i ≥ 4).
   - The image of each factor is normal in Q_k (the factors commute), so exactly one factor surjects and the others die. The center dies too. So Q_k ≅ PSL_{Nd}(F_q) for one factor M_d(F_q).
   - ρ_k := F → A_k → A_k/J → M_d(F_q) is onto. T_k corresponds to e_ij of the images, by construction.
6. **Relations of R.** For p ∈ ker π, e_12(π p) = 1 in G. This is one relator of bounded length (nested commutators), so for large k it holds in Q_k: e_12(ρ_k(p)) is central in SL_{Nd}(F_q). Hence ρ_k(p) = 0.
7. **Ranks.** Steps 1–6 prove the type statement with no condition on d_k. If G is infinite and simple, Proposition B3 of part 1 gives d_k → ∞: approximants of bounded dimension would embed G in a linear group, making G residually finite. For R = R_X, G = EL_N(R_X) is infinite and simple (EL_N over a minimal subshift ring, N ≥ 3). ∎

**Remark D1.**
- Steps 2–7 need only N ≥ 3. N ≥ 4 enters only through [KrM], as in `lef-elementary-groups-force-lef-rings`. For N = 3, St_3 over a free ring surjecting onto F[t] is not finitely presented ([KrM, Cor. 2]), so large approximants need not satisfy all Steinberg relations.
- Theorem D is the "all approximants" upgrade of that node: there, SOME partial model gives a finite quotient ring; here EVERY finite simple approximant IS a projective special linear group over a matrix quotient ring.

## 2. The rational spectrum is an isomorphism invariant in rank ≥ 4

For an infinite minimal subshift X let Cyc(X) be the set of orders of finite cyclic factors of X, equivalently the orders of its rational continuous eigenvalues (part 2, Lemma C1). Put G_X^{(N)} = EL_N(LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Z).

For a finitely generated group G define Div(G) as the set of m ≥ 1 such that every sequence of finite simple groups converging to G, for any finite generating set, eventually consists of groups PSL_{M_k}(F_{q_k}) with m | M_k.
- Div(G) is an isomorphism invariant. A change of generating set rewrites a converging sequence into a converging sequence; the rewritten tuples generate Q_k for large k.
- The natural dimension M of PSL_M(F_q) is well defined for large M.

**Theorem E.** For N ≥ 4 and every infinite minimal subshift X,

  Div(G_X^{(N)}) = {m : m | N·c for some c ∈ Cyc(X)}.

Consequently:
- if G_X^{(N)} ≅ G_Y^{(N′)}, then N·g_∞(X) = N′·g_∞(Y) as supernatural numbers;
- for N = N′ ≥ 4, X and Y have the same rational continuous eigenvalues.

*Proof.*
- **⊇.** By Theorem D every converging sequence of finite simple groups is eventually PSL_{Nd_k}(F_{2^{f_k}}) with ring models ρ_k: F → M_{d_k}(F_{2^{f_k}}). For c ∈ Cyc(X) the cyclic factor idempotents give finitely many ring relations (part 2 §2(1)), which hold for large k by D(6). So c | d_k, since conjugate orthogonal idempotents with sum I have equal F_{2^f}-rank. Hence N·c | M_k.
- **⊆.** Let m ∈ Div(G_X^{(N)}).
  - Choose levels r_k → ∞ slowly, and distinct primes p_k, p′_k → ∞, and put d_k = g_{r_k}p_k, d′_k = g_{r_k}p′_k.
  - For large k these are large multiples of g_{r_k}, so Theorem C(2) gives primitive periodic ring models of sizes d_k and d′_k through degrees → ∞.
  - As in Corollary C2 (the degree argument works for every rank N), (SL_{Nd_k}(F_2), elementary markings) → G_X^{(N)}, and likewise for d′_k. SL_{Nd}(F_2) = PSL_{Nd}(F_2).
  - m ∈ Div gives m | N g_{r_k} p_k and m | N g_{r_k} p′_k for large k, so m | N g_{r_k}, and g_{r_k} ∈ Cyc(X) by Lemma C1.
- **Invariance.** Div determines the supernatural number N·g_∞(X) = lcm{N c : c ∈ Cyc(X)}. For fixed N this determines g_∞(X), hence Cyc(X). ∎

**Examples.**
- For N ≥ 4, EL_N over a Sturmian subshift (Cyc = {1}) is not isomorphic to EL_N over the Thue–Morse subshift (Cyc = all powers of 2), nor to EL_N over a Toeplitz subshift with nontrivial periods.
- Sturmian subshifts with different slopes are NOT separated by this invariant, consistent with `sturmian-elementary-group-lef-growth-is-exp-r-squared`.

**Remark E1 (for the note's rigidity question).**
- The rational spectrum is an invariant of strong orbit equivalence. It is determined by the dimension group, through divisibility of the order unit (recalled, not checked at source).
- So Theorem E is consistent with the note's Question ("does G_X ≅ G_Y force flip conjugacy or SOE?"). It proves the rank ≥ 4 analogue of one SOE invariant.
- Rank 3 stays open; see `subshift-group-finite-simple-shadows-are-ring-models`.
