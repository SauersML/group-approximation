# A finite subgroup controls every normal quotient of a directed-seam group

2026-09-13. Written proof, not Lean-verified.

Let `G_D=EL_(3ell)(R_D)` be the three-marked group attached to a finite
directed graph `D=(V,E)` with nonempty vertex set and infinite minimal
binary endpoint systems. Keep its coding length `ell` fixed throughout.
The [normal-lattice theorem](pestov91-directed-seams-and-normal-lattices-2026-09-13.md)
labels the normal subgroups by `N(J,S)`, where `J subset E`, `S subset V`
and `inc(S) subset J`; here `inc(S)` is the set of edges incident to
at least one vertex in `S`, ignoring orientation.

## 1. One elementary abelian subgroup supplies all normal generators

In the original tagged alphabet, let `p_v` indicate the two payload
letters of vertex `v`, and let `epsilon_e` indicate the unique marker
of edge `e`. These are pairwise orthogonal nonzero idempotents. Use
the same elementary position `r=1`, `s=ell+1` and put

`a_v=e_(r,s)(p_v)`, `b_e=e_(r,s)(epsilon_e)`.

Then

`A=<a_v,b_e> ~= C_2^(|V|+|E|)`.                       (1)

Indeed the root map is injective on the additive span of these
idempotents, and root multiplication adds coefficients. For arbitrary
subsets `S subset V`, `T subset E`, put

`q(S,T)=product_(v in S) a_v product_(e in T) b_e`.

The exact normal-generation formula is

`normalClosure(q(S,T))=N(T union inc(S),S)`.           (2)

To check it, restrict the root to each endpoint: it is nontrivial
exactly at vertices in `S`. On an edge orbit it is nontrivial exactly
when its marker is selected or at least one endpoint is in `S`.
The orbit restriction is faithful for that edge's restricted ring.
In the incidence classification, an element of `N(J',S')` has trivial
endpoint restriction outside `S'` and trivial edge restriction outside
`J'`. Conversely these restrictions characterize membership: after
deleting `J'`, only the isolated vertex factors `S'` can remain.
Thus the smallest normal subgroup containing this root is exactly (2).

Consequently every nontrivial normal subgroup has a single involution
as a normal generator, all chosen inside the same finite subgroup `A`.
The canonical choice for `N(J,S)` is `q(S,J)`. Moreover

`A intersection N(J,S)=<a_v (v in S), b_e (e in J)>`.  (3)

Thus intersection with this one finite subgroup determines every
normal subgroup. It is a coordinate subspace of dimension `|S|+|J|`.

These are explicit short words in the original three generators.
Under binary coding, `b_e` is the cylinder word for its marker code;
`a_v` is the product of the cylinder words for its two payload codes.
The [short-cylinder theorem](pestov91-short-involution-mf-implications-2026-09-13.md)
therefore gives

`|q(S,T)|<=256(2|S|+|T|)ell^2`.                       (4)

The chosen scalar position `1,ell+1` is precisely the first phase in
each of the first two outer blocks, so these binary cylinder words
give the displayed original-ring roots without an extra permutation.

## 2. Every quotient and MF reflection has a single-relator description

Every normal quotient has the form

`G_D / normalClosure(q(S,J))`, `inc(S) subset J`.      (5)

Let `B_J` be the noncycle edges remaining in `D\J`. The entire MF
reflection of this quotient is obtained by adding the one relation

`product_(e in B_J) b_e=1`.                           (6)

If `B_J` is empty no relation is needed. Otherwise the word in (6)
is an involution and normally generates the quotient's MF radical.
Equivalently the reflection can be written directly with the single
relator `q(S,J union B_J)`. This follows from the exact closure formula
`cl_MF(N(J,S))=N(J union B_J,S)` and normal generation of a finite
product of simple edge kernels by the product of their noncentral
involutions.

There is also a finite-premise bound inside each fixed `G_D`. Given
any set of relators `R subset G_D`, some subset `R_0 subset R` of size
at most `|V|+|E|` has the same ordinary normal closure. To see this,
write the normal label of each relator and take the unions of its
edge and vertex coordinates. Choose one relator witnessing each
coordinate present in those unions. Their normal closures already
have the same join. Hence all ordinary and MF consequences of `R`
are already consequences of this bounded subset `R_0`.

This bound is relative to the fixed graph group. The previous
arbitrarily-many-input examples vary the graph and so do not conflict
with it. Formula (5) describes adding one relation to `G_D`; it is
not a finite presentation of `G_D`.

## 3. No nontrivial quotient is finitely presented

Let `H=G_D/N(J,S)` be nontrivial. Then `V\S` is nonempty: if `S=V`,
admissibility forces `J=E`, giving the trivial quotient. Endpoint
restriction gives an exact epimorphism

`H -> product_(v in V\S) Q_v`,

with a finite product of simple finitary edge kernels. That kernel
is normally generated by one involution if it is nontrivial, and
requires no generator otherwise.

If `H` were finitely presented, its endpoint quotient would therefore
be finitely presented, by adding this one relation. But the endpoint
product is infinite, LEF, and has no nontrivial finite quotients.
Such a group cannot be finitely presented: a finitely presented LEF
group is residually finite. For the latter fact, include the generators,
all partial products of a finite defining relator list, and all partial
products of a word to be separated in one finite LEF table. The finite
table makes the defining relators exact in a finite group and preserves
the chosen nonidentity word, giving a separating finite homomorphism.
This contradicts the endpoint product's absence of finite quotients.

Thus every nontrivial normal quotient of every graph group `G_D` is
infinitely presented. This includes the non-MF quotients, their LEF
reflections and all simple endpoint quotients. It gives a uniform
normal-generation proof extending the earlier nearby-intercept argument.

## 4. Polynomial algorithms with fixed quadratic Sturmian endpoints

Now fix the same Sturmian system of slope `alpha=sqrt(2)-1` and its
mechanical point of intercept `1/2` at every vertex. For each fixed
finite graph `D`, the following problems admit polynomial-time algorithms
in the total expanded length of the input words over `c,c^{-1},t,w`:

- the word problem;
- computing the incidence label of the normal closure of finitely
  many words, and membership in that normal closure;
- deciding whether given word relations force another word to vanish
  in every MF-target homomorphism out of `G_D`.

Here are the algorithms and their bounds. In the original tagged
subshift the length-`h` complexity is exactly

`p_(X_D)(h)=|V|(h+1)+|E|h`, `h>=1`.                  (7)

Each tagged Sturmian component contributes `h+1` distinct words.
For each edge, the `h` placements of its unique marker contribute
exactly `h` additional words, all distinguished by marker and position.
The same formula holds for every retained set of vertices and edges.

These words can be enumerated in polynomial time. For the endpoint
language, sort the `h+1` rotation cuts `{-j alpha:0<=j<=h}` and code
the midpoint of each successive interval, including the interval
ending at one. All comparisons are exact comparisons of numbers in
`Q(sqrt(2))` with integers of `O(log h)` bits. The seam words are read
directly from their single marker position and the fixed mechanical
point. The finite-field arithmetic has constant cost.

After binary coding, a length-`N` window is obtained from one of `ell`
alignments of an original word of length at most `ceil(N/ell)+1`.
For fixed `D`, (7) therefore gives `O(N)` binary windows, enumerable
in polynomial time. Always retain the original code table when
restricting to an endpoint or an edge closure.

For an input word of length `L`, evaluate its crossed-product matrix
normal form separately at each allowed window of length `2L+1`.
Keep the coefficients of the nine entries for degrees `-L,...,L`.
On appending a generator on the right, a coefficient at degree `k`
is multiplied by that generator's coefficient at the shifted point
`T^{-k}x`, using the product rule `f u^k g u^j=f(g circ T^{-k})u^(k+j)`.
Each generator has boundedly many nonzero entries and degrees in
`{-1,0,1}`, so an update costs `O(L)` field operations. There are `L`
updates per window and `O(L)` windows, giving `O(L^3)` field operations
and polynomial bit complexity including window enumeration. The word
is the identity exactly when these normal forms are the identity on
every allowed window.

Apply that identity test to the endpoint restrictions and to the
closures of the individual edge orbits. For a word `g`, define

`S(g)={v : endpoint restriction of g is not 1}`,
`J(g)={e : edge-closure restriction of g is not 1}`.

The membership characterization in section 1 gives exactly
`normalClosure(g)=N(J(g),S(g))`. For finitely many premise words take
the coordinatewise unions `J,S`. Ordinary consequence for an output
word `w` is precisely `J(w) subset J` and `S(w) subset S`. MF
consequence is precisely

`J(w) subset J union B_J`, `S(w) subset S`.            (8)

Finding `B_J` is one strongly connected component calculation. Since
the graph is fixed, only a fixed number of word tests are needed per
input word, so the total running time is polynomial. This claim uses
expanded word length; it does not assert a polynomial bound in the
size of a compressed straight-line word DAG.

## 5. Executable solver and bounded checks

The [exact solver](solve-pestov91-sturmian-consequence-2026-09-13.py)
implements the language enumeration and restriction algorithm. Its JSON
input specifies an integer vertex count, a list of directed endpoint
pairs, a list of premise words, and an output word. Words are expanded
strings over `c,C,t,w`, with `C=c^{-1}`. It reports both incidence labels,
the additional radical edges, and the ordinary and MF consequence
answers. Loops and parallel edges are allowed. Arithmetic in the
quadratic field uses integer square roots and no floating point.

The [bounded audit](check-pestov91-finite-normal-control-2026-09-13.py)
compares its Laurent normal forms against the independent finite
cyclic matrix implementation used for the previous word compiler.
It checks all eight binary cycles of length three on 33 words, including
the five marking identities and four short decoder words. It also
compares Sturmian languages of lengths 1--16 against exact mechanical
samples, checks the raw graph complexity, exhausts 110 controller
elements on five small graphs, and tests 18 identity restrictions.
The [receipt](pestov91-control-algebra-receipt-2026-09-13.json) records
that every check passed on MSI in 0.189635 seconds. These are bounded
implementation checks; the arbitrary-word algorithm and infinite-group
claims are justified by the written proof, not by those finite cases.

## Scope

The finite subgroup and non-presentation results apply to all endpoint
systems in the directed-seam construction. The polynomial algorithms
use the specified quadratic Sturmian endpoints. No formal Lean proof
or global priority assertion is claimed.
