- **Diagonal subgroup:** `L = L_X := GL_3(A) = LC(X, GL_3(F_2))`. Since `GL_3(F_2) = E_3(F_2)`, each constant piece

- `L_V := {l ∈ L : l ≡ I_3 off V}` for clopen `V`.
- **Rigid subgroups:** `G_U := G ∩ (I_3 + M_3(e_U R e_U))`, the elements of `G` supported in `U`.
- **Full group:** `[[T]]` is the topological full group. For `γ ∈ [[T]]` equal to `T^{n_i}` on a clopen partition

## 1. Results

**Lemma B (normal subgroups of locally constant groups).** Let `Z` be a Cantor space and `Q` a finite nonabelian
simple group. Every normal subgroup of `LC(Z,Q)` is `N_O := {f : supp f ⊆ O}` for a unique open `O ⊆ Z`, where
`supp f = {z : f(z) ≠ 1}` is clopen.
- The maximal normal subgroups are `N_z := N_{Z∖{z}}`, `z ∈ Z`.
- The hull–kernel topology on the set of maximal normal subgroups is the topology of `Z`.
- So every isomorphism `LC(Z,Q) → LC(Z',Q')` is induced on points by a unique homeomorphism `Z → Z'`.

**Lemma C (rigid supports).** Let `U` be clopen.
- (C1) `span_{F_2}{g − I : g ∈ G_U} = M_3(e_U R e_U)`.
- (C2) `C_G(G_U) = G_{X∖U}`.
- (C3) `C_G(L_{X∖U}) = G_U`.
- (C4) `N_G(G_U)` is the set of elements of `G` commuting with `E_U`, and `⋂_U N_G(G_U) = L`.
- (C5) `N_G(L)` normalizes `D = A·I_3`. Its conjugation action on `D ≅ LC(X,F_2)` is by homeomorphisms in a subgroup
  `Γ_X` with `[[T]]' ≤ Γ_X ≤ [[T]]`, and this action coincides with its action on maximal normal subgroups of
  `L ≅ LC(X, GL_3(F_2))`.

**Theorem A (diagonal-preserving isomorphisms).** There is an isomorphism `α: G_X → G_Y` with `α(L_X) = L_Y` if and
only if `(X,T)` and `(Y,S)` are flip conjugate.

**Firewall F.** A proof that `G_X ≅ G_Y` forces flip conjugacy must prove that a ring isomorphism `R_X ≅ R_Y`
forces flip conjugacy (§5). In the Cantor-minimal C*-setting that fails: the crossed product alone gives only
strong orbit equivalence.

## 2. Proof of Lemma B

`f ∈ LC(Z,Q)` takes finitely many values on a clopen partition, so `supp f` is clopen.

Let `N` be normal and `f ∈ N`, and let `V ⊆ supp f` be clopen with `f ≡ x ≠ 1` on `V`.
- For `y ∈ Q`, let `g_y` equal `y` on `V` and `1` elsewhere. Then `[f, g_y] ∈ N` equals `[x,y]` on `V` and `1`
  elsewhere.
- The subgroup of `Q` generated by `{[x,y] : y ∈ Q}` is normal in `Q` and nontrivial, because `Z(Q) = 1`. Since `Q`
  is simple, it is `Q`.
- So `N` contains every function equal to a constant of `Q` on `V` and `1` elsewhere. Products of these give every
  element of `N_V`.
- Partitioning `supp f` into such pieces gives `N_{supp f} ≤ N`.
- Put `O = ⋃{V clopen : N_V ≤ N}`. This is open and `N = N_O`, since every `f ∈ N` has `supp f ⊆ O`, and every
  `f ∈ N_O` has compact clopen support covered by finitely many `V` with `N_V ≤ N`, then splits as a product.
- `O` is unique because `N_O ⊆ N_{O'}` iff `O ⊆ O'`, using functions supported on small clopen sets.

Maximal proper normal subgroups correspond to maximal proper open sets, which are the complements of points. The hull–kernel
basic sets `{N_z : M ⊄ N_z}` are `{z : z ∈ O_M}`, which are the open sets of `Z`. So the space of maximal normal
subgroups is `Z`. An isomorphism maps normal subgroups to normal subgroups and preserves inclusion and hulls, so it
induces a homeomorphism, and `f` and `α(f)` have corresponding supports. ∎

## 3. Proof of Lemma C

**(C1).** `G_U ∋ e_ij(r)` for `r ∈ e_U R e_U`, `i ≠ j`, so the span contains `E_ij r`. The identity
`(g−I)(h−I) = (gh−I) − (g−I) − (h−I)` shows the span is closed under products. It contains
`(E_ij r)(E_ji s) = E_ii rs`, hence all of `M_3(e_U R e_U)`. The reverse inclusion holds by definition.

**Centre of a corner.** `Z(e_U R e_U) = F_2 e_U`.
- Let `c = Σ_j f_j u^j ∈ e_U R e_U` be central in the corner. Each `f_j` is supported in `U ∩ T^j U`.
- For clopen `V ⊆ U`, `e_V c − c e_V = Σ_j f_j (e_V − e_{T^j V}) u^j`.
- If `x ∈ supp f_j` with `j ≠ 0`, then `x, T^{-j}x ∈ U` and `x ≠ T^{-j}x`, since `T` has no periodic points. Take `V ⊆ U`
  containing `x` but not `T^{-j}x`. Then `f_j(e_V − e_{T^jV})(x) = f_j(x) ≠ 0`, a contradiction. So `c = f_0 ∈ LC(U)`.
- Commuting with `e_U u^j e_U = e_{U ∩ T^jU} u^j` gives `f_0 = f_0∘T^{-j}` on `U ∩ T^jU` for every `j`. So `f_0` is
  constant on `U ∩ (orbit)` for each orbit. Orbits are dense and `f_0` is locally constant, so `f_0` is constant.

The same separation argument shows that `LC(U)` is a maximal commutative subalgebra of `e_U R e_U`.

**(C2).** Let `h ∈ C_G(G_U)`.
- By (C1), `h` commutes with `M_3(e_U R e_U) ∋ E_U`. So `h = E_U h E_U + (1−E_U) h (1−E_U)`, and `E_U h E_U` is
  central in `M_3(e_U R e_U)`.
- So `E_U h E_U = c E_U` with `c ∈ F_2`, and invertibility forces `c = 1`. So `h − I ∈ M_3(e_{X∖U} R e_{X∖U})` and
  `h ∈ G_{X∖U}`.
- Conversely, for `g ∈ G_U` and `h ∈ G_{X∖U}`, `(g−I)(h−I) = 0 = (h−I)(g−I)`, so `g` and `h` commute.

**(C3).** The span of `GL_3(F_2) − I` is a nonzero two-sided ideal of the simple ring `M_3(F_2)`: it is the image of
the augmentation ideal and contains `E_12`. So it is `M_3(F_2)`, and `span(L_{X∖U} − I) = M_3(e_{X∖U} A)`.

Let `h ∈ C_G(L_{X∖U})`.
- `h` commutes with `E_{X∖U}` and with `M_3(e_{X∖U}A)`. So `h` is block diagonal, and its `X∖U` corner is `c I_3`
  with `c ∈ e_{X∖U} R e_{X∖U}` commuting with `e_{X∖U}A`.
- So `c ∈ LC(X∖U, F_2)` by maximal commutativity, and invertibility gives `c = e_{X∖U}`. Hence `h ∈ G_U`.
- Conversely, `G_U` and `L_{X∖U}` have disjoint supports, as in (C2).

**(C4).** If `g` normalizes `G_U`, it normalizes the span `M_3(e_U R e_U)` and fixes its unit `E_U`. Conversely, an
element commuting with `E_U` preserves `I + M_3(e_U R e_U)`.
- So `⋂_U N_G(G_U) = G ∩ C_{M_3(R)}({E_U})`.
- `C_{M_3(R)}({E_U})` is `M_3(C_R(A)) = M_3(A)`, and `G ∩ GL_3(A) = GL_3(A) = L`.

**(C5).** `span L = M_3(A)` by (C3) with `U = ∅`. So `N_G(L)` normalizes the ring `M_3(A)` and its centre `D`.

Let `g ∈ GL_3(R)` with `g D g^{-1} = D`. Write `g (f I) g^{-1} = (f∘φ^{-1}) I` for a homeomorphism `φ`.
- Entrywise, `g_ij f = (f∘φ^{-1}) g_ij`. With `g_ij = Σ_n c_n u^n`, this reads `c_n (f∘T^{-n} − f∘φ^{-1}) = 0` for
  all `f`. So `φ^{-1} = T^{-n}` on `supp c_n`.
- If all coefficient functions of all entries vanished on a clopen `V ≠ ∅`, then `e_V g = 0` and `e_V = e_V g g^{-1} = 0`.
  So these supports cover `X`, and `φ ∈ [[T]]`.

Lower bound:
- Whitehead: `diag(a, a^{-1}) = w(a) w(−1)` with `w(a) = e_12(a) e_21(−a^{-1}) e_12(a)`. So `diag(a,a^{-1},1) ∈ E_3(R)`
  for every unit `a`, and likewise in the other coordinate pairs.
- `diag([b,c],1,1) = diag(b,b^{-1},1) diag(c,1,c^{-1}) diag(b^{-1},b,1) diag(c^{-1},1,c)`.
- So `diag(a,a,a) ∈ G` for `a ∈ [R^×, R^×]`, in particular for `a = a_γ`, `γ ∈ [[T]]'`.
- This element normalizes `L` and induces `γ`.

Compatibility: if `g` normalizes `D` with homeomorphism `φ`, then `g L_V g^{-1} = L_{φ(V)}`, since
`g e_V (l − I) g^{-1} = e_{φ(V)} g(l−I)g^{-1}`. So the action on maximal normal subgroups (Lemma B) is `φ`. ∎

## 4. Proof of Theorem A

Let `α: G_X → G_Y` be an isomorphism with `α(L_X) = L_Y`.
- By Lemma B (with `Q = GL_3(F_2)`, simple of order 168), `α|_L` is induced by a homeomorphism `h: X → Y`.
- `α(N_{G_X}(L_X)) = N_{G_Y}(L_Y)`. By (C5) and equivariance, `h Γ_X h^{-1} = Γ_Y`.

**Piecewise closure.** For `Γ ≤ Homeo(X)`, let `P(Γ)` be the set of homeomorphisms that agree with elements of `Γ` on
the pieces of a finite clopen partition. `P` is defined from the action alone, so `h P(Γ_X) h^{-1} = P(Γ_Y)`.

**Claim:** `P(Γ_X) = [[T]]`.
- `⊆` holds because `Γ_X ≤ [[T]]` and `[[T]]` is closed under piecewise gluing.
- `⊇`: every `φ ∈ [[T]]` is `T^{n}` near each point, so it suffices that `T^n|_V` agrees with an element of `[[T]]'` for
  small clopen `V ∋ x`.
- For `n ≠ 0`, choose `V` with `V, T^nV, T^{2n}V` pairwise disjoint (no periodic points). Let `τ_1` swap `V ↔ T^nV` by
  `T^{±n}`, and `τ_2` swap `V ↔ T^{2n}V` by `T^{±2n}`; both are the identity elsewhere and lie in `[[T]]`.
- On the three levels these act as the transpositions `(01)` and `(02)`. So `[τ_1,τ_2] = τ_1τ_2τ_1τ_2` is the 3-cycle
  `0→1→2→0`, namely `T^n` on `V ∪ T^nV` and `T^{-2n}` on `T^{2n}V`. It lies in `[[T]]'` and equals `T^n` on `V`.

So `h [[T]] h^{-1} = [[S]]`, hence `[[T]] ≅ [[S]]`, and by §4a `(X,T)` and `(Y,S)` are flip conjugate.

**Converse.** Let `ψ: X → Y` be a homeomorphism with `ψ T = S^{ε} ψ`, `ε = ±1`. Then `f u^j ↦ (f∘ψ^{-1}) u^{εj}` is
a ring isomorphism `R_X → R_Y`, since `u^ε (f∘ψ^{-1}) u^{-ε} = (f∘T^{-1})∘ψ^{-1}`. It maps `A_X` onto `A_Y`, so
`EL_3` of it maps `L_X` onto `L_Y`. ∎

## 5. Firewall F: what a classification costs

**(F1).** A ring isomorphism `β: R_X → R_Y` induces `EL_3(β): G_X ≅ G_Y`. So "`G_X ≅ G_Y` ⇒ flip conjugate" implies
"`R_X ≅ R_Y` as rings ⇒ flip conjugate". That is a reconstruction theorem with no diagonal hypothesis.
- The reconstruction theorems located all assume the isomorphism preserves the diagonal (Renault; Li arXiv:1503.01704
  Thm 1.2 for C*-crossed products; Ara–Bosa–Hazrat–Sims arXiv:1601.02872; Steinberg arXiv:1711.01903) or a grading.
- For the C*-completion `C(X)⋊Z`, the algebra alone determines only strong orbit equivalence (Giordano–Putnam–Skau
  1995), which is strictly coarser than flip conjugacy.
- So any proof must use a feature of the algebraic ring that the C*-algebra forgets.
- Bounded check: three web searches plus the abstracts above. No diagonal-free rigidity theorem for algebraic crossed
  products located.

**(F2) What Theorem A leaves.** Theorem A turns flip rigidity into a recognition problem: show that `α(L_X)` is an
`Aut(G_Y)`-translate of `L_Y` for every isomorphism `α`. Two sub-steps would do it:
- (i) group-theoretic recognition: `α(L_X) = GL_3(A')` for a maximal commutative, idempotent-spanned subalgebra
  `A' ⊆ R_Y`, normalized by enough units to generate `R_Y`;
- (ii) Cartan uniqueness: every such `A'` is `β(A_Y)` for a ring automorphism `β` of `R_Y`.

Both steps are OPEN. Step (ii) is a ring statement, and its C*-analogue is false in general.

**(F3) Why no diagonal-preserving counterexample exists.** Clopen sets `W, W'` of the same class in `K^0` are
`[[T]]`-equidecomposable. Conjugation by the full-group unit `a_γ` then maps `e_W R e_W` onto `e_{W'} R e_{W'}`
preserving diagonals. So the induced systems on `W` and `W'` have isomorphic groupoids and are flip conjugate. A
counterexample to flip rigidity therefore needs a ring isomorphism that moves the diagonal to a non-conjugate Cartan
subalgebra. None is known.

## 6. Model tests and scope

- **Periodic X (finite orbit, the trivial model).** `T` has periodic points. `Z(e_U R e_U)` is larger, the 3-cycle
  step fails, and `R` is not simple. Theorem A makes no claim there, and each hypothesis is used.
- **Fibonacci subshift.** Infinite minimal, so all hypotheses hold.
- **Flip.** `(X,T)` and `(X,T^{-1})` give isomorphic `G` via `u ↦ u^{-1}`, consistent with "flip" in Theorem A.
- **Inner moves.** Conjugation by a unit that is not a full-group unit, e.g. `1 + e_U u` with `U ∩ TU = ∅` (a square-zero
  perturbation), sends `L_X` to another subgroup. Theorem A applies after composing with that inner automorphism, so
  the hypothesis is really "some isomorphism carries `L_X` to an `Aut(G_Y)`-translate of `L_Y`".
- **The field.** `F_2` enters only through `GL_3(F_2) = E_3(F_2)`, `L = EL_3(A)`, and the trivial units of `A`. Over
  `F_q`, replace `L` by `EL_3(A)` and `Q` by `SL_3(F_q)/Z`; `(C3)` then gives corners `cI` with `c ∈ LC(X∖U,F_q^×)`.
  This is not checked further here.
