Sources were fetched as arXiv e-prints on MSI and read from the TeX:

- H. Linton, arXiv:2407.09272v2 (rational derived series of one-relator groups);
- V. Alekseev and H. Bradford, *Sofic actions, halo products, and metric
  approximations of groups*, arXiv:2601.18742v1 (below: AB).

Verdict in one paragraph.  Linton proves that `R` is rationally perfect,
normally generated by one short word, and infinitely generated when nontrivial.
He proves nothing about freeness, local freeness, residual finiteness or a
hierarchy of `R`, and a nontrivial `R` is never free.  AB's action-lifting
theorem needs a *functorial* halo (a covariant functor on sets with
injections), and its proof uses the halo intersection law.  No imported theorem
makes `R` or its free-shell action sofic outside the torsion and
primitivity-rank-at-least-three cases already in the graph.  Section 4 proves a
tame class: when `R` is a free product of factors permuted by `F0` with cyclic
point stabilizers and sofic factors, the shell and `G` are sofic, and with free
permutation of isomorphic factors the halo claim holds.  Section 5 shows that
both canonical site assignments break the halo intersection law already for
Baumslag--Gersten.  Neither result touches the open general case.

## 1. Linton, verbatim

Theorem 1.1 (`\label{main}`, TeX lines 109--116):

> Let $F$ be a free group, $w\in F$ a word and $G = F/\normal{w}$ a one-relator
> group. There is a word $r\in F$, of length bounded above by that of $w$, and
> an integer $k\geqslant 1$ such that $w\in r^k[\normal{r}, \normal{r}]$ and
> \[ G_{\Q}^{(\omega+1)} = G_{\Q}^{(\omega)} = \normal{r}_G. \]
> In particular, the maximal residually rationally solvable quotient of $G$ is
> the one-relator group $F/\normal{r}$.

Remark after it (lines 118--122):

> Torsion-free one-relator groups cannot contain finitely generated rationally
> perfect subgroups since they are locally indicable by a result of Brodskii
> [Br84] (see also Howie [Ho82]) and so the subgroup $G^{(\omega)}_{\Q}$ from
> \cref{main} is always infinitely generated when it is non-trivial. The
> prototypical examples of non residually (rationally) solvable one-relator
> groups are the Baumslag--Gersten groups [Ba69]:
> \[ G(1, n) \isom \langle a, t \mid [a^t, a] = a^{n-1}\rangle. \]
> Here we adopt the convention that $a^t = t^{-1}at$ and
> $[a^t, a] = a^ta(a^t)^{-1}a^{-1}$. When $n\neq 1$, the relator expresses (a
> power of) $a$ as a commutator over itself and a conjugate of itself. Thus, the
> normal closure of $a$ is perfect when $n = 2$ and is rationally perfect (has
> torsion abelianisation) otherwise. [...] In fact, it is worth pointing out
> that in general, if $G = F/\normal{w}$ is a one-relator group, $k\geqslant 1$
> and $w\in r^k[\normal{r}, \normal{r}]$, then $\normal{r}_G$ is a
> $\Q$-perfect subgroup of $G$.

Corollary 1.2 (`\label{main_corollary}`, lines 128--136):

> Let $F$ be a free group, $w\in F$ a word and $G = F/\normal{w}$ a one-relator
> group. The following are equivalent:
> (1) $G$ is residually $\Q$-solvable.
> (2) $G^{(n)}_{\Q}$ is a free group for some integer $n\geqslant 0$.
> (3) For all words $r\in F$ of length at most that of $w$, and all integers
> $k\geqslant 1$, if $w\in r^k[\normal{r}, \normal{r}]$, then $w$ is conjugate
> to $r$ or $r^{-1}$.

Line 138:

> The equivalence between (1) and (2) is a consequence of \cref{free_kernel}, a
> key step in the proof of \cref{main}. In fact, a consequence of our proofs is
> that the integer $n$ from (2) may be taken to be the length of the relator
> $w$. If $G$ is not residually rationally solvable, then $G^{(n)}_{\Q}$ is an
> extension of the maximal rationally perfect subgroup by a free group.

Theorem 3.2 (`\label{free_kernel}`, lines 237--240):

> Let $A$ and $B$ be countable groups, let $n\geqslant 1$ be an integer, let
> $w\in A*B$ be a cyclically reduced word of length at least two that is not a
> proper power and consider the one-relator product
> $G = \frac{A*B}{\normal{w^n}}$. If $\phi\colon G\to Q$ is a homomorphism to a
> locally indicable group such that $\phi(u)\neq 1$ for each proper non-empty
> subword $u$ of $w$, then $\ker(\phi)$ splits as a free product of a free
> group, subgroups of $A$, subgroups of $B$ and copies of $\Z/n\Z$.

### 1.1 What is known about `R` (task 1)

- **Trivial** exactly when `G` is residually `Q`-solvable (Cor 1.2); that class
  is closed in [[residually-rationally-solvable-one-relator-sofic]].
- **Rationally perfect and normally one-generated**, `R = <<r>>_G` (Thm 1.1).
- **Infinitely generated when nontrivial** in the torsion-free case (line 118).
- **Never free when nontrivial.**  A nontrivial free group has infinite
  torsion-free abelianization, while `R` is `Q`-perfect.  The same argument
  shows that `R` has no nontrivial free free factor.
- **Locally indicable** as a subgroup of a torsion-free one-relator group (the
  Brodskii--Howie theorem quoted at line 118).  Locally free, residually finite
  or hierarchical structure is not asserted anywhere in the paper.
- **Theorem 3.2 never applies to `G -> G/R` with free factors when `R != 1`.**
  With `A` and `B` free, its conclusion would make `R` free.  So the free-kernel
  theorem cannot itself produce a free-product decomposition of a nontrivial
  radical from the given presentation.
- **Which groups have `R != 1`:** exactly those failing Cor 1.2(3).  The
  standard examples are the Baumslag--Gersten groups `G(1,n)`, the Berlai
  families of [[berlai-perfect-linton-radicals-are-sofic]], and free products
  of such groups with free groups.  The mod-`k` relation module is recorded in
  [[linton-radical-abelianization-is-truncated-group-ring]].

Definition 2.1 (lines 221--234):

> A \emph{halo of groups} over a set $X$ is a family of groups
> $\mathcal{L}_X = \lbrace L(Y) : Y \subseteq X \rbrace$ satisfying the
> following axioms: (i) For all $Z \subseteq Y \subseteq X$ then
> $L(Z) \leq L(Y)$; (ii) $L(\emptyset)$ is the trivial group, and
> $L(X) = \langle L(Y) : Y \subseteq X \text{ finite} \rangle$; (iii) For all
> $Y,Z \subseteq X$, $L(Y \cap Z) = L(Y) \cap L(Z)$.
