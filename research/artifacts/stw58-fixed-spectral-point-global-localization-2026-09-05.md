# LVIII: global localization at a fixed spectral point

Date: 5 September 2026. Complete proof; internal mathematical review completed.
No external review, formal verification, or novelty priority is claimed.
This supplies a global functional-calculus localization criterion and a
necessary spectral obstruction for any surviving kernel. It does not
prove global fragmentation for every stably null family.

The coordinating review checked joint quasitrace evaluation, the
compactness-to-uniform-arc argument, degree-one normalization and the
based logarithm, and the distinction between the positive localization
criterion and the scalar-family obstruction to direct factorization.

## 1. Statement

Let \(B\) be nonzero simple unital finite pure with
\(QT_1(B)\ne\varnothing\). Let \(X\) be a compact metric space of covering
dimension \(d\ge1\), and let \(u\in U(C(X,B))\).

For \(x\in X\) and \(\tau\in QT_1(B)\), restriction of \(\tau\) to
the abelian algebra generated by \(u(x)\) and the unit gives a
probability measure \(\mu_{x,\tau}\) on the circle:
\[
 \tau(g(u(x)))=\int_{\mathbb T}g(z)\,d\mu_{x,\tau}(z)
 \quad(g\in C(\mathbb T)_+).
 \tag{1}
\]

**Theorem 1 (fixed spectral point).** Suppose there is a
\(\lambda\in\mathbb T\setminus\{1\}\) such that
\[
 \mu_{x,\tau}(\{\lambda\})<\frac1{d+1}
 \qquad(x\in X,\ \tau\in QT_1(B)).
 \tag{2}
\]
Then there are a continuous function \(f:\mathbb T\to\mathbb T\)
and a continuous real function \(L:\mathbb T\to\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 f(1)=1,\quad L(1)=0,\quad
 u=f(u)\exp(iL(u)),
 \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{x,\tau}d_\tau(|f(u(x))-1|)<\frac1{d+1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The function \(f\) has degree one and is identically one off one
fixed proper arc containing \(\lambda\). In particular \(u\) is
homotopic to a family of uniformly small support by
\[
 u_t=f(u)\exp\bigl(i(1-t)L(u)\bigr),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
 \tag{5}
\]
If \(u\) is based at the identity, this homotopy is based.

Consequently, if \([u]=0\) in \(K_1(C(X,B))\), then \(u\) contracts.
For \(X=S^k\), the class of \(u\) belongs to the canonical stable
summand \(I_k(B)\) from
[the small-support splitting proof](stw58-canonical-small-support-stable-splitting-2026-09-05.md).

There is no spectral logarithm of \(u\) asserted here. The logarithm
in (3) is that of the scalar ratio \(z/f(z)\), whose degree is zero.
The arc is fixed over all of \(X\), not selected separately at
individual parameter points.

Condition (2) is equivalently
\[
 d_\tau(|u(x)-\lambda1_B|)>\frac d{d+1}
 \qquad(x,\tau).
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, restriction to \(C^*(u(x),1)\) gives
\(d_\tau(|u(x)-\lambda|)=1-\mu_{x,\tau}(\{\lambda\})\).

## 2. Compactness converts a point condition to an arc bound

**Lemma 2.** Fix \(\lambda\in\mathbb T\). If
\[
 \mu_{x,\tau}(\{\lambda\})<c
 \quad\text{for every }(x,\tau)\in X\times QT_1(B),
 \tag{7}
\]
then some open arc \(J\) containing \(\lambda\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{x,\tau}\mu_{x,\tau}(J)<c.
 \tag{8}
\]
If \(\lambda\ne1\), the closure of \(J\) can be chosen disjoint
from \(1\).

**Proof.** The normalized quasitrace space is compact in the
pointwise topology. For every continuous scalar function \(g\)
on the circle, the function
\[
 (x,\tau)\longmapsto\tau(g(u(x)))
\]
is continuous. For self-adjoint \(g\), joint continuity follows
from norm-continuity of functional calculus and the uniform
Lipschitz estimate for normalized quasitraces on self-adjoint
elements; real and imaginary parts handle general \(g\).

Choose decreasing continuous functions \(g_n:\mathbb T\to[0,1]\)
which equal one on a neighborhood of \(\lambda\), have supports
shrinking to \(\{\lambda\}\), and decrease pointwise to
\(1_{\{\lambda\}}\). Put
\[
 F_n(x,\tau)=\tau(g_n(u(x))).
\]
These are continuous functions on the compact space
\(P=X\times QT_1(B)\), and
\[
 F_n(x,\tau)\downarrow\mu_{x,\tau}(\{\lambda\}).
\]
The limit is upper semicontinuous and attains its maximum
\(\alpha\) on \(P\). Hypothesis (7) therefore gives \(\alpha<c\).
Choose \(\alpha<\beta<c\).

The open subsets \(\{F_n<\beta\}\) increase and cover \(P\).
Compactness gives an \(n\) with \(F_n<\beta\) throughout \(P\).
Since \(F_n\) attains its maximum, \(\sup_PF_n<\beta\).
Choose a small arc \(J\) around \(\lambda\) on which \(g_n=1\).
Then
\[
 \mu_{x,\tau}(J)\le F_n(x,\tau),
\]
proving (8). The support functions and the final arc can be
chosen away from \(1\) when \(\lambda\ne1\). \(\square\)

This argument does not require continuity of the atom-mass
function, or an unproved uniform atomlessness assumption.
Compactness and the strict pointwise inequality supply the
uniform margin.

The quasitrace inputs are published prior art:
[Blackadar--Handelman, *Dimension functions and traces on
C*-algebras*](https://ivv5hpp.uni-muenster.de/u/cuntz/Blackadar_and_Handelman-1.pdf),
Corollary II.2.5(ii)--(iii), printed p. 324, and the compactness
statement immediately before Theorem II.4.4, printed p. 326.
Their exact use and a direct compactness verification are recorded in
[the reviewed reservoir proof, Section 5](stw58-soft-support-orthogonal-reservoir-contraction-2026-09-05.md).

## 3. A degree-one function localized in one arc

**Lemma 3.** If \(J\subset\mathbb T\) is a proper open arc whose
closure does not contain \(1\), there is a continuous
\(f:\mathbb T\to\mathbb T\) of degree one which equals one off \(J\).
Moreover \(z/f(z)\) has a continuous real logarithm \(L\) with \(L(1)=0\).

**Proof.** Choose a smaller closed arc inside \(J\), and a positively
oriented angular coordinate \(\theta\in[a,b]\) on that arc. Choose
a continuous increasing function \(q:[a,b]\to[0,1]\) with
\(q(a)=0\) and \(q(b)=1\). Define \(f=e^{2\pi iq(\theta)}\) on
the arc and \(f=1\) outside it. The endpoint values agree, so
\(f\) is continuous. It winds once positively around the circle,
hence has degree one, and \(f(1)=1\).

The map \(r(z)=z/f(z)\) has degree zero and \(r(1)=1\).
The elementary lifting criterion for the covering
\(\mathbb R\to\mathbb T\), \(t\mapsto e^{it}\), gives a
continuous \(L:\mathbb T\to\mathbb R\) with \(e^{iL}=r\).
Choose the lift with \(L(1)=0\). \(\square\)

All functions here are chosen on the scalar circle before they are
applied to the family. No continuous selection of eigenvalues,
moving branch cuts, or logarithm of \(u\) is needed.

## 4. Proof of Theorem 1 and the spectral obstruction

Apply Lemma 2 with \(c=1/(d+1)\), obtaining a fixed arc \(J\)
with uniform mass strictly less than that number. Apply Lemma 3.
Functional calculus gives (3) and (5), and the basepoint conditions
give a based homotopy whenever the original family is based.

The positive displacement of \(f(u(x))\) is nonzero only on
the spectral set where \(f\ne1\). Therefore
\[
 d_\tau(|f(u(x))-1|)
   =\mu_{x,\tau}(\{z:f(z)\ne1\})
   \le\mu_{x,\tau}(J).
 \tag{9}
\]
This proves (4).

If the original unitary has zero stable class, so does \(f(u)\).
The reviewed
[uniform small-support contraction theorem](stw58-small-quasitrace-support-kernel-contraction-2026-09-05.md)
contracts \(f(u)\), and (5) contracts \(u\).

For the assertion about \(I_k(B)\), let \(\xi=[u]=[f(u)]\).
Choose a representative \(v\) of the canonical lift
\(s_B(\sigma_B(\xi))\) so small that
\[
 \delta_B(v)+\delta_B(f(u))<1/(k+1).
\]
The two representatives have the same stable class. Lemma 3 of
the canonical splitting proof makes them based homotopic.
Thus \(\xi\in I_k(B)\). \(\square\)

**Corollary 4 (necessary heavy atoms).** If a based family
\(u:S^k\to U(B)\) represents a nonzero element of
\(\ker(\pi_kU(B)\to K_{k+1}(B))\), then
\[
 \text{for every }\lambda\in\mathbb T\setminus\{1\}
 \text{ there are }x\in S^k,\ \tau\in QT_1(B)
 \text{ with }
 \mu_{x,\tau}(\{\lambda\})\ge\frac1{k+1}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The same necessary condition holds for any class outside \(I_k(B)\),
whether or not its stable class is zero.

The parameter \(x\) and quasitrace \(\tau\) may depend on
\(\lambda\). This is not an assertion that a single measure has
an atom at every point. At \(1\), the based parameter already has
a unit atom, so excluding that point is necessary.

## 5. Why this criterion does not finish global fragmentation

The fixed-point hypothesis cannot be inferred merely from stable
nullness. Indeed, on \(S^k\subset\mathbb R^{k+1}\), with the north
pole as basepoint, set
\[
 \theta(x)=\pi(1-x_{k+1}),\qquad
 u(x)=e^{i\theta(x)}1_B.
 \tag{11}
\]
This is a based, surjective scalar-valued family and has the
explicit based contraction \(e^{i(1-t)\theta(x)}1_B\).
Nevertheless, for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb T\), some \(x\) satisfies
\(u(x)=\lambda1_B\), so
\[
 \mu_{x,\tau}(\{\lambda\})=1
 \quad\text{for every }\tau\in QT_1(B).
\]
Thus even a contractible family may fail (2) at every spectral point.

This example also obstructs **direct** fragmentation by finitely
many scalar functional-calculus factors of the original family.
If \(f:\mathbb T\to\mathbb T\) is not identically one, choose
\(\lambda\) with \(f(\lambda)\ne1\). Surjectivity of (11) gives an
\(x\) where \(f(u(x))-1=(f(\lambda)-1)1_B\), and hence
\[
 \delta_B(f(u))=1.
\]
Therefore no exact factorization
\[
 u=\prod_{j=1}^m f_j(u)
\]
can have every factor of uniform displacement dimension less than
one: all such factors would be identically one. This is an
obstruction to that particular scalar functional-calculus method,
not to factorization after an arbitrary based homotopy; the family
(11) itself contracts.

The new positive result is a genuine global one-factor localization
when a single spectral point satisfies the atom bound. The remaining
task is to alter or fragment an arbitrary family in a way that handles
the heavy-atom configurations in (10). Pointwise functional calculus
and norm-local factorization do not by themselves provide that step.
