# Surjunctivity of the quadratic family x+(1+R_t)R_s(xR_t x)

Date: 2026-09-08. For every group G and every s,t in G, the Boolean
cellular automaton

    F(x)=x+(1+R_t)R_s(xR_t x)

is surjective whenever it is injective. The complete proof appears
in the final section. It combines the spatial bipartite elimination
theorem with two exact collisions and an abelian reduction. This
closes the displayed construction family, not Gottschalk's conjecture.
All checks are symbolic; no computation was used.

The Tarski-group instance below is a control: all the listed elementary
tests leave it undecided, but an explicit nonlinear collision excludes
it. No assertion about the surjunctivity of that group is made or
inferred from the absence of a proof here.

## Literature scope and the rule

Olshanskii constructed, for every sufficiently large prime p, an
infinite group whose nontrivial proper subgroups all have order p.
The original paper is A. Yu. Olshanskii, *Groups of bounded period with
subgroups of prime order*, Algebra i Logika 21:5 (1982), 553-618.
The [original journal scan, first-page theorem](https://alglog.org/21-30/21/21N5-5.pdf)
and [Math-Net abstract](https://www.mathnet.ru/al1786) state this
existence theorem. We use a group G with precisely this subgroup
property and a sufficiently large odd prime p; we make no claim of
existence at every odd prime. Every nonidentity element of G has order p.

Choose noncommuting r,s in G and put t=r s^-1. Such a pair exists:
an abelian group of exponent p with the stated subgroup property could
not be infinite. Noncommuting elements generate G, since every proper
nontrivial subgroup is cyclic. In particular t is nonidentity and has
order p. With R_u x(g)=x(gu), consider the Boolean rule

    F(x)=x+(R_r x)(R_(rt) x)+(R_s x)(R_(st) x).          (1)

Here rt=r^2 s^-1 and st=s r s^-1, and the memory group is G.
All algebra below is over F_2 unless a coloring is being discussed.
The controls invoked here are proved in the adjacent
[quadratic elimination artifact](gottschalk-quadratic-block-elimination-2026-09-08.md)
and [off-guard elimination artifact](gottschalk-off-guard-quadratic-elimination-2026-09-08.md).

## The four possible guards all generate G

First observe that N_G(<r>)=<r>. Otherwise the normalizer, containing
the proper subgroup <r> strictly, would be G. Conjugation on <r> would
then be trivial: G has exponent p, whereas Aut(C_p) has order p-1.
Thus r would be central. Together with any element outside <r> it
would generate a finite abelian group of order p^2, contradicting
the subgroup property. This proves the normalizer assertion.

The four subgroups generated by one operand from each quadratic pair
are as follows.

1. <r,s>=G, by the choice of r,s.
2. <r,st>=<r,s r s^-1>=G. If it were proper, the conjugate
   s<r>s^-1 would equal <r>, so s would be in its normalizer <r>.
3. <rt,s>=G. It contains (rt)s=r^2, hence r, since p is odd.
4. <rt,st>=G. It contains (rt)(st)^-1=r s^-1=t, hence
   r=(rt)t^-1 and s=(st)t^-1.

Consequently every subgroup containing at least one address from each
quadratic pair is G. Neither a proper guard nor the exact-one-guard
criterion excludes (1). If G is known to be surjunctive by some other
argument, the full guard transfer theorem still excludes an injective
nonsurjective rule; the generation checks say nothing about that issue.

All four operand addresses are nonidentity and distinct. Each address
belongs to one of the four displayed pairs generating the noncyclic
group G, so none can be identity, and addresses from different
quadratic pairs cannot coincide. Within either quadratic pair,
equality would give t=1. Thus 1,r,rt,s,st are five distinct addresses.

## All six specified subgroup-mask controls pass

For a general tuple (a,b,c,d), the two zero-mask necessary conditions
are

    a^-1 b in <c,d>,       c^-1 d in <a,b>.

For (a,b,c,d)=(r,rt,s,st), both ratios equal t, and

    <r,rt>=<r,t>=G,       <s,st>=<s,t>=G.

Thus both conditions hold. These tests detect collisions of the
specified form F(1_H)=F(0), as detailed in the cited artifact.

The other four tests exclude complement masks F(1+1_H)=F(1).
Their minimal subgroups, with the required included address, are

    <a,b^-1 c,b^-1 d> must contain b;
    <b,a^-1 c,a^-1 d> must contain a;
    <c,d^-1 a,d^-1 b> must contain d;
    <d,c^-1 a,c^-1 b> must contain c.

In this common-factor family each subgroup is G. Explicitly, their
last two generators have the form u,ut, so they recover t. Their
first generators are respectively r,rt,s,st. Recovering r from rt or
s from st when needed, the generator u then recovers the other actor:

    u=(rt)^-1 s  gives s=rtu;
    u=r^-1 s     gives s=ru;
    u=(st)^-1 r  gives r=stu;
    u=s^-1 r     gives r=su.

Hence all four inclusions hold. In fact this six-test calculation
works for every common-factor tuple, with its own memory group
<r,s,t>; the Tarski assumptions are not needed for it. The conclusion
concerns only the two specified subgroup-mask input forms, not all
coset masks and certainly not all collisions.

## Both spatial coloring controls fail

The operand graph has edges {gr,grt} and {gs,gst}. Its edges are
exactly {h,ht}, so each component is a p-cycle. Since p is odd, this
graph is not bipartite.

The target-triangle graph adds all three edges on each of

    {g,gr,grt},       {g,gs,gst}.

It has no proper coloring with three colors. Indeed, the triangles
based at g r^-1 and g s^-1 contain respectively

    {g r^-1,g,gt},       {g s^-1,g,gt}.

Their common edge {g,gt} uses two distinct colors. Both third vertices
must therefore have the same remaining color:

    c(g r^-1)=c(g s^-1).

Replace g by gr to obtain c(g)=c(g r s^-1)=c(gt), contradicting the
common edge. This is a direct obstruction for arbitrary spatial
colorings; it does not assume that a coloring comes from a quotient
or from a group homomorphism.

## Positive relations and the finite-2-quotient control

The positive monoid generated by the operand addresses contains a
nonempty word equal to identity, namely r^p. Thus the acyclic
dependency surjectivity criterion does not apply.

Every homomorphism from G to a finite 2-group is trivial: the image of
each element has order dividing both p and a power of 2. Consequently

    alpha=r+rt+s+st

maps to 1+1+1+1=0 in the F_2 group algebra of every such quotient.
Thus the finite-2-quotient complement-collision test gives no collision.

## A preserved parity and an exact collision

Since r=ts, rule (1) can also be written

    F(x)=x+(1+R_t) R_s (x R_t x).                       (2)

Let e_t=1+R_t+...+R_(t^(p-1)). Because p is odd, e_t^2=e_t,
and e_t(1+R_t)=0. Therefore

    e_t F(x)=e_t x.

This certificate records the parity on each finite right t-orbit.
It splits the collision problem into parity fibers, but does not by
itself show that the induced maps are injective or onto.
On right-<t>-invariant inputs x, it gives e_t F(x)=x, so the restriction
of F to that subset is automatically injective. That observation
does not establish injectivity on a whole parity fiber.

In fact the zero-parity fiber has the following collision. Put
H=<s>, q=1_H, T=R_t, U=R_s, and Q(x)=xTx. Then Uq=q.
The subgroups <s> and <t> are distinct: otherwise r=ts would commute
with s. Their intersection is trivial, since both have prime order.
Consequently the supports of T^i q, for i modulo p, are pairwise
disjoint right H-cosets H t^-i. Since p is sufficiently large, p>=5.

Define

    x=q+T^-1 q,       y=T^-1 q+Tq.

Each has support of size 2p, and x+y=q+Tq is nonzero. Multiplying
their t-shifts gives

    Q(x)=(q+T^-1 q)(Tq+q)=q;
    Q(y)=(T^-1 q+Tq)(q+T^2 q)=0.

The first equality uses q^2=q and disjointness of the other factors.
The second uses disjointness of the levels -1,0,1,2 modulo p. Hence

    F(x)=x+(1+T)Uq=x+q+Tq=y,
    F(y)=y+(1+T)U0=y.

Thus x and y are distinct inputs with the same output. Both have
e_t parity zero, and y is a fixed point. This excludes (1), despite
its passing all six specified subgroup-mask controls, both coloring
controls failing, and all four guards being the whole group.

The collision has a useful general scope. In any group, for the rule

    F=x+(1+R_t)R_s(xR_t x),

it is enough that t,t^2,t^3 all lie outside <s>. The same q,x,y work:
those three exclusions give precisely the pairwise disjointness of
the translates with indices -1,0,1,2 used above. Neither finiteness
of <s> nor finite order of t is needed for this broader collision.
In the Tarski example, the collision is finitely supported because
<s> has order p.

## Complete closure of the family

Let G now be any group, with arbitrary s,t, and write

    T=R_t, U=R_s, D=1+T, Q(x)=xTx, r=ts, M=<s,t>.

Then F=x+DUQ(x). The memory lies in M, so F acts independently on
the cosets gM. It suffices to prove the assertion on M; injectivity
on G implies injectivity of this restriction, by setting the other
cosets to zero.

If t=1, then D=0 and F is identity. If t has infinite order or finite
even order, the operand graph consists of edges {h,ht} and is
bipartite: alternate colors on each right t-orbit. The spatial
bipartite quadratic theorem in the cited elimination artifact gives
injectivity implies surjectivity. This argument allows coincident
addresses in the expanded rule; canceling terms does not create an
additional edge.

It remains to consider t of finite odd order n. If t is in <s>, the
memory group is cyclic. The same conclusion follows if t^2 is in
<s>, since t=(t^2)^((n+1)/2). We may therefore suppose both t and
t^2 lie outside <s>. If t^3 also lies outside <s>, the exact collision
above proves noninjectivity.

Finally suppose t^3 is in <s>. Set N=<t^3>. This subgroup is central
in M: it commutes with t, and it lies in the cyclic subgroup <s>,
so it commutes with s. Put K=<r,N>. If t lies in K, then

    M=<r,t>=K=<r,N>

is abelian, because N is central. If t lies outside K, its coset
period modulo K is exactly three: t^3 is in K, whereas t and t^2
are not. The latter follows since membership of both t^2 and t^3
would imply membership of t. Put q=1_K. The configurations q,Tq,
T^-1q have pairwise disjoint supports, T^3q=q, and

    TUq=R_r q=q,       hence Uq=T^-1q.

For the nonzero configuration z=q+T^-1q, disjointness gives

    Q(z)=(q+T^-1q)(Tq+q)=q,
    F(z)=z+(1+T)Uq=q+T^-1q+(1+T)T^-1q=0.

Thus this last case either reduces to an abelian memory group or
has an explicit zero collision. Equivalently, one may quotient by
the central subgroup N: the image of t has order dividing three,
and the same collision on that quotient lifts to M.

For completeness, the abelian alternative is sufficient without
any assertion about general groups. The group M is finitely generated
abelian and therefore residually finite. Configurations factoring
through finite quotients of M are dense in {0,1}^M. An injective CA
restricts to a permutation on each such finite set of configurations,
so its image contains this dense set. Compactness makes the full
image closed, proving surjectivity. This also covers the cyclic cases.

These alternatives exhaust all orders of t and prove the stated
theorem. They also give the stronger necessary condition that, when
t has finite odd order, injectivity of F forces M=<s,t> to be abelian.
No converse claim of injectivity is needed or asserted.
