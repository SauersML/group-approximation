## Verbatim statements

Abstract: "We construct an MF C*-algebra A such that the spatial tensor
product A ⊗ R of A and the hyperfinite II1 factor R contains a proper
isometry. Consequently, stable finiteness of C*-algebras is not stable
under tensor product and the hyperfinite II1 factor is not a
quasidiagonal C*-algebra. The C*-algebra A is non-simple and has only
non-faithful tracial states. This result was obtained using OpenAI's
Chat GPT Pro 6.0."

Theorem (p. 1, unnumbered): "There is a unital separable MF C*-algebra A
such that the spatial tensor product A ⊗ R of A and the hyperfinite II1
factor R is infinite. Consequently, stable finiteness of C*-algebras is
not stable under tensor product and the hyperfinite II1 factor is not a
quasidiagonal C*-algebra."

Definitions (p. 1):
- Quasidiagonality follows Exercise 7.1.3 of Brown--Ozawa.
- For a non-separable algebra: "A not necessarily separable C*-algebra,
  such as the hyperfinite II1 factor R, is quasidiagonal if every
  separable C*-subalgebra of it is quasidiagonal."
- MF follows Definition 3.2.1 of Blackadar--Kirchberg.

Scope (p. 1): "The problem whether stable finiteness of simple
C*-algebras is stable under the spatial tensor product has attracted
considerable attention (see e.g., [BK, GS, MR, STW]) in connection with
Kaplansky's problem on AW* factors. Here we answer in the negative the
less interesting analogue of this problem for a non-simple C*-algebra
and the problem in Section 6.6 in [Br] (see also Problem 10.4.9 in [BO]
and Problem X in [STW]). In fact our example has only non-faithful
tracial states, and thus does not resolve Kaplansky's problem."

Acknowledgment (p. 4): "This research was carried out with substantial
assistance from Chat GPT Pro 6.0 through interactive communication. Most
of proofs were provided in the end by Chat GPT Pro 6.0."

## Proof outline (pp. 1--4)

1. `G = SL(3,Z)`, with `π_k` the irreducible representation on
   `ℓ2(P²(F_p)) ∩ {1}^⊥` for the k-th prime p, of dimension `p² + p`.
   The proof uses only that G has property (T), that the `π_k` are
   irreducible, and that `d_k ↗ ∞` with `sup d_k/d_{k-1} < ∞`
   (Bertrand's postulate).
2. A unital normal embedding of `∏_k B(H̄_k)` into R, with minimal
   projections of trace `c^{-k}`.
3. `L_n = ⊕_{k≤n} ℓ2(c^k) ⊗ H_k`, `M = ∏_n B(L_n)`, `K = ⊕_n B(L_n)`.
   The Kazhdan element `(1/|E|) Σ σ(g) ⊗ π̄(g)` has 1 as an isolated
   spectral point (Brown--Ozawa Lemma 12.1.8), which gives a projection
   `q ∈ M ⊗ R`.
4. `q_b = (q_{n,0})_n` and `q_t = (q_{n,n})_n`, with
   `(Q ⊗ id)(q_b) ≠ 0` and `(Q ⊗ id)(q_t) = 0` (Brown--Ozawa Lemma 4.1.8).
5. `w = Σ_{i≤c} a_i ⊗ b_i`, where the `a_i` are forward isometric shifts
   and the `b_i` are partial isometries in R. Then `qw` is a partial
   isometry with `(qw)*(qw) = q − q_t` and `(qw)(qw)* = q − q_b`, so
   `1 − q_t ~ 1 − q_b` in `M ⊗ R` via `(1 − q) + qw`.
6. `A = B/K`, where B is generated by `σ(G)`, the `a_i`, and K. Then
   non-quasidiagonality of R "follows from Lemma 4 in [GS]" (Goldbring--
   Sinclair, Internat. J. Math. 28 (2017) 1750008).
7. Remark (p. 4): a similar, simpler proof shows that `A ⊗ Ā` is
   infinite for an MF algebra A and its opposite; "See Remark 3.7 in
   [RS]" (Rainone--Schafhauser, Adv. Math. 347 (2019)).
