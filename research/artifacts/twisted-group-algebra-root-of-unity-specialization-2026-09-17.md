# Twisted group algebra failures specialize to group rings of central extensions over finite fields

Worker `swarm-0917-w8-w8-gs-pull`, 2026-09-17 (finite-models lane, obstruction-miner).

## 0. Statement

Let `G` be any group, `K` any commutative field, and `α: G × G -> K^x` any 2-cocycle for the trivial action.
`K^α[G]` has `K`-basis `u_g` with `u_g u_h = α(g,h) u_{gh}`.

**Theorem.** Suppose `M_n(K^α[G])` is not directly finite for some `n`: `AB = I_n != BA`. Then there are:
- a prime `p` (`p = char K` if that is positive; all but finitely many `p` work when `char K = 0`),
- a finite field `F_q` of characteristic `p`,
- a finitely generated group `E_0` that is a central extension `1 -> Z -> E_0 -> G_0 -> 1` of a finitely
  generated subgroup `G_0 <= G` by a locally cyclic torsion group `Z` of order prime to `p`,

such that `M_n(F_q[E_0])` is not directly finite.

**Rank failures are included.** If `A (d x r)`, `B (r x d)`, `AB = I_d`, `r < d`, pad with zeros:
`A' = [A | 0]`, `B' = [B ; 0]` (`d x d`). Then `A'B' = I_d` and the lower-right `(d-r) x (d-r)` block of
`B'A'` is `0`, so `B'A' != I_d`.

**Corollary.** Under the hypothesis:
1. `E_0` is not surjunctive (`stable-finiteness-failure-refutes-surjunctivity`);
2. `RBS(E_0)` and `INF(E_0)` fail (contrapositive of `INF ⇒ RBS ⇒ GOT`, Seward import);
3. `∀G POS` fails, since `(∀G POS) ⇒ (∀G INF)` (Seward import, Theorem 1.11). So the root
   `every-group-has-positive-rokhlin-entropy-action` is refuted, together with
   `gottschalk-surjunctivity-conjecture` and Kaplansky stable finiteness over finite fields.

## 1. Standard facts used

- **(N) Nullstellensatz for finitely generated rings.** If `S` is a finitely generated commutative ring (a
  `Z`-algebra) and `m` a maximal ideal, then `S/m` is a finite field (Atiyah–Macdonald, Ex. 5.25 and 7.6;
  equivalently: a finitely generated `Z`-algebra that is a field is finite). If `S ⊆ K` is a domain and
  `c ∈ S` is nonzero, `S[c^{-1}]` is a nonzero finitely generated ring, so it has a maximal ideal, and `c` maps to
  a unit. If `char K = 0`, `S ⊗ Q ≠ 0` and `S[1/N]` is nonzero for every `N`, so maximal ideals of residue
  characteristic `p` exist for all `p` outside a finite set (generic freeness over `Z`); only existence of one
  `p` is needed below.
- **(D) Divisible groups are injective** (Baer). `F̄_p^x` is divisible, so every homomorphism from a subgroup
  `Λ_0 <= Λ` of an abelian group into `F̄_p^x` extends to `Λ`.
- **(C) Normalization.** Every 2-cocycle is cohomologous to a normalized one (`α(1,g) = α(g,1) = 1`), and
  cohomologous cocycles give isomorphic twisted algebras by a rescaling `u_g -> c_g u_g`. That map sends
  matrices to matrices entrywise, so direct finiteness is unchanged. Assume `α` normalized, so `u_1 = 1`.

## 2. Step 1: specialize the cocycle to roots of unity

Write the entries as `A_ij = Σ_g a_ij(g) u_g`, `B_ij = Σ_h b_ij(h) u_h` (finite sums). Let `T_A`, `T_B` be the
unions of the supports and `P = (T_A × T_B) ∪ (T_B × T_A)`. The identities hold in `K`:

    (i)   Σ_l Σ_{gh = k} a_il(g) b_lj(h) α(g,h) = δ_ij δ_{k,1}          for all i, j, k,
    (ii)  c := Σ_l Σ_{hg = k0} b_{i0 l}(h) a_{l j0}(g) α(h,g) - δ_{i0 j0} δ_{k0,1} ≠ 0   for one fixed i0, j0, k0.

Let `S ⊆ K` be the subring generated by all `a_ij(g)`, `b_ij(h)`, the values `α(x,y)` and `α(x,y)^{-1}` for
`(x,y) ∈ P`, and `c^{-1}`. It is finitely generated. By (N), pick `ρ: S -> F`, a ring map onto a finite field
`F` of characteristic `p`. Then `ρ(c) ≠ 0`.

Let `Λ <= K^x` be the subgroup generated by all values of `α`, and `Λ_0 <= Λ` the subgroup generated by the
finitely many `α(x,y)`, `(x,y) ∈ P`. `Λ_0 ⊆ S^x`, so `ρ|Λ_0 : Λ_0 -> F^x ⊆ F̄_p^x` is a homomorphism. By (D),
extend it to `χ: Λ -> F̄_p^x`. Put

    β = χ ∘ α : G × G -> F̄_p^x,      M = χ(Λ) <= F̄_p^x.

- `β` is a normalized 2-cocycle: the cocycle identity `α(g,h)α(gh,k) = α(h,k)α(g,hk)` is an identity in the
  abelian group `Λ`, and `χ` is a homomorphism.
- `M` is a subgroup of `F̄_p^x`: it is torsion, of order prime to `p` elementwise, and locally cyclic (every
  finite subgroup of `F̄_p^x` is cyclic).
- `β(x,y) = ρ(α(x,y))` for `(x,y) ∈ P`.

In `F̄_p^β[G]` (basis `v_g`), put `Ã_ij = Σ ρ(a_ij(g)) v_g` and `B̃_ij = Σ ρ(b_ij(h)) v_h`. The coefficient of
`v_k` in `(ÃB̃)_ij` is `Σ ρ(a_il(g)) ρ(b_lj(h)) β(g,h) = ρ(left side of (i)) = δ_ij δ_{k,1}`, because every pair
occurring is in `P`. So `ÃB̃ = I_n`. In the same way the `(i0,j0)` entry of `B̃Ã - I_n` has coefficient `ρ(c) ≠ 0`
at `v_{k0}`. So `M_n(F̄_p^β[G])` is not directly finite.

**Remark.** This step alone is the new point. The original cocycle may be non-torsion, take transcendental
values, and live in characteristic 0. After Step 1 it takes values in roots of unity of order prime to `p`.
Nothing about `G` is used. The step works because the action is trivial: `ρ` does not need to be equivariant.

## 3. Step 2: untwist by the central extension

Let `E = M ×_β G`, with product `(m,g)(m',h) = (m m' β(g,h), gh)`. It is a group, `M × {1}` is central, and
`E / M = G`. The map `q: F̄_p[E] -> F̄_p^β[G]`, `[(m,g)] -> m v_g`, is a surjective ring map:
`q([(m,g)][(m',h)]) = m m' β(g,h) v_{gh} = (m v_g)(m' v_h)`.

Lift: `Â_ij = Σ ρ(a_ij(g)) [(1,g)]` and `B̂_ij` likewise. Let `M_1 <= M` be the subgroup generated by the
`β(x,y)`, `(x,y) ∈ P`. It is finitely generated torsion abelian, so finite, cyclic, of order `N` prime to `p`.
Put

    e = N^{-1} Σ_{m ∈ M_1} m^{-1} [(m,1)]  ∈ F̄_p[M_1] ⊆ F̄_p[E].

- `e` is central (since `M_1` is central) and `e [(m,1)] = m e` for `m ∈ M_1` (reindex the sum). Hence `e^2 = e`
  and `q(e) = N^{-1} Σ m^{-1} m = 1`, so `e ≠ 0`.
- Since `β` is normalized, `[(1,g)][(1,h)] = [(β(g,h),1)] [(1,gh)]`. So
  `(ÂB̂ - I_n)_ij = Σ_k (γ_k - q(γ_k)) [(1,k)]`, where `γ_k = Σ ρ(a_il(g)) ρ(b_lj(h)) [(β(g,h),1)] ∈ F̄_p[M_1]` and
  `q(γ_k) = δ_ij δ_{k,1}` by Step 1. From `e [(m,1)] = m e` it follows that `e γ = q(γ) e` for `γ ∈ F̄_p[M_1]`.
  Hence `e(ÂB̂ - I_n) = 0`.
- `q(e(B̂Â - I_n)) = B̃Ã - I_n ≠ 0`, so `e(B̂Â - I_n) ≠ 0`.

Put `A* = eÂ + (1-e) I_n` and `B* = eB̂ + (1-e) I_n`. Since `e` is a central idempotent,

    A* B* = e ÂB̂ + (1-e) I_n = I_n,      B* A* - I_n = e(B̂Â - I_n) ≠ 0.

## 4. Step 3: finite field and finitely generated support

The entries of `A*`, `B*` have finitely many coefficients, in `F(M_1) ⊆ F̄_p`, a finite field `F_q`. Their
supports lie in the subgroup `E_0` generated by `M_1 × {1}` and the `(1,g)`, `g ∈ T_A ∪ T_B`. Both identities
hold in `M_n(F_q[E_0])`, which is a subring of `M_n(F̄_p[E])` with the same unit.

`E_0` is finitely generated. `E_0 ∩ M =: Z` is central in `E_0`, a subgroup of `M`, hence locally cyclic torsion
of order prime to `p`. `E_0 / Z` is the subgroup `G_0 <= G` generated by `T_A ∪ T_B`. This proves the Theorem.
(`Z` need not be finite: kernels of finitely generated groups need not be finitely generated.)

`E_0` is infinite: a finite group algebra over a field is finite dimensional, hence stably finite.

## 5. Corollary, and what this does to the need

- `stable-finiteness-failure-refutes-surjunctivity` applies verbatim over `F_q` and `E_0` (swap the names `A`,
  `B`). So `E_0` is not surjunctive, and neither is any group containing it, for example `E`.
- The Seward import gives `INF(E_0) ⇒ RBS(E_0) ⇒ GOT(E_0)`, so `INF(E_0)` fails. By `(∀G POS) ⇒ (∀G INF)`, the
  root `every-group-has-positive-rokhlin-entropy-action` fails as well.

**Class killed.** Twisted group algebras (trivial action, any field, any cocycle) as witnesses for
`some-field-crossed-product-fails-the-rank-condition`, used as a *barrier* to Bernoulli maximality.
- Such a witness is never merely a barrier, because it already refutes the flagship, Gottschalk and Kaplansky
  stable finiteness over finite fields.
- **Invariant:** the image of the cocycle under a character `χ: Λ -> F̄_p^x` extending a residue map.
- **Dying step:** Step 2, where a root-of-unity cocycle becomes a corner `eF̄_p[E]` of a group algebra.
- Finding such a witness is exactly as hard as finding a finite-field group algebra (of a central extension)
  that is not stably finite. The converse inclusion is trivial, taking `α = 1`.
- The ternary route (`S_- = F_3[L^x]/(1+[z])`, `M = {±1}`, `E` the Leavitt unit group) and the Deligne route
  (`M = μ_3`) are the special cases where `α` is already torsion. The graph records them separately
  (`some-field-crossed-product-fails-rank-from-ternary-cohn-family`,
  `deligne-cover-nonsurjunctive-from-twisted-algebra-failure`). The new input is Step 1, which removes the
  torsion, finite-field and characteristic hypotheses.

**Consequences for witness constraints.**
- Over a sofic `G`, a twisted witness would produce a non-surjunctive, hence nonsofic, central extension of a
  sofic group by a locally cyclic torsion group. Whether all such extensions are sofic is open: the Deligne
  cover is the standard test case.
- Char 0 gives nothing extra. Kaplansky's char-0 theorem does not transfer, because Step 1 lands in
  characteristic `p`.

## 6. Where it stops

- **Nontrivial action `σ`.** Step 1 needs `ρ` to be `G`-equivariant on the coefficient ring. That is a
  finite-field point model of the `G`-ring, which is the (P2) problem of
  `finite-field-point-models-give-the-rank-condition`. For finitely generated orbit fields, Lang–Weil kills the
  untwisted case. For Bernoulli fields, `bernoulli-field-rank-failure-gives-rokhlin-deficit` gets `¬INF(G)` with
  `α = 1`.
- **What remains open for an informative barrier:**
  - `σ` nontrivial with coefficient orbits in no finitely generated stable subfield, and not Bernoulli;
  - `σ` Bernoulli with a nontrivial cocycle;
  - `σ` nontrivial with a cocycle that is not a coboundary after any point specialization.
- **Mixed case: trivial action on a subfield carrying the cocycle.** If `σ` acts trivially on a subfield `K_1`
  containing all values of `α`, Step 1 applies to `α` alone, but the coefficients still need an equivariant
  model. One could run Step 1 on `α` and then the Bernoulli point model on `F_q[E]`-valued coefficients. Whether
  the point model stays multiplicative after the lift through `E` is not checked here.
