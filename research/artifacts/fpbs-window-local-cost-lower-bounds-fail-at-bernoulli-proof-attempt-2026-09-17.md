# Pull back the Bernoulli shift of an amenable quotient that is injective on a large ball

Full written proof.

**Construction.** By residual finiteness there is a finite quotient `q: G -> Q` that is
injective on `B_R`. Let `psi = (q, p): G -> Q × A` and `H = psi(G)`. `H` is amenable, as a
subgroup of `Q × A`, and infinite, since it surjects onto `A`. Let `beta = G ↷ (L^H, lambda^H)`
be the Bernoulli shift of `H`, with `G` acting through `psi`. Define
`T: L^H -> L^G`, `T(omega) = omega ∘ psi`, and `nu_R = T_* lambda^H`.

*Equivariance.* `(T(psi(g).omega))(h) = omega(psi(g)^{-1} psi(h)) = omega(psi(g^{-1}h)) = (g.T(omega))(h)`.
So `T` is `G`-equivariant and `nu_R` is `G`-invariant. `T` is injective, since `psi` is onto `H`.
So `T` is a Borel isomorphism of `beta` onto its image, and `G ↷ (L^G, nu_R)` is isomorphic to
`beta`.

**Item 1.** For `h ∈ B_R`, `T(omega)(h) = omega(psi(h))`. `psi` is injective on `B_R`, so these are
distinct coordinates of `omega`, and they are iid `lambda`. Every cylinder event over a finite
window lies over some `B_R`, so `nu_R -> lambda^G`.

**Item 2.** `gamma.T(omega) = T(omega)` iff `psi(gamma).omega = omega`. For `gamma ∈ B_R \ {1}`,
`q(gamma) != 1`, so `psi(gamma) != 1`. The Bernoulli shift of the infinite group `H`, with a base
measure that is not a point mass, is essentially free. So this event is null.

**Item 3.** `beta` is ergodic, since Bernoulli shifts of infinite groups are mixing, and its
`G`-invariant sets are `H`-invariant. Its orbits are `H`-orbits, which are infinite because the
`H`-action is essentially free and `H` is infinite.

**Item 4.** The orbit relation of `G ↷ (L^G, nu_R)` is isomorphic, through `T`, to the orbit
relation of the essentially free p.m.p. action of the infinite amenable group `H` on
`(L^H, lambda^H)`. By `fpbs-amen2-cost-inputs` (published amenable cost: an aperiodic relation
generated by a free action of an infinite amenable group has cost 1), its cost is `1`.

**Jump.** `lambda^G` is a nontrivial Bernoulli shift of `G`. By
`abert-weiss-free-actions-weakly-contain-bernoulli` and `cost-is-constant-on-weak-equivalence-classes`
its cost is `C(b)`. By `fpbs-amen2-betti-cost-input`, `C(b) >= 1 + beta_1^(2)(G)`. Items 1 and 4 give
`liminf_R C(nu_R) = 1 <= C(lambda^G) - beta_1^(2)(G)`.

**Class kill.** Any finite list of conditions of the listed kinds involves only finitely many
window events and finitely many group elements. For `R` large every `nu_R` satisfies all of them:
exactly iid on the window, ergodic, aperiodic, and free on the finite ball. But
`C(nu_R) = 1 < C(b) - eta`.

The example groups follow from the fact that `beta_1^(2)(F_r) = r - 1` and `beta_1^(2)` of the
closed orientable surface group of genus `g >= 2` is `2g - 2`. These are standard values (Lück,
*L2-invariants*, Chapter 1). They are not imported as nodes. They serve only as illustrations and
are not used in the general statement.
