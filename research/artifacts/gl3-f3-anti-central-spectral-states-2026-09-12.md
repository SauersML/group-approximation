# GL_3(F_3) constants, spectral weights, and what states can see beyond the signed Thompson group

Lane `w7-gl3-f3-states`, 2026-09-12. This follows `semidihedral-and-gl2-f3-blocks-forced-states-2026-09-12.md`
(lane w6-wf-stage1-b, the "GL_2 artifact") and `hadamard-spectral-conjugacy-and-forced-states-2026-09-12.md` (the
"Hadamard artifact"). All claims below are landed OPEN pending re-derivation by `w4-vf-linear-b`. Paper work only,
no computation. Whether any state exists is itself open (`anti-central-lifted-trace-is-relatively-positive`), so
statements about all states describe what an obstruction must defeat.

Summary.
- Units of `G` of finite order prime to three are conjugate iff they have the same minimal polynomial over `F_3`
  (Theorem 1.1). Such a unit is conjugate into `H = E x| V` iff its minimal polynomial is a least common multiple of
  binomials `x^m - eps` (Theorem 1.3).
- For every finite subgroup `K` containing `z`, `K_0(eps_- F_3[K]) (x) Q` is spanned by classes induced from abelian
  subgroups `<g, z>` with `g` of order prime to three (Lemma 1.2). So a state on `H_fin(G)` is one universal
  *spectral weight* `W_s`, a function of minimal polynomials (Theorem 2.1).
- Every state has `W_s = 0` at binomial classes: the lifted trace is forced on every finite subgroup whose
  three-regular elements have binomial minimal polynomials, conjugate into `H` or not (Corollary 2.2).
  At non-binomial classes, no finite-subgroup datum forces `W_s` (Proposition 2.3).
- `alpha` acts on spectral weights by inversion, and `delta(g) = s_0 g t_0 + s_1 g t_1` acts trivially (Lemma 2.4).
  The GL_2 theorem is the case where every non-binomial class `g` has `g^(-1) ~ z g`.
- `GL_3(F_3)`: `eps_- F_3[GL_3(F_3)] ~= F_3[SL_3(F_3)]`, nine anti-central three-regular classes. Three of them are
  binomial and forced. Six are free, and three real parameters survive `alpha`: one at `B (+) 1`, with
  `B` of order eight, and two at the Singer cycles (Theorem 3.2). So the averaging proof of Theorem 1.2(5) of the
  GL_2 artifact does not extend to `GL_3`, or to any `GL_n` with `n >= 3` (Corollary 3.3).
- No trace-lowering relation comes from `GL_3(F_3)` alone. Within a finite group algebra `trl` is a dimension count,
  so it is positive. A relation needs elements outside finite subgroups. Existence of a state still depends only on
  `H_fin(H)` (Theorem 2.2 of `lifted-trace-state-extension-2026-09-12.md`). Some state is `trl` on
  `H_fin(GL_3(F_3))` iff `trl` extends positively over `H` and `GL_3(F_3)` (Section 3.4). OPEN.
- `t = s(c_1) = 1/2 + (i sqrt 2 / 2) W_s(hw)`, with `W_s(hw)` purely imaginary. The values of `t` form an interval
  symmetric about `1/2`. `GL_3(F_3)` has no element with minimal polynomial `x^2 +- x - 1`, so its restrictions cannot
  force `t`, and neither can `GL_6(F_3)` constants (Proposition 4.1). `t = 1/2` is OPEN.

## 0. Notation

`R = L_(F_3)(1,2)` with `s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1 = 1`, `t_i s_j = delta_ij`, involution `s_i^* = t_i`. `G = R^x`, `z = -1`,
`eps_- = 2(1 - [z])`, `S_- = eps_- F_3[G]`, `u = [eps_-]`. `H = E x| V` is the signed Thompson group,
`alpha(g) = (g^*)^(-1)`. A *state* is an additive `s : K_0(S_-) -> R`, nonnegative on idempotent classes, `s(u) = 1`.
For finite `K` with `z in K` and an idempotent matrix `p` over `eps_- F_3[K]`, `trl(p) = 2 dim_(F_3)(p F_3[K]^n)/|K|`.
`H_fin(L)` is the subgroup of `K_0(S_-)` generated by such classes over finite subgroups `K <= L`.

A unit `g` of finite order `m` is *three-regular* if `3` does not divide `m`. Its *minimal polynomial* `p_g` is the
monic generator of `{p in F_3[x] : p(g) = 0 in R}`. It divides `x^m - 1`, so it is separable. Call `p_g` *binomial*
if it is the least common multiple of finitely many `x^l - eps` with `eps in {1, -1}` and `3` not dividing `l`.

## 1. Conjugacy of three-regular units

**Theorem 1.1.** Three-regular units `g, g'` of `G` are conjugate in `G` iff `p_g = p_(g')`.

*Proof.* Conjugation preserves `p_g`. Conversely write `p = p_g = prod_f f` over distinct monic irreducibles, and let
`e_f(x) in F_3[x]/(p)` be the primitive idempotents. Then `1 = sum_f e_f(g)` in `R`, and each `e_f(g)` is nonzero:
otherwise `p/f` would kill `g`. So `R_R = (+)_f e_f(g) R` with every summand nonzero.

Fix `f`, put `F = F_3[x]/(f)` and `R_F = F (x)_(F_3) R ~= L_F(1,2)`. Left multiplication by `g` makes `M_f = e_f(g) R`
an `(F, R)`-bimodule, that is a right `R_F`-module. `M_f` is finitely generated projective over `R`, being a summand
of `R`. `F / F_3` is separable, so `R_F` is a separable extension of `R`, and an `R_F`-module that is projective over
`R` is projective over `R_F` (split `M (x)_R R_F -> M` with the separability idempotent). So `M_f` is a nonzero
finitely generated projective `R_F`-module. For every field `F`, the monoid of isomorphism classes of finitely
generated projective `L_F(1,2)`-modules is `{0, [L_F(1,2)]}` (Ara–Goodearl–Pardo: `V(L(1,n)) = {0} ∪ Z/(n-1)`).
So `M_f ~= R_F`, and likewise `M'_f = e_f(g') R ~= R_F`.

Choose `R_F`-isomorphisms `phi_f : M_f -> M'_f` and put `phi = (+)_f phi_f : R -> R`. It is an isomorphism of right
`R`-modules with `phi(g m) = g' phi(m)`. So `phi` is left multiplication by `v = phi(1)`, `v` is a unit, and
`v g = g' v`. QED

**Lemma 1.2 (reduction to abelian three-regular subgroups).** Let `K <= G` be finite with `z in K`. Then
`K_0(eps_- F_3[K]) (x) Q` is spanned by the classes `Ind_C^K(e)`, where `C = <g, z>` with `g in K` three-regular and
`e` a primitive idempotent of the semisimple algebra `eps_- F_3[C]`.

*Proof.* Brauer characters identify `K_0(F_3[K]) (x) C` with class functions on three-regular classes. Artin's
formula `|K| 1_K = sum_A Ind_A^K theta_A`, over cyclic `A <= K`, with `theta_A` supported on generators of `A`, gives
`|K| Phi = sum_A Ind_A^K(theta_A Res_A Phi)` for a projective character `Phi`. `Phi` vanishes on three-singular
elements, and the generators of a cyclic group of order divisible by three are three-singular. So only three-regular
`A` contribute. `theta_A` is a rational combination of permutation characters `Ind_B^A 1`, so `theta_A Res_A Phi`
lies in `K_0(F_3[A]) (x) Q`. Multiply by `eps_-` and enlarge `A` to `<A, z>`, which is abelian and three-regular. QED

**Theorem 1.3.** A three-regular unit `g` is conjugate into `H` iff `p_g` is binomial.

*Proof.* (If.) Say `p_g = lcm_i (x^(l_i) - eps_i)`. Take a complete prefix code with `sum_i l_i` leaves and, on the
`i`-th block of `l_i` leaves, the signed cyclic shift with sign product `eps_i`. This is a signed permutation of the
leaves, an element of `H`. Its minimal polynomial is `lcm_i (x^(l_i) - eps_i) = p_g`, so it is conjugate to `g` by
Theorem 1.1.

(Only if.) `R` acts on the `F_3`-space with basis the infinite words tail-equivalent to `0^infinity`:
`s_i xi = i xi`, `t_i(j xi) = delta_ij xi`. `R` is simple, so the action is faithful, and `p(a) = 0` in `R` iff
`p(a)` acts as zero. Elements of `V` permute the basis, and `tau_C = 1 - 2 1_C` multiplies it by signs. So
`a in H` of order `m` acts as a signed permutation. On the orbit of a basis vector `xi`, of size `l | m`, with
`a^l xi = eps xi`, the vectors `xi, a xi, ..., a^(l-1) xi` are independent up to sign and `a` has minimal polynomial
`x^l - eps` there. Only finitely many pairs `(l, eps)` occur, so `p_a` is their lcm. If `a` is three-regular, every
such `l` is prime to three. QED

**Remark 1.4.** This recovers Remark 2.3 of `hadamard-spectral-conjugacy-and-forced-states-2026-09-12.md`: `hw` has
`p_(hw) = x^2 + x - 1`, which is not binomial, and `-hw` has `x^2 - x - 1`, so `hw` is not conjugate to `-hw`.

## 2. Spectral weights

**Theorem 2.1.** Let `s` be a state. There is a unique complex function `W_s` on the three-regular units of `G` with:
- (a) `W_s(g)` depends only on `p_g`;
- (b) `W_s(1) = 1`, `W_s(z g) = -W_s(g)`, `W_s(g^(-1)) = conj W_s(g)`;
- (c) for every finite `K` with `z in K` and every idempotent matrix `p` over `eps_- F_3[K]`,
  `s([p]) = (1/|K|) sum_(c in K three-regular) W_s(c) Phi_p(c)`, where `Phi_p` is the Brauer character of `p F_3[K]^n`.

For `s = trl` the weight is `W_trl = delta_1 - delta_z`.

*Proof.* For finite `K`, Brauer characters of projective `eps_- F_3[K]`-modules span the class functions on
three-regular classes that are odd under `z`, and `<f, phi> = (1/|K|) sum f(c) phi(c)` is nondegenerate there. So a
unique such `W_K` represents `s` on `K`. If `C <= K`, the class of `q` over `C` equals the class of `Ind_C^K q` in
`K_0(S_-)`, and Frobenius reciprocity gives `Res_C W_K = W_C`. So `W_K(c) = W_(<c, z>)(c)`, independent of `K`.
Conjugating `C` by `v in G` does not change classes, so `W` is constant on `G`-classes, hence a function of `p_g` by
Theorem 1.1. `s` is real and `Phi_p(c^(-1)) = conj Phi_p(c)`, so `conj W(c^(-1))` also represents `s`, and uniqueness
gives (b). `s(u) = 1` on `K = <z>` gives `W_s(1) = 1`. QED

**Corollary 2.2.** `W_s(g) = 0` for every three-regular `g not in {1, z}` with binomial `p_g`. So `s = trl` on
`H_fin(K)` for every finite `K` containing `z` whose three-regular elements all have binomial minimal polynomials,
whether or not `K` is conjugate into `H`.

*Proof.* `<g, z>` is conjugate into `H` (Theorem 1.3), so `s = trl` on its idempotents (Theorem 2.1(b) of the
Hadamard artifact, node `anti-central-k0-states-equal-lifted-trace-on-signed-thompson`, ESTABLISHED). By uniqueness `W_(<g,z>) = delta_1 - delta_z`. The second
sentence follows from (c). QED

Example: `SL_2(F_3)` constants. Its three-regular elements are `1`, `z`, and the elements of order four, with
`p = x^2 + 1`. So every state has the lifted trace on all of `H_fin(SL_2(F_3))`, including its non-semisimple part.

**Proposition 2.3 (no finite-subgroup datum forces a non-binomial weight).** Let `K` be finite with `z in K`, and `U`
a function on the three-regular elements of `K`, constant on `K`-classes, odd under `z`, with
`U(c^(-1)) = conj U(c)`, and supported on elements with non-binomial minimal polynomial. Put
`s_eps(p) = trl(p) + (eps/|K|) sum_c U(c) Phi_p(c)`. For `|eps|` small, `s_eps` is a state on
`K_0(eps_- F_3[K])`, and it equals `trl` on every class induced from a subgroup `J <= K` whose three-regular elements
are binomial, in particular from every `J` conjugate into `H`.

*Proof.* Values are real by the symmetry of `U`. `trl > 0` on the finitely many projective indecomposables, so
positivity survives small `eps`. For such `J`, `Res_J U = 0`, and Frobenius reciprocity gives `s_eps = trl` on
`Ind_J^K`. `u` is induced from `<z>`. QED

So the data internal to finite subgroups (conjugation, inclusions, Theorem 2.1(b)) impose exactly: `W` is a function
of `p_g`, and `W = 0` at binomial classes. Any forcing of a non-binomial weight needs a `K_0` relation through
elements of infinite order, or the global positivity of all finite subgroups at once.

**Lemma 2.4.** For three-regular `g`, `alpha(g)` is conjugate to `g^(-1)`, and `delta(g) = s_0 g t_0 + s_1 g t_1` is
conjugate to `g`. So `W_(s o alpha_*) = W_s o inv` and `W_(s o delta_*) = W_s`. An `alpha_*`-invariant state has real
`W_s`, and `W_s(g) = 0` whenever `p_(g^(-1)) = p_(z g)`.

*Proof.* `*` is an `F_3`-linear anti-automorphism, so `p(g)^* = p(g^*)` and `p_(g^*) = p_g`. The minimal polynomial
of `g^(-1)` is the monic reciprocal of `p_g`, and it is also that of `(g^*)^(-1)`. `delta` is a unital `F_3`-algebra
endomorphism of the simple ring `R`, hence injective, so `p_(delta g) = p_g`. Apply Theorem 1.1. `alpha` maps a finite
`K` isomorphically onto `alpha(K)` with `Phi_(alpha p)(alpha c) = Phi_p(c)`, which gives the formula for `W`. If `s`
is `alpha_*`-invariant, `W_s(g) = W_s(g^(-1)) = conj W_s(g)`. If moreover `g^(-1) ~ z g`,
`W_s(g) = W_s(z g) = -W_s(g)`. QED

**Remark 2.5 (the GL_2 theorem).** In `GL_2(F_3)` the only non-binomial three-regular elements are the twelve elements
of order eight, with `p = x^2 + x - 1` or `x^2 - x - 1`. The reciprocal of `x^2 + x - 1` is `x^2 - x - 1`, which is
`p_(z g)`. So for an `alpha_*`-invariant state Lemma 2.4 forces `W_s = 0` there, and Corollary 2.2 with Theorem 2.1(c)
gives `s = trl` on `H_fin(GL_2(F_3))`. This is Theorem 1.2(5) of the GL_2 artifact by another route. The same test
decides every `GL_n`.

## 3. GL_3(F_3) constants

**3.1 Embeddings.** The code `{0, 10, 11}` gives matrix units `e_ij = sigma_i sigma_j^*` with
`sigma = (s_0, s_1 s_0, s_1 s_1)`, hence a unital `M_3(F_3) -> M_3(R) ~= R`, so `GL_3(F_3) <= G` with `-I_3 = z`.
Any two unital embeddings `M_3(F_3) -> R` are conjugate by a unit: every nonzero finitely generated projective
`R`-module is free of rank one, so `e_11 R ~= f_11 R` by some `w`, and `v = sum_i f_i1 w e_1i` is a unit with
`v e_ij v^(-1) = f_ij`. So there is one class of constant embeddings, and they all give the same map on `K_0`.
Since `e_ij^* = e_ji`, `alpha` restricts to transpose-inverse. A faithful anti-central `F_3[K]`-module `M` of
dimension `m` also embeds `K` through `M (x) R ~= R^m`. By Theorem 2.1 its effect on states depends only on the
minimal polynomials of the images. Conjugacy classes of such group embeddings were not classified, and states do not
see them.

**3.2 Blocks.** `SL_3(F_3)` has trivial centre and `det(-I_3) = -1`, so `GL_3(F_3) = SL_3(F_3) x <z>` and
`eps_- F_3[GL_3(F_3)] ~= F_3[SL_3(F_3)]`. The anti-central simples are the restricted modules `L(a,b)`,
`0 <= a,b <= 2`, twisted by `det`, of dimensions `1, 3, 3, 6, 6, 7, 15, 15, 27` (standard). `|SL_3(F_3)| = 2^4 3^3 13`.
The Steinberg module `L(2,2)` has dimension `3^3` and is a block of defect zero. The other eight simples form the
principal block. Here `trl(P) = dim P / 5616` for every projective `P`, so `trl(St) = 1/208`, and `trl > 0` on all
projective indecomposables. Their dimensions are not needed below.

**3.3 Classes.** `GL_3(F_3)` has 18 three-regular classes, paired by `g -> z g`, giving 9 anti-central directions.
In the table, `B in GL_2(F_3)` has order 8 with `p_B = x^2 + x - 1`, `J^2 = -1`, and `sigma` is a Singer element of
order 13 with eigenvalue `zeta`. The Frobenius orbits on exponents mod 13 are `A = {1,3,9}`, `B' = {2,6,5}`,
`C = {4,12,10}` and `D = {7,8,11}`, with `C = -A` and `D = -B'`.

- `r` and `diag(1,-1,-1)` have the same minimal polynomial. So `r ~ z r` in `G`, although not in `GL_3`.

**Corollary 3.3 (firewall: the GL_2 argument extends to `GL_n` iff `n <= 2`).** Call the *finite data* of `GL_n(F_3)`:
- `W` is a function of the minimal polynomial in `G`;
- `W = trl` on subgroups conjugate into `H`;
- `alpha_*`- and `delta_*`-invariance.

For `n <= 2` the finite data force `s = trl` on `H_fin(GL_n(F_3))` (Remark 2.5). For `n >= 3`, take
`U(c) = mu` when `p_c = (x - 1)(x^2 +- x - 1)`, `U(c) = -mu` when `p_c = (x + 1)(x^2 +- x - 1)`, and `U = 0`
otherwise, with `mu` real and small. Then `trl + U` satisfies all the finite data on `K_0(eps_- F_3[GL_n(F_3)])`
and gives `s(e_1) = 1/8 + mu / 2` on `<B (+) I_(n-2), z>`. So no argument from the finite data of a single `GL_n`,
`n >= 3`, forces the lifted trace there.

*Proof.* `U` is a class function, odd, real, `inv`-invariant, and supported on non-binomial polynomials. Apply
Proposition 2.3, Lemma 2.4, and part (b) of Theorem 3.2 for `n = 3`, the same computation for `n > 3`. QED

**3.4 No intrinsic trace-lowering relation.** On a finite group algebra `trl(P) = 2 dim P / |K| > 0`, so no single
finite subgroup yields a negative class. An obstruction must be a relation `[x] = [y]` in `K_0(S_-)` between `x` from
`GL_3(F_3)` and `y in H_fin(H)` with `trl(x) != trl(y)`. For any state,
`trl(x) - trl(y) = -(1/11232) sum_(c non-binomial) W_s(c) Phi_x(c)`.

So the following are equivalent:
- some state has `mu = w_A = w_B = 0`;
- `trl` is well defined and relatively positive on `H_fin(H) + H_fin(GL_3(F_3))`.

The second implies the first by the same state-extension argument as for `H_fin(H)`.

Rationally, relations in `K_0` of a group algebra over `F_3` beyond those from finite subgroups would have to come
from virtually cyclic Nil terms, and these are 3-primary torsion. So if a degree-0 Farrell–Jones isomorphism held for
`G` with `F_3` coefficients (not known), `K_0(S_-) (x) Q` would be the colimit over finite subgroups. Every admissible
`W` would then define a real functional, and the decision would be purely about the positive cone `K_0^+(S_-)`. That
cone is not generated by finite-subgroup projectives. Heuristic, held OPEN.

**Decision on GL_3.** No trace-lowering relation was found. Corollary 3.3 refutes the GL_2 method for `n >= 3`.
Whether every state, or some state, is `trl` on `H_fin(GL_3(F_3))` is **OPEN**.

## 4. The value t

Let `hw in GL_2(F_3)` be the constant of order eight with `p_(hw) = p_+ = x^2 + x - 1`, and `t = s(c_1)` with
`c_1 = k_+([hw]) eps_-` (Lemma 1.1 of the GL_2 artifact).

**Proposition 4.1.**
- (a) `W_s(hw)` is purely imaginary, and `t = 1/2 + (i sqrt 2 / 2) W_s(hw)` for the Brauer lift with
  `Phi_(c_1)(hw) = i sqrt 2`. So `t = 1/2` iff `W_s(hw) = 0`.
- (b) `t(s o alpha_*) = 1 - t(s)`. So the set of values of `t` over all states is a closed interval symmetric about
  `1/2`, and it contains `1/2` if a state exists.
- (c) No constant of `GL_3(F_3)` has minimal polynomial `x^2 +- x - 1`. No element `x` of order eight in `GL_3(F_3)`
  has `x^4 = z`, so no semidihedral subgroup of `GL_3(F_3)` has central involution `z`. The formula of Theorem 3.2
  involves `W` only at minimal polynomials occurring in `GL_3(F_3)`, so restrictions to `GL_3(F_3)` cannot see `t`.
- (d) Over the code `{00, 010, 011, 10, 110, 111}`, `GL_6(F_3)` constants contain both `GL_2(F_3) (x) I_3` and
  `delta(GL_3(F_3)) = I_2 (x) GL_3(F_3)`. Proposition 2.3 applies there with `U` supported on `x^2 +- x - 1`,
  `U(hw) = i beta`, `beta` real and small. So the finite data of `GL_6(F_3)` still leave `t` free near `1/2`.

*Proof.*
- (a) The powers `(hw)^j`, `j = 0..3`, have `W = 1, W(hw), 0, W(hw)`, since `(hw)^2` has `p = x^2 + 1` and
  `(hw)^3 ~ hw` by Frobenius. With `(hw)^4 = z` and oddness,
  `s(c_1) = (1/4)(2 + 2 i sqrt 2 W(hw)) = 1/2 + (i sqrt 2 / 2) W(hw)`.
  Reality: `(hw)^(-1)` has `p = x^2 - x - 1 = p_(z hw)`, so `conj W(hw) = W(hw^(-1)) = -W(hw)`.
- (b) `W_(s o alpha_*) = W_s o inv` (Lemma 2.4) and `W(hw^(-1)) = -W(hw)`. The set of states is convex and weak-*
  compact, and `t` is affine and continuous.
- (c) A cubic characteristic polynomial divisible by an irreducible quadratic `f` has a linear factor, which must then
  divide the minimal polynomial. `x^4 = -I_3` needs every eigenvalue to be a root of `x^4 + 1 = p_+ p_-`, which has
  no linear factor.
- (d) The code splits each branch of `{0, 1}` into three leaves, so `M_6(R) = M_2(M_3(R))` restricts to the two
  tensor factors. The rest is Proposition 2.3. QED

**Decision on t.** OPEN. `t = 1/2` for every `alpha_*`-invariant state (Theorem 1.2(5) of the GL_2 artifact).
For a general state, `t = 1/2` iff `W_s(hw) = 0`. No finite subgroup, including `GL_3(F_3)` and `GL_6(F_3)`
constants, forces it.

## 5. Where it stops

  - Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3, Proposition 4.1.
