# Obstructions for subgroups of PL_+(I) and of F: lemmas, nilpotent groups, BS(1,n), Z^2 ⋊_A Z

Lane z2-15b-biorderable, 2026-09-13. Written derivations for the necessary conditions
used on root `zaremsky-2-15b-bi-orderable-subgroups-of-v-embed-in-f`. They are standard in
flavour. The nilpotent case parallels Plante–Thurston for C^2 diffeomorphisms, and the
solvable cases are consistent with Bleak's classification of solvable subgroups of
PL_+(I) (J. Algebra 2008), but no theorem number from those sources was read. The
proofs below are self-contained.

## Setting

`PL_+(I)` is the group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of `I = [0,1]` that are
piecewise linear with finitely many breakpoints. Thompson's group `F` is the subgroup
with dyadic breakpoints and slopes in `2^Z`. For `f` in `PL_+(I)` and a point `p` with
`f(p) = p`, write `f'(p+)` for the right derivative. It is a positive real number,
and `f'(p+)` is in `2^Z` when `f` is in `F`.
For `f, g` fixing `p`, the chain rule gives `(fg)'(p+) = f'(p+) g'(p+)`, since `g` maps
`[p, p+ε)` into `[p, p+ε')`. So `f ↦ log f'(p+)` is a homomorphism on the
stabilizer of `p`, and `(h f h^{-1})'(p+) = f'(p+)` whenever `h` fixes `p`.

**Bumps.** `supp(f) = {x : f(x) ≠ x}` is open, and it has finitely many components
because `f` is affine on finitely many pieces and an affine map with a fixed point is
either the identity or has one fixed point. A component `B = (p, q)` is a *bump* of `f`.
At its left endpoint `f'(p+) ≠ 1`: otherwise `f` is the identity on some `[p, p+ε)`,
contradicting `p` being a left endpoint of a component of `supp(f)`. Also
`supp(f^n) = supp(f)` for `n ≠ 0`, since `f(x) > x` implies `f^n(x) > x` for `n ≥ 1`.

**Lemma A (commuting maps).** Let `f, g` in `PL_+(I)` commute. Then `g` maps each bump
of `f` onto itself, and any bump of `f` and any bump of `g` are equal or disjoint.

*Proof.* `g` maps `supp(f)` onto itself, so it permutes the finitely many bumps of `f`.
It preserves their linear order, hence fixes each one. For a bump `B` of `f` and a bump
`D` of `g`: `g(B) = B`, so `g` fixes the endpoints of `B`, so `D` contains no endpoint
of `B`. Since `D` is connected, `D ⊂ B` or `D ∩ B = ∅`. Symmetrically `B ⊂ D` or
`B ∩ D = ∅`. ∎

**Lemma B (germ determination).** Let `B = (p, q)` be a bump of `f`, and let `g` commute
with `f`. If `g` is the identity on some `(p, p+ε)`, then `g` is the identity on `B`.

*Proof.* `f` has no fixed point in `B`, so for `x` in `B` the orbit `f^n(x)` is monotone
and converges to `p` as `n → +∞` or as `n → -∞`. Pick `n` with `f^n(x)` in `(p, p+ε)`.
Then `g(x) = f^{-n} g f^n (x) = f^{-n} f^n (x) = x`. ∎

**Corollary C.** Let `B = (p, q)` be a bump of `f`, and `Z_B` the set of `g` in
`PL_+(I)` commuting with `f`. Each such `g` preserves `B` and fixes `p`, and
`g ↦ log g'(p+)` is a homomorphism `Z_B → R` with trivial kernel on restrictions
to `B`. So the restrictions to `B` of elements of `Z_B` commute with each other.

*Proof.* If `g'(p+) = 1` then `g` is affine with slope 1 fixing `p` on some `[p, p+ε)`,
so it is the identity there, and Lemma B gives `g|_B = id`. The image is abelian, so
commutators of elements of `Z_B` restrict to the identity on `B`. ∎

## N1. Nilpotent subgroups of PL_+(I) are abelian

**Theorem.** Every nilpotent subgroup `H` of `PL_+(I)` is abelian.

*Proof.* Suppose `H` is nilpotent and not abelian. Then `Z_2(H) ≠ Z(H)`. Take `y` in
`Z_2(H) \ Z(H)` and `x` in `H` with `z = [x, y] ≠ 1`. Then `z` is in `Z(H)`, so `x` and
`y` commute with `z`. Let `B` be a bump of `z`. By Corollary C applied to `f = z`, the
restrictions `x|_B` and `y|_B` commute, and `x, y` preserve `B`. So
`z|_B = [x|_B, y|_B] = id`, contradicting `B ⊂ supp(z)`. ∎

So the Heisenberg group, and every non-abelian torsion-free nilpotent group (all of
which are bi-orderable), is isomorphic to no subgroup of `F`.

## N2. BS(1,n) with n ≥ 2 is not a subgroup of PL_+(I)

**Theorem.** For `n ≥ 2`, `BS(1,n) = <a, t | t a t^{-1} = a^n>` is isomorphic to no
subgroup of `PL_+(I)`.

*Proof.* Suppose it is. `a ≠ 1`, so `a` has a bump. The bumps of `t a t^{-1}` are the
images under `t` of the bumps of `a`, and the bumps of `a^n` are those of `a`. So `t`
permutes the finitely many bumps of `a` in an order-preserving way, and fixes each.
Take a bump `B = (p, q)`. Then `t(p) = p` and `λ = a'(p+) ≠ 1`. The chain rule at `p`
gives `(t a t^{-1})'(p+) = λ`, while `(a^n)'(p+) = λ^n`. So `λ^{n-1} = 1`, hence
`λ = 1`, a contradiction. ∎

`BS(1,n)` for `n ≥ 2` is bi-orderable: it is `Z[1/n] ⋊ Z`, with `t` acting by
multiplication by `n`, which preserves the order of `Z[1/n] ⊂ R`. Lexicographic order
with the `Z` coordinate first is then a bi-order.

## N3. Z^2 ⋊_A Z with no eigenvalue 1 is not a subgroup of PL_+(I)

**Theorem.** Let `A` in `GL_2(Z)` have no eigenvalue equal to 1, and let
`Γ_A = Z^2 ⋊_A Z`. Then `Γ_A` is isomorphic to no subgroup of `PL_+(I)`.

*Proof.* Suppose it is. Let `N` be the image of `Z^2`, generated by `u, v`, and let `t`
be the image of the generator of `Z`, with `t w t^{-1} = A(w)` on `N`. Every point
outside `supp(u) ∪ supp(v)` is fixed by `N`, so `supp(N) = supp(u) ∪ supp(v)`. This
is a finite nonempty union of bumps. Let `p = inf supp(N)`. Then `p` is the left
endpoint of a bump of `u` or of `v`, and every element of `N` fixes `p`. Since `t`
normalizes `N`, it maps `supp(N)` onto itself and fixes `p`. Define
`σ(w) = log w'(p+)` for `w` in `N`. This is a homomorphism `Z^2 → R`, and the chain
rule at `p` gives `σ(A w) = σ(t w t^{-1}) = σ(w)`. So `σ` vanishes on `(A − I) Z^2`, which
has finite index because `det(A − I) ≠ 0`. `R` is torsion-free, so `σ = 0`. But the
generator whose bump starts at `p` has slope `≠ 1` there. Contradiction. ∎

The same proof shows, more generally: if `G ≤ PL_+(I)` has a finitely generated
nontrivial normal abelian subgroup `N`, then some nonzero `σ` in `Hom(N, R)` is fixed
by the conjugation action of `G`.

When `A` is hyperbolic with positive eigenvalues (trace ≥ 3), `Γ_A` is bi-orderable.
Order `Z^2` by the sign of a linear functional along an eigenvector of irrational
slope. `A` multiplies that functional by a positive eigenvalue, so it preserves the
order, and the lexicographic order on `Γ_A` is a bi-order. When the trace is `≤ -3`,
`Γ_A` is not bi-orderable.
