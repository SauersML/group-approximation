## 1. Printed text and fidelity

The source is Schafhauser–Tikuisis–White, *Nuclear C\*-algebras: 99 problems*, arXiv:2506.10902v2 (8 May 2026),
§25, p. 80. The first part, verbatim:

> "Let A and B be C*-algebras with Cu(A) ≅ Cu(B) ≅ Cu(Z). Do we have Cu(A ⊗ B) ≅ Cu(Z)?"

The problem then asks, "more generally", whether the minimal tensor product of pure C\*-algebras is pure. The text
around it says the problem was written with the purity of C\*_r(F_r × F_s) in mind. It adds that selflessness has
since settled that case, and that the purity question is still open.

Findings:
- **Separate question.** The first part has its own hypotheses and its own question mark. The root node
  `stw99-problem-xcv-cu-z-tensor` states exactly this sentence, and so does the 08-27 ingestion row.
- **Literal negation.** The node gives A = B = E with Cu(E) ≅ Cu(Z) and Cu(E ⊗ E) ≇ Cu(Z). E is separable, unital,
  simple and nuclear. So E meets any standing assumption, and ⊗_min = ⊗_max for it.
- **The second sentence does not generalize the first.** A pure A ⊗ B need not have Cu(A ⊗ B) ≅ Cu(Z), and the
  node's own tensor products are pure. So the negative first part says nothing about purity. The node is right to
  keep that open in `stw99-problem-xcv-pure-tensor-permanence`.
- **FAIL condition.** The node does not answer a variant, so the FAIL condition does not apply.

## 2. Step-by-step check

### Route B: the Bernoulli certificate (the one to prefer)

Let B_∞ = ⊗_{j∈Z} Z, let β be the shift, and let E = B_∞ ⋊_β Z.

1. B_∞ ≅ Z. So K_0(B_∞) = Z with [1] = 1, K_1(B_∞) = 0, and T(B_∞) = {τ}. Checked; imported.
2. β_* = id on K_*(B_∞), because β fixes [1] and K_1 = 0. Checked.
3. Pimsner–Voiculescu with id − β_* = 0 gives K_0(E) = Z and K_1(E) = Z, with K_1 generated by [u]. Checked.
4. The node proves strong outerness and monotraciality directly, using finite-support approximation, the
   trace-norm distance and Fourier coefficients. I re-derived both, and they are sound. Kishimoto gives simplicity.
5. Z-stability comes from Matui–Sato, Cor. 4.11 and Rem. 4.12 (imported). Their hypotheses hold: B_∞ is unital,
   simple, separable, nuclear, monotracial and Z-stable, and β is strongly outer.
6. E is finite because its trace is faithful. Rørdam's Thm 6.7 then gives stable rank one. With cancellation, and
   τ_*: K_0(E) = Z → R sending 1 to 1, this gives V(E) = N_0. Checked.
7. Brown–Perera–Toms gives Cu(E) = V(E) ⊔ LAff_{++}(T(E)) = N_0 ⊔ (0,∞]. The same formula for Z gives
   Cu(Z) = N_0 ⊔ (0,∞]. Addition and order are identical: n ≤ t iff n < t, t ≤ n iff t ≤ n, and n + t is the soft
   element n + t. So Cu(E) ≅ Cu(Z). Checked.
8. E is in the bootstrap class, so Künneth gives K_0(E ⊗ E) = K_0 ⊗ K_0 ⊕ K_1 ⊗ K_1 = Z². The Tor terms vanish.
   Checked.
   - **Künneth-free alternative (this review's own check).** E ⊗ E ≅ (E ⊗ B_∞) ⋊_{id⊗β} Z. B_∞ ≅ Z is strongly
     self-absorbing and E is Z-stable, so a ↦ a ⊗ 1 is a K-theory isomorphism E → E ⊗ B_∞. Its image is fixed
     by id ⊗ β. A second PV step gives K_0(E ⊗ E) = Z ⊕ Z.
9. **The obstruction.** The certificates argue through compact elements and Grothendieck groups. The version below
   shows that F = E ⊗ E needs neither stable rank one nor Z-stability.
   - F has the faithful trace τ ⊗ τ, so F is stably finite.
   - For projections, Cuntz subequivalence is Murray–von Neumann subequivalence. Since F is finite, p ≾ q ≾ p gives
     p ~ q. So V(F) → Cu(F) is injective, and its image is compact.
   - A Cu-isomorphism preserves ≪, so it preserves compact elements, and Cu(Z)_c = N_0. Hence Cu(F) ≅ Cu(Z) would
     give an injective monoid map V(F) → N_0.
   - Then V(F) is cancellative, and K_0(F) = Gr V(F) embeds in Z. But K_0(F) = Z², and Z² has no injective
     homomorphism to Z. Contradiction.
   - The same argument covers torsion witnesses. If K_1(A) = K_1(B) = Z/p, then K_0(F) contains Z/p and is not
     torsion-free.

### Route R: the range theorem

Here E is the algebra from Gong–Lin–Niu, arXiv:1501.00135v6, **Theorem 13.46**, for the invariant
((Z, N_0, 1), Z, {pt}, r).
- **Citation checked.** I checked the theorem number and statement against the arXiv text. The theorem gives a
  unital simple algebra in N_0^Z for any simple, weakly unperforated Elliott invariant. The algebra is an inductive
  limit of subhomogeneous algebras with injective connecting maps. Its proof records Z-stability.
- **Citation note.** Nuclearity and the UCT come from the inductive-limit structure, not from the theorem's
  statement. Anyone who cites 13.46 for "nuclear, UCT" should add that sentence.
- **Steps.** Steps 6–9 then apply word for word. Checked.

### Route F: C\*_r(F_2), which is not nuclear

- **K-theory.** PV for free groups gives K_0 = Z and K_1 = Z². Also
  C\*_r(F_2) ⊗ C\*_r(F_2) ≅ C\*_r(F_2) ⋊_{r, trivial} F_2, and PV for F_2 gives K_0 = Z ⊕ (Z²)² = Z^5. No Künneth
  is needed.
- **Hypothesis side.** Cu(C\*_r(F_2)) ≅ Cu(Z) needs stable rank one, strict comparison, exactness and a Cu formula
  (imports in §3).
- **Obstruction.** Step 9 applies unchanged. Checked.
- **Motivating example.** C\*_r(F_2 × F_2) is the authors' own motivating example, so the first sentence already
  fails there.

## 3. Imported theorems

Every route uses these, because the statement names Cu(Z):
- **I1.** Jiang–Su (1999): Z is simple and unital, has a unique trace, K_0 = Z and K_1 = 0.
- **I2.** Coward–Elliott–Ivanescu (2008): the category Cu, in which projections are compact.
- **I3.** Brown–Perera–Toms (2008) together with Rørdam Thm 6.7 (2004): Cu(A) = V(A) ⊔ LAff_{++}(T(A)) for simple
  unital exact Z-stable A. Hence Cu(Z) = N_0 ⊔ (0,∞].
- **I4.** Cuntz subequivalence of projections equals Murray–von Neumann subequivalence.

## 5. Is there a witness that is cheaper in Lean?

No. Every witness needs a foundation on the scale of the Jiang–Su construction:
- **Forced properties.** Cu(A) ≅ Cu(Z) forces A to be simple, since Cu(Z) has only trivial ideals. It also forces a
  unique normalized functional, strict comparison and almost divisibility. When A is unital it forces V(A) ≅ N_0,
  so K_0(A) = Z.
- **Tensor side.** A minimal tensor product of simple algebras is simple, and unique traces multiply: if φ is a
  trace on A ⊗ B, then a ↦ φ(a ⊗ b) is a multiple of τ_A. So F can only fail in its compact part, which is
  K-theory, or in comparison and divisibility. A comparison failure would answer the open purity half. So a witness
  must be K-theoretic: two projections with equal trace that are not equivalent, which only a K-theoretic invariant
  can detect.
- **Fidelity.** The Challenge statement must name Cu(Z). Using a concrete Cu-semigroup S = N_0 ⊔ (0,∞] instead
  gives a variant, unless the bridge Cu(Z) ≅ S is proved (I1 + I3).

| Witness | K_0(F) from | Cu(A) ≅ Cu(Z) needs | Lean cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bernoulli E = (⊗Z) ⋊ Z | PV only (the step 8 alternative) | Z, Matui–Sato, Rørdam, BPT | cheapest foundations, still a wall |
| Range-theorem E | Künneth | GLN 13.46 (hundreds of pages) | deepest |
| C\*_r(F_2) | PV for free groups | DHR sr1, strict comparison (2025), QT = T | analytic wall |
| C(X) ⋊ Z, minimal on a point-like X (Deeley–Putnam–Strung) | PV | Z-stability via mean dimension, BPT; X is intricate | not cheaper (not rechecked here) |
| Cuntz–Pimsner algebra of (B_∞, β) | the Bernoulli E itself | same | no gain |
| AT algebras; Cantor or manifold minimal systems | K_0 ≠ Z, or rank ≥ 2 | fail the forced properties | excluded |
| Odd dimension-drop inductive limits | finitely generated K-theory | a construction as long as Jiang–Su's | no gain |

Recommended staging for `x95-lean`. Claim nothing complete.
1. **Terminal algebra (cheap).** There is no injective `ℤ × ℤ →+ ℤ`. More generally, if a commutative monoid M
   injects into ℕ, then its Grothendieck group embeds in ℤ. So it is not ℤ × ℤ and has no torsion.
2. **Abstract Cu-semigroup.** Define S = ℕ ⊔ (0,∞] with ≪, whose compact elements are exactly ℕ. Prove that a
   Cu-isomorphism preserves compact elements.
3. **C\*-level.** Cuntz subequivalence agrees with Murray–von Neumann subequivalence on projections. For stably
   finite algebras, V → Cu is injective and its image is compact.
4. **Wall.** Jiang–Su, and Cu(Z) ≅ S. Then Cu(E) ≅ S, and K_0(E ⊗ E) via PV. This is not realistic in this wave.
