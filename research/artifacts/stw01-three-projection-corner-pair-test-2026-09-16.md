# STW Problem I: one corner pair of words in three projections is a universal test (2026-09-16)

Lane `swarm-stw01-three-projection-algebra`. Proof artifact for:
- the claims `stw01-three-projection-corner-pair-quasitrace-additivity` and
  `stw01-three-projection-corner-pair-is-universal-pair`;
- the routes `stw01-three-projection-via-corner-pair-additivity` and
  `stw01-three-projection-corner-pair-is-universal-pair-proof`.

**Outcome.** A reduction of `stw01-three-projection-algebra-quasitraces-are-traces`. It
settles neither Problem I nor the three-projection claim.

The reviewed universal test,
`research/artifacts/stw01-three-projection-universal-test-2026-09-12.md`, has witness shape
`sigma(g(r_1 r_2 r_1) + beta r_3)`, which involves a functional calculus `g` and a scalar
`beta`. This artifact replaces it by one fixed pair of words.

Put `h_2 = r_1 r_2 r_1` and `h_3 = r_1 r_3 r_1`.
- **Theorem 1.** Problem I holds iff every bounded 2-quasitrace `sigma` on `P_3` satisfies
  `sigma(h_2 + h_3) = sigma(h_2) + sigma(h_3)`.
- **Theorem 2.** A non-additive witness can be chosen such that:
  - it agrees with the canonical group trace on `1`, on every `r_i`, and on every positive
    word `r_i r_j r_i`;
  - it is invariant under `r_2 <-> r_3`;
  - its defect has a prescribed sign.

  So a one-sided inequality for these normalized quasitraces is already equivalent to
  Problem I.
- **Proposition 3.** The unital C*-algebra generated by `h_2, h_3` inside the corner
  `r_1 P_3 r_1`, with unit `r_1`, is the universal unital C*-algebra of two positive
  contractions. The pair satisfies no relation.

This also resolves the target's recorded attempt "Reduce to projections of equal trace".
A witness exists in which all three projections have half trace (Theorem 2).

## 0. Notation and axioms

`P_3 = C^2 * C^2 * C^2` is the universal unital C*-algebra of three projections
`r_1, r_2, r_3`, equivalently `C*(Z_2 * Z_2 * Z_2)` via `s_i = 2 r_i - 1`. The positive
contractions `h_j = r_1 r_j r_1 = (r_j r_1)^* (r_j r_1)` lie in the corner `r_1 P_3 r_1`, and
`r_1 h_j = h_j = h_j r_1`.

A bounded 2-quasitrace `sigma` on a unital `A` is used with the axioms of
`research/artifacts/stw01-certificates-products-universal-tests-2026-09-11.md` §0:
- positive on `A_+`;
- linear on commutative C*-subalgebras;
- `sigma(z^* z) = sigma(z z^*)`;
- `sigma(a + ib) = sigma(a) + i sigma(b)`;
- extends to a quasitrace on `M_2(A)`.

Monotonicity on `A_+` is also used, as the standard axiom recorded in
`research/artifacts/stw01-quasitrace-status-and-local-permanence-audit-2026-08-30.md`.

For `a, b >= 0` write `D_sigma(a, b) = sigma(a + b) - sigma(a) - sigma(b)`.

Three elementary facts are used throughout.
- **(E1) Invariance.** `sigma(w^* w) = sigma(w w^*)` for every `w` (an axiom). In particular
  `sigma(u x u^*) = sigma(x)` for `x >= 0` and `u` unitary: take `w = u x^(1/2)`.
- **(E2) Sums and pullbacks.** A sum of bounded 2-quasitraces is one, since all axioms are
  preserved and the `M_2` extensions add. So is `sigma o gamma` for a unital
  *-endomorphism `gamma`.
- **(E3) Lipschitz bound.** For `X, Y in A_+`, `|sigma(X) - sigma(Y)| <= ||X - Y|| sigma(1)`.
  This follows from `X <= Y + ||X - Y|| 1`, monotonicity, and linearity on `C*(1, Y)`.

The canonical group trace `tau_e` on `P_3` has `tau_e(s_g) = delta_(g, e)`. So

```text
tau_e(r_i) = 1/2,        tau_e(r_i r_j r_i) = tau_e(r_i r_j) = (1 + tau_e(s_i s_j)) / 4 = 1/4   (i != j).
```

## 1. The corner dilation

**Lemma 1.1 (corner dilation).** Let `N` be a unital C*-algebra, and let `a, b in N` with
`0 <= a, b <= 1`. In `M_2(N)` put

```text
P_1 = [[1, 0], [0, 0]],
v_a = (a^(1/2), (1 - a)^(1/2))^T,              P_2 = v_a v_a^* = [[a, a^(1/2)(1-a)^(1/2)], [(1-a)^(1/2)a^(1/2), 1 - a]],
v_b(theta) = (b^(1/2), e^(i theta)(1 - b)^(1/2))^T,   P_3(theta) = v_b(theta) v_b(theta)^*,
```

for a real parameter `theta`. Then:
1. `P_1`, `P_2` and `P_3(theta)` are projections.
2. `P_1 P_2 P_1 = a (+) 0` and `P_1 P_3(theta) P_1 = b (+) 0`.
3. Each of `P_2`, `P_3(theta)` is Murray–von Neumann equivalent to `P_1` in `M_2(N)`.
4. With `w_theta = v_a^* v_b(theta) = a^(1/2) b^(1/2) + e^(i theta) (1-a)^(1/2) (1-b)^(1/2) in N`,
   there is `Z in M_2(N)` with `Z Z^* = P_2 P_3(theta) P_2` and `Z^* Z = (w_theta^* w_theta) (+) 0`.

**Proof.**
1. `a` and `1 - a` commute, so `v_a^* v_a = a + (1 - a) = 1`, and `v_a v_a^*` is a
   projection. Likewise `v_b(theta)^* v_b(theta) = b + (1 - b) = 1`.
2. Read off the `(1,1)` entries.
3. Let `W_a` be the `2 x 2` matrix with first column `v_a` and second column `0`. Then
   `W_a^* W_a = P_1` and `W_a W_a^* = P_2`. The same holds for `v_b(theta)`.
4. `P_2 P_3(theta) P_2 = v_a (v_a^* v_b)(v_b^* v_a) v_a^* = (v_a w_theta)(v_a w_theta)^*`. Let
   `Z` have first column `v_a w_theta` and second column `0`. Then
   `Z^* Z = w_theta^* v_a^* v_a w_theta (+) 0 = w_theta^* w_theta (+) 0`. ∎

By the universal property of `P_3` there is a unital *-homomorphism

```text
pi = pi_(a, b, theta) : P_3 -> M_2(N),     r_1 -> P_1,  r_2 -> P_2,  r_3 -> P_3(theta),
```

with `pi(h_2) = a (+) 0` and `pi(h_3) = b (+) 0`. Its image is `B = C*(1, P_1, P_2, P_3(theta))`.

## 2. Proposition 3: the corner pair is the universal pair of positive contractions

Let `V` be the universal unital C*-algebra of two elements `h_0, k_0` with
`0 <= h_0 <= 1` and `0 <= k_0 <= 1`. The relations bound the norms, so `V` exists. It is the
unital full free product `C([0,1]) * C([0,1])`, but only its universal property is used.

**Proposition 3.** Let `C = C*(r_1, h_2, h_3) <= r_1 P_3 r_1`, a unital C*-algebra with unit
`r_1`. The unital *-homomorphism `psi : V -> C` with `h_0 -> h_2` and `k_0 -> h_3` is an
isomorphism.

**Proof.**
- *Existence and surjectivity.* `h_2` and `h_3` are positive contractions in the unital
  C*-algebra `r_1 P_3 r_1`, whose unit is `r_1`. So `psi` exists by the universal property,
  and its image is `C`.
- *Norm bound.* Let `a, b` be positive contractions on a Hilbert space `H`. Lemma 1.1 with
  `N = B(H)` and `theta = 0` gives a unital *-homomorphism `pi : P_3 -> M_2(B(H))` with
  `pi(r_1) = 1 (+) 0`, `pi(h_2) = a (+) 0` and `pi(h_3) = b (+) 0`. So `pi` maps `C` unitally
  into the corner `B(H) (+) 0`. For every noncommutative *-polynomial `P` with constant
  term,

  ```text
  pi(P(h_2, h_3)) = P(a, b) (+) 0,   hence   ||P(a, b)|| <= ||P(h_2, h_3)||_(P_3).
  ```

  Here the constant term of `P` is read as a multiple of `r_1` on the left, and of `1_H`
  on the right.
- *Isometry.* The norm of `P(h_0, k_0)` in `V` is the supremum of `||P(a, b)||` over all such
  Hilbert-space pairs, since every C*-algebra embeds in some `B(H)`. So

  ```text
  ||P(h_0, k_0)||_V <= ||P(h_2, h_3)|| = ||psi(P(h_0, k_0))|| <= ||P(h_0, k_0)||_V.
  ```

  So `psi` is isometric on a dense *-subalgebra, hence isometric and injective. ∎

**Remarks.**
- *Non-exactness.* `V` surjects onto `Q = C^3 * C^2`. Take `h_0 -> sum_i lambda_i e_i`
  with distinct `lambda_i in (0, 1)`, and `k_0 -> p`; the image is `C*(1, e_1, e_2, e_3, p) = Q`.
  `Q` is not exact, as recorded on `stw01-modular-test-pair-generates-non-exact-algebra`
  (Kirchberg quotient permanence and Wassermann, inherited, not re-verified here). So `C`
  is not exact. No exactness argument reaches any C*-algebra containing `h_2` and `h_3`.
- *Where the defect can live.* `sigma` is a trace on `C*(r_1, r_j)` for `j = 2, 3`. These are
  quotients of the nuclear `C*(D_infinity)`, and Haagerup's theorem applies there
  (inherited from the target claim). So `sigma` is linear on `C*(h_2)` and on `C*(h_3)`.
  Additivity on the pair is a statement about the free position of `h_2` and `h_3` in `V`,
  seen through quasitraces that extend to `P_3`.

## 3. Theorem 1: the corner-pair test

**Theorem 1.** The following are equivalent.
1. STW Problem I: every bounded 2-quasitrace on every unital C*-algebra is a trace.
2. Every bounded 2-quasitrace `sigma` on `P_3` satisfies
   `sigma(r_1 r_2 r_1 + r_1 r_3 r_1) = sigma(r_1 r_2 r_1) + sigma(r_1 r_3 r_1)`.

**Inputs.** These are the inputs of the reviewed universal-test artifact
`research/artifacts/stw01-three-projection-universal-test-2026-09-12.md`, with the same
numbering:
- (I1) Gow arXiv:2601.04431v2, Theorem A (after Blackadar–Handelman);
- (I2) Gow Theorem 2.22;
- (I3) Blackadar–Handelman Corollary II.1.10, as quoted by Gow;
- (I4) Gow Corollary 3.10;
- (I6) restriction to unital subalgebras, and pullback along unital surjections.

(I5), the density of finite-spectrum elements, is not needed.

**Proof of (2) ⟹ (1).** Assume (1) fails.

*Step 0 (reviewed artifact, unchanged).* (I1), (I2) and (I4) give a type `II_1`
AW*-factor `M` with canonical quasitrace `tau`, `tau(1) = 1`, and `x, y in M_+` with
`D_tau(x, y) != 0`. Both `x, y` are nonzero, because `D_tau(0, z) = 0`.

*Step 1 (normalize).* Put `c = max(||x||, ||y||) > 0`. Homogeneity inside `C*(z)` gives
`D_tau(x/c, y/c) = D_tau(x, y)/c`. So assume `||x||, ||y|| <= 1`, and set `a = x`, `b = y`.

*Step 2 (the quasitrace on `M_2(M)`).* As in Step 5 of the reviewed artifact:
- (I3) gives a quasitrace `tau_4` on `M_4(M)` with `tau_4(z (+) 0 (+) 0 (+) 0) = tau(z)`.
- Let `tau~(w) = tau_4(w (+) 0)` for `w in M_2(M)`, embedded in the upper-left `2 x 2`
  block of `M_4(M) = M_2(M_2(M))`.
- Then `tau~` is a quasitrace on `M_2(M)` with `tau~(z (+) 0) = tau(z)`. It is a
  2-quasitrace, because `tau_4` is its extension to `M_2(M_2(M))`.
- It is bounded, by (E3).

*Step 3 (dilate and pull back).* Take `pi = pi_(a, b, 0) : P_3 -> B <= M_2(M)` from Lemma 1.1.
By (I6), `sigma = tau~|_B o pi` is a bounded 2-quasitrace on `P_3`, and

```text
sigma(h_2 + h_3) - sigma(h_2) - sigma(h_3) = tau(a + b) - tau(a) - tau(b) = D_tau(x, y) != 0.
```

So (2) fails.

**(1) ⟹ (2)** is trivial. ∎

**Comparison with the reviewed witness.** The earlier proof used:
- Steps 1–3 (finite spectrum, telescoping to one projection, a shift);
- the dilation `p' = [[A, S], [S, P - A]]`;
- a functional calculus `g`.

Here Step 0 feeds Lemma 1.1 directly: the pair `(x, y)` becomes the pair of compressions of
`r_2` and `r_3` to `r_1`. No functional calculus and no scalar remain, which parallels the
single-pair theorem for `C^3 * C^2` (`stw01-modular-single-pair-quasitrace-additivity`).

**What Theorem 1 adds beyond the universal pair.** Additivity of every bounded 2-quasitrace on
`V` itself on `(h_0, k_0)` is trivially equivalent to Problem I. A non-additive pair `x, y` with
`||x||, ||y|| <= 1` in any unital `A` defines `V -> A` with `h_0 -> x` and `k_0 -> y`, and the
pullback has the same defect. That is the same one-line argument as
`stw01-order-pair-defects-rescale-generic-defects`. Theorem 1 says more: it suffices to test
the quasitraces on `C = V` that extend to bounded 2-quasitraces on `P_3`. A quasitrace on `C`
is not known to extend to `P_3`, and that extension is where the dilation and the AW*-factor
inputs are used.

**An alternative trust path (not used).** Blackadar–Handelman are standardly credited with the
result that a 2-quasitrace extends to a quasitrace on every `M_n(A)` (primary not fetched,
unverified). Granting it, Step 0 and (I1)–(I4) can be dropped. Take a non-additive
pair `x, y` in any unital `A`, dilate it into `M_2(A)` by Lemma 1.1, and pull back the extension
of `sigma` to `M_2(A)`. That extension is itself a 2-quasitrace, because `sigma` extends to
`M_4(A)`.

## 4. Theorem 2: normalized witnesses and the one-sided test

Let `alpha` and `beta` be the unital *-automorphisms of `P_3` given by the universal property:

```text
alpha : r_1 -> r_1,  r_2 -> r_3,  r_3 -> r_2;          beta : r_1 -> r_1,  r_2 -> 1 - r_2,  r_3 -> 1 - r_3.
```

Both are involutions, and `alpha beta = beta alpha` on generators, hence everywhere.

**Lemma 4.1 (the double flip reverses the defect).** Let `sigma` be a bounded 2-quasitrace
on `P_3`.
1. `D_(sigma o beta)(h_2, h_3) = - D_sigma(h_2, h_3)`. So a `beta`-invariant `sigma` is additive on
   the corner pair.
2. `D_(sigma o alpha)(h_2, h_3) = D_sigma(h_2, h_3)`.
3. If `sigma(1) = 1`, `sigma(r_i) = 1/2` for all `i` and `sigma(r_i r_j r_i) = 1/4` for all
   `i != j`, then `sigma o alpha` and `sigma o beta` have the same values on these words.

**Proof.**
1. `beta(h_j) = r_1 (1 - r_j) r_1 = r_1 - h_j`, and `beta(h_2 + h_3) = 2 r_1 - (h_2 + h_3)`. The
   algebras `C*(r_1, h_j)` and `C*(r_1, h_2 + h_3)` are commutative, because `h_j` lies in the
   corner of `r_1`. So `sigma` is linear on each, and
   ```text
   D_(sigma o beta)(h_2, h_3) = (2 sigma(r_1) - sigma(h_2 + h_3)) - (sigma(r_1) - sigma(h_2)) - (sigma(r_1) - sigma(h_3)) = - D_sigma(h_2, h_3).
   ```
2. `alpha` swaps `h_2` and `h_3`, and `D_sigma` is symmetric.
3. By (E1), `sigma(r_i r_j r_i) = sigma(r_j r_i r_j)`, taking `w = r_j r_i`. `alpha` permutes the
   words. For `beta`:
   - `sigma(beta(r_j)) = sigma(1 - r_j) = 1/2`.
   - `sigma(beta(r_1 r_j r_1)) = sigma(r_1) - sigma(h_j) = 1/4`.
   - `beta(r_2 r_3 r_2) = (1 - r_2) - (1 - r_2) r_3 (1 - r_2)`, a difference of commuting
     elements.
   - By (E1) with `w = r_3 (1 - r_2)`, and linearity on `C*(r_3, r_3 r_2 r_3)`:
     `sigma((1 - r_2) r_3 (1 - r_2)) = sigma(r_3 (1 - r_2) r_3) = sigma(r_3) - sigma(r_3 r_2 r_3) = 1/4`.
   - So `sigma(beta(r_2 r_3 r_2)) = 1/2 - 1/4 = 1/4`. ∎

**Theorem 2.** Assume Problem I fails, and fix a sign `epsilon in {+1, -1}`. Then there is a
bounded 2-quasitrace `sigma` on `P_3` with `sigma(1) = 1` and the following properties.
- (N1) `sigma(r_i) = 1/2` for `i = 1, 2, 3`.
- (N2) `sigma(r_i r_j r_i) = 1/4` for all `i != j`.
- (N3) `sigma o alpha = sigma`.
- (N4) `epsilon (sigma(h_2 + h_3) - sigma(h_2) - sigma(h_3)) > 0`.

By (N1)–(N2), `sigma` agrees with the canonical group trace `tau_e` on `1`, on each `r_i`, and on
every positive word of length 3.

**Proof.** Take `M`, `tau` and `x, y in M_+` with `||x||, ||y|| <= 1` and `D_tau(x, y) != 0`, as in
Steps 0-1 of Theorem 1, and `tau~` on `M_2(M)` as in Step 2. Put `eta = 1/32`.

*Step A (affine rescaling to half trace).* Put

```text
a = (1/2 - eta tau(x)) 1 + eta x,        b = (1/2 - eta tau(y)) 1 + eta y.
```

- Since `0 <= tau(x), tau(y) <= 1`, both spectra lie in `[1/2 - eta, 1/2 + eta] ⊂ [0, 1]`.
- `tau` is linear on `C*(1, x)`, `C*(1, y)` and `C*(1, x + y)`. So `tau(a) = tau(b) = 1/2`, and
  `D_tau(a, b) = eta D_tau(x, y) != 0`.

This is the affine rescaling of Lemma R in
`research/artifacts/stw01-order-pair-rescaling-2026-09-12.md`, shifted so that both traces equal `1/2`.

*Step B (tune the phase).* Let `w_theta` be as in Lemma 1.1(4), and put `f(theta) = tau(w_theta^* w_theta)`.
`theta -> w_theta` is norm continuous, so `f` is continuous by (E3). For the estimates:
- For `t in [1/2 - eta, 1/2 + eta]`,
  `|t^(1/2) - 2^(-1/2)| = |t - 1/2| / (t^(1/2) + 2^(-1/2)) <= eta / (2 (3/8)^(1/2)) <= eta`.
- So each of `a^(1/2)`, `(1 - a)^(1/2)`, `b^(1/2)` and `(1 - b)^(1/2)` is within `eta` of `2^(-1/2) 1`.
- These are contractions, so `a^(1/2) b^(1/2)` and `(1 - a)^(1/2)(1 - b)^(1/2)` are each within
  `2 eta` of `(1/2) 1`.
- Hence `||w_0 - 1|| <= 4 eta`, `||w_pi|| <= 4 eta`, and
  `||w_0^* w_0 - 1|| <= (1 + 4 eta) 4 eta + 4 eta = 8 eta + 16 eta^2 = 17/64`.

By (E3) with `tau(1) = 1`, `f(0) >= 1 - 17/64 > 1/2` and `f(pi) <= 16 eta^2 = 1/64 < 1/2`. The
intermediate value theorem gives `theta_0 in (0, pi)` with `f(theta_0) = 1/2`.

*Step C (dilate).* Let `pi = pi_(a, b, theta_0) : P_3 -> B <= M_2(M)` from Lemma 1.1, and
`sigma_0 = (1/2) tau~|_B o pi`. By (I6) it is a bounded 2-quasitrace.
- `sigma_0(1) = (1/2)(tau~(1 (+) 0) + tau~(0 (+) 1)) = 1`. Here `tau~(0 (+) 1) = tau~(1 (+) 0)` by (E1)
  with the swap unitary.
- `sigma_0(r_i) = (1/2) tau~(P_i) = (1/2) tau~(P_1) = 1/2`, by Lemma 1.1(3) and (E1).
- `sigma_0(h_2) = tau(a)/2 = 1/4`, and `sigma_0(h_3) = tau(b)/2 = 1/4`.
- `sigma_0(r_2 r_3 r_2) = (1/2) tau~(w_(theta_0)^* w_(theta_0) (+) 0) = f(theta_0)/2 = 1/4`, by
  Lemma 1.1(4) and (E1).
- By (E1) the reversed words take the same values, so (N1) and (N2) hold.
- `D_(sigma_0)(h_2, h_3) = D_tau(a, b)/2 = (eta/2) D_tau(x, y) != 0`.

*Step D (symmetrize).* `sigma_1 = (sigma_0 + sigma_0 o alpha)/2` is a bounded 2-quasitrace by (E2). It
satisfies (N1)–(N3) by Lemma 4.1(3), and `D_(sigma_1)(h_2, h_3) = D_(sigma_0)(h_2, h_3) != 0` by
Lemma 4.1(2).

*Step E (choose the sign).* `sigma_1 o beta` satisfies (N1)–(N3) by Lemma 4.1(3) and
`alpha beta = beta alpha`, and its defect is `-D_(sigma_1)(h_2, h_3)` by Lemma 4.1(1). One of
`sigma_1`, `sigma_1 o beta` satisfies (N4). ∎

**Corollary 4.2 (one-sided test).** The following are equivalent.
1. STW Problem I.
2. Every bounded 2-quasitrace `sigma` on `P_3` with `sigma(1) = 1` and (N1)–(N3) satisfies
   `sigma(h_2 + h_3) <= sigma(h_2) + sigma(h_3)`.
3. The same with `>=`.

**Proof.** (1) implies (2) and (3), since a trace is additive. If (1) fails, Theorem 2 with
`epsilon = +1` (respectively `-1`) contradicts (2) (respectively (3)). ∎

**Remarks.**
- *Elementary two-sided bounds.* For every bounded 2-quasitrace and all `a, b >= 0`,
  `(sigma(a) + sigma(b))/2 <= sigma(a + b) <= 2 (sigma(a) + sigma(b))`.
  - The lower bound is monotonicity.
  - For the upper bound, put `z = (a^(1/2), b^(1/2))^T`, `X = z z^* in M_2(A)_+` and
    `W = diag(1, -1)`. Then `X + W X W = 2 (a (+) b)` and `sigma_2(X) = sigma(z^* z) = sigma(a + b)`.
    Monotonicity of `sigma_2` gives `sigma_2(X) <= sigma_2(X + W X W) = 2 sigma(a) + 2 sigma(b)`.
  - The same identity gives `D_(sigma_2)(X, W X W) = -2 D_sigma(a, b)`: a matrix-level sign flip,
    and the analogue of Lemma 4.1(1).
  - Under (N2) these bounds only give `sigma(h_2 + h_3) in [1/4, 1]`, while the trace value is
    `1/2`.
- *What the normalization does not reach.* On `C*(r_i, r_j)` the group trace gives
  `r_i r_j r_i` the arcsine law in the corner of `r_i`. In the model of Theorem 2, `h_2` has
  spectrum in `[1/2 - eta, 1/2 + eta]`. Matching the full law would need `a = g(x)` for a
  nonlinear `g`, and then `D_tau(g(x), g(y))` is unrelated to `D_tau(x, y)`. So agreement with
  `tau_e` on all of `C*(r_i, r_j)`, the "free triple" normalization, is not obtained.

## 5. Attempts on the corner-pair claim, and where each dies

- **Exactness of a host (Haagerup).** Dead. Any C*-subalgebra of `P_3` containing `h_2` and
  `h_3` contains `C*(h_2, h_3)`. That algebra is not exact: `C = V` (Proposition 3) is not exact,
  and `C` is either `C*(h_2, h_3)` or its unitization. Exactness passes to subalgebras and to
  unitizations.
- **Traces on two-projection subalgebras.** `sigma` is linear on `C*(r_1, r_2)` and on
  `C*(r_1, r_3)`, hence on `C*(r_1, h_2)` and `C*(r_1, h_3)`. Dead: these facts never relate
  `h_2` to `h_3`. No two-projection subalgebra contains both, since those algebras are nuclear
  and `C` is not exact.
- **One-sided inequalities.** Dead as a simplification. By Corollary 4.2, each of
  `sigma(h_2 + h_3) <= sigma(h_2) + sigma(h_3)` and `>=`, even for normalized `sigma`, is
  already equivalent to Problem I. There is no easy half.
- **Elementary two-sided bounds.** Dead. They give `sigma(h_2 + h_3) in [1/4, 1]` under
  (N2), not `1/2`.
- **Pinching.** Let `Y in M_2(P_3)` have first column `(r_2 r_1, r_3 r_1)^T` and second column
  `0`. Then `Y^* Y = (h_2 + h_3) (+) 0`, and `X = Y Y^*` has diagonal `r_2 r_1 r_2 (+) r_3 r_1 r_3`.
  With `W = diag(1, -1)` this gives `D_sigma(h_2, h_3) = sigma_2(X) - sigma_2(diag X) = -(1/2) D_(sigma_2)(X, W X W)`.
  Dead: the defect is exactly the failure of `sigma_2` to commute with the pinching, so this is
  a restatement, not a proof.
- **Averaging over symmetries.**
  - Averaging with `alpha` keeps the defect (Lemma 4.1(2)). It only makes a witness
    swap-invariant.
  - Averaging with `beta` kills the defect (Lemma 4.1(1)). So a counterexample is never
    `beta`-invariant, but nothing forces invariance.
  - The permutations of all three `r_i` move the pair to the other corner pairs
    `(r_i r_j r_i, r_i r_k r_i)`. An averaged quasitrace sees the sum of three defects, which can
    cancel. No constraint on a single defect results.
- **Free-triple normalization.** Not reached. The affine model gives `h_2` a spectrum near
  `1/2`, not the arcsine law of `tau_e` (remark after Corollary 4.2).
- **Commuting sandwich (order-pair methods).** Dead. In the order-pair test the pair satisfies
  `0 <= k <= h - h^2`, which bounds the defect. Here `h_2` and `h_3` satisfy no relation
  (Proposition 3), so only the elementary bounds remain.
- **Projections and `K_0`.** A 2-quasitrace is additive on orthogonal projections, and equal on
  Murray–von Neumann equivalent ones. If `K_0(P_3) = Z^4` is generated by `[1], [r_1], [r_2], [r_3]`,
  and `sigma` extends to all `M_n(P_3)`, then a normalized witness agrees with `tau_e` on every
  projection over `P_3`. The `K_0` formula is Cuntz's free-product computation, and the `M_n`
  extension is due to Blackadar–Handelman; both are unverified here and not used. This stalls:
  no argument is known that passes from projections to `h_2 + h_3`. `P_3` does not have real rank
  zero, since its quotient `C*(Z_2 * Z_2)` (kill `r_3`) has the corner `C*(r_1, r_1 r_2 r_1)`,
  which is commutative with connected spectrum `[0, 1]`. So the spectral approximation by
  projections that works inside an AW*-factor is not available in `P_3`.
- **Residual finite-dimensionality.** `P_3` is RFD, as a full free product of finite-dimensional
  algebras (Exel–Loring, unverified here). Dead: a non-linear quasitrace is not a weak* limit of
  finite-dimensional traces, so RFD structure gives nothing.
- **The index-two free subgroup.** `s_1 s_2` and `s_1 s_3` generate a free subgroup of rank 2 and
  index 2. So `P_3 = C*(F_2) x| Z_2`, where `s_1` inverts both generators. Dead as a transfer
  to `stw01-full-free-group-algebra-quasitraces-are-traces`. `h_2` and `h_3` have odd parts,
  since `8 h_2 = 2(1 + s_1) + s_2 + s_1 s_2 + s_2 s_1 + s_1 s_2 s_1`. And `P_3` sits inside
  `M_2(C*(F_2))`, but no way is known to extend a quasitrace from `P_3` to `M_2(C*(F_2))`. Both
  claims are equivalent to Problem I, but only through their own reductions.
