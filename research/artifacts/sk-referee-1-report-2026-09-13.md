### GAP (a justification the reader needs is missing)
- **G1.** l.125-128 says "choose two occurrences of the same word of length 2k, separated by a segment w".
  - Read literally, w is the gap between the two occurrences, and then w^∞ can contain new words.
  - The argument works for the segment from the start of the first occurrence to the start of the second, so that wv is
    a word of X.
  - The note should say so, and say why two such occurrences at distance ≥ L exist: pigeonhole on the positions
    0, L, 2L, ….
- **G2.** l.131-133 gives no formula for D_y(f).
  - "Evaluate its local table along y" does not fix the index convention. The relation at l.140 holds for
    D_y(f)δ_n = F(y_{n-ρ}⋯y_{n+ρ})δ_n, where f(x) = F(x_{-ρ}⋯x_ρ), and fails for the reflected convention.
  - The table F is defined only on words of X. That is where the choice of the window parameter enters.
- **G3.** l.218-219 says "Conjugation by k on H_W is conjugation by κ_0". This needs one line:
  - every x ∈ H_W is I_3+M with M ∈ M_3(B_m(W)) constant over W;
  - k = (1-e)I_3 + eke, and the tower over W lies inside the tower over U;
  - so kMk^{-1} = κ_0Mκ_0^{-1}.
  N1 of `ex-pestov91-second-review-2026-09-13.md` makes the same point about the long write-up.

### FREE STRENGTHENING / SIMPLIFICATION
- **S1.** The theorem holds for EL_n for every n ≥ 3 with the same proof: d = n(2m+1), permutation matrices over F_2, a
  third index for the commutators, and EJZ for n ≥ 3. It costs about two lines. sk-coefficients owns F_q and the choice
  of n.
- **S2.** l.257-260: interleaving with 1's is unnecessary. For a noncomputable set S, Σ_{i∈S}2^{-i-1} lies in (0,1), is
  Turing equivalent to S, and is irrational, because the binary expansion of a rational number is eventually periodic,
  hence computable.
- Neutral:
  - the generator s = 1 at l.95 is redundant, since e_ij(1) = ∏_a e_ij(e_a);
  - every finite group embeds in G_X (towers of every height, and GL_d(F_2) ⊇ Sym(d)). I do not recommend adding this.
