# Normalized-HS curvature concentration and regular-sector improvement

This is an affirmative analytic result, with an explicit boundary. It removes
the matrix-dimension factor from one cohomological defect-improvement step.
It does **not** establish unrestricted flexible HS stability, supply exact
moving-subgroup holonomy, or prove a non-hyperlinear group exists.

The stronger conclusion in Section 6 does **not** require uniform
integrability: for any approximate representation of a group satisfying
`(AH1)`, an `O(delta)` same-dimension perturbation concentrates all remaining
defining curvature onto subspaces of vanishing relative dimension. More
precisely, its trace-norm defect is `o(delta)` and its HS defect is
`O(delta)`, with delta the original HS defect. This does not make those
small subspaces invariant under the generators.

## Statement

Let `G=<S|R>` be finitely presented and suppose

```text
H^2(G,H_pi)=0 for every unitary Hilbert-space representation pi.       (AH1)
```

All matrix 2-norms below use normalized trace. Let `U_j:S->U(d_j)` be
generator tuples, with positive defining defect

```text
delta_j=max_(r in R)||U_j(r)-I||_2 -> 0.
```

Assume the rescaled defining errors are uniformly square-integrable:

```text
lim_(M->infinity) limsup_j max_(r in R)
 ||A_(j,r) 1_(|A_(j,r)|>M)||_2 = 0,
A_(j,r)=(U_j(r)-I)/delta_j.                              (AH2)
```

Then, after passing to a subsequence if necessary, there are tuples
`V_j:S->U(d_j)` in the **same dimensions** satisfying

```text
max_s ||V_j(s)-U_j(s)||_2 = O(delta_j),
max_r ||V_j(r)-I||_2 = o(delta_j).                        (AH3)
```

More precisely, the conclusion holds along every free ultrafilter, with an
ultrafilter-dependent finite constant in the first estimate; the displayed
ordinary subsequence form follows. The assertion is one improvement step,
not convergence of an iteration to an exact tuple.

The same result holds for `SL_n(Z)` when `n>=4`: Bader--Sauer Theorem A
gives vanishing of degree-two cohomology on the complement of invariant
vectors, while Theorem C and the compact-dual formula give
`H^2(SL_n(Z),C)=0` for `n>=4`. Decomposing a Hilbert representation into
its invariant part and orthogonal complement gives `(AH1)`; for an arbitrary
trivial Hilbert module the scalar vanishing extends coordinatewise, using a
finite presentation's finite-dimensional degree-two scalar cochain data.
See [Bader--Sauer, arXiv:2308.06517v3, Theorems A and C and equation (1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06517v3).
This rank threshold is not being asserted for `SL_3(Z)`.

## 1. The elementary product estimate

Call a sequence `X_j` regular if it is uniformly bounded in normalized
2-norm and is uniformly square-integrable in the sense of `(AH2)`.
Equivalently it is uniformly approximable in 2-norm by sequences bounded
in operator norm. One direction follows by truncating singular values.
For the other, if `||Y_j||_op<=M`, **clipping** the singular values of
`X_j` at height `M` minimizes 2-distance to that operator-norm ball;
hence its distance is no larger than `||X_j-Y_j||_2`. Clipping means
replacing a singular value `t` by `min(t,M)`, not deleting every singular
value above `M`. The hard tail satisfies

```text
||X_j 1_(|X_j|>2M)||_2
 <= 2||X_j-clip_M(X_j)||_2 <= 2||X_j-Y_j||_2.
```

This gives precisely the hard-tail condition used in `(AH2)`.

If `X_j` is regular and `B_j` is uniformly operator-bounded with
`||B_j||_2->0`, then

```text
||X_j B_j||_2 -> 0 and ||B_j X_j||_2 -> 0.                (AH4)
```

Indeed, given `epsilon>0`, choose `Y_j` with operator norm at most a fixed
`M` and `limsup ||X_j-Y_j||_2<epsilon`. If `||B_j||_op<=C`,

```text
||X_j B_j||_2 <= M||B_j||_2+C||X_j-Y_j||_2.
```

Take the limsup and then let epsilon tend to zero. The left-product bound
is identical. Regular sequences are closed under finite sums, adjoints,
and multiplication on either side by arbitrary unitary sequences.

If `b_j` is a regular sequence of skew-Hermitian matrices and `delta_j->0`,
functional calculus also gives

```text
||exp(delta_j b_j)-I-delta_j b_j||_2=o(delta_j).           (AH5)
```

For `|b_j|<=M`, Taylor's estimate divided by delta is bounded by
`delta M ||b_j||_2/2`. On the spectral tail, use
`|exp(it)-1-it|<=2|t|`, giving at most
`2||b_j 1_(|b_j|>M)||_2` after division by delta. Take the limits in that
order. Also `||exp(delta b)-I||_2<=delta||b||_2`.

## 2. Where the genuine Hilbert representation lives

Fix a free ultrafilter omega. Form the Hilbert ultraproduct of
`(M_(d_j),||.||_2)` and let `H_reg` be the closed subspace generated by
sequences uniformly bounded in operator norm. Work, when needed, with its
closed real subspace of skew-Hermitian vectors.

Choose one fixed word `q_g` for every `g in G`, with the empty word for
the identity, and evaluate `u_j(g)=U_j(q_g)`. A generator symbol `s` has
image `bar(s) in G`; we do not require `q_(bar(s))=s`, which could be
inconsistent for redundant generator symbols. Instead the fixed null word
`q_(bar(s))s^(-1)` has some finite defining-relator area `A_s`, so
`||u_j(bar(s))-U_j(s)||_2<=A_s delta_j`. These maps have
`||u_j(g)u_j(h)-u_j(gh)||_2=O_(g,h)(delta_j)`.

Conjugation by `u_j(g)` defines an isometry of `H_reg`. It defines an
**exact representation of G** there. To check multiplication, its error
is conjugation by a unitary `D_j` with `||D_j-I||_2->0`. On an
operator-bounded sequence `X_j`,

```text
||D_j X_j D_j^*-X_j||_2
 <= 2||X_j||_op ||D_j-I||_2 -> 0.
```

Density extends this to all of `H_reg`. This density argument is the
essential repair to the invalid use of the entire Hilbert ultraproduct.

For representatives of vectors in `H_reg`, regularity may initially hold
only along omega. This is sufficient in all arguments below: replace
ordinary limsup by the ultralimit in `(AH2)` and `(AH4)`. A diagonal choice
of representatives, and finally of a subsequence, gives the ordinary
sequence conclusions on the finitely many coordinates that are used.

## 3. The relator errors yield a genuine 2-cocycle

Define

```text
D_j(g,h)=u_j(g)u_j(h)u_j(gh)^*,
c_j(g,h)=(D_j(g,h)-I)/delta_j.
```

For each fixed pair `(g,h)`, the sequence `c_j(g,h)` is regular. The word
`q_g q_h q_(gh)^(-1)` has a fixed finite van Kampen expression as a
product of conjugates of defining relators and inverses. Expanding
`A_1...A_k-I=sum_i A_1...A_(i-1)(A_i-I)` expresses `c_j(g,h)` as a fixed
finite sum of unitary left/right translates of the sequences in `(AH2)`.
No moving word lengths or dimensions occur in this argument.

The ultraproduct class `c(g,h)` is skew-Hermitian, since

```text
c_j(g,h)+c_j(g,h)^*
 = -(D_j(g,h)-I)^*(D_j(g,h)-I)/delta_j -> 0
```

in 2-norm by `(AH4)`: one factor divided by delta is regular, and the
other is operator-bounded and tends to zero in 2-norm.

Associativity gives the exact identity

```text
D_j(g,h)D_j(gh,k)
 = Ad(u_j(g))(D_j(h,k)) D_j(g,hk).
```

Expand each factor as `I+delta_j c_j`, divide by delta, and apply `(AH4)`
to the product terms. In `H_reg` this yields

```text
c(g,h)+c(gh,k)=g.c(h,k)+c(g,hk).                         (AH6)
```

Thus `c` is an actual Hilbert-valued 2-cocycle for the actual representation
constructed in Section 2. By `(AH1)` there is a skew-Hermitian 1-cochain
`b:G->H_reg` with

```text
c(g,h)=b(g)+g.b(h)-b(gh).                               (AH7)
```

Complex cohomological vanishing applies to the real skew-Hermitian module
by complexification and then taking the equivariant real projection.
The normalized cocycle has `c(e,g)=c(g,e)=0`; `(AH7)` then implies `b(e)=0`.

## 4. Correcting the matrices

Choose skew-Hermitian representatives `b_j(g)` of `b(g)`, regular along
omega and bounded in 2-norm on each fixed group coordinate. Put

```text
v_j(g)=exp(-delta_j b_j(g)) u_j(g).
```

By `(AH5)`, expanding the multiplicative error to first order gives

```text
v_j(g)v_j(h)v_j(gh)^*-I
 = delta_j [c_j(g,h)-b_j(g)-Ad(u_j(g))b_j(h)+b_j(gh)]
   +o_omega(delta_j)
```

in normalized 2-norm. To justify every cross term without assuming
submultiplicativity of normalized HS norm, write one exponential difference
divided by delta as a regular sequence: it differs by `o_omega(1)` from
`-b_j(g)`. The other difference is operator-bounded by 2 and tends to zero
in 2-norm. Equation `(AH4)` applies. The same estimate handles products
with `D_j(g,h)-I`.

The bracket has ultralimit zero by `(AH7)`. Therefore the corrected maps
have multiplicative error `o_omega(delta_j)` on each fixed pair. Set
`V_j(s)=v_j(bar(s))` for generator symbols. For each of the finitely many defining
relators, multiplying its fixed finite string of letters now gives
`V_j(r)=I+o_omega(delta_j)`; the inverse letters are justified by the
corrected pair relation at `(s,s^(-1))` and `v_j(e)=I`.

Finally,

```text
||V_j(s)-U_j(s)||_2 <= delta_j (||b_j(bar(s))||_2+A_s).
```

There are finitely many generators, so the constant is finite. A diagonal
subsequence turns the finitely many ultrafilter estimates into `(AH3)`.
This completes the derivation.

## 5. Why this is not the unrestricted stability theorem

The regularity hypothesis is substantive, even for a finite stable group.
For `C_2=<s|s^2=1>`, let `d_j->infinity`, let `P_j` be a rank-one
projection, and take

```text
U_j(s)=I+(i-1)P_j.
U_j(s)^2-I=-2P_j,
delta_j=2/sqrt(d_j),
(U_j(s)^2-I)/delta_j=-sqrt(d_j)P_j.
```

The last sequence has 2-norm one and all its square mass above every fixed
spectral cutoff, so `(AH2)` fails. The supposedly second-order term is
not negligible:

```text
||(U_j(s)^2-I)^2/delta_j||_2=2.
```

Moreover choose coordinates with `P_j=e_(11)` and set
`X_j=sqrt(d_j)e_(12)`. Then `||X_j||_2=1` but

```text
Ad(U_j(s))^2 X_j=-X_j.
```

Thus approximate conjugation does not even define a representation of
`C_2` on the whole Hilbert ultraproduct. It does define one on `H_reg`.
This example is correctable directly and is not a stability counterexample;
it isolates exactly the failed implication in the unqualified
cohomological argument.

Nor is `(AH2)` preserved automatically by repeated improvement. A remainder
can have tiny absolute 2-norm but become concentrated after division by its
new, much smaller defect. Equation `(AH3)` alone does not give exact
subgroup holonomy. To use the moving-Schreier correction theorem one still
needs an exact subgroup representation and a vanishing measured edge
discrepancy. Replacing that representation by the improved approximate
tuple would silently reintroduce the original missing hypothesis.

The next affirmative target suggested by this derivation is a correction
scheme with a controlled spectral-tail ledger throughout its iteration,
or a flexible surgery on the concentrated defect sector. Neither follows
from cohomology vanishing or from ordinary residual finiteness.

## 6. Unconditional removal of the regular component

There is an affirmative statement for **every** normalized-HS approximate
representation, with no `(AH2)` assumption. Suppose `(AH1)` holds and
`delta_j=max_r||U_j(r)-I||_2->0` is positive. After a subsequence, there
are same-dimension generator tuples `V_j` such that

```text
max_s ||V_j(s)-U_j(s)||_2 = O(delta_j),
max_r ||V_j(r)-I||_2 = O(delta_j),
max_r ||V_j(r)-I||_1 = o(delta_j).                       (AH8)
```

Here `||X||_1=tr_d |X|` is also **normalized**. In particular, the same
correction gives `o(delta_j)` defect in each normalized Schatten p-norm
with `1<=p<2`, by interpolation. This is not a claim about the unrestricted
HS defect at its original scale.

To prove this, let `H_all` be the full Hilbert ultraproduct and let
`P_reg:H_all->H_reg` be the orthogonal projection. Each individual
`Ad(u_j(g))` induces a unitary on `H_all`, preserves `H_reg` and its
orthogonal complement, and therefore commutes with `P_reg`. The resulting
action is known to satisfy the group law only on `H_reg`.

The following observation replaces `(AH4)` when projecting to that subspace.
If `A_j,B_j` have 2-norm `O(delta_j)` and uniformly bounded operator norms,
then `A_j B_j/delta_j` is bounded in 2-norm and

```text
P_reg [A_j B_j/delta_j]=0.                              (AH9)
```

Indeed, for every operator-bounded sequence `Y_j`, Cauchy--Schwarz for
normalized trace gives

```text
|tr_d(Y_j^* A_j B_j)|/delta_j
 <= ||Y_j||_op ||A_j||_2 ||B_j||_2/delta_j
 = O(delta_j) -> 0.
```

The bounded-vector classes are dense in `H_reg`. This proves `(AH9)`.

Form the classes `c(g,h)=[(D_j(g,h)-I)/delta_j]` in `H_all` as before;
they are bounded in 2-norm even without `(AH2)`. Set
`c_reg(g,h)=P_reg c(g,h)`. Equation `(AH9)` applied to the unitarity
identity makes `c_reg` skew-Hermitian, and applied to the associativity
identity makes it a genuine 2-cocycle in `H_reg`. Projection commutes with
the adjoint operators, so there is no representation substitution in this
step. Vanishing `(AH1)` supplies a regular skew-Hermitian cochain `b` with
`db=c_reg`.

Correct the matrices by `v_j(g)=exp(-delta_j b_j(g))u_j(g)`. The full
Hilbert-ultraproduct first-order calculation is still valid even though
the original `c_j` need not be regular: in every cross term involving an
original defect, the exponential difference divided by delta **is**
regular, and the original unscaled defect is operator-bounded and tends
to zero in 2-norm. Thus `(AH4)` handles that term. We obtain

```text
[(v_j(g)v_j(h)v_j(gh)^*-I)/delta_j]=c(g,h)-db(g,h),
P_reg [ (v_j(g)v_j(h)v_j(gh)^*-I)/delta_j ]=0.             (AH10)
```

For a 2-norm-bounded sequence `X_j`, its class is orthogonal to `H_reg`
if and only if `lim_omega ||X_j||_1=0`. One direction follows from
`|tr(Y^*X)|<=||Y||_op||X||_1` and density. For the converse, take the
operator-bounded polar partial isometries `W_j` of `X_j`; the pairing
`tr(W_j^*X_j)=||X_j||_1` must have ultralimit zero. Applying this
equivalence to `(AH10)` gives pairwise trace-norm defect
`o_omega(delta_j)`. Telescoping each of the finitely many relator words
gives the third line of `(AH8)`. The first line follows from the exponential
estimate and the second from the original defect and the finite word
lengths. Extracting a subsequence gives `(AH8)` as written.

There is an explicit small-support consequence. Write
`E_(j,r)=V_j(r)-I` and `a_j=max_r||E_(j,r)||_1/delta_j->0`. Choose
positive `t_j->0` with `a_j/t_j->0`, and let

```text
P_j = join_(r in R) 1_(|E_(j,r)|>t_j delta_j),
Q_j = I-P_j.
```

Then

```text
tr_d(P_j) <= |R| a_j/t_j -> 0,
max_r (||E_(j,r) Q_j||_op + ||Q_j E_(j,r)||_op)
 <= 2 t_j delta_j = o(delta_j).                         (AH11)
```

Markov's inequality proves the trace estimate; `Q_j` is below every
spectral cutoff, proving the operator-norm estimates. Each `E_(j,r)` is
normal because it is a unitary minus identity, so the same cutoff works
on both sides. This localizes the residual curvature without assuming
that `P_j` or `Q_j` is invariant under any generator.

That last distinction remains decisive. Compressing the generators to
`Q_j` introduces boundary leakage, and its generic 2-norm bound is of
order `sqrt(tr(P_j))`; `(AH11)` gives no bound making that quantity
`o(delta_j)`. Therefore this concentration theorem does not yet iterate
to flexible stability or supply exact subgroup holonomy. Its concrete
new target is to control the propagation of these residual-curvature
supports under the lattice generators.

## 7. Finite-word halo surgery: a complete quantitative audit

Here is one specific surgery on the support in `(AH11)`. Let `S` include
inverses, let `P` be that support, and let

```text
P_L=join_(|w|<=L) V(w) P V(w)^*,   Q_L=I-P_L.
```

If `alpha=tr(P)` and `B_L` is the number of words of length at most `L`,
then `tr(P_L)<=B_L alpha`. Since `alpha_j->0`, one can choose
`L_j->infinity` sufficiently slowly that `tr(P_(L_j))->0`. This legitimately
removes a vanishing fraction of dimension while surrounding the original
curvature support by an increasingly thick word neighborhood.

However, the two useful geometric facts point in different directions:

```text
V_s P_L V_s^* <= P_(L+1),
V_s Q_(L+1) V_s^* <= Q_L.
```

They provide nested-domain transport, not an action on one retained space.
The compressions `Q_L V_s Q_L` are contractions. Their failure to be
unitary is measured by

```text
b_s^2=||P_L V_s Q_L||_2^2.
```

Their polar parts can be extended to unitaries on `Q_L C^d`, with
2-norm correction at most `b_s`, because for each singular value
`0<=sigma<=1`, `(1-sigma)^2<=1-sigma^2`. But the word relators then
acquire reconnection errors involving those polar choices. A bound on
`tr(P_L)` only makes these errors small in an absolute sense; it does
not make them zero or give a smaller-order defect at scale delta.

The scale loss is realized by an elementary exact example. Use the
presentation `G=<a,b|b=1>`, so `G` is infinite cyclic and has
`H^2(G,H)=0` for every Hilbert module. On `C^d`, let `S_d` be the cyclic
shift, let `P=e_(00)`, and put

```text
V_a=S_d,
V_b=I+(exp(i theta_d)-1)P,
theta_d=d^(-1/2).
```

Then, exactly,

```text
delta_d=||V_b-I||_2=|exp(i theta_d)-1|/sqrt(d) ~ 1/d,
||V_b-I||_1=|exp(i theta_d)-1|/d=delta_d/sqrt(d),
tr(P)=1/d.
```

Thus the tuple already satisfies `(AH8)` and has zero relator error off
its rank-one support. For the symmetric word halo, if `2L+1<d`, `P_L`
is the coordinate interval `{-L,...,L}` on the cycle. The complement is
one nonempty interval. There is one shift edge from that complement into
the deleted interval, hence

```text
||P_L S_d Q_L||_2=1/sqrt(d) >> delta_d.                   (AH12)
```

This holds for every `L` with a nonempty proper halo, including every
slowly growing `L=o(d)`. A unitary on the retained interval must replace
the missing boundary edge; on its boundary basis vector the compressed
shift is zero, so its distance from any unitary completion is at least
`1/sqrt(d)` in the original normalization. Enlarging the halo cannot
reduce this reconnection cost to `O(delta_d)` until essentially the
whole cycle has been removed.

This is **not** a stability counterexample: setting `V_b=I` corrects the
tuple at cost exactly delta. It demonstrates that deleting the support
and reconnecting its word halo discards the small *angle* of the error
and can cost far more than an angle-sensitive correction. The cohomology
condition alone does not repair that loss.

For the arithmetic application the obstruction is stronger than a poor
constant: independently reconnecting generators gives no equality for
the subgroup relators, and therefore no genuine subgroup holonomy.
Property `(T)` only rounds almost invariant corners of a representation
already known to be genuine; it cannot be applied to certify these new
polar reconnections. A viable surgery needs a jointly compatible
reconnection of every defining two-cell, or an angle-sensitive correction
of the concentrated sector. The finite-word halo construction just
audited supplies neither.

## Verification scope

The displayed argument is analytic and was checked symbolically by hand.
No code, build, or numerical computation was run. The published input used
for the arithmetic specialization is the precisely identified
Bader--Sauer statement above; the proof does not transport unnormalized
Frobenius stability into normalized HS stability. This artifact is a
research derivation, not a kernel-checked formal proof.
