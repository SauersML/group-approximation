  Proposition 5.2.1: for the chromatic choice, `<π(g)ξ, ξ> = (Q − 1)^(−n) Chr_Γ(T+,T−)(Q)`.

## Ideas, with kill tests and verdicts

**W1. Almost-L² Jones coefficients ⇒ amenability of F (import: CHH88 + subfactor planar algebras).**
- *Mechanism.* J14 Prop 5.2.3 puts `1_F` in the weak closure of the chromatic representations `π_Q`. If
  `φ_Q(g) = (Q − 1)^(−n) Chr_Γ(g)(Q)` were in `ℓ^(2+ε)(F)` for all large `Q`, then `π_Q ≺ λ` by CHH88, so
  `1_F ≺ λ` and `F` would be amenable. Chromatic zero-free regions (Birkhoff–Lewis-type bounds for
  planar graphs) would supply the estimates, against the `(8+4√3)^n` element count.
- *Kill test.* Does some vacuum coefficient decay?
- **DEAD (pinned).** ABC19 Prop 2.6 plus Theorem 3.9: the vacuum state is shift-invariant, so
  `φ_Q(shift^k g) = φ_Q(g) ≠ 0` along sequences tending to infinity, and no coefficient vanishes at
  infinity. On the graph side, the shift adds a pendant vertex, which multiplies `Chr` by `Q − 1`.

**W2. Parity lamplighter model of Jones' subgroup (import: Juschenko–Monod extensive amenability into
Jones' TQFT subgroups).** Target: amenability of `F`, through ABC19 Remark 4.7.
- *Mechanism.* GS17 Theorem 2 makes `F/\vec F` the orbit of a parity-of-digits set. Its boundary defects
  form a cocycle into `P_f(D)`, so `F/\vec F` should be an affine lamplighter orbit, and JM13's lemma
  would convert extensive amenability of `F ↷ D` into co-amenability of `\vec F`.
- *Kill tests.* (a) Is the defect cocycle finite-valued? (b) Is the affine orbit of `∅` all of `P_f(D)`?
  If not, an invariant mean on `P_f(D)` need not charge the orbit.
- **EXECUTED; both tests pass with complete proofs.** See the execution section.

**W3. Descending Jones chains `F > \vec F ≅ F_3 > \vec F_3 > …` as a co-amenable filtration.**
- *Mechanism.* If each step were co-amenable and the chain "converged", amenability would propagate.
- **DEAD.** Co-amenable descending chains with trivial intersection prove nothing: a residual chain of
  finite-index subgroups of a free group is co-amenable at each step. Only a single co-amenable step
  with an *amenable* bottom group helps, and here the bottom is again `F_n`.

**W4. Chromatic zero-free regions decide stabilizers `F_Ω(t)`.**
ABC19 §5 (Thomassen–Perrett) shows `F_Ω(t)` is trivial for `t = 3, 4` and `t > 4.63`.
- **DEAD as a separator.** A trivial stabilizer says nothing about weak containment, and Theorem 3.9
  already excludes `c_0` coefficients.

**W5. Unimodular cocommutative Hopf algebras in the 2-complex bordism category (import: Bo23) as
Andrews–Curtis invariants beyond 4-thickenings.**
- *Mechanism.* Bo23: the bordism category of 2-dimensional CW complexes is freely generated by a
  unimodular cocommutative Hopf algebra structure on the circle. Symmetric monoidal functors to
  `Vect` are then 3-deformation invariants, and they need not factor through 4-thickenings, which is
  where the BQ05 barrier lives.
- *Kill test.* Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, cocommutative Hopf algebras are
  smash products `U(g) ⋊ k[G]` (Cartier–Gabriel–Kostant; not re-read). The resulting invariants are
  representation-variety counts, which are invariant under Q**-transformations and cannot separate AC
  from Q**.
- **ALIVE (weak), not executed.** The survivor is Hopf algebras in non-Tannakian symmetric tensor
  categories in positive characteristic (Verlinde categories). Next test: do their invariants of
  presentations factor through `π₁` plus `χ`? Not pinned. Lane hl-andrews-curtis owns AC.

**W6. Continuous model theory: definable approximations for Gromov's RF question.**
- **COVERED.** ideas-f-hyperbolic idea 11 (metric-ultraproduct dichotomy, dead by inheritance) and
  ideas-operator (existentially closed hosts, equivalences only).

**W7. Invariant random subgroups and Stuck–Zimmer rigidity for nonsofic hyperbolic groups.**
- **COVERED.** ideas-f-hyperbolic idea 12 (co-sofic IRS from simple approximants: dead).

- **COVERED.** ideas-boone-higman and hl-bh-hard-simple already use the complexity wall.

**W9. Random groups at density as Kervaire–Laudenbach counterexample hosts (import: Gromov density
model).**
- *Mechanism.* A KL failure needs a non-hyperlinear coefficient group. Random groups at density

- *Kill test.* Below density `1/5`–`1/6` random groups are cubulated, hence linear and RF, and KL holds

**W12. Tropical Newton polytopes for units of `Z[P]` (P = Promislow group).**
- *Kill test.* Restrict a unit to `Z³ ≤ P`: it becomes a 4×4 matrix over `Z[Z³]` whose determinant is a

- **DEAD / covered** by ideas-kaplansky 5 and 7.

**W13. Inverted-orbit certificates for non-extensive amenability of `F ↷ D` (import: the JMBMdlS18
probabilistic reformulation).**
- *Mechanism.* Non-extensive amenability of `F ↷ D` would already make `F` nonamenable (Lemma 2.1),
  and by W2 it is also forced by non-co-amenability of `\vec F`. The JMBMdlS18 §4 reformulation turns it
  into decay of lamplighter return probabilities on the dyadic Schreier graph.
- *Kill test.* A rigorous exponential upper bound on those return probabilities is a spectral-radius
  bound of the same difficulty as the unsettled cogrowth numerics for `F`. Mi15 shows the base graph is
  transient, so the recurrence criterion cannot give the positive direction either.
- **ALIVE, hard; the best next lane is its positive half** (see the end of the artifact).

**W14. Growth series D-finiteness for fp groups of intermediate growth.**
- *Kill test.* Rational growth series force polynomial or exponential growth, but no theorem links
  finite presentability to D-finiteness of the growth series.
- **DEAD.**

**W15. Iwasawa-algebra domains for the zero-divisor conjecture (import: p-adic analytic groups).**
- *Mechanism.* A residually-p torsion-free `Γ` embeds `Z[Γ] ⊂ Z_p[[Γ̂_p]]`. If the closure is a
  torsion-free compact p-adic analytic group, the Iwasawa algebra is a domain (Neumann 1988; not
  re-read), so `Z[Γ]` has no zero divisors.
- *Kill test.* Which groups does this cover beyond the known classes (torsion-free elementary amenable by
  Kropholler–Linnell–Moody; groups satisfying Strong Atiyah)? The obstruction is torsion in the
  completion (virtually-uniform torsion-free groups such as P have 2-torsion in their pro-2 closure).
- **ALIVE, pin first.** Not executed; lane ideas-kaplansky-atiyah-2 owns Kaplansky.

## Execution of W2: the parity lamplighter model of Jones' subgroup

**Notation.**
- `D` is the set of dyadic rationals in `(0,1)`. Write `t ∈ D` as `.a_1 … a_m` with `a_m = 1`, and set
  `ℓ(t) = m` and `p(t) = a_1 + … + a_m mod 2`. Put `ℓ(0) = p(0) = 0`.
- `S = {t ∈ D : p(t) = 1}`, the dyadic fractions with odd sums of digits. By GS17 Theorem 2,
  `\vec F = Stab_F(S)`.
- For a binary word `u`, `I_u = [.u, .u + 2^(−|u|))` is a standard interval and `p(u)` is its number of
  ones mod 2. Then `p(.u) = p(u)`, since trailing zeros add no ones.
- An element `g ∈ F` is given by subdivisions `u_1 < … < u_n` and `v_1 < … < v_n` of `[0,1)` into standard
  intervals, with `g(.u_i w) = .v_i w`. `g'_−(t)` is the left derivative.

**Lemma 1 (defect formula).** Put `d_g(t) = p(t) + p(g t)` on `D ∪ {0}`.
- `d_g` is constant on each `I_(u_i)`, with value `p(u_i) + p(v_i)`, and `d_g = 0` near `0`.
- `d_g` changes value at `t ∈ D` (its value on `(t−ε, t)` differs from `d_g(t)`) iff
  `δ_g(t) := ℓ(g t) − ℓ(t) + log₂ g'_−(t)` is odd.
- So `J(g) := {t ∈ D : δ_g(t) odd}` is finite (contained in the leaf endpoints) and
  `d_g(t) = #(J(g) ∩ (0,t]) mod 2`.

*Proof.*
1. For `t = .u_i w` we have `g t = .v_i w`, so `p(t) + p(gt) = p(u_i) + p(v_i)`. The first leaf is `0^k ↦ 0^(k')`.
2. Write `t = .w1` and `g t = .w'1`, with `m = ℓ(t)`, `m' = ℓ(gt)`, `s = log₂ g'_−(t)`. For large `n`, `g` maps
   `[t − 2^(−n), t) = I_(w 0 1^(n−m))` affinely onto `[gt − 2^(s−n), gt) = I_(w' 0 1^(n−s−m'))`.
3. On that interval `d_g = p(w) + p(w') + m + m' + s`, while `d_g(t) = p(w) + p(w')`. So there is a jump iff
   `m + m' + s` is odd. ∎

**Lemma 2 (cocycle).** `J(gh) = J(h) Δ h⁻¹J(g)`.

*Proof.* `d_(gh)(t) = d_h(t) + d_g(ht)`. Since `h` is an increasing bijection of `D`, the jump set of `d_g∘h` is
`h⁻¹J(g)`, and jump sets of mod-2 sums add. ∎

**Lemma 3 (kernel).** `J(g) = ∅ ⇔ g ∈ \vec F`.

*Proof.* `J(g) = ∅` iff `d_g ≡ 0` iff `p(gt) = p(t)` for all `t ∈ D` iff `gS = S` (`g` permutes `D`). By GS17
Theorem 2 the last condition is `g ∈ \vec F`. ∎

**Theorem A (affine model).** Put `J_L(g) = J(g⁻¹)` and `g ⋆ E = gE Δ J_L(g)` on `P_f(D)`.
- `⋆` is an action of `F`. It is the pullback of the affine action `(A,g)·E = A Δ gE` of the permutational
  wreath product `P_f(D) ⋊ F`, along the homomorphism `σ(g) = (J_L(g), g)`.
- The stabilizer of `∅` is `\vec F`, so `g\vec F ↦ J_L(g)` is an `F`-equivariant bijection of `F/\vec F` onto the
  `⋆`-orbit of `∅`.
- `J_L(g)` is the finite set of boundary points of `gS Δ S = {t : d_(g⁻¹)(t) = 1}`.

*Proof.* Lemma 2 gives `J_L(gh) = J_L(g) Δ g J_L(h)`, which is both the action law and the homomorphism law for
`(A,g)(B,h) = (A Δ gB, gh)`. The stabilizer statement is Lemma 3. ∎

**Lemma 4 (copies).** For `f ∈ F` let `f_(1^j)` act as `.1^j x ↦ .1^j f(x)` on `I_(1^j)` and as the identity on
`[0, 1−2^(−j)]`. Then `J(f_(1^j)) = {.1^j y : y ∈ J(f)}`.

*Proof.* For `t = .1^j x` with `x ∈ D`, the depths shift by `j` on both sides and the left derivatives agree, so
`δ_(f_(1^j))(t) = δ_f(x)`. Every `t ≤ 1 − 2^(−j)` is fixed with left derivative 1. ∎

**Base elements.**
- `x_0` has leaves `0 ↦ 00`, `10 ↦ 01`, `11 ↦ 1`, with defects `0, 0, 1`. So `J(x_0) = {3/4}`.
- Lemma 2 applied to `x_0⁻¹x_0` gives `J(x_0⁻¹) = x_0(J(x_0)) = {1/2}`.
- Lemma 4 then gives `b^+_j = (x_0)_(1^j)` with `J = {.1^j11}` and `b^−_j = (x_0⁻¹)_(1^j)` with `J = {.1^j1}`. Both
  are the identity on `[0, c_j]`, `c_j = 1 − 2^(−j)`.
- The parity patterns `(p(c_j), p(point))` are `(j, j)` for `b^+_j` and `(j, j+1)` for `b^−_j`. The four
  elements with `j ∈ {1,2}` realize all four patterns in `(Z/2)²`.

**Lemma 5 (parity words).** Let `L_q` be the set of parity words `p(w_1)…p(w_n)` of all subdivisions of an
`I_u` with `p(u) = q` into standard intervals. Then `L_q = {q} ∪ q(q+1){0,1}*`.

*Proof.*
- **⊆.** If `n ≥ 2`, the first leaf is `u0^r` with `r ≥ 1`, and the next leaf has prefix `u0^(r−1)1`, of parity `q+1`.
- **⊇.** Subdivisions with `n ≥ 2` are concatenations of subdivisions of `I_(u0)` and `I_(u1)`, so
  `L_q = {q} ∪ L_q L_(q+1)`. Induct on the length of `w = q(q+1)x`.
  - Length 2: `w = q·(q+1)`.
  - `w` ends in `q+1`: take `w = y·(q+1)` with `y ∈ L_q` by induction.
  - `w` ends in `q`: take the last `i ≥ 2` with `w_i = q+1` and `w_(i+1) = q`. Then `z = w_i…w_n` starts with
    `(q+1)q`, so `z ∈ L_(q+1)` by induction, and `y = w_1…w_(i−1)` is `q` or starts with `q(q+1)`, so `y ∈ L_q`. ∎

**Lemma 6 (transport).** Let `0 < c < t < 1` and `0 < c' < t' < 1` be dyadic with `p(c) = p(c')` and `p(t) = p(t')`.
Then some `k ∈ \vec F` has `k(c) = c'` and `k(t) = t'`.

*Proof.*
1. For `[a,b)`, every standard subdivision refines the canonical decomposition into maximal standard intervals
   `P_1 < … < P_r`, with parities `q_i`. So the set of parity words is `Λ[a,b) = L_(q_1)⋯L_(q_r)`.
2. `P_1` starts at `a`, so `q_1 = p(a)`.
3. By Lemma 5, `Λ[a,b)` contains `q(q+1)Σ*·q_2⋯q_(r−1)·q_r(q_r+1)Σ*` (for `r = 1`, just `q(q+1)Σ*`).
4. Hence if `p(a) = p(a') = q`, the word `q(q+1)·[q'_2⋯q'_(r'−1) q'_(r')(q'_(r')+1)]·[q_2⋯q_(r−1) q_r(q_r+1)]`,
   suitably shortened when `r` or `r'` is 1, lies in `Λ[a,b) ∩ Λ[a',b')`.
5. Choose common words for `[0,c)`/`[0,c')`, `[c,t)`/`[c',t')` and `[t,1)`/`[t',1)`, and realize them on both sides.
   The concatenations are two leaf subdivisions of `[0,1)` with equal parity words, having `c, t` and `c', t'` at
   the same indices.
6. The map `k(.u_i w) = .v_i w` then has `d_k ≡ 0`, so `k ∈ \vec F` by Lemmas 1 and 3. ∎

**Lemma 7 (localized singletons).** For dyadic `0 < c < t < 1` there is `h ∈ F` that is the identity on `[0,c]`
with `J(h) = {t}`.

*Proof.*
1. Take a base element `b` with `J(b) = {s}`, identity on `[0,c_j]`, and pattern `(p(c), p(t))`.
2. Lemma 6 gives `k ∈ \vec F` with `k(c) = c_j` and `k(t) = s`. Put `h = k⁻¹bk`.
3. `h` is the identity on `[0,c]`.
4. Lemma 2 with `J(k^(±1)) = ∅` gives `J(h) = J(bk) = k⁻¹J(b) = {t}`. ∎

**Theorem B (transitivity).** `J` (hence `J_L`) maps `F` onto `P_f(D)`. So `⋆` is transitive and
`F/\vec F ≅ (P_f(D), ⋆)` as `F`-sets.

*Proof.* Induct on `|E|`, with `E = {t_1 < … < t_k}`.
1. Take `g` with `J(g) = E∖{t_k}`, and a dyadic `c ∈ (t_(k−1), t_k)`.
2. Lemma 7 gives `h` that is the identity on `[0,c]` with `J(h) = {t_k}`.
3. Then `J(gh) = {t_k} Δ h⁻¹J(g) = E`. ∎

**Explicit Schreier graph.** For the generators `x_0` and `x_1 = (x_0)_(1)`:
`x_0 ⋆ E = x_0E Δ {1/2}` and `x_1 ⋆ E = x_1E Δ {3/4}`. So `F/\vec F` is the "parity lamplighter" over the dyadic
action.

**Corollary C1.** If `F ↷ D` is extensively amenable, then `\vec F` is co-amenable in `F`, i.e.
`1_F ≺ λ_(F/\vec F)`. This would be a positive answer to the question in ABC19 Remark 4.7.

*Proof.*
1. `F` is transitive on `D` (Lemma 6 with `F` in place of `\vec F`, forgetting parities).
2. Extensive amenability (JMBMdlS18 Def. 1.1) gives criterion (iv) of JM13 Lemma 3.1, hence (ii): an invariant
   mean on `P_f(D)` for `P_f(D) ⋊ F`.
3. Pulled back along `σ`, it is `⋆`-invariant. By Theorem B it is an `F`-invariant mean on `F/\vec F`. ∎

**Corollary C2.** A negative answer to ABC19 Remark 4.7 makes `F ↷ D` not extensively amenable, and so `F`
nonamenable (JMBMdlS18 Lemma 2.1). The sandwich is
`F amenable ⇒ F ↷ D extensively amenable ⇒ \vec F co-amenable in F`.

**Corollary C3.** Let `A` be any finite union of intervals `[a,b)` with `a ∈ D` and `b ∈ D ∪ {1}`. Then `S Δ (A ∩ D)` is
an `F`-translate of `S`, and its stabilizer is conjugate to `\vec F` (Theorem B applied to the endpoint set of `A`).

**Checks run (local, tiny).** Exact rational implementations of `x_0^(±1)` and `x_1^(±1)` were used.
- **Model.** The jump set of `t ↦ p(g⁻¹t) + p(t)`, computed on a `2^(−10)` grid, agreed with the affine model on
  150 random words of length ≤ 6 (0 mismatches). The generator values were `J_L(x_0) = {1/2}`,
  `J_L(x_0⁻¹) = {3/4}`, `J_L(x_1) = {3/4}`, `J_L(x_1⁻¹) = {7/8}`.
- **Defect formula.** `{t : ℓ(t) + ℓ(g⁻¹t) + log₂ (g⁻¹)'_−(t) odd}`, over all `t` of depth ≤ 10, agreed with the
  model on 40 random words of length ≤ 5 (0 mismatches).
- **Orbit coverage.** The `⋆`-orbit of `∅` within word length 9 has 7853 elements. It contains all 8 subsets of
  `{1/4, 1/2, 3/4}`, all 15 singletons `{k/16}`, and 113 of the 128 subsets of `{k/8}`; the rest need longer
  words. This is consistent with Theorem B, whose proof does not depend on it.

## Exact gap

- **Remark 4.7 is still open.** Is `\vec F` co-amenable in `F`? It stays OPEN (ABC19 Remark 4.7).
- **One direction is reduced.** C1 reduces the positive answer to extensive amenability of the dyadic action
  `F ↷ D`, which is open.
- **No known criterion applies.** That action is transient (Mi15), so the recurrence criterion does not apply,
