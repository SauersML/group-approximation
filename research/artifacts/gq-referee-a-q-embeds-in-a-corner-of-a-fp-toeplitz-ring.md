## The proof
- **Idempotent.** `E = 1 − ST` is idempotent, since `TS = 1`.
- **Shift rule.** `(N+1)S^{m−1} = S^{m−1}(N+m)`.
- **Vacuum.** `NE = 0`, so `(N+m)E = mE`.
- **The key identity.** `r_m·m = E T^{m−1} A (N+1) S^{m−1} E = E`. It uses only the one-sided
  relation `A(N+1) = 1`.
- **Inverses in the corner.** `m` is central, so `m r_m = r_m m = E`. Hence `Q` maps into the corner
  ring `ERE`, whose unit is `E`.
- **The model.** Checked directly on `Q^(N)`:
  - `TS = 1`;
  - `NS f_k = (k+1) f_{k+1} = S(N+1) f_k`;
  - `1 − ST` is the projection onto `f_0`, and `N f_0 = 0`;
  - `A(N+1) = 1`.
  So `E ≠ 0`, and the map `Q → ERE` is injective.

## The Limits remark
Correct, and correctly restricted to the concrete model.
- `S^a E T^b = e_{ab}`, so the ideal generated by `E` is the ring `J` of finitary matrices.
- `J` is simple and has zero left annihilator, and any ideal `M` of the model satisfies
  `MJ ⊆ M ∩ J`. So every nonzero ideal contains `J`, and therefore contains `E`.
- The remark does not claim that no simple quotient of `R_T` itself contains `Q`, and it should not.
