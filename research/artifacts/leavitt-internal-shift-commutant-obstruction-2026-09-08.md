# A fixed internal Leavitt shift obstructs functorial commutant recovery

2026-09-08. Exact ring, group, and finite-trace arguments. No computation
or build. This does not decide hyperlinearity.

An external tensor-square embedding is not needed to produce an extra
commutant. The canonical prefix shift realizes a diagonal copy inside
the original Leavitt group. Every ambient embedding therefore contains
a shifted copy with the obstruction below. The requirement tested is
recovery functorial under this specified internal substitution, not
recovery for just one original pair of subgroups.

## 1. The native shift is an injective endomorphism

Let `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)`, `H=R^x`. For `m>=2`, choose a complete
binary prefix code `alpha_1,...,alpha_m`. Such a code exists for
every positive `m`, by repeatedly splitting one leaf. Its identities
give the ring isomorphism

```text
Phi_m:M_m(R) -> R,
Phi_m((r_ij))=sum_(i,j) s_(alpha_i) r_ij t_(alpha_j).
```

The inverse has entries `t_(alpha_i) r s_(alpha_j)`. Define

```text
sigma_m(r)=Phi_m(diag(r,...,r))
          =sum_i s_(alpha_i) r t_(alpha_i).           (1)
```

This is a unital ring homomorphism, and
`t_(alpha_1) sigma_m(r) s_(alpha_1)=r` proves injectivity.
It restricts to an injective group homomorphism on `H`. For the
two-leaf code, this is the explicit canonical shift

```text
sigma_2(r)=s_0 r t_0+s_1 r t_1.
```

Fix also the four-leaf identification `H~=GL_4(R)=EL_4(R)`, and
the two native commuting subgroups

```text
L={diag(g,1):g in EL_3(R)},
K={diag(I_3,k):k in R^x}.                            (2)
```

Both are copies of the countably infinite simple group `H`, by
[the GL/EL and prefix identifications](../leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group.md)
and [simplicity](../binary-leavitt-elementary-group-is-simple.md).
They commute, and `K` contains nonidentity elements.

## 2. The full group centralizer of the shifted copy

First, `R` is generated as a ring by its units. Indeed, for any
unital ring `S`, every off-diagonal entry `a E_ij` of `M_2(S)`
is `(I+a E_ij)-I`, a difference of units. Products such as
`(a E_12)E_21=a E_11` and `(a E_21)E_12=a E_22` give all
diagonal entries. Thus all of `M_2(S)` is generated by its units.
Use `R~=M_2(R)` to obtain the assertion for `R`.

Consequently an element of `R` commuting with every unit commutes
with all of `R`. The established
[center calculation](../leavitt-center-is-coefficient-field.md)
therefore says that this commutant is exactly `F_2`.

Work in the coordinates of `Phi_m`. A matrix commuting with
every `diag(h,...,h)`, `h in H`, has each entry commuting with
all units of `R`. Its entries lie in `F_2`. A matrix over `F_2`
is invertible over `R` exactly when it is invertible over `F_2`:
if singular over the field, it has a nonzero kernel vector in
`F_2^m`, which remains a nonzero kernel vector over `R`.
Hence, as subgroups of `H`,

```text
C_H(sigma_m(H))=F_m,
F_m=Phi_m(GL_m(F_2)).                               (3)
```

We henceforth use `GL_m(F_2)` and `F_m` interchangeably under
this fixed inclusion. The finite group `F_m` commutes with
`sigma_m(K)`. Their intersection is trivial: a scalar diagonal
matrix `diag(k,...,k)` over `F_2` has `k=1` when it is a unit.
Thus their product is a subgroup isomorphic to

```text
sigma_m(K) F_m ~= K times GL_m(F_2).                 (4)
```

## 3. Fourier coefficients compute the ambient native commutant

Let `N=L(H)`, with its canonical trace, and define subalgebras

```text
N_m=L(sigma_m(H)),
A_m=L(sigma_m(L)),       B_m=L(sigma_m(K)).
```

The group injection makes `N_m` a normal trace-preserving copy
of `N` inside `N`. No implementation by a conjugating unitary
is used.

Every finite conjugation orbit of `sigma_m(H)` in `H` is a
singleton. Otherwise its action on that finite orbit would give
a nontrivial homomorphism from an infinite simple group into a
finite symmetric group, which is impossible. By (3), the
singletons are exactly `F_m`.

A conjugation-invariant vector in `l^2(H)=L^2(N)` has constant
Fourier coefficients on each orbit. Square summability forces
all coefficients on infinite orbits to vanish. Thus

```text
N_m' intersect N=L(F_m).                            (5)
```

This conclusion concerns the commutant of the shifted whole
group. Its finite algebra is included explicitly below, rather
than being ignored as an ambient multiplicity.

## 4. A one-coordinate right operator survives every extension

Let `N subset Q` be any unital trace-preserving inclusion into
a finite tracial von Neumann algebra. Put

```text
C_Q=N_m' intersect Q,
D_Q=B_m join C_Q,
W_m=L(sigma_m(K) F_m).                              (6)
```

The trace-preserving conditional expectation `E_N:Q -> N` is
`N`-bimodular. Therefore (5) gives

```text
E_N(C_Q) subset L(F_m).
```

The algebras `C_Q` and `B_m` commute. On their algebraic product
span, bimodularity implies `E_N(bz)=b E_N(z) in W_m`.
Taking `L^2` closures gives `E_N(D_Q) subset W_m`. Conversely,
both `B_m` and `L(F_m)` already lie in `D_Q intersect N`.
It follows that

```text
D_Q intersect N=W_m.                               (7)
```

Choose any `k in K\{1}` and set

```text
x_m=Phi_m(diag(k,1,...,1)) in H,
X_m=lambda_(x_m) in N.                             (8)
```

Because `k` commutes with `L`, `x_m` commutes with `sigma_m(L)`;
hence `X_m in A_m' intersect Q`.

On the other hand, `x_m` is outside the subgroup in (4). Indeed,
an equality in `GL_m(R)` of the form

```text
diag(k,1,...,1)=diag(h,...,h) T,
h in K,       T in GL_m(F_2),
```

forces every off-diagonal entry of `T` to vanish, since `h` is
a unit. An invertible diagonal matrix over `F_2` is identity,
so `T=I`. As `m>=2`, the second diagonal entry then forces
`h=1`, contradicting the first entry `k!=1`.

Fourier orthogonality therefore gives `E_(W_m)(X_m)=0`.
For every `y in D_Q`, the expectation inclusion above implies

```text
<X_m,y>_2=<X_m,E_N(y)>_2=0.
```

The resulting exact, dimension-free separation is

```text
X_m in A_m' intersect Q,
||X_m||_op=||X_m||_2=1,
E_(D_Q)(X_m)=0,       dist_2(X_m,D_Q)=1.              (9)
```

It also rules out any estimate controlling this distance by
the commutators with a finite generating set of `sigma_m(L)`
and giving zero when those commutators are zero.

## 5. Quantifiers over embeddings and finite word substitutions

For every trace-preserving embedding `pi:N -> Q`, transport
Sections 3--4 through `pi`. The internally shifted representation
is `pi composed with sigma_m`, with native left and right images
`pi(A_m),pi(B_m)`. Formula (9) holds in that same ambient `Q`.
Thus every embedding contains a bad copy under one fixed native
endomorphism; no external tensor-square ambient algebra has to
be added.

The regular character is preserved, since `sigma_m` is injective:
`tau(lambda_(sigma_m(g)))=delta_(g,1)`. Every native word identity
is preserved as well. The abstract character classification and
IRS rigidity apply unchanged to this copy of `H`.

There is also a finite-word interpretation. By the established
[four-generator theorem](../binary-leavitt-units-generated-by-c3-and-c2-cubed.md),
each image under `sigma_m` of a marked source generator has a
finite word representative in the original generators. Evaluating
those words substitutes the shifted copy into the original matrix
dimension. Every fixed kernel word pulls back to another fixed
kernel word. Consequently full canonical microstate sequences
remain full canonical microstate sequences under this substitution.
The substitution need not preserve the literal finite-factor
matrices or their exact laws at each stage; any restoration of
those exact profiles is a separate correction step.

As in the diagonal tensor construction, the algebras in (7)--(9)
are those generated by the limiting represented groups. They are
not being identified with ultraproducts of full finite-stage
generated algebras; growing word lengths would obstruct that
identification without an additional argument.

## 6. The specific recovery requirement this rules out

Suppose a proposed choice of ambient embedding requires the native
left commutant to be recovered from the native right algebra and
the whole-group ambient commutant, and requires that recovery to
persist under the fixed internal substitutions `sigma_m`. No
embedding can satisfy that functorial requirement, by (9).

This differs from requiring the equality only for the original,
unshifted `L,K` in one specially chosen embedding. The theorem
does not rule out that possibility. It does not identify the
shifted and original embeddings by simultaneous conjugacy, or
assume that `sigma_m` is inner or approximately inner.

In particular, existence of an embedding into a matrix tracial
ultraproduct is still an open assumption in this discussion.
The internal obstruction is exact whenever an embedding is given;
it proves no nonhyperlinearity conclusion on its own.
