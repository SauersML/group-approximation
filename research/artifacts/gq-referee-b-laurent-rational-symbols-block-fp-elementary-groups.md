## 1. Symbols block finite presentation (general form)

**Steps. Correct.**
- `E_N(R)` finitely generated forces `R` finitely generated, via the entries of `e_12(r)`.
- `St_N(R)` is then finitely generated. The subring closure uses (R1) and (R2), `N >= 3`.
- B. H. Neumann: a finitely presented quotient of a finitely generated group has a finitely normally generated
  kernel.
- Stabilization sends `K_2(N,R)` into `K_2(R)`, which is central in `St(R)` (Kervaire; Milnor, *Introduction to
  algebraic K-theory*, Theorem 5.1, "K_2 Λ is the center of St Λ"; standard, not re-read). So the image is
  generated by the images of the `k_i`.
- The rank-`N` symbol `h_12(ab)h_12(a)^{-1}h_12(b)^{-1}` maps to `I` exactly when `a` and `b` commute. It
  stabilizes to `{a,b}`.
- P. Hall: an extension of a finitely presented group by a finite group is finitely presented.

**Uses.** `K_2(Q)` is generated by symbols (Matsumoto; classical, not re-read). Its image in `K_2(R)` consists of
symbols of commuting units. **Correct.**

## 3. Tracial harmonic ring, part 4

**Setting of the determinant.**
- Minimality means every σ-invariant probability measure `μ` has full support. So the trace on `A = C(X) ⋊ Z` is
  faithful, and `A` embeds in the group-measure-space algebra `M = L^∞(X,μ) ⋊ Z`.
- Choose `μ` ergodic. It is atomless, since no periodic points means an atom would force infinitely many equal
  masses.
- The action is free, because there are no periodic points.
- So `M` is a II₁ factor (Murray–von Neumann; classical), and so is `M_N(M)` with its normalized trace.

**Fuglede–Kadison.**
- *Determinant theory in finite factors*, Ann. Math. 55 (1952) 520–530. It was cited through a secondary source,
  and I did not read it either. The factor hypothesis of the 1952 theorem is met here.
- `Δ` is multiplicative on invertible elements, with `Δ(λ·1) = |λ|`.
- `Δ(1 + rE_ij) = 1`, since `1 + rE_ij = exp(rE_ij)` and `τ(rE_ij) = 0`. So `Δ` is trivial on `E_N(R_h)`.
- `Δ(λI_N) = |λ|`, so `λI_N ∈ E_N(R_h)` forces `λ = ±1`.
- **Correct.**
