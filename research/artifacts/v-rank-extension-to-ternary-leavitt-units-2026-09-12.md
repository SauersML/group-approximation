## 0. Summary

**Notation.**
- `R = L_(F_3)(1,2)`, `G_3 = R^x`, `z = −1`, `x = 1 + s_00 t_01`, and `G_3 = ⟨V, x⟩`
  (`leavitt-prime-field-units-generated-by-v-and-one-transvection`). Write `p_α = s_α t_α`.
- `E` is the signed diagonal, generated by the reflections `τ_α = 1 − 2 p_α = 1 + p_α`, and `H = E ⋊ V`.
  `ev : F_3[H] -> R` is the evaluation `[g] -> g`.
- For a Sylvester matrix rank function `rk` on an algebra containing `[z]`, put `e_± = (1 ± [z])/2` and
  `β = rk(e_−) = rk(1 − [z])`. The target asks for `β > 0` on `F_3[G_3]`.
- For a clopen `c`, `P_c^± = (1 ± [τ_c])/2 ∈ F_3[E]`. In characteristic three `P_c^− = [τ_c] − 1`.
  For a complete prefix code `C` and signs `ε ∈ {±}^C`, `q_ε = ∏_(c ∈ C) P_c^(ε_c)`.

**Results.**
1. **Reduction** (Section 1). The target holds iff link 1
   (`sylvester-rank-functions-on-ternary-leavitt-units-kill-minus-one`) and the V gate
   (`thompson-v-ternary-rank-functions-are-augmentation`) have the same truth value. It fails iff link 1 holds
   and `F_3[V]` has a detecting rank function. No Hamming-model version of the extension exists for any `V`,
   and averaging from `V` is unavailable.
2. **The evaluation lift dies at order three** (Section 2.2). `X_ev = 1 + ([τ_00] − 1)[w]` has `ev(X_ev) = x`
   and `rk(X_ev^3 − 1) >= β/4` for every rank function on `F_3[H]`.
3. **The Fock lift passes the local relations and dies at splitting** (Sections 2.3–2.4).
   `X_F = 1 + [w] P_00^+ P_01^−` is a unit of order three in `F_3[H]` with `ev(X_F) = x`. It satisfies the sign
   relations `τ_00 x τ_00 = x^(−1) = τ_01 x τ_01` and the support and centralizer relations exactly. It fails the
   splitting relation `x = x_0 x_1`, `x_i = 1 + s_(00i) t_(01i)`, with defect at least `β/16` in every rank
   function on `F_3[H]`.
4. **Reading** (Section 2.5). In every z-moving completion the parities of disjoint cylinders are independent,
   while `ev` sees orthogonal cylinder idempotents. The order relation exposes this at one depth. Conditioning on
   parity patterns repairs one depth and moves the conflict to the depth-changing relation `x = x_0 x_1`.

5. **Unipotent classes** (Section 3). `T(0,1) = 1 + s_0 t_1`, `x` and the Thompson three-cycle `c_3` lie in three
   different conjugacy classes of elements of order three (Lemma 3.1). On the code `{0, 10, 11}`, a product of two
   conjugates of `x` is conjugate to `c_3 ∈ V`, so an extension couples the profile of `σ(x)` to `rk_V`
   (Proposition 3.2). The regular and the natural module both meet these constraints, so they do not pin `σ(x)`.
6. **The intertwiner candidate** (Section 4). `x` is a level root element. The landed level towers model the level
   unit group `L ∋ x` with the Haar law, and single letters intertwine every partial conjugation
   (`level-unit-hnn-extensions-have-anti-central-rank-models`). `X_F` is the depth-two parity shadow of the
   functorial Fock action, and Proposition 2.4 measures the failure of that shadow to refine.
7. **The obstructing relation** (Section 5). Not identified, and no extension is constructed. Monomial lifts die at
   `x^3 = 1` or at splitting. For the intertwiner candidate, an obstruction must be a relation among two or more
   depth-changing letters, placed outside every depth-monotone subalgebra. The weakly finite version is recorded
   as an implication, not decided.

## 1. Reduction

### 1.1 Logical position

- **Known direction.** A rank function on `F_3[G_3]` with `β > 0` restricts to `F_3[H]` with `β > 0`. Its
  restriction to `F_3[V]` is not the augmentation (`signed-thompson-z-moving-rank-functions-match-v-rank-functions`,
  (1) ⟹ (2)), hence detecting (`simple-group-rank-functions-are-augmentation-or-detecting`).
- **Truth table.** Link 1 says no rank function on `F_3[G_3]` has `β > 0`. By the dichotomy, the V gate says no
  rank function on `F_3[V]` is detecting. The known direction is "V gate ⟹ link 1"; the target is
  "link 1 ⟹ V gate". So the target is equivalent to "link 1 ⟺ V gate".
- **Consequences.**
  - The target fails exactly when link 1 holds and `F_3[V]` has a detecting rank function. An unconditional
    refutation would therefore prove link 1 and give `V` a detecting ternary rank function.
  - If the V gate holds, the target holds vacuously. An unconditional proof must construct `β > 0` on
    `F_3[G_3]` from an arbitrary detecting function on `F_3[V]`.
  - Target plus link 1 gives the V gate, which makes `V` nonsofic (Consequences of the transfer node).

### 1.2 No Hamming-model extension, for any V

The normal subgroups of `G_3` are `1`, `⟨z⟩` and `G_3`: `PG_3` is simple and `G_3` is perfect. Let `ρ` be a
homomorphism from `G_3` into a metric ultraproduct of finite symmetric groups. If `ker ρ = 1`, `G_3` is sofic,
against `d-ary-leavitt-groups-nonsofic-over-finite-fields` (`k = F_3`, `d = 2`). If `ker ρ = ⟨z⟩`, `PG_3` is
sofic, against `projective-leavitt-unit-groups-mod-scalars-are-nonsofic`. So `ρ` is trivial.

Hence no nontrivial Hamming model of `V` extends to `G_3`, whether or not `V` is sofic. An extension in the
target must give `x` genuinely linear data, not a permutation profile. This is the Hamming form of Attempt 2 on
the target node.

### 1.3 No averaging from V

Inducing or averaging a rank function over `G_3/V` needs `V` co-amenable in `G_3`. `G_3` has property (T)
(`leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group`), and a co-amenable subgroup of a Kazhdan group has finite index.
But `[G_3 : V]` is infinite: `GL_(2^n)(F_3) <= G_3` meets `V` in `S_(2^n)`, and `[GL_(2^n)(F_3) : S_(2^n)]` is
unbounded.

## 2. Lifts of x into F_3[H]

A *lift* of `x` is an `X ∈ F_3[H]` with `ev(X) = x`. If an extension `σ` of a rank function sent `x` to an
element rank-equal to a lift `X`, then `X` would satisfy every relation of `G_3` in rank. Sections 2.2–2.4 test
the two natural lifts.

### 2.1 Haar values

**Lemma 2.1.** Let `rk` be a Sylvester matrix rank function on `F_3[H]` with `β = rk(e_−)`. Let `C` be a complete
prefix code with `n` cells and `ε ∈ {±}^C` with an odd number of minus signs. Then `rk(q_ε) >= β 2^(1−n)`.

*Proof.* `e_−` is central and `[z] e_− = −e_−`. Put `r(a) = rk(a e_−) <= rk(a)`. On idempotents, `r` is additive
on orthogonal families and invariant under conjugation by units, with `r(e_−) = β`. This is the setting of
`anti-central-rank-models-see-haar-measure-on-signed-diagonal` (its Scope), which gives `r(q_ε) = β 2^(1−n)`.
The argument, for completeness:
- **Even patterns vanish.** `C` partitions the Cantor set, so `∏_(c ∈ C) [τ_c] = [z]` and
  `q_ε [z] = (∏ ε_c) q_ε`. Multiplying by `e_−` gives `(∏ ε_c + 1) q_ε e_− = 0`, so `q_ε e_− = 0` when
  `∏ ε_c = +1`.
- **Invariance.** Any bijection between complete prefix codes of equal size is realized by some `v ∈ V` acting by
  prefix replacement, and `[v][τ_c][v]^(−1) = [τ_(v(c))]`. So `r(q_ε) = r_n(k)` depends only on `n` and the number
  `k` of minus signs.
- **Refinement.** `[τ_c] = [τ_(c0)][τ_(c1)]` gives `P_c^− = P_(c0)^+ P_(c1)^− + P_(c0)^− P_(c1)^+` and
  `P_c^+ = P_(c0)^+ P_(c1)^+ + P_(c0)^− P_(c1)^−`. Splitting a minus cell gives `r_n(k) = 2 r_(n+1)(k)` for odd
  `k <= n`. Splitting the plus cell of a pattern with `n − 1` minus signs gives
  `r_n(n−1) = r_(n+1)(n−1) + r_(n+1)(n+1)`.
- **Induction.** `r_1(1) = r(P_X^−) = r(e_−) = β`, so `r_n(k) = β 2^(1−n)` for all odd `k`. ∎

### 2.2 The evaluation lift

Let `w ∈ V` exchange `00a <-> 01a` and fix `1`. In `R`, `p_00 w = s_00 t_01`, and
`ev(P_00^−) = τ_00 − 1 = p_00`. So `X_ev := 1 + P_00^− [w]` is a lift of `x`.

**Proposition 2.2.** `X_ev^3 = 1 + P_00^− P_01^− [w]`. Hence `rk(X_ev^3 − 1) = rk(P_00^− P_01^−) >= β/4`.

*Proof.* Put `N = P_00^− [w]`. Since `w = w^(−1)` and `w τ_00 w = τ_01`, `[w] P_00^− [w] = P_01^−`. So
`N^2 = P_00^− P_01^−` and `N^3 = P_00^− P_01^− P_00^− [w] = P_00^− P_01^− [w]`, as `F_3[E]` is commutative.
In characteristic three `(1 + N)^3 = 1 + N^3`. `[w]` is a unit, and on the code `{00, 01, 1}`,
`P_00^− P_01^− = q_(−,−,+) + q_(−,−,−)`. Lemma 2.1 gives `rk >= rk(q_(−,−,−)) >= β/4`. ∎

**Where it dies.** In `R`, `ev(N^2) = p_00 w p_00 w = p_00 p_01 = 0`: disjoint cylinders carry orthogonal
idempotents. In every z-moving completion the parities of `00` and `01` are independent (Lemma 2.1), and
"both minus" has rank at least `β/4`. The order relation `x^3 = 1` sees the conflict.

### 2.3 The Fock lift

Put `e_(+−) = P_00^+ P_01^−`, `N_F = [w] e_(+−)` and `X_F = 1 + N_F`.

**Proposition 2.3.** In `F_3[H]`:
- **(a)** `ev(X_F) = x`.
- **(b)** `N_F^2 = 0`, so `X_F` is a unit of order three with `X_F^(−1) = 1 − N_F`.
- **(c)** `[τ_00] X_F [τ_00] = X_F^(−1) = [τ_01] X_F [τ_01]`, matching `τ_00 x τ_00 = x^(−1) = τ_01 x τ_01` in `R`.
- **(d)** `X_F` commutes with `[τ_c]` whenever `c ∩ (00 ∪ 01) = ∅` or `c ⊇ 00 ∪ 01`. It also commutes with `[v]`
  for every `v ∈ V` with `v(00a) = 00u(a)` and `v(01a) = 01u(a)` for one `u` (arbitrary on `1`). In `R`, `x`
  commutes with the same elements.

*Proof.*
- **(a)** `ev(P_00^+) = −(1 + τ_00) = 1 − p_00` and `ev(P_01^−) = p_01`, so `ev(e_(+−)) = p_01` and
  `ev(N_F) = w p_01 = s_00 t_01`.
- **(b)** `e_(+−) [w] = [w] e_(−+)` with `e_(−+) = P_00^− P_01^+`. So `N_F^2 = [w]^2 e_(−+) e_(+−) = 0`, and
  `(1 + N_F)(1 − N_F) = 1`, `(1 + N_F)^3 = 1 + 3 N_F = 1`.
- **(c)** `[τ_00][w] = [w][τ_01]`, `[τ_01] e_(+−) = −e_(+−)` and `e_(+−) [τ_00] = e_(+−)`, so
  `[τ_00] N_F [τ_00] = −N_F`. Symmetrically `[τ_01][w] = [w][τ_00]` and `[τ_00] e_(+−) [τ_01] = −e_(+−)`. In `R`,
  `(1 + p_00) s_00 t_01 (1 + p_00) = 2 s_00 t_01 = −s_00 t_01`, and likewise for `τ_01`.
- **(d)** These `[τ_c]` and `[v]` commute with `[w]` and fix `[τ_00]`, `[τ_01]` under conjugation. In `R`,
  `p_c s_00 t_01 p_c` is `s_00 t_01` or `0` with `p_c s_00 t_01 = s_00 t_01 p_c`. For `v`,
  `v s_00 = s_00 ū` and `t_01 v^(−1) = ū^(−1) t_01` with `ū = t_00 v s_00 ∈ V`. ∎

### 2.4 Splitting

For `i ∈ {0, 1}` choose `v_i ∈ V` with `v_i(00a) = 00ia` and `v_i(01a) = 01ia`. In `R`,
`x_i := v_i x v_i^(−1) = 1 + s_(00i) t_(01i)`, `x_0 x_1 = x_1 x_0`, and

```text
x = x_0 x_1     (splitting)
```

because `s_000 t_010 + s_001 t_011 = s_00 (s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1) t_01` and the cross terms vanish. An extension `σ` of
a rank function on `F_3[V]` must send `x_i` to `σ(v_i) σ(x) σ(v_i)^(−1)`. So a lift `X` forces
`X_i = [v_i] X [v_i]^(−1)` and must satisfy `X = X_0 X_1` in rank.

For `X_F`, `X_i = 1 + [w_i] f_i`, where `w_i = v_i w v_i^(−1)` exchanges `00ia <-> 01ia` and fixes the rest, and
`f_i = P_(00i)^+ P_(01i)^−`. Neither depends on the choice of `v_i`.

**Proposition 2.4.** `D := X_F − X_0 X_1` lies in `ker(ev)`, and `rk(D) >= β/16` for every Sylvester matrix rank
function on `F_3[H]`.

*Proof.* `ev(D) = x − x_0 x_1 = 0`. `w_1` fixes `000` and `010` pointwise, so `f_0 [w_1] = [w_1] f_0`, and
`w_0 w_1 = w`. Hence

```text
X_0 X_1 = 1 + [w_0] f_0 + [w_1] f_1 + [w] f_0 f_1,
D       = [w] (e_(+−) − f_0 f_1) − [w_0] f_0 − [w_1] f_1.
```

Let `g = P_000^+ P_001^− P_010^− P_011^+`. Since `[τ_00] = [τ_000][τ_001]` acts on `g` by `−1`, `e_(+−) g = 0`. The
factor `P_001^−` gives `f_1 g = 0`, and `f_0 g = g`. So `D g = −[w_0] g`, and

```text
rk(D) >= rk(D g) = rk(g) >= rk(q_(+,−,−,+,−)) >= β/16
```

by Lemma 2.1 on the code `{000, 001, 010, 011, 1}`. ∎

### 2.5 Reading

- **Two lifts of one particle.** Both lifts turn the one-particle operator `s_00 t_01` into parity data. `X_ev`
  uses the minus projection of `00` alone, and the independence of disjoint parities breaks the order relation.
  `X_F` conditions on the pattern `(+, −)` and moves the whole subtree `01` onto `00`. That is one fermionic mode
  per cylinder: exact at one depth, sign-covariant, of order three.
- **Why splitting.** Splitting refines `00 = 000 ∪ 001`. The parity of `00` is a product of two independent
  parities, and "move one mode" is not a monomial move of whole subtrees at the finer depth. The defect `β/16`
  is carried by the pattern `(+, −, −, +)`, where the coarse lift does nothing and the fine lift moves `010`
  onto `000`.
- **What is tested.** Among the relations of `⟨V, x⟩`, monomial lifts die at the order relation (`X_ev`) and at
  the depth-changing relation `x = x_0 x_1` (`X_F`). `X_F` satisfies the sign, support and centralizer relations
  exactly.
- **Not claimed.** That every lift fails, or that `σ(x)` must be rank-equal to an element of `F_3[H]`. An
  extension may send `x` outside the closure of `F_3[H]`, as the functorial Fock action does (Section 4).

## 3. Unipotent classes

An extension must choose a profile for `[x]`. Put `N_g = [g] − 1` for `g` of order three, so `N_g^3 = 0` in
`F_3[G_3]`. The profile of `g` is `(j_1, j_2) = (rk N_g, rk N_g^2)`, with `2 j_2 <= j_1` (Frobenius inequality). It is a
class function.

### 3.1 Three classes

For `g ∈ G_3` of order three put `n_g = g − 1 ∈ R`, acting on `R_R` by left multiplication.

**Lemma 3.1.**
- **(a)** `n_(T(0,1)) = s_0 t_1` has `n^2 = 0` and `ker n = im n = p_0 R`.
- **(b)** `n_x = s_00 t_01` has `n^2 = 0`, `im n = p_00 R`, `ker n = (p_00 + p_1) R`, and `ker n / im n ≅ p_1 R ≅ R`.
- **(c)** Let `c_3 ∈ V` be the prefix replacement `0 -> 10 -> 11 -> 0`. Then `n_(c_3)^2 = 1 + c_3 + c_3^2 ≠ 0`.

So `T(0,1)`, `x` and `c_3` lie in three different conjugacy classes of `G_3`.

*Proof.*
- **(a)** `t_0 s_0 = 1`, so `s_0 t_1 r = 0` iff `t_1 r = 0` iff `r = p_0 r`. And `s_0 t_1 R = s_0 R = p_0 R`.
- **(b)** In the same way `ker n = (1 − p_01) R = (p_00 + p_1) R` and `im n = s_00 R = p_00 R`. The quotient is
  `p_1 R`, and `t_1 : p_1 R -> R` is an isomorphism.
- **(c)** On the code `{0, 10, 11}`, `c_3` is a permutation matrix in `M_3(F_3) ⊂ R`, and `1 + c_3 + c_3^2` is the
  all-ones matrix.
- **Classes.** If `g' = u g u^(−1)`, then `ker n_(g') = u ker n_g` and `im n_(g') = u im n_g`, so left multiplication by
  `u` identifies the quotients. Square-zero is also invariant. ∎

At level `n >= 2`, `T(0,1)` has Jordan type `2^(2^(n−1))`, and `x` has type `2^(2^(n−2)) 1^(2^(n−1))`. Lemma 3.1
records the level-free form of this distinction.

### 3.2 The Heisenberg configuration

The code `{0, 10, 11}` gives a unital embedding `M_3(F_3) -> R`, `e_(αβ) -> s_α t_β`. So
`UT_3(F_3) ⊂ GL_3(F_3) ⊂ G_3`, generated by `T(0,10)`, `T(10,11)` and `T(0,11)`, where `T(α,β) = 1 + s_α t_β`.

**Proposition 3.2.** Among the 26 nonidentity elements of `UT_3(F_3) ⊂ G_3`:
- the 14 with `(u − 1)^2 = 0` are conjugate in `G_3` to `x`;
- the 12 with `(u − 1)^2 ≠ 0` are conjugate in `G_3` to `c_3 ∈ V`.

In particular `T(0,10) T(10,11) = 1 + e_(0,10) + e_(10,11) + e_(0,11)` is conjugate to `c_3`, while both factors are
conjugate to `x`. Every extension therefore satisfies `rk(N_(T(0,10) T(10,11))^k) = rk_V(N_(c_3)^k)` for `k = 1, 2`.

*Proof.* Write `u − 1` with entries `a, b, c` at positions `(1,2), (1,3), (2,3)`. Then `(u − 1)^2` has the single
entry `ac` at `(1,3)`, which gives the counts `26 − 12 = 14` and `2·2·3 = 12`.
- **Square-zero.** All rank-one square-zero matrices are `GL_3(F_3)`-conjugate to `T(0,10)`. Take `v ∈ V` with
  `00 -> 0`, `01 -> 10`, `1 -> 11`. Then `v s_00 = s_0`, `t_01 v^(−1) = t_10`, and `v x v^(−1) = T(0,10)`.
- **Regular.** The rest are single Jordan blocks of size three. `c_3` is a permutation matrix with minimal
  polynomial `(X − 1)^3`, so it is `GL_3(F_3)`-conjugate to each of them. ∎

### 3.3 What the constraints allow

- **Regular module.** `F_3[UT_3(F_3) × ⟨z⟩]` acting on itself gives every nonidentity element of order three the
  free profile `(2/3, 1/3)`.
- **Natural module.** `F_3^3`, with `z` acting by `−1`, gives the 12 regular elements the free profile and the 14
  square-zero elements the profile `(1/3, 0)`.
- **Reading.** If `rk_V` is free on `⟨c_3⟩`, both finite functions meet Proposition 3.2, with different profiles for
  `x`. So the finite unipotent data neither pin `σ(x)` nor obstruct it. This matches the free nilpotent data noted
  on `anti-central-weakly-finite-images-carry-rank-functions`.
- **Firewall.** `z`, `T(0,10)` and `T(10,11)` are depth-monotone (`|σ| <= |κ|`), so this configuration is covered by
  `depth-monotone-configurations-cannot-force-ternary-minus-one`. An obstruction must leave it.

## 4. The intertwiner candidate

### 4.1 Level towers

`x = 1 + e_(00,01)` is a level root element, so `x ∈ L = ∪_j GL_(2^j)(F_3)`. Two landed towers model `L`:
- `σ_reg`, left multiplication on `F_3[GL_(2^j)(F_3)] ε_−`: injective, anti-central, free on finite subgroups containing
  `z` (`level-unit-hnn-extensions-have-anti-central-rank-models`, item 1);
- the Fock tower `Λ_j` on `Λ(F_3^(2^j))`: injective, asymptotically free, with the Haar law on `E`
  (`exterior-tensor-powers-are-asymptotically-free`).

Both satisfy every relation of `L` exactly: `x^3 = 1`, the splitting `x = x_0 x_1` (a level-three identity), and the
sign and centralizer relations of Proposition 2.3 within `L`.

### 4.2 The Fock lift is the parity shadow of Λ

Extend `Λ_j` linearly to the group algebra of the signed permutation matrices of level `j`. In `Λ_j`, `Λ_j(τ_c)`
multiplies a wedge monomial `e_S` by `(−1)^|S ∩ c|`, so `Λ_j(P_c^−)` projects onto the monomials with `|S ∩ c|` odd.

**Proposition 4.1.**
- **(a)** `Λ_2(X_F) = Λ_2(x)` on `Λ(F_3^4)`.
- **(b)** `Λ_3(X_0 X_1) = Λ_3(x)` on `Λ(F_3^8)`, but `Λ_3(X_F) ≠ Λ_3(x)`.

*Proof.*
- **(a)** `Λ_2(x) e_S = e_S` unless `01 ∈ S` and `00 ∉ S`. In that case it adds `e_S` with `e_01` replaced by `e_00`, in the
  same position, so with no sign. That is `Λ_2([w] P_00^+ P_01^−) e_S`.
- **(b)** The same computation at the modes `00i`, `01i` gives `Λ_3(X_i) = Λ_3(x_i)`, and `Λ_3` is multiplicative. For
  `S = {001, 010}`, `|S ∩ 00| = 1` is odd, so `Λ_3(X_F) e_S = e_S`. But `Λ_3(x) e_S = e_S + e_001 ∧ e_000`. ∎

So the Fock action is `X_F` at depth two and `X_0 X_1` at depth three. Proposition 2.4 says that no single element of
`F_3[H]` of the form `X_F` does both, and the pattern `S = {001, 010}` is the pattern `(+, −, −, +)` carrying `β/16`.
The tower's `σ(x)` exists only as a limit over depths.

### 4.3 What the candidate supplies and lacks

**Supplies.**
- Every relation of `L`, with `z` moved.
- For each `v ∈ V` separately, a unit `X_v` with `X_v σ_reg(a) X_v^(−1) = σ_reg(v a v^(−1))` for all `a ∈ B_v`
  (item 2 of the HNN node). With `v = v_i` and `a = x`, this is the splitting data of Section 2.4.
- A model of the multiple HNN extension of `L` along all of `V`, which maps onto `G_3` (item 3), including the orders
  of torsion letters such as `c_3` (item 4).
- Commutation of disjointly supported letters (`fock-local-letters-extend-level-models-with-disjoint-commutation`).

**Lacks.**
- **V's multiplication table.** `X_v X_(v') = X_(v v')` for depth-changing `v, v'`, with each factor an intertwiner.
- **Non-level Steinberg commutators.** The natural Fock compressors miss the Toeplitz commutator
  `[x_12(t_0), x_23(s_0)] = x_13(1)` by exactly `1/4` at every level
  (`natural-fock-quantized-compressors-miss-toeplitz-by-a-quarter`).

**What the target must add.** The hypothesis gives a detecting `rk_V`, which respects V's multiplication table. The
target needs this together with intertwining on every `B_v` at once. Neither tower uses `rk_V`, and no landed node
turns `rk_V` into such intertwiners.

## 5. The obstructing relation

### 5.1 Monomial lifts

- `X_ev` dies at `x^3 = 1`, with defect at least `β/4` (Proposition 2.2).
- `X_F` dies at the splitting `x = (v_0 x v_0^(−1))(v_1 x v_1^(−1))`, with defect at least `β/16` (Proposition 2.4).
- In both cases the defect sits on a parity pattern that `ev` sends to `0`.
- **Splitting kills lifts, not extensions.** Since `x ∈ B_(v_i)`, splitting is a single-letter partial conjugation, and
  `σ_reg` with the letters `X_(v_i)` satisfies it (Section 4.3).

### 5.2 Where an obstructing relation must live

**Corollary 5.1.** Suppose `F_3[V]` has a detecting rank function. Then the target fails iff link 1 holds. So, under
this hypothesis, a relation of `G_3` that kills every extension is exactly an argument for link 1.

*Proof.* Under the hypothesis, the conclusion of the target is "some rank function on `F_3[G_3]` has `β > 0`", which
is the negation of link 1 (Section 1.1). ∎

By the landed firewalls, such an argument must:
- use at least two letters outside `L`, since the multiple HNN extension of `L` has models with `β > 0`
  (`level-unit-hnn-extensions-have-anti-central-rank-models`);
- use more than commutation of disjointly supported letters
  (`fock-local-letters-extend-level-models-with-disjoint-commutation`);
- involve group elements that generate a nonsofic subgroup, since every sofic subgroup containing `z` carries an
  anti-central model (`anti-central-sofic-data-cannot-force-defect-submultiplicativity`). This excludes every
  depth-monotone configuration (`depth-monotone-configurations-cannot-force-ternary-minus-one`), including the
  Heisenberg configuration of Section 3.

**First candidates** (not tested here):
- the four transvections `T(0,1), T(1,0), T(1,00), T(00,1)`, which lie in no depth-monotone unit group;
- the Toeplitz commutator `[x_12(t_0), x_23(s_0)] = x_13(1)` between two non-level root elements.

### 5.3 The cocycle form of the gap

Fix `σ_reg : L -> M^x`. For `v, v' ∈ V`, put `B_(v,v') = {a ∈ B_(v') : v' a v'^(−1) ∈ B_v} ⊆ B_(v v')`.
- **Choice.** If `X_v` and `X'_v` both intertwine on `B_v`, then `X_v^(−1) X'_v` commutes with `σ_reg(B_v)`.
- **Defect.** For intertwiners `X_v`, `X_(v')` and `X_(v v')`, the unit `c(v, v') = X_(v v')^(−1) X_v X_(v')` commutes with
  `σ_reg(B_(v,v'))`.
- **Question.** For this candidate, the extension asks whether the `X_v` can be changed within these commutants so
  that `c(v, v') = 1` in rank for all `v, v'`, compatibly with the relations of `G_3` that mix `V` and `L`.

Not claimed: that `c` is ever essentially nontrivial, or that a completion of this form is necessary.

### 5.4 Weakly finite version

**Proposition 5.2.** Let `ρ : F_3[G_3] -> W` be a unital ring map into a weakly finite `F_3`-algebra that is injective
on `V`. Then either:
- `ρ([z]) ≠ 1`, and the image of `S_− = ε_− F_3[G_3]` in the corner `ρ(ε_−) W ρ(ε_−)` is a nonzero weakly finite
  quotient of `S_−`, which refutes `ternary-anti-central-summand-has-no-weakly-finite-image`; or
- `ρ([z]) = 1`, and `ρ` factors through `F_3[PG_3]`, injective on `PG_3`.

*Proof.* The kernel of `G_3 -> W^x` is normal and misses `V`, so it is `1` or `⟨z⟩`.
- **Kernel 1.** `u = ρ([z]) ≠ 1` and `u^2 = 1`, so `ρ(ε_−) = (1 − u)/2 ≠ 0`. This idempotent commutes with `ρ(F_3[G_3])`.
  So `s -> ρ(s)` maps `S_−` unitally into the corner.
  - **Corners are weakly finite.** If `AB = e 1_n` over `eWe`, then `A + (1 − e) 1_n` and `B + (1 − e) 1_n` multiply to
    `1_n`.
  - **Unital subrings are weakly finite.** A one-sided inverse over the subring is a two-sided inverse over the ring,
    so it is a two-sided inverse over the subring.
- **Kernel `⟨z⟩`.** Immediate. ∎

**Status.**
- An extension of a nontrivial weakly finite representation of `V` exists only if `S_−` has a nonzero weakly finite
  quotient, or `F_3[PG_3]` has a weakly finite representation injective on `PG_3`.
- Whether `V` has such a representation at all is open. It would follow from linear soficity of `V`, by
  `linear-sofic-group-algebra-is-stably-finite`.
- Depth-monotone configurations have weakly finite images (`depth-monotone-configurations-cannot-force-ternary-minus-one`),
  so a weakly finite obstruction also needs non-monotone configurations.
- Not decided here.
