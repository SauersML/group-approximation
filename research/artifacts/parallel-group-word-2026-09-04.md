# Parallel affine-Leavitt investigation: test the atom-matching decoder

This is a finite-matrix diagnostic, not a nonhyperlinearity proof. The
unconditional root remains open.

The concrete affirmative operation investigated here was the following:
exactify the commuting row-root operators, then optimize over permutations
and phases of their joint spectral atoms to turn the paired Steinberg
shears into finite coefficient maps. The joint atom decomposition of the
full infinite coefficient-root family would use finiteness of the ambient
matrix algebra. A fixed finite root table, in contrast, has a finite
spectral decomposition in a finite factor as well. Only the former is a
possible source of the genuinely finite-matrix step missing from
`affine-leavitt-boundary-shear-payment`; the lemmas below do not establish
the passage to that full family.

The calculation below proves that local root covariance alone cannot
authenticate this operation with a constant independent of the number of
root coordinates. It gives an exact formula for the optimization over the
*entire* normalizer, rather than merely showing that one proposed matching
fails.

## An exact growing-window countermodel

Fix the native odd characteristic `p>=5`, write
`zeta=exp(2 pi i/p)`, and put

```text
Z=diag(1,zeta,...,zeta^(p-1)).
```

For `0<theta<pi/4`, let `R(theta)` be the real rotation

```text
[ cos(theta)  -sin(theta) ]
[ sin(theta)   cos(theta) ]
```

on the first two coordinates, and the identity on the other `p-2`.
On `H_n=(C^p)^(tensor n)`, with `d=p^n`, define

```text
V_n=R(theta)^(tensor n),
Z_j=I tensor ... tensor Z tensor ... tensor I,
D_n=the full diagonal algebra generated by Z_1,...,Z_n.
```

All the root clocks commute exactly and have exact order `p`. The entire
additive root table is exact. Its normalized trace is canonical:

```text
tr_d(product_j Z_j^(a_j))=0 unless every a_j=0 in F_p.
```

For `a!=0`, direct multiplication of the two-by-two block gives

```text
||[R(theta),Z^a]||_(2,p)^2
  = (2/p) |1-zeta^a|^2 sin(theta)^2.                  (PGA1)
```

Consequently every root word supported on at most `k` coordinates obeys

```text
||[V_n,product_j Z_j^(a_j)]||_(2,d)
  <= k sqrt(8/p) sin(theta).                         (PGA2)
```

This follows by telescoping the tensor factors and using invariance of
normalized HS norm under tensoring with unitaries. In particular it holds
uniformly over every choice of the supported coordinates and exponents.

## Optimize over every joint-atom matching

The unitary normalizer `N(D_n)` consists exactly of monomial matrices:
arbitrary permutations of the `d` atoms with arbitrary phases. For any
unitary `V`, optimizing the phases after a permutation gives

```text
min_(W in N(D_n)) ||V-W||_2^2
 = 2 - (2/d) max_pi sum_x |V_(x,pi(x))|.              (PGA3)
```

For the tensor rotation above, every row has its largest absolute entry
on the diagonal: `cos(theta)>sin(theta)`, and every unchanged one-site
coordinate has only its diagonal entry. The identity permutation attains
all these row maxima simultaneously. Therefore `(PGA3)` evaluates exactly:

```text
dist_2(V_n,N(D_n))^2
 = 2 - 2 ((p-2+2 cos(theta))/p)^n.                    (PGA4)
```

Take `theta=theta_n=n^(-1/4)` for sufficiently large `n`. For each fixed
`k`, `(PGA2)` tends to zero, while

```text
((p-2+2 cos(theta_n))/p)^n
 = exp(-(1/p+o(1)) sqrt(n)) -> 0.
```

Thus the optimized normalizer distance in `(PGA4)` tends to `sqrt(2)`.
Even arbitrary choices of joint atom permutations and phases do not repair
the covariance at vanishing HS cost.

## The same failure is visible in the full joint PVM

Let `E_n` be the orthogonal projection from `M_d` to `D_n`, and let
`Ad(V_n)(T)=V_n T V_n^*`. Using the unnormalized operator-space trace,

```text
Tr_super(E_n)=d,

Tr_super(E_n Ad(V_n) E_n Ad(V_n)^*) / d
 = (1/d) sum_(x,y) |(V_n)_(x,y)|^4
 = (1 - sin(2 theta)^2/p)^n.                         (PGA5)
```

The one-site sum is `p-4 cos(theta)^2 sin(theta)^2`; tensoring multiplies
these sums. With the same choice `theta_n`, the relative overlap in
`(PGA5)` tends to zero, although each bounded-support root covariance tends
to zero. The normalization by `d` is intentional: the diagonal Reynolds
projection has normalized supertrace `1/d`, so its absolute supertrace
would hide this failure in a vanishing-dimensional sector.

## A sufficient average test, with optimized normalizer rounding

There is a positive dimension-independent statement once covariance is
tested on the whole root character table. In fact, it is enough to test
placement in the old commutative algebra, without prescribing an atom
matching in advance.

Let `H` be a finite abelian group of order `q`. Let its exact representation
on `C^(qm)` be `m` copies of the regular representation, so its character
projections `P_x` all have rank `m`. Write

```text
A=span{P_x : x in dual(H)},
U_h=sum_x x(h) P_x,
E_A=the normalized-trace-preserving expectation onto A.
```

For every unitary `V`, define the average placement loss

```text
L(V)=(1/q) sum_(h in H) ||V U_h V^* - E_A(V U_h V^*)||_2^2.
```

Then

```text
dist_2(V,N(A))^2 <= 2 L(V).                            (PGA6)
```

Here `N(A)` permits an arbitrary permutation of the `q` equal-dimensional
atoms and an arbitrary unitary on each matched multiplicity block. Thus
`(PGA6)` is an actual optimized joint-atom return theorem, including
spectator multiplicities, and its constant is independent of `q,m`.

To prove it, set

```text
B_(x,y)=||P_x V P_y||_F^2/m.
```

The matrix `B` is doubly stochastic. Character orthogonality and the
formula for `E_A` give the exact identity

```text
L(V)=1-(1/q) sum_(x,y) B_(x,y)^2.                     (PGA7)
```

By the finite doubly-stochastic decomposition, some permutation `pi`
satisfies

```text
sum_x B_(x,pi(x)) >= sum_(x,y) B_(x,y)^2.
```

For each matched block `P_x V P_(pi(x))`, choose a unitary polar extension
between its two `m`-dimensional spaces. These blocks assemble into one
normalizing unitary `W`. The singular values of each compressed block lie
in `[0,1]`, so its trace norm is at least its squared Frobenius norm.
Consequently

```text
Re tr_(qm)(W^* V)
 >= (1/q) sum_x B_(x,pi(x))
 >= 1-L(V).
```

Expanding `||V-W||_2^2` proves `(PGA6)`.

For the product-rotation example, `m=1` and character orthogonality turns
`(PGA5)` into

```text
L(V_n)=1-(1-sin(2 theta_n)^2/p)^n -> 1.               (PGA8)
```

Thus the sufficient average test is maximally violated while every
bounded-support test tends to zero. The missing coercivity has a precise
form: derive `L(V)=o(1)` from the actual paired-Steinberg relator ledger on
one fixed returned root algebra. Once that implication is available,
arbitrary atom permutations and multiplicity unitaries are handled by
`(PGA6)`; their optimization is not an additional open step.

If one has a prescribed automorphism `alpha` of `H` and instead knows

```text
(1/q) sum_h ||V U_h V^* - U_(alpha(h))||_2^2 <= epsilon^2,
```

there is a sharper direct bound `dist_2(V,N(A))<=epsilon`. Choose a
normalizer implementing `alpha`, divide `V` by it, and average conjugation
by `H` onto the block-diagonal commutant `A'`. The average squared
commutator is twice the squared distance to that commutant. Taking a
unitary polar extension in every diagonal block costs at most twice that
squared distance. This prescribed-return variant must not be confused
with `(PGA6)`: its average tests covariance, whereas `(PGA6)` tests only
placement and chooses the matching afterward.

## What this changes in the decoder search

The follow-up derivation now supplies one concrete coercive hypothesis:
`one-abelian-anchor-and-label-expansion-round-a-normalizer` proves that one
nontrivial character placement anchor and commutators with exact atom
normalizers suffice, provided their simultaneous action on triples
`(x,y,z), y!=z` has a classical orbit gap. Its profile is
`sqrt(B_(x,y)B_(x,z)/q)`, whose squared norm is exactly `L(V)`.
The anchor charges a fixed positive fraction of every orbit through the
nonzero difference `y-z`. Consequently the full average is controlled with
constants independent of the number of root coordinates. Arbitrary
atom-dependent multiplicity twists are allowed. This is stronger than the
initial bounded-support test: the actor commutators provide the previously
missing mixing, and the theorem explicitly assumes their atom covariance.

The optimized rounding theorem `(PGA6)` and its proof are recorded in
`abelian-root-average-placement-rounds-its-normalizer` and
`abelian-root-placement-birkhoff-polar-proof`. The triple-profile argument
is recorded in `abelian-anchor-triple-profile-proof`.

The tested inference is false:

> Exact additive root tables and vanishing covariance on uniformly bounded
> root supports imply a vanishing-distance simultaneous joint-atom return,
> with a modulus independent of the size of the root window.

The countermodel already has exact, canonically distributed root operators;
there is no finite-group rounding defect to blame. It also allows the
decoder to choose the best possible atom permutation and phases.

A useful affirmative next step must obtain a coercive estimate for the
*whole returned algebra*, for example from additional mixing relations that
spread each undetected root coordinate into a uniformly charged test. The
existing two-Pauli-anchor norm-profile lemma illustrates how a uniform
classical orbit gap can perform this role for a fixed exact Pauli factor.
For the affine-Leavitt roots, one must derive such a gap and its covariance
ledger from the actual group presentation while keeping the expanding
coefficient boundary visible. Merely making every individual root test
accurate as the window grows does not supply it.

This example is not a representation of the full affine-Leavitt Steinberg
presentation, and it does not refute a fixed-window exactification theorem
whose constants depend on the window. It rejects the proposed uniform
atom-matching intermediate step and leaves open the full-group decoder.

No numerical computation was used. The formulas follow from matrix
identities, finite character orthogonality, and a doubly-stochastic
decomposition; the only limit uses `1-cos(theta)~theta^2/2`.
