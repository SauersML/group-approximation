## 1. P1: split Theorem 2 (recommended)

OLD, l.109–121:
```latex
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general}
Let a finitely generated group $\Lambda$ act minimally and topologically
freely on a Cantor set $Z$, and let $R=\LC(Z,\F_2)\rtimes\Lambda$ be
generated by a finite set $S\ni1$. Suppose that there are maps
$\varphi_k\colon R\to M_{N_k}(\F_2)$ with $\varphi_k(1)=I$ such that
$\varphi_k(S)$ generates the ring $M_{N_k}(\F_2)$, and for all $r,s\in R$
we have $\varphi_k(r+s)=\varphi_k(r)+\varphi_k(s)$ and
$\varphi_k(rs)=\varphi_k(r)\varphi_k(s)$ for large $k$. Then for every
$n\ge3$, $\EL_n(R)$ is an infinite, finitely generated, simple Kazhdan
group. Marked by the $e_{ij}(s)$ with $s\in S$, it is the limit of the
finite simple groups $\SL_{nN_k}(\F_2)$ marked by the
$e_{ij}(\varphi_k(s))$, whose Cayley graphs form a family of expanders.
\end{theorem}
```
NEW:
```latex
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general}
Let a finitely generated group $\Lambda$ act minimally and topologically
freely on a Cantor set $Z$, and let $R=\LC(Z,\F_2)\rtimes\Lambda$ be
generated by a finite set $S\ni1$. Then for every $n\ge3$, $\EL_n(R)$ is an
infinite, finitely generated, simple Kazhdan group. Suppose moreover that
there are maps $\varphi_k\colon R\to M_{N_k}(\F_2)$ with $\varphi_k(1)=I$
such that $\varphi_k(S)$ generates the ring $M_{N_k}(\F_2)$, and for all
$r,s\in R$ we have $\varphi_k(r+s)=\varphi_k(r)+\varphi_k(s)$ and
$\varphi_k(rs)=\varphi_k(r)\varphi_k(s)$ for all large $k$. Then $\EL_n(R)$,
marked by the $e_{ij}(s)$ with $s\in S$, is the limit of the finite simple
groups $\SL_{nN_k}(\F_2)$ marked by the $e_{ij}(\varphi_k(s))$, whose Cayley
graphs form a family of expanders.
\end{theorem}
```
**Proof check (line by line).**
- "The ring and property (T)" (l.132–148): generation by the e_ij(s), (T) from EJZ, infinitude from e_12(LC(Z,F_2)). There is no φ_k.
- "Simplicity" (l.150–208): uses minimality, topological freeness and the crossed product rules only. There is no φ_k.
- "Finite simple models" (l.210–235): the only place φ_k is used.

So the NEW statement is exactly what the proof gives. "for all large k" keeps the dependence of k on r and s.

**What P1 buys a user.** Take any minimal topologically free subshift X ⊆ A^Λ over a finitely generated Λ. Its ring is generated by 1, the u_s^{±1} and the letter indicators e_a, since translates of the e_a are cylinder indicators. So EL_n(LC(X,F_2)⋊Λ) is an infinite f.g. simple Kazhdan group with no approximation input. Example: a minimal subsystem of Robinson's aperiodic Z^2 tiling. That example has no finite models (§3, model test), so it is a clean test case for the paper's question whether every sofic infinite simple Kazhdan group is LEF.

**Credit.** Unchanged. Simplicity under topological freeness alone over F_2 is Cairn `binary-el-simple-iff-minimal-topologically-free` (sk-verify-4 PASS), and it is what l.150–208 already prove.

## 2. P2: sharpness over Z (recommended if the page budget allows)

Placement: a new paragraph after l.126 ("…(Corollary~\ref{cor:host}).").
```latex
Over $\Lambda=\Z$ the hypotheses cannot be weakened: if $T$ is a
homeomorphism of a Cantor set $X$ and $\EL_3(\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes_T\Z)$ has
property~\textup{(T)}, then it is finitely generated~\cite[Theorem~1.3.1]{BHV},
so the ring is generated by finitely many $\sum_jf_ju^j$. The translates of
these finitely many $f_j$ generate the clopen sets, so the itinerary map to
the atoms they generate is injective and $(X,T)$ is conjugate to a subshift.
```
Bibitem, flagged for a locator check at source (sk-verify-5 also recalled Theorem 1.3.1 without re-reading):
```latex
\bibitem{BHV}
B.~Bekka, P.~de la Harpe, and A.~Valette, \emph{Kazhdan's property~\textup{(T)}},
New Math. Monogr. \textbf{11}, Cambridge Univ. Press, Cambridge, 2008.
\doi{10.1017/CBO9780511542749}.
```
If Kazhdan's 1967 note states compact generation, cite `\cite{Kazhdan}` instead and add no bibitem. Main or a verifier should check.

**Proof** (every step):
1. (T) ⇒ f.g.: a countable discrete group with property (T) is finitely generated (import above).
2. f.g. ⇒ R f.g.:
   - Let g_1, …, g_m generate EL_3(R), and let R_0 be the subring generated by the entries of the g_i^{±1}.
   - Every element of EL_3(R) is a product of the g_i^{±1}, so its entries lie in R_0.
   - e_12(r) has the entry r, so R = R_0.
3. R f.g. ⇒ translates generate the clopen algebra:
   - Write the generators as Σ_j f_j u^j, and let B be the unital subalgebra of LC(X,F_2) generated by all f_j∘T^t, t ∈ Z.
   - From (f u^a)(f′ u^b) = f (f′∘T^{−a}) u^{a+b}, the coefficients of products lie in B, and sums keep them there.
   - LC(X,F_2) ⊆ R is the coefficient-0 part of R, so LC(X,F_2) = B.
4. Itinerary map:
   - Let 𝒫 be the atoms of the finite Boolean algebra generated by the f_j, and ι(x) = (𝒫-atom of T^t x)_t ∈ 𝒫^Z.
   - ι is continuous and equivariant.
   - If ι(x) = ι(y), then x and y lie in the same members of B = LC(X,F_2), and clopen sets separate points, so x = y.
   - X is compact, so ι is a conjugacy onto the closed invariant set ι(X), a subshift.

Conversely §2 of the paper shows that subshifts give finitely generated rings, so over Z: EL_3 f.g. ⟺ (T) ⟺ subshift. Minimality isn't used in the converse.

**Status.** It matches Cairn `crossed-product-el-kazhdan-iff-finite-field-and-subshift`, sk-verify-5 PASS (all five implications). The printed wording is UNREVIEWED.

## 3. P3: finite models give the maps φ_k over any group (optional)

Placement: after P2, or after l.126 if P2 is not taken.
```latex
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives maps $\varphi_k$ for every
minimal topologically free subshift $X\subseteq A^\Lambda$ with finite
models, that is, such that for every finite $F\subseteq\Lambda$ some finite
invariant set of configurations has all its patterns on $F$ in $X$.
```
**Lemma (UNREVIEWED).** Let Λ be finitely generated, and let X ⊆ A^Λ be an infinite minimal topologically free subshift, with (λx)(η) = x(λ^{−1}η). Suppose that for every finite F ⊆ Λ there is a finite Λ-invariant Y_F ⊆ A^Λ all of whose F-patterns y|_F occur in X. Then R = LC(X,F_2)⋊Λ, with S = {1} ∪ {u_s^{±1}} ∪ {e_a}, has maps φ_F as in Theorem 2.

**Proof.**
1. **Generation of R.**
   - u_λ e_a u_λ^{−1} = e_{λ·[x(e)=a]} is the indicator of {z : z(λ) = a}, since (λ^{−1}z)(e) = z(λ).
   - Products of these give the cylinders, which span LC(X,F_2).
2. **One orbit.** Replace Y_F by one Λ-orbit Y ⊆ Y_F. Its F-patterns still occur in X, and Y is finite and transitive.
3. **Models.**
   - On F_2^Y let P_ξ δ_y = δ_{ξy}, a genuine representation of Λ.
   - For f ∈ LC(X,F_2) depending only on coordinates in F, let D(f)δ_y = f(y)δ_y. This is well defined because y|_F is a pattern of X.
   - Put φ_F(Σ f_ξ u_ξ) = Σ D(f_ξ)P_ξ when every f_ξ depends on F, and 0 otherwise.
4. **Conjugation rule.** P_ξ D(f) P_ξ^{−1} δ_y = f(ξ^{−1}y) δ_y = D(f∘ξ^{−1}) δ_y, whenever f and f∘ξ^{−1} both depend on F; the latter depends on ξF.
   - For fixed r, s ∈ R, all coefficients and all translates occurring in rs depend on F once F is large, so φ_F(r+s) = φ_F(r)+φ_F(s) and φ_F(rs) = φ_F(r)φ_F(s).
   - Also φ_F(1) = I. Index F along an increasing exhausting sequence F_k.
5. **Generation of M_{|Y|}(F_2).**
   - Distinct y ≠ y′ ∈ Y differ at some λ, so the conjugate P_λ D(e_a) P_λ^{−1} for a = y(λ) separates them. Products of these conjugates and their complements I − ·, all in the ring generated by φ_F(S), give every diagonal matrix unit δ_y.
   - Y is transitive, so E_{y,y′} = D(δ_y) P_ξ D(δ_{y′}) with ξy′ = y gives every matrix unit.
6. N_k = |Y_{F_k}| → ∞ follows from Theorem 2's ultraproduct step, since EL_n(R) is infinite.

This is exactly l.249–271 with Z/N_ℓ replaced by Y.

**Model tests.**
- Λ = Z, X Sturmian: Y = the orbit of y_ℓ (l.249–257), and the lemma reduces to §2. ✓
- **Λ = Z^2, X a minimal subsystem of an aperiodic SFT** (Robinson; Labbé's Wang shift). Once F contains the forbidden shapes, no finite models exist. Proof: a finite transitive Z^2-set is Z^2/N with N of finite index, so y ∈ Y is N-periodic and avoids the forbidden patterns, hence a periodic point of the SFT, which contradicts aperiodicity.
  - The lemma does not apply, but P1 still gives simple Kazhdan groups. Consistent with Cairn `z2-containing-groups-have-non-rf-measure-actions` (Labbé's shift has no finite models).
- **Free X over a non-residually-finite Λ:** finite models force Λ residually finite (Cairn `free-subshift-with-finite-models-forces-rf-group`, sk-verify-4 PASS). So over non-RF groups the lemma needs non-free X.
  - The paper's lamplighter actions (l.356–395) evade this with partial actions that are exact on balls. They are NOT covered by the lemma, and the paper proves them directly.
- **Every infinite f.g. RF Λ** has a free minimal subshift with finite models (Cairn `rf-groups-have-free-minimal-subshifts-with-finite-models`, sk-verify-4 PASS after the index-≥4 repair; credit to Krieger and Cortez–Petite for Toeplitz arrays, no locator verified). This lane does NOT propose printing the existence clause: its proof is the lane's own nested-hole design, not a citable theorem.

Every countable LEF group is a subgroup of the derived subgroup of an

## 6. Model tests of Theorem 2's hypotheses

- **Odometer** (dyadic adding machine), minimal but not expansive:
  - finitely many f_j live at some level 2^m, and their translates stay at level 2^m, so R is not f.g.;
  - hence by P2 EL_3 is not f.g. and not Kazhdan, and the hypothesis "generated by a finite set" of Thm 2 fails. ✓
- **Periodic orbit:** X finite, not a Cantor set. EL_3(M_N(F_2)) = SL_{3N}(F_2) is finite. Excluded. ✓
- **Sturmian:** subshift, periodic models; Thm 1. ✓
- **Full shift over Z** (not minimal): a fixed point gives a proper ideal (as in Cairn free-9 P1 step 6), so EL_3 is not simple. Minimality is necessary. ✓
- **Minimal subsystem of an aperiodic Z^2 SFT:** P1 gives simple Kazhdan; there are no finite models (§3); LEF is open. ✓
