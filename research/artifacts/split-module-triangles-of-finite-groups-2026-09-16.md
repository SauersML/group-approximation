# Split-module triangles of finite groups: a linear representation injective on the vertex groups (2026-09-16)

Swarm lane `hi-spec-hyperbolic-groups-virtually-to` (special case / strengthening of
`hyperbolic-groups-virtually-torsion-free`). The proofs are by hand and unreviewed; they are not
Lean-verified. The numerical checks in §7 are floating point and serve as consistency checks, not
certificates. Scripts and outputs are in `experiments/split-module-triangles-2026-09-16/`.

## 0. Summary and what is new

- **Theorem A.** A triangle of finite groups with trivial face group is given a *split module*
  at each vertex. This is a faithful module that is the direct sum of the fixed spaces of the two
  edge groups, with the edge modules matching across each edge (§2). The colimit then has a
  finite-dimensional complex linear representation that is injective on every vertex group. No
  curvature hypothesis and no generation hypothesis is used.
- **Corollary A'.** Under the curvature condition of Caprace–Conder–Kaluba–Witzel (CCKW),
  `Σ 1/r_i <= 1`, with vertex groups generated by their edge groups, the colimit is virtually
  torsion-free. If `Σ 1/r_i < 1` it is also non-elementary hyperbolic.
- **Corollary B.** Suppose every vertex group has a faithful 2-dimensional complex representation in
  which the two edge groups fix two distinct lines ("reflection vertices"). Then split modules
  satisfying the matching condition always exist, via a Galois sum. This covers every triangle
  whose vertex groups are finite rank-2 complex reflection groups generated by one reflection
  subgroup per edge, under any gluing; the Shephard groups `p[m]q` are one case.
- **Computation (§7).** For the CCKW trivalent tables, the method applies exactly to the ten
  groups all of whose vertex labels lie in `{16, 40, 48, 54}`. These are eight groups from the
  `?` columns (already certified in `cckw-question-mark-triangle-groups-virtually-torsion-free`)
  and two `Yes` groups. Theorem A proves them virtually torsion-free by a uniform, computer-free
  argument, once the vertex modules are in hand. A floating-point construction of the
  representation checks all ten.
- **Obstruction (§8).** Each of the 51 undecided CCKW groups has a vertex of label 14, 18 or 26.
  None of these vertex groups (`7:3`, `3^{1+2}` of exponent 3, `13:3`) has a faithful split
  module, so Theorem A cannot decide any of them. This is proved by hand in §8.

**What is new, relative to the graph.** Route `simple-vertex-triangles-are-universal-for-triangle-vtf-proof`
(U6) handles only order-2 edge groups, via Tits' representation of Coxeter groups. Theorem A replaces
Tits' reflection representation by an arbitrary split module: edge groups of any order, non-cyclic
edge groups, and vertex modules of any dimension. The bounded literature search of §9 found no
prior statement of Theorem A or of Corollary B for triangles of finite groups.

## 1. Setting and notation

- `T` is a triangle of finite groups with trivial face group. It has vertices `v`, three edges `e`,
  finite vertex groups `A_v` and finite edge groups `E_e`. For `v in e` there is an injection
  `E_e -> A_v`, and `E_e` is identified with its image.
- `Λ = Λ(T)` is the colimit: the free product of the `A_v` modulo the identification of the two
  images of each `E_e`.
- Each vertex `v` lies on exactly two edges, written `e, f`; the third edge is `g`. For an edge `e`,
  its two vertices are written `v, w`.
- All modules are finite-dimensional complex vector spaces. For a finite group `E` acting on `W`:
  - `W^E` is the fixed space and `P_E = |E|^{-1} Σ_{h in E} h` is the averaging operator;
  - `[E, W] = span{ hx - x : h in E, x in W }`.

**Lemma 1.1.** `P_E` is an idempotent with image `W^E` and kernel `[E,W]`. So `W = W^E ⊕ [E,W]`,
and `[E,W]` is `E`-stable.

*Proof.* `P_E` fixes `W^E`, and `h P_E = P_E` for `h in E`, so `P_E` is an idempotent with image
`W^E`. `P_E(hx - x) = 0`, so `[E,W] <= ker P_E`. Conversely `x - P_E x = |E|^{-1} Σ_h (x - hx)` lies
in `[E,W]`, so `ker P_E <= [E,W]`. `E`-stability: `k(hx - x) = (khx - x) - (kx - x)`. ∎

Write `p_E = 1 - P_E`: the projection onto `[E,W]` along `W^E`. It commutes with the action of `E`.

**Definition (split module).** At a vertex `v` with edges `e, f`, a *split module* is an
`A_v`-module `W_v` with `W_v = W_v^{E_e} ⊕ W_v^{E_f}` (internal direct sum). It is *faithful* if
`A_v` acts faithfully.

## 2. Theorem A

**Theorem A.** Let `T` be a triangle of finite groups with trivial face group. Suppose that for each
vertex `v`, with edges `e, f`, there is an `A_v`-module `W_v` such that:
- **(S1)** `A_v` acts faithfully on `W_v`;
- **(S2)** `W_v = W_v^{E_e} ⊕ W_v^{E_f}`;
- **(S3)** for each edge `e` with vertices `v, w`, the `E_e`-modules `[E_e, W_v]` and `[E_e, W_w]` are
  isomorphic.

Then there is a homomorphism `ρ : Λ(T) -> GL(V)` whose restriction to each `A_v` is injective. Here
`V` is the direct sum of three spaces `U_e`, one per edge, each `E_e`-isomorphic to `[E_e, W_v]`.
In particular each `A_v -> Λ(T)` is injective.

No curvature hypothesis is assumed, and the vertex groups need not be generated by their edge
groups. The `E_e` need not be cyclic.

## 3. Proof of Theorem A

**Step 1: the two commutator spaces also split `W_v`.** Fix an `A_v`-invariant Hermitian inner
product on `W_v` (average any inner product). Each `h in E` is unitary, so `P_E` is self-adjoint,
and Lemma 1.1 gives `[E, W_v] = ker P_E = (W_v^E)^⊥`. By (S2),
`[E_e, W_v] ∩ [E_f, W_v] = (W_v^{E_e} + W_v^{E_f})^⊥ = W_v^⊥ = 0`. Moreover
`dim [E_e, W_v] = dim W_v - dim W_v^{E_e} = dim W_v^{E_f}`, and symmetrically, so the dimensions
add up to `dim W_v`. Hence

    W_v = [E_e, W_v] ⊕ [E_f, W_v].                                            (3.1)

**Step 2: the space `V`.** For each edge `e` with vertices `v, w`, let `U_e` be an `E_e`-module with
action `ρ_e : E_e -> GL(U_e)`, together with `E_e`-isomorphisms
`α_{e,v} : [E_e, W_v] -> U_e` and `α_{e,w} : [E_e, W_w] -> U_e`. These exist by (S3). Put
`V = U_{e} ⊕ U_{f} ⊕ U_{g}`, summed over the three edges. For a vertex `v` with edges `e, f`, (3.1)
gives a linear isomorphism

    ι_v : W_v -> U_e ⊕ U_f,   ι_v(y + z) = (α_{e,v}(y), α_{f,v}(z))   (y in [E_e,W_v], z in [E_f,W_v]).

**Step 3: the edge projections.** Fix an edge `e` with vertices `v` (edges `e, f`) and `w`
(edges `e, g`). Define a linear map `π_e : V -> U_e` blockwise:

    π_e|_{U_e ⊕ U_f} = α_{e,v} ∘ p^v_e ∘ ι_v^{-1},    π_e|_{U_e ⊕ U_g} = α_{e,w} ∘ p^w_e ∘ ι_w^{-1},

where `p^v_e = 1 - P_{E_e}` on `W_v`. The two formulas overlap on `U_e`, and both restrict to the
identity there: for `u in U_e`, `ι_v^{-1}(u) = α_{e,v}^{-1}(u)` lies in `[E_e, W_v]`, which `p^v_e`
fixes. So `π_e` is well defined, and

    π_e|_{U_e} = id.                                                          (3.2)

**Step 4: the edge actions.** For `h in E_e` define `σ_e(h) = 1 + (ρ_e(h) - 1) ∘ π_e`. Here
`(ρ_e(h) - 1) π_e (x)` lies in `U_e ⊂ V`. By (3.2), `π_e (ρ_e(k) - 1) π_e = (ρ_e(k) - 1) π_e`. So

    σ_e(h) σ_e(k) = 1 + (ρ_e(h) - 1)π_e + (ρ_e(k) - 1)π_e + (ρ_e(h) - 1)(ρ_e(k) - 1)π_e
                  = 1 + (ρ_e(hk) - 1)π_e = σ_e(hk),

and `σ_e(1) = 1`. So `σ_e : E_e -> GL(V)` is a homomorphism.

**Step 5: the vertex actions.** Let `v` have edges `e, f`, and put `K_v = ker π_e ∩ ker π_f`.
- The map `(π_e, π_f)` restricted to `U_e ⊕ U_f` is `(α_{e,v} p^v_e, α_{f,v} p^v_f) ∘ ι_v^{-1}`. Its
  kernel is `ι_v(ker p^v_e ∩ ker p^v_f) = ι_v(W_v^{E_e} ∩ W_v^{E_f}) = 0`, by (S2). The source and
  target both have dimension `dim W_v`, so it is an isomorphism `U_e ⊕ U_f -> U_e ⊕ U_f`.
- Hence `V = (U_e ⊕ U_f) ⊕ K_v`. Given `x in V`, the unique `y in U_e ⊕ U_f` with
  `(π_e, π_f)(y) = (π_e, π_f)(x)` gives `x - y in K_v`, and `(U_e ⊕ U_f) ∩ K_v = 0`.

For `a in A_v` define `σ_v(a)` to be `ι_v ∘ a ∘ ι_v^{-1}` on `U_e ⊕ U_f` and the identity on
`K_v`. This is a homomorphism `A_v -> GL(V)`, and it is injective by (S1).

**Step 6: compatibility.** Let `h in E_e`, viewed in `A_v`. We show `σ_v(h) = σ_e(h)`.
- On `K_v`, `π_e = 0`, so `σ_e(h)` is the identity, like `σ_v(h)`.
- On `U_e ⊕ U_f`, write `x = ι_v(y)` and `y = y_0 + y_1`, with `y_0 in W_v^{E_e}` and
  `y_1 = p^v_e(y) in [E_e, W_v]`. Then `hy - y = hy_1 - y_1 in [E_e, W_v]`, so by (3.1)
  `ι_v(hy) - ι_v(y) = (α_{e,v}(hy_1 - y_1), 0)`. Since `α_{e,v}` is `E_e`-equivariant, this equals
  `(ρ_e(h) - 1) α_{e,v}(y_1) = (ρ_e(h) - 1) π_e(x)`. So `σ_v(h)x = σ_e(h)x`.

The same holds at `w`, so `σ_v` and `σ_w` agree on the two images of `E_e`, for every edge.

**Step 7: the colimit.** The face group is trivial, so a homomorphism `Λ(T) -> H` is the same as a
family of homomorphisms `A_v -> H` that agree on the two images of each `E_e`. So the `σ_v` define
`ρ : Λ(T) -> GL(V)` with `ρ|_{A_v} = σ_v`, which is injective. Since `σ_v` factors as
`A_v -> Λ(T) -> GL(V)`, the map `A_v -> Λ(T)` is injective. ∎

**Remark 3.3 (dimension).** `dim V = Σ_e dim [E_e, W_v]`. If `W_v` is the 2-dimensional reflection
representation of a dihedral group `A_v` with `E_e, E_f` of order 2, every `U_e` is a line. The
`σ_e` are then reflections, and `ρ` is a representation of Tits' type for the Coxeter group of U6.

## 4. Virtual torsion-freeness

**Corollary A'.** Let `T` satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem A. Assume also that each `A_v` is
generated by the images of its two edge groups, and that the half-girths `r_v` of the coset graphs
satisfy `Σ_v 1/r_v <= 1`. Then:
- `Λ(T)` has a homomorphism to a finite group that is injective on every `A_v`;
- `Λ(T)` is virtually torsion-free;
- if `Σ_v 1/r_v < 1`, `Λ(T)` is also non-elementary word-hyperbolic.

*Proof.*
1. **A finite quotient.** `ρ(Λ)` is a finitely generated subgroup of `GL(V) ≅ GL_n(C)`, generated by
   the finite sets `ρ(A_v)`. By Mal'cev's theorem, finitely generated linear groups over a field are
   residually finite (A. I. Mal'cev, Mat. Sb. 8 (1940); recalled, not fetched).
   - The set `F = ∪_v ρ(A_v) \ {1}` is finite. For each `t in F` choose a finite-index normal
     subgroup `N_t` of `ρ(Λ)` with `t ∉ N_t`, and put `N = ∩_t N_t`.
   - Let `ψ : Λ -> ρ(Λ)/N`. If `a in A_v` and `ψ(a) = 1`, then `ρ(a) in N`, so `ρ(a) ∉ F`, and
     `ρ(a) = 1`. By Theorem A, `a = 1`.

   Selberg's lemma gives the same conclusion: take `N` torsion-free.
2. **Torsion-free kernel.** `T` has trivial face group, and its vertex groups are generated by the
   edge groups, with `Σ 1/r_v <= 1`. So `triangle-of-groups-half-girth-structure` (iv) (CCKW,
   arXiv:2011.09276, Theorem 3.1(iv); ESTABLISHED literature import on the graph) applies to `ψ`:
   `ker ψ` is a torsion-free subgroup of finite index.
3. **Hyperbolicity.** If `Σ 1/r_v < 1`, the same import, part (v), gives non-elementary
   hyperbolicity. ∎

**Remark 4.1 (only the core needs modules).** Let `T` be any triangle of finite groups with trivial
face group, and let `T°` be its core, with vertex groups `B_v = <E_e, E_f>`.
- If `T°` satisfies (S1)–(S3), Step 1 of the proof gives a finite quotient of `Λ(T°)` injective on
  every `B_v`. No curvature is used there.
- By (U3) of `simple-vertex-triangles-are-universal-for-triangle-vtf` (direction <=, which also uses
  no curvature), `Λ(T)` then has a finite quotient injective on every `A_v`.

This conclusion is recorded as a remark only. The claim node states the generated case.

**Remark 4.2 (the criterion is not necessary).** Virtual torsion-freeness does not force split
modules. Of the 23 groups of `cckw-question-mark-triangle-groups-virtually-torsion-free`, fifteen have
a vertex of label 14, 18, 24 or 26, and these vertex groups have no faithful split module (§8 by hand
for 14, 18, 26; §7 computation for 24). Permutation certificates nevertheless show all fifteen to be
virtually torsion-free.
