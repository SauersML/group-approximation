# The seven-term partner is confined to the five-term subgroup

For `alpha in F_2[K] subset F_2[G]`, right-coset projection sends every right
inverse in `F_2[G]` to a right inverse in `F_2[K]`.  Apply this to a rank-five
factor in a putative `(5,7)` direct-finiteness counterexample.

The projected partner has odd support because its augmentation is one.  Its
rank is at most seven.  Rank one would make the five-term factor a single
group basis unit.  Rank three is excluded by the known universal `(3,5)`
result, and rank five by `(5,5)`.  In either of the latter cases the projected
right inverse is two-sided, so the five-term factor is a unit and its right
inverse in the ambient group algebra is unique, contradicting the marked
reverse defect.

The projection must therefore retain seven terms.  Since the original partner
has only seven, it has no off-subgroup component at all.

For the Leavitt rectangle normal form

```text
alpha=[h_0]+(1+[r])[x](1+[s]),
```

this confines both factors to `<C_3,x>`.  For a normalizer-supported
five-term factor, both lie in the subgroup generated by its at most two
non-base normalizer cosets.  This is the full support-location consequence of
the known rank-pair theorems at `(5,7)`; deciding the remaining internal
coefficient equations is exactly the first unknown ULIE case.
