## 1. Setting and notation

**Balls and languages.** `B_D = [-D,D]^2 ⊆ Z^2` (sup norm). For an SFT `Ω ⊆ A^(Z^2)` and finite `K ⊆ Z^2`,
`L_K(Ω) ⊆ A^K` is the set of restrictions to `K` of points of `Ω` (globally legal patterns). A 2-dimensional word
(rectangular pattern) is *legal* if it occurs in some point of `Ω`. Legality is factorial.

**Covariant families** (`research/artifacts/un-labbe-ring-torus-tilings-2026-09-13.md` §1). A covariant `D`-family
for `Ω` over a field `k` is a finite-dimensional `k`-space `W != 0`, idempotents `E_a(z) ∈ End_k(W)` (`a ∈ A`,
`z ∈ Z^2`) and commuting invertibles `u_1, u_2` with `E_a(z) = u^z E_a(0) u^(-z)` (`u^z = u_1^(z_1) u_2^(z_2)`), such
that:
- (Q1) for each `z`, `{E_a(z)}_a` is a partition of unity into orthogonal idempotents;
- (Q2) `[E_a(z), E_b(z')] = 0` whenever `|z - z'| <= 2D`;
- (Q3) `prod_(h ∈ B_D) E_(π(h))(z + h) = 0` for every `π ∈ A^(B_D) \ L_(B_D)(Ω)` and every `z`.

Consequences of covariance: `u^g E_a(z) u^(-g) = E_a(z + g)`; (Q2) and (Q3) hold at all `z` as soon as they hold at
one base point, since conjugation by `u^g` moves the base point by `g`.

**Evaluation on a box.** Fix a covariant `D'`-family `(W', E', u)` for `Ω_S` with `D' >= 1`, and put
`Q = [0,2D']^2 = (D',D') + B_(D')`. For `K ⊆ Q` and `f : S^K -> k` put

`ev_K(f) = sum_(σ ∈ S^K) f(σ) prod_(c ∈ K) E'_(σ(c))(c)`.

Let `𝒞_Q` be the subalgebra generated by the `E'_a(c)`, `c ∈ Q`. The following are immediate.
- (E1) All `E'_a(c)`, `c ∈ Q`, commute by (Q2), since `Q` has sup-diameter `2D'`. So `𝒞_Q` is commutative, the products
  `e_σ = prod_(c ∈ Q) E'_(σ(c))(c)` are orthogonal idempotents with `sum_σ e_σ = 1` (expand
  `prod_c sum_a E'_a(c)`), and `ev_Q : k^(S^Q) -> 𝒞_Q` is a unital algebra homomorphism.
- (E2) `ev_K(f) = ev_Q(f ∘ res_K)` (insert the partition of unity at the sites of `Q \ K`).
- (E3) If `K + g ⊆ Q`, then `u^g ev_K(f) u^(-g) = ev_(K+g)(f ∘ sh_g)`, where `(f ∘ sh_g)(σ') = f(σ'(· + g))`.
- (E4) **Legality reduction.** `ev_Q(f) = ev_Q(f · 1_(L_Q(Ω_S)))`, since `e_σ = 0` for `σ ∉ L_Q(Ω_S)` by (Q3) at
  `z = (D',D')`.
- (E5) For a set `X ⊆ S` of letters, `sum_(a ∈ X) E'_a(c) = ev_Q(ρ ↦ [ρ(c) ∈ X])`.

**2-dimensional morphisms** (Labbé, arXiv:1802.03265, §"d-dimensional morphisms"; verbatim in §9). Let
`ω : L(Ω_S) -> T^(*2)` be a 2-dimensional morphism: whenever `u, v, u ⊙^i v` are legal, `ω(u) ⊙^i ω(v)` is
well-defined and equals `ω(u ⊙^i v)`. Write `shape(ω(a)) = (w(a), h(a))`, `w_max = max_a w(a)`,
`h_max = max_a h(a)`. Standing hypotheses of Theorem A:
- (H1) `ω` is a 2-dimensional morphism on `L(Ω_S)` with `ω(L(Ω_S)) ⊆ L(Ω_T)`;
- (H2) `w(a) >= 2` and `h(a) >= 2` for every `a ∈ S`.

Consequences of (H1):
- (M1) If the vertical domino `a` below `b` is legal then `w(a) = w(b)`; if the horizontal domino `a` left of `b` is
  legal then `h(a) = h(b)`. (Well-definedness of `ω(a) ⊙^2 ω(b)`, resp. `⊙^1`.)
- (M2) For a legal box `ρ ∈ L_([0,m) × [0,n))(Ω_S)`, put `X_c(ρ) = sum_(0 <= c' < c) w(ρ(c',0))` and
  `Y_r(ρ) = sum_(0 <= r' < r) h(ρ(0,r'))`. By (M1) the letters of column `c` all have width `w(ρ(c,0))` and those of row
  `r` height `h(ρ(0,r))`. By the morphism property, applied row by row and then column by column, `ω(ρ)` is the block
  concatenation placing `ω(ρ(c,r))` on `[X_c, X_(c+1)) × [Y_r, Y_(r+1))`, and it is legal for `Ω_T`.
- (M3) By (H2), `X_c(ρ) >= 2c` and `X_c(ρ) >= w(ρ(0,0)) + 2(c - 1)` for `c >= 1`; likewise for `Y_r`.

## 2. Lemma 1: width projections are column-constant, height projections row-constant

For `m, n >= 1` and `z ∈ Z^2` put

`M_(=m)(z) = sum_(a : w(a) = m) E'_a(z)`, `M_(>i)(z) = sum_(m > i) M_(=m)(z)`,
`N_(=n)(z) = sum_(a : h(a) = n) E'_a(z)`, `N_(>j)(z) = sum_(n > j) N_(=n)(z)`.

These are idempotents, and all of them at one site commute (sums from one partition of unity).

**Lemma 1.** Assume (H1) and `D' >= 1`. For all `m, n` and `z`:
`M_(=m)(z + e_2) = M_(=m)(z)` and `N_(=n)(z + e_1) = N_(=n)(z)`. Hence `M_(=m)(z)` depends only on `z_1` and
`N_(=n)(z)` only on `z_2`, and the same holds for `M_(>i)`, `N_(>j)`.

*Proof.* Put `z' = z + e_2 ∈ z + B_(D')`. Let `a, b ∈ S` with `w(a) != w(b)`. By (M1) the vertical domino `a` below
`b` is illegal, so every `π ∈ S^(B_(D'))` with `π(0) = a`, `π(e_2) = b` is illegal. All factors below commute by (Q2)
(the ball `z + B_(D')` has sup-diameter `2D'`); inserting the partition of unity at the other sites of the ball,

`E'_a(z) E'_b(z') = sum_(π(0)=a, π(e_2)=b) prod_(h ∈ B_(D')) E'_(π(h))(z + h) = 0`

by (Q3). Summing, `M_(=m)(z) M_(=m')(z') = 0` for `m != m'`. Hence, using (Q1) at `z` and at `z'` and (Q2),

`M_(=m)(z) = M_(=m)(z) sum_(m') M_(=m')(z') = M_(=m)(z) M_(=m)(z') = sum_(m'') M_(=m'')(z) M_(=m)(z') = M_(=m)(z')`.

The statement for `N` is the same with horizontal dominoes. ∎

With covariance this reads: `u_2 M_(=m)(0) u_2^(-1) = M_(=m)(e_2) = M_(=m)(0)` and
`u_1 N_(=n)(0) u_1^(-1) = N_(=n)(0)`. So **`u_2` commutes with every `M(0)` and `u_1` with every `N(0)`**.

Lemma 1 proves the sentence "width projections are constant along columns" that the 2026-09-13 review of the target's
Attempts marked as not proved.

## 3. The tower space and its translations

Put `I = [0, w_max) × [0, h_max)`, and for `(i,j) ∈ I`

`P_(ij) = M_(>i)(0) N_(>j)(0) = sum_(a : w(a) > i, h(a) > j) E'_a(0)`, `W_T = ⊕_((i,j) ∈ I) P_(ij) W'`.

Write `v = (v_(ij))` with `v_(ij) ∈ P_(ij) W'`. Since `P_(00) = 1`, `W_T ⊇ W' != 0`, and
`dim W_T = sum_a w(a) h(a) rank E'_a(0) <= w_max h_max dim W'`.

*Classical picture.* If the parent family is a configuration `x'`, the summand `(i,j)` means: the `T`-origin is at
position `(i,j)` of the block `ω(x'(0))`. Moving the `T`-origin by `+e_1` moves the pointer to `(i+1, j)` if it stays in
the block, and otherwise to `(0, j)` of the block `ω(x'(e_1))`, while the parent is shifted. The operators below are
the operator form of this Kakutani--Rokhlin tower, with `u_1^(-1)` playing the parent shift.

**Definition.** For `v ∈ W_T` and `(i,j) ∈ I` (components with index outside `I` are read as `0`):

- `(B_1 v)_(i+1, j) = M_(>i+1)(0) v_(ij)`,  `(B_1 v)_(0j) = sum_i u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) v_(ij)`;
- `(A_1 v)_(ij) = v_(i+1, j) + M_(=i+1)(0) u_1 v_(0j)`;
- `(B_2 v)_(i, j+1) = N_(>j+1)(0) v_(ij)`,  `(B_2 v)_(i0) = sum_j u_2^(-1) N_(=j+1)(0) v_(ij)`;
- `(A_2 v)_(ij) = v_(i, j+1) + N_(=j+1)(0) u_2 v_(i0)`.

**Lemma 2.** These maps send `W_T` to `W_T`; `A_l B_l = B_l A_l = 1` (`l = 1, 2`); and `A_1 A_2 = A_2 A_1`.

*Proof.* **Well defined.**
- `M_(>i+1)(0) v_(ij) ∈ M_(>i+1)(0) N_(>j)(0) W' = P_(i+1,j) W'`, since `M_(>i+1)(0) M_(>i)(0) = M_(>i+1)(0)`.
- `v_(ij) ∈ N_(>j)(0) W'`, and by Lemma 1 `u_1^(-1) N_(>j)(0) = N_(>j)(0) u_1^(-1)`. So
  `u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) v_(ij) ∈ N_(>j)(0) W' = P_(0j) W'`.
- `v_(i+1,j) ∈ P_(i+1,j) W' ⊆ P_(ij) W'`. By Lemma 1, `u_1 v_(0j) ∈ N_(>j)(0) W'`, so
  `M_(=i+1)(0) u_1 v_(0j) ∈ M_(=i+1)(0) N_(>j)(0) W' ⊆ P_(ij) W'`.
- `B_2`, `A_2`: the same with `u_2` commuting with `M(0)` (Lemma 1).

**Inverses.** For `v ∈ W_T`:
- `(A_1 B_1 v)_(ij) = M_(>i+1)(0) v_(ij) + M_(=i+1)(0) u_1 sum_(i') u_1^(-1) M_(=i'+1)(0) v_(i'j)
  = (M_(>i+1)(0) + M_(=i+1)(0)) v_(ij) = M_(>i)(0) v_(ij) = v_(ij)`.
- `(B_1 A_1 v)_(i+1,j) = M_(>i+1)(0) v_(i+1,j) + M_(>i+1)(0) M_(=i+1)(0) u_1 v_(0j) = v_(i+1,j)`.
- `(B_1 A_1 v)_(0j) = sum_i u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) v_(i+1,j) + sum_i u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) u_1 v_(0j) = 0 + v_(0j)`,
  because `v_(i+1,j) ∈ M_(>i+1)(0) W'` and `sum_(i >= 0) M_(=i+1)(0) = 1` (every width is `>= 1`).
- `l = 2` is symmetric.

**Commutation.** It suffices to show `B_1 B_2 = B_2 B_1`. Compare the components.
- `(i+1, j+1)`: both sides give `M_(>i+1)(0) N_(>j+1)(0) v_(ij)`.
- `(0, j+1)`: `B_1 B_2` gives `sum_i u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) N_(>j+1)(0) v_(ij)`. `B_2 B_1` gives
  `N_(>j+1)(0) sum_i u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) v_(ij)`, and `N_(>j+1)(0)` commutes with `u_1^(-1)` (Lemma 1) and with
  `M_(=i+1)(0)`.
- `(i+1, 0)`: symmetric, with `u_2` commuting with `M(0)`.
- `(0,0)`: `B_1 B_2` gives `sum_(i,j) u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) u_2^(-1) N_(=j+1)(0) v_(ij)
  = sum_(i,j) u_1^(-1) u_2^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) N_(=j+1)(0) v_(ij)`. `B_2 B_1` gives
  `sum_(i,j) u_2^(-1) N_(=j+1)(0) u_1^(-1) M_(=i+1)(0) v_(ij) = sum_(i,j) u_2^(-1) u_1^(-1) N_(=j+1)(0) M_(=i+1)(0) v_(ij)`.
  These agree, since `u_1, u_2` commute and same-site idempotents commute. ∎

Put `A^z = A_1^(z_1) A_2^(z_2)` (so `A^(-z) = B_1^(z_1) B_2^(z_2)` for `z >= 0`).

## 4. Tile idempotents and the evaluation formula

**Definition.** For `b ∈ T` and `(i,j) ∈ I` put `S_(ij) = {a ∈ S : w(a) > i, h(a) > j}` and

`(E_b(0) v)_(ij) = sum_(a ∈ S_(ij), ω(a)_((i,j)) = b) E'_a(0) v_(ij)`,  `E_b(z) = A^z E_b(0) A^(-z)`.

`E_b(0)` preserves each summand. On `P_(ij) W'` the operators `E_b(0)` (`b ∈ T`) are orthogonal idempotents summing to
`P_(ij)`, i.e. to the identity there. So (Q1) holds at `z = 0`, and hence at every `z` by conjugation.

**Classical letter functions.** Put `Box = [0, 4D']^2`. For `(i,j) ∈ I`, `z ∈ Box`, `b ∈ T`, define
`f^b_(ij,z) : S^Q -> {0,1}` by `f^b_(ij,z)(ρ) = 1` iff
- `ρ ∈ L_Q(Ω_S)` and `ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij)`, and
- the letter of `ω(ρ)` at position `(i + z_1, j + z_2)` is `b`.

The position lies inside `ω(ρ)`: by (M3), `X_(2D'+1)(ρ) >= w(ρ(0)) + 4D' > i + z_1`, and likewise vertically.

**Lemma 3 (support at pointer `0`).** Let `z ∈ Box`.
- (a) On `L_Q(Ω_S)`, `f^b_(0j,z)(ρ)` depends only on `ρ|_K` with `K = [0, ⌊z_1/2⌋] × [0, ⌈z_2/2⌉]`.
- (b) On `L_Q(Ω_S)`, `f^b_(i0,z)(ρ)` depends only on `ρ|_K` with `K = [0, ⌈z_1/2⌉] × [0, ⌊z_2/2⌋]`.

*Proof of (a).* Let `ρ` be legal with `ρ(0) ∈ S_(0j)`. The position `p = (z_1, j + z_2)` lies in the block of the parent
site `c = (c_1, c_2)` with `X_(c_1) <= z_1 < X_(c_1+1)` and `Y_(c_2) <= j + z_2 < Y_(c_2+1)`.
- By (M3), `2c_1 <= X_(c_1) <= z_1`, so `c_1 <= ⌊z_1/2⌋`.
- If `c_2 >= 1`, by (M3) `h(ρ(0)) + 2(c_2 - 1) <= Y_(c_2) <= j + z_2 < h(ρ(0)) + z_2`, so `c_2 - 1 < z_2/2`, i.e.
  `c_2 <= ⌈z_2/2⌉`.
- The numbers `X_0, ..., X_(⌊z_1/2⌋+1)` depend only on the widths of `ρ(0,0), ..., ρ(⌊z_1/2⌋, 0)`, and
  `Y_0, ..., Y_(⌈z_2/2⌉+1)` only on the heights of `ρ(0,0), ..., ρ(0, ⌈z_2/2⌉)`. All these sites lie in `K`. So `c`
  and the offset `p - (X_(c_1), Y_(c_2))` are determined by `ρ|_K`, and the letter is `ω(ρ(c))` at that offset, with
  `c ∈ K`. The condition `ρ(0) ∈ S_(0j)` also depends on `ρ(0)` only.

(b) is the same with the coordinates exchanged. ∎

For `K` as in Lemma 3(a) define `g^b_(0j,z) : S^K -> {0,1}` by `g(σ) = f^b_(0j,z)(ρ)` for any legal `ρ` extending `σ`
(and `0` if there is none). By (E4), (E2) and Lemma 3,

(4.1) `ev_Q(f^b_(0j,z)) = ev_Q((g^b_(0j,z) ∘ res_K) · 1_L) = ev_Q(g^b_(0j,z) ∘ res_K) = ev_K(g^b_(0j,z))`.

**Lemma 4 (one step of the classical tower).** Let `z, z + e_1 ∈ Box`, `K` as in Lemma 3(a), and `ρ ∈ L_Q(Ω_S)` with
`ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij)`. Then `K + e_1 ⊆ Q` and

`f^b_(ij, z+e_1)(ρ) = [w(ρ(0)) > i+1] f^b_(i+1, j, z)(ρ) + [w(ρ(0)) = i+1] g^b_(0j,z)(ρ(· + e_1)|_K)`.

The analogous identity holds for `z, z + e_2 ∈ Box`, with `h(ρ(0))`, `f^b_(i, j+1, z)` and `g^b_(i0,z)`.

*Proof.* `z_1 <= 4D' - 1` gives `⌊z_1/2⌋ + 1 <= 2D'`, and `⌈z_2/2⌉ <= 2D'`; so `K + e_1 ⊆ Q`.
- If `w(ρ(0)) > i+1`: `f^b_(ij,z+e_1)` reads position `(i+1+z_1, j+z_2)`, which is exactly what `f^b_(i+1,j,z)` reads,
  and `ρ(0) ∈ S_(i+1,j)`.
- If `w(ρ(0)) = i+1`: extend `ρ` to `x ∈ Ω_S`, and let `ρ'' = (σ^(e_1) x)|_Q`, so `ρ''(c) = x(c + e_1)`. Then `ρ''` is
  legal and `ρ''|_K = ρ(· + e_1)|_K`. Apply (M2) to the legal box `x|_([0, 2D'+1] × [0, 2D'])`: its image contains
  `ω(ρ)` as the left sub-block and `ω(ρ'')` as the sub-block starting at column `w(ρ(0)) = i+1`, on the same rows. So
  the letter of `ω(ρ)` at `(i+1+z_1, j+z_2)` is the letter of `ω(ρ'')` at `(z_1, j+z_2)`. Moreover `h(ρ''(0)) =
  h(x(e_1)) = h(ρ(0)) > j` by (M1), so `ρ''(0) ∈ S_(0j)`. Hence `f^b_(ij,z+e_1)(ρ) = f^b_(0j,z)(ρ'') =
  g^b_(0j,z)(ρ(·+e_1)|_K)`.
- `w(ρ(0)) < i+1` is excluded by `ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij)`. ∎

**Proposition 5 (evaluation formula).** For every `z ∈ Box` and `b ∈ T`, `E_b(z)` is diagonal:

`(E_b(z) v)_(ij) = ev_Q(f^b_(ij,z)) v_(ij)`  for all `v ∈ W_T` and `(i,j) ∈ I`.

*Proof.* Induction along a monotone lattice path from `0` to `z` inside `Box`. Write `F_(ij)(z) = ev_Q(f^b_(ij,z)) ∈
𝒞_Q`; it commutes with `P_(ij)`, and `F_(ij)(z) P_(ij) = F_(ij)(z)`, since `f^b_(ij,z)` contains the factor
`[ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij)]` and `P_(ij) = ev_Q([ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij)])` by (E5).

**Base `z = 0`.** On legal `ρ`, `f^b_(ij,0)(ρ) = [ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij), ω(ρ(0))_((i,j)) = b]`. By (E4) and (E5),
`F_(ij)(0) = sum_(a ∈ S_(ij), ω(a)_((i,j)) = b) E'_a(0)`, which is the definition of `E_b(0)`.

**Step `z -> z + e_1`.** `E_b(z + e_1) = A_1 E_b(z) B_1`. By the induction hypothesis and the definitions,

`(A_1 E_b(z) B_1 v)_(ij) = F_(i+1,j)(z) M_(>i+1)(0) v_(ij) + M_(=i+1)(0) u_1 F_(0j)(z) sum_(i') u_1^(-1) M_(=i'+1)(0) v_(i'j)`.

By (4.1) and (E3), `τ := u_1 F_(0j)(z) u_1^(-1) = ev_(K+e_1)(g^b_(0j,z) ∘ sh_(e_1))`, and `K + e_1 ⊆ Q` (Lemma 4), so
`τ ∈ 𝒞_Q` commutes with `M_(=i+1)(0)`. Hence `M_(=i+1)(0) τ M_(=i'+1)(0) = δ_(ii') τ M_(=i+1)(0)`, and

`(E_b(z+e_1) v)_(ij) = [F_(i+1,j)(z) M_(>i+1)(0) + τ M_(=i+1)(0)] v_(ij)`.

By (E1), (E2) and (E5), the bracket is `ev_Q(h)` with
`h(ρ) = [w(ρ(0)) > i+1] f^b_(i+1,j,z)(ρ) + [w(ρ(0)) = i+1] g^b_(0j,z)(ρ(·+e_1)|_K)`.
Since `v_(ij) = P_(ij) v_(ij)`, `ev_Q(h) v_(ij) = ev_Q(h · [ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij)] · 1_L) v_(ij)`, by (E1) and (E4). By Lemma 4
this function equals `f^b_(ij,z+e_1)`, so the bracket acts on `v_(ij)` as `F_(ij)(z+e_1)`.

**Step `z -> z + e_2`.** The same, with `A_2, B_2, u_2, N`, Lemma 3(b) and the second half of Lemma 4. ∎

## 5. Theorem A

**Theorem A.** Assume (H1), (H2) and `D' >= 1`. Let `(W', E', u)` be a nonzero finite-dimensional covariant
`D'`-family for `Ω_S` over a field `k`. Then `(W_T, E, A)` from §3–§4 is a nonzero finite-dimensional covariant
`2D'`-family for `Ω_T` over `k`, with `dim W_T = sum_(a ∈ S) w(a) h(a) rank E'_a(0)`.

*Proof.* `A_1, A_2` are commuting invertibles (Lemma 2), and covariance `E_b(z) = A^z E_b(0) A^(-z)` holds by
definition. (Q1) was shown in §4. Nonzero and dimension: §3.

**(Q2) at scale `2D'`.** Let `|z - z'| <= 4D'`, and let `m` be the coordinatewise minimum of `z` and `z'`. Then
`z - m` and `z' - m` lie in `Box = [0,4D']^2`. By covariance `A^(-m) E_b(z) A^m = E_b(z - m)`, and likewise for
`z'`. By Proposition 5, `E_b(z - m)` and `E_c(z' - m)` are diagonal with entries in the commutative algebra `𝒞_Q`
(E1), so they commute. Conjugating by `A^m` gives `[E_b(z), E_c(z')] = 0`.

**(Q3) at scale `2D'`.** By covariance it suffices to take the base point `z_0 = (2D', 2D')`, so that the sites
`z_0 + h`, `h ∈ B_(2D')`, are exactly `Box`. Let `π ∈ T^(B_(2D'))` be illegal for `Ω_T`. By Proposition 5 each factor
`E_(π(h))(z_0 + h)` is diagonal with entries in `𝒞_Q`. So the product `P = prod_(h ∈ B_(2D')) E_(π(h))(z_0 + h)` (in
any order: the factors commute by (Q2)) is diagonal, and by (E1)

`(P v)_(ij) = ev_Q(φ_(ij)) v_(ij)`,  `φ_(ij) = prod_(h ∈ B_(2D')) f^(π(h))_(ij, z_0 + h)`.

Suppose `φ_(ij)(ρ) = 1`. Then `ρ ∈ L_Q(Ω_S)`, `ρ(0) ∈ S_(ij)`, and the letter of `ω(ρ)` at `(i,j) + z_0 + h` is
`π(h)` for every `h ∈ B_(2D')`. All these positions lie inside `ω(ρ)` (§4, after the definition of `f`). So the
sub-block of `ω(ρ)` on `(i,j) + Box` is a translate of `π`. By (H1), `ω(ρ) ∈ L(Ω_T)`. Legality is factorial and
shift-invariant, so `π ∈ L_(B_(2D'))(Ω_T)`, a contradiction. Hence `φ_(ij) = 0`, `ev_Q(φ_(ij)) = 0` and `P = 0`. ∎

**Remarks.**
- The scale gain comes from (M3): a parent box of side `2D' + 1` covers at least `4D' + 2` child columns and rows.
  With all shapes `>= (s,s)` the same bookkeeping should give scale `sD'`. That is not used and not checked here;
  only `s = 2` is claimed.
- The family is over the same field `k`; nothing is extended.
- For `Ω_S = Ω_T` and `ω` of constant shape `(s,s)`, every `P_(ij) = 1`, so `W_T = W'^(s^2)`. This is the family-level
  analogue of the torus-level inflation of `constant-shape-substitutions-inflate-torus-tiling-modules`.

  module. By DESC, for every `D` some `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω,k)` has one. By TC(d), `R_(Ω,k)` is exactly matricial. ∎

**Theorem C.** Theorem B applies to Labbé's Wang shift `Ω_U` (19 tiles) with the morphism `ω^2`, where

*Proof.*
- `Ω_U` is a free minimal nearest-neighbour SFT, so `r = 1` (`minimal-aperiodic-wang-shift-exists`).
- `ω` is a 2-dimensional morphism `Ω_U -> Ω_U` whose letter images have shape `<= (2,2)`

## 8. Limits, sanity checks, what is not claimed

- **Row 7 is not decided.** Theorem C only reduces it to one question: does some `𝒯_(1,Λ)(Ω_U, F_q)` have a nonzero
  finite-dimensional module? Every reviewed firewall still applies at scale `1`:
  - such a module is contextual (commutative ones give periodic legal points);
  - its torus has both axis periods `>= 6` (thin cylinders at `D = 1`: period `<= 4D+1` kills the family);
  - noncommuting site pairs in narrow strips.
  Theorem A says nothing about existence.
- **What changes.** Before, a "no" answer needed an obstruction at unboundedly many scales, or a scale depending on
  unknown constants. Now any obstruction to scale-`1` torus modules decides row 7 negatively, and any single scale-`1`
  module decides it positively. Scale `1` involves only the `B_1`-language, i.e. legal `3×3` patches of `Ω_U`, and
  commutation within sup-distance `2`.
- **Classical specialization.** Suppose `Ω_S` has a periodic point `x'`, and the parent family is classical: `W' = k^O`
  on its finite orbit `O`, with diagonal idempotents and the shift as `u`. Then `W_T` has the basis of the finite
  Kakutani--Rokhlin tower over `O`, and `E_b(z)` reads the letters of `ω(x')`. So Theorem A is the operator version of
  "`ω` maps tilings to tilings". Periods: a horizontal parent period `ℓ` gives the child period
  `sum_(c<ℓ) w(x'(c,0)) >= 2ℓ`. So the thin-cylinder bound `ℓ >= 4D'+2` gives `>= 8D'+4 > 4(2D')+1`. No conflict.
  (`Ω_U` itself has no periodic points; this is only a consistency check of the construction.)
- **Growth.** With `ω^2`, `dim W_T <= 9 dim W'` per doubling, and period lattices grow. This is compatible with
  `sft-tiling-module-dimensions-and-periods-must-diverge` (dimensions `-> ∞`), which Theorem A does not contradict.
- **Why the firewall recorded in the target's Attempts is bypassed rather than refuted.**
  - The un-labbe-torus-decider heuristic says a renormalized *torus* witness must keep a non-scalar skeleton. That
    remains plausible, and nothing here contradicts it.
  - Theorem A never builds an inflated torus. It builds a covariant family on a tower space, where the skeleton
    projections `M(0)`, `N(0)` are allowed to be non-scalar.
  - Only afterwards does TC(b) restrict to the finite period torus of the new translations `A_1, A_2`. That torus is
    not an "inflated" torus, and its sites are not tied to parent sites.
- **No constant-shape self-similarity is used.** The golden-rotation eigenvalue remark in
  `self-similar-sft-matricial-iff-one-torus-module` is irrelevant here.
- **Scope of Theorem B.** It needs a 2-dimensional morphism on the language with shapes `>= (2,2)` and
  `ω(L) ⊆ L`. It does not need recognizability, primitivity or ontoness.
- **Relation to existing nodes.**
  - Theorem B strengthens `self-similar-sft-matricial-iff-one-torus-module`: scale `3` becomes scale `1`, and constant
    shape becomes letter-dependent shape.
  - Theorem A is a family-level, shape-consistent analogue of
    `constant-shape-substitutions-inflate-torus-tiling-modules`.
