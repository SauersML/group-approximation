# A simple finitary compression defect that survives finite models

Date: 2026-09-08. Written mathematical proof; no new Lean verification claim.

The example below satisfies the algebraic and uniform active-rank hypotheses
of the rank-weighted compression theorem, but omits that theorem's property-T
hypothesis. Its defect survives an explicit MF embedding. Thus it does not
contradict the conditional theorem; it tests a proposed deletion of its
transport input.

That theorem imposes no separate finite-generation hypothesis on the
compressed subgroup. The subgroup used here is infinitely generated.

## 1. The group and the compression cell

Let \(V=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_2e_i\). For a finite subset
\(I\subset\mathbb Z\), regard \(\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb F_2^I)\) as
acting on the corresponding coordinate subspace and as the identity on its
complement. Put

\[
A=\bigcup_{I\subset\mathbb Z,\ I\text{ finite}}
       \operatorname{GL}(\mathbb F_2^I).
\]

This is the group \(\operatorname{GL}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb F_2)\).
Any finitely many of its elements belong to one finite coordinate group,
so \(A\) is locally finite. Let \(t\) be the basis shift
\(te_i=e_{i+1}\), and let

\[
G=A\rtimes\langle t\rangle,
\qquad H=\operatorname{GL}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\mathbb F_2).
\]

The action of \(t\) preserves \(A\). The only shift power in \(A\) is
the identity, because a nonzero shift moves infinitely many basis vectors.
Thus this semidirect product also embeds in \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\).

For distinct indices write \(x_{ij}=I+E_{ij}\). Direct multiplication gives

\[
tx_{ij}t^{-1}=x_{i+1,j+1},\qquad
[x_{ij},x_{jk}]=x_{ik}\quad(i,j,k\text{ pairwise distinct}).   \tag{1}
\]

Set

\[
c=x_{-2,-1},\qquad b=x_{0,1},\qquad a=x_{-1,1}.
\]

The supports of \(c\) and every element of \(H\) are disjoint, so \(c\)
centralizes \(H\). The shift gives

\[
tHt^{-1}=\operatorname{GL}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb Z_{\geq1},\mathbb F_2)
   \lneq H;
\]

strictness is witnessed by \(b\). By (1),

\[
[tct^{-1},b]=[x_{-1,0},x_{0,1}]=x_{-1,1}=a.                 \tag{2}
\]

The matrix \(a\) is nonidentity and \(a^2=I\).

The group \(G\) is generated by \(t,x_{0,1},x_{1,0}\). Indeed their shift
conjugates give all adjacent transvections in either direction, and iterating
(1) gives \(x_{ij}\) for all distinct \(i,j\). These generate every finite
coordinate general linear group: Gaussian elimination over
\(\mathbb F_2\) uses row additions and row swaps, with a swap equal to
\(x_{ij}x_{ji}x_{ij}\); there are no nontrivial diagonal scalar units.

## 2. Simplicity and the exact normal defect

We prove simplicity of \(A\) directly. Let \(N\lhd A\) contain some
\(g\ne I\). Choose a finite coordinate set supporting \(g\), an index
\(j\) in it with \(ge_j\ne e_j\), and an index \(k\) outside it.
Because \(g\) fixes both \(e_k\) and its coordinate functional, one has

\[
[g,x_{jk}]=I+(ge_j-e_j)e_k^*.                               \tag{3}
\]

Here subtraction and addition agree over \(\mathbb F_2\). The vector
\(v=ge_j-e_j\) is nonzero and has zero \(k\)-coordinate, so (3) is a
nonidentity transvection in \(N\). A basis change in the finite supporting
subspace can send \(v\) to a standard basis vector \(e_i\) while fixing
the complementary coordinate \(k\). Conjugation by that basis change sends
(3) to \(x_{ik}\). All standard transvections are conjugate by finitely
supported permutation matrices. Thus \(N\) contains all \(x_{pq}\), hence
all of \(A\). This proves simplicity. The group is nonabelian by (1).

Define \(D_G(H)\) to be the normal subgroup of \(G\) generated by

\[
[ucu^{-1},h],\qquad
uHu^{-1}\subseteq H,\quad c\in C_G(H),\quad h\in H.
\]

Equation (2) gives \(a\in D_G(H)\). Since \(A\) is normal in \(G\) and
simple, the normal closure of \(a\) in \(G\) is \(A\). Therefore
\(A\subseteq D_G(H)\). Conversely \(G/A\cong\mathbb Z\) is abelian,
so every commutator of \(G\) lies in \(A\). This proves

\[
D_G(H)=A.                                                   \tag{4}
\]

## 3. Every finite multiplication table has a faithful periodic model

Let \(F\subset G\) be finite. Write each \(g\in F\) uniquely as
\((A_g,k_g)\), with \(A_g\in A\) and \(k_g\in\mathbb Z\). The
multiplication rule is

\[
(A,k)(B,l)=(A\,t^kBt^{-k},k+l).                             \tag{5}
\]

Choose an integer \(R\geq1\) such that \(|k_g|\leq R\) and every
\(A_g\) is supported in \([-R,R]\). Choose an integer \(N>4R+1\).
The map \(i\mapsto\bar i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) is injective on
\([-2R,2R]\).

Let \(P_N\) be the cyclic permutation matrix on
\(\mathbb F_2^{\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z}\), defined by
\(P_Ne_{\bar i}=e_{\overline{i+1}}\). Define the finite group

\[
K_N=\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb F_2)\rtimes\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z,
\qquad
(B,\bar k)(C,\bar l)=(BP_N^kCP_N^{-k},\overline{k+l}).        \tag{6}
\]

For each \(g\in F\), form \(\overline{A_g}\) by transferring its matrix
on \([-R,R]\) to the corresponding distinct cyclic coordinates and taking
the identity elsewhere. Set

\[
\phi_F(g)=(\overline{A_g},\overline{k_g})\in K_N.             \tag{7}
\]

This map is injective. If two exponents differ, their difference has
absolute value at most \(2R<N\), so their residues differ. If the exponents
agree but the elements differ, their finite supporting matrices differ, and
the injective coordinate transfer preserves that difference.

To check multiplication, suppose \(g,h,gh\in F\). The supports of
\(A_g\), \(A_h\), and \(t^{k_g}A_ht^{-k_g}\) all lie in
\([-2R,2R]\). Transfer on this interval is an actual embedding of its
finite general linear group into \(\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb F_2)\).
Moreover it takes the shifted matrix to
\(P_N^{k_g}\overline{A_h}P_N^{-k_g}\). Consequently (5) gives

\[
\overline{A_{gh}}
 =\overline{A_g}P_N^{k_g}\overline{A_h}P_N^{-k_g},
\qquad \overline{k_{gh}}=\overline{k_g+k_h}.
\]

Equations (6)--(7) imply
\(\phi_F(gh)=\phi_F(g)\phi_F(h)\). Thus every finite multiplication
table embeds in a finite group. This proves that \(G\) is LEF by an
explicit construction.

The cyclic exponent factor in (6) also makes injectivity transparent; no
identification of a nonzero global shift with a finitary matrix is made.

## 4. The resulting MF embedding retains the marked involution

Choose increasing finite sets \(F_n\) exhausting the countable group \(G\),
all containing \(1,a\). Apply Section 3 to obtain finite groups \(K_n\)
and the partial embeddings \(\phi_n\). Let \(\lambda_n\) be the left
regular representation of \(K_n\) on \(\ell^2(K_n)\). For \(g\in F_n\)
put \(U_n(g)=\lambda_n(\phi_n(g))\); assign any unitary, for example the
identity, to the other \(g\).

For every fixed \(g,h\in G\), the elements \(g,h,gh\) eventually belong
to \(F_n\). Thus \(U_n(gh)=U_n(g)U_n(h)\) eventually. The classes of
these sequences define a homomorphism

\[
\rho:G\longrightarrow
U\left(\prod_nM_{|K_n|}(\mathbb C)\big/
                     \bigoplus_nM_{|K_n|}(\mathbb C)\right). \tag{8}
\]

Here the product consists of norm-bounded sequences and the sum consists of
norm-null sequences. For \(g\ne1\), injectivity of \(\phi_n\) gives
\(\phi_n(g)\ne1\) eventually. Applied to the unit vector at the group
identity, the matrices \(U_n(g)-I\) then have norm \(\sqrt2\). Therefore

\[
\|U_n(g)-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\sqrt2
\]

eventually, proving that (8) is injective.

For the specified involution \(a\), the finite element \(\phi_n(a)\)
has order exactly two: the table preserves \(a^2=1\) and does not identify
\(a\) with \(1\). Left translation by \(\phi_n(a)\) consists of
two-cycles. Thus its negative spectral projection has rank \(|K_n|/2\).
The mark survives even with this fixed positive normalized rank in the
displayed corona embedding.

## 5. The uniform active-rank bound still holds

Take the standard exhaustion

\[
A_j=\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb F_2^{[-j,j]}),\qquad j\geq2.
\]

Each \(A_j\) contains \(a\). Since \(\mathbb F_2^*=\{1\}\), this
finite group equals \(\operatorname{SL}_{2j+1}(\mathbb F_2)\). The marked
matrix \(a\) is conjugate to a standard transvection. The established
[uniform transvection estimate](../binary-finitary-transvection-uniformly-controls-active-rank.md)
therefore gives, for every finite-dimensional complex unitary representation
\(\sigma\) of \(A_j\),

\[
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Fix}\sigma(A_j)
\leq\bigl(4-2^{3-(2j+1)}\bigr)
        \operatorname{rank}(\sigma(a)-I)
\leq4\operatorname{rank}(\sigma(a)-I).                       \tag{9}
\]

The estimate includes reducible representations with any number of trivial
summands. Its proof uses the transitive action of the finite Levi group on
nonzero row characters, and the row and column root groups generating the
finite special linear group. It contains no approximation or transport input.

Equations (2), (4), (8), and (9) give the claimed counterexample: the full
simple locally finite normal compression defect satisfies the uniform rank
bound while its marked root survives an MF embedding. A proof of the
independent Jacobson head-collapse target must therefore use additional
relations beyond these algebraic and rank ingredients.
