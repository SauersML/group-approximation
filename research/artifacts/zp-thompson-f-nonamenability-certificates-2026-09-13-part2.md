# Thompson's group F: the C*-simplicity certificate class

Lane `z2-01-f-nonamenable`, 2026-09-13. Part 2 of
`zp-thompson-f-nonamenability-certificates-2026-09-13.md`. It turns non-amenability of `F`
into the existence of one kind of subgroup of Thompson's group `T` (Theorem D), and records
what that subgroup can and cannot look like. It decides nothing about amenability of `F`.

`T` acts on the circle `S^1 = [0,1]/(0 ~ 1)` by PL homeomorphisms with dyadic breakpoints
and slopes powers of 2, and `Stab_T(0) = F`. For `g ∈ T`, `supp(g)` is the open set of points
moved by `g`. A *disjoint pair* is two nontrivial elements `a, b` with
`supp(a) ∩ supp(b) = ∅`, i.e. every point of `S^1` is fixed by `a` or by `b`. Disjoint pairs
commute.

## 1. Imported equivalence

Read verbatim from the arXiv LaTeX sources on 2026-09-13, fetched on MSI:
- A. Le Boudec and N. Matte Bon, *Subgroup dynamics and C*-simplicity of groups of
  homeomorphisms*, arXiv:1605.01651v3, lines 234–241 (Theorem 1.6 in the ar5iv numbering):
  "The following statements about Thompson's groups F and T are equivalent: (i) The group F is
  non-amenable; (ii) The group F is C*-simple; (iii) The group T is C*-simple." Line 232 gives
  a stronger form: the existence of one non-C*-simple overgroup of `F` inside `Homeo(S^1)`
  would imply that `F` is amenable. An ar5iv summarizing fetch reports their Theorem 1.7:
  the uniformly recurrent subgroups of `T` are `1`, `T` and the stabilizer URS of the circle
  action, and those of `F` are the normal subgroups. That statement was not checked against
  the source.
- U. Haagerup and K. K. Olesen, *Non-inner amenability of the Thompson groups T and V*,
  arXiv:1609.05086, lines 810–813 (Theorem 4.5 in the ar5iv numbering): "If C*_r(T) is
  simple, then F is non-amenable." The proof (lines 814–891) uses the quasi-regular
  representation `π` on the dyadic orbit, weakly contained in `λ_T` when `F` is amenable,
  and commuting `a, b ∈ T` for which "the values (a(x), b(x)) and (ab(x), x) agree, up to a
  permutation". So `π(a + b − ab − e) = 0`, and `a, b` form a disjoint pair. Their
  Proposition 4.8 (label `ideal`, lines 963–995) lists equivalent forms. Among them: "The
  closed two-sided ideal generated by 1 + λ(ab) − λ(a) − λ(b) in C*_r(T) is all of C*_r(T)",
  and "The closed convex hull of { λ(hah^-1) + λ(hbh^-1) : h ∈ T } contains 0". They record
  that Breuillard–Kalantar–Kennedy–Ozawa re-obtained Theorem 4.5.

## 2. Theorem D: C*-simple subgroups of T with a disjoint pair

**Theorem D.** Let `H ≤ T` contain a disjoint pair `a, b`. If the reduced C*-algebra
`C*_r(H)` is simple, then `F` is not amenable.

*Proof.*
1. *A nonzero element.* Put `x = 1 − λ(a) − λ(b) + λ(ab) ∈ C[H]`. The group elements
   `1, a, b, ab` are pairwise distinct:
   - `a ≠ 1` and `b ≠ 1`;
   - `a = b` would make `supp(a) = supp(b)` nonempty and disjoint from itself;
   - `ab = 1` gives `b = a^(-1)` with the same support, a contradiction;
   - `ab ≠ a` and `ab ≠ b` because `b ≠ 1` and `a ≠ 1`.

   So `x ≠ 0` in `C[H]`, hence in `C*_r(H)`.
2. *It dies in the quasi-regular representation.* Let `π` be the quasi-regular representation
   of `T` on `l^2(T·0)`, where `T·0` is the dyadic rationals of the circle and
   `π(g)δ_q = δ_(gq)`. For each `q`, `π(x)δ_q = δ_q − δ_(aq) − δ_(bq) + δ_(abq)`.
   - If `aq = q`: either `bq = q`, or `bq ∈ supp(b)`, which `a` fixes. Either way
     `abq = bq`, and the sum is `0`.
   - If `aq ≠ q`: then `q ∈ supp(a)`, so `bq = q` and `abq = aq`, and the sum is `0`.

   So `π(x) = 0`.
3. *If F is amenable, π factors through C*_r(T).* `π = Ind_F^T 1_F`. If `F` is amenable,
   `1_F` is weakly contained in `λ_F`. Induction preserves weak containment (Fell) and
   `Ind_F^T λ_F = λ_T`, so `π ≺ λ_T`, and `π` extends to a unital *-representation `π~` of
   `C*_r(T)`.
4. *Restriction to H.* `λ_T` restricted to `H` is a multiple of `λ_H`, so `C*_r(H)` sits
   unitally and isometrically inside `C*_r(T)`. The restriction `σ` of `π~` to `C*_r(H)` is a
   unital *-representation with `σ(x) = π(x) = 0`.
5. *Contradiction.* `C*_r(H)` is simple and `σ(1) = 1 ≠ 0`, so `σ` is injective. But
   `x ≠ 0` and `σ(x) = 0`. Hence `F` is not amenable. ∎

This is the Haagerup–Olesen mechanism behind Theorem 4.5, stated for subgroups; their proof
was not re-read, only the disjoint-support property of their `a, b`. The only input about `F`
is `Stab_T(0) = F`.

**Converse.** If `F` is not amenable, then `T` is C*-simple (Le Boudec–Matte Bon, Theorem 1.6),
and `T` contains disjoint pairs, e.g. two elements of `F` supported in disjoint dyadic
intervals. Hence:

> `F` is non-amenable ⟺ some C*-simple subgroup of `T` contains a disjoint pair.

The certificate is local: one finitely generated subgroup `⟨a, b, g_1, ..., g_k⟩ ≤ T`
together with a proof that it is C*-simple.

## 3. Which subgroups could serve

1. **`H = T`.** C*-simple iff `F` is non-amenable (Theorem 1.6). Circular.
2. **Micro-supported subgroups acting minimally.** Le Boudec–Matte Bon, Corollary 1.3: if
   every rigid stabilizer `H_U` (`U` nonempty open) is non-amenable, then `H` is C*-simple.
   For `H ≤ T` and `U` a proper open subset of `S^1`, `H_U` lies in the rigid stabilizer of
   `U` in `T`, a subgroup of a product of copies of `F`. Circular.
3. **Free products `H = A * B` with `B ≠ 1` and a disjoint pair `a, b ∈ A`.** These are
   C*-simple (Paschke–Salinas; not re-read), so an embedding would decide the problem.
   Constraints:
   - *Lemma E* (`free-product-disjoint-pair-no-support-separating-element`). In any action,
     for all `w ∈ H \ A` and `u, v ∈ A \ {1}`: `w(supp u) ∩ supp v ≠ ∅`. Otherwise `w u w^(-1)`
     and `v` have disjoint supports and commute. In a free product the centralizer of
     `v ∈ A \ {1}` lies in `A`, and `A ∩ w A w^(-1) = 1` for `w ∉ A`, which forces `u = 1`.
   - *Classical ping-pong fails.* A ping-pong table for `A * B` needs a set `X_B` moved off
     itself by every nontrivial element of `A`, so `X_B ⊆ supp(a) ∩ supp(b) = ∅`.
   - *No separating dynamics.* No `w ∈ H \ A` may map `supp(a)` into `Fix(b)`. For instance no
     `w ∉ A` may contract `S^1 \ N` into `M` for intervals `N ⊆ supp(b)` and `M ⊆ supp(a)`.
     So `H` has no north–south element between the two supports.
   - *Dynamics of powers.* For `h ∈ H \ A` of infinite order with `⟨h⟩ ∩ A = 1` (e.g. `h = βa`
     with `β ∈ B \ {1}`), and `k` with `h^k` having a fixed point (every element of `T` has
     rational rotation number, by Ghys–Sergiescu; not re-read), Lemma E applies to every
     `h^(kN)`, `N ≠ 0`. `Fix(h^k)` is a finite union of points and closed intervals, and
     `h^k` pushes each complementary interval toward one endpoint. So `supp(a)` and `supp(b)`
     must each contain points near attracting and repelling endpoints in a pattern that
     interleaves them in both directions. If some `u ∈ A \ {1}` has support compactly inside
     `S^1 \ Fix(h^k)`, then `h^(kN)(supp u) ∩ supp u = ∅` for large `N`, a relation.

   Status: not excluded. No construction known.
4. **Acylindrically hyperbolic subgroups with trivial amenable radical** (C*-simple by
   Dahmani–Guirardel–Osin; not re-read) containing a disjoint pair. No candidate known.
   Lemma E has an analogue for any C*-simple subgroup only through Theorem D itself, so it
   gives no free obstruction here.

## 4. Reading

If `F` is amenable, then no C*-simple subgroup of `T` contains two commuting elements with
disjoint supports. That is a strong structural prediction about `T`, which contains free
groups, `PSL_2(Z)` and many C*-simple subgroups. Conversely, one explicit C*-simple subgroup
with a disjoint pair would prove non-amenability. The free-product version is the most
concrete target: an embedding `(Z^2) * Z ↪ T` whose `Z^2` factor is generated by a disjoint
pair, respecting the constraints of §3.3. The sharpest open question on this side:

> Does `T` contain a subgroup isomorphic to `A * B` (`B ≠ 1`) with a disjoint pair in `A`?

A negative answer would be a consequence of amenability of `F`, so a proof that no such
subgroup exists would be evidence for amenability, not a decision.
