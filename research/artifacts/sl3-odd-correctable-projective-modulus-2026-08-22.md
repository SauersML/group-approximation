# Uniform projective transfer on the odd-correctable SL3 sector

The established odd-congruence collapse theorem has a direct quantitative
compactness consequence that was not previously wired to the projective
frontier.

Let ambient presentation defect, distance of the `SL_3(Z)` restriction to an
exact congruence representation of level prime to `p`, and total projective
subgroup energy all tend to zero.  Then projective energy at the denominator
diagonal tends to zero uniformly over dimensions, levels, irreducibles, and
multiplicities.

The proof uses no canonical trace.  In a matrix ultraproduct the auxiliary
unitary is projectively `SL_3(Z)`-central.  Perfectness removes the scalar
character, and the exact odd-congruence relative-commutant theorem transfers
ordinary centrality to `SL_3(Z[1/p])`.

Thus an existential modulus

```text
eta_odd(delta + correction_distance + subgroup_projective_energy) -> 0
```

is automatic.  This does not give the linear constant `K` demanded by full
PRCC, nor does it touch uniformly noncorrectable lattice microstates.  Its
value is the exact far-sector confinement: a fixed-gap projective enemy must
either have no correction through levels coprime to the denominator prime,
or remain uniformly far from the entire exact representation variety of the
lattice.

For `p=2`, every remaining correctable enemy therefore uses a congruence
level with a two-primary component (possibly together with an odd component).
This aligns arbitrary projective witnesses with the previously isolated
mixed/two-primary branching problem; it is not restricted to the native lamp
or the quasi-regular leak.  No quantitative lower bound on the two-primary
mass is claimed here.
