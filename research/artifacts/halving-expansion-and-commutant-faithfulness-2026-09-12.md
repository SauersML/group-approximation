## 0. Summary

* **Section 1 (elementary).** The halving relation is coefficient additivity
  `x_12(1) = x_12(e_0) x_12(e_1)`, `e_i = s_i t_i`, inside `iota_P(UT_3(R))`. It factors the
  parent defect as `D_P = (gamma zeta - 1)(alpha beta - 1)`, with `alpha, gamma` in the algebra of
  the units supported on `U_X = P00 ∪ P100 ∪ P110` and `beta, zeta` in the commuting algebra of
  `U_Y = P01 ∪ P101 ∪ P111`. The Heisenberg relation gives the exact identity
  `D + nm = e (1+n)(1+m)`, so `rk(N_23 N_12 + N_12 N_23) = rk(N_13)`.
* **Section 2 (established): commutant faithfulness.** At near-minimal defect the range corner of a
  frame-triple defect is rank-faithful, up to `eps/(1+eps)`, for every element and every matrix of
  the commutant of the clopen cylinder group containing it (Theorem 2.1). Proposition 4.1 of the
  descent artifact is the case of a product of disjoint defects. So the target is one instance of a
  weaker sufficient statement: a commutant element whose rank the defect range distorts by a
  model-independent fraction (Proposition 2.3, open claim
  `some-commutant-element-distorts-defect-range-rank`).
* **Section 3 (cited): the halving expansion dies at a locally finite group.** Everything in
  Section 1 lies in `iota_(P0)(UT_3(R)) x iota_(P1)(UT_3(R)) x| <iota_P(w)>`, whose regular model has
  `theta = 1`. This is Proposition 5.2 of `research/artifacts/rank-four-orthogonality-check-2026-09-12.md`
  (lane `w4-r4-orth`, f25ccc5958). The same model satisfies Theorem 2.1 exactly for unitriangular
  inputs.
* **Section 4 (established): root-group halves.** Every subproduct of disjoint transvections is conjugate
  to one transvection, so two disjoint halves `a, b` have `rk(a) = rk(b) = rk(a+b) = rho`. That forces
  `rk(aM + bM) + rk(Ma + Mb) <= 3 rho` (Theorem 4.3). So halves independent as left and right ideals
  displace `0` or `1/2`. At near-minimal displacement the ranges of disjoint halves overlap in rank at
  least `rho - rho^2 - eps rho` (Proposition 4.5). Claim
  `disjoint-root-halves-row-plus-column-rank-at-most-three-rho`.
* **Section 5.** The opposite-root `S_3` at a child pair splits `rho = d_3/2 + rk(a q_3)`. Where it stops.

## 1. The halving expansion

**Setting.**
* A *clopen proper* set `U` is a finite union of cylinders with nonempty complement. Put
  `p_U = sum_C S[C]T[C]` over a cylinder partition of `U`, and let `G_U` be the group of units `g`
  of `R` with `g - 1 = p_U (g - 1) p_U`. For a cylinder `C`, `G_C = iota_C(R^x)`.
* For disjoint cylinders `C, C'` put `tau(C <- C') = 1 + S[C]T[C']`, an involution.
* A *frame triple* `Theta = (C_1, C_2, C_3)` is a triple of pairwise disjoint cylinders with proper
  union. Its defect is `D_Theta = (sigma(tau(C_2 <- C_3)) - 1)(sigma(tau(C_1 <- C_2)) - 1)`. For
  `Theta = (A0, A10, A11)` this is `D_A`, because the code is `(0, 10, 11)`.
* `M_U` is the subalgebra generated by `sigma(G_U)`, and `C_U` is the commutant of `sigma(G_U)`.

**Lemma 1.1 (transitivity).** Let `U, U'` be clopen proper, and let `(C_i)` and `(C'_i)` be
`k`-tuples of pairwise disjoint cylinders inside `U` and `U'`, each with union a proper subset of
`U` (resp. `U'`). Then some `v` in `V` has `v G_U v^-1 = G_(U')` and
`v tau(C_i <- C_j) v^-1 = tau(C'_i <- C'_j)` for all `i != j`. If `U = U'`, `v` can be chosen in
`V ∩ G_U`.

*Proof.* Split `U` minus the union of the `C_i`, `U'` minus the union of the `C'_i`, and the
complements of `U` and `U'` into equal numbers of cylinders, and take the prefix replacement matching
the pieces. When `U = U'`, match the complement of `U` to itself identically. QED

Consequences, as in Fact 1.2 and Lemma 3.0 of the descent artifact:
* all frame-triple defects have one rank `delta(sigma)`, and all transvection nilpotents
  `sigma(tau(C <- C')) - 1` with `C ∪ C'` proper have one rank `rho(sigma)`;
* `Fix_r(sigma(G_U)) = sigma(v) Fix_r(sigma(iota_C(R^x))) = fM` for every clopen proper `U`.

**Lemma 1.2 (halving).** Let `P` be a proper cylinder. In the `iota_P` frame,

```text
tau(P0 <- P10)  = tau(P00 <- P100) · tau(P01 <- P101)      (= x_12(e_0) x_12(e_1)),
tau(P10 <- P11) = tau(P100 <- P110) · tau(P101 <- P111)    (= x_23(e_0) x_23(e_1)),
```

with commuting factors. Put `alpha = sigma(tau(P00 <- P100))`, `beta = sigma(tau(P01 <- P101))`,
`gamma = sigma(tau(P100 <- P110))`, `zeta = sigma(tau(P101 <- P111))`, and `a = alpha - 1`,
`b = beta - 1`, `c = gamma - 1`, `d = zeta - 1`. Then:
1. `alpha, gamma` lie in `M_(U_X)` and `beta, zeta` in `M_(U_Y)`, and these two algebras commute.
2. `D_P = (gamma zeta - 1)(alpha beta - 1)`.
3. With the child defects `X = ca = D_(P00, P100, P110)` and `Y = db = D_(P01, P101, P111)`,

   ```text
   D_P = (1 + b + d) X + (1 + a + c) Y + XY + cb + da .
   ```

   The cross terms `cb` and `da` are products of commuting transvection nilpotents on four pairwise
   disjoint cylinders.

*Proof.* `S[P0]T[P10] = S[P0](s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1)T[P10] = S[P00]T[P100] + S[P01]T[P101]`, and the two
summands multiply to `0` in both orders; similarly for `P10 <- P11`. Item 1: disjoint supports.
Item 2: `sigma(tau(P0 <- P10)) = alpha beta`. Item 3: expand `(c + d + cd)(a + b + ab)`. Here `a`
commutes with `b, d`; `c` commutes with `b, d`; `ab = ba`, `cd = dc`. Hence `b, d` commute with `X`,
`a, c` commute with `Y`, and `XY = YX`. QED

**Lemma 1.3 (Heisenberg identity).** For a frame triple put `n = N_12`, `m = N_23`,
`e = N_13 = sigma(tau(C_1 <- C_3)) - 1`. In characteristic two

```text
D + nm = e (1+n)(1+m) ,        hence        rk(D + nm) = rk(e) = rho .
```

*Proof.* `[x_12, x_23] = x_13`, and `e` commutes with `n` and `m`. Squaring `(1+n)(1+m)` gives
`e = nm + mn + mnm + nmn + nmnm = nm + (1+n) D (1+m)`. Multiply by the involutions `1+n` on the left
and `1+m` on the right. QED

## 2. Commutant faithfulness at near-minimal defect

Throughout, `sigma` is nontrivial and fixed-point-free, with `delta = delta(sigma) <= (1+eps) c_*`.

**Lemma 2.0 (corner models of clopen groups).** Let `U` be clopen proper, and `k != 0` an idempotent
with `kM` invariant under `sigma(G_U)`. Let `psi = Ad(v) o iota_C : R^x -> G_U` be an isomorphism from
Lemma 1.1. Then `g -> k sigma(psi(g)) k` is a nontrivial fixed-point-free rank model on `kMk`, and
every frame triple `Theta` with union a proper subset of `U` has `rk(D_Theta k) >= c_* rk(k)`.

*Proof.*
* **Model.** `sigma(h) k = k sigma(h) k` for `h` in `G_U`, so it is a homomorphism (Lemma 1.1 of the
  defect-piece artifact).
* **No fixed vectors.** If `x` in `kM` has `k sigma(h) k x = x` for all `h` in `G_U`, then
  `sigma(h) x = x`, so `x` lies in `Fix_r(sigma(G_U)) = fM = 0`.
* **Nontrivial,** because a trivial model on a nonzero corner fixes it.
* **Defects.** Its defects at proper cylinders `C'` of its own frame are `D_(Theta') k`, with
  `Theta' = v(CC'0, CC'10, CC'11)`, of normalized rank at least `c_*`. For a general `Theta` inside
  `U` with proper union, Lemma 1.1 gives `u` in `V ∩ G_U` with `sigma(u) D_(Theta') sigma(u)^-1 = D_Theta`.
  Since `sigma(u)^(±1)` preserve `kM`, `rk(D_Theta k) = rk(D_(Theta') k)`. QED

**Theorem 2.1 (commutant faithfulness).** Let `U` be clopen proper, `Theta` a frame triple with union a
proper subset of `U`, `T = D_Theta`, `n >= 1`, and `Z` in `M_n(C_U)`. With ranks normalized in
`M_n(M)` and `T` acting diagonally,

```text
- eps delta rk(Z) / (1+eps)   <=   rk(TZ) - delta rk(Z)   <=   eps delta (1 - rk(Z)) / (1+eps) .
```

Equivalently, if `q` is the range idempotent of `T`, the unital homomorphism
`pi_q : M_n(C_U) -> M_n(qMq)`, `Z -> Zq`, satisfies `| rk(Zq)/rk(q) - rk(Z) | <= eps/(1+eps)`.

*Proof.*
* **Reduction to `n = 1`.** `sigma (x) 1_n` is fixed-point-free with the same `delta`, and its commutant
  contains `M_n(C_U)`.
* **Commutation.** `T` lies in `M_U`, so `ZT = TZ`.
* **Range corner.** `ZM` is invariant under `sigma(G_U)`. Lemma 2.0 with its range idempotent gives
  `rk(TZ) = rk(T ZM) >= c_* rk(Z)`.
* **Kernel corner.** `K = r.ann(Z)` has rank `1 - rk(Z)` and is invariant, so `rk(TK) >= c_*(1 - rk(Z))`.
* **Triangular count.** `T` maps `K` into `K`. Put `J = TM + K`. Then `rk(TM) >= rk(TK) + rk(J) - rk(K)`,
  and `rk(J) - rk(K) = rk(ZJ) = rk(ZTM) = rk(TZ)` by Fact 1.1(d). So `delta >= c_*(1 - rk Z) + rk(TZ)`.
* **Insert `c_* >= delta/(1+eps)`** into both bounds. For the equivalent form, `rk(Zq) = rk(ZTM) = rk(TZ)`
  and `rk(q) = delta`. QED

**Remark 2.2.**
* Proposition 4.1 of the descent artifact is `U = B`, `Z = D_(A_1) ... D_(A_k)`.
* In the Toeplitz group the gap fails, `c_* = 0`, and Theorem 2.1 is vacuous, as it must be.
* Theorem 2.1 controls ranks of products `TZ` only. It says nothing about sums or intersections of the
  ranges of other elements of `M_U`, such as the transvection halves of Section 4.

**Proposition 2.3 (a weaker sufficient statement).** Consider the statement **(C)**: there are
`kappa > 0`, a clopen proper `U`, a frame triple `Theta` with proper union inside `U`, and `n >= 1`, such
that every nontrivial fixed-point-free characteristic-two rank model `sigma` of `R^x` admits some `Z` in
`M_n(C_U)` with `| rk(D_Theta Z) - delta(sigma) rk(Z) | >= kappa delta(sigma)`.
1. **(C) closes the gate.** If a nontrivial model exists, the gap makes `c_* > 0`. A model with
   `eps < kappa` contradicts Theorem 2.1. So every model is trivial, and `R^x` is not `F_2`-linear
   sofic.
2. **The target implies (C).** Take `U = B`, `Theta` the frame of `B0`, and `Z = D_A` with `A` disjoint
   from `B` and `A ∪ B` proper. Then `rk(D_(B0) D_A) = f(2) <= theta delta^2`, so
   `delta rk(Z) - rk(TZ) >= (1 - theta) delta^2 >= (1 - theta) c_0 delta`.

(C) allows any element of the commutant: `sigma` of opposite roots, torus units and `V`-elements supported
off `U`, where no locally finite firewall applies.

## 3. The halving expansion dies at a locally finite group

**Proposition 3.1.** Let `P, Q` be disjoint proper cylinders with proper union.
* **Where the data live.** The children `a, b, c, d` of Lemma 1.2 are `sigma` of `x_12(e_0), x_12(e_1),
  x_23(e_0), x_23(e_1)` in the `iota_P` frame, so every element of Lemma 1.2 lies in the image of
  `F_2[iota_P(UT_3(R))]`. Products with the same data at `Q` lie in the image of `F_2[L]`, with
  `L = iota_P(UT_3(R)) x iota_Q(UT_3(R))`. `L` is locally finite, because `UT_3` over an `F_2`-algebra is
  nilpotent of exponent dividing 4.
* **The regular model of `L`** (item 2 of `unitriangular-data-cannot-assemble-corner-leavitt-families`)
  satisfies:
  * `rk(D_P) = rk(X) = rk(Y) = 3/8`, the free `D_8` profile;
  * `rk(cb) = rk(da) = 1/4`, the free `(Z/2)^2` profile;
  * `rk(D_P D_Q) = 9/64 = rk(D_P) rk(D_Q)`, so `theta = 1`;
  * `rk(TZ) = rk(T) rk(Z)` for `T` in `F_2[iota_P(UT_3(R))]` and `Z` in `F_2[iota_Q(UT_3(R))]`, so the
    conclusion of Theorem 2.1 holds with `eps = 0` for unitriangular inputs;
  * every rank equality between elements of `F_2[L]` induced by conjugation in `R^x`, since regular
    ranks are invariant under isomorphisms of finite subgroups.

So no derivation of `theta < 1` can use only: the halving identities at every depth, the relations of
`L`, conjugacy rank equalities inside `F_2[L]`, square zero, the Sylvester calculus, and Theorem 2.1 for
unitriangular inputs.

*Proof.*
* **`D_8` profile.** For `e` in `{1, e_0, e_1}`, `x_12(e)` and `x_23(e)` are involutions with central
  commutator `x_13(e) != 1`, so they generate `H_3(F_2) ~= D_8` on the same generators. The rank `3/8` is
  item 3 of the unitriangular firewall node.
* **`(Z/2)^2` profile.** `x_23(e_0)` and `x_12(e_1)` commute, because `e_1 e_0 = 0`. They generate
  `(Z/2)^2`, where `(g-1)(h-1)` has regular rank `1/4`.
* **Products.** `F_2[H_P x H_Q] = F_2[H_P] (x) F_2[H_Q]` for finite `H_P`, `H_Q`, and regular ranks
  multiply on simple tensors. QED

## 4. Root-group halves

Every transvection nilpotent `sigma(tau(C <- C')) - 1` with `C ∪ C'` proper has one rank `rho`
(Lemma 1.1). The root group `{1 + xi : xi in p_U R p_W}`, for disjoint clopen `U, W`, is normalized by the
block torus `G_U x G_W`. Halving refines it into commuting disjoint pieces, all of rank `rho`.

**Lemma 4.1 (subproducts).** Let `(C_i <- C'_i)`, `1 <= i <= k`, be pairs whose `2k` cylinders are pairwise
disjoint with proper union, and `q_i = sigma(tau(C_i <- C'_i)) - 1`. For every nonempty `S`,
`rk(prod_(i in S)(1 + q_i) - 1) = rho`.

*Proof.* `prod_(i in S) tau(C_i <- C'_i) = 1 + sum_(i in S) S[C_i]T[C'_i]`. Take disjoint cylinders `D, D'`
with proper union and a complete prefix code `(w_i)_(i in S)`. A prefix replacement sending `C_i -> D w_i`,
`C'_i -> D' w_i` and complement to complement conjugates the product to
`1 + sum S[D w_i]T[D' w_i] = tau(D <- D')`. QED

**Lemma 4.2 (kernels of sums).** If `a, b` commute and `a^2 = b^2 = 0`, then `a + b + ab = (1+a)(a+b)`, so
`rk((1+a)(1+b) - 1) = rk(a + b)` and the two kernels agree.

**Theorem 4.3 (row plus column).** Let `a, b` be commuting square-zero elements of `M` with
`rk(a) = rk(b) = rk(a+b) = rho`. Then

```text
rk(aM + bM) + rk(Ma + Mb)  <=  3 rho .
```

This applies to the nilpotents of two disjoint transvection pairs with proper union, in every
characteristic-two rank model of `R^x` (Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2).

*Proof.* `K = r.ann(a+b)` has rank `1 - rho`. On `K`, `x -> ax` has kernel `r.ann(a) ∩ r.ann(b)` and
image `aK`, which lies in `aM ∩ bM` because `ax = bx` there. So
`1 - rho <= rk(r.ann(a) ∩ r.ann(b)) + rk(aM ∩ bM)`. In a regular ring `r.ann(a) ∩ r.ann(b) = r.ann(Ma + Mb)`
has rank `1 - rk(Ma + Mb)`, and `rk(aM ∩ bM) = 2 rho - rk(aM + bM)`. QED

The bound is attained by `a = E_12`, `b = E_13` in `M_3(F_2)`.

**Corollary 4.4 (independent halves displace 0 or 1/2).** If also `rk(aM + bM) >= 2rho - rho^2 - eta` and
`rk(Ma + Mb) >= 2rho - rho^2 - eta`, then `rho (1 - 2 rho) <= 2 eta`. Independent tensor factors attain both
with `eta = 0`, so there `rho` is `0` or `1/2`. The regular models of locally finite groups have
`rho = 1/2`, with equality in Theorem 4.3.

**Proposition 4.5 (near-minimal displacement overlaps the ranges of halves).** Let `rho_*` be the infimum
of `rho` over nontrivial fixed-point-free models; `rho_* >= c_0 > 0` because `rho >= delta`. Suppose
`rho(sigma) <= (1+eps) rho_*`. Then:
1. For clopen proper `U`, a transvection nilpotent `q` whose pair has union a proper subset of `U`, and
   `Z` in `M_n(C_U)`, `|rk(qZ) - rho rk(Z)| <= eps rho/(1+eps)`.
2. For disjoint halves `a, b` with proper union: `rk(Ma + Mb) >= 2rho - rho^2 - eps rho`, hence
   `rk(aM ∩ bM) >= rho - rho^2 - eps rho`, while `rk(ab) <= rho^2 + eps rho`.

*Proof.*
1. This is the proof of Theorem 2.1 with `rho_*` in place of `c_*`. Corner models of `G_U` have displacement
   at least `rho_*`, and pairs inside `U` move into the model frame by `V ∩ G_U`.
2. Choose `U` containing the pair of `a`, disjoint from the pair of `b`, with the pair of `a` a proper subset.
   `K = r.ann(b)` is invariant, so `rk(aK) >= rho_*(1 - rho)`. Then `r.ann(a) ∩ r.ann(b)` is the kernel of
   `a` on `K`, of rank at most `(1 - rho)(1 - rho/(1+eps))`, which gives the first bound. Theorem 4.3 gives
   `rk(aM + bM) <= rho + rho^2 + eps rho`, hence the overlap. Item 1 with `Z = b` bounds `rk(ab)`. QED

**Remark 4.6.** At near-minimal displacement with `rho` bounded away from `1/2`, the ranges of disjoint
halves overlap in rank about `rho - rho^2`. That is inside `r.ann(a) ∩ bM`, far above the product range
`abM` of rank about `rho^2`. So `b` maps `r.ann(a)` almost into `aM`. Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 4.5
locate this overlap but do not turn it into a commutant distortion of a defect range (Proposition 2.3).
Either the overlap is excluded, which forces `rho_* = 1/2`, or it is realized.

## 5. Opposite roots at a child cylinder, and where it stops

**Lemma 5.1 (the opposite-root `S_3` split).** For a pair `(C, C')` with proper union, `tau(C <- C')` and
`tau(C' <- C)` generate `GL_2(F_2) ~= S_3`, and `omega = tau(C <- C') tau(C' <- C)` has order `3`. Put
`q_3 = sigma(1 + omega + omega^2)`, `d_3 = rk(sigma(omega) - 1)` and `a = sigma(tau(C <- C')) - 1`. Then
`q_3` is the idempotent onto `Fix_r(sigma(omega))`, `rk(q_3) = 1 - d_3`, and

```text
rho  =  d_3 / 2  +  rk(a q_3) ,        so        d_3/2  <=  rho  <=  1/2 .
```

*Proof.*
* **Splitting.** In characteristic two `epsilon = 1 + omega + omega^2` is a central idempotent of `F_2[S_3]`,
  with `epsilon F_2[S_3] ~= F_2[C_2]` and `(1 - epsilon) F_2[S_3] ~= M_2(F_2)`. It projects onto
  `Fix(omega)`, because `3` is invertible.
* **Complement.** On `(1 - q_3)M` the image of `M_2(F_2)` is unital, so this corner is a matrix-unit module.
  There `tau - 1` acts as a rank-one matrix unit, of rank `rk(1 - q_3)/2 = d_3/2`.
* **Fixed part.** On `q_3 M`, `a` is square-zero, and `q_3` commutes with `a`. QED

**Where it stops.**
* **Opposite roots at a child cylinder.** With the unitriangular data of the frame they generate
  `EL_2(e_0 R e_0) ~= R^x`, so no locally finite firewall applies. The only identities found are Lemma 5.1
  and Theorem 4.3, which split and bound ranks without distorting a defect-range rank.
* **The nine-leaf configuration** was not examined in this lane. Weyl elements and compressors belong to
  `w4-sub-weyl`.
