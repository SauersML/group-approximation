# Bernoulli-field point models: a rank-condition failure is a Rokhlin deficit

swarm-0917-w5-pull-gs-2, 2026-09-17. Supports `bernoulli-field-rank-failure-gives-rokhlin-deficit`.

## 0. Setting and statement

- `G` is a countably infinite group, `k` is a field, and `m >= 1`.
- `K_m(k) = k(x_{g,i} : g ∈ G, 1 <= i <= m)` is the **Bernoulli field**, with the shift action
  `σ_h(x_{g,i}) = x_{hg,i}`, extended to `k`-automorphisms.
- `K_0 ⊆ K_m(k)` is any `σ(G)`-invariant subfield. `K_0 ⋊ G` is the skew group ring (trivial cocycle),
  `(a u_g)(b u_h) = a σ_g(b) u_{gh}`. It is a unital subring of `K_m(k) ⋊ G`.
- **INF(G)** means `h_sup(G) = ∞`. By item 1 of `seward-per-group-rokhlin-entropy-of-bernoulli-shifts`, this is
  equivalent to `h^Rok_G(L^G) = H(L)` for every finite base `L`.

**Theorem A.** Suppose `A ∈ M_{d×r}(K_0 ⋊ G)` and `B ∈ M_{r×d}(K_0 ⋊ G)` satisfy `AB = I_d` with `r < d`. Then
there are a finite field `F_q` and a base `L = F_q^{m+d}` (uniform), with

    h^Rok_G(L^G) <= (m + r) log q + h_2(C/q) + (C d / q) log q  <  (m + d) log q = H(L),

where `C` is a constant depending only on `A`, `B`, and `q` is any power of a suitable prime power `q_0` with
`q >= q_1(C, d)`. Hence `h_sup(G) < ∞`, that is, **not INF(G)**.

**Corollary B.** If INF(G), then for every field `k` and every `m >= 1`:
1. `K_0 ⋊ G` has the rank condition for every invariant subfield `K_0 ⊆ K_m(k)`;
2. `K_m(k) ⋊ G` is stably finite, and so is every unital subring, including every `K_0 ⋊ G`;
3. `k[G]` is stably finite (Kaplansky direct finiteness over `k`), since `k[G] ⊆ K_m(k) ⋊ G`.

So INF(G) ⇒ SF(Bernoulli fields over G) ⇒ KAP(G). This factors item 5 of the Seward import (INF ⇒ KAP)
through an intermediate statement that can fail on its own.

## 1. Reduction to a finite coefficient field

**Lemma 1.** Suppose `K_0 ⋊ G` fails the rank condition with sizes `r < d`, for some invariant `K_0 ⊆ K_m(k)`.
Then so does `K_m(F_{q_0}) ⋊ G` for some finite field `F_{q_0}`, with the same `r, d`. Every entry of the new
`A, B` has the form `Σ_{g ∈ T} (N_g / D_g) u_g`, with `N_g, D_g ∈ F_{q_0}[x_{h,i}]` and `D_g ≠ 0`.

*Proof.*
1. `AB = I_d` holds in `K_m(k) ⋊ G`, because `K_0 ⋊ G` is a unital subring. Replace `K_0` by `K_m(k)`.
2. Every entry is a finite sum `Σ_{g∈T} a_g u_g` with `a_g = N_g/D_g`, where `N_g, D_g ∈ k[x_E]` for a finite set
   `E` of variables and `D_g ≠ 0`. Fix these representations.
3. The identity `AB = I_d` says that for all `i, j` and `t ∈ G`,

       Σ_{k'} Σ_{g g' = t} a^{ik'}_g σ_g(b^{k'j}_{g'}) = δ_{ij} δ_{t,1}     in K_m(k).

   - Here `σ_g(N/D) = σ_g(N)/σ_g(D)`. Since `σ_g` only renames variables, `σ_g(D)` has the same coefficients and
     degree as `D`.
   - Multiply by the product `Π` of all denominators that appear. Each identity becomes a polynomial identity in
     `R_0[x_{E'}]` for some finite `E'`.
   - Here `R_0 ⊂ k` is the subring generated by the prime ring and all coefficients of all the `N` and `D`.
4. For each denominator `D` pick a nonzero coefficient `c_D`, and put `s = Π_D c_D` and `R_1 = R_0[1/s] ⊆ k`.
   - `R_1` is a nonzero finitely generated `Z`-algebra.
   - So it has a maximal ideal `𝔪` whose residue field `F_{q_0} = R_1/𝔪` is finite (Nullstellensatz for
     finitely generated `Z`-algebras).
5. Reduce all coefficients modulo `𝔪`.
   - Every `c_D` is a unit of `R_1`, so every denominator reduces to a nonzero polynomial.
   - The polynomial identities of step 3 reduce to polynomial identities.
   - Divide back by the reduced `Π`, which is nonzero in the field `K_m(F_{q_0})`. The reduced matrices then satisfy
     `A'B' = I_d` in `K_m(F_{q_0}) ⋊ G`. ∎

From now on `k = F_{q_0}`, and `q` is a power of `q_0`, so `F_{q_0} ⊆ F_q`. All `x_E` involved are finitely many.
Let `𝒟` be the finite list of the polynomials `D` (denominators of entries of `A`) together with `σ_g(D')` (for
`D'` a denominator of an entry of `B` and `g` in the support of an entry of `A`). Put `C = Σ_{D ∈ 𝒟} deg D`.

## 2. The point model

**Points.** `Ω = (F_q^m)^G` with the uniform product measure `μ`. Write `ω_i(g)` for coordinates, so
`x_{g,i}(ω) = ω_i(g)`. The shift is `(hω)(g) = ω(h^{-1}g)`.

**Evaluation.** A fraction `f = N/D` (fixed representation) is *defined at* `ω` if `D(ω) ≠ 0`. Then
`f(ω) = N(ω)/D(ω) ∈ F_q`; otherwise put `f(ω) := 0`.
- `f(ω)` depends only on the finitely many coordinates of `ω` in the variables of `N, D`.
- On the set where all fractions of a finite family are defined, evaluation respects sums and products of the
  family (it is a ring homomorphism from the localization at those denominators).
- **Covariance.** `σ_g(f)(ω) = f(g^{-1}ω)`, and `σ_g(f)` is defined at `ω` iff `f` is defined at `g^{-1}ω`.
  Check on variables: `σ_g(x_{k,i})(ω) = ω_i(gk) = (g^{-1}ω)_i(k) = x_{k,i}(g^{-1}ω)`. Both sides are ring
  homomorphisms in `f`, and `σ_g(D)(ω) = D(g^{-1}ω)`.

**Operators.** For `ω ∈ Ω`, let `π_ω` act on column vectors `ξ ∈ (F_q^G)^n`:

    (π_ω(a u_g) ξ)(h) = a(h^{-1}ω) · ξ(hg),

extended additively to finite sums and entrywise to matrices. So `π_ω(B)` maps `(F_q^G)^d -> (F_q^G)^r`, and
`π_ω(A)` maps back.

**Lemma 2 (equivariance).** For `k ∈ G` put `(kξ)(h) = ξ(k^{-1}h)`. Then `π_{kω}(X)(kξ) = k(π_ω(X)ξ)` for every
matrix `X` over `K_m ⋊ G`.

*Proof.* `π_{kω}(a u_g)(kξ)(h) = a(h^{-1}kω) ξ(k^{-1}hg)`, while `k(π_ω(a u_g)ξ)(h) = (π_ω(a u_g)ξ)(k^{-1}h) =
a(h^{-1}kω) ξ(k^{-1}hg)`. ∎

**Lemma 3 (exact off the defect set).** Put `Z = {ω : D(ω) = 0 for some D ∈ 𝒟}` and
`D_ω = {h ∈ G : h^{-1}ω ∈ Z}`. Then `D_{kω} = k D_ω`, and for every `ξ ∈ (F_q^G)^d` and `h ∉ D_ω`,

    (π_ω(A) π_ω(B) ξ)(h) = ξ(h).

*Proof.* `D_{kω} = {h : h^{-1}kω ∈ Z} = k D_ω`. For single terms,

    π_ω(a u_g) π_ω(b u_{g'}) ξ (h) = a(h^{-1}ω) · (π_ω(b u_{g'})ξ)(hg)
                                   = a(h^{-1}ω) · b(g^{-1}h^{-1}ω) · ξ(hgg')
                                   = a(h^{-1}ω) · σ_g(b)(h^{-1}ω) · ξ(hgg').

So `(π_ω(A)π_ω(B)ξ)_i(h) = Σ_j Σ_t [Σ_{k'} Σ_{gg'=t} a^{ik'}_g σ_g(b^{k'j}_{g'})](h^{-1}ω) · ξ_j(ht)`.
- If `h ∉ D_ω`, every fraction `a^{ik'}_g` and `σ_g(b^{k'j}_{g'})` is defined at `h^{-1}ω`, by the choice of
  `𝒟`.
- Evaluation there respects sums and products, and the bracket equals `δ_{ij}δ_{t,1}` in `K_m`.
- So the sum is `ξ_i(h)`. ∎

**Lemma 4 (defect density).** `μ(Z) <= C/q`.

*Proof.* Each `D ∈ 𝒟` is a nonzero polynomial over `F_{q_0} ⊆ F_q` in finitely many coordinates, which are iid
uniform on `F_q`. By Schwartz–Zippel (Lemma 0 of `rational-function-field-crossed-products-rank-condition-proof`),
`μ(D = 0) <= deg D / q`. Take a union bound. ∎

## 3. A cheap generating partition

**Space.** `X = Ω × (F_q^d)^G = (F_q^{m+d})^G` with uniform product measure `ν = μ × u^G`. This is the Bernoulli
shift `L^G` with `L = F_q^{m+d}` uniform, `H(L) = (m+d) log q`. It is free, ergodic and p.m.p. because `G` is
infinite. The action is `k(ω, ξ) = (kω, kξ)`.

**Codes.** For `x = (ω, ξ)` put `η_x = π_ω(B) ξ ∈ (F_q^r)^G`. Define the finite partition `P` of `X` by the label

    ℓ(x) = ( ω(1),  η_x(1),  ζ(x) ),     ζ(x) = ξ(1) if 1 ∈ D_ω,   ζ(x) = * otherwise.

It is measurable: each component depends on finitely many coordinates.

**Lemma 5 (P generates).** The labels `ℓ(h^{-1}x)`, `h ∈ G`, determine `x`.

*Proof.*
- `(h^{-1}ω)(1) = ω(h)`, so the first components give `ω`.
- By Lemma 2, `η_{h^{-1}x} = h^{-1}η_x`, so `η_{h^{-1}x}(1) = η_x(h)`. The second components give `η_x`.
- `D_{h^{-1}ω} = h^{-1}D_ω`, so `1 ∈ D_{h^{-1}ω}` iff `h ∈ D_ω`. The third components give `ξ(h)` for `h ∈ D_ω`.
- For `h ∉ D_ω`, Lemma 3 gives `ξ(h) = (π_ω(A) η_x)(h)`, a function of `ω` and `η_x`.

So the map `x ↦ (ℓ(h^{-1}x))_h` is injective, and the `G`-translates of `P` generate the Borel σ-algebra of the
compact metrizable `X` (an injective Borel map on a standard Borel space). ∎

**Lemma 6 (entropy of P).** With `p = μ(Z) <= C/q <= 1/2`,

    H(P) <= H(ω(1)) + H(η(1)) + H(ζ) <= m log q + r log q + h_2(p) + p · d log q.

*Proof.* Subadditivity. `ω(1)` takes `q^m` values and `η(1)` takes `q^r` values. The variable `ζ` equals `*` with
probability `1 - p`, and otherwise takes at most `q^d` values, so `H(ζ) <= h_2(p) + p d log q`. `h_2` is increasing on
`[0, 1/2]`, so `h_2(p) <= h_2(C/q)`. ∎

**Proof of Theorem A.** Rokhlin entropy is the infimum of `H(Q)` over countable generating partitions `Q`. So

    h^Rok_G(L^G) <= (m + r) log q + h_2(C/q) + (C d/q) log q.

The gap to `H(L) = (m+d) log q` is at least `(d - r) log q - h_2(C/q) - (C d / q) log q`. Since `d - r >= 1`, this
is positive once `q` is large, for example once `q >= max(4C, 2Cd)` and `q >= 4`: then `(Cd/q) log q <= (1/2) log q`
and `h_2(C/q) <= h_2(1/4) < 1 < (1/2) log 4 <= (1/2) log q` (natural logs, `log 4 ≈ 1.386`, `h_2(1/4) ≈ 0.562`).
Such `q` exist among the powers of `q_0`. So `h^Rok_G(L^G) < H(L) < ∞`. By item 1 of the Seward import,
`min{H(L), h_sup(G)} < H(L)`, so `h_sup(G) < ∞`: not INF(G). ∎

## 4. Corollaries

**Proof of Corollary B.** Assume INF(G).
1. Theorem A (with Lemma 1) forbids `AB = I_d` with `r < d` over any `K_0 ⋊ G`, so the rank condition holds.
2. The shift action on `K_m(k)` is faithful: for `g ≠ 1`, `σ_g(x_{1,1}) = x_{g,1} ≠ x_{1,1}`. By item 1 of
   `virtually-hughes-free-field-crossed-products-are-stably-finite`, `K_m(k) ⋊ G` is simple, so the rank condition
   is equivalent to stable finiteness. Stable finiteness passes to unital subrings, because `M_n` of a subring is
   a subring of `M_n`.
3. `k[G] = k ⋊ G ⊆ K_m(k) ⋊ G`. ∎

**Unconditional instances.**
- Sofic `G`: item 4 of the Seward import gives INF(G). So every Bernoulli-field skew group ring over a sofic group
  is stably finite, over any field. This is the case the finite-field point-model attempt on
  `some-field-crossed-product-fails-the-rank-condition` expected to handle "by the same count over sofic models" but
  did not record. Here no sofic model is used: the entropy inequality does the counting.
- Every host where INF is ESTABLISHED in the graph (for example through
  `rokhlin-maximality-closed-under-local-embeddings`) is covered in the same way.

**Belief change on the rank-condition need.** Bernoulli fields were one of the three regimes that survive every
recorded kill of `some-field-crossed-product-fails-the-rank-condition`. By Theorem A, a Bernoulli-field witness over
`G` does more than kill profile-level proofs at `G`: it refutes maximal Bernoulli Rokhlin entropy at `G` outright.
- On the tester host `U = L_(F_2)(1,2)^×`, where POS(U) ⇔ INF(U) decides the flagship, a Bernoulli-field witness
  over `U` refutes the flagship.
- Conversely, any proof of the flagship must prove stable finiteness of `F_p(x_g : g ∈ G) ⋊ G` for every group
  `G` and prime `p`. That is a "random-coefficient" Kaplansky direct finiteness statement, at least as strong as KAP
  over `F_p`, which is itself open for nonsofic groups.

## 5. Where the method stops

1. **Twisted cocycles.** With `α ≠ 1`, `π_ω(u_g)π_ω(u_{g'}) = π_ω(u_{gg'})` but the algebra needs
   `α(g,g') u_{gg'}`, so `π_ω` is not multiplicative. The ternary summand `S_-` lives here.
   - For cocycles valued in a finite group of constants, pass to the finite central extension `Ĝ` instead. A
     rank-condition failure in the summand makes `F_q[Ĝ]` not stably finite. That gives a non-surjunctive linear
     cellular automaton on `Ĝ`, the known route, which concerns `Ĝ`, not `G`.
2. **Non-Bernoulli invariant fields.** A finitely generated invariant field is `Frac(F_p[x_{g,i}]/𝔭)` for a
   `G`-invariant prime `𝔭`. Its points form a subshift `V(𝔭)(F_q) ⊆ (F_q^m)^G`. The same code works if two things
   hold:
   - an invariant measure on those points with defect probability `o(1/log q)`;
   - relative maximality `h^Rok(Ω × L^G | Ω) = H(L)`.

   Neither is available in general. The cancellation of the `m log q` term against the base entropy is what makes
   the Bernoulli case free.
3. **Converse.** Not INF(G) is not known to produce any Bernoulli-field witness. So Theorem A is a one-way
   decomposition: INF ⇒ SF(Bernoulli fields) ⇒ KAP.
