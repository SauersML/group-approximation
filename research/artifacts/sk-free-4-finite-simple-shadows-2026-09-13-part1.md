# sk-free-4: finite simple shadows of Kazhdan limits (part 1: expansion and simplicity)

Lane sk-free-4 (free lane), 2026-09-13. Status: established with complete proofs below, UNREVIEWED (queued with sk-verify-13). A bounded novelty search is in part 3.

Notation.
- A marked group is (G,T) with T a finite ordered generating set.
- (Q_k,T_k) → (G,T) in the space of marked groups means: for every r there is k_r such that, for k ≥ k_r and every word w of length ≤ r in T, w = 1 in Q_k iff w = 1 in G.
- A LEF group is one that is such a limit of finite groups.
- For a finite group Q with symmetric generating set T^± = T ∪ T^{-1}, M = |T^±|^{-1} Σ_{t∈T^±} λ(t) is the normalized adjacency operator of Cay(Q,T) on ℓ²(Q), and ℓ²_0(Q) is the orthogonal complement of the constants.

## 1. Every finite approximation of a Kazhdan group expands

**Theorem A.** Let G have property (T) and finite generating set T. There are κ > 0 and a finite set R_0 of relators of (G,T) with the following property. If (Q_k,T_k) → (G,T) with Q_k finite, then for all large k:
- (Q_k,T_k) is a marked quotient of Γ_0 = F(T)/⟨⟨R_0⟩⟩;
- the spectrum of M on ℓ²_0(Q_k) lies in [−1, 1 − κ²/(2|T^±|)].

If G is infinite, then |Q_k| → ∞, so the Cayley graphs Cay(Q_k,T_k) form a family of expanders.

*Proof.*
- (T) is open in the space of marked groups (Shalom; repo node `fpbs-shalom-property-t-is-open`). So there is a finite R_0 ⊆ ker(F(T) → G) such that Γ_0 = F(T)/⟨⟨R_0⟩⟩ has (T). Let κ be a Kazhdan constant for (Γ_0,T).
- Put r = max_{ρ∈R_0} |ρ|. For k ≥ k_r every ρ ∈ R_0 is trivial in Q_k, so t ↦ t_k defines an epimorphism Γ_0 → Q_k.
- The left regular representation of Q_k on ℓ²_0(Q_k), pulled back to Γ_0, has no nonzero invariant vector: an invariant vector is constant, since Q_k is generated by T_k. So every unit ξ ∈ ℓ²_0(Q_k) has max_t ‖λ(t)ξ − ξ‖ ≥ κ.
- For symmetric T^±,
  ⟨(I − M)ξ, ξ⟩ = |T^±|^{-1} Σ_{t∈T^±} (1 − Re⟨λ(t)ξ,ξ⟩) = (2|T^±|)^{-1} Σ_{t∈T^±} ‖λ(t)ξ − ξ‖² ≥ κ²/(2|T^±|).
- If G is infinite, the ball B_T(r) of G injects into Q_k for large k (distinct elements of the ball differ by a nonrelator of length ≤ 2r). So |Q_k| → ∞. The discrete Cheeger inequality turns the uniform gap into uniform edge expansion. ∎

**Remark A1 (what it adds).** The marking is arbitrary and the approximants are arbitrary. The note and `finite-simple-groups-converge-to-simple-kazhdan-group` get expansion only for approximants built as quotients of EL_3 of a free algebra with the elementary marking. Theorem A says that EVERY sequence of finite groups converging to G_X, for EVERY finite generating set of G_X, is eventually an expander family. Changing the marking changes the constant; nothing else changes.

**Remark A2 (model test).**
- Z, marked by 1, is the limit of Z/n. The cycles Cay(Z/n) are not expanders, consistent with Z not having (T).
- SL_3(Z) has (T), and its congruence quotients SL_3(Z/n) converge to it; Theorem A recovers Margulis' expanders.
- Theorem A needs only (T) of the limit, not of any mother group chosen in advance.

## 2. Simple LEF groups are limits of finite simple groups

**Theorem B.** Let G be an infinite simple group with finite generating set T, and (Q_k,T_k) → (G,T) with Q_k finite. For large k let M_k be any maximal normal subgroup of Q_k. Then Q_k/M_k is a finite nonabelian simple group and

  (Q_k/M_k, T_kM_k) → (G,T).

So an infinite finitely generated simple group is LEF iff it is a marked limit of finite simple groups.

*Proof.*
- For large k, Q_k is nontrivial, since G is, so a maximal normal subgroup M_k exists and Q_k/M_k is simple.
- **Relators survive.** A relator of G of length ≤ r is trivial in Q_k for k ≥ k_r, hence in Q_k/M_k.
- **Nonrelators survive.**
  - Fix r and let W_r be the finite set of words of length ≤ r that are nontrivial in G.
  - For w ∈ W_r the normal closure of w in G is G, because G is simple. So for each t ∈ T there are words h_1,…,h_m and signs ε_i with t = ∏_i h_i w^{ε_i} h_i^{-1} in G. The word c_{w,t} = t^{-1} ∏_i h_i w^{ε_i} h_i^{-1} is a relator of G of some length L(w,t).
  - Let L_r = max over w ∈ W_r and t ∈ T of L(w,t). For k ≥ max(k_r, k_{L_r}), each c_{w,t} is trivial in Q_k, and w is nontrivial in Q_k.
  - If the image of w in Q_k lay in M_k, then every t_k would lie in M_k by c_{w,t}, so Q_k = ⟨T_k⟩ ⊆ M_k, a contradiction. So w is nontrivial in Q_k/M_k.
- **Nonabelian.** G is nonabelian, so some commutator [t,t′] of length 4 is nontrivial in G, hence in Q_k/M_k for large k.
- **Converse.** Limits of finite groups are LEF. ∎

**Corollary B1 (with Theorem A).** Let G be an infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan LEF group. Then:
- G is a marked limit of finite simple groups;
- for every finite generating set T of G, every sequence of finite marked groups converging to (G,T), and in particular every sequence of finite simple groups, is eventually a family of expanders with a uniform spectral gap.

**Corollary B2 (the unified headline in a few lines).** For a finitely generated group Γ the following are equivalent:
- (i) Γ is LEF;
- (ii) Γ is a subgroup of an infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan group G that is a marked limit of finite simple groups, and every sequence of finite marked groups converging to G is eventually an expander family.

*Proof.* (ii) ⇒ (i): LEF passes to subgroups. (i) ⇒ (ii): take the simple Kazhdan LEF host from `lef-groups-embed-in-simple-kazhdan-lef-groups` (rev3 Corollary 2), and apply B1. ∎

The configuration models of sk-strong-6/8 give more: the approximants can be chosen as SL_N(F_2). B2 needs only the existence of the LEF host. So in a note that already proves Corollary 2, "limit of finite simple expanders" costs about six lines and one citation (Shalom), with no new models.

## 3. The finite simple groups must have unbounded rank

**Proposition B3.** Let G be an infinite finitely generated simple group and (Q_k,T_k) → (G,T) with Q_k finite simple. Then for every d, only finitely many Q_k embed in GL_d(K) for some field K. By the classification of finite simple groups, the Q_k are eventually alternating groups of unbounded degree, or groups of Lie type of unbounded Lie rank.

*Proof.*
- Suppose infinitely many Q_k embed in GL_d(K_k). Pass to that subsequence and fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter ω.
- The map t ↦ (t_k)_ω defines a homomorphism F(T) → ∏_ω Q_k whose kernel is exactly the relators of G: a word is trivial in the ultraproduct iff it is trivial in Q_k for ω-almost all k, iff it is trivial in G, by marked convergence. So G embeds in ∏_ω Q_k ≤ ∏_ω GL_d(K_k) = GL_d(∏_ω K_k), an ultraproduct of fields, hence a field.
- A finitely generated linear group is residually finite (Mal'cev). An infinite simple group is not. Contradiction.
- For the last sentence: groups of Lie type of rank ℓ over any finite field embed in GL_{c(ℓ)} of a field, through the adjoint representation of the corresponding algebraic group (twisted forms included), and there are finitely many sporadic groups. ∎

**Remark B4.**
- For G_X the models built in the note are SL_{3N}(F_2) with N → ∞, consistent with B3.
- B3 does not decide whether an infinite simple Kazhdan group can be a marked limit of ALTERNATING groups. See `torsion-free-kazhdan-alternating-mother-limit-gate` (open there in the torsion-free case), and part 3 §2 for the constraints (T) imposes.
