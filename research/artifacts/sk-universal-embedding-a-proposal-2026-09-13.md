## Summary

**New corollary.** Every finitely generated residually finite group is a subgroup of an infinite finitely generated
simple group with property (T) that is LEF.
- Without (T) it is Kionke–Schesler 2023 (arXiv:2304.09307, Theorem 1.2).
- So every f.g. residually finite group, and in particular every f.g. linear group, lies both in an amenable simple
  LEF group and in a Kazhdan simple LEF group.

**Proof ingredients.**
- an overgroup `Δ ⊇ Γ` with `Γ ≤ [Δ,Δ]`, from Ore's theorem;
- the residually finite Toeplitz subshift over `Δ`;
- the note's proof run over `Δ`;
- Whitehead's lemma.

**Why an overgroup is necessary.** The obvious map `γ ↦ diag(u_γ, u_γ^{-1}, 1)` is not a homomorphism for
nonabelian `Γ`. Also `diag(u,1,1)` is never elementary, because its half-line compression index is −1.

## (b) The section, verbatim (Version A, self-contained over Z-note)

```latex
\section{Residually finite subgroups}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:rf}
Every finitely generated residually finite group is a subgroup of an
infinite finitely generated simple group with property~\textup{(T)} that
is LEF.
\end{corollary}

Without property~(T) this is due to Kionke and
Schesler~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{KionkeSchesler}.

\begin{proof}
Let $\Gamma$ be generated by a finite set $S$. Replacing $\Gamma$ by
$\Gamma\times\Z$, we may assume that $\Gamma$ is infinite. Let $\Gamma_n$
be normal subgroups of finite index with trivial intersection.

\emph{An overgroup.} Left multiplication on $\Gamma/\Gamma_n\times\{1,2\}$
is an even permutation, so $\Gamma$ embeds in
$P=\prod_n\operatorname{Sym}(\Gamma/\Gamma_n\times\{1,2\})$. Every even
permutation of a finite set is a commutator of two
permutations~\cite[Theorem~1]{Ore}, so each $s\in S$ equals $[a_s,b_s]$
for some $a_s,b_s\in P$. The subgroup $\Delta$ generated by $S$ and the
$a_s,b_s$ is finitely generated and residually finite, and
$\Gamma\le[\Delta,\Delta]$.

\emph{A Toeplitz subshift.} Fix normal subgroups
$\Delta=\Delta_0\ge\Delta_1\ge\cdots$ of finite index with trivial
intersection, balls $B_m$ of a word metric, and an enumeration
$g_1,g_2,\dots$ of $\Delta$, with
$d_m=[\Delta_{m-1}:\Delta_m]\ge2|B_{m-1}|+m+2$. Put $C_0=\Delta$. Among
the $d_m$ cosets of $\Delta_m$ in $C_{m-1}$ choose $C_m$ avoiding
$g_1,\dots,g_m$, put $x=1$ on one other coset and $x=0$ on the rest.
This defines $x\in\{0,1\}^\Delta$. Let $X$ be the orbit closure of $x$
under $(g\cdot y)(h)=y(g^{-1}h)$.

Off $C_m$ the array $x$ is constant on cosets of $\Delta_m$, and on $C_m$
it takes both values on whole cosets of $\Delta_{m+1}$. Taking limits of
translates $g_k\cdot x$ with $g_k\Delta_{m+1}$ fixed, every $y\in X$ is
constant on all cosets of $\Delta_m$ except one coset $D_m(y)$, and
$D_m(g\cdot y)=gD_m(y)$. So $g\cdot y=y$ forces
$g\in\bigcap_m\Delta_m=\{1\}$, and the action is free. If
$W\cap C_M=\varnothing$ then $x(\gamma w)=x(w)$ for $\gamma\in\Delta_M$
and $w\in W$, so $X$ is minimal.

Let $W\subseteq B_M$ be finite, with $M$ so large that every $W$-pattern
of $X$ occurs as $w\mapsto x(gw)$ for some $g$ with
$gW\cap C_M=\varnothing$. Let $p$ agree with $x$ off $C_M$ and vanish on
$C_M$; it is invariant under $\Delta_M$. Fix $g\in\Delta$. As $\gamma$ runs
through $\Delta_M$, each point $\gamma gw$ with $gw\in C_M$ visits every
coset of $\Delta_{M+1}$ in $C_M$, according to the class of $\gamma$
modulo $\Delta_{M+1}$. So at most $2|W|<d_{M+1}$ classes put one of these
points into the coset where $x=1$ or into $C_{M+1}$, and any other
$\gamma$ gives $x(\gamma gw)=p(gw)$ for all $w\in W$. So $p$ and $X$ have
the same $W$-patterns.

\emph{The simple group.} The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies to
$R=\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes\Delta$, where $u_gfu_g^{-1}=f\circ g^{-1}$.
Exponents become group elements, the bound $|j|\le m$ becomes $g\in B_m$,
the tower units become $e_{aW}u_{ab^{-1}}$ with $a,b\in B_m$, and the
finite model acts on $\F_2^{\Delta/\Delta_M}$ by left translation and by
evaluating functions along the translates of $p$. So $G=\EL_3(R)$ is an
infinite finitely generated simple group with property~(T) that is LEF.

\emph{The embedding.} For a unit $c$ of a ring,
$e_{12}(c)e_{21}(-c^{-1})e_{12}(c)\,e_{12}(-1)e_{21}(1)e_{12}(-1)
=\operatorname{diag}(c,c^{-1})$. So for units $a,b$ the matrix
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1})\operatorname{diag}(b,b^{-1})
 \operatorname{diag}\bigl((ba)^{-1},ba\bigr)
 =\operatorname{diag}(aba^{-1}b^{-1},1)
\]
lies in $\EL_2(R)$. Since $\Gamma\le[\Delta,\Delta]$, the map
$\gamma\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(u_\gamma,1,1)$ is an injective
homomorphism $\Gamma\to G$.
\end{proof}
```

**What changed.** The LEF case is now established, unreviewed (`lef-groups-embed-in-simple-kazhdan-lef-groups`,
through `lamplighter-bernoulli-crossed-products-simple-kazhdan-lef`). It gives a STRONGER corollary, an iff for LEF

**The one new point in the proof** is simplicity for a topologically free action.
- The note's partition "C ∩ T^jC = ∅" uses freeness. Here it is replaced by clopen sets `C` inside the dense open set
  `Ω` of points with trivial stabilizer in `B_(2w+2)`.
- Suppose `g` commutes with `e_ij(e_V)` and `e_ij(e_Vu_s)` for all clopen `V ⊆ Ω`.
  - Entrywise, `g_ki e_V = 0` for `k ≠ i` gives coefficients vanishing on the dense sets `ℓΩ`, so they vanish.
  - `g_ii e_V = e_V g_jj` makes `g = cI` with `c` locally constant.
  - `c e_V u_s = e_V u_s c` makes `c` invariant on `Ω`, hence everywhere, so `c` is constant.
  - So `g = 1`.
- Simplicity of `R` and `Z(R) = F_2` go the same way: choose `U` inside the free region.

**Verbatim LaTeX (Version L; same insertion point as Version A; add the Ore and Kionke–Schesler bibitems).**

```latex
\section{LEF subgroups}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lef}
A finitely generated group is LEF if and only if it is a subgroup of an
infinite finitely generated simple group with property~\textup{(T)} that
is LEF.
\end{corollary}

For residually finite groups and without property~(T), compare Kionke
and Schesler~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{KionkeSchesler}.

\begin{proof}
Subgroups of LEF groups are LEF. Conversely, let $\Gamma$ be LEF and
generated by a finite set $S$. Replacing $\Gamma$ by $\Gamma\times\Z$, we
may assume that $\Gamma$ is infinite.

\emph{An overgroup.} The group $\Gamma$ embeds in an algebraic
ultraproduct of finite groups $F_n$, so it embeds in
$P=\prod_\omega\operatorname{Sym}(F_n\times\{1,2\})$, where every element
acts by even permutations. Every even permutation of a finite set is a
commutator of two permutations~\cite[Theorem~1]{Ore}, so each $s\in S$
equals $[a_s,b_s]$ for some $a_s,b_s\in P$. The subgroup $\Delta$ generated
by $S$ and the $a_s,b_s$ is finitely generated, infinite and LEF, because
finitely many products and inequalities hold in $\omega$-almost every
coordinate. Moreover $\Gamma\le[\Delta,\Delta]$.

\emph{The ring.} Let $L=\Z/2\wr\Delta$ act on $X=\{0,1\}^\Delta$, where
$\Delta$ acts by $(g\cdot x)(h)=x(g^{-1}h)$ and the generator $a$ of the
base group flips the coordinate at $1$. Every orbit contains all finite
modifications of its points, so the action is minimal. A nontrivial
element of the base group fixes no point, and for $g\ne1$ the points
fixed by an element $(c,g)$ satisfy infinitely many coordinate
conditions, so they form a closed set with empty interior. Put
$R=\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes L$, generated by $u_a$, the $u_s$ and
$e_{\{x:x(1)=1\}}$.

\emph{Simplicity and property~(T).} The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
applies to $G=\EL_3(R)$, with group elements in place of exponents and
word-metric balls in place of intervals. The points with nontrivial
stabilizer in a finite set form a closed set with empty interior, so the
sets $U$ and $C$ are chosen inside its complement $\Omega$. If $g$ commutes
with every $e_{ij}(e_V)$ and $e_{ij}(e_Vu_s)$ for clopen $V\subseteq\Omega$,
its off-diagonal entries vanish on a dense set, its diagonal entries equal
a locally constant function, and that function is invariant on a dense
set. So $g=1$. Property~(T) comes from~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.

\emph{Finite models.} Choose maps $\varphi_n\colon\Delta\to Q_n$ into finite
groups that are injective and multiplicative on larger and larger balls.
The group $\Z/2\wr Q_n$ acts on $\{0,1\}^{Q_n}$ in the same way. Evaluating
a locally constant function on the coordinates near $1$, and letting
$u_\ell$ act through $\varphi_n$, gives a unital ring homomorphism
$R\to\prod_\omega M_{2^{|Q_n|}}(\F_2)$. Every configuration on $Q_n$ reads
locally as a configuration on $\Delta$, so each product is preserved for
$\omega$-almost every $n$. Since $R$ is simple, this homomorphism is
injective. So $\GL_3(R)$ embeds in an ultraproduct of finite groups, and
$G$ is LEF.

\emph{The embedding.} For a unit $c$ of a ring,
$e_{12}(c)e_{21}(-c^{-1})e_{12}(c)\,e_{12}(-1)e_{21}(1)e_{12}(-1)
=\operatorname{diag}(c,c^{-1})$. So for units $a,b$ the matrix
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1})\operatorname{diag}(b,b^{-1})
 \operatorname{diag}\bigl((ba)^{-1},ba\bigr)
 =\operatorname{diag}(aba^{-1}b^{-1},1)
\]
lies in $\EL_2(R)$. Since $\Gamma\le[\Delta,\Delta]\le[L,L]$, the map
$\gamma\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(u_\gamma,1,1)$ is an injective
homomorphism $\Gamma\to G$.
\end{proof}
```
