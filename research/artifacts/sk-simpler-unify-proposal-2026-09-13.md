## Block A: introduction (replaces r3 l.85–89)
```latex
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general}
Let a finitely generated group $L$ act minimally on a Cantor set $Z$ so
that no nontrivial element fixes a nonempty open set. Suppose that
$R=\LC(Z,\F_2)\rtimes L$ is finitely generated and has a unital
homomorphism into an algebraic ultraproduct of matrix algebras over
$\F_2$. Then for every $n\ge3$ the group $\EL_n(R)$ is infinite, finitely
generated, simple, LEF, and has property~\textup{(T)}.
\end{theorem}

For $L=\Z$ this gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The lamplighter action of a
LEF group $\Delta$ on $\F_2^{\Delta}$ gives Corollary~\ref{cor:lef}: a
finitely generated group is LEF if and only if it is a subgroup of an
infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan LEF group, and one such group
contains every finitely generated linear group.

```

## §1: proof of Theorem 2 (replaces r3 l.91-252 together with part 2)
```latex
\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general}}

We write the proof for $n=3$. For $n\ge3$ replace $3$ by $n$ throughout.

\subsection*{The ring and property (T)}
Write $e_U$ for the indicator of a clopen set $U\subseteq Z$. The ring
$R=\bigoplus_{\ell\in L}\LC(Z,\F_2)\,u_\ell$ has the multiplication
\[
 (fu_k)(f'u_\ell)=f\,(f'\circ k^{-1})\,u_{k\ell},\qquad\text{so}\qquad
 u_\ell e_Uu_\ell^{-1}=e_{\ell U}.
\]
Fix a finite symmetric generating set of $L$, let $|\ell|$ be the word
length, and let $B_m=\{\ell\in L:|\ell|\le m\}$. Let $G=\EL_3(R)$ be the
subgroup of $\GL_3(R)$ generated by the elementary matrices
$e_{ij}(r)=I_3+rE_{ij}$, $i\ne j$, $r\in R$, where $E_{ij}$ is the usual
matrix unit. With $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elementary}
 \begin{aligned}
 e_{ij}(r+s)&=e_{ij}(r)e_{ij}(s),\qquad i\ne j,\\
 [e_{ik}(r),e_{kj}(s)]&=e_{ij}(rs),\qquad i,j,k\text{ distinct}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
So the matrices $e_{ij}(s)$, with $s$ in a finite generating set of $R$
containing $1$, generate $G$. By Ershov and
Jaikin-Zapirain~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}, $\EL_n(S)$ has
property~\textup{(T)} for every finitely generated associative unital
ring $S$ and every $n\ge3$. So $G$ has property~\textup{(T)}. It is
infinite because $e_{12}(\LC(Z,\F_2))$ is infinite.

\subsection*{Simplicity}
Let $1\ne N\trianglelefteq G$ and $1\ne g\in N$, and let $w\ge0$ bound
the word lengths of the elements $\ell$ with $u_\ell$ occurring in the
entries of $g$ and $g^{-1}$. Call a clopen set $V$ small if
$V\cap\ell V=\varnothing$ for $\ell\in B_{2w}\setminus\{e\}$ and every
$f\circ\ell$, with $\ell\in B_w$ and $f$ a coefficient of an entry of $g$
or $g^{-1}$, is constant on $V$. Each $\ell\ne e$ fixes a closed set with
empty interior, so the points moved by every $\ell\in B_{2w}\setminus\{e\}$
form a dense open set, and every nonempty clopen set contains a nonempty
small one.

Some $h=e_{ij}(e_V)$ with $V$ small does not commute with $g$.
Indeed, $g$ commutes with $e_{ij}(r)$ exactly when $g_{pi}r=0$ for
$p\ne i$, $rg_{jq}=0$ for $q\ne j$, and $g_{ii}r=rg_{jj}$. For
$c=\sum_\ell c_\ell u_\ell$ and small $V$ we have
$ce_V=\sum_\ell c_\ell e_{\ell V}u_\ell$ and $e_Vc=\sum_\ell e_Vc_\ell u_\ell$.
If $c_\ell\ne0$, it equals $1$ on a nonempty clopen set $U$, and a small
set $V\subseteq\ell^{-1}U$ gives $ce_V\ne0$, while a small set
$V\subseteq U$ gives $e_Vc\ne0$. So if $g$ commutes with every such $h$,
then $g$ is diagonal. In $g_{ii}e_V=e_Vg_{jj}$ the coefficients at
$u_\ell$ with $\ell\ne e$ are supported in the disjoint sets $\ell V$ and
$V$, so they vanish, and the same argument shows that the corresponding
coefficients of $g_{ii}$ and $g_{jj}$ vanish. The constant coefficients
agree on every small $V$, hence everywhere. So $g=cI_3$ with
$c\in\LC(Z,\F_2)$, and comparing constant coefficients in $cc^{-1}=1$
gives $c=1$ and $g=1$.

Fix such $h$ and $V$, put $d=3|B_w|$, and for $a,b\in B_w$ put
$\epsilon_{ab}=e_{aV}u_{ab^{-1}}$. They are nonzero, as $h\ne I_3$ gives
$V\ne\varnothing$. Since
$\epsilon_{ab}\epsilon_{a'b'}=e_{aV\cap ab^{-1}a'V}\,u_{ab^{-1}a'b'^{-1}}$
and $V\cap b^{-1}a'V=\varnothing$ for $b\ne a'$, we get
$\epsilon_{ab}\epsilon_{a'b'}=\delta_{ba'}\epsilon_{ab'}$. Index the
coordinates of $\F_2^d$ by pairs $(p,a)$ with $1\le p\le3$ and $a\in B_w$.
Then $\psi(E_{(p,a),(q,b)})=\epsilon_{ab}E_{pq}$ defines an injective
multiplicative linear map $\psi\colon M_d(\F_2)\to M_3(R)$, and
$A\mapsto I_3-\psi(I_d)+\psi(A)$ embeds $\GL_d(\F_2)$ in $\GL_3(R)$. Its
image $H$ lies in $G$. Indeed, transvections generate $\GL_d(\F_2)$. The
transvection between $(p,a)$ and $(q,b)$ maps to $e_{pq}(\epsilon_{ab})$
if $p\ne q$. If $p=q$, it is the commutator of the transvections between
$(p,a)$ and $(p',a)$ and between $(p',a)$ and $(p,b)$, where $p'\ne p$.

Put $k=[g,h]\in N\setminus\{1\}$. If $a,b\in B_w$ and $f,f'$ are
coefficients as above, then
\[
  fu_a\,e_V\,f'u_b=f\,e_{aV}\,(f'\circ a^{-1})\,u_{ab}
  \in\{0,\epsilon_{a,b^{-1}}\},
\]
since $f\circ a$ and $f'$ are constant on $V$. The entries of
$ghg^{-1}-I_3=g\,e_VE_{ij}\,g^{-1}$ are sums of such products, and
$h^{-1}=h=I_3+\epsilon_{ee}E_{ij}$. The image $\psi(M_d(\F_2))$ is the set
of matrices with entries in the span of the $\epsilon_{ab}$, and it is
closed under products. So $k-I_3=(ghg^{-1}-h)h$ and
$k^{-1}-I_3=h(ghg^{-1}-h)$ lie in it, say $k=I_3+\psi(A)$ and
$k^{-1}=I_3+\psi(B)$. As $\psi$ is injective and multiplicative,
$(I_d+A)(I_d+B)=I_d$, so $k$ is the image of $I_d+A$ and $k\in H$. Then
$N\cap H$ is a nontrivial normal subgroup of
$H\cong\GL_d(\F_2)=\PSL_d(\F_2)$, which is simple as $d\ge3$. So
$H\subseteq N$, and $e_{pq}(e_V)\in N$ for all $p\ne q$.

Finally, $J=\{r\in R:e_{pq}(r)\in N\text{ for all }p\ne q\}$ is a
two-sided ideal. It is additive by~\eqref{eq:elementary}, and for
$l\notin\{p,q\}$ we have $e_{pq}(sr)=[e_{pl}(s),e_{lq}(r)]$ and
$e_{pq}(rs)=[e_{pl}(r),e_{lq}(s)]$. It contains $e_V$, so it contains
every $e_{\ell V}=u_\ell e_Vu_\ell^{-1}$. By minimality and compactness
finitely many of these sets cover $Z$, so
$1=1-\prod_i(1-e_{\ell_iV})\in J$. So $J=R$ and $N=G$.

\subsection*{Finite models}
A group is LEF~\cite{VershikGordon} if every finite subset embeds
injectively into a finite group, preserving all products that stay in
that subset. A countable subgroup of an algebraic ultraproduct of finite
groups is LEF. A unital homomorphism from $R$ into an algebraic
ultraproduct $\prod_\omega M_{N_k}(\F_2)$ maps $G$ into
$\prod_\omega\GL_{3N_k}(\F_2)$, and its kernel on $G$ is a normal subgroup
that does not contain $e_{12}(1)$. As $G$ is simple, $G$ embeds and is
LEF. LEF groups are sofic, and sofic groups are
hyperlinear~\cite[Example~4.5 and Theorem~3.3]{Pestov}.

So $L(G)$ embeds in $\mathcal R^\omega$~\cite[Proposition~7.1]{Ozawa}.
Since $G$ is infinite and simple, its nontrivial conjugacy classes are
infinite and $G$ is not residually finite. So $L(G)$ is a
$\mathrm{II}_1$ factor, and $L(G)\mathbin{\bar\otimes}\mathcal R$ is a
McDuff factor that embeds in $\mathcal R^\omega$ and whose unitary group
contains $G$. This is Brown's formulation. Kirchberg proved that a
Kazhdan group with the factorization property is residually
finite~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Kirchberg}. So $G$ does not have the
factorization property, and $C^*(G)$ does not have the local lifting
property~\cite[p.~527]{Ozawa}.\hfill$\square$

```
