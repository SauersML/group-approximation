## 0. Setup

- `A` is the set of 19 Wang tiles, and `Omega = Omega_U ⊆ A^{Z^2}` is Labbé's shift: minimal, aperiodic, with a free action (`minimal-aperiodic-wang-shift-exists`).
  - Conventions: `(sigma^v x)_w = x_{w+v}`; sup norms; `B(r)` is the box of radius `r`.
- `Gamma = D([[Z^2 ~ Omega]])` is finitely generated, infinite and simple (`minimal-z2-subshift-derived-full-groups-are-fg-simple`). Fix a finite symmetric generating set `S`.
- Every `g ∈ [[Omega]]` has the form `g(x) = sigma^{c_g(x)} x` for a continuous cocycle `c_g`. Fix `R` such that, for all `s ∈ S`, `|c_s| <= R` and `c_s(x)` depends only on `x|B(R)`.
- **LEF.** `(Gamma, S)` is a limit of finite marked groups: for every `n` some finite group, generated by images of `S`, has exactly the relators of length `<= 2n` that `Gamma` has.
- **What a refutation needs.** Put `P_n = <S | words of length <= n that are trivial in Gamma>`. `Gamma` is not LEF iff for some `n` a word of length `<= n` is nontrivial in `Gamma` but lies in the finite residual of `P_n`.
- **Why LEF matters.** Finitely presented + LEF implies residually finite (Grigorchuk–Medynets 1105.0719, line 174, as recorded in `labbe-shift-derived-full-group-is-finitely-presented`). An infinite simple group is not residually finite, so LEF would make `Gamma` infinitely presented.

## 1. Theorem L1: a spatial criterion

**Setting.** `Omega ⊆ A^{Z^d}` is a subshift with a free `Z^d`-action, `Gamma ≤ [[Omega]]` is generated by a finite symmetric `S`, and `R` is as in Section 0.

**Extensions.** An extension of `S` of radius `R' >= R` is a family of maps `ĉ_s : A^{B(R')} -> B(R) ∩ Z^d` with `ĉ_s(x|B(R')) = c_s(x)` for all `x ∈ Omega`.
- Put `ŝ(z) = sigma^{ĉ_s(z|B(R'))} z` on all of `A^{Z^d}`.
- For a word `w = s_k ... s_1`, put `ŵ = ŝ_k ∘ ... ∘ ŝ_1`. Then `ŵ(z) = sigma^{ĉ_w(z)} z`, where `ĉ_w(z) = Σ_i ĉ_{s_i}(ŝ_{i-1} ... ŝ_1 z)` depends only on `z|B((k-1)R + R')`.

**Relator shift.** `Z_n` is the set of `z` such that `ĉ_w(sigma^v z) = 0` for every `v ∈ Z^d` and every word `w` of length `<= 2n` that is trivial in `Gamma`.
- `Z_n` is an SFT.
- `Z_n` contains `Omega`. Along a point of `Omega` every `ĉ` equals the corresponding `c`, and `w(x) = x` forces `c_w(x) = 0` because the action is free.

**Theorem L1.** Suppose that for every `n` there are an extension (which may depend on `n`) and a periodic `y_n ∈ Z_n` with period lattice `L_n` such that:
- (H1) every `Omega`-legal pattern of radius `2nR'` occurs in `y_n`;
- (H2) `L_n` contains no nonzero vector of norm `<= 2nR`.

Then `Gamma` is LEF. When `Omega` is minimal and aperiodic, (H1) and (H2) both follow from a single `Omega`-legal patch of radius `rho_n` inside `y_n`, where `rho_n` depends only on `n`.

**Proof.**
1. **Permutations.** Let `O` be the finite shift orbit of `y_n`. Each `ŝ` maps `O` into `O`. Write `ŝ'` for the extension of `s^{-1}`. The relator `s^{-1}s` has length 2, so `ŝ' ∘ ŝ = id` on `O`, and `ŝ|_O ∈ Sym(O)`. Let `G_n` be the group these generate and `pi : F(S) -> G_n` the induced map.
2. **Trivial words stay trivial.** If `w` has length `<= 2n` and is trivial in `Gamma`, then `ĉ_w = 0` on `O ⊆ Z_n`, so `pi(w) = 1`.
3. **Nontrivial words stay nontrivial.**
   - Let `w` have length `<= 2n` and be nontrivial in `Gamma`. Some `x ∈ Omega` has `w(x) ≠ x`, so `u = c_w(x) ≠ 0` and `|u| <= 2nR`.
   - By (H1) some `z ∈ O` has `z|B(2nR') = x|B(2nR')`.
   - After `i` letters, the images of `z` and `x` agree on `B(2nR' - iR) ⊇ B(R')`. So every step reads a legal window, and `ĉ_w(z) = u`.
   - By (H2) `u ∉ L_n`, so `ŵ(z) = sigma^u z ≠ z` and `pi(w) ≠ 1`.
4. **Conclusion.** `(G_n, pi(S))` and `(Gamma, S)` have the same relators of length `<= 2n`. So `(G_n, pi(S)) -> (Gamma, S)` in the space of marked groups, and `Gamma` is LEF.
5. **Minimal aperiodic case.**
   - Uniform recurrence gives `rho` such that every legal patch of radius `rho` contains every legal pattern of radius `2nR'`.
   - Suppose that for every `rho` some legal pattern of radius `rho` had a period `u` with `0 < |u| <= 2nR`. A limit point would be a point of `Omega` with period `u`, which freeness excludes. ∎

**Scope.**
- L1 is only sufficient: it says nothing about non-spatial models (Section 6).
- Freeness enters only through `Omega ⊆ Z_n`.

## 2. The known LEF proofs are collared periodisations

**Lemma L2.** Let `Omega ⊆ A^{Z^2}` be an SFT with forbidden patterns of radius `r_0` and no periodic point. For `r >= r_0`, no periodic configuration has all of its radius-`r` windows `Omega`-legal.

*Proof.* Such a configuration lies in `Omega` and is periodic. ∎

**Collared periodisations.**
- Call a periodic `y` *collared at radius `r`* if all its radius-`r` windows are legal.
- If `y` is collared at radius `2nR'`, then `y ∈ Z_n` for every extension, since every `ĉ` it meets equals the corresponding `c`.
- So a collared family satisfies L1 with no choices at all. The three known theorems below produce exactly such families, and L2 says none exists for `Omega_U`.

### 2.1 Grigorchuk–Medynets (1105.0719, Theorem `TheoremLEF`, read from source)

- **Statement.** `[[T]]` is LEF for every Cantor minimal system `(X, T)`.
- **Mechanism.**
  - Fix a finite set `F`. Proposition 3.9 factors every `Q ∈ F^2` as `Q = S_Q R_Q`.
    - `S_Q` is an `n`-permutation: it permutes the levels of a Kakutani–Rokhlin tower.
    - `R_Q` is an `n`-rotation, supported on levels `[-d, d]`.
  - The `n`-permutations form a finite group `H`, and `phi(Q) = S_Q`.
  - `phi` is multiplicative for two reasons: the factorisation is unique, and `S_Z^{-1} R_Q S_Z` is again an `n`-rotation.
- **Spatial reading (our reconstruction, not in the source).**
  - Take the base to be a cylinder `[u]` of a long word `u`. Every tower word starts with `u` and is followed by `u`.
  - So wrapping a tower into a cycle keeps every window of radius `< |u|` legal, while `phi` drops the seam corrections `R_Q`.
  - This is a collared periodisation.
- **Failure for `Omega_U`.**
  - Wrapping a `Z^2` castle shape into a torus with legal windows would give a collared periodic configuration, which L2 excludes.
  - `n`-rotations would have to live on codimension-one seams and be normalised by within-shape permutations. No such replacement is known.

### 2.2 Cornulier (2002.09342, read from source): Proposition `sdlef`, lines 729–750, and the proof of Theorem `clef` (Grigorchuk–Medynets, line 698), lines 753ff

- **Mechanism.**
  - Let `X` be an irreducible `Z`-subshift and write `X = ∩ X_n` with `X_n` SFTs having dense periodic points (lemma `intirr`).
  - Then `lim [[X_n]]⊙ ≅ [[X]]⊙`, injective by compactness. Each `[[X_n]]⊙` has dense finite orbits, so it is residually finite, and the semigroup `[[X]]⊙` is LEF.
  - The proof of `clef` handles a general minimal Cantor `X` and finite `F ⊆ [[X]]⊙` by passing to a subshift factor `Y`. The elements of `F` have cocycles constant on fibres, and `[[X -> Y]]⊙ -> [[Y]]⊙` is injective on `F`.
- **Spatial reading.**
  - Suppose `X = ∩ X_n` is decreasing. A word occurring in every `X_n` occurs in `X`, by compactness.
  - So for each `l` there is `n(l)` such that, for `n >= n(l)`, the radius-`l` words of `X_n` are exactly those of `X`.
  - The periodic points of such `X_n` are collared periodisations of `X` at radius `l`.
- **Failure for `Omega_U`.**
  - A decreasing sequence of SFTs whose intersection is an SFT `Omega` equals `Omega` from some index on. The set of configurations with a forbidden pattern of `Omega` at the origin is compact, and it misses the intersection.
  - So `X_n = Omega` eventually, and `Omega` has no periodic points.
- **The factor step does not help.**
  1. The cocycles of `Gamma` separate points of `Omega_U`. Given `x ≠ x'`, pick `v ∈ {e_1, e_2}` with `x' ∉ {sigma^v x, sigma^{2v} x}`; freeness excludes both directions failing.
  2. Take a small clopen `C ∋ x` whose translates `C`, `sigma^v C` and `sigma^{2v} C` are disjoint and miss `x'`. The 3-cycle `tau` on `C -> sigma^v C -> sigma^{2v} C` is a commutator of transpositions, so it lies in `Gamma`. Then `c_tau(x) = v ≠ 0 = c_tau(x')`.
  3. By compactness of `[a] × [b]` for tiles `a ≠ b`, there is `n_0` such that the cocycles of `B_{n_0}(Gamma)` determine the tile at the origin.
  4. Let `pi : Omega_U -> Y` be a factor with all cocycles of `F ⊇ B_{n_0}(Gamma)` constant on fibres. If `pi x = pi x'`, then `pi sigma^v x = pi sigma^v x'` for all `v`, so `x_v = x'_v` for all `v`.
  5. So `pi` is a conjugacy, `Y` is an SFT, and the previous bullet applies to `Y`.

### 2.3 Ma (2209.00580, read from source)

- **Statement.** Theorem C (line 405): a minimal, topologically free, residually finite action on the Cantor set has LEF full group.
  - Line 402: "the only known examples of LEF topological full groups are derived from minimal Z-actions".
  - The proof (lemma `compressed LEF representation`, line 1225, and the proof of Theorem C) uses the finite actions `beta_n` exactly. The map is multiplicative "because `beta_n` is an action" (line 1282).
- **Spatial reading.** A finite `epsilon`-dense model `E ⊆ X` carries a genuine action close to the true one, and its points carry their true windows. This is a collared periodisation in all but name.
- **Failure for `Omega_U`.** By Theorem A (`sfts-without-finite-orbits-are-not-residually-finite-actions`), `Z^2 ~ Omega_U` is not residually finite.

### 2.4 Corollary L2': the Ma route fails for every extension

**Lemma.** Let `X` be a compact metric `Lambda`-space without isolated points, `pi : Y -> X` a factor map, and `Lambda ~ Y` residually finite. Then `Lambda ~ X` is residually finite.

Residually finite is in Ma's sense: for finite `F` and `epsilon > 0` there is a finite `epsilon`-dense `E ⊆ Y` with an action `beta` such that `d(alpha(s) z, beta(s) z) < epsilon` for `z ∈ E` and `s ∈ F`.

*Proof.*
1. Given `F` and `epsilon`, choose `eta` from the uniform continuity of `pi` and of each `alpha(s)`, `s ∈ F`. Take a model `(E, beta)` on `Y` at scale `eta`.
2. `X` has no isolated points, so there are distinct `x_e ∈ X` with `d(x_e, pi(e)) < eta`. Transport `beta` to `E' = {x_e}`.
3. Equivariance gives `alpha(s) pi(e) = pi(alpha(s) e)`, so

   ```text
   d(alpha(s) x_e, x_{beta(s)e}) <= d(alpha(s) x_e, alpha(s) pi(e))
                                  + d(pi(alpha(s) e), pi(beta(s) e))
                                  + d(pi(beta(s) e), x_{beta(s)e}).
   ```

   Each term is `< epsilon/3` once `eta` is small.
4. `pi` is surjective and uniformly continuous, so `E'` is `epsilon`-dense. ∎

**Consequences for `Omega_U`.**
- `Omega_U` is infinite and minimal, so it has no isolated points. By Theorem A, `Z^2 ~ Omega_U` is not residually finite, so by the lemma no extension `Y -> Omega_U` is residually finite.
- Every `g ∈ [[Omega_U]]` lifts to `ĝ(y) = sigma^{c_g(pi y)} y` in `[[Y]]`, and the lift is an injective homomorphism. So `Gamma ≤ [[Y]]` for every extension `Y`.
- Ma's Theorem C would give LEF through any residually finite minimal `Y`, and there is none.

## 3. Remark L3: fixed extensions

Fix one extension for all `n`, so that `Z_1 ⊇ Z_2 ⊇ ...` and `Z_∞ = ∩ Z_n ⊇ Omega`.
1. If `Z_∞ = Omega`, then `Z_n = Omega` for large `n`, as in 2.2. So (H1) and (H2) fail from then on.
2. All relators have zero cocycle on `Z_∞`, so `F(S) -> Homeo(Z_∞)` factors through `Gamma`.
   - A periodic `y ∈ Z_∞` has a finite `Gamma`-invariant orbit, and the infinite simple group `Gamma` acts on it trivially.
   - So every `ĉ_s` takes values in `L` along `y`. In particular `y` contains no legal patch on which some generator moves by a vector outside `L`.
   - Each witness `y_n` of L1 therefore leaves `Z_m` for some `m > n`.

So L1 needs extensions that change with `n`, or witnesses each of which breaks some longer relator.

## 4. Remark L4: slivers in the polygon-exchange picture

- **The picture.** By Labbé 1903.06137 (abstract only), the 19-tile partition `P_U` is a Markov partition for a toral `Z^2`-rotation `R_U`, and `Omega_U` is its coding.
  - So, off a null set, `Gamma` acts on `T^2` by piecewise translations with polygonal pieces.
  - The action is faithful: each nontrivial `g` moves a cylinder of positive measure.
- **The IET recipe.** For interval exchanges the LEF recipe is rational specialisation: move the parameters to rationals, keep every sign condition at scale `n`, and act on a finite grid. This works when every sign condition is a `Q`-linear form in the parameters, as for IETs and rectangle exchanges.
- **Why it fails for `P_U`.**
  - No rational specialisation keeps the scale-1 combinatorics.
  - Suppose a rational rotation `R'` coded by `P_U` produced only atoms of `⋁_{|v|<=1} R'^{-v} P_U` that code legal radius-1 patterns. Then a generic `R'`-orbit would code a periodic valid tiling, which L2 excludes.
  - So some atom codes a forbidden adjacency (a "sliver"), and every `R'`-orbit meets a sliver.
- **What survives.** Slivers shrink as `R' -> R_U`, so small defect density is available. Whether a model can be exact over the slivers is the question of Section 6.

## 5. Proposition L5: Schreier rigidity, and why it misses LEF

**Proposition L5 (proof sketch).** There is `r` such that no finite `F(S)`-set `E` has every rooted `r`-ball of its Schreier graph isomorphic to a rooted `r`-ball of some orbital Schreier graph `Sch(Gamma ~ Omega, x)`.

*Sketch.*
1. **Charts.**
   - With a free action, `g x = x` iff `c_g(x) = 0`, a clopen condition in `x`. Also `Stab(x)` determines `x`: a 3-cycle on a small clopen set near `x` separates it from any `x' ≠ x`.
   - By compactness, the `r`-ball determines `x|B(rho(r))`, with `rho(r) -> ∞`. This gives each `e ∈ E` a chart pattern.
2. **Moves.**
   - For large `rho`, the cylinder `C` of a chart pattern satisfies `C ∩ sigma^{e_i} C = ∅` (aperiodicity plus compactness).
   - So the 3-cycle `(C, sigma^{e_i} C, sigma^{2e_i} C)` lies in `Gamma` and moves `x' ∈ C` by `e_i`. It is a word of bounded length.
   - Define `e + e_i` by applying the word read off the chart of `e`.
3. **Contradiction.**
   - Three facts are word identities of bounded length on `Omega`, so they hold on `E` once `r` is large: `+e_1` and `+e_2` commute, both moves are invertible, and charts are compatible with moves.
   - So `E` is a finite union of tori carrying a colouring whose windows are legal, which L2 excludes. ∎

**Why this misses LEF.**
- LEF models act regularly on `G_n`, and a regular action has a locally Cayley Schreier graph, not an orbital one.
- The finite actions to compare are those on `G_n/K`. The natural candidate `K_n = <images of Stab(x) ∩ B_n>` is not controlled by the ball `B_n`.
- So L5 only says that every finite model has non-orbital vertices somewhere.
- Compare `minimal-free-sft-full-group-pattern-models-are-exact`, which is about infinite validly coloured models.

## 6. The crux

Two ways to prove LEF remain, and neither is excluded.
- **(C1) Spatial.** For every `n` there are an extension and a periodic `y_n ∈ Z_n` with an `Omega_U`-patch of radius `rho_n` (`labbe-relator-shifts-carry-patch-rich-periodic-points`).
  - L1 turns this into LEF.
  - By L2, `y_n` must show forbidden patterns. By L3, the extensions must move with `n`.
- **(C2) Non-spatial.** Finite groups exact on `B_n(Gamma)` whose actions are nowhere locally orbital. L5 allows this.

A refutation must produce the word described in Section 0.

**Heuristics, not proofs.**
- **Extending 3-cycles.** 3-cycles `tau_{C,v}` lie in `Gamma` (Section 2.2). Each extends to an order-3 homeomorphism of `A^{Z^2}`: shrink the pattern set to `U'' = U' \ (sigma^v U' ∪ sigma^{2v} U' ∪ sigma^{-v} U' ∪ sigma^{-2v} U')`, so that triples never overlap.
- **Commutation relators.** Gate every extended move by "all windows involved are legal". Commutation relators between generators with disjoint supports then survive, because supports are read from legal windows, where they are disjoint.
- **Conjugation relators.** Relators `tau_1 tau_2 tau_1^{-1} = tau_3` fail at gate boundaries. The gates on the two sides read windows at different places, and a defect can close one gate but not the other.
- **Zero flux.**
  - `W(w) = Σ_{z ∈ O} ĉ_w(z)` is a homomorphism `F(S) -> Z^2` on any spatial model.
  - `Gamma` is perfect, so an exact model needs `W(ŝ) = 0` for every generator.
  - On `Omega`, `∫ c_s dmu = 0` for every invariant `mu`, for the same reason. The defects must make the model's total exactly 0. This is not an obstruction by itself.
- **The amenable case.** Suppose `Gamma` is amenable (open: `labbe-shift-derived-full-group-is-amenable`). Then the stabiliser IRS of `Gamma ~ (Omega, mu)` is a limit of finite Schreier graphs, by co-soficity of IRS for amenable groups (not read from source here). That yields approximately spatial finite actions with small defect density, but not exact ones.

## 7. Positive fp methods, and whether each survives an invariant probability

`Omega_U` carries a full-support invariant probability `mu` (`labbe-shift-derived-full-group-escapes-known-obstructions`, item 2). So no clopen set is compressed: if `A ⊇ B ⊔ C` with `A ≅ B ≅ C` through bisections, then `mu(A) = 0`.

|---|---|---|---|
| Brin's `2V` | math/0501082 `main.tex`, lines 206–367 | Patterns of dyadic rectangles. A rectangle is halved horizontally or vertically (lines 292–300), and an element maps the numbered rectangles of one pattern onto those of another by maps `(x,y) -> (2^a x + b, 2^c y + d)` (lines 363–367). So the whole square is a copy of each rectangle | **No.** A basic piece is equivalent to the whole space, which is compression |

The Bieri–Renz criterion decides finiteness properties of `ker chi` for a `G` already of type `F_m` | **Not obstructed, but vacuous.** `Gamma` is perfect, so `S(Gamma) = ∅`. Applying it to `G = [[Omega_U]] ⊇ Gamma` presupposes that `G` is finitely presented, which is as open as the question |
| Matte Bon | 1801.10133 and 1408.0762, abstracts | Rigidity of homomorphisms between full groups, Schreier growth, and a classification of confined subgroups and URSs; embeddings of Grigorchuk groups into full groups of minimal subshifts | **Nothing to apply.** Neither paper has a finite presentation criterion |

| Nekrashevych 1312.5654 | lines 50–90, as recorded in the fp node | Expanding maps and hyperbolic groupoids | **No.** An expanding local homeomorphism compresses |

**Verdict.**
- Every positive finite presentation method checked rests on compression.
- The one compression-free tool, `Sigma`, is vacuous for a perfect group and presupposes a finitely presented ambient group.
- `mu` does not formally forbid a finite presentation. It forbids these proofs of one.
