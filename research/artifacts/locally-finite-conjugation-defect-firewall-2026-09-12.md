## 0. Summary

  of `R^x`, and a stable letter for every unit `u`, acting on `L cap u^-1 L u` by conjugation.
  The resulting HNN extension has a characteristic-two rank model with three properties:
  * it is the free (regular) profile on every finite subgroup of `L`;
  * it is fixed-point-free when `L` is infinite;
  * it needs no soficity theorem, only finite permutation intertwiners.
* **Section 2 (established): independent defects.** When the two-root configurations of pairwise
  disjoint proper cylinders lie in `L`, the model has `rk(D_A) = 3/8` and
  `rk(D_(A_1) ... D_(A_k)) = (3/8)^k`. So `theta = 1`. No derivation of strict submultiplicativity
  can use only identities of one locally finite subgroup, single conjugations by arbitrary units
  (compressors, torus units, the `V`-conjugacies of Fact 1.2), rank calculus, nontriviality and
  fixed-point-freeness.
* **Section 3 (established): the ternary frame tree.** One prefix replacement conjugates all of the
  following into the locally finite group `union_n GL_(2^n)(F_2)` of degree-zero units:
  * the frames `iota_(Ac)(GL_3(F_2))` for ternary words `c` over `{0, 10, 11}`;
  * the nine-leaf `GL_9(F_2)` at `A`;
  * every halving unit.

  So opposite roots, Weyl elements and the `F_2`-torus at the child cylinders `A0`, `A10`, `A11`

* **Section 4 (established): the first escapes.**

## 1. The HNN regular model

`M` denotes a rank ultraproduct `prod_U M_(m_n)(F_2)` with the normalized rank.

**Theorem 1.1.** Let `L <= R^x` be a countable locally finite subgroup and `U ⊆ R^x` a countable set.
For `u` in `U` put `K_u = L cap u^-1 L u` and `phi_u(k) = u k u^-1`. Let `Lambda = Lambda(L, U)` be the
group generated by `L` and letters `t_u` (`u` in `U`), subject to the relations of `L` and
`t_u k t_u^-1 = phi_u(k)` for `k` in `K_u`. Then there is a homomorphism `sigma : Lambda -> M^x` with:
1. `rk(sigma(alpha)) = rank(lambda_Q(alpha)) / |Q|` for every finite `Q <= L` and `alpha` in `F_2[Q]`,
   where `lambda_Q` is the left regular representation;
2. `sigma` nontrivial, and `Fix_r(sigma(Lambda)) = 0` when `L` is infinite;
3. `sigma(t_u) = sigma(u)` whenever `u` lies in `L`.

*Proof.*
* **Stages.** Exhaust `L` by finite subgroups `H_1 <= H_2 <= ...` and enumerate `U = {u_1, u_2, ...}`.
  At stage `n` let `lambda_n` be the left regular representation of `H_n` on `F_2[H_n]`.
* **Intertwiners.** For `u = u_i` with `i <= n`, put `K_(n,u) = H_n cap u^-1 H_n u`. This is a
  subgroup, and `phi_u : K_(n,u) -> H_n` is an injective homomorphism.
  * Left multiplication by `K_(n,u)` on the set `H_n` is free, with `|H_n|/|K_(n,u)|` orbits (the
    right cosets). The same holds for `phi_u(K_(n,u))`.
  * Choose orbit representatives `x_1, ..., x_m` and `y_1, ..., y_m`, and set
    `pi_(n,u)(k x_l) = phi_u(k) y_l`. This is a permutation of `H_n`, and
    `pi_(n,u) lambda_n(k) = lambda_n(phi_u(k)) pi_(n,u)` for `k` in `K_(n,u)`.
  * If `u` lies in `H_n`, take `pi_(n,u) = lambda_n(u)` instead.
* **Limit.** Put `sigma(h) = [lambda_n(h)]` and `sigma(t_u) = [pi_(n,u)]`, using the identity at the
  finitely many stages where these are undefined.
  * A relation of `L` holds at every stage containing its letters.
  * For `k` in `K_u`, both `k` and `phi_u(k)` lie in `H_n` for large `n`. So `k` lies in `K_(n,u)`,
    and the conjugation relation holds exactly at those stages.
  * So every defining relation of `Lambda` holds in `M`, and `sigma` is a homomorphism.
* **(1).** Once `Q <= H_n`, `F_2[H_n]` is a free left `F_2[Q]`-module of rank `[H_n : Q]`, one summand
  per right coset. So the normalized rank of `lambda_n(alpha)` is `rank(lambda_Q(alpha))/|Q|` for
  every such `n`.
* **(2).** Nontriviality follows from (1). Let `x = [x_n]` satisfy `sigma(g) x = x` for all `g` in
  `L`, and fix `m`.
  * The vectors of `im(x_n)` fixed by `lambda_n(H_m)` form the kernel of
    `v -> ((lambda_n(h) - 1) v)_(h in H_m)`. So their codimension in `im(x_n)` is at most
    `sum_h rank((lambda_n(h) - 1) x_n)`, which is `o(|H_n|)` along `U`.
  * They lie in `Fix(lambda_n(H_m))`, which has normalized dimension `1/|H_m|`: one line per free
    summand.
  * Hence `rk(x) <= 1/|H_m|` for every `m`. An infinite locally finite `L` has `|H_m| -> infinity`,
    so `x = 0`, and `Fix_r(sigma(Lambda)) ⊆ Fix_r(sigma(L)) = 0`.
* **(3)** is the choice made for `u` in `H_n`. QED

**Remark 1.2 (what `Lambda` does not impose).**
* **Products of letters.** No relation among the letters `t_u` beyond (3). In `R^x`, `u u' = u''`,
  but `sigma` need not satisfy `t_u t_(u') = t_(u u')`.
* **Two frames.** No identity between elements of two different locally finite subgroups, unless
  both lie in one `L`.

## 2. Independent cylinder defects

**Corollary 2.1.** Let `A_1, ..., A_k` be pairwise disjoint proper cylinders, and let `L` be a countable
locally finite subgroup of `R^x` containing `iota_(A_i)(x_12(1))` and `iota_(A_i)(x_23(1))` for every
`i`. For example `L = prod_i iota_(A_i)(UT_3(R))`, or the degree-zero units conjugated as in any
frame containing these cylinders. In the model `sigma` of Theorem 1.1:

```text
rk(N^A_12) = 1/2 ,        rk(D_A) = 3/8 ,        rk(D_(A_1) D_(A_2) ... D_(A_k)) = (3/8)^k .
```

In particular `rk(D_A D_B) = rk(D_A) rk(D_B)`, so `theta = 1`.

*Proof.*
* **The configuration group.** `Q_i = iota_(A_i)(<x_12(1), x_23(1)>)` is the Heisenberg group over
  `F_2`, a dihedral group `D_8`. The `Q_i` commute (Fact 1.2 of the descent artifact) and meet
  trivially, since their supports are disjoint. So `Q = Q_1 x ... x Q_k <= L` and
  `F_2[Q] = F_2[Q_1] (x) ... (x) F_2[Q_k]`.
* **Ranks multiply.** The left regular representation of a product of elements from different
  factors is a tensor product of left regular representations, so its rank is the product of ranks.
* **The two values in `F_2[D_8]`.** Let `x, y` be the root involutions and `r = xy`, of order 4.
  * `x + 1` has rank 4: `F_2[D_8]` is free over `F_2[<x>]`, and `x + 1` has rank 1 on `F_2[C_2]`.
  * `(y + 1)(x + 1) = 1 + x + x r + r^3`. Its products with `r^j` and with `x r^j` are the four
    vectors `u_j = r^j + r^(j+3) + x r^j + x r^(j+1)`: those with `x r^j` equal `u_j`, since
    `r^3 x = x r`.
  * The `u_j` sum to zero, and `u_0, u_1, u_2` are independent, as their `r`-parts `1001`, `1100`,
    `0110` show. So the rank is 3.
* **Conclusion.** By Theorem 1.1(1) the normalized ranks are `4/8`, `3/8` and `(3/8)^k`. QED

**Corollary 2.2 (firewall).** Suppose an argument proves `rk(D_A D_B) <= theta rk(D_A) rk(D_B)` with
`theta < 1`. Suppose also that its only inputs are:
1. identities among elements of one countable locally finite subgroup `L` of `R^x` containing both
   configurations;
2. identities `u g u^-1 = g'`, with `u` in `R^x` arbitrary and `g, g'` in `L`;
3. the rank calculus of regular rings with a faithful rank function (Fact 1.1 of the descent
   artifact), nontriviality, and fixed-point-freeness of the model, where `L` is infinite.

Then the argument fails. The model `sigma` of Theorem 1.1, with `U` the conjugators used, satisfies
1–3 and has `theta = 1`.

**Covered inputs.**
* **Halving** at finitely many disjoint cylinders. w4-r4-orth, Section 5 of
  `rank-four-orthogonality-check-2026-09-12.md`, builds this case directly.
* **Single conjugations:** the `V`-conjugacies of Fact 1.2, and compressors, torus units or Weyl
  elements of `R^x` used as conjugators.
* **Finite data:** every finite subgroup containing the configurations carries the free profile.

**Not covered.**
* **Products of conjugators** (Remark 1.2): identities that multiply two conjugating units, or that
  identify a conjugator with a product of elements from different locally finite subgroups.
* **Non-locally-finite configurations:** identities among elements that generate a group which is
  not locally finite. Section 4 gives the first instances.
* **Global inputs** about all rank models of `R^x`:
  * the defect gap, through corner locality, which is where "R has no rank model" enters;
  * globality of fixed ideals, through property (T);
  * compactness over models, as in Lemma 2.3 of the descent artifact.

**Remark 2.3 (scope).**
* **Relation to the unitriangular firewall.** `unitriangular-data-cannot-assemble-corner-leavitt-families`
  uses the direct-limit regular model of a locally finite group against the gate and the corner
  Leavitt family. Theorem 1.1 adds single conjugations by arbitrary units, keeps fixed-point-freeness,
  and Corollary 2.1 applies it to the submultiplicativity target.
* **Breaking the target outright is out of reach.** The target is equivalent to the `F_2` gate, as its
  node records, so a break needs a nontrivial rank model of `R^x`. This lane maps which relational
  inputs are inert.
