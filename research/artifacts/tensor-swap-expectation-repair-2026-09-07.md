# Tensor amplification repairs the group relative-embedding bridge

Date checked: 2026-09-07.

This argument proves a group-specific relative-embedding theorem directly
from matrix microstates. It does not use the plain-double converse in Gao's
Appendix Theorem 7.1. The distinction matters: the displayed commuting square
on page 35 of [arXiv:2012.07940v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07940v3)
requires the amalgamating algebra to commute with the selected unitary, but
the stated relative-Haar hypothesis alone does not give that inclusion.
The direct tensor proof below supplies the needed theorem for group factors.

## 1. The coefficient hypothesis

Let `C <= A` be countable groups. Suppose a Connes-embeddable finite tracial
von Neumann algebra `(P,tau)` contains a canonical copy of `L(A)`, with
group unitaries `u_g`, and a self-adjoint unitary `s` such that

```text
[s,u_c]=0                    (c in C),
tau(u_g^* s u_g s)=1_C(g)    (g in A).                  (TSE1)
```

Then the inclusion `L(C) <= L(A)` is relatively embeddable over the scalar
field in Gao's sense. More concretely there are unitary matrix models
`V_j(g) in U(D_j)` and finite-dimensional unital star-subalgebras
`B_j <= M_(D_j)(C)` such that

```text
||V_j(gh)-V_j(g)V_j(h)||_2 -> 0,
tr(V_j(g)) -> 1_(g=e),
dist_2(V_j(c),B_j) -> 0                  (c in C),
||E_(B_j)(V_j(g))||_2 -> 0              (g outside C). (TSE2)
```

All norms and traces here are normalized. Neither finite generation nor
finite presentability is assumed. The subgroup can be nonamenable and
nonnormal. No exactification of the actor matrices is asserted.

The resulting inclusion is `RE/C` with coefficient algebra `C` meaning the
scalar complex field, not the subgroup denoted `C`.

## 2. One finite-matrix averaging identity

For an exact self-adjoint unitary `S in M_d(C)`, let

```text
B(S)={S}' cap M_d(C),
E_S(X)=(X+SXS)/2.
```

This is the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto `B(S)`. For any
unitary `U`, orthogonality of conditional expectation gives

```text
||E_S(U)||_2^2
 = tr(U^* E_S(U))
 = (1+Re tr(U^* S U S))/2.                            (TSE3)
```

The trace in `(TSE3)` is actually real: `Ad(S)` is a self-adjoint operator
on the Hilbert space of matrices. The real part makes the formula explicit
without needing that observation.

For a positive integer `k`, take `k` tensor factors and put

```text
B(S,k)=B(S) tensor ... tensor B(S),
V(g)=U(g) tensor ... tensor U(g).
```

Equivalently `B(S,k)` is the common commutant of the commuting involutions
which act as `S` in one tensor coordinate. Averaging the finite group they
generate gives the tensor product conditional expectation. Thus

```text
E_(B(S,k))(U^(tensor k))=E_S(U)^(tensor k),
||E_(B(S,k))(U^(tensor k))||_2^2
 = ((1+Re tr(U^* S U S))/2)^k.                        (TSE4)
```

At the exact coefficient `(TSE1)`, this is `1` on the subgroup and `2^-k`
off it. This exponential suppression is the operation absent from the
single-swap argument.

## 3. Exact involution lifts and the diagonal choice

Connes embeddability of the separable algebra generated by `L(A)` and `s`
gives a trace-preserving embedding into a tracial matrix ultraproduct.
Choose unitary lifts `U_n(g)` for the countably many `u_g`, and exact
self-adjoint unitary lifts `S_n` for `s`.

The latter choice introduces no stability assumption. Begin with
self-adjoint bounded lifts `X_n` of `s`, so
`||X_n^2-I||_2 -> 0`. Spectral rounding with `S_n=sign(X_n)`, assigning
either sign to a zero eigenvalue, gives

```text
S_n^*=S_n,     S_n^2=I,
||S_n-X_n||_2 <= ||X_n^2-I||_2 -> 0.
```

Consequently the lift retains all joint moments. Unitary lifts of `u_g`
are obtained by polar rounding, also at vanishing normalized 2-norm cost.
We take `U_n(e)=I`.

Enumerate `A`, its multiplication pairs, and `C`. For each `j>=2`, select
a microstate coordinate `n(j)` on which the following errors, for the first
`j` requested entries, are at most `j^-3`:

* actor multiplication;
* canonical actor trace;
* the traces in `(TSE1)`;
* `||[S_n,U_n(c)]||_2` for the requested subgroup elements.

Each condition holds in the starting ultraproduct, so their finite
intersection is nonempty. Choosing a sequence of such coordinates is
sufficient; it does not require a computable rate or a word-problem
algorithm. Put

```text
V_j(g)=U_(n(j))(g)^(tensor j),
D_j=d_(n(j))^j,
B_j=B(S_(n(j)),j).                                   (TSE5)
```

For any fixed pair `g,h`, tensor telescoping gives

```text
||V_j(gh)-V_j(g)V_j(h)||_2
 <= j ||U_(n(j))(gh)-U_(n(j))(g)U_(n(j))(h)||_2
 <= j^-2
```

eventually. The canonical traces survive because

```text
tr(V_j(g))=tr(U_(n(j))(g))^j.
```

For `g!=e`, the base trace tends to zero; for `e` it equals one.

For `c in C`, the map `E_S` is operator-norm contractive and

```text
||U-E_S(U)||_2=(1/2)||[S,U]||_2.
```

Telescoping products of contractions therefore gives

```text
dist_2(V_j(c),B_j)
 <= ||U_(n(j))(c)^(tensor j)-E_(S_(n(j)))(U_(n(j))(c))^(tensor j)||_2
 <= (j/2)||[S_(n(j)),U_(n(j))(c)]||_2
 <= (1/2)j^-2.
```

For `g outside C`, formula `(TSE4)` gives, eventually,

```text
||E_(B_j)(V_j(g))||_2^2
 <= (1/2+1/(2j^3))^j
 <= (3/4)^j -> 0.                                   (TSE6)
```

This proves all four assertions in `(TSE2)`.

## 4. The complete scalar commuting square

Let `Q=prod_omega M_(D_j)(C)` and `B=prod_omega B_j`. The actor tuples
define a trace-preserving normal embedding

```text
pi:L(A) -> Q,       pi(lambda(g))=(V_j(g))_omega.
```

The usual group-factor extension follows from the canonical trace: the
induced GNS representation of the group algebra is its left regular one.
The coordinate expectations induce the normal trace-preserving expectation
`E_B:Q->B`.

By `(TSE2)`, for each group element,

```text
E_B(pi(lambda(g)))=pi(lambda(g))   if g in C,
E_B(pi(lambda(g)))=0              if g outside C.
```

The group algebra is 2-norm dense in `L(A)`, and both conditional
expectations are 2-norm contractions. Hence

```text
E_B o pi = pi o E_(L(C))
```

on all of `L(A)`. In particular `pi(L(C))` is contained in `B`, and the
square has all four inclusion arrows, in addition to the expectation
identity. Each ambient coordinate is a matrix algebra and each `B_j` is
finite-dimensional, hence hyperfinite. This is exactly relative
embeddability over scalars as defined in [Gao, Definition
2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.07940v3#S2).

## 5. Ordinary and free binary coset lamps

Both of the groups

```text
W=(direct_sum_(A/C) C_2) rtimes A,
E=A *_C (C x C_2)=(*_(A/C) C_2) rtimes A
```

carry the coefficient `(TSE1)` in their canonical traces. Let `s` be the
lamp at `C`. It centralizes `C`. If `g outside C`, the two conjugate lamps
are distinct. Their product is nonidentity both in the direct sum and in
the free product. Thus its canonical trace is zero. If `g in C`, the two
lamps agree and the product is identity.

Therefore hyperlinearity of **either** `W` or `E` gives scalar relative
embeddability of `L(C)<=L(A)` by the direct construction above.

The double `D=A *_C A` and `E` have equivalent hyperlinearity by the
following explicit index-two construction. Let `alpha` exchange the two
vertices of D. In `D rtimes_alpha C_2`, the swap `s` conjugates the first
vertex onto the second and centralizes their common C. Conversely these
relations recover the two vertex maps `a -> a` and `a -> sas` from
`A *_C(C x C_2)`. The two maps between these presentations are inverse,
so `E=D rtimes_alpha C_2` and D is an index-two subgroup.

Given canonical microstates `rho_n` of D, put

```text
rho_tilde_n(d)=diag(rho_n(d),rho_n(alpha(d))),
rho_tilde_n(s)=[[0,I],[I,0]].
```

The swap relation is exact; multiplication errors are bounded by the
corresponding errors for `rho_n` and `rho_n o alpha`. The normalized trace
on D is the mean of those two canonical traces, and every element `ds`
has trace zero because its matrix is off diagonal. These are canonical
microstates of E. Restriction to D gives the converse. Thus this step
uses no arithmetic folding result or finite-dimensional stability theorem.
Consequently,

```text
D hyperlinear => L(C)<=L(A) is RE/scalars.             (TSE7)
```

This repairs the group-level plain-double converse for every countable
pair. It requires no relative-Haar group element and does not assume that
any element of `A outside C` centralizes `C`.

The reverse construction from a scalar relative embedding to the enlarged
amalgam is the unaffected forward implication of Gao's Theorem 7.1. That
enlarged amalgam contains the subgroup-centralizer HNN group. Conversely the
double embeds in the HNN group. Combining these valid implications yields
equivalence of double hyperlinearity, free binary coset-wreath
hyperlinearity, subgroup-centralizer HNN hyperlinearity, and scalar relative
embeddability. The ordinary binary wreath implication established so far is
in the direction `W hyperlinear =>` these equivalent conditions. The next
section proves its converse by a separate bounded tensor construction. No
quotient permanence is used.

## 6. The bounded tensor construction of ordinary Gaussian lamps

Suppose the free binary group `E` is hyperlinear, and put `P=L(E)`. Write
`X=A/C` and denote its canonical free lamp involutions by `s_x`, `x in X`.
They satisfy

```text
tau(s_x)=0,
tau(s_x s_y)=1_(x=y),
u_g s_x u_g^*=s_(gx).                                (TSE8)
```

For every integer `k>=1`, work in the finite tensor product `P_k=P^tensor k`
with its product trace, and set, for `t in Q`,

```text
v_(x,k)(t)=(exp(i t s_x/sqrt(k)))^tensor k,
U_k(g)=u_g^tensor k.                                 (TSE9)
```

These are bounded unitaries. Their actor covariance is exact, and
`v_(x,k)(t+q)=v_(x,k)(t)v_(x,k)(q)` for a fixed `x`. This formulation never
places unbounded Gaussian variables or growing-norm tensor sums into a
tracial ultraproduct.

### Joint lamp moments

Fix a finite ordered list `(x_1,t_1),...,(x_r,t_r)`. Put
`a_x=sum_(l:x_l=x) t_l`. Expanding the finitely many exponentials to second
order in `k^-1/2` gives

```text
tau(product_(l=1)^r exp(i t_l s_(x_l)/sqrt(k)))
 = 1-(1/(2k))sum_x a_x^2+O(k^-3/2).                  (TSE10)
```

The linear term vanishes by centering. The quadratic self-terms are
`-sum_l t_l^2/(2k)`, and the ordered cross-terms are
`-sum_(l<m) t_l t_m tau(s_(x_l)s_(x_m))/k`. By `(TSE8)` these add up to
the quadratic expression in `(TSE10)`. The remainder is bounded in operator
norm by a constant depending only on the fixed list of real parameters;
all `s_x` have operator norm one. No dimension or tensor power enters that
constant.

Taking the product trace on `P_k` therefore yields

```text
lim_(k->infinity) tau_k(product_(l=1)^r v_(x_l,k)(t_l))
 = exp(-(1/2)sum_x a_x^2).                           (TSE11)
```

This is the characteristic function of independent standard real Gaussian
coordinates `Z_x`. The same calculation covers adjoints by replacing `t`
with `-t`.

In particular, using the ordered word
`v_x(t)v_y(q)v_x(-t)v_y(-q)` gives limiting trace one. Its normalized
2-distance to identity tends to zero. Hence the images `v_x(t)` of these
sequences commute in `prod_omega P_k`. Their joint moments identify the
abelian algebra they generate with

```text
L^infinity(R^X,gamma^X),
v_x(t) <-> exp(i t Z_x)                              (TSE12)
```

trace-preservingly. To make this identification precise, start with the
star-algebra spanned by characters for rational `t`. Formula `(TSE11)`
identifies its GNS inner product exactly with the Gaussian one. Rational
characters generate the Borel sigma algebra of `R`, so the resulting normal
closure is the full Gaussian probability algebra, not a proper factor of
it. Since `X` is countable, all generators used are countable.

### Mixed actor moments

For `g!=e`, canonical actor trace gives `tau(u_g)=0`. Tensor factorization
and operator-norm telescoping give

```text
|tau_k((product_l v_(x_l,k)(t_l))U_k(g))|
 = |tau((product_l exp(i t_l s_(x_l)/sqrt(k)))u_g)|^k
 <= ((sum_l |t_l|)/sqrt(k))^k -> 0.                  (TSE13)
```

The estimate follows by subtracting the identity inside the product; its
operator-norm distance to identity is at most `sum_l |t_l|/sqrt(k)`.
For `g=e`, formula `(TSE11)` applies instead. Exact covariance and these
mixed moments identify the generated crossed product with

```text
L^infinity(R^X,gamma^X) rtimes A                      (TSE14)
```

with its canonical crossed-product trace. The verification is on the
algebraic span of Gaussian characters times actor unitaries, and extends
normally by the faithful tracial GNS construction.

### Connes embeddability and binary signs

Every finite tensor power `P_k` is CE because `P` is CE. The separable
algebra generated by the countably many sequences in `(TSE9)` is also CE:
embed each `P_k` into a tracial matrix ultraproduct and diagonalize the
countably many required mixed moments. Equivalently, flatten the resulting
iterated tracial matrix ultraproduct. Thus `(TSE14)` is CE.

Inside its Gaussian base define `b_x=sign(Z_x)`. Zero has Gaussian measure
zero, so the convention there is irrelevant. These are commuting
self-adjoint unitaries with independent uniform signs. The actor permutes
them by `x -> gx`. Every nonempty finite product of distinct signs has
trace zero, while every word with nonidentity actor component has trace
zero by the crossed-product trace. Therefore the subgroup generated by
the `b_x` and `u_g` has exactly the canonical trace of

```text
(direct_sum_X C_2) rtimes A = W.
```

Its group von Neumann algebra embeds trace-preservingly in `(TSE14)`.
Consequently `W` is hyperlinear.

Combining with Sections 1--5 proves

```text
ordinary binary W hyperlinear
  iff free binary E hyperlinear
  iff classical Gaussian crossed product (TSE14) is CE. (TSE15)
```

For the Gaussian-to-ordinary implication, the sign-subalgebra argument
alone suffices. For the ordinary-to-free implication, the relative
embedding from Section 5 feeds the valid forward relative-amalgam theorem
and the HNN construction, whose Haar central letter can be replaced by its
balanced sign to realize the free binary group.

The role of the trace is essential. The original free lamp algebra and the
ordinary lamp algebra have different fourth moments: distinct free
involutions satisfy `tau(s_x s_y s_x s_y)=0`, whereas ordinary signs give
trace one. Formula `(TSE10)` constructs new lamps in growing tensor powers;
it does not identify or quotient the old lamp algebras.

## 7. Source and scope check

Repository searches included the exact new claim names and the phrases
`free coset wreath`, `ordinary wreath`, `Gaussian`, `tensor`, and
`relative embeddability`. The earlier Gaussian and free-Bogoliubov notes
distinguish their trace laws and record failures of finite-Fock truncation.
The bounded tensor exponentials above use a different trace-preserving
construction.

The tracial tensor central limit is established prior art: [Greg Kuperberg,
*A tracial quantum central limit theorem*, Theorem
2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0202035) gives convergence of ordered
products of bounded continuous functions of centered tensor sums to
classical Gaussian expectations. Section 6 supplies the needed special
case directly using exponentials. Its exact actor covariance and mixed
trace estimate `(TSE13)` are additional crossed-product checks. Binary
signs are taken in the limiting Gaussian algebra; no continuity assertion
for sign in a matrix approximation is needed.

The primary permanence theorem checked was [Gao--Kunnawalkam
Elayavalli--Patchell, *Soficity for group actions on sets and
applications*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40687-025-00526-6), Theorems 3.8--3.9.
Those theorems give hyperlinearity of both ordinary and free generalized
wreaths under a soficity hypothesis on the set action. The proof here starts
instead from hyperlinearity of one binary wreath and does not assume that
action hypothesis. No priority claim is made from the absence of a match in
this bounded search.

The separate [primary-source scope audit](coset-hyperlinearity-equivalence-literature-2026-09-07.md)
records the inspected theorem numbers and adjacent halo-product results.

## 8. Why this does not establish the goal

This is a theorem comparing previously separate candidate obstructions.
It does not supply or exclude any of their canonical microstates. In
particular, neither nonsoficity of an ordinary binary wreath nor failure of
residual finiteness of a double proves failure of the relative-embedding
condition. The arithmetic and Kun--Thom normalized-HS existence questions
remain open after this repair.
