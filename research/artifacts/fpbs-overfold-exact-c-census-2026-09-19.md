## 1. Tools

Directory: `experiments/fpbs-overfold-exact-c-2026-09-17/`.

- **`export.py`** writes an instance `j, Q` in plain text:
  - the folded graph `Γ_{j+1}(Q)`;
  - the map `m` onto `Γ_j(Q)`;
  - one seed pair for each single-seed closure class, with lawful classes
    given a lawful representative;
  - the automorphisms of the `L`-set `Q`, lifted to `Γ_{j+1}(Q)` by reading
    BFS words.
- **`deep.c`** does an exact breadth-first search.
  - Level `k` holds the distinct fold-closed partitions generated by `k`
    seeds, taken up to automorphism.
  - Closure uses union-find with class adjacency.
  - The goal test is that every fibre of `m` is a single class.
  - The last level is only tested, not stored.
  - The flag `law` restricts the search to lawful seeds, which gives
    `law_j`.
- **Validation.**
  - `S_3`, three orbits, `inst6.txt`: `law = 3` and `deep = 3`. This agrees
    with `exact_multi_s3_j1.txt`.
  - Order-12 level, `inst12.txt`: the lawful search reaches the goal at
    exactly 6 seeds, which agrees with `law = 6` from `exact_multi.py`.

Search from one level to the next is exhaustive. It is complete for three
reasons:
- the fold-closure of `P ∪ {s}` is the fold-closure of `closure(P) ∪ {s}`;
- the closure depends only on the class of `s` modulo single-seed closure;
- automorphisms of `Q` commute with folding and preserve `ker(m)`.

## 2. A structural lemma (proved)

**Lemma A (types do not mix under folding).**
- Call a same-fibre pair `(u, v)` of `Γ_{j+1}(Q)` *lawful* if
  `m(u) = m(v)`, and *overfold* otherwise.
- Every pair forced by folding from one pair has the same type as that pair.

*Proof.*
- A fold step on `(u, v)` forces `(u·x, v·x)` for a letter `x^{±1}` defined
  at both `u` and `v`.
- `m` is a morphism, so `m(u·x) = m(u)·x` and `m(v·x) = m(v)·x`.
- `Γ_j(Q)` is folded, so `y ↦ y·x` is injective where it is defined.
- Hence `m(u·x) = m(v·x)` if and only if `m(u) = m(v)`. ∎

**Consequence.** Let `P` be a set of overfold seeds whose fold-closure
contains `ker(m)`.
- The first lawful identifications in that closure are transitive
  composites `u ~ w_1 ~ … ~ w_r ~ v` with `m(u) = m(v)`, built from forced
  overfold pairs, with some `m(w_i) ≠ m(u)`.
- Lemma A holds for every pair of the equivalence relation, not only for
  seeds. So after the first lawful composites appear, they propagate
  lawfully.
- Hence `deep < law` needs the overfold orbits to close up into cycles
  through `Γ_j(Q)`.
- A proof of the weak form `deep ≥ c · law` has to control those cycles.
  Counting ranks or Euler characteristics does not see them, because `law`
  is a relative rank over `K = S ∩ L_{j+1}` (w16), not an absolute rank.

## 3. What Hanna Neumann counting gives (proved, and not enough)

This section treats the connected case: one `L_{j+1}`-orbit inside one
`L_j`-orbit.

**Setup.**
- `S = Stab_L(p)`, `K = S ∩ L_{j+1}`, `K' = S ∩ L_j`, and seeds
  `g_1, …, g_k ∈ S`.
- Put `H = ⟨K, g_1, …, g_k⟩`. Success means `H ⊇ K'`. Since `H ≤ S`, this
  forces `H ∩ L_j = K'`.

**The inequality.**
- `L_j` has rank 2. The strengthened Hanna Neumann inequality (Friedman,
  Mineyev) gives
  `Σ_{HgL_j} rk~(H ∩ g L_j g^{-1}) ≤ rk(H) − 1 ≤ rk(K) − 1 + k`.
- The base double coset contributes `rk~(K') = |p·L_j|`, and
  `rk(K) − 1 = |p·L_{j+1}|`.
- Hence `k ≥ (|p·L_j| − |p·L_{j+1}|) + Σ_{g ∉ H L_j} rk~(H ∩ g L_j g^{-1})`.

**Why it is vacuous.**
- The first term is `0` when `Q` is `L_{j+1}`-transitive. In general,
  summed over components, it is the difference of orbit counts, which is
  the cheap bound of w16.
- The other terms have no forced lower bound. `L_j` is malnormal in `L`, so
  `K' ∩ g L_j g^{-1} = 1` for `g ∉ L_j`, and `H ⊇ K'` forces nothing there.
- Take any tower `Q_m` with `o(|Q_m|)` orbits of `L_{j+1}`, for example
  `L_{j+1}`-transitive towers. On it the bound is `o(|Q_m|)`, while the
  floor needs `deep_j(Q_m) ≥ c |Q_m|`.
- So no argument that bounds `deep` through Euler characteristics of
  `core(H) ×_R core(L_j)` alone can give the floor.
- This sharpens the w16 remark that homological certificates do not
  transfer. The exchange has to use the double-coset restriction on seeds,
  or Lemma A.
