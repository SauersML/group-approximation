# Torsor CFI graphs: nonabelian local symmetry in isomorphism syntax

Lane `ex-kac-quantum-compiler`, 2026-09-13.  Part 1: the dictionary and the
port-profile theorem.  The mathematics lands as Cairn nodes
`torsor-cfi-isomorphism-algebra-is-coset-constraint-algebra` and
`torsor-cfi-port-profiles-are-cosets`, each with a `-proof` route.

## 0. Why this family

The target is a pair of graphs that are `qc`- but not `qa`-isomorphic and
whose quantum symmetry is not dual to a group
(`qc-isomorphic-not-qa-isomorphic-graph-pair-exists`).  On colored CFI graphs
over `F_2` the rung is the solution-group endpoint
(`colored-cfi-rung-is-the-solution-group-case`, Roberson--Schmidt
arXiv:2111.12362v1, Theorems 3.8 and 6.7).  The most direct way to put
nonabelian local symmetry into isomorphism syntax is to run the CFI
construction over a finite nonabelian group `H`.  This part computes exactly
what that construction gives, and where it stops.

Summary.

- **Dictionary.**  Over any finite group `H` the isomorphism algebra of two
  torsor CFI graphs is a *coset constraint algebra*: `H`-valued variables,
  commuting inside each context, whose joint spectrum in each context lies in
  one left coset of a subgroup.  Over `F_2` this is Roberson--Schmidt.
- **Genuinely quantum.**  The quantum automorphism group contains the Hopf
  quotients `C(L)` of the local coset groups.  When one of them is
  nonabelian, it is not a group dual.
- **Firewall.**  Scalar port profiles are right cosets of subgroups of
  `H^P`, for `t = q, qa, qc` alike.  With Boolean ports read through
  involutions they are affine.  So nonabelian local symmetry *of torsor type*
  does not cross the affine barrier atom by atom.  The escape needs gadget
  families whose port variations are not twists of one graph.

## 1. Definitions

Colored digraphs and their isomorphism algebras are as in
`closure-safe-support-is-exactly-the-colored-iso-rung`: a colouring
`c_X : V_X x V_X -> C` whose diagonal colours are never off-diagonal colours,
and `A(Iso(X,Y))` generated by a magic unitary `V = (v_(xa))` with
`v_(xa) v_(x'a') = 0` whenever `c_X(x,x') != c_Y(a,a')`.

- `X ≅_q Y`: `A(Iso(X,Y))` has a nonzero finite-dimensional representation.
- `X ≅_qa Y`: it has a unital *-homomorphism into `R^U`.
- `X ≅_qc Y`: it has a tracial state.

**Torsor CFI data.**  A finite group `H`; variables `[n]`; contexts
`k in [m]` with nonempty variable sets `S_k subseteq [n]`; nonempty local
relations `R_k subseteq H^(S_k)`.  For `g, alpha in H^(S_k)` write `g alpha`
for the coordinatewise product.

**Torsor CFI graph `G_H(R)`.**
- Vertices: `(k, alpha)` with `k in [m]` and `alpha in R_k`.
- Diagonal colour of `(k,alpha)`: `("v", k)`.
- Colour of the arc from `(k,alpha)` to `(l,alpha') != (k,alpha)`:

  ```text
  ("e", k, l, (alpha_i^(-1) alpha'_i)_(i in S_k ∩ S_l)).          (TC1)
  ```

For `k != l` with `S_k ∩ S_l` empty the colour is constant, so it imposes
nothing.  Over `H = F_2` with `R_k` the local solutions of equation `k`, this is
Roberson--Schmidt Definition 3.2 (they use non-edges between contexts sharing
no variable and a symmetric colour `alpha △ beta`; the algebra is the same).

**Coset constraint algebra `A_H(S,T)`.**  For subsets `T_k subseteq H^(S_k)`,
the universal unital *-algebra generated by projections `p^k_g`
(`k in [m]`, `g in T_k`) with

```text
sum_(g in T_k) p^k_g = 1,                                            (CC1)
p^k_g p^l_(g') = 0   whenever  g|_(S_k ∩ S_l) != g'|_(S_k ∩ S_l).        (CC2)
```

For `k = l`, (CC2) says `(p^k_g)` is a PVM.  Summing (CC2) gives the usual
consequence: for `i in S_k ∩ S_l` the marginal PVMs of contexts `k` and `l` on
the variable `i` coincide.  So `A_H(S,T)` is the synchronous algebra of the
constraint system with `H`-valued variables and allowed local sets `T_k`.

## 2. The dictionary

**Theorem D.**  Let `R, R'` be torsor CFI data on the same `(H,[n],S)`, and put

```text
T_k = { g in H^(S_k) : g R_k = R'_k }.                               (TC2)
```

Then there is a *-isomorphism `A(Iso(G_H(R), G_H(R'))) ≅ A_H(S,T)` with

```text
v_((k,alpha),(k,beta)) |-> p^k_(beta alpha^(-1))   if beta alpha^(-1) in T_k,  else 0,
v_((k,alpha),(l,beta)) |-> 0                       for k != l.            (TC3)
```

Each nonempty `T_k` is a left coset `h_k K_k` of the subgroup
`K_k = Stab(R_k) = { g : g R_k = R_k }`, for any `h_k in T_k`.

**Proof.**  Let `V` be the universal magic unitary of the isomorphism algebra.

*Step 1: blocks.*  For `k != l`, `v_((k,alpha),(l,beta)) = v v` and the two
diagonal colours `("v",k)`, `("v",l)` differ, so the entry is `0`.  Write
`u_(alpha,beta)` for `v_((k,alpha),(k,beta))`.

*Step 2: shift dependence.*  Fix `alpha, beta` and put `g = beta alpha^(-1)`.
Let `alpha' in R_k`.  Row `alpha'` sums to one and its other blocks vanish, so

```text
u_(alpha,beta) = sum_(beta' in R'_k) u_(alpha,beta) u_(alpha',beta').
```

A term survives only if the arc colours agree.  For `alpha' != alpha` this
means `alpha^(-1) alpha' = beta^(-1) beta'`, i.e. `beta' = g alpha'`.  For
`alpha' = alpha` the diagonal colour forces `beta' = beta = g alpha`.  Hence

- if `g alpha' notin R'_k`, then `u_(alpha,beta) = 0`;
- otherwise `u_(alpha,beta) = u_(alpha,beta) u_(alpha', g alpha')`, so
  `u_(alpha,beta) <= u_(alpha', g alpha')`.

So `u_(alpha,beta) != 0` forces `g R_k subseteq R'_k`.  Block `k` is square
when the algebra is nonzero (sum all entries by rows and by columns), so
`g R_k = R'_k`, i.e. `g in T_k`.  By symmetry in `alpha, alpha'`, all
`u_(alpha, g alpha)` with `alpha in R_k` are equal.  Call this projection
`p^k_g`.

*Step 3: relations.*  Row `alpha` of block `k` is `sum_(g in T_k) p^k_g = 1`,
since `g |-> g alpha` is injective.  For `k != l` the arc colour condition for
`u_((k,alpha),(k,g alpha)) u_((l,alpha'),(l,g' alpha'))` reads

```text
alpha_i^(-1) alpha'_i = (g_i alpha_i)^(-1) g'_i alpha'_i  for i in S_k ∩ S_l,
```

which is `g_i = g'_i`.  So (CC2) holds.  This gives a unital *-homomorphism
`A_H(S,T) -> A(Iso)`.

*Step 4: converse.*  In `A_H(S,T)` define `V` by (TC3).
- Row `(k,alpha)`: the entries are `p^k_g` for `g in T_k`, each once, so they
  sum to one.
- Column `(k,beta)`: `alpha |-> beta alpha^(-1)` is a bijection from
  `{alpha in R_k : beta alpha^(-1) in T_k}` onto `T_k`, because
  `g^(-1) beta in g^(-1) R'_k = R_k` for every `g in T_k`.  So the column sums
  to one.
- Colours.  If the arc colours of `((k,alpha),(k,alpha'))` and
  `((k,beta),(k,beta'))` differ, then `beta alpha^(-1) != beta' alpha'^(-1)`
  and the product of two distinct atoms of one PVM is `0`.  The same holds for
  a diagonal pair against an off-diagonal pair.  Across contexts, differing
  colours mean differing restrictions to `S_k ∩ S_l`, and (CC2) kills the
  product.

So `V` is a magic unitary respecting colours.  The two homomorphisms are
mutually inverse on generators by Step 2.  Finally `g R_k = R'_k` and
`h_k R_k = R'_k` give `h_k^(-1) g in K_k`, so `T_k = h_k K_k`.  QED.

**Coproduct.**  For `R' = R`, `T_k = K_k`, and the comultiplication
`Delta(v_(xz)) = sum_y v_(xy) (x) v_(yz)` of `O(Qut G_H(R))` becomes

```text
Delta(p^k_g) = sum_(h in K_k) p^k_h (x) p^k_(g h^(-1)).                 (TC4)
```

Indeed, with `x = (k,alpha_0)` and `z = (k, g alpha_0)`, the middle vertex
`y = (k, gamma)` contributes only when `h = gamma alpha_0^(-1) in K_k`, and then
`z` is reached from `y` by the shift `g h^(-1)`.

**Corollary G (not a group dual).**  Fix `k`.  The span `B_k` of
`{p^k_g : g in K_k}` is a finite-dimensional commutative Hopf *-subalgebra, by
(TC4), so `B_k ≅ C(Λ_k)` for a finite group `Λ_k`.
- The characters of `B_k` are the evaluations at `g in L_k = {g : p^k_g != 0}`.
- By (TC4), convolving the evaluations at `g` and `g'` gives the evaluation at
  `g' g`.  So `Λ_k ≅ L_k^op`.
- If `L_k` is nonabelian, then `B_k` is not cocommutative.  Every Hopf
  *-subalgebra of a group algebra `C Γ` is cocommutative.  So `Qut G_H(R)`
  is not a group dual.
- `L_k` contains the `k`-components of all classical automorphisms, i.e. of
  all `x in prod_k K_k` with agreeing restrictions on shared variables.  So a
  nonabelian set of classical `k`-components suffices.

Example: `H` nonabelian, two contexts `S_1 = {1}`, `S_2 = {2}`, `R_1 = R_2 = H`.
Then `O(Qut) = C(H) * C(H)`, Wang's free product, which is neither
commutative nor cocommutative.  It is Connes embeddable, so this example
only illustrates the dictionary.
