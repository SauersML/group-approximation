# One-singularity shell presentations give the two-point clauses, 2026-09-16

This artifact proves the claim
`shell-one-singularity-fp-forces-two-point-stabilizers-fp`. It is a
written proof, not a Lean formalization.

## 1. Statement

Let P be any countably infinite group and nu:P->N any bijection. Let
E=E_nu=<V,hat(P)> be the shell group of
`shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`, acting on
C={0,1}^N. Put p=0^infinity and q=1*0^infinity. For a homeomorphism f
of C, sing(f) is the set of points at which f agrees with no element
of V on any neighborhood. Put

    A_1  = {e in E: sing(e) subset {p}, e(p)=p},
    A_12 = {e in A_1: e(q)=q},
    A_2  = {e in E: sing(e) subset {p,q}, e(p)=p, e(q)=q}.

**Theorem.** If A_1 is finitely presented, then A_12 and A_2 are
finitely presented.

The converse direction of the three-clause statement is trivial, so
for a fixed enumeration the three clauses of
`decidable-inputs-have-fp-shell-stabilizers` are equivalent to the
single clause "A_1 is finitely presented".

External inputs, all ESTABLISHED claims of the graph:

* `contracting-srn-rational-stabilizers-are-fp`, applied to the trivial
  (contracting) self-similar group on the binary tree, so that B=V.
  It makes V_p=Fix_V({p}) and W=Fix_V({p,q}) finitely presented, since
  p and q are eventually periodic. (Belk--Hyde--Matucci, arXiv:2407.03149,
  Appendix A, independently gives type F_infinity for stabilizers of
  finite sets of rational points in V.)
* `thompson-v-finitely-presented-infinite-simple`: V is finitely
  presented (in particular finitely generated) and simple.
* `shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`, for the
  definition of E, the fact that hat(g) fixes p and is locally V away
  from p, and its identification of the isotropy germ group (E)_p as
  generated by the germs of hat(P) and (V)_p=<tau>. This is used only
  in (4.0) below, to see that every germ at p of an element of E fixing
  p is the germ of an element of A_1.

No structural property of the germ group Q is used, and no property of
P (finite generation, decidability) is used.

## 2. Notation and four standing facts

Words are finite binary strings, uC is the cone of u, and a prefix map
uz |-> u'z sends uC onto u'C. For a clopen X, V(X) is the subgroup of
elements of V equal to the identity on C minus X. For v in V and a
cone, "v is a prefix map on uC" means v(uz)=u'z for all z.

Let sigma in V be the swap 0z <-> 1z, so sigma(p)=q and sigma(q)=p.
Let s in V be

    s(0z)=00z,  s(10z)=01z,  s(11z)=1z.

It fixes p and, for all n>=0, s^n(1C) = C minus 0^(n+1)C.

**(F1) Local V everywhere means V.** A homeomorphism h of C that agrees
with an element of V near every point lies in V. Indeed each point has
a cone on which h is a prefix map. By compactness finitely many such
cones cover C, and then all cones wC with |w|=L cover C as a partition
for L at least the maximal cone length. h is a prefix map w z |-> w' z
on each. The images w'C are pairwise disjoint (h injective) and cover C
(h surjective), so the two lists of words are complete prefix codes and
h is the element of V they define.

For composition: if f agrees with v in V near x and g agrees with w in
V near f(x), then gf agrees with wv near x. Consequently
sing(gf) subset sing(f) union f^-1(sing(g)) and sing(f^-1)=f(sing(f)),
so A_1, A_12 and A_2 are subgroups.

**(F2) Interpolation.** If A and B are nonempty clopen sets, there is a
homeomorphism A->B that is a prefix map on each cone of some finite cone
partition of A. Write A and B as disjoint unions of a and b cones.
Splitting a cone uC into u0C and u1C raises the count by one, so both
counts can be made equal, and cones are then matched in order.

**(F3) Cone swaps generate V(X).** For disjoint cones aC, bC let (a b)
be the element of V exchanging az and bz and fixing everything else.
Let X be a nonempty clopen set. Then V(X) is isomorphic to V: choose a
homeomorphism phi:C->X as in (F2) and conjugate. Hence V(X) is simple.
The swaps (a b) with aC, bC inside X generate a subgroup of V(X) that
is nontrivial (X contains a cone uC, and (u0 u1) is nontrivial) and
normal: for v in V(X) choose L so large that v is a prefix map on every
cone auC and buC with |u|=L, say au z |-> c_u z and bu z |-> d_u z.
Then v (a b) v^-1 is the product of the commuting disjoint swaps
(c_u d_u). By simplicity these swaps generate V(X).

**(F4) Overlap lemma.** If X_1, X_2 are clopen and X_1 meets X_2, then

    V(X_1 union X_2) = <V(X_1), V(X_2)>.

By (F3) it suffices to write each swap (a b) with aC, bC inside
X_1 union X_2 in the right side. The three clopen sets X_1 minus X_2,
X_2 minus X_1, and X_1 intersect X_2 partition X_1 union X_2 and are
finite unions of cones. So for L large every cone auC and buC with
|u|=L lies in one of them, and (a b) is the product over |u|=L of the
commuting swaps (au bu). If auC and buC lie in the same X_i, that swap
lies in V(X_i); this includes every case where one of them lies in
X_1 intersect X_2. Otherwise, say auC is inside X_1 minus X_2 and buC
is inside X_2 minus X_1. Choose a cone eC inside X_1 intersect X_2. It
is disjoint from both, and

    (au bu) = (au e) (e bu) (au e),

with (au e) in V(X_1) and (e bu) in V(X_2).

## 3. Local copies of A_1

Put Q=germ_p(A_1), the group of germs at p of elements of A_1.

**Lemma 3.1 (characterization).** Let f be a homeomorphism of C with
f(p)=p, locally V at every x!=p, and germ_p(f) in Q. Then f is in A_1.

Proof. Choose e in A_1 with the same germ at p. Then e^-1 f is the
identity near p. At x!=p, f is locally V and f(x)!=p, where e^-1 is
locally V; so e^-1 f is locally V at x (F1). By (F1), e^-1 f is in V,
so f=e(e^-1 f) lies in E, fixes p and has sing(f) subset {p}. QED.

Define G_p = {e in A_1: e=id on 1C}. Every element of G_p preserves 0C.

**Lemma 3.2.** The map f |-> f^(0), where

    f^(0)(0z)=0 f(z),   f^(0)(1z)=1z,

is an isomorphism A_1 -> G_p. It restricts to an isomorphism
V_p -> V_p(0C) := V_p intersect V(0C) and to an isomorphism
G_p -> G_00 := {e in A_1: e=id on C minus 00C}.

Proof. Let f be in A_1. Then f^(0) is a homeomorphism fixing p and
equal to the identity on 1C. At 0z with z!=p, f agrees near z with a
prefix map uw |-> u'w, so f^(0) agrees near 0z with 0uw |-> 0u'w; at
points of 1C it is the identity. Near p it agrees with s f s^-1: for k
at least 2 and 0^k y near p, s^-1(0^k y)=0^(k-1)y, whose image y'' under
f lies in 0C, and s(y'')=0y''=f^(0)(0^k y). Since s is in V_p, which
is contained in A_1, s f s^-1 is in A_1, so germ_p(f^(0)) is in Q and
Lemma 3.1 puts f^(0) in G_p. The map is a homomorphism because
conjugation by the embedding z |-> 0z respects composition, and it is
injective.

Conversely let g be in G_p. Put f(z)=w where g(0z)=0w. This is a
homeomorphism of C fixing p. If g agrees near 0z (z!=p) with a prefix
map 0uw |-> u'w, then u' begins with 0 because g preserves 0C, so f is
locally V at z. Near p, f agrees with s^-1 g s, which lies in A_1.
By Lemma 3.1, f is in A_1, and f^(0)=g. So the map is onto.

If f is in V_p, then f^(0) is locally V everywhere, hence in V by
(F1), so f^(0) is in V_p(0C); conversely the inverse construction sends
V_p(0C) into V_p by the same argument. Finally f is the identity on 1C
exactly when f^(0) is the identity on 01C union 1C, i.e. on C minus
00C. QED.

Consequently G_p and G_00 are isomorphic to A_1, and V_p(0C) is
isomorphic to V_p. Under the hypothesis of the Theorem, G_p is finitely
presented and G_00 finitely generated; V_p(0C) is finitely generated.

Transport by sigma. Put

    G_q  = sigma G_p sigma^-1  = {e in E: sing(e) subset {q}, e(q)=q, e=id on 0C},
    G_10 = sigma G_00 sigma^-1 = {e in G_q: e=id on C minus 10C},
    V_q(1C) = sigma V_p(0C) sigma^-1 = Fix_V(q) intersect V(1C).

The displayed descriptions hold because conjugation by sigma in V
preserves "locally V", sends p to q and exchanges 0C and 1C. So G_q is
finitely presented, G_10 finitely generated and V_q(1C) finitely
generated. All of G_p, G_q, G_00, G_10 lie in A_2, and G_p, G_00 lie in
A_12. Also V_p(0C) and V_q(1C) lie in W=Fix_V({p,q}).

The shifts. Put s_p=s^(0) in V_p(0C) and s_q=sigma s_p sigma^-1 in
V_q(1C). Then s_p(0^k z)=0^(k+1)z for k>=2, and s_q(10^k z)=10^(k+1)z
for k>=1. Every v in Fix_V(p) is, near p, a prefix map 0^a z |-> u z with
u 0^infinity = 0^infinity, so u=0^b and germ_p(v)=germ_p(s_p^(b-a)).
Likewise at q with s_q. The germ of s_q^j at q is nontrivial for j!=0,
since s_q^j moves the points 10^k 1 z for large k.

## 4. Kernels and generation

Put Y = 01C union 11C and

    K_2   = {v in V: v=id on a neighborhood of {p,q}},
    K(0C) = {v in V(0C): v=id near p},   K(1C) = {v in V(1C): v=id near q}.

K_2 is normal in W and in A_2 and A_12 (it is the kernel of the joint
germ map at p and q; see below). K(0C) is contained in V_p(0C) and
K(1C) in V_q(1C); V(Y), K(0C), K(1C) are contained in K_2.

**Lemma 4.1 (kernels).** (a) The map A_2 -> germ_p times germ_q has
kernel K_2; so does its restriction to A_12. (b) The germ map
G_p -> Q has kernel K(0C); the germ map G_q -> germ_q(G_q) has kernel
K(1C).

Proof. An element of A_2 with trivial germs at p and q is locally V at
p, at q and elsewhere, hence in V (F1), and it fixes p, q and is the
identity near both. An element of G_p with trivial germ at p is in V by
(F1), is supported in 0C and is the identity near p. Apply sigma for
G_q. QED.

**Lemma 4.2 (generation).**
(a) A_2 = G_p G_q K_2, so A_2 = <G_p, G_q, W>.
(b) A_12 = G_p <s_q> K_2, so A_12 = <G_p, W>.
(c) W = V_p(0C) V_q(1C) K_2, and V_q(1C) = <s_q> K(1C).

We use one input from the shell claim here. The isotropy germ group
(E)_p (germs at p of all elements of E fixing p) is generated by the
germs at p of the elements hat(g), g in P, and by (V)_p=<tau>, where
tau=germ_p(s) (every v in Fix_V(p) has the germ of a power of s, as in
Section 3). All of these generators have representatives hat(g) and s
in A_1. Hence

    Q = germ_p(A_1) = (E)_p.                                  (4.0)

Proof of Lemma 4.2. (a) Let e be in A_2. Its germ at p lies in (E)_p=Q.
The germ map G_p -> Q is onto: by the proof of Lemma 3.2,
germ_p(f^(0)) = tau germ_p(f) tau^-1, and this runs over all of Q as f
runs over A_1. Choose g_p in G_p with germ_p(g_p)=germ_p(e). Next,
sigma^-1 e sigma lies in E and fixes p, so its germ at p lies in Q;
choose g in G_p with that germ and put g_q = sigma g sigma^-1 in G_q.
Then germ_q(g_q)=germ_q(e). Since g_q=id on 0C, the product g_p g_q
agrees with g_p near p. Since g_p=id on 1C and g_q maps a small
neighborhood of q into 1C, the product agrees with g_q near q. So
(g_p g_q)^-1 e lies in K_2 by Lemma 4.1(a). As K_2 is contained in W,
A_2=<G_p,G_q,W>; the reverse inclusion is clear.

(b) Let e be in A_12. Choose g_p in G_p with the germ of e at p. Then
g_p^-1 e is in A_12, equals the identity near p, and is locally V
everywhere else (including q, because sing(e) subset {p}). By (F1) it
lies in Fix_V({p,q})=W. Its germ at q is that of some s_q^j (Section 3),
and s_q^j is the identity on 0C. So s_q^-j g_p^-1 e is in V, fixes p and
q, is the identity near q and near p, hence lies in K_2. Therefore
A_12 = G_p <s_q> K_2 and A_12=<G_p,W>.

(c) Let w be in W. Its germ at p is that of some s_p^i and its germ at q
that of some s_q^j (Section 3). The element s_p^i s_q^j agrees with s_p^i
near p (s_q is supported in 1C) and with s_q^j near q (s_p is supported
in 0C). So (s_p^i s_q^j)^-1 w is in K_2. The same argument inside V(1C)
gives V_q(1C)=<s_q>K(1C). QED.

**Lemma 4.3 (generators of K_2).** K_2 = <K(0C), K(1C), V(Y)>.

Proof. For m>=1 put X_1(m)=0C minus 0^(m+1)C and X_2(m)=1C minus 10^m C.
Every v in K_2 is the identity on some neighborhood 0^(m+1)C union
10^m C of {p,q}, so K_2 is the increasing union of the groups
V(X_1(m) union X_2(m)). Now X_1(m) contains 01C and X_2(m) contains
11C. By (F4), since X_1(m) meets Y in 01C,

    V(X_1(m) union 11C) = <V(X_1(m)), V(Y)>,

and since X_1(m) union 11C meets X_2(m) in 11C,

    V(X_1(m) union X_2(m)) = <V(X_1(m)), V(Y), V(X_2(m))>.

Finally V(X_1(m)) is contained in K(0C) and V(X_2(m)) in K(1C). QED.

**Lemma 4.4 (absorbing a shell element into K(0C)).** For every g in
G_p there are h in K(0C) and u in G_00 with g=hu. For every g in G_q
there are h in K(1C) and u in G_10 with g=hu.

Proof. Let g be in G_p and Z=g(01C). Z is a clopen subset of 0C that
does not contain p (g fixes p and 01C does not contain p). Choose m>=2
so large that 0^m C is disjoint from Z. Put X=0C minus 0^(m+1)C. Then
01C and Z are contained in X, and both X minus 01C and X minus Z contain
0^m 1C, so they are nonempty clopen sets. Define h to be g on 01C, a
homeomorphism X minus 01C -> X minus Z from (F2) on X minus 01C, and the
identity on C minus X. Then h is a bijection of C that is locally V
everywhere (g is locally V on the compact set 01C, which avoids p), so
h is in V by (F1). It is the identity on 1C and on 0^(m+1)C, so h lies
in K(0C). The element u=h^-1 g lies in A_1, is the identity on 1C, and
on 01C it is h^-1 g = id. Hence u is supported in 00C, i.e. u is in G_00.
Conjugate by sigma for G_q. QED.

## 5. A finite presentation of A_2

Assume A_1 is finitely presented. Fix the following finite data.

* A finite presentation <S_p | R_p> of G_p (Lemma 3.2), and its sigma
  transport <S_q | R_q> of G_q.
* A finite presentation <S_W | R_W> of W
  (`contracting-srn-rational-stabilizers-are-fp`).
* A finite generating set T_p of V_p(0C) (Lemma 3.2 and finite
  generation of V_p), with, for each t in T_p, a word alpha_t in S_p and
  a word beta_t in S_W both representing t. Its sigma transport T_q of
  V_q(1C) with words alpha'_t in S_q and beta'_t in S_W.
* A finite generating set U_00 of G_00 (Lemma 3.2), each u written as a
  word mu_u in S_p. Its transport U_10 of G_10 with words mu'_u in S_q.
* A finite generating set Z_Y of V(Y), isomorphic to V by (F3), each
  z written as a word zeta_z in S_W (V(Y) is contained in W).

Let Gamma be the group with generators S_p, S_q, S_W (disjoint
alphabets) and the finitely many relators

    (R1) R_p, R_q, R_W;
    (R2) alpha_t = beta_t  (t in T_p),   alpha'_t = beta'_t  (t in T_q);
    (R3) [a, b] = 1        (a in S_p, b in S_q);
    (R4) [mu_u, zeta_z] = 1   (u in U_00, z in Z_Y);
    (R5) [mu'_u, zeta_z] = 1  (u in U_10, z in Z_Y).

All relators hold in A_2: (R1) and (R2) by construction, (R3) because
G_p and G_q have disjoint supports 0C and 1C, (R4) because G_00 is
supported in 00C and V(Y) in Y, (R5) because G_10 is supported in 10C.
So there is a homomorphism pi: Gamma -> A_2, onto by Lemma 4.2(a).

Subgroups of Gamma. Let G_p*, G_q*, W* be the subgroups generated by
S_p, S_q, S_W. By (R1) there are onto homomorphisms G_p -> G_p*,
G_q -> G_q*, W -> W*; composed with pi they are the inclusions into
A_2, hence injective. So these maps are isomorphisms; write x* for the
image of x. By (R2), for x in V_p(0C) the two images of x via G_p and
via W coincide, since the two homomorphisms V_p(0C) -> Gamma agree on
the generating set T_p; likewise for V_q(1C). By (R3), G_p* and G_q*
commute. By (R4), (R5), the images of G_00 and G_10 (inside G_p* and
G_q*) commute with the image of V(Y) (inside W*).

Let K_2* be the image of K_2 in W*. Then pi restricts to an isomorphism
K_2* -> K_2.

**Lemma 5.1.** K_2* is normal in Gamma.

Proof. By Lemma 4.3, K_2* is generated by the images of K(0C), K(1C)
and V(Y). It suffices to show x K_2* x^-1 is contained in K_2* for x in
G_p* union G_q* union W*, since these subgroups generate Gamma and are
closed under inverses. For x in W* this holds because K_2 is normal in W.
Let g be in G_p.

(i) k in K(1C). As K(1C) is contained in V_q(1C), k* lies in G_q* by
(R2), so g* k* g*^-1 = k* by (R3).

(ii) k in K(0C). As K(0C) is contained in V_p(0C), k* lies in G_p*, and
g* k* g*^-1 = (g k g^-1)*. The element g k g^-1 lies in G_p, is the
identity near p (k is), hence is in V by (F1) and lies in K(0C). Its
image lies in K_2*.

(iii) y in V(Y). By Lemma 4.4 write g=hu with h in K(0C) and u in G_00.
In G_p* this gives g*=h* u*. By (R4), u* commutes with y*. So

    g* y* g*^-1 = h* y* h*^-1 = (h y h^-1)*,

computed in W*, because h* is also the W*-image of h by (R2). As h is
in W and K_2 is normal in W, this is in K_2*.

The case g in G_q is the sigma-symmetric argument, using (R3) and (R2)
for K(0C), (R2) for K(1C), and Lemma 4.4 with (R5) for V(Y). QED.

**Lemma 5.2.** The induced map pibar: Gamma/K_2* -> A_2/K_2 is an
isomorphism.

Proof. The quotient Gamma/K_2* is generated by the images of G_p*,
G_q*, W*. By Lemma 4.2(c) and (R2), the image of W* is contained in the
subgroup generated by the images of G_p* and G_q*. The map
G_p -> Gamma/K_2* kills K(0C) (its image is K(0C)* in K_2*), which is
the kernel of germ_p on G_p by Lemma 4.1(b); so it factors through
Q_p := germ_p(G_p)=Q. Likewise G_q -> Gamma/K_2* factors through
Q_q := germ_q(G_q). These images commute by (R3). Hence

    psi: Q_p x Q_q -> Gamma/K_2*,   (a, b) |-> [g_a*][g_b*],

with g_a in G_p of germ a and g_b in G_q of germ b, is a well-defined
onto homomorphism. Compose with pibar and with the map
A_2/K_2 -> Q_p x Q_q induced by the joint germ map (injective by
Lemma 4.1(a)). The element g_a g_b has germ a at p and germ b at q, as
in the proof of Lemma 4.2(a). So the composite is the identity of
Q_p x Q_q. Therefore psi is injective, hence an isomorphism, and pibar
is an isomorphism. QED.

**Conclusion for A_2.** Let x be in the kernel of pi. By Lemma 5.2, x
lies in K_2*. Since pi is injective on K_2*, x=1. So pi is an
isomorphism and A_2 has the finite presentation Gamma.

## 6. A finite presentation of A_12

Keep the data of Section 5 except S_q, R_q, U_10. Let Gamma_12 have
generators S_p, S_W and the finitely many relators

    (R1') R_p, R_W;
    (R2') alpha_t = beta_t          (t in T_p);
    (R3') [a, beta'_t] = 1          (a in S_p, t in T_q);
    (R4') [mu_u, zeta_z] = 1        (u in U_00, z in Z_Y).

They hold in A_12; (R3') because V_q(1C) is supported in 1C where G_p
is the identity. So pi_12: Gamma_12 -> A_12 exists and is onto by Lemma
4.2(b). As before G_p -> G_p* and W -> W* are isomorphisms, the two
images of V_p(0C) coincide, G_p* commutes with the image of V_q(1C)
(inside W*), and the image of G_00 commutes with the image of V(Y). Let
K_2* be the image of K_2 in W*; pi_12 is injective on it.

**Lemma 6.1.** K_2* is normal in Gamma_12.

Proof. The generators are the images of K(0C), K(1C), V(Y) (Lemma 4.3),
and Gamma_12 is generated by G_p* and W*. W* normalizes K_2*. For g in
G_p: (i) for k in K(1C), k* lies in the image of V_q(1C), which
commutes with g* by (R3'); (ii) and (iii) are verbatim as in Lemma 5.1,
using (R2') and (R4'). QED.

**Lemma 6.2.** Gamma_12/K_2* -> A_12/K_2 is an isomorphism.

Proof. By Lemma 4.2(c), W = V_p(0C) <s_q> K(1C) K_2 and K(1C) is inside
K_2, so the image of W* in Gamma_12/K_2* lies in the subgroup generated
by the image of G_p* and the class c=[s_q*]. By (R3'), c commutes with
the image of G_p*. The map G_p -> Gamma_12/K_2* factors through
Q=germ_p(G_p) as in Lemma 5.2. Hence

    psi_12: Q x Z -> Gamma_12/K_2*,   (a, j) |-> [g_a*] c^j,

is a well-defined onto homomorphism. The joint germ map at p and q
induces an injection A_12/K_2 -> Q x germ_q(W) by Lemma 4.1(a). The
element g_a s_q^j has germ a at p and germ germ_q(s_q^j) at q, and
germ_q(s_q^j) is nontrivial for j!=0 (Section 3). So the composite
Q x Z -> Q x germ_q(W), (a,j) |-> (a, germ_q(s_q)^j), is injective.
Therefore psi_12 is injective, hence an isomorphism, and the induced
quotient map is an isomorphism. QED.

**Conclusion for A_12.** As in Section 5, pi_12 is injective, so A_12 is
finitely presented. This proves the Theorem.

## 7. Consequences and limits

1. For every fixed enumeration nu, the three clauses of
   `decidable-inputs-have-fp-shell-stabilizers` are equivalent to the
   single clause "A_1 is finitely presented". The known chain becomes

        three clauses FP <==> A_1 FP ==> Q FP <==> F_nu FP ==> R_nu FP,

   using `shell-fp-stabilizer-forces-fp-germ-group` and
   `shell-germ-group-has-index-two-fiber-product` for the later arrows.
   Here F_nu=R_nu x_Z R_nu is the fiber product of that claim, and the
   last arrow F_nu FP ==> R_nu FP is asserted there only for finitely
   generated P (which holds for the decidable finitely presented inputs
   of the target). The Theorem itself needs no hypothesis on P.

2. Every negative control that kills A_1 (square-spiral Z^2, or any
