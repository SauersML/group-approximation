## (b) Option A (+22 lines): conjugacy of root transvections sees the dimension group

```latex
Conjugacy in $G_X$ sees the dimension group of $(X,T)$~\cite{GPS}.

\begin{corollary}
For clopen $A,B\subseteq X$, the elements $e_{13}(1_A)$ and $e_{13}(1_B)$
of $G_X$ are conjugate if and only if $1_A-1_B=g\circ T-g$ for some
$g\in C(X,\Z)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $1_A-1_B=g\circ T-g$. Take the towers of first return to a nonempty
clopen set on which $g$ is constant, refined so that each level lies in
$A$ or its complement, and in $B$ or its complement. Along each tower
$1_A-1_B$ sums to $0$, so a permutation of levels gives $\gamma$ with
$\gamma(A)=B$ and $\gamma=T^k$ on clopen sets $U_k$. Then
$w=\sum_k1_{\gamma U_k}u^k$ is a unit with $w1_Aw^{-1}=1_B$. By the first
identity at the end of Section~2, $k=\operatorname{diag}(w,w^{-1},1)
\operatorname{diag}(1,w^{-1},w)$ lies in $G_X$, and
$ke_{13}(1_A)k^{-1}=e_{13}(1_B)$. Conversely, let $k$ conjugate them. If
$x_{[a,a+L)}=x_{[b,b+L)}$ with $a<b$ and $L$ large, all words of length
$L$ of the periodic word with period $x_{[a,b)}$ are words of $X$, and
the matrices $D(f)$ and $P$ of Section~1 give a ring map, defined on the
entries involved, in which $k$ conjugates $I+E_{13}D(1_A)$ to
$I+E_{13}D(1_B)$. Comparing ranks, $1_A-1_B$ sums to $0$ over
$x_{[a,b)}$. For $x$ with dense forward orbit,
$\sum_{i<m}(1_A-1_B)(T^ix)$ therefore depends only on $x_{[m-\ell,m+\ell]}$,
which defines $g$.
\end{proof}
```

## (c) Option B (Option A + 20 lines): the conjugacy problem can be the jump

```latex
\begin{corollary}
For every Turing degree $d$ there is $X$ such that the word problem of
$G_X$ has degree $d$ and its conjugacy problem has degree $d'$. In
particular some $G_X$ has solvable word problem and unsolvable conjugacy
problem.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Searching for a conjugator, $L(X)'$ computes the conjugacy problem. Fix
$S\subseteq\mathbb N$ and oracle machines $M_e$. Over growing alphabets
$\Sigma_m=\{s,t,w\}\cup\{x_e,y_e:e<m,\ M_e^S\text{ runs }m-1\text{ steps}\}$
let $\tau_m(c)=s^{2+\chi_S(m)}\pi_m(c)W_mt$, where $W_m=wst\cdots$ lists
$\Sigma_m$ once, and $\pi_m(c)$ are distinct words of equal length
without $s$, with $\pi_m(x_e)=x_ew^{p}$ and $\pi_m(y_e)=y_ew^{p}$ while $e$
runs and no letter $x_e,y_e$ once it has halted. Let $X$ be generated by
the blocks $\tau_0\cdots\tau_{m-1}(c)$. Runs $ss$ mark block boundaries,
so blocks are recognizable; $W_m$ makes $X$ minimal, and $L(X)\equiv_TS$.
Let $A_e$ and $B_e$ be the sets where a block of $x_e$, respectively $y_e$,
starts at $0$. If $M_e^S$ halts, all later blocks contain as many blocks of
$x_e$ as of $y_e$, and counting within blocks gives $g$. If not, a block
of $x_e$ has one more, while $g$ of small radius is constant at block
boundaries, which all look alike. So $e\in S'$ if and only if
$e_{13}(1_{A_e})$ and $e_{13}(1_{B_e})$ are conjugate.
\end{proof}
```

## (e) Reader-facing trade-off
- Option A tells the reader that conjugacy classes in G_X carry the Giordano–Putnam–Skau invariant. It adds a new fact with no new machinery, and it bears on the rigidity Question (l.417–420).
- Option B separates the conjugacy problem from the word problem inside simple Kazhdan LEF groups. It is the strongest computability statement in §3, but it costs a construction the reader must check.
- Main's call under the page budget. My recommendation: A plus the corollary statement of B, with B's proof kept to the 20 lines above.
