# Elementary groups over Z²-SFT crossed products: marked isolation and what soficity still needs

Lane `sk-fp-sofic-b`, 2026-09-13. Written proofs, UNREVIEWED (review requested from sk-verify-3).

## 0. Question and verdict

**Question.** Let `Ω ⊆ A^(Z^2)` be a free minimal subshift of finite type (e.g. Labbé's 19-tile Wang shift,
`minimal-aperiodic-wang-shift-exists`), `q` a prime power, `n >= 3`, and
`S_n(Ω) = EL_n(LC(Ω,F_q) ⋊ Z^2)/Z`. Is `S_n(Ω)` sofic or hyperlinear? On main this is the open claim
`free-minimal-subshift-elementary-groups-are-sofic`.

**Verdict.** Undecided. New here:
- **Theorem M.** For every finitely generated group `Γ`, the map sending a subshift `Y ⊆ A^Γ` to
  `EL_n(LC(Y,F_q) ⋊ Γ)` is a topological embedding of the space of subshifts into the space of marked
  groups, with explicit moduli in both directions. The marking is the standard elementary one, and the same
  holds for the quotient by constant scalars.
- **Corollary I.** A minimal subshift is an isolated point of the space of subshifts iff it is of finite type.
  So for a free minimal `Z^d`-SFT `Ω`, groups over other subshifts never converge to `S_n(Ω)`.
- **Corollary P.** The note's finite models are marked approximations by the residually finite groups over
  finite subshifts. They exist iff `X` is a limit of finite subshifts: always for minimal `Z`-subshifts,
  never for infinite minimal SFTs.
- **Proposition F.** Suppose a Kazhdan group acts on a countable set with Følner sets on which each nontrivial
  element moves a positive proportion of points. Then the group is residually finite.

Combine these with the firewalls already on main (§1). A sofic approximation of `S_n(Ω)` that is not a local
embedding must:
- take values in permutation groups without a uniform fixed-point gap;
- not come from a genuine amenable action;
- not pass through groups over other subshifts.

## 2. Theorem M

**Setting.**
- `Γ` is a finitely generated group with a finite symmetric generating set `Σ`, word length `|γ|` and balls
  `B(r) = {γ : |γ| <= r}`.
- `A` is a finite alphabet, and `Γ` acts on `A^Γ` by `(γ·y)(δ) = y(γ^(-1)δ)`.
- A *subshift* is a nonempty closed invariant `Y ⊆ A^Γ`. `L_F(Y) = {y|_F : y ∈ Y}` for finite `F ⊆ Γ`.
- The space `𝒮` of subshifts has the topology in which `Y_k → Y` iff for every finite `F`,
  `L_F(Y_k) = L_F(Y)` for all large `k`. It is compact and Hausdorff.
- `R_Y = LC(Y,F_q) ⋊ Γ = ⊕_γ LC(Y,F_q) u_γ`, with `u_γ f u_γ^(-1) = γ·f` and `(γ·f)(y) = f(γ^(-1)y)`.
- `χ_a(y) = [y(e) = a]`. Then `u_δ χ_a u_δ^(-1) = [y(δ) = a]`, and `χ_[p] = ∏_(δ ∈ F) u_δ χ_(p(δ)) u_δ^(-1)`
  for `p ∈ A^F`.
- `n >= 3`, `G_Y = EL_n(R_Y)`, `C = {c·1_n : c ∈ F_q^x}`, and `P_Y = G_Y/(G_Y ∩ C)`.
- Letters: `𝓛 = {(i,j,s) : i ≠ j, s ∈ S_0}` with `S_0 = {u_σ : σ ∈ Σ} ∪ {χ_a : a ∈ A} ∪ {c_0}`, where `c_0`
  generates `F_q` over `F_p` (omit `c_0` when `q = p`). The marking is `σ_Y(i,j,s) = e_ij(s)`.
  - The letters generate `G_Y`. The subring generated by `S_0` contains `1 = Σ_a χ_a`, every scalar, every
    `u_γ` and every cylinder indicator, so it is `R_Y`.
  - The elementary identities `e_ij(r)e_ij(s) = e_ij(r+s)` and `[e_ik(r), e_kj(s)] = e_ij(rs)`, with
    `[g,h] = ghg^(-1)h^(-1)` and `i,j,k` distinct, then give every `e_ij(r)`.
- `N_Y` and `N'_Y` are the kernels of the free group `F(𝓛)` onto `G_Y` and onto `P_Y`.

**Theorem M.**
1. **Cylinder words.** Fix a finite `F ⊆ B(ρ)` and `p ∈ A^F`. There is a word `W_p ∈ F(𝓛)`, independent of `Y`,
   with `W_p(σ_Y) = e_12(χ_[p])` and `|W_p| <= 3·2^(m-1)`, where `m = |F|(2ρ+1)`. Moreover `W_p ∈ N_Y` iff
   `W_p ∈ N'_Y` iff `p ∉ L_F(Y)`.
2. **Window bound.** For every `r`, the sets `N_Y ∩ B_(F(𝓛))(r)` and `N'_Y ∩ B_(F(𝓛))(r)` depend only on
   `L_(B(r))(Y)`.
3. **Embedding.** `Y ↦ (G_Y, σ_Y)` and `Y ↦ (P_Y, σ_Y)` are homeomorphisms of `𝒮` onto closed subsets of the
   space of `𝓛`-marked groups.
4. **Order.** `Y ⊆ Y'` iff there is a marked epimorphism `G_(Y') → G_Y`, and likewise for `P`.

**Proof.**
- **(1)** Write each `u_δ`, `|δ| <= ρ`, as a product of `|δ|` letters `u_σ`. Then `χ_[p]` is a product
  `s_1 ⋯ s_m'` of elements of `S_0` with `m' <= m`. Put `W(s) = (1,2,s)` for one letter, and
  `e_ij(s_1 s_2 ⋯ s_l) = [e_ik(s_1), e_kj(s_2 ⋯ s_l)]` with `{i,j,k} = {1,2,3}`. The recursion
  `ℓ(1) = 1`, `ℓ(l) = 2 + 2ℓ(l-1)` gives length `3·2^(l-1) - 2`.
  - If `e_12(r) = c·1_n`, the diagonal entries force `c = 1` (since `Y ≠ ∅`), and then `r = 0`.
  - `χ_[p] = 0` in `LC(Y,F_q)` iff `[p] ∩ Y = ∅` iff `p ∉ L_F(Y)`.
- **(2)**
  - Let `𝔉 = R_(A^Γ)`. Restriction `LC(A^Γ) → LC(Y)` is equivariant and onto. So it gives a ring
    epimorphism `𝔉 → R_Y` sending letters to letters, with kernel `{Σ f_γ u_γ : f_γ|_Y = 0 for all γ}`.
  - For a word `w` of length `<= r`, each entry of `w(σ_(A^Γ))` is an `F_q`-combination of products of at most
    `r` elements of `S_0 ∪ {1}`.
  - Moving all `u`'s to the right turns such a product into `f u_γ` with `|γ| <= r`. Here `f` is a product of
    translates `u_δ χ_a u_δ^(-1)`, `|δ| <= r`, so `f` depends only on `y|_(B(r))`.
  - So `w(σ_(A^Γ)) - c·1_n` has entries `Σ_(|γ|<=r) f_γ u_γ` with every `f_γ` a function of the
    `B(r)`-pattern. Then `w ∈ N_Y` iff all these `f_γ` (with `c = 1`) vanish on `L_(B(r))(Y)`. And
    `w ∈ N'_Y` iff the same holds for some `c ∈ F_q^x`.
- **(3)**
  - Injectivity: `L(Y)` determines `Y`, so (1) gives it.
  - Continuity: (2).
  - Continuity of the inverse: by (1), agreement of relations through length `3·2^(m-1)` forces
    `L_F(Y_k) = L_F(Y)`.
  - Closed image: `𝒮` is compact and the marked space is Hausdorff.
- **(4)**
  - If `Y ⊆ Y'`, restriction `R_(Y') → R_Y` is onto and sends letters to letters and constant scalars to
    constant scalars.
  - Conversely, a marked epimorphism gives `N_(Y') ⊆ N_Y`. By (1), every `p ∉ L(Y')` also lies outside
    `L(Y)`, so `Y ⊆ Y'`. ∎

## 3. Isolation and the note's finite models

**Corollary I.** Let `X ∈ 𝒮` be minimal. Then `X` is isolated in `𝒮` iff `X` is of finite type. Hence
`(G_X,σ_X)` and `(P_X,σ_X)` are isolated points of the images in Theorem M(3) iff `X` is of finite type.

**Proof.**
- **Finite type ⇒ isolated.** Suppose `X` is cut out by forbidden patterns on a finite window `K` and
  `L_K(Y) = L_K(X)`. Every point of `Y` avoids the forbidden patterns, so `Y ⊆ X`, and `Y = X` by minimality.
- **Not finite type ⇒ not isolated.** For finite `F`, put `X^(F) = {y : (γ·y)|_F ∈ L_F(X) for all γ}`.
  - It is a subshift containing `X`, with `L_F(X^(F)) = L_F(X)`.
  - `X^(F) ≠ X`, since otherwise `X` would be of finite type.
  - So `X^(F) → X` through subshifts different from `X`.
- The group statement follows from Theorem M(3). ∎

**Corollary P.** Let `X` be infinite and minimal.
1. `(G_X,σ_X)` is a marked limit of `(G_(Y_k),σ)` with each `Y_k` a finite subshift iff `X` is a limit of
   finite subshifts in `𝒮`.
2. For `Γ = Z^d` and finite `Y`, `G_Y` is residually finite.
3. For `Γ = Z`, every minimal `X` is a limit of finite subshifts. The note's periodic word `y`, which has the
   same words of length `2k+1` as `X`, gives the finite subshift `Y_k` = orbit of `y`, with
   `L_([-k,k])(Y_k) = L_([-k,k])(X)`.
4. If `X` is an infinite minimal SFT over any `Γ`, no sequence `Y_k ≠ X` converges to `X`. So for a free minimal
   `Z^2`-SFT `Ω`, neither `G_Ω` nor `S_n(Ω) = P_Ω` is a marked limit of groups over other subshifts, finite or
   not. Here `Z(G_Ω) ⊆ F_q^x 1_n` by `free-minimal-subshift-elementary-groups-are-simple-kazhdan`, so
   `S_n(Ω) = P_Ω`.

**Proof.**
- 1 and 4 are Theorem M(3) and Corollary I. 3 is the construction in the note (disk l.123-129).
- **For 2.**
  - A finite transitive `Γ`-set `Γ/H` gives `LC(Γ/H,F_q) ⋊ Γ ≅ M_([Γ:H])(F_q[H])`, the Steinberg algebra of a
    groupoid equivalent to `H`. For `Γ = Z^d`, `H ≅ Z^d`.
  - So `G_Y ⊆ ∏_O GL_(n|O|)(F_q[t_1^(±1),...,t_d^(±1)])`, a finitely generated linear group over a commutative
    domain, and it is residually finite by Mal'cev's theorem. ∎

**Reading for the note.** For `d = 1` the periodic words are exactly approximations of `X` by finite
subshifts, and Theorem M turns them into marked approximations of `G_X` by residually finite groups. For an
infinite minimal SFT over `Z^2`, the whole family of subshift groups is discrete around `G_Ω`, so a finite
model of `G_Ω` cannot be a subshift group. Any LEF model must be a genuinely non-commutative finite ring
model: periodic quantum tilings (`matricial-aperiodic-sft-rings-are-not-quantum-rigid`).

## 4. Proposition F (the geometric sofic certificate is dead for Kazhdan groups)

**Proposition F.** Let `G` be a countable group with property (T), acting on a countable set `Z`. Suppose there
are finite `F_k ⊆ Z` with `|gF_k Δ F_k|/|F_k| → 0` for every `g`. Let `Z_fin` be the union of the finite orbits.
- Then `|F_k \ Z_fin|/|F_k| → 0`.
- If moreover `liminf_k |{z ∈ F_k : gz ≠ z}|/|F_k| > 0` for every `g ≠ e`, then `G` acts faithfully on `Z_fin`.
  So `G` is residually finite, and an infinite simple Kazhdan group admits no such action.

**Proof.**
- Let `(K,ε)` be a Kazhdan pair and `P` the projection of `ℓ²(Z)` onto the invariant vectors. For a unit
  vector `ξ`, `||ξ - Pξ|| <= ε^(-1) max_(s∈K) ||sξ - ξ||`. Apply this to the component of `ξ` orthogonal to the
  invariant vectors, which has no invariant vectors.
- Take `ξ_k = |F_k|^(-1/2) 1_(F_k)`. Then `||sξ_k - ξ_k||² = |sF_k Δ F_k|/|F_k| → 0`.
- An invariant vector in `ℓ²(Z)` is constant on orbits and square-summable, so it vanishes off `Z_fin`.
- Hence `|F_k \ Z_fin|/|F_k| = ||1_(Z∖Z_fin) ξ_k||² <= ||ξ_k - Pξ_k||² → 0`.
- **Second statement.** For `g ≠ e`, the proportion of `F_k` moved by `g` stays bounded below, and most of
  `F_k` lies in `Z_fin`. So `g` moves a point of some finite orbit `O`, and `G → Sym(O)` does not kill `g`. ∎

This is the countable-set version of `kazhdan-subgroups-of-amenable-orbit-full-groups-are-rf`, and it is
folklore-level. It removes "soficity from an amenable locally free action" for `S_n(Ω)`.

## 5. What remains open, and where a resolution could come from

- **LEF branch.** If `LC(Ω,F_q) ⋊ Z^2` is exactly matricial (`labbe-wang-shift-crossed-product-is-exactly-matricial`
  for Labbé's shift), then `S_n(Ω)` is LEF, hence sofic. Periodic quantum tilings at every scale are necessary,
  and by Corollary I they are the only kind of finite pattern model left.
- **Rigid branch.**
  - If the ring is finitely presented (quantum rigid), then `S_n(Ω)` is not LEF.
  - A sofic approximation would then be a new principle, and the first sofic non-LEF infinite simple Kazhdan
    group on main. Its images would lie in permutation groups with no fixed-point gap and, by Proposition F,
    would not be genuine amenable actions.
  - A negative answer would answer Arzhantseva–Păunescu Question 8.5 over `F_q` with a simple Kazhdan witness.
- **Measure-theoretic action (Attempts on main): essential freeness confirmed.**
  - For `g ≠ 1` and a point `x`, the fixed set in the fibre `(F_q^n)^(Z^2)` is `ker(π_x(g) - 1)`, a closed
    subgroup.
  - A nonzero coefficient function of `g - 1` is nonzero on a clopen set, which the orbit of `x` visits
    syndetically. So `π_x(g) - 1` has nonzero columns at infinitely many sites, with far-apart supports.
  - Its image is therefore infinite, so the kernel has infinite index and is Haar-null.
  - Soficity of the orbit relation would give soficity of `S_n(Ω)`, but no argument for that is known.
