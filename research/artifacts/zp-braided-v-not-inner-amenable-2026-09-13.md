# Braided V is not inner amenable (Zaremsky Problem 2.11)

Lane z2-11-bv-inner-amen, 2026-09-13. Status: complete written argument, UNREVIEWED
(review requested from z-verify-groups).

Problem (M. C. B. Zaremsky, *Some open problems*, version of July 12, 2026, Section 2,
Problem 11, verbatim): "Is braided V inner amenable? (Probably not.)"

Answer: no.

## 0. Sources

[HO] U. Haagerup and K. K. Olesen, "Non-inner amenability of the Thompson groups T and V",
arXiv:1609.05086v1 (16 Sep 2016), read from the PDF on 2026-09-13.

- §3, definition: a discrete group G is inner amenable if there is a state m on `ℓ^∞(G)` with
  `m(δ_e) = 0` and `m(f) = m(α(g)f)` for all f and g, where `(α(g)f)(h) = f(g^{-1}hg)`;
  equivalently, the conjugation action of G on `G ∖ {e}` is amenable.
- Theorem 2.2 (Thurston; Imbert, their [12, Theorem 1.1]): there is a homeomorphism φ of
  `R ∪ {∞}` onto `R/Z` such that `g ↦ φ ∘ g ∘ φ^{-1}` is an isomorphism `Φ: PPSL(2,Z) → T`.
  Definition 2.1: PPSL(2,Z) is the group of homeomorphisms of `R ∪ {∞}` that are piecewise
  in PSL(2,Z) with finitely many breakpoints, all in `Q ∪ {∞}`.
- Proposition 2.4: `Λ := Φ(PSL(2,Z)) ≤ T`, freely generated by `D² = CA = Φ(a)` (order 2) and
  `C = Φ(b)` (order 3); `Λ ≅ Z_2 * Z_3`.
- Proposition 2.5: an element of PSL(2,Z) is uniquely determined by its values on two distinct
  points of `Q ∪ {∞}`. Remark 2.6: φ restricts to a bijection from `Q ∪ {∞}` onto
  `[0,1) ∩ Z[1/2]`.
- Proposition 3.2 (Rosenblatt): if a non-amenable group acts on a set and every point
  stabilizer is amenable, the action is non-amenable.
- Corollary 3.3: "Let G be discrete a group. If G has a non-amenable subgroup H such that
  {g ∈ H : ghg^{-1} = h} is amenable, for each h ∈ G ∖ {e}, then G is non-inner amenable."
- Theorem 3.4: "The Thompson groups T and V are not inner amenable." Its proof shows that
  `{g ∈ Λ : gf = fg}` is amenable for every `f ∈ V ∖ {e}`: cyclic when `f ∈ Λ ∖ {e}`
  (their [17, Theorems 2.3.3 and 2.3.5]), finite when `f ∈ T ∖ Λ` (elements of Λ commuting
  with f permute the at least two breakpoints of `φ^{-1} f φ`) and finite when `f ∈ V ∖ T`
  (they permute the at least two dyadic discontinuity points of f).
- Theorem 3.1 (Effros): for an ICC group G, if LG has property Γ then G is inner amenable.
- §1: Jolissaint proved that F is inner amenable.

[Brin] M. G. Brin, "The algebra of strand splitting. I. A braided version of Thompson's group V",
J. Group Theory 10 (2007), arXiv:math/0406042. The same group is V_br in Bux–Fluch–Marschler–
Witzel–Zaremsky, "The braided Thompson's groups are of type F_∞", arXiv:1210.2931.

## 1. Braided V and its pure kernel

Elements of bV are classes of triples `(T_-, β, T_+)`, where `T_±` are finite rooted binary trees
with the same number n of leaves (numbered left to right) and `β ∈ B_n` is read from the leaves of
`T_+` to the leaves of `T_-`; `σ_β ∈ S_n` is its permutation. An expansion adds a caret at leaf i
of `T_+` and at leaf `σ_β(i)` of `T_-`, and replaces β by `c_i(β)`, the braid in which strand i is
doubled into two parallel strands. Products are taken on common expansions. Forgetting β to `σ_β`
gives a homomorphism `π: bV → V`, onto because every permutation is the permutation of a braid.
Let `K := ker π`.

Leaf j of a tree S determines a closed standard dyadic arc `I_j^S ⊆ R/Z`. The arcs of the leaves
of S cover `R/Z` and meet only at dyadic endpoints. If `π(h) = (S_-, σ, S_+)` lies in T, then
`π(h)` is a homeomorphism of `R/Z` mapping `I_j^{S_+}` affinely onto `I_{σ(j)}^{S_-}`.

**Fact 1.1 (deleting strands).** For a braid β on a strand set P and `Q ⊆ P`, deleting the strands
that start outside Q gives a well-defined braid `f_Q(β)` from Q to `σ_β(Q)`, and
`f_Q(αβ) = f_{σ_β(Q)}(α) f_Q(β)`. Let `c_i(β)` double strand i into `i', i''`, and let p collapse
`i', i''` to i. For a set Q' of strands of `c_i(β)`:
- (a) if Q' contains at most one of `i', i''`, then `f_{Q'}(c_i β) = f_{p(Q')}(β)`, relabelled;
- (b) if Q' contains both, then `f_{Q'}(c_i β) = c_i(f_{p(Q')}(β))`.

In particular `c_i` is injective (delete `i''`), and in case (b) `f_{Q'}(c_i β) = 1` iff
`f_{p(Q')}(β) = 1`. All of this is geometric. Isotopies of braids restrict to isotopies of
sub-braids. Deleting strands other than the doubled one commutes with doubling. Deleting one of
two parallel strands returns the original strand up to isotopy.

**Lemma 1.2.** Every `k ∈ K` has representatives `(S, β, S)` with β pure, and `(S, β, S) = 1`
iff `β = 1`.

*Proof.* Take a representative `(T_-, β, T_+)`. Since `π(k) = 1`, the V-triples
`(T_-, σ_β, T_+)` and `(T_+, id, T_+)` have a common expansion, necessarily `(S, id, S)`. Every
V-expansion is the image of the bV-expansion at the same leaves, so `k = (S, β', S)` with
`σ_{β'} = id`. If `(S, β, S) = 1`, then it has a common expansion with `(S, 1, S)`; expansions
apply cablings, which are injective, so `β = 1`. ∎

## 2. Minimal linked box sets

For `k ∈ K ∖ {1}` choose `(S, β, S)` as in Lemma 1.2, with leaf set P and arcs `I_j = I_j^S`. Put:
- `r(k) := min{|Q| : Q ⊆ P, f_Q(β) ≠ 1}`. Then `2 ≤ r(k) ≤ |P|`, since `f_P(β) = β ≠ 1` and pure
  braids on at most one strand are trivial.
- `M(k) := {Q ⊆ P : |Q| = r(k), f_Q(β) ≠ 1}`, which is nonempty.
- `B(k) := ⋃_{Q ∈ M(k)} ⋃_{τ} I_{τ(1)} × ⋯ × I_{τ(r)} ⊆ (R/Z)^r`, where τ runs over bijections
  `{1, …, r} → Q`.

**Lemma 2.1.** `r(k)` and `B(k)` do not depend on the representative.

*Proof.* Representatives are related by expansions `(S, β, S) ↦ (S', c_i β, S')`, which split
`I_i` into two closed arcs `I_{i'} ∪ I_{i''}`. Let `|Q'| < r(β)`. In case (a),
`f_{Q'}(c_i β) = f_{p(Q')}(β)` with `|p(Q')| = |Q'| < r`, so it is trivial. In case (b), it is
`c_i` of `f_{p(Q')}(β)` with `|p(Q')| = |Q'| − 1 < r`, again trivial. So `r(c_i β) ≥ r(β)`.
A member of `M(β)` with i replaced by `i'` (when i occurs) has nontrivial restriction by (a), so
`r(c_i β) = r(β)`. If `|Q'| = r` and `f_{Q'}(c_i β) ≠ 1`, then Q' does not contain both `i'` and
`i''` (otherwise `p(Q')` would have `r − 1` elements and nontrivial restriction), so p is
injective on Q' and `p(Q') ∈ M(β)`. Conversely, every injective lift of a member of `M(β)` lies in
`M(c_i β)`. So the boxes of `c_i β` are the boxes of β with each factor `I_i` replaced by `I_{i'}`
or `I_{i''}`, and B is unchanged. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (equivariance).** If `h ∈ bV` and `π(h) ∈ T`, then `r(hkh^{-1}) = r(k)` and
`B(hkh^{-1}) = π(h)^{×r}(B(k))`.

*Proof.* Expand to `h = (S_-, σ, S_+)` and `k = (S_+, β, S_+)` on a common tree. Expanding
`(S, β, S)` with β pure at leaf i adds carets at leaf i of both trees, so k keeps this form. Then
`hkh^{-1} = (S_-, σβσ^{-1}, S_-)` with `σβσ^{-1}` pure. By Fact 1.1,
`f_{σ_σ(Q)}(σβσ^{-1}) = f_Q(σ) f_Q(β) f_Q(σ)^{-1}`, which is trivial iff `f_Q(β)` is. So
`M(hkh^{-1}) = {σ_σ(Q) : Q ∈ M(k)}`. Since `π(h)` maps `I_j^{S_+}` onto `I_{σ_σ(j)}^{S_-}`, it
maps each box of k onto the corresponding box of `hkh^{-1}`. ∎

## 3. Breakpoint sets

For `k ∈ K ∖ {1}` with `r = r(k)`, let `E(k) ⊆ R/Z` be the set of points x for which there are an
index i and a point `y ∈ (R/Z)^r` with `y_i = x` such that the indicator of `B(k)`, restricted to
the circle `{z : z_j = y_j for all j ≠ i}`, is not constant on any neighbourhood of x.

**Lemma 3.1.** `E(k)` is a finite nonempty subset of `D := Z[1/2]/Z`, and
`E(hkh^{-1}) = π(h)(E(k))` whenever `π(h) ∈ T`.

*Proof.* On a coordinate circle, `B(k)` restricts to a finite union of closed arcs taken from the
finite list of arcs used in its boxes. So its indicator is locally constant away from their
endpoints, which are dyadic. For equivariance: `π(h)` is a homeomorphism of `R/Z`,
`π(h)^{×r}` maps coordinate circles to coordinate circles, and
`B(hkh^{-1}) = π(h)^{×r}(B(k))` by Lemma 2.2. For nonemptiness, suppose `E(k) = ∅`. Then the
indicator of `B(k)` is locally constant along every coordinate circle, hence constant along it, and
so constant on `(R/Z)^r` (change one coordinate at a time). But `B(k) ≠ ∅`, and
`B(k) ≠ (R/Z)^r`: for non-dyadic x, the point `(x, …, x)` lies in no box, because x lies in
exactly one arc of the leaf partition and each box uses `r ≥ 2` different arcs. ∎

## 4. Theorem

**Theorem 4.1.** Braided V is not inner amenable.

*Proof.* `Λ ≅ Z_2 * Z_3` ([HO] Prop. 2.4). The kernel of `Λ → Z_2 × Z_3` has finite index and is
torsion-free: an element of finite order in a free product is conjugate into a factor, and the
factors embed in `Z_2 × Z_3`. By the Kurosh subgroup theorem it is free, and it is non-abelian
because Λ is non-amenable. Pick `γ_1, γ_2` in it freely generating a free group of rank 2, lifts
`h_1, h_2 ∈ bV`, and put `H := ⟨h_1, h_2⟩`. The homomorphism from the free group on `x_1, x_2`
onto H becomes an isomorphism onto `⟨γ_1, γ_2⟩` after composing with π. So H is free of rank 2,
`π|_H` is injective, and H is non-amenable.

By [HO] Cor. 3.3 it suffices to show that `C_H(f) := {h ∈ H : hfh^{-1} = f}` is amenable for every
`f ∈ bV ∖ {1}`. Since `π|_H` is injective, `C_H(f) ≅ π(C_H(f))`.

*Case `f ∉ K`.* Then `π(f) ∈ V ∖ {e}`, and `π(C_H(f)) ≤ {g ∈ Λ : gπ(f) = π(f)g}`, which is
amenable by the proof of [HO] Thm. 3.4.

*Case `f ∈ K ∖ {1}`.* For `h ∈ C_H(f)`, Lemma 3.1 gives `π(h)(E(f)) = E(f)`, since `π(h) ∈ Λ ≤ T`.
Transport by φ: `g̃ := φ^{-1} π(h) φ ∈ PSL(2,Z)` permutes the finite nonempty set
`Y := φ^{-1}(E(f)) ⊆ Q ∪ {∞}` ([HO] Rem. 2.6). If `|Y| ≥ 2`, then [HO] Prop. 2.5 shows g̃ is
determined by its permutation of Y, so `π(C_H(f))` is finite. If `Y = {y}`, then `π(C_H(f))`
embeds in the stabilizer of y in PSL(2,Z). PSL(2,Z) acts transitively on `Q ∪ {∞}`, so this
stabilizer is conjugate to the stabilizer of ∞, which is `{z ↦ z + n : n ∈ Z} ≅ Z`. Either way
`C_H(f)` is amenable. ∎

**Corollary 4.2.** bV is ICC, `L(bV)` is a II_1 factor without property Γ (so not McDuff), and
every subgroup of bV containing H is not inner amenable.

*Proof.* A nontrivial finite conjugacy class would give a conjugation-invariant mean on
`bV ∖ {1}`, so bV is ICC, and [HO] Thm. 3.1 (Effros) gives the statement about property Γ. The
last statement is [HO] Cor. 3.3 applied to the subgroup. ∎

## 5. Checks and scope

- Why V's theorem does not transfer by itself: a conjugation-invariant mean on `bV ∖ {1}` could
  live on the kernel K, a directed union of pure braid groups under cabling. Section 3 is what
  rules this out. Non-inner amenability does not pass from a quotient to an extension in general.
- Consistency with inner amenable groups: F is inner amenable (Jolissaint), and F has no
  non-abelian free subgroup (Brin–Squier), so Cor. 3.3 has no H there. For braided F (F_br) the
  kernel analysis of Sections 1–3 still applies, but its quotient F contains no copy of Λ, so
  nothing is claimed about F_br.
- The argument does not claim that K itself is non-inner amenable: K acts trivially on the arcs,
  and only conjugation by elements over Λ is controlled.
- Not used: torsion-freeness, finite presentability, the type F_∞ property.
- Adjacent, unchecked: the proof applies to any group mapping onto a subgroup of V that contains
  Λ, provided every nontrivial kernel element carries an equivariant nonempty finite set of
  breakpoints. Candidates are Thompson-like groups over other strand families with an injective
  splitting map and a strand-deleting functor satisfying Fact 1.1; each needs its own check.
