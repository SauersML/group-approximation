# SL_n(Z) acting on the space of unimodular lattices is sofic: Hecke orbits as finite models (2026-09-12)

Lane `ex-lattice-space-action`. Status: **unreviewed**, complete proofs. Continued in
`research/artifacts/sl3z-lattice-space-hecke-sofic-part2-2026-09-12.md` (Sections 4-8).

## 0. Summary

**Theorem P (equidistributed finite orbits).** Let `Γ` be a countable sofic group acting by
homeomorphisms on a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space `X`, preserving a Borel
probability measure `μ`. Suppose there are finite `Γ`-invariant sets `V_k ⊆ X` whose normalized
counting measures `ν_k` satisfy `∫ f dν_k → ∫ f dμ` for every `f ∈ C_c(X)`. Then `Γ ↷ (X,μ)` is sofic
(Păunescu Definition 1.4).

**Theorem H (Hecke orbits).** Let `n >= 2`, `G = SL_n(R)`, `Γ = SL_n(Z)`, `X_n = G/Γ` with its
`G`-invariant probability `μ`, `p` a prime, and `a_k = diag(p^k, p^(−k), 1, ..., 1) ∈ SL_n(Z[1/p])`.
The sets `V_k = Γ·a_k^(−1)Γ ⊆ X_n` are finite `Γ`-orbits, and their normalized counting measures
converge to `μ` against `C_c(X_n)`. Hence `SL_n(Z) ↷ SL_n(R)/SL_n(Z)` is sofic.

For `n = 3` this refutes `sl3z-lattice-space-action-is-not-sofic`. With
`induced-actions-sofic-iff-homogeneous-action-sofic`, induction through `SL_3(R)` then carries every
sofic seed of `SL_3(Z)` to a sofic action. The mechanism is arithmetic. A point `gΓ` has finite `Γ`-orbit iff `g`
commensurates `Γ`, and the commensurator of `Γ` in `G`, which contains `SL_n(Q)`, is dense.

**Proof map.** Theorem P follows from Lemma LC, the Portmanteau theorem, and a product with a sofic
approximation of `Γ` (Section 2). Theorem H uses the one-prime S-arithmetic cover
`Y = (SL_n(R) × SL_n(Q_p))/SL_n(Z[1/p])` (Section 3), Howe--Moore for `SL_n(Q_p)`, and thickening in
the real factor (part 2, Section 4).

**Credit.** Equidistribution of Hecke points is classical: Clozel--Oh--Ullmo (Invent. Math. 144,
2001) with rates, and Eskin--Oh (Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 26, 2006) by mixing. The thickening
device goes back to Margulis's thesis and Eskin--McMullen (Duke Math. J. 71, 1993). **Theorem numbers
from these papers were not verified.** Section 4 proves the case needed from Howe--Moore alone, so no
citation route depends on them. Theorem P is a folklore-level principle; no source for it was checked.
Novelty of Theorem H as a soficity statement: bounded check only (part 2, Section 8).

## 1. Imports

2. **Lemma LC** (landed, `research/artifacts/nonsofic-action-mixing-transfer-2026-09-12.md`, Section
   2). Fix increasing finite partitions `P_m` generating the Borel σ-algebra mod null sets, and finite
   `F_m ↑ Γ`. Let `𝒲_m` be the words of length `<= m` in `u_g, u_g^*` (`g ∈ F_m`) and `1_p`
   (`p ∈ P_1 ∪ ... ∪ P_m`). A *model of level `m` and error `ε`* is a finite set `Y`, a map
   `σ : F_m → Sym(Y)` and a partition `(Y_q)_(q ∈ P_m)` of `Y` with `|tr_Y(w(σ,1_Y)) − τ(w)| < ε` for all
   `w ∈ 𝒲_m`, where `1_p ↦ Σ_(q ⊆ p) 1_(Y_q)`. *The action is sofic iff it has models of every level
   and error.*
3. **Howe--Moore.** C. Ciobotaru, arXiv:1403.0223, verbatim from the PDF: *"Theorem 1.1. (See Theorem
   4.19 and Section 4.1) Let G be a connected, non-compact, simple real Lie group, with finite center,
   or an isotropic simple algebraic group over a non Archimedean local field, or a closed,
   topologically simple subgroup of Aut(T) that acts 2–transitively on the boundary ∂T, where T is a
   bi-regular tree with valence ≥ 3 at every vertex. Then G admits the Howe–Moore property."* Graph
   node: `howe-moore-property-for-simple-lie-and-p-adic-groups`, which lists `SL_n(Q_p)`. Used form: a
   unitary representation of `SL_n(Q_p)` without nonzero invariant vectors has `C_0` coefficients.
4. **Standard facts.** (a) `SL_n(Z)` is a lattice in `SL_n(R)`. (b) `SL_n(k)` is generated by
   elementary matrices `E_ij(t)` for a field `k`, and so is `SL_n(R)` for a local ring `R` such as
   `Z/p^m`.

## 2. Proof of Theorem P

**Step 1 (null-boundary partitions).** `X` is metrizable. Fix a compatible metric `d` and a dense
sequence `(x_i)`. For fixed `i` the spheres `{d(x_i,·) = r}` are disjoint, so all but countably many
`r` have `μ(∂B(x_i,r)) = 0`. So `X` has a countable base `O_1, O_2, ...` of open sets with
`μ(∂O_j) = 0`. Let `P_m` be the partition generated by `O_1, ..., O_m`. These partitions increase and
generate the Borel σ-algebra. Since `∂(A ∩ B) ⊆ ∂A ∪ ∂B`, `∂(X ∖ A) = ∂A`, `∂(gA) = g∂A` and `μ` is
invariant, every set built from atoms by finitely many complements, intersections and translations has
`μ`-null boundary.

**Step 2 (Portmanteau).** Given `ε > 0`, pick a compact `K` with `μ(K) > 1 − ε` and `φ ∈ C_c(X)` with
`1_K <= φ <= 1`. Then `liminf_k ν_k(supp φ) >= lim_k ∫ φ dν_k = ∫ φ dμ > 1 − ε`, so `(ν_k)` is tight.
Tightness plus convergence on `C_c` gives convergence on bounded continuous functions. By the
Portmanteau theorem, `ν_k(B) → μ(B)` for every Borel `B` with `μ(∂B) = 0`.

**Step 3 (models).** Let `σ_k : Γ → Sym(W_k)` be a sofic approximation. For a word
`S = g_1^(±1) ⋯ g_r^(±1)` with product `h ∈ Γ`, the permutation `S(σ_k)` is Hamming-close to `σ_k(h)`,
so `tr_(W_k)(S(σ_k)) → δ_(h,1)`. On `Y_k = V_k × W_k` put

```text
ρ_k(g) = π_k(g) × σ_k(g),        Y_(k,q) = (q ∩ V_k) × W_k   (q ∈ P_m),
```

where `π_k(g)` is the restriction of `g` to `V_k`. In `L^∞(X) ⋊ Γ`, pushing the projection letters of
`w ∈ 𝒲_m` to the left (`u_g 1_A = 1_(gA) u_g`) gives `w(u,1) = 1_(B_w) u_(h_w)`. Here `h_w` is the
product of the group letters and `B_w` is an intersection of translates of unions of atoms. So
`μ(∂B_w) = 0` by Step 1, and `τ(w) = δ_(h_w,1) μ(B_w)`. On `Y_k` the same pushing is exact, because
`π_k` is an honest action that agrees with the action on `X`, and a projection letter is
`1_(A ∩ V_k) ⊗ 1`. So `w(ρ_k,1) = (1_(B_w ∩ V_k) ⊗ 1)(π_k(h_w) ⊗ S_w(σ_k))`, and

```text
tr_(Y_k)(w(ρ_k,1)) = ( |{v ∈ B_w ∩ V_k : h_w v = v}| / |V_k| ) · tr_(W_k)(S_w(σ_k)).
```

If `h_w = 1`, this is `ν_k(B_w) · tr(S_w(σ_k)) → μ(B_w)` by Step 2. If `h_w != 1`, its modulus is at most
`|tr(S_w(σ_k))| → 0`. `𝒲_m` is finite, so for each `m` and `ε`, `Y_k` is a model of level `m` and error
`ε` for all large `k`. Lemma LC gives soficity. ∎

*Remarks.* Freeness is not assumed: elements acting trivially, such as `−1 ∈ SL_n(Z)` for even `n`,
are handled by the factor `W_k`. If the finite `Γ`-sets `V_k` are themselves asymptotically free,
`W_k` can be dropped (part 2, Section 6).

## 3. The one-prime S-arithmetic cover

Notation for Sections 3-6: `n >= 2`, `G = SL_n(R)`, `Γ = SL_n(Z)`, `H = SL_n(Q_p)`, `K = SL_n(Z_p)`
(compact open in `H`), and `Δ = SL_n(Z[1/p])` embedded diagonally in `S = G × H`. Let `Y = S/Δ`, with
`S` acting on the left.

**Lemma S1.** (a) `Δ ∩ (G × K) = Γ`. (b) `Δ` is discrete in `S`. (c) The projections of `Δ` to `G` and
to `H` are dense. (d) `Γ` is dense in `K`. (e) `H = KΔ`.

*Proof.* (a) `Z[1/p] ∩ Z_p = Z`. (b) On the neighbourhood `{‖g − 1‖_∞ < 1} × K` of `1`, an element of
`Δ` has integer entries by (a), hence equals `1`. (c) `Z[1/p]` is dense in `R` and in `Q_p`, since
`p^(−j)Z` is dense in `p^(−j)Z_p`. So the closure of `Δ` in either factor contains every elementary
matrix, and these generate (Import 4b). (d) Import 4b for `Z/p^m` makes `SL_n(Z) → SL_n(Z/p^m)` onto,
and the kernels of `K → SL_n(Z/p^m)` form a base at `1`. (e) `Kh` is open and meets the dense `Δ`. ∎

**Lemma S2 (coordinates).** Let `D ⊆ G` be a Borel fundamental domain for right multiplication by `Γ`;
it has finite Haar measure (Import 4a). Then `D × K` is a Borel fundamental domain for right
multiplication by `Δ` on `S`. So `Y` carries an `S`-invariant probability `m_Y`, which in these
coordinates is normalized Haar measure on `D × K`. Left multiplication by `(1,k)`, `k ∈ K`, acts on the
coordinates by `(g,k') ↦ (g,kk')`.

*Proof.* Existence: given `(g,h)`, pick `γ ∈ Δ ∩ Kh` (S1(e)), so `(g,h)γ^(−1) ∈ G × K`. Then pick
`δ ∈ Γ` with `gγ^(−1)δ ∈ D`; since `δ ∈ K`, `(g,h)γ^(−1)δ ∈ D × K`. Uniqueness: `(g,k)γ = (g',k')` with
`k, k' ∈ K` forces `γ ∈ K ∩ Δ = Γ` (S1(a)), and then `g, gγ ∈ D` forces `γ = 1`. ∎

**Lemma S3 (projection to X_n).** For `y = (g,h)Δ` put `π(y) = gγ^(−1)Γ`, where `γ ∈ Δ ∩ Kh`.

1. `π` is well defined, `π((u,1)y) = uπ(y)` for `u ∈ G`, and `π((1,k)y) = π(y)` for `k ∈ K`.
2. `π_* m_Y = μ`.
3. For `a ∈ Δ`, the map `Φ_a : K → X_n`, `k ↦ π((1,ak)Δ)`, pushes normalized Haar measure on `K` to
   the normalized counting measure on the finite `Γ`-orbit `V(a) = Γ·a^(−1)Γ`.

*Proof.* (1) Two choices `γ, γ' ∈ Kh` satisfy `γ'γ^(−1) ∈ K ∩ Δ = Γ`, so `gγ'^(−1)Γ = gγ^(−1)Γ`.
Replacing `(g,h)` by `(gδ,hδ)` with `δ ∈ Δ` replaces `γ` by `γδ` and leaves `gγ^(−1)` unchanged.
Equivariance is clear, and `K`-invariance holds because `Kkh = Kh`. (2) In the coordinates of S2,
`π(g,k) = gΓ`, so `π_* m_Y` is normalized Haar measure on `D`, which is `μ`.

(3) Put `U = K ∩ a^(−1)Ka`, an open subgroup of `K`.

* *Constant on cosets `Uk`.* For `u ∈ U`, `auk = (aua^(−1))ak` with `aua^(−1) ∈ K`, and `π` is
  `K`-invariant. So `Φ_a` factors through `U\K`, whose `[K:U]` points each have Haar measure `1/[K:U]`.
* *Image in `V(a)`.* `Φ_a(k) = γ^(−1)Γ` with `γ ∈ Δ ∩ Kak`. Write `K = ⊔_j Uδ_j` with `δ_j ∈ Γ`
  (possible by S1(d), since each coset is open). If `k ∈ Uδ_j`, then `Kak = Kaδ_j`, so
  `γδ_j^(−1)a^(−1) ∈ K ∩ Δ = Γ` and `γ ∈ Γaδ_j ⊆ ΓaΓ`. Hence `Φ_a(k) ∈ Γa^(−1)Γ/Γ = V(a)`.
* *Onto.* For `δ ∈ Γ`, the element `γ = aδ^(−1)` lies in `Δ ∩ Kaδ^(−1)`, so `Φ_a(δ^(−1)) = δa^(−1)Γ`.
* *Sizes.* For `δ ∈ Γ` we have `aδa^(−1) ∈ Δ`, and it lies in `Γ` iff it lies in `K` (S1(a)). So the
  stabilizer of `a^(−1)Γ` in `Γ` is `Γ ∩ U`, and `|V(a)| = [Γ : Γ ∩ U] = [K : U]` by density (S1(d)).
  The induced map `U\K → V(a)` is onto between sets of equal size, hence bijective. ∎

**Lemma S4 (mixing on the cover).** The representation `λ(h)F(y) = F((1,h)^(−1)y)` of `H` on
`L²_0(Y,m_Y)` has `C_0` matrix coefficients.

*Proof.* By Import 3 it suffices to show `L²_0(Y)` has no nonzero `H`-invariant vector. Let `F` be
invariant and `F̃(s) = F(sΔ)`. For each `h`, `F̃((1,h)s) = F̃(s)` for a.e. `s`. By Fubini on
`G × H × H`, for a.e. `g` the function `f_g = F̃(g,·)` satisfies `f_g(hh') = f_g(h')` for a.e. `(h,h')`.
So it is a.e. equal to a constant, which is `φ(g) = ∫_K F̃(g,k) dk`. Thus `F̃(g,h) = φ(g)` a.e., and
right `Δ`-invariance gives `φ(gδ) = φ(g)` a.e. for every `δ ∈ Δ`. For `ψ ∈ C_c(G)`, the map
`t ↦ ∫ φ(gt)ψ(g) dg = ∫ φ(g)ψ(gt^(−1)) dg` is continuous (`G` is unimodular). It is constant on the
dense set `Δ` (S1(c)), so it is constant. Hence `φ(·t) = φ` a.e. for all `t`, `φ` is a.e. constant, and
`F` is constant. ∎
