## 0. Setting

- Let P be countably infinite, nu: P -> N a bijection, and E = E_nu = <V, hat(P)>
  the shell group of `shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`. Put p = 0^inf and
  C_n = 0^n 1 C.
- Let A_1 = {e in E : sing(e) subset {p}, e(p) = p}. Let d: A_1 -> Q = <rho(P), tau> be the
  germ map at p, where Q sits in the near permutation group NSym(N) as in that claim.
- Let eta: NSym(N) -> Z be the near index. It is a homomorphism, it is invariant under
  conjugation, and eta(tau) = 1.
- For a subgroup H <= Q write A(H) = d^-1(H). In particular K = A(1) = ker d.

**Rigid form near p.** Every e in A_1 has an M and an injection pi: [M, inf) -> N with cofinite
image such that

    e(0^n 1 z) = 0^(pi(n)) 1 z    for all n >= M and all z,

and pi represents d(e). The reason is as follows.
- An element of V fixing p is, near p, a prefix map 0^a z -> 0^b z (reduction artifact, Section
  on Fix_V(p)). Such a map is rigid on C_n for n >= a, with pi(n) = n + b - a.
- hat(g) is rigid on every cone.
- At points u 0^inf of Omega with u nonempty, hat(g) acts by a prefix replacement.
- The germ at p of a product fixing p is therefore a composite of such rigid cone maps.

**The kernel.**
- An element of K is the identity on some neighbourhood {p} u (union over n >= m of C_n) and
  is locally V elsewhere. Hence it lies in V, supported in C_0 u ... u C_(m-1).
- So K is the increasing union of the groups V(C_0 u ... u C_(m-1)), which contain every V(C_n).
- Every subgroup of V(C_n) lies in K, so K <= A(H) for every H.

## 1. Local pieces

**Lemma 1 (edge pieces are infinite).** K is not finitely generated. So A(H) is not finitely
generated whenever H is finite.

*Proof.*
- A finitely generated subgroup of K lies in a single stage V(C_0 u ... u C_(m-1)). That stage
  does not contain the nontrivial elements of V(C_m). So K is not finitely generated.
- If H is finite, K has finite index in A(H). A finitely generated group has finitely generated
  finite-index subgroups, so A(H) is not finitely generated. QED

**Lemma 2 (coarse orbit condition).** Suppose A(H) is generated by a finite set F. Choose M
uniformly for all f in F, with rigid maps pi_f: [M, inf) -> N whose images have finite
complements T_f. Let O be the smallest subset of N that contains [0, M) and every T_f and is
closed under every pi_f and every pi_f^-1, applied where defined. Then O = N.

So N is the orbit of a finite set under the pseudogroup generated by representatives of
finitely many elements of H.

*Proof.*
- Let n be outside O. Then n >= M, and n lies in the image of every pi_f.
- By closure, pi_f(n) and pi_f^-1(n) are also outside O. For example, if pi_f(n) were in O, then
  n = pi_f^-1(pi_f(n)) would be in O.
- Hence f and f^-1 map C_n rigidly onto a cone C_m with m outside O.
- By induction on word length, every element of <F> is rigid on C_n for every n outside O.
- The nonidentity elements of V(C_n) are not rigid on C_n, yet they lie in K <= A(H) = <F>.
  So no such n exists. QED

*Example.* H = rho(P) fixes every odd n near infinity. Choosing M large, every odd n >= M that is
not in any T_f stays outside O. So A(rho(P)) is not finitely generated. The same holds for any H
whose elements all fix a common infinite set near infinity.

## 2. Obstruction for commensurable-with-tau pieces

**Theorem 3.** Let P be finitely generated, so Q = <rho(S), tau> is finitely generated. Suppose Q
acts on a connected graph X such that:

- (i) every vertex stabilizer Q_v is virtually infinite cyclic;
- (ii) some vertex stabilizer contains a nonzero power of a Q-conjugate of tau;
- (iii) every edge stabilizer Q_e is infinite.

Then tau^a is central in Q for some a >= 1, and P is virtually cyclic.

*Proof.*
1. **Pick a base vertex.** Replacing a vertex by a translate, some Q_(v0) contains tau^m with
   m != 0.
2. **Adjacent stabilizers are commensurable.** Let e be an edge with endpoints v and w. A
   subgroup of Q_e of index at most 2 fixes both endpoints, and it is infinite by (iii). By (i),
   an infinite subgroup of a virtually infinite cyclic group has finite index. So Q_v and Q_w
   are commensurable.
3. **All stabilizers are commensurable.** X is connected, so all vertex stabilizers are
   pairwise commensurable.
4. **Q commensurates <tau>.** For q in Q, the stabilizer Q_(q v0) = q Q_(v0) q^-1 is
   commensurable with Q_(v0). Also <tau^m> has finite index in Q_(v0) by (i). So every q
   commensurates <tau>.
5. **Each element centralizes a power of tau.** Commensuration gives nonzero integers a and b
   with q tau^a q^-1 = tau^b. Applying eta gives a = b. So q commutes with tau^(a_q) for some
   a_q >= 1.
6. **A central power.** Let a be the least common multiple of the a_q over the finite
   generating set rho(S) u {tau}. Then tau^a is central, hence normal, in Q.
7. **Conclusion.** By `shell-prefix-core-detects-finite-ray-enumerations` (clause 2 implies that
   P is virtually cyclic), P is virtually cyclic. QED

## 3. Where the approach class dies

**The approach.** Present A_1 by Brown's criterion. Here A_1 acts through d on a simply
connected complex X with finitely many cell orbits in dimensions at most 2. The A_1-stabilizer of
a cell sigma is A(Q_sigma). The criterion (K. S. Brown, Finiteness properties of groups, JPAA 44,
1987; not re-fetched) yields A_1 FP from two inputs:

- the vertex pieces A(Q_v) are FP;
- the edge pieces A(Q_e) are finitely generated.

**The natural pieces.** These are the positive-control pieces: Q_v virtually <tau>, so that A(Q_v)
contains a finite-index subgroup of Fix_V(p) = A(<tau>) with finite index.

**Where it dies.**
- The edge clause forces every Q_e to be infinite (Lemma 1).
- Theorem 3 then forces P to be virtually cyclic.
- So for every non-virtually-cyclic input, in particular every route input P_0 x Z and every
  enumeration, no Brown decomposition over d has vertex pieces commensurable with Fix_V(p).

**What any surviving decomposition needs.** Some vertex stabilizer Q_v must fail to be virtually
cyclic, or no vertex stabilizer may meet a conjugate of <tau> nontrivially. By Lemma 2, every edge
stabilizer Q_e must have a finite coarse orbit generating set on N. So the local pieces are groups
A(H) with H infinite, not virtually <tau>, and coarsely cofinite-orbit. These pieces are of the
same nature as A_1 itself. That is exactly the circularity recorded in the target's Attempts.

## 4. Not claimed

- Nothing about finite presentation of A_1 for any input. Brown's criterion is sufficient, not
  necessary.
- Nothing about complexes whose vertex stabilizers are virtually cyclic of near index zero, where
  condition (ii) fails.
- Finite presentation of Fix_V(p) is not used anywhere above.
