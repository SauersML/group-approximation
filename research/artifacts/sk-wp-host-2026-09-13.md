# sk-wp-host: a simple Kazhdan LEF host with solvable word problem for all finitely presented residually finite groups

Lane `sk-wp-host`, 2026-09-13. All claims below are established through complete routes and UNREVIEWED (queued with
sk-verify-7).

## 0. Task and verdict
The brief asked for one infinite finitely generated simple Kazhdan LEF group with **solvable word problem** containing
every finitely presented residually finite group. This was the "next target" in sk-strong-3's artifact
(`research/artifacts/sk-strong-3-universal-host-2026-09-13.md` §6).

**Verdict: no such group exists.** More generally, no finitely generated group with solvable word problem contains
every finitely presented residually finite group (Theorem A). A second, independent obstruction applies to LEF hosts
without any decidability hypothesis: a LEF host whose finite partial models have recursively bounded size misses
some finitely presented residually finite group (Theorem B).

So the universal hosts of `universal-simple-kazhdan-lef-groups-exist` (sk-strong-1) and
`simple-kazhdan-lef-host-for-countably-many-rf-groups` (sk-strong-3) necessarily have unsolvable word problem, and their
LEF growth outgrows every recursive function. Nothing in those nodes is contradicted: they never claim decidability.

McKinsey's uniform algorithm for finitely presented residually finite groups does not help. It is uniform in the
presentation, but its running time is not bounded uniformly, and a single host bounds the running time of all its
subgroups at once.

## 1. Theorem A: `no-decidable-group-contains-every-fp-rf-group`
**Statement.** If a finitely generated group `U` contains every finitely presented residually finite group, then the
word problem of `U` is unsolvable.

**Proof.**
- Suppose an algorithm `A` decides the word problem of `U`.
- Let `t(n)` be its largest running time over words of length at most `n`. It is recursive and non-decreasing, and
  `WP(U) ∈ F(t)` in the notation of `kms-arbitrarily-hard-fp-rf-groups`.
- Apply main's established `uniform-wp-bound-excludes-bh-universal-targets` to the class `{U}` with `g = t`. It gives a
  finitely presented residually finite group embedding in no member of the class.
- That route substitutes fixed words for generators, so a subgroup's word problem stays in `F(t)`, and it takes a
  Kharlampovich–Myasnikov–Sapir group whose word problem is not in `F(t)`.

## 2. Theorem B: `recursive-lef-growth-hosts-miss-some-fp-rf-group`
Let `L_U(r)` be the least order of a finite group receiving an injective partial homomorphism of the `r`-ball of `U`
(Bradford's LEF growth).

**Statement.**
- (a) If `U` is LEF and `L_U ≤ h` for a recursive `h`, then some finitely presented residually finite group does not
  embed in `U`.
- (b) So universal hosts have non-recursive LEF growth.
- (c) LEF plus solvable word problem makes `L_U` recursive, so (a) re-proves Theorem A for LEF hosts.

**The key inequality.** For a finitely presented `Γ ≤ U`, `ρ_Γ(n) ≤ L_Γ(n) ≤ L_U(Cn)` once `n` is at least the relator
length and the generator-length constant `C`.
- A partial model of a ball containing all relators extends to a genuine homomorphism, which then separates the ball.
- Kharlampovich–Myasnikov–Sapir Theorem 4.19 supplies finitely presented residually finite groups with
  `ρ_Γ(n) > h(n²)`.

Full proof: `recursive-lef-growth-hosts-miss-some-fp-rf-group-proof`, Steps 1–6, with model tests (finite `U`, `U = Z`).

**Why B is worth having beside A.** It needs no decidability of the host, only a recursive bound on its finite models.
It connects main's LEF growth computations (`subshift-elementary-group-lef-growth-complexity-bounds`, the Sturmian
`exp(Θ(r²))` result) to embedding theorems:
- a `G_X` with recursively bounded LEF growth misses some finitely presented residually finite group. This covers every
  Sturmian `G_X`, whatever the slope, since `L(r) ≤ 2^{9(8r+4)²}` (`sturmian-elementary-group-lef-growth-is-exp-r-squared`,
  reviewed PASS by sk-referee-2), and more generally every `G_X` whose recurrence function is recursively bounded;
- every finitely presented subgroup of such a `G_X` has recursively bounded depth.

## 3. Sources read
Kharlampovich–Myasnikov–Sapir, arXiv:1204.6506v5. The source `KMS-322.tex` and the PDF were fetched on MSI and read
there (pdftotext is broken on MSI; the text was extracted with ghostscript). Journal: Bull. Math. Sci. 7 (2017) 309–352.

- **Theorem 4.19** (PDF p. 26, §4.4), verbatim:
  > For every recursive function f there exists a finitely presented residually finite group G from A_p²A ∩ ZN₃A such
  > that ρ_G(n) > f(n) for all n. In addition, we can assume that the word problem in G is as hard as the membership
  > problem for any prescribed recursive set of natural numbers.
- **Depth function** (§1.1, introduced by Bou-Rabee), verbatim:
  > the depth function ρ_G(n) is the smallest function such that every two words w ≠_G w′ of length at most n are
  > separated by a homomorphism to a group (semigroup) H with |H| ≤ ρ_G(n).
- **Journal numbering** of Theorem 4.19: not checked. Main's time-complexity import cites Theorem 4.21 of the same
  paper.

## 4. What survives, and open targets
- **One host per group.**
  - `perfect-erf-groups-have-decidable-simple-kazhdan-hosts` (sk-wp-embedding, established, unreviewed) and its open
    generalization `erf-groups-have-decidable-simple-kazhdan-hosts` are about one group at a time. Theorem A does not
    touch them.
- **Uniformly bounded families (open, not landed as a node).**
  - For a recursive `g`, is there a decidable simple Kazhdan LEF host containing every perfect finitely generated
    group that is effectively residually finite with chain and word problem computable within bound `g`?
  - Theorem A shows a bound of this kind is forced. It is the only way a decidable universal host can exist.
  - The obstacle is an effective form of Wilson's embedding into a 2-generator residually finite group, together with
    uniform Toeplitz languages.
- **Depth versus LEF growth.**
  - Theorem B gives `ρ_Γ ≤ L_U(C·)` for finitely presented subgroups.
  - Whether a Sturmian `G_X` (LEF growth `exp(Θ(r²))`) contains every finitely presented residually finite group with
    depth `exp(O(n²))` is open.
