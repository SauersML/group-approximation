## New theorem

Let a countable finite-rank abelian group `A` act on `S`, and suppose a
subgroup `H<=A` fixes every site while every `A_s/H` is finite.  For every
countable locally finite lamp `L`, the generalized permutation wreath

`(direct_sum_S L) rtimes A`

has finite nuclear dimension.  This does not require `H` to be a direct
summand of `A`.  It therefore covers varying positive-rank stabilizers which
are all commensurable over one common core, including every finite menu of
pairwise commensurable stabilizer types.

If `r=rank(A)`, `h=rank(H)`, and `q=rank(A/H)>=1`, the proof gives

`r <= dim_nuc <= 9^r(h+1)(b(q)+1)-1`,

where `b(q)` is the finite uniform bound for scalar-twisted group algebras of
finitely generated abelian groups of rank at most `q`, enlarged to be at
least one.  If `q=0`, the exact value is `h=r`.

## Mechanism

At a finite-stage Eckhardt--Wu stabilizer fiber `D_x rtimes N`, the subgroup
`J=N intersection H` is central and acts trivially.  Decompose over
`J_hat`.  Every resulting fiber is a scalar-twisted crossed product by
`M=N/J`, whose action on the lamp sites has finite stabilizers.

After a torsion-free finite-index restriction, the coefficient regroups as a
matrix/UHF Bernoulli tensor.  Non-scalar fibers have nuclear dimension at
most one by strongly outer twisted-crossed-product permanence.  The scalar
fiber is `C*(M,omega)` and has a uniform rank-dependent bound by
Eckhardt--Wu's twisted virtually-polycyclic theorem.  The continuous-field
inequality costs the factor `h+1`, and the global long-thin-cover estimate
costs `9^r`.

This is precisely where nonsplitting is absorbed: it changes the fiber
cocycle but does not change the residual finite-stabilizer geometry.

## Strict example and remaining boundary

Take `A=Z[1/2] direct_sum Z` and `H=Z direct_sum 0`.  Then

`A/H=(Z[1/2]/Z) direct_sum Z`

has rank one and nonzero torsion, while `A` is torsion-free, so `H` cannot be
a direct summand.  Pull back the disjoint union of the regular quotient orbit
and the coset orbits by the cyclic groups generated by `2^(-n)+Z`.  The
point stabilizers vary, all have rank one, and all are finite over `H`.
The regular orbit also makes kernel-finiteness fail for every nontrivial
finite lamp.

The later theorem `stw84-finite-stabilizer-menu-wreath-bound` crosses this
boundary whenever only finitely many distinct stabilizers occur, even if
their rational subspaces have zero intersection.  It intersects only the
types active in a given non-scalar fiber, then simultaneously purifies their
residual sublattices.  Thus the unresolved boundary is now an infinite menu
of infinite stabilizer types with no common finite-index core.  Mere uniform
rank bounds do not yet produce one finite-index lattice that is pure relative
to all of those subgroups.
