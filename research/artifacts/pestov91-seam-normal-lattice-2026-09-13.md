# Every proper quotient of the three-generator seam group is LEF

2026-09-13. Written proof, not Lean-verified.

The [prescribed-endpoint construction](pestov91-heteroclinic-prescribed-mf-quotients-2026-09-13.md)
gives, for any two infinite minimal binary subshifts, an exact sequence

`1 -> L -> H --pi--> Q_- x Q_+ -> 1`,

where `H` is Kazhdan and generated by three elements of orders `7,2,2`,
`L ~= SL_infinity(F_2)` is infinite simple locally finite, and each
`Q_i` is the specified infinite simple Kazhdan LEF endpoint group.
Moreover `Rad_MF(H)=L` and `H` has no nontrivial finite quotients.

The same construction determines all normal subgroups, not just the
MF radical.

## Theorem

Put `N_-=pi^{-1}(Q_- x 1)` and `N_+=pi^{-1}(1 x Q_+)`.
The complete normal-subgroup lattice of `H` consists of the five groups

`1`, `L`, `N_-`, `N_+`, `H`.

Here `L<N_-<H` and `L<N_+<H`, while `N_-` and `N_+` are incomparable,
their intersection is `L`, and their product is `H`. In particular:

- `L` is the unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup, and every
  nontrivial normal subgroup contains it;
- `H` is centerless and directly indecomposable;
- every quotient by a nontrivial normal subgroup is LEF and Kazhdan:
  its isomorphism type is among `Q_- x Q_+`, `Q_-`, `Q_+`, and `1`;
- `H` is **just non-MF**: it is non-MF and every proper quotient is MF;
  in fact every proper quotient is LEF;
- any homomorphism out of `H` is either injective or has image of one
  of the four displayed types.

The two simple endpoint factors may be isomorphic. The lattice still
has five distinct normal subgroups; a diagonal subgroup in the product
does not supply an additional normal subgroup.

## 1. The finitary kernel has trivial centralizer

The dense free joining orbit gives a faithful representation of the
seam ring on `F_2^(Z)`, where the superscript denotes the algebraic
direct sum. Faithfulness follows from the crossed-product normal form:
different shift powers occupy different matrix diagonals, and a
coefficient function vanishing on the dense orbit vanishes everywhere.
Consequently `H=EL_6(R)` acts faithfully on

`V=F_2^(Omega)`, `Omega=Z x {1,...,6}`.

Its kernel `L` is the full finitary general linear group on this basis,
so it contains `1+E_ij` for every pair of distinct basis indices `i,j`.

Let `A` be any linear automorphism of `V` centralizing `L`. Commuting
with `1+E_ij` is equivalent to `A E_ij=E_ij A`. For `k!=j`, applying
this equality to the basis vector `v_k` gives

`0=(A v_k)_j v_i`.

For every `j` one can choose `i!=j`, so all off-diagonal entries of
`A` vanish. Write `A v_k=lambda_k v_k`. Applying the same equality to
`v_j` now gives `lambda_i=lambda_j`. Thus `A` is a nonzero scalar.
The only nonzero scalar over `F_2` is one, so `A=1`. In particular

`C_H(L)=1`.                                             (1)

This also proves the assertion in the full linear automorphism group;
no assumption about finite support of `A-1` was made.

## 2. Every nontrivial normal subgroup contains the kernel

Let `N` be normal in `H`. Both `N` and `L` are normal, so

`[N,L] <= N intersection L`.

Simplicity of `L` says that the intersection is either `1` or `L`.
In the first case `N` centralizes `L`, hence `N=1` by (1). In the
second case `L<=N`. This proves the unique-minimal-normal assertion.

It also gives `Z(H)=1` directly from (1). If `H=A x B` were a direct
product with both factors nontrivial, the two normal factors would
both contain `L` although their intersection is trivial. This proves
direct indecomposability.

## 3. Classify the quotient's normal subgroups

Each `Q_i` is infinite simple, so it is nonabelian and centerless.
Let `M` be normal in `Q_- x Q_+`. If an element `(a,b)` of `M` has
`a!=1`, some `x in Q_-` has `[a,x]!=1`. Commuting `(a,b)` with `(x,1)`
gives a nontrivial element of `M intersection (Q_- x 1)`. Simplicity
then gives `Q_- x 1<=M`. The same argument applies to the other factor.
Thus `M` is exactly the product of those factors on which its
projection is nontrivial. Its four possibilities are

`1`, `Q_- x 1`, `1 x Q_+`, `Q_- x Q_+`.

The correspondence theorem, together with section 2, now gives the
five asserted normal subgroups of `H`. The quotients by them are,
respectively,

| Kernel | Quotient |
| --- | --- |
| `1` | `H` |
| `L` | `Q_- x Q_+` |
| `N_-` | `Q_+` |
| `N_+` | `Q_-` |
| `H` | `1` |

Finite products of LEF groups are LEF: combine the finite partial
embeddings in the two factors. The proper quotients in the table are
therefore LEF and MF, and are Kazhdan as quotients of `H`. On the other
hand `Rad_MF(H)=L!=1`, so `H` itself is not MF. Finally the first
isomorphism theorem gives the homomorphic-image dichotomy.

## Scope

The normal lattice is an additional property of this concrete faithful
finitary extension. An abstract exact sequence with the same simple
kernel and quotient need not have trivial centralizer, so the quotient
calculation alone would not prove the theorem. The representation in
section 1 is the essential extra input.

The theorem is a relative minimality statement for MF approximability:
every nontrivial normal quotient removes the entire obstruction. It
does not assert that proper subgroups are MF, that `H` is simple, or
that any sofic or hyperlinear obstruction has been established here.
There is no global priority assertion. The proof has not been
formalized in Lean.
