# Prescribed Kazhdan coefficient for ordinary and projective transfer

Independent mathematical audit, 2026-09-07. The claim is an equivalence
of four still-conditional properties of a pair; its direct proof does
not assert RCC or nonhyperlinearity for an arithmetic group.

## What is added beyond the existing equivalences

The repository already contains
`rf-perfect-pair-transfer-equals-regular-rcc`, whose assumptions are
residual finiteness of the finitely presented actor and perfectness of
both groups. It obtains an unspecified linear coefficient by tensor
amplification. The present argument instead assumes a hyperlinear actor
and a Kazhdan subgroup, and prescribes the subgroup-energy coefficient

    K=4/kappa^2.

The presentation-defect modulus at this exact coefficient is obtained
by compactness and is not claimed effective or linear. At every strictly
larger projective coefficient, tensor amplification makes the defect
term linear, with the explicit inverse-modulus choice proved below.
Perfectness is unnecessary because matrix
doubling removes a scalar target commutator, and tensoring with complex
conjugate matrices converts projective energies to ordinary ones.

The existing exact finite-dimensional statement
`exact-projective-codensity-has-a-uniform-trace-square-gap` supplies
related Kazhdan estimates under perfectness. Here the conditional exact
estimate is proved for every CE ambient actor, and this larger scope
is what justifies compactness for arbitrary approximate assignments.

## Assumptions and precise normalization

Fix A=<X|R>, a finite presentation of a hyperlinear group; C=<S><=A;
and h in A such that A=<C,h>. Choose fixed X-words for each element of
S and for h. A Kazhdan constant kappa for S means

    max_(s in S)||beta(s)xi-xi|| >= kappa||xi||

for every vector perpendicular to the fixed subspace of every unitary
representation beta of C. Squaring and bounding the maximum by the sum
gives the energy convention used in the claim. No factor |S| enters
the upper bound with this convention.

Every matrix trace is normalized; matrix tensor products and block
doublings carry their normalized tensor and average traces. The ordinary
commutator is x v-v x. The projective word is the group commutator
U v U^* v^*. Their norms are related by

    ||U v-v U||_2=||U v U^* v^*-I||_2

when U,v are unitaries. The ordinary approximate inequality is asserted
only for contractions x. Scaling an arbitrary x would also scale the
necessary additive error, so omitting this restriction would change the
statement.

## Why actor regularization is legitimate

Suppose an exact actor rho:A->U(Q) has arbitrary trace, with Q Connes
embeddable, and x commutes with rho(C). It suffices to work in the
separable algebra generated by rho(A) and x. Hyperlinearity of A gives
the canonical group algebra L(A) a tracial embedding into a matrix
ultraproduct. Tensor products of finite moment models show that
Q tensor L(A) is Connes embeddable: the trace of a word of simple tensors
is the product of the corresponding traces, and finite sums can be
approximated in 2-norm. Finite moment diagonalization handles the
separable algebra needed here.

The actor a |-> rho(a) tensor lambda_A(a) has canonical trace, and

    [x tensor 1,rho(a) tensor lambda_A(a)]
       =[x,rho(a)] tensor lambda_A(a).

The commutator norms are therefore identical. Canonical RCC implies
RCC in Q for rho. This argument does not use residual finiteness, finite
quotients, or any property of the auxiliary element beyond its presence
in the CE ambient algebra.

## Exact estimate and the fixed coefficient

Conjugation by rho(C) is a genuine unitary representation on L^2(Q).
Its fixed subspace is L^2(rho(C)' cap Q). Let E be the trace-preserving
conditional expectation onto that commutant. For bounded x,

    ||x-E(x)||_2^2
      <= kappa^(-2) sum_(s in S)||x rho(s)-rho(s)x||_2^2.

RCC implies that E(x) also commutes with rho(h). Consequently

    ||x rho(h)-rho(h)x||_2
      =||(x-E(x))rho(h)-rho(h)(x-E(x))||_2
      <=2||x-E(x)||_2.

Squaring gives K=4/kappa^2. The argument is on L^2(Q), but does not
claim that B(L^2(Q)) is a finite or Connes-embeddable algebra. Only Q
needs to be CE. The Kazhdan representation is used for its Hilbert-space
spectral gap, not as an actor to which RCC is applied.

## Compactness preserves the coefficient

For delta>=0 let eta_o(delta) be the supremum over all finite-dimensional
assignments with defect at most delta and all contractions x of the
positive part of target ordinary energy minus K times subgroup ordinary
energy. The supremum is bounded by 4, is nondecreasing, and equals zero
at delta=0 by the exact estimate.

If it did not tend to zero at zero, choose a sequence with defining
relator defects tending to zero and excess bounded below by a fixed
positive number. In the tracial matrix ultraproduct the finite relators
hold exactly, so the actor assignment descends to an exact representation
of A. The contraction and all displayed 2-norms pass to the limit.
Its generated ambient algebra is CE because it is a separable subalgebra
of that matrix ultraproduct. The positive excess contradicts the exact
estimate. This proves the modulus with exactly K, without a slackening
of its subgroup coefficient.

No modulus can be computed merely from this compactness argument. It
also does not imply eta_o(delta)<=L delta with this prescribed K.

## Projectivization and phase removal

For an assignment rho and auxiliary unitary U, work in actual finite
matrices of dimension d^2:

    rho'(a)=rho(a) tensor conjugate(rho(a)),
    U'=U tensor conjugate(U).

Every word respects this operation. For a unitary matrix R,

    ||R tensor conjugate(R)-I||_2<=2||R-I||_2,

so the new actor defect is at most twice the original one. If
W=U rho(a) U^* rho(a)^*, the new ordinary commutator energy is

    ||W tensor conjugate(W)-I||_2^2=2-2|tr(W)|^2.

Apply the ordinary estimate and divide by two. This proves the
projective estimate with the same K and eta_p(delta)=eta_o(2delta)/2.
There is no perfectness requirement and no passage to an unrestricted
infinite-dimensional inner-superoperator algebra.

Conversely, let U centralize the subgroup in a matrix ultraproduct.
Apply the projective estimate to actor pi direct_sum pi and auxiliary
unitary U direct_sum I, using finite unitary representatives. The source
energies and relator defects vanish. The target group commutator is
W direct_sum I, whose normalized trace is (tau(W)+1)/2. Modulus one
forces tau(W)=1, then faithful trace forces W=I. Thus U centralizes h,
and hence all A because A=<C,h>.

Matrix doubling is permitted by the universal dimension quantifier. It
does not preserve a prescribed joint trace distribution for the auxiliary
unitary, and none is imposed in this theorem. In particular the theorem
does not assert phase removal inside one fixed joint model.

## A linear defect term at every strictly larger projective coefficient

Write the projective inequality at the prescribed coefficient as

    e<=Kq+eta_p(delta),

where 0<=e<=1 is the target projective energy, q is the sum of subgroup
projective energies, and delta is the ordinary unsquared relator defect.
Here eta_p can be the nondecreasing function eta_o(2delta)/2 already
constructed. Fix K'>K, and set

    r=K/K',   t=(1-r)/2.

Then 0<t<1/2 and 1-t-r=t. Choose any L>=1 with

    eta_p(1/L)<t^2.

We prove e<=K'q+L delta for every assignment and dimension. Suppose
instead that e>K'q+L delta. This implies e>0, q<e/K', and
delta<e/L<=1/L.

If e>t, the original excess satisfies

    e-Kq >= (1-r)e=2te>2t^2.

But it is at most eta_p(delta)<=eta_p(1/L)<t^2, a contradiction.

If 0<e<=t, take k=ceil(t/e), so t<=ke<=2t<=1. Replace both the actor
assignment and its auxiliary unitary by their k-fold diagonal tensor
powers. Every word tensors exactly. The target projective energy,
subgroup sum, and ordinary relator defect obey

    e'=1-(1-e)^k,   q'<=kq,   delta'<=k delta.

For the subgroup inequality use 1-(1-z)^k<=kz, separately for every
subgroup energy z. For the ordinary relator bound telescope the k
tensor factors. This is why delta is the unsquared HS defect here.
The elementary exponential estimates give

    e'>=1-exp(-ke)>=ke-(ke)^2/2>=ke(1-t).

Therefore

    e'-Kq'>=ke(1-t-r)=ke t>=t^2,
    delta'<ke/L<=2t/L<=1/L.

Applying the original projective inequality to this amplified finite
assignment contradicts eta_p(delta')<t^2. This completes both cases.
No additional restriction delta<delta_0 was used: every putative
violation automatically forces its defect into the required range.

Thus for every K'>K the coefficient L chosen from the modulus gives

    e<=K'q+L delta

uniformly over all assignments and dimensions. The value of L need not
be effectively recoverable from the presentation, since neither was
the initial compactness modulus. The conclusion is a linear defect
term at every strictly larger slope, and does not assert one at the
exact limiting slope K.

Conversely, suppose these linear bounds hold for every K'>K. Choose
L_m for K'=K+1/m. Since q<=|S|, every assignment satisfies

    e<=Kq+|S|/m+L_m delta.

The infimum over m of the last two terms is a nonnegative nondecreasing
modulus that vanishes at zero and tends to zero there. Hence the family
of larger-slope linear bounds recovers the original fixed-slope
qualitative inequality. This verifies the fourth equivalence directly.

## Arithmetic consequence and the remaining condition

For C=SL_n(Z), A=SL_n(Z[1/p]), n>=3, the actor is hyperlinear, the
subgroup has property (T), and one denominator diagonal together with C
generates A. The theorem therefore identifies canonical RCC with an
ordinary or projective transfer inequality of slope 4/kappa^2 for any
chosen Kazhdan constant in the stated convention. Equivalently, at
every strictly larger projective slope, it asks for a finite uniform
coefficient multiplying the unsquared defining-relator defect.

This is a prescribed quantitative target conditional on RCC. Proving
one of the equivalent transfer assertions for the arithmetic pair remains
necessary; neither the tensor regularization nor the Kazhdan projection
alone proves RCC.
