# sk-open-6 plan (open explorer, free taste): the object in one line

## Target
State and prove the note's theorem with no subshifts, clopen sets or crossed products.

- Let x: Z → A be a sequence over a finite alphabet. Let V = F_2[Z] be the space of finitely supported sequences, with basis δ_n.
- Let σ be the shift, σδ_n = δ_{n+1}. For a ∈ A, let D_a be multiplication by the indicator of a: D_a δ_n = [x_n = a] δ_n.
- Let R_x ⊆ End_{F_2}(V) be the ring generated by σ, σ^{-1} and the D_a.
- **Theorem (target form).** If x is uniformly recurrent and not periodic, then G_x = EL_3(R_x) is an infinite finitely generated simple group with property (T) that is LEF.
- **Sharpness.** G_x is always infinite. It is simple iff x is uniformly recurrent and not periodic.
  - If x is not uniformly recurrent, main's `aperiodic-subshift-ring-ideals-are-invariant-open-sets` gives a proper nonzero ideal, so a nontrivial proper normal subgroup.
  - If x is periodic of least period N, then R_x ≅ M_N(F_2[t,t^{-1}]), so G_x = SL_{3N}(F_2[t,t^{-1}]). This group is residually finite and infinite.

## Bridge (to be established)
R_x ≅ LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Z, where X is the orbit closure of x.
- The isomorphism is the orbit representation; it is faithful because the orbit is dense.
- So G_x = G_X, and R_x depends only on the set of finite words of x.

## Pattern-language proof
- The "window" operators D_P, for P a set of words of length 2k+1, replace clopen sets.
- Uniform recurrence replaces compactness.
- Non-periodicity gives separated occurrences of long words, and these replace "no periodic points + clopen partition".
- The finite models are the SAME construction on a circle. For a periodic y = w^∞ with the same (2k+1)-words as x and w primitive, R_y on F_2[Z/N] is M_N(F_2). So the models are EL_3(R_y) = SL_{3N}(F_2).
