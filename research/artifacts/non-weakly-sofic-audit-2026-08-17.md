> **Verdict.** The existence of an abstract non-weakly-sofic group is not presently proved or disproved in the literature I could verify through the date above. The uploaded repository does not prove such an example. Its strongest relevant results prove the existence of nonsofic groups and non-MF groups, but neither property implies failure of weak soficity. In fact, two constructions in the repository give counterexamples to those proposed implications:
>
> * the explicit non-MF group `E` is sofic, hence weakly sofic;
> * the Kun--Thom nonsofic wreath products are weakly sofic by Glebsky's extension theorem.
>
> What *is* known is an exact necessary-and-sufficient criterion due to Glebsky--Rivera. A non-weakly-sofic group exists exactly when a fixed finite product of conjugacy classes in a finitely generated free group has profinite closure escaping the normal subgroup generated by its factors. This document proves that criterion in full, derives the exact finite certificate that a successful construction must provide, and explains rigorously why the uploaded finite-quotient, non-MF, nonsofic, and commutant-wall arguments do not provide that certificate.

This is therefore not a disproof of the existence conjecture. It is:

1. a complete proof of the exact reduction of the conjecture to profinite topology;
2. a proof that the candidate deductions visible in the uploaded code and notes are invalid;
3. a precise specification of the missing theorem that would turn one of the candidates into a genuine non-weakly-sofic example.

## 1. Terminology and conventions

All groups below are abstract groups unless a topology is explicitly mentioned. The distinction matters: connected nonabelian Lie groups give non-approximable examples in a *topological* sense, but linear Lie groups are approximable as abstract groups. Thus a topological example such as `SO(3)` would not answer the question posed by this repository.

Let `H` be a group. A metric `d` on `H` is **bi-invariant** when

```math
d(ax,ay)=d(x,y)=d(xa,ya)
```

for all `a,x,y in H`. Equivalently, the length

```math
\ell(h):=d(h,1)
```

satisfies

```math
\ell(1)=0,\qquad \ell(h^{-1})=\ell(h),\qquad
\ell(uhu^{-1})=\ell(h),\qquad
\ell(hk)\leq \ell(h)+\ell(k).
```

### Definition 1.1: local metric model

Let `G` be a group, let `Phi subseteq G` be finite, and let `epsilon,alpha>0`. A map

```math
\theta:\Phi\longrightarrow H
```

into a finite group `H` with a bi-invariant metric `d` is a
`(Phi,epsilon,alpha)`-model when:

1. if `1 in Phi`, then `theta(1)=1`;
2. whenever `a,b,ab in Phi`,
   ```math
   d(\theta(a)\theta(b),\theta(ab))<\epsilon;
   ```
3. whenever `a in Phi` and `a != 1`,
   ```math
   d(\theta(a),1)>\alpha.
   ```

The second condition says that multiplication is locally accurate; the third says that nonidentity elements remain uniformly visible.

### Definition 1.2: weak soficity

A group `G` is **weakly sofic** if there exists a constant `alpha>0` such that, for every finite `Phi subseteq G` and every `epsilon>0`, there is a `(Phi,epsilon,alpha)`-model into some finite group equipped with some bi-invariant metric.

The finite group and its metric may depend on `Phi` and `epsilon`. The metric need not be Hamming and need not arise from a permutation representation.

### Definition 1.3: soficity

A group is **sofic** when the targets in Definition 1.2 can always be chosen to be finite symmetric groups with normalized Hamming metric. Consequently,

```math
\boxed{\text{sofic}\Longrightarrow\text{weakly sofic}.}
```

The reverse implication is false: Section 7.3 gives a weakly sofic nonsofic group.

### Definition 1.4: profinite closure

Let `F` be a group and `A subseteq F`. The **profinite closure** of `A`, denoted
`overline(A)^prof`, is characterized by

```math
x\in\overline A^{\mathrm{prof}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q(x)\in q(A)
\text{ for every homomorphism }q:F\to Q
\text{ with }Q\text{ finite}.
```

This characterization may be taken as the definition for the purposes of this document. If `x notin overline(A)^prof`, then some finite quotient separates `x` from all of `A` at once. Throughout, a **finite quotient** may be taken to mean a surjective homomorphism onto a finite group. Quantifying over all finite-target homomorphisms is equivalent, since the target can be replaced by the finite image.

For `g in F`, write

```math
[g]^F:=\{ugu^{-1}:u\in F\}
```

for its conjugacy class. For `g_1,...,g_k in F`, put

```math
P(g_1,\ldots,g_k):=[g_1]^F[g_2]^F\cdots[g_k]^F.
```

Thus `P(g_1,...,g_k)` consists of products of **exactly one** conjugate of each listed element, in the listed order. Repetitions and inverses may be inserted in the list when needed.

## 2. The Glebsky--Rivera criterion

We now prove the exact criterion. The proof is included rather than merely cited because it identifies the precise bounded-conjugacy certificate missing from the uploaded development.

Let `F=F(S)` be a free group on a finite basis `S`. Fix the word length `|.|` associated with `S`, and let

```math
B_R:=\{w\in F:|w|\leq R\}.
```

Let `N normal F`.

### Definition 2.1: finite separability of a normal subgroup

We call `N` **finitely separable** if there exists `delta>0` such that, for every integer `R>=0` and every `eta>0`, there are

* a finite group `H`,
* a bi-invariant metric `d` on `H`, and
* a homomorphism `phi:F->H`

such that for every `w in B_R`,

```math
\begin{cases}
d(\phi(w),1)<\eta,&w\in N,\\[2mm]
d(\phi(w),1)>\delta,&w\notin N.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.1}
```

The constant `delta` is independent of `R` and `eta`.

### Lemma 2.2

For a finitely generated free group `F` and `N normal F`, the quotient `F/N` is weakly sofic if and only if `N` is finitely separable.

#### Proof: weak soficity implies finite separability

Let

```math
\pi:F\longrightarrow F/N
```

be the quotient map, and suppose `F/N` is weakly sofic with separation constant `alpha>0`.

Fix `R>=1` and `eta>0`. Choose `rho>0` so small that

```math
2R\rho<\eta
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2R\rho<\frac{\alpha}{2}.
\tag{2.2}
```

Set

```math
\Phi:=\pi(B_R)\subseteq F/N.
```

Choose a `(Phi,rho,alpha)`-model

```math
\theta:\Phi\longrightarrow H
```

into a finite bi-invariant metric group `(H,d)`.

Define a homomorphism `phi:F->H` by assigning to every free generator `s in S`

```math
\phi(s):=\theta(\pi(s)).
```

The universal property of the free group gives a unique homomorphism with these values.

We claim that, for every nonempty reduced word `w` of length at most `R`,

```math
d(\phi(w),\theta(\pi(w)))<(2|w|-1)\rho.
\tag{2.3}
```

Write `w=t_1...t_m`, where every `t_j` is a generator or inverse generator, and let `p_j=t_1...t_j`. If `t_j=s` is a positive generator, then

```math
\phi(t_j)=\theta(\pi(t_j)).
```

If `t_j=s^{-1}`, then local multiplicativity and `theta(1)=1` give

```math
\begin{aligned}
d(\phi(t_j),\theta(\pi(t_j)))
&=d(\theta(\pi(s))^{-1},\theta(\pi(s^{-1})))\\
&=d(1,\theta(\pi(s))\theta(\pi(s^{-1})))\\
&<\rho.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
```

Every `pi(p_j)` belongs to `Phi`. Using bi-invariance and the local multiplicativity of `theta`, we obtain the recurrence

```math
\begin{aligned}
d(\phi(p_j),\theta(\pi(p_j)))
&\leq d(\phi(p_{j-1}),\theta(\pi(p_{j-1})))\\
&\quad+d(\phi(t_j),\theta(\pi(t_j)))+\rho.
\end{aligned}
```

The first step costs at most `rho`, and each later step costs at most `2rho`. Induction gives (2.3).

If `w in N cap B_R`, then `pi(w)=1`, so (2.3) and (2.2) imply

```math
d(\phi(w),1)<2R\rho<\eta.
```

If `w in B_R\setminus N`, then `pi(w)!=1`, and therefore

```math
\begin{aligned}
d(\phi(w),1)
&\geq d(\theta(\pi(w)),1)
      -d(\phi(w),\theta(\pi(w)))\\
&>\alpha-2R\rho\\
&>\frac{\alpha}{2}.
\end{aligned}
```

Thus (2.1) holds with the uniform constant

```math
\delta=\frac{\alpha}{2}.
```

The case `R=0` is immediate. Hence `N` is finitely separable.

#### Proof: finite separability implies weak soficity

Conversely, suppose `N` is finitely separable with constant `delta>0`. Let
`Phi subseteq F/N` be finite and let `epsilon>0`.

Choose, for each `a in Phi`, a word `w_a in F` satisfying `pi(w_a)=a`. If
`1 in Phi`, choose `w_1=1`. Put

```math
R:=\max_{a\in\Phi}|w_a|.
```

Finite separability, applied with radius `3R` and tolerance `epsilon`, gives a finite bi-invariant metric group `(H,d)` and a homomorphism

```math
\phi:F\longrightarrow H
```

satisfying (2.1). Define

```math
\theta(a):=\phi(w_a),\qquad a\in\Phi.
```

If `a!=1`, then `w_a notin N`, so

```math
d(\theta(a),1)>\delta.
```

If `a,b,ab in Phi`, then

```math
w_aw_bw_{ab}^{-1}\in N
```

and its length is at most `3R`. Hence

```math
\begin{aligned}
d(\theta(a)\theta(b),\theta(ab))
&=d(\phi(w_a)\phi(w_b),\phi(w_{ab}))\\
&=d(\phi(w_aw_bw_{ab}^{-1}),1)\\
&<\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
```

Also `theta(1)=1`. Thus `theta` is a `(Phi,epsilon,delta)`-model. The separation constant `delta` is independent of `Phi` and `epsilon`, so `F/N` is weakly sofic. ∎

### Lemma 2.3

The normal subgroup `N` is finitely separable if and only if, for every finite sequence `g_1,...,g_k in N`,

```math
\boxed{
\overline{[g_1]^F\cdots[g_k]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}\subseteq N.
}
\tag{2.5}
```

#### Proof: finite separability implies the closure condition

Assume finite separability with constant `delta>0`. Fix

```math
g_1,\ldots,g_k\in N
```

and `x notin N`. We must find a finite quotient `q:F->Q` such that

```math
q(x)\notin[q(g_1)]^Q\cdots[q(g_k)]^Q.
\tag{2.6}
```

Choose `R` larger than the lengths of `x,g_1,...,g_k`. Apply finite separability with tolerance

```math
\eta:=\frac{\delta}{2k}
```

when `k>=1`. We obtain `q:F->Q` and a bi-invariant metric `d` on `Q` such that

```math
d(q(x),1)>\delta
\quad\text{and}\quad
d(q(g_i),1)<\frac{\delta}{2k}.
```

If (2.6) failed, there would be `u_1,...,u_k in Q` with

```math
q(x)=u_1q(g_1)u_1^{-1}\cdots u_kq(g_k)u_k^{-1}.
```

Conjugacy invariance and the triangle inequality would give

```math
\begin{aligned}
d(q(x),1)
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^k d(u_iq(g_i)u_i^{-1},1)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^k d(q(g_i),1)\\
&<\frac{\delta}{2},
\end{aligned}
```

contradicting `d(q(x),1)>delta`. Therefore (2.6) holds, and the finite-quotient characterization of profinite closure yields (2.5).

For the empty sequence, the product is `{1}`; residual finiteness of the free group separates any `x!=1` from it.

#### Proof: the closure condition implies finite separability

Assume (2.5). Fix a radius `R` and a tolerance `eta>0`. Let

```math
X:=N\cap B_R,
\qquad
Y:=B_R\setminus N.
```

Both sets are finite, and `X=X^{-1}`. Choose an integer `n>=1` such that

```math
\frac1n<\eta.
\tag{2.7}
```

For every `y in Y`, every integer `0<=m<=n`, and every sequence

```math
x_1,\ldots,x_m\in X,
```

condition (2.5) implies

```math
y\notin
\overline{[x_1]^F\cdots[x_m]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}.
```

Therefore some homomorphism to a finite group separates `y` from this product. There are only finitely many choices of `y,m,x_1,...,x_m`. Taking the diagonal product of all the corresponding finite quotients gives a single homomorphism to a finite group

```math
q:F\longrightarrow H.
```

If surjectivity is part of the convention for "finite quotient," replace `H` by the finite image `q(F)`; all the nonmembership statements below remain valid because conjugacy classes can only shrink. Thus, simultaneously for every one of those choices,

```math
q(y)\notin[q(x_1)]^H\cdots[q(x_m)]^H.
\tag{2.8}
```

For `m=0`, equation (2.8) says `q(y)!=1`; residual finiteness of `F` supplies these finitely many additional quotient factors.

Now define the conjugation-invariant symmetric subset

```math
C:=\bigcup_{x\in X}[q(x)]^H\subseteq H.
```

For `h in H`, let `L_C(h)` be the least nonnegative integer `m` for which

```math
h=c_1\cdots c_m,
\qquad c_i\in C,
```

and put `L_C(h)=infinity` if no such expression exists. Define

```math
\ell(h):=
\begin{cases}
\min\{L_C(h)/n,1\},&L_C(h)<\infty,\\
1,&L_C(h)=\infty.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.9}
```

We verify that `ell` is an invariant length.

* `ell(1)=0`, because `L_C(1)=0`.
* If `h!=1`, then either `L_C(h)>=1` or `L_C(h)=infinity`; hence `ell(h)>0`.
* Since `C=C^{-1}`, one has `L_C(h^{-1})=L_C(h)`.
* Since `C` is a union of conjugacy classes,
  `L_C(uhu^{-1})=L_C(h)`.
* Concatenating expressions gives
  ```math
  L_C(hk)\leq L_C(h)+L_C(k)
  ```
  in the extended nonnegative integers. Since
  ```math
  \min\{a+b,1\}\leq\min\{a,1\}+\min\{b,1\},
  ```
  equation (2.9) gives
  ```math
  \ell(hk)\leq\ell(h)+\ell(k).
  ```

Consequently

```math
d(a,b):=\ell(a^{-1}b)
\tag{2.10}
```

is a bi-invariant metric on `H`.

If `x in X`, then `q(x) in C`, so

```math
d(q(x),1)=\ell(q(x))\leq\frac1n<\eta.
\tag{2.11}
```

If `y in Y` and `L_C(q(y))<=n`, then `q(y)` would be a product of at most `n` conjugates of elements `q(x)` with `x in X`, contradicting (2.8). Thus

```math
L_C(q(y))>n
\quad\text{or}\quad
L_C(q(y))=\infty,
```

and therefore

```math
d(q(y),1)=\ell(q(y))=1.
\tag{2.12}
```

Equations (2.11)--(2.12) prove finite separability with any fixed constant smaller than `1`, for example `delta=1/2`. ∎

### Theorem 2.4: Glebsky--Rivera

Let `F` be a finitely generated free group and `N normal F`. Then

```math
\boxed{
F/N\text{ is weakly sofic}
\iff
\forall k\geq0\ \forall g_1,\ldots,g_k\in N,
\quad
\overline{[g_1]^F\cdots[g_k]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}
\subseteq N.
}
\tag{2.13}
```

#### Proof

Combine Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.3. ∎

The metric constructed in the reverse direction is worth emphasizing. It is not Hamming distance. It is a capped, rescaled word metric in the conjugation-invariant error set `C`:

```math
\ell(h)=\min\{L_C(h)/n,1\}.
```

This freedom to manufacture an arbitrary invariant metric is exactly why a nonsoficity argument based on permutation geometry does not automatically obstruct weak soficity.

## 3. The exact existence certificate

The preceding theorem immediately turns the existence problem into a finite-word problem.

### Theorem 3.1

The following statements are equivalent.

1. There exists a non-weakly-sofic abstract group.
2. There exists a finitely presented non-weakly-sofic group.
3. There exist
   * a free group `F=F(s_1,...,s_r)` of finite rank,
   * words `g_1,...,g_k,x in F`,

   such that, with

   ```math
   N_0:=\langle\!\langle g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle\!\rangle_F,
   ```

   one has

   ```math
   x\notin N_0,
   \tag{3.1}
   ```

   but for every homomorphism `q:F->Q` to a finite group,

   ```math
   q(x)\in[q(g_1)]^Q\cdots[q(g_k)]^Q.
   \tag{3.2}
   ```

When these conditions hold, the finite presentation

```math
G=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_r\mid g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle
\tag{3.3}
```

is non-weakly-sofic.

#### Proof

`(3) implies (2)`. Condition (3.2) is exactly the assertion

```math
x\in\overline{[g_1]^F\cdots[g_k]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}.
```

Each `g_i` lies in `N_0`, while (3.1) says `x notin N_0`. Therefore the containment in Theorem 2.4 fails for `F/N_0`. The group in (3.3) is finitely presented and non-weakly-sofic.

`(2) implies (1)` is immediate.

`(1) implies (3)`. Suppose `G` is non-weakly-sofic. First observe that the separation constant in Definition 1.2 can always be normalized to `1`: if a group has models with separation `alpha>0`, request multiplicative error `alpha*epsilon` and rescale the target metric by `1/alpha`. Therefore, if every finitely generated subgroup of `G` were weakly sofic, then for any finite `Phi subseteq G` the subgroup generated by `Phi` would supply a model with the common separation constant `1`; this would make `G` weakly sofic. Hence `G` has a finitely generated non-weakly-sofic subgroup. Replacing `G` by that subgroup, write

```math
G=F/N
```

with `F` a free group of finite rank. By Theorem 2.4 there are

```math
g_1,\ldots,g_k\in N
```

and

```math
x\in
\overline{[g_1]^F\cdots[g_k]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}
\setminus N.
```

Let `N_0=<<g_1,...,g_k>>`. Since `N_0 subseteq N`, the condition `x notin N` implies `x notin N_0`. The same profinite-closure membership remains true, because it depends only on `F,x,g_1,...,g_k`. Thus (3.1)--(3.2) hold. ∎

### What must be uniform

Condition (3.2) has a subtle but decisive uniformity feature:

* the finite quotient `Q` may vary;
* the conjugating elements in `Q` may vary;
* but the **number and ordered list of conjugacy-class factors are fixed once and for all**.

It is not enough to know merely that `q(x)` lies in the normal closure of the `q(g_i)` for each finite quotient. Normal-closure membership allows the required number of conjugates to grow without bound as `Q` changes. Theorem 2.4 needs a fixed finite product.

This is the exact distinction that defeats the finite-quotient route in the uploaded non-MF construction.

## 4. Why the known nonclosed conjugacy products do not settle the problem

There are two different profinite questions:

1. Is a finite product of conjugacy classes itself closed?
2. Is its profinite closure contained in the normal subgroup generated by the factors?

A negative answer to the first question does not imply a negative answer to the second.

For example, results of Nikolov--Segal imply that certain finite products of conjugacy classes in a nonabelian free group are not profinitely closed. A standard instance uses the conjugacy classes of the basis elements and their inverses; the closure contains the whole commutator subgroup even though the product has bounded commutator width. But the basis elements normally generate the whole free group. Therefore

```math
\langle\!\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle\!\rangle=F,
```

so the desired containment in the normal closure is vacuous. That nonclosedness cannot produce an `x` satisfying (3.1).

*(Filing note: this paragraph is the audit's own reading and was not source-verified. The primary source supplies a sharper and fully verified near-miss; see Section 13.3.)*

The unresolved question is the stronger one:

> Do there exist `g_1,...,g_k` in a finitely generated free group `F` such that
> ```math
> \overline{[g_1]^F\cdots[g_k]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}
> \not\subseteq
> \langle\!\langle g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle\!\rangle?
> ```

Glebsky--Rivera proved that a positive answer is equivalent to existence of a non-weakly-sofic group. Andreas Thom's survey records that products can be nonclosed but that escape from the normal closure remained open. The August 2026 literature checked for this audit contains new nonsofic groups and weakly-sofic nonsofic groups, but no verified resolution of this stronger containment problem. Contemporary expert commentary likewise describes weak soficity as an approximation property for which nonexamples remain to be found.

Thus the honest research-status statement is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{No abstract non-weakly-sofic group is currently verified, and no theorem that all groups are weakly sofic is verified.}
}
```

This statement is date-sensitive. It records the literature search performed for this audit, not a claim that future work cannot settle the problem.

## 5. Audit of the uploaded Lean development, notes, and TeX

The repository contains a substantial and unusually explicit development of soficity, nonsoficity, MF approximation, finite-dimensional invisibility, and Clifford/commutant obstructions. The issue is not absence of hard mathematics; it is a mismatch between the approximation class formalized under the name `WeakSoficModel` and Glebsky--Rivera weak soficity.

### 5.1 The implemented `WeakSoficModel` is still a permutation model

In

`GroupApproximation/Sofic/Sofic.lean:171-184`,

`SoficModel` consists of:

* a finite carrier;
* a map `G -> Equiv.Perm carrier`;
* normalized Hamming multiplicativity;
* normalized Hamming separation.

In

`GroupApproximation/Sofic/SoficAmplification.lean:140-154`,

`WeakSoficModel` again has target

```lean
Equiv.Perm carrier
```

and again uses Hamming distance. The only weakening is that separation is required to exceed a fixed constant `delta`, rather than `1-epsilon`.

The same file then proves:

* `isSofic_iff_weak` at lines 272-277;
* `isSofic_iff_weakLocal` at lines 279-283;
* `isSofic_iff_weak_quarter` at lines 285-287.

Those theorems are mathematically appropriate for the fixed-separation convention for ordinary soficity: tensor powers amplify any positive Hamming separation toward `1`. But this is **not** Glebsky--Rivera weak soficity. The latter permits arbitrary finite target groups and arbitrary bi-invariant metrics, and tensor-power Hamming amplification need not apply.

A less misleading name for the current structure would be

```text
FixedSeparationSoficModel
```

or

```text
HammingWeakSeparationModel.
```

No declaration found in the development formalizes the target class

```text
all finite groups with all bi-invariant metrics.
```

### 5.2 The endpoint proves nonsoficity, not non-weak-soficity

`GroupApproximation/Endpoint/MainResults.lean:205-225` defines and proves:

```lean
def NonsoficGroupExists : Prop :=
  ∃ (G : Type) (_ : Group G), ¬ IsSofic G
```

and its countable analogue. These are genuine nonsofic endpoints relative to the repository's `IsSofic` predicate. They do not mention arbitrary finite groups with arbitrary invariant metrics, so they do not imply a non-weakly-sofic endpoint.

This distinction is now demonstrably substantive, not merely terminological: Section 7.3 proves that some nonsofic groups are weakly sofic.

### 5.3 The TeX manuscript explicitly makes the non-MF witness sofic

The abstract of `non_mf_groups_exist.tex`, lines 150-178, constructs an explicit finitely presented non-MF group `E` and states at lines 173-174:

```text
The group E is sofic, hence hyperlinear.
```

The detailed theorem at lines 1252-1273 states that `E` is finitely presented, sofic, hyperlinear, non-MF, non-LEF, and non-residually finite. The README repeats the soficity assertion at lines 103-105 and warns at lines 115-124 that the operator-norm obstruction makes no additional sofic or hyperlinear classification claim.

Since every sofic group is weakly sofic,

```math
\boxed{E\text{ is weakly sofic}.}
```

Therefore `E` cannot be a witness to the requested theorem.

### 5.4 The exact finite-quotient kill theorem is real but insufficient

`GroupApproximation/Sofic/LiteralFiniteDimensionalObstruction.lean:55-78` proves that every homomorphism from the literal marked group to a finite group kills the marked element. The TeX version appears at `non_mf_groups_exist.tex:2068-2087`:

* `w != 1` in `E`;
* every finite-dimensional linear representation kills `w`;
* every homomorphism to a finite group kills `w`.

This is a strong, exact failure of residual finiteness. It says that a lift of `w` belongs to the profinite closure of the full relation subgroup. It does **not** say that the lift belongs to the profinite closure of one fixed bounded product of conjugacy classes. Section 7.1 proves this distinction precisely and shows that no such bounded certificate can exist for `E`, because `E` is weakly sofic.

### 5.5 The finite universal Horn obstruction is MF-specific

At `non_mf_groups_exist.tex:3031-3059`, the manuscript exhibits a finite quasi-identity

```math
\bigwedge_{r\in R}r(x_1,\ldots,x_8)=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
w(x_1,\ldots,x_8)=1
```

which holds in every MF group but fails on the canonical tuple of `E`.

Because finite groups are MF, this sentence holds in all finite groups. It still does not obstruct weak soficity. Weakly sofic groups embed in quotients of direct products of finite groups; universal Horn sentences are preserved by subgroups and direct products, but not by arbitrary quotients. The quotient step is exactly where approximate satisfaction of the relators can coexist with survival of the marked word.

Again, `E` itself is the concrete counterexample to the proposed inference: it violates the displayed quasi-identity and is nevertheless weakly sofic.

### 5.6 The repository already proves a weakly sofic nonsofic calibration example

The notes

* `research/weakly-sofic-not-sofic-proof.md:12-34`, and
* `research/kun-thom-clifford-cover-weakly-sofic-proof.md:12-30`

correctly combine:

1. Glebsky's theorem that an extension with weakly sofic normal subgroup and residually finite quotient is weakly sofic;
2. Kun--Thom's nonsofic wreath-product theorem.

The resulting wreath product is weakly sofic and nonsofic. The longer note

`notes/TRUE_RELATIVE_WALL_IS_COMMUTANT_GROWTH.md:1106-1147`

proves the same calibration for the Clifford extension `E_S`: Theorem 9 says it is weakly sofic and nonsofic, and Corollary 10 realizes the Clifford wall inside a metric ultraproduct of finite groups with bi-invariant metrics.

This is decisive for the requested problem. Any proposed obstruction that uses only the abstract Clifford relations, compression relations, or commutant wall also holds in a known weakly sofic group. Such an obstruction cannot by itself prove non-weak-soficity; it must use a restriction on the metric geometry of the targets, and weak soficity intentionally permits arbitrary invariant metrics.

### 5.7 Bounded conjugacy products are recognized, but no weak-sofic certificate is proved


with `x notin <<g_1,...,g_k>>`. Without such a theorem there is no Glebsky--Rivera witness.

## 6. A precise analysis of the non-MF witness `E`

This section turns the preceding audit into a theorem. It explains exactly what the finite-quotient kill result means in profinite topology and exactly why it stops short of non-weak-soficity.

Let

```math
E=F/N
```

be the finite presentation from the manuscript, where `F=F_8` is free on the eight marked generators. Let `tilde w in F` be the distinguished word and let `w=tilde w N in E`.

The repository proves:

1. `w!=1` in `E`, so `tilde w notin N`;
2. every homomorphism `E->Q` to a finite group sends `w` to `1`;
3. `E` is sofic.

### Proposition 6.1

The word `tilde w` belongs to the profinite closure of `N` in `F`:

```math
\widetilde w\in\overline N^{\mathrm{prof}}\setminus N.
\tag{6.1}
```

#### Proof

Only membership in the closure needs proof. Let `q:F->Q` be any homomorphism to a finite group. Replace `Q` by the finite image `q(F)`, so that `q` is surjective. Since `N normal F`, the subgroup `q(N)` is then normal in `Q`. There is an induced homomorphism

```math
\bar q:E=F/N\longrightarrow Q/q(N),
\qquad
fN\longmapsto q(f)q(N).
```

Every finite-target homomorphism from `E` kills `w`. Therefore

```math
q(\widetilde w)q(N)=\bar q(w)=1,
```

so `q(tilde w) in q(N)`. Since this holds for every finite quotient `q`, the finite-quotient characterization of profinite closure gives (6.1). ∎

Thus the finite-quotient theorem says that `N` is not closed in the profinite topology. That is not yet a weak-sofic obstruction.

Let the finite relator set be

```math
R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_t\},
\qquad
N=\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle_F.
```

For each integer `m>=0`, define the set of products using **at most** `m` relator conjugates by

```math
P_m:=
\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}
\bigcup_{(a_1,\ldots,a_j)\in(R\cup R^{-1})^j}
[a_1]^F\cdots[a_j]^F,
\tag{6.2}
```

where the `j=0` product is `{1}`.

Then

```math
P_0\subseteq P_1\subseteq P_2\subseteq\cdots,
\qquad
N=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}P_m.
\tag{6.3}
```

The set `P_m` consists of elements admitting a relator-area expression with at most `m` conjugacy factors.

### Proposition 6.2

For every fixed `m`,

```math
\widetilde w\notin\overline{P_m}^{\mathrm{prof}},
\tag{6.4}
```

but

```math
\widetilde w\in
\overline{\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}P_m}^{\mathrm{prof}}.
\tag{6.5}
```

#### Proof

Because `E` is sofic, it is weakly sofic. Theorem 2.4 therefore gives

```math
\overline{[a_1]^F\cdots[a_m]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}
\subseteq N
```

for every fixed sequence `a_1,...,a_m in N`. For fixed `m`, the union in (6.2) is finite, because `R` is finite. Closure commutes with finite unions, so

```math
\overline{P_m}^{\mathrm{prof}}
\subseteq N.
```

Since `tilde w notin N`, equation (6.4) follows.

On the other hand, equations (6.1) and (6.3) give

```math
\widetilde w\in\overline N^{\mathrm{prof}}
=
\overline{\bigcup_mP_m}^{\mathrm{prof}},
```

which is (6.5). ∎

### Corollary 6.3: the conjugacy width is necessarily unbounded

For every fixed `m`, there exists a finite quotient

```math
q_m:F\longrightarrow Q_m
```

such that

```math
q_m(\widetilde w)\notin q_m(P_m).
\tag{6.6}
```

Nevertheless, for every finite quotient `q:F->Q`, one has

```math
q(\widetilde w)\in q(N)=\bigcup_mq(P_m).
\tag{6.7}
```

Hence the minimum number of conjugates of relators needed to express
`q(tilde w)` inside `q(N)` cannot be bounded independently of the finite quotient.

#### Proof

Equation (6.6) is the finite-quotient characterization of (6.4). Equation (6.7) is Proposition 6.1 together with (6.3). If a uniform bound existed, then `q(tilde w) in q(P_m)` for one fixed `m` and every finite `q`, contradicting (6.6). ∎

This is the exact mathematical obstruction to converting the repository's finite-quotient invisibility into non-weak-soficity. The candidate `E` does not merely fail to establish the needed uniform bound; its proven weak soficity implies that **no such uniform bound is true**.

## 7. Rigorous disproof of the proposed implication routes

The uploaded materials suggest several possible bridges to non-weak-soficity. Each bridge can now be decided exactly.

### 7.1 `non-MF => non-weakly-sofic` is false

The group `E` is non-MF by the main theorem of `non_mf_groups_exist.tex`, and it is sofic by Theorem `thm:Esofic`. Therefore

```math
E\text{ is non-MF and weakly sofic}.
```

So failure of operator-norm matrix approximation does not imply failure of approximation by arbitrary finite metric groups.

The geometric reason is clear. MF approximation uses unitary matrices with operator norm; weak soficity allows every finite group and every bi-invariant metric. An obstruction that depends on spectral projections, normalized Hilbert--Schmidt commutants, or operator-norm perturbation need not persist for a capped conjugacy-word metric on an abstract finite group.

### 7.2 `a nontrivial element killed by every finite quotient => non-weakly-sofic` is false

The same group `E` supplies the counterexample. Its nontrivial mark `w` is killed by every homomorphism to a finite group, but `E` is sofic and hence weakly sofic.

Propositions 6.1--6.2 identify the exact logical error:

```math
\widetilde w\in\overline N^{\mathrm{prof}}\setminus N
```

is compatible with

```math
\overline{[g_1]^F\cdots[g_k]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}
\subseteq N
```

for every **fixed** finite list `g_1,...,g_k in N`. Profinite closure does not commute with the increasing countable union of bounded-area relation products.

### 7.3 `nonsofic => non-weakly-sofic` is false

We give the proof because it is a particularly important post-2026 calibration.

Let `Gamma<G` be one of the residually finite Kazhdan pairs in Kun--Thom's Theorem E, and put

```math
X=G/\Gamma,
\qquad
B=\bigoplus_X\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z,
\qquad
W=B\rtimes G.
\tag{7.1}
```

Kun--Thom prove that `W` is nonsofic.

On the other hand, `B` is residually finite. Indeed, a nonidentity element of the restricted direct sum has finite nonempty support, and projection to one occupied coordinate detects it in `Z/2Z`. Therefore `B` is sofic and hence weakly sofic.

The quotient in the exact sequence

```math
1\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow1
\tag{7.2}
```

is residually finite by Kun--Thom's construction. Glebsky's extension theorem states that if the normal subgroup in such an exact sequence is weakly sofic and the quotient is residually finite, then the middle group is weakly sofic. Applying it to (7.2) gives

```math
W\text{ is weakly sofic}.
```

Combining the two conclusions,

```math
\boxed{W\text{ is weakly sofic and nonsofic}.}
\tag{7.3}
```

Consequently

```math
\boxed{\text{sofic groups form a proper subclass of weakly sofic groups}.}
```

This also shows why the repository's new nonsofic endpoints cannot be promoted to non-weakly-sofic endpoints merely by weakening the target theorem's name.

### 7.4 `violation of a finite quasi-identity valid in all finite groups => non-weakly-sofic` is false

The `E` presentation gives a finite quasi-identity valid in every MF group and therefore in every finite group, but violated by `E`. Since `E` is weakly sofic, the implication is false.

At the structural level, a weakly sofic group is isomorphic to a subgroup of a quotient of a direct product of finite groups. Quasi-identities are preserved under direct products and subgroups, but not under quotient groups. The metric-zero normal subgroup used in the quotient can make the antecedent relators exact in the quotient while leaving the conclusion word nontrivial.

The Glebsky--Rivera condition is stronger and differently shaped. It is an existential statement in every finite quotient with a **fixed bounded number of conjugators**, not merely a quasi-identity.

### 7.5 `the Clifford/commutant wall => non-weakly-sofic` is false

The repository itself constructs the calibration group

```math
E_S=\widetilde V_S\rtimes G
```

and proves in `notes/TRUE_RELATIVE_WALL_IS_COMMUTANT_GROWTH.md`, Theorem 9, that it is weakly sofic and nonsofic. Corollary 10 realizes the same marked Clifford wall in a metric ultraproduct of finite groups with arbitrary bi-invariant metrics.

Therefore the abstract wall is realizable in the weak-sofic category. A valid non-weak-sofic argument must exploit a metric inequality that holds for **every** invariant metric on **every** finite group. The trace and Pimsner--Popa index arguments in the MF/hyperlinear setting do not supply such an inequality.

### 7.6 `same finite quotients as a bad group => non-weakly-sofic` is unsupported

Weak soficity is not determined merely by the collection of exact homomorphisms to finite groups. It concerns approximate homomorphisms together with freely chosen invariant metrics. The `ProfiniteTwins.lean` results show that exact finite representation data can remain unchanged while MF status changes. That is evidence against, not for, a naive finite-quotient classification of metric approximation properties.

What finite quotients do determine in Theorem 3.1 is the profinite closure of one **specified bounded product** of conjugacy classes. The bounded product is extra data absent from an unstructured comparison of finite quotient categories.

### 7.7 Implication table

| Proposed implication | Status | Counterexample or reason |
|---|---|---|
| non-MF `=>` non-weakly-sofic | False | `E` is non-MF and sofic |
| nonsofic `=>` non-weakly-sofic | False | Kun--Thom wreath `W` is nonsofic and weakly sofic |
| non-residually-finite `=>` non-weakly-sofic | False | `E` is not residually finite and is sofic |
| one nontrivial element dies in all finite quotients `=>` non-weakly-sofic | False | the mark of `E` |
| finite quasi-identity obstruction `=>` non-weakly-sofic | False | the quasi-identity of `E` |
| abstract Clifford wall `=>` non-weakly-sofic | False | the weakly sofic group `E_S` realizes the wall |
| fixed profinite conjugacy-product escape `=>` non-weakly-sofic | **True** | Theorem 3.1 |

## 8. The attempted upgrade, isolated as one missing lemma

Suppose one starts with a finite presentation

```math
G=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_r\mid R\rangle=F/N
```

and a word `x in F` that survives in `G` but is invisible in every finite quotient of `G`. Then

```math
x\notin N,
\qquad
q(x)\in q(N)
\quad\text{for every finite }q:F\to Q.
\tag{8.1}
```

Because `N` is normally generated by the finite set `R`, equation (8.1) says that, for each finite quotient `q`, there is some integer `m(q)` and some sequence of relators or inverse relators

```math
a_1(q),\ldots,a_{m(q)}(q)\in R\cup R^{-1}
```

such that

```math
q(x)\in[q(a_1(q))]^Q\cdots[q(a_{m(q)}(q))]^Q.
\tag{8.2}
```

The quotient-dependent expression (8.2) is automatic. The missing theorem is a uniformization:

> **Required bounded-width lemma.** There are one integer `k` and one fixed list
> ```math
> g_1,\ldots,g_k\in N
> ```
> such that, for every finite quotient `q:F->Q`,
> ```math
> q(x)\in[q(g_1)]^Q\cdots[q(g_k)]^Q.
> \tag{8.3}
> ```

Together with `x notin <<g_1,...,g_k>>`, equation (8.3) would prove that

```math
F/\langle\!\langle g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle\!\rangle
```

is non-weakly-sofic.

For the group `E`, Proposition 6.2 proves that no bounded-width lemma of this form can hold. Therefore any attempt to obtain (8.3) from the finite-dimensional kill theorem for `E` must contain a false step.

For the nonsofic Leavitt groups in `Endpoint/MainResults.lean`, the repository proves nonsoficity by permutation/Hamming rigidity. It does not prove either side of the needed pair:

1. a specific word `x` outside a finitely normally generated subgroup;
2. uniform membership (8.3) in every finite quotient.

The files about bounded normal generation in alternating groups concern transfer inside permutation ultraproducts. They do not establish (8.3) for all finite groups, whose invariant metrics may be unrelated to Hamming geometry.

### 8.1 A useful reformulation as a finite-group sentence

Condition (8.3) says that every finite group satisfies the implication

```math
\left(\text{the chosen values are images of the free generators}\right)
\Longrightarrow
\exists u_1,\ldots,u_k\quad
x=\prod_{i=1}^k u_i g_i u_i^{-1}.
\tag{8.4}
```

The existential variables `u_i` may depend on the finite group and on the chosen generator values, but their number is fixed. The presented group violates the conclusion because `x` lies outside the normal closure of the `g_i`.

This is not a universal Horn obstruction. It is a uniform finite existential-conjugacy consequence. That logical form is exactly what the capped conjugacy-word metric converts into weak-sofic approximations.

### 8.2 Why compactness does not automatically give the bound

It is tempting to argue as follows:

* in every finite quotient, `q(x)` belongs to the union of all bounded relator products;
* therefore one bounded product should work uniformly.

This is invalid. The sets `P_m` of (6.2) form an increasing sequence, and

```math
N=\bigcup_mP_m.
```

Profinite closure need not commute with an increasing countable union:

```math
\overline{\bigcup_mP_m}^{\mathrm{prof}}
\quad\text{may strictly contain}\quad
\bigcup_m\overline{P_m}^{\mathrm{prof}}.
```

The group `E` proves that this strictness actually occurs in the exact setting at issue. The mark lies in the left-hand side and in none of the terms on the right-hand side.

A compactness argument would work only after proving an additional closedness or bounded-width theorem for the normal closure inside the profinite completion. That additional theorem is essentially the open problem itself.

*(Filing note: the primary source states exactly this compactness dichotomy as its Remark 4.8; see Section 13.2.)*

## 9. What would constitute a genuine proof or a genuine disproof

### 9.1 A proof that a non-weakly-sofic group exists

It is enough to provide explicit reduced words

```math
g_1,\ldots,g_k,x\in F_r
```

and prove the following two statements.

#### Algebraic survival

```math
x\notin\langle\!\langle g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle\!\rangle.
\tag{9.1}
```

Equivalently, `x` is nontrivial in the finitely presented group

```math
\langle s_1,\ldots,s_r\mid g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle.
```

This part might be proved by a normal-form theorem, an action, a representation, a small-cancellation argument, a homological invariant, or an explicit quotient in which all `g_i` vanish while `x` survives.

#### Uniform finite-quotient conjugacy consequence

For every finite group `Q` and every homomorphism `q:F_r->Q`,

```math
q(x)\in[q(g_1)]^Q\cdots[q(g_k)]^Q.
\tag{9.2}
```

This is the difficult part. It must hold across solvable groups, alternating groups, groups of Lie type, and arbitrary extensions, without choosing a metric. A proof would likely require a structural finite-group theorem or an identity whose conjugacy width is uniformly bounded independently of `Q`.

Once (9.1)--(9.2) are proved, Theorem 3.1 is a complete one-line conclusion: the displayed finite presentation is non-weakly-sofic.

### 9.2 A proof that every group is weakly sofic

By Theorem 2.4, it would suffice to prove the universal containment

```math
\overline{[g_1]^F\cdots[g_k]^F}^{\mathrm{prof}}
\subseteq
\langle\!\langle g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle\!\rangle
\tag{9.3}
```

for every finitely generated free group `F` and every finite tuple `g_1,...,g_k in F`.

Equivalently: whenever `x` lies outside the normal closure of the `g_i`, there is a finite quotient `q` in which `q(x)` lies outside the corresponding product of conjugacy classes.

This is stronger than residual finiteness of one-relator quotients, stronger than conjugacy separability, and different from closedness of the product itself. A proof must separate `x` from the *entire product* in one finite quotient.

### 9.3 A practical search protocol for an existence proof

A computational or computer-assisted search should not merely enumerate non-residually-finite presentations. It should target the two-sided certificate directly.

1. Choose low-rank free groups and short tuples `g_1,...,g_k,x`.
2. Prove or certify (9.1), perhaps using rewriting, automatic structures, low-dimensional representations, or a quotient with decidable normal form.
3. Test (9.2) over a wide range of finite quotients. Failure in one quotient eliminates the tuple.
4. Search for a conceptual finite-group reason behind surviving tuples. Raw verification over bounded orders can suggest a theorem but cannot prove the universal quantifier.
5. Keep the factor count fixed. A program that merely finds some normal-closure expression in each tested quotient is measuring `m(Q)`, not testing weak-sofic obstruction.
6. Record conjugators as witnesses and inspect whether they arise from a uniform commutator identity, transfer formula, or bounded-width theorem.

The uploaded `IsBoundedConjProduct` API could support step 5 after it is generalized from one seed element and permutation targets to ordered products of arbitrary conjugacy classes in arbitrary finite groups.

## 10. Suggested Lean formalization

The repository should retain its existing sofic development but separate the two notions at the type level.

### 10.1 Rename the current fixed-separation model

The present declaration

```lean
WeakSoficModel
```

should be renamed, for example, to

```lean
FixedSeparationSoficModel
```

because its target is still `Equiv.Perm` with Hamming distance. The theorem

```lean
isSofic_iff_fixedSeparation
```

would then state exactly what the file proves.

### 10.2 Define arbitrary finite bi-invariant metric targets

A schematic API is:

```lean
structure FiniteBiInvariantMetricGroup where
  H : Type u
  groupH : Group H
  fintypeH : Fintype H
  dist : H -> H -> Real
  dist_eq_zero : forall x y, dist x y = 0 <-> x = y
  dist_symm : forall x y, dist x y = dist y x
  dist_triangle : forall x y z, dist x z <= dist x y + dist y z
  left_invariant : forall a x y, dist (a * x) (a * y) = dist x y
  right_invariant : forall a x y, dist (x * a) (y * a) = dist x y
```

One may instead build on Mathlib's metric structures and add the two invariance axioms.

Then define a genuinely Glebsky--Rivera model:

```lean
structure GRWeakSoficModel
    (G : Type*) [Group G] (Phi : Finset G) (eps alpha : Real) where
  target : FiniteBiInvariantMetricGroup
  map : G -> target.H
  map_one : 1 ∈ Phi -> map 1 = 1
  multiplicative : forall a ∈ Phi, forall b ∈ Phi, a * b ∈ Phi ->
    target.dist (map a * map b) (map (a * b)) < eps
  separated : forall a ∈ Phi, a != 1 ->
    alpha < target.dist (map a) 1
```

and

```lean
def IsWeaklySoficGR (G : Type*) [Group G] : Prop :=
  ∃ alpha : Real, 0 < alpha ∧
    ∀ (Phi : Finset G) (eps : Real), 0 < eps ->
      Nonempty (GRWeakSoficModel G Phi eps alpha)
```

The first bridge theorem should be

```lean
theorem isWeaklySoficGR_of_isSofic :
  IsSofic G -> IsWeaklySoficGR G
```

using symmetric groups with Hamming metric.

### 10.3 Formalize profinite closure by its finite-quotient universal property

A topology-free initial definition avoids substantial infrastructure:

```lean
def InProfiniteClosure
    {F : Type*} [Group F] (A : Set F) (x : F) : Prop :=
  ∀ (Q : Type*) [Group Q] [Finite Q] (q : F ->* Q),
    q x ∈ q '' A
```

For a normal subgroup `N`, one can similarly define

```lean
def InProfiniteClosureSubgroup (N : Subgroup F) (x : F) : Prop :=
  ∀ (Q : Type*) [Group Q] [Finite Q] (q : F ->* Q),
    q x ∈ N.map q
```

and later prove equivalence with topological closure if desired.

### 10.4 Define products of conjugacy classes

For a list `gs : List F`, define recursively:

```lean
def conjClass (g : F) : Set F := {x | ∃ u, x = u * g * u⁻¹}

def conjClassProduct : List F -> Set F
  | [] => {1}
  | g :: gs => conjClass g * conjClassProduct gs
```

The endpoint witness is then:

```lean
def NonWeakSoficCertificate
    (F : Type*) [Group F] (gs : List F) (x : F) : Prop :=
  x ∉ Subgroup.normalClosure (Set.range gs.get) ∧
  InProfiniteClosure (conjClassProduct gs) x
```

The main formal target should be:

```lean
theorem nonWeaklySofic_of_certificate
    (h : NonWeakSoficCertificate F gs x) :
    ¬ IsWeaklySoficGR (F ⧸ Subgroup.normalClosure ...)
```

### 10.5 Formalize the proof through finite separability

The most modular route mirrors Sections 2.2--2.3:

1. define word balls in a finite-rank free group;
2. define finite separability of a normal subgroup;
3. prove `weakSofic_iff_finitelySeparable`;
4. define conjugate-word length in a finite group;
5. prove that its capped rescaling is a bi-invariant metric;
6. prove `finitelySeparable_iff_conjClassProductClosure`;
7. derive the main criterion and finitely presented reduction.

The capped-length construction is finite and elementary. It is likely easier to formalize than a general metric-ultraproduct characterization and has the advantage of exposing the fixed-width invariant directly.

### 10.6 Add the `E` calibration theorem

Once the true predicate is present, the repository should prove:

```lean
theorem literalMarkedGroup_isWeaklySoficGR :
  IsWeaklySoficGR MarkedGroup :=
  isWeaklySoficGR_of_isSofic markedGroup_isSofic
```

and then derive a no-uniform-width theorem for the marked word, formalizing Corollary 6.3. This would prevent future attempts to use the same finite-quotient kill theorem as a non-weak-sofic endpoint.

## 11. Topological caveat

There is a nearby theorem that can be mistaken for the requested result. Nikolov--Schneider--Thom prove that a connected Lie group is approximable by finite groups as a **topological group** only when it is abelian. Hence a connected nonabelian compact Lie group, such as `SO(3)`, is a nonexample in their topological approximation category.

This does not answer the abstract question. The same paper emphasizes that topology matters: every linear Lie group is approximable by finite groups as an abstract group, because its finitely generated subgroups are residually finite. In the abstract embedding, the induced topology is discrete.

The uploaded repository works with discrete group predicates and finite subsets, so the topological counterexamples are outside its scope.

---

## 12. Final conclusion

The requested existence statement cannot currently be supplied as an established theorem. Nor can it currently be disproved by proving that all groups are weakly sofic. The exact mathematical situation is:

```math
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{A non-weakly-sofic group exists}\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\\
&\text{some fixed finite product of conjugacy classes in a finite-rank free group}\\
&\text{has profinite closure escaping the normal closure of its factors.}
\end{aligned}
}
```

The uploaded development does not establish that escape. Its apparent candidates fail for provable reasons:

* `E` is non-MF but sofic, hence weakly sofic;
* its marked word is in the closure of the entire relation subgroup but in no fixed bounded relation-product closure;
* finite quasi-identities do not survive the quotient step in weak-sofic ultraproduct representations;
* the Kun--Thom nonsofic wreath products are weakly sofic;
* the Clifford wall is explicitly realized in a weakly sofic group;
* the implemented `WeakSoficModel` is ordinary Hamming soficity with fixed separation.

The shortest honest route to a genuine example is therefore not another operator-norm or Hamming obstruction. It is an explicit tuple of free-group words satisfying (9.1)--(9.2), or an equivalent finite-group bounded-conjugacy theorem. Until such a tuple or a general containment proof is produced, the abstract existence problem remains open.

## 13. Source verification performed at filing time (2026-08-17)

The audit above cites Glebsky--Rivera without quoting it.  Because the graph's
doctrine is that an imported theorem must be checked against the primary
source before it becomes a node, arXiv:0709.0026v2 (the arXiv version of
J. Algebra **320** (2008), no. 9, 3512--3518) was fetched and read in full at
filing time.  Everything the audit attributes to that paper is confirmed, and
two things are added that the audit did not have.

### 13.1 The criterion, verbatim

The audit's Definition 1.2 is the paper's Definition 4.1 together with
Definition 3.1, verbatim in content:

> **Definition 3.1** Let `H` be a finite group with a bi-invariant metric `d`.
> Let `G` be a group, `Phi subseteq G` be a finite subset, `epsilon>0`, and
> `alpha>0`.  A map `phi:G->H` is said to be a
> `(Phi,epsilon,alpha)`-homomorphism if:
> (1) For any two elements `a,b in Phi`, with `a.b in Phi`,
> `d(phi(a)phi(b),phi(a.b))<epsilon`
> (2) If `e_G in Phi`, then `phi(e_G)=e_H`
> (3) For any `a != e_G`, `d(phi(a),e_H)>alpha`

> **Definition 4.1** A group `G` is called w-sofic if there exists `alpha>0`
> such that for any finite set `Phi subset G`, for any `epsilon>0` there
> exists a finite group `H` with a bi-invariant metric `d` and a
> `(Phi,epsilon,alpha)`-homomorphism to `(H,d)`.

> **Remark 4.2** In Definition 4.1 we do not ask the metric to be normalized.
> So, `alpha` may be any fixed positive number. [...] It is easy to see, that
> any sofic group is w-sofic.

The audit's Theorem 2.4 is the paper's Theorem 4.3, verbatim:

> **Theorem 4.3** Let `F` be a finitely generated free group and `N normal F`.
> Then `F/N` is w-sofic if and only if for any finite sequence
> `g_1,g_2,...,g_k` from `N` one has
> `closure([g_1]^F[g_2]^F...[g_k]^F) subseteq N`.
> (`closure(X)` denote the closure of `X` in the profinite topology on `F`.)

The audit's Theorem 3.1 `(1) implies (2)` is the paper's Corollary 4.4,
verbatim:

> **Corollary 4.4** If there exists a non w-sofic group then there exists a
> finitely presented non w-sofic group.

and the paper's proof is the audit's, including the step
`N(g_1,...,g_k) subseteq N`.

The existence question and its profinite equivalent are the paper's
Conjecture 4.5, Conjecture 2.1 and Corollary 4.6:

> **Conjecture 2.1** For a finitely generated free group `F`, there exists a
> sequence `g_1,g_2,...,g_k in F` such that
> `closure([g_1]^F[g_2]^F...[g_k]^F) not-subseteq N(g_1,g_2,...,g_k)`.

> **Conjecture 4.5** There exists a non w-sofic group.

> **Corollary 4.6** Conjecture 4.5 and Conjecture 2.1 are equivalent.

Note that Glebsky and Rivera *conjecture* the escape, i.e. they expect
non-w-sofic groups to exist.  The audit's Section 12 is neutral on the
direction; the source is not.

The audit's Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.3 are the paper's Lemma 6.2 and Lemma 6.4,
with the paper's Definition 6.1 being the audit's Definition 2.1 (the paper
also calls it "finitely separable").  The audit reconstructed both proofs
independently; they agree with the paper's, including the `3r` word-length
budget in the converse of Lemma 6.2 and the `alpha/k` tolerance in Lemma 6.4.
The manufactured metric differs only in presentation: the paper builds
`||.||_C` from a *conjugacy class graph* `Gamma(G,C)` (Section 5, item III)
and sets `d(x,e)=epsilon||x||_C` with `k>alpha/epsilon`; the audit builds the
capped rescaled conjugate-word length `min{L_C(h)/n,1}` directly.  These are
the same construction, and the audit's is the same object already recorded on
`glebsky-manufactured-invariant-length` from the 2017 paper.

### 13.2 Remark 4.8 states the audit's Section 8 dichotomy in the source

The audit's "required bounded-width lemma" (8.3) and its Section 8.2 warning
about compactness are the paper's own Remark 4.8, which makes the width
dichotomy sharp:

> **Remark 4.8** Let `S = [g_1]^Fhat[g_1^(-1)]^Fhat[g_2]^Fhat[g_2^(-1)]^Fhat
> ... [g_k]^Fhat[g_k^(-1)]^Fhat`.  Then
> `Nhat(g_1,...,g_k) = union_(n=1)^infinity S^n`.  So,
> `Nhat(g_1,...,g_k)` is a closed set if and only if
> `Nhat(g_1,...,g_k) = S^n` for some `n`, due to the fact that `S` is a
> compact set.

So in the profinite completion the normal closure is closed exactly when it
has *bounded conjugacy width*.  That is precisely the missing lemma the audit
isolates, and it explains why the audit's Section 8.2 compactness objection is
not a technicality: bounded width and closedness are the same condition.

The paper also records the completion form of the criterion:

> **Corollary 4.7** The group `F/N` is w-sofic iff `N = Nhat cap F` and the
> group `F/N` is residually finite iff `N = Ntilde cap F`.

where `Nhat` is the minimal normal subgroup of the profinite completion
`Fhat` containing `N`, and `Ntilde` is the topological closure of `N` in
`Fhat`; `Nhat <= Ntilde`, with equality iff `Nhat` is closed.  This is the
clean statement of why residual finiteness implies weak soficity and not
conversely.

### 13.3 Correction: the concrete near-miss is Howie's, not Nikolov--Segal's

The audit's Section 4 attributes the known non-closedness of products of
conjugacy classes to Nikolov--Segal and then correctly observes that the
standard instance is vacuous because the basis elements normally generate `F`.
That paragraph was not source-verified and should not be relied on.  The
primary source supplies a much sharper, fully explicit near-miss, and it is
the single most actionable fact for anyone hunting a certificate.  From
Section 2 of the paper, citing Howie [12]:

> in the 2-generated free group `F = <x,y>` there exist elements `g_1` and
> `g_2` such that `[g_1]^F[g_2]^F` is not closed in any pro-`p` topology, even
> more, its closure is not contained in `N(g_1,g_2)`, the normal subgroup
> generated by `g_1,g_2`.  [...]  If `g_1 = x^(-2)y^(-3)`,
> `g_2 = x^(-2)(xy)^5` and `a = xy^2`, then `a not-in N(g_1,g_2)`.  On the
> other hand, for any `i`, one has `a = w_i^(-1)g_1w_iv_i^(-1)g_2v_i mod F_i`
> for some `w_i,v_i in F`, where `F_0 = F` and `F_(i+1) = [F_i,F_i]`.  So, for
> any homomorphism `phi` from `F` to a nilpotent group
> `phi(a) in phi([g_1]^F[g_2]^F)` (it must be `phi(F_i) = {e}` for some `i`).
> Since a finite `p`-group is nilpotent, `a` belongs to the closure of
> `[g_1]^F[g_2]^F` in any pro-`p` topologies, but `a not-in N(g_1,g_2)`.  So,
> the same statement may be valid for the profinite topology.

Reference [12] is **J. Howie, *The p-adic topology on a free group: A
counterexample*, Math. Z. 187 (1984), 25--27.**

So an explicit two-element tuple with `k=2` and rank `2` already satisfies the
audit's condition (9.1) and satisfies condition (9.2) *for every nilpotent
finite quotient*.  What is unproved is (9.2) for the remaining finite
quotients — non-nilpotent ones, where the derived series argument is
unavailable.  Section 2 of the paper also states, as of 2008: "Up to our
knowledge it is an open question if a product of several conjugacy classes
`[g_1]^F[g_2]^F...[g_k]^F` is closed in the profinite topology in a free
group `F`", and offers Liebeck--Shalev diameters and Nikolov--Segal I/II as
the techniques the authors hoped would resolve the conjecture.  That is
presumably the ancestor of the audit's Nikolov--Segal remark.
