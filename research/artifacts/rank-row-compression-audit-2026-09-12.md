## Summary

* **Section 1.** At linear scale the rank row has both ingredients of Theorem 10 of
  `research/artifacts/rigid-compression-defect-ladder-2026-09-11.md`.
  * Rounding of fixed subspaces is free, with no property (T).
  * Normalized dimension is a faithful size.
  * So compressors conserve the fixed right ideal of the compressed subgroup in every
    tensor degree, and its normal closure acts trivially there.
* **Section 2.** This layer is too coarse. The sofic group `E` has linear models with a
  nontrivial compression defect of a Kazhdan subgroup, and every conclusion of
  Section 1 holds in them.
* **Section 3.** A step-by-step transcription of the Hamming proof.
  * The two counting steps transfer: size conservation under a bijection, and the
    more-than-half collision.
  * Two steps fail. Rounding into expanding pieces (K) has no rank replacement.
    One-piece transport (T1) is the permutation-specific step: it consumes that model
    matrices act on the multiplicity coordinates of pieces by permutations.
* **Section 4.** What this means for `binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-f2-linear-sofic`.

## 0. Setting

Let `k` be a field, `n_i -> infinity` and `omega` an ultrafilter. Put

```text
M = prod_omega M_(n_i)(k) / { (x_i) : rank(x_i)/n_i -> 0 along omega },
rk(x) = lim_omega rank(x_i)/n_i .
```

`M^x = prod_omega GL_(n_i)(k) / d_omega`, and a homomorphism `sigma : G -> M^x` has
representatives `sigma_i(g)` in `GL_(n_i)(k)` with
`rank(sigma_i(gh) - sigma_i(g) sigma_i(h)) = o(n_i)`. For a finite `S` in `G` put

```text
K_i(S) = intersection_(s in S) ker(sigma_i(s) - 1)     in k^(n_i).
```

Two elementary facts:

* **(R1)** If `rank(A - B) <= r`, then `ker(A-1) n ker(B-1)` has codimension at most `r`
  in `ker(A-1)`, because `B = A = 1` on `ker(A-1) n ker(A-B)`.
* **(R2)** If `W` is a subspace and `(A-1)W` has dimension at most `r`, then
  `W n ker(A-1)` has codimension at most `r` in `W`.

## 1. The linear-scale layer

**Lemma 1 (free rounding).** Let `Gamma = <S>` and `sigma : Gamma -> M^x`. Let `e_i` be
any idempotent with image `K_i(S)`, and `e = [(e_i)]`. Then

```text
e M = Fix_r(sigma(Gamma)) := { x in M : sigma(gamma) x = x for all gamma },
rk((1-e) x) <= sum_(s in S) rk((sigma(s) - 1) x)        for every x in M.
```

*Proof.* `(sigma_i(s) - 1) e_i = 0` exactly, so `eM` is fixed by `sigma(S)`, hence by
`sigma(Gamma)`. For `x = [(x_i)]` put `W_i = im x_i`. By (R2), applied once for each `s`,
`W_i n K_i(S)` has codimension at most `sum_s rank((sigma_i(s)-1) x_i)` in `W_i`. Since
`1 - e_i` vanishes on `K_i(S)`, `rank((1-e_i) x_i)` is at most that codimension. Take
limits. If `x` is fixed, the right side is `0`, so `x = ex`. QED

No property (T) enters. For vectors in a Hilbert space the same inequality is a spectral
gap, which is property (T). Rank measures dimensions of images, so the pointwise-fixed
layer rounds for free.

**Lemma 2 (tensor degrees).** For `a, b >= 0` put

```text
sigma^(a,b)(g) = sigma(g)^(tensor a) tensor (sigma(g^-1)^T)^(tensor b)     in GL_(n_i^(a+b))(k).
```

This is a homomorphism into the rank ultraproduct over the dimensions `n_i^(a+b)`. In
degree `(1,1)`, under `k^n tensor k^n = M_n(k)`, it is `X -> sigma_i(g) X sigma_i(g)^-1`,
and `K_i^(1,1)(S)` is the exact commutant of `sigma_i(S)`.

*Proof.* `g -> sigma(g^-1)^T` is a homomorphism, since transposition and inversion each
reverse products, and `rank(A^T - B^T) = rank(A - B)`. Also

```text
X_1 (x) ... (x) X_m - Y_1 (x) ... (x) Y_m = sum_j Y_1 (x) ... (x) Y_(j-1) (x) (X_j - Y_j) (x) X_(j+1) (x) ... (x) X_m
```

has rank at most `sum_j rank(X_j - Y_j) n^(m-1)`. So the normalized error at scale `n^m`
is at most the sum of the factor errors. QED

**Theorem 3 (conservation).** Let `Gamma = <S>` be finitely generated, and let `G` be
generated by elements of `P_Gamma = { g : g Gamma g^-1 <= Gamma }`. Let
`sigma : G -> M^x` be any homomorphism and `(a,b)` any tensor degree, and write
`Fix^(a,b)(Gamma)` for the fixed right ideal of `sigma^(a,b)(Gamma)`. Then:

1. `sigma^(a,b)(g) Fix^(a,b)(Gamma) = Fix^(a,b)(Gamma)` for every `g` in `G`;
2. `sigma^(a,b)(x)` is the identity on `Fix^(a,b)(Gamma)` for every `x` in the normal
   closure `<<Gamma>>_G`;
3. `Fix^(a,b)(Gamma) = Fix^(a,b)(<<Gamma>>_G)`.

No property (T) is assumed, of `Gamma` or of `G`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2 it suffices to treat `sigma` itself. Let `s` be a compressor and
`u_i = sigma_i(s)`.

* **Exact transport.** `u_i K_i(S) = intersection_(x in S) ker(u_i sigma_i(x) u_i^-1 - 1)`.
* **The word relations.** Each `s x s^-1` equals a word `w_x` in `S^(+-)`. Every vector of
  `K_i(S)` is fixed by `w_x(sigma_i(S))`, and
  `rank(w_x(sigma_i(S)) - u_i sigma_i(x) u_i^-1) = o(n_i)`.
* **Counting.** By (R1), `K_i(S) n u_i K_i(S)` has codimension `o(n_i)` in `K_i(S)`. As
  `dim u_i K_i(S) = dim K_i(S)`, the subspaces `K_i(S)` and `u_i K_i(S)` agree up to
  `o(n_i)` dimensions. In `M` this says `sigma(s) eM = eM`, with `e` as in Lemma 1.

So compressors and their inverses preserve `eM`, and part 1 follows because they generate
`G`. By Lemma 1, `sigma(gamma)` fixes `eM` pointwise. For `g` in `G` and `y` in `eM`,
`sigma(g)^-1 y` lies in `eM`, so `sigma(g gamma g^-1) y = y`. Products of conjugates of
elements of `Gamma` therefore fix `eM` pointwise, which is part 2. Part 3 follows from
part 2 and `Gamma <= <<Gamma>>_G`. QED

This is `invariant-size-collapse` in rank ultraproducts: axioms (A1)-(A3) of Theorem 10
hold for `A(L) = Fix^(a,b)(L)` with size `rk`. Axiom (A4) fails: preserving
`Fix^(1,1)(Gamma)` does not make an element commute with `sigma(Gamma)`.

## 2. The layer cannot kill defects

`E` is the literal finitely presented group of `non_mf_groups_exist.tex`.

* It is sofic (`literal-group-sofic`), hence linear sofic over every field
  (`sofic-implies-linear-sofic-over-every-field`).
* Its affine base `Gamma` is Kazhdan, with the doubling compressor `t`,
  `t Gamma t^-1 <= Gamma`.
* Some `c` in `C_E(Gamma)` and `v_1` in `Gamma` give `u = [t c t^-1, v_1] != 1`
  (ladder artifact Theorem 4; `literal-kazhdan-clifford-inputs`,
  `literal-mf-radical-is-unsquared-defect`).

Take any injective `sigma : E -> M^x`. Theorem 3 applies to `Gamma <= <Gamma, t>` in every
tensor degree, and `sigma(u) != 1`. So the conclusions of Theorem 3, in every degree and
for every compressed finitely generated subgroup, are compatible with a nontrivial defect
of a Kazhdan subgroup. Theorem 3 never uses property (T) of the compressor group, and
ladder Theorem 4 shows the Hamming row needs it. So a proof of the rank row must use
property (T) of `G` below linear scale.

## 3. Transcribing the Hamming proof

These are the steps of Kun--Thom Theorem 4.1 and the OpenAI criterion, as recorded in
`openai-bounded-median-component-matching`, `openai-expander-matching-criterion` and
Section 3 of the ladder artifact.

* **(K) Rounding.** Kun's decomposition turns the almost-action of Kazhdan `Gamma` on
  `Y_n` into a partition into expanding `Gamma`-components with `o(N)` leakage, through
  the spectral gap on `l^2(Y_n)`.
* **(T1) One-piece transport.** The transported component `p_t(C)` expands for
  `t Gamma t^-1 <= Gamma`. The `Gamma`-components are invariant subsets for that
  subgroup, and an expanding set cannot straddle a partition into invariant subsets. So
  `p_t(C)` lies almost inside one `Gamma`-component.
* **(M) No room to drift.** Every ambient generator is a bijection of `Y_n`, so
  `sum_z (f(sz) - f(z)) = 0`, and the median normalization compares sizes.
* **(C) Collision.** A transported component fills more than half of its target, so
  distinct transported components pick distinct targets.
* **(N) Conclusion.** Compressors permute the retained components, so they normalize the
  centralizer.

In the rank metric, pieces are independent subspaces `W_j` of `k^n` with
`sum_j dim W_j = n`. Write `d_j = dim W_j`.

**(M) transfers, given (K) and (T1).** Suppose an invertible `s` maps each `W_j` into
`W_(tau(j))` up to `l_j` dimensions. Then `sum_(tau(j)=l) d_j <= d_l + sum_(tau(j)=l) l_j`.
Both totals equal `n`, so

```text
sum_l | d_l - sum_(tau(j)=l) d_j | <= 2 sum_j l_j,
| sum_j d_j (phi(tau(j)) - phi(j)) | <= 2 sup|phi| sum_j l_j     for bounded phi.
```

That is the rank form of "no room to drift".

**(C) transfers.** A subspace `D` cannot contain two independent subspaces each of
dimension more than `dim D / 2`. The transported pieces `sW_j` are independent, because
the `W_j` are and `s` is invertible.

**(K) has no free rank form.** Almost invariant subspaces do not round in general. Over
`F_2` with `n = 2^m`, let `A` be the cyclic permutation matrix and
`W = span(e_1, ..., e_(n/2))`.

* `dim(W + AW) = dim W + 1`.
* `A` is one Jordan block, since `x^n - 1 = (x-1)^n` in characteristic two, so its
  invariant subspaces are the `ker(A-1)^j`.
* Every nonzero invariant subspace contains the all-ones vector, which is not in `W`.
* The obstruction is linear, not just one dimension. Identify `k^n` with
  `F_2[x]/((x-1)^n)`, so that `A` is multiplication by `x` and `W` is the set of
  polynomials of degree `< n/2`.
  * `ker(A-1)^j` is the ideal `((x-1)^(n-j))`, of dimension `j`.
  * A nonzero polynomial of degree `< n/2` is not divisible by `(x-1)^(n/2)`, so
    `dim(ker(A-1)^j ∩ W) = max(0, j - n/2)`.
  * So every invariant subspace `V` has `dim V + dim W - 2 dim(V ∩ W) >= n/2`.

  (Added by gk-verify-pos, 2026-09-12.)

The Hamming row supplies the missing expansion from property (T) through `l^2(Y_n)`.
No rank-metric replacement is known. Bachner (arXiv:2408.15614v2, abstract, as recorded
on `f2-linear-sofic-groups-kill-rigid-compression-defects`) shows higher-rank lattices
are not strictly C-stable in rank. Lemma 1 rounds pointwise-fixed subspaces, which is a
different object.

*Later the same day: property (T) does not supply (K).*
`research/artifacts/kazhdan-rank-rounding-counterexample-2026-09-12.md`.
* The shift obstruction reappears inside a Kazhdan group. `EL_3(F_2[t])` acts on
  `F_(2^k)^3` irreducibly over `F_2`, and multiplication by a field generator is a
  companion-matrix shift, so degree truncations have bounded boundary.
* So no rounding modulus exists, and every expander decomposition leaks linearly
  (`kazhdan-group-rank-models-admit-no-expander-decomposition`).
* The rank ultraproduct has a diffuse relative commutant
  (`kazhdan-rank-ultraproducts-can-have-diffuse-commutants`).

**(T1) fails, and it is the permutation-specific step.**

* **Sets versus subspaces.** Pieces are subsets in the Hamming row, and every model
  matrix is a permutation matrix. So a compressor permutes the multiplicity coordinates
  of a piece. Over a field, two pieces `W (x) e_1` and `W (x) e_2` of `W (x) k^2`, with
  `Gamma` acting on `W` alone, can be rotated by a compressor acting as
  `rho(t) (x) B` with `B` non-monomial.
* **Diagonal transport.** With `B e_1 = e_1 + e_2`, the image of `W (x) e_1` is the graph
  `{ w (x) e_1 + w (x) e_2 }`. It meets both pieces in `0`, projects isomorphically onto
  each, and lies almost inside neither.
* **Effect on (C).** The transported piece never fills more than half of one target, so
  (C) gets no input.

The Hilbert--Schmidt row fails at the same step for exact representations
(`nonmonomial-multiplicity-obstructs-hs-compressor-transport`), although it has (K).
The rank row lacks both.

## 4. Consequences for the gate

**The configuration.** Take the nine-leaf configuration
`Gamma = EL_alpha(R) <= G_L = EL_D(R)` over `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)`:

* `G_L` is generated by compressors of `Gamma`
  (`leavitt-unit-group-carries-nontrivial-rigid-defect`);
* `G_L = R^x` (`leavitt-packet-linear-sofic-kills-kaplansky-target`);
* `R^x` is simple (`binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-simple`), so `<<Gamma>>_(G_L) = R^x`.

**The constraint.** By Theorem 3, every homomorphism `sigma : R^x -> M^x`, over any field,
satisfies

```text
Fix^(a,b)(sigma(Gamma)) = Fix^(a,b)(sigma(R^x))     for every tensor degree (a,b).
```

In degree `(1,1)`, the linear-scale part of the commutant of `sigma(Gamma)` equals that of
`sigma(R^x)`. The same holds for every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup of `R^x`
whose compressors generate `R^x`.

**Where each direction stops.**

* **Negative** (`R^x` not `F_2`-linear sofic, through the rank row). It stops at (K) and
  (T1), below linear scale. Theorem 3 is the complete linear-scale layer, and Section 2
  shows it cannot finish the proof.
* **Positive** (a construction). A model must satisfy the constraint above in every degree.
  It cannot factor through finite groups, since `R^x` is simple and infinite, and the
  natural Cantor truncation loses rank (recorded on the gate node). No construction is
  attempted here.
