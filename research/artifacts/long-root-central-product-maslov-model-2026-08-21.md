# Exact nontrivial-sector models on the two long-root central product

This audits the bounded redirect from the refuted commuting-root cycle to the
two rank-one long-root copies of `SL2(Z)`.

Use Kassel's `C2` notation.  Put

```text
H_beta = <X_beta,X_(-beta)>       < Sp4(Z),
H_delta = <X_delta,X_(-delta)>   < Sp4(Z),    delta=2alpha+beta.
```

The displayed matrices in Kassel (2.17), (2.19) show that these are the two
coordinate `SL2(Z)` subgroups.  They commute and have trivial intersection.
Let `E_beta,E_delta` be their inverse images in the connected Deligne
three-cover `E_3`, with central kernel `<z>=C3`.

## 1. The rank-one factors and their central product

All four cross-root pairs commute in Kassel (2.1)--(2.3).  Equivalently, the
canonical real-root lifts commute in the universal cover of `Sp4(R)`.
Consequently `E_beta` and `E_delta` commute in `E_3`.  Their projections have
trivial intersection, while both contain the global kernel, so

```text
E_beta cap E_delta = <z>,
P := <E_beta,E_delta>
   = (E_beta x E_delta)/<(z,z^(-1))>.
```

Each `E_gamma` is virtually free.  Indeed `SL2(Z)` has a finite-index free
subgroup `F`; the inverse image of `F` is an extension of `F` by `C3`, which
splits because `H^2(F,C3)=0`.  Hence it contains a free subgroup of finite
index.  In particular `E_gamma` is residually finite.

Kassel Section 2.4 gives the compatible algebraic picture: for either long
root `gamma`, the infinite Steinberg kernel is generated by

```text
w_gamma^4,   w_gamma=x_gamma x_(-gamma)^(-1) x_gamma.
```

Passing to the three-cover turns this into the common order-three central
mark (up to the harmless orientation choice of its generator).

## 2. Exact finite-dimensional models with `z=omega`

Let `omega=exp(2 pi i/3)`.  Residual finiteness of `E_gamma` gives a finite
quotient `q_gamma:E_gamma -> Q_gamma` in which `q_gamma(z)` is nontrivial,
hence has order three.  In the left regular representation of `Q_gamma`, the
`omega` eigenspace of the central unitary `lambda(q_gamma(z))` is nonzero and
is invariant under `Q_gamma`.  Restriction to that eigenspace gives a
finite-dimensional representation

```text
rho_gamma:E_gamma -> U(V_gamma),   rho_gamma(z)=omega I.
```

The tensor product representation of `E_beta x E_delta` descends through the
central-product kernel because

```text
(rho_beta tensor rho_delta)(z,z^(-1))=omega omega^(-1) I=I.
```

It therefore gives an exact finite-dimensional representation

```text
rho:P -> U(V_beta tensor V_delta),   rho(z)=omega I.
```

The conjugate construction gives the `omega^2` sector.

## 3. Consequence for the proposed positive cycle

Thus every relation internal to the two rank-one factors, their common cyclic
overlap, and their cross-commutation can hold **exactly** in a nontrivial
Maslov sector.  No positive cycle whose existence is forced only by these
relations can have near-unitary edges with product `omega I`: applied to the
exact model above, `positive-cycle-near-unitary-edges-force-trivial-holonomy`
would give the contradiction `omega=1`.

There is also a separate synchronization warning.  Factorwise HS stability
does not generally round cross commutators: Ioana, *Almost commuting matrices
and stability for product groups*, arXiv:2108.09589, Theorem B, proves that
`F_m x F_k` is not even flexibly HS-stable for `m,k>=2`.  But this warning is
not needed for the no-go: even granting perfect cross synchronization, the
central product `P` has the exact nontrivial-sector models above.

Therefore the global Deligne obstruction enters only through mixed,
noncommuting `C2` Chevalley relations outside `P`.  Rank-one stability plus
cyclic-overlap synchronization cannot by itself compile the Maslov gap.
