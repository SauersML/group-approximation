# Central powers move deeper in the lower central series

Date: 2026-09-20. Status: subsidiary theorem, not a solution of Problem 141.

Let B denote the exactly marked integral extension B_c0 of K7, and let z
be its infinite-order central generator. Earlier certificates establish
z in gamma_5(B). Its marked presentation generates B by six elements of
order seven: z is the inverse of the first face word and is not an
additional necessary generator.

## Collection lemma

If a group G is generated by elements of order dividing a prime p, then

    gamma_i(G)^p <= gamma_(i+p-1)(G)       (i >= 1).

Here the left side denotes the subgroup generated by p-th powers, not
merely the set of powers. There is no finiteness assumption on G.

Proof. Work in G/gamma_(i+p-1)(G), whose nilpotency class c is at most
i+p-2. Descend on r from c to i, proving that gamma_r has exponent
dividing p. Assume this for gamma_(r+1).

A left-normed commutator w of length r in the given generators has w^p=1.
For r=1 this is the assumed order of a generator. For r>=2:
Indeed, substitute the p-th power of one generator into that commutator.
The result is 1. The commutator collection formula expresses it as w^p
times higher-weight commutators v with exponents that are integer linear
combinations of binomial(p,j), with 1 <= j <= weight(v)-r+1.
All terms of weight greater than c vanish. For the others,
weight(v)-r+1 <= c-r+1 <= p-1, so their exponents are divisible by p.
They vanish by the induction hypothesis.

The subgroup gamma_r is generated by left-normed commutators of length
at least r in the generators and their inverses. The generators of
length r have order dividing p by the preceding paragraph; those of
larger length do so by induction. Products also have order dividing p:
in the collection formula for a p-th power of a product of elements of
gamma_r, a surviving commutator in the factors has length at most
floor(c/r), which is less than p because c <= i+p-2 <= r+p-2 < pr.
Its exponent is therefore divisible by p. Every such commutator lies
in gamma_(2r), hence in gamma_(r+1), where the induction hypothesis
applies. Iterating the two-factor formula proves the assertion for any
finite product. This completes the descent and proves the inclusion.

The collection inputs are Hall's power formula and the powered-entry
commutator formula, as stated in Ruth Rebekka Struik, *On nilpotent
products of cyclic groups*, Theorem H2 and Lemma H2, pp. 449–450.
The latter gives the bound weight(v)-(r-1) on the binomial degree.
The subgroup-generation statement follows by collecting words by weight.

## Application to the canonical extension

For every integer a >= 0,

    z^(7^a) in gamma_(5+6a)(B).

This follows by induction from z in gamma_5 and the collection lemma.
In particular z^7 lies in gamma_11. Every nilpotent image of B of class
at most ten has image(z) of order dividing seven. An image in which z
has order at least 49 must have nilpotency class at least eleven.
More generally, order 7^(a+1) requires class at least 5+6a.

Any finitely generated nilpotent image here is a finite seven-group:
its successive lower-central factors are finitely generated abelian
groups generated by commutators of seven-torsion generators, and hence
are finite groups of seven-power order. This observation does not bound
the nilpotency classes of all finite images.

## A fixed-detector lifting obstruction

Let F be the verified exponent-seven, class-five detector of order 7^28.
If a lift B -> E lands in a central extension with finite kernel C7
and quotient F, its image has nilpotency class at most six. The result
above therefore forces image(z)^7=1. Such a lift cannot turn this
particular detector into an order-49 detector.

This does not exclude a different finite quotient of higher class, a
nonnilpotent finite quotient, or unbounded finite orders of z. In
particular, z^7 in gamma_11 is not a proof that z^7 lies in the finite
residual, nor that it dies in every nilpotent quotient.
