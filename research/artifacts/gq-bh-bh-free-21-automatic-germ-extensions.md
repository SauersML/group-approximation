- **Definitions (pp. 2–4).**
  - A *language of normal forms*: one word per element over a finite symmetric generating set.
  - It is *continuous* if the left action on `L` extends continuously to `L ∪ ∂L`.
  - `G` is *continuous (asynchronous) automatic* if some (asynchronous) automatic structure is a
    continuous normal form.
- **Remark 1.2 (p. 3).** "We do not know whether every infinite, finitely generated group G has a
  continuous normal form L such that G acts faithfully on ∂L."
- **Criterion.** Prop. 1.3 = Prop. 2.1 (pp. 3, 8), over monoid generators.
- **Closure.** Prop. 1.4 (p. 4): commensurability, products, and free products. For free
  products the action on the normal-form boundary is faithful when `|G| >= 2, |H| >= 3`.
- **Results.**
  - Theorems 1.6–1.7 (p. 5): rational homeomorphisms of closed rational sets.
  - Cor. 1.8, 1.9 (pp. 5–6): continuous asynchronous automatic groups embed in the rational
    group.
- **Examples.**
  - Thm 1.10: `BS(m,n)`.
  - Thm 1.11: finite-type Artin groups.
  - Thm 1.13: virtually torsion-free cubulated groups.
- **What is not proved** (pp. 2, 6, 7). No Boone--Higman result: "we do not prove that any of the
  associated topological full groups [[G | ∂_h G]] are finitely presented ... the actions are not
  contracting — so new techniques will be needed". Also: "Whenever it is finitely presented, such
  a group ought to admit an embedding into a finitely presented, simple twisted Brin–Thompson
  group". For braid groups: "we do not know whether the corresponding topological full group
  [[A | ∂L]] is finitely presented".
- **Named questions (p. 7).**
  - **Q1.12:** "Does every Artin group of finite type admit an embedding into a finitely
    presented simple group?"
  - **Q1.15:** "Does every virtually torsion-free cubulated group admit an embedding into a
    finitely presented simple group?"
  - **Q1.16:** "Are mapping class groups of finite-type surfaces continuous automatic? Do all
    such groups admit embeddings into finitely presented simple groups?"

## 2. The construction: germ extensions over the boundary Thompson group

Let `L` be a continuous asynchronous automatic structure with faithful action on the Cantor
space `∂L`.

- **B**, the boundary Thompson group of `L`: all homeomorphisms of `∂L` that are locally prefix
  replacements `uw -> u'w` with `u, u'` having equal follower sets.
  - Since `L` is regular, there are finitely many follower sets.
  - So `∂L` is the path space of a finite graph, and `B` is the topological full group of its
    tail groupoid.
- **Γ**: the full group of homeomorphisms that agree locally with `B` off finitely many points,
  with singular germs in the germ groupoid generated by `G`.

A generator `s` is singular at `ξ` exactly when the word differences along the normal forms of
`ξ` and `sξ` never settle into a single prefix replacement. In ECHLPT terms, the word-difference
state along `ξ` never becomes the "equal suffix" state.

BBCMP say the obstruction is that "the actions are not contracting". Belk--Hyde--Matucci
(arXiv:2407.03149, read at source) do not need contraction. They need:
- finitely many singular points per element;
- finiteness of the pointwise fixers `Fix_B(M)`;
- finitely many `B`-orbits on `sing^n`;
- germ groups either virtually `(B)_p`, or with `(B)_p` normal and a quotient of type `F_n`.

That is `cor:MainFinitenessCorollary`.

## 3. Calibration: Z^2 * Z (node `z2-free-z-normal-form-boundary-has-corner-singular-orbits`)

Take the BBCMP normal form `L_2` of Example 1.1(2) on `Z^2`, extended to `Z^2 * <t>` by
alternating syllables.

- `t ∈ B`.
- The singular points of `x^±`, `y^±` are exactly the four corners `(x^±y^±)^∞`. All ray points
  `(ab)^m a^∞` and `(ab)^m b^∞` are nonsingular: each generator is locally one explicit prefix
  replacement there.
- At a corner `c`, `(Γ)_c ≅ Z^2 ⊃ (B)_c = <diagonal> ≅ Z`, normal with quotient `Z`.

This is the opposite of the finite-nucleus picture
(`finite-nucleus-rsg-abelian-stabilizers-have-rank-one-germs`). Relative to a trivial-nucleus
base, a flat is harmless: it creates finitely many singular orbits with exactly the germ shape
Belk--Hyde--Matucci accept.

## 4. Where it stands

- **Proved:**
  - (E1) and (E4) for `Z^2 * Z`;
  - the reduction route `automatic-groups-bh-via-normal-form-germ-extensions`.
- **Open**, as the premise `automatic-groups-embed-in-corner-germ-extensions`:
  - **(E2)** `Fix_B(M)` finitely presented, `|M| <= 2`. For trivial `M`, X. Li's finiteness
    theorems for graph-groupoid full groups should apply. Pointwise fixers of rational points
    need an analogue of BHM Appendix A for graph path spaces.
  - **(E3)** finitely many `B`-orbits on pairs of corner-tail points. Expected, by prefix
    exchange, but depends on the `K_0` class of complements.
  - **(E5)** clopen transitivity of `B`, again a `K_0` condition.
  - (E1) and (E4) for general automatic groups.
- **Where it fails.** Product normal forms on `F_2 × F_2`, and `F × Z` Seifert pieces: a
  commuting factor is singular along the whole boundary of the other factor, which is an
  uncountable singular set. So the premise needs non-product normal forms or a supergroup. `V`
  contains `F_2 × F_2`, so this is not a dead end, but it is the crux for:
  - closed mapping class groups (`closed-surface-mapping-class-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`);
  - nonfibered graph manifold groups (`nonfibered-closed-graph-manifold-groups-satisfy-pbh`), which
    are automatic, having no Nil or Sol pieces (ECHLPT; recalled, not re-read);
  - large-type Artin groups with odd labels, which are automatic (Holt--Rees; recalled).

## 6. Second landing: recoding into V, and Z^2 * Z done in full

**Correction.** The first version of the corner node listed several local replacements between
prefixes ending in different automaton states. An example is `[x] -> [xx]` at `x^∞`, where the
right map is `[xx] -> [xxx]`. The node now carries the explicit 17-state automaton and a
corrected table, and every row has been checked against the normal-form formula. The
conclusions (four corners, germ `Z^2` over `Z`) are unchanged.

**Why the automaton's own base fails.** Let `B_𝒜` be the full group of state-preserving prefix
replacements. Assign to each clopen set the sum of the end states of its cylinders, taken in
`K_0 = Z^16/<s - Σ_{s->s'} s'>`; this is an invariant of `B_𝒜`-local equivalence.
- Solving the relations: `q + q' = 0`; `p_a + p_b = 0` for all four pairs, so `p_x = p_X = π`
  and `p_y = p_Y = -π`; `e_ab = d_ab + π`; `Σ d_ab = 0`.
- So `K_0 ≅ Z^5`, freely generated by `q`, `π`, `d_xy`, `d_xY`, `d_Xy`. The class of `[∂L]` is 0.
- `[t]` and `[x]` have different classes, so `B_𝒜` is not clopen transitive. E5 fails for this
  base.

**The fix: recode into V** (`prefix-replacement-actions-recode-into-germ-extensions-of-v`).
- **The recoding.** Choose a complete binary prefix code on the out-edges at each vertex and
  concatenate. Every state-preserving prefix replacement becomes a `V` prefix replacement, and
  eventually periodic paths go to rational points.
- **The host.** Take the full germ closure `Γ` of `V ∪ G`. It is a full, clopen-transitive
  finite germ extension of `V`.
- **Finiteness.** BHM Appendix A (`thm:Stabilizers`: `Fix_{V_{d,r}}(S)` is `F_∞` for finite sets
  of rational points) and the standard orbit lemma give the remaining finiteness hypotheses. So
  only the germ condition is left.

**Z^2 * Z in full** (`z2-free-z-lies-in-an-fp-full-germ-extension-of-v`).
- **Codes.** At `D_ab` and `E_ab` use `{0, 10, 110, 111}`. The corner cycle words are `010`,
  `0110`, `0111` and `11110`: primitive and in distinct rotation classes.
- **Germs.** The germ groups are `Z^2 ⊃ <δ> = (V)_p`, with quotient `Z`.
- **Result.** `Γ` is `F_∞`, full and clopen transitive, so it is in `B_A`. It has
  `Γ^ab ≅ Z^4`, so it is not simple, but type (A) gives the simple envelope.
- **Where it sits.** `Z^2 * Z` is not in `V` (Bleak--Salazar-Díaz). `Γ` is not a finite-nucleus
  RSG, since its rational germ groups are not virtually cyclic.

**The request "a non-product structure for F_2 × F_2 that passes E1".**
- **Interleaving fails.** In the normal form `u_1 v_1 u_2 v_2 ...`, prepending a first-factor
  letter shifts the phase of the whole interleaving, so it is singular on all of
  `∂F_2 × ∂F_2`.
- **Diagonal-first fails for Z^3.** The Z^2 trick is to put the diagonal first, as in
  `(ab)^m a^n`, where prepending equals appending by commutativity. In the diagonal-first form on
  `Z^3`, the corners of each 2-face stratum `(a_1a_2a_3)^m (a_1a_2)^∞` are singular for `a_1`
  for every `m`. That is infinitely many singular points, so E1' fails.
- **Why it doesn't matter.** Both `F_2 × F_2` and `Z^3` are subgroups of `V`, so they have
  actions with no singular points at all. The automatic-structure boundary is the wrong source
  for them.
- **What the method is for.** Groups outside `V` that have finitely-singular prefix-replacement
  actions, such as `Z^2 * Z`. The next natural targets are `Z^2 *_Z Z^2`-type amalgams and
  graph manifold pieces. Their Seifert parts `F × Z` contain `F_2 × Z`, which is in `V`; the
  difficulty is the gluing.
