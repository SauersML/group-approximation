# A finite stable-letter presentation closing the Jacobson recursion

2026-09-08. Adding seven explicit conjugation relations to `Theta`
forces its entire finite boundary packet to recur at every shifted
level. A bilateral linear action retains the original head mark, so
these additional relations are consistent with that mark. This is a
new finitely presented candidate; no assertion that the new relations
already hold in `Theta` is made.

The second part gives a precise operator-norm variational problem for
the new candidate. All finite packets and all stable-letter relations
on the original finite packet can be made exact in the original
matrix dimension. Three mixed errors remain, and their uniform positive
lower bound is not proved here.

## 1. Finite groups, their embeddings, and a finite generator list

Use the reconstructed groups and the full intersection proved in
[the opposite-root construction](jacobson-full-boundary-opposite-roots-and-gl6-packet-2026-09-08.md)
and [the intersection calculation](jacobson-gl6-boundary-compatible-regular-profiles-2026-09-08.md):

```text
G=GL_3(F_2),
B=K x delta(G) ~= GL_6(F_2) x GL_3(F_2),
C=C_0 x C_far ~= GL_5(F_2) x GL_3(F_2),
Z=B intersect C=C_0 x delta(K_P),
K_P=GL(<e_1,e_3>) <= G.
```

In `K`, the ordered coordinates are

```text
e_(1,0),e_(2,0),e_(3,0),e_(1,1),e_(2,1),e_(3,1).
```

The original head `g_Q` acts on the first three coordinates.
The homomorphism `tau:G->K` acts on the last three. The original
tail element is

```text
g_P=tau(g)delta(g),
F={g_Q g'_P : g,g' in G} ~= G x G.
```

The group `C_0` fixes `e_(2,1)` and acts on the other five
coordinates. Its copy of the common `delta(K_P)` in `C_far`
acts on coordinates `1,3` of that factor. These statements specify
both finite embeddings of `Z`.

Let `h_0 in C_0` be the transposition of `e_(2,0),e_(1,1)`.
In the product coordinates of `C`, put

```text
h=(h_0,w_12),       h_1=(1,w_12)=h_0 h.
```

The three matrices

```text
Sigma={c=x_13, k=x_31, q=w_23}                       (1)
```

generate `G`. Indeed `c,k` generate the elementary roots in
coordinates `1,3`; conjugating by `q` provides the roots in
coordinates `1,2`; their commutators provide the remaining two.
Elementary row operations over `F_2` generate `GL_3(F_2)`.

Retain the two mixed relators defining `Theta`:

```text
r=x_23,P,       a=x_23,Q,
u=[h c_P h,k_P],
b=q_Q q_P,
W=[u,h(a r)h] a r,
(h b)^3=1,      W=1.                                (2)
```

The group `Theta` is generated by `Sigma_Q`, `Sigma_P`, and `h`.
This follows either from its original finite packets or from the fact
that `G_Q` and the shifted group `<c_P,k_P,h>` generate `C`.

## 2. Seven additional relations

Adjoin one generator `t` and the following seven equations:

```text
t g_Q t^-1=tau(g)       for g in Sigma,
t g_P t^-1=delta(g)     for g in Sigma,
t h t^-1=h_1.                                         (3)
```

Call the resulting group `Theta_shift`. The symbols `tau(g)` and
`delta(g)` are the elements already reconstructed as words in `Theta`.
Equivalently, use the two finite factors `B,C`, identify `Z`, impose
(2), and impose (3). Finite multiplication tables give an entirely
finite presentation for each factor.

Sending `t` to the integer `1` and every original generator to zero
gives a surjective homomorphism from `Theta_shift` to the additive
group of integers: all seven new relators have total `t` exponent zero.

The latter formulation only adjoins the already established finite
packet `B`. To see explicitly that it introduces no independent
generators beyond the old finite packets, note that `F,C_0` generate
`B`: conjugation of the elementary roots of `C_0` by `q_P` supplies
roots involving the missing coordinate `e_(2,1)`. The resulting roots
generate `K`, and then `delta(g)=tau(g)^(-1)g_P` supplies the second
factor. All those identities hold in the reconstructed `B` in `Theta`.

Since `Sigma` generates `G`, the first six equations in (3) imply
the corresponding identities for every `g in G`. Thus conjugation
by `t` sends the original finite packet `F` to

```text
F_1=tau(G) x delta(G) <= B                            (4)
```

by the specified isomorphism `alpha_F:F->F_1`.

## 3. A literal bilateral model retaining the head

Let `V_Z` be the vector space over `F_2` with basis
`{e_(i,n):1<=i<=3, n in Z}` and finite-support vectors. Extend
the literal half-line Jacobson operators by the identity at every
negative level. In particular,

```text
g_Q acts naturally at level 0 and fixes every other level;
g_P acts naturally at each level n>=1 and fixes levels n<=0;
h exchanges e_(2,n) with e_(1,n+1) for every n>=0,
  and fixes the other basis vectors.
```

These operators retain all of the original `Theta` relations; the
negative-level summand is simply an added trivial representation.
Define the invertible linear operator

```text
T e_(i,n)=e_(i,n+1)             for every integer n.       (5)
```

There is no unilateral-inverse issue: (5) is a bijection of the
bilateral basis. Direct conjugation gives

```text
T g_Q T^-1 acts naturally at level 1 = tau(g),
T g_P T^-1 acts naturally at levels n>=2 = delta(g),
T h T^-1 exchanges e_(2,n),e_(1,n+1) for n>=1 = h_1.
```

Thus sending `t` to `T` satisfies all seven equations (3).
The original mark `w=x_13,Q` still sends `e_(3,0)` to
`e_(3,0)+e_(1,0)`, and is nonidentity. Both finite packets
`B,C` retain their faithful literal actions.

This proves nontriviality of the mark in `Theta_shift`, without
claiming that the entire original `Theta` embeds in the new group.
The linear action is an algebraic consistency witness over `F_2`,
not a claimed complex unitary or MF representation.

## 4. The recursive boundary relation now follows

The old boundary identity is an exact relation in the original `C`:

```text
[u,h a h]=a,                  a=x_23,Q.                  (6)
```

Conjugating (6) by `t` and applying (3) yields

```text
[u_1,h_1 tau(x_23) h_1]=tau(x_23),
u_1=[h_1 delta(c) h_1,delta(k)].                         (7)
```

This is precisely the previously missing recursive boundary relation.
More is true: the entire finite group `C` is transported by the one
conjugation, not just the individual relation (6). Its new head is
`tau(G)`, its new shifted generators are `delta(c),delta(k),h_1`,
and all of its finite multiplication-table relations hold exactly.

Iteration of the same inner automorphism transports all finite-packet
and mixed relations at every integer shift. For nonnegative shifts,
the literal images are the expected successive half-line Jacobson
packets. Thus recursive closure has been proved for the new candidate.
This step does not prove a uniform matrix obstruction.

## 5. An exact finite-packet variational problem

Consider the following data, over all positive dimensions `d`:

```text
rho_B:B->U(d),        rho_C:C->U(d),
rho_B|Z=rho_C|Z,
rho_B(w)!=I,
T in U(d),
T rho_B(f) T^*=rho_B(alpha_F(f))     for every f in F.
```

Use the same `H=rho_C(h)` everywhere, and set
`H_1=rho_C(h_1)`. Evaluate `b,W` using (2) in the common
matrix space. Define

```text
gamma_shift = inf max( ||(H rho_B(b))^3-I||,
                       ||W-I||,
                       ||T H T^*-H_1|| ).                (8)
```

The finite packets, their full intersection, and the stable-letter
conjugations on `F` are exact in this definition. Among the seven
new relations (3), only the `h` relation remains in the objective.
The two original mixed relators remain as well; this proof does not
derive their vanishing from the new stable-letter equation.

The domain is nonempty. On dimension `d=|B|`, take the regular
representation of `B` and `2016` copies of the regular representation
of `C`, glued on `Z` as in the intersection artifact. The two
representations of `F` given by `rho_B(f)` and
`rho_B(alpha_F(f))` are both `|B|/|F|` copies of its regular
representation: the two subgroups have the same finite order.
They therefore have a unitary intertwiner `T`. The head survives.
All distances in (8) are between unitaries, so

```text
0<=gamma_shift<=2.                                      (9)
```

## 6. Exactifying all stable-letter relations on F simultaneously

Let `rho_0,rho_1:F->U(d)` be exact representations and let
`T in U(d)` satisfy

```text
epsilon=max_(f in F)||rho_1(f)T-T rho_0(f)||<1.
```

Define the averaged intertwiner

```text
X=(1/|F|) sum_(f in F) rho_1(f) T rho_0(f)^*.             (10)
```

Then `rho_1(f)X=X rho_0(f)` and `||X-T||<=epsilon`.
In particular `X` is invertible. Its unitary polar factor `T'`
is also an intertwiner. Every singular value of `X` lies in
`[1-epsilon,1+epsilon]`, so

```text
||T'-X||<=epsilon,       ||T'-T||<=2epsilon.              (11)
```

Consequently the seven stable-letter relations can be reduced to
the single remaining error involving `h`, with

```text
||T'H(T')^*-H_1|| <= ||THT^*-H_1||+4epsilon.             (12)
```

The original mixed errors in (8) do not involve `T` and are
unchanged by this correction. The averaging group is the fixed
finite group `F`; no estimate for an infinite subgroup is used.

Only the six generator errors need be tested. Put

```text
d_F=max_(s in Sigma_Q union Sigma_P)
          ||rho_1(s)T-T rho_0(s)||.
```

The Cayley graph of `G` on `Sigma` is connected and has
`|G|=(8-1)(8-2)(8-4)=168` vertices. Removing loops from a
shortest path bounds its length by `167`. Decomposing the two
coordinates of `F=G x G` separately therefore gives length at
most `334` in the six displayed involutions. Telescoping exact
representation products yields

```text
epsilon<=334 d_F.
```

Thus if `334 d_F<1`, one can choose the exact intertwiner with
`||T'-T||<=668 d_F`, increasing the `h` error by at most
`1336 d_F`.

For completeness, let `eta_shift` be the infimum over the same
exact `B,C,Z` data and nontrivial head as in Section 5, but with
`T` unrestricted and objective the maximum of the three errors
in (8) and the six generator errors `d_F`. Then

```text
eta_shift<=gamma_shift<=1337 eta_shift.                  (12a)
```

The first inequality restricts to the tuples with exact `F`
intertwining. For the second, take any unrestricted tuple with
maximum error `delta`. If `delta<1/334`, the preceding correction
gives an admissible tuple in (8) with maximum error at most
`delta+1336 delta`. Otherwise `gamma_shift<=2` from (9)
already implies `gamma_shift<=1337 delta`. Take the infimum.
This relates the exact-intertwiner problem to precisely nine
finite tests, independently of dimension; it does not prove
that either infimum is positive.

## 7. Positive gap is exactly the remaining marked corona obstruction

For the usual norm matrix corona
`Q_d=product M_(d_n)/directSum M_(d_n)`, one has

```text
gamma_shift>0
 iff every homomorphism Theta_shift->U(Q_d),
     for every dimension sequence, kills w.             (13)
```

If the infimum is zero, choose admissible tuples whose three mixed
errors tend to zero. Their exact finite relations and exact `F`
intertwining pass to the corona; the three remaining relations
also become exact. This gives a homomorphism from `Theta_shift`.
Every head image is a nonidentity involution, hence has distance
exactly two from the identity, so the mark survives in the corona.

Conversely, start with a corona homomorphism retaining `w`. Lift
the finitely many values in `B,C`, and `t`, to unitary matrix
sequences. Correct each finite group in its original dimension
using the finite-group correction lemma in
[the relative-rounding proof](jacobson-boundary-packet-relative-rounding-2026-09-08.md).
The corrections tend to zero in norm. The two restrictions to `Z`
are then exact representations which are asymptotically close.
The unitary polar factor of their finite averaged intertwiner makes
them agree exactly, again by a correction tending to zero.

Now the two copies of `F` inside the corrected `B` give the exact
representations `rho_0,rho_1` in (10), and the lifted stable letter
asymptotically intertwines them. Apply (10)--(11) to obtain an exact
intertwiner in the original dimension. Every remaining fixed-word
error still tends to zero. The exact head involutions represent
the original nonidentity corona element; infinitely many must be
nonidentity. Restricting to those coordinates yields admissible
tuples for (8) with objective tending to zero.

By Section 3 the mark is nontrivial in the abstract group. Thus a
strictly positive lower bound in (8) would prove this explicit new
group non-MF. No such lower bound is supplied here. In particular,
unitary conjugacy of `H,H_1` alone does not force a rank loss:
the commuting finite involution `H_0` permits balanced joint
eigenspaces in arbitrary complex representations.
