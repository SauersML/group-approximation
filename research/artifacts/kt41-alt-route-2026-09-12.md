# Kun–Thom 4.1 core: is there a shorter proof through what the repository already proves?

Lane `kt41-alt-route`, 2026-09-12. Campaign: the negative answer to Bowen–Chapman
arXiv:2511.06586, Problem 1.1, unconditional. The open theorem is the per-compressor
core consumed as `hcore` by
`hasSequentialCentralizerNormalization_of_kazhdan_infranormal_of_core`
(`KunThom/NormalizationProducer.lean`): `SeqNormalizes Γ t` for a compressor `t` of an
infranormal pair `Γ ≤ G` of Kazhdan groups, with `G` countable.

Write `A` for a sofic approximation of `G`, `u = A(t)`, `v` for a sequence that almost
commutes with `A(Γ)`, and `w = u v u⁻¹`. The goal is that `w` almost commutes with `A(Γ)`.
Since `u A(γ) u⁻¹ ≈ A(tγt⁻¹)`, the sequence `w` always almost commutes with `A(tΓt⁻¹)`.
The core therefore says that the centralizers of `σ(Γ)` and `σ(tΓt⁻¹)` in the universal
sofic group coincide. It is a co-Hopfian statement at component scale.

## Verdict

**Not shorter.** None of the four candidates reaches the core. Each of them either:
- proves an existence statement about one selected expander component, where
  normalization needs almost all of the mass; or
- is an exact co-Hopfian collapse, which needs an exact size that a sofic approximation
  does not carry.

Every stage of the §4 route in `kt-theorem-4-1-blueprint-2026-09-12.md` is load-bearing:
- the cluster groupoid with improvement (gap 1, landed at `2458bbc45`);
- the relative functor;
- the median step with property (T) of `G`;
- the integrality counting;
- patching and the Hamming estimate.

Two small savings inside that route do hold (§5). This lane opens no
`AltCompressorNormalization*` files.

## 1. The 2019 expander-centralizer theorem, and `Γ × ⟨v⟩` on good components

`KunThomTheorem.isLEF_of_exactProductExpansion` takes:
- a Kazhdan pair `(Q, κ)` of `K`;
- a `SoficApproximation (K × J)`;
- an eventual directed Cheeger lower bound for the first-factor labels on the whole
  model.

It concludes `IsLEF J`. `KunThomEssential.isLEF_of_matchingCertificate` first repairs a
selected graph into that exact form.

The proposed application to `Γ × ⟨v⟩` fails on three counts.

- **No sofic approximation of `Γ × ℤ`.** The sequence `v` is arbitrary. Its powers may
  stay close to the identity, or to model permutations `A(γ)`, so the combined map is
  not asymptotically faithful.
- **No expander on the whole model.** `Γ` expands only on the components of an
  expander decomposition. On each component the theorem's internal step applies:
  products of `η`-good almost-centralizers round to `η`-good ones. That per-component
  step is Kun–Thom Lemma 4.2 / Alekseev–Thom Proposition 3.3, which is exactly gap 1.
  It is landed as `CentralizerNormalizationImprove.ComponentFamily.eventually_improveCloseAt`,
  and `kt41-g1-alt-route-2026-09-12.md` records that a direct transfer only
  re-proves it.
- **The conclusion is vacuous.** `IsLEF ℤ` holds, so the theorem itself carries no
  information about `v`.

What "constraining `v` inside components" produces is Kun–Thom Lemma 4.2(4): `v` is
close to a patched bisection of the cluster groupoid. That is the bisection
representation step already assigned to the `kt41-bisection-rep` lanes.

Combining it with compression counting *between* components gives the partition-level
Proposition 3.1. By `kt-component-counting-route-2026-09-12.md` and
`kt-norm-fixedpoint-route-2026-09-12.md`, that cannot see the commutation failures of
`w`, which live inside components. The within-component cluster group and the
between-component matching meet in exactly one place: the counting of orbit sizes
`o(i)` and isotropy orders `k(i)` in §4.

## 2. The compression criterion

`isLEF_of_ambientDecomposition` and `isLEF_of_soficApproximation`
(`Criterion/CriterionAssembly.lean`) conclude `IsLEF J` for a factor `J` that commutes
with the compressed copy. Their proof runs through `SelectionOutput`: a weighted
diagonal selection of **one** expanding source component that carries a localized
approximation of `Γ × J`, followed by `KunThomEssential`.

That suffices for LEF, because one good component of positive mass is enough. It does
not suffice for normalization, for two reasons.

- **The core needs almost all of the mass.** A compressor moves components:
  `u C_i ≈ C_{π i}`. The finite co-Hopfian step compares the cluster group of `C_{π i}`
  with that of `C_i` on *different* objects. So the co-Hopfian step on one component
  would need `k(π i) = k(i)`, which is a conservation statement over all of the mass.
- **With `J = PUnit` the criterion says nothing.** That is the setup that
  `CompressionSetup.ofCompressor` builds, and its conclusion is trivially true.

## 3. Exact co-Hopfian collapses

The following modules are all one-line collapses under an exact invariant size:
- `Criterion/ExactCompression`: fixed sets and fixed subspaces;
- `Criterion/FiniteQuotientBlindness`: `invariant-size-collapse`;
- `Criterion/CommutantRigidity` and `FiniteDimensionalKill`: finite-dimensional
  commutants;
- `Sofic/FreeLampRigidity`: the free-lamp witness in every finite-dimensional
  representation.

`Analysis/KazhdanProjectionOneSidedOrder` gives `U* P U ≤ P` for the Kazhdan projection
`P`. Turning that one-sided order into equality needs a size on projections that the
approximation preserves. Operator norm preserves rank. Normalized Hamming distance
preserves no rank, and the `II₁` trace is refinement-blind (`invariant-size-collapse`).

A sofic approximation is not an action. The graph already records where each transfer
to an approximate setting stops:
- **Rank metric.** `rank-ultraproduct-compressors-conserve-fixed-right-ideals` conserves
  only the linear-scale layer, and `linear-scale-rank-conservation-does-not-kill-defects`
  shows that layer is compatible with nontrivial defects.
- **Tracial ultraproduct.** The analogue is `kt-centralizer-normalization-hs`, which is
  open. `hs-normalization-needs-coarse-actor-scale-pinning` records the missing input:
  an exact rounding of the actor would make the median unnecessary, and nothing
  produces one.

## 4. Why the median step cannot be dropped

Let `R` be the retained objects and `F` the relative functor, with `F(π i) = i`.
- `F` is faithful, so for `π i ∈ R` it embeds `Aut(π i)` into `Aut(i)`, giving
  `k(π i) ≤ k(i)`.
- `F` is injective on objects, so for a clean orbit
  `(1 − ζ) o(π i) ≤ o(i)`.

The index `k(i)/k(π i)` is an integer. Normalization needs it to equal `1` on most of the
mass. Conservation along `π` gives that only in the following limited forms.

- **Bounded observables.** For `1/k`, or for `k(i)/|C_i|`, conservation gives an `L¹`
  bound of order the exceptional mass. An index of `2` changes `1/k` by only `1/k(i)`,
  and `k/|C|` by only `k(i)/(2|C_i|)`. So the bound counts bad *objects*, not their mass,
  and components have size tending to infinity.
- **`log k`.** This observable is bounded by `log |Y_n|`. Conservation excludes an
  index of at least `2` on mass `1 − δ` only when the exceptional mass is
  `o(1/log |Y_n|)`, and nothing forces that rate.
  - Example: a `π`-cycle of `L` objects of equal mass, with
    `k = 2^{L−1}, …, 2, 1` and one jump back.
  - The jump is the exceptional set, of mass `1/L`, and `log |Y| ≈ L`.
  - `1/k` and `k/|C|` are conserved up to `O(1/L)`, yet the index is `2` on mass
    `1 − 1/L`.
  - This is the integer form of the cycle example in §3 of
    `kt-component-counting-route-2026-09-12.md`.
- **A weaker interface.** The consumers only need commutation on positive mass,
  because freeness then contradicts the witness. That does not help: excluding an
  index of at least `2` on almost all mass is the same `log k` conservation, with the
  same rate problem.

So one needs the clamp and the median pinning with property (T) of `G`. That layer is
already landed as `ComponentCountingNormalizationMedian`, with `deviation_negligible`
and `drop_negligible`.

## 5. Two savings inside the §4 route

**(S1) One median application instead of two.** Put `f(i) = |Hom(i, −)| = o(i) k(i)`.
- The functor gives `(1 − ζ) f(π i) ≤ f(i)` on clean retained objects.
- Suppose the median step gives `f(π i) ≥ (1 − ε) f(i)` on most of the mass. Then,
  since `k(π i) ≤ k(i)`, we get `o(π i)/o(i) ≥ 1 − ε`.
- Since `(1 − ζ) o(π i) ≤ o(i)`, we get `k(π i)/k(i) ≥ (1 − ε)(1 − ζ)`.
- For `ε, ζ ≤ 1/4` the isotropy ratio exceeds `1/2`. So
  `map_bijective_of_isotropy_lt_two_mul` applies, and the orbit ratio feeds
  `nonempty_hom_of_card_orbit_lt`
  (`KunThom/ComponentCountingNormalizationGroupoid.lean`).

This replaces separate median arguments for `o` and `k` by one application to their
product.

**(S2) Involutive sequences suffice.** Double the model to `Y_n ⊕ Y_n`, with `G` acting
diagonally. This is again a sofic approximation, since the multiplicative defects and
the distances to the identity are unchanged. Let `ṽ` send `(y, 0)` to `(v y, 1)` and
`(y, 1)` to `(v⁻¹ y, 0)`.
- **Involution and almost commutation.** `ṽ` is an involution. Its commutation defect
  with `A(γ) ⊕ A(γ)` is the mean of the defects of `v` and `v⁻¹`, and these two
  defects are equal.
- **Conjugation.** Conjugating `ṽ` by `u ⊕ u` gives the swap built from `w`. On the
  original model, the defect of `w` is at most twice the defect of that swap.

So `SeqNormalizes` for involutive sequences implies `SeqNormalizes`. This matters only
if Lemma 4.2(4) is easier for involutions. `Sofic/InvolutiveCentralizerComponents`
treats ambient involutions only.
