## 1. Scope (D1–D7, C1–C4)

The definitions pin down the covered class:
- **(D7): extra relations must lie in the base closure `ℬ`.** This is the commutative subring generated by the base
  generators and their transports `t_w b s_w`.
- **(D3): substitutions are integer polynomials.**
- **(D5): availability is a Boolean polynomial in the zero-indicators `e_P`.**
- **Diagonal modules** fix how every base generator acts, by evaluation at values in any field `⊇ Q`. That is all
  Lemma 1 needs.
- Covariance in (D6) is imposed only for registers. Lemma 1 concerns actions on diagonal modules, not identities in
  `R(𝒫)`, so no covariance for indicators, reciprocals or resolvents is needed.
- **Consistent.**

## 2. The proof

**Lemma 1. Correct.**
- Base generators give constructible piecewise-rational functions: `Y`, `[P=0]`, `1/P` off `{P=0}`, and `1/Q` on
  `{Q ≠ 0}`.
- Sums and products are handled by common refinement.
- Transports: `s_xη = xη` exactly when `α_x(v(η)) = 1`, and `v(xη) = σ_x(v(η))`. So
  `F_{t_x f s_x} = α_x · (F_f∘σ_x)`.
- Preimages of constructible cells under a polynomial map are constructible, and the denominators stay nowhere zero
  on the pulled-back cells.

**The generic cell.** A finite constructible partition of an irreducible space has exactly one cell containing a
nonempty open set. Two disjoint cells cannot both contain one, since nonempty opens meet.

- **W1.** The proof needs `{P_f ≠ 0} ⊆ C_f` over every field `K ⊇ Q`: over `Q` in Lemma 2, over `Q(δ)` in §3
  Part 1. Choose `P_f` as the product of all nonzero polynomials that occur in the Boolean description of `f`'s
  partition, resolvents included.
  - Then `{P_f ≠ 0}` is the atom on which all of them are nonzero.
  - That atom is contained in a single cell over every field, and that cell is the generic cell.
  - As written, "choose `P_f` with `{P_f ≠ 0} ⊆ C_f`" leaves open whether the inclusion was checked over `Q` only.

**Lemma 2. Correct.**
- `M_Z`'s values are dense (C3), and removing `{P_f = 0}` keeps them dense, by irreducibility.
- `a/b = 0` on this dense set forces `a = 0`.

**§3. Correct.**
- `P_* = Π P_{f_j} · Π Q_i`, and (C4)'s third bullet applies to it, type by type.
- At `δ`-dependent values the extra relations vanish (Lemma 2 plus W1), and the extra resolvents are invertible.
- At `δ`-free values these are values of `M_Z`.
- So `M_k` is a module of the full presentation, with the extra resolvents acting diagonally. That is compatible
  with (D6), because diagonal operators commute with every `s_yt_y`.
- `X_k = 1` on chain states: `λ_k = 1`, and `Δ ∉ Z` makes every `ε_i = 0`.
- `X_k = 0` on `M_Z`, by (C2).
- Hence the kernel of `R(𝒫) → R_conc` is nonzero, and it is proper because `R_conc ≠ 0`.

**Nothing uncited.** The inputs are:
- irreducibility of affine space over an infinite field;
- the unique dense cell of a finite constructible partition;
- a polynomial vanishing on a Zariski-dense set is `0`;
- polynomial preimages of constructible sets are constructible.

All four are elementary and used within their hypotheses.

## 3. Loopholes

Each loophole is outside the **statement**, because it violates an explicit hypothesis:
- **Dilation fibres:** (C1), and hence (C4), which asks for a `c`-chain.
- **`sl_2`-type relations:** they are not in `ℬ`, so outside (D7).
- **Non-register hosts:** outside (D1)–(D6).
- **Sparse models:** (C3), which Lemma 2 needs.

**W3.** §3 never uses (C1) directly. (C1) enters only through the `c`-chain required in (C4). So the argument would
also run with any `δ`-generic chain of states that meets (C2)–(C4). The dilation loophole is therefore a loophole of
this statement, not shown to escape the mechanism. Say "not covered by this theorem", not "escapes".

## 4. The capacity instance

- **(C1).** `σ_c(Δ) = Δ − 1` and `σ_c(L) = L`. `α_c = p_u − e_Δ` is `1` when `Δ ∉ Z`. **Met.**
- **(C2).**
  - `ε_i = t_c^i e_Δ s_c^i` acts as `[Δ = i]` on **every** diagonal module: the prefix `c^i` is legal iff
    `Δ, …, Δ−i+1 ≠ 0`, and then `e_Δ` reads `[Δ = i]`.
  - `λ_k = t_d t_st ε_{k−1} s_st s_d` acts as `[L = k]` on every diagonal module: `N(dη) = L(η)`; `st` is legal iff
    `L(η) ≠ 0`, and then `Δ = L(η) − 1`.
  - The concrete `u`-values with `L = k` have `Δ ∈ {0..k} = F_k`.
  - **Met.**
- **(C3).**
  - `v`-values: `N ∈ Z_{>=0}`, dense in `Q`.
  - `u`-values: the integer triangle `0 <= Δ <= L`. It is dense in `Q^2`: a polynomial vanishing there vanishes on
    `L = l` at `l+1` points for every `l`, hence identically.
  - **Met.**
- **(C4).**
  - The value bookkeeping holds: `v`-values are integers, `u`-values are `(δ+j, k')` with `k' >= k` or triangle
    points, and triangle points are values of `M_Z`.
  - `P(δ+j, k') = 0` iff `(L − k') | P`, since `δ + j` is transcendental over `Q`. This happens for at most `deg_L P`
    values of `k'`.
  - The module is over `Q(δ)`, with `δ` transcendental over `Q`. That is legitimate: diagonal modules allow any field
    `⊇ Q`.
  - **Met.** This rests on the §2 verification of `capacity-chain-presentation-is-not-simple-proof`, which I did not
    review.
- **W2 (wording).** The instance says "any finite set of extra commutative relations true in its concrete model".
  The theorem covers relations **in the base closure `ℬ`** (D7). A commutative relation among arbitrary elements of
  `R(𝒫)` need not lie in `ℬ`. Say "any finite set of extra relations from `ℬ` true in its concrete model".
