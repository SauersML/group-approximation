## 0. Conventions and imported tools

- `Gamma` is finitely generated and `S` is a finite symmetric generating set.
- `G = Cay(Gamma,S)` has edges `{x, xs}`, and `Gamma` acts on the left by graph
  automorphisms.
- For a word `w = s_1...s_l` in `S` and a vertex `x`, `P(x,w)` is the path
  `x, x s_1, ..., x s_1...s_l`. Left multiplication maps `P(x,w)` to `P(gx,w)`.
- A random subgraph with invariant law means a Borel probability measure on
  `{0,1}^{E(G)}` invariant under `Gamma`.

**Mass-transport principle (MTP).** Let `f(x,y,omega) >= 0` be measurable with
`f(gx,gy,g omega) = f(x,y,omega)`, where `omega` has invariant law. Then
`E sum_y f(o,y,omega) = E sum_x f(x,o,omega)`. For a Cayley graph with its
left action this is the standard unimodular MTP. Its proof is one line:
`E f(o, y, omega) = E f(y^{-1}, o, omega)` by invariance, and one sums over
`y`. It is also the tool used in `fpbs-soft-collapse-iff-invariant-sparse-spines-proof`.

## 1. Lemma 1 (commensurability)

**(a) Change of generators, and finite-index overgroups.** Let
`Gamma' <= Gamma` have finite index `k`, let `S'` and `S` be finite symmetric
generating sets of `Gamma'` and `Gamma`, and suppose `G' = Cay(Gamma',S')` has
invariant sparse spines. The case `Gamma' = Gamma` is change of generators.

1. For each `s' in S'`, fix an `S`-word `w_{s'}` representing `s'`. Let `U` be
   the finite set of prefixes of the words `w_{s'}`.
2. Given a spine `L'` of `G'` with density `<= eps`, let `L_0` be the union of
   the paths `P(x, w_{s'})` over the edges `{x, xs'}` of `L'`, taken from both
   endpoints.
   - `L_0` is connected, since every edge of `L'` is replaced by a path joining
     its endpoints.
   - `L_0` is infinite and contains `V(L')`.
   - Its law is `Gamma'`-invariant.
   - A vertex `v` of `V(L_0)` has the form `x u` with `x in V(L')` and
     `u in U`. So `P(v in V(L_0)) <= sum_{u in U} P(v u^{-1} in V(L')) <= |U| eps`,
     where terms with `v u^{-1} notin Gamma'` vanish and the others are
     `<= eps` by `Gamma'`-invariance.
3. Let `c_1, ..., c_k` represent the left cosets of `Gamma'`, and let `nu_0` be
   the law of `L_0`. Put `nu = (1/k) sum_i (c_i)_* nu_0`.
   - For `gamma' in Gamma'`, `(c gamma')_* nu_0 = c_* nu_0`. So `nu` depends
     only on the cosets.
   - For `h in Gamma`, `h_* nu = (1/k) sum_i (h c_i)_* nu_0 = nu`, because left
     multiplication by `h` permutes the cosets.
   - A sample of `nu` is a translate of a sample of `nu_0`, hence connected and
     infinite, with `P(v in V) = (1/k) sum_i P(c_i^{-1} v in V(L_0)) <= |U| eps`.

**(b) Finite-index subgroups.** Let `Gamma' <= Gamma` have index `k`, and let
`L` be a spine of `G = Cay(Gamma,S)` with density `<= eps`. Its law is in
particular `Gamma'`-invariant.

1. Write `Gamma = disjoint union_i Gamma' g_i` (right cosets, which are the
   `Gamma'`-orbits for the left action). Define `rho(gamma' g_i) = gamma'`.
   Then `rho` is `Gamma'`-equivariant and `rho(x)^{-1} x` lies in
   `{g_1, ..., g_k}`.
2. For an edge `{x, xs}` of `G`, `rho(x)^{-1} rho(xs) = g_i s g_j^{-1}` lies in
   a finite set `F subset Gamma'`. For each `f in F`, fix an `S'`-word `w'_f`,
   and let `U'` be the set of its prefixes.
3. Let `L'` be the union over edges `{x, xs}` of `L`, from both endpoints, of
   the paths `P'(rho(x), w'_{rho(x)^{-1} rho(xs)})` in `Cay(Gamma',S')`.
   - `L'` is connected, because consecutive images are joined.
   - `L'` is infinite, because `rho` is at most `k`-to-one.
   - Its law is `Gamma'`-invariant.
   - `P(v in V(L')) <= sum_{u in U'} sum_i P(v u^{-1} g_i in V(L)) <= k |U'| eps`.

In both cases the density bound tends to `0` with `eps`, which proves Lemma 1. ∎

## 2. Theorem 1 (finitely generated normal subgroup of infinite index)

**Setup.**
- Let `N` be generated by `n_1, ..., n_m`, written as `S`-words `w_1, ..., w_m`,
  and let `U` be the set of prefixes of these words, so `|U| <= 1 + sum_j |w_j|`.
- Let `Q = Gamma/N`, with `pi: Gamma -> Q`. `Q` is infinite, and `Gamma` acts
  on `Q` by `g.q = pi(g) q`.
- Let `D = pi(U) subset Q`.

**Sheets.** For `q in Q`, let `Sh_q = union_{y in pi^{-1}(q), j} P(y, w_j)`.

- *Connected.* `pi^{-1}(q) = yN`. The path `P(y n, w_j)` joins `yn` to
  `y n n_j`, and `P(y n n_j^{-1}, w_j)` joins `y n n_j^{-1}` to `yn`. Since the
  `n_j` generate `N` as a group, all of `yN` lies in one component of `Sh_q`,
  and every vertex of `Sh_q` lies on a path starting in `yN`.
- *Location.* Every vertex of `Sh_q` is `y u` with `u in U`, so it lies in
  `pi^{-1}(q D)`.
- *Equivariance.* `g Sh_q = Sh_{pi(g) q}`.

**Randomness.** All of the following are independent:
- `a = (a_q)_{q in Q}`, iid Bernoulli(`eps`); put `A = {q : a_q = 1}`;
- `lambda = (lambda_x)_{x in Gamma}`, iid uniform on `[0,1]`;
- for every `x in Gamma`, an iid sequence `(s^x_n)_{n >= 1}` uniform on `S`.

`Gamma` acts by `(g a)_q = a_{pi(g)^{-1} q}`, `(g lambda)_y = lambda_{g^{-1} y}`
and `(g s)^y = s^{g^{-1} y}`. Each field is an iid field on a countable set that
`Gamma` permutes, so the joint law is invariant.

**Connectors.**
1. For `x in Gamma`, let `X^x_n = x s^x_1 ... s^x_n`. For `x` with
   `pi(x) in A`, let `T_x = inf{n >= 1 : pi(X^x_n) in A \ {pi(x)}}`.
2. *`T_x < infinity` a.s.* The projection `pi(X^x_n) = pi(x) pi(s_1) ... pi(s_n)`
   is an irreducible random walk on the infinite group `Q`, since `pi(S)`
   generates `Q`.
   - If it is recurrent it visits every state.
   - If it is transient it visits each finite set finitely often.
   - Either way its range is infinite a.s. `A` is independent of the walk and
     iid Bernoulli(`eps`), so, conditionally on the walk, the probability that
     none of the first `r` distinct states other than `pi(x)` is in `A` is
     `(1-eps)^r -> 0`.
3. The connector `P_x` is the path `X^x_0, ..., X^x_{T_x}`. It is added exactly
   when `pi(x) in A` and `lambda_x < eta/(T_x + 1)`. Let `C` be the union of
   the added connectors, and put `L = union_{q in A} Sh_q ∪ C`.
4. *Equivariance.* In the configuration `g.(a, lambda, s)`, the walk from `gx`
   is `g X^x_n`, and its projection meets `pi(g) A` exactly when that of `X^x`
   meets `A`. So `T` and the added connectors transform covariantly, and
   `L(g config) = g L(config)`. The map is Borel, as a countable combination of
   coordinate events. So `L` has invariant law.

**Density.**
- *Sheets.* If `o in V(Sh_q)`, then `e = pi(o) in q D`, so `q in D^{-1}`. Hence
  `P(o in V(union_{q in A} Sh_q)) <= P(A ∩ D^{-1} ≠ ∅) <= |U| eps`.
- *Connectors.* Let `f(x,y) = 1{P_x added, y in V(P_x)}`, which is diagonally
  invariant. Then `sum_y f(o,y) <= (T_o + 1) 1{P_o added}`.
  Since `lambda_o` is independent of `(a, s)`,
  `E[(T_o+1) 1{P_o added}] = E[1{pi(o) in A} (T_o+1) min(1, eta/(T_o+1))] <= eta eps`.
  Also `sum_x f(x,o) >= 1{o in V(C)}`. By the MTP, `P(o in V(C)) <= eta eps`.
- Total: `P(o in V(L)) <= (|U| + eta) eps`.

**Connected and infinite.**
- `A` is infinite a.s., since `Q` is infinite. So `L` contains the infinite set
  `pi^{-1}(q)` for some `q`.
- Every added connector starts at `x in pi^{-1}(pi(x)) subset Sh_{pi(x)}` and
  ends at `X^x_{T_x} in pi^{-1}(A) subset union_{q in A} Sh_q`. Every sheet is
  connected. So it suffices to show that for all `q ≠ q'` in `Q`, a.s. on
  `{q, q' in A}` the sheets `Sh_q` and `Sh_{q'}` are joined in `L`. There are
  countably many pairs.

Fix `q ≠ q'`, and a geodesic `q = r_0, r_1, ..., r_l = q'` in
`Cay(Q, pi(S) \ {e})` with `r_{i+1} = r_i pi(t_i)` for some `t_i in S`. Its
vertices are distinct.

1. Condition on `a` with `q, q' in A`. The walks and labels are independent of
   `a`, so it suffices by Fubini to prove the statement for each fixed such `a`.
2. Let `0 = i_0 < i_1 < ... < i_k = l` be the indices `i` with `r_i in A`. Fix
   consecutive indices `i < i'`, and put `b = r_i`, `b' = r_{i'}` and
   `d = i' - i`.
3. For `x in pi^{-1}(b)`, let `E_x` be the event that
   `s^x_1, ..., s^x_d = t_i, ..., t_{i'-1}` and `lambda_x < eta/(d+1)`.
   - On `E_x` the projection of `X^x` visits `r_{i+1}, ..., r_{i'}`. These are
     distinct from `b`, and the first of them in `A` is `r_{i'} = b'`. So
     `T_x = d`, the connector is added, and it joins `Sh_b` to `Sh_{b'}`.
   - `P(E_x) = |S|^{-d} min(1, eta/(d+1)) > 0`, which is the same for every
     `x in pi^{-1}(b)`. The events `E_x` are independent across `x`, because
     they depend on disjoint coordinates.
   - `pi^{-1}(b) = yN` is infinite, so a.s. some `E_x` occurs (Borel--Cantelli
     II).
4. Chaining over `j = 0, ..., k-1` joins `Sh_q` to `Sh_{q'}`.

Hence `L` is a.s. connected and infinite, with density `<= (|U|+eta) eps`.
Letting `eps -> 0` gives invariant sparse spines on `G`. `S` was arbitrary. ∎

**Where normality is used, and why it cannot be dropped.** Step 3 needs the
success probability of an excursion from `x` to the next selected coset to be
the same for infinitely many `x` in one sheet. For normal `N`,
`pi(x s_1...s_d) = pi(x) pi(s_1)...pi(s_d)` depends on `x` only through
`pi(x)`. For a non-normal infinite finitely generated subgroup `H`, such as
`H = <a>` in `F_2 = <a,b>`, the walk from `x in yH` reaches a fixed coset `y'H`
with probabilities summable over `x`. Borel--Cantelli then gives nothing, and
the conclusion is false: `F_2` has cost `2`, so by `fpbs-sparse-spine-cost-bound`
(applied to the spine action times Bernoulli) it has no sparse spines.

## 3. Theorem 2 (infinite Kazhdan groups)

**Nonamenability.** If `Gamma` is amenable and infinite, the Følner vectors
`|F_n|^{-1/2} 1_{F_n}` are almost invariant unit vectors of the left regular
representation on `l^2(Gamma)`. Property (T) then gives a nonzero invariant
vector. That vector is a constant function in `l^2(Gamma)`, which forces
`Gamma` to be finite. So an infinite Kazhdan group is nonamenable, as
Theorem A requires.

**Import.** Hutchcroft--Pete, *Kazhdan groups have cost 1*, arXiv:1810.11015v2.
Text read on 2026-09-17 from `https://arxiv.org/html/1810.11015v2`. The
following is quoted verbatim.

- Definition (Section 2.1): "We define 𝒰(Γ) ⊆ {0,1}^{Γ×Γ} to be the set of
  graphs on Γ that have a unique infinite connected component."
- Recursion (Section 2.2): "Fix p ∈ (0,1), and let μ_1 be the law of
  Bernoulli-p site percolation on G. For each i ≥ 1, we recursively define
  μ_{i+1} to be the law of the random configuration ω ∈ {0,1}^V obtained as
  follows: 1. Let ω_1, ω_2 ∈ {0,1}^V be independent random variables each with
  law μ_i. 2. Let η_1 and η_2 be obtained from ω_1 and ω_2 respectively by
  choosing to either delete or retain each cluster independently at random with
  retention probability q(p) := (1−√(1−p))/p ∈ (1/2, 1). 3. Let ω be the union
  of the configurations η_1 and η_2. It follows by induction that if G is a
  Cayley graph of a finitely generated group Γ then μ_i ∈ M(Γ,Ω) for every
  i ≥ 1."
- Proposition 2.2: "Let G = (V,E) be a connected, locally finite graph, let
  p ∈ (0,1) and let (μ_i)_{i≥1} be as above. Then μ_i({ω : ω(u) = 1}) = p for
  every i ≥ 1 and u ∈ V and μ_i weak∗ converges to the measure
  p δ_V + (1−p) δ_∅ as i → ∞."
- Corollary 2.7 (Condensation): "Let G be a Cayley graph of a countably infinite
  Kazhdan group, let p ∈ (0,1) and let (μ_i)_{i≥1} be as above. Then
  i_freq < ∞."
- Proof of Theorem 1.2: "Recall that every countable Kazhdan group is finitely
  generated [4, Theorem 1.3.1]. Let G = (V,E) be a Cayley graph of Γ, let
  p ∈ (0,1), and let (μ_i)_{i≥1} be as above. It follows from Corollaries 2.7
  and 2.6 that 1 ≤ i_freq < ∞ and that μ_{i_freq} is supported on ℱ. Let
  ω ∈ {0,1}^V be sampled from μ_{i_freq}, so that ω ∈ ℱ almost surely. Fatou's
  lemma implies that the total frequency of all components of ω is at most 1
  almost surely, and consequently that ω has at most finitely many components
  of maximal frequency almost surely. Let ω′ be obtained from ω by choosing one
  of the maximum-frequency components of ω uniformly at random, retaining this
  component, and deleting all other components of ω, so that ω′ has a unique
  infinite cluster almost surely. Let η ∈ {0,1}^{Γ×Γ} be defined by setting
  η(u,v) = 1 if and only if u and v are adjacent in G and have
  ω′(u) = ω′(v) = 1, and let ν be the law of η, so that ν ∈ M(Γ, 𝒰(Γ))."
- After Theorem 1.2: "The proof of Theorem 1.2 establishes that for every ε > 0
  and every finite symmetric generating set S of Γ, there is a Γ-invariant
  measure on connected, spanning subgraphs of the associated Cayley graph with
  average degree at most 2 + ε."

**Deduction.** Fix any Cayley graph `G` of `Gamma` and `p in (0,1)`. Let `eta`
be as in the quoted proof, with invariant law `nu in M(Gamma, U(Gamma))`, and
let `L` be the unique infinite connected component of `eta`.

1. The map from `eta in U(Gamma)` to its unique infinite component is Borel and
   equivariant. So `L` is a random connected infinite subgraph of `G` with
   invariant law. Its edges are edges of `G`, by the definition of `eta`.
2. Every vertex of `L` has `omega′ = 1`, hence `omega = 1`, since `omega′` only
   deletes components. By Proposition 2.2,
   `P(o in V(L)) <= mu_{i_freq}(omega(o) = 1) = p`.
3. Letting `p -> 0` gives invariant sparse spines on `G`. ∎

**Check of the imported uniform selection.** This is not needed beyond the
quote, but it guards the invariance claim. Let `M(omega)` be the finite
nonempty set of maximal-frequency clusters on `ℱ`, and let
`kappa_omega = |M(omega)|^{-1} sum_{C in M(omega)} delta_C`.

- `freq` is Borel and invariant ([HP] Lemma 2.3), and the cluster relation is
  Borel. So `omega -> kappa_omega(B)` is Borel for cylinder events `B`, for
  example `B = {A subset C, C ∩ B' = ∅}` with `A, B'` finite.
- `M(g omega) = g M(omega)` gives `kappa_{g omega} = g_* kappa_omega`. Hence
  `integral kappa_omega d mu_{i_freq}` is invariant, by invariance of
  `mu_{i_freq}`.

## 4. Corollary 3

1. By Theorems 1 and 2 and Lemma 1, every Cayley graph of every group in `K`
   has invariant sparse spines.
2. By Theorem A of `fpbs-soft-collapse-iff-invariant-sparse-spines`, every
   Cayley graph of a nonamenable group with invariant sparse spines carries a
   soft collapse family:
   - `omega_t = xi_t` for `t <= p_c`;
   - `omega_t = xi_{p_c} ∪ Omega_{n(t)}` for `t > p_c`.
   It satisfies (S1)--(S4) and has a unique infinite cluster for every
   `t > p_c`.
3. Suppose an argument derived nonuniqueness at some `t > p_c` from Bernoulli
   facts at `p <= p_c` together with (S1)--(S4) alone. Applied to that family,
   whose joint law through `p_c` is Bernoulli, it would give nonuniqueness at
   `t`, which is false for the family. So the argument is invalid on that graph.
4. The inference that fails is the passage from `p_c` to `p_c + eps`. Any valid
   argument must use a supercritical property that the family lacks:
   - independent increments above `p_c` (Russo, BK, sprinkling);
   - deletion tolerance, which is not claimed for these families. The spine
     levels are forced open, and nothing in Theorems 1 or 2 lets a single spine
     edge close with positive conditional probability;
   - mixing;
   - an FIID or cost structure.

**Membership checks for the examples in the target.**

- `H x K'` with `H` infinite finitely generated and `K'` infinite: `H x {e}` is
  normal of infinite index.
- Infinite finitely generated centre of infinite index: central subgroups are
  normal.
- `B_n` for `n >= 3`: the centre is `<Delta^2> ≅ Z`, of infinite index, and
  `B_3 / <Delta^2> ≅ PSL_2(Z)` contains `F_2`. For `n >= 3`, `B_n` contains
  `B_3`, so it is nonamenable.
- Fibred hyperbolic 3-manifold groups and surface-by-surface groups: the fibre
  group is finitely generated, normal, and of infinite index.
- The Kazhdan examples (`SL_n(Z)` for `n >= 3`, lattices in `Sp(n,1)` and
  `F_4^(-20)`, Żuk's and Ershov's groups) have (T) by their standard theorems.
  Only (T) and infiniteness are used.
