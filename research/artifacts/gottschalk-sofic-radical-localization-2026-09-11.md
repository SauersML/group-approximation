# Sofic-radical localization of Garden-of-Eden windows

Date: 2026-09-11. Fork `gottschalk-ca`. Handwritten proofs; no computation.

This note records which step of the Gromov--Weiss counting proof fails over
the binary Leavitt unit group, as a theorem about every countable group. It
then turns the failure into a design constraint on strict automata. No
injective nonsurjective automaton is constructed here.

## 0. Conventions

A cellular automaton over a group G with finite alphabet A computes

    tau(x)(g) = f((x(gm))_(m in M)),        M finite, 1 in M.

It commutes with (h.x)(g)=x(h^-1 g). An injective automaton has a left
inverse automaton sigma, with local rule d and finite memory S containing 1,
satisfying sigma tau = id. Its inverse on the compact image is continuous and
equivariant, and the local rule may be extended arbitrarily off image patterns.
A pattern p in A^Omega, with Omega finite, is a *Garden of Eden* for tau if no
x has tau(x)|_Omega = p.

**Models.** For a finite F in G containing 1 and delta >= 0, an (F,delta)-model
is a finite set V with a map phi: F -> Sym(V) such that phi(1)=id and

    d(phi(a)phi(b), phi(ab)) <= delta      whenever a, b, ab lie in F,

where d(pi,pi') = |{v : pi(v) != pi'(v)}| / |V| is the normalized Hamming
distance. It is bi-invariant. Nothing is assumed about freeness: points may
collide arbitrarily.

**Sofic radical.** Rad(G) is the set of g in G such that for every eps>0 there
are a finite F containing g and delta>0 with d(phi(g),id) <= eps in every
(F,delta)-model. In ultraproduct language, Rad(G) is the intersection of the
kernels of all homomorphisms from G to metric ultraproducts of symmetric groups.
The finitary definition is the one used below.

### Lemma 1. Rad(G) is a normal subgroup, and for countable G, Rad(G)=1 iff G is sofic.

*Closure.* For g,h in Rad(G) and eps>0, take (F_g,delta_g) and (F_h,delta_h)
witnessing eps/3. Put F = F_g union F_h union {g,h,gh} and
delta = min(delta_g, delta_h, eps/3). An (F,delta)-model restricts to models at
both smaller scales, and bi-invariance gives

    d(phi(gh),id) <= d(phi(gh),phi(g)phi(h)) + d(phi(g),id) + d(phi(h),id) <= eps.

Inverses and conjugates follow the same way: phi(g)phi(g^-1) is within delta
of id, and phi(kgk^-1) is within 2 delta of phi(k)phi(g)phi(k)^-1, whose
distance to id equals d(phi(g),id). Include the needed products in F.

*Sofic implies Rad=1.* A sofic approximation at (F,delta) moves each g in
F minus {1} at a fraction at least 1-eps.

*Rad=1 implies sofic.* Fix a finite F containing 1 and eps>0. For each g in F
minus {1} there is eps_g>0 such that every scale (F',delta') with g in F' has a
model moving g at a fraction greater than eps_g. Put eps_0 = min_g eps_g.

1. Given delta, choose one (F,delta)-model for each g, replicate them to a
   common cardinality, and take the disjoint union V'. This is an
   (F,delta)-model in which every g in F minus {1} moves at a fraction greater
   than eps_0/|F|.
2. Take the r-fold product V = V'^r with the coordinatewise maps. This is an
   (F, r delta)-model, since d(pi^(xr), pi'^(xr)) <= r d(pi,pi'). A product
   point is fixed only if every coordinate is fixed, so the fixed fraction of
   each phi(g) is at most (1-eps_0/|F|)^r.

Choose r with this below eps, then delta with r delta below eps. The result is
an (F,eps)-sofic approximation. QED.

## 1. The localization theorem

**Theorem A.** Let G be countable, A finite with |A| >= 2, tau an injective
automaton over G, and p in A^Omega a Garden of Eden for tau. Then
Omega^-1 Omega contains an element of Rad(G) other than 1. In particular there
is no one-cell Garden of Eden.

*Refinement.* Let K = <M> be the subgroup generated by the memory. The image of
tau is the product of the images of the independent automata on the left
K-cosets. So p restricts to a Garden of Eden on Omega intersect gK for some g.
Translating by g^-1 and applying Theorem A over K gives a nontrivial element of
Rad(K) inside Omega^-1 Omega intersect K. Restricting a G-model to a finite
subset of K gives a K-model, so Rad(K) is contained in Rad(G) intersect K. The
refined statement is therefore stronger.

*Proof.* Suppose instead that D := {k^-1 k' : k != k' in Omega} misses Rad(G).
Fix the memories M,S of tau,sigma and local rules f,d. Let F be a finite set
containing 1, M, S, Omega, D and all products SM, Omega M, together with
their factors. Write v.a := phi(a)^-1(v) for a model phi; this is a right
action up to defect.

**Step 1: a model separating D.** For each d in D there is eps_d>0 such that
every scale with d in F has a model moving d at a fraction greater than eps_d.
Let eps_0 = min_d eps_d. As in Lemma 1, a disjoint union followed by an r-fold
product gives, for every delta, an (F, r delta)-model V in which each phi(d),
d in D, fixes at most a fraction (1-eps_0/|D|)^r. Fix r with
|D|(1-eps_0/|D|)^r <= 1/4; r depends only on eps_0 and |D|.

**Step 2: good points and charts.** Let W be the set of v in V such that
(v.a).b = v.(ab) for all a,b in F with ab in F. Then |V minus W| <= beta|V| with
beta = |F|^2 r delta. At v in W and distinct k,k' in Omega, v.k = v.k' holds
exactly when v.k is fixed by phi(k^-1 k'). The map v -> v.k is a bijection, so
at most a fraction 1/4 of v have a non-injective Omega-chart. Hence the set U of
v in W with k -> v.k injective on Omega has |U| >= (3/4 - beta)|V|.

**Step 3: the model automata.** Put

    tau_V(x)(v) = f((x(v.m))_(m in M)),     sigma_V(y)(v) = d((y(v.s))_(s in S)).

(i) *Decoding at good points.* For v in W, (v.s).m = v.(sm). Set z(k) := x(v.k)
for k in SM; this is a function on the set SM, whatever collisions occur. Extend
z arbitrarily to A^G. Then sigma_V(tau_V(x))(v) = sigma(tau(z))(1) = z(1) = x(v).
So x|_W is a function of tau_V(x), and

    |tau_V(A^V)| >= |A|^|W| >= |A|^((1-beta)|V|).

(ii) *The orphan survives on injective charts.* For v in U and w in Omega,
tau_V(x)(v.w) = f((x(v.(wm)))_m). With z(k) := x(v.k) on Omega M,
extended arbitrarily, the pattern k -> tau_V(x)(v.k) on Omega equals
tau(z)|_Omega. Since p is a Garden of Eden, this pattern differs from p.

(iii) *Disjoint charts.* Choose a maximal family C of points of U with pairwise
disjoint chart images v.Omega. Every u in U has a chart meeting a chosen one:
u.w = v.w' for some v in C and w,w' in Omega. Given v,w,w' there is at most one
such u, because u -> u.w is a bijection. Hence |C| >= (3/4-beta)|V| / |Omega|^2.

(iv) *Count.* The configurations avoiding p on the |C| disjoint charts number
|A|^|V| (1-|A|^-|Omega|)^|C|. Combining with (i) and log(1-t) <= -t,

    (3/4 - beta) |A|^-|Omega| / (|Omega|^2 ln|A|) <= beta.

Here beta = |F|^2 r delta tends to 0 with delta, and every other quantity is
fixed. This is a contradiction. QED.

For a one-cell window D is empty, Step 1 is vacuous, and one-point models
suffice. The theorem then reduces to the familiar fact that an injective
automaton permutes the constant configurations.

## 2. Consequences for coset-invariant outputs

**Corollary B.** Let tau be injective and let H be a finite subgroup with
tau(x)(gh) = tau(x)(g) for all x, g and h in H. Then H lies in Rad(K), where K
is generated by the effective memory. If H != 1, tau is not surjective.

*Proof.* Let M_eff be the set of coordinates on which f depends; f is not
constant. Invariance for all x forces h M_eff = M_eff and invariance of f under
the relabeling m -> hm. So h lies in M_eff M_eff^-1, inside K. For h != 1, the
pattern on Omega = {1,h} with distinct symbols is a Garden of Eden. By the
refinement of Theorem A, h or h^-1 lies in Rad(K). QED.

Over a sofic memory group this excludes every such automaton, as Gromov--Weiss
already implies. The content is the converse direction for design: an
invariant-output strict automaton must be built on a symmetry that no
almost-multiplicative permutation model of the memory group can see.

**Proposition C (linear case).** Let k be a finite field of characteristic p,
and a in k[G] with tau_a(x)(g) = sum_m a_m x(gm).

1. The output is right-H-invariant iff ha = a for all h in H.
2. If p divides |H|, no such tau_a is injective.
3. If p does not divide |H|, put e = |H|^-1 sum_(h in H) h. Such an injective
   tau_a exists iff there are b,c in k[G] with c e b = 1. Then (eb)c != 1 when
   H != 1, a direct-finiteness failure.

*Proof.* (1) Direct substitution. (2) The coefficients of a are constant on the
free left H-orbits, so the augmentation eps(a) is |H| times a sum, which is 0 in
k. By the scalar duality in `central-c3-quadratic-surjunctivity-iff-direct-finiteness`,
tau_a is injective iff c a = 1 for some c. Then eps(c)eps(a) = 1, which is
impossible. (3) ha = a for all h iff a = ea. Injectivity gives c e a = 1. The
element (ea)c lies in e k[G], which cannot contain 1: 1 = ey would force
e = ey = 1, i.e. H = 1. QED.

So over F_2 an involution-invariant output is available only to genuinely
nonlinear rules. Order-three invariance brings back the corner questions of
`order-three-axis-feedback-surjunctivity-iff-corner-finiteness` and
`noncentral-norm-feedback-surjunctivity-iff-corner-finiteness`.

## 3. The binary Leavitt unit group has full sofic radical

**Theorem D.** Rad(R^x) = R^x for R = L_(F_2)(1,2). Equivalently, every
homomorphism from R^x to a metric ultraproduct of symmetric groups is trivial.

*Proof.* The three-leaf prefix code identifies R with M_3(R), so R^x = GL_3(R).
This equals EL_3(R) by `leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group`, and it is
simple by `binary-leavitt-elementary-group-is-simple`. Rad(R^x) is normal by
Lemma 1, so it is 1 or R^x. If it were 1, Lemma 1 would make R^x sofic,
contradicting `openai-leavitt-unit-nonsofic`. QED.

The same holds for EL_n(R), n >= 3, since these groups are isomorphic to R^x.

**What fails in Gromov--Weiss.** Quantitatively, for each finite set E of
nontrivial elements and eps>0 there is a scale (F,delta) at which every model
fixes each element of E on all but an eps-fraction of points. Take E to contain
k^-1 k' for one pair k != k' of a window Omega. At good points v.k' = (v.k).(k^-1 k'),
so the Omega-chart is non-injective on all but an (eps + |F|^2 delta)-fraction of
points. The counting step of Gromov--Weiss needs injective charts at positive
density along some model; over R^x no model supplies them. Theorem A is exactly
that step, and Theorem D says it is vacuous over R^x whenever the memory
generates R^x, or any EL_n(R) with n >= 3.

## 4. Design consequences and scope

Over R^x:

- Coset-invariant-output automata with an involution H = <w> are not excluded
  by any counting along permutation models, once the effective memory generates
  a simple nonsofic subgroup. If the memory generates a sofic subgroup,
  Corollary B excludes them.
- Linear invariant-output rules are dead for even |H| over F_2 (Proposition C).
  For odd |H| they are the corner compression 1 <= e of the Kaplansky lane.
- Constants are the one canonical exact test: f restricted to the diagonal must
  permute A. The guard theorems in `surjunctive-guard-subgroups-force-surjectivity`
  still apply to rules whose monomials all touch a surjunctive guard.

Theorem A complements `forward-memory-lifts-exclude-strict-cellular-sections`.
That theorem forbids a surjunctive *cover* matching the decoder-encoder
rectangle. Theorem A forbids a permutation *model*, collisions allowed, that
separates the Garden-of-Eden window. The counting in Theorem A is the
Gromov--Weiss counting run along models with collisions, plus the
disjoint-union and product amplification. No novelty is claimed for that method.
Nothing here constructs a strict automaton or proves surjunctivity of any group
not already known to be sofic.

## 5. The composition tables themselves must present an invisible window difference

**Theorem F.** Let tau, sigma be automata over any group G, with local rules
f, d on memories M, S (both containing 1) and sigma tau = id. Let p in A^Omega
be a Garden of Eden for tau. Let U be the group with generators x_a,
a in M union S union Omega (x_1 = 1), and relators

    x_s x_m = x_s' x_m'      whenever  sm = s'm'  in G   (s,s' in S; m,m' in M),
    x_w x_m = x_w' x_m'      whenever  wm = w'm'  in G   (w,w' in Omega; m,m' in M).

Then some distinct w, w' in Omega have x_w^-1 x_w' in Rad(U). In particular U is
a finitely presented nonsofic group with relators of length at most four.

*Proof.* The map x_a -> a defines a homomorphism pi from U to G. Products in
S x M, and in Omega x M, coincide in U exactly when they coincide in G: one
direction is imposed, the other follows by applying pi. Also pi separates the
x_w. Over U put tau_U, sigma_U with the same local rules.

- At the identity, sigma_U tau_U reads the positions x_s x_m. Any assignment
  there transports bijectively to an assignment on SM with the same
  coincidences. Extend it to A^G and apply sigma tau = id. Translation gives
  sigma_U tau_U = id, so tau_U is injective.
- The pattern of tau_U(x) on x_Omega depends only on x at the positions
  x_w x_m. These carry the coincidence pattern of Omega M, so the pattern equals
  tau(z)|_Omega for some z in A^G. It therefore differs from p: p is a Garden of
  Eden for tau_U on x_Omega.

U is countable, so Theorem A gives the invisible difference. It is nontrivial
because pi maps it to w^-1 w' != 1. QED.

For the reverse-defect window Omega = MS the second family of relators records
the triple products MSM.

**Reading.** Theorem F sharpens `forward-memory-lifts-exclude-strict-cellular-sections`,
whose presentation U_SM must be nonsurjunctive. Any certificate refuting
Gottschalk carries, inside its own finite product tables, a finite
presentation with an explicit nontrivial sofic-invisible element on a window
difference. So a short certificate over R^x would hand over a short
nonsofic presentation: at most |S||M| + |Omega||M| relators of length at most
four. The only nonsoficity mechanism currently known is the one-sided
compression criterion (Kazhdan subgroup, compressors, commuting non-LEF
factor), and its presentations are large. Within current knowledge, then, a
certificate's decoder window must realize such a configuration in its product
table. Small truth-table windows can succeed only if they realize a small
nonsofic presentation. The theorem does not rule them out.

**Invariant outputs.** With H x M relators x_h x_m = x_m' (hm = m') added, the
same proof over the table group places each x_h in its sofic radical.
