# Blackadar--Kirchberg: primitive quotients that are quasidiagonal or traceless, and the reduction of T_1 spectra to the simple case (2026-09-12)

Lane `ex-blackadar-kirchberg`.  This builds on
`research/artifacts/bk-spectral-extension-permanence-2026-09-12.md` (Theorem 1:
extensions by exact ideals with no compact open primitive subset of nuclear
quasidiagonal algebras are quasidiagonal) and
`stw07-separating-qd-quotients-force-quasidiagonality`.  Traceless is in Gabe's
sense: every lower semicontinuous extended trace `A_+ -> [0,∞]` takes only the
values `0` and `∞`.  All algebras are separable.

## 1. Three elementary trace facts

**Fact T1 (bounded pieces).**  If `ρ` is a lower semicontinuous trace on `A` and
`0 < ρ(a) < ∞`, then for small `ε > 0` the hereditary subalgebra
`B = her((a-ε)_+)` carries the nonzero bounded trace `ρ|_B`.

*Proof.*  `ρ((a-ε)_+) -> ρ(a) > 0` as `ε -> 0` by lower semicontinuity, so it is
positive for small `ε`.  Let `h` be continuous with `h = 1` on `[ε,∞)`,
`0 ≤ h ≤ 1`, and `h(t) ≤ t/ε`.  Then `h(a)` is a unit for `B`, so
`0 ≤ c ≤ 1` in `B` gives `c = h(a) c h(a) ≤ h(a)^2 ≤ a/ε`.  Hence
`ρ(c) ≤ ρ(a)/ε`.  QED

**Fact T2 (inducing traces upward).**  Let `D` be a C\*-algebra, `L` an ideal of
`D`, and `B_0` a σ-unital hereditary subalgebra of `L` with a nonzero bounded
trace `σ`.  Then `D` has a lower semicontinuous trace with a finite nonzero value,
so `D` is not traceless.

*Proof.*  Let `L_0` be the ideal of `D` generated by `B_0`.  `σ ⊗ Tr` is a
lower semicontinuous trace on `B_0 ⊗ K`, finite and nonzero on `b ⊗ e_11` for
some `b`.  Brown's stable isomorphism `B_0 ⊗ K ≅ L_0 ⊗ K` transports it to
`L_0 ⊗ K`.  Its restriction `ρ` to `L_0 ⊗ e_11 ≅ L_0` is not `{0,∞}`-valued,
since a `{0,∞}`-valued `ρ` would make `ρ ⊗ Tr` `{0,∞}`-valued.  Extend `ρ` to `D`
by `∞` on `D_+ \ L_0`.  This is additive because `L_0` is hereditary,
homogeneous, tracial because `a*a ∈ L_0` iff `aa* ∈ L_0`, and lower
semicontinuous because `L_0` is closed.  QED

**Fact T3 (simple algebras).**  A simple exact C\*-algebra is stably finite iff it
is not traceless.

*Proof.*  If it is traceless and stably finite, Gabe's Corollary C says its
primitive ideal space, a point, has no nonempty compact open subset.  That is
false.  Conversely, let `ρ` be a trace with a finite nonzero value.  Simplicity
makes `ρ` faithful: its null ideal `{x : ρ(x*x) = 0}` is a proper closed ideal.
By T1 a full hereditary subalgebra `B` carries a faithful bounded trace, so `B`
is stably finite.  If `v` is an isometry in `M_n(B~)`, then `1 - vv* ∈ M_n(B)`,
and `v = u + d` with `u` scalar unitary and `d ∈ M_n(B)`.  Hence
`(ρ⊗Tr)(1 - vv*) = (ρ⊗Tr)(v*v - vv*) = 0`.  Stable finiteness passes to
`B ⊗ K ≅ A ⊗ K`, and from there to `A`.  QED

## 2. Theorem 2

**Theorem 2.**  Let `E` be separable, nuclear and stably finite.  If every
primitive quotient of `E` is quasidiagonal or traceless, then `E` is
quasidiagonal.

*Proof.*  Let `F ⊆ Prim(E)` be the set of `P` with `E/P` not quasidiagonal (so
traceless), `V` the interior of `F`, and `E_V` the ideal with `Prim(E_V) = V`.

(a) *`E_V` is traceless.*  Otherwise T1 gives a hereditary `B ⊆ E_V` with a
nonzero bounded trace.  Its tracial functionals of norm `≤ 1` form a weak\*-compact
convex set containing a nonzero point.  So by Krein--Milman it has a nonzero
extreme point `σ`, necessarily of norm `1`, i.e. an extreme tracial state.  The
GNS representation of an extreme tracial state generates a factor, so
`ker π_σ` is a prime, hence primitive, ideal of the separable `B`.  It has the
form `B ∩ P` for some `P ∈ Prim(E_V) = V`.  Thus `σ` is a nonzero bounded trace on
the hereditary subalgebra `B/(B∩P)` of `E_V/P`.  Let `P'` be the primitive ideal
of `E` with `P' ∩ E_V = P`.  Then `E_V/P ≅ (E_V + P')/P'` is an ideal of `E/P'`,
and `P' ∈ V ⊆ F`.  By T2, `E/P'` is not traceless, a contradiction.

(b) *`E_V` has no compact open primitive subset.*  `E_V` is separable, exact,
traceless, and stably finite as an ideal of `E`.  Apply Gabe's Corollary C
((iii) ⟹ (iv)).

(c) *`E/E_V` is quasidiagonal.*  Its primitive spectrum is the closed set
`Prim(E) \ V`, and the quasidiagonal primitive quotients `(Prim(E) \ V) \ F` are
dense there.  Indeed, if an open `W` has `W \ V ⊆ F` and `W \ V != ∅`, then
`W ⊆ V ∪ F = F`, so `W ⊆ V`, contradicting `W \ V != ∅`.  In the hull-kernel
topology the primitive ideals of a dense subset intersect in `0`.  So `E/E_V` has
a separating family of quasidiagonal quotients and is quasidiagonal
(`stw07-separating-qd-quotients-force-quasidiagonality`).

(d)  `0 -> E_V -> E -> E/E_V -> 0` has exact compact-free ideal and nuclear
quasidiagonal quotient.  Theorem 1 of the spectral artifact makes `E`
quasidiagonal.  QED

## 3. Corollaries

**Corollary 3 (T_1 spectra reduce to simple algebras).**  Let `E` be separable,
nuclear and stably finite with `T_1` primitive ideal space, so every primitive
quotient is simple.  If every simple, stably finite primitive quotient is
quasidiagonal, then `E` is quasidiagonal.  In particular, the simple case of
Problem VII (`stw99-vii-restricted-to-simple-algebras`) implies Problem VII for
all separable nuclear algebras with `T_1` primitive spectrum.

*Proof.*  A simple nuclear primitive quotient is stably finite or traceless by
T3.  In the first case it is quasidiagonal by hypothesis.  Apply Theorem 2.  QED

Note that primitive quotients of a stably finite algebra need not be stably
finite.  Corollary 3 lets them be `O_2`-like, and asks the simple case only of
the stably finite ones.

**Corollary 4 (unconditional UCT case).**  Problem VII holds for every separable
nuclear `E` with `T_1` primitive spectrum whose primitive quotients satisfy the
UCT.  This includes every continuous `C_0(X)`-algebra over a locally compact
Hausdorff `X` whose fibres are simple and satisfy the UCT.

*Proof.*  A simple nuclear stably finite UCT algebra has a faithful densely
finite trace (T3), whose bounded full corner has a faithful amenable trace.  By
Tikuisis--White--Winter (`tww-gabe-schafhauser-af-embedding-theorem`) the corner
is quasidiagonal, hence so is the algebra, by stabilisation.  For continuous
fields with simple fibres, `Prim(E) ≅ X` is Hausdorff.  QED

**Corollary 5 (mixed fibres).**  Let `E` be a separable nuclear stably finite
continuous `C_0(X)`-algebra over a locally compact Hausdorff space `X`.  If each
fibre `E_x` is quasidiagonal or traceless, then `E` is quasidiagonal.  So a
field may degenerate from classifiable stably finite fibres to Kirchberg algebras
on arbitrary sets of parameters, and stable finiteness of the total algebra
still forces quasidiagonality.

This does not follow from Theorem 2 as stated, because a quasidiagonal fibre can
have non-quasidiagonal traced primitive quotients.  The proof repeats Theorem 2
fibrewise.

*Proof.*  Let `F = {x : E_x not quasidiagonal}`, `V` its interior, and
`E_V = C_0(V)E`.
(a') `E_V` is traceless.  As in (a), a nontrivial trace gives a hereditary
`B ⊆ E_V` with an extreme tracial state `σ` whose GNS closure is a factor.
`C_0(V)` acts by central multipliers of `B`, so `π_σ(C_0(V))` consists of
scalars.  This is a nonzero character, since `C_0(V)B` is dense in `B`, i.e.
evaluation at some `x ∈ V`.  So `σ` vanishes on `C_0(V\{x})B = B ∩ C_0(V\{x})E_V`
and factors through the image of `B` in `E_x`, a hereditary subalgebra.  By T2,
`E_x` is not traceless, contradicting `x ∈ F`.
(b') `E_V` is separable, exact, traceless and stably finite, so it is
compact-free by Gabe's Corollary C.
(c') `E/E_V` is the restriction of the field to the closed set `X \ V`, again
a continuous field.  Its quasidiagonal fibres are dense there, by the argument of
(c).  For a continuous field `x -> ||a_x||` is continuous.  So for `a != 0` the
nonempty open set `{x : ||a_x|| > ||a||/2}` meets the dense set, and the fibres
over the dense set separate points.  Hence `E/E_V` is quasidiagonal.
(d') Theorem 1.  QED

Continuity matters in (c'): for merely upper semicontinuous `C(X)`-algebras, a
dense set of fibres need not separate points, although all fibres do.

More generally, by Theorem 2, the same conclusion holds for any `E` all of whose
primitive quotients are quasidiagonal or traceless.

## 4. Tests and boundaries

- Cone unitisation `C_0((0,1], O_2)~`: `T_1` spectrum, primitive quotients
  `O_2` (traceless) and `C` (quasidiagonal), stably finite, quasidiagonal.
  Consistent with Theorem 2.
- `C(T, O_2)`: all primitive quotients traceless, but `V = T` is compact, so (b)
  fails.  Indeed the algebra is not stably finite, and the hypothesis of the
  theorem fails.  Consistent.
- Toeplitz algebra: primitive quotient `T` itself is neither quasidiagonal nor
  traceless (it has `Tr` on `K`), and the spectrum is not `T_1`.  Consistent.
- What Theorem 2 does not reach: primitive quotients with traces that are not
  quasidiagonal, such as prime non-simple quotients like `T`.  Any counterexample
  to Problem VII has such a primitive quotient, or a simple stably finite
  non-quasidiagonal one.  Combined with
  `stw07-qd-radical-criterion-compact-open`, the obstruction is a traced compact
  ideal glued along non-simple, traced primitive quotients.
