## E2. Define `\LC` and `\EL_3` where they first matter (clarity; +1 line)
(a) Theorem, l.44–53. Before:
```latex
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $X\subseteq A^{\Z}$ be an infinite minimal subshift over a finite
alphabet, with shift $T$. Then
\[
  G_X=\EL_3\bigl(\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes_T\Z\bigr)
\]
is an infinite finitely generated simple group with Kazhdan's
property~\textup{(T)} that is locally embeddable into finite groups
\textup{(LEF)}. In particular, it is sofic and hyperlinear.
\end{theorem}
```

```latex
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $X\subseteq A^{\Z}$ be an infinite minimal subshift over a finite

constant functions $X\to\F_2$. Then
\[
  G_X=\EL_3\bigl(\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes_T\Z\bigr)
\]
is an infinite, finitely generated, simple group with Kazhdan's
property~\textup{(T)}. It is locally embeddable into finite groups
\textup{(LEF)}, so it is sofic and hyperlinear.
\end{theorem}
```

## E3. Opening paragraph (count fix; +1 line)
Before, l.38–42:
```latex
Can an infinite simple Kazhdan group be hyperlinear? Brown asked this
in its von Neumann algebra form in 2001~\cite[\S11, Question~7]{Brown},
and Ozawa in the hyperlinear form in 2003~\cite[p.~527]{Ozawa}.
Pestov's Open question~9.1 adds the sofic version~\cite{Pestov}.
The following construction answers both forms positively.
```
After:
```latex
Can an infinite simple group with Kazhdan's property~\textup{(T)} be
hyperlinear? Brown asked this in its von Neumann algebra form in
2001~\cite[\S11, Question~7]{Brown}, and Ozawa asked it in the
hyperlinear form in 2003~\cite[p.~527]{Ozawa}. Pestov's Open
question~9.1 adds the sofic form~\cite{Pestov}. The following theorem
answers all three forms positively.
```
Trade-off:
- It fixes "both" after three named forms. All three are answered: a hyperlinear G has L(G) ⊂ R^ω, so G ⊂ U(L(G)⊗R), and L(G)⊗R is an R^ω-embeddable McDuff factor. That is Brown's form.
- It glosses "Kazhdan" once.
- Names, years, citations and locators are unchanged.

Observation for sk-citations, not a proposal: the text says "Ozawa ... in 2003", but the bibitem is the 2004 journal article. The year is part of the lineage decision, and the page locator refers to the journal version.

## E4. Abstract (0 lines)
Before, l.27–35:
```latex
\begin{abstract}
For every infinite minimal subshift $X$, the group
$\EL_3(\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes\Z)$ is infinite, simple, finitely generated,
and has property~\textup{(T)}. It is locally embeddable into finite
groups, hence sofic and hyperlinear. This answers the questions of
Brown, Ozawa, and Pestov. The proof combines periodic approximation
with a commutator supported on a finite tower. Every Turing degree
occurs as the word-problem degree of one of these groups.
\end{abstract}
```
After:
```latex
\begin{abstract}
For every infinite minimal subshift $X$, the group
$\EL_3(\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes\Z)$ is an infinite, finitely generated,
simple group with property~\textup{(T)}. It is locally embeddable into
finite groups, so it is sofic and hyperlinear. This answers the
question of Brown, Ozawa, and Pestov whether an infinite simple
Kazhdan group can be hyperlinear or sofic. Every Turing degree is the
word-problem degree of one of these groups.
\end{abstract}
```
Trade-off: the abstract now says which question is answered. It drops the method sentence, which a reader cannot use without the proof. The three names are unchanged; the user's choice to name Pestov here stands.

If the user prefers to keep a method sentence: "Simplicity is proved with commutators supported on finite clopen towers."

## E5. Second intro paragraph (0 lines; +2 optional)
Before, l.55–65:
```latex
The subshift algebra is classical; see Nekrashevych~\cite{Nekrashevych}.
Property~(T) is supplied by Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain~\cite{EJZ}.
Our finite models use the periodic-approximation mechanism of
Grigorchuk--Medynets~\cite{GM}. The new step is the simplicity argument:
a nontrivial normal subgroup meets a finite simple matrix group
supported on a clopen tower, and therefore contains an elementary
matrix. This is an explicit elementary-matrix extraction argument,
in the normal-structure tradition described by Stepanov~\cite{Stepanov}.
Thom had already constructed a finitely generated Kazhdan LEF group
that is not residually finite~\cite[Theorem~1.4]{Thom}; his example
is not simple.
```
After:
```latex
The crossed product $\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes_T\Z$ is classical; see
Nekrashevych~\cite{Nekrashevych}. Property~\textup{(T)} follows from
the theorem of Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain~\cite{EJZ}. The finite models
use periodic sequences with the same short words as $X$, as in
Grigorchuk and Medynets~\cite{GM}. The new step is simplicity: a
nontrivial normal subgroup meets a finite simple group $\GL_d(\F_2)$
acting on a clopen tower nontrivially, so it contains an elementary
matrix. The argument extracts an elementary matrix from the normal
subgroup, as in the normal structure theory of linear groups over rings
described by Stepanov~\cite{Stepanov}. Thom constructed a finitely
generated Kazhdan LEF group that is not residually
finite~\cite[Theorem~1.4]{Thom}, but his example is not simple.
```

```latex
The groups $G_X$ realize every Turing degree as a word-problem degree
(Corollary~\ref{cor:degrees}), so there are continuum many of them.
```

- Removes the metaphors "supplied" and "mechanism".
- Removes the metacommentary "This is an explicit ... argument".
