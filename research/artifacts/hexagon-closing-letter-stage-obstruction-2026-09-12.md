# The closing letter of the Leavitt hexagon: what `t = 1` costs in stage models

Lane `w7-hexagon-closing`, 2026-09-12, on paper.

`w4-vf-gate` passed Sections 1–6 with no mathematical corrections (§38 of
`research/artifacts/gk-vf-gate-verification-2026-09-12.md`, 427f2bfa50). Its advisories 38.5 are applied here, and `w4-vf-gate` §40 (20848c2409) passed this revision.
Premise: `leavitt-unit-groups-have-no-unstable-k2-at-rank-three` (verified by `w4-vf-gate` §34, b55d89486d), point 4:
`G = R^x ≅ π_1(Y_hex)/<<t>>`.

## 0. Summary

* **Section 1: the letter.** `t` is not a word in the radicals. It maps to a generator of `π_1(hexagon) = Z`, acts
  hyperbolically on the Bass–Serre tree and has infinite order. Killing it is the same as killing the closing words
  `W_12(r)` and `W_13(r)` in the tree amalgam. In the Theorem 1.1 model the forest data fix only a coset
  `π_n^0 Z_n`, where `Z_n ≅ K_n wr Sym(r_n)` is the permutation form of the free external holonomy of
  `isolated-regular-a2-hexagon-has-free-external-holonomy`.
* **Section 2 (elementary): no exact closing.** `G` is finitely presented and has no nontrivial finite quotient. So by
  Łoś, every homomorphism of `π_1(Y_hex)` into an algebraic ultraproduct of finite groups that kills `t` is trivial.
  Per stage: at all large stages the six-piece colimit `C_n` of finite pieces has no finite quotient in which
  `x_12(1)` survives.
  So in every Theorem 1.1 model on `Y_hex`, no choice of forest bijections makes the letter permutation the identity.
* **Section 3: no rank-null closing.** If `rk(σ(t) - 1) = 0`, then `σ` descends to a nontrivial homomorphism of `G`
  into a universal sofic group (`monomial-rank-models-are-hamming-models`), which contradicts nonsoficity. There is a
  uniform gap `rk(σ(t) - 1) >= ε_p > 0`.
* **Section 4: calibrations.** The hexagon closes exactly over the finite fields `F_2` and `F_3` (radicals of order
  8 and 27, inside `SL_3(F_p)`). It also closes over the locally finite ring `R_0` and over `F_2[x]` in finite
  quotients. So each hypothesis of Section 2 (infinite, finitely presented, simple) is needed. Model tests against
  the graph-of-groups, HNN, Fock and UT₄ models are recorded there.
* **Section 5: ternary.** Theorem 1.1 transfers verbatim over `F_3`, and Sections 2–3 hold for `L_(F_3)(1,2)^x`
  conditional as the prime-field host theorem is.
* **Section 6: rank-two kernel.** Its elements never enter `Y_hex` as inputs. Lifted, they are native words in
  `<<t>>`, such as `ω = (x_12(1) x_21(1))^3`.
* **Section 7: verdict.**
  * Neither proved nor refuted.
  * The refutation route "close the hexagon in a stage model" is dead.
  * A refutation needs a weakly finite target outside algebraic ultraproducts of finite or commutative-linear matrix
    groups and outside monomial rank models. The next class is non-monomial `F_2`-linear sofic models
    (`binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-f2-linear-sofic`).
  * On the proof side, "must use `t = 1`" is no constraint for arguments valid in ultraproduct targets. A proof of the
    representation form must be a certificate valid in every weakly finite ring.

## 1. The letter

**Setting.**
* `p ∈ {2,3}`, `R = L_(F_p)(1,2)` and `G = R^x = St_3(R)`.
* Order the hexagon `123, 213, 231, 321, 312, 132`. `U_π = <x_ij(R) : i <_π j>`.
* Edges `e_1, ..., e_6` join consecutive orders. `e_6 : 132 -> 123` carries `K = <x_12(R), x_13(R)>`.
* Every edge group is abelian, isomorphic to `R ⊕ R`. The two roots positive at both ends of an edge are never in
  the relation `i -> j -> k`, so they commute.
* The maximal tree is the path `123 - 213 - 231 - 321 - 312 - 132`, and `P` is its tree amalgam.
* For `x ∈ U_π` write `x^π` for its image in `P`.
* `B` is an `F_p`-basis of `R`.

**Proposition 1.1.**
1. `π_1(Y_hex) = <P, t | t x^132 t^-1 = x^123 (x ∈ K)>`. So
   `G ≅ P / <<W_12(r), W_13(r) : r ∈ B>>`, with `W_ij(r) = x_ij^132(r)^-1 x_ij^123(r)`.
2. In `P`, `x_12^132 = x_12^312` (edge `e_5`) and `x_13^123 = x_13^213` (edge `e_1`). The root `(1,2)` is positive on
   `312, 132, 123`, and `(1,3)` on `132, 123, 213`. The tree cuts each of these arcs once, between `132` and `123`, and
   `W_12` and `W_13` compare the two halves.
3. `t` lies in no subgroup generated by vertex groups. Collapsing the vertex groups gives
   `π_1(Y_hex) -> π_1(hexagon graph) = Z`, which kills every `U_π` and sends `t` to a generator. So `t` has infinite
   order, and no nonzero power of `t` is conjugate into any `U_π`. Equivalently, `t` is hyperbolic on the Bass–Serre
   tree.
4. **The model.** In Theorem 1.1 of `research/artifacts/mismatched-factor-graph-of-groups-firewall-2026-09-12.md` on
   `Y_hex` (`p = 2`), let `λ_π = λ_(n,π)` and `K_n = K_(n,e_6)`.
   * The valid letter permutations at stage `n` form the coset `π_n^0 Z_n`, where `Z_n` is the centralizer of
     `λ_132(K_n)` in `Sym(Ω_n)`.
   * `K_n` is abelian and acts freely with `r_n = N_n/|K_n|` orbits, so `Z_n ≅ K_n wr Sym(r_n)`.
   * Only this coset is determined by the forest data. The order of `σ(t)` is a choice, not an invariant.

*Proof.*
* **(1).** This is the definition of `π_1` for one non-forest edge with inclusion as edge map. Killing `t` imposes
  `x^132 = x^123` on `K`, which is generated by `x_12(r)` and `x_13(r)` for `r ∈ B`.
* **(2).** Read off the table in Proposition 5.1 of `research/artifacts/leavitt-unstable-k2-steinberg-reduction-2026-09-12.md`.
* **(3).** The graph-of-groups map to the graph's `π_1` sends vertex groups to `1` and the non-forest letter to the
  generator.
* **(4).** An intertwiner is unique up to right multiplication by `Z_n`. The centralizer of a free action of an
  abelian group `K` on `K × [r]` is `K wr Sym(r)`. ∎

**Remark 1.2 (external versus forced).** In IRH, `1_E ⊗ Z` is removable because one coherent finite realization
exists: the six radicals over `F_q` sit in `SL_3(F_q)`. In the permutation model `Z_n` plays the role of `U(K)`. The
question is whether `π_n^0 Z_n` contains `id`, or elements near it. Sections 2 and 3 answer no at all large stages,
for all forest choices.

## 2. No exact closing

**Lemma 2.1 (Łoś).** Let `Γ = <S | Q>` be finitely presented and `H_n` groups, with `U` a nonprincipal ultrafilter.
Every homomorphism `ρ : Γ -> prod_U H_n` (algebraic ultraproduct) has the form `[ρ_n]`, with `ρ_n : Γ -> H_n` a
homomorphism for `U`-almost every `n`.

*Proof.* Choose `ρ(s) = [g_(s,n)]`. Each of the finitely many relators holds at `U`-almost every `n`, so for almost
every `n`, `s -> g_(s,n)` defines `ρ_n`, and `ρ(γ) = [ρ_n(γ)]` for every word `γ`. ∎

**Lemma 2.2.** `G` has no nontrivial finite quotient. If `A` is a commutative ring, every homomorphism
`G -> GL_m(A)` is trivial.

*Proof.*
* **Binary.** `G` is simple (`binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-simple`) and infinite, so a finite-index normal subgroup
  is `G`.
* **Ternary.** `G = St_3(R)` is perfect, and `G/Z` is simple with `Z = {±1}`
  (`prime-field-leavitt-tensor-hosts-fp-and-simple-mod-centre`). Let `N` be normal of finite index. Then `NZ/Z` is
  `1` or `G/Z`.
  * If `NZ = Z`, then `N` is finite of finite index, and `G` would be finite.
  * If `NZ = G`, then `G/N ≅ Z/(N ∩ Z)` is abelian and perfect, so `N = G`.
* **Linear targets.** The image is finitely generated and linear over a commutative ring, hence residually finite
  (Malcev, literature). Its finite quotients are quotients of `G`, so it is trivial. ∎

**Theorem 2.3.** Let `ρ : π_1(Y_hex) -> prod_U H_n` be a homomorphism into an algebraic ultraproduct of finite
groups, or of groups `GL_(m_n)(A_n)` with `A_n` commutative. If `ρ(t) = 1`, then `ρ` is trivial. In particular, in
every Theorem 1.1 model on `Y_hex`, `σ(t) != 1`.

*Proof.* `ρ` factors through `G`, which is finitely presented (`leavitt-unit-group-finitely-presented`, or the explicit
presentation of `leavitt-unit-group-steinberg-weyl-presentation`; ternary: the prime-field host theorem). Lemmas 2.1
and 2.2 give `ρ_n = 1` almost everywhere. The model is nontrivial by Theorem 1.1(2). ∎

**Corollary 2.4 (per stage).** Fix finite `H_(n,π) <= U_π`, increasing in `n` and exhausting `U_π`. Let `C_n` be the
colimit of the six `H_(n,π)` along the six edge pieces `K_(n,e)`. There is `n_1` such that for every `n >= n_1`,
every homomorphism from `C_n` to a finite group kills `x_12(1)`. So for `n >= n_1` there are no actions `λ_π` of the
`H_(n,π)` on a finite set that agree on all six edges and move a point under `x_12(1)`. In the Theorem 1.1 model,
where `x_12(1)` acts freely, `id ∉ π_n^0 Z_n` for all `n >= n_1`, whatever forest bijections are chosen.

*Proof.*
* **The limit.** Colimits commute with directed colimits, so `colim_n C_n = colim_π U_π = G` by (SPG2).
* **Words.** Fix a finite presentation `<S | Q>` of `G`, root-element words for the elements of `S`, and a word `w`
  in `S` equal to `x_12(1)` in `G`. The relators, and `x_12(1)^-1 w`, are finitely many elements that are trivial in
  the directed colimit, so they are trivial in `C_n` for `n >= n_1`.
* **The stage.** A homomorphism `C_n -> F` with `F` finite then defines `G -> F`. This is trivial by Lemma 2.2, so
  `w -> 1`, and `x_12(1) -> 1`.
* **The model.** Actions agreeing on all six edges are a homomorphism `C_n -> Sym(Ω)`. ∎

**Remark 2.5.** For `n >= n_1` the six-piece colimit `C_n` of finite pieces is not residually finite.
This is the finite-stage
face of the fact that the radius-`r` hexagon colimit is freer than `St_3(R)`
(`research/artifacts/leavitt-defect-floor-experiment-2026-08-26.md`). What fails here is the hypothesis of IRH: there
is no coherent finite realization.

## 3. No rank-null closing

**Proposition 3.1.** Let `σ : π_1(Y_hex) -> prod_U Mon_(m_n)(F_q) <= M^x` be a monomial rank model, with
`q = p^k`, such that `rk(σ(x_12(1)) - 1) > 0`. Then `rk(σ(t) - 1) > 0`. This covers every Theorem 1.1 model.

*Proof.*
* **Descent.** Suppose `rk(σ(t) - 1) = 0`. In `M̄ = M / ker(rk)`, `σ̄` kills `t`, so it factors through `G`.
* **Hamming.** By `monomial-rank-models-are-hamming-models`, `j` carries `prod_U Mon_(m_n)(F_q)` into
  `prod_U Sym(X_(m_n))`, and rank-null agrees with Hamming-null. This gives a homomorphism `G -> prod_U Sym / null`,
  nontrivial on `x_12(1)`.
* **Binary.** The kernel is `1` by simplicity, so `G` embeds in a universal sofic group and is sofic (Elek–Szabó,
  literature). This contradicts `openai-leavitt-unit-nonsofic`.
* **Ternary.** The kernel lies in `Z`, by the argument of Lemma 2.2. The image is `G` or `G/Z`, nonsofic by
  `d-ary-leavitt-groups-nonsofic-over-finite-fields` and `projective-leavitt-unit-groups-mod-scalars-are-nonsofic`. ∎

**Corollary 3.2 (uniform gap).** There is `ε_p > 0` such that every Theorem 1.1 model on `Y_hex` has
`rk(σ(t) - 1) >= ε_p`.

*Proof.*
* **Setup.** Suppose models `σ_k` have `rk(σ_k(t) - 1) < 1/k`. Fix `S`, `Q`, `w` and derivations of the relators and
  of `x_12(1) = w` in `colim U_π`. They use finitely many vertex relations and edge identifications `D`, with at most
  `L` uses of `e_6`.
* **A stage.** For each `k` pick a stage `n_k` of `σ_k` where all of `D` is available,
  `rank(π_(n_k) - I)/m < 1/k`, and `x_12(1)` acts freely. There, `d_H(λ_132(x), λ_123(x)) <= 4/k` for `x ∈ K`.
* **Relators.** The maps `S -> Sym(Ω_(n_k))` given by the root words satisfy each relator up to `4L/k`, and
  `x_12(1)` moves every point (its rank defect is `(p-1)/p`).
* **The limit.** The ultraproduct over `k` is a homomorphism `G -> prod_U Sym / null`, nontrivial on `x_12(1)`.
  Proposition 3.1 excludes it. ∎

## 4. Calibrations and model tests

Each hypothesis of Theorem 2.3 is needed:
* **Infinite.** For `R = F_p`, (SPG2) gives `colim U_π(F_p) = St_3(F_p) -> SL_3(F_p)`. The regular representation of
  `SL_3(F_p)` restricts literally along all six edges. The radicals have order `8` (`|SL_3(F_2)| = 168`, 21 orbits)
  and order `27` (`|SL_3(F_3)| = 5616`, 208 orbits). So `σ(t) = 1` with the free profile. This is (IRH5).
* **Finitely generated.** For `R_0 = union M_(2^k)(F_2)`, each stage piece lies in `GL_3(M_(2^k)(F_2))`, which is
  finite. The hexagon closes exactly at every stage, which gives a nontrivial model of `EL_3(R_0)`, a locally finite
  group.
* **Simple.** For `F_2[x]`, `colim U_π = St_3(F_2[x])` maps onto the finite groups `SL_3(F_2[x]/(f))`, which close the
  hexagon nontrivially.
* **Kills t.** On `π_1(Y_hex)` itself, Theorem 1.1 is a nontrivial weakly finite model.

Tests against the landed models:
* **Graph-of-groups** (`graphs-of-locally-finite-groups-carry-regular-rank-models`). Theorem 2.3 predicts
  `σ(t) != 1`, and the construction never chooses `1`. Consistent.
* **HNN** (`locally-finite-hnn-data-allow-independent-cylinder-defects`) **and Fock**
  (`fock-local-letters-extend-level-models-with-disjoint-commutation`). Their groups are firewalls for relation
  subsets. None has `G` as a quotient by the relations it models, so Section 2 does not apply to them. Consistent.
* **UT₄** (`one-shared-root-plus-dyadic-depth-has-ut4-countermodels`). The targets are nilpotent, and `G` is perfect,
  so any model factoring through `G` is trivial, in agreement with Theorem 2.3. The UT₄ models are of a non-perfect
  relation family. Consistent.

## 5. Ternary

Theorem 1.1 transfers verbatim over `F_3`. Its proof uses:
* free actions of finite groups on finite sets;
* permutation matrices;
* `rank(λ_Q(α))` for `α ∈ F_3[Q]`;
* `rank(I - AB) = rank(I - BA)`.

None of these depends on the characteristic. This is a reading of the proof, not re-derived by a verifier.

The pieces are 3-groups. A root pair `x_ij(a)`, `x_jk(b)` generates `{x_ij(ua) x_jk(vb) x_ik(z)}` with
`u, v ∈ F_3` and `z ∈ F_3 ab`, a group of order at most 27 and exponent 3. Sections 2–3 for `L_(F_3)(1,2)^x` are
conditional as `prime-field-leavitt-tensor-hosts-fp-and-simple-mod-centre` is.

## 6. The rank-two kernel

* **No input.** No `U_π` contains opposite roots: `(i,j)` is positive on one arc of three orders and `(j,i)` on the
  complementary arc. So `ker(St_2(R) -> E_2(R))` never enters `Y_hex` as an input.
* **Native words.** Each of its elements, lifted through vertex groups, lies in `<<t>>`. Binary example:
  `ω = (x_12^123(1) x_21^213(1))^3`.
  * Its image in `GL_2(F_2)` is the cube of `[[0,1],[1,1]]`, which is `1`, so `ω -> 1` in `G`.
  * In `U_123 *_(E_1) U_213 <= P`, `x_12(1)` and `x_21(1)` lie outside `E_1 = <x_13, x_23>`. So `ω` is a reduced
    word of length 6, and `ω != 1`.
* **What it contributes.** These are the authenticated Weyl occurrences IRH asks for, but as consequences of
  `t = 1`, not new relations. One native word is no obstruction by itself: calibration `F_2` kills `ω` at every stage.
  Corollary 2.4 concerns the whole stage colimit. The kernel itself was not computed.

## 7. Verdict

* **Proved on paper, held OPEN.**
  * Proposition 1.1.
  * Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4 (elementary).
  * Proposition 3.1 and Corollary 3.2, from nonsoficity.
  * The calibrations.
* **Refuted.** Nothing. The route "choose forest bijections so the hexagon closes" is dead, exactly and in rank.
* **Where a refutation must live.** A nontrivial weakly finite representation of `G` has a target that is:
  * not an algebraic ultraproduct of finite or commutative-linear matrix groups (Section 2);
  * not a monomial rank model (Section 3).

  The next class is non-monomial `F_2`-linear rank models, i.e. `binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-f2-linear-sofic`. A
  model there that closes `t` would make `G` linear sofic but not sofic. To my knowledge no group separating these is
  known. I attributed this question to Arzhantseva–Păunescu from memory, without checking the source (advisory 38.5.5).
* **Where a proof must bite.** Corollary 1.3 of the firewall artifact says a relation-only proof must use `t = 1`.
  For arguments whose conclusions are checked only in ultraproduct targets this is automatic, and it gives no
  information about the gate.
  * A proof of the representation form of `binary-complement-corner-has-no-weakly-finite-image` must be a certificate
    valid in every weakly finite ring of characteristic two.
  * The certificate is a finite chain of square identities `X_i Y_i = I + (earlier deficits)` over `F_2[G]`, lifted
    to `F_2[π_1(Y_hex)]` modulo `(t - 1)`, with `[g] - 1` in the ideal of the deficits.
  * None was found.
* **Next lead.** Test linear rank models on `Y_hex`. Take free-profile linear actions of the `U_π(R)` that are not
  monomial, and ask whether the closing defect `rk(σ(t) - 1)` can tend to `0`.
