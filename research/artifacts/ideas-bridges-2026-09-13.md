## 2. New bridges

**B1. PROVED: a Kervaire failure over a balanced presentation is a Whitehead counterexample.**
- *Statement:* `deficiency-zero-kervaire-failure-yields-whitehead-counterexample`.
  Take `G != 1` with a finite presentation of `n` generators and `m <= n`
  relators, and `(G * <t>)/<<w>> = 1`. Then:
  - `m = n` and `deg_t w = +-1`;
  - the presentation complex of `<X, t | R, W>` is contractible;
  - `K(X|R)` sits inside it with `pi_2 != 0`;
  - `G` has torsion and is neither hyperlinear nor MF.
  If `m < n`, no `w` kills `G`.
- *Why new:* main had no node linking Kervaire to Whitehead or to deficiency
  (a `git grep -i deficiency` over the Kervaire, Whitehead and AC nodes returned
  nothing).
- *Proof:* the route `deficiency-zero-kervaire-failure-whitehead-proof`: cell
  count, then Hurewicz plus Whitehead, then Klyachko.

**B2. PROVED (item 3 of B1): normal-closure transfer.** A failing `G` inside a
balanced finitely presented `H` with `H = <<G>>` makes `H` fail, so `H`'s
complex is a Whitehead counterexample. Any Boone--Higman-type envelope that is
balanced and normally generated by `G` carries the refutation.

**B3. PROVED as wiring: finite Whitehead implies Kervaire (killing form) over
nonnegative deficiency.**
- OPEN claim `kervaire-killing-form-holds-over-nonnegative-deficiency-groups`;
- route `nonnegative-deficiency-kervaire-via-finite-whitehead`.

**B4. IDEA: a triple-hit search target.**
- *Statement:* restrict the Kervaire counterexample search (lane
  hl-kl-counterexample) to balanced presentations with torsion and no small
  finite quotients.
- *Payoff:* any certified hit refutes Kervaire and finite Whitehead, and gives a
  finitely presented non-hyperlinear group (B1).
- *Test:* rerun its pipeline with `m = n` as a hard filter.
- *Kill criterion:* a theorem that balanced presentations with torsion always
  have a nontrivial hyperlinear quotient, or satisfy Kervaire. None is known.

**B5. IDEA: Andrews--Curtis candidates from B1.**
- *Statement:* a balanced Kervaire failure hands a balanced presentation of the
  trivial group, `<X, t | R, W>`, whose sub-presentation `<X | R>` is
  non-aspherical with torsion. Conjecture-shaped test: can such a presentation be
  AC-trivial at all?
- *Test:* is there an AC-invariant "every balanced sub-presentation obtained by
  deleting one generator and one relator is aspherical or trivial"?
- *Kill criterion:* one AC-trivial balanced trivial presentation with a
  non-aspherical deletion. Small cases with a torsion relator such as `x^2` are
  cheap to test on MSI. Not run.

**B6. IDEA: Gottschalk plus Boone--Higman.**
- *Statement:* if a nonsurjunctive CA exists over a group whose memory subgroup
  has solvable word problem, then Boone--Higman gives a finitely presented simple
  nonsurjunctive group. This sharpens `finitely-generated-simple-groups-are-surjunctive`
  from finitely generated to finitely presented for decidable witnesses.
- *Test:* combine `surjunctivity-passes-to-subgroups` with an fp simple envelope.
- *Kill criterion:* it adds nothing unless some witness candidate on main is
  decidable. Most are, so this is a wiring task, not a theorem.

**B7. IDEA: deficiency constraints on fp intermediate growth.**
- *Statement:* a finite presentation of deficiency at least 2 gives a large group
  (Baumslag--Pride; not pinned here), which has exponential growth. So an fp
  intermediate-growth witness has deficiency at most 1.
- *Test:* pin Baumslag--Pride, then check `fp-intermediate-growth-witness-exclusions`.
- *Kill criterion:* already recorded there. Not checked.

**B8. IDEA: Kervaire over hyperbolic groups, sofic side.**
- *Statement:* a unimodular failure over a hyperbolic group gives a
  non-hyperlinear, hence nonsofic, hyperbolic group, settling `nonsofic-hyperbolic-group`.
  Restrict B4 to C'(1/6) presentations with proper-power relators. They are
  hyperbolic, have torsion, and can be balanced.
- *Test:* add a small-cancellation filter to B4.
- *Kill criterion:* if C'(1/6) torsion groups are residually finite (open in
  general), candidates need further structure.

**B9. IDEA: Whitehead-type asphericity for Kervaire relative presentations.**
- *Statement:* Howie's relative asphericity for `<G, t | w>` (used by main's
  Kervaire lanes) versus B1's absolute complex `L`. When `G` has a balanced
  aspherical presentation, is `<G, t | w>` relatively aspherical exactly when
  `L` is aspherical?
- *Test:* compare `pi_2` through the Mayer--Vietoris decomposition of `L`.
- *Kill criterion:* a known relatively aspherical `<G, t | w>` with
  non-aspherical `L`.

**B10. KILLED: free group factors via non-hyperlinear groups.** A non-hyperlinear
group says nothing about `L(F_2)` versus `L(F_3)`, since both are Connes-embeddable.

**B11. KILLED: Thompson F amenability versus surjunctivity.** Both amenability and
soficity imply surjunctivity, so no new link in either direction.

**B12. KILLED: Kervaire failure implies nonsurjunctive.** No mechanism turns a
killing word into a cellular automaton; the problems share only the simple-host
reduction.

**B13. IDEA: Eilenberg--Ganea versus strong Atiyah on Bestvina--Brady kernels.**
- *Statement:* `H_L` is a subgroup of a right-angled Artin group, so
  `L^2`-Betti numbers of `H_L` are integral. Strong Atiyah is inherited by
  subgroups, and holds for RAAGs by locally-indicable results; not pinned. Does
  integrality constrain a 2-dimensional `K(H_L,1)` for the Poincaré-sphere `L`,
  e.g. through the `L^2`-Euler characteristic of a putative 2-complex model?
- *Test:* compute `chi^(2)(H_L)` from the level-set complexes, and compare with
  what a finite-type 2-dimensional model forces.
- *Kill criterion:* `H_L` is not of type F, so there is no Euler characteristic
  constraint to use. Likely killed.

## 4. Exact gap left by B1

The refutation needs a nontrivial group with a balanced finite presentation that
one unimodular relator kills. None of main's reductions (finitely presented,
two-generator simple, absorbing) controls deficiency. So B1 does not show that
a general Kervaire failure refutes Whitehead. The missing lemma is a balanced
`H = <<G>>` envelope (B2) for an arbitrary finitely presented failing `G`, and
no such embedding theorem is known here.
