# An explicit nonuniqueness interval for the torsion-detour presentations

Research continuation — September 9, 2026

## Status and scope

This note supplies a direct quantitative Bernoulli bond-percolation proof for the **standard four-regular Cayley graphs** of the groups constructed in the preceding note, and for a larger class including finite subpresentations. It does not use their cost-one property, acylindrical hyperbolicity, the Choi–Seo theorem, or a universal pivotal estimate.

The new conclusions are an explicit interval `[7/20,37/100]`, a uniform lower bound `theta(p)>1/20`, and an explicit random-walk separation estimate. These are written deductions from the classical small-cancellation input specified below, basic spectral calculations, and elementary percolation facts. No originality, independent referee approval, or formal verification is claimed.

Neither universal Fixed Price nor universal Benjamini–Schramm is solved. This proof concerns a designated generating set of a specially presented class. It does not provide the required argument for arbitrary groups or arbitrary generating sets. Previous broad cost claims are not premises.

## 1. Statement

Let J be any subset of the positive integers, finite or infinite, possibly empty. For i in J choose a prime p_i>=13. Set

    Gamma = <a,b | (a b^i)^(p_i)=1, i in J>,
    G = Cay(Gamma,{a,a^(-1),b,b^(-1)}).

The graph is simple and four-regular by the algebraic arguments below. Let theta(p) be the probability that its identity belongs to an infinite Bernoulli bond-p cluster. Let X_k be independent simple random walk on G, with no holding step.

**Theorem.** For every p in [7/20,37/100]:

1. There are infinitely many infinite clusters almost surely.
2. theta(p)>1/20.
3. For every integer k>=300,

       P_p(o and X_k lie in different infinite clusters)>1/800.

In fact, let alpha_6 be the unique positive root of

    p+2p^2+4p^3+8p^4+16p^5+32p^6=1.

Then

    p_c(G)<=alpha_6 = 0.345997218806744...,
    p_u(G)>=(sqrt(29)-sqrt(5))/(6 sqrt(2))
                         = 0.371124620474627... .       (1.1)

The decimal values are numerical evaluations; the exact polynomial and radical expressions are the mathematical bounds. A somewhat stronger upper-threshold bound follows by replacing sqrt(29/2) in the proof with

    D_*=2 sqrt(1+sqrt(3)+sqrt(455)/24),

and is approximately 0.3716399214. No optimality is claimed.

For J infinite satisfying

    sum_(i in J) q^(p_i-1)=infinity for every q in (0,1),

the prior finite-selection proof additionally gives fixed price one. That cost conclusion is logically separate from this percolation proof. The constant profile p_i=13, J={1,2,...}, is a concrete example.

## 2. Algebra: the specific small-cancellation presentation

### 2.1. Classical input and the linear-word convention

We use the standard Greendlinger–Dehn consequence of classical C'(1/6): every nonempty freely reduced word representing the identity contains a **linear subword** longer than half of a symmetrized defining relator. This is the usual Dehn-reduced-word criterion (Lyndon–Schupp, Chapter V, Theorem 4.4); Sapir [S, Theorem 3.7] presents the diagrammatic Greendlinger lemma. The linear-word consequence is important below, rather than an unsupported switch between linear and cyclic runs. It also follows from the standard two-exterior-cells version of the lemma: at most one of the disjoint exposed arcs crosses the chosen boundary basepoint. The single-cell case is immediate, after removing any boundary spur.

An infinite presentation causes no difficulty: a particular null word has a finite van Kampen diagram, using a finite subpresentation which still satisfies C'(1/6). Proper powers are allowed. Symmetrization is a SET of distinct words, not a multiset of rotations.

Put u_i=ab^i and r_i=u_i^(p_i), with |r_i|=p_i(i+1). For different i,j, a common positive segment cannot contain two a's: the distances between consecutive a's would force i=j. Relative to r_i its length is at most 2i+1. Distinct rotations of the same periodic word have no common a-containing segment, since one a would align their phases and make them the same word; their common prefix has length at most i. The inverse cases are identical. Positive and negative relators have no letters in common.

Thus every piece in r_i has length at most

    2i+1 < p_i(i+1)/6,

because p_i>=13. Hence the presentation satisfies C'(1/6). Every relator is entirely positive in {a,b}, or entirely negative after taking its inverse, and every relator has length at least 26.

### 2.2. Two explicit free subgroups

The subgroup generated by a^2,b^2 is F_2. Indeed, substitute these into a nonempty reduced word on two generators. There is no free cancellation between its surviving syllables. Every run of a's or b's has a nonzero even length. A segment matching r_i cannot pass through an internal a-run, so has length at most i+2 (or i if it contains no a). This is shorter than |r_i|/2. The same holds for inverse relators. The Dehn criterion proves nontriviality.

Also

    <a b^(-1), a^(-1)b> is F_2.                         (2.1)

To see this, substitute u=aB and v=Ab, using capitals for inverses. Between noninverse consecutive u,v syllables there is no cancellation: the only inverse letter junctions arise from adjacent inverse syllables, which were excluded. Each length-two block has one positive and one negative letter. Therefore every constant-sign run has length at most two. It cannot contain a relator half, whose length would be at least 14. Again the Dehn criterion proves freeness.

These arguments in particular imply that a and b have infinite order and that the four Cayley neighbors are distinct.

### 2.3. A third explicit subgroup

Let c=ab and d=ba. If 1 is in J, put p=p_1. Since d is conjugate to c, there is a homomorphism

    C_p * C_p -> Gamma,

sending the cyclic generators to c,d. This homomorphism is injective.

Take a nonempty free-product normal form, alternating nonzero c- and d-powers. Choose exponents in [-(p-1)/2,(p-1)/2], since p is odd. Substitution into a,b is freely reduced. For adjacent syllables, equal signs create a doubled a or doubled b, while opposite signs create a sign change; none causes free cancellation.

A constant-sign alternating segment matching r_1=(ab)^p lies within one syllable, hence has length at most p-1<|r_1|/2. For i=2, a matching segment can contain at most one full b^2 run. Two such runs would require two c-to-d positive junctions, with an intervening d-to-c junction that doubles a or changes sign. Its length is therefore at most six. For i>=3, no full b^i run exists in the substituted word; a matching segment has length at most five. These bounds are all below |r_i|/2. Negative relators have the same bounds.

Greendlinger excludes a nontrivial normal form representing the identity. Thus

    <ab,ba> is C_p*C_p if 1 is in J.                   (2.2)

If 1 is not in J, the same proof permits arbitrary nonzero integer exponents because there is no r_1 to check, and gives

    <ab,ba> is F_2 if 1 is not in J.                   (2.3)

Only the specified subgroup statements are used below. The group is not asserted to be a free product itself.

## 3. A genuinely embedded tree and supercritical survival

Let L_6 be the set of freely reduced words on a,b,A,B such that each consecutive run of positive letters {a,b}, and each consecutive run of negative letters {A,B}, has length at most six. It is prefix closed and includes the empty word.

Evaluation of L_6 in Gamma is injective. If v,w are different words, freely reducing v^(-1)w leaves a nonempty concatenation of an inverse suffix of v and a suffix of w. Each part has sign-runs at most six; only the junction can merge two runs. Every **linear** sign-run consequently has length at most twelve. Since a relator half must be a constant-sign linear subword of length at least fourteen, Greendlinger excludes v^(-1)w=1.

Thus the prefix tree on L_6 embeds as a subgraph of G, using actual Cayley edges. It need not be an induced subgraph. Extra ambient edges can only help percolation.

From a nonroot vertex with last sign-run length j<6, there are two children continuing the sign and one child changing it. At j=6 only the sign-changing child remains. At the root there are four children.

### 3.1. A finite-offspring Galton–Watson process

Start at a vertex immediately after a sign change (run length one). Stop each descendant path at its first subsequent sign change. If this occurs after j additional same-sign steps, j=0,...,5, there are 2^j possible stopped descendants at distance j+1. These descendants form an antichain.

Under bond-p percolation, let Y count stopped descendants connected to their ancestor. Its mean is

    m(p)=sum_(j=0)^5 2^j p^(j+1).                    (3.1)

The indicators within a single offspring count need not be independent. However, different stopped vertices have disjoint unexplored descendant trees. Therefore their future offspring counts are independent with the same distribution. This is an ordinary Galton–Watson process, with at most 63 offspring.

If m(p)>1 it survives with positive probability. The corresponding infinite skeleton path has all segments open and gives an infinite open path in G. Since m is strictly increasing, p_c(G)<=alpha_6.

At p_0=7/20,

    m(p_0)=2058819/2000000>1.                         (3.2)

This is an exact rational computation, not an estimated growth rate.

### 3.2. An explicit survival-probability lower bound

Let f_j(z) be the offspring probability generating function when at most j further same-sign steps are allowed before the next sign change. Then

    f_0(z)=1-p+p z,
    f_j(z)=(1-p+p z)(1-p+p f_(j-1)(z))^2,
                                          j=1,...,5. (3.3)

Put m_j=f_j'(1), B_j=f_j''(1). Differentiating gives

    m_0=p, B_0=0,
    m_j=p+2p m_(j-1),
    B_j=4p^2 m_(j-1)+2p^2 m_(j-1)^2+2p B_(j-1).      (3.4)

Let s>0 be the survival probability of this Galton–Watson process. The extinction identity f_5(1-s)=1-s and Taylor's theorem (f_5''' is nonnegative on [0,1]) give

    1-s <= 1-m_5 s+(B_5/2)s^2,
    s >= 2(m_5-1)/B_5.                              (3.5)

At p_0, the probability that a particular root edge leads to an infinite open descendant tree is at least

    z_0=p_0*2(m_5-1)/B_5
       =23527600000/1803394208413
       =0.0130462878777... .                         (3.6)

The four root branches use disjoint edges of the embedded tree. Consequently

    theta(p_0)>=1-(1-z_0)^4>1/20.                    (3.7)

The last inequality is verified by exact rational arithmetic in the replay. Monotonicity gives theta(p)>1/20 for every p>=p_0. We are not assuming independence between any ambient clusters.

## 4. A spectral bound from the three subgroups

Let lambda be the regular representation and

    P=(lambda(a)+lambda(a)^*+lambda(b)+lambda(b)^*)/4,
    A=4P.

Define Q_0,Q_-,Q_+ as the symmetric four-point averages on the generator pairs

    (a^2,b^2),
    (ab^(-1),a^(-1)b),
    (ab,ba),

respectively. Direct multiplication of the sixteen terms gives

    P^2=(I+Q_0+Q_-+Q_+)/4.                          (4.1)

Restriction of the regular representation of Gamma to a subgroup is a Hilbert-space sum of copies of that subgroup's regular representation. By Section 2,

    ||Q_0||=||Q_-||=sqrt(3)/2.                       (4.2)

For reference, the four-regular tree adjacency norm is 2sqrt(3). The upper bound follows from a weighted Schur test with a Busemann weight giving one neighbor ratio sqrt(3) and three ratios 1/sqrt(3). Radial truncations of 3^(-distance/2) give the reverse bound. Dividing by four proves (4.2).

### 4.1. Bound for C_p*C_p

When Q_+ is not the free-group average, its Cayley graph is a cactus made of p-cycles. This follows from free-product normal form: the only simple cycle blocks are cosets of the two cyclic factors and their block-incidence graph is a tree.

For a nonempty finite vertex set W, let k be the number of full p-cycles in its induced graph and c the number of components. The cactus identities give

    |E(W)|=|W|-c+k,
    (p-1)k<=|W|-c.

Thus

    |boundary_E W|=4|W|-2|E(W)|
                  >=[2-2/(p-1)]|W|.

For p>=13, the unnormalized edge-isoperimetric constant is at least 11/6.

The needed spectral-isoperimetric inequality has a short proof. For a finitely supported nonnegative f, layer-cake/coarea and Cauchy–Schwarz give

    h||f||_2^2
       <=sum_{edges{x,y}} |f(x)^2-f(y)^2|
       <=sqrt((4||f||_2^2-<Af,f>)(4||f||_2^2+<Af,f>)).

Since the adjacency kernel is nonnegative, absolute values of general f reduce the norm upper bound to nonnegative f. Therefore

    ||A/4||<=sqrt(1-(h/4)^2)<=sqrt(455)/24.            (4.3)

The free-group value in (2.3) also satisfies this upper bound. Combining (4.1)–(4.3),

    ||A||^2 <=4(1+sqrt(3)+sqrt(455)/24)
             <29/2<(381/100)^2.                      (4.4)

For the strict rational comparison, sqrt(3)<26/15 and sqrt(455)<107/5. Their substitutions make the middle expression strictly less than 29/2. Hence

    ||A||<sqrt(29/2)<381/100,
    ||P||<381/400.                                  (4.5)

No finite quotient or truncated Cayley-ball numerical eigenvalue is used to establish this infinite-operator bound.

## 5. Nonbacktracking paths control the connectivity operator

This section holds for any infinite d-regular simple graph, with adjacency operator A and an upper bound D>=2sqrt(d-1) for ||A||. Put b=d-1 and

    sigma=(D+sqrt(D^2-4b))/2.

Let N_k(x,y) count length-k walks from x to y with no immediate reversal. Then

    N_0=I, N_1=A, N_2=A^2-dI,
    N_k=A N_(k-1)-b N_(k-2),                  k>=3.   (5.1)

The count is of walks, not probabilities. Each percolation connection has a simple open path; simple paths are among the nonbacktracking walks. Therefore the union bound over SIMPLE paths, followed by enlargement of the nonnegative sum, gives

    T_p(x,y):=P_p(x connected to y)
        <=sum_(k>=0) p^k N_k(x,y).                    (5.2)

We do NOT assign probability p^k to a walk which repeats an edge.

The recurrence makes each N_k a polynomial in A. Chebyshev's recurrence, or its two-root solution, gives

    ||N_k|| <= (k+1)sigma^k+(k-1)sigma^(k-2),   k>=2.

For example, explicitly

    N_k=b^(k/2) U_k(A/(2sqrt(b)))
          -b^((k-2)/2) U_(k-2)(A/(2sqrt(b))),

and the asserted bound follows on the spectral interval [-D,D]. Thus the operator series converges absolutely when p sigma<1. Summing (5.1),

    sum_(k>=0) p^k N_k
       =(1-p^2)(I-pA+b p^2 I)^(-1).                  (5.3)

All entries of the sum are nonnegative. Entrywise domination in (5.2) consequently gives domination of the l2 norm: apply the kernel inequality to |f| for finitely supported f. We obtain

    ||T_p||_(2->2)
       <=(1-p^2)/(1-Dp+b p^2),   p<1/sigma.          (5.4)

The inverse alone does not justify (5.4) beyond this convergence range. This restriction avoids using the other positive branch of the quadratic denominator.

For our four-regular G use D=sqrt(29/2). Then

    1/sigma=(sqrt(29)-sqrt(5))/(6sqrt(2)).             (5.5)

In particular T_p is bounded for p<=37/100. A convenient uniform estimate uses the slightly weaker rational D'=381/100. The denominator at p=37/100 is exactly

    1-(381/100)(37/100)+3(37/100)^2=1/1000.

It is decreasing on [0,37/100] and positive throughout that interval, which lies below the smaller root. Therefore

    ||T_p||<=1000,       0<=p<=37/100.                (5.6)

## 6. Nonuniqueness and separation

The use of bounded connectivity operators to exclude uniqueness is standard [H]. Here is the required argument.

If Bernoulli percolation at a supercritical parameter has a unique infinite cluster, then, by Harris's inequality,

    T_p(x,y)>=P_p(x and y both in the infinite cluster)
              >=theta(p)^2>0

for every x,y. For a finite set F,

    <1_F,T_p 1_F>/||1_F||_2^2>=theta(p)^2|F|.

This contradicts boundedness of T_p as |F| tends to infinity. Hence p_u is at least (5.5).

At every p in [7/20,37/100], Section 3 gives theta(p)>1/20 and Section 5 rules out uniqueness. The number of infinite clusters is almost surely a constant by Bernoulli ergodicity. It cannot be a finite k>=2: with positive probability a finite connected ball meets two infinite clusters, and forcing the ball's finitely many edges open merges clusters, reducing k with positive probability, contrary to constancy. This finite-energy argument leaves only 0,1,infinity. Here the answer is infinity.

For the quantitative random-walk statement put

    a_k(p)=P_p(o connected to X_k).

By (4.5), (5.6), and Cauchy–Schwarz,

    a_k(p)=<T_p delta_o,P^k delta_o>
             <=1000(381/400)^k.                       (6.1)

The probability that both o and X_k belong to some infinite cluster is at least theta(p)^2 by Harris's inequality, averaged over X_k. Subtracting their probability of belonging to the same infinite cluster (which is at most a_k(p)) gives

    P_p(o and X_k belong to different infinite clusters)
       >=theta(p)^2-a_k(p)
       >1/400-1000(381/400)^k.                        (6.2)

Exact rational arithmetic verifies

    1000(381/400)^300<1/800.

The right side decreases with k. Hence (6.2)>1/800 for all k>=300, as stated. The probabilities include both independent sources of randomness: percolation and the walk. The usual statement about each fixed parameter is intended; no unproved simultaneous uncountable intersection is being taken.

## 7. Relationship to the Fixed Price progress and the remaining universal gap

If the detour abundance condition in Section 1 holds, the prior proof constructs in every free action a graphing {T_b on X, T_a restricted to A} with mu(A) arbitrarily small. Its ingredients are the exact torsion orders, pairwise disjoint power sets, finite partition selection, and direct repair of every failure. Therefore that group has fixed price one. The present proof gives a direct numerical nonuniqueness interval for the standard Cayley graph of every such group, without deriving percolation from cost.

The percolation argument is actually valid for arbitrary finite or infinite J, regardless of detour abundance. For instance J empty gives F_2 with fixed price two, and one defining relation gives a different free-product example. Thus this common percolation interval is not evidence that all the group costs coincide.

The positivity of the defining words was essential to the embedded-tree proof. In a fixed-exponent free Burnside group, the nonempty reduced word

    (a b^(-1))^n

is the identity and has sign-run length one. It lies in L_6. Evaluation of that language is therefore NOT injective there. This is an explicit failure of the proposed extension, not a gap that can be filled by reusing the same argument.

The special subgroup norm estimates are also not available in arbitrary nonamenable groups: groups of bounded exponent do not contain the free subgroups used here. Nonamenability alone says ||P||<1, but does not supply the quantitative matching of the survival and operator thresholds.

Accordingly no proof or counterexample for universal Fixed Price or universal Benjamini–Schramm is established here. The every-generating-set and every-group quantifiers are not supplied by the explicit standard-graph bound. The earlier Choi–Seo consequence for all Cayley graphs of this class is an external qualitative theorem, not a replacement for these missing universal arguments.

## 8. Audit and finite computations

The accompanying replay checks the exact rational endpoint and survival calculations; the length-two group-algebra identity; nonbacktracking recurrences by direct walk enumeration on four finite four-regular simple graphs; finite normal forms for the auxiliary free-product and free subgroup arguments; and finite run-length combinatorics. Decimal evaluations of the thresholds are recorded separately from exact assertions.

These tests do not prove Greendlinger's lemma, injectivity for all words, an infinite operator norm, survival of an infinite branching process, or either universal conjecture. The written arguments above, together with the explicitly imported classical lemma, address those steps. A successful Cairn dependency check is not formal theorem verification or independent mathematical validation.
