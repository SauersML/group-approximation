# A two-generator witness for free-action cost above one

Research proof and audit — September 8, 2026

## Status and scope

**Action-wise theorem.** Let alpha be an essentially free probability-measure-preserving action of a countably infinite group G. If

    C(alpha restricted to <g,h>) <= 1 for every g,h in G,

then C(alpha)=1. Equivalently, any action of cost greater than one has a two-generated subgroup on which its restriction has cost greater than one.

**Class corollary.** If every two-generated subgroup of G is amenable, G has fixed price one. No bounded-exponent or finite-generation hypothesis occurs in this corollary.

The implication is one-way. A cost-one action can restrict to a two-generated subgroup of cost greater than one. The theorem does NOT identify the cost of the whole action with the maximum of its two-generated restriction costs.

The proof below is a complete written deduction from the explicitly identified published inputs. Its bounded-exponent step uses the subgroup-relative-cost argument from the preceding research note, reproved here rather than assumed. For unbounded exponent we give a same-action calculation using cyclic complete sections; it follows the common-high-order-element strategy of Ershov–Golan–Sapir, whose published theorem treats the amenable-pair class. The algebraic input about Shunkov groups is due to Suchkova–Shunkov; it is stated explicitly in Chernikov's primary research paper cited below.

No priority claim, external referee approval, or formal proof verification is asserted. The universal Fixed Price problem and the universal Benjamini–Schramm conjecture are NOT proved by this note. A Cairn ESTABLISHED label records a written deduction or imported theorem, not an independent check of mathematical truth.

## 1. Definitions and published inputs

All actions are on standard probability spaces and all relation identities are modulo invariant null sets. After removing an invariant null set, write a free action on the right, x -> xg. For H <= G let R_H be its orbit equivalence relation. A graphing Phi is a countable family of partial measure-preserving bijections. Its cost is the sum of its domain measures. The cost C(R) is the infimum over graphings generating R.

For S <= R define

    relC(R;S) = inf { Cost(Psi) : S join R_Psi = R }.

This is an infimum; relC=0 does not mean that a zero-cost graphing attains it. Write Cmax(G) for the supremum of free-action costs. Do not identify it with infimal group cost.

We use these published facts.

**Cost facts [G, L].** A relation with almost all classes infinite has cost at least one. Every free action of an infinite amenable group has cost one. A free finite-group action of a group of order h has cost 1-1/h. These statements hold without an ergodicity assumption.

**Relative bound [L, Proposition 2.39].** If S <= R is aperiodic, then

    relC(R;S) <= C(R)-1.

Only the case C(R)=1 is needed later. One proof chooses arbitrarily small complete sections A for S and uses the complete-section induction formula

    C(R)-1 = mu(A) [ C(R|A, normalized measure)-1 ].

Then S joined with R|A generates R, at additional unnormalized cost at most C(R)-1+mu(A)+epsilon.

The unbounded-exponent argument uses the same complete-section induction formula above for finite cyclic subrelations, not a finite-index multiplicativity theorem. Ershov–Golan–Sapir's Theorem 5.6 and Lemma 5.7 provide the published antecedent for the common-high-order-element strategy; their gluing lemma need not be imported separately here.

**Suchkova–Shunkov [SS; C, paragraph preceding its Theorem].** A Shunkov group satisfying the descending chain condition on abelian subgroups is Chernikov. Here G is Shunkov if, for each finite K <= G, every subgroup of N_G(K)/K generated by two conjugate elements of prime order is finite. A Chernikov group is an extension of a finite direct product of quasicyclic groups by a finite group; in particular an infinite Chernikov group has an infinite abelian subgroup.

**Betti lower bound [L, Theorem 2.64].** For an infinite group and any free p.m.p. action alpha,

    beta_1^(2)(G) <= C(alpha)-1.

This last input is used only for the corollary, not the fixed-price proof.

## 2. Finite measurable selection on the original action

### Lemma 2.1

Let F be a finite set of distinct elements of G and eta>0. There exists a finite measurable partition P of X such that, except on a set of measure less than eta, the points xg, g in F, lie in distinct atoms.

**Proof.** Choose refining finite measurable partitions separating points of a standard Borel model. For distinct g,h, freeness gives xg != xh almost surely. Thus the probability that xg and xh remain in the same atom tends to zero. There are finitely many pairs. Take a sufficiently fine partition and apply the union bound. QED.

Let P_1,...,P_k be disjoint finite subsets of F. Temporarily select each atom of P independently with probability q, 0<q<1, and let A be the union of selected atoms. Define

    D(A) = { x : for each i, some g in P_i has xg not in A }.

For a root outside the exceptional set, all tested atom colors are independent. Fubini and averaging over this finite auxiliary probability space give

    E_colors mu(A) = q,
    E_colors mu(D(A)) <= product_i (1-q^|P_i|) + eta.

Consequently some deterministic measurable A satisfies

    mu(A)+mu(D(A)) <= q+product_i(1-q^|P_i|)+eta.       (2.1)

The sum, rather than separate bounds for the chosen A, is what is proved and used. This auxiliary randomization chooses a set in the original action space. No Bernoulli extension replaces the action.

## 3. Two sparse extension lemmas

### Lemma 3.1: first-return detours

Let K <= G and a not in K. Suppose there are arbitrarily many disjoint coordinate sets

    c_i = a b_i, b_i in K,
    2 <= d_i <= N, c_i^d_i in K,
    P_i = {c_i,...,c_i^(d_i-1)} subset G\K.

Then relC(R_<K,a>;R_K)=0.

**Proof.** For a finite family of k detours, choose A by (2.1). Add the transformation x -> xa on A and on D(A), or just on their union. The cost is at most

    q+(1-q^(N-1))^k+eta.                              (3.1)

A failed root has its direct a-edge. Otherwise choose successful i, so xc_i^j belongs to A for 1<=j<d_i. The path

    xa --b_i--> xc_i --a--> xc_i a --b_i--> xc_i^2
       --a--> ... --b_i--> xc_i^d_i --c_i^(-d_i)--> x

uses R_K-steps and only retained a-steps. It recovers the a-connection at every root. All maps used are inside R_<K,a>, so the joined relation is exactly R_<K,a>.

Given epsilon>0, choose q<epsilon/3, then finite k with (1-q^(N-1))^k<epsilon/3, then eta<epsilon/3. This gives arbitrarily small additional cost. QED.

No normality of K is needed: c_i^d_i in K is a first-return assertion, not a quotient-group assertion. Abstract R_K steps expand into finite paths in an R_K graphing on a common conull set. No bound on those lengths is assumed.

### Lemma 3.2: centralizer extensions

If K intersect C_G(w) is infinite, relC(R_<K,w>;R_K)=0.

**Proof.** Choose distinct l_1,...,l_k in K intersect C_G(w). Apply (2.1) to singleton sets {l_i}. Keep w-edges on A and repair roots for which none of xl_i is in A. The additional cost is at most q+(1-q)^k+eta. For a successful root,

    x --l_i--> xl_i --w--> xl_i w=xw l_i --l_i^(-1)--> xw

recovers the connection using R_K and the selected w-edge. Send the cost bound to zero in the same order. QED.

For comparison, if |K intersect C_G(w)|=ell<infinity, a measurable transversal for its free finite action gives the corresponding bound 1/ell. This finite version is used in the preceding note's quantitative finite-subgroup theorem, not in the new class theorem here.

## 4. The bounded-exponent subgroup theorem

### Theorem 4.1

Suppose G is countably infinite and g^N=e for all g, for a fixed finite N. For every infinite subgroup H <= G and every free p.m.p. action alpha,

    relC(R_G;R_H)=0,
    C(alpha) <= C(alpha|H).                            (4.1)

**Proof.** Keep the specified action fixed throughout.

Zero-relative-cost extensions over H are closed under countable joins. For subgroups K_j containing H, each with relC(R_Kj;R_H)=0, take additions with costs below epsilon/2^(j+1). Together with R_H their union generates the relation of <union_j K_j>, at total additional cost below epsilon. Zero relative cost is also transitive, by splitting an error budget.

There is a largest subgroup K containing H with relC(R_K;R_H)=0. For each element of G that belongs to any qualifying subgroup, choose one witness subgroup. Countability gives countably many witnesses, and their join is qualifying and contains all qualifying subgroups.

By Lemma 3.2 and maximality,

    w not in K  =>  |K intersect C_G(w)| < infinity.    (4.2)

Suppose a not in K. The coset aK is infinite. For c in aK set

    d(c)=min { j>=1 : c^j in K },
    P(c)={c,...,c^(d(c)-1)}.

The exponent law gives 2<=d(c)<=N. All entries of P(c) are distinct and outside K; an earlier repetition would yield a smaller positive power equal to e and in K.

Fix w not in K. If w belongs to P(c), then c commutes with w. If aK intersect C_G(w) is nonempty, choose c_0 in it. Direct multiplication gives

    aK intersect C_G(w) = c_0 (K intersect C_G(w)).     (4.3)

This set is finite by (4.2). Hence every external w lies in only finitely many of the P(c).

We can greedily choose arbitrarily many pairwise disjoint P(c). At each step the union W of previously chosen sets is finite. Each w in W excludes only finitely many new c, so the infinite coset aK still supplies a further choice.

Lemma 3.1 adds a at zero relative cost over K. Transitivity makes <K,a> a qualifying subgroup larger than K, a contradiction. Thus K=G. Appending the arbitrarily cheap additions to a nearly optimal H-graphing yields (4.1). QED.

### Corollary 4.2

A countably infinite bounded-exponent group containing an infinite fixed-price-one subgroup has fixed price one. In particular, an infinite amenable subgroup suffices.

**Proof.** Restrict any given free action to that subgroup. Its cost is one. Theorem 4.1 supplies the upper bound one for the given G-action; the infinite-class lower bound supplies the reverse inequality. QED.

## 5. The algebraic step missing from the previous continuation

Call a group binary finite when each subgroup generated by at most two elements is finite. Do not confuse this with local finiteness, which requires every finitely generated subgroup to be finite.

### Lemma 5.1

Every infinite binary-finite group contains an infinite abelian subgroup.

**Proof using Suchkova–Shunkov.** First, a binary-finite group is Shunkov. Given finite K <= G and u,v in N_G(K), the subgroup <u,v> is finite. Its image in N_G(K)/K is therefore finite. In particular this holds for quotient pairs that are conjugate and have prime order, as required by the definition.

Suppose all abelian subgroups of G are finite. Every descending chain of abelian subgroups begins with a finite group and consequently stabilizes. Suchkova–Shunkov makes G Chernikov. Since G is infinite, it then has an infinite abelian subgroup, a contradiction. QED.

This is an application of the cited algebraic theorem, not a new proof of that theorem. Nothing here asserts that all binary-finite groups are locally finite.

### Theorem 5.2: the bounded-exponent Amen_2 case

Let G be countably infinite of bounded exponent. If every two-generated subgroup of G is amenable, G has fixed price one.

**Proof.** If some two-generated subgroup H is infinite, it is amenable by hypothesis, so Corollary 4.2 applies. If no such subgroup is infinite, G is binary finite. Lemma 5.1 gives an infinite abelian subgroup, again amenable, and Corollary 4.2 applies. QED.

More generally, any infinite bounded-exponent Shunkov group has fixed price one: the same contradiction in Lemma 5.1 supplies an infinite abelian subgroup.

## 6. Same-action cyclic compression and the witness theorem

### Lemma 6.1: a common cyclic subgroup

Let G=<s_1,...,s_d> and let alpha be a specified free p.m.p. action. For g in G write H=<g>, H_i=<s_i,g>, and ell=ord(g), allowing ell=infinity with 1/ell=0. Then

    C(alpha)-1 <= sum_(i=1)^d [C(alpha|H_i)-1] + (d-1)/ell.   (6.1)

**Proof for finite ell.** A measurable transversal A for the free H-action has measure 1/ell and is a complete section for each R_Hi. The induction formula supplies, for any epsilon>0, graphings of R_Hi restricted to A whose unnormalized costs are below

    C(alpha|H_i)-1+1/ell+epsilon/d.

Joining these graphings with R_H gives every R_Hi: move each endpoint along H to A, then use the restricted relation. The H-relation has a treeing of cost 1-1/ell. Their union generates R_G. Adding the costs and taking epsilon to zero gives (6.1).

**Proof for infinite ell.** The H-action has cost one. Since R_H is aperiodic, the relative bound gives

    relC(R_Hi;R_H) <= C(alpha|H_i)-1.

Use additions approaching these bounds, together with a cost-one H-graphing, and take their finite union. This yields (6.1) with the reciprocal term zero. QED.

Every H_i has at most two generators, so all the costs in the sum are finite. The proof works on the original action; no assumption about costs of other actions appears.

### Theorem 6.2: two-generator witness, action-wise

Let G be countably infinite and alpha a free p.m.p. action. Suppose

    C(alpha|<g,h>) <= 1 for all g,h in G.                (6.2)

Then C(alpha)=1.

**Step 1: finitely generated G of bounded exponent.** If G has an infinite two-generated subgroup H, then C(alpha|H)=1 by (6.2) and the infinite-class lower bound. Theorem 4.1 gives C(alpha)<=1. If all two-generated subgroups are finite, Lemma 5.1 gives an infinite abelian subgroup L. Its restricted action has cost one by the amenable cost theorem; again Theorem 4.1 applies. The lower bound gives equality.

**Step 2: finitely generated G without bounded exponent.** Write G=<s_1,...,s_d>. For every positive integer M there is g with ord(g)>=M. Otherwise all element orders would be bounded, and their finite least common multiple would be a group exponent. Apply (6.1). Hypothesis (6.2) gives

    1 <= C(alpha) <= 1+(d-1)/M.

Let M tend to infinity. The strategy is the published EGS high-order-element argument, but (6.1) makes the inference for this specific action without an amenability assumption on H_i.

**Step 3: countably generated G.** If G is locally finite, its free actions have cost one. Otherwise choose an infinite finitely generated H_1 and exhaust G by increasing finitely generated subgroups

    H_1 <= H_2 <= ... , union_j H_j=G.

The hypothesis on pair restrictions passes to each alpha|H_j. The first two steps give C(alpha|H_j)=1. Put S=R_H1. The relative bound gives relC(R_Hj;S)=0 for each j. For epsilon>0 choose additions for all j with summable total cost below epsilon. Together with a graphing of S of cost below 1+epsilon, they generate R_G at cost below 1+2epsilon. Let epsilon tend to zero and use the lower bound one. QED.

There is no prior finite-cost assumption on R_G in Step 3. Its finite cost follows from the construction.

### Corollary 6.3: quantitative witnesses

If G has bounded exponent and C(alpha)>1, there is an infinite two-generated H, and every infinite subgroup H (including every such pair subgroup) satisfies

    C(alpha|H) >= C(alpha).

Indeed, if all pair subgroups were finite, the infinite abelian subgroup argument would force C(alpha)=1; comparison is Theorem 4.1.

If G is d-generated without bounded exponent and C(alpha)=1+delta>1, choose g with order at least 2(d-1)/delta. Formula (6.1) implies

    sum_i [C(alpha|<s_i,g>)-1] >= delta/2.

At least one i therefore satisfies

    C(alpha|<s_i,g>) >= 1+delta/(2d).                   (6.3)

For d=1 a free action of an infinite cyclic group cannot have delta>0, so the positive-cost case automatically has d>=2.

## 7. Fixed-price and Betti consequences; precise boundary

### Theorem 7.1: the complete Amen_2 class conclusion

Every countably infinite group all of whose two-generated subgroups are amenable has fixed price one.

**Proof.** Fix any free p.m.p. action alpha. Its restriction to an infinite amenable pair subgroup has cost one. Its restriction to a finite pair subgroup of order h has cost 1-1/h<=1. Thus (6.2) holds, and Theorem 6.2 gives C(alpha)=1. Since alpha was arbitrary, this is fixed price one. QED.

The finitely generated unbounded-exponent Amen_2 case is EGS Theorem 5.6, not a newly discovered theorem. The additional bounded-exponent argument and countable action-wise assembly are spelled out here. Novelty of the assembled results has not been established.

**Corollary 7.2.** A countable group with some free action of cost greater than one contains a two-generated nonamenable subgroup. The witness from Theorem 6.2 cannot be amenable, by the known amenable cost formula.

This does not assert that the witness subgroup itself lacks fixed price. Groups of fixed price greater than one can also satisfy the premise.

**Corollary 7.3.** If all two-generated subgroups of G are amenable, beta_1^(2)(G)=0. Apply the published Betti lower bound to a cost-one free action. For finitely generated groups this supplies the vanishing conclusion discussed in EGS Remark 5.9 through the stronger fixed-price-one statement.

**Retained earlier results.** The preceding bounded-exponent proof and its quantitative finite-subgroup argument still give

    Cmax(G) <= 1+((N-1) log h/h)^(1/N)

for every finite subgroup of order h>=3. Thus unbounded finite subgroup orders imply fixed price one. Held's theorem gives the bounded-exponent 2-group corollary, and the quoted Ivanov–Olshanskii input gives the specified large even Burnside examples. Those deductions are documented in the included previous note; they are not used in the new action-wise theorem.

**What is not proved.** Cost-one actions need not have cost-one restrictions, so the witness theorem cannot be reversed. Groups containing two-generated nonamenable subgroups are not covered solely by Theorem 7.1. The large odd-exponent free Burnside upper-cost constant remains undetermined. The exact-normal-subgroup theorem giving infimal group cost one cannot be substituted for an upper-cost theorem. Neither finite-index action-cost multiplicativity nor a uniform pivotal-count bound is used or proved. The original universal Fixed Price and Benjamini–Schramm goals remain unresolved by this work.

## 8. Audit and computational scope

The previous bounded-exponent replay was re-run in this continuation: 48,584 exact assertions passed, including 5,952 exhaustive finite colorings. It checks finite first-return identities, centralizer fibers, packing counts, selected-edge repairs, and selection probabilities. Numerical parameter sanity checks are reported separately in that replay.

Those computations do not verify the infinite subgroup closure, the imported Suchkova–Shunkov theorem, amenable cost facts, or the universal targets. The written proof above carries the infinite argument. Cairn checks dependencies and artifact availability, not the validity of the mathematical reasoning.

Critical audit points: (i) selection bounds the SUM of selected and repair costs; (ii) first return is to a possibly nonnormal subgroup; (iii) maximality is justified by a countable join; (iv) external power fibers are finite, not uniformly finite; (v) one uses first-return sets, not full cyclic subgroups; (vi) binary finite is not replaced by locally finite; (vii) quotient normalizer pairs lift to a finite two-generated subgroup; (viii) the final countable exhaustion uses summable relative costs; (ix) unbounded exponent is handled by the cyclic-section calculation, not by extending Theorem 4.1 beyond its hypothesis; (x) the action-wise witness implication is not reversed.
