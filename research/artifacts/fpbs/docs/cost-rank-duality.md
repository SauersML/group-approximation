# Cost and the obstruction to restriction-consistent matroid duality

September 10, 2026. Written deductions from the cited cost theorems.
Neither Fixed Price nor Benjamini--Schramm is resolved here.

## 1. The candidate that fails

For a bounded-degree pmp graphing G=(X,E,mu), let R_F be the
equivalence relation generated by a measurable unoriented edge set
F contained in E. Distinguish its optimized cost C(R_F) from the
edge cost c(F)=one half of its expected degree. Consider

    kappa_G(F) = C(R_F).

This is neither monotone nor submodular in general. The failure
occurs in free actions of finitely generated groups whose fixed
prices are already known, and has arbitrarily large size.

Fix r>=2 and Gamma=F_r x Z^2, with free generators a_1,...,a_r
and central generators z,w. Let Gamma act essentially freely and
pmp on a standard probability space. Work on an invariant conull
set where the action is free. Write H for the union of the
unoriented a_i-edge sets and Z,W for the z- and w-edge sets. Put

    A=H union Z,     B=H union W,     E=H union Z union W.

Freeness makes these edge labels distinct, so A intersect B=H
and A union B=E. The restricted F_r action is free and H is a
treeing of degree 2r. Hence C(R_H)=r. The other three restricted
actions are of F_r x Z, F_r x Z, and F_r x Z^2, respectively;
each has cost one. Consequently

    kappa_G(H)=r,
    kappa_G(A)=kappa_G(B)=kappa_G(E)=1.

Monotonicity would require r<=1. Submodularity would require

    kappa_G(A)+kappa_G(B)
      >= kappa_G(A intersect B)+kappa_G(A union B),

that is, 2>=r+1. Its deficit is r-1.

These calculations hold for every essentially free pmp action
of this Gamma, not just for an action obtained by adjoining
extra independent randomness. A Bernoulli action is one example.

The cost inputs are Gaboriau's treeing theorem and the fixed-price-one
case of a group generated by a connected family of commuting
infinite-order generators. Alternatively, for the latter case keep
the full z graphing and restrict each other generator to a common
arbitrarily small complete section D of the aperiodic z action.
Every x can move along its z orbit to D. Apply the desired generator
there and return along z, using centrality. The graphing generates
the whole action and has cost at most 1+(r+1)mu(D). For A omit w;
for B use w as the full generator. The aperiodic lower bound gives
cost exactly one. Small complete sections and the treeing theorem
are recorded in [Gaboriau's lectures, Theorem 2.24 and the proof of
Proposition 2.39](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/gaboriau/Travaux-Publi/ME-Cost-L2-Lectures/ME-Cost-L2-lectures.pdf).

## 2. The precise impossibility statement

Suppose an assignment G |-> q_G supplies setfunctions on the
measurable edge subsets of every bounded-degree pmp graphing and
has both properties:

1. Total-cost normalization: q_G(E)=C(R_E).
2. Restriction consistency: for F contained in E, q_G(F)=q_(G^F)(F),
   where G^F=(X,F,mu).

Then q_G(F)=C(R_F) by applying the first property to G^F. Section 1
shows that such an assignment cannot always be monotone and cannot
always be submodular. This also rules out a monotone assignment
that attains the ambient cost and lower-bounds every subrelation's
cost: on the same graph it would require

    r=C(R_H) <= q_G(H) <= q_G(E)=C(R_E)=1.

This is an impossibility statement about these simultaneous
requirements. It does not prohibit a submodular function whose
total mass equals cost but whose values on proper edge subsets
depend on the ambient graphing. It does not prove that all cost
dualities are impossible, nor does it answer the broader question
of existence of a cost-attaining cycle matroid.

## 3. What the graphing-matroid literature actually supplies

[Berczi, Borbenyi, Lovasz and Toth, arXiv:2406.08945v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08945v2)
define the rank

    rho_G(F) = 1 - integral_X 1/|[x]_(R_F)| dmu(x),

with 1/infinity=0. Their Theorem 1.3 identifies exposed bases with
hyperfinite essential spanning forests. Essential allows several
infinite forest components inside a single ambient component.
On H,A,B,E above, this rank is always one. In particular it does
not equal the cost r of the treeing H.

Their Theorem 1.9 gives a cost-attaining rank using the dual of a
planar graphing, under their stated planar hypotheses and measure
normalization. Their proof uses a one-ended forest in the dual
whose complement connects each primal component. Question 1.5
asks for a general cost-attaining construction. Section 2 rules
out an additional restriction-consistency requirement; that
requirement is not part of their question.

Thus the useful feature of planar duality is a mechanism proving
primal connectivity, not simply availability of submodularity.
There is no planar dual supplied here for an arbitrary Cayley graph.

## 4. Why cheap completion alone does not repair this rank

Let R=R_E and S=R_H in Section 1. Then C(R)=1, C(S)=r, and S
is aperiodic. The established relative-cost inequality

    0 <= relC(R;S) <= C(R)-1

gives relC(R;S)=0. Here relC is the infimum of c(Psi) over graphings
Psi with S join R_Psi=R, with all maps measurable on the original X.
This uses `fpbs-cost-one-backbone-fixes-completion-bill`, whose
general sandwich applies also when the starting cost exceeds one.

There are consequently arbitrarily cheap completions of H. But
any graphing that retains all H edges has edge cost at least r.
Cheap completion of that fixed backbone cannot approach C(R)=1:
the infimum of c(H union Psi) over such completions is exactly r.
The upper bound follows from c(H union Psi)<=r+c(Psi), and the
lower bound follows from inclusion of H. Achieving cost one must
also replace some of H. Optimized relation cost and the edge cost
of a fixed generating presentation must stay distinct.

In the traffic programme, this is why a guarantee of arbitrarily
cheap repair is useful only together with a bound on the retained
graphing's cost. No amount of submodular bookkeeping can identify
the two quantities in this example.

## 5. Remaining direction, with no unproved theorem registered

A viable dual approach must specify which ambient information its
certificate retains. A certificate for R_E cannot automatically
serve as a cost certificate for every R_F. Conversely, a forest
certificate that only guarantees infinite components must carry
an additional argument for connecting the whole orbit. The
planar theorem exhibits one such argument in its own setting.

For the present free factor Y->X, the desired certificate would
have to bound the costs of all generating graphings on Y after
pullback from X. No such complete family of certificates is
constructed here. In particular, this audit does not establish
C(X)<=C(Y), the missing reverse inequality in the live route.
It eliminates the restriction-consistent cost-rank shortcut and
keeps ambient-dependent duality available for further work.

The subsequent [isoperimetric test-descent argument](isoperimetric-test-descent.md)
does remove source information from all finite symmetric-vertex
tests for a fixed pulled-back graphing. It uses finite-set kernels
and approximate measurable edge coloring, rather than the failed
rank assignment. Its Section 6 isolates the still open comparison
between the two classes of graphings over which one optimizes.
