# Prime-divisor cost bounds and local, uniformly expanding torsion sparsifiers

## Status and scope

This note gives a constructive strengthening of the earlier action-level torsion detour argument. It does not solve Fixed Price or the Benjamini–Schramm conjecture, and makes no bibliographic priority claim. Its substantive outputs are (i) the improved action-cost bound 2-2/p, where p is the smallest prime divisor of the large odd exponent n, and (ii) simultaneous control of edge intensity, deterministic replacement distances, locality, and pointwise expansion. The original-edge geometric theorem has cost bound 2-2/n+epsilon; attaining the sharper 2-2/p+epsilon bound may use bounded-length power edges not present in the original generating graph.

The construction uses the cyclic-centralizer algebra and torsion detours recorded by Donoso-Echenique–Silva [DS]. The new derivations here combine a half-boundary cycle-deletion lemma, exponentially many short disjoint detours, and an explicit local repair rule. The algebraic inputs and Abért–Weiss are imported theorems; the finite replay does not verify them. All group actions used for cost are on standard probability spaces and are essentially free and probability preserving.

The preceding note, `torsion-actions.md` in the package, permits arbitrarily small relative extension cost but explicitly does not bound paths in an arbitrary nearly optimal subgroup graphing. Here we use a particular cyclic backbone, not an arbitrary nearly optimal graphing. Consequently the geometric theorem below is not an assertion of short routes near the unknown minimal action cost.

## 1. Main theorem: the standard Burnside graphs

Fix an odd integer n >= 1003. Let Gamma_m = B(m,n), m >= 2, with standard generators a_1,...,a_m, and let G_m be its simple undirected standard Cayley graph. Let

    h_n = inf { |boundary_E F| / |F| :
                empty != F finite subset of Cay(B(2,n), {a_1^+-1,a_2^+-1}) }.

The imported nonamenability theorem gives h_n > 0. The subgroup generated by a_1,a_2 is canonically B(2,n): the homomorphism that kills the other free Burnside generators is a retraction. Thus h_n is independent of m.

**Theorem 1.** There is a constant C_n < infinity such that, for every m >= 2 and 0 < epsilon < 1, a Gamma_m-equivariant finite-radius factor of independent continuous vertex labels produces a random subgraph K_(m,epsilon) of G_m with the following properties almost surely.

1. K_(m,epsilon) is connected and spanning.
2. Its half expected degree is at most 2 - 2/n + epsilon.
3. Every nonempty finite vertex set F satisfies

       |boundary_K F| >= (h_n/2) |F|.

4. Every original generator edge has a K-path of length at most

       R_(m,epsilon) <= C_n [1 + log(m/epsilon)].

   The bound holds simultaneously for all edges. In particular,

       d_G(x,y) <= d_K(x,y) <= R_(m,epsilon) d_G(x,y).

5. The edge decisions are determined by independent labels in an ambient ball of radius at most C_n[1+log(m/epsilon)]. This is a deterministic coding radius, not merely an almost-sure finite random radius. Equivariance is for the specified group action and generator labels, not claimed for every unlabeled-graph automorphism.
6. The total edge intensity of retained types a_3,...,a_m is at most epsilon.

The same numerical cost, replacement-distance and orbitwise expansion bounds are achievable by a generating graphing of each given free p.m.p. action of Gamma_m, using only restrictions of its original generator transformations. The arbitrary-action assertion is not an iid-factor assertion and does not replace the given action by a Bernoulli extension.

The half-degree bound is a bound for a specific graphing, and hence an upper bound on action cost. It is not an equality for the infimum defining the action's cost.

For m=2 the construction is just the cyclic backbone of Section 2. For m>=3, Sections 3–5 produce the additional edges.

**Corollary 1.1 (prime-divisor improvement).** If p is the smallest prime divisor of n, then every free p.m.p. action alpha of B(m,n) satisfies

    Cost(alpha) <= 2 - 2/p.

In particular, for odd n>=1003 divisible by 3, every such action has cost at most 4/3. This improves the previous 2-2/n bound when n is composite. Section 10 gives the proof and a geometric realization using bounded-range power edges. It does not compute any action cost exactly or prove fixed price.

## 2. The cyclic backbone and a pointwise half-boundary lemma

**Lemma 2.1.** Let C be a finite simple cycle and delete any one edge e. For every subset A of its vertices,

    |boundary_(C-e) A| >= (1/2) |boundary_C A|.

**Proof.** The number of cycle edges crossing A is either zero or at least two. Deletion removes at most one of them. QED.

For each positive a_1-cycle and each positive a_2-cycle, delete the outgoing edge at the unique minimum-priority vertex. Use independent continuous priorities; uniqueness holds simultaneously on the countably many finite cycles almost surely. Each generator has order n, so each such cycle becomes a path of n-1 edges. Every removed edge has a replacement path of length n-1.

Call the union of the resulting paths L. Its components are precisely the left cosets of H=<a_1,a_2>. Its edge intensity is exactly

    (1/2) E deg_L(o) = 2 - 2/n.                         (2.1)

To justify the count, each directed positive generator edge has a distinct underlying undirected edge, since n is odd and at least 3. A proportion 1/n of its sources are the selected cycle minimum. Incoming and outgoing intensities agree by invariance. There is no overlap between the two generator edge types.

Apply Lemma 2.1 separately to the a_1-cycles and a_2-cycles. For every finite F,

    |boundary_L F|
       >= (1/2) |boundary_(full a_1,a_2 graph) F|
       >= (h_n/2)|F|.                                  (2.2)

The last step sums the Cayley expansion inequality over the finitely many H-cosets met by F. The first graph may be disconnected when H is proper, but every component is infinite and expanding. Additional edges preserve (2.2). This is the reason later sparsification does not destroy the expansion constant.

The backbone rule has bounded radius at most n in the original graph.

## 3. Short independent torsion detours

We state this portion more generally. Suppose a group Gamma has abelian centralizers of nonidentity elements, all of size at most n. Distinct such centralizers have trivial intersection. Indeed, if w != e lies in C(u) intersect C(v), then u,v lie in the abelian C(w) and commute. Abelianness of C(u) now implies C(u) subset C(v), and symmetry gives equality.

In a subgroup of B(m,n), the required centralizer properties follow by intersecting its centralizers with the ambient cyclic centralizers of size n. Nonidentity element orders are at most n and odd.

Let H=<s,t> be a nonamenable subgroup with s,t drawn from the chosen generating set. Write h for the edge expansion constant of its degree-four Cayley graph, and

    gamma = 1 + h/4 > 1.

Its intrinsic balls satisfy

    |B_H(L)| >= gamma^L.                                (3.1)

Indeed, at least h|B_H(L)| boundary edges lead out of B_H(L), and each new vertex accounts for at most four of them. Thus |B_H(L+1)| >= (1+h/4)|B_H(L)|, starting from |B_H(0)|=1.

Fix an extra generator a outside H. All elements of a B_H(L) are nonidentity. A centralizer contains at most n of them, so a B_H(L) meets at least |B_H(L)|/n distinct centralizers. If |B_H(L)| >= nk, choose

    c_i = a b_i,   b_i in B_H(L),   1 <= i <= k,

in distinct centralizers. Set d_i = ord(c_i), and

    P_i = {c_i, c_i^2, ..., c_i^(d_i-1)}.

The sets P_i are pairwise disjoint, exclude the identity, and d_i <= n. Every point of every P_i has ambient word length at most (n-1)(L+1).

This is the local independence resource: exponentially many choices in L, with at most n-1 required label tests per choice. The sets of tested coordinates are disjoint for one root and one extra generator. No independence between the final retained edges at different roots is asserted or needed.

## 4. The selector and paid-repair rule

For each extra generator a use its own iid vertex field U_(x,a), uniform in [0,1]. Write

    A_a = {x : U_(x,a) < q}.

A vertex x is a failure if every candidate detour has an unselected required point:

    D_a = {x : for every i, some 1 <= ell < d_i
                    has x c_i^ell not in A_a }.

Retain the positive a-edge {x,xa} precisely when x belongs to A_a union D_a. The rule pays directly for every failure.

Because the P_i are disjoint and do not contain the identity, the exact edge-retention probability is

    P(x in A_a union D_a)
       = q + (1-q) product_i (1-q^(d_i-1))
       <= q + exp(-k q^(n-1)).                           (4.1)

The first equality uses independence of the label at x from all detour labels. The final inequality uses 0<q<1 and d_i<=n.

**Deterministic routing.** If the a-edge at x was not retained, x is not a failure, and some i has x c_i^ell in A_a for every 1<=ell<d_i. Route backwards from xa to x:

    xa --b_i--> x c_i --a--> x c_i a --b_i--> x c_i^2
       --a--> ... --b_i--> x c_i^(d_i) = x.

There are d_i b_i-steps and d_i-1 a-steps. Every displayed a-step starts at a selected point, and is therefore retained. Replace each b_i-step by a word of length at most L in s^+-1,t^+-1, and each of its letters by a backbone path of length at most n-1. The total path length is at most

    d_i(n-1)L + (d_i-1) <= (n-1)(nL+1).                 (4.2)

If the a-edge was retained, its replacement path simply has length one. Therefore (4.2), not merely a high-probability estimate, holds for every a-edge in every selector configuration. Continuous priorities are needed only for unique cycle minima; labels on null tie events can be assigned arbitrary outputs.

The decision for an outgoing a-edge inspects only the source label and the labels at xP_i. The full root star also includes incoming positive edges, so a coding-radius bound is

    max{ n, (n-1)(L+1)+1 }.                              (4.3)

A single iid continuous mark can encode all finitely many independent fields used at each vertex.

## 5. Explicit parameters and the rank-uniform bound

For the standard Gamma_m with m>=3 let r=m-2, take H=<a_1,a_2>, and choose

    q = epsilon/(2r),
    k = ceiling( q^(-(n-1)) log(1/q) ),
    L = ceiling( log(nk) / log(1+h_n/4) ).                (5.1)

Then gamma^L >= nk provides k short detours for each extra generator. Equation (4.1) is at most 2q, so summing over the r extra types gives total extra intensity at most epsilon.

Equation (2.1) gives the asserted total half-degree. Equations (2.2), (4.2), and (4.3) give expansion, routes, and locality. Every original generator edge has a replacement, so the graph is connected and spanning.

For clarity about the big-O constant, q<=1/2 and

    log(nk) <= log(2n) + (n-1)log(1/q) + log log(1/q).

For t>0, log t <= t, so the right side is bounded by log(2n)+n log(1/q). Hence there is C_n, depending only on n and h_n, such that both the coding and routing radii are at most

    C_n [1 + log(m/epsilon)].                            (5.2)

The number of tests k is large, approximately q^(-(n-1))log(1/q). A logarithmic radius is not a claim of small computational running time, few inspected vertices, or practical efficiency. The constants can be very large.

This proves the iid assertion of Theorem 1.

## 6. Every generating set of every infinite finitely generated subgroup

**Theorem 2.** Let Gamma be an infinite finitely generated subgroup of B(m,n), with odd n>=1003. For any finite symmetric generating set S excluding the identity, choose representatives S_+ of its inverse pairs. There are noncommuting s,t in S_+. Put H=<s,t> and let h_H be the edge expansion constant of Cay(H,{s^+-1,t^+-1}). Then h_H>0. For every 0<epsilon<1 there is a finite-radius FIID connected spanning subgraph K_epsilon of Cay(Gamma,S) such that

    (1/2)E deg_K(o)
       <= (1-1/ord(s)) + (1-1/ord(t)) + epsilon
       <= 2-2/n+epsilon,                                (6.1)

    h(K_epsilon) >= h_H/2,                              (6.2)

and its original-edge routing and coding radii are at most

    C_(Gamma,S,n) [1+log(1/epsilon)].                    (6.3)

The retained edge intensity for positive generator types outside {s,t} is at most epsilon; types in H other than s,t may all be omitted. Every given free p.m.p. action admits the corresponding graphing version.

**Algebraic justification.** If all generators commuted, Gamma would lie in the finite cyclic centralizer of one of them, contrary to infinitude. Thus s,t exist. Their subgroup is noncyclic, and [I, DS] gives a subgroup isomorphic to B(infinity,n), hence B(2,n). Since B(2,n) is nonamenable, H is nonamenable. These are imported results, not supplied by the finite tests.

**Construction.** Delete one edge from each finite s- and t-cycle, of lengths ord(s), ord(t). The cycle lemma and the H-coset decomposition prove (6.2). Every backbone letter has a route of length at most n-1.

Let r=|S_+ outside H|. For r>0, use (5.1) with h_H in place of h_n and one independent selector field for each such generator. For the finitely many generators in (S_+ intersect H) outside {s,t}, choose fixed s,t-words. Let ell_0 be their maximum length, or zero if there are none, and omit all their original edge types. They are replaced by paths of length at most (n-1)ell_0. A bound valid for all original edges is

    R_epsilon = (n-1) max{1, ell_0, nL+1}.               (6.4)

If r=0, Gamma=H and the backbone alone suffices, with a constant radius. Otherwise Sections 3–5 prove all assertions. QED.

The construction in fact only needs bounded finite abelian centralizers, no involutions, and the existence of a nonamenable two-generator subgroup generated by two of the chosen generators. The Burnside algebra is used to ensure these hypotheses for all the specified generating sets.

## 7. Implementing the construction in each original free action

Fix a given essentially free p.m.p. action and discard an invariant null set so that every orbit is free. We use right-action notation x->xg. A Borel injective real label on the action space picks a unique minimum on each finite s- and t-orbit. Delete its outgoing generator edge. These measurable finite-class selectors have measures exactly 1/ord(s) and 1/ord(t). Thus the backbone graphing cost is exactly the first two terms of (6.1), without requiring iid priorities.

For each extra a outside H choose q=epsilon/(4r), k and L as above. By Abért–Weiss [AW], the original free action weakly contains the Bernoulli shift. Concretely, for

    F_a={e} union P_1 union ... union P_k

and eta>0 there is a measurable A_a in the original space whose binary F_a-pattern has law within eta in total variation of the iid Bernoulli(q) pattern. Finite-pattern total variation follows by approximating the finitely many atom probabilities to sufficiently small error.

Define D_a by the identical finite formula from Section 4, and retain the restriction of T_a to A_a union D_a. This union is a single finite-pattern event. Its measure is therefore at most

    q + exp(-kq^(n-1)) + eta <= 2q+eta.

Choose eta<=q. Each extra type costs at most 3q, and their total is below epsilon. No independence between different A_a, or between these sets and the backbone selector, is necessary.

All routing and boundary statements are deterministic properties of the selected graph. Thus they hold on every free orbit in the conull set, with the same bounds. Every failed detour is repaired directly, so no exceptional positive-measure failures or limiting-connectivity assertions enter the proof.

This graphing is a family of restrictions of the original finite list of generator transformations. Its cost is the sum of their domain measures, which agrees with half of the average orbit degree since inverse duplicates and involutions have been excluded.

The arbitrary-action construction does not claim to be a Bernoulli factor, to have iid output, or to have a finite coding radius relative to any preassigned labeling of that action. Its rules depend on finitely many translates of newly selected measurable sets. Its graph-metric replacement bound is deterministic and logarithmic as stated.

## 8. The logarithmic route order is necessary for sparse coset reconnection

**Proposition 3.** Let G=Cay(Gamma,S) be d-regular with d>=3, H<=Gamma, and a in S outside H. Let J be an invariant random subgraph of G. Call an edge external when its generator label lies outside H, and suppose its intensity is

    delta=(1/2)E deg_external(o)>0.

If d_J(o,a)<=R almost surely, then

    1 <= delta |B_G(o,R)|.                              (8.1)

**Proof.** A path between o and a cannot consist solely of H-generator steps. A path of length at most R stays in B_G(o,R), so there is an external retained edge with both endpoints in that ball. Hence

    1 <= E[# external retained edges internal to B_G(o,R)]
      <= (1/2) sum_(v in B_G(o,R)) E deg_external(v)
      = delta |B_G(o,R)|.

The half-sum counts internal edges exactly once and may also count outgoing edges by halves, so its use as an upper bound is valid. QED.

The standard degree bound

    |B_G(o,R)| <= 1 + d[(d-1)^R-1]/(d-2)

then implies, for delta<=1,

    R >= log(1 + ((d-2)/d)(delta^(-1)-1)) / log(d-1).     (8.2)

In particular, any family with external intensity at most epsilon and almost-sure deterministic replacement radii needs R=Omega_G(log(1/epsilon)). When H is proper, the construction in Theorem 2 has exactly this order in epsilon. For the iid parameter choice, the external intensity is also at least rq=epsilon/2, so epsilon represents the actual intensity up to a factor two.

This optimal-order assertion concerns sparse inter-coset reconnection and the limit epsilon->0 for a fixed graph. It does not assert optimal constants, optimal rank dependence, optimal average degree, or any lower bound on the cost of the action. If H=Gamma the proposition is inapplicable and constant routes may suffice.

## 9. Robustness under further independent percolation

**Lemma 4.** Any infinite connected locally finite graph X with edge expansion at least h>0 has

    p_c(X) <= 1/(1+h).                                  (9.1)

Here p_c is the infimum retention parameter giving the root an infinite cluster. Root choice is immaterial on a connected graph.

**Proof.** Explore the root cluster by testing unexamined edges with exactly one endpoint already discovered. An open test discovers one new vertex. Continue until no such unexamined edge exists, or indefinitely, using a fixed exhaustive ordering. If exploration ends in a finite cluster C, the number S of successful tests is |C|-1, and the number F of failed tests is at least |boundary_X C|. Therefore

    F-hS >= h.

The tests reveal fresh independent Bernoulli(p) variables. Pad the coin sequence by independent coins after a possible stopping time. The process Z=F-hS has increments 1 with probability 1-p and -h with probability p. If p>1/(1+h), its drift is negative. There is a positive probability it never reaches h. For completeness, choose t>0 with E exp(t increment)<1, which is possible by the negative derivative at t=0. Then exp(t Z_j) is a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one; the maximal inequality bounds the probability of ever reaching h by exp(-th)<1. On the complementary event exploration cannot stop in a finite cluster. This proves positive survival probability and (9.1). QED.

Apply this conditional on any realization in Theorem 1 or 2:

    p_c(K_(m,epsilon)) <= 1/(1+h_n/2) < 1,              (9.2)

or p_c(K_epsilon)<=1/(1+h_H/2). The first bound is uniform in rank m and epsilon; the second is uniform in epsilon for the specified backbone.

This supplies robustness to a positive density of further independent deletions, not merely the absence of individual bridges. In the earlier vertical-fiber example on a tree times Z, the backbone fibers were amenable and the retained graphs' critical thresholds tended to one. The present backbone is nonamenable and its pointwise boundary lower bound prevents that particular fragility mechanism.

The law of K itself is dependent, even though it is produced from independent labels. Conditional independent thinning of K is not ordinary uniform-parameter Bernoulli percolation on the original Cayley graph. Neither (9.2) nor the expanding construction proves p_c(G)<p_u(G).

## 10. A genuine numerical improvement using the smallest prime divisor

**Theorem 5.** Fix odd n>=1003, let p be its smallest prime divisor, and let m>=2. Every free p.m.p. action alpha of B(m,n) satisfies

    Cost(alpha) <= 2-2/p.                                (10.1)

The same bound holds for every free action of every infinite finitely generated subgroup of B(m,n).

**Proof for the whole Burnside group.** Put ell=n/p and choose

    s=a_1^ell,   t=a_2^ell.

Both elements have order exactly p. They do not commute. Indeed, the ambient centralizer C(s) is cyclic of order n and contains the order-n element a_1, so C(s)=<a_1>. If t commuted with s, it would belong to <a_1> intersect <a_2>. This intersection is trivial, as witnessed by the independent coordinates of a_1,a_2 in the abelianization (Z/nZ)^m. That contradicts ord(t)=p>1.

Consequently H=<s,t> is noncyclic and nonamenable by the subgroup input [I, DS]. Enlarge the original finite generating set by s,t and their inverses. Apply Theorem 2 and its arbitrary-action version using this pair as the backbone. Each of the two cyclic orbit relations has a spanning-path graphing of cost 1-1/p. For every epsilon>0 the other generators can be adjoined at cost at most epsilon. Thus, within the original action space,

    Cost(alpha) <= 2-2/p+epsilon.

Take the infimum over these graphings as epsilon decreases to zero to obtain (10.1). This step does not claim that the infimum is attained by a graphing.

For an infinite finitely generated subgroup Gamma, use [I, DS] to find an embedded copy of B(2,n) inside Gamma. The same two power elements of its free generators have order p and do not commute. Adjoin these two elements to any finite generating set of Gamma and apply Theorem 2 and Section 7. QED.

This improves 2-2/n to 2-2/p whenever n is composite. In particular,

    3 divides n  ==>  Cost(alpha) <= 4/3,
    5 is the smallest prime divisor of n  ==>  Cost(alpha) <= 8/5.

For example, n=1005 is in the allowed range and is divisible by 3. The former bound was 2-2/1005=2008/1005, whereas the new bound is 4/3. For prime n the numerical bound is unchanged.

**Bounded-range geometric realization.** In the standard Cayley graph of B(m,n), the two added power-edge types have ambient lengths at most ell=n/p. Thus the stronger cost bound can be realized up to epsilon by a finite-radius FIID connected graph J_(m,epsilon) with all edges of original word length at most n/p, half expected degree at most 2-2/p+epsilon, and a positive edge expansion bound independent of m and epsilon. The two-power-generator subgroup and its degree-four Cayley graph are fixed inside the canonical B(2,n), so its expansion constant depends only on n.

The local construction and its parameter calculation apply to the augmented generating set. Its number of extra generators is at most m, and each step has original length at most n/p. Therefore the original-metric coding radius and the J-replacement length for each original generator are still O_n(1+log(m/epsilon)). Its independent-thinning threshold is uniformly bounded below one by Section 9.

The improved numerical graphings are not claimed to be subgraphs of the original standard Cayley graph: they may use those power edges. Expanding a power edge into an original-edge path can increase graphing cost, so it is not legitimate to retain the 2-2/p bound after such an expansion. Theorem 1 is the original-edge version, with bound 2-2/n.

## 11. What has been gained, and the unsolved core

There are now two positive conclusions. The original-edge bound 2-2/n has a much stronger geometric realization: graphings in every free action, a block FIID realization, deterministic logarithmic replacement paths, rank-independent expansion in the standard family, robustness to subsequent independent deletion, and a matching-order lower bound for sparse coset reconnection. Allowing bounded-range power edges gives the smaller action-cost bound 2-2/p, including 4/3 when 3 divides n.

The cheaper two-generator core still has graphing cost 2-2/p, which is greater than one for odd p. Replacing it by arbitrary nearly optimal subgroup graphings loses the explicit cycle geometry and the route/expansion proof. Reducing the core to one cyclic generator makes the subgroup finite, eliminating the infinite supply of disjoint detours used in Sections 3–5. Thus iteration of the construction as written does not force cost one.

The common maximal Burnside cost c_n in the earlier note is now bounded by

    1 <= c_n <= 2-2/p,

but remains undetermined. For the explicit finite-sheet action beta in that note, its proved formula Cost(beta)=1+(c_n-1)/n now gives the improved upper bound 1+(1-2/p)/n, which is 1+1/(3n) when p=3. No strict separation between the two action costs follows. Both universal Cairn goals remain OPEN.

## 12. Verification, reproducibility, and package semantics

Run the standard-library replay script:

    python3 scripts/replay_local_sparsifiers.py --output research/artifacts/local-sparsifier-checks.json

It checks:

- 28,216 exact cycle-cut/deletion cases for odd cycle lengths 3,5,7,9,11;
- 18 exact rational identities for the independent selector/repair probability;
- 512 selector masks on a finite 27-element Heisenberg fixture, including 13,824 prescribed routes, 41,472 actual generator distances, and 13,824 local sparse-edge witnesses;
- 160 exact ball-volume formula identities;
- 1,000 odd-integer power-order and prime-divisor cost identities (finite cyclic arithmetic, not an infinite-group verification).

The finite Heisenberg fixture is not asserted to satisfy the infinite Burnside hypotheses. Its disjoint torsion witnesses are explicitly checked. It tests the local route-and-repair mechanism, not the existence of witnesses in the infinite groups. In that fixture the maximum observed shortest generator replacement distance is 4, and the maximum constructed detour length is 15, below the proved fixture bound 20.

The script does not verify Burnside algebra, weak containment, infinite equivariance, measure-theoretic cost infima, percolation survival in infinite graphs, or priority. The written proofs and cited inputs handle those issues; none has been formalized in a proof assistant here.

Cairn checks dependency and artifact consistency. An ESTABLISHED node records an asserted written proof or cited theorem, not independent mathematical certification. Earlier package content is retained as provenance and has not all been re-audited in this continuation. Both universal target nodes are deliberately not given solved routes.
