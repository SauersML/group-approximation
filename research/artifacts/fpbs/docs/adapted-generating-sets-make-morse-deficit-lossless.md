# Adapted generating sets: the Morse deficit is lossless, and the premise's surplus is subgraphing attainment

Written 2026-09-17 by swarm worker `swarm-0917-w5-pull-fp-3` (transplanter, group-rings lane). This is a written
deduction from the definition of cost and two established nodes. Nobody has refereed it or checked it formally. It
uses the notation of `bernoulli-morse-collapse-criterion.md` (the "collapse note") and
`morse-l2-inequality-and-lamplighter-gap.md` (the "l2 note").

## 0. Summary

- **Theorem E (quantifier shift).** For every essentially free pmp action `a` of an infinite finitely generated
  group `Gamma` and every finite generating set `S_0`, `C(a) = lim_R C_sub^a(B_R)`, where `B_R` is the `S_0`-ball of
  radius `R` and `C_sub^a(S)` is the infimum of the costs of Borel subgraphings of `Phi_S^a` that generate `R_a`. The
  sequence is nonincreasing. So cost is always localized to the Cayley graphings of *some* finite generating sets.
- **Corollary F (lossless Morse form of the cost–Betti target).** For the Bernoulli shift `b`,
  `lim_R lim_L m_(B_R)(P_L) = C(b) - 1 - beta_1^(2)(Gamma)`. Both limits are monotone. So `Target(Gamma)`, i.e.
  `C(b) = 1 + beta_1^(2)(Gamma)` (equivalently `fpbs-bernoulli-cycle-tail-compactness` for `Gamma`), is *exactly*
  vanishing of the double-limit Morse deficit. Nothing is lost by the Morse reformulation once the generating set is
  allowed to grow.
- **Corollary G (the premise, split).** For a fixed `S`, `fpbs-morse-deficit-vanishes-for-full-presentations`
  at `S` is equivalent to `Target(Gamma)` together with `(Q3)(S)`: `C_sub(S) = lim_R C_sub(B_R(S))`. The property
  `(Q3)(S)` is upward closed in `S`. The premise as stated (all `S`) is therefore `Target` plus the S-uniformity of
  subgraphing cost, and the only part of it beyond `Target` is a statement about *small* generating sets.

## 1. Setting

- `Gamma` is infinite, finitely generated by a finite set `S_0`, and `a : Gamma -> Aut(X, mu)` is essentially free
  and pmp. Discard an invariant null set so that `a` is free.
- For a finite subset `S` of `Gamma \ {1}` that generates `Gamma`, `Phi_S^a` is the graphing with edge set `X x S`,
  where `(x, s)` joins `x` and `s.x` (collapse note, Section 1). A *subgraphing* is a Borel set `E` of edges, with cost
  `nu(E)`, where `nu = mu x counting`. It *generates* if its connected components are the `R_a`-classes.
- `C(a)` is the infimum of costs of generating graphings, where a graphing is a countable family of Borel partial
  isomorphisms `phi_i : A_i -> B_i` with graphs inside `R_a`, and its cost is `sum_i mu(A_i)` (Levitt, Gaboriau).
- `C_sub^a(S)` is the infimum of `nu(E)` over generating subgraphings `E` of `Phi_S^a`. For the Bernoulli shift we
  write `C_sub(S)`, as in Theorem B of the collapse note.

**Monotonicity.** If `S ⊆ S'`, then every subgraphing of `Phi_S` is one of `Phi_(S')`, so
`C_sub^a(S') <= C_sub^a(S)`. Every subgraphing is a graphing, so `C(a) <= C_sub^a(S)`. Every finite `S` lies in
some ball `B_R = B_R(S_0) \ {1}`, and the balls increase. Hence

```text
C(a) <= lim_R C_sub^a(B_R) = inf_S C_sub^a(S).                                  (1.1)
```

## 2. Theorem E

**Theorem E.** `C(a) = lim_R C_sub^a(B_R) = inf_S C_sub^a(S)`, the infimum over finite generating subsets `S` of
`Gamma \ {1}`. More precisely, for every `eps > 0` and every graphing `Psi` generating `R_a` with `c(Psi) < infinity`
there is a finite `F ⊆ Gamma \ {1}` such that `Phi_(S_0 ∪ F)^a` has a generating subgraphing of cost
`< c(Psi) + eps`.

**Proof.** By (1.1) it suffices to prove the second sentence, applied to `Psi` with `c(Psi) < C(a) + eps`. Note
`C(a) <= |S_0| < infinity`.

1. *Pieces are translations.* Let `Psi = (phi_i : A_i -> B_i)_i`. For `gamma` in `Gamma` put
   `A_(i,gamma) = {x in A_i : phi_i(x) = gamma.x}`. These sets are Borel, and by freeness they partition `A_i`.
   Let `D_gamma = ∪_i A_(i,gamma)` for `gamma != 1`, and `E_Psi = {(x, gamma) : gamma != 1, x in D_gamma}`. Then
   `nu(E_Psi) <= sum_i mu(A_i) = c(Psi)`, and every non-loop `Psi`-edge `(x, phi_i(x))` is an `E_Psi`-edge
   `(x, gamma)` joining `x` and `gamma.x`. So `E_Psi` generates `R_a`.
2. *Truncation.* Enumerate `Gamma \ {1} = {gamma_1, gamma_2, ...}`. Let `E_N` be the edges of `E_Psi` with label in
   `{gamma_1, ..., gamma_N}`, and `R_N` the equivalence relation they generate. `R_N` is Borel (a countable union of
   graphs of finite compositions of Borel partial translations), `R_N ⊆ R_(N+1)`, and `∪_N R_N = R_a`: a pair in
   `R_a` is joined by a finite `E_Psi`-path, which uses finitely many labels.
3. *Cayley backup.* Let `F_N = {(x, s) in X x S_0 : (x, s.x) not in R_N}`. It is Borel and decreasing in `N`. For
   each `(x, s)`, the pair `(x, s.x)` lies in `R_a`, hence in `R_N` for large `N`. So the indicators of `F_N` decrease
   to `0` pointwise on `X x S_0`, and `nu(F_N) -> 0` by dominated convergence (`nu(X x S_0) = |S_0|`).
4. *The subgraphing.* Put `S_N = S_0 ∪ {gamma_1, ..., gamma_N}` and `E'_N = E_N ∪ F_N`, a Borel set of edges of
   `Phi_(S_N)^a`.
   - *It generates.* `R_N ⊆ R_(E'_N)`. Every `S_0`-edge `(x, s)` joins `R_(E'_N)`-equivalent points: either
     `(x, s.x)` is in `R_N`, or `(x, s)` is in `F_N`. Since `S_0` generates `Gamma`, `Phi_(S_0)^a` generates `R_a`,
     so `R_(E'_N) = R_a`.
   - *Its cost.* `nu(E'_N) <= nu(E_Psi) + nu(F_N) <= c(Psi) + nu(F_N)`.

   Choose `N` with `nu(F_N) < eps` and take `F = {gamma_1, ..., gamma_N}`. QED.

**Remarks.**

- `E'_N` has degree at most `2 |S_N|`, and its components are infinite because `Gamma` is infinite. So it is
  admissible in `fpbs-graphing-cost-betti-cycle-dimension-identity`.
- The proof is a finite-support truncation with a vanishing Cayley backup. The long edges `gamma_j` are *kept as
  generators* rather than rerouted. This is exactly the step at which the recorded dead attempt "route a near-optimal
  graphing through the Cayley graphing (Q3)" on `fpbs-morse-deficit-vanishes-for-full-presentations` dies: with `S`
  fixed it must pay `l` Cayley edges per long edge. Theorem E avoids the count by enlarging `S`.
- Nothing Bernoulli-specific is used. Theorem E holds for every free pmp action.

## 3. Corollaries F and G

**Corollary F.** Let `b` be the Bernoulli shift. For a finite generating set `S` let `m_S(P_L)` be the Morse deficit
of the list of all trivial words of length at most `L` in `S^(+-1)` (collapse note, Theorem B). Then

```text
lim_(R -> infinity) lim_(L -> infinity) m_(B_R)(P_L) = C(b) - 1 - beta_1^(2)(Gamma),
```

and both limits are nonincreasing. Hence `C(b) = 1 + beta_1^(2)(Gamma)` iff the double limit is `0`.

*Proof.* Theorem B(ii) of the collapse note, applied to the generating set `B_R`, gives
`lim_L m_(B_R)(P_L) = C_sub(B_R) - 1 - beta_1^(2)(Gamma)`, nonincreasing in `L`. By monotonicity (Section 1) this is
nonincreasing in `R`, and by Theorem E it tends to `C(b) - 1 - beta_1^(2)(Gamma)`. QED.

By the l2 note (3.1), `m_S(P_L) = gap_S(P_L) + A_S(P_L)` with `gap_S(P_L) -> 0` in `L`. So equally
`lim_R lim_L A_(B_R)(P_L) = C(b) - 1 - beta_1^(2)(Gamma)`: the cost–Betti excess of the Bernoulli shift *is* the
asymptotic attainment defect of well-founded matchings over adapted generating sets.

**Corollary G.** Fix a finite generating set `S`. Then `lim_L m_S(P_L) = 0` iff both

- `Target(Gamma)`: `C(b) = 1 + beta_1^(2)(Gamma)`; and
- `(Q3)(S)`: `C_sub(S) = C(b)`, equivalently `C_sub(S) = lim_R C_sub(B_R(S))`.

Moreover `(Q3)(S)` implies `(Q3)(S')` for every finite generating set `S' ⊇ S`.

*Proof.* By Theorem B(ii), `lim_L m_S(P_L) = C_sub(S) - 1 - beta_1^(2)(Gamma)`. By (1.1) and the identity (I),
`C_sub(S) >= C(b) >= 1 + beta_1^(2)(Gamma)`. The sum of the two nonnegative gaps vanishes iff each does, and
Theorem E with `S_0 = S` identifies `C(b)` with `lim_R C_sub(B_R(S))`. For `S ⊆ S'`,
`C(b) <= C_sub(S') <= C_sub(S) = C(b)`. QED.

**Known instances of `(Q3)(S)`.**

- `Gamma` amenable, every `S`: the forest construction of Section 4.7 of the l2 note uses only that `R_b` is
  hyperfinite and that `Phi_S` generates. It gives `C_sub(S) = 1 = C(b)`.
- `F_r` with a free basis `S` (remark, using the standard value `beta_1^(2)(F_r) = r - 1`): `c(Phi_S) = r`, and
  `C(b) >= 1 + beta_1^(2) = r` by (I). So `C_sub(S) = C(b) = r`. By upward closure `(Q3)` holds at every `S' ⊇ S`.

## 4. Consequences for the Morse route

1. **The Morse reformulation of the cost–Betti target is lossless.** Corollary F shows that the Bernoulli cost excess
   equals the double-limit Morse deficit. So a Morse attack on `Target(Gamma)` may choose its generating set: add
   commuting elements of infinite order, roots of proper powers, or elements of a Kazhdan subgroup. This is what the
   product calibration of the collapse note (Section 4.2) and Gaboriau's commuting-chain criterion do in effect.
2. **The open premise, as stated for all `S`, is `Target` plus S-uniformity.** By Corollary G its surplus over
   `Target` is `(Q3)(S)` for the small generating sets. That surplus is not needed by any route to
   `fpbs-bernoulli-cycle-tail-compactness`: the route `fpbs-bernoulli-cycle-tail-compactness-via-morse-deficit` uses
   the premise only to produce bounded-degree generating graphings of cost tending to `1 + beta_1`, and Corollary F
   produces them from the double limit.
3. **Failure at one `S` is not a counterexample to `Target`.** A group with `lim_L m_S(P_L) > 0` at some `S` refutes
   `Target` only if `(Q3)(S)` holds, and `(Q3)` can only improve as `S` grows.
4. **What the group-rings transplant gives, and where it stops.** On every orbit `d2^R` is a matrix over `Z Gamma`,
   and by the trace convention of the l2 note `rho(R)` is its von Neumann rank over `L Gamma`. So `rho` and `gap` are
   the same for every free action, and all action dependence of the Morse deficit sits in `A(R)`. Ring-theoretic
   tools (Atiyah-type rationality, Linnell's division closure, Lück approximation) compute `rho` but produce pivots
   with coefficients in a division closure. A Morse matching needs `+-1` pivots with no fill-in, ordered well-founded.
   Already for the finite `Z`-matrix `[[1, 1], [1, -1]]` (rank 2) every acyclic `+-1` matching has size 1: matching
   both rows makes each pivot column a child of the other. So rank computations alone cannot supply attainment. Any
   proof of `lim A = 0` must use the infinite orbits, as the sparse columns of 4.1 in the collapse note do.

## 5. Not established

- `(Q3)(S)` for any nonamenable, non-treeable group at a small `S`. Natural tests: `<S | w^m>` at the standard `S`,
  where the proper-power ceiling of the collapse note is sharp, and an infinite Kazhdan group at a two-element `S`.
- Whether `C_sub(S)` is independent of `S` for every group. A group with `C_sub(S) > C_sub(S')` for some `S ⊆ S'`
  would be a new calibration world for Cayley-graph-based cost methods; none is known here.
- `Target(Gamma)` itself for any group not already covered.
