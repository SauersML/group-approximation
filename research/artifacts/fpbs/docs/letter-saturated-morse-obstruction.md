# Letter-saturated Morse matchings die on Bernoulli; balanced ones escape every per-letter sublevel argument

Written 2026-09-18 by swarm worker `swarm-0917-w9-w9-fp-pull` (transplanter, calibration). Written deduction; not
refereed. It extends `research/artifacts/fpbs/docs/single-letter-morse-spectral-obstruction.md` (cited below as
**[SL]**, Sections 1-4) from single-letter matchings at one relator to arbitrary matchings over arbitrary finite
word lists. Notation and (M1)-(M3) are those of `research/artifacts/fpbs/docs/bernoulli-morse-collapse-criterion.md`.

## 0. What this settles

[SL] kills single-letter matchings at one relator and lists three survivors: multi-letter matchings, several cell
types, and longer word lists. This note splits all three survivors by one number, the **letter share**
`mu(r -> s)`, the mass of type-`r` cells matched to `s`-edges.

- **Theorem A (kill).** On Bernoulli, in *every* Morse matching over *every* finite list, every pair `(r, s)` with
  `k_rs >= 2` occurrences and nonamenable transport group `Lambda_rs` has
  `mu(r -> s) <= 1 - (k_rs - 1)(1 - rho_rs)/(6 k_rs^2)`. Other letters, other types and other matches are
  unrestricted. [SL] is the case of one type and one letter, where `mu(r -> s) = mu(M)`.
- **Proposition D (barrier).** For *balanced* matchings, where no pair `(r, s)` is near saturation, the full
  inequality system that the sublevel method produces is satisfiable with zero deficit. So no argument built from
  per-letter height sublevels, a Kesten gap and thick-level counting can go further. The method dies at Lemma 1'.
  There, the straddling mass of letter `s` at level `j` is controlled only by `tau_s(j)`, which contains the other
  letters' removed mass above level `j`. That quantity is the same size as the quantity being controlled.

So after this note, the target `fpbs-morse-deficit-vanishes-for-full-presentations` is untouched only on matchings
that are `c`-balanced in every `(r, s)` with `c = c_rs`. Proving or refuting it on those needs cross-letter
information that no per-letter spectral bound carries.

## 1. Setting

- `Gamma` is generated by a finite set `S` and acts freely, preserving the probability measure `mu` on `X`.
- `R = (w_1, ..., w_m)` is a finite list of trivial words, the cell types. The cells are `(y, w_r)`, `y in X`.
- `M` is any Morse matching for `R` ((M1)-(M3)). Its removed edges may carry any letters, and the choice of edge in
  each cell may be any Borel function of the cell.
- For a type `r` and a letter `s in S` with `s^2 != 1`:
  - `k = k_rs` is the number of occurrences of `s^{+-1}` in `w_r`;
  - `h_a^{(r)}` for those occurrences are the occurrence bases, defined as in [SL] Section 1;
  - `Q_rs = {h_a h_b^{-1} : a != b} \ {1}` and `Lambda_rs = <Q_rs>`;
  - `rho_rs` is the Kesten spectral radius of `(Lambda_rs, Q_rs)`.
- **(H_rs)** means that the `h_a^{(r)}` are pairwise distinct. For a relator of a one-relator group this is
  Weinbaum's theorem ([SL] Section 1).
- **Letter shares.** `mu(r -> s)` is the measure of the type-`r` cells that `M` matches to an `s`-edge. Put
  `eps_rs = 1 - mu(r -> s)` and `delta_r = 1 - sum_s mu(r -> s)`, the unmatched density of type `r`. So
  `mu(M) = sum_{r,s} mu(r -> s)`.

## 2. Global heights and the per-pair lemmas (any free p.m.p. action)

**Heights.** Kept edges (of every letter) have height `0`. A removed edge `x = M(y)` has height
`1 + max ht(children)`, where the children are the removed edges of the cell `y` other than `x`, of *any* letter.
By (M3) and König's lemma (at most `l_r - 1` children), `ht` is finite a.e. and Borel. It has the:

- *top property.* In a matched cell `y`, `ht(M(y)) > ht(e)` for every other edge `e` of `y`, removed or kept.

Fix `(r, s)` with `k = k_rs >= 2` and (H_rs). Let `A_j = A_j^s ⊆ X` be the set of `s`-edges of height `<= j`, and
`n_j = mu(A_j)`. Then:

- `n_0 = mu(kept s-edges) = 1 - sum_{r'} mu(r' -> s) <= eps_rs`;
- `n_j` is nondecreasing, and `n_j -> 1`.

As in [SL] (2.1), each `x in X` is the `a`-th `s`-edge of exactly one type-`r` cell, `h_a^{-1} x`. So the type-`r`
incidences `(y, a)` with `h_a y in A` have measure `k mu(A)`.

**Upper mass.** Put

```text
tau_rs(j) = delta_r + mu{ type-r cells y matched to a non-s edge with ht(M(y)) > j }  <=  eps_rs.
```

**Lemma 1' (straddling).** Let `beta_j` be the measure of type-`r` incidences `(y, a)` with `h_a y in A_j` such that
`y` is not *s-inside* `A_j`, i.e. not all `k` of its `s`-edges lie in `A_j`. Then `beta_j <= k tau_rs(j)`.

*Proof.* Take a type-`r` cell `y` matched to an edge of any letter with `ht(M(y)) <= j`. By the top property every
other edge of `y` has smaller height, so `y` is s-inside `A_j`. Let `I_j` be the measure of such cells, and let
`I_j^s` be the part of `I_j` matched to `s`-edges.

These cells carry `k I_j` incidences into `A_j`, out of the total `k n_j`, so `beta_j <= k (n_j - I_j)`.

The removed `s`-edges of height `<= j` come in two kinds:

- those matched from type-`r` cells, of measure `I_j^s`;
- those matched from other types, of measure at most `sum_{r' != r} mu(r' -> s)`.

Hence

```text
n_j  <=  (1 - sum_{r'} mu(r' -> s)) + sum_{r' != r} mu(r' -> s) + I_j^s  =  1 - mu(r -> s) + I_j^s,
n_j - I_j  <=  1 - mu(r -> s) - (I_j - I_j^s)  =  delta_r + (mu(r -> not s) - (I_j - I_j^s))  =  tau_rs(j).
```

The last step uses `1 - mu(r -> s) = delta_r + mu(r -> not s)`, where `mu(r -> not s)` is the mass of type-`r` cells
matched to non-`s` edges and `I_j - I_j^s` is its part of height `<= j`. ∎

**Lemma 2' (almost invariance).** For `q in Q_rs` and every `j`, `mu(A_j Δ q A_j) <= 2 k tau_rs(j) <= 2 k eps_rs`.

*Proof.* This is [SL] Lemma 2 verbatim, with type-`r` cells and Lemma 1' in place of Lemma 1. Let `q = h_a h_b^{-1}`.
For `x in A_j` with `q x notin A_j`, the type-`r` cell `h_b^{-1} x` gives a straddling incidence `(h_b^{-1} x, b)`,
and distinct `x` give distinct incidences. This bounds `mu(A_j \ q^{-1} A_j)`, and `Q_rs` is symmetric. ∎

**Lemma 3' (thick level).** For every `J >= 0`,

```text
(k - 1) mu{ x : ht(x) = J }  <=  k ( tau_rs(J) + (1 - n_J) )  <=  k ( eps_rs + (1 - n_J) ).
```

*Proof.* Fix an `s`-edge `x` of height `J`. By (H_rs) it has `k` type-`r` incidences `(y', a)` with distinct cells
`y' = h_a^{-1} x`. At most one cell in all is matched to `x`. Take one of the others. There are three cases.

1. `y'` is unmatched. This is counted in `delta_r`.
2. `y'` is matched to a non-`s` edge `e`. Then `x` is another edge of `y'`, so `ht(e) > J` by the top property
   (if `J = 0` then `ht(e) >= 1`). This is counted in `tau_rs(J)`.
3. `y'` is matched to an `s`-edge `e != x`. Then `ht(e) > J`, so `e in E_s \ A_J`. Such cells inject, preserving
   measure, into a set of measure `<= 1 - n_J`.

Each cell carries at most `k` of the incidences. ∎

**Lemma 4** is [SL] Lemma 4 ((3.1)) applied to the finitely generated subgroup `Lambda_rs` with the symmetric set
`Q_rs`. It holds on the Bernoulli shift and on every free factor of it. It rests on
`kesten-amenable-stabilizer-uniform-gap`.

## 3. Theorem A: the letter-share bound

**Theorem A.** Let `Gamma ↷ X` be the Bernoulli shift or a free factor of it. Let `M` be any Morse matching for any
finite list `R`, and let `(r, s)` satisfy `s^2 != 1`, `k = k_rs >= 2` and (H_rs). Then

```text
eps_rs = 1 - mu(r -> s)  >=  c_rs := (k - 1)(1 - rho_rs) / (6 k^2).
```

*Proof.* This is [SL] Section 4 word for word, with `delta` replaced by `eps_rs`, Lemmas 1'-3' for Lemmas 1-3, and
`Lambda_rs` for `Lambda_s`. The only inputs [SL] uses are:

1. `mu(A_j Δ q A_j) <= 2 k delta` (here Lemma 2', with `eps_rs`);
2. `n_0 <= delta` (here `n_0 <= eps_rs`, Section 2);
3. `n_j -> 1`;
4. (2.2) (here Lemma 3', with `eps_rs`);
5. (3.1).

Explicitly:

- Lemma 4 gives `n_j (1 - n_j) <= theta_0 := k eps_rs / (1 - rho_rs)`.
- If `theta_0 >= 1/4`, then `eps_rs >= (1 - rho)/(4k) >= c_rs`.
- Otherwise every `n_j` lies in `[0, theta] ∪ [1 - theta, 1]`, where `theta = 2 theta_0`. Since
  `n_0 <= eps_rs <= theta`, there is a level `J` with `mu{ht = J} >= 1 - 2 theta`.
- Lemma 3' then gives `(k - 1)(1 - 2 theta) <= k(theta + eps_rs)`, and so `eps_rs >= c_rs`. ∎

**Corollary B (letter-pure matchings).** Suppose each type `r` sends all of its matched cells to one letter
`sigma(r)`, with arbitrary Borel positions among the occurrences of that letter. Then

```text
mu(M)  <=  sum_r (1 - c_{r, sigma(r)}).
```

A letter-pure family can reach the Morse ceiling `z` only if `sum_r (1 - c_{r,sigma(r)}) >= z`.

- At `m = 1`, `z = 1` this is [SL].
- For a fixed list with `m` types of which `m'` have `c_{r,sigma(r)} >= c > 0`, the deficit is at least
  `z - m + m' c` whenever that is positive.

**Corollary C (one relator, any letters).** Let `<S | w>` be torsion-free one-relator, so the ceiling at `{w}` is
`1` ([SL] Section 1). Any family `M_n` of matchings for `{w}` with `mu(M_n) -> 1` satisfies

```text
limsup_n mu(n-th matching -> s)  <=  1 - c_s     for every letter s used k_s >= 2 times,
```

so at least `c_s` of the cell mass is eventually matched away from `s`.

- For `abaBabcdcDcd` (all four `Lambda_s` nonamenable, [SL] Section 5), every ceiling-reaching family is spread over
  at least two letters. No letter carries more than `1 - min_s c_s`.
- For `abacad` the letter `a` is capped and `b`, `c`, `d` (`k = 1`) are not, consistent with the single-letter
  ceiling at `b`.

**Scope of the kill.** Theorem A is uniform over positions, word lists, other letters, and the rest of the
matching. It kills every Morse construction that needs some cell type to be almost entirely matched to one letter
used `>= 3` times with nonamenable transport. This covers:

- the dual-forest and Borel-peeling constructions of `fpbs-one-relator-twice-used-letter-fixed-price`, transplanted
  to `k >= 3`, with helper matchings at other letters or other types added;
- "one main letter plus small corrections" schemes at a fixed relator;
- every scheme at a fixed list in which a type's matches concentrate on one letter as the deficit goes to `0`.

*Invariant:* `1 - rho(Lambda_rs, Q_rs)`, which bounds the letter share `mu(r -> s)`.

*Step where it dies:* (M3), through the top property. As in [SL], the gap forces a thick level, and Lemma 3'
forbids it.

## 4. Proposition D: balanced matchings escape every per-letter sublevel argument

Take one relator `w` (`m = 1`), and let `T` be the set of letters that `M` uses. For `s in T` put:

- `r_s = mu(1 -> s)`, the share of `s`, so that `sum_{s in T} r_s = 1 - delta`;
- `u_j^s = 1 - n_j^s`, the removed `s`-mass of height `> j`.

Because `M` is a measure-preserving bijection from matched cells to removed edges, at one relator

```text
tau_s(j) = delta + sum_{t in T, t != s} u_j^t.
```

**The sublevel system `Sigma`.** Everything Sections 2-3 extract, for each `s in T` and each level:

- **(I1)** `u_0^s = r_s`. Each `u_j^s` is nonincreasing and tends to `0`.
- **(I2)** `(1 - u_j^s) u_j^s <= k_s tau_s(j) / (1 - rho_s)`. This is Lemma 2' together with Lemma 4.
- **(I3)** `(k_s - 1) mu{ht_s = J} <= k_s (tau_s(J) + u_J^s)`, where `mu{ht_s = 0} = 1 - r_s` and
  `mu{ht_s = J} = u_{J-1}^s - u_J^s` for `J >= 1`. This is Lemma 3'.
- **(I4)** For `|S| = 2`, the height-tail inequality of `fpbs-near-optimal-morse-matchings-have-non-tight-heights`:
  `2 sum_s u_H^s >= h - d delta |B_G((l - 1)^H)|` for every `H`.

**Proposition D.** Let `|T| >= 2`, `k = max_s k_s`, and let `rho_s in [0, 1)` be arbitrary. For `delta = 0` and equal
shares `r_s = 1/|T|`, the lockstep profile

```text
u_j^s = lambda^j / |T|        (every s in T)
```

satisfies (I1)-(I3) if and only if `lambda >= lambda_min = (k - 1)/(k - 1 + k|T|)`, for every choice of the `rho_s`.
Suppose (I4) is satisfiable at all, which forces `h <= 2`. Then the family `lambda_n -> 1` satisfies (I4) in the
limit form `liminf_n sum_s u_H^s >= h/2` for every `H`.

*Proof.* Put `u = u_j^s`. Then `tau_s(j) = (|T| - 1) u >= u`.

- **(I2).** `(1 - u) u <= u <= k tau / (1 - rho)`, since `k >= 1` and `1 - rho <= 1`.
- **(I3) at `J = 0`.** The inequality is `(k - 1)(1 - 1/|T|) <= k (tau(0) + u_0) = k`. This holds.
- **(I3) at `J >= 1`.** Both sides scale by `lambda^{J-1}`. The inequality becomes
  `(k - 1)(1 - lambda)/|T| <= k lambda`, which holds iff `lambda >= lambda_min`. This is sharp at `J = 1`.
- **(I4).** `sum_s u_H^s = lambda_n^H -> 1 >= h/2`. ∎

`experiments/letter-saturated-morse-2026-09-17/lockstep_barrier.py` checks this in exact arithmetic. It covers
`|T| in {2, 3, 4, 6}`, `k = 2..10`, four values of `rho` and three values of `lambda` including `lambda_min`, over the
first 60 levels. It also checks the sharpness failure of (I3) at `J = 1` just below `lambda_min`, and exits 0.

**Where the method dies (the barrier).** At Lemma 1'. The straddling mass of letter `s` at level `j` is bounded by
`k tau_s(j)`, and `tau_s(j)` contains the other letters' removed mass above `j`. In a balanced matching that mass is
at least a constant times `u_j^s`. So (I2) is implied by the trivial `u(1 - u) <= u`, and the Kesten gap forces no
thick level.

- In [SL] and in Theorem A, `tau` is instead bounded by `eps` *uniformly in `j`*, including the level where `n_j` is
  near `1/2`. That uniform bound is exactly what saturation (`eps_rs` small) buys.
- *Remark (heuristic, not part of Proposition D).* Taking the Kesten gap of all letters at once does not obviously
  help. Let `A_j` be the joint sublevel in the edge space
  `⊔_s X`, and let the cross-letter transport be `x -> h_a h_b^{-1} x` between occurrences of different letters.
  The straddling mass of `A_j` is bounded only by `l (1 - I_j) = l (delta + sum_s u_j^s)`, while the variance of
  `1_{A_j}` is at most `sum_s u_j^s (1 - u_j^s)` plus a between-sheet term that is zero for equal shares. The same
  self-bounding holds.

So `Sigma`, together with (I4), is consistent with zero deficit for every balanced share vector. Any proof that
balanced multi-letter matchings die on Bernoulli must use information outside `Sigma`: the *joint* law of the height
vector of a cell across letters, not its per-letter marginals.

Conversely, Proposition D is not a construction. It does not say that balanced matchings reaching the ceiling
exist.

## 5. Consequences for the target over growing lists `P_L`

**Theorem A is vacuous in the regime where the target lives.** Over `P_L` the number of types `m_L` grows, while the
ceiling `z = |S| - 1 - beta_1` stays fixed. A ceiling-reaching matching needs total share `z`. It can spread that
share as `z/m_L -> 0` per type, far below every cap `1 - c_rs`.

The same holds for letter-pure matchings: Corollary B's cap `sum_r (1 - c_r) >= m_L min_r (1 - c_r)` exceeds `z` as
soon as `m_L > z / (1 - max c)`.

So the Morse node is not touched by any per-pair spectral bound, even for letter-pure matchings. The whole content of
`fpbs-morse-deficit-vanishes-for-full-presentations` sits in the region where each type is lightly used. That is the
region of the recovery-height tail (`fpbs-near-optimal-morse-matchings-have-non-tight-heights`) and of Theorem B.

**Decomposition of what remains at a fixed list.** For a fixed finite list `R` on Bernoulli, a ceiling-reaching family
must be:

1. `c`-*unsaturated*: `mu(r -> s) <= 1 - c_rs` for all `(r, s)`. This is Theorem A, and it is ESTABLISHED.
2. *height-non-tight*: `liminf nu(E^{>H}) >= h/2` for every `H`, when `beta_1 = 0`. This is
   `fpbs-near-optimal-morse-matchings-have-non-tight-heights`, and it is ESTABLISHED.
3. *jointly balanced across letters*, in some sense that `Sigma` cannot see (Proposition D). This is OPEN. The first
   test case is `w = abaBabcdcDcd`, where the group has fixed price by Gaboriau's amalgam theorem.

The question "does `m({w}) = 0` for `abaBabcdcDcd`?" is now sharp. It is exactly the question whether a balanced
multi-letter Borel peeling exists on Bernoulli, and no per-letter spectral method can answer it.

## 6. Calibrations

- **`k_rs = 2`.** `Lambda_rs` is cyclic and `rho = 1`, so `c_rs = 0`. There is no cap, consistent with the dual-forest
  theorem.
- **Amenable `Gamma`.** `rho = 1` for every pair, so there is no cap, consistent with fixed price 1.
- **[SL] recovered.** With `m = 1` and `T = {s}`: `eps_s = delta`, `tau_s(j) = delta` for all `j`, and Theorem A is
  [SL] Theorem 4.
- **Single letter in `Sigma`.** At `|T| = 1`, (I2) reads `(1 - u) u <= k delta / (1 - rho)`, with no `u` on the right.
  The lockstep family does not exist, and [SL] applies. The barrier appears exactly at `|T| = 2`.
- **Two letters, unequal shares `(1 - eta, eta)`.** Theorem A caps `1 - eta <= 1 - c_s`, i.e. `eta >= c_s`. For
  `eta + delta < c_s`, the system `Sigma` is infeasible, because Theorem A uses only (I1)-(I3) with `tau <= eps`. For
  `c_s <= eta < 1/2`, feasibility of `Sigma` is not settled here. Proposition D covers only equal shares.
