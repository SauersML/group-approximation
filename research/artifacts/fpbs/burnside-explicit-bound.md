# An explicit action-wise upper bound from torsion repair and cycle pruning

Research continuation — September 9, 2026

## Status

This note gives a quantitative upper bound for every essentially free p.m.p. action of sufficiently large odd-exponent free Burnside groups and their infinite subgroups. It improves the elementary two-generator bound `2-2/n`. The new ingredient in this continuation is a fully accounted union-and-pruning estimate: selected and repaired domains are charged only once, and a redundant edge is then removed from every fully retained finite generator cycle.

No novelty, independent referee approval, or formal verification is asserted. This is not a proof of either the universal Fixed Price problem or the universal Benjamini–Schramm conjecture. In particular, the bound below remains close to two, not one. The finite replay checks only finite repaired connectivity, probability identities, and arithmetic. Earlier broad subgroup theorems from the conversation are NOT premises; the narrower subgroup comparison used here is proved again.

## 1. Conventions and external inputs

All actions are essentially free, probability-measure-preserving actions of countable groups on standard probability spaces. Work on a conull free invariant set and write actions on the right, `x -> xg`. For `H <= G`, let `R_H` denote the orbit relation of the restriction. A graphing has cost equal to the sum of the measures of its partial-bijection domains. Its relation has cost equal to the infimum of generating graphing costs.

We import:

1. Every free action of an infinite group has cost at least one [G].
2. For odd `n >= 1003` and finite `m >= 2`, `B(m,n)` is infinite, every nonidentity centralizer is cyclic of order `n`, and every noncyclic subgroup contains a copy of `B(infinity,n)`, hence a copy of `B(2,n)` [DS, Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 2.5]. The numerical threshold is a conservative common range for these inputs.
3. The standard generators of `B(2,n)` and each `a b^i` have order exactly `n`: the exponent gives the upper bound, and the images `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(1,i)` in the abelianization `(Z/nZ)^2` give the lower bound.

The finite-group graphing upper bound is elementary and is proved below. Bernoulli maximality [AW] is mentioned only when describing the unresolved target, not in the proof of the action-wise numerical bound.

## 2. Finite union-and-pruning lemma

### Theorem 2.1

Let `G=<H,a>`, where `H` is a finite subgroup of order `h` and `a` has finite order `m >= 2`. Suppose there are `b_1,...,b_k` in `H` such that

    c_i = a b_i,  d_i = ord(c_i) >= 2,
    P_i = {c_i,...,c_i^(d_i-1)}

are pairwise disjoint. Then every free p.m.p. action alpha satisfies, for every `0<q<1`,

    C(alpha) <= 1-1/h + q
                + (1-q) product_i(1-q^(d_i-1)) - q^m/m.       (2.1)

All quantities on the right are deterministic. This is an upper bound, not an exact action-cost formula.

### Proof

**Finite measurable approximation of the needed independent labels.**
Let

    F = {e} union P_1 union ... union P_k
        union {a,a^2,...,a^(m-1)}.

For any eta>0 choose a finite measurable partition separating the distinct points `xg`, `g in F`, outside a set of roots of measure below eta. To see this, take refining finite partitions separating points in a standard Borel model. Freeness ensures `xg != xh` almost surely for `g != h`. The collision event for each of the finitely many pairs decreases to a null set.

Temporarily color the partition atoms independently with probability q, and let U be their selected union. Define

    D(U) = {x: for every i, at least one point of xP_i is outside U},
    A(U) = U union D(U).

At a root without a tested collision, the root color is independent of all tested detour colors, since `e` is in none of the `P_i`. The detour supports are pairwise disjoint. Thus

    P_colors(x in A(U))
      = q + (1-q) product_i(1-q^(d_i-1)).                      (2.2)

After integrating over x, the same upper bound has an additional error at most eta.

**Every generator connection is recovered.**
Add the single partial transformation `T_a|A(U)`. If x is in D(U), the direct a-edge is present. Otherwise a successful i gives a path from xa back to x:

    xa --b_i--> xc_i --a--> xc_i a --b_i--> xc_i^2
       --a--> ... --b_i--> xc_i^d_i = x.

All b_i steps belong to R_H. All a-steps in this path start in U, hence in A(U). Therefore R_H together with `T_a|A(U)` generates R_G.

**Prune complete a-cycles without changing any connections.**
Put

    E_A = {x: x a^j is in A(U) for all 0<=j<m}.

This is a-invariant. Choose a measurable transversal B_A for the free finite cyclic action on E_A. Its measure is `mu(E_A)/m`. Delete the a-edge starting at each point of B_A. Each deleted edge had all the other m-1 a-edges around its cycle retained. Its endpoints therefore remain connected. The subrelation generated by `T_a|A(U)` is unchanged.

The cost of the pruned a-graphing is exactly

    mu(A(U)) - mu(E_A)/m.                                    (2.3)

For m=2, the two possible starting points describe the same unoriented edge in opposite directions; pruning one of them preserves that edge and the same formula is valid.

If the whole a-orbit of x was selected in U, it is contained in A(U). Define E_U analogously. Outside the same partition-collision exception, all m points of that a-orbit have independent colors. Hence

    E_colors mu(E_A) >= E_colors mu(E_U) >= q^m - eta.

Combining with (2.2),

    E_colors[mu(A(U))-mu(E_A)/m]
      <= q+(1-q) product_i(1-q^(d_i-1))-q^m/m
         +(1+1/m)eta.                                        (2.4)

Averaging over the finite auxiliary coloring space supplies a deterministic measurable U attaining this upper bound on the TOTAL pruned cost. No independent extension of the original action has been substituted.

Finally, choose a measurable transversal for the H-action. Joining its representative to the other h-1 vertices of each H-orbit gives a generating graphing of cost `1-1/h`. Add the pruned a-graphing. The union still generates R_G. Let eta decrease to zero in the cost infimum. This proves (2.1). QED.

### Quantifier and accounting checks

- The sum of selected and repaired domains is improved to the measure of their UNION. The formula uses independence of the root label from the detour labels, not independence of the random sets as subsets of X.
- Pruning happens AFTER all failures are repaired. It preserves the relation generated by the retained a-edges, so it cannot invalidate the detour proof.
- The lower estimate for the fully retained a-orbits uses only E_U, not an unproved independence assumption for the much more dependent set A(U).
- Graphing cost counts the H-connections once. There is no additional multiplication by the lengths of their realizations in an H-graphing.
- eta goes to zero only after an actual generating graphing has been constructed for every eta. No connectivity of a limiting sparse edge set is asserted.

## 3. A finite family of n disjoint cycles in B(2,n)

Let `G=B(2,n)=<a,b>`, with n in the range of Section 1. Put `H=<b>` and, for `0<=i<n`,

    c_i = a b^i,
    P_i = {c_i,c_i^2,...,c_i^(n-1)}.

### Lemma 3.1

These n sets are pairwise disjoint.

**Proof.** Suppose a nonidentity w belongs to P_i and P_j. Then c_i and c_j lie in the cyclic centralizer C_G(w), so they commute. If i differs from j, their quotient

    c_i^(-1)c_j = b^(j-i)

is nonidentity and commutes with c_i. But `<b>` has order n and is contained in the order-n centralizer of `b^(j-i)`, so

    C_G(b^(j-i)) = <b>.

It follows that c_i lies in `<b>`, hence that a does too. This contradicts the independent standard-generator images in `(Z/nZ)^2`. Therefore i=j. Within each P_i, the entries are distinct because c_i has order n. QED.

## 4. Explicit upper bound for every free action

Define, for n>=3 and 0<=q<=1,

    B_n(q) = 1-1/n + q
             + (1-q)(1-q^(n-1))^n - q^n/n,
    U_n = min_{0<=q<=1} B_n(q).                              (4.1)

The endpoints are included by continuity, and the minimum exists on a compact interval.

### Theorem 4.1

For odd n>=1003, every free p.m.p. action alpha of B(2,n) satisfies

    1 <= C(alpha) <= U_n < 2-2/n.                            (4.2)

**Proof.** The lower bound is the infinite-action cost bound. Apply Theorem 2.1 with `h=m=k=n` and every `d_i=n`, using Lemma 3.1. This proves the upper bound for each q and hence for U_n. To see the strict improvement, set `q=1-t`. For fixed n>=3,

    B_n(1-t) = 2-2/n - (n-1)t^2/2 + O_n(t^3)

as t decreases to zero: the failure term is `O_n(t^(n+1))`, whereas pruning contributes the displayed quadratic saving. Every sufficiently small positive t gives a strict reduction. QED.

### Completely explicit parameter without numerical optimization

Let

    q_n = (log(n)/n)^(1/(n-1)).

Then `(1-q_n^(n-1))^n = (1-log(n)/n)^n <= 1/n`, so

    C(alpha) <= 1+(1-1/n)q_n - q_n^n/n.                      (4.3)

All logarithms are natural.

## 5. Extension to infinite subgroups and all finite ranks

For completeness the precise subgroup comparison needed here is proved again. No earlier bounded-exponent theorem is assumed.

### Lemma 5.1

Suppose a countable group G has abelian nonidentity centralizers of uniformly bounded finite sizes. For every infinite H<=G and every free p.m.p. action,

    relC(R_G;R_H)=0,
    C(alpha) <= C(alpha|H).                                  (5.1)

**Proof.** If nontrivial centralizers intersect nontrivially, choose a nonidentity point w in their intersection. The corresponding centralizer C_G(w) is abelian, which implies the two original elements commute. Their abelian centralizers then contain one another, so are equal.

Fix a outside H. The infinite coset aH meets infinitely many distinct centralizers. Choose c_i=a b_i, b_i in H, in distinct such centralizers. Every c_i has order bounded by the uniform centralizer-size bound, and the nonidentity power sets are pairwise disjoint.

Use finite measurable atom-coloring and paid torsion repair, as in Section 2 without the finite-H cost computation or cyclic-pruning term. With k cycles and upper order bound N, the additional cost is at most

    q+(1-q)(1-q^(N-1))^k + eta.

First choose q small, then finite k large, then eta small. Thus adjoining a costs arbitrarily little relative to R_H. Enumerate G and allocate summable positive error budgets to all needed a-extensions. Their union, together with R_H, generates R_G. Adding them to a nearly optimal H-graphing proves (5.1). QED.

### Corollary 5.2

For odd n>=1003, finite m>=2, and every infinite subgroup H<=B(m,n),

    Cmax(H) <= U_n.                                          (5.2)

Here Cmax is the supremum over free-action costs, not the infimum.

**Proof.** The centralizer condition passes to subgroups, with the same size upper bound n. The external subgroup theorem gives J<=H isomorphic to B(2,n), since H cannot be cyclic and infinite in an exponent-n group. Restrict any given H-action to J. Its cost is at most U_n by Theorem 4.1. Lemma 5.1 extends this bound back to H. QED.

The corollary includes infinitely generated H. It makes no assertion that every action of H attains the same cost.

## 6. What the gain does asymptotically

The improvement over the coarse upper bound is logarithmic on the 1/n scale:

    lim_{n->infinity} n(2-U_n)/log n = 1.                     (6.1)

This is a statement about the explicit bound U_n, NOT about any action's actual cost.

**Proof.** Write t=1-q and

    s_n(q)=(1-q)[1-(1-q^(n-1))^n].

Then

    B_n(q)=2-1/n-s_n(q)-q^n/n.

Use `1-(1-z)^n<=min(1,nz)` and `q^(n-1)<=exp(-(n-1)t)`. With `t_0=log(n)/(n-1)`, if t<=t_0 then s_n<=t_0. If t>=t_0, the function `nt exp(-(n-1)t)` is decreasing there for n>=3, and its value at t_0 is t_0. Therefore

    s_n(q) <= log(n)/(n-1)

uniformly in q, yielding

    2-U_n <= 2/n + log(n)/(n-1).                              (6.2)

For a reverse asymptotic bound, use q_n from Section 4. Since

    -log q_n = (log n-log log n)/(n-1),

we have `n(1-q_n)/log n -> 1`, while the detour failure probability is at most 1/n. Hence

    2-U_n >= 1/n+(1-q_n)(1-1/n),

which proves (6.1). QED.

In particular, U_n tends to TWO, not one. Increasing the exponent does not close the Fixed Price gap. Equation (6.2) also proves that optimizing only this explicit one-stage certificate cannot make its bound approach one for large n.

## 7. Why this does not provide a universal solution or a self-improving iteration

The published theorem [DS] gives infimal group cost one for these Burnside groups and explicitly does not assert fixed price one. The present conclusion is an upper bound on EVERY free action, but it is still greater than one.

The reconstruction depends on all H-connections being available. After we thin and repair the a-edges, there is no proved statement that the resulting partial graphing admits another family of independent certificates with the same cost advantage. Reapplying the same upper-bound construction therefore gives the SAME upper bound; it does not multiply excess cost by a factor below one.

The high-capacity networks required in the preceding cut-repair note are still unconstructed in the Burnside action. Counting these n disjoint torsion cycles is not a substitute for a capacity that diverges at a fixed exponent. Nor does an upper bound below two imply fixed price, equal action costs, or a percolation nonuniqueness interval.

An affirmative fixed-price-one proof for B(2,n) would still need Bernoulli cost one; Bernoulli maximality [AW] would then transfer that value to all its free actions. No such conclusion is claimed here. Both original universal goals remain OPEN.

## 8. Replay and audit scope

`replay.py` exhaustively checks every vertex labeling in the regular S_3 and A_4 actions (64 and 4096 labelings). These are finite models of the hypotheses of Theorem 2.1, not finite approximations of B(2,1003). It checks reconstructed connectivity after cyclic pruning, the exact union probability, and the subtraction for all-selected cycles at three rational probabilities. It also checks the outward-rounded interval evaluator against 234 exact small rational formulas, and evaluates four large-n formulas at specified rational q.

The run reports 12,732 exact assertions and 4,160 exhaustive labelings. These figures describe finite tests only. The real-action measurable argument, cost infima, Burnside algebra, and the universal conjectures are not formally verified by that replay.
