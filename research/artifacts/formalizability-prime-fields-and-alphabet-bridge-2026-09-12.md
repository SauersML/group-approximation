# Formalizability over prime fields, stabilization, and the alphabet bridge

Lane `gk-fz-alphabets` (family F, lead lane gk-free-neg), 2026-09-12. Supports the Cairn claims
`formalizable-pairs-induce-polynomial-automorphisms-on-constants`,
`tame-maps-induce-every-permutation-of-prime-field-cubes`,
`sitewise-pairs-over-prime-fields-are-stably-formalizable`,
`formalizable-strict-pairs-certify-non-linear-soficity`,
`prime-field-left-inverse-pairs-are-stably-formalizable`,
`stable-finiteness-forces-prime-power-surjunctivity`,
`strict-rule-pairs-ascend-to-larger-alphabets`,
`every-group-is-surjunctive-over-binary-power-alphabets` and
`linear-sofic-groups-are-surjunctive`, with their routes.

## 0. Summary

The binary reduction (`binary-left-inverse-pairs-are-formalizable` plus Kaplansky over `F_2` gives
Gottschalk over alphabets of size `2^n`) is extended here as follows.

1. **Odd primes need stabilization.** Any formalizable pair induces a polynomial automorphism on
   constant configurations (Section 2). Over one track `F_q` with `q >= 4`, every non-affine
   sitewise permutation is therefore a bijective pair with no formalizing representatives, on every
   group. So the naive analogue of the binary claim is false for `F_p`, `p >= 5`.
2. **One identity track repairs sitewise pairs.** Formally invertible polynomial maps of
   `F_p^n`, `n >= 2`, induce every permutation of `F_p^n` (Section 3, self-contained proof). Every
   sitewise pair over a prime field is formalizable after adding one identity track (Section 4).
3. **The Bennett form works in every characteristic** (Section 5), so the open step over `F_p` is the
   same ideal-solvability problem as over `F_2`, inside `(X_h^p - X_h)`.
4. **The reduction over `F_p`** (Section 6): stable formalizability over `F_p` plus stable finiteness
   of `F_p[G]` gives surjunctivity over every alphabet of size `p^n`.
5. **Non-prime-power alphabets need an alphabet bridge** (Section 7). Product closure only reaches
   multiples, and no multiple of a non-prime-power is a prime power. The bridge is either descent to
   two symbols or ascent to every larger alphabet; ascent is recorded here as an open table-level
   claim, with the failed constructions.
6. **Consequences** (Section 8): a formalizable strict pair certifies non-linear-soficity (established);
   conditionally, every group linear sofic over some prime field is surjunctive, a genuine extension
   of Gromov–Weiss once a linear sofic nonsofic group exists.

## 1. Setting

- `q = p^m`. An automaton on `(F_q^n)^G` has a finite memory and local rules `mu_i: (F_q^n)^M -> F_q`.
- A **representative** of a local rule is a polynomial in variables `X_(h,i)`, `h` in a finite subset
  of `G` (possibly larger than the memory), whose function is the rule. Two representatives differ by
  an element of the ideal generated by `X_(h,i)^q - X_(h,i)`.
- A pair `(tau, sigma)` with `sigma o tau = id` is **formalizable** if some equivariant representatives
  satisfy `sigma* o tau* = id` as a polynomial identity. Products of memory addresses are computed in
  `G`; `x^q = x` may not be used.
- `(tau, sigma)` is **stably formalizable** if `(tau (+) id_k, sigma (+) id_k)` on `(F_q^(n+k))^G` is
  formalizable for some `k >= 0`, where `id_k` acts as the identity on `k` extra tracks. Strictness and
  the left-inverse identity are unchanged by stabilization.
- Theorem 1 of `research/artifacts/formal-polynomial-strict-pairs-2026-09-12.md`
  (`formal-polynomial-strict-pairs-need-unstable-linearization`, verified): a formalizable pair on
  `(k^n)^G` with `M_n(k[G])` directly finite is bijective.

## 2. Diagonal specialization

**Proposition 2.1.** Let `(tau, sigma)` be formalizable on `(F_q^n)^G`, with equivariant representatives
`P = (P_i)` of `tau` and `Q = (Q_i)` of `sigma`. Put `p(T) = P(T, ..., T)` and `q(U) = Q(U, ..., U)`,
polynomial maps `F_q[T_1..T_n]`-valued, where every variable `X_(h,i)` is replaced by `T_i`. Then `p` is
a polynomial automorphism of affine `n`-space over `F_q`, with inverse `q`. Its induced map on `F_q^n`
is the map `c -> tau(c)(1)` on constant configurations.

*Proof.*
1. `epsilon: F_q[X_(h,i) : h in G, i] -> F_q[T_1..T_n]`, `X_(h,i) -> T_i`, is a ring homomorphism.
2. By equivariance `tau*(X_(g,i)) = P_i((X_(gm,j))_(m,j))`, so `epsilon(tau*(X_(g,i))) = p_i(T)` for
   every `g`.
3. The formal identity reads `Q_i((tau*(X_(s,j)))_(s,j)) = X_(1,i)`. Applying `epsilon` gives
   `q_i(p(T)) = T_i`. So `q o p = id` as polynomial maps.
4. Let `p*` be the endomorphism `f -> f o p` of `F_q[T]`. Then `p* o q* = (q o p)* = id`, so `p*` is
   onto. A surjective endomorphism of a Noetherian ring is injective (the kernels of its powers form an
   ascending chain), so `p*` is an automorphism and `p` is a polynomial automorphism with inverse `q`.
5. At a constant configuration `c`, every variable takes the value `c_i`, so `p(c) = mu(c, ..., c)`,
   which is `tau(c)(1)`. QED

**Corollary 2.2 (one track).** For `n = 1` every polynomial automorphism of `F_q[T]` is affine, so a
formalizable pair over `F_q` acts on constant configurations by `t -> a t + b`, `a != 0`.

**Corollary 2.3.** For `q >= 4` take any non-affine permutation `phi` of `F_q`. It exists, since
`|AGL(1,q)| = q(q-1) < q!`. The sitewise pair `tau = phi`, `sigma = phi^-1` (memory `{1}`) is
bijective and has **no** formalizing representatives, on every group, even allowing larger memories.
Over prime fields this happens exactly for `p >= 5`.

*Scope.* This does not contradict `binary-left-inverse-pairs-are-formalizable`, which is over `F_2^n`,
where every permutation is affine-tame (Section 3). It shows that the identification of the alphabet
with `F_q^n` matters: `F_4` as one track has non-formalizable sitewise pairs, and `F_2^2` does not.

## 3. Formally invertible maps induce every permutation of `F_p^n`, `n >= 2`

**Elementary maps.** For a coordinate `i`, a constant `c` in `F_p^x` and a polynomial `f` in the other
coordinates, `E(x)_i = c x_i + f(x_(j != i))`, other coordinates fixed. Its formal inverse is
`x_i -> c^-1 (x_i - f)`. Let `T_n <= Sym(F_p^n)` be the group of permutations induced by composites of
elementary maps.

**Proposition 3.1.**
- `T_n = Sym(F_p^n)` for every prime `p` and `n >= 2`.
- `T_1 = AGL(1, p)`, which equals `Sym(F_p)` iff `p <= 3`.

**Lemma 3.2 (commutator of cycles meeting once).** Let `alpha = (u a_2 ... a_r)` and `beta = (u b_2 ... b_s)`
be cycles whose supports meet only in `u`. Then `alpha beta alpha^-1 beta^-1 = (u a_2 b_2)`
(composition right to left).

*Proof.* Points outside both supports are fixed. Direct evaluation: `b_2 -> u` (via `beta^-1` then
`alpha^-1` to `a_r`, fixed by `beta`, then `alpha` to `u`); `u -> a_2`; `a_2 -> b_2`. Every other
`a_k` and `b_k` is fixed, because the conjugations move it back along its own cycle. QED

**Lemma 3.3 (connected 3-cycles generate the alternating group).** Let `H <= Sym(Omega)` contain a
set `C` of 3-cycles whose supports connect `Omega` (the graph with edges between points moved by a
common element of `C` is connected), `|Omega| >= 3`. Then `Alt(Omega) <= H`.

*Proof.* Let `S` be a maximal subset with `Alt(S) <= H`; the support of one element of `C` qualifies.
If `S != Omega`, connectivity gives `gamma` in `C` with support meeting `S` and not inside `S`.
- **Case A**, `gamma = (s s' x)` with `s, s'` in `S` and `x` not in `S`. Conjugating by `Alt(S)` gives all
  3-cycles `(t t' x)` with `t != t'` in `S`. For `|S| >= 4` this uses 2-transitivity of `Alt(S)`; for
  `|S| = 3` the rotation of `S` and inverses suffice. Together with `Alt(S)` these are all 3-cycles on
  `S ∪ {x}`, so `Alt(S ∪ {x}) <= H`.
- **Case B**, `gamma = (s x y)` with `x, y` not in `S`. Take `delta = (s s' s'')` in `Alt(S)`. By
  Lemma 3.2 the commutator `[gamma, delta]` is the 3-cycle `(s x s')`, which is Case A, so
  `Alt(S ∪ {x}) <= H`. Then `gamma` is Case A for `S ∪ {x}`, so `Alt(S ∪ {x, y}) <= H`.

Both cases contradict maximality. QED

*Proof of 3.1.*
1. **Line cycles.** For coordinate `i` and `b` in `F_p^(n-1)`, let `delta_b(x_(j != i)) = prod_(j != i) (1 - (x_j - b_j)^(p-1))`,
   the indicator of `b`. The elementary map `x_i -> x_i + delta_b` induces the `p`-cycle translating the
   line `l_(i,b) = {x : x_(j != i) = b}` by `+1`, and fixes everything else.
2. **`p = 2`.** Line cycles are the transpositions along edges of the hypercube, which is connected, so
   they generate `Sym(F_2^n)`.
3. **`p` odd, `n >= 2`.** For a point `u` and coordinates `i != j`, the lines through `u` along `i` and
   along `j` meet only in `u`. By Lemma 3.2 their line cycles give the 3-cycle `(u, u + e_i, u + e_j)` in
   `T_n`. These supports connect `F_p^n`, so `Alt(F_p^n) <= T_n` by Lemma 3.3.
4. **An odd permutation.** For a generator `g` of `F_p^x`, the map `x_1 -> g x_1` fixes `x_1 = 0` and
   is a `(p-1)`-cycle on the rest of each of the `p^(n-1)` lines along coordinate 1. A `(p-1)`-cycle is
   odd, and `p^(n-1)` is odd, so the permutation is odd. Hence `T_n = Sym(F_p^n)`.
5. **`n = 1`.** Elementary maps are `x -> c x + d`. Every formally invertible polynomial map in one
   variable is affine (degrees multiply). So `T_1 = AGL(1,p)`. QED

*Scope.* Non-prime fields `F_(2^m)`, `m >= 2`, are not treated here; the permutation group induced by
tame automorphisms there may be smaller than the symmetric group. No source was checked for that case,
and no claim here depends on it.

## 4. Sitewise pairs over prime fields

**Theorem 4.1.** Let `p` be prime, `n >= 1`, `G` any group, and `(tau, sigma)` a sitewise bijective pair
on `(F_p^n)^G`: memory `{1}`, local permutation `phi` of `F_p^n`, `sigma = phi^-1`.
1. If `n >= 2`, or `p <= 3`, the pair is formalizable.
2. If `n = 1` and `p >= 5`, the pair is formalizable iff `phi` is affine.
3. After adding one identity track, every such pair is formalizable.

*Proof.*
1. By Proposition 3.1, `phi` is induced by a composite `F` of elementary maps, formally invertible with
   inverse `F^-1`. Use `tau*(X_g) = F(X_g)` and `sigma*(X_g) = F^-1(X_g)`. The identity is formal.
2. "If": affine maps are elementary. "Only if": Corollary 2.2, since the constant-configuration map of a
   sitewise pair is `phi` itself.
3. `phi (+) id` is a permutation of `F_p^(n+1)` with `n + 1 >= 2`, so part 1 applies. QED

## 5. The Bennett form in every characteristic

Let `tau, sigma` on `(F_q^n)^G` with `sigma o tau = id`, and on two tracks put
- `T(x, y) = (x, y + tau(x))`, formal inverse `(x, y - tau(x))`;
- `S(x, y) = (x - sigma(y), y)`, formal inverse `(x + sigma(y), y)`;
- `P` the track swap, and `V = P o S o T`.

Then `V` is formally invertible with any representatives, and `V(x, 0) = (tau(x), x - sigma(tau(x)))`,
which is `(tau(x), 0)` functionally. So `V` preserves `X_0 = A^G x 0`, restricts to `tau` there, and
`tau` is strict iff `V(X_0) ⊊ X_0`. The arithmetic `x^q = x` enters only in the invariance of `X_0`.
Stable formalizability asks for representatives in which the second coordinate of `V(x, 0)` vanishes
formally, after adding identity tracks. That is an equivariant finite-memory solvability problem inside
the ideal `(X_h^q - X_h)`, the same shape as in characteristic 2 (formal-polynomial artifact,
Section 6.3).

## 6. The reduction over `F_p`

- **Open:** `prime-field-left-inverse-pairs-are-stably-formalizable`. For every prime `p`, every group and
  every `n`, every left-inverse pair on `(F_p^n)^G` is stably formalizable. At `p = 2` it is implied by
  `binary-left-inverse-pairs-are-formalizable` (take `k = 0`).
- **Open:** `stable-finiteness-forces-prime-power-surjunctivity`. If `F_p[G]` is stably finite, `G` is
  surjunctive over every alphabet of size `p^n`.
- **Route** `stable-formalizability-reduces-prime-power-surjunctivity`:
  1. An injective automaton on `(F_p^n)^G` has a left inverse `sigma`, by compactness.
  2. Stable formalizability gives `k` with `(tau (+) id_k, sigma (+) id_k)` formalizable.
  3. Stable finiteness makes `M_(n+k)(F_p[G])` directly finite, so Theorem 1 makes `tau (+) id_k`
     bijective, hence `tau` bijective.
- **Route** `prime-power-surjunctivity-specializes-to-binary-powers`: the `p = 2` instance is
  `stable-finiteness-forces-binary-surjunctivity`.
- **What the odd-`p` statement adds:** Kaplansky over any single prime field `F_p` would suffice for
  `p`-power alphabets. By `stable-finiteness-reduces-to-prime-field`, stable finiteness over one field of
  characteristic `p` is equivalent to stable finiteness over `F_p`.

## 7. Non-prime-power alphabets: the alphabet bridge

**Product closure reaches multiples only.** By `strict-rule-pairs-pass-to-product-alphabets`, strictness
passes from `k` symbols to `kl` symbols, so surjunctivity over `kl` implies surjunctivity over `k`. If `k`
has two distinct prime factors, no multiple of `k` is a prime power. So even the conjunction of
`stable-finiteness-forces-prime-power-surjunctivity` over all primes, with Kaplansky over every prime
field, gives Gottschalk exactly over prime-power alphabets. A non-prime-power alphabet needs a bridge.

**Bridge options.**
- **Descent** to two symbols: `surjunctivity-failure-descends-to-binary-alphabet` (open, changes tables).
- **Ascent** to every larger alphabet: `strict-rule-pairs-ascend-to-larger-alphabets` (open, new here,
  owned by this lane at gk-n-boolean's request). Stated at table level: if tables `(T_f, T_r)` admit
  strict rules over some alphabet of size `k`, they admit strict rules over every size `n >= k`.
  Group-level ascent with the same memories follows through `surjunctivity-is-axiomatized-by-rectangle-clauses`:
  a strict automaton's rules are strict for its realized tables, and rules strict for those tables are
  strict on the same group.

**Attempts on ascent, each dead as stated.**
1. **Retraction** `r: A' -> A` with `mu o r`, `nu o r` (gk-n-boolean artifact
   `boolean-core-alphabets-and-network-coding-2026-09-12.md`, Section 5): the demanded cell's position
   inside its fiber is lost unless the fibers of `r` have equal size, and equal fibers are exactly
   products.
2. **Erasure symbol** `*`: the sink loses the values of relays that read a starred cell, while the
   forward identity uses every relay (same artifact, Section 5).
3. **Walls at group level** (this lane). On `B = A ⊔ {*}` output `*` at starred sites, the raw symbol at
   sites within radius `R` of a star, and `mu` of the window elsewhere. Two configurations with equal
   images have the same stars and agree within radius `R` of them. Far from the stars their `A`-parts
   have equal `tau`-images. The decoder recovers the far sites only where its window reads `tau`-values
   that are both output and agree. Sites in the annulus between radius `R - r_sigma` and `R` read band
   values that are output raw, not as `tau`-values, so they are not certified. Enlarging `R` moves the
   annulus without closing it. Outputting `tau`-values in the band as well needs two symbols per band
   site, which is a product alphabet again.
4. **Evidence consistent with ascent.** Every classified table has upward-closed admissible sizes:
   copy-code tables admit every `n >= 2`, and `x_a = x_b` and `x_a = x_b = x_a x_b` admit every
   `n >= 3` (boolean artifact, Section 6, verified by gk-vf-nonlinear at 370dc8c2b3). Descent fails for
   fixed tables (`equal-label-table-is-strict-at-three-symbols-not-two`), so ascent cannot be proved by
   passing through two symbols.

**Route into the goal** `gottschalk-via-alphabet-ascent`: ascent plus
`every-group-is-surjunctive-over-binary-power-alphabets` gives surjunctivity over every alphabet, since
any `k` sits below some `2^n`.

## 8. Consequences

**Established: `formalizable-strict-pairs-certify-non-linear-soficity`.** If `G` carries a formalizable
strict pair over `F_p^n`, then `M_n(F_p[G])` is not directly finite (Theorem 1, contrapositive), so
`F_p[G]` is not stably finite, so `G` is not `F`-linear sofic for any field `F` of characteristic `p`
(`linear-sofic-group-algebra-is-stably-finite`). A formalizable strict pair on any group would close the
root `non-linear-sofic-group`, and on `R^x` over `F_2` it would refute
`binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-f2-linear-sofic`.

**Open: `linear-sofic-groups-are-surjunctive`.** If `G` is `F_p`-linear sofic for some prime `p`, then `G`
is surjunctive over every finite alphabet. Route `linear-sofic-surjunctivity-via-formalizability-and-ascent`:
linear soficity gives stable finiteness; stable formalizability gives surjunctivity over `p^n`; ascent
gives every alphabet. Without ascent the same argument gives prime-power alphabets for every prime at
which `G` is linear sofic.

**Census of the recorded nonsofic hosts.**
- **Sofic groups:** linear sofic over every field, so the conditional statement contains Gromov–Weiss.
- **Kun–Thom wreath:** linear soficity open (`kun-thom-wreath-linear-sofic`), but its group algebras are
  stably finite over every field (`kun-thom-wreath-stably-finite`). So the stable-finiteness form
  already covers it, consistent with its known surjunctivity by permanence.
- **`R^x`, `L_(F_3)(1,2)^x`, `PG`:** linear soficity open (`binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-f2-linear-sofic`,
  `projective-ternary-group-is-f3-linear-sofic`). The rank models that would build it are dead in the
  natural forms (`leavitt-algebra-has-no-unital-rank-model`,
  `block-unipotent-rank-models-of-leavitt-el3-are-trivial`, `monomial-rank-models-are-hamming-models`), and
  property (T) does not round rank models over finite fields
  (`kazhdan-group-rank-models-admit-no-expander-decomposition`).
- **Thompson's `V`, Fournier–Facio group:** linear soficity not recorded.
- **No nonsofic group is known to be linear sofic** (`linear-sofic-nonsofic-group`, Arzhantseva–Paunescu
  Question 8.5). So the linear-sofic conditional exceeds Gromov–Weiss only once such a group exists, while
  the stable-finiteness form already reaches hosts with stably finite group algebras and no soficity.

**Route** `leavitt-units-surjunctive-via-f2-linear-soficity`: `F_2`-linear soficity of `R^x` plus
`linear-sofic-groups-are-surjunctive` gives `leavitt-unit-group-surjunctive`. Read the other way: a strict
automaton on `R^x`, together with stable formalizability and ascent, would make `R^x` the first
non-linear-sofic group.
