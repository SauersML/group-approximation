# Exact commutation stabilizers and relative embeddability

2026-09-07. This extends the tensor expectation construction in
`tensor-swap-expectation-repair-2026-09-07.md`. The new point is that a
strict, element-dependent commutation defect suffices. The coefficient
need not be the subgroup indicator, and no uniform lower bound on its
defect is assumed.

## 1. Any involution extracts its exact stabilizer

Let `A` be a countable group and let `(P,tau)` be a CE finite tracial
von Neumann algebra containing a canonical trace-preserving copy of
`L(A)`, with group unitaries `u_g`. Let `s=s^*=s^-1` belong to `P` and put

```text
K={g in A: [s,u_g]=0}.
```

This is a subgroup. Define

```text
q_g=(1+Re tau(u_g^* s u_g s))/2
   =1-||[s,u_g]||_2^2/4.                             (CSR1)
```

The operator `(1+Ad(s))/2` is the orthogonal conditional expectation onto
`{s}' cap P`, so

```text
q_g=||E_({s}')(u_g)||_2^2 in [0,1].
```

Faithfulness of the trace gives `q_g=1` exactly when `g in K`. For every
other fixed `g`, `q_g<1`. If instead one starts with a projection `p`,
take `s=2p-1`, in which case `q_g=1-||[p,u_g]||_2^2`.

**Theorem.** `L(K)<=L(A)` is relatively embeddable over scalars.

Here is the finite-coordinate proof, including the rate choice. Embed
the separable algebra generated by `L(A)` and `s` in a tracial matrix
ultraproduct. Lift actor unitaries to exact matrix unitaries `U_n(g)` and
lift `s` to exact matrix involutions `S_n` by spectral rounding. For

```text
B_n={S_n}' cap M_(d_n),
E_n(X)=(X+S_n X S_n)/2,
```

one has

```text
q_(g,n)=||E_n(U_n(g))||_2^2
       =(1+Re tr(U_n(g)^* S_n U_n(g) S_n))/2 -> q_g.    (CSR2)
```

As in the previous tensor proof, choose coordinates `n(j)` satisfying
the first `j` actor multiplication and canonical trace conditions,
the first `j` coefficients in `(CSR2)`, and commutation with the first
`j` elements of `K`, all with error at most `j^-3`. The choice is an
existence argument; it uses no decision procedure for `K`. Set

```text
V_j(g)=U_(n(j))(g)^tensor j,
D_j=d_(n(j))^j,
C_j=B_(n(j))^tensor j.
```

Actor multiplication errors grow at most by `j`, so they tend to zero.
The canonical trace remains canonical because
`tr(V_j(g))=tr(U_(n(j))(g))^j`. For `g in K`, telescoping the contraction
`E_(n(j))(U_(n(j))(g))` gives

```text
dist_2(V_j(g),C_j)<= (j/2)||[S_(n(j)),U_(n(j))(g)]||_2
                 <=(1/2)j^-2.
```

For a fixed `g outside K`, tensor factorization instead gives

```text
||E_(C_j)(V_j(g))||_2^2=q_(g,n(j))^j.
```

Choose any `r_g` strictly between `q_g` and `1`. Since the coefficient
error is at most `j^-3` eventually, `q_(g,n(j))<=r_g` eventually. Thus
the last expression is bounded by `r_g^j` and tends to zero. The number
`r_g` can depend on `g`; each group word is fixed before taking the
limit. A uniform gap over `A-K` is unnecessary.

In the resulting tracial matrix ultraproduct, the coordinate expectations
therefore fix `lambda(K)` and kill every `lambda(g)` for `g outside K`.
Linearity and 2-norm density extend this to

```text
E_(prod C_j) o pi = pi o E_(L(K)).                    (CSR3)
```

The lower-left coordinate algebras `C_j` are finite-dimensional and hence
hyperfinite; `(CSR3)` is the full scalar relative-embedding commuting
square. This proves the theorem without replacing `K` by its normalizer
or by a subgroup containing only finitely many tested elements.

## 2. Countable families require no joint commutation

Let `(p_i)_(i>=1)` be any countable family of projections in the same
CE algebra `P`, and put

```text
K=intersection_i {g in A:[p_i,u_g]=0}.
```

Use the commutative probability algebra `ell^infinity(N)` with weights
`w_i=2^-i`. In `P tensor ell^infinity(N)`, embed the actor diagonally
and take the single involution

```text
s=(2p_i-1)_i.
```

Every weight is positive, so `[s,u_g tensor 1]=0` exactly when all
`[p_i,u_g]=0`. The ambient algebra remains CE: finite weighted direct
sums have block-diagonal microstates with asymptotically correct block
proportions, and the remaining trace weight can be made arbitrarily
small. Equivalently one tensors with an abelian, hence hyperfinite,
probability algebra. Section 1 applies to this `s`.

The projections `p_i` need not commute with each other. No product of
their conditional expectations is taken in one matrix coordinate;
the weighted direct sum places them on separate central blocks.
For a finite family use any strictly positive weights summing to one;
for an empty family `K=A` and the identity inclusion is scalar-RE
because the canonical actor is already CE.

The same construction combines different canonical CE actor models
`(P_i,u^(i))`. Their weighted direct sum has canonical actor
`u_g=(u_g^(i))_i`, since every block has trace `1_(g=e)`. A projection
chosen in one block and zero on the others keeps its commutation
stabilizer. Therefore projections living initially in different models
can be put into one canonical CE model before taking their common
stabilizer.

## 3. Relative embeddability is exact commutant separation

For arbitrary countable `C<=A`, the following are equivalent:

1. `L(C)<=L(A)` is scalar relatively embeddable.
2. Some canonical CE actor model carries countably many projections in
   `u(C)'` whose common commutation stabilizer is exactly `C`.
3. `A` is hyperlinear and, for every `g outside C`, some canonical CE
   actor model contains a `C`-central projection moved by `u_g`. The
   model and projection may depend on `g`.

For `2=>1`, use Section 2. For `3=>2`, enumerate the countable set
`A-C`, choose its witnesses, and combine their models by a weighted
direct sum as above. The explicit hyperlinearity condition in 3 covers
the case `C=A`, where there are no outside elements and an empty
condition would otherwise say nothing about the actor.

For `1=>2`, the established coset hyperlinearity equivalence supplies
the canonical CE model of

```text
E=A *_C(C x C_2).
```

Its binary generator `s` commutes with `C`. For `g outside C`, the
reduced amalgam word `u_g^* s u_g s` has canonical trace zero, so
`||[s,u_g]||_2^2=2`. Thus the single projection `(1+s)/2` has exact
commutation stabilizer `C`. Condition 3 follows as well.

This result does not require the separating projections to have any
specified trace, uniformly positive commutation defect, prescribed
joint distribution, or free conjugates. The canonical binary model
with all those additional moment conditions is constructed only after
the scalar relative embedding has been extracted, potentially in a
different embedding and a larger ultraproduct.

### The least scalar-RE overgroup

Assume `A` is hyperlinear. Define `K_C` to be the intersection of the
commutation stabilizers of **all** `C`-central projections in **all**
canonical CE actor models. It is a subgroup containing `C`.

For each `g outside K_C`, choose one model and projection witnessing
that exclusion. Countability of `A` and Section 2 show that
`L(K_C)<=L(A)` is scalar-RE. If `K_C=A`, use any canonical actor model
and the trivial projection instead. Furthermore, if `C<=J<=A` and
`L(J)<=L(A)` is scalar-RE, the canonical binary `J`-coset lamp has
stabilizer exactly `J` and is `C`-central. Hence `K_C<=J`.

Consequently `K_C` is the least intermediate subgroup with scalar-RE
inclusion into `A`, and

```text
L(C)<=L(A) is scalar-RE iff K_C=C.                    (CSR4)
```

This also proves that intersections of scalar-RE subgroups of a
hyperlinear actor are scalar-RE: the same countable witness selection
reduces an arbitrary family to one countable projection family.
The assignment `C -> K_C` is a closure operation on the subgroup lattice:
it is extensive; `C<=D` implies `K_C<=K_D`, because every `D`-central
projection is `C`-central; and `K_(K_C)=K_C` by scalar-RE of `K_C` and
`(CSR4)`. In particular, global relative-commutant collapse for `C<=A`
is equivalent to `K_C=A`, or equivalently to there being no proper
scalar-RE intermediate subgroup containing `C`.

## 4. Maximal subgroups turn every leak into the whole HNN model

Suppose `C` is a maximal proper subgroup of `A`. Let

```text
H=A *_C(C x Z).
```

Then `H` is nonhyperlinear if and only if the following global
relative-commutant identity holds:

```text
pi(L(C))' cap Q = pi(L(A))' cap Q                     (CSR5)
```

for **every** canonical trace-preserving embedding
`pi:L(A)->Q` into a tracial matrix ultraproduct. Here maximality is
ordinary subgroup maximality, not maximality among normal, finite-index,
closed, or co-dense subgroups.

To prove the substantive direction, suppose `(CSR5)` fails. Choose
an element of the left commutant that is not in the right one.
Taking its self-adjoint real or imaginary part and then a spectral
projection produces

```text
p in pi(L(C))' cap Q,      [p,pi(u_g)]!=0
```

for some `g in A`. The spectral-projection step is justified because
a unitary commuting with every spectral projection of a self-adjoint
operator commutes with the operator. Put

```text
K={a in A:[p,pi(u_a)]=0}.
```

Then `C<=K<A`, so maximality forces `K=C`. The separable subalgebra
generated by the actor and `p` is CE. Section 1 therefore gives
scalar-RE for `L(C)<=L(A)`, and the established HNN equivalence makes
`H` hyperlinear.

Conversely, if `H` is hyperlinear, its canonical CE free binary coset
model provides a projection commuting with `C` and moved by every
element outside `C`, as in Section 3. Embedding that separable CE
algebra into a tracial matrix ultraproduct violates `(CSR5)`.

If `A` itself is nonhyperlinear, `(CSR5)` has no canonical embeddings
to quantify over and is true vacuously; `H` is nonhyperlinear because
it contains `A`. Thus the equivalence also covers that case. For a
hyperlinear `A`, its nonvacuous content can alternatively be written

```text
H hyperlinear iff K_C=C,
H nonhyperlinear iff K_C=A,
global relative-commutant collapse iff K_C=A.
```

No assertion is made that the initial leaked projection becomes a Haar
letter in its original embedding. The proof constructs a new canonical
HNN model through relative embeddability.

## 5. Interface with the goal

The existing arithmetic claims ask for commutant collapse for the
inclusions `SL_n(Z)<=SL_n(Z[1/p])`, sometimes in every canonical actor
embedding and sometimes only in embeddings extending to the HNN group.
Section 4 makes those quantifier scopes equivalent **after** ordinary
maximality of the named subgroup has been proved. It does not assume
maximality for any arithmetic pair. That group-theoretic input is a
separate obligation; co-density or commensuration alone is not a
substitute.

Sections 1--3 are available even without maximality. A counterexample
to global collapse over `C` then produces an intermediate subgroup
`K` with `C<=K<A` and scalar-RE inclusion, possibly larger than `C`.
One must not replace `K` by `C` without an additional argument.

The result does not prove either collapse or a leak for any of the
open arithmetic or Kun--Thom instances. It strengthens the exact
connection between their remaining hypotheses and the requested
nonhyperlinear endpoint.
