# LXI through ideals with a unital copy of O_2 in the multiplier algebra

Lane `ex-k1-properly-infinite`, 2026-09-12. Theorem 1 is proved completely below. It has not been
externally reviewed. It does not decide STW Problem LXI. Its point is to move the search for a
counterexample from properly infinite algebras, where every unital image is properly infinite and
finite-dimensional detection is impossible, to unitizations of non-unital algebras. Those may be
stably finite and carry characteristic-class invariants.

## 1. Statement

**Theorem 1.** Let I be a non-unital C\*-algebra with isometries `S_1, S_2 in M(I)` satisfying
`S_1 S_1^* + S_2 S_2^* = 1`. Put `A = C*(I, S_1, S_2) subset M(I)`, which contains `1 = S_1^* S_1`. Then:

(a) A is unital and properly infinite, `A = I + C*(S_1,S_2)`, and `A/I ~= O_2`.
(b) For `u in U(I~)` (the unitization `I~ = I + C1 inside A`): `u in U_0(A)` iff `u in U_0(I~)`.
(c) A is K1-injective iff `I~` is K1-injective.

**Corollary 2.** If some non-unital C\*-algebra I with a unital copy of O_2 in `M(I)` has a unitization
that is not K1-injective, then LXI fails. Conversely, LXI implies that every such unitization is
K1-injective.

## 2. Inputs (standard, with sources)

- (J) X. Jiang, *Nonstable K-theory for Z-stable C\*-algebras*, arXiv:math/9707228, abstract:
  "Let A != 0 be a unital C\*-algebra with A = A tensor Z. Then the homotopy groups of the group U(A) of
  unitaries in A are stable invariants, namely, pi_i(U(A)) = K_{i-1}(A) for all integers i >= 0"
  (fetched 2026-09-12; the repo's `z-stable-unital-k1-bijective-citation` imports Theorem 2.8).
  O_2 is strongly self-absorbing (Toms--Winter, Trans. AMS 359 (2007)), and strongly self-absorbing
  algebras are Z-stable (Winter, J. Noncommut. Geom. 5 (2011)). Both facts are recorded in Blanchard,
  arXiv:1507.02535v1, section 1. Hence `pi_1(U(O_2)) = K_0(O_2) = 0`.
- (C) Cuntz, Ann. of Math. 113 (1981): purely infinite simple algebras are K1-injective, so
  `U(O_2) = U_0(O_2)` because `K_1(O_2) = 0`.
- (BG) Bartle--Graves: a bounded linear surjection of Banach spaces has a continuous right inverse.
- (H) Hurewicz: a locally trivial principal bundle over a paracompact base is a Serre fibration,
  with the long exact sequence of homotopy groups.
- The six-term exact sequence of K-theory for `0 -> I -> A -> O_2 -> 0`.

## 3. Proof

**(a).** `S_1, S_2 in A` are isometries with orthogonal ranges, so A is unital and properly infinite.
I is an ideal of `M(I)`, hence of A, and `I + C*(S_1,S_2)` is closed (ideal plus C\*-subalgebra), so it
equals A. The quotient `A/I` is generated by the images of `S_1, S_2`, which satisfy the Cuntz relation.
It is nonzero, since `1 in I` would make I unital. As O_2 is simple, `A/I ~= O_2`.

**Lemma 3 (fibration).** Let `pi: A -> C` be a surjection of unital C\*-algebras. Then
`pi: U_0(A) -> U_0(C)` is a principal bundle with structure group `G = U_0(A) cap pi^{-1}(1)`, so
`pi_1(U_0(C), 1) -> pi_0(G) -> pi_0(U_0(A)) = *` is exact.

*Proof.* `pi` maps `U_0(A)` onto `U_0(C)`: products of exponentials lift. By (BG) applied to the real
Banach space surjection `A_sa -> C_sa`, choose a continuous right inverse sigma, normalized so that
`sigma(0) = 0` (subtract `sigma(0)`, which lies in the kernel). For `||w - 1|| < 2` put
`s(w) = exp(i sigma(-i Log w))` with the principal logarithm. This is continuous, `s(1) = 1` and
`pi(s(w)) = w`. Left translation gives local sections around every point. So `U_0(A) -> U_0(C)` is a
locally trivial principal G-bundle (the quotient topology coincides with the norm topology because
local sections make pi open). Its base is metrizable, hence paracompact, and (H) applies. Square.

**(b).** The implication from right to left is trivial. Conversely, let `u in U(I~) cap U_0(A)`. Its
scalar part is some `lambda in T`, and `lambda^{-1} u` lies in the same components of `U(I~)` and of
`U(A)`. So assume `pi(u) = 1`, that is, `u in G`. By Lemma 3 with `C = O_2` and (J),
`pi_0(G)` is the image of `pi_1(U(O_2)) = 0`. So G is path-connected, and u is joined to 1 inside
`G subset U(I~)`, giving `u in U_0(I~)`.

**(c), A K1-injective => I~ K1-injective.** Let `u in U(I~)` with `[u] = 0` in `K_1(I)`. Its class in
`K_1(A)` is the image of `[u]` under `K_1(I) -> K_1(A)`, hence zero. By K1-injectivity of A, `u in U_0(A)`,
and by (b), `u in U_0(I~)`.

**(c), I~ K1-injective => A K1-injective.** Let `u in U(A)` with `[u] = 0` in `K_1(A)`. By (C),
`pi(u) in U_0(O_2)`, so some `w in U_0(A)` has `pi(w) = pi(u)`. Then `y = u w^*` lies in `U(I~)` with
`pi(y) = 1` and `[y] = 0` in `K_1(A)`. The six-term sequence has `K_0(O_2) = 0 -> K_1(I) -> K_1(A)`,
so `K_1(I) -> K_1(A)` is injective and `[y] = 0` in `K_1(I)`. By hypothesis `y in U_0(I~)`, which is
contained in `U_0(A)`, so `u = y w in U_0(A)`. Square.

## 4. What a counterexample ideal must look like

**Proposition 4 (constraints).** Let I be as in Theorem 1.
1. `Phi(x) = [S_i^* x S_j]_{i,j}` is a \*-isomorphism `I -> M_2(I)`, with inverse `[x_ij] -> sum S_i x_ij S_j^*`.
   Under it, the stabilization `u -> diag(u,1)` becomes `1 + x -> 1 + S_1 x S_1^*`. So K1-injectivity of
   `I~` fails iff some non-null `1 + x` becomes null after finitely many applications of `Ad S_1`.
2. I has no nonzero finite-dimensional representation: an irreducible representation of dimension
   `d < infinity`, pulled back through `Phi^{-1}`, is `id_{M_2} (x) sigma` with `dim sigma = d/2`, and
   iterating gives `d/2^k` for all k.
3. I is not stable. If `I ~= J (x) K`, a unitary in `I~` is homotopic to one in `M_n(J)~`, and a
   K1-null-homotopy in `M_N(J)~` transports into `I~` through a corner embedding `M_N(K) -> K`. Corner
   embeddings are homotopic to each other through strictly continuous isometries.
4. `I = C_0(Y) (x) D` with D unital and `O_2 subset D` reduces K1-injectivity of `I~` to injectivity of
   `pi_k(U(D)) -> K_{k+1}(D)` for the properly infinite D. That is LXI-type again, so such I give nothing new.
5. Module picture: I is `K(E)` for the Hilbert I-module `E = I`, and `E ~= E (+) E` through `(S_1, S_2)`.
   A witness is approximated at finite stages: if `E = closure(union E_n)` with complemented finitely
   generated `E_n`, then `1 + x` is null in `I~` iff its compression is null in `End(E_n)` for some n.

**Proposition 5 (Euler-class protection cannot be used).** Let `B = C(X)` and let E be a countably
generated Hilbert B-module with `E ~= E (+) E`, exhausted by increasing complemented finitely generated
projective submodules `E_n`. Suppose E contains two orthonormal sections spanning a summand `theta^2`,
and write `E_n = theta^2 (+) P_n` for n large, with `r_n = rank P_n` constant on a connected X. Then
`e(P_n) = c_{r_n}(P_n) = 0` for all large n.

*Proof.* Through `S_1, S_2`, `E` contains `2^k` orthonormal sections for every k (apply the words
`S_w`, `|w| = k`, to one unit section). Take k = 2. The strict convergence of the projections onto `E_n`
and Gram--Schmidt put four orthonormal sections inside some `E_n`. So `E_n ~= theta^4 (+) eta` with
`rank eta = r_n - 2 < r_n`. The Whitney formula gives `c(P_n) = c(theta^2 (+) P_n) = c(eta)`, and
`c_{r_n}(eta) = 0` because the rank of eta is below `r_n`. Square.

The LIX protection (`stw59-simple-ah-non-k1-injective`: an odd top Chern number of the complement H at
critical rank) and Toms's trapped spin-bordism class (an Euler class of `Hom(Q, C^2)`, which is zero once
`e(Q) = 0`) both need the complement of the witness to have nonzero Euler class at every stage.
Proposition 5 shows that the O_2 self-similarity destroys exactly that. So an ideal as in Corollary 2
over homogeneous stages needs a protection that survives trivial summands of the complement: an
unstable invariant of `u (+) 1_P` that is not killed by `P (+) theta^2 ~= theta^4 (+) eta`. The
characteristic-class obstructions used so far do not have this property.

## 5. Novelty check (bounded)

Not found in BRR, Blanchard arXiv:0804.4624v13 or 1507.02535v1 (all pages read), or the table of
contents of Rohde's thesis. Extensions by O_2 and nonstable exact sequences are standard tools, so the
observation is elementary. Its value is the change of arena for detection.
