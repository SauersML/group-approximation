## 21. Problem 1.04 region (z1-04-fp-fh, f2acd7511b): five PASS

1. **`proper-and-free-type-fhn-coincide` (Theorem C): PASS.**
   - S_e is nonempty: e = h e_j gives K(e) ⊆ hC.
   - S_e is finite: g⁻¹e ∈ C, and stabilizers are finite.
   - S_e is equivariant, and monotone along faces. So W = ⋃ e × J(S_e) is a subcomplex: the boundary
     faces e′ ⊆ ∂e have J(S_{e′}) ⊇ J(S_e).
   - G acts freely on the simplices of the join of the discrete set G, hence freely on W, with
     finitely many orbits of cells.
   - The fibre over an open cell is J(S_e), an (n+1)-fold join of a nonempty finite set, so it is
     (n−1)-connected.
   - In the skeletal spectral sequences, the map W → X is an isomorphism on E¹ in rows q ≤ n−1:
     row 0 is the cellular chains of X, and rows 1…n−1 vanish.
   - A differential into total degree j ≤ n−1 starts in a row q ≤ n−1, and one leaving it lands in
     total degree ≤ n−2. So H_j(W) ≅ H_j(X) for j ≤ n−1.
2. **`type-fhn-groups-are-of-type-fpn` (Theorem A): PASS.** Free replacement, then cellular chains
   through degree n.
3. **`type-fp2-groups-are-of-type-fh2` (Theorem B): PASS.** Z₁ of a Cayley graph is finitely generated
   by FP₂ and Schanuel. Hurewicz is onto, so finitely many orbits of 2-cells kill H₁. So FP_n ⇔ FH_n
   for n ≤ 2.
4. **`fn-groups-mod-acyclic-normal-subgroups-are-type-fhn` (Theorem E): PASS.**
   - Z is the G-cover of a K(Q,1) with finite (n−1)-skeleton, and Z = K(P,1).
   - X = Z^{(n−1)} is (n−2)-acyclic. H_{n−1}(X) is finitely generated by FP_n and Lemma S.
   - H_{n−1}(X) dies in H_{n−1}(X^{(n)}) = H_{n−1}(P) = 0, so finitely many orbits of n-cells kill it.
5. **`type-fh3-iff-fp3-and-superperfect-kernel-over-fp-group` (Theorem D): PASS.**
   - (⇐) is item 4 with n = 3.
   - (⇒): Y/G is finite, so Q = π₁(Y/G) is finitely presented and P = π₁(Y). Then
     H₁(P) = H₁(Y) = 0, and by Hopf H₂(Y) = 0 ↠ H₂(P).
   - At n = 3, Problem 1.4 is therefore exactly: is every group of type FP₃ a finitely presented
     group modulo a superperfect normal subgroup?

## 25. Problem 4.05 products of trees (z4-05-raag-rips, bfe1be0558): PASS

**`l1-products-of-trees-have-contractible-rips-complexes` (+ `-proof`), re-derived.**
- **Lemma J.** Moving the barycentre b toward x, first variation at the minimizer gives
  E[d(b,Y); Y on the x-side] ≤ E[d(b,Y); Y off it]. With d(x,y) = d(x,b) + d(b,y) off the x-side and
  d(x,y) ≥ d(x,b) − d(b,y) on it, this yields E d(x,Y) ≥ d(x,b). It transfers coordinatewise to d₁.
- **Lemma H.** A d₁-ball is a sublevel set of a sum of convex tree distances, so it is convex in the
  l² product. If a minimal non-intersecting family has m ≥ k+2 members, its union has nerve ∂Δ^{m−1}
  and is ≃ S^{m−2} with m−2 ≥ k. But a compact subset A of the contractible k-dimensional P has
  Ȟ^j(A) = 0 for j ≥ k, by the exact sequence of (P,A). So the Helly number is ≤ k+1.
- **First criterion hypothesis.** k+1 of the balls B(s, kt/(k+1)) meet at the barycentre of their
  centres. By Helly all of them meet, and rounding (d₁ ≤ k/2) gives S ⊆ B(v, r_t) with
  r_t = kt/(k+1) + k/2 < t.
- **Second hypothesis.** The balls B(s, kt/(k+1)) and B(y, t − k/2), y ∈ Y(S), have the
  (k+1)-intersection property because r_t ≤ t − k/2 ⇔ t ≥ k(k+1). Helly and rounding give y₀ ∈ Y(S)
  within t of every y.
- Zaremsky's Theorem 3.1 (imported verbatim) gives contractibility for t ≥ k(k+1) − 1.

**Corollaries.**
- `complete-multipartite-raags-are-rips-type-r`: PASS, since d₁ on F_{n₁}×⋯×F_{n_k} with standard
  generators is the l¹ product of tree metrics.
- `raags-with-multipartite-or-triangle-free-components-type-r`: PASS, by free-product closure.
- New dimension-3 cases covered: F₂³ and Z²×F₂. The smallest open graph is the triangle with a pendant

- Novelty unchecked. A bounded check is worth doing before calling F₂³ "new", given Hulbert–Zaremsky's
