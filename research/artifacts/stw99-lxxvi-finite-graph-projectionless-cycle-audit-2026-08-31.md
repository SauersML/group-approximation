# STW LXXVI finite-graph projectionless cycle audit

## Result

Let `X` be a finite graph and let `D` be a simple separable
stable-rank-one C*-algebra which is stably projectionless. Point evaluation
is a natural isomorphism

```text
Cu(C(X,D)) ~= Lsc(X,Cu(D)).                              (PG1)
```

There is no restriction on `K_1(D)`. Stable projectionlessness supplies a
soft spectral slice at which every one of the finitely many cycle-clutching
mismatches can be changed at arbitrarily small action cost.

Consequently, if `D` is also stably finite and pure, then `C(X,D)` is pure
and Cuntz semigroup regular for every finite graph `X`, even when `K_1(D)`
is nonzero.

## Cycle-opening comparison lemma

Let `a,b` be positive in `C(Y,D tensor K)`, where `Y` is a connected finite
graph and `b(y)` is nonzero for every `y`. Assume

```text
[a(y)] <= [b(y)] in Cu(D)       for every y in Y.          (PG2)
```

Choose an interior point on every chord of a spanning tree and cut `Y` at
those points. The resulting quotient map `q:T -> Y` has `T` a finite tree
and gives two endpoints over every cut point. The finite-tree theorem gives

```text
q^*a Cuntz-below q^*b.                                   (PG3)
```

The order of the next choices matters. Fix an action tolerance `rho>0` and
let `E` be the finite set of cut endpoints `e` for which `a(q(e))` is
nonzero. Stable projectionlessness implies that zero is not isolated in
`spectrum(a(q(e)))`: a spectral gap would make the nonzero support projection
of `a(q(e))` an element of `D tensor K`. Hence, for every `e in E`, choose

```text
0 < lambda_e in spectrum(a(q(e))),       lambda_e < rho^2. (PG4)
```

Only after all these values have been chosen, take a single `delta>0` below
the comparison tolerance and below `min_e lambda_e`. If `E` is empty, take
any sufficiently small `delta`. The exact comparison-vector form of (PG3)
supplies `y_1` with

```text
y_1^*y_1=(q^*a-delta)_+,
y_1y_1^* in Her(q^*b).                                   (PG5)
```

Spectral mapping, together with equality of the nonzero spectra of
`y_1^*y_1` and `y_1y_1^*`, gives at every `e in E`

```text
0 < lambda_e-delta
    in spectrum(y_1(e)y_1(e)^*) and lambda_e-delta < rho^2. (PG6)
```

## One simultaneous endpoint lift

At each original cut point choose one coefficient comparison vector for
`(a-delta)_+` into `Her(b)`. After shrinking a neighborhood, multiplying it
on the left by a large root of `b` gives a continuous local comparison field.
Pull the same downstairs field to the two endpoint collars. Thus the local
field has exactly equal paired endpoint values and will descend after the
collar splice.

The stable-rank-one comparison-unitary lemma gives, at each endpoint,
a unitary in the unitization of `Her(b(q(e)))` whose action approximately
aligns `y_1(e)` with the chosen local vector. Antoine--Dadarlat--Perera--
Santiago, Lemma 3.1, applied with the identity as the comparison unitary,
replaces each alignment unitary by one of zero `K_1`-class while changing
its action on `y_1(e)` by less than

```text
5 sqrt(lambda_e-delta) < 5 rho.                           (PG7)
```

This is the step for which choosing `lambda_e` before `delta` is essential;
nonisolation at zero does not imply spectral density above a preassigned
cutdown.

Put `B=Her(q^*b)`. Every fiber of `B` is nonzero and full because `D` is
simple. Proposition 2.2 of the same paper applies to the whole finite
endpoint list: since all adjusted unitaries have the same image, zero, in
`K_1(D)`, one unitary in the unitization of `B` has all their prescribed
endpoint values simultaneously. Thus the proof does not make incompatible
one-cut-at-a-time choices.

Multiply `y_1` on the left by this global unitary. On pairwise disjoint
endpoint collars, splice it to the pullbacks of the local downstairs
comparison fields. Both fields lie in the closed right ideal generated by
`q^*b`, so scalar partition-of-unity splicing stays in that ideal. Choose the
collars narrow and `rho` small. The resulting vector `z` has source square
arbitrarily close to `q^*a` and has exactly equal values at every paired
endpoint. It therefore descends through `q` to a vector `c` on `Y`.

Hereditary membership also reflects through the quotient map. Indeed, if
`q^*c` lies in the closed right ideal generated by `q^*b`, then

```text
(q^*b)^(1/n) q^*c = q^*(b^(1/n)c) -> q^*c.
```

Since `q^*` is isometric, `b^(1/n)c -> c` on `Y`, and therefore
`cc^* in Her(b)`. Letting the collar error, `rho`, and `delta` tend to zero
proves that (PG2) implies global comparison on `Y`.

## Removing the nowhere-zero target hypothesis

For general `a,b` satisfying (PG2), fix `epsilon>0`. The compact set
`closure(supp((a-epsilon)_+))` is compactly contained in the open set
`supp(b)`. It can meet only finitely many components of that open set.
Choose a finite disjoint union

```text
Y_0 = disjoint_union_j Y_j
```

of connected compact finite subgraphs such that

```text
closure(supp((a-epsilon)_+)) contained in int(Y_0),
Y_0 contained in supp(b).                                (PG8)
```

Run the cycle-opening argument on each `Y_j`, equivalently using
Antoine--Dadarlat--Perera--Santiago, Lemma 2.4(i), for the finite disjoint
union. Lemma 2.4(ii) then promotes the restricted comparison to `X`. Let
`epsilon` decrease to zero. Thus evaluation in (PG1) reflects order.

The published one-dimensional lower-semicontinuous realization theorem says
that evaluation is surjective once it is an order embedding. This proves
(PG1), and the evaluation construction is natural in `D`.

## Pure-fiber corollary

Put `E=D tensor_min Z`. For simple stably finite pure `D`, Lin gives stable
rank one and the simple-case STW theorem gives an isomorphism for the actual
first-factor map

```text
Cu(D) -> Cu(E).                                           (PG9)
```

The target is simple and stable rank one. It is stably projectionless:
a nonzero projection over `E` would determine a nonzero compact Cu class;
its inverse image under (PG9) would be a nonzero compact class in `Cu(D)`,
hence a projection over the stably finite coefficient `D`, a contradiction.
Apply (PG1) to both coefficients. Naturality identifies the graph
first-factor map with pointwise application of (PG9), so it is an
isomorphism. Separately, Seth--Vilalta supplies purity of `C(X,D)`.

## Phillips obstruction audit and scope

Phillips's circle-with-a-tail example in Antoine--Dadarlat--Perera--Santiago,
Remark 3.9, uses a nonzero projection bundle on the whole circle, with a
`K_1(D)` clutching coordinate, and only then fades one element along the
tail. The obstruction is compact on the cycle and cannot occur in a stably
projectionless coefficient. More substantively, every nonzero positive
fiber here has spectrum accumulating at zero, which is exactly the soft
slice used in (PG7) to erase its clutching mismatch. The circle case is also
consistent with Theorem 3.7 of the same paper: its compact/noncompact
decomposition collapses to the pointwise formula when the coefficient is
stably projectionless.

This does not say that `K_1(D)` vanishes. The proof is specific to finite
graphs because it makes only finitely many simultaneous chord corrections.
No assertion is made for infinite graphs, arbitrary one-dimensional
compacta, or coefficients carrying nonzero projections.
