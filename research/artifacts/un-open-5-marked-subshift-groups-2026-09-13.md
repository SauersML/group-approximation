# The marked Pestov group of a subshift remembers the subshift: a continuum of simple LEF Kazhdan groups

Lane `un-open-5`, 2026-09-13. Status: complete written proof by this lane, UNREVIEWED.
Inputs used: `simple-kazhdan-lef-group-from-minimal-subshift` and its three requires (reviewed PASS twice), the
Steinberg commutator relation, and an elementary argument about rotation codings (§5).

## 0. Statements

Fix a prime power `q` and a finite alphabet `A`. For an infinite minimal subshift `X ⊆ A^Z` put
`R_X = LC(X,F_q) ⋊_T Z`, `G_X = EL_3(R_X)` and `S_X = G_X / Z(G_X)`. By
`simple-kazhdan-lef-group-from-minimal-subshift`, `S_X` is an infinite, finitely generated, simple Kazhdan group
that is LEF, hence sofic and hyperlinear. Let `L(X)` be the set of finite words occurring in points of `X`.

**Theorem 1 (the marking determines the language).** There is a finite tuple `σ_X` of standard generators of
`S_X`, indexed by a set that depends only on `(A, q)`, and for every word `w ∈ A^*` a group word `W_w` in those
indices, independent of `X`, such that for every infinite minimal subshift `X ⊆ A^Z`:
`W_w(σ_X) = 1` in `S_X` iff `w ∉ L(X)`.

**Theorem 2 (countable fibres).** For every group `H`, at most countably many infinite minimal subshifts
`X ⊆ A^Z` satisfy `S_X ≅ H`.

**Corollary 3 (continuum).** For every prime power `q`, there are continuum many pairwise non-isomorphic
infinite, finitely generated, simple groups with property (T) that are LEF, hence sofic and hyperlinear, and have
no nontrivial finite quotient.

Comparison. The graph already has continuum many infinite simple torsion-free Kazhdan quotients of one hyperbolic
group (`kazhdan-hyperbolic-continuum-non-fng-maximal-kernels`). Those are not known to be sofic or hyperlinear.
The new content of Corollary 3 is the LEF, sofic and hyperlinear clause. Before 2026-09-12 the graph knew no
infinite simple Kazhdan group that is sofic.

## 1. Generators

Let `S_0 = {1, u, u^(-1)} ∪ {χ_a : a ∈ A}`, where `χ_a = χ_[x_0 = a]`. When `q` is not prime, add a
primitive element `θ` of `F_q`. By item 1 of `minimal-subshift-algebra-is-simple-lef-ring`, `S_0` generates
`R_X` as a ring.

**Lemma 1.1.** For any associative unital ring `R` and any `a, b ∈ R`:
- `e_ij(a) e_ij(b) = e_ij(a + b)`;
- `[e_ij(a), e_jk(b)] = e_ik(ab)` for distinct `i, j, k`, with `[x, y] = x y x^(-1) y^(-1)`.

Proof. Direct matrix computation with `E_ij E_kl = δ_jk E_il`. ∎

Consequently `EL_3(R)` is generated by `{e_ij(s) : s ∈ S, i ≠ j}` whenever `S ∋ 1` generates `R` as a ring:
the set of `r` with `e_ij(r)` in the subgroup for all `i ≠ j` is closed under sums, negatives and products.

The standard tuple is `σ_X = (e_ij(s) Z)_(i≠j, s ∈ S_0)` in `S_X`. Its index set
`I = {(i,j,s)}` depends only on `(A, q)`.

## 2. The words `W_w`

Let `F = Z⟨U, V, C_a (a ∈ A), Θ⟩` be the free associative ring, and put `J = {1, 2, 3}`. For every
nonempty monomial `m` and every `i ≠ k` in `J`, define a group word `ω_ik(m)` over the alphabet `I` by recursion
on the degree:
- for a letter `s ∈ {U, V, C_a, Θ}`, `ω_ik(s)` is the index `(i, k, s)`;
- for `m = s · m'` with `s` a letter and `m'` nonempty, `ω_ik(m) = [ω_ij(s), ω_jk(m')]`, where `j` is the
  unique element of `J ∖ {i, k}`.

**Lemma 2.1 (evaluation).** Let `R` be a ring and `ev : F → R` a ring homomorphism. Evaluate each index
`(i, k, s)` at `e_ik(ev(s))`. Then `ω_ik(m)` evaluates to `e_ik(ev(m))` in `EL_3(R)`.

Proof. Induction on the degree. For `m = s m'`, the induction hypothesis and Lemma 1.1 give
`[e_ij(ev s), e_jk(ev m')] = e_ik(ev(s) ev(m')) = e_ik(ev(m))`. ∎

For `w = w_0 w_1 ⋯ w_(n-1) ∈ A^n` with `n ≥ 1`, put `m_w = ∏_(t=0)^(n-1) V^t C_(w_t) U^t` (ordered
product, with `V^0 C U^0 = C`), and `W_w = ω_12(m_w)`.

**Lemma 2.2.** Under `ev_X : U ↦ u`, `V ↦ u^(-1)`, `C_a ↦ χ_a`, `Θ ↦ θ`,
`ev_X(m_w) = χ_[x_[0,n) = w]`, the indicator of the cylinder of `w` at coordinate 0.

Proof. The crossed product relation is `u f u^(-1) = f ∘ T^(-1)`, where `(Tx)_n = x_(n+1)`. So
`u^(-t) χ_a u^t = χ_a ∘ T^t = χ_[x_t = a]`. Locally constant functions commute, so the product is
`∏_t χ_[x_t = w_t]`. ∎

## 3. Proof of Theorem 1

By Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2, `W_w(σ_X)` is the image in `S_X` of `e_12(f)` with `f = χ_[x_[0,n) = w]`.
- `e_12(f)` is trivial in `S_X` iff `e_12(f) ∈ Z(G_X)`. The centre consists of scalar matrices
  (`subshift-elementary-group-is-simple-modulo-centre`), and the `(1,2)` entry of `e_12(f)` is `f`. So this
  happens iff `f = 0`.
- `LC(X,F_q) → R_X` is injective, so `f = 0` iff the cylinder `[x_[0,n) = w]` is empty.
- By shift invariance of `X`, the cylinder is nonempty iff `w` occurs in some point of `X`, i.e. iff
  `w ∈ L(X)`. ∎

## 4. Proof of Theorem 2 and Corollary 3

Suppose `φ : S_X → H` is an isomorphism, and put `t = φ(σ_X) ∈ H^I`. For every `w`,
`W_w(t) = φ(W_w(σ_X))`, so `W_w(t) = 1` iff `w ∉ L(X)` by Theorem 1. Hence `L(X) = {w : W_w(t) ≠ 1}` is
determined by `(H, t)`. A subshift is determined by its language:
`X = {x : every finite subword of x lies in L(X)}`. So the map `X ↦ t` from
`{X : S_X ≅ H}` (choosing one `φ` for each `X`) to `H^I` is injective.

`H ≅ S_X` is finitely generated, hence countable, and `I` is finite, so `H^I` is countable. That proves
Theorem 2.

Corollary 3. By §5, there are continuum many infinite minimal subshifts `X ⊆ {0,1}^Z`. The groups `S_X` fall
into isomorphism classes, each containing at most countably many `X` (Theorem 2). A continuum is not a countable
union of countable sets, so there are continuum many classes. Every `S_X` is infinite, finitely generated,
simple, Kazhdan and LEF (hence sofic and hyperlinear). It has no nontrivial finite quotient, since it is simple
and infinite. ∎

## 5. Continuum many infinite minimal binary subshifts (Sturmian codings)

Fix an irrational `α ∈ (0,1)` and the rotation `R_α(y) = y + α mod 1` on the circle `𝕋 = [0,1)`. Code
`y ∈ 𝕋` by `c(y)_n = 1` if `R_α^n y ∈ [1−α, 1)` and `0` otherwise. Let `X_α ⊆ {0,1}^Z` be the closure of
`{c(y) : y ∈ 𝕋}`.

1. **Cylinders are intervals.** For a word `w` of length `n`, the set `J_w = {y : c(y)_[0,n) = w}` is a finite
   intersection of half-open arcs `R_α^(-t)(I_(w_t))`. So it is a finite union of half-open arcs whose
   endpoints lie in `{−tα mod 1 : 0 ≤ t ≤ n}`.
2. **Legal words have positive-length cylinders.** If `w ∈ L(X_α)`, some `c(y)` contains `w`, hence (shifting)
   `J_w ≠ ∅`. A nonempty half-open arc has positive length.
3. **Uniform recurrence.** For an arc `J` of length `ℓ > 0`, the irrational rotation visits `J` from every
   `y` within a bounded time `N(ℓ)`: the finite orbit segment `{−tα}_(t<N)` is `ℓ`-dense for large `N`, by
   density of the orbit and compactness. So every legal `w` occurs in every `c(y)` with gaps at most
   `N(|J_w|) + n`.
4. **Minimality.** Uniform recurrence of every legal word passes to limits. So every point of `X_α` contains
   every word of `L(X_α)`, hence has dense orbit. The subshift `X_α` is minimal, and it is infinite because
   `c(y)` is not periodic (`α` is irrational).
5. **Frequency.** In every `c(y)` the frequency of `1` in windows of length `N` tends to
   `|[1−α,1)| = α`, uniformly in `y`, by Weyl equidistribution. This passes to all `x ∈ X_α`.
   So `X_α ∩ X_β = ∅` for `α ≠ β`, and `α ↦ X_α` is injective on irrationals in `(0,1)`. ∎

(These are the Sturmian subshifts of Morse–Hedlund. The self-contained argument above is what the proof route
uses.)

## 6. Scope notes and firewalls

- Theorem 1 uses only the scalar centre, injectivity of `LC(X) → R_X`, and the Steinberg relation. It does NOT
  use (T) or LEF. Those enter only in Corollary 3's list of properties.
- Theorem 2 is purely a cardinality argument. It does NOT identify which `S_X, S_Y` are isomorphic. Whether
  `S_X ≅ S_Y` forces conjugacy, flip conjugacy or strong orbit equivalence is open
  (`subshift-elementary-group-isomorphism-forces-orbit-equivalence`, landed with Attempts).
- Model test. For a periodic subshift, `R_X` is not simple, its centre is larger, and the scalar-centre step in
  §3 is unavailable. That is consistent with the hypothesis "infinite minimal".
