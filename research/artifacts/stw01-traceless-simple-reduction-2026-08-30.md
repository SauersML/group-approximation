# Problem I: traceless separable-simple reduction

A hypothetical failure of Kaplansky's quasitrace problem can be realized by
a separable unital simple stably finite algebra which has no tracial state.

The classical reduction first gives a finite AW*-factor `M` with faithful
nonadditive dimension quasitrace and no tracial state.  Absence of traces is
detected by the closed conditions

```text
phi(ab-ba)=0
```

inside the compact state space.  Compactness therefore selects finitely many
commutators which no state annihilates simultaneously.  The separable
subalgebra generated by those commutators and a pair witnessing quasitrace
nonadditivity has no trace: any trace would extend to a state on `M` and
contradict the finite obstruction.

Embedding that algebra into a separable simple subalgebra of the simple
finite AW*-factor preserves the finite no-trace obstruction.  The restricted
dimension quasitrace remains faithful and nonadditive and forces stable
finiteness in every matrix level.  Thus a counterexample needs neither a
large ideal lattice, nonseparability, nor even a linear trace; its essential
pathology can be concentrated in one separable simple traceless finite
algebra.
