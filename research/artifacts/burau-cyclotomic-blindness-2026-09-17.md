# Clean Moody counts are blind at every evaluation point; cyclotomic congruence kernels meet `Brun_4`

Lane: sw-095 (obstruction-miner), wave flagship-2026-09-17. Target: `zaremsky-3-05-four-strand-burau-faithful`.

## 0. Summary and graph map

The established obstruction `parity-clean-arc-counts-see-only-burau-mod-t-plus-one` shows that parity-clean
intersection counts see only `ρ_n mod (t+1)`, and `burau-minus-one-kernel-meets-brunnian-four-braids` gives a
nontrivial Brunnian 4-braid invisible to them. §10.2 of the artifact
`zp-burau-parity-t-minus-one-obstruction-2026-09-16.md` lists "values at other roots of unity" as a candidate
repair. This artifact kills that whole family, and every bounded-cyclotomic refinement of it.

- **Theorem 1** (`brunnian-4-braids-meet-cyclotomic-burau-congruence-kernels`). For every monic
  `g ∈ Z[t]` that is a product of cyclotomic polynomials there is an explicit `1 ≠ Ψ_g ∈ Brun_4` with
  `ρ_4(Ψ_g) ≡ I mod (t−1)^2 g`.
- **Theorem 2** (`clean-moody-counts-are-blind-at-every-evaluation-point`). Fix any `t_0 ∈ C^×` and call a
  transverse arc `t_0`-clean when its Moody polynomial has no cancellation at `t_0` (triangle equality). Then
  either no Brunnian braid is ever certified by `t_0`-clean counts (when `t_0/|t_0|` has odd or infinite
  order), or there is an explicit `1 ≠ Ψ ∈ Brun_4` that no `t_0`-clean count certificate sees, with conjugate
  powers and five-strand push maps allowed (when `t_0/|t_0|` has order `2^a r`, `a ≥ 1`, `r` odd).
- **Theorem 3** (part of the same claim). Any invariant of `𝕄_Γ mod (t−1)g`, with `g` as in Theorem 1, is
  unchanged by `Ψ_g` for all `Γ`. So finite jets of the Moody polynomial at finitely many roots of unity
  cannot certify faithfulness on `Brun_4` uniformly.

**What this kills.** Every repair of Section 6 of arXiv:2607.05283 that replaces the parity condition by a
"no cancellation at an evaluation point" condition, at any fixed point of `C^×`, and every certificate that
reads the Moody polynomial only modulo a fixed product of cyclotomic polynomials.

**What survives (sharpness, §7).** For each individual `Ψ ∉ ker ρ_4`, `ρ_4(Ψ) ≢ I mod Φ_q` for all but finitely
many `q`. So a surviving certificate must either let the evaluation point depend on the braid (with unbounded
order), or use information that is not a ring-quotient of `𝕄`: full cancellation-freeness `‖𝕄‖_1 = |A ∩ α|`
(Bigelow's criterion), weighted height counts `Σ r^{k_i}` with `r ≠ 1`, or extreme exponents.

**Calibration (§8).** In `B_3` the 3-point twist is central, and `[Δ_3^4, Brun_3] = 1`. The mechanism does not
produce an obstruction to parity in `B_3`, consistent with the verified `n = 3` proof of arXiv:2607.05283. For
orders `q = 2^a`, `a ≥ 2`, the obstruction already exists in `B_3`, where Burau is faithful: those certificate
classes are strictly weaker than faithfulness.

## 1. Conventions

the module `H = H_1(D̃, P̃_*)`, the unreduced Burau representation
`ρ_n(σ_i) = I ⊕ [[1−t, t], [1, 0]] ⊕ I`, and the Moody polynomial `𝕄(α, A) = Σ_i ε_i t^{k_i}` of a transverse
arc `A` crossing `α` at `q_1, …, q_m` with signs `ε_i` and heights `k_i`. `𝕄_Γ := 𝕄(α, (β_*^3)Γ)`.

- `Φ_q` is the `q`-th cyclotomic polynomial. `R = Z[t^{±1}]`.
- `A_ij`, `[x, y] = x y x^{-1} y^{-1}`, `b = [A_14, [A_24, A_34]]` as in [P] §8.
- `T_c` is the (positive) Dehn twist about a simple closed curve `c`. For the round curve `c_12` around
  `p_1, p_2` we have `T_{c_12} = σ_1^2 = A_12`. For the round curve `c_123` around `p_1, p_2, p_3` we have
  `T_{c_123} = Δ_3^2 = (σ_1σ_2)^3`.
- For an ideal `𝔞 ⊂ R`, `ρ_n(Ψ) ≡ I mod 𝔞` means every entry of `ρ_n(Ψ) − I` lies in `𝔞`. The set
  `G_n(𝔞) = {Ψ ∈ B_n : ρ_n(Ψ) ≡ I mod 𝔞}` is a normal subgroup (the kernel of `B_n → GL_n(R/𝔞)`).

We use from [P], all established there:

- **[P] Theorem A.** If `𝔞 = 𝔞(t^{-1})` and `ρ_n(Ψ) ≡ I mod 𝔞`, then for every `Γ ∈ B_n`,

## 2. Twist congruences

**Lemma 2.1 (power formula).** Let `X = I + (t − 1)N` with `N ∈ M_n(R)` and `N^2 = cN` for some `c ∈ R`. For
`j ≥ 1`, `X^j = I + e_j N` with `e_j = Σ_{i=1}^{j} C(j, i) (t−1)^i c^{i−1} ∈ R`, and
`c · e_j = (1 + (t − 1)c)^j − 1`.

*Proof.* `N^i = c^{i−1} N` for `i ≥ 1`, and `I` commutes with `N`, so the binomial theorem gives the first formula.
Multiplying by `c` gives the second. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (two-point twist).** `ρ_n(σ_1^2) = I + (t − 1)N_2`, where `N_2 = [[t, −t], [−1, 1]] ⊕ 0`, and
`N_2^2 = (1 + t) N_2`. Hence for `j ≥ 1`,
`ρ_n(σ_1^{2j}) = I + ((t^{2j} − 1)/(t + 1)) N_2`, and
`ρ_n(T_c^j) ≡ I mod (t^{2j} − 1)/Φ_2` for every curve `c` surrounding two marked points.

*Proof.* `[[1−t, t], [1, 0]]^2 = [[1 − t + t^2, t − t^2], [1 − t, t]]`, and subtracting `I` gives
`(t − 1)[[t, −t], [−1, 1]]`. Then `N_2^2 = [[t^2 + t, −t^2 − t], [−t − 1, t + 1]] = (1 + t)N_2`. By Lemma 2.1 with
`c = 1 + t`, `1 + (t − 1)c = t^2`, so `(1 + t)e_j = t^{2j} − 1`. `R` is a domain, so `e_j = (t^{2j} − 1)/(t + 1)`,
which lies in `R` because `t + 1 | t^{2j} − 1`. Any curve around two marked points is `y(c_12)` for some
`y ∈ B_n` (classification of surfaces), so `T_c^j = y σ_1^{2j} y^{-1}` and the congruence is conjugation
invariant. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (three-point twist).** `ρ_n(Δ_3^2) = I + (t − 1)N_3`, with
`N_3 = [[t^2 + t, −t, −t^2], [−1, t^2 + 1, −t^2], [−1, −t, t + 1]] ⊕ 0` and `N_3^2 = (1 + t + t^2) N_3`. Hence
`ρ_n(T_c^j) ≡ I mod (t^{3j} − 1)/Φ_3` for every curve `c` surrounding three marked points.

*Proof.* `ρ_3((σ_1σ_2)^3) − I` is printed by check 7 of the script:
`[[t^3 − t, t − t^2, t^2 − t^3], [1 − t, t^3 − t^2 + t − 1, t^2 − t^3], [1 − t, t − t^2, t^2 − 1]]`, which is
`(t − 1)N_3`. The identity `N_3^2 = (1 + t + t^2)N_3` is check 2 of the script (and a direct 3×3
multiplication; for instance the `(1,1)` entry is `(t^2+t)^2 + t + t^2 = (1 + t + t^2)(t^2 + t)`). Lemma 2.1
with `c = 1 + t + t^2` gives `1 + (t − 1)c = t^3`, so `e_j = (t^{3j} − 1)/Φ_3`. Conjugation invariance as
before. ∎

**Corollary 2.4.** `t^N − 1 = Π_{d | N} Φ_d` is squarefree. Therefore:
- `ρ_n(T_c^j) ≡ I mod Φ_q` for a two-point curve `c` whenever `q | 2j` and `q ≠ 2`;
- `ρ_n(T_c^j) ≡ I mod Φ_q` for a three-point curve `c` whenever `q | 3j` and `q ≠ 3`.

Put, for `q ≥ 1`:
- `X_1 = σ_1^2`;
- `X_2 = Δ_3^4 = T_{c_123}^2` (so `(t^6 − 1)/Φ_3 = Φ_1Φ_2Φ_6`; this is the braid `T` of [P] §8);
- `X_q = σ_1^{2 j_q}` for `q ≥ 3`, with `j_q = q/2` for even `q` and `j_q = q` for odd `q`.

Then `ρ_n(X_q) ≡ I mod Φ_q` for every `q ≥ 1`, and each `X_q` is a nonzero power of a Dehn twist about an
essential curve surrounding two or three of the marked points.

**Remark (sharpness of the choice).** The exponent of `Φ_2` in `(t^{2j} − 1)/(t + 1)` is `0`, and that of
`Φ_3` in `(t^{3j} − 1)/Φ_3` is `0`. The script confirms `ρ_4(σ_1^6) ≢ I mod Φ_2` and `ρ_4(Δ_3^6) ≢ I mod Φ_3`.
So two-point twists never help at `t = −1`, and three-point twists never help at primitive cube roots of
unity; each order needs the right kind of curve.

## 3. Congruence filtration and Brunnian braids

**Lemma 3.1 (commutators multiply ideals).** Let `X, Y ∈ GL_n(R)` with `X ≡ I mod 𝔞` and `Y ≡ I mod 𝔟`.
Then `[X, Y] ≡ I mod 𝔞𝔟`.

*Proof.* Write `X = I + x`, `Y = I + y`. Then `XY − YX = xy − yx` has entries in `𝔞𝔟`, and
`[X, Y] − I = (XY − YX) X^{-1} Y^{-1}`. ∎

**Lemma 3.2.** `ρ_n(P_n) ≡ I mod (t − 1)`.

*Proof.* `R/(t − 1) = Z` via `t ↦ 1`, and `ρ_n(σ_i)(1)` is the permutation matrix of `(i, i+1)`. So
`ρ_n(w)(1)` is the permutation matrix of the permutation of `w`, which is `I` for a pure braid. ∎

**Lemma 3.3.** For `n ≥ 3`, `Brun_n ⊆ [P_n, P_n]`.

*Proof.*
- For `i < j` let `F_ij : P_n → P_2 = ⟨σ_1^2⟩ ≅ Z` forget every strand except `i` and `j`. Put
  `Λ = (F_ij)_{i<j} : P_n → Z^{C(n,2)}`.
- `A_kl` becomes trivial when strand `k` or strand `l` is deleted. So `F_ij(A_kl) = 1` unless
  `{k, l} = {i, j}`, and `F_ij(A_ij) = σ_1^2` (deleting the strands that pass between gives the full twist of
  two strands). So `Λ(A_ij)` is the standard basis vector `e_ij`.
- The `A_ij` generate `P_n` (Artin), so `P_n^{ab}` is generated by `C(n,2)` elements. Composing a surjection
  `Z^{C(n,2)} → P_n^{ab}` (basis to generators) with the map induced by `Λ` gives the identity of
  `Z^{C(n,2)}`. So both maps are isomorphisms, and `ker Λ = [P_n, P_n]`.
- If `x ∈ Brun_n` and `i < j`, pick `k ∉ {i, j}` (possible as `n ≥ 3`). `F_ij` factors through deleting strand
  `k`, which kills `x`. So `Λ(x) = 0`. ∎

**Corollary 3.4.** For `n ≥ 3`, `Brun_n ⊆ G_n((t − 1)^2)`.

*Proof.* By Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 every commutator of pure braids lies in the subgroup `G_n((t − 1)^2)`, hence so
does `[P_n, P_n]`. Apply Lemma 3.3. ∎

Check 3 of the script confirms `ρ_4(b) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2` and `ρ_4(b) ≠ I`.

## 4. Theorem 1: cyclotomic congruence kernels meet `Brun_4`

**Theorem 1.** Let `g = Φ_{q_1} Φ_{q_2} ⋯ Φ_{q_r}` (`r ≥ 0`, repetitions allowed). Put `Ψ_0 = b` and
`Ψ_i = [X_{q_i}, Ψ_{i−1}]` for `1 ≤ i ≤ r`, and `Ψ_g := Ψ_r`. Then:
1. `Ψ_g ∈ Brun_4`;
2. `ρ_4(Ψ_g) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2 g`;
3. `Ψ_g ≠ 1`. So `Ψ_g` is a nontrivial element of `Brun_4 ∩ G_4((t − 1)^2 g)`.

Every monic `g ∈ Z[t]` all of whose roots are roots of unity has this form (Kronecker: a monic integer
polynomial with all roots on the unit circle and `g(0) ≠ 0` is a product of cyclotomic polynomials; here the
roots are roots of unity by hypothesis, so `g = Π_q Φ_q^{k_q}` by unique factorisation over `Q` and Gauss's
lemma).

*Proof.*
1. `b ∈ Brun_4` ([P] Proposition 8.1(a)). `Brun_4` is normal in `B_4`, so
   `[X, Ψ] = (X Ψ X^{-1}) Ψ^{-1} ∈ Brun_4` whenever `Ψ ∈ Brun_4`. Induct.
2. `ρ_4(b) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2` by Corollary 3.4, and `ρ_4(X_q) ≡ I mod Φ_q` by Corollary 2.4. Lemma 3.1 and
   induction give `ρ_4(Ψ_i) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2 Φ_{q_1} ⋯ Φ_{q_i}`.
3. Induction on `i`. `Ψ_0 = b ≠ 1` ([P] Proposition 8.1, structural backup: `A_14, A_24, A_34` are free
   generators of `ker F_4` and `b` is a basic commutator). Suppose `1 ≠ Ψ_{i−1} ∈ Brun_4`.
   - By the established claim `brunnian-four-braids-are-pseudo-anosov`, the image of `Ψ_{i−1}` in the mapping
     class group of the sphere `S` with the five marked points `p_1, …, p_4, ∞` is pseudo-Anosov.
   - `X_{q_i} = T_c^m` with `m ≠ 0` and `c` a curve around two or three of `p_1, …, p_4`. In `S`, `c` separates
     the five marked points 2|3 or 3|2, so it is essential, and the image of `T_c^m` is `T_c^m ≠ 1`.
   - If `Ψ_i = 1`, then in `Mod(S)`: `T_{Ψ_{i−1}(c)}^m = Ψ_{i−1} T_c^m Ψ_{i−1}^{-1} = T_c^m`. Powers of Dehn
     twists about essential curves satisfy `T_a^m = T_{a'}^m`, `m ≠ 0` ⇒ `a = a'` (Farb–Margalit, *A Primer on
     Mapping Class Groups*, Ch. 3; locator not re-checked). So `Ψ_{i−1}(c) = c`, and a pseudo-Anosov class fixes
     no essential curve. Contradiction. ∎

**Certificate.** Check 4 of the script verifies, for `g = Φ_q` and `q = 2, …, 24`, that
`ρ_4(Ψ_{Φ_q}) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2 Φ_q`, `ρ_4(Ψ_{Φ_q}) ≠ I` (an independent proof of `Ψ ≠ 1` for these `q`), and that
`Ψ_{Φ_q}` is pure. Check 5 does the same for the jets `g = Φ_2^2`, `Φ_4^2 Φ_3` and `Φ_1Φ_2^2Φ_8`, with the
modulus `(t − 1)^2 g`. For `q = 2` the element is `Φ_0` of [P].
- The Artin-action Brunnian control of [P] is not repeated for `q ≥ 3`: the free-group images of `Ψ_{Φ_2}`
  already have about `10^6` letters. Brunnian-ness is the two-line normality argument above.

For `q = 2` and `X_2 = T_c^2`, Theorem 1 is the construction of [P] §10.2 (with modulus `(t − 1)^2 Φ_2` instead
of `Φ_2`). The new content is that every other cyclotomic factor, and every finite product of them, is
reached by the same commutator mechanism once the right kind of curve (two-point or three-point) is used.

## 5. Clean representatives at an evaluation point

Let `A` be a transverse arc crossing `α` at `q_1, …, q_m` with signs `ε_i ∈ {±1}` and heights `k_i ∈ Z`, so
`𝕄(α, A) = Σ_i ε_i t^{k_i}` ([P] §1). The heights are fixed up to a common shift and the signs up to a common
flip; nothing below depends on those choices.

**Definition 5.1.** Fix `t_0 ∈ C^×` and put `u = t_0/|t_0|`. The arc `A` is **`t_0`-clean** if
`|𝕄(α, A)(t_0)| = Σ_i |t_0|^{k_i}`, i.e. the sum `Σ_i ε_i t_0^{k_i}` has no cancellation. A **`t_0`-clean count
certificate** for `Ψ ∈ B_n` is a pair `(Γ, A', B')` with `Γ ∈ B_n`, `A'` a `t_0`-clean transverse representative
of `A = (β_*^3)ΨΓ`, `B'` a `t_0`-clean transverse representative of `B = (β_*^3)Γ`, and `|A' ∩ α| ≠ |B' ∩ α|`.

For `t_0 = −1` this is exactly the parity condition, by [P] Lemma 6.2 (1) ⇔ (3), and a certificate is the
input of [P] Theorem B. Common shifts and flips multiply both sides of the defining equality by the same
positive number, so the definition is well posed.

**Lemma 5.2 (cleanness is an argument condition).** `A` is `t_0`-clean iff `ε_i u^{k_i}` is independent of `i`.
In that case `𝕄(α, A)(u) = m ω` with `ω = ε_1 u^{k_1}`, so `|A ∩ α| = m = |𝕄(α, A)(u)|`.

*Proof.* The complex numbers `z_i = ε_i t_0^{k_i}` are nonzero with `|z_i| = |t_0|^{k_i}` and
`z_i/|z_i| = ε_i u^{k_i}`. Equality `|Σ z_i| = Σ |z_i|` holds iff all `z_i/|z_i|` coincide (for `m = 0` both
conditions are vacuous). Then `Σ_i ε_i u^{k_i} = m ω` and `|ω| = 1`. ∎

So `t_0`-cleanness and the count it certifies depend only on the point `u` of the unit circle.

**Lemma 5.3 (classification).** Let `A` be `t_0`-clean and `u = t_0/|t_0|`.
1. If `u` has infinite order or odd order, then all `ε_i` are equal, and `|A ∩ α| = |𝕄(α, A)(1)|`.
2. If `u` has order `N = 2^a r` with `a ≥ 1` and `r` odd, let `ζ` be any primitive `2^a`-th root of unity.
   Then `A` is also `ζ`-clean, and `|A ∩ α| = |𝕄(α, A)(ζ)|`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 5.2, `u^{k_i − k_j} = ε_i ε_j ∈ {±1}` for all `i, j`.
1. If `u` has infinite order, `u^d = ±1` forces `d = 0`, so `ε_i ε_j = u^0 = 1`. If `u` has odd order, `−1` is
   not a power of `u` (the group `⟨u⟩` has odd order and `−1` has order 2), so again `ε_i ε_j = 1`. All signs
   agree, so `𝕄(α, A)(1) = Σ_i ε_i = ± m`.
2. From `u^{2(k_i − k_j)} = 1` we get `N | 2(k_i − k_j)`, so `k_i − k_j = (N/2) c` with `c ∈ Z`, and
   `ε_i ε_j = u^{(N/2)c} = (−1)^c` because `u^{N/2} = −1`. Now `ζ^{2^{a−1}} = −1` and `N/2 = 2^{a−1} r`, so
   `ζ^{k_i − k_j} = (−1)^{rc} = (−1)^c = ε_i ε_j`. Hence `ε_i ζ^{k_i} = ε_j ζ^{k_j}`, and Lemma 5.2 applies at
   `ζ`. ∎

## 6. Theorem 2: clean counts are blind

**Lemma 6.1 (blindness transfer).** Let `𝔞 ⊂ R` be generated by a product of the polynomials `t − 1` and
`Φ_q`, `Ψ ∈ B_n` with `ρ_n(Ψ) ≡ I mod 𝔞`, `Γ ∈ B_n`, and `A = (β_*^3)ΨΓ`, `B = (β_*^3)Γ`.
1. If `𝔞 ⊆ ((t − 1)^2)`, then `𝕄(α, A)(1) = 𝕄(α, B)(1)`.
2. If `𝔞 ⊆ (Φ_Q)` with `Q ≥ 2` and `ζ` is a primitive `Q`-th root of unity, then
   `𝕄(α, A)(ζ) = 𝕄(α, B)(ζ)`.

*Proof.* `𝔞` is self-reciprocal (§1), so [P] Theorem A and Lemma 3.1 give
`(1 − t^s)(𝕄(α, A) − 𝕄(α, B)) ∈ 𝔞`.
1. `1 − t^s` is a unit times `t − 1` (`1 − t = −(t − 1)`, `1 − t^{-1} = t^{-1}(t − 1)`). `R` is a UFD and `t − 1` is prime, so
   `𝕄(α, A) − 𝕄(α, B) ∈ (t − 1)`, i.e. the difference vanishes at `t = 1`.
2. Evaluate at `ζ` (a ring map `R → C` killing `Φ_Q`): `(1 − ζ^s)(𝕄(α, A)(ζ) − 𝕄(α, B)(ζ)) = 0`, and
   `ζ^s ≠ 1` because `Q ≥ 2` and `s = ±1`. ∎

**Theorem 2.** Fix `t_0 ∈ C^×`, `u = t_0/|t_0|`. Let `n ≥ 4` and `f : B_4 → B_n` the standard inclusion.
1. If `u` has infinite or odd order, then no element of `[P_n, P_n]` (in particular no element of `Brun_n`,
   and no push `f(y Ψ^k y^{-1})` of a Brunnian 4-braid) has a `t_0`-clean count certificate in `B_n`.
2. If `u` has order `2^a r` with `a ≥ 1` and `r` odd, put `Ψ = Ψ_{Φ_{2^a}} ∈ Brun_4 ∖ {1}` (Theorem 1). Then for
   all `k ∈ Z`, `y ∈ B_4` and `Γ ∈ B_n`, any `t_0`-clean transverse representatives of
   `A = (β_*^3) f(y Ψ^k y^{-1}) Γ` and `B = (β_*^3) Γ` cross `α` equally often. The same holds for every element
   of the normal subgroup `Brun_4 ∩ G_4(Φ_{2^a})`, which is nontrivial.

*Proof.*
1. `[P_n, P_n] ⊆ G_n((t − 1)^2)` (proof of Corollary 3.4). For pushes: `ρ_n ∘ f = ρ_4 ⊕ I_{n−4}` (for `n = 5`
   this is [P] Proposition 9.1; for general `n` the same generator check, since `ρ_n(σ_i)` for `i ≤ 3` is
   `ρ_4(σ_i) ⊕ I`). So `f` maps `G_4(𝔞)` into `G_n(𝔞)`, and `yΨ^k y^{-1} ∈ Brun_4 ⊆ G_4((t−1)^2)` by normality.
   Let `A', B'` be `t_0`-clean representatives. By Lemma 5.3(1), Corollary 3.2 of [P] and Lemma 6.1(1),
   `|A' ∩ α| = |𝕄(α, A)(1)| = |𝕄(α, B)(1)| = |B' ∩ α|`.
2. `ρ_4(Ψ) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2 Φ_{2^a}` by Theorem 1, and `G_4(𝔞)` is a normal subgroup, so
   `ρ_4(y Ψ^k y^{-1}) ≡ I mod Φ_{2^a}` and `ρ_n(f(yΨ^k y^{-1})) ≡ I mod Φ_{2^a}`. By Lemma 5.3(2), Lemma 6.1(2)
   with `Q = 2^a`, and invariance: `|A' ∩ α| = |𝕄(α, A)(ζ)| = |𝕄(α, B)(ζ)| = |B' ∩ α|`. ∎

**Remarks.**
- Part 2 gives a single braid for all odd `r` at once: `u = e^{2πi/6}` and `u = −1` are both defeated by
  `Ψ_{Φ_2}`, and `u = e^{2πi/12}` by `Ψ_{Φ_4}`.
- Part 1 includes `t_0 > 0` real (`u = 1`, "all signs equal", i.e. algebraic intersection equals geometric)
  and all `t_0` with `u` not a root of unity. These certificates see only the abelianised pure braid (the
  winding numbers), not `Brun_n`.
- Mixing finitely many points does not help. If `A` and `B` may use representatives that are clean at points
  `u_0`, `u_1`, … taken from a fixed finite set, let `g` be the product of `Φ_{2^a}` over the orders `2^a r` that
  occur. Then `Ψ_g` of Theorem 1 satisfies both hypotheses of Lemma 6.1 for every point in the set, so every
  count `|𝕄(α, ·)(1)|` and `|𝕄(α, ·)(ζ_{2^a})|` is the same for `A` and `B`. Any comparison of these counts
  is blind to `Ψ_g`.

## 7. Theorem 3 (jets) and sharpness

**Theorem 3.** Let `g` be a product of cyclotomic polynomials, `Ψ_g` as in Theorem 1, `n ≥ 4`, `k ∈ Z`,
`y ∈ B_4` and `Γ ∈ B_n`. Then `𝕄_{f(y Ψ_g^k y^{-1}) Γ} ≡ 𝕄_Γ mod (t − 1) g`. So every function of the residue
`𝕄 mod (t − 1) g` (for instance the values and first `e_q − 1` derivatives at primitive `q`-th roots of unity,
where `e_q` is the multiplicity of `Φ_q` in `g`) takes the same value on the two arcs.

*Proof.* As in Theorem 2, `ρ_n(f(yΨ_g^k y^{-1})) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2 g`. Theorem A gives
`(1 − t^s)(𝕄_{f(…)Γ} − 𝕄_Γ) ∈ ((t − 1)^2 g)`. Cancel the unit multiple `1 − t^s` of the prime `t − 1` in the
UFD `R`. ∎

So a certificate that reads only a fixed finite jet of `𝕄` at finitely many roots of unity cannot certify
all of `Brun_4`: it is blind to `Ψ_g` for the corresponding `g`.

**Proposition 7.1 (the obstruction is about uniformity).** Let `Ψ ∈ B_n` with `ρ_n(Ψ) ≠ I`.
1. `ρ_n(Ψ) ≢ I mod Φ_q` for all but finitely many `q`.
2. If `𝕄_{ΨΓ} ≠ 𝕄_Γ` for some `Γ`, then `𝕄_{ΨΓ}(ζ_q) ≠ 𝕄_Γ(ζ_q)` for all but finitely many `q` (any
   primitive `ζ_q`).

*Proof.* A nonzero Laurent polynomial `p` has finitely many roots, and `Φ_q | p` means `p(ζ_q) = 0`. Apply this
to a nonzero entry of `ρ_n(Ψ) − I`, respectively to `𝕄_{ΨΓ} − 𝕄_Γ`. ∎

Hence, if `ρ_4` is faithful, every `1 ≠ Ψ ∈ Brun_4` is seen at almost every root of unity at the level of
`ρ_4(Ψ)` and of Moody polynomials (Moody's criterion). What Theorems 2 and 3 exclude is a **fixed** point or a
fixed finite jet serving all Brunnian braids. What they do not exclude:

- **Braid-dependent evaluation points.** A proof that, for each `Ψ`, finds `Γ` and representatives clean at a
  point `ζ` whose order grows with `Ψ`. By Lemma 5.3 only orders `2^a` matter, so `a` must be unbounded along
  the Brunnian braids considered. This is the natural surviving extension of the preprint's Section 6.
- **Certificates that are not ring quotients of `𝕄`.** Bigelow's full no-cancellation criterion
  `‖𝕄(α, A)‖_1 = |A ∩ α|` (it forces minimal position but is not an evaluation at a point), extreme exponents
  (span of `𝕄`), or weighted height counts `Σ_i r^{k_i}` with `r > 0`, `r ≠ 1`. A representative that is clean
  at `t_0` with `|t_0| ≠ 1` carries the weighted number `Σ_i |t_0|^{k_i} = |𝕄(α, A)(t_0)|`. Theorem 2 covers its
  crossing count (which equals `|𝕄(α, A)(u)|`), not this weighted number. They depend on `𝕄` through
  `|𝕄(α, A)(t_0)|`, which is not a residue modulo cyclotomic factors, so Theorems 2 and 3 say nothing about them. Such weighted counts are the second surviving class.

## 8. Calibration: `B_3`

- `ρ_3` is faithful (classical). In `B_3` the three-point twist `Δ_3^2` is central, so `[Δ_3^4, Ψ] = 1` for
  all `Ψ`, and the case `q = 2` of the mechanism produces nothing (check 6b). This is consistent with the
  verified `n = 3` part of arXiv:2607.05283, which uses parity (`u = −1`).
- For `q = 3, 4, 8` the braid `Ψ_3 = [A_12^{j_q}, [A_13, A_23]]` is Brunnian (normality; `[A_13, A_23]` is
  Brunnian by the Artin-action control), satisfies `ρ_3(Ψ_3) ≡ I mod (t − 1)^2 Φ_q`, and has `ρ_3(Ψ_3) ≠ I`
  (check 6). By the proof of Theorem 2(2), `u`-clean count certificates with `u` of order `4r` or `8r` (`r` odd)
  are blind to a nontrivial Brunnian 3-braid, although `ρ_3` is faithful.
- Likewise, by Theorem 2(1), sign-coherent certificates (`u` of odd or infinite order) are blind to all of
  `[P_3, P_3] ⊇ Brun_3`.
- So the method does not "prove" a false statement: it never claims that `Ψ` lies in a kernel. It shows that
  these certificate classes are strictly weaker than faithfulness, already in `B_3`, except at `u` of order
  `2r`, where `B_3` gives no obstruction and `B_4` does (Theorem 2(2) with `a = 1`, i.e. [P]).
