Journal page:

## 3. Pestov (l.41, l.150-152)
Source: arXiv:0804.3968v8 (4 Aug 2008), which "incorporates minor corrections made in the Bulletin of Symbolic Logic
galley proofs". p. 21:
> Open question 9.1 (Cf. Ozawa [64]). Let G be an infinite simple group with Kazhdan's property (T). Can it be
> hyperlinear (sofic)?

Numbering in the v8 source: `\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}[section]`, with every environment on that counter. §3
"Definitions" contains:
- Definition 3.1: sofic = "isomorphic to a subgroup of a metric ultraproduct of a suitable family of symmetric groups
  of finite rank with their normalized Hamming distances";
- Definition 3.2: hyperlinear, the same with unitary groups and normalized Hilbert–Schmidt distances;
- Theorem 3.3: "(Elek and Szabó [ES]) Every sofic group is hyperlinear". The proof compares the Hamming and
  Hilbert–Schmidt distances of permutation matrices.

The guide never mentions LEF.

Verdict: VERIFIED for l.41. l.150-152 is correct as cited; [Pestov, Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.3] would locate it.
The journal numbering is assumed equal to v8's; the journal PDF was not seen.

## 5. Grigorchuk–Medynets (l.57-58)
Source: arXiv:1105.0719v4 (2 Jul 2012). p. 2:
> One of the main techniques in Cantor dynamics is the method of periodic approximation, which mimics the behaviour
> of (X,T) by periodic transformations. This roughly means that there exists a refining sequence of clopen
> (Kakutani-Rokhlin) partitions {Ξ_n} (Section 3) such that the action of T can be seen as nearly a permutation of
> Ξ_n-atoms. [...] We then use Theorem 4.7 to establish that the topological full group of a Cantor minimal system
> is locally embeddable into finite groups (LEF groups) (Theorem 2.6).

p. 5:
> Definition 2.5. A group G is called locally embeddable into finite groups (abbr. LEF) if for every finite set
> F ⊂ G there is a finite group H and a map φ : G → H such that (a) φ is injective on F and (2) φ(gh) = φ(g)φ(h)
> for every g, h ∈ F. The notion of an LEF group was introduced by Vershik and Stepin in 1980's [...] We refer the
> reader to [VG] and [CSC, Chapter 7] [...]
> Theorem 2.6. The topological full group of any Cantor minimal system is an LEF group.

Verdict: FIX (credit precision).
- GM present periodic approximation as a standard technique of Cantor dynamics. What is theirs is its use to prove
  LEF (Theorem 2.6).
- "the periodic-approximation mechanism of Grigorchuk--Medynets" assigns the mechanism itself to them.

## 6. Thom (l.63-65)
Source: arXiv:0810.2180v1. p. 3:
> In this note we describe explicitly a finitely generated hyperlinear (in fact locally embeddable into finite
> groups (LEF), hence sofic) Kazhdan group which is not residually finite and hence does not have the factorization
> property. In fact, we show that our example admits a surjective and non-injective endomorphism (i.e. it is
> non-hopfian) [...]

p. 4:
> Theorem 1.4. There exists a finitely generated Kazhdan group G which is locally embeddable into finite groups (in
> particular sofic and hence hyperlinear) but does not have the factorization property.

Theorem 1.1 (Kirchberg), pp. 2-3: for a Kazhdan group, the factorization property, residual finiteness, and embedding
in the unitary group of the hyperfinite II_1 factor are equivalent.

Verdict: VERIFIED.
- "Not residually finite" is Theorem 1.4 read through Theorem 1.1, and p. 3 says it directly.
- "His example is not simple" holds: a non-hopfian group has a nontrivial proper normal subgroup, namely the kernel of
  its surjective, non-injective endomorphism.
- The journal numbering (GGD) was not checked.

## 7. BCFS, Clark–Edie-Michell, Steinberg (l.102-104)
- BCFS, arXiv:1204.3127v2. The abstract says the complex *-algebra "as introduced by Steinberg, is simple if and only
  if G is both effective and minimal", and p. 2 points to Theorem 4.1. This covers ℂ only.
- Clark–Edie-Michell, arXiv:1403.4684v1.
  - p. 7: "Corollary 4.6. Let G be a Hausdorff, ample groupoid and R a commutative ring with identity. Then A_R(G)
    is simple if and only if G is effective and minimal, and R is a field."
  - p. 8: "Theorem 5.2. [...] (1) Suppose G is effective and minimal, and G^(0) is compact. Then
    Z(A_R(G)) = R1_{G^(0)}."
- Steinberg, arXiv:1408.6014v2.
  - p. 10: "Corollary 3.6. Let 𝒢 be a Hausdorff ample groupoid and k a field. Then k𝒢 is simple if and only if 𝒢
    is effective and minimal."
  - p. 10: Proposition 3.7 says that for effective Hausdorff ample 𝒢, Z(k𝒢) consists of the compactly supported
    functions on 𝒢^(0) that are constant on orbits. If 𝒢^(0) has a dense orbit and is compact, Z(k𝒢) = k·χ_{𝒢^(0)}.
  - p. 7: "The results of this section were first obtained in [4] for Hausdorff groupoids over k = ℂ".
- R is the algebra of the transformation groupoid of the free minimal Z-action. That groupoid is Hausdorff, ample,
  effective and minimal, with compact unit space; see item 8.

Verdict: VERIFIED as credit.
- Over F_2 the results actually used are CEM Corollary 4.6 with Theorem 5.2(1), and Steinberg Corollary 3.6 with
  Proposition 3.7. BCFS is the complex origin.
- Pointers to these results would help the reader.

## 9. Stepanov (l.62)
Quotes from main's node (Springer PDF pp. 2151-2152, read by un-stepanov-credit):
> Lemma 4.1. "Let F be a subgroup of GL(n,R) normalized by E(n,R) and let c ∈ F. Assume that F does not contain
> elementary transvections. If for some unimodular column α ∈ R^n and some indices i, j ∈ {1,…,n}, the elements
> α_i and (cα)_j are equal to zero, then c is a central matrix."
> Lemma 4.3. "Let F be a subgroup of GL(n,R) normalized by E(n,R), ξ ∈ R, and c ∈ F. Assume that F does not contain
> nontrivial elementary transvections and ξc_ij = 0 for some indices i, j. Then ξ ∈ I^⊥(c)."

The Springer abstract describes the paper as studying "normal subgroups of the general linear group over a ring and
the centrality of the extension St(n,R) → E(n,R)", using "the notions of the standard commutator formula and the
standard normal structure".

Verdict: VERIFIED as a relevant source.
- "The normal-structure tradition described by Stepanov" is vague. Stepanov proves extraction lemmas (4.1 and 4.3)
  and a standard normal structure theorem (4.4), and a pointer to the lemmas is more useful.
- Outside this lane: `research/artifacts/un-stepanov-credit-2026-09-13.md` classifies the extraction step of the
  Steinberg-algebra criterion as prior (Lemma 4.3). The note's tower argument extracts transvections through the
  finite simple groups GL_d(F_2), which is a different mechanism. Whether "The new step is the simplicity argument"
  (l.58) needs qualifying is for sk-novelty to decide.
