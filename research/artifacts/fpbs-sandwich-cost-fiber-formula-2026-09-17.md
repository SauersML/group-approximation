# Fiber formula for relative sandwich cost over a finite transitive factor

Worker swarm-0917-w11-w11-fp-break, 2026-09-17 (wave 11).

## 0. Notation

For a countable group `Δ`, a p.m.p. action `b` of `Δ` on `(X, μ)` and a
subgroup `K ≤ Δ`, write

    p^Δ_K(b) = inf { C(E) : E_{b|K} ⊆ E ⊆ E_b },

the infimum over measurable (automatically p.m.p.) equivalence relations
`E` sandwiched between the orbit relation of the restriction `b|K` and the
orbit relation of `b`. When `K = ⟨F⟩` for a finite `F`, this is the
sandwich cost `p^Δ_F(b)` of `fpbs-relative-sandwich-cost-bernoulli-lower-bound`.
`C` is Levitt–Gaboriau cost, normalised so that `C(E) ≥ 1` for aperiodic
`E` on a probability space.

For a finite-index subgroup `N ≤ Δ` with `d = [Δ : N]`, let `Δ ↷ Δ/N` be
the left translation action, with uniform measure, and for a p.m.p. action
`b` of `Δ` on `(X, μ)` let

    b_N := b × (Δ ↷ Δ/N)    on   (X × Δ/N, μ × unif).

If `b` is free, so is `b_N`.

The only imported input is the graph node
`gaboriau-induction-formula-complete-section` (ESTABLISHED import;
Gaboriau's lecture notes Proposition 2.33, quoting [Gab00]): "Let Y ⊂ X be
a complete section of the p.m.p. equivalence relation R. The cost of R and
the cost of the restriction R↾Y are related according to the following
formula: C(R) − 1 = µ(Y)(C(R↾Y) − 1). ... the cost of R↾Y is computed
using the restricted normalized measure µY = µ↾Y / µ(Y)." We write this as

    C(R) − 1 = μ(A) · ( C_{μ_A}(R|A) − 1 ).                             (IF)

## 1. The fiber formula

**Theorem 1.** Let `Δ` be a countable group, `K ≤ Δ`, and `N ≤ Δ` of
finite index `d` with `K N = Δ` (equivalently `K` acts transitively on
`Δ/N`). Let `b` be any p.m.p. action of `Δ` on a standard probability
space `(X, μ)`. Then

    p^Δ_K(b_N) = 1 + ( p^N_{K∩N}(b|N) − 1 ) / d.

Here `b|N` is the restriction of `b` to `N`, acting on `(X, μ)`, and the
formula holds in `[0, ∞]` (both sides infinite together).

More precisely, restriction `E ↦ E|A` to `A = X × {N}` is a bijection from
the sandwiches of `(b_N, K)` onto the sandwiches of `(b|N, K∩N)`, with
inverse `E' ↦ E' ∨ E_{b_N|K}` (after identifying `A` with `X`), and

    C(E) − 1 = ( C(E|A) − 1 ) / d    for every such `E`.                (1)


**Proof.** Write `Y = X × Δ/N`, `ν = μ × unif`, `A = X × {N}`,
`R = E_{b_N}` and `T = E_{b_N|K}`. So `ν(A) = 1/d`.

*Step 1: the two restrictions.* For `γ ∈ Δ`, `γ·(x, N) = (γx, γN)` lies
in `A` iff `γ ∈ N`. Hence `R|A = {((x,N),(γx,N)) : γ ∈ N}`, which is
`E_{b|N}` under `A ≅ X`, `(x, N) ↦ x`. Likewise `T|A` is `E_{b|K∩N}`.
This identification carries `ν_A` to `μ`.

*Step 2: `A` is a complete section of `T`.* Given `(x, γN)`, transitivity
gives `k ∈ K` with `kγN = N`. Then `k·(x, γN) ∈ A`.

*Step 3: the bijection.* Let `E` satisfy `T ⊆ E ⊆ R`. Then
`T|A ⊆ E|A ⊆ R|A`, so `E|A` is a sandwich of `(b|N, K∩N)` by Step 1.
Also `E = E|A ∨ T`. The inclusion `⊇` is clear. For `⊆`, take `(y, z) ∈ E`
and use Step 2 to choose `y' , z' ∈ A` with `(y, y'), (z, z') ∈ T`. Then
`(y', z') ∈ E|A`, so `(y, z)` is in the relation generated by
`T ∪ E|A`.

Conversely, let `E'` be a relation on `A` with `T|A ⊆ E' ⊆ R|A`, and put
`E = E' ∨ T`. Then `T ⊆ E ⊆ R`. We claim `E|A = E'`. The inclusion
`E' ⊆ E|A` is clear. Take `(y, z) ∈ E|A`. It is joined by a finite chain
`y = y_0, y_1, …, y_m = z` whose steps lie alternately in `T` and in `E'`.
Merge consecutive `T` steps using transitivity of `T`. Each `E'` step has
both endpoints in `A`, since `E'` lives on `A`. So every maximal `T`
segment starts and ends in `A`: at `y` or `z`, or at an endpoint of an `E'`
step. Such a segment therefore lies in `T|A ⊆ E'`, and the whole chain lies
in `E'`. So `(y, z) ∈ E'`.

The two maps `E ↦ E|A` and `E' ↦ E' ∨ T` are therefore mutually inverse
bijections between the two sets of sandwiches.

*Step 4: costs.* `A` is a complete section of `T ⊆ E`, hence of `E`.
By (IF),

    C(E) − 1 = ν(A)(C_{ν_A}(E|A) − 1) = (C(E|A) − 1)/d.

This is (1). Take infima over the bijection of Step 3 and use Step 1 to
identify `(A, ν_A, R|A, T|A)` with `(X, μ, E_{b|N}, E_{b|K∩N})`. This gives
the theorem. ∎

**Remarks.**
1. Nothing about `b` is used beyond being p.m.p. If `b` is free, then
   `b_N` is free, and `b|N` is a free action of `N`.
2. For the Bernoulli shift `s_Δ` on `[0,1]^Δ`, the restriction `s_Δ|N` is
   conjugate to the Bernoulli shift `s_N` of `N` with base
   `([0,1]^{Δ/N}, Leb)`, which is isomorphic to `([0,1], Leb)`. Choose a
   transversal `τ` for `N∖Δ` and write `[0,1]^Δ = ([0,1]^τ)^N`. So
   `p^N_{K∩N}(s_Δ|N) = p^N_{K∩N}(s_N)`.
3. If `K` is finitely generated, so is `K ∩ N`, since it has finite index
   in `K`. Hence both sides are sandwich costs `p_F` for finite `F`, as in
   `fpbs-relative-sandwich-cost-bernoulli-lower-bound`.

## 2. Consequences for the relative Bernoulli lower bound (RBL)

RBL for `(Δ, K)` is the statement that `p^Δ_K(s_Δ) ≤ p^Δ_K(b)` for every
free p.m.p. action `b` of `Δ`
(`fpbs-relative-sandwich-cost-bernoulli-lower-bound`, with `K = ⟨F⟩`).

**Corollary 2 (RBL forces virtual upper bounds; unconditional part).**
Let `Δ, K, N, d` be as in Theorem 1, with `K` finitely generated.

(a) Unconditionally, `p^Δ_K(s_Δ × Δ/N) = 1 + (p^N_{K∩N}(s_N) − 1)/d`.

(b) If RBL holds for `(Δ, K)`, then

        p^Δ_K(s_Δ) ≤ 1 + (p^N_{K∩N}(s_N) − 1)/d.                       (2)

(c) If `Δ` is free and `M'` is a finitely generated subgroup with
    `K∩N ≤ M' ≤ N`, then `p^N_{K∩N}(s_N) ≤ rk M'`. So RBL for `(Δ, K)` gives

        p^Δ_K(s_Δ) ≤ 1 + (mrk_N(K∩N) − 1)/d,                           (3)

    where `mrk_N(H)` is the least rank of a subgroup `M'` with
    `H ≤ M' ≤ N`.

*Proof.* (a) is Theorem 1 with `b = s_Δ` and Remark 2. For (b), `s_Δ × Δ/N`
is free, so RBL applies to it; now use (a). For (c), `M'` is free of finite
rank `m`, being a subgroup of a free group. `s_N|M'` is a free action of
`M'`, and its orbit relation `E_{s_N|M'}` is a sandwich for
`(s_N, K∩N)`. The graphing given by a free basis of `M'` is a treeing of
it, because the action is free, and it has cost `m`. By
`gaboriau-treeable-free-action-realizes-min-cost` item 1 ("If Ψ is a
treeing of a p.m.p. equivalence relation R, then C(Ψ) = C(R)"), we get
`C(E_{s_N|M'}) = m`. ∎

**Corollary 3 (index multiplicativity; uses a non-node input).** Assume
Proposition 2.5 of `research/artifacts/fpbs-sandwich-cost-quantifier-shift-2026-09-17.md`:
for countably infinite `Δ` and free `b`, `p^Δ_F(b) ≤ p^Δ_F(s_Δ)`. This is
recorded there, not as a graph node, and it rests on Abért–Weiss and
Tucker-Drob openness. If RBL holds for `(Δ, K)` with `Δ` infinite, then
for every `N` as in Theorem 1,

    p^N_{K∩N}(s_N) − 1 = [Δ : N] · ( p^Δ_K(s_Δ) − 1 ).                 (4)

*Proof.* Under RBL and Proposition 2.5, `p^Δ_K` is constant on free
actions. In particular `p^Δ_K(s_Δ) = p^Δ_K(s_Δ × Δ/N)`. Now use
Corollary 2(a). ∎

For `K = Δ`, (4) reads `C(s_N) − 1 = d (C(s_Δ) − 1)`. This is the Bernoulli
instance of multiplicativity of cost under finite index. Here it comes out
of RBL; it is not claimed unconditionally. For proper `K`, (4) is a new and
falsifiable consequence of RBL: *Bernoulli sandwich cost minus one is
multiplicative under passing to finite-index subgroups that `K` still
surjects onto*.

**Reduction (RBL on finite-factor actions).** By Theorem 1, for every free
`b`,

    p^Δ_K(b_N) = 1 + (p^N_{K∩N}(b|N) − 1)/d.

So RBL for `(N, K∩N)` implies that `s_Δ × Δ/N` minimises `p^Δ_K` among
all actions of the form `b_N` with `b` free. The reason is that
`s_Δ|N ≅ s_N` and `b|N` is free. Hence RBL for `(Δ, K)`, restricted to
actions with a `Δ/N` factor on which `K` is transitive, is equivalent to
the single inequality

    p^Δ_K(s_Δ) ≤ p^Δ_K(s_Δ × Δ/N),

given RBL for `(N, K∩N)`.

## 3. The Hanna Neumann seal on virtually group-generated sandwiches

**Definition.** In the setting of Theorem 1, a sandwich `E` of `(b_N, K)`
is *virtually group-generated* if `E|A = E_{b|M'}` for some subgroup `M'`
with `K∩N ≤ M' ≤ N`, under `A ≅ X`. By Theorem 1, `E` is then
`E_{b|M'} ∨ E_{b_N|K}`.

**Theorem 4.** Let `Δ` be a free group, `K ≤ Δ` a subgroup of rank 2, and
`N ≤ Δ` of finite index `d` with `K N = Δ`. Put `H = K ∩ N`.

(i) `[K : H] = d` and `rk H = 1 + d`.

(ii) `K ∩ M' = H` for every `M'` with `H ≤ M' ≤ N`.

(iii) Every such `M'` has `rk M' ≥ 1 + d`, with `rk M' = ∞` allowed.

(iv) Let `b` be a free p.m.p. action of `Δ`. Then every virtually
group-generated sandwich `E` of `(b_N, K)` has `C(E) ≥ 2`, and `E = E_{b_N}`
attains 2 when `Δ = F_2`. If moreover `Δ = F_2`, every sandwich `E_{b|M}` of
`(b, K)` with `K ≤ M ≤ F_2` has `C(E_{b|M}) = rk M ≥ 2`, and `M = F_2`
attains 2.

*Proof.* (i) `K` acts transitively on `Δ/N`, and the stabiliser of the
coset `N` is `K ∩ N`. So `[K : H] = |Δ/N| = d`. `K` is free of rank 2,
being a rank-2 subgroup of a free group, so it is `π_1` of the rose `R_2`.
The subgroup `H` of index `d` is `π_1` of a connected `d`-sheeted cover of
`R_2`. That cover is a graph with `d` vertices and `2d` edges, so
`rk H = 2d − d + 1 = d + 1`.

(ii) `H ≤ K` and `H ≤ M'`, so `H ≤ K ∩ M'`. Conversely
`K ∩ M' ≤ K ∩ N = H`.

(iii) If `M'` is not finitely generated there is nothing to prove. Otherwise
apply `rank-two-free-subgroups-are-strongly-inert` (ESTABLISHED import of
Friedman's SHNC, arXiv:1105.0129v2 (2.4) and Theorem 2.2). Its statement:
"Let `F` be a free group and `H <= F` a subgroup of rank at most 2. Then for
every subgroup `K <= F`, sum over double cosets K g H of rr(H cap K^g) <=
rr(K), where `rr(A) = max{0, rk(A) - 1}`." Take the node's `H` to be our
rank-2 `K`, and the node's `K` to be our `M'`. All terms of the sum are
`≥ 0`, and the term of the trivial double coset is `rr(K ∩ M') = rr(H) = d`
by (i) and (ii). Hence `d ≤ rr(M') = rk M' − 1`.

(iv) Let `M'` be finitely generated of rank `m`; if `m = ∞` then `C(E) = ∞`
and there is nothing to prove. `b|M'` is a free action of the free group
`M'`. The graphing given by a free basis of `M'` has cost `m`. It is a
treeing, since on each orbit it is the Cayley tree of `M'`: a reduced word
fixing a point would contradict freeness.
`gaboriau-treeable-free-action-realizes-min-cost` item 1 ("If Ψ is a
treeing of a p.m.p. equivalence relation R, then C(Ψ) = C(R)") gives
`C(E_{b|M'}) = m`. By (1), `C(E) = 1 + (m − 1)/d ≥ 1 + d/d = 2` using
(iii). For `M' = N` we have `m = rk N = 1 + d(rk Δ − 1)`. For `Δ = F_2`
this gives `C(E_{b_N}) = 2`, and for general free `Δ` it gives
`C(E_{b_N}) = rk Δ`.

For the last assertion, `M ⊇ K` is non-abelian, so `rk M ≥ 2`, and the same
treeing argument gives `C(E_{b|M}) = rk M`. ∎

**Remark (higher rank).** The same argument with Friedman's full inequality
(2.4), quoted verbatim in the node above as `sigma(K, L) <= rk_(-1)(K)
rk_(-1)(L)` together with "Theorem 2.2: The Strengthened Hanna Neumann
Conjecture holds", gives `rr(M') ≥ d` for every `K` of rank `≥ 2`. So the
bound `C(E) ≥ 2` of (iv) holds for all non-cyclic finitely generated `K`.
This uses the full SHNC and not the rank-2 node, so it is recorded as a
remark rather than as part of the claim.

**What (iv) says for the `F_2` test of RBL.** For `Δ = F_2` and rank-2 `K`,
consider the finite-cover test actions `b_N` together with their virtually
group-generated sandwiches, and ordinary group-generated sandwiches in any
free action. This family gives the value 2 exactly, whatever the base
action `b`, and so in particular for `b = s`. So:

- The fiber trick together with subgroup combinatorics cannot produce a
  free action `a` with `p_K(a) < 2`. A fortiori it cannot produce one with
  `p_K(a) < p_K(s)`. The invariant that seals it is the reduced rank `rr`.
  It dies at step (iii): `K ∩ M' = K ∩ N` makes the trivial double coset
  alone carry `d · rr(K)`.
- Any refutation of RBL on `F_2` needs an action carrying a sandwich of
  cost `< 2` that is not virtually group-generated.
- Any proof of RBL on `F_2` along the lines "`p_K ≡ 2`" needs a measured
  form of (iii). See §5.

## 4. Census: searching for a virtual rank drop

The test in (3) is useful only if `mrk_N(K∩N)` can fall below the value
forced by an overgroup. If `K ≤ M ≤ Δ` with `rk M = rmin(K)` (the least
rank of an overgroup), then `M ∩ N` has index `d` in `M`, since
`MN ⊇ KN = Δ`. It also satisfies `K∩N ≤ M∩N ≤ N`. So

    mrk_N(K∩N) ≤ forced := 1 + d (rmin(K) − 1).

A *drop* is a pair `(K, N)` with `mrk_N(K∩N) < forced`. A drop in `F_2`
would give a virtually group-generated sandwich of cost
`< 1 + (forced − 1)/d = rmin(K)`. Theorem 4(iii) and the Remark exclude
drops whenever `rmin(K) = 2`, so they are excluded for every non-cyclic `K`
in `F_2`. The census checks the code and searches `F_3` where the theorem
is silent.

**Method** (`experiments/fpbs-sandwich-fiber-rank-drop-2026-09-17/`):
- `census.py`: Stallings folding, fibre products of `Γ(K)` with the Schreier
  graph of `N`, and the transitivity check `KN = Δ`. It finds `rmin(K)`
  from all vertex partitions of `Γ(K)`, then folds. It finds `mrk_N(K∩N)`
  from all colour-respecting partitions of `Γ(K∩N)`, meaning vertices over
  the same coset of `N`, then folds. By Stallings theory, overgroups of `H`
  inside `N` correspond to such quotients followed by folding and adding
  edges, which only raises rank. So the minimum over quotients is exact.
  This is the algebraic-extension picture of Miasnikov–Ventura–Weil.
- `run.py`, `run3.py`: exhaustive search over tuples of reduced words
  and all transitive `d`-covers.
- `rand.py`, `census_fr.py`: random search in `F_2` and `F_r`.
  `census_fr.py` keeps only `K` with `rmin(K) ≥ 3`.

**Results** (no drop anywhere; `mrk_N = forced` in every case):

| search | range | pairs `(K,N)` | drops |
|---|---|---|---|
| exhaustive `F_2`, rank 2 | words of length ≤ 3, `d = 2` | 839 (338 K) | 0 |
| random `F_2`, rank 2 | length ≤ 6, `d ≤ 3` | 400 | 0 |
| exhaustive `F_2`, rank 3 | words of length ≤ 2, `d ≤ 3` | 28 | 0 |
| random `F_2`, rank 3 | length ≤ 6, `d ≤ 3` | 300 | 0 |
| random `F_3`, rank 3, `rmin = 3` | length ≤ 4, `d ≤ 3` | 150 | 0 |

Some `d = 3` searches hit the partition limit and report `complete=False`.
For those, the value found is only an upper bound on `mrk`. It already
equals `forced`, and `forced` is a lower bound only where Theorem 4
applies. The `F_3` rows are therefore evidence, not proof. Output files are
`out_*.txt` in the experiment directory.

## 5. The measured Hanna Neumann statement

**Statement (MC; node `fpbs-free-group-sandwich-cost-is-two`, OPEN).** For
every free p.m.p. action `a` of `F_2` and every non-cyclic finitely
generated `K ≤ F_2`, each `E` with `E_{a|K} ⊆ E ⊆ E_a` has `C(E) ≥ 2`.

**MC implies RBL for `Δ = F_2`.** Let `F` be finite.
- If `⟨F⟩` is non-cyclic, MC and `C(E_a) = 2` give `p_F(a) = 2` for every
  free `a`.
- If `⟨F⟩ ≅ Z`, every sandwich is aperiodic, so it has cost `≥ 1`.
  `E_{a|⟨F⟩}` is generated by one automorphism, so it has cost `≤ 1`.
  Hence `p_F ≡ 1`.
- If `⟨F⟩ = 1`, `p_F ≡ 0`.

In each case `p_F(s_{F_2}) = p_F(a)`.

**The group proof does not transfer.** For subgroups, `K ≤ M` forces
`rk M ≥ 2` only because `M` is non-abelian. The measured analogue of that
reason is false:

*Example.* Let `α` be a free p.m.p. action of `F_2` on `(Y, μ_Y)`, and let
`X = Y ⊔ Z`, where `Z` is a copy of `Y`, each half with mass `1/2`. Let
`φ : Z → Y` be the identity map between the copies. On `X`, let `F_2` act
by `α` on `Y` and by `φ^{-1} α φ` on `Z`. This is a free action. Let `R`
be generated by it together with `φ`.

`R` is treed by a free basis of `F_2` on `Y` plus `φ`. So by
`gaboriau-treeable-free-action-realizes-min-cost` item 1,

    C(R) = 2 · (1/2) + 1/2 = 3/2.

So a treeable relation of cost `3/2` contains a free `F_2`-action
relation. Any proof of MC must use `E ⊆ E_a`.

Counting also fails, because restriction to a complete section does not
see the trivial double coset of Theorem 4(iii). If `Y` is a complete
section of `E_{a|K}`, then (IF) gives `E_{a|K}|Y` the normalised cost
`1 + rr(K)/μ(Y)`. This scales exactly like the rank of a subgroup of index
`1/μ(Y)`, so no count of reduced costs over complete sections gives
`C(E) ≥ 2`.

**Lemma 5 (no equivariant copies inside `E_a`).** Let `a` be a free
p.m.p. action of `F_2`, and let `K ≤ F_2` be non-cyclic. Let
`D ⊆ X` be an `a|K`-invariant set of positive measure. Let `φ : D → X` be
a partial isomorphism with graph in `E_a` and
`φ(k·z) = k·φ(z)` for all `k ∈ K` and a.e. `z ∈ D`. Then `φ = id_D`.

*Proof.* By freeness there is a unique measurable `g : D → F_2` with
`φ(z) = g(z)·z`. Equivariance gives `g(kz)·kz = k·g(z)·z`. Freeness then
gives

    g(kz) = k g(z) k^{-1}.

So `g_*(μ|D)` is a finite measure on `F_2` that is invariant under
conjugation by `K`. Every atom of it therefore has a finite `K`-conjugacy
orbit. If `h ≠ 1`, its centraliser in `F_2` is cyclic. A finite orbit
would put a finite-index subgroup of `K` inside that cyclic centraliser,
making `K` virtually cyclic, and hence cyclic, since `K` is free. So the
only possible atom is `h = 1`. Hence `g = 1` a.e. on `D`, and `φ = id_D`. ∎

So the amplification in the example, which copies the `K`-action along an
intertwiner, cannot be carried out inside `E_a` over `a|K`. A cheap
sandwich would have to identify `K`-orbits along a map that twists the
`K`-action by a non-constant cocycle.

**Suggested attack (not carried out).** Jaikin-Zapirain's proof of SHNC
works with `L²`-Betti numbers over the Linnell division ring `D_{F_2}`.
For a treeable relation with infinite classes, Gaboriau's formula gives
`C(E) = 1 + β_1^{(2)}(E)`. Every intermediate `E` is treeable, because
`E ⊆ E_a` is. So MC is exactly `β_1^{(2)}(E) ≥ 1`.

The subgroup case `E = E_{a|M}` has `E_{a|K} ∩ E_{a|M} = E_{a|K∩M}` by
freeness. So MC is the trivial-double-coset instance
`rr(S ∩ T) ≤ rr(S) · rr(T)` of a measured SHNC with `S = E_{a|K}`, where
`rk K = 2` and `rr(S) = 1`, and `T = E`. Here `rr = β_1^{(2)}` for treeable
relations.

The proposed route replaces `D_{F_2}` by the division closure of the
groupoid algebra of `E_a` inside its von Neumann algebra. The measured
SHNC for arbitrary treeable `S, T` is false. In the example, let `S` be the
free `F_2`-action relation on `X` and `T = R`. Then `S ∩ T = S`, so
`rr(S ∩ T) = 1 > 1/2 = rr(S) · rr(T)`. Any valid version must therefore
use that `S` and `T` both sit inside one free `F_2`-action relation `E_a`,
with `S` coming from a subgroup. Lemma 5 is the first rigidity fact of
this kind.

A counterexample to MC would have to be a twisted sandwich in the sense of
Lemma 5, which is beyond the reach of the census method.
