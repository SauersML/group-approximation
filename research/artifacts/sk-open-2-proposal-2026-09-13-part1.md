## (a) Replacement text, verbatim (candidate v5)
```latex
\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

Write $(Tx)_n=x_{n+1}$ and let $R=\LC(X,\F_2)\rtimes_T\Z$ be the ring of finite
sums $\sum_jf_ju^j$ with $f_j\colon X\to\F_2$ locally constant and
$ufu^{-1}=f\circ T^{-1}$. So $ue_Vu^{-1}=e_{TV}$ for the indicator $e_V$ of a
clopen set $V$. Put $G=\EL_3(R)$, $e_{ij}(r)=I_3+rE_{ij}$ and
$[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$. For distinct $i,j,k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elementary}
  e_{ij}(r+s)=e_{ij}(r)e_{ij}(s),\qquad [e_{ik}(r),e_{kj}(s)]=e_{ij}(rs).
\end{equation}
Translates and products of the letter indicators $e_a=e_{\{x:x_0=a\}}$ give all
cylinder indicators, so $R$ is generated by $u^{\pm1}$ and the $e_a$, and
by~\eqref{eq:elementary} $G$ is generated by the $e_{ij}(s)$ for these $s$. So
$G$ has property~(T) by~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}. It is infinite since
$e_{12}(\LC(X,\F_2))$ is.

\subsection*{Finite models}
A group is LEF if every finite subset embeds injectively into a finite group,
preserving the products that stay in the subset. Fix $\ell$ and $x\in X$. By
uniform recurrence there is $p>2\ell$ with $x_{[p-\ell,p+\ell]}=x_{[-\ell,\ell]}$
such that every word of length $2\ell+1$ in $X$ occurs in $x_{[0,p)}$. On
$\F_2^{\Z/p}$ let $P\delta_n=\delta_{n+1}$ and $D(f)\delta_n=f(T^nx)\delta_n$ for
$0\le n<p$, and put $\pi_\ell(\sum_jf_ju^j)=\sum_jD(f_j)P^j$. Then $\pi_\ell$ is
additive, $D$ is multiplicative, and $P^iD(f)P^{-i}=D(f\circ T^{-i})$ if $f$
depends only on $x_{[-\rho,\rho]}$ with $\rho+|i|\le\ell$, since $T^mx$ and
$T^{m+p}x$ agree on $[-\rho,\rho]$ for $|m|\le\ell-\rho$. So for fixed
$r,r'\in R$ and all large $\ell$, $\pi_\ell(rr')=\pi_\ell(r)\pi_\ell(r')$, and
$\pi_\ell(r)\ne0$ if $r\ne0$, since some $f_j(T^nx)=1$ with $0\le n<p$ and the
$P^j$ with $|j|<p/2$ have disjoint supports. For a finite $F\subseteq G$
containing $I_3$ and closed under inverses, apply this to the finitely many
entries, sums and products that occur in multiplying and subtracting elements
of $F$. For large $\ell$, $\pi_\ell$ applied entrywise maps $F$ injectively into
$\GL_{3p}(\F_2)$ and preserves the products that lie in $F$. So $G$ is LEF, and
LEF groups are sofic and hyperlinear~\cite{Pestov}.

\subsection*{Simplicity}
Let $1\ne g\in N\trianglelefteq G$, and let $w$ bound the exponents in the
entries of $g$ and $g^{-1}$. As $X$ is infinite and minimal, $T$ has no periodic
points. So compactness gives a finite clopen partition $\mathcal P$ of $X$ such
that $V\cap T^jV=\varnothing$ for $V\in\mathcal P$ and $0<|j|\le2w$, and every
coefficient $f$ of an entry of $g^{\pm1}$ has $f\circ T^i$ constant on each
$V\in\mathcal P$ for $|i|\le w$. Some $h=e_{ij}(e_V)$ with $V\in\mathcal P$ does
not commute with $g$. Otherwise, summing over $V$, $g$ commutes with every
$e_{ij}(1)$, so $g=cI_3$ where $c=\sum_{|j|\le w}f_ju^j$ commutes with each
$e_V$. Comparing coefficients in $ce_V=e_Vc$ gives $f_je_V=f_je_{T^jV}e_V=0$ for
$j\ne0$, so $c=f_0$. Comparing constant coefficients in $cc^{-1}=1$ shows that
$f_0$ is a unit of $\LC(X,\F_2)$, so $c=1$ and $g=I_3$.

Fix such $V$ and put $k=[g,h]\in N\setminus\{1\}$. For $|a|,|b|\le w$ the
elements $E_{ab}=e_{T^aV}u^{a-b}$ satisfy $E_{ab}E_{cd}=\delta_{bc}E_{ad}$, so
they span a ring $A\cong M_{2w+1}(\F_2)$ with unit $e=\sum_aE_{aa}$.
If $f,f'$ are coefficients of entries of $g^{\pm1}$ and $|i|,|i'|\le w$, then
$fu^ie_Vf'u^{i'}=f\,e_{T^iV}(f'\circ T^{-i})\,u^{i+i'}\in\F_2E_{i,-i'}$, as $f$
and $f'\circ T^{-i}$ are constant on $T^iV$. Since $h=h^{-1}=I_3+e_VE_{ij}$, we
have $k=(I_3+ge_VE_{ij}g^{-1})h$ and $k^{-1}=h(I_3+ge_VE_{ij}g^{-1})$, so
$k-I_3$ and $k^{-1}-I_3$ lie in $M_3(A)$. So $k$ lies in the group
$H=\{(1-e)I_3+x:x\in\GL_3(A)\}\cong\GL_d(\F_2)$, where $d=3(2w+1)$. In
coordinates $(\mu,a)$ with $1\le\mu\le3$ and $|a|\le w$, the transvections of
$H$ are the $e_{\mu\nu}(E_{ab})$ with $\mu\ne\nu$ and the commutators
$[e_{\mu\nu}(E_{ac}),e_{\nu\mu}(E_{cb})]$, so $H\subseteq G$. As
$\GL_d(\F_2)=\PSL_d(\F_2)$ is simple and $k\in N\cap H$, we get $H\subseteq N$,
so $e_{ij}(e_V)\in N$ for all $i\ne j$.

Finally, $I_N=\{r\in R:e_{ij}(r)\in N\text{ for all }i\ne j\}$ is a two-sided
ideal of $R$ by~\eqref{eq:elementary}, since $N$ is normal. It contains $e_V$,
and finitely many translates $T^{n_i}V$ cover $X$ by minimality, so
$1=1-\prod_i(1-e_{T^{n_i}V})\in I_N$ and $N=G$.\hfill$\square$
```

## (b) Architecture changes behind the length
- **Finite models from orbit points (new in this lane).** D(f) = diag(f(T^n x)) evaluates at fixed points of X, so it is multiplicative for every function. Only the cyclic seam needs a return condition x_{[p−ℓ,p+ℓ]} = x_{[−ℓ,ℓ]}. This removes the periodic-word lemma (l.123-129) and the window bookkeeping (l.137-144).
- **Units in place of centres (new in this lane).** A g that commutes with the local transvections e_ij(e_V) is cI_3 with c ∈ LC(X,F_2) invertible. Over F_2 that forces c = 1. This removes the simplicity of R, Z(R), Z(G) and the subring argument (l.102-116, l.185-187).
- **Constant towers (found concurrently by sk-simplicity-short-a/-b).** V is chosen so small that the coefficients of g^{±1} are constant along the tower. Then k = [g, e_ij(e_V)] already lies in a finite copy of GL_d(F_2), with no κ function, no W and no absorption lemma (l.154-203).
- **Level ideal over all positions.** The commutator formula on both sides gives the ideal property without permutation matrices (l.223-225), and covering by translates gives 1.
- **Partition need not refine letters.**

## Firewalls: radical alternatives that do not shorten the proof
These are argued in the lane, with no literature search beyond standard facts.
- Other finitely generated infinite simple rings over finite fields:
  - Leavitt algebras are not LEF (EL_n over them have no MF quotient; see the non-MF paper).
  - Group rings are never simple (augmentation ideal).
  - Quantum tori over F_q are finite over their centre, so not simple and infinite.
  - Weyl algebras in characteristic p are finite over their centre.
  - Odometer crossed products are not finitely generated, so EJZ does not apply (`odometer-crossed-product-elementary-groups-not-kazhdan` on main).
  - Direct limits of matrix algebras are locally finite, so not finitely generated.
  - Crossed products of Toeplitz or Z^d subshifts use the same proof and are not shorter.
- Simplicity through the ultraproduct ∏_ω SL_{3p}(F_2): its normal subgroups (rank-ratio cuts) do not control normal subgroups of the subgroup G.
- Soficity from approximate models without exact seams: normalized-rank closeness in GL_{3p}(F_2) gives no Hamming closeness on F_2^{3p}, since fixed-point sets of g ≠ h have density 2^{−rank(g−h)}. Exact cyclic models are the short route.
- A stable-range citation (Bass–Vaserstein sandwich) could replace the Simplicity subsection only if sr(R) is small. That is owned by sk-stable-rank-a/-b. The constant-tower argument above is already 20 lines.
