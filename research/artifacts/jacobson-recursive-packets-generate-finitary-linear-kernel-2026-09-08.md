# The recursive packets generate the full finitary linear kernel

2026-09-08. In `Theta_shift`, the adjacent six-coordinate packets
generate exact general linear groups on every finite interval of
levels. Their union over bilateral finite intervals is a normal
locally finite subgroup, isomorphic to the finitary general linear
group over `F_2`. It is precisely the normal closure of the head.

The proof reconstructs all finite groups abstractly from the given
packets. The literal bilateral action supplies faithfulness only after
the finite-group relations have been proved. No infinite-group
representation estimate, uniform matrix gap, or non-MF conclusion is
used or asserted.

## 1. Notation and previously established relations

Use the finite presentation and literal model in
[the stable-letter artifact](jacobson-stable-letter-recursion-and-matrix-gap-2026-09-08.md).
Write `alpha=Ad_t`, and let `H` be the subgroup generated by the
original `Theta` generators. For every integer `i`, put

```text
Theta_i=alpha^i(H),
G_i=alpha^i(G_Q),           P_i=alpha^i(G_P),
K_i=alpha^i(K),             C0_i=alpha^i(C_0),
a_i=alpha^i(h),             s_i=alpha^i(h_0).
```

Here `h_0` denotes the pure boundary swap from the stable-letter
artifact; `a_0=h` is the original generator. This distinguishes the
pure swap from the original, infinite-support involution. The exact
relations are

```text
Theta_(i+1)<=Theta_i,
Theta_i=<G_i,P_i,a_i>,
P_i(g)=G_(i+1)(g) P_(i+1)(g),
B_i=K_i x P_(i+1),
a_i=s_i a_(i+1),
[C0_i,a_(i+1)]=1.                                      (1)
```

Both factors in the displayed decomposition of `P_i(g)` commute.
The coordinate identifications of the exact finite packets are

```text
K_i=GL(level i plus level i+1) ~= GL_6(F_2),
C0_i=GL(level i plus coordinates 1,3 of level i+1),
s_i swaps e_(2,i) and e_(1,i+1).
```

Each level has its three ordered coordinates. The notation `GL(E)`
below means the specified finite packet on a finite coordinate set
`E`, fixing its displayed complementary coordinates. These coordinate
descriptions abbreviate the already proved abstract embeddings in
the finite packets.

[The commuting-head argument](jacobson-shift-exact-collapse-and-monomial-gap-2026-09-08.md)
also gives

```text
[G_i,Theta_(i+1)]=1.                                    (2)
```

Indeed `G_i` commutes with the three generators
`G_(i+1),P_(i+1),a_(i+1)`, by `B_i` and `C_i`.
Finally, `a_i,a_(i+1)` commute: their product is `s_i`, and
`s_i` commutes with `a_(i+1)` in `C_i`. All statements in (1)--(2)
hold for negative as well as nonnegative indices by conjugation.

## 2. A one-coordinate extension lemma

Let `U` have a fixed basis of at least two coordinates, and let
`z,w` be two additional coordinates. In an ambient group suppose
there are specified copies

```text
A_z=GL(U plus z),       A_w=GL(U plus w),
D=GL(U0 plus z plus w),                                (3)
```

where `U0` is spanned by two chosen basis coordinates of `U`.
The copies `A_z,A_w` agree on every element of `GL(U)`. The
copy `D` agrees with them on `GL(U0 plus z)` and
`GL(U0 plus w)`, respectively, with the stated elementary-root
labels. These are compatibility requirements on the embeddings;
no assertion about their full pairwise intersections is needed.

Assume the elementary Steinberg presentation is valid for
`GL_(dim(U)+2)(F_2)`, as it is in every application below,
where `dim(U)+2>=7`. Then the subgroup generated by (3) is a
quotient of

```text
GL(U plus z plus w).                                   (4)
```

If a homomorphism of the ambient group sends the displayed roots
to their literal elementary matrices on these coordinates, the
quotient is faithful, and the subgroup is exactly (4).

Here is a proof of the relations, including the potentially missing
ones. Use the elementary root `X_ab=I+E_ab` from `A_z` or
`A_w` whenever its indices occur there. The roots `X_zw,X_wz`
come from `D`. The two assignments agree wherever both are defined.

First these last two roots centralize all of `GL(U)`. If `r` is
either of the two basis coordinates of `U0`, the seed table gives

```text
X_zw=[X_zr,X_rw],       X_wz=[X_wr,X_rz].                (5)
```

Take an elementary root `X_ab` of `GL(U)`. If one of the two
seed coordinates `r` is outside `{a,b}`, the exact relations in
`A_z,A_w` show that `X_ab` commutes with both factors in each
commutator in (5). If no such `r` exists, then `{a,b}` is the
two-coordinate set of `U0`, and centralization follows from the
finite table of `D`. The elementary roots generate `GL(U)`, so
the claim follows.

Now consider a pair of root generators for the proposed group (4).
If both belong to `A_z`, or both to `A_w`, their required
relations already hold. Any other pair uses at most two coordinates
from `U`. A coordinate permutation in the common `GL(U)` moves
those coordinates into `U0`. Conjugation has the prescribed root
action within both large packets, and fixes `X_zw,X_wz` by the
preceding paragraph. Thus every remaining pair relation transports
from the exact table of `D`. In particular all the relations

```text
X_ab^2=1,
[X_ab,X_bc]=X_ac                   for distinct a,b,c,
[X_ab,X_cd]=1                     if b!=c and a!=d       (6)
```

hold. The finite-field Steinberg presentation therefore gives the
surjective homomorphism asserted in (4). The imported presentation
is the same one used in the
[six-coordinate proof](jacobson-full-boundary-opposite-roots-and-gl6-packet-2026-09-08.md):
Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley Groups*, Section 6, Theorem 9 and
the following special-linear example, transcribed at
[Generators and relations](https://math.soimeme.org/~arunram/Resources/LOCGS6GeneratorsAndRelations.html).
Here every field coefficient is in `F_2`, so root additivity is
involutivity. This step is needed before using the literal model:
a literal quotient by itself would not rule out an additional cover.
The composite with the stated literal homomorphism is the identity
on all elementary matrices, proving injectivity when that
homomorphism is available.

One generation observation will be useful. The group (4) is already
generated by `A_z` and the seed `D`. For a coordinate `b in U`
outside the seed, choose a seed coordinate `r!=b` and use

```text
X_bw=[X_br,X_rw],       X_wb=[X_wr,X_rb].                (7)
```

These produce all additional roots. In particular the second large
copy in (3), initially useful for verifying relations, lies in the
join of the first copy and the seed.

## 3. Two adjacent six-coordinate packets generate GL_9

It suffices to treat `K_0,K_1`; conjugation gives every other case.
Let `E` be the six coordinates of levels zero and one, and set

```text
z=e_(2,1),       w=e_(1,2),
U=E without z,
U0=span(e_(1,1),e_(3,1)).
```

The first large packet is `K_0=GL(U plus z)`. The second is
`a_1 K_0 a_1`. They agree pointwise on `GL(U)=C0_0`, by
(1). Give the second copy the root labels that replace `z` by
`w` and fix `U`.

The required compatible seed is the exact `GL_4` inside `K_1`
on level one together with `e_(1,2)`. The compatibility with
`K_0` on level one is already part of the shifted finite-packet
identification. For compatibility with its conjugate, use

```text
a_1=s_1 a_2,
[a_2,C0_1]=1,
G_1<=C0_1.
```

Thus `a_1` conjugates `G_1=GL(U0 plus z)` exactly as the
pure coordinate swap `s_1` does. That swap belongs to `K_1`,
interchanges `z,w`, and fixes `U0`. The seed therefore has
precisely the two identifications required by Section 2; they are
abstract consequences of `C_1`, not deductions from literal supports.

The extension lemma gives a faithful `GL_7` on `E plus w`.
By (7) it equals

```text
<K_0, GL(level 1 plus e_(1,2))> <= <K_0,K_1>.            (8)
```

The group `P_1` centralizes `K_0` by `B_0`. On `K_1` its
conjugation is the natural `G_2` action on level two, because
`P_1(g)=G_2(g)P_2(g)` and `P_2` centralizes `K_1`.
Conjugate the group (8) by the coordinate swap `w_13` in
`P_1`. The two resulting `GL_7` groups agree on `K_0`
pointwise and use the additional coordinates `e_(1,2)` and
`e_(3,2)`, respectively. A seed `GL_4` on these two coordinates
and two coordinates of level one belongs to `K_1` and is compatible
with both groups. The lemma gives a faithful `GL_8` on
`E plus e_(1,2),e_(3,2)`, equal to

```text
<K_0, GL(level 1 plus e_(1,2),e_(3,2))>.                 (9)
```

Finally conjugate (9) by `w_23 in P_1`. This fixes `K_0`
pointwise and also fixes the seed `GL(level 1 plus e_(1,2))`
pointwise, as follows from its exact natural action on `K_1`.
It therefore fixes the entire `GL_7` in (8), not just its
coordinate labels. The two `GL_8` groups have the common
`GL_7` on `E plus e_(1,2)`; their additional coordinates are
`e_(3,2)` and `e_(2,2)`. Another seed `GL_4` using two
level-one coordinates lies in `K_1`. The lemma now gives

```text
<K_0,K_1>=GL(levels 0,1,2) ~= GL_9(F_2).                (10)
```

All constructed roots lie in the join by (7), and the final group
contains every elementary root of `K_1`, proving the stated equality.
At each application the bilateral literal action makes the quotient
faithful, as in Section 2. In particular (10) holds with the
specified root embeddings of both original packets.

## 4. Entire distant packets now commute

Conjugating (10) gives `GL_9` on levels `i,i+1,i+2`.
Inside this finite group the pure swaps `s_i,s_(i+1)` have
disjoint coordinate supports, and hence commute. Since

```text
s_i=a_i a_(i+1),
s_(i+1)=a_(i+1) a_(i+2),
[a_i,a_(i+1)]=[a_(i+1),a_(i+2)]=1,
```

their commutation gives

```text
[a_i,a_(i+2)]=1.                                       (11)
```

The packet `K_i` is generated by

```text
G_i, G_(i+1), a_i G_i a_i.                              (12)
```

To check this in its finite table, the third group supplies both
directions of a root between level `i` and coordinate `1` of
level `i+1`. Conjugation by the first two groups supplies every
root between the two levels; their internal roots are already
present. These are all elementary generators of `K_i`.

Equation (2) says that `a_(i+2)` centralizes `G_i,G_(i+1)`.
Equation (11) then shows that it centralizes the third group in
(12), so

```text
[K_i,a_(i+2)]=1.
```

The exact `GL_9` in (10) also gives `[K_i,G_(i+2)]=1`.
The relation `P_(i+1)(g)=G_(i+2)(g)P_(i+2)(g)`, together
with `[K_i,P_(i+1)]=1` from `B_i`, consequently gives
`[K_i,P_(i+2)]=1`. These are all generators of the next
compressed subgroup, and hence

```text
[K_i,Theta_(i+2)]=1.                                   (13)
```

Since the `Theta_j` are nested, this proves in particular

```text
[K_i,K_j]=1                    whenever j>=i+2.         (14)
```

Unlike commutation of the head groups alone, (13)--(14) control
the mixed roots in the full six-coordinate packets.

## 5. Induction to every finite interval

For `n>=2`, define

```text
A_n=<K_0,...,K_(n-2)>.
```

We prove that `A_n` is the faithful `GL_(3n)(F_2)` on levels
`0,...,n-1`, with the stated roots of each packet. The case `n=2`
is the original `K_0`, and `n=3` is (10).

Suppose `n>=3` and the statement is established for `A_n`.
Let `E` be its coordinates, choose `z=e_(2,n-1)` in its last
level, and let `w` be one new coordinate in level `n`. The exact
group `K_(n-1)` contains the pure permutation `k` interchanging
`z,w` and fixing its four other coordinates. We claim that `k`
centralizes the entire subgroup `GL(E without z)` of `A_n`.

This latter subgroup is generated by

```text
A_(n-1)=GL(levels 0,...,n-2),
GL(level n-2 plus (level n-1 without z)).
```

The generation assertion is a calculation inside the already known
finite group `A_n`: roots between the two displayed sets are
commutators through any suitable shared level-`n-2` coordinate.
The element `k` centralizes the first group by (14), since it
belongs to `K_(n-1)` and that first group is generated by
`K_0,...,K_(n-3)`. It centralizes the second group in the
faithful adjacent `GL_9=<K_(n-2),K_(n-1)>`: the displayed
coordinate swap is disjoint from its support. This proves the claim
as an abstract relation.

Now apply Section 2 to `A_n` and `k A_n k`, using
`U=E without z`. A compatible `GL_4` seed on `z,w` and
the two other coordinates of level `n-1` lies in `K_(n-1)`.
It supplies exactly the conjugation by `k` required on that seed.
The lemma gives the faithful general linear group on `E plus w`,
contained in `<A_n,K_(n-1)>`.

Repeat this step with the other two coordinates of level `n`.
For clarity, after some new coordinates `J` have been added, keep
the same `z` in level `n-1` and swap it with a not-yet-added
coordinate `w`. The required common subgroup is
`GL((E plus J) without z)`. It is generated by `A_(n-1)`
and

```text
GL(level n-2 plus (level n-1 without z) plus J).
```

The latter group lies in the already known adjacent `GL_9`.
Its root labels also agree with those in the packet constructed so
far. At every extension step the seed contains the entire last old
level together with the newly added coordinate. It therefore fixes
all roots between that level and each added coordinate. Roots from
level `n-2` to an added coordinate, and roots between two added
coordinates, are commutators through a last-old-level coordinate.
These are the same commutator words in the constructed packet and
the adjacent `GL_9`. Thus the assertion about this common subgroup
uses proved root identities, not an assumed agreement of literal
images.
The swap centralizes it there, and centralizes `A_(n-1)` by
(14). These subgroups have the same shared level `n-2`, so
they generate the claimed common subgroup inside the general linear
group constructed at the preceding step. The same compatible seed
on two other level-`n-1` coordinates, `z,w`, applies again.
Thus both repetitions satisfy every hypothesis of the lemma.

After the third new coordinate is added, the result contains every
root of `K_(n-1)` and is contained in its join with `A_n`.
Therefore

```text
A_(n+1)=<A_n,K_(n-1)> ~= GL_(3n+3)(F_2).                (15)
```

This proves the induction. Conjugating by `t^j` gives the same
statement on every finite interval of integer levels, with all
standard elementary-root relations and their prescribed embeddings.
No additional Curtis--Tits or infinite Jacobson-group identification
is needed.

## 6. A normal locally finite subgroup and the head's normal closure

Let

```text
L=<K_i : i in Z>.
```

Every finite set of its elements belongs to the group on some finite
interval of levels. By Section 5 that group is a faithful finite
general linear group, with the standard inclusions. Hence `L` is
locally finite and the bilateral literal action identifies it with

```text
GL_fin(F_2^(Z x {1,2,3})),                              (16)
```

the invertible matrices that equal the identity outside a finite
coordinate set. This identification is faithful on the whole union:
an element in its kernel already lies in one of the faithful finite
interval groups.

We next check normality using the actual generators of
`Theta_shift`, namely `G_0,P_0,a_0,t`. Conjugation by `t`
permutes the `K_i`, and `G_0` is contained in `L`.

For `g in G` and `i>=0`, repeated use of (1) gives

```text
P_0(g)=G_1(g) ... G_(i+1)(g) P_(i+1)(g).
```

In its conjugation action on `K_i`, the head factors with index
less than `i` centralize `K_i` by (2); any remaining displayed
head factors belong to `K_i`; and the residual `P_(i+1)`
centralizes `K_i` by `B_i`. Thus `P_0(g)` normalizes `K_i`.
For `i=-1`, the same centralization is directly part of `B_(-1)`.
For `i<=-2`, it follows from (13), since
`P_0(g) in Theta_0<=Theta_(i+2)`. This proves that `P_0`
normalizes `L`.

The construction of `GL_7` in Section 3, after shifting, gives

```text
a_(i+1) K_i a_(i+1) <= <K_i,K_(i+1)> <= L.              (17)
```

For `i>=0`, write

```text
a_0=s_0 s_1 ... s_i a_(i+1).
```

All the `s_j` belong to `L`, so (17) implies
`a_0 K_i a_0<=L`. For `i=-1`, this is directly (17).
For `i<=-2`, it follows from (13), since
`a_0 in Theta_0<=Theta_(i+2)`. As `a_0` is involutive,
these inclusions show that it normalizes `L`. Thus `L` is normal
in the entire group.

It is precisely the normal closure of the head, or equivalently of
its marked transvection `w=x_13,Q`. The normal closure of `w`
contains all `G_i`: within each copy of `G`, the conjugates of
one elementary transvection include all elementary transvections,
and the heads are conjugate by powers of `t`. Within `K_i` the
same elementary conjugacy then gives every root of `K_i` from a
head transvection. Hence that normal closure contains `L`.
The reverse inclusion holds because `L` is normal and contains
the original mark. We have proved

```text
L=normalClosure_(Theta_shift)(G_Q)
 =normalClosure_(Theta_shift)(w).                       (18)
```

This locally finite group is also simple. Let a normal subgroup of
`L` contain `b!=I`. Choose a finite coordinate set supporting `b`,
a basis vector `e_j` with `b e_j!=e_j`, and a fresh coordinate
`k` outside that set. The exact finite matrix calculation gives

```text
[b,I+E_jk]=I+(b e_j-e_j)e_k^*.
```

The right side is a nonidentity transvection: its displayed vector
is nonzero and has zero `k` coordinate. A finite change of basis
fixing `e_k` conjugates it to an ordinary elementary transvection.
All elementary transvections are conjugate by finite coordinate
permutations and generate `L`. Thus the normal subgroup is all
of `L`, proving simplicity. The group is infinite and nonabelian.

Consequently every homomorphism from `Theta_shift` to any group
either kills `L` or is injective on `L`. In particular retaining
`w` is equivalent to faithful restriction to this whole finitary
subgroup. This does not imply faithfulness on `Theta_shift`: the
kernel of such a homomorphism can still be disjoint from `L`.

## 7. The quotient has an explicit smaller presentation

This subsection records the quotient presentation without identifying
it with any arithmetic group. Set

```text
Lambda=<G_P *_(K_P) G_* | (h q_P)^3=1,
                          [[h c_P h,k_P],h r_P h] r_P=1>, (19)
```

where both displayed factors are the prescribed copies of
`GL_3(F_2)`, their common `K_P` acts on coordinates `1,3`,
and `h=w_12` in the second factor `G_*`.

Quotienting `Theta_shift` by `L` kills all of `K` and `C_0`.
The finite group `B` becomes its residual `G` factor, and `C`
becomes `C_far=G_*`; their overlap becomes precisely the prescribed
`K_P`. The two mixed relators become (19), because the head is
trivial. The stable-letter equations on `G_P` become commutation
with `t`, since `g_P=delta(g)` after killing `tau(G)`. The
remaining stable-letter equation says that `t` commutes with `h`,
since `h_0` is killed. The stable-letter equations on the head
become trivial. These are exactly the presentation relations of

```text
Theta_shift/L ~= Lambda x Z.                            (20)
```

Conversely the assignments from the two finite factors to (19),
together with the independent central generator of `Z`, satisfy
the quotient presentation. Thus (20) is a presentation elimination,
not merely a surjection. In particular there is a short exact
sequence with the explicitly proved locally finite kernel (16).

## 8. What this establishes for the matrix-gap problem

The missing abstract finite-packet growth is now resolved: every
finite interval has its full general linear group, all distant root
commutations are actual relations, and the head normally generates
a specified locally finite subgroup. A proposed operator-norm
matrix-corona model must respect all these finite groups.

For any fixed interval, finite-group correction can make its
representation exact along an approximating sequence. The interval
size must remain fixed while taking that limit unless an additional
uniform estimate is proved. No dimension-independent correction
bound over this growing family, positive lower bound for
`gamma_shift`, or contradiction for unrestricted matrix-corona
representations is obtained here. The subgroup (16) is itself
locally finite and is not, by itself, an MF obstruction.
