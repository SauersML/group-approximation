## The proof
- **Step 1.** `φ(R) ⊆ M_k(A)`, where `A` is the ring generated by the entries of the generators' images.
  - `A` is finitely generated and commutative.
  - `A ≠ 0`, because `1_C ≠ 0`.
- **Step 2.** `pX = φ(1) = I_k`, where `X = φ(1/p)`. Its `(1,1)` entry gives `p x_11 = 1` in `A`. So every prime is
  a unit of `A`.
- **Step 3.** Take a maximal ideal `m` of `A`.
  - `A/m` is a field that is finitely generated as a ring, so it is finite (Nullstellensatz over `Z`).
  - It also receives a unital map from `Q`, so it has characteristic 0.
  - These contradict each other.

Correct. This is the same mechanism as step 2 of `integral-form-algebras-have-no-fg-subring-containing-q`
(reviewed at 7bf46a046).

## Wording (non-blocking)
- The consequence about rings "exhausted by locally matricial pieces" needs a **directed** union. A finitely
  generated subring lies in a single piece only when the pieces are directed, as they are in the AF application.
- The statement assumes a **unital** map `Q → R`. It says nothing about copies of `Q` whose unit is a proper
  idempotent. See the AF report for the easy extension when the target is `M_k(Q)`.
