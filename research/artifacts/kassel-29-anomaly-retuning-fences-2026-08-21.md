# Relation (2.9): anomaly and two exact retuning fences

For the exact packet of `kassel-through-28-has-retuned-omega-model`, define
the omitted-relator unitary

```text
Delta_9 = [x_(alpha+beta),x_(-alpha)] x_(-beta)^2.
```

Relation (2.9) is exactly `Delta_9=I`.  In the packet,
`x_(alpha+beta)=I` and `x_(-beta)=nu I`, so

```text
Delta_9=nu^2 I=q^(-1)I,
w_beta^4=nu^(-4)I=Delta_9^(-2)=omega I.       (A)
```

The scalar identity in `(A)` is preserved by tensor products, conjugates,
amplifications, and direct sums having a common scalar anomaly.  Hence the
tensor category generated by this packet and its conjugate cannot make
(2.9) exact while retaining a nontrivial scalar mark.

This is not asserted for arbitrary six-relator representations.  Two broad
but still restricted retunings can nevertheless be excluded exactly.

## 1. Keeping the scalar `B` and trivial middle root cannot work

Suppose `c=x_(alpha+beta)=I`, `b=x_beta` is an involution, and
`B=x_(-beta)` is scalar, as in both 24-dimensional packets.  If (2.9) is
exact, then

```text
I=[c,A]=B^(-2),
```

so `B^2=I`.  Since `b^2=I` and `B` is scalar,

```text
(b B^(-1) b)^4=B^(-4)=I.
```

Thus any successful (2.9) retuning must free at least `c`, the scalar nature
of `B`, or the involutive `b` packet.

## 2. A single cyclic Weyl clock for `c` is arithmetically impossible

The most economical freeing of `c` also fails.  Let `Z` have order `N`, let
`A` be a Weyl shift with `[Z,A]=qI`, where `q` has order divisible by three,
and impose the cyclic-normalizer ansatz

```text
c=Z,       d=Z^r,       b=q^(-r) Z^(-1),
a Z a^(-1)=Z^s.
```

The choices of `b` and `d` make (2.12), `[d,A]=c^(-1)b^(-1)`, exact.
Relation (2.5), `[a,c]=d^2`, gives

```text
s-1 = 2r mod N.                                      (C1)
```

Relation (2.4), `[a,b]=cd`, gives

```text
1-s = r+1 mod N.                                    (C2)
```

Combining `(C1)` and `(C2)` yields

```text
3r = -1 mod N.                                      (C3)
```

But an exact scalar commutator of order divisible by three on an `N`-clock
requires `3|N`, and `(C3)` then has no solution.  Therefore a single cyclic
Weyl clock, even with arbitrary normalizer power `s`, cannot absorb (2.9).

## Scope

These calculations explain why the first two natural retunings stop at
(2.9), but they do not give a representation-independent gap.  A surviving
model must use a noncyclic/non-normalizer middle-root packet, or make
`x_(-beta)` genuinely non-scalar.  Those are the exact remaining branches.
