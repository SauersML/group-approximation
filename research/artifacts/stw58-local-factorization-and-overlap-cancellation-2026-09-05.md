# STW LVIII: controlled local factorization and conditional stable-range gluing

Date: 2026-09-05. **Proved local statements, internally reviewed.**
No external peer review or formal verification is claimed.
The global localization step for pure-algebra K-stability remains open here.
This artifact does not claim a solution of STW LVIII.

## 1. What is proved

Let \(B\) be unital and let \(h_0,h_1\in B_+\) satisfy
\(h_0+h_1=1\). Write
\[
C_i=\operatorname{Her}(h_i),\qquad
c=h_0h_1,\qquad C_{01}=\operatorname{Her}(c).
\]
The \(h_i\) commute, and \(C_{01}=C_0\cap C_1\).

We prove two distinct statements.

1. If \(c\) is full, every unitary in a fixed norm neighborhood
   of \(1\) has a continuously chosen finite product factorization
   into fixed conjugates of endpoint-supported unitaries. The
   factors tend to \(1\) with the original unitary, with a norm
   estimate. The conjugating unitaries lie in \(U_0(1+C_1)\).
2. If both endpoint pieces and their full overlap have the matrix
   stable range described below, every stably null based sphere
   family that admits a global finite supported factorization
   contracts. This remains true for fixed conjugates of supported
   factors and for factorizations approximating the family
   uniformly within distance strictly less than \(2\).

The first statement is a genuine neighborhood factorization; the
second supplies the missing K-class cancellation for any given
global factorization. A neighborhood factorization does not
produce compatible factorizations on a whole parameter sphere.

For a possibly nonunital hereditary algebra \(C\subset B\), use
\[
U_B(C)=\{u\in U(B):u-1\in C\}.
\]
If \(C\) has a unit \(p\), this is the usual inclusion
\(u\mapsto u+(1-p)\) of \(U(C)\).

## 2. Fullness gives a bounded linear decomposition

**Lemma 2.1.** If \(c\) is full, there are finitely many elements
\(b_1,\ldots,b_s\in B\) such that
\[
\sum_{j=1}^s b_jc^2b_j^*=1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

**Proof.** Since the closed ideal generated by \(c\) is \(B\),
choose a finite sum \(T=\sum d_jce_j\) within \(1/2\) of \(1\);
then \(T\) is invertible. Put \(D=(d_1c,\ldots,d_sc)\) and let
\(E\) be the column with entries \(e_j\). The matrix inequality
\[
TT^*=DEE^*D^*\le \|E\|^2 DD^*
\]
shows that \(s_0=DD^*=\sum d_jc^2d_j^*\) is invertible.
Taking \(b_j=s_0^{-1/2}d_j\) proves (2.1). \(\square\)

All subsequent linear maps are between real Banach spaces of
self-adjoint elements. For \(a=a^*\in B\), set
\[
x_j(a)=h_0ab_jc,\qquad y_j=cb_j^*h_1.
\]
Then \(x_j(a)\in C_0\), \(y_j\in C_1\), and
\[
\sum_jx_j(a)y_j=h_0ah_1.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Moreover \(y_jx_j(a)\in C_{01}\), since it has the form
\(c z c\) for an element \(z\in B\).

Write
\[
x_j(a)=A_j(a)+iB_j(a),\qquad y_j=P_j+iQ_j
\]
with self-adjoint real and imaginary parts. The maps \(A_j,B_j\)
are bounded real-linear maps into \((C_0)_{\rm sa}\), and
\(P_j,Q_j\in(C_1)_{\rm sa}\) are fixed. The identity
\[
[x,y]+[x,y]^*
=2i\bigl([\operatorname{Re}x,\operatorname{Im}y]
        +[\operatorname{Im}x,\operatorname{Re}y]\bigr)
\]
and (2.2) give an exact bounded linear decomposition
\[
a=L_0(a)+L_1(a)
 +2i\sum_j\bigl([A_j(a),Q_j]+[B_j(a),P_j]\bigr),
\tag{2.3}
\]
where
\[
L_0(a)=h_0ah_0+\sum_j\bigl(y_jx_j(a)+(y_jx_j(a))^*\bigr)
        \in(C_0)_{\rm sa},\qquad
L_1(a)=h_1ah_1\in(C_1)_{\rm sa}.
\]
Thus the off-diagonal coefficients have not been discarded or
assumed to commute with the dimension-drop image.

## 3. Exact continuous factorization near the identity

**Theorem 3.1.** Suppose \(c\) is full. There are a finite integer
\(q\), indices \(\epsilon_\nu\in\{0,1\}\), fixed unitaries
\(v_\nu\in U_0(U_B(C_1))\), a number \(\delta>0\), and continuous
maps
\[
a_\nu:\{u\in U(B):\|u-1\|<\delta\}
          \longrightarrow (C_{\epsilon_\nu})_{\rm sa}
\]
such that
\[
a_\nu(1)=0,\qquad
u=\prod_{\nu=1}^q v_\nu\exp(i a_\nu(u))v_\nu^*,\qquad
\|a_\nu(u)\|\le K\|u-1\|
\tag{3.1}
\]
for one finite constant \(K\). Here \(U_0(U_B(C_1))\) means
the identity component of \(U_B(C_1)\).

In particular, (3.1) applies continuously to any compact
parameter family whose image lies in this neighborhood, and
all variable factors are \(1\) wherever the original family is
\(1\). Expanding the conjugates also gives a finite word in the
two original endpoint groups; the fixed conjugators in that
expanded word need not individually equal \(1\) at a parameter
basepoint.

**Proof.** For a fixed \(Y=Y^*\in C_1\),
\[
\frac{X-\operatorname{Ad}(e^{itY})(X)}{t}
   \longrightarrow i[X,Y]
\tag{3.2}
\]
in operator norm as maps \(B_{\rm sa}\to B_{\rm sa}\).
The exponential power series gives this convergence uniformly
on the unit ball of \(B_{\rm sa}\).

Replace every commutator in (2.3) by (3.2), using one sufficiently
small nonzero \(t\). The resulting bounded real-linear operator
\(T_t:B_{\rm sa}\to B_{\rm sa}\) satisfies
\[
\|T_t-I\|<1/2
\]
and is invertible by a Neumann series. Substituting \(T_t^{-1}a\)
in its defining formula gives finitely many bounded real-linear
maps \(R_\nu:B_{\rm sa}\to(C_{\epsilon_\nu})_{\rm sa}\) and
fixed \(v_\nu\), each either \(1\), \(e^{itP_j}\), or \(e^{itQ_j}\),
such that
\[
\sum_{\nu=1}^q \operatorname{Ad}(v_\nu)R_\nu=I.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Negative coefficients and \(2/t\) are absorbed into the \(R_\nu\).
Each \(v_\nu\) has the path \(s\mapsto e^{istP_j}\), or its
\(Q_j\) version, inside \(U_B(C_1)\).

Define
\[
\Phi(a)=\prod_{\nu=1}^q
       v_\nu\exp(iR_\nu(a))v_\nu^*.
\]
For \(a\) sufficiently small the principal logarithm exists, and
\[
G(a)=-i\log\Phi(a)
\]
is a continuously differentiable map between real Banach spaces.
Equation (3.3) says \(G(0)=0\) and \(DG(0)=I\).

Here is an explicit local inversion argument. Choose \(\rho>0\)
so that \(G\) is defined on the closed ball of radius \(\rho\)
and \(\|DG(a)-I\|\le1/2\) there. For
\(\|z\|<\rho/2\), the map
\[
a\longmapsto z-(G(a)-a)
\]
maps that closed ball to itself and is a contraction with
constant \(1/2\). Its fixed point \(R(z)\) satisfies
\[
G(R(z))=z,\qquad \|R(z)\|\le2\|z\|,
\]
and depends Lipschitz-continuously on \(z\), with constant at
most \(2\). For \(\delta\) small enough, take
\(z=-i\log u\) and set \(a_\nu(u)=R_\nu(R(z))\).
The principal logarithm is bounded by a fixed multiple of
\(\|u-1\|\) in this neighborhood. This proves every assertion
in (3.1). \(\square\)

The radius and constants depend on the actual fullness
coefficients in (2.1). No uniform bound in the dimension-drop
matrix size is asserted.

## 4. The stable-range input and its precise use

We use the coefficient-dependent matrix-piece lemma from
[the soft-module note](../../notes/stw58-soft-module-compression-attempt-2026-09-05.md),
in its isomorphism form:

> If \(D\) has stable rank one, \(k\ge1\), and \(n\ge k+2\),
> stabilization identifies
> \(\pi_k U_n(D)\) with \(K_{k+1}(D)\), where
> \(U_n(D)=\{1_n+a:a\in M_n(D),\ 1_n+a\text{ unitary}\}\).
> For unital \(D\), use the ordinary \(U(M_n(D))\).

This is the conservative stable range obtained by the stable-rank
bound for \(C(S^k,\widetilde D)\), Rieffel's degree-zero theorem,
and splitting evaluation and the scalar quotient. The quotient
splitting is an actual map:
\[
H\longmapsto H\,j(q(H))^*.
\]
It preserves a based homotopy and both endpoints whenever their
scalar quotients are \(1\). Thus the input gives surjectivity as
well as injectivity for the nonunital hereditary pieces. It is
not an inference that stable rank one alone gives all-degree
K-stability.

If \(C\subset B\) is full hereditary, its inclusion induces the
Morita isomorphism in stable K-theory. Consequently a piece
\(C\cong M_n(D)\) in the stated range has
\[
\pi_k U_B(C)\ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\ K_{k+1}(B).
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 5. Cancellation through the full overlap

**Theorem 5.1.** Fix \(k\ge1\). Suppose \(C_0,C_1,C_{01}\)
are full hereditary subalgebras of \(B\), with
\(C_{01}\subset C_0\cap C_1\), and each has an isomorphism
\(C_\alpha\cong M_{n_\alpha}(D_\alpha)\) with
\[
\operatorname{sr}(D_\alpha)=1,\qquad n_\alpha\ge k+2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let \(\gamma:S^k\to U(B)\) be based at \(1\), and suppose
\[
\gamma=\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_l
\tag{5.2}
\]
for based continuous families
\(\gamma_j:S^k\to U_B(C_{\epsilon_j})\),
\(\epsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\). If the stable K-class of \(\gamma\)
is zero, then \(\gamma\) admits a based null-homotopy in \(U(B)\).

The same conclusion holds if the factors in (5.2) are conjugated
by fixed unitaries in \(U_0(B)\), or if the right-hand side only
approximates \(\gamma\) uniformly within distance strictly less
than \(2\).

**Proof.** Let \(\alpha_j\in K_{k+1}(B)\) be the stable class of
\(\gamma_j\). The overlap isomorphism (4.1) supplies a based
\(\eta_j:S^k\to U_B(C_{01})\) with stable class \(\alpha_j\).
Because \(C_{01}\subset C_{\epsilon_j}\), the family
\[
\delta_j=\gamma_j\eta_j^{-1}
\]
is supported in \(C_{\epsilon_j}\). Its stable class in \(B\)
is zero and hence, by full hereditary Morita invariance, its
stable class in that piece is zero. Injectivity in (4.1)
gives a based contraction \(H_j\) of \(\delta_j\).

Multiplying \(H_j\) by \(\eta_j\) gives a based homotopy
\(\gamma_j\simeq\eta_j\) within its endpoint piece. Multiplying
these homotopies in their original order gives
\[
\gamma\simeq\eta_1\cdots\eta_l.
\]
The product on the right lies in \(U_B(C_{01})\), and its stable
class is \(\sum_j\alpha_j=0\). Injectivity for the overlap
contracts it there. All homotopies fix the sphere basepoint.

For a conjugated factor \(v_j\gamma_jv_j^*\), a fixed path
from \(v_j\) to \(1\) in \(U(B)\) gives a based homotopy to
\(\gamma_j\). This reduces that case to (5.2).

Finally, if \(f\) denotes the product and
\(\sup_x\|\gamma(x)-f(x)\|<2\), then
\[
(x,t)\longmapsto
\gamma(x)\exp\!\bigl(t\log(\gamma(x)^*f(x))\bigr)
\]
is a based homotopy from \(\gamma\) to \(f\). The principal
logarithm is continuous because the spectrum avoids \(-1\)
uniformly. Apply the preceding case to \(f\). \(\square\)

This proof does not require the separate factor classes to
vanish. The overlap transfers and cancels those classes before
any contraction is requested.

## 6. Application to a prime dimension-drop map

Let \(B\) be simple unital of stable rank one and let
\(\theta:Z_{N,N+1}\to B\) be unital. For the canonical commuting
order-zero maps, put
\[
h_0=\phi_0(1),\qquad h_1=\phi_1(1),\qquad h_0+h_1=1.
\]
Assume \(h_0h_1\ne0\). The supporting matrix homomorphisms in
the multiplier algebras give
\[
C_0\cong M_N(D_0),\qquad
C_1\cong M_{N+1}(D_1),\qquad
C_{01}\cong M_{N(N+1)}(D_{01}).
\]
The hereditary coefficient corners have stable rank one.
Simplicity makes all three pieces full. Thus for \(N\ge k+2\)
Theorem 5.1 applies, while Theorem 3.1 supplies the continuous
factorization on a neighborhood of \(1\).

A finite simple pure algebra is one source of the stable-rank-one
hypothesis. Purity is not additionally used in either theorem.
The nonzero-overlap assumption is explicit: an endpoint quotient
of the dimension-drop algebra can have zero overlap.

## 7. What still prevents a global contraction

Let a based sphere family be stably null, with no factorization
given. Covering its parameter sphere by sets on which local
logarithms or Theorem 3.1 apply does not yield (5.2): the
factorizations on different sets need not agree on their
intersections. Likewise, Lie generation of \(U_0(B)\) pointwise
does not supply a continuous global choice of factors.

Theorem 5.1 removes the K-class obstruction *after* global
factorization exists. Theorem 3.1 proves continuous local
factorization with exact unitary and based conditions. Neither
theorem controls the transition topology needed to combine those
local factorizations over a whole sphere. No homotopy-pushout
claim for the hereditary unitary groups is used.

Therefore the remaining task is now precisely the global
parameterized factorization (or an alternative deformation into
one endpoint piece). LVIII remains unresolved by this artifact.
