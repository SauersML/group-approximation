# Three coordinates for bilateral compression; one involution for localization

2026-09-13. Written proofs, not Lean formalizations. No literature-priority claim.

Let X be nonempty, compact, metrizable and zero-dimensional, let T be a
homeomorphism, and let R=LC(X,k) crossed_T Z over a finite field k.
Write u f u^-1=f composed with T^-1, Y=CR(T), and
I=LC_c(X\Y,k) crossed_T Z. The existing core theorem proves that R/I
is LEF and I is locally matricial, generated by compressed-clopen defects.

## 1. The fourth coordinate is unnecessary in the general linear group

**Theorem.** For every n>=3, GL_n(R) is MF if and only if it is LEF,
if and only if R is directly finite, if and only if X=Y. Over F2,

    Rad_MF(GL_n(R)) = ker(GL_n(R) -> GL_n(R_Y)).                 (1)

This kernel is locally finite, and the maximal MF quotient is the LEF
restriction image. Over any finite field, every relative elementary
root with coefficient in I is MF-invisible in GL_n(R).

The distinction between GL and EL is essential to this proof. It uses a
diagonal copy of the bilateral shift, which need not be elementary.
No unrestricted EL_3 theorem is asserted.

### A finite central defect in three coordinates

Take a clopen P with T(P) contained in P and nonempty D=P\T(P). Put
p=1_P, d=1_D. The sets T^j(D), j in Z, are pairwise disjoint. Define

    f_ab = u^a d u^-b,       a,b in Z.

Then f_ab f_ce=delta_bc f_ae. For a,b<0, both supports of this partial
shift lie outside P. The corner pRp contains the finitely generated
unital k-algebra S generated by

    s=up,          t=pu^-1,          ts=p,          st=p-d.

Let L=EL_3(S), embedded in GL_3(R) by adjoining identity on 1-p.
Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain gives property (T) for L. Put

    r = 1-f_-1,-1-f_-2,-2-f_-3,-3
          +f_-2,-1+f_-3,-2+f_-1,-3,
    W = diag(u,u,u) diag(1,1,r).

The unit r cycles the three negative levels -1 -> -2 -> -3 -> -1.
It is the identity off those levels and centralizes every element
supported in P. Consequently W conjugates L into L: on an elementary
coefficient a in S, conjugation sends a to u a u^-1=sat in S.

On the third coordinate W fixes the levels -1 and -2 individually.
On the first coordinate it sends level -1 to level 0. Define

    c = e_13(f_-1,-2),       ell = e_21(p),
    y = e_32(f_-1,0),        z = 1+E_33 f_-1,-2.

The element c centralizes L, and direct multiplication gives

    W c W^-1 = e_13(f_0,-2),
    delta = [W c W^-1,ell] = e_23(-f_0,-2),
    [y,delta] = z^-1.                                         (2)

For B=<L,W,c,y>, (2) puts z in the intrinsic compression defect D_B(L).
The element z commutes with L because its supports are outside P;
with W because its two third-coordinate levels are fixed; and with c
and y by the displayed matrix-unit products. Thus <z> is a nontrivial
finite central subgroup of B, of order char(k).

The manuscript's compression criterion kills z under every MF-target
homomorphism from B, hence under every such homomorphism from GL_3(R).
Two further commutators recover the ordinary defect root:

    [z,e_31(f_-2,0)] = e_31(f_-1,0),
    [e_23(f_0,-1),e_31(f_-1,0)] = e_21(d).                     (3)

Normal root commutators and root addition propagate (3) to every
coefficient in the two-sided ideal generated by d. Extending by identity
does the same in GL_n(R), n>=3. All compressed-clopen defects together
generate I, so every elementary root over I is invisible.

If X differs from Y, some such d is nonzero; therefore GL_n(R) is not
MF. If X=Y, the ring core theorem gives LEF. This proves the criterion
over every finite field. Over F2 the full restriction kernel is generated
by the roots over I, by the existing finite matrix-block argument, and
its restriction image is LEF. This proves (1).

The analytic input is still property (T) and the finite-normal compression
criterion. The simplification is algebraic: the incoming side of an
invertible dynamical system supplies two fixed auxiliary levels, so a
fourth matrix coordinate is unnecessary for GL.

## 2. Every finite transient pattern moves into a return corner by one involution

**Localization theorem.** Let C be a full clopen section with bounded
positive return times, let p_C=1_C and J=p_C I p_C. Over any finite field,
for every finite F contained in I there is a unit w in 1+I such that

    w^2=1,                 w F w^-1 contained in J.            (4)

Thus, over F2, every finite subset of K_n(I) is simultaneously conjugate
into the unitized K_n(J) by the scalar involution w I_n. Here K_n(I) is
the kernel of general linear restriction, and the unitization sends
h in GL_n(p_C R p_C) to (1-p_C)I_n+h. When n>=2, the conjugating
involution itself belongs to EL_n(R).

This strengthens normal generation: a single conjugate contains any
prescribed finite collection, including any finite subgroup of K_n(I).

**Proof.** Express F using finitely many compact clopen partial shifts,
and let K contained in X\Y contain all sources and ranges. Take a cover
of K by m wandering clopen sets. Each orbit meets K in at most m points.
Let H bound the return gaps to C.

For every point of K, add arrows to its first 2m strictly future returns
to C. They have displacement at most 2mH and split into finitely many
clopen domains. The union K' of all sources and targets is compact and
contained in X\Y. The local matrix-algebra construction, including all
these arrows and refining by K and C, gives a finite product of full
matrix algebras B_0 contained in I.

Every generated equivalence class meets K. The 2m chosen return points
from any such point are distinct, since the transient dynamics is free;
at most m belong to K. Hence the class contains at least m points of
C\K, enough to receive every point of its intersection with K.

In each finite matrix factor, match the coordinates belonging to K
injectively to fresh coordinates in C\K and swap each matched pair.
The resulting permutation is an involution. The finite clopen partition
in the matrix-algebra construction makes these choices constant on
each representative cell. Unitizing these permutations gives w in 1+I,
with w^2=1, and w 1_K w^-1 <= p_C. Since F lies in 1_K I 1_K, (4) follows.

For a finite collection of kernel matrices, include the entries of their
deviations and inverse deviations in F. Scalar conjugation moves all
those entries into J, so the matrices belong to the unitized K_n(J).
Over F2, K_n(I) is elementary for n>=2, which also locates w I_n there.

## 3. The improved rank budget and a rank-free aperiodic theorem

Suppose T has no points of periods less than k_0, and n*k_0>=3.
Then over every finite field,

    GL_n(R) MF iff GL_n(R) LEF iff R directly finite iff X=Y.    (5)

Over F2 its full MF radical is K_n(I), with LEF restriction image.
In particular **periods one and two are the only periods excluded by
this unit-group theorem**; period three is now allowed. For aperiodic
systems no external matrix amplification is needed at all.

To prove this, use the clopen marker construction to obtain a full
section C with return gaps in [k_0,2k_0-1]. Its return ring P_C=p_C R p_C
is again a zero-dimensional crossed product. Its core is C intersect Y,
and its transient ideal is J=p_C I p_C. The first k_0 tower levels give

    M_(k_0)(P_C) -> R

as a corner embedding, and hence GL_(n*k_0)(P_C) -> GL_n(R) after
unitization. Section 1 applies in this embedded group.

Over F2 its entire core kernel is invisible. It contains the phase-zero
placement of every element of K_n(J). Section 2 conjugates any element
of K_n(I) into that placement, proving the full-kernel assertion.
The LEF restriction image gives the opposite radical inclusion.

Over other finite fields it suffices to transfer one nonzero invisible
defect root from the return corner. If R is not directly finite, neither
is P_C: otherwise its ring core theorem makes it stably finite, and the
full tower corner realization of R would make R directly finite.
Thus section 1 supplies the nonzero root needed to prove (5).

The elementary-group threshold is still n*k_0>=4, n>=2, by the earlier
return argument. In particular aperiodic systems have the exact F2
radical formula at every elementary rank n>=2 and in the unit group.
No claim is made for unrestricted EL_3 or for fixed-point GL_2.

## 4. Fixed endpoints genuinely obstruct a universal unit-group statement

Let X=Z union {-infinity,+infinity} be the two-ended compactification of
the integers. Let T(j)=j+1 and fix the two endpoints. This is a compact
metrizable zero-dimensional system; its core is the two endpoints.
The coefficient functions over F2 are eventually constant at each end.
In the faithful action on F2^(Z), R consists of band matrices which are
Laurent convolution operators sufficiently far out at either end.
For P={0,1,...} union {+infinity}, the clopen defect P\T(P) is {0};
the ring is not directly finite, and its transient ideal is M_fin(Z,F2).

Nevertheless

    R^x = GL_fin(Z,F2) semidirect_<shift> Z                     (6)

is LEF. Here GL_fin means g-1 has finite matrix support, not merely
finite rank.

Indeed a unit's two Laurent symbols are z^(a_-) and z^(a_+). On the
negative and positive tails it therefore acts by the corresponding
translations. Choose N so both tail descriptions are exact. The tail
subspace spanned by basis points j<=-N and j>=N has codimension 2N-1.
Its image has codimension 2N-1+a_+-a_-. Invertibility forces equality,
so a_+=a_-=a. Multiplying by u^-a leaves a finite invertible block
extended by identity. Conversely every such block and every u^a is a
unit, proving (6).

For a finite group table in (6), include the finite supports of its
finitary factors and all translated supports used in the selected
products in one interval. Replace the shift by translation on a much
longer finite cycle and copy those finite matrices without wraparound.
This preserves all selected products. Distinct shift exponents remain
distinct on points outside the copied supports; equal exponents are
distinguished by the finitary factors. Thus the table embeds in a
finite general linear group, proving LEF.

So the unit group can be LEF while GL_3(R) is not MF and has full
locally finite core kernel as its MF radical. This example is inside
the same dynamical class as the theorem. The older binary Jacobson
unit calculation is a related, already established boundary example;
it is not claimed as a new result here. Neither example decides the
unrestricted GL_2 or EL_3 cases.

## 5. Why lowering the remaining bound needs a different method

Suppose every point of Y is fixed. Then **every Kazhdan subgroup of
GL_2(R) is finite**, even if X itself has transient points. More generally,
the same holds for R^x if every core point has period at most two.
These assertions hold over every finite field.

For GL_2, restrict a Kazhdan subgroup H to the core. Its image is finitely
generated. When T is the identity on Y, the finitely many locally constant
coefficients of its generators refine one finite clopen partition. The
image therefore lies in a finite product of GL_2(k[z,z^-1]), hence in a
finite product of GL_2(k(z)). These groups have the Haagerup property by
[Guentner--Higson--Weinberger, Theorem 4](https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_2005__101__243_0.pdf).
A Kazhdan subgroup of a Haagerup group is finite, so H has finite image.
Its finite-index kernel is locally finite by the transient matrix lemma.
It is also finitely generated, hence finite, proving H finite.

For the unit statement, T^2 is the identity on Y. Refine the coefficients
by a finite T-invariant clopen partition. Its atom cycles have lengths
one or two, and the corresponding crossed-product ring is a finite
product of M_l(k[z,z^-1]) with l<=2. Apply the same field-group argument
to the restriction image; the transient unit kernel is locally finite
over every finite field, so the conclusion follows as before.

If L is finite and gLg^-1 is contained in L, equality holds. Then g
normalizes both L and its centralizer, so every defining compression
commutator is trivial. Consequently the entire Kazhdan compression
criterion is inert in these groups, independently of how one chooses
the witness subgroup. This is a limitation of that criterion, **not a
proof that the groups are MF**. It also does not rule out a different
method, including one that does not use Kazhdan compression.

The manuscript therefore states the consequences without a free rank
budget parameter: no fixed points gives rank two, no periods one or two
gives units, and aperiodicity gives the exact unit-group reflection.
The bookkeeping inequality n*k_0>=3 is retained here to explain their
common return-tower proof.

## Proof and verification boundary

The new ingredients are the three-coordinate bilateral compression cell,
the simultaneous localization by one involution, and their combined
rank budget. The core construction, its locally matricial ideal, and
the clopen marker/return-ring identities are earlier results in this
repository. Finite matrix checks can verify (2)--(3) and the permutation
placement; they cannot verify a genuine one-sided compression in a
finite model. The general proofs above use the infinite system.
