## 0. Setting and notation
- X is an infinite minimal subshift with shift T, and q is a prime power.
- R = LC(X,F_q) ⋊_T Z, with u f u^{-1} = f∘T^{-1}, so u e_U u^{-1} = e_(TU). Every element is uniquely Σ_j f_j u^j,
  and (f u^i)(f' u^j) = f (f'∘T^{-i}) u^{i+j}.
- G = EL_2(R) = ⟨e_12(r), e_21(r) : r ∈ R⟩, M = M_2(R), and [x,y] = xyx^{-1}y^{-1}.
- T has no periodic points (minimal and infinite).

## 1. Theorem
**Theorem.** Every normal subgroup of G is central or equal to G. The centre Z(G) consists of scalar matrices λI_2,
λ ∈ F_q^×. Consequently G/Z(G) is an infinite, finitely generated, simple group with property (T) that is LEF.

The case q = 2 is `subshift-el-2-is-simple-kazhdan-lef`. There Z(G) = 1, and step 1 below reduces to the unit trick
LC(X,F_2)^× = {1}. Over F_q with q > 2 that trick fails, since a nonconstant unit c ∈ LC(X,F_q)^× commutes with every
e_ij(e_V). Steps 1–3 replace it, and step 4 is the same as over F_2.

## 2. Centre
**Lemma C.** Suppose g ∈ GL_2(R) commutes with e_12(1), e_21(1), e_12(u) and e_12(e_U) for every clopen U. Then
g = λI_2 with λ ∈ F_q^×. In particular Z(G) ⊆ F_q^× I_2.

*Proof.*
- g E_12 = E_12 g and g E_21 = E_21 g force g_21 = g_12 = 0 and g_11 = g_22. So g = cI_2 with c = Σ_j c_j u^j ∈ R.
- For clopen U, e_U c − c e_U = Σ_j c_j (e_U − e_(T^jU)) u^j = 0. Evaluate the u^j-coefficient at x with x ∈ U and
  T^{-j}x ∉ U, which exists for j ≠ 0 because T^{-j}x ≠ x. This gives c_j(x) = 0. So c ∈ LC(X,F_q).
- cu = uc means c∘T^{-1} = c. The level sets of c are clopen and invariant, so c is a constant λ by minimality.
- c is invertible, so λ ≠ 0. ∎

## 3. Simplicity
Let 1 ≠ N ⊴ G with N ⊄ Z(G), and pick g ∈ N ∖ Z(G). Let w ≥ 0 bound the absolute values of the exponents of u in
the entries of g and g^{-1}, and put m = w + 1.

**Small sets.** A clopen V is *small* if
- (a) V ∩ T^jV = ∅ for 0 < |j| ≤ 2m, and
- (b) f∘T^i is constant on V for every |i| ≤ m and every coefficient f of every entry of g or g^{-1}.

T has no periodic points and only finitely many functions f∘T^i occur, so every point has a small clopen
neighborhood. By compactness every clopen set is a finite disjoint union of small ones.

### Step 1: a noncommuting root through a short tower
*Claim.* Some h = e_ij(s), with {i,j} = {1,2}, s ∈ {e_V, e_V u} and V small, does not commute with g.

*Proof.*
- Suppose g commutes with all of them. Write a clopen U as a disjoint union of small V_t. Then
  e_ij(e_U) = Π_t e_ij(e_(V_t)) and e_ij(e_U u) = Π_t e_ij(e_(V_t) u), by additivity of e_ij.
- So g commutes with e_12(e_U), e_12(e_U u), e_21(e_U) and e_21(e_U u) for every clopen U. Taking U = X gives
  e_ij(1) and e_12(u).
- Lemma C gives g = λI_2, which is central in G. This contradicts g ∉ Z(G). ∎

Fix such h = e_ij(s), put c ∈ {0,1} with s = e_V u^c, and put k = [g,h] = g h g^{-1} h^{-1}. Then k ∈ N, since N is
normal, and k ≠ 1. Also V ≠ ∅, since s ≠ 0.

### Step 2: k lies in a finite general linear group and is not scalar
**Tower units.** For |a|, |b| ≤ m put ε_ab = e_(T^aV) u^(a−b).
- ε_ab ε_a'b' = e_(T^aV ∩ T^(a−b+a')V) u^(a−b+a'−b').
- If b = a' this is ε_ab'. If b ≠ a', then 0 < |a'−b| ≤ 2m, and by (a) the intersection is empty.
- So ε_ab ε_a'b' = δ_(b a') ε_ab'. The ε_ab are nonzero, because V ≠ ∅, hence linearly independent over F_q.

**The finite group.**
- Put d = 2(2m+1) = 2(2w+3) ≥ 6. Index the coordinates of F_q^d by pairs (p,a) with p ∈ {1,2} and |a| ≤ m.
- ψ(E_((p,a),(p',b))) = ε_ab E_pp' defines an injective, multiplicative, F_q-linear map ψ: M_d(F_q) → M.
- With e = ψ(I_d), A ↦ I_2 − e + ψ(A) is an injective homomorphism GL_d(F_q) → GL_2(R). Call its image H, and call
  the image of SL_d(F_q) S.

**S ⊆ G.**
- SL_d(F_q) is generated by the transvections t_αβ(λ) = I_d + λE_αβ, α ≠ β, λ ∈ F_q.
- If α = (p,a) and β = (p',b) with p ≠ p', the image is I_2 + λ ε_ab E_pp' = e_pp'(λ ε_ab) ∈ G.
- If p = p' and a ≠ b, let r be the other block index, and γ = (r,a). Then α, γ, β are distinct and
  t_αβ(λ) = [t_αγ(λ), t_γβ(1)] in SL_d(F_q). Both factors map into G, so the image of t_αβ(λ) lies in G.

**The product formula.**
- Let f be a coefficient of an entry of g, and f' of an entry of g^{-1}. Let |i|, |j| ≤ w. Then
  `(f u^i)(e_V u^c)(f' u^j) = f e_(T^iV) (f'∘T^(−i−c)) u^(i+c+j)`.
- On T^iV, f is constant by (b), because |i| ≤ m. At T^i v, f'∘T^(−i−c) equals (f'∘T^(−c))(v), which is constant on
  V by (b), because |c| ≤ 1 ≤ m.
- So the product is μ ε_(i, −(c+j)) for some μ ∈ F_q. Its indices satisfy |i| ≤ w and −(w+1) ≤ −(c+j) ≤ w.

**k − I_2 and k^{-1} − I_2.**
- Put X = ghg^{-1} − I_2 = g(sE_ij)g^{-1} and Y = h^{-1} − I_2 = −sE_ij = −ε_(0,−c) E_ij. Since (sE_ij)^2 = 0,
  h = I_2 − Y and g h^{-1} g^{-1} = I_2 − X.
- By the product formula every entry of X lies in span{ε_ab : |a| ≤ w, −m ≤ b ≤ w}. Every entry of Y lies in
  span{ε_(0,−c)}.
- The entries of XY are sums of ε_(a'b') ε_(0,−c) = δ_(b'0) ε_(a',−c), and those of YX are sums of
  ε_(0,−c) ε_(a'b') = δ_(−c,a') ε_(0,b'). Both have second index in [−m, w].
- k = (I+X)(I+Y), so k − I_2 = X + Y + XY. And k^{-1} = h g h^{-1} g^{-1} = (I−Y)(I−X), so k^{-1} − I_2 = −X − Y + YX.
- So k = I_2 + ψ(A) and k^{-1} = I_2 + ψ(B). Injectivity and multiplicativity of ψ give (I_d+A)(I_d+B) = I_d. So k is
  the image of κ := I_d + A ∈ GL_d(F_q), and k ∈ H.

**κ is not scalar.**
- Every entry of k − I_2 lies in the span of the ε_ab with second index b ≤ w < m. So the columns of A indexed by
  (1,m) and (2,m) vanish, and κ fixes those two basis vectors.
- If κ = λI_d, then λ = 1 and k = I_2, a contradiction. So κ is not scalar.
- This is the rank-two analogue of `root-localization-commutator-is-never-scalar`. The margin m = w+1 leaves the top
  tower level untouched even though the roots e_V u shift the second index by one.

### Step 3: the finite simple group lies in N
- For σ ∈ S, [k,σ] ∈ N because k ∈ N and σ ∈ G. Also [k,σ] ∈ S, because k ∈ H and S ⊴ H. So [k,S] ⊆ N ∩ S, and
  N ∩ S ⊴ S.
- Suppose N ∩ S ⊆ Z(S). Then σ ↦ [k,σ] is a homomorphism S → Z(S), since [k,στ] = [k,σ]·σ[k,τ]σ^{-1} and [k,τ] is
  central in S. SL_d(F_q) is perfect for d ≥ 3, so this homomorphism is trivial. Then κ centralizes SL_d(F_q), which
  makes κ scalar, contradicting step 2.
- So N ∩ S is a noncentral normal subgroup of S ≅ SL_d(F_q). S is perfect and S/Z(S) = PSL_d(F_q) is simple (d ≥ 6),
  so N ∩ S = S: if K ⊴ S is not central, then KZ(S) = S, so S/K is abelian and K ⊇ [S,S] = S.
- In particular e_12(e_V) = image of t_((1,0),(2,0))(1) ∈ N, because ε_00 = e_V.

### Step 4: the level through three Peirce blocks
- Put e = e_V and e' = 1 − e_V. By minimality and compactness finitely many translates T^aV cover X, and
  e_(T^aV) = u^a e u^{−a}. So 1 = 1 − Π_t(1 − e_(T^(a_t)V)) ∈ ReR.
- V ≠ X, because V ∩ TV = ∅ and V ≠ ∅, so X∖V is a nonempty clopen set. The same argument gives Re'R = R.
- Put E_1 = eE_11, E_2 = eE_22 and E_3 = e'I_2. These are orthogonal idempotents with sum I_2, each full in M
  (part 1, "Fullness"). Put Y_AB = I_2 + E_AME_B for A ≠ B.
- **Lemma 1 (part 1).** If x² = y² = 0 and yx = 0, then [I+x, I+y] = I+xy.
- **Lemma 2 (part 1, (i)(ii)).** Each Y_AB lies in G, and the Y_AB generate G. Both proofs use only Lemma 1 and
  fullness, with no hypothesis on q. For x ∈ E_AME_B, the diagonal entries z = f_A^p z f_B^p satisfy
  I + zE_pp = [e_pp'(z), e_p'p(f_B^p)]. And e_12(e'r e') is a product of commutators of Y_32 and Y_23 elements.
- For A ≠ B put J_AB = {x ∈ E_AME_B : I_2 + x ∈ N}. It is an additive group, because (I+x)(I+x') = I+x+x' on Y_AB.
- **Propagation.** Let A, B, C be distinct.
  - For x ∈ J_AB and y ∈ E_BME_C we have yx = 0, so I + xy = [I+x, I+y] ∈ N. Hence J_AB·E_BME_C ⊆ J_AC.
  - For z ∈ E_CME_A we have xz = 0, so I + zx = [I+z, I+x] ∈ N. Hence E_CME_A·J_AB ⊆ J_CB.
  - These identities are sign-free and hold over every F_q.
- **The chain.** Let x_0 = e_V E_12 ∈ J_12, which holds since I + x_0 = e_12(e_V) ∈ N by step 3. Additive spans and
  fullness give:
  - J_13 ⊇ x_0·E_2ME_3 = E_1ME_3 (row 2 of y ∈ E_2ME_3 runs through eRe');
  - J_12 ⊇ J_13·E_3ME_2, which spans E_1ME_2 (E_3 full);
  - J_32 ⊇ E_3ME_1·J_12, which spans E_3ME_2 (E_1 full);
  - J_31 ⊇ J_32·E_2ME_1, which spans E_3ME_1 (E_2 full);
  - J_23 ⊇ E_2ME_1·J_13, which spans E_2ME_3 (E_1 full);
  - J_21 ⊇ J_23·E_3ME_1, which spans E_2ME_1 (E_3 full).
- So every Y_AB ⊆ N. By Lemma 2 they generate G, and N = G. ∎

G is not abelian, since e_12(1) and e_21(1) do not commute. So the normal subgroups are exactly the central ones and
G, and G/Z(G) is simple.

## 4. The remaining properties of G/Z(G)
- **Finite centre.** Z(G) ⊆ F_q^× I_2 by Lemma C.
- **Infinite.** G contains e_12(LC(X,F_q)), which is infinite, and Z(G) is finite.
- **(T) and finite generation.** Take W clopen with ∅ ≠ W ≠ X. By minimality e_W and 1 − e_W are full.
  `complementary-full-idempotents-make-el-2-kazhdan` gives property (T) for G, and so for G/Z(G). A countable group
  with (T) is finitely generated (Bekka–de la Harpe–Valette, Thm 1.3.1; recalled, not re-read). Unlike EL_n for n ≥ 3,
  EL_2 of a finitely generated ring need not be finitely generated, so (T) is used here.
- **LEF.**
  - Let φ: R → ∏_ω M_(N_k)(F_q) be the unital F_q-linear embedding (import, item 3). It sends λ ∈ F_q to λ·1.
  - Take a finite F ⊆ G/Z(G) and lifts F̃ ⊆ G. For distinct classes, g g'^{-1} ∉ Z(G). A scalar matrix lying in G is
    central, so g g'^{-1} − λI_2 ≠ 0 in M_2(R) for every λ ∈ F_q.
  - These finitely many nonzero matrices, the entries of the lifts and their inverses, and the products stay nonzero
    or are preserved for ω-almost every k. So F̃ maps into GL_(2N_k)(F_q), product-preservingly, and distinct classes
    stay distinct modulo scalars.
  - The induced map F → PGL_(2N_k)(F_q) is injective and preserves products that stay in F. So G/Z(G) is LEF, hence
    sofic and hyperlinear.

## 5. Model tests
- **q = 2.** Lemma C gives Z(G) = 1, and the theorem recovers `subshift-el-2-is-simple-kazhdan-lef` part 2. The extra
  roots e_V u and the non-scalar step are harmless there.
- **Periodic X** (a finite orbit of size p). The hypothesis fails. R maps onto M_p(F_q[t^{±1}]), and EL_2(R) has
  noncentral proper normal subgroups (congruence kernels), consistent with the need for no periodic points in the
  small-set construction.
- **Non-minimal X without periodic points.** Steps 1–3 go through (the small sets exist). Step 4 fails: e_V need not be
  full, and a closed invariant subset gives a proper ideal I with a normal subgroup EL_2(R,I) ⊆ N. Lemma C also fails
  (invariant nonconstant c).
- **Why the margin.** With m = w, the root e_V u shifts the second index to −(w+1), outside the tower; and b = ±m could
  both occur, so κ could a priori be scalar. With m = w+1 all products stay inside the tower and the level b = +m is
  never hit.
- **n ≥ 3.** Steps 1–3 are the same with d = n(2m+1), and the level step is the note's two-sided level ideal J with
  covering by translates. This recovers `subshift-elementary-group-is-simple-modulo-centre` (n = 3, every q) along the
  note's architecture, with no simplicity of R needed.

## 6. Novelty (bounded)
- Grep of main for EL_2 simplicity over F_q: only sk-el2's F_2 claim (explicitly not claiming q > 2), the cyclic-factor
  Morita case, and the n ≥ 3 nodes. No literature search was done.
- Normal subgroups of GL_2 and E_2 over general rings are known to be wild. The structure used here, complementary
  full idempotents with tower matrix units, is what makes rank two behave like rank three.
