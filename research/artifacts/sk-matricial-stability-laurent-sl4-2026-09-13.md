# sk-matricial-stability: is SL_4(F_q[t^±1]) point-norm matricially stable?

Lane `sk-matricial-stability`, 2026-09-13. All results below are unreviewed; the review requests are queued with sk-verify-7.

## 0. Verdict
- **The question stays open.** No proof of stability and no counterexample. The positive-characteristic Laurent case
  hits the same wall as `SL_4(Z)`: the full-spectrum corona representations.
- **Proved, unconditional.** Every finite-dimensional unitary representation of `SL_n(F_q[t^{±1}])`, `n ≥ 3`, is
  congruence. For `n ≥ 4` each one fixes a vector under the block `SL_2`, so the Laurent lattice is not purely matricial
  field. The proof uses torsion root subgroups, a level ideal and Bass–Milnor–Serre, with no superrigidity.
  - Node: `laurent-sl-n-representations-have-sl2-invariant-vectors`.
  - It replaces the superrigidity sketch in sk-cstar-mf §4.
- **Proved, conditional theorem.** If `Λ = SL_4(F_q[t^{±1}])` is matricially stable, then `C*_r(Γ)` is not MF for every
  countable `Γ ⊇ Λ`, in particular for every `G_X`.
  - Node: `laurent-sl4-stability-makes-overgroups-reduced-non-mf`.
  - Dichotomy form: any MF embedding of `C*_r(G_X)` forces `Λ` to be non-stable.
- **Landed as open, with the map of attempts.** `laurent-sl4-matricially-stable`, and the negative root at the note's group,
  `subshift-elementary-group-reduced-cstar-is-not-mf` (conditional route
  `subshift-el-reduced-not-mf-from-laurent-sl4-stability`).
- **For the note: no change.** The note does not discuss MF of reduced algebras, and rev2 decision 18 keeps
  "is C*_r(G_X) MF?" out of its questions.

## 1. Why the question matters
- sk-cstar-mf (79f422ef29) proved that `G_X ⊇ SL_{3k}(F_q[t^{±1}])` for every `k`, and that every representation of the
  note's finite models is a genuine congruence representation on these lattices. So those models never converge strongly.
- An MF embedding of `C*_r(G_X)` must therefore use asymptotic representations that are not genuine on the Laurent
  lattice. Stability of the lattice would say none exist.

## 2. Theorem 1: finite-dimensional representations are congruence and fix block-SL_2 vectors
Let `A = F_q[t,t^{-1}]`, `n ≥ 3`, and `ρ` a unitary representation of `SL_n(A)` on `C^d`.

**Step 1: finite images on root subgroups.** `U_ij = e_ij(A)` is elementary abelian of exponent `p`. Commuting
unitaries of order dividing `p` diagonalize simultaneously with eigenvalues in `μ_p`, so `|ρ(U_ij)| ≤ p^d`. The kernel
`I_ij = {r : ρ(e_ij(r)) = 1}` has finite index in `A`, and it is nonzero.

**Step 2: a level ideal.** For distinct `i,j,k`, `r ∈ I_ik` and `s ∈ A`, `e_ij(rs) = [e_ik(r),e_kj(s)] ∈ ker ρ`. So
`(I_ik) ⊆ I_ij`. The product `J` of all `(I_ik)` is a nonzero ideal with `e_ij(J) ⊆ ker ρ` for all `i ≠ j`.

**Step 3: congruence.**
- `ker ρ ⊇ E_n(A,J)`, the normal closure in `E_n(A) = SL_n(A)`.
- BMS Corollary 4.3 b), as quoted in the next list, gives `SL_n(A,J) = E_n(A,J)`.
- So `ρ` factors through `SL_n(A/J)`. The reduction map is onto because `A/J` is finite and `SL_n(A/J)` is elementary.

**Step 4: invariant vector.** `A` is a PID, so `A/J = F_q[t]/(m)` is a finite principal ideal ring. The chain-ring
transfer of Magee–de la Salle Proposition 2.1 (`sl4-over-finite-chain-rings-has-sl2-invariant-vectors`) gives a vector
fixed by the upper-left `SL_2(A/J)`, hence by `ρ(SL_2(A))`.

**Step 5: norms.** Take free `S,T ∈ SL_2(F_q[t])` (Nagao) and `z = S+S^{-1}+T+T^{-1}`. Then `‖ρ(z)‖ = 4` and
`‖λ(z)‖ = 2√3`.

**Import, verified from page images of the Numdam scan.** Bass–Milnor–Serre, Publ. IHÉS 33 (1967). Verbatim quotes
are in `bms-congruence-kernel-trivial-not-totally-imaginary-citation`:
- p. 60: the results apply to function fields.
- p. 71: "We shall call A totally imaginary if S_∞ consists of complex primes. This means that k is a totally imaginary
  number field, and that A is its ring of algebraic integers."
- p. 94: `E_n(A,q)` is the normal subgroup of `E_n(A)` generated by elementary matrices with `t ∈ q`.
- p. 95, Corollary 4.3: a) `SL_n(A) = E_n(A)`; b) "If A is not totally imaginary then C_q = {1} for all q."
- Not re-read: the Appendix definition of arithmetic type, because the MSI master dropped.

**Model tests.**
- `n = 2`: no third index, so Step 2 stops.
- `A = Z`: unipotents have infinite order, so Step 1 fails. This is why the `SL_4(Z)` theorem needs superrigidity.
- The trivial representation is consistent with the conclusion.

**Scope.** Steps 1–3 need only positive characteristic, arithmetic type and not totally imaginary. Step 4 needs finite
principal quotients, which Chinese remainder supplies for any Dedekind `A`. So Theorem 1 should extend to all rings of
`S`-integers of global function fields. That extension is not written out.

## 3. Theorem 2: stability of the Laurent lattice kills MF of every overgroup's reduced algebra
Let `ι : C*_r(Γ) → ∏M_{k_n}/⊕M_{k_n}` be injective, with `Λ ≤ Γ`.
1. `C*_r(Λ) ⊆ C*_r(Γ)` isometrically, so `ι` restricts.
2. Lift `ι(λ(g))` to unitaries `φ_n(g)`. This is an asymptotic homomorphism of `Λ`.
3. Stability gives genuine `π_n` with `‖π_n(g) − φ_n(g)‖ → 0`, so `limsup ‖π_n(x)‖ = ‖λ_Λ(x)‖` for `x ∈ C[Λ]`.
4. Theorem 1 gives `‖π_n(z)‖ = 4` whenever `k_n ≥ 1`, which happens infinitely often, while `‖λ_Λ(z)‖ = 2√3`.
   Contradiction.

The difference from `matricially-stable-overgroups-of-sl4z-are-not-reduced-mf` is that there the hypothesis is
stability of the overgroup. Restricting the MF embedding first shows that stability of the lattice alone suffices.
The same restriction sharpens that node as well.

Instances:
- `G_X` for every infinite minimal subshift `X` (via `SL_6(A) ≤ G_X`);
- `EL_n(R)`, `n ≥ 4`, for every ring `R ⊇ A`.

## 4. Map of the stability question

### 4.1 Facts about Λ
- f.p. of type `F_5` (Rank Theorem, local ranks `3+3`).
- Perfect (BMS 4.3 a).
- `H^ν(Λ;Q) = 0` for `ν ≠ 0, 6` (Harder), so `H_2(Λ;Z)` is finite.
- All finite-dimensional representations are congruence (Theorem 1).

### 4.2 Obstructions that are void or reduced
- **Projective models.** `finite-multiplier-projective-models-round-strictly` applies (finite `H_1`, `H_2`), so every
  asymptotically projective model rounds. Non-roundable models need non-scalar defects.
- **Even rational cohomology (Dadarlat Thm 1.1).** Only degree 6 is possible.
  - Sketch: the one-chamber quotient on the `t`-adic building gives
    `H^6(Λ;Q) = coker(⊕_panels H^3(P_i;Q) → H^3(I;Q))`, the Steinberg ⊗ Steinberg multiplicity at level one.
  - Through Lafforgue a cuspidal contribution would be a tame rank-4 local system on `G_m` with maximally unipotent
    monodromy at `0` and `∞`. The geometric tame fundamental group is procyclic, generated by inertia at `0`, so such a
    system cannot be geometrically semisimple.
  - Heuristically `H^6 = 0`. Not source-checked, and the residual spectrum is not treated.
- **Two-homology / Bott winding.** Dadarlat's quasi-representation invariants are maps on the finite group
  `H_2(Λ;Z)` into `Z`, so they vanish. Almost-commuting torus pairs such as `diag(t,t^{-1},1,1)` and
  `diag(1,1,t,t^{-1})` carry no Bott obstruction. The identification of the invariant with the Bott index is recalled.
- **Voiculescu pairs on unipotents.** Absent: the root subgroups are torsion. Almost-representations of finite groups
  are uniformly near genuine ones.

### 4.3 Where it is hard
- **Gluing.** `Λ` is the fundamental group of an infinite complex of finite groups on `X_0 × X_∞`, with quotient a
  chamber times a sector. Corrections on finite cell groups must agree across all cells, and pointwise defect gives no
  control deep in the sector.
- **Uniform stability (GLMR-type).** Controls uniformly small defect only (`corona-defect-is-not-uniform`), so it
  does not apply.
- **Spectrum.**
  - Stability implies the robust claim that every corona representation gives block-`SL_2` norm 4.
  - Characters of the column unipotent `A^3` with finite `SL_3(A)`-orbit factor through `A/J`, and there Magee–de la
    Salle averaging gives norm 4.
  - Generic characters have infinite orbits, where averaging gives only spectral gap (the positive-characteristic
    analogue of `mdls-averaging-at-non-torsion-character-gives-spectral-gap`), and MF embeddings have Haar spectrum.
  - This is the `SL_4(Z)` full-spectrum crux in positive characteristic.

### 4.4 What a counterexample needs
An asymptotic homomorphism of `Λ` whose block-`SL_2` Laplacian has norm bounded away from 4. Any such model is
far from genuine representations. It must also have non-scalar defects and full unipotent spectrum.
