# Bernoulli bundles over free actions: relative Rokhlin entropy is an orbit-relation invariant

Worker a-gs-tester-host, wave swarm-0917, 2026-09-17. Target hole:
`tester-host-has-positive-rokhlin-entropy-action`, toward `gottschalk-surjunctivity-conjecture`.

Conventions. Every group is countably infinite, and every action is p.m.p. on a standard probability
space. For `G ↷ (X, μ)` and a G-invariant sub-sigma-algebra F, Seward's relative Rokhlin entropy is

    h_G(X, μ | F) = inf { H(α | F ∨ I_G) : α a countable partition, σ-alg_G(α) ∨ F ∨ I_G = B(X) }.

Here `σ-alg_G(α)` is the smallest G-invariant sigma-algebra containing α. The shift is
`(g·ξ)(k) = ξ(g^{-1}k)` on `L^G`. For a free action `G ↷ (Y, ν)`, R_Y denotes its orbit relation and
`[R_Y]` its full group. Given a standard probability space (L, λ), put

    E_λ(G ↷ Y) := h_G(L^G × Y, λ^G × ν | B(Y)),

where G acts diagonally.

## 1. Theorem A (orbit-relation invariance)

**Theorem A.** Let `G ↷ (Y, ν)` and `H ↷ (Z, ζ)` be essentially free p.m.p. actions that are orbit
equivalent: there is a measure-preserving isomorphism `θ: Y → Z` of conull invariant sets with
`θ(G·y) = H·θ(y)` for almost every y. Then, for every standard probability space (L, λ),

    E_λ(G ↷ Y) = E_λ(H ↷ Z).

So `E_λ(R) := E_λ(G ↷ Y)` is well defined for any measured relation R generated by a free action, and
it depends only on the isomorphism class of R.

### Proof

**Step 1: the invariant sigma-algebra lies inside B(Y).** The Bernoulli shift `G ↷ L^G` is mixing for
infinite G, and hence weakly mixing (if λ is a point mass, `L^G` is a point). For a weakly mixing
`G ↷ X_1` and any `G ↷ Y`, a G-invariant vector of `L^2(X_1 × Y)` lies in `L^2(Y)`. The reason:
`L^2(X_1×Y) = L^2(Y) ⊕ (L^2_0(X_1) ⊗ L^2(Y))`, and weak mixing means that `L^2_0(X_1) ⊗ π` has no
nonzero invariant vector for any unitary representation π. So `I_G ⊆ B(Y)` modulo null sets, and

    E_λ(G ↷ Y) = inf { H(α | B(Y)) : σ-alg_G(α) ∨ B(Y) = B(L^G × Y) }.       (1.1)

**Step 2: the bundle as orbit labellings.** For y in Y and z in G·y, let `κ(y, z)` be the unique k in G
with `z = k·y` (freeness). To `(ξ, y)` attach the labelling of the orbit

    ℓ_{(ξ,y)} : G·y → L,   ℓ_{(ξ,y)}(z) = ξ(κ(y, z)^{-1}).

Equivalently `ξ(k) = ℓ(k^{-1}·y)`. The labelling does not move under G: with `z = k'·g·y`,

    ℓ_{(gξ, gy)}(z) = (gξ)(k'^{-1}) = ξ(g^{-1}k'^{-1}) = ξ((k'g)^{-1}) = ℓ_{(ξ,y)}(z).

In these coordinates, `g·(y, ℓ) = (g·y, ℓ)`: the group moves only the root. For fixed y, `ξ ↦ ℓ` is a
reindexing of coordinates by the bijection `k ↦ k^{-1}·y` from G to G·y, so it carries λ^G to the
product measure `λ^{G·y}`.

**Step 3: the full group acts on the bundle.** For φ in [R_Y], let `g_φ(y)` be the unique g with
`φ(y) = g·y`, and put `Y_g = {y : g_φ(y) = g}`. Define

    φ̃(ξ, y) = (g_φ(y)·ξ, φ(y)),   in labelling coordinates   φ̃(y, ℓ) = (φ(y), ℓ).

φ̃ agrees with g on `L^G × Y_g`, and the images `φ(Y_g)` are disjoint, so φ̃ is a measure-preserving
automorphism fibered over φ.

**Step 4: G-generation equals [R_Y]-generation.** Let α be a countable partition of `L^G × Y`, and put
`𝒜 = σ-alg_G(α) ∨ B(Y)`. This sigma-algebra is G-invariant and contains every `L^G × Y_g`. For A in 𝒜,

    φ̃(A) = ⋃_g g·(A ∩ (L^G × Y_g)) = ⋃_g (gA ∩ (L^G × g·Y_g)) ∈ 𝒜.

So 𝒜 is [R_Y]-invariant. Since G ⊆ [R_Y], 𝒜 is the smallest [R_Y]-invariant sigma-algebra containing
α and B(Y). Call it `𝒜_{[R_Y]}(α)`. Then (1.1) reads

    E_λ(G ↷ Y) = inf { H(α | B(Y)) : 𝒜_{[R_Y]}(α) = B(L^G × Y) }.            (1.2)

**Step 5: transport along θ.** Define `Θ: L^G × Y → L^H × Z` in labelling coordinates by

    Θ(y, ℓ) = (θ(y), ℓ ∘ θ^{-1}|_{H·θ(y)}).

In the product coordinates this is `η(h) = ξ(c(h, y))`, where `c(h, y) ∈ G` is defined by
`θ^{-1}(h^{-1}·θ(y)) = c(h, y)^{-1}·y`. For each h, the map `y ↦ c(h, y)` is measurable and countably
valued, so Θ is measurable. For fixed y, `h ↦ c(h, y)` is a bijection H → G, because θ maps G·y
bijectively onto H·θ(y) and both actions are free. So Θ carries λ^G to λ^H fiberwise, and by Fubini it
is measure-preserving. The inverse is built the same way from θ^{-1}, so Θ is an isomorphism fibered
over θ, and `Θ(B(Y)) = B(Z)`. For φ in [R_Y] and `ψ = θφθ^{-1}` in [R_Z],

    Θ φ̃ (y, ℓ) = (θφ(y), ℓ∘θ^{-1}) = ψ̃ (θ(y), ℓ∘θ^{-1}) = ψ̃ Θ (y, ℓ).

As φ runs over [R_Y], ψ runs over [R_Z]. Hence `Θ(𝒜_{[R_Y]}(α)) = 𝒜_{[R_Z]}(Θα)`, and
`H(α | B(Y)) = H(Θα | B(Z))`. The infima (1.2) for Y and for Z therefore agree. ∎

## 2. Consequences

**Lemma B (free Bernoulli shifts).** If λ is not a point mass, `G ↷ L^G` is essentially free. Fix
`g ≠ 1`. If g has infinite order, the orbits of `⟨g⟩` on G are infinite, and a point fixed by g is constant
on one infinite orbit, which has probability 0. If g has finite order n ≥ 2, G decomposes into infinitely
many ⟨g⟩-orbits of size n. Being constant on each of them is an independent event of probability at most
`p < 1`, so a fixed point has probability 0.

**Corollary A1 (Bernoulli entropy as an orbit-relation value).** Let `G ↷ (Y, ν)` be a free action that is
weakly contained in every free action of G. The Bernoulli action `[0,1]^G` qualifies, by
`abert-weiss-free-actions-weakly-contain-bernoulli`. Then for every (L, λ),

    h^Rok_G(L^G, λ^G) = E_λ(R_Y).

*Proof.* If λ is a point mass, both sides are 0. Otherwise `L^G` is free by Lemma B. Apply
`seward-weak-containment-relative-entropy-equality` (Seward, arXiv:1602.06680v2, Theorem 1.2) with
`X = L^G`, F trivial and this Y: `h_G(L^G) = h_G(L^G × Y | B(Y))`. The ergodic Bernoulli shift has trivial
I_G, so the left side is `h^Rok_G(L^G, λ^G)`. ∎

**Corollary A2 (upper bound from any free regenerator).** Let `Γ ↷ (W, ω)` be any free action. Then
`E_λ(R_W) <= h^Rok_Γ(L^Γ, λ^Γ) <= H(λ)`.

*Proof.* Let β be a generating partition of `L^Γ`. Its pullback generates `L^Γ × W` relative to B(W), and
`H(β | B(W)) <= H(β)`. Take the infimum over β. ∎

**Corollary A3 (transfer of the Rokhlin supremum).** Let `G ↷ Y` be free and weakly contained in all free
G-actions, and suppose `G ↷ Y` is orbit equivalent to a free action `Γ ↷ W`.

1. `h_sup(G) <= h_sup(Γ)`.
2. If `Γ ↷ W` is also weakly contained in all free Γ-actions, then `h_sup(G) = h_sup(Γ)`. Consequently
   INF(G) ⇔ INF(Γ), RBS(G) ⇔ RBS(Γ) and POS(G) ⇔ POS(Γ).

*Proof.* For λ with `H(λ) < ∞`, Theorem A, A1 and A2 give

    min{H(λ), h_sup(G)} = h^Rok_G(L^G) = E_λ(R_Y) = E_λ(R_W) <= h^Rok_Γ(L^Γ) = min{H(λ), h_sup(Γ)}.

The outer equalities are Seward's Theorem 1.10 (`seward-per-group-rokhlin-entropy-of-bernoulli-shifts`,
item 1). Letting H(λ) run over (0, ∞) gives (1). Under the hypothesis of (2) the inequality is an
equality by A1 for Γ, which gives (2). For the consequences: INF is `h_sup = ∞`. RBS implies INF, since
`min{H, h_sup} = H` for every finite H forces `h_sup = ∞`, and INF ⇒ RBS is Seward's. POS is `h_sup > 0`
by `positive-rokhlin-entropy-action-gives-positive-supremum`. ∎

**Corollary A4 (stable relations force the dichotomy).** Let `G ↷ Y` be free and weakly contained in all
free G-actions, and suppose `R_Y ≅ R_Y × R_0`, with R_0 the ergodic hyperfinite relation. Then
`h_sup(G) ∈ {0, ∞}`.

*Proof.* Let `P = ⊕_N Z/2` act by translation on `Z = (Z/2)^N` with Haar measure. The action is free, and
its orbit relation is R_0. The product action `G × P ↷ Y × Z` is free, with orbit relation `R_Y × R_0 ≅ R_Y`.
By Theorem A, A1 and A2,

    min{H(λ), h_sup(G)} = E_λ(R_Y) = E_λ(R_Y × R_0) <= min{H(λ), h_sup(G × P)}.

If `h_sup(G) < ∞`, then `h_sup(P × G) = 0` by Seward's Theorem 1.11, because P has arbitrarily large finite
subgroups. The right side is then 0 for every λ, which forces `h_sup(G) = 0`. ∎

**Reformulation of Seward's direct-product conjecture at Bernoulli X.** By A1 and A2,
`seward-direct-product-relative-rokhlin-entropy-conjecture` with `X = L^G` holds for every free Y if and
only if `R ↦ E_λ(R)` is constant, with value `h^Rok_G(L^G)`, on the orbit relations of free G-actions.
Theorem A makes each value of this function depend on the orbit relation alone.

## 3. Where the reframing dies on `U_*`

The reframed hole reads: *some weakly minimal free action Y of U_* has `E_λ(R_Y) > 0`.* Any lower bound
must be computed on the relation. The natural attack regenerates R_Y as the orbit relation of a free
action W of a better-understood group Γ, then bounds `E_λ(R_W)` from below on the Γ side. It has two
branches.

* **Branch (a): Seward's equality on Γ.** This needs W weakly minimal for Γ and `h_sup(Γ) = ∞`, and it
  gives INF(U_*) by A3(2). The only groups with `h_sup = ∞` certified in the graph are sofic, whether
  directly or through the permanence toolkit.
* **Branch (b): relative sofic entropy on Γ.** Here Γ is sofic, and sofic models of `L^Γ × W` relative to W
  would give `E_λ(R_W) >= H(λ)`.

**Invariant: Păunescu soficity of the orbit relation.** The target node says every infinite recursively
presented group embeds in U_*, and `finitely-presented-nonsofic-group-exists` supplies a nonsofic one, N.
The restriction of Y to N is a free action of a nonsofic group whose orbit relation lies inside R_Y. By
`free-action-containing-nonsofic-group-relation-is-nonsofic`, Y is not sofic. By
`paunescu-orbit-equivalent-free-actions-share-soficity`, **every regenerator W is a nonsofic action**,
whatever the group Γ.

**The step where every member dies** is certifying the lower bound on W.

* In branch (b), sofic models of the extension restrict to sofic models of W. Existence of models is
  expected to force W to be Păunescu-sofic, so the relative sofic entropy is `-∞`.
* In branch (a), any Γ certified with `h_sup = ∞` by sofic means is sofic. Its weakly minimal actions are
  weakly equivalent to Bernoulli shifts, and they are expected to be sofic.

Two steps are *not imported* in the graph: "Σ-models of a free action ⇒ Păunescu-sofic" and "a weakly
minimal free action of a sofic group is sofic". So this obstruction is recorded as an attempt, not as an
ESTABLISHED claim.

**Relation to existing barriers.** The Borel-structurability node asks for a lower bound that is an
isomorphism invariant of the measured action, neither factor-monotone nor weak-equivalence invariant.
E_λ is an invariant of that kind, and it is coarser, since it depends on the orbit relation only. Theorem
A shows the lower bound may be sought on the measured relation, so the choice of group is irrelevant. The
soficity barrier survives unchanged, because Păunescu soficity is itself an orbit-relation invariant.

**Side spark (finite kernels).** Case (iii) of `finite-kernel-rokhlin-ascent-from-weakly-minimal-lift`
asks for `h_Q(Ω/N × L^Q | B(Ω/N)) >= H − c`. By Theorem A this is a statement about the relation `R_{Ω/N}`.
It would follow from an orbit equivalence of Ω/N with a weakly minimal action of a Rokhlin-maximal group,
by A1. With a sofic Γ, however, it certifies only relations that are already sofic.
