## B. One repair this audit makes to the source

The source's Theorem 4.1 attributes to Linton a **two-case finite-stage
corollary**: for some finite `n`, either `G_Q^(n)` is free (residually
rationally solvable case) or `G_Q^(n)` is an extension of `R = G_Q^(ω)` by a
free group.  Only the *first* case appears in Linton's Corollary 1.2 as
verified above; the second is not a quoted statement of that paper.

It is nevertheless **true**, and derivable from the two verified sentences.
Recording the derivation removes a transcription premise from the graph.

**Lemma (quotient compatibility).**  Let `A` be a group and
`R ⊆ A_Q^(ω)`.  Then `(A/R)_Q^(i) = A_Q^(i)/R` for every `i < ω`.

*Proof.*  Induction on `i`; the case `i = 0` is trivial.  Write
`B = A_Q^(i)`, so by induction `(A/R)_Q^(i) = B/R`, and note
`R ⊆ A_Q^(ω) ⊆ A_Q^(i+1) = B_Q^(1)`.  Since `B → B/R` is onto,
`aR ∈ (B/R)_Q^(1)` iff `a^m ∈ [B,B]R` for some `m ≥ 1`.  Now
`[B,B]R ⊆ B_Q^(1)`, so `a^m ∈ B_Q^(1)`; and `B/B_Q^(1)` is torsion-free
abelian by construction, so `a ∈ B_Q^(1) = A_Q^(i+1)`.  The reverse inclusion
is immediate.  ∎

**Derivation of the second case.**  Let `r` and `R = <<r>>_G` be as in
Linton's Theorem 1.1.  Since `w ∈ <<r>>_F`, the quotient
`G/R = F/<<r>>` is again a **one-relator group**, with relator `r` of length
at most that of `w`; and Linton's corollary says it is residually rationally
solvable.  Applying Corollary 1.2(2) to it gives an `n ≤ |r| ≤ |w|` with
`(G/R)_Q^(n)` free.  By the Lemma, `(G/R)_Q^(n) = G_Q^(n)/R`.  So with
`E := G_Q^(n)`,

    1 --> R --> E --> free --> 1 ,

and since a free quotient splits, `E ≅ R ⋊ F₀`.  ∎

Both cases therefore hold with the *same* bound `n ≤ |w|`, and the graph node
`one-relator-soficity-localizes-at-rational-derived-stage` states the theorem
with this proof rather than the source's attribution.

# Pestov Open Question 4.9: are all one-relator groups sofic?

## Resolution attempt, 17 August 2026

### Verdict

I did **not** obtain a valid proof or a valid counterexample. I therefore do
**not** claim to have resolved Pestov's Open Question 4.9.

This file records the full attempt rather than disguising a gap as a proof.
The attempt did, however, produce several rigorous reductions that
substantially shrink the problem. The strongest new deductions I obtained are:

1. **Every residually rationally solvable one-relator group is sofic.** This
   follows from Marco Linton's 2025/2026 structure theorem together with the
   standard permanence theorem "sofic kernel + amenable quotient implies
   sofic." A finite rational-derived term is free, while the quotient by that
   term is solvable.
2. **Every one-relator group reduces at a finite rational-derived stage to a
   "free shell."** More precisely, for some finite `n` bounded in terms of the
   relator length, if `R = G_Q^(ω)` and `E = G_Q^(n)`, then `G/E` is solvable
   and hence amenable, and either `E` is free or `E ≅ R ⋊ F` for a free group
   `F`. Consequently `G` is sofic if and only if `E` is sofic. Thus the
   unresolved case is a very special free-by-rationally-perfect shell, not an
   arbitrary one-relator group.
3. **The abelianization of Linton's maximal rationally perfect radical can be
   computed exactly.** In Linton's notation, if
   `w ∈ r^k[<<r>>_F, <<r>>_F]` and `H = F/<<r>>_F`, and the radical is
   nontrivial, then `R_ab ≅ (Z/kZ)[H]`. In particular `k = 1` makes `R`
   perfect.
4. **Any counterexample must have primitivity rank exactly `2`.** Current
   one-relator structure theory gives residual finiteness when the primitivity
   rank is not `2`, hence soficity. A counterexample is therefore torsion-free
   and locally indicable.
5. **The currently known torsion-free nonsofic constructions do not transfer
   by subgroup embedding.** The Fournier-Facio construction contains an
   infinite finitely generated property-(T) subgroup (indeed the final group
   itself can be property (T)); a torsion-free one-relator group is locally
   indicable and therefore cannot contain such a subgroup.
6. **A tempting extension theorem that would nearly collapse the remaining
   problem cannot be used.** Ji--Ogle--Ramsey's claim that an extension of a
   sofic group by a residually amenable quotient is sofic is in a withdrawn
   preprint; arXiv explicitly records an error in the proof of its Theorem
   3.15. I initially followed this shortcut during the attempt and then
   rejected it after source verification.
7. **The generic semidirect-product shortcut is now known to be false.**
   Kun--Thom (August 2026) construct nonsofic generalized wreath products whose
   lamp and acting groups are sofic (indeed the acting examples are residually
   finite). Therefore one cannot simply assert `R` sofic and `F` free/sofic
   implies `R ⋊ F` sofic. The special Linton action remains a legitimate
   target, but it needs its own proof.

The sections below prove the valid statements, reconstruct the Magnus

## 1. The problem and notation

A **one-relator group** is a group admitting a presentation
`G = <X | w> = F(X)/<<w>>`.  The question is whether every such group is
sofic.

I use the rational derived series

    G_Q^(0) = G ,
    G_Q^(i+1) = ker( G_Q^(i) --> H_1(G_Q^(i); Q) ) ,
    G_Q^(ω)  = ∩_{i<ω} G_Q^(i) .

The quotient `G_Q^(i)/G_Q^(i+1)` is torsion-free abelian: by definition it is
the image of `G_Q^(i)` in its rationalized abelianization.  Hence for each
finite `n`, `G/G_Q^(n)` is solvable, in particular amenable.

The standard sofic permanence facts used below are:

- subgroups of sofic groups are sofic;
- residually finite groups are sofic;
- amenable groups are sofic;
- directed unions of sofic groups are sofic;
- if `N` is a normal sofic subgroup and `G/N` is amenable, then `G` is sofic;
- free groups are sofic.

I do **not** use closure under arbitrary quotients, arbitrary extensions,
arbitrary semidirect products, or arbitrary amalgamation over nonamenable
subgroups.  Those closure properties are unavailable, and in the
semidirect-product case generic closure is now false.

## 3. First global compression: a counterexample must have primitivity rank 2

Let `w` be the relator in a free group `F`.  Denote by `π(w)` its primitivity
rank.  Recent one-relator structure theory gives: if `π(w) ≠ 2`, then the
one-relator group `F/<<w>>` has a finite-index subgroup that is compact
special; in particular it is residually finite.

### Proposition 3.1

If a one-relator group `G = <X | w>` is nonsofic, then `π(w) = 2`.

**Proof.**  If `π(w) ≠ 2`, the cited virtual-specialness theorem gives
residual finiteness of `G`.  Every residually finite group is sofic,
contradiction.  ∎

### Corollary 3.2

Any nonsofic one-relator group is torsion-free and locally indicable.

**Proof.**  Primitivity rank `2` excludes the proper-power/torsion case.  By
the classical Brodskii--Howie theorem, every torsion-free one-relator group is
locally indicable.  ∎

This already rules out a large fraction of conceivable counterexample
mechanisms.  In particular, a counterexample cannot obtain nonsoficity merely
from the known torsion one-relator pathology, and it cannot contain an
infinite finitely generated subgroup with no infinite cyclic quotient.

## 4. Linton's radical and the finite-stage free-shell reduction

Marco Linton's theorem on residually rationally solvable one-relator groups is
the strongest structural input I found.

Let `G = F/<<w>>`.  Linton proves that there exist `r ∈ F` and `k ≥ 1` such
that, if `N = <<r>>_F`, then `w ∈ r^k[N,N]`, and the maximal rationally
perfect normal subgroup is `R := G_Q^(ω) = G_Q^(ω+1) = <<r>>_G`.  Moreover
`G/R ≅ F/<<r>>` is residually rationally solvable.

His finite-stage corollary supplies some finite `n` (with an explicit bound in
terms of the original relator length) such that either

- if `G` is residually rationally solvable, `G_Q^(n)` is free; or
- otherwise, `G_Q^(n)` is an extension of `R` by a free group.

This has an immediate sofic consequence.

### Theorem 4.1 — finite-stage free-shell reduction

For every one-relator group `G`, there is a finite `n` and a characteristic
subgroup `E := G_Q^(n)` such that:

1. `G/E` is solvable, hence amenable;
2. either `E` is free, or there is an exact sequence
   `1 → R → E → F₀ → 1` with `F₀` free and `R = G_Q^(ω)`;
3. consequently, `G` is sofic if and only if `E` is sofic;
4. in the non-residually-rationally-solvable case the extension splits, so
   `E ≅ R ⋊_α F₀` for some action `α : F₀ → Aut(R)`.

**Proof.**  Choose `n` from Linton's finite-stage corollary.

Because the rational derived series is characteristic, `E` is normal in `G`.
The quotient `G/E` has the finite normal series
`G/E ⊵ G_Q^(1)/E ⊵ ⋯ ⊵ G_Q^(n-1)/E ⊵ 1`.  Every factor is a quotient of
`G_Q^(i)/G_Q^(i+1)`, which is torsion-free abelian.  Hence `G/E` is solvable,
therefore amenable.

If `G` is sofic, then the subgroup `E` is sofic.  Conversely, if `E` is sofic,
the exact sequence `1 → E → G → G/E → 1` has a sofic kernel and amenable
quotient, hence `G` is sofic.

Finally, in the second Linton case, `E/R` is free.  Any extension of a free
group splits: choose arbitrary lifts in `E` of a free basis of `E/R`; the
universal property of the free group gives a section.  Therefore `E` is a
semidirect product `R ⋊ F₀`.  ∎

This theorem is an exact reduction, not merely a sufficient condition.  The
complete open question is now concentrated in the special shell `E`.

### Corollary 4.2 — a large new positive class

Every **residually rationally solvable one-relator group is sofic**.

**Proof.**  In this case Linton supplies a finite `n` for which `E = G_Q^(n)`
is free.  Free groups are sofic, and `G/E` is solvable/amenable by the proof
of Theorem 4.1.  Therefore `G` is sofic.  ∎

This is the cleanest successful theorem produced by the attempt.

### Corollary 4.3 — exact shape of the remaining case

If a one-relator group `G` is a counterexample, then:

- `G` is not residually rationally solvable;
- its Linton radical `R` is nontrivial and infinitely generated;
- for some finite `n`, `E = G_Q^(n) ≅ R ⋊ F₀` is itself nonsofic;
- `G/E` is solvable.

The problem is therefore not "prove every arbitrary extension of sofic groups
sofic."  It is: understand the very special free action on the very special
normally-one-generated rationally perfect radical coming from a one-relator
group.

## 5. Why the reduction does **not** continue from the shell to the radical

A highly tempting next line is: if `R` is sofic and `E/R` is free, then `E` is
sofic.  That statement is not a standard permanence property and must not be
inserted silently.  There are two reasons for special caution in August 2026.

### 5.1 Generic semidirect-product closure is false

Kun--Thom construct nonsofic generalized wreath products
`(⊕_{G/Γ} Z/2Z) ⋊ G` for explicit residually finite Kazhdan groups `Γ < G`.
The lamp group is amenable and hence sofic; the acting group is residually
finite and hence sofic; nevertheless the semidirect product is nonsofic.

Thus there is no valid blanket theorem "`N` sofic and `Q` sofic implies
`N ⋊ Q` sofic."

This does not rule out a special theorem when the acting group is free, but it
means such a theorem would have to be proved rather than assumed.

### 5.2 The "residually amenable quotient" extension theorem is withdrawn

During this attempt I initially found the preprint of Ji--Ogle--Ramsey
claiming that a sofic kernel with residually amenable quotient gives a sofic
total group.  If valid, this would combine beautifully with Linton because
`G/R` is residually rationally solvable and therefore residually amenable.

It is **not** a valid source for that step.  The arXiv record says the paper is
withdrawn and explicitly notes "Error in proof of Theorem 3.15."

Therefore I retract the provisional shortcut "`R` sofic implies `G` sofic"
that one could otherwise infer from that paper.  The only unconditional
equivalence established here is "`G` sofic iff `E` sofic" for the finite-stage
shell `E`, because `G/E` is genuinely amenable.

## 6. Exact computation of the Linton radical's abelianization

Although I could not prove the radical sofic, its abelianization has a
particularly rigid form.

Retain Linton's notation: `F` free, `N = <<r>>_F`, `W = <<w>>_F`, `G = F/W`,
`R = N/W`, `H = F/N ≅ G/R`, with `w ∈ r^k[N,N]`.  Assume `R` is nontrivial
(the trivial radical is already in the positive case).

### Proposition 6.1

There is an isomorphism of abelian groups, in fact of natural `ZH`-modules,
`R_ab ≅ ZH/kZH ≅ (Z/kZ)[H]`.

**Proof.**  First, `H = F/<<r>>` is residually rationally solvable by Linton.
Every rationally solvable quotient is torsion-free: its finite rational-derived
series has torsion-free abelian factors, and an extension of torsion-free
groups is torsion-free.  Hence a residually rationally solvable group is
torsion-free.  Therefore `H` is torsion-free.

By the torsion theorem for one-relator groups, if `r` were a proper power then
`H` would contain torsion.  Hence `r` is not a proper power.

Now apply Lyndon's identity theorem to the one-relator presentation
`H = F/<<r>>`.  It says that the relation module `N_ab = N/[N,N]` is a free
rank-one `ZH`-module generated by the class `[r]`: `N/[N,N] ≅ ZH`.

Since `w ∈ r^k[N,N]`, we have in `N_ab` that `[w] = k[r]`.  The image of the
normal closure `W = <<w>>_F` in `N_ab` is the `ZH`-submodule generated by
`[w]`, hence exactly `kZH`.

Finally, `R_ab = (N/W)_ab ≅ N/(W[N,N]) ≅ (N/[N,N])/(kZH) ≅ ZH/kZH`.  This is
`(Z/kZ)[H]`.  ∎

### Consequences

- If `k = 1`, then `R_ab = 0`; hence `R` is perfect.
- If `k > 1`, then every element of `R_ab` has exponent dividing `k`, and as an
  abelian group it is a direct sum of copies of `Z/kZ` indexed by `H`.
- If `k > 1` and `R` were nonsofic, then `R'` would also have to be nonsofic.
  Indeed `R/R'` is abelian/amenable; if `R'` were sofic, `R` would be
  sofic-by-amenable.

This calculation makes the radical considerably less mysterious algebraically,
but it does not imply soficity.

## 7. A precise "free-action" formulation of the unresolved shell

In the non-residually-rationally-solvable case, Theorem 4.1 yields
`E ≅ R ⋊_α F₀`.

Alekseev--Bradford introduced a notion of a **sofic action by automorphisms**
and prove, in particular, that if `R` and `F₀` are sofic and the action `α` is
sofic in their sense, then the semidirect product is sofic.

This gives a legitimate conditional target:

### Proposition 7.1

If the Linton radical `R` is sofic and the induced free-group action
`α : F₀ → Aut(R)` is sofic in the Alekseev--Bradford sense, then the original
one-relator group `G` is sofic.

**Proof.**  Their semidirect-product theorem makes `E = R ⋊_α F₀` sofic.  The
quotient `G/E` is amenable by Theorem 4.1, so `G` is sofic.  ∎

This is not yet a resolution because I could not verify either hypothesis
automatically for Linton radicals.

Alekseev--Bradford have a useful automatic criterion for actions on **finitely
generated residually finite** target groups.  But Linton explicitly shows that
in the unresolved torsion-free one-relator case, a nontrivial rationally
perfect radical is infinitely generated.  So that automatic criterion misses
precisely the difficult case.

There are also recent sofic-action theorems for free-group actions on
sets/graphs in other senses.  Those notions do not automatically imply the
Alekseev--Bradford automorphism-action condition needed for a group semidirect
product.  I found no theorem bridging that mismatch for Linton's radical.

## 8. The classical Magnus attack and its exact failure point

A direct positive proof should exploit the one-relator presentation.  The
natural attempt is Magnus induction.

For clarity take a two-generator torsion-free one-relator group and choose an
epimorphism `χ : G → Z`.  After Nielsen transformations one may arrange a
presentation `G = <a, t | w(a,t)>` in which the exponent sum of `t` in `w` is
zero.

Set `a_i = t^{-i} a t^i`.  Rewrite the relator as a word
`W_0 = W_0(a_m, a_{m+1}, …, a_n)` where `m` and `n` are the minimum and
maximum indices appearing.  Conjugating gives shifted relators
`W_i = W_0(a_{m+i}, …, a_{n+i})`.  Then `K := ker χ` has the infinite
presentation `K = < a_i (i ∈ Z) | W_i (i ∈ Z) >`.

The local one-relator pieces are `B_i = <a_{m+i}, …, a_{n+i} | W_i>`.  By the
Freiheitssatz, the overlap `C_i = <a_{m+i+1}, …, a_{n+i}>` is a free Magnus
subgroup.  The kernel is assembled as a bi-infinite iterated amalgam of the
`B_i` over the `C_i`.

This suggests induction on relator length because the `B_i` have shorter
Magnus complexity.

### Where it works

If each overlap `C_i` has rank at most one, then `C_i` is amenable.  Existing
sofic permanence under amalgamation/HNN extension over amenable subgroups
combines with directed-union closure to prove `K` sofic.  Since `G/K ≅ Z` is
amenable, `G` is then sofic.

### Where it fails

When `rank C_i = n - m ≥ 2`, the edge group is a nonamenable free group.
There is no general theorem saying an amalgam of sofic groups over an
arbitrary free subgroup is sofic.

Moreover, taking a finite interval of the infinite chain does **not** remain a
one-relator group.  A finite window has many staggered relators `W_i`.  Thus
one cannot simply say "each finite window is a smaller one-relator group and
use induction."  The windows are multi-relator staggered groups.

This is, in concrete form, the same obstruction emphasized in Berlai's work and
in the supplied repository.

### Exact equivalence for the cyclic quotient

For any epimorphism `χ : G → Z`, `G` is sofic if and only if `ker χ` is sofic.
The forward implication is subgroup closure; the reverse follows because the
quotient is amenable.  So the Magnus-kernel problem is not cosmetic: for such a
`χ` it is exactly the soficity problem.

### A theorem that would finish this route

A positive answer would follow from the special statement: every staggered
Magnus chain obtained from a one-relator kernel by iterated amalgamation over
its free Magnus subgroups is sofic.  This is much narrower than closure under
all free amalgams, but I did not prove it.

## 9. Why the newest graph-of-groups theorems did not close the Magnus gap

I checked recent graph-of-groups soficity results because they are the most
plausible way to repair §8.

Gao--Kunnawalkam Elayavalli--Mj prove soficity for important graph-of-groups
constructions, including certain doubles and graphs where the edge embeddings
have a controlled identical form or a separability hypothesis.

The Magnus HNN decomposition of a general one-relator group instead has **two
distinct embeddings** of a free associated subgroup into the base.  The theorem
I found does not identify those embeddings or turn arbitrary Magnus subgroups
into the required special configuration.

Likewise, recent "all free-group actions on graphs are sofic" results concern
soficity of actions on sets/graphs.  They do not, without another theorem,
imply that the corresponding action by automorphisms of Linton's group radical
is a sofic action in the semidirect-product sense of Alekseev--Bradford.

So the latest action machinery gives promising targets but no completed bridge.

## 10. Attempted disproof by embedding a known nonsofic group

Since soficity passes to subgroups, the most direct negative strategy is:
start with a known nonsofic group `Q`; embed `Q` into a one-relator group `G`;
conclude `G` is nonsofic.

The current torsion-free examples make this strategy look plausible at first.
It fails for a strong structural reason.

### Lemma 10.1 — property (T) obstruction

No torsion-free one-relator group contains an infinite finitely generated
property-(T) subgroup.

**Proof.**  Let `G` be torsion-free one-relator.  By Brodskii--Howie, `G` is
locally indicable: every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup maps onto `Z`.

Suppose `P ≤ G` is infinite, finitely generated, and has property (T).  Then
local indicability gives a surjection `P ↠ Z`.  Property (T) passes to
quotients, while `Z` does not have property (T).  Contradiction.  ∎

### Application to Fournier-Facio

Fournier-Facio's August 2026 paper constructs a finitely presented torsion-free
nonsofic group using an infinite property-(T) subgroup `Γ`, and the final
construction can be arranged so that the final group itself has property (T).

That group therefore cannot embed in a torsion-free one-relator group.

Could it embed in a torsion **one-relator** group?  No: torsion one-relator
groups are residually finite (indeed virtually special in the modern theory),
hence sofic, and every subgroup of a sofic group is sofic.

So the most immediate use of the new torsion-free nonsofic example is blocked
on both sides.

### Application to Kun--Thom wreath products

Kun--Thom's explicit nonsofic generalized wreath products also rely on
property-(T) acting groups and finite `Z/2` lamps.  They are even farther from
the torsion-free locally indicable one-relator regime.  They disprove generic
semidirect closure, but they do not directly furnish a one-relator
counterexample.

### Broader lesson

The known nonsoficity mechanism is rigidity-heavy; the unresolved one-relator
core is local-indicability-heavy.  A negative solution probably needs a
nonsoficity mechanism compatible with local indicability, not merely a clever
embedding of the current property-(T) examples.

## 11. Relation-module obstruction to "compress a perfect two-generator witness to one relation"

The supplied repository contains a candidate route toward a 2-generated perfect
nonsofic quotient, although, as emphasized in §2.1, that candidate is
explicitly not certified.  Independently of whether that particular group
exists as claimed, there is a clean algebraic obstruction to the most naive
one-relator compression.

### Proposition 11.1

Let `Q = F_2/K` be a 2-generated perfect group.  Then the relation module
`M = K/[K,K]` is **not** cyclic as a `ZQ`-module.  In particular `K` cannot be
normally generated in `F_2` by a single element, so this particular
two-generator presentation of `Q` cannot be a one-relator presentation.

**Proof.**  Take coinvariants of the relation module.  They are
`M_Q ≅ K/[F_2, K]`.  The five-term exact sequence in homology for
`1 → K → F_2 → Q → 1` contains

    H_2(F_2;Z) → H_2(Q;Z) → K/[F_2,K] → H_1(F_2;Z) → H_1(Q;Z) → 0 .

Now `H_2(F_2) = 0`, `H_1(F_2) = Z^2`, and `Q` is perfect, so `H_1(Q) = 0`.
Therefore the map `K/[F_2,K] ↠ Z^2` is surjective.

If `M` were cyclic as a `ZQ`-module, then its coinvariants
`M_Q = M ⊗_{ZQ} Z` would be cyclic as an abelian group.  A cyclic abelian
group cannot surject onto `Z^2`.  Contradiction.  ∎

### What this blocks, and what it does not

It blocks the simplistic plan "take a two-generator perfect nonsofic group and
discover that the kernel of `F_2 → Q` was secretly normally generated by one
word."

It does **not** block: embedding `Q` into a one-relator group; using more
generators and then eliminating them by a more sophisticated construction;
finding a nonperfect nonsofic quotient; or constructing a one-relator group
that merely contains enough of `Q` to inherit nonsoficity.

The proposition is still useful because it identifies a real homological
obstruction rather than a presentation-search inconvenience.

## 12. Why quotient constructions do not automatically give a counterexample

Another natural plan is to force a known nonsofic group `Q` as a quotient of a
one-relator group `G`.

That is insufficient: soficity is **not known to pass to arbitrary quotients**.
Indeed, the modern nonsofic constructions make quotient behavior one of the
delicate points of the theory.

One may try to repair the idea by arranging an amenable kernel
`1 → A → G → Q → 1` with `A` amenable.  But the standard permanence theorem
goes in the other direction: if the **kernel** is sofic and the **quotient** is
amenable, then the total group is sofic.  It does not say that a quotient by an
amenable normal subgroup of a sofic group must be sofic.

Therefore "make the kernel amenable and quotient nonsofic" is not presently a
contradiction to soficity of `G`.  This killed a number of seemingly attractive
Rips/HNN quotient constructions.

## 13. Cohen--Lyndon/orbit-relator attempt

The identity-theorem calculation in §6 suggests a more ambitious possibility.
The free normal closure `N = <<r>>_F` has an especially transparent relation
module, and the relator `w` has abelianized form `k[r]`.  One can try to choose
a transversal of `H = F/N` and regard `N` as generated by conjugates of `r`, so
that the `F`-orbit of `w` becomes an orbit of relations.

At the level of the **abelianized relation module**, this is diagonal: each
translated relator contributes multiplication by `k` on the corresponding
module generator.

The tempting but invalid leap is then: the orbit-relators each solve for their
own orbit-generator, so eliminate them by Tietze transformations.

That is false in general.  The equality `[w] = k[r]` holds only in `N/[N,N]`.
The original word `w` may contain the relevant conjugate of `r` many times and
intertwined nonlinearly with other generators.  Abelian diagonalization is not
a nonabelian elimination theorem.

I found no valid way to upgrade the relation-module diagonalization to a
local-freeness or local-soficity theorem for `R`.

The useful residue of this attempt is Proposition 6.1, not a Tietze
simplification.

## 14. Halo and wreath-style attempts

The August 2026 nonsofic developments make halo/wreath constructions impossible
to ignore.  One might hope to realize Linton's radical as a "lamp group"
indexed by the quotient `H = G/R`, with the quotient permuting conjugate
copies.  The normal closure description `R = <<r>>_G` indeed makes such
language natural.

The problem is that the relator orbit couples the conjugate coordinates.  A
genuine restricted direct-sum lamp group has controlled finite supports and
clean intersection/functoriality properties.  In the one-relator radical, a
finite set of orbit relations produces precisely the sort of staggered
multi-relator windows seen in Magnus theory.  Their intersections are not
automatically the intersections required by halo product theorems.

Thus the halo analogy currently repackages the same missing theorem rather than
solving it.

## 15. Marked-group openness and finite presentation do not force one relator

Since nonsoficity is open in the space of marked groups, the existence of any
finitely generated nonsofic group yields finitely presented nonsofic groups by
adding finitely many relators that already witness failure of sofic
approximation.

This is useful for finite presentability, and it explains why finitely
presented nonsofic groups appeared immediately after the first examples.

It does **not** control the number of relators.  Openness gives a finite
obstruction, not a one-word obstruction.  There is no reason for the finite set
of relations to have normal closure generated by a single word.

Proposition 11.1 shows that, for perfect two-generator targets, a
one-normal-generator compression can be homologically impossible in the same
generating set.

So marked-group openness alone is not a one-relator machine.

## 16. The Boone/Higman/HNN compression attempts from the supplied tree

The repository contains serious work on universal embeddings, Boone-style
bases, and HNN towers.  I checked whether those could convert a finitely
presented nonsofic group into a one-relator overgroup.

The obstacle is presentation count.

The clean Boone base in the tree is `<t, x, y | [x,y]>`, which is genuinely
one-relator.  But the tower that encodes further structure adds stable letters
with conjugacy relations.  There is no demonstrated Tietze collapse of the
resulting family of HNN relations to one relator while preserving the embedded
nonsofic subgroup.

Higman-type embedding theorems solve **finite presentation**, not one-relator
presentation.  A hypothetical theorem "every finitely presented group embeds in
a one-relator group" would immediately settle the question negatively now that
nonsofic groups exist, but no such theorem is available; indeed the subgroup
structure of one-relator groups makes such a universal embedding statement
implausible in the torsion-free case.

The local-indicability obstruction in §10 gives an explicit reason current
property-(T) witnesses cannot be embedded this way.

## 17. The supplied ascending-HNN action route

The repository's ascending HNN skeleton is worth separating from the arbitrary
Magnus HNN extension.

For an ascending HNN extension `G = <Γ, t | tΓt^{-1} ⊆ Γ>`, let
`N = ∪_{n≥0} t^{-n} Γ t^n`.  If `Γ` is sofic, then every level is sofic and `N`
is a directed union of sofic groups, hence sofic.  Since `G/N ≅ Z`, `G` is
sofic.

So ascending HNN **group** soficity is not the missing theorem.

The repo studies the stronger statement that the coset action on `G/Γ` is sofic
in an action sense needed for later wreath constructions.  It obtains several
sufficient hypotheses, including subgroup separability conditions, but
explicitly leaves the general infinite-index case open.

This is interesting technology for constructing sofic/non-MF examples, but it
does not imply that a general one-relator Magnus HNN extension is sofic: a
Magnus HNN need not be ascending, and its two associated free subgroups are
generally different.

## 18. False lead: "strong Atiyah / Lück approximation implies sofic"

A search through recent one-relator papers surfaced very strong approximation
statements: the strong Atiyah conjecture, Lück approximation, coherence,
virtual specialness in large families, and residual properties.

None of the results I found states or implies in any standard way that all
one-relator groups are sofic.  In particular, operator-algebraic or homological
approximation theorems must not be conflated with existence of symmetric-group
approximations satisfying the sofic definition.

I discarded this route rather than treating a similarly named approximation
theorem as a bridge.

## 19. Complete constraints on a hypothetical counterexample obtained in this attempt

### Theorem 19.1 — necessary conditions for a counterexample

If `G = <X | w>` is a nonsofic one-relator group, then all of the following
hold.

1. **Primitivity rank:** `π(w) = 2`.
2. **Torsion:** `G` is torsion-free.
3. **Local indicability:** every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup of `G`
   maps onto `Z`.
4. **No rigid finitely generated subgroup:** `G` contains no infinite finitely
   generated property-(T) subgroup.
5. **Failure of residual rational solvability:** `G` is not residually
   rationally solvable.
6. **Nontrivial Linton radical:** `R = G_Q^(ω) = <<r>>_G ≠ 1`, and `R` is
   infinitely generated.
7. **Finite-stage localization of nonsoficity:** for some finite `n`,
   `E = G_Q^(n)` is nonsofic and `G/E` is solvable.
8. **Free-shell form:** `E ≅ R ⋊_α F₀` for a free group `F₀`.
9. **Radical abelianization:** for the Linton integer `k`,
   `R_ab ≅ (Z/kZ)[G/R]`.
10. **Dichotomy:** either `R` itself is nonsofic, so the nonsoficity already
    lives in an infinitely generated locally indicable rationally perfect
    radical; or `R` is sofic but the specific free action `α` creates a
    nonsofic semidirect product, in which case `α` cannot satisfy any
    action-soficity hypothesis strong enough to invoke the Alekseev--Bradford
    semidirect theorem.

**Proof.**  Items 1--4 are §3 and §10.  Items 5--8 are Theorem 4.1 and
Corollaries 4.2--4.3.  Item 9 is Proposition 6.1.  Item 10 is tautological from
the shell form together with the conditional action theorem in §7.  ∎

## 20. What would now constitute a positive resolution

### Route A — prove the special free shells sofic

Prove: if `G` is one-relator and `E = R ⋊ F₀` is the finite rational-derived
shell supplied by Linton, then `E` is sofic.  This attacks exactly the
remaining object and does not ask for false generic semidirect closure.

### Route B — radical soficity plus action soficity

Prove both: (1) every Linton radical `R` is sofic; (2) the induced free-group
action on `R` is a sofic action in the Alekseev--Bradford sense.  Then §7
completes the proof.

### Route C — a special Magnus amalgamation theorem

Prove: the staggered bi-infinite Magnus chain of smaller one-relator pieces
over free Magnus subgroups is sofic.  Because a cyclic quotient is amenable, §8
would then close the induction.  This theorem can be strictly weaker than
closure of sofic groups under arbitrary amalgamation over free groups.

### Route D — prove a structural theorem on the radical

For example, it would suffice to show that every finitely generated subgroup of
`R` lies in a sofic subgroup drawn from a class closed under directed unions
(free, residually finite, residually amenable with an actually valid permanence
bridge, etc.).  I did not find such a local structure theorem.

## 21. What would now constitute a negative resolution

### Route N1 — construct a nonsofic locally indicable primitive-rank-2 one-relator group

This would be the most direct answer.  The nonsoficity mechanism must be
compatible with local indicability, unlike the current property-(T) mechanisms.

### Route N2 — make a Linton shell nonsofic

Construct a one-relator group for which `E = R ⋊ F₀` violates soficity.  If `R`
is itself sofic, this would also exhibit a new, very structured failure of
semidirect-product closure with a free acting group.  If `R` is nonsofic, the
problem reduces to constructing that radical inside a one-relator group.

### Route N3 — find a locally indicable nonsofic subgroup compatible with one-relator embedding

The current torsion-free nonsofic examples are blocked by property (T), but
future nonsofic groups may be locally indicable.  An embedding theorem
specialized to such a witness could then solve the problem negatively.

### Route N4 — violate soficity directly in a Magnus chain

Rather than import an external nonsofic group, prove that a specific
primitive-rank-2 relator creates a sequence of free-edge amalgams whose finite
permutation models violate a rigidity inequality.  This would be technically
difficult but structurally aligned with the real unresolved core.

## 22. Failed approaches, condensed research log

**22.1 Embed Fournier-Facio's torsion-free nonsofic group.**  *Failure:* the
construction has an infinite finitely generated property-(T) subgroup;
torsion-free one-relator groups are locally indicable, so such an embedding is
impossible.  Torsion one-relator groups are already sofic.

**22.2 Use the repo's strengthened normally-generated nonsofic quotient.**
*Failure:* the repo itself marks this route "candidate, not a theorem" and
explicitly prohibits using it as a closure step.  Additionally, if the target is
2-generated and perfect, Proposition 11.1 blocks the naive same-generator
one-relator presentation.

**22.3 Force a nonsofic quotient with amenable kernel.**  *Failure:* quotient
closure in that direction is not available.  The standard extension theorem is
sofic **kernel**, amenable **quotient**.

**22.4 Use Ji--Ogle--Ramsey to pass from the radical.**  *Failure:* the preprint
making the needed claim is withdrawn for an error in the relevant theorem's
proof.

**22.5 Assume arbitrary semidirect products of sofic groups are sofic.**
*Failure:* Kun--Thom's August 2026 examples disprove generic semidirect closure.

**22.6 Use Alekseev--Bradford automatically.**  *Failure:* their automatic
LEF/residual-finiteness criterion targets finitely generated residually finite
normal groups.  A nontrivial Linton radical is infinitely generated, and I did
not prove the specific action sofic.

**22.7 Transfer free-group graph-action soficity.**  *Failure:* this is a
different action notion from the automorphism-action approximation required for
a semidirect-product theorem.

**22.8 Magnus induction on relator length.**  *Failure:* finite windows are
multi-relator staggered groups, and adjacent one-relator pieces amalgamate over
free subgroups of rank potentially at least two.  Existing amenable-edge closure
does not apply.

**22.9 Apply recent graph-of-groups soficity directly.**  *Failure:* the theorems
found impose identical/separable/specially controlled edge embeddings; a generic
Magnus HNN uses two distinct free subgroup embeddings.

**22.10 Cohen--Lyndon eliminate orbit generators.**  *Failure:* the diagonal
statement `[w] = k[r]` is only in the abelianized relation module.  It does not
give a nonabelian Tietze elimination.

**22.11 Treat the radical as a halo/lamp group.**  *Failure:* finite supports are
coupled by orbit relators, and the required clean intersection axioms are not
automatic.  The finite pieces revert to staggered Magnus windows.

**22.12 Boone/Higman one-relator compression.**  *Failure:* the repo's initial
base is one-relator, but the tower adds multiple HNN relators.  Higman embedding
produces finite presentations, not a single relator.  Local indicability
independently blocks embedding current property-(T) witnesses.

**22.13 Use marked-group openness.**  *Failure:* it yields a finite set of
relators witnessing nonsoficity, not one relator.  Relation modules show
one-normal-generator compression can fail for algebraic reasons.

**22.14 Infer soficity from strong Atiyah/Lück approximation/coherence.**
*Failure:* no valid implication of this kind was found.  The approximation
notions are different.

**22.15 Prove "locally indicable implies sofic".**  *Failure:* I found no such
theorem or proof.  It would solve a far broader problem than one-relator
soficity and is not justified by the methods here.

## 23. A focused next attack suggested by the reductions

**Step 1: work only at primitivity rank 2.**  Everything else is already sofic
by residual finiteness.

**Step 2: compute Linton data `(r, k, R, H, n, E)` for explicit
primitive-rank-2 families.**  The radical abelianization `R_ab ≅ (Z/k)[H]` is
computable from the data and separates the perfect (`k = 1`) and
bounded-abelianization (`k > 1`) regimes.

**Step 3: study the induced free action on finite pieces of `R`.**  Rather than
proving arbitrary semidirect closure, attempt to verify the Alekseev--Bradford
finite modeling axioms for the **specific** free action obtained from the
rational-derived splitting.  The one-relator origin may impose enough coherence
on conjugates of `r` to do this even though the target `R` is infinitely
generated.

**Step 4: compare those finite action models with Magnus staggered windows.**
The shell action and the Magnus chain are two descriptions of the same
group-theoretic complexity.  A successful proof should probably explain why the
nonamenable free-overlap obstruction in one picture is harmless in the other.

**Step 5: if finite action models fail, turn the failure into a nonsoficity
witness.**  Because generic semidirect closure is now known to fail, failure of
action-soficity is not merely a technical annoyance.  A concrete
primitive-rank-2 relator whose Linton action has a Kun--Thom-style rigidity
obstruction could potentially produce the desired negative answer.

This is the most promising fork I found.

---

## 24. Final conclusion

I cannot honestly write "TRUE" or "FALSE" at the top of this file.  None of the
proof paths survived all checks.

What did survive is a significant contraction of the question: **every
residually rationally solvable one-relator group is sofic.**  For an arbitrary
one-relator group, some finite rational-derived subgroup `E` has amenable
quotient in `G`, and the only unresolved case has `E ≅ R ⋊ F₀` with
`R = G_Q^(ω)` nontrivial, infinitely generated, rationally perfect and `F₀`
free.  Moreover any counterexample must have primitivity rank `2`, hence must
be torsion-free and locally indicable, and the radical satisfies the exact
module formula `R_ab ≅ (Z/kZ)[G/R]`.

These constraints exclude the currently known property-(T)-based nonsofic
examples from the most obvious embedding strategy and identify the exact missing
positive theorem: control the special free action on the Linton radical, or
equivalently overcome the nonamenable free-edge obstruction in the Magnus
hierarchy.

I regard Theorem 4.1 / Corollary 4.2 and Proposition 6.1 as the main concrete
advances of this attempt.  They do not resolve Pestov 4.9, but they are
rigorous and should be reusable in a subsequent attack.
