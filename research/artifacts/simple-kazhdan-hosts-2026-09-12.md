# Simple Kazhdan hosts: containers, reduction, and what (T) and simplicity give an automaton

Lane `gk-fixed-host`, 2026-09-12. Supports the Cairn claims:

* `countable-group-embeds-in-fg-simple-kazhdan-group` (Section 1);
* `gottschalk-reduces-to-fg-simple-kazhdan-groups` (Section 2);
* `fg-simple-kazhdan-groups-are-surjunctive`, open (Section 3 records its attempts);
* `compressed-coset-shifts-carry-strict-equivariant-embeddings` (Section 3.3), which refutes
  `kazhdan-bernoulli-rigidity-forces-surjective-embeddings`;
* `range-projection-far-from-translation-observables` (Section 3.4). The result and its proof are
  due to the lane `gk-observables` and are recorded here at its request.

**Conventions.** A cellular automaton over `G` with finite alphabet `A`, memory `M` and rule
`mu` is `tau(x)(g) = mu((x(gm))_(m in M))`. `G` acts on `A^X`, for a left `G`-set `X`, by
`(g.y)(w) = y(g^-1 w)`. `mu_A` is the uniform Bernoulli measure.

## 1. Every countable group embeds in a finitely generated simple Kazhdan group

**Theorem 1.1.** Every countable group `C` embeds in a finitely generated infinite simple group
with property (T).

**Inputs.**

* **(I1)** Every countable group embeds in a two-generator simple group (Schupp 1976;
  `countable-group-embeds-in-two-generator-simple-group`).
* **(I2)** There is a finitely presented, torsion-free, non-elementary word-hyperbolic group `H`
  with property (T) (`torsion-free-hyperbolic-kazhdan-partner-exists`).
* **(I3)** From Hull, *Small cancellation theory in acylindrically hyperbolic groups*,
  arXiv:1308.4345v2, and Osin, *Acylindrically hyperbolic groups*, arXiv:1304.1246. The
  statements were read from the PDFs on 2026-08-14 by the torsion-free normal-generation lane,
  and the clauses of Theorem 7.1 were rechecked on 2026-09-10:
  * *Definition 1.4.* For `G in AH`, a generating set `A` and `S <= G`, `S` is *suitable with
    respect to `A`* if (1) `Gamma(G, A)` is hyperbolic and the action of `G` on it is
    acylindrical; (2) the induced action of `S` is non-elementary; (3) `S` does not normalize
    any nontrivial finite subgroup of `G`. By Hull's Lemma 5.5, (3) is equivalent to
    `K_G(S) = 1`, where `K_G(S)` is the maximal finite subgroup of `G` normalized by `S`.
  * *Theorem 7.1.* Suppose `G in AH` and `S` is suitable with respect to `A`. Then for any
    `t_1,...,t_m in G` and `N in N` there are a group `Gbar` and a surjection
    `gamma: G -> Gbar` with: (a) `Gbar in AH`; (b) `gamma` is injective on the ball `B_A(N)`;
    (c) `gamma(t_i) in gamma(S)`; (d) `gamma(S)` is suitable with respect to some
    `A_0 ⊇ gamma(A)`; (e) every element of order `n` in `Gbar` is the image of an element of
    order `n`. Hull adds that `N` can always be chosen so that `B_A(N)` contains any given
    finite subset.
  * *Corollary 5.7.* If `S` is suitable with respect to `A`, then `S` contains
    non-commensurable loxodromic elements `h_1, h_2` with `E_G(h_i) = <h_i>` and
    `{<h_1>, <h_2>} ,->_h (G, A)`.
  * *Lemma 3.5.* If `{H_i} ,->_h G` and `{K^i_j} ,->_h H_i`, then `{K^i_j} ,->_h G`.
  * *Lemma 5.8.* If `S` contains an infinite-order element `h` with `<h>` a proper subgroup of `S`
    and `<h> ,->_h (G, X)`, then `S` is suitable with respect to some `A ⊇ X`.
  * *Osin, Lemma 7.1.* If `G` acts acylindrically and non-elementarily on a hyperbolic space,
    every s-normal subgroup of `G` acts non-elementarily. Every infinite normal subgroup is
    s-normal.
* **(I4)** Free factors are hyperbolically embedded: `{G_1, G_2} ,->_h G_1 * G_2`
  (Dahmani--Guirardel--Osin). Hull uses it in this form in the proof of his Corollary 7.4.
* **(I5)** Quotients of Kazhdan groups are Kazhdan (Bekka--de la Harpe--Valette, Theorem 1.3.4).
* **(I6)** `proper-normal-subgroups-miss-the-normally-generating-core`: if a nontrivial simple
  `S <= Gamma` contains a normal generator of `Gamma`, every proper normal subgroup of `Gamma`
  meets `S` trivially. If `Gamma` is finitely generated, a maximal proper normal subgroup `M`
  exists and `Gamma/M` is simple and contains `S`.

**Proof.**

*Step 0: the simple envelope.* Apply (I1) to `C x Z`. This gives
`C <= C x Z <= C'` with `C'` simple, generated by `c_1, c_2`, and infinite. Fix `1 != c in C'`.
Since `C'` is simple, a homomorphism out of `C'` is injective if and only if it does not kill `c`.

*Step 1: put `C'` inside the image of `H`.* Let `F = H * C'`.
* `H` is non-elementary hyperbolic and torsion-free, so `H in AH` and `K(H) = 1`. So `H` is
  suitable in itself.
* Corollary 5.7 gives non-commensurable infinite-order `h_1, h_2 in H` with
  `{<h_1>, <h_2>} ,->_h H`.
* By (I4) and Lemma 3.5, `{<h_1>, <h_2>} ,->_h F`.
* Lemma 5.8, with `h = h_1` and `S = <h_1, h_2>`, makes `S` suitable in `F` with respect to some
  generating set `A`. In particular `F in AH`.

Apply Theorem 7.1 to `F`, `A`, `S`, the elements `t_i = c_i`, and `N` with `c in B_A(N)`. This
gives `gamma_1: F ->> F_1` such that:
* `gamma_1(c_i) in gamma_1(S) <= gamma_1(H)` by (c), so `F_1 = gamma_1(H)`;
* `gamma_1(c) != 1` by (b), so `gamma_1` is injective on `C'`;
* `gamma_1(S)` is suitable with respect to some `A_1 ⊇ gamma_1(A)` by (d).

So `Gamma(F_1, A_1)` is hyperbolic, `F_1` acts on it acylindrically and non-elementarily, and
`K(F_1) = 1`, since a finite normal subgroup is normalized by `gamma_1(S)`.

*Step 2: put `H` inside the normal closure of `C'`.* Let `D` be the normal closure of
`gamma_1(C')` in `F_1`. `D` is suitable with respect to `A_1`:
* (1) holds by Step 1.
* (2) `D` is infinite (it contains a copy of `C'`) and normal, hence s-normal, so it acts
  non-elementarily by Osin's Lemma 7.1.
* (3) For `g in F_1`, `g K_(F_1)(D) g^-1` is finite and normalized by `g D g^-1 = D`, so it lies
  in `K_(F_1)(D)` by maximality. Thus `K_(F_1)(D)` is a finite normal subgroup, contained in
  `K(F_1) = 1`.

Let `X_H` be a finite generating set of `H`. Apply Theorem 7.1 to `F_1`, `A_1`, `D`, the elements
`gamma_1(x)` for `x in X_H`, and `N_1` with `gamma_1(c) in B_(A_1)(N_1)`. This gives
`gamma_2: F_1 ->> Q` with `gamma_2 gamma_1(x) in gamma_2(D)` for every `x in X_H`, and
`gamma_2(D)` is the normal closure of `gamma_2 gamma_1(C')` in `Q`. Hence

```text
Q = gamma_2 gamma_1(H) = <<gamma_2 gamma_1(C')>>_Q ,
```

and `gamma_2 gamma_1(c) != 1`, so `C'` embeds in `Q`.

*Step 3: pass to a simple quotient.* `Q` is finitely generated and Kazhdan, as a quotient of `H`
(I5). Take `1 != s in C'`. Its normal closure in `Q` contains its normal closure in `C'`, which is
`C'`, and so contains `<<C'>>_Q = Q`. By (I6), a maximal proper normal subgroup `M` of `Q`
exists, meets `C'` trivially, and `Q/M` is simple. So `Q/M` is:
* finitely generated;
* infinite (it contains `C'`);
* simple;
* Kazhdan (I5);
* contains `C' ⊇ C`. QED

*Remarks.* This is the standard small-cancellation technique, and no novelty is claimed. The
container is not claimed finitely presented; for groups with unsolvable word problem it cannot
be. If `C` is nonsofic, the container is nonsofic.

## 2. The reduction

**Theorem 2.1.** Fix a finite alphabet `A`. Every group is `A`-surjunctive if and only if every
finitely generated infinite simple Kazhdan group is `A`-surjunctive. The same holds for
nonsofic such groups, since sofic groups are surjunctive (`sofic-groups-are-surjunctive`).

*Proof.* The forward direction is immediate. For the converse, let `tau` be injective over `G`
with memory `M`, and put `L = <M>`.
* `tau` acts coset by coset as copies of the automaton `tau_L` with the same rule, so `tau` is
  surjective if and only if `tau_L` is.
* `L` is countable, so it embeds in a finitely generated infinite simple Kazhdan group `S` by
  Theorem 1.1.
* If `S` is `A`-surjunctive, so is `L` (`surjunctivity-passes-to-subgroups`), and then `tau_L`
  and `tau` are surjective.
QED

Related reductions:
* `finitely-generated-simple-groups-are-surjunctive` (lane `gk-other-hosts`) reduces to the larger
  class of finitely generated simple groups, by Schupp alone.
* `gottschalk-surjunctivity-fixed-two-generator-tester` reduces to one finitely presented host,
  which is not simple.
* `fixed-host-outside-sofic-permanence-closure`: the finitely presented members, `R^x` for
  instance, lie outside every permanence closure of the sofic groups.

## 3. What simplicity and property (T) give an automaton

Fix an infinite simple Kazhdan group `G`, an alphabet `A` with `q = |A| >= 2`, and an injective
automaton `tau` with image `Y` and `nu = tau_* mu_A`.

### 3.1 Finite orbits are the constants

A configuration with finite orbit has a stabilizer of finite index. An infinite simple group has
no proper subgroup of finite index, since such a subgroup would contain a proper normal subgroup
of finite index. So the finite orbits in `A^G` are the `q` constant configurations, and `tau`
permutes them. Lawton's periodic-point argument sees `q` points. Every argument through finite
quotients of `G` is empty.

### 3.2 A strict automaton is a measure conjugacy

`tau` is a measure conjugacy from `(A^G, mu_A)` onto `(Y, nu)`: `f -> f o tau` intertwines the
Koopman representations. So all of the following agree on `mu_A` and `nu`:
* spectral gap and strong ergodicity, which (T) gives every ergodic action (Schmidt; Connes--Weiss);
* orbit-equivalence class and cocycle superrigidity;
* Rokhlin entropy.

What differs is the coordinate process. `nu([p]) = 0` for a missing cylinder `p`, while `mu_A`
has full support. An injective `tau` is onto exactly when `nu = mu_A`
(`injective-measure-preserving-ca-is-surjective`, `bijective-ca-preserve-uniform-bernoulli-measure`).

A partition-comparing invariant can see the coordinate process:
* `strict-automaton-lowers-bernoulli-rokhlin-entropy` proves the upper bound.
* The missing input is a lower bound on Bernoulli Rokhlin entropy (INF). No property (T)
  mechanism supplying one is known (lane `gk-rokhlin`, 2026-09-12).
* Seward--Tucker-Drob give free factors of arbitrarily small Rokhlin entropy, for Kazhdan groups
  too.
* INF passes to subgroups (`bernoulli-rokhlin-maximality-passes-to-subgroups`). By Theorem 1.1,
  INF for every countable group therefore also reduces to finitely generated simple Kazhdan
  groups.

### 3.3 Bernoulli dynamics cannot force surjectivity: a coset-shift witness

**Theorem 3.3.** Let `G` be a group, `H <= G` and `u in G` with `u H u^-1` a proper subgroup of `H`.
Put `psi(gH) = g u^-1 H`. Then `psi` is well defined, `G`-equivariant, surjective and not injective.
For every finite alphabet with at least two letters, `Psi(y) = y o psi` is a continuous,
`G`-equivariant, injective, non-surjective self-map of `A^(G/H)`.

*Proof.*
* *Well defined.* For `h in H`, `g h u^-1 H = g u^-1 (u h u^-1) H = g u^-1 H`.
* *Equivariant and surjective.* `psi(g' g H) = g' psi(gH)`, and `psi(k u H) = kH`.
* *Not injective.* `u H u^-1 < H` gives `H < u^-1 H u`. Choose `k in u^-1 H u` outside `H`. Then
  `kH != H`, but `psi(kH) = u^-1 (u k u^-1) H = u^-1 H = psi(H)`.
* *Continuity and equivariance of `Psi`.* `Psi` is continuous coordinatewise, and
  `Psi(g.y)(w) = y(g^-1 psi(w)) = y(psi(g^-1 w)) = (g.Psi(y))(w)`.
* *Injective.* `psi` is onto.
* *Not surjective.* The image consists of the configurations constant on the fibres of `psi`.
  The fibre through `gH` is in bijection with `u^-1 H u / H`, of size `[u^-1 H u : H] >= 2`. So
  the image is a proper closed set. QED

*The instance.* Put `G = R^x` with `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)`.
* `G` is infinite and simple (`binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-simple`).
* `G` is Kazhdan: `R^x ~= EL_12(R)` by `leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group`, and that group
  is Kazhdan by `rank-twelve-leavitt-simple-non-mf`.
* By `leavitt-cylinder-swaps-generate-thompson-in-el`, `G` contains the copies `V_l` of Thompson's
  group `V`, acting by prefix replacements inside the cylinder `[l]` and fixing its complement.

Put `H = V_1`, and let `u in V` be the prefix-code bijection `00 -> 0`, `01 -> 10`, `1 -> 11`.
* `u` carries `[1]` onto `[11]`, so `u H u^-1 = V_11`.
* `V_11` is a proper subgroup of `V_1`, because `V_1` contains elements moving points of `[10]`.
* `[G : H]` is infinite: `H != G`, and an infinite simple group has no proper subgroup of finite
  index.

Properties of the witness `(A^(G/H), mu_(G/H), Psi)`:
1. `mu_(G/H)` has full support and is ergodic, since every orbit of `G` on `G/H` is infinite. By
   (T) it is strongly ergodic with spectral gap.
2. `Psi` is a measure conjugacy onto its image, and `Psi_* mu_(G/H)` is supported on a proper
   closed set, hence singular to `mu_(G/H)`. All the invariants of 3.2 agree.
3. The finite orbits in `A^(G/H)` are the constants: a finite-index stabilizer is `G`, and `G` is
   transitive on `G/H`.
4. The ergodic invariant measures form a closed set (Glasner--Weiss, for Kazhdan groups).
5. `G` has no nontrivial finite quotient.

So everything (T) and simplicity give a Bernoulli `G`-space holds on `A^(G/H)`, yet a strict
continuous equivariant injection exists. A proof for simple Kazhdan hosts has to use what
separates the regular `G`-set from `G/H`: trivial point stabilizers, which is where
Curtis--Hedlund--Lyndon locality comes from. `Psi` reads `y` at `g u^-1 H`, and no bounded
neighbourhood of `gH` sees that point; by
`full-shift-maps-to-infinite-stabilizer-coset-shifts-are-constant`, no equivariant map from the
full shift reaches such coset shifts.

The construction needs only a compressor. So it also runs over amenable surjunctive groups, for
example `BS(1,2) = <a, t | t a t^-1 = a^2>` with `H = <a>` and `u = t`. The existence of strict
equivariant coset-shift embeddings therefore carries no surjunctivity information in either
direction.

### 3.4 Operators add nothing (lane `gk-observables`)

*Setup,* as in Section 1 of `research/artifacts/gottschalk-product-trace-zero-one-law-2026-09-12.md`:
* `tau` is injective and strict over an infinite group `G`;
* `b` and `c = tau(b)` are constant backgrounds, and `C_b`, `C_c` are their finite perturbations;
* `H_c = l^2(C_c)`, `V|x> = |tau(x)>`, and `P = VV*` is the projection onto `Y ∩ C_c`;
* `A_G` is the quasilocal algebra on `H_c`, `pi_c(g)|z> = |g.z>`, and `B_c = C*(A_G ∪ pi_c(G))`.

**Theorem 3.4 (gk-observables).**
* (a) `dist(P, B_c) = 1/2`.
* (b) If `G` has (T), then the Kazhdan projection `p in C*_max(G)` satisfies
  `pi_c(p) = |c><c|`, so `B_c` contains every compact operator on `H_c`. `P` is still at distance
  `1/2` from `B_c`.

*Proof of (a).* `pi_c(g) A_F pi_c(g)* = A_(gF)`, so `B_c` is the closed span of the products
`a pi_c(g)` with `a` local. Fix `O = sum over g in S_O of a_g pi_c(g)`, with every `a_g` in `A_F`
for a finite `F`.
1. *An excluded configuration.* Let `p in A^Omega` be a missing cylinder. Let `u = c` off `Omega`
   and `u = p` on `Omega`. Then `u in C_c` and `u` is not in `Y`. `u != c`, because
   `c = tau(b) in Y`.
2. *Its stabilizer.* `Stab(u)` maps the finite nonempty support of `u` to itself, so it lies in
   `supp(u) supp(u)^-1`. It is a finite group `K`.
3. *An image configuration with the same stabilizer.* Pick `t in G` and `b' != b`, and let `x = b`
   off the right coset `Kt` and `x = b'` on it. Then `g.x = x` if and only if `g Kt = Kt`, if and
   only if `g in K`. So `Stab(x) = K`. The configuration `y = tau(x)` lies in `Y ∩ C_c`, and
   `Stab(y) = K` because `tau` is injective and equivariant.
4. *Translate both far away.* Choose `h` with `h.supp(u)` and `h.supp(y)` disjoint from
   `F ∪ S_O^-1 F`, and put `z = h.u` or `z = h.y`. For `g in S_O`, both `z` and `g.z` equal `c` on
   `F`. So `<z| a_g |g.z>` vanishes unless `g.z = z`, that is `g in h K h^-1`, and then it is the
   entry of `a_g` at `(c|F, c|F)`.

Hence `<h.u| O |h.u> = <h.y| O |h.y>`, while `<h.u| P |h.u> = 0` and `<h.y| P |h.y> = 1`. So
`||P - O|| >= 1/2`. Density gives `dist(P, B_c) >= 1/2`, and `||P - I/2|| = 1/2`.

*Proof of (b).* `pi_c(p)` is the projection onto the `pi_c(G)`-invariant vectors of `l^2(C_c)`.
Every orbit other than `{c}` is infinite, since stabilizers of finite perturbations are finite,
so `pi_c(p) = |c><c|`. Local matrix units `a` and `a'` turn `a |c><c| a'` into every rank-one
`|x><z|`, so `B_c` contains the compact operators. Part (a) still applies. QED

*Consequences (gk-observables).*
* The Kazhdan projection produces only the vector state at `c`, which is `1` on `P` whether or not
  `tau` is onto.
* In the tracial representation it produces the product trace, which obeys
  `injective-ca-product-trace-zero-one-law`.
* `V` intertwines the translations and the Kazhdan Laplacians.
* So operators add nothing here. The open hole must be attacked through INF or through the
  combinatorics of the group.

### 3.5 What survives

For `fg-simple-kazhdan-groups-are-surjunctive`:
* INF on finitely generated simple Kazhdan groups.
* Injectivity with Curtis--Hedlund--Lyndon locality over the free regular action forces
  preservation of `mu_A` (lane `gk-other-hosts`'s formulation).
* On the negative side, a strict automaton on such a host. By
  `sofic-radical-localizes-garden-of-eden-windows`, its Garden-of-Eden windows must fold on
  sofic-invisible elements; in a simple nonsofic group every nontrivial element is sofic-invisible.
