# Fixed-dimension Hilbert--Schmidt commutator bounds for Thompson's group F

Date: 2026-09-16. Swarm agent on `thompson-f-is-not-hyperlinear`.

Proofs for `profinite-kernel-words-have-fixed-dimension-hs-bounds` and
`thompson-f-commutator-bound-holds-in-each-fixed-dimension`.

## 0. Conventions

- `S` is a finite alphabet, `F(S)` the free group, `R subset F(S)` a finite set of relators,
  `P = F(S)/<<R>>`, and `w in F(S)` a word.
- `tr_n` is the normalized trace on `M_n(C)`, `||X||_2 = tr_n(X^* X)^(1/2)`, and
  `||X||_HS = n^(1/2) ||X||_2` is the unnormalized Frobenius norm.
- For `U = (U_s)_(s in S) in U(n)^S` and a word `g`, `g(U)` is the evaluation, and
  `e_U(g) = ||g(U) - I||_2^2 = 2 - 2 Re tr_n g(U)`.
- `delta_R(U) = max_(r in R) ||r(U) - I||_2` (zero when `R` is empty).
- The **dimension-`n` constant** is the least `C_n = C_n(R, w) in [0, infinity]` with

```text
||w(U) - I||_2 <= C_n delta_R(U)        for every U in U(n)^S.            (FD1)
```

  Equivalently `C_n = sup ||w(U)-I||_2 / delta_R(U)` over tuples with `delta_R(U) > 0`, provided
  every tuple with `delta_R(U) = 0` has `w(U) = I`; otherwise `C_n = infinity`.
- `w` is **profinitely trivial in `P`** if every homomorphism from `P` to a finite group kills the
  image of `w`, that is, the image of `w` lies in the finite residual `Res_fin(P)`, the
  intersection of all finite-index normal subgroups of `P`.
- The metric `d(g,h) = ||g - h||_2` on `U(n)` is bi-invariant. Hence
  `||gh - I||_2 <= ||g - I||_2 + ||h - I||_2` and `||g h g^-1 - I||_2 = ||h - I||_2`.

All constants below are ineffective as stated. They are computable in principle (Section 2.5), but no
explicit value is claimed except the verified lower bounds of Section 4.

## 1. Profinitely trivial words: the ideal-membership theorem

Fix `n >= 1`. Let

```text
B_0 = Z[x_(s,ij), y_s : s in S, 1 <= i,j <= n],     X_s = (x_(s,ij))_(i,j),
X_s^# = y_s adj(X_s) in M_n(B_0).                                          (FD2)
```

For a reduced word `g` let `g(X) in M_n(B_0)` be the matrix obtained by substituting `X_s` for `s` and
`X_s^#` for `s^-1` and multiplying out. Let `J = J_n(R) subset B_0` be the ideal generated by

```text
y_s det(X_s) - 1   (s in S)     and the entries of   r(X) - I   (r in R).  (FD3)
```

**Theorem 1.1 (ideal membership).** If `w` is profinitely trivial in `P`, then every entry of
`w(X) - I` lies in `J`.

*Proof.* Put `A = B_0 / J` and let `xi_s` be the image of `X_s` in `M_n(A)`.

1. **Universal representation.** The adjugate identity gives `X_s X_s^# = X_s^# X_s = y_s det(X_s) I`,
   which is `I` modulo `J`. So `xi_s` is invertible with inverse the image of `X_s^#`, and `s -> xi_s`
   defines a homomorphism `rho : F(S) -> GL_n(A)`. By induction on word length, `rho(g)` is the image
   of `g(X)` for every reduced word `g`. For `r in R` the entries of `r(X) - I` lie in `J`, so
   `rho(r) = I` and `rho` factors through `P`.
2. **Suppose not.** Let `e in A` be a nonzero entry of `rho(w) - I`.
3. **Noetherian.** `A` is a finitely generated commutative ring, hence Noetherian (Hilbert basis
   theorem over `Z`).
4. **Localize.** `Ann_A(e)` is a proper ideal because `1 . e != 0`. Choose a maximal ideal `m`
   containing it. The image of `e` in `A_m` is nonzero: `s e = 0` with `s notin m` would put `s` in
   `Ann_A(e) subset m`.
5. **Krull.** The local ring `A_m` is Noetherian, so `intersection_k m^k A_m = 0` (Krull's
   intersection theorem). Hence `e/1 notin m^k A_m` for some `k`, and so `e notin m^k`.
6. **Finite residue field.** `A/m` is a field which is finitely generated as a ring, hence finite.
   (If its characteristic is `p > 0`, it is a finitely generated `F_p`-algebra that is a field, so it is
   finite over `F_p` by Zariski's lemma. Characteristic zero is impossible: then `Q subset A/m`, the field
   is finite over `Q` by Zariski's lemma, and the Artin--Tate lemma makes `Q` a finitely generated
   `Z`-algebra, which it is not, since finitely many denominators cannot produce every prime.)
7. **Finite congruence quotient.** `A/m^k` has the filtration `A ⊃ m ⊃ ... ⊃ m^k`. Each factor
   `m^i/m^(i+1)` is a finitely generated `A`-module (Noetherian) killed by `m`, so it is a finite-dimensional
   vector space over the finite field `A/m`. Hence `A/m^k` is finite and `GL_n(A/m^k)` is a finite group.
8. **Contradiction.** The composite `P -> GL_n(A) -> GL_n(A/m^k)` is a homomorphism to a finite group.
   The entry `e` of `rho(w) - I` survives modulo `m^k` by step 5, so the image of `w` is not `I`. This
   contradicts profinite triviality. So every entry of `rho(w) - I` is zero in `A`, that is, every entry
   of `w(X) - I` lies in `J`. ∎

**Corollary 1.2 (integer certificate).** For every pair `(a,b)` there are integer polynomials
`p^(ab)_(r,kl)`, `q^(ab)_s in B_0` with

```text
(w(X) - I)_(ab) = sum_(r in R) sum_(k,l) p^(ab)_(r,kl) (r(X) - I)_(kl)
                + sum_(s in S) q^(ab)_s (y_s det(X_s) - 1).                    (FD4)
```

Let `L_n = L_n(R,w)` be the largest sum of absolute values of the coefficients of one of the
polynomials `p^(ab)_(r,kl)`, minimized over certificates.

**Theorem 1.3 (fixed-dimension linear HS bound).** If `w` is profinitely trivial in `P`, then for every
`n >= 1` and every `U in U(n)^S`

```text
||w(U) - I||_2 <= |R| n^2 L_n delta_R(U).                                    (FD5)
```

So `C_n(R,w) <= |R| n^2 L_n < infinity`.

*Proof.* Substitute `x_(s,ij) = (U_s)_(ij)` and `y_s = det(U_s)^-1 = conj(det U_s)` in (FD4). Then
`X_s^#` evaluates to `det(U_s)^-1 adj(U_s) = U_s^-1`, so every `g(X)` evaluates to `g(U)`, and the last sum in
(FD4) vanishes. Every substituted value has modulus at most `1`, so each `|p^(ab)_(r,kl)(U)| <= L_n`.
By Cauchy--Schwarz over the `n^2` entries, `sum_(k,l) |M_(kl)| <= n ||M||_HS = n^(3/2) ||M||_2`. Hence

```text
|(w(U) - I)_(ab)| <= L_n sum_(r in R) n^(3/2) ||r(U) - I||_2 <= |R| n^(3/2) L_n delta_R(U).
```

Finally `||w(U) - I||_2 = n^(-1/2) ||w(U) - I||_HS <= n^(1/2) max_(a,b) |(w(U) - I)_(ab)|`, which gives
(FD5). ∎

**Remarks.**
- **Linear, not Hölder.** The exponent is exactly one. Compactness and the Łojasiewicz inequality alone
  give only a Hölder bound `||w(U)-I||_2^N <= A delta_R(U)` in each dimension (compare `(FDA11)` in
  `notes/TRUE_FIXED_DIMENSIONAL_ARC_BOUNDARY.md` and `bounded-dimension-jacobson-head-has-algebraic-collapse`).
  The difference is that Theorem 1.1 places `w` in the ideal `J` itself, not merely in its radical.
- **Global on `U(n)^S`.** The bound holds on the whole compact torus of tuples, not only near exact
  solutions, and with scalar phases retained.
- **Credit.** Steps 3--8 are Malcev's proof that finitely generated linear groups over commutative rings
  are residually finite, run on the universal representation. The conclusion of Theorem 1.1 is the
  object-level content of Grothendieck's theorem that, for finitely generated groups, representations
  over all commutative rings depend only on the profinite completion (A. Grothendieck, *Représentations
  linéaires et compactification profinie des groupes discrets*, Manuscripta Math. 2 (1970) 375--396;
  cited from memory, not fetched in this session). The evaluation in Theorem 1.3 is elementary. We know
  no source that states (FD5) in this form, and claim nothing beyond this repository.

## 2. Consequences for an arbitrary finite presentation

### 2.1 Finiteness in every dimension characterizes profinite triviality

**Proposition 2.1.** For a finite presentation `P = <S | R>` and a word `w` the following are equivalent.

1. `w` is profinitely trivial in `P`.
2. `C_n(R,w) < infinity` for every `n >= 1`.
3. `w(U) = I` for every `n` and every `U in U(n)^S` with `delta_R(U) = 0`, that is, `w` dies in every
   finite-dimensional unitary representation of `P`.

*Proof.* (1 ⇒ 2) is Theorem 1.3. (2 ⇒ 3) is the definition of `C_n`. (3 ⇒ 1): let `pi : P -> Q` be a
homomorphism onto a finite group, and let `lambda : Q -> U(|Q|)` be the left regular representation by
permutation matrices. The tuple `U_s = lambda(pi(s))` has `delta_R(U) = 0`, so `lambda(pi(w)) = I`.
Since `lambda` is faithful, `pi(w) = 1`. ∎

So Theorem 1.3 is sharp at the qualitative level: a word that survives in some finite quotient has
`C_n = infinity` in the dimension of that quotient's regular representation.

### 2.2 Monotonicity in the dimension

**Proposition 2.2.** `C_n(R,w) <= C_(n+1)(R,w)` and `C_n(R,w) <= C_(nm)(R,w)` for all `n, m >= 1`.

*Proof.* For `U in U(n)^S` put `U'_s = U_s ⊕ 1 in U(n+1)`. Then `g(U') = g(U) ⊕ 1` for every word, so
`e_(U')(g) = (n/(n+1)) e_U(g)`. Every ratio `e(w)/e(r)` is unchanged, and `delta_R(U') = 0` exactly when
`delta_R(U) = 0`. The second inequality uses `U ⊗ I_m`, which leaves every `e_U(g)` unchanged. ∎

Hence `C_infinity(R,w) := sup_n C_n(R,w) = lim_n C_n(R,w)`.

*Credit.* The multiple-dimension inequality is the scalar-sensitive analogue of `(FDA34)` in
`notes/TRUE_FIXED_DIMENSIONAL_ARC_BOUNDARY.md`. There the defect is projective, `q_U(g) = 1 - |tr_n g(U)|^2`,
and padding by a trivial block changes the character, so only multiples of `d` are available. With the
scalar-sensitive defect `e_U`, padding works in every dimension. It is the same padding used in
`padding-makes-local-hs-rigidity-global`.

### 2.3 Bounded irreducible degree, and the mass in small blocks

A tuple `U in U(n)^S` generates a finite-dimensional C*-algebra, so `C^n` splits orthogonally into
irreducible invariant subspaces. Correspondingly `U = ⊕_i U^(i)` with `U^(i) in U(n_i)^S` irreducible,
and the multiset `{n_i}` does not depend on the choice of splitting. For `D >= 1` let `mu_D(U)` be
`(1/n) sum_(i : n_i <= D) n_i`, the proportion of the dimension in irreducible summands of degree at most `D`.

**Proposition 2.3.** For every `n`, every `U in U(n)^S` and every `D >= 1`,

```text
e_U(w) <= |R| C_D(R,w)^2 delta_R(U)^2 + 4 (1 - mu_D(U)).                   (FD6)
```

In particular, if every irreducible summand of `U` has degree at most `D`, then
`||w(U) - I||_2 <= |R|^(1/2) C_D(R,w) delta_R(U)`.

*Proof.* For every word `g` we have `g(U) = ⊕_i g(U^(i))`, so `e_U(g) = sum_i (n_i/n) e_(U^(i))(g)`.
For a summand with `n_i <= D`, (FD1) and Proposition 2.2 give

```text
e_(U^(i))(w) <= C_(n_i)^2 delta_R(U^(i))^2 <= C_D^2 sum_(r in R) e_(U^(i))(r).
```

For a summand with `n_i > D` use `e_(U^(i))(w) <= 4`. Summing with weights `n_i/n`,

```text
e_U(w) <= C_D^2 sum_(r in R) sum_(i : n_i <= D) (n_i/n) e_(U^(i))(r) + 4 (1 - mu_D(U))
       <= C_D^2 sum_(r in R) e_U(r) + 4 (1 - mu_D(U)),
```

and `sum_r e_U(r) <= |R| delta_R(U)^2`. ∎

*Credit.* The bounded-degree case is the scalar-sensitive analogue of
`boundedIrreducibleDegree_linearDomination` in `GroupApproximation/Domination/PaperStatements.lean`,
where the fixed-dimension input is a hypothesis. The additive mass term in (FD6) is what the escape
statements below use.

### 2.4 The projective defect

Write `q_U(g) = 1 - |tr_n g(U)|^2`, as in `(FDA2)` of `notes/TRUE_FIXED_DIMENSIONAL_ARC_BOUNDARY.md`.

**Corollary 2.4.** If `w` is profinitely trivial in `P`, then for every `U in U(n)^S`

```text
q_U(w) <= C_(n^2)(R,w)^2 max_(r in R) q_U(r).                                  (FD7)
```

So the constant `C_fd(d) = sup q_U(w) / sum_r q_U(r)` of `(FDA9)` satisfies `C_fd(d) <= C_(d^2)(R,w)^2`.

*Proof.* Put `Uhat_s = U_s ⊗ conj(U_s) in U(n^2)`. Then `g(Uhat) = g(U) ⊗ conj(g(U))`, and its normalized
trace is `|tr_n g(U)|^2`. So `e_(Uhat)(g) = 2 q_U(g)` (this is `(FDA5)`). Apply (FD1) in dimension `n^2`. ∎

The note proves `C_fd(d) < infinity` (`(FDA20)`) under `w in Rad_sof(P)` by semialgebraic curve selection,
jet truncation and Malcev's theorem. Every finite group is sofic, so `Rad_sof(P) subset Res_fin(P)`, and
Corollary 2.4 recovers `(FDA20)` with no curve selection or arc parametrization. Both proofs rest on
Malcev residual finiteness. Theorem 1.1 applies it to the universal representation over `B_0/J` instead of a
truncated arc. The Lean claim map (`notes/DOMINATION_DIMENSION_ESCAPE_CLAIM_MAP.md`) lists the analytic
curve-selection/jet bridge as not yet formalized. The algebraic route here needs only the Hilbert basis theorem,
Krull's intersection theorem, the Zariski/Artin--Tate lemmas and evaluation. I have not checked whether the
Lean predicate `HasFDCharacterDominationInDimension` matches (FD7) exactly, so no Lean statement is claimed.

### 2.5 Computability

For fixed `(R, w, n)` the statement "`e_U(w) <= K max_r e_U(r)` for all `U in U(n)^S`" is a first-order
formula over the real closed field with rational coefficients, in the real and imaginary parts of the
entries. As in Corollary 6 of `notes/TRUE_FIXED_DIMENSIONAL_ARC_BOUNDARY.md`, the least such `K`,
`C_n(R,w)^2`, is a real algebraic number computable in principle by real quantifier elimination. A
certificate (FD4) can also be found by exhaustive search over integer polynomial combinations, which
terminates by Theorem 1.1, and so gives an explicit upper bound `|R| n^2 L`. Neither procedure is practical.

### 2.6 Where a uniform bound can fail

**Corollary 2.5 (degree escape).** Let `w` be profinitely trivial in `P`, and let `U^(k) in U(n_k)^S` satisfy
`delta_R(U^(k)) -> 0` and `e_(U^(k))(w) >= epsilon > 0`. Then for every `D`,

```text
liminf_k (1 - mu_D(U^(k))) >= epsilon / 4.
```

So at least a quarter of the defect mass `epsilon` lives, asymptotically, in irreducible summands of degree
greater than `D`, for every `D`. In particular `n_k -> infinity`.

*Proof.* Rearrange (FD6): `4 (1 - mu_D(U^(k))) >= epsilon - |R| C_D^2 delta_R(U^(k))^2 -> epsilon`. ∎

This is the scalar-sensitive, quantitative form of the escape statements
`obstruction_requires_matrixDimensionEscape` and `obstruction_requires_irreducibleDegreeEscape` of
`character-domination-collapse-equivalences`. Here the fixed-dimension hypothesis is proved, not assumed.

## 3. Thompson's group F

### 3.1 Setting and the one import

`F = <a, b | R_1, R_2>` with `R_1 = [ab^-1, a^-1ba]`, `R_2 = [ab^-1, a^-2ba^2]`, `R = {R_1, R_2}`, and
`c = [a,b] = aba^-1b^-1` (so `a -> x_0`, `b -> x_1`). Write `C_n(F) = C_n(R, c)`. By bi-invariance,
`||UW - WU||_2 = ||c(U,W) - I||_2`.

**Import (trust surface).** Every proper quotient of `F` is abelian (Cannon--Floyd--Parry, Enseign. Math.
42 (1996)). It is pinned as a quotation in `thompson-f-is-lea-only-if-amenable-proof` and used the same way
in `thompson-f-verbal-commutator-bounds-proof`. The primary source was not re-read in this session.

**Lemma 3.1.** The finite residual of `F` is `F' = [F,F]`, the normal closure of the image of `c`. In
particular a word `w in F(a,b)` is profinitely trivial in `F` exactly when both of its exponent sums vanish.

*Proof.* `F` is infinite (`x_0` has infinite order), so every finite quotient is proper, hence abelian by the
import, and kills `F'`. Conversely `R_1` and `R_2` are commutators, so `F/F' = Z^2`, which is residually
finite, and `Res_fin(F) subset F'`. In a group generated by `a, b`, `F'` is the normal closure of `[a,b]`, and
a word lies in `F(a,b)' <<R>>` exactly when its exponent sums vanish. ∎

### 3.2 Every fixed dimension

**Theorem 3.2.** `C_n(F) < infinity` for every `n >= 1`, and `C_1(F) = 0`. More generally
`C_n(R, w) < infinity` for every word `w` with vanishing exponent sums.

*Proof.* Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 1.3. In dimension one the matrices commute. ∎

So, in each fixed dimension `n`, unitary pairs satisfy
`||UW - WU||_2 <= C_n(F) max(||R_1(U,W) - I||_2, ||R_2(U,W) - I||_2)`, globally on `U(n)^2`.

### 3.3 The representation schemes of F and Z^2 coincide

Let `J_n(c) subset B_0` be the ideal (FD3) for the presentation `<a, b | [a,b]>` of `Z^2`.

**Theorem 3.3.** `J_n(R) = J_n(c)` for every `n`. Equivalently, for every commutative ring `A`, every
homomorphism `F -> GL_n(A)` has abelian image. Hence the affine `Z`-schemes `Rep_n(F)` and `Rep_n(Z^2)`
agree as closed subschemes of `GL_n^2`. Over a field `K`, for every `rho : F -> GL_n(K)` the cocycle spaces
`Z^1(F, M_n(K)_(Ad rho))` and `Z^1(Z^2, M_n(K)_(Ad rho))` coincide under inflation.

*Proof.* `J_n(c) subset J_n(R)`: `c` is profinitely trivial in `F` (Lemma 3.1), so Theorem 1.1 puts the
entries of `c(X) - I` in `J_n(R)`. `J_n(R) subset J_n(c)`: over `A = B_0/J_n(c)` the universal
representation of step 1 of Theorem 1.1 factors through `Z^2 = F(a,b)/<<c>>`. It kills the commutators
`R_1, R_2`, so the entries of `R_i(X) - I` lie in `J_n(c)`.

`B_0/J_n(R)` represents `A -> Hom(F, GL_n(A))`: a homomorphism is a tuple `(xi_s) in GL_n(A)^2` with
`r(xi) = I`, and `y_s` is forced to be `det(xi_s)^-1`. So equal ideals mean equal functors. The Zariski tangent
space of `Rep_n(P)` at `rho` is `Z^1(P, M_n(K)_(Ad rho))`, read off from `K[epsilon]/(epsilon^2)`-points
`rho (1 + epsilon u)`, so equal schemes have equal tangent spaces. ∎

*Remarks.*
- A direct proof of the functor statement: the image of `F` in `GL_n(A)` lies in `GL_n(A_0)` for a finitely
  generated subring `A_0`. So it is residually finite (Malcev), and it is a quotient of `F`. By the import it
  is either abelian or all of `F`, and `F` is not residually finite (`thompson-f-is-not-residually-finite`).
- Theorem 3.3 is an instance of Grothendieck's theorem as recalled in Section 1, Remarks (from memory; the
  exact hypotheses of the 1970 paper were not re-checked). `F -> Z^2` induces an isomorphism of profinite
  completions, and the representation functors over commutative rings then agree.
- The cocycle statement is also visible from perfectness of `F'`. Inflation-restriction gives
  `Z^1(F, V) = Z^1(Z^2, V)` whenever `F'` acts trivially and `Hom(F', V) = 0`.

*Consequence for strategy.* No finite-order (jet, deformation or tangent-space) computation in a fixed
dimension can separate `F` from `Z^2`. If `xi(t) in GL_n(C[[t]])^2` satisfies `R_i(xi(t)) = I mod t^k` for
`i = 1, 2`, then `[xi_a(t), xi_b(t)] = I mod t^k`. This is Theorem 3.3 over `A = C[[t]]/(t^k)`, and it is the
infinitesimal shadow of Theorem 3.2. It explains why every fixed-dimension "nonlinear rate" obstruction is
empty for `F`.

### 3.4 Nonhyperlinearity is boundedness of one monotone sequence

**Theorem 3.4.** The following are equivalent.
1. `F` is not hyperlinear.
2. `C_infinity(F) = sup_n C_n(F) < infinity`.

Moreover, if statement 3 of `thompson-f-hyperlinear-iff-relator-system-hs-unstable` holds at `epsilon = 1`
with some `delta`, then `C_infinity(F) <= 2/delta`. The sequence `0 = C_1(F) <= C_2(F) <= C_3(F) <= ...` is
nondecreasing (Proposition 2.2), each term is a finite real algebraic number (Theorem 3.2 and Section 2.5), and
`C_infinity(F)` is its limit.

*Proof.*
- (2 ⇒ 1) With `C = C_infinity(F)`, every pair in every dimension has
  `||UW - WU||_2 <= C delta_R(U,W)`. Given `epsilon > 0`, `delta = epsilon/(C+1)` witnesses statement 3 of
  `thompson-f-hyperlinear-iff-relator-system-hs-unstable`, which is equivalent there to statement 1.
- (1 ⇒ 2) Statement 3 at `epsilon = 1` gives `delta > 0` such that `||c(U,W) - I||_2 >= 1` forces
  `delta_R(U,W) >= delta`, in every dimension. By `fixed-hs-word-gap-is-linear-domination` with
  `eta = delta`, `||c(U,W) - I||_2 <= (2/delta) delta_R(U,W)` for every pair in every dimension, so
  `C_n(F) <= 2/delta` for all `n`. ∎

Theorem 3.2 shows that no single dimension can obstruct 2 in Theorem 3.4. The equivalence places the whole
problem in the growth of `C_n(F)`, equivalently of the certificate sizes `L_n`, as `n -> infinity`. Note
`C_n(F) <= 2 n^2 L_n(R, c)` by (FD5).

### 3.5 Structure forced on hyperlinearity witnesses

**Corollary 3.5.** For every pair `(U, W) in U(n)^2` and every `D >= 1`,

```text
||UW - WU||_2^2 <= 2 C_D(F)^2 delta_R(U,W)^2 + 4 (1 - mu_D(U,W)).                (FD8)
```

So if `F` is hyperlinear and `(U^(k), W^(k))` is any sequence with `delta_R -> 0` and
`||U^(k) W^(k) - W^(k) U^(k)||_2^2 >= epsilon`, then for every `D` at least `epsilon/4` of the dimension
eventually lies in irreducible summands of degree greater than `D`.

*Proof.* Proposition 2.3 with `|R| = 2`, and Corollary 2.5. ∎

This is independent of the law-based restrictions of `thompson-f-bounded-derived-length-pairs-obey-linear-bounds`.
Their item 1 uses only the identity `c = r w` in `F_2` and bi-invariance of the metric, so it holds verbatim for
`||.||_2`: pairs generating a group that satisfies a law `v` with `V(F) != 1` obey
`||UW - WU||_2 <= N_v delta_R`. Bounded-degree unitary groups satisfy no law in general, since `U(2)` contains
free groups, and groups satisfying a law can have irreducible representations of every degree. So a hyperlinearity
witness must escape both constraints at once: unbounded irreducible degree with mass (Corollary 3.5), and every
variety that `F` escapes. Neither constraint alone is new in qualitative form. The quantitative mass form (FD8) is new here.

## 4. Verified numerical lower bounds in small dimension

Scripts and outputs are in `experiments/thompson-f-fixed-dimension-hs-constants-2026-09-16/`. All
numbers below are **lower bounds** on `C_n(F)`. A bounded search that finds no larger ratio proves nothing
about `C_n(F)` or its growth.

### 4.1 An exact two-dimensional witness: C_2(F) >= 1 + sqrt 2

**Proposition 4.1.** Let `U = diag(e^(i alpha), e^(-i alpha))` and `W = [[0, 1], [-1, 0]]`. Then

```text
c(U,W) = U^2,     R_1(U,W) = U^6,     R_2(U,W) = U^10.
```

At `alpha = 3 pi/8` this gives `e(c) = 2 + sqrt 2` and `e(R_1) = e(R_2) = 2 - sqrt 2`, so
`C_2(F) >= sqrt((2 + sqrt 2)/(2 - sqrt 2)) = cot(pi/8) = 1 + sqrt 2`.

*Proof.* `W D W^-1` swaps the diagonal entries of a diagonal `D`, so `W U W^-1 = U^-1`, and `W^2 = -I`. Hence
`c = U (W U^-1 W^-1) = U^2`. For diagonal `D_1, D_2` put `A = W D_1` and `B = W D_2`. Then `A^2 = -I`, so
`A^-1 = -A`, and `AB = -D_1^-1 D_2`. So `[A,B] = ABAB = D_1^-2 D_2^2`. Now `x = UW^-1 = W(-U^-1)`,
`y = U^-1 W U = W U^2` and `z = U^-2 W U^2 = W U^4`. So `R_1 = [x,y] = U^2 U^4 = U^6` and
`R_2 = [x,z] = U^10`. With `e(U^k) = 2 - 2 cos(k alpha)`, the ratio is
`sin^2 alpha / max(sin^2 3alpha, sin^2 5alpha)`. At `alpha = 3pi/8`, `|sin 3alpha| = |sin 5alpha| = sin(pi/8)`. ∎

`inspect_witnesses.py` confirms the three identities and the values symbolically (sympy). The pair
generates a binary dihedral group, which is metabelian. So it also sits under the metabelian bound
`||c - I||_2 <= 2||R_1 - I||_2 + ||R_2 - I||_2 <= 3 delta_R`, which transfers to `||.||_2` by bi-invariance
from `thompson-f-metabelian-pairs-obey-sharp-linear-commutator-bound`, as in Section 3.5. The float search
over all of `U(2)^2` (below) converges to the same value `2.414213562...` from 25 random restarts and two floors.
That is evidence, not proof, that `C_2(F) = 1 + sqrt 2`.

### 4.2 Search and rigorous rounding

`ratio_search.py` maximizes `e(c) / max(e(R_1), e(R_2))` over Cayley-parametrized pairs
`U = (I - K)(I + K)^-1`, with `K` skew-Hermitian and a floor `e(c) >= tau`. The stages are L-BFGS-B on an
`l^1` surrogate, then an `l^16` surrogate, then Nelder--Mead on the true maximum. The best parameters are
rounded to multiples of `10^-5`. The script rebuilds `U, W` exactly over `Q(i)`, asserts exact unitarity, and
evaluates `e(c), e(R_1), e(R_2)` as exact rationals. `replay_verify.py` repeats the exact evaluation from the
stored integer parameters with no search. It checks `e(c) >= B^2 max(e(R_1), e(R_2))` in exact rational arithmetic
for the tabulated bound `B`, and prints `ALL_EXACT_CHECKS_HOLD`.

| `n` | file (seed) | float optimum | exactly verified `B` | witness structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `ratio_search_n2.json` (20260916), `tau = 0.01, 0.5` | 2.4142135624 | 2.4141 (and `1 + sqrt 2` exactly, Prop. 4.1) | angles of `U` `±3pi/8` up to scalar, `W` of order 4 in `SU(2)`, axes orthogonal |

So `C_2(F) >= 1 + sqrt 2 = 2.41421...` and `C_n(F) >= 3.2286` for every `n >= 4` by Proposition 2.2. The best
four-dimensional witness is irreducible and beats every padded two-dimensional one. That is consistent with,
but no evidence of, unbounded growth of `C_n(F)`. The `n = 6` run did not even reach the monotone bound, which
shows how weak the heuristic search is as `n` grows.

**Remark 4.2 (the four-dimensional witness is not metabelian).** Item 2 of
`thompson-f-metabelian-pairs-obey-sharp-linear-commutator-bound` is an identity in the free group:
`c = u_2 (a^2 R_1 a^-2)(a R_1 a^-1)(a^2 R_2 a^-2)^-1` with `u_2 in F_2''`. Its words `bAABaaBAba` and
`bAAABaaaBAAbaa` are cyclic conjugates of `R_1 = [ab^-1, a^-1ba]` and `R_2 = [ab^-1, a^-2ba^2]`. Bi-invariance
gives, for every unitary pair,
`||c - I||_2 <= ||u_2(U,W) - I||_2 + 2 ||R_1 - I||_2 + ||R_2 - I||_2 <= ||u_2(U,W) - I||_2 + 3 delta_R`.
The four-dimensional witness satisfies `||c - I||_2 >= 3.2286 delta_R > 3 delta_R` exactly, so `u_2(U,W) != I`, and
`<U, W>` is not metabelian. The same holds for the six-dimensional witness, since `3.2221 > 3`. The two-dimensional
optimum of Proposition 4.1 is metabelian and has ratio `1 + sqrt 2 < 3`.

`metabelian_word_check.py` rechecks the relator conventions and the identity by free reduction. It checks
`u_2 in F_2''` through the Magnus embedding (sympy). As a floating-point illustration it prints
`||u_2(U,W) - I||_2`: `1.171` on the four-dimensional witness, `0.960` on the six-dimensional one, and `5 x 10^-5`
on the rounded two-dimensional one.

### 4.3 Comparison with permutation witnesses

For a permutation matrix `P`, `||P - I||_2^2 = 2 x (fraction of moved points)`. So a permutation pair with
Hamming ratio `t/D` gives `C_n(F) >= (t/D)^(1/2)`. The 27-point pair of
`thompson-f-bounded-derived-length-pairs-obey-linear-bounds` (ratio `4/21`, so `t/D = 21/4`) gives only
`C_27(F) >= 2.2913`. The unitary four-dimensional witness has squared ratio `e(c)/e(R) = 10.42`. The two
problems differ, since hyperlinearity is weaker than soficity, so the comparison only illustrates how much
room unitary pairs have.

## 5. Scope, credit and what remains

**What is proved.**
- Theorem 1.3: fixed-dimension, global, scalar-sensitive linear HS bounds for every profinitely trivial word of a
  finite presentation.
- Proposition 2.1: finiteness in every dimension characterizes profinite triviality.
- Proposition 2.2: monotonicity in the dimension.
- Proposition 2.3: the bounded-degree bound with its mass term (FD6).
- Corollary 2.4: the projective form, which recovers `(FDA20)` of `notes/TRUE_FIXED_DIMENSIONAL_ARC_BOUNDARY.md`
  without curve selection.
- For `F`: `C_n(F) < infinity` in each dimension (Theorem 3.2), `Rep_n(F) = Rep_n(Z^2)` as schemes
  (Theorem 3.3), nonhyperlinearity equals boundedness of one nondecreasing sequence of algebraic numbers
  (Theorem 3.4), quantitative degree escape (Corollary 3.5), and the lower bounds `C_2(F) >= 1 + sqrt 2` and
  `C_4(F) >= 3.2286`.

**Trust surface.**
- The Cannon--Floyd--Parry import (Section 3.1), quoted in the repository but not re-read.
- Statement 3 of `thompson-f-hyperlinear-iff-relator-system-hs-unstable`. Its status is whatever the graph
  computes; its route has not been independently reviewed. Both directions are used: 3 ⇒ 1 for (2 ⇒ 1) of Theorem 3.4, and 1 ⇒ 3 for (1 ⇒ 2).
- `fixed-hs-word-gap-is-linear-domination`.
- Standard commutative algebra: the Hilbert basis theorem, Krull's intersection theorem, Zariski's lemma, Artin--Tate.

Theorem 1.3 and Propositions 2.1--2.3 and Corollaries 2.4--2.5 use no import.

**Credit and novelty.**
- The mechanism is Malcev's residual finiteness of finitely generated linear groups over commutative rings.
- Theorem 3.3 is an instance of Grothendieck (1970, cited from memory).
- The projective fixed-dimension finiteness is already in `notes/TRUE_FIXED_DIMENSIONAL_ARC_BOUNDARY.md` by a
  different, analytic proof, and the escape statements are in `character-domination-collapse-equivalences` at
  hypothesis level.
- New in this repository: the algebraic ideal-membership proof with the explicit shape `|R| n^2 L_n`, the
  scalar-sensitive global bound and all-`n` monotonicity, the mass form (FD6)/(FD8), the scheme equality for `F`,
  the equivalence of Theorem 3.4 with the limit `C_infinity(F)`, and the verified lower bounds. No claim of
  novelty beyond this repository is made.

**What remains.**
- `thompson-f-is-not-hyperlinear` is untouched as a problem. It is now exactly boundedness of `C_n(F)`, and no
  bound on `L_n(R,c)` uniform in `n` is known. Any upper bound for `L_n` from Theorem 1.1 is ineffective: the
  proof is by contradiction through a maximal ideal and a Krull exponent.
- An effective certificate in small `n` (a Gröbner-basis computation over `Z` for `n = 2`) would make `C_2(F)`
  explicit from above and test whether `1 + sqrt 2` is the true value. It was not attempted here.
- A proof that `C_n(F) -> infinity` would show `F` is hyperlinear by Theorem 3.4. By Corollary 3.5 it requires
  witnesses with irreducible degree tending to infinity carrying a fixed fraction of the dimension, and by
  Section 3.5 they must escape every variety that `F` escapes.
