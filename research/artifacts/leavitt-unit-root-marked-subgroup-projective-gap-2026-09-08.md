# A marked-subgroup gap from one invertible root coefficient

2026-09-08. Pure ring and matrix proof; no code or build was run.

The marked-subgroup bound does not require the native product `ts`
to be invertible. One invertible coefficient in a backward root is
enough. This includes cases in which `s`, `t`, `v`, and `ts` are all
singular.

The subsequent [semisimple coefficient theorem](leavitt-semisimple-path-algebra-marked-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md)
also handles the unequal-rank normalization failures when the
coefficient-generated algebra is semisimple. The theorem here
continues to allow nonzero radicals under its unit-root hypothesis.

## 1. Statement and concrete membership certificates

Let `A` be a nonzero finite unital algebra over `F_2`, let

```text
K=<c,x_12(s),x_12(t),x_12(v)> <= EL_3(A),
c=(123),                         Z=ts,
```

and use the native words `r_*,R_v,R_0` defined in the
[full finite-coefficient theorem](leavitt-all-finite-coefficient-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md).
Suppose there is a unit `z in A` for which `x_13(z) in K`.
Conjugating by `c` then also gives `x_21(z),x_32(z) in K`.

**Theorem.** For every homomorphism `rho:K -> PU(N)`, put
`L(g)=d_sc(rho(g),1)`, with normalized Hilbert--Schmidt scalar
distance. Then

```text
L(c) <= 49 L(r_*)+24 L(R_v)+24 L(R_0).             (1)
```

In particular, a trace-zero representative of `rho(c)` forces

```text
max{L(r_*),L(R_v),L(R_0)} >= sqrt(2)/97.           (2)
```

This requires a representation only of `K`, with no ambient-group
extension. The following are explicit sufficient certificates for
the root-membership hypothesis:

- `ts` is a unit: use `z=ts` and the native word `d`.
- `v` is a unit: use `z=v^2` and `[x_12(v),c x_12(v)c^(-1)]`.
- Any finite sum of coefficient words in `s,t,v`, all of length
  congruent to `2 mod 3`, is a unit: use that sum as `z`.

For the last assertion, all words of length `1 mod 3` occur in the
three forward root positions `12,23,31`, and all words of length
`2 mod 3` occur in the backward positions `13,21,32`. Lengths one
and two start the induction. For length `ell=1 mod 3`, `ell>=4`,
split the coefficient word into lengths `2` and `ell-2`; for
`ell=2 mod 3`, `ell>=5`, split it into lengths `1` and `ell-1`.
Distinct-index root commutators multiply the two coefficients in
their original order. Same-root multiplication supplies sums.

For example, in `A=M_2(F_2)` take

```text
s=e_12,                  t=e_21,                  v=0.
```

All three coefficients are singular and `ts=e_22` is singular,
but `z=st+ts=1`. The required root is the product of the roots
obtained from `[x_12(s),c x_12(t)c^(-1)]` and
`[x_12(t),c x_12(s)c^(-1)]`. Thus (1) covers this assignment.

## 2. Recover an internal ring using z

Set

```text
Delta=diag(1,z^(-1),z),       K'=Delta K Delta^(-1),
C=Delta c Delta^(-1),        w=z^3.
```

Transport `rho` to `K'`; this does not require evaluating `rho`
on `Delta`. The three available roots with coefficient `z`
become `x_13(1),x_21(1),x_32(w)`. The commutator recursion of
[the unit-product proof, Section 2](leavitt-unit-product-marked-subgroup-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md)
produces `x_32(w^m)` for every positive integer `m`. The finite
order of `w` therefore gives all constant roots and permutations
inside `K'`. This is a membership argument, without a word-length
bound and without a metric cost.

Define the finite unital subring

```text
B={b in A : x_ij(b) in K' for every i!=j}.
```

It contains `w,w^(-1)` and `b_s=sz,b_t=tz,b_v=vz`. As in the
unit-product proof,

```text
H=C c_0^(-1)=diag(z^(-1),z^(-1),z^2) in K',
H^(-1)x_12(b)H=x_12(sigma(b)),       sigma=Ad(z),
sigma(B)=B,       sigma(w)=w,       sigma^3=Ad(w), (3)
```

where `c_0` is the constant coordinate cycle. Thus roots with
coefficient `sigma^i(b)` are conjugate inside `K'` to roots with
coefficient `b`.

Write a prime for conjugation by `Delta`, and set

```text
a_0=Zz^(-1),       F=1+Z^3,
Q=(v^2+Z)z^(-1),   T=tvsZ^2z.                    (4)
```

Here `a_0,Q,T in B`, because

```text
d'=x_13(a_0),       R_v'=x_13(Q),       R_0'=x_12(T).
```

Also

```text
Z^3=a_0 sigma(a_0) sigma^2(a_0) w in B,
```

so `F in B`. No commutation of `z` with `s,t,v,Z` is assumed.

## 3. Extract the defect coefficients inside K'

The first row of the conjugated first relator is

```text
(Z^10, Fz, Z^5 F z^(-1)).
```

It is annihilated on the right by `(F,z^(-1)Z^10,0)^T`.
Both nonzero column entries lie in `B`: besides `F in B`,

```text
z^(-1)Z^10 = sigma^(-1)(a_0) (Z^3)^3 in B.        (5)
```

The internal proof of
[Preusser, Proposition 9(i)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03536)
therefore gives every root `x_ij(alpha F beta)`, `alpha,beta in B`,
as eight `K'`-conjugates of `(r_*')^(+/-1)`. Specifically, its
conjugators are generated by

```text
tau=x_13(F)x_23(z^(-1)Z^10),
x_21(alpha), x_31(-beta), and constant permutations,
```

all in `EL_3(B) <= K'`. The proposition is being used through
its proof; the entries of `r_*'` need not belong to `B`.

Put `eta_*=L(r_*), eta_v=L(R_v), eta_0=L(R_0)`.
Conjugating the extracted root by (3), and allowing arbitrary
left and right coefficients, yields for `i=0,1,2`

```text
L(x_kl(alpha sigma^i(F) beta)) <= 8 eta_*,
L(x_kl(alpha sigma^i(Q) beta)) <= 4 eta_v,
L(x_kl(alpha sigma^i(T) beta)) <= 4 eta_0.          (6)
```

The last two estimates use the two distinct-index commutators
`[[x_ki(alpha),x_ij(b)],x_ji(beta)]=x_ki(alpha b beta)` and
constant permutations, exactly as in the earlier proof.

## 4. Nine coefficient terms generate the unit

For each simple component map `pi:B -> M_d(F_(2^a))` of `B/J(B)`,
define

```text
D(b)=diag(pi(b),pi(sigma(b)),pi(sigma^2(b))),
W=[ 0  I  0 ]
  [ 0  0  I ]
  [pi(w) 0 0]
```

Equations (3) give `W D(b)=D(sigma(b)) W` and `W^3=D(w)`.
Set

```text
S_hat=D(b_s)W^(-1),       T_hat=D(b_t)W^(-1),
V_hat=D(b_v)W^(-1),       Z_hat=T_hat S_hat.
```

Direct multiplication gives

```text
Z_hat=D(a_0)W,
V_hat^2+Z_hat=D(Q)W,
T_hat V_hat S_hat Z_hat^2=D(T)W^(-1),
1+Z_hat^3=D(F).                                  (7)
```

For clarity, the third equality follows from
`T_hat V_hat S_hat=D(tvs w)W^(-3)` and
`Z_hat^2=D(a_0 sigma(a_0))W^2`: the resulting coefficient is
`tvs a_0 sigma(a_0)w=tvsZ^2z=T`. Thus these identities use only
the displayed skew relations, not an assumed extension of `pi`
to the original coefficient algebra.

Apply the finite matrix rank inequality

```text
rank(T V S Z^2)+rank(V^2+Z)+3 rank(1+Z^3) >= m,
Z=TS,
```

to the `m=3d` matrices in (7). This gives

```text
sum_(i=0)^2 rank pi(sigma^i(Q))
+sum_(i=0)^2 rank pi(sigma^i(T))
+3 sum_(i=0)^2 rank pi(sigma^i(F)) >= 3d.
```

Consequently the sum of the nine ranks of

```text
F,sigma(F),sigma^2(F),Q,sigma(Q),sigma^2(Q),
T,sigma(T),sigma^2(T)
```

is at least `d`. Partition a basis into nine pieces with these
rank capacities. Every corresponding diagonal projection factors
as `alpha b beta` for its assigned coefficient `b`. Do this in
every simple component, lift the coefficients to `B`, and absorb
the inverse of their sum, which is one modulo `J(B)`, into their
left coefficients. The result is an exact nine-term identity

```text
1=sum_(j=1)^9 alpha_j b_j beta_j                 (8)
```

with precisely the nine coefficient types above.

For every `b in B`, multiply (8) on the left by `b`. Equation
(6) and same-root addition then give the uniform bound

```text
L(x_ij(b)) <= kappa:=24 eta_*+12 eta_v+12 eta_0.  (9)
```

This estimate holds for arbitrary `b`, not just the unit.

## 5. Finish and scope

The native word `j'` is conjugate inside `K'` to

```text
e'=x_21(z^(-1)Z)=x_21(sigma^(-1)(a_0)).
```

Its coefficient lies in `B`, so (9) bounds `L(j')` by `kappa`.
Finally `r_*'=j'Cj'C` and `C^3=1` give

```text
L(c)=L(C^2) <= eta_*+2L(j') <= eta_*+2 kappa,
```

which is (1). Trace zero gives `L(c)=sqrt(2)` and proves (2).

The argument covers singular `ts` whenever the stated invertible
root coefficient is available. It does not prove that every finite
coefficient assignment has such a root, and it does not identify
arbitrary frozen complex unitary tuples with coefficient models.
The general marked-subgroup problem and hyperlinearity of the
Leavitt unit group remain unresolved.

## 6. Exact characterization by the finite path algebra

Let `A_0=F_2<s,t,v> <= A`, including its unit, and let
`P <= M_3(A_0)` be the unital algebra generated by the three
coordinate idempotents `e_1,e_2,e_3` and the nine increments

```text
e_12 b, e_23 b, e_31 b,                  b in {s,t,v}.
```

The cycle `c` normalizes `P`. Let `T_2 <= A_0` be the linear
span over `F_2` of coefficient words of length `2 mod 3`.
Multiplying the displayed generators shows exactly that

```text
e_1 P e_3 = {e_13 z : z in T_2}.                 (10)
```

The following three conditions are equivalent:

1. There is a unit `z in A_0` with `x_13(z) in K`.
2. The space `T_2` contains a unit of `A_0`.
3. In every simple matrix factor of `P/J(P)`, the images of
   `e_1,e_2,e_3` have equal ranks.

The condition is unchanged if units in the ambient finite algebra
`A` are allowed in (1): an ambient unit belonging to the finite
unital subalgebra `A_0` has its inverse in `A_0`.

For `(1) => (2)`, let `K_0` be the normal closure of the three
marked roots in `K`. It is generated by their nine cyclic
conjugates: that subgroup contains the marked roots and is
normalized by `c`, hence by `K`. Those nine generators and their
inverses belong to `P`, so `K_0 <= P^x`. Also `K/K_0` is cyclic
of order dividing three. The root `x_13(z)` has order dividing
two, so its image in this quotient is trivial. Therefore
`e_13 z=x_13(z)-1` belongs to `e_1 P e_3`, and (10) gives
`z in T_2`. Condition `(2) => (1)` is the word-membership proof
in Section 1.

For `(2) => (3)`, the three cyclic increments with coefficient
`z` lie in `P`, and

```text
(e_13 z)(e_32 z)(e_21 z)=e_11 z^3.
```

The element `diag(z^3,1,1)` belongs to `P` and is an ambient
unit. Finiteness puts its inverse in `P`. Consequently

```text
(e_32 z)(e_21 z)(e_11 z^(-3))=e_31 z^(-1)
```

is an internal Peirce inverse for `e_13 z`: the two products are
`e_1` and `e_3`. Conjugating by `c` similarly identifies the
other coordinate idempotents. Their images therefore have equal
ranks in every simple factor.

For `(3) => (2)`, choose `x in e_1 P e_3` whose image in each
simple factor is an isomorphism from the image of `e_3` to that
of `e_1`. Such a simultaneous choice lifts from `P/J(P)` by
surjectivity on this Peirce corner. Write `x=e_13 z` using (10).
The cyclic conjugates of `x` are also full isomorphisms in every
simple factor, since `c` permutes those factors. Their product

```text
x(c^2 x c^(-2))(c x c^(-1))=e_11 z^3
```

is thus invertible on the first corner modulo `J(P)`. Hence
`diag(z^3,1,1)` is invertible modulo `J(P)` and consequently
invertible in `P`. Its inclusion in `M_3(A_0)` shows that `z^3`,
and therefore `z`, is a unit of `A_0`. This proves (2).

On `P/J(P)`, the action of `c` has simple-factor orbits of size
one or three. In a fixed factor it cyclically permutes the three
coordinate idempotents, so their ranks are equal. Therefore the
unit-root criterion fails exactly when an orbit of three factors
has unequal coordinate ranks in a component. This is an exact
obstruction to the normalization used above; it is not an
obstruction to a projective gap by some other argument.

## 7. An irreducible coefficient example outside the normalization

In `A=M_4(F_2)`, choose

```text
s=e_13+e_42,       t=e_23+e_41,       v=e_34.       (11)
```

Their coefficient-generated algebra is all of `M_4(F_2)`:

```text
svt=e_11,       tvs=e_22,       vst=e_33,       stv=e_44.
```

These diagonal idempotents isolate the five matrix units
`e_13,e_23,e_34,e_41,e_42` from `s,t,v`. Their directed graph
is strongly connected, so their products give every matrix unit.

Nevertheless `T_2` contains no unit. With `xi_1,...,xi_4` the
standard basis of the coefficient module, grade that space by

```text
V_0=span(xi_1,xi_2),     V_1=span(xi_3),     V_2=span(xi_4).
```

Each coefficient in (11) maps `V_i` into `V_(i-1 mod 3)`.
Therefore every coefficient word of length `2 mod 3`, and every
linear combination of such words, maps the two-dimensional `V_0`
into the one-dimensional `V_1`. Its restriction to `V_0` has a
nonzero kernel. Every element of `T_2` is consequently singular.
Section 6 shows that `K` has no backward root with a unit
coefficient, even though the coefficient algebra acts irreducibly.

This example still has a direct projective gap. More generally, for
arbitrary coefficients in any unital characteristic-two ring, if
`v^2=0`, then the literal root formula gives

```text
R_v=x_13(v^2+ts)=x_13(ts)=d.
```

The native word `j` is conjugate to `e=c d c^(-1)`, so
`L(j)=L(R_v)` in every projective representation of the marked
subgroup. Thus

```text
L(c) <= L(r_*)+2L(R_v).                           (12)
```

This uses only `r_*=jcjc` and `c^3=1`; no finiteness, unit
coefficient, or ambient representation is required. For (11),
`ts=e_43` and `v^2=0`, so (12) applies. If `c` has trace zero,
`max{L(r_*),L(R_v)}>=sqrt(2)/3`.

The unequal-rank normalization obstruction is therefore a real
structural distinction among finite coefficient models, rather
than a counterexample to a uniform gap.
