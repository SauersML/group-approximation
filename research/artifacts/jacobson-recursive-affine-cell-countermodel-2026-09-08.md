# A finite countermodel to closing the recursive cell from its affine family

2026-09-08. The missing recursive boundary relation in
[the full boundary reconstruction](jacobson-full-boundary-opposite-roots-and-gl6-packet-2026-09-08.md)
has not been proved from `Theta`. This note identifies a strictly weaker
collection of its consequences which has a finite countermodel. In
particular, enlarging the head row family from dimension two to dimension
five does not by itself eliminate the cross terms. The countermodel can
also retain a full `GL_6(F_2) x GL_3(F_2)` packet.

It does **not** retain every relation of `Theta`: the additional shifted
`GL_3` relations identifying `u_1` with
`[h_1 delta(c) h_1,delta(k)]`, and the first braid involving the original
tail, are not established in the countermodel below. Thus this is not
a finite or MF countermodel to `Theta` itself.

## 1. The larger affine family available in Theta

Use the notation of the linked proof. The group `C_0=GL_5(F_2)` acts
on the five-coordinate complement to `e_(2,1)` in the reconstructed
`K=GL_6(F_2)`. It centralizes `h_1,u_1`, and `delta(r)` centralizes
all of `K`. Write

```text
V=(F_2^5)^*,
a_v=I+e_(2,1) v in K,
r_1=delta(r),       D_v=h_1 a_v h_1,       C_1=h_1 r_1 h_1.
```

The row roots `a_v` form an elementary abelian group with
`a_(v+w)=a_v a_w`. Conjugating the new TS relation by `C_0`, which
is transitive on its nonzero row covectors, gives all 31 identities

```text
[u_1,D_v C_1]=a_v r_1              for every v!=0.        (1)
```

Moreover the two families `{a_v r_1}` and `{D_v C_1}` are commuting
families of involutions. The desired recursive boundary relation would
give

```text
[u_1,D_v]=a_v                     for every v.           (2)
```

The following model satisfies (1), including even its extra case `v=0`,
but violates (2) for every nonzero `v`.

## 2. A finite group with vector-valued cross terms

The construction works with any nonzero finite-dimensional vector space
`V` over `F_2`. Put `E=V direct-sum F_2`, and define

```text
M((v,t),(w,s))=s v+t w in V,
Q(v,t)=(1+t)v in V.
```

The map `M` is symmetric, alternating, and bilinear. The quadratic
identity is

```text
Q(e+f)+Q(e)+Q(f)=M(e,f).                              (3)
```

On the finite set `P=E x E x V`, define multiplication by

```text
(a,b,z)(a',b',z')
  =(a+a', b+b', z+z'+M(b,a')).                        (4)
```

Bilinearity verifies associativity: the central terms in either triple
product are `M(b,a')+M(b,a'')+M(b',a'')`. The identity is `(0,0,0)`;
the inverse of `(a,b,z)` is `(a,b,z+M(b,a))`. Thus (4) is a group,
of order `2^(3 dim(V)+2)`. Write

```text
X(e)=(e,0,0),    Y(e)=(0,e,0),    Z(v)=(0,0,v).
```

The `X` and `Y` families are each additive commuting involutions;
`Z(V)` is central. Direct multiplication gives

```text
[X(e),Y(f)]=Z(M(e,f)).                               (5)
```

Define two automorphisms of `P` by

```text
h(a,b,z)=(b,a,z+M(a,b)),
u(a,b,z)=(a+b,b,z+Q(b)).                             (6)
```

For `h`, symmetry and bilinearity of `M` verify multiplication.
For `u`, the extra central terms match exactly by (3). Both square
to the identity. They satisfy `(hu)^3=1`: its linear action on
`(a,b)` is `(a,b)->(b,a+b)`, and the three central corrections are

```text
Q(b)+M(a,b),       Q(a)+Q(b),       Q(a)+M(a,b),
```

whose sum is zero. Their linear actions show that they generate a
group of order six.

Every linear automorphism `g` of `V` acts on `E` by `(v,t)->(gv,t)`
and on `P` by applying this to `a,b` and applying `g` to `z`.
Equivariance of `M,Q` proves this is an automorphism commuting with
both maps in (6). Form the finite semidirect product

```text
S=P semidirect (S_3 x GL(V)),                         (7)
```

where the `S_3` generators are the displayed `h,u`. When using the
row-covector convention in Section 1, the `GL_5` action on `V` is
contragredient; this is the same full linear automorphism group.

## 3. All affine cells hold, while the boundary defect survives

Inside `S`, put

```text
a_v=X(v,0),        D_v=Y(v,0),
r=X(0,1),          C=Y(0,1),
z_v=a_v r=X(v,1),  B_v=D_v C=Y(v,1).
```

Conjugation by `h` exchanges `a_v,D_v` and exchanges `r,C`.
The elements `h,u` commute with `GL(V)`, while `r` centralizes
the row parabolic generated by the `a_v` and `GL(V)`.

Because `Q(v,1)=0`, formula (6) gives

```text
u B_v u=z_v B_v,
[u,B_v]=z_v                         for every v.       (8)
```

The diagonal commutation `[z_v,B_v]=1` follows either from (5) or
from `M((v,1),(v,1))=0`. Thus (8) is precisely the affine family
(1). Its `v=0` instance even gives the pure-tail relation `[u,C]=r`.

But `Q(v,0)=v`, so

```text
[u,D_v]=a_v Z(v),
[u,D_v]a_v=Z(v)!=1                   for v!=0.          (9)
```

The corresponding cross term is

```text
[r,D_v]=Z(v).                                         (10)
```

It is exactly this term that cancels in the product cell (8).
More generally (5) gives
`[z_v,B_w]=Z(v+w)`. These cross terms transform as the entire
`GL(V)` module `V`; they are not scalar invariant forms that could
be excluded simply by increasing `dim(V)`.

## 4. Keeping the full GL6 and residual GL3 packets does not remove
the countermodel to these selected identities

Now take `dim(V)=5`. The subgroup of `S` generated by all `a_v`,
`GL(V)`, and `r` is

```text
Q_0=(V semidirect GL_5(F_2)) x <r>.                    (11)
```

Embed the first factor as the row parabolic of `GL_6(F_2)` fixing
the distinguished sixth vector. Its matrices, in the five-plus-one
coordinate order, are

```text
[ g  0 ]
[ v  1 ].
```

The action on the row subgroup is `v->v g^-1`. Embed the second
factor of (11) as the subgroup generated by the nontrivial
transvection `x_23` in a second factor of

```text
T=GL_6(F_2) x GL_3(F_2).                             (12)
```

These are injective copies of the same finite group `Q_0` in `S,T`.
There is a finite permutation group containing faithful copies of
both factors with these copies of `Q_0` identified. Here is an
explicit existence construction: choose an integer divisible by
`|S|` and `|T|`, take that many points with repeated left regular
actions of each group, and identify their restricted `Q_0` actions.
Both restrictions are free actions with the same number of orbits,
so a `Q_0`-equivariant identification exists. The finite permutation
group generated by the two actions contains each factor faithfully.

In this finite group, all equations proved in `S` remain true, and
the new full packet (12) is exact. In particular `r` now centralizes
the whole `GL_6` factor, not merely its row parabolic. Yet the defect
`Z(v)` in (9) survives because the copy of `S` is faithful.

This proves a precise limitation: the affine cells (1), the common
`S_3` pair `h,u`, the commuting `GL_5`, and the full exact packet
`GL_6 x GL_3` with `r` in its second factor do not imply the
recursive boundary relation. The remaining mixed definitions and
relations must be used. No assertion that this finite group satisfies
the complete shifted `GL_3` packet or the first braid is made.

## 5. Remaining requirement

For the actual `Theta`, one must still eliminate the defect using
its specified common elements

```text
u_1=[h_1 delta(c) h_1,delta(k)],
<delta(c),delta(k),h_1> ~= GL_3(F_2),
(h_1 tau(q_P)delta(q_P))^3=1,
```

and any further consequences of the existing finite packets. The
countermodel establishes neither independence from these full data
nor the desired recursive identity. Its role is to exclude an
otherwise tempting but false shortcut through the 31 affine cells
and the enlarged head packet alone.
