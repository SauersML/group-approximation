# Polycyclic guard subgroups: Hirsch-length extension

Date: 2026-09-08. This checks the exact rank axioms needed to extend
the finite coset stratification theorem from virtually abelian to
virtually polycyclic subgroups. It is a positive class theorem, not
a solution of Gottschalk surjunctivity. No computation was run.

The complete local-map proof being extended is in
`research/artifacts/gottschalk-virtually-abelian-guard-stratification-2026-09-08.md`.
Its uniform local recoverability hypothesis remains in force.

## Statement

Let P be a finite-memory map on A^G whose coordinate rule depends
on membership in finitely many cosets K_i a_i, is the identity when
all mask bits vanish, and has a uniformly local inverse on its image.
If every K_i is virtually polycyclic, then P is bijective.

Consequently, let F:A^G -> A^G be a cellular automaton on a finite
alphabet with zero, and suppose F preserves supports on all unions
of cosets of H<=G. Injectivity implies surjectivity whenever the
subgroup generated by the guard addresses in its finite memory is
virtually polycyclic. In particular this holds for every locally
virtually polycyclic H, meaning that every finitely generated
subgroup of H is virtually polycyclic.

The proof requires no commensuration assumption on H in G. The
virtually abelian case is included, as are finitely generated
nilpotent guard subgroups such as the integer Heisenberg group.

## All subgroup and rank facts used by the induction

A polycyclic group has a finite subnormal series with cyclic factors.
Its Hirsch length h is the number of infinite cyclic factors; this
is independent of the chosen series. For a virtually polycyclic
group, use the Hirsch length of a finite-index polycyclic subgroup.
The standard equal-length/finite-index property and its extension
to virtually polycyclic groups are recorded in Section 2 of
[Matt Clay, A fixed point theorem for deformation spaces of G-trees](https://mattclay.hosted.uark.edu/Papers/real_3.pdf).

Here are the precise properties needed, with their series arguments.

1. Every subgroup of a polycyclic group is polycyclic: intersect it
   with a cyclic series. Each resulting factor embeds in a cyclic
   factor of the original series and is therefore cyclic. For a
   subgroup A of a virtually polycyclic group K, its intersection
   with a finite-index polycyclic subgroup P has finite index in A,
   so A is virtually polycyclic. This proves closure under all
   subgroup intersections used in the masks.

2. If A<=K are virtually polycyclic, then h(A)<=h(K), with equality
   exactly when [K:A] is finite. For polycyclic K, prove this by
   induction on a cyclic series. If N is its first proper term,
   normal in K with cyclic quotient, the induced series gives

       h(A)=h(A intersect N)+h(AN/N),
       h(K)=h(N)+h(K/N).

   Each summand on the first line is bounded by the corresponding
   one on the second. Equality forces A intersect N to have finite
   index in N by induction, and AN/N to have finite index in K/N
   because a subgroup of a cyclic group has equal Hirsch length
   exactly in that case. The index identity

       [K:A]=[K:AN] [N:A intersect N]

   then gives finite index. Conversely, finite index makes both
   factors have finite index and induction gives equality. For
   virtually polycyclic K, take a normal finite-index polycyclic
   subgroup P and apply the result inside P to A intersect P.
   Such P is obtained by taking the core of any finite-index
   polycyclic subgroup.

3. Hirsch length zero means finite: in a polycyclic series all
   factors are then finite, and a finite extension preserves
   finiteness. This is the finite-output base case of the induction.

4. Virtually polycyclic groups are amenable. Finite and cyclic groups
   are amenable, and amenability is preserved by extensions. One
   can see the latter directly by averaging first on a normal
   subgroup with an invariant mean, then on the quotient with an
   invariant mean; left translation changes the inner coset
   representative by an element of the normal subgroup, which the
   inner mean absorbs. Apply this along the cyclic series and then
   through the finite extension.

In particular, two stabilizers of the same Hirsch length have an
intersection of that length exactly when they are commensurate.
Finite intersections of members of one commensurability class have
finite index in each member. Intersections with another class, or
with a smaller-length stabilizer, have strictly smaller length.

## Replacing the lattice-avoidance step

The only part of the virtually abelian proof that explicitly used
a lattice was isolating one maximal-rank commensurability class.
The following amenable-group argument supplies exactly the same
avoidance conclusion.

Let L be amenable and let mu be a left-invariant finitely additive
probability measure on all subsets of L. If J<=L has infinite index,
then arbitrarily many distinct left J-cosets are disjoint and all
have measure mu(J). Hence mu(J)=0. Every right coset J h also has
measure zero, since

    J h=h (h^-1 J h)

is a left coset of another infinite-index subgroup. Therefore no
finite union of infinite-index subgroup cosets covers L.

For a maximal-Hirsch-length class j, take L to be the intersection
of its stabilizers. It is virtually polycyclic and amenable. For
every finite observation window, the forbidden translations are
finitely many cosets of intersections L intersect K_i with non-j
stabilizers. All these intersections have smaller Hirsch length,
and thus infinite index in L. The preceding measure argument
provides a translation avoiding all of them. All class-j masks
remain unchanged under that translation.

This yields the same exact coefficientwise isolation as in the
virtually abelian proof. The uniformly local inverse radius passes
injectivity to the isolated map; ordinary nonuniform injectivity
alone is still not used for that limit step.

## The remaining steps retain their hypotheses

The isolated class has finitely many free left L-orbits because
L has finite index in each of its stabilizers. With the exterior
set to zero, its local map is therefore a finite-alphabet CA over
the amenable group L. Injectivity implies surjectivity by the
[Myhill theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5965).
Preserved exterior certificate fibers, finite-output perturbations,
and compactness then make the whole isolated map bijective.

Uniform local recoverability gives its inverse a finite memory.
Left L-equivariance and finitely many active L-orbits give that
inverse finitely many coset masks with stabilizer L. Under local
composition, the masks are right-translated; their left stabilizer
class is unchanged.

After cancelling this isolated bijection, the residual map is the
identity off the original union W of non-j masks. Replacing every
remaining j-mask C by C intersect W expresses it as finitely many
cosets of strictly smaller Hirsch length. Thus one maximal-length
class disappears. Uniform local recoverability survives the
composition, exactly as proved in the virtually abelian artifact.

Induct on maximum Hirsch length and then on the number of classes
at that length. Length zero has only finite masks and is handled
by the finite-fiber argument. These checked facts prove the stated
finite-mask theorem; no other use of commutativity or lattice
coordinates occurs in the local-map induction.

## Guard application and finite-memory reduction

For a finite union V of H-cosets, zero-fill the input off V, apply
F on V, and preserve exterior coordinates. When H is virtually
polycyclic, the resulting map has masks g_i H and g_i H s^-1 for
memory addresses s. Their left stabilizers g_i H g_i^-1 are all
virtually polycyclic. The original injective CA F has a uniform
local inverse on its image; support preservation makes this same
recoverability condition valid for the zero-filled restriction.
The finite-mask theorem therefore makes each such restriction
bijective. Density of finitely supported targets and compactness
prove surjectivity of F.

Finally, for arbitrary H and finite memory S, put H_0=<S intersect H>.
If x vanishes on gH_0, one can choose z agreeing with x on gS and
vanishing on gH. The overlap g(S intersect H) already has zero
values, so these requirements are consistent. Then

    F(x)(g)=F(z)(g)=0.

Thus H_0 is itself a guard subgroup. If it is virtually polycyclic,
the preceding theorem applies. This gives the stated locally
virtually polycyclic corollary without imposing finite generation
or a finite Hirsch-length bound on the original guard H.
