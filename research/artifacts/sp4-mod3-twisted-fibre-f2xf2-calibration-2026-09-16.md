## 0. Statements

**Theorem A (untwisting).**  Let `H` be one of the following subgroups of

2. the Siegel parabolic `P_S(Z)`, the stabilizer of the Lagrangian
   `Z e_1 + Z e_2`, isomorphic to `Sym_2(Z) x| GL_2(Z)`;
3. the Klingen parabolic `P_K(Z)`, the stabilizer of the line `Z e_1`,

## 1. Conventions

- **The group.**  `V = R^4` with basis `e_1, f_1, e_2, f_2` and symplectic
  form `b` given by
  - `b(e_i, f_j) = delta_ij`;
  - `b(e_i, e_j) = b(f_i, f_j) = 0`.

  Put `G = Sp(V, b) = Sp_4(R)`.  `Sp_4(Z)` is the subgroup preserving the
  lattice `Z^4` spanned by the basis.
- **The cover.**  `p : G_3 -> G` is the connected three-fold covering group,
  as in `deligne-triple-cover-fd-central-invisibility`.
  - `p` is a continuous surjective homomorphism and a covering map.
  - Its kernel is `<z>`, cyclic of order three.
  - `E_3 = p^{-1}(Sp_4(Z))`.
  - For `Q <= G`, write `Q~ = p^{-1}(Q)`.
- **The multiplier.**  Let `sigma : Sp_4(Z) -> E_3` be a set-theoretic
  section with `sigma(1) = 1`.
  - `sigma(g) sigma(h) = c(g,h) sigma(gh)` with `c(g,h) in <z>`.
  - `chi_1, chi_2` are the two faithful characters of `<z>`, with
    `chi_1(z) = omega` and `chi_2(z) = omega^2`.
  - `alpha = chi_1 o c`, and then `alpha^2 = chi_2 o c`.
  - `C*(Sp_4(Z); alpha)` is the universal C*-algebra generated by unitaries
    `v_g` with `v_g v_h = alpha(g,h) v_{gh}` and `v_1 = 1`.

  Changing `sigma` changes `alpha` by a coboundary and the algebra by an
  isomorphism.  Some authors use the conjugate relation; that swaps `chi_1`
  and `chi_2`.  Every statement below is proved for both characters, so the
  convention does not matter.
- **Full group C*-algebras.**  For a discrete group `Q`, `C*(Q)` has
  canonical unitaries `u_x`, `x in Q`.  For central `z in Q` of order three,
  put

  ```text
  p_chi = (1/3) sum_{k=0}^{2} chi(z)^{-k} u_z^k .
  ```

  This is a central projection: it is self-adjoint because
  `chi(z)^{-k} = conj(chi(z)^k)` and `u_z^{-k} = u_z^* ^k`, and the
  convolution identity gives `p_chi^2 = p_chi`.  Moreover
  `p_chi u_z = chi(z) p_chi`.

## 2. Covering-group lemmas

All topological groups below are subgroups of `G` or `G_3` with the subspace
topology.

**Lemma 2.1 (central kernel).**  `ker p = <z>` is central in `G_3`.

*Proof.*  Fix `k in ker p`.  The map `g -> g k g^{-1}` is continuous from
`G_3` into `ker p`, since `ker p` is normal.  `ker p` is discrete, because
`p` is a covering map.  `G_3` is connected and the map takes the value `k`
at `g = 1`, so it is constant.  []

**Lemma 2.2 (homomorphic sections).**  Let `Q <= G` be a subgroup which is
connected in the subspace topology.

- (a) Two continuous homomorphisms `s, s' : Q -> G_3` with
  `p o s = p o s' = id_Q` are equal.
- (b) A continuous map `s : Q -> G_3` with `p o s = id_Q` and `s(1) = 1` is
  a homomorphism.
- (c) Let `s` be as in (a), and let `y in G_3` be such that `p(y)` normalizes
  `Q`.  Then `y s(q) y^{-1} = s(p(y) q p(y)^{-1})` for all `q in Q`.
- (d) Suppose further that `Q` is path-connected and locally path-connected,
  and that the inclusion induces the zero map `pi_1(Q, 1) -> pi_1(G, 1)`.
  Then a continuous homomorphic section `s : Q -> G_3`, with `p o s = id_Q`,
  exists, and it is unique by (a).

*Proof.*

- (a) `q -> s'(q) s(q)^{-1}` is continuous `Q -> ker p`.  The domain is
  connected and the target discrete, and the value at `q = 1` is `1`.
- (b) `(q, q') -> s(q) s(q') s(q q')^{-1}` is continuous
  `Q x Q -> ker p`.  The domain is connected, and the value at `(1,1)` is
  `1`.
- (c) Put `g = p(y)`.  The map `q -> y s(g^{-1} q g) y^{-1}` is a continuous
  homomorphism `Q -> G_3`.  Conjugation by `g` preserves `Q` and is
  continuous, and so is conjugation by `y`.  Applying `p` gives
  `g g^{-1} q g g^{-1} = q`.  By (a) this map equals `s`.  Substituting
  `g q g^{-1}` for `q` gives the claim.
- (d) This is the lifting criterion for covering spaces.  `Q` is
  path-connected and locally path-connected, and the inclusion
  `f : Q -> G` satisfies `f_* pi_1(Q,1) = 0 <= p_* pi_1(G_3, 1)`.  So there
  is a continuous `s : Q -> G_3` with `p o s = f` and `s(1) = 1`.  By (b) it
  is a homomorphism.  []

**Lemma 2.3 (commuting lifts).**  Let `S_1, S_2 <= G` be connected subgroups
with `s_1 s_2 = s_2 s_1` for all `s_1 in S_1` and `s_2 in S_2`.  Then every
element of `p^{-1}(S_1)` commutes with every element of `p^{-1}(S_2)`.

*Proof.*

1. For `s_i in S_i` choose lifts `x_i in p^{-1}(s_i)`, and put
   `c(s_1, s_2) = x_1 x_2 x_1^{-1} x_2^{-1}`.
   - Changing `x_i` multiplies it by an element of `ker p`, which is central
     by Lemma 2.1.  So `c` does not depend on the choices.
   - `p(c(s_1,s_2)) = [s_1, s_2] = 1`, so `c` takes values in `ker p`.
2. The map `c` is continuous.  Every `g in G` has an evenly covered open
   neighbourhood `U`.  On such a `U` there is a continuous local section
   `tau_U : U -> G_3` of `p`, namely the inverse of `p` restricted to one
   sheet.  On `(U_1 cap S_1) x (U_2 cap S_2)` we have
   `c(s_1,s_2) = [tau_{U_1}(s_1), tau_{U_2}(s_2)]`, which is continuous.
3. `S_1 x S_2` is connected, `ker p` is discrete, and `c(1,1) = 1`.  Hence
   `c = 1`.  []

**Connectedness of the groups used.**  `SL_2(R)` is path-connected.  It is
generated by the elementary matrices `[[1,t],[0,1]]` and `[[1,0],[t,1]]`, and
each lies on the path `r -> [[1,rt],[0,1]]` (resp. `[[1,0],[rt,1]]`) from the
identity.  Products of paths from `1` give paths from `1`.

## 3. Free groups, SL_2(Z), and the Sanov subgroup

**Lemma 3.1 (free groups).**  Let `F` be free on a set `X`, and let
`q : F~ -> F` be a surjective homomorphism.  Then there is a homomorphism
`s : F -> F~` with `q o s = id_F`.

*Proof.*  Choose `s(x) in q^{-1}(x)` for `x in X`, and extend by the
universal property of `F`.  Then `q o s` fixes `X`, so it is the identity.  []

**Lemma 3.2P (the presentation of `SL_2(Z)`).**  Put `a = [[0,-1],[1,0]]` and
`b = [[0,-1],[1,1]]`, and let `F_0 = < A, B | A^4, A^2 B^{-3} >`.  Then
`A -> a`, `B -> b` defines an isomorphism `theta_0 : F_0 -> SL_2(Z)`.

This is the classical amalgam `SL_2(Z) = Z/4 *_{Z/2} Z/6`; the usual
reference is Serre, *Trees*, Chapter I, section 4 (location not re-checked in
this session).  Nothing below depends on that citation, because the lemma is
proved here in full.

*Proof.*

1. **The relations hold.**  `a^2 = -I`, `b^2 = [[-1,-1],[1,0]]` and
   `b^3 = b^2 b = -I`.  So `a^4 = I` and `a^2 = b^3`, and `theta_0` is a
   well-defined homomorphism.
2. **`theta_0` is surjective.**  Put `tau = [[1,1],[0,1]]`.  Since
   `a^{-1} = [[0,1],[-1,0]]`, we get `a^{-1} b = tau`, so `tau in <a, b>`.  Let
   `g = [[p,q],[r,s]] in SL_2(Z)`; we show `g in <a,b>` by induction on `|r|`.
   - If `r = 0`, then `ps = 1`, so `p = s = +-1` and
     `g = [[p, q],[0, p]] = (a^2)^{(1-p)/2} tau^{pq}`, using `a^2 = -I` and
     `p^2 = 1`.
   - If `r != 0`, choose `k in Z` with `0 <= p + k r < |r|` (division with
     remainder).  Then `tau^k g = [[p + kr, q + ks],[r, s]]`, and
     `a tau^k g = [[-r, -s],[p + kr, q + ks]]`.  Its lower-left entry has
     absolute value `< |r|`, so `a tau^k g in <a, b>` by induction, and hence
     `g in <a, b>`.
3. **Ping-pong for `PSL_2(Z) = SL_2(Z)/{+-I}`.**  Let `Omega = R \ Q`.  For
   `g = [[p,q],[r,s]] in SL_2(Z)` and `x in Omega` put
   `g.x = (p x + q)/(r x + s)`.
   - *Well defined on `Omega`.*  `(r, s) != (0, 0)` and `x` is irrational,
     so `r x + s != 0`.  If `g.x = t in Q`, then `x (p - t r) = t s - q`.
     If `p - t r != 0`, `x` would be rational.  If `p - t r = 0`, then
     `t s = q`; for `r != 0` this gives `p s = q r`, contradicting
     `ps - qr = 1`, and for `r = 0` it gives `p = 0`, contradicting `ps = 1`.
     So `g.x in Omega`.
   - *An action.*  `(g h).x = g.(h.x)`: this is the matrix product applied
     to the column `(x, 1)^T`, followed by division of the first coordinate
     by the second.  `-I` acts trivially, so `PSL_2(Z)` acts on `Omega`.
   - *The generators.*  Write `abar, bbar` for the images of `a, b`.  Then
     `abar.x = -1/x`, `bbar.x = -1/(x+1)` and `bbar^2.x = -1 - 1/x`.  With
     `Omega_+ = Omega cap (0, oo)` and `Omega_- = Omega cap (-oo, 0)`:
     `abar(Omega_-) <= Omega_+`, `bbar(Omega_+) <= Omega_-` and
     `bbar^2(Omega_+) <= Omega_-`.
   - *No extra relations.*  Let `P = < X, Y | X^2, Y^3 >` and let
     `P -> PSL_2(Z)` send `X -> abar`, `Y -> bbar`.  Every element of `P` is
     represented by a word alternating between the letter `X` and letters
     `Y^e`, `e in {1, 2}`: replace `X^{-1}` by `X` and `Y^{-1}` by `Y^2`,
     then repeatedly delete `XX` and `YYY` and merge adjacent powers of `Y`.
     Each step shortens the word.  The empty word represents `1`.  Let `w`
     be a nonempty alternating word; we show its image acts nontrivially.
     - (i) `w = Y^{e_1} X Y^{e_2} X ... X Y^{e_k}` with `k >= 1`.  Reading
       from the right, the letters move `Omega_+` into `Omega_-`, then into
       `Omega_+`, and so on, ending in `Omega_-`.  So `w(Omega_+) <= Omega_-`
       and `w` acts nontrivially.
     - (ii) `w = X`.  `abar.sqrt(2) = -1/sqrt(2) != sqrt(2)`.
     - (iii) `w = X Y^{e_1} X ... X Y^{e_k} X` with `k >= 1`.  Then `X w X`
       reduces to a word of type (i), and a conjugate of a nontrivially
       acting element acts nontrivially.
     - (iv) `w` begins with `X` and ends with `Y^{e_k}`.  Pick
       `d in {1, 2}` with `d != e_k`.  Then `Y^d w Y^{-d}` is the word
       `Y^d X ... X Y^{e_k - d}` with `e_k - d != 0 mod 3`, of type (i).
     - (v) `w` begins with a power of `Y` and ends with `X`.  Its inverse
       is represented by an alternating word of type (iv).

     So `P -> PSL_2(Z)` is injective.
4. **`theta_0` is injective.**  In `F_0` put `c = A^2`, which equals `B^3`.
   It commutes with `A` and with `B`, so `<c>` is central, and
   `c^2 = A^4 = 1`.  Adding the relator `A^2` to the presentation of `F_0`
   makes `A^4` redundant and turns `A^2 B^{-3}` into `B^{-3}`, so
   `F_0/<c> = P`.  The composite `F_0 -> SL_2(Z) -> PSL_2(Z)` kills `c`, since
   `theta_0(c) = -I`, and induces the injective map of step 3.  If
   `theta_0(w) = I`, then the class of `w` in `P` is trivial, so
   `w in {1, c}`.  Since `theta_0(c) = -I != I`, `w = 1`.  []

**Lemma 3.2 (central `Z/3`-extensions of `SL_2(Z)`).**  Let
`1 -> <z> -> Gamma~ -> SL_2(Z) -> 1` be a central extension with `z` of order
three.  Then there is a homomorphism `phi : Gamma~ -> T` with
`phi(z) = omega`.

Keep `a, b` as in Lemma 3.2P.

*Proof.*

1. **Lifts and the comparison group.**  Choose lifts `a~, b~ in Gamma~`.  The
   relations of `SL_2(Z)` give `a~^4 = z^i` and `a~^2 b~^{-3} = z^j` for some
   `i, j in Z/3`.  Let `F` be the group with generators `A, B, Z` and
   relators

   ```text
   Z^3,  [Z,A],  [Z,B],  A^4 Z^{-i},  A^2 B^{-3} Z^{-j}.
   ```

   The assignment `A -> a~`, `B -> b~`, `Z -> z` respects these relators.
   It therefore defines a homomorphism `theta : F -> Gamma~`.
2. **`theta` is surjective.**  `a` and `b` generate `SL_2(Z)` (Lemma 3.2P,
   step 2), and the kernel of `Gamma~ -> SL_2(Z)` is `<z>`.  So `a~, b~, z` generate `Gamma~`.
3. **`theta` is injective.**  Let `N = <Z> <= F`, which is central of order at
   most three.  Adding the relator `Z` to the presentation of `F` gives
   `< A, B | A^4, A^2 B^{-3} >`.  So `F/N` is isomorphic to `SL_2(Z)` via
   `A -> a`, `B -> b` (Lemma 3.2P), and `theta` covers this isomorphism.
   - If `theta(x) = 1`, then the image of `x` in `F/N` is trivial.
   - So `x = Z^k`, and `1 = theta(x) = z^k`.
   - Hence `3 | k`, and `x = 1`.
4. **The character.**  Choose `c in T` with `c^4 = omega^i`, and then `d in T`
   with `d^3 = c^2 omega^{-j}`.  The homomorphism from the free group on
   `A, B, Z` to `T` sending `A -> c`, `B -> d`, `Z -> omega` kills every
   relator:
   - `omega^3 = 1`;
   - `T` is abelian, so the commutators die;
   - `c^4 omega^{-i} = 1`;
   - `c^2 d^{-3} omega^{-j} = 1`.

   So it factors through `F`, and `phi = (that character) o theta^{-1}` works.  []

*Caveat.*  Lemma 3.2 does not say that the `Z/3`-extension splits.  In the
Klingen case below it need not, and nothing here uses a splitting over
`SL_2(Z)`.

**Lemma 3.3 (Sanov).**  `M_1 = [[1,2],[0,1]]` and `M_2 = [[1,0],[2,1]]` generate
a free subgroup of rank two in `SL_2(Z)`.

*Proof (ping-pong).*

1. **The two regions.**  Let `X_1 = {(x,y) in R^2 : |x| > |y|}` and
   `X_2 = {(x,y) : |x| < |y|}`.  They are disjoint and nonempty.  For
   `k != 0`:
   - if `(x,y) in X_2`, then `M_1^k (x,y) = (x + 2ky, y)`, and
     `|x + 2ky| >= 2|y| - |x| > |y|`.  So `M_1^k X_2 subset X_1`.
   - Symmetrically `M_2^k X_1 subset X_2`.
2. **Good words are nontrivial.**  Let `w` be a reduced word
   `M_1^{k_1} M_2^{l_1} ... M_2^{l_{m-1}} M_1^{k_m}` with all exponents
   nonzero and `m >= 1`.  For `v in X_2` the letters send
   `X_2 -> X_1 -> X_2 -> ... -> X_1`, so `w v in X_1`.  Hence `w v != v` and
   `w != I`.
3. **Every nontrivial reduced word is conjugate to a good one.**  Let `w'` be
   a nontrivial reduced word, written in alternating syllables.
   - **One syllable.**  If `w' = M_1^a` or `w' = M_2^l`, it is not `I`: its
     upper-right entry is `2a != 0`, resp. its lower-left entry is `2l != 0`.
   - **At least two syllables.**  Let the first and last syllables be `s`
     and `s'`, and form `M_1^r w' M_1^{-r}` with `r != 0`.
     - If `s = M_1^a`, it merges into `M_1^{r+a}`; require `r + a != 0`.
     - If `s` is an `M_2`-power, `M_1^r` is a new first syllable.
     - If `s' = M_1^{a'}`, it merges into `M_1^{a'-r}`; require `a' != r`.
     - If `s'` is an `M_2`-power, `M_1^{-r}` is a new last syllable.

     Only finitely many `r` are excluded.  The result is a reduced
     alternating word beginning and ending with nonzero powers of `M_1`,
     that is, a good word.  So it is not `I`, and neither is its conjugate
     `w'`.

So `M_1, M_2` satisfy no nontrivial relation.  []

## 4. Theorem A(1): the block-diagonal SL_2(Z) x SL_2(Z)

**The two factors.**  For `i = 1, 2` let `S_i(R) <= G` be the set of `g`
that act on the plane `P_i = <e_i, f_i>` by a matrix of `SL_2(R)` and fix
the other plane pointwise.  Put `S_i(Z) = S_i(R) cap Sp_4(Z)`.

- **Each factor is a copy of `SL_2(R)` in `G`.**  `b` restricted to `P_i` is
  the standard area form, and `SL_2(R) = Sp_2(R)` preserves it.  The planes
  `P_1` and `P_2` are `b`-orthogonal, so `g` preserves `b`.
- **The factors commute**, since they act on complementary invariant planes.
- **The integer points.**  `S_i(Z) = SL_2(Z)`, and `S = S_1(Z) S_2(Z)` is
  isomorphic to `SL_2(Z) x SL_2(Z)`.

**Proposition 4.1.**  There is a homomorphism `phi_S : S~ -> T` with
`phi_S(z) = omega`.

*Proof.*

1. **Commuting preimages.**  Put `S~_i = p^{-1}(S_i(Z))`.
   - `S_1(R)` and `S_2(R)` are connected commuting subgroups (section 2,
     last paragraph).  By Lemma 2.3, `S~_1` and `S~_2` commute elementwise.
   - `S~ = S~_1 S~_2`.  Given `x in S~` with `p(x) = s_1 s_2`, choose
     `x_1 in S~_1` over `s_1`; then `x_1^{-1} x` lies over `s_2`.
   - `S~_1 cap S~_2 = p^{-1}(S_1(Z) cap S_2(Z)) = p^{-1}(1) = <z>`.
2. **Characters on the factors.**  Each `S~_i -> S_i(Z) = SL_2(Z)` is a
   central extension by `<z>`.  By Lemma 3.2 there are homomorphisms
   `phi_i : S~_i -> T` with `phi_i(z) = omega`.
3. **Definition of `phi_S`.**  Put `phi_S(x_1 x_2) = phi_1(x_1) phi_2(x_2)`.
   - *Well defined.*  Suppose `x_1 x_2 = x_1' x_2'`.  Then
     `x_1'^{-1} x_1 = x_2' x_2^{-1}` lies in `S~_1 cap S~_2`, so it equals
     some `z^k`.  Therefore

     ```text
     phi_1(x_1) phi_2(x_2) = phi_1(x_1') omega^k phi_2(x_2)
                           = phi_1(x_1') phi_2(z^k x_2)
                           = phi_1(x_1') phi_2(x_2').
     ```
   - *A homomorphism.*  `x_2` commutes with `y_1`, so
     `(x_1 x_2)(y_1 y_2) = (x_1 y_1)(x_2 y_2)`.
   - *The value at `z`.*  `phi_S(z) = phi_1(z) phi_2(1) = omega`.  []

**Corollary 4.2 (splitting over products of free groups).**  Let
`F <= S_1(Z)` and `F' <= S_2(Z)` be free subgroups.  Then `p` has a
homomorphic section over `F F' = F x F'`.  Hence `p^{-1}(F x F')` is
`s(F x F') x <z>`, and `phi(s(k) z^j) = omega^j` is a character.

*Proof.*

1. By Lemma 3.1 there are homomorphic sections `s_1 : F -> p^{-1}(F)` and
   `s_2 : F' -> p^{-1}(F')`.
2. Their images commute (Lemma 2.3).  So
   `s(w, w') = s_1(w) s_2(w')` is a homomorphism `F x F' -> E_3` lifting the
   inclusion.
3. `s(F x F')` meets `<z>` trivially, since `p o s` is injective, and `z` is
   central.  []

With the Sanov subgroup of Lemma 3.3 in both factors this gives
`F_2 x F_2 <= S` with a split preimage.  This part uses neither Lemma 3.2
nor the presentation of `SL_2(Z)` (Lemma 3.2P).

## 5. Theorem A(2): the Siegel parabolic

**Coordinates.**  In this section only, order the basis as
`(e_1, e_2, f_1, f_2)` and write matrices in `2 x 2` blocks.  Then
`b(x, y) = x^T J y` with `J = [[0, I],[-I, 0]]`, and `g in G` means
`g^T J g = J`.

**The real parabolic.**  Let `P_S(R) <= G` be the stabilizer of the real
Lagrangian `L = R e_1 + R e_2`.  An element of `M_4(R)` preserving `L` has
the form `g = [[A, B],[0, D]]`, and

```text
g^T J g = [[0, A^T D],[-D^T A, B^T D - D^T B]].
```

So `g in G` iff `D = A^{-T}` and `A^{-1} B` is symmetric.  Put
`m(A) = [[A, 0],[0, A^{-T}]]` and `n(S) = [[I, S],[0, I]]`.  Then

```text
P_S(R) = { m(A) n(S) = [[A, A S],[0, A^{-T}]] : A in GL_2(R), S in Sym_2(R) },
m(A) n(S) m(A)^{-1} = n(A S A^T).
```

The map `(S, A) -> m(A) n(S)` is a homeomorphism
`Sym_2(R) x GL_2(R) -> P_S(R)`: its inverse reads off `A` as the upper-left
block and `S = A^{-1} B`.

**The integral parabolic.**  `P_S(Z) = P_S(R) cap Sp_4(Z)`.  For `g in Sp_4(Z)`,
preserving `L` is the same as preserving the lattice `L cap Z^4 = Z e_1 + Z e_2`,
because `g Z^4 = Z^4`.  If `g = m(A) n(S)` is integral, then `A` and
`A^{-T}` are integral, so `A in GL_2(Z)`; and `S = A^{-1}(A S)` is integral.
The converse is clear.  So

```text
P_S(Z) = { m(A) n(S) : A in GL_2(Z), S in Sym_2(Z) } = Sym_2(Z) x| GL_2(Z),
```

with `GL_2(Z)` acting by `S -> A S A^T`.

**The identity component.**  `g = [[A, B],[0, D]] -> sign det A` is a
continuous homomorphism `P_S(R) -> {+-1}`; the upper-left block of a product
of block upper-triangular matrices is the product of the blocks.  Let
`P_S(R)_0` be its kernel.  Let `T^+ <= GL_2(R)` be the upper-triangular
matrices with positive diagonal; it is homeomorphic to an open convex subset
of `R^3`.

Gram--Schmidt on the columns of `A` gives continuous maps in both
directions, `A -> (K, R)` with `A = K R`, `K in O(2)` and `R in T^+`.  The
formulas are:

- `k_1 = a_1 / |a_1|`;
- `k_2 = (a_2 - <a_2,k_1> k_1) / |a_2 - <a_2,k_1> k_1|`;
- `R = K^T A`.

They involve only square roots of positive quantities, so both directions
are continuous.  `det R > 0`, so `det K = sign det A`.  Hence

```text
Sym_2(R) x SO(2) x T^+  ->  P_S(R)_0,   (S, K, R) -> m(K R) n(S)
```

is a homeomorphism.  Consequently `P_S(R)_0` is a connected manifold, in
particular path-connected and locally path-connected.  Its fundamental group
is `Z`, generated by the loop

```text
gamma(theta) = m(R_theta) = [[R_theta, 0],[0, R_theta]],   theta in [0, 2 pi],
R_theta = [[cos theta, -sin theta],[sin theta, cos theta]],
```

using `R_theta^{-T} = R_theta`.

**Lemma 5.1.**  The inclusion `P_S(R)_0 -> G` induces the zero map on `pi_1`.

*Proof.*

1. **The unitary embedding.**  For `X, Y in M_2(R)` put
   `rho(X + iY) = [[X, -Y],[Y, X]] in M_4(R)`.  Multiplying out,
   `rho(U U') = rho(U) rho(U')`, `rho(I) = I` and `rho(U^*) = rho(U)^T`.
   So `rho` maps unitaries to orthogonal matrices.
2. **`rho(U(2)) <= G`.**  `J = rho(-i I)`, and `-iI` is central in `M_2(C)`,
   so `rho(U)` commutes with `J`.  For unitary `U`,
   `rho(U)^T J rho(U) = rho(U)^T rho(U) J = J`.
3. **The loop lies in `rho(SU(2))`.**  For real `X = R_theta` and `Y = 0`,
   `rho(R_theta) = gamma(theta)`.  `R_theta` is real orthogonal with
   determinant one, hence lies in `SU(2)`.
4. **Null-homotopy.**  `SU(2) = {[[a, -conj(b)],[b, conj(a)]] : |a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1}`
   is homeomorphic to the three-sphere, which is simply connected.  So
   `theta -> R_theta` is null-homotopic in `SU(2)` relative to the base point.
   Composing a null-homotopy with the continuous map `rho` gives a
   null-homotopy of `gamma` in `G`.  Since `gamma` generates
   `pi_1(P_S(R)_0, 1)`, the induced map is zero.  []

**Proposition 5.2.**  There is a homomorphism `sigma_S : P_S(Z) -> E_3` with
`p o sigma_S = id`.  Hence `p^{-1}(P_S(Z)) = sigma_S(P_S(Z)) x <z>`
(internal direct product), and `phi(sigma_S(g) z^j) = omega^j` is a character
with `phi(z) = omega`.

*Proof.*

1. **The connected part.**  By Lemma 5.1 and Lemma 2.2(d) there is a
   continuous homomorphic section `s : P_S(R)_0 -> G_3`.  Put
   `P_S(Z)_+ = P_S(Z) cap P_S(R)_0`, the elements with `det A = 1`.  For
   `y in P_S(Z)_+`, `p(s(y)) = y in Sp_4(Z)`, so `s(y) in E_3`.
2. **The coset representative.**  Put `epsilon = m(diag(1,-1))`, that is
   `diag(1, -1, 1, -1)` in the basis `(e_1, e_2, f_1, f_2)`.  Then
   `epsilon^2 = 1` and `det A(epsilon) = -1`.  So
   `P_S(Z) = P_S(Z)_+ x| <epsilon>`: `P_S(Z)_+` is the kernel of
   `sign det A`, and `<epsilon>` meets it trivially and maps onto `{+-1}`.
3. **A lift of order two.**  Choose `epsilon~_0 in p^{-1}(epsilon)`.  Then
   `epsilon~_0^2 in ker p`, say `epsilon~_0^2 = z^k`.  Put
   `epsilon~ = epsilon~_0 z^k`.  Since `z` is central,
   `epsilon~^2 = z^{3k} = 1`.
4. **The section.**  Define `sigma_S(y epsilon^j) = s(y) epsilon~^j` for
   `y in P_S(Z)_+` and `j in {0, 1}`.  This is well defined by the
   semidirect decomposition, and `p o sigma_S = id`.
5. **`sigma_S` is a homomorphism.**  `epsilon` normalizes `P_S(R)_0`, which
   is a kernel.  By Lemma 2.2(c) with `y = epsilon~^j`,
   `epsilon~^j s(y') epsilon~^{-j} = s(epsilon^j y' epsilon^{-j})`.  Hence

   ```text
   sigma_S(y epsilon^j) sigma_S(y' epsilon^{j'})
     = s(y) s(epsilon^j y' epsilon^{-j}) epsilon~^{j + j'}
     = s(y epsilon^j y' epsilon^{-j}) epsilon~^{(j + j') mod 2}
     = sigma_S( (y epsilon^j y' epsilon^{-j}) epsilon^{(j+j') mod 2} ),
   ```

   using `epsilon~^2 = 1`.  The argument of the last `sigma_S` is exactly the
   product `(y epsilon^j)(y' epsilon^{j'})` in normal form.
6. **The product decomposition.**  `sigma_S(P_S(Z))` meets `<z>` trivially,
   since `p o sigma_S` is injective.  `z` is central, and every
   `x in p^{-1}(P_S(Z))` equals `sigma_S(p(x)) z^j` for a unique `j mod 3`.
   So the stated `phi` is a well-defined homomorphism.  []

## 6. Theorem A(3): the Klingen parabolic

We return to the basis `e_1, f_1, e_2, f_2` of section 1.

**The flag.**  Put `W_0 = 0`, `W_1 = R e_1`, `W_2 = e_1^perp` and `W_3 = V`.
Since `b(e_1, f_1) = 1` and `b` vanishes on the other pairs with `e_1`,
`W_2 = span(e_1, e_2, f_2)`.

Let `P_K(R) = {g in G : g W_1 = W_1}` and `P_K(Z) = P_K(R) cap Sp_4(Z)`, the
stabilizer of the line `Z e_1`.  Every `g in P_K(R)` preserves the whole
flag, because `g(e_1^perp) = (g e_1)^perp`.

**The radical.**  Let

```text
N(R) = { g in G : (g - 1) W_i <= W_{i-1} for i = 1, 2, 3 }.
```

- **`N(R)` is a subgroup.**  Each `g in N(R)` preserves every `W_i`, so
  `g W_i = W_i`.  Then `gh - 1 = (g-1)(h-1) + (g-1) + (h-1)` and
  `g^{-1} - 1 = -g^{-1}(g - 1)` both map `W_i` into `W_{i-1}`.
- **`N(R)` is normal in `P_K(R)`.**  For `g in P_K(R)`,
  `g n g^{-1} - 1 = g (n - 1) g^{-1}` maps `W_i` into `W_{i-1}`.

**Parametrization.**  An element `n in N(R)` has

```text
n e_1 = e_1,  n e_2 = e_2 + x e_1,  n f_2 = f_2 + y e_1,
n f_1 = f_1 + u e_1 + v e_2 + w f_2 .
```

Preservation of `b` on the six pairs of basis vectors gives the following
conditions:

- `b(n e_2, n f_1) = x + w = b(e_2, f_1) = 0`, so `w = -x`;
- `b(n f_2, n f_1) = -v + y = b(f_2, f_1) = 0`, so `v = y`;
- the pairs `(e_1, f_1)`, `(e_1, e_2)`, `(e_1, f_2)` and `(e_2, f_2)` give no
  condition.

So `N(R) = { n(x, y, u) : x, y, u in R }`, where

```text
n(x,y,u): e_1 -> e_1,  e_2 -> e_2 + x e_1,  f_2 -> f_2 + y e_1,
          f_1 -> f_1 + u e_1 + y e_2 - x f_2 ,
```

and each such matrix is symplectic.  The map `(x,y,u) -> n(x,y,u)` is a
homeomorphism `R^3 -> N(R)`, since the coordinates are matrix entries.  So
`N(R)` is contractible, path-connected, locally path-connected and simply
connected.

The group law, computed on `f_1`, is

```text
n(x,y,u) n(x',y',u') = n(x + x', y + y', u + u' + x y' - x' y).
```

**The integral radical.**  `N(Z) = N(R) cap Sp_4(Z) = {n(x,y,u) : x,y,u in Z}`.
The substitution `u -> u + xy` changes the cocycle `x y' - x' y` into
`2 x y'`.  So `N(Z)` is isomorphic to the index-two subgroup
`{(a, b, c) : a even}` of the integral Heisenberg group with law
`(a,b,c)(a',b',c') = (a+a', b+b', c+c'+ab')`.

Its commutator subgroup is `{n(0,0,2k)}`, so its abelianization is
`Z^2 x Z/2`.  It is therefore not itself isomorphic to that Heisenberg
group; nothing below uses its isomorphism type.

**The Levi factor.**  Let

```text
L(R) = { g in G : g e_1 = lambda e_1, g f_1 = lambda^{-1} f_1 for some lambda != 0,
                  and g P_2 = P_2 }.
```

Its elements act on `P_2 = <e_2, f_2>` by `SL_2(R)`.  Put
`L(Z) = L(R) cap Sp_4(Z)`; then `lambda = +-1` and

```text
L(Z) = { +-1 on P_1 } x S_2(Z) <= S_1(Z) S_2(Z) = S.
```

**Lemma 6.1 (Levi decomposition).**  `P_K(R) = N(R) L(R)` with
`N(R) cap L(R) = 1`, and `P_K(Z) = N(Z) L(Z)`.  Hence every element of
`P_K(Z)` is uniquely `n l` with `n in N(Z)` and `l in L(Z)`.

*Proof.*

1. **The data of `g`.**  Let `g in P_K(R)`, so `g e_1 = lambda e_1` with
   `lambda != 0`.  Since `g W_2 = W_2`, write `g e_2 = a e_2 + c f_2 + p e_1`
   and `g f_2 = b' e_2 + d f_2 + q e_1`.  Put `M = [[a, b'],[c, d]]`.
   - Then `b(g e_2, g f_2) = ad - b'c`, because `e_1` pairs to zero with
     `e_1, e_2, f_2`.  This equals `b(e_2, f_2) = 1`, so `M in SL_2(R)`.
   - Write `g f_1 = mu f_1 + (element of W_2)`.  Then
     `1 = b(g e_1, g f_1) = lambda mu`.
2. **The Levi part.**  Let `l(g) in L(R)` act by `lambda` on `e_1`, by
   `lambda^{-1}` on `f_1`, and by `M` on `P_2`, in the basis `(e_2, f_2)`.
   Put `n = g l(g)^{-1}`.  Then:
   - `n e_1 = e_1`;
   - on `P_2`, `l(g)^{-1}` acts by `M^{-1}`, and `g` acts by `M` modulo `W_1`,
     so `(n - 1) P_2 <= W_1`;
   - `n f_1 = g(lambda f_1) = f_1 + lambda (element of W_2)`, so
     `(n - 1) f_1 in W_2`.

   Hence `n in N(R)`.
3. **Uniqueness.**  If `g in N(R) cap L(R)`:
   - `g e_1 = e_1` gives `lambda = 1`;
   - `(g - 1) P_2 <= W_1 cap P_2 = 0` gives `M = I`.

   So `g = 1`.
4. **Integral case.**  Let `g in P_K(Z)`.  Then `lambda in Z` and
   `g^{-1} e_1 = lambda^{-1} e_1` is integral, so `lambda = +-1`.  The entries
   `a, b', c, d` are matrix entries of `g`, so `M in SL_2(Z)`.  Hence
   `l(g) in L(Z)` and `n = g l(g)^{-1} in N(R) cap Sp_4(Z) = N(Z)`.  []

**Proposition 6.2.**  There is a homomorphism `phi_K : p^{-1}(P_K(Z)) -> T`
with `phi_K(z) = omega`.

*Proof.*

1. **The radical lifts.**  By Lemma 2.2(d), applied to the simply connected
   group `N(R)`, there is a continuous homomorphic section
   `s_N : N(R) -> G_3`.  It maps `N(Z)` into `E_3`.
2. **The Levi part carries a character.**  Put `L~ = p^{-1}(L(Z))`.  Since
   `L(Z) <= S`, `L~ <= p^{-1}(S)`.  Let `phi_L` be the restriction of the
   character `phi_S` of Proposition 4.1; then `phi_L(z) = omega`.
3. **Normal form.**  Every `x in p^{-1}(P_K(Z))` is uniquely
   `x = s_N(n) y` with `n in N(Z)` and `y in L~`.
   - *Existence.*  Write `p(x) = n l` (Lemma 6.1), and put
     `y = s_N(n)^{-1} x`.  Then `p(y) = l`.
   - *Uniqueness.*  `s_N(n) y = s_N(n') y'` gives `n p(y) = n' p(y')`.
     Lemma 6.1 then gives `n = n'`, and hence `y = y'`.
4. **Definition and homomorphism property.**  Put `phi_K(s_N(n) y) = phi_L(y)`.
   For `y in L~`, `p(y) in L(Z) <= P_K(R)` normalizes `N(R)`.  Lemma 2.2(c)
   gives `y s_N(n') y^{-1} = s_N(p(y) n' p(y)^{-1})`.  Hence

   ```text
   (s_N(n) y)(s_N(n') y') = s_N(n) s_N(p(y) n' p(y)^{-1}) y y'
                          = s_N( n p(y) n' p(y)^{-1} ) (y y').
   ```

   Here `n p(y) n' p(y)^{-1} in N(Z)`, as a product of integral elements of
   `N(R)`.  So this is the normal form, and `phi_K` of the product is
   `phi_L(y y') = phi_L(y) phi_L(y')`.
5. **The value at `z`.**  `z = s_N(1) z` with `z in L~`, so
   `phi_K(z) = phi_L(z) = omega`.  []

*Remark.*  Proposition 6.2 uses Lemma 3.2 through Proposition 4.1, and so
Lemma 3.2P.  It does not claim that the `Z/3`-extension splits over `P_K(Z)`; that would require a splitting over
`L(Z)`, which is not available (section 9).

## 7. Operator-algebra lemmas

All C*-algebras are unital, and *-homomorphisms between unital algebras are
unital unless said otherwise.  For a discrete group `Q`, `C*(Q)` is the
universal C*-algebra of unitary representations of `Q`.

**Lemma 7.1 (twisted fibres are corners).**  Let `Q~` be a discrete group
and `z in Q~` central of order three.  Put `Q = Q~/<z>` with quotient map
`q`, and choose a set-theoretic section `sigma : Q -> Q~` with `sigma(1) = 1`.
Write `sigma(g) sigma(h) = c(g,h) sigma(gh)` with `c(g,h) in <z>`.  Let `chi`
be a character of `<z>`, and put `alpha_chi = chi o c`.  Then there is a
*-isomorphism

```text
Psi : C*(Q; alpha_chi) -> p_chi C*(Q~),   v_g -> p_chi u_{sigma(g)} .
```

For `Q~ = E_3`, `chi = chi_1` gives `C*(Sp_4(Z); alpha)` and `chi = chi_2`
gives `C*(Sp_4(Z); alpha^2)`.

*Proof.*

1. **`Psi` exists.**  `p_chi` is a central projection and is the unit of the
   corner.  Put `w_g = p_chi u_{sigma(g)}`.
   - `w_g^* w_g = w_g w_g^* = p_chi`, so each `w_g` is a unitary of the
     corner.
   - `w_1 = p_chi`.
   - `w_g w_h = p_chi u_{c(g,h)} u_{sigma(gh)} = chi(c(g,h)) w_{gh}`, using
     `p_chi u_{z^m} = chi(z)^m p_chi`.

   By the universal property of `C*(Q; alpha_chi)`, `Psi` exists.
2. **An inverse candidate.**  Every `x in Q~` is uniquely
   `x = sigma(g) z^k` with `g = q(x)` and `k mod 3`.  Put
   `pi(sigma(g) z^k) = chi(z)^k v_g in C*(Q; alpha_chi)`.
   - This is well defined, since `chi(z)^3 = 1`, and `pi(1) = v_1 = 1`.
   - It is multiplicative.  Write `c(g,h) = z^m`.  Then
     `sigma(g) z^k sigma(h) z^l = sigma(gh) z^{k+l+m}`, and
     `chi(z)^{k+l} v_g v_h = chi(z)^{k+l} chi(z)^m v_{gh}`.

   So `pi` is a unitary representation of `Q~`, and it extends to a
   *-homomorphism `Pi : C*(Q~) -> C*(Q; alpha_chi)`.  Moreover
   `Pi(u_z) = chi(z) 1`, so `Pi(p_chi) = (1/3) sum_k chi(z)^{-k} chi(z)^k = 1`.
3. **Mutually inverse.**
   - `Pi(Psi(v_g)) = Pi(p_chi) Pi(u_{sigma(g)}) = v_g`.
   - The corner `p_chi C*(Q~)` is the closed span of the elements
     `p_chi u_x = chi(z)^k w_g`, where `x = sigma(g) z^k`.  On these,
     `Psi(Pi(p_chi u_x)) = chi(z)^k w_g = p_chi u_x`.

   Both composites are *-homomorphisms, which are the identity on
   generating sets.  []

**Lemma 7.2 (expectation onto a subgroup algebra).**  Let `H~ <= Q~` be
discrete groups, and let `iota : C*(H~) -> C*(Q~)` be induced by the
inclusion.  Then there is a ucp map `Phi : C*(Q~) -> C*(H~)` with the
following properties:

- `Phi(u_g) = 1_{H~}(g) u_g`;
- `Phi o iota = id`, so in particular `iota` is injective;
- `Phi(iota(a) x iota(b)) = a Phi(x) b` for all `a, b in C*(H~)` and
  `x in C*(Q~)`.

If moreover `z in H~` is central in `Q~` of order three, then `iota` and
`Phi` restrict to an injective unital *-homomorphism
`p_chi C*(H~) -> p_chi C*(Q~)` and a ucp left inverse
`p_chi C*(Q~) -> p_chi C*(H~)`.  Their composite `iota o Phi` is a
conditional expectation onto the image.

*Proof.*

1. **The induced representation.**  Let `pi : C*(H~) -> B(K)` be a faithful
   unital representation.  Let `Hc` be the space of functions
   `xi : Q~ -> K` such that:
   - `xi(h x) = pi(u_h) xi(x)` for `h in H~` and `x in Q~`;
   - `||xi||^2 = sum_{H~ x in H~\Q~} ||xi(x)||^2 < infinity`.

   Each term depends only on the coset, since `pi(u_h)` is unitary.  The
   polarized inner product is well defined for the same reason.  A choice of
   coset representatives identifies `Hc` with `l^2(H~\Q~; K)`, so `Hc` is a
   Hilbert space.
2. **The action.**  Put `(lambda(g) xi)(x) = xi(x g)`.
   - `lambda(g) xi` again satisfies the covariance condition.
   - Right multiplication by `g` permutes the right cosets, so `lambda(g)`
     is isometric.
   - `lambda(g) lambda(g') = lambda(g g')` and `lambda(1) = 1`.

   So `lambda` is a unitary representation of `Q~`.  Let
   `Lambda : C*(Q~) -> B(Hc)` be its extension.
3. **The isometry.**  Define `V : K -> Hc` by `(V k)(x) = pi(u_x) k` for
   `x in H~`, and `(V k)(x) = 0` otherwise.
   - `V k` satisfies the covariance condition, since `H~ x = H~` iff
     `x in H~`.
   - Only the coset `H~` contributes to the norm, so `||V k|| = ||k||`.
   - `<V k, xi> = <k, xi(1)>`, so `V^* xi = xi(1)`.
   - Hence `V^* lambda(g) V k = (V k)(g) = 1_{H~}(g) pi(u_g) k`.
4. **The map `Phi`.**  `Phi_0 = V^* Lambda(.) V` is ucp and satisfies
   `Phi_0(u_g) = 1_{H~}(g) pi(u_g)`.
   - It maps the span of the `u_g` into `pi(C*(H~))`.
   - By continuity it maps `C*(Q~)` into `pi(C*(H~))`, which is closed.
   - Put `Phi = pi^{-1} o Phi_0`, which is ucp.

   `Phi o iota` and `id` are continuous and agree on the `u_h`, so they are
   equal.
5. **Bimodule property.**  For `h, h' in H~` and `g in Q~`, `h g h' in H~`
   iff `g in H~`.  So both sides of the bimodule identity agree on
   `a = u_h`, `x = u_g`, `b = u_{h'}`.  Linearity and continuity give the
   general case.
6. **Corners.**  `iota(p_chi) = p_chi`, by the same formula.  By step 5,
   `Phi(p_chi x) = p_chi Phi(x)`, so `Phi` maps `p_chi C*(Q~)` into
   `p_chi C*(H~)` and `Phi(p_chi) = p_chi`.
   - `iota` maps the corner `p_chi C*(H~)` into `p_chi C*(Q~)`, unitally.
   - `Phi o iota = id` restricts.
   - `E = iota o Phi` is ucp, idempotent, has range `iota(p_chi C*(H~))`, and
     is a bimodule map over that range.  So `E` is a conditional
     expectation.  []

**Lemma 7.3 (untwisting).**  Let `H~` be a discrete group with central `z`
of order three, and put `H = H~/<z>` with quotient map `q`.  Suppose
`phi : H~ -> T` is a homomorphism with `phi(z) = chi(z)`.  Then there is a
*-isomorphism

```text
Theta : C*(H) -> p_chi C*(H~),   u_{q(x)} -> phi(x)^{-1} p_chi u_x .
```

*Proof.*

1. **`Theta` exists.**  `x -> phi(x)^{-1} p_chi u_x` is a unitary
   representation of `H~` in the corner, because `phi` is a homomorphism and
   `p_chi` is central.  At `z` it gives
   `chi(z)^{-1} p_chi u_z = p_chi`, the unit.  So it factors through `H`.
2. **An inverse.**  `x -> phi(x) u_{q(x)}` is a unitary representation of
   `H~` in `C*(H)`.  Let `Pi : C*(H~) -> C*(H)` be its extension.  Since
   `Pi(u_z) = chi(z) 1`, we get `Pi(p_chi) = 1`.
3. **Mutually inverse.**
   - `Pi(Theta(u_{q(x)})) = phi(x)^{-1} phi(x) u_{q(x)} = u_{q(x)}`.
   - `Theta(Pi(p_chi u_x)) = Theta(phi(x) u_{q(x)}) = p_chi u_x`.

   These elements generate the respective algebras.  []

**Lemma 7.4 (LLP descends along ucp left inverses).**  We use Kirchberg's
definition, in the form stated here.  A unital C*-algebra `A` has the
**LLP** if the following holds.  Let `B` be a unital C*-algebra, `I <= B` a
closed two-sided ideal with quotient map `Q_I : B -> B/I`, `T : A -> B/I` a
ucp map, and `F <= A` a finite-dimensional operator system.  Then there is a
ucp map `psi : F -> B` with `Q_I o psi = T|_F`.

The bibliographic location of this definition (Kirchberg 1993; Pisier's
monograph on tensor products) was not re-verified in this session.  The
Enders--Shulman abstract (arXiv:2403.12224v3) mentions an equivalent
characterization by *-homomorphism lifting; that equivalence is not used
here.  The target node refers to "Kirchberg's local lifting property"; if a
reader's definition differs, the needed input is the stability of LLP under
ucp left inverses, proved below for this form.

Now let `C` and `A` be unital C*-algebras, `j : C -> A` an injective unital
*-homomorphism, and `E : A -> C` a ucp map with `E o j = id_C`.  If `A` has
the LLP, then so does `C`.  The LLP is also invariant under
*-isomorphism.

*Proof.*

1. Let `T : C -> B/I` be ucp and `F <= C` a finite-dimensional operator
   system.  Then `T o E : A -> B/I` is ucp.  `j(F) <= A` is a
   finite-dimensional operator system, since `j` is a unital *-isometry.
2. By the LLP of `A` there is a ucp `psi : j(F) -> B` with
   `Q_I o psi = T o E` on `j(F)`.
3. Then `psi o j|_F : F -> B` is ucp, and
   `Q_I o psi o j = T o E o j = T` on `F`.
4. The invariant statement is the case where `j` is an isomorphism and
   `E = j^{-1}`.  []

## 9. Remarks and scope

**9.1 Theorem A(1) is not a formal consequence of cohomology.**  This
remark is not used anywhere.  Assume the standard values
`H_1(SL_2(Z); Z) = Z/12` and `H_2(SL_2(Z); Z) = 0`, which follow from the
amalgam decomposition by Mayer--Vietoris; they were not re-derived here.

- The Kuenneth formula gives `H_2(S; Z) = Z/12 (x) Z/12 = Z/12`.
- Hence `H^2(S; T) = Hom(Z/12, T) = Z/12`.

So `S` carries nontrivial `T`-valued multipliers, and triviality of
`alpha|_S` is a genuine statement.  In the proof, the factor classes die by
Lemma 3.2, and the cross term dies by Lemma 2.3 (commuting lifts).

**9.2 No `Z/3`-splitting is claimed over `SL_2(Z)`.**  Again this is not used,
and it is only sketched.

1. The inclusion `SL_2(R) -> Sp_4(R)` on a symplectic plane is an
   isomorphism on `pi_1`: up to homotopy it is `U(1) -> U(2)`, and `det`
   restricts to the identity.  So `p^{-1}(S_1(R))` is the connected triple
   cover of `SL_2(R)`.
2. If, as classically, the preimage of `SL_2(Z)` in the universal cover is
   the braid group `B_3`, with `Delta^4` generating the kernel, then
   `p^{-1}(S_1(Z)) = B_3 / <Delta^12>`.
3. The image of `sigma_1 sigma_2` has order six in `SL_2(Z)`, and every lift
   of it has sixth power `z != 1`.  So that extension would not split.

This is why Theorem A is stated with `T` coefficients, and only Corollary 4.2
and Proposition 5.2 claim splittings.

**9.3 The multiplier survives on every finite-index subgroup.**  This remark
assumes the claim `deligne-triple-cover-fd-central-invisibility`, (DTC2).
Let `Lambda <= Sp_4(Z)` have finite index `d`, and suppose some homomorphism
`phi : p^{-1}(Lambda) -> T` has `phi(z) = omega`.

1. Induce `phi` to `E_3`.  The representation space is
   `{xi : E_3 -> C : xi(h x) = phi(h) xi(x) for h in p^{-1}(Lambda)}`, with
   `(rho(g) xi)(x) = xi(x g)`.  It has dimension `d`, and `rho` is unitary
   for the coset inner product.
2. Because `z` is central,
   `(rho(z) xi)(x) = xi(x z) = xi(z x) = phi(z) xi(x) = omega xi(x)`.  So
   `rho(z) = omega 1`.
3. This contradicts (DTC2).

So every subgroup on which `alpha` untwists has infinite index, as `S`,
`P_S(Z)` and `P_K(Z)` do.  No finite-index shortcut from `(TF3)` to an
untwisted algebra exists.
