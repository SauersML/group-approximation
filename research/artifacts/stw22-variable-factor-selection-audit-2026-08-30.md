## Boundary crossed

Finite derived-set descent leaves a residual quotient at a limit
Cantor--Bendixson rank.  The new theorem avoids descent entirely for factor
fields whose trace-preserving expectations into a common ambient finite
factor vary pointwise continuously in `2`-norm.  It applies over every
compact metrizable zero-dimensional base, including scattered spaces of
arbitrary countable successor or limit height.

## Why moving fibres are different

The earlier zero-dimensional theorem placed weighted copies in fixed
orthogonal corners of one constant fibre.  Fixed corners need not belong to
a moving subfactor.  Here the tuple of orthogonal copies is selected inside
`N_x` itself.  Continuous expectations approximate an existing tuple in a
nearby fibre; polar correction handles a high spectral cut, and the strict
trace slack places the low remainder in unused range projections.  Its
weighted `2`-norm is at most the square root of the spectral threshold, so
no support continuity is required.

## Selection check

The moving-factor correction proves lower semicontinuity of the exact
solution multifunction in the common complete `L^2(R)^m` ball.
Zero-dimensional clopen refinement gives a continuous selection for any
closed base subset.  The selected limit lies in each moving factor because
the solution sets are closed; intermediate locally constant approximants
need not lie in the moving fibres.

## Limit-rank consequence

Every small spectral cut in a fibre gap has arbitrarily many orthogonal
equivalent weighted copies.  Thus its value under a bounded trace is zero.
In particular, the escaping-rank quotient which survives closure of all
successor-layer kernels cannot support a trace in a continuous-expectation
field.  This eliminates rather than merely renames the first and all later
countable limit-rank obstructions.

## Remaining risk and scope

The theorem assumes a common finite factor `R` and pointwise
`2`-continuity of `x |-> E_x(z)` for every `z in R`.  It does not prove
that arbitrary factorial tracial bundles admit such an embedding.  Without
this hypothesis, fixed-corner and moving-fibre selection can both fail, and
the general Problem XXII remains open.
