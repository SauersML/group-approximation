## Structure of the imported proof (for referees)

1. Uniform perfectness bounds every quasimorphism. A non-elliptic,
   non-horocyclic, non-general-type action fixes a point at infinity with a
   loxodromic, and so gives an unbounded quasimorphism [Man08, §4.1]. No index-2
   subgroup rules out non-oriented lineal actions (Corollary 6.5).
2. General-type actions are excluded using the canonical kernel `SK_G`. In
   the faithful case `SK_G = 1`, so the elliptic branch applies and the
   contradiction is BFFG25 Proposition 5.23 itself.

## Belk--Zaremsky, extracted passages

- Theorem A: "The group SV_G is finitely generated if and only if G is finitely
  generated and the action of G on S has finitely many orbits."
- Theorem 3.4: "The twisted Brin–Thompson group SV_G is simple. Moreover, if
  SV_G is finitely generated then it can be generated by two elements of finite
  order."
- Introduction: "There is a natural monomorphism ι_∅ : G → SV_G, corresponding
  to the canonical action of G on the Cantor space C^S."

Two torsion generators are not by themselves enough for FA. Serre's criterion
needs their product to be elliptic too. So FA really comes from NL.

## Calibration

- **Thompson's F** is a Thompson-like group that fails NL ("F-like groups do
  not [BFFZ]"). It maps onto `Z^2`, so it acts on a line without a fixed point.
  The FA argument uses exactly the NL input that F lacks.
- **Z wr C_2** is finitely generated and maps onto `Z`. It is not perfect, and
  it is not a twisted Brin--Thompson group. Uniform perfectness is the first
  step of the imported proof, and that is where it fails.
