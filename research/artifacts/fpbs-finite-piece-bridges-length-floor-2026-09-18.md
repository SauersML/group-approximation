# Finite-piece bridges pay the b-length floor

Worker swarm-0917-w10-w10-fp-follow (minimal-counterexample), September 18,
2026. Target: `fpbs-mal-bernoulli-single-stage-floor`, part (D0).

## 0. Setting and the route being killed

- `L = F(a, b)`, `c = b a b^{-2}`, `K = <a, c>` (malnormal). `L` acts freely
  and p.m.p. on `(X, μ)`, with `R_L = E_{L ↷ X}` and `R_K = E_{K ↷ X}`.
- `|h|_{K,b}` is the double-coset `b`-length, and
  `σ_b(X) = inf { μ(A) : cl(A) conull }`, as in
  `research/artifacts/fpbs-chord-unfolding-length-floor-2026-09-18.md`
  (Theorem B).
- **Bridge route.** For any subrelation `E ⊆ R_L` with `R_K ∨ E = R_L` and
  `F := E ∩ R_K`,

  ```text
  relC(R_L ; R_K)  ≤  relC(E ; F).                                   (0.1)
  ```

  Indeed, if `F ∨ Ψ = E`, then `R_K ∨ Ψ ⊇ F ∨ Ψ = E` and
  `R_K ∨ Ψ ⊇ R_K`, so `R_K ∨ Ψ = R_L`.
- `fpbs-malnormal-cosets-admit-no-amenable-bridges` kills (0.1) for amenable
  `E` with `F` aperiodic, since then `E ⊆ R_K`. Its scope note leaves open the
  **approximate bridges**: amenable `E` with `F` finite, where
  `relC(E; F)` is small but positive. This artifact treats every `E` whose
  pieces (`F`-classes) are finite.

## 1. The projection bound

Let `F ⊆ E ∩ R_K` have finite classes a.e., and let `Y` be a Borel
transversal of `F`. (`F` is finite, hence smooth, so `Y` exists.) Write
`π : X → Y` for the map sending `x` to the representative of its piece.
Since `F ⊆ R_K` and the action is free, there is a unique `κ(x) ∈ K` with
`x = κ(x) π(x)`, and `κ` is Borel.

All costs of relations on `Y` are unnormalized, taken with `μ|_Y`.

**Lemma 1.1.** `relC(E ; F) ≥ C(E|_Y)`.

*Proof.* Let `Ψ = ⊔ ψ_i|_{D_i}` be a graphing with `F ∨ Ψ = E` and finite
cost. By freeness, `ψ_i x = g x` for a unique `g ∈ L`, depending Borel on
`x`. Partition `D_i` into the countably many Borel sets

```text
D_{i,k,g,k'} = { x ∈ D_i : κ(x) = k,  ψ_i x = g x,  κ(ψ_i x) = k' }.
```

On `D_{i,k,g,k'}` the map `x ↦ π x = k^{-1} x` is injective, and the map
`π x ↦ π(ψ_i x) = k'^{-1} g k (π x)` is the restriction of the group element
`γ = k'^{-1} g k`. So `ψ'_{i,k,g,k'} = γ|_{k^{-1} D_{i,k,g,k'}}` is a
partial isomorphism of `Y`. Its domain has measure `μ(D_{i,k,g,k'})`, since
`k^{-1}` preserves `μ`. So `Ψ' = ⊔ ψ'_{i,k,g,k'}` has `C(Ψ') = C(Ψ)`.

`Ψ'` generates `E|_Y`. Let `y ~_E y'` with `y, y' ∈ Y`, and take an
`F ∨ Ψ`-path from `y` to `y'`. `F`-steps do not change `π`, and a `Ψ`-step
`x → ψ_i x` projects to the `Ψ'`-step `π x → π(ψ_i x)`. So the projected
path is a `Ψ'`-path from `y` to `y'`. Hence `C(E|_Y) ≤ C(Ψ') = C(Ψ)`. ∎

*Remark.* Conversely, `F` together with any graphing of `E|_Y` generates
`E`, so `relC(E ; F) = C(E|_Y)`. Only the inequality of Lemma 1.1 is used
below.

**Label bookkeeping.** The proof shows more. If every edge of `Ψ` has its
label in a set `S ⊆ L`, then every edge of `Ψ'` has its label in `K S K`.
Since `|k' g k|_{K,b} = |g|_{K,b}` for `k, k' ∈ K`, projecting to `Y` does not
change `b`-lengths. This is the whole point. A cheap witness `Ψ` for (0.1)
can have labels of any length, but `Ψ'` inherits its labels from `Ψ`, not
from `E`. What follows replaces `Ψ` by a treeing built from a generating
graphing `G` of `E`, whose labels are controlled.

## 2. Treeings of `E|_Y` with labels from `G`

Fix a graphing `G` of `E` (so `G` generates `E`) whose edges have labels in
`S ⊆ L`. Let `Q` be the projected graph on `Y`, which is the union over
`(k, g, k')` of the partial maps `k'^{-1} g k` built from `G` as in the
proof of Lemma 1.1. `Q` is Borel and generates `E|_Y` (the same path
projection argument), and every `Q`-edge has its label in `K S^{±1} K`.

**Lemma 2.1 (hyperfinite `E`).** If `E` is hyperfinite, then `E|_Y` has a
Borel treeing `T ⊆ Q`. In particular every edge of `T` has its label in
`K S^{±1} K`.

*Proof.* `E|_Y` is hyperfinite, so `E|_Y = ⋃_n F_n` with `F_0 = id_Y ⊆ F_1 ⊆ ⋯`
finite Borel equivalence relations. Let `E_n` be the equivalence relation
generated by `Q ∩ F_n`. It is Borel, has finite classes, and satisfies
`E_n ⊆ E_{n+1}`. Also `⋃_n E_n = E|_Y`, because a `Q`-path has finitely many
edges and each edge lies in some `F_n`.

Fix a Borel linear order `<` on `Y`. Put `T_0 = ∅`, and build `T_{n+1} ⊇ T_n`
so that `T_n ⊆ Q ∩ F_n` spans each `E_n`-class by a tree.
- An `E_{n+1}`-class `C` is finite. It is a union of finitely many
  `E_n`-classes, each spanned by `T_n`, and `Q ∩ F_{n+1}` connects them.
- Add to `T_n` the edges of `Q ∩ F_{n+1}` inside `C` chosen by Kruskal's rule:
  scan the edges in lexicographic `<`-order and keep an edge exactly when it
  joins two components not yet joined.
- The rule is canonical on each finite class, so `T_{n+1}` is Borel, and it
  spans `C` by a tree.

`T = ⋃_n T_n` is acyclic, because a cycle is finite and would lie in some
`T_n`. It spans every `E|_Y`-class, because each such class is an increasing
union of `E_n`-classes, each spanned by `T_n ⊆ T`. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (connected pieces in a treeing).** Suppose `G` is a treeing of
`E`, and each `F`-class is connected in `G`. Then `Q` itself is a treeing of
`E|_Y`.

*Proof.* A cycle `y_0, y_1, …, y_m = y_0` in `Q` with `m ≥ 1` and no
repeated edges lifts to a closed `G`-walk. Each `Q`-edge becomes the
`G`-edge that produced it, and consecutive lifted edges are joined inside
one piece by that piece's `G`-subtree. The lifted walk crosses between
pieces only at the `Q`-edges, and it uses distinct crossing edges. So it
contains a cycle of `G`, which is a contradiction. The degenerate case is two
`G`-edges between the same pair of pieces, which give the same `Q`-edge
(labels are determined by freeness). They would close a cycle of `G` through
the two piece subtrees, so this case does not occur either. ∎

## 3. Theorem C: the bridge floor

**Theorem C.** Let `L ↷ (X, μ)` be free and p.m.p. Let `E ⊆ R_L` be a
subrelation with `R_K ∨ E = R_L`, and let `F ⊆ E ∩ R_K` have finite classes.
Let `G` be a graphing generating `E` whose labels have `b`-length at most
`r`, that is `|g|_{K,b} ≤ r` for all labels `g`. Suppose that either
- (i) `E` is hyperfinite, or
- (ii) `G` is a treeing of `E` in which every `F`-class is connected.

Then

```text
r · relC(E ; F)  ≥  σ_b(X).                                           (3.1)
```

If the action is weakly contained in a Bernoulli shift of `L`, the right side
is at least `1/80`. So `relC(E; F) ≥ 1/(80 r)`, and no such bridge has `r = 0`.

*Proof.* Let `Y` be a transversal of `F`, and let `T` be the treeing of `E|_Y`
given by Lemma 2.1 in case (i) or Lemma 2.2 in case (ii). Every label of `T`
lies in `K S^{±1} K`, so it has `b`-length at most `r`.
1. **Cost.** By Lemma 1.1, `relC(E; F) ≥ C(E|_Y)`. By Gaboriau's treeing
   theorem (`gaboriau-treeable-free-action-realizes-min-cost`, item 1,
   applied on `(Y, μ|_Y)` after normalizing), `C(E|_Y) = C(T)`.
2. **Generation.** Orient each edge of `T` from its smaller endpoint in a fixed Borel linear
   order of `Y`.
   Split by label, `T = ⊔_γ γ|_{D_γ}`, so that `C(T) = Σ_γ μ(D_γ)`. If
   `x ~_E x'`, then `x F π x`, `π x ~_T π x'` and `π x' F x'`. So
   `F ∨ T ⊇ E`, and therefore `R_K ∨ T ⊇ R_K ∨ E = R_L`.
3. **Theorem B**, applied to the graphing `T` of `X`
   (`fpbs-mal-graphing-b-length-weighted-floor`), gives
   `σ_b(X) ≤ Σ_γ |γ|_{K,b} μ(D_γ) ≤ r · C(T) ≤ r · relC(E; F)`.
   The last bound for Bernoulli is the same claim's `σ_b ≥ 1/80`. ∎

**Where each member dies.** Suppose a finite-piece bridge of type (i) or
(ii) is cheap, with `relC(E; F) = ε`. Then it gives, through `T`, a
generating graphing of `R_L` over `R_K` of mass `≤ ε`, whose labels are
`K`-double translates of the labels of `G`. It dies at Step 3 of Theorem B:
the fold closure of the unfolded seed set must be conull (fold exactness),
and the seeds have mass at most `r ε`.

## 4. Corollaries

**4.1 (Cayley-subgraph bridges).** Let `E` be generated by `a`-, `c`- and
`b`-edges, `G = a|_{A_1} ⊔ c|_{A_2} ⊔ b|_{B}` for arbitrary Borel sets.
Suppose that `E` is hyperfinite, or that `G` is a treeing of `E` with
`F`-connected pieces. Then `r = 1`,
so `relC(E; F) ≥ σ_b(X) ≥ 1/80` under weak containment in Bernoulli. Here
`a, c ∈ K` have length 0 and `b` has length 1.

**4.2 (Approximate bridges need long generators).** For weakly Bernoulli
actions, a sequence of hyperfinite finite-piece bridges `E_n` with
`relC(E_n; F_n) → 0` must have

```text
inf { sup_{g ∈ lab(G)} |g|_{K,b} : G generates E_n }  ≥  1/(80 relC(E_n; F_n))  →  ∞ .
```

The treeing `T_n` is then itself a witness of exactly the shape of survivor
P2 of the chord artifact. It has mass `→ 0`, and its labels have `b`-length
`→ ∞`. So the approximate-bridge route of
`fpbs-malnormal-cosets-admit-no-amenable-bridges` (scope note) is contained
in P2 and gives no independent route to `relC = 0`.

**4.3 (Stage form for `Gamma_mal`).** Transport by `psi_2 : H_2 → L`, which
sends `a ↦ a`, `b_2 ↦ b` and `H_1 ↦ K`. With Lemma 2.2 of
`research/artifacts/fpbs-deep-promotion-reduction-2026-09-18.md`,
`beta|H_2` is a Bernoulli shift of `L`. So every finite-piece bridge
`E ⊆ E_{beta|H_2}` for `relC(E_{H_2}; E_{H_1})`, of type (i) or (ii), with
`b_2`-length at most `r` modulo `H_1`, has value `≥ σ_b/r ≥ 1/(80 r)`. This
is part (D0), the depth-0 term `Q_0 = r` of the deep-promotion reduction.

## 5. What is not covered, and the step where it would have to happen

- **Unbounded labels (P2).** Theorem C is sharp in form: with labels of
  unbounded length, (3.1) with `r = ∞` gives nothing. Every remaining
  finite-piece bridge route runs through P2.
- **Non-amenable bridges without connected pieces.** If `E` is not
  hyperfinite and no treeing of `E` has `F`-connected pieces, `E|_Y` still
  has cost `relC(E; F)`. But no treeing of it with labels in `K lab(G) K` is
  produced. The Kruskal construction needs an exhaustion by finite
  relations, and that is where the argument stops. For such `E` the cheap
  graphings of `E|_Y` are exactly as uncontrolled as the witnesses of
  `relC(R_L; R_K)` themselves. So this route is no easier than the target.
- **Mixed traces.** For amenable `E` whose trace on `R_K` is aperiodic on
  some pieces and finite on others, the two results do not glue directly.
  The saturation of the aperiodic part is `E`-invariant but not
  `R_K`-invariant.
- **Deep labels (P3, D1).** Bridges inside `E_rho` for the promotion
  `S_{j+1} → S_j` with labels outside `L_j` are not of the form (0.1) and
  are not covered.
- **No computation.** This is a proof-only artifact. Its inputs are Theorem B
  and Gaboriau's treeing theorem.
