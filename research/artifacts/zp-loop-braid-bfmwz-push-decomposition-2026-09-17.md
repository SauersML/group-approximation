## 0. Summary

1. **The right push is a pair twist, not a ring passing through a ring.** Let `u_0 = [(a,b)]` be a
   vertex of the fundamental domain Q, let `r ∉ {a, b}` be a ring, and let `T` be the stabilizer of
   the triple cable `(a, b, r)` (cable product `x_a x_b x_r`). The pair twist
   `τ_{br}` (conjugate `x_b` and `x_r` by `x_b x_r`) **centralizes T** (Lemma 2.1, a three-line
   hand check, also machine-checked).
2. **Mutual-link containment (Prop. 2.2, proved).** Consequently
   `L := T · Q_{abr} ⊂ lk(u_0) ∩ lk(τ_{br} u_0)`, and `L ≅ X_{n−2}(F ∪ {t})`, a family member of free
   count `N − 3` (r free) or `N − 2` (r forbidden). Also `τ_{br} u_0 ≠ u_0` has the same type.
   This is the exact analogue of the BFMWZ Thm 3.10 fact `lk(α) ∩ lk(α') ⊇ MA(smaller)`, obtained
   with no cutting of the 3-manifold. It answers the w8 open sub-question (★) [w8 §7] in modified
   form: the one-ring push `A_{rc}` does **not** conjugate `T` into the cable stabilizer
   (computation §6), but the pair twist does.
3. **Type exclusion (Lemma 2.3, proved).** Every vertex of `lk(u_0) ∩ lk(τ_{br} u_0)` has type
   avoiding `a, b, r`. Proof by a clasp homomorphism.
4. **Decomposition (Thm 4.2, proved).** The target follows from the w8 Morse step, the BFMWZ
   simplexwise-injective reduction (Lemma 3.9 in its erratum form), Prop. 2.2, and one purely
   combinatorial statement:

   **Push Complexity Statement CX(N).** There is a complexity on the vertices of
   `X^0 = X_n(F ∪ {v, w})` whose zero set spans a subcomplex of `lk(b_0)`, `b_0 = [(v,w)]`, and such
   that every finite subcomplex K with a vertex of positive complexity has a vertex u that some
   pair-twist push lowers, with `K ∩ lk(u)` inside the corresponding translate of L.

   This **replaces** the w8 Coning Statement CS(N) and its nerve criterion: only pairwise mutual
   links are needed, exactly as in BFMWZ Thm 3.10, and the connectivity input comes from the
   induction. CX contains no topology: it is a statement about cosets of cabling subgroups.
5. **What stays open.** CX(N) for `N ≥ 11`. §5 records the geometric heuristic (Hatcher flow on
   cabling annuli against the annulus of `b_0`), the abelian proxy (clasp numbers change by ±1 under
   the pushes), and why the proxy alone does not suffice.

## 1. Setting and conventions

- `P = PLB_n = PΣAut_n` acts on `X_n` with strict fundamental domain `Q ≅ OM(K_n)` and type
  retraction t [model §2, w8 (F1)]. `A_ij : x_i ↦ x_j x_i x_j^{-1}`.
- `X_n(F) = t^{-1}(OM(K_{[n]∖F}))`, free count `N = n − |F|` [w8 §0]. `c(N)` is the infimum of the
  connectivity of `X_n(F)` over all n, F with free count N [w8 §4].
- **Cabling subgroups.** For an ordered pair `u = (a, b)`, the stabilizer of the Q-vertex
  `u_0 = [(a,b)]` is `P_u = s κ_k(PLB_{n−1}) s^{-1}` [model §2(c)], with s the permutation moving
  `k, k+1` to `a, b`. On the McCool generators of `PLB_{n−1}` the cabling map is (cabling.py,
  2026-09-16 experiment, check (4); relabelled by s):
  - `A_ij ↦ A_ij` for `i, j ∉ {a, b}`;
  - `A_{kf} ↦ A_{kb} ∘ A_{ka}`: `x_k` conjugated by the cable product `x_a x_b`;
  - `A_{fq} ↦ A_{aq} ∘ A_{bq}`: `x_a` and `x_b` both conjugated by `x_q` (no twist).

  So `P_u` is generated by these three families. For a Q-simplex σ, `P_σ` contains every `A_ij` with
  `i, j` outside the rings of σ.
- **Iterated cabling.** For an ordered triple `(c_1, c_2, c_3)`, let `T(c_1, c_2, c_3)` be the
  subgroup generated by `A_ij` (`i, j ∉ {c_1, c_2, c_3}`), by `x_k ↦ P x_k P^{-1}` with
  `P = x_{c_1} x_{c_2} x_{c_3}`, and by `x_{c_s} ↦ x_q x_{c_s} x_q^{-1}` (`s = 1, 2, 3` together).
  With `f` the ring fused from `u = (a,b)` at level `n − 1` and `e ∈ {(f, r), (r, f)}` a level-`(n−1)`
  pair:
  - `κ_u(P'_e) = T(a, b, r)` for `e = (f, r)`, and `= T(r, a, b)` for `e = (r, f)`,

  where `P'_e ⊂ PLB_{n−1}` is the cabling subgroup of e. This is the cabling formula above applied
  to the generators of `P'_e`, for example `κ_u(A_{kr} ∘ A_{kf}) = A_{kr} ∘ A_{kb} ∘ A_{ka}`.
- **Pair twists.** For distinct rings `c, d`, `τ_{cd}` conjugates `x_c` and `x_d` by `x_c x_d` and
  fixes the other generators. Thus `τ_{cd} = A_{dc} ∘ A_{cd} ∈ P`, and `τ_{cd}` fixes the word
  `x_c x_d`. The cable twist of [model §4] is `τ_{ab}`.
- **The clasp homomorphism** [model §3]. `e_ij(φ)` is the exponent sum of `x_j` in a conjugator
  of `φ(x_i)`; `e: P → Z^{n(n−1)}` is a homomorphism. For ordered pairs `u = (a,b)` and
  `z = (c,d)` with `{a,b} ∩ {c,d} = ∅`, put
  `χ_{u,z} := e_ca − e_da − e_cb + e_db`.

## 2. The pair-twist push

Fix `u_0 = [(a,b)] ∈ Q` with `a, b ∉ F`, and a ring `r ∉ {a, b}`. Put
- `(T, g) := (T(a,b,r), τ_{br})` for the order `e = (f, r)`;
- `(T, g) := (T(r,a,b), τ_{ra})` for the order `e = (r, f)`.

In both cases g is the pair twist of the two rings that are adjacent in the triple product and not
both in u.

**Lemma 2.1 (the push centralizes the triple-cable subgroup).** `g` commutes with every element of
T.

*Proof.* Take `e = (f, r)`, so `g = τ_{br}` and `P = x_a x_b x_r`. Note that `g(P) = P`, because g
fixes `x_a` and fixes the word `x_b x_r`. Check the three generator families of T:
- `A_ij`, `i, j ∉ {a, b, r}`. The supports are disjoint: `A_ij` moves only `x_i`, and fixes
  `x_b, x_r`; g moves only `x_b, x_r`, and fixes `x_i, x_j`. So they commute.
- `θ_k : x_k ↦ P x_k P^{-1}`. Both `g θ_k` and `θ_k g` send `x_k ↦ g(P) x_k g(P)^{-1} = P x_k P^{-1}`
  and agree with g on `x_b, x_r` (θ_k fixes `x_b, x_r`, and g fixes `x_k`).
- `ζ_q : x_s ↦ x_q x_s x_q^{-1}` for `s ∈ {a, b, r}`. On `x_a` both composites give
  `x_q x_a x_q^{-1}`. On `x_s`, `s ∈ {b, r}`:
  `g ζ_q (x_s) = x_q (x_b x_r) x_s (x_b x_r)^{-1} x_q^{-1}`, and
  `ζ_q g (x_s) = ζ_q(x_b x_r) ζ_q(x_s) ζ_q(x_b x_r)^{-1}`, which is the same word, since
  `ζ_q(x_b x_r) = x_q x_b x_r x_q^{-1}`. Both composites fix every other generator.

The order `(r, f)` is the same computation with `P = x_r x_a x_b` and `g = τ_{ra}` fixing `x_r x_a`
and `x_b`. ∎

**Lemma 2.1′ (the push moves the vertex).** `g ∉ P_u`, so `g u_0 ≠ u_0`, while `t(g u_0) = (a,b)`.

*Proof.* Every element of `P_u` sends `x_a, x_b` to `U x_a U^{-1}, U x_b U^{-1}` with **one** U.
This holds on the generators and is preserved under products. For `g = τ_{br}` we have
`g(x_a) = x_a`, which forces `U ∈ ⟨x_a⟩`. We also have `g(x_b) = (x_b x_r) x_b (x_b x_r)^{-1}`, which
forces `U ∈ x_b x_r ⟨x_b⟩`. These two cosets are disjoint in `F_n`. For `g = τ_{ra}` the argument is
symmetric. Types are P-invariant. ∎

Let `Q_{abr}` be the subcomplex of Q on the simplices whose rings avoid `a, b, r` and F.

**Proposition 2.2 (mutual-link containment).** Put `L := T · Q_{abr} ⊂ X_n(F)`.
1. `L ⊆ lk(u_0) ∩ lk(g u_0)`.
2. `L ≅ X_{n−2}(F' ∪ {t})` as simplicial complexes, where t is the fused triple.

   Its free count is `N − 3` if r is free, and `N − 2` if `r ∈ F`.

*Proof.*
1. Let `p ∈ T` and `σ ∈ Q_{abr}`.
   - **In `lk(u_0)`.** `σ ∪ {u_0}` is a Q-simplex, since the rings are disjoint. Also `p ∈ T ⊆ P_u`,
     because T is a κ_u-image. So `p(σ ∪ {u_0}) = pσ ∪ {u_0}` is a simplex, and `pσ ∈ lk(u_0)`.
   - **In `lk(g u_0)`.** `g ∈ P_σ`, since g is a product of `A_ij` with `i, j ∈ {a, b, r}`, outside
     the rings of σ. By Lemma 2.1, `g^{-1} p σ = p g^{-1} σ = pσ`. Hence
     `pσ = g·(pσ) ∈ g·lk(u_0) = lk(g u_0)`.
2. By the Link Lemma [w8 Lemma 2.1], `lk_{X_n(F)}(u_0) = P_u · lk_Q(u_0)`. The isomorphism with
   `X_{n−1}(F' ∪ {f})`, the full subcomplex of `X_{n−1}(F')` on types avoiding f, is
   κ_u-equivariant and relabels types by λ_u.
   - Under this isomorphism, `L = κ_u(P'_e) · Q_{abr}` corresponds to
     `P'_e · (Q_{n−1}-simplices avoiding f, r, F')`.
   - That set is `lk_{X_{n−1}(F')}(e)`, by the Link Lemma at level `n − 1` for the vertex e
     (f is free in `X_{n−1}(F')`).
   - It lies inside `X_{n−1}(F' ∪ {f})`, because its types avoid f.
   - Applying the Link Lemma once more, it is `≅ X_{n−2}(F'' ∪ {t})`.

   The free count is `(n − 2) − |F| − 1 = N − 3` when r is free. When `r ∈ F`, one forbidden ring is
   absorbed into t, and the free count is `N − 2`. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (type exclusion).** Let y be a vertex of `lk(u_0) ∩ lk(g u_0)`. Then `t(y)` avoids
`a, b, r`.

*Proof.* Adjacent vertices have disjoint types, so `t(y)` avoids `a, b`.

Suppose `t(y) = z` contains r, say `z = (r, c)` or `(c, r)`. Write `y = s[z] = g s'[z]` with
`s, s' ∈ P_u`, using the Link Lemma for `u_0` and for `g u_0 = g·u_0`. Then `s^{-1} g s' ∈ P_z`.

The homomorphism `χ := χ_{u,z}` vanishes on `P_u` and on `P_z`. Check it on generators; the value of
e on a generator is the sum of the listed unit vectors.
- `P_u`:
  - `A_ij`, `j ∉ {a,b}`: every term of χ has second index in `{a,b}`, so the value is 0.
  - `x_k` through the cable (`e_ka + e_kb`): for `k = c` the value is `1 − 1 = 0`, for `k = d` it is
    `−1 + 1 = 0`, and it is 0 otherwise.
  - The cable through q (`e_aq + e_bq`): the first index lies in `{a, b}`, but every term of χ has
    first index in `{c, d}`, so the value is 0.
- `P_z`:
  - `A_ij`, `i ∉ {c,d}`: the value is 0.
  - `x_k` through z (`e_kc + e_kd`, `k ∉ {c,d}`): the value is 0.
  - z through q (`e_cq + e_dq`): for `q = a` the value is `1 − 1 = 0`, for `q = b` it is `−1 + 1 = 0`,
    and it is 0 otherwise.

So `χ(g) = χ(s) + χ(s^{-1} g s') − χ(s') = 0`.

But `χ(g) = ±1`. For `g = τ_{br}`, the conjugator of `x_r` is `x_b`, so `e_rb(g) = 1`, and every
other entry appearing in χ is 0. For `g = τ_{ra}`, `e_ra(g) = 1`. This is a contradiction. ∎

**Remarks.**
1. Whether `lk(u_0) ∩ lk(g u_0) = L` exactly is not decided. By Lemma 2.3 it would amount to
   `P_u ∩ g P_u P_σ = T P_σ` for `σ ∈ Q_{abr}`. The decomposition below needs only the containment
   2.2(1).
2. **The one-ring push fails at the stabilizer level.** The w8 artifact (§7) proposed pushes
   `b' = A_{vc}·b`. For `g ∈ {A_ra^{±1}, A_rb^{±1}}`, the conjugate `g^{-1} T g` is **not**
   contained in `P_u`. The generators `x_k ↦ P x_k P^{-1}` fail a necessary condition for
   membership in `P_u`: the conjugator of `x_r` is not in `⟨x_a x_b, x_i⟩` (`mutual_link.py`,
   check (3)). So the mechanism of Prop. 2.2 is unavailable for one-ring pushes. This is not a
   proof that `L ⊄ lk(u_0) ∩ lk(A_ra u_0)`, which would need `g^{-1} T g ⊄ P_u P_σ`.
3. **Geometric reading.** A vertex is a framed band joining two rings [model §3]. `τ_{br}` is the
   full twist of rings b and r about each other. It drags the band of `(a, b)` once around r, which
   is the loop analogue of Figure 15 of BFMWZ: α and α' bound a disk containing only w. The
   centralizer statement says that anything bundled together with the triple `(a, b, r)` does not
   see this drag. That is why no cutting of the 3-manifold (the step (★) of [w8 §7]) is needed.

## 3. The simplexwise-injective reduction applies

We import BFMWZ Lemma 3.9 in its corrected form (the erratum to Bux–Fluch–Marschler–Witzel–Zaremsky,
"The braided Ptolemy–Thompson group is of type F_∞", quoted verbatim):

> "Let Y be a compact m-dimensional combinatorial manifold. Let X be a simplicial complex and assume
> that the link of every k-simplex in X is (m−k−2)-connected. Let ψ : Y → X be a simplicial map
> whose restriction to ∂Y is simplexwise injective. Then after possibly subdividing the simplicial
> structure of Y, ψ is homotopic relative ∂Y to a simplexwise injective map."

Put `η(M) := ⌊(M − 5)/3⌋`. "(−1)-connected" means nonempty, and "(≤ −2)-connected" is vacuous.

**Lemma 3.1 (hypothesis check).** Let `N ≥ 11`. Assume that `X_{n'}(F')` is `η(M)`-connected for
every family member of free count `M < N` (the induction hypothesis of §4; for `M ≤ 10` this is the
established base: connected for `M ≥ 5` [w8 Lemma 3.1], simply connected for `M ≥ 8`
[w8 Lemma 3.2, which uses `ordered-matching-complex-kn-simply-connected-n-ge-8`]). Let `m ≤ η(N)`. Then the link
of every k-simplex of `X^0 = X_n(F ∪ {v, w})` is `(m − k − 2)`-connected, and so is the link of
every k-simplex of `X_n(F)`.

*Proof.* A k-simplex σ of `X^0` uses `2(k+1)` rings, all among the `N − 2` free rings of `X^0`. By the
Link Lemma [w8 Lemma 2.1], applied k+1 times, `lk_{X^0}(σ)` is a family member of free count
`M = N − 2k − 4 ≥ 0`.
- **The inequality.** For integers x and `d ≥ 0` we have `⌊x/3⌋ − ⌊(x−d)/3⌋ ≤ ⌈d/3⌉`. With
  `d = 2k + 4` and `⌈(2k+4)/3⌉ ≤ k + 2`, this gives `η(M) ≥ η(N) − k − 2 ≥ m − k − 2`.
- **Every free count is covered.**
  - `M ≥ 11`: this is the induction hypothesis.
  - `5 ≤ M ≤ 10`: `η(M) ∈ {0, 1}`, and the base gives connected, resp. simply connected.
  - `2 ≤ M ≤ 4`: `η(M) = −1`, and the link is nonempty, since two free rings give a vertex.
  - `M ∈ {0, 1}`: `η(M) = −2`, and the condition is vacuous.

  In every case the link is `η(M)`-connected, hence `(m−k−2)`-connected.

For `X_n(F)` the free count of the link is `N − 2k − 2 ≥ N − 2k − 4`, and the same estimate
applies. In both cases `M ≤ N − 2`, so only the induction hypothesis (never the statement being
proved) is used. ∎

So every simplicial `ψ : S^m → X^0` with `m ≤ η(N)` is homotopic in `X^0`, after subdivision, to a
simplexwise injective map. Here `∂Y = ∅`.

## 4. The push-complexity decomposition

Fix n, F with free count N, distinct free rings v, w, and put `X^0 := X_n(F ∪ {v, w})` and
`b_0 := [(v, w)] ∈ Q ⊂ X_n(F)`. For a vertex `u = p·[(a,b)]` of `X^0` (so `a, b ∉ F ∪ {v, w}`), a ring
`r ∉ {a, b}` and an order `e ∈ {(f, r), (r, f)}`, write `g_e ∈ {τ_{br}, τ_{ra}}` and `L_e` for the
push and the complex L of §2 (built from `u_0 = [(a,b)]`). The **push of u along (r, e, p)** is the
vertex `p g_e p^{-1} u = p g_e u_0`.

- By Prop. 2.2(1), translated by p: `pL_e ⊆ lk(u) ∩ lk(p g_e p^{-1} u)`.
- By Lemma 2.1′: the push is a different vertex of the same type, so it again lies in `X^0`.
- The push depends on p, not only on u: another choice `p s` (`s ∈ P_u`) gives `p s g_e u_0`.
  CX below lets us pick p.

### 4.1 The statement

**Push Complexity Statement CX(N).** For all n, F with free count N and all distinct free v, w,
there is a function `c : V(X^0) → Z_{≥0}` such that:
- **(CX0)** the full subcomplex of `X^0` spanned by `c^{-1}(0)` is contained in `lk_{X_n(F)}(b_0)`;
- **(CX1)** for every finite subcomplex `K ⊆ X^0` having a vertex with `c > 0`, there are a vertex
  u of K with `c(u) > 0`, a presentation `u = p[(a,b)]`, a ring `r ∉ {a, b}` and an order e with
  - `c(p g_e p^{-1} u) < c(u)`, and
  - `K ∩ lk(u) ⊆ p L_e`.

Equivalently, (CX1) asks for a vertex u of K that has a lower push and whose K-neighbours all lie
in the translate `pL_e`. By the Link Lemma, `p L_e` corresponds under `lk(u) ≅ X_{n−1}(F' ∪ {f})`
to the link of the single level-`(n−1)` vertex `p̄·e` (e of type `(f, r)` or `(r, f)`). So (CX1) says:
*all K-neighbours of u lie in the link of one vertex of type `(f, r)` or `(r, f)` in the link picture*.

CX involves no topology. Membership in `p L_e` and adjacency are statements about cosets of the
cabling subgroups, `pT P_σ` versus `P_u P_σ`.

### 4.2 The theorem

**Theorem 4.2.** Assume CX(N) for every `N ≥ 11`. Then `X_n(F)` is `η(N)`-connected for every n and
every F of free count `N ≥ 5`. In particular `conn L_n(LB_*) ≥ ⌊(n − 5)/3⌋ → ∞`.

*Proof.* Induct on N. For `N ≤ 10` this is the base [w8 Lemmas 3.1, 3.2]. Let `N ≥ 11`, assume the
statement for all free counts `< N`, and fix n, F, v, w.

**Step 1 (reduction to PL).** By induction, `c(N−2) ≥ η(N−2) ≥ η(N) − 1` and
`c(N−3) ≥ η(N−3) = η(N) − 1`. By the Morse step [w8 (4.1)], `(X_n(F), X^0)` is `η(N)`-connected, so
`π_j(X^0) → π_j(X_n(F))` is onto for `j ≤ η(N)`. It therefore suffices to show that every map
`S^m → X^0`, `m ≤ η(N)`, is null-homotopic **in `X_n(F)`**.

**Step 2 (simplexwise injective).** By simplicial approximation, take `ψ : Y → X^0` simplicial, Y a
triangulated m-sphere (a closed combinatorial manifold). By Lemma 3.1 and BFMWZ Lemma 3.9 (erratum
form, `∂Y = ∅`), after subdividing Y we may assume ψ is simplexwise injective. For a vertex x of Y,
simplexwise injectivity gives `ψ(lk_Y x) ⊆ lk_{X^0}(ψ(x))`.

**Step 3 (the push).** Suppose `K := ψ(Y)` has a vertex with `c > 0`. Take u, p, r, e from (CX1),
and put `u' := p g_e p^{-1} u`. Let x be any vertex of Y with `ψ(x) = u`.
- `ψ(lk_Y x) ⊆ K ∩ lk(u) ⊆ p L_e`.
- `p L_e ≅ X_{n−2}(F'' ∪ {t})` has free count `N − 3` or `N − 2` (Prop. 2.2(2)), hence it is
  `η(N−3) = (η(N) − 1)`-connected by induction, and in particular `(m − 1)`-connected.
- Since `p L_e ⊆ lk(u) ∩ lk(u')`, and `u, u'` are not adjacent (same type), the join
  `{u, u'} * p L_e` is a subcomplex of `X_n(F)`, the suspension of `p L_e`. It is m-connected.
- Define `ψ'` by `ψ'(x) := u'` and `ψ' := ψ` on the other vertices. It is simplicial, because
  `ψ(lk x) ⊆ lk(u')`.
- ψ and ψ' agree off the open star of x. On `St(x) = x * lk(x)` they are the two cones on
  `ψ|_{lk x}` with apexes u and u'. Together they form a map `x * lk x ∪ x' * lk x ≅ S^m` into the
  m-connected complex `{u, u'} * p L_e`. So `ψ ≃ ψ'` rel the complement of the open star, in
  `X_n(F)`.
- `ψ'` still lands in `X^0` (the type of u' is `(a,b)`), and it is still simplexwise injective:
  the neighbours of x map into `p L_e ⊆ lk(u')`, which does not contain u'.

**Step 4 (termination).** Each push lowers `Σ_{x ∈ V(Y)} c(ψ(x))` by `c(u) − c(u') > 0`. After
finitely many pushes, every vertex of `K = ψ(Y)` has `c = 0`. Then K lies in the full subcomplex on
`c^{-1}(0)`, hence in `lk(b_0)` by (CX0). So ψ lands in `lk(b_0)` and is null-homotopic in the cone
`St(b_0) ⊆ X_n(F)`. Here `b_0` is a vertex of `X_n(F)` because v and w are free.

Hence `π_j(X^0) → π_j(X_n(F))` is zero for `j ≤ η(N)`, and with Step 1, `π_j(X_n(F)) = 0` for
`j ≤ η(N)`. ∎

**Remarks.**
1. **Only the key degree needs CX.** As in [w8 §4.4], for `m ≤ η(N − 2)` the conclusion already
   follows from the induction. So CX(N) is needed only at `m = η(N)` when `N ≡ 0, 2 (mod 3)`. The
   proof uses CX only for images of such spheres, though CX as stated quantifies over all finite K.
2. **What replaces the nerve.** The w8 route (CS plus the nerve criterion of [w8 §5]) needed
   connectivity of the multiple intersections `lk(b_0) ∩ … ∩ lk(b_p)`. Theorem 4.2 needs only the
   pairwise mutual link of one vertex and its push. That is exactly the shape of BFMWZ Thm 3.10,
   and the connectivity input comes from the induction, via the explicit copy `pL_e` of a smaller
   family member.
3. **The connectivity budget.** `pL_e` has free count `N − 3`, the same as the worst descending link
   of the Morse step [w8 (4.1)]. So the rate `η(N) = ⌊(N−5)/3⌋` is exactly what the push can carry.
   A push whose mutual link had free count `N − 4` would lower the rate.

## 5. Status of CX(N) (open)

### 5.1 The clasp proxy: what it gives

Fix `z = (v, w)` and `b_0 = [z]`. For a vertex `u = p u_0` with `t(u) = (a,b)`, put
`χ(u) := χ_{(a,b),z}(p)`.
- **Well defined.** χ vanishes on `P_{b_0} = P_z` and on `P_u` (the computation in Lemma 2.3), so
  `χ(s p s') = χ(p)` for `s ∈ P_z`, `s' ∈ P_u`. Hence χ is a function on vertices, relative to the
  base vertex `b_0`.
- **Obstruction.** If `u ∈ lk(b_0)`, then `u = s u_0` with `s ∈ P_z` (Link Lemma), so `χ(u) = 0`.
  Hence `χ(u) ≠ 0` forces `u ∉ lk(b_0)`.
- **Pushes move χ by one.** For `r ∈ {v, w}`, `χ(p g_e u_0) = χ(p) + χ(g_e)`, with
  `χ(g_e) = ±1` (the value computed in Lemma 2.3, checked in `mutual_link.py` check (1)). The sign
  depends on r and e. So whenever `χ(u) ≠ 0`, one of the four pushes `(r, e)`, `r ∈ {v, w}`, lowers
  `|χ|` for every presentation p.

So `c := |χ|` satisfies the "lower push" half of (CX1) with no condition on K.

### 5.2 Why the proxy does not suffice

1. **(CX0) fails for |χ|.** `χ = 0` does not imply adjacency to `b_0`. The model artifact's
   Whitehead-clasp example [model §3] has all linking-type invariants zero, and so do commutators
   in `[P, P]`, which leave e unchanged. So the zero set of `|χ|` is much larger than
   `lk(b_0) ∩ X^0`.
2. **The K-condition of (CX1) is not addressed.** For a given K, the vertex u where `|χ|` is
   maximal need not have all its K-neighbours in `pL_e`. The neighbours can use the rings
   `v, w` in their conjugators, i.e. clasp through the triple.

A working complexity must refine χ to a non-abelian invariant: an intersection pattern, not an
intersection count.

### 5.3 The geometric heuristic (R1 of [model §5])

Read a vertex as a framed band joining two rings [model §3], and `b_0` as a band `B_0` from v to
w. Put `c(u) :=` the minimal number of transverse intersection arcs of the band (or cabling annulus)
of u with `B_0`.
- If `c(u) = 0` and the band of u is disjoint from `B_0`, one expects u to be adjacent to `b_0`.
  That is (CX0), and it is exactly the statement that disjoint bands whose fusion is an unlink span
  a simplex. This "realization" statement is not proved for `L_n(LB_*)`.
- Given K, take the intersection arc on `B_0` closest to w, over all vertices of K (the BFMWZ
  choice in Thm 3.10). Let u be the vertex it belongs to, with rings (a,b). No band of K meets
  `B_0` between this arc and w. Pushing the band of u across w is the pair twist `τ_{bw}` or
  `τ_{wa}` in a suitable presentation p (Remark 3 after Lemma 2.3). The K-neighbours of u are disjoint from
  u's band and from the part of `B_0` beyond the arc, so they should "see the triple (a, b, w) as
  one ring". That is `K ∩ lk(u) ⊆ pL_e`.

The two unproved steps are:
- (G1) realization: disjoint bands whose fusion is an unlink form a simplex, and conversely;
- (G2) "a band disjoint from a region containing the triple can be isotoped into the triple-cable
  picture", i.e. its stabilizer data lies in `pT P_σ`.

(G2) is where the 3-dimensional clasping of [model §3] enters. In BFMWZ the analogue is automatic,
because in a surface an arc disjoint from the bigon region is disjoint from the pushed arc.

### 5.4 Comparison with the w8 Coning Statement

- CS(N) [w8 §4.3] asks for connectivity of the space Y obtained by coning off all `lk(b)`, b of type
  `{v, w}`, and by the w8 nerve criterion reduces to multiple intersections
  `lk(b_0) ∩ … ∩ lk(b_p)`.
- CX(N) asks for **one** base vertex `b_0` and pairwise pushes. Every push comes with an explicit,
  already controlled, mutual link `pL_e` (Prop. 2.2). CX implies PL directly (Thm 4.2) and does not
  pass through Y.
- Neither is known to imply the other. CX is the natural next target because its topological
  inputs are all proved, and what remains is combinatorics of cosets (or, geometrically, (G1) and
  (G2)).
- Small instances: CX(N) is only needed on images of spheres of dimension `η(N)` at the key
  degrees, first at `N = 11, 12` with `m = 2` (§4.2, Remark 1).

## 6. Other routes checked

1. **Hatcher–Wahl condition (3′).** Routing through the Hatcher–Wahl labeled-join theorem
   (arXiv:0709.2173v4, Def. 3.2 and Thm 3.6) needs their condition (3′) for the projection
   `X_n → OM(K_n)`. That condition fails for the same reason the complete-join description fails
   [model §3]: two lifts of a base edge need not span a simplex. So this is the recorded
   complete-join dead end, not a new route. It is not pursued.
2. **One-ring pushes (the w8 sub-question (★)).** Answered in the negative at the stabilizer level
   (Remark 2 of §2, `mutual_link.py` check (3)): for `g = A_{ra}^{±1}, A_{rb}^{±1}` the conjugate
   `g^{-1} T g` fails a necessary condition for membership in `P_u`. The pair twist replaces them.

### Computation

`experiments/loop-braid-bfmwz-push-2026-09-17/mutual_link.py` (output
`mutual_link_output.txt` in the same directory; ends with `ALL CHECKS PASSED`). For `n = 5, 6, 7`
and every ordered triple `(a, b, r)` of distinct rings in `{1, …, 4}`:
- (0) the necessary-condition test for `P_u` accepts the generators of `P_u` and random products,
  and rejects `τ_{ab}`, `A_{ra}`, `A_{rb}`, `τ_{br}`, `τ_{ra}`;
- (1) the clasp homomorphisms `χ_{u,z}`, `z = (r, c), (c, r)`, vanish on `P_u` and `P_z` and are
  nonzero on the pushes;
- (2) `τ_{br}` centralizes `T(a, b, r)` and `τ_{ra}` centralizes `T(r, a, b)`; the generators of T
  pass the `P_u` test; the push does not; the conjugates `g^{-1} T g` pass;
- (3) the one-ring pushes fail (2) on the `x_k`-through-cable generators.

The test in (0) is only a necessary condition for membership in `P_u` (common conjugator of
`x_a, x_b` in `⟨x_a x_b, x_i⟩`, conjugators of the other rings there, and `e_ab = e_ba = 0`). Its
use is as a certificate of **non**-membership; the positive facts (Lemma 2.1, Prop. 2.2) are proved
by hand.
