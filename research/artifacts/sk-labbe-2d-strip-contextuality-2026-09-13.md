# Narrow strips must be contextual: a directional strip theorem for quantum tilings of Z^2 SFTs

Lane `sk-labbe-2d`, 2026-09-13.
- Status: Theorem A and Corollaries B–D are ESTABLISHED but unreviewed; review is queued with sk-verify-7.

## 0. Results

**Setting.**
- `Ω ⊆ A^(Z^2)` is an SFT with forbidden patterns in `B_r` (sup norm).
- A *radius-`r` family* on a vector space `W` over any field consists of idempotents `E_a(z)` (`a ∈ A`, `z ∈ Z^2`) with:
  - (Q1) for each `z`, the `E_a(z)` are orthogonal idempotents with sum `1`;
  - (Q3_r) for every `z` and every `π ∉ L_(B_r)(Ω)`, `prod_(h ∈ B_r) E_(π(h))(z+h) = 0`, whenever these factors commute.
- A scale-`D` family (`D >= r`; axioms (Q1)–(Q3) of `sft-crossed-product-fp-iff-quantum-rigid`) is a radius-`r` family (Lemma 1).

**Directions.**
- Take `e = (p,q) ∈ Z^2` primitive, `ℓ_e(z) = q z_1 − p z_2` and `m_e = r(|p| + |q|)`.
- An *`e`-strip* is `{z : c <= ℓ_e(z) <= c + 2m_e}`.
- A family is *`Le`-periodic* if `E_a(z + Le) = E_a(z)` for all `a` and `z`.

**Theorem A (directional strips).** Let `(E_a(z))` be a nonzero radius-`r` family that is `Le`-periodic for some `L >= 1`.
1. Suppose `E_x(z)` and `E_y(z')` commute whenever `|ℓ_e(z) − ℓ_e(z')| <= 2m_e`. Then `Ω` has a point with period `Le`, and so a doubly periodic point (§4).
2. Suppose `Ω` has no periodic point. Put `N_e(L) = |A|^(2 m_e L) + 2m_e`. Every run of `N_e(L)` consecutive `ℓ_e`-levels contains sites `z, z'` on levels of the run, with `|ℓ_e(z) − ℓ_e(z')| <= 2m_e`, whose idempotents do not commute.
3. **Converse.** A point `x ∈ Ω` with period `Le` gives the classical one-dimensional family `E_a(z) = [x(z) = a]`, which satisfies the hypothesis of 1.

**Corollary B (thin cylinders).** Let `D >= r`, `L <= 4D+1`, and let the family be a `(0,L)`-periodic scale-`D` family. For `e = (0,1)` it satisfies the hypothesis of A.1. So `Ω` has a point with period `(0,L)`. This is the (⇒) direction of `thin-cylinder-sft-tiling-families-force-periodic-points`, as the special case of a statement with no bound on `L`.

**Corollary C (contextuality in every direction).** Let `Ω` be an aperiodic SFT, `D >= r`, `Λ ≤ Z^2` of finite index, and `V != 0` a module of `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω,k)` (any field, any dimension), or any nonzero `Λ`-periodic scale-`D` family. For every primitive `e` there are:
- sites `t, t'` of `Z^2/Λ` and letters `a, b` with `e_a(t) e_b(t') != e_b(t') e_a(t)`;
- representatives `z ∈ t`, `z' ∈ t'` with `|ℓ_e(z) − ℓ_e(z')| <= 2m_e`;
- and no representative of `t' − t` in `B_(2D)`.

So contextual pairs occur inside narrow strips along every rational direction, and each pair is separated by more than `2D` along its strip.

**Corollary D (covariant families over finite fields).** Let `Ω` be aperiodic and let the family be covariant over a finite field (commuting translations `U_1, U_2` with `E_a(z) = U^z E_a(0) U^(-z)`). Then for every primitive `e` and every `c ∈ Z`, the algebra generated by the `E_a(z)` with `c <= ℓ_e(z) <= c + 2m_e` is noncommutative.

**Labbé's `Ω_U`** (`r = 1`, 19 tiles, free; `minimal-aperiodic-wang-shift-exists`). Consider any witness for row 7 at any scale `D`: a nonzero module of some `𝒯_(D,Λ)(Ω_U, F_q)`, or a covariant family over `F_q`.
- It has noncommuting site idempotents inside a vertical strip of width `3`, at sup-distance `> 2D`.
- The same holds for horizontal strips of width `3`, for diagonal strips `{c <= z_1 − z_2 <= c+4}`, and so on for every rational direction.
- Covariant witnesses have this in every such strip.
- For vertical strips on a torus of vertical period `L`, the explicit run length is `N = 19^(2L) + 2`.

## 1. Lemma 1 (descent to radius r)

Let `D >= r`, and let `(E_a(z))` satisfy (Q1)–(Q3) at scale `D`. Then (Q3_r) holds.

*Proof.*
- Take `π ∉ L_(B_r)(Ω)`. Every extension `π'` of `π` to `B_D` lies outside `L_(B_D)(Ω)`, since a sub-pattern of a legal pattern is legal.
- The factors `E_(π'(h))(z+h)`, `h ∈ B_D`, commute by (Q2), because `B_D` has sup-diameter `2D`.
- Insert (Q1) at the sites of `B_D \ B_r`:
  `prod_(h ∈ B_r) E_(π(h))(z+h) = prod_(h ∈ B_r) E_(π(h))(z+h) · prod_(h ∈ B_D \ B_r) sum_a E_a(z+h) = sum_(π' ⊇ π) prod_(h ∈ B_D) E_(π'(h))(z+h)`.
- Each term vanishes by (Q3). ∎

## 2. Proof of Theorem A

**Coordinates.**
- Choose `w ∈ Z^2` with `ℓ_e(w) = 1`. Then `{w, e}` is a basis, since `det(w, e) = ℓ_e(w) = 1`.
- Every `z` is uniquely `c w + t e`, with `c = ℓ_e(z)` the *level* of `z` and `t = t(z)`.
- By `Le`-periodicity, `E_a(c w + t e)` depends only on `t mod L`.
- For `h ∈ B_r`, `|ℓ_e(h)| <= r(|p| + |q|) = m_e`. So each `r`-ball meets `2m_e + 1` consecutive levels.

**Step 1 (levels are commuting blocks).** Put `B = A^(Z/L)` and, for `β ∈ B`, `E_β(c) = prod_(t ∈ Z/L) E_(β(t))(c w + t e)`. Assume the hypothesis of A.1, or for A.2 the same hypothesis restricted to pairs on levels of a run.
- The factors commute, so `E_β(c)` is an idempotent.
- The `E_β(c)`, `β ∈ B`, are orthogonal with sum `1`, by (Q1).
- `E_β(c)` commutes with `E_(β')(c')` whenever `|c − c'| <= 2m_e`.

**Step 2 (illegal windows vanish).**
- Call a word `β_c ⋯ β_(c+2m_e) ∈ B^(2m_e + 1)` *legal* if, for every `z_0` with `ℓ_e(z_0) = c + m_e`, the pattern `h ↦ β_(ℓ_e(z_0 + h))(t(z_0+h) mod L)` on `B_r` lies in `L_(B_r)(Ω)`.
- Suppose the word is illegal at `z_0`. Then `prod_(s=0)^(2m_e) E_(β_(c+s))(c+s) = 0`.
- Reason: all factors commute (Step 1). After regrouping, the product contains `prod_(h ∈ B_r) E_(π(h))(z_0 + h)` for an illegal `π`, whose factors commute, and this vanishes by (Q3_r).

**Step 3 (a periodic point).**
- Call a word *legal* if all its windows of length `2m_e + 1` are legal.
- Suppose legal words of every length exist. König's lemma gives a bi-infinite legal word `(β_c)_(c ∈ Z)`.
- Put `x(c w + t e) = β_c(t mod L)`. Every `r`-ball of `x` lies in the window around its central level, so all `B_r`-patterns of `x` are legal. Hence `x ∈ Ω`, and `x(z + Le) = x(z)`.
- So if `Ω` has no point with period `Le`, some length `W_0` admits no legal word.

**Step 4 (ordered product).** Multiply in increasing order of `c`:
`1 = prod_(c = c_0)^(c_0 + W_0 − 1) sum_(β ∈ B) E_β(c) = sum E_(β_(c_0))(c_0) E_(β_(c_0+1))(c_0+1) ⋯ E_(β_(c_0+W_0−1))(c_0+W_0−1)`.
- Every term contains an illegal window of consecutive factors.
- Those factors multiply to `0` by Step 2.
- So `1 = 0`, and `W = 0`. ∎ (A.1)

**Step 5 (explicit run length, A.2).** Let the run have `N >= |A|^(2m_e L) + 2m_e` levels, and suppose all pairs on levels of the run at `ℓ_e`-distance `<= 2m_e` commute.
- Steps 1, 2 and 4 apply inside the run. So if `W != 0`, some legal word `β_0 ⋯ β_(N−1)` exists.
- Consider its blocks of `2m_e` consecutive letters starting at `0, …, N − 2m_e`. There are at least `|A|^(2m_e L) + 1` of them, so two coincide: `β_(p+s) = β_(p'+s)` for `0 <= s < 2m_e`, with `p < p'`.
- Put `y_c = β_(p + ((c − p) mod (p' − p)))`.
- Take a window of `y` of length `2m_e + 1` starting at `c ∈ [p, p')`. For `i ∈ [p', c + 2m_e]` we have `y_i = β_(i − (p'−p)) = β_(p + (i − p')) = β_i`, since `i − p' < 2m_e`, and `i <= N − 1`. So the window equals `β_c ⋯ β_(c+2m_e)`, and `y` is legal.
- Then `x(c w + t e) = y_c(t mod L)` lies in `Ω` and has the independent periods `Le` and `(p' − p) w`. So `Ω` has a periodic point. ∎ (A.2)

**Converse (A.3).** If `x ∈ Ω` has period `Le`, the family `E_a(z) = [x(z) = a]` on `W = k` commutes and satisfies (Q1) and (Q3_r). ∎

## 3. Proofs of the corollaries

**B.** Here `e = (0,1)`, `ℓ_e(z) = z_1`, `m_e = r <= D`.
- Take two sites with `|z_1 − z'_1| <= 2r`. Replacing `z'` by `z' + tLe_2` changes no operator, and some `t` gives `|z_2 − z'_2| <= ⌊L/2⌋ <= 2D`.
- So the pair commutes by (Q2).
- Lemma 1 and A.1 then give a point with period `(0,L)`. ∎

**C.** Put `E_a(z) = e_a(z mod Λ)` on `V`.
- (T1)–(T3) of `torus-quantum-tilings-decide-matriciality-of-sft-rings` give (Q1)–(Q3), so this is a nonzero `Λ`-periodic scale-`D` family, and by Lemma 1 a radius-`r` family.
- `Λ` contains `L_e e`, where `L_e` is the order of `e` in `Z^2/Λ`.
- If all pairs at `ℓ_e`-distance `<= 2m_e` commuted, A.1 would give a periodic point. So some `E_a(z)`, `E_b(z')` at `ℓ_e`-distance `<= 2m_e` do not commute.
- If some representative of `z' − z` mod `Λ` lay in `B_(2D)`, (T2) would make them commute. ∎

**D.** Over a finite field `U^e` has finite order `L`, so the family is `Le`-periodic.
- Conjugation by `U^w`, with `ℓ_e(w) = 1`, maps the strip `{c <= ℓ_e <= c + 2m_e}` onto the next one.
- If one strip algebra were commutative, all of them would be.
- Any two sites at `ℓ_e`-distance `<= 2m_e` lie in a common strip, so all such pairs would commute, and A.1 would give a periodic point. ∎

## 4. Calibration

- **Periodic tile set** `Ω = {a^(Z^2)}`: it has points of every period, and classical families satisfy every strip hypothesis. A.1 is sharp; A.2, C and D are vacuous.
- **SFTs.** A point with period `Le` gives a doubly periodic point. The points of period `Le` form a one-dimensional SFT over `A^(Z/L)` along `w`, with finitely many constraints, and a nonempty one-dimensional SFT has a periodic point.
- **Correction to a calibration line** in `sk-labbe-matricial-thin-cylinders-2026-09-13.md` §2: "SFTs with vertically periodic points but no doubly periodic point" do not exist, by the previous item. The thin-cylinder theorem and its corollaries are unaffected.
- **Infinite fields.** Corollary D needs `Le`-periodicity. Covariant families whose translations have infinite order are not covered by A.1, which is why D is stated over finite fields. Row 7 is over `F_q`.
- **Labbé.** A.2 with `e = (0,1)` gives `N = 19^(2L) + 2`.

## 5. What this says about row 7, and what it does not reach

**Necessary structure of witnesses.** Every witness for exact matriciality of `LC(Ω_U, F_q) ⋊ Z^2` is contextual inside narrow strips along every rational direction:
- width `3` for the axes, and `ℓ_e`-width `2(|p| + |q|) + 1` for `e = (p,q)`;
- covariant witnesses in every strip. Theorem 1(a) of the torus criterion induces covariant families from torus modules, and the induced family's strips commute iff the torus strips do.

Periods `<= 4D+1` are the case where short circumference forces narrow strips to commute.

**Known firewalls, now including this one:**
- classical families die;
- one-dimensional composition factors die;
- two-dimensional factors die if `Ω_U` is wall-rigid (`labbe-shift-is-wall-rigid`, unreviewed);
- dimensions and period tori diverge;
- both axis periods must be `>= 4D+2`;
- narrow-strip contextuality in every direction is required (this artifact).

**Holonomy picture.**
- A narrow `e`-strip on a torus is an annulus of circumference `L_e`. Its levels are commuting blocks at `ℓ_e`-distance `<= 2m_e`, but sites far apart along `e` need not commute.
- Theorem A says: if every such annulus is classical, the family is zero.
- So the non-classicality of a witness is carried by holonomy around the `e`-cycles of narrow annuli, for all `e` at once.

**What it does not reach.** A single annulus can carry a noncommutative family. For a one-dimensional cyclic local algebra the ordered product around the cycle leaves exactly the legal *linear* words of length `L_e`, and those exist. So the theorem forces contextuality without forbidding it. Deciding row 7 needs an interaction between annuli in different directions.

**Routes that would decide it (none established):**
- **Strip determinism (negative polarity).** Suppose that in some narrow strip every legal block of `2m_e` levels had a unique legal continuation to the next level. Then each level's projections would lie in the commutative algebra of the previous block, strips would commute, and A.1 would kill every witness. For `Ω_U` this fails as stated: `P_U` has boundary segments in the four directions `(1,0), (0,1), (1,−1), (1,−φ)` (`labbe-shift-is-a-toral-rotation-coding`), so new levels bring new cutting lines and extensions are not unique. An operator-level substitute for determinism is needed.
- **Annular holonomy (positive polarity).** A construction must give narrow annuli nontrivial holonomy along every rational cycle simultaneously, with dimension growing in `D`. The nearest known mechanism is quantum satisfiability of parity constraint systems on non-planar graphs (Arkhipov, arXiv:1209.3819; recalled, not read here). The torus is non-planar, but Wang-tile constraints are not parity constraints, so no such template transfers directly.
