# Cost preservation splits along the relative Furstenberg-Zimmer tower

Agent b-t-reuse, wave swarm-0917, September 17, 2026. Target hole:
`fpbs-correlated-reuse-flags-removable`, toward `fpbs-fixed-price-universal`.

## 0. Summary

Fix a countably infinite group G. Say that a factor map pi: Y -> X of free
p.m.p. G-actions on standard probability spaces *preserves cost* if C(Y)=C(X).

* **P_c(G).** Every relatively compact extension V -> W of free G-actions with
  C(W) < infinity preserves cost.
* **P_w(G).** Every relatively weakly mixing extension V -> W of free
  G-actions with C(W) < infinity preserves cost.

**Theorem 1.** If P_c(G) and P_w(G) hold, then every factor map Y -> X of
free G-actions with C(X) < infinity preserves cost. For finitely generated G
the converse is trivial, so P_c(G) and P_w(G) together are equivalent to
fixed price for G (`fpbs-factor-invariance-equals-fixed-price`).

The proof uses two established inputs and nothing else:

1. the relative dichotomy of Jamneshan (Theorem 1.2 / 6.2 of arXiv:2103.17167),
   imported as `relative-furstenberg-zimmer-dichotomy`. It holds for arbitrary groups
   and assumes no ergodicity;
2. continuity of cost along increasing factors, (10.1) of
   `research/artifacts/fpbs/docs/fixed-price-conditional-traffic.md`, which is
   part of the established claim `fpbs-traffic-variational-cost-identities`.

Section 3 records a census. Every construction in the reuse artifacts that
defeats a particular *removal method* by a finite or group-rotation fiber is a
relatively compact extension. So those countermodels say nothing about
P_w. Section 4 explains why this is a decomposition and not an easier
problem. P_w already contains fixed price among weakly mixing actions, and
P_c contains finite-fiber questions of finite-index type. Neither is proved
here.

## 1. Definitions used

Relative notions are taken exactly from Jamneshan (citation node
`relative-furstenberg-zimmer-dichotomy-citation`).

* An extension pi: X -> Y is *relatively weakly mixing* if every f in L^2(X)
  with E(f|Y)=0 satisfies: for every epsilon>0 there is gamma with
  ||<(T^gamma)^* f, f>_{L^2(X|Y)}||_{L^2(Y)} < epsilon (Definition 6.1).
* It is *relatively compact* if L^2(X) is the L^2-closure of the union of its
  Gamma-invariant finitely generated L^infinity(Y)-submodules (Theorem 4.1
  (ii)', one of six equivalent conditions). Finite-fiber extensions and
  isometric (compact group rotation) extensions are of this kind.

Both notions depend only on the pair of sigma-algebras, so they transfer to
standard models. For a countable group acting on a standard probability space
Y, a G-invariant complete sub-sigma-algebra F of B_Y defines a factor action
Y_F on a standard space, unique up to isomorphism. If B_X is contained in F
and X is free, then Y_F is free, because Y_F factors onto X.

## 2. Proof of Theorem 1

Let pi: Y -> X be a factor map of free G-actions with C(X) < infinity.
Identify X with the sub-sigma-algebra B_X of B_Y.

**Step 1: the relative tower.** Define invariant sigma-algebras F_alpha by
transfinite recursion, as long as the recursion has not stopped.

* F_0 = B_X.
* Successor. If Y -> Y_{F_alpha} is relatively weakly mixing, stop and set
  beta = alpha. Otherwise, by Theorem 1.2 (ii) there are a system Z and
  extensions phi: Y -> Z and psi: Z -> Y_{F_alpha}, with psi a non-trivial
  relatively compact extension. In the proof of Theorem 6.2, Z is built
  from an invariant subalgebra A of L^infinity(Y), and psi comes from a
  non-trivial factor inclusion L^infinity(Y_{F_alpha}) -> A. So Z corresponds
  to an invariant sigma-algebra F_{alpha+1} with F_alpha strictly contained
  in F_{alpha+1}, and F_{alpha+1} contained in B_Y. Also Y_{F_{alpha+1}} ->
  Y_{F_alpha} is relatively compact. (Every standard countable-complexity
  version of the dichotomy states the intermediate factor in this form; see
  the references quoted in the citation node.)
* Limit lambda. F_lambda is the complete sigma-algebra generated by the union
  of the F_alpha, alpha < lambda. It is again invariant, and it is strictly
  larger than every F_alpha, alpha<lambda, because F_alpha is strictly
  contained in F_{alpha+1}.

**Step 2: the recursion stops at a countable ordinal.** Let P_alpha be the
orthogonal projection of L^2(Y) onto L^2(Y_{F_alpha}). These projections
increase strictly with alpha. Fix a countable dense set {e_n} in L^2(Y). If
alpha < alpha', then

||P_{alpha'} e||^2 - ||P_alpha e||^2 = ||(P_{alpha'} - P_alpha) e||^2,

and since P_{alpha'} differs from P_alpha, this is positive for some e_n.
For each successor alpha+1 pick n(alpha) with a positive jump from alpha to
alpha+1. For fixed n, the function alpha -> ||P_alpha e_n||^2 is nondecreasing
with values in [0, ||e_n||^2]. Its positive jumps occupy disjoint intervals of
positive length, so there are only countably many of them. Hence only
countably many successor stages exist. If the recursion ran through all
countable ordinals, omega_1 would have uncountably many successors, which is
impossible. So it stops at a countable ordinal beta, where Y -> Y_{F_beta} is
relatively weakly mixing. (Jamneshan's Remark 5.6 makes the same observation
for the absolute tower.) Write Z_alpha = Y_{F_alpha}. Each Z_alpha is free
and lies between Y and X.

**Step 3: transfinite induction, C(Z_alpha) = C(X) for all alpha <= beta.**

* alpha = 0: Z_0 = X.
* Successor. Z_{alpha+1} -> Z_alpha is a relatively compact extension of free
  actions, and C(Z_alpha) = C(X) < infinity. By P_c(G),
  C(Z_{alpha+1}) = C(Z_alpha) = C(X).
* Limit lambda. lambda is a countable limit ordinal, so it has a cofinal
  increasing sequence alpha_1 < alpha_2 < ... . The sigma-algebras
  F_{alpha_r} increase, contain B_X, and generate F_lambda. Apply (10.1) to the
  free action Z_lambda, its factors Z_{alpha_r}, and the base X with
  C(X) < infinity: C(Z_{alpha_r}) decreases to C(Z_lambda). Every term equals
  C(X), so C(Z_lambda) = C(X).

**Step 4: the last step.** Y -> Z_beta is relatively weakly mixing, and
C(Z_beta) = C(X) < infinity. By P_w(G), C(Y) = C(Z_beta) = C(X). QED.

**Converse for finitely generated G.** Free actions of a finitely generated
group have finite cost, so fixed price gives C(V) = C(W) for every factor map.

**Remarks on scope.**

* Only the two special classes are used. The inverse limits are absorbed by
  (10.1), with no hypothesis on the group.
* Neither hypothesis can be weakened to extensions of Bernoulli bases alone.
  Intermediate stages Z_alpha are arbitrary free actions.
* The theorem is per group. A single group G without fixed price must violate
  P_c(G) or P_w(G). The failing step is a single compact or weakly mixing
  extension between two factors of a cost-changing pair.
* No ergodicity is needed. Jamneshan's dichotomy is unconditional, and cost,
  (10.1), and the definitions above are all non-ergodic.

## 4. What the two halves contain (context, not proved here)

**P_w already contains fixed price among weakly mixing actions.** Let a be a
free weakly mixing action of a finitely generated G, and b a Bernoulli shift.
The projection a x b -> b has relative product b x a x a over b. When a is
weakly mixing this is ergodic, hence relatively ergodic over the ergodic b.
By the criterion in Jamneshan's Remark 6.3 ("relatively weakly mixing if and
only if X x_Y X is a relatively ergodic extension of Y", stated there
without proof), the projection is relatively weakly mixing. The same argument
appears in `strict-pair-flip-lifts-are-relatively-weakly-mixing`. So P_w(G)
gives C(a x b) = C(b). Together with `fpbs-bernoulli-noise-does-not-change-cost`
(C(a x b) = C(a)), this gives C(a) = C(b) for every free weakly mixing a. So
P_w is not a small side case. It carries the lower bound against Bernoulli for
the whole weakly mixing class.

**P_c contains finite-fiber questions.** Finite-to-one free extensions
V -> W, for example W x G/H -> W for a finite-index subgroup H, are
relatively compact. By `fpbs-finite-fiber-cost-bound` and Section 2 of
`finite-fiber-cost-descent.md`, cost preservation along finite fibers encodes
the finite-index question. Over a Bernoulli base and a Kazhdan group with no
compact homomorphisms, ergodic compact extensions are trivial
(`kazhdan-map-bernoulli-finite-extensions-are-trivial` for the finite case).
Stages Z_alpha of the tower are not Bernoulli, however, so this does not
dispose of P_c even there.

**Why this is a genuine decomposition.** P_c and P_w are each implied by
fixed price. A group without fixed price must break at least one of them.
Either can fail while the other holds without contradicting anything here.
Their mechanisms differ. Compact steps carry an invariant finite-rank module,
a phase that plans can reuse coherently. Weakly mixing steps carry no
invariant finite-rank information, and relative correlations can be made small
along group elements. That is the situation the "group-correlated reuse
flags" of the target were meant to exclude.
