# Exact finite-order rounding can destroy the scalar gap on GHB7 itself

2026-09-20. No local or remote computation was run. The hyperbolic
residual-finiteness problem and GHB7 flexible HS stability remain open.

This pass isolates and falsifies a concrete analytic preprocessing
estimate at the actual GHB7 frontier. It does not restate stability
as another open equivalent condition. In particular, the counterexample
below is already close to a genuine representation and is **not**
an instability witness.

## 1. The candidate and the precise estimate tested

The direct non-RF route uses flexible HS stability of a hyperbolic
Kazhdan group with positive second rational Betti number. Its explicit
candidate is the torsion-free congruence kernel `H` of
`G=G_HB2(7) -> Sp_4(F_7)`. Stability of `G` would suffice by the
established Kazhdan finite-index descent theorem. The kernel has
`b2=159091199`; neither its stability nor that of `G` is established.

For `G`, Liu's named large-block theorem reduces the missing task
to doubled approximate tuples
`W=(W_a^+,W_a^-,W_b^+,W_b^-,W_c^+,W_c^-)` with a fixed scalar gap

```
(1/24) sum_(s,sign) ||[W_s^sign,X]||_2^2
    >= c ||X-tr(X)I||_2^2.                         (SG)
```

The wave-two phase-normalization theorem already shows that only
nonscalar defects remain after scalar phases are removed. Its final
unclosed preprocessing proposal imposes exact seventh powers by
nearest-root functional calculus and tries to retain a usable scalar
gap, possibly after additional pruning.

Let `q7:T -> {z:z^7=1}` choose the nearest seventh root, with any
tie convention. The concrete estimate tested here is:

> For fixed `c>0`, a doubled GHB7 tuple satisfying (SG), with all
> defining and copy-equality defects sufficiently small in normalized
> HS norm, has `q7(W)` with a positive dimension-independent scalar gap.

This estimate is false, even for tuples whose distance to genuine
representations tends to zero. The following construction satisfies
all the group-specific and doubled hypotheses, not just an arbitrary
matrix example.

## 2. Growing genuine irreducible representations exist

The primary CCKW source was reread on 2026-09-20:
[*Hyperbolic generalized triangle groups, property (T) and finite
simple quotients*, Corollary 7.19, printed page 49](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09276).
It says `G_HB2(p)` has finite simple quotients containing alternating
groups of arbitrarily large degree. Theorem 1.3 supplies the
hyperbolic and Kazhdan properties at `p=7`.

Consequently `G` has nontrivial complex irreducible unitary
representations `pi_n` with dimensions `d_n -> infinity`. Here is
a direct degree argument, without invoking a minimum-degree table.
Choose a simple quotient containing `Alt(4r)`, and choose any
nontrivial irreducible complex representation of that quotient.
It is faithful by simplicity. The alternating subgroup contains
`(C2)^r`, generated by double transpositions on disjoint sets of
four letters. A faithful complex representation of this elementary
abelian subgroup requires at least `r` characters: those characters
must span its `r`-dimensional dual. Thus its dimension is at least
`r`. Pull back and unitarize these irreducibles to obtain `pi_n`.

The presentation of `G` has three seventh-power relators and eight
iterated commutators. Hence any assignment of seventh roots to
`a,b,c` defines a one-dimensional character. Choose an eigenvalue
`lambda_n` of `pi_n(a)` and a unit eigenvector `v_n`. Let `chi_n`
be the character with `chi_n(a)=lambda_n`, `chi_n(b)=chi_n(c)=1`.
Set

```
Pi_n=pi_n direct-sum chi_n,   D_n=d_n+1,
```

and write `e_n` for the new one-dimensional summand's unit vector.
The exact commutant of `Pi_n(G)` consists of the two block scalars,
since `pi_n` is irreducible of dimension greater than one.

## 3. A rank-two perturbation with opposite doubled signs

Fix once and for all `0<theta<pi/7`, and let `H_n` be the
self-adjoint matrix exchanging `e_n` and `v_n` and zero on their
orthogonal complement. It has rank two, operator norm one, and
commutes with `Pi_n(a)`, since that generator has the same eigenvalue
on those two vectors. Define

```
W_a,n^+ = Pi_n(a) exp(i theta H_n),
W_a,n^- = Pi_n(a) exp(-i theta H_n),
W_b,n^+ = W_b,n^- = Pi_n(b),
W_c,n^+ = W_c,n^- = Pi_n(c).                      (1)
```

Then

```
||W_a,n^sign-Pi_n(a)||_2
    = 2 sqrt(2) sin(theta/2) / sqrt(D_n),
||W_a,n^+-W_a,n^-||_2
    = 2 sqrt(2) sin(theta) / sqrt(D_n).           (2)
```

All other copy defects vanish. Word telescoping bounds every fixed
relator defect by its length times the first expression in (2).
Thus all fourteen doubled-presentation defects tend to zero.
Indeed the tuples already round to `Pi_n` in the same dimension
with error `O(D_n^-1/2)`.

Nevertheless nearest-root functional calculus removes the entire
rank-two rotation. Its only moved eigenvalues are
`lambda_n exp(±i theta)`, each strictly closer to `lambda_n` than
to any other seventh root. Therefore

```
q7(W_s,n^sign)=Pi_n(s)  for all six entries.      (3)
```

The rounded tuple has scalar gap zero because the added-line
projection is a non-scalar member of its exact commutant.

## 4. The original doubled tuple has a uniform positive scalar gap

This is the essential verification; small rank alone does not
establish a gap. Property (T) and the fixed generating triple give
`kappa>0` such that, for every finite-dimensional unitary
representation `Pi` and every matrix `X`,

```
sum_(s=a,b,c) ||[Pi(s),X]||_2^2
   >= kappa^2 ||X-E_(Pi(G)')(X)||_2^2.           (4)
```

To obtain (4), apply a Kazhdan pair to the conjugation representation
on the orthogonal complement of its fixed vectors, and express its
finite set as words in the fixed generators. This gives a constant
independent of dimension, with normalized or unnormalized HS norm.

For the tuple (1), put
`E_W(X)=sum_(s,sign)||[W_s^sign,X]||_2^2`. The opposite signs give
the exact identities

```
W_s^+ W_s^- = Pi_n(s)^2,
Pi_n(s) = (Pi_n(s)^2)^4,
```

the latter because `Pi_n(s)^7=I`. Product commutator estimates
therefore imply

```
sum_s ||[Pi_n(s),X]||_2^2 <= 32 E_W(X).          (5)
```

Assume `tr(X)=0`, and write `X=B+Y`, where
`B=E_(Pi_n(G)')(X)` and `Y` is orthogonal to that commutant.
Equations (4)--(5) give

```
||Y||_2 <= K sqrt(E_W(X)),   K=sqrt(32)/kappa.   (6)
```

Let `P_n` be the added-line projection. The traceless block scalar
has the form `B=t(P_n-I/D_n)`. Direct computation on
`span(e_n,v_n)` gives

```
||B||_2 = |t| sqrt(d_n)/D_n,
||[W_a,n^+,B]||_2 = |t| sqrt(2/D_n) sin(theta).
```

Consequently, uniformly in `n`,

```
||B||_2 <= c_theta ||[W_a,n^+,B]||_2,
c_theta = 1/(sqrt(2) sin(theta)).                (7)
```

Using `||[U,Y]||_2<=2||Y||_2` and (6), (7) yields

```
||X||_2
 <= [K+c_theta(1+2K)] sqrt(E_W(X)).              (8)
```

Thus all tuples (1) satisfy (SG) with the fixed positive constant

```
c = 1 / {24 [K+c_theta(1+2K)]^2}.
```

Subtracting `tr(X)I` proves the inequality for arbitrary `X`.
The entire proof uses the doubled tuple; no gap for its plus half
is assumed. Equations (2), (3), and (8) prove the claimed failure
of gap-preserving seventh-root rounding on GHB7 itself.

## 5. What remains analytically necessary

There is a true elementary operator-norm perturbation estimate.
If two six-unitary tuples `U,V` satisfy
`max_t ||U_t-V_t||_op<=epsilon` and `U` has scalar gap `c`, then

```
scalar_gap(V) >= max(0,sqrt(c)-epsilon)^2.       (9)
```

For a traceless `X`, the Euclidean norm of the six commutators
changes by at most `2 sqrt(6) epsilon ||X||_2`; divide by
`sqrt(24)` to obtain (9). The example above has fixed nonvanishing
operator-norm perturbation on rank two, although its normalized-HS
perturbation vanishes. It pinpoints why replacing the operator norm
in this estimate by normalized HS norm is invalid.

Discarding the added line in this particular example restores the
genuine irreducible representation and its uniform adjoint gap.
Hence the example does not refute an estimate allowing removal of
`o(D_n)` dimensions. Nor does it prove that such pruning is always
possible after finite-order rounding. A proof using that strategy
still needs a dimension-uniform way to perform finite-order
rounding and microscopic-sector removal together, while controlling
the normalized-HS generator error and a retained gap. Repeated
compression and polar correction can create new small bad sectors;
no convergent bound for that process is established here.

More fundamentally, even successful finite-order/gap preprocessing
would leave the candidate-specific noncentral relator correction
problem. The existing phase theorem gives comparable centrality
and phase-normalized defect, not a contraction. Neither this
counterexample nor the true operator-norm estimate supplies the
missing contraction or proves GHB7 unstable.
