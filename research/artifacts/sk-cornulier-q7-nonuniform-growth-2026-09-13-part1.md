# sk-cornulier-q7: derived full groups of dyadic substitution subshifts have non-uniform exponential growth (part 1: setup and generation)

Lane sk-cornulier-q7, 2026-09-13. Status: established by this lane, UNREVIEWED (queued with sk-verify-13).

## 0. Statement
**Theorem Q.** Let ζ be a primitive substitution of constant length 2 over a finite alphabet A with a non-periodic fixed point. Let X ⊆ A^Z be its two-sided subshift, with shift T. Then the derived topological full group [[T]]′ has non-uniform exponential growth: ω([[T]]′) = 1, and [[T]]′ has exponential growth.

**Corollary Q1.** There is a finitely generated infinite simple amenable group of non-uniform exponential growth, for example [[T]]′ for the Thue–Morse substitution 0↦01, 1↦10 or the period-doubling substitution 0↦01, 1↦00.
- X is an infinite minimal subshift, since ζ is primitive and its fixed point is non-periodic.
- [[T]]′ is finitely generated (Matui) and simple (Matui).
- [[T]] is amenable (Juschenko–Monod).

**What this answers.** Cornulier, Séminaire Bourbaki 1064 (arXiv:2002.09342), "Problèmes et questions ouvertes" (7): "Le groupe [[φ]]′ a-t-il une croissance exponentielle uniforme?" The answer is **no** for these φ, and the question stays open for general minimal subshifts (e.g. Sturmian).
- Kourovka 21.117(a) asks for a finitely generated simple group of non-uniform exponential growth, answered by Thompson's V (Sauer–Schesler, arXiv:2605.30163). Corollary Q1 gives an amenable example; V is not amenable.
- Osin (2003): elementary amenable groups of exponential growth have uniform exponential growth. [[T]]′ is simple, infinite and finitely generated, so not elementary amenable, and there is no conflict.

**The proof** transfers Sauer–Schesler's proof for V (arXiv:2605.30163 §§3–5, read at source on MSI) to [[T]]′. Two substitutions are what make the transfer work.
1. X is a tower of constant height 2^m over a clopen copy of itself, for every m. This replaces "V contains V_Z for every finite Z".
2. The local generators are chosen to commute: partial 3-cycles over the letter cylinders, plus one shift pair. This replaces Sauer–Schesler's two commuting Thompson generators X_0^{(n)}, X_1^{(n)}.

The Grigorchuk-group combinatorics (inverted orbits, separation, the linking graph) are Sauer–Schesler's and Bartholdi–Erschler's.

## 1. Inputs
- **(M1) Tower partition** (node `constant-length-substitution-tower-partition`, PASS sk-verify-14, conditional on Mossé's recognizability import). ζ: X → X is injective, V_1 = ζ(X) is clopen, X = V_1 ⊔ TV_1, and ζ∘T = T²∘ζ.
  - Iterating: for every m, V_m = ζ^m(X) is clopen, X = ⊔_{i<2^m} T^i V_m, T^{2^m}V_m = V_m, and ζ^m: (X,T) → (V_m, T^{2^m}|V_m) is a conjugacy.
- **(M2) Generation by good 3-cycles** (Cornulier, bou1064.tex l.573–586, Définition `d_bon` and Lemme `eng_cb`; proof uses simplicity of [[φ]]′ and Matui Lemma 5.1).
  - Definition: U clopen is *good* if φ^{−1}U, U, φU are pairwise disjoint.
  - σ_U ∈ [[φ]] is the identity off φ^{−1}U ∪ U ∪ φU and cyclically permutes U →(φ) φU →(φ^{−2}) φ^{−1}U →(φ) U.
  - Lemma: for an infinite minimal subshift, [[φ]]′ is generated by the σ_U with U good.
- **(M3) Exponential growth** (Matui; Cornulier bou1064.tex l.780 and l.166). Every subgroup of [[φ]] containing [[φ]]′ contains a lamplighter group, hence a free semigroup, so [[φ]]′ has exponential growth.
- **(SS) Grigorchuk-level estimates** (node `sauer-schesler-grigorchuk-level-estimates`, items 1–5). Notation 𝒢, S_n = {a_n,b_n,c_n,d_n} ⊂ Sym(X_n), X_n = {0,1}^n, ρ_n, η_n, θ_n = ρ_n a_n, r_n = 2^⌊n/2⌋, inverted orbits 𝒪_ξ(w).
  - (SS1) γ_𝒢^S(k) ≤ exp(Ck^γ), γ<1.
  - (SS2) For ξ ∈ {ρ_n, η_n} and k ≤ r_n: |𝒪_ξ(w)| ≤ Ck^α for words of length ≤ k, and there are at most exp(Ck^α) such orbits, α<1.
  - (SS3) d_{Γ_n}(ρ_n, η_n) ≥ 2^{n−3}.
  - (SS4) The 𝒢_n-translates of {θ_n, ρ_n} form a connected graph on X_n.
  - (SS5) 𝒢_n is transitive on X_n.

Right actions for S_n, as in Sauer–Schesler; homeomorphisms act on points on the left. Only the permutation of level indices is used from S_n.

## 2. The tower model at scale n
Fix n ≥ 5 and put m = n+3, N = 2^m, V = V_m, S = T^N|_V. By (M1), (V,S) ≅ (X,T) through ζ^m.
- **Levels.** Put Y_n = X_n × {1,…,8}, so |Y_n| = N. Call {x}×{1,…,8} the *fibre* F_x and the second coordinate the *sheet*. Fix a bijection β: Y_n → {0,…,N−1}. Every point of X is uniquely T^{β(y)}v with v ∈ V and y ∈ Y_n; write it (v,y).
- **Dynamics.** T(v,y) = (v, β^{−1}(β(y)+1)) if β(y) < N−1, and T(v,y) = (Sv, β^{−1}(0)) if β(y) = N−1.
- **Uniform level permutations.** For π ∈ Sym(Y_n), put L_π(v,y) = (v, πy). On each clopen level V×{y}, L_π equals T^{β(πy)−β(y)}, so L_π ∈ [[T]], and π ↦ L_π is an injective homomorphism. Alt(Y_n) is perfect (N ≥ 5), so L(Alt(Y_n)) ≤ [[T]]′.
- **Shift on one level.** For a sheet-point y, s_y(v,y) = (Sv,y), and s_y is the identity elsewhere. On V×{y} it equals T^N, so s_y ∈ [[T]].
- **Partial level permutations.** For a clopen B ⊆ V and π ∈ Sym(Y_n), L_π^B equals L_π on B×Y_n and the identity elsewhere; L_π^B ∈ [[T]].
  - For a 3-cycle π = (y y′ y″), write ζ_B^{(y,y′,y″)} = L_π^B.
  - It is the commutator of the partial transpositions L_{(y y′)}^B and L_{(y′ y″)}^B, since pointwise over v ∈ B the commutator of (y y′) and (y′ y″) is a 3-cycle on {y,y′,y″}. Replacing π by π^{±1} if needed, ζ_B^{(y,y′,y″)} ∈ [[T]]′.
- **Shift pairs.** For distinct y, y′: σ_{y,y′} = s_y s_{y′}^{−1}. With t = L_{(y y′)}, s_y t s_y^{−1} t^{−1} maps (v,y) ↦ (Sv,y) and (v,y′) ↦ (S^{−1}v,y′). So σ_{y,y′} = [s_y, t] ∈ [[T]]′.
- **Letter cylinders.** U_a = ζ^m({x ∈ X: x_0 = a}) for a ∈ A. These are pairwise disjoint clopen sets covering V, and the Boolean algebra generated by their S-translates is the whole clopen algebra of V, because (V,S) ≅ (X,T) is a subshift.

## 3. The generating sets T_n
Types, with sheets written as the second coordinate:
- **Type 1:** L_s for s ∈ S_n acting diagonally, (x,i) ↦ (xs,i). Each s permutes 8 copies of X_n identically, so L_s is even and lies in [[T]]′.
- **Type 2:** Alt(8)_{η_n} = {L_π : π ∈ Alt(F_{η_n})}.
- **Type 3:** δ_n = L_{((ρ_n,1)(θ_n,1))((ρ_n,2)(θ_n,2))}, an even involution.
- **Type 4:** ζ_a := ζ_{U_a}^{((ρ_n,3),(ρ_n,4),(ρ_n,5))} for a ∈ A, and σ_n := σ_{(ρ_n,6),(ρ_n,7)}.

T_n = S_n ∪ Alt(8)_{η_n} ∪ {δ_n} ∪ {ζ_a : a∈A} ∪ {σ_n}. So |T_n| = 4 + 20160 + 1 + |A| + 1, independent of n, and T_n ⊂ [[T]]′. Put W_n = ⟨T_n⟩.

**The type-4 group is abelian.**
- The ζ_a act by the same 3-cycle (3 4 5) pointwise over the disjoint sets U_a, so they commute and generate (Z/3)^{|A|}.
- σ_n lives on sheets 6, 7, so it commutes with every ζ_a.
- So A_{ρ_n} := ⟨type 4⟩ ≅ (Z/3)^{|A|} × Z.

## 4. Generation: W_n = [[T]]′
**Step 1 (Alt(Y_n) ≤ W_n).**
- By (SS5), conjugating type 2 by words in type 1 gives L(Alt(F_x)) ≤ W_n for every x, since type 1 acts diagonally on sheets.
- Let {x,y} = {ρ_n g, θ_n g} be an edge of the graph in (SS4), with g a word in S_n. Then δ_n^g swaps (x,i) ↔ (y,i) for i = 1,2 and fixes all else.
  - For distinct b, c ∈ F_x ∖ {(x,1),(x,2)}, conjugating the 3-cycle ((x,1) b c) ∈ Alt(F_x) by δ_n^g gives ((y,1) b c).
  - Conjugating that by Alt(F_x) × Alt(F_y) gives every 3-cycle with one point in F_y and two in F_x, and symmetrically. So L(Alt(F_x ∪ F_y)) ≤ W_n.
- For overlapping sets P, Q with |P∩Q| ≥ 2, ⟨Alt(P), Alt(Q)⟩ contains every 3-cycle on P∪Q. Take (p q z) with p ∈ P∖Q, z ∈ P∩Q, q ∈ Q∖P, and conjugate a 3-cycle (p z′ z) ∈ Alt(P) by a 3-cycle in Alt(Q) sending z′ ↦ q and fixing z.
- The graph of (SS4) is connected, so Alt(Y_n) ≤ W_n (as uniform level permutations).

**Step 2 (local 3-cycles over every clopen set).** Work inside the fibre F := F_{ρ_n}; label its sheets 1,…,8, and write ζ_B^{(i,j,k)}, σ_{i,j}.
- (a) Alt(8) = L(Alt(F)) ≤ W_n by Step 1. It is 6-transitive on the sheets. Conjugating type 4 gives ζ_{U_a}^{(i,j,k)} for all distinct i,j,k and σ_{i,j} for all distinct i,j.
- (b) Let 𝔅 = {B ⊆ V clopen : ζ_B^{(1,2,3)} ∈ W_n}. By (a), B ∈ 𝔅 gives ζ_B^{(i,j,k)} ∈ W_n for all triples. Then:
  - V ∈ 𝔅 (a uniform 3-cycle), and every U_a ∈ 𝔅;
  - **complements:** ζ_{V∖B}^{(1,2,3)} = ζ_V^{(1,2,3)}(ζ_B^{(1,2,3)})^{−1};
  - **intersections:** [ζ_B^{(1,2,3)}, ζ_{B′}^{(3,4,5)}] = ζ_{B∩B′}^{(1,4,3)}. Pointwise over v ∈ B∩B′ it is [(1 2 3),(3 4 5)] = (1 4 3); over v in exactly one of the sets, one factor is trivial;
  - **S-translation:** ψ = σ_{1,4}σ_{2,5}σ_{3,6} (commuting factors) acts by S on sheets 1,2,3 and by S^{−1} on sheets 4,5,6, which ζ_B^{(1,2,3)} does not touch. So ψ ζ_B^{(1,2,3)} ψ^{−1} = ζ_{SB}^{(1,2,3)}, and ψ^{−1} gives S^{−1}B.
  - So 𝔅 is an S-invariant Boolean algebra containing every U_a. By §2 it contains every clopen subset of V.
- (c) **Twisted 3-cycles.** For clopen B, distinct sheets i,j,k and p,q,r ∈ Z, let ζ_B^{(i,p),(j,q),(k,r)} map (S^p v,i) → (S^q v,j) → (S^r v,k) → (S^p v,i) for v ∈ B, and be the identity elsewhere.
  - Choose distinct sheets d,d′,d″ outside {i,j,k} (8 ≥ 6), and put χ = σ_{i,d}^p σ_{j,d′}^q σ_{k,d″}^r.
  - Then χ ζ_B^{(i,j,k)} χ^{−1} is that twisted 3-cycle, so it lies in W_n.

**Step 3 (every good σ_U).**
- Let U ⊆ X be good for T, and write U = ⊔_y U_y×{y} with U_y ⊆ V clopen.
  - Each piece W_y = U_y×{y} is good.
  - The supports T^εW_y (ε ∈ {−1,0,1}) of the σ_{W_y} are pairwise disjoint over different y: same ε by injectivity of T, and different ε because U is good.
  - The restriction of σ_U to them is σ_{W_y}. So σ_U = ∏_y σ_{W_y}.
- For W = B×{y}: T^{−1}W and TW are B′×{y′} with B′ ∈ {B, S^{±1}B} by §2. So σ_W is a twisted 3-cycle of B on the three distinct levels β^{−1}(β(y)−1), y, β^{−1}(β(y)+1), indices mod N, with twists in {0,±1} (N ≥ 32).
- Choose π ∈ Alt(Y_n) mapping these three levels into F (Alt(Y_n) is 3-transitive). L_π commutes with every s_z-structure in the sense L_π s_z L_π^{−1} = s_{πz}, so L_π σ_W L_π^{−1} is a twisted 3-cycle inside F. It lies in W_n by Step 2(c), and so does σ_W.
- By (M2), [[T]]′ ≤ W_n. With T_n ⊂ [[T]]′ from §3, **W_n = [[T]]′ for all n ≥ 5.** ∎
