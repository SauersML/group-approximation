# Gamma_mal: normal subgroups and intermediate relations carry no leverage

Worker swarm-0917-w23-w23-fp-break (belief breaker), September 20, 2026.
Target: `fpbs-mal-bernoulli-single-stage-floor`.

This artifact proves two class-kills. Both hold for every free action, so
neither can separate the Bernoulli shift `beta` (whose cost is open) from the
odometer products `beta x Z^_chi` (which have cost 2).

## 0. Notation

- `Gamma = Gamma_mal = <a, b_1, b_2, ... | b_k = b_{k+1} a b_{k+1}^{-2}>`, with
  `H_n = <a, b_n>` free on `{a, b_n}`.
- `L = F(a, b)`, `phi(a) = a`, `phi(b) = b a b^{-2}`, and `L_j = phi^j(L)`.
- `C*` is the infimal cost over free p.m.p. actions, and `beta_1` is the first
  L2-Betti number.

## 1. The normal closure of `a`

**Lemma 1.1.**
- The map `chi(a) = 0`, `chi(b_k) = (-1)^{k+1}` defines an epimorphism
  `Gamma -> Z`.
- `N := ker chi` equals the normal closure `<<a>>`.
- `Gamma = N ⋊ <b_1>`.
- `N ∩ H_n` is the normal closure of `a` in `H_n`, which is free on
  `{ b_n^k a b_n^{-k} : k in Z }`.
- `N` is the increasing union of the free groups `N ∩ H_n`, each of infinite
  rank.

*Proof.*
- **`chi` is well defined.** The relation gives
  `chi(b_k) = chi(b_{k+1}) - 2 chi(b_{k+1}) = -chi(b_{k+1})`.
- **`N = <<a>>`.** Killing `a` leaves `<b_k | b_k = b_{k+1}^{-1}>`, which is `Z`
  generated by `b_1`. The image of `b_1` is `chi(b_1) = 1`, so `<<a>> = ker chi`.
  The splitting is `n -> b_1^n`.
- **The stages.** `chi|H_n` sends `a -> 0` and `b_n -> ±1`. Its kernel is the
  normal closure of `a` in `F(a, b_n)`, and Reidemeister–Schreier gives the
  basis. QED

## 2. Lemma N: infinite-index normal subgroups are infinitely expensive

**Lemma N.** Let `1 != Λ ⊴ Gamma_mal` with `[Gamma_mal : Λ] = infinity`, for
example `Λ = N`. Then:

1. `beta_1(Λ) = infinity`;
2. every free p.m.p. action of `Λ` has infinite cost, so `C*(Λ) = infinity`;
3. in particular, `C(α|Λ) = infinity` for every free action `α` of `Gamma_mal`,
   including `beta` and the odometer products.

*Proof.*
- `Gamma_mal` is locally free, so it is torsion free and `Λ` is infinite.
- `beta_1(Gamma_mal) = c* - 1 = 1`, by
  `locally-free-groups-first-l2-betti-equals-c-star-minus-one`.
- **Item 1.** Gaboriau's contrapositive of Théorème 6.8
  (`fpbs-gaboriau-l2-betti-normal-subgroup-and-finite-index`, verbatim: "Si Γ
  est un groupe qui vérifie β1(Γ)≠0 et N est un sous-groupe normal tel que
  β1(N) soit fini ..., alors N est fini ou d'indice fini") gives item 1.
- **Item 2.** Gaboriau's inequality `C(R) - 1 >= beta_1(R) - beta_0(R)`
  (`fpbs-amen2-betti-cost-input`), with `beta_1(R) = beta_1(Λ)` for a free
  action and `beta_0 = 0`, gives item 2.
- **Independent route to item 2.** If `C*(Λ) < infinity`, then
  `fpbs-finite-cost-normal-subgroup-infimal-cost-one` gives `C*(Gamma) = 1`.
  This contradicts `C*(Gamma_mal) = 2`
  (`fpbs-mal-odometer-product-actions-have-cost-two`).
- **Item 3.** The restriction of a free action is free. QED

**What this kills.** Every normal-subgroup cost transfer is inert at
`Gamma_mal` for proper normal subgroups of infinite index, in both directions.
The finite-index case is the index formula, which gives nothing new.
- Upper bounds all read `C(α) <= infinity`:
  - Gaboriau's `C(R_Gamma) <= C(R_Λ)` (lecture notes Cor. 2.48);
  - the finite-cost normal subgroup theorem;
  - `fpbs-normal-subgroup-bounded-cost-fixed-price-one` and its trichotomy
    subroutes;
  - `fpbs-amenable-outer-kernel-forces-price-one`.
- There is no lower-bound mechanism through `Λ` at all. The known normal
  subgroup theorems only produce upper bounds or vanishing.
- The earlier kill (artifact `research/artifacts/fpbs-amalgam-descent-amenably-generated-2026-09-16.md`, item (iv))
  covered normal bases over finitely generated `Λ`. Lemma N covers every
  infinite-index normal `Λ`, and it shows that the obstruction is L2 and not
  finite generation.
- This agrees with the w22 finding that only stage malnormality remains as a
  floor source.

## 3. Lemma I: intermediate relations over a malnormal stage

**Lemma I.** Let `L` be countable, and let `K <= L` be infinite and malnormal.
Let `α` be a free p.m.p. action of `L` on `(X, mu)`, and let `T` be a Borel
equivalence relation with `E_K ⊆ T ⊆ E_L`. Then for a.e. `x`, the `T`-class of
`x` is either one `E_K`-class or a union of infinitely many `E_K`-classes.

*Proof.*
- **Setup.** Let `X_m` be the set of points whose `T`-class contains exactly
  `m` `E_K`-classes. It is Borel and `T`-invariant. Suppose `mu(X_m) > 0` for
  some `2 <= m < infinity`.
- **The coset sets.** For `x in X_m`, put `S_x = { g in L : g x T x }`.
  - Freeness makes `g` unique for each point.
  - `E_K ⊆ T` makes `S_x` a union of right cosets `K g`.
  - Exactly `m` cosets occur, and `K` itself is one of them.
  - For `k in K`: `S_{kx} = { g : g k x T x } = S_x k^{-1}`.
- **The invariant measure.** Let `Ω` be the countable set of `m`-element
  subsets of `K\L`. The map `F(x) = K\S_x` is Borel from `X_m` to `Ω` and
  satisfies `F(kx) = F(x) k^{-1}`. So `nu = F_*(mu|X_m)` is a nonzero finite
  measure on `Ω`, invariant under the right action of `K`.
  - An atom of an invariant finite measure has a finite orbit.
  - So some `ω` with `nu(ω) > 0` has a finite-index stabiliser `K_ω <= K`.
- **The contradiction.** Let `K'` be the finite-index subgroup of `K_ω` that
  fixes each of the `m` cosets in `ω`. It is infinite, because `K` is.
  - Pick `K g in ω` with `g ∉ K`, possible since `m >= 2`.
  - For `k in K'`, `K g k = K g` gives `g k g^{-1} in K`, so
    `k in g^{-1} K g ∩ K`.
  - That intersection is `{1}` by malnormality, contradicting `|K'| = infinity`.
    QED

**Where it applies.**
- `L_{j+1} <= L_j` is malnormal, being `phi^j` of the malnormal pair `K <= L`.
- `H_n` is malnormal in `H_{n+1}` and in `Gamma_mal`.
- So every relation `T` with `R_n ⊆ T ⊆ E` (for example `T = R_n v Φ` for any
  graphing `Φ`) grows, wherever it grows at all, by infinitely many
  `R_n`-classes per class. The same holds for `S_{j+1} ⊆ T ⊆ S_0` on the
  `F_2` side.

**What this kills.** No promotion argument can pass through an intermediate
relation of finite index over a stage. This covers:
- finite-index cocycle or compact-extension steps (a finite-index intermediate
  `T` is exactly what a finite compact-extension step between `R_n` and a
  larger relation would provide);
- bounded-multiplicity absorption;
- "attach finitely many classes at a time" schemes.

Every nontrivial enlargement of a stage relation merges infinitely many stage
classes. This holds in all free actions, so the lemma gives neither a floor
nor a refutation. It removes the finite-index layer from the search space, and
it explains why the known mechanisms (the fold rule, 2-of-3 closure,
percolation) are all infinite-cluster phenomena.

## 4. Smaller observations (no new node)

- **Antitone check.** `relC` is antitone in the subrelation. So
  `relC(S_0; S_j) >= relC(S_0; S_1) = Q_0 >= 2/25` for every `j >= 1`. Any
  sufficient route that asks `relC(S_0; S_j) -> 0` is therefore void.
- **Free-factor witnesses.** If `R_2 = R_1 * S` (a free product of relations),
  then `C(R_2) = C(R_1) + C(S)`, with `C(R_2) = C(R_1) = 2`. So `C(S) = 0` and
  `S` is trivial. So `R_1` is never a proper measured free factor of `R_2`, and
  no witness of the floor can be a free-factor splitting of a stage pair.
