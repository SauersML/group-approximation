**a finitely generated group is LEF iff it is a subgroup of an infinite, finitely generated, simple Kazhdan LEF group.**

## 1. Setup and Lemma 1
- `L = ⟨Δ, a⟩` consists of the maps `x ↦ δx + f`, with `f ∈ ⊕_Δ Z/2`.
  - Composition: `(δ,f)(δ',f') = (δδ', f + δ·f')`, so `L ≅ Z/2 ≀ Δ`.
  - The action is faithful, since an infinite `Δ` acts faithfully on `2^Δ`.
  - `δaδ^(-1)` is the flip at `δ`; I checked that `(δaδ^(-1)x)(h) = x(h) + [h = δ]`.
- **Minimal.** Every orbit contains `x + ⊕Z/2`, which is dense.
- **Topologically free.**
  - If `δ = e` and `f ≠ 0`, there is no fixed point.
  - If `δ ≠ e`, fix a cylinder on `W` and choose `h ∉ W ∪ supp f ∪ δ(W ∪ supp f)`, using that `Δ` is infinite.
  - Then `h ≠ δ^(-1)h` and both lie outside `W ∪ supp f`.
  - A point of the cylinder with `x(h) ≠ x(δ^(-1)h)` is moved, because `(ℓx)(h) = x(δ^(-1)h)`.
- **Generation.** `u_δ p u_δ^(-1) = 1_[x(δ)=1]`, and cylinders span `LC(Z,F_2)`. So `R` is generated by
  `u_s^(±1)`, `u_a` and `p`. It is finitely generated, and EJZ applies.

PASS.

## 2. Lemma 2 (simplicity and centre of R)
- Each `Fix(ℓ)` with `ℓ ≠ 1` is closed and nowhere dense, and `L` is countable, so by Baire the free points are dense.
- **Ideal.**
  - Normalize so that `f_1 ≠ 0`.
  - Pick a free point `x_0` with `f_1(x_0) = 1`, and a clopen `U ∋ x_0` with `f_1|_U = 1` that is disjoint from `ℓU` for
    the finitely many `ℓ ≠ 1` in the support.
  - Then `1_U r 1_U = f_1 1_U = 1_U`.
  - By minimality finitely many translates of `U` cover `Z`, so the ideal contains `1`.
- **Centre.**
  - `[1_V, r] = Σ f_ℓ(1_V − 1_(ℓV))u_ℓ`.
  - At `x ∈ supp f_ℓ` with `ℓ^(-1)x ≠ x`, choose `V ∋ x` with `ℓ^(-1)x ∉ V`.
  - So `supp f_ℓ ⊆ Fix(ℓ^(-1))`. It is open and nowhere dense, hence empty.
  - `f_1` is invariant, hence constant.

PASS.

## 3. Lemma 3 and Proposition 4 (matricial models)
- **Generators.** `φ_n(e) = 1` (from `φ_n(e)^2 = φ_n(e)`) and `φ_n(s^(-1)) = φ_n(s)^(-1)`. Then
  `x_(σ_s z)(h) = c(qφ_n(s^(-1)h)) = x_z(s^(-1)h)`, and `x_(σ_a z)(h) = x_z(h) + [φ_n(h) = 1] = x_z(h) + [h = e]` by
  injectivity on `B_n`.
- **The affine formula.** `F_ℓ(c,q) = (c + Σ_(h∈supp f) 1_(q'φ_n(h)), q')`, with `q' = qφ_n(δ^(-1))`, depends only on
  `(δ,f)`. Left induction checks it:
  - `F_s∘F_ℓ = F_(sℓ)`, from `qφ_n(δ^(-1))φ_n(s^(-1)) = qφ_n((sδ)^(-1))` and `q'φ_n(h) = q''φ_n(sh)`;
  - `F_a∘F_ℓ = F_(aℓ)`, where adding `1_(q')` is the symmetric difference with `{e}` over `F_2`.

  Every step uses the partial-homomorphism identities on `B_n`, with `n` large compared with the word length.
- **Configurations.** `x_(F_ℓ z)(h) = x_z(δ^(-1)h) + f(h) = (ℓ·x_z)(h)` on `B_(n/2)`.
- **Diagonal.** `D_n(f)` is defined once `ρ ≤ n`, because every pattern on `B_ρ` is legal in the full shift `Z`. `D_n` is
  additive, multiplicative and unital.
- **Conjugation.** `P_(σ_ℓ)D_n(f)P_(σ_ℓ)^(-1) = D_n(f∘ℓ^(-1))`. The reason: `(f∘ℓ^(-1))(y)`, for an extension `y` of
  `x_(σ_ℓ z)` on `B_(ρ+|ℓ|)`, depends only on `(ℓ^(-1)y)|_(B_ρ) = x_z|_(B_ρ)`.
- **Ring map.**
  - With `σ_(ℓℓ') = σ_ℓσ_(ℓ')`, `π_n` is multiplicative on any fixed pair for large `n`.
  - So `π = (π_n)_ω` is a unital ring homomorphism into the algebraic ultraproduct.
  - It is injective because `R` is simple.

PASS.

## 4. Theorem 5
- **(T).** `R` is finitely generated, so EJZ `thm:main` applies.
- **Simplicity.**
  - `L ⋉ Z` is Hausdorff and ample, with compact infinite unit space.
  - It is minimal, and effective because the action is topologically free.
  - `A_(F_2)(L ⋉ Z) = LC(Z,F_2) ⋊ L`, so the Steinberg criterion gives: every normal subgroup is central or all of `G`.
  - A central `g` commutes with every `e_ij(1)`, so `g = cI` with `c ∈ Z(R)^× = {1}`.
- **LEF.** `M_n(∏_ω M_(N_k)) = ∏_ω M_(nN_k)`, and invertibility is coordinatewise ω-almost everywhere. So `EL_n(R)` is a
  countable subgroup of an algebraic ultraproduct of finite groups, hence LEF.
- **Embedding.**
  - `diag(a,a^(-1))diag(b,b^(-1))diag((ab)^(-1),ab) = diag(1, a^(-1)b^(-1)ab)`, multiplied out.
  - Whitehead gives `diag(c,c^(-1)) ∈ E_2`.
  - `e_12(1)e_21(1)e_12(1)` is the swap matrix because `R` has characteristic 2; conjugating by it gives
    `diag([x,y],1)`.
  - `c ↦ diag(c,1,…,1)` is an injective homomorphism on `GL_1(R)` sending `[GL_1R, GL_1R]` into `E_n(R)`.
  - `ℓ ↦ u_ℓ` is injective.

PASS.

## 5. Corollary 6
- (⇒)
  - Part 1 §3 gives a f.g. LEF `Δ` with `Γ ≤ [Δ,Δ]`.
  - `Δ × Z` is f.g., LEF and infinite, and `[Δ×Z, Δ×Z] = [Δ,Δ] × 1`.
  - Theorem 5 over `Δ × Z` gives `Γ ≤ [Δ×Z, Δ×Z] ≤ [L,L] ↪ EL_3(R)`.
- (⇐) Subgroups of LEF groups are LEF.
