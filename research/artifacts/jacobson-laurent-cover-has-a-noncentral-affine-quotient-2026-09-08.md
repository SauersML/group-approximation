# The Laurent kernel surjects onto the natural affine Laurent module

2026-09-08. Put `R=F_2[z,z^(-1)]`. The abstract Laurent cover
`Lambda` surjects onto `R^3 semidirect SL_3(R)`, with linear
projection exactly the established Laurent map `kappa`. The
coherence word `e` maps to translation by `z e_1`, and the image
of `ker(kappa)` is the entire natural translation module `R^3`.

Consequently the Laurent-cover kernel is noncentral, infinite, and
not finitely generated as an abstract group. Evaluation at `z=1`
gives a concrete eight-point affine quotient of order 1344. Both
constructions respect the entire presentation of `Lambda`, including
both complete finite factors and their common opposite root. Neither
identifies the entire kernel or asserts that the abstract word `e`
has order two.

## 1. The presentation and the word being tested

Use the exact quotient presentation established in
[the finitary-kernel proof](jacobson-recursive-packets-generate-finitary-linear-kernel-2026-09-08.md)
and the Laurent map established in
[the Laurent quotient calculation](jacobson-stable-letter-laurent-quotient-and-local-inner-action-2026-09-08.md).
The group `Lambda` is generated by two prescribed copies of
`GL_3(F_2)`:

```text
G_1=<c,k,q>,             c=x_13, k=x_31, q=w_23,
G_2=<c,k,h>,             c=x_13, k=x_31, h=w_12.
```

Their common subgroup is the full `GL_2(F_2)` generated by
`c,k` on coordinates `1,3`. Put

```text
r=[q k q,c],
u=[h c h,k],
v=h u h,
d=h r h.
```

The two additional relators are

```text
(h q)^3=1,              [u,d]=r.                       (1)
```

Throughout, `[a,b]=a b a^(-1)b^(-1)`. The coherence word is

```text
e=(q v q)d.                                            (2)
```

The established Laurent homomorphism is

```text
kappa:Lambda -> SL_3(F_2[z,z^(-1)]).
```

It sends `c,k,q` to their constant matrices and sends `h` to

```text
        [ 0     z     0 ]
H(z) =  [ z^-1  0     0 ].
        [ 0     0     1 ]
```

Its values on `v,d` are `x_12(z),x_13(z)`, respectively.
Thus `kappa(e)=I`, since conjugation by the constant `w_23`
sends `x_12(z)` to `x_13(z)`.

## 2. The affine Laurent quotient

Use the affine group `R^3 semidirect SL_3(R)`. An element
`(A,a)` acts on column vectors by `x |-> A x+a`, with

```text
(A,a)(B,b)=(AB,a+A b),
tau_a=(I,a).
```

Let `D_scale=diag(z,1,z)`. This is invertible over `R`, and
conjugation by it preserves determinant one. Define two embeddings
of the complete finite group `GL_3(F_2)` by

```text
J_1(A)=(A,0),
J_2(A)=tau_(e_2) (D_scale A D_scale^(-1),0) tau_(e_2).
```

The first is constant and linear. The second is conjugate to the
constant linear group by an invertible affine map, so is faithful
as well. On the common coordinate subgroup on `1,3`, the matrix
`D_scale` acts as the scalar `z`, while every group element fixes
`e_2`. Thus `J_1` and `J_2` agree on the entire common
`GL_2(F_2)`, including both `c` and `k`.

The resulting generator assignments are

```text
Phi_R(c)=(x_13(1),0),
Phi_R(k)=(x_31(1),0),
Phi_R(q)=(w_23,0),
Phi_R(h)=(H(z),a),          a=z e_1+e_2.                (L1)
```

These already respect all relations internal to either finite factor
and all prescribed common-factor identifications. The two mixed
relators must still be checked. In particular, the first packet's
`r=x_23(1)` is not fixed by conjugation with `D_scale`; its
identification with the corresponding second-packet root is not
being used.

Within the second finite packet, the usual formulas for `u,v`
give

```text
Phi_R(u)=(x_21(z^(-1)),0),
Phi_R(v)=(x_12(z),z e_1).                              (L2)
```

For a direct calculation of `d=h r h`, write `r_0=x_23(1)`.
Both `r_0` and `H(z)` fix the vector `a` in (L1). Consequently
the translation part of this conjugate is

```text
a+H(z) r_0 a=0,
```

and its linear part is `H(z) r_0 H(z)=x_13(z)`. Hence

```text
Phi_R(d)=(x_13(z),0),
[Phi_R(u),Phi_R(d)]
   =(x_23(z^(-1)z),0)=(x_23(1),0)=Phi_R(r).            (L3)
```

This proves the mixed commutator relation directly.

For the braid, put `M=H(z)w_23`. It satisfies `M^3=I`,
and the three successive translation vectors are

```text
a=z e_1+e_2,
M a=e_2+e_3,
M^2 a=e_3+z e_1.
```

They sum to zero, so the affine multiplication law gives
`Phi_R(hq)^3=1`. All defining relations have now been checked,
and (L1) defines a homomorphism

```text
Phi_R:Lambda -> R^3 semidirect SL_3(R).                 (L4)
```

Its linear part is exactly `kappa`, as is seen on each generator.
Conjugating (L2) by the constant `w_23`, and using (L3), gives

```text
Phi_R(e)=tau_(z e_1),
Phi_R([e,k])=tau_(z e_1+x_31(1)z e_1)
             =tau_(z e_3) != 1.                       (L5)
```

In particular `e` and `[e,k]` are nontrivial in `Lambda`.

To prove the complete image assertion, let
`N=Phi_R(ker(kappa))`. Equality of the linear projection with
`kappa` first gives `N<=R^3`. The previously established map
`kappa` is surjective. For any `A in SL_3(R)`, choose a lift
`lambda in Lambda`; conjugation by `Phi_R(lambda)` sends a
translation vector `x` to `A x`, independently of the translation
part of the lift. Thus `N` is invariant under the full natural
`SL_3(R)` action.

Equation (L5) gives `z e_1 in N`. For every `f in R`,
conjugating this translation by a lift of `x_21(f)` and adding
back `z e_1` gives

```text
x_21(f)(z e_1)+z e_1=f z e_2 in N.
```

Because `z` is a unit, this gives every vector in `R e_2`.
Constant coordinate swaps belong to `SL_3(R)` in characteristic
two, so invariance gives each of the three coordinate lines.
Their sums give

```text
Phi_R(ker(kappa))=R^3.                                 (L6)
```

The image of `Phi_R` therefore contains every translation and
projects onto every linear matrix. Subtracting the translation part
of any chosen lift proves

```text
Phi_R(Lambda)=R^3 semidirect SL_3(R).                   (L7)
```

This restriction to `ker(kappa)` is equivariant for the full
`Lambda` action, with that action on the target factoring through
`kappa` and the natural `SL_3(R)` module. It identifies a quotient
of the kernel, not the entire kernel.

The additive group `R^3` has infinite dimension over `F_2`,
since the Laurent monomials `z^n`, `n in Z`, are linearly
independent. A finitely generated group cannot surject onto this
infinitely generated abelian group. Thus `ker(kappa)` is infinite
and is not finitely generated as an abstract group. Equation (L5)
also proves that it is not central in `Lambda`. The image of `e`
has order two. The later
[integral-twist calculation](jacobson-third-packet-cannot-be-corrected-with-both-edges-fixed-2026-09-08.md)
maps `e` to a nonzero integral lattice vector and proves that
`e` itself has infinite order.

## 3. The concrete eight-point specialization

Evaluating all coefficients at `z=1` specializes (L4) to an affine
map over `F_2`. The following independent formulas record its
complete finite action and make the word's nontriviality visible
on eight points.

Let `V=F_2^3`, with basis `e_1,e_2,e_3`, and use the natural
affine action on its eight points. Write an affine map as `(A,a)`,
acting by `x |-> A x+a`. The multiplication convention is

```text
(A,a)(B,b)=(AB,a+A b).
```

Write `tau_a=(I,a)` for translation by `a`, and identify
`GL_3(F_2)` with the linear maps `(A,0)`. Use capital letters
for the following constant linear matrices:

```text
C=x_13,       K=x_31,       Q=w_23,       H=w_12,
R_23=x_23,    U=x_21,       V_12=x_12,    D=x_13.
```

Define the first packet by its linear action, and define the second
packet by conjugating the linear action with `tau_(e_2)`:

```text
j_1(A)=(A,0),
j_2(A)=tau_(e_2) (A,0) tau_(e_2)
      =(A,e_2+A e_2).                                 (3)
```

Both are faithful copies of the entire finite group `GL_3(F_2)`.
Every matrix in the common coordinate subgroup on `1,3` fixes
`e_2`. Therefore `j_1` and `j_2` agree on that entire subgroup,
including both `C` and `K`. In particular the assignments

```text
Phi(c)=(C,0),
Phi(k)=(K,0),
Phi(q)=(Q,0),
Phi(h)=(H,e_1+e_2)                                    (4)
```

respect all finite-factor relations and all common-factor
identifications. It remains to check exactly the two relators (1).

The matrix `R_23` also fixes `e_2`, so

```text
j_1(R_23)=j_2(R_23)=(R_23,0).                          (5)
```

This additional equality is permitted by the presentation and will
make the mixed commutator relation immediate.

## 4. Both mixed relators in the finite specialization

The finite matrix identities in the second packet are

```text
[H C H,K]=U,
H U H=V_12,
H R_23 H=D.
```

Both `U=x_21` and `D=x_13` fix `e_2`, whereas
`V_12 e_2=e_1+e_2`. Equations (3)--(5) therefore give

```text
Phi(u)=(U,0),
Phi(v)=(V_12,e_1),
Phi(d)=(D,0),
Phi(r)=(R_23,0).                                      (6)
```

Since the elementary matrix identity `[U,D]=R_23` holds over
`F_2`, equations (6) prove the second relator in (1).

For the braid, the affine map `Phi(hq)` has linear part `HQ`
and translation part `e_1+e_2`. The linear part sends

```text
e_1 |-> e_2 |-> e_3 |-> e_1.
```

Thus `(HQ)^3=I`, and its three translated contributions sum to

```text
(e_1+e_2)+(e_2+e_3)+(e_3+e_1)=0.
```

The affine multiplication law now gives `Phi(hq)^3=1`.
Consequently (4) defines an actual homomorphism

```text
Phi:Lambda -> AGL_3(F_2).                              (7)
```

No relation of the presentation was omitted. This differs from the
earlier selected-cell example, where the two opposite-root matrices
were not identified.

## 5. The finite image is all AGL_3 and the word is a translation

Conjugation by the linear `Q` sends `V_12` to `D` and fixes
`e_1`. Therefore (2) and (6) give

```text
Phi(q v q)=(D,e_1),
Phi(e)=(D,e_1)(D,0)=(I,e_1)=tau_(e_1) != 1.           (8)
```

In particular `e` is nontrivial in the actual group `Lambda`.
The nontriviality can be seen on the eight-point affine set itself:
its image sends the zero vector to `e_1`.

The first packet already gives every linear matrix in the image of
`Phi`. Conjugating `tau_(e_1)` by those matrices gives every
nonzero translation, because `GL_3(F_2)` is transitive on the
nonzero vectors. Thus `Phi` is surjective onto the full affine
group, whose order is

```text
|AGL_3(F_2)|=8 |GL_3(F_2)|
            =8(8-1)(8-2)(8-4)=1344.                   (9)
```

The value in (8) has order two. Equation (8) does not prove that
`e` itself has order two in `Lambda`.

## 6. The finite specialization of the kernel quotient

Let `ell:AGL_3(F_2)->GL_3(F_2)` be the linear-part map.
On every generator, (4) shows that

```text
ell o Phi = evaluation_(z=1) o kappa.                  (10)
```

In particular `Phi(ker(kappa))` is contained in the translation
subgroup `V`. Since `e` belongs to `ker(kappa)`, equation (8)
puts `tau_(e_1)` in that image. The subgroup `ker(kappa)` is
normal in `Lambda`, so conjugating by the first finite packet
puts all translations in its image. Hence

```text
Phi(ker(kappa))=V ~= (F_2^3,+).                        (11)
```

The restriction in (11) is a surjective homomorphism onto an
abelian group. It is equivariant for the conjugation action of the
first `GL_3(F_2)` packet: that action on `V` is its natural
three-dimensional action. This provides a quotient of the Laurent
kernel, not an identification of the kernel with `V`.

There is an explicit witness to noncentrality. Since
`K e_1=e_1+e_3`, the affine multiplication law gives

```text
Phi([e,k])
 =tau_(e_1) (K,0) tau_(e_1) (K,0)^(-1)
 =tau_(e_1+K e_1)
 =tau_(e_3) != 1.                                    (12)
```

Thus `[e,k]!=1` in `Lambda`. In particular `ker(kappa)`
is not central in `Lambda`.

## 7. The lifted coherence defect in the full stable-letter group

Let `Gamma=Theta_shift`, let `L` be its authenticated normal
finitary subgroup, and let `pi` be its literal bilateral action.
The established quotient and kernel comparison are

```text
Gamma/L ~= Lambda x Z,
ker(pi) intersect L={1},
ker(pi) ~= ker(kappa) x {0},
[ker(pi),L]=[ker(pi),t]=1.                             (13)
```

Use the fully decorated word and boundary factor from
[the coherence-word calculation](jacobson-missing-laurent-coherence-word-and-finite-cell-countermodel-2026-09-08.md):

```text
b=q_Q q_P,              zeta=r_Q r_P,
u=[h c_P h,k_P],         v=h u h,
D_full=h zeta h,
E=b v b D_full.
```

That calculation proves `E in ker(pi)` and identifies its image
in `Gamma/L` as `(e,0)`. More explicitly, it proves the boundary
identity

```text
E=e_P v_boundary^(-1),
e_P=q_P v q_P (h r_P h),
v_boundary=I+e_(1,1) epsilon_(2,0) in C_0.
```

Compose `Gamma->Lambda x Z` with projection to `Lambda`
and then with `Phi_R`. Equations (L5)--(L6) show that this
quotient sends `E` to `tau_(z e_1)`, sends `[E,k_P]` to
`tau_(z e_3)`, and maps `ker(pi)` onto `R^3`. Its evaluation
at `z=1` gives the finite witnesses `tau_(e_1),tau_(e_3)`.
Therefore the following conclusions all hold in the abstract group
`Gamma`:

```text
E!=1,
[E,k_P]!=1,
[E,L]=1,
t E t^(-1)=E.                                        (14)
```

The last two equations come from (13), and are compatible with the
first two. The literal kernel centralizes the finitary subgroup and
the stable letter, but it is not central in the entire group.
Its quotient `R^3` also proves that it is infinite and not finitely
generated as an abstract group. The later integral-twist calculation
also proves that `E` itself has infinite order.

## 8. Scope

The Laurent affine construction and its eight-point specialization
are exact algebraic proofs. They need no search, numerical model,
or build. They prove that the specific coherence word cannot be
deduced from the current presentation of `Lambda` or `Gamma`.

The finite quotient kills the head subgroup `L`; it therefore
does not settle whether a norm-corona homomorphism can retain the
marked head. No non-MF conclusion is obtained here.

One may instead impose `E=1` to define a new quotient of `Gamma`.
That quotient is proper by (14), and the literal representation
factors through it because `pi(E)=1`. In particular the known
nontrivial literal head survives this additional relation. These
facts justify studying that different presentation; they do not
establish its unrestricted operator-norm gap.
