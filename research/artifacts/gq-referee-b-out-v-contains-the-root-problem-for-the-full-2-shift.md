## Mathematics (checked)

- **Item 3.** `π(g) = 1` forces `g = σ^j` with `j ≠ 0`. Then `dim(h_n)^n = 2^j` makes `n | j` for every `n ∈ I`,
  which is impossible for infinite `I`. The `Z[1/2]` equivalence holds via `E ∩ ⟨σ⟩ = 0`.
- **Item 2.** It follows from residual finiteness and the absence of finite quotients of `Q`.
- **The periodic-orbit remark.** The action is faithful, `Q` acts trivially on finite invariant sets, and there are
  finitely many cyclic words of each length. So some orbit has unbounded lengths. Correct.
- **W2 (item 4).** "It also shows `Z[1/2] ∉ G`": `G` is Salo's class for an arbitrary nontrivial full shift, but the
  argument only reaches `Aut(X_2^Z)`.
  - It needs `G` to be independent of the alphabet: `Aut(Σ^Z)` embeds in `Aut(X_2^Z)` for every `|Σ| ≥ 2`. That is
    presumably Kim–Roush 1990, Salo's [12].
  - Neither source I read states it.
  - Either restrict to "`Z[1/2]` does not embed in `Aut(X_2^Z)`", or cite the mutual-embedding theorem at source.
