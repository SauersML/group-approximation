# Simplicity exits for GL_n(Q) hosts (gq-pp-simple)

Lane `gq-pp-simple`, 2026-09-17. Target: `gl-n-q-embeds-in-fp-simple-group`. This lane is the simplicity
specialist for families A (germ extensions) and C (piecewise projective). Nothing here is independently
reviewed.

**Bottom line.** Simplicity is not the bottleneck. Once a finitely presented host of `SL_m(Q)` (any `m >= 3`)
exists, one of the exits below turns it into a finitely presented simple host of `GL_(m-1)(Q)`. Each exit has
one checkable finiteness condition. Spend construction effort on finite presentation, and on getting
`SL_m(Q)` inside at all.

## 1. The exits

| Exit | Needs, besides `Γ` finitely presented with `SL_m(Q) <= Γ` | Simple host | Node |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 type (A), Cantor | `V <= Γ <= Homeo(C)` in standard form, and ONE clopen-set stabilizer finitely generated (automatic if `Γ` is full) | Zaremsky's `SV_Γ`, simple by Belk–Zaremsky Thm 3.4 | `fp-cantor-groups-containing-v-type-a-iff-clopen-stabilizer-fg`, `fp-full-binary-cantor-groups-have-type-a-actions` |
| E1' type (A), general | a faithful action with finitely generated point stabilizers and finitely many orbits of pairs | `SV_Γ` | `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`, `gl-n-q-in-permutational-boone-higman-class` |
| E2 monolith | `Γ` locally moving over a simple `T` (e.g. `T = V`), and `[Γ : ncl_Γ(T)] < ∞` | `ncl_Γ(T)`; `SL_m(Q)` lies in it | `fp-groups-with-a-locally-moving-simple-subgroup-are-isolated`, `sl-m-q-passes-to-finite-index-and-commutator-cores` (item 3) |
| E3 commutator | `Γ` a finite germ extension satisfying BHM Thm 1.3 (same orbits as `B`), plus `Γ/Γ′` finite or all characters in `Σ^2(Γ)` | `Γ′`; `SL_m(Q)` perfect, so it lies in `Γ′` | `sl-m-q-passes-to-finite-index-and-commutator-cores` (item 4) |

Notes.
- E1 and E1' need no simplicity of `Γ` and no index condition. They are the most robust exits.
- In E2 and E3, `GL_n(Q)` itself may not lie in the simple subgroup, since it has finite quotients. Put
  `SL_(n+1)(Q)` in, which has none, and use `g ↦ diag(g, det(g)^-1)`.
- `(Q,+)`, and every divisible subgroup, likewise lies in every finite-index subgroup.

## 2. Which exit fits which family

**A. Finite germ extensions of V (germs off finite sets).** E1 applies whenever `Γ` is finitely presented and
the clopen stabilizer is finitely generated. E3 applies when the orbit hypothesis holds. The catch:
- `germ-extension-base-trivial-characters-lie-in-sigma` gives E3's Σ-condition only when every germ group is
  a finite extension of `V`'s. Germ groups of `V` are trivial or cyclic, so those are virtually cyclic and hold
  no divisible subgroup.
- A family-A host whose divisible pieces sit in large germ groups (the VA pattern: `Q` enters through the
  germs, not through `V`) must therefore check `Γ/Γ′` finite, or the Σ-condition, directly. Otherwise it must
  use E1.

**C. Piecewise-projective and projective Cantor models.** `pgl-n-q-acts-on-rational-polyhedral-cantor-set`
gives `PGL_n(Q)` a faithful action on the Cantor set `S_n` of rational polyhedral regions of `RP^(n-1)`. For
`n = 2`, `S_2` is the blow-up of `RP^1` at `P^1(Q)`, carrying standard `T <= V`.
- There, every element of `PGL_2(Q) − PGL_2(Z)` is a non-`V` germ at every point, and orbits grow
  (`x ↦ 2x` moves `√2` out of `V·√2`). So hosts on this model are not finite germ extensions of `V`, and E3
  and the BHM finite-presentation theorems do not apply. Only E1 and E2 do.
- A finitely generated `Γ` generated by `V` and finitely many piecewise-`PGL_2(Q)` elements is piecewise
  `PGL_2(Z[1/S])` (the analytic-continuation dead end on the `gq-pp-psl2q` board). So in this model `PSL_2(Q)`
  can only enter through generators that are not piecewise projective.

**C'. Lifts to R (T̄ pattern).** A lifted group `L` is a central extension, and a copy of `(Q,+)` inside it
meets the centre when the base circle group is `Q`-free. So quotienting by the centre, the usual route to a
simple quotient, kills divisibility, and the host must contain `L` faithfully, as in `T̄ <= A <= VA`. The
obstruction that lifts of `Q`-free groups contain no `GL_n(Q)`, `Aff(Q)` or `U_3(Q)` is `gq-pp-lift`'s
(landed 970b120c7). It is not repeated here.

**B. Brin–Thompson nV.** `nV` is itself finitely presented and simple (Brin; survey §4, twisted
Brin–Thompson subsection). So every subgroup of `nV`, including Kojima–Sheng's `Q <= nV`, already sits in a
finitely presented simple group, and for family B the only question is containment.
- Caution for E1: digit interleaving `C^2 ≅ C` does not put standard binary `V` inside `2V`. A prefix
  replacement between cones of different length parity swaps the roles of the two coordinates on the tail.
  Use 4-ary digit pairs instead, which puts `V_4` inside `2V` in standard form.

## 3. Calibration for simplicity claims (for referees)

1. A claimed simple host must say which exit it uses, and check that exit's finiteness condition, not only
   simplicity.
2. `ncl_Γ(V)` is always simple (`fp-groups-with-a-locally-moving-simple-subgroup-are-isolated`), but it is
   finitely presented only with finite index or an extra argument. "`Γ` finitely presented, hence its simple
   monolith is" is a gap.
3. BHM Thm 1.3 needs `B` and `G` to have the same orbits. Projective actions of `PGL_2(Q)` on the Cantor model
   break it (§2 C).
4. E3's Σ-shortcut needs finite-index germ groups, and those hold no divisibility (§2 A).
5. For `GL_n(Q)`, the embedded copy must lie in the simple subgroup. Use `SL_(n+1)(Q)`, not `GL_n(Q)`, in
   E2 and E3.

## 4. Sparks (not pursued here)

- **Nekrashevych exit.** For a minimal groupoid of germs `G`, `A(G)` is simple and lies in every nontrivial
  normal subgroup of the full group (`brin-thompson-nekrashevych-groups-have-simple-alternating-core`).
  `A(G)` is finitely generated when `G` is expansive (Nekrashevych arXiv:1511.08241, Thm 1.2; not re-read).
  A finitely generated `Γ >= SL_m(Q)` acting expansively on a Cantor set, with `SL_m(Q) <= A(G_Γ)`, would give a
  finitely generated simple host; finite presentation would still be needed.
- **Higher-dimensional V on S_n.** The piecewise-`PGL_n(Z)` rational scissors group of `S_n` contains
  `PGL_n(Z)`. Whether it contains standard `V`, and whether it is finitely presented, is the natural next
  question for `gq-pp-higher`.
