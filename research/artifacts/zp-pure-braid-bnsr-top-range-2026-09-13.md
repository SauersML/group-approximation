# BNSR invariants of pure braid groups: the top range (2026-09-13)

Lane z1-09-pure-braid (Zaremsky Problem 1.9). Status: UNREVIEWED.

## 0. Setting and notation

- `P_n`: pure braid group; `omega_{ij}` (`1 <= i < j <= n`): winding-number characters, a basis of
  `Hom(P_n, R) = R^{binom(n,2)}` (Zaremsky, arXiv:1507.08597v1, Section 3.1). A character is
  `chi = sum a_{ij} omega_{ij}`.
- `Delta`: positive half twist; `Delta^2` generates `Z(P_n)` (arXiv:1507.08597, Citation 3.1).
  `omega_{ij}(Delta) = 1/2` for all `i < j` (proof of Lemma 3.7 there), so
  `chi(Delta^2) = sum_{i<j} a_{ij}`.
- `H_n := P_n / Z(P_n)`. `P_n = H_n x Z` (proof of Theorem 3.9 in arXiv:1507.08597: `omega_{12}` splits
  via `1 -> Delta^2`), and `H_n` is the pure mapping class group of the `(n+1)`-punctured sphere.
- `Sigma^m(G)`: homotopical BNSR invariant; `Sigma^m(X, Z)`: homological invariant of a space
  (Farber–Geoghegan–Schütz), equal to Bieri–Renz `Sigma^m(G; Z)` when `X = K(G,1)`;
  `Sigma^m(G) <= Sigma^m(G; Z)` (Bieri–Renz 1988).

## 1. Sources used (read on 2026-09-13 from the arXiv PDFs)

## 2. Lemma L (Euler characteristic of H_n)

For `n >= 3`, `H_n` has a finite `K(H_n, 1)` of dimension `n - 2` and `chi(H_n) = (-1)^n (n-2)!`.

*Proof.* Dimension: [Z], proof of Theorem 3.9 (a free cocompact action on a contractible
`(n-2)`-complex; the quotient is a finite `K(H_n,1)`). Euler characteristic: let
`phi = phi_{1,...,n-1}: P_n -> P_{n-1}`, with kernel `F_{n-1}` ([Z], proof of Theorem 5.7). Erasing a
strand from the full twist gives the full twist, so `phi(Delta_n^2) = Delta_{n-1}^2` and `phi` maps
`Z(P_n)` isomorphically onto `Z(P_{n-1})`. Hence `phi` induces a surjection `H_n -> H_{n-1}` with
kernel `F_{n-1} Z(P_n) / Z(P_n) = F_{n-1}` (as `F_{n-1} ∩ Z(P_n) = 1`). `H_3 = F_2` by [KMM] Lemma 4.2.
All groups involved have finite classifying spaces, so the Euler characteristic is multiplicative
(Brown, *Cohomology of Groups*, Section IX.7): `chi(H_n) = (2-n) chi(H_{n-1})`, `chi(H_3) = -1`, giving
`chi(H_n) = (-1)^n (n-2)!`. (Cross-check: `H_n = pi_1(M_{0,n+1})`, and the Fadell–Neuwirth fibrations
`M_{0,k+1} -> M_{0,k}` with fibre a sphere minus `k` points give `chi(M_{0,5}) = 2`, `chi(M_{0,6}) = -6`.) □

## 3. Lemma E (nonzero Euler characteristic empties the top invariant)

Let `X` be a finite connected CW complex of dimension `d` with `chi(X) != 0`. Then `Sigma^d(X, Z)` is
empty. If `X = K(G,1)`, then `Sigma^d(G; Z)` and `Sigma^d(G)` are empty.

*Proof A (tropical bound).* For every `rho in H^1(X, C^x)`, the twisted cellular chain complex has the
same ranks as the cellular complex, so `sum_{i=0}^{d} (-1)^i dim_C H_i(X; C_rho) = chi(X) != 0`. Some
`H_i(X; C_rho)` with `i <= d` is nonzero, so `V^{<=d}(X)` is the whole character group. Its tropical
variety is all of `H^1(X, R)` (the valuation map is onto the rational points of the identity component,
whose closure is everything). By [S] Theorem 1.1, `Sigma^d(X,Z) <= S(H^1(X,R))^c = ∅`. □

*Proof B (trace; independent of the tropical theory).* Suppose `[chi] in Sigma^d(G; Z)`. By the
Novikov–Sikorav characterization quoted in [S], `H_i(X; Nov) = 0` for `i <= d`, where `Nov` is the
Novikov completion of `ZG` for `±chi` (sign convention irrelevant here). The complex
`C_*(X~) ⊗_{ZG} Nov` is a bounded complex of finitely generated free `Nov`-modules, of ranks the cell
counts `c_i`, with zero homology in every degree. Such a complex splits, so
`⊕_{i even} Nov^{c_i} ≅ ⊕_{i odd} Nov^{c_i}`. The coefficient-of-the-identity map `tau(sum n_g g) = n_1`
is well defined on `Nov` and satisfies `tau(xy) = tau(yx)`: for Novikov series `x, y`, the sum
`sum_g x_g y_{g^{-1}}` has finitely many nonzero terms, because the supports are finite below every
level of `chi` and `chi(g) + chi(g^{-1}) = 0` confines `chi(g)` to a bounded interval. For mutually
inverse matrices `A, B` implementing the isomorphism, `tau(tr(AB)) = tau(tr(BA))` gives
`sum_{even} c_i = sum_{odd} c_i`, i.e. `chi(X) = 0`. Contradiction. □

## 4. Lemma S (split quotients with nonzero Euler characteristic)

Let `pi: G -> Q` be a split epimorphism with `G` of type `F_d`, and suppose `Q` has a finite `K(Q,1)` of
dimension `d` with `chi(Q) != 0`. Then `[psi o pi] notin Sigma^d(G)` for every nonzero character `psi`
of `Q`.

*Proof.* If `[psi o pi] in Sigma^d(G)` then `[psi] in Sigma^d(Q)` by Meinert's Corollary 2.8 ([Z]
Citation 1.3). But `Sigma^d(Q) <= Sigma^d(Q; Z) = ∅` by Lemma E. □

## 5. Theorem T (top range)

For `n >= 3` and every `m >= n - 2` (including `m = infinity`):

    Sigma^m(P_n) = { [chi] : chi(Delta^2) != 0 } = { [sum a_{ij} omega_{ij}] : sum_{i<j} a_{ij} != 0 }.

Equivalently, the complement of `Sigma^m(P_n)` is the great subsphere `{sum a_{ij} = 0}` of dimension
`binom(n,2) - 2`. Moreover `Sigma^m(H_n) = ∅` for `m >= n - 2`.

*Proof.* If `chi(Delta^2) != 0` then `chi(Delta) != 0` and `[chi] in Sigma^infinity(P_n)` ([Z] Corollary
3.8). Conversely let `chi != 0` with `chi(Delta^2) = 0`. Then `chi = psi o pi` for the split projection
`pi: P_n = H_n x Z -> H_n` and a nonzero character `psi` of `H_n`. `P_n` is of type `F`, and by Lemma L,
`H_n` has a finite `(n-2)`-dimensional classifying space with `chi(H_n) != 0`. Lemma S gives
`[chi] notin Sigma^{n-2}(P_n)`, hence `[chi] notin Sigma^m(P_n)` for all `m >= n - 2`. The statement for
`H_n` is Lemma E. □

## 6. Consequences

1. `n = 4`: `Sigma^2(P_4) = Sigma^infinity(P_4)` is the complement of the great 4-sphere
   `{a12 + a13 + a14 + a23 + a24 + a34 = 0}` in `S^5`. This is the computation [Z] p. 2 records as open.
   With [KMM] Theorem A, every BNSR invariant of `P_n` is now computed for `n <= 4`
   (`P_1 = 1`, `P_2 = Z`, `P_3 = F_2 x Z`).
2. Finiteness of coabelian subgroups (via [BGK10, Theorem 1.1] = [Z] Citation 1.2): a normal subgroup
   `N <= P_n` with abelian quotient is of type `F_{n-2}` (equivalently `F_infinity`) iff the space of
   characters vanishing on `N` meets `{sum a_{ij} = 0}` only in 0, i.e. `P_n / N` has torsion-free rank
   at most 1 and, when the rank is 1, `Delta^2` has infinite order in `P_n / N`. In particular no
   coabelian subgroup of `P_n` of rank-1 quotient containing `Delta^2`, and no one of rank >= 2, is of
   type `F_{n-2}`.
3. The same argument applies to any group `G = Q x Z^k` or `G -> Q` split with `Q` aspherical of
   dimension `d` and `chi(Q) != 0`: characters pulled back from `Q` never lie in `Sigma^d(G)`.

## 7. Relation to the resonance picture

For the braid arrangement, [S] Section 13 (Example 13.2) and [KMM] identify `Sigma^1(P_n)` with the
complement of the real first resonance variety. Theorem T is the analogous statement in degree `n-2`:
the Euler characteristic forces `V^{n-2}(M_{0,n+1})` to be the whole torus, so the resonance upper bound
is attained. Whether `Sigma^m(P_n)` is the complement of the real resonance variety `R^m(P_n)` for the
intermediate degrees `2 <= m <= n-3` is open (next artifact).
