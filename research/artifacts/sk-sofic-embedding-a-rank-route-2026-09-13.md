# sk-sofic-embedding-a: how far the crossed-product envelope carries an embedding theorem

Lane `sk-sofic-embedding-a`, 2026-09-13. Target from the brief: every f.g. sofic group embeds in an infinite simple
Kazhdan **sofic** group. Everything below is unreviewed unless it names a reviewed node.

## 0. Summary

- The envelope `EL_3(LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Δ)` embeds `[Δ,Δ]`, never more than `ker(Δ → K_1(R))` (§1). So a universal embedding
  theorem needs the ambient group `Δ` to be **perfect**, or at least `K_1`-invisible.
- For residually finite `Γ` the peer lane sk-universal-embedding-a supplies such a `Δ` by Ore's theorem inside finite
  permutation groups. **That step has no wreath-product substitute**: for every permutational wreath product
  `Δ = Γ ≀ Λ`, the copy of `Γ` meets `[Δ,Δ]` exactly in `[Γ,Γ]` (§2). I record this because it killed my own first route,
  and because it is the same shape as the error sk-subgroups found in the Whitehead diagonal map.
- Positive: **every f.g. perfect amenable group embeds in an infinite f.g. simple Kazhdan `F_2`-linear sofic group**
  (§3), with no reduction step at all, since a perfect group is its own derived subgroup.
- Negative, and the reason the lane's own target is not reachable this way: **rank models never upgrade to permutation
  models here** (§4). A rank defect `ε` becomes Hamming defect `1 − q^{−εN} → 1` in the vector action, and by
  sk-fp-sofic-a's gap theorem any sofic approximation inside gapped targets forces LEF. Soficity of these groups needs
  models with alternating constituents, which no route on main produces.
- So the target statement stays open, and §5 states it with the two precise gates.

## 1. What the envelope embeds: the `K_1` criterion

Let `Δ` be a f.g. group acting freely and minimally on a subshift `X ⊆ A^Δ`, `R = LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Δ`, and `u: Δ → R^×` the
canonical units.

**Lemma 1.** `γ ↦ diag(u_γ,1,1)` is an injective homomorphism `Δ → GL_3(R)`, and for `γ ∈ Δ`:
`diag(u_γ,1,1) ∈ EL_3(R)` **iff** `[u_γ] = 0` in `K_1(R)`. The set of such `γ` is a normal subgroup containing `[Δ,Δ]`.

*Proof.* `u` is multiplicative and injective (the coefficients of a crossed product are unique), and `a ↦ diag(a,1,1)`
is a homomorphism `R^× → GL_3(R)`. By definition `K_1(R) = GL(R)/E(R)`, and by Whitehead's lemma
`diag(a,a^{-1},1) ∈ E_3(R)` for `a ∈ R^×`; also `E(R) ∩ GL_3(R) ⊇ EL_3(R)`. The class `[diag(u_γ,1,1)] = [u_γ]`, so the
matrix lies in the elementary subgroup exactly when that class vanishes (stability: for these rings
`EL_3(R) = GL_3(R) ∩ E(R)` is not claimed, so read the criterion as: `[u_γ] = 0` is necessary, and sufficient for
`γ ∈ [Δ,Δ]` by the displayed identity below). The map `γ ↦ [u_γ]` is a homomorphism into an abelian group, so its
kernel is normal and contains `[Δ,Δ]`.
For `γ = [a,b]` with `a,b ∈ R^×`:
```
diag(a,a^{-1},1)·diag(b,b^{-1},1)·diag((ba)^{-1},ba,1) = diag(aba^{-1}b^{-1},1,1),
```
each factor elementary by Whitehead, so `diag(u_{[α,β]},1,1) ∈ EL_3(R)`. ∎

**Model test.** `Δ = Z` and `X` an infinite minimal `Z`-subshift: `K_1(R) = Z` and `[u] = −1`
(`binary-subshift-ring-k1-is-half-orbit-index`, `shift-unit-of-subshift-crossed-product-is-not-elementary`), and indeed
`[Z,Z] = 1`. So the criterion is sharp, not vacuous.

## 2. Wreath products cannot supply the perfect overgroup

**Lemma 2.** Let `Λ` act on a nonempty set `Ω`, and `Δ = Γ ≀_Ω Λ = (⊕_Ω Γ) ⋊ Λ`. Then
`Δ^{ab} ≅ Γ^{ab} × Λ^{ab}` when `Λ` is transitive on `Ω`, and for the copy `Γ_ω ≤ Δ`,
`Γ_ω ∩ [Δ,Δ] = [Γ,Γ]_ω`. In particular `Γ ⊆ [Δ,Δ]` forces `Γ` perfect.

*Proof.* Abelianizing kills the `Λ`-action, so the image of `⊕_Ω Γ` in `Δ^{ab}` is the largest abelian quotient of the
coinvariants `(⊕_Ω Γ)_Λ`. Transitivity identifies all coordinates, so the coinvariants are `Γ`, and `Δ^{ab} = Γ^{ab} ×
Λ^{ab}`. The composite `Γ_ω → Δ → Δ^{ab} → Γ^{ab}` is the abelianization map of `Γ`, whose kernel is `[Γ,Γ]`. ∎

**Lemma 3 (the multiplicativity trap).** For `ω ≠ ω'`, the map `γ ↦ γ_ω (γ^{-1})_{ω'}` is a homomorphism iff `Γ` is
abelian. Indeed `γ_ω(γ^{-1})_{ω'} · δ_ω(δ^{-1})_{ω'} = (γδ)_ω ((δγ)^{-1})_{ω'}`, since coordinates commute, and this is
`(γδ)_ω((γδ)^{-1})_{ω'}` iff `γδ = δγ`.

This is the same failure as `whitehead-diagonal-map-is-not-multiplicative` (sk-subgroups): an inverse placed in a second
coordinate reverses the order. Every "shift by one coordinate" reduction I tried dies here, and Lemma 2 says the
obstruction is not an artifact of the map: no wreath overgroup puts a non-perfect `Γ` into its derived subgroup.

## 3. Perfect amenable groups

**Theorem A.** Let `Γ` be a f.g. perfect amenable group. Then `Γ` embeds in an infinite, f.g., simple group with
property (T) that is `F_2`-linear sofic.

*Proof.* Take a free minimal subflow `X ⊆ 2^Γ` (`gjs-countable-groups-have-free-minimal-subflows`) and put
`R = LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Γ`, a countable ring, finitely generated by the indicator of `[x_e = 1]` and the units of a generating
set. Then:
- `R` is the Steinberg algebra of a Hausdorff ample principal minimal groupoid with compact infinite unit space
  (`crossed-products-are-transformation-steinberg-algebras`), so it is simple
  (`steinberg-algebra-simple-iff-minimal-effective`) with centre `F_2` (`minimal-effective-steinberg-algebras-have-scalar-centre`).
- `G = EL_3(R)` has property (T) (`elementary-groups-over-fg-rings-have-property-t`) and is simple modulo its centre
  (`steinberg-elementary-groups-are-simple-mod-centre`); over `F_2` the centre is `{1}`, so `G` is simple. It is
  infinite because `e_{12}(LC(X,F_2))` is.
- `Γ` is amenable and acts minimally, so `R` has a faithful Sylvester rank function from Følner truncations
  (`amenable-minimal-crossed-products-have-faithful-rank-models`, reviewed PASS), and `G` is `F_2`-linear sofic
  (`rank-modelled-simple-rings-give-linear-sofic-projective-el`).
- `Γ = [Γ,Γ]`, so `γ ↦ diag(u_γ,1,1)` embeds `Γ` in `EL_3(R)` by Lemma 1. ∎

**Examples not covered by the residually finite theorem.** `[[T]]'`, the derived topological full group of a minimal
`Z`-subshift, is f.g. (Matui), simple hence perfect, amenable (Juschenko–Monod), and not residually finite. The same
applies to every f.g. simple amenable group. sk-subgroups already put `[[T]]'` inside the note's own `G_X`; Theorem A is
the general statement.

**What is missing for all amenable `Γ`.** A f.g. perfect amenable overgroup. Lemma 2 rules out wreath products, and the
peer's Ore step uses finite permutation groups, so it needs residual finiteness. I did not find a substitute.

## 4. Why this route cannot give soficity

**Lemma 4.** Let `Φ_i: S → GL_{N_i}(F_q)` be maps with rank defect `ε_i = max rk(Φ_i(st) − Φ_i(s)Φ_i(t)) / N_i → 0` and
`N_i → ∞`, and let `π_i` be the induced permutation representation on `F_q^{N_i}` (degree `q^{N_i}`). If
`Φ_i(st) ≠ Φ_i(s)Φ_i(t)` then the normalized Hamming distance between `π_i(s)π_i(t)` and `π_i(st)` is
`1 − q^{−r}` with `r = rk(Φ_i(st)−Φ_i(s)Φ_i(t)) ≥ 1`, so it is at least `1 − 1/q`.

*Proof.* For `g ∈ GL_N(F_q)`, the fixed points of `g` on `F_q^N` are `ker(g − 1)`, of size `q^{N − rk(g−1)}`. Apply this
to `g = Φ_i(st)^{-1}Φ_i(s)Φ_i(t)`, whose fixed-point set has the same size as that of `Φ(st) − Φ(s)Φ(t)`'s kernel. ∎

So the vector action converts "rank-small" into "Hamming-almost-everything": the rank models of §3 give no sofic
approximation, whatever `ε_i → 0` does. The same computation is the gap in `gap-sofic-approximations-are-local-embeddings`
(sk-fp-sofic-a): a sofic approximation whose image sits in finite groups where every nontrivial element moves at least a
fixed fraction `δ` of the points is exactly multiplicative and injective on any finite set, so its group is LEF. Linear,
affine and projective actions over `F_q` all have such a gap.

**Consequence.** For a non-LEF `EL_n(R)`, a sofic approximation must use permutation models whose transitive constituents
have fixed-point ratios tending to `1` on some nontrivial element, i.e. alternating-type constituents in the sense of the
minimal-degree classification. No construction on main produces such models for these groups, and Proposition F of
sk-fp-sofic-b kills the remaining amenable-action route. This is why the lane's target is open rather than hard.

## 5. The target, with its two gates

`sofic-groups-embed-in-simple-kazhdan-sofic-groups` (open): every f.g. sofic group embeds in an infinite simple Kazhdan
sofic group. Through this envelope it needs both of:
- **(G1) a perfect (or `K_1`-invisible) sofic overgroup** of the given group, f.g. (Lemma 1, Lemma 2);
- **(G2) a sofic non-LEF `EL_n(R)`**, which by §4 needs approximations outside the algebraic families. Even one example
  of an infinite simple Kazhdan sofic non-LEF group is not known.

Gate (G2) is the harder one, and it is exactly what sk-fp-sofic-a/b's firewalls describe. For the weaker conclusion
"linear sofic" only (G1) remains, and §3 settles it for perfect groups.
