# Audit: strong pure infiniteness solves the traceless LXXVI corridor

Date: 2026-08-30

## Result

Every separable strongly purely infinite C\*-algebra is pure and the actual
first-factor map

```text
Cu(A) -> Cu(A tensor_min Z)
```

is an isomorphism.  More generally, it is enough that both `A` and
`A tensor_min Z` be purely infinite in the Kirchberg--Rordam sense.

## Source audit

Two published structural inputs are used.

1. Kirchberg--Rordam pure infiniteness identifies Cuntz comparison with
   ideal containment for stabilized positive elements: `a` is Cuntz below
   `b` exactly when `a` belongs to the ideal generated by `b`.  The usual
   `Lat(A tensor K)~=Lat(A)` correspondence transports these ideals back to
   `A`.  For separable algebras this identifies `Cu(A)` with the whole
   closed-ideal lattice, since every ideal has a strictly positive element.
2. Kirchberg--Sierakowski,
   [*Filling families and strong pure infiniteness*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08519),
   proves that the minimal tensor product of a strongly purely infinite
   C\*-algebra and an exact C\*-algebra is strongly purely infinite.  It
   applies with the exact algebra `Z`.

The remaining input was already audited in the LXXVI graph: simplicity and
nuclearity of `Z` make `I |-> I tensor Z` an isomorphism from `Lat(A)` onto
`Lat(A tensor Z)`.

## Naturality check

The proof does not merely produce an abstract isomorphism.  If
`Lambda_C([c])=Ideal_C(c)`, then

```text
Lambda_(A tensor Z)(Cu(iota_A)([a]))
 = Ideal_(A tensor Z)(a tensor 1_Z)
 = Ideal_A(a) tensor Z.
```

This is exactly the image of `Lambda_A([a])` under the slice-map ideal
isomorphism.  Hence the map proved invertible is the concrete first-factor
map required in Definition 24, not some unrelated identification of the two
Cuntz semigroups.

## Purity check

Every class in the Cuntz semigroup of a separable purely infinite algebra is
idempotent.  An idempotent ordered semigroup is almost unperforated, and the
choice `y=x` verifies almost divisibility for every compact containment
`x'<<x`.  Thus the strong-pure-infiniteness class genuinely lies within the
hypothesis of Problem LXXVI.

## Distinction from existing lanes

The locally subhomogeneous and continuous-field theorems use stable rank one,
projection cancellation, and explicit pointwise or clutching formulas.  None
of those inputs appears here.  The general coarse-shadow theorem says that
ideals alone cannot decide LXXVI for arbitrary algebras; the present class is
the exact exception because pure infiniteness collapses every Cuntz class to
its generated ideal.

The argument also does not infer Cu-regularity from Z-stability.  Tensoring
with `Z` is used only to form the map in the problem; its target remains
strongly purely infinite by the exact-factor tensor theorem, and naturality
of ideal generation proves invertibility.

## Limitation

This does not address pure algebras with finite or genuinely non-idempotent
Cuntz classes.  Plain pure infiniteness of `A` alone is not promoted to the
claim that `A tensor Z` is purely infinite; the proof records that target
hypothesis explicitly and invokes strong pure infiniteness only as a
published sufficient condition.
