## 1. Statements
**Theorem U.** For every countable set `F` of finitely generated residually finite groups there is an infinite,
finitely generated, simple group `S` with property (T) that is LEF, hence sofic and hyperlinear, and contains a copy of
every member of `F`.

**Corollary U′ (one host for all of them).** There is one infinite, finitely generated, simple Kazhdan LEF group that
contains:
- every finitely presented residually finite group;
- every finitely generated linear group over any field;
- more generally, every finitely generated residually finite group with solvable word problem.

For example it contains `SL_n(Z)` for every `n`, every finitely generated subgroup of `GL_n(C)`, surface groups, and
every finite group. Recalled but not re-verified, and used only as examples through linearity or finite presentation:
- braid groups (Bigelow, Krammer), right-angled Artin groups and Coxeter groups are linear;
- mapping class groups are finitely presented and residually finite (Grossman);
- hyperbolic 3-manifold groups are linear.

**For a non-specialist.** A single finitely generated group with no normal subgroups and Kazhdan's rigidity, all of
whose finite pieces live inside finite groups, holds every finitely generated matrix group at once.

## 2. Proof
1. **Direct sum.** `P = ⊕_i Γ_i` is countable and residually finite. It separates `p ≠ e` through a coordinate where
   `p_i ≠ e` and a finite quotient of `Γ_i`.
2. **Two generators.** Wilson 1980, Theorem A, verbatim (Math. Z. 174, p. 149): "Every countable residually finite group
   G can be embedded in a 2-generator residually finite group Ĝ." So `P ≤ W` with `W` 2-generated and residually
   finite.
3. **Envelope.** Theorem E (sk-universal-embedding-a, 3bcc003284; `rf-groups-embed-in-simple-kazhdan-lef-groups`,
   sk-verify-3 PASS):
   - `W ≤ [Δ,Δ]` for a finitely generated residually finite `Δ` (Ore's commutator theorem, coordinatewise in a product
     of finite symmetric groups);
   - `X_Δ` is a free minimal Toeplitz subshift with periodic window models;
   - `S = EL_3(LC(X_Δ,F_2) ⋊ Δ)` is infinite, finitely generated, simple, Kazhdan and LEF;
   - `w ↦ diag(u_w,1,1)` embeds `W` in `S`.

   sk-strong-3 re-read three steps: the Ore overgroup (even permutations from doubled coset spaces; subgroups of
   products of finite groups are residually finite), freeness at every point (unique non-constant coset `D_m(y)` and
   normality of `Δ_m`), and `diag([a,b],1,1) ∈ E_3`. All three check.
4. So `Γ_i ≤ S` for all `i`. ∎

## 3. Countability of the example classes
- **Finitely presented groups:** countably many finite presentations.
- **Solvable word problem:** a recursive relator set on a finite alphabet, and there are countably many recursive sets.
  The finitely presented residually finite groups belong here (McKinsey 1943, Mal'cev 1958;
  `fp-residually-finite-groups-have-solvable-word-problem`).
- **Linear groups:** `Γ = ⟨g_1,…,g_k⟩ ≤ GL_n(K)` lies in `GL_n(K_0)`, where `K_0` is the field generated by the entries.
  - `K_0` is a finite extension of a rational function field over the prime field, so there are countably many
    isomorphism types of such `K_0`, each countable.
  - There are countably many tuples of matrices over each `K_0`.
  - Residual finiteness is Malcev's theorem (`finitely-generated-linear-groups-are-residually-finite`).

## 4. Sharpness
- **Countable is needed.** A finitely generated group has only countably many finitely generated subgroups. Chong–Wise
  (arXiv:2207.00410, "Varying H yields uncountably many groups up to isomorphism", abstract read via fetch) give
  uncountably many finitely generated residually finite groups. So no finitely generated group contains them all.
- **Residual finiteness cannot be relaxed beyond LEF.** Every subgroup of `S` is LEF. The class of finitely generated
  subgroups of such hosts contains every finitely generated residually finite group and consists of LEF groups.
  Whether it is all finitely generated LEF groups is `lef-groups-embed-in-simple-kazhdan-lef-groups` (open; lanes
  sk-universal-embedding-b, sk-lef-embedding-c/d/e).
- **The word problem of S bounds its subgroups.** A finitely generated subgroup has word problem Turing-reducible to
  that of `S`, through fixed words for its generators. So if `F` contains residually finite groups with unsolvable word
  problem (Meskin), `S` has unsolvable word problem.
