# Referee report (gq-referee-b, citation lens): KMS Minsky groups contain no Z_(l)

**Reviewed:** `kms-minsky-groups-contain-no-z-localized`, its route `-proof`, and the artifact
`gq-gq-infinite-primes-kms-reading.md` (lane gq-infinite-primes, a1c262c67), read on origin/main.

**Source read:** Kharlampovich–Myasnikov–Sapir, arXiv:1204.6506v5, e-print `KMS-322.tex` (1829 lines), the local
copy in `gq/src/kms/`. I counted theorem numbers from `\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}[section]`. Lemma, remark,
corollary, proposition, definition and example all share that counter, and no `\setcounter` touches it. For
Rauzy I read the arXiv abstract of 2002.02540 only.

**Verdict: PASS.** Every quote the node uses matches the tex, and the one imported theorem applies to every
Minsky machine with no extra hypotheses. The proof needs only the `𝒜_p^2𝒜` conjunct, and Lemmas 4.4 and 4.5
prove that conjunct directly. The seven wording fixes below are all non-blocking.

## Quotes and numbers checked against the tex

- **Theorem 4.3(a).** It is the third counter-bearing environment of §4, after Lemma 4.1 (Baumslag–Remeslennikov)
  and Remark 4.2. The tex reads verbatim "The group $G(M)$ belongs to ${\mathcal A}_p^2{\mathcal A}\cap
  {\mathcal Z}{\mathcal N}_{K+1}{\mathcal A}$." **Match.**
- **Hypotheses of 4.3(a): none beyond the setting.** §4.1 opens "Let $M$ be a Minsky machine with $K$ glasses
  and $N+1$ commands" and then says "Also fix a prime $p$ (say, $p=2$)."
  - `U_0` is the set of divisors of `A_0⋯A_K` in the free commutative monoid, so it has `2^(K+1)` elements.
    Then `U = {q_j w}` is finite.
  - So the generating set `L_0 ∪ L_1 ∪ L_2` is finite, and G1–G8 are finitely many relations.
  - The node's "every `G(M)`" (any `M`, any `p`) is therefore in scope.
- **Lemma 4.4, verbatim.** "The subgroup $\la H_1\cup H_2\ra$ of $G$ is metabelian and a semidirect product of the
  Abelian normal subgroup $H_1^{H_2}$ of exponent $p$, and $H_2$." **Match.**
- **Lemma 4.5, verbatim.** "The normal subgroup $T$ of $G$ generated by all the elements $x_{u}, u\in U,$ is
  Abelian of exponent $p$." **Match.**
- **How KMS conclude (a).** They write: "By construction, the group $G$ is a semidirect product of $T$ and the
  metabelian group $H_1^{H_2}\rtimes H_2$. By Lemma \ref{lH}, $G$ is solvable of class 3 and, moreover, belongs to
  ${\mathcal A}_p^2{\mathcal A}$."
  - So KMS use `𝒜_p^2𝒜 = 𝒜_p𝒜_p𝒜`, the product reading.
  - The proof node does not need the semidirect-splitting claim. `G` is generated by `L_0 ⊂ T` together with
    `H_1 ∪ H_2`, so `G/T` is a quotient of `<H_1 ∪ H_2> ∈ 𝒜_p𝒜`, and varieties are closed under quotients.
  - Let `N` be the preimage of the image of `H_1^{H_2}`. Then `N` has exponent dividing `p^2`, and `G/N` is abelian.
  - The node's "≅" is stronger than it needs, but KMS assert it.
- **Product of varieties.** "the class of groups consisting of extensions of groups from $\mathcal U$ by groups
  from $\mathcal V$ is again a variety (the {\em product} ...)". **Match**, but it is in the *second* paragraph of
  §4. See W4.
- **Theorem 4.17.** It is the 17th counter-bearing environment in §4. The tex reads: "If a Minsky machine $M$ is
  sym-universally halting then the group $G(M)$ is residually finite. Its word problem is at least as hard as the
  halting problem for $M$." **Match.**
- **The finite-exponent step of 4.17.** The tex reads: "the image of the set of elements (\ref{e:x}) with $u\in Y_D$
  in $G'(M)$ form a normal $N$ subgroup of $G'(M)$ of finite index (because $T$ is an Abelian group of finite
  exponent $p$)". **Match.**
  - "Finite index" must mean finite index in `T/T'`. The next sentence makes `G'(M)/N` "a semidirect product of a
    finite group and the metabelian group", which is infinite.
  - The artifact's paraphrase "span a subgroup of finite index" is right if read in `T/T'`.
- **Theorems 4.18 and 4.19.** The labels are `t:rfg` and `t:rhog`, and they are the 18th and 19th environments of
  §4.
  - 4.18 says "residually finite finitely presented solvable of class 3 group $G$ with Dehn function greater than
    $f$".
  - 4.19 says "$\rho_G(n)>f(n)$".
  - The introduction, line 297, hard-codes "As a corollary of Theorem 4.20", just after stating `\ref{t:rhog}`.
    So "4.19, cited as 4.20" is **correct**.
- **Theorem 4.16 (Kh81).** It uses a 2-glass machine and the class `𝒵𝒩_3𝒜`. This is consistent, since `K + 1 = 3`.
- **Remark 1.2.** Section 1 has Lemma 1.1 (line 141) and then Remark 1.2 (lines 220–223). The quoted sentence
  "(elementary Abelian)-by-linear since they are solvable of class 3 with the second derived subgroup elementary
  Abelian" is inside it. **Match.**
- **§1.1.7 "What next?".** It is the seventh `\subsubsection` of §1.1: Methods 1–5, then "The main results", then
  "What next?". It reads: "Thus it would be very interesting to find out whether every finitely generated residually
  finite group with solvable word problem embeds into a finitely presented residually finite group. ... hence we can
  consider this paper as a step toward the residually finite version of Higman's theorem." **Match.**
  - No effectiveness hypothesis appears, so "unrestricted form" is right.
- **Rauzy, arXiv:2002.02540v2.** The abstract reads: "We prove that, for a finitely generated residually finite
  group, having solvable word problem is not a sufficient condition to be a subgroup of a finitely presented
  residually finite group." So "refuted that form" is **supported**.
- **Mathematics of the lemma.** It is correct, and gq-referee-a checked it too.
  - Of the named groups, `Z_(l)` contains `Z[1/q]` for every prime `q ≠ l`. Both are torsion-free and not finitely
    generated.
  - `G_l ⊇ Z_(l)`.

## Wording fixes (non-blocking)

- **W1 (false quotation).** Artifact §3.3 puts the phrase "all but finitely many basis elements" in quotation
  marks. That phrase does not occur in `KMS-322.tex`; `grep -i 'all but finitely'` finds nothing.
  - It is an accurate paraphrase of the `Y_D` step, because the complement of `Y_D` indexes finitely many spanning
    elements.
  - Remove the quotation marks, or quote the `Y_D` sentence given above.
- **W2.** Artifact §1 says "`F_2 × F_2`, respectively `S_3 × S_3`". In KMS, `S_3(X)` is "a free solvable group of
  class 3 with free generating set $X$" (lines 363 and 1474), not the symmetric group. Write `S_3(X) × S_3(X)`.
- **W3.** Artifact §1 locates the definition of sym-universally halting at "(§1.1.6; also §1.6)".
  - §1.1.6 is right; its informal version reads "that is their transition graphs are vertex-disjoint unions of
    finite trees".
  - §1.6 is "Methods of proof" and has no such definition.
  - The formal definition is in §2.2, line 485: "We call a deterministic machine $M$ sym-universally halting if
    $\Sym(M)$ halts if it starts with any non-accepted configuration."
- **W4.** The node says "(§4, first paragraph)" for the product of varieties. It is the second paragraph; the first
  defines a variety via Birkhoff.
- **W5.** Artifact §1 labels "G2–G4" as BR-conjoints.
  - G2 is commutation between the blocks `M_i`, `M_j` for `i ≠ j`.
  - Only G3 and G4 are BR-conjoint relations, invoking Lemma 4.1.
- **W6 (scope).** The node says "Its finitely presented residually finite groups are torsion-by-abelian". Better:
  "the finitely presented residually finite groups it constructs". These are `G(M)` in Theorems 4.17–4.19 and
  `G'(M_n)` in §6.
  - §5 also uses `F_2 × F_2`, which is finitely presented and residually finite but not torsion-by-abelian.
  - The conclusion is unaffected: the abelian subgroups of `F_2 × F_2` have rank at most 2 and are finitely
    generated.
- **W7 (source inconsistency; do not cite).** Theorem 4.19 puts its group `G(M_n)` in
  `𝒜_p^2𝒜 ∩ 𝒵𝒩_3𝒜`. That class does not follow from the printed Theorem 4.3(a).
  - `M_n` from the proof of Theorem 3.9 (`t:rho`) has "the $K$ glasses of $M$ plus two more glasses", and `K ≥ 2`.
    Theorem 4.3(a) then gives only `𝒵𝒩_(K+3)𝒜`.
  - §6 says the same thing for this "4-glass" machine: "$G'(M_n)$ belongs to $\mathcal{A}_p^2{\mathcal A}\cap
    {\mathcal Z}{\mathcal N}_5{\mathcal A}$".
  - Likewise, the proof of 4.3(a) opens with "${\mathcal Z}{\mathcal N}_3{\mathcal A}$", but Lemma 4.8 proves
    `𝒵𝒩_(K+1)𝒜`.
  - Neither the node nor the artifact uses the `𝒵𝒩` conjunct, and the `𝒜_p^2𝒜` conjunct holds for every `M`.
    Lanes should not cite the `𝒵𝒩_3` class of Theorem 4.19.
