# Bare Thompson V as a one-word Connes-embeddability test

**Date:** 2026-08-21

This note isolates a nonduplicate Thompson-group lane. It does not use the
repository's Thompson-addressed Clifford tape, a central phase, a BCS
decoder, or a multiplicity return.

## Exact reduction

Let `V=G_(2,1)`, fix `w!=1`, and put

```text
q_w=(1-w)^*(1-w).
```

Dudko--Medynets classify the indecomposable characters of `G_(n,r)` as the
regular character and scalar characters coming from the abelianization
(*Finite factor representations of Higman--Thompson groups*, Groups Geom.
Dyn. 8 (2014), Corollary 3.6(2)). For even `n` the relevant abelianization is
trivial. Hence every trace of `V` is

```text
tau_t=t tau_reg+(1-t)tau_triv,       0<=t<=1,
```

and `tau_t(q_w)=2t`.

If a trace with `t>0` is Connes embeddable, its GNS algebra contains the
regular central summand `L(V)` with nonzero trace. Connes embeddability passes
to corners, so `V` is hyperlinear. Conversely, hyperlinearity of `V` makes
all mixtures Connes embeddable. Therefore

```text
{tau(q_w): tau is a CE trace of V}={0} or [0,2].
```

Every exact finite-dimensional representation of `V` is trivial: a
nontrivial representation would be faithful by simplicity, and its finitely
generated linear image would be residually finite by Mal'cev, contradicting
the absence of nontrivial finite quotients. Thus the exact finite-dimensional
ceiling of `q_w` is zero.

One scope warning is important. For any simple group, one nontrivial
homomorphism into a tracial matrix ultraproduct is already injective, so a
strict ceiling below the regular value would already prove nonhyperlinearity.
The character classification contributes the exact `{0}`-versus-`[0,2]`
description and rules out larger or irregular trace values; it does not
itself supply the missing ceiling.

## The precise open estimate

For one finite presentation of `V`, it suffices to prove constants
`delta,epsilon>0` such that

```text
def_R(U)<delta  =>  ||w(U)-I||_2^2<=2-epsilon
```

in every matrix dimension. Character rigidity then upgrades this strict
ceiling to zero at the CE-trace level. This estimate is equivalent to
nonhyperlinearity of `V`, but concentrates the problem into one scalar moment
and removes the need for a full correction theorem.

## Literature boundary

Fournier-Facio--Rangarajan, *Ulam stability of lamplighters and Thompson
groups*, Math. Ann. 389 (2024), Theorem 1.2, prove uniform stability with a
linear estimate for submultiplicative matrix norms. Their theorem covers the
operator norm and unnormalized Frobenius/Schatten norms. It does not cover
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm with dimension-free constants; Section 7
explicitly leaves Hilbert--Schmidt approximability of Thompson groups open.
The published proof architecture is therefore a possible source of ideas,
not evidence that the displayed estimate already holds.

The location of the failure is sharp. Their final bounded-generation lemma
only multiplies an already available error by the bounded number of
point-stabilizer factors, so that terminal step is compatible with normalized
HS. But the required input is uniform collapse on the germ stabilizer
`V(x)`, and their sketch identifies `V(x)` as a directed union of copies of
`V`. Without their submultiplicative-norm stability theorem, restricting a
pointwise HS model to one of those copies simply reproduces the original
problem. Self-similarity alone gives no decaying parameter.

The obstruction appears earlier than the bounded-generation step. The paper's
asymptotic-cohomology construction linearizes `O(epsilon)` near-identity
errors modulo `o(epsilon)` errors and uses submultiplicativity to make that
quotient abelian. Normalized HS admits a vanishing-rank Pauli block on which

```text
||A-I||_2,||B-I||_2=O(epsilon),
||[A,B]-I||_2=Theta(epsilon).
```

So the tangent quotient remains nonabelian. It becomes abelian under the
additional conditions `||A-I||_op,||B-I||_op=o(1)`, but ordinary hyperlinear
microstates allow exactly the sparse operator-norm outliers that violate those
conditions.

There is also a quantifier mismatch: the cited theorem starts from a uniform
defect over every pair of group elements. Hyperlinear microstates provide
pointwise control, or finite-presentation control on a fixed relator list.
Unbounded relator areas prevent an automatic passage from the latter to the
former.

Pure self-replication has an exact countermodel. Take the direct-limit binary
vector space generated by `e_x=e_(x0)+e_(x1)` and its finitary general linear
group. The locally finite affine semidirect product makes every tree vector a
commuting involution, makes all nonzero tree vectors conjugate, and realizes
each parent as the product of its two children. Every finite prefix tree and
finite leaf-symmetry packet lies in a finite affine subgroup, whose regular
representation keeps the root at squared HS distance `2`. Therefore the live
relation cannot be clone/refinement alone; it must be a genuinely Thompson
overlap or collision absent from the affine model.

The paper's separate permutation proof has another sharp finite-only step: a
permutation shift has an exact return power. Unitary shifts may have arbitrary
spectrum, and the `d`-cycle matrices have no return below exponent `d`.
Choosing a dimension-dependent return makes relator telescoping pay an
unbounded word-area factor, with no defect-versus-dimension rate available
from hyperlinearity.

Heavy-tail averaging does not repair the return. For the unitary whose
spectrum consists uniformly of all `d`th roots, `tr(U^k)` vanishes unless
`d|k`. Hence every summable detector

```text
sum_k c_k |tr(U^k)|^2
```

is at most the tail `sum_(k>=d)c_k`, which tends to zero. Any weighting whose
individual `k`th error costs `A(k)delta` needs `sum c_k A(k)<infinity` to keep
a dimension-free error budget and is subject to the same obstruction. This
leaves only a literal constant-cost infinite family of checks, plus a
V-specific collision that rules out the affine clone process.

## Duplication boundary

The generic character/corner maneuver and its use for a Thompson subgroup of
the Leavitt construction already occur in `notes/NOTEPAD.md`. The Cairn graph
did not previously package the bare-`V` binary support value. The generic
one-positive-element framework is `cyclic-carrier-energy-stability`; this
artifact records the special fact that for `V` merely obtaining a strict
bound below the regular value forces equality with the finite-dimensional
ceiling.

No formal positivity or sum-of-squares inequality valid in every tracial
representation can establish the strict ceiling: the exact regular
representation itself has energy `2`. Any successful proof must distinguish
matricial/Connes-embeddable traces from arbitrary tracial representations.
