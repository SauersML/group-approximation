# Flexible finite-packet exactification remains entropy-null after diffuse anchoring

Exact finite packet orbits were already known to have no quadratic anchored
covering entropy.  The flexible Hilbert--Schmidt stability theorem enlarges
the representation dimension, so two additional pieces are needed before
that exact no-go applies to approximate packet tables.

First, a vanishing-HS tubular stabilizer of a diffuse pin has zero quadratic
covering entropy.  Spectral cuts with small exceptional mass reduce every
approximate stabilizer to a product of block unitary groups; the sum of the
squared block ranks can be made arbitrarily small relative to `d^2`.

Second, an `m=d+o(d)` compression family has only `o(d^2)` Grassmannian
freedom modulo the right `U(d)` action.  Once the compressed pin is fixed,
that right action lies in the tubular diffuse stabilizer and is entropy-null.
Thus subquadratically many exact classes remain entropy-null under flexible
compression.

Gowers--Hatami supplies exactly this geometry for an all-pairs
`epsilon_d`-representation of any finite group: the enlargement is
`O(epsilon_d^2 d)` and the pointwise error is `O(epsilon_d)`, uniformly in
the group.  Therefore growing finite-packet models with vanishing complete
table defect cannot generate positive anchored entropy unless the exact
representation-class count itself is already quadratic-exponential.  The
remaining wreath-entropy lane must exploit the presentation-to-moving-table
seam or genuinely nonlinear models far from every complete finite packet.
