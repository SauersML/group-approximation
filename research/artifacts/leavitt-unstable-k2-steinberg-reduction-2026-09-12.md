## 1. Stable K-theory

`abc-leavitt-path-k-theory-exact-sequence` quotes Ara–Brustenga–Cortiñas, Theorem 7.6: for a regular supercoherent
ring `k`, every field included, and a row-finite quiver `E`, there is a long exact sequence

```text
K_n(k)^(E^0 \ Sink(E)) --(1 - N_E^t)--> K_n(k)^(E^0) -> K_n(L_k(E)) -> K_(n-1)(k)^(E^0 \ Sink(E)) -> ...
```

For `L_k(1,2)` the quiver is one vertex with two loops, so `N_E = (2)` and `1 - N_E^t = -1`, an isomorphism of
`K_n(k)` for every `n` (no transpose issue for a `1 x 1` matrix). Exactness gives `K_n(L_k(1,2)) = 0` for every `n`.
This is the `R = k` case of `ara-cortinas-leavitt-tensor-k-theory-vanishes` ("K_*(L_2) = 0", Ara–Cortiñas
Proposition 6.2). It covers `k = F_2` and `k = F_3`.

## 2. Binary: `K_2(3, R_2) = 0`

This is (KH2) of `leavitt-steinberg-map-iso-from-rank-three` at `r = 3` (Khanh arXiv:2609.08428v1, Theorem 5.4).
The proof of Theorem 5.4 was read from the saved v1 PDF and is re-derived here at `r = 3`.

**Notation.** `S_r = St_r(R)`, `G_r = GL_r(R)`, `φ_r : S_r -> G_r`, `N_r = ker φ_r = K_2(r,R)`, and
`j_r : S_r -> S_(r+1)` the standard inclusion.

**Step 1: `j_3(N_3) = 1` in `S_4`.**
- `S_3` is perfect: `x_ij(a) = [x_ik(a), x_kj(1)]` for `{i,j,k} = {1,2,3}`.
- `φ_3` maps onto `E_3(R) = G_3`.
- The five-term sequence of `1 -> N_3 -> S_3 -> G_3 -> 1` gives `H_2(G_3) -> N_3/[S_3,N_3] -> H_1(S_3)`. Here
  `H_2(G_3) = 0` by (KH1) and `H_1(S_3) = 0`, so `N_3 = [S_3, N_3]`.
- Lemma 5.2 ("for any unital ring B and m >= 3, j_m(N_m(B)) is central in St_(m+1)(B)") makes `j_3(N_3)` central in
  `S_4`. So `j_3(N_3) = j_3([S_3,N_3]) = [j_3(S_3), j_3(N_3)] = 1`.

**Step 2: `N_4 = 1`.** Theorem 5.1 at `n = 4`, quoted in item 2 of `khanh-acyclicity-and-steinberg-criteria`:
- hypothesis (1), `GL_3 = E_3` and `GL_2 = E_2`, is Lemma 5.3;
- hypothesis (2) is Step 1;
- hypothesis (3), simple connectivity of `|X_4(R)|`, is Proposition 4.2.

So `φ_4` is an isomorphism.

**Step 3: the refinement `D_3 : S_3 -> S_4` is an isomorphism.**
- In Khanh's notation the Leavitt elements satisfy `e*e = f*f = 1`, `e*f = f*e = 0` and `ee* + ff* = 1`.
  `T_3 : R^3 -> R^4` sends `(x_1, x_2, x_3)` to `(x_1, x_2, e*x_3, f*x_3)`, and `U_3 : R^4 -> R^3` sends
  `(x_1, x_2, y, z)` to `(x_1, x_2, ey + fz)`. Then `U_3 T_3 = I_3` and `T_3 U_3 = I_4`, so `α_3(A) = T_3 A U_3` is a
  unital ring isomorphism `M_3(R) -> M_4(R)` and a group isomorphism `G_3 -> G_4`.
- `α_3(E_11) = E_11`, `α_3(E_22) = E_22`, and `α_3(E_33)` is the block `[[e*e, e*f], [f*e, f*f]] = I_2` in rows and
  columns 3, 4, that is `E_33 + E_44`.
- Khanh's refinement in the proof of Theorem 5.4: `D_3 X_ij(a) = X_ij(a)` for `i, j < 3`, `D_3 X_i3(a) = X_i3(ae) X_i4(af)` and
  `D_3 X_3j(a) = X_3j(e*a) X_4j(f*a)`. On generators `φ_4 D_3 = α_3 φ_3`. For `i < 3`,
  `(1 + ae E_i3)(1 + af E_i4) = 1 + ae E_i3 + af E_i4 = α_3(1 + a E_i3)`, and the `X_3j` case is the same on columns.
- **Voronetsky, read.** A. Voronetsky, *Centrality of K_2-functor revisited*, JPAA 225 (2021) 106547,
  arXiv:2004.08551v2, pp. 1–11. The setting is a unital algebra `R` over a commutative ring with a complete family of
  Morita-equivalent orthogonal idempotents `e_1, ..., e_n`, and `St(R, Φ)` generated by `x_ij(a)`, `a ∈ e_i R e_j`,
  with the Steinberg relations. Merging `e_(n-1)` and `e_n` into `e_∞ = e_(n-1) + e_n` gives the coarse family `Φ/α`
  and `F_α : St(R, Φ/α) -> St(R, Φ)`:
  - `x_ij(a) ↦ x_ij(a)` for `i, j < n-1`;
  - `x_i∞(a) ↦ x_(i,n-1)(a e_(n-1)) x_in(a e_n)`;
  - `x_∞j(a) ↦ x_(n-1,j)(e_(n-1) a) x_nj(e_n a)`.

  Lemma 5: `F_α` is an epimorphism for `n >= 3`. Proposition 1: `F_α` is an isomorphism in `Pro(Grp)` for `n >= 4`.
  Page 9: "If S = {1}, then this means that F_α: St(R,Φ/α) → St(R,Φ) is a surjection for n ≥ 3 and a bijection for
  n ≥ 4. The proof also shows that the group St(R,Φ) is perfect for n ≥ 3."
- **Application.** Take `M_4(R)` with the matrix units `E_11, ..., E_44`, `n = 4` and `S = {1}`. Each `E_ii` lies in
  `M_4(R) E_jj M_4(R)`, so the family is Morita equivalent, and `St(M_4(R), Φ) = S_4`. The coarse family
  `(E_11, E_22, E_33 + E_44)` is `α_3` of the standard family of `M_3(R)`, so `St(α_3)` identifies `S_3` with
  `St(M_4(R), Φ/α)`. It sends `X_i3(a)` to `x_i∞(ae E_i3 + af E_i4)`, and `F_α` sends that to `X_i3(ae) X_i4(af)`.
  Likewise for `X_3j`. So `D_3 = F_α ∘ St(α_3)`, and `D_3` is bijective.

**Step 4: `N_3 = 1`.** Since `α_3` is injective, `φ_4 D_3 = α_3 φ_3` gives `N_3 = D_3^(-1)(N_4)`. Steps 2 and 3 give
`N_3 = 1`. Khanh's Theorem 5.4 runs the same step `D_r(N_r) = N_(r+1)` at every `r >= 3`.

**Answer to (b).** `K_2(3, R_2) = 0` and `K_2(R_2) = 0`, so the stability map is injective because both groups vanish.
The rank-two kernel `ker(St_2(R) -> E_2(R))` is not addressed. Khanh makes no assertion there, and the firewall does
not use it.

## 4. The Steinberg residue, and questions (c), (d)

With `N_3 = 1`, `R^x ≅ S_3` is presented by generators `x_ij(a)`, `i != j` in `{1,2,3}`, `a ∈ R`, and the relations
- (St1) `x_ij(a) x_ij(b) = x_ij(a+b)`;
- (St2) `[x_ij(a), x_jk(b)] = x_ik(ab)` for distinct `i, j, k`;
- (St3) `[x_ij(a), x_kl(b)] = 1` for `j != k`, `i != l`.

Each instance holds in a finite root-generated subgroup of `S_3` of order at most `p^3`:
- (St1) in `{x_ij(λa + μb) : λ, μ ∈ F_p}` and (St3) in `{x_ij(λa) x_kl(μb)}`, abelian of order at most `p^2`;
- (St2) in `{x_ij(λa) x_jk(μb) x_ik(νab) : λ, μ, ν ∈ F_p}`. By (St3) `x_ik(ab)` commutes with `x_ij(a)` and `x_jk(b)`,
  and `x_jk(b) x_ij(a) = x_ik(-ab) x_ij(a) x_jk(b)`, so this is a quotient of the Heisenberg group over `F_p`.

Over `F_2` this is the bound 8 of Remark 3.2. Over `F_3` it is 27.

**Option 2 is empty.** Every relation of `R^x` is a consequence of (St1)–(St3). A relation-only proof that some class
of models of `R^x` kills the two-root defect, or has `theta < 1`, has no element of `K_2(3,R)` to use. It must use a
cycle of these finite relations that no graph of locally finite subgroups routes (option 1), or an input about all
models (option 3).

**(c).** There is no element, so there are no symbols and no global element.

**(d).** There is no candidate relation to test against `graphs-of-locally-finite-groups-carry-regular-rank-models`,
`fock-local-letters-extend-level-models-with-disjoint-commutation`,
`locally-finite-hnn-data-allow-independent-cylinder-defects` or `one-shared-root-plus-dyadic-depth-has-ut4-countermodels`.

## 5. The hexagon presentation and its limits

**Proposition 5.1.** Let `p ∈ {2,3}` and `R = R_p`. For a total order `π` of `{1,2,3}` let
`U_π = <x_ij(R) : i <_π j> <= S_3`. Let `Y_hex` be the graph of groups on the hexagon of orders, where adjacent
orders differ by one adjacent transposition, with vertex groups `U_π`, edge groups `U_π ∩ U_π'` and inclusions as
edge maps. Take a maximal tree omitting one edge, with letter `t`. Then `R^x ≅ π_1(Y_hex)/<<t>>`.

*Proof.*
- (SPG2) of `steinberg-group-is-glued-over-the-permutohedron`, for algebras over a finite field and `n = 3`:
  `S_3 = colim U_π` over all `U_π` and their pairwise intersections. By (SPG1) each intersection is generated by the
  roots positive in both orders.
- Order the hexagon `123, 213, 231, 321, 312, 132`. The root `(i,j)` is positive on exactly the three consecutive
  vertices where `i` precedes `j`; for `(1,2)` these are `312, 132, 123`. Adjacent orders share two roots, orders at
  distance two share one, and opposite orders share none. The one-root intersection at distance two lies in both edge
  groups through the middle vertex of its arc. So the edges of the hexagon already make every identification.
- The colimit of a graph of groups with injective edge maps is its `π_1` modulo the letters of the edges off a maximal
  tree. The hexagon has six edges, and a maximal tree has five.
- `N_3 = 1` (Sections 2 and 3), and `GL_3(R) ≅ R^x`. ∎

**Calibration on the binary host.** Each `U_π(R_2)` is a countable locally finite subgroup of `R_2^x`, and every edge
unit is `1`. So Theorem 1.1 of the firewall artifact applies to `Y_hex`. It gives a nontrivial homomorphism
`σ : π_1(Y_hex) -> M^x` into the units of a weakly finite `F_2`-algebra, with the free profile on every finite subgroup
of every `U_π`, and `σ(t) = [π_(n,e)]`, a permutation matching the two free actions of the closing edge group.
- Every instance of (St1)–(St3), read inside one `U_π`, holds under `σ`. The two roots positive at both ends of the
  closing edge have two lifts in `π_1(Y_hex)`, conjugate by `t`.
- A homomorphism of `π_1(Y_hex)` descends to `R^x` exactly when it kills `t`. A nontrivial weakly finite one that
  kills `t` is a nontrivial weakly finite representation of `R_2^x`, which would refute the representation form of
  `binary-complement-corner-has-no-weakly-finite-image`. Theorem 1.1 does not choose `σ(t) = 1`: at each stage it matches
  the two free actions of the closing edge group by some permutation, and nothing in the construction makes that
  permutation the identity.
- So an argument for that representation form fails if its inputs are relations of `π_1(Y_hex)`, rank calculus,
  nontriviality and fixed-point-freeness, as in Corollary 1.3 of the firewall artifact. On this presentation the
  residue is the one closing relation `t = 1`. It is the rank-model counterpart of the hexagon holonomy in
  `steinberg-group-is-glued-over-the-permutohedron`.

**No `D_A` statement.**
- Corollary 1.2 of the firewall artifact needs the unit root pairs `ι_(A_i)(x_12(1))`, `ι_(A_i)(x_23(1))` of the
  cylinders in one vertex group. The `U_π` are unipotent radicals of `S_3`, and nothing here places a cylinder frame
  in any of them.
- Adding a frame vertex `L_0` by a tree edge along `K = L_0 ∩ U_π` gives `π_1(Y_hex) *_K L_0`. Killing `t` leaves
  `R^x *_K L_0`, not `R^x`: the identifications of `L_0` with words in the `U_π` are further relations.
- So the hexagon gives no `theta = 1` statement for `D_A`, and no new firewall for
  `leavitt-disjoint-cylinder-defects-strictly-submultiplicative`.

**Not checked.** Theorem 1.1 is stated over `F_2`. A ternary analogue on `Y_hex` was not checked.

**Earlier hexagon work.** `research/artifacts/leavitt-defect-floor-experiment-2026-08-26.md` records that a radius-`r`
hexagon colimit is strictly freer than `St_3(R)`. `radius-one-hexagon-probe-omits-regular-packet` shows that
one radius-one gauge probe is a thin packet, and `isolated-regular-a2-hexagon-has-free-external-holonomy` shows that
one isolated regular braid hexagon has no external gauge obstruction. Proposition 5.1 is the full-radius statement: the colimit of the whole `U_π(R)` is `R^x`.
