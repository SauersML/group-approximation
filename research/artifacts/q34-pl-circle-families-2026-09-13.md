## 1. New mechanism: orbit actions of piecewise-linear groups

**Hypotheses.** `A ⊆ R` is a subring. `P` is a finitely generated group of positive units of `A`. Every positive element of `A` is a finite sum of elements of `P`, and `1 = q + q'` with `q, q' ∈ P`.

| group | action | pairs | stabilizer | type (A) iff | node |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `F(A,P)` (interval) | on `A ∩ (0,1)` | one orbit of 2-subsets | `F × F` | `F` finitely presented | `pl-interval-and-circle-groups-have-type-a-orbit-actions` |
| `T(A,P)` (circle) | on `A ∩ [0,1)` | 2-transitive | `F(A,P)` | `T` finitely presented (with `F` f.g.) | same |
| `V(A,P)` (rearrangements) | on `A ∩ [0,1)` | 2-transitive | `<h_p, h, copy of V>` | `V` finitely presented | `pl-rearrangement-groups-have-two-transitive-orbit-actions` |

**Instances checked in the proofs.**
- `(Z[1/2], <2>)`: Thompson; sanity only.
- `(Z[1/6], <2,3>)`: Stein.
- `(Z[τ], <τ>)`, `τ = (√5−1)/2`: irrational slope. Hypothesis (S) holds via `aφ^N = m_N + n_N τ` with Fibonacci–Binet coefficients.

**Why it matters for Question 3.4.**
- BFFHZ (arXiv:2503.21882v2, l.246) name circle groups as the finitely presented simple groups that are not MIF: "such as Thompson's group $T$ and certain variants, including torsion-free examples". There Theorem C has nothing to act on.
- The orbit actions give type (A) directly, with no MIF.

## 3. Lodha's group `S` (arXiv:1710.06220v2, `FPSimple.tex` on MSI)

**Pinned from the source.**
- l.89–100: "$S$ is a finitely presented, infinite, simple group", acting faithfully on the circle.
- l.108–118: `S = <T, s>`, with `s` equal to `2t/(1+t)` on `[0,1]` and `2/(3−t)` on `[1,2]`, and the identity elsewhere.
- l.141–157: `T` is the piecewise-`PSL_2(Z)` group of `R ∪ {∞}`, with breakpoints in `Q ∪ {∞}`.
- l.1443–1444: "$S$ is generated by $G_0',T$".
- Cantor model l.357–380:
  - `y(00η) = 0y(η)`, `y(01η) = 10y^{-1}(η)`, `y(1η) = 11y(η)`;
  - l.413: `S = <x, x_1, p_0, y_{10} y_{110}^{-1}>`.

**Hand remarks, not landed.**
1. **`S` preserves `QP^1`.** Every generator has rational coefficients and rational breakpoints; the matrices of `s` are `[[2,0],[1,1]]` and `[[0,2],[−1,3]]`. `PSL_2(Z) ≤ T` is transitive on `QP^1`, and `QP^1` is dense, so the action is faithful.
2. **Reduction to one clause.** Assume `Stab_T(∞)`, Thurston's `F`, is transitive on `Q` (standard, not re-checked). Then `S` acts 2-transitively on `QP^1`. Since `S` is finitely presented, `two-transitive-action-reduces-type-a-to-two-clauses` makes this action of type (A) exactly when **`Stab_S(∞)` is finitely generated**. That is the precise remaining clause for `S`.
3. **`Stab_S(∞)` is larger than the translation-germ groups.** With `l(t) = −1/t`, the element `l s l^{-1}` fixes `∞` and has germ `t ↦ t/2 − 1/2` at `−∞`. The germ group at `∞` is therefore affine, not just translations. So the stabilizer argument of §1 would need a germ group together with a support-compressing conjugator inside `S`.
4. **A Cantor-space host.** One `D` with a type (A) action through `fp-full-binary-cantor-groups-have-type-a-actions` would be a finitely presented full group containing standard V and `y_{10}y_{110}^{-1}`. From the definition, `y^2(0000η) = 0y^2(η)`, and in general `⟨y⟩` has infinitely many restrictions along 0-cones. So finite-nucleus rational similarity hosts do not obviously contain `S`; BBMZ's exact definitions were not re-read.

## 4. Rattaggi-type lattices

**What Le Boudec's argument uses** (BFFHZ l.491, quoted on `burger-mozes-simple-lattices-are-highly-transitive`):
- (a) a projection dense in a closed subgroup of `Aut(T)` that is 2-transitive on `∂T`;
- (b) no direct product of three nontrivial subgroups;
- (c) FLMMS22 Theorem B.

**Hand remarks.**
- For a torsion-free lattice acting freely and cocompactly on a product of two trees, `cd ≤ 2`. Three commuting nontrivial subgroups would give `Z^3`, so (b) holds.
- The argument therefore transfers verbatim to any finitely presented simple torsion-free irreducible lattice whose projection closure is boundary 2-transitive.

**Not checked:**
- the local actions of Rattaggi's examples;
- the local-to-boundary 2-transitivity criterion;
- FLMMS22 Theorem B at source (their TeX was not located on MSI within the search limits).
