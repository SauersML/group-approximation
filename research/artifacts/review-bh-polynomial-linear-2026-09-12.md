## 1. Zaremsky's theorem

Source: M. C. B. Zaremsky, arXiv:2405.09722, TeX source.
- **Theorem 1.1** (l.122): "Every finitely presented self-similar group embeds in a
  finitely presented simple group, and hence satisfies the Boone--Higman
  conjecture."
- **Definition** (l.159--160): "A subgroup `G <= Aut(T_d)` is *self-similar* if its
  image in `S_d wr_d Aut(T_d)` ... is contained in `S_d wr_d G`."
- **Hypotheses.** `G` is finitely presented and acts faithfully on a rooted
  `d`-regular tree, with every state in `G`. There is no contracting or transitivity
  hypothesis. The proof (l.222--237) passes to another faithful self-similar
  representation on some `T_(d')`, so that `V_(d')(G)` has finite abelianization.
- **Use.** Route step 5 applies it to `G = R^n x| E_n(R)`, using §2 and §3--4 below.

**PASS.**

## 2. Self-similarity through parameter coordinates

The node is `polynomial-parameter-affine-groups-are-self-similar`, with
`R = Z[1/m][t_1..t_k]`, a prime `p` not dividing `m`, and the action
`(g,b)·(v,s) = (g(s)v + b(s), s)` on `Z_p^n x Z_p^k`. Checked by hand:
- **Tree.** The entries lie in `Z_p[t]`, so the image mod `p^j` depends only on
  `(v,s)` mod `p^j`.
- **States.** Write `v = e + p v'` and `s = a + p s'`, and let `σ_a` be
  `t_j -> a_j + p t_j`. Then `g(s) = σ_a(g)(s')`, so
  `g(s)v + b(s) = c + p(σ_a(g)(s') v' + w(s'))`, with
  `w = (σ_a(g)e + σ_a(b) - c)/p`. This is the node's state.
- **`w` lies in `R^n`.**
  - The expansion `f(a+pt) = Σ_α p^|α| t^α (∂^α f)(a)/α!` has Taylor
    coefficients in `Z[1/m]`, so `σ_a(f) ≡ f(a)` mod `pR`.
  - `g(a)e + b(a) - c` lies in `pZ[1/m]^n` by the choice of `c`.
- **`Γ` is preserved.** `σ_a` is a ring endomorphism, so it maps `E_n(R)` into
  `E_n(R)` and `SL_n(R)` into `SL_n(R)`.
- **Faithful.** A polynomial over `Q` that vanishes on `Z_p^k` is zero. So an element
  acting trivially has `b = 0` and `g = I`.

**PASS.** Landed note: 7f0fa6048.

## 3. The affine extension

The node is `affine-extension-of-fp-elementary-group-is-fp`, with presentation (E)
and (C1)--(C3). Its proof was checked step by step:
- **A.** `[x_ij(r), τ_k] = 1` for `k != j`.
- **B.** `y^k_i(r)` commutes with every `τ_l`.
- **F1.** Independence of the auxiliary index.
- **F2.** Additivity, and `y_i(1) = τ_i`.
- **F3.** Commutation.
- **F4.** `x_ab(r') y_i(r) x_ab(r')^-1 = y_i(r) y_a(r'r)`.

**Conclusion.**
- Let `N` be the normal closure of the `τ_i`. By F3 and F4, `N` is generated by the
  `y_i(r)`, and by F2 and F3 it is an abelian quotient of `R^n`.
- The map to `R^n x| E_n(R)` sends `y_i(r)` to the translation by `r e_i`, so `N`
  maps isomorphically onto `R^n`.
- Killing the `τ_i` leaves (E), so the quotient by `N` is `E_n(R)`.
- Each step needs one index outside a set of at most three, hence `n >= 4`.

**PASS.** Landed note: 9b961631a.

## 4. `E_n` over polynomial S-integers

Source: U. Rehmann and C. Soulé, *Finitely presented groups of matrices*, LNM 551
(1976). Pages 164--165 were read from the scan, and the OCR was checked against it.
- **Theorem I.** "Assume `A` is finitely generated commutative `Z`-algebra with 1,
  with Krull dimension `d`, and `n` an integer greater or equal to `d+3`. If
  furthermore `K_1(A)` and `K_2(A)` are finitely generated, then `GL_n(A)` is
  finitely presented."
- **Remark.** "If a regular ring `A` satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem, so do
  `A[t]`, `A[t,t^-1]`."
- **§2.** For `n >= d+3`, the maps `GL_n(A)/E_n(A) -> K_1(A)` and
  `Ker(St_n(A) -> E_n(A)) -> K_2(A)` "are isomorphisms" (Bass; Dennis, Stein and
  van der Kallen).
- **Lemma.** In an exact sequence `1 -> G_1 -> G_2 -> G_3 -> 1` with `G_1` finitely
  presented, `G_2` is finitely presented if and only if `G_3` is.
- **Theorem 2.** "Let `A` be as in Th. I, `Φ` a reduced irreducible root system ...
  if the rank of `Φ` is greater or equal to 3, `St(Φ,A)` is finitely presented."

**The chain for `A = Z[1/m][t_1..t_k]`.**
- `d = k+1`, and `A` is regular.
- `K_1(A)` and `K_2(A)` are finitely generated, by homotopy invariance and the case
  `Z[1/m]`.
- Since `n >= k+4 = d+3`, `St_n(A)` is finitely presented and `K_2(n,A) ≅ K_2(A)` is
  finitely generated.
- So `E_n(A)` is finitely presented.

**PASS.** Two GAPs were fixed in 610d22972:
- Import 1 now cites the primary source, Theorem 2.
- The struck citation for homotopy invariance (Weibel III, Ex. 5.2.3) did not
  support the step. It was replaced by Weibel V, Thm 6.3 and IV, Cor 1.7.1.

## 5. The embedding step

Route step 1, as landed in aaae1b31c: "`g -> diag(g, g^-1)` is an injective
homomorphism `GL_N(R) -> E_(2N)(R)` (Whitehead's lemma; Weibel, *K-book* III,
Ex. 1.2.1)."

**FAIL.** The map is not a homomorphism.
- **Counterexample.** Take `g = e_12(1)` and `h = e_21(1)`. The image of `gh` has
  second block `h^-1 g^-1`, but the product of the images has `g^-1 h^-1`. These
  differ.
- **What Ex. 1.2.1 gives.** Only that `E_(2N)(R)` contains each `diag(g, g^-1)`.

**Fix** (ea5f86557 route, 73d270ae5 claim, 2243f9b4c artifact):
- **The map.** `g -> diag(g, (g^T)^-1, I_(n-2N))` is an injective homomorphism into
  `SL_n(R)`.
- **`SL_n(R) = E_n(R)` for `n >= k+3`.**
  - The stable range is at most `d+1` (Weibel III, Rem. 5.5.2).
  - Vaserstein's theorem (III, Rem. 1.2.5) gives `GL_n/E_n = K_1` for `n >= d+2`.
  - `K_1(R) = K_1(Z[1/m])` (III, Thm 3.8, applied `k` times), and that group is
    `Z[1/m]^×` (III, Ex. 1.3.5).
  - So `SK_1(R) = 0`.
- **Nothing downstream changes.** `n = max(k+4, 2N)` is unchanged.

## 6. Restriction of scalars and (R)

**Restriction of scalars.** For a finite projective extension of `Z[1/m][t]`, left
multiplication gives a unital ring embedding into matrices over the base, as a
corner of a free module. **PASS.**

**(R)** is `char-zero-linear-groups-embed-in-polynomial-s-integer-groups`, proved
through `char-zero-polynomial-embedding-via-noether-normalization`.
- **Coefficients such as `Z[i]`.**
  - The algebraic part is removed first. `K_0` has degree `d` over `Q(t)`, and a
    `Q(t)`-basis embeds `M_N(K_0)` in `M_(Nd)(Q(t))`.
  - Only then is a ring chosen, so no ring of integers enters.
- **The ring actually used.**
  - `A = Z[t][1/f]` and `A' = A[1/c]`.
  - The host is `P = Z[1/c][s_1..s_k]`, after the Nagata substitution
    `s_i = t_i - u^(D^i)` with `u = 1/f`.
  - `c` is an integer, so only an integer is inverted in the host. The denominator
    `f` is absorbed into `u`, which is integral over `P`.
- **Regularity and CM, for `A'` and `P` themselves.**
  - Both are localizations of polynomial rings over `Z`, whose local rings are
    regular (00OQ, 031E).
  - So `A'_q` is regular, hence Cohen--Macaulay (00NQ), and `P_p` is regular.
- **Heights at every prime.**
  - `P` is a UFD (034V, 0BC1), hence normal.
  - `P ⊂ A'` is integral, so going down holds (00H8).
  - `dim A'_q = dim P_p + dim A'_q/pA'_q` (00ON), and the fibre is zero-dimensional.
  - So the heights agree at every prime `q`, and in particular at every maximal
    ideal.
- **Freeness.**
  - Miracle flatness (00R4) makes `A'` flat over `P`.
  - Finite and flat over a Noetherian ring gives projective (00HT, 00NX).
  - Quillen's Theorem 4 makes it free.
  - No generic freeness is used, so no non-constant element is inverted.
- **Result.** `Γ <= GL_(Nd)(A') <= GL_(Ndr)(P)`.

**PASS.**

**Baumslag's group** (01e86d638). `B = <a,s,t | [s,t], [a,a^t], a^s = a a^t>`
`≅ R x| Z^2`, where `R = Z[x^(±1), (1+x)^-1] = Z[x][1/(x+x^2)]`.
- **Affine image.** `s` and `t` act by multiplication by `1+x` and `x`, and
  `(1+x)r = r + xr`. The units `x` and `1+x` are multiplicatively independent.
- **The Nagata relation, by hand.** Put `u = 1/(x+x^2)` and `s' = x - u^3`. Then
  `u(x+x^2) = 1` expands to `u^7 + (1+2s')u^4 + (s'+s'^2)u - 1 = 0`.
- **The degree.** In lowest terms, `s' = (x^4(1+x)^3 - 1)/(x^3(1+x)^3)`, which has
  degree 7.
- **Consequences.**
  - `1, u, ..., u^6` is a basis, so `B <= GL_14(Z[s'])`.
  - The host is `Z[s']^28 x| E_28(Z[s'])`.

**PASS.** Landed note: 14869392d.

## 8. Spherical Artin groups

Source: A. M. Cohen and D. B. Wales, *Linearity of Artin groups of finite type*,
arXiv:math/0010204v2, TeX source.
- **Theorem 1.1** (l.104--107): "Every Artin group of finite type is linear."
- **l.111--115.** "We focus on the Artin groups of type `A`, `D`, `E`. Since the other
  Artin groups of finite and irreducible type can be embedded in these
  (cf. [Crisp]), it will suffice ... to exhibit a faithful representation for each
  of the groups `A`, `D`, `E`."
- **Theorem 1.2** (l.148 on). Types `A`, `D`, `E` act on a free module over
  `Z[r,t,r^-1,t^-1]`, a ring of characteristic 0.
- **Proof** (l.1647--1661).
  - Reducible types use direct sums.
  - "By [Crisp], every Artin group `B` of finite type `M` such that `M` has a multiple
    bond occurs as a subgroup of an Artin group of finite type without multiple
    bonds."
- **No Digne.** The bibliography (l.1766 on) has no Digne entry.
- **Coverage.** `B_n`, `F_4`, `H_3`, `H_4` and `I_2(m)` come through Crisp only. A
  subgroup of a group linear over `Q(r,t)` is linear over `Q(r,t)`.
- **No specialization.** The chain does not specialize `r` or `t`, which could only
  enlarge the kernel.

**PASS.**
- **Trust caveat.** Crisp's *Injective maps between Artin groups* was read only
  through Cohen--Wales and Paris (arXiv:0711.2372).
