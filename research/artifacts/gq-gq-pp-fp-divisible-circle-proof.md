# Divisible subgroups of finitely-pieced projective and PL circle groups: full proof

Lane `gq-pp-fp`, 2026-09-17. This is a lane proof, not independently reviewed. The arguments are
elementary and no priority is claimed; novelty was not searched.

**Purpose.**
- It supplies a complete proof of items 1–5 of the claim `pp-circle-divisible-subgroups-act-freely`,
  which lane `gq-pp-psl2q` drafted (statement only) before it stopped. That claim is not on main yet.
  It also proves the consequence the draft names, `pp-circle-and-lift-hosts-miss-gl-n-q`, and adds
  the interval/line case.
- **Verdict for the pp family (§8).** No finitely generated host of this kind, and no lift of one, can
  contain GL_n(Q) for n >= 2, whether embedded standardly or not. So no piecewise-projective candidate
  reaches the finite-presentation stage.

## 1. Setting (as in the draft)

`S^1` is oriented and `Homeo_+(S^1)` is its orientation-preserving group. `Λ` is one of the following.
- **(P)** A subgroup of `PSL_2(R)` acting on `S^1 = RP^1`.
- **(L)** A subgroup of `Aff_+(R) = {x ↦ ax + b : a > 0}` that contains `x ↦ x + 1`, acting on the
  universal cover `R` of `S^1 = R/Z`.

`g ∈ Homeo_+(S^1)` is **Λ-piecewise** if there is a finite set `B_g ⊂ S^1` such that on the closure
of each component of `S^1 \ B_g`:
- in case (P), `g` agrees with an element of `Λ`;
- in case (L), a lift of `g` agrees with an element of `Λ` on each lift of that closed arc.

These `g` form the group `PP_Λ(S^1)`.

**(D)** `Λ` has no nontrivial divisible abelian subgroup. Every finitely generated `Λ` satisfies (D):
it is linear, hence residually finite (Mal'cev), and a divisible group has no nontrivial finite
quotient. If `Γ ≤ PP_{PSL_2(R)}(S^1)` or `Γ ≤ PP_{Aff_+(R)}(S^1)` is finitely generated, then every
element of `Γ` is `Λ_0`-piecewise, where `Λ_0` is generated by the pieces of the generators (and by
`x ↦ x+1` in case (L)). This is the product rule, as in `germ-extensions-omit-standard-gl-n-q`, part 3.

## 2. Facts used

- **F1 (rotation number).** `rot : Homeo_+(S^1) → R/Z` is invariant under conjugation in
  `Homeo_+(S^1)`, and its restriction to any abelian subgroup is a homomorphism. `rot(g) = 0` if and
  only if `g` has a fixed point. An element of finite order is conjugate to the rotation by its rotation
  number, so a finite-order element with `rot = 0` is the identity.
- **F2 (few fixed points).** Let `I` be an arc on which `g` agrees with one piece `φ ∈ Λ`.
  - (P): if `g` fixes three distinct points of `I`, then `φ` fixes them, so `φ = 1`: a nontrivial
    Möbius map has at most two fixed points on `RP^1`.
  - (L): if `g` fixes two distinct points of `I`, then on a lift `Ĩ` we have `φ(x̃) = x̃ + m` at the
    two lifted points, for one integer `m` (the same `m` for both, by continuity along `Ĩ`). So the
    affine map `φ - (x + m)` has two zeros, `φ = x + m`, and `g` is the identity on `I`.
  - Write `N = 2` in case (P) and `N = 1` in case (L).
- **F3 (germ homomorphism).** Let `H ≤ PP_Λ(S^1)` fix a point `a`. Each `h ∈ H` has a first
  component of `S^1 \ B_h` to the right of `a`, and on it `h` agrees with some `φ_h ∈ Λ`. In case (L),
  use the lift of `h` that fixes a chosen lift `ã`. Then `φ_h(a) = a`.
  - By the identity principle, `φ_h` depends only on the right germ of `h` at `a`: a Möbius map, or an
    affine map, that is the identity on an open interval is the identity.
  - Right germs at a common fixed point compose, because orientation-preserving maps send right
    neighbourhoods of `a` to right neighbourhoods of `a`.
  - So `h ↦ φ_h` is a homomorphism `H → Stab_Λ(a)`.
  - If `H` is divisible abelian, its image is divisible abelian, and so trivial by (D). Every `h ∈ H`
    is then the identity on a right neighbourhood of `a`, and likewise on a left neighbourhood.

## 3. Lemma A: a copy of (Q,+) has no global fixed point

**Lemma A.** Let `Λ` satisfy (D), `Γ ≤ PP_Λ(S^1)`, and `Q_0 ≤ Γ` with `Q_0 ≅ (Q,+)`. Then `Q_0` has no
global fixed point. Equivalently, since the fixed sets below are nested, `rot|_{Q_0}` is not identically 0.

*Proof.* Choose `r_1 ≠ 1` in `Q_0` and elements `r_k` corresponding to `r_1/k!`, so that
`r_k = r_{k+1}^{k+1}`. Then `Q_0 = ∪_k <r_k>`, and every element of `Q_0` is a power of some `r_k`.

*Nested fixed sets.* `r_k` is a power of `r_{k+1}`, so `Fix(r_{k+1}) ⊆ Fix(r_k)`. If `rot|_{Q_0} ≡ 0`,
each `Fix(r_k)` is nonempty and compact (F1). Then `Fix(Q_0) = ∩_k Fix(r_k) ≠ ∅`. Conversely, a global
fixed point forces `rot ≡ 0`.

Suppose `Fix(Q_0) ≠ ∅`. `Q_0` is nontrivial and acts faithfully, so `Fix(Q_0) ≠ S^1`. Let `J` be a
component of `S^1 \ Fix(Q_0)`, and let `a ∈ Fix(Q_0)` be its left endpoint in the circular order.

*Step 1 (F3).* Every `r_k` is the identity on some interval `(a, a + ε_k)`. So
`c_k := sup{c : r_k = id on (a, c)}` lies strictly after `a`, and `c_k ∈ J̄`.

*Step 2 (monotone).* `Fix(r_{k+1}) ⊆ Fix(r_k)`, so `c_{k+1} ≤ c_k`.

*Step 3 (finite order on an arc).* Let `C` be the component of `Fix(r_k)` containing `a`. It is a
closed arc containing `[a, c_k]`, with right end `c_k`, and `C ≠ S^1` because `r_k ≠ 1`. `r_{k+1}`
commutes with `r_k`, so it permutes the components of `Fix(r_k)`. It fixes `a`, so `r_{k+1}(C) = C`.
On the arc `C`, `r_{k+1}` is an increasing homeomorphism with `(r_{k+1}|_C)^{k+1} = r_k|_C = id`. An
increasing self-map `f` of an interval with `f^m = id` is the identity: if `f(x) > x` then
`f^m(x) > x`. So `r_{k+1} = id` on `C`, and `c_{k+1} ≥ c_k`.

*Step 4.* All `c_k` equal `c_1`, which lies strictly after `a`. Every element of `Q_0` is a power of
some `r_k`, so all of `Q_0` is the identity on `(a, c_1)`. That contradicts `(a, c_1) ∩ J ≠ ∅`, since
`J ∩ Fix(Q_0) = ∅`. ∎

## 4. Lemma B: rot is injective on a copy of (Q,+)

**Lemma B.** Under the hypotheses of Lemma A, `K := ker(rot|_{Q_0})` is trivial.

*Proof.* Suppose `K ≠ 0`.

*Step 1.* `K` is a nonzero subgroup of `Q`, so it is locally cyclic. By the nested argument of
Lemma A, `Fix(K) ≠ ∅`. `K ≠ 1` acts faithfully, so every component of `S^1 \ Fix(K)` is moved by `K`.
Fix a component `J` and an element `k ∈ K` that is nontrivial on `J`.

*Step 2 (the orbit of J is infinite).* `Q_0` is abelian, so `d(Fix(K)) = Fix(dKd^{-1}) = Fix(K)`, and
`Q_0` permutes the components of `S^1 \ Fix(K)`. Suppose the orbit `Q_0 J` were finite. Then
`Stab_{Q_0}(J)` would have finite index in `Q_0 ≅ Q`, so it would be all of `Q_0`, because `Q` has no
proper subgroup of finite index. An orientation-preserving homeomorphism that maps the open arc `J`
onto itself fixes its endpoints. So `Q_0` would have a global fixed point, contradicting Lemma A.

*Step 3 (infinitely many active arcs).* For `d ∈ Q_0` we have `k = d k d^{-1}`. So `k` is nontrivial on
`d(J)`, and `k` fixes both endpoints of `d(J)`, which lie in `Fix(K)`. By Step 2 there are infinitely
many pairwise disjoint such arcs.

*Step 4 (finitely many pieces).* `B_k` is finite, and each point of `B_k` lies in the closure of at
most two of the disjoint arcs. So infinitely many arcs `d(J)` have closures that meet no breakpoint of
`k`. Each such closure lies in one component `I` of `S^1 \ B_k` (or `I = S^1` if `B_k = ∅`). On it
`k` agrees with one piece that fixes the two endpoints of the arc and is nontrivial on the arc.
- Case (L): F2 with `N = 1` already gives a contradiction for one arc.
- Case (P): two disjoint such arcs inside one proper arc `I` would give at least three fixed points
  of that piece, contradicting F2. If `I = S^1`, at most two arcs can share the piece's two fixed
  points.

So each of the finitely many components of `S^1 \ B_k` holds at most two such arcs, which contradicts
Step 3. ∎

## 5. The draft's items 1–5

Let `Λ` satisfy (D), `Γ ≤ PP_Λ(S^1)`, and `D ≤ Γ` divisible abelian (written additively).

**rot is injective on D.**
- *Torsion.* A torsion element `t` with `rot(t) = 0` is trivial (F1).
- *Infinite order.* Let `v ∈ D` have infinite order with `rot(v) = 0`. Put `v_1 = v` and choose `v_{k+1}`
  with `(k+1) v_{k+1} = v_k`. Then `v = k! v_k`.
  - `m/k! ↦ m v_k` is a well-defined homomorphism `Q → D`.
  - It is injective: `m v_k = 0` gives `m v = k!(m v_k) = 0`, so `m = 0`.
  - Its image `Q_v ≅ Q` has `v ∈ ker(rot|_{Q_v})`, which contradicts Lemma B.

This gives the draft's items 1–5.
1. *Freeness.* If `d ≠ 0` then `rot(d) ≠ 0`, so `d` has no fixed point (F1).
2. *Rotation number.* `rot|_D` is an injective homomorphism.
3. *Normalizers.* Let `g ∈ N_Γ(D)` and `d ∈ D`. Then `gdg^{-1} - d ∈ D`, and
   `rot(gdg^{-1} - d) = rot(gdg^{-1}) - rot(d) = 0` by conjugation invariance and additivity on `D`.
   So `gdg^{-1} = d`.
4. *Amenable subgroups.* An amenable `A ≤ Γ` preserves a probability measure on `S^1`. On a group
   preserving a probability measure, `rot` is a homomorphism: it is the translation number of the
   measure semiconjugacy to rotations. So `rot([A,A]) = 0`, and `D ≤ [A,A]` forces `D = 0` by item 2.
5. *Rational rotation numbers.* If `rot(Γ) ⊆ Q/Z`, item 2 embeds `D` in `Q/Z`. So `D` is torsion and
   `Γ` contains no `(Q,+)`. This applies to Thompson's `T`, all of whose rotation numbers are rational
   (Ghys--Sergiescu); that `T` is `Q`-free also follows from `T ≤ V`, survey Theorem 4.4.

**Not proved.** `Q`-freeness in general. A copy of `(Q,+)` acting freely with irrational rotation
numbers, conjugate to a dense group of rotations, is not excluded. Example question: is there one in a
Stein-type PL circle group whose rotation numbers can be irrational?

## 6. Consequences for hosts and for lifts

**Corollary 1 (hosts).** Let `Λ ≤ PGL_2(R)` or `Λ ≤ Aff(R)` satisfy (D) (for instance, the pieces of a
finitely generated host). Let `Γ` be a group of `Λ`-piecewise circle homeomorphisms, orientation
reversing ones allowed. Then `Γ` contains no subgroup isomorphic to any of:
- `Aff(Q)`, `B_2(Q)`, `SL_2(Q)`, `PSL_2(Q)`;
- `GL_n(Q)`, `SL_n(Q)`, `PGL_n(Q)`, `PSL_n(Q)` for `n >= 2`;
- `U_3(Q)`;
- `Q x|_λ Z` for `λ ∈ Q^x \ {±1}`, or for `λ ≠ 1` when `Γ ≤ Homeo_+(S^1)`.

*Proof.* Let `Γ^+ = Γ ∩ Homeo_+(S^1)`, of index at most 2. Each listed `H` has a copy `D` of `Q` or
`Q^2` and an element of `H ∩ Γ^+` acting on `D` nontrivially by conjugation. That contradicts item 3.
- `Q x|_λ <t>`: `D = Q`, and `t^2 ∈ Γ^+` acts by `λ^2 ≠ 1`.
- `Aff(Q) ⊇ Q x| <x ↦ 4x>`, and `4 = 2^2`; `B_2(Q) ⊇ {[[a,b],[0,1]]} ≅ Aff(Q)`.
- `SL_2(Q)` and `PSL_2(Q)` are perfect, so they lie in `Γ^+`. The unipotent `U ≅ Q` (injective in
  `PSL_2`) is scaled by 4 under `diag(2, 1/2)`.
- `GL_n`, `SL_n`, `PGL_n`, `PSL_n` (`n >= 2`) contain the image of the upper-left block `SL_2(Q)`. That
  image meets the scalars in at most `{±1}`, so it is `SL_2(Q)` or `PSL_2(Q)`.
- `U_3(Q)` is divisible, so it has no subgroup of index 2 and lies in `Γ^+`. Take
  `D = {x(a) z(c)} ≅ Q^2`, abelian. `y(1)` conjugates `x(a) z(c)` to `x(a) z(c ± a)`, a nonidentity
  automorphism. ∎

**Corollary 2 (lifts).** Let `Γ̂ ≤ Homeo_+(R)` commute with `τ(x) = x + 1`, with image
`Γ ≤ PP_Λ(S^1)` and `Λ` satisfying (D). This holds if `Γ̂` is finitely generated and its elements are
lifts of finitely-pieced projective or PL circle maps. Then `Γ̂` contains none of the groups of
Corollary 1. This does not need the base to be `Q`-free; compare `lifts-add-no-unipotent-divisibility`.

*Proof.* `ker(Γ̂ → Γ) = Γ̂ ∩ <τ>` is central and torsion-free cyclic. For each listed `H` it suffices
to show that the image of `H` in `Γ` contains a group excluded by Corollary 1.
- *Trivial or finite centre* (`Aff(Q)`, `PSL`, `PGL`, `SL`, `Q x|_λ Z` with `λ ≠ ±1`): `H` meets the
  kernel trivially.
- *`GL_n(Q)`, `B_2(Q)`:* the block `SL_2(Q)`, resp. the copy of `Aff(Q)`, meets the centre in a finite
  group, so it embeds.
- *`U_3(Q)`:* let `C = H ∩ ker ≤ Z(U_3(Q)) = {z(c)} ≅ Q`, which is cyclic. If `C = 1`, `U_3(Q)` embeds.
  Otherwise the image `X̄` of `X ≅ Q` is a copy of `(Q,+)`, and `ȳ(1)` conjugates `x̄(a)` to
  `x̄(a) z̄(a)` inside the abelian image of `XZ`. By conjugation invariance and additivity,
  `rot(z̄(a)) = 0` for all `a`. But `Z/C` is a torsion group, and a finite-order element with `rot = 0`
  is trivial (F1). So `Z = C` is cyclic, which is false. ∎

## 7. Lines and intervals

**Corollary 3.** Let `Γ` be a finitely generated group of homeomorphisms of a compact interval, or of
`R`, each with finitely many breakpoints. Let the pieces be Möbius maps (for `R ⊂ RP^1`) or affine maps
`x ↦ λx + c` with `λ > 0`. Then `Γ` has no nontrivial divisible subgroup; in particular it contains no
`(Q,+)`.

*Proof.* Pass to the orientation-preserving subgroup, of index at most 2, which contains every divisible
subgroup. It is torsion-free, so a nontrivial divisible subgroup contains some `Q_0 ≅ Q`.
- *Projective case on `R`.* `Γ` fixes `∞ ∈ RP^1`, so `Q_0` has a global fixed point. This contradicts
  Lemma A, with pieces in the group generated by the generators' pieces, which satisfies (D).
- *Compact interval.* `Q_0` fixes the endpoints. The proof of Lemma A runs verbatim on the interval.
- *Affine case on `R`.* Use `[-∞, +∞]`. The germ of an element at `±∞` is one affine map, determined by
  the germ, and germs at `±∞` compose. So F3 holds at `±∞` with values in the finitely generated
  piece group, and Lemma A's proof runs verbatim.

*Examples covered:* Thompson's `F`, Stein groups `F_{n_1,...,n_k}`, finitely generated Bieri--Strebel
groups, every finitely generated subgroup of `PL_+(I)` or of Monod's `H(A)`, and the Lodha--Moore group
(finitely presented, arXiv:1308.4250v3; see `gq-gq-lit-hosts.md` §5). None contains `(Q,+)`.

*Sanity check.* `Q ≤ T̄` (Belk--Hyde--Matucci) is consistent with this. As maps of `R`, elements of `T̄`
have infinitely many breakpoints, so Corollary 3 does not apply. Corollary 2 applies: it allows the
central `Q` of `T̄` but forbids `Aff(Q)` and the rest, in line with root obstruction O4.

## 8. Verdict for the finite-presentation question

1. Consider hosts that are one-dimensional, piecewise-projective or PL, with finitely many breakpoints
   per element, together with their central lifts to `R`. For `G` and every stepping stone (`Aff(Q)`,
   `U_3(Q)`, `SL_2(Q)`, `GL_2(Q)`, ...), all of these are excluded before finite presentation matters,
   for every embedding, standard or not. Standard copies were already excluded in every dimension by
   `germ-extensions-omit-standard-gl-n-q`.
2. No candidate on the boards of `gq-pp-psl2q`, `gq-pp-lift` or `gq-pp-higher` therefore needs a
   finite-presentation verdict for `G`. The finite-presentation tools are moot on this family:
   Lodha--Moore (3 generators, 9 relators, type F_∞), Brown's criterion via Stein--Farley complexes,
   and BHM Theorem 2.1 for germ extensions (`gq-gq-lit-hosts.md` §§2, 5).
3. Only three piecewise-projective escapes stay open:
   - **(E1) Singular points.** Finite germ extensions of one-dimensional groups, in which breakpoints
     accumulate at finitely many points, like Brin's `A ⊇ T̄ ⊇ Q`. Lemma A's germ step (F3) fails at a
     singular point. Standard copies are still excluded there (`germ-extensions-omit-standard-gl-n-q`).
     Nonstandard `Aff(Q)` is excluded inside `VA ⊇ A` by O4, and open in general.
   - **(E2) Dimension >= 2.** Piecewise-projective maps of `RP^{n-1}` for `n >= 3`. No rotation number
     is available there, and only standard copies are excluded.
   - **(E3) Cantor-set hosts.** V-type prefix maps violate the identity principle.
4. Recommendation: stop one-dimensional finitely-pieced constructions for `G`. Any piecewise-projective
   idea must live in E1–E3.
