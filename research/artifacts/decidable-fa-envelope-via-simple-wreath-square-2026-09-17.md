# Square a decidable infinite simple envelope by a swap

*Attempt artifact (2026-09-17), formerly route `decidable-fa-envelope-via-simple-wreath-square` into `decidable-groups-have-decidable-fa-envelopes`, requires [boone-higman-thompson-simple-envelope, fg-perfect-wreath-c2-has-property-fa]. Demoted from the graph because one of three referee votes was lost; the two returned votes found no gap. Restore it as a route once a full referee pass survives.*

Let `G` be finitely generated with solvable word problem.

**Step 1 (infinite decidable simple envelope).** `G x Z` is finitely generated,
has solvable word problem and contains `G`. By
`boone-higman-thompson-simple-envelope`, it embeds in a finitely generated
computably presented simple group `S`. `S` contains `Z`, so it is infinite,
hence nonabelian, hence perfect.

`S` has solvable word problem. This is the refinement recorded in that claim,
and it is also Kuznetsov's argument: to decide `w = 1`, enumerate the
consequences of the computable relators, looking for `w`. In parallel,
enumerate the consequences of the relators together with `w`, looking for a
fixed generator `s != 1`. Exactly one search halts, since `S` is simple.

**Step 2 (wreath square).** Put `W = (S x S) ⋊ <sigma>` with
`sigma (x, y) sigma^-1 = (y, x)`. `G <= S = S x 1 <= W`.

`W` is generated by the generators of `S x 1` together with `sigma`, since
`sigma (s, 1) sigma^-1 = (1, s)`.

**Step 3 (word problem).** Each element of `W` has a unique normal form
`(x, y) sigma^e` with `e in {0, 1}`. Given a word in the generators of `S x 1`
and `sigma`, read it left to right and keep a parity bit `e`. Append a
generator `s` to the first coordinate word if `e = 0` and to the second if
`e = 1`, and flip `e` on each `sigma`. The word is trivial iff
- `e = 0` at the end, and
- both coordinate words are trivial in `S`,

which is decidable by Step 1.

**Step 4 (FA).** `S` is finitely generated and perfect, so `W` has property FA
by `fg-perfect-wreath-c2-has-property-fa`.

Take `W_G = W`. `∎`
