# Stable formalization: which erasure and ancilla steps can remove the Bennett remainder

Lane `gk-fz-strict`, family F, 2026-09-12. Supports the claims
`garbage-output-tracks-formalize-every-left-inverse-pair`,
`invertible-ancilla-outputs-collapse-stable-formalization` and
`sitewise-gates-cannot-erase-nonlocal-defects`, and an Attempts entry on
`injective-binary-automata-are-stably-formalizable`.

The question is the step from reversible to injective. `formalizable-left-inverse-iff-clean-shear-dilation`
puts the whole obstruction to plain formalizability in the Bennett erasure, where the formal
remainder `X − σ~(τ~(X))` appears. Stabilization adds identity tracks that are inputs as well as
outputs. What follows decides which erasure and absorption steps can remove that remainder.

## 0. Setting

- `p` prime, `F = F_p`, `G` a group. Configurations live on `(F^N)^G`, with variables `X_(h,i)`.
- `I` is the ideal generated by all `Z^p − Z`, `Z` a variable. A polynomial uses finitely many
  variables, and it vanishes at every `F`-point exactly when it lies in `I`.
- A *representative* of an automaton is an equivariant finite-memory polynomial map that agrees with
  it on `F`-points. Composites are computed by substitution.
- A *stable formalization* of an automaton `τ` on `(F^n)^G` with a left inverse consists of:
  - `m >= 0` identity tracks, with variables `Y`;
  - a representative `U(X, Y)` of the data output of `τ × id_m`, so `U = τ(x)` on points for every `y`;
  - `V(X, Y) = Y + e(X, Y)` with `e ∈ I`;
  - a left-inverse automaton `σ'` of `τ × id_m`, with a representative `σ~' = (σ~'_X, σ~'_Y)` such that
    `σ~'(U, V) = (X, Y)` formally.

  For `p = 2` this is what `injective-binary-automata-are-stably-formalizable` asks for, and
  `prime-field-left-inverse-pairs-are-stably-formalizable` asks it for every left inverse.
- The *relative ancilla map* is `Φ(X, Y) = (X, V(X, Y))`. It is the identity on `F`-points.
- A *plain formalization* is the case `m = 0`.

## 1. Garbage output tracks are free

**Theorem 1.1.** Let `(τ, σ)` be a left-inverse pair on `(F^n)^G` with representatives `τ~`, `σ~`,
and put `d(X) = X − σ~(τ~(X))`.
- `d` vanishes on `F`-points.
- The rectangular formal map `X ↦ (τ~(X), d(X))` represents the automaton `x ↦ (τ(x), 0)` from
  `(F^n)^G` to `(F^(2n))^G`.
- It has the formal left inverse `(U, E) ↦ σ~(U) + E`.

*Proof.* `d` vanishes on points because `σ ∘ τ = id`, so the second output is `0` on points. And
`σ~(τ~(X)) + d(X) = X` as polynomials. ∎

**Remark 1.2 (no linearization content).**
- The refutation `binary-left-inverse-pairs-need-not-be-formalizable` disappears once output-only
  tracks that are functionally zero are allowed.
- This certifies nothing. At a constant configuration the Jacobians form a rectangular pair
  `B·A = I_n`, with `A` an `(2n × n)` and `B` an `(n × 2n)` matrix over `F[G]`.
- Such pairs exist over every ring, for instance `A = [I; 0]` and `B = [I 0]`. Direct finiteness
  constrains only square pairs.
- So what `formal-polynomial-strict-pairs-need-unstable-linearization` consumes is squareness. The
  extra tracks of a stable formalization must be inputs as well as outputs.

## 2. Right-invertible ancilla outputs collapse

**Theorem 2.1.** Let `(U, V, σ~')` be a stable formalization of `τ` with `m` identity tracks.
Suppose the relative ancilla map `Φ(X, Y) = (X, V(X, Y))` has a formal right inverse `Θ`, meaning an
equivariant finite-memory polynomial map with `Φ ∘ Θ = id` formally. Then `Θ(X, Y') = (X, θ(X, Y'))`,
and the pair
- `τ~⁰(X) = U(X, θ(X, 0))`,
- `ρ~(W) = σ~'_X(W, 0)`

is a plain formalization of `τ`: `τ~⁰` represents `τ`, `ρ~` represents a left inverse `ρ` of `τ`, and
`ρ~(τ~⁰(X)) = X` formally.

*Proof.*
1. The `X`-component of `Φ ∘ Θ` is the `X`-component of `Θ`, so `Θ_X = X` formally. Its ancilla
   component gives `V(X, θ(X, Y')) = Y'` formally.
2. Substitute `(X, Y) := (X, θ(X, Y'))` in `σ~'(U, V) = (X, Y)`. Substitution is a ring homomorphism,
   so step 1 gives `σ~'(U(X, θ(X, Y')), Y') = (X, θ(X, Y'))`.
3. Substitute `Y' := 0` and take `X`-components: `σ~'_X(U(X, θ(X, 0)), 0) = X` formally.
4. On points `Φ` is the identity and `Φ ∘ Θ = id`, so `Θ` is the identity on points and `θ(x, 0) = 0`.
   Hence `τ~⁰(x) = U(x, 0) = τ(x)`.
5. `ρ(w) := σ'_X(w, 0)` is an automaton, and `σ'(τ(x), 0) = (x, 0)` gives `ρ ∘ τ = id`. Both
   representatives are equivariant with finite memory. ∎

**Corollary 2.2 (additive absorption collapses).** Suppose `e` depends only on `X`, so the identity
tracks return `Y` plus a polynomial computed from the data. Then `θ(X, Y') = Y' − e(X)` is a right
inverse, and `τ` has a plain formalization. So every Bennett-type construction that adds the
remainder `d(X)`, or any other data polynomial, into the identity tracks yields nothing beyond plain
formalizability. The same holds for any `e` whose relative map is an elementary shear or a
finite-memory invertible affine map in `Y` over the data.

**Corollary 2.3 (square left inverses of `Φ`).** Suppose `M_(n+m)(F[G])` is directly finite and `Φ`
has a formal left inverse `Λ(X, V) = (X, λ(X, V))`, equivariant with finite memory.
- Then `formal-polynomial-strict-pairs-need-unstable-linearization`, applied on `n + m` tracks, gives
  `Φ ∘ Λ = id` formally, and Theorem 2.1 gives a plain formalization of `τ`.
- **Case 1:** the ancilla recovery reads only `V`, that is `σ~'_Y(U, V) = λ(V)`. Then `λ(V(X, Y)) = Y`
  formally, so `Λ(X, V) = (X, λ(V))` is such a left inverse.
- **Consequence:** on such hosts a stable formalization of a pair with no plain formalization must
  recover the identity tracks through the data output `U`.

**Corollary 2.4 (the integers).** Let `G = Z`, `p = 2`, `n = 1`, and let `τ` not be a translation
composed with a complement, for instance the marker involution of
`binary-left-inverse-pairs-need-not-be-formalizable`. By
`formalizable-binary-pairs-over-biorderable-groups-are-affine`, `τ` has no plain formalization.
`F_2[Z]` is commutative, so every `M_N(F_2[Z])` is directly finite (determinants). Hence every stable
formalization of `τ`, with any number of identity tracks, has both properties:
- (a) a relative ancilla map with no finite-memory formal right inverse;
- (b) an ancilla recovery `σ~'_Y` that genuinely reads the data output `U`.

The same conclusion holds over every bi-orderable group whose group algebras over `F_2` are
stably finite. By the Malcev–Neumann embedding into a division ring that is all of them, a standard
fact not re-derived here, so no node depends on it.

**Corollary 2.5 (the escape class is nonempty).** Take `p >= 5`, `n = 1`, and a non-affine sitewise
permutation. It has no plain formalization but has a stable formalization with one identity track
(`sitewise-pairs-over-prime-fields-are-stably-formalizable`). So its stable formalizations satisfy
(a), and the class of right-invertible relative ancilla maps is proper: Theorem 2.1 is sharp.

## 3. Sitewise gates cannot erase a nonlocal remainder

**Lemma 3.1.** Let `k` be a field, `S` a finite set of variables, `T` a set of further variables, and
`d ∈ k[S][T] \ k[S]`. Then `d` is transcendental over `k(S)`.

*Proof.* Suppose `P(d) = 0` with `P ∈ k(S)[w]` of degree `r >= 1`. Over `k(S)`, the total degree of
`P(d)` in the variables `T` is `r · deg_T(d) > 0`, so `P(d) != 0`. That is a contradiction. ∎

**Theorem 3.2.** Let `S` be a finite set of variables, `c ∈ k[S]`, `d ∈ k[S][T] \ k[S]` and
`F ∈ k[S][w]`. If `F(c + d) ∈ k[S]`, then `F` does not depend on `w`.

*Proof.* `P(w) = F(w) − F(c + d)` lies in `k(S)[w]` and vanishes at `c + d`, which is transcendental
over `k(S)` by Lemma 3.1. So `P = 0`, and `F(w) = F(c + d)` lies in `k[S]`. ∎

**Application: sitewise erasure onto a clean track.**
- **Setup.** In the Bennett dilation, after computing `τ~(X)` and then `w = σ~(τ~(X)) = X − d(X)`, the
  formal copy of the data at cell `g` is `w_g = X_g − d_g`.
- **Local variables.** Let `S` be the variables at cell `g` of the tracks that still hold their
  original variables: the data variable `X_g`, and the identity-track variables `Y_g` if those tracks
  are untouched so far.
- **Nonlocal remainder.** Suppose the remainder `d_g` involves a variable outside `S`.
- **Conclusion.** Take a sitewise gate component `F(S, w_g)` that must return a value in `k[S]`: a
  clean track returning `0`, the data track returning `X_g`, or an identity track returning `Y_g`. By
  Theorem 3.2 it does not read `w_g`.
- **So:** no sitewise gate, on any enlarged alphabet and through any tame realization of a symbol
  permutation, removes a nonlocal remainder from a clean track.
- **What erasure needs instead.** It must read the window of `d`, and making such a gate formally exact
  is the original problem. Alternatively the remainder can be sent into the identity-track outputs,
  which Section 2 constrains.

**Remark 3.3 (scope: regrouping).** Theorem 3.2 is algebraic and holds for every group, but "sitewise"
is relative to the sites.
- **With a finite-index subgroup.** If `G` has a proper finite-index subgroup `H`, one may regroup
  along `H`, as in Proposition 1.4 of `research/artifacts/formalizability-by-shear-decomposition-2026-09-12.md`.
  Blocks of cells become sites, `S` becomes the variables of a block, and a remainder local to a block
  is no longer nonlocal. So regrouping escapes the application, as the landed
  `marker-involution-is-formalizable-after-regrouping` shows over `4Z`, with the same Kaplansky payoff.
- **Without one.** On hosts with no proper finite-index subgroup, which is every finitely generated
  simple host relevant to the goal, sites are single cells and the application stands as stated.

## 4. What a stable formalization must do

For a pair with no plain formalization:
- **Clean tracks.** No sitewise step can clear a nonlocal remainder onto a clean track (Theorem 3.2).
- **Additive absorption.** Adding the remainder, or any data polynomial, into the identity tracks
  collapses to plain formalization (Corollary 2.2).
- **Directly finite hosts.** The identity tracks cannot be recovered from their own output alone
  (Corollary 2.3).

So the identity tracks must act as nonlinear catalysts:
- their output `V = Y + e(X, Y)` has no formal right inverse over the data;
- their recovery reads the data output `U`, which itself may depend on `Y`.

The sitewise stabilizations over `F_p`, `p >= 5`, are instances, so catalysis does occur. No
catalytic stabilization is known for any automaton that reads more than one cell and has no plain
formalization, and no invariant is known that forbids catalysis.

**Where it stops.**
- **Reversible-to-injective.** Not reduced. For strict `τ` there is no bijective automaton `β` with
  `β(x, y) = (τ(x), y)` for all `y`, so stabilization of an injective automaton cannot be read off from
  any reversible one. The Bennett map `(x, z) ↦ (z + τ(x), x + σ(z + τ(x)))` is structurally
  reversible but agrees with `τ × id` only at `z = 0`.
- **Positive side.** A construction must produce a catalytic `e` of degree at least 2 in `Y`, or a
  finite-memory non-invertible `Y`-linear part, together with a recovery that reads `U`.
- **Negative side.** An invariant must survive catalysis. Candidates are the higher layers `I^k/I^(k+1)`
  of `formalizability-is-a-boolean-ideal-cokernel-class`, now for square stabilized maps.
