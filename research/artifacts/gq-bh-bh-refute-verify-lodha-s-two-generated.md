# Adversarial check: Lodha's question "Is S generatable by 2 elements?" (`lodha-simple-group-s-is-two-generated`) (bh-refute, 2026-09-18)

## Checks

- **Step 1: fragmentation of `T`.**
  - *Interval chain.* By induction, with `γ ∈ V_{r−1} ∩ V_r`:
    - transitivity comes from (F2);
    - germ matching comes from (F3);
    - after that, `h'` is the identity near `γ`, and it splits into a piece supported in `V_r` and a piece
      supported in `(a_1, γ) ⊆ V_1 ∪ … ∪ V_{r−1}`.
  - *Circle.* Correct `g` at one dyadic point, then apply the interval case. **Correct.**
- **Step 4.** The arcs `t^m((a,b))` have length `3/(4n)` and are rotated by `1/(2n)`, so they cover `S^1`. Hence
  `T = ⟨t, σxσ^{-1}, σx'σ^{-1}⟩`. The conjugation by `σ` is a dyadic PL map, so its images lie in `T`.
- **Step 5: packing.**
  - The differences `u_a − u_b` of `u_a = 2^a` are distinct mod `2^{K+1}`. This follows from the size bound and
    uniqueness of binary expansions, and the case `a = d` forces `a = b`.
  - Hence the side factors of `X_a` and `X_b` sit on arcs disjoint from each other and from `J_0`.
  - Elements supported in arcs that meet only at endpoints commute, since each fixes the endpoints.
  - So `[X_a, X_b] = [f_a, f_b]`. **Correct.**
- **Step 6.**
  - Each `c_l` is a product of `h_I^{-1}yh_I` with `y ∈ C` and `h_I ∈ T ≤ ⟨t, f⟩`.
  - (G) holds for every finitely generated simple `G ⊇ T`: the normal subgroup generated by the `[G_I, G_I]`
    contains `[F, F] ≠ 1`.
- **Lodha's `S`.**
  - It is f.g., `⟨T, s⟩`.
  - It is simple (Lodha's Proposition).
  - It contains `T` in the `PSL_2(Z)` model. That model is conjugate to the PL model by the Minkowski question-mark
    map: both codings identify the same pairs `w01^∞ ~ w10^∞`, `0^∞ ~ 1^∞`.
  - So `S = ⟨t, f⟩`.

## Attacks tried (all failed)

- **Repeated entries among the `f_a`.** Harmless: indices, not values, carry the packing.
- **A single `u_i − u_a ≡ 0`.** Excluded, since `2^i ≢ 2^a mod 2^{K+1}` for `i, a <= K`.

## Lesson for general BH

Generation rank is never an obstruction inside dynamical hosts that have a finite-order shift permuting a
Sidon family of disjoint pieces. The generators of any finite configuration can be packed into one element and
recovered as commutators. For BH this means the finite data of an embedding can always be carried by a
bounded number of host elements. So a "few generators" constraint cannot separate inputs from hosts. The
in-host Higman–Neumann–Neumann principle holds for every f.g. simple circle group containing `T`.
