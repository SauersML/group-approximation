# Kadison symmetry bases and the groupification boundary

## Source

Yixin He, Quanyu Tang and Teng Zhang, *The separable case of Kadison's
problem on orthonormal bases of unitaries for type II_1 factors*,
arXiv:2605.15006v2 (16 May 2026), Theorem 1.

The theorem states that every diffuse finite von Neumann algebra with
faithful normal trace and separable `L^2` has a complete orthonormal basis
of self-adjoint unitaries.  The proof repeatedly solves finitely many real
orthogonality constraints by a projection using the Akemann--Weaver
noncommutative Lyapunov theorem and turns that projection into a symmetry.
It is a Hilbert-space basis theorem; no multiplication table is produced.

## Exact closure calculation

Assume a symmetry basis `B={s_i}` is closed under products modulo scalars.
Its image in the projective unitary group is a cancellative subsemigroup and
every element is its own inverse, hence it is a group `G` of exponent two.
Such a group is abelian because

```text
(xy)^2=1  =>  xy=(xy)^(-1)=y^(-1)x^(-1)=yx.
```

For chosen representatives, multiplication has the form

```text
s_g s_h=omega(g,h)s_(gh).
```

Associativity gives the two-cocycle identity.  Orthonormality gives
`tau(s_g^*s_h)=delta_(g,h)` and, in particular, the twisted regular trace.
Completeness identifies `L^2(M)` with `ell^2(G)` and the left multiplication
operators with the twisted left regular representation, proving
`M=L_omega(G)` trace-preservingly.

Because `G` is abelian, `L_omega(G)` is injective even when `omega` has
infinite phase.  Thus it is hyperfinite in the separable case.

## Consequence for Cairn

Take the separable non-CE factor generated by the fixed non-RU BCS.  The new
theorem supplies a countable orthonormal symmetry basis, but the calculation
above proves that its multiplication must delocalize: some product has at
least two nonzero basis coefficients.  More generally, no choice of
orthonormal symmetry basis can turn that factor into a projective group
basis.

This cleanly separates two notions which previously looked dangerously
close:

1. a **unitary orthonormal basis**, now universal in the separable diffuse
   tracial setting; and
2. a **group basis**, whose projective multiplication law is exactly the
   canonical-trace promotion needed by the nonhyperlinear-group goal.

The source therefore supplies useful raw bounded coordinates but no direct
group-factor shortcut.  A live use would require a finite ordinary-word
mechanism that represents the spread structure constants while retaining
the regular trace; free labels erase those coefficients, and literal
projective closure would force hyperfiniteness by the theorem above.
