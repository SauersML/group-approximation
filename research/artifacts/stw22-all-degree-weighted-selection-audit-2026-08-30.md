# STW XXII: all finite-dimensional trivial hyperfinite bundles

## Result

The dimension-two argument extends to every finite covering dimension.
For each finite sphere degree, conjugator fibres admit a uniform
ambient-`L^2` contraction modulus supplied by the Popa--Takesaki semigroup.
Gutev's controlled Michael theorem lifts small sphere families close to the
identity; the same contraction fills the lifts.  This proves uniform
equi-connectivity of strong Stiefel spaces in every finite degree.

The spectral-band weighted boundary proof is dimension-free.  Over
`S^m subset B^(m+1)`, each band bounded away from zero has a continuous
polar frame.  Farah--Vaccaro's finite-dimensional McDuff projection
equivalence trivializes its moving target; the all-degree Stiefel theorem
fills the resulting fixed-target sphere.  Recursive orthogonal extension
and the exact spectral-tail identity give a uniform weighted limit with the
prescribed boundary.

A high/low spectral split transfers these moduli to every weighted
purification fibre.  For a base of dimension `d`, the fibres are closed,
lower semicontinuous, equi-`LC^(d-1)`, and `(d-1)`-connected in one common
complete metric target.  Michael's Theorem 1.2 therefore gives weighted
corner selections, and the existing `(d+1)`-colour replication argument
kills all bounded fibre-gap traces.

This proves Problem XXII for trivial UHF/hyperfinite bundles over every
finite-dimensional compact metrizable base.  It supersedes, without
contradicting, the dimension-two claim.  It does not cover
infinite-dimensional bases, nontrivial W*-bundles, or general non-Bauer
trace spaces.

## Source boundaries

- The Popa--Takesaki contraction is used only for hyperfinite corners and
  with its nonexpansive `2`-norm estimate.
- The controlled near-selection statement is exactly Gutev, Theorem 5.3.
  Ordinary global `k`-connectivity uses Michael, Theorem 1.2, with the
  dimension, completeness, closedness, lower semicontinuity,
  equi-connectivity, and connectivity hypotheses checked explicitly.
- Moving projection targets occur only over finite-dimensional balls and
  are trivialized by Farah--Vaccaro, Theorem 1.1 together with their stated
  McDuff extension.  No Popa--Takesaki quotient cross-section is invoked.
