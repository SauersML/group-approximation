## 1. Every CCKW matrix map lifts to the stable Steinberg group

Let R be any associative unital F_7-algebra, not necessarily commutative or finite. Write e_ij(r)=I+rE_ij, with indices 0,1,2,3. Set

    A=e_03(x)e_12(x), B=e_10(y)e_23(-y), C=e_31(z),

for arbitrary x,y,z in R. Let St(R) be the stable Steinberg group, with standard generators X_ij(r), and pi:St(R)->E(R) its natural map. Define

    A~=X_03(x)X_12(x), B~=X_10(y)X_23(-y), C~=X_31(z).

**Theorem.** The assignments a->A~, b->B~, c->C~ satisfy all eleven relators of G=G_HB2(7). Consequently every such matrix map G->E(R) lifts to St(R), before specializing R or taking a finite quotient.

We use the convention [u,v]=u^-1 v^-1 u v and left-nested iterated commutators. The three seventh-power relations hold because the two root factors in A~ commute, those in B~ commute, and each root element has seventh power one.

Here is the elementary fact that checks all remaining relations. For any total ordering of a finite set of indices, the subgroup of St(R) generated by positive roots X_ij(r) for that ordering maps isomorphically onto the corresponding unitriangular matrix group. Indeed the Steinberg commutation and addition relations collect every word into a fixed ordered product with one factor per positive root. The matrix coefficients recover those factors uniquely, successively by root height; thus a collected product whose matrix is identity has every parameter zero. Permuting indices preserves the defining Steinberg relations, so the fact applies to every ordering used below. This argument uses no K_2 vanishing or field assumption.

For A and B, the directed matrix supports (column to row) are

    3->0, 2->1, 0->1, 3->2.

There is no directed path of length three. The subgroup of the unitriangular group supported on these arrows and their composites has nilpotency class at most two. Therefore [A~,B~,A~]=[A~,B~,B~]=1.

For A and C, the arrows form the directed chain 2->1->3->0. For B and C they form 0->1->3->2. Each pair lies in a unitriangular group of class at most three, so every left-nested commutator of length four vanishes. These are exactly the four length-four relators involving c.

It remains to check [C~,A~,C~]=[C~,B~,C~]=1. In either chain, C is the middle-arrow transvection 1+P, while the other generator is 1+Q, where Q is the sum of the two outer-arrow entries, with the indicated sign in B. Here P^2=Q^2=PQP=0. A direct expansion gives

    [1+P,1+Q]=1+PQ-QP-QPQ.

Both multiplication orders between P and each of PQ, QP, QPQ are zero. The displayed commutator therefore commutes with 1+P. By injectivity of the positive-root subgroup, the same identity holds for the chosen Steinberg lifts. This proves every relator.

The source matrix family is Caprace--Conder--Kaluba--Witzel, [Proposition 7.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09276), printed p.46. Taking R=Mat_k(F_q) gives its arbitrary block-matrix family. The lifting argument above is a separate deduction. In particular it includes the noncommuting block choices of their Proposition 7.16, not merely scalar parameters.

## 2. Precise consequence for central detectors

Let K=K_7 and let B=B_c0 be the canonical central extension with central generator z_B. The integral class c0 has primitive evaluation on H_2(K;Z), equivalently z_B is in [B,B], by [the wave-three primitive-evaluation theorem](../wave3-canonical-k7-class-has-primitive-evaluation.md). The following consequence explicitly uses that separate prerequisite.

Let rho:K->E(R) be the restriction of a map in Section 1. Pull back the stable Steinberg central extension along rho. The pullback splits, with section k->(k,rho~(k)). Every central pushout of this pullback also splits. Equivalently, every class on E(R) obtained by pushing out K_2(R)->St(R)->E(R) restricts to zero on K along rho.

**Detector obstruction.** No such pullback or pushout realizes c0 modulo an integer m>1. Primitivity makes the mod-m evaluation nonzero, whereas the Steinberg pullback class has zero evaluation and is in fact zero as an extension class.

A slightly more concrete formulation is useful. Suppose a central extension F->Q with cyclic kernel D receives a map f:B->F taking z_B to a generator of D, and the induced map K->Q admits a lift sigma:K->F. Then

    h(b)=f(b) sigma(b mod <z_B>)^-1

takes values in D and is a homomorphism, because D is central. It takes z_B to f(z_B). Since z_B belongs to [B,B], any homomorphism from B to the abelian group D kills it. Thus D must be trivial. This applies to all finite central pushouts and restrictions of the stable Steinberg construction just described.

**Scope.** This does not compute H_2(Q;Z) for the actual matrix image Q. An abstract central extension of Q need not extend to a stable Steinberg central extension of E(R). Unstable symplectic extensions also require a separate argument. Thus the theorem eliminates a proposed K_2 detector mechanism, not all finite quotients of B. Neither nonzero K_2(R) nor increasing the number of nilpotent parameters escapes this lifting obstruction.

## 3. Finite-field full symplectic images are completely blind

For a finite quotient f:B->F, put D=<f(z_B)> and Q=F/D. If z_B belongs to [B,B], then D is contained in [F,F]. The five-term homology sequence of 1->D->F->Q->1 gives a surjection

    H_2(Q;Z) -> D.

Indeed the next map is D->F_ab, which is zero. Therefore ord(f(z_B)) divides the exponent of H_2(Q;Z). In particular H_2(Q;Z)=0 forces f(z_B)=1.

For Q=Sp_4(F_(7^r)), r>=1, the multiplier is zero. Steinberg's [Lectures on Chevalley Groups](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/steinberg/steinberg-yale-notes.pdf), Section 7, Corollary 2 after Theorem 10, scanned PDF pp.88-89, gives central closure of universal Chevalley groups over finite fields of size greater than four, with the irrelevant SL_2(9) exception. The explicit examples include symplectic groups. Hence central quotients of B whose quotient by the indicated central image is a full Sp_4(F_(7^r)) detect no nontrivial power-order of z_B.

The already established base matrix image is Sp_4(F_7). This assertion does not assume that every parameter specialization has full symplectic image. It applies exactly when Q has the stated isomorphism type. If a map G->Sp_4(F_(7^r)) is surjective, its restriction to K is surjective: the image of K is normal with quotient a quotient of G/K=C_7, while Sp_4(F_(7^r)) is perfect.

## 4. Scalar unit parameters collapse to one parameter

For commutative R and units x,y, define

    D=diag(x^-1,(xy)^-1,y^-1,1), t=xyz.

Entry-by-entry conjugation gives

    D A(x) D^-1=A(1), D B(y) D^-1=B(1), D C(z) D^-1=C(t).

Thus the conjugated image is defined over the monogenic subalgebra F_7[t], even if R has arbitrarily many ambient nilpotent parameters. This conclusion does not require z to be a unit. It does require commutativity and invertibility of x,y.

If R is finite, S=F_7[t] is a finite quotient of F_7[T]. Factoring its defining polynomial and applying the Chinese remainder theorem expresses S as a product of rings F_(7^d)[u]/(u^e). To see the coefficient field in each local factor, lift the residue field as the roots of X^(7^d)-X; its derivative is -1, so each root lifts uniquely through the nilpotent ideal. A generator of the maximal ideal is a uniformizer, giving the displayed truncated-polynomial presentation.

Hesselholt--Madsen's [Cyclic polytopes and the K-theory of truncated polynomial algebras](https://web.math.ku.dk/~larsh/papers/007/) proves that the even relative K-groups of k[u]/(u^e) relative to (u) vanish for perfect k of positive characteristic. The split residue map and K_2 of a finite field being zero therefore imply K_2(S)=0. This is an additional explanation of why adding commuting unit parameters supplies no stable K_2 detector. Section 1 is stronger and does not require this vanishing theorem.
