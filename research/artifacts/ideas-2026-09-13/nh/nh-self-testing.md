## Approach A: a robust self-test at J for a non-RFD solution group

**Idea.**  Slofstra's embedding gives solution groups where J is nontrivial in
the group but trivial in every finite-dimensional representation.  If such a
game also had a dimension-uniform robust self-test for the sector where
J = -1 (near-perfect finite strategies are close to exact ones), approximate
representations keeping J far from 1 would round to exact finite-dimensional
ones, which do not exist.  The group would then be non-hyperlinear.

**Would prove.**  A non-hyperlinear finitely presented group.

**Kill test.**  Is robustness at J anything other than flexible HS-stability
of the solution group at J, and does the known embedding have it?

**Result.**  It is exactly flexible HS-stability at J (a robust self-test in
normalized HS is a stability statement), so this is the HS-stability core,
not a new mechanism.  For the embedding family itself the repo already has
`wagon-wheel-exact-radical-has-hs-wall`: Slofstra's wagon wheel preserves exact
finite-dimensional invisibility but has a fixed normalized-HS wall, so near
strategies keep J away from 1 without rounding.

**Verdict.**  Killed as a new route (duplicate of the stability core;
obstructed for the wagon-wheel family).

## Approach B: incompatible rigid towers

**Idea.**  Combine self-tests forcing incompatible dimension structure: Pauli
tests forcing 2^n-divisible dimensions against Weyl tests forcing p^m, or one
finitely generated group carrying a whole n-qubit Pauli tower sharing J, so
dimension must exceed 2^n for every n at a fixed error.

**Would prove.**  Non-hyperlinearity from a dimension contradiction.

**Kill test.**  Padding, and locality of hyperlinearity.

**Result (hand argument).**
- Divisibility is destroyed by padding with an o(d) block: normalized HS
  distance is unchanged in the limit, so no divisibility obstruction survives
  approximation.
- Hyperlinearity tests finitely many words at a time.  A finitely generated
  container of the Pauli tower (for example central products of copies of
  the one-qubit Pauli group with a shift) is locally-finite-by-cyclic, hence
  amenable and hyperlinear; words of length L only see the first O(L) qubits,
  realizable exactly in dimension 2^O(L).  Tower rigidity with n-dependent
  robustness is therefore automatically consistent.

**Verdict.**  Killed.

## Approach C: self-test the MIP*=RE non-CE trace and pass to the group of observables

**Idea.**  Build a synchronous game whose optimal tracial state is unique and
non-Connes-embeddable (MIP*=RE compression), then take the abstract group
generated by the ±1 observables and hope its canonical trace is that state.

**Would prove.**  A non-hyperlinear group of observables.

**Kill test.**  Does a non-CE character of a group make the group
non-hyperlinear?

**Result.**  No.  Hyperlinearity only needs the regular trace to be
approximable; a non-CE character is a different trace.  The repo already
records a non-CE character on a sofic right-angled Coxeter host
(`non-ce-trace-on-sofic-racg`, cited from the root node), so non-CE
characters coexist with soficity.  Selecting the regular trace by symmetry is
the EX lane's quantum-symmetry rung (artifact above), not this lane's.

**Verdict.**  Killed (character vs regular trace gap; symmetry version is a
duplicate).

## Approach D: Stone--von Neumann rigidity coupled to a Deligne center

**Idea.**  Form `(H_{2n+1}(Z) ⋊ Sp~_{2n}(Z)) / <z = c^{2m}>`, identifying the
Heisenberg center z with a power of the Deligne covering generator c (m chosen
to kill the abelianization of the cover).  In an exact representation with a
nontrivial central character, uniqueness of the Heisenberg irreducible forces
the cover to act through the Weil representation, which makes c^{2m} act
trivially and contradicts z acting nontrivially.  The hope is that the robust
version forces c^{2m} to be approximately trivial in every tracial approximation.

**Would prove.**  A non-hyperlinear central quotient, independent of whether
the Deligne cover alone is hyperlinear.

**Kill test.**  Is there generator-level robust Stone--von Neumann rigidity
in normalized HS, uniform in the central character?

**Result.**  No.
- The repo's `heisenberg-irrational-center-has-rank-one-sector-tuples`
  (ESTABLISHED) gives, in every dimension and for every central character,
  approximate Heisenberg sector tuples with rank-one defects, hence
  normalized-HS defect O(d^(-1/2)).  So the Heisenberg factor imposes no HS
  constraint on the central value at all.
- Gowers--Hatami rounds only maps on the complete multiplication table (repo
  audit above), so for H(Z/q) the table-level error grows with word length
  about q; there is no q-uniform generator-level rigidity.
- What remains at bounded level is already covered by
  `deligne-rank-models-have-no-fixed-level-scalar-sectors` and
  `deligne-sector-truncations-have-bounded-dimension`.
- Exact finite-dimensional representations of Deligne covers already kill
  twice the covering generator on finite quotients
  (`deligne-covers-of-sp2g-z-have-finite-residual-2z`), so exact-level
  rigidity adds nothing.

**Verdict.**  Killed as a mechanism.

## Approach E: rigid finite gadgets for a tracial-sector groupification of the MIP*=RE BCS

**Idea.**  The minimal type (i) statement in the root is
`non-ru-bcs-is-satisfiable-in-a-group-factor` (CORE B): model the fixed
tracially satisfiable, non-R^U Boolean constraint system in a group factor.
Paddock--Slofstra's obstacle (quoted in the EX artifact) says not every BCS
algebra embeds in a linear-system algebra, because linear systems collapse
the algebraic, C* and tracial levels.  That blocks an algebraic embedding of
all BCS algebras, but not a reduction for one fixed system that only has to
preserve tracial satisfiability forward and R^U-satisfiability backward.
Replace each nonlinear clause by a linear system over Z_2 plus one rigid
self-tested finite gadget (magic-square anticommutation, or a small Pauli or
Weyl packet), enforcing the clause only on the canonical-trace corner.

**Would prove.**  CORE B, hence a non-hyperlinear finitely presented group,
by Paddock--Slofstra 5.8 as read in the repo.

**Kill test (partial, run now).**
- Gowers--Hatami constants are uniform for a fixed finite gadget group (repo
  audit), so each gadget is individually robust with an absolute constant.
- Gadget rigidity cannot hold on the algebraic level for all clauses at once
  (the Paddock--Slofstra obstacle), so the reduction must use the trace.
- Not run: whether the repo's S3-packet route
  (`algebraic-pcp-s3-radical-groupification-route`, and
  `wagon-wheel-stops-before-bcs-groupification` for the wagon-wheel version)
  already subsumes anticommutation gadgets, and whether errors accumulate
  across the linearly many clauses (each clause contributes O(eps); the
  normalized-HS sum over clauses is where the proof would live or die).

**Verdict.**  Unclear.  Survives only as a variant of the existing CORE B
groupification lane; the first real test is a comparison with the S3-packet
route and a clause-accumulation estimate on the trace corner.
