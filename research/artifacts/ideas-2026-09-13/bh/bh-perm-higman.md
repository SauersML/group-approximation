# Idea lane bh-perm-higman: a permutational Higman embedding

Target: `boone-higman-conjecture`, through `permutational-boone-higman-conjecture`
and `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups` (Zaremsky, arXiv:2405.18354,
Corollary B, as quoted on main).

This file lists approaches with kill tests. It claims no theorem and changes no
node status.

## The step that loses the action

A type (A) action of a transitive `Gamma` on `Gamma/H` needs four things:
- `Gamma` finitely presented;
- `H` finitely generated;
- `H` core-free;
- finitely many double cosets `H \ Gamma / H`
  (`simple-core-bi-index-counts-coset-action-pair-orbits`).

Higman's embedding theorem, Clapham's decidable refinement and the
Boone--Higman--Thompson envelope (`boone-higman-thompson-simple-envelope`) get
finite presentation from towers of HNN extensions and amalgams over benign
edge groups. Approach 1 shows those towers force infinitely many double
cosets for every stabilizer that fixes a vertex of the tree. The constructions
that do give finitely many pair orbits are full Cantor groups and twisted
Brin--Thompson groups (`fp-full-binary-cantor-groups-have-type-a-actions`).
Those never come with finite presentation for free. A permutational Higman
embedding must get both at once.

## Approach 1: elliptic stabilizers see the Bass--Serre distance

- **Idea.** Let `K` act on its Bass--Serre tree `T` with unbounded orbits,
  and let a subgroup `H` fix a vertex `w`. For `h1, h2` in `H`,
  `d(w, h1 g h2 w) = d(h1^{-1} w, g w) = d(w, g w)`, so
  `H g H |-> d(w, g w)` is well defined on double cosets. It takes unboundedly
  many values, so `|H \ K / H|` is infinite.
- **Consequence.** A finitely generated `H` that fixes no vertex contains a
  hyperbolic element; this is the standard Serre fixed-point lemma, not re-read
  here. So in every splitting of the actor with an unbounded tree, a type (A)
  stabilizer must contain a hyperbolic element.
- **What it proves.** A necessary condition, not a construction. It kills
  every variant that acts on cosets of a vertex group, an edge group or a benign
  subgroup of a Higman tower. Those subgroups all fix vertices.
- **Kill test run.** The hand proof above. Not done: a consistency check
  against a known type (A) example that splits, such as Thompson's `F` acting
  on dyadic points, with `F` as an ascending HNN extension.
- **Verdict:** survives as a constraint; kills the benign-coset variant.

## Approach 2: finite-state compression inside Brin--Thompson groups

- **Idea.** Realize the computably presented Boone--Higman--Thompson envelope
  as a full Cantor group generated by `nV` and finitely many finite-state
  elements, such as reversible Turing machine steps or asynchronous
  transducers. Then compress its infinitely many relations inside an `nV`-type
  group. If the result is finitely presented and contains standard `V`, then
  `fp-full-binary-cantor-groups-have-type-a-actions` gives type (A) on clopen
  sets.
- **What it would prove.** PBH, hence BH.
- **Kill test run.**
  - Every such group is generated by rational homeomorphisms.
  - `rational-homeomorphism-subgroups-have-exponential-wp` gives single-exponential
    word problem.
  - `decidable-fp-groups-need-not-embed-in-rational-group` then excludes some
    decidable inputs.
  - The uniform wall `uniform-wp-bound-excludes-bh-universal-targets` excludes
    the whole family.
  - On main, `nV` itself is recorded as coNP-complete (Birget, arXiv:1902.03852).
- **Verdict:** killed. Lesson: germs must leave the rational group, and
  their complexity must grow with the input.

## Approach 3: boundary full group of the Higman tower

- **Idea.**
  - Approach 1 forces hyperbolic stabilizers. The natural points with
    hyperbolic stabilizers live at infinity: half-trees of the Bass--Serre
    tree of the tower `K`.
  - Take the countable Boolean algebra generated by half-trees, and its Stone
    space. Adjoin a Thompson-type group of almost-automorphisms that cut and
    paste half-trees.
  - Let `Gamma = <K, almost-automorphisms>`. If `Gamma` is a finitely
    presented full group containing a standard `V`, the clopen action is of
    type (A).
- **What it would prove.** PBH for `K`, hence for every decidable input.
- **Kill test run.**
  - The known finite-presentation criteria for full groups with rich germs
    need contraction or finite-state germ data: Nekrashevych's contracting
    self-similar hosts, and the hyperbolic-group theorem cited as
    `hyperbolic-groups-satisfy-boone-higman-citation`.
  - Here the germs at half-trees contain copies of the vertex groups, and hence
    of the input `G`, at unbounded depth. So any contracting or finite-state
    version falls under the approach 2 wall.
  - No finite-presentation criterion for non-contracting, non-rational germ
    groupoids is known to this lane.
  - Possible overlap: `boone-higman-boundary-wreath-germ-construction-2026-09-08.md`
    builds boundary-orbit wreath products. Only its headings were read here.
- **Verdict:** unclear. The contracting version is killed; the
  non-contracting version needs a new finite-presentation criterion.
- **Next test.**
  - Check whether the half-tree algebra of an HNN tree with infinite-index edge
    groups is atomless.
  - Check whether the almost-automorphism group contains a standard binary `V`
    acting fully.

## Approach 4: non-faithful Higman actions, then remove the kernel

- **Idea.** Accept a type [A_2]-style action with a kernel on a Higman tower,
  then remove the kernel afterwards, in the style of FFWZ Question 5.9.
- **What it would prove.** PBH for the image.
- **Kill test run.** `a2-kernel-removable-iff-image-in-type-a2-actor`
  (established on main, 2026-09-13): removal works exactly when the image
  embeds in a type (A_2) actor. That is PBH for the image again.
- **Verdict:** killed. The argument goes in a circle.

## Approach 5: algebraic affine towers cannot supply finite pair orbits

- **Idea.** Get the richness from a matrix or affine group over a finitely
  generated ring that encodes the input, as in the ring routes on main.
- **What it would prove.** Nothing, as the hand argument shows in rank one.
- **Kill test run.**
  - Let `R` be an infinite finitely generated commutative domain. `R` is not a
    field: a field that is finitely generated as a ring is finite.
  - For `AGL_1(R)` acting on `R`, the orbit of a pair `(x, y)` fixes `y - x`
    up to units.
  - Powers of a nonunit `a` are pairwise non-associate: `a^n = u a^m` with
    `n > m` makes `a^(n-m)` a unit. So there are infinitely many pair orbits.
  - In rank four, the corner obstruction `direct-ck-corner-core-has-infinite-bi-index`
    on main shows the same effect for the canonical Chatterji--Kassabov core.
- **Verdict:** killed in rank one by the hand proof, and for the canonical
  rank-four corner by main. The general higher-rank case is unclear.
- **Lesson.** Finiteness of pair orbits has to come from cut-and-paste
  (Thompson-type) dynamics, not from ring arithmetic.

## What survives

One shape survives: a finitely presented full Cantor group containing a
standard `V`, whose germ pseudogroup is not rational and consumes the input's
word-problem algorithm at unbounded complexity (approach 3). The missing theorem
is a finite-presentation criterion for full groups with non-contracting,
non-rational germs, which this lane does not have. Approaches 1, 2 and 5 are
constraints any such construction must meet.
