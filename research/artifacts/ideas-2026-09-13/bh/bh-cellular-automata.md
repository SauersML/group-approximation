# Idea lane bh-cellular-automata: subshift automorphism groups and Boone--Higman

Target: `boone-higman-conjecture`. Lens: automorphism groups of subshifts and groups
of reversible cellular automata (CA) as hosts, and CA undecidability as a
counterexample template. This file records ideas and kill tests only. It claims
nothing and edits no Cairn node.

Walls already on main that every approach below must respect:
- `complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal`: a class of hosts with one
  recursive word-problem bound is not universal.
- `rational-homeomorphism-subgroups-have-exponential-wp`: finite-state and rational
  germs have single-exponential word problems.
- `kazhdan-subgroups-of-amenable-orbit-full-groups-are-rf`: Kazhdan subgroups of
  amenable-orbit full groups are residually finite.
- Root Attempts 1--8: maximal quotients, Zorn, simple rings, universal Leavitt
  hosts, HNN compression, diagonalization.

Source status. Checked at abstract level: arXiv:1603.08715, 1405.0982, 1808.09194,
2001.09530 and 2501.14463. Seen only through search summaries: Boyle--Lind--Rudolph
(Trans. AMS 306, 1988), Gordon--Vershik, and Hochman's multidimensional-SFT
automorphism paper. Nothing below rests on an unread theorem number.

## 1. Automorphism groups of full shifts over residually finite bases, including stabilized versions

**Idea.**
- Use Aut(A^Λ), the reversible CA over a base group Λ, as a universal Boone--Higman
  container. Kim--Roush-type marker constructions put many groups inside:
  free groups, finite groups, and generalizations on groups via
  Barbieri--Carrasco-Vargas--Rivera-Burgos, arXiv:2501.14463.
- Or use the stabilized group SAut(A^Z) = ∪_n Aut(A^Z, σ^n). By Hartman--Kra--Schmieding
  (arXiv:2001.09530, abstract), its subgroup generated by finite-order elements is
  simple.

**What it would prove.** A single host containing every decidable group, with an
fp simple subgroup around each input.

**Kill tests run.**
- **Residual finiteness.**
  - For Λ = Z, Boyle--Lind--Rudolph show Aut(σ) is residually finite (search summary).
  - Hand proof for every residually finite base Λ.
    - Let N ≤ Λ be normal of finite index. A CA F commutes with every left shift
      σ_n, so it maps Fix(N) = {x : σ_n x = x for all n ∈ N} to itself.
    - Fix(N) is finite, of size |A|^[Λ:N].
    - Since Λ is residually finite, the union of the Fix(N) is dense, so Aut(A^Λ)
      acts faithfully on this dense union of finite invariant sets.
    - Hence Aut(A^Λ) is residually finite. Every subgroup is residually finite, so
      there is no infinite simple subgroup at all, and the finitely presented,
      non-residually-finite decidable inputs are excluded. The canonical witness is
      the Baumslag--Gersten group.
- **Stabilized version.**
  - SAut is a directed union: Aut(σ^n) ⊆ Aut(σ^{nm}), and each Aut(σ^n) is the
    automorphism group of a full shift on alphabet A^n, hence residually finite.
  - So every finitely generated subgroup of SAut is residually finite. The simple
    HKS subgroup is not finitely generated, and SAut contains no fp simple container
    for a non-residually-finite input.
- **Complexity, independently.** For a fixed base and alphabet, a word of length n
  is a CA of radius O(n), and equality is checked on patterns over a ball of radius
  O(n). That gives one recursive time bound (2^O(n) for Z; doubly exponential on an
  exponential-growth base), so the complexity wall applies too.

**Verdict: killed.** Constraint C1: the base must not be residually finite. More
precisely, periodic points must not be dense.

## 2. Reversible Turing-machine groups and topological full groups of Z^d full shifts as hosts

**Idea.** The group RTM of reversible Turing machines acting on Σ^{Z^d}, and its
finite-state subgroups, runs algorithms by local head moves. So it looks like a
natural home for a decidable input's word-problem algorithm, with a commutator
subgroup as the simple container.

**What it would prove.** A CA-native universal host, or one indexed by the input
algorithm.

**Kill tests run.**
- **LEF.** Barbieri--Kari--Salo (arXiv:1603.08715, abstract): the one-dimensional RTM
  group is "not amenable, not residually finite, locally embeddable in finite
  groups".
  - LEF passes to subgroups.
  - By Gordon--Vershik, a finitely presented LEF group is residually finite
    (search summary).
  - So no finitely presented, non-residually-finite group embeds in RTM. The
    Baumslag--Gersten group is finitely presented, decidable and not residually
    finite, so it does not embed. RTM therefore cannot be a universal container.
- **Fixed host.** A fixed RTM(n,k) has one recursive word-problem bound, since each
  element moves the head boundedly per word letter. So the complexity wall applies,
  even for inputs that are residually finite.

**Verdict: killed as universal hosts.** An RTM-type group can still serve as a
building block. Constraint C2: a host whose finitely generated subgroups are LEF
misses every finitely presented non-residually-finite input, so universality needs
genuine non-LEF behaviour.

## 3. Counterexample template: undecidable dynamics of CA as a hereditary obstruction

**Idea.**
- CA carry many undecidable properties. Barbieri--Kari--Salo (arXiv:1603.08715,
  abstract): the torsion problem for finite-state RTMs is undecidable in dimension 2.
  Kari's classical results make 2D reversibility and nilpotency undecidable.
- Take a decidable finitely generated group G with an undecidable torsion problem,
  for instance a finitely generated subgroup of 2D finite-state RTM. If every fp
  simple group had a decidable torsion problem, G would not embed, since a host
  decision procedure restricts to G's words.
- That would refute Boone--Higman.

**What it would prove.** A counterexample to Boone--Higman.

**Kill test run.** Belk--Bleak (arXiv:1405.0982, abstract, verbatim): "we prove that
the torsion problem for elements of the Brin-Thompson group 2V is undecidable". 2V is
finitely presented and simple, so decidability of the torsion problem is not
hereditary from fp simple groups. The same template dies for any "undecidable CA
dynamics" invariant that 2V-type groups can already simulate.

**Verdict: killed.** Constraint C4: an obstruction has to fail in 2V, and 2V already
has undecidable torsion.

## 4. Right translations, gate lattices, and a non-residually-finite base

**Idea.**
- **Embedding (hand check).** For any group Λ and any finite alphabet A, the map
  ρ_h(x)(g) = x(gh) is a CA with memory set {h}. It commutes with every left shift σ_k,
  since both σ_kρ_h x(g) and ρ_hσ_k x(g) equal x(k^{-1}gh). The map h ↦ ρ_h is
  injective, so Λ ≅ ρ(Λ) ≤ Aut(A^Λ).
- **Setup.**
  - Take P to be Clapham's finitely presented overgroup of the decidable input G,
    and Λ = P × V, with V Thompson's group. Λ is not residually finite, so approach
    1's periodic-point kill does not apply.
  - As a container candidate, take the commutator subgroup of ⟨ρ(Λ), gate lattices⟩.
    The gates act on finite-index subgroups of the P-direction.
  - For residually finite bases with the eventual filling property, the monolith of
    the stabilized automorphism group is simple and equals the commutator subgroup of
    the group generated by gate lattices. That is from search summaries of the
    gate-lattice papers.

**What it would prove.** G ≤ ρ(P) ≤ K, with K a simple subgroup of Aut(A^{P×V}). With
finite presentation it would give Boone--Higman for G.

**Kill tests run.**
- **The P-direction only gives residually finite pieces.**
  - Gates on finite-index subgroups of P act through finite quotients.
  - By approach 1's argument applied to P, any finitely generated subgroup built only
    from P-direction gates and ρ(P) is residually finite.
  - So simplicity of a finitely generated container has to come from the V-direction
    mixing with the gates.
  - That is structurally a twisted Brin--Thompson product of V with a P-indexed
    gadget, close to the existing `SV_H` / type (A) route, and possibly a duplicate
    in CA language.
- **Finite presentation.** No finite-presentation mechanism is known for subgroups of
  Aut(A^Λ). Aut(σ) itself is not even finitely generated (search summary of
  Boyle--Lind--Rudolph).

**Verdict: unclear, leaning toward a reformulation of the twisted Brin--Thompson
route.**
- **Next test:** for P = Z, compute whether ⟨ρ(Z × V), one gate⟩ has a simple
  commutator subgroup containing ρ(V), and whether that commutator is finitely
  generated.
- **Duplication check:** compare the resulting group with SV_Z of the existing
  envelope nodes.

## 5. Degree-matched subshift automorphism groups: escaping the complexity wall

**Idea.**
- Guillon--Jeandel--Kari--Vanier (arXiv:1808.09194, abstract, verbatim): "for any
  Turing degree, there exists a subshift whose automorphism group contains a subgroup
  whose word problem has exactly this degree."
- So, unlike full shifts over residually finite bases, finite-state groups or RTM,
  subshift automorphism groups as a class carry no uniform word-problem bound.
  Automorphisms also commute with the shift instead of being piecewise translations,
  so the amenable-orbit Kazhdan wall does not apply directly either.
- **Proposal.** For a decidable input G:
  - build a subshift X_G whose language encodes G's word-problem algorithm, with no
    periodic points, so that C1 fails;
  - realize G inside Aut(X_G), for example by right-translation-type CA over a base
    containing G, restricted to X_G;
  - find a finitely presented simple subgroup of Aut(X_G) containing G.

**What it would prove.** Boone--Higman, by an input-dependent host class that is not
complexity-bounded (C2 satisfied by design).

**Kill tests run.**
- **Degree versus group.** The GJKV abstract realizes word-problem degrees, not
  prescribed groups. Embedding the given G is a separate step, and I did not check
  whether their construction embeds arbitrary decidable groups.
- **Known simple subgroups are not finitely generated.**
  - Hochman (search summary): some positive-entropy Z² SFT has aut X = Z² ⊕ G with G
    locally finite and virtually simple.
  - HKS: the simple stabilized subgroup is a directed union.
  - So every infinite simple subgroup of a Z^d-subshift automorphism group I found is
    locally finite or not finitely generated.
  - Infinite finitely generated simple subgroups do exist over simple bases: V ≤
    Aut(A^V) by approach 4's right translations. But a fixed base gives a fixed host,
    and C2 kills that.
- **No finite-presentation input.** As in approach 4.

**Verdict: unclear. This is the best CA-specific survivor.**
- **Missing input:** a finitely generated, ideally finitely presented, simple subgroup
  of Aut(X) for some subshift X over Z^d, or over an input-dependent base, whose
  automorphism group is not complexity-bounded.
- **Next tests:**
  - Does any Z² SFT automorphism group contain Thompson's V, or any infinite finitely
    generated simple group, by a marker construction?
  - Does the GJKV construction embed a prescribed decidable group, or only a degree?

## Constraints this lens adds

- **C1.** Dense periodic points, in particular a residually finite base, force
  residually finite automorphism groups. A universal CA host needs a
  non-residually-finite base or a subshift without periodic points.
- **C2.** A host whose finitely generated subgroups are LEF, or a fixed base and
  alphabet with one recursive bound, is not universal. The subshift or base must
  depend on the input. GJKV shows the degree of the word problem can be tuned.
- **C3.** The known infinite simple subgroups of subshift automorphism groups (Hochman,
  Hartman--Kra--Schmieding) are locally finite or directed unions. A finitely
  generated container needs a new marker mechanism, and finite presentation needs an
  input nobody has.
- **C4.** CA undecidability (torsion, reversibility, nilpotency) gives no hereditary
  obstruction, because the fp simple group 2V already has undecidable torsion problem.
