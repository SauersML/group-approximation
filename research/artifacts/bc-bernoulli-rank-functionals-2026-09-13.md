# Rank functionals at finite-support configurations: the Bernoulli comparison is injective, and what a surjectivity failure must look like

Lane `ex-bc-measured-coefficient`, 2026-09-13. Supports `bernoulli-assembly-injective-beyond-the-host` (route `-proof`).
Continues `research/artifacts/bc-bernoulli-cylinder-comparison-2026-09-13.md` (cited below as CC), whose notation is
kept.

## 0. Setting

- `G` is a countable torsion-free group, and `Z` is a free countable `G`-set. Take `Z = G` in Section 2.
- `Λ = {0, ..., n}` with `n >= 1`, and `X = Λ^Z`. We write `U_z = {y : y_z ≠ 0}`, `I_k = C_0({|supp y| >= k})`, and
  `I_1 = C_0(X \ {0})`.
- A **pattern** is a pair `s = (F, a)` with `F ⊆ Z` finite and nonempty and `a : F -> Λ \ {0}`. Its configuration
  `x_s` equals `a` on `F` and `0` elsewhere, and its cylinder is `C_s = {y : y|_F = a}`.
- Patterns and nonzero finite-support configurations are the same thing. Write `t <= x` when `t = (F, x|_F)` for some
  nonempty `F ⊆ supp x`.
- `T_r : ⊕_([s] ∈ G\S) Z -> K_0(I_1 ⋊_r G)` sends the generator of `[s]` to `[1_(C_s)]`. Also
  `K_1(c_0(S) ⋊_r G) = 0` (CC, item 4).

## 1. Rank functionals and injectivity

**Lemma R1 (orbit representations land in the compacts).** Let `x ≠ 0` have finite support. Let `π_x` be the regular
representation of `C(X) ⋊_r G` on `l^2(G)` induced from evaluation at `x`, so that `f ∈ C(X)` acts diagonally by
`f(h^(-1)·x)` at `δ_h`. Then `π_x(I_1 ⋊_r G) ⊆ K(l^2 G)`.

*Proof.* A regular representation is bounded by the reduced norm, so `π_x` is defined on `C(X) ⋊_r G`. For
`f ∈ C_c(X \ {0})`, compactness gives `supp f ⊆ U_(z_1) ∪ ... ∪ U_(z_m)`. Now `f(h^(-1)x) ≠ 0` forces
`z_i ∈ supp(h^(-1)x) = h^(-1) supp x` for some `i`. That allows at most `m |supp x|` elements `h`, because the action on
`Z` is free. So `π_x(f)` has finite rank. Elements `f u_g` with such `f` span a dense subspace of `I_1 ⋊_r G`, and
their images have finite rank. QED.

Define `r_x = K_0(π_x) : K_0(I_1 ⋊_r G) -> K_0(K) = Z`. Since `π_(gx)` is unitarily equivalent to `π_x`, `r_x` depends
only on the orbit of `x`.

**Lemma R2 (ranks of cylinders count occurrences).** `r_x[1_(C_s)] = #{t <= x : t ∈ G s}`.

*Proof.* `π_x(1_(C_s))` is the diagonal projection onto the `δ_h` with `h^(-1)x ∈ C_s`, that is, with `h·s <= x`.
The map `h ↦ h·s` is injective: if `h·s = s` then `h` permutes `F`, so a power of `h` fixes a point of the free
`G`-set `Z`, and that power is `e`. Since `G` is torsion-free, `h = e`. QED.

**Proposition R3 (the comparison is injective).** `T_r` is injective in both degrees.

*Proof.* Degree 1: the source is `0`. Degree 0: take finitely supported `m ∈ ⊕_([s]) Z` with `T_r(m) = 0`. By R2,
`r_x(T_r m) = Σ_(t <= x) m_([t])` for every finite-support `x ≠ 0`. Suppose `m ≠ 0`. Choose `[s']` with
`m_([s']) ≠ 0` and `|F_(s')|` minimal, and put `x = x_(s')`. A sub-pattern `t <= x` with `m_([t]) ≠ 0` has
`|F_t| >= |supp x|`, hence `t = x`. So `0 = r_x(T_r m) = m_([s'])`, a contradiction. QED.

**Corollary R4.**
- `mu_(G, I_1)` is injective in both degrees (CC, item 3).
- Reduced assembly for `F_p wr_Z G` is injective iff reduced assembly for `G` is (CC, items 1 and 6).
- So beyond the host, question (Q) at a Bernoulli coefficient can fail only by **non-surjectivity**. That is the same
  sign as the only known monster coefficient failure (Finn-Sell Theorem 3.7, recorded in
  `monster-bc-detector-lives-on-a-proper-orbit-ideal`).

## 2. Anatomy of a surjectivity failure (`Z = G`)

**Lemma R5 (pattern expansion).** For `y ∈ K_0(I_1 ⋊_r G)`, define `n(y) ∈ ∏_([s]) Z` by Möbius inversion over the
Boolean lattice of sub-patterns:

`n_([x])(y) = Σ_(t <= x) (-1)^(|supp x| - |F_t|) r_(x_t)(y)`.

Then `r_x(y) = Σ_(t <= x) n_([t])(y)` for every finite-support `x ≠ 0`. By R2 and R3, `y ∈ im T_r` iff `n(y)` is
finitely supported and `y = T_r(n(y))`.

**Lemma R6 (truncation).** Let `n^(<k)(y)` be the restriction of `n(y)` to patterns with `|F| < k`. Then
`y - T_r(n^(<k)(y))` lies in the image of `K_0(I_k ⋊_r G)`.

*Proof.*
- Let `P_k = {1 <= |supp| <= k-1}`. This is closed in `X \ {0}`, and `I_1 / I_k = C_0(P_k)`.
- `C_0(P_k)` has a finite filtration with successive quotients `c_0(S_j)`, `j < k`. Each has equal maximal and reduced
  crossed products.
- Equality of the two crossed products passes to extensions. If `x` is in the kernel of the comparison for the middle
  algebra, it maps to `0` in the maximal crossed product of the quotient, so it lies in the ideal's maximal crossed
  product, where the comparison is injective. So `C_0(P_k) ⋊_max G = C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G`.
- Lemma E of CC then makes `0 -> I_k ⋊_r G -> I_1 ⋊_r G -> C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G -> 0` exact.
- By CC item 5 (finite part), `K_0(C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G)` is freely generated by the cylinder classes with `|F| < k`. So
  `K_0(C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G) ≅ ⊕_(|F| < k) Z` and `K_1(C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G) = 0`.
- The functionals `r_x` with `|supp x| < k` kill `I_k`, so they factor through `C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G`. The argument of R3
  shows they detect that group.
- The image of `y - T_r(n^(<k))` in `K_0(C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G)` has all these ranks equal to `0`, so it vanishes.
  Exactness finishes. QED.

**Lemma R7 (eventual additivity).** For each `y` there is a finite `B ⊆ G` with the following property. Whenever `x_1`
and `x_2` have finite, nonempty supports with `supp x_2 ∩ (supp x_1)·B = ∅`,

`r_(x_1 + x_2)(y) = r_(x_1)(y) + r_(x_2)(y)`.

Hence `n_([t])(y) = 0` for every pattern whose support splits into two nonempty `B`-separated parts.

*Proof.*
- **Reduction.** It is enough to treat `y = [p]` with `p ∈ M_m(I_1 ⋊_r G)` a projection.
- **Approximation.** Choose a self-adjoint `a ∈ M_m(C_c(G, C_c(X \ {0})))` with locally constant coefficients and
  `||a - p|| < 1/4`. Let `E ⊆ G` be its group support, enlarged to contain `e`. Let `W ⊆ G` be a finite set such that every coefficient depends
  only on the coordinates in `W` and is supported in `∪_(w ∈ W) U_w`.
- **Where `π_x(a)` lives.** The row of `π_x(a)` at `δ_h` is nonzero only when `supp x ∩ hW ≠ ∅`. Its entries depend on
  `x|_(hW)`, and its columns lie in `hE`.
- **Block splitting.** Take `B = W^(-1) E E^(-1) W ∪ W^(-1) W`. If `supp x_2 ∩ (supp x_1)·B = ∅`, the rows split into
  those meeting `supp x_1` and those meeting `supp x_2`. The column sets are disjoint, and on each block `x` agrees
  with `x_1` (resp. `x_2`). So `π_(x_1+x_2)(a) = π_(x_1)(a) + π_(x_2)(a)` with orthogonal supports.
- **Ranks.** For any finite-support `x`, `||π_x(a) - π_x(p)|| < 1/4`, so `χ_([1/2, ∞))(π_x(a))` is a projection within
  `1/2` of `π_x(p)` and has the same rank. Functional calculus respects the orthogonal splitting, which gives
  additivity.
- **Möbius vanishing.** Set `r_∅ = 0`. Sub-patterns of `t = t_1 ⊔ t_2` are `u_1 ⊔ u_2` with `u_i <= t_i`. Additivity
  gives `n_([t]) = Σ_(u_1, u_2) (-1)^(|t_1|-|u_1|) (-1)^(|t_2|-|u_2|) (r_(u_1) + r_(u_2))`, and each alternating sum
  over `u_2 <= t_2` (resp. `u_1`) vanishes because `t_2 ≠ ∅` (resp. `t_1 ≠ ∅`). QED.

**Corollary R8 (the three shapes of a Bernoulli witness beyond the host).** `T_r` fails to be surjective iff at least
one of the following holds.
- **(a) Infinite expansion.** Some `y ∈ K_0(I_1 ⋊_r G)` has `n(y) ≠ 0` on `B`-connected patterns of unbounded size.
- **(b) Rank-invisible tail class.** Some nonzero `y` lies in `∩_k im K_0(I_k ⋊_r G)` and has `r_x(y) = 0` for every
  finite-support `x`.
- **(c) Odd class.** `K_1(I_1 ⋊_r G) ≠ 0`. By exactness of the truncations, every such class comes from
  `K_1(I_k ⋊_r G)` for every `k`.

*Proof.* If `n(y)` is infinitely supported, R7 gives (a). If it is finitely supported, `y - T_r(n(y))` has all ranks
`0` and, by R6, lies in every `im K_0(I_k ⋊_r G)`; so `y ∉ im T_r` gives (b). Degree 1 is (c), using
`K_1(C_0(P_k) ⋊_r G) = 0`. Conversely each shape is a class outside `im T_r`. QED.

## 3. Consequences for the crux

- **Proper-orbit rank detectors do exist on dual-module coefficients**, on the ideal of the fixed point. The finite-support
  configurations form proper orbits that are not open. Their orbit representations send `I_1 ⋊_r G` into the compacts
  (R1), which is the Higson--Lafforgue--Skandalis rank at infinity in module form. This does not contradict
  `module-dual-actions-have-no-wandering-open-sets`, which is about open invariant sets. They certify injectivity.
  They also see shape (a), but not (b) or (c).
- A counterexample to trivial-coefficient Baum--Connes for `F_p wr G`, over a torsion-free host `G` that satisfies the
  conjecture, would be a projection over `C_0((Z/p)^G \ {0}) ⋊_r G` of shape (a) or (b), or an odd class (c).
  Shape (a) is the natural home of expander phenomena. A rank function `x ↦ r_x(p)` that is not a finite combination
  of pattern counts needs `B`-connected contributions from arbitrarily large patterns, such as the
  finite-support markings of large embedded graphs.

## 4. Model tests

- `G = Z = Z`, `n = 1`: by CC Section 4, `T_r` is an isomorphism, so shapes (a)--(c) are absent, as Baum--Connes for
  `F_2 wr Z` requires. R2 on `x = δ_0 + δ_1` and `s = (F = {0}, a = 1)` gives `r_x[1_(C_s)] = 2`, the two
  one-site sub-patterns.
- `n = 0`: there are no patterns and `I_1 = 0`.
- R3 needs the free action. With finite nontrivial stabilizers, `r_x` sees only dimensions. The representation-ring
  summands of `K_0(c_0(S) ⋊_r G)` are not covered here.

## 5. Scope

- The rank functionals are the standard orbit-representation device. The injectivity statement R4 for wreath products
  over torsion-free groups may be known; bounded check: not found in CEKN Sections 1--2.
- R5--R8 are new here as far as this lane knows. They are elementary consequences of R1--R2 and CC.
