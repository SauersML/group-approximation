# Problem I: separable simple reduction

Kaplansky's bounded-quasitrace problem is unchanged if it is restricted to
separable unital simple stably finite non-elementary C*-algebras.

Starting with a hypothetical nonadditive quasitrace, the classical
AW*-factor reduction gives a finite AW*-factor `M` with faithful nonadditive
dimension quasitrace.  Finite AW*-factors are simple as C*-algebras: a
nonzero positive element dominates a nonzero spectral projection, and
finite-factor comparison makes finitely many copies of that projection
full.  A standard recursive fullness construction then puts the two
elements witnessing nonadditivity inside a separable unital simple
C*-subalgebra `B` of `M`.

The restricted quasitrace stays faithful and nonadditive.  Its matrix
extension rules out proper isometries in every matrix algebra over `B`, so
`B` is stably finite; finite-dimensionality is impossible because every
finite-dimensional quasitrace is linear.  Haagerup's theorem additionally
shows that `B` must be nonexact.

This is a reduction, not a solution: simplicity and separability do not
supply exactness.  It does show that neither a complicated ideal lattice nor
nonseparability can be essential to a counterexample.
