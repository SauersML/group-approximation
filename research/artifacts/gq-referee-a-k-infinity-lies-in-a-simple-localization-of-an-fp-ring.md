## Item 1: the presentation (the author asked about this)
- *The relations hold in `T`.* `y·t = t_(1+s) = t + t_s = t + x·t`.
- *The group.* The subgroup of `T'` generated by `x` and `y` is abelian on two generators and maps onto `Z^2`, so it
  is `Z^2`.
- *`L ≅ V`.*
  - `L = Z[Z^2]·t` under conjugation, and it is killed by `y − 1 − x`. So it is a quotient of
    `Z[x^±,y^±]/(y−1−x) = Z[s^±,(1+s)^(−1)] = V`.
  - The composite `V → L → V`, `r ↦ r·t ↦ t_r`, is the identity. So `V → L` is injective, and `L ≅ V`. ✓
- *The Baumslag induction.*
  - Conjugation by `x^a` reduces everything to `c_k = [t, t_k]` with `k ≥ 1`.
  - `y` acts on `L` as `1 + x`. So `y^k·t = Σ_i C(k,i) t_i` and `y^k·t_1 = Σ_j C(k,j) t_(j+1)`.
  - `[t_i, t_(j+1)]` has gap `j + 1 − i ∈ [1−k, k+1]`. Every gap of absolute value `≤ k` vanishes by the hypothesis,
    transported by `x`, and `|1−k| ≤ k`.
  - The only gap `k+1` term is `i = 0`, `j = k`, with coefficient 1. So `y^k·c_1 = c_(k+1) = 0`. ✓
  - For `g·t` and `h·t` in general, conjugate by `y^m` with `m` large. This makes both `Z`-combinations of the `t_i`,
    and conjugation is injective. ✓
- *The normal form.*
  - `P'` is generated by the pairwise commuting `L ≅ V`, so it is a quotient of `Sym(V) = P` that maps onto `P`, and
    hence `P' ≅ P`.
  - `P'` is stable under conjugation by the group (N3). So `g p = (g·p)g` moves every group element to the right, and
    `T' = Σ_g P'g`.
  - In `T` the `g` form a `P`-basis, so `T' → T` is injective. ✓

## Item 2: simplicity (the author asked about this)
- *The localization.* `S` is `Z^2`-invariant, so it is an Ore set in `T`, and `S^(−1)T = K_∞ ⋊ Z^2`.
- *Faithfulness.* `s^a(1+s)^b = 1` only for `a = b = 0`, so the action on `t` is faithful, hence on `K_∞`.
- *Minimal support.*
  - Normalize so that `f_1 = 1`. Then `λα − αλ = Σ (λ − g(λ)) f_g g` loses the term at 1, so it is 0.
  - So every `g` in the support fixes `K`, which gives `g = 1` and `1 ∈ I`. ✓
  - The shift on `Q(t_k : k ∈ Z)` is faithful, so `K_∞[x^±; σ]` is simple as well.

## Item 3: no finitely generated subring contains `Frac(P)` (the author asked about this)
- *The coefficient ring.* Products of the `a_i` have coefficients in `L_0`, the ring generated by the `G`-translates
  of the finitely many `f_(i,g)`.
  - `h(n/d) = h(n)/h(d)` with `h(d) = Π h(e_j)^(k_j)`. So `L_0 ⊆ P[1/h(e_j)]`, a localization of the UFD `P` at primes,
    since each `h(e_j)` is irreducible.
- *Units.* The units of `P[1/h(e_j)]` are the scalars times products of the inverted primes. So `1/q ∈ L_0` forces
  `q = c·h(e_j)`, and `deg q = deg e_j`, because `G` preserves degree.
- *The witness.* `t^d + 2` is irreducible in `Q[t]` by Eisenstein at 2. It stays irreducible in the polynomial ring
  in all variables, since a factorization cannot involve the other variables. Its degrees are unbounded. ✓
- *The application.* It applies to `P_Q = P ⊗ Q = Sym_Q(V ⊗ Q)` with the linear substitutions `t_v ↦ t_(hv)`, and to
  the shift (N2). ✓

## Attempt 4, the "rigorous" bullet (checked as asked)
- *Step 1.* Conjugate `s_m^* M s_m = tM` by `u`. The left side is fixed, and the right side becomes
  `(utu^(−1))(uMu^(−1)) = (utu^(−1))M`. So `(t − utu^(−1))M = 0`.
- *Step 2.* Multiply on the left by `s_v^*` and on the right by `s_v`. Since `s_v^*` commutes with `t` and `utu^(−1)`,
  this gives `(t − utu^(−1))·s_v^* M s_v = t − utu^(−1) = 0`. ✓
- Only `s_v^*` needs to commute with `t` and `utu^(−1)`. The hypothesis on `s_v` can be dropped.

## Nits
- **N1 (the Setting).** "`P = Sym_Z(V)`, a domain" needs `V` to be free abelian, and it is.
  - `s` and `1+s` are comaximal over `Z`, since `(1+s) − s = 1`, and `1+s` is monic linear. So partial fractions
    over `Z` give `V = Z[s^±] ⊕ ⊕_(b≥1) Z·(1+s)^(−b)`.
  - Hence `P` is a polynomial ring over `Z` in countably many variables, and `P ⊗ Q` is one over `Q`. Item 2 uses this
    for `Frac(P) = K_∞`, and item 3 for the UFD property. Add the line.
- **N2 (item 3, the last bullet).** Say that the polynomial ring over `Q` is `P ⊗ Q`, not `P` itself. The
  statement of item 3 is over `Q`.
- **N3 (item 1, the normal form).** Record that `P'` is stable under conjugation by `x^(±1)` and `y^(±1)`, because
  `L` is. This is what justifies `g p = (g·p) g` inside `T'`.
