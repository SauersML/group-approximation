# Descending links of the loop braid cloning system: structure, obstruction, and plan

Lane z2-12-loop-bv, 2026-09-13. Unreviewed notes. The definitions are those of Witzel–Zaremsky
(WZ), arXiv:1405.5491v3, §5.3, applied to the loop braid system of §10. Conventions (products as
right actions, the left form L, the merge maps δ_k) are as in
`zp-loop-braid-cloning-properly-graded-2026-09-13.md`.

## 1. The complex

A simplex of `L_n(LB_*)` is a class `[g, Γ]`, with `g ∈ LB_n` and Γ a nontrivial matching of the
linear graph `L_n`. The relation `(g_1, Γ_1) ~ (g_2, Γ_2)` holds when:
- both matchings have m edges;
- `g_2^{-1} g_1 ∈ im κ_{Γ_1}`;
- `Γ_2 = (g_2^{-1} g_1)κ_{Γ_1}^{-1} · Γ_1`.

`LB_n` acts on the left. By WZ Observation 5.8 and the proper grading theorem, this is the
descending link at a vertex with n feet. What still has to be shown is that the connectivity of
`L_n(LB_*)` tends to infinity (WZ Proposition 5.9).

**Connected for n ≥ 5.** The proof of WZ Lemma 5.10 (p. 32) connects any vertex to `[1, J_1]`,
using only two facts: `G_n` is generated by cloning images, and `M(L_n)` is connected for `n ≥ 5`.
Both hold for `LB_n` (proper-grading artifact, §4). So `L_n(LB_*)` is 0-connected for `n ≥ 5`.

## 2. A strict fundamental domain for the pure group

Let `P = PLB_n` act by left multiplication, and let `Q = {[s, Γ] : s ∈ S_n = ⟨σ_1, …, σ_{n−1}⟩}`.

- (a) `Q ≅ L_n(S_*)`, the descending link of Thompson's V.
  - The restriction of κ to `S_*` is the symmetric-group cloning ς, since both are cloning systems
    given by the same generator formulas.
  - If `s ∈ S_n` equals `(h)κ_Γ` for some `h ∈ LB_{n−m}`, then `h ∈ S_{n−m}`. Proof: by Lemma A,
    `δ_Γ(L(h)(x_i))` is a generator or a product `x_j x_{j+1}`, so every conjugator of `L(h)`
    centralizes a generator image and is trivial (F1).
  - So `L_n(S_*) → Q` is injective. Vertices of `L_n(S_*)` are ordered pairs `(a, b)` of distinct
    indices, and simplices are disjoint families of such pairs: the ordered matching complex of
    `K_n`.
- (b) Every simplex is P-equivalent to exactly one simplex of Q.
  - Existence: `LB_n = P ⋊ S_n`.
  - Uniqueness: if `p·[s_1, Γ_1] = [s_2, Γ_2]`, apply ρ. Using
    `ρ((h)κ_Γ) = (ρ(h))ς_Γ`, which holds on generators and propagates by (FCS1), gives
    `s_2^{-1} s_1 ∈ im ς_{Γ_1}` with the matching dangling condition.
- (c) The stabilizer of `[s, Γ]` fixes that simplex pointwise and equals `s·(PLB_{n−m})κ_Γ·s^{-1}`,
  a conjugate of a cabling subgroup.

## 3. Obstruction: not a complete join over the ordered matching complex

- Define `e: PΣAut_n → Z^{n(n−1)}` by `e_{ij}(φ)` = exponent sum of `x_j` in a conjugator `w_i`
  with `L(φ)(x_i) = w_i x_i w_i^{-1}`.
  - It is well defined, since the centralizer of `x_i` is `⟨x_i⟩`.
  - It is a homomorphism (compare Zaremsky, arXiv:1607.03043, Lemma 2.1).
- Let `H_1, H_3` be the cabling subgroups at `e_1 = {1,2}` and `e_3 = {3,4}`.
  - `e(H_1) ⊆ {e_13 = e_23, e_14 = e_24}` and `e(H_3) ⊆ {e_13 = e_14, e_23 = e_24}`.
  - So `f := e_13 − e_23 − e_14 + e_24` vanishes on `H_1 H_3`, while `f(α_13) = 1`.
- Hence the vertices `[1, e_1]` and `[α_13, e_3]` each lie over the edge `{(1,2), (3,4)}` of the
  base, but they span no simplex.
- Geometric reading:
  - a vertex is a band `b` (an arc joining two loops, with a framing) along which the two loops
    fuse into one unknotted loop;
  - the band for `(1,2)` threads through loop 3 after `α_13`;
  - fusing `(3,4)` as well then creates a Whitehead-type clasp between the fused loops (linking
    number 0, but not split).
- So compatibility is a global "the fused configuration is still an unlink" condition. Hatcher–Wahl
  joins over the matching complex fail by (3′) of their Definition 3.2
  (arXiv:0709.2173v4, p. 15).

## 4. Plan for simple connectivity (would give `V_loop` finitely presented)

- By §2, P acts on `X = L_n(LB_*)` with a strict fundamental domain `Q ≅ OM(K_n)`.
  - `OM(K_n)` is a complete join over the matching complex `M(K_n)` with 2-point fibers, so for
    large n it is simply connected (BFMWZ Theorem 3.8: `M(K_n)` is (ν(n)−1)-connected; plus
    Hatcher–Wahl Proposition 3.5).
- The standard complex-of-groups criterion: with Q simply connected, X is simply connected iff the
  direct limit of the stabilizers over Q maps isomorphically onto P. Bridson–Haefliger, Chapter
  II.12, simple complexes of groups; the exact statement is still to verify. So the target is
  **PΣAut_n is the colimit of its cabling subgroups `s H_Γ s^{-1}` over `OM(K_n)`, for n large.**
- Method: use McCool's presentation, with generators `α_ij` and relators
  `[α_ij, α_kl]`, `[α_ij, α_kj]`, `[α_ij α_kj, α_ik]`. Each relator involves at most 4 indices,
  so for `n ≥ 7` there is a cable pair disjoint from them.
  - Map each generator to the colimit through some vertex group containing it.
  - Independence of that choice follows from connectivity of the subcomplex of `OM(K_n)` whose
    vertex groups contain the generator: the pairs disjoint from `{i, j}`, which form a copy of
    `OM(K_{n−2})`.
  - Every relator is then trivial in the colimit.
- **Normalization issue.** One must show that `α_ij ∈ s H_{(a,b)} s^{-1}` whenever
  `{i,j} ∩ {a,b} = ∅`.
  - The intertwining relation determines a cabled image only up to powers of the cable twist
    `τ: x_a, x_b ↦` conjugates by `x_a x_b`.
  - The invariant `e_{ab} + e_{ba}` detects τ. The twist power should be pinned to 0 by computing
    `e_{ab}` on WZ's generator formulas, via the crossed-homomorphism extension of `e` to `LB_n`.

## 5. Higher connectivity: candidate routes

- (R1) **Band complexes in 3-space.**
  - Model the base as the complex of systems of framed bands whose fusion is an unlink, and prove
    connectivity with a Hatcher-flow and defect Morse argument, as in BFMWZ §3.
  - The lightbulb trick (Hatcher–Wahl p. 13) makes homotopy classes of arcs to boundary spheres
    behave well. The needed new ingredient is handling clasping between bands.
- (R2) **Hatcher–Wahl labeled joins.** Their Theorem 3.6 gives connectivity of a join complex over
  a wCM base when certain link subcomplexes are highly connected.
  - Candidate base: sphere systems in `B³ \ unlink` cutting off `(S¹×D²)#(S¹×D²)`.
  - Warning: a sphere around two loops is NOT preserved by cabling. Threading a third loop through
    the fused loop moves `x_j` out of the outside free factor. Vertices are tori (cabling solid
    tori), not spheres.
- (R3) **Colimits (§4) at every level.** Abels–Holz higher generation of `PΣAut_n` by cabling
  subgroups.
- (R4) **Cactus graphs.** Compare with Collins / McCullough–Miller marked cactus graphs, where a
  merge of adjacent cladodes is an elementary collapse. Brady–McCammond–Meier–Miller show the
  relevant posets are Cohen–Macaulay.
