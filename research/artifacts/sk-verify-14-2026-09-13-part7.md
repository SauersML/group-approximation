## 1. Theorem D (line 21)
**Sources checked.**
- **Krstić–McCool, via the EJZ e-print arXiv:0809.4095, l.2496–2501:** "By a theorem of Krsti\'c and McCool [Theorem 3]{KrM}, the Steinberg group $St_n(R)$ is finitely presented for any $n\geq 4$ and any finitely presented ring $R$, in particular for $R=\dbZ\la x_1,\ldots, x_d\ra$." The bibitem (l.3220–3222) is J. Pure Appl. Algebra 141 (1999) 175–183.
- **Voronetsky:** §3 below.

**Steps.**
1. **Cover.**
   - St_N(F) is generated by the x_ij(1), x_ij(t_m): x_ij(ab) = [x_ik(a), x_kj(b)], and additivity.
   - Its finitely many relators on this set map to relators of G, so they hold in Q_k for large k, and ψ_k: St_N(F) → Q_k is onto.
2. **Level ideal.**
   - K_ij = {a : ψ_k(x_ij(a)) = 1} is additive.
   - [x_ij(a), x_jl(1)] = x_il(a) and [x_li(1), x_ij(a)] = x_lj(a) make all K_ij equal (N ≥ 3).
   - [x_ij(a), x_jl(b)] = x_il(ab) makes K a two-sided ideal.
   - a ↦ ψ_k(x_12(a)) injects F/K into Q_k, so A_k = F/K is finite.
   - St_N(F)/⟨⟨x_ij(K)⟩⟩ = St_N(A_k).
3. **Central kernel.** A_k is finite, hence semilocal, so K_2(N, A_k) is central in St_N(A_k) (§3). Its image in the nonabelian simple Q_k is central, hence trivial.
4. **Radical.**
   - J = J(A_k) is nilpotent, and E_N(A_k) → E_N(A_k/J) is onto.
   - Its kernel lies in I + M_N(J), which is nilpotent: [I + M_N(J^i), I + M_N(J^j)] ⊆ I + M_N(J^{i+j}).
   - The image of a nilpotent normal subgroup in a nonabelian simple group is trivial.
5. **Semisimple part.**
   - A_k/J = ∏M_{d_i}(F_{q_i}), and E_N = ∏SL_{Nd_i}(F_{q_i}), which are perfect since Nd_i ≥ 4.
   - The images of the factors are normal and commute. Two nontrivial images would make Q_k abelian, so exactly one surjects.
   - The centre dies, and PSL_{Nd}(F_q) is simple, so Q_k ≅ PSL_{Nd}(F_q), with T_k matching the images of e_ij(1), e_ij(ρ_k t_m).
6. **Relations.** For p ∈ ker π the relator e_12(πp) = 1 holds in Q_k for large k. So p ∈ K and ρ_k(p) = 0.
7. **Ranks.** For G infinite simple, d_k → ∞ by Proposition B3 (reviewed PASS in part 1 of this review).

## 2. Theorem E (line 22)
- **Invariance of Div.** If (Q_k,T_k) → (G,T) and each t′ ∈ T′ is a word w_{t′} in T, then for large k the words w_{t′}(T_k) generate Q_k (the relators t = v_t(w(T)) hold). The rewritten sequence converges to (G,T′).
- **Natural dimension.** For large M the dimension of PSL_M(F_q) is determined by the group.
- **⊇.**
  - By Theorem D and invariance, every converging sequence is eventually PSL_{Nd_k}(F_{q_k}) with ring models ρ_k.
  - 2 ∈ ker π, so eventually q_k is a power of 2.
  - For c ∈ Cyc(X), the cyclic-factor relations (orthogonal idempotents summing to 1, cyclically conjugated by ρ_k(τ_+)) are finitely many elements of ker π, so they hold for large k. Conjugate idempotents have equal F_{q_k}-rank, so c | d_k, and Nc | M_k = Nd_k.
- **⊆.**
  - Choose levels r_k → ∞ slowly, and distinct primes p_k ≠ p′_k. The sizes g_{r_k}p_k and g_{r_k}p′_k are large multiples of g_{r_k}.
  - Theorem C(2) of part 2 (reviewed PASS in part 2 of this review) gives primitive periodic ring models of both sizes, faithful through degrees → ∞. So both sequences SL_{N·size}(F_2) converge to (G_X^{(N)}, T); the degree argument works in every rank N.
  - m ∈ Div divides N g_{r_k}p_k and N g_{r_k}p′_k, hence N g_{r_k}. And g_{r_k} ∈ Cyc(X), by the grading of W_{r_k} (Lemma C1).
- **Recovery.** Cyc(X) is divisor-closed and lcm-closed (product factor maps onto cyclic groups, by minimality). So Div is exactly the set of divisors of the supernatural number N·g_∞(X), which gives the invariance statement and, for N = N′, Cyc(X) = Cyc(Y).
- **Note.** The correction F1 of part 2 (degree 6r−4) does not affect Theorem E, which uses only "finitely many relations are eventually killed".

## 4. Rank lengths (line 34)
- **Invariance.** ℓ_μ(xgx^{−1}) = rk_μ(x(g−I)x^{−1}) = ℓ_μ(g), because rk(ab) ≤ min(rk a, rk b) and x is invertible.
- **Symmetry.** g^{−1} − I = −g^{−1}(g−I).
- **Subadditivity.** gh − I = (g−I)h + (h−I).
- **Faithfulness on M_3(R).** If a ≠ 0 has a nonzero entry a_ij, then rk(a) ≥ rk(e_ii a e_jj) = rk_μ(a_ij) > 0.
- **Root elements.** rE_ij = (rE_ii)Π with Π a permutation matrix, so ℓ_μ(e_ij(r)) = rk_μ(r) and ℓ_μ(e_12(χ_U)) = μ(U).
- **Lower bound.** If h is a product of N conjugates of g^{±1}, then ℓ_μ(h) ≤ Nℓ_μ(g). So ν_{e_12(χ_V)}(e_12(1)) ≥ 1/μ(V) for every ergodic μ.
- **Extreme points.** The supremum over M_T(X) is attained at ergodic measures, because the ratio of positive affine functions is quasi-affine.
- **Unboundedness.** For clopen V shrinking to a point, sup_μ μ(V) → 0 (compactness, nonatomic invariant measures of a minimal infinite system). So the normal generation numbers are unbounded, and G is not uniformly simple.
- **Dependencies.** The rank function is `cantor-z-crossed-product-embeds-in-continuous-factor` (Ara–Claramunt, by citation). Simplicity for subshifts is also the note's Theorem 1.
