# Full finite-packet averaging does not preserve operator norm under spectral cuts

2026-09-08. This is a counterexample to a specific proposed norm
promotion. Positive cutoffs obtained from nested whole finite-subgroup
averages can commute exactly with the complete third `GL_3(F_2)`
packet and almost commute with a shift in operator norm. Nevertheless,
every nonzero spectral cut can have a shift commutator whose operator
norm is bounded below by `sqrt(167/168)`.

The commutator ranks of all cuts at thresholds in `[1/4,3/4]`
are negligible relative to the marked support rank. Their normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norms tend to zero. Thus neither whole-packet
averaging nor this rank budget upgrades that spectral-cut step to
operator-norm control.

These are exact finite models of the specified core, packet, and shift
data. They are not models of all defining relations of
`Gamma_triangle`. In particular they do not settle the marked
non-MF problem. The construction tests the step after the positive
cutoff in [the tracial-core proof](jacobson-surviving-head-has-a-tracial-core-2026-09-08.md),
with the entire finite packet from
[the coherent quotient](jacobson-coherent-quotient-and-four-word-matrix-gap-2026-09-08.md)
present exactly.

## 1. One exact finite ambient group at every stage

Fix an integer `N>=4` and put `m=2N+3`. Let

```text
W_N=directSum_(j in Z/mZ) F_2^3,
Q_N=GL(W_N),
d_N=|Q_N|,
lambda_N:Q_N -> U(ell^2(Q_N))
```

be the complex left regular representation. All matrices below are
operators on this single space of dimension `d_N`. The construction
is mathematical; no enumeration of these large finite groups is needed.

Use representatives `-N-1,...,N+1` for the cyclic block indices.
For `0<=i<=N`, let `K_i` be the full general linear group on
the blocks `[-i,i]`, acting as identity on the other blocks. Thus

```text
K_i ~= GL_(3(2i+1))(F_2),
K_0 < K_1 < ... < K_N <= Q_N.
```

Let `s` be the cyclic block shift and put `U_N=lambda_N(s)`.
There is no wrap at any interval endpoint used below. Consequently

```text
s K_i s^(-1) <= K_(i+1),
K_i <= s K_(i+1) s^(-1)                 (0<=i<N).     (1)
```

Let `D<=Q_N` be the diagonal copy of `GL_3(F_2)` acting
by the same matrix on every block. Its four elements

```text
h=w_12,       u=x_21,       b=w_23,       zeta=x_23
```

have precisely the full finite third-packet multiplication table.
In particular, writing `v=h u h` and `d=h zeta h`, one has

```text
(h b)^3=1,       [u,d]=zeta,       b v b=d.
```

This is the complete faithful group `D~=GL_3(F_2)`, not just
a selection of these relators. It normalizes every `K_i` and
commutes with `s`. Every subgroup generated by `K_i,D` is an
actual finite subgroup of `Q_N`, so all their core and covariance
relations hold in the same regular representation.

The block coordinates can be labeled as the three positions in a
staggered triple. Here the diagonal packet acts on every cyclic block;
this does not impose the infinite one-sided boundary pattern of the
full Jacobson presentation.

## 2. Whole-subgroup cutoffs with a positive marked rank

For a subgroup `H<=Q_N`, set

```text
P_H=(1/|H|) sum_(g in H) lambda_N(g),
p_H=I-P_H.
```

The first is the orthogonal projection onto the `H`-fixed
space, of rank `d_N/|H|`. Put

```text
P_i=P_(K_i),             p_i=I-P_i,
e_N=(1/N) sum_(i=0)^(N-1) p_i.                        (2)
```

The `p_i` are increasing projections. Let `w` be a standard
transvection on block zero, for example its `x_13`, and put

```text
p_w=(I-lambda_N(w))/2,
r_N=rank(p_w)=d_N/2.                                  (3)
```

The rank in (3) follows because left multiplication by the
nonidentity involution `w` partitions `Q_N` into two-cycles.
Since `w in K_0`, every `p_i` contains `p_w`. Thus

```text
0<=e_N<=I,             e_N p_w=p_w,
rank(e_N)=rank(p_(N-1))
          =d_N(1-1/|K_(N-1)|)<2 r_N.                 (4)
```

The packet `D` normalizes `K_i`, so every `p_i` and
`e_N` commutes exactly with its entire representation. The
whole finite-group conditional expectation

```text
E_D(X)=(1/168) sum_(g in D) lambda_N(g) X lambda_N(g)^*
```

therefore leaves all these operators unchanged. It also leaves
their spectral projections unchanged.

Write `a_i=U_N p_i U_N^*`. The subgroup inclusions (1) give
`a_i<=p_(i+1)` and `p_i<=a_(i+1)`. Summing each inequality
for `0<=i<N` gives

```text
U_N e_N U_N^*-e_N <= (p_N-p_0)/N <= I/N,
e_N-U_N e_N U_N^* <= (a_N-a_0)/N <= I/N.
```

Consequently

```text
||[e_N,U_N]||_op <= 1/N.                              (5)
```

This uses full subgroup averages and exact finite covariance, with
no error in a presentation or finite-group correction.

## 3. Every nonzero spectral cut is one of the subgroup projections

Set `p_(-1)=0`. The nested projections decompose their union
into orthogonal layers `p_j-p_(j-1)`, for `0<=j<N`.
On that layer, `e_N` has eigenvalue `(N-j)/N`; on
`I-p_(N-1)` it has eigenvalue zero. Therefore, for every
`0<t<=1`,

```text
q_(N,t)=1_[t,1](e_N)=p_j,
j=min(N-1,floor(N(1-t))).                            (6)
```

Every such cut contains `p_w`, has rank less than `2 r_N`,
and commutes exactly with the entire group `D`. In particular,
the marked negative eigenspace occupies more than half of its rank.

## 4. A uniform operator-norm lower bound for every cut

Take `H=K_j` and `H'=s K_j s^(-1)`. They have the same
order. In the regular representation use the unit vector

```text
xi_H=|H|^(-1/2) sum_(g in H) delta_g.
```

Then `P_H xi_H=xi_H`. Distinct cosets of `H` are
disjoint, so

```text
||P_(H') xi_H||^2
 =<P_(H') xi_H,xi_H>
 =|H intersect H'|/|H'|.
```

As `P_(H')` is an orthogonal projection, this proves

```text
||P_H-P_(H')||_op
 >=sqrt(1-|H intersect H'|/|H|).                      (7)
```

Let `r=3(2j+1)` denote the number of finite-field coordinates
of `K_j`, distinct from the complex marked rank `r_N`.
The intervals `[-j,j]` and `[-j+1,j+1]` overlap in `2j`
blocks. The intersection of their full coordinate groups is exactly
the full coordinate group on that overlap. Thus

```text
H intersect H' ~= GL_(r-3)(F_2),
|H intersect H'|/|H|=|GL_(r-3)(F_2)|/|GL_r(F_2)|.
```

Use `|GL_0(F_2)|=1`. The block diagonal subgroup
`GL_(r-3)(F_2) x GL_3(F_2)` of `GL_r(F_2)` shows
that this ratio is at most `1/168`. Since complements of
projections have the same norm difference, (6)--(7) give

```text
||[q_(N,t),U_N]||_op >= sqrt(167/168)
                                      (0<t<=1).      (8)
```

Thus no choice of a positive spectral threshold makes the shift
commutator small in operator norm, despite (4)--(5) and exact
commutation with the full third packet.

## 5. The relative commutator rank and Hilbert--Schmidt norm still vanish

For the cut `q_(N,t)=p_j`, the commutator has the same rank
as `P_j-U_N P_j U_N^*`. Hence

```text
rank([q_(N,t),U_N]) <= 2 d_N/|K_j|,
rank([q_(N,t),U_N])/r_N <= 4/|K_j|.                    (9)
```

The norm of a difference of two orthogonal projections is at most
one. Thus its unnormalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm satisfies

```text
||[q_(N,t),U_N]||_HS/sqrt(r_N) <= 2/sqrt(|K_j|).       (10)
```

If `t in [1/4,3/4]`, (6) gives `j>=floor(N/4)`.
The group orders `|K_j|=|GL_(3(2j+1))(F_2)|` tend to
infinity with `j`. The right sides of (9)--(10) consequently
tend to zero uniformly over that whole threshold interval.
Normalizing the Hilbert--Schmidt norm instead by
`sqrt(rank(q_(N,t)))` only improves this bound, since the
cut contains `p_w`.

The operator-norm floor (8) holds throughout the same interval.
The part lost by a cut is small in relative rank, but the regular
coset vector used in (7) still detects a large operator-norm error.

## 6. Exact scope of the counterexample

The construction simultaneously has whole finite-subgroup cutoffs,
exact finite-core covariance, an exact faithful third `GL_3`
packet, a surviving involution with fixed positive rank density,
and an operator-norm almost central positive cutoff. Twirling over
the whole third packet changes none of its spectral cuts. A rank
error tending to zero on the head scale also fails to repair the
operator-norm conclusion for those cuts.

It disproves that particular promotion of the spectral coarea step.
It does not rule out choosing a different reducing projection:
the identity itself is available in this example and has rank
`2 r_N`. Nor does it represent all of `B,C`, the original
one-sided boundary equations, or the full stable-letter presentation
of `Gamma_triangle`. An argument using those additional relations,
or a different way to construct a matrix carrier, is not excluded.
No positive or zero value of `gamma_triangle` is proved here.
