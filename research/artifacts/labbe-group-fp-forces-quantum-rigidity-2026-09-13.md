## 0. Summary

**Theorem Q.** Let `X ⊆ A^(Z^2)` be a free minimal SFT and `k` a field with `char k ∉ {3, 5}`.
If `G = D([[Z^2 ~ X]])` is finitely presented, then `X` is `D`-quantum rigid over `k` for all large `D`.
Hence `LC(X, k) ⋊ Z^2` is finitely presented (`sft-crossed-product-fp-iff-quantum-rigid`).

- **For Labbé's shift.** Finite presentation of `D([[Omega_U]])` would give
  `labbe-wang-shift-crossed-product-is-finitely-presented` for every `F_q` with `q` prime to `15`. By `labbe-shift-is-wall-rigid`, that means `J_D = 0`. So the group problem contains the
  ring problem, and a noncommuting operator family at every scale refutes the group claim.
- **Engine.** A single relation of `G`, `[a, b]^3 = 1` for two 3-cycles whose site triples share one
  site, together with the same relation for the complementary pattern set, forces the two coefficient
  idempotents to commute. This is Lemma NC, an exact certificate in the free algebra on two idempotents.
- Part 1 (all fields, finite-dimensional families only) remains the characteristic-free statement.

## 1. Lemma NC: a two-idempotent certificate

- `T = k<P, Q | P^2 = P, Q^2 = Q>`.
- On `k^5` put `alpha = (0 1 2)` and `beta = (0 3 4)`, as permutation matrices.
- In `M_5(T)` put `a = I + P(alpha - I)` and `b = I + Q(beta - I)`. Then
  `a^(-1) = I + P(alpha^(-1) - I)` and likewise for `b`.
- Use `[x, y] = x^(-1) y^(-1) x y`. Let `r(P, Q)` be the set of entries of `[a, b]^3 - I`: 9 nonzero
  noncommutative polynomials of degree `<= 12`.

**Lemma NC.** `PQ - QP` lies in the two-sided ideal of `T` generated by `r(P, Q) ∪ r(P, 1 - Q)`,
with coefficients in `Z[1/15]`.

*Proof (exact computation).* `experiments/labbe-relation-climbing/idealcert.py` works in `T` with the
basis of alternating words. It row-reduces the span of `w_1 g w_2`, with `g ∈ r(P, Q) ∪ r(P, 1 - Q)` and
`w_1, w_2` of length `<= 2`, and extracts a certificate with 17 terms (fixed pivot order, seed 5). It recomputes the sum exactly and
confirms that it equals `PQ - QP`. The primes in the denominators are `3` and `5`, and both relation sets are used. ∎

**Consequence.** Let `P, Q` be idempotents on a `k`-space `W` with `char k ∉ {3, 5}`. Suppose
`[a, b]^3 = I` and `[a, b']^3 = I` on `W ⊗ k^5`, where `b' = I + (1 - Q)(beta - I)`. Then `PQ = QP`.

**Calibration** (`probes/pairrel.py`, exact, not landed):
- In the 2-dimensional family `P = diag(1,0)`, `Q = [[q,1],[q(1-q),1-q]]`, with `(P-Q)^2 = (1-q)I`:
  - the relation `[a,b]^3 = I` fails at every tested `q ∉ {1}`;
  - at `q = 1` (a nilpotent-difference extension) it holds;
  - at `q = 0` (the complementary extension) it fails.
- Neither relation set alone puts `PQ - QP` in its ideal, for multipliers of length `<= 3`.

## 2. Setting

- `R_1'` is a radius such that no legal `B(R_1')`-pattern is compatible with its translate by any
  `u` with `0 < |u|_1 <= 4`. It exists by freeness and compactness, as ChJN's `R_1` does for `<= 3`.
- `R >= R_1' + 2`, with the rule 3-cycles `S` and the words `W(q, e)` of
  `zd-derived-full-group-fp-forces-finite-type-proof`.
  - The recursion builds words for patterns on `ℓ_1`-balls `B_1(ρ)`, and on the auxiliary dominoes
    `B_1(ρ) ∪ (B_1(ρ) + g)` for any unit vector `g` (reorder the enumeration of directions).
  - Each word acts on every `(R_1' + 3)`-legal colouring as the 3-cycle on the occurrences of its
    pattern (Lemma B there, with `R_1'` in place of `R_1`).
- The family: `W`, with idempotents `E_a(z)` satisfying (Q1), (Q2) at sup-distance `<= 2D`, and (Q3)
  on `B_D`.
- The module: `V = W ⊗ k[Z^2]`, with `e_a` acting at site `w` by `E_a(w)`, and `u^g` translating, so
  that `P_h^a` acts at `w` by `E_a(w + h)`. This is an `A_D`-module.
- `phi_D : G -> A_D^×` is the unit map of part 1, Theorem 1, step 2 (it needs `D >= D_0(L, R)`).
  `rho = pi o phi_D` is a representation of `G` on `V`.
- For a pattern `q` on a finite `Δ` whose observables pairwise commute,
  `E_q(c) = prod_(y ∈ Δ) E_(q(y))(c + y)`.

## 3. Proof of Theorem Q

**Step 1 (generators in product form).**
`rho(s(σ, e)) = prod_c (1 + E_σ(c) ⊗ (γ_c - 1))`, where `γ_c` permutes the triple `{c, c+e, c-e}`.
- This is `pi(Û_s)`: at site `w` it keeps `(1 - sum_b E_σ(w - b))` and pulls from the triple with
  coefficient `E_σ(w - b)`.
- Factors with disjoint triples commute, because `(γ_c - 1)(γ_(c') - 1) = 0`.
- Factors with overlapping triples have `E_σ(c) E_σ(c') = 0` in both orders, by (Q3) and `R_1'`, so
  they commute too.

**Invariant `I(d)`.**
- (a) `[E_a(z), E_b(z')] = 0` whenever `|z - z'|_1 <= d`.
- (b) `E_q(c) = 0` for every illegal `q` on a ball or domino of `ℓ_1`-diameter `<= d`.

`I(2D)` holds. Part (a) is (Q2). For part (b), a set of sup-diameter `<= 2D` lies in a translate of
`B_D`, every extension of an illegal pattern is illegal, and (Q1) with (Q3) kill the sum.

**Step 2 (product form in the commuting range).** Assume `I(d)` with `d >= 2(R_1' + 8)`. For `q` legal
on a ball or domino of diameter `<= d`,
`rho(W(q, e)) = prod_c (1 + E_q(c) ⊗ (γ_c - 1))`.

*Proof.* Induction along the recursion; each step is the operator form of ChJN's Lemma A, with
`a = prod (1 + P_c ⊗ (α_c - 1))` and `b = prod (1 + Q_c ⊗ (β_c - 1))`.
1. **Orthogonality.** Consider any two factors sharing a site, other than the intended pairs (`a` at
   `c`, `b` at `c + h`).
   - Their coefficients contain cores `E_(π_0)(c + u)` and `E_(π_0)(c + u')` with `0 < |u - u'|_1 <= 4`,
     which form an illegal union, so the cores multiply to `0`.
   - To bring the cores together, each must pass the other coefficient's observables, at distance
     `<= ρ + R_1' + 4 <= d`. So the product vanishes in both orders.
2. **Exact cluster.** Let `Π = E_(π_0)(c)`. It commutes with the intended coefficients and kills the
   others. So `ΠW ⊗ span(cluster sites)` is invariant, and on it `a`, `b` act as single factors.
3. **Classical computation.** The intended coefficients commute by `I(d)(a)`. In their joint
   eigenspaces the classical Lemma A applies, and gives the factor `1 + P_c Q_(c+h) ⊗ (α_c - 1)`,
   whose coefficient is `E` of the enlarged pattern. ∎

**Step 3 (frontier).** Assume `I(d)` and let `|u|_1 = d + 1`.
1. **Domains.**
   - If `d = 2ρ`, put `Δ_a = c + B_1(ρ)` and `Δ_b = Δ_a + h`, where `h` is a unit vector along a
     coordinate of `u` with the same sign. Then `u ∈ Δ_b - Δ_a`.
   - If `d = 2ρ + 1`, use dominoes `Δ_a = c + (B_1(ρ) ∪ (B_1(ρ) + h))` and `Δ_b = Δ_a + h`, where `h`
     points along a coordinate with `|u_i| >= 2`. Then `u - 2h ∈ B_1(2ρ)`.
   - In both cases the internal differences lie in `B_1(d)`, and every difference in `Δ_b - Δ_a` has
     norm `<= d + 1`.
2. **Compatible patterns.** Let `p` be legal on `Δ_a` and `p'` legal on `Δ_b`.
   - If they disagree at an overlap site `y`, then `E_p E_(p') = 0 = E_(p') E_p`, by (Q1) at `y` and
     `I(d)(a)`.
   - Otherwise put `a = W(p, e)` with `e ⊥ h`, `b = W(p', h)`, `P = E_p(c)` and `Q = E_(p')(c + h)`. The
     triples share exactly the site `c`, and Step 2 gives both elements in product form.
3. **Relations in `G`.**
   - `[a, b]^3 = 1`. On common occurrences, `<a, b>` acts on the five block points as `Alt(5)` and
     `[a, b]` is a 3-cycle. Elsewhere one of `a`, `b` acts trivially on the other's blocks, so
     `[a, b] = 1` there.
   - Let `b'` be the product of `W(p~, h)` over legal `p~` on `Δ_b` that are compatible with `p` and
     differ from `p'`. It is in product form with coefficient `Q_S = sum E_(p~)(c + h)`, and
     `[a, b']^3 = 1` in `G`.
4. **Local model.**
   - `Q_tot = Q + Q_S` is the overlap observable `E_(p|overlap)`. It commutes with `P`, `Q` and `Π`,
     and `P Q_tot = P`.
   - On `Π Q_tot W ⊗ k^5` the cluster is exact, as in Step 2: `a = I + P(α - I)`, `b = I + Q(β - I)`,
     `b' = I + (1 - Q)(β - I)`. Orientations are matched by relabelling within each triple.
   - Lemma NC gives `PQ = QP` there. Since `P = Π Q_tot P` and both projections commute with `P` and
     `Q`, `[P, Q] = 0` on `W`.
5. **Conclusion.** By (Q1) and `I(d)(b)`, `E_a(z) = sum_(p(z) = a) E_p` over legal `p` on `Δ_a`, and
   likewise for `E_b(z')`. So `[E_a(z), E_b(z')] = 0`. This is `I(d+1)(a)`.

**Step 4 (legality at the next scale).** Given `I(d+1)(a)`, let `q` be illegal on a ball or domino of
diameter `d + 1`.
- Step 2 at `d + 1` gives `rho(W(q, e))` in product form with coefficient `E_q`.
- `W(q, e) = 1` in `G` (Corollary C of the peer route).
- The `δ_(c+e)` component of `rho(W(q, e))(ξ ⊗ δ_c)` is `(E_q(c) + E_q(c + e)) ξ`. These two
  idempotents are orthogonal and commute, so both vanish.
- This is `I(d+1)(b)`. Only balls and dominoes are needed in Step 3.

**Step 5.** By induction `I(d)` holds for every `d`, so all `E_a(z)` commute. Every family at scale
`D >= max(D_0, r)` commutes: `X` is `D`-quantum rigid over `k`. ∎

## 4. What it changes

- **Positive side.** A proof that `D([[Omega_U]])` is finitely presented must contain a proof of
  quantum rigidity of Labbé's tiles over `F_q`, for `q` prime to `15`: that is, `J_D = 0`
  (`wall-rigid-z2-sft-is-quantum-rigid`, open). The converse, ring finite presentation implying group
  finite presentation, is open. No mechanism is known: cylinder idempotents do not lie in the image of
  `k[G]` (the image fixes constants).
- **Negative side.** Any noncommuting operator family of Labbé's tiles at every scale, in allowed
  characteristic, refutes finite presentation of the group. Periodic ones work in every characteristic
  (part 1).
- **Characteristic.** The first certificate found had denominators `2, 3, 5, 53`. Reordering the
  pivots gave a certificate with denominators `3, 5` only (landed script), which covers
  characteristic 2.
  - Modulo `p` (`experiments/labbe-relation-climbing/idealmodp.py`), `PQ - QP` lies in the ideal for
    `p = 2, 7, 53`, with multipliers of length `<= 2`.
  - For `p = 3` and `p = 5` it does not, for multipliers of length up to 4. The span grows by exactly
    4 per step there, the same as in the other characteristics.
  - So in characteristics 3 and 5 these two relations do not suffice. Both primes divide `|Alt(5)|`.
    Other relations of `G` might suffice; that is not searched, and the theorem excludes those two
    characteristics.
