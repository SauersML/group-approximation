# Configuration modules of the Cantor set are V-acyclic: a germ groupoid proof

2026-09-16, swarm lane `swarm-zaremsky-2-09-braided-v-acycli`, working on the hole
`cantor-power-function-modules-are-v-acyclic`. Unreviewed.

- **Theorem 1.** For every `m >= 1` and every `p >= 0`,
  `H_p(V; C_c(Conf_m(C), Z)) = 0`.
- **Corollary 2.** For every `j >= 1` and every `p >= 0`, `H_p(V; C(C^j, Z)) = 0`.
- **Consequence.** With the existing route
  `braided-thompson-group-bv-is-acyclic-from-cantor-modules` (parts 1 and 3 of
  `zp-braided-v-rational-acyclicity-2026-09-13`), `H_n(bV; Z) = 0` for all `n >= 1`.
  This answers Zaremsky Problem 2.9 affirmatively.

**Idea.** `H_*(V; C_c(X, Z))` is the homology of the transformation groupoid
`X ⋊ V`. Forgetting group elements down to their germs at the `m` points gives a
homomorphism from `X ⋊ V` onto a groupoid of germs. The Crainic–Moerdijk Leray
spectral sequence of this homomorphism has coefficient stalks equal to the homology
of the groups of elements that are the identity near the `m` points. Those groups
are directed unions of copies of `V`, so they are acyclic by Szymik–Wahl. Hence
`X ⋊ V` has the homology of the germ groupoid. The germ groupoid is a reduction of
the `m`-th power of the full 2-shift groupoid, and Matui's Künneth formula and
`H_*(G_2) = 0` make that homology vanish.

## 0. Conventions and imported facts

- `C = {0,1}^N`. For a finite word `u`, `uC` is the cone of sequences beginning with
  `u`. For words `u, v`, the prefix replacement `τ_{v,u} : uC → vC` is `uw ↦ vw`.
- `V` is the group of homeomorphisms `g` of `C` with finite partitions of `C` into
  cones `u_1C, ..., u_nC` and `v_1C, ..., v_nC` such that `g = τ_{v_i,u_i}` on
  `u_iC`. It acts on `C^m` diagonally.
- `Conf_m(C) ⊆ C^m` is the open set of `m`-tuples of pairwise distinct points.
  `C_c(X, Z)` is the group of compactly supported locally constant integer functions,
  with `V` acting by translation.
- Every clopen subset of `C` is a finite disjoint union of cones. A cone splits into
  two cones, so a nonempty clopen set is a disjoint union of `p` cones for every
  large enough `p`.
- An étale groupoid is a locally compact Hausdorff groupoid whose range map is a local
  homeomorphism (Matui's convention). All groupoids below are also second countable
  with totally disconnected unit spaces. For an open `F ⊆ G^(0)`,
  `G|F = {g : r(g), s(g) ∈ F}`. `F` is `G`-full if every `x ∈ G^(0)` is the range of
  an arrow with source in `F`.
- `H_n(G) = H_n(G; Z)` is Matui's homology (Matui 2012, Definition 3.1): the homology
  of `C_c(G^(n), Z)` with `δ_n = Σ_i (-1)^i d_{i*}`, where `d_{i*}` sums along the
  fibres of the face maps. Isomorphic étale groupoids have isomorphic homology,
  since the complex is functorial in isomorphisms.

Imported facts (graph nodes in brackets):

  action of a discrete group `Γ` on a locally compact Hausdorff space `X`,

- **(F2)** [same node, item 2] If `G^(0)` is σ-compact and totally disconnected, and

- **(F3)** [`matui-groupoid-homology-kunneth-and-full-shift-vanishing`] For the étale
  groupoid `G_2` of the one-sided full 2-shift, `H_*(G_2) = 0`, and
  `H_*(G × G_2) = 0` for every étale groupoid `G`.
- **(F4)** [`crainic-moerdijk-groupoid-leray-spectral-sequence`] For a homomorphism
  `φ : K → G` of étale groupoids and a `K`-sheaf `A` there is a spectral sequence
  `E²_{p,q} = H_p(G; L_qφ_!A) ⇒ H_{p+q}(K; A)` (CM Theorem 4.4). The stalks are
  `(L_qφ_!A)_x ≅ H_q(x/φ; π_x^*A)` (CM Proposition 4.3). For c-soft `A`,
  `H_n(G; A)` is the homology of the bar complex `Γ_c(G^(n); τ_n^*A)` (CM 3.1, 3.4).
  For a discrete groupoid it is the simplicial homology of the nerve (CM 3.5(2)).
- **(F5)** [`thompson-v-is-integrally-acyclic`] `V` is integrally acyclic.

## 1. The germ groupoid of V is the full 2-shift groupoid

Let `𝒱` be the groupoid of germs of `V` acting on `C`.

- Its arrows are the classes `[g]_y` for `g ∈ V` and `y ∈ C`, where `[g]_y = [h]_y`
  iff `g = h` on a neighbourhood of `y`.
- The source is `y`, the range is `g(y)`, and multiplication is composition.
- The topology has basis `{[g]_z : z ∈ W}` for `g ∈ V` and `W ⊆ C` open. On each such
  basic set the source map is a homeomorphism onto `W`.

Let `G_2 = {(x, k − l, y) ∈ C × Z × C : k, l >= 0, σ^k x = σ^l y}` with the topology
generated by the sets `U(v, u) = {(vw, |v| − |u|, uw) : w ∈ C}` for words `u, v`.

- Matui's basis uses the sets `{(x, k − l, y) : x ∈ A, y ∈ B, σ^k x = σ^l y}` with
  `A, B` open and `σ^k|_A`, `σ^l|_B` injective. Each such set is a union of sets
  `U(v, u)`. Given a point `(x, k − l, y)` in it, put `w = σ^k x = σ^l y`. Choose a
  prefix `a` of `w` so long that the cone `vaC` around `x`, with `|v| = k`, lies in
  `A`, and the cone `uaC` around `y`, with `|u| = l`, lies in `B`. Then
  `U(va, ua)` contains the point and lies in the set.
- So this is the étale groupoid of the full 2-shift used in (F3).
- `G_2` is Hausdorff, because the inclusion `G_2 → C × Z × C` is continuous and
  injective.

**Lemma 1.1.** The map `Φ : 𝒱 → G_2`, `[g]_y ↦ (g(y), |v| − |u|, y)`, where
`g = τ_{v,u}` on a cone `uC ∋ y`, is an isomorphism of topological groupoids. In
particular `𝒱` is an étale groupoid and `H_*(𝒱) = 0` by (F3).

*Proof.*

- **Well defined.** The germ of `g` at `y` is the germ of the piece `τ_{v,u}` with
  `y ∈ uC`. If also `g = τ_{v',u'}` near `y`, lengthen both pairs by the same letters
  of `y` until `|u| = |u'|`. Lengthening `(u, v)` to `(ua, va)` gives the same map
  on `uaC` and the same difference `|v| − |u|`. Now `vw = v'w` for
  `w = σ^{|u|} y`, so `|v| = |v'|` and the integers agree. Moreover
  `σ^{|v|} g(y) = w = σ^{|u|} y`, so the image lies in `G_2`.
- **Homomorphism.** Composing prefix replacements adds the length differences.
- **Injective.** Suppose `[g]_y` and `[h]_y` have the same image, with
  `g = τ_{v,u}` and `h = τ_{v',u'}` near `y`. Lengthen as above so that `|u| = |u'|`.
  Then `|v| = |v'|`, and `vw = g(y) = h(y) = v'w`, so `v = v'`. Hence `g = h` on
  `uC`, and the germs agree.
- **Surjective.** Let `(x, k − l, y) ∈ G_2` and `w = σ^k x = σ^l y`. Let `v` be the
  first `k` letters of `x` and `u` the first `l` letters of `y`, and let `a` be the
  first letter of `w`. Then `uaC` and `vaC` are proper cones, so their complements
  are nonempty clopen sets. Write both complements as unions of the same number of
  cones and match them by prefix replacements. Together with `τ_{va,ua}` this gives
  `g ∈ V` with `[g]_y ↦ (x, k − l, y)`.
- **Homeomorphism.** The basic set `{[g]_z : z ∈ uC}`, with `g = τ_{v,u}` on `uC`,
  maps onto `U(v, u)`.
  - Every basic set of `𝒱` is a union of such sets, taking small cones inside `W` on
    which `g` is a single prefix replacement.
  - Conversely, `U(v, u) = U(v0, u0) ∪ U(v1, u1)`. Each of these is the image of such a
    set, for the `g` built in the surjectivity step.
  - So `Φ` and `Φ^{-1}` map open sets to open sets. ∎

## 2. Products, restriction and vanishing

Fix `m >= 1` and put `X = Conf_m(C)`. `𝒱^m` is the product groupoid, with unit space
`C^m`, and `𝒢 := 𝒱^m|X`.

**Lemma 2.1.** `X` is open and `𝒱^m`-full.

*Proof.* Openness is clear. Let `y = (y_1, ..., y_m) ∈ C^m`, possibly with repeated
entries. Choose `L` with `2^L >= m` and pairwise distinct words `p_1, ..., p_m` of
length `L`. Let `x_i = p_i σ^L(y_i)` and let `q_i` be the first `L` letters of `y_i`.

- The `x_i` are pairwise distinct because their prefixes differ, so `x ∈ X`.
- The germ at `x_i` of any `g_i ∈ V` extending `τ_{q_i,p_i}` is an arrow of `𝒱`
  from `x_i` to `y_i`. Such `g_i` exist by the matching of complements in Lemma 1.1.
- The tuple of these germs is an arrow of `𝒱^m` with source `x ∈ X` and range `y`. ∎

**Proposition 2.2.** `H_*(𝒢) = 0`.

*Proof.*

- `C^m` is compact metrizable and totally disconnected, so (F2) and Lemma 2.1 give
  `H_*(𝒢) ≅ H_*(𝒱^m)`.
- For `m = 1`, `H_*(𝒱) = H_*(G_2) = 0` by Lemma 1.1 and (F3).
- For `m >= 2`, `𝒱^m = 𝒱^{m−1} × 𝒱 ≅ 𝒱^{m−1} × G_2`, and (F3) gives
  `H_*(𝒱^{m−1} × G_2) = 0`. ∎

## 3. The comparison homomorphism

Let `X ⋊ V` be the transformation groupoid, with arrows `(g, x)`, source `x` and range
`g(x)`. Define

`φ : X ⋊ V → 𝒢`, `(g, x) ↦ ([g]_{x_1}, ..., [g]_{x_m})`.

- **Well defined.** `g` is injective, so `g(x) ∈ X`, and `φ` respects sources and
  ranges.
- **Homomorphism.** Germs of a composite are composites of germs.
- **Continuous.** On the open bisection `{g} × X`, `φ` is `x ↦ ([g]_{x_i})_i`, which
  is the inverse of the source map on the basic set of germs of `g`.
- **Étale in CM 3.7(2).** `φ_0 = id_X`. On `{g} × X`, `φ` is the composite of two
  homeomorphisms onto open sets, so every `φ_n` is a local homeomorphism.

**Lemma 3.1 (realization).** Let `x, y ∈ X` and `γ_i ∈ 𝒱` with `s(γ_i) = x_i` and
`r(γ_i) = y_i`. Then some `g ∈ V` has `[g]_{x_i} = γ_i` for all `i`.

*Proof.*

1. Write `γ_i = [τ_{v_i,u_i}]_{x_i}` with `x_i ∈ u_iC`.
2. Lengthening `(u_i, v_i)` to `(u_i a, v_i a)`, with `u_i a` a prefix of `x_i`,
   keeps the germ and shrinks the cones towards `x_i` and `y_i`. Since the `x_i` are
   pairwise distinct and so are the `y_i`, long enough extensions make the cones
   `u_iC` pairwise disjoint and the cones `v_iC` pairwise disjoint.
3. Extend each pair by the next letter of `x_i`. If disjoint cones
   `u_1C, ..., u_mC` covered `C` before, then `u_1 b C`, where `b` is the other letter,
   misses all new cones. The same holds for the `v_iC`.
   So `U = ⊔ u_iC` and `U' = ⊔ v_iC` both have nonempty complements.
4. Write `C \ U` and `C \ U'` as unions of the same number of cones and match them by
   prefix replacements. Together with the `τ_{v_i,u_i}` this defines `g ∈ V`.
5. Each `x_i` is interior to `u_iC`, so `[g]_{x_i} = γ_i`. ∎

**Lemma 3.2 (germ-trivial stabilizers).** For `x ∈ X`, the group
`K_x = {h ∈ V : h is the identity on a neighbourhood of each x_i}` is integrally
acyclic.

*Proof.* Let `𝒰` be the set of clopen `U ⊆ C` that contain `x_1, ..., x_m` and have
`C \ U ≠ ∅`. For `U ∈ 𝒰` put `V_U = {h ∈ V : h|_U = id}`.

- **Directed union.** `𝒰` is closed under finite intersections, and `U ⊆ U'` implies
  `V_{U'} ⊆ V_U`. Every `h ∈ K_x` is the identity on small cones around the `x_i`.
  Shrinking them once more as in Lemma 3.1 gives a union `U ∈ 𝒰` with `h ∈ V_U`. So
  `K_x` is the directed union of the subgroups `V_U`.
- **Each `V_U` is isomorphic to `V`.** Let `W = C \ U`, a nonempty clopen set, and
  write it as a union of `p` cones `w_jC`. Choose a partition of `C` into `p` cones
  `c_jC`, and let `ψ : W → C` be `τ_{c_j,w_j}` on `w_jC`.
  - Let `h ∈ V_U`. Refine a partition for `h` so that each piece lies in `U` or in
    `W`. Then `h` fixes `U` pointwise, preserves `W`, and restricts on `W` to a
    homeomorphism that is piecewise a prefix replacement.
  - Conjugation by `ψ` preserves being piecewise a prefix replacement, in both
    directions. Extending by the identity on `U` inverts the restriction.
  - So `h ↦ ψ h|_W ψ^{-1}` is an isomorphism `V_U → V`.
- **Acyclic.** Group homology commutes with directed unions, since the bar complex
  does. So `H_q(K_x; Z) = colim_U H_q(V_U; Z) = 0` for `q >= 1` by (F5). ∎

**Lemma 3.3 (comma groupoids).** For `x ∈ X`, the comma groupoid `x/φ` of CM 4.1 is a
discrete connected groupoid whose isotropy group at the object `(x, 1_x)` is `K_x`.
Hence `H_q(x/φ; Z) = H_q(K_x; Z)`, which is `Z` for `q = 0` and `0` for `q >= 1`.

*Proof.* Per CM 4.1, an object of `x/φ` is a pair `(y, γ)` with `y ∈ X` and
`γ : x → φ(y) = y` in `𝒢`. An arrow `(y, γ) → (y', γ')` is an arrow `k : y → y'` of
`X ⋊ V` with `φ(k) γ = γ'`.

- **Discrete.** The object space is homeomorphic to `s^{-1}(x) ⊆ 𝒢`, which is
  discrete because `𝒢` is étale.
  - For an arrow `(k, γ)` with `k = (g, y)`, choose an open bisection `B ∋ γ` of `𝒢`.
    It meets `s^{-1}(x)` only in `γ`.
  - The neighbourhood `({g} × X) ×_X B` then meets the arrow space of `x/φ` only in
    `(k, γ)`.
  - The same argument applies to every nerve space `(x/φ)^(n)`, so `x/φ` is a
    discrete groupoid.
- **Connected.** Given an object `(y, γ)`, Lemma 3.1 applied to
  `γ^{-1} = (γ_i^{-1})_i` gives `g ∈ V` with `φ(g, y) = γ^{-1}`. Then
  `(g, y) : (y, γ) → (x, 1_x)` is an arrow.
- **Isotropy.** An arrow `(x, 1_x) → (x, 1_x)` is `k = (g, x)` with `φ(k) = 1_x`, that
  is, `[g]_{x_i} = [id]_{x_i}` for all `i`. That says exactly `g ∈ K_x`, and then
  `g(x) = x` automatically.

By CM 3.5(2), the homology of the discrete groupoid `x/φ` with coefficients
`π_x^*Z` is the simplicial homology of its nerve. Here `π_x^*Z` is the constant
coefficient system `Z`, with trivial action. The inclusion of the one-object full
subgroupoid on `(x, 1_x)` is an equivalence of categories, so it induces a homotopy
equivalence of nerves `BK_x → N(x/φ)`. Hence `H_q(x/φ; Z) = H_q(K_x; Z)`, and
Lemma 3.2 finishes the proof. ∎

**Proposition 3.4.** `H_p(X ⋊ V; Z) ≅ H_p(𝒢; Z)` for all `p >= 0`.

*Proof.* Apply (F4) to `φ` and the constant sheaf `Z` on `X`:
`E²_{p,q} = H_p(𝒢; L_qφ_!Z) ⇒ H_{p+q}(X ⋊ V; Z)`.

(i) **Standing hypotheses and identification.**
- Every space here is second countable, locally compact, Hausdorff and totally
  disconnected. So CM's overall assumptions 1.8 hold with cohomological dimension 0.
- The constant sheaf `Z` on such a space is c-soft. A section over a compact set `K` is
  a locally constant function on an open set containing `K`; a compact open set lies
  between them; extend by zero.
- By CM 3.1 and 3.4, `H_n(G; Z)` for each of our groupoids is the homology of
  `Γ_c(G^(n); Z) = C_c(G^(n), Z)` with faces given by summation along fibres. This is
  Matui's complex of Definition 3.1; the orders of the faces agree, with `δ_1 = s_* − r_*`.
- Since `φ` is étale and `Z` is c-soft, `Lφ_!Z` is the simplicial `𝒢`-sheaf
  `B_·(φ; Z)` itself (CM 4.2 and 4.5(2)). So `L_qφ_!Z = 0` for `q < 0`, and the
  spectral sequence lies in the first quadrant.

(ii) **Higher rows vanish.** By CM Proposition 4.3 and Lemma 3.3,
`(L_qφ_!Z)_x ≅ H_q(x/φ; Z) = 0` for `q >= 1` and every `x ∈ X`. A sheaf with zero
stalks is zero, so `L_qφ_!Z = 0` for `q >= 1`.

(iii) **`L_0φ_!Z ≅ Z` as `𝒢`-sheaves.**
- By CM 4.2, `B_0(φ; Z) = (β_0)_!α_0^*Z`, with `β_0 : X ×_X 𝒢^(1) = 𝒢^(1) → X` the
  source map. This map is étale, so `B_0(φ; Z) = s_!Z`, and its stalk at `x` is the
  free abelian group `Z[Obj(x/φ)]`.
- Summation along the fibres of `s` is a sheaf map `ε : s_!Z → Z`, as in CM 3.7(2). On
  stalks it is the augmentation `Z[Obj(x/φ)] → Z`.
- On stalks, the two face maps `B_1(φ; Z) → B_0(φ; Z)` send a basis element (an arrow
  of `x/φ`) to a basis element (an end of that arrow). So `ε d_0 = ε d_1`, and `ε`
  descends to `ε̄ : L_0φ_!Z = coker(d_0 − d_1) → Z`.
- On stalks `ε̄_x : Z[π_0(x/φ)] → Z` is the augmentation. It is bijective because
  `x/φ` is connected (Lemma 3.3), and a map of sheaves bijective on stalks is an
  isomorphism.
- The `𝒢`-action on `B_·(φ; Z)` is induced stalkwise by the maps
  `γ^* : x'/φ → x/φ`, `(y, δ) ↦ (y, δγ)` (CM 4.1, 4.2). These send basis elements to
  basis elements, so `ε̄` intertwines them with the identity action on the constant
  sheaf.

This is the step CM use at the end of the proof of Corollary 4.6, here with `A = Z`.

(iv) **Collapse.** The `E²` page is concentrated in the row `q = 0`, where
`E²_{p,0} = H_p(𝒢; Z)`. So the spectral sequence collapses:
`H_p(X ⋊ V; Z) ≅ H_p(𝒢; Z)`. ∎

## 4. Proofs of Theorem 1 and Corollary 2

*Proof of Theorem 1.* Chain the four results:

`H_p(V; C_c(X, Z)) ≅ H_p(X ⋊ V; Z)` (F1) `≅ H_p(𝒢; Z)` (Proposition 3.4) `= 0`
(Proposition 2.2). ∎

*Proof of Corollary 2.* Use strong induction on `j`, with the integral Lemmas C1 and
C2 of `zp-braided-v-rational-acyclicity-2026-09-13-part3.md`. Those lemmas are
restriction and closed-union exact sequences, valid for any discrete coefficient
group.

- **`j = 1`.** `Conf_1(C) = C`, so `C(C, Z) = C_c(Conf_1(C), Z)` is V-acyclic by
  Theorem 1.
- **`j >= 2`.**
  - Let `D ⊆ C^j` be the fat diagonal, the union of `Δ_{ab} = {x_a = x_b}`. For a
    nonempty set `E` of pairs, `Δ_E = ∩_{e∈E} Δ_e` is V-equivariantly homeomorphic to
    `C^i`, where `i` is the number of classes of the equivalence relation generated
    by `E`. So `i < j`, and `C(Δ_E, Z)` is V-acyclic by induction.
  - The induction of Lemma C4 over unions of `k` such sets uses only these
    `Δ_E ≅ C^i` with `i < j`, because intersections `Δ_{E_i ∪ E_k}` again have `E`
    nonempty. With C2 and the long exact sequences it shows that `C(D, Z)` is
    V-acyclic.
  - Lemma C1 gives the exact sequence `0 → C_c(Conf_j(C), Z) → C(C^j, Z) → C(D, Z) → 0`
    of V-modules.
  - Both outer terms are V-acyclic, by Theorem 1 and by the previous item. The long
    exact sequence, including its right-exact end in degree 0, shows that
    `C(C^j, Z)` is V-acyclic. ∎

## 5. Consequence for bV

The route `braided-thompson-group-bv-is-acyclic-from-cantor-modules` needs exactly
Corollary 2 and `thompson-v-is-integrally-acyclic`. Its argument runs as follows.

1. **Step A integrally.** Cohen's computation is torsion-free, so for `q >= 1`,
   `H_q(K; Z) ≅ ⊕_{m=q+1}^{2q} C_c(Conf_m(C), Z) ⊗_{Z[Σ_m]} W_{m,q}`.
2. **Configuration modules.** They are V-acyclic, by Theorem 1 directly.
3. **Tensoring with `W_{m,q}`.**
   - The lexicographic order makes `{x_1 < ... < x_m}` a clopen fundamental domain
     for `Σ_m` on `Conf_m(C)`, so `C_c(Conf_m(C), Z)` is a free `Z[Σ_m]`-module.
   - Tensoring over `Z[Σ_m]` with a free resolution of `W_{m,q}` gives a resolution
     of `C_c ⊗_{Z[Σ_m]} W_{m,q}` by finite sums of copies of `C_c`.
   - For such a resolution `Q_·` of a module `M` by V-acyclic modules, the
     first-quadrant double complex `F_· ⊗_{ZV} Q_·` has two readings, where `F_·` is a
     projective resolution of `Z` over `ZV`.
   - Taking homology along `Q` first leaves `F_· ⊗_{ZV} M`, whose homology is
     `H_*(V; M)`.
   - Taking homology along `F` first gives `H_*(V; Q_q) = 0`.
   - So `H_*(V; M) = 0`.
4. **Assembly.** In the integral Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of
   `1 → K → bV → V → 1`, every row `q >= 1` vanishes. Row `0` is `H_p(V; Z)`, which
   vanishes for `p >= 1`. So `H_n(bV; Z) = 0` for `n >= 1`.

Theorem 1 feeds step 2 directly. Going through Corollary 2 and the route's own Step 2
is only the graph's bookkeeping.

## 6. Sanity checks

- **Degree 0.** For `m = 1`, `H_0(V; C(C, Z)) = 0` agrees with the direct
  subdivision argument recorded in `cantor-power-function-modules-are-v-acyclic`
  (`1_U = 1_{U0} + 1_{U1}` and transitivity on cones, so `f = 2f`).
- **Palmer–Wu.** `V(Z) = C(C, Z) ⋊ V` is acyclic (arXiv:2510.16879). The low-degree
  part of its Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence has row 0 equal to
  `H_*(V) = 0` in positive degrees. So `E²_{0,1} = H_0(V; C(C, Z))` and
  `E²_{1,1} = H_1(V; C(C, Z))` must vanish, since no differential can reach or leave
  them. This agrees with Theorem 1.
- **Rational case.** Theorem 1 tensored with `Q` recovers Lemma C5 of part 3. That
  lemma was obtained there by an independent route (Palmer–Wu with rational
  coefficients).
- **The machine detects isotropy.** Let `Γ` be a nontrivial group acting trivially
  on a point, and compare with the trivial germ groupoid, a point. The comma groupoid
  is `Γ` itself, and the spectral sequence returns `H_*(Γ)`, not the homology of a
  point. So the collapse in Proposition 3.4 really uses Lemma 3.2 and is not
  automatic. The hypothesis that fails in this example is exactly acyclicity of the
  germ-trivial stabilizer.
- **The role of fullness.** For `m >= 2`, `𝒢` is a proper restriction of `𝒱^m`. The
  diagonal points are not in `X`, but every tail-equivalence class meets `X`
  (Lemma 2.1). This is what makes `𝒢` homologically similar to `𝒱^m`.

## 7. Points a referee should check first

1. **Proposition 3.4 (iii).** The identification `L_0φ_!Z ≅ Z` as `𝒢`-sheaves. CM
   assert the analogous fact for Morita equivalences without detail. The argument here
   uses only that `B_0(φ; Z) = s_!Z` with an étale `s`, and that `x/φ` is connected.
2. **Discreteness of `x/φ` in Lemma 3.3.** It is what lets CM 3.5(2) turn stalk
   homology into group homology of `K_x`.
3. **Integrality of Step A** in `zp-braided-v-rational-acyclicity-2026-09-13-part1.md`,
   written by an earlier lane. It uses the integral Gerstenhaber basis of
   `H_*(Conf_n(R^2); Z)` and the support decomposition, both of which hold over `Z`.
