## Checks

1. **Ghys–Sergiescu: rational rotation numbers in `T`.**
   - Verified through Calegari, arXiv:math/0609573 (abstract), verbatim: "Ghys and Sergiescu showed that the
     rotation number of every element of T is rational".
   - The Ghys–Sergiescu primary, Comment. Math. Helv. 62 (1987), was not read.
   - Cite it as "Ghys–Sergiescu (1987), as stated in Calegari arXiv:math/0609573".
2. **Calegari's generalization.** The abstract, verbatim, says the method generalizes to "PL homeomorphism of the
   circle with rational break points and derivatives which are powers of some fixed integer". This matches the
   node's wording.
3. **Lodha, arXiv:1710.06220.**
   - The abstract says "a finitely presented, infinite, simple group that acts by homeomorphisms on the circle …
     a group of piecewise projective homeomorphisms of S¹=ℝ∪{∞}".
   - The introduction (ar5iv), verbatim: "Our group S is generated by T together with the following piecewise
     projective homeomorphism of the circle", with
     `s(t) = t (t≤0), 2t/(1+t) (0≤t≤1), 2/(3−t) (1≤t≤2), t (t≥2)`.
   - The finite presentation is his Theorem 3.17.
   - The breakpoints of `s` are `0, 1, 2`, and `T` has finitely many rational breakpoints per element. So every
     element of `S` has finitely many breakpoints.
   - The pieces of `s` have determinant 2 > 0, so they lie in `PSL_2(R)`, not `PSL_2(Z)`. The node says "piecewise
     projective", which is correct.
4. **Belk–Hyde–Matucci: `(Q,+) ≤ T̄`.**
   - The survey arXiv:2306.16356v3, §4.3, verbatim: "In [BHM], the first author, James Hyde, and the third author
     gave the first explicit description of a finitely presented group $\overline{T}$ that contains the additive
     group $\Q$". [BHM] is Bull. AMS 59 (2022) 561–567.
   - The survey also says `T̄` was "introduced by Ghys and Sergiescu in 1987".
   - It matches.
5. **Host list in part (c).**
   - Lodha–Moore groups: piecewise projective, finitely many breakpoints, finitely presented.
   - Monod's `H(A)`: piecewise `PSL_2(A)`, finitely many breakpoints. The node restricts to finitely generated
     subgroups, which is correct.
   - Stein–Thompson groups: PL, with slopes in a finitely generated multiplicative group and finitely many
     breakpoints; finitely presented (Stein).
   - Cleary's golden-ratio groups: PL, slopes powers of the golden ratio, breakpoints in `Z[τ]`.
   - Each has finitely many breakpoints per element, and the named groups are finitely generated. This agrees with
     standard descriptions.
   - Not re-read at the primaries: Lodha–Moore (GGD 2016), Monod (PNAS 2013), Stein (Trans. AMS 1992), Cleary
     (2000), and Burillo–Nucinkis–Reeves for the finite presentation of `F_τ`.
