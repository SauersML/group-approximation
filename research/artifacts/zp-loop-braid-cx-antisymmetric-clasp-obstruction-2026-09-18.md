# The antisymmetric clasp invariant: the Push Complexity Statement CX(N) is false

swarm-0917-w11-w11-z-last1, 2026-09-18. Unreviewed notes.

Target: `loop-braid-push-complexity-statement` (CX), the only open prerequisite of the route
`loop-braid-desc-links-connected-via-bfmwz-push` for `loop-braid-descending-links-are-increasingly-connected`
(Zaremsky 2.12). Notation and all inputs are from the w9 artifact
`zp-loop-braid-bfmwz-push-decomposition-2026-09-17.md`, cited [w9 §k].

## 0. Summary

1. **Invariant.** Fix free rings v, w and put, for a ring c and `p ∈ P = PLB_n`,
   `y_c(p) := e_cv(p) − e_vc(p) − e_cw(p) + e_wc(p)`.
   For a vertex `u = p·[(a,b)]` of `X^0 = X_n(F ∪ {v,w})`, put `Φ(u) := y_a(p) − y_b(p)`.
   Then Φ is well defined on vertices (independent of the presentation p), vanishes on every vertex
   of `lk(b_0)`, and is **unchanged by every pair-twist push** of [w9 §4] (Lemmas 2.1, 2.2).
   Equivalently `Φ = χ_{z,u} − χ_{u,z}` for `u = (a,b)`, `z = (v,w)`: the clasp numbers of [w9 §5.1]
   in the two directions, antisymmetrized.
2. **CX is false (Theorem 3.1).** For every free count `N ≥ 4`, all n, F and all distinct free
   v, w, there is no function c satisfying (CX0) and (CX1). The witness is the single vertex
   `u_1 = A_av·[(a,b)]`, with `Φ(u_1) = 1`: (CX0) forces `c(u_1) > 0`, and (CX1) applied to
   `K = {u_1}` then produces an infinite strictly decreasing chain of values of c.
3. **The sphere form also fails (Theorem 3.2).** For every `m` with `2m + 4 ≤ N` there is a
   simplexwise injective m-sphere in `X^0` containing the two vertices `[(a,b)]` and `A_av[(a,b)]`,
   with Φ-values 0 and 1. No sequence of Φ-preserving pushes moves it into the star of **any** vertex
   of type `(v,w)` or `(w,v)`. So restricting CX to sphere images at the key degree `η(N)`, or
   choosing the base vertex depending on K, does not rescue the route.
4. **What dies.** Every argument of the shape of [w9 Thm 4.2] (push into the star of one vertex of
   type `{v,w}`) whose pushes are right multiplications by elements g with `Φ(g) = 0`. These include
   all pair twists `τ_cd`, all of `P_u` and `P_z`, and every McCool generator `A_ij` except the eight
   with one index in `{a,b}` and the other in `{v,w}`. The route
   `loop-braid-desc-links-connected-via-bfmwz-push` is dead as stated.
5. **What a repair needs.** A push g with `Φ(g) ≠ 0`, for example the one-ring push `A_av` or
   `A_va`, **with** a mutual link `lk(u_0) ∩ lk(g u_0)` that is highly connected enough and holds
   the K-neighbours. By [w9 §2 Remark 2] the triple-cable mechanism is unavailable for one-ring
   pushes. Alternatively, abandon the single base vertex: the Φ-levels are separated only by the
   stars of distinct `(v,w)`-vertices, which is exactly what the w8 Coning Statement cones off.

## 1. Setting

As in [w9 §1]:
- `P = PLB_n = PΣAut_n` acts on `X_n(F)` with strict fundamental domain Q; Q has exactly one vertex
  `[(a,b)]` of each ordered type `(a,b)`, and every vertex of type `(a,b)` is `p·[(a,b)]` for some
  `p ∈ P`, unique up to right multiplication by `P_{(a,b)}` = Stab`[(a,b)]`
  (`pure-loop-braid-group-is-colimit-of-cabling-stabilizers`, model artifact §2).
- `P_{(a,b)}` is generated by
  - `A_ij` with `i, j ∉ {a,b}`,
  - `θ_k`: `x_k ↦ (x_a x_b) x_k (x_a x_b)^{-1}`, `k ∉ {a,b}`,
  - `ζ_q`: `x_a ↦ x_q x_a x_q^{-1}` and `x_b ↦ x_q x_b x_q^{-1}` together, `q ∉ {a,b}`.
- `e_ij(φ)` is the exponent sum of `x_j` in a conjugator of `φ(x_i)`. It is well defined because the
  centralizer of `x_i` in `F_n` is `⟨x_i⟩`, and `e: P → Z^{n(n−1)}` is a homomorphism: if
  `ψ(x_i) = w x_i w^{-1}` then `φψ(x_i) = φ(w)·φ(x_i)·φ(w)^{-1}`, and `φ(w)` has the same exponent
  sums as w because φ acts trivially on `H_1(F_n)`.
- **Link Lemma** [w8 Lemma 2.1]: `lk_{X_n(F)}(b) = P_b · lk_Q(b)` for a Q-vertex b.
- Fix distinct free rings v, w, and put `z := (v,w)`, `b_0 := [z]`, `X^0 := X_n(F ∪ {v,w})`.
  A **push** of `u = p·[(a,b)]` is `p g [(a,b)]` with `g ∈ {τ_br, τ_ra}`, `r ∉ {a,b}` [w9 §4];
  here `τ_cd` conjugates `x_c` and `x_d` by `x_c x_d` and fixes the other generators.

## 2. The invariant

For distinct rings a, b outside `{v, w}` define the homomorphism
`φ_{ab} := y_a − y_b : P → Z`, with `y_c = e_cv − e_vc − e_cw + e_wc`. Written out,

  `φ_ab = e_av − e_bv − e_aw + e_bw − e_va + e_vb + e_wa − e_wb = χ_{z,u} − χ_{u,z}`,

where `u = (a,b)` and `χ_{u,z} = e_va − e_wa − e_vb + e_wb` is the clasp homomorphism of [w9 §1].

**Lemma 2.1.** `φ_ab` vanishes on
1. `P_{(a,b)}`;
2. `P_{(v,w)}` and `P_{(w,v)}`;
3. every pair twist `τ_cd`, for all ordered pairs of distinct rings `c, d`.

Also `φ_ab(A_av) = 1`.

*Proof.* φ_ab is a combination of the eight coordinates `e_ij` with one of i, j in `{a,b}` and the
other in `{v,w}`. On a generator, `e` is the sum of the unit vectors `e_ij` listed below, and we
evaluate the combination.
1. `P_{(a,b)}`.
   - `A_ij`, `i, j ∉ {a,b}`: the single coordinate `e_ij` has no index in `{a,b}`, so it gives 0.
   - `θ_k`: `e_ka + e_kb`. For `k = v` this is `−1 + 1 = 0`; for `k = w` it is `1 − 1 = 0`;
     otherwise no coordinate of φ_ab occurs.
   - `ζ_q`: `e_aq + e_bq`. For `q = v` this is `1 − 1 = 0`; for `q = w` it is `−1 + 1 = 0`;
     otherwise 0.
2. `P_{(v,w)}`; the generators of `P_{(w,v)}` have the same e-values.
   - `A_ij`, `i, j ∉ {v,w}`: no index in `{v,w}`, so 0.
   - `x_k` through the cable `x_v x_w`: `e_kv + e_kw`. For `k = a` this is `1 − 1 = 0`; for `k = b`
     it is `−1 + 1 = 0`.
   - `x_v, x_w` through `x_q`: `e_vq + e_wq`. For `q = a` this is `−1 + 1 = 0`; for `q = b` it is
     `1 − 1 = 0`.
3. `τ_cd`: `e_cd + e_dc`. φ_ab is antisymmetric, meaning the coefficient of `e_ij` is minus that of
   `e_ji`. Its coefficients are `(av, va) = (1, −1)`, `(bv, vb) = (−1, 1)`, `(aw, wa) = (−1, 1)`
   and `(bw, wb) = (1, −1)`. So `φ_ab(τ_cd) = 0` for every pair.

Finally `A_av(x_a) = x_v x_a x_v^{-1}`, so `e(A_av)` is the unit vector `e_av`, and
`φ_ab(A_av) = 1`. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (Φ on vertices).** For a vertex `u = p[(a,b)]` of `X^0`, `Φ(u) := φ_ab(p)` is
independent of p.
1. For `g ∈ {τ_br, τ_ra}`, every push satisfies `Φ(p g [(a,b)]) = Φ(u)`. More generally so does
   `p g [(a,b)]` for any g with `φ_ab(g) = 0`.
2. If `β = q·[(v,w)]` or `β = q·[(w,v)]` with `q ∈ P`, then every type-`(a,b)` vertex of `lk(β)`
   has `Φ = φ_ab(q)`. In particular `Φ = 0` on `lk(b_0)`.

*Proof.*
- Independence: `p` is unique up to `P_{(a,b)}` on the right, and φ_ab vanishes there (Lemma 2.1(1)).
- Item 1: `φ_ab(pg) = φ_ab(p) + φ_ab(g)`, and `φ_ab(g) = 0` by Lemma 2.1(3).
- Item 2: `lk(β) = q·lk([z']) = q P_{z'} lk_Q([z'])`, with `z' ∈ {(v,w), (w,v)}`, by the Link Lemma.
  The only type-`(a,b)` vertex of Q is `[(a,b)]`. So a type-`(a,b)` vertex of `lk(β)` is
  `q s [(a,b)]` with `s ∈ P_{z'}`, and `φ_ab(qs) = φ_ab(q)` by Lemma 2.1(2). ∎

## 3. CX is false

**Theorem 3.1.** Let n, F, v, w be such that v, w are free and the free count is `N ≥ 4`. There is
no function `c : V(X^0) → Z_{≥0}` satisfying (CX0) and (CX1) of [w9 §4.1]. In particular CX(N) is
false for every `N ≥ 11`.

*Proof.* Pick free rings `a, b ∉ {v, w}`; they exist because `N ≥ 4`. Put `u_0 := [(a,b)]` and
`u_1 := A_av·u_0`. Then `u_1` is a vertex of `X^0`, because its type `(a,b)` avoids `F ∪ {v,w}`.
By Lemma 2.1, `Φ(u_1) = φ_ab(A_av) = 1`.

Suppose c satisfies (CX0) and (CX1). We build vertices `u_1, u_2, …` of type `(a,b)`, all with
`Φ = 1` and `c(u_1) > c(u_2) > …`, which is impossible in `Z_{≥0}`.
- **Every vertex of type `(a,b)` with `Φ ≠ 0` has `c > 0`.** Let u be such a vertex. By
  Lemma 2.2(2), `u ∉ lk(b_0)`. If `c(u) = 0`, the full subcomplex on `c^{-1}(0)` contains u, so
  by (CX0) `u ∈ lk(b_0)`, a contradiction.
- **The induction step.** Given `u_k` with `Φ(u_k) = 1`, we have `c(u_k) > 0`. Apply (CX1) to the
  finite subcomplex `K = {u_k}`. The only vertex of K with positive complexity is `u_k`, so (CX1)
  gives a presentation `u_k = p[(a,b)]`, a ring r and an order e with `c(u_{k+1}) < c(u_k)`, where
  `u_{k+1} := p g_e [(a,b)]` and `g_e ∈ {τ_br, τ_ra}`. (The condition `K ∩ lk(u_k) ⊆ pL_e` holds
  vacuously.) `u_{k+1}` has type `(a,b)`, and `Φ(u_{k+1}) = Φ(u_k) = 1` by Lemma 2.2(1). ∎

The argument uses only three facts:
- c is `Z_{≥0}`-valued;
- (CX0) holds;
- the lowering half of (CX1) holds on one-vertex complexes.

So it kills every variant of CX with a Φ-preserving push set, whatever the complexity and whatever
condition is put on K.

**Theorem 3.2 (sphere form).** Let `m ≥ 0` with `2m + 4 ≤ N`. There is a simplicial embedding
`ψ : S → X^0` of a triangulated m-sphere S such that:
- ψ is simplexwise injective;
- ψ hits `u_0 = [(a,b)]` and `u_1 = A_av u_0`;
- no finite sequence of **Φ-preserving vertex moves** carries ψ into `lk(β)`, for any vertex β of
  `X_n(F)` of type `(v,w)` or `(w,v)`.

A Φ-preserving vertex move replaces `ψ(x) = p[(a',b')]` by `p g [(a',b')]`, for any
`g ∈ P` with `φ_{a'b'}(g) = 0`. This includes every pair-twist push of [w9 §4] with any
presentation.

*Proof.*
- **The sphere.** Choose `2m` further free rings `c_1, d_1, …, c_m, d_m`, distinct from `a, b, v, w`
  and from each other. For each i let `S^0_i := {[(c_i,d_i)], [(d_i,c_i)]} ⊂ Q`. These two vertices
  share rings, so they are not adjacent. Vertices from different `S^0_i` have disjoint rings, and
  any choice of at most one vertex from each `S^0_i`, together with `u_0`, is a Q-simplex. Hence
  `J := S^0_1 * … * S^0_m` (a cross-polytope boundary, an `(m−1)`-sphere) satisfies
  `u_0 * J ⊆ Q ∩ X^0`.
- **Its image in X^0.** Every simplex σ of J has rings outside `{a, v}`, so `A_av ∈ P_σ`
  [w9 §1]. Then `A_av·(u_0 * σ) = u_1 * σ`, and so `u_1 * J ⊆ X^0` as well.
- **The embedding.** `u_0 ≠ u_1`, because `Φ(u_0) = 0 ≠ 1 = Φ(u_1)`. Both have type `(a,b)`, so they
  are not adjacent. Let `S := {x_0, x_1} * J` be the suspension, and let ψ send `x_i ↦ u_i` and J
  identically. Then ψ is an injective simplicial map of an m-sphere, in particular simplexwise
  injective.
- **No move reaches a star.** A Φ-preserving move keeps the type and the Φ-value of each vertex
  image. So after any sequence of moves, `ψ(x_0)` and `ψ(x_1)` still have type `(a,b)` and
  Φ-values 0 and 1. By Lemma 2.2(2), all type-`(a,b)` vertices of `lk(β)` have the same Φ-value
  `φ_ab(q)`, so the moved ψ never lands in `lk(β)`. ∎

**Consequence for the route.** Step 4 of [w9 Thm 4.2] needs the pushed sphere to land in `lk(b_0)`.
At the key degree `m = η(N)`, we have `2η(N) + 4 ≤ N` for all `N ≥ 5`. So Theorem 3.2 applies to
exactly the spheres the route must handle, and the argument cannot terminate on them. This holds for
every complexity, and even if the base vertex β is chosen after seeing ψ.

**The sphere is inessential when `2m + 5 ≤ N`** (in particular at `m = η(N)`, `N ≥ 5`). Pick one
more free ring s and put `y := [(w,s)] ∈ Q`. The rings of y are disjoint from those of `u_0` and of
J, so `y * u_0 * J ⊆ Q`. Also `A_av ∈ P_y`, because `a, v ∉ {w, s}`. Applying `A_av` gives
`y * u_1 * J ⊆ X_n(F)`. So ψ lands in the cone `y * ψ(S)` inside `X_n(F)` and is null-homotopic
there. The cone point y has type containing w, so it is not in `X^0`; it is also not a type-`{v,w}`
vertex, so it gives no base for the route. The obstruction concerns the method, not the target
`loop-braid-descending-links-are-increasingly-connected`, which stays OPEN.
