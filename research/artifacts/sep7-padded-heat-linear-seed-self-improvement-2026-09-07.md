# Padded heat flow makes the linear spectral seed necessary

Date: 2026-09-07. This is an analytic deduction from the existing curved
Hodge theorem and universal-host theorem. It supplies the converse from
flexible stability to the linear seed. It does not prove flexible
stability of SL5 or existence of a nonhyperlinear group.

## 1. Statement

Use the fixed inverse-compatible elementary presentation of
`G=SL_5(Z)`, the symmetric generating set `S`, and `h=|S|`. For
`U_s in U(n)` let `delta(U)` be its maximum normalized-HS
defining-relator defect. Let `Q_(2n)` be the universal finite quotient
specified in `moving-subgroup-schreier-correction-has-no-index-loss`.
Write

```text
Delta_(rho,U)=(1/h) sum_s D_s*D_s,
D_s(A)=rho(s)A-AU_s.
```

There exist `eta_0>0`, `delta_0>0`, and `L_0<infinity`, depending
only on the presentation and its curved Hodge constants, with this
property: if an exact representation `rho:G->U(D)` satisfies

```text
n <= D <= 2n,
max_s ||rho(s)-(U_s direct_sum I_(D-n))||_(2,D) <= eta_0,
0 < delta(U) < delta_0,
```

then

```text
lambda_min(Delta_(rho,U)) <= L_0^2 delta(U)^2,
lambda_min(Delta_(Q_(2n),U)) <= L_0^2 delta(U)^2.       (PH1)
```

The comparison representation may be reducible. Its padding fraction
need not tend to zero within this theorem; the bound `D/n<=2` suffices.
The conclusion concerns one vector. It does not assert that the original
tuple is linearly close to a full exact correction.

Consequently the following three assertions for SL5 are equivalent:

1. Flexible normalized-HS stability.
2. The dimension-uniform linear first-vector estimate `(MSC19)`.
3. The dimension-uniform logarithmic first-vector estimate `(OE17)`.

The existing extraction results prove `2=>1` and `3=>1`, and `2=>3`
is immediate. The new argument below proves `1=>2`. Thus logarithmic
slack enlarges the admitted input in the extraction theorem, but the
two uniform SL5 seed assertions have the same truth value. In particular,
the logarithmic hypothesis is not a qualitatively weaker group property.

## 2. Audit of the load-bearing curved heat estimate

The supporting proof is
`research/artifacts/curved-hodge-heat-transfers-fixed-schreier-mass-2026-09-05.md`,
Sections 1–3. Its estimate already holds for arbitrary rectangular
targets; the restriction to equal dimensions appears only in its
subsequent example application. No change to the estimate is needed.

For clarity, its uniformity was checked directly. Property (T) supplies
vanishing of `H^1(G,pi)` for every genuine Hilbert-unitary coefficient
representation. A Hilbert ultraproduct of a hypothetical sequence of
unit cochains with Hodge energy tending to zero would be a nonzero
cocycle orthogonal to the coboundaries. This proves a uniform positive
Hodge bound for genuine coefficients. The finite presentation ensures
that the coboundary and Fox-differential matrices have uniformly bounded
finite-word coefficients and pass to that ultraproduct.

For approximate coefficients `T_s(A)=rho(s)AU_s*`, failure of the
curved bound with constants growing to infinity supplies normalized
cochains `f_j` such that

```text
delta_j max_s ||f_(j,s)||op -> 0.
```

Inside the normalized rectangular-HS ultraproduct, the closed subspace
generated by bounded-HS representatives `A_j` satisfying
`delta_j||A_j||op->0` is invariant under every `T_s` and its inverse.
Each defining relator acts trivially there because

```text
||(T(r)-I)A_j||_(2,n_j)
 <= ||A_j||op ||U_j(r)-I||_(2,n_j) -> 0.
```

It therefore carries a genuine group representation containing the
limiting cochain, which contradicts the exact Hodge bound. This validates
uniform constants `mu>0`, `C_H`, and a small-defect threshold for

```text
mu ||f||_2^2 <= ||d0* f||_2^2+||d1 f||_2^2
                 +C_H delta^2 max_s ||f_s||op^2.       (PH2)
```

All rectangular HS norms in (PH2) use the source dimension n. The
generating presentation must include the inverse-consistency rows when
inverse labels are included as separate cochain coordinates, as already
specified in the supporting artifact.

Set `K_0=|R|+4C_H`, `L=d0*d0`, and start heat at any operator
contraction `A_0:C^n->C^D`. Convex averaging of the maps `T_s` and
their inverses shows that `A_t=exp(-tL)A_0` remains an operator
contraction. With

```text
E(t)=||d0 A_t||_(2,n)^2,
M(t)=||A_t||_(2,n)^2,
```

the curved estimate and `d1 d0 A_t=(T(r)-I)A_t` give

```text
E(t) <= E(0)exp(-2mu t)+(K_0/mu)delta^2,
M(t) >= M(0)-E(0)/mu-(2K_0/mu)delta^2 t.             (PH3)
```

This is the precise heat input used below. It is independent of D and n.
It neither assumes nor creates a comparison representation.

## 3. Proof of the padded first-vector assertion

Let `J:C^n->C^D` be the coordinate inclusion and put `A_0=J`.
Then `||J||op=1` and `M(0)=1`. Restricting the supplied full padded
error to its first n columns gives

```text
||rho(s)J-JU_s||_HS^2 <= eta_0^2 D,
E(0) <= h eta_0^2 D/n <= 2h eta_0^2.                 (PH4)
```

Fix `eta_0` with `2h eta_0^2<=mu/4`. Shrink the defect threshold
below one and apply (PH3) at

```text
t_delta=log(1/delta)/mu.
```

Since `delta^2 log(1/delta)->0`, the threshold can be chosen
uniformly so that

```text
M(t_delta) >= 1/2,
E(t_delta) <= (mu/4+K_0/mu)delta^2.                  (PH5)
```

The heat vector is nonzero. Its Rayleigh quotient for `Delta=L/h`
is `E/(hM)`, so the first inequality in (PH1) holds with

```text
L_0^2=(2/h)(mu/4+K_0/mu).
```

Every irreducible constituent of rho has dimension at most D, hence at
most `2n`, and factors through `Q_(2n)` by the existing universal-host
theorem. Decompose the nonzero heat vector into its irreducible target
components. At least one component has Rayleigh quotient no larger than
the full vector, by the weighted-average identity for orthogonal sums.
That irreducible occurs in the regular representation of `Q_(2n)`.
Moving the component into one such copy preserves its Rayleigh quotient
and proves the second inequality in (PH1).

This last step does not require rho itself to embed into the regular
host: its irreducible multiplicities may be larger than the regular
multiplicities. Only the selected single constituent is needed.

## 4. Flexible stability supplies the fixed basin uniformly

Assume flexible normalized-HS stability. For the fixed `eta_0` above,
there exists `delta_stab>0` such that every tuple with defect below
`delta_stab` has an exact padded correction with `D/n<=2` and
full padded generator error at most `eta_0`.

Indeed, otherwise select for each j a counterexample with defect below
`1/j`. Flexible stability supplies corrections to this sequence with
`D_j/n_j->1` and full padded errors tending to zero, contradicting
their selection. No quantitative stability modulus has been assumed.
The compressed-corner definition of flexible stability gives the same
conclusion: vanishing compression error and padding yield vanishing full
padded error by unitarity and the trace overlap formula.

Applying (PH1) below
`min(delta_stab,delta_0)` establishes `(MSC19)` with `L=L_0`.
Together with the already proved linear/Osgood extraction implications,
this proves the three-way equivalence in Section 1.

## 5. Consequences for attempted slow-decay counterexamples

No sequence which is flexibly correctable, even at arbitrarily slow
convergence rates, can violate the uniform linear first-vector estimate
at sufficiently small defect. Its eventual corrections enter the single
fixed basin used in Section 4. A sequence with

```text
delta_j -> 0,
lambda_min(Delta_(Q_(2n_j),U_j))/delta_j^2 -> infinity
```

must eventually stay outside that fixed basin: it has no exact correction
with `D<=2n_j` and full padded error at most `eta_0`. Such a sequence
would witness a fixed positive obstruction to flexible stability, not
merely slow convergence to an exact representation.

This excludes the standard candidate recipes that start from exact
representations and retain known corrections with vanishing padding and
error, including codimension-one and other vanishing-codimension
compressions. Independently, `(MSC15)` gives, for every fixed cutoff e,

```text
N_(U_j)(e)/n_j >= 1-eta_j^2 D_j/(e^2 n_j) -> 1
```

as a lower bound along any flexibly correctable sequence. Thus those
examples also cannot have fixed-cutoff count decaying like `1/log`.

For the fixed small cutoff `epsilon_*` of `(MSC21)`, each of these
uniform assertions is consequently equivalent to flexible stability:

- A fixed positive lower bound for `N_U(epsilon_*)/n` at small defect.
- A lower bound `c/log(e/delta^2)` at small defect, for some `c>0`.

The first implies the second; the second gives the logarithmic seed by
the elementary heat-count calculation in
`sep7-osgood-seed-independent-audit-2026-09-07.md`; flexible stability
gives the first through `(MSC15)`. This equivalence does not establish
either lower bound.

## 6. Primary-source checks and the remaining target

Becker–Lubotzky's [*Group stability and Property (T)*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/am/pii/S002212361930271X)
proves an obstruction to strict normalized-HS stability of infinite
hyperlinear Kazhdan groups and distinguishes normalized HS from
unnormalized Schatten stability. The dimension-deletion construction is
consistent with the padded theorem above; it does not contradict it.

Ioana's [*Almost commuting matrices and stability for product groups*](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/51186)
proves failure of flexible HS stability for products of two nonabelian
free groups. Its introduction also distinguishes these examples and the
relative-(T) semidirect-product examples from the open flexible-stability
question for integral higher-rank special linear groups. These groups do
not furnish approximate SL5 tuples with the required obstruction.

Dogon–Vigdorovich's [*Hyperlinearity, stability and asymptotic spectral gap
of higher rank lattices*, version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20843v2)
provides character-rigidity reformulations through hyperfinite stability
and robust asymptotic spectral gaps. Those restricted conclusions do not
establish the arbitrary-tuple fixed basin used here.

The source search did not locate a known approximate SL5 sequence
contradicting these equivalent seed assertions or a theorem establishing
them. The concrete new deduction is the necessary linear seed and the
resulting exclusion of all merely slowly correctable counterexamples.
The existence question remains the original flexible-stability/nonhyperlinearity
boundary, with no new unconditional group witness.
