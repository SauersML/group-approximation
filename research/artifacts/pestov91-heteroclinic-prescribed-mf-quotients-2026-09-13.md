# Prescribed simple Kazhdan LEF factors as the maximal MF quotient

2026-09-13. Written proof, not Lean-verified. The fixed endpoints in the
monotone example can be replaced by two prescribed minimal subshifts.
The transition-orbit kernel stays the same, while the maximal MF quotient
can have infinite simple Kazhdan LEF factors and no finite quotients.

## Theorem

For any two infinite minimal binary subshifts `Y_-` and `Y_+`, set

`R_i=LC(Y_i,F_2) semidirect Z`, `Q_i=EL_6(R_i)`.

There is a finitely generated Kazhdan group `H` with an exact sequence

`1 -> L -> H -> Q_- x Q_+ -> 1`,                         (1)

such that:

- each `Q_i` is infinite, simple, Kazhdan and LEF, and is generated by
  three elements of orders `7,2,2`;
- `L ~= SL_infinity(F_2)` is infinite, simple and locally finite;
- `Rad_MF(H)=L`, and (1) is the maximal MF quotient of `H`;
- `H` has no nontrivial finite quotient, so `Res_fin(H)=H` while its MF
  radical is proper;
- `H` is generated by three torsion elements of orders `7,2,2`.

The endpoint factors are the specified matrix amplifications `EL_6(R_i)`.
No assertion that `EL_6(R_i)` is isomorphic to `EL_3(R_i)` is needed.
Each factor is itself a binary Pestov group through the explicit
letter-doubling construction below. Choosing either input from the
universal-odometer construction makes its factor a host for every
countable locally finite group, since it contains `EL_3(R_i)`.

## 1. Letter repetition realizes every matrix amplification

Let `Y` be an infinite minimal binary subshift and `k>=1`. Replace each
letter of `y` by `k` identical consecutive letters, writing

`D_k(y)_(ki+j)=y_i`, `0<=j<k`.

Let `Z_k(Y)` be the union of the `k` sets `T^a D_k(Y)`, `0<=a<k`.
These sets are compact and pairwise disjoint. Indeed a change between
adjacent letters in a sequence of phase `a` can occur only at one
residue class modulo `k`. Two different phases would force there to be
no changes at all. That would be a constant sequence, which does not
belong to an infinite minimal binary subshift or its repeated image.
Within each phase the original sequence is recovered by taking every
`k`th letter. Thus the phase sets form a clopen partition.

The shift cycles the phases, and `T^k` on the zero phase is conjugate
to `T` on `Y`. It follows directly that `Z_k(Y)` is an infinite minimal
binary subshift. Denote its phase cells by `C_a=T^a D_k(Y)` and its
crossed-product shift unit by `v`. The elements

`E_ab=1_(C_a) v^(a-b)`, `0<=a,b<k`,

are full matrix units. The corner at `C_0` is the crossed product of
the return system, hence isomorphic to `R_Y`. Explicitly, only powers
of `v` divisible by `k` survive compression to that corner. The full
matrix units therefore give

`R_(Z_k(Y)) ~= M_k(R_Y)`,
`EL_3(R_(Z_k(Y))) ~= EL_(3k)(R_Y)`.                      (2)

For the last equality, flatten outer elementary roots into scalar
coordinate roots. A root inside one outer colour is a commutator
through a different colour, so both inclusions of elementary groups
hold even over the noncommutative coefficient ring.

The existing binary Pestov theorem applies to `Z_k(Y)`: its elementary
group is infinite, simple, Kazhdan, LEF, and generated by the triple of
orders `7,2,2`. Taking `k=2` proves every assertion about the factors
`Q_i` in the theorem. This argument also gives (2) and the same group
properties for every positive `k`.

## 2. Join two minimal systems by one isolated orbit

Choose points `y^- in Y_-`, `y^+ in Y_+`. Use the four-letter alphabet
`{0,1} x {0,1}`, with the second coordinate recording a phase. Define

`z_j=(y^-_j,0)` for `j<0`,
`z_j=(y^+_j,1)` for `j>=0`,

and let `X` be the orbit closure of `z`. Write `widehat(Y_-)` and
`widehat(Y_+)` for the copies with constant phases 0 and 1. Then

`X = widehat(Y_-) disjointUnion widehat(Y_+)
     disjointUnion {T^j z:j in Z}`.                      (3)

To check (3), a convergent sequence of shifts with bounded shift indices
has a subsequence at one orbit point. Along indices tending to positive
infinity, every fixed window is in the right tail, and the limit lies
in `widehat(Y_+)`; negative infinity gives `widehat(Y_-)`. Conversely,
minimality makes the positive orbit of `y^+` and the negative orbit of
`y^-` dense in their systems, so all points in both components occur
as limits. Each point of the joining orbit is isolated: a phase change
at a prescribed coordinate singles it out among (3).

Let `R=LC(X,F_2) semidirect Z`. Restriction to the closed invariant set
`widehat(Y_-) union widehat(Y_+)` gives a ring epimorphism

`R -> R_- x R_+`.                                       (4)

Surjectivity on coefficient functions is elementary: a locally constant
function on that closed set is specified by a sufficiently large finite
window. Its table extends to the remaining, mixed-phase windows of `X`.
The shift unit goes to the pair of shift units, so (4) is onto on the
whole crossed product.

## 3. The same finite-support kernel, independently of the endpoints

A locally constant function vanishing on the two closed components has
compact support in the discrete joining orbit, hence finite support.
Conversely each singleton of that orbit is clopen. The orbit is dense,
so normal forms act faithfully on its vector space `F_2^(Z)` exactly
as in the monotone example. Thus

`ker(4)=M_fin(Z,F_2)`.

Writing `p` for the phase-one indicator, its values on `T^j z` are
`1_(j>=0)`. Therefore

`e=p-upu^{-1}`

is the singleton indicator of `z`; all matrix units of this ideal are
`u^i e u^{-j}`.

For every `n>=3`, (4) induces the exact group sequence

`1 -> L_n -> EL_n(R) -> EL_n(R_-) x EL_n(R_+) -> 1`,      (5)

where `L_n=GL_fin(Z x {1,...,n},F_2)`. This identifies the entire
kernel: a kernel matrix is an invertible finite block extended by the
identity, and every such block belongs to the elementary group because
its determinant over `F_2` is one. Between different outer colours its
transvections are roots with coefficient `u^i e u^{-j}`; within one
colour they are commutators through another colour. As before, `L_n`
is infinite, simple and locally finite.

Since the phase-one set is strictly forward invariant, the pair

`s=up+1-p`, `t=pu^{-1}+1-p`

satisfies `ts=1` and `1-st=e!=0`. The printed rank-four torsion-defect
theorem puts `e_12(e)` in `Rad_MF(EL_n(R))` for `n>=4`. Simplicity of
`L_n` gives `L_n<=Rad_MF(EL_n(R))`. The endpoint rings are LEF by the
minimal-subshift ring theorem, so their elementary groups are LEF and
MF. The quotient in (5) then gives the reverse containment:

`Rad_MF(EL_n(R))=L_n` for every `n>=4`.                  (6)

Thus (5) is the maximal MF quotient, with unique factorization of every
homomorphism to an MF group. This proof computes the radical using only
the joining orbit; the internal complexity of the two minimal systems
does not change the kernel.

## 4. The finite residual is the whole ambient group

Each `EL_3(R_i)` is infinite simple, hence has no nontrivial finite
quotient. For `n>=3`, `EL_n(R_i)` is generated by its rank-three copies
on triples of coordinates. A homomorphism to a finite group kills each
copy, and therefore the whole group. Thus both factors in (5) have no
nontrivial finite quotient.

A homomorphism from `EL_n(R)` to a finite group also kills `L_n`, which
is infinite simple. It consequently factors through (5), where it is
trivial by the preceding paragraph. Hence

`Res_fin(EL_n(R))=EL_n(R)` for every `n>=3`.

For `n>=4` this differs from (6): the quotient there is nontrivial and
MF. At `n=6`, (2) additionally says that its two factors are simple.
The finite residual therefore carries strictly less information here
than the MF radical.

## 5. A recognizable binary coding gives three torsion generators

For `x in X`, write `x_j=(b_j,p_j)` for its payload and phase. Interleave
the two bits to define a binary sequence

`J(x)_(2j)=b_j`, `J(x)_(2j+1)=p_j`.

The two compact sets `J(X)` and `T J(X)` are disjoint. Every phase
sequence `p` is nondecreasing, while no payload sequence `b` is
nondecreasing: in each minimal component both letters recur, and in
the joining orbit this remains true on both tails. An equality
`J(x)=T J(x')` would identify the payload of `x` with the phase of
`x'`, a contradiction. The original point is recovered within each
parity by reading consecutive bit pairs.

Thus `widehat X=J(X) union T J(X)` is a binary subshift with two clopen
parity cells. Its shift exchanges the cells, and the return map `T^2`
on `J(X)` is conjugate to the shift on `X`. The same full cyclic-corner
calculation as in section 1, which does not require minimality, gives

`R_(widehat X) ~= M_2(R)`,
`EL_3(R_(widehat X)) ~= EL_6(R)`.                        (7)

The binary coefficient ring is generated by its shift unit, inverse
and letter-one idempotent. Apply the unit-idempotent three-generator
theorem, which also does not require minimality. For explicit formulas
inside `M_2(R)`, let `b,p` be the payload and phase indicators and put

`a=[[0,u],[1,0]]`, `r=diag(b,p)`.

Under the corner identification these are the binary shift unit and
letter-one idempotent. The three generators of `EL_3(M_2(R))` are

`c=[[0,0,1],[1,0,1],[0,1,0]]`,
`t=e_12(1-r)e_13(r)`,
`w=[[0,a,0],[a^{-1},0,0],[0,0,1]]`,

where the constants are the identity or zero of `M_2(R)`. Flattening
gives three explicit matrices in `EL_6(R)`, of orders `7,2,2`.

The ring is finitely generated, so Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain gives property
(T). Set `H=EL_6(R)` and `L=L_6`. Equations (2), (5)--(7), together
with section 4, prove every assertion of the theorem. In particular this
specific nonminimal binary system `widehat X` has a non-MF rank-three
elementary group; its coefficient ring is a matrix amplification, so
the analytic obstruction still applies at rank six over `R`.

## 6. Scope and validation

This is a prescription for two amplified binary Pestov factors, not for
arbitrary MF groups. The exact MF computation over the original seam
ring uses rank at least four. Binary recoding realizes its rank-six
instance in rank three over a different ring; this does not prove the
negative direction for every rank-three subshift group. The earlier monotone
example is recovered by using constant endpoints in the seam argument,
but the new infinite minimal endpoints make the finite residual equal
to the whole group instead of the MF radical.

The new ingredients are binary letter repetition for matrix amplification
and the joining-orbit construction. The finite-support kernel argument
and torsion-defect obstruction are the existing checked mathematical
inputs. No statement about soficity or hyperlinearity of the non-MF
ambient extension is used. These are written proofs, not Lean
formalizations, with no global priority assertion.
