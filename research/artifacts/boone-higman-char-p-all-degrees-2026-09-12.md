# Positive-characteristic linear groups at every transcendence degree, 2026-09-12

Lane `bh-char-p-all-degrees`, in the Boone–Higman swarm. Boone–Higman stays open, and so does the full positive-characteristic linear class. What this artifact does:
- lands every finitely generated linear group in characteristic `p`, at every transcendence degree, in an explicit simple group;
- reduces Boone–Higman for that whole class to one finite-presentation statement about elementary groups of Leavitt tensor powers, which needs no `K_1` input.

Write `L_p = L_(F_p)(1,2)`, `R_d = L_p^(⊗d)`, and
`P_m(R) = E_m(R) / Z(E_m(R))`.

**Results.**
1. **Theorem C (unconditional).** Let `K` be a field of characteristic `p > 0`, and `H <= GL_n(K)` finitely generated with entry field of transcendence degree at most `d >= 1`. There is `N` such that for every `m >= 2N+1`, `H` embeds in `P_m(R_d)`. That group is simple for `m >= 3`.
2. **Theorem D (conditional).** If `P_m(R_d)` is finitely presented for infinitely many `m`, then every such `H` satisfies Boone–Higman. So the full positive-characteristic class needs only `leavitt-power-projective-elementary-groups-finitely-presented`.
3. **Sufficient condition.** `K_1(R_d) = K_2(R_d) = 0` implies that premise, through `prime-field-leavitt-tensor-hosts-fp-and-simple-mod-centre`(2,3). By `projective-elementary-fp-kernel-criterion`, the premise is equivalent to finite normal generation of `ker(St_m(R_d) -> E_m(R_d))`, and `K_1` plays no role.
4. **Odd `p`.** The scalar centre `F_p^x` needs no special treatment. The Whitehead embedding already meets the centre trivially, and the quotient by a finite central subgroup preserves finite presentation.

Novelty is not claimed. The swarm's literature lane `bh-lit-map` has not reported yet.

## 1. Inputs used

- `prime-field-leavitt-tensor-hosts-fp-and-simple-mod-centre`, part 4: for `B` central simple over `F_p` and `n >= 3`, `E_n(B ⊗ L_p)` modulo its scalar centre is simple. This part uses no `K`-theory.
- `leavitt-center-is-coefficient-field`: `Z(L_k(1,2)) = k` for every field `k`.
- `projective-elementary-fp-kernel-criterion`: for a finitely presented ring `R` and `n >= 4`, `E_n(R)/Z` is finitely presented iff the unstable Steinberg kernel is finitely normally generated and `Z(E_n(R))` is finitely generated.
- The Laurent realization of `char-two-laurent-linear-groups-proof` and Lemma 7.1 of `boone-higman-leavitt-tensor-hosts-2026-09-12.md`. Both are rechecked over `F_p` below.

## 2. The coefficient ring inside `R_d`

**Lemma A (Laurent realization over `F_p`, every `d`).** Let `d >= 1` and `0 != h ∈ F_p[t_1, ..., t_d]`. There is a unital injective ring map `F_p[t_1, ..., t_d][1/h] -> R_d`.

*Proof.* This is Lemma 7.1 of the `F_2` artifact, with `F_p` in place of `F_2`. Every step is characteristic-free.
1. **The Thompson unit.** On the test module `M` (basis: the binary sequences with finitely many ones), the unit
   `g = s_0 t_0 t_0 + s_1 s_0 t_1 t_0 + s_1 s_1 t_1` sends
   `v = 1 0 0 ...` to the pairwise distinct basis vectors `g^k(v)`, `k ∈ Z`.
   Distinct basis vectors are linearly independent over any field. So
   `F_p[g^±] -> L_p` is injective.
2. **`d = 1`.** Put `f = t h / lc(h)`, monic. Then `F_p[t][1/h] ⊆ F_p[t][1/f] ≅ F_p[g^±][T]/(f(T) - g)`. This is free over `F_p[g^±]` with basis `1, ..., T^(deg f - 1)`, so multiplication embeds it unitally in `M_(deg f)(F_p[g^±]) ⊆ M_(deg f)(L_p) ≅ L_p`.
3. **`d >= 2`.** Let `c` be the leading coefficient of `h` in `t_d`, `C = F_p[t_1, ..., t_(d-1)][1/c]` and `f = t_d h / c`, monic in `C[t_d]`.
   - `1/h = t_d / (c f)`, so `F_p[t_1..t_d][1/h] ⊆ C[t_d][1/f] ≅ C[g^±][T]/(f(T) - g)`, free over `C[g^±]` of rank `deg f`.
   - `C[g^±] = C ⊗ F_p[g^±]` embeds in `R_(d-1) ⊗ L_p = R_d`, by induction and injectivity of tensor products of injections over a field.
   - `M_k(R_d) ≅ R_d` through leaf coordinates in the last factor. `∎`

**Reduction to the ring.** Let `H <= GL_n(K)` be finitely generated.
- The field generated by the entries of the generators and their inverses is finite, of degree `e`, over `F_p(t_1, ..., t_d)` for a transcendence basis. For `d = 0` it is a finite field, which embeds in `M_e(F_p)` by a companion matrix.
- The regular representation gives `H <= GL_(ne)(F_p(t_1..t_d))`.
- Clearing all denominators of the generators and of their inverses into one `h` gives `H <= GL_(ne)(F_p[t_1..t_d][1/h])`.
- By Lemma A and leaf coordinates, `H <= GL_N(R_d)` for some `N`.

## 3. Landing in the elementary group

**Lemma B (Whitehead, any ring).** For `X ∈ GL_N(R)`,

```text
diag(X, X^-1) = [[I, X],[0, I]] [[I, 0],[-X^-1, I]] [[I, X],[0, I]]
                [[I, -I],[0, I]] [[I, 0],[I, I]] [[I, -I],[0, I]]
```

in `GL_(2N)(R)`. The first three factors multiply to `[[0, X],[-X^-1, 0]]`, the last three to `[[0, -I],[I, 0]]`, and their product is `diag(X, X^-1)`. Each factor is block unitriangular, hence a product of elementary matrices. So `diag(X, X^-1) ∈ E_(2N)(R)`. `∎`

**Lemma C (centre).** Let `R` be a ring with centre `k` and `m >= 2`. `Z(E_m(R))` consists of scalar matrices `cI` with `c ∈ k^x`.
- Commuting with every `e_ij(1)` forces a diagonal matrix with equal entries.
- Commuting with every `e_ij(r)` forces that entry to be central. `∎`

For `R = R_d`, `Z(R_d) = Z(L_p)^(⊗d) = F_p` (centres of tensor products over a field, and `leavitt-center-is-coefficient-field`). So `Z(E_m(R_d)) ⊆ F_p^x I`, a finite group.

**Theorem C.** In the setting above, `X |-> diag(X, X^-1, 1, ..., 1)` is an injective homomorphism `H -> E_m(R_d)` for every `m >= 2N+1`, meeting `Z(E_m(R_d))` trivially. So `H` embeds in `P_m(R_d)`, and `P_m(R_d)` is simple for `m >= 3`.

*Proof.*
- **Into `E_m`.** Lemma B and block inclusion.
- **Injective.** The first block recovers `X`.
- **Centre.** A scalar `cI` with last entry `1` has `c = 1`, so `X = I`.
- **Simplicity.** `R_d = R_(d-1) ⊗ L_p`, and `R_(d-1)` is central simple over `F_p`. By induction, a tensor product of a central simple algebra and a simple algebra over a field is simple with the other factor's centre, and `L_p` is simple with centre `F_p`. So part 4 of `prime-field-leavitt-tensor-hosts-fp-and-simple-mod-centre` applies with `B = R_(d-1)`. `∎`

This uses no `K`-theory and no hypothesis on `p`.

## 4. What finite presentation needs

**Theorem D.** Suppose that for every `d >= 1` there are infinitely many `m` with `P_m(R_d)` finitely presented. Then every finitely generated linear group over a field of positive characteristic satisfies Boone–Higman.

*Proof.* Given `H`, take `d` and `N` from Section 2, choose such an `m >= 2N+1`, and apply Theorem C. `∎`

**Where the premise stands.**
1. **`d <= 2`: known.** `K(R_1) = 0` and `K(R_2) = 0` (Ara–Brustenga–Cortiñas; Ara–Cortiñas over `F_p`, as used in `odd-leavitt-unit-groups-mod-scalars-are-fp-simple`). Parts 2 and 3 of the prime-field host claim then give `E_m(R_d) = GL_m(R_d) ≅ R_d^x`, finitely presented.
   - `M_m(R_d) ≅ R_d` for every `m`, since `gcd(m, 2 - 1) = 1` (Ara–Goodearl–Pardo).
   - Dividing by the finite centre keeps finite presentation.
   - This recovers `positive-char-surface-linear-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`, with the host `P_m(R_2)` in place of `R_2^x / F_p^x`.
2. **Sufficient for every `d`.** `K_1(R_d) = K_2(R_d) = 0`, the open `prime-field-leavitt-tensor-powers-have-trivial-k-theory`. For `p = 2` this is `leavitt-tensor-powers-have-trivial-k-theory`.
3. **Equivalent form.** `R_d` is a finitely presented ring, as a tensor product of finitely presented algebras over a field. `St_m(R_d)` is finitely presented for `m >= 4` (Krstić–McCool). So `projective-elementary-fp-kernel-criterion` makes `P_m(R_d)` finitely presented iff `ker(St_m(R_d) -> E_m(R_d))` is finitely normally generated, since the centre is finite.
   - `K_1(R_d)` never enters.
   - In a stable range where this kernel is central and equals `K_2(R_d)`, finite generation of `K_2(R_d)` suffices. Its vanishing is not required.

## 5. The `K`-theory input over `F_p`

Everything recorded on `leavitt-tensor-powers-have-trivial-k-theory` over `F_2` is characteristic-free.
- **`KH` vanishes.** Ara–Brustenga–Cortiñas Theorem 8.6 gives `KH(A ⊗ L_k(1,2)) = 0` for every coefficient ring `A` over any field `k`.
- **The obstruction is a twisted Nil group.** Their Theorem 5.10 needs only H-unitality of the coefficient ring, and expresses `K(L_p ⊗ A)` through `NK(L_0 ⊗ A; φ^(±1))`. Here `L_0` is the ultramatricial degree-zero part and `φ` is the corner map.
- **Regular supercoherence.** Ara–Cortiñas Proposition 6.2, over `k = F_p`, gives `K(L_p ⊗ S) = 0` for regular supercoherent `S`. The missing input is again regular coherence of free algebras over `R_(d-2)[t_1, ..., t_n]`.

So a characteristic-free proof of the `F_2` claim settles the `F_p` claim.

## 6. Limits

- **Transcendence degree without tensor powers.** One or two Leavitt factors are not known to contain a commutative domain of transcendence degree 3.
  - In one factor, two commuting Thompson units with disjoint supports satisfy `(u-1)(v-1) = 0`; see the Attempts on `char-two-linear-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`.
  - Not disproved: a unital embedding `F_p[t_1, t_2, t_3] -> R_2` would bypass the premise at `d = 3`.
- **Trust.** The simplicity input is a paper argument reading the Lean root-detection proof. It was reviewed, not formalized. The `d <= 2` finite presentation rests on Khanh arXiv:2609.08428v1, which is unrefereed. Theorem C and Lemmas A–C are elementary and unreviewed.
