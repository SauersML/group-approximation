# A marked projective gap for semisimple coefficient-generated algebras

2026-09-08. Pure ring, group, and representation arguments. No code,
build, or numerical experiment was run.

The full-group extension hypothesis can be removed whenever the
algebra generated by the literal coefficients is semisimple. This
covers singular coefficient products and unequal coordinate ranks.
The proof separates two types of simple path-algebra factors and
uses an exact three-cycle character bound for one of them.

## 1. Statement and the path-algebra radical

Let `A` be a nonzero finite unital algebra over `F_2`, let
`s,t,v in A`, and put

```text
A_0=F_2<s,t,v>,              K=<c,x_12(s),x_12(t),x_12(v)>,
c=(123),                    Z=ts.
```

The coefficient-generated algebra `A_0` includes the unit.
Use the native words `r_*,R_v,R_0` defined in the
[full finite-coefficient theorem](leavitt-all-finite-coefficient-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md).
For an exact homomorphism `rho:K -> PU(N)`, let

```text
L(g)=d_sc(rho(g),1),
eta_*=L(r_*),               eta_v=L(R_v),               eta_0=L(R_0).
```

Here `d_sc(U,I)=min_(|zeta|=1)||U-zeta I||_2`, with normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm.

**Theorem.** Suppose `A_0` is semisimple. For a unitary representative
of `rho(c)`, put `a=|tau(rho(c))|`. Then

```text
49 eta_*+24 eta_v+24 eta_0 >= beta(a),
beta(a)=sqrt(2-2sqrt((2+3a^2)/5)).                   (1a)
```

In particular, if this trace is zero, then

```text
49 eta_*+24 eta_v+24 eta_0 >= beta,
beta=sqrt(2-2sqrt(2/5)).                              (1)
```

Consequently

```text
max{eta_*,eta_v,eta_0} >= beta/97.                    (2)
```

The representation need only be defined on the marked subgroup
`K`; there is no extension hypothesis. In particular, the theorem
applies when `s,t,v` generate `M_d(F_q)` as a unital `F_2`-algebra.
Semisimplicity of the ambient algebra `A` alone is not the stated
hypothesis.

Let `P <= M_3(A_0)` be generated by the coordinate idempotents
`e_1,e_2,e_3` and the nine increments

```text
e_12 b, e_23 b, e_31 b,                         b in {s,t,v}.
```

The cycle `c` normalizes `P`. The following equivalence justifies
using the path algebra in the proof:

```text
P is semisimple  <=>  A_0 is semisimple.             (3)
```

Indeed, `Q=<P,c>=M_3(A_0)`. The elements `e_i c e_j` give
constant matrix units on the coordinate cycle, their products give
all constant matrix units, and the nine increments then generate
all matrix entries with coefficients in `A_0`. Moreover

```text
Q=P+Pc+Pc^2,
I=J(P)+J(P)c+J(P)c^2
```

is a nilpotent two-sided ideal of `Q`: `c` preserves `J(P)`, and
`I^m` is contained in `sum_(i=0)^2 J(P)^m c^i`. Thus

```text
J(P) <= J(Q)=M_3(J(A_0)).
```

Conversely `P intersect M_3(J(A_0))` is a nilpotent ideal of `P`,
so

```text
J(P)=P intersect M_3(J(A_0)).                       (4)
```

This proves that semisimplicity of `A_0` implies that of `P`.
For the converse, suppose `P` is semisimple. Given a submodule of
a finite-dimensional `Q`-module, choose a `P`-linear projection
onto it and average that projection over `1,c,c^2`. Division by
three is allowed in characteristic two. Each conjugated projection
is `P`-linear, and the average commutes with `c`; its range is the
given submodule and it is the identity there. It is therefore a
`Q`-linear projection. Hence `Q`, and thus `A_0`, is semisimple.

This argument also gives `I=J(Q)`: the image of `P` in `Q/I` is
`P/J(P)`, and the same averaging makes `Q/I` semisimple. It does
not say that a complex representation of `K` kills a radical.

## 2. The exact normal subgroup and its simple factors

Let `K_0` be the normal closure of the three marked roots in `K`.
It is generated by their nine cyclic conjugates. In fact

```text
K_0=<1+x : x in e_i P e_j, i!=j> <= P^x.           (5)
```

To see equality, off-diagonal Peirce corners of `P` consist of
linear combinations of coefficient words of lengths `1` or
`2 mod 3`, in the corresponding forward or backward root
positions. The distinct-index commutator induction in
[the unit-root theorem, Section 1](leavitt-unit-root-marked-subgroup-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md)
generates all of these roots from the nine initial ones. Addition
of coefficients is same-root multiplication. The reverse inclusion
in (5) is immediate from the generators.

Assume henceforth that `P` is semisimple, and write it as a product
of simple algebras `M_n(F_q)`. In a component let
`r_1,r_2,r_3` be the ranks of the coordinate idempotents.
If at least two are positive, (5) induces the whole `SL_n(F_q)`.
Indeed, the off-diagonal Peirce spaces provide all elementary
transvections between basis vectors in different coordinate parts.
A transvection between two vectors in the same part is a
commutator through a vector in another nonzero part. These generate
`SL_n(F_q)`. If just one coordinate part is nonzero, the root
group in that component is trivial. Small groups such as
`SL_2(F_2)` require no exception.

The factors occur independently in (5), because an arbitrary
off-diagonal Peirce element supported on just one simple factor
belongs to `P`. Thus `K_0` is the exact direct product of these
component groups.

Call a component **balanced** when `r_1=r_2=r_3`, and call the
other components **unbalanced**. Conjugation by `c` preserves this
distinction. A component fixed by `c` is balanced, so unbalanced
components occur in orbits of three.

Let `p` be the sum of the central idempotents of the balanced
components. It commutes with `e_1,e_2,e_3`, hence is diagonal in
`M_3(A_0)`. Since it is fixed by `c`, it has the form

```text
p=diag(p_0,p_0,p_0).
```

Its commutation with the nine increments implies that `p_0`
commutes with `s,t,v`, hence is central in `A_0`. Put
`q_0=1-p_0`. This is an actual coefficient decomposition, not a
projection chosen in the complex representation.

Write `J` for the direct product of the groups in the balanced
components. The unbalanced components form triples of isomorphic
groups, and the action of `c` on each triple can be identified with
the ordinary cyclic permutation: transport identifications around
the orbit, using `c^3=1` for consistency. Combining the triples
gives a finite group `N` with

```text
K_0=J times N^3.                                  (6)
```

Factors with just one nonzero coordinate part contribute the
trivial group to `N`. If there is any unbalanced component,
`c` permutes nonzero central idempotents of `P`, whereas every
element of `K_0 <= P^x` fixes them. Thus `c notin K_0`, and

```text
K=(J times N^3) semidirect <c>,                    (7)
```

where `c` has order three, acts by an arbitrary automorphism of
order dividing three on `J`, and cycles `N^3`.

We do not assert a direct-product splitting that separates the
actual coordinate cycle into balanced and unbalanced cycles in
`K`. A cycle recovered after a diagonal gauge can differ from the
original cycle by a residual cyclic action.

## 3. Localized extraction controls the balanced part of j

The native involution `j` belongs to `K_0`: it is conjugate to
`e=c d c^(-1)`, where `d` is a root commutator. By (6), write

```text
j=j_g j_b,             j_g in J,             j_b in N^3.
```

The two factors commute and each squares to one. We claim

```text
L(j_g) <= kappa:=24 eta_*+12 eta_v+12 eta_0.       (8)
```

If there are no balanced components, take `j_g=1` and use the
stronger value `kappa=0`. Otherwise work in `A_g=p_0 A_0`, with
unit `p_0` and coefficients `s_g=p_0s,t_g=p_0t,v_g=p_0v`.
Its path algebra is `pP`. The balanced-Peirce characterization in
[the unit-root theorem, Section 6](leavitt-unit-root-marked-subgroup-projective-gap-2026-09-08.md)
gives a unit `z` of `A_g` with `x_13(z) in K_0`.
Here and below a root with a coefficient in `A_g` acts as the
identity on the complementary coefficient summand. Formula (5)
also puts every localized marked or intermediate root in `K_0`.

Extend `z` to the unit `z+q_0` of `A_0`, and make the diagonal
gauge

```text
Delta=diag(1,(z+q_0)^(-1),z+q_0),
K'=Delta K Delta^(-1),              C=Delta c Delta^(-1).
```

Transport `rho` accordingly. The unit-root construction gives all
partial constant roots `x_ij(p_0)` in `K'`. Let `c_0g` be their
constant coordinate cycle, acting identically on the complement.
Then `H=C c_0g^(-1)` acts as `diag(z^(-1),z^(-1),z^2)` on
the balanced summand and as `c` on the complementary summand.
The latter action is harmless when conjugating roots supported on
the balanced summand.

Define

```text
B={b in A_g : x_ij(b) in K' for every i!=j},
sigma=Ad(z),       w=z^3,       Z_g=t_gs_g,
a_0=Z_g z^(-1),   F=p_0+Z_g^3,
Q=(v_g^2+Z_g)z^(-1),            T=t_gv_gs_gZ_g^2z.
```

The ring `B` is finite with unit `p_0`; conjugation by `H`
gives `sigma(B)=B`, `sigma^3=Ad(w)`, and `sigma(w)=w`.
The coefficient memberships and skew-matrix identities of the
unit-root proof apply in this corner.

It is essential to bound these roots using the **full** relators,
since their balanced components need not individually have small
scalar distance. On the balanced summand the first row of `r_*'`
is

```text
(Z_g^10,Fz,Z_g^5 F z^(-1)).
```

The column `(F,z^(-1)Z_g^10,0)^T`, zero on the complement,
annihilates the full first row. Its entries lie in `B`, with
`z^(-1)Z_g^10=sigma^(-1)(a_0)(Z_g^3)^3`.
The internal proof of Preusser's Proposition 9(i), used in the
unit-root artifact, therefore extracts any
`x_ij(alpha F beta)` as eight conjugates of the full
`(r_*')^(+/-1)`. All auxiliary roots are supported on the balanced
summand; partial constant permutations suffice to move the output
root. The complementary terms cancel in those commutators.

Likewise, the two distinct-index commutators applied to the full
`R_v'` or `R_0'`, with both outer coefficients in `B`, kill
their complementary coefficients. Conjugating by `H` gives

```text
L(x_kl(alpha sigma^i(F) beta)) <= 8 eta_*,
L(x_kl(alpha sigma^i(Q) beta)) <= 4 eta_v,
L(x_kl(alpha sigma^i(T) beta)) <= 4 eta_0,
```

for `i=0,1,2` and arbitrary `alpha,beta in B`.
The nine-term unit identity from the induced three-block rank
calculation in the unit-root proof holds in `B`, with unit
`p_0` and coefficient types

```text
F,sigma(F),sigma^2(F),Q,sigma(Q),sigma^2(Q),
T,sigma(T),sigma^2(T).
```

Multiplying that identity on the left by any `b in B` shows
`L(x_ij(b))<=kappa`. Finally `j_g'` is conjugate inside `K'`
to `x_21(z^(-1)Z_g)`, whose coefficient belongs to `B`.
This proves (8).

## 4. The unbalanced part supplies the trace gap

If there are no unbalanced components, the global unit-root theorem
already gives

```text
L(c) <= 49 eta_*+24 eta_v+24 eta_0.
```

Here `L(c)=sqrt(2-2a)>=beta(a)`, so (1a) follows in this case.
Assume now that an unbalanced component is present, so
the exact semidirect-product description (7) applies. Set

```text
r_mod=(j_b c)^2.
```

Replacing the two copies of `j=j_gj_b` in `r_*=(jc)^2`
costs at most `2L(j_g)` in the bi-invariant projective metric.
Consequently

```text
L(r_mod) <= eta_*+2kappa
         <=49 eta_*+24 eta_v+24 eta_0.             (9)
```

The
[three-cycle projective trace lemma](wreath-three-cycle-projective-trace-gap-2026-09-08.md)
applies to (7), with `j_b` trivial in its `J` factor. It states
that every projective representation satisfies

```text
1-|tau(rho(c))|^2 <= (5/3)(1-|tau(rho(r_mod))|^2).
```

For clarity, its ordinary representation proof separates the
irreducible types of `J times N^3`. Non-invariant types have zero
trace on both cosets. An invariant type is `theta tensor pi^3`;
if `dim(pi)>=2`, both normalized trace magnitudes are at most
`1/dim(pi)^2<=1/4`. If `dim(pi)=1`, then `j_b` acts as a
scalar sign and `r_mod` acts exactly as `c^2`, even for an
arbitrary `theta`. Direct-sum weights yield the constant `5/3`.
Tensoring a projective representation with its conjugate gives
the displayed squared-trace inequality without multiplier
assumptions.

Thus `|tau(rho(r_mod))|^2<=(2+3a^2)/5`, giving
`L(r_mod)>=beta(a)`. Combining this with (9) proves (1a),
and its specialization at `a=0` gives (1)--(2).
When all components are unbalanced, `j_g=1`, so the argument
gives the stronger bound `eta_*>=beta(a)` directly.

## 5. Scope of the completed reduction

This proves a uniform scalar-HS gap for arbitrary projective
representations of the marked subgroup whenever the literal
coefficient-generated finite algebra is semisimple. It covers
all simple components, including unequal coordinate ranks,
cyclic permutation orbits, and the small special linear groups.
The whole ambient elementary group is never represented.

The remaining finite coefficient-generated algebras can have a
nonzero radical. Equation (4) identifies the corresponding
path-algebra radical, but it does not permit replacing `P` by
`P/J(P)` in a complex representation of `K`. Radical components
can couple the simple-factor summands used above. No factorization
of that representation through a semisimple quotient has been
proved or assumed.

Finally, this is still a theorem about literal finite coefficient
models. It does not assign such coefficients to arbitrary frozen
complex unitary tuples, and it does not decide hyperlinearity of
the Leavitt unit group.
