# Blackadar--Kirchberg: ideals with no compact open primitive subset are invisible to quasidiagonality (2026-09-12)

Lane `ex-blackadar-kirchberg`.  Sources read from the arXiv PDFs on 2026-09-12:

- J. Gabe, *Traceless AF embeddings and unsuspended E-theory*, arXiv:1804.08095v2
  (GAFA 30 (2020)), pp. 1--9: Theorem A, Corollaries B--E, Remark 1, Theorem 4
  (ideal-related O_2-embedding), Lemma 6, and the definition of "traceless".
- N. P. Brown, M. Dadarlat, *Extensions of quasidiagonal C\*-algebras and
  K-theory*, arXiv:math/0008182, pp. 4--14: Lemmas 2.1--2.2, Theorem 2.3,
  Definition 2.4, Proposition 2.5, Definition 3.1 with the remarks after it,
  Theorem 3.4, Propositions 4.1, 4.2, 4.6, Definitions 4.3--4.4.

  Theorem 1.3, Definitions 2.1--2.4, Remark 2.5.

Conventions.  All algebras are separable.  For a C\*-algebra `E` let `N(E)` be the
set of closed ideals `J` of `E` such that `Prim(J)` (an open subset of `Prim(E)`)
has no nonempty compact open subset.  By Gabe's Remark 1, compact open subsets of
`Prim(J)` correspond to nonzero compact elements of the ideal lattice below `J`.

## 1. Facts about N(E)

**Lemma 1.**  (a) `N(E)` is closed under passing to smaller ideals, under
extensions (if `J ∈ N(E)` and `L ⊇ J` with `L/J ∈ N(E/J)` then `L ∈ N(E)`), and
under closures of increasing unions.  So `N(E)` has maximal elements.
(b) Every `J ∈ N(E)` is stably projectionless, hence stably finite, and if `J` is
exact it is AF-embeddable (Gabe, Corollary B and the footnote to Corollary D).

*Proof.*  (a) Smaller ideals: a compact open subset of a smaller open set is
compact open in the larger one.  Extensions: let `W ⊆ Prim(L)` be compact open.
`Prim(L/J) = Prim(L) \ Prim(J)` is closed in `Prim(L)`.  So `W ∩ Prim(L/J)` is
compact, and it is open in `Prim(L/J)`, hence empty.  Then `W ⊆ Prim(J)` is compact
open there, hence empty.  Unions: `Prim` of the closure of `∪ J_α` is `∪ Prim(J_α)`.
A compact open `W` inside it is covered by finitely many members of the chain, so
it lies in one of them and is empty.  Zorn's lemma gives maximal elements.
(b) A nonzero projection `p` over `J` generates a compact ideal: if `p` lies in
the closure of an increasing union of ideals, functional calculus puts `p` in one
of them.  QED

## 2. Theorem 1 (extension permanence)

**Theorem 1.**  Let `0 -> I -> E -> B -> 0` be exact with `E` separable, `I` exact
and `B` nuclear and quasidiagonal.  If `I ∈ N(E)`, then `E` is quasidiagonal.

*Proof.*
Step 1 (a quasidiagonal hull with no KK-theory).  By Gabe, Theorem A
(iv) ⟹ (ii), there is an injective \*-homomorphism
`ρ : I -> D := C_0((0,1], O_2)`.  `D` is quasidiagonal (Voiculescu: cones are
quasidiagonal; quoted on p. 1 of Gabe).  Let `J` be the hereditary subalgebra of
`D` generated by `ρ(I)`.  By the remark after Brown--Dadarlat Definition 3.1,
`ρ : I -> J` is approximately unital.  `J` is separable and quasidiagonal as a
subalgebra of `D`.  Let `J'` be the closed ideal of `D` generated by `ρ(I)`; `J`
is a full hereditary subalgebra of `J'`.  Since `O_2` is simple and nuclear, the
ideals of `D = C_0((0,1]) ⊗ O_2` are `C_0(U) ⊗ O_2` for open `U ⊆ (0,1]`.  So
`J' ≅ C_0(U) ⊗ O_2`.

Step 2 (push the Busby invariant into the hull).  Let `γ : B -> Q(I)` be the Busby
invariant and `γ^s : K⊗B -> Q(K⊗I)` its stabilisation (BD Definition 2.4, with
`E ≅ E(γ) ↪ E(γ^s)`).  The map `id_K ⊗ ρ : K⊗I -> K⊗J` is approximately unital, so
by BD Definition 3.1 it induces `η : K⊗B -> Q(K⊗J)` with `E(γ^s) ↪ E(η)`.  Hence
`E ↪ E(η)`.

Step 3 (the Ext class vanishes).  `K⊗B` is separable and nuclear and `K⊗J` is
σ-unital.  Kasparov identifies the Brown--Dadarlat Ext group (BD p. 5: Busby
invariants modulo addition of trivial ones, a group by the absorption Theorem 2.3
and nuclearity) with `KK^1`.  So
`Ext(K⊗K⊗B, K⊗K⊗J) ≅ KK^1(B, J)` after stability of `KK`.  `J` is full and
σ-unital in `J'`, so Brown's stable isomorphism `K⊗J ≅ K⊗J'` gives
`KK^1(B, J) ≅ KK^1(B, C_0(U) ⊗ O_2)`.  `O_2` is in the bootstrap class with
`K_*(O_2) = 0`, so `1_(O_2) = 0` in `KK(O_2, O_2)`.  By the exterior product,
`1_(C_0(U)⊗O_2) = 0`, and `KK^1(B, C_0(U) ⊗ O_2) = 0`.  So `[η^s] = 0`.

Step 4.  In `0 -> K⊗J -> E(η) -> K⊗B -> 0` both ends are quasidiagonal, `K⊗B` is
separable and nuclear, `K⊗J` is σ-unital, and the stabilised Ext class vanishes.
BD Proposition 2.5 makes `E(η)` quasidiagonal, and `E` is quasidiagonal as a
subalgebra.  QED

No UCT, no trace hypothesis and no stable finiteness hypothesis enters.  Stable
finiteness of `E` is a consequence.  Theorem 1 contains BD Proposition 4.2 (the
ideal `SI` has `Prim(SI) = R × Prim(I)`, which has no compact open subset) and
Gabe's Corollary B (take `B = 0`).  It also covers cones, `C_0(X, A)` for
noncompact connected `X`, and non-Hausdorff examples such as Rørdam's `A_[0,1]`,
whose ideal lattice is `[0,1]`.

*Alternative Step 3.*  Using Gabe's Theorem 4 one can make `ρ` full in `D`.  Then
`J' = D` is zero-homotopic, and homotopy invariance of `Ext` in the second
variable suffices, exactly as in the proof of BD Proposition 4.2.

## 3. Consequences for STW Problem VII

**Corollary 2.**  Let `E` be separable and nuclear.
1. If `E/J` is quasidiagonal for some `J ∈ N(E)`, then `E` is quasidiagonal.  So
   Problem VII holds for every separable nuclear stably finite `E` having such a
   `J`.
2. If `E` is not quasidiagonal and `J` is maximal in `N(E)`, then `E/J` is not
   quasidiagonal, and every nonzero ideal of `E/J` contains a nonzero compact
   ideal of `E/J`.
3. (Gluing.)  Problem VII is equivalent to its special case of algebras
   `A = J_1 + J_2` with `J_1, J_2 ∈ N(A)`.

*Proof.*  (1) is Theorem 1 with `I = J`, which is exact as an ideal of a nuclear
algebra.  (2) Nonquasidiagonality of `E/J` is (1).  A nonzero ideal `L/J` of
`E/J` with `Prim(L/J)` free of compact open subsets would put `L ∈ N(E)` by
Lemma 1(a), contradicting maximality.  So `Prim(L/J)` contains a nonempty
compact open `W`.  `W` is open in `Prim(E/J)` because `Prim(L/J)` is, so it gives
a nonzero compact ideal inside `L/J`.
(3) If `B` is a counterexample, `A = C([0,1], B)` is separable, nuclear and stably
finite: an isometry over `C([0,1], B~)` is fibrewise an isometry, hence fibrewise
unitary.  It is not quasidiagonal, since it contains `B`.  And
`A = C_0([0,2/3), B) + C_0((1/3,1], B)`.  `Prim C_0(X, B) = X × Prim B`, and the
first projection is open and continuous.  So a compact open subset would project
to a nonempty compact open subset of the connected noncompact interval, which is
impossible.  QED

Caution on (2): `E/J` need not be stably finite.  In
`{f ∈ C([0,1], O_2) : f(0) ∈ C1}`, which is quasidiagonal, the ideal
`C_0((0,1), O_2) ∈ N` has quotient `C ⊕ O_2`.  So (2) is a normal form for
non-quasidiagonality, not for counterexamples.

Reading of (3): quasidiagonality of every member of an open cover of the primitive
ideal space, even by AF-embeddable ideals, carries no information beyond
Problem VII itself.  The difficulty is entirely in gluing.

## 4. Theorem 3 (radical criterion)

Let `R_qd(E) = ∩ {J : E/J quasidiagonal}`.  By
`stw07-separating-qd-quotients-force-quasidiagonality`, `E/R_qd(E)` is
quasidiagonal.

**Theorem 3.**  A separable nuclear `E` is quasidiagonal iff
`R_qd(E) ∈ N(E)`, i.e. iff `Prim(R_qd(E))` has no nonempty compact open subset.

*Proof.*  If `E` is quasidiagonal then `R_qd(E) = 0`.  Conversely,
`E/R_qd(E)` is quasidiagonal and nuclear, so Theorem 1 applies.  QED

**Corollary 4.**  If `E` is separable, nuclear, stably finite and not
quasidiagonal, then `R_qd(E)` contains a nonzero compact ideal `K` of `E`.  Every
such `K` carries a lower semicontinuous trace taking a value in `(0, ∞)`.

*Proof.*  By Theorem 3, `Prim(R_qd(E))` has a nonempty compact open subset `W`.
Its ideal `K` is compact in the ideal lattice of `R_qd(E)`, and hence in that of
`E`, because ideal intersection distributes over sums.  `K` is separable,
nuclear, and stably finite as an ideal of `E`.  If `K` were traceless, Gabe's
Corollary C ((iii) ⟹ (iv)) would say that `Prim(K) = W` has no nonempty compact
open subset, which is false.  QED

In the stable-character normal form `E = (A ⊗ K)~` of
`stw07-bk-reduces-to-stable-character-kernels`, `E/(A⊗K) = C` is quasidiagonal,
so `R_qd(E) ⊆ A ⊗ K`, and the traced compact ideal of Corollary 4 lies in the stable
kernel.

## 5. Model tests and boundaries

- Trivial model failing the hypothesis: the Toeplitz algebra,
  `0 -> K -> T -> C(T) -> 0`.  `Prim(K)` is a point, compact open; `T` is not
  quasidiagonal.  Consistent.
- Real object satisfying it: `0 -> C_0(R^2) ⊗ K -> E -> C(T) -> 0` with index
  `1 ∈ K_0(C_0(R^2)) = Z`.  Theorem 1 makes `E` quasidiagonal although the index
  map is nonzero, so this is outside BD Theorem 3.4.  The UCT case already
  follows from BD Proposition 4.6, since `ρ_*` kills all of `K_0`; Theorem 1 drops
  the UCT on the quotient.
- What Theorem 1 does not reach: an ideal with a compact piece.  By Corollary 4
  such a piece of a counterexample carries a nontrivial trace, so every index
  obstruction producing a counterexample is supported on traced compact ideals.
  This is where `stw07-qd-trace-positive-class-kills-qd-extension` (a
  nonquasidiagonal trace) and the trace-invisible residual
  `every-separable-nuclear-qd-algebra-has-k0-hahn-banach` sit.

## 6. Literature boundary

Moutzouris (2022, Introduction) lists the known cases of the conjecture as
simple UCT algebras, traceless algebras (Gabe, Cor. C), and crossed products of
real rank zero AH algebras by Z.  The extension cases listed there are
Brown--Dadarlat's and Moutzouris' own class Y.  Brown--Dadarlat Proposition 4.2
treats suspension ideals.  Gabe's paper does not discuss extensions.  One web
search on 2026-09-12 for extension permanence of quasidiagonality for ideals
without compact open primitive subsets found nothing further.  This check is
bounded; Theorem 1 is a short combination of Gabe Theorem A with the
Brown--Dadarlat machinery, and experts may regard it as known.
