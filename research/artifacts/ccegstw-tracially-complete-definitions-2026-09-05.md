## The definitions

**Definition 3.4.**

> A tracially complete C\*-algebra is a pair `(ℳ, X)` where `ℳ` is a
> C\*-algebra and `X ⊆ T(ℳ)` is a compact convex set such that
> (i) `X` is a faithful set of traces on `ℳ`, and
> (ii) the unit ball of `ℳ` is `∥·∥_{2,X}`-complete.

**Definition 3.13.**

> A tracially complete C\*-algebra `(ℳ, X)` is said to be *factorial* if `X` is
> a closed face of `T(ℳ)`.

**Proposition 3.14.**

> A tracially complete C\*-algebra `(ℳ, X)` is factorial if and only if
> `π_τ(ℳ)''` is a factor for every extreme point `τ` of `X`.

**Proposition 3.15.**

> If `(ℳ, X)` is a tracially complete C\*-algebra, then every
> `∥·∥_{2,X}`-continuous trace on `ℳ` belongs to the closed face of `T(ℳ)`
> generated by `X`.  In particular, if `(ℳ, X)` is factorial, then `X` is
> precisely the set of all `∥·∥_{2,X}`-continuous traces on `ℳ`.

**Proposition 3.23(iv).**

> The completion `(Ā^X, X̃)` is factorial if and only if `X` is a face in
> `T(A)`.

**Question 1.1 (the trace problem; = STW Problem XXII).**

> Let `(ℳ, X)` be a factorial tracially complete C\*-algebra.  Are all traces
> on `ℳ` automatically `∥·∥_{2,X}`-continuous?  Equivalently, is the inclusion
> `X ⊆ T(ℳ)` an equality?

**§3.2, on which examples are factorial.**

> The examples of greatest interest --- uniform tracial completions of
> C\*-algebras --- are automatically factorial.

**§1.1, the uniform two-norm.**  For a trace `τ`, `∥a∥_{2,τ} = τ(a*a)^{1/2}`,
and for a set `X` of traces `∥a∥_{2,X} = sup_{τ ∈ X} ∥a∥_{2,τ}`.

## The one consequence this repository needs (not a quotation)

For a uniform tracial completion the designated set is `X = T(A)`, which is
trivially a face of `T(A)`.  So Proposition 3.23(iv) makes **factoriality a
theorem, not a hypothesis to be assumed**, and the paper says as much in the
§3.2 sentence quoted above.

In particular the expensive route --- Bauer simplex, centre computation,
`W*`-bundle with factor fibres, Propositions 3.6 and 3.14 --- is not needed to
establish the hypothesis of Problem XXII for such a pair.  The cheap route is
the forward half of 3.23(iv): if a proper convex combination of two traces is
`∥·∥_{2,X}`-continuous then each summand is dominated by a multiple of it,
hence is itself `∥·∥_{2,X}`-continuous, hence --- by density of `A` in `Ā^X` ---
is the canonical extension of its own restriction, hence lies in `X`.
