# Cluster entropy functionals certify nothing on one-ended groups

Lane a-gs-pull-7 (swarm-0917, role obstruction-miner), 2026-09-17. Target hole:
`bernoulli-rokhlin-maximal-on-fg-simple-kazhdan-groups`.

## 0. What this kills, and why it matters

The only lower bound for Rokhlin entropy on the target's dead list that uses no finite
models is Bowen's f-invariant. The graph recorded the reason it fails on other groups,
"its invariance needs a tree Cayley graph" (Attempts, *Tree mechanisms (gk-p-inf-f)*; artifact
`rokhlin-lower-bounds-without-finite-models-2026-09-12.md`, Section 2.3). That was a
mechanism analysis, not a theorem. It left open the whole family of Bethe or Kikuchi
(cluster-variation) relatives: finite linear combinations of joint entropies of translates,
with weights adapted to the relations of the group.

This artifact proves that this family is dead on every one-ended finitely generated group,
and hence on every infinite group with property (T), including all hosts of the target. The
invariant is a combinatorial valuation `psi` on finite subsets of `G`, and the step where every
member dies is the **fat digon**. On the Bernoulli coordinate partitions, splitting
monotonicity makes `psi` non-increasing on connected sets. The exact inclusion–exclusion
identity for `psi` on a fat digon then forces `psi <= 0` on large balls. So the certified
bound on **every** Bernoulli shift, whatever its base, is `<= 0`, not merely below `log|A|`.

## 1. Setting

Let `G` be generated by a finite symmetric set `S`. Write `|g|` for the word length and
`d(g,h) = |g^-1 h|` (the left-invariant word metric; `g` and `gs` are adjacent for `s in S`).
"Connected" means connected in this Cayley graph.

Let `G` act on the full shift by `(g.x)(h) = x(hg)`. For finite `D ⊆ G` let `Q_D` be the
partition by `x|_D`. Then `g^-1 Q_D = Q_{Dg}` and `Q_D v Q_E = Q_{D u E}`.

**Definition (cluster functional).** A cluster functional is a finite family of pairs
`(W, c_W)`, with `W ⊆ G` finite and `c_W` real. For a finite measurable partition `Q` of a
p.m.p. `G`-space it is

    F(Q) = sum_W c_W H( join_{w in W} w^-1 Q ).

Conditional entropies and mutual informations of translates are differences of such terms,
so they are included. Bowen's functional on `F_r` is the case `W = {e}` with coefficient
`1 - 2r`, together with `W = {e, s_i}`, coefficient `1`, for `i = 1..r`. Its radius is

    R(F) = max { |w' w^-1| : w, w' in the same W }.

**Mechanism axioms.** The f-invariant route uses the following on the uniform Bernoulli shift
`(A^G, u^G)` (Bowen 2010, and Section 2 of
`free-group-f-invariant-bernoulli-preservation-2026-09-12.md`):

- **(M1) splitting monotonicity:** `F(beta v t^-1 gamma) <= F(beta)` for every partition `beta`,
  every `gamma <= beta` and every `t in S`;
- **(M2) Shannon bound:** `F(Q) <= H(Q)` for generating `Q`;
- **(M3) certification:** `lim inf_n F(Q_{B_n}) >= log|A|`, where `B_n` is the ball of radius `n`
  at `e`.

The lower bound `h^Rok >= log|A|` is obtained as `log|A| <= f = inf F(P^n) <= F(P) <= H(P)`.

## 2. The valuation

For the Bernoulli shift with base law `p` (any `p`), the coordinates are independent, and
`join_{w in W} w^-1 Q_B = Q_{BW}`. So

    F(Q_B) = H(p) * psi(B),     psi(B) := sum_W c_W |B W|.                         (2.1)

Also `psi(gB) = psi(B)`.

**Lemma 2.2 (exact valuation identity).** Let `B_1, B_2` be finite, put `C = B_1 ∩ B_2`, and
suppose `d(B_1 \ C, B_2 \ C) > R(F)`. Then

    psi(B_1 u B_2) = psi(B_1) + psi(B_2) - psi(C).

*Proof.* For each `W` we have `(B_1 u B_2)W = B_1W u B_2W`, so
`|(B_1 u B_2)W| = |B_1W| + |B_2W| - |B_1W ∩ B_2W|`. It suffices to show
`B_1W ∩ B_2W = CW`.

- `⊇` is clear.
- Take `g = b_1 w = b_2 w'` with `b_i in B_i` and `w, w' in W`. If `b_1 in C` or `b_2 in C`,
  then `g in CW`.
- Otherwise `b_1 in B_1 \ C` and `b_2 in B_2 \ C`, and `d(b_2, b_1) = |w' w^-1| <= R(F)`. This
  contradicts the hypothesis.

Multiply by `c_W` and sum. ∎

In particular (take `C = ∅` and `psi(∅) = 0`): if `d(C', C'') > R` then
`psi(C' u C'') = psi(C') + psi(C'')`.

**Lemma 2.3 (monotone growth).** Assume (M1). Let `D ⊆ D'` be finite with `D'` connected and
`D` nonempty. Then `psi(D') <= psi(D)`.

*Proof.* List `D' \ D` so that each new element is adjacent to an earlier element or to `D`.
This is possible by breadth-first search in `D'` from `D`. Each step adds `dt` with `d` in the
current set `E` and `t in S`. Apply (M1) with `beta = Q_E` and `gamma = Q_{{d}} <= Q_E`:
`beta v t^-1 gamma = Q_{E u {dt}}`, so `psi(E u {dt}) <= psi(E)` by (2.1), when `H(p) > 0`. ∎

## 3. Fat digons

**Definition.** An `R`-fat digon is a quadruple `(B_1, B_2, C', C'')` of finite connected sets
with:

- `B_1 ∩ B_2 = C' ⊔ C''`;
- `C', C'' ⊆ B_1 ∩ B_2`;
- `d(C', C'') > R`;
- `d(B_1 \ C, B_2 \ C) > R`, where `C = C' u C''`.

**Proposition 3.1 (the kill).** Assume (M1). If `G` has an `R(F)`-fat digon, then on every
Bernoulli shift with `H(p) > 0` and every connected finite `D` containing a translate of
`B_1 u B_2`,

    F(Q_D) <= 0.

In particular `lim_n F(Q_{B_n}) <= 0` along balls, and along every exhaustion of `G` by
connected finite sets.

*Proof.*

1. Lemma 2.2 gives `psi(B_1 u B_2) = psi(B_1) + psi(B_2) - psi(C)`.
2. Lemma 2.2 again, with empty intersection, gives `psi(C) = psi(C') + psi(C'')`.
3. Lemma 2.3 with `C' ⊆ B_1` and `C'' ⊆ B_2` gives `psi(B_1) <= psi(C')` and
   `psi(B_2) <= psi(C'')`.
4. Hence `psi(B_1 u B_2) <= 0`. The union is connected because `C ≠ ∅` joins `B_1` and `B_2`.
5. Lemma 2.3 gives `psi(D) <= psi(B_1 u B_2) <= 0`. By left invariance a translate is enough.
∎

So (M1) and (M3) are incompatible, since `0 < log|A|`. (M2) is not even needed.

**Proposition 3.2 (fat digons exist on one-ended groups).** Let `G` be finitely generated
and one-ended: for every finite `K ⊆ G`, the Cayley graph minus `K` has exactly one infinite
component. Then `G` has an `R`-fat digon for every `R >= 0`.

*Proof.*

- **A bi-infinite geodesic.** `G` is infinite, so for each `n` there is a geodesic of length
  `2n`. Translate it so that its midpoint is `e`. The ball `B_n` is finite, so König's lemma
  (a diagonal choice of initial segments) gives a bi-infinite geodesic `l : Z -> G` with
  `l(0) = e`: every finite subpath of `l` is a subpath of a geodesic, hence geodesic.
- **The detour.** Put `Delta = 2R + 4`, `L = Delta + 1` and `a = R + 2 = Delta/2`. The rays
  `l[L, ∞)` and `l(-∞, -L]` are infinite connected sets that avoid `B_Delta`. By one-endedness
  they lie in the same component of the complement, so there is a path `pi` from `l(-L)` to
  `l(L)` avoiding `B_Delta`.
- **The sets.** Put `sigma = l[-L, L]`, a geodesic with midpoint `e`, and

      A_1 = l[-a, a],   C' = l[-a-R-1, -a-1],   C'' = l[a+1, a+R+1],
      B_1 = l[-a-R-1, a+R+1],
      B_2 = pi u l[-L, -a-1] u l[a+1, L].

Now check the conditions.

- `B_1` is a geodesic segment, so it is connected. `B_2` is connected: `pi` joins the two
  outer segments at `l(±L)`. `C'` and `C''` are segments.
- `a + R + 1 = 2R + 3 < Delta`, so `B_1 ⊆ B_Delta` and `pi ∩ B_1 = ∅`. Also
  `a + R + 1 <= L - 1`, so `C' ⊆ l[-L, -a-1]` and `C'' ⊆ l[a+1, L]`. Hence
  `B_1 ∩ B_2 = C' ⊔ C''`.
- `d(C', C'') = 2a + 2 > R`, because `l` is geodesic.
- `B_1 \ C = A_1`. The set `B_2 \ C` consists of `pi` and the segments `l[-L, -a-R-2]` and
  `l[a+R+2, L]`.
  - The distance from `A_1` to these segments is at least `R + 2`, because `l` is geodesic.
  - The distance from `A_1` to `pi` is at least `Delta - a = R + 2`, because `A_1 ⊆ B_a` and
    `pi` avoids `B_Delta`.

So `d(B_1 \ C, B_2 \ C) > R`. ∎

**Theorem 3.3.** Let `G` be a finitely generated one-ended group and `F` any cluster functional
satisfying (M1). Then `lim sup_n F(Q_{B_n}) <= 0` on every Bernoulli shift over `G`. No
cluster functional satisfies (M1) and (M3) over `G`.

**Corollary 3.4.** This holds for every infinite group with property (T), and so for every
host of `bernoulli-rokhlin-maximal-on-fg-simple-kazhdan-groups`.

- Property (T) implies FA (`property-t-implies-property-fa`).
- A finitely generated group with more than one end acts on a tree without a global fixed point
  (Stallings, imported as `several-ended-groups-act-fixed-point-freely-on-trees`).
- An infinite finitely generated group has at least one end.

## 4. Calibration and sanity checks

- **Free groups.** There is no fat digon: in a tree, any two connected sets meeting in two far
  pieces must contain the geodesic between them in both, so the two pieces are joined inside
  `B_1 ∩ B_2`. Bowen's `psi(B) = |B| - #edges(B)` is the Euler characteristic of the induced
  subgraph, which equals `1` on subtrees. This agrees with Bowen's theorem, and the mechanism
  survives exactly where the proof of Proposition 3.2 fails.
- **Z (two-ended).** `psi(interval) = 1` for `F(Q) = H(Q v sQ) - H(Q)`. No digon exists.
- **Z^2 (one-ended, amenable).** Bowen's `r = 2` functional gives `psi(B) = |B| - #edges`. It is
  monotone on the Bernoulli coordinate partitions, it equals `0` on the explicit digon union, and
  it tends to `-∞` on balls (`-59` at L1-radius 6).
- **Script.** The valuation identity and the digon hypotheses were checked on random functionals
  over `Z^2`, and the subtree identity on `F_2`. See
  `experiments/cluster-entropy-functionals-2026-09-17/check_cluster_valuation.py` (exit 0,
  output: all checks pass).

## 5. The gate: what a surviving local mechanism must change

Every member of the class dies at step 4 of Proposition 3.1, whatever its windows and weights.
The proof uses only four things:

- (a) `F` is **linear** in joint entropies of translates of `Q`;
- (b) its **radius is bounded**, fixed before the digon is chosen;
- (c) (M1) holds for single-coordinate splittings of Bernoulli coordinate partitions;
- (d) `G` is one-ended.

A mechanism escaping it must drop one of (a)–(c). Taking a supremum of cluster certificates does
not help, since each one is `<= 0`.

- **Dropping (a):** a nonlinear function of the window law. Lemma 2.2 is the only place
  linearity enters. With (M1)–(M3), such a functional must equal `log|A|` on every connected
  Bernoulli coordinate partition, and nothing in this proof forbids that. So nonlinear local
  functionals escape the obstruction. They must violate inclusion–exclusion on separated
  pieces: on the digon, `F(Q_{C' u C''}) != F(Q_{C'}) + F(Q_{C''})`, or the identity of
  Lemma 2.2 must fail. Entropy-like functionals of independent pieces are additive, so this is a
  strong constraint, but it is not a proof that the class is empty.
- **Dropping (b):** radius growing with the partition, as in Kolmogorov–Sinai-type limits.
  Invariance then has to come from somewhere other than local splitting, and on nonamenable
  one-ended hosts no such source is known (the subgroup-induction and co-amenability collapse in
  the target's Attempts).
- **Dropping (c):** invariance without monotone certification. Then (M2) has to be proved for
  every generating partition separately, which is the original problem.

By Manning's bottleneck criterion (mentioned, not imported), a geodesic space is
quasi-isometric to a tree exactly when the configuration of Proposition 3.2 is absent for
large `Delta`. So the natural expected converse is that cluster certificates exist only over
virtually free groups, where the f-invariant already works.
