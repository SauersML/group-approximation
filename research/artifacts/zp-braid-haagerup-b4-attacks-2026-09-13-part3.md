## 1. Theorem F: the induction step reduces to Brunnian braids

For `n ≥ 2` and `1 ≤ i ≤ n`, let `d_i: P_n → P_{n−1}` delete strand `i`. The Brunnian subgroup is
`Brun_n = ∩_i ker d_i`. It is normal, and free, since it lies in `ker d_n ≅ F_{n−1}` (Artin combing). It is
infinite for `n ≥ 3`: `[A_{13}, A_{23}] ∈ Brun_3`, and `[[A_{14}, A_{24}], A_{34}] ∈ Brun_4` is a nontrivial
commutator of free basis elements of `ker d_4`, which becomes trivial when any strand is deleted.

**Theorem F.** Suppose `B_{n−1}` has the Haagerup property. Then `B_n` has it iff `P_n` admits a conditionally
negative definite function whose restriction to `Brun_n` is proper.

*Proof.* (⇒) Restrict a proper cnd function of `B_n`.

(⇐) Let `ψ_{n−1}` be a proper cnd function on `P_{n−1}` (by (H1)), and put `ψ_0 = Σ_i ψ_{n−1} ∘ d_i`, which is
cnd on `P_n`. Let `ψ_K` be cnd on `P_n` and proper on `Brun_n`, and set `ψ = ψ_0 + ψ_K`. Fix `R`.

- If `ψ(g) ≤ R`, each `d_i(g)` lies in a finite set, so the image of `g` under `D = (d_1, …, d_n)` lies in a
  finite set. Since `ker D = Brun_n`, `g` lies in finitely many cosets `g_1 Brun_n, …, g_M Brun_n`.
- Write `ψ_K = ‖b‖²` for an affine isometric action with cocycle `b`. From `b(xy) = b(x) + π(x) b(y)`,
  `√ψ_K(xy) ≤ √ψ_K(x) + √ψ_K(y)`. So for `g = g_j k` with `k ∈ Brun_n`,
  `√ψ_K(k) ≤ √ψ_K(g_j^{-1}) + √ψ_K(g) ≤ C + √R`. Properness on `Brun_n` leaves finitely many `k`.

So `ψ` is proper, `P_n` has the Haagerup property, and so does `B_n` by (H3). ∎

**For `n = 4`.** `B_3` has the property (part 1), so `B_4` has the Haagerup property iff `P_4` admits a cnd
function proper on the free normal subgroup `Brun_4 ≤ ker d_4 ≅ F_3`. The same functions `ψ_0` show that
`(P_4, ker d_4)` has no relative property (T), and that no relative (T) set can escape outside finitely many
`Brun_4`-cosets. A negative answer at `n = 4` is exactly the statement that no cnd function is proper on
`Brun_4`.

## 2. Equivalent forms of the smallest case

The following have the Haagerup property simultaneously:

- `B_4`;
- `P_4 ≅ Z × PMod(S_{0,5})` (Proposition A);
- `PMod(S_{0,5}) ≅ F_3 ⋊ F_2` (Birman exact sequence with free quotient `PMod(S_{0,4})`);
- `B_4 / Z(B_4)`;
- the preimage in `B_4 / Z(B_4)` of a torsion-free finite-index `Λ ≤ SL_2(Z)`. This preimage is a free-by-free
  group `F_2 ⋊ Λ`, for the following classical reason. The map `B_4 → B_3`, `σ_3 ↦ σ_1`, has free kernel of rank
  2 generated by `σ_1σ_3^{-1}` and `σ_2σ_1σ_3^{-1}σ_2^{-1}`; it sends `Δ_4² = (σ_1σ_2σ_3)^4` to `Δ_3^4`; and
  `B_3/⟨Δ_3^4⟩ ≅ SL_2(Z)`. So `B_4/Z(B_4)` is an extension of `SL_2(Z)` by `F_2`. Dyer–Formanek–Grossman (1982)
  identify it with an index-2 subgroup of `Aut(F_2)`; that is a pointer, not re-verified here.

Picture, assuming the outer action of `Λ` on `F_2` is the natural one (as the Dyer–Formanek–Grossman
identification gives): for `Λ` torsion-free, `F_2 ⋊ Λ` is the fundamental group of the universal elliptic curve
over the open modular curve `Λ \ H` with the zero section removed. An extension of a free group by the
center-free `F_2` is determined by its outer action. Keeping the zero section gives `Z^2 ⋊ Λ`, the Burger
pair, which fails the Haagerup property. So the question for `B_4` is whether removing the zero section
destroys relative property (T).

## 3. Attack: products of trees from forgetful maps (where it stops)

The five maps `PMod(S_{0,5}) → PMod(S_{0,4}) ≅ F_2` forgetting one puncture give an action on a product of five
trees, a CAT(0) cube complex, with cnd displacement function `ψ_0`. It is proper exactly modulo the joint kernel.

Inside the point-pushing subgroup `π_1(S_{0,4}, p_5)`, forgetting another puncture `p_i` restricts to the push map
of the sphere with `p_i` filled in, which is injective on `π_1` of the thrice-punctured sphere. So the joint
kernel is the group of Brunnian loops `∩_{i ≤ 4} ker(π_1(S_{0,4}) → π_1(S_{0,4} ∪ {p_i}))`: free of infinite
rank and normal. This matches Theorem F with the center split off.

No other quotient of `PMod(S_{0,5})` onto a virtually free group is known to this lane that sees the Brunnian
loops. Any tree action on which `Brun_4` acts unboundedly would have to come from a splitting that is not a
forgetful map.

## 4. Attack: complex hyperbolic monodromy (lead, not worked out)

`π_1(M_{0,5}) = PMod(S_{0,5})`. For cone-angle parameters `α` (Thurston, *Shapes of polyhedra*; Deligne–Mostow)
the moduli space carries a complex hyperbolic structure with holonomy `ρ_α: PMod(S_{0,5}) → PU(2,1)`.
Distance in complex hyperbolic space is a conditionally negative definite kernel (Faraut–Harzallah), so
`g ↦ d(o, ρ_α(g) o)` is cnd on `PMod(S_{0,5})`.

**Where it dies for finitely many `α`.** When the holonomy is a lattice, Dehn twists about collision curves
have finite-order images. So `ker ρ_α` contains powers `T_c^m`, and commutators `[k, T_c^m]` with `k ∈ Brun_4`
lie in `Brun_4 ∩ ker ρ_α`. For finitely many `α`, a common power `m` shows that `Brun_4` meets the joint kernel
in an infinite subgroup, so no finite sum is proper on `Brun_4`.

**Open.** Can an infinite family `α_j`, with suitable weights `w_j`, give a convergent sum that is proper on
`Brun_4`? This needs growth estimates for `d(o, ρ_{α_j}(g) o)` along `Brun_4` that are uniform in `j`.

## 5. Attack: cubulation through the Sanov subgroup (where it stops)

Take `Λ = ⟨[[1,2],[0,1]], [[1,0],[2,1]]⟩`, free of rank 2 and finite index in `SL_2(Z)`. Its preimage
`F_2 ⋊ Λ ≤ B_4/Z(B_4)` is the amalgam `(F_2 ⋊ ⟨ã⟩) *_{F_2} (F_2 ⋊ ⟨b̃⟩)` of two punctured-torus mapping tori whose
monodromies lift squares of Dehn twists about curves meeting once. Each factor is a graph-manifold group with
boundary, hence virtually special by Przytycki–Wise (2014, *Graph manifolds with boundary are virtually special*;
reported, not re-verified here), and so a-T-menable.

**Where it stops.** The edge group `F_2` is normal and infinite-index in both factors, hence neither quasiconvex
nor malnormal. Combination theorems for cubulations (Hsu–Wise type) need quasiconvex, malnormal-type edge
groups, so they do not apply. A Sageev construction would need codimension-1 subgroups separating `Brun_4`
(equivalently, walls in the fiber direction that survive both monodromies). The natural tori `⟨x, ã⟩ ≅ Z^2`
(the twist curve times the monodromy) are moved by `b̃`, so they do not form a family of walls invariant under
the whole group.

## 6. Negative side (where Burger's argument stops)

- Burger's relative (T) for `(Z^2 ⋊ Γ, Z^2)` uses the spectral measure of a representation restricted to `Z^2`,
  living on the Pontryagin dual torus. For a non-abelian normal subgroup such as `F_2` or `Brun_4` there is no
  such spectral picture, and relative (T) does not lift from the quotient `(B_4/Z)/[F_2, F_2]`, which is
  `Z^2`-by-`SL_2(Z)` and does have relative (T).
- `(B_4/Z(B_4), F_2)` has no relative (T). `u = σ_1σ_3^{-1}` lies in this `F_2`, and `u² = σ_1²σ_3^{−2} ∈ P_4`
  maps to `A_{12}` under `d_4`, a non-central element of `P_3 = F_2 × Z`. The tree function on `P_3/Z` is
  unbounded on its powers; induce it to `B_4/Z` by (H3).
- So any relative (T) set in `B_4` must escape within finitely many `Brun_4`-cosets (Theorem F).
- Structural hint only: Barré–Pichot (arXiv:0809.0645) show `B_4` has property RD and exponential mesoscopic
  rank for its action on Brady's complex. This implies nothing about the Haagerup property.
