# The Pimsner--Toeplitz algebra of the Dixmier--Douady module is properly infinite

Lane `ex-k1-properly-infinite`, 2026-09-12. Complete proof below. It has not been externally
reviewed. It does not decide STW Problem LXI.

## 0. The question and the literature check

E. Blanchard, *Continuous families of properly infinite C\*-algebras*, arXiv:1507.02535v1
(9 Jul 2015), and *Continuous fields of properly infinite C\*-algebras*, arXiv:1312.7722v7
(15 Apr 2015), both end with (read from the PDFs, page 9 and page 8):

> Question 5.4. The Pimsner-Toeplitz algebra T(E_DD) is locally purely infinite since all its
> simple quotients are isomorphic to the Cuntz algebra O_infty. But is T(E_DD) properly infinite?

The 1507.02535v1 version also asks, in Remark 5.3 a'): "If H~ is the Hilbert C(Y)-module
considered in (4.6), is the Pimsner-Toeplitz algebra T(H~) properly infinite?"

Why this matters for LXI: by Blanchard--Rohde--Rordam, J. Noncommut. Geom. 2 (2008), Theorem 5.5
(i)<=>(ii), LXI is equivalent to "every unital C(X)-algebra with properly infinite fibres is
properly infinite". Blanchard's Section 5 proposes Pimsner--Toeplitz algebras of Hilbert modules
with infinite-dimensional fibres over infinite-dimensional compact spaces as the test case: they are
C(X)-algebras with fibres O_infty, and E_DD is the classical module all of whose sections vanish
somewhere. A non-properly-infinite T(E) would refute LXI.

Bounded check for later answers: two web searches (2026-09-12) and the arXiv listings of both
preprints found no answer. The Rohde thesis (Odense 2009) and BRR predate the question.

## 1. Setup, verbatim from Blanchard (1507.02535v1, proof of Proposition 4.3)

- `B_infty = {x in l^2(N) : sum_p |x_p|^2 <= 1}` with the metric `d(x,y) = sum_p 2^(-p-2)|x_p - y_p|`
  (a compact metric space, the "complex Hilbert cube").
- `eta(x) = x (+) sqrt(1 - ||x||^2)` in `l^2(N) (+) C`.
- `F'' = [C(B_infty, l^2(N) (+) 0) + C(B_infty) . eta]`, a closed Hilbert C(B_infty)-submodule of
  `l^infty(B_infty, l^2(N) (+) C)` with pointwise inner product. Note `<eta,eta> = 1`.
- `theta_{eta,eta}(zeta) = eta <eta, zeta>`, a projection in `L(F'')`, and
  `E_DD = (1 - theta_{eta,eta}) F''`.

Pimsner--Toeplitz algebra (Blanchard Definition 5.1): for a full Hilbert C(X)-module E, `F(E) =
(+)_{m >= 0} E^{(x) m}` with `E^{(x)0} = C(X)`, and `T(E)` is the unital C\*-subalgebra of `L(F(E))`
generated by the creation operators `l(zeta)`, `l(zeta)(z_1 (x) ... (x) z_k) = zeta (x) z_1 (x) ... (x) z_k`.

Conventions: right modules, inner products C(X)-linear in the second variable. Tensor products are
internal over C(X), where C(X) acts by multiplication, so
`<a (x) b, c (x) d> = <b, <a,c> d> = <a,c> <b,d>` (C(X) is commutative).

## 2. Lemma 1 (orthonormal tensor sections give isometries)

**Lemma 1.** Let E be a full Hilbert C(X)-module and k >= 1. Suppose `theta_1, theta_2` lie in the
linear span of elementary tensors `z_1 (x) ... (x) z_k` (z_i in E) and satisfy
`<theta_i, theta_j> = delta_ij 1` in C(X). Then `T(E)` is properly infinite.

*Proof.* For an elementary tensor put `l_k(z_1 (x) ... (x) z_k) = l(z_1) ... l(z_k)`, which is in `T(E)`.
It acts by `omega |-> z_1 (x) ... (x) z_k (x) omega`, so extending linearly, `l_k(theta)` acts by
`omega |-> theta (x) omega` and lies in `T(E)`. For `theta, theta'` in the span and `omega, omega'` in
`F(E)`,
`<l_k(theta) omega, l_k(theta') omega'> = <theta (x) omega, theta' (x) omega'> = <omega, <theta,theta'> omega'>`.
So `l_k(theta)^* l_k(theta') = <theta,theta'>` acting by multiplication. With `s_i = l_k(theta_i)` we get
`s_i^* s_j = delta_ij 1`. So `T(E)` contains two isometries with orthogonal ranges. Square.

## 3. Proposition 2 (finite deficiency)

**Proposition 2.** Let F be a Hilbert C(X)-module, X compact. Let `n >= 1` and `N = 3n + 1`.
Suppose F contains orthonormal sections `e_1, ..., e_{2N}` (i.e. `<e_k, e_l> = delta_kl`) and
orthonormal sections `eta_1, ..., eta_n`. Put `Q = sum_j theta_{eta_j, eta_j}`, `P = 1 - Q`, `E = PF`.
Then `E (x) E` contains `theta_1, theta_2` as in Lemma 1. So `T(E)` is properly infinite.

*Proof.* Put `zeta_k = P e_k = e_k - sum_j eta_j <eta_j, e_k>` in E. Then
`<zeta_k, zeta_l> = <e_k, P e_l> = delta_kl - M_kl`, where `M_kl = sum_j <e_k, eta_j><eta_j, e_l>`.

Fix x in X and evaluate every function at x. Let B be the `2N x n` matrix `B_kj = <e_k, eta_j>(x)`.
Then `M(x) = B B^*`. Bessel's inequality in the module, `<eta - sum_k e_k<e_k,eta>, same> >= 0`, gives
`sum_k |<e_k, eta_j>|^2 <= <eta_j, eta_j> = 1` for each j. More precisely `B^* B` is the Gram matrix
of the components of `eta_1(x), ..., eta_n(x)` along `span{e_k(x)}`, so `0 <= B^*B <= 1`. Hence
`0 <= M(x) <= 1` and `tr M(x) = tr B^*B <= n`.

Let `I = {1..N}`, `J = {N+1..2N}`, `V = sum_{k in I} zeta_k (x) zeta_k`, `W = sum_{l in J} zeta_l (x) zeta_l`.
By the tensor inner product formula,
`<V,V>(x) = sum_{k,l in I} (delta_kl - M_kl)^2 = N - 2 sum_{k in I} M_kk + sum_{k,l in I} M_kl^2`.
The last sum is `tr(M_I conj(M_I))` for the positive matrix `M_I = [M_kl]_{k,l in I}`; since
`conj(M_I) >= 0`, `tr(M_I conj(M_I)) = tr(M_I^{1/2} conj(M_I) M_I^{1/2}) >= 0`. Also
`sum_{k in I} M_kk <= tr M <= n`. So `<V,V> >= N - 2n = n + 1` everywhere, and likewise `<W,W> >= n + 1`.

For the cross term, the Kronecker deltas vanish off the diagonal blocks, so
`|<V,W>(x)| = |sum_{k in I, l in J} M_kl^2| <= sum_{k,l} |M_kl|^2 = tr(M^2) <= tr M <= n`.

Gram--Schmidt with continuous functions: `v = <V,V>`, `theta_1 = V v^(-1/2)`,
`W' = W - V (v^(-1) <V,W>)`, `w = <W',W'> = <W,W> - |<V,W>|^2 / v >= (n+1) - n^2/(n+1) > 0`,
`theta_2 = W' w^(-1/2)`. Each is a finite sum of elementary tensors `zeta (x) (zeta' f)` with f in C(X),
and `<theta_i, theta_j> = delta_ij`. Square.

For n = 1 the exact computation is sharper: `<V,V> = N - 2 beta + |sigma|^2` with
`beta = sum_{k in I} |<e_k,eta>|^2 <= 1`, `sigma = sum_{k in I} <e_k,eta>^2`, so N = 3 already gives
`<V,V> >= 1`. The proposition does not need this.

## 4. Theorem 3 (Blanchard's two test algebras)

**Theorem 3.** (a) `T(E_DD)` is properly infinite. (b) `T(H~)` is properly infinite, for the
Hilbert C(Y)-module `H~ = C(Y) (+) E_DD (x) C((B_3)^infty) (+) C(B_infty) (x) [f~ . l^2(N, C(B_3^infty)^2)]`
of 1507.02535v1, (4.6), over `Y = B_infty x (B_3)^infty`.

*Proof.* (a) Apply Proposition 2 with `F = F''`, `n = 1`, `eta_1 = eta`, and `e_k` the constant
sections `e_k (+) 0` for k = 1..8. These are orthonormal in F'' because the inner product is pointwise.
Then `E = (1 - theta_{eta,eta}) F'' = E_DD`, and Lemma 1 applies.

(b) The summand `E' = E_DD (x) C((B_3)^infty)` is the pull-back of E_DD along the coordinate
projection `Y -> B_infty`. It equals `(1 - theta_{eta',eta'}) F'''` with `F''' = F'' (x) C((B_3)^infty)`,
`eta' = eta (x) 1`, and constant sections `e_k (x) 1`, all orthonormal. Proposition 2 gives orthonormal
`theta_1, theta_2` in `E' (x) E'`, which is an orthogonal direct summand of `H~ (x) H~`. Lemma 1 applies
to H~. Square.

The sections of E_DD used here all vanish somewhere: `zeta_k(x) = e_k - eta(x) <eta(x), e_k>` vanishes
exactly at `x = e_k`. The point is that the DD obstruction is a codimension-one obstruction in E, and
it disappears in `E (x) E`: a vanishing locus for a tensor needs the whole block `V(x)` to lie in
`ker(P (x) P) = C eta (x) H + H (x) C eta`, whose elements have tensor rank at most 2, while V has rank N.

## 5. What this does and does not say about LXI

- It closes the two explicit Pimsner--Toeplitz candidates that Blanchard proposed for non-proper
  infiniteness of continuous fields of O_infty over infinite-dimensional spaces. Every field of the
  form `T(E)` with E of finite deficiency inside a module with enough orthonormal sections is
  properly infinite.
- It does not treat modules whose deficiency is infinite at every tensor power, and it says nothing
  about `O_infty * O_infty`. LXI stays open.
- The proof is exactly of the kind LXI needs globally: a globally defined pair of isometries built by
  passing to a larger tensor power, where the local-to-global K1 obstruction of Blanchard's
  Proposition 5.2(3) is never met.

## 6. Firewall: the abstracts of 1312.7722v7 and 1507.02535v1

Both abstracts say: "Any unital separable continuous C(X)-algebra with properly infinite fibres is
properly infinite as soon as the compact Hausdorff space X has finite topological dimension."
Do not import this. The body proves only that `M_p(C) (x) A` is properly infinite for large p
(Proposition 3.1(2), via BRR Proposition 2.7). The same paper's Corollary 3.3 shows that the
statement for the specific continuous C([0,1])-algebra `D = {f in C([0,1], T_2 *_C T_2) : f(0) in
j_0(T_2), f(1) in j_1(T_2)}` is equivalent to K1-injectivity of `T_2 *_C T_2`, hence (Blanchard,
Clay Math. Proc. 11 (2010), Proposition 4.2) to LXI. Since [0,1] is finite-dimensional, the abstract
claim would solve LXI, and no proof of it appears in the body (all of 1507.02535v1 was read, and
pages 1--8 of 1312.7722v7). STW arXiv:2506.10902v2 lists LXI as open.
