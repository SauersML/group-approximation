`R = L_(F_2)(1,2)`, `S[P] = s_P`, `T[P] = t_P`, `iota_A(g) = S[A] g T[A] + (1 - S[A]T[A])`, the code

## 0. Summary

* **Section 1 (established): regular models of graphs of locally finite groups.** Take a countable graph of groups
  whose vertex groups are countable locally finite subgroups of `R^x`, amalgamated along subgroups of their
  intersections or joined by partial conjugations. It carries a characteristic-two rank model with the free profile
  on every finite subgroup of every vertex group. The model is fixed-point-free and weakly finite. Disjoint cylinder
  defects in one vertex group have rank `3/8` and multiply exactly (`theta = 1`).
* **Section 2 (established): the mismatched pair is inert as a factor.**
  * `a = T_(0,1)` and `c = T_(1,00)` are involutions, and `ac` has infinite order.
  * `a` lies in `R_0^x`, and `c` lies in the conjugate `g^-1 R_0^x g` by a `V`-element.
  * `c` commutes with `h = T_(01,00)` inside a locally finite block-triangular group `H`.
  * The tree `L_0 - R_0^x - H - g^-1 R_0^x g`, with the frames in `L_0`, carries a Section 1 model.

  So an argument cannot force `theta < 1`, the Sylvester gate or the weakly finite gate if it uses the pair as a
  factor with only relations inside these subgroups and exact identifications along the tree. Task 2 needs no new
  letter.
* **Section 3: the relation the natural extension breaks, and where a proof must bite.**
  * The single-letter HNN extension containing the pair breaks `[T_(01,00), T_(1,00)] = 1`, and the vertex `H`
    repairs it.
  * Every relation of `R^x` follows from Steinberg relations, each inside a finite subgroup of order at most 8, and
    from `K_2(3,R)`.
  * So a relation-only proof using the pair must do one of three things: close a cycle of Steinberg relations that
    no locally finite subgroup absorbs, use `K_2(3,R)`, or use a global input.

## 1. Regular models of graphs of locally finite groups

**Setting.** A graph of groups `Y` consists of a countable graph with oriented edges, a countable locally finite
group `L_v` for each vertex `v`, and for each edge `e : v -> w` a subgroup `K_e <= L_v` with an injective
homomorphism `phi_e : K_e -> L_w`. Fix a maximal forest `F ⊆ Y`. The fundamental group `pi_1(Y)` is generated by
the `L_v` and letters `t_e`, subject to the relations of each `L_v`, `t_e k t_e^-1 = phi_e(k)` for `k in K_e`,
and `t_e = 1` for `e in F`.

**Main case.** Each `L_v` is a countable locally finite subgroup of `R^x`, and each edge carries a unit `u_e` with
`K_e <= L_v cap u_e^-1 L_w u_e` and `phi_e(k) = u_e k u_e^-1`, where `u_e = 1` on forest edges. Then vertex
inclusions and `t_e -> u_e` define a homomorphism `psi : pi_1(Y) -> R^x`. A forest edge amalgamates `L_v` and
`L_w` along a common subgroup; a non-forest edge is a single partial conjugation.

**Theorem 1.1.** There is a homomorphism `sigma : pi_1(Y) -> M^x`, with `M = prod_U M_(m_n)(F_2)` and normalized
rank `rk`, such that:
1. `rk(sigma(alpha)) = rank(lambda_Q(alpha)) / |Q|` for every vertex `v`, finite `Q <= L_v` and `alpha in F_2[Q]`,
   where `lambda_Q` is the left regular representation;
2. `sigma` is nontrivial when some `L_v != 1`, and `Fix_r(sigma(pi_1(Y))) = 0` when some `L_v` is infinite;
3. every matrix algebra `M_k(M)` is directly finite, so `sigma` is a representation into the units of a weakly
   finite `F_2`-algebra.

*Proof.*
* **Stages.** Enumerate the vertices and edges. At stage `n` let `Y_n` be the finite subgraph on the first `n`
  vertices with the edges among them that are among the first `n` edges, and choose finite subgroups
  `H_(n,v) <= L_v`, increasing in `n` and exhausting `L_v`. For `e : v -> w` in `Y_n` put
  `K_(n,e) = {k in K_e cap H_(n,v) : phi_e(k) in H_(n,w)}`, a subgroup mapped injectively into `H_(n,w)`. Every
  finite subset of `K_e` lies in `K_(n,e)` for large `n`.
* **Free actions along the forest.** Put `N_n = prod_(v in Y_n) |H_(n,v)|` and `|Omega_n| = N_n`. In each component
  of `F cap Y_n` choose a root `v_0` and a free action of `H_(n,v_0)` on `Omega_n`. Suppose a free action of
  `H_(n,v)` is given and `e` is a forest edge from `v` to a new vertex `w`.
  * Restricted to `K_(n,e)` it is free with `N_n / |K_(n,e)|` orbits.
  * Take any free action of `H_(n,w)` on `Omega_n`, which exists because `|H_(n,w)|` divides `N_n`. Restricted
    along `phi_e` it is free on `phi_e(K_(n,e))` with the same number of orbits.
  * Two free actions of one finite group on sets of equal size are isomorphic, so there is a bijection `beta` of
    `Omega_n` with `beta(k x) = phi_e(k) beta(x)`. Transport the `H_(n,w)`-action by `beta^-1`. Now
    `lambda_(n,w)(phi_e(k)) = lambda_(n,v)(k)` for `k in K_(n,e)`. An edge oriented towards `v` uses `phi_e^-1`.

  Induction over the forest gives free actions `lambda_(n,v)` of all `H_(n,v)`, agreeing on forest edges.
* **Letters.** For a non-forest edge `e : v -> w`, `K_(n,e)` acts freely through `lambda_(n,v)` and
  `phi_e(K_(n,e))` acts freely through `lambda_(n,w)`, with the same number of orbits. Matching orbits and applying
  `phi_e` inside each gives a permutation `pi_(n,e)` with `pi lambda_(n,v)(k) pi^-1 = lambda_(n,w)(phi_e(k))`.
* **Limit.** Put `sigma(h) = [lambda_(n,v)(h)]` for `h in L_v`, `sigma(t_e) = [pi_(n,e)]` off the forest and
  `sigma(t_e) = 1` on it, using the identity at the finitely many stages where a value is undefined. A relation of
  `L_v` holds at every stage containing its letters, and an edge relation for `k in K_e` holds exactly once
  `k in K_(n,e)`. So `sigma` is a homomorphism.
* **(1).** Once `Q <= H_(n,v)`, `Omega_n` is a free `Q`-set, so `F_2[Omega_n]` is `F_2[Q]^(N_n/|Q|)` as a left
  `F_2[Q]`-module and `rank(lambda(alpha)) = (N_n/|Q|) rank(lambda_Q(alpha))`.
* **(2).** Nontriviality follows from (1). A vector fixed by `sigma(L_v)` has rank at most `1/|H_(m,v)|` for every
  `m`, by the argument of Theorem 1.1(2) of the firewall artifact, and `|H_(m,v)| -> infinity` when `L_v` is
  infinite.
* **(3).** `M_k(M) = prod_U M_(k m_n)(F_2)` with its normalized rank. If `AB = I` there, then at almost every stage
  `rank(I - A_n B_n) = o(m_n)`, and `rank(I - B_n A_n) = rank(I - A_n B_n)` in `M_(k m_n)(F_2)`. So `I - BA` has rank
  zero, hence is zero in the ultraproduct. QED

**Corollary 1.2 (independent defects).** In the main case, let `A_1, ..., A_k` be pairwise disjoint proper
cylinders whose unit root pairs `iota_(A_i)(x_12(1))`, `iota_(A_i)(x_23(1))` lie in one vertex group `L_v`. Then
`rk(sigma(N^A_12)) = 1/2`, `rk(sigma(D_A)) = 3/8` and `rk(sigma(D_(A_1) ... D_(A_k))) = (3/8)^k`. So `theta = 1`,
and `sigma(D_A) != 0` in a weakly finite algebra.

*Proof.* The configurations span `D_8 x ... x D_8 <= L_v`, and Theorem 1.1(1) gives the free profile on it. The
rank counts `4/8` and `3/8` in `F_2[D_8]` are part 3 of `locally-finite-hnn-data-allow-independent-cylinder-defects`,
and ranks of tensor products multiply. QED

**Corollary 1.3 (firewall).** Suppose an argument proves strict submultiplicativity, or that every rank function on
`F_2[R^x]` kills `D`, or that every weakly finite representation of `R^x` kills `D`. Suppose its group-theoretic
inputs are relations that hold in `pi_1(Y)` for one graph `Y` of the main case containing the configurations it
uses, and that its other inputs are rank calculus in rank ultraproducts, nontriviality and fixed-point-freeness.
Then the argument fails: `sigma` of Theorem 1.1 satisfies every input, and `sigma(D_A) != 0` with `theta = 1`.

**Remark 1.4 (what is new).** One vertex with letters is Theorem 1.1 of the firewall artifact
(`locally-finite-hnn-data-allow-independent-cylinder-defects`) and the letterwise model of
`binary-locally-finite-hnn-models-violate-two-root-identity`. Forest edges add genuine amalgamation. Products of
elements from different locally finite subgroups are realized as products, and conjugation chains routed through
the intersections hold exactly. Those are the inputs Remark 1.2 of the firewall artifact listed as not covered
("products of conjugators", "two frames").

**Remark 1.5 (relation to existing firewalls).**
* **Conjugate vertex groups.** If `L_2 = u L_1 u^-1`, nothing new arises. In the HNN group `Λ` of the firewall
  artifact with base `L = L_1` and letter `t = t_u`, Britton's lemma gives `<L, t L t^-1> = L *_C t L t^-1` with
  `C = L cap u L u^-1`. Theorem 1.1 is needed for vertex groups that are not conjugate, such as `H` in Section 2.
* **Soficity.** `pi_1(Y)` is sofic, because amalgams and HNN extensions over amenable subgroups preserve soficity
  (literature, not used here). `sofic-subgroups-carry-independent-cylinder-defects` covers sofic subgroups of `R^x`.
  Theorem 1.1 covers the relations of `pi_1(Y)` whatever its image in `R^x`. That image can be all of `R^x`, which is
  generated by involutions.

## 2. The mismatched pair

Put `a = T_(0,1)`, `c = T_(1,00)` and `h = T_(01,00)`. Let `g = s_10 t_1 + s_11 t_00 + s_0 t_01 ∈ V`, which maps the
code `(1, 00, 01)` to `(10, 11, 0)`, with `g^-1 = s_1 t_10 + s_00 t_11 + s_01 t_0`. Put `L_1 = R_0^x` and
`L_2 = g^-1 R_0^x g`.

**Proposition 2.1.**
1. `a`, `c` and `h` are involutions, `[h, c] = 1`, and `ac` has infinite order.
2. `a, h ∈ L_1` and `c ∈ L_2`, with `g c g^-1 = T_(10,11)` and `g h g^-1 = T_(0,11)`. So `c ∉ L_1` and `h ∉ L_2`.
3. `K = L_1 cap L_2` is the group of degree-zero units that are block diagonal for `X = [1] ⊔ [00] ⊔ [01]`.
4. `U = {1 + s_1 x t_00 + s_01 y t_00 : x, y ∈ R}` is an elementary abelian 2-group normalized by `K`. So
   `H = U ⋊ K` is a countable locally finite group containing `K`, `c` and `h`.

*Proof.*
* **(1), involutions.** `t_1 s_0 = t_00 s_1 = t_00 s_01 = 0`. So each element is `1` plus a square-zero element, and
  `(s_1 t_00)(s_01 t_00) = 0 = (s_01 t_00)(s_1 t_00)`.
* **(1), infinite order.** Over `F_2`, `ac = 1 + A` with `A = s_0 t_1 + s_1 t_00 + s_0 t_00`.
  * **The representation.** `R` acts unitally on the `F_2`-span of the points of `X` by `s_i δ_x = δ_(ix)` and
    `t_i δ_(jx) = δ_(ij) δ_x`. Put `q_n = δ_(0^n 1^∞)` and `r_m = δ_(1 0^m 1^∞)`, so `r_0 = q_0`.
  * **The action of `A`.** `A q_0 = q_1`, `A q_1 = 0`, `A q_n = r_(n-2) + q_(n-1)` for `n >= 2`, and
    `A r_m = q_(m+1)` for `m >= 1`.
  * **Levels.** `q_0` and `q_1` get level 1, `q_n` gets level `n` and `r_m` gets level `m + 1`. Let `W_l` be the
    span of the basis vectors of level at most `l`. Then `A W_l ⊆ W_l` and `A^2 W_l ⊆ W_(l-1)`, so `A` is nilpotent
    on `W_l` and `(ac)^(2^k) = 1 + A^(2^k)` there.
  * **Conclusion.** For `l >= 2`, modulo `W_(l-2)`, `A^2 q_l = q_(l-1)` and `A^2 r_(l-1) = r_(l-2) + q_(l-1)`. This
    maps the level-`l` piece isomorphically onto the level-`(l-1)` piece. So `A^(2(l-1)) q_l != 0`, and the order of
    `ac` on `W_l` exceeds `2(l-1)`. Hence `ac` has infinite order.
* **(2).** `a` and `h` have degree zero. `g s_1 = s_10`, `g s_01 = s_0` and `t_00 g^-1 = t_11` give the two
  conjugates. `T_(10,11)` has degree zero, while `c` and `T_(0,11)` have nonzero components of degree `-1`.
* **(3).** For `P ∈ {1, 00, 01}`, `g s_P = s_(P')` with `|P'| - |P| = d_P`, where `d_1 = 1`, `d_00 = 0` and
  `d_01 = -1`. For `k ∈ R_0`, the block `s_P (t_P k s_Q) t_Q` has degree zero, and conjugation by `g` sends it to
  `s_(P') (t_P k s_Q) t_(Q')`, which is homogeneous of degree `d_P - d_Q`. So `g k g^-1 ∈ R_0` iff every
  off-diagonal block of `k` vanishes.
* **(4).**
  * **`U` is normalized.** `t_00 s_1 = t_00 s_01 = 0`, so the square-zero parts multiply to zero and `U` is
    elementary abelian. A block-diagonal `k` satisfies `k s_P = s_P (t_P k s_P)` and
    `t_00 k^-1 = (t_00 k^-1 s_00) t_00`. So `k U k^-1 = U`, and `U cap K = 1`.
  * **`H` is locally finite.** `K` is locally finite as a subgroup of `R_0^x`. A finitely generated subgroup of `H`
    has finite image in `H/U = K`. Its intersection with `U` has finite index, so it is finitely generated, hence
    finite. QED

**Corollary 2.2 (the pair as a factor).** Let `L_0` be a countable locally finite subgroup of `R^x` containing the
frames `iota_(A_i)(D_8)` of disjoint proper cylinders `A_1, ..., A_k`, for instance the finite group they generate.
Let `Y` be the tree `L_0 - L_1 - H - L_2` with edge groups `L_0 cap L_1`, `L_1 cap H` and `H cap L_2`, plus any
partial conjugations as non-forest edges. Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 give a weakly finite rank model of
`pi_1(Y)` with `rk(D_(A_1) ... D_(A_k)) = (3/8)^k`. In `pi_1(Y)`:
* `a`, `c` and every word in them are realized, although `ac` has infinite order;
* every relation inside `L_0`, `L_1`, `H` or `L_2` holds;
* each element of `K` is a single element of `pi_1(Y)` across `L_1`, `H` and `L_2`;
* in particular `[h, c] = 1` holds, and so does the block-triangular calculus of `c` and `h` against `K`.

So suppose an argument uses the pair as a factor, takes only these relations as its group-theoretic inputs, and
otherwise uses rank calculus, nontriviality and fixed-point-freeness. It proves nothing about `theta`, the Sylvester
gate or the weakly finite gate.

## 3. What the models break

**Proposition 3.1 (the relation the single-letter extension breaks).** Let `Λ` be the HNN group of the firewall
artifact with base `L = R_0^x` and a letter `t = t_u` for `u = g^-1`. The lift `ĉ = t T_(10,11) t^-1` maps to `c`.
Then `[h, ĉ] != 1` in `Λ`, although `[T_(01,00), T_(1,00)] = 1` in `R^x`.

*Proof.* The word `h t T_(10,11) t^-1 h t T_(10,11) t^-1` has no pinch. A pinch `t T_(10,11) t^-1` needs
`u T_(10,11) u^-1 = c ∈ L`, and a pinch `t^-1 h t` needs `u^-1 h u = g h g^-1 ∈ L`. Both fail by
Proposition 2.1(2), so Britton's lemma gives `[h, ĉ] != 1`. QED

The statement concerns this natural lift. A letter conjugating `<h, c>` into `R_0^x`, if one exists, would route the
relation as well.

The vertex `H` of Corollary 2.2 repairs the relation. It also absorbs the triangle of exact identifications
(`L_1 ∋ h`, `L_2 ∋ c`, `L_1 cap L_2 = K`), because `K`, `h` and `c` all lie in the locally finite group `H`. A cycle of
identifications is harmless once one locally finite subgroup contains the elements it passes through.

**Remark 3.2 (where a relation-only proof must bite).**
* **Steinberg residue.** Through `psi`, `R^x = EL_3(R)` (`leavitt-gl-equals-el-and-perfect-unit-group`). So every
  relation of `R^x` follows from the Steinberg relations together with `K_2(3,R) = ker(St_3(R) -> EL_3(R))`.
* **Each Steinberg relation is local.** Over `F_2`, each holds in a finite subgroup:
  * `x_ij(r) x_ij(s) = x_ij(r+s)`, and the commutation of commuting root elements, hold in abelian groups of order at
    most 4;
  * `[x_ij(r), x_jk(s)] = x_ik(rs)` holds in `{x_ij(p) x_jk(q) x_ik(z)}` with `p ∈ {0,r}`, `q ∈ {0,s}` and
    `z ∈ {0,rs}`, a group of order at most 8.
* **What is inert.**
  * By Theorem 1.1, Steinberg relations are inert when they, together with a vertex carrying the frames, can be
    arranged in a tree with exact identifications along shared elements.
  * Cycles are inert when one locally finite subgroup contains their elements, as after Proposition 3.1.
  * By `sofic-subgroups-carry-independent-cylinder-defects`, any family inside a sofic subgroup is inert.
* **What must be used.** A relation-only proof that uses the mismatched pair as a factor must use one of:
  * a cycle of Steinberg relations through the pair whose elements generate a nonsofic subgroup that no graph of
    locally finite subgroups routes;
  * an element of `K_2(3,R)`;
  * a global input about all models of `R^x`, such as the defect gap, near-minimal multiplicativity or compactness.

  None was found here.
