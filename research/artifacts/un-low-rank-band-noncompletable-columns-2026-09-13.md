# UN low-rank band: every module-rank conjugation into EL_m has non-completable columns

Lane `un-low-rank-band`, 2026-09-13. Unreviewed. Target: the band `3 ≤ N < 2p` of
`el-n-mf-quotient-below-twice-paradoxical-level-exists`, first at `EL_3(L_K(3,4))`.

## 0. Summary
- For `R = L_K(m, n)` (`1 ≤ m < n`), take any right-module isomorphism `R^M ≅ R^N` with `M ≠ N` and
  `min(M, N) = m`. No column of either matrix is completable to an invertible matrix (Theorem 1).
- At rank `N = m` this kills both mechanisms proposed for closing the band:
  - "some column of X is an E_m-image of e_1", which un-middle's firewall asked us to test;
  - "a free coordinate", the mechanism of `non-ibn-ring-el-n-full-mf-radical-above-module-rank`.
- Ranks `N < m` admit no conjugation between free modules of different ranks at all.
- So at `EL_3(L_K(3,4))`, the only band rank for module type `(3,4)`, closing the band needs genuinely non-completable
  transvections `1 + x_i r y_j` to lie in `E_3(R)` and to normally generate it. Nothing on main or in the literature read
  gives this. The instance stays OPEN, reduced to one sharp claim (§3).

## 1. The monoid input
Ara–Goodearl, arXiv:1004.4979 (Leavitt path algebras of separated graphs), Proposition `keyexample`, verbatim from the
TeX source: "Let $m\le n$ be positive integers … (2) The monoids $\mathcal V(L_K(m,n))$, $\mathcal V(A_{m,n})$,
$\mathcal V(vA_{m,n}v)$, and $\mathcal V(wA_{m,n}w)$ are all of the form $\langle x\mid mx=nx\rangle$, where the
generator $x$ corresponds to the classes $[1]$, $[w]$, $[\alpha_1\alpha^*_1]$, $[w]$ in the four respective cases."
Earlier forms are due to Leavitt (module type) and Bergman; credit recorded on the import node.

**Consequence (free modules).** In the monoid `⟨x | mx = nx⟩`, `ix = jx` iff `i = j`, or `i, j ≥ m` with
`i ≡ j (mod n − m)`. Indeed the congruence on `ℕ` generated by `m ~ n` identifies exactly such pairs, and that
congruence is already additively closed. Since `[R^i] = i[1]`:

```text
(IBN-m)   R^i ≅ R^j  ⟺  i = j,  or  i, j ≥ m and i ≡ j (mod n − m).
```

Left versus right modules: `P ↦ Hom_R(P, R)` is a duality between f.g. projective left and right modules and sends
`R^i` to `R^i`, so (IBN-m) holds on both sides.

## 2. Theorem 1 (non-completable columns at module rank)
**Setting.** `R` is a unital ring satisfying (IBN-m) for some `m ≥ 1` and `n > m`, e.g. `R = L_K(m, n)`. Let
`M ≠ N`, `X ∈ M_{N×M}(R)` and `Y ∈ M_{M×N}(R)` with `XY = 1_N` and `YX = 1_M`, i.e. mutually inverse isomorphisms
`R^M ⇄ R^N` of right modules. Write `x_i` for the columns of `X`, and `y'_k` for the columns of `Y`.

**Theorem 1.** If `min(M, N) = m`, then no `x_i` lies in `GL_N(R)·e_1`, and no `y'_k` lies in `GL_M(R)·e_1`. In
particular no column of `X` or `Y` is a standard basis vector: neither side has a free coordinate. If `min(M, N) < m`,
such `X, Y` do not exist.

**Proof.**
- **Existence.** An isomorphism `R^M ≅ R^N` with `M ≠ N` forces `M, N ≥ m` by (IBN-m). That settles the second
  assertion.
- **Decomposition.** `X` maps `R^M = ⊕_i e_i R` isomorphically onto `R^N`, so `R^N = ⊕_i x_i R`. Each `x_i R ≅ R`,
  since `r ↦ x_i r` is injective (`y_i x_i = 1`, where `y_i` is row `i` of `Y`). Hence `x_i R` is a direct summand with
  complement `⊕_{j≠i} x_j R ≅ R^{M−1}`.
- **Completability.** Suppose `x_i = g e_1` with `g ∈ GL_N(R)`. Then `R^N = g(e_1 R) ⊕ g(e_2 R ⊕ … ⊕ e_N R)`, so
  `x_i R` also has a complement `≅ R^{N−1}`.
- **Contradiction.** Two complements of the same direct summand are isomorphic, both being `≅ R^N / x_i R`. So
  `R^{M−1} ≅ R^{N−1}`. Now `min(M−1, N−1) = m − 1 < m` and `M − 1 ≠ N − 1`, contradicting (IBN-m).
- **Columns of `Y`.** They are the columns of the inverse isomorphism `R^N → R^M`, so the same argument applies with
  `M` and `N` exchanged. ∎

**Model tests.**
- `m = 1`, `R = L_K(1,2)`, `N = 1`, `M = 2`: the columns `s_1, s_2 ∈ R` are not units. Correct: they are proper
  isometries. The first nontrivial free coordinate appears at `N = 2` (un-middle Theorem 5, `N ≥ 2` for `m = 1`).
- `m = 2`, `R = L_K(2,3)`, `N = 3 > m`: Theorem 1 is silent, and indeed isomorphisms `R^4 ≅ R^3` with a free coordinate
  exist (`diag(X_{2×3}, 1)`), as used in Theorem 5.
- `m = 3`, `R = L_K(3,4)`, `N = 3`, `M = 4`: all four columns of any `X` are non-completable. This is the band case.

## 3. What this leaves at EL_3(L_K(3,4))
Put `R = L_K(3,4)` and `Φ(g) = X g Y`, a group isomorphism `GL_4(R) → GL_3(R)`. Then `Φ(e_ij(r)) = 1 + x_i r y_j`
with `y_j x_i = 0`.
- **Dead route 1 (Suslin conjugation).** If `x_i = g e_1` with `g ∈ E_3(R)`, then `1 + x_i r y_j = g(1 + e_1 w)g^{-1}`
  with `w = r y_j g` and `w e_1 = r y_j x_i = 0`. So `1 + e_1 w` is a product of `e_12, e_13`, and the transvection
  would lie in `E_3`. Theorem 1 says no column is even `GL_3`-completable, so this route cannot start for any
  isomorphism.
- **Dead route 2 (free coordinate).** Theorem 1: no isomorphism `R^M ≅ R^3` has one.
- **Dead route 3 (conjugation by an E_3-element in the normal-closure step).** The standard isolation of `e_12(1)` from
  the normal closure of `1 + x_i r y_j` again conjugates `x_i` to `e_1`, which is impossible by Theorem 1.
- **The reduced open claim** `leavitt-3-4-module-iso-image-normally-generates-e3`: `Φ(E_4(R)) ⊆ E_3(R)`, and its normal
  closure in `E_3(R)` is `E_3(R)`.
  - This claim implies full MF radical of `EL_3(R)`. Given `φ : EL_3(R) → M` with `M` MF, `φ∘Φ` is a homomorphism
    `EL_4(R) → M`, trivial by `non-ibn-ring-el-n-full-mf-radical-above-module-rank` at `N = 4 = m+1`.
  - So `ker φ ⊇ Φ(E_4(R))`, and hence `ker φ` contains its normal closure, which is `E_3(R)`.
  - The claim is independent of the choice of `X`: another isomorphism is `E X F` with `E ∈ GL_3`, `F ∈ GL_4`, and the
    `E_3`-invariant parts are unchanged up to conjugation.
- **Why the literature does not settle it.** Membership `1 + v r w ∈ E_n` for unimodular `v` with `wv = 0` is
  Suslin normality. It is proved for commutative rings and for rings module-finite over their centre, and it fails in
  general (Gerasimov, recalled, not re-read). `L_K(3,4)` is neither.
- **Negative direction.** An MF quotient of `EL_3(R)` would need an operator-norm asymptotic representation not killed
  by the transport. No ring approximation exists, since `[R]` is paradoxical (no state), so it would have to be a
  non-ring approximation. Nothing was found.
