# The third finite packet cannot be corrected with both edges fixed

2026-09-08. There are eight-dimensional unitary representations of the
original group in which every old relator is exact and the new coherence
word is arbitrarily close to, but different from, the identity. They give
an explicit obstruction to correcting the third finite packet while
holding both of its rank-one restrictions fixed. The obstruction already
occurs for an approximate table of the single finite group `GL_3(F_2)`.

The representations below kill the original head. They do not disprove
positivity of `gamma_triangle`, and they do not rule out a simultaneous
correction that also changes one or both neighboring packets.

Use the exact quotient `Gamma/L=Lambda x Z` from
[the finitary-kernel proof](jacobson-recursive-packets-generate-finitary-linear-kernel-2026-09-08.md),
the word `E` and its quotient image `e` from
[the coherence-word calculation](jacobson-missing-laurent-coherence-word-and-finite-cell-countermodel-2026-09-08.md),
and the new finite packet in
[the coherent quotient](jacobson-coherent-quotient-and-four-word-matrix-gap-2026-09-08.md).
All commutators mean `[x,y]=x y x^(-1)y^(-1)`.

## 1. The complete old presentation and the two new edges

The group `Lambda` has two complete `GL_3(F_2)` factors, identified
on the full subgroup generated by `c=x_13` and `k=x_31`.
In the first factor put `q=w_23` and `r=x_23`; in the second
put `h=w_12`. The remaining defining relations and words are

```text
u=[h c h,k],       v=h u h,       d=h r h,
(h q)^3=1,        [u,d]=r,
e=q v q d.                                             (1)
```

The coherent quotient adds `e=1`. Its third finite group is
`D=<h,u,q,r> ~= GL_3(F_2)`, with specified subgroups

```text
A=<h,u> ~= GL_2(F_2) on coordinates {1,2},
J=<q,r> ~= GL_2(F_2) on coordinates {2,3},
A intersect J={1}.                                    (2)
```

Upstairs in `Gamma_triangle`, the same packet uses
`q=b=q_Q q_P` and `r=zeta=r_Q r_P`. Its intersections with
the old finite packets are exactly

```text
D intersect C=<h,u>,
D intersect B=<b,zeta>,
D intersect B intersect C={1}.                        (3)
```

For the upper bounds in (3), inspect the literal action on the far
triples `(e_(1,n+1),e_(2,n),e_(3,n))`. Membership in `B`
forces the block decomposition `1 | {2,3}`; membership in `C`
forces `{1,2} | 3`. These give the two indicated subgroups.
Their common diagonal group over `F_2` is trivial. The lower
bounds are the specified embeddings. Faithfulness of each of these
finite packets in the literal action upgrades these literal upper
bounds to the abstract intersections in (3).

## 2. An integral quotient showing that the word has infinite order

There is a simple discrete version of the construction. Let
`G=GL_3(F_2)`, let `Omega=F_2^3\{0}`, and let
`P=Z^Omega` with basis `delta_v`, on which `G` permutes the
indices. Write

```text
P_0={sum_v n_v delta_v : sum_v n_v=0} ~= Z^6,
a=delta_(e_2).
```

Use pairs `(g,p)` with multiplication
`(g,p)(g',p')=(g g',p+g p')`. Map the first finite factor
by `j_1(g)=(g,0)` and the second by

```text
j_2(g)=(g,a-g a).                                    (4)
```

The latter is conjugation by `(1,a)` in `P semidirect G`;
its values lie in `P_0 semidirect G`. Consequently both full
finite-factor tables hold. Since `c,k` fix `e_2`, their two
images agree, including the entire common `GL_2`.

The defining word for `u` gives `(x_21,0)`, since `x_21`
fixes `e_2`. Also `r` fixes `a`, so conjugating it by
`j_2(h)` gives `j_2(h r h)=j_2(c)=(c,0)`. Thus `d`
is `(x_13,0)`, and the second mixed relation is exactly the
ordinary identity `[x_21,x_13]=x_23`.

For the braid, put `M=h q`. This cycles `e_1,e_2,e_3` in
that order, and the additive component of `j_2(h)j_1(q)` is
`delta_(e_2)-delta_(e_1)`. Its three translates sum to zero.
The linear part satisfies `M^3=1`, proving the first mixed
relation as well. Finally,

```text
v maps to (x_12,delta_(e_2)-delta_(e_1+e_2)),
e maps to (1,delta_(e_3)-delta_(e_1+e_3)).              (5)
```

This verifies every relation of `Lambda`. The homomorphism is onto
`P_0 semidirect G`: it contains the first `G` and the difference
`delta_(e_2)-delta_(e_1)`, obtained by multiplying `j_2(h)`
by `j_1(h)`. The action of `G` is transitive on ordered pairs
of distinct nonzero vectors, so the conjugates of that difference
generate the whole augmentation lattice `P_0`.

In particular (5) has infinite order. Therefore `e` has infinite
order in `Lambda`, and `E` has infinite order in `Gamma`.
The linear projection of this quotient equals the Laurent map
`kappa` followed by evaluation at `z=1`. Thus the image of
`ker(kappa)` lies in `P_0`; it equals `P_0` because it
contains (5) and all its `G` conjugates. These conclusions concern
the old group, before imposing the new coherence word.

## 3. An exact continuous family in dimension eight

On the orthonormal basis of `C^8` indexed by `F_2^3`, let
`L_g` be the permutation matrix of the linear map `g`, and let
`S_a` be translation by `a`. These translations are commuting
self-adjoint involutions and satisfy

```text
S_a S_b=S_(a+b),       L_g S_a L_g^*=S_(g a).
```

For a real parameter `theta` put

```text
U_theta=exp(i theta S_(e_2)),
H_theta=U_theta L_h U_theta^*.
```

Keep the entire first finite factor as `L_G` and conjugate the
entire second factor by `U_theta`. This also follows from (4)
by sending `(1,sum n_a delta_a)` to
`exp(i theta sum n_a S_a)`. Here is a direct verification of
all the old relations and of the defect word.

The matrices `L_c,L_k,L_r,L_u` commute with `U_theta`,
since all four corresponding linear maps fix `e_2`. Hence both
complete factors share `c,k`, and

```text
u_theta=[H_theta L_c H_theta,L_k]=L_(x_21),
d_theta=H_theta L_r H_theta=L_(x_13),
[u_theta,d_theta]=L_r.                                (6)
```

For example, the second equality follows by commuting `L_r`
past `U_theta`, using `h r h=c`, and then commuting
`L_c` past `U_theta`. For the braid, with `M=L_(h q)`,

```text
H_theta L_q=exp(i theta(S_(e_2)-S_(e_1))) M.
```

Conjugation by `M` cycles the three coordinate translations.
The exponents in the cube are therefore

```text
S_(e_2)-S_(e_1),
S_(e_3)-S_(e_2),
S_(e_1)-S_(e_3).
```

They commute and sum to zero; also `M^3=I`. Thus the braid
is exact for every `theta`. The remaining word is

```text
v_theta=U_theta L_(x_12) U_theta^*,
e_theta=L_q v_theta L_q d_theta
       =exp(i theta(S_(e_3)-S_(e_1+e_3))).             (7)
```

The two translations in (7) are distinct commuting involutions.
Their difference has eigenvalues `0,2,-2`, with both nonzero
values attained. For example, this follows on the character basis
of the additive group `F_2^3`. Consequently, for
`0<theta<pi/2`,

```text
||e_theta-I||=2 sin(theta)>0.                          (8)
```

Composing with `Gamma -> Lambda x Z` and sending the final
`Z` generator to the identity gives exact representations of the
full original `Gamma`. In its finite-packet formulation, `K`
and `C_0` are killed, the two remaining `GL_3` factors are
the two just specified, and the stable-letter matrix is `T=I`.
All old finite tables, full overlaps, and stable-letter relations
are exact. In particular the three old mixed errors are zero.
The original head mark is the identity throughout this family.

## 4. Arbitrarily accurate finite tables with no correction fixing both edges

Consider the abstract finite group `D=GL_3(F_2)` with the
subgroups `A,J` from (2). Define a unitary-valued map on its
entire multiplication table by

```text
f_theta(g)=U_theta L_g U_theta^*    if g in A,
f_theta(g)=L_g                    if g notin A.        (9)
```

It is unital. Its restriction to `A` is an exact representation,
as is its restriction to `J`, since `A intersect J={1}`.
These are precisely the two edge representations above.

For every `g in G`, the commuting-translation formula gives

```text
U_theta L_g U_theta^* L_g^*
 =exp(i theta(S_(e_2)-S_(g e_2))).
```

Its distance from the identity is zero if `g e_2=e_2`,
and at most `2 sin(theta)` otherwise. Thus for
`0<theta<pi/2`,

```text
max_g ||f_theta(g)-L_g|| <= 2 sin(theta),
max_(g,g') ||f_theta(g)f_theta(g')-f_theta(g g')||
                              <= 6 sin(theta).       (10)
```

The second bound compares the three entries to the exact table of
`L_G`. It tends to zero with `theta`, in the fixed dimension
eight.

Nevertheless there is no representation `sigma:D->U(8)` that
agrees with `f_theta` on both `A` and `J`. Such agreement
would prescribe `h,u,q,r` as `H_theta,L_u,L_q,L_r`.
The identity `q(h u h)q(h r h)=1` holds in the finite group
`D`, but the prescribed matrices evaluate it as `e_theta`,
which is nonidentity by (8).

There is also a quantitative necessary movement. For any exact
representation `sigma:D->U(8)`, put

```text
a=max_(g in A)||sigma(g)-f_theta(g)||,
b=max_(g in J)||sigma(g)-f_theta(g)||.
```

The word `q v q h r h` contains three letters of `A` and
three letters of `J`, with `v=h u h` treated as one specified
element of `A`. Telescoping against its exact value under
`sigma` gives

```text
2 sin(theta) <= 3(a+b).                               (11)
```

Thus a correction must move at least one edge. Ordinary finite-group
relative averaging cannot be applied with both edges frozen: their
union is not a subgroup, and (9)--(10) show that replacing a
subgroup by this union makes that relative-correction statement false.

## 5. Scope for the marked matrix problem

The example isolates an actual obstruction to one proposed correction
step, including the exact two embeddings appearing in (3). It does not
show that moving `B,C,D` together is impossible. Indeed the original
representation `L_G` corrects (9), fixes `J`, and moves `A`
by at most `2 sin(theta)`.

Nor is this a sequence in the domain defining `gamma_triangle`:
that domain requires the head mark to survive, whereas the family
in Section 3 kills it. Positivity of the marked operator-norm gap,
and the requested non-MF conclusion, remain unresolved. All arguments
here use finite permutation matrices, an integral permutation module,
and their exact algebraic identities.
