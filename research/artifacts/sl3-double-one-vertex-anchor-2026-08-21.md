# The one-vertex outlier sector of the SL3 arithmetic double is empty

Let

```text
C=SL_3(Z),  A=SL_3(Z[1/2]),  D=A *_C A,
h=diag(2,1,1/2).
```

This note proves a relative stability statement tailored to the double.
It does not assume, and does not prove, Hilbert--Schmidt stability of `A`.

## 1. The finite-index anchor

The group `A` is generated by `C` and `h`.  Set

```text
B=C cap h^(-1) C h.
```

Conjugation by `h` changes integral matrix entries only by powers dividing
`4`, so `B` contains a principal congruence subgroup of 2-power level and
has finite index in `C`.  In particular `B` has property (T).

Every finite-dimensional unitary representation `alpha` of `A` has finite
image and factors through a congruence quotient of odd level `n`: the prime
2 is inverted in `Z[1/2]`.  Reduction of the 2-power principal congruence
subgroup onto `SL_3(Z/n)` is surjective by Chinese remaindering.  Therefore

```text
alpha(B)=alpha(C)=alpha(A).                              (1)
```

The second equality is the usual finite-dimensional co-density; the first
is the extra fact needed below.

## 2. An exact first vertex anchors the second

Let `pi_0,pi_1` be the vertex restrictions of a small-defect assignment of
`D`, and suppose `pi_0` is close on fixed generators to an exact
`alpha:A->U(d)`.  The amalgam relators imply

```text
pi_1(c) approximately alpha(c)  (c in a fixed generating set of C). (2)
```

Write `H=alpha(h)`, `U=pi_1(h)`, and `u=H^*U`.  If `b in B`, then
`hbh^(-1) in C`.  Compare the corresponding relative relation for `pi_1`
with the exact relation for `alpha`.  Equation (2) gives

```text
[u,alpha(b)] approximately 0.                            (3)
```

A fixed Kazhdan set of `B` and the conjugation representation on normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt matrices now give

```text
dist_2(u,alpha(B)') <= K(def(pi)+dist(pi_0,alpha)).       (4)
```

Conditional expectation followed by polar decomposition in the commutant
produces a unitary `v in alpha(B)'` obeying the same bound.  By (1),

```text
v in alpha(A)'.                                          (5)
```

## 3. The relative relators kill the commutant gauge

Fix a finite relative presentation `A=<C,h|R>`.  On the comparison tuple

```text
c -> alpha(c),   h -> alpha(h)v,
```

each `r in R` evaluates exactly to `v^(e_r)`, where `e_r` is its total
`h`-exponent.  The original small-defect tuple is close to this comparison
tuple, so every one of these powers is close to the identity.

The exponents have gcd one.  To see this, kill `C` normally in the relative
presentation.  The quotient is

```text
A/<<C>> = <h | h^(e_r), r in R>.
```

It is cyclic.  Since `A` is perfect, every quotient of `A` is perfect; a
cyclic perfect group is trivial.  Hence the gcd is one.  For fixed Bezout
coefficients `a_r`,

```text
v = product_r (v^(e_r))^(a_r),
```

and telescoping gives a dimension-free bound `||v-I||_2=o(1)`.  Combining
this with (4) proves `pi_1(h) approximately alpha(h)`, and (2) handles `C`.
Thus the second vertex is near the same exact representation.

## 4. Sharp consequence and stability boundary

The previously proved joint-near-exact theorem now applies whenever either
vertex is near exact.  Therefore a canonical regular microstate that keeps
the fold word nontrivial must place both vertex restrictions uniformly far
from every exact finite-dimensional representation.

Regular-character HS stability of `A` would be sufficient to exclude this
last sector, because each vertex restriction of a canonical microstate of
`D` has the canonical character of `A`.  It is not equivalent to what was
proved here: only `D`-extendible pairs occur, and the amalgam gives no method
to extend an arbitrary regular-character approximate representation of `A`
to a canonical microstate of `D`.  The double's extra mixed moments are a
real restriction, not a formal source of stability.
