## Source

Belk–Fournier-Facio–Hyde–Zaremsky, arXiv:2503.21882v2, read in arXiv HTML.

**Remark 3.5**, verbatim:
- "Now that we know that all finitely presented simple groups that are highly transitive, or more generally MIF,
  satisfy PBH, it is worth recording which finitely presented simple groups are known to have these properties."
- Then: "First there are the ones that are "obviously" highly transitive, hence MIF. This includes any finitely
  presented simple groups of homeomorphisms of the Cantor space {1,…,n}ℕ that contain the commutator subgroup of
  the Higman–Thompson group Vn."

**Definition (Introduction)**, verbatim:
- "Given a group Γ acting faithfully on a set S, we say that the action is of type (A) if: (i) The group Γ is
  finitely presented. (ii) The stabilizer Stab_Γ(s) is finitely generated for all s ∈ S. (iii) The action has
  finitely many orbits of pairs."
- The permutational Boone–Higman conjecture: "Every finitely generated group with solvable word problem embeds in
  a group admitting an action of type (A)."
- So "satisfies PBH" means exactly membership in the repo's `B_A`.

**Theorem C**, verbatim:
- "For a finitely generated group G (with solvable word problem) the following are equivalent: (i) G satisfies the
  permutational Boone–Higman conjecture … (iii) G embeds in a finitely presented simple group that is highly
  transitive."

**Corollary D** is the universality statement that the survey cites at the end of §4.4.

## Hypothesis match for `VA`

- `VA` is a group of homeomorphisms of the Cantor space `{0,1}^ℕ`, the case `n = 2`. It is generated by
  Thompson's `V = V_2` and Brin's `A` (survey §4.3: "the group VA of homeomorphisms of the Cantor set generated by
  A and Thompson's group V").
- `VA` is finitely presented and simple (survey Theorem 4.11, Belk–Hyde–Matucci arXiv:2407.03149).
- `VA` contains `V_2`, and `[V_2, V_2] = V_2` because `V` is simple. **All hypotheses of Remark 3.5 hold.**
- So `VA` is highly transitive.
- `VA` is finitely generated, and it has solvable word problem because it is finitely presented and simple
  (survey Prop. 3.1). Theorem C (iii)⇒(i), applied to `G = VA` embedded in itself, gives `VA ∈ B_A`.
- `B_A` is closed under subgroups by definition. So every subgroup of `VA` is in `B_A`, finitely generated or not.
  That covers every countable abelian group (survey Theorem 4.11), and in particular `(Q,+)` and `Q^×`.
