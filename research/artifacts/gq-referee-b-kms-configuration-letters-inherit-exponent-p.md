- `uA_i ∈ U` holds. `U_0` is the set of all divisors of `A_0⋯A_K`, so `uA_i ∈ U` whenever `A_i` does not occur in
  `u`.

### Proof

The proof is correct. `(x_u x_(uA_i)^n)^(A_i) = x_u^(A_i) (x_(uA_i)^(A_i))^n`, and then G5 a), G5 c) and `A_i^p = 1`
give `x_(uA_i)^p = 1`.

The G8 bookkeeping also matches. G8 defines
`f*a_i = f^(-1) f^(a_i) f^(-a_i^(-1)) f^((a_i')^(-1))` and `f*A_i = [f, A_i]`. So both sides of each machine
relation are products of conjugates of `x_(q_i A_0)^(±1)`.

The node grants Lemma 4.5 in `G^0(M)` without re-deriving it, and says so. The printed proof of Lemma 4.5 cites
G2, G4, G5 a) and c), G6, Remark 4.2 and Lemma 4.1. Remark 4.2 cites "G7 together with G1 and G5b)". There G1
enters only as condition (1) of Lemma 4.1: `<F ∪ F'>` abelian, that is, `H_2` abelian. I found no use of the `H_0`
exponent law, which agrees with the node. I did not re-derive the lemma either.

### Wording fixes

- **W1.** The route writes "(conventions there: `u^a = a^(-1) u a`, `[x, y] = x^(-1) y^(-1) x y`)". The second
  convention appears nowhere in `KMS-322.tex`; no definition of `[x,y]` is printed.
  - It is the convention implied by display (e00) in the proof of Lemma 4.5. The step marked "G5 a), c), G6" turns
    `x_(q_j)^(... A_i^(t_1) ...)` into `x_(q_j)^(...) (x_(q_j A_i)^(t_1))^(...)`. That is the node's
    `x_u^(A_i^n) = x_u x_(uA_i)^n`.
  - The conclusion also does not depend on the convention. Under `[x,y] = x y x^(-1) y^(-1)`, G5 a) gives
    `(x_u^(-1))^(A_i^(-1)) = x_u^(-1) x_(uA_i)`. The same induction over powers of `A_i^(-1)` then gives
    `x_(uA_i)^p = 1`.
  - Suggested wording: "`u^a = a^(-1)ua` (printed in §4.1); the commutator convention is not printed, but (e00)
    uses `[x,y] = x^(-1)y^(-1)xy`, and the conclusion holds under either."
- **W2.** The node says the proof "uses only the relations G1, G5 a), G5 c) and G7". G7 is not used.
  - The route's step "By G7 this is `x_u x_(uA_i)^(n+1)`" is only associativity: `x_u x_(uA_i) x_(uA_i)^n`.
  - The claim then rests on G1 (`A_i^p = 1`), G5 a) and G5 c) alone. Harmless, since this only makes the claim
    stronger.

- **Lemma 4.15, the reason for finite index.** The artifact says `T/T(V_R)` is spanned by finitely many `x_(i,w)`
  of order `p`. That is the right reading.
  - KMS's own proof says the finite set `{x_(i,w) : w ∈ S(M) \ V_R}` "is a set of representatives of all cosets of
    $T(V_R)$ in $T$". Taken literally, that is false: the set generates the quotient, and it is finite only because
    of exponent `p`.
  - `S(M) \ V_R` is finite for every `M` (Lemma 3.6, no hypothesis).
