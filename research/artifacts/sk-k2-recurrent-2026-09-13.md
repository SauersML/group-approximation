# sk-k2-recurrent: restriction kernels over recurrent orbit closures

Lane `sk-k2-recurrent`, 2026-09-13. Status: complete proofs, UNREVIEWED.
Notation follows `research/artifacts/sk-k2-subsystems-2026-09-13-part1.md`:
- X is a subshift, R_X = LC(X,F_2) ⋊_T Z, and I_Y is the ideal of coefficients vanishing on a closed invariant Y.
- ρ_Y : R_X → R_Y is restriction and π_Y = EL_n(ρ_Y).
- E_n(I) is generated by the e_ij(r) with r ∈ I, and E_n(R,I) is its normal closure.
- Min(X) is the set of minimal subsets of X.

## 0. Summary
**The brief.** Let X be the orbit closure of a recurrent point that is not uniformly recurrent, and Y a minimal subset. Is K_2(3,R_X) → K_2(3,R_Y) onto? Equivalently (Lemma A of sk-k2-subsystems), is ker π_Y = E_3(R_X,I_Y)?

**Answer.**
- YES whenever Y contains every minimal subset of X, for example when X has a unique minimal subset. Then ker π_Y = E_n(I_Y) is even locally finite.
- More generally YES whenever the minimal subsets not in Y form a closed set disjoint from Y.
- Mechanism (Chain Lemma, §1): bounded chains of visits need only Y ⊇ Min(X), not the limit-set hypothesis of sk-k2-subsystems.
- This corrects the claim in sk-k2-subsystems §7 that such corners have unbounded chains.

**Corollary E′ (§4).** If X has finitely many minimal subsets, all infinite, then for n ≥ 3 the normal subgroups of EL_n(R_X) correspond exactly to closed invariant subsets. This strictly extends Corollary E, which needed the closure of the limit set to be a finite union of minimal sets. For recurrent points that closure is all of X.

**Example (§5).** An orbit closure of a recurrent, not uniformly recurrent point whose only minimal subset is a Sturmian subshift. The dictionary holds and the kernel onto the Sturmian quotient is locally finite.

**Splittings (§6).** Equivariant retractions X → Y split π_Y, and clopen invariant pieces of X∖Y split off. Both allow minimal subsets that accumulate on Y.

**Open (§8).** Minimal subsets accumulating on Y with neither splitting available.

## 1. Chain Lemma
**Statement.** Let Y ⊆ X be closed invariant. The following are equivalent.
- (a) Every minimal subset of X lies in Y.
- (b) For every clopen V ⊆ X∖Y and every J ≥ 1 there is M < ∞ with the following property: whenever z ∈ X and t_0 < … < t_{m−1} satisfy T^{t_i}z ∈ V and t_{i+1} − t_i ≤ J, we have m ≤ M.

**Proof of (a) ⇒ (b).** Suppose chains of every length exist.
1. Let V_m be the set of points of V that start a J-chain of length m at time 0.
   - V_m depends only on the window [0,mJ], so it is clopen.
   - The V_m are decreasing and nonempty, since a chain can be shifted to start at time 0.
   - König's lemma, over the finitely many gap sequences, gives z ∈ V with visits at times 0 = t_0 < t_1 < …, all gaps ≤ J.
2. Let w = lim T^{m_k}z with m_k → ∞. Fix N. For large k, every interval of length J inside [−N,N] contains a visit of T^{m_k}z to V.
   - Each such interval has finitely many times, so after passing to a subsequence a fixed time works for all k.
   - V is clopen, so w visits V at that time.
   - As N is arbitrary, w visits V in every interval of length J. The same argument applies to every point of cl O(w).
3. Let M ⊆ cl O(w) be minimal. Its points visit V, so M ∩ V ≠ ∅. Since V ∩ Y = ∅, M ⊄ Y, which contradicts (a). □

**Proof of (b) ⇒ (a).** Let M be minimal with M ⊄ Y. Then M ∩ Y = ∅, because M ∩ Y is closed invariant inside the minimal M. So some clopen V ⊆ X∖Y meets M. A point of M ∩ V visits V syndetically by uniform recurrence, which gives chains of every length. □

**Comparison.** Proposition C of sk-k2-subsystems assumes α(x) ∪ ω(x) ⊆ Y for every x ∉ Y.
- That implies (a): a minimal M ⊄ Y consists of points m with ω(m) = M ⊄ Y.
- The converse fails: a recurrent x ∉ Y has x ∈ ω(x).

## 2. Proposition C′ (the complement ideal is ultramatricial)
**Statement.** If Y ⊇ Min(X), then every finite subset of I_Y lies in a subring B ⊆ I_Y with a unit e_B and B ≅ ∏_i M_{k_i}(F_2). So by Lemma B (`ultramatricial-ideals-have-elementary-congruence-kernels`), for every n ≥ 2, GL_n(R_X, I_Y) = E_n(I_Y), and this group is locally finite.

**Proof.** This is the proof of Proposition C (sk-k2-subsystems §4), with its step (ii) replaced by the Chain Lemma.
- **(i) Localization.** Let r_1, …, r_m ∈ I_Y have coefficients f_j. The set V = ⋃_j (supp f_j ∪ T^{-j} supp f_j) is clopen, and V ⊆ X∖Y because X∖Y is invariant. Every r_i lies in the corner C_V = e_V R_X e_V.
- **(ii) Bounded chains.** Let J bound the absolute exponents in the r_i. By §1, maximal J-chains of visits to V have at most M elements.
- **(iii) Classes.** For x, T^t x ∈ V, write x ~ T^t x if the visits of x to V between times 0 and t have gaps ≤ J.
  - This is an equivalence relation on V.
  - A class has at most M points and time diameter at most D = (M−1)J.
  - For |t| ≤ D the set {x ∈ V : T^t x ∈ V, x ~ T^t x} is clopen.
- **(iv) The subring.** Let B_{V,J} be the set of Σ_{|j|≤D} f_j u^j with supp f_j ⊆ {x ∈ V : T^{-j}x ∈ V, T^{-j}x ~ x}.
  - It contains e_V.
  - It is closed under products, by transitivity of ~ and (fu^i)(gu^j) = f(g∘T^{-i})u^{i+j}.
  - It contains every element of C_V with exponents ≤ J, because two visits at time distance ≤ J are ~-equivalent.
- **(v) Structure.** Let k(x) be the class size and p(x) the position in time order. On {p = 1}, let s_q(x) be the time of the q-th element. All of these are locally constant.
  - For clopen W ⊆ {k = k_0, p = 1} on which the s_q are constant, put ε_pq(W) = e_{T^{s_p}W} u^{s_p − s_q}.
  - Then ε_pq(W) ε_{q′r}(W′) = δ_{qq′} ε_pr(W ∩ W′), and these elements span B_{V,J}.
  - So B_{V,J} ≅ ∏_{k_0 ≤ M} M_{k_0}(LC(W_{k_0},F_2)). Refining to a finite clopen partition on which all coefficients of the r_i are constant gives B ≅ ∏ M_{k_0}(F_2)^{c_{k_0}}. □

## 3. Theorem D′ (restriction kernels off the minimal sets)
**Statement.** Let Y ⊆ X be closed invariant and n ≥ 2. Suppose some closed invariant Z ⊆ X∖Y satisfies Min(X) ⊆ Y ∪ Z. Then
  ker π_Y = E_n(I_Y) = E_n(R_X, I_Y),
and for n ≥ 3 the map K_2(n,R_X) → K_2(n,R_Y) is onto.

**Proof.** The proof of Theorem D (sk-k2-subsystems §5) goes through, with Proposition C′ for Y′ = Y ∪ Z in place of Proposition C.
- Y and Z are disjoint closed invariant sets, so R_{Y′} = R_Y × R_Z.
- By Proposition C′ and Lemma B, ker π_{Y′} ⊆ GL_n(R_X, I_{Y′}) = E_n(I_{Y′}) ⊆ E_n(I_Y).
- Choose a clopen U ⊇ Z with U ∩ Y = ∅. Each r ∈ R_Z lifts to an element of e_U R_X ⊆ I_Y restricting to (0,r). So π_{Y′}(E_n(I_Y)) ⊇ 1 × EL_n(R_Z).
- For g ∈ ker π_Y, pick h ∈ E_n(I_Y) with π_{Y′}(h) = π_{Y′}(g). Then g h^{-1} ∈ ker π_{Y′} ⊆ E_n(I_Y).
- The K_2 clause is Lemma A of sk-k2-subsystems. □

## 4. Corollary E′ (finitely many minimal subsets)
**Statement.** Let X be a subshift with finitely many minimal subsets M_1, …, M_s, all infinite. Let n ≥ 3 and G = EL_n(R_X).
- (a) Y ↦ ker π_Y is an inclusion-reversing bijection from closed invariant subsets of X onto normal subgroups of G, and G/ker π_Y ≅ EL_n(R_Y).
- (b) K = ker π_{M_1 ∪ … ∪ M_s} is locally finite, and G/K ≅ ∏_i EL_n(R_{M_i}). Each factor is infinite, finitely generated, simple, Kazhdan and LEF, by the note's theorem.

**Proof.**
- X has no periodic points, because a periodic orbit would be a finite minimal subset. So `aperiodic-subshift-el-normal-subgroups-are-sandwiched` applies: every N ⊴ G satisfies E_n(R_X,I_Y) ≤ N ≤ ker π_Y for exactly one closed invariant Y.
- Given Y, let Z be the union of the M_i not contained in Y. Minimal sets either lie in Y or miss it, so Z is closed, invariant and disjoint from Y, and Min(X) ⊆ Y ∪ Z.
- Theorem D′ gives ker π_Y = E_n(R_X,I_Y), so N = ker π_Y.
- ker π_Y determines Y through I_Y = {r : e_12(r) ∈ ker π_Y} (`aperiodic-subshift-ring-ideals-are-invariant-open-sets`), and π_Y is onto.
- (b): Y = ⋃M_i contains Min(X), so Proposition C′ makes ker π_Y = GL_n(R_X,I_Y) ∩ G locally finite. Also R_{⋃M_i} = ∏ R_{M_i}. □

## 5. Example: a recurrent orbit closure with a unique Sturmian minimal set
**Construction.** Fix irrational α and y ∈ X_α. Choose integers n_1 < n_2 < … with N_k = n_1 + … + n_k such that:
- (G) n_{k+1} ≥ 3N_k + k;
- (R) y[−N_k, N_k] = y[n_{k+1} − N_k, n_{k+1} + N_k].

(R) is possible because the word y[−N_k,N_k] recurs syndetically in the uniformly recurrent point y.

Put P = {Σ_{i∈F} n_i : F ⊆ {1,2,…} finite}, so 0 ∈ P. Put x_t = (y_t, 1_P(t)) ∈ ({0,1}×{0,1})^Z, X = cl O(x), and Y = {z ∈ X : second coordinate ≡ 0}.

1. **Windows copy.**
   - P ∩ [−N_k,N_k] = S_k, the set of subsums of n_1,…,n_k, and P ∩ [n_{k+1}−N_k, n_{k+1}+N_k] = n_{k+1} + S_k.
   - Why: a subsum using some n_j with j ≥ k+2 is at least n_{k+2} > n_{k+1} + N_k, and S_k ⊆ [0,N_k] with N_k < n_{k+1} − N_k, both by (G).
   - With (R), x[−N_k,N_k] = x[n_{k+1}−N_k, n_{k+1}+N_k]. So T^{n_{k+1}}x → x, and x is recurrent.
2. **Not uniformly recurrent.** The central letter of x has second coordinate 1, and the times with second coordinate 1 are exactly P. The gap after N_k = max S_k is at least n_{k+1} − N_k → ∞.
3. **Y = X_α × {0^∞}.**
   - The first coordinate of every point of X lies in cl O(y) = X_α.
   - Conversely, x has windows with second coordinate 0 of every length, and Sturmian words recur with bounded gaps in y. So every word of X_α × {0^∞} occurs in x.
4. **Bounded chains in P.** Given J, let k be least with n_{k+1} − N_k > J.
   - Let p = Σ_F n_i and p′ = Σ_{F′} n_i. If F and F′ differ above k, let j > k be the largest index where they differ. Then |p − p′| ≥ n_j − N_{j−1} ≥ n_{k+1} − N_k > J, because n_j − N_{j−1} is increasing by (G).
   - So a J-chain in P lies in one translate of S_k, and has at most 2^k elements.
   - The second-coordinate 1-set of any z ∈ X is a limit of translates of P, so its J-chains are bounded in the same way.
5. **Unique minimal set.** A minimal subset containing a point with a 1 would have syndetic 1-sets, i.e. unbounded chains, contradicting 4. So every minimal subset lies in Y ≅ X_α, which is minimal.
6. **Limit sets are not separated.** x ∉ Y and ω(x) = X meets Y. So Theorem D and Corollary E of sk-k2-subsystems do not apply.

**Conclusion.** By Corollary E′ with s = 1 and n ≥ 3:
- the normal subgroups of EL_n(R_X) correspond exactly to the closed invariant subsets of X;
- ker π_Y is locally finite;
- EL_n(R_X)/ker π_Y ≅ EL_n(R_{X_α}) is infinite, simple and Kazhdan.

## 6. Two splittings that allow accumulating minimal sets
**Proposition S (clopen invariant pieces).** Let Y ⊆ X be closed invariant and n ≥ 2. If X∖Y is a union of clopen invariant sets, then ker π_Y = E_n(I_Y).

*Proof.*
- For g ∈ ker π_Y, the coefficients of g − I and g^{-1} − I are supported in a clopen V ⊆ X∖Y. Finitely many clopen invariant U_i ⊆ X∖Y cover V.
- U = ⋃U_i is clopen and invariant, so e_U is central and g = (1−e_U)I_n + e_U g.
- e_U g is the image of g under the ring map R_X → e_U R_X = R_U, so it lies in EL_n(R_U).
- Writing it as a product of elementary matrices over R_U writes g as a product of e_ij(r) with r ∈ e_U R_X ⊆ I_Y. □

*Example.* Take α, α_1, α_2, … irrational with α_k → α, and X = X_α ∪ ⋃_k X_{α_k} (closed, because Sturmian subshifts vary continuously in irrational slopes).
- Each X_{α_k} is clopen in X, since the others accumulate only on X_α.
- So Proposition S gives ker π_{X_α} = E_n(I_{X_α}). The minimal subsets outside X_α accumulate on it, so Theorem D′ does not apply.

**Theorem R (equivariant retracts).** Let r : X → Y be continuous with r∘T = T∘r and r|_Y = id, and n ≥ 2. Then ker π_Y = E_n(R_X,I_Y), and EL_n(R_X) = ker π_Y ⋊ EL_n(R_Y). For n ≥ 3, K_2(n,R_X) → K_2(n,R_Y) is split onto.

*Proof.*
- f ↦ f∘r is an equivariant ring map LC(Y,F_2) → LC(X,F_2). With u ↦ u it gives s : R_Y → R_X with ρ_Y∘s = id.
- For g ∈ ker π_Y write g = ∏_t e_{i_tj_t}(r_t), and put r′_t = s(ρ_Y(r_t)) and d_t = r_t − r′_t ∈ I_Y. Then e(r_t) = e(r′_t)e(d_t).
- Moving the factors e(d_t) to the right conjugates them, so g ∈ (∏_t e(r′_t))·E_n(R_X,I_Y). But ∏_t e(r′_t) = EL_n(s)(π_Y(g)) = 1.
- EL_n(s) splits π_Y. St_n(s) splits St_n(R_X) → St_n(R_Y) and maps K_2(n,R_Y) into K_2(n,R_X). □

*Example.* Let S ⊆ {0,1}^Z be the closure of the periodic orbits of (0^k1)^∞, k ≥ 1. Put X = X_α × S and Y = X_α × {0^∞}.
- The minimal subsets of X_α × O((0^k1)^∞) accumulate on Y.
- (w,s) ↦ (w,0^∞) is an equivariant retraction, and X has no periodic points.

## 7. Correction to sk-k2-subsystems §7
- **The claim.** For "X the orbit closure of a recurrent, not uniformly recurrent point, with Y ⊊ X minimal", that section says corners e_V R e_V with x ∈ V have unbounded J-chains.
- **Why it fails.** By §1 this is false whenever Y ⊇ Min(X), for instance in §5.
- **What unbounded chains need.** A minimal subset of X outside Y that meets V.

## 8. What stays open
**The remaining case.**
- The minimal subsets of X outside Y accumulate on Y, so no Z as in Theorem D′ exists.
- X∖Y is not a union of clopen invariant sets.
- Y is not an equivariant retract.

Open claim: `accumulating-minimal-sets-el-restriction-kernel-elementary`.

**Attempts.**
- **Element-wise Z.**
  - For a given g one can take Z = W_{V,J}, the closed invariant set of points visiting V in every interval of length J. It is disjoint from Y.
  - The lifting step of Theorem D replaces g by g h^{-1}, whose coefficients carry the exponents of a factorization of g|_Z. So J grows, and minimal subsets outside Z can enter. The argument does not close.
- **Stable range.** Stably K_2(R_Y) = 0 (`binary-cantor-ring-stable-steinberg-group-centrally-closed`, unreviewed). So each g ∈ ker π_Y lies in E_m(R_X,I_Y) for some m ≥ n; whether m = n works is the question.
- **No counterexample found.** A counterexample needs an element of K_2(3,R_Y) outside the image of K_2(3,R_X). The only known nonzero unstable classes over F_2 come from K_2(3,F_2) = Z/2, and those lift through the scalars F_2 ⊆ R_X.

## 9. Model tests
- **X minimal.** Min(X) = {X}. Y = X gives a trivial kernel; Y = ∅ violates Y ⊇ Min(X), and indeed I_∅ = R_X is not ultramatricial. ✓
- **Two disjoint minimal sets, Y = M_1.** Proposition C′ fails, and indeed I_Y contains R_{M_2}, which is not locally finite. Theorem D′ with Z = M_2 applies. ✓
- **Y = {0^∞} inside the orbit closure of a point asymptotic to 0^∞ on both sides.** Y ⊇ Min(X), and Proposition C′ gives a locally finite kernel. The sandwich for other normal subgroups needs no periodic points. ✓
- **Over F_3.** Lemma B fails by the determinant, so F_2 is used. ✓
- **§5.** Claims 1–5 check recurrence, non-uniform recurrence and uniqueness of the minimal set. ✓
