# Rank-weighted transport for locally finite normal subgroups

Date: 2026-09-08. Mathematical proof; no Lean certification is asserted.

The conclusion below uses property (T) on the compression subgroup. It
does not prove the separate property-(T)-free Cairn goals. Its new input
is a uniform bound on the finite subgroup's active dimension, and its
analytic step uses bounded positive cutoffs rather than a displacement
cocycle or a claim about extending MF traces.

## 1. The hypothesis and theorem

Let G be countable, H<=G have property (T), and A be a locally finite
normal subgroup of G. Fix an element a in A of finite order m>=2 and
an increasing exhaustion A=union_j A_j by finite subgroups containing a.
Assume a constant C works in every finite-dimensional unitary
representation sigma of every A_j:

    dim((Fix sigma(A_j))^perp)
      <= C rank(sigma(a)-I).                              (1)

Let D_G(H) be the normal subgroup generated by

    [u c u^(-1),h],
    u H u^(-1)<=H,  c in C_G(H),  h in H.

**Theorem.** If a belongs to D_G(H), every homomorphism from G to a
norm matrix corona kills a.

Neither A nor G is assumed to have property (T). No finite generation
or uniform normal-generation bound on A is assumed. The uniformity in
(1) is essential to this proof; local finiteness alone is not claimed
to imply it.

## 2. Transport with an arbitrary scalar Hilbert norm

Suppose V_n:G->U(d_n) are operator-norm asymptotic representations and
r_n>0 are any positive numbers. Equip M_(d_n) with the Hilbert norm

    |x|_n = (Tr(x*x)/r_n)^(1/2).                          (2)

Conjugation by V_n(g) is unitary on this Hilbert space. Moreover,

    ||Ad(V_n(g))-Ad(V_n(k))|| <= 2||V_n(g)-V_n(k)||_op,   (3)

and the operator norms in (3) do not change when a coordinate Hilbert
norm is multiplied by a scalar. Consequently Ad(V_n(g)) defines an
exact representation of G in the norm matrix corona with dimensions
d_n^2.

The established claim `scaled-kazhdan-transport`, with weight w_n=r_n,
applies with these Hilbert spaces: if

    sup_n |x_n|_n < infinity,
    |Ad(V_n(h))x_n-x_n|_n -> 0  for every h in H,

then the same statements hold for Ad(V_n(u))x_n whenever uHu^-1<=H.
Here is the precise reason the normalization is harmless. The image P
of the Kazhdan projection in the d_n^2 norm corona satisfies

    Ad(V(u))* P Ad(V(u)) <= P.

Stable finiteness of that corona turns this inequality into equality.
Lift P to Hilbert-space orthogonal projections P_n. Approximation of
the Kazhdan projection by fixed finite group-algebra sums proves

    x_n is H-central in (2) iff |P_n x_n-x_n|_n -> 0

for every bounded sequence x_n in (2). The error of a fixed finite
sum is bounded by its operator norm times sup |x_n|_n, independently
of r_n. Finally ||[P_n,Ad(V_n(u))]||->0 proves transport.

The arbitrary-weight transport step is already present in
`GroupApproximation/Sofic/ScaledKazhdanTransport.lean`, theorem
`compressionSet_scaled_transport_both`. Its squared Frobenius-mass
bound with weight r_n is precisely boundedness in (2). This supplies
the applicable interface without imposing a matrix operator-norm
bound on a rescaled vector. The finite-cutoff construction and its
application below are handwritten mathematics, not a claim of a
Lean-certified proof of this new theorem.

## 3. Finite cutoffs in a surviving corona representation

Suppose rho:G->U(Q_d) has rho(a)!=1. Lift it to operator-norm
asymptotic representations V_n. After passing to a subsequence and
correcting on successively larger finite subgroups of A, arrange:

* V_n restricts to a genuine representation sigma_n of one finite
  subgroup containing every finite group used at coordinate n;
* T_n=V_n(a)=sigma_n(a), T_n^m=I, and
  r_n=rank(T_n-I)>0;
* finite multiplication and conjugacy errors required below tend to
  zero in operator norm.

These choices need no uniform stability constant over all finite
groups. For each fixed finite group, its finite-dimensional complex
group algebra lifts through the tail of a norm matrix corona. One can
prove this by correcting its finitely many Wedderburn matrix units.
Choose the finite group first and then choose a sufficiently late
coordinate. A diagonal subsequence handles the growing list of groups
and ambient words. Since a has finite order, its nonzero spectral
projection has a nonzero lift on an infinite subsequence. All finite
subgroup projections at a coordinate come from the *same* sigma_n.

For an increasing finite symmetric window F_n exhausting G, choose
finite groups

    K_0 <= K_1 <= ... <= K_N <= A,
    a in K_0,
    g K_i g^(-1) <= K_(i+1)  (g in F_n, i<N),           (4)

where N=N_n tends to infinity. Each K_i can be chosen from the given
exhaustion: normality puts the finitely many conjugates in A, and
local finiteness puts their union in a later finite stage. The exact
lift sigma_n is taken on a finite stage containing K_N.

Set

    p_i=I-(1/|K_i|) sum_(b in K_i) sigma_n(b),
    e_n=(1/N) sum_(i=0)^(N-1) p_i.                     (5)

The p_i are increasing orthogonal projections. Since (1) applies at
each chosen exhaustion stage,

    0<=e_n<=I,  rank(e_n)<=C r_n,
    e_n p_a=p_a,  p_a=1_(spectrum(T_n)\{1})(T_n).       (6)

In particular |e_n|_n<=sqrt(C). Exact finite-group averaging and the
operator-norm conjugacy errors give, for g in F_n,

    V_n(g) p_i V_n(g)* <= p_(i+1)+epsilon_n I,
    p_i <= V_n(g) p_(i+1) V_n(g)*+epsilon_n I.           (7)

The second inequality uses g^(-1) in the symmetric window; choose
epsilon_n to include the inverse and covariance errors. Finite
averages have coefficient l1-norm one, so the bound does not acquire
a group-order multiplier. Sum (7) and telescope the increasing
projections to obtain

    ||V_n(g)e_n V_n(g)*-e_n||_op <= 1/N+epsilon_n.       (8)

The missing first or last projection in either telescope is bounded
between zero and I. Regardless of the ranks of the covariance
errors, the *actual* matrix in (8) has rank at most 2C r_n: both
summands have rank at most C r_n. Therefore

    |[V_n(g),e_n]|_n
       <= sqrt(2C)(1/N+epsilon_n) -> 0                 (9)

for every fixed g in G. This is the essential conversion. Applying
the ambient estimate sqrt(d_n/r_n)||.||_op would lose the argument.

## 4. The weighted null subgroup

Define

    q_n(g)=|(V_n(g)-I)e_n|_n,
    N_e={g in G : q_n(g)->0}.

Every occurrence of an operator-norm multiplication error in the
following calculations is multiplied by e_n. Its weighted norm is
therefore at most sqrt(C) times the operator-norm error. It is never
bounded by sqrt(d_n/r_n) times that error.

Expanding V_n(g)V_n(k)-I shows

    q_n(gk)<=q_n(g)+q_n(k)+o(1).

The analogous inverse expansion gives q_n(g^-1)<=q_n(g)+o(1).
For conjugation, use (9) to move e_n past V_n(k)*. The additional
error has norm at most 2|[V_n(k)*,e_n]|_n. Unitary invariance then
gives

    q_n(kgk^-1)<=q_n(g)+o(1).

Thus N_e is a normal subgroup of G.

Let uHu^-1<=H and c commute with H. The vector sequence

    x_n=V_n(c)e_n

is bounded in (2), and H-central by (9), the exact group relation
[c,h]=1, and operator-norm asymptotic multiplicativity. Section 2
transports x_n by u. Equations (9) and the multiplication relations
identify the transported sequence, up to an error tending to zero
in (2), with

    V_n(u c u^-1)e_n.

Its H-centrality, together with (9) once more, yields

    |(V_n(h)V_n(u c u^-1)-V_n(u c u^-1)V_n(h))e_n|_n -> 0.

Multiply by the relevant unitary inverses and use (9). This proves
[u c u^-1,h] in N_e. Hence D_G(H)<=N_e.

But e_n is the identity on the nontrivial spectral subspace of T_n.
If eta_m=min{|zeta-1|:zeta^m=1,zeta!=1}, then (6) gives

    q_n(a)^2
      =Tr((T_n-I)*(T_n-I))/r_n >= eta_m^2>0.            (10)

For an involution it is exactly 4. This contradicts a in D_G(H),
and proves the theorem.

## 5. An elementary source of the uniform bound

For the binary finitary group the constant can be C=4, without a
character-table theorem. Let K=SL_d(F_2), d>=3, and a=e_12(1).
Let U be the row-one root group and V the column-one root group.
They generate K: the missing roots are commutators
[e_i1(x),e_1j(y)]=e_ij(xy), i!=j.

The Levi SL_(d-1)(F_2) acts transitively on nonzero characters of U.
Thus their multiplicities in any unitary representation of K are
equal, say b. Exactly 2^(d-2) of those characters are nontrivial on
a. Consequently

    codim Fix U=(2^(d-1)-1)b,
    rank(sigma(a)-I)=2^(d-2)b.

The same calculation applies to V. Its chosen transvection is
conjugate to a, so its moving rank is the same. The subgroups U and
V themselves need not be conjugate; that assertion is unnecessary.
Since Fix K=Fix U intersection Fix V,

    codim Fix K <= (4-2^(3-d)) rank(sigma(a)-I)<4 rank(sigma(a)-I).

The proof works for reducible representations, including arbitrary
trivial summands. The companion composite-modulus audit extends the
same bound to EL_d(Z/q).

## 6. The binary Jacobson consequence

Let J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>, Q=1-ST, E=EL_5(J), and a=x_13(Q).
The established exact symbol sequence identifies a normal locally
finite subgroup A=SL_fin(N x {1,...,5},F_2); a is one of its ordinary
transvections. Exhaust it by standard finite special linear groups.
Section 5 gives (1).

The manuscript's rank-four compression cell, extended by the identity
on coordinate five, places a in D_E(H), H=EL_3(J). Specifically its
u,c satisfy uHu^-1<=H, c in C_E(H), and

    [u c u^-1,x_23(1)]=x_13(Q).

H has property (T), since J is finitely generated. The theorem kills
a in every norm-corona homomorphism. The established Jacobson
radical dichotomy then selects its nontrivial branch:

    Rad_MF(EL_5(J))=SL_fin(N x {1,...,5},F_2).

The quotient is EL_5(F_2[z,z^-1]), and every map to an MF target
factors through this quotient. The existing identification of St_5(J)
with EL_5(J) gives the same answer for the Steinberg target.

This resolves that previously open Jacobson target using Kazhdan
transport. It does not satisfy a provenance condition excluding
property (T), even though the head-root conclusion is the same.
