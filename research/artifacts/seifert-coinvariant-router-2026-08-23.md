# Seifert coinvariant routing as a Cairn gadget pattern

Date: 2026-08-23

Source: user-provided manuscript `s6.pdf`, *A compact complex threefold fibred by tori over the projective line, and the six-sphere.*  The source-backed statements below are taken from Lemma 2.7, Remark 6.8, Lemma 7.16 and Theorem 7.17 (especially pp. 10, 40, 55--57 of the uploaded PDF).  The sections labelled **Cairn inference** are not claims made by the manuscript.

## 1. Source-backed algebraic mechanism

The manuscript starts with an explicit rank-four lattice `Lambda` carrying the contragredient monodromy generated by `A_1,A_2`, with an invariant integral functional

```text
gamma : Lambda -> Z.
```

Lemma 2.7 proves the unusually strong collapse

```text
sum_g im(A(g)-1) = ker(gamma),
Lambda_G = Lambda / ker(gamma) ~= Z,
```

and, more strongly for the filling argument, the smallest `<A_1,A_2>`-invariant subgroup containing the cusp vanishing lattice `Lambda_tor` is exactly `ker(gamma)`.  Equivalently the combined matrix `[(A_1-I) | (A_2-I)]` has Smith normal form `diag(1,1,1,0)`.  Thus three integral directions are forced into the relation closure and one primitive coordinate survives.

The three local gluings carry translation data whose only discrete contribution after this collapse is

```text
ell_j = gamma(v_j),
ell_0 in Z,
```

and Remark 6.8 states that `(ell_0,ell_1,ell_2)` is the complete list of discrete gluing data relevant to the topology.  Transcendental period constants do not affect this integer router.

Van Kampen then gives the presentation

```text
<c,x,y |
   c central,
   x y = c^ell_0,
   x^3 = c^ell_1,
   y^4 = c^ell_2>.
```

Theorem 7.17 proves that this group is abelian and

```text
pi_1(X) ~= Z / |p| Z,
p = 12 ell_0 - 4 ell_1 - 3 ell_2,
gcd(p,12)=1
```

for admissible twists.  The proof eliminates `y` and reads the order from the determinant of the two remaining relations in `(x,c)`.

The sign is not a bookkeeping accident.  Lemma 7.16 shows that the order-3 and order-4 elliptic generators must rotate in the same sense because their product has to close to the unique cusp; the two local twist signs therefore synchronize globally.  With the tautological cusp gluing,

```text
(v_1,v_2) = (epsilon,-epsilon')  -> |p| = |4-3| = 1,
(v_1,v_2) = (epsilon,+epsilon')  -> |p| = |4+3| = 7.
```

So one sign flip changes the residual fundamental group from trivial to `Z/7` without changing the ambient monodromy representation.

## 2. Extracted algebraic lemma

The topology is not needed for the final determinant calculation.  For coprime positive integers `m,n`, consider

```text
R(m,n;e,a,b) =
<c,x,y | c central, x y = c^e, x^m = c^a, y^n = c^b>.
```

Since `y=x^{-1}c^e`, the group is generated by the commuting pair `(x,c)` with relation matrix

```text
[ m   -a     ]
[ n   b-ne   ].
```

Because `gcd(m,n)=1`, the first Smith divisor is one.  Hence, when

```text
D = m n e - n a - m b
```

is nonzero, `R(m,n;e,a,b) ~= Z/|D|`; when `D=0`, it is infinite cyclic.  The manuscript is the case `(m,n)=(3,4)`.

This is recorded separately in Cairn as `two-cone-central-router-determinant` so later arguments can use the arithmetic gadget without importing the complex geometry.

## 3. Cairn inference: global coupling after local sectors

The strongest connection is to `central-word-state-face-compiler`.  That frontier explicitly says the remaining positive route must add a **global coupling** which excludes locally phase-compatible forbidden sectors only after contexts are assembled.  The Seifert mechanism is an existence proof for a very specific kind of global coupling architecture:

1. keep several local affine twist parameters;
2. act on a larger lattice so the normal closure kills all nuisance directions;
3. retain one primitive coinvariant `gamma` as the distinguished mark;
4. make the global closure relation combine the local twists into one determinant.

This does **not** solve the nonlinear state-face compiler: the Seifert router is integral and affine, while `central-word-state-face-compiler` needs a coupling that removes nonlinear forbidden central characters without destroying the desired tracial face.  The useful lesson is architectural: do not try to forbid each bad local sector separately if one can first quotient the nuisance coordinates and then test one global primitive defect.

A concrete experiment for that frontier is therefore to search for a finite central-word construction with a module `L`, a distinguished `gamma:L->Z/q`, and context actions `A_c` such that

```text
normal_closure(sum_c im(A_c-I) + nuisance) = ker(gamma).
```

Then all clause-sector ambiguity would be forced into one marked cyclic coordinate before the final phase relation is imposed.  The hard part remains making the surviving `gamma` distinguish exactly the nonlinear allowed face.

## 4. Cairn inference: source-specific pointed rank routing

The same pattern is relevant to `source-specific-pointed-rank-router-face`.  That claim currently asks for a global source face that permits separate rank cells while preventing tensor-closure pollution.  The Seifert example suggests a different way to think about the word “global”:

- do not encode every local degree of freedom faithfully;
- arrange an action whose relation closure annihilates all locally movable directions;
- preserve only one primitive source coordinate;
- route every local correction to that coordinate and test a single global compatibility equation.

This is a **quotient-first router**, whereas the present pointed-rank route is phrased as a face-selection problem.  It may be useful to ask whether the global row-clock moments can be made to play the role of `gamma`: not by being central-character data themselves, but by being the only statistic invariant under all permitted local source-gauge moves.

Again, the manuscript gives no matrix-microstate rounding theorem and no non-tensor-closure theorem.  It only supplies a sharp finite-rank model of the quotient architecture.

## 5. Cairn inference: a phase-survival toggle

The `1` versus `Z/7` comparison is more than a curiosity.  It gives a clean design target for marked central defects:

```text
same ambient action + same local orders + one routed sign
    -> marked defect killed,
or  -> marked defect survives with prescribed finite order.
```

In approximation problems, this suggests building paired exact presentations that differ only in one twist relation and then asking whether the approximation invariant sees the survival of the central mark.  Because the source determinant is explicit, the algebraic side can be certified by Smith normal form rather than by a global normal-form argument.

The limitation is equally useful: the determinant only controls the ordinary quotient group.  Nothing in the manuscript implies MF, hyperlinear, sofic, tracial, or finite-dimensional-representation behavior is preserved under an analogous attachment.  Any such transfer needs a separate Cairn claim.

## 6. Cairn inference: synchronized signs as a coherence gadget

Lemma 7.16 is a compact example of **global sign synchronization**.  The two local rotations cannot choose their orientations independently because the product must be parabolic and generate the unique cusp.  This is structurally close to several Cairn phase-coherence problems where local sign choices look independent until a global relation is imposed.

Reusable design principle:

```text
local phase choices
  + one global product relation constrained to a rigid conjugacy class
  => synchronized local signs.
```

This is potentially useful wherever a construction currently spends separate relations forcing multiple phase conventions.  A single rigid “cusp” word may be able to synchronize them instead.  The source proves this only for the `(3,4,infinity)` triangle group; a finite-group or presentation-level analogue would need its own proof.

## 7. Secondary structural connections

Two further source facts may matter later.

- The cusp map `B_0 : Lambda/Lambda_tor -> Lambda_tor` is unimodular.  This is what prevents multiplicity in the toric quotient; the manuscript remarks that determinant `k>1` would produce `k` components.  Algebraically, unimodularity is acting as a no-hidden-index condition.  In a finite presentation gadget, the analogous check is that the nuisance lattice is killed *saturatedly*, not merely over `Q`.
- The unique primitive invariant alternating form has nilpotent pairing of signature `(1,1)`, not a definite polarization.  This is why the construction lives outside the polarized-abelian setting.  For Cairn this is a warning against importing positivity assumptions into a router that only needs integral monodromy and saturation.

## 8. Immediate research questions created by the connection

These are leads, not established consequences.

1. Can a finite central-word compiler be organized as a module action with rank-one coinvariants, so forbidden local selector sectors differ only in the surviving `gamma` coordinate?
2. Can the final nonlinear predicate be moved from local central characters into a determinant/Smith-normal-form condition on several globally coupled twists?
3. Is there a finite presentation analogue of the Seifert sign lemma in which one rigid product word forces all local phase conventions to agree?
4. Can one construct a paired “kill/survive” family, analogous to the source's `1` versus `Z/7`, whose surviving mark is invisible to the unwanted approximation class but visible to the desired tracial representation?
5. In any such attempt, is the relation closure saturated integrally?  The source's Smith form `diag(1,1,1,0)` and unimodular `B_0` show exactly where torsion/index errors would otherwise enter.
