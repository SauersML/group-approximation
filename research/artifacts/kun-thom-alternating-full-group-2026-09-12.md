## 0. Setting and imports

`(G, Gamma)` is the pair of Kun--Thom Theorem E (`kun-thom-nonsofic-wreath`): `R_+ = F_q[x_1..x_d]`,
`R = F_q[x_1^(+-1)..x_d^(+-1)]`, `Gamma = EL_r(R_+)`, `G = EL_r(R) semidirect SL_d(Z)`, `r, d >= 3`.
Put `X = G/Gamma`, `N = directSum_X Z/2`, `W = N semidirect G`.

`W` acts on the Cantor set `C = {0,1}^X` by `(n,g).x = n + g.x`, `(g.x)(s) = x(g^-1 s)`.

For a Cantor `W`-space `Y`:

- `[[Y]]` is the topological full group: homeomorphisms `h` of `Y` with a finite clopen partition
  `Y = U_1 ⊔ ... ⊔ U_k` and `w_i in W` such that `h = w_i` on `U_i`.
- `[[Y]]_U` is the subgroup of elements that are the identity off a clopen `U`.
- `A(Y)` is Nekrashevych's alternating full group, generated by the images of `Alt(d)` on multisections.
  A multisection is disjoint clopens `U_1..U_d` with pieces `F_ij` of elements of `[[Y]]` mapping `U_j` onto `U_i`,
  `F_ii = id`, `F_ij F_jk = F_ik`. A permutation `pi` acts by `F_(pi(j) j)` on `U_j` and by the identity elsewhere.

**Elementary fact (E).** `A(Y)` is normal in `[[Y]]`: conjugating a multisection by `h in [[Y]]` gives the multisection
`h(U_i)`, `h F_ij h^-1`.

**Imported, not re-read from source.** The source is not on this machine; these statements are recalled.
- (N1) Nekrashevych, *Simple groups of dynamical origin*, Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 39 (2019): if the groupoid of
  germs of the action is minimal, then `A(Y)` is simple.
- (N2) Same paper: if the action is expansive and orbits are infinite, then `A(Y)` is finitely generated.
- The exact hypotheses of (N1) and (N2) (effectiveness, Hausdorffness, orbit sizes, compact generation) must be checked
  against the paper. No established node below depends on (N1) or (N2).

## 1. The actions meet the dynamical hypotheses

Let `K` be a normal subgroup of finite index in `G` with `|G/K| >= 3`. It exists because `G` is infinite and
residually finite. Put `Q_0 = G/K` and `Y = C x Q_0`, with `(n,g).(x,p) = (n + g.x, g p)`.

**Lemma 1.1.**
1. *Minimal.* Every `W`-orbit is dense in `C` and in `Y`.
2. *Expansive.* The partition `{x(s_0) = a} x {p}`, with `s_0 = Gamma`, separates points under translates.
3. *Orbits are infinite.*
4. *Kernel.* The kernel of `W` on `C` is `core_G(Gamma)`; on `Y` it is `Z = core_G(Gamma) ∩ K`.

*Proof.*
1. Lamps change any finite set of coordinates, so `N.x` is dense in `C`. On `Y`, pick `g` with `g p = p'`.
   Then `N g.(x,p)` is dense in `C x {p'}`.
2. If `x(s) != y(s)` with `s = h s_0`, then `h^-1.x` and `h^-1.y` differ at `s_0`. Layers are separated directly.
3. `N.x` is infinite.
4. `(n,g)` acts trivially only if `n = 0` (take `x = 0`) and `g` fixes every site.

**Lemma 1.2.** The image `W/Z` is nonsofic.

*Proof.* `W/Z = (Z/2) wr_(G/Gamma) (G/Z)`, because `Z <= Gamma` acts trivially on `X`. The pair `(G/Z, Gamma/Z)` meets
Theorem A: (T) passes to quotients; the image of the compression semigroup `P_Gamma` compresses `Gamma/Z` and generates
`G/Z`; and `Gamma/Z` is normal in `G/Z` only if `Gamma` is normal in `G`. QED

## 2. What lies in A(C), and where fragmentation stops

**Lemma 2.1 (rigid stabilizers).** Let `U` be clopen and suppose some `q in A(Y)` has `q(U) ∩ U = ∅`.
Then `[[Y]]_U' <= A(Y)`.

*Proof.* Take `a, b in [[Y]]_U`.
- `[b,q] = (b q b^-1) q^-1` lies in `A(Y)` by (E).
- It equals `b` on `U` and `q b^-1 q^-1` on `q(U)`, and these supports are disjoint.
- `a` commutes with the second factor, so `[a,[b,q]] = [a,b]`.
- `[a,[b,q]]` lies in `A(Y)` by (E). QED

**Lemma 2.2 (lamps).** `delta_s in A(C)` for every site `s`.

*Proof.* Fix distinct sites `t, u, v` different from `s`.
- *Moving pieces off themselves.* The cylinders `[00], [01], [10]` at `(t,u)` form a multisection with pieces
  `delta_u`, `delta_t`, `delta_t delta_u`. Its 3-cycle lies in `A(C)` and moves each of them off itself; `[11]` is
  handled the same way.
- *Restricted lamps are commutators.* On each such cylinder `V`, the flips at `(s,v)` restricted to `V` give
  `Alt(4) <= [[C]]_V`, permuting the four cylinders `V ∩ [cd]_(s,v)`. Its commutator subgroup is the Klein group of
  translations, which contains `delta_s|_V`.
- By Lemma 2.1, `delta_s|_V in A(C)`, and `delta_s` is the product of the four restrictions. QED

**Lemma 2.3 (stabilizers).** `[G_s, G_s] <= A(C)` for every site `s`, where `G_s = Stab_G(s)`. So `EL_r(R) <= A(C)`.

*Proof.*
- *Restriction.* `g in G_s` preserves `U_a = [a]_s` for `a in {0,1}`, and `g -> g|_(U_a)` is a homomorphism
  `G_s -> [[C]]_(U_a)`. So `[G_s,G_s]|_(U_a) <= [[C]]_(U_a)'`.
- *Inclusion.* `delta_s in A(C)` swaps `U_0` and `U_1`, so Lemma 2.1 applies.
- *Perfect stabilizers.* `G_s` is conjugate to `Gamma = EL_r(R_+)`, which is perfect for `r >= 3`
  (`x_ij(a) = [x_ik(a), x_kj(1)]`).
- *Generation.* For `v in Z^d` there are `A in SL_d(Z)` and `w in N^d` with `A w = v`, because `SL_d(Z)` is
  transitive on primitive vectors. So `x_ij(c x^v) = A x_ij(c x^w) A^-1`, and the conjugates of `Gamma` generate
  `EL_r(R)`. QED

**Proposition 2.4.** `A(C)` contains the image of `N semidirect EL_r(R)`. The following are equivalent:
1. `A(C)` contains the image of `[W,W]`;
2. `A(C)` contains the image of `W`;
3. `A(C)` contains the image of `SL_d(Z)`.

*Proof.* `G` is perfect: `EL_r(R)` and `SL_d(Z)` are perfect for `r, d >= 3`. `X` is transitive, so `[N,G]` is the
augmentation kernel `N_0`. Hence `[W,W] = N_0 semidirect G`, and all three conditions amount to `G <= A(C)` given
Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3. QED

**Proposition 2.5 (fragmentation stops at SL_d(Z)).**
1. Every `g in G` with nontrivial image in `SL_d(Z)` fixes no site.
2. If that image has infinite order, `g` preserves no clopen set other than `∅` and `C`.

*Proof.*
1. `Stab_G(h Gamma) = h Gamma h^-1 <= EL_r(R)`, which is normal.
2. A clopen `U` has a smallest finite determining coordinate set `D(U)`, and `D(gU) = g D(U)`. If `g^k` fixed a site,
   then `g^k in EL_r(R)` and the image of `g` would have finite order. So every `<g>`-orbit on `X` is infinite, and a
   `g`-invariant `U` has `D(U) = ∅`. QED

So such `g` lie in no product of rigid stabilizers, and the argument of Lemma 2.3 cannot place them. Whether
`SL_d(Z) <= A(C)` remains open, so the conditional in w3-free-pos's candidate is neither confirmed nor refuted for
`C`.

Matui's equality of `A` with `[[·]]'` for almost finite or purely infinite groupoids is not available, as recalled.
The uniform measure is invariant, so the action is not purely infinite, and `W` is nonamenable. Section 3 removes the
question by changing the space.

## 3. The corrected candidate is nonsofic

Put `Q' = A(Y)` with `Y = C x Q_0` as in Section 1, and `L_p = C x {p}` for `p in Q_0`.

**Proposition 3.1.** `Q'` contains the image of `N semidirect [K,K]`. That image has finite index in `W/Z`, so `Q'` is
nonsofic.

*Proof.*
- **Moving layers.** Take distinct `p, p', p''` and `g', g''` with `g' p = p'` and `g'' p = p''`. The pieces
  `g'|_(L_p)`, `g''|_(L_p)` and `g'' g'^-1|_(L_p')` form a multisection on `L_p, L_p', L_p''`. Its 3-cycle `q_p`
  lies in `Q'` and moves `L_p`, and every subset of it, off itself.
- **Lamps.** The proof of Lemma 2.2 runs inside each layer: every multisection and every `Alt(4)` it uses lives in
  `L_p`. So `delta_s|_(L_p) in Q'`, and `delta_s`, the product over `p`, lies in `Q'`. So `N <= Q'`.
- **The normal subgroup.** `K` is normal, so each `k in K` fixes every `p` and preserves every layer. Restriction
  `K -> [[Y]]_(L_p)` is a homomorphism, so `[K,K]|_(L_p) <= [[Y]]_(L_p)' <= Q'` by Lemma 2.1 with `q = q_p`. Taking
  the product over `p` gives `[K,K] <= Q'`.
- **Finite index.** `K` has finite index in the Kazhdan group `G`, so `K` has (T) and `K^ab` is finite. Then
  `[G : [K,K]] = [G:K] |K^ab|` is finite, and so is the index of the image of `N semidirect [K,K]` in `W/Z`.
- **Nonsofic.** Suppose `Q'` were sofic. Then the image `H` of `N semidirect [K,K]` is sofic, and so is its normal
  core `H_0` in `W/Z`, which has finite index. A finite extension of a sofic group is sofic (amenable extensions of
  sofic groups are sofic, Elek--Szabo 2006). So `W/Z` would be sofic, contradicting Lemma 1.2. QED
