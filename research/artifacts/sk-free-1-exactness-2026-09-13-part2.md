# sk-free-1: exactness, part 2: the universal recursive LEF host is not exact (2026-09-13)

Part 1: `research/artifacts/sk-free-1-exactness-2026-09-13-part1.md` (Theorem E1).

## 1. Theorem E1′ (established, unreviewed)
The host of `simple-kazhdan-lef-host-for-recursive-lef-groups` is not exact: one infinite f.g. simple Kazhdan LEF group containing every recursively presented f.g. LEF group. That host node is reviewed PASS by sk-verify-7, -8 and -11.

**Proof.**
1. `recursive-residually-finite-non-exact-groups-exist` (§2) gives a recursively presented f.g. residually finite group Γ without property A.
2. Residually finite implies LEF (part 1, §1 step 2), so Γ lies in the host.
3. The inclusion of a f.g. subgroup is a coarse equivalence onto its image, and property A passes to subspaces (Willett, part 1 §3). So the host has no property A.
4. For countable groups, no property A means not exact (part 1 §3). ∎

## 2. Osajda's residually finite non-exact groups can be chosen recursively presented
**Claim.** Osajda's construction (arXiv:1703.03791, §3 "The construction") produces, from a recursive input sequence, a recursive sequence of finite labelled graphs (Θ̂_i, l̂_i). So the graphical presentation ⟨S | Θ̂_1, Θ̂_2, …⟩ has a recursively enumerable relator set, and the group is recursively presented.

### Input: recursive, by Osajda 2014
The construction fixes λ ∈ (0,1/24], D ≥ 3, and a sequence of D-regular labelled graphs "with a labelling $l$ satisfying the following stronger version of $C'(\lambda)$--small cancellation: every path in $\Theta_i$ of length greater or equal to $\lambda \mr{girth}(\Theta_i)$ has labelling different from any other path. Such sequences are constructed in \cite{Osajda-Monster}." (1703.03791, l.313–315).

In Osajda-Monster (arXiv:1406.5015, `sclsiga200514.tex`):
- **§ s:mLLL (l.402ff).** It builds the labelling l′_n in which distinct relatively long paths have distinct labellings, by the Lovász Local Lemma.
- **Remark r:recurs (l.695–705), verbatim:** "Observe that, for every $n$, a given finite labelling $(\txT_1,l_1), (\txT_2,l_2),\\ \ldots, (\txT_{n},l_n)$ from Subsection~\ref{s:LLL} can be extended to $(\txT_{n+1},l_{n+1})$ by using the brute force algorithm (see Lemma~\ref{l:scLLL}). The same holds for the labelling $(\txT,l')$ from Subsection~\ref{s:mLLL}. Therefore, if the sequence $\txT$ of finite graphs is recursive, the small cancellation labelling $(\txT,m)$, as well as the resulting small cancellation presentation (see Section~\ref{s:grexp}) are recursive. … Recursive sequences of finite graphs $\txT$ (satisfying our assumptions from the beginning of Section~\ref{s:constr}) exist. Examples are expander graphs given by Cayley graphs of some finite linear groups".
- **l.878:** the presentation "is also recursive -- the brute force algorithm can be used to find the labelling".

So a recursive input sequence (Θ_i, l_i) with the stronger small cancellation property exists.

### Each inductive step of 1703.03791 (l.346–412) is a terminating search
1. **Basis (l.341–359).** Θ̂_1 is the Z_2-homology cover of Θ_1, a finite computation. φ_1 : G_1 → F_1 injective on the 1-ball: search over finite groups and assignments of S (a fuller account appears under step 6).
2. **H_i (l.375)** is generated by the words read along cycles of the finite graph Θ_{i+1}. A finite generating set comes from a spanning tree.
3. **K_i = ⋂_{j≤i} ker φ_i^j (l.375)** is given by finitely many maps to finite groups.
4. **The cover Θ̄_{i+1} (l.376–378).** The paper asserts that a finite normal cover exists with the normal closure of its cycle words inside K_i. Enumerate finite normal covers and check that a finite generating set of cycle words maps trivially to every F_j (K_i is normal, so this is decidable). The search terminates because a cover exists.
5. **No choices for (A_{i+1}) and (B_{i+1}) (l.379–391).** The small cancellation and walling properties of Θ̄_{i+1} and of its Z_2-homology cover Θ̂_{i+1} follow from the stronger input property and are proved, not chosen. Θ̂_{i+1} is computable.
6. **φ_{i+1} (l.395–399).** G_{i+1} is finitely presented and residually finite (Wise–Agol, l.395–396). So its word problem is uniformly solvable by McKinsey's algorithm: enumerate consequences of the relators in parallel with homomorphisms to finite groups. The ball B_{i+1} is computable. Search over finite groups F and maps S → F that satisfy the finitely many relators and are nontrivial on B_{i+1}∖{1}. The search terminates because such a map exists (residual finiteness, l.397–399).
7. **φ_{i+1}^j for j ≤ i (l.404)** is defined by composition, a finite computation.

Every step is effective, so i ↦ (Θ̂_i, l̂_i) is recursive and G = ⟨S | Θ̂_1, Θ̂_2, …⟩ is recursively presented. G is residually finite and has no property A by the paper's own proof (l.420–437): its Cayley graph contains D-regular graphs of growing girth isometrically, and Willett 2011 gives no property A.

## 3. Scope
- **Not checked at source:** McKinsey's algorithm and the Wise–Agol import are standard, and the latter is quoted from the paper. The statement of Lemma l:mLLL itself was not opened (only §s:mLLL's opening paragraph and Remark r:recurs).
- **Why E1′ matters for the note:** the note's universal host is not exact. That is a precise C*-side boundary for the universal host, while the Z-family question stays open (`subshift-elementary-groups-are-exact`).
