# The Tate diagonal and the wreath object `Y`, part 2: where `Y` vanishes, the split, and fences

Lane ex3-uct-tate-diagonal, 2026-09-13. Unreviewed. The notation is that of part 1: `W = Γ ≀ G`,
`N = Γ^p`, `H = ΔΓ × <σ>`, `ε = [ℓ²(W/H)]`, `Y = cone(ε) ⊗ q^*1_T`, `Z_Γ = cone(T(Δ))`.

## 5. Proposition B: `Y` vanishes on every subgroup one can see

Let `Γ` be torsion-free hyperbolic, so that centralizers of nontrivial elements are cyclic. Then
`Res^W_K Y ≃ 0` in `KK^K` for each of the following:
- (a) every finite `K`;
- (b) `K ⊆ N`;
- (c) every amenable `K`;
- (d) every `K ⊆ wHw^(−1)`.

So `KK^W(Ind_K^W B, Y) = 0` for all such `K` and all `B`, by Frobenius reciprocity for open
subgroups.

*Proof.*
- (a) and (b) are part 1, Theorem A.1.
- (c) `Res_K Y` is weakly contractible by (a). `K` is amenable, so it vanishes by Meyer–Nest Thm. 8.5.
- (d) Conjugate so that `K ⊆ H`. As an `H`-algebra, `C_0(W/H)` is the `c_0`-sum over `H`-orbits.
  The orbit of `wH` has stabilizer `K_w = H ∩ C_W(wσw^(−1))`.
  - The trivial orbit contributes `id`.
  - For `w ∉ H`, write `wσw^(−1) = cσ` with `c` non-constant, as in the proof of part 1, Lemma 1.
  - `(δ, …, δ)σ^k ∈ K_w` iff `δ c_(i−k) δ^(−1) = c_i` for all `i`.
    - So `K_w ∩ ΔΓ` is the centralizer of the nontrivial set `{c_i}`, which is cyclic.
    - `K_w / (K_w ∩ ΔΓ)` embeds in `G`.
    - A torsion element `(δ)σ^k` with `k ≠ 0` forces `δ = 1` and hence `c` constant, which is excluded.
  - So `K_w` is torsion-free and virtually cyclic, hence `Z` or `1`.
  - The summand `C_0(H/K_w) ⊗ q^*1_T = Ind_(K_w)^H Res_(K_w) q^*1_T` vanishes by (c).
  - So `Res_H(ε ⊗ 1)` is an equivalence and its cone vanishes. ∎

**Consequence (generation form).** Let `ℱ` be the family generated by the subgroups in (a)–(d). If
`q^*1_T` lies in the localizing subcategory `<Ind_K^W B : K ∈ ℱ>`, then `Y = Y ⊗ q^*1_T ≃ 0`, and
the Tate diagonal is a `KK^G`-equivalence. For this, use that `Ind_K^W(B) ⊗ Y ≅ Ind_K^W(B ⊗ Res_K Y) ≃ 0`
and that the class of objects `X` with `X ⊗ Y ≃ 0` is localizing. For Kazhdan `W` no geometric
resolution of `q^*1_T` by such cells is available. `W` has property (FA), so Pimsner-type tree
resolutions degenerate. Any proof of this kind must build a non-geometric resolution.

## 6. The split into a crossed-product question and a phantom question

**Proposition C.** `T(Δ)` is a `KK^G`-equivalence iff both of the following hold:
- (P1) `W ⋉_r Y ≃_KK 0`, i.e. the inclusion `1_T ⋊_r (ΔΓ × G) → 1_T ⋊_r (Γ ≀ G)` is a
  KK-equivalence. This is a non-equivariant statement.
- (P2) `1_T ⊗ C*_r(Γ^p)` has zero phantom part, i.e. the shift is trivial on it in `KK^G`.

*Proof.* `Res Z_Γ ≃ 0`. By `zp-restriction-trivial-splits-cellular-plus-phantom`, item 3,
`Z_Γ ≅ eZ_Γ ⊕ (1 − e)Z_Γ`.
- `eZ_Γ` is a retract of `τ(Z_Γ ⋊ G)`, and `Z_Γ ⋊ G ≅ W ⋉_r Y` by part 1, Theorem A.2. So
  `eZ_Γ ≃ 0` iff (P1).
- The phantom part `(1 − e)Z_Γ` equals the phantom part of `T(C*_r Γ)`, by part 3, Lemma 2
  (phantom transfer), since the source of `T(Δ)` has `θ = 1`. So `(1 − e)Z_Γ ≃ 0` iff (P2).
- Conversely, `Z_Γ ≃ 0` forces both. ∎

(P2) is `tate-norm-shift-is-kkg-trivial` at `A = C*_r Γ`. (P1) involves no `G`-equivariance.

## 7. Fences (approaches that die, and where)

- **(f1) Maximal crossed products.**
  - The Kazhdan projection `p ∈ C*_max Γ` is central, so `C*_max Γ = Cp ⊕ J`.
  - In `C*_max(Γ^p) = ⊗^p (Cp ⊕ J)`, words in free `G`-orbits are induced and die after `⊗1_T`.
  - The maximal Tate-diagonal crossed product becomes lower triangular: the identity on
    `1_T ⋊ G`, then `J ⊗ (1_T ⋊ G) → (J^(⊗p) ⊗ 1_T) ⋊ G`, plus an off-diagonal term from the
    `J^(⊗p)`-component of `Δ(p)`. That component is nonzero: for `p = 2`, `ρ ⊗ ρ̄` has diagonal
    invariants.
  - So the maximal version reduces to the `J`-part, and the Kazhdan projection supplies no integer
    invariant there. Dies.
- **(f2) Banach KK.**
  - Lafforgue's `γ = 1` in Banach `KK` for hyperbolic groups yields Baum–Connes with coefficients,
    here quoted as Nishikawa–Petrosyan Thm. 5.1; only that statement was read.
  - That controls `K_*(W ⋉_r (Y ⊗ B))`, which is part 1, Theorem A.4. Banach descent lands in
    K-theory of Banach crossed products, not in `KK(W ⋉_r Y, W ⋉_r Y)`. Dies at the KK-level.
- **(f3) Norm sum formula.**
  - Write `e = j_r(γ_Γ)` and `f = 1 − e` on `D = C*_r Γ`.
  - If `N(x + y) = N(x) + N(y) + (transfer terms)` held on `KK(D, D)`, then after `⊗1_T` we would get
    `T(D) ≅ T(D)_(N e) ⊕ T(D)_(N f)`. The diagonal intertwines `e` with `N(e)`, by naturality of
    descent along `ΔΓ ≤ Γ^p`.
  - The question would then reduce to the Skandalis summand `D_f` alone, as the statement
    "`1_T ⊗ τ(D_f) → 1_T ⊗ N(f)N(D)` is a `KK^G`-equivalence".
  - The sum formula is not proved (part 1 of the norm-functor artifact). Conditional only.
- **(f4) Spectra analogy.** In genuine `C_p`-spectra, `Φ^(C_p) N(X) ≃ X` for every `X`, because
  spectra are generated by the sphere under colimits. Here `(1_T ⊗ X) ⋊ G` plays the role of `Φ^G X`,
  and the non-cellular summand `D_f` is exactly what that generation argument cannot reach. Dies at
  generation, as recorded in `tate-norm-shift-is-kkg-trivial`.
- **(f5) Finite-index transfer.** `Res_N Y ≃ 0` makes the regular character `Σ_χ χ` act by `0` on
  `Y`, so `Y` is a `Z[ζ_p, 1/p]`-module. That is all it gives: the finite-group model `N = 1, W = G`
  shows restriction to `N` alone never forces vanishing (`1_T ≠ 0`).

## 8. Status

Open in both directions. The sharpest forms are (P1) and (P2), and (P1) needs no equivariance.
Every subgroup-local and K-theoretic test passes (Proposition B and part 1, Theorem A.4). A negative
answer needs a KK-invariant of `W ⋉_r Y` that is not K-theoretic, supported on non-induced Kazhdan
objects. A positive answer needs a non-geometric resolution of `q^*1_T` by cells induced from `ℱ`, or a
direct KK-level homotopy.
