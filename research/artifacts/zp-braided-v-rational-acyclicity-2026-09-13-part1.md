# bV is rationally acyclic: part 1, overview and the homology of the kernel

Lane z2-09-bv-acyclic, 2026-09-13. Parts 2 and 3 complete the proof.
Refereed 2026-09-16 by an agent panel (see
`notes/zaremsky-2-09-braided-v-acyclic-swarm-2026-09-16.md`). The panel found
that the original Step A (a Gerstenhaber-derivation formula for cabling and a
direct-sum decomposition of `H_q(K)`) was false for blocks of size `≥ 3`.
Steps A2–A6 below are the corrected version: `H_q(K)` has a finite `V`-stable
filtration with the same graded pieces, and that is all Step D needs. Human
expert review is still recommended.

**Theorem.** For the Brin–Dehornoy braided Thompson group `bV`, `H_n(bV; Q) = 0`
for every `n ≥ 1`.

**Plan.**

- `K = ker(bV → V)` is the colimit of pure braid groups under cabling.
- Step A (this part): `H_q(K; Z)` has a finite `V`-stable filtration whose
  graded pieces are configuration modules
  `C_c(Conf_m(C), Z) ⊗_{Z[Σ_m]} W_{m,q}`, `q + 1 ≤ m ≤ 2q`.
- Step B (part 2): `C(C^j, Q)` is V-acyclic, from Palmer–Wu's acyclicity of the
  labelled Thompson groups `V(Q^j)`.
- Step C (part 3): configuration modules are V-acyclic, by stratifying `C^m`.
- Step D (part 3): V-acyclicity passes to extensions, then the
  Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence with `V` acyclic (Szymik–Wahl).

**External inputs.**

- Szymik–Wahl, arXiv:1411.5035: `V` is acyclic.
- Palmer–Wu, arXiv:2510.16879, Theorem 2.6: `V(G)` is acyclic for every discrete `G`.
- V. I. Arnold, "The cohomology ring of the colored braid group", Mat. Zametki
  5 (1969): `H^*(Conf_n(C); Z)` is torsion-free, generated by the degree-1
  classes `w_{ab}` subject to the Arnold relations.
- P. Orlik and L. Solomon, "Combinatorics and topology of complements of
  hyperplanes", Invent. Math. 56 (1980); E. Brieskorn, "Sur les groupes de
  tresses", Séminaire Bourbaki exp. 401, LNM 317 (1973): the integral
  cohomology of a complex hyperplane complement is the Orlik–Solomon algebra,
  which is graded by the intersection lattice.

## Step A: `H_q(K)` as a `V`-module

**A1 (kernel and action).** As in `braided-thompson-group-bv-is-perfect-proof`
(Step 1), `K = colim_T P_{L(T)}` over binary trees `T` ordered by expansion,
with cabling maps as transition maps. `P_n` is the fundamental group of the
aspherical space `Conf_n(R^2)`.

For `g = (T_-, γ, T_+) ∈ bV`, conjugation sends `(T_+, β, T_+)` to
`(T_-, γβγ^{-1}, T_-)`. On `H_*(P_n) = H_*(Conf_n(R^2))`, conjugation by
`γ ∈ B_n` is the deck transformation relabelling points by the permutation of
`γ`. Braids with the same permutation differ by pure braids, which act
trivially on homology. So `bV` acts on `H_*(K)` through `V`, by relabelling
leaves.

**A2 (Orlik–Solomon over `Z`).** Write `A = H^*(Conf_n(C); Z)`, with
`w_{ab} = w_{ba}` the pullback of the generator of `H^1(C^×)` along
`z_a − z_b`. For a set `F` of pairs, write `w_F` for the product of its
`w_{ab}` in a fixed order.

- If the graph `([n], F)` has a cycle, then `w_F = 0`. For a repeated pair this
  is `w^2 = 0`. For a cycle `C`, the Orlik–Solomon relation `∂w_C = 0` gives
  `w_C = w_{e} ∂w_C = 0` for any edge `e` of `C`.
- Flats of the braid arrangement are partitions `π` of `[n]`, of rank
  `n − #π`. The lattice grading gives `A^q = ⊕_{n − #π = q} A_π`, where `A_π`
  is spanned by the `w_F` for forests `F` whose components are the blocks of
  `π`.
- Every group here is free of finite rank. So
  `H_q(Conf_n; Z) = ⊕_π H_π` with `H_π = Hom(A_π, Z)`.

The *support* `supp π` is the union of the blocks of size `≥ 2`. If `π` has `k`
such blocks, then `q = |supp π| − k` with `1 ≤ k ≤ |supp π|/2`. So for `q ≥ 1`,
`m = |supp π|` satisfies `q + 1 ≤ m ≤ 2q`.

The projection `Conf_n → Conf_S` has a section, which adds the remaining points
far away. So `H^*(Conf_S) → H^*(Conf_n)` is injective, and it maps `A_ρ`
isomorphically onto `A_π` when `π` is `ρ` plus singletons. Set
`W_{S,q} = ⊕_ρ Hom(A_ρ(Conf_S), Z)`, over partitions `ρ` of `S` into blocks of
size `≥ 2` with `|S| − #ρ = q`. Put `W_{m,q} = W_{[m],q}`, a free `Z`-module of
finite rank with a `Σ_m`-action. Then

`⊕_{|supp π| = m} H_π = ⊕_{|S| = m} W_{S,q} ≅ Z[Inj([m], [n])] ⊗_{Z[Σ_m]} W_{m,q}`.

**A3 (cabling).** Cabling strand `i` is induced by
`c: Conf_n(C) → Conf_{n+1}(C)`. This map puts `x_{i'} = x_i` and
`x_{i''} = x_i + ε(x)`, with `ε(x)` one third of the minimal distance, and
keeps the other points. Let `r: [n+1] → [n]` identify `i'` and `i''` with `i`.
Then `c^* w_{i'i''} = 0`, because `z_{i'} − z_{i''}` is a negative real number.
For every other pair, `c^* w_{ab} = w_{r(a)r(b)}`: shrinking `ε` to `0` is a
homotopy through nonzero values. Take a partition `ρ` of `[n+1]` and a forest
`F` with components `ρ`. There are three cases.

- (a) `i'`, `i''` lie in one block of `ρ`. Then `F` contains the edge `i'i''`,
  or `r(F)` contains a cycle or a repeated edge. So `c^* A_ρ = 0`.
- (b) They lie in different blocks. Then `r(F)` is a forest with `|F|` edges,
  and `c^*` maps `A_ρ` into `A_{r(ρ)}`, where `r(ρ)` merges the two blocks.
- (c) Within (b), at least one of `i'`, `i''` is a singleton of `ρ`. Then `r`
  maps `supp ρ` bijectively onto `supp r(ρ)`. Under A2, `c^*: A_ρ → A_{r(ρ)}`
  is the relabelling isomorphism.

Dually, `c_* = (c^*)^T`. Take `π` of support `S`, and let `B ∋ i` be the block of
`i` in `π`.

- If `B = {i}`, then `c_*` maps `H_π` by the relabelling isomorphism.
- If `|B| ≥ 2`, then `c_* H_π` has two relabelling components, of supports
  `S[i → i']` and `S[i → i'']`. Its other components lie in `H_ρ` for the
  splittings `ρ` of `B[i → i', i'']` into two blocks of size `≥ 2`, and those
  have support of size `|S| + 1`. No other `ρ` satisfies `r(ρ) = π`.

**Witness that the extra terms occur.** Take `n = 3` and cable strand `1`. Let
`T ∈ H_2(Conf_3)` be the torus where `z_1 = 0`, `z_2 = e^{iθ}` and
`z_3 = 2e^{iφ}`, oriented so that `⟨T, w_{12} w_{13}⟩ = 1`. Then
`⟨c_* T, w_{1''2} w_{1'3}⟩ = ⟨T, c^*(w_{1''2} w_{1'3})⟩ = 1`. The form
`w_{1''2} w_{1'3}` lies in `A_ρ` with `ρ = {1'',2}{1',3}`, of support 4. So
`c_* T` has a component of support 4. The earlier two-term formula, supported on
`{1',2,3}` and `{1'',2,3}`, misses it. No `V`-equivariant direct-sum
decomposition by support is claimed.

**A4 (support filtration).** Let `F_{≥m} H_q(Conf_n) = ⊕_{|supp π| ≥ m} H_π`.
By A3, cabling maps `F_{≥m}` into `F_{≥m}`, and permutations preserve support
size. On `gr_m = ⊕_{|supp π| = m} H_π`, the induced map is as follows:

- cabling at `i ∉ S` relabels the `W_{S,q}` data;
- cabling at `i ∈ S` sends it to the sum of the same data on `S[i → i']` and on
  `S[i → i'']`.

For `q ≥ 1`, `F_{≥q+1} = H_q` and `F_{≥2q+1} = 0`.

**A5 (colimit).** Homology commutes with filtered colimits, and filtered
colimits are exact. So `H_q(K; Z) = colim_T H_q(Conf_{L(T)}; Z)` has the
filtration `F_{≥m} = colim_T F_{≥m}`. By A2 and A4,

`gr_m H_q(K; Z) = colim_T ( Z[Inj([m], L(T))] ⊗_{Z[Σ_m]} W_{m,q} )`,

where refinement of a leaf acts on injections additively: an injection hitting
the leaf goes to the sum of the two injections hitting its children.

An injection `[m] → L(T)` is an ordered `m`-tuple of pairwise disjoint dyadic
cones, i.e. a box `U_1 × ... × U_m ⊆ Conf_m(C)`. Sending a box to its indicator
identifies the additive colimit with `C_c(Conf_m(C), Z)`:

- it is compatible with refinement;
- the boxes at one level are disjoint, so their indicators are independent;
- every compact open subset of `Conf_m(C)` is a finite disjoint union of boxes
  at a common level.

By A1, `V` acts by relabelling leaves. This preserves support size, so each
`F_{≥m}` is a `V`-submodule, and `V` acts on `gr_m` through its action on `C`,
commuting with `Σ_m`. Hence, for every `q ≥ 1`, `H_q(K; Z)` has a finite
filtration by `V`-submodules

`H_q(K; Z) = F_{≥q+1} ⊇ F_{≥q+2} ⊇ ... ⊇ F_{≥2q} ⊇ F_{≥2q+1} = 0`,

`gr_m H_q(K; Z) ≅ C_c(Conf_m(C), Z) ⊗_{Z[Σ_m]} W_{m,q}`.

Tensoring with the flat module `Q` gives the same statement for
`H_q(K; Q) = H_q(K; Z) ⊗ Q`, with graded pieces
`C_c(Conf_m(C), Q) ⊗_{Q[Σ_m]} (W_{m,q} ⊗ Q)`.

**A6 (extensions).** Call a `V`-module `M` V-acyclic if `H_p(V; M) = 0` for all
`p ≥ 0`. If `0 → M' → M → M'' → 0` is exact and `M'` and `M''` are V-acyclic,
then so is `M`, by the long exact sequence. By induction along the filtration
of A5, if every graded piece is V-acyclic then so is `H_q(K)`.

**Check (q = 1).** `m = 2` and `W_{2,1} = Z` with trivial `Σ_2`-action, since
the swap fixes `w_{12}`. The filtration has a single step. This gives `C_c` of
unordered pairs of distinct points, matching `H_1(K) ≅ C_c(Y, Z)` in
`braided-thompson-group-bv-is-perfect-proof`.
