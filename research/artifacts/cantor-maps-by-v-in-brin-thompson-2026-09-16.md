# Continuous Γ-maps by V inside the next Brin–Thompson group

Artifact for `cantor-maps-by-v-embed-in-next-brin-thompson-group` and for the two
routes that tie Brothier's Problem B (arXiv:1906.03789v3) to Zaremsky Problem 2.7.
Written 2026-09-16 by swarm lane `brin-thompson-groups-nv-are-a-t-menable`.

## 1. Notation

- `C = {0,1}^N` is the Cantor space. For a finite word `w`, `[w]` is the cylinder of
  sequences starting with `w`. The canonical map `[w] → [w']` is `wu ↦ w'u`.
- A *brick* in `C^n` is a product `[v_1] × … × [v_n]`. The canonical map between two
  bricks is the product of the canonical maps of the coordinates.
- `nV` is the group of homeomorphisms `g` of `C^n` for which there is a finite
  partition of `C^n` into bricks `B_1, …, B_k`, such that each `g|B_j` is the
  canonical map onto a brick `B'_j`. The images `B'_j` then also partition `C^n`
  (Brin's definition). `1V = V`.
- For a discrete group `Γ`, `C(C,Γ)` is the group of continuous, i.e. locally
  constant, maps `f: C → Γ` under pointwise multiplication. `V` acts on it by
  `(h·f)(x) = f(h^{-1}x)`. The semidirect product `G_Γ = C(C,Γ) ⋊ V` has the
  product `(f,h)(f',h') = (f·(h·f'), hh')`. This is the group of Brothier's Problem B.

Every locally constant `f: C → Γ` is constant on the leaves of some complete
prefix code `w_1, …, w_k`, because `C` is compact.

## 2. The embedding lemma

**Lemma 1.** Let `n ≥ 1` and let `Γ ≤ nV`. Write `C^{n+1} = C × C^n` with points
`(x,p)`. Define, for `f ∈ C(C,Γ)` and `h ∈ V`,

- `Φ(f)(x,p) = (x, f(x)(p))`,
- `Φ(h)(x,p) = (h(x), p)`,
- `Φ(f,h) = Φ(f)Φ(h)`.

Then `Φ` is an injective homomorphism `G_Γ → (n+1)V`.

*Proof.*

**(i) `Φ(h) ∈ (n+1)V`.** Suppose `h` maps the cylinders `[v_j]` canonically onto
`[v'_j]`. Then `Φ(h)` maps the bricks `[v_j] × C^n` canonically onto
`[v'_j] × C^n`.

**(ii) `Φ(f) ∈ (n+1)V`.** Let `f` equal `γ_i` on `[w_i]`, where `{w_i}` is a complete
prefix code. Say `γ_i` maps the bricks `B_{ij}` of a partition of `C^n` canonically
onto the bricks `B'_{ij}`. On `[w_i] × B_{ij}`, the map `Φ(f)` is
`(x,p) ↦ (x, γ_i(p))`. This is the canonical map onto `[w_i] × B'_{ij}`: the prefix
replacement in the first coordinate is trivial. The bricks `[w_i] × B_{ij}` partition
`C^{n+1}`. So `Φ(f)` is a bijection that is brick-canonical on a finite brick
partition, and `Φ(f) ∈ (n+1)V`.

**(iii) Homomorphism.** The following three identities hold pointwise.

- `Φ(f)Φ(f')(x,p) = (x, f(x)(f'(x)(p))) = Φ(ff')(x,p)`.
- `Φ(h)Φ(h') = Φ(hh')`.
- `Φ(h)Φ(f)Φ(h)^{-1}(x,p) = Φ(h)Φ(f)(h^{-1}x, p) = Φ(h)(h^{-1}x, f(h^{-1}x)(p))`,
  which equals `(x, f(h^{-1}x)(p)) = Φ(h·f)(x,p)`.

Hence
`Φ(f,h)Φ(f',h') = Φ(f)Φ(h)Φ(f')Φ(h)^{-1}Φ(h)Φ(h') = Φ(f)Φ(h·f')Φ(hh')`, and this is
`Φ(f·(h·f'))Φ(hh') = Φ((f,h)(f',h'))`.

**(iv) Injective.** We have `Φ(f,h)(x,p) = Φ(f)(hx, p) = (hx, f(hx)(p))`. If this is
the identity, then `hx = x` for all `x`, so `h = 1`. Then `f(x)(p) = p` for all `p`,
so `f(x) = 1` for all `x`. ∎

**Corollary 2.**

1. `C(C,Z) ⋊ V ≤ 2V`. Take `Γ = ⟨s⟩ ≤ V = 1V`, with `s ∈ V` of infinite order,
   for instance a nontrivial element of `F`.
2. `C(C,V) ⋊ V ≤ 2V`. More generally `C(C,nV) ⋊ V ≤ (n+1)V`, and the iterates
   `C(C, C(C,V) ⋊ V) ⋊ V ≤ 3V`, and so on.
3. With `brin-thompson-nv-embeds-in-mv` and the fact that the Haagerup property
   passes to subgroups: if some `nV`, `n ≥ 2`, is a-T-menable, then Problem B has
   a positive answer for every `Γ` embeddable in `(n-1)V`. In particular it has a
   positive answer for `Γ = Z`, and for every countable group that embeds in `V`.
   If `2V` is a-T-menable, then `C(C,Z) ⋊ V` is.
4. Contrapositive: a negative answer to Problem B for any single `Γ` that embeds in
   some Brin–Thompson group proves that no `nV` with `n` large enough is
   a-T-menable. For `Γ = Z` it proves that `2V` is not a-T-menable.

**Remark (finite Γ).** For finite `Γ` the same formula with `C × Γ` in place of
`C × C^n` gives `C(C,Γ) ⋊ V ≤ V_{2,|Γ|} ≅ V`, where `f` acts by
`(x,γ) ↦ (x, f(x)γ)`. This is the case `φ0 = φ1 = id` of the repository claim
`twisted-power-cloning-groups-embed-in-v`, whose `𝒱_(G,id,id)` is exactly
`C(C,G) ⋊ V`. So Problem B is trivial for finite `Γ` (Farley), and `Γ = Z` is the
first open case.

Novelty: not audited beyond the checks in the lane note. Lemma 1 is the obvious
"fibred" construction and is plausibly folklore.

## 3. Structural facts about G = C(C,Z) ⋊ V

Write `A = C(C,Z)` additively. `A` is free abelian of countable rank, with basis
`1_C` together with `1_{[w0]}` for all words `w`.

**(a) Generation.** `V` acts transitively on the proper nonempty clopen sets. So
every `1_{[w]}` with `w ≠ ∅` is conjugate to `1_{[0]}`, and `1_C = 1_{[0]} + 1_{[1]}`.
Hence `G` is generated by `V` and `1_{[0]}`, and is finitely generated.

**(b) G is perfect.** `V` is simple, so `V ≤ [G,G]`. For `h ∈ V` we have
`[h, f] = h·f - f ∈ [G,G]`, so `1_{[u]} - 1_{[u']} ∈ [G,G]` for any two proper
cylinders. The identity
`(1_{[00]} - 1_{[1]}) + (1_{[01]} - 1_{[1]}) - (1_{[0]} - 1_{[1]}) = -1_{[1]}`
puts `1_{[1]}`, hence every `1_{[w]}`, and so all of `A`, into `[G,G]`.

**(c) Norms and distortion.** Fix the generating set `S = S_V ∪ {±1_{[0]}}`.
- Right multiplication by a generator changes `f` in `(f,h)` by at most one unit in
  sup-norm, since `(f,h)(±1_{[0]},1) = (f ± h·1_{[0]}, h)`. Hence
  `‖f‖_∞ ≤ |(f,h)|_S`.
- It follows that every nonzero `f ∈ A` is undistorted.
- `1_C` is central, since it is `V`-fixed and `A` is abelian, and it is undistorted.
- If `h` has finite order `k`, then `(f,h)^k ∈ A`. So `(f,h)` either has finite order
  or is undistorted.
- If `h` has infinite order, undistortion of `(f,h)` follows from undistortion of
  `⟨h⟩` in `V`, because the projection `G → V` is 1-Lipschitz.
  Bleak–Bowman–Gordon–Graham–Hughes–Matucci–Sapir, *Centralizers in R. Thompson's
  group V_n*, prove that cyclic subgroups of `V` are undistorted. This is recalled,
  not re-read.

So the Callard–Salo distortion obstruction to cubical actions of `2V`
(arXiv:2208.00685) is not visible inside `G`.

**(d) Haagerup reduces to A.** `G` is a-T-menable iff some conditionally negative
definite (cnd) function `ψ` on `G` is proper on `A`.
- The "only if" direction is trivial.
- For "if", let `φ` be Farley's proper cnd function on `V`, pulled back to `G`, and
  put `ψ' = ψ + φ`. Suppose `ψ'(g_k) ≤ R` along a sequence. Then the `V`-parts of
  the `g_k` lie in a finite set, so after passing to a subsequence `g_k = a_k h`
  with `h` fixed.
- Then `√ψ(a_k) ≤ √ψ(g_k) + √ψ(h^{-1})` is bounded, so the `a_k` range over a
  finite set. Hence `ψ'` is proper.

**(e) V-invariant quadratic parts vanish.** Let `B` be a `V`-invariant positive
semidefinite symmetric bilinear form on `A`, with `Q(f) = B(f,f)`.
- `V` acts transitively on ordered `k`-tuples of pairwise disjoint nonempty clopen
  sets whose union has nonempty complement.
- So `q = Q(1_U)` and `b = B(1_U, 1_W)` do not depend on the choices, for
  `U, W` disjoint with `U ∪ W ≠ C`.
- From `Q(1_{U⊔W}) = q`: `2q + 2b = q`, so `b = -q/2`.
- From `Q(1_{U⊔W⊔Y}) = q`, with nonempty complement: `3q + 6b = q`, so `q = 0`.
- Then `B(1_U, ·) = 0` by Cauchy–Schwarz. The `1_U` with `U` a proper cylinder span
  `A`, so `Q = 0`.

By uniqueness of the Lévy–Khinchin decomposition, a `V`-invariant cnd function
`F` on `A` is therefore a pure Lévy integral
`F(f) = ∫_{Â∖{0}} (1 - cos 2πχ(f)) dν(χ)`, with `ν` a `V`-invariant measure. Here `Â`
is the compact group of finitely additive `T`-valued measures on the clopen sets.
Any such `F` gives a cnd function `(f,h) ↦ F(f)` on `G`. If `F` is proper, then `G`
is a-T-menable by (d).

**(f) Relative (T) criterion.** `(G, A)` has relative property (T) iff there is
no sequence of probability measures `μ_k` on `Â ∖ {0}` with `μ_k → δ_0` weak* and
`‖h_*μ_k - μ_k‖_TV → 0` for every `h ∈ V`.
- Given such `μ_k`, the vectors `√(dμ_k/dμ̃_k)` in the quasi-regular representation
  on `L²(Â∖{0}, μ̃_k)` are almost invariant. Here `μ̃_k = Σ_{h ∈ V} c_h h_*μ_k`, for
  any strictly positive weights with `Σ c_h = 1`, so `μ̃_k` is `V`-quasi-invariant.
  `A` acts by multiplication by `e^{2πiχ(f)}`, and `V` by the Radon–Nikodym-corrected
  push-forward.
  - For `h ∈ V`, `‖π(h)ξ_k - ξ_k‖² ≤ ‖h_*μ_k - μ_k‖_TV`, since the Hellinger distance
    is dominated by total variation.
  - For `f ∈ A`, `⟨π(f)ξ_k, ξ_k⟩ = ∫ e^{2πiχ(f)} dμ_k → 1` by weak* convergence.
- Take the direct sum over `k`. It has no `A`-invariant vector: such a vector is
  supported on `{χ : χ = 0}`, which is `μ̃_k`-null.
- Conversely, if `π` has almost invariant unit vectors `ξ_k` and no `A`-invariant
  vectors, then the spectral measures `μ_k` of `ξ_k` on `Â` satisfy `μ_k({0}) = 0`,
  `μ_k → δ_0`, and `‖h_*μ_k - μ_k‖_TV ≤ 2‖π(h)ξ_k - ξ_k‖`.
  - The first holds because `μ_k({0})` is the squared norm of the projection of
    `ξ_k` onto the `A`-invariant vectors.
  - The second is Lévy continuity on the compact group `Â`, with the `ξ_k` chosen
    `(F_k, 1/k)`-invariant for an exhausting sequence of finite sets `F_k`.
  - The third holds because the spectral measure of `π(h)ξ` is `h_*μ_ξ`, and
    `‖μ_ξ - μ_η‖_TV ≤ ‖ξ - η‖(‖ξ‖ + ‖η‖)` by Cauchy–Schwarz on a Borel partition.

A relative-(T) pair `(G, A)` would, by Lemma 1, prove
`brin-thompson-2v-is-not-a-t-menable`.

**(g) Localized characters cannot carry almost-invariant measures.** Fix `m ≥ 1`.
Let `Y_m ⊆ Â∖{0}` be the characters `Σ_{i≤m} t_i δ_{x_i}`, i.e. atomic with at most
`m` atoms. `Y_m` is `V`-paradoxical with Borel pieces, as follows.
- Choose `L` with `2^L > m`. Every configuration of at most `m` points avoids some
  depth-`L` cylinder `[w]`.
- On `C ∖ [w]` the partial map `x ↦ 0x` has image with nonempty complement, so it
  extends to an element `g_w ∈ V`.
- These `2^L` elements move `Y_m` Borel-piecewise into the characters supported in
  `[0]`, and similarly into those supported in `[1]`. These are two disjoint subsets.
  - Pieces: `P_w` is the set of `χ ∈ Y_m` that vanish on every clopen subset of
    `[w]` (a closed condition), with `w` lexicographically first among such
    depth-`L` words.
  - The piecewise map `χ ↦ g_w χ` on `P_w` is injective, since `g_w χ` has atoms
    `0x_i` with the weights `t_i` of `χ`, and deleting the leading `0` recovers `χ`.
    The same holds with `1` in place of `0`.
- So `Y_m` carries no `V`-invariant finitely additive mean on its Borel sets: the
  two images would each have mean `1`, but they are disjoint. Hence no sequence of
  probability measures supported on `Y_m` is `TV`-almost invariant under the finite
  set of elements used. A weak* cluster point would be such a mean.

Measures witnessing failure of relative (T) must therefore give vanishing mass to
every `Y_m` in the limit. Indeed `Y_m` is `V`-invariant, so if `μ_k(Y_m) ≥ ε`, then
the normalized restrictions `μ_k|_{Y_m}/μ_k(Y_m)` are still `TV`-almost invariant,
with defect at most `ε^{-1}‖h_*μ_k - μ_k‖_TV`. The characters must be non-atomic, or have an unbounded
number of atoms. This is why the wreath-product mechanism of Brothier's Theorem A
and of Cornulier–Stalder–Valette (arXiv:0905.3960), which works with point-localized
characters and counting measure on an orbit, does not transfer to `C(C,Z)`. There,
`f = 1_{[0]}` has infinitely many orbit points in its support, so the counting-measure
integral is infinite.
