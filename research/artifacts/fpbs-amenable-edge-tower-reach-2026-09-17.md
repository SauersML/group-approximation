# Which groups can amenable-edge towers reach from a finitely generated group?

Worker swarm-0917-w2-fp-pull-3, 2026-09-17. Target claim:
`fpbs-fixed-price-non-amenably-generated-from-fg`.

## 0. The question and why it matters

The amalgam-descent artifact
(`research/artifacts/fpbs-amalgam-descent-amenably-generated-2026-09-16.md`)
has two parts.
- Theorem 1 pushes fixed price down from `Gamma *_A B` to `Gamma` when `A` is
  infinite amenable.
- Proposition 4 shows that one step up from a finitely generated group reaches
  only groups in `AG`, the groups generated by finitely many amenable
  subgroups.

Its §5 leaves two things unanalysed: longer towers, and HNN extensions or
finite graphs of groups. If some multi-step tower reached a group outside
`AG`, descent would reach into the open class (modulo an HNN/graph analogue
of Theorem 1). This note decides how far such towers can reach, whatever
descent theorem is used at each step.

**Tower.** An *amenable-edge tower* over `Gamma` is a sequence

    Gamma = K_0 <= K_1 <= ... <= K_n.

- Each `K_j` is the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups `G_j` with
  all edge groups amenable, and `K_{j-1}` is one of its vertex groups.
- Edge groups may be finite or infinite. Amalgams and HNN extensions are the
  one-edge cases.
- The tower *reaches* `Gamma` if `K_n` is finitely generated.

Descent theorems of the Theorem 1 kind pass fixed price from `K_j` to the
vertex group `K_{j-1}`. So "reachable" is an upper bound on what any such
descent can do.

## 1. Theorem A: a quotient invariant of tower reach

**Definition (class T).** Define classes of groups inductively.
- `T_0 = {1}`.
- `T_r` is the class of groups `Q` that have an amenable subgroup `M` with
  `Q / <<M>> in T_{r-1}`. Here `<<M>>` is the normal closure in `Q`.
- `T` is the union of the `T_r`.

Since `M = 1` is allowed, `T_{r-1} ⊆ T_r`. Equivalently, `Q in T` iff there is
a finite chain of quotients

    Q = Q_0 ->> Q_1 ->> ... ->> Q_r = 1

in which each kernel is the normal closure of one amenable subgroup of the
group it is taken in.

**Lemma A1.** `T_r` is closed under quotients.

*Proof.* Induct on `r`. The case `r = 0` is trivial. Let `Q in T_r` via `M`,
and let `pi: Q ->> P` be surjective. Then:
- `pi(<<M>>_Q) = <<pi(M)>>_P`, because `pi` is surjective.
- So `P / <<pi(M)>>` is a quotient of `Q / <<M>> in T_{r-1}`, and by induction
  it lies in `T_{r-1}`.
- `pi(M)` is amenable, so `P in T_r`. ∎

**Lemma A2.** `AG ⊆ T`. In particular every finitely generated group lies in
`T`.

*Proof.* Let `Q = <M_1, ..., M_m>` with the `M_i` amenable. Kill `M_1`, then
the image of `M_2` in the quotient, and so on. Each image is amenable. After
`m` steps the quotient is trivial, so `Q in T_m`. ∎

**Lemma A3 (retraction).** Let `G = pi_1(G)` for a finite graph of groups
with all edge groups amenable. Let `Gamma = G_{v_0}` be a vertex group. Let
`N` be the normal closure in `Gamma` of the images in `Gamma` of the edge
groups of all edges incident to `v_0`. There are finitely many such images,
say `C_1, ..., C_p`, all amenable. Then `Gamma / N` is a quotient of `G`.

*Proof.* Use Serre's presentation of `G`.
- **Generators:** the vertex groups, and letters `t_e` for the edges.
- **Relations:** those of the vertex groups; `t_e = 1` for `e` in a maximal
  tree; `t_{ebar} = t_e^{-1}`; and `t_e alpha_e(c) t_e^{-1} = omega_e(c)` for
  `c` in `G_e`.

Define `rho` on the generators:
- on `Gamma`, the quotient map `Gamma -> Gamma/N`;
- `rho(G_w) = 1` for every other vertex group `G_w`;
- `rho(t_e) = 1` for every edge.

This respects the relations. For an edge relation, both sides map to `1`:
- an edge-group element sitting in `Gamma` lies in some `C_i`, so it is
  killed;
- an edge-group element sitting in another vertex group maps to `1` by
  definition.

The vertex relations and `t_e = 1` are clearly respected. `rho` is onto,
since `Gamma -> Gamma/N` already is. ∎

**Theorem A.** If an amenable-edge tower reaches `Gamma`, then `Gamma in T`.

*Proof.* `K_n` is finitely generated, so `K_n in T` by Lemma A2. Suppose
`K_j in T`.
- By Lemma A3, `K_{j-1}/N_j` is a quotient of `K_j`, where `N_j` is the normal
  closure of finitely many amenable subgroups `C_1, ..., C_p` of `K_{j-1}`.
- By Lemma A1, `K_{j-1}/N_j in T`.
- Kill `C_1`, then the image of `C_2`, and so on. Their joint normal closure
  is `N_j`, so `K_{j-1} in T`.

By descending induction, `Gamma = K_0 in T`. ∎

**Proposition A4 (infinite free products are outside T).** Let
`Q = *_{i in I} G_i` with `I` infinite and every `G_i` nontrivial. Then no
group that surjects onto `Q` lies in `T`.

*Proof.* By Lemma A1 it suffices to show `Q ∉ T`. We show by induction on `r`
that every `r`-step quotient chain as in the definition ends in a group of
the form

    P * ( *_{i in I \ S} G_i )

with `S ⊆ I` finite. Such a group is nontrivial, because `I \ S` is
nonempty. At `r = 0` take `P = 1` and `S` empty.

For the inductive step, let `Q' = P * *_{i ∉ S} G_i` and let `M <= Q'` be
amenable.
- **Kurosh subgroup theorem** (valid for arbitrary subgroups). `M` is a free
  product `F * *_k (M ∩ g_k H_k g_k^{-1})`, where `F` is free and the `H_k`
  range over the free factors `P` and `G_i`.
- A free product of two nontrivial groups contains a nonabelian free group
  unless both factors have order 2. Amenable `M` contains no nonabelian free
  group, so one of three cases holds:
  - (a) `M` lies in a conjugate of one factor;
  - (b) `M ≅ Z`;
  - (c) `M ≅ Z/2 * Z/2`.
- In cases (b) and (c), `M` is finitely generated. Its generators involve
  only finitely many free factors, so `M <= P * *_{i in S'} G_i =: P'` for a
  finite `S' ⊆ I \ S`.
- In case (a), `<<M>> = <<g^{-1} M g>>`, and `g^{-1} M g` lies in one factor.
  That factor is contained in `P' := P * G_i` for a single `i`, or `P' = P`.
- In every case `<<M>>_{Q'}` is the normal closure of a subset `X` of `P'`.
  Presentations give

      (P' * R) / <<X>> = (P'/<<X>>_{P'}) * R,

  where `R` is the free product of the remaining `G_i`, with finite
  complement index set.

This is the claimed form with `S` enlarged by finitely many indices. ∎

**Corollary A5 (class killed).** No amenable-edge tower, of any height and
with any finite graphs of groups (amalgams, HNN extensions, several edges),
reaches a countable group that surjects onto an infinite free product of
nontrivial groups. Examples:
- `F_inf`;
- `*_N Z/2`;
- every extension `N ⋊ F_inf` or `N -> Gamma ->> F_inf`, for any countable
  kernel `N`;
- fundamental groups of infinite-genus surfaces.

So every descent method of the amalgam/HNN kind dies on this class. The
invariant is membership in `T`. The step where every member dies is the
bottom of the quotient chain: the last quotient keeps infinitely many free
factors and is never trivial.

Relevance to fixed price:
- `Gamma * F_inf` has cost `infinity` for every free action, by Gaboriau's
  free-product formula, so it is not an interesting target.
- For extensions `N -> Gamma ->> F_inf` with `N` infinitely generated and not
  of cost 1, fixed price is not known in general. Tower descent provably
  cannot reach them.

**What Theorem A does not exclude.** `Gamma_mal` (§5.1 of the amalgam
artifact) is the direct limit of `F_2` under `phi(a) = a`,
`phi(b) = b a b^{-2}`. Writing `b_n` for the image of `b` at stage `n`, we
have `b_n = b_{n+1} a b_{n+1}^{-2}`. So `Gamma_mal/<<a>>` is generated by the
images of the `b_n`, with `b_n = b_{n+1}^{-1}`. That quotient is cyclic, and
killing it gives `1`. So `Gamma_mal in T_2`, and Theorem A says nothing about
it. §3 handles `Gamma_mal` at heights `≤ 2` by a different invariant.

## 2. Theorem B: one level of a finite graph of groups (Proposition 4 for all splittings)

**Lemma B1 (full-edge subgraph of groups).** Let `G` be a finite graph of
groups with fundamental group `G`. Suppose `G'_v <= G_v` for each vertex, and
each `G'_v` contains the images of all edge groups at `v`. Build `G'` with
the same graph, the same edge groups and vertex groups `G'_v`. Then:
- (i) `pi_1(G') -> pi_1(G)` is injective;
- (ii) its image `G'` satisfies `G' ∩ G_v = G'_v`;
- (iii) `g in G` lies in `G'` iff, in some reduced word for `g`, every letter
  from each `G_v` lies in `G'_v`. Equivalently, the same holds in every
  reduced word for `g`.

*Proof.* Reducedness of a word only asks whether letters lie in edge-group
images. Those images are the same in `G'` and `G`, so reduced words of `G'`
are reduced words of `G`.

(i) A nonempty reduced word of `G'` either has positive length, or is a
single nontrivial vertex letter. In both cases it is nontrivial in `G`, by
Serre's normal form theorem (*Trees*, I.5.2, Theorem 11).

(ii) Let `g in G_v` be represented by a reduced word `w` of `G'` of positive
length. Then `g^{-1} w`, obtained by merging `g^{-1}` into the first letter,
is still reduced of positive length. It is nontrivial, a contradiction. So
`w` has length 0 and `g in G'_v`.

(iii) Two reduced words for the same element differ by inserting edge-group
elements between consecutive letters (Serre, loc. cit.). This preserves the
condition, because the edge images lie in every `G'_v`. ∎

**Theorem B.** Let `G = pi_1(G)` be finitely generated, where `G` is a finite
graph of groups with amenable edge groups. Then every vertex group lies in
`AG`. More precisely, it is generated by the images of its incident edge
groups together with a finite set.

*Proof.* Let `S` be a finite generating set, and let `E_v` be the set of
`G_v`-letters occurring in chosen reduced words for the elements of `S`. Set
`G'_v = <incident edge images, E_v>`. By Lemma B1(i), `G'` is a subgroup
containing `S`, so `G' = G`. By B1(ii), `G_v = G ∩ G_v = G'_v`. ∎

Theorem B is Proposition 4 of the amalgam artifact extended to HNN extensions
and finite graphs of groups. So one level of any amenable-edge splitting from
a finitely generated group reaches exactly into `AG`.

## 3. Theorem C: a vertex condition that blocks the second level

**Definition (finite intersection hull).** Let `Gamma = G_{v_0}` be a vertex
group of a finite graph of groups, and let `A`, `A'` be edge images at `v_0`,
possibly equal. Put

    D(A, A') = { x in Gamma : A ∩ x A' x^{-1} ≠ 1 }.

Condition **(FH)** says: for all incident edge images `A`, `A'`, the set
`D(A, A')` lies in a finitely generated subgroup of `Gamma`.

**Imported fact (amenable groups on trees).** Let an amenable group `M` act
on a tree without inversions. Then `M` fixes a vertex, fixes an end, or
leaves a line invariant.
- Reference: Pays–Valette, *Sous-groupes libres dans les groupes
  d'automorphismes d'arbres*, Enseign. Math. 37 (1991). This was not re-read
  verbatim, so this is the one import of §3 that lacks a quotation.
- **Proof route.**
  - If every element of `M` is elliptic, apply Serre I.6.5, Corollary 3 to
    each finitely generated `M_n` in an exhaustion `M = ∪ M_n`.
    - Each `Fix(M_n)` is a nonempty subtree, and these subtrees decrease.
    - Let `p_n` be the projections of a base point onto them. The `p_n` lie
      on one geodesic ray from the base point.
    - If the `p_n` stabilise, `M` fixes a vertex. Otherwise `M` fixes the end
      of that ray.
  - If `M` has a hyperbolic `h`, then no `m in M` moves the end pair of `h`
    off itself. Otherwise `h^N` and `m h^N m^{-1}` play ping-pong and give an
    `F_2`.
  - So the translates of the pair of ends of `h` pairwise intersect. A family
    of 2-sets that pairwise intersect either shares a point, giving a fixed
    end, or lies inside a 3-set, or is a single pair, giving an invariant
    line.
  - The 3-set case is impossible. A finite-index subgroup would fix three
    ends, but `h^6` lies in it and a hyperbolic element fixes exactly two
    ends.

**Theorem C.** Let `G = pi_1(G)` for a finite graph of groups with amenable
edge groups, and suppose the vertex group `Gamma = G_{v_0}` satisfies (FH). If
`G in AG`, then `Gamma in AG`.

*Proof.* Let:
- `R <= Gamma` be generated by the finitely generated hulls of the sets
  `D(A, A')` and by the incident edge images;
- for a finite set `E ⊆ Gamma` and finitely many amenable `L_1, ..., L_q <=
  Gamma`, `Gamma_{E,L} = <R, E, L_1, ..., L_q>`;
- `G_{E,L}` be the subgroup given by Lemma B1, with `Gamma` replaced by
  `Gamma_{E,L}` and every other vertex group kept.

These subgroups increase with `(E, L)`, and `G_{E,L} ∩ Gamma = Gamma_{E,L}`.

**Claim:** every amenable `M <= G` lies in some `G_{E,L}`. Given the claim,
suppose `G = <M_1, ..., M_m, F>` with the `M_i` amenable and `F` finite.
- Each `f in F` lies in `G_{E_f, ∅}`, where `E_f` is its set of
  `Gamma`-letters.
- Taking unions, `G = G_{E,L}` for one `(E, L)`.
- Then `Gamma = Gamma_{E,L}`. This group is generated by finitely many
  finitely generated or amenable subgroups, so it lies in `AG`.

*Proof of the claim.* Let `T` be the Bass–Serre tree and `v_*` the vertex
with stabiliser `Gamma`. Conjugating `M` by one element only adds finitely
many letters to `E`, so we may move a chosen vertex of `M`'s invariant
structure to lie near `v_*`.

Consider the geodesic path from `v_*`. At a vertex `h v_*` of
`Gamma`-type, the path enters along an edge with stabiliser `h A h^{-1}` and
leaves along `h x A' x^{-1} h^{-1}`. The joint stabiliser of the two edges is
`h (A ∩ x A' x^{-1}) h^{-1}`. Here `x` is the `Gamma`-letter of the reduced
word read along the path.

- **(a) `M` fixes a vertex `g w`.** Then `M = g L g^{-1}`, where `L` lies in
  a vertex group.
  - If `w` is not of `Gamma`-type, `L <= G_w`, which is kept whole. So
    `M <= G_{E_g, ∅}`.
  - If `w` is of `Gamma`-type, `L <= Gamma` is amenable, and
    `M <= G_{E_g, {L}}`.
- **(b) `M` fixes an end `xi` but no vertex.**
  - Let `M_0` be the set of elliptic elements of `M`. It is the kernel of the
    Busemann homomorphism `M -> Z`, so `M = <M_0, t>` for at most one extra
    element `t`.
  - If `M_0 = 1`, then `M` is cyclic and lies in `G_{E_t, ∅}`.
  - Otherwise fix a ray `rho = (v_0', v_1, v_2, ...)` to `xi` starting at a
    translate of `v_*`, and pick `m_* in M_0 \ 1` fixing the subray
    `rho_{>= k_0}`.
  - At every `Gamma`-type vertex `v_i` with `i > k_0`, `m_*` fixes both ray
    edges. So the letter `x_i` lies in `D(A, A') ⊆ R`.
  - Every `m in M_0` fixes a vertex and `xi`, hence a subray `rho_{>= k}` with
    `k >= k_0`. Thus `m = g_k s g_k^{-1}`, where `g_k` reads the path to
    `v_k`.
  - `s` fixes the next edge, so `s` lies in an edge image. Edge images are in
    `R` or in kept vertex groups.
  - The `Gamma`-letters of `g_k` are the letters `x_i` with `i <= k_0` (a
    fixed finite set `E_0`) followed by letters in `R`.
  - Hence `M_0 <= G_{E_0, ∅}`, and `M <= G_{E_0 ∪ E_t, ∅}`.
- **(c) `M` leaves a line `l` invariant.**
  - Let `L_0` be the pointwise stabiliser of `l` in `M`. Then `M / L_0` embeds
    in `Aut(l) ≅ D_inf`, so it is finitely generated.
  - If `L_0 = 1`, then `M` is finitely generated and lies in `G_{E, ∅}`.
  - Otherwise some `m_* ≠ 1` fixes all of `l`. So every `Gamma`-letter along
    `l` lies in `R`, and the argument of (b) applies with no exceptional
    initial segment.

This proves the claim, and with it Theorem C. ∎

**Corollary C1 (`Gamma_mal` is unreachable at height `≤ 2`).** Take any finite
graph of groups with `Gamma_mal` as a vertex group and amenable edge groups.
Its fundamental group is not in `AG`, and by Theorem B it is not a vertex
group of any finitely generated amenable-edge graph of groups. Hence no
amenable-edge tower of height `≤ 2` reaches `Gamma_mal`.

*Proof.* We verify (FH). By Proposition 5 of the amalgam artifact:
- (iii) every amenable subgroup of `Gamma_mal` is cyclic;
- (i) `Gamma_mal` is locally free, not finitely generated, and not in `AG`.

So the edge images at `Gamma_mal` are cyclic. It suffices to take them
nontrivial, since `D(A, A')` is empty if either is trivial.

- **Maximal cyclic subgroups.** Every nontrivial element lies in a unique
  maximal cyclic subgroup. An increasing union of cyclic subgroups is
  amenable, hence cyclic, so chains stabilise.
- **Commutative transitivity.** For `u ≠ 1`, the centraliser `C(u)` is
  locally cyclic. Each finitely generated `<S, u>` is free with `u` central,
  hence cyclic. So `C(u)` is amenable, hence cyclic.
- **The set `D(A, A')`.** Let `A <= <r>` and `A' <= <r'>` be maximal cyclic,
  and let `x in D(A, A')`. Some `u ≠ 1` lies in `<r> ∩ x <r'> x^{-1}`. The
  centraliser `C(u)` is cyclic and contains both maximal cyclic groups `<r>`
  and `x <r'> x^{-1}`, so both equal `C(u)`.
- **Uniqueness of `x` up to `<r'>`.** Suppose `x, y` both conjugate `<r'>`
  onto `<r>`. Then `z = x^{-1} y` normalises `<r'>`, so `z r' z^{-1} =
  r'^{±1}`. In the free group `<z, r'>` the minus sign forces `r' = 1`. So
  `z in C(r') = <r'>`, and `D(A, A') ⊆ x_0 <r'> ⊆ <x_0, r'>`, which is
  finitely generated. ∎

## 4. Where the analysis stops (height `>= 3` for `Gamma_mal`)

Theorem C needs (FH) at the vertex being descended to. Take `K_1 = Gamma_mal
*_{<w>} B_1` with `B_1` arbitrary. Then (FH) can fail for edge groups
`A_2 <= B_1` at the vertex `K_1` of `K_2`, because `D_{K_1}(A_2, A_2)`
contains `Comm_{B_1}(A_2)`, which need not be finitely generated.

So the induction of Theorem C does not propagate upward. Whether a height-3
tower reaches `Gamma_mal` is open.

What does survive:
- Theorem A still forces `K_1 in T`.
- Any `x in Gamma_mal` with `A_2 ∩ x A_2 x^{-1}` infinite has the joint
  stabiliser of two edges at a `Gamma_mal`-vertex in a conjugate of
  `<w> ∩ x <w> x^{-1}`. That is trivial unless `x in <root of w>`.

This suggests that sweeping letters from `Gamma_mal` itself stays confined to
finitely many cyclic cosets. It is not proved: a sweeping `A_2` can pass
through `Gamma_mal`-vertices only along letters in root subgroups, but the
bookkeeping across `B_1`-vertices with non-finitely generated commensurators
was not completed.
