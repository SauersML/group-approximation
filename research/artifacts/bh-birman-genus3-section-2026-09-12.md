# The genus-3 Birman sequence does not virtually split

Lane `bh-birman-genus3-section`, 2026-09-12. This follows up the genus-3 question left by
`bh-out-fn-closed-mcg` (ce98de0fc).

## 0. Result

**Claim** (`birman-sequence-genus-three-does-not-virtually-split`).
- Let `Σ = Σ_3` be closed and orientable, and let `p: Mod(Σ, ∗) → Mod(Σ)` forget the marked point.
- There is no finite-index subgroup `L ≤ Mod(Σ)` with a homomorphism `σ: L → Mod(Σ, ∗)` such that
  `p∘σ = id_L`.

**Corollary** (`genus-three-mcg-has-no-covering-lift-to-marked-covers`). The BFFHZ one-puncture
covering template fails in genus 3 as well, not only from genus 4 on. Boone--Higman for `Mod(S_3)`
stays OPEN.

**How.** This adapts Chen--Salter's proof of their Theorem A (arXiv:1804.11235v1). They state it
for `g ≥ 4`, over finite-index subgroups of the Torelli group. Here it is proved for `g = 3`, over
finite-index subgroups of the full mapping class group. Four changes do it (§3):
- every rank bound uses twists outside Torelli;
- a forget-and-restrict argument replaces the pseudo-Anosov case analysis;
- a one-sided `H_1` argument replaces the two-sided Euler-class lemma;
- two disjoint handle curves put the marked point on a side of genus 2.

**Scope.** The Torelli-level form of Chen--Salter's "Moreover" clause is **not** claimed in genus 3.

**Status.**
- Not independently reviewed, and no Lean.
- There was no novelty search: WebSearch was unavailable, and arXiv listings were not searched.
- Luo--Watanabe (v2, 2025) cite Chen--Salter for `g ≥ 4`. They prove genus 3 only for subgroups
  containing the Johnson kernel. No source read here states the general genus-3 case.

## 2. Where Chen--Salter use g ≥ 4

Quoted from the extracted text.

- **(G1) Lemma 2.9, Step 2.** "This contradicts the fact that the centralizer of σ((TaT−1b)k)
  contains a subgroup Z2g−3, since g≥ 4 and hence 2g− 3> 3." Here `Z^{2g−3}` is a Torelli
  centralizer. In genus 3 its rank equals the bound 3, so there is no contradiction.
- **(G2) Lemma 2.9(2).** "The same argument works for Tmc∈ Γ the Dehn twist about a separating curve
  c as long as both components of Σg\{c} have genus two or greater." Every essential separating curve
  of `Σ_3` has a side of genus 1. On that side Torelli is only `⟨T_c⟩`, so Step 1's commuting
  separating twist does not exist.
- **(G3) Lemma 2.15.**
  - The text: "since p≥ 2 and q≥ 2 by assumption, b1(π1(UT Σp)×π1(UT Σq)) =b1(π1(Σp)×π1(Σq))".
  - For `q = 1`, `π1(UT Σ_1) = Z^3`, and the rational Betti count is `7` against `6`. The Euler class
    of extension (3) then vanishes rationally, so the lemma fails for `q = 1`.
- **(G4) Section 3.**
  - "By the hypothesis that g≥ 4, there exists a separating simple closed curve c⊂ Σg that divides Σg
    into subsurfaces Σp,1 and Σq,1 with p,q≥ 2."
  - "Without loss of generality, we assume that the marked point ∗∈ Σp,1."
- **(G5) Uses of p ≥ 2** in Lemmas 3.3, 3.5--3.8 and §3.4:
  - the surface-group facts in Lemma 3.3;
  - "Since p≥ 2, an easy argument...";
  - `χ(Σ_p) ≠ 0`.

  These need only the genus of the side carrying the marked point.

## 3. The genus-3 proof

**Setting.**
- Suppose `L` and `σ` exist. Then `σ` is injective, and it may be restricted to finite-index subgroups
  at will. `T_e` is the Dehn twist about `e`, and `CRS` is the canonical reduction system.
- Chen--Salter's Propositions 2.2--2.7 and Lemma 2.8 hold in any `Mod(S)`, and are used as they are.

**Restriction.**
- Let `f ∈ Mod(Σ, ∗)` fix each curve of a multicurve `M̃` and each component of `Σ∖M̃`.
- For a component `C̃`, `ρ_{C̃}(f)` is the restriction to `C̃`, with its boundary collapsed to
  punctures.
- The cutting homomorphism `f ↦ (ρ_{C̃}(f))_{C̃}` has kernel generated by the twists about `M̃`
  (Farb--Margalit Prop. 3.20).

**(F) Compatibility.**
- **Setting.** Let `C̃` project to the component `C` of `Σ∖p(M̃)`, where parallel copies are
  identified.
- **Statement.** Then `ρ_C(p(f)) = forget_∗(ρ_{C̃}(f))`. Here `forget_∗` is the identity when
  `∗ ∉ C̃`.
- **Proof.** The same representative diffeomorphism computes both sides.

**(R) Rank.**
- **Twists.** A pants decomposition of `Σ_3` has 6 curves. Powers of their twists lie in `L` and
  generate `Z^6`.
- **Centralizer bound.** Let `g` have CRS `M̃` and be pseudo-Anosov on `j` components. Suppose every
  other component has finite pure mapping class group modulo boundary twists. Then the centralizer of
  `g` is virtually abelian of rank `≤ |M̃| + j`, by McCarthy (Chen--Salter Prop. 2.6).

### Lemma A: bounding pairs (Chen--Salter Lemma 2.9(1))

Let `{a, b}` be a bounding pair in `Σ_3`; its sides `S_1, S_2` are both `Σ_{1,2}`. Let `x = T_a T_b^{-1}`
and `x^k ∈ L`. After enlarging `k`, and up to swapping `a` and `b`,

    σ(x^k) = (T_{a'} T_{b'}^{-1})^k (T_{a'}^{-1} T_{a''})^n,

where `a', a''` are isotopic to `a` and `b'` to `b` in `Σ_3`.

**Step 1: every curve of CRS(σ(x^k)) is isotopic to `a` or `b`.** Follow Chen--Salter Claim 2.10.
- **Case 1** (`γ` meets `a ∪ b`) is unchanged.
- **Case 2.**
  - Here `γ` lies in some `S_i`, essential and nonperipheral. Take any curve `δ ⊂ S_i` with
    `i(δ, γ) ≠ 0`, and `T_δ^r ∈ L`.
  - `σ(T_δ^r)` commutes with `σ(x^k)`, so it permutes the CRS; a power fixes `γ'`.
  - Applying `p`, a power of `T_δ^r` fixes `γ`, which Prop. 2.3 forbids.

**Step 2: curves isotopic to `a` and to `b` both occur.** Suppose none is isotopic to `a`.
- **Empty CRS.** Then `σ(x^k)`, of infinite order, is pseudo-Anosov, and its centralizer is
  virtually cyclic. But it contains `σ(Z^6)`, for a pants decomposition through `a` and `b`.
- **Otherwise.** The CRS is `{b'}` or `{b', b''}`. The components are `C ≅ Σ_3∖b`, of genus 2, and
  possibly the once-punctured annulus `P`.
  - If `σ(x^k)` is the identity on `C`, some power is a multitwist about curves isotopic to `b`.
    Applying `p` gives `x^{kN} = T_b^j`, which is false.
  - If it is pseudo-Anosov on `C`, (R) bounds the rank by 3, against `6`.

**Step 3: `σ(x^k)` is a multitwist.** Suppose a power is pseudo-Anosov on a component `C̃` over a side
`S`.
- **Setup.** Take `δ ⊂ S` essential and nonperipheral, with `T_δ^r ∈ L`, raised to a power that
  fixes the components.
- **Commuting.** `ψ = ρ_{C̃}(σ(T_δ^r))` commutes with the pseudo-Anosov `φ = ρ_{C̃}(σ(x^k))`, so
  `ψ^j = φ^i` for some `j ≥ 1`, by McCarthy.
- **Contradiction.** By (F), `forget_∗(φ) = ρ_S(x^k) = 1`, since `x^k` is a boundary multitwist of
  `S`. Also by (F), `forget_∗(ψ) = ρ_S(T_δ^r) = T_δ^r`. So `T_δ^{rj} = 1` in `Mod(S^)`, which is
  false.

So some power is a multitwist about curves isotopic to `a` or `b`, including `a'` and `b'`. There is
one marked point, so at most one of `a`, `b` is doubled. `p` fixes the exponents.

### Lemma B: separating twists (Chen--Salter Lemma 2.9(2), in genus 3)

Every essential separating curve `c` of `Σ_3` bounds a one-holed torus `T`; call the other side `R`,
of genus 2. If `T_c^k ∈ L` then `σ(T_c^k) = T_{c'}^{k-n} T_{c''}^n`, with `c'` and `c''` isotopic to
`c`.

**Proof.** Run Steps 1--3 of Lemma A with `c` in place of `a ∪ b`.
- **Step 1.** The crossing curve `δ` exists on either side.
- **Step 2.** An empty CRS contradicts (R), against `6`.
- **Step 3.** Take `δ` nonperipheral in `T` or in `R`. There is no pseudo-Anosov on `P`.

### Lemma C: a single lift (replaces Chen--Salter Lemmas 2.15--2.16)

In Lemma B, `σ(T_c^k) = T_{c̃}^k` for a single curve `c̃`.

**Proof.**
- **Setup.** Suppose the CRS is `{c_T, c_R}`, both exponents nonzero, cobounding the once-punctured
  annulus `P`. The components are `T̃` (adjacent to `c_T`), `P` and `R̃` (adjacent to `c_R`). Neither
  `T̃` nor `R̃` contains `∗`.
- **Disk-pushing group.** Let `D = π1(UT Σ_2) ≤ Mod(R)`, with center generated by `T_c` (Chen--Salter
  Prop. 2.7(3)). `Mod(R) → Mod(Σ_3)` is injective, because `c` bounds a one-holed torus and not a disk
  or once-punctured disk (Farb--Margalit Thm. 3.18). Let `D'' ≤ D ∩ L` have finite index, with
  `σ(D'')` fixing curves and components.
- **Restrictions.** For `h ∈ D''`:
  - `ρ_{T̃}(σ(h)) = ρ_T(h) = 1` by (F), since `h` is supported on `R`;
  - `ρ_P(σ(h)) = 1`, because the pure mapping class group of a thrice-punctured sphere is trivial.
- **Splitting.**
  - The cutting sequence puts `σ(D'')` inside `Mod(R̃)·⟨T_{c_T}⟩`.
  - That subgroup is `Mod(R̃) × ⟨T_{c_T}⟩`: the supports are disjoint, and `c_T` is neither isotopic
    into `R̃` nor to `c_R`.
  - So `σ(h) = μ(h) T_{c_T}^{ν(h)}`, with `ν: D'' → Z` a homomorphism.
- **The center.** For `h = T_c^{kN} ∈ D''`, `ν(h) ≠ 0`, because the exponent of `c_T` is nonzero.
  - `D''` is a central extension of a finite-index subgroup of `π1(Σ_2)` by `D'' ∩ ⟨T_c⟩`.
  - Its rational Euler class is a nonzero multiple of the restriction of `e(UT Σ_2)`. That class is
    nonzero because `χ(Σ_2) = −2`, and restriction to finite index is injective on `H^2(−; Q)`.
  - By the five-term exact sequence, the center vanishes in `H_1(D''; Q)`, so `ν(T_c^{kN}) = 0`.
    Contradiction.

Only the genus-2 side is used, which is why `q = 1` is allowed.

### Lemma D: placing the marked point

**Statement.** Let `c_1, c_2` be disjoint and bound disjoint one-holed tori `T_1, T_2`. With `c̃_i`
from Lemma C, the point `∗` lies in the genus-2 side `R̃_i ≅ Σ_{2,1,∗}` for some `i`.

**Proof.**
- **Disjoint lifts.** The two lifts commute, so `c̃_1` and `c̃_2` are disjoint (Prop. 2.4).
- **No nesting of curves.** If `c̃_2 ⊂ T̃_1`, then `c̃_2` is peripheral in `T̃_1` up to the marked
  point, so `c_2 ≃ c_1`. That is false.
- **No nesting of tori.** If `T̃_2 ⊋ T̃_1`, the difference has genus 0, so again `c_1 ≃ c_2`.
- **Conclusion.** So `T̃_1 ∩ T̃_2 = ∅`, and `∗` lies in at most one of them.

### Chen--Salter Section 3, with p = 2 and c = c_i from Lemma D

1. **Support.**
   - For `h ∈ Mod(R) ∩ L`, `σ(h)` commutes with `T_{c̃}^k`.
   - By (F), `ρ_{T̃}(σ(h)) = ρ_T(h) = 1`, so `σ(h)` is supported on `R̃ = Σ̃_{2,1,∗}`.
   - This replaces the "standard argument" at the start of their Section 3.
2. **Diagram (6)**, the map `s̃ = p_{1,∗}∘σ` on `H = π1(UT Σ_2) ∩ L`, and Lemma 3.1 go through as
   written, with Lemma C in place of Lemma 2.16.
3. **Lemma 3.2 and the input to Lemma 3.3: `s(α^k) = α^{m(α,k)}` for every simple `α`.**
   - **Nonseparating `α`.** The push map is a genus-(1,1) bounding pair map `T_{α_L} T_{α_R}^{-1}`
     in `Σ_3`. Lemma A replaces Lemma 2.9(1), and computations (10)--(11) are unchanged.
   - **Separating `α` in `Σ_2`.** `α_L` and `α_R` are separating curves of `Σ_3`, each bounding a
     one-holed torus in `R`.
     - Lemma C gives `σ((T_{α_L} T_{α_R}^{-1})^k) = T_{α̃_L}^k T_{α̃_R}^{-k}`.
     - After capping `c̃`, the lifts `α̃_L` and `α̃_R` cobound an annulus in `Σ_{2,∗}`, so
       `s(α^k) ∈ {1, α^{±k}}`.
   - **Why separating curves matter.** Lemma 3.6 applies Corollary 3.4(B) to elements conjugate to
     a separating curve `d`. Chen--Salter's text derives the formula through bounding pairs only; in
     genus 3, Lemma C covers the separating case.
4. **Lemma 3.3, Corollary 3.4, and Lemmas 3.5--3.8** are arguments in `π1(Σ_p)` that need only
   `p ≥ 2`. They hold for `p = 2`: separating curves `d ⊂ R` exist and fill `Σ_2`.
5. **Section 3.4.** `(i × s)^∗[Δ] = 0`, but it equals `χ(Σ_2)[H̄] ≠ 0` in Case (A), and
   `[H̄] ≠ 0` in Case (B). Contradiction. `∎`

## 4. Consequences

- **Covering lifts.** `outer-covering-lifts-are-virtual-sections` (ii) turns any covering lift to a
  marked cover into a virtual section. The corollary claim kills the BFFHZ one-puncture template in
  genus 3, and the route `closed-mcg-bh-via-covering-lift-to-marked-cover` is dead for every `g ≥ 3`.
- **Boone--Higman for closed surfaces** (`closed-surface-mapping-class-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`)
  stays OPEN in genus 3. Two routes survive:
  - non-covering embeddings into punctured hosts (`closed-mcgs-virtually-embed-in-punctured-mcgs`);
  - PIP homeomorphisms of the Thurston boundary.
- **Luo--Watanabe.** The discrete consequence of their Theorem 1 is a special case of the claim. Their
  profinite statement is not.

- **Imported facts.** Used as standard, not re-read from source:
  - Farb--Margalit Prop. 3.20 and Thm. 3.18;
  - McCarthy's centralizer theorem;
  - Kra's theorem, inside Chen--Salter Prop. 2.7(5).
- **Review.** No independent review. Chen--Salter's argument descends from Mess's 1990 preprint,
  which contained an error, so Lemmas A--D should get line-by-line review.
- **Torelli level.** Lemma A Step 2 and Lemma B Step 2 use twists outside Torelli, so the genus-3
  Torelli-level statement is not addressed.
