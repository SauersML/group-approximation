# The canonical index-seven class has primitive integral evaluation

20 September 2026. This computes a previously unspecified integral feature
of the actual central cover used in wave 2. It does not bound its central
orders over all finite quotients and does not solve Problem 141.

## Statement

Let G=GHB2(7), chi:G->C7 send a,b,c to one, and K=ker chi. Label the
seven faces of K\Y by a^i, 0<=i<7. Let c0 in H2-cohomology(K;Z) be the
pullback of the integer cochain taking value one on face zero and zero
on the other faces, along the Borel-to-orbit map. Then

    c0(H_2(K;Z)) = Z.

Consequently, in its central extension

    1 -> <z>=Z -> B_c0 -> K -> 1,

the central generator z lies in [B_c0,B_c0], and the map on abelianizations
is an isomorphism B_c0^ab -> K^ab. In particular this center has no
nontrivial abelian finite detector.

## Finite stabilizers: an exact certificate

The source CCKW matrix realization is injective on each vertex group.
The standard-library script
`experiments/hrf-wave3-canonical-class-2026-09-20/stabilizers.py`
enumerates each vertex while tracking the homomorphism sending its two
edge generators x,y to one in C7. The kernel is generated by
x*y^-1 and [x,y]. All its elements have seventh power one. The results are:

| vertex | vertex order | kernel order | kernel derived order | kernel abelianization |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| X0=<a,b> | 343 | 49 | 1 | C7^2 |
| X1=<c,b> | 2401 | 343 | 7 | C7^2 |
| X2=<c,a> | 2401 | 343 | 7 | C7^2 |

The script uses exact integer arithmetic modulo seven, checks character
labels for every multiplication in its enumeration, checks generation of
the entire kernel, and computes the normal closure of the commutator of
its two generators. It assumes only the already established injectivity
of the CCKW vertex realization. Output is `stabilizers.json` beside it.

## Integral spectral sequence and the deck-module argument

K\Y has three vertices, three edges, and seven faces, each attached along
the same oriented three-edge loop. Its first homology is zero and its
second homology is the augmentation lattice

    D = {(n0,...,n6) in Z^7 : sum ni=0}.

K-edge and K-face stabilizers are trivial. The degree-one part of the
integral equivariant homology spectral sequence therefore gives the
exact sequence

    H_2(K;Z) -> D --d2--> (C7)^6 -> H_1(K;Z) -> 0.

No other differential can leave bidegree (2,0): the complex has dimension
two. Write L=ker d2, the image of the first map. Its target has exponent
seven, so 7D<=L<=D. The construction is equivariant for the deck action
C7, which cyclically permutes face coordinates. Thus L/7D is a submodule
of D/7D.

The abelianization of G is (C7)^3: the three power relations give exponent
seven and the remaining relators have zero exponent sum. Restriction to
K maps onto the two-dimensional kernel of the coordinate-sum map
(C7)^3->C7. Hence H_1(K;Z) maps onto (C7)^2. In the displayed exact
sequence the image of d2 has dimension at most four, so L/7D has
dimension at least two and in particular is nonzero.

Identify D/7D with the augmentation ideal in

    F7[C7] = F7[t]/(t-1)^7.

This is the length-six indecomposable cyclic module (t-1). Every nonzero
submodule contains its one-dimensional socle (t-1)^6. In characteristic
seven that socle vector is 1+t+...+t^6, whose seven coordinates all
equal one. Therefore L contains a vector whose face-zero coordinate is
one modulo seven. On the other hand 7D<=L supplies face-zero value seven.
The ideal of face-zero evaluations on L consequently has gcd one.
This proves the asserted primitive evaluation. In particular it is not
an inference merely from rational nonvanishing.

The homology five-term sequence for B_c0 now sends H_2(K;Z) onto its
central Z, giving the stated assertion about its abelianization.

## What every finite central detector must now supply

For a finite image rho:B_c0->F, replace F by its image and put
A=<rho(z)>, Q=F/A. The subgroup A is central and cyclic. Primitivity
puts rho(z) in [F,F], hence A->[F^ab] is zero. The five-term sequence

    H_2(F;Z) -> H_2(Q;Z) -> A -> F^ab -> Q^ab -> 0

therefore makes H_2(Q;Z)->A surjective. In particular

    ord(rho(z)) divides exponent(H_2(Q;Z)).

This conclusion does not require Q perfect. If Q has trivial Schur
multiplier, then rho(z)=1. More generally uniformly bounded multiplier
exponents on a particular quotient family give bounded central orders
only on that family. They give no bound on all finite quotients unless
that family has been proved exhaustive.

The theorem supplies a sharper screening condition for the actual c0
cover. It establishes neither unbounded central visibility nor a uniform
bound over all finite quotient families.
