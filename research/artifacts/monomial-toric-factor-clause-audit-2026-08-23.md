# Monomial toric factor-clause audit — 2026-08-23

## Outcome

The direct generator-visible groupification of a finite-phase monomial game
does not survive the full normal-closure requirement.  In any full tracial
representation of a two-player toric quotient, every player component of a
clause is central in that player's von Neumann algebra.  On an extreme
synchronous trace, that algebra is a factor, so every such component is a
scalar.  Therefore the finite Fourier projections of the quantum-reflection
entries cannot be spectral projections of the clause components if they are
to generate the noncommutative monomial algebra.

This does not refute a groupification using noncentral decoded words and
separate central shadow clauses.  It proves that such a decoder is
load-bearing: simply promoting each Fourier answer projection to a cyclic
clause letter necessarily classicalizes the corner.

## 1. Centrality in every full toric model

Let

```text
Gamma = B x G_A x G_B
```

and let the toric clauses be

```text
r_t = beta_t a_t b_t,
a_t in G_A,  b_t in G_B.
```

Suppose `rho` is a tracial representation of the **full** universal group
algebra, faithful on the scalar phase group, and

```text
||rho(r_t)-1||_2=0.                                    (MTF1)
```

Pass to the trace GNS image, where the trace is faithful.  Then `(MTF1)` is
the operator identity

```text
beta_t rho_A(a_t) rho_B(b_t)=1.                        (MTF2)
```

Put

```text
M_A=rho_A(G_A)'',    M_B=rho_B(G_B)''.
```

The player algebras commute.  Rearranging `(MTF2)` gives

```text
rho_A(a_t)=conjugate(beta_t) rho_B(b_t)^*.
```

The left side lies in `M_A`; the right side commutes with `M_A`.  Hence

```text
rho_A(a_t) in Z(M_A),    rho_B(b_t) in Z(M_B).         (MTF3)
```

If `M_A` (respectively `M_B`) is a factor, its clause components are scalar.
This is the representation-level form of the normal toric quotient formula:
in the quotient by the normal closure, each `a_t` is centralized in the
Alice quotient and each `b_t` in the Bob quotient.

## 2. Consequence for the monomial Fourier projections

Let `A_mon` be an `s`-monomial game algebra and let

```text
e_(ki)^p=(1/s) sum_(r=0)^(s-1) zeta^(-rp) u_(ki)^r   (MTF4)
```

be its sudoku/Fourier projections.  They generate `A_mon`, because
`u_(ki)=sum_p zeta^p e_(ki)^p` and the entries `u_(ki)` generate the
monomial algebra.

Assume a proposed full-corner toric compiler maps every projection in
`(MTF4)` into the spectral algebra generated by the clause player components.
By `(MTF3)`, that spectral algebra is central.  Hence all the images of the
`e_(ki)^p` are central, and the generated corner is commutative.

If `A_mon` has a trace and no nonzero homomorphism to `R^omega`, this is
impossible.  Choose an extreme trace.  Its GNS algebra is a factor.  A
commutative factor is `C`, which would give a character

```text
A_mon -> C -> R^omega,
```

contrary to the gap hypothesis.  The same contradiction applies directly
to any claimed generator-preserving full corner of the phase-safe normal
quotient: compressed spectral projections of central elements remain
central.

Thus a valid compiler must keep the Fourier projections in a genuinely
noncentral decoded algebra which is **not** generated by clause components.
The clauses can see only central shadows/projective-kernel words, and a
separate whole-face decoder must recover every losing monomial projection.

## 3. Compactness of a genuine projective-kernel face gap

There is one useful positive reduction.  Let `G` be a group, let `K normal G`,
and let

```text
chi:K -> mu_q
```

be conjugation invariant.  Let `S_fd(G)` denote finite-dimensional vector
states and set

```text
F_(K,chi)={phi: phi(conjugate(chi(k)) k)=1 for every k in K}.
```

If

```text
F_(K,chi) intersect closure(S_fd(G)) = empty,          (MTF5)
```

then a finite subset `F subset K` already has this property.  Indeed, the
closed fixed-point faces for all `k in K` have empty intersection with the
compact set `closure(S_fd(G))`; the finite-intersection property supplies a
finite subfamily.

Moreover the graph

```text
C_chi={(chi(k)^(-1),k):k in K} <= mu_q x G             (MTF6)
```

is normal.  Therefore the normal closure of the finitely many graph elements
from `F` is contained in `C_chi` and meets `mu_q` trivially.  Once the entire
finite-phase projective-kernel face gap `(MTF5)` has been proved, neither
finite extraction nor scalar phase survival is an additional problem.

Two obligations remain, and neither follows from non-Connes-embeddability of
one trace:

1. prove `(MTF5)` for a finite-phase normal subgroup of the monomial extreme
   trace's projective kernel; and
2. realize the resulting finite binomial list as a determining set of a
   finite two-player game, with a decoder for its whole finite-dimensional
   face.

This is the narrowed projective-kernel route.  The first obligation is the
relative-FD character-face gap; the second is testability.  Local preservation
of the monomial answer table proves neither.

## 4. Stronger CE-face separation bypasses game testability

There is a direct root route if the face is separated from all
Connes-embeddable **traces**, rather than only from finite-dimensional vector
states.  Let `T_CE(G)` be the weak-star compact CE trace set.  If

```text
F_(K,chi) intersect T_CE(G)=empty,                      (MTF7)
```

compactness again gives a finite subset `F subset K`.  Form

```text
Q_F=(mu_q x G)/normalClosure({(chi(k)^(-1),k):k in F}). (MTF8)
```

The graph-normality calculation proves that `mu_q` embeds.  If `Q_F` were
hyperlinear, the defining-character central Fourier corner of `L(Q_F)` would
be Connes embeddable.  Its normalized trace pulls back to a CE trace of `G`
fixing every graph clause from `F`, contradicting the compact separation.
Thus `Q_F` is finitely presented and nonhyperlinear.

This bypass is useful but not cheap: `(MTF7)` is already a nonhyperlinear
central-extension certificate.  The original monomial gap only excludes CE
traces satisfying the full losing-projection ideal.  It does not show that
scalar projective-kernel words cut out that ideal on every trace.

## 7. Normal phase safety closes the finite-game face by a product state

For clauses `(beta_i,a_i,b_i)` normal closure makes the images of `a_i` and
`b_i` central in their player quotients.  If the normal closure meets `B`
trivially, `(RTF10)` says exactly that
`(abar_i,bbar_i) -> conjugate(beta_i)` is a well-defined character on the
subgroup generated by the central word pairs.  Extend that character to the
direct product of the two generated abelian groups, then split it into Alice
and Bob characters.  State extension to `C*(G_A)` and `C*(G_B)` gives a
product state fixed by every determining clause.

Each one-player algebra is RFD, so approximate the two states separately by
finite-dimensional vector states and tensor them.  Therefore

```text
normalClosure(clauses) intersect B={1}
   ==> omega_qa=1.                                      (MTF9)
```

This refutes every finite phase-safe toric `qc/qa` gap.

## 8. The finite-dimensional projective-kernel face is never empty

For the monomial extreme trace, a scalar projective-kernel equation in the
standard factor representation has the form

```text
L(A_a)R(B_b)=lambda I.
```

Thus `L(A_a)=lambda R(B_b)^*` lies in
`L(M) intersect R(M)=C`, so both player components are scalar.  The product
of the two restricted source states satisfies the whole projective kernel,
and separate one-player RFD approximates it by finite-dimensional vector
states.  Hence

```text
F_(K,chi) intersect closure(S_fd(G_A x G_B)) != empty.  (MTF10)
```

This refutes `(MPK2)`.  It does not refute the CE-**tracial** face route: the
product state above need not be tracial or Connes embeddable.  The remaining
viable cascade is

```text
monomial-finite-phase-ce-kernel-face-gap
 -> ce-projective-kernel-face-gap-gives-nonhyperlinear-quotient
 -> non-hyperlinear-group.
```
