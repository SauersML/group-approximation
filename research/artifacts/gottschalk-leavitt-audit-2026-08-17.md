# 2026-08-17 — Gottschalk surjunctivity via the Leavitt unit group (ingestion audit)

unrefereed.  It audits this repository's `GroupApproximation/Leavitt/` code

**Headline of the source, which this audit confirms.**  Nothing in this
repository proves or disproves Gottschalk's conjecture (every group is
surjunctive: for every finite alphabet `A`, every injective cellular
automaton `A^G -> A^G` is surjective).  What the repository does supply,
together with a short duality argument, is an explicit injective
non-surjective four-site linear cellular automaton on a **proper closed
invariant subshift** of `F_2^(R^x)`, plus an exact statement of the group-ring
lifting problem that separates that from a full-shift counterexample.

**Every derivation in the source was re-checked line by line this session and

## Four corrections and additions this ingestion makes

Three of these strengthen the source; one is an attribution error.

### 1. The subshift theorem carries **no** surjunctivity content at all

The source's Section 11 correctly warns that a strict self-embedding of a
proper invariant subshift does not give a full-shift counterexample, and
calls the tempting inference "false".  It supports this only by observing
that one particular extension fails (its Section 7).  The warning can be
upgraded to a **proof**, and the proof shows the gap is total rather than
accidental.

**Proposition.**  There is a finitely generated **free** group `F` for which
the entire construction of the source's Sections 3–6 goes through verbatim:
a surjection `F_2[F] ->> R`, a proper closed `F`-invariant linear subshift
`X = ker(pi)^perp ( F_2^F`, and an explicit finite-memory linear cellular
automaton on `X` that is injective and not surjective.

*Proof.*  By the unit-span theorem (`unitAdditiveSpan_eq_top`), each of
`s_0, s_1, t_0, t_1` is a finite sum of units of `R`.  Let `U <= R^x` be the
subgroup generated by the finitely many units so used, and let `F` be the
free group on that finite generating set, mapped onto `U`.  The `F_2`-span
of a subgroup of `R^x` is closed under multiplication and contains `1`, so it
is a unital subring; it contains `s_0, s_1, t_0, t_1`, hence is all of `R`.
Therefore `pi : F_2[F] -> R` is a surjective unital ring homomorphism.  Its
kernel is nonzero, because `F_2[F]` carries the augmentation `F_2[F] -> F_2`
while `R` admits no unital ring homomorphism to `F_2` (Section A below), so
`pi` cannot be an isomorphism.  Everything else in Sections 4–6 uses only
that `pi` is a surjection onto `R` with nonzero kernel and that
`M_(s_0) : r |-> r s_0` is surjective and not injective on `R`.  []

Free groups are residually finite, hence sofic, hence **surjunctive** by
Gromov–Weiss.  So the hypotheses of the source's strict-embedding theorem are
satisfied over a group for which the desired conclusion is known **false**.
The inference "strict self-embedding of an invariant subshift `=>` not
surjunctive" therefore has a counterexample, and no repair of the particular
lift can rescue it.

One half of this is even independent of Gromov–Weiss: `F_2[F]` for `F` free
is a domain (free groups are biorderable), hence directly finite, so the
subshift data cannot imply failure of direct finiteness either.  The
external input is needed only for the sharper phrasing.

This also **subsumes** the source's Section 7.  That the specific lift
`a = [uv] + [u] + [v] + [w]` has augmentation `0` and therefore extends to a
non-injective map is one instance of a general failure, not the obstacle.

### 2. The lifting problem is an equivalence, not merely a sufficient condition

The source separates its Section 7 (parity kills this lift) from its
Section 9 (a corrected lift would suffice).  They are the same statement.

For `a` in `S = F_2[G]`, the dual map `T_a = R_a^*` on the full shift is
injective if and only if `R_a : s |-> s a` is surjective, and `R_a` is
surjective if and only if `a` has a **left inverse** in `S` (if `b a = 1`
then `s = (s b) a`; conversely surjectivity produces `b` with `b a = 1`).
So:

> **Criterion.**  For any `a` in `F_2[R^x]` with `pi(a) = s_0`, the cellular
> automaton `T_a` on the full shift `F_2^(R^x)` is injective **iff** `a` is
> left-invertible in `F_2[R^x]`; and in that case it is automatically not
> surjective.

The second half: if `b a = 1` then `pi(a b) = s_0 t_0 != 1`, so `a b != 1`,
so `R_a` is not injective (else `R_a R_b R_a = R_a` cancels to `R_b R_a =
id`), so its dual is not surjective.  Hence a single left-invertible lift of
`s_0` disproves Gottschalk's conjecture, and the augmentation condition
`eps(a) = 1` — necessary, since `eps(b) eps(a) = 1` — is only the first of
infinitely many necessary conditions.

This is what makes the graph node for the lift a genuine target rather than a
restatement: it is strictly stronger than failure of direct finiteness (which
asks for *some* one-sided inverse pair, with no constraint on its image under
`pi`).

### 3. The evaluation map admits no **ring** section, by this repository's own no-go

The source's Theorem 10.1 asks for a splitting of `pi` as a map of left
`F_2[G]`-modules, and does not say why the obvious stronger thing is not
available.  It is not available, for a two-line reason already in this graph —
and, as it turns out, already recorded there by the Kaplansky lane
(`leavitt-corner-idempotent-unital-surjection`), which is why this ingestion
adds no node for it and cites that one instead.

A unital ring section `sigma : R -> F_2[G]` of `pi` would exhibit a binary
Leavitt family inside `F_2[G]`.  Composing with the augmentation
`eps : F_2[G] -> F_2` gives a unital ring homomorphism `R -> F_2`, which
cannot exist: `t_0 s_0 = 1` forces `eps(sigma(t_0)) = eps(sigma(s_0)) = 1`,
`t_1 s_1 = 1` likewise forces `eps(sigma(t_1)) = eps(sigma(s_1)) = 1`, and
then `t_0 s_1 = 0` reads `1 = 0`.  The same computation with `F_2` in place
of `Z` is the graph's `augmentation-blocks-unital-leavitt-family`, whose
matrix half (rank over the residue field) likewise rules out a Leavitt family
in `M_n(F_2[G])` for every finite `n`.

So the *module* splitting is the only kind on offer, and the distance between
"module summand" and "subring" is exactly where the argument must be won.

### 4. Attribution: the nonsoficity of `R^x` is an unrefereed announcement

The source repeatedly writes "the newly constructed nonsofic group" and "the
current manuscript in the repository proves/announces that `G` is nonsofic".
The nonsoficity of `L_(F_2)(1,2)^x` is an **external** announcement (OpenAI,
2026-08-01), recorded in this graph as `openai-leavitt-unit-nonsofic` and
deliberately **not** established, per repository doctrine on unrefereed
inputs.  It is not a theorem of this repository.

Fortunately nothing in the mathematics depends on it.  Every statement in the
source — and every node added from it — is unconditional, and would read the
same if `R^x` turned out to be sofic.  Soficity enters only as motivation:
`R^x` is interesting here because a *sofic* group is already known to be
surjunctive, so only a nonsofic candidate could possibly work.

## A. The evaluation map (source Section 3, verified)

Let `R = L_(F_2)(1,2) = F_2<s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1 | t_i s_j = delta_ij,
s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1 = 1>`, let `G = R^x`, and let `S = F_2[G]` with evaluation

    pi : S ->> R,   pi(sum c_g [g]) = sum c_g g,     K = ker pi.

*Surjective.*  `unitAdditiveSpan_eq_top` (`UnitAdditiveSpan.lean:108`) says
the additive subgroup generated by the units of a ring carrying a binary
Leavitt family is the whole ring; in characteristic two an additive
combination of units is an `F_2`-linear combination of units, which is a
value of `pi`.

*No unital ring homomorphism `phi : R -> F_2`.*  `t_0 s_0 = 1` forces
`phi(t_0) = phi(s_0) = 1`; `t_1 s_1 = 1` forces `phi(t_1) = phi(s_1) = 1`;
`t_0 s_1 = 0` then reads `1 = 0`.

*Kernel nonzero.*  If `K = 0` then `pi` is an isomorphism and the
augmentation `eps` transports to such a `phi`.

*Kernel not inside the augmentation ideal.*  If `eps(K) = 0` then `eps`
factors through `S/K ~ R`, again producing `phi`.  So some `k` in `K` has
`eps(k) = 1`, and the parity of a lift is not an invariant.

## B. The annihilator subshift and the four-site rule (Sections 4–6, verified)

`S` is the direct sum of one copy of `F_2` per group element, so
`S^* ~ F_2^G` with `x(g) = x([g])`.  Put

    X = K^perp = {x : x(k) = 0 for all k in K} = im(pi^*).

Each `k` imposes one finite-support linear equation, so `X` is closed; `K` is
a left ideal, so `X` is shift-invariant; `K != 0` gives `X ( F_2^G` proper.

Right multiplication `M_(s_0) : R -> R`, `r |-> r s_0`, is surjective
(`(r t_0) s_0 = r`) and not injective (`t_1 s_0 = 0`, `t_1 != 0` because
`t_1 s_1 = 1`).  Its dual is therefore injective and not surjective.

The explicit lift.  With

    x = s_0^2 t_1,   y = s_1 t_0,   z = s_0 s_1 t_1,

`t_1 s_0 = 0` and `t_0 s_1 = 0` give `x^2 = y^2 = z^2 = 0`, so
`u = 1+x`, `v = 1+y`, `w = 1+z` are units (self-inverse in characteristic
two), and `x y = s_0^2 t_1 s_1 t_0 = s_0^2 t_0`.  Hence

    u v + u + v + w = x y + z = s_0^2 t_0 + s_0 s_1 t_1
                    = s_0 (s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1) = s_0.

The four units are distinct — `x, y, z` are distinct nonzero monomials in the
prefix-code normal form, and `uv = 1 + x + y + xy` differs from each — so
`a = [uv] + [u] + [v] + [w]` has a four-element memory set

    F = {uv, u, v, w},   (T_a x)(g) = x(g uv) + x(g u) + x(g v) + x(g w).

`T_a = R_a^*` and `pi . R_a = M_(s_0) . pi`, so `T_a` preserves
`X = im(pi^*)` and restricts there to a copy of `M_(s_0)^*`: injective, not
surjective.  This is the strongest correct conclusion available from the
repository.

## C. The one-sided-inverse criterion (Section 8, verified)

Let `k` be a finite field, `S = k[G]`, and suppose `B A = I_n` and
`A B != I_n` in `M_n(S)` for some finite `n >= 1`.  Right multiplication
`R_A` on row vectors `S^n` is left `S`-linear, surjective (`R_A R_B = id`)
and not injective (else cancel `R_A` in `R_A R_B R_A = R_A`).  Dualizing,
`(S^n)^* ~ (k^n)^G` and `R_A^*` is a finite-memory linear cellular automaton
on the full shift over the finite alphabet `k^n` that is injective and not
surjective.  So `G` is not surjunctive.

This is the contrapositive of an implication this repository already cites:
`official/counterexample.tex` line 109, read from source this session, says
"If `R^x` were surjunctive, then `k[R^x]` would be stably finite for every
field [BFF24, Corollary 3.25]".  So the bridge is not new — what the source
adds, and what is reproved in full above, is the explicit automaton, and what
this ingestion adds is that the bridge now has a graph node
(`stable-finiteness-failure-refutes-surjunctivity`) with a complete proof and
therefore no trust surface.

## D. The module-splitting criterion (Section 10, verified)

A left `S`-linear `sigma : R -> S` with `pi . sigma = id` gives
`S = sigma(R) (+) K` as left `S`-modules.  With `f(r) = r s_0` and
`g(r) = r t_0` on `R` — both left `S`-linear, with `f . g = id_R` and
`g . f != id_R` since `s_0 t_0 != 1` — the endomorphisms `F = f (+) id_K` and
`H = g (+) id_K` of the left regular module satisfy `F . H = id_S` and
`H . F != id_S`.  Every endomorphism of the left regular module is right
multiplication by `F(1)`, so with `a = F(1)`, `b = H(1)` this reads
`b a = 1 != a b` in `F_2[G]`, and Section C applies with `n = 1`.

`s_0 t_0 != 1`: otherwise `s_1 t_1 = 0`, and then
`0 = t_1 (s_1 t_1) s_1 = (t_1 s_1)^2 = 1`.

## E. What would actually settle it

Any one of the following disproves Gottschalk's conjecture:

1. a left-invertible lift of `s_0` in `F_2[R^x]` (equivalently, by Section 2
   above, a full-shift injective extension of the four-site rule);
2. `A, B` in `M_n(F_2[R^x])` with `B A = I_n != A B`, any finite `n`;
3. a left `F_2[R^x]`-module splitting of `pi`, which manufactures 1;
4. any nonlinear construction of an injective non-surjective cellular
   automaton over `R^x`, bypassing group-ring finiteness entirely.

Proving instead that `R^x` **is** surjunctive would not prove Gottschalk's
conjecture; it would furnish a surjunctive nonsofic group, separating the two
properties, which is itself a result worth having.

## F. Where this lands in the graph, and how it meets the Kaplansky lane

A parallel session ingested a Kaplansky direct-finiteness audit the same day,
building the lane headed by `leavitt-unit-group-algebra-not-directly-finite`.
The two ingestions overlap, and the overlap was resolved by **reuse**, not by
parallel nodes:

* The surjectivity of `pi` is theirs
  (`leavitt-unit-group-algebra-surjects-onto-leavitt`).  This ingestion's own
  surjectivity node was withdrawn and replaced by
  `leavitt-evaluation-kernel-hits-augmentation-one`, which carries only the
  two kernel facts of Section A that their node does not state.
* The no-go against a ring section was withdrawn: their
  `leavitt-corner-idempotent-unital-surjection` already records that the
  `F_2`-augmentation forbids a unital Leavitt family in any `F_2[G]`.
* The element-level targets are kept separate on purpose.  Their
  `leavitt-corner-one-sided-lift-exists` lives in the corner `eAe` with
  identity `e`; this lane's `left-invertible-lift-of-s0-in-leavitt-group-`
  `algebra` lives in the whole algebra with identity `1` and is pinned over
  `s_0`, which is what the dynamical criterion of Section 2 above requires.
  Their support-thirteen floor constrains the whole-algebra form only.
* The lanes are ordered: a Kaplansky witness gives a stable-finiteness
  failure, which gives the automaton.  So the corner constructions, the
  lifting problems and the support bound are all evidence about Gottschalk's
