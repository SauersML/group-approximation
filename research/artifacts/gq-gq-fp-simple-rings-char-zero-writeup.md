## 0. Framing: finitely presented over Z, not over a field

Every node in this family carries the same credit paragraph. The ring nodes repeat it, and
`research/artifacts/gq-gq-lit-arxiv-priority.md` §6 and §9 back it.
- **Classical over a field.** Finitely presented simple *algebras over a field* of characteristic 0 are
  classical: the Weyl algebra `A_1(Q)`, the Leavitt algebras `L_Q(1,n)`, and the Nekrashevych algebras
  (Steinberg–Szakács). All of these are finitely presented over `Q`, not as rings.
- **New here.** The results are finite presentation **as a ring**, that is as a `Z`-algebra. That forces all of
  `Q` to be generated by finitely many elements. Every statement should say "finitely presented as a ring (over
  Z)" (priority §9, credit risk).
- **Not a posed problem.** No printed source asks whether a finitely presented simple ring of characteristic 0
  exists (priority §9(3)). So these results answer no open problem. They are the characteristic-0 case of the
  algebra form of Boone–Higman (`algebra-boone-higman-conjecture`), for the inputs covered.
- **Neighbours.**
  - Bokut (Algebra i Logika 15, 1976): simple envelopes that are finitely generated, not finitely presented.
  - Belyaev (Algebra i Logika 17, 1978): envelopes that are finitely presented, not simple.
  - Evans–Mandelberg–Neff (1975): the weak form, listed by the BBMZ survey (§3) for rings of characteristic `p`.
  - The construction may be folklore among algebraic Cuntz–Pimsner rings (Carlsen–Ortega).
  - The search was bounded: web and arXiv, with no MathSciNet, zbMATH or Dniester Notebook.

**The exact form of Evans–Mandelberg–Neff** (`char-zero-ring-boone-higman`, fbeff3477). Per its abstract:
- the first half: finitely presented simple algebras have solvable word problem;
- for particular varieties, the second half "up to but not including the embedding in a finitely presented
  algebra". That is a simple envelope with solvable word problem, not a finitely presented one.

So even in characteristic `p` the finitely presented form is open. The full text was not read (paywall).

## 1. The ring R_L and existence

    R_L = Z< s_1, s_2, t_1, t_2, N, A |
             t_i s_j = δ_ij,  s_1 t_1 + s_2 t_2 = 1,
             N s_1 = s_1 (N + 1),  N s_2 = 0,  A (N+1) = 1 = (N+1) A >

It has 6 generators and 9 relations.

**Theorem A** (`leavitt-resolvent-ring-is-fp-simple-of-char-zero`; refereed PASS by gq-referee-a 8f4edc918,
gq-referee-b dfd8468b9, gq-referee-c f7c7c3e04).
1. `R_L` is a `Q`-algebra with `1/m = t_2 t_1^(m-1) A s_1^(m-1) s_2`.
2. `R_L` is simple and nonzero.
3. `R_L ≅ M_n(R_L)` for all `n`, and `GL_n(Q) ≤ [R_L^x, R_L^x]`.

**Corollary A′** (`fp-simple-ring-of-characteristic-zero-exists`, route
`fp-simple-ring-of-char-zero-via-leavitt-resolvent-ring`). A simple ring finitely presented over `Z` of
characteristic 0 exists. Equivalently, `Q` embeds in a finitely presented simple ring, unitally or not.

*Proof sketch.*
- **The vacuum.** `N s_2 = 0` makes `N + m` act as the integer `m` on the range of `s_2`. The one resolvent `A`,
  transported along `s_1`, gives every `(N+m)^(-1)`.
- **Structure.** `R_L` is the Leavitt–Nekrashevych completion `O_ψ` over `B = Q[N][(N+c)^(-1)]`, with
  `ψ(f) = diag(f(N+1), f(0))`. `B` is not finitely generated, yet `O_ψ` is finitely presented.
- **ψ-minimality.** A nonzero rational function has finitely many zeros.
- **Aperiodicity.** Use `ψ_u(b) = b(a)` for `u = 1^a 2 x`.
- **Simplicity.** Apply `leavitt-nekrashevych-completion-simplicity-criterion`. Referee c proved simplicity
  independently, without the criterion.
- **Nonzero.** A faithful model on `Q^(X)`, where `X` is the tail class of `2^∞`.

This is the mechanism the Toeplitz ring (`q-embeds-in-a-corner-of-a-fp-toeplitz-ring`) lacked. There the vacuum
idempotent is minimal, so the ring is not simple. Here the vacuum is glued to a Leavitt isometry, and
`E = s_2 t_2` is full.

## 2. Envelope theorems

**Theorem B** (`fp-simple-q-algebras-embed-in-fp-simple-rings`; referees a 69a3cc64f, b d7414d7a4).
1. `Z(R_L) = Q`.
2. If `S` is simple and finitely presented as a `Q`-algebra, then `R_L ⊗_Q S` is a simple ring finitely
   presented over `Z`, and `S ↪ R_L ⊗_Q S` unitally.
3. A `Q`-algebra embeds in a finitely presented simple ring if and only if it embeds in a finitely presented
   simple `Q`-algebra.

*Proof sketch.*
- **The centre.** Aperiodicity kills the nonzero degrees. Commuting with the matrix units forces scalar
  coefficients `w ∈ B`, and `w(N+1) = w(0)` forces `w ∈ Q`.
- **The tensor product.** Adjoin the generators of `S` commuting with `R_L`, and write each rational coefficient
  `p/q` as `p·u_q`. Simplicity comes from the tensor lemma (simple ⊗ central simple).

**Theorem C** (`rational-function-fields-embed-in-fp-simple-rings`; referees a 69a3cc64f, b d7414d7a4). For each
`k >= 1` there is an explicit ring `R_k`, finitely presented over `Z` and simple, with `Q(t_1..t_k)` a unital
central subfield and `Q(t_1..t_k)<x_1..x_m> ↪ R_k` for every `m`.

*The presentation.*
- **Generators.** Registers `P_i, M, C_i, N`, central `t_j`, one resolvent `Γ`, and a Leavitt `(1,d)` family
  with `d = 5k+3`. That is `2d + 3k + 3` generators: 22 for `k = 1`.
- **Letters.** Each letter names an integer-polynomial substitution of the registers.
- **The resolvent.** `Γ` inverts `G = N + 1 + Σ(t_i − C_i)P_i`.

*Proof sketch.*
- **Values.** On words ending in the vacuum, `G` takes the values `N + 1 + Σ(t_i − c_i)p_i`. These are never
  zero, since they equal `N + 1 >= 1` at `t = c`.
- **Every polynomial is a value.** Every nonzero `q ∈ Z[t]` is `±` such a value, taking an integer point with
  `q(c) ≠ 0`. So `Γ` inverts every nonzero polynomial.
- **Simplicity.** From the completion criterion.

**Theorem D** (`char-zero-representable-rings-embed-in-fp-simple-rings`; referees a 69a3cc64f, b d7414d7a4).
Every subring of `M_D(C ⊗_Q Q<x_1..x_m>)` embeds in the finitely presented simple ring `M_e(R_k)`. Here `C`
embeds unitally in some `M_e(Q(t_1..t_k))`. This holds for every finitely generated commutative `Q`-algebra and
every finitely generated field of characteristic 0.

This covers:
- every finitely generated commutative `Q`-algebra;
- every commutative ring, finitely generated as a ring, with torsion-free additive group;
- number fields and function fields;
- every finitely generated subring of `M_n(F)` for a field `F` of characteristic 0.

*Proof sketch.*
- **Matrix rings.** `M_e` of a finitely presented simple ring is again one: add matrix units.
- **The commutative part.** Primary decomposition and Noether normalization give `C ↪ M_e(F_k)` through the
  regular representation.
- **Assembly.** Then `C ⊗ Q<x> ⊆ M_e(F_k<x>) ⊆ M_e(R_k)` by Theorem C, part 3.

**Theorem E** (`field-envelopes-pass-to-algebraic-closures`; referees a 69a3cc64f, b d7414d7a4).
1. If `K` embeds in a finitely presented simple ring `R`, then `K̄` embeds in `R ⊗_Q L_Q(1,2)`.
2. Every countable field of characteristic 0 and finite transcendence degree `k` embeds unitally in
   `R_k ⊗_Q L_Q(1,2)`, with `R_0 = R_L`. In particular `Q̄ ⊆ R_L`.
3. `GL_n(Q̄) ≤ R_L^x`.

*Proof sketch.*
- A countable algebraic extension is an ascending union of finite extensions. It embeds in the ultramatricial
  algebra `colim M_(d_i)(K)` by regular representations.
- That algebra embeds in `L_K(1,2) ≅ M_r(L_K(1,2))` compatibly along the chain.

**Theorem F** (`fg-semiprime-pi-q-algebras-embed-in-fp-simple-rings`, 66b7264f4; referees b fbf1d1170,
a 6a5453ccc). Every finitely generated semiprime PI `Q`-algebra embeds in a simple ring finitely presented over
`Z`. In particular this holds for every finitely generated prime PI algebra, and every finitely generated
semiprime algebra of GK dimension at most 1 (Small–Stafford–Warfield 1985).

*Proof sketch* (classical PI theory; the new input is the host of Theorem D).
- Write the algebra as a subdirect product of its prime quotients.
- Posner–Formanek–Rowen: each prime quotient embeds in `M_(n_P)(F̄_P)`, and Amitsur–Levitzki gives `n_P <= m`.
- Use the one size `N = m!` for all of them, and pass to the finitely generated subalgebra `C_0` of matrix
  entries. Then apply Theorem D.

## 3. The no-go: resolvent vacua

**Theorem G** (`resolvent-vacuum-rings-have-no-k0-state`, b936655b4; referees a 061bc8d2d, b 9a2dd39e7).
*Setting.* A ring `R` has a *resolvent vacuum* if it has:
- a nonzero idempotent `E` and an element `N`;
- for infinitely many `m`, elements `x_m ∈ RE` and `y_m ∈ ER` with `y_m x_m = E` and integer eigenvalues
  `N x_m = λ_m x_m`, `y_m N = λ_m y_m`;
- `λ_m − λ_(m')` a non-zero-divisor for `m ≠ m'`.

*Statement.*
1. The `P_m = x_m y_m` are pairwise orthogonal idempotents, each equivalent to `E`. So `M[E] <= [1]` for all `M`.
2. If `E` is full, and in particular if `R` is simple, then `R` is not stably finite and `(K_0, V, [1])` has no
   state.

*Instances.* `R_L`, `R_k`, and the Toeplitz resolvent ring, in each case exactly through the elements that
produce `1/m`.

*Proof sketch.*
- **Orthogonality.** `λ_(m') y_m x_(m') = y_m N x_(m') = λ_m y_m x_(m')`.
- **No state.** Fullness gives `[1] <= k[E]`. Taking `M = 2k` in item 1 then contradicts positivity of any
  state.
- **Not stably finite.** `(ER)^k ≅ (ER)^k ⊕ (ER)^k ⊕ …`.

*Consequence.* A finitely presented simple ring that gets `Q` from a resolvent vacuum cannot be stably finite,
UHF-like or traced. The design target of 2026-09-18 is `fp-simple-resolvent-ring-with-divisible-unit-class`
(OPEN). It asks for `[1]` of infinite order and divisible in `K_0`, finitely generated rational `K_2` symbols,
`R ≅ M_n(R_n)`, and no Leavitt tuple. Its necessary shape:
- not a unital Leavitt–Nekrashevych completion;
- not a finite graph algebra over a field (Ara–Brustenga–Cortiñas Thm 7.6);
- room in Cuntz–Pimsner completions whose `K_0` cokernel is not finitely generated.

## 4. Group-side consequences (context)

- The natural simple group candidate `[R_L^x, R_L^x]/Z` is **not finitely presented**
  (`leavitt-resolvent-derived-units-mod-centre-not-fp`; referees a and b PASS).
  `leavitt-resolvent-derived-units-mod-centre-fp-simple` is REFUTED.
- More generally, `leavitt-scalar-commutators-block-fp-central-quotients`: no unit group of a ring with an
  infinite central field and a Leavitt family is finitely presented modulo its scalars.
- The ring `R_L` does feed Problem 2.7: `St_10(R_L)` contains every `GL_n(Q)`. See
  `research/artifacts/gq-gq-problem-2-7-writeup.md`.

## 5. Open

- `countable-char-zero-fields-embed-in-fp-simple-rings` (OPEN). It reduces to the single field
  `K_∞ = Q(t_1, t_2, …)`. The register method dies at infinite transcendence degree.
- `char-zero-ring-boone-higman` (OPEN). It is equivalent to the case `K = Q` of
  `algebra-boone-higman-conjecture`, by Theorem B(3).
  - It is known for representable inputs (Theorems D and F) and for fields of finite transcendence degree
    (Theorem E).
  - All of these share one recursive word-problem bound, so they do not reach the benchmark
    `no-fp-simple-algebra-hosts-every-decidable-algebra`.
  - Non-semiprime PI algebras need not be representable, and are not covered.
- `fp-simple-resolvent-ring-with-divisible-unit-class` (OPEN; §3).

## 8. What an expert should check

1. The nine relations of `R_L`, the formula for `1/m`, and the tail-class model.
2. `R_L ≅ O_ψ`, ψ-minimality and aperiodicity. Compare referee c's criterion-free proof of simplicity.
3. The completion simplicity criterion, especially for a base `B` that is not finitely generated. It is used for
   `R_k`, and `R_k` has no criterion-free proof.
4. `Z(R_L) = Q`, and the tensor lemma used for `R_L ⊗_Q S`.
5. The register presentation of `R_k`: every nonzero `q ∈ Z[t]` is a value of `G` up to sign, and the
   localization `B` is ψ-minimal.
6. The PI chain: Posner–Formanek–Rowen, Amitsur–Levitzki and the size `m!`. Also Small–Stafford–Warfield for GK
   dimension 1.
7. Theorem G: the orthogonality computation, and the no-state argument from fullness.
8. Priority: MathSciNet and zbMATH for finitely presented simple rings of characteristic 0, the Dniester Notebook,
   the Kharlampovich–Sapir survey, the algebraic Cuntz–Pimsner literature (Carlsen–Ortega), and the
   Evans–Mandelberg–Neff full text.
