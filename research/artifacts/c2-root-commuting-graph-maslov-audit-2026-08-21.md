# The `C2` root-commuting cycle is Maslov-trivial

Source checked 2026-08-21:

- Christian Kassel, *A braid-like presentation of the integral Steinberg
  group of type `C2`*, Journal of Algebra 607 (2022), 344--359,
  arXiv:2006.13574v2.  Proposition 2.1 gives the eight root generators and
  all 24 Steinberg relations.  Equations (2.1)--(2.3) are exactly the twelve
  commuting-root pairs.  Section 2.4 records that the kernel of
  `St(C2,Z) -> Sp4(Z)` is infinite cyclic, generated by `w_gamma^4` for any
  long root `gamma`; Corollary 4.2 gives an explicit finite presentation of
  `Sp4(Z)` by adding that relation.

Write

```text
a = alpha,       b = beta,       c = alpha+beta,       d = 2alpha+beta,
A = -alpha,      B = -beta,      C = -(alpha+beta),    D = -(2alpha+beta).
```

The commuting graph read directly from Kassel (2.1)--(2.3) has edges

```text
ad, bc, bd, cd, aB, bA, bD, Bd, AD, BC, BD, CD.
```

Its maximal cliques are

```text
{b,c,d}, {a,d,B}, {B,C,D}, {A,b,D}.
```

The central vertices `b,d,B,D` induce the unique chordless cycle

```text
b -- d -- B -- D -- b.
```

Each of the four other vertices completes one edge of this square to a
triangle.  In particular this completely describes the proposed chordal
root-chart cover: deleting any one central vertex breaks the only induced
cycle, while the four maximal cliques cover every commuting edge.

This combinatorics does **not** localize the Deligne class.  Let `A_G` be the
graph product with one infinite-cyclic vertex group for each of the eight
roots and the twelve displayed commutation relations.  The canonical
homomorphism `A_G -> Sp4(Z)` lifts to `St(C2,Z)` by sending the vertex
generator at `gamma` to Kassel's `x_gamma`: equations (2.1)--(2.3) say
precisely that every defining edge commutator remains trivial upstairs.

More importantly, splitting through the **specific topological Deligne
cover** does not require identifying it with a Steinberg quotient.  Use
the path `t -> X_gamma(t)` in the real root subgroup to lift `X_gamma(1)` to
the universal cover of `Sp4(R)`.  For a commuting root pair the two root
flows form a homomorphism `R^2 -> Sp4(R)`; its unique lift to the universal
cover is again a homomorphism.  Hence the two canonical lifts commute.  The
universal property of the graph product produces a global lift to the
universal cover of `Sp4(R)`, and projection gives a lift to its connected
three-fold quotient.  Equivalently, the pulled-back mod-three multiplier on
`A_G` is a coboundary.

Thus the unique nonchordal root-commuting `C4` has identity central holonomy
in every gauge.  The nonzero central word occurs outside the commuting graph:
Kassel locates it at the rank-one Weyl word

```text
w_gamma = x_gamma x_(-gamma)^(-1) x_gamma,
c = w_gamma^4
```

for a long root.  Relations comparing the long-root realizations of `c` use
the noncommuting Chevalley relations (2.4)--(2.15).  Therefore a successful
finite-chart attack must include those nonlinear relations; chordal
commuting-root exactification and synchronization alone cannot make the edge
product of the surviving `C4` equal a nontrivial mod-three Maslov scalar.
