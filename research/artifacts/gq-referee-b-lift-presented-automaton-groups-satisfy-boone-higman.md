## 1. Zaremsky's regrouping (R), read at source

Source: Zaremsky, arXiv:2405.09722, TeX at `gq/src/bh-lattices/zaremsky-2405.09722.tex`, proof of Theorem 1.1
(l.223–245).

- **Regrouped action.** "consider the action of `G` on `T_(md)` defined by the wreath recursion
  `g ↔ σ^(⊕m)(g_1,…,g_d, g_1,…,g_d, …)`". Also: "Since the original action of `G` is self-similar, each `g_i` lies
  in `G`, and so this new action is also self-similar. Since the original action is faithful, it is easy to see that
  this new action is also faithful." Neither step uses finite presentation. ✓
- **Abelianization for even `m`.** "if `m` is even then the abelianization of `V_(md)(G)` is given by taking the
  abelianization of `G` and then modding out the relation `ḡ = m(ḡ_1+⋯+ḡ_d)` for every `g ∈ G`." ✓
- **Finiteness.**
  - "it suffices to confirm finiteness after modding out just those equations coming from every generator of `G`;
    say `G` is generated by `{a_1,…,a_n}`."
  - Then: "choose some even number `m ∈ N` such that `1/m` is not an eigenvalue of `A`. Then `1` is not an eigenvalue
    of `mA`, so the columns of `I_n − mA` span all of `R^n`."
  - **The only hypothesis used is finite generation.** The quotient of `Z^n` by the columns of `I − mA` surjects onto
    `V^ab`, for any integer lifts of the columns, and it is finite when `det(I − mA) ≠ 0`.
  - Finite presentation of `G` enters Zaremsky's proof only afterwards, through Scott (`cit:fp`). The node replaces
    that step with Theorem A. ✓
- **Parity.** `md` is even for even `m`, so the sign term in the odd-degree formula never enters. ✓

**The underlying abelianization theorem**, read at source: Nekrashevych, *Finitely presented groups associated with
expanding maps*, arXiv:1312.5654v1, §4.2, p. 21. This is the paper Zaremsky cites as [Nek18].
- Theorem 4.8: "Let `(G,X)` be a self-similar group. ... Suppose that `d` is even. Then `V_G/V_G'` is isomorphic to
  the quotient of `G/G'` by the relations `π(g) = Σ_(x∈X) π(g|_x)` for all `g ∈ G`."
- Lemma 4.9 and Theorem 4.10 give it as the cokernel of `1 − σ`. So the relations for generators span the image. ✓

## 2. (S) and (FP3)

- **(S).** Nekrashevych, same paper, Theorem 4.7, p. 21: "The commutator subgroup of `V_G` is simple." This is stated
  in §4.2 for an arbitrary self-similar group `(G, X)`, with no finiteness hypothesis. Zaremsky quotes it as
  `cit:simple`: "For any self-similar `G ≤ Aut(T_d)`". ✓
- **(FP3).** Zaremsky l.204: "if the abelianization of `V_d(G)` is finite, then the commutator subgroup has finite
  index and is thus finitely presented". This is Reidemeister–Schreier. ✓
- **Pin for §3.** Nekrashevych Proposition 4.6, p. 20, pins the route's claim that all proper cones are conjugate by
  prefix replacements. Non-empty proper clopen sets `U_1`, `U_2` lie in one `V_X`-orbit iff `m(U_1) = m(U_2)` in
  `Z/(d−1)Z`, and every cone has `m = 1`. The route's own counting argument agrees.

## 3. Consequence 3: citations

- **Aleshin automaton.** M. Vorobets and Y. Vorobets, "On a free group of transformations defined by an automaton",
  arXiv:math/0601231, Geom. Dedicata 2007. Abstract, read: "three automorphisms of the rooted binary tree defined by a
  certain 3-state automaton generate a free non-Abelian group of rank 3".
  - SVV's introduction (below) identifies this as the Aleshin automaton: "In [16] the second and third authors
    answered positively the conjecture of Brunner and Sidki, showing that the Aleshin automaton freely generates a
    free group of rank 3." ✓
- **Steinberg–Vorobets–Vorobets.** "Automata over a binary alphabet generating free groups of even rank",
  arXiv:math/0610033. Read at source, pp. 1–2:
  - "We construct automata over a binary alphabet with `2n` states, `n ≥ 2`, whose states freely generate a free
    group of rank `2n`."
  - "All automata discussed in this paper are bireversible". ✓
- **Hypothesis match.** In both cases the states are a free basis, and they are closed under sections, since they are
  the states of the automaton. So `F(S_0) → G` is injective, `N = 1` and `K_∞ ⊆ N` is `1`. ✓

**C1 (required): consequence 3 is not new.**
- A free group of finite rank is finitely presented. So `V_d(G)` is finitely presented already by Scott (FP1,
  `cit:fp`: "if `G` is finitely presented then so is `V_d(G)`").
- The finitely presented simple commutator host is exactly Zaremsky's Theorem 1.1 for the finitely presented
  self-similar group `G`.
- So consequence 3 is a special case of consequence 1. The sentence "the finite presentation of these Nekrashevych
  groups is the point" should go, or should say that FP1 already gives it.
- Suggested replacement: "Automata that freely generate free groups (Aleshin; SVV) satisfy the hypothesis with
  `N = K_∞ = 1`. This is a special case of consequence 1, since free groups of finite rank are finitely presented."

## 4. Scope: SWZ arXiv:1712.05361

Read at source, v3, pp. 21–22.

- **Theorem 7.1.** "Let `n` be a positive integer and let `d ≥ 3`. If `n = 1`, let `d ≥ 4`. There exists a
  self-similar group `G ≤ Aut(T_d)` such that the Röver–Nekrashevych group `V_d(G)` is virtually simple and of type
  `F_(n−1)` but not of type `FP_n`."
  - The statement does not say finite-state. Its proof does: by Theorem 6.13 and Lemma 5.3 the action is
    "self-similar, finite-state, and coarsely diagonal" and "also persistent".
  - With `n = 2`, `V_d(G)` is not finitely presented. So the node's citation is right, provided "finite-state" is
    credited to the proof. Suggest "(Thm 7.1 with `n = 2`; finite-state by its proof)".
- **Example 7.2.** For the Grigorchuk group Grig: "These generators can be extended to create a persistent action on
  `T_3` ... Therefore, although `V_2(Grig)` is of type `F_∞` [BM16], our results show that the virtually simple group
  `V_3(Grig)` is of type `F_1` but not of type `F_2`." ✓ as a finite-state action whose `V` is not finitely presented.

**C2 (required): the hypothesis belongs to the action, not the group.**
- Example 7.2 itself shows this. The same group Grig fails the hypothesis on `T_3`, by Theorem A, but satisfies it on
  `T_2`, where `V_2(Grig)` is `F_∞`. Grig is also contracting there, so it is covered by consequence 1.
- The repo's `rn-groups-of-affine-lamplighters-f-q-wr-z-are-fp` says the same for `AGL_1(F_q[t^(±1)])`, the kind of
  group behind Theorem 7.1 with `n = 2`. There `V_q` is finitely presented in the non-persistent `q`-ary action.
- So "It does not cover every automaton group" is right only if an automaton group means the group together with its
  automaton. "Those groups need an overgroup" is not supported: the cited examples need a different action, not an
  overgroup.
- Suggested text: "The hypothesis depends on the action. SWZ (Thm 7.1, Ex. 7.2) give finite-state actions whose `V_d`
  is not finitely presented, and persistent actions satisfy it only for finitely presented groups. The group may
  still be covered through another self-similar action: Grig is, on `T_2` (SWZ Ex. 7.2, citing [BM16]). No automaton
  group is known that fails the hypothesis for every finite-state action."
