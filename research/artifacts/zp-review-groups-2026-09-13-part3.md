### 13.1 Theorem G: `fp-alternating-full-groups-of-free-subshifts-force-sft` (+ `-proof`)

Statement: over any infinite finitely generated `Λ`, if a finitely presented subgroup of the topological
full group of a free subshift `X ⊆ A^Λ` contains the rule 3-cycles `S_R` of some radius, then `X` is of
finite type; in particular `D([[Λ ⤳ X]])` and `A([[Λ ⤳ X]])` finitely presented forces `X` to be an SFT.

Checked:
- **Lemma A**, the load-bearing commutator identity, verified by direct computation on the five-point
  model `a = (p a_1 a_2)`, `b = (p b_1 b_2)`: with `[x,y] = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy` and left action,
  `[[b^{-1}, a^{-1}], [b, a]]` is exactly the 3-cycle `(p a_1 a_2)`, moving only `p, a_1, a_2`. So the
  double commutator isolates the overlap `C = A_1 ∩ B_1`, as the route claims.
- **Lemma 0** (disjointness of translated occurrence sets on colourings whose `B(R_1+12)`-windows occur
  in `X`) is the right use of the freeness radius `R_1`: a common site would give a point of `X` agreeing
  with a nontrivial translate of itself on `B(R_1)`.
- **The climb** from `B(r)` to `B(r+1)` needs, at each step, a shape in `B(5)` that aligns one block with
  the intersecting block and keeps the other blocks off the first 3-cycle's blocks. At most four elements
  of `B(5)` are excluded, and `B(5)` has at least six elements because `Λ` is infinite. Correct.
- **The contradiction**: relators read bounded windows that occur in `X`, so by freeness they act
  trivially and the rule action factors through `Γ`; an illegal pattern gives a word that is trivial in `Γ`
  yet moves a site of a legal colouring carrying that pattern. So the legal colourings are exactly `X`.
  Correct, and neither minimality nor commutativity of `Λ` is used, as claimed.
- The recorded consequence for `Λ = Z` (no nonempty free Z-subshift is an SFT, so `D([[Z ⤳ X]])` is never
  finitely presented) is right and properly generalizes the minimal case.
- Verdict: **PASS**.

### 13.2 Proposition R: `circle-rotation-zd-codings-are-not-of-finite-type` (+ `-proof`)

- Freeness: for `u ≠ 0`, `t = u·θ ∉ Z` by rational independence; if `f = f(· + t)` off a null set then `f`
  would be invariant under the dense group `Zt` and so constant, which the hypothesis excludes; the
  positive-length arc `J` where they differ is hit by every long enough orbit segment (unique ergodicity of
  the rotation), so no point of `X` has period `u`. Correct.
- Patterns on `B(r)` are constant on the arcs between consecutive points of `Σ = {e − k·θ}`, so the pattern
  set depends only on the cyclic order of `Σ` with its coincidences. Correct.
- Perturbation: coincidences are exactly the relations `e ∼ e'` with `v_e − v_{e'} = k − k'`; putting
  `e' = e_0 + v_e θ'` preserves every coincidence, and for `θ'` near `θ` the distinct points keep their
  order. Rational `θ'` are dense, giving a periodic configuration all of whose `B(r)`-windows are legal and
  which is not in `X` by freeness. Since an SFT is determined by legal `B(r)`-windows for one `r`, `X` is
  not an SFT. Correct.
- Verdict: **PASS**. Route `circle-rotation-zd-full-groups-not-fp-via-finite-type` composes the two
  correctly, and both prerequisites are established, so no placeholder `requires: []` fires.

## 14. Problem 1.20 tool claims (z1-20-simple-fp2, c7dc2895dd and d921b976e3)

- **`fp2-iff-quotient-of-fp-group-by-perfect-normal-subgroup`** (+ `-proof`). The relation-module
  criterion (`G = F/R` finitely generated is FP_2 iff `R_ab` is finitely generated over `ZG`) is used
  correctly in both directions: (⇐) `R = [R,R]R_0` makes the images of `r_1, …, r_k` generate `R_ab`;
  (⇒) the `ZG`-submodule generated by chosen `r_i` is `R_0[R,R]/[R,R]`, so `R = R_0[R,R]` and
  `N = R/R_0` is perfect. Consequence 1 checks out: with `P` the preimage of `M`, `P = [P,P]N` and
  `N = [N,N] ≤ [P,P]`, so `P` is perfect. **PASS**.
- **`fg-groups-with-re-or-co-re-word-problem-are-countable`** (+ `-proof`). Counting Turing machines
  against pairs `(k, W)`, plus generating-set independence by substitution. **PASS**. The Kuznetsov
  remark (a finitely generated simple recursively presented group has solvable word problem) is correctly
  stated as a remark and is not load-bearing here.
- **`simple-fp2-uncountability-via-perfect-simple-quotients`** (+ `-proof`). (1 ⇒ 2) uses that there are
  only countably many finitely presented groups, so one `H` carries uncountably many kernels; (2 ⇒ 1) uses
  that a finitely generated `H` has only countably many homomorphisms onto a fixed countable group, so the
  fibres of `N ↦ H/N` are countable. Both correct. The node is right not to wire this as a route into
  `uncountably-many-simple-groups-of-type-fp2`, since the prerequisite would restate the target: that is
  exactly the restatement-dressed-as-reduction trap. **PASS**.
- **`rover-nekrashevych-groups-have-co-re-word-problem`** (+ `-proof`). The semi-decision procedure
  (enumerate `u`, compute `W(u)` right to left, accept when `W(u)` is incomparable with `u`) is sound, and
  complete because taking `|u| ≥ q + 1 + rL` keeps every intermediate string long enough for each
  generator's prefix code to apply. Section computations terminate because each recursive call shortens the
  input. **PASS**.
- **`v-times-shift-alternating-group-restrictions-are-simple`** (+ `-proof`). Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 are
  correct as written: the invariant closed sets are `C × Y` because `V`-orbits are dense in `C`; multisections
  of the restriction lift; distinct minimal subshifts give distinct kernels; and there are continuum many
  Sturmian minimal subshifts. Item 3 rests on Nekrashevych arXiv:1511.08241 ("if G is a minimal groupoid of
  germs then A(G) is simple"), which the route itself says is quoted from the abstract with the theorem
  numbers still to be taken from the PDF, and it also flags the degree-3 versus degree-5 definition of
  `A(G)`. **PASS conditional on that import.** Recommendation: quote the theorem number and the definition
  of `A(G)` verbatim from the PDF, and state minimality of `G|_{C×Y}` explicitly (dense `V`-orbits in `C`
  together with dense shift orbits in a minimal `Y`). Until then the claim should not be treated as fully
  independent of the literature.
