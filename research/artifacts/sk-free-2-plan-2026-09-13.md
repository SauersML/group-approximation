# sk-free-2 plan: stable rank one for subshift crossed products, and bounded generation of G_X (2026-09-13)

Lane sk-free-2, a free structure lane in wave 10. The problem was chosen after a main-graph survey. The K-theory of `R_X` is already computed (sk-open-4, Theorem K: `K_1(R_X) ≅ Z ⊕ K^0(X,T)⊗F_q^×`, `K_2(R_X) ≅ F_q^×`). The marked-group homeomorphism `Y ↦ G_Y` and "simple iff infinite minimal" are also on main. The structural crux that many open nodes wait on is the **stable rank** of `R = LC(X,k) ⋊_T Z`.

## Choice
**Main target (A): `sr(R) = 1` for every minimal homeomorphism T of the Cantor set and every field k.** This is the algebraic analogue of Putnam's 1990 theorem on topological stable rank one for `C(X) ⋊ Z`.

Open nodes it would close or feed:
- `minimal-cantor-crossed-product-has-stable-rank-at-most-two` and `subshift-crossed-product-ring-has-bass-stable-rank-at-most-two` (both OPEN; sk-stable-rank-a/b died);
- `minimal-cantor-crossed-product-is-an-exchange-ring` (OPEN), if route A1 goes through;
- `aperiodic-subshift-ring-unstable-k2-vanishes` (OPEN; its "unstable n = 3 gap" is a stable-range question).

Consequences to prove once (A) holds, each with sources read verbatim:
- **C1, bounded generation.** Every invertible matrix over a ring of stable rank one factors into a bounded number of unitriangular matrices and a diagonal matrix (Vaserstein–Wheland type; check the exact statement at source). So every element of G_X = EL_3(R_X) is a product of a bounded number of elementary matrices e_ij(r), and G_X is boundedly generated by its six root subgroups `{e_ij(r) : r ∈ R_X}`. Consequences: G_X is uniformly perfect, and its quasimorphisms are bounded.
- **C2, K_1 stability** (Vaserstein: injective for n ≥ sr+1, surjective for n ≥ sr).
  - `GL_n(R_X)/EL_n(R_X) ≅ K_1(R_X) ≅ Z ⊕ K^0(X,T)⊗F_q^×` for all n ≥ 2.
  - Over F_2 this gives `GL_n(R_X) = G_X ⋊ ⟨diag(u,1,…,1)⟩`, with `G_X = [GL_n(R_X), GL_n(R_X)]`.
  - With `amenable-crossed-product-gl-normal-structure-is-standard`, the normal subgroups of GL_n(R_X) are the central ones and the preimages of subgroups of K_1(R_X).
- **C3, K_2 stability** (van der Kallen): K_2(n,R_X) → K_2(R_X) = F_q^× is onto for n ≥ 3 and bijective for n ≥ 4. This closes the K_2 layer of the normal-subgroup dictionary for n ≥ 4.

Routes to (A):
- **A1, exchange property plus internal cancellation.** For exchange rings, sr 1 ⟺ internal cancellation (Yu 1995; Khurana–Lam). V(R) should be the cancellative cone K^0(X,T)^+. So (A) reduces to "R is an exchange ring" plus cancellation.
  - Literature check first: exchange criteria for Steinberg algebras of ample groupoids (the analogue of Condition (K) for Leavitt path algebras, Aranda Pino–Pardo–Siles Molina), and V(A_K(G)) versus the type semigroup.
- **A2, direct tower proof.** Reduce a unimodular pair (a,b) to finite-state block data along Kakutani–Rokhlin towers. Use `a + Rb = {z : z = a on ker b}` (orbit picture), and build `z = D(1 + D^{-1}N)` with D ∈ A_Y invertible and seam part N, where (D^{-1}N)^2 = 0 (the nilpotent seam trick behind `1 + u e_{X∖V}`), after a flux (index) shift by a power of u.
- **Model tests:**
  - `k[u^{±1}]` (T with a fixed point) has sr 2 and is not exchange, so every route must use minimality or aperiodicity;
  - the odometer algebra (a union of `M_N(k[t^{±1}])`) is a calibration case;
  - the Fibonacci subshift must satisfy every hypothesis.

**Quick side result (B), landed first if (A) stalls.** The closure of the finite models of the note in the space of marked groups:
- the infinite limits of `(SL_{3N}(F_2), subshift marking)` are exactly the groups `EL_3(R_Y)` over non-periodic chain-transitive subshifts Y (periodic orbits are dense exactly in the chain-transitive subshifts);
- they form a perfect compact set;
- the simple ones (Y minimal) form a dense G_δ in it.

So the generic infinite limit of this expander family is an infinite simple Kazhdan LEF group. It builds on `subshift-algebra-groups-embed-in-marked-groups` and `subshift-elementary-group-simple-iff-infinite-minimal`.
