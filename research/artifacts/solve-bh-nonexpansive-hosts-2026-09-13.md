## 1. Why BS(1,2) inside V_10(Gamma_2) is consistent with the obstruction

- **The premise.** The lane directive suggested that `BS(1,2) ≤ V_10(Gamma_2)`
  (`bs12-lies-in-fp-simple-rn-envelope-of-quadratic-dehn-host`) works by escaping equicontinuity
  through non-isometric dynamics. That is not what happens.
- **The host is isometric.** `V_10(Gamma_2)` is a Röver–Nekrashevych group, so it lies in the
  almost-automorphism group of the 10-regular forest, and its sections are tree automorphisms. The
  image `s` of the base generator is conjugate to `s^2`. By
  `almost-automorphism-conjugate-to-proper-power-is-equicontinuous`, `<s>` is equicontinuous.
- **Equicontinuity does not kill BS(1,2).** `BS(1,2)` has faithful equicontinuous actions, for
  example `x ↦ x+1` and `x ↦ 2x` on `Z_3`. Only the stable letter of `BG` (`t a t^-1 = b`) turns
  equicontinuity of `a` into triviality of `a`, through the smallest-prime argument of
  `baumslag-gersten-equicontinuity-smallest-prime-proof`.
- **Linear and profinite images.** Every finite image of `BG` kills `a`, and finitely generated
  linear groups are residually finite (Malcev, as recalled in Rivas–Triestino arXiv:1905.00938v2 §1).
  So every linear image kills `a`. Any action on a compact zero-dimensional space that factors
  through a compact group has `a` equicontinuous, so it also kills `a`. Profinite-type completions
  are therefore dead hosts.

## 2. Isometric (non-expansive) groupoids: dead by a hand argument

Suppose a compact zero-dimensional metrizable `X` carries a compatible ultrametric in which every
germ of the groupoid is locally isometric. Then each element of the full group, having finitely
many pieces, preserves distances below some scale `r`. A finitely generated subgroup preserves
distances below the minimum of the generators' scales. So it is equicontinuous, and its closure in
`Homeo(X)` is profinite. Hence:
- every homomorphism `BG → [[G]]` kills `a`, by `baumslag-gersten-equicontinuous-generator-acts-trivially`;
- every homomorphism `H4 → [[G]]` is trivial, because `H4` has no nontrivial finite quotient.

Examples are transformation groupoids of equicontinuous actions (odometers) and AF groupoids.
**Consequence.** A Cantor host for `BG` needs germs that are not locally isometric. The image of `a`
has to fail equicontinuity somewhere.

## 3. Cantor hosts with non-isometric germs: where a can be non-equicontinuous

1. **Coordinate permutations** (twisted Brin–Thompson `SV_G`). If `a` has infinite orbits on `S`,
   it is not equicontinuous on `C^S`. Finite presentation needs a type (A) actor containing `BG`
   (`twisted-brin-thompson-finite-presentation-criterion`). None is known (root Attempts).
2. **Singular germs at one countable orbit** (shell envelopes). `BG` embeds, as every countable
   group does (`shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`). Finite presentation of
   `E_nu` is the OPEN `decidable-inputs-have-fp-shell-envelopes`, specialized to `BG`.
3. **Bass–Serre compactification** (sketch only).
   - `BG` acts on the vertices and ends of the Bass–Serre tree of its HNN structure over
     `BS(1,2)`, with the topology in which a neighbourhood of a vertex contains all but finitely
     many of its branches.
   - At the base vertex, `a` permutes the branches indexed by `BS(1,2)/<b>`. That coset space
     identifies with `Z[1/2]`, where `a` acts as `+1`. So the orbits are infinite, and `a` is not
     equicontinuous at that vertex.
   - Every vertex is a singular point with isotropy germs containing a conjugate of `BS(1,2)`. So a
     generator is singular at infinitely many points, and no evident coding makes this a finite
     germ extension of `V`.
   - No finiteness test was possible.

## 4. One-dimensional non-isometric hosts: dead (landed)

- **Setting.** `PA(S^1)` is the group of piecewise real-analytic circle homeomorphisms with finitely
  many pieces. It contains PL circle groups and piecewise-`PSL_2(R)` groups: Thompson's `T` in
  Thurston's model, and Lodha's finitely presented infinite simple group `S` (arXiv:1710.06220v2,
  Theorem 1.1, read from the PDF text; `S` is generated by `T` and one piecewise projective
  homeomorphism).
- **The host is not reached by the zero-dimensional arguments.** It contains `BS(1,2)` with `a`
  parabolic (`x ↦ x+1`, `x ↦ 2x` on `RP^1`), and that `a` is not equicontinuous.
- **Correction (same day).** PL circle groups and `T` contain no `BS(1,2)`: by Item 2 of the
  lemma a PL element conjugate to its square is trivial (for the interval,
  `bs-1-n-does-not-embed-in-pl-plus-interval`). Commit 2379c90d5's message wrongly said these hosts
  contain `BS(1,2)`. For them the new obstructions add nothing beyond `BS(1,2)`. The new cases
  are hosts that do contain `BS(1,2)`, such as `PSL_2(R)` and Lodha's `S`
  (arXiv:1710.06220v2, Corollary 5.3: `BB(1,2) < S`; §2.7: `BS(1,2) = < η, ν_1 ν_2 >`).
- **Mechanism.**
  - An element `g` with a fixed point that is conjugate to `g^n` by `h` has tangency order `m` at
    every gap end `p`, and some `h^N` fixes `p` with derivative `n^(-N/m) ≠ 1`.
  - In `BG`, the conjugator `b` is itself conjugate to `b^2` (by `t b t^-1`). So `b` is tangent to
    the identity at its fixed points, and `p` is one of them. That is a contradiction unless `a = 1`.
  - In `H4` the same happens around the 4-cycle.
- **Literature.**
  - Rivas–Triestino Theorem C: every `C^1` action of `H4` on the line or circle is trivial.
  - Their Theorem A: `H4` acts faithfully on the line by homeomorphisms.
  - Lodha Theorem 1.1(2): `S` has no nontrivial `C^1` or PL circle action.
  - `PA(S^1)` is neither `C^1` nor PL, so none of these statements covers the landed claims.
  - No broader search was made.

## 5. Stein–Thompson rearrangement groups (`V_{2,3}` type): not decided

These are PL rearrangements of `[0,1)` with slopes in `2^Z 3^Z` and breakpoints in `Z[1/6]`.
- **What survives.** The log-slope cocycles are conjugation-invariant at periodic points and scale
  under powers, as in Item 2 of `graph-almost-automorphism-exponent-pumping-proof`. So an element
  conjugate to a proper power has zero periodic slope rates and zero mean log-slope for every
  invariant measure (hand remark).
- **What fails.** The pumping step fails for two reasons.
  - `2^Z 3^Z` is dense in `R_{>0}`, so bounded log-slope sums do not give finitely many slopes.
  - Intervals with `Z[1/6]` endpoints fall into infinitely many similarity classes, so there is no
    pigeonhole on cone types.
- **Rotation numbers are unavailable,** because the elements are discontinuous.
- **Exact gap.** Show that unbounded slope sums of an element of `V_{2,3}` force a hyperbolic
  periodic point, or build `BS(1,2) ≤ V_{2,3}`.

## 6. Exact gap for BG after this lane

A finitely presented simple host for `BG` must satisfy all of the following:
- it is outside the almost-automorphism groups of finite graphs (`b06f8b4fd`);
- it is outside isometric groupoid full groups (§2);
- it is outside piecewise-analytic circle groups (§4);
- the image of `a` is not equicontinuous on any zero-dimensional model.

The live candidates are:
- twisted Brin–Thompson groups over a type (A) actor containing `BG`;
- a finitely presented shell envelope of `BG`;
- `nV` and rational similarity groups (other lanes);
- Stein–Thompson rearrangement groups (§5);
- circle groups with `C^0` germs or infinitely many breakpoints.
