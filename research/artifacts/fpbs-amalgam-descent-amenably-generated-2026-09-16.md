# Fixed price descends along amalgams over infinite amenable subgroups

## Status

Research artifact written on September 16, 2026, for
`fpbs-fixed-price-countable-from-finitely-generated`. That node stays **OPEN**.
It continues `research/artifacts/fpbs/docs/fixed-price-countable-passage.md`
(the "passage artifact"), whose conventions are kept.

What is proved here:

1. **Theorem 1 (unconditional descent).** Let Γ_1 and Γ_2 be countable groups
   with a common infinite amenable subgroup A, and let G = Γ_1 *_A Γ_2. Assume
   every free action of Γ_1 and every free action of Γ_2 has finite cost. If G
   has fixed price, then Γ_1 and Γ_2 have fixed price, and
   c(G) = C(Γ_1) + C(Γ_2) − 1.
2. **Theorem 2 (under H).** Assume every infinite finitely generated group has
   fixed price. Then every countable group generated by finitely many amenable
   subgroups has fixed price.
3. **Corollary 3 (under H).** A counterexample to the passage satisfies
   Theorem G of the passage artifact and, in addition, is not generated by
   finitely many amenable subgroups.
4. **Proposition 4 (the limit of the method).** If Γ_1 *_A Γ_2 is finitely
   generated, then Γ_1 is generated by A and finitely many elements.

Why Theorem 2 is new against the passage artifact. The artifact's §6 remarks
that shape (A) of Theorem G ("no fixed pseudocost") is invisible to any
argument that passes only through subgroups of Γ. Theorem 2 applies H to a
finitely generated **supergroup** G = Γ *_A B of Γ. Gaboriau's amalgamation
formula then turns fixed price of G into the equality C(s_Γ) = C(Γ). This
excludes shape (A) on the class of Theorem 2, and the finite-cost bound of
Lemma 1.1 excludes shape (B) there.

Novelty. Theorem 1 is the converse direction of the "moreover" clause of [GL]
Cor 2.28 ("if Γ_1 and Γ_2 have fixed price, then so has Γ"). It is a short
consequence of [G00] Théorème IV.15. It was not found in the sources consulted
(listed in the session note
`notes/fpbs-fixed-price-countable-from-finitely-swarm-2026-09-16.md`), and no
priority is claimed.

Not proved: the passage itself. The groups that remain are those not generated
by finitely many amenable subgroups (§5).

## 0. Conventions and imported tools

**Conventions.**
- Actions are p.m.p. actions of countable groups on standard probability
  spaces. *Free* means essentially free.
- E_a is the orbit relation of a, C(a) = C(E_a), and a|Λ is the restriction to
  a subgroup Λ.
- C(Γ) is the infimum of C(a) over free actions a of Γ ([GL] writes C_*).
- Γ has *fixed price* if all free actions of Γ have the same cost, and c(Γ)
  denotes that common value.
- s_Γ is the Bernoulli shift on ([0,1]^Γ, Leb^Γ), with (γ·x)(k) = x(γ⁻¹k).
- H is the hypothesis "every infinite finitely generated group has fixed
  price".

**Imported tools.**

- [GL] D. Gaboriau, *Around the orbit equivalence theory, measure equivalence,
  cost and ℓ² Betti numbers*, lecture notes, version of October 3, 2025
  ([PDF](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/gaboriau/Travaux-Publi/ME-Cost-L2-Lectures/ME-Cost-L2-lectures.pdf)).
  Its text was extracted from the PDF in this session. Items used:
  - **Thm 2.27** ("Free product with amalgamation over amenable [Gab00]"):
    "If R = R_1 *_{R_3} R_2 where R_3 is hyperfinite (possibly trivial) (and
    where R_1, R_2 have finite cost), then
    C(R_1 *_{R_3} R_2) = C(R_1) + C(R_2) − C(R_3)."
    [GL] Remark 2.29 points to [G00, Theorem IV.15] for the proof.
  - **The sentence before Thm 2.27:** "when R is generated by a free action of
    a group Γ, any decomposition of Γ = Γ_1 *_{Γ_3} Γ_2 induces the analogous
    decomposition of R = R_{Γ_1} *_{R_{Γ_3}} R_{Γ_2}". Lemma 1.5 re-derives
    the freeness conditions.
  - **Thm 2.20** (Ornstein–Weiss): for an infinite amenable group, every p.m.p.
    action with almost all orbits infinite has an orbit relation generated by a
    free Z-action, so it has a treeing of cost 1.
  - **Cor 2.14:** every free p.m.p. action of a finite group Γ has cost
    1 − 1/|Γ|.
  - **Thm 2.12** (a) cost does not increase from a free factor to a free
    extension; (b) co-induction. Lemmas 1.2 and 1.3 re-derive both.
- [G00] D. Gaboriau, *Coût des relations d'équivalence et des groupes*, Invent.
  Math. 139 (2000). Théorème IV.15 is used only through [GL] Thm 2.27. The
  exact wording of its definition of R_1 *_{R_3} R_2 was **not re-read** in
  this session. Lemma 1.5 verifies the natural freeness conditions, which
  imply any variant with length or parity restrictions.
- [AW] M. Abért and B. Weiss, arXiv:1103.1063: every free action of a countably
  infinite group weakly contains the Bernoulli shift.
- [TD] R. Tucker-Drob, arXiv:1211.6395, Cor 6.20 (a free and a ≼ b imply
  PC(b) ≤ PC(a)) and Cor 6.8(1) (PC = C when C < ∞), with PC ≤ C from Def 6.6.
  [AW] and [TD] were read in full text on September 12, 2026, per §1 of the
  passage artifact. They were not re-read in this session.
- **Embedding.** The established graph claim
  `countable-group-embeds-in-two-generator-simple-group` (Schupp). Only its
  consequence "every countable group embeds in a finitely generated group" is
  used.
- **Normal form (NF).** In G = Γ_1 *_A Γ_2 the factors embed, Γ_1 ∩ Γ_2 = A,
  and every product g_1 ⋯ g_n with n ≥ 1 and the g_i alternately in Γ_1 ∖ A
  and Γ_2 ∖ A is ≠ 1. This is the normal form theorem for amalgamated free
  products (Serre, *Trees*, Chapter I; Lyndon–Schupp, *Combinatorial Group
  Theory*, Chapter IV).

## 1. Elementary lemmas

**Definitions used.**
- A *graphing* of a p.m.p. relation E on (X, μ) is a countable family
  Φ = (φ_i) of partial measure-preserving Borel isomorphisms whose graphs lie
  in E and which generate E on a conull set.
- The cost of a graphing is C(Φ) = Σ_i μ(dom φ_i), and C(E) is the infimum of
  C(Φ) over graphings of E, as in [GL] §2.
- Null sets are handled once and for all. The E-saturation of a null set is
  null, because E is countable and μ is E-invariant. So countably many
  "almost everywhere" conditions hold together on one E-invariant conull set.
- p.m.p. actions are Borel actions.

**Lemma 1.1 (joins).** Let E be a p.m.p. relation on (X, μ). Suppose E is the
equivalence relation generated by subrelations R_1, …, R_m together with
partial isomorphisms ψ_1, …, ψ_n whose graphs lie in E. Then
C(E) ≤ Σ_i C(R_i) + Σ_j μ(dom ψ_j).

*Proof.* Assume all C(R_i) are finite, and let ε > 0.
1. Choose graphings Φ_i of R_i with C(Φ_i) < C(R_i) + ε/m.
2. The family Φ = Φ_1 ∪ … ∪ Φ_m ∪ {ψ_1, …, ψ_n} has graphs in E.
3. On a conull E-invariant set, the relation generated by Φ contains every R_i
   and every ψ_j. So it contains the relation they generate, which is E.
4. So C(E) ≤ C(Φ) < Σ_i C(R_i) + ε + Σ_j μ(dom ψ_j). ∎

**Corollary 1.1′.** Let Γ be a countable group generated by amenable subgroups
A_1, …, A_m and a finite set S. Every free p.m.p. action a of Γ has
C(a) ≤ m + |S|.

*Proof.*
1. Every γ ∈ Γ is a word in A_1 ∪ … ∪ A_m ∪ S ∪ S⁻¹. So E_a is generated by
   the subrelations E_{a|A_i} and the maps x ↦ s·x (s ∈ S), whose domains
   have measure 1.
2. If A_i is infinite, a|A_i is free, so almost all its orbits are infinite.
   [GL] Thm 2.20 gives a graphing of cost 1, so C(E_{a|A_i}) ≤ 1.
3. If A_i is finite, a|A_i is free and [GL] Cor 2.14 gives
   C(E_{a|A_i}) = 1 − 1/|A_i| ≤ 1.
4. Lemma 1.1 gives C(a) ≤ m + |S|. ∎

**Lemma 1.2 (lifting graphings).** Let Λ be a countable group and a a free
p.m.p. action of Λ on (X, μ). Let b be a p.m.p. action of Λ on (Y, ν), and
π: Y → X a Borel map with π_*ν = μ and π(λ·y) = λ·π(y). Then C(b) ≤ C(a).
This is [GL] Thm 2.12(a), whose hint is "pull-back any graphing".

*Proof.* Assume C(a) < ∞, and let ε > 0.
1. Let X_0 be a Λ-invariant conull set on which a is free and on which a
   graphing Φ = (φ_i) of E_a with C(Φ) < C(a) + ε generates E_a. Put
   Y_0 = π⁻¹(X_0), which is Λ-invariant and conull.
2. For each i and λ ∈ Λ put D_{i,λ} = {x ∈ dom φ_i : φ_i(x) = λ·x}.
   - These sets are Borel.
   - They cover dom φ_i because graph(φ_i) ⊂ E_a.
   - They are pairwise disjoint on X_0 by freeness.
3. Put ψ_{i,λ}: π⁻¹(D_{i,λ}) → Y, y ↦ λ·y. This is a partial
   measure-preserving isomorphism with graph in E_b. Hence
   Σ_{i,λ} ν(π⁻¹D_{i,λ}) = Σ_{i,λ} μ(D_{i,λ}) = C(Φ).
4. *Generation.* Let y ∈ Y_0, λ ∈ Λ and x = π(y).
   - There is a Φ-path x = x_0, …, x_k = λ·x with x_l = φ_{i_l}^{ε_l}(x_{l−1})
     and ε_l = ±1.
   - Let λ_l be the unique element with x_l = λ_l·x_{l−1}, and put
     y_l = λ_l ⋯ λ_1·y, so that π(y_l) = x_l.
   - If ε_l = 1, then x_{l−1} ∈ D_{i_l,λ_l} and ψ_{i_l,λ_l}(y_{l−1}) = y_l.
   - If ε_l = −1, then x_{l−1} = λ_l⁻¹·x_l with x_l ∈ D_{i_l,λ_l⁻¹}. So
     ψ_{i_l,λ_l⁻¹}(y_l) = y_{l−1}.
   - So y and y_k are joined by a Ψ-path.
   - Also λ_k ⋯ λ_1·x = λ·x, so freeness at x gives λ_k ⋯ λ_1 = λ, and
     y_k = λ·y.
5. So Ψ = (ψ_{i,λ}) generates E_b on Y_0, and C(b) ≤ C(Ψ) < C(a) + ε. ∎

**Lemma 1.3 (co-induction).** Let Λ ≤ G be countable groups with Λ infinite,
and let a be a free p.m.p. action of Λ on (X, μ). Then G has a free p.m.p.
action b = CoInd(a) with a factor map from b|Λ onto a in the sense of
Lemma 1.2. This is [GL] Thm 2.12(b), whose hint is "use co-induction".

*Proof.*
0. *μ is non-atomic.*
   - An atom has positive mass, so by freeness it has trivial stabilizer.
   - Then its infinitely many translates are distinct atoms of the same mass,
     which is impossible.
1. *Model.*
   - Let T be a set of representatives of the left cosets gΛ, with 1 ∈ T.
   - For h ∈ G and t ∈ T write h⁻¹t = σ_h(t)·λ(h,t) with σ_h(t) ∈ T and
     λ(h,t) ∈ Λ.
   - Let Y = X^T and ν = μ^T.
   - The map f ↦ (f(t))_{t∈T} is a bijection from
     {f: G → X : f(gλ) = λ⁻¹·f(g) for all g, λ} onto Y.
   - On functions, (h·f)(k) = f(h⁻¹k) is a left action preserving the
     condition f(gλ) = λ⁻¹·f(g).
   - In coordinates it reads (h·y)(t) = λ(h,t)⁻¹·y(σ_h(t)).
   - Here σ_h is a bijection of T, so h permutes coordinates and applies a
     measure-preserving map in each coordinate. Hence b is a Borel p.m.p.
     action.
2. *Factor.*
   - Put π(y) = y(1), so π_*ν = μ.
   - For λ ∈ Λ, λ⁻¹·1 = 1·λ⁻¹ gives σ_λ(1) = 1 and λ(λ,1) = λ⁻¹.
   - So π(λ·y) = λ·π(y).
3. *Freeness.* Let h ≠ 1.
   - If h ∈ Λ, then {y : h·y = y} ⊂ π⁻¹{x : h·x = x}, which is null by
     freeness of a.
   - If h ∉ Λ, then t = σ_h(1) ≠ 1 because h⁻¹ ∉ Λ. Now h·y = y forces
     y(1) = λ(h,1)⁻¹·y(t).
   - The coordinates y(1) and y(t) are independent with law μ. For each value
     of y(t) the admissible y(1) is a single point, which is null by step 0.
     By Fubini the fixed set is null. ∎

**Corollary 1.4.** Let Γ_1, Γ_2 be infinite subgroups of a countable group G,
and let ε > 0. There is a free p.m.p. action b of G with
C(b|Γ_i) < C(Γ_i) + ε for i = 1, 2 (if C(Γ_i) = ∞ there is nothing to prove).

*Proof.*
1. Choose free actions a_i of Γ_i with C(a_i) < C(Γ_i) + ε, and let
   b_i = CoInd(a_i) on (Y_i, ν_i).
2. The diagonal action b = b_1 × b_2 on (Y_1 × Y_2, ν_1 × ν_2) is p.m.p.
3. It is free: for h ≠ 1, {y : h·y = y} ⊂ {y_1 : h·y_1 = y_1} × Y_2, which is
   null.
4. The projection Y_1 × Y_2 → Y_i, composed with the map π of Lemma 1.3, is a
   factor map from b|Γ_i onto a_i.
5. Lemma 1.2 gives C(b|Γ_i) ≤ C(a_i) < C(Γ_i) + ε. ∎

[GL], after Cor 2.28, makes the same observation: "it is easy to build a free
p.m.p. of Γ that realizes both C*(Γ_1) and C*(Γ_2)".

**Lemma 1.5 (orbit relation of a free action of an amalgam).** Let
G = Γ_1 *_A Γ_2 and let b be a free p.m.p. action of G on (Y, ν). Put
R = E_b, R_i = E_{b|Γ_i} for i = 1, 2, and R_3 = E_{b|A}. Restrict everything
to the G-invariant conull set Y_0 of points with trivial stabilizer. Then:
(i) R_1 ∩ R_2 = R_3.
(ii) R is generated by R_1 ∪ R_2.
(iii) Suppose n ≥ 1, j_1, …, j_n ∈ {1, 2} with j_k ≠ j_{k+1}, and
y_0, …, y_n ∈ Y_0 with (y_{k−1}, y_k) ∈ R_{j_k} ∖ R_3 for all k. Then
y_n ≠ y_0.

*Proof.* For (y, y') ∈ R with y ∈ Y_0, let g(y, y') be the unique element of G
with y' = g(y, y')·y.
- (y, y') ∈ R_i iff g(y, y') ∈ Γ_i, and (y, y') ∈ R_3 iff g(y, y') ∈ A.
- g(y, y'') = g(y', y'')·g(y, y').

Then:
- (i) follows from Γ_1 ∩ Γ_2 = A (NF).
- (ii) follows because Γ_1 ∪ Γ_2 generates G.
- (iii): g_k = g(y_{k−1}, y_k) lies in Γ_{j_k} ∖ A, and
  g(y_0, y_n) = g_n ⋯ g_1 is an alternating product of length n ≥ 1. It is
  ≠ 1 by NF, so y_n ≠ y_0. ∎

(i)–(iii) are the natural freeness conditions for R = R_1 *_{R_3} R_2. §2 uses
the decomposition in the form stated by [GL] (the sentence quoted in §0).

**Lemma 1.6 (restricting a Bernoulli shift).** If Λ ≤ G are countable groups,
then s_G|Λ ≅ s_Λ.

*Proof.*
1. Choose representatives R for the right cosets, so G = ⊔_{r∈R} Λr.
2. Define Θ: [0,1]^G → ([0,1]^R)^Λ by Θ(x)(k) = (x(kr))_{r∈R} for k ∈ Λ. It is
   a Borel bijection carrying Leb^G to (Leb^R)^Λ.
3. For λ ∈ Λ, Θ(λ·x)(k) = (x(λ⁻¹kr))_r = Θ(x)(λ⁻¹k) = (λ·Θ(x))(k). So s_G|Λ
   is the Bernoulli shift of Λ with base ([0,1]^R, Leb^R).
4. R is countable and nonempty, so this base is a non-atomic standard
   probability space. A Borel isomorphism θ with ([0,1], Leb), applied in every
   coordinate, conjugates the shift to s_Λ. ∎

## 2. Theorem 1: descent along an amalgam

**Theorem 1.** Let Γ_1 and Γ_2 be countable groups with a common infinite
amenable subgroup A, and let G = Γ_1 *_A Γ_2. Assume every free p.m.p. action
of Γ_1 and every free p.m.p. action of Γ_2 has finite cost. If G has fixed
price, then:
- C(b|Γ_i) = C(Γ_i) for every free p.m.p. action b of G and i = 1, 2;
- Γ_1 and Γ_2 have fixed price;
- c(G) = C(Γ_1) + C(Γ_2) − 1.

*Proof.*

**Step 1 (the amalgamation formula for every free b).** Let b be a free p.m.p.
action of G on (Y, ν).
1. By the sentence of [GL] before Thm 2.27 (re-derived in Lemma 1.5),
   E_b = E_{b|Γ_1} *_{E_{b|A}} E_{b|Γ_2}.
2. E_{b|A} is hyperfinite by [GL] Thm 1.13 (Ornstein–Weiss: "Any p.m.p. action
   of an amenable group is hyperfinite").
3. b|Γ_i is free, so C(E_{b|Γ_i}) < ∞ by hypothesis.
4. [GL] Thm 2.27 gives C(b) = C(b|Γ_1) + C(b|Γ_2) − C(E_{b|A}).
5. b|A is free and A is infinite, so almost all A-orbits are infinite.
   - [GL] Thm 2.20 gives a treeing of E_{b|A} of cost 1.
   - [GL] Thm 2.24 ("If Ψ is a treeing of a p.m.p. equivalence relation R then
     C(Ψ) = C(R)") gives C(E_{b|A}) = 1.

Hence, for every free b,

    (★)  C(b) = C(b|Γ_1) + C(b|Γ_2) − 1.

**Step 2 (the value of c(G)).**
1. C(Γ_i) is finite, since it is at most the cost of any free action of Γ_i.
2. Γ_i is infinite because it contains A. For ε > 0, Corollary 1.4 gives a
   free b_ε with C(b_ε|Γ_i) < C(Γ_i) + ε for i = 1, 2.
3. By fixed price and (★), c(G) = C(b_ε) < C(Γ_1) + C(Γ_2) − 1 + 2ε.
4. Conversely, for every free b, the restriction b|Γ_i is free, so
   C(b|Γ_i) ≥ C(Γ_i). By (★), c(G) = C(b) ≥ C(Γ_1) + C(Γ_2) − 1.
5. So c(G) = C(Γ_1) + C(Γ_2) − 1.

**Step 3 (the restrictions are rigid).** Let b be free.
- Step 2 and (★) give C(b|Γ_1) + C(b|Γ_2) = C(Γ_1) + C(Γ_2).
- All four numbers are finite, and C(b|Γ_i) ≥ C(Γ_i).
- So C(b|Γ_1) = C(Γ_1) and C(b|Γ_2) = C(Γ_2).

**Step 4 (Bernoulli restrictions).**
1. G is infinite, so s_G is free. For h ≠ 1, the fixed set is contained in
   {x : x(h⁻¹) = x(1)}, which is null because the coordinates x(h⁻¹) and x(1)
   are independent and uniform.
2. Step 3 with b = s_G, together with Lemma 1.6, gives
   C(s_{Γ_i}) = C(s_G|Γ_i) = C(Γ_i).

**Step 5 (fixed price of Γ_i).** Let a be a free p.m.p. action of Γ_i.
1. Γ_i is countably infinite and s_{Γ_i} is free. [AW] gives s_{Γ_i} ≼ a.
2. C(a) < ∞ by hypothesis. So [TD] Cor 6.22 ("a free and a ≼ b ⇒
   C(b) ≤ C(a) if C(b) < ∞", in the tabulation of §1 of the passage artifact)
   gives C(a) ≤ C(s_{Γ_i}) = C(Γ_i).
   - The same inequality also follows from [TD] Cor 6.20 and Cor 6.8(1):
     PC(a) ≤ PC(s_{Γ_i}) ≤ C(s_{Γ_i}), and C(a) = PC(a).
3. C(a) ≥ C(Γ_i) by definition. So C(a) = C(Γ_i) for every free a, and Γ_i has
   fixed price c(Γ_i) = C(Γ_i). ∎

Step 5 is the direction (ii) ⇒ fixed price of Theorem B in the passage
artifact, written out here so that this section cites only primary sources.

**Remarks.**
1. *Equivalence.* Combined with the "moreover" clause of [GL] Cor 2.28, under
   the finite-cost hypothesis: G = Γ_1 *_A Γ_2 has fixed price iff Γ_1 and Γ_2
   both do. Only the direction proved above is used below.
2. *The finite-cost hypothesis* is used in Step 1 (hypothesis of [GL]
   Thm 2.27) and in Step 5 (hypothesis of [TD] Cor 6.22). Footnote 17 of [GL]
   says the assumption in Thm 2.27 "can be removed. See Remark 2.29". Remark
   2.29 only sketches this, and it is not used here.
3. *Finite A.* Not needed below, and not checked here.
4. *Why A must be amenable.* Step 1 needs E_{b|A} hyperfinite. If a free action
   of A is hyperfinite, then A is amenable ([GL] Remark 1.12). So for
   nonamenable A, Thm 2.27 does not apply to the decomposition of Lemma 1.5.

## 3. Theorem 2: groups generated by finitely many amenable subgroups

Write AG for the class of countable groups generated by finitely many amenable
subgroups.
- A finite set S can be absorbed as the cyclic subgroups ⟨s⟩ (s ∈ S).
- So Γ ∈ AG iff Γ = ⟨A_1, …, A_m, S⟩ for some amenable subgroups A_i and some
  finite S.
- AG contains every finitely generated group and every countable amenable
  group.

**Theorem 2′ (unconditional).** Let Γ = ⟨A_1, …, A_m, S⟩ with S finite and
every A_i an infinite amenable subgroup.
- For each i, let B_i be a finitely generated group containing a subgroup
  isomorphic to A_i. Such B_i exist by the embedding claim.
- Put G_0 = Γ and G_i = G_{i−1} *_{A_i} B_i for i = 1, …, m, where A_i ≤ Γ ≤
  G_{i−1} is amalgamated with its copy in B_i.

Then G_m is finitely generated. If G_m has fixed price, then Γ has fixed price.

*Proof.*
1. By NF, each G_{i−1} embeds in G_i, so Γ and all B_j embed in G_m.
2. Let T_i be a finite generating set of B_i. Since A_i ⊆ B_i = ⟨T_i⟩, G_i is
   generated by A_{i+1}, …, A_m, S, T_1, …, T_i. In particular G_m is
   generated by the finite set S ∪ T_1 ∪ … ∪ T_m.
3. Corollary 1.1′ gives the following for every free action:
   - G_{i−1} has cost ≤ (m − i + 1) + |S| + |T_1| + … + |T_{i−1}|;
   - B_i has cost ≤ |T_i|.
4. Suppose G_i has fixed price. Theorem 1 applies to
   G_i = G_{i−1} *_{A_i} B_i: A_i is an infinite amenable common subgroup, and
   both factors have finite-cost free actions by step 3. So G_{i−1} has fixed
   price.
5. Downward induction from i = m to i = 1 shows that G_0 = Γ has fixed
   price. ∎

**Theorem 2 (under H).** Assume every infinite finitely generated group has
fixed price. Then every group in AG has fixed price.

*Proof.* Let Γ = ⟨A_1, …, A_m, S⟩ with the A_i amenable and S finite.
1. Absorb the finite A_i into S. Then every remaining A_i is infinite, and S
   is still finite.
2. If no A_i remains, Γ is finitely generated.
   - If Γ is finite, [GL] Cor 2.14 gives fixed price.
   - If Γ is infinite, H gives fixed price.
3. Otherwise, form G_m as in Theorem 2′.
   - G_m is finitely generated and infinite, so it has fixed price by H.
   - Theorem 2′ gives fixed price for Γ. ∎

Theorem 2 uses H only once, at the single finitely generated group G_m, which
contains Γ. It uses no subgroup of Γ beyond the A_i and S. This is how
Theorem 2 escapes the remark of passage artifact §6 that shape (A) is
invisible to arguments passing only through subgroups: G_m is a supergroup.

**Corollary 3 (under H).** Let Γ be a countably infinite group without fixed
price. Then Γ is not generated by finitely many amenable subgroups. In
addition, all the conclusions of Theorem G of the passage artifact hold:
- Γ is not finitely generated and not locally finite;
- C(Γ) < ∞;
- Γ has shape (A) or shape (B).

*Proof.* The first sentence is the contrapositive of Theorem 2. The rest is
Theorem G. ∎

**What Theorem 2 adds to Theorem G.**
- Shape (A) excluded on AG. Theorem 2′ gives C(s_Γ) = C(Γ) for Γ ∈ AG under
  H, via Step 4 of Theorem 1 applied along the tower. By Theorem B, this and
  the finite-cost bound give fixed price.
- Shape (B) excluded on AG. Every free action of Γ ∈ AG has finite cost
  (Corollary 1.1′).
- Theorem G already excludes finitely generated and locally finite groups. AG
  is strictly larger; see the examples in §5.

## 5. The limit of the method

H gives fixed price only at finitely generated groups. Theorem 1 moves fixed
price from an amalgam down to its factors. The first result below says which
factors a single descent step from a finitely generated group can reach.

**Proposition 4.** Let G = Γ_1 *_A Γ_2 be finitely generated. Then there are
finite sets S_1 ⊆ Γ_1 and S_2 ⊆ Γ_2 with Γ_1 = ⟨A, S_1⟩ and Γ_2 = ⟨A, S_2⟩.
In particular, if A is amenable, then Γ_1 and Γ_2 lie in AG.

*Proof.*
1. *Finite letters.* Take a finite generating set of G. Each generator is a
   finite product of elements of Γ_1 ∪ Γ_2. Collect the letters from Γ_1 into
   a finite set S_1 and those from Γ_2 into S_2. Then S_1 ∪ S_2 generates G.
   Put H_j = ⟨A, S_j⟩ ≤ Γ_j. It suffices to show Γ_1 ⊆ H_1; the case of Γ_2 is
   symmetric.
2. *A shortest expression.* Let g ∈ Γ_1. Since G = ⟨S_1 ∪ S_2⟩, g is a product
   g = g_1 ⋯ g_n with every g_k ∈ H_1 ∪ H_2. Choose one with n minimal.
   - If n = 0, then g = 1 ∈ H_1.
   - If n = 1 and g_1 ∈ H_1, then g ∈ H_1.
   - If n = 1 and g_1 ∈ H_2, then g ∈ Γ_1 ∩ Γ_2 = A ⊆ H_1 (NF).
3. *Suppose n ≥ 2.* Minimality gives the following.
   - No g_k lies in A. Otherwise, since A ⊆ H_1 ∩ H_2, merge g_k with a
     neighbour g_{k±1}: the product lies in the same H_j as that neighbour,
     and the expression gets shorter.
   - So each g_k lies in exactly one of Γ_1 ∖ A and Γ_2 ∖ A. Call its index
     j_k, so g_k ∈ H_{j_k} ∖ A ⊆ Γ_{j_k} ∖ A.
   - j_k ≠ j_{k+1}. Otherwise g_k g_{k+1} ∈ H_{j_k}, which shortens the
     expression.
   So g_1 ⋯ g_n is an alternating product of length n ≥ 2 as in NF.
4. *Contradiction.* The relation g⁻¹ g_1 ⋯ g_n = 1 holds. In each case below, a
   product equal to 1 is rewritten as an alternating product of length ≥ 1,
   which NF says is ≠ 1.
   - *g ∈ A.* Then g⁻¹g_1 ∈ Γ_{j_1} ∖ A, and (g⁻¹g_1) g_2 ⋯ g_n is
     alternating of length n.
   - *g ∈ Γ_1 ∖ A and j_1 = 2.* Then g⁻¹ g_1 ⋯ g_n is alternating of length
     n + 1.
   - *g ∈ Γ_1 ∖ A, j_1 = 1, and h = g⁻¹g_1 ∉ A.* Then h ∈ Γ_1 ∖ A, and
     h g_2 ⋯ g_n is alternating of length n.
   - *g ∈ Γ_1 ∖ A, j_1 = 1, and h = g⁻¹g_1 ∈ A.* Then hg_2 ∈ Γ_2 ∖ A because
     j_2 = 2. So (hg_2) g_3 ⋯ g_n is alternating of length n − 1 ≥ 1.
   So n ≥ 2 is impossible, and g ∈ H_1 by step 2. ∎

**Consequence.** Take Γ, a finitely generated G ⊇ Γ, and a splitting
G = Γ *_A B with A infinite amenable. Proposition 4 forces Γ ∈ AG. Conversely,
Theorem 2′ shows that every member of AG is reached, though it needs towers of
such splittings. So the method "embed Γ as an amalgam factor of a finitely
generated group and descend once" gives exactly AG. That is why the residual
claim is the passage restricted to groups outside AG.

**Not analyzed here.** The following descents could reach further. None of
them was checked in this session.
- *Longer towers.* Consider towers G_0 ≤ G_1 ≤ ⋯ ≤ G_m where
  G_i = G_{i−1} *_{A_i} B_i and only G_m is finitely generated. Proposition 4
  gives G_{m−1} ∈ AG, but it does not continue downward.
  - Proposition 4 does not show that an amalgam factor of a group in AG lies
    in AG.
  - The reason is that an amenable subgroup of an amalgam need not be
    conjugate into a factor. For example, it may fix only an end of the
    Bass–Serre tree.
- *Other splittings.* HNN extensions over amenable subgroups ([GL] Cor 2.32
  gives the cost formula) and finite graphs of groups with amenable edge
  groups. Analogues of Theorem 1 and Proposition 4 are expected, but they were
  not checked.
- *Supergroups where Γ is not a factor*, for example Γ normal of infinite index
  in a finitely generated group. Fixed price of the supergroup gives no
  information about the free actions of Γ through any tool used here.

**Members of AG beyond Theorem G.** Theorem G already excludes finitely
generated and locally finite groups. The following groups lie in AG and in
neither of those classes.
- **Q ∗ Z.** It is generated by Q and one element. It is not finitely generated,
  because it maps onto Q. It is not locally finite, because it contains Z.
- **SL(2, Q).**
  - It is generated by the two root subgroups
    {(1 x; 0 1)} and {(1 0; x 1)}, each isomorphic to (Q, +). Over any field,
    SL_2 is generated by elementary matrices.
  - It is not finitely generated. A finite set lies in SL(2, Z[1/N]) for some
    N, and (1 1/p; 0 1) with p prime and p ∤ N does not lie there.
  - It is not locally finite, because it contains SL(2, Z).
  - Per T4 of the passage artifact, [GL] Cor 2.59 gives C_*(SL(2, Q)) = 1, and
    the Nota after it credits Mellick with fixed price 1. Theorem 2 is
    consistent with this.

### 5.1 A test group outside AG

This subsection constructs an explicit countable group that none of the tools
here or in the passage artifact decides. It is evidence about where the method
stops. It is not a graph node.

**Construction.**
- Let F_2 = F(a, b), and let φ: F_2 → F_2 be the endomorphism φ(a) = a,
  φ(b) = bab⁻².
- Let Γ_mal be the direct limit of F_2 →φ F_2 →φ F_2 →φ ⋯, with canonical maps
  ι_n: F_2 → Γ_mal satisfying ι_n = ι_{n+1} ∘ φ.
- Put H_n = ι_n(F_2).

**Standard facts used** (Stallings, *Topology of finite graphs*, Invent. Math.
71 (1983); Lyndon–Schupp, Ch. I; neither re-read in this session).
- (S1) In a folded labelled graph, for a vertex v and a word w there is at most
  one path starting at v with label w, and at most one ending at v.
- (S2) Let Γ_K be the folded graph obtained by folding the petals of generators
  of K ≤ F_2, based at v_0. The labels of closed paths at v_0 represent
  exactly the elements of K. Every element of K is the label of a closed path
  at v_0 whose label is reduced. Removing backtracks from a path keeps its
  endpoints and freely reduces its label.
- (S3) Every g ≠ 1 in F_2 has a reduced form c k c⁻¹ without cancellation, with
  k cyclically reduced. Cyclically reduced conjugate elements are cyclic
  permutations of each other as words.
- (S4) The centralizer of k ≠ 1 in a free group is cyclic, generated by some r
  with k = r^m, m ≥ 1. If k is cyclically reduced, so is r, and r^j is a
  reduced word for every j.
- (S5) A path whose label is a nonempty reduced word has no backtracking. So a
  graph with a closed path of nonempty reduced label is not a tree.
- F_2 is Hopfian, and a subgroup of a free group is free (Nielsen–Schreier).

**Lemma 5.1 (the Stallings graph of φ(F_2)).** Folding the petals of a and
bab⁻¹b⁻¹ gives the graph Γ_φ with vertices 0 (base), 1, 2 and edges
0 →a 0, 0 →b 1, 1 →a 2, 1 →b 2. Hence:
- (a) φ(F_2) is free of rank E − V + 1 = 2.
- (b) b ∉ φ(F_2).
- (c) φ is injective and not surjective.

*Proof.*
1. *Folding.* The petal of bab⁻¹b⁻¹ is 0 →b p →a q, r →b q, 0 →b r. Vertex 0
   has two outgoing b-edges, to p and to r. Identify p = r (= 1) and put
   q = 2. Now every vertex has at most one edge of each label in each
   direction, so the graph is folded.
2. *(a)* The graph is connected with V = 3 and E = 4, so its fundamental group,
   which is φ(F_2) by (S2), is free of rank 2.
3. *(b)* The only path from 0 labelled b ends at 1 ≠ 0. So by (S1) and (S2), b
   is not in φ(F_2).
4. *(c)* By (a) there is an isomorphism θ: φ(F_2) → F_2. Then θ ∘ φ is a
   surjective endomorphism of F_2, hence injective by Hopficity, so φ is
   injective. By (b), φ is not surjective. ∎

**Lemma 5.2 (pullback criterion).** Let K ≤ F_2 have folded graph Γ_K based at
v_0. Let P be the product graph:
- its vertices are the pairs (v, w);
- it has an x-edge (v, w) → (v', w') for every pair of x-edges v → v' and
  w → w' of Γ_K.

If every component of P that contains a vertex (v, w) with v ≠ w is a tree,
then K is malnormal in F_2.

*Proof.*
0. *Components.* By (S1), an edge at a diagonal vertex (v, v) leads to a
   diagonal vertex. So each component of P is either all diagonal or all
   off-diagonal.
1. *Setup.* Let g ∈ F_2 ∖ K, and suppose 1 ≠ h ∈ K ∩ gKg⁻¹.
2. *h at v_0.* By (S3), h = c k c⁻¹ reduced without cancellation, with k ≠ 1
   cyclically reduced. By (S2) there is a closed path at v_0 labelled h. It
   reads c from v_0 to a vertex v, then k from v to v', then c⁻¹ from v' to
   v_0. Reversed, the last segment is a path from v_0 labelled c, so v' = v by
   (S1). Hence k labels a closed path at v.
3. *g⁻¹hg at v_0.* The element g⁻¹hg ∈ K is ≠ 1. By (S3) its reduced form is
   d' k' d'⁻¹ without cancellation, where k' is a cyclic permutation of k:
   k = k_1k_2 and k' = k_2k_1 as words. As in step 2, there is a vertex w with
   a path from v_0 labelled d' to w, and a closed path at w labelled k'.
4. *A second closed path.* Let v_1 be the vertex reached from v by reading
   k_1 along the closed path of step 2. Then k' = k_2k_1 labels a closed path
   at v_1.
5. *First use of the hypothesis.* Pairing the closed paths at v_1 and at w
   gives a closed path in P at (v_1, w) with the nonempty reduced label k'.
   By (S5) its component is not a tree. So v_1 = w.
6. *A centralizer.* As group elements, g⁻¹ckc⁻¹g = d'k'd'⁻¹ and
   k' = k_1⁻¹kk_1. So z := k_1d'⁻¹g⁻¹c commutes with k. By (S4), z = r^j with
   k = r^m (m ≥ 1) and r cyclically reduced. So g = c r^{−j} k_1 d'⁻¹.
7. *One factor lies in K.* The word ck_1 labels a path from v_0 to v_1, and d'
   labels a path from v_0 to w = v_1. So ck_1d'⁻¹ labels a closed path at v_0
   and lies in K by (S2).
8. *Reading powers of r from v.*
   - Let x_0 = v and let x_i be the end of the path reading r from x_{i−1}.
     These paths exist because r^m labels a closed path at v, and x_m = v.
   - By (S1) the sequence is periodic of period dividing m. So r^j labels a
     path from v to x_j for every j ∈ Z, and k = r^m labels a closed path at
     every x_i.
   - *If x_j ≠ v:* pairing the closed paths labelled k at v and at x_j gives a
     non-tree off-diagonal component of P, which is impossible.
   - *If x_j = v:* then r^{−j} labels a closed path at v, so c r^{−j} c⁻¹ ∈ K.
     Hence g = (c r^{−j} c⁻¹)(c k_1 d'⁻¹) ∈ K, contradicting step 1.
So K ∩ gKg⁻¹ = 1 for every g ∉ K. ∎

**Lemma 5.3 (certificate).** φ(F_2) is malnormal in F_2.

*Proof.* In Γ_φ the a-edges are 0 → 0 and 1 → 2, and the b-edges are 0 → 1
and 1 → 2. The edges of P between off-diagonal vertices are:
- a-edges (0,1) → (0,2) and (1,0) → (2,0);
- b-edges (0,1) → (1,2) and (1,0) → (2,1).

So the off-diagonal components are {(0,1), (0,2), (1,2)} and
{(1,0), (2,0), (2,1)}, each with two edges. Both are trees. Lemma 5.2
applies. ∎

The script `experiments/fpbs-amalgam-descent-amenably-generated-2026-09-16/malnormal_image.py`
repeats the folding and the component check. Its output
(`run-2026-09-16.txt` in the same folder) contains the line
`u=a v=baBB folded V=3 E=4 rank=2 proper malnormal`, where B = b⁻¹.

**Lemma 5.4 (transitivity).** If K ≤ L ≤ G with K malnormal in L and L
malnormal in G, then K is malnormal in G.

*Proof.* Let g ∈ G ∖ K.
- If g ∈ L, then K ∩ gKg⁻¹ = 1 because K is malnormal in L.
- If g ∉ L, then K ∩ gKg⁻¹ ⊆ L ∩ gLg⁻¹ = 1. ∎

**Proposition 5.** The group Γ_mal has the following properties.
- (i) It is countable, locally free and not finitely generated, and
  H_n ⊊ H_{n+1}.
- (ii) Every H_n is malnormal in Γ_mal.
- (iii) Every amenable subgroup of Γ_mal is cyclic. Hence Γ_mal ∉ AG.
- (iv) Γ_mal contains none of the subgroups of Proposition F of the passage
  artifact.
- (v) c*(Γ_mal) = 2, with c* as in §4 of the passage artifact.

*Proof.*
- **(i)**
  - Each ι_n is injective: ι_n(x) = 1 means φ^k(x) = 1 for some k, and φ is
    injective (Lemma 5.1(c)).
  - Γ_mal = ∪_n H_n.
  - H_n = ι_{n+1}(φ(F_2)) ⊊ ι_{n+1}(F_2) = H_{n+1}, by Lemma 5.1(b) and the
    injectivity of ι_{n+1}.
  - A finite subset lies in some H_n, and H_n ≠ Γ_mal. So Γ_mal is not
    finitely generated.
  - Every finitely generated subgroup lies in some H_n ≅ F_2, so it is free.
- **(ii)**
  - ι_{n+1}: F_2 → H_{n+1} is an isomorphism carrying φ(F_2) onto H_n. So H_n
    is malnormal in H_{n+1} by Lemma 5.3.
  - By Lemma 5.4 and induction, H_n is malnormal in H_N for every N > n.
  - If g ∈ Γ_mal ∖ H_n, choose N > n with g ∈ H_N. Then H_n ∩ gH_ng⁻¹ = 1.
- **(iii)**
  - Let U ≤ Γ_mal be amenable and U ≠ 1, and choose 1 ≠ p ∈ U and n with
    p ∈ H_n.
  - Every finitely generated subgroup of U is free and amenable, hence cyclic.
  - So every r ∈ U commutes with p. Then p ∈ H_n ∩ r⁻¹H_nr, so r ∈ H_n by
    (ii).
  - Hence U lies in the centralizer of p in H_n, which is cyclic by (S4). So U
    is cyclic.
  - If Γ_mal = ⟨A_1, …, A_m, S⟩ with the A_i amenable and S finite, every A_i
    would be cyclic and Γ_mal finitely generated, contradicting (i).
- **(iv)** Let Λ ≤ Γ_mal be infinite and finitely generated, so Λ ⊆ H_n for
  some n. Choose g ∈ Γ_mal ∖ H_n.
  - *Normal:* Λ = gΛg⁻¹ ⊆ H_n ∩ gH_ng⁻¹ = 1, which is impossible.
  - *Commensurated:* Λ ∩ gΛg⁻¹ has finite index in Λ, so it is infinite. But it
    lies in H_n ∩ gH_ng⁻¹ = 1.
  - *Chain:* let Λ = Γ_0 ≤ Γ_1 ≤ ⋯ with ∪Γ_k = Γ_mal and
    Γ_{k+1} = ⟨Γ_k, γ_{k+1}⟩.
    - Since Γ_mal ⊄ H_n, there is a least k with Γ_{k+1} ⊄ H_n. Then
      Γ_k ⊆ H_n and γ_{k+1} ∉ H_n.
    - So γ_{k+1}⁻¹Γ_kγ_{k+1} ∩ Γ_k ⊆ γ_{k+1}⁻¹H_nγ_{k+1} ∩ H_n = 1 by (ii).
    - This violates the chain condition.
- **(v)**
  - Every infinite finitely generated Δ ≤ Γ_mal is free of finite rank, with
    fixed price equal to its rank ([G00] Corollaire 1, as in the passage
    artifact's remark on locally free groups).
  - Every finite F lies in some H_N of rank 2, so c_F ≤ 2.
  - If F contains the basis ι_0(a), ι_0(b) of H_0, every Δ ⊇ F contains the
    nonabelian H_0, so it has rank ≥ 2 and c_F = 2.
  - Hence c* = sup_F c_F = 2. ∎

**Status of Γ_mal.**
- *What the target says about Γ_mal.* The target for Γ_mal is the implication
  "H ⇒ Γ_mal has fixed price". The finitely generated subgroups of Γ_mal are
  free, with fixed price equal to the rank ([G00] Corollaire 1), so H holds
  for them unconditionally.
  - Hence any argument that applies H only to subgroups of Γ_mal (as all of
    §4–§6 of the passage artifact do) would prove the unconditional statement
    "Γ_mal has fixed price" outright. That statement is an instance of [GL]
    Q 2.62.
  - This is the sense of the passage artifact's remark on locally free groups.
  - An argument that applies H to a finitely generated supergroup, as
    Theorem 2 does, could still settle the implication without settling the
    unconditional statement.
- *Neither artifact decides it.* Theorem 2 does not apply by (iii), and
  Proposition F does not apply by (iv).
- *The obvious supergroup.* M = ⟨a, b, t | tat⁻¹ = a, tbt⁻¹ = bab⁻²⟩ is
  finitely generated. Presumably Γ_mal ≅ ker(M → Z), as in T5 step 1, but this
  was not re-derived for this φ. There Γ_mal is normal of infinite index, not
  an amalgam factor, and M is an HNN extension over the nonamenable φ(F_2). So
  neither Theorem 1 nor [GL] Cor 2.32 applies.
- *Conditional remark (not checked).* Theorem G of the passage artifact applies
  to Γ_mal with no hypothesis. If Γ_mal had no fixed price, then
  1 + β_1^{(2)}(Γ_mal) ≤ C(Γ_mal) ≤ PC(s_{Γ_mal}) ≤ c* = 2.
  - If β_1^{(2)}(Γ_mal) = 1, shape (A) would be impossible, and a failure of
    fixed price would have to be of shape (B): a free action of infinite cost.
  - The T5 step 4 computation would give β_1^{(2)}(Γ_mal) = 1 if the Fox map
    (v_1, v_2) ↦ (v_1 + v_2b, v_2(1 − bab⁻¹ − bab⁻²)) were injective on
    N(Γ_mal)².
  - Its second entry is a trinomial, while T5's elementary argument covers only
    binomials 1 ∓ g. Injectivity would need a zero-divisor input such as
    Linnell's theorem for free groups, which was not consulted.
  - This shape of φ cannot avoid the trinomial. No proper rank-2 malnormal
    K = ⟨a, v⟩ has v with exactly two b-letters, which is what a binomial
    entry would need.
    - Multiplying v on either side by powers of a does not change K, so one
      may take v = b^ε a^j b^δ.
    - If δ = −ε, then j ≠ 0, because otherwise K = ⟨a⟩ has rank 1. Now
      1 ≠ b^εa^jb^{−ε} ∈ K ∩ b^εKb^{−ε}, and b^ε ∉ K since K is proper.
    - If δ = ε, replace v by v⁻¹ if needed so that v = ba^jb. Then
      (a^jb)² = a^j·v ∈ K and (a^jb)² = b⁻¹·(v·a^j)·b ∈ b⁻¹Kb. This element
      is ≠ 1, and b ∉ K since K is proper, so K is not malnormal.
    - In graph terms, with petal vertices 0 →b p_1 → ⋯ → p_{|j|+1} →b 0, the
      pairs (0, p_1), …, (0, p_{|j|+1}), (p_1, 0), …, (p_{|j|+1}, 0) form a
      cycle of length 2(|j| + 1) in an off-diagonal component. It uses the
      a-loop at 0 and reads (a^jb)². Lemma 5.2 is only the sufficient
      direction, so the explicit witness above is what proves
      non-malnormality.
