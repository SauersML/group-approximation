# The arithmetical position of the paradox/measure dichotomy

Lane `un-open-6`, 2026-09-13. Unreviewed. Plan: `un-open-6-plan-2026-09-13.md`.

## 0. Statements

- **Theorem A** (`effective-subshift-invariant-measure-existence-is-pi01`). For an effectively closed subshift `X_e`
  of a finitely generated group `Γ` with solvable word problem, "X_e carries a Γ-invariant probability measure" is
  `Π⁰₁` uniformly in `e`.
- **Theorem B** (`effective-free-group-kazhdan-lef-type-is-pi01`). On the promise class `𝒫` of effectively closed,
  nonempty, minimal, topologically free `F_r`-subshifts (`2 ≤ r < ∞`), the predicate "EL_N(LC(X_e,F_q)⋊F_r) is LEF for
  every N ≥ 3" equals the `Π⁰₁` set of Theorem A, and "there is n with no nontrivial MF quotient of EL_N for every
  N ≥ 2n" equals its complement.
- **Open C** (`effective-free-group-kazhdan-lef-type-is-pi01-complete`). Hardness on `𝒫`.

## 1. Proof of Theorem A

Notation. `S` is a finite symmetric generating set, `B_n` the ball of radius `n` (computable, since the word problem
is solvable), `A` a finite alphabet, `(g·x)(h) = x(g⁻¹h)`. A pattern is `p : F → A` with `F ⊆ Γ` finite, and `g·p` is
the pattern on `gF` with `(g·p)(gh) = p(h)`. The cylinder of `w ∈ A^F` at `F` is `[w at F] = {x : x|F = w}`; then
`g·[w at F] = [g·w at gF]`. `W_(e,n)` is the set of the first `n` patterns enumerated by `e`, and
`X_e = ⋂_n {x : for all p ∈ W_(e,n), g ∈ Γ, x|(g·supp p) ≠ g·p}`.

**The programs.** `Adm(e,n) ⊆ A^(B_n)` consists of the `q` with `q|(g·supp p) ≠ g·p` whenever `p ∈ W_(e,n)` and
`g·supp p ⊆ B_n`. Only finitely many `g` qualify, and they are computable. `LP(e,n)` is the system in the unknowns
`ρ(q)`, `q ∈ Adm(e,n)`:
- `ρ(q) ≥ 0` and `Σ_q ρ(q) = 1`;
- for every `s ∈ S` and `w ∈ A^(B_(n-1))`: `Σ_{q|B_(n-1) = w} ρ(q) = Σ_{q|sB_(n-1) = s·w} ρ(q)`.

Here `sB_(n-1) ⊆ B_n` because `|s| = 1`. A finite rational linear system is feasible over the reals iff it is feasible
over the rationals, and feasibility is decidable (Fourier–Motzkin elimination). So `LP` is a computable predicate, and
`{e : ∀n LP(e,n)}` is `Π⁰₁`.

**Measure ⇒ programs.** Let `μ` be invariant with `μ(X_e) = 1`, and put `ρ(q) = μ([q at B_n])`. If `q ∉ Adm(e,n)`,
then `[q at B_n]` contains an occurrence of a forbidden pattern, so it misses `X_e` and `ρ(q) = 0`. The equality for
`(s,w)` reads `μ([w at B_(n-1)]) = μ([s·w at sB_(n-1)]) = μ(s·[w at B_(n-1)])`, which is invariance.

**Programs ⇒ measure.** Choose feasible `ρ_n` for every `n` (extend by `0` off `Adm(e,n)`). For `m ≤ n` let
`q_(n,m) ∈ Prob(A^(B_m))` be the marginal of `ρ_n`. Each `Prob(A^(B_m))` is a compact simplex, so a diagonal
subsequence `n_j` has `q_(n_j,m) → q_m` for every `m`. Marginalization is continuous and the `q_(n,m)` are consistent
(the marginal of `q_(n,m+1)` on `B_m` is `q_(n,m)`), so the limits are consistent, and Kolmogorov extension gives a
unique Borel probability `μ` on `A^Γ` with `μ([w at B_m]) = q_m(w)`.

*Invariance.* Fix `m ≥ 1`, `s ∈ S` and `u ∈ A^(B_(m-1))`. For `n ≥ m`, sum the level-`n` equations over all `w` on
`B_(n-1)` extending `u`: the left side gives the `ρ_n`-mass of `[u at B_(m-1)]` and the right side the mass of
`[s·u at sB_(m-1)]`. Both are masses of cylinders inside `B_m`, so they are values of `q_(n,m)`; letting `n = n_j → ∞`,
`μ([u at B_(m-1)]) = μ(s·[u at B_(m-1)])`. Every cylinder `[w at F]` is a finite disjoint union of ball cylinders, so
`μ ∘ s = μ` on the cylinder algebra, hence on Borel sets, for every generator; so `μ` is `Γ`-invariant.

*Support.* Fix `p ∈ W_e`, enumerated by stage `n0`, and `g ∈ Γ`. Take `m` with `g·supp p ⊆ B_m`. For `n ≥ max(m, n0)`
every `q ∈ Adm(e,n)` avoids `g·p` on `g·supp p`, so `q_(n,m)` gives mass `0` to every `u ∈ A^(B_m)` with
`u|(g·supp p) = g·p`. In the limit, `μ` gives mass `0` to the set of `x` containing `g·p` at `g·supp p`. The
complement of `X_e` is a countable union of such sets, so `μ(X_e) = 1`. ∎

## 2. Proof of Theorem B

Fix `e ∈ 𝒫`; write `X = X_e` and `R = LC(X,F_q) ⋊ F_r` (algebraic crossed product, `u_g χ_Y u_g⁻¹ = χ_(gY)`).

- **Finite generation and (T).** For `a ∈ A` put `c_a = χ_{x(1)=a}`. Then `u_g c_a u_g⁻¹ = χ_{x(g)=a}`, since
  `g·{x : x(1) = a} = {y : y(g) = a}`. Finite products of these give every cylinder indicator, and cylinder indicators
  span `LC(X,F_q)`. So `R` is generated by the `c_a` and the `u_s^(±1)`, and `EL_N(R)` has (T) for `N ≥ 3` by
  `elementary-groups-over-fg-rings-have-property-t`.
- **If `X` has an invariant probability measure.** `free-group-crossed-product-matricial-iff-invariant-measure` gives
  residual finiteness of the action (its (i) ⇒ (ii), resting on Kerr–Nowak). Then
  `residually-finite-actions-give-matricial-crossed-products`, item 3, makes `EL_N(R)` LEF for every `N ≥ 3`.
- **If not.** `R` is the Steinberg algebra of the transformation groupoid
  (`crossed-products-are-transformation-steinberg-algebras`), which is ample, Hausdorff and second countable with
  compact unit space `X` and no invariant probability measure. `no-invariant-measure-steinberg-elementary-no-mf-quotient`
  gives `n` such that every homomorphism from `EL_N(R)` to an MF group is trivial for `N ≥ 2n`. Since `EL_N(R) ≠ 1`, it
  is not MF, hence not LEF (`lef-implies-operator-mf`).
- **Conclusion.** On `𝒫`, "LEF for every `N ≥ 3`" holds iff `e ∈ P` and "no nontrivial MF quotient from some rank on"
  holds iff `e ∉ P`. `P` is `Π⁰₁` by Theorem A. ∎

**Tarski reading.** For ample groupoids with compact unit space, `[X]` is completely non-paradoxical iff there is an
invariant probability measure (`rainone-sims-type-semigroup-dichotomy`, Thm `Tarski`, with
`abbl-type-semigroup-states-extend-to-invariant-measures`). A paradoxical decomposition is a finite object whose
verification on an effectively closed `X` is `Σ⁰₁` (emptiness of a clopen set inside `X` is `Σ⁰₁` by compactness). So
both sides of the dichotomy carry finite certificates of complementary kinds: an existential paradox certificate on
the non-MF side, and a universal family of feasible frequency programs on the LEF side.
