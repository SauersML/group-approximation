## Kun--Thom v3

### Introduction, p. 2 (verbatim)

> There are also applications to probability-measure-preserving (p.m.p.)
> actions. We use Păunescu's notion of soficity, formulated through
> diagonal-preserving embeddings of group-measure-space crossed products into
> tracial ultraproducts of matrix algebras [16, Definition 1.4]. Combining the
> breakthrough of OpenAI with [2, Corollary E] gives a hyperbolic group
> admitting a nonsofic p.m.p. action. Păunescu asked whether every p.m.p.
> action of a sofic group is sofic [16, Section 3]. Now, Theorem C shows that
> the Γ-fixed algebra of every sofic p.m.p. action is G-invariant, and
> Corollary D deduces that every nontrivial generalized Bernoulli action over
> G/Γ is nonsofic when Γ is not normal. Since the groups in Theorem E are
> residually finite and hence sofic, Corollary D answers Păunescu's question
> in the negative.

> Let N = ⟨⟨Γ⟩⟩ be the normal subgroup generated by Γ. Theorem C implies, in
> particular, that L²(X,μ)^Γ ⊆ L²(X,μ)^N for every sofic action: if a bounded
> f is Γ-fixed, then g⁻¹f is again Γ-fixed, and hence gγg⁻¹f = f for g ∈ G
> and γ ∈ Γ.

### Definitions 2.1 and 2.2, p. 4 (verbatim)

> **Definition 2.1.** A homomorphism σ : G → 𝒮_𝒰 is a *sofic representation*
> if tr(σ(g)) = 0 for every g ≠ 1. For H ≤ G, let D_𝒰^{σ(H)} be the algebra of
> all f ∈ D_𝒰 satisfying σ(h)fσ(h)⁻¹ = f for every h ∈ H.

> **Definition 2.2.** A p.m.p. action G ↷ (X,μ) is *sofic* if there are finite
> sets Y_n and an embedding Θ : L^∞(X) ⋊ G → ∏_{n→𝒰} M_{|Y_n|}(ℂ) such that
> Θ(L^∞(X)) ⊂ D_𝒰 and Θ(u_g) ∈ 𝒮_𝒰 for every g ∈ G.

> The canonical trace of L^∞(X) ⋊ G vanishes on u_g for g ≠ 1. Thus
> g ↦ Θ(u_g) is a sofic representation.

(The crossed product is the von Neumann crossed product and an embedding is a
unital trace-preserving ∗-embedding, per the sentence preceding Definition 2.2.)

### Section 3.2, p. 7 (verbatim)

> **Proof of Theorem C.** Discrete Kazhdan groups are finitely generated. Let
> G ↷ (X,μ) be sofic and let Θ be an embedding as in Definition 2.2. Identify
> L^∞(X) with its image in D_𝒰 and put σ(g) = Θ(u_g). The expander
> decomposition theorem [13, Theorem 1] supplies both decompositions required
> in Proposition 3.1. Hence D_𝒰^{σ(Γ)} is normalized by σ(G).
>
> Moreover, Θ(L^∞(X)^Γ) = Θ(L^∞(X)) ∩ D_𝒰^{σ(Γ)}. Both algebras on the right
> are normalized by σ(G). Their intersection is therefore normalized by σ(G),
> and injectivity and equivariance of Θ imply that L^∞(X)^Γ is G-invariant. □

> **Proof of Corollary D.** Choose a measurable set B ⊆ K with 0 < κ(B) < 1.
> For gΓ ∈ G/Γ, let e_{gΓ} be the indicator of the event x_{gΓ} ∈ B.
> Coordinate projections belonging to distinct cosets are distinct in L^∞,
> because they disagree on a set of measure 2κ(B)(1 − κ(B)) > 0.
>
> The projection e_Γ is Γ-invariant. Its translate by g ∈ G is e_{gΓ}, which
> is Γ-invariant precisely when Γ fixes gΓ. Equivalently, Γ ≤ gΓg⁻¹. If the
> action were sofic, Theorem C would imply this inclusion for every g ∈ G.
> Applying it to g⁻¹ gives the reverse inclusion, so gΓg⁻¹ = Γ for every g,
> contrary to nonnormality. □

> Soficity of p.m.p. actions passes to factors: one restricts the crossed
> product embedding in Definition 2.2 to the invariant subalgebra defining the
> factor. We obtain a strengthened free-action consequence.

> **Corollary 3.2.** *Under the assumptions of Corollary D, the group G admits
> a free ergodic nonsofic p.m.p. action. This action is strongly ergodic.*
>
> **Proof.** First note that [G : Γ] = ∞. Otherwise, for every t ∈ P_Γ, the
> conjugate tΓt⁻¹ would be contained in Γ and have the same finite index in G,
> hence would equal Γ. Since Γ is infranormal in G, this would make Γ normal.
>
> Equip {0,1} with the fair measure ν = (δ₀ + δ₁)/2. The generalized Bernoulli
> action on ({0,1},ν)^{G/Γ} is ergodic. Take its product with the ordinary
> Bernoulli shift G ↷ ({0,1},ν)^G. The latter is mixing and essentially free
> because G is infinite. The product is therefore free and ergodic. If it were
> sofic, its first factor would be sofic, contradicting Corollary D. Finally,
> every ergodic p.m.p. action of a Kazhdan group is strongly ergodic [5]. □

> Elek and Lippner introduced sofic measured equivalence relations [6]. For
> free actions, Păunescu proved that soficity of the action is equivalent to
> soficity of its orbit equivalence relation [16, Proposition 1.15]. Thus
> Corollary 3.2 also yields a nonsofic ergodic equivalence relation generated
> by a free action of a residually finite group.

## Păunescu v5

### Definitions, p. 2 (verbatim)

> **Definition 1.3.** An action α of a countable group G on a standard
> Borelian space (X,ℬ,μ) is called *hyperlinear* if the crossed product
> L^∞(X) ⋊_α G embeds in R^ω.

> **Definition 1.4.** An action α of a countable group G on a standard
> Borelian space (X,ℬ,μ) is called *sofic* if the crossed product
> L^∞(X) ⋊_α G embeds in Π_{k→ω} M_{n_k}(ℂ) such that
> L^∞(X) ⊂ Π_{k→ω} D_{n_k}(ℂ) and G ⊂ Π_{k→ω} P_{n_k}(ℂ).

### p. 7 (verbatim)

> **Proposition 1.15.** Let α be a free action. Then E_α is a sofic
> equivalence relation if and only if α is a sofic action.

### Section 3, pp. 13-16 (verbatim)

> The goal would be to prove that every (free) action of a sofic group is
> sofic. While this remains open we shall prove this fact for a family of
> groups. Let's first solve this ambiguity: free or general actions.

> **Theorem 3.1.** Let G be a group such that every free action is sofic.
> Then every action of G is sofic.

(Proof: product with a free action β, embed L^∞(X) by id ⊗ 1, extend to
L^∞(X) ⋊_α G ⊆ L^∞(X × Y) ⋊_{α⊗β} G.)

> **Definition 3.2.** Denote by 𝒮 the class of groups for which every action
> is sofic.

> While we can not prove that every sofic group is in 𝒮, we will provide some
> examples. First goal is to deal with amenable groups.

> **Proposition 3.3.** Each action of the integers admits a sofic embedding.

> **Proposition 3.4.** Amenable groups are in 𝒮.

> **Theorem 3.5.** Let α₁ and α₂ be two sofic actions of G₁ and G₂ on the same
> space X. Consider H, a common amenable subgroup of G₁ and G₂. Assume α₁ and
> α₂ coincide on H, and this action of H is free. Then the action α₁ ∗_H α₂ of
> G₁ ∗_H G₂ is sofic.

> **Proposition 3.6.** Let {α_i}_{i∈ℕ} be a family of sofic actions of
> {G_i}_{i∈ℕ} on the same space. Assume H is an amenable common subgroup of
> G_i and the actions α_i coincide on H. Then ∗_H α_i is sofic.

> **Corollary 3.7.** Each action of a free group, including 𝔽_∞ is sofic.

> **Proposition 3.8.** Every treeable equivalence relation is sofic.

> **Theorem 3.9.** Class 𝒮 is closed under amalgamated product over amenable
> groups. It is strictly larger than the class of treeable groups.

(Proof of 3.9: "First part of the theorem is 3.5 and 3.1. By 3.8 (and again
3.1) every treeable group is in 𝒮." The strictness example is
ℤ ∗_{(2,3)ℤ} ℤ.)
