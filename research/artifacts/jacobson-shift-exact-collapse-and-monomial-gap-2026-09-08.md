# Exact collapse and restricted matrix gaps for the recursive Jacobson candidate

2026-09-08. Every exact finite-dimensional complex unitary representation
of `Theta_shift` kills its head group. The proof uses commuting conjugate
copies of a fixed perfect finite group and elementary finite-dimensional
representation theory. There is also a dimension-independent monomial
gap, an explicit excluded norm neighborhood of that class, and a positive
gap for models permuting blocks of any fixed bounded size. None of these
results establishes a uniform gap for arbitrary approximate unitary
representations.

Use the presentation and notation of
[the stable-letter construction](jacobson-stable-letter-recursion-and-matrix-gap-2026-09-08.md).
In particular `G=GL_3(F_2)`, the original head is `G_Q`, and
`w=x_13,Q` is nontrivial in `Theta_shift` by its bilateral literal model.

## 1. A finite-dimensional bound for commuting perfect groups

Let `D` be a nontrivial perfect finite group. Suppose that
`rho_i:D->U(d)`, for `1<=i<=n`, are nontrivial homomorphisms and
that the images of different homomorphisms commute elementwise. Then

```text
d>=2n.                                                   (1)
```

Indeed, multiplication of the commuting images defines a representation
of the finite direct product `D^n` on `C^d`. Its irreducible summands
are tensor products

```text
sigma_1 tensor ... tensor sigma_n,
```

where each `sigma_i` is an irreducible representation of `D`. This
standard finite-group decomposition follows successively by decomposing
into isotypic subspaces for one factor: the other commuting factors act
on its multiplicity spaces. Complete reducibility follows by averaging
an inner product over the finite group.

A perfect group has no nontrivial one-dimensional complex representation,
since every homomorphism into the abelian group `C^x` kills all
commutators. Thus every nontrivial `sigma_i` has dimension at least two.
If one tensor summand has `s` nontrivial factors, its dimension is at
least `2^s`, which is at least `2s` when `s>=1`. For a trivial summand
the bound by `2s=0` also holds. Count summands with their multiplicities.
Each of the `n` factors acts nontrivially somewhere, so the sum of
these support sizes `s` is at least `n`. Adding the dimension bounds
proves (1). Neither faithfulness of the individual maps nor simplicity
of `D` is required.

Consequently, suppose a group contains pairwise commuting copies
`D_i=t^i D_0 t^(-i)` for every `i>=0`. Every finite-dimensional
unitary representation kills `D_0`. Otherwise all its conjugates
act nontrivially, and applying (1) to arbitrarily many of them contradicts
the fixed dimension.

## 2. The stable-letter equations give the required centralization

Inside `Theta_shift`, let `H` denote the subgroup generated by the
original `Theta` generators, and let `alpha=Ad_t`. The seven new
equations give

```text
alpha(g_Q)=tau(g),
alpha(g_P)=delta(g),
alpha(h)=h_1.                                            (2)
```

Their right sides are elements of `H`, so `alpha(H)<=H`. This
statement concerns the image of the old generators in the new group;
it does not assume that the original abstract `Theta` embeds.

The exact finite packets also give

```text
[G_Q,tau(G)]=1,
[G_Q,delta(G)]=1,
[G_Q,h_1]=1.                                             (3)
```

The first two identities hold in
`B=GL_6(F_2) x delta(G)`: the head and the local tail have
disjoint coordinate blocks, and `delta(G)` centralizes the
six-coordinate factor. The last identity holds in
`C=C_0 x C_far`, since `G_Q<=C_0` and `h_1` belongs to
the second direct factor. Because the elements in (2) generate
`alpha(H)`, equations (3) prove

```text
[G_Q,alpha(H)]=1.                                        (4)
```

For `j>i>=0`, the inclusion `alpha(H)<=H` implies
`alpha^(j-i)(G_Q)<=alpha(H)`. Apply `alpha^i` to (4).
The resulting head subgroups

```text
G_i=alpha^i(G_Q),           i>=0,
```

therefore commute pairwise. They are all conjugate by powers of the
single stable letter `t`. Their literal actions are the natural copies
of `G` on the successive level triples, so each is a copy of `G`.
The dimension argument needs only their conjugacy, centralization,
and the nontriviality of any retained image.

## 3. Every exact finite-dimensional representation kills the head

The finite group `GL_3(F_2)` is perfect by an elementary calculation.
Its elementary transvections generate it by row reduction. For distinct
indices `i,j,k`, its matrix identities give

```text
x_ij=[x_ik,x_kj].                                        (5)
```

Thus every elementary generator belongs to the commutator subgroup,
which is the whole group. This proves exactly the perfectness required
in Section 1 without using any infinite-group representation estimate.

Let `rho:Theta_shift->U(d)` be an exact representation. If its
restriction to `G_Q` is nontrivial, each `rho(G_i)` is nontrivial,
since the restrictions are conjugate by powers of `rho(t)`. By
Section 2 their images commute. Applying (1) to `G_0,...,G_(n-1)`
gives `d>=2n` for every positive integer `n`, a contradiction.
Therefore

```text
rho(G_Q)={I},                 rho(w)=I.                   (6)
```

Since the bilateral action retains `w`, this is an actual obstruction
to separating that group element by exact finite-dimensional unitary
representations. It does not yet exclude norm matrix corona
representations: a nonidentity involution can survive in approximate
models of growing dimension. The proof chooses a number of commuting
heads larger than half the matrix dimension and supplies no uniform
control of the errors needed to construct that many heads in an
approximate model. The positive lower bound for `gamma_shift` remains
an additional requirement.

## 4. A uniform gap for simultaneous monomial matrices

A monomial unitary maps each vector of a chosen orthonormal basis to
a scalar multiple of another basis vector. Its underlying permutation
defines a homomorphism

```text
pi:Mon(d)->Sym(d).
```

If two monomial unitaries `A,B` have different underlying permutations,
there is a basis vector whose two images are orthogonal. Consequently

```text
pi(A)!=pi(B)  implies  ||A-B||>=sqrt(2).                  (7)
```

Take admissible data for `gamma_shift` from Section 5 of the
stable-letter artifact. Thus the representations of `B,C`, their
agreement on `Z`, and the conjugations by `T` on `F` are exact, and
the head mark survives. Suppose every matrix in these two finite
representations, and `T`, is monomial in one common orthonormal basis.
Let `delta` denote the maximum of the three mixed errors defining
`gamma_shift`.

If `delta<sqrt(2)`, equation (7) makes all three mixed relators exact
after applying `pi`. The other relations were already exact. The
permutation images therefore define a homomorphism from `Theta_shift`
to `Sym(d)`. By Section 3 this homomorphism kills `G_Q`: compose it
with the faithful permutation representation in `U(d)` to apply that
section. Thus the original monomial representation of `G_Q` is
diagonal. Since `G_Q` is perfect and the diagonal unitary group is
abelian, that representation is trivial. This contradicts the retained
head. Hence

```text
delta>=sqrt(2),
inf_(simultaneous monomial admissible data) delta>=sqrt(2). (8)
```

The common basis is essential. Individual finite-group representations
having monomial realizations in unrelated bases is not the assumption.
This is a bound for the displayed restricted infimum, not a lower bound
for unrestricted `gamma_shift`.

## 5. An explicit neighborhood of the monomial class

Keep arbitrary admissible data `rho_B,rho_C,T`, and the same `delta`.
Define their distance `a` from simultaneous monomial matrices as
follows. Choose one orthonormal basis and arbitrary monomial unitaries

```text
M_b       for every b in B,
N_c       for every c in C,
S         approximating T.
```

Take the maximum of

```text
||rho_B(b)-M_b||,   ||rho_C(c)-N_c||,   ||T-S||,
```

over the two finite groups, and then the infimum over the common basis
and all these choices. The approximants are not assumed to be
representations, to agree on the intersection, or to intertwine `F`.
The definition uses every element of the two fixed finite packets,
not just their generators. Then

```text
delta+6a>=sqrt(2).                                      (9)
```

Here is the full rounding argument. Suppose otherwise, and choose
approximants with maximum distance `a'` such that
`delta+6a'<sqrt(2)`. Exact multiplication in each finite representation
gives

```text
||M_b M_b'-M_(bb')||<=3a',
||N_c N_c'-N_(cc')||<=3a'.
```

Equation (7) makes their permutations exact homomorphisms of `B` and
`C`. The two approximants to a common `z in Z` have distance at most
`2a'`, so their permutations agree. Each exact intertwining relation
on `F` gives

```text
||S M_f S^*-M_(alpha_F(f))||<=4a',
```

so these relations also hold for the permutations.

To check the remaining three relators, regard an arbitrary element
of either finite packet as one letter. The braid `(h b)^3` has
length six. The stable-letter relation `t h t^-1=h_1` has length
four when written as a relator. Finally, set `z=a_Q r_P in B` and
recall that

```text
c_P,k_P in Z,
u=[h c_P h,k_P] in C.
```

The first inclusion holds because `c,k` belong to `K_P`: their local
copies act on the two coordinates `e_(1,1),e_(3,1)` contained in
`C_0`, and their residual copies belong to `delta(K_P)`. The second
inclusion follows from the finite multiplication table of `C`.
Thus the same defining word `W` can be evaluated as the six-letter
word

```text
W=(u h) z (h u^-1 h) z^-1 h z.                         (10)
```

The three parenthesized or individual `C` factors are `u h`,
`h u^-1 h`, and `h`; the other three factors lie in `B`.
There is no added relation here. The exact rounded permutation table
of `C` identifies its grouped factors with their constituent words,
including the commutator defining `u`, and it agrees with `B` on
`Z`. The permutation indexed by `z` likewise equals the product
indexed by `a_Q,r_P`. Grouping is available precisely because the
distance `a` tests every element of the two finite packets.

Telescoping products of unitaries bounds the three approximated
relator errors by `delta+6a'`, `delta+4a'`, and `delta+6a'`.
They are all below `sqrt(2)`. Their permutations therefore satisfy
the entire presentation of `Theta_shift`, giving a finite permutation
quotient. Section 3 kills the head permutations. In particular the
approximants to `x_12,Q` and `x_23,Q` are diagonal and commute.
The exact head identity

```text
w=x_13,Q=[x_12,Q,x_23,Q]
```

and a four-letter telescoping estimate now give

```text
||rho_B(w)-I||<=4a'<4sqrt(2)/6<2.
```

This contradicts the fact that a nonidentity unitary involution has
distance exactly two from the identity. It proves (9), including
when the defining infimum for `a` is approached rather than attained.

In particular, data at distance at most `a_0<sqrt(2)/6` from this
class have mixed error at least `sqrt(2)-6a_0`. Any admissible
sequence whose mixed errors tend to zero must stay at asymptotic
distance at least `sqrt(2)/6` from simultaneous monomial matrices
in this finite-packet metric. This still permits unrestricted matrix
models outside that neighborhood.

## 6. Fixed-size permuted blocks also have a positive gap

Fix an integer `m>=1`. Suppose there is an orthogonal decomposition
into blocks of dimensions at most `m` such that every matrix
`rho_B(b),rho_C(c),T` maps each whole block unitarily onto another
whole block. The number of blocks is unrestricted. Then there is a
constant `kappa_m>0`, independent of the total dimension and number
of blocks, such that every admissible tuple of this kind has

```text
delta>=kappa_m.                                         (11)
```

No explicit value of `kappa_m` is asserted for `m>1`.

Indeed these block-permuting unitaries also map homomorphically to
the finite permutation group of their blocks. Two such unitaries
whose block permutations differ have operator-norm distance at least
`sqrt(2)`, by testing a unit vector in a moved block. For
`delta<sqrt(2)`, the argument of Section 4 therefore gives a finite
permutation quotient of `Theta_shift`, and kills the head's block
permutation. It also kills the block permutation of all of
`K=GL_6(F_2)`: the conjugates in `K` of the head transvection
`w` include every elementary transvection, and these generate `K`.
Consequently `rho_B(K)` preserves each block individually.

For every integer `i>=0`, put

```text
R_i(g)=T^i rho_B(g_Q) T^(-i),
L_i(k)=T^i rho_B(k) T^(-i)   for k in K.
```

These are exact finite-group representations. Since `T` permutes
the blocks and `rho_B(K)` preserves them, each `L_i` preserves every
block. The exact stable-letter identities on `F` give

```text
R_i(g)=L_i(g_Q),
R_(i+1)(g)=L_i(tau(g)).                                 (12)
```

Choose a block on which `rho_B(w)` is nonidentity. In `K`, the
transvections `w` and `tau(x_13)` are conjugate by a permutation
of the six coordinates. Their images under the restriction of
`L_i` to this same block are therefore conjugate unitaries. Equation
(12) propagates the nontriviality of `R_i(w)` through every `i`
on this one block. Each such restriction is an exact nontrivial
representation of `G`, with the marked involution at distance two
from the identity.

If (11) failed, choose such admissible tuples with `delta->0` and
one marked block for each tuple. Pad these blocks by a trivial
summand to dimension `m` if needed. For any fixed number `N`,
compactness of finitely many copies of `U(m)` gives a subsequence
on which all matrices of `R_0,...,R_(N-1)` on the chosen block
converge. The limits remain exact nontrivial representations of
`G`.

These limiting representations commute pairwise. To justify this
without assuming that the approximate tuple satisfies a derived
relation exactly, fix `i<j` and two elements of `G`. Their
commutator word is an identity of `Theta_shift` by Section 2.
In its finite presentation that word is a finite product of conjugates
of defining relators and their inverses. Evaluating it on the tuples
gives an error tending to zero: the finite-packet and `F`
intertwining errors are zero, and the three remaining defining
errors tend to zero. The number of factors needed is finite for
this fixed word. Restriction to the marked block and passage to
the limit therefore give exact commutation.

Choose `N=floor(m/2)+1`. Section 1 would then require
`m>=2N`, a contradiction. This proves (11).

This class allows a bounded fiber dimension with an arbitrarily
large permutation action on the fibers. The algebra generated by
the whole tuple can consequently have arbitrarily large irreducible
blocks; the assertion is not merely a bound on its irreducible
matrix sizes. Nevertheless, no positive lower bound uniform in
`m` has been obtained. Every matrix tuple belongs to the one-block
class with `m` equal to its total dimension, so dependence on `m`
is precisely why (11) does not prove positivity of unrestricted
`gamma_shift`.
