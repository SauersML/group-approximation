# Composite-modulus Jacobson rank and normality audit

This is an independent proof of two inputs to the proposed rescaled
Kazhdan-transport argument. It does not by itself prove an MF obstruction.
No executable checks were run.

## 1. Uniform finite-group rank bound

Let q>=2, B=Z/qZ, N>=3, and G_N=E_N(B). For the transvection
t=I+E_12 and every finite-dimensional complex representation rho, put

    r_t=rank(rho(t)-I).

Since G_N is finite, choose an invariant inner product. Then

    codim Fix(rho(G_N)) <= 4 r_t.                       (1)

If r_t>0, the inequality obtained below is strict. No irreducibility
hypothesis is needed, and the constant is independent of q and N.

### Elementary facts over Z/qZ

For every r>=2, SL_r(B)=E_r(B), and this group acts transitively on
primitive vectors of B^r. Here primitive means that the coordinates
generate the unit ideal. One proof reduces by the Chinese remainder
theorem to B=Z/p^aZ. A primitive vector then has a unit coordinate.
Elementary row operations move it to the first coordinate, clear the
others, and normalize the remaining unit by diag(u,u^(-1),1,...,1),
which is elementary. Applying this to the first column and inducting
on the remaining block also proves SL_r(B)=E_r(B); the final one-by-one
block is 1 by the determinant condition. CRT allows each component's
elementary operations to be performed in that component alone.

If k divides q, reduction E_r(Z/qZ)->E_r(Z/kZ) is onto, since every
elementary parameter lifts. Consequently SL_r(Z/qZ) is transitive on
the vectors a in (Z/qZ)^r for which gcd(a_1,...,a_r,q)=d, for each
divisor d of q: such vectors are exactly a=d b, with b primitive
modulo k=q/d. This includes the reduction needed below.

### Character counting for the row subgroup

Put r=N-1 and

    U={I+sum_(j=2)^N v_(j-1) E_1j : v in B^r}.

The characters of the additive group U are indexed by a in B^r:

    chi_a(v)=exp(2 pi i (a dot v)/q).

The Levi subgroup diag(1,SL_r(B)) lies in G_N and normalizes U. It
acts transitively on the characters with each fixed nonzero gcd class
d<q. Hence all characters in that class occur in rho|_U with the same
multiplicity m_d. The effective modulus of this class is k=q/d>=2.

The number of primitive vectors in (Z/kZ)^r is

    P_r(k)=k^r product_(p|k)(1-p^(-r)).

Among them, exactly P_(r-1)(k) have first coordinate zero. Therefore
the fraction of this character orbit on which rho(t) acts nontrivially
is

    1-P_(r-1)(k)/P_r(k)
      =1-(1/k) product_(p|k)
                    (1-p^(-(r-1)))/(1-p^(-r))
      >1-1/k >=1/2.                                    (2)

The strict inequality follows because r>=2 and every displayed factor
in the product is strictly smaller than 1. Thus

    codim Fix(rho(U))=sum_(d<q) m_d P_r(q/d)
                    <=2 rank(rho(t)-I).                  (3)

It is strictly less when the left-hand side is nonzero. This argument
counts nonidentity eigenvalues, rather than a particular eigenspace;
t need not be an involution when q is composite or odd.

### The column subgroup and generation

Let V={I+sum_(i=2)^N v_(i-1) E_i1}. The same argument, using the dual
Levi action, gives

    codim Fix(rho(V)) <=2 rank(rho(I+E_21)-I).

An elementary Weyl element conjugates I+E_12 to I-E_21. Since a unitary
and its inverse have the same moving rank, the right-hand side is
2 r_t. No assertion that U and V are conjugate is needed; in general
they are not.

The subgroups U and V generate G_N. They already contain all roots
with a row or column index 1, and for distinct i,j>=2,

    [I+a E_i1, I+E_1j]=I+a E_ij.

It follows that Fix(rho(G_N))=Fix(rho(U)) intersection Fix(rho(V)).
Codimension of an intersection is at most the sum of codimensions.
Combining this with (3) proves (1). The case N=2, if needed, has the
stronger bound 2 r_t because U is cyclic generated by t; it is not
needed for the tower below.

## 2. The universal finite-order defect ideal

Let

    R_q=Z<s,t | ts=1, q(1-st)=0>,
    Q=1-st,                 e_ij=s^i Q t^j  (i,j>=0).

Then Q^2=Q, tQ=0, Qs=0, and direct multiplication gives

    e_ij e_kl=delta_(j,k)e_il.                            (4)

The finite additive span of the e_ij is a two-sided ideal: multiplying
on either side by s or t moves the appropriate index by one, or gives
zero at the boundary. It contains Q and therefore equals I=R_q Q R_q.
All e_ij have additive order dividing q.

To see that there are no additional additive relations, represent R_q
on W=direct_sum_(n>=0) B by the unilateral shift s and backward shift
t. They satisfy ts=1, and Q is the projection onto the zeroth basis
vector. Each e_ij is the ordinary matrix unit on W. Thus a finite
linear combination of e_ij acts as zero exactly when every coefficient
is zero in B. We have proved

    I is isomorphic to M_fin(B).                         (5)

The representation need not be faithful on all of R_q; faithfulness
on I is the only assertion used. The matrices of every element of R_q
in this representation are both row-finite and column-finite, because
they are polynomials in the two shifts. The same holds after passing
to four-by-four matrices.

## 3. Embed the full finitary elementary group

Index the basis of W^4 by P=N x {1,2,3,4}. Equation (5) identifies
M_4(I) with M_fin(P,B). The group 1+M_4(I) of invertible finitary
perturbations is consequently identified with GL_fin(P,B).

Every elementary transvection on distinct indices of P belongs to
E_4(R_q). If the outer indices differ, it is directly

    I+a e_ij E_ab,                 a!=b.

If the outer indices agree and tape indices i!=j differ, choose a
different outer index b and any tape index k. In the full P-indexing,
the three indices (i,a),(k,b),(j,a) are distinct, so the elementary
commutator identity produces the desired transvection:

    [I+c e_ik E_ab, I+e_kj E_ba]=I+c e_ij E_aa.

The reverse product e_kj e_ik is zero because j!=i, which also verifies
this identity directly inside M_4(R_q). Thus

    A=E_fin(P,B) <= E_4(R_q).                            (6)

Over B=Z/qZ the finite-stage equality SL_n(B)=E_n(B) proved above gives

    A=ker(det:GL_fin(P,B)->B^x).                         (7)

The determinant is well-defined by adjoining identity rows and columns
to any finite supporting block.

## 4. Normality in E_4(R_q)

Let g in E_4(R_q), and let 1+a in A, with a in M_4(I). Since M_4(I)
is a two-sided ideal,

    g(1+a)g^(-1)=1+gag^(-1),             gag^(-1) in M_4(I).

It remains to check determinant one; ideal membership alone would
only prove normality of GL_fin, not of A.

Use the row- and column-finite representation on W^4. Choose a finite
set S of indices supporting a, and write a=i_S a_S p_S, where i_S
is inclusion and p_S coordinate projection. Put

    B_0=g i_S,                  C_0=p_S g^(-1).

The matrix B_0 has finite support because g is column-finite, and C_0
has finite support because g^(-1) is row-finite. Choose one finite set
T containing both supports, and regard them as finite rectangular
matrices B_T and C_T. Since g^(-1)g=1,

    C_T B_T=I_S.

Sylvester's determinant identity over the commutative ring B now gives

    det(I_T+B_T a_S C_T)
      =det(I_S+a_S C_T B_T)
      =det(I_S+a_S)=1.                                  (8)

This identity holds over every commutative ring, for example by taking
the determinant of the corresponding two-by-two block matrix in two
ways using elementary block elimination. Faithfulness on M_4(I)
identifies its finitary matrix with the literal element gag^(-1).
Equations (7)--(8) therefore show that the conjugate lies in A. Hence

    E_fin(Z/qZ) is normal in E_4(R_q).                   (9)

No description of the symbol quotient or assertion of faithfulness of
the whole Toeplitz representation is used in this normality proof.

## 5. What these inputs do and do not provide

The embedded group A is locally finite. Exhaust it by standard finite
groups E_N(B), with N>=3 and a fixed head transvection present from the
first stage. In any exact complex representation of a containing stage,
every smaller stage's active projection has rank at most 4 r_t by (1).
Normality (9) permits finite conjugation windows to be absorbed into
later stages.

For a non-involutive head of order q, the final norm-to-rank estimate
must retain its spectral constant: every nonidentity qth root of unity
has distance at least 2 sin(pi/q) from 1. Thus on the moving projection
p_t, an exact unitary head T satisfies

    Tr((T-I)^*(T-I))/r_t >=4 sin(pi/q)^2.

The constant is positive for each fixed q. It is not asserted uniform
as q varies. Completing the MF argument still requires the rescaled
transport theorem and the literal group-theoretic identification of
the head with the normal subgroup generated by transported defects.
