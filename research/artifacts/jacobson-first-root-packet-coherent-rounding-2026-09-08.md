# Coherent rounding of the first full matrix root packet

2026-09-08. The finite packet reconstructed in
[the TS splitting proof](jacobson-ts-splitting-and-first-level-root-packet-2026-09-08.md)
can be corrected together with the boundary packet, on the original
dimension and with the entire original finite packet fixed. This is a
finite-group averaging argument. It leaves two mixed relators with small
errors; it does not prove a positive matrix gap or non-MF conclusion.

## 1. The packets and the claim

Let `pi_F` and `pi_J` be exact unitary representations on `C^d` of

```text
F=G_Q x G_P,             G=GL_3(F_2),
J=Hom(F_2^2,F_2^3) semidirect (K_Q x G_*),
```

agreeing on `L'=P_Q x K_P`. All notation and embeddings are those of
[the boundary correction proof](jacobson-boundary-packet-relative-rounding-2026-09-08.md).
In particular `H=pi_J(h)`, and the words `u,c,k,a_i,r,b,W` use
these same matrices. Put

```text
w=||W-I||,       beta=||(H b)^3-I||,
epsilon=322 w+368 beta,
delta=6057 epsilon <= 2,228,976 (w+beta).             (1)
```

Define the abstract finite group

```text
P=N semidirect F,
N=Mat_(3 x 3)(F_2)_add,
(g_Q,g_P).X=g_P X g_Q^(-1),
|P|=14,450,688.
```

Let `N_0` be the four-dimensional matrix subspace supported on rows
`{1,3}` and columns `{1,3}`. In the literal realization, the specified
common subgroup of `P` and `J` is

```text
I=N_0 semidirect L'
 ~= Hom(F_2^2,F_2^3) semidirect (K_Q x K_P),
|I|=2304,          I intersect F=L'.                  (2)
```

Here the four `N_0` entries are the boundary roots whose targets are
the shifted first and third coordinates. The other two boundary-root
entries, with target the shifted second coordinate, are exactly
`A_Q` in `F`. The action of `A_Q` on `N_0` is trivial. These facts
verify the embeddings. For the reverse inclusion in the literal
intersection, inspect the far shifted blocks: a shifted linear factor
outside `K_P` mixes the second shifted coordinate with the first or
third, and hence mixes original levels arbitrarily far out. An element
of `P` has no such far mixing. Its `J` linear factor must therefore
belong to `K_Q x K_P`, leaving precisely the six boundary roots
described above. This proves (2), without identifying the six boundary
roots with six first-level roots: two of them remain in `F`.

If `delta<=1/100`, there are exact representations `sigma_P` and
`sigma_J` on the same `C^d` such that

```text
sigma_P|F=pi_F,
sigma_J|L'=pi_F,
sigma_P|I=sigma_J|I,
H'=sigma_J(h),             ||H'-H||<=340 delta.       (3)
```

In particular, the distinguished root of `P` is the same matrix
`H' a_1 H'` supplied by `J`. Thus this correction does not choose
an unrelated representation of the larger packet.

## 2. An approximate orbit with a uniform stabilizer bound

Write `D=H a_1 H`. The exact boundary packet makes `D` an
involution. The TS splitting calculation gives

```text
||[r,D]-I||<=2w,
||[b,D]-I||<=2 beta.                                 (4)
```

The second bound uses two replacements of `b H b` by `H b H`;
each replacement has error `beta`. Let `r'=b r b=x_32,P`.
The exact boundary identities and (4) give the following errors for
generators of the tail vector stabilizer `S_P=Stab_(G_P)(e_1)`:

```text
generator       bound for ||[generator,D]-I||
x_13,P          0
x_12,P          4 beta
x_23,P          2w
x_32,P          2w+4 beta.
```

For the second row use `x_12,P=b x_13,P b` and the exact
commutation of `D` with `x_13,P`. Since `q_P=r r' r`,
`q_Q=b q_P`, we also have

```text
||[q_P,D]-I||<=6w+4 beta,
||[q_Q,D]-I||<=6w+6 beta.
```

The head covector stabilizer
`S_Q=Stab_(G_Q)(e_1^*)`, for the action `f -> f g_Q^(-1)`,
is generated by

```text
x_21,Q, x_31,Q, x_23,Q, x_32,Q.
```

The first three commute exactly with `D` in `J`. The last equals
`q_Q x_23,Q q_Q` and has commutator error at most
`12w+12 beta`. Each of `S_Q,S_P` has order 24 and is generated
by the indicated involutions. Its connected Cayley graph therefore
has diameter at most 23: a shortest path cannot repeat a vertex.
Consequently every `t` in `S_Q x S_P` satisfies

```text
||[pi_F(t),D]-I||
 <=23(12w+12 beta)+23(2w+4 beta)=epsilon.              (5)
```

For each nonzero vector `v` and nonzero covector `f`, choose
`g_(v,f)=(g_Q,g_P)` in `F` taking `(e_1,e_1^*)` to `(v,f)`.
Choose the identity at the base pair. When both arguments lie in
their respective `{1,3}` planes, choose representatives in
`K_Q x K_P`. Set

```text
R(v,f)=pi_F(g_(v,f)) D pi_F(g_(v,f))^*,
R(v,0)=R(0,f)=I.
```

These are actual unitary involutions. Different representatives
of a nonzero pair differ by its base stabilizer. Equation (5) gives
the uniform covariance estimate

```text
||pi_F(g) R(v,f) pi_F(g)^*-R(g_P v,f g_Q^(-1))||
 <=epsilon.                                         (6)
```

The zero cases have zero error. On the two specified planes these
matrices are exactly the corresponding roots of `J`.

## 3. Approximate bilinearity and the nine-root normal form

The boundary packet gives exact addition at the base pairs
`(e_1,e_3)` in the target and `(e_1^*,e_3^*)` in the source.
Transporting either addition identity by `F` and applying (6) to
its three entries gives, with `eta=3 epsilon`,

```text
||R(v+v',f)-R(v,f) R(v',f)||<=eta,
||R(v,f+f')-R(v,f) R(v,f')||<=eta.                    (7)
```

The action of `GL_3(F_2)` is transitive on ordered pairs of
distinct nonzero vectors, and likewise on covectors. This proves
(7) for all distinct nonzero inputs. Repeated or zero inputs have
zero error because every `R(v,f)` is an involution.

Expand `R(v+v',f+f')` in both orders, first in targets and then
sources, and conversely. Each resulting four-factor word is within
`3 eta` of that matrix. Cancel their common first and last factors.
For every pair of rank-one roots this proves

```text
||[R(v,f'),R(v',f)]-I||<=6 eta=18 epsilon.            (8)
```

For a matrix `X=(X_ij)` define a unitary, in row-major order, by

```text
f_N(X)= product_(i=1..3, j=1..3) R(e_i,e_j^*)^(X_ij).
```

To multiply two such words, move each root in the second word to
its ordered position in the first and cancel identical involutions.
There are at most `9*8/2=36` interchanges. Equation (8) yields

```text
||f_N(X) f_N(Y)-f_N(X+Y)||<=648 epsilon.              (9)
```

There is also a uniform covariance bound for this normal form.
Expand `R(v,f)` first along the at most three nonzero target
coordinates, then along the at most three source coordinates in
each resulting row. There are at most eight applications of (7),
and the result is already in row-major order. Hence

```text
||R(v,f)-f_N(v f)||<=8 eta=24 epsilon.                (10)
```

Conjugating the at most nine factors of `f_N(X)` by `pi_F(g)`
and using (6), (10) costs at most `9*(1+24) epsilon`.
Combining their nine normal forms into the normal form of their
sum costs at most eight uses of (9). Thus

```text
||pi_F(g) f_N(X) pi_F(g)^*-f_N(g_P X g_Q^(-1))||
 <=(225+8*648) epsilon=5409 epsilon.                  (11)
```

Define, using the unique semidirect-product normal form in `P`,

```text
f_P(X,g)=f_N(X) pi_F(g).
```

One covariance step (11) and one multiplication step (9) give

```text
||f_P(p) f_P(p')-f_P(pp')||<=6057 epsilon=delta.       (12)
```

The map is unital and unitary valued. It is exactly `pi_F` on
`F`. It is also exactly the old `pi_J` on `I`: the four
coordinate roots in `N_0` were selected inside `J` and commute
there, and its remaining normal-form factor is in `L'`. This
exactness on the entire intersection is what makes the next step
coherent.

## 4. Correcting P and aligning the entire boundary packet

Apply the finite-group relative correction lemma from
[the boundary correction proof, Section 4](jacobson-boundary-packet-relative-rounding-2026-09-08.md)
to `f_P`, relative to `F`. It supplies an exact representation
`sigma_P` on `C^d` with

```text
sigma_P|F=pi_F,
sup_(p in P) ||sigma_P(p)-f_P(p)||<=85 delta.         (13)
```

On `I`, both `sigma_P` and `pi_J` are now exact and within
`85 delta`. Form the finite average

```text
T=|I|^(-1) sum_(i in I) sigma_P(i) pi_J(i)^*.
```

It satisfies `sigma_P(i)T=T pi_J(i)` and
`||T-I||<=85 delta<1`, so it is invertible. Its unitary polar
factor `U` has the same intertwining property and

```text
||U-I||<=170 delta.
```

Moreover, `T` and `U` commute with the original representation
of `L'`, because the two representations agree there exactly.
Set

```text
sigma_J(j)=U pi_J(j) U^*.
```

Then `sigma_J|I=sigma_P|I` and `sigma_J|L'=pi_F`. We have
not conjugated the original packet `F`; only the boundary packet
is conjugated, by a unitary that fixes its overlap with `F`.
Finally

```text
||sigma_J(h)-H||<=2||U-I||<=340 delta,
sigma_P(D_1)=sigma_J(D_1)=H' a_1 H'.                 (14)
```

This proves (3). All averages used are over fixed finite groups.
Their auxiliary-space sizes do not affect the constants or the
dimension of the corrected representations.

## 5. Remaining mixed errors

The matrices from `F` stay fixed. Since the first braid has three
occurrences of `H` and `W` has twelve, unitary telescoping gives

```text
||(H' b)^3-I||<=beta+1020 delta,
||W(H')-I||<=w+4080 delta.                            (15)
```

The new `J` and `P` are exact and agree on `I`. In particular
the new cross commutations `[r,H' a_i H']=1` are exact, because
the tail matrix `r=x_23,P` fixes the target of those first-level
roots. Consequently the norm of the remaining TS defect is exactly
the norm of its pure tail defect:

```text
||W(H')-I||=||[u',H' r H']-r||,
u'=[H' c H',k].                                     (16)
```

For (16), use the exact identity `[u',D_2']=a_2`, the
commutation of `D_2'` with `r`, and multiply the TS expansion
by unitary factors. No estimate or additional relation is used.

Thus sequences with the original two errors tending to zero can
be replaced on the same dimensions by models with both finite
packets exact and coherently glued, while the remaining two errors
still tend to zero. Neither (15) nor (16) makes those mixed
relators exact or proves a positive lower bound on them.

## 6. An explicit gap in dimensions at most 97

The [single rank-one orbit proof](jacobson-single-rank-one-orbit-obstruction-2026-09-08.md)
uses the first braid only to identify the traces of `h` and `b`.
After `P,J` are exact and agree on `I`, that trace equality
already follows from the strict inequality

```text
||(H b)^3-I||<2.                                    (17)
```

Indeed, `H b H` and `b H b` are self-adjoint unitary
involutions, and their distance is exactly the left side of (17).
Their negative spectral projections therefore have distance less
than one, so they have equal ranks. Since they are conjugate to
`b` and `H`, respectively, this gives `trace(b)=trace(H)`.
The exact shifted packet also gives `trace(H)=trace(c)`.

The remainder of Sections 2--6 of the single-orbit proof uses only
these trace equalities, the exact representations of `P,J`, and
their common identity `D_1=H a_1 H`. Therefore a representation
of these coherently glued packets satisfying (17) and retaining the
head cannot have its entire nontrivial `N`-part equal to one
rank-one character orbit with one-dimensional fibers. Its `N`-trivial
complement can have arbitrary dimension. This conclusion does not
require the TS relation to be exact.

Now set

```text
C=2,228,976,
e=max(w,beta),
e_0=1/(8192 C)=1/18,259,771,392.                     (18)
```

If `e<=e_0`, then (1) gives `delta<=2 C e<=1/4096`,
so the coherent correction applies. The corrected first braid obeys

```text
||(H' b)^3-I||<=e+1020/4096<2.                      (19)
```

The head remains nontrivial because all of `F` remains fixed.
Consequently the corrected representation has a nonzero
`N`-character. The nonzero `F`-orbits in its character group have
sizes `49,168,294`, classified by ranks one, three, and two.
In dimension at most 97 the only possibility is the single rank-one
orbit with one-dimensional fibers, with a possible `N`-trivial
complement. Equations (17)--(19) exclude that possibility. Thus every
pair of exact compatible packets `F,J` on dimension at most 97
retaining the head satisfies the numerical bound

```text
max(||W-I||,||(H b)^3-I||)>1/18,259,771,392.          (20)
```

This is approximately `5.48*10^(-11)`. The argument also excludes
that single-orbit type, after correction, in arbitrary dimension
with an arbitrary `N`-trivial complement. It does not exclude
higher multiplicities or the other nonzero orbits, so (20) supplies
no bound uniform over all dimensions and does not establish non-MF.
