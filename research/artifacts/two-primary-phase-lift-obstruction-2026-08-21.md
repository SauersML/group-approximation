# Two-primary phase lifts do not transport modular direct-finiteness defects

Let `G` be a group.  Suppose finite subsets `S,T` define

```text
a = sum_(s in S) s,       b = sum_(t in T) t in F_2[G]
```

with `ab=1` and `ba!=1`.  This note audits the most literal proposed bridge
from that defect to complex unitary microstates: decorate each support term by
a Pauli/Clifford phase and ask the decorated sums to become a one-sided
inverse over `C[G]`.

## Cyclotomic reduction

Fix `m>=1`, put `zeta=zeta_(2^m)`, and let `O=Z[zeta]`.  Reduction modulo
`(1-zeta)` maps `O` onto `F_2` and every `2^m`-th root of unity to `1`.  (For
`m=1`, this is simply `Z/(2)`; for `m>1`, the cyclotomic polynomial
`Phi_(2^m)(X)=X^(2^(m-1))+1` becomes `(X+1)^(2^(m-1))` in characteristic
two.)  Therefore any choices

```text
alpha_s,beta_t in mu_(2^m)
```

give elements `A=sum alpha_s s`, `B=sum beta_t t` of `O[G]` reducing exactly
to `a,b`.

If `AB=1`, view the equality in the group von Neumann algebra `L(G)`.  A
finite von Neumann algebra is directly finite, so `BA=1`.  The regular
representation of `C[G]` is faithful, hence this is an equality in `O[G]`.
Reduction gives `ba=1`, contradiction.

This covers the scalar phase menus generated by Pauli and Clifford word
multiplication (`mu_4`), and in fact every fixed two-primary cyclotomic phase
menu.  It does not cover a construction that attaches a new non-scalar
matrix carrier to each coefficient term; such a construction is a genuine
coefficient decoder, not a phase correction.

## Uniform fixed-menu gap

For fixed `m,S,T`, the set of phase assignments is finite.  The preceding
argument excludes zero for every number

```text
|| (sum alpha_s lambda(s))(sum beta_t lambda(t)) - 1 ||_2.
```

Their minimum is therefore positive.  Regular-character matrix microstates
converge on this fixed word window to precisely these group-factor norms, so
no dimension-dependent choice from the same finite Clifford menu can make the
decorated forward product tend to the identity.

## Boundary of the result

Allowing arbitrary circle phases destroys the reduction map: a general unit
complex number is not a cyclotomic integer reducing to one at a prime above
two.  Allowing non-scalar Pauli labels also leaves the theorem's scope because
the decorated terms no longer lie in the scalar group algebra.  Either escape
must therefore carry new matrix-valued coefficient information.  In
particular, the bare hyperlinear microstate of the unit group supplies neither
escape automatically.  Producing the latter information is at least the
linear-sofic coefficient-decoder problem recorded elsewhere in Cairn, not a
formal consequence of hyperlinearity.
