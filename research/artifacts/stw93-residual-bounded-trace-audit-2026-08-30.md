# XCIII: residual bounded-trace audit and doubled-slice countermodel

## Exact intrinsic criterion

Write

```text
E=D/K_Z^sat,                 R=I/K_Z^sat.
```

A nonzero bounded trace on `R` canonically extends to a finite trace on
`M(R)`, and composition with the multiplication map `E->M(R)` produces a
normalized trace on `E` charging `R`.  Conversely, because
`E/R=D/I` is a II_1 factor, every noncanonical normalized trace on `E`
must restrict nontrivially to `R`.  Since all traces on `D` annihilate
`K_Z^sat`, this proves

```text
R has no nonzero bounded trace
  iff E has a unique normalized trace
  iff D has a unique normalized trace.
```

No hereditary exhaustion is needed for this ordinary-trace equivalence.
The exhaustion theorem is needed only to show that arbitrary quasitraces
are ordinary traces.

## Why the established slice data do not decide the criterion

Let `G=F(Z)`, let `J` be its trace-kernel ideal, and let `Q=G/J`.  The
algebra `G` has a unique normalized quasitrace and `Q` is a II_1 factor.
Set

```text
D_0=G direct_sum G,
tau_0(x,y)=tau_G(x),
I_0=J direct_sum G.
```

The diagonal copy of `G` surjects onto `D_0/I_0=Q`.  Every unital
homomorphism from `G` to `D_0` has two unital coordinate maps, both of which
preserve the unique quasitrace of `G`.  Saturating over the trace-zero parts
of all these copies gives exactly

```text
K_0^sat=J direct_sum J.
```

Every quasitrace on the direct sum is a convex combination of the two
canonical coordinate quasitraces, so all quasitraces annihilate `K_0^sat`
and quotienting preserves the entire normalized quasitrace space.  Yet

```text
I_0/K_0^sat ~= Q
```

carries its factor trace.

Thus the following inputs, even together, do not imply residual
bounded-trace-freeness:

- a unique-quasitrace II_1-factor quotient;
- exhaustion of that quotient by one canonical `F(Z)` slice;
- automatic trace preservation for every unital `F(Z)` embedding;
- annihilation of the ideal generated by every trace-zero slice; and
- preservation of quasitraces after quotienting by the saturated ideal;
- pureness of the ambient algebra.

For the last point, the Perera--Thiel--Vilalta theorem makes `F(Z)` pure,
and pureness passes coordinatewise to a finite direct sum at the level of
the Cuntz semigroup.

The model isolates the missing XCIII-specific input: one must rule out an
additional tracial direction contained entirely in the norm trace kernel.
Equivalently, one must prove uniqueness of the ordinary trace on the actual
central sequence algebra `D`.  None of the established slice statements
does this.

## Scope

The doubled model is not asserted to arise as a central sequence algebra,
so it does not answer XCIII negatively.  Its role is logical: it prevents a
proof from concluding residual trace-freeness using only the currently
formalized quotient and saturated-slice properties.
