## Setting

`C = {0,1}^N` with Thompson's group `V` (prefix replacements; `V` is full). `G` is a
finite germ extension of `V` in the sense of Belk–Hyde–Matucci (BHM, arXiv:2407.03149v1,
TeX lines 145–176), generated by `V` and elements fixing their singular points (BHM's
"same orbits" hypothesis of Theorems 1.3/1.4, needed for any simple host they build).
For a rational (eventually periodic) point `q = u w^∞` with `w` primitive, `(V)_q = ⟨τ_q⟩ ≅ Z`,
where `τ_q` is the germ of `u w^k x ↦ u w^(k+1) x`. For `a ≥ 1` let
`Cent_q(a)` be the group of germs at `q` of homeomorphisms fixing `q`, locally `V` on a
punctured neighbourhood, that commute with `τ_q^a`.

## Statement

Suppose BHM Corollary 2.10 (TeX lines 527–540) applies to a finitely generated `G` as above
for some `n ≥ 1`, with base `B = V`. Then:

- **(a) Rational singular points only.** Every point of `sing(G)` is rational. Indeed for
  an irrational `p`, `Fix_V(p)` is not finitely generated, so hypothesis (2) of Corollary
  2.10, and already hypothesis (2) of Theorem 2.1 at `(M,M') = (∅,{p})`, fails for every `n ≥ 1`.
- **(b) Germs commute with a power of the V-germ.** At each `q ∈ sing(G)` the germ group
  `(G)_q` lies in `Cent_q(a)` for some `a ≥ 1`, with `a = 1` under the normal clause of
  hypothesis (3).
- **(c) The centralizers are explicit.** `Cent_q(a) ≅ C(C,Z) ⋊ V`, the group of pairs
  `(f,d)` with `f` in the full group of a fundamental domain `D` of `τ_q^a` (≅ `V`) and `d`
  a locally constant function `D → Z`, acting by `τ^(ak) y ↦ τ^(a(k+d(y))) f(y)`. Under the
  normal clause `(G)_q/(V)_q` embeds in `(C(C,Z)/Z) ⋊ V`. Consequently `G` lies in the group
  `V♯_{S,a}` of all orbit-preserving homeomorphisms of `C` that are locally `V` off finitely
  many points of `⋃S` (`S` = the finitely many `V`-orbits of `sing(G)`), whose germs at those
  points commute, after transport by `V`-germs, with `τ_q^a`.
- **(d) Bounded complexity.** Every finitely generated subgroup of every `V♯_{S,a}` has a
  word-problem algorithm in time `C·2^(C·l)` for its generating set.
  By `complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal` (bound `T(l) = 2^l`), the hosts that
  Corollary 2.10 certifies over the base `V` cannot contain every two-generated group with
  solvable word problem.

**Reading.** Over the base `V`, a proof of Boone–Higman through BHM germ extensions must
verify Theorem 2.1's hypothesis (2), including `SingFix({p},{p})` of type `F_2`, at germ groups
lying in no `Cent_q(a)`. Shell envelopes are the natural such case; the landed statement
that Corollary 2.10 certifies no shell outside virtually cyclic inputs is the shell instance
of (b). The ceiling is attained: see `cantor-cover-germ-extension-of-v-is-fp-simple`.

## Proof

**(a)** Let `p` be irrational and `v ∈ V` with `v(p) = p`. Near `p`, `v(ux) = u'x` on a cone
`[u] ∋ p`, so `p = u y = u' y`. If `|u| ≠ |u'|`, say `u' = u c`, then `y = c y`, so `y = c^∞` and
`p` is eventually periodic; hence `u = u'`, and `v` is the identity on `[u]`. So
`Fix_V(p) = ⋃_m Fix_V([p_1…p_m])` is a strictly increasing union: the complement of
`[p_1…p_m]` grows strictly. It is therefore not finitely generated. Since
`SingFix_G(∅,{p}) = Fix_V(p)`, hypothesis (2) fails for every `n ≥ 1`.

**(b)** In the finite-index clause `⟨τ_q⟩` has finite index in `(G)_q`, so its normal core is
`⟨τ_q^a⟩` for some `a ≥ 1`, and every `γ ∈ (G)_q` conjugates the generator `τ_q^a` to `τ_q^{±a}`. The sign is `+`: `τ_q^a` maps some clopen neighbourhood `U` of `q` into itself,
so the conjugate maps `γU` into itself, while `τ_q^{-a}` maps no small neighbourhood into itself.
In the normal clause, `γ τ_q γ^{-1} ∈ {τ_q, τ_q^{-1}}`, and the same contraction argument gives `τ_q`.

**(c)** Let `γ` commute with `σ := τ_q^a` on a punctured neighbourhood `W` of `q`, and let `D` be a
fundamental domain for `σ` on `W` (for `q = 0^∞`, `a = 1`: `D = 0^m 1 C`). Every `x ∈ W` is
uniquely `σ^k y` with `y ∈ D`, `k ≥ 0`. Commutation gives `γ(σ^k y) = σ^k γ(y)`, and
`γ(y) = σ^(d(y)) f(y)` with `f(y) ∈ D`. Local `V`-ness of `γ` makes `f` locally `V` and `d`
locally constant, hence bounded (`D` is compact); `γ` bijective near `q` makes `f` a bijection of `D`.
Conversely every such `(f,d)` defines a germ commuting with `σ`. Composition is
`(f,d)(f',d') = (f f', d∘f' + d')`, so `Cent_q(a) ≅ C(D,Z) ⋊ V_D ≅ C(C,Z) ⋊ V`. Here `σ = (id,1)`
is central. For `a = 1` it generates `(V)_q`, which gives the quotient statement.

For containment, each element of `G` is locally `V` off finitely many points of `sing(G)`. Every
element preserves each `V`-orbit: a generator is locally `V`, hence locally equal to a `V`-element,
at every non-singular point, and it fixes its singular points. The germ at a singular point
`q ↦ q'` transported back by any `v ∈ V` with `v(q') = q` lies in `(G)_q ≤ Cent_q(a)`.
Take `a` to be the least common multiple over the finitely many orbits.

**(d)** (Sketch.) Fix generators. There is a depth `c` such that each generator acts on every
cylinder of depth `c` avoiding its singular points as a prefix replacement. Near each of its
(rational) singular points it acts by explicit `(f,d)` data of depth `c`. A word of length `l`
then acts on cylinders of depth `c·l` away from the at most `l·c` singular points of the product
(each rational, of description size `O(l)`) as prefix replacements. Near those points it acts by
a composite `(f,d)` with `f` of depth `O(l)` and `|d| = O(l)`, and deep cylinders at a singular
point are all translates of one period, so finitely many cylinder types need checking. Testing
the word for the identity on the `2^(O(l))` cylinder types takes time `2^(O(l))`. The constant
depends on the generators, which is the form required by
`complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal`.
