**OPEN** (lane bh-free-54, 2026-09-18). Holds for `m = 1`. For `m = 2`, even its
first sub-question is open: are there only finitely many prime dissections?

## Statement

Fix `m ≥ 2`, at least for `m = 6g − 7` with `g ≥ 3`.
- Let `P_m` be the poset of nontrivial unimodular dissections of
  `Δ = cone(e_0, …, e_m)` ordered by refinement. The definitions are those of
  `unimodular-dissections-have-egyptian-fraction-volumes`, with
  T-junctions allowed.
- `P_m` is graded by the number of pieces.
- **Claim.** The spine of `P_m` in Thumann's sense is finite (arXiv:1409.1085,
  Definition `79040`). The spine is the smallest set containing the minimal elements
  such that every element outside it has a greatest element of the set below it.
  It is built by iterating minimal common upper bounds of pairs.
- Via `rational-projective-full-groups-fp-via-thumann-operad-finiteness`, the claim
  makes `𝒯_m` of type `F_∞`.

## Terms

- **Prime dissection.** A dissection is *prime* if no set of at least 2 but not all
  of its pieces has union a unimodular cone. The prime dissections are exactly the
  minimal elements of `P_m`, Thumann's *very elementary* classes; see the route for
  the one-line proof.
- **Finite generation.** The operad is *finitely generated* when there are finitely
  many prime dissections. This is necessary for the claim, because the minimal
  elements lie in the spine.
- **Exact test.** By part 3 of the Egyptian-fraction node, a set of pieces is
  mergeable exactly when a unimodular cone's weight equals the sum of the weights of
  the pieces it contains. So primality is a finite arithmetic check.

## Known cases

- **`m = 1`: holds.** By the tree property of Stern–Brocot subdivisions, every
  nontrivial Farey dissection refines the mediant split. So the spine is that single
  split (`rank-one-rational-projective-full-group-is-flip-v`).

## Known prime dissections at `m = 2`

Write `v_{ij} = e_i + e_j` and `c = e_0 + e_1 + e_2`. The hand checks below verify
unimodularity and the weight sum 1. Primality is checked by hand for `E`, `Z` and
`M`, and by the exact test for the rest.

- **`E`, 2 pieces (3 positions).** The stellar split at `v_{01}`.
- **`Z`, 3 pieces.** The stellar split at `c`. It refines no `E`, because the cut
  `e_2 — v_{01}` passes through `c` and crosses the interior of `cone(e_0, e_1, c)`.
- **`M`, the medial split, 6 pieces.** Weights sum `3/4 + 1/4`.
  - Pieces: the corners `cone(e_i, v_{ij}, v_{ik})`, and the middles
    `cone(v_{ij}, v_{jk}, c)`.
  - `M` is the barycentric fan `B_6 = Z∘(E, E, E)` after three Atiyah flops across
    the spokes `e_i — c`. Each flop uses `e_i + c = v_{ij} + v_{ik}`.
  - The only unimodular triangles that are unions of `M`-pieces are the single
    pieces and `Δ`. The middle triangle `cone(v_{01}, v_{12}, v_{02})` has
    determinant 2.
  - So **the operad is not generated by stellar moves**. The restricted complex of
    Attempt 2 on `rational-projective-full-groups-are-finitely-presented` misses
    `M`.
- **`M'`, 6 pieces.** Its pieces are:
  - `cone(e_2, (0,1,2), e_0)`;
  - `cone(e_1, (0,1,1), v_{01})`;
  - `cone((0,1,1), (0,1,2), v_{01})`;
  - `cone((0,1,2), e_0, c)`;
  - `cone((0,1,2), v_{01}, c)`;
  - `cone(e_0, v_{01}, c)`.

  The weights `1/3, 1/4, 1/12, 1/9, 1/18, 1/6` sum to 1. The edge `[e_0, e_2]` is
  unsubdivided.
- **A 9-piece prime.** Its pieces are:
  - `cone(e_2, (0,1,1), (1,0,1))`;
  - `cone(e_1, (0,1,1), c)`;
  - `cone(e_1, e_0, (2,0,1))`;
  - `cone(e_1, c, (2,0,1))`;
  - `cone((0,1,1), (1,0,1), (3,0,2))`;
  - `cone((0,1,1), c, (2,1,2))`;
  - `cone((0,1,1), (2,1,2), (3,0,2))`;
  - `cone(c, (2,0,1), (3,0,2))`;
  - `cone(c, (2,1,2), (3,0,2))`.

  The weights, over the common denominator 900, are
  `225 + 150 + 300 + 100 + 45 + 30 + 18 + 20 + 12 = 900`.

## Attempts

1. **Minimal common upper bounds of primes, by hand** (lane bh-free-54,
   2026-09-18). These are the first spine elements beyond the primes.
   - **`E_{01}` and `Z`: unique, 4 pieces.** The cuts `c — e_i` and `e_2 — v_{01}`
     cut out four unimodular triangles. So `Z∘(E, id, id) = E_{01}∘(E, E)`, a
     relation in the operad.
   - **`E_{01}` and `E_{02}`: exactly two, 5 pieces each, related by a flop.** The
     cuts cross at `c` and leave the quadrilateral `(e_0, v_{01}, c, v_{02})`. Both of
     its diagonals give unimodular triangles.
   - **`E_{01}` and `M`: unique, 8 pieces.** The cut `e_2 — v_{01}` splits `M`'s
     corner at `e_2` and one middle piece at `(1,1,2)`. All four halves are
     unimodular.
   - **So minimal upper bounds need not be unique.** Unlike Brin–Thompson cube
     cutting, the spine is not a lattice closure of the primes. Its finiteness is a
     genuine question even if the primes are finite.
2. **Bounded MSI search for prime dissections** (lane bh-free-54, 2026-09-18; a few
   cores for a few minutes). The script and raw output are in
   `research/artifacts/gq-bh-free-54-unimodular-prime-search.md`.
   - **Moves.** Stellar splits, their inverses and Atiyah flops, on fans and, for
     T-junction dissections, piece by piece.
   - **Invariants.** Every generated configuration is checked against the weight
     identity. Primality uses the exact union test.
   - **Exhaustive part.** This enumerates every configuration reachable from `Δ`
     through configurations with at most one more piece. It is not proved to be
     every dissection of that size.
     - Fans with at most 9 pieces, counted up to `S_3`:
       `1, 1, 3, 11, 46, 217, 1013, 4920, 23967` of sizes 1 to 9. The primes number
       1, 1, 2 and 8 at sizes 2, 3, 6 and 9, and there are none at sizes 4, 5, 7
       and 8.
     - Dissections with T-junctions and at most 7 pieces:
       `1, 1, 4, 26, 214, 1969, 18959`. The primes number 1, 1 and 4 at sizes 2, 3
       and 6, and there are none at sizes 4, 5 and 7.
   - **Random part.**
     - Annealing from `Δ`, on the number of mergeable sets, found prime fans with 9,
       10, 11 and 14 pieces.
     - Annealing seeded with those primes and then grown found:
       - 34 distinct primes of size 12;
       - primes of sizes 13 and 14;
       - two of size 15, one of them with a vertex of height 8.
     - Runs confined to 18–26 and 22–34 pieces found none.
   - **Reading.**
     - Prime sizes observed so far: 2, 3, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15. The counts
       grow (2 at size 6, 8 at size 9, at least 34 at size 12).
     - This is strong evidence that **the canonical operad `𝒪_2` is not finitely
       generated**. If so, Thumann's theorem cannot be applied to `𝒪_2` itself; see
       the lesson below.
     - It is evidence, not proof. The silence at sizes above 15 is consistent with
       the search thinning out.
   - **What would decide it.**
     - Against finite generation: an explicit infinite family of primes with a
       uniform primality proof. `M`'s flop structure is the natural template.
     - For finite generation: a structural lemma, for example that every dissection
       with more than `N` pieces has a mergeable proper set. The local picture is
       governed by the link relations at interior vertices, which are smooth toric
       surfaces.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Where the difficulty sits.** For any group with a faithful
  piecewise-integral-projective action, the full clopen-transitive host comes for
  free (`closed-mcg-embeds-in-rational-projective-full-group`). Its finite
  presentation depends only on the local subdivision operad, not on the group.
- **The canonical operad is too big.** In dimension 2 it has neither the tree
  property that gives Nekrashevych/Li finiteness nor the lattice property of
  Brin–Thompson cube cutting. Its primes appear to be unbounded.
- **The way out.** Pass to a finite-type suboperad `𝒪' ⊂ 𝒪_m`. The price is that
  the group must still act inside `π_1(𝒪')`. Every `g` then needs an
  `𝒪'`-dissection whose image under `g` is again an `𝒪'`-dissection.
- **Two gates, one condition.** That requirement is the Serret gate G1 of
  `train-track-trace-groupoid-hosts-closed-mcg`, in operad form. So G1 (invariance
  of a coding) and G4 (finite presentation) are two faces of one condition: a
  finite-type coding that the group preserves.
- **The general task.** This moves BH, for groups with piecewise-linear integral
  actions, to finding a finite-type subdivision scheme stable under the group.
