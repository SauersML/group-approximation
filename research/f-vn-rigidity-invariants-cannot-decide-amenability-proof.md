**Conventions.** `F` is the group of piecewise-linear dyadic homeomorphisms
of `[0,1]`. Its generators are `x_0` (equal to `t/2` on `[0,1/2]`) and
`x_1` (the identity on `[0,1/2]`). For an interval `J`, `F_J` denotes the
elements supported in `J`. For `[a,b]` dyadic, `F_{[a,b]} ≅ F`, via a
dyadic PL conjugacy. Two elements whose open supports are disjoint
commute. `M = L(F)` carries its trace `τ`, and `||x||_2 = τ(x*x)^{1/2}`.

## Step 0: the two elementary halves of Connes' theorem

"Injective" means that a von Neumann algebra `Q ⊆ B(ℓ²G)` is the range
of a conditional expectation `E : B(ℓ²G) → Q`, that is, of a ucp
idempotent. By Tomiyama, `E` is then `Q`-bimodular.

**(0a) Let `H ≤ G`. If `L(H) ⊆ L(G) ⊆ B(ℓ²G)` is injective, then `H` is
amenable.** Put `φ = τ ∘ E` on `ℓ^∞(G)`, acting by multiplication. For
`h ∈ H` we have `λ_h M_f λ_h^* = M_{f(h^{-1}·)}`, and by bimodularity
`φ(λ_h T λ_h^*) = τ(λ_h E(T) λ_h^*) = φ(T)`. So `φ` is an
`H`-invariant mean on `ℓ^∞(G)`. Choose right-coset representatives,
`G = ⊔_s Hs`, and send `f ∈ ℓ^∞(H)` to `f̃(hs) = f(h)`. This map commutes
with left translation by `H`. Hence `m(f) = φ(f̃)` is a left-invariant
mean on `H`.

**(0b) If `G` is amenable, `L(G) ⊆ B(ℓ²G)` is injective.** Let `m` be a
right-invariant mean. Define `E(T)` weakly by
`⟨E(T)ξ,η⟩ = m(g ↦ ⟨ρ_g T ρ_g^* ξ, η⟩)`. Then `E` is ucp,
`ρ_h E(T) ρ_h^* = E(T)`, so `E(T) ∈ ρ(G)' = L(G)`, and `E` is the
identity on `L(G)`.

**(0c)** Let `Q ⊆ P ⊆ B(ℓ²G)` with `P` finite and injective. Then `Q` is
injective: compose `E_P` with the trace-preserving normal conditional
expectation `P → Q`.

## Step 1 (Part A): the commuting pair

Fix `c ∈ F_{[0,1/2]} \ {1}`. It has infinite order, since `F` is
torsion-free, so `A = L(⟨c⟩) ≅ L^∞(T)` is diffuse. Every
`f ∈ F_{[1/2,1]}` has open support disjoint from that of `c`, so it
commutes with `c`. Hence

```text
L(F_{[1/2,1]}) ⊆ A' ∩ M,   and   F_{[1/2,1]} ≅ F.          (*)
```

*Item 1 (solid).* If `M` is solid, then `A' ∩ M` is injective. By (0c),
`L(F_{[1/2,1]})` is injective, and by (0a), `F_{[1/2,1]} ≅ F` is
amenable. Conversely, if `F` is amenable then `M` is injective by
(0b), and every `A' ∩ M` is injective by (0c). So `M` is solid.

*Item 2 (normalizer version).* The normalizer of `A` contains
`λ(F_{[1/2,1]})`. If the von Neumann algebra it generates is injective,
then (0c) and (0a) again make `F` amenable. The converse is (0b)+(0c).

*Item 3 ((AO)).* (AO) ⇒ solid is (I1) Theorem 6, since `M` is finite,
and then Item 1 applies. Conversely, suppose `F` is amenable. Take
`B = C*_λ F` and `C = C*_ρ F`. Then `B` is nuclear (standard for
amenable groups; recalled). So `B ⊗_min C = B ⊗_max C`, and `ν` is the
max-continuous product map followed by the Calkin quotient, hence
continuous. Nuclearity gives property (C) of Archbold–Batty, since the
min and max norms agree on every tensor product. By (I1) §2
(Effros–Haagerup), `B` is therefore locally reflexive, so `M` satisfies
(AO).

This converse is the only step that recalls a textbook fact (amenable ⇒
`C*_λ` nuclear) instead of proving it. The direction that kills
certificates, (AO) ⇒ amenable, is fully proved.

*Item 4.* `Z × F ≅ ⟨c⟩ × F_{[1/2,1]} ≤ F`. If `F ∈ P`, then heredity
gives `Z × F ∈ P`, and product-rigidity makes `F` amenable. If `F` is
amenable, then `F ∈ P` by hypothesis. Solidity of `L(·)` satisfies all
three conditions:
- hereditary: for `H ≤ G`, `A' ∩ L(H) ⊆ A' ∩ L(G)`, then apply (0c);
- product-rigid: repeat Item 1 with `c` a generator of `Z`;
- holds for amenable groups: this is (0b).

(AO) needs no heredity, because Item 3 handles it directly.

## Step 2 (Part B): indecomposability fails for F

**The x_0-conjugates.** Fix `g ∈ F_{[1/4,1/2]} \ {1}` and put
`g_k = x_0^k g x_0^{-k}`. Since `x_0(t) = t/2` on `[0,1/2]`, the open
support of `g_k` lies in `(2^{-k-2}, 2^{-k-1})`. So the `g_k` are
distinct, pairwise commuting, and of infinite order, and
`⟨g_0,…,g_n⟩ ≅ Z^{n+1}`. Each `g_k` is supported in `[0,1/2]`, where
`x_1` is the identity, so `x_1 g_k x_1^{-1} = g_k`. Also
`x_0 g_k x_0^{-1} = g_{k+1}`.

**(B1) Inner amenability.** Let `μ_n` be the uniform probability on
`{g_0,…,g_{n-1}} ⊆ F \ {1}`. Conjugation by `x_1` fixes `μ_n`, and
conjugation by `x_0` sends it to the uniform measure on `{g_1,…,g_n}`,
so `||x_0·μ_n − μ_n||_1 = 2/n`. Words in `x_0^{±1}, x_1^{±1}` then give
`||h·μ_n − μ_n||_1 → 0` for every `h ∈ F`, by the triangle inequality.
Any weak*-cluster point of `μ_n` in `ℓ^∞(F \ {1})^*` is therefore a
conjugation-invariant mean on `F \ {1}`.

**(B2) Property Γ.** In `L(⟨g_0,…,g_n⟩) ≅ L^∞(T^{n+1})`, the trace is
Haar measure and `λ(g_k) ↔ e^{iθ_k}`. Put
`X_k = (λ(g_k) + λ(g_k)^*)/√2 ↔ √2 cos θ_k`. These are i.i.d. under
`τ`, with mean 0 and variance 1. Set `S_n = n^{-1/2} Σ_{k<n} X_k` and
`u_n = exp(2πi Φ(S_n))`, where `Φ` is the standard normal CDF, which is
Lipschitz with constant `(2π)^{-1/2}`. Then:

- `τ(u_n) → E[e^{2πiΦ(N)}] = E[e^{2πiU}] = 0`, by the central limit
  theorem, since the integrand is bounded and continuous. Here
  `N ~ N(0,1)` and `U` is uniform on `[0,1]`. So
  `||u_n − τ(u_n)1||_2 → 1`.
- `λ(x_1)` commutes with `u_n`.
- `λ(x_0) u_n λ(x_0)^* = exp(2πiΦ(S'_n))`, with
  `S'_n = n^{-1/2} Σ_{1≤k≤n} X_k`. Both lie in one abelian algebra, so
  pointwise
  `||u_n − λ(x_0)u_nλ(x_0)^*||_2 ≤ 2π(2π)^{-1/2} ||S_n − S'_n||_2 = √(2π)·n^{-1/2}·√2 → 0`.
- For a unitary `u` and `x, y ∈ M`,
  `||[x,u]||_2 ≤ 2||x − y||_2 + ||[y,u]||_2`. By the Leibniz rule and
  density of `C[F]`, this gives `||[x,u_n]||_2 → 0` for every `x ∈ M`.

So `(u_n)` is a nontrivial central sequence. `M` is a II_1 factor by
(I2), so `M` has property Γ and is not full. This is independent of
whether `F` is amenable.

**(B3) Not prime.** (I2) gives `M ≅ M ⊗̄ R` with `R` the hyperfinite
II_1 factor. Hence `M` is a tensor product of two II_1 factors, and
therefore not prime.

## Step 3 (Part C): the class kill

Any certificate of type (A) is equivalent to the root by Step 1. Any
certificate of type (B) is refuted outright by Step 2. So a route to
*nonamenability* of `F` through "L(F) is solid / strongly solid / (AO),
and non-injective" dies at (*). Such a route would already contradict
itself. A route through "L(F) is prime / full / non-Γ" or "F is not
inner amenable" dies at the `x_0`-conjugate construction. A route to
*amenability* through (A) is the root restated. Bi-exactness is covered
only conditionally, through the recalled implication "bi-exact ⇒ (AO)"
(Brown–Ozawa Ch. 15), which was not verified verbatim here. ∎
