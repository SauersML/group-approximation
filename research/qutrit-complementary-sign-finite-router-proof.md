## The complete sign cube

The three involutions `H,C_1,W` commute and are independent: their
nonzero root entries occupy the distinct positions
`(c_2,c_1),(c_2,c_0),(c_2,c_3)`. Therefore their eight joint sign
projections are nonzero. Restricting to `E=(1-H)/2` leaves the four
projections `G_(sigma,tau)` in `(QCS1)`. Orthogonality and

```text
sum_(sigma,tau)G_(sigma,tau)=E
```

follow from the two binary Fourier resolutions.

Expanding a sign projection, only its identity term contributes after
multiplication by a qutrit atom `z`. Every nonempty product of
`H,C_1,W` retains a nonzero entry in one of the three distinct root
positions and is not in `C=<J,Z>`. Hence

```text
tau(zG_(sigma,tau))=tau(z)/8=1/72.                      (QCS7)
```

This proves `(QCS2)`.

The conjugation action on the sign labels is also exact. If
`(eta,kappa,omega)` denotes the eigenvalues of `(H,C_1,W)`, then

```text
Ad(U): (eta,kappa,omega) |-> (eta kappa,kappa,omega),
Ad(V): (eta,kappa,omega) |-> (eta,kappa omega,omega).   (QCS8)
```

The first formula is `UHU=HC_1`; the second is
`VC_1V=C_1W`. Thus the plus support of `EUE` consists of the two
quarters with `kappa=+1`, and the plus support of `PVP` is the single
quarter `(kappa,omega)=(+1,+1)`. The other signs are literal leakage
blocks, not missing formal cases.

## The exact order before torus closure

Put `M={c_0,d_0,d_1}`. For `m in M` define matrix directions

```text
h   =Q E_(c_2,c_1),
u_m =T E_(c_1,m),
v_m =S E_(m,c_3),
a_m =QT E_(c_2,m),
d   =E_(c_1,c_3),
w   =Q E_(c_2,c_3).
```

Let `mathcal n` be their `F_2`-span. The displayed twelve directions
are linearly independent: either their matrix positions differ, or they
are the single listed coefficient at that position.

The only nonzero products of basis directions are

```text
h u_m=a_m,
u_m v_n=delta_(mn)d,
h d=w,
a_m v_n=delta_(mn)w.                                   (QCS9)
```

The coefficient reductions are exactly

```text
TS=1,                         QTS=Q.
```

All products of four directions vanish. Hence `mathcal n` is a
twelve-dimensional nilpotent algebra and

```text
|1+mathcal n|=2^12.                                     (QCS10)
```

The roots `H,U_m,V_m` lie in `1+mathcal n`. Their commutators give

```text
[H,U_m]=1+a_m,
[U_m,V_m]=1+d,
[1+a_m,V_m]=1+w.
```

Thus they generate every root direction in `mathcal n` and hence all of
`1+mathcal n`. Conjugation by `g_1` cycles the three values of `m` and
fixes `h,d,w`. Therefore `g_1` normalizes `1+mathcal n`. Its order is
three, while `1+mathcal n` is a 2-group, so their intersection is
trivial. This proves `(QCS3)`.

## Finite closure under the qutrit torus

Replace each named coordinate by the full `C`-invariant scalar summand
containing it. Let

```text
mathcal V_2 -> mathcal V_1 -> mathcal V_M -> mathcal V_3 (QCS11)
```

be the resulting four-stage flag, where `mathcal V_M` is generated by
the three middle coordinates. Both `C` and `g_1` preserve every stage:
`C` preserves its isotypic summands, and `g_1` acts only inside
`mathcal V_M`. Thus the scalar group

```text
K_0=<C,g_1>
```

is finite and preserves the flag.

Let `mathcal n_C` be the `F_2`-span of all `K_0`-conjugates of
`mathcal n` and all their products. Every element is strictly upper
triangular for `(QCS11)`, so `mathcal n_C^4=0`. There are only finitely
many scalar matrix positions. Along a directed path the only coefficient
products are contained in the finite-dimensional span of

```text
Q, T, S, QT, 1,
```

because no path can use the `T` or `S` edge twice and `(QCS9)` reduces
the only mixed products. Hence `mathcal n_C` is finite dimensional over
`F_2`.

The finite algebra group `1+mathcal n_C` is normalized by `K_0` and
contains every nonscalar generator. Therefore

```text
<C,g_1,H,U,V>
 subset (1+mathcal n_C) semidirect K_0,                  (QCS12)
```

which proves `(QCS5)`.

## Consequence for sums before polarizing

All four sign projections, the internal polars, the leakage operators
from `(QCS8)`, and every `g_1`-routed version belong to the complex
group algebra of the finite group in `(QCS12)`. Take its left regular
representation. The restriction to `C` is a multiple of the regular
representation, so every qutrit atom has normalized rank `1/9` and
`(QCS7)` holds literally.

Put
```text
A_(sigma,tau)=G_(sigma,tau)R.
```
The sign projections are orthogonal, so the exact source-side cross
terms are
```text
A_(sigma,tau)^*A_(sigma',tau')
 =R G_(sigma,tau)G_(sigma',tau')R
 =0
```
for distinct signs. Moreover
```text
sum_(sigma,tau)A_(sigma,tau)=ER.                         (QCS13)
```
Thus summing all four branches before polarizing and applying one common
router gives
```text
sum_(sigma,tau)g_1A_(sigma,tau)=g_1ER.
```
After left compression by `e_2`, this is exactly the original router
`e_2g_1ER`, whose squared norm is `1/81`; sign resolution has not
created four independent ranges.

Notice that `G_(sigma,tau)R` is generally not a projection because
the sign roots do not commute with `R`. The number `1/36` in
`(QCS7)` is its squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm, not its polar-support
rank. For branch-dependent routers, those support ranks and range cross
terms must be computed rather than inferred from energy.

All such words using powers of `g_1` still lie in the faithful finite
model `(QCS12)`. Hence any proposed exact Cuntz-pair or over-capacity
identity made from this packet can be checked there and fails if it
would contradict finite-dimensional rank. The result simultaneously
models every sign recombination and every cross term without asserting
a false rank formula.

The actor `g_2` does not preserve `(QCS11)`. Once it is adjoined, scalar
propagation gives `EL_28(J)` and the finite model disappears. Any
surviving complementary-sign attack must therefore use `g_2` in the
same word that claims range orthogonality or full source.
