Given `Λ ->> F ->> Q` over the canonical map, the image of `N` is
exactly the kernel of `F -> Q`. It is perfect, being a quotient of
the perfect group `N`. A solvable perfect group is trivial, proving
the first assertion.

For a nonzero twist `c`, its extension class in `H^2(Λ;F_5)` is
nonzero because the vertices are perfect and have `H^2(-;F_5)=0`.
The finite-quotient computation theorem gives `H^1(N;F_5)=0`, and
the PSL2 vanishing proof gives `H^2(Q;F_5)=0`. The reviewed restriction
injectivity theorem therefore says the twist remains nonsplit over
`N`.

Write `E=N_c`. Since `E/C_5=N` is perfect, the abelianization of `E`
is generated by the central `C_5` and is either zero or `C_5`. In the
latter case the abelianization map would restrict to an isomorphism
on the central kernel. Its kernel would map isomorphically onto `N`,
giving a splitting of `E -> N`, a contradiction. Thus `N_c` is perfect.

Its image is the kernel in any factorization `Λ_c ->> F ->> Q`.
If that kernel is solvable, it is trivial, so the whole `N_c` is killed.
This includes the central `C_5`. No finite-group approximation or
classification is used in this argument.
