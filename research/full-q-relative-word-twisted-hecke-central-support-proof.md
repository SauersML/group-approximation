Use the binary matrix restriction to the eight projective summands

```text
q@2, q@4, q@5, q@6, e_1@7, e_2@8, e_3@9, e_3@2.       (1)
```

The subgroup `K=<L_0,r,l>` is the full pattern group for the acyclic arrow
closure

```text
q2->q4, q2->u2, q2->u3, q2->q5, q2->q6,
q4->u3, q4->q5, q4->q6,
u1->u3, u1->q5, u1->q6,
u2->q5, u2->q6, u3->q6, q5->q6.                       (2)
```

There are fifteen binary coordinates in `(2)`, hence `|K|=2^15`.  The
character `chi` is negative on the first three center-chain roots and
positive on the remaining simple coordinates; the defining commutators lie
in its kernel.  Its idempotent is exactly

```text
F=e_(K,chi)=Q(1+r)(1+l)/4.                             (3)
```

Put `g=J_1J_2`.  Canonical left-`K` row reduction in the pattern order

```text
{q2,u1} < {q4,u2} < {u3,q5} < q6                     (4)
```

enumerates `1344` left cosets of `K` in `H=<K,g>`.  Right `K`-orbits on
this list are the double cosets.  There are `156` of them.  For a
representative `x`, the corner element `F x F` is nonzero precisely when

```text
chi(k)=chi(x k x^(-1))
```

on `K intersect x^(-1)Kx`.  Propagating this sign condition around each
right-`K` orbit leaves exactly `52` compatible double cosets.  Their signed
orbit sums have disjoint group-basis supports and form a basis

```text
c_0=F, c_1, ..., c_51                                  (5)
```

of `A=F C[H] F`.  The orbit of `g` has sixteen left cosets and gives

```text
h=FgF=(1/16)c_1.                                      (6)
```

For each pair in `(5)`, multiply the two signed orbit sums, reduce every
group term by `(4)`, and collect it back in the disjoint basis `(5)`.  All
structure constants are integers.  Starting from `c_1`, close under left
and right multiplication by all `c_i`.  Fraction-free Gaussian elimination
over the resulting integer coefficient vectors stabilizes at dimension

```text
44.                                                     (7)
```

Reducing the identity vector `c_0` against that exact echelon basis leaves
the nonzero remainder `c_3`.  Hence `c_0` is not in the two-sided ideal,
which proves `(RHC3)`.

There is also a direct exact kernel check before central support is taken.
Let `a` be the missing binary root `q2->u1` and let `d:q2->u2`, which is a
positive generator of `K`.  The root `a` normalizes `(K,chi)`, while

```text
g a = d g.                                             (8)
```

Thus `aF=Fa` and `Fd=F`, so

```text
h a=FgFa=FgaF=FdgF=h,
h F(1-a)=0,                 F(1-a)!=0.                (9)
```

Equation `(9)` proves that the least singular value already vanishes in the
faithful regular corner.  The stronger ideal computation `(7)` proves that
the vanishing is not merely a kernel direction inside otherwise supported
simple blocks.

Finally a finite-dimensional complex group algebra is semisimple.  The
corner `A` is the direct sum, over irreducible `H`-representations with
nonzero `F`-space, of the corresponding full matrix corners.  The ideal
generated by `h` contains exactly the simple blocks on which `h` is nonzero.
Since it misses the identity, one such block has `F` nonzero and `h` zero,
which is `(RHC4)`.

`experiments/full_q_twisted_hecke_regular_audit.py` performs only the exact
binary row, sign-orbit, integer multiplication, and fraction-free ideal
calculation described above.  It is intended to run through MSI only.
