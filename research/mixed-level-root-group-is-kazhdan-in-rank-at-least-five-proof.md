Notation: `J = {1..r} \ {2,3}`, so `|J| = r - 2 >= 3`. Write `e(M) = 1 + M` and `[a,b] = a b a^(-1) b^(-1)`. For
indices `x, y, z`,

```text
eps_xy + eps_yz = eps_xz + [y = 2] + [y = 3],
```

so `I_xy I_yz ⊆ I_xz`, with `t^(eps_xz) w = (t^(eps_xy) w)(t^(eps_yz))` when `y ∈ J`. **(★)**

**Step 1 (full indices route every level).** If `x ≠ z` and `y ∈ J \ {x, z}`, then
`e_xz(t^(eps_xz) w) = [e_xy(t^(eps_xy) w), e_yz(t^(eps_yz))]`. Both factors are generators of `K`.

**Step 2 (a strong A_2-grading).** Split `{1..r} = A ⊔ B ⊔ C` with `3 ∈ A`, `2 ∈ B`, and each of `A, B, C` meeting
`J`. This is possible because `|J| >= 3`. For distinct blocks `X, Y` let

```text
X_XY = { e(M) : M supported on X × Y, M_xy ∈ I_xy },
```

and identify `(X,Y)` with the root `e_X - e_Y` of `A_2`.

- `X_XY` is an abelian subgroup of `K`, since `M M' = 0` and `e(M) = ∏ e_xy(M_xy)`.
- Let `X, Y, Z` be distinct, `M` on `X × Y` and `N` on `Y × Z`. Then `M^2 = N^2 = NM = 0`, so `[e(M), e(N)] = e(MN)`,
  and `MN` has entries in `I_xz` by (★). So `[X_XY, X_YZ] ⊆ X_XZ`, and `[X_YZ, X_XY]` is its inverse set.
- Two roots sharing a row block or a column block, such as `(X,Y),(X,Z)` or `(X,Y),(Z,Y)`, give `MN = NM = 0`, so the
  groups commute. No positive combination of such roots is a root.
- `K = < ∪ X_XY >`. A generator `e_xz(I_xz)` with `x, z` in different blocks lies in `X_XZ`. If `x, z` share a block,
  choose `y ∈ J` in another block and apply Step 1.

So `{X_XY}` is an `A_2`-grading of `K`. The Borel sets of `A_2` are `{(X,Y), (Y,Z), (X,Z)}`, with core `{(X,Z)}`.
Strongness asks `X_XZ ⊆ < X_XY, X_YZ >`. `X_XZ` is generated by the `e_xz(I_xz)`, and each is a commutator from Step 1
with `y ∈ Y ∩ J`. By part (a) of `strongly-root-graded-groups-have-kazhdan-root-unions`,
`kappa_0 := kappa(K, ∪ X_XY) > 0`.

**Step 3 (linear relative (T) for the fine root groups `Y_xz = e_xz(I_xz)`).** Let `P = EL_2(R) ⋉ R^2`. By
`elementary-linear-semidir-pair-relative-t`, some finite `S_0 ⊆ P` has `mu := kappa(P, R^2; S_0) > 0`. By part (b) of
the grading claim, `R^2` is normal in `P`, so in every unitary representation of `P` an `(S_0, delta)`-invariant
vector is `(R^2, 2 delta / mu)`-invariant. Pulling representations back along a homomorphism `psi : P -> K` shows
that `(psi(S_0), delta)`-invariant vectors are `(psi(R^2), 2 delta / mu)`-invariant.

Model `P` as `{[[g, w], [0, 1]]}` (column vectors, standard action) or as `{[[1, v], [0, g]]}` (row vectors). The two
models are identified by the automorphism `g -> (g^T)^(-1)` of `EL_2(R)`.

- **Column type.** Take `x ∈ J`, `z ≠ x`, and a pivot `p ∈ J \ {x, z}`. Put `g` on the rows and columns `(x, p)`, and
  put `t^([z=2]) w` in column `z`, rows `(x, p)`. This is a homomorphism because `w -> t^([z=2]) w` is `R`-linear.
  Generators go to `e_xp(R), e_px(R), e_xz(I_xz), e_pz(I_pz)`, all in `K` since `x, p ∈ J`, and `psi(R^2) ⊇ Y_xz`.
- **Row type.** Take `z ∈ J`, `x ≠ z`, and a pivot `p ∈ J \ {x, z}`. Put `g` on `(z, p)`, and `t^([x=3]) v` in row `x`,
  columns `(z, p)`. Generators go to `e_zp(R), e_pz(R), e_xz(I_xz), e_xp(I_xp)`, and `psi(R^2) ⊇ Y_xz`.
- **The two roots with `x, z ∈ {2, 3}`.** Here `e_23(u) = [e_2p(u), e_p3(1)]` and `e_32(t^2 u) = [e_3p(t u), e_p2(t)]`
  for `p ∈ J`, and for unitaries `||[a, b] v - v|| <= 2 ||a v - v|| + 2 ||b v - v||`.

Every pivot exists because `|J| >= 3`. Let `S` be the union of the finitely many `psi(S_0)`, together with
`e_p3(±1)` and `e_p2(±t)`. Suppose `v` is `(S, delta)`-invariant.

- `v` is `(Y_xz, 2 delta / mu)`-invariant when `x ∈ J` or `z ∈ J`.
- `v` is `(Y_xz, (4/mu + 2) delta)`-invariant for `(x, z) ∈ {(2,3), (3,2)}`.
- Each `X_XY` is a product of at most `r^2` fine groups, and `||∏ g_k v - v|| <= Σ ||g_k v - v||`. So `v` is
  `(∪ X_XY, C delta)`-invariant with `C = r^2 (4/mu + 2)`.

**Step 4.** By part (b) of the grading claim, `kappa(K, S) >= kappa_0 / C > 0`. `S` is finite, so `K` has
property (T). ∎
