Conventions are as in the target. An edge `e : u -l-> v` gives the two
half-edges `(u, l)` and `(v, l^{-1})`. A *fold* takes two distinct edges `e_1`
and `e_2` with half-edges `(x, l)` at a common vertex `x`, with other ends
`y_1` and `y_2`. It identifies `e_1` with `e_2` and `y_1` with `y_2`. The
Stallings fold of a finite graph is reached by any maximal sequence of folds,
and the result does not depend on the order.

## 1. Domination lemma (item 1)

Add the paths one at a time, and fold completely after each one. Write
`H_i = fold(Γ ∪ γ_1 ∪ … ∪ γ_i)`, so that `H_0 = Γ`, since `Γ` is folded.

**Classes.** Every vertex and every edge of `H_i` is a class of vertices or
edges of `Γ ∪ γ_1 ∪ … ∪ γ_i`.
- A vertex is a *Γ-vertex* if its class contains a vertex of `Γ`, and a
  *Δ-vertex* otherwise.
- An edge is a *Γ-edge* if its class contains an edge of `Γ`, and a *Δ-edge*
  otherwise.
- Both ends of a Γ-edge are Γ-vertices.

Put `δ_i = E_Δ(H_i) − V_Δ(H_i)`, and let `R_i` be the relation "same vertex of
`H_i`" on `V(Γ)`.

**Counted pairs.** We build a list `P_i` of pairs of vertices of `Γ` and keep
the invariant

```text
(I)   R_i ⊆ cl_Γ(P_i)   and   |P_i| + δ_i ≤ i .
```

At `i = 0` we have `R_0` equal to the identity, `P_0 = ∅` and `δ_0 = 0`.

**Attaching a path.** Attach `γ_{i+1}`, of length `ℓ ≥ 1`, between two
vertices of `H_i`. This adds `ℓ` Δ-edges and `ℓ − 1` Δ-vertices, so `δ`
increases by exactly 1. A path of length 0 changes nothing.

**Folding.** Now fold. Consider one fold of `e_1` and `e_2` at `x` with ends
`y_1` and `y_2`. There are four cases.

(a) **`y_1 = y_2`.** Two edges become one, and no vertex changes.
- If at least one of `e_1, e_2` is a Δ-edge, then `E_Δ` drops by 1. The
  merged edge is a Γ-edge when either one was.
- Otherwise `E_Δ` is unchanged.
- In both cases `δ` does not increase, and `R` is unchanged.

(b) **`y_1 ≠ y_2`, and both are Δ-vertices.**
- Both edges are Δ-edges, since Γ-edges end at Γ-vertices.
- `V_Δ` and `E_Δ` both drop by 1, so `δ` is unchanged.
- `R` is unchanged.

(c) **`y_1 ≠ y_2`, with one Γ-vertex and one Δ-vertex.**
- `V_Δ` drops by 1.
- The edge ending at the Δ-vertex is a Δ-edge, so the two edges merge to one
  and `E_Δ` drops by 1.
- `δ` is unchanged. The Γ-vertices in the merged class are those of one old
  class, so `R` is unchanged.

(d) **`y_1 ≠ y_2`, both Γ-vertices.** Pick vertices `y_1' ∈ y_1` and
`y_2' ∈ y_2` of `Γ`.
- **Both edges are Γ-edges.** Their classes contain edges
  `f_1 : x_1 -l-> y_1''` and `f_2 : x_2 -l-> y_2''` of `Γ`, with
  `x_1, x_2 ∈ x`, `y_1'' ∈ y_1` and `y_2'' ∈ y_2`. So `(x_1, x_2)` is in the
  current relation, and `(y_1'', y_2'')` is in its fold-closure. We add nothing
  to `P`, and the new relation still lies in `cl_Γ(P)`.
- **At least one edge is a Δ-edge.** Add `(y_1', y_2')` to `P`. The new
  relation is generated by the old one and this pair, so it lies in
  `cl_Γ(P)`. Two edges become one edge, so `E_Δ` drops by 1 and `V_Δ` is
  unchanged. Hence `|P| + δ` is unchanged.

In every case `|P| + δ` does not increase. So (I) holds after each fold, and
therefore at `H_{i+1}`.

**Equality.**
- `R_k` is fold-closed on `Γ`. If `u R_k v` and `u -l-> u'`, `v -l-> v'` are
  edges of `Γ`, then their images in the folded graph `H_k` are two edges with
  the same label at one vertex. So they coincide, and `u' R_k v'`.
- Every counted pair lies in `R_k`.
- Hence `cl_Γ(P_k) ⊆ R_k`, and with (I), `R_k = cl_Γ(P_k)`.

**`E_Δ ≥ V_Δ` in `H_k`.**
- Every vertex of `Γ ∪ γ_1 ∪ … ∪ γ_k` is joined to `V(Γ)` by a path, because
  each `γ_i` has its ends in `Γ`. So every vertex of `H_k` is joined to a
  Γ-vertex.
- Let `C` be a connected component of the subgraph spanned by the
  Δ-vertices. Then `C` has at least `|C| − 1` edges inside it and at least one
  edge to a Γ-vertex.
- All these edges are Δ-edges, since they have a Δ-end. Distinct components
  use distinct edges.
- So `E_Δ ≥ V_Δ`, which gives `|P_k| ≤ k − δ_k ≤ k`. ∎

## 2. Structure of Γ_Q^K (item 2)

**The paths.** The `c`-path at `p` is

```text
p -b-> x_1(p) -a-> x_2(p) <-b- x_3(p) <-b- p·c .
```

Its vertices lie over `p`, `pb`, `pba`, `pbab^{-1}` and `pc`.

**Folding.**
- The real vertex `q` carries two edges labelled `b`: one to `x_1(q)`, and
  one to `x_3(q c^{-1})`, whose path ends at `q`.
- Fold them. Call the merged vertex `w(q)`, which lies over `qb`.
- Then `w(q)` has an `a`-edge to `x_2(q)`, and a `b`-edge to `x_2(q c^{-1})`
  coming from `x_3(q c^{-1})`.

**The result.** The graph now has `3n` vertices: `q`, `w(q)` and `x_2(q)`.
Its `4n` edges are:
- `q -a-> qa`;
- `q -b-> w(q)`;
- `w(q) -a-> x_2(q)`;
- `w(q) -b-> x_2(q c^{-1})`.

Rename by the point underneath. Put `R_y = y`, `W_y = w(y b^{-1})` and
`X_y = x_2(y a^{-1} b^{-1})`. Here `φ(x_2(q)) = qba`, and
`q c^{-1} b a = q b b`. This gives the four edge families of item 2.

**The labels at each vertex.** Among the half-edge labels `a, a^{-1}, b,
b^{-1}`:
- `R_y` carries `a, a^{-1}, b`;
- `W_y` carries `b^{-1}, a, b`;
- `X_y` carries `a^{-1}, b^{-1}`.

No label repeats at a vertex, so the graph is folded, and it is `Γ_Q^K`.
Transitivity of `K` is not used.

## 3. Merges are seeds (item 3)

**Shared labels.** Two vertices over the same `y` share exactly these labels:
- `R_y, W_y` share `a` (targets `R_{ya}` and `X_{ya}`) and `b` (targets
  `W_{yb}` and `X_{yb}`);
- `R_y, X_y` share `a^{-1}` (targets `R_{ya^{-1}}` and `W_{ya^{-1}}`);
- `W_y, X_y` share `b^{-1}` (targets `R_{yb^{-1}}` and `W_{yb^{-1}}`).

So a merge over `y` forces exactly the following, and nothing else:

```text
R_y ~ W_y  ⟹  R_{ya} ~ X_{ya}  and  W_{yb} ~ X_{yb}
R_y ~ X_y  ⟹  R_{ya^{-1}} ~ W_{ya^{-1}}
W_y ~ X_y  ⟹  R_{yb^{-1}} ~ W_{yb^{-1}}
```

**The set S.** Let `~` be fold-closed and relate only vertices over the same
point. Put `S = {y : R_y ~ W_y}`.
- The second and third rules give `R_y ~ X_y ⟹ ya^{-1} ∈ S` and
  `W_y ~ X_y ⟹ yb^{-1} ∈ S`.
- Applied at `ya^{-1}` and `yb^{-1}`, the first rule gives the converses.
- So `~` is determined by `S`.
- Transitivity at `y` says: if two of `R_y ~ W_y`, `R_y ~ X_y`, `W_y ~ X_y`
  hold, so does the third. That is, `S` is closed under 2-of-3 on
  `{y, ya^{-1}, yb^{-1}}`.
- With repeated entries the rule is read with multiplicity. For example, if
  `ya^{-1} = y` and `y ∈ S`, then `R_y ~ W_y ~ X_y`, so `yb^{-1} ∈ S`.

**Conversely.** Given a closed `S`, define `~` by the three equivalences of
item 3.
- It is transitive at each `y`, by closedness.
- By the displayed rules it is fold-closed. Each forced pair is again one of
  the three types, at `ya`, `yb`, `ya^{-1}` or `yb^{-1}`, and it corresponds
  to a point already in `S`.

**Counting merges.** A merge of two vertices over `y` adds one point to `S`:
- `y` for `R ~ W`;
- `ya^{-1}` for `R ~ X`;
- `yb^{-1}` for `W ~ X`.

The merged relation is the one given by the closure. The quotient has `n`
vertices iff all three vertices over every `y` coincide, iff `S = Q`. So

```text
ρ(Q) = min { |S_0| : cl(S_0) = Q } = r(Q),
```

where the triples `{y, ya^{-1}, yb^{-1}}` are the triples `(y, a·y, b·y)` of
the left action `g·y = y·g^{-1}`.

`rho_vs_r.py` checks `ρ = r` by exhaustive search, on 48 random transitive
`Q` with `n ≤ 9`, with 0 mismatches (`rho_vs_r_out.txt`).

## 4. Chords (item 4)

**`d ≤ ρ`.** Let `u, v` be vertices over the same point.
- Every vertex of `Γ_Q^K` lies on the `c`-path of a real vertex. So there are
  paths `α` from a real vertex `p` to `u`, and `β` from a real vertex `p'` to
  `v`.
- The chord `(p, lab(α) lab(β)^{-1})` ends at `p'`.
- Its path folds onto `α` from `p` and onto `β` from `p'`, since `Γ_Q^K` is
  folded. So its middle vertex is identified with both `u` and `v`.
- Hence one chord performs one merge, and `d(Q) ≤ ρ(Q)`.

**`d ≥ ρ`.** Let `k` chords fold `Γ_Q^K` to a graph `H` with `n` vertices.
- `H` maps onto the Schreier graph of `Q`, so `R_H` is "same point" on
  `V(Γ_Q^K)`.
- By §1, `R_H = cl(P)` with `|P| ≤ k`. So `ρ(Q) ≤ k`, and `d(Q) ≥ ρ(Q)`.

With §3, `d = ρ = r`. ∎
