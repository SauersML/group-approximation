Recall `[HA_2]` (`ha2-actions-give-fp2-twisted-brin-thompson-groups`): the
acting group is of type FP_2, point stabilizers are finitely generated, and there
are finitely many orbits on `S × S`.

**(A).** Multiplication in `Γ = S ⋊ W` is `(s,w)(t,v) = (s·w(t), wv)`.

1. *Action.* `(s,w)·((t,v)·x) = s·w(t·v(x)) = s·w(t)·wv(x) = ((s,w)(t,v))·x`,
   and `(1,id)` acts trivially.
2. *Faithful.* If `(s,w)` fixes every `x`, then `x = 1` gives `s = 1`, and then
   `w(x) = x` for all `x`, so `w = id`.
3. *Transitive, stabilizer W.* `(x,id)·1 = x`. `(s,w)·1 = s`, so
   `Stab(1) = {(1,w)} = W`, finitely generated by hypothesis. All stabilizers are
   conjugate to it, hence finitely generated.
4. *Pair orbits.* Since `Γ` is transitive, every orbit on `S × S` meets
   `{1} × S`, and `(1,x)`, `(1,y)` lie in one orbit iff some `g ∈ Stab(1) = W`
   has `g·x = y`, i.e. `w(x) = y`. So orbits on `S × S` correspond bijectively to
   `W`-orbits on `S`: finitely many.
5. *FP_2.* Hypothesis.

**(B).** Let `Γ` be FP_2, conjugacy-finite, `Z(Γ) = 1`.

1. `(a,b)·x = a x b^-1` is an action: `(a,b)·((c,d)·x) = (ac)x(bd)^-1`.
2. *Faithful.* Trivial action forces `a = b` (at `x = 1`) and then `a ∈ Z(Γ) = 1`.
3. *Stabilizers.* Transitive (`(x,1)·1 = x`); `Stab(1)` is the diagonal `≅ Γ`,
   finitely generated because an FP_2 group is finitely generated.
4. *Pair orbits.* As in (A)4, orbits on `Γ × Γ` correspond to orbits of the
   diagonal on `Γ` by `x ↦ a x a^-1`, i.e. conjugacy classes: finitely many.
5. *FP_2 of `Γ × Γ`.* By `fp2-iff-quotient-of-fp-group-by-perfect-normal-subgroup`,
   `Γ ≅ H/N` with `H` finitely presented and `N` perfect. Then
   `Γ × Γ ≅ (H × H)/(N × N)`. `H × H` is finitely presented (two copies of a
   finite presentation plus the commutators of generators across the copies), and
   `N × N` is perfect, since `[N×N, N×N] = [N,N] × [N,N] = N × N`. The same claim
   gives `Γ × Γ` of type FP_2.

Torsion-free implies centreless when there are finitely many classes: a central
`z ≠ 1` has infinite order, and the powers `z^n` are pairwise distinct central
elements, each its own conjugacy class. `∎`

(B) is also the case `S = Γ`, `W = Inn(Γ) ≅ Γ` of (A), via
`Γ ⋊ Inn(Γ) ≅ Γ × Γ`, `(s, c_g) ↦ (sg, g)`; the direct argument above avoids
checking that isomorphism.
