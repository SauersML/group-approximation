Exhibit a triangle of groups with the following data:
- finite simple vertex groups;
- nontrivial edge groups;
- trivial face group;
- Gersten–Stallings angle sum `< π`.

Its fundamental group `G` must have no nontrivial finite quotient. Such a `G` is
infinite and word-hyperbolic. By `simple-vertex-groups-vtf-iff-some-finite-quotient`,
the negative answer in this class is exactly a member that is not virtually
torsion-free, and it would decide `hyperbolic-groups-virtually-torsion-free` and
`non-residually-finite-hyperbolic-group` together.

**OPEN.** No member is known either way in general: neither every member having a
nontrivial finite quotient, nor some member having none.

## Attempts

- **Structured members have quotients (calibration).** If the three vertex groups lie
  in one finite group `S` with the edge groups as their pairwise intersections, the
  inclusions give a vertex-injective quotient. Congruence-type examples behave the same
  way. `cckw-ghb2-kms-group-structure` has `SL_4(F_7)`, but its vertex groups are
  unipotent, not simple. A candidate therefore needs edge identifications with no
  common finite overgroup of small size.
- **Closest literature.** Lubotzky–Manning–Wilton, arXiv:1702.08200v2, build k-fold
  triangle groups from LPS Ramanujan graphs. Their vertex groups are finite quotients
  of free groups, and the edge groups meeting `G_0` are trivial, so the Lemma does not
  apply. They ask, verbatim, "Question 1.8. Are the examples from Corollary 1.7
  virtually torsion-free? Residually finite? Linear?"
- **Counting heuristic (not a proof).** For a finite simple `S`, the expected number of
  compatible triples of embeddings, averaged over the positions of the edge subgroups
  inside the vertex groups, is `∏_e |C_S(E_e)| / ∏_v |C_S(A_v)|` up to bounded factors.
  In the free-orbit regime this is about `|S|^{1 - χ_orb}` for alternating and
  bounded-rank Lie type `S`. Here `χ_orb = Σ_v 1/|A_v| - Σ_e 1/|E_e| + 1`, so conjugacy
  classes of epimorphisms number about `|S|^{-χ_orb}`. Actions in which edge
  generators fix many points dominate instead, and they inflate the count. So
  candidates should use edge groups generated by elements with uniformly small
  fixed-point ratios, for example regular semisimple elements. The whole count is
  beaten by structure, as the calibration shows.
- **Where it dies.** Showing that no quotient exists means excluding epimorphisms onto
  every finite simple group. This is the same large-quotient barrier as
  `random-complex-group-no-quotients-above-exp-pn`. Low-rank Lie type is the worst
  case: in `PSL(2,q)` the normalizers of cyclic edge groups have order about `q`, and
  three of them already balance `|S|`. So vertex groups should not embed in `PSL(2,q)`.
