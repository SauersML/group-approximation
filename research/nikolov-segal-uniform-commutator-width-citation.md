Read from the arXiv PDF (math/0604399v1, page 2) on 2026-09-13 by lane ex-weak-sofic-deep-wreath:

> **Theorem 1.2** *Let d be a natural number, and let w be a group word. Suppose either that w is
> d-locally finite or that w is a simple commutator. Then there exists f = f(w, d) such that: in any
> finite d-generator group G, every product of w-values in G is equal to a product of f w-values.*

Page 3 of the same PDF: "by 'simple commutator' we mean one of the words `[x_1, x_2] = x_1^-1 x_2^-1 x_1 x_2`,
`[x_1, ..., x_n] = [[x_1, ..., x_(n-1)], x_n]` (`n > 2`), and a `w`-value means an element of the form
`w(g_1, g_2, ...)^(+-1)` with the `g_j in G`."

Take `w = [x_1, x_2]`. Products of `w`-values are exactly the elements of `[G, G]`. The commutator
convention (`x^-1 y^-1 x y` there) does not change the set of commutators.

The theorem number is from arXiv v1. The numbering in the Annals version was not re-checked. The
proof relies on the classification of finite simple groups (Part II).
