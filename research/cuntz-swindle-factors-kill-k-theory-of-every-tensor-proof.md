Notation as in the claim. `⊗` means `⊗_k`.

**Step 0. The structure passes to tensors.** Let `D` be a unital `k`-algebra and `R = D ⊗ A`.
Put `s_i := 1 ⊗ s_i`, `s_i^* := 1 ⊗ s_i^*` and `σ_R := id_D ⊗ σ`, a unital endomorphism of `R`.
- On a pure tensor, `s_1 (d ⊗ a) s_1^* + s_2 (d ⊗ σ(a)) s_2^* = d ⊗ (s_1 a s_1^* + s_2 σ(a) s_2^*) = d ⊗ σ(a)`.
- Both sides of (S) are additive in `r`.

So (S) holds in `R`, and `R` carries a Cuntz swindle structure. Hence item 1 follows once we show
`K_q(A') = 0` for every `A'` carrying such a structure, and we may rename `A' = A`.

**Step 1. The Cuntz sum map.** `θ : A × A -> A`, `θ(a, b) = s_1 a s_1^* + s_2 b s_2^*`, is a unital ring
homomorphism.
- The cross terms of `θ(a,b) θ(a',b')` contain `s_1^* s_2 = 0` or `s_2^* s_1 = 0`.
- `θ(1,1) = 1`.

(S) says `σ = θ ∘ (id, σ)`, where `(id, σ) : A -> A × A`.

**Step 2. θ induces addition, `q >= 1`.** Fix `N` and `X, Y ∈ GL_N(A)`.
- Put `S = (s_1 I_N , s_2 I_N)` (size `N × 2N`) and `T = (s_1^* I_N ; s_2^* I_N)` (size `2N × N`).
  Then `S T = I_N`, `T S = I_(2N)` and `θ(X, Y) = S diag(X, Y) T`.
- Put `U = [[S, 0], [0, T]]` and `V = [[T, 0], [0, S]]` in `M_(3N)(A)`, with row and column blocks sized
  so the products make sense. Then `U V = I_(3N) = V U`, and
  ```text
  U · diag(X, Y, I_N) · V = diag(S diag(X,Y) T, T S) = diag(θ(X, Y), I_(2N)).
  ```

So on every `GL_N(A × A) = GL_N(A) × GL_N(A)`, the homomorphism `GL(θ)` into `GL(A)` agrees with the
block sum `(X, Y) -> X ⊕ Y` up to conjugation by an element `U_N` of `GL(A)`.

Two standard facts finish this step (Weibel, *The K-book*, Ch. IV §1):
- homomorphisms `GL(R) -> GL(R')` that agree up to conjugation in `GL(R')` on each `GL_N(R)` induce the
  same maps on `K_q = π_q BGL^+`, `q >= 1`. This is the argument for Morita invariance;
- block sum induces the H-space product of `BGL(A)^+`, hence addition on `π_q`.

With `K_q(A × A) = K_q(A) ⊕ K_q(A)`, this gives `K_q(θ)(x, y) = x + y`.

For the two degrees used downstream, the classical statements suffice:
- **`q = 1`:** `K_1 = GL/E` is abelian and conjugation-invariant, and block sum is the product there;
- **`q = 2`:** `K_2` is central in `St`, and `GL` acts trivially on it (Milnor, *Introduction to
  Algebraic K-theory*, §§3–5).

**Step 3. θ induces addition, `q = 0`.** For idempotent matrices `e, f ∈ M_N(A)` the same `U, V` give
`U diag(e, f, 0_N) V = diag(θ(e, f), 0_(2N))`. Conjugate idempotents have equal classes, so
`[θ(e,f)] = [e] + [f]`. Since `K_0(A × A) = K_0(A) ⊕ K_0(A)` is generated by such pairs,
`K_0(θ)(x, y) = x + y`.

**Step 4. The swindle, `q >= 0`.** By Steps 1–3, for `x ∈ K_q(A)`,
```text
σ_*(x) = K_q(θ)(x, σ_* x) = x + σ_*(x),
```
so `x = 0`. So `K_q(D ⊗ A) = 0` for all `D` and all `q >= 0`.

**Step 5. Negative degrees.** For `q <= -1`, Bass's definition makes `K_q(R)` the cokernel of
`K_(q+1)(R[t]) ⊕ K_(q+1)(R[t^(-1)]) -> K_(q+1)(R[t, t^(-1)])`. Apply this with `R = D ⊗ A`. Then
`R[t, t^(-1)] = (D ⊗ k[t, t^(-1)]) ⊗ A`, which carries a swindle structure by Step 0. Descending
induction on `q`, over all `D` at once and starting from Step 4, gives `K_q(D ⊗ A) = 0` for every
`q ∈ Z`. This proves item 1.

**Item 2 (traces).** Let `τ` be additive with `τ(xy) = τ(yx)`. Apply `τ` to (S) and use
`τ(s_i b s_i^*) = τ(b s_i^* s_i) = τ(b)`:
```text
τ(σ(a)) = τ(a) + τ(σ(a)),
```
so `τ(a) = 0`. Taking `τ : A -> A/[A,A]` gives `A = [A, A]`.

**Item 3 (L_2).** Suppose `L = L_k(1,2)` carried a swindle structure, for some Cuntz pair in `L`. By
item 1 with `D = k[ε]`, `K_1(k[ε] ⊗ L) = 0`. That contradicts Corollary 3 of
`leavitt-tensor-k1-sees-traces-of-nilpotent-matrices`, which gives `K_1(k[ε] ⊗ L) ≠ 0`.

**Item 4.** Assume `A ≠ 0`.
- **Inner.** Suppose `σ = Ad t` for a unit `t`. Then `σ(t) = t`, and (S) at `a = t` gives
  `t = s_1 t s_1^* + s_2 t s_2^*`. Right-multiplying by `s_i` gives `t s_i = s_i t`, so `σ(s_1) = s_1`.
  Then (S) at `a = s_1` gives `s_1 = s_1 s_1 s_1^* + s_2 s_1 s_2^*`. Right-multiplying by `s_2` gives
  `s_1 s_2 = s_2 s_1`, and left-multiplying by `s_1^*` gives `s_2 = s_1^* s_2 s_1 = 0`. But
  `s_2^* s_2 = 1`, so `1 = 0`.
- **Idempotent.** Suppose `σ² = σ`, and put `y = σ(s_1)`.
  - (S) at `a = y` gives `y = σ(y) = s_1 y s_1^* + s_2 y s_2^*`. Right-multiplying by `s_i` gives
    `y s_i = s_i y`.
  - (S) at `a = s_1` gives `y = s_1 s_1 s_1^* + s_2 y s_2^*`. Right-multiplying by `s_1` gives
    `y s_1 = s_1 s_1`.
  - So `s_1 y = s_1 s_1`, and left-multiplying by `s_1^*` gives `y = s_1`. This is the inner case's
    `σ(s_1) = s_1`, which gives `1 = 0` in the same way. `∎`
