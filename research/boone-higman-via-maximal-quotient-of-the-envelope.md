The argument that looks like it finishes, in four steps:

1. by `boone-higman-thompson-simple-envelope` and
   `chatterji-kassabov-normal-generator-embedding`, embed `G` in a finitely
   generated computably presented simple `S`, then in a finitely presented
   `Gamma` normally generated by an element of `S`;
2. by `proper-normal-subgroups-miss-the-normally-generating-core`, choose a
   maximal proper normal `M <| Gamma`; the quotient `Gamma/M` is simple and
   still contains `G`;
3. `Gamma` is finitely presented;
4. therefore `Gamma/M` is finitely presented.

Steps 1--3 are correct and are exactly the near-solution.  **Step 4 is
false.**

Dead at `fp-quotient-iff-kernel-finitely-normally-generated`.
A quotient of a finitely presented group is finitely presented if and only if
its kernel is finitely normally generated; for a general normal subgroup it
is only finitely generated.  The standing counterexample is any surjection
`F_k ->> Q` onto a finitely generated group `Q` that is not finitely
presented.

**What survives the demolition.**  Everything except step 4.  Steps 1--3 are
the reason `boone-higman-conjecture` is a root of this graph at all, and they
are what makes `ck-envelope-has-a-finitely-normally-generated-maximal-kernel`
a *sufficient* condition rather than merely a necessary one.  The route is
recorded dead rather than deleted because the four-step shape is the first
thing anyone writes down, and because the precise location of the failure —
the last step, and no earlier one — is the useful information.

**Why no weakening of step 4 rescues it.**  Zorn's lemma is the only source
of `M` here, and it produces a maximal element of a poset with no finiteness
data attached.  There is no version of the maximality argument that also
returns a finite normal generating set: maximality is a statement about
having no proper normal overgroup, and finite normal generation is a
statement about generating data, and the poset argument sees only the first.
