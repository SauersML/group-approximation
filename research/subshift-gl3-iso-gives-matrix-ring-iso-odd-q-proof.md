**Lemma G1.** For any unital ring `A` and `m ≥ 2`, `M_m(A)` is generated as a ring by `GL_m(A)`.
For `i ≠ j`, `r E_ij = e_ij(r) − 1`. For the diagonal, `r E_ii = (r E_ij)(e_ji(1) − 1)` with `j ≠ i`. These span
`M_m(A)`. ∎

**Lemma G2.** If `R` is simple, then `M_n(R)` is simple. Every ideal of `M_n(R)` is `M_n(I)`, where `I` is the set of
`(1,1)` entries; conjugating by matrix units moves any entry there. ∎

**Proof.** Let `φ_iso: GL_3(R_X) → GL_3(R_Y)` be an isomorphism. Put `R = R_X`, `n = 3`, `G = GL_3(R_X)`,
`S = M_3(R_Y) = A_3` with `A = R_Y`, and let `φ` be `φ_iso` followed by `GL_3(R_Y) = S* ⊆ S`.

Zel'manov's standing assumptions and the hypotheses of Corollary 4 hold:
1. `2` is invertible in `R_X, R_Y ⊇ F_q`, since `q` is odd.
2. `E_3(R) ⊆ G ⊆ GL_3(R)`.
3. `φ` is a monomorphism with `φ(G) = GL_3(A) = S*`.
4. `φ(Σ)_S ⊆ S* = φ(G)`, because the orthogonal closure is a subgroup of `S*`.
5. `S` is generated by `φ(G)` (Lemma G1).
6. `m = 3 ≥ 2`.

Corollary 4 gives `M_3(R_X) = I_0 ⊕ I_1` and `M_3(R_Y) = S_0 ⊕ S_1`, with `φ_0: I_0 → S_0` an isomorphism and
`φ_1: I_1 → S_1` an anti-isomorphism.

By `minimal-subshift-algebra-is-simple-lef-ring` (item 1), `R_X` and `R_Y` are simple, so `M_3(R_X)` and `M_3(R_Y)`
are simple (Lemma G2). A simple ring is not a direct sum of two nonzero ideals.
- If `I_1 = 0`, then `S_0 ≅ M_3(R_X) ≠ 0`, so `S_1 = 0` and `M_3(R_X) ≅ M_3(R_Y)`.
- Otherwise `I_0 = 0`, so `S_1 ≠ 0`, `S_0 = 0`, and `M_3(R_X) ≅ M_3(R_Y)^op`. ∎
