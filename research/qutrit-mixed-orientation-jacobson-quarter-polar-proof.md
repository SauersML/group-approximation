## The two-letter return

Use the root positions in `(QJM1)`. The first Steinberg commutator is

```text
[H,U]=x_(c_2,c_0)(QT)=C_1.                              (QJM8)
```

The root `C_1` is nonidentity: `QT=T-ST^2` is a nonzero sum of
distinct Leavitt normal forms. The second pair is composable, and

```text
[C_1,V]
 =x_(c_2,c_3)(QTS)
 =x_(c_2,c_3)(Q)
 =W.                                                     (QJM9)
```

Here the middle equality is exactly the one-sided inverse relation
`TS=1`. Neither the negative algebra `F_2<Q,T>` nor the positive
algebra `F_2<Q,S>` contains this two-orientation reduction.

The elements `H,U,C_1` are involutions, `C_1` commutes with `H,U`,
and `[H,U]=C_1`. They form `D_8`. Similarly
`C_1,V,W` form `D_8`. The root positions also show

```text
[H,C_1]=[H,V]=[H,W]=[C_1,W]=1.                          (QJM10)
```

Thus `E`, `C_1`, and `W` commute.

## The nested polar

For the first D8 pair, `UHU=HC_1`. Hence

```text
(EUE)^2
 =E(UEU)E
 =E(1-UHU)E/2
 =E(1+C_1)/2
 =P.                                                     (QJM11)
```

The operator `EUE` is self-adjoint, so its polar has equal source and
range projection `P`.

For the second pair, `V` commutes with `E` and
`VC_1V=C_1W`. Therefore

```text
VPV=E(1+C_1W)/2.
```

Using `PC_1=P` and the commutation in `(QJM10)`,

```text
(PVP)^2
 =P(VPV)P
 =P(1+W)/2
 =G.                                                     (QJM12)
```

Again `PVP` is self-adjoint and has both polar supports equal to `G`.
Equations `(QJM11)--(QJM12)` prove `(QJM4)`.

## Exact qutrit corner dimensions

Let `z` be any Fourier atom of `C=<J,Z>`. The head `H` commutes with
`C` and is not a scalar element, so

```text
tau(zE)=tau(z)/2=1/18.                                  (QJM13)
```

Expand

```text
P=(1-H)(1+C_1)/4,
G=(1-H)(1+C_1)(1+W)/8.                                  (QJM14)
```

Every nonempty product of `H,C_1,W` has a nonzero root entry in at least
one of the three distinct positions

```text
(c_2,c_1),             (c_2,c_0),             (c_2,c_3).
```

Its coefficients cannot cancel because the positions are distinct.
Consequently no such product lies in the scalar group `C`, and no term
containing it contributes an identity coefficient after multiplication by
`z`. Only the empty product in `(QJM14)` contributes. Since
`tau(z)=1/9`,

```text
tau(zP)=1/36,                 tau(zG)=1/72.              (QJM15)
```

Summing over `e_0,e_1` gives `(QJM6)`.

The first router `g_1` fixes the support of `H`, hence commutes with
`E`. Since `G<=E`,

```text
e_2g_1GR=Ee_2g_1GR.
```

Its range projection is below `Ee_2`. Moreover
```text
||e_2g_1GR||_2^2<=||GR||_2^2=tau(RG)=1/36.
```
But `G` and `R` need not commute, so `GR` is not a source
projection and this energy does not determine the initial polar-support
trace. No Hall lower bound follows from the overlap number alone. The
finite complementary-sign packet constructed next supplies an exact
countermodel for the natural common-router recombination.

## Identification of the generated group

Let `J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>`. The coefficients `S,T` generate `J`, and
`Q=1-ST` belongs to it. The scalar group `K` moves the roots `U,V`
to every ordered position. Root additivity and three-index commutators
then generate every `J`-labelled elementary root. Hence

```text
<K,H,U,V>=EL_28(J).                                     (QJM16)
```

The reverse containment is immediate. Since `J` has a finite unital ring
presentation, the Krstic--McCool theorem makes this group finitely
presented.

The ideal generated by `Q` is the finitary locally finite radical of
the Jacobson algebra. The established finite-dimensional representation
theorem for `EL_28(J)` kills every elementary root labelled by this
ideal. In particular every finite-dimensional unitary representation
sends

```text
H,                 C_1=[H,U],                 W=[C_1,V]
```

to the identity. The head is nonidentity in the canonical group trace,
so no finite quotient or finite regular representation of this whole
packet can retain its canonical carrier. This is precisely where the
one-letter residual-finiteness proof stops applying.

The result does not decide hyperlinearity. The canonical nested polar exists, and its qutrit overlap energies are
`1/9,1/18,1/36`; these are not ranks of the cut-down polar supports.
A successful continuation must combine complementary sign projections
with distinct routers and compute those support ranks, not infer them
from Hilbert--Schmidt energy.
