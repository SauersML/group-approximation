## Setting

The notation is that of `two-cell-core-deletions-need-a-unit-fox-coefficient` and
`two-cell-ab-nonzero-admissible-needs-g-in-conj-closure`. A **configuration** consists of:
- a word `w ∈ F(x,y)` that is cyclically reduced and not a proper power;
- a finite folded `{x,y}`-graph `Γ` and vertices `v_0 ≠ v_1`, where `w` reads a closed path at
  each of them;
- `H ⊂ G = ⟨x, y | w⟩`, the image of `π_1(Γ, v_0)`.

Put `C = Γ ∪ e_0 ∪ e_1`, with `e_j` the `w`-cell attached along the path at `v_j`. It maps to the
cover `X_H`. When `Γ` embeds in the Schreier graph of `H`, the map is an embedding, and `C` is a
subcomplex of `X_H` with two distinct 2-cells. Item 4 of (C1) and of (C2) shows that it embeds in
both examples. `C` is a **two-cell core** when, in addition, `π_1(C) → G` is injective.

The remaining notation:
- `B` is the basis of `π_1(Γ, v_0)` from a spanning tree, and `u_j` is the `B`-word of `e_j`;
- `ε = 1` (both cells read `w`), and `g = t_1` is the tree path from `v_0` to `v_1`;
- `c_x = g ∂_x u_0 − ∂_x u_1 ∈ ZG`;
- `c^ab` is the image of `c` in `Z[G^ab] = Z[s^±, t^±]`, with `x ↦ t` and `y ↦ s`.

The letters `a, b, c, …` name basis elements of `B`. The row is always written with a coordinate
index, as `c_x` or `c^ab_x`, and the (E5) integers appear only as the pair `(a, b)`.

For a character `χ: G → k^*` over any field `k`, `c^χ` factors through `c^ab`. By the
Nullstellensatz, some character kills `c` if and only if the coordinates of `c^ab` generate a proper
ideal of `Z[s^±, t^±]`.

## Statement

**(C2) The rank-5 configuration.**
- The word is `w = y x y⁻¹ x⁻² y² x y⁻¹ x⁻¹ y⁻¹ x y⁻² x y x⁻¹ y` (`yxYXXyyxYXYxYYxyXy`, length 18).
- `Γ` has vertices `0, …, 4` and edges
  - `x: 0→0, 1→1, 2→3, 3→4`;
  - `y: 0→1, 1→2, 2→0, 3→3, 4→4`.
- Take `v_0 = 3` and `v_1 = 0`, so `g = x⁻¹ y`.
- The basis `B` consists of:
  - `a = x⁻¹yxy⁻¹x`, `b = x⁻¹y³x`, `c = x⁻¹y⁻¹xyx`;
  - `d = y`, `e = xyx⁻¹`.

Then:
1. The `B`-words are
   `u_0 = d e⁻¹ b c b⁻¹ a⁻¹ d⁻² e d` and `u_1 = b c b⁻¹ a⁻² b d⁻¹ c b⁻¹ d`.
   Here `[u_0] = p = −a* + c*` is primitive and `[u_1] = 2p`. This is the (E5) normal form with
   `(a, b) = (1, 2)`, so `ab ≠ 0` and `|εa − b| = 1`.
2. Up to the unit `s⁻¹t⁻¹`, `c^ab` is the row
   `(s(t+1−s), (t−s)(st−s+1), s³(s²−st−1), s³+s²−t−1, 1−s²)`.
   Moreover `(1+s) c^ab_a + s⁻² c^ab_c = 1`. So no character of `G` over any field kills `c`.
3. `g ∈ P = ⟨g^k H g^{-k}⟩`. In fact `g = c⁻¹ · (g d⁻¹ g⁻¹) · d²` already in `F(x,y)`.
4. `g ∉ H`. The permutations `x = (2 3 4 5)(6 7 8)` and `y = (0 1 2)(5 6 7)` of `{0,…,8}` satisfy
   `w = 1`. They extend `Γ`, and `3^g = 0 ≠ 3`.

**(C1) The rank-4 configuration.**
- The word is `w = yXyXyxYxYXXyyxxYXyXyxYxYYYXXyyxYYx` (length 34).
- `Γ` has vertices `0, …, 3` and edges
  - `x: 0→0, 2→1, 3→2`;
  - `y: 0→1, 1→0, 2→2, 3→3`.
- Take `v_0 = 0`, `v_1 = 1`, `g = y`, and the basis `a = x`, `b = y²`, `c = yx⁻¹yxy⁻¹`,
  `d = yx⁻²yx²y⁻¹`.

Then:
1. `[u_1] = 2[u_0]`, with `[u_0]` primitive.
2. `s⁻²t c^ab_a − s⁻¹t c^ab_b − s⁻¹t⁻¹ c^ab_d = 1`, so no character kills `c`.
3. `g = a (g⁻¹ c g) a⁻¹ ∈ P`.
4. `g ∉ H`, by the index-4 action `x ↦ (1 3 2)`, `y ↦ (0 1)`.
5. The degree-3 permutation representation `x ↦ (0 1 2)`, `y ↦ (1 2)` over `F_2` kills `c`: the
   block row has rank 2 < 3. So `c` is not unimodular over `ZG`, and (C1) is not admissible even if
   it is a core.

**Consequence (the step reduces to one explicit core question).** The open step says: "every
`ab ≠ 0` degree-one two-cell core has a character `χ` with `c^χ = 0`." By the Nullstellensatz, this
is equivalent to saying that no such core has `c^ab` generating the unit ideal. So the step implies
that neither `C` of (C1) nor `C` of (C2) is a core. Conversely, if `C` of (C2) is a core, it
is a degree-one `ab ≠ 0` two-cell core that passes all of the following:
- (N2) `g ∈ P`, with `g ∉ H` as (U1) requires;
- the character half of (N3);
- the abelian shadow of (N2), `[g] ∈ im(H_1(H) → H_1(G))`. This holds because `H` maps onto
  `G^ab`: `d ↦ s` and `a ↦ t`.

So no argument that uses `c^ab` alone can prove the step. That rules out the Alexander polynomial
under `s ↦ −s` and every other specialization of `(s, t)`. Any proof must use the
`π_1`-injectivity of `C` beyond its abelian data.

## Evidence (computational, not part of the ESTABLISHED statement)

All numbers below are exact integer or finite-field linear algebra. Tier: script output, not
independently replayed.

**(C2) survives every finite permutation quotient tried.** Take each transitive permutation
representation `G → S_k` with `k ≤ 7`: 3, 5, 17, 42, 191 and 818 of them for `k = 2, …, 7`, one `x`
per cycle type. For each of them the block row `(ρ(c_x))` has full rank over `F_p` for
`p ∈ {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 10007}` (`reptest.py`).

Now let `Q` be the image group, with `|Q| ≤ 400`. The orders that occur are
`2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 20, 21, 24, 36, 42`, over 1064 representations. For every such `Q`, `c` is
unimodular over `Z[Q]`, by the Hermite normal form of the regular representation (`zq.py`). No
finite quotient found so far obstructs admissibility of (C2).

**Census (`census2.py`).** This samples fiber-product configurations, as in (A) of
`degree-one-two-cell-cores-with-ab-nonzero-exist`, with `λ ∈ {2, 3/2, 4/3}` and `|w| ≤ 80`. It
decides unimodularity of `c^ab` exactly: a Gröbner basis over `Q`, an integer of the ideal from
iterated resultants, then Gröbner bases mod each prime factor of that integer. The two
5-vertex runs stopped at the 1200 s limit.

- In every unimodular case `L = im(H → G^ab)` is all of `Z²`. So the kills from characters that
  are trivial on `H` (the `Q = ⟨ḡ⟩` case of (N1)) cannot occur there.
- Of the three rank-5 unimodular rows:
  - (C2) survives up to degree 7;
  - a length-74 one survives up to degree 5;
  - a length-44 one is killed by a degree-5 representation over `F_3`.

**Pattern.** Every rank-3 configuration dies by a character over `F_3` or `F_5`, and so does the
rank-3 core of `degree-one-two-cell-cores-with-ab-nonzero-exist`. Unimodular `c^ab` first appears
at rank 4.

## What is open

1. **Is `C` of (C2) a core?** If it is, the step is false. `C` would then be a degree-one `ab ≠ 0`
   two-cell core that no character kills, with `g ∈ P`. It would be the first candidate for
   relative defect `≥ 1`, and admissibility would reduce to unimodularity of `c` over `ZG`.
   - The finite-cover method of `degree-one-two-cell-cores-with-ab-nonzero-exist` does not apply.
     It adds the missing 2-cells of a finite cover one at a time, by Brodskii–Howie, and that needs
     `(#extra 2-cells) ≤ (#extra free generators)`, that is `m − 2 ≤ m − r + 1`, so `r ≤ 3`.
   - For (C2), `r = 5`. The smallest cover containing `Γ` has index `m = 9`: none exists for
     `m = 5, …, 8`, and there are 48 extensions at `m = 9` (`ext.py`). That is 7 extra 2-cells
     against 5 extra generators.
   - A Magnus–Moldavanskii analysis is the natural route. Put `y_i = x^i y x^{-i}`. Then
     `G = HNN(G_0, x)` with `G_0 = ⟨y_{-1}, y_0, y_1 | y_1^{-1} V y_1 = y_0^{-2}⟩`, where
     `V = y_{-1}^2 y_0^{-1} y_{-1}^{-1} y_0^{-2}`.
   - In these letters `H = ⟨y_0, y_1, y_{-1}^3, τ⟩`, with `τ = c = y_{-1}^{-1} y_0 x`.
   - `K = ⟨y_0, y_1, y_{-1}^3⟩ ≤ G_0` is the HNN extension of the free group
     `K_1 = ⟨y_0, y_{-1}^3, y_{-1} y_0 y_{-1}⟩` along `V ↔ y_0^{-2}`.
   - But `τ y_0 τ^{-1} ∉ K`, so `H ∩ G_0` is strictly larger than `K`, and computing it needs a
     folding in the graph of groups.
2. **Rank 3.** Prove that for `r = 3` some character always kills `c`. In rank 3 the kernel of
   `J^ab` is free of rank 1, so `c^ab = φ ν` with `ν` the primitive vector of 2×2 minors. This is
   the range where cores are provable, and the census finds no exception there.
3. **Unimodularity of (C2) over `ZG`.** A certificate `Σ c_x d_x = 1` can be checked in `ZF`
   modulo the two-sided ideal of `w − 1`. The abelian certificate `(1+s) c_a + s⁻² c_c = 1`
   is the starting point.
