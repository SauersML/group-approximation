# Proof

Notation as in the claim. `[CUR]` is `fpbs-ray-code-deaths-depend-only-on-the-current`, `[DINI]` is
`fpbs-dini-boundary-class-diagonal-dissipative`, `[TC]` is `fpbs-zero-entropy-accumulation-needs-tail-collapse`.
`[LY]` is Ledrappier--Young, *The metric entropy of diffeomorphisms*, Ann. of Math. 122 (1985), Parts I and II.
`[EL]` is Einsiedler--Lindenstrauss, *Diagonal actions on locally homogeneous spaces*, Clay Math. Proc. 10 (2010), §7.

## Step 0. Reductions and the flow model

**(0.a) Torsion-free model.** By Selberg's lemma pick a torsion-free normal `Gamma' < Gamma` of finite index. A
`Gamma`-invariant current is `Gamma'`-invariant. `M = Gamma' \ T^1 X` is a compact smooth manifold and `phi_t` is a
smooth Anosov flow on it. Put `m = m_mu`, the image of `mu x dt` on `M`, normalized to a probability. `m` is finite
because `mu x dt` is Radon on `T^1 X` and a fundamental domain for `Gamma'` is relatively compact. For two choices
`Gamma'`, `Gamma''`, the measures on `Gamma' \ T^1X` and `(Gamma' ∩ Gamma'') \ T^1 X` are related by a finite-to-one
factor map, so they have the same entropy. Every conclusion below is about `mu` on `d^2 X`, so it does not depend on
`Gamma'`.

**(0.b) Product structure.** In Hopf coordinates, `mu x dt` is the lift of `m`, and `phi_s(theta, eta, t) =
(theta, eta, t+s)`. The strong stable leaf of `v = (theta, eta, t)` is `W^ss(v) = {(theta', eta, t) : theta' ≠ eta}`.
The strong unstable leaf is `W^uu(v) = {(theta, eta', t') : eta' ≠ theta}`, with `t'` fixed by the Busemann function
at `theta`. With the leaf Riemannian metrics `d_ss`, `d_uu` there is `C_0` with
`d_ss(phi_s w, phi_s v) <= C_0 e^{-s} d_ss(w, v)` for `w ∈ W^ss(v)`, `s >= 0` (rank one, exponents `1` and possibly
`2`), and the symmetric statement for `W^uu` and `s <= 0`. For fixed `theta' ≠ eta`,
`d_ss((theta', eta, t+s), (theta, eta, t+s)) -> 0` as `s -> infinity`.

**(0.c) Leaf conditionals are the current's conditionals.** Let `U` be a flow box in `M` of small diameter, with an
isometric lift `U~`, and let `xi` be a measurable partition of `U` whose atoms lie in local strong stable plaques, so
that `xi(v)` is contained in `{(theta', eta(v), t(v))}`. Disintegrating `mu x dt` first over `(eta, t)` gives
conditional `mu^eta` on the slice `{(theta', eta, t)}`, up to normalization. If `xi(v)` contains a relatively open
neighbourhood of `v` in its slice for a.e. `v`, then, since disjoint relatively open sets in a slice are countably
many, the conditional of `m` on `xi(v)` is the normalized restriction of `mu^{eta(v)}` to `xi(v)` (read in the
`theta'` coordinate). The same holds for unstable plaques and `mu_theta`.

## Step 1. A thin-boundary finite partition

Let `d` be the Riemannian distance on `M`, and `eps_0` an expansivity constant of `phi`: if
`d(phi_t v, phi_t v') < eps_0` for all `t ∈ R`, then `v' = phi_s v` with `|s| < eps_1`, where `eps_1` is small
(Bowen--Walters). Take `eps < eps_0 / 4` and less than a third of the injectivity radius of the flow boxes.

For `x ∈ M`, `r ↦ F_x(r) = m(B(x, r))` is monotone, so it is differentiable at Lebesgue-a.e. `r`. Choose finitely
many centers `x_j` and radii `r_j ∈ (eps/2, eps)` at which `F_{x_j}` is differentiable, with the balls covering
`M`. Then there is `K` with

`m({y : |d(x_j, y) - r_j| <= s}) <= K s` for all `s > 0` and all `j`,

because `F_{x_j}(r_j + s) - F_{x_j}(r_j - s) <= (F'_{x_j}(r_j) + 1) 2 s` for small `s`, and the left side is at most `1`.
Let `P` be the finite partition generated by the balls. Every atom lies in one ball, so it has diameter `< 2 eps`
and lies in a flow box `U_j` with a fixed lift `U_j~`. `dP ⊂ ∪_j {d(x_j, .) = r_j}`, so

`m(N_s(dP)) <= K' s` for all `s > 0`, and `m(dP) = 0`.

Here `N_s` is the open `s`-neighbourhood. For `y` in an atom `A` and `d(y, y') < d(y, dA)`, `y' ∈ A`, since the
Riemannian ball `B(y, d(y, dA))` is path connected and misses `dA`.

## Step 2. Proof of Theorem 1

Assume `h(m) = 0`, where `h` is the entropy of `phi = phi_1`.

**(2.a) The future determines the backward endpoint.** Let `P^+ = sigma(phi^{-n} P : n >= 0)` and
`P^± = sigma(phi^{-n} P : n ∈ Z)`. Since `h(phi, P) = H(P | sigma(phi^{-n}P : n >= 1)) <= h(m) = 0`, `P` is
measurable for `sigma(phi^{-n}P : n >= 1)` mod `m`. Applying `phi^k` and inducting gives `P^± = P^+` mod `m`. No
ergodicity is used.

Define `Theta : M -> dX` by `Theta(v) =` backward endpoint of the lift of `v` in `U_j~`, where `P(v) ⊂ U_j`. If `v`,
`v'` have the same two-sided `P`-name, then `d(phi_n v, phi_n v') < 2 eps` for all `n ∈ Z`. By uniform continuity
of `phi` on `[0, 1]` (shrink `eps` if needed), `d(phi_t v, phi_t v') < eps_0` for all `t`, so `v' = phi_s v` with
`|s| < eps_1`. Both lie in `U_j`, and the lifts lie on one lifted flow line, because `U_j~` is embedded. So
`Theta(v') = Theta(v)`. Hence `Theta` is `P^±`-measurable, so it is `P^+`-measurable mod `m`. There are a Borel `Phi`
and a conull Borel set `G ⊂ M` with `Theta(v) = Phi(P^+`-name of `v)` for `v ∈ G`.

**(2.b) Plaques inside future atoms.** By Step 1 and invariance,
`sum_n m(phi^{-n} N_{C_0 e^{-n}}(dP)) <= sum_n K' C_0 e^{-n} < infinity`. By Borel--Cantelli there is, for a.e. `v`,
an `N(v)` with `d(phi_n v, dP) >= C_0 e^{-n}` for `n >= N(v)`. Also `d(phi_n v, dP) > 0` for all `n` a.e., since
`m(dP) = 0`. Put

`delta(v) = min(eps, min_{n < N(v)} d(phi_n v, dP) / (2 C_0)) > 0`.

(Taking `delta(v) <= eps` keeps `W^ss_{delta(v)}` of a lift inside the lifted box, since distinct lifts of one
point are more than `3 eps` apart.)

If `w ∈ W^ss(v)` with `d_ss(w, v) < delta(v)`, then for every `n >= 0`,
`d(phi_n w, phi_n v) <= d_ss(phi_n w, phi_n v) <= C_0 e^{-n} delta(v) < d(phi_n v, dP)` (for `n >= N(v)` use
`delta(v) < 1`; for `n < N(v)` use the second term in the minimum), so `phi_n w` lies in the atom of `phi_n v`. Hence
`W^ss_{delta(v)}(v) ⊂ P^+(v)`, the `P^+`-atom of `v`.

**(2.c) Dirac on plaques.** Lift to `T^1 X` and use Fubini in `mu x dt = ∫∫ mu^eta(d theta) dt d nu_1(eta)`. The lift
`G~` of `G` is conull, so for `nu_1`-a.e. `eta` and a.e. `t`, `mu^eta`-a.e. `theta'` has `(theta', eta, t) ∈ G~`.
Fix such `(eta, t)` and a point `v = (theta, eta, t) ∈ G~` with `delta(v) > 0`. If `v' = (theta', eta, t) ∈ G~` and
`d_ss(v', v) < delta(v)`, then `v'` lies in `P^+(v)` by (2.b). Both are in one atom of `P` and so in one lifted box,
and `Theta` reads their backward endpoints `theta`, `theta'`. By (2.a),
`theta' = Phi(P^+`-name of `v') = Phi(P^+`-name of `v) = theta`. Therefore

(D) `mu^eta({theta' : d_ss((theta', eta, t), v) < delta(v)} \ {theta}) = 0`

for `mu x dt`-a.e. `v = (theta, eta, t)`. The function `delta~(v) = sup{delta : (D) holds with delta}` is Borel,
`Gamma'`-invariant and positive a.e., so it descends to `M`.

**(2.d) Recurrence spreads the plaque over the leaf.** For rational `delta_0 > 0` let `A = {delta~ >= delta_0}`.
By Poincare recurrence for `phi` on the probability space `(M, m)`, a.e. `v ∈ A` has `phi_{n_k} v ∈ A` along some
`n_k -> infinity`. Since `A` increases to a conull set as `delta_0` decreases, a.e. `v` has such a sequence for some
`delta_0`. Now `phi_{n_k} v = (theta, eta, t + n_k)` carries the same `mu^eta`, and (D) at `phi_{n_k} v` says that
`mu^eta` has no mass except `theta` in `{theta' : d_ss((theta', eta, t+n_k), (theta, eta, t+n_k)) < delta_0}`. By
(0.b) these sets exhaust `dX \ {eta}` as `k -> infinity`. So `mu^eta = c delta_theta`.

**(2.e) Both directions.** `phi_{-1}` has the same entropy `0`. Running (2.a)--(2.d) for the reversed flow, with
`W^uu` in place of `W^ss`, gives that `mu_theta` is a single atom for `nu^-_1`-a.e. `theta`. So `mu` is carried by
`{(f(eta), eta)}`, where `f(eta)` is the atom of `mu^eta`. `f` is Borel (it is the barycenter of the normalized
`mu^eta` in a chart) and `Gamma`-equivariant by uniqueness of disintegrations. It is injective off a null set,
because a second preimage of a `nu^-`-typical `theta` would give `mu_theta` two atoms. Also `f_* nu_1` is the
backward marginal, so `f_* nu = nu^-`.

**(2.f) The general current.** The entropy of the ergodic component through `v` is a flow-invariant Borel function,
so `F_0` and `F_+` are invariant. `m|_{F_0}` has entropy `0`, and its current is `mu_0`. Apply (2.a)--(2.e) to it.

(Alternative for (2.a)--(2.d): lift `m` to the `M`-invariant measure on `Gamma' \ G` (an isometric extension, so the
entropy is still `0`) and apply [EL, Thm 7.9], which says the leafwise measures on the stable horospherical subgroup
are trivial a.e. when the entropy vanishes. By (0.c) these leafwise measures are the measures `mu^eta`. The direct
argument above is self-contained and shows the mechanism.)

## Step 3. Proof of Theorem 2

Let `m_omega` be an ergodic component of `m` with entropy `h_omega > 0`, and `mu^omega` its current. `phi_1` is a
`C^infinity` diffeomorphism of the compact manifold `M`, and its unstable manifolds are the strong unstable leaves
`W^uu` (the flow direction has exponent `0`). By [LY, Part II, Theorems C' and F], the conditional measures of
`m_omega` on a measurable partition subordinate to `W^uu` are exact dimensional for the leaf Riemannian metric, of
dimension `delta^u_omega`, and `h_omega = sum_i chi_i (delta_i - delta_{i-1}) <= chi_max delta^u_omega`, the sum
running over the positive exponents. So `delta^u_omega >= h_omega / chi_max > 0`.

By (0.c) these conditionals are normalized restrictions of `mu^omega_theta`, read through the forward-endpoint map
`W^uu(v) -> dX \ {theta}`. In the upper half-space model with `theta = infinity`, this map is the identity of the
nilpotent group `N`. On compact sets the leaf Riemannian metric `d_R` and the Cygan metric `d_C` satisfy
`d_R <= c d_C`, so `B_C(x, r) ⊂ B_R(x, c r)` and the local dimension for `d_C` is at least the one for `d_R`. On
compact subsets of `dX \ {theta}`, `d_C` is bi-Lipschitz to the standard visual metric of `dX`, and that metric is
bi-Holder to any visual metric of the Cayley graph (the boundary map of the orbit quasi-isometry is quasisymmetric
between uniformly perfect spaces). So for `nu^-`-a.e. `theta`, `mu^omega_theta` has lower local dimension at least
`a_omega > 0` a.e., for a fixed visual metric, where `a_omega` is `delta^u_omega` times a Holder exponent.

**Sets of positive measure.** Let `E ⊂ dX` with `nu[mu_+](E) > 0`. Then `nu[mu^omega](E) > 0` for a set of
components of positive weight, and for one of them `mu^omega_theta(E) > 0` for some `theta` at which
`mu^omega_theta` has lower local dimension `>= a_omega` a.e. The mass distribution principle (a subset `E'` with
`mu^omega_theta(E') > 0` and `mu^omega_theta(B(x, r)) <= r^{a_omega - e}` for `x ∈ E'` and `r < r_0` has positive
`H^{a_omega - e}` measure) gives `dim_H E >= a_omega > 0`.

**Local dimension of a dominated measure.** Let `C << nu[mu_+]` and suppose `C` has lower local dimension `0` at
`C`-a.e. point. For `s > 0` put `E_s = {x : lower local dim of C at x < s}`, conull for `C`. For `x ∈ E_s` there are
arbitrarily small `r` with `C(B(x, r)) >= r^s`. The `5r`-covering lemma (valid in every metric space for balls of
bounded radius) gives a disjoint countable family `B(x_i, r_i)` with `E_s ⊂ ∪ B(x_i, 5 r_i)`, so
`H^s_{10 r_0}(E_s) <= 10^s sum_i C(B(x_i, r_i)) <= 10^s C(dX)`. So `dim_H E_s <= s`, and `E_0 = ∩_s E_s` is a
`C`-conull set of Hausdorff dimension `0`. But `C(E_0) > 0` gives `nu[mu_+](E_0) > 0`, so `dim_H E_0 > 0`, a
contradiction. So `C` has positive lower local dimension on a set of positive `C`-measure. This proves Theorem 2.

## Step 4. Proof of Theorem 3

Take a (UQ) ray code with finite invariant `rho^#` and current `mu_R` ([CUR], Step 1).

**(4.a) `mu_R` is a current in the sense of the claim.** It is `Gamma`-invariant and Radon ([CUR]). Its forward class
is `nu`. So Step 0 applies, and `mu_R = mu_0 + mu_+` with forward classes `nu_0`, `nu_+`, both `Gamma`-quasi-
invariant, and `nu = [nu_0 + nu_+]`.

**(4.b) Ergodic splitting of `C`.** `C` is ergodic and `Gamma`-quasi-invariant. The Lebesgue decomposition of `C`
against `nu_0` is `Gamma`-invariant (both are quasi-invariant), so `C << nu_0` or `C ⊥ nu_0`, and in the second case
`C << nu_+`.

**(4.c) Positive-entropy side.** If `C << nu_+`, Theorem 2 gives `C` positive lower local dimension on a set of
positive measure. By [DINI] Theorem C, `C` is absolutely continuous to one finite Dini measure, and by [DINI]
Theorem B the crossing diagonal of `Z` is totally dissipative and `Z` is not PDR.

**(4.d) Zero-entropy side.** Suppose `C << nu_0`. Let `F_0~ ⊂ d^2 X` be the lift of `F_0`, and `X_0` the set of
positions `p` with `(zeta^{-#}(p), zeta^#(p)) ∈ F_0~` and with `zeta^#(p)` not an atom of `nu_0` (the atoms form a
countable `Gamma`-invariant set, and `C` is nonatomic, so it is `C`-null). `X_0` is `T^#`-invariant because
`(zeta^{-#}, zeta^#)(T^# p) = a_0(p)^{-1} (zeta^{-#}, zeta^#)(p)`. If `rho^#(X_0) = 0` then `nu_0` is atomic off a
null set, which contradicts `C << nu_0`. The restricted code on `X_0` has a finite invariant measure, its current is
the restriction of `mu_0`, and its forward class `nu_0'` is nonatomic with `C << nu_0'`. By Theorem 1 its
conditionals are single atoms, so (LF_mu) holds, and its backward class `f_* nu_0'` is nonatomic because `f` is
injective a.e. [CUR] Theorem 1 then gives total dissipativity of the diagonal of every imp action with a boundary map
into `nu_0'`, in particular of `Z`, and no ergodic such `Z` is PDR.

In both cases `Z` is not PDR. The hypotheses never used the entropy of `rho^#`, the spelling or the tail structure.

## Step 5. Proofs of the corollaries

**Corollary 4.** Replace `T^#` by a power `T^{#N}` so that the Busemann increment `tau_N(p)` along the line of `p`
over `N` steps lies in `[1, C_1]` (possible by (UQ), and entropy stays `0`). Let `v(p)` be the unit vector on the
line `(zeta^{-#}(p), zeta^#(p))` at the projection of `o = 1`. Then `v(T^{#N} p) = P_p(N)^{-1} phi_{tau_N(p)} v(p)`,
so `(p, s) -> Gamma' g phi_s v(p)` on the suspension of the finite skew extension `X^# x (Gamma' \ Gamma)` with roof
`tau_N` is a factor map of flows onto `(M, m')`. Its image measure is in the class of `m_{mu_R}` (both are sums of
translates of `ell_*(f rho^#)` with `f > 0`, [CUR] Step 1). The suspension flow has entropy
`h(T^{#N}) / ∫ tau_N = 0` (Abramov), a finite extension adds none, and factors do not increase entropy. So
`h(mu_R) = 0`, and Theorem 1 applies to `mu_R`. By [TC] Theorem 3, essential accumulation at zero entropy forces
infinitely many past endpoints over `nu`-positively many `eta`, which contradicts one atom per endpoint. So tail
collapse with separated pasts does not occur. For the odometer cell-flip codes the invariant measure is a
zero-entropy extension of the odometer, so the same applies.

**Corollary 5.** If `mu ~ C x C` on `d^2 X` and `C` is nonatomic, a graph `{(f(eta), eta)}` is `C x C`-null by
Fubini. By Theorem 1, `mu_0` is carried by a graph, so `mu_0 = 0` and `mu = mu_+`. Its forward class is `C`, so by
Theorem 2 `C` has positive lower local dimension on a positive set, and [DINI] Theorems C and B finish.

## Remarks

1. **Where the mechanism of [TC] breaks.** [TC] Theorem 3 needs typical futures `(h_k, w_k)` whose paths
   fellow-travel to `eta` and whose pasts differ. In the flow picture these are distinct atoms of one stable
   conditional `mu^eta`. Step 2 shows that a zero-entropy flow measure has no two typical points on one strong stable
   leaf: the forward `P`-name already fixes the backward endpoint on a whole plaque, and recurrence enlarges the
   plaque to the leaf. The symbolic proof in [TC] could not see this, because different S-words can give the same
   flow orbit, and the flow sees only the line.
2. **What the theorem does not cover.** It needs an invariant Radon current. Nonsingular codes without a finite
   invariant measure, and BB classes `C` for which `C x C` carries no equivalent invariant Radon measure, are
   untouched (claim, open statement).
3. **General hyperbolic groups.** Steps 1--2 use only a compact metric flow with expansivity, a local product
   structure and uniform exponential contraction of strong stable sets. They should run on Mineyev's flow space of a
   torsion-free hyperbolic group, but this is not claimed here. Step 3 uses the smooth structure through [LY].
