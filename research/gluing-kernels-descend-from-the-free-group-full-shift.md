## Setting

- `C = {0,1}^N` with Thompson's `V`, and `G_V` its groupoid of germs.
- `Λ` is a finitely generated group, and `X` is any nonempty compact, zero-dimensional, metrizable `Λ`-space. The
  action need not be free, topologically free, minimal or expansive.
- `T_(Λ,X) = G_V × (Λ ⋉ X)` on `Ω = C × X`. **`F(T)` denotes the group of full compact open bisections.** When `T`
  is effective, for instance when `Λ` acts topologically freely, this is the topological full group used in
  `v-times-sft-full-groups-split-into-two-wreath-pieces` and `gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-full-shift`. For
  infinite `Λ` the full shift `A^Λ` is topologically free, so there the two readings agree.
- The pieces and the gluing group are those of `v-times-sft-full-groups-split-into-two-wreath-pieces`, read as
  bisections:
  - `M = V(Λ) = C(C, Λ) ⋊ V`;
  - `N_X = LC(X, V) ⋊ Λ`;
  - `I = V × Λ = M ∩ N_X` (at the bisection level this needs no freeness);
  - `Γ~_(Λ,X) = M *_I N_X / ⟨⟨[M_0, N_1]⟩⟩`, with `π : Γ~ → F(T)` and `K_(Λ,X) = ker π`.
- `π` is onto for every `(Λ, X)`:
  - `F(T)` is generated by transpositions of product pieces (`v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions`,
    whose proof works with full bisections of any ample `H`);
  - each such transposition is a product of `M`- and `N_X`-elements, by the lift formulas of
    `v-times-full-groups-have-local-transposition-presentations`.

  Neither step uses freeness.

## Theorem

1. **Subsystems, with no freeness.** Let `X ⊆ Y` be closed and `Λ`-invariant, and let
   `J = {f ∈ LC(Y, V) : f|_X = 1} ⊴ N_Y`. Then:
   - restriction `r : F(T_Y) → F(T_X)` is onto, with kernel the normal closure of `J`;
   - `Γ~_X = Γ~_Y / ⟨⟨J⟩⟩`;
   - hence **`K_X` is the image of `K_Y`**.

   For topologically free `X` this is items 1–3 of `gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-full-shift`. The only change
   is that "`g` acts trivially on `C × X`" is replaced by "every arrow of `g` over `C × X` is a unit". That makes
   topological freeness unnecessary.
2. **Quotient groups.** Let `Λ = Λ~ / R`, with `R` the normal closure of a set `R_0`, and view a `Λ`-space `X` as a
   `Λ~`-space. Then:
   - the groupoid quotient induces a surjection `p : F(T_(Λ~,X)) → F(T_(Λ,X))`, whose kernel is the normal closure
     of `{r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`. Here `r|_([0])` is the label `r` on the column `[0] × X`;
   - `Γ~_(Λ,X) = Γ~_(Λ~,X) / ⟨⟨r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0⟩⟩`;
   - hence **`K_(Λ,X)` is the image of `K_(Λ~,X)`**.
3. **Rank universality.** Let `Λ = F_S / R`, and let `X ⊆ A^Λ` be any subshift, with `A` finite. Pull back along the
   quotient: `ι(x) = x ∘ (F_S → Λ)` embeds `A^Λ` into `A^(F_S)`, equivariantly for `F_S`. Then
   `Γ~_(Λ,X) = Γ~_(F_S, A^(F_S)) / ⟨⟨ J ∪ {r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0} ⟩⟩`,
   with `J` the conditioned elements off `ι(X)`. So **`K_(Λ,X)` is the image of `K_(F_S, A^(F_S))`**.

## Corollaries

- **(a) One statement per rank and alphabet.** Write FSG(F_n, A) for the statement of
  `gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-full-shift` at the free group: `K_(F_n, A^(F_n))` is the normal closure of
  finitely many elements.
  - FSG(F_n, A) gives P2′b for **every** subshift over `A` over **every** group with `n` generators: rigid or not,
    minimal or not, free or not.
  - In particular, FSG(Λ, A) for every such `Λ` follows from FSG(F_n, A).
  - For exactness this is an equivalence. `K_(Λ,X) = 1` for all such `(Λ, X)` iff `K_(F_n, A^(F_n)) = 1`, since
    the full shift over `F_n` is one of the instances.
- **(b) For the master route.** Route 2 of the v5 synthesis, `(★′) ∧ FSG(Λ₀, A) ⇒ BH`, now reads
  `(★′) ∧ FSG(F_n, A) ⇒ BH`, where:
  - `n` is the number of generators of the fp overgroup `Λ₀`;
  - `A` is the alphabet of the (★′) SFT.

  The geometry of `Λ₀` (non-amenability, one-endedness, Kazhdan) and its relators play no part in the gluing.
  They enter only through `M = V(Λ₀)`, which is fp when `Λ₀` is, and through `N_X` (P2′a). For fp `Λ₀`, item 2
  adds only the finitely many relators `r|_([0])`, `r ∈ R_0`.
- **(c) Finite models are free necessary tests of exactness.**
  - A finite quotient `Q` of `F_n`, acting on a finite set that is a subshift over `A`, is an instance.
  - If exactness fails there, it fails for `F_n` and `A`.
  - The smallest instance is below. It is an image of `K_(Z, {0,1}^Z)`, so exactness of FSG(Z, 2) implies it. If it
    fails, exactness fails for every `F_n` with `n ≥ 1` and every alphabet with at least two letters.
- **(d) Robust to enlarging the relators.** Items 1–3 hold verbatim for gluing groups with further relators added.
  The relators must be written in `M ∪ N_(A^(F_S))`, and their images are the corresponding relators downstairs.
  If exactness needs more relators than disjoint-cone commutation, the right gluing presentation can be designed
  once, over `A^(F_n)`, and then holds for every `(Λ, X)`.

## The smallest finite test

Take `Λ = Z/2` acting on its two points `{x_1, x_2}`, that is, `Z` acting on the period-two orbit of `{0,1}^Z`
through `Z → Z/2`.
- `F(T) = V_(2,2) ≅ V` acts on `C × {x_1, x_2}`, and `s` swaps the copies.
- `M = V(Z/2)` is exactly the centralizer of the fixed-point-free involution `s`. An element commuting with `s`
  has the form `(c, x) ↦ (v(c), f(c)·x)`.
- `N = (V × V) ⋊ ⟨s⟩` is the stabilizer of the partition into the two copies.

So exactness of FSG(Z, 2) implies the following statement about Thompson's group alone:

> `V` is the amalgam of the centralizer of a fixed-point-free involution `s` and the stabilizer of a two-piece
> partition swapped by `s`, over their intersection, modulo commutation of `M_0` with `N_1`.

All groups here are finitely presented, so this kernel is finitely normally generated. Only exactness is tested.

**The first relation to check** is an entangled braid. Take incomparable words `u, u'`, and let:
- `A = (1, w) ∈ N`, where `w ∈ V` swaps the cones `[u]` and `[u']`, acting on the copy of `x_2` only;
- `B = s|_([u']) ∈ M`, the local swap of copies over `[u']`.

In `F(T)` their supports share the one piece `[u'] × {x_2}`, so `(AB)^3 = 1`.
- Relations (R1) and (R2) of `v-times-sft-gluing-kernel-is-relatively-perfect` fix how an `M`-element and an
  `N`-element interact in two cases only: when their supports are disjoint, or when one acts on the other's
  support by a single label. The pair `(A, B)` is in neither case.
- The manipulations tried here only move the question around. These are conjugating by `V × Z/2`, rewriting `A`
  as `(w, w)(w, 1)`, and using (R2) for `s|_([u] ∪ [u'])`. Each turns the braid for `(A, B)` into the braid for
  another entangled pair.
- Splitting `[u], [u']` into halves shows that `x = (AB)^3 ∈ K` satisfies `x = x_0 x_1`, where `x_0, x_1` are
  commuting `V`-conjugates of `x`. This is consistent with relative perfectness, and it is not a proof that
  `x = 1`.

In the piece-permutation shadow, `M` and `N` give two dihedral groups of order 8 in `Sym(4)`, meeting in a Klein
four-group. `Sym(4)` is not their amalgam. So exactness must come from the room that splitting into cones
provides, and not from the pieces alone.

**Update (bh-one-relator, 2026-09-18): the braid holds.**
`splitting-kills-the-finite-shadow-parity-of-the-gluing-group` proves `x = 1` in `Γ~`. Finite shadows with 3 to 5 cones
are `Sym(2m) × Z/2`, where the `Z/2` is the parity of copy swaps, and the doubling `x = x_0 x_1` kills it. As a
consequence, every symmetric piece-permutation group lifts to `Γ~`. What remains for exactness of this model is
consistency across partitions.

## Lesson for general BH

The gluing layer of the group route is universal in both variables:
- it descends to closed invariant subsets with no freeness, and
- it descends along quotients of the acting group.

So the one object is the full shift over the free group of the right rank. Nothing about the acting group's
geometry, amenability or relations can help or hurt the gluing. Those features act only through `V(Λ)` and
`N_X`.

This is the precise sense in which P2′b is "combinatorics of V with labels". A proof has to work for `F_n` acting
on `A^(F_n)`. A refutation can be found in a finite model: the first is a question about `V` alone.

## Proof

**A lemma.** Let `π : A → B` be onto with kernel `K`. Let `E ⊆ A` and `F ⊆ B` be subsets with `π(E) = F`, and write
`A' = A/⟨⟨E⟩⟩`, `B' = B/⟨⟨F⟩⟩` and `π' : A' → B'`. Then `ker π'` is the image of `K`.

Since `π` is onto, `π(⟨⟨E⟩⟩) = ⟨⟨F⟩⟩`. If `π'(a⟨⟨E⟩⟩) = 1`, then `π(a) = π(e)` for some `e ∈ ⟨⟨E⟩⟩`, so `a e^-1 ∈ K`
maps to the class of `a`. ∎

**Item 1, the gluing groups.** Restriction `q : N_Y → N_X` is onto:
- every clopen partition of the closed set `X` extends to one of `Y`, so `LC(Y, V) → LC(X, V)` is onto;
- the label group is the same.

Its kernel is `J`, and `J ∩ I = 1`. So `M *_I N_X = (M *_I N_Y) / ⟨⟨J⟩⟩`, by the universal property of the pushout.
Also `q(N_(Y,1)) = N_(X,1)`, so the commutation relators map onto the commutation relators. Hence
`Γ~_X = Γ~_Y / ⟨⟨J⟩⟩`.

**Item 1, the full groups.** `r` is a homomorphism, because `C × X` is invariant.
- *Onto.* `F(T_X)` is generated by `M` and `N_X`, and both lift.
- *Kernel, first step.* Let `r(g) = 1`, and write `g` in normal form: pieces
  `B_(u → u') × (λ, P)`, with source rectangles `[u] × P` partitioning `Ω`. If `P ∩ X ≠ ∅`, the restricted
  arrows `(uw ↦ u'w, (λ, x))` with `x ∈ P ∩ X` are units, so `u = u'` and `λ = 1`, and the piece is a unit piece.
- So `g` fixes the union `U` of those pieces with unit arrows, and it maps the complement `W` onto itself. `W` is
  a finite union of rectangles `[u_i] × P_i` with `P_i ∩ X = ∅`. Put `Z = ⋃ P_i`, a clopen set disjoint from `X`.
  Then `g ∈ F(G_V × (Λ ⋉ Y)|_Z)`, which is generated by its transpositions
  (`v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions` for the ample groupoid `(Λ ⋉ Y)|_Z`).
- *Kernel, second step.* After splitting in the `C` direction, each such transposition is a commuting product of
  elementary ones `t`, which exchange `[u] × P` and `[u'] × λP` with `P, λP ⊆ Z`, `u, u' ≠ ∅`.
  - If `u, u'` are comparable, the rectangles are disjoint only if `P ∩ λP = ∅`. Then `n_1 = v^[λP]` fixes
    `[u] × P`, where `v ∈ V` maps `[u']` by prefix onto a cone `[u'']` with `u'' ⊥ u` (such a cone exists since
    `u ≠ ∅`). So `n_1 t n_1^-1` is elementary with incomparable addresses.
  - If `u ⊥ u'`, then `t = m^-1 n m`, with `m = λ|_([u]) ∈ M` and `n = (u u')^[λP] ∈ LC(Y, V)`. Evaluate on the six
    regions `[u]` or `[u']` or elsewhere, times `P` or `λP` or elsewhere. Since `λP ⊆ Z` misses `X`, `n ∈ J`.
- Hence `ker r ⊆ ⟨⟨J⟩⟩_(F(T_Y))`. The reverse inclusion is clear.

**Item 1, conclusion.** `π_Y` is onto (Setting). Apply the lemma with `E = J`.

**Item 2, the gluing groups.** `V(Λ~) → V(Λ)` is onto, with kernel `C(C, R)`. This kernel is the normal closure in
`V(Λ~)` of `{r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`:
- `v r|_([0]) v^-1 = r|_([w])` for `v ∈ V` mapping `[0]` onto a proper cone `[w]` by prefix;
- `s|_([w]) r|_([w]) s|_([w])^-1 = (s r s^-1)|_([w])` for `s ∈ Λ~`;
- products give every `ρ|_([w])` with `ρ ∈ R`, and `ρ|_(C) = ρ|_([0]) ρ|_([1])`;
- finite products of these give `C(C, R)`.

`LC(X, V) ⋊ Λ~ → LC(X, V) ⋊ Λ` has kernel `R`, acting as global labels. `R` acts trivially on `X`, so it is
normal there, and `R ⊆ C(C, R)` as constants. Also `I_(Λ~) ∩ C(C, R) = R`. So killing `C(C, R)` in `M` kills
exactly `R` in `I` and in `N`, and the commutation relators map onto the commutation relators. Hence
`Γ~_(Λ,X) = Γ~_(Λ~,X) / ⟨⟨r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0⟩⟩`.

**Item 2, the full groups.** The quotient map `T_(Λ~,X) → T_(Λ,X)` is the identity on units, and on arrows it is
`(γ, (s, x)) ↦ (γ, (s R, x))`.
- *Onto.* Pieces lift through any choice of preimages of labels.
- *Kernel.* A full bisection lies in the kernel iff every arrow has unit `G_V`-part and label in `R`. So the
  kernel consists of products of commuting label-only bisections `r|_([u] × P)`, with `r ∈ R`.
- *Each `r|_([u] × P)` lies in the normal closure `𝒩` of `{r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`.*
  - `𝒩` contains every `r|_([w])` with `r ∈ R`, by the computation above, done inside `M ≤ F(T)`.
  - Let `[u], [w]` be disjoint cones, `v ∈ V` the prefix swap of `[u]` and `[w]`, and `n = v^[P] ∈ N`. Then
    `n r|_([u]) n^-1 = r|_(([w] × P) ∪ ([u] × P^c))`, by evaluating arrows on the four regions. So
    `r|_([u])^-1 · n r|_([u]) n^-1 = r^-1|_([u] × P) · r|_([w] × P)` lies in `𝒩`.
  - Modulo `𝒩`, therefore, `r|_([u] × P) ≡ r|_([w] × P)` whenever `u ⊥ w`.
  - For `u ≠ ∅`, pick `w ⊥ u`. Then `u0 ⊥ w` and `u1 ⊥ w`, so
    `r|_([w] × P) ≡ r|_([u] × P) = r|_([u0] × P) · r|_([u1] × P) ≡ r|_([w] × P)^2`.
    So both sides are trivial modulo `𝒩`.
  - For `u = ∅`, split into `[0]` and `[1]`.

**Item 2, conclusion.** `π_(Λ~)` is onto. Apply the lemma with `E = {r|_([0]) : r ∈ R_0}`.

**Item 3.** `ι` is continuous and injective, its image is closed, and
`(t·ι(x))(g) = ι(x)(t^-1 g) = x(t̄^-1 ḡ) = ι(t̄·x)(g)`. So `ι(X)` is a closed `F_S`-invariant subset of `A^(F_S)`,
isomorphic as an `F_S`-space to `X` viewed through `F_S → Λ`. Apply item 1 with `Y = A^(F_S)` and item 2 with
`Λ~ = F_S`. ∎
