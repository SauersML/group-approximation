## Proof

**1.**  An element of `Gamma = Z^3 x| SL_3(Z)` is `(b,A,0)` with
`b in Z^3` and `A in SL_3(Z)`.  For `k in {0,-1,-2}` we have
`A(2^k Z^3) = 2^k Z^3`, since `A` is an integral unimodular matrix, and
`b in Z^3 <= 2^k Z^3`.  So `(b,A,0)` fixes `2^k Z^3` for those `k`, i.e.
it fixes `x_0, x_1, x_2`.  A permutation supported inside
`{x_0,x_1,x_2}` therefore commutes with every element of `Gamma`: on
those three points `Gamma` acts as the identity, and off them `C` does.
Hence `C <= C_G(Gamma)`, and `C = S_3` is finite.

**2.**  `u = a^3` is `(0,I,3)`, dilation by `8`.  Conjugating
`(b,A,0)` gives `(8b, A, 0)`, so
`u Gamma u^-1 = 8Z^3 x| SL_3(Z)`, a subgroup of `Gamma`.  And
`ell = t_(e_1) = (e_1,I,0)` lies in `Gamma`.

**3.**  `u x_0 = 8Z^3`, `u x_1 = 4Z^3`, `u x_2 = 2Z^3`, so `uCu^-1` is
the symmetric group on `Y`; applying `ell` translates by `e_1`, giving
`Z'`.  The two sets are disjoint.  Cosets of different lattice scale are
never equal, so it is enough to compare at each of the scales `2, 4, 8`;
there the two cosets are `2^k Z^3` and `e_1 + 2^k Z^3` with
`k in {1,2,3}`, and `e_1` is not in `2^k Z^3` for `k >= 1`.  Two
permutations with disjoint supports commute, so
`[uCu^-1, ell u C u^-1 ell^-1] = 1`.

**4.**  In `S_3` the commutator of two distinct transpositions is a
three-cycle, and the sign homomorphism puts the derived subgroup inside
`A_3`; since `A_3` is generated by any of its nonidentity elements,
`[C,C] = A_3`.

Items 1--4 are the four hypotheses of
`locally-finite-centralizer-derived-subgroup-is-mf-invisible` — property
(T) of `L = Gamma` being supplied separately by
`affine-semidirect-sl3-source-has-property-t` — together with the
identification of `[C,C]`.
