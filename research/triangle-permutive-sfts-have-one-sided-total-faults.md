**ESTABLISHED (lane proof, bh-g2-permutive, unreviewed, no priority claimed).** Let `(Q, ⋆)` be a finite quasigroup,
`X_⋆ = {x ∈ Q^(Z^2) : x(z+e_2) = x(z) ⋆ x(z+e_1)}`, and let `Ω ⊆ X_⋆` be any closed shift-invariant subset (for instance
a sub-SFT). Write `R, C, D` for the sets of rows, columns and antidiagonals (lines `i+j = const`) of points of `Ω`, and
`F(r)_i = r_i ⋆ r_(i+1)`, `G(c)_j = c_j \ c_(j+1)`, `H` for the three bipermutive radius-1/2 automata with
`row_(j+1) = F(row_j)`, `col_(i+1) = G(col_i)`, and antidiagonal `k` determined from antidiagonal `k+1` by `H`.
By Evans' embedding (as in `free-minimal-triangle-permutive-sft-exists`) this covers every triangle-permutive SFT.

1. **RPCA form.** Restriction to the diagonal strip `{0 <= i-j <= 1}` is a conjugacy `X_⋆ ≅ (Q×Q)^Z`, equivariant for
   `e_1+e_2` (the shift) and `e_1` (the reversible partitioned automaton `τ(a,b)_j = (b_j, b_j \ a_(j+1))`). So
   `X_⋆` is the full spacetime of an RPCA whose local bijection is the *Latin controlled permutation*
   `(u, v) ↦ (u, u \ v)`, and sub-SFTs of `X_⋆` are exactly the SFT-constrained spacetimes of that RPCA.
2. **Directions.** Every direction outside the three side lines `R e_1, R e_2, R(e_1-e_2)` is expansive for `Ω`, with a
   staircase strip of width 2. Across each side line the determinism is *seedless and one-sided*: a row determines the
   upper half-plane, a column the right half-plane, an antidiagonal the lower-left half-plane.
3. **Faults are total.** If `x, y ∈ Ω` agree on row `j` they agree on `{j' >= j}`; if they then differ on row `j-1`,
   they differ at every site of row `j-1`. Consequently: the horizontal line is nonexpansive for `Ω` iff `F|_R` is
   not injective; likewise vertical iff `G|_C`, antidiagonal iff `H|_D`. If `F|_R` is injective it is an automorphism
   of `(R, σ)` whose inverse is a sliding block code.
4. **No period relations.** If `F^n|_R = σ^k|_R` for some `n >= 1`, `k ∈ Z`, every point of `Ω` has period
   `n e_2 - k e_1`. So in a free `Ω` the monoid generated by `F|_R` and `σ^(±1)|_R` is free abelian `N × Z`; the same
   holds for `(G, C)` and `(H, D)`.
5. **Quadratic row complexity (Cyr–Kra).** Let `p_x(m)` be the number of length-`m` words in the rows of `x ∈ X_⋆`.
   Then `P_x(n,k) <= p_x(n+k-1)` for the `n×k` block complexity. By Cyr–Kra (arXiv:1208.4090, Theorem `mainthm`, read
   at source), a point with `P_x(n,k) <= nk/2` is periodic. So every aperiodic `x ∈ X_⋆` has
   `p_x(m) > ⌊(m+1)^2/4⌋ / 2` for all `m`, and the same for its columns and antidiagonals.
   By their Theorem `singleperiodic`, if moreover `p_x(n+k-1) <= nk` for some `n, k`, then at least two of the three
   side lines are nonexpansive for the orbit closure of `x`, i.e. at least two of `F, G, H` are non-injective there.

**Consequence for gate G2(b).** A free triangle-permutive SFT is the space of bi-infinite `F`-orbits in its row
subshift `R`, and then:
- `R` is non-sofic (a periodic row gives a periodic point) with complexity at least `(m+1)^2/8`;
- `F|_R` satisfies no relation `F^n = σ^k`;
- whenever some `p(n+k-1) <= nk`, at least two of the three transversal automata fail to be injective.

In symbolic-dynamics language, the SFT must be **semi-expansive** (seedless one-sided deterministic) in three
directions whose seedless normals `e_2, e_1, -(e_1+e_2)` positively span `R^2`, and expansive with bounded radius in
every other direction. The constructions surveyed in Zinoviadis' thesis (arXiv:1603.05464) give either one
semi-expansive direction (non-reversible CA spacetimes, as in Gács and Zinoviadis' earlier paper) or a unique two-sided
nonexpansive direction (extremely expansive RPCA spacetimes). Kari–Papasoglu's 4-way deterministic tiles give two
*seeded* nonexpansive directions. None of these has three semi-expansive directions.

**Lesson for general BH.** The permutive mechanism buys quantum rigidity exactly through *seedless* determinism: every
fault is one-sided and total. Freeness then forces rich rows (at least quadratic complexity) that a bipermutive
automaton moves non-injectively and non-shift-like, inside an SFT. That is the precise target for G2(b). If it is
impossible (the small-window search evidence points that way), then rigid free SFTs must come from a *contextual*
mechanism in which faults are two-sided but short relators still pin them (Labbé walls, fixed-point parsing), not from
local permutivity.

## Proof

1. *RPCA form.* Diagonal `d = i-j`. Each up-triangle `{y, y+e_1, y+e_2}` with base `y` on diagonal `d` has `y+e_1` on
   `d+1` and `y+e_2` on `d-1`. Given diagonals `d, d+1`, each point `z` of `d+2` is `y+e_1` for the unique base
   `y = z-e_1` on `d+1`, whose apex `y+e_2` lies on `d`; left division gives `x(z) = x(y) \ x(y+e_2)`. Symmetrically
   each point of `d-1` is `y+e_2` for a base on `d`, with `x(y+e_2) = x(y) ⋆ x(y+e_1)`. Extending outward from the
   strip uses every up-triangle exactly once (bases on `m >= d+1` define diagonal `m+1`, bases on `m <= d` define
   `m-1`), so no constraint is used twice and every strip configuration extends uniquely. The shift by `e_1` moves the
   strip `{d, d+1}` to `{d+1, d+2}`, which is the displayed `τ`; `e_1+e_2` shifts along diagonals.
2. *Directions.* For a monotone staircase with steps `e_1, e_2`, each horizontal step `(z, z+e_1)` determines the apex
   `z+e_2` (upper-left side), and each vertical step `(z, z+e_2)` determines `z+e_1` (lower-right side); iterating
   fills both sides. The affine maps permuting `{0, e_1, e_2}` preserve up-triangles and triangle permutivity (they
   replace `⋆` by a parastrophe), and they act transitively enough on the six open sectors between the rays
   `±e_1, ±e_2, ±(e_1-e_2)` to transfer the argument. Seedless one-sided determinism across the three sides is the
   definition of `F`, `G` and left division.
3. *Faults.* Rows above row `j` are `F`-iterates of row `j`. If `F(u) = F(v)` and `u(p) = v(p)`, then
   `u(p+1) = u(p) \ F(u)(p) = v(p+1)` and `u(p-1) = F(u)(p-1) / u(p) = v(p-1)`, so `u = v`. Hence distinct
   preimages differ everywhere. If `u ≠ v` in `R` with `F(u) = F(v)`, pick `x, y ∈ Ω` with row `-1` equal to `u`, `v`:
   they agree on `{j >= 0}`, so horizontal strips do not separate them. Conversely, if `F|_R` is injective, row `j`
   determines row `j-1` within `Ω`; the inverse is continuous and commutes with `σ`, so it is a block code, and one
   row determines the point.
4. *Relations.* `row_(j+n) = F^n(row_j) = σ^k(row_j)` gives `x(i, j+n) = x(i+k, j)`.
5. *Complexity.* The `n×k` block at `(i, j)` has entries `x(i+s, j+t) = F^t(row_j)(i+s)`, which depend only on
   `row_j` on `[i, i+n+k-2]`. The bound follows by maximizing `nk` subject to `n+k = m+1`. For columns and
   antidiagonals apply the transpose and the cyclic affine symmetry of item 2. For the two-direction statement, the
   orbit closure of `x` lies in `X_⋆`, so its nonexpansive lines are among the three sides, and Cyr–Kra's Theorem
   `singleperiodic` says a unique nonexpansive line with `P <= nk` forces periodicity.
