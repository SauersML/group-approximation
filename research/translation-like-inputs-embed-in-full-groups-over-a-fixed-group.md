**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-invent-09; elementary; not reviewed; no priority claimed). It answers the
invention half of gate U of `research/artifacts/gq-bh-synthesis-master-route.md` (v3): *what structure in
the language of X lets a decidable G map injectively into the units?* Answer: **move tables of a
bounded-displacement action of G on the acting group itself.**

## Setting

`Λ` is finitely generated with word metric `d`. `Γ` is generated by a finite symmetric `S`, and `N` is
the set of all words in `S` that are trivial in `Γ`. A *translation-like action* of `Γ` on `Λ` (Whyte's
term) is a free right action `(v, γ) ↦ v·γ` of `Γ` on the set `Λ` with `sup_v d(v, v·γ) < ∞` for each `γ`.
Examples: `Γ ≤ Λ` acting by right multiplication; compositions and products of such actions.

## Theorem 1 (move-table subshift)

Fix `R`, put `M = B_Λ(R)` and alphabet `A = M^S`. For `x ∈ A^Λ` write `φ_s^x(v) = v·x(v)_s`, and for a word
`w = s_1⋯s_k` put `φ_w^x = φ_(s_k)^x ∘ ⋯ ∘ φ_(s_1)^x`. Let `X_Γ ⊆ A^Λ` be the set of `x` with:
- (T1) `φ_(s^(-1))^x ∘ φ_s^x = id` for every `s ∈ S`;
- (T2) `φ_w^x = id` for every `w ∈ N`;
- (T3) `φ_w^x(v) ≠ v` for every `w ∉ N` and every `v ∈ Λ`.

Then:
1. `X_Γ` is a Λ-subshift for the shift `(λx)(v) = x(λ^(-1)v)`. Each condition forbids patterns on a ball of
   radius `R|w|`, and the conditions are translation invariant. If `WP(Γ)` and `WP(Λ)` are decidable, the
   forbidden list is recursively enumerable, so `X_Γ` is effectively closed.
2. `X_Γ ≠ ∅` iff `Γ` has a translation-like action on `Λ` in which every generator moves points at most
   `R`.

*Proof of 2.* Given the action, set `x(v)_s = v^(-1)(v·s)`. Conversely, (T1) makes each `φ_s` a bijection
with inverse `φ_(s^(-1))`, and (T2) makes `w ↦ φ_w` factor through `Γ`. So `v·γ := φ_w(v)` for any word
`w` representing `γ` is a right action; (T3) makes it free, and generators move at most `R`. `∎`

## Theorem 2 (full-group and unit embedding)

Let `Y` be a nonempty Λ-subshift on which `Λ` acts freely. For finitely generated `Λ` with decidable word
problem an effectively closed one exists (Aubrun–Barbieri–Thomassé; recalled, not re-read). Put
`X = X_Γ × Y` and, for `s ∈ S`, `σ_s(x, y) = (x(e)_s)^(-1)·(x, y)`.

Then `s ↦ σ_s` extends to an injective homomorphism `Γ → [[Λ ⋉ X]] ⊆ (LC(X, k) ⋊ Λ)^×` for every field
`k`. Its restriction to any nonempty closed Λ-invariant `Z ⊆ X`, for example a minimal subsystem, is
still injective.

*Proof.* For `z = (x, y)` and `v ∈ Λ` put `z_v = v^(-1)·z`, whose `X_Γ`-coordinate at `e` is `x(v)`. Then
`σ_s(z_v) = (x(v)_s)^(-1)v^(-1)·z = (v·x(v)_s)^(-1)·z = z_(φ_s(v))`.
- Since `Λ` acts freely on `Y`, the map `v ↦ z_v` is a bijection of `Λ` onto the orbit of `z`, and
  `σ_s` acts on that orbit as `φ_s` acts on `Λ`. So the relations of `Γ` hold (T2), giving a homomorphism.
- For `γ ≠ 1`, (T3) gives `φ_γ(e) ≠ e`, so `σ_γ(z) = z_(φ_γ(e)) ≠ z`. This holds at every point, so the
  restriction to any nonempty invariant `Z` is faithful.
- `σ_s(z) = c_s(z)·z` with `c_s(z) = (x(e)_s)^(-1)` locally constant, so `σ_s ∈ [[Λ ⋉ X]]`.
- `u_s = Σ_(m ∈ M) 1_([x(e)_s = m]) δ_(m^(-1))` is a unit implementing `σ_s` on `LC(X, k)`, and the map
  on units is injective because `LC(X, k)` sits faithfully in the crossed product. `∎`

## Theorem 3 (where the input sits, and what is left)

1. **The input is in the language.** For every `w`, either `φ_w(e) = e` on all of `X_Γ` (when `w ∈ N`) or
   nowhere (when `w ∉ N`). So `WP(Γ) ≤_m` the pattern-extension problem of `X_Γ`, and the whole word
   problem of `Γ` is written into the forbidden patterns (T2) and (T3). `Λ` carries none of it.
2. **The K-gate is closed once, for all such inputs.** If `Λ` is torsion-free and satisfies
   Farrell–Jones with additive coefficients, then `K_*((LC(Z, F_2) ⋊ Λ) ⊗ L) = 0` for every such `Z`, by
   `farrell-jones-groups-acting-freely-have-k-trivial-leavitt-hosts`. One Farrell–Jones instance, for
   the single group `Λ`, serves every `Γ` that acts translation-like on `Λ`.
3. **What remains for Boone–Higman for such `Γ`, over a fixed host group.** First, an SFT `X'` over the
   fixed group `Λ' = Λ × Z²` whose `Λ`-projection factors onto a minimal subsystem of `X`: this is gate
   M1, via the simulation theorems, recalled. `Γ` then acts on `X'` by `σ'_s(x') = (c_s(πx'), 0)^(-1)·x'`,
   still faithfully. Second, quantum rigidity of `X'` (gate G2). Then:
   - `quantum-rigid-sfts-over-fp-groups-have-fp-crossed-products` makes `B = LC(X', F_2) ⋊ Λ'`
     finitely presented, provided `Λ` is;
   - `B` is simple, since the action is minimal and free;
   - item 2 gives `K_*(B ⊗ L) = 0`, since `Λ × Z²` is Farrell–Jones;
   - Khanh (arXiv:2609.08428) makes `(B ⊗ L)^×` finitely presented and simple, and it contains `Γ`.

   So the master route's G1 and G2 need to be solved only over one fixed group, `Λ × Z²`, and G3r is
   free.

**Limits of the mechanism** are the separation-profile cap in
`translation-like-hosts-are-capped-by-separation-profiles`, and its escape through non-free orbits.

## Lesson for general BH

The acting group should be a fixed geometric scaffold, and the input should be written into the local
rules as move tables of a bounded-displacement action on that scaffold. The input's word problem becomes
the language of the subshift, while K-theory, finite presentation of the scaffold and freeness are paid
for once. The exact interface between the input and a fixed host is then "translation-like on Λ", or
more generally the wobbling group of `Λ`. This replaces "`Γ ≤ Λ`", which cost a new Farrell–Jones case,
by a coarse-geometric condition.
