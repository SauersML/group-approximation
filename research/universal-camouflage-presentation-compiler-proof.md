The quotient of `F_X x D` by the normal closure of `R x {1}` is `Q x D`.
Taking the corresponding quotient of the amalgam `(UPC1)` changes no
relation in the `B` vertex and gives

```text
B *_D (Q x D)=C_d(Q).
```

This proves `(UPC2)`.  In the latter group, quotienting by the normal closure
of `d` kills `B`, because `d` normally generates `B`, and kills the `D`
coordinate of the second vertex.  The remaining quotient is `Q`, proving
`(UPC3)`.

If `Q` is MF, the camouflage radical and target-factorization theorems give
`(UPC4)`.  The element `d` remains nonidentity in `C_d(Q)` by amalgam normal
form, so the intermediate group is non-MF.

Write `L_R=normalClosure_(U_X)(s(R))`.  If `R<=S`, then `L_R<=L_S`.
Conversely, the quotient `(UPC2)` contains the canonical embedded section

```text
F_X/R -> C_d(F_X/R).
```

Hence an element of `s(F_X)` lies in `L_R` exactly when its free-group
coordinate lies in `R`, proving `(UPC6)` and showing that `(UPC5)` reflects
order.  The normal closure of a union of visible relation subgroups is the
normal closure corresponding to their join, so `(UPC5)` preserves arbitrary
joins.

Use the split decompositions

```text
U_X=K_(F_X) semidirectProduct F_X,
C_d(Q)=K_Q semidirectProduct Q.
```

The kernel of the induced map `K_(F_X)->K_Q` is `F_infinity` by the
noninjective star-fold theorem.  The full kernel maps onto `R` under the
first split projection, and the canonical copy of `R<=F_X` supplies a
section.  Its kernel is exactly the star-fold kernel.  This proves `(UPC7)`.

Every group generated by `X` is `F_X/R` for some `R`, which proves the first
and third consequences.  If `X` is finite, `B` is finitely presented, and
`R` is finitely normally generated, then the amalgam presentation and the
finite list of visible relators give finite presentations for both `U_X`
and `C_d(Q)`, proving the second consequence.

Finally, the ordinary correspondence theorem for

```text
U_X -> F_X,
```

whose kernel is `normalClosure(d)`, identifies the quotient interval above
that kernel with the normal-subgroup lattice of `F_X`.  A quotient in the
interval is MF precisely when the corresponding quotient of `F_X` is MF.
This proves the last assertion.
