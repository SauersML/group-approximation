Use the notation and hypotheses of the target claim. First assume G
contains an infinite-order element x; the last section removes this
assumption. All products of
homeomorphisms are composed from right to left. For a finite word v,
L_v(h) acts as h on the suffix of v*C and fixes the complement.

## Faithful realization of the lamps

Set Z=Y union {#}, m=|Z|, and C=Z^N. Extend g in G to hat(g) on Z* by
its original recursion on letters in Y, while fixing the letter # and
having identity section there. Equivalently it acts as g up to the first
# and leaves the remaining suffix unchanged. This is a faithful
self-similar copy hat(G), contracting with nucleus contained in the
hats of a nucleus of G together with the identity.

Define gamma by its trivial root permutation and sections

    gamma|0=gamma,    gamma|#=hat(x),
    gamma|y=1 for y in Y minus {0}.

Thus gamma acts as hat(x) on each of the mutually disjoint cones
0^j*#*C, j>=0, and is the identity elsewhere. Continuity at p follows
since the supports accumulate only at p and preserve each such cone.
In particular gamma is a tree automorphism fixing p.

For r=g(p) let gamma_r=hat(g)*gamma*hat(g)^-1. Because the sections of
hat(g) after the first # are trivial, this map acts as hat(x) on exactly
the cones

    (r restricted to its first j letters)*#*C, j>=0.

This formula makes gamma_r independent of the choice of g. Two such
families of cones agree at common prefixes; every other pair of cones
is disjoint. The gamma_r therefore commute. For a finite set of
distinct rays, choose j beyond all their common prefixes. On the cone
r[0:j]*#*C a product of these lamps acts as hat(x) to precisely the
exponent of gamma_r. Infinite order of x proves independence of all
the lamp coordinates.

Every lamp fixes Y^N pointwise, whereas hat(G) acts faithfully there.
Thus its intersection with the lamp subgroup is trivial. Conjugation
permutes lamps according to the G action on Omega_0, proving

    H=<hat(G),gamma> is isomorphic to W.

H is self-similar: all sections of its specified generators belong
to H, and the section formulas for products and inverses preserve H.

## The two full groups

Put B=V_m(hat(G)) and E=V_m(H). The localized recursion is

    gamma=L_0(gamma)*L_#(hat(x)).

Consequently <B,gamma> contains L_0(gamma), and conjugating by prefix
maps gives L_v(gamma) for every nonempty v. A regular H-table factors
as a pure prefix table followed by localized H coefficients. Those
coefficients are words in localized hat(G) and gamma. Hence

    E=<B,gamma>.

B is a full group of homeomorphisms: a map locally equal to elements
of B has a finite regular table by compactness of C. B is finitely
presented by the contracting theorem used in the stabilizer route.

## Singular points and relative germs

Call q singular for e if e does not agree near q with any element of B.
The only singular point of gamma is p. Away from p, its action is
locally either hat(x) on a side cone or the identity, and these local
maps extend to B. For any nonzero n, gamma^n is not locally B at p:
if it were, choose a regular cone 0^k*C for that B representative on
which they agree. Its image must be exactly 0^k*C, because gamma^n
preserves that cone. Its section would therefore be gamma^n, an element
fixing Y^N pointwise but not the identity. It cannot belong to hat(G).

Gamma preserves every B orbit: it is locally B away from p and fixes
p. Set Omega=B*p. Every E element has finitely many singularities,
all in Omega, by writing it as a finite word in B and gamma. Conversely
a B conjugate of gamma is singular at any prescribed q in Omega.
Thus sing(E)=Omega. This set is dense, since it contains the V_m orbit
of p. It consists of rational points: a contracting automorphism is
finite state, and its transducer sends an eventually periodic input
to an eventually periodic output; prefix changes preserve this.

For a point q, write (E)_q and (B)_q for the isotropy germ groups.
At p any B germ is represented on a cone by

    0^k*z -> 0^l*hat(g)(z),        g(p)=p.

The displayed lamp-conjugation formula says hat(g) commutes with gamma
when g(p)=p. Inserting or deleting leading zeroes also commutes with
gamma as a germ, because gamma|0=gamma. Thus the germ c of gamma
centralizes (B)_p.

Every element of (E)_p is generated by (B)_p and c. To check this
without assuming a groupoid normal form, follow p through a word in
B and gamma, and choose for every intermediate point q in Omega a
B transporter t_q with t_q(p)=q, choosing t_p=1. A letter a from q
to q' contributes the isotropy germ of t_(q')^-1*a*t_q at p.
For a B letter this belongs to (B)_p. For a gamma or gamma^-1 letter
at q!=p it again belongs to (B)_p, because the letter is locally B.
At q=p it contributes c or c^-1. These germs telescope to the original
word's germ. The nonlocality of every nonzero power proved above gives

    (E)_p = (B)_p times <c>,       (E)_p/(B)_p = Z.

The first equality means an internal direct product of germ groups.
Conjugating by t_q gives the same normal-subgroup and cyclic-quotient
statement at every q in Omega.

For completeness this also proves the single-singularity realization
condition for a finite germ extension. Given e singular at q, first
compose on the left by a B map taking e(q) to q. The resulting isotropy
germ is b*c_q^n, with b a B germ, n!=0, and c_q the germ of
gamma_q=t_q*gamma*t_q^-1. Choose a B representative of b and undo the
initial left composition. The resulting product with gamma_q^n has
exactly one singular point, q, and agrees with e near q. Elements
with no singularities lie in B by fullness. Thus E is a finite germ
extension of B in the precise sense of the source below.

## Finite presentation from the germ complex

[Belk--Hyde--Matucci, Finite Germ Extensions, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03149v1#S2.SS1)
states the following sufficient criterion at n=2. For a finite germ
extension E of B, require finitely many B orbits on sing(E)^2 and
finite presentation of

    SingFix_E(M,M')={e: sing(e) subset M and e fixes M' pointwise}

for every M subset M' subset sing(E), |M'|<=2. Then E is finitely
presented. The definition also requires finite singular sets, relative
fullness, and realization of each singular germ with just that one
singular point; these were verified above.

In our case B is highly transitive on Omega: finitely many desired
point maps can be restricted to disjoint regular cones and completed
to a table using the cone extension lemma. In particular there are
only two orbits on Omega^2.

For any finite M subset M' subset Omega, taking the relative germs
at the fixed points of M gives a homomorphism

    SingFix_E(M,M') -> Z^M.

Its kernel is exactly Fix_B(M'): vanishing relative germs remove
every allowed singularity, after which fullness gives membership in
B. Its image is a subgroup of a finite-rank free abelian group and
is therefore finitely presented. The kernel is finitely presented
by contracting-srn-rational-stabilizers-are-fp. Finite-presentation
permanence under extensions proves the required condition. Theorem
2.1 yields finite presentation of E.

## Stabilizers in E and the simple envelope

We also need point stabilizers in E to be finitely generated. In fact
the same argument proves finite presentation of Fix_E(F) for every
finite F subset Omega. Here are the details needed to restrict the
finite germ extension to stabilizers.

Cone extension allows the following interpolation in B. For finitely
many distinct source and target points, respectively, with each pair
in the same B orbit, their point maps can be realized by B while
preserving a prescribed regular map on a sufficiently small disjoint
cone. Restrict individual B maps to smaller regular cones around the
source points, and extend this finite partial table. Both unions can
be made proper. In particular the prescribed regular map can be the
identity near a further point.

Write E_F=Fix_E(F), B_F=Fix_B(F). If e in E_F is locally B at q, choose
a B representative of its germ and correct its values on the other
points of F by a B map which is the identity near e(q). This is
possible by the interpolation just described: all source and target
points lie in Omega, and, after excluding q if q is in F, none equals
e(q). The corrected representative belongs to B_F. Thus singularities
relative to B_F agree with those relative to B for elements of E_F.

Similarly a single-singularity representative at q for an E_F germ
can be corrected on F by left multiplication by a B element equal
to the identity near its image of q. It remains singular only at q.
Starting with gamma_q and making this correction shows that every
q in Omega is a singular point of some E_F element. Finally, any
isotropy germ at q in E can be realized by E_F using this correction,
and any B isotropy germ can be realized by B_F. Consequently E_F
is a finite germ extension of B_F, with singular set Omega and the
same cyclic relative isotropy germ groups as before.

B_F has finitely many orbits on Omega^2: it fixes F pointwise and
is highly transitive on its complement, by the same interpolation.
The analogue of SingFix has a homomorphism to Z^M with kernel
Fix_B(F union M'), which is finitely presented. Theorem 2.1 therefore
applies again and proves E_F finitely presented.

E acts faithfully on Omega because Omega is dense in C and E consists
of homeomorphisms. Its action is highly transitive because B is a
subgroup. E is finitely presented and its point stabilizers are
finitely generated, as just proved. The required twisted
Brin--Thompson criterion now makes Omega V_E finitely presented and
simple, containing E and hence the faithfully embedded W.

This argument uses no projection that kills lamp homeomorphisms while
fixing prefix homeomorphisms. Its cyclic quotients are quotients of
point isotropy germ groups, not quotients of the full group E.

## Removing the infinite-order hypothesis on G

Let A=<a> be the binary adding-machine group acting on {0,1}*. It is
infinite cyclic and contracting: at depth n the sections of a^k have
exponents floor(k/2^n) or ceil(k/2^n), so eventually they lie in
{a^-1,1,a}. Give J=G times A its coordinatewise tree action on
(Y times {0,1})*. This action is faithful and self-similar. A section
is the pair of the corresponding coordinate sections; contracting
nuclei for the two factors give a finite product nucleus for J.
Thus J satisfies the already proved case, with infinite-order
element (1,a).

The constant ray p'=(0,0)^infinity has J orbit naturally identified
with (G*p) times (A*0^infinity). Under the subgroup G times {1}, the
subset (G*p) times {0^infinity} is invariant and has exactly the
original G action. The lamp direct sum supported on that subset,
together with G times {1}, is an embedded copy of the target W in
Z wr_(J*p') J. Apply the preceding construction to the latter wreath
product and restrict its embedding to W.
