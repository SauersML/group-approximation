Notation as in the target. Write `U ~ U'` when a compact open bisection of `T`
has source `U` and range `U'`. This relation is symmetric (invert `B`) and
transitive (the product `B'B` of compact open bisections with `r(B) = s(B')` is
a compact open bisection with source `s(B)` and range `r(B')`). It is also
additive: if `U_1, ..., U_k` are pairwise disjoint, `U'_1, ..., U'_k` are
pairwise disjoint, and `U_i ~ U'_i` by `B_i`, then `⊔ B_i` is a compact open
bisection from `⊔ U_i` onto `⊔ U'_i`.

**Step 0. Two kinds of bisections.**
- *V-part.* For nonempty clopen `P, P' ⊆ C` there is a compact open bisection
  `β ⊆ G_V` from `P` onto `P'`. Write `P` as a disjoint union of `a` cones and
  `P'` of `b` cones. Splitting a cone `[u]` into `[u0] ⊔ [u1]` raises the count by
  one, so both counts can be made equal. Match the cones by prefix replacements
  `uw ↦ u'w`. Every point has a proper subcone on which such a replacement
  agrees with an element of `V`: shrink the domain and range to proper cones and
  complete the prefix bijection on the complements after refining them to equal
  leaf counts. So the germs of the matching form a compact open bisection.
  Then `β × Q`, with `Q ⊆ X` clopen viewed as unit arrows of `H`, is a compact open
  bisection of `T` from `P × Q` onto `P' × Q`.
- *Λ-part.* For `λ ∈ Λ` and clopen `R ⊆ X`, the set
  `{(1_c, (λ, x)) : c ∈ C, x ∈ R}` is a compact open bisection of `T` from
  `C × R` onto `C × λR`. The same holds with `C` replaced by any clopen
  `P ⊆ C`, and with the V-part and Λ-part combined as a product.

**Step 1. `U ~ U'` whenever `π_X(U) = π_X(U')`.** Here `π_X : Y -> X` is the
projection. It is open and `U` is compact, so `π_X(U)` is clopen. By compactness
`U` is a finite union of rectangles `[u] × Q`. Let `Q_1, ..., Q_m` be the
nonempty atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by the clopen sets `Q` occurring
for `U` and for `U'`. On each atom the fibres of `U` and of `U'` are constant, so

`U = ⊔_k P_k × Q_k`,     `U' = ⊔_k P'_k × Q_k`,

with `P_k, P'_k ⊆ C` clopen. An atom `Q_k` lies in `π_X(U)` exactly when `P_k` is
nonempty, and in `π_X(U')` exactly when `P'_k` is nonempty. Because the
projections agree, discard the atoms where both are empty; on the rest both are
nonempty. Step 0 gives `P_k × Q_k ~ P'_k × Q_k`, and additivity gives `U ~ U'`.

**Step 2. `C × Q ~ C × X` for every nonempty clopen `Q ⊆ X`.**
- The union `O = ∪_{λ ∈ Λ} λQ` is open, nonempty and invariant. Its complement is
  closed and invariant, so it is empty by minimality. By compactness there are
  `λ_1 = 1, λ_2, ..., λ_m` with `X = ∪_j λ_j Q`.
- Put `R_j = λ_j Q \ ∪_{i<j} λ_i Q`, so the `R_j` are clopen and partition `X`,
  and `R_1 = Q`. Drop the empty ones; `R_1` stays. Put `Q_j = λ_j^{-1} R_j ⊆ Q`,
  so `Q_1 = Q`.
- Partition `C` into `m'` nonempty cones `C_j`, one for each remaining index.
  By Step 0 there are compact open bisections from `C × R_j` onto `C_j × Q_j`:
  combine a V-part `C -> C_j` with the Λ-part `λ_j^{-1}` on `R_j`. The sources
  partition `C × X` and the ranges are disjoint because the `C_j` are. By
  additivity `C × X ~ W`, with `W = ⊔_j C_j × Q_j ⊆ C × Q`.
- `π_X(W) = ∪_j Q_j = Q = π_X(C × Q)`, because `Q_1 = Q`. Step 1 gives
  `W ~ C × Q`. By transitivity `C × X ~ C × Q`.

**Step 3. Conclusion.** For nonempty clopen `U, U'` put `Q = π_X(U)` and
`Q' = π_X(U')`, both nonempty and clopen. By Steps 1 and 2,

`U ~ C × Q ~ C × X ~ C × Q' ~ U'`.

∎
