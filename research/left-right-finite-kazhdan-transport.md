For a unital C-star algebra `A` with tracial state `tau`, let

```text
D_tau(A) = C*( L_a, R_b : a,b in A ) <= B(L^2(A,tau)),
```

where `L` and `R` are left and right multiplication.  Call `(A,tau)`
**left-right finite** when `D_tau(A)` is a finite C-star algebra, i.e. every
isometry in it is unitary.

Let `(A_n,tau_n)` be left-right-finite coordinates, let
`U_n : H -> U(A_n)` be an operator-norm asymptotic representation, and let
`Gamma` have property `(T)` with `iota : Gamma -> H` and
`s iota(Gamma) s^-1 <= iota(Gamma)`.  If a uniformly norm-bounded sequence
`x_n in A_n` asymptotically centralizes `iota(Gamma)` in the trace 2-norm,
then so does

```text
U_n(s) x_n U_n(s)^*.
```

Thus the matrix Kazhdan-transport theorem does not require finite-dimensional
coordinate algebras as such.  What it requires is finiteness of the
left-right algebra in which the fixed-space projection and its conjugate
live.

The hypothesis is sharp relative to the proof architecture: for an
infinite factor in standard form the left-right algebra is
`M or M' = B(L^2 M)`, which is not finite, and the constant-coordinate
`C*_r(E)` example from the manuscript violates transport.
