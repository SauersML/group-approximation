**Source.**  Y. Lodha and J. T. Moore, *A finitely presented group of piecewise
projective homeomorphisms*, arXiv:1308.4250.  TeX source `vN_fp.tex`, fetched
through MSI on 2026-09-13.  Quoted verbatim:

- l.124--127: "The group $G_0$ generated by the functions $a(t)$, $b(t)$, and
  $c(t)$ is nonamenable and finitely presented."
- l.133--135: "by an unpublished result of Thurston, $a(t)$ and $b(t)$ generate
  the subgroup $P(\Zbb) \leq H$, consisting of those homeomorphisms which are
  $C^1$ and piecewise $\PSL_2(\Zbb)$, which he moreover showed is a copy of
  Richard Thompson's group $F$."
- l.268--271: "Thurston observed that the functions $a$ and $b$ from the
  introduction become the generators $a$ and $b$ for Thompson's group $F$ defined
  above when ``conjugated'' by $\Phi$."
- l.444--446: "we will identify $a$, $b$, and $c$ with $x$, $x_\seq{1}$, and
  $y_{\seq{10}}$, respectively and suppress all mention of $\Phi$."
- l.466--468: "The first two groups of relations are known to give a presentation
  for $F$ ... We will use $F$ to denote the group generated by
  $\{x_s : s \in 2^{<\Nbb}\}$."
- l.476--477: "The group $G_0$ defined in the introduction is therefore generated
  by the (redundant) generating set $S_0 = X \cup Y_0$", with
  `X = {x_s : s in 2^(<N)}` (l.472).

**Argument.**
1. By l.268--271, conjugation by `Phi` carries `a` and `b` to the standard
   generators of Thompson's `F` acting on `2^N` by prefix replacement
   (l.205--219).  Conjugation is a group isomorphism, so `<a, b>` is isomorphic
   to `F`.
2. By l.444--446 and l.466--477, `a = x` and `b = x_1` lie in `G_0`, and
   `<x_s> = F` sits inside `G_0` through the generating set `S_0 ⊇ X`. ∎

**Trust surface.**  Thurston's observation is used exactly as Lodha--Moore state
it; their paper does not reprove it.
