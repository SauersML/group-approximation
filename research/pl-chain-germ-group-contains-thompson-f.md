## Setting

As in `bs-chain-configurations-exist-in-finite-germ-extensions-of-v`:
- `σ(x) = x + 1` and `δ(x) = 2x`.
- `a` is the odd dyadic square map: `a(2x) = 4a(x)`, with `a(x) = 2x - 1` on `[1, 3/2)` and
  `a(x) = 4x - 4` on `[3/2, 2)`.
- `Q = ⟨σ, δ, s = [a]⟩` is the germ group at `+∞` of the host `Γ̄ = ⟨V, a, b⟩`, a quotient of the chain
  group `G_1 = ⟨s, δ, σ | s δ s^-1 = δ^2, δ σ δ^-1 = σ^2⟩`.

Notation on the real line `y`:
- `T_c(y) = y + c` and `D_λ(y) = λ y`.
- `ε(w) = w + 1` for `w <= -1`, `ε(w) = (w+1)/2` on `[-1, 0]`, and `ε(w) = w + 1/2` for `w >= 0`.

## Statement

1. **Square-map coordinates.** Let `Λ(y) = 2^⌊y⌋ (1 + {y})`, a PL homeomorphism `R -> (0, ∞)` with
   slope `2^n` on `[n, n+1)`. Conjugation by `Λ` sends:
   - `δ` to `T_1`;
   - `a` to `D_2`, exactly;
   - `ρ = s^-1 δ s` to `T_(1/2)`;
   - `σ` to the **carry map** `e_0`. For `n >= 0`, `e_0(y) = y + 2^-n` on `[n, n+1-2^-n)`, and
     `e_0(y) = n + 1 + (y - n - 1 + 2^-n)/2` on `[n+1-2^-n, n+1)`.

   So `Q` is the germ group at `+∞` of `⟨e_0, T_1, D_2⟩`. Here `⟨T_1, D_2⟩` is the affine `BS(1,2)`,
   and `e_0` is the only non-affine generator.
2. **Splitting.** Let `M = {g ∈ Q : g(y) - y -> 0 as y -> ∞}` (in the `y`-coordinate). Then
   `Q = M ⋊ ⟨δ, s⟩`, with `⟨δ, s⟩ ≅ BS(1,2)` and `M = ⟨⟨σ⟩⟩_Q`.
3. **An exactly represented subgroup.** Put `e_1 = ρ σ ρ^-1`. Then the germ group `⟨σ, e_1⟩ ≤ M` is
   isomorphic to the subgroup of `PL(R) × PL(R)` generated by `(ε, T_(1/2))` and `(T_1, ε)`, with
   `σ ↦ (ε, T_(1/2))` and `e_1 ↦ (T_1, ε)`. The two coordinates are the pictures of a germ at the
   integers and at the half-integers of the `y`-line. These are the points `2^m` and the bends
   `3·2^(m-1)` of `a` on the `x`-line.
4. **Thompson's F.** Define these words in `Q`:
   - `C = σ e_1^-1`;
   - `D_n = e_1^n C e_1^-n`;
   - `f = [D_2, D_3] = D_2 D_3 D_2^-1 D_3^-1`;
   - `g = σ f σ^-1`;
   - `x_0 = f g` and `x_1 = g`.

   Then `x_0, x_1` satisfy the Cannon–Floyd–Parry relations
   `[x_0 x_1^-1, x_0^-1 x_1 x_0] = [x_0 x_1^-1, x_0^-2 x_1 x_0^2] = 1`, and `[x_0, x_1] ≠ 1`. So
   `⟨x_0, x_1⟩ ≅ F`.
   - In the pictures, `f` is the standard one-bump element (slopes `1/2, 1, 2`) on `[5/2, 7/2]` in the
     first coordinate, and `g` is its translate on `[3, 4]`.
   - On the `x`-line, for every large `m`, the support of `[x_0, x_1]` near scale `2^m` is the union of
     two sets. One lies in `x - 2^m ∈ [11/2, 7]` and the other in `x - 3·2^(m-1) ∈ [2, 3]`, and each
     spans its interval. This is the picture support scaled by `x - 2^m = 2w` (respectively
     `x - 3·2^(m-1) = 2w`).
5. **Consequences.** `Q` and `M` contain `F`. So:
   - they are not soluble;
   - they are not elementary amenable, since `F` is not (Chou 1980; Cannon–Floyd–Parry);
   - they are not residually soluble, since `F'` is simple and non-abelian.

   This answers the question left open in `finite-hirsch-length-soluble-groups-hold-no-bs-chain`. The
   germ group of the only concrete finite germ extension of `V` carrying a chain configuration is
   neither soluble nor elementary amenable.

## What this changes

- **Soluble-group finiteness methods are dead for Q.** Finite presentation of `Q` is the remaining hole
  on the host route to `G_1 ∈ B_A` (`bhm-singfix-condition-forces-fp-germ-groups`,
  `fp-germ-extensions-of-locally-moving-bases-have-fp-germ-groups`). These methods cannot decide it:
  - Bieri–Strebel and Σ-invariant criteria for metabelian or soluble groups;
  - finite-Hirsch-length structure theory;
  - Noetherian-module arguments of the kind in `chain-configurations-need-unboundedly-many-dilation-eigenvalues`.

  `Q` lives in the same world as Thompson-like groups and their germ groups. There the tools are Brown's
  criterion on Stein–Farley complexes and the zoom towers of
  `zoom-tower-germ-groups-give-f-infinity-germ-extensions`.
- **Where the difficulty sits.** Item 3 shows that `⟨σ, ρ⟩` (and so `σ`, `δ = ρ^2`, `ρ`) acts on
  bend-local pictures uniformly. Only `s = D_2` does not: it sends the window at the integer `m` to the
  window at `2m` and rescales pictures by `2^(m+1)`, a factor that grows with `m`.
  - So words mixing `e_0` and `D_2 e_0 D_2^-1` see two bump widths whose ratio tends to infinity.
  - The kernel relations of `scale-periodic-bs-chain-germ-representations-are-not-faithful` live exactly
    there, as limits of relations in `⟨ε, D_λ ε D_λ^-1⟩` as `λ -> ∞`.
  - Finite presentation of `Q` is therefore a question about these scale-divergent limits. It is not a
    question about the picture groups, which are finitely generated subgroups of `PL_2(R)`.
- **Finite presentation of Q stays OPEN.**

## Lesson for general BH

In a germ host, a chain of Baumslag–Solitar squarings has a top level: a square map that bends once
per dyadic scale. In the right coordinates that top level becomes affine (`D_2`), and the bottom level
(the zoom `x+1`) becomes a *carry map* with bends at the lattice. Its bend-local pictures generate
Thompson-type groups. So a PL tower host of the chain group is never a soluble or "small" group, and
its finiteness has to be settled with Thompson-group technology, not with soluble-group technology.
