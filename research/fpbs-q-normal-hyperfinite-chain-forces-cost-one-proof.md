Complete written proof.

**Imported inputs.**

* (I1) An aperiodic hyperfinite p.m.p. relation has cost one. Every aperiodic
  relation has cost at least one. Both are in the standard cost facts of
  `fpbs-amen2-cost-inputs`.
* (I2) The marker lemma (Kechris--Miller, *Topics in orbit equivalence*,
  Lemma 6.7). An aperiodic countable Borel equivalence relation admits a
  decreasing sequence of Borel complete sections with empty intersection.
  Since `mu` is finite, such sections have measure tending to `0`. This is the
  same import used in `fpbs-quotient-split-actions-cost-one-proof`.

**Proof.** Fix `delta>0`.

1. By (I1), choose a graphing `Phi_0` of `T_0` with cost at most `1+delta`.
2. Each `T_k` is a countable Borel relation. `T_0` is, and `T_(k+1)` is
   generated by `T_k` and countably many Borel automorphisms. Choose a
   countable generating set `S_k = {g_(k,1), g_(k,2), ...}` of `H_k` with
   every `g_(k,j)` in `QN(T_k)`. The relation
   `U_(k,j) = T_k cap T_k^(g_(k,j))` is Borel and aperiodic on a conull set.
   Discard a null invariant set. By (I2), choose a Borel complete section
   `D_(k,j)` of `U_(k,j)` with `mu(D_(k,j)) <= delta 2^(-k-j)`.
3. Let `Psi` consist of `Phi_0` together with the partial maps
   `x -> g_(k,j) x` restricted to `D_(k,j)`. Its cost is at most
   `1 + delta + sum_(k>=0, j>=1) delta 2^(-k-j) <= 1 + 3 delta`. Every edge lies in
   `R`, so `<Psi> <= R`.
4. **Induction: `T_k <= <Psi>` for every `k`.** For `k=0` this is step 1.
   Suppose `T_k <= <Psi>`, and let `g = g_(k,j)` and `y in X`. Because
   `D = D_(k,j)` is a complete section of `U_(k,j)`, some `x in D` has
   `(y,x) in T_k` and `(y,x) in T_k^g`, that is `(gy,gx) in T_k`. The chain

       y ~_(T_k) x -> gx ~_(T_k) gy

   uses `T_k <= <Psi>` twice and one edge of `Psi`. So `(y,gy) in <Psi>` for
   a.e. `y` and every `g in S_k`. Hence `R_(H_k) <= <Psi>`, and
   `T_(k+1) = T_k v R_(H_k) <= <Psi>`.
5. So `R = union_k T_k <= <Psi> <= R`. `Psi` generates `R`, so
   `C(R) <= 1+3 delta`. Since `delta` was arbitrary and `R` is aperiodic,
   (I1) gives `C(R)=1`.

For a chain indexed by a countable ordinal `alpha`, enumerate the pairs
`(beta,j)` with `beta<alpha` by the natural numbers and assign weights
`delta 2^(-n)`. The induction in step 4 becomes transfinite induction. A limit
stage is a union, so it is contained in `<Psi>` as soon as every earlier stage
is.

**Consequences.** For the Bernoulli action `b`, `C(b)=1`, and
`fpbs-bernoulli-maximal-cost` bounds the cost of every free action above by
`C(b)` and below by `1`. So `Gamma` has fixed price one.
`fpbs-amen2-betti-cost-input` gives `beta_1^(2)(Gamma) <= C(R)-1 = 0`.

For the subgroup case, freeness gives `(x,hx) in (R_H)^g` if and only if
`g h g^(-1) in H`. So `R_H cap (R_H)^g = R_(H cap g^(-1)Hg)`, and it is
aperiodic exactly when that intersection is infinite.

This is dependency bookkeeping for a written deduction, not a formal
verification.
