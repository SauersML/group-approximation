Let `k` be a field, `D = k(<x_0, x_1>)` and `D^e = D ⊗_k D^op`.

## 1. Import: the derivation bimodule of `D` is free

Source: H. Derksen and J. Volčič, *Invariants of finite groups acting on (free) skew fields*, arXiv:2512.03223,
§5. Read from the arXiv PDF on September 19, 2026.
- Standing convention (§2), verbatim: "Throughout this paper let k be a fixed ground field."
- Definition, verbatim: "Given a k-algebra A, its universal derivation bimodule Ω_k(A) is the A-subbimodule in
  A ⊗_k A^op generated by {da : a ∈ A}, where da = a ⊗ 1 − 1 ⊗ a."
- Statement, verbatim: "For the free skew field M = k(<x_1, x_2, ..., x_m)>, the bimodule Ω_k(M) is freely
  generated by dx_1, dx_2, ..., dx_m [C95, Proposition 5.8.7 and Theorem 5.8.10]."
- [C95] is P. M. Cohn, *Skew Fields: Theory of General Division Rings*, CUP 1995.
- The extracted PDF text drops the subscript and superscript layout. The symbols above were restored from context.

## 2. `D` is quasi-free

Let `μ : D^e -> D` be multiplication. Then `Ω_k(D) = ker μ`.
- `⊆`: each `da` lies in `ker μ`, and `ker μ` is a sub-bimodule.
- `⊇`: if `Σ a_i b_i = 0`, then `Σ a_i ⊗ b_i = -Σ a_i · d(b_i)`, because
  `a_i · d(b_i) = a_i b_i ⊗ 1 - a_i ⊗ b_i`.

By §1 with `m = 2`, the sequence `0 -> (D^e)^2 -> D^e -> D -> 0` is exact. So `pd_(D^e) D <= 1`, and
`quasi-free-algebras-have-no-trdeg-two-subfields` applies with `A = D`.

## 3. Conclusion

By item 3 of that claim, every commutative subfield `F ⊆ M_r(D)` with `k · 1 ⊆ F` has `trdeg_k F <= 1`, for
every `r >= 1` and every field `k`. ∎

The same holds for the free skew field on any number `m` of generators, and for `m = ∞` by direct limits.

## 4. A second proof of §1 (not load-bearing)

This uses Cohn's theorem that `D` is the universal localization `P_Σ` of `P = k<x_0, x_1>` at the set `Σ` of full
matrices. It is recorded because the same argument works for any universal localization.

- Let `N` be a `D`-bimodule and `δ : P -> N` a derivation. Map `P` into `T = [[D, N], [0, D]]` by
  `p ↦ [[p, δp], [0, p]]`.
- This map is `Σ`-inverting, since `[[M, δM], [0, M]]^(-1) = [[M^(-1), -M^(-1) δM M^(-1)], [0, M^(-1)]]`.
- So it extends uniquely to `D -> T`. Both diagonal corners are the identity, because `P -> P_Σ` is an
  epimorphism.
- Hence `Der_k(D, N) ≅ Der_k(P, N) ≅ N^2`, naturally in `N`. By Yoneda, `Ω_k(D) ≅ (D^e)^2`.

## 5. Corollary: the homological transcendence degree of `D` is 1

Yekutieli--Zhang, *Homological transcendence degree*, Proc. LMS (2006), define, verbatim:
"HtrD = injdimD ⊗kD◦ whereD ⊗kD◦ is viewed as a left module over itself."

- By item 1 of `quasi-free-algebras-have-no-trdeg-two-subfields`, with `U = D^op`, we get `l.gl.dim D^e <= 1`.
  Hence `Htr D <= 1`.
- Their Proposition 0.4, verbatim: "(a) Htr C ≤ HtrD." and "(d) If D is a PI division ring and if the center is
  ﬁnitely generated over k as a ﬁeld, then HtrD = trD." With `C = k(x_0)`, this gives `Htr D >= 1`.

Their Remark 7.11, verbatim: "We expect that the free skew fields have inﬁnite homological transcendence degree."
That expectation is false.

By their Theorem 0.2 ("HtrQ(A) = gldimA" for Artin--Schelter regular graded Ore domains) and Proposition 0.4(a),
no quotient division ring of an Artin--Schelter regular Ore domain of global dimension `>= 2` embeds in `D`.
- Examples are the quantum-plane skew fields `Q(k_q[u, v])` with `uv = q vu` and `q ∈ k^×`.
- The case `q = 1` is `k(u, v)` itself, which recovers §3 for `r = 1`.
