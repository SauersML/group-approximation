Let B_F=Fix_V(F). The rational-stabilizer claim makes B_F finitely
presented and hence finitely generated. For x in Omega, put
Q_x=(E)_x and H_x=(V)_x. Transport by V identifies Q_x with
<rho(P),tau> and H_x with <tau>, so Q_x is finitely generated.

## Independent germ realization

Given x in F and an isotropy germ q_x in Q_x, the shell construction
supplies a representative h fixing x, singular at most at x. For
every y in F minus {x}, h is locally V at y and h(y)!=x.
Choose small disjoint cones around the h(y) on which h^-1 is a
prefix map. Their inverse images are small disjoint cones around
the y. Shrink them to avoid a neighborhood of x on both sides.

Extend these finitely many prefix maps, together with the identity
on a sufficiently small cone around x, to v in V. This is possible
by refining binary prefix partitions of the remaining proper clopen
sets to equal leaf counts. Then v*h has the same germ q_x at x
and is the identity near every other point of F. Its singular set
is still contained in {x}.

Likewise all prefix germs H_x can be prescribed independently by
elements of B_F, using disjoint-cone prefix interpolation. Therefore
the joint germ map is onto:

    S(M,F) -> product_(x in M) Q_x times product_(x in F minus M) H_x.

Its kernel K_F consists of elements equal to the identity near F.
An element of S(M,F) with this property is locally V everywhere,
so K_F is contained in B_F. The germ quotient is finitely generated.
Choose finitely many lifts of its generators. Together with a finite
generating set of B_F they generate S(M,F): match the joint germ of
an arbitrary element using the lifts, then the remaining element
belongs to K_F<=B_F.

More economically, choose a finite generating set S of P. It is
enough to add to generators of B_F one corrected representative of
the transported rho_g at each x in M for every g in S. The prefix
germs are already generated by the image of B_F. Thus the number
of required generators is at most d(B_F)+|M|*|S|. In particular
finite generation of the neighborhood kernel itself is not needed.

## Finite point stabilizers of E

For finite F, E_F is a finite germ extension of B_F with singular
set Omega. A local V representative of an E_F germ can be made
to fix F by a prefix correction equal to the identity near the
image of the germ's basepoint. If the basepoint is in F, it is
already fixed. Correct the remaining finitely many points on
disjoint cones. The same correction applies to a one-singularity
representative. Relative fullness follows because an element
locally B_F is locally V and, if it fixes F, belongs to B_F.
The independent realization above also produces a nontrivial
singularity at any x in Omega while fixing F (use F union {x}
when x is not in F). Thus the singular set remains Omega.

B_F has finitely many orbits on Omega: the individual points of F
and their complement, on which it is highly transitive. For
M subset M' subset Omega with |M'|<=1, its singularity subgroup is

    SingFix_(E_F)(M,M')=S(M,M' union F),

which is finitely generated by the first part. Apply
[Belk--Hyde--Matucci, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03149v1#S2.SS1)
with n=1 to obtain E_F finitely generated. This application uses
only finite generation of the displayed groups, not their finite
presentation. The proof gives no assertion that they are finitely
presented for an arbitrary enumeration.
