## Imports

- **(I1)** `T` is infinite and simple (Cannon--Floyd--Parry, *Introductory notes on Richard
  Thompson's groups*). This is the same standard import (I1) as in
  `lifted-thompson-t-escapes-haagerup-permanence-closure-proof`.
- **(I2)** Bass--Serre theory (Serre, *Trees*). The fundamental group of a graph of groups acts on
  its Bass--Serre tree without inversions, and vertex stabilizers are conjugates of vertex groups.
  This is import (I3) of the same route.
- **(I3)** A word-hyperbolic group contains no subgroup isomorphic to `Z^2` (Gromov; see
  Bridson--Haefliger, *Metric spaces of non-positive curvature*, Ch. III.Γ, Cor. 3.10). Standard.
  The corollary number was not re-checked.

## Inputs from the graph

- **(Q1)** `thompson-t-lochak-schneps-presentation`: `T` is generated by `alpha`, `beta` with
  `alpha^4 = beta^3 = (beta alpha)^5 = 1` and three further relators. So `T` is finitely
  presented and generated by two elements of finite order whose product has finite order.
- **(Q2)** `lifted-thompson-t-escapes-haagerup-permanence-closure`, item 2: `T` is nonamenable.
- **(Q3)** `lifted-thompson-t-perfect-centre-cubically-elliptic`: `T̄` is a group of
  orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of `R`.
- **(Q4)** `finitely-generated-linear-groups-are-residually-finite` (Malcev).

## (L0) Simplicity lemma

Every homomorphism `phi : T -> H` is trivial or injective, since `ker phi` is normal (I1). So
`T` embeds in `H` as soon as some homomorphism `T -> H` is nontrivial.

## (L1) Finite-order elements are elliptic

Let `g` act on a tree `X` without inversions, with `g^n = 1`. Take a vertex `x`. Its orbit is
finite, so the subtree `Y` spanned by it is finite and `g`-invariant. Remove all leaves of `Y`
repeatedly. This ends at a single vertex or a single edge, and the result is `g`-invariant
because the procedure is. In the first case `g` fixes that vertex. In the second case `g` maps
the edge to itself, and since `g` does not invert edges it fixes both endpoints.

## (L2) Serre's bridge lemma

Let `a`, `b` act on a tree without inversions, each fixing a vertex, and suppose their fixed
vertex sets `A`, `B` are disjoint. Then `ab` has infinite order.

*Proof.* `A` spans a subtree: if `a` fixes `x` and `y` it fixes the unique geodesic `[x, y]`,
and it fixes each edge of it since it fixes both endpoints. The same holds for `B`. Two disjoint
subtrees of a tree are joined by a unique shortest geodesic `[p, q]` with `p ∈ A`, `q ∈ B`,
which meets `A` only in `p` and `B` only in `q`. Its length is `d ≥ 1`.

Let `e_p` be the edge of `[p, q]` at `p` and `e_q` the edge of `[p, q]` at `q`. The far endpoint
of `e_p` is not in `A`, so `a e_p ≠ e_p`. Likewise `b e_q ≠ e_q`.

- The path `S = [p, q] · b[q, p]` runs from `p` to `bp`. At `q` it arrives along `e_q` and leaves
  along `b e_q ≠ e_q`, so it does not backtrack. A path without backtracking in a tree is a
  geodesic, so `S` has length `2d`.
- Put `g = ab`. The path `aS` runs from `ap = p` to `gp`. It ends by arriving along `ab e_p = g e_p`
  and it starts by leaving along `a e_p`. So `g(aS)` starts at `gp` by leaving along `g a e_p`.
  Since `a e_p ≠ e_p`, we have `g a e_p ≠ g e_p`, so the concatenation `aS · g(aS)` does not
  backtrack at `gp`.
- Hence the bi-infinite concatenation of the paths `g^n(aS)`, `n ∈ Z`, has no backtracking and is
  a geodesic line on which `g` translates by `2d ≥ 2`. So `d(p, g^n p) = 2dn` for `n ≥ 1`, and `g`
  has infinite order. ∎

## Item 1 (FA)

Let `T` act on a tree without inversions. By (Q1) and (L1), `alpha` and `beta` each fix a vertex.
Apply (L2) with `a = beta`, `b = alpha`: the product `beta alpha` has order `5`, so the fixed
vertex sets of `alpha` and `beta` meet. A common fixed vertex is fixed by `<alpha, beta> = T`. ∎

## Item 2 (closure)

Throughout, suppose `T ≤ G` and derive that `T` embeds in one of the inputs.

- **Subgroups.** If `G ≤ H` and `T ≤ G`, then `T ≤ H`.
- **Extensions.** Let `N ⊴ E` and `T ≤ E`. Then `T ∩ N ⊴ T`. By (I1), either `T ∩ N = T`, so
  `T ≤ N`, or `T ∩ N = 1`, so `T` maps injectively to `E/N`. No hypothesis on `N` or `E/N`
  beyond membership of `D_T` is used.
- **Graphs of groups.** Let `G = pi_1` of a graph of groups. By (I2), `G` acts on the Bass--Serre
  tree without inversions. By item 1, `T` fixes a vertex, so `T` lies in a conjugate of a vertex
  group, and hence embeds in that vertex group. Edge groups and edge maps play no role.
- **Directed colimits.** Let `G = colim G_i` over a directed set, with arbitrary structure maps
  `f_ij`, and let `iota : T -> G` be injective. Lift `iota(alpha)`, `iota(beta)` to elements of some
  `G_i`. Each of the finitely many relators of (Q1) becomes trivial in `G`, hence in some `G_j`,
  `j ≥ i`. So for one `j` there is a homomorphism `T -> G_j` whose composite with `G_j -> G` is
  `iota`. That homomorphism is injective, because `iota` is.
- **Unrestricted products.** Let `T ≤ prod_i G_i`. The coordinate projections restricted to `T`
  are trivial or injective (L0). They cannot all be trivial, since `T ≠ 1`. So `T` embeds in some
  `G_i`.
- **Ultraproducts.** Let `T ≤ prod_U G_i` for an ultrafilter `U`. Write `alpha`, `beta` as classes
  of sequences `(a_i)`, `(b_i)`, and fix a word `w` in `alpha`, `beta` with `w ≠ 1` in `T`. For
  each relator `r` of (Q1), the set of `i` where `r(a_i, b_i) = 1` is in `U`. So is the set where
  `w(a_i, b_i) ≠ 1`. The finite intersection of these sets is in `U`, hence nonempty. For `i` in
  it, `alpha -> a_i`, `beta -> b_i` defines a homomorphism `T -> G_i` that is nontrivial on `w`.
  By (L0) it is injective.
- **Local embeddability and marked limits.** Let `T ≤ G` and let `K` be the finite subset of `G`
  formed by the prefixes of the relators of (Q1) and of `w`, read in `alpha`, `beta` and their
  inverses. A partial embedding of `K` into some `H`, multiplicative on `K`, sends the images of
  `alpha`, `beta` to elements satisfying the relators (their prefixes multiply consistently and the
  relator itself lands on the image of `1`, which is `1`). So it defines a homomorphism `T -> H`,
  and this homomorphism sends `w` to the image of `w`, which differs from the image of `1` by
  injectivity on `K`. By (L0) it is injective. A limit of marked groups agrees with its
  approximants on a ball containing `K` from some point on, so the same argument applies.

So `D_T` is closed under each operation. ∎

## Item 3 (base)

- **`T̄`.** `T` has torsion (`alpha^4 = 1`, `alpha ≠ 1`). `T̄` is torsion-free by (Q3): if `h` is an
  orientation-preserving homeomorphism of `R` with `h(x) > x` for some `x`, then
  `h^n(x) > ... > h(x) > x`, so `h^n ≠ 1`; the case `h(x) < x` is symmetric. So `T ⊄ T̄`.
- **Finite groups.** `T` is infinite (I1).
- **Amenable groups.** Subgroups of amenable groups are amenable, and `T` is not (Q2).
- **Residually finite groups.** Subgroups of residually finite groups are residually finite. An
  infinite simple group is not residually finite: the kernel of a map to a finite group is a
  normal subgroup of finite index, hence all of `T`, so no nontrivial element survives.
- **Linear groups.** A copy of `T` in `GL_n(k)` would be finitely generated and linear, hence
  residually finite by (Q4). This contradicts the previous bullet.
- **Hyperbolic groups.** `T` contains `Z^2`. Take two nontrivial elements of `F ≤ T` whose supports
  lie in disjoint open dyadic intervals of `(0,1)`. They commute, have infinite order, and
  generate a group whose elements `f^m g^n` act as `f^m` on one interval and `g^n` on the other,
  so the group is `Z^2`. By (I3), `T` is not in a hyperbolic group.
- **LEF and LEA groups.** They are locally embeddable into finite, respectively amenable, groups.
  Both classes are in `D_T`, so item 2 (local embeddability) puts LEF and LEA groups in `D_T`.

## Item 4 (no factorization)

`𝒞 ⊆ D_T` by items 2 and 3, since `D_T` contains the base and is closed under the operations.
Let `phi : T -> G` with `G ∈ 𝒞`. If `phi` were nontrivial it would be injective (L0), so `T` would
be a subgroup of `G ∈ D_T`, which is impossible. So `phi` is trivial.

`V` contains `T` (both act on the Cantor set; `T` is the subgroup preserving the cyclic order),
and `V ≤ nV`. So neither is in `D_T`, and in particular neither is in `𝒞`. ∎

## Remark: why the central quotient is the only exit

By item 1 of `lifted-thompson-t-escapes-haagerup-permanence-closure`, every normal subgroup of
`T̄` is `<z^j>` or `T̄`. So the quotients of `T̄` are `T̄` itself, the central quotients
`T̄/<z^j>` with `j ≥ 1` (which include `T` for `j = 1`), and `1`. Item 4 shows that every
construction by the operations of item 2 from `T̄` and the base classes yields a group through
which `T` maps trivially. So an argument that reaches a nontrivial corona representation of `T`
from one of `T̄` must, at the level of groups, use one of the quotients `T̄ -> T̄/<z^j>`. At the
level of representations this is a corona representation of `T̄` in which `z` is a scalar or of
finite order. That is the central fibre of `lifted-thompson-t-centre-has-commutant-eigencorners`,
or an equivalent twist statement.
