The proof has three parts:
- two lemmas about left orders (§1);
- unique normal forms for `Γ_c`, including transposed letter pairs (§2);
- for each listed class, a finite list of identities in `Γ_c` (§3).

`verify_klein2.py` checks every identity by rewriting and prints each rewriting step. The logs
`verify_X.log` hold the full traces. §4 works through `50_30` #470 by hand. §5 proves a structural
fact about Klein pairs in `Γ^+` that explains why pure words suffice. The proof does not use §5 or the
search (§6).

## 1. Two lemmas on left orders

For `g` in a left-ordered group, write `|g| = max(g, g^{-1})`.

**Lemma 1 (Klein).** Let `<` be a left order on `G`, and let `a, b ∈ G` with `b ≠ 1` and
`a b a^{-1} = b^{-1}`. Then `|b| < |a|`.

This is Lemma 1 of `bmw-census-sat-free-klein-chain-certificates-proof`. Here is its proof again, so
this route stands alone.

*Proof.*
1. The hypothesis is invariant under `b ↦ b^{-1}`. It is also invariant under `a ↦ a^{-1}`, since it
   is equivalent to `a^{-1} b a = b^{-1}`. So assume `b > 1` and `a ≥ 1`.
2. `a ≠ 1`, because otherwise `b² = 1`, while `b > 1` gives `b² > b > 1`. Also `a ≠ b`, because
   otherwise `b = b^{-1}`.
3. Suppose `a < b`, and put `g = b^{-1} a`.
   - Then `g < 1`, so `g² < g < 1`.
   - From `a b^{-1} = b a` we get `g² = b^{-1}(a b^{-1}) a = a²`, and `a > 1` gives `a² > 1`.
   - This is a contradiction.
4. So `a > b > 1 > b^{-1}`, and `|b| = b < a = |a|`. ∎

**Lemma 2 (powers).** Let `<` be a left order on `G`, let `r ∈ G`, and let `e, f` be integers with
`1 ≤ |e| ≤ |f|`. Then `|r^e| ≤ |r^f|`.

*Proof.*
1. `|g| = |g^{-1}|`, so we may assume `e, f > 0`. We may also assume `r ≥ 1`, replacing `r` by `r^{-1}`
   if needed.
2. For `k ≥ 1`, `r^k ≥ 1`. This holds by induction, since `r^k = r · r^{k-1} ≥ r · 1 = r ≥ 1`.
3. So `|r^k| = r^k` for `k ≥ 1`.
4. Also `r^f = r^e · r^{f-e} ≥ r^e · 1 = r^e`, by left invariance. ∎

The left order in Lemma 2 need not be bi-invariant, because only left multiplication is used.

**Corollary 3.** Let `x_0, …, x_{k-1}` be nontrivial elements of `G`, with indices taken mod `k`.
Suppose that for every `i` one of the following holds:
- **(K)** `x_{i+1} x_i x_{i+1}^{-1} = x_i^{-1}`;
- **(P)** `x_{i+1} = x_i^e` for some `|e| ≥ 2`;
- **(R)** `x_i = r^e` and `x_{i+1} = r^f` for some `r ∈ G` and some `1 ≤ |e| < |f|`.

Suppose also that at least one step is (K). Then `G` is not left-orderable.

*Proof.* Lemmas 1 and 2 give `|x_i| < |x_{i+1}|` at every (K) step and `|x_i| ≤ |x_{i+1}|` at every
(P) or (R) step. Going around the cycle gives `|x_0| < |x_0|`. ∎

With (P) and (R) steps allowed, this generalises the argument that the Promislow (Hantzsche–Wendt)
group is not left-orderable. There, one element inverts the square of another, but not the element
itself.

## 2. Normal forms, with transposed letters

Fix a class `c` of `census_{m}{pA}_{n}{pB}.json`. The class has `m` letters `h_i` and `n` letters
`v_j`, with inverse maps `iA`, `iB`. A letter with `iA[x] = x` is an involution. A pair
`x ≠ iA[x]` is a transposed pair, with `x^{-1} = iA[x]`.

The squares give `SQ(h, v) = (v', h')`, meaning `h v = v' h'`. The group is
`Γ_c = ⟨h_i, v_j | x · x^{-1} = 1, h v = SQ(h, v)⟩`. Its parity kernel `Γ_c^+` consists of the elements
represented by words with an even number of `v`-letters and an even number of `h`-letters. Every
relator preserves both parities, so `Γ_c^+` is a well-defined subgroup of index 4.

`verify_klein2.py` first checks the following:
- `iA` and `iB` are involutive permutations, with exactly `2pA` and `2pB` non-fixed points;
- `SQ` is a bijection from the `m·n` pairs `(h, v)` onto the `m·n` pairs `(v, h)`.

The rewriting system is `R = {x x^{-1} → 1 (every letter x), h v → v' h' (every square)}`.

**Termination.** Each rule either shortens the word, or keeps its length and removes one pair
"`h`-letter before `v`-letter".

**Local confluence.** The overlaps of left-hand sides are of three kinds.
- `x x^{-1} x`: both ways give `x`.
- `h h^{-1} v`: one way gives `v`. The other rewrites `h^{-1} v` first and gives `h v'' h''`, which
  must reduce to `v`.
- `h v v^{-1}`: one way gives `h`. The other gives `v' h' v^{-1}`, which must reduce to `h`.

`verify_klein2.py` checks the last two kinds for every pair `(h, v)` before it reads any chain.

**Consequence.** Newman's lemma gives every word a unique `R`-normal form. The normal form is a
freely reduced `v`-word followed by a freely reduced `h`-word. Each rule is a defining relation. So
two words are equal in `Γ_c` exactly when their normal forms agree, and a word is trivial exactly when
it reduces to the empty word.

In particular, a nonempty freely reduced word in one family is a normal form, so it is nontrivial.
Equality of two words is decided by comparing normal forms. This is what the verifier does.

## 3. The certificates

For each listed class `c`, `chains_X.json` gives a list of strings `x_0, …, x_{k-1}`. The string
`v…|h…` means the `v`-word followed by the `h`-word. Some classes also carry a map `roots`, which sends
some step indices `i` to a string `r`.

`verify_klein2.py census_X.json chains_X.json --trace` checks the following for each class.
1. **Each `x_i`, and each `r`, is a nonempty normal form in `Γ_c^+`.** Its `v`-length and `h`-length
   are both even, so it lies in `Γ_c^+`. It is a nonempty normal form, so it is nontrivial by §2.
2. **Each step is (K), (P) or (R).**
   - (K): the word `x_{i+1} x_i x_{i+1}^{-1} x_i` reduces to the empty word.
   - (P): `x_{i+1}^{-1} x_i^e` reduces to the empty word for some `2 ≤ |e| ≤ |x_{i+1}|`.
   - (R): `roots` gives `r` for step `i`, and `x_i^{-1} r^e` and `x_{i+1}^{-1} r^f` reduce to the
     empty word for some `1 ≤ |e| < |f|`.
   - The trace of every reduction is printed, one rule application per line.
3. **At least one step is (K).**

The verifier reads only the census file and the chain file. It shares no code with the search
(`kpure2.py`), with the SAT pipeline, or with the involution-only verifier `verify_klein.py`.

`crosscheck2.py` repeats the identity checks with a different normal-form algorithm. It pushes each
letter through the `h`-suffix using the square table, and compares the resulting normal forms.

By Corollary 3, applied in `G = Γ_c^+`, each listed parity kernel is not left-orderable.

Every group containing `Γ_c^+` is then not left-orderable either, because left orders restrict to
subgroups. In particular `Γ_c` is not left-orderable. ∎

## 4. `50_30` #470 by hand

All letters are involutions. The squares that are not commutations are:

`h0v1=v1h1`, `h0v2=v2h2`, `h1v1=v1h0`, `h1v2=v2h3`, `h2v0=v1h2`, `h2v1=v0h2`, `h2v2=v2h0`,
`h3v0=v1h4`, `h3v1=v0h4`, `h3v2=v2h1`, `h4v0=v1h3`, `h4v1=v0h3`.

So every `h`-letter maps `v2 ↦ v2` and `{v0, v1}` to itself. `h2`, `h3` and `h4` swap `v0 ↔ v1`,
while `h0` and `h1` fix both.

Write `u = v0v1` and `t = h0h1h0`. The certificate (`chains_50_30_470.json`) is:

| | element | shape |
|---|---|---|
| `x_0` | `v2012` | `v2 u v2` |
| `x_1` | `v201012` | `x_0²` |
| `x_2` | `h01024010` | `t · h2h4 · t` |
| `x_3` | `v020120` | `(v0v2) u (v2v0)` |
| `x_4` | `v02010120` | `x_3²` |
| `x_5` | `h01032010` | `t · h3h2 · t` |

The steps are:
- step 0 is (R), with root `x_0`;
- steps 1, 2, 4 and 5 are (K);
- step 3 is (R), with root `x_3`.

Corollary 3 then gives the following cycle of inequalities:

`|x_0| ≤ |x_0²| < |x_2| < |x_3| ≤ |x_3²| < |x_5| < |x_0|`.

The two squarings are essential. Among pure words of length at most 6, every `v`-word that some pure
`h`-word inverts is a conjugate of `u^{±2}`, never of `u`. This is why the pure Klein-chain scan of
`bmw-census-sat-free-klein-chain-certificates` finds nothing for #470, even at length 8.

**Checking a (K) step.** Let `a` be an `h`-word and `b` a `v`-word. Then `a b a^{-1} = b^{-1}` is
equivalent to `a b = b^{-1} a`. That is, pushing `b` leftwards through `a` must give output `b^{-1}`
and section `a`.

Let `a` be a `v`-word and `b` an `h`-word. Then `a b a^{-1} = b^{-1}` is equivalent to
`b^{-1} a = a b`. That is, pushing `a` through `b^{-1}` must give output `a` and section `b`.

In the lists below, each entry `hk: w → w', hk'` means `hk · w = w' · hk'`. It is obtained by applying
the squares letter by letter. The entries run from the rightmost `h`-letter to the leftmost one.

- **Step 1: `x_2` inverts `x_1`.** Push `x_1 = v201012` through `x_2 = h01024010`.
  - `h0: 201012 → 210102, h0`; `h1: 210102 → 201012, h1`; `h0: 201012 → 210102, h0`;
    `h4: 210102 → 201012, h4`.
  - `h2: 201012 → 201012, h2`; `h0: 201012 → 210102, h0`; `h1: 210102 → 201012, h1`;
    `h0: 201012 → 210102, h0`.
  - The output is `v210102 = x_1^{-1}` and the section is `h01024010 = x_2`.
- **Step 2: `x_3` inverts `x_2`.** Push `x_3 = v020120` through `x_2^{-1} = h01042010`.
  - `h0: 020120 → 021020, h0`; `h1: 021020 → 020120, h1`; `h0: 020120 → 021020, h0`;
    `h2: 021020 → 121021, h4`.
  - `h4: 121021 → 021020, h2`; `h0: 021020 → 020120, h0`; `h1: 020120 → 021020, h1`;
    `h0: 021020 → 020120, h0`.
  - The output is `x_3` and the section is `h01024010 = x_2`.
- **Step 4: `x_5` inverts `x_4`.** Push `x_4 = v02010120` through `x_5 = h01032010`.
  - `h0: 02010120 → 02101020, h0`; `h1: 02101020 → 02010120, h1`; `h0: 02010120 → 02101020, h0`;
    `h2: 02101020 → 12101021, h2`.
  - `h3: 12101021 → 02010120, h3`; `h0: 02010120 → 02101020, h0`; `h1: 02101020 → 02010120, h1`;
    `h0: 02010120 → 02101020, h0`.
  - The output is `v02101020 = x_4^{-1}` and the section is `x_5`.
- **Step 5: `x_0` inverts `x_5`.** Push `x_0 = v2012` through `x_5^{-1} = h01023010`.
  - `h0: 2012 → 2102, h0`; `h1: 2102 → 2012, h1`; `h0: 2012 → 2102, h0`; `h3: 2102 → 2102, h2`.
  - `h2: 2102 → 2102, h3`; `h0: 2102 → 2012, h0`; `h1: 2012 → 2102, h1`; `h0: 2102 → 2012, h0`.
  - The output is `x_0` and the section is `h01032010 = x_5`.

The (R) steps are free reductions:
- `x_0² = v2012 · v2012 = v201012 = x_1`;
- `x_3² = v020120 · v020120 = v02010120 = x_4`.

Every entry can be read off the square list above. For example,
`h2 · v0v2v1v0v2v0 = v1 h2 · v2v1v0v2v0 = v1v2 h0 · v1v0v2v0 = ⋯ = v1v2v1v0v2v1 · h4` is the first
entry of step 2's second line, `h2: 021020 → 121021, h4`.

So `Γ_{470}^+` is not left-orderable, with no SAT solver. The only earlier refutation was the 11045771-clause
`R8 (short 4)` positive-cone instance of `bmw-degree-44-53-parity-kernels-not-left-orderable`.

## 5. Every Klein pair is conjugate to a pure pair

This section explains why chains of pure words are the natural certificates. The proof above does
not use it.

Let `Γ` be a BMW group, acting simply transitively on the vertices of `T_h × T_v` by left
multiplication. Here the vertex `g` has first coordinate its `v`-line `g⟨v-words⟩` and second
coordinate its `h`-line `g⟨h-words⟩`.
- `g` fixes the first coordinate `P` of the vertex `1` exactly when `g · 1 = g` lies in the `v`-line of
  `1`, i.e. when `g` is a pure `v`-word.
- Likewise, `g` fixes the second coordinate `Q` of `1` exactly when `g` is a pure `h`-word.

Elements of `Γ^+` preserve the `h`-parity of the vertices of `T_h` and the `v`-parity of the vertices of
`T_v`. So `Γ^+` acts on both trees without inversions, and each of its elements is elliptic (fixes a
vertex) or hyperbolic on each tree.

**Theorem 4.** Let `a, b ∈ Γ^+` with `b ≠ 1` and `a b a^{-1} = b^{-1}`. Then there is `g ∈ Γ` such
that one of `g^{-1} a g`, `g^{-1} b g` is a pure `h`-word and the other is a pure `v`-word.

*Proof.*
1. **No nontrivial element is elliptic on both trees.** If `z` fixes `p ∈ T_h` and `q ∈ T_v`, it fixes
   the vertex `(p, q)`, so `z = 1`.
2. **If `b` is hyperbolic on one tree, then `a` is elliptic on that tree.** Say `b` is hyperbolic on
   `T_v` with axis `ℓ`.
   - `a b a^{-1}` is hyperbolic with axis `aℓ`, translating in the direction that is the image under
     `a` of the direction of `b`.
   - `b^{-1}` has axis `ℓ` and translates in the opposite direction.
   - So `aℓ = ℓ` and `a` reverses the orientation of `ℓ`.
   - A reversal of a simplicial line fixes a vertex or inverts an edge, and `a` inverts no edge. So
     `a` fixes a vertex `q ∈ ℓ`.
3. **`b` is hyperbolic on exactly one tree.**
   - If `b` were hyperbolic on both, step 2 would make `a` elliptic on both, so `a = 1` by step 1.
     Then `b² = 1`.
   - But then `⟨b⟩` is a finite group acting without inversions on each tree, so it fixes a vertex of
     each. By step 1, `b = 1`, a contradiction.
   - By step 1, `b` is also not elliptic on both trees.
4. **Conclusion.** Say `b` is hyperbolic on `T_v` and fixes a vertex `p ∈ T_h`. The other case is
   symmetric.
   - Step 2 gives a vertex `q ∈ T_v` fixed by `a`.
   - Let `g ∈ Γ` be the element with `g · 1 = (p, q)`. Then `g^{-1} b g` fixes `P`, so it is a pure
     `v`-word.
   - And `g^{-1} a g` fixes `Q`, so it is a pure `h`-word. ∎

**Consequences.**
- A (K) step joins an element hyperbolic on `T_v` only (a "`v`-type" element, conjugate into the
  `v`-words) to an `h`-type element, and conversely. Power steps preserve the type.
- So in a certificate, the (K) steps alternate between the two types.
- Every Klein chain in `Γ^+` is a chain of `Γ`-conjugates of pure inverting pairs. The pure scans of §6
  search the chains whose elements are all pure. A search over `Γ`-conjugates of pure inverting pairs
  would cover the rest, and by Theorem 4 such a search is complete as the conjugator radius grows.

## 6. How the certificates were found (not part of the proof)

The certificates come from a graph search over pure words, `kpure2.py` with `--powers`. Commands and
run scripts are in the experiment `README.md`.

**Graph.**
- The nodes are the classes `{x, x^{-1}}` of freely reduced pure words of even length at most `L`.
- An inverting edge `b → a` is added when `a b a^{-1} = b^{-1}`. It is detected by pushing one pure
  word through the other with the square table. By the free-group argument in the README, inverting
  edges only join a `v`-word to an `h`-word.
- A power edge `r^k → r^{k'}` (`k < k'`) is added between pure words with the same primitive root.
- A directed cycle through an inverting edge is a certificate. `collect.py` turns it into a chain file
  and recomputes the roots of the power steps.

**Runs.**
- `L = 6` on all 681 candidate classes of the sixteen transposed-letter patterns, and on the eleven
  reducible Promislow-type classes.
- `L = 8` on `50_30` #470 (the involution-only scan of `bmw-census-sat-free-klein-chain-certificates`
  had found no pure four-term chain for it up to length 8), and on `31_30` #26 and `31_31` #12.

**Results.**
- 167 of the 681 candidates have a certificate, and so do `41_40` #1174 and `41_41` #486, #541 among
  the reducible classes. With #470 there are 171 certificates. The per-pattern list is the claim's
  table.
- Of the 171 chains:
  - 10 are four-term chains of (K) steps alone;
  - 161 use power steps, and 152 of these have six elements with two (R) steps, the shape of #470;
  - the other lengths are 5 (3 chains), 7 (4), 9 (1) and 12 (`41_41` #486).
- All words have length at most 6, except in #470, where they reach length 8.
- `31_30` #26 and `31_31` #12 have no pure inverting pair up to length 8. By Theorem 4 their Klein
  pairs are conjugates of longer pure pairs, if they exist at all.

**Why power steps matter.** The search found four-term (K) chains for only 10 of the 681 candidates. Power steps
raise the yield to about a quarter. In the typical six-element chain, an `h`-word inverts `x²` but
not `x` for a pure `v`-word `x`, which is the configuration of Promislow's group. §4 is an instance.
