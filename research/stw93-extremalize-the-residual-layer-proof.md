Let `tau_1` be a noncanonical normalized trace on `E`.  It charges some
`a in R_+`; after scaling, take `a` to be a contraction.  Evaluation

```text
T(E) -> R,                    rho -> rho(a)
```

is continuous and affine.  Its positive maximum is attained at an extreme
point `sigma` of the compact trace simplex.  Thus `sigma(a)>0` and `sigma`
is an extreme trace.  The GNS von Neumann algebra

```text
M=pi_sigma(E)''
```

is consequently a finite factor.

By `stw93-extra-trace-is-a-central-residual-layer-over-the-factor`, the weak
closure of `pi_sigma(R)` is `zM` for a central projection `z in M`.
Since `sigma(a)>0`, this projection is nonzero.  Factoriality forces `z=1`,
which proves `(1)`.

Every trace on `E` restricts to the canonical trace on the unital section
`s(Q)`.  The coefficient estimate from
`stw93-residual-gns-central-support-proof` shows that `pi_sigma|s(Q)` is
normal for the tracial representation of `Q`; unitality and factorial
simplicity make it faithful.  Its weak closure is therefore the II_1 factor
`Q` sitting as a trace-preserving subfactor of `M`.

Choose `epsilon>0` such that

```text
sigma((a-epsilon)_+)>0.
```

The dimension-function bound in
`stw93-singular-trace-needs-finite-central-cuntz-capacity` makes
`b=(a-epsilon)_+` a finite-capacity element of `E`.  Its GNS image is
nonzero, so its support projection in `M` is nonzero.  Since `b in R`, that
support lies in the weak ideal generated by `pi_sigma(R)`, completing the
factor-completion package.

The passage to an extreme trace is essential.  For a nonextreme trace the
residual weak support can be a proper central projection with scalar
expectation onto `Q`; extremality collapses that projection to the unit.
