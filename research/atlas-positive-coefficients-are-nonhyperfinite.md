**OPEN.**  Suppose a positive purified Atlas certificate exists and let `A`
be the tracial-ultraproduct von Neumann algebra generated by the real and
imaginary parts of its finitely many block-coefficient sequences.  Must `A`
be nonhyperfinite (equivalently, in the standard separable finite setting,
nonamenable)?

Finite-stage fullness does not answer this: every stage is a full matrix
algebra, while their tracial ultraproduct can contain hyperfinite generated
subalgebras.  The tempting proof from the bounded-complexity gaps `(BMG2)`
has a precise quantifier failure, recorded by
`bmg2-does-not-exclude-hyperfinite-atlas`.  A proof needs either a uniform
lower bound on the Atlas gaps along the relevant hyperfinite approximation
modulus, or a new obstruction to hyperfinite approximation which is not
present in `(BMG2)`.
