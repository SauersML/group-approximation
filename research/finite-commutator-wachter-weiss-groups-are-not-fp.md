**Correction (2026-09-18).** The first version (f454b873d, f52803391) claimed that the
automaton group `G_R` itself is locally-finite-by-`Z` and not finitely presented. That
was wrong. `G_R` is generated by *all* states, including the intermediate states `S2`,
`S3`, `S4` and `skip` of the check-mark automaton. These act on the encoding subtree
`W^*` by maps that are not powers of the adder. For example, `skip` fixes every
one-configuration word, while no nonzero power of the adder does. The statement below
separates what survives.

## Setting

Wächter--Weiß (Theorem `thm:nonuniformPSPACE` and its proof) start from any automaton
`R` over an alphabet `Σ ⊇ W = {■0, ■1}` with nontrivial balanced iterated commutators
(their commutator-mode hypothesis, §"The Commutator Mode"). From it they build an
automaton `T_R`. It has:
- a **TM mode**: the binary encoding `T_2` of the automaton `T'` of their Proposition
  `prop:TMmode`, in copies `T_{2,r}`, `r ∈ R`;
- a **commutator mode**: `R`, together with the delayed copy `R_0`.

Their reduction maps each Turing-machine input to a word `q` in the **initial states**:
- the check-mark states `checksub{g}`;
- the start states of the checkers `z`, `f`, `c` and of the transition checkers;
- the start states of `R_0`;
- the `R`-states.

Then `q = 1` iff the machine rejects. Let `G_R = G(T_R)`, generated by all states, and
let `H_R ≤ G_R` be generated by the initial states.

## Statement

Let `R` be a finite group acting on letters of `Σ̃ = Σ \ W` and fixing `■0`, `■1`, as an
automaton with trivial sections. An example is `A_5` on five letters `e_1, …, e_5`,
with `Σ = W ∪ {e_1, …, e_5}`; it satisfies the commutator-mode hypothesis.

1. **Kernel.** Restriction to the invariant subtree `W^*` is a homomorphism
   `ρ: G_R -> Aut(W^*)`. Its kernel `K` embeds in the unrestricted power `R^{W^*}`, so
   `K` is locally finite. It is also infinite.
2. **The automaton group.** `ρ(G_R) = C` is the automaton group of the encoded
   check-mark automaton (Figure `fig:checkmarkingAutomaton`, with its continuation `g`
   read as `id`). So `G_R` is locally-finite-by-`C`. `C` is not cyclic.
3. **The reduction group.** `ρ(H_R) = <c> ≅ Z`, where `c` is the adder. So `H_R` is
   locally-finite-by-`Z`, with infinite kernel `K ∩ H_R`, and is not virtually cyclic.
   Hence **`H_R` is not finitely presented**. More generally, a finitely generated group
   with a homomorphism onto `Z` and locally finite kernel is finitely presented only if
   it is virtually cyclic.
4. **Hardness.** `H_R` has PSPACE-complete word problem.
   - *Lower bound.* The reduction's words lie in `H_R`.
   - *Upper bound.* Words in `H_R` are words in states of an automaton group, which is
     in `PSPACE`.
   - *Scope.* Wächter--Weiß state their theorem for a binary alphabet. Their proof is
     written for general `Σ ⊇ W` and any `R` meeting the commutator-mode hypothesis
     (§"Encoding over Two Letters"), and its two directions use nothing else about `R`.
     The accepting direction needs a letter moved by the commutator; the rejecting
     direction uses Fact `fct:malformedWords`.

## What this does and does not show

- `H_R` is a PSPACE-complete finitely generated group that is not finitely presented.
  It is not self-similar, so it is not a candidate input for Zaremsky's Theorem 1.1
  anyway.
- **Whether `G_R` is finitely presented is open**, for finite `R` as for the binary
  headline group (`R` = Aleshin's `F_3` on `W`). The paper says nothing about finite
  presentation.
  - *For finite `R`,* the question is about the extension `K -> G_R -> C`.
  - *For the Aleshin group,* the `R`-states act on `■0`, `■1` themselves, so even
    step 1 fails. The same holds for any `R` that moves the encoding letters.
- **Röver--Nekrashevych overgroups.** Zaremsky's corollary `cor:simple_and_qr`
  (arXiv:2305.15176v2) needs a weakly diagonal action: some generating set `S` with
  `s_x s^{-1}` of finite order in `G^{ab}` for every level-1 state. Every section of
  every element of `G_R` at a `Σ̃`-letter is trivial. So `G_R` is weakly diagonal only
  if its abelianization is finite. That was not checked. When the corollary does apply,
  it gives a quasi-retraction `V_d(G) -> G`. With Alonso's theorem (finite presentation
  passes to quasi-retracts) and Skipper--Witzel--Zaremsky Theorem 4.15, `V_d(G)` is then
  finitely presented iff `G` is. So a Röver--Nekrashevych overgroup cannot make
  finite presentation easier in that setting.
- **Finitely presented self-similar overgroups.** Any finitely presented self-similar
  group containing `H_R` would have PSPACE-hard word problem. By
  `fp-self-similar-groups-embed-in-fp-simple-groups`, it would give a finitely
  presented simple group beyond Birget's coNP. Whether one exists is
  `decidable-fg-self-similar-groups-embed-in-fp-self-similar` for this input.
