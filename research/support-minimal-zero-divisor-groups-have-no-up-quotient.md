Let `k` be a field and `G` a group. Suppose `k[G]` has zero divisors. Among
all pairs of nonzero `alpha, beta in k[G]` with `alpha beta = 0`, choose one
minimizing `|supp alpha| + |supp beta|`. Put

```text
H = < a^-1 c : a, c in supp alpha >.
```

Then:

1. `H = < b c^-1 : b, c in supp beta >`. After translating to
   `g^-1 alpha` and `beta h^-1`, with `g in supp alpha` and `h in supp beta`,
   the pair is again support-minimal. Both supports contain `1`, both lie in
   `H`, and each of them generates `H`.
2. Every homomorphism from `H` to a group with unique products is trivial.
3. Consequently, `H` is a nontrivial finitely generated group with:
   - finite abelianization;
   - no nontrivial homomorphism to `Z`, to a free group, or to a
     left-orderable group;
   - no unique products.

   In particular, `H` is not locally indicable.

So the zero-divisor conjecture over `k` holds for all torsion-free groups iff
it holds for every finitely generated torsion-free group `H` with no
nontrivial homomorphism to a unique-product group. In that reduction the
zero divisor may be taken with `1` in both supports, and with each support
generating `H`.

This is the support-minimal form of the standard crossed-product argument that
a unique-product quotient preserves the domain property. No source for it was
re-checked on 2026-09-16. The graph proof is self-contained.
