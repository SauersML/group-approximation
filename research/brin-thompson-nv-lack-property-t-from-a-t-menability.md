title: "An infinite a-T-menable group has no property (T), so a-T-menable nV lack (T)"

Let `G` be infinite with a proper conditionally negative definite function
`ψ`. Schoenberg's theorem makes `e^{-tψ}` positive definite for every `t > 0`,
and these functions converge to `1` pointwise as `t → 0`. They vanish at
infinity since `ψ` is proper. The corresponding unitary representations
therefore have almost invariant vectors. If `G` had (T), one of them would have
a nonzero invariant vector, whose coefficient function is a nonzero constant.
But each coefficient `⟨π(g)ξ, ξ⟩` of a `C_0`-representation vanishes at
infinity, and this cannot be constant on an infinite group. So `G` does not
have (T).

Each `nV` is infinite. So a-T-menability of every `nV` gives the absence of (T)
for every `nV`.
