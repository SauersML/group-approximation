**Input (Higman 1974).** Belk--Bleak--Matucci--Zaremsky, *Progress around the
Boone--Higman conjecture*, arXiv:2306.16356v3, Theorem 4.3(3), verbatim: "The
following countable groups embed into Thompson's group V: ... (Higman 1974
[HigmanSimple, Theorem 6.6]) Any countable group (such as Q/Z, and the finitely
supported infinite symmetric group) that is an ascending union of finite
groups". Theorem 4.1 of the same survey: "Thompson's group V is simple and
finitely presented."

**Proof.**
- `K` is countable, since it is algebraic over the countable field `F_p`.
  Enumerate it as `a_1, a_2, ...`.
- Let `F_k` be the subfield generated by `a_1, ..., a_k`. It is finite, being a
  finitely generated algebraic extension of `F_p`. The `F_k` ascend, and their
  union is `K`.
- So `GL_n(K)` is the ascending union of the finite groups `GL_n(F_k)`, because
  every matrix has finitely many entries.
- By the quoted theorem, `GL_n(K)` embeds in `V`. If `K` is finite, `GL_n(K)`
  is a finite group, and that case is already in Theorem 4.3(1).
`∎`
