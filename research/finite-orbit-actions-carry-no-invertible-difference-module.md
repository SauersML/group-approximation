**ESTABLISHED** by `finite-orbit-invertible-difference-module-proof`. Elementary;
no priority is claimed.

## Statement

Let `p` be a prime and `X` a set. Let `V <= Sym(X)` be an elementary abelian
`p`-group and `a in Sym(X)` a permutation with `a V a^(-1) = V`. Write `V`
additively and put `c(v) = a v a^(-1)`. Assume:

- **(H1)** every `V`-orbit on `X` is finite;
- **(H2)** every `v in V` moves points in only finitely many `V`-orbits;
- **(H3)** the map `c - 1 : V -> V`, `v |-> c(v) - v`, is onto.

Then every `v in V` has a finite `c`-orbit.

## Placements covered

1. **Finitary permutations.** If every element of `V` moves only finitely many
   points of `X`, then (H1) and (H2) hold. This is the case whenever `V` is
   generated by finitary permutations.
2. **Finite lamps, abelian or not.** Let `B` be a finite group, `Q` a group acting
   on a set `Omega`, and `W = B^(Omega) x| Q` acting on `X = Omega x B` by
   `(f, g) . (s, b) = (g s, f(g s) b)`. If `V` lies in the base `B^(Omega)`, then
   (H1) and (H2) hold, and `a` may be any element of `W` normalizing `V`.

## Sharpness

Neither finiteness hypothesis can be dropped.
- **Without (H2).** Let `R = F_p[t, t^(-1), (t-1)^(-1)]` and `L = R x| Z^2`, the
  two generators acting as multiplication by `t` and by `t - 1`. `L` acts faithfully
  on the disjoint union of the coset spaces `L/K`, over all finite-index subgroups
  `K <= R`. The `R`-orbits have size `[R : c_g(K)]`, so (H1) holds. With `a` the first
  generator, (H3) holds, yet `t^n` runs through infinitely many elements of `R`.
- **Without (H1).** The same `L` acts on `R` affinely. There is one `R`-orbit, so
  (H2) holds, and the conclusion fails as before.

## Use

`kms-configuration-letters-avoid-finite-orbit-placements` applies it to the
Kharlampovich--Myasnikov--Sapir Minsky-machine groups. It closes every placement of
their configuration module into finite lamps, abelian or not, and into finitary
permutations.
