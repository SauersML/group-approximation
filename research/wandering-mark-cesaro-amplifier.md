Let `Gamma=<S|R>` be finitely presented, let `p in Q[Gamma]`, and let
`w in Gamma`.  Put

```text
Sigma_t=1+w+...+w^(t-1).                               (WCA1)
```

Assume there are constants `a>0,B<infinity` and, for every fixed `t`, a
modulus `omega_t(delta)->0` such that:

```text
||p Sigma_t||_(2,tau_Gamma)^2 >= a t                 (WCA2)
```

in the canonical group trace, while every finite-dimensional unitary tuple
`U` satisfies

```text
||p(U) Sigma_t(U)||_2^2
 <= B+omega_t(Def_R(U)).                              (WCA3)
```

Then `Gamma` is nonhyperlinear.

## Proof

Choose `t>B/a`.  Canonical-trace hyperlinear microstates reproduce the
finitely many moments in `p Sigma_t`, so their squared normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm converges to the left side of `(WCA2)`, which is greater
than `B`.  Their relator defect tends to zero, and `(WCA3)` bounds the same
quantity by `B+o(1)`, a contradiction.

## Dirichlet certificate

A particularly useful way to prove `(WCA3)` is to take `p=P` to be a
projection after fixed packet exactification and prove, for the weighted
microstate state generated by `Sigma_t`,

```text
tr(Sigma_t^* P Sigma_t)
 <= C tr(Sigma_t^*(1-Re W)Sigma_t)+omega_t(def),      (WCA4)
```

where `W=w(U)`.  For every unitary `W`, spectral calculus gives

```text
Sigma_t^*(1-Re W)Sigma_t <= 2 I.                      (WCA5)
```

Indeed `(1-cos theta)|sum_(k<t)e^(ik theta)|^2`
equals `2 sin^2(t theta/2)` and is at most two.  Hence `(WCA3)` holds with
`B=2C`.

For the native involution mark `P=(1-d)/2`, `(WCA2)` is completely explicit
if the `2t` group elements

```text
w^k, d w^k,             0<=k<t,                      (WCA6)
```

are pairwise distinct: then

```text
||P Sigma_t||_(2,tau_Gamma)^2=t/2.                   (WCA7)
```

Thus one robust finite-matrix inequality

```text
P <= C(1-Re w)                                          (WCA8)
```

in the sense of `(WCA4)`, together with a Britton/normal-form proof of
wandering, is enough to produce a finitely presented nonhyperlinear group.
The inequality must be matrix-only; a universal tracial operator inequality
would contradict `(WCA7)` in the regular representation.
