OPEN. Is there an amenable group `Q` containing
`BS(1,2) = <x_0, x_1 | x_1^(-1) x_0 x_1 = x_0^2>` as a subgroup, together with
elements `x_2, x_3, ...` such that

    x_(j+1)^(-1) x_j x_(j+1) = x_j^2     for all j >= 0 ?

## Why this is the next test

* **It gives (AA) for BG.** Put `x_j = 1` for `j < 0`. Then
  `W_0(x_j, x_(j+1)) = x_(j+1)^(-1) x_j x_(j+1) x_j^(-2) = 1` for all `j in Z`,
  and `psi(a_j) = x_j` is a homomorphism from the Magnus kernel of BG to `Q`
  that is injective on the piece `B_0 = BS(1,2)`. So it separates every finite
  set as hypothesis (AA) of
  [[amenable-alphabet-magnus-shift-dictionary-sofic]] requires. BG is already
  sofic by amenable-edge HNN permanence, so this is a calibration of the
  dictionary at a singular extreme, not a new soficity result. If the answer is
  **no**, no amenable alphabet passes the zero-exponent forward step at BG, and
  singular extremes are out of reach of the shift dictionary.
* **Where it must live.** By
  [[bg-shift-chains-die-in-residually-linear-alphabets]], `Q` is not residually
  periodic, linear, solvable or compact. `M = <x_j : j >= 0>` is perfect.
* **Inductive form.** It suffices to prove the extension lemma: *every
  amenable group `P` with an element `s` of infinite order embeds in an
  amenable group containing `t` with `t^(-1) s t = s^2`*. Iterate from
  `P = BS(1,2)`, `s = x_1`, and take the directed union, which is amenable. The
  new letter `t` automatically has infinite order, since `t^m = 1` forces
  `s = s^(2^m)`.

## What is known here

* **The first step exists and is solvable.** Let `R` be the ring generated by
  all `2^(±2^(-k))`, `A = R^((Z))` with basis `e_n`, `alpha e_n = 2^(2^(-n)) e_n`
  and `beta e_n = e_(n-1)`. As operators, `beta^(-1) alpha beta = alpha^2`,
  since both send `e_n` to `2^(2^(1-n)) e_n`. In `A rtimes <alpha, beta>`, with
  `g v g^(-1) = g(v)`, put `x_0 = e_0`, `x_1 = alpha^(-1)`, `x_2 = beta`. Then
  `x_1^(-1) x_0 x_1 = alpha(e_0) = 2 e_0 = x_0^2`, and
  `x_2^(-1) x_1 x_2 = (beta^(-1) alpha beta)^(-1) = alpha^(-2) = x_1^2`. The group is
  solvable, since `<alpha, beta>` lies in (diagonal operators) `rtimes <beta>`. It is a length-3 chain faithful on
  `BS(1,2)`, since `<e_0, alpha>` contains `Z[1/2] e_0 rtimes <alpha>`.
* **The obstruction to naive iteration.** Adding `x_3` with
  `x_3^(-1) beta x_3 = beta^2` forces the lower abelian layers to be rebuilt,
  because `beta` must acquire square roots and its conjugation must intertwine
  two distinct spectra. `Q` itself cannot be solvable, since `M` is perfect,
  and by the chain obstruction it cannot be residually linear.
* **Sufficient condition, not verified.** If some amenable `P' >= P` has an
  injective endomorphism `theta` with `theta(s) = s^2`, the ascending HNN
  extension of `(P', theta)` is amenable and supplies `t`. This reduces the
  extension lemma to building self-similar amenable overgroups.
