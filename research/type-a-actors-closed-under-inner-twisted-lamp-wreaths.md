**ESTABLISHED** for the closure theorem (lane proof, elementary; not reviewed; no priority claimed).
The corollary is a conditional reduction.

## Closure theorem

Let `Γ ↷ S` and `Λ ↷ Y` be faithful actions of type (A). Fix `s_0 ∈ S`, put `Γ_0 = Stab_Γ(s_0)`, and
let `h : Γ_0 -> Λ` be any homomorphism.
- `I_h` is the group of functions `f : Γ -> Λ` with `f(γ γ_0) = h(γ_0)^-1 f(γ) h(γ_0)` for
  `γ_0 ∈ Γ_0`, supported on finitely many cosets `γΓ_0`, under pointwise product. `Γ` acts on it by
  `(γ·f)(γ') = f(γ^-1 γ')`.
- `W_h = I_h ⋊ Γ`. For `h = 1` this is `Λ wr_S Γ`.
- `Z = Γ ×_(Γ_0) Y`, the quotient of `Γ × Y` by `(γ γ_0, y) ~ (γ, h(γ_0) y)`. It fibres over
  `S = Γ/Γ_0` with fibre `Y`.
- `W_h` acts on `Z` by `γ'·[γ, y] = [γ'γ, y]` and `f·[γ, y] = [γ, f(γ) y]`. This is well defined, since
  `f(γγ_0) h(γ_0)^-1 = h(γ_0)^-1 f(γ)`.

**Theorem.** `W_h ↷ Z` is of type (A).

**Proof.**
- **Faithful.** `Z -> S` is equivariant. So an element `(f, γ)` acting trivially has `γ = 1`. Then
  `f(γ') ∈ Λ` acts trivially on `Y` for every `γ'`, so `f = 1`.
- **Finite presentation.** Write `Λ_(s_0)` for the functions supported on `Γ_0`, a copy of `Λ` via
  `f ↦ f(1)`. `W_h` is generated by `Γ` and `Λ_(s_0)`, with these relations:
  - (R1) a finite presentation of `Γ`;
  - (R2) a finite presentation of `Λ`;
  - (R3) `γ_0 λ γ_0^-1 = h(γ_0) λ h(γ_0)^-1`, for `γ_0` in a finite generating set of `Γ_0` and `λ` a
    generator of `Λ`;
  - (R4) `[λ, g λ' g^-1] = 1`, for `λ, λ'` generators of `Λ` and `g` in a finite set of representatives
    of the double cosets `Γ_0 g Γ_0 ≠ Γ_0`. These double cosets are the off-diagonal orbits on `S × S`.

  In the group `P` presented this way:
  - (R3) makes `Λ_s := g Λ g^-1` (for `s = g s_0`) depend only on `s`.
  - Conjugating by `Γ_0`, which normalizes `Λ_(s_0)` by (R3), extends (R4) from representatives to all
    pairs `s ≠ s'`.
  - So the normal closure of `Λ` in `P` is a quotient of `⊕_S Λ_s`, and `P` maps onto
    `W_h = (⊕_S Λ_s) ⋊ Γ` with trivial kernel.

  This is Cornulier's argument for untwisted wreaths. The twist enters only through (R3).
- **Pair orbits.** Move pairs by `Γ` to lie over `(s_0, s_0)` or over one of finitely many
  representatives `(s_0, g s_0)`.
  - Over `(s_0, s_0)`: `Λ_(s_0)` has finitely many orbits on `Y × Y`.
  - Over `(s_0, g s_0)`: `Λ_(s_0) × Λ_(g s_0)` has (number of `Λ`-orbits on `Y`)^2 orbits.
- **Stabilizers.** Let `z_0 = [1, y]`. Then `(f, γ)` fixes `z_0` iff `γ ∈ Γ_0` and
  `f(1) h(γ) ∈ Λ_y`. So `Stab(z_0)` is generated by:
  - `D = {(λ_γ, γ) : γ ∈ Γ_0}`, where `λ_γ` is the lamp at `s_0` with value `h(γ)^-1`. This is a
    subgroup isomorphic to `Γ_0` (direct check with (R3)).
  - The lamps `Λ_y` at `s_0`.
  - The lamps `Λ_s` for `s ≠ s_0`. `D` permutes these as `Γ_0` permutes `S ∖ {s_0}`, with finitely many
    orbits.

  So `Stab(z_0)` is finitely generated, because `Γ_0`, `Λ_y` and `Λ` are. ∎

## Corollary (the twisted chain lamplighter)

Let `A = ⟨a, b | a^-1 b a = b^2⟩` and `E = ⟨c, e | e^-1 c e = c^2⟩`, both copies of `BS(1,2)`, and let
`N ≅ Z[1/2]` be the normal closure of `c` in `E`. Suppose:

**(H)** some type (A) actor `Γ ↷ S` contains `A`, there is a point `s_0` with `A ∩ Γ_0 = ⟨b⟩`, and
there is a homomorphism `h : Γ_0 -> Λ` into a type (A) actor `Λ ⊇ E` with `h(b) = e`.

Then `⟨A, N_(s_0)⟩ ≤ W_h` is the twisted coset lamplighter
`M_1 = (⊕_(A/⟨b⟩) Z[1/2]) ⋊ A` of `higman-chain-group-pbh-iff-chain-configuration`. So `M_1 ∈ B_A`,
and by (2) ⟹ (1) there the chain group `G_1` (the vertex group of `H4`, the base `K` of `BG`) lies in
`B_A`. That last step uses the lane chain `bs12-base-generator-is-a-centralizer-in-b-a` and item 3 of `pbh-amalgams-over-centralizer-separated-edges`, both unreviewed.

*Proof.* `A` permutes the lamp groups `Λ_s`, `s ∈ A·s_0 ≅ A/⟨b⟩`, and these commute pairwise. `b ∈ Γ_0`
acts on `N_(s_0)` by conjugation with `h(b) = e`, which doubles. So `⟨A, N_(s_0)⟩` is the induced
module semidirect `A`, and `A` meets the lamps trivially. ∎

## What (H) asks for

- **The stabilizer homomorphism.** A character `χ : Γ_0 -> Z` with `χ(b) = 1` is enough: compose with
  `1 ↦ e`. Such characters exist at nonsingular rational points of finite germ extensions of `V`, as the
  germ degree `Stab(p) -> (V)_p ≅ Z`.
- **a must move s_0.** If `a ∈ Γ_0` then `χ(b) = χ(a^-1 b a) = χ(b^2)`, so `χ(b) = 0`.
- **b is non-tame.** `b` fixes `a^n s_0` for all `n >= 0`, with germ degree `2^n`, so it has infinitely
  many hyperbolic fixed points. It cannot be tame anyway: by
  `tame-power-conjugators-have-more-hyperbolic-points`, tame `a`, `b` would give `F_b ⊊ F_a`, which puts
  `s_0` in `F_a`.
- **Natural actions fail.** In the standard actions of `BS(1,2)` on the extended line, on `Q_2 ∪ {∞}`,
  and in the doubling extension of `V` (`zoom-tower-germ-groups-give-f-infinity-germ-extensions`), every
  fixed point of `b` is fixed by `a`.
- **The target.** A finitely presented finite germ extension of `V`, of type (A) on one orbit, containing
  `BS(1,2)` whose base generator has a regular hyperbolic fixed point of degree one, moved by the dilator.
  Its fixed points `a^n s_0` accumulate at a singular point.

## Lesson for general BH

**Put the twist in the actor, not in a cocycle.** A twisted induced module is cohomologically
non-trivial. Its Kaloujnine–Krasner cocycle takes the value `b^(2^n)` at every coset `a^n⟨b⟩` that `b`
fixes, and no change of transversal alters values at fixed cosets. So no restricted wreath untwists it.
The way around is to let the **point stabilizer act on its own lamp**, through a homomorphism to the lamp
actor. Type (A) survives this: pair orbits, stabilizers and Cornulier's presentation all go through
with one extra family of relations. The price moves to the host, which needs a point stabilizer with a
free "degree" on the input's base generator. That is a germ, and the germ must be non-tame.
