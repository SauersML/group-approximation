Notation as in the target. All operators act on countable-dimensional
`F_2`-spaces. "Finite rank" means finite-dimensional image.

## Step 0. Two linear-algebra facts

**(I) Index.** Suppose `X : U -> U` has finite-dimensional kernel and
cokernel. Put `ind X = dim ker X - dim coker X`. If `F` has finite rank, then
`X + F` has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel and the same index.

Proof. Let `U_0 = ker F`, a subspace of finite codimension `c`. Then `X + F`
and `X` agree on `U_0`.
- For any `Y` with finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel and any `U_0` of
  codimension `c`, the restriction `Y|U_0 : U_0 -> U` has index `ind Y - c`.
  This holds for `c = 1` by a direct count of kernel and image, and in general
  by induction on `c`.
- Apply this to `Y = X` and `Y = X + F`. The two restrictions to `U_0` are the
  same map, so `ind X - c = ind (X+F) - c`.

**(II) Compression.** For `j in J` and `f = pi_+(j)`, the operator `j` on
`V_+`, the compression `P_+ f^ P_+`, and `P_+ f^` all differ by finite rank.
Here `f^` is the Laurent operator of `f` on `W`, and `P_+-` are the coordinate
projections. The mirror statement holds for `V_-` and `pi_-`. Also `P_- f^ P_+`
and `P_+ f^ P_-` have finite rank.

Proof.
- The normal forms `S^a T^b` span `J`. The operator `S^a T^b` sends `e_k` to
  `e_(k+a-b)` for `k >= b` and to `0` for `k < b`.
- The compression of `z^(a-b)` agrees with it except on the finitely many
  `e_k` with `k < max(b, b-a)`.
- `f^` has finite propagation, so it moves only boundedly many coordinates
  across `0`. This gives the off-diagonal blocks.
- For `V_-`, the substitution `e'_k = e_(-1-k)` reverses the direction of the
  line, so `S` compresses `z^(-1)` there. This is `pi_- = theta o pi_+`.

## Step 1. (MQ1) GL_n(J) = EL_n(J)

The ideal `I = JQJ` is the span of the matrix units `S^aQT^b`, and
`J/I = A` (`binary-jacobson-weak-sofic-status-proof`, Section 1).

1. **The kernel of the symbol lies in `EL_n(J)`.** Let `g in GL_n(J)` with
   `pi_+(g) = 1`. Then `g - 1` and `g^(-1) - 1` have entries in `I`, so `g`
   lies in some corner `GL_(nm)(F_2)`.
   - Over `F_2`, that group is generated by transvections.
   - A cross-block transvection is `x_ij(S^aQT^b)`.
   - A within-block transvection is the commutator of two cross-block ones,
     through a third block (this needs `n >= 2`).
   - So `g in EL_n(J)`.
2. **Every symbol has determinant 1.** Let `g in GL_n(J)`. Then
   `det pi_+(g) = z^k` for some `k`, since the units of `A` are the monomials.
   - `A` is Euclidean, so `SL_n(A) = E_n(A)`. Write
     `pi_+(g) = e diag(z^k, 1, ..., 1)` with `e in E_n(A)`.
   - Every elementary matrix over `A` lifts to one over `J`. Lift `e` to
     `e~ in EL_n(J)`.
   - Then `g' = e~^(-1) g` has symbol `diag(z^k,1,...,1)`. So `g'` differs by
     finite rank from `D = diag(S^k,1,...,1)` if `k >= 0`, or from
     `diag(T^(-k),1,...,1)` if `k < 0`.
   - `ind S^k = -k` and `ind T^m = m`, so `ind D = -k`.
   - `g'` is invertible, so its index is `0`. By (I), `k = 0`.
3. **Conclusion.** So `g'` is in the kernel of the symbol, and by item 1,
   `g = e~ g' in EL_n(J)`. Also `pi_+(GL_n(J)) = SL_n(A)`, because every
   elementary symbol lifts.

## Step 2. (MQ2) The mirror fibre product lies in H_bi

Let `(g,h) in P` and `gamma = pi_+(g) = pi_-(h) in SL_n(A)`.
- By (II), entrywise, `g - P_+ gamma^ P_+` and `h - P_- gamma^ P_-` have finite
  rank, and so do the off-diagonal blocks of `gamma^`.
- Hence `X = g (+) h` satisfies `X - gamma^ = finite rank`.
- The same holds for `X^(-1) = g^(-1) (+) h^(-1)` with `gamma^(-1)`.
- So `gamma^(-1)^ X - 1` has finite rank, and `gamma^(-1)^ X` is invertible.
  Hence `gamma^(-1)^ X in GL_fin(W)`, and `X in GL_fin(W) gamma^`.

`EL_n(A) = SL_n(A)`, and `GL_fin(W)` meets the Laurent operators only in `1`:
a Laurent matrix of finite rank is zero. So `GL_fin(W) EL_n(A)^` is the
concrete realization of `H_bi = GL_fin(W) x| EL_n(A)`. Block-diagonal sums
multiply blockwise, so `(g,h) |-> g (+) h` is an injective homomorphism
`P -> H_bi`.

`H_bi` is LEF by `untwisted-bilateral-symbol-enrichment-is-lef`, and LEF passes
to subgroups. So `P` is LEF.

## Step 3. (MQ3) and (MQ4)

**Surjectivity.** By Step 1, `pi_-` maps `E_-` onto `SL_n(A)`. So for every
`g in E_+` there is `h` with `(g,h) in P`.

**Kernel.** The kernel of the projection is `{(1,h) : pi_-(h) = 1}`, and by
Step 1, item 1, this is `{1} x L_-`.
- `L_-` is the increasing union of the `GL_m(F_2)`.
- These are simple for `m >= 3`, so `L_-` is locally finite and simple.

**The head.** `w - 1 = Q e_13` has rank one, so `(w,1)` maps to the finitary
transvection `1 + e_((0,1)) (x) e_((0,3))^*` of `W`. This is not the identity.

**(MQ4).** Let `s` be a homomorphic section of `pi_-` over `pi_+(A_0)`. Then
`a |-> (a, s(pi_+ a))` is an injective homomorphism `A_0 -> P`.
- For polynomial matrices in `z`, the Toeplitz rules give `S^a S^b = S^(a+b)`.
  So compression is multiplicative on `M_n(F_2[z])`.
- This gives a section over `SL_n(F_2[z])`.
- Under `theta`, and with `T` in place of `S`, the same works for
  `SL_n(F_2[z^(-1)])`.

## Step 4. (MQ5) No normalized finite kernel

Suppose `M <= L_-` is finite and normalized by a generating set of `P`. Its
projection to `E_-` generates `E_-`, so `M` is normalized by `E_-`.
- Put `U = sum over m in M of (m-1)V_-^n`. This is finite-dimensional.
- For `h in E_-`, `hU = sum over m of (hmh^(-1) - 1)V_-^n = U`. So `U` is
  `E_-`-invariant.
- The `F_2`-span of `E_-` contains every `x_ij(r) - 1 = r e_ij` (with `i != j`).
  Their products give all of `M_n(J)`, so `U` is an `M_n(J)`-submodule.

`V_-^n` is a simple `M_n(J)`-module:
- for `v != 0`, some `Q T^a` sends a coordinate of `v` to a nonzero multiple of
  `e_0`;
- then the matrix units reach every basis vector.

Hence `U = 0` (it is finite-dimensional, while `V_-^n` is not). So `M = 1`.
