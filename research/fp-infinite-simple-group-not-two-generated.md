**OPEN.** Printed by Y. Lodha, *A finitely presented infinite simple group of
homeomorphisms of the circle*, arXiv:1710.06220v2, section "Open questions"
(TeX FPSimple.tex l.1689–1691):

> Is there a finitely presented infinite simple group which cannot be generated by
> two elements?

Lodha notes the finitely generated version (Kourovka) was answered affirmatively by
Guba, and also asks (l.1700–1702) whether his group `S` is 2-generated and what the
minimal number of relations for `S` is.

**Why it sits next to Boone–Higman.** The finitely presented simple hosts used on
main (V, nV, twisted Brin–Thompson groups `SV_G`, Röver–Nekrashevych commutator
subgroups) are the natural places to look, and those whose rank is known are
2-generated (V by Mason; Lodha notes T is). A positive answer needs an invariant bounding the generating rank of a simple group from below, since
abelianization and finite quotients give nothing. Lower bounds from actions (e.g.
ranks of germ groups or of stabilizers in type (A) actions) are the natural
candidates.

**Also printed.** AIM problem list *Amenability of discrete groups*
(http://aimpl.org/amenablediscrete), section "Other problems", Problem 5.2: "Is there a
finitely presented simple group that is not 2-generated?" (accessed 2026-09-18; recorded
in `research/artifacts/gq-bh-openq-lists.md`).

## Attempts

1. **Vigorous hosts are excluded (2026-09-18, lane bh-openq-papers; literature).**
   *Generating simple vigorous groups*, arXiv:2607.21754, Theorem 1.1 (TeX l.162–166): "Let G ≤
   Homeo(C) be a finitely generated simple vigorous group. Let m ≥ 2 and n ≥ 3. Then G is
   (m, n)-generated." The paper says (l.136, l.409) that this class contains:
   - `V`, the Higman–Thompson groups `V_n'` and the Brin–Thompson groups `nV`;
   - Nekrashevych's groups of dynamical origin;
   - the finitely generated twisted Brin–Thompson groups.

   So every finitely presented simple group produced by the Boone–Higman machinery on main
   (twisted Brin–Thompson hosts of type (A) actions) is 2-generated. The same holds for any
   vigorous Cantor full group. A positive answer must come from a simple group that is not
   vigorous. Candidates:
   - Burger–Mozes and Rattaggi lattices in products of trees;
   - Caprace–Rémy simple Kac–Moody lattices;
   - groups acting on the circle or the line, such as Lodha's `S` from arXiv:1710.06220, whose
     own 2-generation is Lodha's l.1700 question.
2. **Rank lower bounds for simple groups (lane remark).** An infinite simple group has no
   nontrivial finite quotient, and no nontrivial finite-dimensional representation over a
   field (by Malcev, a finitely generated linear group is residually finite). So bounds from
   quotients, profinite completions and representation varieties give nothing. What remains:
   - `d(G) ≥ 1 + b_1^(2)(G)`. It needs a finitely presented simple group with `b_1^(2) > 1`.
   - For lattices in products of trees, `ℓ²`-Betti numbers are concentrated in degree 2, so
     `b_1^(2) = 0`.
   - Kazhdan groups have `b_1^(2) = 0`.
   - A group generated by infinite subgroups `H_1, …, H_n` with `b_1^(2)(H_i) = 0` and
     `H_i ∩ H_(i+1)` infinite has `b_1^(2) = 0` (standard chain lemma, recalled, not re-read).
     Thompson-like groups are generated by chains of infinite cyclic subgroups with commuting
     neighbours, so they are excluded again.

   Judged deep; not pursued further.
3. **Circle groups containing T are 2-generated; Lodha's S is (2026-09-18, lane bh-free-13).**
   - `fg-simple-circle-groups-with-t-are-two-generated` (lane proof): every finitely generated
     simple `G ≤ Homeo_+(S^1)` containing a conjugate of Thompson's `T` is generated by a finite-order
     rotation in `T` and one further element.
   - The method is Bleak–Elliott–Hyde's commutator extraction, in the circle form BHM use for `TA`. Two things
     are new: `T` is generated by a rotation together with one copy of `F` inside a small arc, and every
     generator is written as boundedly many commutators of elements supported in that arc. So no perfect
     arc group is needed.
   - Corollary `lodha-simple-group-s-is-two-generated`: this answers the first half of Lodha's l.1700
     question, and removes `S` from the candidate list of Attempt 1.
   - What survives on the circle is a finitely presented simple circle group that contains no conjugate of `T`.
     No such group is known here. So the live candidates are:
     - the product lattices (Burger–Mozes, Wise, Rattaggi);
     - Caprace–Rémy Kac–Moody lattices;
     - groups of homeomorphisms of the line;
     - non-vigorous Cantor groups.
4. **The L² route is Osin's problem (2026-09-18, lane bh-free-13).**
   - *Families closed.* The bound `d ≥ 1 + β_1^(2)` gives nothing for:
     - every simple group with a fixed-point-free tree action with a finitely generated edge stabilizer, which
       includes all simple product-of-trees lattices (`simple-groups-on-trees-with-fg-edge-groups-have-b1-zero`);
     - every locally moving simple group (`locally-moving-groups-have-vanishing-first-l2-betti-number`);
     - every Kazhdan group.
   - *In general.* A finitely presented simple group with `β_1^(2) > 0`
     (`fp-simple-group-with-positive-first-l2-betti-number`, OPEN) would refute Osin's printed problem
     `fp-positive-l2-betti-groups-are-acylindrically-hyperbolic`. So the L² route to this question requires a
     negative answer to Osin's problem, with a Betti number above 1.
   - *Precedent.* Osin–Thom (arXiv:1108.2411, TeX l.84) have finitely generated simple groups with
     `β_1^(2) ≥ n − 1 − ε`. Their groups are limits of hyperbolic groups, not finitely presented.
   - *Lesson.* Every known finitely presented simple group has an infinite, finitely generated, ws-normal
     subgroup of infinite index, which forces `β_1^(2) = 0` (Peterson–Thom). A three-generator answer through
     L² needs a finitely presented simple group with none: a "hyperbolic-like" finitely presented simple
     group. Otherwise it needs a rank invariant that is not L², and none is known for simple groups.
