Standard facts used:
- (H) A countable group `G` is hyperlinear iff `L(G)` is CE (Radulescu). CE passes to
  trace-preserving subalgebras, and `L(H) ⊆ L(G)` trace-preservingly for `H ≤ G`.
- (P) Pontryagin duality. For a countable discrete abelian group `C` with an action of `Lambda` by
  automorphisms, the dual `K = Ĉ` is compact metrizable abelian and `Lambda` acts on it by continuous
  automorphisms that preserve Haar measure. The Fourier transform `L(C) ≅ L^infinity(K, Haar)` sends
  `u_c` to the character `chi_c` and intertwines the two actions. Hence `L(C ⋊ Lambda) ≅ L^infinity(K) ⋊ Lambda`.
  Conversely, every compact metrizable abelian `K` with a continuous automorphic `Lambda`-action is
  `Ĉ` for `C = K̂`, which is countable and discrete, with the dual action.

**Step 1 (Kaloujnine--Krasner, explicit).** Let `A ⊲ G` with quotient map `p : G -> Lambda`, and fix a
set-theoretic section `s : Lambda -> G` with `s(1) = 1`. Let `A^Lambda` be the group of all functions
`Lambda -> A` under pointwise product, with `Lambda` acting by `(mu . f)(lambda) = f(mu^-1 lambda)`.
Define

```text
f_g(lambda) = s(lambda)^-1 g s(p(g)^-1 lambda),        phi(g) = (f_g, p(g)) in A^Lambda ⋊ Lambda.
```

- `f_g(lambda)` lies in `A`, because its image in `Lambda` is `lambda^-1 p(g) p(g)^-1 lambda = 1`.
- `phi` is a homomorphism. Inserting `s(p(g)^-1 lambda) s(p(g)^-1 lambda)^-1` gives
  `f_(gh)(lambda) = f_g(lambda) f_h(p(g)^-1 lambda) = (f_g · (p(g) . f_h))(lambda)`, which is the
  semidirect-product law.
- `phi` is injective. If `p(g) = 1` and `f_g = 1`, then `g = s(1)^-1 g s(1) = f_g(1) = 1`.

Let `C` be the subgroup of `A^Lambda` generated by `{mu . f_g : mu in Lambda, g in G}`. It is countable,
`Lambda`-invariant, and abelian when `A` is abelian. Since `phi(g) = (f_g, p(g))` with `f_g in C`,
`phi(G) ≤ C ⋊ Lambda`.

**Step 2 (item 1).**
- (a) ⟹ (b): `C ⋊ Lambda` has abelian normal subgroup `C` with quotient `Lambda`.
- (b) ⟹ (c): by (P), `L^infinity(K) ⋊ Lambda ≅ L(K̂ ⋊ Lambda)`, which is CE by (b) and (H).
- (c) ⟹ (a): by Step 1, `G ≤ C ⋊ Lambda`, so by (H) and (P),
  `L(G) ⊆ L(C ⋊ Lambda) ≅ L^infinity(Ĉ) ⋊ Lambda`. This is CE by (c), so `G` is hyperlinear.

**Step 3 (item 2).** Assume `Q1(Lambda)`. By item 3 of `free-action-covariant-models-contain-the-crossed-product`,
quoted verbatim: "(a) every pmp action of `Gamma` has a covariant model in a Connes-embeddable algebra;
... (c) every pmp action of `Gamma` has a Connes-embeddable crossed product." These are equivalent
there. So the Haar action `Lambda ↷ (K, Haar)` has a CE crossed product, which is (c) of item 1.

This holds whether or not `K` is atomic. When `Lambda` is infinite, item 2 there pads it to the
nonatomic free action on `K × {0,1}^Lambda`, whose crossed product contains `L^infinity(K) ⋊ Lambda`.
When `Lambda` is finite, `L^infinity(K) ⋊ Lambda` is injective, hence CE.

If `A` is central, then `G/A` is hyperlinear and `A` is abelian, so this is a special case.

**Step 4 (item 3).** Assume Q1, so item 2 holds for every hyperlinear quotient.
- *Finite kernel.* Let `F ⊲ G` be finite with `G/F` hyperlinear. Put `Z = C_G(F)`. It is normal in `G`,
  because `F` is. `G/Z` embeds in the finite group `Aut(F)`. Also `Z ∩ F = Z(F)` is abelian and
  central in `Z`, and `Z/Z(F) ≅ ZF/F ≤ G/F` is hyperlinear. By item 2, `Z` is hyperlinear.
  `amenable-quotient-preserves-metric-approximability` says, verbatim: "be an exact sequence of groups
  with `A` amenable. ... if `N` has the property, then `G` has the same property", for the property
  "hyperlinear". Apply it to `1 -> Z -> G -> G/Z -> 1`. So `G` is hyperlinear.
- *Chains.* Let `N = N_0 ≥ ... ≥ N_k = 1` be normal in `G` with each `N_i/N_(i+1)` abelian or finite.
  Induct on `i`: `G/N_i` is hyperlinear. The base `i = 0` is the hypothesis. For the step,
  `G/N_(i+1)` has normal subgroup `N_i/N_(i+1)` with quotient `G/N_i`. Item 2 applies if that factor
  is abelian, and the finite case above applies if it is finite. At `i = k`, `G` is hyperlinear.
- *Solvable kernel.* The derived series `N^(j)` of `N` is characteristic in `N`, hence normal in `G`.
  It reaches `1` with abelian factors, so it is such a chain.

**Step 5 (item 4).** Each listed candidate `X` has a normal subgroup that is abelian (or solvable) with
hyperlinear quotient:
- the Kun--Thom wreath `W = (Z/2) wr_(G/Gamma) G`: base abelian, quotient residually finite;
- `A ⋊ Lambda` for the algebraic envelopes (the groups compiled by `algebraic-envelope-principle`):
  base a countable abelian module;
- `W_N = (⊕_(L/N) C_2) ⋊ SL_3(Z)` and `A_0 ⋊ SL_3(Z)`: abelian base, quotient linear;
- a central extension `E` of a hyperlinear group: central kernel.

For the envelopes the conclusion needs no group at all. A non-CE algebra in `L^infinity(K) ⋊ Lambda`
with `Lambda` hyperlinear contradicts `Q1(Lambda)` directly, by item 3(c) of the cited equivalence.
If `X` is non-hyperlinear, item 2 (or item 3) shows Q1 fails. For the Kun--Thom double this goes
through `kt-wreath-hyperlinear-iff-double-hyperlinear`, as w11 recorded.

**Step 6 (item 5).**
- *QF ⟹ NH ∨ ¬Q1.* Let `Gamma ↷ X` be free with a non-CE crossed product. If `Gamma` is not
  hyperlinear, NH holds. Otherwise (c) of the cited item 3 fails for `Gamma`, so Q1 fails.
- *NH ⟹ QF.* A non-hyperlinear `Gamma` is infinite. Its Bernoulli shift on `{0,1}^Gamma` is
  essentially free, and the crossed product contains `L(Gamma)`, which is non-CE.
- *¬Q1 ⟹ QF.* Q1 fails at some hyperlinear `Gamma`, which is infinite because finite groups have
  injective crossed products. Some action of `Gamma` has a non-CE crossed product. Item 2 of the
  cited claim pads it to a free action whose crossed product contains it, so it is still non-CE.
- *Q2 ⟹ QF.* Q2's own Necessary conditions record that `L^infinity(X) ⋊ Gamma_0` is not CE, since the
  crossed product is a covariant model. `Gamma_0` is infinite, being dense in `[R]` over a nonatomic
  space. Pad as above.

Under Q1, `QF ⟺ NH`, and the witness group of QF is non-hyperlinear, by the first bullet.
