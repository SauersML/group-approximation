Let `A=<S|T>` be finitely presented and let

```text
F_X=F_2[A]^k,       F_Z=F_2[A]^ell
```

be finite-rank left regular modules.  Let `R_X<=F_X` and `R_Z<=F_Z` be
`A`-submodules generated by finite lists of finitely supported vectors, and
put `M_X=F_X/R_X`, `M_Z=F_Z/R_Z`.  Suppose there is an `A`-invariant
bilinear form

```text
beta:M_X times M_Z -> F_2,
beta(a u,a v)=beta(u,v).                                    (CBP1)
```

Fix finitely many mixed prototypes `(i,p,j,b)` with
`i<=k`, `j<=ell`, `p in A`, and `b in F_2`, and suppose

```text
beta([e_i],[p e_j])=b.                                      (CBP2)
```

This hypothesis has an exact linear-algebraic criterion.  Put

```text
C=(M_X tensor_F2 M_Z)_A                                     (CBP3)
```

for diagonal coinvariants and let

```text
c_(i,p,j)=[[e_i] tensor [p e_j]] in C.
```

The prescribed signs extend to an invariant `beta` if and only if

```text
sum_q alpha_q c_q=0    implies    sum_q alpha_q b_q=0         (CBP4)
```

for every binary linear dependence among the finite prototype list.  Thus
the global sign-safety question is a concrete separation problem in one
coinvariant tensor module, not an additional quantifier over van Kampen
diagrams.

Adjoin involutions `x_1,...,x_k,z_1,...,z_ell,J` to `A`, make `J`
central, impose the finitely many same-basis prototype words represented by
the chosen generators of `R_X,R_Z`, and impose

```text
[x_i,p z_j p^-1]=J^b                                       (CBP5)
```

for the mixed prototypes.  One may also impose any finite list of pure-X or
pure-Z commutator prototypes.  The resulting finite presentation has an
exact unitary representation with `J=-I`.  Every translated occurrence and
every translated local face is a literal conjugate of one of the displayed
prototypes.

Consequently no bounded or unbounded multi-basin contour can identify the
two signs in this presentation.  Relative girth, graphical piece bounds,
and a separate composite-diagram argument are unnecessary once `(CBP1)`--
`(CBP2)`, equivalently `(CBP4)`, have been exhibited.

The theorem is a certificate, not an existence assertion for the private
sampler.  In that application the remaining task is to place the repeated
LDPC occurrence relations in finite-rank regular `F_2[A]`-modules and make
the sampled Weyl signs the restriction of one invariant pairing on their
quotients.
