**ESTABLISHED** through `lef-burnside-groups-iff-finite-simple-limits-proof`.

Marked groups, LEF, convergence and `LRB(m,N)` are as in
`local-restricted-burnside-iff-lef-burnside-groups-are-finite`. `R(m,N)` is the
restricted Burnside group of `restricted-burnside-finiteness`.

**Statement.** Fix `N >= 2`.
1. The following are equivalent:
   - (i) some infinite finitely generated LEF group has exponent dividing `N`;
   - (ii) some infinite finitely generated group of exponent dividing `N` is the
     limit, in the space of marked groups, of a sequence of nonabelian finite
     simple groups;
   - (iii) `LRB(m,N)` fails for some `m >= 2`.
2. **Generator bound.** If the group in (i) is generated by `m` elements, the
   group in (ii) can be taken generated by at most `|R(m,N)|(m-1)+1` elements.
   So if no infinite group of exponent dividing `N` with at most
   `|R(m,N)|(m-1)+1` generators is a limit of nonabelian finite simple groups,
   `LRB(m,N)` holds.
3. **Shape.** The group `T` in (ii) can be taken to be perfect, with no
   nontrivial finite quotient, and a quotient of a finite-index subgroup of the
   group in (i). For every `d`, only finitely many of its simple approximants
   have a faithful linear representation of dimension `<= d` over a field.
4. **Coulon stages.** Let `N` be odd with `N >= n_1(F_m)`, and let `G_k` be the
   stages of `free-burnside-is-a-limit-of-hyperbolic-groups`.
   - If no infinite finitely generated group of exponent dividing `N` is a limit
     of nonabelian finite simple groups, then `G_k` is not residually finite for
     every sufficiently large `k`.
   - If infinitely many `G_k` are residually finite, then some infinite group of
     exponent dividing `N` is a limit of nonabelian finite simple groups.

So the LEF form of the Burnside problem at exponent `N` is Caprace's Problem
`prob:limits`(ii), "Can an infinite group of finite exponent be a limit of
non-abelian finite simple groups?" (arXiv:1709.05949, l.1299), at that exponent.
Caprace's Corollary `cor:Olsh5` and the remark after it (l.1344–1348) state the
stronger form for the Burnside groups themselves, under a hypothesis
`E_G(G) = {1}` not checked here for the Coulon stages. Clause 4 avoids it. No
novelty is claimed: the argument is elementary.

**Remark (not used).** By the classification of finite simple groups (not
pinned here), the simple approximants in clause 3 are eventually alternating
groups of unbounded degree or classical groups of unbounded rank.
