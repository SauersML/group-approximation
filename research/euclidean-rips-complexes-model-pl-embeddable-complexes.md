For every finite simplicial complex `K` that is PL-embeddable in `R^n` there
is a finite `X ⊆ R^n` with `R(X) ≃ K`. In particular every finitely presented
group is the fundamental group of the Rips complex of a finite subset of
`R^4`.

Context for Zaremsky Problem 4.2: in `R^4` this already gives Rips complexes
that are not wedges of spheres (for example `RP^2` embeds in `R^4`, so its
homology torsion is realized). In the plane the theorem only yields complexes
PL-embeddable in `R^2`, which are homotopy equivalent to graphs. So dimension
two is exactly where the wedge question is open.
