Conventions: `p` is odd, `[x,y]=x^-1y^-1xy`, `G=Gamma^` is the pro-p
completion of `Gamma=Gamma^A2_p`, `H=I_1`. `P_1=G`,
`P_{k+1}=[P_k,G]P_k^p` (closed subgroups). `gr_k(G)=P_k/P_{k+1}`, an
`F_p`-space. Group commutators induce a graded Lie bracket
`gr_i x gr_j -> gr_{i+j}`, and `x -> x^p` induces `pi: gr_k -> gr_{k+1}`.
From the criterion claim `kms-hc1-a2-pro-p-completion-is-iwahori-criterion`
(part (D) and its proof, section 1) we use: `phi^: G -> H` is onto,
`P_k(H)` is the height filtration, and `gr(H)=L_p` as a graded Lie algebra,
generated in degree 1 by `E_12, E_23, tE_31`. Since `phi^` is onto, it maps
`P_k(G)` onto `P_k(H)`. So `gr(phi^)` is a surjective map of graded Lie
algebras. Also `|Gamma/P_k(Gamma)|=|G/P_k(G)|`.

## 1. (G): `pi=0` on `gr(G)`

**Hall--Petresco.** For `x,z` in a group,
`(xz)^p = x^p z^p c_2^{binom(p,2)} ... c_{p-1}^{binom(p,p-1)} c_p`, with
`c_i` in `gamma_i(<x,z>)`.

*Step 0: `x^p in P_3` for every `x in G`.* Take `x,z in G` with `x^p,z^p in P_3`.
Then `c_i in P_i`, so `c_i^{binom(p,i)} in P_i^p <= P_{i+1} <= P_3` for
`2<=i<=p-1`. Also `c_p in P_p <= P_3`, because `p>=3`. Hence
`(xz)^p in P_3`. This also holds for `z in P_2`: then `z^p in P_3` and
`c_i in P_{i+1}`. The generators satisfy `a^p=b^p=c^p=1`. So the set
`{x : x^p in P_3}` contains the generators and `P_2`, and is closed under
products modulo `P_2` (use the `z in P_2` case). So it is all of `G`, since
`G/P_2` is elementary abelian and generated by `a,b,c`. (Finite quotients
`G/P_N` suffice throughout, since each `P_k` is closed of finite index.)

*Induction.* Suppose `y^p in P_{j+2}` for all `y in P_j`, for some `j>=1`.
Let `y in P_j`, `g in G` and `u=[y,g] in P_{j+1}`. Then
`[y^p,g] = y^-p (y u)^p = u^p c_2^{binom(p,2)} ... c_p`, with
`c_i in gamma_i(<y,u>)`. For `i>=2` every basic commutator involves `u`, so
`c_i in P_{ij+1} <= P_{2j+1}`. Hence `c_i^{binom(p,i)} in P_{2j+2} <= P_{j+3}`
for `2<=i<=p-1`, and `c_p in P_{pj+1} <= P_{j+3}`, as `(p-1)j>=2`. The left
side lies in `[P_{j+2},G] <= P_{j+3}`. So `u^p in P_{j+3}`. Now let
`x in P_{j+1}`. By the hypothesis `P_j^p <= P_{j+2}`, so modulo `P_{j+2}` the
element `x` is a product of commutators `[y,g]^{+-1}`. By Hall--Petresco in
`P_{j+1}` (there `c_i in P_{2j+2}` and `c_i^p`, `c_p` lie in `P_{j+3}`), and
since `w^p in P_{j+3}` for `w in P_{j+2}`, the p-th power is additive modulo
`P_{j+3}`. So `x^p in P_{j+3}`, which completes the induction.

So `pi=0` on every `gr_k(G)`. Then
`gr_{k+1}(G) = image of [P_k,G]P_k^p = [gr_k(G), gr_1(G)]`, so `gr(G)` is
generated in degree 1 as a Lie algebra. `gr_1(G)=G/P_2=F_p^3` with basis
`a,b,c`. Map `e_1,e_2,e_0 -> a,b,c`. This gives `gr(G)=F/J`. Since
`gr(phi^)` sends `a,b,c` to `E_12,E_23,tE_31`, we get `J <= I`. **(G)**

## 2. (B): the Hopf bound

Assume `|G/P_j(G)| = |H/P_j(H)|` for `j<=k`. Then
`dim F_j/J_j = dim F_j/I_j` for `j<=k-1`, and with `J<=I` this gives
`J_j=I_j` for `j<k`. In degree `k`, `[F,I]_k = sum_{i>=1} [F_i, I_{k-i}]`
with `k-i<k`. So `[F,I]_k = [F,J]_k <= J_k`. Hence

```text
dim gr_k(G) - dim gr_k(H) = dim I_k - dim J_k <= dim (I/[F,I])_k.
```

For `L=F/I` with `F` free, the five-term exact sequence of
`0 -> I -> F -> L -> 0` in Lie algebra homology reads
`H_2(F) -> H_2(L) -> I/[F,I] -> H_1(F) -> H_1(L) -> 0`. Here `H_2(F)=0`, and
`H_1(F)=F_1 -> H_1(L)=L_1` is an isomorphism, because `I` starts in degree
`>=2`. Everything is graded. So `H_2(L_p)_k = (I/[F,I])_k`, which proves
(B). If the towers agree through class `c` (that is, `|G/P_j|=|H/P_j|` for
`j<=c+1`), induction on `k` with this bound gives agreement for every
`k<=c'` with `c'` the least element of `D_p` greater than `c`. So the towers
agree through class `c'-1`. **(B)**

## 3. (K): Kassel cocycles

Fix `m>=1`. Define `omega_m` on the basis of `L_p` by
`omega_m(t^aX, t^bY) = a tr(XY)` if `a+b=mp`, and `0` otherwise.

- *Alternating.* `omega_m(t^bY,t^aX) = b tr(XY) = (mp-a) tr(XY) = -a tr(XY)`
  modulo `p`, and `omega_m(x,x)=a tr(X^2)[2a=mp]`. For a basis element `X`
  either `tr(X^2)=0` (off-diagonal) or `X=h` in degree `3a` with `2a=mp`. In
  the latter case, write `mp=2a`; the value is `a tr(h^2)` and `2a=mp=0` in
  `F_p`, so `a=0` in `F_p`. So `omega_m` is alternating.
- *Cocycle.* For `x=t^aX`, `y=t^bY`, `z=t^cZ` with `a+b+c=mp`,
  `omega([x,y],z)+omega([y,z],x)+omega([z,x],y)
  = ((a+b)+(b+c)+(c+a)) T = 2mp T = 0`, where
  `T=tr([X,Y]Z)=tr([Y,Z]X)=tr([Z,X]Y)`. If `a+b+c!=mp`, all terms vanish.
- *Homogeneous of degree `3mp`.* `tr(XY)!=0` on root vectors forces opposite
  weights, and `tr(hh')` pairs heights `3a` and `3b`. So the degrees add to
  `3(a+b)=3mp`.
- *Not a coboundary.* A coboundary of degree `3mp` has the form
  `f([x,y])` with `f` linear on `(L_p)_{3mp}`. Now
  `[E_12, t^{mp}E_21] = [tE_12, t^{mp-1}E_21] = t^{mp}(E_11-E_22)`, and both
  pairs lie in `L_p` because `mp-1>=1`. The values of `omega_m` on the two
  pairs are `0` and `1`. So no `f` exists.

The Chevalley--Eilenberg complex splits by degree, and each degree is
finite-dimensional. So `H^2(L_p)_{3mp} = (H_2(L_p)_{3mp})^*` is nonzero.
Hence `3mp in D_p`, and `dim H^2(L_p)=infinity`. **(K)**

## 4. (O)

Let `Ltilde` be the central extension of `L_p` by `F_p z` given by `omega_m`.
Lift `e_1,e_2,e_0` to `Ltilde`, and let `S` be the subalgebra they generate.
`S` maps onto `L_p`. If `z` were not in `S`, then `S` would be a complement
and `omega_m` would split, contradicting (K). So `Ltilde` is generated in
degree 1. It satisfies the Serre relations, since `Ltilde=L_p` in degree
`3<3mp`. It has dimension 3 in degree `3mp`, while `L_p` has 2 there. Hence
an upper bound for `gr(G)` that uses only degree-1 generation, the Serre
relations and the relations of `L_p` below `3mp` cannot force the `I_1`
tower at class `3mp`. Also `dim H_2(L_p)=infinity` makes the graded relation
count useless for `r(I_1)`. The element `x_12(s)`, `s in F_p[[t]]`, has
`x_12(s)^p=1`, and `x_12(F_p[[t]])` is an infinite elementary abelian closed
subgroup. A p-adic analytic group has no such subgroup, because its closed
abelian subgroups are finitely generated `Z_p`-modules up to finite index.
So `I_1` is not uniform and Lazard's method does not apply. **(O)**

## 5. (C): the computation

`h2loop.py p K` builds, for each `k<=K`, the basis of `(L_p)_k`: `t^nE_ij`
with `3n+j-i=k` (`n>=0`, and `n>=1` when `i>j`), plus `t^n h_0`, `t^n h_1` when
`k=3n`. Brackets are matrix commutators mod `p`. It forms the degree-`k`
pieces of `Lambda^2 L_p` and `Lambda^3 L_p` (increasing basis tuples of total
degree `k`), and computes over `F_p` the ranks of
`d_2(x^y)=[x,y]` and
`d_3(x^y^z)=[x,y]^z-[x,z]^y+[y,z]^x`. This is the Chevalley--Eilenberg
differential, up to a global sign. The script then reports
`dim H_2(L_p)_k = dim Lambda^2_k - rk d_2 - rk d_3`. Two checks: degree 3
gives the six Serre relations for every `p`, which matches the rational
answer of Garland--Lepowsky (length-2 affine Weyl elements), and `p=3`
degree 6 gives 6, equal to the observed tower excess (T3). The outputs
`h2_p*.out` record the tables of (C).

## 6. (T) and (Cons)

`pqtower.sh` feeds the standalone ANU p-Quotient program the presentation
`{a^p, b^p, c^p, [a,b,a], [a,b,b], [b,c,b], [b,c,c], [a,c,a], [a,c,c]}`.
The relation `[a,c,a]=[a,c,c]=1` is equivalent to `[c,a]` being central in
`<c,a>`. The program prints `|Gamma/P_{k+1}|` for each class `k`.
`towers.out` records the orders: `5^134` at class 50, `7^120` at class 45
and `11^94` at class 35, with every increment in `3,3,2` repeating. So
`|Gamma/P_k|=|I_1/P_k|` for `k<=51` (`p=5`), `k<=46` (`p=7`) and `k<=36`
(`p=11`). The next elements of `D_p` are `60`, `63` and `66` (section 5). By
(B) the towers agree through classes 59, 62 and 65. By criterion (R),
clause 3, of the required claim, every p-group quotient of `Gamma` of class
`<= 59` (resp. `62`, `65`) is a quotient of `I_1/P_60(I_1)` (resp. `P_63`,
`P_66`), so it kills `w_p`. For `p=13`, the tower of the required claim
agrees through class 20. The next element of `D_13` is 39 (section 5), which
gives the bound 38. **(T), (Cons)**
