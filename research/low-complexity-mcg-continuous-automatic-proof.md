**Imports used as printed.** BBCMP (preliminary, `gq/src/bbcmp/`):
- **(P1)** Proposition 1.4(1): finite groups and `Z` are continuous automatic.
- **(P2)** Proposition 1.4(2): continuous automaticity passes to commensurable groups.
  Its proof is not in the preliminary text read, so it is used as stated.
- **(P3)** Proposition 1.4(4) and Example 2.7: free products; finite-rank free groups are
  continuous automatic.

**Standard mapping class group facts.** From Farb--Margalit, *A primer on mapping class
groups*; recalled, not re-read.
- **(M1) Capping.** Let `D_n` be the disk with `n` marked points, so `Mod(D_n) ≅ B_n`.
  Capping the boundary by a once-punctured disk gives a homomorphism
  `Mod(D_n) → Mod(S_{0,n+1})`.
  - Its kernel is generated by the boundary twist `T_∂ = Δ^2`.
  - Its image is the stabilizer of the new puncture, of index `n+1`.
  - `Δ^2` generates the center of `B_n` for `n ≥ 3`.
- **(M2) Birman--Hilden in genus two.** `Mod(S_2)/⟨ι⟩ ≅ Mod(S_{0,6})`, where `ι` is the
  hyperelliptic involution, which is central of order 2.
- **(M3)** `Mod(S_{1,0}) ≅ Mod(S_{1,1}) ≅ SL_2(Z)`.
- **(M4)** Mapping class groups of finite-type surfaces are virtually torsion-free
  (Serre: the level-3 congruence subgroup).

**The surfaces.**
1. **`S_{0,n}` with `n ≤ 3`.** The mapping class group is finite, so (P1) applies.
2. **`S_{0,n+1}` with `n ≥ 3`.**
   - By (M1), `B_n/⟨Δ^2⟩` is isomorphic to a subgroup of index `n+1` in `Mod(S_{0,n+1})`.
   - `B_n` is the Artin group of type `A_{n−1}`, of finite type, and `Δ^2` is central.
     So `artin-groups-mod-central-garside-power-are-continuous-automatic` (with `c = 2`)
     makes `B_n/⟨Δ^2⟩` continuous automatic.
   - By (P2), so is `Mod(S_{0,n+1})`.
   - This covers every `S_{0,m}` with `m ≥ 4`.
3. **`S_{1,0}` and `S_{1,1}`.** `SL_2(Z)` has a free subgroup of finite index (for
   example, the commutator subgroup, free of rank 2). By (P3) and (P2), with (M3), both
   groups are continuous automatic.
4. **`S_{2,0}`.**
   - Let `Γ ≤ Mod(S_2)` be torsion-free of finite index (M4). Then `Γ ∩ ⟨ι⟩ = 1`.
   - So the quotient map of (M2) is injective on `Γ`, and it sends `Γ` onto a
     finite-index subgroup of `Mod(S_{0,6})`.
   - Hence `Mod(S_2)` and `Mod(S_{0,6})` are commensurable.
   - `Mod(S_{0,6})` is continuous automatic by item 2, so `Mod(S_2)` is too, by (P2). □
