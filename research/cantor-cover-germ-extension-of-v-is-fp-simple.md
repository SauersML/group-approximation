**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-free-08, 2026-09-18; not reviewed; no priority claimed.
This is the Cantor analogue of Belk–Hyde–Matucci's `VA`, whose germ groups at dyadic points
are `T̄`, and it may coincide with a group in the literature).

## Objects

- `C = {0,1}^N` with Thompson's group `V`.
- `Ω_0 = V·0^∞` and `Ω_1 = V·1^∞`. For rational `q`, `τ_q` generates `(V)_q ≅ Z`.
- **The group.** `V♯` is the set of homeomorphisms `h` of `C` such that:
  - `h` preserves every `V`-orbit;
  - `h` is locally in `V` off a finite set `F_h ⊆ Ω_0 ∪ Ω_1`;
  - at each `q ∈ F_h`, the germ at `q` of `v∘h` commutes with `τ_q`, where `v ∈ V` is any element with `v(h(q)) = q`.

  The condition does not depend on `v`, since two choices differ by a `V`-germ fixing `q`, which is a power of `τ_q`. Germs compose, so `V♯` is a group.
- **The translation and the lamplighter.** `t ∈ V` is `0x ↦ 00x`, `10x ↦ 01x`, `11x ↦ 1x`, and `D = [10]`.
  - Then `X_2 := C ∖ {0^∞,1^∞} = ⊔_{k∈Z} t^k(D)`, with `t^k(D) = [0^k1]` for `k ≥ 1` and `[1^(1−k)0]` for `k ≤ 0`.
  - `V̄` is the set of maps `t^k y ↦ t^(k+d(y)) f(y)` (`y ∈ D`), extended by fixing `0^∞` and `1^∞`, where `f` is in the full group `V_D ≅ V` of `D` and `d: D → Z` is locally constant.

## Statement

1. `V♯` is a finite germ extension of `V` with `sing(V♯) = Ω_0 ∪ Ω_1`, and its germ group at
   `0^∞` (and at `1^∞`) is the full centralizer `Cent(τ) ≅ C(C,Z) ⋊ V` of
   `bhm-corollary-hosts-over-v-are-cover-centralizer-groups` (c).
2. `V♯` is finitely generated and **finitely presented**, by BHM Corollary 2.10 with `n = 2`.
3. `V♯` is **simple**, by BHM Theorems 1.3 and 1.4.
4. `V̄ ≤ V♯`, `V̄ ≅ C(C,Z) ⋊ V`, and `Q ≤ V̄`.
5. **Universality for the method.** Every finite germ extension `G` of `V` that is generated by
   `V` and elements fixing their singular points, has `sing(G) ⊆ Ω_0 ∪ Ω_1`, and satisfies the
   normal clause of Corollary 2.10, is a subgroup of `V♯`. So on these orbits the normal clause
   of Corollary 2.10 embeds exactly the subgroups of the single group `V♯`.

## Proof

**1. Finite germ extension.**
- Singular points are finite by definition.
- An element with no singular points is locally `V`, hence in `V` since `V` is full.
- For BHM's condition (3), take `g ∈ V♯`, `q ∈ sing(g)` and `v ∈ V` with `v(g(q)) = q`; the germ of `vg` at `q` is some `(f,d) ∈ Cent(τ_q)`.
  - Define `k` on a punctured neighbourhood `W = ⊔_{j≥m} τ_q^j D_q` by `(f,d)`.
  - For `m` large, `C∖W` and `C∖k(W)` (with `q` removed) are nonempty clopen sets. Any two nonempty clopen subsets of `C` are identified by a piecewise prefix replacement, which extends `k` to a homeomorphism fixing `q`, locally `V` off `q`, with germ `(f,d)`.
  - So `k ∈ V♯`, and `v^{-1}k` agrees with `g` near `q` with singular set `{q}`.
- The same construction realizes every element of `Cent(τ_q)`, so the germ group at `q` is all of it.

**2. Generation.**
- `C(C,Z) ⋊ V` is generated by `V` and `e = 1_{[0]}`: every proper nonempty clopen set is a `V`-translate of `[0]`, and `1_C = 1_{[0]} + 1_{[1]}`.
- By induction on `|sing(g)|` (multiply by the single-singularity element from 1), `V♯` is generated by `V` and elements with one singular point.
- Conjugating by `V`, those can be taken to fix `0^∞` or `1^∞`. Modulo `V` they are products of lifts of the finitely many generators of the two germ groups.

**Finite presentation.** Check the three hypotheses of Corollary 2.10 (TeX lines 527–540) with `n = 2`:
- `V` acts with finitely many orbits on `(Ω_0 ∪ Ω_1)^2` (BHM line 591 for each orbit; mixed pairs form one orbit, by prefix replacements on disjoint cones).
- `Fix_V(M)` is `F∞` for finite sets `M` of rational points (BHM Appendix A, Theorem `thm:Stabilizers`).
- `(V)_q = ⟨τ_q⟩` is the central subgroup of constants in `Cent(τ_q) ≅ C(C,Z) ⋊ V`. It remains to show that the quotient `Q_0 = (C(C,Z)/Z) ⋊ V` is finitely presented. Take:
  - `⟨X | R⟩`, a finite presentation of `V`;
  - `U = Stab_V([0]) ≅ V × V`, which is finitely generated;
  - `a, b, σ ∈ V` with `a[0] = [00]`, `b[0] = [01]` and `σ[0] = [1]`;
  - `c_1, …, c_N ∈ V` representing the finitely many `U`-orbits of proper nonempty clopen sets. The orbit is determined by which of `B∩[0]`, `[0]∖B`, `B∩[1]`, `[1]∖B` are empty.

  Write `e^g = g e g^{-1}` and let
  `Γ = ⟨X, e | R, [e,u] (u ∈ gens U), e = e^a e^b, e·e^σ = 1, [e, e^(c_i)] (i ≤ N)⟩`.
  Sending `e` to the class of `1_{[0]}` maps `Γ` onto `Q_0`. It is injective:
  - `⟨X⟩ ≅ V`, since `V` is simple and maps onto itself.
  - `N = ⟨⟨e⟩⟩` is generated by `e_A := e^g` with `A = g[0]`, and this is well defined because `U` fixes `e`.
  - Every pair `(A,B)` is a `V`-translate of `([0], u c_i[0])`, so all `e_A` commute.
  - `V` is transitive on ordered clopen partitions into 3 (respectively 2) proper pieces. So `e_A = e_(A') + e_(A'')` for every splitting of a proper `A`, and `e_A + e_(A^c) = 0`.
  - Sending `Σ n_i 1_(A_i)`, over a partition into at least two proper pieces, to `Σ n_i e_(A_i)` is well defined by refinement and kills `1_C`. It inverts the map `N → C(C,Z)/Z`.

  So `Γ ≅ Q_0` is finitely presented, and `V♯` has type `F_2`.

**3. Simplicity.**
- `V` is simple, locally moving, and has no global fixed points. `V♯` is generated by `V` and elements fixing their singular points, so BHM Theorem 1.3 makes `(V♯)'` simple.
- By Theorem 1.4, since `V` is perfect, `V♯/(V♯)' ≅ A_(0^∞) ⊕ A_(1^∞)`, where `A_q = coker((V)_q^ab → Cent(τ_q)^ab)`.
- `(C(C,Z) ⋊ V)^ab = C(C,Z)_V ⊕ V^ab`, and `C(C,Z)_V = 0`: all `[1_A]` are equal to one class `x`, `1_{[0]} = 1_{[00]} + 1_{[01]}` gives `x = 2x`, and `[1_C] = 2x = 0`.
- So `V♯` is perfect, hence simple.

**4. The lamplighter and `Q`.**
- Elements of `V̄` are homeomorphisms of `X_2`: bounded `d` gives continuity at the two ends. They are locally `V` and commute with `t`. Near `0^∞`, `t = τ_(0^∞)`; near `1^∞`, `t = τ_(1^∞)^{-1}`. Composition is `(f,d)(f',d') = (ff', d∘f' + d')`, so `V̄ ≅ C(D,Z) ⋊ V_D ≤ V♯`.
- For `Q`, take mixed radix `m_j = j+1` and nested clopen partitions `P_j` of `D` into `m_1⋯m_j` cones, compatibly identified by prefix replacements.
  - Let `r_j ∈ V_D` add 1 to the digit string `(i_1,…,i_j)` with `i_j` least significant, carrying toward `i_1` and wrapping.
  - Let `d_j = 1` on the piece with all digits maximal, and `γ_j = (r_j, d_j)`.
  - Then `γ_(j+1)^(m_(j+1)) = γ_j`, because the digit `i_(j+1)` wraps once and the full overflow occurs exactly when `(i_1,…,i_j)` is maximal. Also `γ_1^(m_1) = (id,1) = t`.
  - Each `γ_j` has infinite order, so `⋃_j ⟨γ_j⟩ ≅ ⋃_j (1/(j+1)!)Z = Q`.

**5. Universality.** By `bhm-corollary-hosts-over-v-are-cover-centralizer-groups` (b)–(c) with `a = 1`, every
element of such a `G` satisfies the defining conditions of `V♯`.

## Scope

- **What this is.** A calibration and a ceiling: it identifies exactly what the cheap normal clause can reach on two
  dyadic orbits.
- **Bounded word problem.** By `bhm-corollary-hosts-over-v-are-cover-centralizer-groups` (d), `V♯` has word problem in
  time `2^(O(l))`. So `V♯` is not a universal Boone–Higman host, and neither is any group obtained this way.
- **Not claimed.** Type `F∞`, which would need `Q_0` to be `F∞`.
- **Open.** Which groups beyond `C(C,Z) ⋊ V` embed in `V♯`, for example every countable abelian group as for `VA`.
