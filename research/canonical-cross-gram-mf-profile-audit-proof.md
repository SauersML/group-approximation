Active-core reblocking supplies operator-norm asymptotic representations and
a uniform lower bound for the marked spectral mass.  It asserts no vanishing
trace moment for any other nonidentity word.  Passing from operator norm to
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm controls multiplicative defects only:

```text
||X||_2 <= ||X||_op.
```

It does not compare `tr(U(g))` with the canonical value.  Exact
representations of a fixed finite packet already realize arbitrary
irreducible multiplicity vectors, whereas the canonical trace is the one
specific regular vector.  Reblocking and amplification retain those
normalized multiplicities.  Hence the canonical hypothesis of the native
cross-Gram claim is unavailable in the arbitrary norm-corona profile used by
the shared-gauge MF route, proving the stated quantifier mismatch.
