Notation of the claim. Throughout, `p >= 5` and `d0 = 3np < infinity`.
We use these standing facts:
- (Z1) `D_(d0)/D_(d0+1)` is central in `G/D_(d0+1)`.
- (Z2) `K` lies in `D_(d0)`.
- `gr_d G = (L_p)_d` for `d < d0`.
- By (FD), `Kd0` is the image of `W_n` in `gr_(d0) G`, and `W_n` is
  multihomogeneous of degree `(np,np,np)`.

`U` is the universal restricted Lie algebra generated by `a, b, c` in
degree 1, subject to all relations of `L_p` of degree `< d0`. So
`U_d = (L_p)_d` for `d < d0`, and `U -> gr G` is onto.

## 0. The symmetry group

- **Power maps.** In each Heisenberg vertex group, `a^al` and `b^be`
  again satisfy `x^p = 1` and `[x,y]` central. So
  `sigma = sigma_(al,be,ga)` preserves the nine relators, and it is an
  automorphism of `Gamma^A2_p`.
  - Put `mu = al be ga` and `d = diag(al be, be, 1)`, and let `tau` be
    `Ad(d)` composed with `t -> mu t`. Then `tau` maps `x12(1)`, `x23(1)`
    and `x31(t)` to `x12(al)`, `x23(be)` and `x31(ga t)`
    (`symmetry_check.py`, part 1).
  - So `phi o sigma = tau o phi`. Here `tau` is a continuous automorphism
    of `I_1`.
- **Rotation.** `rho : a -> c, b -> a, c -> b` permutes the three vertex
  pairs. Note that `[c,a]` is central in `<c,a>` iff `[a,c]` is. It is
  compatible with `Ad(pi)`, which normalizes `I_1` (part 2).
- **Flip.** `theta : a -> b, b -> a, c -> c^-1` is compatible with
  `X -> J X^-T J^-1` (part 3).

The automorphisms of `G` preserve the filtration `D_d` and `K`, and act on
`gr G`. On the Lie side they act by graded automorphisms of `L_p`, since
these are Lie automorphisms that send degree 1 to degree 1. So they
preserve the relations of degree `< d0`, and they act on `U`, compatibly
with `U -> gr G -> L_p`. So `W_n`, `V_n` and `Kd0` are `H`-modules.

On `U`, `sigma` multiplies a multihomogeneous element of multidegree
`(i,j,k)` by `al^i be^j ga^k`. This holds because it does so on
generators, and because `(al^i)^p = al^i`, which covers p-th powers.
Hence `sigma` acts on `W_n` as the scalar `(al be ga)^(np) = (al be ga)^n`,
and so also on its quotient `Kd0`.

## 1. Proof of (S)

Let `v` be in `G`, and let `c` be in `I_1` with `[c, phi(v)] = 1`. Let
`ct` be a lift of `c`.
1. **Well defined.** `phi^([ct, v]) = 1`, so `[ct, v]` is in `K`, which is
   in `D_(d0)`. If `ct` is replaced by `ct k` with `k` in `K`, then
   `[ct k, v] = [ct, v]^k [k, v]`. Here `[k, v]` is in `[D_(d0), G]`, which
   is in `D_(d0+1)`, and conjugation acts trivially on `D_(d0)/D_(d0+1)`
   by (Z1). So `delta_v(c)` in `Kd0` is well defined.
2. **Linearity in v.** `[x, v^2] = [x, v][x, v]^v`, and
   `[x,v]^v = [x,v] [[x,v],v]`, with `[[x,v],v]` in `D_(d0+1)`. By
   induction, `delta_(v^k)(c) = k delta_v(c)`.
3. **Equivariance.** For `sigma` in `H` with partner `tau` on `I_1`,
   `sigma([ct, v]) = [sigma(ct), sigma(v)]`, and `sigma(ct)` lifts
   `tau(c)`. So `sigma(delta_v(c)) = delta_(sigma v)(tau c)`.
4. Now let `c` be diagonal and `v = v0^k`, with `v0` one of `a, b, c,
   [a,b], [b,c], [c,a]`. In `Gamma`,
   `sigma(v0) = v0^chi` exactly, where `chi` is:
   - `al`, `be` or `ga` for `a`, `b` or `c`;
   - `al be`, `be ga` or `ga al` for the centres. For the centres this
     holds because `[x^i, y^j] = [x,y]^(ij)` in a Heisenberg group.
5. Choose `(al, be, ga)` with `al be ga = 1` and `chi != 1`. For `a` take
   `(2, 1, 1/2)`. For `[a,b]` take `(2, 1, 1/2)`, where `chi = 2`. The
   other cases follow by symmetry. This is possible since `p >= 5`; part 6
   of the script checks it.
   - Then `mu = 1`, and `tau = Ad(diag(al be, be, 1))` fixes the diagonal
     `c`.
   - By Section 0, `sigma` acts on `Kd0` as `(al be ga)^n = 1`.
   - Step 3 gives `delta_v(c) = delta_(v^chi)(c) = chi delta_v(c)`, so
     `(chi - 1) delta_v(c) = 0` and `delta_v(c) = 0`.

The torus `diag(u,u,u^-2)`, `u` in `1+tO`, is diagonal and centralizes
`x12(1)`. So `delta_a = 0` on it, at every level.

## 2. Proof of (T)

1. **g^p is lift-independent.** Every lift `g` of `h(1+t^n)` lies in
   `D_(3n)`. This holds because `D_(3n)(G)` maps onto `D_(3n)(I_1)`, and two
   lifts differ by `K`, which is in `D_(d0)` and so in `D_(3n)`.
   - Let `k` be in `K`. By the Hall–Petrescu formula,
     `(gk)^p = g^p k^p c_2^(C(p,2)) ... c_p`, where `c_i` lies in
     `gamma_i(<g,k>)`.
   - For `i >= 2`, each `c_i` is a product of commutators involving both
     `k` and `g`, so it lies in `[D_(d0), D_(3n)]`, which is in
     `D_(d0+3n)`.
   - Also `k^p` is in `D_(p d0)`.
   - So `(gk)^p = g^p mod D_(d0+1)`.

   In particular, the root-element lifts in the Bruhat word of
   `h(1+t^n)`, and any correction of them by centralizer elements, change
   `g^p` only inside `D_(d0+1)`.
2. **The Bruhat word of h(1+t^N), N = np.** The identity
   `h(1+s) = x21(-s/(1+s)) x12(1) x21(s) x12(-1/(1+s))` holds (part 5).
   Take `s = t^N`.
   - `x12(-1/(1+t^N)) = x12(-1) z4`, with `z4 = x12(t^N - t^(2N) + ...)`
     in `D_(3N+1)(I_1)`.
   - `x21(-t^N/(1+t^N)) = y_N^-1 z1`, with `z1` in `D_(6N-1)(I_1)`.

   Lift `z1` and `z4` inside `D_(3N+1)(G)`, and lift `x12(-1)` by
   `a^-1`. Since `D_(3N+1)(G)` is normal, the lifted word equals
   `yt^-1 a yt a^-1 = [yt, a^-1]` modulo `D_(d0+1)`, because `3N = d0`.
   Here `yt` is a lift of `y_N`. It lies in `D_(3N-1)`, and its class in
   `gr_(3N-1) G = (L_p)_(3N-1)` is `t^N E21`, independent of `yt`.
   Replacing `yt` by `yt k` with `k` in `K` changes `[yt, a^-1]` only by
   elements of `[D_(d0), G]` and `[D_(3N-1), D_(d0)]`, all in `D_(d0+1)`.
3. **Class.** Let `R_n = g^p W^-1`, where `W` is the lifted Bruhat word
   of `h(1+t^(np))`. It maps to `h(1+t^n)^p h(1+t^(np))^-1 = 1`, so it is
   in `K`, and its class lies in `Kd0`.
   - By Step 1, the class of `g^p` is `(t^n h_A)~^[p]`, the p-map in
     `gr G` of the class of `g`. That class is the unique lift of
     `t^n h_A` in degree `3n < d0`.
   - By Step 2, the class of `W` is the image of the bracket of
     `a` and `(t^N E21)~`. This is a lift of `t^N h_A` built from
     canonical lower-degree classes.
   - So `class(R_n)` is the image of
     `C_nA = (t^n h_A)~^[p] - (t^(np) h_A)~` for this choice of
     `(t^(np) h_A)~`.
   - No centralizer pairing and no choice of root lift appears. In
     particular, `C_nA` is in `V_n` iff `R_n` is in `D_(3np+1)(G)`.
4. **Why this is an obstruction.** Any identity in `I_1` among root and
   torus elements, lifted to `G`, gives an element of `K`. Its class in
   `Kd0` is determined, by Steps 1 and 2 applied to each factor, by the
   degree-`d0` Lie and restricted structure alone, that is, by an element
   of `W_n`. Whether that element lies in `V_n` is a statement about the
   relators of `Gamma` at class `3np`. It is not decided by `I_1`, and it
   is not decided by the pairings, which are zero by (S) or drop out by
   Step 1.

## 3. Proof of (H)

Let `U^Lie` be the Lie subalgebra of `U` generated by `U_1`. It is
`S_3`-stable. Put `L_n = W_n cap U^Lie_(3np)`, the kernel of
`U^Lie_(3np) -> (L_p)_(3np)`.

1. **dim L_n <= 1.** Let `F` be the free Lie algebra on `a, b, c`, let
   `R = ker(F -> L_p)`, and let `R'` be the ideal generated by
   `R_(<d0)`.
   - `F -> U^Lie` is onto and kills `R_(<d0)`, because `U_d = (L_p)_d` for
     `d < d0`.
   - So `L_n` is a quotient of `R_(d0)/R'_(d0)`.
   - We have `R' cap F_(d0) = sum_i [F_i, R_(d0-i)] = [F, R]_(d0)`, and
     `R` lies in `[F,F]`, since `(L_p)_1` is 3-dimensional. So
     `R_(d0)/R'_(d0) = (R/[F,R])_(d0) = H_2(L_p)_(d0)`, and this has
     dimension 1 by Open 1.
   - `K_n` spans `L_n`: by definition it is the Lie element representing
     that class.
2. **The restricted coordinate.** Define
   `psi : h -> U_(3np)/U^Lie_(3np)` by `psi(h) = (t^n h)~^[p] mod U^Lie`.
   - **Linear.** For `h, h'` in the Cartan, the lifts `(t^n h)~` and
     `(t^n h')~` commute in `U`, since their bracket lies in degree
     `6n < d0 = 3np`, where `U = L_p`, and there it is 0. So Jacobson's
     formula gives `(x + y)^[p] = x^[p] + y^[p]`. Also
     `(lam x)^[p] = lam^p x^[p] = lam x^[p]` for `lam` in `F_p`.
   - **Equivariant.** For `s` in `S_3`, `s((t^n h)~) = (t^n s(h))~`, since
     `U_(3n) = (L_p)_(3n)`. The `I_1` automorphisms `Ad(pi)` and
     `-J X^T J^-1` send `t^n h` to `t^n s(h)`: diagonal entries pick up no
     power of `t`. And `s` commutes with `[p]`.
   - **Image.** For every Lie lift `(t^(np) h)~` in `U^Lie`, the element
     `C_n(h) = (t^n h)~^[p] - (t^(np) h)~` lies in `W_n`, and it maps to
     `psi(h)`. Since `W_n = span(K_n, C_nA, C_nB)` and `K_n` is in
     `U^Lie`, the injection `W_n/L_n -> U/U^Lie` has image `psi(h)`.
3. **h is irreducible.** `S_3` acts on `h = {(d1,d2,d3) : sum = 0}` as
   follows (part 4):
   - the rotation acts by the 3-cycle `(d1,d2,d3) -> (d2,d3,d1)`;
   - the flip acts by `(d1,d2,d3) -> (-d3,-d2,-d1)`.

   An invariant line would be an eigenline of the 3-cycle.
   - The only fixed vectors of the 3-cycle are `(d,d,d)`, and these meet
     `h` in 0 since `p != 3`.
   - If `p = 1 mod 3`, the two eigenlines, for `omega` and `omega^2`, are
     swapped by the flip, since the flip inverts the 3-cycle.
   - If `p = 2 mod 3`, the eigenvalues are not in `F_p`.

   So `h` is irreducible. It is the reflection representation `Std`,
   possibly twisted by the sign, and `Std (x) sgn = Std` for `S_3`.
4. **Decomposition.** By Step 2, `W_n/L_n` is a quotient of the
   irreducible module `h`. So it is 0 or isomorphic to `h`. By (FD) it is
   2-dimensional, so it is isomorphic to `h`. Since `p` does not divide 6,
   Maschke's theorem gives `W_n = L_n (+) S_n` with `S_n = h = Std`.
   `L_n` is 1-dimensional, so it carries a character `eps`.
5. **Submodules.** The simple summands `L_n` and `S_n` are not isomorphic,
   since their dimensions differ. So `W_n` is multiplicity-free, and every
   submodule is the sum of the summands it contains. `V_n` is a
   submodule, so it is one of `0`, `L_n`, `S_n`, `W_n`.
   - If `v = l + s` is in `V_n` with `l != 0` and `s != 0`, the central
     idempotents of `F_p[S_3]` for the two isotypic types give `l` and `s`
     in `V_n`. So `V_n` contains `L_n` and `S_n`.
   - `C_nA` maps to `psi(h_A) != 0` in `W_n/L_n`. So if `C_nA` is in
     `V_n + L_n`, then `V_n + L_n` is not `L_n`, hence it is `W_n`, and
     `C_nB` is also in `V_n + L_n`. The converse holds by the same
     argument.

## 4. Scope

- (S) and (T) use only (Z1), (Z2), `d0 < infinity` and the multidegree of
  `W_n`.
- (H) uses Open 1 through `dim L_n <= 1`. It uses (FD) only for
  `dim W_n/L_n = 2`. Without (FD), `W_n/L_n` is still 0 or `Std`, and the
  lattice statement still holds.
- None of this decides `V_n`. The information has to come from the
  relators of `Gamma` at class `3np`, as Section 2, Step 4 says.
