# Strict compression, one mixed return, and the exact low-energy boundary

All Hilbert--Schmidt norms are normalized in the displayed matrix
dimension.

## 1. The mixed return is a genuinely different site

Assume
\[
        tHt^{-1}<H.
\]
Choose \(\gamma\in H\setminus tHt^{-1}\) and put
\[
        r=t^{-1}\gamma t.
\]
If \(r\in H\), then
\(\gamma=trt^{-1}\in tHt^{-1}\), a contradiction.  Hence
\[
        rH\ne H.                                          \tag{1}
\]
The shared-Pauli presentation therefore contains the off-site row
\[
        [A_H,B_{rH}]=1,\qquad
        B_{rH}=rB_Hr^{-1}.                                \tag{2}
\]
Only this one off-site row will be used.

If \(H\) is infranormal but not normal, such a strict compressor exists.
Indeed, if every element of
\[
        P_H=\{g\in G:gHg^{-1}\leq H\}
\]
normalized \(H\), then the group generated by \(P_H\) would normalize
\(H\), contrary to nonnormality.

## 2. Finite dimension turns the compressor into a normalizer

Let \(\rho:G\to U(D)\) be exact and set
\[
        N=\rho(H)''.
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}\rho(t)(N)
   =\rho(tHt^{-1})''
   \subseteq \rho(H)''=N.                                \tag{3}
\]
Conjugation by a unitary is a linear isomorphism of \(M_D\).  Thus the
left side of (3) has the same finite vector-space dimension as \(N\).
An inclusion of finite-dimensional vector spaces of equal dimension is
equality:
\[
        \operatorname{Ad}\rho(t)(N)=N.                    \tag{4}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \rho(r)=\rho(t)^*\rho(\gamma)\rho(t)\in N.               \tag{5}
\]
Equivalently, every element of the commutant \(N'\) commutes with
\(\rho(r)\).

Notice the direction.  The abstract element \(r\) is outside \(H\) by
(1), but every genuine finite-dimensional actor representation puts
\(\rho(r)\) inside the algebra generated by \(\rho(H)\).  The Pauli row
(2) distinguishes the two abstract sites, while (5) collapses them as far
as an \(H\)-central matrix is concerned.

## 3. The quantitative exact-actor estimate

Let \(Q\) be a Kazhdan set for \(H\), with constant \(\kappa\) in the
max convention
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(\xi,\mathcal K^H)
 \leq\kappa^{-1}\max_{q\in Q}\|\pi(q)\xi-\xi\|.           \tag{6}
\]
Apply (6) to the conjugation representation of \(H\) on
\(L^2(M_D)\).  Its fixed space is \(N'\).  If \(E_{N'}\) is the
orthogonal projection onto that space, then
\[
 \|B-E_{N'}B\|_2\leq {d_H\over\kappa},\qquad
 d_H=\max_{q\in Q}\|[B,\rho(q)]\|_2.                     \tag{7}
\]
By (5), \(E_{N'}B\) commutes with \(\rho(r)\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\rho(r)B\rho(r)^*-B\|_2
 &\leq
 \|\rho(r)(B-E_{N'}B)\rho(r)^*\|_2
       +\|B-E_{N'}B\|_2\\
 &\leq {2d_H\over\kappa}.                                \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
No polar decomposition is needed.  This is why only the \(B\)-stabilizer
rows occur and why the constant improves over the finite-orbit-rank
compactness estimate.

For every unitary \(A\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|AB-BA\|_2
 &\leq
 \|[A,\rho(r)B\rho(r)^*]\|_2
 +2\|\rho(r)B\rho(r)^*-B\|_2\\
 &\leq d_X+{4d_H\over\kappa}.                            \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
For unitaries \(A,B\),
\[
 \|ABA^*B^*-I\|_2=\|AB-BA\|_2.                           \tag{10}
\]
If
\(d_0=\|ABA^*B^*-J\|_2\), the triangle inequality and
(9)--(10) give
\[
 \|J-I\|_2\leq d_0+d_X+{4d_H\over\kappa}.                \tag{11}
\]
This proves (MR8).  At zero defect it uses neither property \((T)\) nor
a generating set: exact \(H\)-centrality puts \(B\) in \(N'\), and (5)
immediately fixes it under \(\rho(r)\).

## 4. Transfer from a nearby exact actor

Let \(u_q,u_r\) be arbitrary actor matrices and assume
\[
 \eta=\max\bigl(\{\|u_q-\rho(q)\|_2:q\in Q\}
                  \cup\{\|u_r-\rho(r)\|_2\}\bigr).
\]
Commutators and unitary conjugation obey
\[
 \|[B,\rho(q)]\|_2\leq\|[B,u_q]\|_2+2\eta,               \tag{12}
\]
\[
 \|\rho(r)B\rho(r)^*-u_rBu_r^*\|_2\leq2\eta,             \tag{13}
\]
and commuting the difference in (13) past \(A\) costs another factor
two.  Therefore the exact-actor defects are bounded by
\[
        d_H^\rho\leq d_H+2\eta,\qquad
        d_X^\rho\leq d_X+4\eta.                          \tag{14}
\]
Substitution in (11) yields
\[
 \|J-I\|_2
 \leq d_0+d_X+{4\over\kappa}d_H+
       \left(4+{8\over\kappa}\right)\eta.                \tag{15}
\]

## 5. Arbitrary finite-corner dilations

Let \(u_s\in U(d)\), \(\rho(s)\in U(D)\), \(D\geq d\), and suppose an
isometry \(V:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^D\) has compression error at most
\(\alpha\) for \(s\in Q\cup\{r\}\).  Identify \(V\mathbb C^d\) with the
first block.  The finite-corner completion lemma supplies
\(z_s\in U(D-d)\) such that
\[
 \|\rho(s)-(u_s\oplus z_s)\|_{2,D}
 \leq\lambda\sqrt{\alpha^2+6\alpha},\qquad
 \lambda=\sqrt{d/D}.                                     \tag{16}
\]
Pad the Pauli matrices by identities:
\[
 A^+=A\oplus I,\quad B^+=B\oplus I,\quad J^+=J\oplus I.  \tag{17}
\]
Every original Pauli defect scales by \(\lambda\), and
\[
 \|J^+-I_D\|_{2,D}=\lambda\|J-I_d\|_{2,d}.               \tag{18}
\]
Apply (15) in dimension \(D\), use (16), and divide by \(\lambda\).
This gives
\[
 \|J-I_d\|_{2,d}
 \leq d_0+d_X+{4\over\kappa}d_H+
 \left(4+{8\over\kappa}\right)\sqrt{\alpha^2+6\alpha}.   \tag{19}
\]
The dilation ratio cancels exactly.  Only the fixed packet
\(Q\cup\{r\}\) is compressed.

## 6. The direct approximate condition

For an arbitrary actor tuple \(u\), the one-row Pauli argument itself is
the identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|J-I\|_2
 &\leq d_0+\|AB-BA\|_2\\
 &\leq d_0+d_X+2\|u_rBu_r^*-B\|_2.                       \tag{20}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the precise missing statement is not full actor stability.  It is the
uniform implication
\[
 \operatorname{Def}_F(u)\to0,\quad
 \max_{q\in Q}\|[B,u_q]\|_2\to0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|u_rBu_r^*-B\|_2\to0                                  \tag{21}
\]
on the Pauli-compatible microstate class.  In a tracial matrix
ultraproduct this is
\[
        \sigma(H)'\cap\mathcal M\subseteq \sigma(r)'.     \tag{22}
\]
For all \(\gamma\in H\), (22) is equivalent to
\[
        \sigma(H)'=\sigma(tHt^{-1})',                    \tag{23}
\]
or, equivalently, normalization of \(\sigma(H)'\) by
\(\operatorname{Ad}\sigma(t)\).  This is the compressor
centralizer-normalization problem, restricted in (21) to the one mixed
return actually consumed by the Pauli wall.

Finite-corner exactification implies (21) through (15) or (19).  It is
sufficient, but stronger than the direct condition.

## 7. A spectral low-energy sufficient condition

There is a second, genuinely weaker-looking route which makes clear what
a robust spectral theorem would have to prove.  Enlarge \(Q\), if
necessary, to a finite symmetric generating Kazhdan set.  Put \(m=|Q|\)
and let \(L=|\gamma|_Q\).  For an actor tuple \(u\), define on
\(L^2(M_d)\)
\[
 \Delta_u={1\over m}\sum_{q\in Q}
   (I-\operatorname{Ad}u_q)^*(I-\operatorname{Ad}u_q),   \tag{24}
\]
\[
 P_\eta=1_{[0,\eta]}(\Delta_u),\qquad
 \nu_\eta(u,t)=
 \|(I-P_\eta)\operatorname{Ad}u_tP_\eta\|_{2\to2}.       \tag{25}
\]
Here \(u_\gamma\) and \(u_r=u_t^*u_\gamma u_t\) are word evaluations.
If
\[
        d_H=\max_{q\in Q}\|[B,u_q]\|_2,
\]
the spectral theorem gives
\[
        \|(I-P_\eta)B\|_2\leq {d_H\over\sqrt\eta}.        \tag{26}
\]
Equation (25) then implies
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_2(
   \operatorname{Ad}u_t(B),\operatorname{ran}P_\eta)
 \leq\nu_\eta(u,t)+{d_H\over\sqrt\eta}.                  \tag{27}
\]
Every vector \(\xi\in\operatorname{ran}P_\eta\) satisfies
\[
 \max_{q\in Q}\|(\operatorname{Ad}u_q-I)\xi\|_2
 \leq\sqrt{m\eta}\,\|\xi\|_2.                            \tag{28}
\]
Telescoping the fixed word for \(\gamma\), and paying twice for the
component in (27), gives
\[
 \|u_rBu_r^*-B\|_2
 \leq L\sqrt{m\eta}
       +2\nu_\eta(u,t)+{2d_H\over\sqrt\eta}.              \tag{29}
\]
Combining (20) and (29),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|J-I\|_2
 \leq d_0+d_X+2L\sqrt{m\eta}
       +4\nu_\eta(u,t)+{4d_H\over\sqrt\eta}.
 \quad}                                                  \tag{30}
\]

Accordingly, a spectral route must produce cutoffs
\(\eta_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 {d_{H,n}\over\sqrt{\eta_n}}\to0,\qquad
 \nu_{\eta_n}(u_n,t)\to0.                                \tag{31}
\]
The second requirement is **operator-norm normalization of the whole low
band by the compressor**.  A normalized trace estimate saying that only
\(o(d_n^2)\) adjoint directions leak is not enough: the named Pauli
unitary is one vector and can occupy an exceptional subspace of adjoint
density \(1/d_n^2\).  Likewise, a robust gap which only separates a low
band from the rest does not prove (31), because it does not say that
\(\operatorname{Ad}u_t\) preserves that band.

For a genuine finite-dimensional actor, the zero band is \(N'\), and
(4) says \(\operatorname{Ad}\rho(t)\) normalizes it exactly.  Thus (31)
is precisely the spectral deformation of the equal-dimension step, not an
automatic consequence of property \((T)\).

## 8. Sharp generic boundaries

There are two exact countermodels to dropping the normalization input.

First, in the negative central corner of the shared-Pauli group von Neumann
algebra, the base is
\[
        R=\bar\bigotimes_{x\in G/H}M_2,
\]
the actor permutes tensor legs, and every Pauli and actor row holds exactly
with \(J=-1\).  The root unitary \(B_H\) is \(H\)-fixed, but
\[
        \operatorname{Ad}u_t(B_H)=B_{tH}\ne B_H
\]
for a strict compressor.  Hence the zero-energy space need not be
compressor-normalized in a finite tracial algebra.  Finite-dimensional rank,
not tracial positivity, is what made (3) an equality.

Second, this boundary already occurs in matrix microstates for an amenable
strict-compressor pair.  Take
\[
 G=BS(1,2)=\langle a,t\mid tat^{-1}=a^2\rangle,\qquad
 H=\langle a\rangle .
\]
Then \(tHt^{-1}=\langle a^2\rangle<H\), and
\(r=t^{-1}at\notin H\).  The shared-center extraspecial base is locally
finite, \(G\) is amenable, and their semidirect product is amenable.  It
therefore has canonical trace-faithful matrix microstates.  In the group
trace,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &aB_Ha^{-1}=B_H,\\
 &rB_Hr^{-1}=B_{rH},\qquad
   \|B_{rH}-B_H\|_2=\sqrt2,\\
 &[A_H,B_{rH}]=1,\qquad
   [A_H,B_H]=J,\qquad \|J-I\|_2=\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{32}
\]
All fixed relations and the displayed trace identities pass to the
microstates.  Thus pointwise approximate multiplicativity, even together
with the full Pauli presentation, does not imply (21) in general.

The Baumslag--Solitar stabilizer is not Kazhdan.  This countermodel does not
settle (21) for the Kun--Thom Kazhdan pair.  It proves exactly why that case
needs either source-specific low-band normalization (31) or finite-corner
compression stability: strict compression and generic approximation alone
cannot supply the missing implication.
