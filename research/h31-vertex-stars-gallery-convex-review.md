## Pivot-path reduction: PASS

- No shared edge `f_i` contains `v`, because each lies in a chamber `D_1, …, D_(k−1)` outside `star(v)`.
- `f_i ≠ f_(i+1)`: otherwise `D_(i−1)` and `D_(i+1)` share an edge, which contradicts geodesicity. So
  the pivots `p_i = f_i ∩ f_(i+1)` are vertices, and consecutive distinct pivots span `f_(i+1)`.
- The pivots give a path of at most `k − 2` edges avoiding `v`. Its endpoints are neighbours of `v`, lying
  in `D_0` and `D_k`.
- Link edges are chambers, so the fan gallery around `v` has length `d + 1`. With `k − 2 ≥ d` this gives
  the contradiction.
- The case `p_1 = p_(k−1)` is immediate, since then `d_𝒞 ≤ 1 < k`.

## Lemma Σ (Gauss–Bonnet): PASS, checked in each case

**The formula.** Every face has angle sum `19π/20`. In units of `π`, with
`κ = 2 − θ/π` at interior vertices and `κ = 1 − θ/π` at boundary vertices,
`Σ_∂ κ + Σ_int κ = 2 + F/20`. A reduced, minimal-area disc has non-backtracking link walks at interior
vertices, so `θ ≥ girth × angle = 2π` and `κ_int ≤ 0`.

**Fact 2 (three corners on the `P`-side).**
- One corner gives a shortcut.
- Two corners with middle vertex `s ≠ v` allow `z_i` to be replaced by `s`, which lowers the area by 2.
- This works even when `s` is a fan vertex on `Q`: `s` is still interior to `Δ` and different from `v`,
  and minimality is taken over all `P` and all diagrams.
- Middle vertex `v` makes `z_i` a cut point. Cut points are piece endpoints, not interior `P`-vertices.

**Bounds.**
- A `Q`-interior vertex loses its two fan corners, so `κ ≤ 2α/π − 1`: `−3/5` (A), `−1/2` (B),
  `0` (C). It keeps at least `g − 2` faces: 8, 6 and 2 respectively.
- A `P`-interior vertex has `κ ≤ 1 − 3α/π`: `2/5`, `1/4`, `−1/2`. The maximizing type pattern alternates
  A and B, which gives `S(r)`.
- Endpoints have `κ ≤ 1 − α/π`.

**The three cases**, each assuming `m′ ≤ M − 1`, so at most `M − 2` interior `P`-vertices:
- **`v` of type A.** Endpoints are of type B or C, so `κ ≤ 3/4`. Faces at distinct B-vertices are
  distinct, so `F ≥ 6β` with `β ≥ ⌊(M−1)/2⌋`. This gives `S(M−2) ≥ 0.5 + 0.8β`, which fails for
  `M − 2 = 2t` (`0.65t` against `0.5 + 0.8t`) and for `2t + 1` (`0.65t + 0.4` against `1.3 + 0.8t`).
- **`v` of type B.** `F ≥ 8a`, and endpoints have `κ ≤ 4/5`. This gives `S(M−2) ≥ 0.4 + a`, which fails
  in both parities.
- **`v` of type C.** Double counting gives `2F ≥ 8a + 6β`. With `a + β = M − 1`, this gives
  `S(M−2) ≥ 0.4 + 0.8a + 0.65β ≥ 0.4 + 0.65(M − 1)`. That exceeds `S(M−2) ≤ 0.325(M−2) + 0.2` for all
  `M`.
- The small cases `M ≤ 2` are covered, including the direct parity argument for `M = 2`.

Summing over pieces and shared edges gives `m ≥ N ≥ d_(L_v)(p, q)`.

**Implicit facts to state.** A minimal-area diagram for a simple cycle is a nonsingular disc, and each
piece of `Δ′` is a disc. Both are standard.
