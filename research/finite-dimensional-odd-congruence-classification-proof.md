Let `U=rho(u)` and `H=rho(h)`. From `HUH^*=U^4`, unitary conjugacy preserves
the spectral multiset, so fourth powering permutes the finitely many
eigenvalues of `U`, including multiplicities. Every eigenvalue lies on a
finite cycle and satisfies `(FDU3)`. Taking the least common multiple of
the finitely many odd integers `4^k-1` gives an odd `N` with `U^N=I`.

The kernel is normal and contains `u^N`, hence contains `<<u^N>>`. The
element `u^N` is noncentral. By
`sl2-s-arithmetic-noncentral-normal-subgroups-have-finite-index`, its normal
closure has finite index in `Gamma`. This proves that the image of `rho` is
finite without any irreducibility assumption on `rho`.

Serre's congruence subgroup property for `SL_2` over these S-integers says
that a finite-index subgroup contains a principal congruence subgroup.
Ideals of `Z[1/2]` are generated by odd integers, so `rho` factors through
reduction modulo an odd `m`. Reduction is defined because `2` is invertible
modulo `m`. The converse pullback is immediate.

The Weyl and opposite-root relations are consistent with, but not needed
in addition to, the normal-closure step: the Weyl conjugate of `u^N` is the
corresponding lower-root power and already belongs to `<<u^N>>`.
