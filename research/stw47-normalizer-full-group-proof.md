Put `X=Spec(D)`.  The Weyl reconstruction of the C-star-diagonal pair is a
twist

```text
T x X ---> Sigma ---> R,
```

where `R` is a second-countable Hausdorff principal etale groupoid with unit
space `X`.  We identify `R` with its image under `(r,s):R->X x X`, so it is an
etale equivalence relation.  The proof has three independent steps.

## 1. The quotient of unitary normalizers is the topological full group

Every `u in N_U(A,D)` induces a homeomorphism `alpha_u` of `X`; its graph is
a compact-open full bisection of `R`.  The assignment

```text
u U(D) |--> alpha_u
```

is injective.  Indeed, if `alpha_u=id_X`, then `u` commutes with `D`, and the
masa property of a C-star diagonal gives `u in U(D)`.

It is also onto the topological full group `[R]`.  If `alpha in [R]`, its graph
`U_alpha` is a compact-open bisection homeomorphic to `X`.  The restriction of
the twist to `U_alpha` is a principal circle bundle over a compact
zero-dimensional space.  Refine a finite trivializing cover by a finite
clopen partition; the local sections paste on this partition.  A unit-modulus
twisted section supported on `U_alpha` is therefore a unitary normalizer and
induces `alpha`.  Consequently

```text
F(A,D) ~= [R].                                           (1)
```

This also proves countability: compact-open bisections form a countable set in
a second-countable ample groupoid.

## 1a. A local subhomogeneity reformulation

Fix `u_1,...,u_k in N_U(A,D)`, let `H` be the subgroup of `[R]` generated by
their images, and put `B=C*(D,u_1,...,u_k)`.  If `H` is finite, the Weyl
subrelation generated by these full bisections has orbits of size at most
`|H|`.  Its twisted regular representations therefore have dimension at most
`|H|`, and these representations exhaust the irreducible representations of
the principal finite-orbit relation.  Thus `B` is subhomogeneous.

Conversely, suppose `B` is `N`-subhomogeneous.  At every `x in X`, the twisted
regular representation of `B` on `ell^2(Hx)` is irreducible.  Indeed, an
operator in its commutant first commutes with `C(X)`, so it is diagonal, and
then commutation with the relation arrows makes its diagonal constant on the
orbit `Hx`.  Hence `|Hx|<=N` for every `x`.

The action gives homomorphisms `H->Sym(Hx)` with degrees at most `N`, and
their kernels have trivial intersection because `H` is already a group of
homeomorphisms.  There are only finitely many homomorphisms from the free
group on `k` named generators to the finitely many symmetric groups
`Sym(q)`, `q<=N`: each is determined by a `k`-tuple of permutations.
Consequently only finitely many kernels occur.  Their intersection has finite
index, so the faithful group `H` is finite.  We have proved

```text
H finite  <=>  C*(D,u_1,...,u_k) is subhomogeneous.      (1a)
```

Together with `(1)`, local finiteness of `F(A,D)` is therefore equivalent to
subhomogeneity of every finitely-normalizer-generated intermediate algebra.

## 2. An etale Cantor equivalence relation is AF iff its full group is locally finite

Suppose first that `R=union R_n`, where the `R_n` form an increasing sequence
of compact-open finite equivalence relations with unit space `X`.  The graphs
of finitely many elements of `[R]` form a compact subset of `R`, hence lie in
one `R_n`.  A compact etale finite relation admits a finite clopen
decomposition

```text
R_n ~= disjoint_union_j Y_j x {1,...,q_j} x {1,...,q_j}.
```

After refining the finitely many coefficient clopens of the chosen full-group
elements, the group they generate embeds in a finite product of finite
symmetric groups.  It is finite.  Hence `[R]` is locally finite.

Conversely, enumerate `[R]` and let `H_n` be the subgroup generated by its
first `n` elements.  Local finiteness makes every `H_n` finite.  Set

```text
R_n={(alpha(x),x):alpha in H_n, x in X}.
```

This is a compact-open finite equivalence relation, and the `R_n` increase.
They exhaust `R`.  To see the last assertion, take `(y,x) in R` with `x!=y`.
Choose a compact-open bisection `U` through `(y,x)` and shrink it so that
`s(U)` and `r(U)` are disjoint.  The bisection

```text
U union U^(-1) union (X \ (s(U) union r(U)))
```

is the graph of an involution in `[R]` and contains `(y,x)`.  Unit arrows are
already in every `R_n`.  Thus

```text
R is AF  <=>  [R] is locally finite.                    (2)
```

## 3. Twists over AF Cantor equivalence relations are trivial

For completeness, this step prevents an unnoticed twist obstruction in the
passage from `(2)` back to AF Cartans.  Let `R=union R_n` be as above and let
`Sigma->R` be any circle twist.  We recursively construct multiplicative
continuous sections `sigma_n:R_n->Sigma` with
`sigma_(n+1)|R_n=sigma_n`, starting with the canonical section on
`R_0=X`.

The extension step is finite and clopen.  The quotient of `X` by the proper
finite etale relation `R_(n+1)` is compact and zero-dimensional.  Refine that
quotient into finitely many clopen charts, and pull them back to saturated
clopen reductions of `X`, on each of which an `R_(n+1)`-class is a fixed
finite set of sheets and its partition into `R_n`-classes is constant.  The
saturation is essential: it ensures that every arrow is handled inside one
chart.  In each reduction choose an anchor sheet `i_b` in every old class
`b`, choose one base old class `0`, set `lambda_0` equal to the canonical
unit lift, and choose a continuous twisted lift `lambda_b` of the arrow from
`i_0` to `i_b` for `b!=0`.  Such lifts exist after one further finite clopen
refinement of the quotient chart.  Retain
`sigma_n` on all arrows inside old classes and define the lift of the arrow
from a point `k` in old class `c` to a point `i` in old class `b` by

```text
sigma_n(i,i_b) lambda_b lambda_c^* sigma_n(i_c,k).       (3)
```

For `b=c`, formula `(3)` is exactly `sigma_n(i,k)`.  For composable arrows,
the two middle old-class factors cancel, so `(3)` is multiplicative.  All
choices are continuous because the charts are clopen.  The formulas agree
with the prescribed section on `R_n`, and hence give the required extension.

The compatible union `sigma=union sigma_n` is continuous for the inductive
limit topology of `R` and is a groupoid homomorphism splitting the twist.
Thus every twist over an AF Cantor equivalence relation is trivial.

It follows directly from finite clopen sheet decompositions that a trivial
twist over `union R_n` is the inductive limit of finite-dimensional diagonal
pairs: at stage `n`, refine the zero-dimensional bases `Y_j` by finite clopen
partitions, and use the matrix units indexed by the finite sheets.  These
partitions can be chosen recursively: include the finitely many coefficient
clopens used at the preceding stage before refining for the next dense finite
packet.  The resulting finite-dimensional diagonal pairs are nested and their
union contains a dense compact-open section algebra.  Conversely, the Weyl
relation of an inductive limit of finite-dimensional Cartan pairs is the
increasing union of its finite-stage equivalence relations.  Therefore

```text
D is an AF Cartan  <=>  R is AF.                         (4)
```

Combining `(1)`, `(1a)`, `(2)`, and `(4)` proves all formulations of the
claim.
