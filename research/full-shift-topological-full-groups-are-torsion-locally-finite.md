**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof by `bh-free-06`; not independently reviewed). No
priority claimed, but not found in the literature checked. Barbieri--Kari--Salo,
arXiv:1603.08715, Theorem 7 (read from the PDF), prove the single-element version:
the torsion problem of `RFA(Z,n,k)` is decidable, via a pumping to a spatially
periodic point. The group version below needs an extra Ramsey step. It is not
stated in the abstract of Salo, arXiv:2103.06663, which proves `[[Σ^Z]] ≤ 2V`.

## Statement

Let `Σ` be a finite alphabet, `σ` the shift of `Σ^Z`, and `[[σ]]` its topological
full group: the homeomorphisms `g` with `g(x) = σ^{c_g(x)}(x)` for a continuous
`c_g : Σ^Z -> Z`. Every finitely generated torsion subgroup `H ≤ [[σ]]` is finite.

The same holds, with the same proof and vertex set `Z × Q`, for every finitely
generated torsion subgroup of the group `RFA(Z,n,k)` of reversible finite-state
automata in dimension one (Barbieri--Kari--Salo). These are one-head machines
with `k` states that never write.

## Proof

Fix a finite symmetric generating set `S` of `H` and a radius `R` such that each
`c_s` depends only on `x_{[-R,R]}` and `|c_s| <= R`.
- **The graph `Γ_x`.** For `x ∈ Σ^Z`, let `Γ_x` be the graph on `Z` with an edge
  `j — j + c_s(σ^j x)` for each `j` and `s`. It is `σ`-local: edges at `j` depend
  only on `x_{[j-R, j+R]}`, and have length at most `R`.
- **Paths are words.** A path `v = j_0, ..., j_m` with step labels `s_1, ..., s_m`
  gives `w = s_m ⋯ s_1 ∈ H` with `w(σ^v x) = σ^{j_m} x`.
- **Orbits.** `j ↦ σ^j x` maps the component of `v` onto the orbit `H σ^v x`.

**Step 1: some `Γ_x` has an infinite component.** `H` acts faithfully. If all
`H`-orbits had size at most `D`, the kernels of the actions on orbits would be
subgroups of index at most `D!`. A finitely generated group has only finitely many
of these, and their intersection is trivial, so `H` would be finite. Hence, for
every `D` some `x_D` has a component of `Γ_{x_D}` with more than `D` vertices.
Recentre it to contain `0`. It then contains a path from `0` to some vertex of
absolute value `>= d` for every `d <= D/2`, and the part of the path up to that
point uses only `x_D` on `[-d-R, d+R]`. A limit point `x` of the `x_D` therefore
has an unbounded component `K ∋ 0`. By symmetry, `K` is unbounded to the right.

**Step 2: a homogeneous triple of cuts.** For a cut `c` put
`B_c = [c-R+1, c+R]`. Any edge joining `(-∞, c]` to `[c+1, ∞)` has both ends in
`B_c`.
- By pigeonhole, infinitely many cuts `c >= R` share the window
  `x_{[c-2R, c+2R]}`.
- For two such cuts `c < c'`, let `J(c,c') = [c-R+1, c'+R]`. Let `Π(c,c')` be the
  partition of `B_c ⊔ B_{c'}` induced by connectivity in `Γ_x` restricted to
  `J(c,c')`. Record it with vertices labelled by their offset from the cut, so all
  `Π(c,c')` live on one finite set `B ⊔ B`.
- By Ramsey's theorem for pairs there are `c_1 < c_2 < c_3` in this set with
  `Π(c_1,c_2) = Π(c_2,c_3) = Π(c_1,c_3) =: Π`.

Write `ρ ⊆ B × B` for the left-to-right connections of `Π`, and `≡_L`, `≡_R` for its
two one-sided equivalences.
- **`ρ` is nonempty.** Take a path in `K` from `0` to a vertex beyond `c_2 + R`.
  After its last visit to `B_{c_1}` it stays right of `B_{c_1}`. Until its next
  visit to `B_{c_2}` it stays left of `B_{c_2}`. That segment lies in `J(c_1,c_2)`.
- **Gluing.** `J(c_1,c_2) ∩ J(c_2,c_3) = B_{c_2}`, and no edge of length `<= R`
  jumps over `B_{c_2}`. Cutting a path at its visits to `B_{c_2}` gives
  `ρ = ρ(c_1,c_3) = ρ ∘ E ∘ ρ`, where `E` is the equivalence generated by `≡_R` and
  `≡_L`.
- **A cycle.** Hence `ρ = ρ ∘ (E∘ρ)^n` for every `n`. So every power of the
  relation `E∘ρ` on the finite set `B` is nonempty, and `E∘ρ` has a cycle:
  `(b, b) ∈ (E∘ρ)^q` for some `b ∈ B` and `q >= 1`.

**Step 3: a periodic tape with drift.** Let `p = c_2 - c_1`, and let `y` be the
`p`-periodic tape equal to `x` on `[c_1, c_2)`.
- The windows agree at `c_1` and `c_2`, so `y` has the same `R`-windows as `x` at
  every position of `J(c_1,c_2)`. Hence `Γ_y` is the union of the translates by
  `pZ` of `Γ_x|J(c_1,c_2)`, glued along the zones `Z_n = B_{c_1} + np`.
- Each block realizes `Π`, and `E` is realized inside each zone by the two
  adjacent blocks. So the cycle gives a path in `Γ_y` from `u = c_1 + b` to
  `u + qp` inside a bounded region `A`, and thus a word `w ∈ H` with
  `w(σ^u y) = σ^{u+qp} y`.

**Step 4: contradiction.** Let `N` be the order of `w`. Choose a tape `y'` that
agrees with `y` on the `R`-neighbourhood of `A + jqp` for `0 <= j < N`, and is `0`
far to the left and `1` far to the right, so `y'` is not periodic. Iterating the
path gives `w^N(σ^u y') = σ^{u + Nqp} y'`. Since `w^N = 1`, this forces
`σ^{Nqp} y' = y'`, a contradiction. `∎`

## Consequences

- **Inside `2V`.** By Salo (arXiv:2103.06663, abstract), `[[Σ^Z]]` embeds in `2V`.
  So the subgroup of `2V` consisting of head machines that do not write contributes
  no infinite finitely generated torsion group. Together with Röver's theorem for
  `V` (Burillo--Cleary--Röver, arXiv:1402.3860, Theorem 1.3), torsion is locally
  finite both for one stack (`V`) and for a moving head on a read-only
  two-sided tape (`[[Σ^Z]]`).
- **What survives.** Any infinite finitely generated torsion subgroup of `2V`
  (`brin-thompson-2v-is-torsion-locally-finite`) must genuinely write on both
  stacks. The natural home is the group of reversible Turing machines,
  `RTM(Z,2,1) ≤ 2V` (Barbieri--Kari--Salo, arXiv:2303.17270, as quoted from its
  HTML). That group is LEF (arXiv:1603.08715, Theorem 6), and its elementary
  subgroup has undecidable torsion problem (arXiv:2303.17270, Theorem 1.2).
- **Matte Bon.** This is consistent with Matte Bon (arXiv:1408.0762), who finds
  infinite finitely generated torsion subgroups in `[[σ_X]]` for minimal subshifts
  `X`. Step 3 needs the pumped periodic tape to lie in the subshift, which fails
  for minimal aperiodic `X`. The same proof works for any subshift of finite type
  that contains, near the pumped periodic orbit, a non-periodic point agreeing
  with it on arbitrarily long blocks. This extension is recorded but not claimed
  here.
