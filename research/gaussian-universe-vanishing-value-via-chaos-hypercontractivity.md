Notation is as in the target. `gamma` is the law of `G`. `s = G 1 = sum_a g_a`.
`T_P f = E[f(G) | G M_P]`, `T = E_P T_P`, and
`Stab_T(rho) = sum_a <rho_a, T rho_a>` in `L^2(gamma)`. Column permutations
act by `(sigma G)_a = g_(sigma^-1(a))`. For a left strategy `A`, put
`rho_(A,c)(G) = Pr_sigma[A(sigma G) = sigma(c)]`.

**Step 1 (the reduction transfers verbatim).** The proof of
`rotated-frame-value-reduces-to-partition-stability`, route
`equivariant-partition-stability-reduction-proof`, Steps 1 to 5, uses exactly
these facts about the frame model:

* (F1) the left law is invariant under row permutations, which fix `s`;
* (F2) `P` is uniform and independent of the left vertex, and independent of
  the right vertex (richness);
* (F3) a permutation `sigma` maps the fibre `{Pi_P X = Y}` onto
  `{Pi_(P o sigma^-1) X = Y}` and preserves the left law, so it carries
  `law(X | P, Y)` to `law(X | P o sigma^-1, Y)`;
* (F4) `T_P` is the conditional expectation onto the right vertex, hence a
  self-adjoint idempotent contraction.

In the Gaussian model:

* (F1) holds because `gamma` has iid columns.
* (F2) is the richness paragraph of the target.
* (F3) holds because `(sigma G) M_(P o sigma^-1) = G M_P`, and `(sigma G, P o sigma^-1)`
  has the same joint law as `(G, P)`. So the conditional laws are carried
  onto each other.
* (F4) is the definition.

No other property of frames appears in Steps 1 to 5. Their conclusion
therefore holds for `Gamma_(n,d)`:

* `rho_A` is an `S_2n`-equivariant fractional partition;
* it is s-balanced, `E[rho_(A,c) | s] = 1/(2n)` almost surely;
* `Pr[P(A(G)) = B(H)]^4 <= 4 Stab_T(rho_A)`.

It remains to show that every s-balanced fractional partition `rho`
satisfies `Stab_T(rho) <= 1/(2n) + e^2 D (ln 2n)^D/(2n) + lambda_D(n)`.

**Step 2 (T_P is a second quantisation).** Identify the Gaussian Hilbert space
of linear functionals `G -> <A, G>_F` with `R^d tensor R^(2n)`. The
sigma-algebra of `G M_P` is generated by the closed Gaussian subspace
`R^d tensor range(M_P) = range(I tensor Pi_P)`, where `Pi_P = (I + sigma_P)/2`
projects onto `span{e_a + e_a'}` and `sigma_P` is the partner involution.
Conditional expectation onto the sigma-algebra of a Gaussian subspace `K` is
the second quantisation `Gamma(proj_K)` (Janson, *Gaussian Hilbert Spaces*,
Cambridge 1997, Ch. 4: conditional expectations). Concretely, it acts on the
`k`-th Wiener chaos, identified with `Sym^k`, as `proj_K^(tensor k)`. The
identification is checked on Wick exponentials `:exp(xi):`, where both sides
give `:exp(proj_K xi):`. So `T_P = Gamma(I tensor Pi_P)`.

**Step 3 (split off the frame sum).** `R^(2n) = span(1) (+) 1^perp`, and
`Pi_P` fixes `1` and preserves `1^perp`. Put `G' = G (I - J/(2n))`. Then `s`
and `G'` are independent Gaussians, `G = s 1^T/(2n) + G'`, and
`L^2(gamma) = L^2(s) tensor L^2(G')`. Second quantisation turns orthogonal
sums into tensor products, so

`T_P = Id_(L^2(s)) tensor Gamma(I tensor Pi_P|_(1^perp))`.

So `T_P` acts on `G'` fibrewise, for each fixed `s`. Expand
`f(s, .) = sum_k f_k(s, .)` in the Wiener chaos of `G'`. Here `f_0(s) = E[f | s]`.
On the `k`-th chaos, `Sym^k(R^d tensor 1^perp)` sits inside
`(R^d)^(tensor k) tensor (1^perp)^(tensor k)`. There `T = E_P T_P` acts as
the restriction of `I tensor M_k`, with `M_k` as in the required claim. So,
with `lambda_0 = 1` and `f_k` meaning the whole function `(s, G') -> f_k(s, G')`,

`<f, T f> <= sum_k lambda_k ||f_k||^2`. (3)

This holds because `T` preserves each chaos, fibre by fibre. On chaos `k` it is
self-adjoint and PSD, with norm at most `||I tensor M_k|| = lambda_k`.

**Step 4 (low degrees: fibrewise hypercontractivity).** Let `rho` be
s-balanced and fix `c`. Put `f = rho_c`, so `0 <= f <= 1` and
`E[f | s] = alpha = 1/(2n)` for almost every `s`. Fix such an `s` and write
`phi = f(s, .)`, a function of the Gaussian vector `G'`. Let `phi_k` be its
`k`-th chaos component. Take `q = 1 + ln(2n)` and `p = q/(q-1)`.

Nelson's hypercontractivity for the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck semigroup,
`||P_t h||_q <= ||h||_2` when `e^(-2t) <= 1/(q-1)`, applied to `P_t phi_k = e^(-kt) phi_k`,
gives `||phi_k||_q <= (q-1)^(k/2) ||phi_k||_2`. Then:

* `||phi_k||_2^2 = <phi, phi_k> <= ||phi||_p ||phi_k||_q <= ||phi||_p (q-1)^(k/2) ||phi_k||_2`.
* `||phi||_p^p = E phi^p <= E phi = alpha`, since `0 <= phi <= 1`.

So `||phi_k||_2^2 <= alpha^(2/p) (q-1)^k = alpha^2 alpha^(-2/q) (ln 2n)^k`, and
`alpha^(-2/q) = exp(2 ln(2n)/(1 + ln 2n)) <= e^2`. Integrating over `s`,
`||f_k||^2 <= e^2 (ln 2n)^k / (2n)^2`.

The fibrewise step matters here. A global Hölder bound would need `E[f | s]`
to be small in `L^infinity`. Balance gives exactly that, fibre by fibre.

**Step 5 (assembly).** Sum (3) over the `2n` labels, splitting degrees into
three ranges:

* **Degree 0.** Balance gives `f_0 = 1/(2n)`, contributing
  `2n (2n)^-2 = 1/(2n)`.
* **Degrees `1 <= k < D`.** Use `lambda_k <= 1` and Step 4. Since
  `ln 2n >= ln 4 > 1`, these contribute at most
  `2n sum_(k<D) e^2 (ln 2n)^k/(2n)^2 <= e^2 D (ln 2n)^D/(2n)`.
* **Degrees `k >= D`.** Use `lambda_k <= lambda_D` (item 1 of the required
  claim). These contribute at most
  `lambda_D sum_c ||rho_c||^2 <= lambda_D sum_c E rho_c = lambda_D`.

Hence `Stab_T(rho) <= 1/(2n) + e^2 D (ln 2n)^D/(2n) + lambda_D(n)`. With
Step 1 this is the theorem.

**The limit.** Fix `D`. By item 3 of the required claim,
`lambda_D(n) -> 2^-ceil(D/2)` or below, and the other two terms tend to 0.
So `limsup_n sup_d val^4 <= 4 * 2^-ceil(D/2)` for every `D`, and the limit
is 0. Nothing depends on `d`.

**Sanity check.** The artifact's linear strategy
`A = argmax_a <g_a, w>`, `B = argmax_b <h_b, w>` has Monte Carlo value
`0.693, 0.479, 0.336, 0.244` at `n = 4, 16, 64, 256` (seed `20260917`,
20000 trials). This decays, and it agrees with the frame-model table in
`rotated-frame-value-reduces-to-partition-stability` to Monte Carlo accuracy.
That agreement is exact in law. Only the `2n` projections `<g_a, w>` matter.
In the frame model they form a uniform unit vector, which is a normalised
Gaussian vector, and both argmaxes are invariant under positive scaling.
