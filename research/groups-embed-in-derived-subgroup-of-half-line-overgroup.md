**ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** Let `Γ` be a group generated by a finite set `S`. On `X = Γ × Z` put
- `σ(x,m) = (x, m+1)`;
- `h_γ(x,m) = (γx, m)` for `m ≥ 0`, and `h_γ(x,m) = (x,m)` for `m < 0`;
- `δ_0(γ)(x,0) = (γx, 0)`, and `δ_0(γ)` is the identity on the other levels.

Let `C(Γ) = ⟨σ, h_s : s ∈ S⟩ ≤ Sym(X)`, the half-line overgroup. Then:
1. `δ_0 : Γ → C(Γ)` is an injective homomorphism with `δ_0(s^(-1)) = [σ, h_s]` for `s ∈ S`. So `δ_0(Γ) ≤ [C(Γ), C(Γ)]`,
   and the image of every generator is a single commutator.
2. `C(Γ) = E ⋊ ⟨σ⟩ ≅ E ⋊ Z`. Here `E ≤ Γ^Z`, acting levelwise by left translations, is the group of sequences that are
   trivial for `m ≪ 0` and constant for `m ≫ 0`. `E` is the directed union of subgroups `E_M ≅ Γ^(2M+1)`.
3. `C(Γ)` is infinite and generated by `|S| + 1` elements.
4. If `Γ` is amenable, elementary amenable, sofic, torsion-free, or solvable of derived length `d`, then `C(Γ)` is
   amenable, elementary amenable, sofic, torsion-free, or solvable of derived length at most `d + 1`, respectively.
5. If `Γ` is not abelian, `C(Γ)` is not residually finite.

**Use.** `perfect-groups-embed-in-crossed-product-elementary-groups` puts `[Δ,Δ]` inside `EL_3(LC(Y,k) ⋊ Δ)` for
every `Δ`-system `Y` and every field `k`. So `γ ↦ diag(u_(δ_0(γ)), 1, 1)` embeds `Γ` in `EL_3(LC(Y,k) ⋊ C(Γ))`. This is
the overgroup step for amenable and sofic inputs; see `amenable-groups-embed-in-kazhdan-linear-sofic-via-half-line`.

**Scope.**
- Residual finiteness is lost (item 5), so residually finite and LEF envelopes still go through the Ore overgroup of
  `rf-groups-lie-in-derived-subgroups-of-rf-groups`.
- Whether `C(Γ)` is LEF when `Γ` is LEF is not decided here. The naive cyclic truncation of the half-lines is not a
  partial homomorphism (artifact §4).
- `C(Γ)` is not perfect, since `σ` survives in the abelianization. A perfect overgroup is not needed for the use above.

**Credit.** The half-line commutator is the classical device behind embeddings into Cartesian wreath products (compare
B. H. Neumann and H. Neumann, *Embedding theorems for groups*, J. London Math. Soc. 34 (1959), 465–479; recalled, not
re-read). What is used here is only that the generated subgroup stays inside the eventually constant sequences, a
directed union of finite powers. No novelty is claimed. Bounded check: a grep of this graph on 2026-09-13 for
derived-subgroup overgroups, which found the Ore route, the swap double and the wreath firewall. No literature search.
