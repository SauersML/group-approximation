below by section as §n. Literature imports come from the LaTeX source of

**Step 1 (§1, Lemma E).**
- Let `C_c` be the class of finite groups whose composition factors have order
  at most `c`.
- A countable residually-`C_c` group acts faithfully on `T_c`:
  - intersect finitely many separating normal subgroups to get a descending
    chain;
  - refine it through composition series to a subgroup chain with indices in
    `[2, c]`;
  - embed the coset tree into `T_c`, extending each automorphism by the
    identity on the suffix after the longest in-tree prefix.
- So clause 2 of the claim follows from clause 1, with `c = d(p)`.

**Step 2 (§2).**
- `G_0 = T_0 x| B`, where `B = <L_1 u L_2 | G1--G4>`. Every relation G5--G8,
  and every stop relation, contains an `x`-letter.
- By G2, `B = B_0 x ... x B_K`.
- **Lemma 2.1.** Each block `B_i` is isomorphic to
  `F_p[s^±, (s-1)^-1] x| <s, s-1>`, with `a_i^-1 -> s` and
  `(a_i')^-1 -> s - 1`. Block `0` uses `K` variables.
- The proof of Lemma 2.1:
  - the normal closure of the `A`-letter is abelian of exponent `p` (KMS Lemma
    4.1 through G1 and G3/G4, as in KMS Lemma 4.4);
  - it is a cyclic module killed by `τ - σ + 1`;
  - `F_p[σ^±, τ^±]/(τ - σ + 1)` is that ring;
  - the `a`-letters generate a free abelian group, because the images `s_k`
    and `s_k - 1` are non-associate irreducibles and free abelian groups are
    Hopfian.

**Step 3 (§3): elements outside `T_0`.**
- If the image `b` of `g` in `B` lies outside `V = H_1^B`, reduce
  `H_2 ≅ Z^(4K)` modulo `p^m`.
- Otherwise some component `v_i != 0` lies in a ring `R_i`.
  - Evaluate every `s_k` at `ω`, where `ω = -1` for odd `p`, and `ω` is a
    primitive cube root of unity in `F_4` for `p = 2`. This gives a maximal
    ideal `m` with residue field `F_(q_p)`.
  - `v_i` does not lie in `m^j` once `j` exceeds the degree of its numerator,
    because a nonzero polynomial vanishes to order at most its degree.
  - The quotient `(R_i/m^j) x| (image of U_i)` is finite and solvable, and its
    primes lie in `{p} u primes(q_p - 1)`.

**Step 4 (§4): the filtration of the basis group.**
- By clauses 1 and 3 of [[kms-group-with-stop-relations-simulates-all-words]],
  there is a surjection `ψ: G_0 -> Ḡ` that restricts to an isomorphism
  `φ: T_0 -> T_1` with `φ(b t b^-1) = ψ(b)·φ(t)`.
- By clause 6 of [[kms-minsky-machine-groups-simulate-their-machines]] and
  KMS pp. 23--24 (formula `(ea)` and the displays after it), each `L_2` letter
  is `D + N`:
  - `D` is `α ⊗ 1` or `(α - 1) ⊗ 1` on every slice `u`, where
    `α = [[1,-1,1],[1,1,0],[1,0,0]]`;
  - `N = S E_11` moves slice `u` to slice `u a_j`;
  - `E_11 α^-1 E_11 = E_11 (α-1)^-1 E_11 = 0`, so the inverse has the same
    shape.
- The `A`-letters are `1 + N_A` with `N_A^2 = 0`.
- Let `ν(u)` be the largest total `a`-exponent of a representative of `u`.
  This is finite by (FC).
- `E_n = span{z_(i,u) : ν(u) >= n}` is invariant, of finite index, and has
  normal preimage `F_n`. The intersection of the `F_n` is trivial.

**Step 5 (§5): orders on `T_1/E_n`.**
- On `E_m/E_(m+1)` the `N`-parts vanish, so the `L_2` letters act through
  `α` or `α - 1`. Their eigenvalues are roots of `chi(x)` or of `chi(x+1)`.
- Hence each letter has order dividing `n_0(p) p^e` on `T_1/E_n`.
- The image of `B` has order with primes in `{p} u primes(n_0(p))`: it is an
  elementary abelian `p`-group extended by an abelian group generated by such
  letters.

**Step 6 (§6): elements inside `T_0`.**
- `Φ_n(t b) = (φ(t) + E_n, ρ_n(b))` is a homomorphism from `G_0` onto a finite
  solvable group with primes in `pi(p)`.
- It separates any given `t != 1` in `T_0` once `n` is large.
- Together with Step 3 this proves clause 1.

**Step 7 (§7): clause 3.**
- *Clocked machines.* Clause 1 of
  [[pumped-halting-clocked-minsky-group-is-residually-finite]] says that for a
  simple deterministic clocked universally halting `M`, every nonzero element
  of `S(M)` has finitely many representative words. Their images in `Š` are
  finitely many, so (FC) holds, and clauses 1 and 2 apply.
- *The counterexample.* Take the 2-glass machine of clause 3.
  - Its command domains are disjoint, so it is deterministic.
  - Every configuration with state `1` reaches `(0; 0, 0)`, and so does every
    configuration with state `2` and `e_2 > 0`. The remaining configurations
    `(2; x, 0)` with `x > 0` and `(0; x, y) != (0; 0, 0)` are not accepted, and
    their `Sym(M)`-components have at most two elements. So `M` is
    sym-universally halting in the sense of KMS v5 §2.
  - Its Minsky relations are `q_1 a_1 = q_1`, `q_1 A_1 = q_2 A_1`,
    `q_2 a_2 = q_1` and `q_2 A_2 = q_0 A_2`.
  - The words `q_1 a_1^x` and `q_2 a_2 a_1^x`, up to commutation, form a set
    closed under every elementary transformation of the presentation. None of
    them contains `q_0` or a forbidden two-letter subword. So `q_1 != 0`.
  - In `S(M)`, `q_1 = q_1 a_1^x` for all `x`. Only commutativity relations act
    on nonzero `q`-free words, and they preserve `a`-exponents. So the `a_1^x`
    are pairwise distinct divisors of `q_1`, and the `q_1 a_1^x` are pairwise
    distinct in `Š`.
- This refutes KMS v5 Lemma 3.5(a) as stated. Its proof applies Lemma 3.3
  (`l:l9`). That proof bounds both the forward and the backward part of a
  computation on a partial word by property (c) of Theorem 2.6. Property (c)
  is stated only for the machines `MM_2`, `MM_3`, and says that computations
  empty each glass. It gives nothing for a general sym-universally halting
  machine, and the backward chain `q_1 <- q_1 a_1 <- q_1 a_1^2 <- ...` above
  never tests a glass.

**Numerical check.** `experiments/kms-groups-bounded-factors-2026-09-16/`
builds the automorphisms on `T_1/E_n` for small `(K, n, p)` and for one Add
command. It checks G1 for `H_1` and `H_2` and G2 on the letters. It checks the
BR relation `A^(a^-1) A^-1 = A^((a')^-1)` of G3/G4, in the right-action
convention `u^a = a^-1 u a` of KMS, with the mirrored identity as a failing
control. Condition (3) of KMS Lemma 4.1 is tested on random `H_2`-conjugates.
It also checks that
generators and random words have order dividing `n_0(p) p^e`, and the
prime-to-`p` parts of the generator orders. All checks pass (`output.txt`).
This is evidence only. The proof above does not depend on it.
