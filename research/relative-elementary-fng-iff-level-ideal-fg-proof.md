If `I=sum_l U a_l U`, the normal closure of the finite set `e_12(a_l)`
contains `e_ij(+-a_l)` after elementary changes of indices.  The Steinberg
commutator identities then put `e_ij(u a_l v)` in that normal closure for
all `u,v in U`; additivity gives every `e_ij(a)` with `a in I`.  Thus these
finitely many matrices normally generate `E_n(U,I)`.

Conversely, suppose `E_n(U,I)` is normally generated by
`h_1,...,h_r`.  Express each `h_k`, as permitted by the definition of the
relative elementary subgroup, as a finite product of conjugates of
elementary matrices with entries in `I`.  Let `b_1,...,b_t` be all entries
occurring in these expressions and put `J=sum_q U b_q U`.  Then every
`h_k` lies in `E_n(U,J)`, so

```text
E_n(U,I) <= E_n(U,J) <= E_n(U,I).
```

For `a in I`, reduce `e_12(a)` modulo `J`.  The equality of relative
elementary subgroups makes its image the identity, which is possible only
when `a in J`.  Hence `I<=J`; the reverse inclusion is immediate, so
`I=J` is finitely generated.
