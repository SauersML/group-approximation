**ESTABLISHED** (lane `bh-solvable`, lane proof, elementary; not independently reviewed). The method
is the finitary analogue of the necessity half of Cornulier's criterion for permutational wreath
products (Y. Cornulier, *Finitely presented wreath products and double coset decompositions*,
arXiv:math/0509090, Theorem 1.1: `W ≀_X G` is finitely presented iff `W` and `G` are, stabilizers
are finitely generated, and `G` has finitely many orbits on `X^2`). No priority is claimed.

## Statement

Let a finitely generated group `G` act on a set `X` with finitely many orbits. For a finite `F ⊆ X`, let
`Ĉ_F` be the colimit of the groups `Sym(B)`, over all `B ⊆ gF` with `g ∈ G`, along the inclusions.
`G` acts on `Ĉ_F`, and there is a natural map `Ĉ_F → FSym(X)`.

1. **Necessity.** If `FSym(X) ⋊ G` (equivalently `Alt_fin(X) ⋊ G`) is finitely presented, then
   `Ĉ_F → FSym(X)` is an isomorphism for some finite `F`.
2. **Covering obstruction.** Let `K_F` be the simplicial complex on `X` whose simplices are the
   subsets of translates `gF`. If `K_F` is connected and has a connected covering of degree at least
   2, then `Ĉ_F → FSym(X)` is not injective.
3. **Consequence.** If for every finite `F` the complex `K_F` is disconnected or has nontrivial `π_1`,
   then `FSym(X) ⋊ G` is not finitely presented.

## Proof

1. **Necessity.**
   - *Setup.* Fix orbit representatives `x_k`, a finite generating set `S` of `G`, and base
     transpositions: `(x_k, s x_k)` for `s ∈ S`, plus finitely many more so that together with `G`
     they generate `FSym(X)` (connecting the orbits). Take a finite presentation `⟨S, τ_j | R⟩`.
   - *Relators live in a bounded set.* Moving the `G`-letters of a relator to the right writes it as
     `∏ g_i τ_j g_i^{-1} · g`, where the `g_i` are images of prefixes. The `G`-image `g` is `1`.
     So `∏ g_iτ_jg_i^{-1} = 1` in `FSym(X)`, and all supports lie in
     `F = {g x_k, g y : |g| ≤ L, y ∈ supp τ_j}`, a finite set, where `L` bounds the relator lengths.
   - *The finite set is connected.* Enlarge `F` to the ball `{g x_k : |g| ≤ L'}`, for `L'` large.
     Consecutive points along a word are joined by base-type transpositions inside `F`. So every
     `Sym(gF)` is generated inside `Ĉ_F` by `G`-conjugates of the base transpositions.
   - *Splitting.* Each relator, read in `Ĉ_F ⋊ G`, becomes the image of an identity in `Sym(F)`, so
     it holds. This gives `ψ : FSym(X) ⋊ G → Ĉ_F ⋊ G` with `π ∘ ψ = id` for the natural map `π`.
   - *Conclusion.* `Ĉ_F ⋊ G` is generated by `G` and the images of `Sym(gF)`, hence by `S` and the
     `τ_j`, so `ψ` is onto. So `π` is an isomorphism, and `Ĉ_F ≅ FSym(X)`.
2. **Covering obstruction.** Let `p : X̃ → X` be a connected covering of `K_F` of degree `≥ 2`.
   - *The cocone.* Each simplex `gF` lifts to disjoint copies. Send `σ ∈ Sym(B)`, `B ⊆ gF`, to
     `σ̃ ∈ Sym(X̃)`, acting as `σ` on every lift of `B`. Lifts of a simplex are compatible with the
     lifts of its faces, so this is a cocone and induces `Ĉ_F → Sym(X̃)`.
   - *A failing relation.* Take a simple edge loop `x_0, …, x_{n−1}, x_0` in `K_F` with nontrivial
     monodromy `μ`, and put `c = (x_0x_1)(x_1x_2)⋯(x_{n−2}x_{n−1})`. In `FSym(X)`,
     `c^{n−1}(x_0x_1)c^{-(n−1)} = (x_{n−1}x_0)` or its analogue for the chosen orientation: an
     identity among `n` transpositions.
   - In `Sym(X̃)` the left side swaps the lifts of `x_{n−1}` and `x_0` along the lifted path, which
     lie in one sheet. The right side swaps them across the closing edge, where they differ by `μ`.
     So the identity fails in the image of `Ĉ_F`, and `Ĉ_F → FSym(X)` is not injective.
3. Combine items 1 and 2. ∎

## Calibrations

- **Wreath products.** With `W^B` in place of `Sym(B)`, far copies of `W` stay free of each other in
  the colimit. The analogue of item 1 then forces every pair of points into a common translate, which
  is Cornulier's condition (finitely many orbits on `X^2`).
- **FSym can do better than W^B.** Transpositions compose, so far commutations can follow from local
  relations. This is consistent with Houghton's `H_3` (a near action, not split) being finitely
  presented.
- **The line.** For `X = Z` with `G = Z`, take `F` inside an interval of `r + 1` points. For `F` an
  interval, `K_F` is contractible. Yet `Ĉ_F` maps onto the Coxeter group `W_r` on `s_i = (i, i+1)`
  with `m = 3` for adjacent, `m = 2` for `2 <= |i − j| <= r − 1`, and `m = ∞` beyond. The parabolic
  subgroup on each window is a symmetric group, and parabolic intersections are parabolic, so these
  form a cocone. `W_r` is infinite, so `Ĉ_F ≠ FSym(Z)`, which recovers Brown's theorem that `H_2`
  is not finitely presented. So item 2 is sufficient for failure but not necessary.
- **Free and levelled actions.** These fail by `levelled-actions-make-finitary-extensions-not-fp2`
  (after Genevois–Tessera, arXiv:2401.13520, Theorem 1.5, for lampshufflers).

- **Item 2 is vacuous for finitely presented `G` with finitely generated point stabilizers**
  (added 2026-09-18). Let `X = G/H` with `G` finitely presented and `H` finitely generated, and
  take `F` a large ball `{g x_0 : |g| <= L}`.
  - Every edge of `K_F` lies in a simplex that also contains a Schreier-graph path between its
    ends, so edge loops reduce to Schreier loops.
  - A Schreier loop at `x_0` reads a word in `H`. It differs from a product of generators of `H`
    by relators of `G`.
  - Both kinds of bounded closed paths lie in translates of `F`, so they are null-homotopic.
  - So `π_1(K_F) = 1`, and any obstruction for such actions must be of the line (Coxeter) type or
    homological, as in `levelled-actions-make-finitary-extensions-not-fp2`.

## Application to gate (b)

For `P = A_p/C` acting on `Ω' = P/T`, finite presentation of `Alt_fin(Ω') ⋊ P` needs a finite
`F ⊆ Ω'` at whose scale `FSym(Ω')` is locally presented. Two things block the easy derivations.
- Two points in one fibre of the central quasi-cyclic group `A`, differing by an element of large
  order, never lie in a common translate of `F`.
- `P` acts isometrically for its invariant pair-length, so conjugation by `P` never shortens a far
  configuration, and the Houghton-type induction on distance is unavailable.

Whether the Coxeter-type (line) obstruction or a derivation wins is the open content of gate (b).

**Partial-torus hosts (bh-free-60, `partial-torus-abels-quotients-are-finitely-presented-with-max-n`).**
- The torus-coset action of `P_Δ/e_1n(L)` passes the stabilizer law. Every root space has a
  nontrivial torus weight, and the corner `R/L` is torsion when `L ≠ 0`, so characters of finite-index
  subgroups are carried by the torus, which fixes the base point.
- They meet the same open point. The order of the difference of two points in one fibre of the
  Prüfer radical is invariant under the action, even when the radical is scaled (as by `t = 2` in
  `G_2`). So pair types are unbounded, and far fibre pairs never share a translate of `F`.
- These hosts therefore change nothing for gate (b). The fallback is a near action or a germ host,
  whose elements can shorten far configurations.

## Lesson for general BH

Finite presentation of a split finitary overgroup `Alt_fin(X) ⋊ G` requires local presentability of
`FSym(X)` at one `G`-finite scale (the converse is not proved here).
- This is Cornulier's wreath criterion with symmetric groups in place of powers.
- Finitary routes to Boone–Higman therefore need inputs whose permutation sets are small at a finite
  scale: no nontrivial covers, and no line-like directions.
- Near actions (Houghton), whose elements are not isometries, escape this, because they can shorten
  far configurations.
- For the soluble minimax inputs, this favours a near-action or germ route over split finitary
  extensions.
