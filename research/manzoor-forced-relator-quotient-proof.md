Every relator in `R_str` lies in `H` with probability one.  Invariance of
`mu` gives the same statement for each conjugate, and there are only
countably many conjugates and finite products.  Their simultaneous conull
intersection proves

```text
N<=H almost surely.                                     (MRP1)
```

The pushforward `bar(mu)` under `H |-> H/N` is therefore defined and remains
ergodic.  For `n in N`, `(MRP1)` gives `chi_mu(n)=1`; in the tracial GNS
representation,

```text
||pi_mu(n)-1||_2^2=2-2 Re chi_mu(n)=0.                  (MRP2)
```

Thus `pi_mu` factors through `Q`.  Its factored character is exactly the
membership character of `bar(mu)`, so the two GNS algebras coincide.

If `Q` were finite, `bar(mu)` would be supported on the finite set `Sub(Q)`.
Its character would have finite-dimensional GNS algebra, contradicting
non-Connes-embeddability.  Hence `[F:N]=infinity`.  The subgroup `N` is
nontrivial because it contains the square of a free strategy generator, and
it is finitely normally generated by construction.

Recall the standard free-group fact:

```text
a nontrivial finitely generated normal subgroup of a finite-rank free group
has finite index.                                       (MRP3)
```

One proof uses the Schreier covering of the finite rose.  Finite generation
makes the covering core finite.  If the subgroup is nontrivial and normal,
one fixed nontrivial subgroup word gives a loop based at every coset vertex,
so every vertex lies in the core.  The covering therefore has finitely many
vertices.  Applying `(MRP3)` to `N` proves that `N` is infinitely generated.

If `bar(mu)` gives positive mass to finitely generated subgroups of `Q`,
ergodicity makes that mass one.  A countable group has only countably many
finitely generated subgroups, so `bar(mu)` has an atom.  Its character is the
same non-CE character, and
`atomic-noncohyperlinear-irs-forces-nonhyperlinear` finishes.  This proves
the alternative `(MFR2)`.

Finally `C_mu` is a normal subgroup, contains `N`, and is nontrivial.  If it
were finitely generated, `(MRP3)` would make it finite index.  Then every
`H` in the conull support contains `C_mu`, so `mu` would be supported on the
finite set of subgroups above `C_mu`; it would have an atom and again be
terminal.  The same argument applies to any fixed nontrivial finitely
generated normal `K normal F`: the event `{H:K<=H}` is conjugation invariant,
so positive probability and ergodicity make it conull; then `K<=C_mu`, while
`(MRP3)` makes `K` finite index and forces atomicity.  This proves all final
claims.
