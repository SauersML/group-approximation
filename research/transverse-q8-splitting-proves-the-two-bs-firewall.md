## 6. What a genuine successor must change

The first splitting (3) proves that changing the visible edge from
(mathbb Z	imes C_2) to (F_2	imes C_2) is algebraically easy.
The transverse splitting (8) proves that finite duplication does not couple
the two invisible words: the group is still assembled from one-word pieces
over finite (Q_8).

A useful nonamenable or relative-property-((T)) successor must therefore
make an entire nonamenable packet interact irreducibly with the sign. Adding
a Kazhdan spectator, taking a free product of invisible actors, or attaching
finitely many independent invisible words leaves an amenable transverse
decomposition or an uncontrolled commutator term in (14). The shared-center
Pauli wreath avoids finite duplication by using arbitrarily large site
packets, but its fixed-window multiplicity reservoir is exactly the
remaining quantitative obstruction. No nonhyperlinear group is obtained.
