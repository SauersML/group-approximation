For the compatible reset plane `(CRH4)`, let `K(B)` be the class-two
rank-fifteen affine Schur packet with center

```text
C=<J,z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4> ~= F_2^5.
```

Adjoin the dummy Pauli pair to obtain the directional source `E`.  Its
quotient by `C` has dimension `30+2=32`, so

```text
|E|=2^37.                                               (RCK1)
```

On the fresh side, retain the relabelled Schur packet and adjoin the two
balanced pairs of `(GRS3)`.  The fresh quotient has dimension `30+4=34`, so

```text
|E'|=2^39.                                              (RCK2)
```

In the common selector coordinates, the old-to-fresh map is the identity on
the relabelled Schur normal form and sends the dummy pair by

```text
x_0 |-> p_-p_+,             z_0 |-> q_-q_+.             (RCK3)
```

Because the two controlled pairs commute across signs,

```text
[p_-p_+,q_-q_+]=s(Js)=J.                               (RCK4)
```

All squares and all cross commutators are preserved.  More explicitly, the
exact presentation replay checks all `32^2=1024` ordered commutator pairs.
The quotient map in `(RCK3)` has rank `32`, while the selector-center map has
rank `5`; therefore its kernel is zero.  Each of the three derivative
matrices has rank fifteen, and its words span the same complete residual
`q`-Lagrangian, so the replay includes the subgroup generated by all three
directional derivative packets rather than just three dimension counts.

Central-product six component maps over their common `J`.  The source center
and quotient dimensions are respectively

```text
1+6*4=25,                  6*32=192,                    (RCK5)
```

and the fresh quotient dimension is `6*34=204`.  The block component map has
ranks `25` and `192`, hence zero kernel.  The regular factor permutations
satisfy `s^2=u^2=(su)^3=1`, are faithful, place each of the three directions
in exactly two factors, and commute with the component map on all `384`
tested quotient basis vectors.  Consequently there is an injective
semidirect edge map

```text
E_reg semidirect S_3  -->  E'_reg semidirect S_3,       (RCK6)
```

whose two finite groups have orders `6*2^217` and `6*2^229`.

The private factors are necessary.  On one residual copy, put

```text
Omega(D)=[[0,D],[D^T,0]],
K=Omega(D_1)^(-1)Omega(D_2).
```

An arbitrary Clifford congruence realizing the reset swap would make `K`
similar to `K^(-1)`, while the reset shear would make it similar to `I+K`.
The exact nullity witnesses are

```text
nullity((K)^4+K+I)=8,       nullity((K^-1)^4+K^-1+I)=0,
nullity(f_9(K))=0,          nullity(f_9(I+K))=18,
f_9(t)=t^9+t^4+t^3+t+1.                              (RCK7)
```

Thus neither generator exists even after allowing arbitrary mixing of the
two Pauli Lagrangians.  Regular induction avoids this obstruction by keeping
six private residual copies.  If those copies are instead identified with
one literal fresh residual packet, the residual quotient map

```text
F_2^(6*30) -> F_2^30
```

has kernel dimension `150`; for example the same `p` generator in two
different factors gives a nontrivial kernel word.  Hence `(RCK6)` permits
identification of the common logical BCS products but not identification of
the private Schur residual generators.

This closes the full finite presentation and semidirect-injectivity audit.
It does not identify the fresh events with the global Fanizza endpoint words.
