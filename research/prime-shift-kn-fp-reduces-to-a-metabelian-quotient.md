**ESTABLISHED (2026-09-18)** through
`prime-shift-kn-fp-reduces-to-a-metabelian-quotient-proof`. Lane proof,
elementary apart from the cited Bieri--Strebel and Baumslag theorems. Whether
`K_n` is finitely presented stays open: `prime-shift-group-k2-is-finitely-presented`.

**Notation** (as in `gl-n-q-lies-in-prime-shift-permutation-group`), `n >= 2`.
- `K_n = <GL_n(Z), e, σ_n> <= Sym(Q^n)` with `e = diag(2,1,...,1)`; it contains
  `GL_n(Q)`.
- `N = <<GL_n(Z)>>_(K_n)`, the normal closure. It equals
  `<<x_12(1), diag(-1,1,...,1)>>`, and it contains `SL_n(Q)`.
- `Q_>0` is written additively as the free `Z[t,t^-1]`-module on `2`, with
  `t` acting by `σ`, so that `p_k` corresponds to `t^k`.
- The content `c(w)` of `w in Q^n \ 0` is the positive generator of the
  subgroup `Z w_1 + ... + Z w_n` of `Q`.

**Statement.**
1. **Degree character.** For every `k in K_n` there is a unique `d(k) in Z` with
   `k(t w) = σ^(d(k))(t) k(w)` for all `t in Q`, `w in Q^n`. The map `d` is a
   surjective homomorphism `K_n -> Z`, with `d(σ_n) = 1` and `d(GL_n(Q)) = 0`. So
   `K_n = K_n^0 ⋊ <σ_n>`, where `K_n^0 = ker d = <σ_n^j GL_n(Q) σ_n^-j : j in Z>`,
   and `K_n^ab` maps onto `Z`.
2. **Centre of the kernel.** The centre of `K_n^0` is exactly the scalar group
   `Q^x · I`. It is normal in `K_n`, and `σ_n` acts on it by `σ`.
3. **The quotient by GL_n(Z).** Let `I_n = {r in Q_>0 : diag(r,1,...,1) in N}`.
   Then `I_n` is a `σ`-invariant subgroup, and `K_n/N ≅ (Q_>0/I_n) ⋊_σ Z`. This is
   a metabelian group whose base is a cyclic `Z[t,t^-1]`-module, generated by the
   class of `e`.
4. **Finite presentation criterion.** If `K_n` is finitely presented, then
   `Q_>0/I_n` is finitely generated over `Z[t]` or over `Z[t^-1]`. Equivalently,
   for some finite product `r` of primes `p_k`, all with `k >= 1` or all with
   `k <= -1` (exponents in `Z`; the empty product is allowed),
   `e · diag(r,1,...,1)^-1 in <<GL_n(Z)>>_(K_n)`.
   - This sharpens item 2 of `prime-shift-hnn-groups-are-not-finitely-presented`.
   - Contrapositive: if no such relation holds, for example if
     every element of `I_n` is a perfect square, then `K_n` is
     not finitely presented.
5. **Germs on points are blind to e.** Let `D ⊆ Q^n \ 0` be `GL_n(Z)`-invariant,
   and let `F : D -> Y` satisfy `F(g w) = F(w)` for `g in GL_n(Z)`, `w in D`, and
   `F(e w) = φ(F(w))` whenever `w, e w in D`, for some map `φ : Y -> Y`. Then
   `φ` is the identity on `F(D)`.
   - Every domain cut out by conditions on the valuations of `c(w)` is
     `GL_n(Z)`-invariant. So the deep-denominator germs that settled `A_1`
     cannot detect `e`.
   - The domain of points with one uniquely deepest coordinate is not
     closed under `x_21(1)`, which sends `(λ,0,...)` to
     `(λ,λ,...)`. Enlarging it to a `GL_n(Z)`-invariant domain puts it under item 5.
6. **The dilation is the whole obstruction.** The subgroup
   `K_n' = <GL_n(Z), 2I, σ_n>` is not finitely presented:
   - `c(k w) = ψ_k(c(w))` defines a homomorphism `ψ` of `K_n'` onto
     `Q_>0 ⋊_σ Z ≅ Z ≀ Z`, killing `GL_n(Z)`;
   - `K_n'/<<GL_n(Z)>> ≅ Z ≀ Z`.

   But `e ∉ K_n'`. Also, `K_n'` does not contain `GL_n(Q)`, so this does not
   bear on the Boone--Higman route.

**What stays open.** Whether some one-sided relation as in item 4 holds in
`K_n`, and in particular whether `I_n = 1`. By items 5 and 6, a proof that it fails
must use data on which `GL_n(Z)` acts nontrivially. Injectivity of
`Π_n -> K_n` would settle it (`I_n = 1`) and is a Dickson-type problem, as for
`Π_1 -> A_1`.
