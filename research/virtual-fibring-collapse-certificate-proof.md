Notation is that of the target claim. `X` is the presentation complex of `<a, t | w>`. Pieces are
vertices, edges and 2-cells.

**Step 0. `w` is not a proper power in `F(a, t)`.**

Suppose `w = u^m` in `F(a,t)` with `m ≥ 2` and `u ≠ 1`.
- Write the reduced form of `u` as `g v g^{-1}`, with `v` cyclically reduced and nontrivial, and
  with no cancellation in the product.
- Then `v^m` is reduced, so `g v^m g^{-1}` is the reduced form of `w`.
- If `g` were nonempty, this reduced word would begin with a letter and end with its inverse.
  It would then not be cyclically reduced, contradicting (V0).
- So `g` is empty, and `w = v^m` as strings, which (V0) excludes.

By `non-proper-power-one-relator-complexes-are-aspherical`, `X` is aspherical.

**Step 1. The finite cover.**

Since `α` and `τ` are permutations, every vertex of the 1-skeleton of `X_H` has exactly one
outgoing and one incoming edge for each letter. So the 1-skeleton is a covering of the rose
`X^{(1)}`. A path starting at `i` and reading `u` ends at `i·u`.

Since `i·w = i`, the lift from each `s` of the attaching path of the 2-cell of `X` closes up. The lifts of the 2-cell of
`X` correspond to the lifts `s ∈ Q` of the base point of its boundary. So `X_H`, with one 2-cell
`σ_s` for each lift, is the covering space of `X` attached to the transitive right `G`-set `Q`.

It is connected by transitivity. A loop reading `u` lifts to a closed path at `0` exactly when
`0·u = 0`, so `π_1(X_H, 0) ≅ H = Stab_G(0)`, and `[G : H] = |Q| = k`.

`X_H` is a finite 2-complex with `k` vertices, `2k` edges and `k` 2-cells. It is aspherical, since
it covers `X`, and `χ(X_H) = 0`. So `H` has a finite 2-dimensional `K(H, 1)` of Euler
characteristic `0`.

**Step 2. The character.**

By (V2), `c` sums to zero along every 2-cell boundary, so it is a cellular 1-cocycle. The value
`c(γ)` of an edge loop `γ` is unchanged by inserting or deleting a backtrack or a 2-cell boundary.
So `φ[γ] = c(γ)` is a well-defined homomorphism `π_1(X_H, 0) = H → Z`.

`π_1` of the 1-skeleton surjects onto `π_1(X_H)` and is generated by the fundamental loops of any
spanning tree. So `φ(H)` is generated by the fundamental-loop values, whose gcd is `1`. Hence `φ`
is onto.

**Step 3. The infinite cyclic cover.**

The map `p : X̃ → X_H`, `(i, y) ↦ i`, is a covering map, with deck group `Z` acting by the
translation `T : y ↦ y + 1`. An edge path from `(0, 0)` lying over a loop `γ` at `0` ends at
`(0, c(γ))`. So `γ` lifts to a closed loop exactly when `[γ] ∈ ker φ`.

`X̃` is connected:
- a path in `X_H` from `0` to `i` lifts to a path from `(0, 0)` to some `(i, y')`;
- if `β` is a path from `0` to `i` and `γ` a loop at `0` with `c(γ) = 1`, then `β^{-1} γ β`
  lifts from `(i, y)` to a path ending at `(i, y+1)`.

Hence `p_*` identifies `π_1(X̃, (0,0))` with `N = ker φ`. `X̃` is aspherical, because it covers
`X`.

The translation `T` is a cellular automorphism preserving all incidences and multiplicities, and
`T(X[p, q]) = X[p+1, q+1]`.

Every piece of `X̃` lies over a piece of `X_H`, so its height span is at most `S`:
- for a 2-cell this is the definition of `S`;
- for an edge, `|c| ≤ S` by (V3).

`X[p, q]` is a finite subcomplex: a piece all of whose vertices have heights in `[p, q]` has all
its faces in `X[p, q]`.

**Step 4. Moves are strong deformation retractions.**

Let `A` be a finite subcomplex of `X̃` and `A'` the result of one move.
- *Vertex move `(v, e)`.* Since `e` is the only edge at `v`, is not a loop, and no 2-cell passes
  through `v`, `A'` is a subcomplex and `A = A' ∪ e`. The closed edge `e` meets `A'` in its other
  endpoint only. Retracting the interval onto that endpoint is a strong deformation retraction of
  `A` onto `A'`.
- *Edge move `(e, σ)`.*
  - No other 2-cell of `A` contains `e`, so `A'` is a subcomplex.
  - Let `Φ : D² → A` be the characteristic map of `σ`, with `∂D²` divided into arcs along the
    boundary word. Since `e` occurs exactly once, exactly one arc `I` maps onto `e`, and `Φ` maps
    `int I` homeomorphically onto the open edge `e`.
  - Let `J` be the closure of `∂D² \ I`, so that `Φ(J) ⊆ A'`. By (V0), `w` has length at least
    `2`, so `J` is an arc. The map `A' ⊔ D² → A` is a closed
    continuous surjection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff one, hence a quotient map. Its
    only identifications are `z ∼ Φ(z)` for `z ∈ J`. So `A ≅ A' ∪_{Φ|J} D²`.
  - The pair `(D², J)` is homeomorphic to a square with three of its sides. Radial projection
    from a point beyond the fourth side is a strong deformation retraction of the square onto
    those three sides.
  - This homotopy is stationary on `J`, so it descends to a strong deformation retraction of `A`
    onto `A'`.

So a finite sequence of moves from `A` to `B` gives a strong deformation retraction of `A` onto
`B`. We write `A ↘ B`.

**Step 5. Translation lemma. If `q − p ≥ S + 1`, then `X[p, q] ↘ X[p, q−1]` and
`X[p, q] ↘ X[p+1, q]`.**

*Top.* Put `r = q − S − 1 ≥ p`. Translating the certified sequence `X[0, S+1] ↘ X[0, S]` by `T^r`
gives a sequence of moves `X[r, q] ↘ X[r, q−1]`. Let `D_r = X[r, q] \ X[r, q−1]` and
`D_p = X[p, q] \ X[p, q−1]`.

1. **`D_r = D_p`.** Both consist of the pieces with a vertex at height `q` and all vertices in the
   respective range.
   - A piece of `D_p` has span at most `S` (Step 3), so its vertices have heights in
     `[q − S, q] ⊆ [r, q]`. Hence `D_p ⊆ D_r`.
   - Conversely, `[r, q] ⊆ [p, q]`, so `D_r ⊆ D_p`.
2. **The moves removing `D_r` are exactly its pieces.** Moves only remove pieces, and the
   sequence ends at `X[r, q−1]`, so the pieces removed are exactly `D_r`.
3. **Run the same moves on `X[p, q]`.** Before the `j`-th move, the current complexes are
   `X[r, q−1] ∪ R_j` and `X[p, q−1] ∪ R_j`, where `R_j ⊆ D_r` is the set of pieces not yet
   removed.
   - A vertex removed by a move lies in `D_r`, so it is at height `q`. Every edge at it and every
     2-cell through it has a vertex at height `q`. So it belongs to neither `X[r, q−1]` nor
     `X[p, q−1]`.
   - An edge removed by a move has an endpoint at height `q`. Every 2-cell containing it has a
     vertex at height `q`, so again it belongs to neither lower window.

   So the edges at the vertex, the 2-cells through it, and the 2-cells containing the edge are
   the same in both current complexes, namely those in `R_j`. Being a loop and occurrence
   multiplicities are properties of `X̃`. Hence each move is legal in the second run, and the
   second run ends at `X[p, q−1]`.

*Bottom.* Put `r = p + 1`. Translate `X[−1, S] ↘ X[0, S]` by `T^r` to get
`X[p, p+S+1] ↘ X[p+1, p+S+1]`, where `p + S + 1 ≤ q`. The same argument, with "a vertex at height
`p`", gives `X[p, q] ↘ X[p+1, q]`.

**Step 6. The window carries the homotopy type.**

Let `m ≥ S`.
- For `q = m, m−1, …, S+1` we have `q + m ≥ S + 1`, so the top case gives
  `X[−m, m] ↘ X[−m, S]`.
- For `p = −m, …, −1` we have `S − p ≥ S + 1`, so the bottom case gives `X[−m, S] ↘ X[0, S]`.

Hence `X[−m, m]` strong deformation retracts onto `X[0, S]`, through a retraction
`r_m : X[−m, m] → X[0, S]` fixing `X[0, S]` pointwise.

A compact subset of the CW complex `X̃` meets only finitely many cells, so it lies in some
`X[−m, m]` with `m ≥ S`. Put the base point at `(0, 0) ∈ X[0, S]`. For every `j ≥ 0`, inclusion
induces a bijection `π_j(X[0, S]) → π_j(X̃)`:
- *onto:* a based map `S^j → X̃` lands in some `X[−m, m]` and is homotopic, rel base point,
  into `X[0, S]`;
- *injective:* a null-homotopy in `X̃` lands in some `X[−m, m]`, and composing with `r_m` gives
  a null-homotopy in `X[0, S]`.

The case `j = 0` shows `X[0, S]` is connected. So `X[0, S]` is a finite connected aspherical
complex with `π_1(X[0, S]) ≅ N`. In particular `N` is finitely presented.

**Step 7. The kernel is free.**

By Step 1, `H` has a finite 2-dimensional `K(H,1)` of Euler characteristic `0`. `φ : H → Z` is
onto by Step 2, and its kernel `N` is finitely generated by Step 6. By the `n = 2` consequence in
`kochloukova-euler-zero-cyclic-quotient-kernels-have-cd-drop`, `N` is free, possibly trivial, and
finitely generated, so `N ≅ F_n` for some `n ≥ 0`.

`X[0, S]` is a connected aspherical CW complex with fundamental group `F_n`. So it is homotopy
equivalent to a wedge of `n` circles (uniqueness of `K(π, 1)` up to homotopy), and hence
`χ(X[0, S]) = 1 − n`.

**Step 8. Splitting.**

Choose `h ∈ H` with `φ(h) = 1`. Every `g ∈ H` is uniquely `g = u h^{φ(g)}` with `u ∈ N`. So
`H ≅ N ⋊_β Z`, where `β(u) = h u h^{-1}` is an automorphism of `N`.

**Step 9. `F_n` is residually finite.**

Let `u = x_1 ⋯ x_L`, with `L ≥ 1`, be a nontrivial reduced word in a free basis
`s_1, …, s_n`. On `{0, …, L}`, let `s` send `j−1` to `j` whenever `x_j = s`, and `j` to `j−1`
whenever `x_j = s^{-1}`.

Each `s` is a partial injection. Two different assignments with the same source `i` would be
`i → i+1` (from `x_{i+1} = s`) and `i → i−1` (from `x_i = s^{-1}`), making `x_i x_{i+1} = s^{-1} s`.
Two with the same target `i` would force `x_i x_{i+1} = s s^{-1}`. Reducedness excludes both.

Extend each partial injection to a permutation of `{0, …, L}`. By induction on `j`, `0` moves
to `j` under `x_1 ⋯ x_j`. So `u` sends `0` to `L ≠ 0`, and `u` survives in a finite quotient.

**Step 10. `H` and `G` are residually finite.**

By Steps 8–9 and `finitely-generated-rf-automorphism-mapping-torus-is-rf`, applied to the
finitely generated residually finite group `N ≅ F_n` and the automorphism `β`, `H` is residually
finite.

Let `g ∈ G \ {1}`.
- If `g ∉ H`, the kernel of the action on `Q` is a finite-index normal subgroup that does not
  contain `g`.
- If `g ∈ H`, choose `M ⊴ H` of finite index with `g ∉ M`. `M` has finite index in `G`, so its
  normaliser does, and `M` has finitely many `G`-conjugates. Their intersection `core_G(M)` is a
  finite-index normal subgroup of `G` that does not contain `g`.

So `G` is residually finite.

**Step 11. Soficity.**

Given finite `F ⊆ G`, intersect finitely many finite-index normal subgroups to get `M ⊴ G` of
finite index with `F ∩ M ⊆ {1}`. Left multiplication on `G/M` is a homomorphism
`σ : G → Sym(G/M)`. For `g ∈ F \ {1}` and any coset `xM`, `g x M = x M` would force
`x^{-1} g x ∈ M`, hence `g ∈ M`. So `σ(g)` has no fixed points.

Such `σ` are exact on `F` and at normalised Hamming distance `1` from the identity on `F \ {1}`.
So `G` is sofic.
