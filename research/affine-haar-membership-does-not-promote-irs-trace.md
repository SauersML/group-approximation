Passing the affine-Haar Fourier test for the direct subgroup-membership name
law is not yet a trace-promotion theorem for an IRS.

Let `Gamma` be any countable group and `N normal Gamma`.  The Dirac IRS
`delta_N` has deterministic indicator configuration `1_N`.  Its law is Haar
measure on the singleton affine coset `{1_N}`, so every finite Fourier moment
has modulus one and the criterion in
`affine-haar-process-fourier-modulus-compiler` passes maximally.

Under conjugation, however, that name process is the one-point action.
Consequently its algebraic action crossed product is

```text
L^infinity({1_N}) rtimes Gamma = L(Gamma).              (AIP1)
```

The IRS character and its GNS algebra are instead

```text
tau_N(g)=1_(g in N),
pi_tau(Gamma)'' = L(Gamma/N).                           (AIP2)
```

For every `n in N\{e}`, the canonical actor unitary in `(AIP1)` has trace
zero, whereas its IRS-GNS image in `(AIP2)` is the identity and has trace one.
Thus the canonical Fourier/Pontryagin construction supplied by the
membership name does not preserve the IRS trace and does not produce the IRS
GNS algebra.  This is not repaired by the fact that some unrelated abstract
embedding between the two algebras may exist.

The example also identifies the circular extreme: a Dirac normal-subgroup
IRS has an affine-Haar singleton law for free, while non-cohyperlinearity of
that IRS is exactly non-hyperlinearity of `Gamma/N`.  Therefore a direct
membership-law Fourier check cannot be the missing promotion step.

For the ZPC route, a sufficient nonlinear affine-Haar factor must retain more
than the stabilizer-name sigma-algebra: it must give a trace-preserving
embedding of the non-CE IRS GNS/relation algebra (including its normalizer
multiplication) into the algebraic crossed product.  Equivalently, one may
start with a source action crossed product already known to contain that
algebra and construct the action factor `(HSD1)` of
`finite-field-haarization-survival-dichotomy`.  No such retention follows
from the joint membership moments alone.
