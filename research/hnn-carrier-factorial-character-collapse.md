Let `n >= 3`, `Gamma = SL_n(Z[1/p])`, `Lambda = SL_n(Z)`,
`H = < Gamma, t | [t, Lambda] = 1 >`.  Claim: every factorial
hyperlinear character `tau` of `H` with `tau|_Gamma = delta_e`
satisfies

```text
tau(t^(-1) gamma^(-1) t gamma) = 1  for all gamma in Gamma,      (FC1)
```

equivalently `pi_tau(t)` is central in the II_1 factor
`N = pi_tau(H)''`.

By `rcc-carrier-character-face-compression` (whose center hypotheses
are trivial for odd `n`) this claim is EQUIVALENT to the collapse
`(RC3)` of `sl3-pair-relative-commutant-route`, hence reaches the goal
through `non-hyperlinear-from-sl3-relative-commutant-collapse`.  The
gain over the operator form is the shape of the enemy: one separable
II_1 factor `N` generated by the irreducible property (T) inclusion
`L(Lambda) subseteq L(Gamma)` together with a single unitary
`k = pi_tau(t)` commuting with `L(Lambda)`.

## Attempts

- **The letter is trace-orthogonal to the whole lattice algebra.**
  `E_(L(Gamma))(k)` commutes with `L(Lambda)` (bimodularity), and
  `L(Lambda)' cap L(Gamma) = C` because `Lambda` is relatively icc in
  `Gamma` (recorded in `relative-commutant-collapse-for-sl2-pair`).
  So `E_(L(Gamma))(k) = tau(k) 1`: the enemy letter, minus a scalar,
  is orthogonal to `L(Gamma)` in `L^2(N)`.  Any classification of the
  `L(Gamma)`-bimodule `L^2(N) ominus L^2(L(Gamma))` therefore controls
  the enemy directly.
- **The enemy factor is strongly 1-bounded.**  `Lambda` has (T), so
  `h(L(Lambda)) = 0` (Jung); `k in L(Lambda)' cap N` and every
  `gamma in Gamma` wq-normalize `L(Lambda)` (commutation, resp.
  commensuration), so `N = W*(L(Gamma), k) subseteq W*(wqN_N(L(Lambda)))`
  and Hayes' monotonicity gives `h(N) <= 0`.  So every enemy is a
  strongly 1-bounded hyperlinear factor -- constrained, but as row 3 of
  `rc3-standard-tools-all-fail-audit` records, size constraints alone
  do not force `(FC1)`; an absorption theorem must use the
  commensuration dynamics.
- **Hecke averaging inside the enemy.**  Applying
  `hecke-averaging-realizes-commutant-expectation` with `M = N`:
  `E_(pi_tau(Gamma)' cap N)(k) = lim_N S^N(k)` geometrically.  If the
  enemy has irreducible lattice image (`pi_tau(Gamma)' cap N = C`,
  plausible for extremal `tau` but not automatic), the averages crush
  `k` to the scalar `tau(k)`, and `(FC1)` becomes `|tau(k)| = 1`
  versus the far-defect value `2(1 - |tau(k)|^2)` -- the enemy is then
  a single complex number away from contradiction.  Deciding
  `pi_tau(Gamma)' cap N` for extremal enemies is a concrete first
  target.
- **No amenable enemy, automatically.**  The GNS factor of any enemy
  contains `L(Gamma)` (`tau|_Gamma = delta_e`), which is non-amenable,
  so `N` is never amenable: the Dogon--Vigdorovich hyperfinite-sector
  closure, which in the operator form of the collapse is a separate
  argument, is built into the character form for free.
- **Rigidity technology now applies verbatim.**  `tau|_Gamma` is the
  regular character, the unique non-almost-periodic point of Peterson
  and Bekka--Boutonnet--Houdayer character rigidity for higher-rank
  `Gamma`; charmenability of `Gamma` gives strong constraints on ANY
  trace of a group containing it with expectation.  The missing piece
  is a relative version over the commutation locus of `Lambda`: a
  classification of traces of `H` extending `delta_e`, parallel to how
  `character-rigidity-equals-hyperfinite-hs-stability` classifies
  traces of `Gamma` itself.
- **Falsification gate.**  A factorial hyperlinear enemy would refute
  `(RC3)` (through the compression), killing the whole
  relative-commutant lane at `n >= 3` -- but would NOT by itself make
  the carrier hyperlinear, since only the commutator values of `tau`
  are pinned, so the goal would survive on other lanes.  This
  asymmetry makes the claim strictly safer to attack than a stability
  conjecture: refutation is informative, not fatal.
