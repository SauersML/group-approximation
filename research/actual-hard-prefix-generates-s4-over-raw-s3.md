Let `K~=S3` be generated by the two actual second-chart units occurring in
the raw word.  Across the twelve hard orientations in boundary classes 11
and 30, there are four distinct cross-chart prefix units `q` in the binary
Leavitt quotient.  For every one of them,

```text
|<K,q>| = 24,
order histogram = {1:1, 2:9, 3:8, 4:6},
core_<K,q>(K) = 1.                                  (AHP1)
```

The faithful action of `<K,q>` on the four left cosets of `K` embeds this
order-24 group in `S4`; hence

```text
<K,q> ~= S4.                                         (AHP2)
```

The four prefix orders, in the replay's canonical first-occurrence order,
are `4,2,4,2`.

The group inverse in `(AHP1)` is computed from the source prefix word by
reversing its letters and inverting each `GL4(F2)` chart matrix.  It is not
computed with the Leavitt transpose.  If `q^star` is adjoined as a separate
group word, together with its true inverse `(q^{-1})^star`, then in all four
cases

```text
|<K,q,q^star>| = 168,
order histogram = {1:1, 2:21, 3:56, 4:42, 7:48}.     (AHP3)
```

In particular `q^star!=q^{-1}` in every case.  Equation `(AHP3)` is a finite
packet statement, not an adjoint statement in a complex microstate.

Consequently a hypothetical quotient microstate may be exactified on the
actual hard-prefix `S4`, so the multiplicity-free branching `S4 downarrow
S3` and its central character projectors are legitimate carrier data.  This
does not align the external multiplicity decompositions arising from two
different `S4` extensions.
