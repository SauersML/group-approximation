**Priority.** No novelty is claimed. A bounded search (web and arXiv, 2026-09-18) found
no statement that `C_n` is linear, or residually nilpotent, for `n >= 4`. Kofinas,
arXiv:2603.27648 §2.3, records that Hurley's residual-nilpotence argument "fails" for
`n >= 4`, and works in rank 3 only. Both inputs used here are classical: Gupta–Levin 1986,
Lemma 4.4, and C. K. Gupta 1973.

## Statement

Let `C_n = F_n/[F_n'', F_n]` for `n >= 1`. Then:
1. `C_n ↪ ρ_n(C_n) × C_n/γ_7(C_n)`, where `ρ_n` is the `(2n+1)`-dimensional corner
   representation over `Z[x_i^(±1)]` of
   `free-centre-by-metabelian-corner-kernel-is-the-torsion`. So `C_n` embeds in
   `GL_N(Q(x_1, …, x_n))` for some `N`.
2. `T_n ∩ γ_7(C_n) = 1`. So the torsion survives in the class-6 quotient, and `C_n` is
   residually nilpotent.
3. `C_n` is residually finite, and it is in `B_A` (`free-centre-by-metabelian-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`).

For `n <= 3` the second factor in item 1 is unnecessary.

Item 3 has a variety-theoretic reading. Relatively free groups of every finite rank are
residually finite, so the variety of centre-by-metabelian groups is generated by its
finite members, indeed by its finite nilpotent members.
