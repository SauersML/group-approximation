Let `n >= 3`, and let `Delta <= EL_n(R)` be an infinite subgroup of finite
index `d`.  Left-orderability passes to subgroups and forces torsion-freeness.
So it is enough to find, inside `Delta`, either a subgroup that is not
left-orderable or a nontrivial element of finite order.

**Case 1: the additive order of `1` in `R` is infinite.**
- The ring map `Z -> R` is injective.  Applied entrywise, it gives an injective homomorphism `SL_n(Z) -> GL_n(R)`.
- The image lies in `EL_n(R)`.  `SL_n(Z)` is generated by the matrices `e_ij(1)`, and these map to the elementary matrices `e_ij(1)` of `EL_n(R)`.
- Identify `SL_n(Z)` with its image.  Then `Delta cap SL_n(Z)` has index at most `d` in `SL_n(Z)`.
- By the special case in `higher-rank-real-lattices-are-not-left-orderable`, `Delta cap SL_n(Z)` is not left-orderable, so `Delta` is not either.

**Case 2: the additive order of `1` in `R` is a finite number `m`.**
- `m >= 2`, because `m = 1` means `R = 0`, and then `EL_n(R)` is trivial.
- `R` is infinite.  If `R` were finite, `GL_n(R)` would be finite, and so would `Delta`.
- `a -> e_12(a)` is an injective homomorphism `(R, +) -> EL_n(R)`, since `e_12(a) e_12(b) = e_12(a + b)`.  Its image `U` is infinite, and every element of `U` has order dividing `m`.
- `Delta cap U` has index at most `d` in `U`, so it is infinite.  It therefore contains some `e_12(a) != 1`, and `e_12(a)^m = e_12(m a) = 1`.
- So `Delta` has torsion and is not left-orderable.  ∎
