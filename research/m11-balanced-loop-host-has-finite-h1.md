**ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** Proof: `m11-balanced-loop-host-has-finite-h1-proof`. The proof is
by hand. The script checks the two commutator identities behind item 2 and the shape of
`C^m`, at depth 4 on the tree.

**Setting.** `X = {1, …, 11}`, with right actions. `α = (1, …, 11)` and
`β = (1,2,3,4,8)(5,10,7,11,6)`, so that `⟨α, β⟩ = M_11`. `b = (b, α, β, 1, …, 1)` and
`R_0 = ⟨M_11, b⟩`, as in `tits-hosts-for-transitive-local-actions` item 6. Put

- `C = (1, 1, 1, C, bα, C^{−1}, 1, …, 1)`: `C` at letter `4`, `bα` at `5`, `C^{−1}` at `6`,
  trivial root;
- `R° = ⟨M_11, b, C⟩`.

This is `R'` of `m11-separated-loop-host-has-b1-one` with one more exit, `C^{−1}` at `6`.

**Theorem.**

1. **Infinite order, not contracting.** `C^m = (1, 1, 1, C^m, (bα)^m, C^{−m}, 1, …)`, and
   `bα` has infinite order. So `C` has infinite order, and `R°` is not contracting.
2. **`R°` is the normal closure of `M_11 ∪ {b}`.** Take `π_1 = α³βα²βα`, which fixes `4`
   and `5` and sends `6 ↦ 7`, and `π = α²βα²β`, which sends `6 ↦ 4` and `7 ↦ 6`. Then
   `[C, π_1] = C@6 · (C^{−1})@7` and
   `C = π^{−1}[C, π_1]π · (bα)@5`. So `H_1(R°; Z) = ⟨[b]⟩` is cyclic of order dividing `55`,
   a quotient of `H_1(R_0) = Z/55`. In particular `b_1(R°) = 0`.
3. **Fully self-replicating.** `R°@x ⊆ R°` for every `x`, so `R° ≅ R° ≀_X M_11`, with root
   image `M_11`. `R°` is generated by the finite automaton `{C^{±1}, bα, b, α, β, 1}`.
4. **Loop groups.** The loop subgroups of `fp-self-similar-groups-have-fp-ray-loop-subgroups`
   are:
   - `⟨b⟩ ≅ C_55` at `1^∞`;
   - `⟨C⟩ ≅ Z` at every periodic ray in `{4, 6}^ω`;
   - trivial at every other periodic ray.

   All of them are finitely presented, so the ray-loop test passes.
5. **The loop character is silent.** (FS) of
   `fp-self-similar-finitely-supported-loops-give-characters` fails at `4^∞`: the
   support of `C` there is `{v 4^∞ : v ∈ {4, 6}*}`. It also fails at `1^∞`, with support
   `{4,6}* 5 1^∞`. This is consistent with `b_1 = 0`.
6. **Where it stands.**
   - `H_1(R°; Q) = 0`, and `H_1(R°; Z)` has order dividing `55`, whose primes divide
     `|M_11| = 7920`. So item 3 of `fully-self-replicating-fp-infinity-groups-are-acyclic`
     gives no obstruction in degree 1, at any finiteness length.
   - The ray-loop test passes (item 4), the loop-character test is silent (item 5), and
     `R°` is not contracting, so Bartholdi's theorem does not apply.
   - Its activity is exponential: `C` has `2^n` nontrivial level-`n` sections, on
     `{4, 6}^n`. So amenability is unknown.

   Whether `R°` is finitely presented is **open**. So is `FP_∞`. The same holds for its Tits
   host `(R° ≀ M_12) *_{R°×R°} (R° ≀ M_12)`, which is simple and finitely generated, and
   finitely presented iff `R°` is, by `tits-hosts-for-transitive-local-actions`.

**Why the balance matters.** The exit `C^{−1}` at `6` does two jobs. It makes `C` a product
of conjugates of `M_11` and of a section of `b` (item 2), so no character survives. It also
makes the loop cocycle along `{4,6}^ω` infinitely supported, so the dichotomy of
`fp-self-similar-finitely-supported-loops-give-characters` lets it through. The loop
state stays off `1^∞`, so the branch-group failure of `R''` is avoided too.

**What would decide it.** An `H_2` computation, or a relator analysis of an L-presentation
of `R°`, would decide `FP_2` for `R°`. An exact word-problem check of the order of `[b]` in
`H_1(R°)` is also open.
