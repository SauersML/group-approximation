Notation is as in the target. The one import is the Theorem of
`jacobson-double-parabolic-two-step-models-kill-head`, used with `d = 1` over
the field `K = F_2`. That theorem allows any field `K` and any
finite-dimensional `W`, and asks only for a partial model `rho : B_0 -> GL(W)`
and a filtration stable under `rho(L_0 ∪ N_0)` on which every `n in N_0` has
degree at least `d`. Its conclusion is that `rho(h) - 1` has degree at least
`d + 1`.

## Step 0. Bookkeeping for sigma

- `sigma(1) = sigma(1)^2` in the group `F`, so `sigma(1) = 1`.
- For `x in B_*`, `x^(-1) in B_*` and `x x^(-1) = 1 in B_0`, so
  `sigma(x^(-1)) = sigma(x)^(-1)`.
- **U\* is normal in G_0.** `U*` is normalised by `Lambda`, since
  `Lambda`-conjugates of generators are generators. It contains `sigma(N_0)`
  (take `lambda = 1`), so it is normalised by `sigma(N_0)`. `G_0` is finite,
  and in a finite group `g U g^(-1) ⊂ U` forces equality. So every generator
  of `G_0` normalises `U*`, and hence so does all of `G_0`.

## Step 1. The module

Put `A = F_2[G_0]`, and let `I*` be the augmentation ideal of `F_2[U*]`.
Since `U*` is normal, `a := I* A = A I*` is a two-sided ideal of `A`, and
`a^k = (I*)^k A`. Define

```text
W = A / a^3,     W_k = a^k / a^3   (k = 0, 1, 2, 3),     rho(x) = left multiplication by sigma(x).
```

- `W` is finite-dimensional, because `G_0` is finite.
- `rho : B_0 -> GL(W)` is a partial model, because `sigma` is a partial
  homomorphism and left multiplication is a homomorphism `F_2[G_0]^x -> GL(W)`.
  This needs `sigma(B_0) ⊂ G_0`. By Step 0, `sigma(B_*) ⊂ G_0`. Write
  `b = b_1 b_2 b_3` with `b_i in B_*`. Since `1 in B_*`, the product
  `b_1 b_2` lies in `B_0`. So `sigma(b) = sigma(b_1) sigma(b_2) sigma(b_3)`
  lies in `G_0`.
- Each `W_k` is stable under every `rho(x)`, because `a^k` is a left ideal.
- **Degree at least 1.** For `n in N_0`, `sigma(n) - 1 in I*`, and so
  `(sigma(n) - 1) a^k ⊂ I* (I*)^k A = a^(k+1)`. That is,
  `(rho(n) - 1) W_k ⊂ W_(k+1)`.

## Step 2. The trap

By the imported Theorem with `d = 1`, `rho(h) - 1` has degree at least 2.
Apply it to the class of `1 in W_0`:

```text
sigma(h) - 1 in a^2 = (I*)^2 F_2[G_0].                                  (P1)
```

## Step 3. Coset lemma

Choose a right transversal `T` of `U*` in `G_0` with `1 in T`. Then
`A = ⊕_(t in T) F_2[U*] t` as left `F_2[U*]`-modules. Since `(I*)^2` is a
left ideal of `F_2[U*]`,

```text
(I*)^2 A = sum_t (I*)^2 F_2[U*] t = ⊕_(t in T) (I*)^2 t.
```

The element `sigma(h) - 1` lies in the summand `F_2[U*] · 1`, because
`sigma(h) in U*`. Comparing components at `t = 1` in (P1) gives

```text
sigma(h) - 1 in (I*)^2      inside F_2[U*].                              (P2)
```

## Step 4. Degree one is the abelianisation mod 2

**Lemma.** Let `U` be a finite group and `I` the augmentation ideal of
`F_2[U]`. If `u in U` and `u - 1 in I^2`, then `u in [U,U] U^2`.

*Proof.*
- Put `Ubar = U / [U,U] U^2`, an elementary abelian 2-group of finite rank
  `r`, with basis `g_1, ..., g_r`.
- The quotient map induces an algebra map `F_2[U] -> F_2[Ubar]` that sends
  `I` onto the augmentation ideal `Ibar`, and so `I^2` into `Ibar^2`.
- Put `x_i = g_i - 1`. Then `x_i^2 = g_i^2 - 1 = 0`, and a dimension count
  (`2^r` on both sides) gives `F_2[Ubar] = F_2[x_1, ..., x_r] / (x_i^2)`.
- In this ring, `Ibar = (x_1, ..., x_r)`, and `Ibar^2` is spanned by the
  squarefree monomials of degree at least 2.
- Write `ubar = prod_(i in S) g_i`. Then
  `ubar - 1 = prod_(i in S)(1 + x_i) - 1 = sum_(i in S) x_i + (degree >= 2)`.
- This lies in `Ibar^2` only if `S` is empty, that is, only if `ubar = 1`.

Applying the Lemma to `U = U*` (finite, as a subgroup of `G_0`) and (P2)
gives (EC1): `sigma(h) in [U*, U*] (U*)^2`.

## Step 5. Characteristic 2

Suppose `2 = 0` in `R`. For `n = n(Y) in N_0`, `n n = n(2Y) = 1`, and
`n, n, 1 in B_0`. So `sigma(n)^2 = sigma(1) = 1`, and every generator
`lambda sigma(n) lambda^(-1)` of `U*` is trivial or an involution. Then
`U*/[U*,U*]` is generated by elements of order at most 2, so it is
elementary abelian, and `(U*)^2 ⊂ [U*,U*]`. With (EC1) this gives (EC2). For
`R = J`, `q = Q` and `h = x_13(Q)`.

## Step 6. Corollaries

**Corollary 1.**
- *Pairwise commutation.* Assume
  `[lambda sigma(n) lambda^(-1), sigma(n')] = 1` for all `lambda` and `n, n'`.
  Given two generators `lambda sigma(n) lambda^(-1)` and
  `lambda' sigma(n') lambda'^(-1)`, conjugate by `lambda'^(-1)` and use
  `lambda'^(-1) lambda in Lambda`. The two generators commute, so `U*` is
  abelian, and (EC2) gives `sigma(x_13(Q)) = 1`.
- *Abelian subgroup normalised by the Levi.* Suppose `sigma(N_0)` lies in an
  abelian subgroup `A'` normalised by `sigma(L_0)`. Then `A' ∩ G_0` is
  normalised by `Lambda`, by finiteness as in Step 0. So `U* ⊂ A'` is
  abelian.
- *Split models `A x| F_L`.* `sigma(N_0) ⊂ A`, which is normal.
- *Exact homomorphisms.* Let `phi : P -> F` be a homomorphism to a finite
  group, and restrict it to `B_0`. Every `lambda in Lambda` has the form
  `phi(w)` with `w in <L_0>`. So
  `lambda phi(n) lambda^(-1) = phi(w n w^(-1)) in phi(n(M_2(J)))`, which is
  abelian. Hence `phi(x_13(Q)) = 1` for every such `phi`.

**Corollary 2.** For `A = F_2^r`, `F_2[A] = F_2[x_1..x_r]/(x_i^2)` (Step 4).
Its radical filtration is by monomial degree, so its Loewy length is `r + 1`.
The regular module of `A x| F_L` restricts to `F_L`-conjugates of this, and
Corollary 1 still kills the head.

**Corollary 3.** Suppose the word `w` and the elements `n, n'` are such that
every partial product used below lies in the ball on which `sigma` is
multiplicative. Then:
- `sigma(w) sigma(n) sigma(w)^(-1) = sigma(w n w^(-1))`;
- `w n w^(-1)` and `n'` commute in `n(M_2(J))`;
- so `[sigma(w n w^(-1)), sigma(n')] = sigma([w n w^(-1), n']) = sigma(1) = 1`.

Injectivity is not used. So the noncommuting pairs required by (EC2) in a
head-keeping model involve `lambda in Lambda` that are not realised by such
in-ball words.

**Corollary 4.** Steps 0–5 use the trap only through its `d = 1` conclusion
for the root in question. Step 7 of
`jacobson-double-parabolic-two-step-trap-proof` supplies that conclusion for
every root configuration listed there. The regular-module argument then
applies word for word, with `L_0`, `N_0` and `U*` replaced by the
corresponding sets.
