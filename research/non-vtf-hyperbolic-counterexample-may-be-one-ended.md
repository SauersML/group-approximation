**Theorem.** Let `G` be the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with finite
edge groups. If every vertex group is virtually torsion-free, so is `G`.

**Corollary.** If a word-hyperbolic group `G` is not virtually torsion-free, then some
vertex group of its Dunwoody decomposition over finite subgroups is a one-ended
word-hyperbolic group that is not virtually torsion-free. So Lück 11.1(i)
(`hyperbolic-groups-virtually-torsion-free`) holds for all hyperbolic groups iff it
holds for one-ended hyperbolic groups.

The same argument with residual finiteness in place of virtual torsion-freeness is
the classical fact that such graphs of groups with residually finite vertex groups are
residually finite. No novelty is claimed.

**Calibration.**
- Finite edge groups are essential. The amalgam `Γ *_{h=t} (Z/p x| Z)` of artifact §5
  of `research/artifacts/hyperbolic-vtf-chern-quillen-persistence-2026-09-12.md` has
  virtually torsion-free vertex groups, a torsion-free simple lattice and a virtually
  cyclic group, but an infinite cyclic edge group, and it is not virtually
  torsion-free.
- One-endedness alone forces nothing: `Z/p x (surface group)` is one-ended and
  virtually torsion-free.
