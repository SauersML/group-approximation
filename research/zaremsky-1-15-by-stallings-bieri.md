For `n ≥ 2` put `G_n = SB_{n+1}`. Since `n + 1 ≥ 3`, the required claim says
three things about `G_n`:

- it has quadratic Dehn function;
- it is of type `F_n`, hence of type `F_2` and finitely presented;
- it is not of type `F_{n+1}`.

These are exactly the groups Problem 1.15 asks for. The claim also answers the
parenthetical question for every Stallings--Bieri group that has a Dehn
function.
