Notation is that of the target. For a family `E` and a pattern `α` on `B_D`, put
`P_α(w) = ∏_(f ∈ B_D) E_(α(f))(wf)`. By (Q2) its factors commute when the family is `D'`-quantum with `D' ≥ D`.

- **Monotonicity** (`fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity-proof`, Section 4): a `D'`-quantum
  family is a `D`-quantum family for every `D ≤ D'`.
- **Expansion.** If `D ≤ D'`, then `P_α(w) = Σ_β P_β(w)` over the patterns `β` on `B_(D')` with `β|_(B_D) = α`.
  Insert `I = Σ_c E_c(wf)` for `f ∈ B_(D') ∖ B_D` and use commutation. The terms with `β ∉ L_(D')(Y)` vanish.

## 1. The forcing lemma

**Lemma F.** Let `x` be locally forced with radius `ρ`, isolated by `π` at `g`. Put
`P_x = ∏_(f ∈ B_ρ) E_(π(f))(gf)`. For every `D'`-quantum family on `Y` with `D' ≥ ρ`, and all `u ∈ Λ`, `c ∈ A`:

    E_c(u) P_x = P_x E_c(u) = [x(u) = c] P_x.

*Proof.* Induction along `F_0 ⊆ F_1 ⊆ …`. The claim for `u ∈ F_j` is that the displayed identity holds.
- **Base, `u = gf ∈ gB_ρ`.** All factors of `P_x` and `E_c(u)` lie in `gB_ρ`, of diameter `≤ 2ρ ≤ 2D'`, so they
  commute. The factor `E_(π(f))(u)` absorbs `E_(π(f))(u)`. For `c ≠ π(f)`, `E_c(u) E_(π(f))(u) = 0` by (Q1).
- **Step.** Let `h ∈ F_(j+1)` be witnessed by the ball `kB_ρ`. Put `J = kB_ρ ∩ F_j`, `c = x(h)`, and
  `Q = ∏_(v ∈ J) E_(x(v))(v)`.
  - By the induction hypothesis, applied factor by factor, `P_x Q = P_x = Q P_x`.
  - Expanding over the sites of `kB_ρ ∖ J` writes `Q` as a sum of products `T_β = ∏_(f ∈ B_ρ) E_(β(f))(kf)`,
    over `β` agreeing with `x` on `J`.
  - By (Q3) at scale `ρ` (monotonicity), only `β ∈ L_ρ(Y)` survive. By the forcing condition, each of them has
    `β(k^(-1) h) = c`.
  - So `E_c(h)` is a factor of each surviving `T_β`, and commutes with it: `E_c(h) Q = Q = Q E_c(h)`.
  - Hence `E_c(h) P_x = E_c(h) Q P_x = Q P_x = P_x`, and `P_x E_c(h) = P_x Q E_c(h) = P_x Q = P_x`.
  - For `c' ≠ c`, `E_(c')(h) P_x = E_(c')(h) E_c(h) P_x = 0`, and likewise on the right.

The `F_j` exhaust `Λ`. `∎`

**Lemma A (atoms).** Under Lemma F, the following hold for isolated points `x ≠ x'`.
- **Well-defined.** `P_x` does not depend on the isolating pattern chosen. Two choices `P`, `P'` are central
  idempotents in the algebra generated by the family. Lemma F gives `P P' = P` and `P' P = P'`, since `x` has both
  patterns, so `P = P'`.
- **Orthogonal.** `P_x P_(x') = 0 = P_(x') P_x`.
  - Lemma F gives `P_x P_(x') = ∏_f [x(g'f) = π'(f)] P_x`.
  - If every bracket were `1`, then `x` would carry the isolating pattern of `x'` at `g'`, so `x = x'`.
- **Diagonal.** On `P_x W_0`, every `E_c(u)` acts as the scalar `[x(u) = c]`. `∎`

## 2. Finiteness near a pattern

**Lemma P.** Let `α ∈ L_D(Y) ∖ L_D(Y')`. Then `U_α = {x ∈ U : x|_(B_D) = α}` is finite.

*Proof.* Suppose it contains infinitely many distinct points. They have an accumulation point `x_∞ ∈ Y`, and
`x_∞|_(B_D) = α`. A point of `U` is isolated, so it is not an accumulation point of other points. So `x_∞ ∈ Y'`, and
`α ∈ L_D(Y')`, a contradiction. `∎`

**Constants.** Fix `D`. For each `x` in the finite set `R_D = ⋃_α U_α` (`α ∈ L_D(Y) ∖ L_D(Y')`), fix an isolating
pattern at `g_x` with forcing radius `ρ_x`. Put `c_D = max_(x ∈ R_D) (|g_x| + ρ_x)` and `D_1 = max(D, c_D)`.

## 3. Proof of the theorem

**Part 1.** A `D`-quantum family for `Y'` is one for `Y`, since `L_D(Y') ⊆ L_D(Y)`. So rigidity of `Y` gives
rigidity of `Y'`. No forcing is used.

**Part 2.** Let `D' ≥ D_1`, let `E` be a `D'`-quantum family for `Y` on `W_0`, and fix `q, q' ∈ Λ`, `a, b ∈ A`.

*Step 1: a finite certificate.*
- Let `𝔄` be the free `k`-algebra on symbols `E_c(u)` modulo the two-sided ideal `I` generated by the relators
  (Q1)–(Q3) of `Y'` at scale `D`.
- Left multiplication makes `𝔄` a `D`-quantum family for `Y'` on `W_0 = 𝔄`. By rigidity, the commutator acts as
  zero on `1`, so `[E_a(q), E_b(q')] ∈ I`.
- So `[E_a(q), E_b(q')] = Σ_i x_i r_i y_i` in the free algebra, with finitely many relators `r_i`.
- Let `Φ` be the finite set of centres `w` of the (Q3)-relators `P_α(w)` among the `r_i`.

*Step 2: the relevant isolated points.*
- Put `Z = ⋃_(w ∈ Φ) ⋃_α w.U_α`, over `α ∈ L_D(Y) ∖ L_D(Y')`. It is finite by Lemma P.
- Its points are translates of points of `R_D`, and forcing radii are translation invariant. So Lemma F applies to
  each `x ∈ Z`, because `D' ≥ c_D`.
- Put `S = Σ_(x ∈ Z) P_x`. By Lemma A, `S` is an idempotent commuting with every `E_c(u)`, and `P_x S = P_x` for
  `x ∈ Z`.
- So `W_0 = S W_0 ⊕ (1 − S) W_0`, and both summands are invariant under every `E_c(u)`.

*Step 3: the commutator vanishes on `S W_0`.* `S W_0 = ⊕_(x ∈ Z) P_x W_0`, and on each summand every `E_c(u)` is a
scalar (Lemma A).

*Step 4: the relators hold on `(1 − S) W_0`.*
- **(Q1) and (Q2) at scale `D`.** They hold on `W_0`, since `D ≤ D'`.
- **(Q3) with `α ∉ L_D(Y)`.** It holds on `W_0`, by monotonicity.
- **(Q3) with `α ∈ L_D(Y) ∖ L_D(Y')` at `w ∈ Φ`.** Expand `P_α(w) = Σ_β P_β(w)` over `β ∈ L_(D')(Y)` extending
  `α`, and fix such a `β`.
  1. Some `x̂ ∈ Y` has `x̂(wf) = β(f)` on `B_(D')`. Then `w^(-1).x̂` has `α` on `B_D`, so `w^(-1).x̂ ∈ U_α`, and
     `x̂ ∈ w.U_α ⊆ Z`.
  2. Write `x̂ = w.x` with `x ∈ R_D`. Then `x̂` carries the isolating pattern of `x` at `wg_x`. Its support
     `wg_x B_(ρ_x)` lies in `wB_(D')`, since `|g_x| + ρ_x ≤ c_D ≤ D'`.
  3. So `β` contains that pattern. The factors of `P_(x̂)` are among those of `P_β(w)`, and they commute in the
     window, so `P_β(w) = P_β(w) P_(x̂)`.
  4. Hence `P_β(w)(1 − S) = P_β(w)(P_(x̂) − P_(x̂) S) = 0`.

  So `P_α(w)` vanishes on `(1 − S) W_0`.

  Every `r_i` therefore vanishes on the invariant subspace `(1 − S) W_0`. So `Σ_i x_i r_i y_i` does too.

*Conclusion.* `[E_a(q), E_b(q')] = 0` on `W_0`. Since `q, q', a, b` were arbitrary, `Y` is `D'`-quantum rigid
over `k`. `∎`

## 4. Corollaries A and B

- **A.** Under (C2), an isolated point `x` has a neighbourhood `{x}`, which meets the dense orbit, so
  `x ∈ Λ.y_*`. So `U = Λ.y_*` and `Y' = ∂Y`. Every point of `U` is a translate of `y_*`, hence locally forced.
- **B.** Apply the theorem to `Y^(j)` for `j = n − 1, …, 0`. The scale grows at each level through `D_1`. There
  are finitely many levels, so this is a finite composition.

## 5. Corollary C: SFTs over `Z`

Let `Y` be defined by forbidden words of length `≤ m`, and let `x` be isolated by `π` on `[−R, R]`, with `R ≥ m`.
Put `ρ = R`. We force `h > R` in increasing order (the case `h < −R` is symmetric) with the window `[h − ρ, h + ρ]`.
The forced part contains `[h − m, h − 1]`.
- **Setup.** Let `β ∈ L_ρ(Y)` agree with `x` there, and let `z ∈ Y` carry `β` on the window.
- **Glue.** Take `x` on `(−∞, h − 1]` and `z` on `[h − m, ∞)`. They agree on the overlap `[h − m, h − 1]`.
- **The glued point lies in `Y`.** Every interval of length `≤ m` meeting `[h, ∞)` starts at `≥ h − m + 1`. So it
  lies where the glued point is `z`, and every other interval lies where it is `x`.
- **Conclusion.** The glued point carries `π` on `[−R, R] ⊆ (−∞, h − 1]`, so it equals `x`. Hence the value of
  `β` at the site `h` is `z(h) = x(h)`. `∎`

Over `Z^2`, a finite window does not separate the plane, and this gluing is unavailable. That is why local forcing
is a hypothesis in general.
