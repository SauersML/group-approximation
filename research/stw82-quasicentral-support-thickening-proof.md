## Step 1: all multiplier cross terms lie in the generated ideal

For `a in A`,

```text
(pa)^*(pa)=a^*pa in L.
```

An ideal contains an element whenever it contains its squared modulus, so
`pa in L`.  Applying the same observation to `a^*` and taking adjoints gives
`ap in L`.  Hence

```text
pA+Ap subset L,          [p,A] subset L.                 (QSP1)
```

In particular `J=L+C p` is the ideal generated by `p` in `K=A+C p`, and
`K/J=A/L`.

## Step 2: repair the approximate-unit projections to commute with p

Define the block diagonal part of `e_n` by

```text
d_n=pe_np+(1-p)e_n(1-p) in L.                            (QSP2)
```

It is a positive contraction commuting with `p`.  Its difference from the
projection `e_n` consists exactly of the two off-diagonal `p`-corners, so

```text
norm(d_n-e_n)->0                                         (QSP3)
```

by `(QST1)`.  Thus `norm(d_n^2-d_n)->0`.  For all sufficiently large `n`,
the spectrum of `d_n` avoids `1/2`; continuous functional calculus on that
spectrum gives

```text
r_n=chi_[1/2,1](d_n) in C*(d_n) subset L.                (QSP4)
```

The cutoff vanishes at zero, which is why `r_n` belongs to `L` rather than
only its unitization.  Moreover

```text
r_n p=p r_n,             norm(r_n-e_n)->0.               (QSP5)
```

Consequently `(r_n)` is still an approximate unit of projections for `L`
and is quasicentral in `A`.

## Step 3: thicken the repaired cuts to the larger ideal

Set `q_n` as in `(QST3)`.  Since `r_n` commutes with `p`, this is the sum of
the orthogonal projections `p` and `(1-p)r_n`, so `q_n` is a projection in
`J`.  It fixes `p`, and for `l in L`,

```text
q_n l=pl+(1-p)r_nl -> pl+(1-p)l=l,
l q_n=lp+lr_n(1-p) -> lp+l(1-p)=l.                      (QSP6)
```

Thus `(q_n)` is an approximate unit for `J`.

It remains to check quasicentrality, where the cancellation created by
adjoining `p` is essential.  For `a in A`, direct expansion gives

```text
[q_n,a]=(1-p)[r_n,a]+[p,a](1-r_n).                       (QSP7)
```

The first term tends to zero because `(r_n)` is quasicentral in `A`; the
second tends to zero because `[p,a] in L` by `(QSP1)` and `(r_n)` is an
approximate unit for `L`.  Also `[q_n,p]=0`.  Therefore `(q_n)` is
quasicentral in all of `K=A+C p`.

## Step 4: apply quasidiagonal extension permanence

Equation `(QSP6)--(QSP7)` says exactly that `(QST4)` is a quasidiagonal
extension.  The nuclear-dimension maximum theorem for separable
quasidiagonal extensions gives

```text
dim_nuc(K)=max(dim_nuc(J),dim_nuc(A/L)).                 (QSP8)
```

The approximate unit `(e_n)` makes `L` sigma-unital, while
`L=closure(LpL)` follows by expanding `ApA` and using `(QSP1)`.  Thus `p` is
full as a multiplier of `L`, and the full-support part of
`stw82-split-af-is-one-multiplier-projection-problem` gives

```text
dim_nuc(J)=dim_nuc(L).                                   (QSP9)
```

Ideal and quotient monotonicity give

```text
dim_nuc(L)<=dim_nuc(A),       dim_nuc(A/L)<=dim_nuc(A).
```

Equations `(QSP8)--(QSP9)` imply `dim_nuc(K)<=dim_nuc(A)`.  Since `A` is an
ideal in `K`, the reverse inequality is automatic, proving `(QST2)`.

## Step 5: audit the proper essential example

Represent `B tensor K(H)` on `H tensor H`.  It contains
`K(H tensor H)` as an essential proper ideal.  Tensoring with `C([0,1])`
preserves this fibrewise essentiality, so the displayed `L` is proper and
essential in `A`; a proper essential ideal cannot be complemented.

The projections `P_n tensor Q_n tensor 1` approximate `L`.  They commute
with `p` because `e_11<=P_n`.  They are quasicentral on the dense algebraic
span: commutators with compact--compact tensors vanish by the approximate-
unit property, and

```text
[P_n tensor Q_n,1 tensor k]=P_n tensor [Q_n,k] ->0
```

for every compact `k`.  Finally, `A/L` is
`K(H) tensor C([0,1])`, which has nuclear dimension one, while the tensor
product of the AF algebra `B tensor K(H)` with `C([0,1])` has nuclear
dimension at most one.  Hence `dim_nuc(A)=1`, as asserted.
