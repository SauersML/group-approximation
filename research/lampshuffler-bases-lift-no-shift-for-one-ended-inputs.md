**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, proved below; not independently reviewed; no priority claimed).

**Setting.** `P`, `nu`, `n >= 1`, `X = [n] x N` and its `2n` strands are as in
`strand-map-bases-lift-only-bounded-displacement-letters`. Identify each strand with `P` through `nu`.
- `Γ = H_n(E_nu(P))` contains, for every strand `j`, the copy `lambda^(j)(P)` of the left regular action on strand
  `j` (fixing the other strands). On even strands these are the `rho^(k)`. On odd strands they are their flip
  conjugates, which lie in `E_nu(P)^n <= Γ` (Observation 5.1).
- The *lampshuffler base* is `K' = FSym(X) · ∏_j lambda^(j)(P) ≅ FSym(X) ⋊ P^(2n)`. Let `psi_j : K' -> P` be the
  `j`-th coordinate.
- Every `γ ∈ Γ` permutes the strands far out. On strand `j` it is an injective near map
  `mu_(γ,j) : strand j -> strand π_γ(j)` with cofinite image. Let `eta_j(γ)` be its near index. The elements with
  all `eta_j = 0` form a proper subgroup `Γ^0`, which contains `K'` but not the slide.

**Lemma.** Let `P` be one-ended, `A <= P` of finite index generated by a finite set `S_A`, and `mu : P \ M_1 -> P \ M_2`
a bijection with `M_1, M_2` finite. Suppose there is `phi : S_A -> P` such that, for each `a ∈ S_A`,
`mu(a y) = phi(a) mu(y)` for all but finitely many `y`. Then `|M_1| = |M_2|`.

**Statement.** Let `P` be one-ended. If `y ∈ Γ` and a finite `C ⊂ K'` satisfy `y C y^-1 ⊂ K'`, and `<C>` has
finitely many orbits on 2-element subsets of `X`, then `y ∈ Γ^0`. So any certificate for `Γ` in
`fsym-extensions-lift-fp-via-pair-transitive-stable-subgroups` with base `K'` and every `C_i ⊂ K'` generates at most
`Γ^0 ≠ Γ`. In other words, no such certificate exists.

**Proof of the lemma.**
1. As in step 1 of `one-ended-shell-shift-normalization-proof`, `phi` extends to an injective homomorphism
   `A -> P`. A word `w` with `w = 1` in `A` gives `lambda_(phi(w)) = mu lambda_w mu^-1 = 1` near infinity, and a
   nontrivial `lambda_h` moves every point.
2. The graph on `P` with edges `{y, a y}`, `a ∈ S_A`, has the cosets `A p_1, ..., A p_k` as components, each a copy
   of `Cay(A, S_A)`. `A` is one-ended, being of finite index in `P`. Remove the finite set `B` consisting of the
   endpoints of the edges where the identity fails, together with `M_1`. Then each `A p_i \ B` has exactly one
   infinite component `E_i`, and `E = ∪ E_i` is cofinite.
3. On `E_i`, `phi(b)^-1 mu(b p_i)` is constant along edges, hence equal to some `c_i`. So `mu = theta` on `E`,
   where `theta(b p_i) = phi(b) c_i`. It maps `A p_i` bijectively onto the coset `phi(A) c_i`.
4. For `i ≠ i'`, the cosets `phi(A) c_i` and `phi(A) c_i'` are disjoint. Otherwise they are equal, and then
   `mu(E_i)` and `mu(E_i')` are cofinite in the same infinite set, which contradicts injectivity. The image of
   `mu` is cofinite and lies in `∪ phi(A) c_i` together with a finite set. A complement of a union of cosets is a
   union of cosets, hence empty or infinite. So `theta` is a bijection of `P`.
5. `mu` maps `E` onto `theta(E)`, and `M_2` is disjoint from `theta(E) = mu(E)`. So `mu` maps `(P \ E) \ M_1`
   bijectively onto `(P \ theta(E)) \ M_2`. Both `P \ E` and `P \ theta(E)` have `|P \ E|` elements. Hence
   `|M_1| = |M_2|`. ∎

**Proof of the statement.**
1. *The coordinates of `<C>` have finite index.* Fix a strand `j` and put `A = psi_j(<C>)`. Outside a finite set
   `F` of strand `j`, each generator `c ∈ C` acts as `lambda_(psi_j(c))`. So an orbit of `<C>` that avoids `F` lies
   in a single coset `A x`. The finitely many orbits through `F` meet only finitely many cosets, since every edge
   leaving a coset starts in `F`. If `[P : A] = ∞`, then `<C>` has infinitely many orbits on points, and hence on
   pairs. So `[P : A] < ∞`.
2. *The near relation.* Let `j' = π_y(j)` and `mu = mu_(y,j)`. For `c ∈ C`, on far points of strand `j'`,
   `y c y^-1 = mu lambda_(psi_j(c)) mu^-1`. It lies in `K'`, so it agrees near infinity with
   `lambda_(psi_j'(y c y^-1))`. So `mu lambda_a = lambda_(phi(a)) mu` near infinity for `a` in the finite
   generating set `psi_j(C)` of `A`.
3. By the lemma, `mu` has index `0`. This holds for every `j`, so `y ∈ Γ^0`. Finally, `Γ^0` is a subgroup:
   composites of index-zero near maps have index zero. The slide has index `±1` on the even strand of its ray, so
   `Γ^0 ≠ Γ`. ∎

## Reading

- The two natural bases fail in opposite ways.
  - The strand-map base `H_n(E_2)` sees only bounded displacement, hence only virtually cyclic inputs
    (`strand-map-bases-lift-only-bounded-displacement-letters`).
  - The lampshuffler base sees the regular action fully, but no letter with nonzero index can be certified over it
    for one-ended inputs.
- Not treated here:
  - inputs with more than one end: the lemma can fail there (the zigzag enumeration of `Z`, with `mu = s`, fails
    it for two ends), and infinitely-ended inputs are not checked;
  - whether lampshuffler bases are finitely presented at all;
  - certificates in which a later `C_i` uses earlier letters outside `K'`.

  A finitely presented `H_n(E_nu(P))` for one-ended `P` must come, along this criterion, through a base that is
  neither strand maps nor regular copies, or through such iterated certificates.
