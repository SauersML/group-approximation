**Inputs.**

- **Kharlampovich--Myasnikov--Sapir**, arXiv:1204.6506v5, read from the TeX
  source this session (§4.1).
  - The generating sets `L_0, L_1, L_2`.
  - Relations G1--G8.
  - Lemma `lBRG` (Baumslag--Remeslennikov). If `(1)` `<F ∪ F'>` is abelian,
    `(2)` `x^{f(a)} = x^{a'}`, and `(3)`
    `[x_1^{a_1^{α_1}…a_m^{α_m}}, x_2] = 1` for `α_i ∈ {0, 1, −1}`, then "the
    normal subgroup generated by $X$ in the group $H=\la X\cup F\cup F'\ra$
    is Abelian, and $H$ is metabelian".
  - Lemma `lH`: "by relations G2,
    $\la M_i,i=0,...,K\ra=\prod_{i=1}^K\la a_i,a_i',A_i\ra\times \la\tilde a_i, \tilde a_i', A_0, i=1,\ldots,K\ra$".
  - Relation G4, verbatim: "The elements of the set
    $\{(\tilde a_i')^{-1}, i=1,...,K\}$ are a BR-conjoints to elements of the
    set $\{\tilde a_i^{-1},i=1,...,K\}$ with respect to $\{A_0\}$", with
    `f(t) = t − 1`. G3 is the same statement for `(a_i')^{-1}`, `a_i^{-1}` and
    `{A_i}`.
- **Kochloukova--Sidki**, arXiv:1710.04745 (Geom. Dedicata 204 (2020)
  241--264), read from the PDF this session.
  - Theorem D, verbatim: "Let p be a prime number,
    g ∈ Fp[x] \ (∪j≥0 Fp x^j ∪ (x − 1)Fp[x]) and G̃ be the group Ã ⋊ Q̃, where
    Ã = Fp[x1^±1, 1/g(x1)] ⊗Fp … ⊗Fp Fp[xd^±1, 1/g(xd)] and
    Q̃ = Z^2d = ⟨x1, …, xd, y1, …, yd⟩ and yi and xi act on Ã (via conjugation)
    as multiplication by g(xi) and xi respectively. Then G̃ is a transitive
    self-similar group."
  - Lemma 7.2: "f̃ is a simple, virtual endomorphism". Here
    `f̃ : H̃ = A_0 S^{-1} ⋊ Q̃_0 → G̃`, where
    - `A_0` is the augmentation ideal of `F_p[x_1^{±1}, …, x_d^{±1}]`;
    - `S` is generated by the `g(x_i)`;
    - `Q̃_0 = <x_1^p, x_2, …, x_d> × <y_1^p, y_2, …, y_d>`;
    - `f̃(x_1^p) = x_2`, …, `f̃(x_d) = x_1`, and likewise for the `y_i`.
  - Lemma 7.4: "With the appropriate choice of polynomial g the group G̃ is
    finitely presented. Proof. It suffices to choose g an irreducible
    polynomial coprime to x such that g is not divisible by x − 1."
- **Kochloukova--Luiz**, arXiv:2509.05798, read from the PDF this session.
  - The Main Theorem, conditions 1)--4).
  - The remark "the condition that A is Z-torsion-free ... is important, as in
    [18] were constructed examples of G transitive self-similar with A of Krull
    dimension bigger than 1 but A is of finite exponent", where [18] is
    Kochloukova--Sidki.
  - Section 8, Example 2: `A = Z[x^{±1}, 1/(x+1)]`, `Q = <q_1, q_2>`, with
    `q_1` acting by `x` and `q_2` by `x + 1`, "is not self-similar".

## Proof

**Step 1: the top is a retract with the obvious presentation.**
- Let `Q^abs` be the group presented on `L_1 ∪ L_2` by:
  - the `H_1` and `H_2` clauses of G1;
  - G2, G3 and G4.

  Here "BR-conjoint" includes all three conditions of Lemma `lBRG`.
- Every relator of G5--G8, and the `H_0` clause of G1, lies in the normal
  closure `⟨⟨L_0⟩⟩`, because both sides of each such equation are words in
  conjugates of `x`-letters.
  - G5 a) `[x_u, A_i] = x_{uA_i}`.
  - G5 b) `x_u^{a_i − 1} = x_u^{a_i'}`.
  - G5 c), G6 and G7 are equalities or commutators of conjugates of `x_u`.
  - G8 equates two `*`-expressions in `x_{q_jA_0}`.
- Hence `G(M)/⟨⟨L_0⟩⟩ ≅ Q^abs`.
- The composite `Q^abs → G(M) → G(M)/⟨⟨L_0⟩⟩ ≅ Q^abs` is the identity on
  generators. So `Q^abs → Q = <L_1 ∪ L_2>` is an isomorphism and `Q` is a
  retract of `G(M)`.
- The generating set splits into the blocks `M_0, …, M_K`. G2 makes different
  blocks commute. The G1 clauses restricted to a block, together with G3 or G4,
  involve only that block. So `Q^abs = ∏_{i=1}^K B_i × B_0`, where each factor
  is presented on its block by its own relations. This is Lemma `lH`, now at
  the level of presentations.

**Step 2: `B_0 ≅ (F_p[Λ]/I_0) ⋊ Λ`.**
- Put `Λ = Z^{2K}`, free abelian on `σ_i, τ_i`.
- `B_0` is presented on `ã_i, ã_i', A_0` by:
  - `A_0^p = 1`;
  - `<ã, ã'>` is abelian;
  - G4, that is,
    - `A_0^{ã_i^{-1} − 1} = A_0^{(ã_i')^{-1}}`;
    - `[A_0^{z}, A_0] = 1` for `z = ∏ ã_i^{−α_i}` with `α_i ∈ {0, ±1}`.
- By Lemma `lBRG`, the normal closure `N` of `A_0` is abelian. It has
  exponent `p`, since it is generated by conjugates of `A_0`.
- Killing `A_0` leaves the presentation of a free abelian group, so
  `B_0/N ≅ Λ` via `ã_i ↦ σ_i`, `ã_i' ↦ τ_i`. Since `Λ` is free abelian and the
  `ã`-letters commute in `B_0`, the quotient map splits. So
  `B_0 = N ⋊ Λ`, and `N = F_p[Λ]/I` is cyclic on `A_0`, written additively as
  a right module.
- The relation G4 puts `ρ_i = σ_i^{-1} − 1 − τ_i^{-1}` in `I`. Let
  `I_0 = (ρ_1, …, ρ_K)`. The semidirect product `(F_p[Λ]/I_0) ⋊ Λ`, with `A_0`
  sent to the class of `1` and the `ã`-letters sent to `Λ`, satisfies every
  defining relation of `B_0`:
  - the module has exponent `p` and is abelian, so the commutator clause
    holds;
  - the G4 equation holds because `ρ_i ∈ I_0`.
- So there is a surjection `B_0 → (F_p[Λ]/I_0) ⋊ Λ` that restricts to a
  surjection `F_p[Λ]/I → F_p[Λ]/I_0`. Hence `I ⊆ I_0`, and so `I = I_0`.

**Step 3: the Möbius change of variable.**
- Put `X_i = τ_i^{-1}` and `Y_i = σ_i^{-1}`. Then `ρ_i = Y_i − 1 − X_i`.
- `F_p[Λ] = ⊗_i F_p[X_i^{±1}, Y_i^{±1}]`, and `I_0` is generated by one element
  in each tensor factor. So
  `F_p[Λ]/I_0 = ⊗_i F_p[X_i^{±1}][Y_i^{±1}]/(Y_i − (1 + X_i)) = ⊗_i F_p[X_i^{±1}, (1 + X_i)^{-1}]`.
- On this ring, `X_i` acts as multiplication by `X_i`, and `Y_i` as
  multiplication by `1 + X_i`.
- This is verbatim the group `KS_K(1 + x)` of Theorem D, with `x_i = X_i` and
  `y_i = Y_i`. Both are `Ã_K ⋊ Z^{2K}` with the same action. So
  `B_0 ≅ KS_K(1 + x)`.
- The same computation with G3 and `K = 1` gives `B_i ≅ KS_1(1 + x)`.
- In the coordinates `s_i = σ_i` of
  `two-counter-kms-top-has-no-affine-self-similar-action`:
  - `x_i = s_i^{-1} − 1`;
  - `s_i = (1 + x_i)^{-1}`;
  - `1 − s_i = x_i/(1 + x_i)`;
  - `ã_i' = τ_i` acts as `s_i/(1 − s_i) = x_i^{-1}`.

  These rational identities were checked with sympy this session. So `R_K`
  and `U_K = <s_i, 1 − s_i>` correspond to `Ã_K` and `<x_i, 1 + x_i>`.

**Step 4: the hypotheses of Theorem D and Lemma 7.4.**
- Take `g = 1 + x`. It has two terms, so `g ∉ ∪_j F_p x^j`.
- `g ∈ (x − 1)F_p[x]` iff `g(1) = 2 = 0` iff `p = 2`.
- So for `p` odd, Theorem D and Lemma 7.2 apply: `KS_d(1 + x)` has a simple
  virtual endomorphism `f̃_d` on `H̃_d = A_0S^{-1} ⋊ Q̃_0`.
- Its index is `p^3`:
  - `[Q̃ : Q̃_0] = p^2`;
  - `AS^{-1}/A_0S^{-1} = (A/A_0)S^{-1} = F_p`, because the elements of `S`
    map to the nonzero scalars `g(1)^n`.
- `g = 1 + x` is irreducible, coprime to `x` (`g(0) = 1`), and not divisible by
  `x − 1` when `p` is odd. So Lemma 7.4 applies: `KS_d(1 + x)` is finitely
  presented.

**Step 5: direct products.**
- Let `f_j: H_j → G_j` be simple virtual endomorphisms, for `j = 1, 2`. Then
  `f = f_1 × f_2: H_1 × H_2 → G_1 × G_2` is a virtual endomorphism of index
  `[G_1:H_1][G_2:H_2]`.
- Let `N ≤ H_1 × H_2` be normal in `G_1 × G_2` with `f(N) ≤ N`.
  - Its projection `π_1(N)` lies in `H_1`, is normal in `G_1` (conjugate by
    `(g, 1)`), and satisfies `f_1(π_1 N) = π_1(f N) ≤ π_1 N`.
  - So `π_1(N) = 1` by simplicity of `f_1`. Likewise `π_2(N) = 1`, so `N = 1`.
- So `f` is simple. By the standard correspondence (Kochloukova--Sidki §2.1),
  `G_1 × G_2` is transitive self-similar.
- Induction gives item 2 of the claim, with index `p^{3(K+1)}`. Direct
  products of finitely presented groups are finitely presented, which gives
  item 3.
- Independently, `Q` is a retract of `G(M)` (Step 1). So it is finitely
  presented whenever `G(M)` is.

**Step 6: the characteristic-p Kochloukova--Luiz analogue.**
- Take `K = 2`, `A = Ã_2 = F_p[x_1^{±1}, x_2^{±1}, (1 + x_1)^{-1}, (1 + x_2)^{-1}]`
  and `Q̃ = Z^4`.
- `A` is a localization of a polynomial ring in two variables, so it is a
  domain of Krull dimension 2.
- `A` is cyclic, generated by `1`, since it is spanned by the monomials
  `x^a (1 + x)^b`, which are the images of `Q̃`.
- A nontrivial `q ∈ Q̃` acts by `∏ x_i^{a_i}(1 + x_i)^{b_i}`. This is a
  nonconstant element of `F_p(x_1, x_2)`, because `x_i` and `1 + x_i` are
  distinct primes. `F_p` is algebraically closed in a purely transcendental
  extension, so `q` is not algebraic over `F_p`.
- `B_0` is finitely presented (Step 4) and self-similar (Step 4).
- So conditions 1, 3 and 4 of the Kochloukova--Luiz Main Theorem hold with
  `Z` replaced by `F_p`, but the conclusion fails. Condition 2 has no
  characteristic-`p` content.
- Reducing their Example 2 modulo `p` gives
  `A/pA ⋊ Q = F_p[x^{±1}, 1/(x+1)] ⋊ <x, x+1> = KS_1(1 + x) ≅ B_i`.

**Step 7: consistency with the affine kill.**
- For `K ≥ 2`, `two-counter-kms-top-has-no-affine-self-similar-action` is
  established. So the realization of Step 5 cannot be affine with the lamps as
  translations.
- Concretely, the transversal of `Q̃_0` in `Q̃` is `{x_1^j y_1^k : 0 ≤ j, k < p}`.
  Multiplying `x_1^{p−1}` by `x_1` lands on `x_1^p ∈ Q̃_0`, whose image is `x_2`.
  This is an odometer carry into the next counter, the nonlinear escape that
  node's Consequence names.

**Step 8: `p = 2`.**
- `Ã_1 = F_p[x^{±1}, 1/g]` is the coordinate ring of `P^1` with `∞`, `0` and the
  roots of `g` removed. `R_1` is `P^1` with three rational points removed.
- A ring isomorphism of this Theorem D shape, matching `<x, g(x)>` with
  `<s, 1 − s>`, therefore needs `g = c(x − β)^n` with `β ∈ F_p \ {0}`.
- Over `F_2` this forces `β = 1`, which Theorem D excludes.
- The identification therefore gives nothing for `p = 2`. Self-similarity of
  the top for `p = 2` is not decided here. ∎
