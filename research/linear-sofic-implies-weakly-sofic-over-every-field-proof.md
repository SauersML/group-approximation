Let `G` be `F`-linear sofic and let `B_1 <= B_2 <= ...` be finite symmetric
sets containing `1` and exhausting `G`.  Choose maps
`theta_k: B_k -> GL_(n_k)(F)` realizing an injective morphism into the rank
ultraproduct.

1. Let `R_k <= F` be the ring generated by all entries of `theta_k(s)`,
   `s in B_k`: a finitely generated `Z`-algebra and a domain.
2. For each `s`, pick a nonzero `a_s x a_s` minor `b_s` of `I - theta_k(s)`
   with `a_s = rk(I - theta_k(s))`.  Put `c = prod_s b_s det(theta_k(s))`,
   which is nonzero.
3. A finitely generated `Z`-algebra is a Jacobson ring, so some maximal ideal
   `m` avoids `c`, and `R_k/m` is a finite field.
4. Reduction mod `m` kills no chosen minor or determinant, so each
   `phi_k(s)` is invertible and `rk(I - phi_k(s)) = rk(I - theta_k(s))`.
   Reduction never raises rank, since every larger minor already vanishes in
   `R_k`, so `rk(phi(st) - phi(s)phi(t)) <= rk(theta(st) - theta(s)theta(t))`.
5. `prod_omega GL_(n_k)(R_k/m_k)/d_omega` is a metric ultraproduct of finite
   groups with bi-invariant metrics, and `prod phi_k` is an injective
   morphism into it.  That is weak soficity in the sense of
   Arzhantseva--Paunescu Definition 8.1.

Source: Arzhantseva--Paunescu arXiv:1212.6780, Theorem 8.2 and Claim 8.3,
written over `C`.  Two repairs: the chosen minor belongs to `I - theta_k(s)`,
and the determinant is included in `c` so the reduced matrices stay
invertible.  Nothing else uses `C`.
