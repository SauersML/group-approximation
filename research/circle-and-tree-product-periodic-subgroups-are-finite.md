## Statement

Let `H` be a finitely generated periodic group (every element has finite order).

(a) If `H` acts faithfully on the circle `S^1` by homeomorphisms, then `H` is
finite. More precisely, its orientation-preserving part is finite cyclic.

(b) If `H` acts on a product `X = T_1 × ... × T_k` of finitely many trees by
automorphisms of `X` (factors may be permuted), and every vertex of `X` has a
finite stabilizer in `H`, then `H` is finite.

## Proof

**(a)** Let `H_+ = H ∩ Homeo_+(S^1)`, of index at most 2; it is finitely generated
and periodic.
- *Margulis's alternative* (G. A. Margulis, "Free subgroups of the homeomorphism
  group of the circle", C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 331 (2000)): a subgroup of
  `Homeo(S^1)` either contains a non-abelian free subgroup or preserves a Borel
  probability measure. A periodic group has no free subgroup, so `H_+` preserves
  some probability measure `μ`.
- For a group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms preserving `μ`, the
  rotation number `ρ : H_+ -> R/Z` is a homomorphism, and `ρ(g) = 0` iff `g` has a
  fixed point (classical; e.g. Ghys, "Groups acting on the circle", §6).
- A finite-order orientation-preserving homeomorphism of `S^1` with a fixed point
  is the identity (lift to a periodic homeomorphism of `R` fixing a point; an
  increasing map of `R` of finite order is the identity).
- So `ker ρ = 1`, `H_+` embeds in `R/Z`, and being finitely generated and periodic
  it is finite cyclic. Hence `H` is finite.

**(b)** Let `H_0 ≤ H` be the finite-index subgroup preserving each factor (the
kernel of the permutation action on the factors); it is finitely generated and
periodic. Replace each `T_i` by its barycentric subdivision `T_i'`, on which
`H_0` acts without inversions.
- Each element of `H_0` has finite order, so it has a bounded orbit on `T_i'`,
  hence fixes a vertex of `T_i'`.
- *Serre* (Trees, §I.6.5, Corollary 3): a group generated by finitely many
  elements `s_j` such that every `s_j` and every product `s_j s_l` has a fixed
  point, has a fixed point. Every product is again of finite order, so `H_0` fixes
  a vertex `x_i` of each `T_i'`, i.e. a vertex or an edge midpoint of `T_i`.
- Let `H_1 ≤ H_0` be the subgroup that fixes an endpoint of each such edge; its
  index is at most `2^k`. Then `H_1` fixes a vertex of `X`, so `H_1` is finite by
  hypothesis, and `H` is finite. `∎`

## Consequences for Boone--Higman hosts

The following finitely presented simple groups contain **no** infinite finitely
generated periodic subgroup. In particular none of them contains an infinite free
Burnside group `B(m,n)`, Grigorchuk's group, or any other infinite f.g. torsion
group.
- By (a): Thompson's `T`, the Higman--Thompson and Brown--Stein circle groups
  `T_{n,r}`, and every finitely presented simple group of piecewise-projective or
  piecewise-analytic circle homeomorphisms.
- By (b): every finitely presented simple lattice in a product of trees acting
  with finite vertex stabilizers (Burger--Mozes, Wise, Rattaggi type groups), and
  more generally every group acting properly on a finite product of trees. This
  includes Kac--Moody lattices over finite fields of rank 2, which are lattices in
  products of two trees.

Together with the dead host classes recorded in
`free-burnside-groups-satisfy-boone-higman` (linear, residually finite,
self-similar, hyperbolic, cubulated), this leaves as candidate hosts for infinite
bounded-exponent groups only the Cantor-set families: `V`, `nV`, Röver--Nekrashevych
groups, twisted Brin--Thompson groups, finite germ extensions of `V`, and
lattices in products of higher-dimensional buildings, for which no statement is made here. See
`some-fp-simple-group-has-an-infinite-bounded-exponent-subgroup`.
