Retain the actual split residual extension

```text
E=R+s(Q),                    Q=F(Z)/J.
```

If `E` has a normalized trace different from the canonical trace, then there
exist an **extreme** normalized trace `sigma`, a finite factor

```text
M=pi_sigma(E)'',
```

and a positive contraction `a in R` such that:

1. `sigma(a)>0`;
2. `pi_sigma|s(Q)` is a faithful normal trace-preserving representation and
   `pi_sigma(s(Q))''` is a copy of the II_1 factor `Q`;
3. the residual ideal is weakly dense:

   ```text
   closure^weak(pi_sigma(R))=M;                         (1)
   ```

4. some nonzero spectral cut `b=(a-epsilon)_+` has finite Cuntz capacity in
   `E`; and
5. the support projection of `pi_sigma(b)` is nonzero in `M` and belongs to
   the weak ideal generated by `pi_sigma(R)`, which is all of `M` by `(1)`.

Equivalently, an ordinary negative answer cannot be supported on a proper
central summand over the canonical factor section.  It must furnish a second
factor completion of the same split C*-extension in which the canonically
tracially invisible residual ideal becomes weakly full.

This is a necessary obstruction, not a construction of the alternate factor
completion and not a statement about nonlinear quasitraces.
