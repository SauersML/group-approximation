Write `(T)`, `(G)`, and `(FG)` for clauses 1, 2, and 3.

`(T) => (G)` is exactly
`nonce-twisted-factor-gives-nonhyperlinear-group`: the countable subgroup of
`T` generated by the cocycle values gives a countable central extension, and
Thom's character-sector criterion makes that extension nonhyperlinear.

For `(G) => (T)`, take a nonhyperlinear countable group `G` and the trivial
multiplier.  The standard characterization recorded in
`thom-central-corner-criterion` says

```text
G hyperlinear  iff  L(G) embeds trace preservingly into R^omega.
```

Consequently `L_1(G)=L(G)` is non-Connes-embeddable, which is `(T)`.

Clearly `(FG) => (G)`.  Conversely, hyperlinearity is local on finitely
generated subgroups.  Indeed, every finite microstate request for `G`
involves finitely many elements and finitely many products, hence lies in one
finitely generated subgroup.  If every finitely generated subgroup were
hyperlinear, that subgroup would answer the request.  Therefore a
nonhyperlinear `G` contains a finitely generated nonhyperlinear subgroup,
proving `(G) => (FG)`.

It remains to justify the stronger final assertion: a bad twist restricts to
a bad twist on some finitely generated subgroup of its *original* projective
quotient.  Enumerate `Gamma` by an increasing sequence of finitely generated
subgroups `Gamma_n` and put

```text
M_n=L_(omega|Gamma_n)(Gamma_n) subseteq
M=L_omega(Gamma).
```

Their union is weakly dense in `M`.  If every `M_n` were Connes embeddable,
then `M` would be Connes embeddable by the local microstate criterion.  More
explicitly, the trace-preserving conditional expectations `E_n:M->M_n`
converge to the identity in `L^2` on every fixed finite tuple.  Choose `n`
so that `E_n` approximates that tuple, take a matrix microstate for the tuple
in `M_n`, and transfer its finitely many moments back across the `L^2`
approximation.  Uniform operator bounds and the usual telescoping estimate
control the transfer.  Hence every finite tuple in `M` would have matrix
microstates, a contradiction.  Some `M_n` is therefore non-CE.

This also pinpoints why constructing a projective orthonormal basis in a
known non-CE game or relation algebra is not preliminary progress: once the
basis is proved, the nonhyperlinear group has already been constructed.
