This is Theorem A of Fournier-Facio--Zaremsky, *Finiteness properties and
Higman's rope trick*, arXiv:2607.21727v1, at `n = ∞`. The paper states
Theorem A for `n`; its proof uses only the case of the universal group. The
derivation is written out here, including the isomorphism the paper uses.

**Input.** `U` is a finitely presented group containing every finitely
presented group (Higman 1961). `ι: U → H` is injective, and `H` is recursively
presented and of type `FP_∞`.

**Step 1 (Higman back-embedding).** `H` is finitely generated, because it is
of type `FP_1`, and it is recursively presented. By Higman's embedding theorem
`H` embeds in a finitely presented group, which embeds in `U`. Call the
composite `ȷ: H → U`. Then `ψ := ȷι: U → U` is injective.

**Step 2 (two descriptions of one group).** Let
`E = ⟨U, t | t u t^{-1} = ψ(u), u ∈ U⟩` and
`E' = ⟨H, s | s h s^{-1} = ιȷ(h), h ∈ H⟩`.
Define `α: E → E'` by `u ↦ ι(u)` and `t ↦ s`. It respects the relations:
`α(t u t^{-1}) = s ι(u) s^{-1} = ιȷι(u) = α(ψ(u))`.
Define `β: E' → E` by `h ↦ t^{-1} ȷ(h) t` and `s ↦ t`. It respects the
relations: `β(s h s^{-1}) = ȷ(h)`, and
`β(ιȷ(h)) = t^{-1} ψ(ȷ(h)) t = t^{-1} t ȷ(h) t^{-1} t = ȷ(h)`.
Check the composites. `βα(u) = t^{-1} ȷι(u) t = t^{-1} ψ(u) t = u` and
`βα(t) = t`. `αβ(h) = s^{-1} ιȷ(h) s = s^{-1} s h s^{-1} s = h` and
`αβ(s) = s`. So `E ≅ E'`.

**Step 3 (finite presentation).** `E` is an HNN extension of the finitely
presented group `U`. It is generated by the generators of `U` and `t`, with
the relators of `U` and the finitely many relators `t u_i t^{-1} ψ(u_i)^{-1}`
for the generators `u_i`. So `E` is finitely presented.

**Step 4 (type `FP_∞`).** `E'` acts on its Bass--Serre tree. Vertex and edge
stabilizers are conjugates of `H` and of its image, both isomorphic to `H`.
This gives the exact sequence of `ZE'`-modules
`0 → Z[E'/H] → Z[E'/H] → Z → 0`.
`Z[E'/H] = ZE' ⊗_{ZH} Z` is of type `FP_∞`, since induction carries a
finite-type free resolution of `Z` over `ZH` to one over `ZE'`. In a short
exact sequence `0 → A → B → C → 0`, if `A` is of type `FP_{n-1}` and `B` is of
type `FP_n`, then `C` is of type `FP_n` (Bieri, *Homological dimension of
discrete groups*, QMC Notes 1981, §1). So `Z` is of type `FP_∞` over `ZE'`,
and `E ≅ E'` is of type `FP_∞`.

**Step 5 (`F_∞`).** A finitely presented group of type `FP_n` is of type
`F_n` (arXiv:2607.21727v1, Lemma 2.1: "If a finitely presented group G is of
type FP_n, then it is of type F_n."; classical, Wall). This holds for every
`n`, so `E` is of type `F_∞`.

**Conclusion.** Every finitely presented group `G` embeds in `U`, which embeds
in `E`, a group of type `F_∞`.
