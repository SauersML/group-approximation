title: Does Labbe's aperiodic Wang shift have polynomial pattern complexity?

**OPEN (input claim).** Let `Ω_U` be Labbé's aperiodic Wang shift on 19 tiles. Does its pattern complexity
`p(n)`, the number of distinct `n × n` patterns, grow polynomially in `n`? Subexponential growth of
`m ↦ m^2 p(2m+1)` suffices for everything downstream.

**Why it is wanted.** Together with
`subexponential-complexity-zd-subshift-rings-have-rank-models` it discharges, unconditionally and in
characteristic `p`, the rank-model assumption that
`rank-modelled-simple-algebra-with-non-lef-kazhdan-el-group` currently carries. The middle rung of
`kazhdan-elementary-approximation-type-mirrors-ring-type` would then rest only on non-matriciality, which is
`labbe-wang-shift-crossed-product-is-not-exactly-matricial`.

## Attempts

- **Expected from self-similarity, not yet verified here.** `Ω_U` is self-similar: it is generated by a
  primitive substitution rule. Primitive substitutive subshifts of `Z^d` are the standard source of zero
  entropy and polynomial complexity, by the usual recognizability and desubstitution argument counting
  patterns through supertiles. I did not read Labbé's paper, so this is stated as expected behaviour rather
  than as established; the substitution's primitivity and a recognizability statement both need quoting from
  the source before the claim is established.
- **Zero entropy alone is not enough.** Entropy for `Z^2` subshifts is `lim log p(n)/n^2`, so zero entropy
  only gives `p(n) = e^{o(n^2)}`, which permits `p(n) = e^{n}` and hence exponential growth of the ring. The
  downstream route needs genuinely subexponential `p`, not merely zero entropy. This is the one place where
  the `d = 1` and `d ≥ 2` cases differ: for `d = 1`, zero entropy is exactly subexponential complexity.
