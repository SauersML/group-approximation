Notation as in the claim. Write `A = F_q[t]`, `A' = F_q[t,t^-1]`, and `^` for
profinite completion. `SL_r` is a simple, simply connected Chevalley group of
rank `r-1 >= 2`.

**Step 1. `Γ^ ≅ SL_r(A^)`.** `A` is the ring of `S`-integers of `F_q(t)` for
`S = {∞}`. The place `∞` is non-Archimedean and `rk_(F_q((1/t)))(SL_r) = r-1 >= 2`.
By (C3) the kernel of `Γ^ -> overline(Γ)` is trivial, and by the strong
approximation statement quoted with (C3) the `S`-congruence completion is
`SL_r(A^)`. (CLR quote strong approximation in the case `S = {0,∞}`; its
hypotheses, no anisotropic place and simple connectedness, do not involve
`S`, and the same statement is applied here with `S = {∞}`.)

**Step 2. Split `A^`.** By the Chinese remainder theorem `A^ = ∏_f A^_f`
over monic irreducible `f ∈ F_q[t]`, with `A^_f ≅ F_(q^deg f)[[x]]`. The
factor at `f = t` is `F_q[[t]]`. The remaining factors form
`∏_(f≠t) A^_f`, which is the profinite completion `A'^` of `A'`, since the
prime ideals of `A'` are those of `A` other than `(t)` and `A'/(f^n) = A/(f^n)`
for `f ≠ t` (this is the decomposition CLR write in the proof of
Proposition 1.2). So `A^ = F_q[[t]] × A'^`, and `SL_r` of a product of rings
is the product of the groups:

```text
SL_r(A^) = SL_r(F_q[[t]]) × SL_r(A'^).
```

**Step 3. `SL_r(A'^) ≅ SL_r(A')^`.** `A'` is the ring of `S'`-integers for
`S' = {0,∞}`, with `rk_(S')(SL_r) = 2(r-1) >= 2` and non-Archimedean places.
(C3) and strong approximation give `SL_r(A')^ ≅ SL_r(A'^)`. This gives (PF1).

**Step 4. Both factors are finitely presented profinite groups.**
- By (C1), `SL_r(A')` has a finite presentation `<X | R>` as an abstract
  group. The same finite presentation presents `SL_r(A')^` as a profinite
  group: the finite quotients of `SL_r(A')` are exactly the finite groups
  generated by images of `X` satisfying `R`, so `F^(X)/cl<<R>> ≅ SL_r(A')^`.
- By (C2) with `e = 1` and the presentation from (C1), `SL_r(F_q[[t]])` has a
  profinite presentation with finitely many generators and relations.

**Step 5. Products of finitely presented profinite groups are finitely
presented.** Let `G = F^(X)/cl<<R>>` and `H = F^(Y)/cl<<S>>` with `X, Y, R, S`
finite. Let `P = F^(X ⊔ Y)/cl<<R ∪ S ∪ {[x,y] : x ∈ X, y ∈ Y}>>`.
- The assignment `x -> (x,1)`, `y -> (1,y)` kills every relator, so it
  induces a continuous surjection `P -> G × H`.
- In `P`, the closed subgroups `P_X` and `P_Y` generated by the images of `X`
  and `Y` commute elementwise, since their generators commute and commutation
  is a closed condition. They generate `P`. The relators `R` hold in `P_X`,
  so `G -> P_X`, `x -> x` is a continuous surjection, and likewise
  `H -> P_Y`. Hence `(g,h) -> g·h` is a continuous surjective homomorphism
  `G × H -> P`.
- The composite `G × H -> P -> G × H` is the identity on generators, hence the
  identity. So `P -> G × H` is injective, and `P ≅ G × H` has the finite
  presentation above.

Steps 1--5 prove the claim.
