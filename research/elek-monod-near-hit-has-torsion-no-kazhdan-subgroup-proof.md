**Primary sources.**  Elek--Monod, *On the topological full group of a
minimal Cantor `Z^2`-system*, arXiv:1201.0257, Theorem 1, construct a free
minimal Cantor `Z^2`-action whose topological full group contains `F_2`; the
construction is a minimal subshift.  Their closing paragraph invokes
Elek--Szabo to show that this topological full group is sofic and records the
resulting finitely generated infinite simple sofic subgroup.  This soficity
also follows from Elek, *Full groups and soficity*, arXiv:1211.0621, Theorem
1, which proves that the measurable full group of a sofic equivalence
relation is sofic.

Nekrashevych, *Simple groups of dynamical origin*, arXiv:1511.08241,
Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 (Theorems 4.1 and 5.6 in the body), proves that the
dynamical alternating group of a minimal groupoid of germs is simple and is
finitely generated when the groupoid is expansive with infinite orbits.
Kerr--Tucker-Drob, *Dynamical alternating groups, stability, property Gamma,
and inner amenability*, arXiv:1902.04131, introduction and Corollary 6.1,
identify the Elek--Monod instance as a finitely generated simple nonamenable
group and prove that every subgroup of the topological full group containing
`A(Z^2,X)` is inner amenable.  Soficity passes to subgroups.  An infinite
simple group has no nontrivial finite quotient, proving all assertions in
`(EM1)` except torsion.

**The torsion is intrinsic.**  The definition of `A(Z^2,X)` is generated by
the images of finite alternating groups on clopen multisections.  Freeness
and infinitude of an orbit give three distinct orbit points; after shrinking
to pairwise disjoint clopen neighborhoods, the three corresponding partial
translations form a degree-three multisection.  Its `A_3` image is an
embedded cyclic group of order three.  Thus the simple group `A` is not
torsion-free; this is not an artifact of an ambient envelope.

**The ambient Kazhdan fence.**  Fix `x in X`.  Freeness identifies its orbit
with `Z^2`.  Restriction to that orbit defines an injective homomorphism

```text
[[Z^2 actson X]] --> W(Z^2).
```

It is injective because the orbit is dense by minimality and a homeomorphism
which fixes it is the identity.  Its image lies in the wobbling group because
the locally constant orbit cocycle of each topological-full-group element
takes only finitely many values, hence has uniformly bounded displacement on
the orbit.  The metric space `Z^2` has uniform polynomial, in particular
uniform subexponential, growth.  Juschenko--de la Salle, *Invariant means for
the wobbling group*, arXiv:1301.4736, Theorem 4.1, proves that `W(Y)` contains
no infinite countable property-`(T)` subgroup whenever `Y` has uniform
subexponential growth.  This proves the first assertion in `(EM2)` and rules
out extracting any infinite Kazhdan subgroup from the entire full group.

There is also an independent fence on `A` itself.  Kerr--Tucker-Drob
Corollary 6.1 makes `A` inner amenable.  The infinite nonabelian simple group
`A` is ICC.  Inner amenability supplies almost invariant vectors for the
conjugation representation on `ell^2(A minus {1})`; property `(T)` would
supply a nonzero invariant vector, while ICC makes every nontrivial conjugacy
class infinite and therefore leaves no such square-summable invariant
vector.  Hence `A` is not Kazhdan.

**Boundary check.**  Alekseev--Thom, *Centralizers of sofic approximations of
Kazhdan groups*, arXiv:2608.05362, pages 17--18 and Open Problem 6.1, state
that finitely generated sofic Kazhdan non-residually-finite examples are
known but the finitely presented case is open.  Titz Mite--Witzel,
*Non-residually finite `C~_2`-lattices*, arXiv:2509.05054v2, Theorem A and
Corollary B, give five simple property-`(T)` CAT(0) groups; their free
cocompact deck actions make them torsion-free and finitely presented.  No
soficity theorem occurs in that paper.  These statements justify only the
near-hit and fence recorded here, not existence or nonexistence of the target
seed.
