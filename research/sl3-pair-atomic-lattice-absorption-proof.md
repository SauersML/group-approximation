Let `Q = pi(Gamma)''` and let `chi = tau o pi` restricted to `Gamma`, a
tracial state of `C^*(Gamma)`.  By the character dichotomy of Bader--
Boutonnet--Houdayer--Peterson for the S-arithmetic group
`Gamma = SL_3(Z[1/p])` (statement-level citation, see the claim), the
extreme tracial states of `C^*(Gamma)` are the normalized traces of
finite-image representations and the traces supported on the finite center
`Z`.  Decompose `chi` accordingly: there is a central projection
`z_infty in Q` such that the trace `tau(. z_infty)/tau(z_infty)` on
`Q z_infty` restricts on `Gamma` to a function vanishing off `Z`, while
`Q (1 - z_infty)` is generated by a representation whose character is a
sum of finite-dimensional characters, hence atomic.

If `z_infty != 0`, then `pi(Lambda)'' z_infty` is the von Neumann algebra
generated by `Lambda` with a character vanishing off `Z cap Lambda`, i.e. a
corner of `L(Lambda)`, which is diffuse because `Lambda` is infinite.  But
`pi(Lambda)'' z_infty = {p z_infty : p in P}` is the image of the atomic
algebra `P` under the `*`-homomorphism `p |-> p z_infty` (`z_infty` is
central in `Q supseteq P`), and every von Neumann quotient of an atomic
algebra is atomic.  Contradiction; so `z_infty = 0`.

Hence `Q` is atomic, in particular amenable, and
`sl3-atomic-ambient-is-absorbed-by-lattice` gives `Q = pi(Lambda)''`.
(`kazhdan-generated-amenable-algebra-is-atomic` is what makes "atomic" and
"amenable" interchangeable hypotheses on `P` in the statement.)
