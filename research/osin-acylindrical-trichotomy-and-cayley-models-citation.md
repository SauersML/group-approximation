D. Osin, *Acylindrically hyperbolic groups*, arXiv:1304.1246v4 [math.GR] (16 Apr 2015). Read from
the arXiv PDF on 2026-09-19 by swarm-0917-w17-w17-titz-last1.

**Definition of acylindricity (verbatim, p. 1).**

> The action of a group G on a metric space S is called acylindrical if for every ε > 0 there exist
> R, N > 0 such that for every two points x, y with d(x, y) ≥ R, there are at most N elements
> g ∈ G satisfying d(x, gx) ≤ ε and d(y, gy) ≤ ε.

**Elliptic and loxodromic (verbatim, p. 1).**

> Given a group G acting on a hyperbolic space S, an element g ∈ G is called elliptic if some
> (equivalently, any) orbit of g is bounded, and loxodromic if the map Z → S defined by n ↦ g^n s
> is a quasi-isometry for some (equivalently, any) s ∈ S.

**Theorem 1.1 (verbatim, p. 2).**

> Let G be a group acting acylindrically on a hyperbolic space. Then G satisfies exactly one of the
> following three conditions.
> (a) G has bounded orbits.
> (b) G is virtually cyclic and contains a loxodromic element.
> (c) G contains infinitely many independent loxodromic elements.

**Non-elementarity (verbatim, p. 3).**

> Recall that an action of a group G on a hyperbolic space S is called elementary if the limit set
> of G on ∂S contains at most 2 points. If the action is acylindrical, non-elementarity is
> equivalent to condition (c) from Theorem 1.1.

**Theorem 1.2 (verbatim excerpt, p. 3).**

> For any group G, the following conditions are equivalent.
> (AH1) There exists a generating set X of G such that the corresponding Cayley graph Γ(G, X) is
> hyperbolic, |∂Γ(G, X)| > 2, and the natural action of G on Γ(G, X) is acylindrical.
> (AH2) G admits a non-elementary acylindrical action on a hyperbolic space.

(Conditions (AH3) and (AH4) are not used.)

**Coboundedness remark (verbatim, p. 3).**

> Note that we do not assume that the actions in (AH2) and (AH3) are cobounded, while the action in
> (AH1) obviously is.

**Definition 1.3 (verbatim, p. 3).**

> We call a group G acylindrically hyperbolic if it satisfies either of the equivalent conditions
> (AH1)–(AH4) from Theorem 1.2.
