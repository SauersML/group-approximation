## Step 1. `W(Gamma(2)) = Z (1, ..., 1)`

1. By [TA] (TA4), `W(Gamma(2))` has rank 1 on the 10 classes. Write it as `Z w_0`.
2. By [PR], `5c` is in `W(Gamma(2))`, and every coordinate of `c` is positive. So `w_0` is a
   rational multiple of a vector with all coordinates positive.
3. Every coordinate of `w_0` is a nonzero integer, so `||w_0||_1 >= 10`.
4. By (TA4), some `k w_0` has `3` not dividing `aug(k w_0)` and `||k w_0||_1 = 10`. Then `|k| = 1`,
   and all ten coordinates of `w_0` equal `+-1` with a common sign. So `W(Gamma(2)) = Z (1, ..., 1)`.
5. By [TA] the 10 classes at `Gamma(2)` are indexed by the 10 level-two splittings (Step 3.1 of the
   [G4] route). The cross-check at the end of that route recomputes `W = Z (1, ..., 1)` on the 10
   classes directly.

## Step 2. Block sums (BS)

1. Let `Gamma' <= Gamma(2)`, `M' = pi^(-1)(Gamma')` and `M[2] = pi^(-1)(Gamma(2))`. Inclusion induces
   `j : H_1(M') -> H_1(M[2])`.
2. For a coset `M' g_c`, `j(v_c)` is the class of `g_c t_s g_c^(-1)` in `H_1(M[2])`, which is
   `v_(cbar)` for the coset `cbar = M[2] g_c`. This class depends only on the level-two splitting
   `m(c)` of `g_c(s)`.
   - Suppose `g` and `g'` give the same splitting mod 2. Then `pi(g)^(-1) pi(g')` stabilizes the
     splitting of `s` mod 2.
   - The stabilizer of `s` in `M` maps onto the stabilizer `(SL_2(Z) x SL_2(Z)) . 2` of the integral
     splitting. That stabilizer maps onto the mod-2 stabilizer `(SL_2(F_2) x SL_2(F_2)) . 2`.
   - So `g' in M[2] g z` for some `z` fixing `s`. Such a `z` commutes with `t_s`, and
     `g' t_s g'^(-1)` is `M[2]`-conjugate to `g t_s g^(-1)`.
   So the 10 splittings map onto the 10 classes of (TA4), and the map is a bijection.
3. So if `sum_c w_c v_c = 0` in `H_1(M')`, then `sum_m (sum_(m(c) = m) w_c) v_m = 0` in `H_1(M[2])`.
   That is, the block-sum vector `p(w)` lies in `W(Gamma(2)) = Z (1, ..., 1)`.
4. Hence all ten block sums are equal to one integer `k(w)`, and `aug(w) = 10 k(w)`. This is (BS1).
5. *(BS2).* Suppose `3` does not divide `aug(w)`. Then `k(w) != 0`, and
   `||w||_1 >= sum_m |sum_(m(c)=m) w_c| = 10 |k(w)| >= 10`.
   Equality needs `|k(w)| = 1`, and it needs no sign cancellation inside any block, with block
   `l1`-norm 1. So `w` is `+-` a 0/1 vector with exactly one entry `1` over each splitting.
6. *(BS3).* Such a `w` gives `d(Gamma') >= 1/30` by (TA3), and `d(Gamma') <= d(Gamma(2)) = 1/30` by
   (TA2). The positive-relation mass `A_+(Gamma')` lies in `10 Z`, and it equals `10` exactly when
   a transversal relation exists.

## Step 3. The index-two subgroups of `Gamma(4)`

1. `Gamma(4)/Gamma(8) = {I + 4Y : Y in sp_4(F_2)}` with `sp_4(F_2) = {Y : JY symmetric}`, which is
   elementary abelian of rank 10.
2. `H_1(Gamma(4)) = (Z/4)^6 + (Z/8)^4`, which has `F_2`-rank 10.
   - `pi : M_4 -> Gamma(4)` is onto with kernel the Torelli group `I`.
   - `I` is generated by the separating twists (Powell 1978; Mess 1992, genus 2), and every
     `M`-conjugate of `t_s` lies in `M_4`. So the image of `I` in `H_1(M_4)` is spanned by the
     `v_c`, and right exactness gives `H_1(Gamma(4)) = H_1(M_4) / span(v_c)`.
   - `level8_lattice.py` computes this quotient as `h1_Gamma2prime_*` in the `N4` entry of
     `level8-results.json`: rank 0, torsion `[4,4,4,4,4,4,8,8,8,8]`.
   - So the maximal elementary abelian 2-quotient of `Gamma(4)` is `Gamma(4)/Gamma(8)`, and it has
     exactly 1023 index-two subgroups.
3. Hence every index-two `Gamma'' <= Gamma(4)` contains `Gamma(8)`. These subgroups correspond to
   the nonzero functionals `f` on `sp_4(F_2)`, via `Gamma'' = ker(I + 4Y -> f(Y))`.
4. `Sp_4(Z)` acts on them by the adjoint action of `Sp_4(F_2)`, and conjugate `Gamma''` have the
   same `W` up to relabelling. `level8_index2.py orbits` finds 15 orbits, of sizes
   `15, 60, 45, 20, 180, 180, 60, 60, 90, 120, 15, 90, 72, 10, 6` (total 1023).

## Step 4. Reduction to `K2 = PB_5/<Delta^2>`

1. *The subgroup.* As in [G4] Step 1, `M'' = pi^(-1)(Gamma'')` meets `<iota>` trivially. It is
   isomorphic to its image in `K2 = PMod(S_(0,6)) = PB_5/<Delta^2>`. That image is the stabilizer
   of the class of `I` in `(+-Gamma''/Gamma(8)) \ Sp_4(Z/8)` under the right action. Pure braids act
   trivially mod 2, so the image lies in `K2`.
2. *Presentation.* Artin's generators are
   `A_ij = s_(j-1) ... s_(i+1) s_i^2 s_(i+1)^(-1) ... s_(j-1)^(-1)`.
   - Combing gives `PB_n = U_n x| PB_(n-1)`, with `U_n` free on `A_(1n), ..., A_(n-1,n)` (Artin 1947;
     Birman, *Braids, links and mapping class groups*, Lemma 1.8.2).
   - So `PB_5` is presented by the ten `A_ij` and, for each `A_rs` with `s < j` and each `A_ij`, one
     relation `A_rs^(-1) A_ij A_rs = u`. Here `u` is any word in `U_j` that is equal in `B_5`.
   - All 35 relations (Artin's words) are checked exactly in `B_5` via Artin's faithful action on
     `F_5`. So is `Delta^2 = A_12 A_13 A_23 A_14 A_24 A_34 A_15 A_25 A_35 A_45` (`verify_all`).
   - `Delta^2` is central, so adding this relator presents `K2`.
3. *Separating classes.*
   - By [G4] Step 1, every separating twist is an `H`-conjugate of `t_s = (s_1 s_2)^6`.
   - Write `g = k h` with `k in K2`. Then `h t_s h^(-1)` is the squared twist about a curve enclosing
     a 3-set `S` of `{1..5}`.
   - `PMod(S_(0,6))` is transitive on curves with a given puncture partition. So the distinct
     classes are the `[k t_S k^(-1)]`, with `k` over `K2/M''`, `S` over the ten 3-sets, and
     `t_S = (A_ab A_ac A_bc)^2`. That `A_ab A_ac A_bc` is conjugate to `(s_1 s_2)^3` is checked
     exactly in `B_5`, via explicit words (`CONJ`).
   - `W` lives on distinct classes, so multiplicities play no role.
   - A class is labelled by the level-two splitting of the matrix of `path_k w_S^(-1)`, using the
     [G4] convention.
4. *Computation.* Abelian Reidemeister-Schreier over this presentation, with inverse letters
   traced along reversed edges. Each relator and each `t_S` is checked to act correctly on the
   cosets. This is followed by exact unit elimination and then `level8_lattice.py` (HNF, class
   reduction, `W`, the block-sum check, a MILP for `A_+ = 10`, `d`, and the best certificate).
5. *Validation.*
   - `Gamma(2)` (1 coset): `H_1 = Z^9`, 10 classes, `W = Z (1, ..., 1)`, `d = 1/30`.
   - `Gamma(4)` (512 cosets): `H_1 = Z^54`, 160 classes (16 per splitting), `rank W = 106`,
     `d = 1/30`, and a transversal relation exists. All of these agree with [G4], which used a
     different presentation (`H` on 61440 cosets).

## Step 5. The census

`python3 run_all.py <scratch>` writes `level8-results.json`. It has one entry per orbit
representative `f<code>`; the code is the bit vector of `f` in the `J S` basis of
`level8_index2.py`. Each entry is an exact integer computation.
1. *Common to all 15 representatives.*
   - `H_1(M'')` has rank 54.
   - `gcd aug(W) = 10`, and the block-sum identity holds on a `W` basis, which is a check of (BS1).
   - The MILP finds `w >= 0` in `W` with `aug(w) = 10`. The script asserts that the returned `w` is
     nonnegative with augmentation 10. By (BS2) it is a transversal 0/1 vector, so `A_+ = 10`.
   - `C.distance_milp` gives `d = 1/30`, with exact rational point `theta = 3/10` on every class.
   - `C.best_certificate` gives `||w||_1 = aug = 10`.
2. *The 14 representatives other than `f4`.* `H_1(M'')` is torsion-free. There are 160 classes,
   16 per splitting. `rank W = 106`, and the SNF of the `W` basis is all units.
3. *`f4` (orbit of size 45).* `H_1(M'') = Z^54 + Z/2`. There are 192 classes, with 32 over two
   splittings and 16 over the others. `rank W = 138`, and the SNF of the `W` basis has one entry 2.
4. `H_1(Gamma'') = H_1(M'') / span(v_c)`, by the argument of Step 3.2 applied to `M''`. It is
   `(Z/4)^7 + (Z/8)^3` for `f1` and `Z/2 + (Z/4)^5 + (Z/8)^4` for the others.

## Conclusion

- (BS) is Step 2. (L0) is Step 3.
- (L1) is Step 5, and it is transported to all 1023 subgroups by conjugation invariance (Step 3.4).
- The lower bound `d >= 1/30` comes from (BS3) with the transversal. The upper bound
  `d <= d(Gamma(2)) = 1/30` is (TA2). The MILP value agrees.
- (L2) is Step 4.
