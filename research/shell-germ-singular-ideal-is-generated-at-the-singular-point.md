**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, not reviewed; no priority claimed).

## Setting

As in `shell-germ-steinberg-algebras-are-never-simple`. `K` is a field, `A = A_K(G_nu)`, `J` is
its singular ideal, `Ω = V·p`, and `Q` is the germ group at `p`. An `e ∈ E_nu` with `e(p) = p`
acts near `p` shell by shell: there are `N` and an injective `σ_e : [N, ∞) -> N` with
`e(0^n 1 w) = 0^(σ_e(n)) 1 w` for `n >= N`. To see this, follow the orbit of `p` through a word
for `e`. Near a point `u 0^∞ ≠ p`, each generator is a prefix replacement, so it only shifts
the depth `k` of `u 0^k 1 w`. Near `p`, `hat(g)` sends depth `n` to `rho_g(n)`. The germ of
`e` at `p` is `σ_e` modulo finite changes. This is the near action of `Q` on `N` from
`shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`. Let

    M = K^N / K^(N),     J_p = Ann_(K[Q]) (M),

where `K^N` is all functions, `K^(N)` the finitely supported ones, and `Q` acts on `M` through
the near action. `J_p` is a two-sided ideal of `K[Q]`. It lies in the augmentation ideal,
because `x·e_n = 0` forces the coefficients of `x` to sum to `0`.

## Statement

1. Every `f ∈ J` is finitely supported, on germs with source and range in `Ω`.
2. Identify a function on `G_p^p = Q` with an element of `K[Q]`. The elements of `A`
   supported on `G_p^p` are exactly `J_p`.
3. `J = A J_p A`. Also, `J` is finitely generated as a two-sided ideal of `A` iff `J_p` is
   finitely generated as a two-sided ideal of `K[Q]`.
4. If `K[Q]` is left or right Noetherian, for instance if `Q` is virtually polycyclic, then
   `J` is finitely generated.
5. Put `P_e = <r_g : g ∈ P>` and `P_o = τ P_e τ^(-1)`. Then `P_e P_o ≅ P × P`, and
   `J_p ∩ K[P_e × P_o] = ω(P_e) ω(P_o)`, where `ω` denotes augmentation ideals.

## Proofs

**Test at one point.** For `x = Σ c_q q ∈ K[Q]`, `x` kills `M` iff `x·e_n = 0` for all
large `n`. If `x·e_n ≠ 0` for infinitely many `n`, choose them so sparse that the supports of
the `x·e_n` are disjoint. Then their sum is a function `φ` with `x·φ ∉ K^(N)`.

**1.** Write `f = Σ_j d_j 1_([e_j, U_j])` with `U_j` clopen. Every `h ∈ E_nu` agrees with an
element of `V` near each point outside a finite set `F_h ⊆ Ω`: each `hat(g)` is a prefix
replacement on every shell, and a product of generators is locally in `V` away from the
finitely many points that its partial products send to `p`. Those points lie in `Ω`, because
`E_nu` preserves `V`-orbits. Near such a point `y`, `h` is a
prefix replacement `wz |-> w'z` on a cylinder `[w] ∋ y`. If `w ≠ w'`, it fixes no open subset
of `[w]`: `wz = w'z` on an open set would force `z = u^∞` for one word `u`. So either `h` is
the identity on `[w]`, or its germ is nontrivial at every point of `[w]`. Apply this to
`h = e_j^(-1) e_(j')` for all pairs, and let `F` be the union of the finite sets `F_h`.
- For `y ∉ F`, there is a neighborhood `N_y` over which the pattern of which germs `[e_j, ·]`
  coincide is constant, and the `U_j` are constant as well.
- So if `f` were nonzero at a germ over `y`, it would be nonzero at the matching germ over
  every `y' ∈ N_y`. Those germs form an open bisection, which contradicts `f ∈ J`.

Hence `f` lives over the finite set `F`, with at most one germ per `j` at each point.

**2.** Choose `e_q` with germ `q` at `p`, and `N` so large that every `e_q` in the support of
`x` acts shell-wise on `[0^N]`. Put `h_x = Σ c_q 1_([e_q, [0^N]])`. At `p` it restricts to `x`.
At a point of `C_n` with `n >= N`, the germ of `e_q` is fixed by the target shell `σ_q(n)`.
So the value of `h_x` at the germ with target shell `m` is the coefficient of `e_m` in `x·e_n`.
- If `x ∈ J_p`, enlarge `N` so that `x·e_n = 0` for `n >= N`. Then `h_x` is supported on
  `G_p^p`.
- Conversely, let `g ∈ A` be supported on `G_p^p`. For large `N`, `g 1_([0^N])` is a combination
  of the `1_([e_j, [0^N]])` with `e_j(p) = p`. Grouping the `j` by germ at `p` gives `h_x`,
  with `x = g|G_p^p`. So `x·e_n = 0` for `n >= N`.

**3.** Take `f ∈ J`, with sources in a finite set `Y` and ranges in a finite set `Z`. With
small disjoint clopen neighborhoods, `f = Σ_(z,y) 1_(W_z) f 1_(U_y)`, where each term lives on
germs from `y` to `z`. For `y = u 0^∞` let `C_y` be the compact open bisection of
`0^(|u|) z |-> u z` over `[0^(|u|)]`. Then:
- `1_(C_z^(-1)) (1_(W_z) f 1_(U_y)) 1_(C_y)` is supported on `G_p^p`, so it lies in `J_p` by 2;
- conjugating back recovers the term.

So `J ⊆ A J_p A`, and the reverse inclusion is clear. For finite generation:
- **⇐.** `K[Q] j K[Q] ⊆ A j A`, since `q·j = 1_([e_q, U]) j` for small `U`.
- **⇒.** Let `Φ : J -> K[Q]` be restriction to `G_p^p`. For `f ∈ J` and `x, y ∈ Ω`, let
  `f_(xy) ∈ K[Q]` be the entry of `f` with germs conjugated to `p` by the `C`'s. By the
  argument above, `f_(xy) ∈ J_p`. Now, if `J = Σ_i A f_i A`, then `Φ` of each summand lies in
  `Σ_(x,y) K[Q] (f_i)_(xy) K[Q]`, and `Φ` is the identity on `J_p`. So finitely many
  entries generate `J_p`.

**4.** A one-sided ideal of a Noetherian ring is finitely generated, and so is any two-sided
ideal, which is in particular one-sided. Group rings of virtually polycyclic groups are
Noetherian (P. Hall).

**5.** `r_g` moves only even shells, by the free regular action, and `τ r_h τ^(-1)` moves
only odd ones. So the two subgroups commute and meet trivially, and each is isomorphic to `P`.
For `x ∈ K[P_e] ⊗ K[P_o]` and `n` even, `x·e_n = ((id ⊗ ε) x)·e_n`, where the first factor acts
freely. So `x` kills the even part iff `(id ⊗ ε) x = 0`, and similarly for the odd part.
Finally, `ker(id ⊗ ε) ∩ ker(ε ⊗ id) = ω ⊗ ω`. `∎`

## What it gives

- The germ algebra at `p` of `A/J` is `K[Q]/J_p`, which acts faithfully on `M`. This is the
  permutation-type local algebra of the Attempts on `shell-germ-algebra-fp-forces-fp-germ-group`.
- The elements `f_(g,h) = (1 - u_g) s_0 (1 - u_h) t_0` have `(p,p)` entry
  `(1 - r_g)(1 - τ r_h τ^(-1))`. They generate exactly the part of `J` coming from
  `ω(P_e) ω(P_o)`. For the zigzag enumeration of `Z`, `J_p` is strictly larger
  (`zigzag-shell-singular-ideal-is-finitely-generated`), so the `f_(g,h)` do not generate `J`
  in general.
- For the inputs the route needs (infinite simple `P`), `K[Q]` is not Noetherian. Whether
  `J_p` is finitely generated there is open.
