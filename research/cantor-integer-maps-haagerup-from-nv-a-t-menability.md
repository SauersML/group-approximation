## Why sufficient

`Z` embeds in `V = 1V`, for example as the cyclic group generated by any nontrivial
element of `F`. By `cantor-maps-by-v-embed-in-next-brin-thompson-group` with `n = 1`,
`C(C,Z) ⋊ V` embeds in `2V`. By `brin-thompson-groups-nv-are-a-t-menable`, `2V` has
a proper conditionally negative definite function, and its restriction to a
subgroup is again proper and conditionally negative definite. So `C(C,Z) ⋊ V` has
the Haagerup property.

The implication records that the affirmative side of Zaremsky Problem 2.7 must
solve the case `Γ = Z` of Brothier's Problem B. Any proof for `2V` has to produce a
cnd function on the explicit subgroup `C(C,Z) ⋊ V` that is proper along
`n·1_{[0]}`.
