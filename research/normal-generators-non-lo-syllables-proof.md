**Standard facts used** (textbook normal form theory for free products,
Magnus–Karrass–Solitar and Lyndon–Schupp Ch. IV; not re-read here):
- (N1) every element of `Ā * B̄` has a unique reduced alternating normal form;
- (N2) every element is conjugate to a cyclically reduced element, and an element
  whose cyclically reduced conjugates have length at most 1 is conjugate into a
  factor.

**Step 0 (quotients).** If `f: G -> G'` is onto and `G = <<w>>`, then
`G' = <<f(w)>>`: `f` maps each conjugate `g w g^{-1}` to `f(g) f(w) f(g)^{-1}`,
and every element of `G'` is some `f(g)`. The map `p * q: A * B -> Ā * B̄` is
onto, so `Ā * B̄ = <<w̄>>`. Normal closures are conjugation invariant, so
`Ā * B̄ = <<x>>` for every conjugate `x` of `w̄`.

**Item 1.** Suppose `w̄` is conjugate to `ā ∈ Ā` (possibly `ā = 1`). Then
`(Ā * B̄)/<<w̄>> = (Ā * B̄)/<<ā>>`. This group has the universal property of
`(Ā/<<ā>>_Ā) * B̄`: a homomorphism out of either is a pair consisting of a
homomorphism `Ā -> H` killing `ā` and a homomorphism `B̄ -> H`. So the two
groups are isomorphic, and `B̄` injects into the quotient. (Chen–Lodha record
the same identity, `wiegold.tex` l.1466.) Since `B̄ ≠ 1`, the quotient is
nontrivial, which contradicts step 0. The case of `B̄` is symmetric.

**Item 2.** By (N2) and item 1, no cyclically reduced conjugate of `w̄` has length
at most 1. A cyclically reduced element of length at least 2 begins and ends in
different factors, so its length is even, `2m` with `m >= 1`, and after a cyclic
rotation it has the form `a_1 b_1 ... a_m b_m`. A rotation is a conjugation, so
it changes neither the normal closure nor the sets `{a_i}` and `{b_i}`.

**Item 3.** Let `x = a_1 b_1 ... a_m b_m` be a cyclically reduced conjugate of
`w̄`, and suppose `A'(x)` and `B'(x)` are both left-orderable. By
`lo-syllable-relators-keep-free-factors`, `Ā` injects into `(Ā * B̄)/<<x>>`. By
step 0 that quotient is trivial, so `Ā = 1`, a contradiction.

**Consequences.**
- **Clause (b).** Subgroups of a left-orderable group are left-orderable, by
  restricting the order. So if `Ā` and `B̄` are left-orderable, item 3 fails.
- **K1–K2.** Take `p = id`, `q = id`, which is allowed because torsion-free
  groups in Kirby 66 are nontrivial. An infinite cyclic group `<g>` is
  left-orderable (`g^i < g^j` iff `i < j`). The trivial group does not occur,
  since the syllables are nontrivial. So a non-orderable syllable subgroup of a
  torsion-free factor is not cyclic, hence `m >= 2`, and its factor is not
  left-orderable.
- **K3.** A syllable subgroup of a relator of syllable length `2m <= 2N` is
  generated by `m <= N` elements, so under the hypothesis both are
  left-orderable, which contradicts item 3.
- **K4.** This is items 2–3 for general `p`, `q`. ∎
