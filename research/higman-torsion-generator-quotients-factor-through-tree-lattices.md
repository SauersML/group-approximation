**ESTABLISHED** (proposed) by `higman-tree-lattice-factorization-proof`.
Items 1, 2, 3 and 5 are elementary: normal forms in amalgams and arithmetic. Item 4 cites the
Gersten–Stallings theorem for squares of groups. Item 6 is a certified finite computation.

**Setting.** `H4(n) = < a, b, c, d | b^a = b^n, c^b = c^n, d^c = d^n, a^d = a^n >`, with `n >= 2` and
`x^y = y^-1 x y`. Call `m >= 1` **n-admissible** if `m | n^m - 1`. For admissible `m` put

- `Gamma(n,m) = H4(n) / << a^m, b^m, c^m, d^m >>`;
- `G(x,y) = < x, y | x^m, y^m, y^x = y^n >` for each cyclically consecutive pair
  `(x,y) in {(a,b), (b,c), (c,d), (d,a)}`;
- `K1 = <a,b,c>`, `K2 = <c,d,a>`, `L1 = <d,a,b>`, `L2 = <b,c,d>`, `A = <a,c>`, `B = <b,d>`, all
  inside `Gamma(n,m)`.

**Statement.**
1. **Universality.** Let `Q` be a quotient of `H4(n)` in which `a, b, c, d` all have finite order, and
   let `m` be the lcm of the four orders. Then `m` is n-admissible, and `Q` is a quotient of
   `Gamma(n,m)`.
2. **Arithmetic.** If `m > 1` is n-admissible, then the smallest prime factor of `m` divides `n - 1`.
   Every prime power `p^k` with `p | n - 1` is n-admissible, and the n-admissible numbers are closed
   under lcm. For `n = 3` they include `2^k` and also `20`.
   For `n = 2`, only `m = 1` is admissible. So in a quotient of `H4` whose four generators are all
   torsion, the quotient is trivial.
3. **Amalgam structure.** Let `m` be n-admissible.
   - `G(x,y) ≅ Z/m ⋊_n Z/m` has order `m^2`, with `<y>` normal and `<x> ∩ <y> = 1`.
   - `K1 = G(a,b) *_<b> G(b,c)` and `K2 = G(c,d) *_<d> G(d,a)`.
   - `A ≅ Z/m * Z/m`, freely generated by the factors `<a>` and `<c>`.
   - `Gamma(n,m) = K1 *_A K2`.
   - `[K1 : A] = [K2 : A] = m`, with transversals `<b>` and `<d>`.
   - On `K1/A = { b^j A : j in Z/m }`, `c` acts trivially, `b` acts as `j -> j+1` and `a` acts as
     `j -> n^-1 j`.
   - By the cyclic symmetry, `Gamma(n,m) = L1 *_B L2` with `B ≅ Z/m * Z/m` of index `m`.
   - Consequently each `G(x,y)` embeds in `Gamma(n,m)`, and each generator has order **exactly** `m`.
   - Every finite subgroup of `Gamma(n,m)` is conjugate into one of the four `G(x,y)`.
4. **Geometry.**
   - `Gamma(n,m)` is the fundamental group of a non-positively curved square of groups. The vertex
     groups are the `G(x,y)`, the edge groups are the cyclic groups `<x>`, and the face group is
     trivial.
   - Its development is `T_m x T_m`, the product of the Bass–Serre trees of the two splittings in
     item 3, both `m`-regular.
   - `Gamma(n,m)` acts on it properly, cocompactly and preserving the factors, and freely
     transitively on squares. So it is a uniform lattice in `Aut(T_m) x Aut(T_m)`.
   - For `m = 2` and odd `n`, `Gamma(n,2)` is the right-angled Coxeter group of the 4-cycle,
     `D_oo x D_oo = <a,c> x <b,d>`. This is exactly item 4 of
     `higman-power-pair-collapse-is-base-uniform-up-to-torsion`.
   - If `m | n - 1`, then `Gamma(n,m) = (Z/m * Z/m) x (Z/m * Z/m) = A x B`.
   - If `m` does not divide `n - 1`, for example `(n,m) = (3,4)`, then `a` and `b` do not commute and
     the local actions are twisted.
5. **Edge images in the torsion regime.**
   - `H4(n) / << b, d >> = Z/(n-1) * Z/(n-1)`, with free factors `<a>` and `<c>`.
   - Hence every group generated by two elements whose orders divide `n - 1` is the edge image `A` of a
     quotient of `H4(n)`, with `B = 1`. For example:
     - `n = 3`: every dihedral group;
     - `n = 4`: every 2-generated group whose two generators have order dividing 3, including the
       Gupta–Sidki 3-group;
     - `n = 5`: every quotient of `Z/4 * Z/4`.
   - Together with item 2 of `higman-power-pair-collapse-is-base-uniform-up-to-torsion`: every
     quotient whose edge image lies in the class `X` is a quotient of some `Gamma(n,m)`, and its edge
     image is a quotient of `Z/m * Z/m`.
6. **Computation, for `n = 3` and `m = 2^e`.**
   - **Virtual torsion-freeness for `e <= 5`.** In the class-`e` lower exponent-2 central quotient
     `P_e` of `Gamma(3,2^e)`, of order `2^10`, `2^20`, `2^36`, `2^64` for `e = 2..5`, all four vertex
     groups inject: `|<x,y>| = 2^(2e)` (`gamma_vt-output.txt`). By item 3 the kernel is a torsion-free
     normal subgroup of finite index. It is the fundamental group of a finite square complex covered by
     `T_m x T_m`.
   - **Adding `R = [a,c]^2`.** In `Gamma(3,2^e) / <<[a,c]^2>>`, for `e = 2..5`, the finite 2-quotients
     still give `a` order `2^e`. For `e = 5` this first happens at class 7. The order of `<a,c>` keeps
     growing with the class: `2^5`, `2^7`, `2^10`, `2^14` for `e = 2`.
   - **Calibration with `R = [a,c]`.** In `H4(3) / <<[a,c]>>`, the order of `a` is stuck at `2^3` in
     the 2-quotients of class 3 to 9. With `[a,c], [b,d]` both killed, the whole 2-quotient is stuck at
     order `2^12` (`comm_ac-output.txt`).

**Reduction for question (a) with `R = [a,c]^2`.** Let `R` be an element of `A`, and put
`Abar = A / <<R>>_A`. Suppose `Abar` embeds in both `K1 / <<R>>_K1` and `K2 / <<R>>_K2`. Then
`Gamma(n,m) / <<R>> = K1/<<R>> *_Abar K2/<<R>>`, and `a` has the same order there as in `Abar`
(proof, section 6).

Take `n = 3`, `R = [a,c]^2`, and suppose this holds for infinitely many `m = 2^e`, with `a` of order
`m` in `<a, c | a^m, c^m, [a,c]^2>`. Then `a` has infinite order in `H4(3) / <<[a,c]^2>>`, which is
the open half of the w9-053 question. Item 6 is consistent with this for `e <= 5`.

**When `m | n + 1`, the reduction holds (proof, Prop 6.3).** Take `m` even with `m | n + 1`, and `R`
such that `R(a, c^-1)` and `R(a^-1, c)` lie in `<<R>>`, for example `R = [a,c]^2`. Then the induced
representation `K1 -> Abar wr Sym(m)` kills `R`. The `j`-th Kaloujnine–Krasner coordinate of `[a,c]`
is `a^-1 c^-u a c^v`, with `u = n^(n^-1 j)` and `v = n^j`. So `Gamma(3,4) / <<[a,c]^2>>` is the
amalgam over `<a, c | a^4, c^4, [a,c]^2>`, and `a` has order exactly 4 there.

**For `m = 8` and `m = 16` the induced representation fails** (`kk_coords-output.txt`). At odd `j` the
coordinate square is `[a,c^3]^2`, or `(a^-1 c^-11 a c^3)^2`, and it is nontrivial in a finite
2-quotient of `Abar`. So for `m = 2^e >= 8` the embedding needs a genuine Freiheitssatz for the
proper power `[a,c]^2` over the virtually free group `K1`.

**Dead end (self-similar models, option 1 of the w10-053 next step).** `search_sym.py` enumerates all
cyclically symmetric wreath recursions `a = (u,v)pi`, with `b, c, d` the rotations of `a` and sections
`u, v` of length at most 2 in `a^±1, ..., d^±1`. Of the 8450 recursions, 7538 satisfy the four
relations of `H4(3)` to level 10. In every one of them `a` has order 1 or 2 at level 10
(`search_sym-output.txt`). So short symmetric automata only produce the torsion regime of item 1. This
is expected from item 1: finite-level images are torsion-generator quotients, so they factor through
`Gamma(3,2^e)`. An `Aut(T_2)` model with infinite-order generators needs level-`L` images of unbounded
exponent.

`lift.py` builds such images greedily. It computes the `F_2`-space of all lifts of a level-`L` action
to level `L+1`, which is `Z^1(G, F_2[level L])` by Shapiro's lemma. For `H4(3) + [a,c]^2` it reaches
only `ord(a) = 2^4` at level 9, far below the class-11 value `2^7` of
`experiments/higman-diagonal-pro2-2026-09-18`.
