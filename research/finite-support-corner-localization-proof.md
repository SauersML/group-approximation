Let `Y` be a finite star-generating set of `B`.  Every element of the
algebraic group ring has finite support, so the subgroup

```text
Lambda=< supp(q), supp(Phi(y)) : y in Y >
```

is finitely generated.  It contains `q` and every `Phi(y)`.  Since
`C[Lambda]` is a star subalgebra of `C[Gamma]`, it contains every star
polynomial in these elements.  Hence it contains `Phi(B)`.  Moreover
`Phi(b)=q Phi(b) q`, so `(FSL2)` holds and the same map is a unital star
homomorphism

```text
B -> q C[Lambda] q.
```

If `Lambda` were hyperlinear, its faithful canonical
corner trace, normalized on `q`, would pull back to a Connes-embeddable
tracial state on `B`, contrary to the hypothesis.  Therefore `Lambda` is
nonhyperlinear.

For the chord formulation, order the finitely many supports used by a
completed atlas and form the increasing sequence of subgroups they
generate.  The tree-stage group is hyperlinear in the standard finite
Bass--Serre construction, while the final support group is nonhyperlinear by
the preceding paragraph.  Since the sequence is finite, it has a first
nonhyperlinear term.  The corresponding chord-support extension is therefore
where nonhyperlinearity itself enters; it cannot be postponed to unused
ambient generators.
