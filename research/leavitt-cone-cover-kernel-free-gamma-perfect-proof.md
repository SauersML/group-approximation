Notation as in the target. `φ : Delta = St_20(R) -> GL_20(R)` is the Steinberg map, `φ(x_ij(r)) = 1 + r e_ij`.

**Imports (recalled, standard).**
- (S) Serre, *Trees*, Ch. I §4.1 (the tree of an amalgam `A *_C B`: vertices `Γ/A ⊔ Γ/B`, edges `Γ/C`, no
  inversions) and Ch. I §3.3 (a group acting freely without inversion on a tree is free, and is `π_1` of the
  quotient graph). Both already used in `leavitt-cuntz-cone-halves-rf-cover-proof` on the landing branch.
- (H) The five-term exact sequence of `1 -> K -> Γ -> Δ -> 1` with trivial `Z` coefficients,
  `H_2(Γ) -> H_2(Δ) -> K/[K,Γ] -> H_1(Γ) -> H_1(Δ) -> 0`, and its cohomological form with a `Δ`-module `M`,
  `0 -> H^1(Δ;M) -> H^1(Γ;M) -> Hom_Γ(K, M) -> H^2(Δ;M)` (Brown, *Cohomology of Groups*, VII.6).
- (MV) The Mayer–Vietoris sequence of an amalgam (Brown, II.7 and VII.9):
  `0 -> M^Γ -> M^A ⊕ M^B -> M^C -> H^1(Γ;M) -> H^1(A;M) ⊕ H^1(B;M)`.
- (DG) Delorme–Guichardet, as used in `brick-host-nonisomorphism-from-nv-haagerup` (BdlHV Theorem 2.12.4): a countable
  group with (T) has `H^1(G; π) = 0` for every unitary representation `π`.

**In-graph inputs.**
- `leavitt-steinberg-map-iso-from-rank-three`: `H_n(GL_r(R); Z) = 0` for `n > 0` and `St_r(R) ≅ GL_r(R)` for
  `r >= 3`. So `H_1(Δ) = H_2(Δ) = 0` and `φ` is injective.
- `ershov-jaikin-kassabov-steinberg-groups-have-property-t`: `St_20(A)` has (T) for every finitely generated ring `A`,
  and so does every quotient. `R`, `R_+`, `R_-` are finitely generated, and `Δ_+-` is the image of `St_20(R_+-)`
  in `Δ` (the defining relations of `St_20(R_+-)` hold in `Δ`). So `Δ`, `Δ_+` and `Δ_-` have (T).

## Step 0. Grading facts

`R = ⊕_n R_n` is `Z`-graded with `s_i` of degree `1` and `t_i` of degree `-1`: the defining relations `t_i s_j = δ_ij`
and `s_1t_1 + s_2t_2 = 1` are homogeneous of degree `0`. `s_1, t_1 ≠ 0` since `t_1 s_1 = 1`.
- `R_+` is spanned by words in `s_1, s_2, q`, all homogeneous of degree `>= 0`, so `R_+ ⊆ ⊕_(n>=0) R_n` and `R_+` is a
  graded subspace. Likewise `R_- ⊆ ⊕_(n<=0) R_n`.
- Hence `t_1 ∉ R_+` and `s_1 ∉ R_-`, and `R_+ ∩ R_- ⊆ (R_+)_0 = span{words in q} = span{1, q}`. As `q^2 = q`,
  `q ≠ 0, 1`, this is `F_2 × F_2`. (Only the inclusion is used.)
- `φ(Δ_+-) ⊆ GL_20(R_+-)`, because each generator `1 + a e_ij` and its inverse `1 - a e_ij` have entries in `R_+-`.
- So `φ(Δ_0) ⊆ GL_20(F_2 × F_2)`, and since `φ` is injective, `Δ_0` is finite.
- `a = x_12(s_1) ∉ Δ_-` and `b = x_23(t_1) ∉ Δ_+`, because `φ(a)` has the entry `s_1 ∉ R_-` and `φ(b)` has the entry
  `t_1 ∉ R_+`. In particular `a, b ∉ Δ_0`.

## Step 1. `p` is onto, and `K` is free

- *Onto.* `Δ` is generated by the `x_ij(r)` with `r` running over a spanning set of `R`, by additivity
  `x_ij(r + r') = x_ij(r) x_ij(r')`. `R` is spanned by the `s_μ t_ν` (Leavitt normal form, recalled), with
  `s_μ ∈ R_+` and `t_ν ∈ R_-`. For `k ∉ {i, j}`, `x_ij(s_μ t_ν) = [x_ik(s_μ), x_kj(t_ν)]` in `Δ`. So
  `Δ = <Δ_+, Δ_->` and `p` is onto.
- *Free.* The composite `Δ_+- -> Γ -> Δ` is the inclusion, so `K ∩ Δ_+- = 1`, and since `K` is normal,
  `K ∩ γΔ_+-γ^(-1) = 1` for every `γ`. By (S) the vertex stabilizers of the Bass–Serre tree `T` are exactly these
  conjugates, and `Γ` acts without inversion. So `K` acts freely without inversion on `T`, hence `K` is free and
  `K ≅ π_1(K \ T)`. As `K` is normal with `Γ/K = Δ`, the quotient `K \ T` has vertex set
  `K\Γ/Δ_+ ⊔ K\Γ/Δ_- = Δ/Δ_+ ⊔ Δ/Δ_-` and edge set `Δ/Δ_0`. This is `X`.
- *Nonabelian.* `K ≠ 1` by Step 5. If `K ≅ Z`, conjugation acts on `K` by `±1`, so every `[k, γ]` lies in `2K`, and
  `[K, Γ] ⊆ 2K ≠ K`, contradicting Step 2.

## Step 2. `K = [K, Γ]`

- `Δ_+` and `Δ_-` are perfect: for distinct `i, j, k`, `x_ij(a) = [x_ik(a), x_kj(1)]`, and `1 ∈ R_+-`. They generate
  `Γ`, so `H_1(Γ) = 0`.
- `H_2(Δ) = 0` by Khanh.
- By (H), `K/[K, Γ]` sits between `H_2(Δ) = 0` and `H_1(Γ) = 0`, so it vanishes. `K/[K,Γ]` is `H_0(Δ; K^ab)`.

## Step 3. No invariant charge

- A `Γ`-invariant homomorphism `h : K -> A` into an abelian group kills every `[k, γ] = k (γ k^(-1) γ^(-1))`, since
  `h(γ k^(-1) γ^(-1)) = h(k)^(-1)`. So `h` vanishes on `[K, Γ] = K`.
- If `rho(K)` is central in `rho(Γ)`, then `rho([k, γ]) = 1`, so `rho(K) = rho([K, Γ]) = 1` and `rho` factors
  through `Δ`. For a unitary representation in which `K` acts by scalars, the image of `K` is central.

## Step 4. Charges with unitary coefficients

Let `M` be a unitary `Δ`-module, viewed as a `Γ`-module through `p`, so `K` acts trivially.
- By (DG) and (T) for `Δ_+-` (in-graph input), `H^1(Δ_+; M) = H^1(Δ_-; M) = 0`. By (MV),
  `H^1(Γ; M) ≅ M^(Δ_0) / (M^(Δ_+) + M^(Δ_-))`, the connecting map sending `f ∈ M^(Δ_0)` to the class of the cocycle
  `c_f` with `c_f = 0` on `Δ_+` and `c_f(g) = (g - 1) f` on `Δ_-`.
  - Concretely, `g ↦ (0, g)` on `Δ_+` and `g ↦ ((g - 1) f, g) = (-f, 1)(0, g)(f, 1)` on `Δ_-` are homomorphisms into
    `M ⋊ Δ` that agree on `Δ_0` (there `(g - 1) f = 0`). The amalgam property gives `Γ -> M ⋊ Δ`, whose first
    coordinate is `c_f`.
- By (DG) and (T) for `Δ`, `H^1(Δ; M) = 0`. So by (H) the restriction `H^1(Γ; M) -> Hom_Γ(K, M)` is injective.
  `Hom_Γ(K, M)` is `Hom_Δ(K^ab, M)`, since `K` acts trivially on `M`.

## Step 5. The explicit regular charge on `kappa`

Take `M = l^2(Δ)` with the left regular action and `f = 1_(Δ_0)`. It is `Δ_0`-invariant, since `λ(g) 1_(Δ_0) = 1_(gΔ_0)`.
Here `kappa = a b a^(-1) b^(-1) z^(-1)` with `a ∈ Δ_+`, `b ∈ Δ_-`, `z ∈ Δ_0 ⊆ Δ_+`. Multiplying in `M ⋊ Δ`:

```text
(0,a)·((b-1)f, b)·(0,a^(-1))·((b^(-1)-1)f, b^(-1))·(0,z^(-1))
   = ( a(b-1)f + aba^(-1)(b^(-1)-1)f ,  [a,b] z^(-1) ).
```

The second coordinate is `1` in `Δ`, because `[a, b] = x_13(s_1 t_1) = z` there. So

```text
c_f(kappa) = (ab - a + z - aba^(-1)) f = 1_(abΔ_0) - 1_(aΔ_0) + 1_(Δ_0) - 1_(aba^(-1)Δ_0).
```

The indicators of distinct cosets are linearly independent. The coset `aΔ_0` differs from the other three:
- `aΔ_0 = Δ_0` would give `a ∈ Δ_0`;
- `aΔ_0 = abΔ_0` would give `b ∈ Δ_0`;
- `aΔ_0 = aba^(-1)Δ_0` would give `b ∈ Δ_0 a ⊆ Δ_+`.

All three are excluded by Step 0. So the coefficient of `1_(aΔ_0)` is `-1` and `c_f(kappa) ≠ 0`. Hence `kappa ≠ 1`
in `Γ`, `K ≠ 1`, and `f ↦ c_f|_K` embeds `l^2(Δ_0\Δ) = M^(Δ_0)` into `Hom_Δ(K^ab, l^2 Δ)`. Here
`M^(Δ_+-) = 0`, because `Δ_+-` is infinite (it contains the distinct `x_12(s_1^k)`), and an `l^2` function
invariant under left translation by an infinite group is `0`.

## What is and is not claimed

- Established: (1)–(5) of the target, with the imports above.
- Not claimed: finite normal generation of `K` by Steinberg relators (the next step of the cone-cover node), and
  anything about finite-dimensional `M`. For those, `Hom_Δ(K^ab, M)` is controlled by
  `M^(Δ_0)/(M^(Δ_+) + M^(Δ_-))` together with `ker(H^2(Δ; M) -> H^2(Γ; M))`, and neither is computed here.
