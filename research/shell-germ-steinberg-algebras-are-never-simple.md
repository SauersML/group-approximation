**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, not reviewed; no priority claimed).

## Setting

`P` is countably infinite, `nu : P -> N` is a bijection, and `rho_g` is the transferred regular
action on the even integers, fixing every odd integer
(`shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`). On `C = {0,1}^N`, `p = 0^∞` and
`C_n = 0^n 1 C`, and `hat(g)` sends `0^n 1 z` to `0^(rho_g(n)) 1 z`. Let `E_nu = <V, hat(P)>`,
let `G_nu` be its germ groupoid, and let `K` be a field. In `A = A_K(G_nu)` write `u_g` for the
indicator of the germ class of `hat(g)`, `s_0` for that of `z |-> 0z`, and `t_0 = s_0^*`. Let
`τ` be the germ at `p` of `z |-> 0z` and `r_g` that of `hat(g)`, so `τ, r_g` lie in the germ
group `Q` at `p`.

## Statement

Fix `g ∈ P`, `g ≠ 1`, and put

    f_g = (1 - u_g) s_0 (1 - u_g) t_0  ∈  A.

1. `f_g ≠ 0`. As a function on `G_nu` it is supported on the four germs
   `1, r_g, τ r_g τ^(-1), r_g τ r_g τ^(-1)` over `p`, with values `1, -1, -1, 1`.
2. The two-sided ideal generated by `f_g` is proper. So `A` is not simple, and `G_nu` is not
   Hausdorff.
3. `f_g` acts as zero on the orbit module `K[Ω]`, where `Ω = V·p`, and on the fibre module
   `K[s^(-1)(x)]` for every `x ∉ Ω`. It acts as a nonzero operator on the fibre module
   `K[s^(-1)(p)]`, the free right `K[Q]`-module of
   `shell-germ-algebra-fp-forces-bounded-germ-presentation`.
4. Let `J` be the singular ideal of `A` (functions whose support has empty interior). Then
   `E_nu -> (A/J)^x`, `h |-> u_h + J`, is injective.

## Proof

**Shells.** Every map in sight sends a shell `C_n` onto a shell `C_m` by
`0^n 1 z |-> 0^m 1 z`. So on `C_n` it is determined by `m`, and two of them have the same germ
at a point of `C_n` exactly when they send `n` to the same `m`. On the level of shells,
`hat(g)` acts by `rho_g`: it fixes the odd shells and permutes the even shells without fixed
points, since the regular action is free. The partial map `k = s hat(g) s^(-1)`, defined on
`[0]`, sends shell `n >= 1` to `rho_g(n-1) + 1`. So `k` fixes the even shells and permutes the
odd shells without fixed points. In the algebra, `1_([k])` is `s_0 u_g t_0`.

**1.** Expand `f_g = s_0 t_0 - u_g s_0 t_0 - s_0 u_g t_0 + u_g s_0 u_g t_0`: four bisections,
each over the domain `[0]`, of the maps `id, hat(g), k, hat(g) k`. Take `x ∈ [0]`, `x ≠ p`, so
`x ∈ C_n` with `n >= 1`.
- `n` odd. Then `hat(g)` fixes `C_n`, and it fixes `k(C_n)`, which is odd. So
  `[id, x] = [hat(g), x]` and `[k, x] = [hat(g) k, x]`. The coefficients cancel in pairs.
- `n` even. Then `k` fixes `C_n`, so `[id, x] = [k, x]` and `[hat(g), x] = [hat(g) k, x]`.
  Again they cancel in pairs.

If more germs coincide, the total over each germ is still zero, since the four coefficients
sum to zero. So `f_g` vanishes at every germ whose source is not `p`. Over `p` the four germs
are pairwise distinct in `Q`: on large shells, `r_g` moves infinitely many even shells and
fixes the odd ones; `τ r_g τ^(-1)` moves infinitely many odd shells and fixes the even ones;
`r_g τ r_g τ^(-1)` moves infinitely many of each. Two germs at `p` are equal only if they
agree on all large shells. So `f_g` takes the values `1, -1, -1, 1` there, and `f_g ≠ 0`.

**2.** For compact open bisections `B, B'`, the product `1_B f_g 1_(B')` is supported in
`B · supp(f_g) · B'`, whose elements have source in the orbit `Ω` of `p`. So every element
of the ideal `(f_g)` is supported in `s^(-1)(Ω)`, which is countable: `Ω` is countable and
`G_nu` is étale and second countable. The unit `1 = 1_C` has uncountable support. So
`0 ≠ (f_g) ≠ A`. By `cepss-nonhausdorff-steinberg-simplicity` (S), or directly from this
ideal, `A` is not simple. Non-Hausdorffness: points `x_j ∈ C_(2j+1)` tend to `p`, and the
germs `[id, x_j] = [hat(g), x_j]` converge both to `[id, p]` and to `[hat(g), p] ≠ [id, p]`.

**3.** On a fibre module, `f_g δ_γ` is the sum of `f_g(β) δ_(βγ)` over `β` with source
`r(γ)`. By 1 this is `0` unless `r(γ) = p`, which never happens in the fibre over `x ∉ Ω`. On
`K[Ω]`, `f_g δ_y = Σ f_g(β) δ_(r(β))` over `β` with source `y`. This is `0` for `y ≠ p`. For
`y = p` the four germs lie in the isotropy group, so it is `(1 - 1 - 1 + 1) δ_p = 0`. On
`K[s^(-1)(p)]`, by contrast, `f_g δ_1 = δ_1 - δ_(r_g) - δ_(τ r_g τ^(-1)) + δ_(r_g τ r_g τ^(-1)) ≠ 0`.

**4.** The map is a homomorphism by part 2 of
`germ-steinberg-algebras-over-v-are-fg-central-simple`. If `h ≠ 1`, then `h` moves some point,
hence every point of a nonempty open `U`. So `u_h - 1` takes the value `-1` at every unit
`[id, x]`, `x ∈ U`. That set is open in `G_nu`, so `u_h - 1 ∉ J`.

## What it changes

- Step 3 of `boone-higman-via-germ-steinberg-algebra-presentation` states that `A` is central
  simple. For shell envelopes this is false: the hypotheses of part 3 of
  `germ-steinberg-algebras-over-v-are-fg-central-simple` never hold here. The route needs
  a simple quotient. The natural one is `A/J`, which by item 4 still carries `E_nu` in its
  units. Two inputs are then needed and neither is proved here: simplicity of `A/J`, and
  finite presentation of `A/J`. Given a finite presentation of `A`, the second is
  equivalent to finite generation of `J` as a two-sided ideal.
- `f_g` is invisible in the orbit module `K[Ω]`, where near-permutation arguments live, and
  visible in the fibre module over `F_2[Q]` used by
  `shell-germ-algebra-fp-forces-bounded-germ-presentation`. So an algebra presentation of `A`
  must also carry the relations among germs at `p` that only the fibre module sees. Whether
  all of `J` acts as zero on `K[Ω]` is not addressed here.
