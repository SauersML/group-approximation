Let `A` be a unital ring. For an ideal `J` let `Phi(J)` be the ideal generated by `J` and the entries of
`BA - I_n`, over all `n` and all square `A, B` over `A` with `AB - I_n` having entries in `J`. Put `J_0 = 0`,
`J_(k+1) = Phi(J_k)` and `J_omega = ∪ J_k`. Then:
- `A / J_omega` is weakly finite;
- every homomorphism from `A` to a weakly finite ring kills `J_omega`, so `J_omega` is the smallest ideal with
  weakly finite quotient;
- `A` has a nonzero weakly finite image iff `1 ∉ J_omega`. When `1 ∈ J_omega` there is a finite certificate:
  finitely many pairs at finitely many stages;
- `J_1 = 0` iff `A` is weakly finite, and then `J_omega = 0`.

**For the ternary target.** `ternary-anti-central-summand-has-no-weakly-finite-image` is `1 ∈ J_omega(S_-)`.
Refuting Gottschalk on `L_(F_3)(1,2)^x` needs only `J_1(S_-) != 0`, a stable-finiteness failure of `F_3[G]`
(artifact Corollary 1.2).

The construction is presumably classical (Cohn; Malcolmson), but no source was read locally.
