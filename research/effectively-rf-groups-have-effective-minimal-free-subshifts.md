**ESTABLISHED (lane proof, bh-g1-effective, 2026-09-18; not independently reviewed).**

**Statement.** Let `Δ` be finitely generated, infinite, with solvable word problem, and
effectively residually finite. The last condition means there is a computable chain
`Δ_1 ≥ Δ_2 ≥ …` of finite-index normal subgroups, with decidable membership and
`∩_m Δ_m = {1}`. Then `Δ` carries a nonempty effectively closed subshift on which it acts
minimally and freely. That is (M2) of `decidable-fp-groups-have-effective-minimal-free-subshifts`
for `Δ`, without assuming finite presentation.

**Proof.** Let `X_Δ` be the Toeplitz subshift of
`rf-toeplitz-subshifts-over-residually-finite-groups`, built from the chain. Pass to a
computable subsequence if needed to meet that node's index bounds; the indices are computable
because membership is decidable.
- *Minimal:* by that node.
- *Free:* by `rf-toeplitz-subshifts-from-normal-chains-are-free`.
- *Decidable language:* by `rf-toeplitz-subshift-has-computable-language`, which is stated
  for exactly this setting, with a computable chain with decidable membership. Its review
  notes ask that every choice be the least admissible one in a computable enumeration.
- A subshift with decidable language is effectively closed: forbid the decidable set of
  non-occurring patterns. ∎

**Corollary 1 (fp residually finite groups).** Every infinite finitely presented residually
finite group satisfies (M2).
- By McKinsey–Mal'cev (classical), its word problem is solvable.
- *Chain:* enumerate all homomorphisms `φ_i` to finite symmetric groups; they can be
  enumerated because a map on generators is checked on the finitely many relators. Put
  `Δ_m := ∩_{i≤m} ker φ_i`.
- These are finite-index normal subgroups with decidable membership (evaluate `φ_i`), and
  `∩_m Δ_m = {1}` by residual finiteness.
- The indices are computable (the size of the image of `(φ_1,…,φ_m)`) and unbounded, so a
  computable subsequence meets any computable index bound.

**Corollary 2 (f.g. linear groups).** Every infinite finitely generated subgroup
`Δ ≤ GL_n(K)`, with `K` any field, satisfies (M2).
- Let `R` be the finitely generated ring generated by the matrix entries and inverse
  determinants. Its word problem is solvable by computing in `R`.
- `R` is a Jacobson ring whose maximal ideals have finite residue fields and intersect in
  `0` (classical). The maximal ideals, or powers of finitely many at a time, can be
  enumerated effectively.
- The congruence kernels `Δ ∩ ker(GL_n(R) → GL_n(R/𝔪_1⋯𝔪_m))` then form a computable normal
  chain with decidable membership and trivial intersection (Mal'cev).

**Corollary 3 (examples).** Every infinite group in the following classes satisfies (M2):
- free groups, surface groups;
- `SL_n(Z)`, `GL_n(Z[1/N])`, and every finitely generated subgroup of `GL_n(Q)`;
- polycyclic-by-finite groups;
- finitely generated metabelian groups: residually finite by P. Hall, and finitely presented
  in the metabelian variety, so their finite quotients can be enumerated.

Closure: finite direct products keep (M2)
(`decidable-fp-groups-have-effective-minimal-free-subshifts`, instance list).

**What this does NOT reach.** No infinite simple group is residually finite, and nor is any
group containing one, so this gives nothing for the envelopes that Boone–Higman actually
needs. Clapham envelopes of simple Kazhdan groups are never residually
finite: every nontrivial element of the simple subgroup dies in every finite quotient. The
first target `St_10(R_L)` has no nontrivial finite quotients at all, because it is generated
by the root subgroups `x_ij(R_L)`, which are Q-vector spaces and hence divisible.

**Lesson for general BH.** (M2) was listed on main with a handful of instances (`Z^d`,
products, groups carrying minimal free SFTs such as `F_n × Z`), but it already held for the
whole residually finite world. Its true content is
"free minimal effective dynamics with no finite quotients". Any construction for the Boone–Higman
envelopes must replace the profinite odometer skeleton by a non-profinite one; see
`free-products-with-z-inherit-m2-from-universal-points` for a Bass–Serre replacement.
