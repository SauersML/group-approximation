There is a finitely generated torsion-free property-`(T)` group `Gamma_0`
with epimorphisms onto alternating groups of unbounded degree.  Namely, take a
torsion-free finite-index normal subgroup of `Out(F_5)`.  Property `(T)` comes
from Kaluba--Nowak--Ozawa and finite-index permanence; unbounded alternating
quotients come from Gilman's residual-alternating theorem and remain
surjective after restriction to `Gamma_0`, apart from finitely many bounded
degrees.

Consequently every convergent subsequence of these finite marked quotients has
an infinite finitely generated LEF, hence sofic, property-`(T)` limit `K`, and
`K` is a quotient of `Gamma_0`.  For a sequence

```text
q_i : Gamma_0 ->> Alt(n_i),       n_i -> infinity,
```

the two extra properties needed for the desired seed have exact finite-model
gates:

```text
K is torsion-free
iff every persistent nonidentity word has unbounded order in q_i(Gamma_0);

K is simple
iff every persistent nonidentity word eventually has bounded normal-
    generation certificates for the marked generators, with both the number
    of conjugates and the marking lengths of their conjugators bounded.     (AMG1)
```

The bounds in the second line may depend on the persistent word.  Simplicity
would imply that every nonidentity element normally generates `K`, hence that
`K` has no nontrivial finite quotient.

In fact the two conditions in `(AMG1)` can never produce a simple limit here.
Every marked limit of finite groups is LEF, while every quotient of
`Gamma_0` has property `(T)`.  By
`exact-kazhdan-radical-kernel-cannot-be-lef`, a nontrivial LEF Kazhdan group
with no finite quotients cannot exist.  An infinite simple limit would have
no finite quotients, giving an immediate contradiction.  Thus along every
convergent alternating subsequence at least one persistent nonidentity word
must fail the bounded normal-certificate condition.  If the limit is also
torsion-free, that failure is necessarily on the simplicity side.

The cited constructions do not supply either `(AMG1)` condition separately.
In particular, abstract simplicity of each finite alternating quotient is not
enough: its normal-generation witnesses must escape every simultaneous bound
on factor count and marking length for at least one persistent word.  The
Ozawa--Thom obstruction proves that this escape is compulsory, not merely a
current limitation of alternating-quotient technology.

One must also pass to `Gamma_0`; the full `Aut(F_5)` or `Out(F_5)` cannot have
a nontrivial torsion-free quotient.  Bridson--Vogtmann prove that `Aut(F_n)` is
normally generated by an involution, and the same remains true in every
quotient.  Thus every nontrivial quotient retains a nontrivial involution.

Accordingly this is not a surviving alternate seed program.  No choice of
finite alternating quotient maps can satisfy the simplicity half of `(AMG1)`
for an infinite limit, and hence none can provide the no-finite-quotient
Kazhdan kernel forced by the exact-radical target.  A successful kernel must
be sofic but non-LEF and must use genuinely approximate permutation models.
