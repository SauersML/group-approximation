The group `SL_2(Z[t,t^{-1}])` is not finitely generated. This is the negative
answer to Zaremsky Problem 3.3 (`zaremsky-3-03-sl2-z-t-t-inverse-finitely-generated`).

By `sl2-laurent-integers-fg-iff-gamma0-t-in-fg-subgroup`, it is equivalent to: for
every finite set `F`, `SL_2(Z[t]) ⊄ <E_2(Z[t,t^{-1}]) ∪ F>`.

## Attempts

- **Congruence quotients (dead end, recorded).** A non-finitely-generated quotient of
  the form `SL_2(R/I)`, `R = Z[t,t^{-1}]`, does not exist. The quotients `R/I` are
  finite rings; `F_p[t,t^{-1}]` and its nilpotent thickenings; orders `Z[α,α^{-1}]`
  in number fields; or nilpotent thickenings of these. In each case `SL_2 = E_2`
  (Euclidean rings, rings of `S`-integers with infinitely many units by Vaserstein,
  or lifting along nilpotent ideals), and `E_2` is finitely generated by the same
  unit-conjugation trick. So `SL_2(Z[t]) → SL_2(F_2[t])`, which shows `SL_2(Z[t])` is
  not finitely generated, has no Laurent analogue. Any obstruction must see the
  two-dimensional ring.
- **Normal closure of E_2.** Since `E_2(R)` is finitely generated, a
  non-finitely-generated quotient of `SL_2(R)` by the normal closure of `E_2(R)`
  would give NO. Every element of `E_2(R)` is a product of unipotents, which fix
  vertices of `T_0 × T_∞`. So this quotient maps onto `π_1` of the quotient complex
  `Y = SL_2(R) \ (T_0 × T_∞)` (Armstrong), and `Y` is the double mapping cylinder
  of `SL_2(Z[t])\T_∞ ← Gamma_0(t)\T_∞ → D SL_2(Z[t]) D^{-1}\T_∞`
  (from `sl2-laurent-integers-splits-over-gamma0-t`). Computing `π_1(Y)` is the
  concrete next step.
