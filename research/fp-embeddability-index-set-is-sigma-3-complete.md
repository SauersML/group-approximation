**OPEN.** `EMB_fp = {P finite presentation : G_P` embeds in a finitely presented
simple group`}` is `Σ_3`-complete.

## What is proved (lane proofs, not reviewed; route `fp-embeddability-index-set-sigma-3-from-mihailova-hnn`)

1. **Bounds.** `EMB_fp ∈ Σ_3` (`boone-higman-is-equality-of-two-sigma-3-index-sets`).
   It is `Σ_1`-hard, hence not co-c.e.
2. **Under Boone–Higman** it is `Σ_3`-complete. This is item 4 of the index-set
   node.
3. **Under hypothesis MH it is `Σ_3`-complete.** MH is much weaker than
   Boone–Higman. Let `Q = ⟨X | R⟩` be a finite presentation with `|X| = m ≥ 2` and
   solvable word problem, and let `M(Q) = {(u,v) ∈ F_X × F_X : u =_Q v}` be its
   Mihailova subgroup. It is generated by the `(x,x)` and the `(r,1)`. MH says
   that the centralizing HNN extension
   `H(Q) = ⟨F_X × F_X, s | [s, c] = 1 (c ∈ M(Q))⟩` embeds in a finitely presented
   simple group.
   - MH is a special case of `bh-embeddability-survives-decidable-edge-hnn`. Here
     `A = F_m × F_m ≤ V`, and membership in `M(Q)` is decidable because it is
     equivalent to the word problem of `Q`.
   - Each `H(Q)` is finitely presented, with word problem Turing-equivalent to
     that of `Q`.

MH is now its own node, `mihailova-centralizing-hnn-extensions-embed-in-fp-simple-groups` (OPEN). That node proves MH implies `fp-simple-groups-with-arbitrarily-complex-word-problem`, so this route is at least as hard as that open root.

## Why the direct attack stops

The recursively presented witnesses `G_U` lie in `DV_F` when `U` is cofinite
(`circulant-graph-wreath-products-embed-in-dv-f-proof`). But the embedding uses
the finite complement `E = N_{≥1} ∖ U`, which is not computable from an index for
`U`.
- **Machines are needed.** A uniformly finitely presented family must carry a
  mechanism that enumerates `U`. The Higman–Clapham machinery does this, and so
  does Mihailova's subgroup of such a group.
- **What remains.** Its cofinite members must still be shown to embed. MH isolates
  the smallest such instance found here: one HNN extension of `F_m × F_m`.
