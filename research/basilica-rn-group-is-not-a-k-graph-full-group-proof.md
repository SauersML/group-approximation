## Conventions

- Notation is as in `torsion-germ-rn-groups-are-not-k-graph-full-groups-proof` (cited below as **[TG]**).
- For `w, v ∈ X^*` and `h ∈ H`, the map `λ_w h λ_v^{-1}` sends `v u ↦ w h(u)`, on the cylinder `vX^ω`.
- `B(w, h, v) ⊆ 𝒢_H` is the set of its germs at the points of `vX^ω`. These *basic bisections* form a basis of
  compact open bisections of `𝒢_H`.
- Homology of an ample groupoid `G` is Crainic–Moerdijk/Matui homology, computed by the complex
  `C_c(G^(n), Z)` with `∂ = Σ_i (-1)^i (d_i)_*`. In degree 1, `∂_1 f = s_* f - r_* f`. On composable pairs,
  `d_0(γ_1, γ_2) = γ_2`, `d_1(γ_1, γ_2) = γ_1 γ_2` and `d_2(γ_1, γ_2) = γ_1`.
- The pushforward `(d_i)_*` of the indicator of a set on which `d_i` is injective is the indicator of the image.
- Homology is an invariant of isomorphism of topological groupoids.

## Step 1. Every arrow of `G_Λ` lies in a full bisection (Lemma B')

Let `G` be an ample Hausdorff groupoid whose unit space `Y` is a Cantor set, and suppose every orbit has at least two
points. Then every arrow of `G` lies in some full compact open bisection.

*Proof.*
- **Units.** They lie in `Y`, which is a full bisection.
- **An arrow `γ : x → y` with `x ≠ y`.** Take a compact open bisection `U_0 ∋ γ`, and write `α` for its partial
  homeomorphism, so that `α(x) = y ≠ x`.
  - `Y` is Hausdorff and `α` is continuous, so there is a clopen `W ∋ x` inside `s(U_0)` with `W ∩ α(W) = ∅`.
  - `Y` has no isolated points, so shrinking `W` gives `W ∪ α(W) ≠ Y`.
  - Put `U = U_0 W`. Then `U ∪ U^{-1} ∪ (Y \ (W ∪ α(W)))` is a compact open bisection, and it is full. It contains
    `γ`.
- **A non-unit isotropy arrow `γ` at `x`.** Choose `y ≠ x` in the orbit of `x`, and an arrow `δ : x → y`. Then
  `γ = (γ δ^{-1}) δ`, and both factors join distinct points. Each lies in a full bisection, by the previous case, and
  a product of full bisections is full. ∎

**For `G_Λ` with (H).** `Λ^∞` is a Cantor set, and `G_Λ` is ample and Hausdorff ([TG], imported items 3–4). For the
orbit condition, let `x ∈ Λ^∞` and `v = r(σ^{e_1} x)`.
- There are at least two colour-1 edges `ε ≠ ε'` with source `v`.
- The paths `ε σ^{e_1} x` and `ε' σ^{e_1} x` are distinct, since their initial segments of degree `e_1` differ.
- Both are joined to `x` by arrows of lag `0`, since `σ^{e_1}(ε σ^{e_1} x) = σ^{e_1} x`.
- So at least one of them is a point of the orbit of `x` other than `x`.

**Consequence (part 1).** Suppose `φ : V_d(H) → F(G_Λ)` is an isomorphism.
- By [TG] Lemma C and Rubin's theorem, `φ` is spatial, so `𝒢_H ≅ Germ(F(G_Λ) ↷ Λ^∞)`.
- By [TG] Lemma B, the map `Θ` identifies the right-hand side with the union of all full bisections of `G_Λ`.
  Lemma B uses effectiveness, which follows from aperiodicity by [TG] item 4.
- By Lemma B', that union is all of `G_Λ`.

So `𝒢_H ≅ G_Λ` as topological groupoids, and `H_*(𝒢_H) ≅ H_*(G_Λ)`. In particular `𝒢_H` is Hausdorff. ∎

## Step 2. `H_0(𝒢_H)` is a quotient of `Z/(d-1)`

`H_0(𝒢_H) = C(X^ω, Z) / ⟨1_{s(U)} - 1_{r(U)}⟩`, where `U` runs over compact open bisections.
- `C(X^ω, Z)` is spanned by the indicators of cylinders.
- The bisection `B(w, 1, ∅)` has source `X^ω` and range `wX^ω`. So every cylinder has the class `c = [1_{X^ω}]`.
- `1_{X^ω} = Σ_x 1_{xX^ω}` gives `c = d c`.

So `H_0(𝒢_H)` is generated by `c`, with `(d-1)c = 0`. Hence `H_0(𝒢_H) ⊗ Q = 0`. ∎

## Step 3. For a finite `k`-graph, `H_0(G_Λ; Q) = 0` forces `H_*(G_Λ; Q) = 0`

**Import (FKPS).** Farsi–Kumjian–Pask–Sims, arXiv:1808.07807: for a row-finite `k`-graph with no sources,
`H_*(G_Λ)` is the homology of the Koszul complex `K(1 - M_1^t, ..., 1 - M_k^t)` on `Z^{Λ^0}`. Here
`M_i(v, w) = |vΛ^{e_i}w|`.
- The chain groups are `D_n = Λ^n(Z^k) ⊗ Z^{Λ^0}`.
- The matrices commute, `M_i M_j = M_j M_i`, by the factorisation property.
- This is the form used in `rn-lamplighter-is-f-infinity-and-acyclic-via-a-2-graph-proof` §4.
- A finite `Λ` with (H) is row-finite and has no sources.

**Lemma K.** Let `N_1, ..., N_k` be commuting endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space `V` over a field
`F`, and let `K = K(N_1, ..., N_k; V)` be their Koszul complex. If `H_0(K) = V / Σ_i N_i V = 0`, then `H_n(K) = 0`
for all `n`.

*Proof.* The Koszul complex commutes with field extension, so we may assume `F` is algebraically closed. Then
`V = ⊕_c V_c` is the sum of the joint generalised eigenspaces, indexed by `c = (c_1, ..., c_k)`. Each `V_c` is
invariant under every `N_i`, and `K` splits as `⊕_c K(N_1, ..., N_k; V_c)`.

- **Some `c_j ≠ 0`.** Then `N_j` is invertible on `V_c`. The complex `K(N; V_c)` is the mapping cone of `N_j`
  acting on `K' = K(N_i, i ≠ j; V_c)`. Since `N_j` commutes with the `N_i`, it acts on `K'` by a chain map, and
  that map is invertible: `N_j^{-1}` is a polynomial in `N_j`. So it induces an isomorphism on `H_*(K')`, and the
  cone is acyclic.
- **`c = 0`.** Every `N_i` is nilpotent on `V_0`. Now `H_0(K(N; V_0))` is a direct summand of `H_0(K) = 0`, so
  `V_0 = Σ_i N_i V_0`. Iterating gives `V_0 = Σ_{|I| = m} N^I V_0` for every `m`, where `N^I` runs over the
  monomials of degree `m`. For `m ≥ k·dim V_0`, each monomial contains some `N_i` to a power at least `dim V_0`, so
  it is `0`. Hence `V_0 = 0`.

So every summand of `K` is acyclic. ∎

Apply Lemma K with `F = Q`, `V = Q^{Λ^0}` and `N_i = 1 - M_i^t`, using `H_*(K ⊗ Q) = H_*(K) ⊗ Q`. If
`H_0(G_Λ) ⊗ Q = 0`, then `H_n(G_Λ; Q) = 0` for all `n`.

**Consequence (part 2).** If `V_d(H) ≅ F(G_Λ)`, Steps 1 and 2 give `H_0(G_Λ) ⊗ Q ≅ H_0(𝒢_H) ⊗ Q = 0`. So
`H_n(𝒢_H; Q) ≅ H_n(G_Λ; Q) = 0` for every `n`. ∎

`section_characters.py` (part C) checks Lemma K on 400 random commuting pairs `(A, p(A))` over `Q`. It is a
calibration only.

## Step 4. A section character is a rational functional on `H_1(𝒢_H)`

Assume `𝒢_H` is Hausdorff. Under the hypothesis of part 3 this follows from Step 1. Let `ψ` be a section character.

**(a) Basic bisections are unique and refine.**
- **Uniqueness.** `B(w, h, v) = B(w', h', v')` as sets forces `(w, h, v) = (w', h', v')`. The source cylinders
  agree, so `v = v'`. The germs agree at every point, so the maps agree on `vX^ω`. Their images are equal
  cylinders, so `w = w'`. Then `h = h'`, because the action is faithful.
- **Refinement.** `B(w, h, v) = ⊔_{x ∈ X} B(w h(x), h|_x, v x)`, and `Σ_x ψ(h|_x) = ψ(h)`.
- **Iterated refinement.** Refining to depth `N` keeps the sum: `Σ_{|u| = N} ψ(h|_u) = ψ(h)`.

**(b) Decompositions.** Every compact open `C ⊆ 𝒢_H` is a finite disjoint union of basic bisections.
- `C` is a finite union of basic bisections, since these form a basis.
- Two basic bisections, refined to a common source cylinder `uX^ω`, meet in the set of germs at the points where
  their maps agree near the point. This set is open, and in the Hausdorff groupoid it is also closed. So it is
  clopen in `uX^ω`, hence a finite union of cylinders.
- Refining along those cylinders makes the pieces either equal or disjoint.

**(c) Definition.** For `f = Σ_j n_j 1_{B_j}`, with `B_j = B(w_j, h_j, v_j)` pairwise disjoint, put
`Ψ(f) = Σ_j n_j ψ(h_j)`.
- Two such decompositions have a common refinement.
- By (a), if a basic `B` is partitioned into basic `B_1, ..., B_m`, then refining everything to a common depth
  turns each `B_i` into some of the depth-`N` pieces of `B`. Here uniqueness is used.
- So `Σ_i ψ(h_i) = Σ_{|u|=N} ψ(h|_u) = ψ(h)`, and `Ψ : C_c(𝒢_H, Z) → Q` is a well-defined homomorphism.

**(d) `Ψ` kills boundaries.**
- `C_c(𝒢_H^(2), Z)` is spanned by indicators of sets
  `E(B, B') = {(γ_1, γ_2) ∈ B × B' : s(γ_1) = r(γ_2)}`, with `B = B(w, h, v)` and `B' = B(v, h', u)`, so that
  `s(B) = r(B')`.
  - A compact open subset of `𝒢^(2)` is a finite union of sets `(B × B') ∩ 𝒢^(2)`.
  - Refine `B'` until each piece has range inside or disjoint from `s(B)`.
  - Restrict `B` to that range. The restriction of `B(w, h, v)` to `vzX^ω` is `B(w h(z), h|_z, vz)`, still basic.
  - Disjointify as in (b).
- Each `d_i` is injective on `E(B, B')`, with images `B'`, `BB'` and `B`. Also `BB' = B(w, h h', u)`, because
  `λ_w h λ_v^{-1} λ_v h' λ_u^{-1} = λ_w h h' λ_u^{-1}`.
- So `Ψ(∂ 1_E) = ψ(h') - ψ(hh') + ψ(h) = 0`.

Hence `Ψ` induces a homomorphism `Ψ_* : H_1(𝒢_H; Z) → Q`.

**(e) A cycle it detects.** For `h ∈ H`, the full bisection `U_h = B(∅, h, ∅)` has `s_* 1_{U_h} = 1_{X^ω} =
r_* 1_{U_h}`. So `1_{U_h}` is a 1-cycle, and `Ψ_*[1_{U_h}] = ψ(h)`.
- If `ψ(h) ≠ 0`, the class `[1_{U_h}]` has infinite order.
- Hence `H_1(𝒢_H; Q) = H_1(𝒢_H; Z) ⊗ Q ≠ 0`.

**Consequence (part 3).** Suppose `V_d(H) ≅ F(G_Λ)` and `H` has a nonzero section character. Step 1 gives Hausdorff
`𝒢_H`. Step 4 gives `H_1(𝒢_H; Q) ≠ 0`. Step 3 gives `H_1(𝒢_H; Q) = 0`. This is a contradiction. ∎

*Remark.* `Ψ_*` composed with Matui's index map `F(𝒢_H) → H_1(𝒢_H)` is the map `V_d(H) → Q` given by
`g ↦ Σ_i ψ(h_i)` over the pieces `λ_{w_i} h_i λ_{v_i}^{-1}` of `g`. This matches (Ab) of
`rover-nekrashevych-finite-presentation-criteria`. The proof above does not use (Ab) or the index map.

## Step 5. The basilica exponent sum is a section character

Let `a = σ(1, b)` and `b = (1, a)`, so `a(0w) = 1w`, `a(1w) = 0 b(w)`, `b(0w) = 0w` and `b(1w) = 1 a(w)`. The sections of
the letters are:

| letter | `a` | `a⁻¹` | `b` | `b⁻¹` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| active point `p(l)` | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| section at `p(l)` | `b` | `b⁻¹` | `a` | `a⁻¹` |
| image of `p(l)` | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

At the other point, the section is trivial.

**(a) Syntactic splitting.** Let `W = l_1 ⋯ l_n` be a word, applied right to left. Its syntactic section at `x` is
`W|_x = l_1|_{x_1} ⋯ l_n|_{x_n}`, where `x_n = x` and `x_{i-1} = l_i(x_i)`. For fixed `i`, the map `x ↦ x_i` is a
bijection of `X`. So letter `l_i` contributes one letter to exactly one of `W|_0` and `W|_1`, and that letter has the
same exponent. Writing `ε` for the exponent sum on free words, this gives `|W|_0| + |W|_1| = |W|` and
`ε(W) = ε(W|_0) + ε(W|_1)`.

**(b) `ε` is well defined on `B`.** Suppose some word that is trivial in `B` has `ε ≠ 0`. Choose such a `W` of minimal
length.
- Free reduction changes neither triviality nor `ε`, so `W` is freely reduced.
- Both `W|_0` and `W|_1` are trivial in `B`, and by (a) one of them, say `W|_x`, has `ε ≠ 0`.
- Minimality gives `|W|_x| ≥ |W|`, so by (a) every letter contributes to branch `x`. That is, `x_i = p(l_i)` for all `i`.

So each `l_{i-1}` has active point equal to the image of `p(l_i)` under `l_i`. From the table:
- after `a` (image 0), only `a⁻¹` can follow, which is not freely reduced. So `a` can only be `l_1`;
- before `a⁻¹` (active point 0), only `a` can come, which is not freely reduced. So `a⁻¹` can only be `l_n`;
- the rest are `b^{±1}`, and being freely reduced, they form `b^m`.

This leaves four shapes.
- `W = a b^m`: it moves `1` to `0`, so it is not trivial.
- `W = b^m a⁻¹`: it moves `0` to `1`, so it is not trivial.
- `W = a b^m a⁻¹`: it is conjugate to `b^m`, which is trivial with `ε = m ≠ 0` and is shorter. This contradicts minimality.
- `W = b^m` with `m ≠ 0`: then `W|_1 = a^m` is also a minimal counterexample. For `|m| ≥ 2` it has `a` or `a⁻¹` in a
  forbidden position, and for `|m| = 1` it is not trivial.

Every shape is a contradiction, so `ε` descends to a homomorphism `ψ : B → Z ⊂ Q` with `ψ(a) = ψ(b) = 1`.

**(c) Section invariance.** For `h ∈ B` represented by `W`, the section `h|_x` is represented by `W|_x`. So (a) gives
`ψ(h) = ψ(h|_0) + ψ(h|_1)`. Hence `ψ` is a nonzero section character, and part 3 excludes `V_2(B)` for every `k ≥ 1`. ∎

The script's part B checks (b) independently: all 16 nonempty trivial reduced words of length `≤ 8` have `ε = 0`.
Part A shows that the section characters of `B` form exactly the line `Q·ψ`.

## Step 6. Odometer, and calibration

- **Odometer.** `a = σ(1, a)` generates `H ≅ Z`, since `a` is `+1` on the 2-adic integers and has infinite order.
  - `ψ(a^n) = n` is well defined, and (a) of Step 5 holds verbatim, with each letter contributing one letter.
  - So `ψ` is a section character: `ψ(a) = 1 = ψ(1) + ψ(a)`.
  - Hence `V_2(odometer)` is not a finite `k`-graph full group, for any `k ≥ 1`.
- **Calibration.** A section character forces `ψ(g) = Σ_x ψ(g|_x)` on the generators. The script solves these
  equations, together with `ψ(g) = 0` for involutions and `ψ(bcd) = 0` for Grigorchuk.
  - Lamplighter `a = σ(a, b)`, `b = (a, b)`: the only solution is 0. This is consistent with its 2-graph model in
    `rn-lamplighter-is-f-infinity-and-acyclic-via-a-2-graph`.
  - Grigorchuk and Hanoi: the only solution is 0. They are excluded anyway, by torsion germs.
  - Thompson's `V` (trivial `H`): there are no characters. This is consistent with `V` being the full group of the
    2-rose.
- **Part C of the script** tests the linear algebra of Step 3 on 400 random commuting pairs. `H_0 = 0` always forced
  `H_1 = H_2 = 0`.
