ESTABLISHED.  Let `K` be an infinite compact group and let
`sigma_1,...,sigma_r` be finite-dimensional continuous unitary
representations.  On the central Hilbert space

```text
H=L^2(K)^Ad(K)                                           (FTF1)
```

define the normalized fusion operators

```text
T_i f=(chi_(sigma_i)/dim(sigma_i)) f.                   (FTF2)
```

In the orthonormal irreducible-character basis, `(FTF2)` is precisely
tensoring a type by `sigma_i`, divided by its dimension:

```text
chi_(sigma_i) chi_theta
  =sum_eta N_(sigma_i,theta)^eta chi_eta.               (FTF3)
```

For every `epsilon>0` and every finite-dimensional subspace `E` spanned by
irreducible characters, there is a unit vector `f` with **finite character
support**, orthogonal to `E`, such that

```text
max_i ||T_i f-f||_2<epsilon.                            (FTF4)
```

Indeed the class functions `chi_(sigma_i)/dim(sigma_i)` are continuous and
equal one at the identity.  Hence there is a conjugation-invariant open
neighborhood `U` of the identity on which all of them differ from one by at
most `epsilon/4`.  Since `K` is infinite, Haar measure is nonatomic; choose
such neighborhoods with measure tending to zero.  The normalized central
vectors

```text
g_U=1_U/sqrt(mu(U))                                     (FTF5)
```

then satisfy `||T_i g_U-g_U||_2<=epsilon/4` and converge weakly to zero.
Choose `U` so that the projection of `g_U` onto `E` is arbitrarily small.
Central Peter--Weyl density approximates the remaining vector by a finite
linear combination of irreducible characters.  Since `||T_i||<=1`, the
approximation and orthogonal projection change each defect by less than
`3epsilon/4`, proving `(FTF4)` after normalization.

Consequently no finite family of fixed tensor packets has a positive
Poincare/spectral-gap constant on the finite-dimensional type space, even
after deleting any fixed finite set of exceptional types.  Equivalently,
the dimension-weighted fusion graph admits arbitrarily remote finitely
supported almost-invariant vectors.  If the compact type closure is finite
instead, it contains no unbounded scale family in the first place.

This applies to the finite-dimensional types of any discrete group by
passing to their compact (Bohr) closure.  Thus property `(T)` of the discrete
actor and the square-function estimate of
`kazhdan-type-blocks-pack-a-square-function` do not turn ordinary tensor
branching into the strict recurrence needed by the dyadic terminal seam.
A successful type actuator must use structure not generated by finitely many
fixed fusion operators--for example an expanding endomorphism of the type
parameter, a separated nonamenable incidence action, or a matrix-only
proper-corner transition.
