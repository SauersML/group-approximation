Let `A` be a unital C\*-algebra, `X` a compact Hausdorff space, and
`theta: C(X) -> Z(A)` a unital star homomorphism.  For `x in X` let `J_x` be the
closed two-sided ideal of `A` generated by `theta(C_0(X \ {x}))`.  Suppose every
fiber `A_x = A/J_x` is exact.  Then every bounded 2-quasitrace on `A` is a trace.

This strengthens Haagerup's theorem that quasitraces on exact unital C\*-algebras
are traces (Theorem 5.11 of *Quasitraces on exact C\*-algebras are traces*,
C. R. Math. Rep. Acad. Sci. Canada 36 (2014)).

- Take `X` to be a point.  Then `J_x = 0` and the hypothesis is exactness of `A`.
- Quotients of exact algebras are exact, so every exact `C(X)`-algebra is fiberwise
  exact.
- Fiberwise exactness does not imply exactness.  A standard example is sketched in
  §5 of the artifact over `N ∪ {infinity}`, with matrix fibers and `C*_r(F_2)` at
  infinity; that sketch cites Effros--Haagerup and Rosenberg without re-reading
  them and is not load-bearing.

The proof is one line.  Haagerup's theorem makes each nonzero fiber linear, and
`stw01-central-cx-fibers-preserve-qt-linearity` lifts linearity from all fibers to
`A`.

**Scope.**  No example has been certified to lie outside the union of all classes
already settled in the graph, namely exact algebras, ideal extensions, directed
limits, and central-projection gluings.  The example above is also an extension of
an exact quotient by a nuclear ideal.  The same argument, combined with
`stw01-matrix-product-algebras-have-qt-linearity`, also allows fibers that are
quotients of products of matrix algebras.  That variant is recorded in the artifact
(Corollary 6.3) and is not asserted by this node.
