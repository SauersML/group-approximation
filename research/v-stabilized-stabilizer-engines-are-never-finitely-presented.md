**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary; bh-star-b, 2026-09-18; not reviewed).
- **Dependencies.** It rests on `topologically-free-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`,
  itself an unreviewed lane proof.
- **Scope.** It does **not** decide finite presentation of the engine `D(F_B)` itself, or of Reid's
  group (k = 2, B = Z, MathOverflow 339541).

## Setting

- **(a) The engine.** `D = Z/k * B`, with `B` infinite and generated by a finite set `S_B`, and
  generating set `(Z/k \ 1) ∪ S_B`. `T` is the Bass–Serre tree: vertices `D/A ⊔ D/B` with
  `A = Z/k`, edges `D`, and edge `d` joining `dA` to `dB`.
  - `X_B` is the set of ends of `T` together with its `B`-vertices, as in
    `free-product-boundary-groupoids-carry-the-vertex-group-homology`, coded as a subshift of
    `{0,1}^D` by `c_x(d) = [x ∈ dH]`.
  - Here `H` is the half-tree of the edge `1` on the side of `u = 1A`.
  - `Δ(x, y)` is the set of edges separating `x` from `y`.
- **(b) The HNN form.** `D' = B * ⟨t⟩`. The tree has vertices `gB`, edges `g`, and edge `g` joining
  `gB` to `gtB`. `X'_B` is the set of ends together with all vertices, coded by half-tree indicators
  in the same way.
  - The dynamics are as in item 1 of the parent node: `X'_B` is a Cantor set, and every nontrivial
    element fixes at most two points (one vertex, if it is conjugate into `B`, or two ends).
  - So `X'_B` is an expansive, topologically free `D'`-space.

In both cases `B` embeds isometrically in the free product (normal forms), so `|β|_D = |β|_B` for
`β ∈ B`.

## Theorem

1. **No rigidity.** For every `D_0 >= 1` and every field, neither `X_B` nor `X'_B` is
   `D_0`-quantum rigid. The same holds for every subshift conjugate to either, and for every subshift
   that contains either as a closed invariant subset.
2. **No finite presentation after Cuntz stabilization.** Let `T_B = G_V × (D ⋉ X_B)`, with `t_s` and
   `e_a` as in `fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`.
   - No finitely presented `Γ ≤ F(T_B)` contains every `t_s` and every `e_a`.
   - In particular `F(T_B) = A(T_B)` is not finitely presented.
   - The same holds for `G_V × (D' ⋉ X'_B)`.

## Proof

1. **A homoclinic partner at a point with isotropy `B`.**
   - (a) Let `v = 1B` and fix `a ∈ A \ 1`. The path `v — u — av` crosses the edges `1` and `a`, so
     `Δ(v, a.v) = {1, a}`, a finite set. `Stab(v) = B` is infinite.
   - (b) Let `v = 1B`. The edge `1` joins `v` to `tB`, so `Δ(v, t.v) = {1}`, and `Stab(v) = B`.
   - Item (3) of the clustering theorem of `quantum-rigid-subshifts-cluster-the-modifications-of-each-point`
     applies. Explicitly, in case (a), take `β ∈ B` with `|β|_B > 2D_0`. Then `β.v = v` and
     `Δ(v, βa.v) = {β, βa}` lies at distance `|β|_B > 2D_0` from `{1, a}`, so Lemma 1 there gives a
     noncommuting `D_0`-quantum family on `k^2`.
   - Conjugates and supersystems are covered by Corollaries 1 and 4 there.
2. **The necessity theorem.** `D` and `D'` are finitely generated, and both actions are topologically
   free. So by part 1 of `topologically-free-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`, a finitely
   presented `Γ` containing every `t_s` and `e_a` would make the subshift `D_0`-quantum rigid for all
   large `D_0`, which contradicts item 1.
   - `F(T_B)` contains every `t_s` and `e_a`, and equals `A(T_B)` by the Corollary of
     `fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`. ∎

## What this closes, and what it leaves open

- **Emitter isotropy is dead on every rigid route.** Both master routes need (★′), a quantum-rigid
  subshift:
  - the ring route through `boone-higman-via-leavitt-units-of-rigid-topologically-free-sfts`;
  - the group route through P2′.

  So neither can use a subshift in which the input is the isotropy of an emitter vertex point. This
  applies to the stabilizer engine of `boone-higman-via-stabilizer-engines`, and to the "input vertex at
  an infinite emitter" form of Track A′ over `Λ_1 * Z` (case (b)). The free design of
  `free-products-with-z-inherit-m2-from-decidable-free-subshifts` escapes: its fibre data break the
  symmetry, and there the input acts freely.
- **Track C survives only without `V`.** `boone-higman-via-stabilizer-engines` needs `D(F_S)`, not its
  Cuntz stabilization, so it is not refuted.
  - Any proof that `D(F_S)` is finitely presented must use a feature that `× G_V` destroys.
  - One candidate: the engine's groupoid homology `H_n = H_n(S; Z)`, which the acyclic factor `G_V`
    kills.
  - A Brown or Stein–Farley argument that runs verbatim for the product with the Cuntz groupoid is
    excluded.
- **The model case for Reid's group.**
  - The local picture at a `B`-vertex point, for `B = Z`, is the one-point compactification of `Z`.
  - Its `V`-less full group is Houghton's `H_2`, which is not finitely presented (row 2 of the
    calibration in `quantum-rigid-subshifts-cluster-the-modifications-of-each-point`).
  - This is suggestive against finite presentation of Reid's group, not a proof: a finitely
    presented group can contain `H_2`, and `V` does.
- **Blow-ups.**
  - The engines `F_S^K` of `stabilizer-engines-embed-in-every-branch-compactification` replace the
    emitter by an `S`-compactification `K`.
  - The clustering test must then pass at the remainder points of `K`. For `S = Z` it passes for
    `Z ⊔ {±∞}` and fails for `Z⁺`.
  - For hard inputs `S` (one-ended, Kazhdan, no finite quotients), such a `K` needs infinite remainder
    (part 2 there), with end-type remainder points. Whether one exists is open.

## Lesson for general BH

The V-stabilized necessity theorem turns every non-rigidity certificate into a
non-finite-presentation theorem. Emitters always carry such a certificate: a point with infinite
isotropy whose neighbours differ from it on finitely many sites. So "put the hard input at an infinite
emitter" does not bypass geometry on any rigid route. Either the emitter is blown up into an end-type
compactification of the input, which asks the input for a compression boundary of its own, or the route
must be `V`-less and non-rigid, as `D(F_S)` is.
