**Conventions.** `K` is a computable field. A monoid with zero `M` has an
absorbing element `z`. The contracted algebra `K_0[M] = K[M]/Kz` has basis
`M \ {z}`, with `m . n = mn` if `mn != z`, and `0` otherwise.

1. **Monomial algebras are contracted monoid algebras with a character.** Let
   `W` be the free monoid on `x_1, ..., x_k`, and let `L` be the set of words
   with a factor in `F`.
   - **Degenerate case.** If the empty word lies in `F`, then `A = 0`, which is
     finitely presented with solvable word problem. Assume it does not.
   - **The ideal.** `(F)` is the span of `L`: that span is an ideal containing
     `F`, and every product `p f q` lies in `L`. Words form a basis of the free
     algebra. So a polynomial vanishes in `A` exactly when every word with a
     nonzero coefficient, after collecting terms, lies in `L`. Hence `A` has
     solvable word problem iff `L` is decidable.
   - **The monoid.** Let `M = (W \ L) ⊔ {z}`, with `u . v = uv` if `uv` is not in
     `L`, and `z` otherwise. This is the Rees quotient `W/L`, with a zero
     adjoined if `L` is empty. `K_0[M]` is isomorphic to `A` by `w -> w`.
   - **Its word problem.** As a semigroup, `M` is generated by
     `1, x_1, ..., x_k, z`, and its word problem reduces to `L`. Delete the
     letters `1`. A word containing `z` equals `z`. Any other word is some
     `w in W`, and it equals `z` iff `w in L`. Two words are equal iff both equal
     `z` or both give the same word outside `L`.
   - **The character.** `chi(1) = 1`, `chi(w) = 0` for nonempty `w`, and
     `chi(z) = 0` define a character of `K_0[M]`. It is multiplicative because
     `uv` is empty only when `u` and `v` both are.
2. **Envelope.** `M` is a finitely generated semigroup with solvable word
   problem. By `birget-semigroup-embedding-preserves-word-problem`, there are:
   - a finite alphabet `G`;
   - a finite set `R` of pairs of nonempty words over `G`;
   - an injective semigroup homomorphism `phi : M -> H = <G | R>`, where `H` has
     solvable word problem.
3. **The algebra of the envelope.** Let `B = K<G>/(u - v : (u,v) in R)`, a
   finitely presented algebra.
   - **Identify `B`.** Let `H^1` be `H` with a new identity adjoined. The monoid
     map `G* -> H^1` extends to a surjection `pi : K<G> -> K[H^1]`, and `pi`
     kills the relators. Collecting coefficients over the fibres shows that
     `ker pi` is spanned by the differences `w - w'` with `pi(w) = pi(w')`. Two
     distinct such words are nonempty and are joined by a chain of moves
     `p u q <-> p v q`, so `w - w'` lies in the ideal of the relators. Hence
     `B = K[H^1]`, and `H^1` is a basis of `B`.
   - **Its word problem.** Given a polynomial over `G`, sort its words into
     classes with the word problem of `H`; the empty word is a class of its own.
     The polynomial vanishes in `B` iff every class has coefficient sum `0`,
     which is decidable in `K`.
4. **Cancel the zero without a quotient.** Put `e = phi(z)`. For every `m in M`,
   `phi(m) e = phi(mz) = e = e phi(m)`, and `e` is idempotent.
   - **The map.** Define `psi : K_0[M] -> B` on the basis by `psi(m) = phi(m) - e`.
     The same formula gives `0` at `m = z`.
   - **Multiplicative.** For `m, n in M`,
     `(phi(m) - e)(phi(n) - e) = phi(mn) - e - e + e = phi(mn) - e`. So
     `psi(m) psi(n) = psi(mn)`, which is `0` when `mn = z`, and `psi` is an
     algebra homomorphism.
   - **Injective.** `psi(sum c_m m) = sum c_m phi(m) - (sum c_m) e`. The elements
     `phi(m)` for `m != z`, together with `e`, are distinct elements of `H`,
     because `phi` is injective, so they are linearly independent in `B`.
5. **Unital form.** `p = psi(1)` is an idempotent, and `p psi(a) = psi(a) = psi(a) p`.
   Given a character `chi`, set `psi'(a) = psi(a) + chi(a)(1 - p)`.
   - **Unital homomorphism.** It follows from `psi(a)(1 - p) = 0 = (1 - p) psi(a)`
     and `(1 - p)^2 = 1 - p`.
   - **Injective.** `p psi'(a) = psi(a)`.
   - **Without units.** For the non-unital category `psi` is already enough: it
     lands in `K[H]`, which is finitely presented as a non-unital algebra, and
     its word problem is decided in the same way.
6. **Conclusion.** Steps 2--5 give part 2 of the claim, and step 1 adds part 1.
   - **For `A_X`.** Here `F = {x y^n x : n in X}`, and `L` is decidable when `X`
     is, since a word has only finitely many factors of the form `x y^m x`.
   - **The unital map.** `chi(x) = chi(y) = 0`, so the unital embedding is
     `x -> phi(x) - e`, `y -> phi(y) - e`.
