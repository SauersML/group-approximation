Let `Gamma` be a group and `S <= Gamma` a subgroup such that

- `N ∩ S = 1` for every proper normal subgroup `N` of `Gamma`, and
- `r = |S \ Gamma / S|` is finite.

Then every strictly monotone chain `N_1 < N_2 < ... < N_m` of proper normal
subgroups of `Gamma` has `m <= r`. Consequently:

1. `Gamma` has both the maximal and the minimal condition on proper normal
   subgroups, and every proper normal subgroup is normally generated by at
   most `r` elements;
2. `Gamma` has a maximal normal subgroup `M`, the core `S` embeds in the
   simple group `Gamma/M`, and if `Gamma` is finitely presented then so is
   `Gamma/M`;
3. `Gamma` has a chief series, and every chief series has length at most `r`;
4. if `Gamma` is nonabelian then its center `Z(Gamma)` is finite.

The map `N |-> NS` sends proper normal subgroups injectively along chains
into subgroups `S <= K <= Gamma`, and each such `K` is a union of double
cosets containing `S e S`. That is why the bound is `r`.

## Consequence for the Chatterji--Kassabov hole

For a finitely presented property-(T) envelope `Gamma` with `Gamma = <<s>>`
and `s in S`, the hypothesis `N ∩ S = 1` is supplied by
`proper-normal-subgroups-miss-the-normally-generating-core`. If the core also
has finite bi-index, clause 2 yields a finitely presented simple Kazhdan
group `Gamma/M` containing `S`. So a positive answer to
`ck-envelope-simple-core-has-finite-bi-index` for an envelope forces a
positive answer to `ck-envelope-has-a-finitely-normally-generated-maximal-kernel`
for that same envelope.

The clause also gives an obstruction. **Any envelope with an infinite
strictly monotone chain of normal subgroups has infinite core bi-index, for
every choice of core.** Such a chain can come from a quotient, a congruence
filtration, or an infinite center. The invariant is the normal height of
`Gamma`. See `sandwich-ring-envelopes-have-infinite-core-bi-index` for the
application to every universal-sandwich marking.
