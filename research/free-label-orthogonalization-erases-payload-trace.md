Let `(M,tau)` be generated by unitaries `u_1,...,u_r`, let
`lambda(s_i)` be the canonical free generators of `L(F_r)`, and put

```text
V_i=u_i tensor lambda(s_i).                             (FLO1)
```

For every nontrivial reduced word `w in F_r`,

```text
(tau tensor tau_F)(w(V))
 =tau(w(u)) tau_F(lambda(w))=0.                         (FLO2)
```

Consequently the trace-preserving generated algebra `W*(V_1,...,V_r)` is a
copy of `L(F_r)`, independently of the payload algebra `M`.  In particular,
free-label orthogonalization can turn a non-CE payload trace into the
embeddable canonical free-group trace only because it has erased all payload
moments.

Adjoining the label unitaries

```text
L_i=1 tensor lambda(s_i)                                (FLO3)
```

recovers the payload exactly, since `V_i L_i^*=u_i tensor 1`, and gives

```text
W*(V_i,L_i:1<=i<=r)=M bar tensor L(F_r).                (FLO4)
```

But the enlarged tuple no longer has the canonical trace of `2r` independent
free generators: the reduced mixed word `V_iL_i^*` has trace `tau(u_i)`, and
more generally label-cancelling words recover every original payload moment.
Quotienting or identifying the labels to erase them has the same effect.

Hence one cannot simultaneously use free labels to make every nontrivial
word trace zero and retain a readable non-CE payload.  Any positive compiler
needs a selective return which cancels labels on the desired certificate but
does not restore all unwanted payload moments.
