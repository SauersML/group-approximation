Source: `numdam.org/item/10.1007/BF02684586.pdf`, downloaded on MSI 2026-09-13. The scan has no text layer, so pages
were rendered with Ghostscript and read as images. Journal pages below.

p. 60 (Introduction), verbatim:

> The results apply to function fields over finite fields as well as to number fields, and to any subring 𝒪 of
> "arithmetic type".

p. 71 (§3), verbatim:

> Throughout this section A denotes a Dedekind ring of arithmetic type defined by a finite set, S_∞, of primes in a
> global field k. This terminology as well as that to follow, is taken from the appendix on number theory, to which
> frequent reference will be made here.
>
> We shall call A *totally imaginary* if S_∞ consists of complex primes. This means that k is a totally imaginary
> number field, and that A is its ring of algebraic integers.

p. 94 (§4), verbatim:

> Let A be a commutative ring and let q be an ideal of A. E_n(A) denotes the subgroup of SL_n(A) generated by all
> "elementary" matrices, I + te_ij (t∈A, i≠j), and E_n(A, q) denotes the *normal* subgroup of E_n(A) generated by those
> with t∈q. This is a subgroup of SL_n(A, q) = ker(SL_n(A) → SL_n(A/q)).
>
> *Theorem 4.1.* — *Let A be a Dedekind ring, let q be an ideal of A, and suppose n ≥ 3.*
> *a)* E_n(A, q) = [SL_n(A), SL_n(A, q)].
> *b)* *Write* C_q(n) = SL_n(A, q)/E_n(A, q), *and let* κ : SL_n(A, q) → C_q(n) *be the natural epimorphism.* [...]

p. 95, verbatim:

> *Corollary 4.2.* — *For n ≥ 3 the natural maps* C_q(n) → C_q(n−1) *are isomorphisms.*
>
> *Corollary 4.3.* — *Suppose that A is of arithmetic type and that n ≥ 3.*
> *a)* SL_n(A) *is equal to* E_n(A) *and it is a finitely generated group, equal to its own commutator subgroup.*
> *b)* *If A is not totally imaginary then* C_q = {1} *for all* q.

p. 96, verbatim, on the function field case: "In the function field case, however, finite generation of SL_n(A)
(n ≥ 3) was only recently proved by O'Meara [20]". §3 treats the "Function field case" explicitly (p. 73).

**Application.** `A = F_q[t,t^{-1}]` is the ring of elements of `k = F_q(t)` integral outside `S_∞ = {t = 0, t = ∞}`.
Its primes in `S_∞` are not complex, so `A` is not totally imaginary, and Corollary 4.3 b) gives `C_q = {1}` for every
ideal `q`.
