# Extract the perfect qc graph algebra and audit the new embedding arrows

## 1. The 2025 source is already perfectly complete

For a finite nonlocal game \(G\), write \(\omega^*(G)\) for its
finite-dimensional tensor-product value and \(\omega^{\mathrm{co}}(G)\)
for its commuting-operator value.

Definition 6.14 of Lin's 2025 paper defines the
\(\delta\)-Bell-separation promise by

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_{\mathrm{yes}}
   &=\{G:\omega^*(G)=\omega^{\mathrm{co}}(G)\},\\
 L_{\mathrm{no}}
   &=\{G:|\omega^*(G)-\omega^{\mathrm{co}}(G)|\geq\delta\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1}
\]

Theorem 6.15 makes the \(1/2\)-problem RE-complete. More importantly for
the present route, the paragraph following the theorem specifies the
promise family used in the reduction. Its yes instances obey

\[
 \omega^*(G)=\omega^{\mathrm{co}}(G)=1,
 \tag{2}
\]

and its no instances obey

\[
 \omega^{\mathrm{co}}(G)=1,
 \qquad
 \omega^*(G)\leq\frac12.
 \tag{3}
\]

The games in both cases are synchronous, conditionally-linear-samplable
finite games. Lin also cites the pre-existing explicit separation theorem
to verify that the no set is nonempty. Choose one no instance. This gives
(PQB1) directly; no limiting value of an external completeness parameter
is involved.

If desired, replace the question distribution by a distribution with full
support on its finite support. This does not change perfect strategies. It
also cannot turn the tensor value into one: if strategies had loss tending
to zero for the reweighted distribution, their loss for the original
finite distribution would tend to zero as well, contradicting (3).

Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07162, Definition 6.14, Theorem 6.15, and the
paragraph immediately following Theorem 6.15.

## 2. Pass to one independent-set predicate

Let \(Q,A\) be the question and answer sets of \(G\). Its game graph has

\[
 V(X(G))=Q\times A,
 \tag{4}
\]

and \((q,a)\) is adjacent to \((q',a')\) exactly when at least one of

\[
 V(q,q';a,a')=0,
 \qquad
 V(q',q;a',a)=0
 \tag{5}
\]

holds. Set \(t=|Q|\) and let \(I=(X(G),t)\) be the \(t\)-independent-set
game.

The perfect forward map works in every tracial von Neumann algebra. If
\(\{P_q^a\}_a\) is the PVM for question \(q\), then on independent-set
question \(q\) answer the vertex \((q,a)\) using \(P_q^a\). When the
reverse predicate in (5) vanishes, traciality changes the corresponding
moment to the forward ordering. Thus all forbidden overlaps vanish.

For completeness, the exact reverse map from a perfect independent-set
strategy \(\{F_i^{(q,a)}\}_{(q,a)}\), \(1\leq i\leq t\), is

\[
 P_q^a=\sum_{i=1}^t F_i^{(q,a)}.
 \tag{6}
\]

The independent-set losing relations make distinct summands in (6)
orthogonal and make \(P_q^aP_q^{a'}=0\) for \(a\neq a'\). For each fixed
\(q\),

\[
 \sum_aP_q^a\leq1.
 \tag{7}
\]

Summing (7) over \(q\), while using that each of the \(t\) original PVMs
sums to one, gives

\[
 t=\sum_{i,q,a}F_i^{(q,a)}
  =\sum_{q,a}P_q^a\leq t.
 \tag{8}
\]

All positive deficits in (7) therefore vanish, so each
\(\{P_q^a\}_a\) is a PVM. The remaining graph-edge relations imply all
losing relations of \(G\). This is the proof of Theorem 3.1 of
Mancinska--Spaas--Spirig--Vernooij and applies without a dimension
assumption.

Their Remark 3.2 and Theorem A give the approximate reverse direction:
in particular,

\[
 \omega^*(I)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\omega^*(G)=1.
 \tag{9}
\]

The forward construction and compactness of the commuting correlation set
give \(\omega^{\mathrm{co}}(I)=1\), while (3) and (9) give
\(\omega^*(I)<1\). Their Theorem A supplies a positive quantitative loss
depending on \(t\) as well.

Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05253, Theorem 3.1, Remark 3.2, Theorem A,
and the authors' statement that the argument also applies to the
commuting-operator value.

## 3. The finite projection algebra has no matrix model

Write \(Y=V(X(G))\). The synchronous algebra of \(I\) is generated by
finitely many projections \(e_{i,v}\), \(i\in[t]\), \(v\in Y\), with

\[
 e_{i,v}=e_{i,v}^*=e_{i,v}^2,
 \qquad
 \sum_{v\in Y}e_{i,v}=1,
 \tag{10}
\]

and

\[
 e_{i,u}e_{j,v}=0
 \quad\text{whenever }(i,j;u,v)\text{ loses }I.
 \tag{11}
\]

A perfect commuting strategy gives a tracial representation of
(10)--(11), so the universal algebra \(\mathcal A_I\) is nonzero and has
a tracial state.

Suppose that a unital star-homomorphism

\[
 \rho:\mathcal A_I\longrightarrow M_d(\mathbf C)
 \tag{12}
\]

existed. Applying normalized matrix trace to the PVMs
\(\rho(e_{i,v})\) would give a perfect finite-dimensional strategy for
\(I\), contrary to \(\omega^*(I)<1\). This proves (PQB3).

The same gap excludes every Connes-embeddable trace on
\(\mathcal A_I\). Indeed, let \(\tau\) be any trace. Equations
(10)--(11) make

\[
 p(v,w\mid i,j)=\tau(e_{i,v}e_{j,w})
 \tag{13}
\]

a perfect commuting correlation. If the trace-GNS algebra embedded into
\(R^\mathcal U\), each of the finitely many PVMs in (10) could be lifted
to matrix PVMs, with all mixed second moments converging to (13). The
resulting finite-dimensional synchronous correlations would have winning
probability tending to one. Hence \(\omega^*(I)=1\), a contradiction.
Thus every trace on \(\mathcal A_I\) is non-CE.

## 4. The source is a finite BCS, exactly rather than approximately

For every \(i,v\), put

\[
 z_{i,v}=1-2e_{i,v}.
 \tag{14}
\]

These are Boolean symmetries. Paddock's SynchBCS construction uses:

- commutation of all \(z_{i,v}\) for a fixed \(i\);
- the one-hot relation \(\prod_{v\in Y}z_{i,v}=-1\);
- \(\widetilde{\mathrm{AND}}(z_{i,u},z_{j,v})=1\) for every forbidden
  pair.

For commuting Boolean symmetries, the last equation is exactly

\[
 \frac{1-z_{i,u}}2\,\frac{1-z_{j,v}}2=0.
 \tag{15}
\]

Proposition 4.22 proves that (14), with inverse
\(e_{i,v}=(1-z_{i,v})/2\), descends to a star-isomorphism

\[
 \mathcal A_I\cong\mathcal A(B_I).
 \tag{16}
\]

Therefore this finite general BCS algebra has a trace but no unital matrix
representation, and every trace on it is non-CE.

Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02525, Definition 4.21 and Proposition 4.22.

## 5. The 2025 nested-conjugacy theorem has the wrong terminal codomain

Theorem 5.5 of Fanizza--Kroell--Mehta--Paddock--Rochette--Slofstra--Zhao
starts with a nested-conjugacy BCS algebra and constructs a finite BCS
\(\widetilde B\) such that

\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{nest}}^X(B)
 \hookrightarrow\mathcal A(\widetilde B),
 \tag{17}
\]

every tracial state across the left side extends across (17), and relation
decompositions incur the explicit bound

\[
 2^{16}M^2\ell^2\Lambda.
 \tag{18}
\]

This is a genuine trace-extending embedding, but its codomain is another
general BCS algebra. The arbitrary source constraint in each nested
context survives as an arbitrary BCS constraint; the theorem linearizes
the conjugacy-routing auxiliaries, not the source predicate.

Lemma 3.12 in the same paper gives, for an involutively generated finitely
presented group \(H\),

\[
 \mathbf C H\hookrightarrow\mathcal A(B_H),
 \tag{19}
\]

with a trace on the BCS codomain and controlled decompositions of the
group relators. Equation (19) again runs from a group algebra into a BCS
algebra. Neither (17) nor (19) supplies a map from the no-matrix BCS in
(16) into a group corner.

Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04943, Lemma 3.12 and Theorem 5.5.

## 6. The 2026 Fourier extension theorem is positive, not multiplicative

For a finite set \(\Sigma\) in a discrete group, Theorem 4.22 of
Kakariadis--Leimbach--Todorov--van Suijlekom identifies positive operator
extension with bounded operator extension and the complete
Fejer--Riesz property. Their Theorem 5.21 gives the analogous equivalence
for a positivity domain \(\Delta\), replacing Fejer--Riesz by complete
factorisation. These are exact and useful criteria for extending
operator-valued positive-definite data on a group Fourier window.

They do not state that an arbitrary finite-dimensional operator system
embeds in a group algebra, and their maps are completely positive rather
than multiplicative. Projection preservation cannot be appended as a
minor extra condition. The following standard multiplicative-domain
calculation shows why.

Let \(A\) be a unital C-star algebra generated by projections
\(p_1,\ldots,p_n\), and let \(\phi:A\to B\) be ucp. If every
\(\phi(p_i)\) is a projection, then

\[
 \phi(p_i^*p_i)=\phi(p_i)
               =\phi(p_i)^*\phi(p_i),
 \qquad
 \phi(p_ip_i^*)=\phi(p_i)\phi(p_i)^*.
 \tag{20}
\]

Thus every \(p_i\) lies in the multiplicative domain of \(\phi\). The
multiplicative domain is a C-star subalgebra, so it contains the
C-star algebra generated by the \(p_i\), namely all of \(A\). Therefore

\[
 \phi\text{ is a unital star-homomorphism.}
 \tag{21}
\]

Consequently, a Fourier-window embedding which also carried the game
generators to genuine projections would already solve the full
multiplicative embedding problem. The 2026 extension theorem supplies no
such projection preservation; finite Fourier windows can also fail its
extension criterion, as the paper's finite-cyclic and two-variable
examples show.

Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29958, Theorems 4.22 and 5.21 and Example
4.11.

## 7. Exact terminal specification

The new source reduces the group problem to one precise construction.
Seek a finitely presented group \(\Gamma\), a central involution \(J\),
and \(p_-=(1-J)/2\) with all three properties below.

1. There is a unital star-homomorphism

\[
 \Phi:\mathcal A(B_I)\longrightarrow p_-C^*(\Gamma)p_-.
 \tag{22}
\]

2. Some tracial state on \(C^*(\Gamma)\) has value one on \(p_-\) and
restricts along \(\Phi\) to the perfect non-CE trace.

3. There is a dimension-independent function \(f(\delta)\to0\) such that
a \(\delta\)-representation of the finite presentation of \(\Gamma\)
with negative-\(J\) mass \(\eta\) decodes, on a corner of mass controlled
by \(\eta\), to a finite-dimensional strategy for \(I\) of value at least
\(1-f(\delta)\).

By (PQB3), (22) alone makes every exact finite-dimensional
representation of \(\Gamma\) kill \(p_-\). Property 2 makes \(J\neq1\).
Property 3 and the strict gap in (PQB2) would force the negative-\(J\)
mass to vanish in every asymptotic normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt model.
Then the nonidentity element \(J\) could not be separated, proving that
\(\Gamma\) is nonhyperlinear.

The exact finite-dimensional conclusion without Property 3 is
insufficient: finitely presented hyperlinear examples with
finite-dimensionally invisible central torsion already occur in this
repository. The sole residual gate is therefore the robust
projection-preserving multiplicative compiler in (22) together with Property 3, not perfect
completeness of the source.
