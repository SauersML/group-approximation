Constant Weyl elements in `EL_5(D)` conjugate the two selected roots to
`x_ab(s_0)` and `x_ab(t_0)` for every `a!=b`.  Root additivity and the
Steinberg commutator

```text
[x_ab(c),x_bc(d)]=x_ac(cd)              (a,b,c distinct)
```

then give `x_ab(c)` for every `c` in the ring generated by `D,s_0,t_0`.
Conversely every generator has its coefficient in that ring.  This proves
`G=EL_5(S)`.

Put `y=1^infinity`, `y_n=0^n y`, and let `V` have basis `e_n`, `n>=0`.
The span of these boundary paths is invariant under `S`.  Its action is

```text
s_0 e_n=e_(n+1),
t_0 e_0=0,                 t_0 e_(n+1)=e_n,
d e_n=d(y_n)e_n            (d in D).                    (JOQ5)
```

If `d` is locally constant, then `d(y_n)=d(0^infinity)` for all sufficiently
large `n`.  Thus the last operator is a scalar plus a finite diagonal matrix.
The first two operators are the unilateral shifts `S,T`, and the image ring
is exactly

```text
J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>.
```

Indeed `S,T` generate the image, while their defect matrix units

```text
E_mn=S^m(1-ST)T^n
```

contain every finite diagonal correction.  The ring epimorphism `S->J`
therefore induces the group epimorphism in `(JOQ2)`.

Conversely the universal relation `TS=1` holds for `t_0,s_0`, so
`S->s_0`, `T->t_0` defines a ring map `J->S`.  Its composite with the orbit
map fixes the generators `S,T`, hence is the identity on `J`.  Passing to
elementary groups gives a section of `(JOQ2)`.

The diagonal projection `q=s_1t_1` selects paths beginning in `1`.  Exactly
`y_0` has that property, so its image is

```text
Q e_0=e_0,                  Q e_n=0  (n>0),
Q=1-ST.
```

Hence `x_13(Q)` is a nonidentity elementary transvection.

Finally let `P` be the constant swap of coordinates `i,j` and use the
Whitehead involution

```text
W=[[Q,S],[T,0]].
```

Then `WP` sends `(i,n)` to `(i,n+1)`, sends `(j,n)` to `(j,n-1)` for
`n>0`, and sends `(j,0)` to `(i,0)`.  This proves the chain description
`(JOQ4)`.
