**ESTABLISHED** (citation import plus a two-line argument; no novelty claimed).

For `n ≥ 2` and every abelian `A ≤ Out(F_n)` there are a finite-index `A_0 ≤ A`
and a homomorphism `s: A_0 → Aut(F_n)` lifting the inclusion.

**Source.** Feighn--Handel, *Abelian subgroups of Out(F_n)*, arXiv:math/0612705,
read from its TeX on MSI:
- the introduction: "Abelian subgroups are both virtually rotationless and
  virtually pure";
- the definition of a principal set: `X ⊂ ∂F_n` with at least three points such
  that each element of `A` has a representative fixing `X`. The assignment is "a
  lift of `A` from `Out(F_n)` to `Aut(F_n)`";
- Lemma "principal sets", item 3: if `A` is generated by rotationless elements,
  `ψ ∈ A` is rotationless, `Ψ ∈ PA^±(ψ)` and `Fix(Ψ)` has rank at least two, then
  `∂Fix(Ψ)` contains a principal set for `A`.

**Argument.**
1. Let `A_0 ≤ A` be a finite-index subgroup of rotationless elements.
2. Apply item 3 to `ψ = 1` and `Ψ = id`. `Fix(id) = F_n` has rank `n ≥ 2`, and
   `id` is principal, since `Fix_N` is all of `∂F_n`. This gives a principal set
   `X`.
3. The lift is a homomorphism. The representative fixing `X` is unique: if
   `Φ` and `ι_g Φ` both fix `X`, then `ι_g` fixes three points of `∂F_n`, so
   `g = 1`. Products of representatives fixing `X` fix `X`.

**Why the identity is rotationless.** Item 2 needs the identity of `Out(F_n)`
to be rotationless. This follows from FH's definitions (principal automorphism;
forward rotationless; `PA^±`):
- For `g ≠ 1`, `Fix_N(ι̂_g)` is the single attracting point `g^{+∞}`, so `ι_g`
  is not principal.
- So `PA^±(1) = {id}`.
- `Fix_N = Per_N = ∂F_n` for `id`, and `Φ ↦ Φ^k` is a bijection of `{id}`.

FH's explicit convention concerns only `Out(F_1)`.

**Consequence.** For commuting `α, β ∈ IA_n`, the powers `α^K, β^K` have
commuting lifts, so `ω = 0` for them and (Q) of
`class-two-aut-out-obstruction-on-commuting-pairs` is solvable. By item 4 of
that node, (Q) is then solvable for `α, β` too. **So the commuting-pair test is
empty.** A class-two obstruction to virtual sections of `Aut(F_n) → Out(F_n)`,
if any, is not supported on abelian subgroups.
