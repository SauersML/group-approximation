Write `B = ⊕_Ω Γ` and `Δ = B ⋊ Λ`.

1. **Abelianization.** `Δ^(ab)` is the quotient of `Δ` by `[Δ,Δ]`. The subgroup `[B, Λ]` is generated by the elements
   `b·(λ·b)^(-1)`, so the image of `B` in `Δ^(ab)` is the largest abelian quotient of the coinvariants `B_Λ = B/[B,Λ]`.
   Transitivity identifies all coordinates, so `B_Λ ≅ Γ`, and `Δ^(ab) ≅ Γ^(ab) × Λ^(ab)`.
2. **Intersection with the derived subgroup.** The composite `Γ_ω → Δ → Δ^(ab) → Γ^(ab)` is the abelianization map of
   `Γ`, whose kernel is `[Γ,Γ]`. An element of `Γ_ω` lies in `[Δ,Δ]` exactly when its image in `Δ^(ab)` is trivial, so
   `Γ_ω ∩ [Δ,Δ] = [Γ,Γ]_ω`. Conversely `[Γ,Γ]_ω ⊆ [Γ_ω, Γ_ω] ⊆ [Δ,Δ]`.
3. **The map is not multiplicative.** Fix `ω ≠ ω'`. Distinct coordinates of `B` commute, so for `γ, δ ∈ Γ`

       (γ_ω (γ^(-1))_(ω')) · (δ_ω (δ^(-1))_(ω')) = (γδ)_ω · (γ^(-1) δ^(-1))_(ω') = (γδ)_ω · ((δγ)^(-1))_(ω') ,

   while the value at `γδ` is `(γδ)_ω ((γδ)^(-1))_(ω')`. The two agree for all `γ, δ` exactly when `Γ` is abelian. The
   inverse in the second coordinate reverses the order, which is the same failure as in
   `whitehead-diagonal-map-is-not-multiplicative`.
4. **Model tests.** `Γ` abelian: the map is the injective homomorphism `γ ↦ γ_ω (γ^(-1))_(ω')`, consistent with
   `[Γ,Γ] = 1` only when the image is trivial in `Δ^(ab)`, which it is, since `γ_ω(γ^(-1))_(ω')` is the commutator
   `[γ_ω, λ]`. `Γ` perfect: step 2 gives `Γ_ω ⊆ [Δ,Δ]`, as it must. `Γ = Z`, `Λ = Ω = Z`: `Δ` is the lamplighter over
   `Z`, `Δ^(ab) = Z × Z`, and no nontrivial `γ_0` is a product of commutators.
