Use the notation of `atlas-a4-packet-biclique-decomposition` and
`atlas-a4-19243-s3xs3-localization`.  Thus `H_6 ~= S3` is the small packet
component, `H_18=K x Z` with `K ~= S3` the factor generated by the four
second-chart syllables of `q_19243`, and `Z ~= C3` is the center of `H_18`.
Let `b` be the repeated first-chart involution of `q_19243`.

Inside the concrete identification

```text
A8 ~= GL_4(F2),
```

the following subgroup ladder is exact.

```text
H := <H_6,K>                 ~= GL_3(F2),      |H|=168,
P := <H,b>                   ~= AGL_3(F2),     |P|=1344,
<P,z> = GL_4(F2) ~= A8       for 1 != z in Z.                (A4-LADDER)
```

Moreover `H` is literally the standard block-diagonal rank-three subgroup:
every element fixes both the last basis vector and the last coordinate
covector.  The group `P` is literally the stabilizer of the last coordinate
covector: every element has last row `(0,0,0,1)`, and its order `1344` is the
full hyperplane-stabilizer order.  Neither nontrivial element of `Z` belongs to
`P`.

Thus the formerly separate `H=GL_3(2)` tangent geometry and the shortest A4
packet are the **same finite subgroup geometry**.  Collision `19243` itself
never leaves the affine parabolic: its visible `S3` factor together with its
repeated involution only enlarges `H` to `P`.  The central `C3` directions of
the large packet component are exactly what escape `P` and complete the full
`A8` chart.

This explains structurally why tiny collision tests admit affine/coset
escapes.  A successful finite-multiplicity argument must use the `C3` sector of
`H_18`, or an equivalent packet consequence outside the parabolic, rather than
only the `S3 x S3` cell seen by `q_19243`.
