"Good" is as in `pbh-coset-condition-passes-to-finite-index-overgroups`.

**Trust note (not read at source).** For `G` hyperbolic relative to `{P_j}` and `g` of
infinite order not conjugate into any `P_j`, `E_G(g)` is virtually cyclic and
`E_G(g) = {x : x g^k x^(-1) = g^(±k) for some k ≠ 0}` (Olshanskii, IJAC 1993, Lemma
1.16, for hyperbolic `G`; Osin, IJAC 16 (2006), for relatively hyperbolic `G`).

**Step 1 (E_i good).** `C_G(g_i) = C_G({g_i})` is good (case 2 of
`pbh-coset-wreaths-iff-identity-edge-hnns`, with `L = G`, `m = g_i`). It lies in `E_i` by
the formula with `k = 1`, and it contains `<g_i>`. `E_i` is virtually cyclic and infinite,
so its infinite subgroup `<g_i>` has finite index, and so does `C_G(g_i)`. By
`pbh-coset-condition-passes-to-finite-index-overgroups`, item 2, `E_i` is good. Item 3 of
the claim is (b) of the equivalence.

**Step 2 (one wreath over all cusps).** Let `X = ⊔_i G/E_i`, with base points `ω_i = E_i`,
and `K = K_1 × ... × K_n ∈ B_A` (closure under finite direct products,
`boone-higman-type-a-class-closed-under-finite-extensions`). By the "Multiple orbits"
clause of `pbh-coset-wreaths-iff-identity-edge-hnns` (finitely many orbits, each
stabilizer good on its own), `W = (*_{x∈X} K_x) ⋊ G ∈ B_A`.

**Step 3 (the star amalgam inside W).** Let `Y ≤ W` be generated by `G` and the copies
`(K_i)_{ω_i}` of the factors `K_i ≤ K`. Then `g (K_i)_{ω_i} g^(-1) = (K_i)_{gω_i}`. The
subgroups `(K_i)_x` (`x` in the `i`-th orbit) lie in distinct free factors `K_x`, so
they generate their free product `Y_0` (standard). `G` normalizes `Y_0`, so
`Y = Y_0 ⋊ G`. The proof of (W) in `centralizer-amalgams-free-wreath-proof`, run with
several orbits, identifies `Y` with `Γ`. Send `(e, k) ∈ E_i × K_i` to `k_{ω_i} e`, and
send `k_{gω_i}` back to `g k g^(-1)`. This is well defined because `E_i` centralizes
`K_i` in `Γ`. So `Γ ≅ Y ∈ B_A`.

**Step 4 (relative hyperbolicity).** Read at source: Kharlampovich--Myasnikov,
arXiv:0904.2423v4, p. 6, Lemma 1.4 (proved in Osin [19]). If `G` is hyperbolic relative
to `{H_λ}` and `g` is a hyperbolic element of infinite order (not conjugate into any
`H_λ`), then `G` is hyperbolic relative to `{H_λ} ∪ {E_G(g)}`. Apply this to `g_1`, then
`g_2`, and so on. At step `i`, `g_i` is not conjugate into `E_k` for `k < i`. Otherwise
`x g_i x^(-1) ∈ E_k`, and since `<g_k>` has finite index in `E_k`, some
`(x g_i x^(-1))^a = g_k^b` with `a ≠ 0`, and `b ≠ 0` because `g_i` has infinite order.
That contradicts non-commensurability. So `G` is hyperbolic relative to
`{P_j} ∪ {E_i}`. Then apply Dahmani's Theorem 0.1(2), as quoted in
`rel-hyperbolic-fp-simple-peripherals-proof`, once for each `i`, with `A = E_i × K_i`
(finitely generated). The trust note there, on Osin's versus Bowditch's definition,
applies. Each `E_i × K_i ∈ B_A`: `E_i ≤ G` and closure under products.
