Verification tier: proposed-established. Items 1, 2 and 5 are proved in
full below. Item 3 uses one standard fact of locally compact metric
geometry. Item 4 and the table use standard theorems. They are cited here and
not reproved:
- **Roe**, *Lectures on coarse geometry*, Ch. 11. Property A and coarse
  embeddability in Hilbert space pass to subspaces and are invariant under
  coarse equivalence. For finitely generated groups, exact is equivalent to
  property A (Higson–Roe, Ozawa, Guentner–Kaminker).
- **Bell–Dranishnikov**, *Asymptotic dimension* (Topology Appl. 2008).
  `asdim` is monotone under coarse embeddings and `asdim Z^n = n`.
- **Roe**, PAMS 133 (2005). Hyperbolic proper geodesic spaces of bounded
  growth have finite asdim.
- **Dymara–Schick**, Russ. J. Math. Phys. 16 (2009). Buildings (Davis
  realization, finite thickness) have finite asdim.
- **Wright**, Geom. Topol. 16 (2012). Finite-dimensional CAT(0) cube
  complexes have finite asdim.
- **Anantharaman-Delaroche**, Trans. AMS 354 (2002). Amenability at infinity
  passes to closed subgroups. `G` acts amenably on `G/Q` for `Q` closed
  amenable with `G/Q` compact.
- **Cornulier–de la Harpe**, *Metric geometry of locally compact groups*
  (EMS 2016). This covers proper compatible metrics on compactly generated
  groups, the Cayley–Abels graph, and the fact that a closed subgroup
  inclusion is a coarse embedding.
- **Sapir**, *A Higman embedding preserving asphericity*, JAMS 27 (2014).
  There is a closed aspherical 4-manifold whose fundamental group coarsely
  contains an expander, so it does not coarsely embed in Hilbert space. This
  is external: the result is used in the form of its abstract.
- **Genevois–Lonjou–Urech**, IMRN 2024. Neretin groups act properly on a
  CAT(0) cube complex, so they are Haagerup. This is also used in
  `haagerup-cut-out-completions-force-haagerup-quotients`.

## Step 0. The Hecke graph is a well-defined connected locally finite graph

Let `T = Σ S^{±1} Σ`. Then `T = T^{-1}` and `ΣTΣ = T`.

- **Well defined.** If `xΣ = x'Σ`, then `x' = xσ` and `x'tΣ = x(σt)Σ` with
  `σt ∈ T`. So the edge set `{ {xΣ, xtΣ} : x ∈ C, t ∈ T }` is well defined.
  It is symmetric because `T = T^{-1}`, and it is `C`-invariant.
- **Locally finite.** The neighbours of `Σ` are `TΣ/Σ = ⋃_{s ∈ S^{±1}} ΣsΣ/Σ`.
  Each `ΣsΣ/Σ` has `[Σ : Σ ∩ sΣs^{-1}]` elements, which is finite because
  `F` commensurates `Σ`. Every vertex is a `C`-translate of `Σ`, so every
  vertex has the same finite degree.
- **Connected.** `C` is generated by `Σ ∪ S`. Write `c = σ_1 s_1 σ_2 s_2 ⋯
  σ_k s_k σ_{k+1}` with `σ_i ∈ Σ` and `s_i ∈ S^{±1}`. Put
  `x_j = σ_1 s_1 ⋯ σ_j s_j`. Then `x_{j+1} = x_j (σ_{j+1} s_{j+1})` with
  `σ_{j+1} s_{j+1} ∈ T`. So `Σ, x_1Σ, …, x_kΣ = cΣ` is a path of length `k`.

## Step 1. The coarse embedding (item 1)

- **Well defined.** `N ⊆ Σ`, so `fN = f'N` implies `fΣ = f'Σ`.
- **Injective.** `fΣ = f'Σ` implies `f^{-1}f' ∈ Σ ∩ F = N`.
- **Equivariant.** `φ(gp) = g·φ(p)` for `g ∈ F`, by definition.
- **1-Lipschitz.** For `s ∈ S^{±1}`, `s = 1·s·1 ∈ T`. So `fΣ` and `fsΣ` are
  adjacent or equal, and `d(φ(p), φ(ps̄)) ≤ 1` for the image `s̄` of `s` in
  `P`. Induct on word length.
- **Metrically proper.** By equivariance, `d(φ(p), φ(q)) = d(Σ, φ(p^{-1}q))`.
  Let `ρ(r) = min{d(Σ, φ(p)) : |p| ≥ r}`. Fix `R`. The ball
  `B_Hk(Σ, R)` is finite (Step 0). `φ` is injective, so
  `φ^{-1}(B_Hk(Σ, R))` is finite and lies in some `B_P(r_0)`. Then `|p| ≥ r_0`
  implies `d(Σ, φ(p)) > R`, so `ρ(r) > R` for `r ≥ r_0`. Hence `ρ -> ∞`, and
  `d(φ(p), φ(q)) ≥ ρ(d_P(p, q))`.
- **Growth.** `φ` is injective and maps `B_P(r)` into `B_Hk(Σ, r)`.

## Step 2. The Hecke graph is a Cayley–Abels graph of the completion (item 2)

`G` is the closure of the image of `C` in `Sym(C/Σ)`, with the topology of
pointwise convergence. This is the relative Schlichting completion of Step 6
of the proof of `commensurated-witnesses-survive-browder-but-die-when-linear`.

- `K = Stab_G(Σ)` is open by definition of the topology.
- `K` is compact: each `K`-orbit on `C/Σ` is contained in a `Σ`-orbit, which
  is finite by commensuration, and `K` is closed in the product of these
  finite orbits (Tychonoff).
- `C ∩ K = Σ`, and `C` is dense in `G` and acts transitively. So `G = CK`, and
  `G/K = C/Σ` as `G`-sets.
- **`G` preserves the edges.** An element `g ∈ G` agrees on any finite set
  with some `c ∈ C`. Checking an edge `{u, v}` involves two points, and `C`
  preserves the edge relation, so `g` preserves it too. The same argument
  applies to `g^{-1}`. So `G ≤ Aut(Hk)`, acting vertex-transitively with
  vertex stabilizers conjugate to `K`.
- A connected locally finite graph with such an action is a Cayley–Abels
  graph. Hence `G` is compactly generated (by `K` and the finitely many
  elements taking `Σ` to its neighbours), and the orbit map `G -> Hk`,
  `g ↦ gΣ`, is a quasi-isometry for any word metric on `G` over a compact
  generating set (Cornulier–de la Harpe, Prop. 2.E.10 and Ch. 4).

## Step 3. General cut-outs (item 3)

Let `(H, K, ρ)` be a cut-out. Put `H_0 = ⟨K, ρ(S)⟩`. It is open because it
contains `K`, and it is compactly generated by `K ∪ ρ(S)`. Let `F̂` be the
closure of `ρ(F)` in `H_0` and `M = F̂ ∩ K`.

- By the transfer lemma of `haagerup-cut-out-completions-force-haagerup-quotients`
  (item 1), `M` is compact open and normal in `F̂`, and `P ≅ F̂/M`.
- `F̂` is compactly generated by `M ∪ ρ(S)`, since `ρ(F)` is dense and `M` is
  open. With word metrics over these generators, `F̂ -> F̂/M = P` is a
  quasi-isometry because the kernel is compact. So `P` is coarsely
  equivalent to `F̂`.
- The inclusion of the closed subgroup `F̂` in `H_0` is a coarse embedding.
  It is coarsely Lipschitz because the compact generating set of `F̂` is
  bounded in `H_0`. It is metrically proper because bounded sets in `H_0` are
  relatively compact and `F̂` is closed.
- Composing gives a coarse embedding `P -> H_0`, and hence
  `P -> H_0/K` (the Cayley–Abels graph).

For the Schlichting completion of Step 2, `H_0 = G` and `H_0/K = Hk`, so
item 3 recovers item 1 up to coarse equivalence. Item 1 is sharper: it is
injective, 1-Lipschitz and explicit.

## Step 4. Descent of coarse invariants (item 4)

`Hk` and every Cayley–Abels graph are bounded-geometry graphs, since vertex
degrees are constant. Property A, coarse embeddability in Hilbert space or in
`ℓ^p`, finite decomposition complexity and `asdim ≤ n` all pass to subspaces
and are coarse invariants, so they pull back along a coarse embedding
(Roe Ch. 11; Bell–Dranishnikov).

## Step 5. Exactness descent through `F̂/M` (item 5)

Assume `H` acts continuously and topologically amenably on a compact space
`X`. By Anantharaman-Delaroche, the closed subgroup `F̂` does too. So there
are weak*-continuous maps `x ↦ μ_i^x ∈ Prob(F̂)` with
`sup_{x ∈ X} ‖g μ_i^x − μ_i^{gx}‖_1 -> 0` uniformly for `g` in compact sets.

- `M` is compact, so `X/M` is compact Hausdorff.
- Since `M ⊴ F̂`, `F̂` permutes the `M`-orbits: `g(Mx) = M(gx)`. So `P = F̂/M`
  acts continuously on `X/M`.
- Let `π : F̂ -> P` and let `dm` be normalized Haar measure on `M`. Define
  `ν_i^{Mx} = ∫_M π_*(μ_i^{mx}) dm`. This depends only on `Mx` because `dm`
  is invariant under translation. It is a weak*-continuous function of the
  orbit.
- For `p = π(g)`, `ν_i^{p·Mx} = ∫_M π_*(μ_i^{mgx}) dm`. Substitute
  `m = g m' g^{-1}`. Conjugation by `g` is a continuous automorphism of the
  compact group `M`, so it preserves `dm`, and the integral becomes
  `∫_M π_*(μ_i^{g m' x}) dm'`.
- Therefore
  `‖p ν_i^{Mx} − ν_i^{p·Mx}‖_1 ≤ ∫_M ‖g μ_i^{m'x} − μ_i^{g m' x}‖_1 dm' -> 0`,
  uniformly in `x`, because `π_*` is `ℓ^1`-contractive and equivariant.

So `P` acts amenably on a compact space, that is, `P` is exact. The same
computation applies to any closed `D ≤ H` with compact normal `M ⊴ D`. This
is the form used for the `V`-inside-Neretin remark.

## Step 6. The `N_U` consequences and the table

`P = U` is Higman's universal finitely presented group. Every finitely
presented group embeds in `U`, and every recursively presented finitely
generated group embeds in a finitely presented one (Higman). So:

- **`Z^n ≤ U` for all `n`.** Then `asdim Hk ≥ asdim P ≥ n` for all `n`
  (Step 4).
- **Osajda's group `O ≤ U`.** `O` is recursively presented and non-exact
  (`recursive-residually-finite-non-exact-groups-exist`). Exactness passes to
  subgroups of discrete groups, so `U` is non-exact and has no property A.
  By Step 4, `Hk` has no property A. By Step 5, `H` is not amenable at
  infinity.
- **Sapir's group `Γ ≤ U`.** `Γ` is finitely presented and coarsely contains
  an expander, so `U`, and hence `Hk`, does not coarsely embed in Hilbert
  space (Step 4).

Checking the rows of the table:

- **Amenable or amenable at infinity.** An amenable `H` acts amenably on a
  point. Apply Step 5 and Osajda.
- **Trees.** `Aut(T)` for a locally finite tree `T`, and closed subgroups of
  it, act properly on `T`. The orbit map of the compactly generated `H_0` is
  a coarse embedding into `T`, and `asdim T ≤ 1`.
- **Linear.** A closed `H ≤ ∏_j GL_{d_j}(k_j)` over local fields: the product
  acts amenably on the compact `∏ G_j/B_j` (`B_j` the amenable Borel
  subgroups). The action restricts to `H`. Apply Step 5.
- **Buildings and cubes.** If `H` acts properly by isometries on a proper
  metric space `Y`, the orbit map `H_0 -> Y` is a coarse embedding: it is
  coarsely Lipschitz on the compact generating set, and metrically proper
  because the action is proper. So finite `asdim Y` (Dymara–Schick for
  buildings; Wright for finite-dimensional cube complexes) gives finite
  `asdim H_0`, which contradicts `Z^n ≤ U`.
- **Hyperbolic.** For a Gromov-hyperbolic compactly generated tdlc group,
  the Cayley–Abels graph is hyperbolic with constant degree, so it has finite
  asdim (Roe 2005).
- **Haagerup.** A proper continuous affine isometric action on a Hilbert
  space with cocycle `b` gives an orbit map that is coarsely Lipschitz,
  because `‖b(gs) − b(g)‖ = ‖b(s)‖` is bounded on the compact generating set,
  and metrically proper. So Haagerup `H_0` coarsely embeds in Hilbert space.
  Apply Sapir. For Neretin groups, see Genevois–Lonjou–Urech.
- **Products.** Coarse embeddings into Hilbert spaces combine to one into
  their direct sum, so a finite product of coarsely embeddable groups is
  coarsely embeddable. `SL_3(Q_p) × N_{d,k}` is therefore dead, although its
  closed subgroup `SL_3(Q_p)` is non-compact with (T), which is where the
  Haagerup kill fails.

## Step 7. Sharpness

- **Normal case.** If `Σ ⊴ C`, then `T = SΣ ∪ S^{-1}Σ` (modulo `Σ`), so `Hk`
  is the Cayley graph of `C/Σ` with respect to the image of `S ∪ Σ`, with
  `Σ` trivial. `φ` is the inclusion `P ≤ C/Σ`.
- **Non-discrete, non-A, type F example.** `BS(1,2) = ⟨a, t | tat^{-1} = a^2⟩`
  is torsion-free of type F (one-relator). `⟨a⟩` is commensurated, since
  `t⟨a⟩t^{-1} = ⟨a^2⟩` has index 2. It contains no subgroup of finite index
  that is normal in `BS(1,2)`, because `t^{-1} a^{2^k} t = a^{2^{k-1}}`. So
  the completion `Q_2 ⋊ Z` is not discrete.
- For `Λ = Γ × BS(1,2)` and `Σ = 1 × ⟨a⟩`, with generating set
  `S_Γ ⊔ {a, t}`: `ΣsΣ = sΣ` for `s ∈ S_Γ`, so `Hk` is the Cartesian product
  `Cay(Γ) □ Hk(BS(1,2), ⟨a⟩)`. The fibre `Cay(Γ) × {Σ}` is isometrically
  embedded. So `Hk` coarsely contains an expander, while `Λ` and `Σ` are
  torsion-free of type F.
- **`Z/2 ≀ Z`.** It is a cut-out in `Aff(F_2((t))) × Aff(F_2((1/t)))`, as
  shown in `haagerup-cut-out-completions-force-haagerup-quotients`. That host
  is linear over local fields, hence amenable at infinity, with finite
  asdim. So no property of the host that descends to `P` can exclude it.

## What this does not decide

- Whether any torsion-free type F commensurated pair `(Λ, Σ)` with
  `F ≤ Λ` has `Σ ∩ F = N_U`. The gate requires `Hk` to coarsely contain `U`.
  The example above shows that this is achievable in type F in general,
  though not for `N_U`.
- Whether almost-automorphism groups of right-angled or `Ã_2` buildings are
  coarsely embeddable in Hilbert space, exact, or of finite asymptotic
  dimension. The Neretin (tree) case is Haagerup.
- Whether `V ≅ D/M` for a closed `D` of a Neretin group.
