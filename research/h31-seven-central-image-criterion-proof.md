## Why sufficient

Caprace--Conder--Kaluba--Witzel,
[arXiv:2011.09276](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09276), Theorem 1.2,
prove that the specified `H_31` is infinite, hyperbolic, and Kazhdan.
It is non-elementary because an infinite elementary hyperbolic group
is virtually cyclic, whereas property (T) passes to finite-index
subgroups and an infinite cyclic group does not have property (T).

The theorem identifies the vertex groups of its simple triangle of
groups as `PSL_2(31)`, `C_5 x C_5`, and a Sylow `5` subgroup of
`Sp_4(5)`. Their orders are `14880`, `25`, and `625`. All are prime
to `7`. Every central `C_7` extension uniquely splits on each vertex
group: existence follows by averaging a cocycle, and uniqueness
follows from the absence of homomorphisms from a group of order prime
to `7` to `C_7`. The splittings agree on the edge groups and hence
extend over the colimit presentation of `H_31`. Therefore
`H^2(H_31;F_7)=0`. Its generation by the same vertex groups also
gives `H^1(H_31;F_7)=0`.

For a finite quotient `F=H_31/L`, the five-term cohomology sequence
now makes transgression an isomorphism

```text
H^1(L;F_7)^(H_31) ~= H^2(F;F_7).
```

This proves equivalence of statements 2 and 3. For statements 1
and 2, an invariant nonzero character `L -> C_7` has kernel normal
in `H_31`; the resulting finite quotient has central subgroup
`L/ker(chi)=C_7`. Conversely, lift a central `C_7` subgroup of a
finite quotient to a normal finite-index subgroup of `H_31` to
obtain the invariant character. Cauchy's theorem identifies absence
of central order-`7` elements with center order prime to `7`.

For the final assertion, killing `c` retracts onto the vertex group
`Q=PSL_2(31)` and has kernel `K` normally generated by `c`. In the
contractible triangle development `X`, the quotient `K\X` is a
cone: the unique vertex of `<a,b>` type is its apex, its base has
vertices `Q/<a>` and `Q/<b>`, and base edges and triangles are
indexed by `Q`. All `K` cell stabilizers are `5` groups. For
`p!=5` their higher cohomology vanishes, so the equivariant
cohomology spectral sequence identifies `H^*(K;F_p)` with the
cohomology of this cone. Thus all positive-degree groups vanish.

The artifact provides the averaging formula, explicit presentation,
central-cover equations, and the cone's cell counts. No uniform
statement about every finite quotient of `H_31` is assumed in this
proof.
