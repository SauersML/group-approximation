**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed; elementary). No priority claimed: the
argument is the standard one for Houghton-type and Steinberg-type presentations.

**Lemma (general).** Let `R` be a finitely presented ring. Let a finitely presented
group `Γ` act on a set `S` such that
- `Γ` has finitely many orbits on ordered `k`-tuples of distinct points of `S` for
  `k ≤ 5`, and
- the pointwise stabilizer in `Γ` of an ordered pair of distinct points is finitely
  generated.

Let `St(R; S)` be the finitary Steinberg group indexed by `S` (generators `x_ab(r)`,
`a ≠ b ∈ S`, `r ∈ R`, with the Steinberg relations), on which `Γ` acts by
`g·x_ab(r) = x_{ga,gb}(r)`. Then `St(R; S) ⋊ Γ` is finitely presented.

**Theorem.** Let `F` be Thompson's group acting on the dyadic rationals `D` of `(0,1)`.
Then `G_D := St(Z; D) ⋊ F` and `G_D^E := E(Z; D) ⋊ F` (finitary elementary matrices over
`Z` with rows and columns indexed by `D`) are finitely presented, have solvable word
problem, and contain the group `Σ = E(Z) ⋊ Z` of
`elementary-shift-group-contains-every-integral-linear-group`. So `G_D^E` is an explicit
finitely presented group containing every `GL_n(Z)`: every polycyclic, finitely
generated nilpotent, finite and finitely generated virtually special group, and every
`GL_n(O_K)`. It is neither linear nor residually finite.

**Consequence.** Boone–Higman for the single explicit finitely presented group `G_D^E`
gives one finitely presented simple group containing every integral linear group
(route `integral-linear-one-host-via-fp-elementary-thompson-group`).

## Proof

**Lemma.** Let `(a_1,b_1), …, (a_m,b_m)` represent the `Γ`-orbits on ordered pairs of
distinct points, and let `r_1, …, r_q` generate `R`. Take the presentation `P`:
- generators: those of `Γ`, and `y_{i,j}` for `i ≤ m`, `j ≤ q` (meant as `x_{a_i b_i}(r_j)`);
- the relators of `Γ`;
- `[y_{i,j}, h] = 1` for `h` in a finite generating set of the pointwise stabilizer of
  `(a_i, b_i)`.

These make `x_ab(r_j) := g y_{i,j} g^{-1}`, for any `g` with `g(a_i,b_i) = (a,b)`, well
defined. Every Steinberg relation involves at most four distinct indices:
`[x_ab(r), x_cd(r')] = 1` for `b ≠ c`, `a ≠ d`, and `[x_ab(r), x_bc(r')] = x_ac(rr')`. By
additivity and `x_ab(rr') = [x_ac(r), x_cb(r')]`, the words `x_ab(r_j)` generate every
`x_ab(r)`, and a finite presentation of `R` becomes finitely many relations
`x_{ab}(ρ) = 1`, each through a third index. For each of the finitely many orbits of
ordered tuples of at most five distinct points, add the Steinberg and ring relations
for one representative tuple, written in the conjugates `x_ab(r_j)`. That these
relations present the finitary Steinberg group on every 5-point subset is the
reduction of `steinberg-groups-of-fp-rings-are-fp-in-rank-five` (for `R = Z` it is
Milnor's classical presentation of `St_n(Z)` by the `x_ij(1)` and the two commutator
relations, which uses at most four indices). Every Steinberg
relation of `St(R; S)` is then a `Γ`-conjugate of an imposed one.

Map `P → St(R; S) ⋊ Γ` in the evident way; it is onto. Conversely `x_ab(r) ↦ x_ab(r)`
defines a homomorphism `St(R; S) → P`, since the images satisfy every Steinberg relation,
and it is `Γ`-equivariant, so it extends to `St(R; S) ⋊ Γ → P`. The two maps are inverse
on generators.

**Theorem.** `F` is finitely presented, and it acts transitively on increasing
`k`-tuples of dyadic rationals for every `k`. So it has `k!` orbits on ordered `k`-tuples
of distinct points. The pointwise stabilizer of `d < d'` is
`F_[0,d] × F_[d,d'] × F_[d',1] ≅ F^3`, finitely generated. With `R = Z` the lemma gives
`G_D`. `K_2` of the finitary Steinberg group over `Z` is `K_2(Z) = Z/2`, central and
generated by the single symbol `(x_12(1) x_21(−1) x_12(1))^4` on any two indices
(Milnor), so `G_D^E = G_D/⟨that element⟩` is finitely presented too.

For `Σ`: pick a one-bump `a ∈ F` and a dyadic point `p` in the interior of its support.
The orbit `O = ⟨a⟩p` is infinite and `a` translates it. `a` normalizes `E(Z; O)`, and
`⟨a⟩ ∩ E(Z; D) = 1`, so `E(Z; O) ⋊ ⟨a⟩ ≅ E(Z) ⋊ Z = Σ` inside `G_D^E`. Word problem: an
element is a pair (finitary integer matrix, element of `F`), computed exactly. `G_D^E`
contains `Σ`, which is not linear (unbounded derived length of `UT_n(Z)`); and `E(Z; D)`
has no nontrivial finite quotient (same congruence-subgroup argument as for `Σ`), so
`G_D^E` is not residually finite. ∎
