Fix a prime `p >= 7`. Write `K = G_HC(1)(p)` for the KMS group quoted in
`caprace-kassabov-hyperbolic-kazhdan-alternating-quotients`, and
`T = G_(F_p,(1,1,2)) <= Aut(F_p[x_1,x_2,x_3])` for the Caprace–Kassabov group
generated by three polynomial transvections.

## Standard facts

- **(H1)** Every torsion subgroup of a word-hyperbolic group is finite (Gromov;
  Ghys–de la Harpe).
- **(H2)** The quotient of a hyperbolic group by a finite normal subgroup is
  hyperbolic, since the quotient map is a quasi-isometry.
- **(T1)** A quotient of a Kazhdan group is Kazhdan, and an extension of a Kazhdan
  group by a Kazhdan group is Kazhdan (Bekka–de la Harpe–Valette, §1.7).

## Proof

**Step 1: the kernel onto `T` is infinite.** Let `ψ: K ->> T` be the map of (CK1)
and `M_K = ker ψ`. Suppose `M_K` were finite. Then `T ≅ K/M_K` would be
hyperbolic by (H2), while (CK3) gives it an infinite torsion subgroup,
contradicting (H1). So `M_K` is infinite, and by (H1) it contains an element of
infinite order.

**Step 2: a torsion-free cover.** Let `H` be a torsion-free non-elementary
hyperbolic Kazhdan group. For example, take a torsion-free cocompact lattice in
`Sp(2,1)`: it exists by Selberg's lemma and has property (T) by Kostant. `K` is
finitely presented, with the presentation quoted in the import.
`belegradek-osin-rips-construction` gives `1 -> N -> G -> K -> 1` with `G`
hyperbolic, `G` torsion-free because `H` is, and `N` a quotient of `H`. By (T1),
`N` is Kazhdan, and so is `G`, since `K` is Kazhdan.

**Step 3: pull back.** Let `π: G ->> K` be the quotient map and put
`M = π^(-1)(M_K) = ker(ψ ∘ π)`. `M` contains `N` and `M/N ≅ M_K` is infinite, so
`M` is infinite. For each prime `ℓ >= 3`:
- (CK2) gives `χ_ℓ: T ->> Alt(d_ℓ)` with `d_ℓ = (p^(3ℓ) − p^3)/ℓ`;
- put `N_ℓ = ker(χ_ℓ ∘ ψ ∘ π)`. Then `G/N_ℓ ≅ Alt(d_ℓ)` and `M ⊆ N_ℓ`.

`d_ℓ` is strictly increasing in `ℓ`, so the `N_ℓ` are pairwise distinct. `G` is
torsion-free, so every nontrivial element of `M` has infinite order. `QED`
