## Setting

`W(Z)` is the group of bijections `f: Z → Z` with `sup_n |f(n) − n| < ∞`. For a finite set
`Q ⊂ W(Z)` put `r(Q) = max_{f ∈ Q} sup_n |f(n) − n|`.

A pair `(Γ, H)`, with `H ≤ Γ` a subgroup of a discrete group, has *relative property (T)* if every
unitary representation of `Γ` with almost invariant vectors has a nonzero `H`-invariant vector.
(Almost invariant: for every finite `Q ⊂ Γ` and `ε > 0` there is a unit vector `ξ` with
`max_{g ∈ Q} ‖gξ − ξ‖ < ε`.)

## Statement

Let `Γ ≤ W(Z)` be any subgroup (countable or not), and let `H ⊴ Γ` be a normal subgroup such that
`(Γ, H)` has relative property (T).

1. **(Window bound.)** For every `δ ∈ (0, 1)` there are a finite `Q ⊂ Γ` and `ε > 0` with the
   following property. Put `L_0 = max(1, ⌈4 r(Q)/ε²⌉)`. For every interval `I ⊂ Z` with `|I| = L ≥ L_0`, at
   most `2δ² L` points `n ∈ I` have an `H`-orbit that is infinite or has more than `2L` points.
2. **(Finitely generated subgroups have bounded orbits.)** Let `H' ≤ H` be generated by a finite set
   `S'` with `c = max(1, r(S'))`. Take `δ` with `2δ² < 1/(2c)`, and `Q, ε, L_0` as in item 1. Then
   every `H'`-orbit on `Z` has at most `2 max(L_0, 2c)` points.
3. **(Local finiteness.)** Every finitely generated subgroup of `H` is finite. In particular every
   element of `H` has finite order, and `H` contains no copy of `Z`.

**Corollary for the full shift.** Let `X = {0,1}^Z` and let `[[X]]` be its topological full group.
Items 1–3 hold for every `Γ ≤ [[X]]` and every `H ⊴ Γ` with `(Γ, H)` relative (T), with `Z`
identified with a dense `σ`-orbit. So no subgroup of `[[X]]` contains a normal subgroup with
relative (T) that is infinite and finitely generated, or that contains an element of infinite
order.

## Role

- A standard way to refute the Haagerup property is a relative-(T) pair `(Γ, H)` with `H` infinite.
  If `H` is also normal in `Γ`, this node says that inside `W(Z)`, and so inside `[[X]]`, `H` must
  be an infinite *locally finite* group.
- This kills every characteristic-zero semidirect witness `EL_2(R) ⋉ R²` with `R` a finitely
  generated ring of characteristic `0` (by `elementary-linear-semidir-pair-relative-t`,
  `R² ⊇ Z` is normal with relative (T)). The same goes for every witness whose normal part is
  torsion-free, finitely generated, or contains a central `Z`.
- **Surviving class.** Positive characteristic. The candidate is `EL_2(F_p[t, t^{-1}]) ⋉
  F_p[t, t^{-1}]²`, whose base is an infinite elementary abelian `p`-group. See
  `full-shift-full-group-has-infinite-normal-relative-t-subgroup`.
- It is a necessary condition for any refutation of `full-shift-topological-full-group-is-a-t-menable`
  that goes through a normal pair. Such a refutation would refute
  `brin-thompson-groups-nv-are-a-t-menable`.

## Remark (non-normal pairs)

The proof uses normality at one place: to know that the projection onto `H`-invariant vectors
commutes with `Γ`. Jolissaint's quantitative characterization of relative (T) for arbitrary pairs,
recalled from memory and not checked verbatim here, would remove that use. So the statement should
hold for every `H ≤ Γ`. This is not claimed.
