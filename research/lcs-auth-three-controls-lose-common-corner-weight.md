Let X_2,Z_2 be the qubit Pauli reflections. In the source space C^2 set

    v_0=(0,1),   v_1=(-4,3)/5,   v_2=(4i,3)/5,
    p_j=|v_j><v_j|.

On C^2 tensor C^2 define

    X=X_2 tensor I,    Z=Z_2 tensor I,
    U_j=I tensor (1-p_j)+X_2 tensor p_j,
    V_j=I tensor (1-p_j)+Z_2 tensor p_j  (j=0,1,2).

Let H be the subgroup of U(4) generated by these eight matrices and rho
its inclusion. The representation

    R_m=rho tensor [1 direct-sum (rho tensor conjugate(rho))]^(tensor m)

has dimension `4 times 17^m`, satisfies every ordinary group-word relation
of the original matrices exactly, and retains every scalar central value,
including the Pauli sign -I. Put `b=(1+X)(1-U_0)/4 in C[H]`.

For every sequence of projections P_m, if

    ||[P_m,R_m(g)]||_2 -> 0
       for g in {X,Z,U_0,U_1,U_2,V_0,V_1,V_2},
    ||R_m(b)P_m||_2 -> 0,

then `tr(P_m)->0`. All traces and Hilbert--Schmidt norms are normalized
on the full representation space.

In particular, this holds for exact common reducing corners for the eight
ports on which the compressed U_0 has the literal form

    P_m U_0^(m) P_m = (P_m-q_m)+(P_m X^(m) P_m) q_m

for any projection `q_m<=P_m`, including arbitrary non-affine readouts.
Requiring every U_j,V_j to have its controlled form is stronger. There is
therefore no uniformly positive-weight common reducing-corner decoder of
this specified form that works on all these exact models.

The artifact also proves the finite-test estimate

    sqrt(tr(P)) <= 4 epsilon + sqrt(3/N+9 beta^m),

where N projective conjugate supports are pairwise disjoint, beta<1 is
explicitly defined from their finite matrix-word support, and epsilon
is the sum of the b-annihilation error and the largest commutator error
against those conjugators. Such conjugators exist for every N and can
be found by an exact terminating rational-matrix word search; no search
or complexity bound is claimed.

This is not absence of all good corners: an original-model summand has
weight `17^(-m)`. It is not a theorem about nonreducing compressions,
replacement ports or anchors, arbitrary matrix readouts, or the full
perfect-completeness LCS hardness target. Applying it to a further group
completion requires that completion to extend the displayed honest port
model, possibly with identity multiplicity. Mere existence-only YES
completeness does not imply this stronger model-preservation hypothesis.
