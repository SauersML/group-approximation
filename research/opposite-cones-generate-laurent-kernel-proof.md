Conjugation by `z=-I` negates the first two exponent coordinates and fixes
the others.  Hence

```text
z Gamma_+ z^(-1)=Gamma_-.
```

## The two opposite cones generate the Laurent elementary group

The ring generated by `R_+` and `R_-` is `R_pm).  More concretely, split
any Laurent monomial as

```text
x_1^m x_2^n x_3^(v_3)...x_d^(v_d)=f_+ f_-,
```

where `f_+` has all first-two exponents nonnegative and `f_-` has them
nonpositive.  For distinct matrix indices `i,j,k`, the elementary
commutator identity gives

```text
[e_(i k)(f_+),e_(k j)(f_-)]=e_(i j)(f_+ f_-).                 (OCL1)
```

Thus `<Gamma_+,Gamma_->` contains every elementary matrix over `R_pm),
and the reverse containment is immediate.  Therefore

```text
<Gamma_+,Gamma_->=E_pm,
<Gamma_+,z>=E_pm rtimes <z>.                                  (OCL2)
```

This proves (FTC1).

## Lift the external Bass--Serre presentation

Let `C_4=<J>`, `C_2=<J^2>=<z>`, and take the standard order-six
subgroup `C_6` in `SL_2(Z)`.  The classical presentation is

```text
SL_2(Z)=C_4 *_(C_2) C_6.                                     (OCL3)
```

All three groups act on the same normal subgroup `E_pm`.  Presenting the
two semidirect vertex groups and identifying their common
`E_pm rtimes C_2` gives exactly the presentation of the semidirect product
by (OCL3); normal forms in the quotient external group give the inverse.
Hence

```text
E_pm rtimes SL_2(Z)
 =(E_pm rtimes C_4) *_(E_pm rtimes C_2) (E_pm rtimes C_6).    (OCL4)
```

Together with (OCL2), this shows that the order-two edge already contains the
entire Laurent kernel.  It proves (FTC2).

## The missing relation in a free cone amalgam

Both `R_+` and `R_-` are polynomial rings, so Suslin stability gives

```text
Gamma_+=SL_r(R_+),       Gamma_-=SL_r(R_-).
```

Their matrix intersection has entries in

```text
R_+ intersect R_-=R_0=k[x_3,...,x_d],
```

and is therefore `SL_r(R_0)=EL_r(R_0)=Gamma_0`.  Thus the two inclusions
give a natural homomorphism

```text
Psi:Gamma_+ *_(Gamma_0) Gamma_- -> E_pm.                      (OCL5)
```

Set

```text
g=e_12(x_1) in Gamma_+,
h=e_23(x_1^(-1)) in Gamma_-.
```

The Steinberg relation (OCL1) says

```text
[g,h]=e_13(1),
```

so

```text
w=g h g^(-1) h^(-1)e_13(-1)
```

lies in `ker(Psi)`.

It is nontrivial in the source of (OCL5).  Indeed, `g,g^(-1)` lie outside
`Gamma_0), `h` lies outside `Gamma_0), and
`h^(-1)e_13(-1)` still has a nonzero `x_1^(-1)` coefficient, so it also
lies outside `Gamma_0`.  The displayed word is therefore an alternating
reduced amalgam normal form of length four.  This proves (FTC3).

Finally, the exponent vectors in (FTC3) are `e_1` and `-e_1`.  No
pointed cone contains both, while their sum is zero and produces the constant
coefficient on the right side of the Steinberg relation.  Hence the
geometric failure of a common positive level and the algebraic kernel of the
tree-free amalgam are the same finite opposite-cone cycle.  Passing from
(OCL5) to its quotient is exactly the step for which the standard sofic,
hyperlinear, and relative-embedding permanence theorems provide no rule.
