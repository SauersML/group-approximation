Let `G` be finitely generated with solvable word problem.

1. By `clapham-fp-embedding-preserves-word-problem` embed `G <= P` with `P`
   finitely presented and still having decidable word problem.
2. By `shift-higman-compiler` choose `nu : P -> N` so that the envelope
   `E_nu(P)` is finitely presented.
3. By `mallery-zaremsky-shift-similar-envelope` that envelope is a finitely
   generated **strongly** shift-similar subgroup of `Sym(N)` containing `P`.
4. By `fp-strongly-shift-similar-admits-type-a-action` its natural action on
   `N` is of type (A).
5. By `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups` every subgroup of
   `E_nu(P)` embeds in a finitely presented simple group.  `G <= P <=
   E_nu(P)`.  `∎`

## Why every prerequisite is genuinely used

Clapham is what makes step 2 applicable at all -- the compiler statement
quantifies over finitely presented groups, and the input `G` is only
finitely generated.  It is also the step that keeps decidability, which by
`compilers-cannot-drop-the-decidability-hypothesis` is not optional.
Mallery--Zaremsky supplies the envelope and, critically, the word
"strongly"; Proposition 5.3 hypothesizes strong shift-similarity and would
not fire on a merely shift-similar group.  Corollary B supplies both the
simple group and the descent to subgroups.

Step 2 is the only open input, and it is the only step that is not in print.

## What makes this reduction worth carrying beside the other one

Every object in it is explicit.  `P` is a finite presentation, `nu` is a
bijection to the naturals, `E_nu(P)` is generated by a transported regular
representation together with the eventually 2-periodic permutations.  There
is no envelope produced by Zorn's lemma, no maximal normal subgroup, no
coefficient ring, and no simple group to construct -- the simple group is
`SV_{E_nu(P)}` and the imported corollary builds it.  So a proof attempt has
something to compute with from the first line, which is what
`boone-higman-conjecture` records as the deficiency of its first route.

Against that: the statement being reduced to is a finite-presentability
question, and finite presentability of a permutation group defined by an
enumeration is not obviously easier than any other formulation.  What is
gained is that the question is **one** question about **one** explicit
family, and that it is known to require the decidability input, which tells a
prover what the proof must consume.

## Relation to the fiber-product route

`boone-higman-via-fiber-product-criterion` reaches the same imported
corollary through a different action of a different group.  The two routes
share only the corollary.  They are not variants: one asks for an abstract
group with a normal subgroup meeting four conditions, the other asks for a
presentation of a concrete permutation group.  Neither open input is known to
imply the other.
