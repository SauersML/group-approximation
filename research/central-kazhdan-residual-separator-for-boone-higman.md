**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary from the cited nodes; no priority claimed). The separating side is the OPEN node `fp-simple-group-with-kazhdan-subgroup-of-non-residual-centre`.

## Definition

- For a group `K`, let `R_fin(K)` be its finite residual, the intersection of all finite-index subgroups.
- Let `P_Z` be the class of groups `G` such that every finitely generated subgroup `K ≤ G` with property (T) has `Z(K) ∩ R_fin(K)` finite. These are the central elements of `K` that die in every finite quotient.

## Theorem

1. **Hereditary.** `P_Z` is closed under isomorphism and subgroups, since a finitely generated Kazhdan subgroup of a subgroup is one of `G`.
2. **(S2) holds, with decidable witnesses outside `P_Z`.** Each of the following has solvable word problem.
   - **Deligne's lattice** `Γ~_g`, `g >= 2`. It is Kazhdan with centre `Z` (`deligne-universal-cover-lattice-is-non-rf-kazhdan`). Every finite quotient kills `2Z` (Deligne; for `g >= 4` the finite residual is exactly `2Z`, by `deligne-lattice-is-universal-central-extension-for-g-ge-4`). So `Z(Γ~) ∩ R_fin = 2Z` is infinite.
   - **`St_N(R_L)`, `N >= 3`.** It is finitely presented and Kazhdan (`steinberg-finite-presentation-and-kazhdan-theorem`), has solvable word problem, and has centre containing `K_2(N,R_L) ≅ Q^×` (`leavitt-resolvent-k2-stability-holds`). It has no nontrivial finite quotient: it is generated by the root subgroups `x_ij(R_L) ≅ (R_L,+)`, which are divisible because `Q ⊆ R_L`, and a divisible group has trivial image in any finite group. So `R_fin = St_N(R_L)`, and `Z ∩ R_fin ⊇ Q^×` is infinite.
   - **Nowak's `Q_{g,c}`,** `g >= 3`, `c >= 2`. Its Kazhdan subgroup `E`, with infinite centre inside the non-residually-finite part, is exhibited in `torelli-lower-central-quotients-are-not-residually-finite`.
3. **So `P_Z` separates Boone–Higman iff (S1_Z) holds:** every finitely generated Kazhdan subgroup of every finitely presented simple group has `Z ∩ R_fin` finite. If (S1_Z) holds, Boone–Higman fails already at the finitely presented decidable group `St_N(R_L)`, and at Deligne's lattice. Conversely, Boone–Higman implies `fp-simple-group-with-kazhdan-subgroup-of-non-residual-centre`.
4. **(S1_Z) holds on every host family where Kazhdan subgroups are understood.**
   - **Rank-one cone hosts.** By `rank-one-cone-hosts-put-fw-groups-in-local-map-groups`, FW (hence Kazhdan) subgroups are finite for V, V_{n,r} and SFT or graph full groups. They are residually finite for Röver–Nekrashevych groups, Exel–Pardo and Katsura hosts. A virtually residually finite `K` has `R_fin(K) = 1`, since every finite-index subgroup of a finite-index subgroup has finite index in `K`.
   - **Normal germ extensions of V.** Kazhdan subgroups are finite (`normal-germ-extensions-of-v-have-only-finite-kazhdan-subgroups`).
   - **Linear groups and the host `W`.** Their Kazhdan subgroups are residually finite. The witness of `kazhdan-subgroups-of-fp-simple-groups-can-have-infinite-centre` has infinite centre, but that centre is detected by finite quotients.
   - **Simple hosts themselves.** Every finitely presented simple group is centreless.
5. **Not a complexity separator.** Deligne's lattice is a central extension of `Sp_2g(Z)` by `Z`, and its word problem is elementary recursive. The image in `Sp_2g(Z)` comes from matrix multiplication. The central coordinate is a sum of values of a bounded computable 2-cocycle; that the cocycle is computable from matrices is recalled, not re-derived. So a word-problem bound `P_T` of `bh-separators-must-omit-nested-decidable-hosts` §3, with `T` large enough to admit this witness, does not see why `Γ~` is excluded. `P_Z` is a "genuinely new invariant" in the sense of §4 there: neither a decision problem nor a complexity bound.
6. **Host transfer is consistent.** The simple Kazhdan host `K ⊇ Γ~` of §1 there contains `Γ~`, so `K ∉ P_Z`. So does its finitely presented overgroup `C`.

## Where (S1_Z) could break

A finitely presented simple group violating (S1_Z) must lie outside every family in item 4. These are exactly the swarm's rank ≥ 2 programmes:
- **Deligne's lattice** sits in the finitely generated simple commutator subgroup of the Lagrangian Brin–Thompson group (`deligne-lattice-in-fg-simple-lagrangian-brin-thompson-group`). Finite presentation of that group, the 𝒯_m / 2V_Λ non-LCM gate, would kill `P_Z`.
- **A finitely presented simple overgroup of `St_N(R_L)`** would kill it. The GL_n(Q) Steinberg route needs one.
- **Master-route hosts** (board/SYNTHESIS.md) applied to `Γ~` or `St_N(R_L)` would kill it.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Residual invisibility.** Every host family whose Kazhdan subgroups are understood detects central elements by finite quotients. A universal host has to do the opposite: carry a Kazhdan group whose centre dies in every finite quotient.
- **One test, three places.** This single test covers Deligne's lattice, Q_{g,c}, and the gq swarm's scalar obstruction (the central Q^× of St_N(R_L)). It is also the sharpest reason rank-one hosts cannot be universal.
- **Where the question now sits.** The question "is Boone–Higman false?" now has a concrete invariant to aim at, and the question "is it true?" has a concrete object to build: a finitely presented simple group containing Deligne's lattice.
