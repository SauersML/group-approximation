Write

```text
s = sup_(lambda in Lambda) ctdim(B_lambda).
```

For every `lambda`, let `K_lambda` be the ideal of `A` generated by
`B_lambda`.  The hereditary algebra `B_lambda` is full in `K_lambda`, so
Morita invariance and ideal monotonicity give

```text
ctdim(B_lambda)=ctdim(K_lambda)<=ctdim(A).
```

Taking the supremum proves `s<=ctdim(A)`.

Separability reduces the opposite inequality to one increasing sequence.
Choose a dense sequence `(a_k)` in `A`.  For every pair `(k,m)`, choose
`b_(k,m)` in some `B_(lambda(k,m))` with

```text
norm(a_k-b_(k,m)) < 1/m.
```

Enumerate the pairs `(k,m)`.  Directedness lets us recursively choose
indices `mu_1,mu_2,...` so that

```text
B_(mu_1) subset B_(mu_2) subset ...
```

and the first `n` chosen approximants all belong to `B_(mu_n)`.  Put
`C_n=B_(mu_n)`.  Then the `C_n` are hereditary and their union is dense in
`A`.

It remains to prove the upper bound from this countable hereditary core.
Fix a continuous-trace subquotient

```text
D=J/I
```

of `A`, and put

```text
H_n=(J intersect C_n)/(I intersect C_n).
```

The canonical map identifies `H_n` with the image of `J intersect C_n` in
`J/I`.  This image is hereditary in `D`: if `x,y` lie in `J intersect C_n`
and `a in J`, then `xay` lies in `J intersect C_n`, since
`C_n A C_n subset C_n`.  Thus every nonzero `H_n` has continuous trace and
is one of the subquotients tested by `ctdim(C_n)`.

The union of the `H_n` is dense in `D`.  First,

```text
J = closure(union_n (J intersect C_n)).                 (1)
```

For a positive `a in J`, approximate `a^(1/2)` by `b in C_n`, and choose a
positive contraction `e` from an approximate identity for `J` such that
`a^(1/2)e a^(1/2)` is close to `a`.  Then

```text
b* e b belongs to J intersect C_n
```

and is as close to `a` as desired.  Here membership in `C_n` uses
`C_n A C_n subset C_n`.  Linear combinations of positive elements give
(1), and passing (1) through the quotient map gives the asserted density.

Let `L_n` be the ideal of `D` generated by `H_n`.  These are increasing
ideals with dense union.  Since `H_n` is full hereditary in `L_n`, their
primitive spectra are homeomorphic.  Thus the `L_n` correspond to
increasing open subsets `U_n` of `X=Prim(D)` such that

```text
X = union_n U_n,
dim(U_n)=dim(Prim(H_n))<=ctdim(C_n)<=s.                 (2)
```

If `s` is infinite there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise, `X` is a
second-countable locally compact Hausdorff space, hence metrizable.  Every
`U_n` is an `F_sigma` subset of `X`; express it as a countable union of
closed subsets of `X`, each of dimension at most `dim(U_n)`.  Equation (2)
and the countable closed-sum theorem for covering dimension give

```text
dim(X)<=s.
```

Taking the supremum over all continuous-trace subquotients `D` of `A`
proves `ctdim(A)<=s`, and hence `(H)`.

For the inductive-limit formulation, identify every stage with its image in
the limit.  Each stage image is hereditary in the limit.  Indeed, for two
stage elements and a limit element, approximate the middle element at some
stage, pass to one later stage dominating both indices, use heredity there,
and take the norm limit.  The stage images form an upward-directed family
with dense union, so `(H)` applies.  Finally,
`stw81-continuous-trace-shadow-invariant` identifies `ctdim<=1` with the
LXXXI shadow hypothesis.

**Trust boundary.**  Separability is essential to the countable extraction;
no covering-dimension theorem for uncountable unions is invoked.  The family
need only be directed and hereditary.  In particular, the proof does not
smuggle an uncountable inductive-limit continuity assertion through an
ordinal limit stage.
