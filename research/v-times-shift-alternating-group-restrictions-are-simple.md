title: "The alternating full group of V times the full shift maps onto uncountably many pairwise distinct simple restriction quotients"
---

Notation:

- `C = {0,1}^N` and `Σ = {0,1}^Z`.
- `G_V` is the groupoid of germs of Thompson's group `V` acting on `C`.
- `Z ⋉ Σ` is the transformation groupoid of the shift `σ`.
- `G = G_V × (Z ⋉ Σ)` is the product groupoid on `X = C × Σ`.
- `A(·)` is Nekrashevych's alternating full group (arXiv:1511.08241).

Then:

1. The closed `G`-invariant subsets of `X` are exactly the sets `C × Y` with
   `Y ⊆ Σ` closed and shift-invariant.
2. For each such `Y`, restriction is a surjective homomorphism
   `ρ_Y : A(G) -> A(G|_{C×Y})`.
3. If `Y` is an infinite minimal subshift, `A(G|_{C×Y})` is a nontrivial
   simple group.
4. If `Y_1 ≠ Y_2` are minimal subshifts, then `ker ρ_{Y_1} ≠ ker ρ_{Y_2}`.
5. `Σ` contains `2^aleph_0` distinct infinite minimal subshifts (the Sturmian
   subshifts `X_α` for irrational `α`).

So one finitely generated group `A(G)` (finitely generated by Nekrashevych,
since `G` is expansive) has uncountably many distinct kernels with simple
quotients. Whether the kernels are perfect
(`v-times-shift-restriction-kernels-are-perfect`) and whether `A(G)` is of type
FP_2 (`v-times-shift-alternating-group-is-of-type-fp2`) are open. Together they
would answer the first part of Zaremsky's Problem 1.20
(`uncountably-many-simple-fp2-via-v-times-shift-restrictions`).
