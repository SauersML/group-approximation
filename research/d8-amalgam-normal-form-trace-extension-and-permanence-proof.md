# Realize the mixed reflection relation by a controlled Pauli pair

## 1. The universal two-involution cell is \(D_8\)

Consider

\[
 D=\langle u,v,z\mid
 u^2=v^2=z^2=1,\ [z,u]=[z,v]=1,\ [u,v]=z\rangle.
 \tag{1}
\]

Because \(u\) and \(v\) are involutions,

\[
 [u,v]=uvuv=(uv)^2.
 \tag{2}
\]

Writing \(r=uv\), relations (1)--(2) give

\[
 r^2=z,\qquad r^4=1,\qquad uru=r^{-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]

Thus every element has one of the eight forms

\[
 r^j,\quad ur^j\qquad(0\leq j<4).
 \tag{4}
\]

The usual symmetries of a square realize all eight forms and have
\(r^2\neq1\), so \(D\cong D_8\) and \(z\) is its nontrivial central
involution.

Let \(G\) contain an involution \(w\).  Identifying the two order-two
subgroups gives

\[
 \Gamma_w=G*_{\langle w\rangle=\langle z\rangle}D_8.
 \tag{5}
\]

The normal-form theorem for free products with amalgamation embeds both
vertex groups.  In particular the identified element remains nontrivial.
If a finite presentation of \(G\) is given, presentation (1) and the one
additional relation \(w=z\) give a finite presentation of \(\Gamma_w\).

## 2. Controlled Pauli matrices extend every representation

Let \(\pi:G\to\mathcal U(M)\) be a unitary representation in a finite von
Neumann algebra.  Put

\[
 W=\pi(w),\qquad P=\frac{1-W}{2}.
 \tag{6}
\]

Since \(W=W^*=W^{-1}\), \(P\) is a projection and

\[
 W=1-2P.
 \tag{7}
\]

Choose Pauli involutions

\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad XZ=-ZX.
 \tag{8}
\]

Define in \(M\overline\otimes M_2\)

\[
 U=(1-P)\otimes I+P\otimes X,\qquad
 V=(1-P)\otimes I+P\otimes Z.
 \tag{9}
\]

The two summands in each expression have orthogonal central supports
relative to the algebra generated by \(P,U,V\), so

\[
 U^*=U,\quad V^*=V,\quad U^2=V^2=1.
 \tag{10}
\]

On the \(1-P\) corner, \(U=V=I\).  On the \(P\) corner, their commutator is

\[
 XZXZ=-I.
 \tag{11}
\]

Therefore

\[
 [U,V]=(1-P)\otimes I-P\otimes I
       =(1-2P)\otimes I
       =W\otimes I.
 \tag{12}
\]

Moreover (12) commutes with \(U,V\), so all relations (1) hold with
\(z\mapsto W\otimes I\).  The universal property of (5) now extends

\[
 g\longmapsto\pi(g)\otimes I
 \tag{13}
\]

to a representation of \(\Gamma_w\).

If \(\tau\) is a trace on \(M\), then
\(\tau\otimes{\rm tr}_2\) restricts along (13) to \(\tau\).  Thus the
extension preserves every old mixed moment and the old trace exactly.  No
centrality of \(w\) in \(G\) is needed; its image need commute only with the
new \(D_8\) generators.

## 3. Hyperlinearity is unchanged

If \(\Gamma_w\) is hyperlinear, then its subgroup \(G\) is hyperlinear.

Conversely, suppose \(G\) is hyperlinear.  The finite group \(D_8\) is
hyperlinear, and the common subgroup \(\langle w\rangle\cong C_2\) is
amenable.  Hyperlinear permanence for amalgamated free products over
amenable subgroups gives hyperlinearity of (5).  Hence

\[
 \Gamma_w\text{ hyperlinear}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G\text{ hyperlinear}.
 \tag{14}
\]

This equivalence is independent of the particular representation in
Section 2.  It is a statement about the canonical group traces.

More generally, if a reflection \(w\) in one vertex and a reflection \(c\)
in a second vertex are both identified with \(z\), the result is a
three-vertex tree of groups with two \(C_2\) edges.  If the two old vertices
are hyperlinear, the whole group is hyperlinear by two applications of the
same permanence theorem.  Thus adding a separate external BCS-reflection
vertex does not change the conclusion.

## 4. What an arbitrary unitary representation recovers

Let \(\rho:\Gamma_w\to\mathcal U(N)\) be arbitrary.  The relation \(w^2=1\)
makes

\[
 P_\rho=\frac{1-\rho(w)}2
 \tag{15}
\]

a projection.  Since \(w=[u,v]\) in \(\Gamma_w\),

\[
 P_\rho=\frac{1-\rho([u,v])}2.
 \tag{16}
\]

This is the strongest universal decoding supplied by the cell.  It
identifies a spectral projection of one group word.  It contains no
reference to a proposed coefficient formula

\[
 p=\sum_g c_g g
 \tag{17}
\]

in other generators.  Equality between (15) and the evaluation of (17) is
a linear identity in a group algebra, not a group-word identity.

For the Whitehead construction over a coefficient projection \(p\), one
has inside the actual matrix group

\[
 W_p^2=\operatorname{diag}(1-2p,1-2p).
 \tag{18}
\]

Using \(w=W_p^2\) in (5) transfers this particular reflection to the
\(D_8\) commutator and prevents the relation from surviving the assignment
which kills \(W_p\) while retaining \(z\).  Bass--Serre normal form proves
the exact transferred mark survives.

But Section 3 also proves that the transfer has no independent
hyperlinearity content.  When the coefficient host is linear or otherwise
hyperlinear, the enlarged group remains hyperlinear.  When the host is
unknown, (14) makes the new question exactly the old one.

Finally, suppose one tries to add finitely many group relators forcing
(15) to equal a nonaffine selector atom.  If those relators replace (17) by
a word in commuting finite-order selector elements inside one simple finite
central block, the flat-restriction theorem forces the resulting joint
spectrum to be a character coset, hence affine in the Boolean case.  If
(17) is retained as a genuine sum, it has not become a group relator.
Accordingly the controlled-Pauli cell closes perfect completeness and exact
mark survival, but not predicate authentication.
