Notation as in the target. All rings are `F_2`-algebras, so `-1 = 1`. Norms are normalized Hilbert--Schmidt.
`delta = max_i ||rho(k_i) - 1||_2`.

## Step 0. The shadow homomorphism

Let `rho = pi o St_20(phi) o Phi_P` with `phi : P -> B` unital and `pi : St_20(B) -> U(N)`, `N < infinity`.
Put `phi_+- = phi|R_+-`. These agree on `R_0`, so `Phi := St_20(phi) o Phi_P : Gamma -> St_20(B)` is exactly the
shadow homomorphism of Step 0 of `leavitt-cone-head-bound-semisimple-ring-shadows-proof`, now over an arbitrary `B`.
Write `S_j = phi(s_j)`, `T_i = phi(t_i)`, `Q = phi(q)`. Then `Phi(z) = x_13(Q)`.

## Step 1. Reduction to a finite shadow ring

`B` has `2 = 0` (it is an `F_2`-algebra through `phi`). By `positive-characteristic-steinberg-unitary-images-are-finite`
(`m = 2`, `n = 20`), `I = {a in B : pi(x_12(a)) = 1}` is a two-sided ideal with `|B/I| <= 2^N`, and `pi` factors as
`pi' o St_20(pr)` with `pr : B -> B' := B/I`. Replacing `phi` by `pr o phi` and `pi` by `pi'` leaves `rho` unchanged.
So assume `B` finite.

This reduction must come *before* Step 3: for infinite `B` the defects need not generate `B` (for `B = R` they are
all zero). For finite `B` they do.

## Step 2. The six defects

Step 1 of the semisimple route uses only the Steinberg relation `[x_12(a), x_23(b)] = x_13(ab)` in `St_20(B)` and
the Leavitt relations in `Delta`. It is valid verbatim for every `B`. It gives six words `g_d in K`, each a fixed
product of `L_d` conjugates of `k_i^(+-1)` independent of `B, phi, pi`. They satisfy `Phi(g_d) = x_13(d)` for

```text
d in  { T_i S_j + delta_ij (i, j = 1, 2),   S_1 T_1 + Q,   S_2 T_2 + 1 + Q },          (D6)
```

and hence, by unitary invariance and the triangle inequality,

```text
nu(d) := ||pi(x_13(d)) - 1||_2 = ||rho(g_d) - 1||_2 <= L_d delta.                     (1)
```

## Step 3. The defects generate the finite ring

Step 2 of the semisimple route, up to and including Lemma 2.1, uses only finiteness of `B`. Let `D` be the
two-sided ideal generated by `(D6)`. Then `B/D` receives a unital map from `R`, and Lemma 2.1 ("a finite ring that
receives a unital map from `R` is zero") gives `D = B`. In particular `Q in D`. The semisimple decomposition that
follows Lemma 2.1 there is not used.

## Step 4. Ideal domination at position (1, 3)

Apply `char-two-root-displacement-is-two-sided-ideal-dominated` with
- `n = 20`, `p = r = 10`, `(i0, j0) = (1, 3)`;
- the partition `I = {1, 2, 4, 5, ..., 11}` (so `1 in I`) and `K = {3, 12, 13, ..., 20}` (so `3 in K`);
- generators `e_1, ..., e_6` the defects `(D6)`, and target element `Q`.

`C_(10,10) = 4 / (0.9 * 0.9) = 400/81 < 5`. So

```text
||rho(z) - 1||_2^2 = nu(Q)^2 <= 5 sum_d nu(d)^2.                                         (2)
```

No Weyl element is needed, because `x_13` already lies in the block root group of this partition.

## Step 5. Conclusion

By `(1)` and `(2)`,

```text
||rho(z) - 1||_2^2 <= 5 * 6 * (max_d L_d)^2 delta^2 = 30 (max_d L_d)^2 delta^2.
```

So `(RS_all)` holds with `L = sqrt(30) max_d L_d`. `L` depends only on the fixed relators `k_i` of `K`. It is
independent of `B`, `phi`, `pi` and `N`. ∎

## Where the argument stops

The only input from `rho` is that it factors through `Phi_P`, which gives the exact identities `rho(g_d) =
pi(x_13(d))` and `rho(z) = pi(x_13(Q))` inside one Steinberg group over one ring. For a general genuine
representation of `Gamma`, the two halves `rho|Delta_+-` are representations of `St_20(R_+-)`. Each is individually a
ring shadow of its half after Step 1's reduction, since `R_+-` has characteristic two. But the two finite shadow rings
`B_+-` need not be glued along one ring: the cross relators `[x_ij(a), x_kl(b)]`, with `a in R_+`, `b in R_-` and
disjoint index pairs, lie in `ker Phi_P ∩ K` and are only `delta`-trivial. Closing `(QC)` now needs exactly the
following. A representation in which these relators are `delta`-trivial must have its `x_13`-block measure within
`O(delta)` of that of a `St_20(P)`-representation. That is a stability statement, not a ring statement.
