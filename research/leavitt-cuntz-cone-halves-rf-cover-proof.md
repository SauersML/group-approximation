Notation as in `(CC1)`. `F = F_2`, `n = 20`; Steps 1–3 work for every `n >= 3`. Commutators are `[g,h] = g h g^(-1) h^(-1)`,
so `[x_ik(a), x_kj(b)] = x_ij(ab)` for distinct `i,j,k`.

## Step 0. Identifications

By `leavitt-steinberg-map-iso-from-rank-three` (KH2), `St_20(R) -> GL_20(R)` is an isomorphism, and
`GL_20(R) = E_20(R)`. So the subgroup of `Delta` generated by the Steinberg symbols `x_ij(a)`, `a in R_+`, is
identified with `EL_20(R_+) <= GL_20(R_+)`, and likewise for `R_-`. By
`leavitt-corner-host-is-the-simple-leavitt-unit-group`, `Delta ≅ R^x` is simple, and by
`leavitt-unit-group-finitely-presented` it is finitely presented.

## Step 1. A filtered faithful module

Let `Omega_0` be the set of sequences in `{1,2}^N` that are eventually constant `2`. Every `omega in Omega_0` is
uniquely `omega = u 2^infinity` with `u` a finite word not ending in `2`. Its **canonical length** is `l(omega) = |u|`.
Let `N = F[Omega_0]` and define

```text
s_i(omega) = i omega,        t_i(j omega) = delta_ij omega.        (P1)
```

`Omega_0` is closed under prepending and deleting a first letter. Pointwise, `t_i s_j = delta_ij`, and
`s_1 t_1 + s_2 t_2 = 1` because every sequence starts with `1` or `2`. So `N` is a unital `R`-module. It is nonzero,
and its annihilator is a two-sided ideal not containing `1`. By `leavitt-algebras-l-k-1-n-are-simple`, `N` is
**faithful**.

Put `N_k = span{omega : l(omega) >= k}`. Then:
- `l(1 omega) = l(omega) + 1`;
- `l(2 omega) = l(omega) + 1` if `l(omega) >= 1`, while `2 · 2^infinity = 2^infinity`;
- `q omega` is `omega` or `0`.

So `s_1`, `s_2` and `q` map every `N_k` into itself, and hence so does all of `R_+`. Also `dim N/N_k = 2^(k-1)`
(the words of length `< k` not ending in `2`), and `∩_k N_k = 0`.

## Step 2. Residual finiteness of the halves (item 1)

`M_n(R_+)` acts on `N^n` preserving `N_k^n`. This gives unital ring maps `rho_k : M_n(R_+) -> End_F(N^n / N_k^n)`
into finite rings.

Let `a != 0` in `M_n(R_+)`. Pick an entry `a_ij != 0`. By faithfulness there is a basis vector `omega` with
`a_ij omega != 0`. Then `a(omega e_j)` is a nonzero, finitely supported vector. It is not in `N_k^n` once `k` exceeds
the canonical lengths in its support, so `rho_k(a) != 0`.

For `g != 1` in `GL_n(R_+)`, apply this to `a = g - 1`: `rho_k(g) != 1` in the finite group
`GL(N^n / N_k^n)`. So `GL_n(R_+)` is residually finite.

`*` is an anti-automorphism of `R`, since the defining relations are `*`-symmetric. So `g -> (g^*)^(-1)`, with `g^*`
the `*`-transpose, is a group isomorphism `GL_n(R_+) -> GL_n(R_-)`. It sends `x_ij(a)` to `x_ji(a^*)`
(characteristic `2`), so it carries `Delta_+` onto `Delta_-`.

*Finite generation.* `R_+` is additively spanned by monomials in `s_1, s_2, q`. By `x_ij(a+b) = x_ij(a) x_ij(b)`
and the commutator formula, `Delta_+` is generated by the `x_ij(c)`, `c in {1, s_1, s_2, q}`. `z = x_13(q)` lies in
`Delta_+ ∩ Delta_-`.

## Step 3. The intersection (item 2)

`R` is `Z`-graded with `deg s_i = 1` and `deg t_i = -1`: the defining relations are homogeneous of degree `0`.
- Every monomial in `s_1, s_2, q` is homogeneous of degree equal to its number of `s`-letters, which is `>= 0`. The
  degree-`0` monomials are the powers of `q`, that is `1` and `q`. Dually, `R_-` is spanned by homogeneous elements
  of degree `<= 0`, with degree-`0` part `span{1,q}`.
- Take `a in R_+ ∩ R_-`. Its homogeneous components are unique. The first description gives components only in
  degrees `>= 0`, the second only in degrees `<= 0`. So `a = a_0 in span{1,q}`.
- `q != 0`, since `t_1 q s_1 = 1`. And `q != 1`: otherwise `s_2 t_2 = 0` and `1 = (t_2 s_2)(t_2 s_2) = t_2 (s_2 t_2) s_2 = 0`.
- As `q^2 = q`, `span{1,q} = Fq x F(1-q) ≅ F x F`.

An element of `Delta_0` is a matrix `g` with `g, g^(-1)` in `M_n(F x F)`. So
`Delta_0 <= GL_n(F)^2 = SL_n(F)^2 = EL_n(F x F) <= Delta_0`. Hence `Delta_0 = SL_n(F)^2`, and `z = (x_13(1), 1)`.

## Step 4. The cover (item 3)

`R` is generated as a ring by `s_1, s_2, t_1, t_2`, which lie in `R_+ ∪ R_-`. By the commutator formula,
`Delta = E_n(R) = <Delta_+, Delta_->`. The inclusions agree on `Delta_0`, so they induce `p : Gamma -> Delta`, which is
onto.

**Amalgam lemma.** Let `A, B` be residually finite and `H` finite. Then `A *_H B` is residually finite.
- *Finite case.* Take `A, B` finite with `m = |A||B|`. Let `A` and `B` act freely on sets of size `m`. Restricted to
  `H`, both are free `H`-sets of size `m`, hence isomorphic. Identifying them gives
  `theta : A *_H B -> Sym(m)`, injective on `A` and on `B`.
- `K_0 = ker theta` meets every conjugate of `A` and `B` trivially. So it acts freely, without inversions, on the
  Bass–Serre tree, whose vertex stabilizers are the conjugates of `A` and `B` (Serre, *Trees*, Ch. I §4.1). A group
  acting freely on a tree is free (Serre, *Trees*, Ch. I §3.3).
- So `A *_H B` is virtually free. Free groups are residually finite, and a group with a residually finite normal
  subgroup of finite index is residually finite (take the core of the separating subgroup). So `A *_H B` is
  residually finite.
- *General case.* Take `1 != g = c_1 ... c_l` in reduced form, with alternating syllables from `A \ H` and `B \ H`, or
  `g in A ∪ B`. Choose finite-index normal subgroups `A_1 <| A` and `B_1 <| B` that avoid the finitely many elements
  `c_i h` (`h in H`, `c_i` a syllable of the matching factor), avoid `H \ {1}`, and avoid `g` when `l = 1`. Then `H`
  embeds in `A/A_1` and `B/B_1`, and every syllable maps outside `H`.
- So the image of `g` in `(A/A_1) *_H (B/B_1)` is reduced, hence nontrivial by the normal form theorem (Serre,
  *Trees*, Ch. I §1.2), and the finite case separates it.

Apply the lemma with `A = Delta_+`, `B = Delta_-` and `H = Delta_0` (Steps 2–3).

*Finite normal generation.* `Gamma` is generated by the finitely many generators of Step 2, and their images generate
the finitely presented group `Delta`. By Tietze moves, `Delta = <Y | r_1..r_m>` on these generators, and `K` is the
normal closure in `Gamma` of the images of the `r_j`.

*The mixed relator.* `a = x_12(s_1) in Delta_+ \ Delta_0` and `b = x_23(t_1) in Delta_- \ Delta_0`, since
`s_1, t_1 ∉ span{1,q}`. So `a b a^(-1) b^(-1)` is reduced of length `4` in `Gamma`, so it lies outside `Delta_0`, and in
particular it is `!= z`. In `Delta` it equals `x_13(s_1 t_1) = z`. So `kappa in K \ {1}`.

*Faithful MF representation.* List the finite quotients of the countable residually finite group `Gamma`, each
repeated infinitely often, and take their regular representations `lambda_k`. For `g != 1` in a finite group, the
permutation matrix `lambda(g)` moves some basis vector, so `||lambda(g) - 1|| >= sqrt 2`. Thus
`Gamma -> ∏_k U(|Q_k|) / ⊕_k` is injective. In particular `z` survives.

## Step 5. Consequences (item 4)

**(a)** `Delta` is simple, so `Rad_MF(Delta) = Delta` iff every MF representation `pi` of `Delta` has `pi(z) = 1`.
MF representations of `Delta` are exactly the MF representations of `Gamma` trivial on `k_1..k_r`, via `pi -> pi∘p`.
If a proof of `pi(z) = 1` used only that `pi∘p` is a homomorphism on `Gamma`, then it would apply to the faithful
representation of Step 4, which is a contradiction. So some `pi(k_i) = 1` must enter.

**(b)** Suppose `Ã_x in C[Gamma]` lift the `A_x` of `(LNC3)` and satisfy the BCS relations in `P C[Gamma] P`, with
`P = (1-z)/2`. `P` is a nonzero projection, since `z != 1` in `Gamma`. By `lin-explicit-fixed-bcs-gap-via-generic-conversion`
with `paddock-slofstra-bcs-forbidden-projection-dictionary`, `A(B_loop)` has no Connes-embeddable tracial state. So
`nonce-bcs-unital-algebraic-corner-map-forces-nonhyperlinear` makes `Gamma` nonhyperlinear. But `Gamma` is residually
finite, hence sofic, hence hyperlinear: a contradiction. Since `ker(C[Gamma] -> C[Delta])` is the ideal spanned by the
`g(k - 1)` with `k in K`, every `(LNC3)` certificate needs some identity that holds only modulo this ideal.
