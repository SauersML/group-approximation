**ESTABLISHED.**  Use the notation and synchronized factorization of
`compressed-k2m-locks-gauge-but-needs-matrix-separator`.  Put

```text
S_j=B_jG_jB_j^*,             S=sum_j S_j,
R_S=I-2S,                    R_C=I-2C.                 (KAS1)
```

The `S_j` are orthogonal, hence `S` is a projection.  The synchronized
capacity leakage is exactly

```text
ell_sync=sum_j||(I-C)A_1B_jG_j||_2^2
        =||(I-C)A_1S||_2^2.                            (KAS2)
```

Moreover

```text
tr(sum_jG_j)-tr(C)
 <=ell_sync
 <=1/4||R_C A_1-A_1R_S||_2^2
  =1/4||R_C A_1R_SA_1^*-I||_2^2.                      (KAS3)
```

Thus, once `R_S` and `R_C` have been authenticated as finite-matrix phase
tags, one ordinary covariance word pays the complete `K_(2,m)` source
surplus.  No further block-Gram estimate or multiplicity alignment is needed
for this scalar.

This does **not** make `(KAS3)` a universal group relation.  Both `S` and
`C` are analytic forbidden-weighted cuts.  Declaring their reflections to be
literal packet words with the covariance word as a relator would make the
right side zero in the regular representation and contradict a positive
source-capacity surplus.  The remaining compiler gate is therefore exactly
finite-matrix authentication of these two aggregate signs, with the
covariance row derived from the shared non-CE source rather than imposed on
every representation.

Fourier normalizer commutators do not supply this missing comparison.  They
force the transported source projections to reduce the target phase
partition, but they are blind to a source projection lying wholly in the
wrong target phase.  The covariance word in `(KAS3)` compares the phase
labels themselves and detects that diagonal mismatch.
