Citation import, not a reproof.

**Primary source (read).** L. Bartholdi and B. Virág, "Amenability via random walks",
arXiv:math/0305262v1 (19 May 2003). The PDF was fetched from arXiv on 2026-09-16 and its text
extracted. Mathematical symbols were partly lost in extraction; they are restored below from
context, and restorations are marked [restored]. The published version, Duke Math. J. 130 (2005),
39–56, is listed with this bibliographic data by Benli–Grigorchuk–de la Harpe (below). It was not
read, so its numbering was not checked.

- Section 1 defines `a = (1, b)`, `b = (1, a)ε`, with right action `v ↦ v^g`, and calls the group
  `G`. Page 1–2: "We have EG ⊆ SG ⊆ AG" [restored: the inclusion signs were dropped].
- **Theorem 1**: "We have lim |Z_n|/n = 0 with probability 1. This implies that the heat kernel
  decays sub-exponentially, the group G is amenable (see Kaimanovich and Woess (2002)), and
  SG ≠ AG."
- **Lemma 11**: "G has the following presentation: G = ⟨a, b | σ^n[a, a^b] ∀n ∈ N⟩, where σ is the
  substitution b ↦ a, a ↦ b^2." The proof starts from "Proposition 9 of Grigorchuk and Żuk
  (2002a)".
- **Theorem 12**: "G embeds in the finitely presented group
  G~ = ⟨a, t | a^(t^2) = a^2, [[[a, t^(−1)], a], a] = 1⟩. Furthermore, G~ is also amenable, and does
  not belong to the class SG. This implies that the classes SG and AG are distinct, even in the
  realm of finitely presented groups."
- **Proof of Theorem 12** (extracts): "Let G~ be the HNN extension of G along the endomorphism σ
  identifying G and σ(G): it is given by the presentation
  G~ = ⟨a, b, t | a^t = σ(a), b^t = σ(b), relations in G⟩. A simpler presentation follows by
  eliminating the generator b. Consider the kernel H of the map a ↦ 1, t ↦ t from G~ to ⟨t⟩. Since
  the HNN extension is 'ascending', we have H = ⋃_(n∈Z) G^(t^n), an ascending union. Therefore H is
  amenable, and since G~ is an extension of H by Z, it is also amenable. Finally, if G~ were in SG,
  then G would also be in SG, since it is the subgroup of G~ generated by a and a^(t^(−1)).
  However, Proposition 13 of Grigorchuk and Żuk (2002a) shows that G is not in SG."
- Reference list: "R. I. Grigorchuk and A. Żuk (2002a). On a torsion-free weakly branch group
  defined by a three state automaton. Internat. J. Algebra Comput., 12(1-2):223–246."

**From the source to the target.**

- Items 1–3 of the target are Theorem 12. The target's `B~` is the HNN extension described in the
  proof, since `a^t = t^(−1) a t` in Bartholdi–Virág's notation.
- For item 4: `B~ ∉ SG` is Theorem 12. `EG ⊆ SG` is printed in the source. It also follows from
  the definitions recalled there. `EG` is the closure of finite and abelian groups under
  subgroups, quotients, extensions and directed unions, and `SG` is the closure of groups of
  subexponential growth under the same operations. Finite groups and finitely generated abelian
  groups have polynomial growth, and every abelian group is the directed union of its finitely
  generated subgroups. So the generators of `EG` lie in `SG`.
