* `rho(V)` is a finitely generated linear group, hence residually finite by Mal'cev's theorem.
  `V` is infinite, simple and not residually finite, so `ker rho` restricted to `V` is all of `V`.
* `V` acts transitively on the proper nonempty clopens of Cantor space, and
  `g tau_C g^-1 = tau_(gC)`. So `rho(tau_C) = sigma` is the same for every such `C`.
* `tau_[00] tau_[01] = tau_[0]`, since the three clopens are proper and nonempty. So
  `sigma^2 = sigma`, hence `sigma = 1`.
* Then `rho(z) = rho(tau_[0] tau_[1]) = 1`. `H` is generated by `E` and `V`, so `rho(H) = 1`.
