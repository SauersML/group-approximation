Work on the memory subgroup M=<s,t>. Infinite or even-order t gives
a bipartite operand graph, covered by the proved spatial elimination
theorem. For finite odd-order t, N=<s> intersect <t> is finite and
central. If u lies in N, the cyclic subgroup <s> is a surjunctive
guard and the proved guard transfer theorem applies.

Otherwise quotient by N; injectivity passes to the quotient full
shift through pullback of configurations. The cyclic subgroups
generated by the images of s and t intersect trivially. The first
explicit coset collision in the artifact forces u_1=t_1^-2.
The reflected three-coset and period-three witnesses then force
t_1 to lie in <t_1 s_1>. Thus M/N is cyclic and contains a nontrivial
finite-order element, so it is finite. A central extension of a
cyclic group is abelian; hence M is finite abelian. Finite counting
gives surjectivity on each memory coset and on the whole full shift.

The linked artifacts provide the collisions and the two elimination
lemmas used here. No assertion about the surjunctivity of an arbitrary
ambient group is a prerequisite of this family proof.
