**ESTABLISHED (written proof; see `research/fpbs-quasiconvex-limit-set-classes-are-never-pdr-proof.md`).**
Theorems 1 and 2 are self-contained, given Bevilacqua--Bowen's definitions of `Con`, `Dis` and PDR and the
convergence property of a hyperbolic group on its boundary. Theorem 3 imports three standard facts about
quasiconvex subgroups: intersections are quasiconvex (Short), limit sets intersect as the subgroups do
(Gitik--Mitra--Rips--Sageev, Swenson), and height is finite (Gitik--Mitra--Rips--Sageev).

**Why this matters for the flagship.** For a cocompact `Sp(n,1)` lattice, the Bevilacqua--Bowen (BB) criterion is the
only known route to fixed price one without commensurated amenable structure. A BB certificate is an amenable,
ergodic, partially doubly recurrent (PDR) imp action with infinite non-atomic components. Its Zimmer boundary map is
a point map `xi : Z -> dGamma` (`fpbs-hyperbolic-bb-criterion-obstruction`, item 4). Three previous lanes killed
boundary classes coded inside a free subgroup `F`:
- rotation rays;
- zero-entropy ray codes;
- Schottky and paperfolding codes.

Each of these arguments stopped at a named boundary: positive entropy, the malnormality import (M'), or two-way
supports. This claim removes every one of those boundaries at once, for every hyperbolic group that contains `F` as a
proper subgroup. It does so by changing the invariant. Joint returns of pairs are no longer counted. What is used is
that the action is **induced** from `F`, and that the chain condition in PDR cannot cross cosets of `F`.

**Setting.**
- `Gamma` is a countable group. `(Z, mu)` is an ergodic, infinite measure-preserving (imp) `Gamma`-action.
- `Con(Z^2)` and `Dis(Z^2)` are as in Bevilacqua--Bowen, arXiv:2510.05459, Definition 39. So `Dis` is the union of
  the ergodic components of `Z^2` that are atomic orbits with finite stabilizers, and `Con` is its complement.
- PDR is their Definition 40: the equivalence relation generated by `Con(Z^2)` is conull in `Z x Z`.
- For `L <= Gamma` hyperbolic, `Lambda_L` is its limit set in `dGamma`. The **private part** of the limit set is
  `Lambda*_L = {eta in Lambda_L : gamma eta in Lambda_L implies gamma in L}`.

**Theorem 1 (coset obstruction).** Let `L` be a proper subgroup of `Gamma`, and let `c : Z -> Gamma/L` be measurable
and equivariant. For `beta` not in `L`, put `H_beta = L ∩ beta L beta^{-1}` and
`Q_beta = c^{-1}(L) x c^{-1}(beta L)`. Suppose each `H_beta` acts on `Q_beta` with a smooth orbit relation and
finite stabilizers, up to a null set. Then `Con(Z^2)` is contained, up to a null set, in the invariant equivalence
relation `Delta_c = {(x,y) : c(x) = c(y)}`. Its complement has positive measure, so `Z` is not PDR.

**Theorem 2 (private limit points induce).** Let `Gamma` be hyperbolic and `L` a proper subgroup. Let
`xi : Z -> dGamma` be measurable and equivariant, with `mu(xi^{-1}(Gamma Lambda*_L)) > 0`. Then:
- `c(z) =` the unique coset `gamma L` with `gamma^{-1} xi(z)` in `Lambda*_L` is an a.e. defined equivariant map
  `Z -> Gamma/L`, so `Z = Ind_L^Gamma c^{-1}(L)`;
- the hypotheses of Theorem 1 hold, because `H_beta` acts properly discontinuously on `dGamma \ Lambda_{H_beta}` and
  `xi(x)` lies there for `(x,y)` in `Q_beta`.

Hence `Z` is not PDR. No quasiconvexity, malnormality or non-atomicity is assumed.

**Theorem 3 (quasiconvex descent).** Let `Gamma` be hyperbolic and `H` a quasiconvex subgroup of infinite index. Let
`xi` be as above, with `mu(xi^{-1}(Gamma Lambda_H)) > 0`. Then some subgroup `L` satisfies
`mu(xi^{-1}(Gamma Lambda*_L)) > 0`. Here `L` contains, as a normal subgroup of finite index, an intersection
`K = g_1 H g_1^{-1} ∩ ... ∩ g_n H g_n^{-1}` of `n <= height(H)` conjugates by distinct cosets. So `Z` is not PDR.

**Corollary 4 (what a certificate's boundary class must avoid).** Let `Gamma` be nonelementary hyperbolic, and let
`nu` be the Zimmer boundary class of a BB certificate. Then `nu(Gamma Lambda_H) = 0` for **every** quasiconvex
subgroup `H` of infinite index. In particular, none of the following is the boundary class of a BB certificate:
- any class carried by `Gamma Lambda_F` for a quasiconvex free subgroup `F` of infinite index. This includes the
  rotation classes, the zero-entropy and **positive-entropy** ray codes (Bernoulli, Markov or Gibbs weights), every
  Krieger type `III_lambda`, the Schottky-coded Dini-singular classes and the paperfolding classes;
- any class charging the limit set of a quasiconvex surface subgroup or of a quasi-Fuchsian subgroup (for 3-manifold
  groups);
- the atomic classes on orbits of loxodromic fixed points, taking `H = <h>`. This agrees with Step 7 of the Dini
  proof.

**Answers to the questions the previous lanes left open.**
- *Positive-entropy thin classes* (named in `fpbs-zero-entropy-ray-codes-are-support-rigid`). In every hyperbolic
  `Gamma` in which the coding free group `F` is quasiconvex of infinite index, every class carried by those rays dies,
  whatever the entropy. For `Gamma = F_2` itself the question is void: `F_2` has cost 2, so Bevilacqua--Bowen's theorem
  already excludes every certificate.
- *Removing Kapovich's theorem.* The secondary step was to prove (M') for `<a^N, b^N>` directly. That step is no longer
  needed: Theorem 3 uses no malnormality. Any quasiconvex free subgroup of infinite index, for instance a ping-pong
  subgroup `<a^N, b^N>`, will do.
- *"Support-rigid or killed through the fibre?"* (named in `fpbs-rotation-support-rigidity-kills-type-iii-classes`).
  Classes on `Gamma Lambda_F` die by a third mechanism, coset separation. It uses neither the support nor the fibre.

**The exact step where the BB proof fails for these actions.** In Bevilacqua--Bowen's proof of Theorem 7.1, Claim 1
joins `E(x)` to `E(y)` by a Bernoulli percolation edge. This needs infinitely many joint returns of `(x,y)`. Claim 2
then chains such pairs together. Here, points of a Poisson configuration that lie in different cosets `gamma L` have
only finitely many joint returns, and they are never joined by a chain of pairs in `Con(Z^2)`. So the graph
`hat E(Pi) ∪ G` has no component containing `hat E(Pi)`, and the cost bound is not reached.

**Calibration.**
- *The finite-stabilizer hypothesis is load-bearing.* Let `Gamma = Z^2` act on `Z = Z^2/L` with counting measure,
  where `L = 0 x Z`, and let `c` be the identity. Each pair of cosets has infinite stabilizer `L`, so `Con(Z^2)` is
  everything and the action is PDR. The hypothesis of Theorem 1 fails exactly there. This is correct, since `Z^2` is
  amenable and has fixed price 1.
- *`L = Gamma` gives nothing.* There is only one coset.
- *A normal subgroup gives nothing.* Take the fibre subgroup `N` of a fibred hyperbolic 3-manifold group. Then
  `Lambda_N` is all of `dGamma` and `Lambda*_N` is empty. Theorem 3 does not apply, since `N` is not quasiconvex. This
  is correct: coset separation fails when the cosets share their limit set.
- *Size is not the invariant.* For a cocompact `Sp(n,1)` lattice, the Lebesgue class gives zero mass to `Lambda_H`
  for every quasiconvex `H` of infinite index, since `dim Lambda_H = delta_H` is less than the full dimension. So
  Theorem 3 does not touch it, and it is killed instead by the Dini claim. The two obstructions are complementary.
- *`F_2` and surface groups.* Their cost is greater than 1, so everything must die, and Corollary 4 only removes
  candidates.
- *Null sets in the definition of PDR.* `Con(Z^2)` is defined only up to null sets. If PDR allowed *some* null
  modification of `Con`, every non-atomic ergodic imp action would be PDR: add the null set
  `Gamma({x_0} x Z ∪ Z x {x_0})`. Then `F_2` would have fixed price 1 by Theorem 8.1, through the Maharam extension of
  its harmonic class. So the definition must be read modulo null sets. That is exactly how BB's proof uses it: by
  the Mecke formula, Poisson pairs avoid any fixed null set. Theorem 1 is stated for this reading.

**Named open statement left (quasiconvex-null small classes).** A BB certificate for a cocompact `Sp(n,1)` lattice
needs an ergodic, nonatomic, quasi-invariant class `nu` on `dGamma` with three properties:
- `nu` is singular to every Dini measure (Dini claim);
- no measure in `nu` is shadow-summable (one-way claim);
- `nu(Gamma Lambda_H) = 0` for every infinite-index quasiconvex `H` (this claim).

Do such classes exist? The first test is `F_2`, where every finitely generated subgroup is quasiconvex. There one wants
a Glimm--Effros block code whose blocks eventually leave every finitely generated subgroup of infinite index, for
example by inserting long powers of elements `y_n` with no power conjugate into `H_1, ..., H_n`. If no such class is
small, then no hyperbolic group has a BB certificate.
