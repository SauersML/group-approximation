## Statement

1. **Lifting.** Let `Λ` be finitely generated and `K ◁ Λ` finitely generated as a group, and let
   `p : Λ -> Λ/K`. If `(Λ/K, D)` has a rigid relative seed, then so does `(Λ, p^(-1)(D))`.
2. **Abelian Mihailova pairs.** Let `F` be free of finite rank, `Q` a finitely generated abelian group,
   and `π : F -> Q` onto. Then `(F × F, M(Q))` has a rigid relative seed.
   - `Q = Z^2`, which is infinite and not free, is an instance.
   - By item 2 of `mihailova-relative-seeds-must-force-across-both-factors`, no row of this seed is
     forced inside its factor. The forcing runs through the mixed rules `y(γ(s, s)) = y(γ)`.
3. **The limit of the mechanism.** Let `K ◁ F × F` be finitely generated, with `1 ≠ K ≤ M(Q)`. Then either
   `Q` is finite, or `π(p_1(K))` is a central subgroup of finite index in `Q`.
   - So lifting along a finitely generated kernel reaches only Mihailova pairs with virtually central
     `Q`. Their word problems are easy.
   - The local handle of item 1, right invariance, therefore never forces a Mihailova subgroup whose
     quotient has a hard word problem.

## Proofs

**1.**
- **The SFT.** Fix finite generating sets `S` of `Λ` and `R` of `K`. Let `Ȳ` be the seed SFT over `Λ/K`
  (with generators `p(S)`), with forced point `z̄_*`. Put
  `Y = {y : y(γk) = y(γ) for all γ and all k ∈ R, and ȳ ∈ Ȳ}`.
  - Here `ȳ(p(γ)) = y(γ)`. It is well defined because right invariance under `R` gives right invariance
    under `K`, and `γK = Kγ`.
  - A forbidden pattern of `Ȳ` on `p(B_r)` is read from `y|_(γ B_r)`, so `Y` is an SFT.
- **The correspondence.** `y ↦ ȳ` is a bijection `Y -> Ȳ`, with inverse `ȳ ↦ ȳ ∘ p`. It is equivariant
  along `p`.
  - `y_* = z̄_* ∘ p` is isolated through any `W` with `p(W)` containing the seed window of `z̄_*`.
  - `Stab(y_*) = p^(-1)(D)`.
  - The orbit of `y_*` is dense because that of `z̄_*` is. So (RS1)–(RS2) hold.
- **(RS3).** Let `(E)` be a `D`-family, with `D ≥` the rigidity scale of `Ȳ`, the rule radius and
  `max_(k ∈ R) |k|`.
  - The rule "`ℓ` at `γ` iff `ℓ` at `γk`" gives `E_ℓ(γ) = E_ℓ(γk)` by (L1). So `E` is constant on
    `K`-cosets, and `F_ℓ(p(γ)) = E_ℓ(γ)` is well defined.
  - `F` is a `D`-family for `Ȳ`:
    - (Q1) holds;
    - (Q2) holds, since nearby cosets have representatives `γ, γw` with `|w|` equal to their distance;
    - (Q3): a forbidden pattern on a `D`-ball of `Λ/K`, read on one lift of each site, is a
      non-occurring pattern of `Y` of diameter `≤ 2D`, so (L0) kills its product.
  - Hence `F`, and so `E`, commutes.

**2.**
- **Normal.** `Q` abelian makes `M(Q)` normal in `F × F`.
- **Finitely generated.** `M(Q)` is generated by the pairs `(s, s)` (`s ∈ S`) and `(r, 1)` (`r` a relator
  of `Q`), since `(w r w^(-1), 1) = (w, w)(r, 1)(w, w)^(-1)`.
- **The quotient.** `(a, b) ↦ π(a) π(b)^(-1)` is a homomorphism onto `Q` with kernel `M(Q)`.
- **The seed downstairs.** `Q ≅ Z^n × T` with `T` finite carries a rigid seed: the product of `C_Z^(⊠n)`
  and the finite shift on `T`, as in corollary (b) of `relative-seeds-glue-graphs-of-groups-into-class-c`.
- **Conclusion.** Apply 1 with `K = M(Q)` and `D = 1`.
- **For `Q = Z^2` with `π` the abelianization.** The seed is:
  - right invariance under `(x, x)`, `(y, y)` and `([x, y], 1)`;
  - on the quotient, the `C_Z ⊠ C_Z` rules along `(x, 1)` and `(y, 1)`.

**3.**
- `p_1(K)` is a finitely generated normal subgroup of `F`. So it is trivial or of finite index
  (Karrass–Solitar).
- **If `p_1(K) = 1`.** Then `K = 1 × K_2`, with `K_2` finitely generated, normal and nontrivial, so of
  finite index. Also `K_2 ≤ N`, since `(1, b) ∈ M(Q)` iff `b ∈ N`. So `Q` is finite.
- **Otherwise.** For `(a, b) ∈ K` and `g ∈ F`, `[(a, b), (g, 1)] = ([a, g], 1) ∈ K ≤ M(Q)`. So `[a, g] ∈ N`,
  and `π(p_1(K))` is central in `Q`, of finite index.

## Lesson for general BH

**Forcing across both factors does occur, for abelian quotients.** Item 2 of
`mihailova-relative-seeds-must-force-across-both-factors` shows no row is forced inside its own factor. Yet
for abelian `Q` the mixed diagonal rules force the whole configuration, because `M(Q)` is then a finitely
generated normal subgroup. Right invariance under its generators is local, and it propagates to the
entire subgroup.

**That handle stops at virtually central `Q`.** Inside `M(Q)`, finitely generated normal kernels exist only
when `Q` is finite or virtually central (item 3).

**What a hard Mihailova seed needs.** Local rules can impose right invariance, which makes a configuration
constant on left cosets `γM`. A relative seed needs constancy on right cosets `Mγ`. For non-normal `M(Q)`
these differ, and the difference is the conjugation action of `Q`. So a hard Mihailova seed must force
left-coset structure without invariance. That is the same two-sided problem as the diagonal of `Q × Q`
(bh-free-56). This sub-line adds no shortcut past CAP for `Q`, and I recommend stopping it here.
