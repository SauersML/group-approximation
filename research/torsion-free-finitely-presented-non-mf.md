There exists a torsion-free finitely presented group that is not MF in the
norm-matrix-corona sense.

The *finite-normal* mechanism cannot prove this directly: every finite
subgroup of a torsion-free group is trivial, whereas its final obstruction
is a nontrivial finite normal subgroup.  That statement is about one branch
only, and the claim used to stop there.  It is not the state of the art in
this repository.

## Which branch is torsion-critical, and which is not

Every corona-transport proof faces the same wall.  Kazhdan transport
concludes in the **normalized Hilbert--Schmidt** norm, while MF separates in
**operator** norm, and by `hs-invisibility-inflation-no-go` the first can
never by itself contradict the second: any corona model can be inflated by
a trivial block until every element is HS-invisible without changing a
single operator norm.  So every proof must renormalize the trace on a corner
cut by a nonzero projection commuting with the image.  There are three known
ways to get that projection, and only the first two need torsion.

1. **Finite normal subgroup** — average over it (`finite-normal-compression-obstruction`).
2. **Central mark of finite order** — its spectrum is finite, so the
   projection is its own spectral projection (`central-sign-corona-obstruction`).
   `corona-corner-detection-criterion` restates this branch without the order
   hypothesis and shows torsion is consumed at exactly one step; the residual
   hole is `commutant-projection-extraction`.
3. **Normal subgroup with property (T) inside the defect** — the projection
   is the Kazhdan fixed-space projection, and *normality* is what makes it
   invariant under the ambient group.  **This branch needs no finiteness,
   no centrality and no torsion at all**, and it is machine-checked:
   `normal-kazhdan-defect-non-mf`.

So the root has no remaining analytic obstacle.  What it needs is a
torsion-free finitely presented group whose compression defect contains a
nontrivial normal Kazhdan subgroup.

## Ideas

The primary route is `torsion-free-non-mf-from-normal-kazhdan-defect`, and
it is now **closed** (status updated 2026-08-15): Fournier-Facio's published
torsion-free finitely presented Kazhdan group (arXiv:2608.02025) carries the
compression core and a finitely presented simple group inside its
compression defect (`fournier-facio-torsion-free-skeleton`), and the former
single gap, `defect-normally-generates-torsion-free-quotient`, is
ESTABLISHED in the strong form `<<pi(S)>>^G = G` (not the finite-index form
this section originally proposed — that framing was superseded; see the
Ideas verdicts in that claim's own file) and passed its adversarial audit
on 2026-08-15 (`notes/TORSION_FREE_NORMAL_GENERATION_HULL_QUOTIENT.md` §9).
The route's group theory is a written argument on source-verified Hull/Osin
statements; nothing in it is formalized — the Lean boundary is
`DefectRoutingData`.

Two secondary routes are recorded and are worth keeping because they need no
small-cancellation machinery: `torsion-free-non-mf-from-infinite-order-mark`
and `torsion-free-non-mf-from-coordinate-invariants` (replace the Clifford
sign by a Heisenberg centre, then detect it either on a commutant corner or
on the Kazhdan-invariant subspace of the coordinate ultraproduct), and
`torsion-free-fp-from-countable-witness` (shed finite presentation via
subgroup heredity and a torsion-free Higman embedding).  Branch 3 does not
subsume the central-mark branch: the cyclic group generated by a mark is
amenable and never has property (T), so branch 3 bites only when the mark
happens to sit inside a *larger* normal Kazhdan subgroup of the defect —
which is possible (Kazhdan groups may have infinite centre, as Deligne's
does) but is a different design.

Analyzed dead space.

- **Covers.**  Non-MF does not pass down along surjections: free groups are
  residually finite, hence MF, and surject onto every finitely generated
  group, including `E`.  A torsion-free group mapping onto `E` therefore
  carries no obstruction unless the mark itself lifts, which is the
  infinite-order central mark route and not a cover argument.
- **Embeddings of the existing witness.**  Non-MF does pass **up**:
  subgroups of MF groups are MF (`mf-positive-controls`).  But `E` and the
  Clifford witness contain an involution, so no torsion-free group contains
  either.  The useful residue is the reduction
  `torsion-free-countable-non-mf`: any torsion-free non-MF **subgroup**
  suffices, so finite presentation is not the difficulty.
- **Winding numbers.**  `matrix-corona-k1-vanishes`: `K_1` of every norm
  matrix corona is zero, so no Bott or index invariant of the mark alone
  can obstruct anything.  Relative indices of a *pair* do survive
  (`matrix-corona-k0-index-survives`), and the developed form of that
  invariant exists — Dadarlat's Exel--Loring formula.  What it buys and what
  blocks it is `exel-loring-mark-quantization`; note in particular that it
  is **incompatible with the stability route** on the same base group, since
  a base carrying a usable mark has nonvanishing even rational cohomology
  and hence fails point-norm matricial stability.
- **Roots of unity by relations.**  Forcing `sigma eps sigma^-1 = eps^d`
  makes `sp(Theta(eps))` invariant under `z -> z^d`, which with clause 2 of
  `corona-corner-detection-criterion` leaves only `{1}` and the full
  circle.  It does not close the full-circle case, and it costs centrality
  in the whole group (a central `eps` with `eps^{d-1} = 1` is torsion
  again), so `eps` may only be central in the transport subgroup.

Literature boundary, source-verified this session.  Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky,
*On L^1-approximation of groups* (arXiv:2508.17392, revised April 2026),
Definition 1.2 defines a group of **Deligne type** by the existence of a
central element `J` **of order 2** with `J` trivial in every finite
quotient; their Proposition 1.5 shows such a group is not MF **if** it is
operator-HS-stable, and their Conjecture 1.8 would supply that stability
from vanishing of `H^2` with unitary coefficients.  Their proof rounds `J`
to an exact involution, cuts to its `-1` eigenspace, and compresses — the
same corner step, torsion-critical in the same place.  The paper still
records the MF question as open.  So **both** routes to non-MF known outside
this repository are torsion-critical, and `commutant-projection-extraction`
is their shared bottleneck; branch 3 above, which is not torsion-critical,
has no counterpart in the published literature.  The
uniform-stability results for high-rank lattices in submultiplicative norms
(Glebsky--Lubotzky--Monod--Rangarajan, *Asymptotic cohomology and uniform
stability for lattices in semisimple groups*, arXiv:2301.00476; abstract
read, theorem statements not) do not close it, and Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky
conjecture their stability input rather than cite them.  Two reasons, the
first structural rather than cited: a Deligne centre that is infinite
cyclic — the torsion-free case — requires a lattice in a group with
infinite fundamental group, that is of Hermitian type, whereas the
`SL_n` families are the ones stability is known for; and condition (`*`) is
a **mixed**-norm statement (defect in operator norm, closeness in
Hilbert--Schmidt), which is not uniform stability in one submultiplicative
norm.
