All algebras and tensor coefficients in this proof are over C. Fix a
group G and a finite n >= 1, and put X = K = G^n. All crossed products
and matrix sums are algebraic finite sums.

## Action and faithful tuple representation

For h in K let alpha_h(f)(x) = f(xh), with coordinatewise products.
Then alpha_h alpha_l = alpha_{hl}. Write

    (f u_h)(g u_l) = f alpha_h(g) u_{hl}.             (FCO2)

On the space C^(X) of finitely supported functions, let M_f be
multiplication by f and let T_h xi(x) = xi(xh). These operators satisfy
T_h M_g = M_{alpha_h(g)} T_h, so f u_h -> M_f T_h is a representation.
It is faithful: if a finite sum sum_h M_{f_h} T_h is zero, fix x in X.
The points xh are distinct for distinct h. Testing against the delta
function at xh proves f_h(x) = 0 for each h. Varying x proves f_h = 0.

Every coefficient in C_n is bounded, being a finite linear combination
of products of indicators. Hence this representation also acts on
l^2(X), where T_h is unitary and M_f^* = M_{conjugate(f)}. Thus

    (f u_h)^* = alpha_{h^(-1)}(conjugate(f)) u_{h^(-1)}.  (FCO3)

The coefficient algebra is preserved by conjugation and translations.
For real coefficients (FCO3) is also the matrix transpose in the tuple
basis. Faithfulness holds entrywise for every finite matrix algebra.

## Collision components and their direct sum

Let pi be a partition of {1,...,n}, and let b(B) be the least element
of each block B. An offset assignment a chooses a_i in G for every i,
with a_{b(B)} = 1. Define

    D_{pi,a} = {x in G^n : x_i = x_{b(B)} a_i for i in B},
    delta_{pi,a} = 1_{D_{pi,a}}.

For fixed pi these sets are nonempty, disjoint, and cover X. Put C_pi
equal to their finite complex span. Its distinguished basis consists
of orthogonal idempotents delta_{pi,a}. In particular C_pi is a copy
of the finitely supported function algebra on the offset set

    P_pi = G^{n-|pi|}.

The discrete partition contributes C times 1. Every individual
collision indicator belongs to one such component, and every
delta_{pi,a} is a finite product of individual collision indicators.

The intersection of D_{pi,a} and D_{sigma,b} is either empty or exactly
one D_{rho,c}, where rho = pi join sigma is their common coarsening
generated by the two equivalence relations. Indeed, concatenate the
specified relative equations along paths in each joined block. An
inconsistent loop makes the intersection empty; otherwise those paths
specify unique offsets relative to the chosen block root. Therefore

    C_pi C_sigma is contained in C_{pi join sigma}.    (FCO4)

Assume now that G is infinite. We prove that the sum of the C_pi is
direct. Suppose a finite linear combination of the delta_{pi,a}
vanishes, and select an active partition pi with maximal block count.
Choose an offset assignment a whose coefficient is nonzero. Evaluate
inside D_{pi,a}, writing x_i = r_B a_i for i in B.

Any other active partition sigma has at most |pi| blocks. If sigma
is not pi, it cannot refine pi: a distinct refinement would have
strictly more blocks. Thus sigma imposes a relation between coordinates
in two different pi-blocks. For each of the finitely many other active
offset indicators, choose one such relation. In the block-root
variables it has the form r_B = r_C d, with B != C and fixed d in G.
Choose the finitely many roots successively, avoiding these finitely
many forbidden values at each choice. This is possible because G is
infinite. Every selected competing indicator then vanishes. Indicators
at pi with offset different from a also vanish by disjointness. Only
the selected nonzero coefficient remains, a contradiction. Hence

    C_n = direct_sum_pi C_pi.                         (FCO5)

This is an algebraic direct sum over the finite partition set; each
component itself still has finite offset support.

## Translation and the finite-matrix identification

The action preserves every C_pi. With the convention (FCO2),

    alpha_h(delta_{pi,a}) = delta_{pi,h dot a},
    (h dot a)_i = h_{b(B)} a_i h_i^(-1).              (FCO6)

This is a left action on the offset labels. It is the inverse support
action to the point right translation x -> xh. It acts transitively
on P_pi. The stabilizer of the all-identity offset o is

    H_pi = {h in G^n : h_i = h_{b(B)} for i in B}
           isomorphic to G^{|pi|}.                    (FCO7)

For each offset a choose t_a in K with (t_a)_{b(B)} = 1 and
(t_a)_i = a_i^(-1), so t_a dot o = a. Put R_pi = C_pi semidirect K,
a possibly nonunital subalgebra of D_n. There is an explicit isomorphism

    R_pi -> Mat_fin(P_pi, C[H_pi]),
    delta_{pi,a} u_h -> E_{a,c}[t_a^(-1) h t_c],
                         c = h^(-1) dot a.           (FCO8)

The bracketed group element lies in H_pi. Products on both sides
vanish unless the intermediate offset labels agree; when they agree,
the group tags multiply as

    (t_a^(-1) h t_c)(t_c^(-1) l t_d)
       = t_a^(-1) hl t_d.

Conversely E_{a,c}[s], s in H_pi, comes from h = t_a s t_c^(-1).
This verifies (FCO8), including surjectivity. Mat_fin means matrices
with only finitely many nonzero entries, each a group-algebra element.

From (FCO4)-(FCO6) we also have

    D_n = direct_sum_pi R_pi,
    R_pi R_sigma is contained in R_{pi join sigma}.   (FCO9)

This is a grading by the partition semilattice, not a direct product
of rings or a decomposition into mutually annihilating ideals.

## Polynomial trace and its normalization

Let z be a formal indeterminate. By (FCO5), the prescription

    mu_z(delta_{pi,a}) = z^{|pi|}

defines a complex-linear map C_n -> C[z]. It is translation-invariant
by (FCO6). Define

    tau_z(sum_h f_h u_h) = mu_z(f_1).                 (FCO10)

This is a trace. It suffices to compare f u_h and g u_l. Both products
have zero trace unless l = h^(-1). In that case invariance and
commutativity of the coefficient algebra give

    mu_z(f alpha_h(g))
       = mu_z(alpha_{h^(-1)}(f) g)
       = mu_z(g alpha_{h^(-1)}(f)).

For matrices use the unnormalized diagonal extension
tau_z^(m)(E) = sum_i tau_z(E_{ii}); this remains cyclic.

Under (FCO8), the coefficient of z^{|pi|} on R_pi is precisely the
finite matrix trace followed by the canonical group trace

    tr_{H_pi}(sum_s c_s[s]) = c_1.                  (FCO11)

Indeed delta_{pi,a} u_h can contribute to the target diagonal only
if c = a. Its diagonal H_pi-tag is then t_a^(-1) h t_a, which is the
identity exactly when h = 1. Such a term contributes 1 on both sides.
Thus every offset diagonal has weight 1, with no orbit-index factor.
The same calculation applies to matrices over R_pi.

We recall the needed positivity fact. A nonzero idempotent P over
C[H_pi] in any finite matrix size embeds into a matrix algebra over
the group von Neumann algebra L(H_pi), with its faithful finite
canonical trace. Let p be the orthogonal projection onto the range of
P. This range is closed, and p belongs to that von Neumann algebra
(equivalently, p is the range projection of PP^*). Then pP = P and
Pp = p. Cyclicity gives tr(P) = tr(p) > 0. This proves positivity
also for non-self-adjoint idempotents. A nonzero idempotent in any
finite matrix algebra over Mat_fin(P_pi,C[H_pi]) lies in one finite
offset corner, so the same fact applies there.

## Leading partition degree detects every matrix idempotent

Let E be a nonzero idempotent in M_m(D_n). Decompose E = sum_pi E_pi
using (FCO9), entrywise, and let k be the maximal |pi| for which
E_pi != 0. Fix an active pi with |pi| = k.

For a product E_sigma E_rho to contribute to the pi component,
sigma join rho must equal pi. Both sigma and rho then refine pi,
so both have at least k blocks. Maximality bounds their active block
counts above by k. A refinement with equal block count is the same
partition, hence sigma = rho = pi. The pi component of E^2 = E
therefore says

    E_pi^2 = E_pi.                                  (FCO12)

Each nonzero E_pi at this leading level is a nonzero finite-matrix
idempotent over C[H_pi], via (FCO8). By (FCO11) and the positivity
fact, the coefficient of z^k in tau_z^(m)(E) is the sum of strictly
positive real numbers, one from each active leading partition.
Consequently

    E != 0 and E^2 = E imply tau_z^(m)(E) != 0.       (FCO13)

There is no assertion of positivity for arbitrary elements of C_n;
the ordered leading coefficient on idempotents is what is used.

Now let BA = I_m. The element E = I_m - AB is idempotent, and cyclicity
gives

    tau_z^(m)(E) = tau_z^(m)(I_m) - tau_z^(m)(AB)
                = tau_z^(m)(I_m) - tau_z^(m)(BA) = 0.

By (FCO13), E = 0. This proves stable finiteness for infinite G.

If G is finite, X and K are finite, so C_n and D_n are finite-dimensional
complex algebras. Every matrix algebra over D_n is finite-dimensional.
In a finite-dimensional unital algebra, BA = 1 makes left multiplication
by A injective, hence surjective. Thus AC = 1 for some C, and
B = B(AC) = (BA)C = C, giving AB = 1. This handles the finite case
without using (FCO5), which need not hold for finite G.

Finally, stable finiteness passes to every idempotent corner: extend
a corner inverse pair by 1-e in the ambient matrix algebra. Combined
with faithfulness and (FCO3), this also proves that a tuple-basis
isometry lying in D_n, in its finite matrix algebras, or in such a
corner is surjective onto the corresponding basis space whenever its
initial projection is that space's identity.
