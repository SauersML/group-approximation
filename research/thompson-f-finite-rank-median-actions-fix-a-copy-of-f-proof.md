*Kato.* M. Kato, *On groups whose actions on finite-dimensional CAT(0) spaces have global fixed points*,
arXiv:1804.10506 (J. Group Theory 2018).
- (K0) p.2: "the support of an element g ∈ G is supp(g) = {a ∈ A | ga ≠ a} ⊂ A. The support of a subset L
  is supp(L) = ⋃_{l∈L} supp(l) ⊂ A. For a topological space X, dim(X) denotes the topological dimension of
  X. We say X is k-dimensional if dim(X) = k."
- (K1) Theorem 1.1: "Let G be a group acting faithfully on a set A. Let s ∈ G be an element satisfying the
  following conditions: there is a sequence of subgroups ⟨s⟩ = H0 < H1 < · · · < Hk < Hk+1 = G with
  elements gi ∈ Hi+1 (1 ≤ i ≤ k) such that for every 1 ≤ i ≤ k, (i) every homomorphism from Hi to R is
  trivial, and (ii) gi(supp(Hi)) ∩ supp(Hi) = ∅ in A. Then for every complete k-dimensional CAT(0) space
  X, for every semi-simple action of G on X, s has a fixed point."
- (K2) Theorem 1.2 ([1], [18]): "Let k1, . . . , kr be positive integers and let X be a complete CAT(0)
  space of dim(X) < k1 + · · · + kr. Let S1, . . . , Sr ⊂ Isom(X) be subsets with [si, sj] = 1 for all
  si ∈ Si and sj ∈ Sj (i ≠ j). If each ki-element subset of Si has a fixed point in X for i = 1, . . . , r,
  then for some i, every finite subset of Si has a fixed point."
- (K3) §2: "A semi-simple isometry γ is called hyperbolic if |γ| > 0, and called elliptic if |γ| = 0,
  i.e. γ has a fixed point. We say that an isometric action of a group G on a metric space is semi-simple
  if every g ∈ G is either elliptic or hyperbolic."
- (K4) Remark 3.1: "The commutator subgroup [F, F] of F is simple, and is equal to the subgroup
  consisting of elements which restrict to the identity on some neighborhoods of 0 and 1."
- (K5) §3: "For every standard dyadic interval I properly contained in [0, 1], let GI = {g ∈ G | supp(g)
  ⊂ I} < G. Considering the canonical identification of I with [0, 1], we may observe that every GI is
  isomorphic to F. Its commutator subgroup [GI, GI] coincides with the subgroup consisting of elements
  which restrict to the identity on some neighborhoods of two boundary points of I." Here G is F or T.
- (K6) Lemma 3.1(2): "Let G be either F, T or V. Let I be a a standard dyadic interval in [0, 1]. For every
  nonempty open subset U1 of I and every compact subset U2 in the interior of I, there is g in the
  commutator subgroup of HI = {h ∈ G | supp(h) ⊂ I} such that g(U2) is contained in U1."
- (K7) §3: "F is generated by A and B of Figure 1."

*Fioravanti.* E. Fioravanti, *The Tits alternative for finite rank median spaces*, arXiv:1708.01215.
- (Fi1) Theorem E: "Let X be a complete, finite rank median space with an isometric action Γ ↷ X. Either
  Γ contains a nonabelian free subgroup or the action is Roller elementary."
- (Fi2) Theorem A: "Let X be a complete, finite rank median space. Let Γ be a group with an isometric
  action Γ ↷ X. Suppose that Γ has no nonabelian free subgroups."
- (Fi3) Proof of Theorem A (§4), under the hypotheses (Fi2): "By hypothesis, Γ does not have any
  nonabelian free subgroups; thus, the action Γ ↷ X is Roller elementary by Theorem E. Theorem 4.1 yields
  a finite-index subgroup Γ0 ≤ Γ and a normal subgroup N ◁ Γ0 such that Γ0/N is abelian and every finitely
  generated subgroup of N has an orbit with at most 2^r elements."
- (Fi4) Theorem F: "Let X be complete and finite rank. Suppose that Γ ↷ X fixes a point in the Roller
  boundary of X. A finite-index subgroup Γ0 ≤ Γ fits in an exact sequence 1 → N → Γ0 → R^r, where
  r = rank(X) and every finitely generated subgroup of N has an orbit in X with at most 2^r elements."
- (Fi5) §1: "the 1-skeleton becomes a median graph when endowed with its intrinsic path-metric" and "The
  notion of rank of a median space was introduced in [Bow13]; for CAT(0) cube complexes it coincides with
  the usual concept of dimension."
- (Fi6) Theorem A(3): "If all point stabilisers are amenable, Γ is amenable." Its hypotheses are: X
  complete, of finite rank, and Γ with no nonabelian free subgroups.

*Brin–Squier* (via `thompson-f-has-no-free-subgroups`): `F` has no nonabelian free subgroup.

**Step 0 (group facts).** Let `I ⊂ (0,1)` be a standard dyadic interval, for example `[1/4,1/2]`.
- (a) `F_I ≤ F'`: elements supported in the compact set `I ⊂ (0,1)` are the identity near `0` and `1`
  (K4). `F_I ≅ F` (K5) is finitely generated (K7), infinite and nonabelian.
- (b) `F'` is perfect. It is simple (K4) and nonabelian by (a), so `[F',F']` is a nontrivial normal
  subgroup, hence all of `F'`. Every homomorphism from `F'`, or from a group isomorphic to `F'`, to an
  abelian group is trivial.
- (c) `F'` has no proper finite-index subgroup. If `L ≤ F'` has finite index, its normal core is a
  finite-index normal subgroup. By simplicity (K4) it is `1` or `F'`, and it is not `1` because `F'` is
  infinite. So `L = F'`.
- (d) Homeomorphisms with disjoint supports commute. Supports are invariant, so for `x ∈ supp u` we have
  `uv(x) = u(x) = vu(x)`. The case `x ∈ supp v` is symmetric, and outside both supports both maps fix `x`.

**Step 1 (part 1).**
- *Setup.* `F` acts isometrically on the complete median space `X` of rank `r`. By Brin–Squier, `F` has
  no nonabelian free subgroup, so (Fi2) applies with `Γ = F`. By (Fi3) there are:
  - a finite-index `Γ_0 ≤ F`;
  - a normal `N ◁ Γ_0` with `Γ_0/N` abelian;
  - every finitely generated subgroup of `N` has an orbit with at most `2^r` elements.
- *`F' ≤ N`.* `F' ∩ Γ_0` has finite index in `F'`, so it equals `F'` by (c), and `F' ≤ Γ_0`. The image of
  `F'` in the abelian group `Γ_0/N` is trivial by (b), so `F' ≤ N`.
- *Finite orbits.* Every finitely generated subgroup of `F'` has an orbit with at most `2^r` points.
  For `K = F_I`, choose `x` with `|K·x| = m ≤ 2^r`. Then `Stab_F(x) ∩ F_I = Stab_K(x)` has index `m` in
  `F_I`.
- *Cubical case.* Let `Y` be a CAT(0) cube complex of dimension `r` with a cubical action of `F`, and let
  `X = Y^(0)` carry the path metric of the 1-skeleton.
  - `X` is a median space of rank `r`, by (Fi5): "for any three vertices, there exists a unique vertex
    that lies between any two of them", and rank equals dimension.
  - `X` is complete, because distances are integers, so Cauchy sequences are eventually constant.
  - Cubical automorphisms preserve the 1-skeleton, so `F` acts on `X` by isometries.

**Step 2a (part 2: each element of `F'` is elliptic).** This is Kato's own argument for `T` (proof of
Corollary 1.3), run inside `F`. `F` acts faithfully on `A = [0,1]` and semi-simply on the complete CAT(0)
space `X`, with `dim X = k < ∞`.
- *Trivial case.* If `k = 0`, then `X` is a point: a geodesic space with two points contains a segment.
  So assume `k ≥ 1`.
- *Intervals.* Put `I_{k+1} = [0,1]`. For `j = k, …, 0`, let `I_j` be the second quarter of `I_{j+1}`,
  so `[m2^{-n},(m+1)2^{-n}]` gives `[(4m+1)2^{-n-2},(4m+2)2^{-n-2}]`. These are standard dyadic
  intervals with `I_j ⊂ Int(I_{j+1})`, and `I_k = [1/4,1/2] ⊂ (0,1)`.
- *Conjugating `s` into `I_0`.* Let `s ∈ F'`. By (K4), `supp(s)` lies in a compact `[c,d] ⊂ (0,1)`.
  (K6), with `I = [0,1]`, `U_1 = Int(I_0)` and `U_2 = [c,d]`, gives `g_0 ∈ F` with
  `g_0([c,d]) ⊂ Int(I_0)`. Put `s' = g_0 s g_0^{-1}`. Then `supp(s') = g_0(supp s)` is contained in a
  compact subset of `Int(I_0)`.
- *The chain.* Put `H_0 = ⟨s'⟩`, `H_i = [F_{I_i}, F_{I_i}]` for `1 ≤ i ≤ k`, and `H_{k+1} = F`.
  - By (K5), `H_i` is the set of elements of `F_{I_i}` that are the identity near both endpoints of
    `I_i`.
  - So `s' ∈ H_1`, and `H_i ≤ H_{i+1}`, because supports lie in `I_i`, a compact subset of
    `Int(I_{i+1})`. Also `H_k ≤ F`.
  - The inclusions are strict. `H_1` is nonabelian while `H_0` is cyclic. `H_{i+1}` contains the element
    `g_i` below, which moves points of `I_i` off `I_i`, while `H_i` preserves `I_i`.
- *(i) holds.* `F_{I_i} ≅ F` (K5) carries `H_i` onto `[F,F]`, which is perfect by Step 0(b). So every
  homomorphism `H_i → R` is trivial.
- *(ii) holds.* Fix `1 ≤ i ≤ k`. `I_i` is a proper closed subinterval of `Int(I_{i+1})`, so
  `I_{i+1} \ I_i` contains a nonempty open `U_1`. (K6), with `I = I_{i+1}` and `U_2 = I_i`, gives `g_i` in
  `[F_{I_{i+1}}, F_{I_{i+1}}]` with `g_i(I_i) ⊂ U_1`.
  - That group is `H_{i+1}` when `i < k`, and lies in `F = H_{k+1}` when `i = k`, since
    `[0,1] = I_{k+1}` is standard dyadic.
  - Then `g_i(supp H_i) ⊂ g_i(I_i) ⊂ U_1`, which is disjoint from `I_i ⊇ supp(H_i)`.
- *Conclusion.* (K1) gives `y ∈ X` with `s'y = y`, and then `s(g_0^{-1}y) = g_0^{-1}y`.

**Step 2b (finite subsets of `F'` fix a point).**
- *Setup.* Let `T ⊂ F'` be finite, with `⋃_{t∈T} supp t ⊂ [c,d] ⊂ (0,1)` compact. Choose pairwise
  disjoint nonempty open intervals `J_1, …, J_{k+1} ⊂ (0,1)`.
- *Commuting conjugates.* (K6), with `I = [0,1]`, gives `f_j ∈ F` with `f_j([c,d]) ⊂ J_j`. Put
  `S_j = f_j T f_j^{-1}`. It is a subset of `F'`, because `F'` is normal, and its supports lie in `J_j`.
  - For `j ≠ j'`, elements of `S_j` and `S_{j'}` have disjoint supports, so they commute (Step 0(d)).
    Their images in `Isom(X)` commute too.
  - Each one-element subset of `S_j` has a fixed point, by Step 2a.
- *Applying (K2).* Use `r = k+1` and `k_1 = … = k_{k+1} = 1`, so `dim X = k < k+1`. For some `j`, every
  finite subset of `S_j` has a fixed point. In particular `S_j` fixes some `y`, so `T` fixes `f_j^{-1}y`.
- *Copies of `F`.* `F_I` is generated by two elements, by (K5) and (K7). So it fixes the common fixed
  point of those two elements. Every finitely generated subgroup of `F'` fixes a point.

**Step 3 (the class kill).** Let `G ≥ F` act on `X` with all point stabilizers amenable. The action is
isometric on a complete median space of finite rank, or semi-simple on a complete finite-dimensional
CAT(0) space; semi-simplicity is elementwise, so it passes to `F`.
- *Forward.* `Stab_F(x) = Stab_G(x) ∩ F` is amenable. By Steps 1 and 2, some `Stab_F(x)` contains a
  subgroup `K` of index at most `2^r` in `F_I ≅ F`. So `K` is amenable, hence `F_I` is amenable
  (finite-index overgroup), hence `F` is amenable.
- *Converse.* If `F` is amenable, the action on a point has amenable stabilizers.

So Fioravanti's (Fi6), applied to `F` or to any host `G ≥ F`, has a hypothesis equivalent to the root.
- *No proper actions.* A metrically proper action has finite point stabilizers, but `K` is infinite.
- *Exactness.* Exactness passes to subgroups and to finite-index overgroups. So stabilizer-exactness
  routes presuppose exactness of `F`.

**Step 4 (sharpness, part 3).**
- *`F` itself is not elliptic.* The homomorphism "`log_2` of the slope at `0`" maps `F` onto `Z`, which
  acts on the standard cubulation of `R` by translations. This is a 1-dimensional CAT(0) cube complex, the
  action is semi-simple, and elements outside `F'` are hyperbolic. So the restriction to `F'` in parts 1–2
  cannot be dropped.
- *`F'` as a whole.* Parts 1–2 claim nothing about a global fixed point for `F'`, which is not finitely
  generated.
