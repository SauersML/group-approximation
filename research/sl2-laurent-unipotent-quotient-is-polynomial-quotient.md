title: "SL_2(Z[t,t^-1]) modulo its unipotent subgroup equals SL_2(Z[t]) modulo the Laurent-unipotent elements it contains"
---

For a commutative ring `S`, let `U_2(S) ⊂ SL_2(S)` be the subgroup generated by the
unipotent matrices `I + N` (`N` nilpotent with entries in `S`). Let `R = Z[t,t^{-1}]`,
`D = diag(1,t)`. Then:

1. `U_2(R) = < U_2(Z[t]), D U_2(Z[t]) D^{-1} >`;
2. `U_2(R)` is normal in `SL_2(R)`, acts edge-transitively on the Bruhat–Tits tree of
   `Q((t))`, and `SL_2(R) = U_2(R) · SL_2(Z[t])`;
3. `SL_2(R) / U_2(R) ≅ SL_2(Z[t]) / (U_2(R) ∩ SL_2(Z[t]))`.

Since `U_2(R) ⊇ U_2(Z[t])`, the quotient `SL_2(R)/U_2(R)` is a quotient of
`SL_2(Z[t])/U_2(Z[t])`. Grunewald–Mennicke–Vaserstein (Israel J. Math. 86 (1994),
Zbl 0805.20042) show that `SL_2(Z[t])/U_2(Z[t])` has free quotients of every finite
rank. Item 3 locates exactly where that obstruction could survive the passage to the
Laurent ring: in the elements of `SL_2(Z[t])` that are products of Laurent unipotents
but not of polynomial ones.
