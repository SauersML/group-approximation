**Imports, read at source.**
- **Abels, via CGP.** Cornulier–Guyot–Pitsch, arXiv:math/0511714v2 §5.4, Prop. 5.6: "The
  groups A_n and A_n/Z are finitely presented for all n ≥ 4". Here `A_n ⊂ GL_n(Z[1/p])` is
  upper triangular with `a_11 = a_nn = 1` and positive diagonal, and `p` is any prime.
- **Kneser, as quoted by Cornulier–de la Harpe**, *Metric geometry of locally compact groups*,
  arXiv:1403.3796, §1.E (read in the arXiv HTML rendering, 2026-09-18): "In [Knes–64], Martin Kneser has introduced the notion of compact
  presentability, and has shown that G(Z_S) is finitely presented if and only if G(Q_p) is
  compactly presented for all p ∈ S." `G` is a linear algebraic group over `Q`, and `S` is a
  finite set of primes.
- **Standard, not re-read.** P. Hall (1954): a finitely generated abelian-by-polycyclic group
  satisfies max-n, and if `N ◁ G` is nilpotent and `G/N'` satisfies max-n, so does `G`.

## Statement

Let `m >= 2`, `R = Z[1/m]` and `n >= 4`.
- `𝐀_n` is the `Q`-group of upper triangular matrices with `a_11 = a_nn = 1`, and `A_n(R)` is
  the finite-index subgroup of `𝐀_n(R)` with positive diagonal.
- Let `Δ` be a subgroup of the positive diagonal matrices `diag(δ_1, …, δ_n)`, `δ_i ∈ R^×`,
  acting on `A_n(R)` by conjugation. It is finitely generated, since `R^×` is.
- Put `P_Δ = A_n(R) ⋊ Δ`.
- `Δ` acts on the corner `Z(A_n(R)) = e_1n(R)` through the character `χ(δ) = δ_1/δ_n`. Let
  `L ⊆ R` be a `χ(Δ)`-invariant additive subgroup that is generated by one element as a
  `Z[χ(Δ)]`-module; for example `L = cZ` always, or `L = cZ[1/m']` when `Z[χ(Δ)] = Z[1/m']`.

Then:
1. `P_Δ` and `P_Δ/e_1n(L)` are finitely presented, soluble, minimax, and satisfy max-n.
2. Every finitely generated subgroup of `P_Δ/e_1n(L)` embeds in an isolated group.
3. **Example with non-central Prüfer radical.** Let `p` be an odd prime and
   `H = N ⋊ ⟨s, t⟩`, where
   - `N = {(α, β, γ) : α, γ ∈ Z[1/2p], β ∈ Z[1/p]}` with
     `(α,β,γ)(α',β',γ') = (α+α', β+β', γ+γ'+αβ')`;
   - `s(α,β,γ) = (pα, p^{-1}β, γ)` and `t(α,β,γ) = (2α, β, 2γ)`.

   Put `G_2 = H / {(0,0,γ) : γ ∈ Z[1/2]}`. Then:
   - `G_2` is a 4-generator soluble minimax group;
   - its periodic radical is `D = Z[1/2p]/Z[1/2] ≅ C_(p^∞)`;
   - `t` acts on `D` as multiplication by `2`, which has infinite order in `Aut(D) = Z_p^×`.
     So `D` is central in no finite-index subgroup, and `G_2` is not of the central Hall–Abels
     type treated in that node.

   Nevertheless `G_2` embeds in an isolated group.

## Proof

1. **Finite presentation of `A_n(R)`.**
   - For each prime `q`, `𝐀_n(Z[1/q])` contains CGP's `A_n` (over `Z[1/q]`) with finite index
     (signs of the diagonal). So it is finitely presented, and by Kneser `𝐀_n(Q_q)` is
     compactly presented.
   - Applying Kneser with `S` the primes dividing `m`, `𝐀_n(R)` is finitely presented, and so
     is its finite-index subgroup `A_n(R)`.
2. **`P_Δ` and its quotient.**
   - `P_Δ` is an extension of a finitely presented group by the finitely generated abelian
     `Δ`, so it is finitely presented.
   - `e_1n(L)` is central in `A_n(R)` and `Δ`-invariant, so it is normal. It is the normal
     closure of one element, so `P_Δ/e_1n(L)` is finitely presented.
   - Both groups are finitely generated and soluble of finite rank: the entries lie in `R`,
     and `Δ` is finitely generated abelian.
3. **Max-n.** The unipotent radical `U = U(R)` is nilpotent and normal, and `P_Δ/U'` is
   finitely generated abelian-by-abelian. By Hall, `P_Δ` satisfies max-n, and so do its
   quotients.
4. **Item 2.** Finitely generated subgroups of soluble minimax groups are soluble minimax.
   Apply item 3 of `isolated-embedding-of-soluble-minimax-groups-via-max-n-hosts`.
5. **Item 3.** Take `n = 4`, `m = 2p` and `Δ = ⟨σ, τ⟩` with `σ = diag(1, p^{-1}, 1, 1)`
   and `τ = diag(2, 1, 1, 1)`. Then `χ(Δ) = 2^Z`, and `L = Z[1/2]` is generated by `1` as a
   `Z[2^{±1}]`-module.
   - **The map on `N`.** `φ(α, β, γ) = I + αE_12 + βE_24 + γE_14` is an injective
     homomorphism into `U(R)`, since `E_12 E_24 = E_14` and `E_24 E_12 = 0`.
   - **The torus.** Conjugation by `σ` scales `E_12, E_24, E_14` by `p, p^{-1}, 1`, and by `τ`
     by `2, 1, 2`. These are the actions of `s` and `t`. So `φ` extends to an injective
     homomorphism `H → P_Δ` with `s ↦ σ` and `t ↦ τ`.
   - **Injectivity after the quotient.** `φ(0,0,γ) ∈ e_14(Z[1/2])` iff `γ ∈ Z[1/2]`. So `G_2`
     embeds in `P_Δ/e_14(L)`, and item 2 applies.
   - **The radical.** `H/N ≅ Z^2`, and `N/z(Z[1/2p]) ≅ Z[1/2p] ⊕ Z[1/p]` is torsion-free. So
     `τ(G_2) = z(Z[1/2p])/z(Z[1/2]) = D`. Generation by `x = (1,0,0)`, `y = (0,1,0)`, `s`
     and `t` is checked by conjugating and commuting. `∎`

## Why the outer torus is needed (remark, not a theorem)

- In an S-arithmetic host `𝐆(Z_S)` with split torus, a torus character `χ ≠ 0` on a root
  line takes values `∏_(q ∈ S) q^(k⟨χ,λ⟩)` along each cocharacter `λ`. So it cannot contain
  `2^k` without `p^k`.
- A lattice `L` with `R/L ≅ C_(p^∞)` is preserved by `2` but not by `p^{-1}`. So `L` is normal
  in the full S-arithmetic group only if the host acts on that line by `±1`. That is the
  Hall–Abels central case.
- The outer torus `Δ` is not the group of `S`-points of any subtorus. It is exactly what makes
  such a lattice normal while the rest of `A_n(R)` stays finitely presented.

## Lesson for general BH

**Finite presentation lives in the S-arithmetic core, and invisibility lives in a
non-arithmetic outer torus.**
- Kneser plus Abels make `A_n(Z[1/m])` finitely presented for every `m`.
- An extension by any finitely generated diagonal group keeps finite presentation. It lets a
  corner lattice be normal with respect to a torus that sees only some primes, so the corner
  quotient can be a Prüfer group scaled with infinite order.
- **Consequence for Question 4 and for Boone–Higman.** Non-residually-finite soluble inputs
  whose invisible radical is twisted by rational S-units embed in finitely presented,
  max-n, hence residually isolated, hosts. The twist costs nothing beyond one outer torus.
