Citation import, not a reproof. Source: M. Bestvina, K.-U. Bux, D. Margalit, *The dimension of the Torelli
group*, arXiv:0709.0287v1. The PDF was fetched on MSI on 2026-09-13 and extracted with ghostscript
`txtwrite`, into `/scratch.global/sauer354/hl-nh-quantum-reps/src/bbm0709.txt`.

Verbatim, extracted text lines 110–121 (Section 1, paragraph "Genus 2"):

> Genus 2. It is a theorem of Stallings and Swan that a group of cohomological dimension 1 is free [37, 38].
> Thus, Theorem A implies that I(S2) = K(S2) is a free group. A celebrated theorem of Mess, Theorem D
> below, gives a much more precise picture of this group.
> For the statement, note that each separating simple closed curve in S2 gives rise to an algebraic
> splitting of H1(S2;Z): the two subspaces are the ones spanned by the curves on the two different sides
> of the separating curve. Each component of the splitting is a 2-dimensional symplectic subspace of
> H1(S2;Z), and so we call such a splitting a symplectic splitting of H1(S2;Z). Note that two separating
> curves give rise to the same symplectic splitting if they differ by an element of I(S2).
> Theorem D (Mess). I(S2) is an infinitely generated free group, with one Dehn twist generator for each
> symplectic splitting of H1(S2;Z).

Items 1 and 2 of the target are these sentences. The source says that curves differing by an element of
`I(S_2)` give the same splitting; the converse direction stated in item 2 is not in this passage and is
not used downstream. Item 3 follows because Theorem D's generators are Dehn twists attached to separating
curves, and a twist about an inessential curve is the identity, which can't be a free generator.

**Scope note.** Downstream nodes use only item 3: `I(S_2)` is generated by Dehn twists about essential
separating curves.
