Let `L` be a finite quotient of the universal seam group that preserves the
labeled first-packet `S3`, and let `K` be the image of `N`.  Suppose `K` is
solvable.  Take an `L`-chief series through `K` and choose the factor on which
the order-three element `z` first has nonzero image.  This is an elementary
abelian 3-factor.  Since the marked subgroup

```text
A=<r,u> ~= S3
```

centralizes `z`, the `A5`-submodule generated by that image is a quotient of
the induced module

```text
F3[A5/A],                    dim=10.                       (Q1920-SOL-1)
```

There is one conjugacy class of such `S3` subgroups in `A5`.  Exact MeatAxe
calculation gives

```text
irreducible F3[A5] dimensions:              1,4,6,
marked-S3 fixed-space dimensions:           1,1,0,
composition factors of F3[A5/A]:            4,1,4,1.      (Q1920-SOL-2)
```

Thus every solvable-kernel countermodel exposes a nonzero 3-chief-factor
packet of dimension at most ten, with only one- and four-dimensional `A5`
composition factors.  The six-dimensional irreducible cannot occur in the
submodule generated by `z`.

## Attempts

The nilpotent-residual proof does not extend formally to solvable kernels:
`S3` itself has 2-group abelianization and a surviving order-three derived
subgroup.  Nor does `(Q1920-SOL-2)` by itself give a contradiction, because
the four-dimensional `F3[A5]` type genuinely occurs and has a nonzero vector
fixed by the marked `S3`.  Closing the solvable case therefore requires the
additional action of the collision involution `c`, which inverts `z`, on the
one/four-dimensional chief-factor packet.
