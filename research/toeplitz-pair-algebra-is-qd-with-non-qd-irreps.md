**ESTABLISHED (Proposition C of the artifact, §5).**

Let `S` be the unilateral shift, `T = C*(S)` the Toeplitz algebra with symbol map
`q : T → C(T)`, and `T' = C*(S ⊕ S*) ⊆ B(ℓ²(N) ⊕ ℓ²(N))`.

1. `T' = {(a, b) ∈ T ⊕ T : q(a)(λ) = q(b)(λ̄) for all λ}`.  There is an exact
   sequence `0 → K ⊕ K → T' → C(T) → 0`.  So `T'` is a separable type I
   algebra.
2. `T'` is quasidiagonal, hence stably finite.  On `ℓ²(Z)`, `S ⊕ S*` is a
   rank-one perturbation of the bilateral shift, which is normal.  By
   Weyl--von Neumann--Berg it is diagonal plus compact.
3. Up to unitary equivalence, the irreducible representations of `T'` are the
   characters of `C(T)` and the coordinate representations `π_1(a, b) = a` and
   `π_2(a, b) = b`.  Every family of irreducible representations with faithful
   direct sum contains `π_1` and `π_2`.  Both have image `T`, which is infinite,
   not quasidiagonal and has a tracial state.
4. Consequences:
   - `T'` has traced non-quasidiagonal primitive quotients.
   - The quotient `T'/(0 ⊕ K) ≅ T` of the stably finite `T'` is not stably
     finite.
   - `T'` has no separating family of irreducible representations with
     quasidiagonal images.  By the abstract of Blackadar--Kirchberg
     arXiv:0711.4949, which was not read further, this should mean `T'` is not
     inner quasidiagonal.
5. The boundary `∂[z] = ±(−1, 1) ∈ K_0(K ⊕ K)` is nonzero and singular.
   Quasidiagonality here comes from a reducible representation, not fibrewise.
