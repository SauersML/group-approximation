Let `F=F_m` and `K_N=<<w^N:w in F>>`.

## 1. The restricted quotient collapses to the ordinary one

By definition, `R(m,N)` is the quotient of `B=F/K_N` by the intersection
of its finite-index normal subgroups.  If `B` is finite, `{1}` itself is
one of those subgroups.  Their intersection is therefore `{1}`, so

```text
R(m,N)=B(m,N),       ker(F -> R(m,N))=K_N.                        (FBP1)
```

Consequently each relator `s_j` in a presentation of `R(m,N)` on the
canonical generator images lies in `K_N`.

## 2. Normal closure is a finite certificate

The normal closure `K_N` is the subgroup generated by the conjugates of the
words `w^N` and their inverses.  Group elements are finite products, hence
for each `s_j in K_N` there is a finite identity

```text
s_j = product_(t=1)^L v_t u_t^N v_t^(-1).                        (FBP2)
```

Indeed, a factor `v(w^N)^(-1)v^(-1)` can be rewritten as
`v(w^(-1))^N v^(-1)`.  Thus every factor in (FBP2) uses a positive
`N`th power, exactly the form controlled by the hypothesis.  If the relator
is freely trivial, insert the harmless factor `1^N` to arrange `L>=1`.

## 3. Unitary products telescope without dimension loss

Evaluate (FBP2) at any `U in U(d)^m` and set
`A_t=v_t(U)u_t(U)^N v_t(U)^(-1)`.  Every `A_t` is unitary and conjugation
preserves operator norm, so

```text
||A_t-I||_op = ||u_t(U)^N-I||_op.                                (FBP3)
```

The exact identity

```text
A_1...A_L-I
 = sum_(t=1)^L A_1...A_(t-1)(A_t-I)
```

and unitary invariance give

```text
||s_j(U)-I||_op
 <= sum_(t=1)^L ||u_t(U)^N-I||_op
 <= L max_(w in W_j) ||w(U)^N-I||_op,                            (FBP4)
```

where `W_j={u_t:1<=t<=L}`.  Repetitions may be removed because the last
bound uses a maximum.

## 4. Read off the modulus

Given `eps>0`, take `eta=eps/(L+1)`.  If every named defect in `W_j` is
strictly below `eta`, (FBP4) yields

```text
||s_j(U)-I||_op < L eps/(L+1) < eps.
```

The certificate is fixed before `d` and `U` are quantified, so the modulus
is dimension-free.  Taking `eps=2 sin(pi/N)` gives the sharpened Burnside
threshold.

Finiteness of the ordinary `B(m,N)` was used only in (FBP1), to put every
restricted relator inside `K_N`.  Finiteness of `R(m,N)` alone supplies no
such membership when `B(m,N)` is infinite.
