**Proof (lane proof at design level, bh-g1-simulation, 2026-09-18; unreviewed).**

## Conventions

These are those of `free-products-with-z-inherit-m2-from-universal-points`.
- `Γ` is infinite and finitely generated by `S`, with solvable word problem.
- The pattern of `x` at `g` is `a ↦ x(ag)`, and `(λ·x)(h) = x(hλ)`.
- `d(u,v) = |vu^{-1}|`. It is right-invariant, and `B(ρ)g` is the ball around `g`.
- Shortlex order on words is computable from the word problem.

**Parameters.** All are computable.
- `D_j = 6r_j + j + 1`.
- Palette `P_j = ⌊|B(D_j + r_j/2)| / |B(r_j/2)|⌋ + 1`. This bounds the number of `r_j`-separated
  points in a `D_j`-ball, plus one.
- Data length `ℓ_j = 2 + ⌈log_2 P_j⌉`.
- Radii `r_0 < r_1 < …`, chosen so that every level-`j` marker has at least `ℓ_j` children
  (step 1 below). It suffices that `|B(r_j/2 − 3r_{j−1})| ≥ ℓ_j · |B(6r_{j−1})|`. This holds for
  `r_j` large, since `ℓ_j = O(log |B(8r_j)|)`.
- Parse radius `π_j = Σ_{i ≤ j} 10r_i`. It covers the nearest-marker comparisons, which need markers within `6r_i`, at every level `i ≤ j`.

## The alphabet and the parse

Take `C = {0,1} x {◦, •}`: a bit, and a level-0 marker flag. The parse of `x` is deterministic,
bottom-up and local.
- `M_0` is the set of `•` cells, and `M_{−1} = Γ`.
- **Children.** For `j ≥ 0`, each `h ∈ M_{j−1}` is a child of its nearest `m ∈ M_j`. Ties are
  broken by shortlex order of `hm^{-1}`. The index of a child is the shortlex rank of `hm^{-1}`
  among its siblings. Every `m ∈ M_j` is its own child, of index 0.
- **Data.**
  - A cell contributes its bit to its level-0 parent.
  - A level-`(j−1)` marker `h` contributes its relay bit `D_{j−1}(h)[1]` to its level-`j`
    parent.
  - `D_j(m)` is the string of contributions of the children of index `< ℓ_j`, with fields
    `[0]` = promote flag, `[1]` = relay bit, and `[2..]` = `ID_j(m)`.
- **Levels.** `M_{j+1} = {m ∈ M_j : D_j(m)[0] = 1}`.

Reading `M_j` and `ID_j` near a cell needs radius `π_j`.

## The subshift

`X` is the set of `x` such that, for every `j ≥ 0`:
- **(A_j) Separation.** Distinct points of `M_j` are at distance `≥ r_j`.
- **(B_j) Covering.** Every point of `Γ` is within `3r_j` of `M_j`.
- **(C_j) Padding.** Children of index `≥ ℓ_j` contribute `0`.
- **(D_j) Identifiers.** Distinct `m, m' ∈ M_j` with `d(m,m') ≤ D_j` have `ID_j(m) ≠ ID_j(m')`.

Each condition has a computable window, so `X` is an effectively closed subshift.

## Proof

1. **Children suffice.**
   - If `m ∈ M_j`, the level-`(j−1)` markers in `B(r_j/2)m` are children of `m`, by (A_j).
   - By (B_{j−1}), there are at least `|B(r_j/2 − 3r_{j−1})| / |B(6r_{j−1})| ≥ ℓ_j` of them.
2. **Freeness (H).** Let `x ∈ X`, `s ≠ 1`, `n = |s|`, and `R(s) = 3r_n + π_n`. Suppose the
   patterns at `g` and at `sg` agree on `B(R(s))`.
   - By (B_n), some `c = ag ∈ M_n` has `|a| ≤ 3r_n`.
   - The parse of `c` reads cells `b·c`, `|b| ≤ π_n`, which lie at positions `ba ∈ B(R(s))` of
     the pattern at `g`. The same positions of the pattern at `sg` are the cells `b·(asg)`.
   - So `c' = asg ∈ M_n`, with `ID_n(c') = ID_n(c)`.
   - But `c' ≠ c`, and `d(c,c') = |asa^{-1}| ≤ 6r_n + n < D_n`. This contradicts (D_n).
   - Hence every `x ∈ X` satisfies (H) with this computable `R`, and `X` is free.
3. **Non-emptiness (greedy).** Build `x` by processing points in shortlex order, level by level
   from the bottom.
   - Promote an `M_{j−1}` point to `M_j` whenever it is at distance `≥ r_j` from the
     current `M_j`.
   - Give identifiers greedily with the least free colour. (D_j) has at most `P_j − 1` conflicts.
   - Write the data bits into children, which exist by step 1.
   - Every `M_{j−1}` point ends within `r_j` of `M_j`. Every point is within `3r_{j−1}` of
     `M_{j−1}`, so (B_j) holds.
4. **Finite-horizon lemma (E).** This is the step to review. Let `p` be a pattern on `F = B(ρ)`,
   and let `J = J(ρ)` be the least level with `r_J > 4ρ + π_{J−1}`. Then `p ∈ L(X)` iff:
   - `p` extends to a pattern `q` on `N = B(ρ + 10r_J)` satisfying every level-`≤ J` condition
     whose window lies in `N`; and
   - `p`'s visible level-`> J` data is consistent. That data is at most one marker per level
     together with the bits of its storage that fall in `F`.

   Both conditions are finite checks, so `L(X)` is decidable.
   - *(⇒)* Restrict.
   - *(⇐)* Freeze `p`, and freeze three regions:
     - (i) the whole level-`≤ J` structure of `q` (markers, promote flags, identifiers) on
       `B(ρ + 6r_J)`;
     - (ii) the markers of `q` in `B(ρ + 7r_J)` that cover the points of (i), with their lower
       promotions, but with the flag above their covering level left free;
     - (iii) nothing else.

     Then run the greedy procedure of step 3 on everything else, regenerating unfrozen
     children and data bits.
     - *Separation* is respected by greedy.
     - *Covering.* The rigid obligations are the zero flags in (i). By validity of `q` they are
       met by the markers in (ii). A marker in (ii), or a new one, has its flag free, so greedy
       either promotes it or finds it within `r_{j+1}` of `M_{j+1}`.
     - *Voronoi assignments.* The markers whose data involves cells of `F` lie within `3r_J` of
       `F`. New promotions happen only outside `B(ρ + 6r_J)`, so no new marker can become
       nearer to those markers than their `q`-parents.
     - *Levels `> J`.* `F` holds at most one marker per level, and its storage mostly lies
       outside `F`. Greedy covers `F` by a promoted marker at distance about `2r_j`, which is
       separated because `r_j > 4ρ`.
     - *Identifiers.* Frozen partial identifiers are consistent in `q`, and new neighbours
       avoid them, since the palette exceeds the conflict degree.
   - *Pockets of the Cayley graph* can only make `p` non-extendable. They are detected by the
     finite search in the first condition, since `q` is required to exist.
5. **Separated gluing (G).** Let `p_1, p_2 ∈ L(X)` have radius `≤ ρ`, placed at distance
   `≥ 20r_{J(ρ)+2}`.
   - Realize each with no promotion beyond `J(ρ)+2`. The level-`j` flag of `m` is relayed through
     the index-1 child, which is a different `M_{j−2}` point at distance `≥ r_{j−2}`. So a
     radius-`ρ` pattern shows no flag above level `J(ρ)+1`.
   - Their level-`≤ J+2` neighbourhoods are then disjoint, and lemma (E) applies to the union.
6. **Computable points.** Step 3 is effective, with each finite stage checked by lemma (E). So
   `X` has computable points. `∎`

## Remark

Nested markers with locally distinct identifiers are a standard way to get freeness (compare
the blueprints of Gao–Jackson–Seward; context only). What is new here is:
- the four-symbol bottom-up encoding;
- lemma (E), which makes the language decidable;
- lemma (G), which lets one computable point exhibit the whole language.
