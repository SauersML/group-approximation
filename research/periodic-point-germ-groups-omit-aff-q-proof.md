**Step 1 (modular maps).** Commensuration gives, for each `i`, a homomorphism
`mu_i: Gamma_i -> Q^x_{>0}` with `mu_i(g) = n/m` when
`g delta_i^m g^-1 = delta_i^n`. It is well defined and a homomorphism by the
computation in Corollary (ii) of `aff-q-forces-distortion-in-abelian-kernels`.
Its kernel is `U_{m>=1} C_{Gamma_i}(delta_i^m)`. Put `Gamma = prod Gamma_i`,
finitely generated, and `mu = (mu_1, ..., mu_r): Gamma -> (Q^x_{>0})^r`, a map to an
abelian group.

**Step 2 (the kernel after Aff(Q)).** Suppose `Aff_K(Q) <= Gamma`. By parts 1–2 of
the lemma there is `a != 1` in `K` such that `H = <T_1, D_a> <= ker mu`, with
`<T_1>` distorted in `H`. Each generator of `H` lies in
`prod_i C_{Gamma_i}(delta_i^(m_i))` for suitable `m_i >= 1`. With `M` the
product of the finitely many exponents that occur,
`H <= prod_i C_i`, where `C_i` is the group of germs at `p_i` commuting with
`delta_i^M`.

**Step 3 (structure of C_i).** Fix `p = p_i` and `delta = delta_i`.
- **Fundamental domain.** Take a clopen neighbourhood `U` of `p` on which `delta`
  is defined, with `delta(U)` a proper clopen subset of `U` and
  `∩_k delta^k(U) = {p}`. Put `D = U \ delta^M(U)`, a nonempty clopen set, hence a
  finite union of cones and a Cantor space. So `U \ {p}` is the disjoint union
  `⊔_{k>=0} delta^(Mk)(D)`.
- **Periodic extension.** A germ `h` commuting with `delta^M` near `p` is
  represented on `delta^(Mk_0)(U)` by a map with `h delta^M = delta^M h`. Its
  conjugates by powers of `delta^M` then define one homeomorphism `h~` of the
  space `Y = D x Z`. Here the point `delta^(Mk)(x)` is `(x, k)`, and negative `k`
  are added formally, as in `commensurating-end-germ-groups-omit-aff-q-proof`
  Step 2. `h~` commutes with the shift `s(x,k) = (x, k+1)`, and `h -> h~` is an
  injective homomorphism.
- **Coordinates.** `h~` induces a homeomorphism `v` of `Y/s = D` and satisfies
  `h~(x, 0) = (v(x), c(x))`, with `c: D -> Z` continuous, hence locally constant.
  `v` is locally a prefix replacement, because `h` is locally in `B` off `p` and
  `delta` is a prefix replacement. By compactness, `v` lies in the Higman--Thompson
  group `V_{d,t}` of the `t` cones making up `D`.
- **Composition.** `(c, v)(c', v') = (c' + c∘v', v v')`.
- **Embedding.** `C_i` embeds in `P = C(D, Z) x| V_{d,t}` with this product.

**Step 4 (no distortion in P).** Let `L <= P` be finitely generated by elements
with `||c||_inf <= R`. By the composition law, a word of length `l` in these
generators has cocycle of sup-norm at most `lR`. Let `g = (c, v) in L` have
infinite order.
- **If v has infinite order.** `V_{d,t}` embeds in `V_{d,t'} ≅ V_d`, where
  `t' >= t` is chosen with `t' ≡ 1 mod (d-1)` and the extra cones are acted on
  trivially. So `<v>` is undistorted in the finitely generated image of `L` in
  `V_d` (`thompson-v-cyclic-subgroups-are-undistorted`, applied as in its
  statement). Projection is 1-Lipschitz for the image generating set, so `<g>` is
  undistorted in `L`.
- **If v has order e.** Then `g^e = (s, 1)` with
  `s = sum_{j<e} c∘v^j != 0`, since `g` has infinite order. So `g^(eN) = (Ns, 1)`.
  Its sup-norm `N||s||_inf` is at most `R l_L(g^(eN))`, so
  `l_L(g^(eN)) >= N/R`. So `<g>` is undistorted in `L`.

**Step 5 (products, and the contradiction).** In a finite product `prod_i P_i`, an
element of infinite order has an infinite-order coordinate. Projection to that
factor shows its cyclic subgroup is undistorted in every finitely generated
subgroup containing it. `H` is finitely generated and lies in
`prod_i C_i <= prod_i P_i`, yet `<T_1>` is distorted in `H`. This contradiction
proves the statement.

**Consequence.** The normal-subgroup fact is proved in
`commensurating-end-germ-groups-omit-aff-q-proof`. That `V_{d,r}` embeds in `V` is
Higman's; see survey Remark 4.2.
