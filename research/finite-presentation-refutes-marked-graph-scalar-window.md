Fix a prime power `q` and `d >= 3`, put

```text
R_+ = F_q[x_1,...,x_d],
R   = F_q[x_1^(+-1),...,x_d^(+-1)],
Gamma = EL_r(R_+),
G     = EL_r(R) semidirect SL_d(Z),
```

and choose the elementary rank in the stable range, for example
`r >= d+4`.  This is an allowed instance of Kun--Thom Theorem E, which
holds for every `r,d >= 3`.  Let `o=Gamma`,
`h=e_12((x_1...x_d)^(-1))`, and `y=h o`; the minimal marked orbital form
satisfies

```text
b(delta_o,delta_y)=1.                                  (FPS1)
```

**Claim.**  The actor `G` is finitely presented, and there is one finite
actor table and the two-lamp window `{o,y}` for which no Clifford lifts can
have scalar-only multiplication curvature as demanded by
`minimal-marked-graph-finite-window-phase-space-section`.

## Finite presentability of the exact Theorem E actor

The coefficient ring `R` is a regular noetherian ring of Krull dimension
`d`, generated as a ring by finitely many units.  For type `A_(r-1)`,
Allcock's *Steinberg groups as amalgams*, Theorem 1.4, gives a finite
presentation of the pre-Steinberg group: one may use item (ii), because the diagram is
2-spherical without `A_1` components and `R` is a rank-one module over the
subring generated by its finitely many unit generators, or item (iii) in
rank at least four.  Since `A_(r-1)` is spherical, Allcock's Theorem 1.1
identifies the pre-Steinberg and Steinberg groups, so `St_r(R)` is finitely
presented.

Bass stable range gives `sr(R) <= d+1`.  Suslin--Tulenbaev's stabilization
theorem for `K_2` says that

```text
K_2(r,R) -> K_2(R)
```

is an isomorphism once `r-1 > sr(R)`; `r >= d+4` is therefore safely in
range.  Since `R` is regular, the iterated Bass--Heller--Swan fundamental
theorem and Quillen's calculation for a finite field give

```text
K_2(R)
  ~= K_2(F_q) directSum d K_1(F_q)
       directSum binom(d,2) K_0(F_q)
  ~= (F_q^times)^d directSum Z^(binom(d,2)).            (FPS2)
```

Thus the central kernel of `St_r(R) -> EL_r(R)` is finitely generated.
Quotienting the finite Steinberg presentation by finitely many kernel
generators proves that `EL_r(R)` is finitely presented.  The arithmetic
group `SL_d(Z)` is finitely presented, and a semidirect product of two
finitely presented groups is finitely presented after writing the action of
each acting generator on each normal-factor generator.  Hence `G` is
finitely presented.

Primary sources for the load-bearing steps are:

- D. Allcock, *Steinberg groups as amalgams*, Theorem 1.4;
- A. Suslin--M. Tulenbaev, *Stabilization theorem for the Milnor
  `K_2`-functor* (the map is bijective when `r-1 > sr(R)`);
- the Bass--Heller--Swan fundamental theorem and Quillen's
  `K_2(F_q)=0`, recorded for example in Weibel's *The K-book*,
  Corollary III.6.1.1 and the fundamental-theorem chapters.

No finite-presentation assertion at the small unstable ranks is needed.
The Kun--Thom construction and the minimal marked-graph arithmetic both
allow this stable-rank specialization.

## One finite table gives the contradiction

Choose a finite presentation `G=<S|Q>`, with `S` symmetric.  Since `Gamma`
has property `(T)`, it is finitely generated; fix a finite generating set
`A subset Gamma` and words over `S` for every `a in A` and for `h`.

Form one finite actor table `E` containing `1`, `S`, `A`, `h`, and every
prefix occurring in:

1. every presentation relator in `Q`;
2. the inverse relations for the symmetric generators;
3. the chosen words for `A` and `h`.

Let `T` contain the corresponding prefix multiplication triangles, together
with `(1,1,1)`.  Test covariance on the lamp window `F={o,y}` for

```text
U_a W_o U_a^* = W_o       (a in A),
U_h W_o U_h^* = W_y.                              (FPS3)
```

Suppose the scalar-curvature certificate existed.  Project each Clifford
normalizer `U_g` to its symplectic action `s_g in Sp(K)`.  Scalar curvature
on every triangle says exactly

```text
s_g s_k = s_(gk).                                   (FPS4)
```

The triangle `(1,1,1)` makes `s_1=1`.  Traversing the finitely many prefix
triangles makes every relator in `Q` vanish and identifies the named
elements `A,h` with their words.  Therefore `s|S` extends to a genuine
homomorphism

```text
q : G -> Sp(K).                                      (FPS5)
```

The target is finite.  Finite-quotient blindness for this Kun--Thom
compression pair gives

```text
q(h) in q(Gamma).                                    (FPS6)
```

Concretely, for every chosen compressor `t` one has
`t Gamma t^(-1) <= Gamma`; hence its finite image is a subgroup of
`q(Gamma)` having the same order and is equal to it.  The compressors and
`Gamma` generate `G`, so `q(Gamma)` is normal in `q(G)`.  The marked Laurent
root `h` lies in the normal closure of `Gamma`.
Indeed `(-1,...,-1)` is primitive, so some matrix in `SL_d(Z)` sends a
coordinate vector to it; conjugating the corresponding positive elementary
root from `Gamma` gives `h`.

Write `v_o,v_y in K` for the two tested Weyl labels.  Equations `(FPS3)`
say that every `q(a)`, `a in A`, fixes `v_o`, while

```text
q(h)v_o=v_y.                                         (FPS7)
```

Thus all of `q(Gamma)=<q(A)>` fixes `v_o`.  Equations `(FPS6)--(FPS7)` give
`v_y=v_o`, contradicting `(FPS1)` because an alternating form has
`b(v_o,v_o)=0`.

This refutes the scalar-only finite-window section.  Notice that no scalar
Weil multiplier, conjugate doubling, trace estimate, or Hilbert--Schmidt
limit enters: the contradiction already occurs after projecting one finite
table to its finite symplectic group.

## Exact scope and the weaker finiteness property

The crossed-product target `minimal-marked-graph-crossed-product-ce`
remains **OPEN**.  Its microstates may keep small nonzero phase-space
curvature on the actor relators; finite presentability does not turn
normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt approximate representations into exact finite
quotients.

The sharp finiteness hypothesis used above is weaker than finite
presentability.  It is enough that there be finite words `S,A,h` and a
finite relator fragment `Q_0` such that every map of `S` into a finite group
satisfying `Q_0` obeys

```text
h(image) in <A(image)>.                              (FPS8)
```

Call `(FPS8)` a **finite marked-collapse certificate**.  Finite
presentability of `G`, finite generation of `Gamma`, and relative profinite
blindness supply it by taking the whole finite presentation.  Without such
a certificate, successive finite relation windows can evade this particular
argument even though every genuine finite quotient of `G` collapses the
mark.
