## The problem

Kourovka 21.44 (S. Eberhard). Let `W = ··· ≀ A_5 ≀ A_5 ≀ A_5` be the infinite iterated
(permutational) wreath product of `A_5` in its natural action on 5 points, so that `W` is the
closed subgroup of `Aut(T_5)` all of whose vertex permutations lie in `A_5`. Does `W` contain a
finitely generated dense subgroup of subexponential word growth?

Notation: `W_m` is the finite quotient of `W` acting on the first `m` levels (so `W_1 = A_5`,
`W_m = W_{m-1}^5 ⋊ A_5` with `A_5` permuting the five level-one subtrees), and `St(k)` is the
stabilizer of level `k`.

## Proposition N (normal subgroups of `W_m`)

For every `m ≥ 1`, the normal subgroups of `W_m` are exactly the level stabilizers
`St_{W_m}(k)`, `0 ≤ k ≤ m`. In particular:
- every nontrivial normal subgroup contains the bottom base `A_5^{5^{m-1}}`;
- `W_m` has trivial solvable radical and trivial Fitting subgroup;
- the normal closure of any `g ∈ St(k) ∖ St(k+1)` is `St(k)`.

*Proof.* Every `W_j` is perfect: `A_5` is, and `W_j` is generated by `A_5` and the perfect
`W_{j-1}^5`. We prove the last statement by induction on `m`; the others follow, since a normal
subgroup is the product of the normal closures of its elements and the `St(k)` form a chain.
For `m = 1` this is simplicity of `A_5`.

Let `N` be the normal closure of `g`.

*Case `k = 0`.* Here `g` has nontrivial root permutation `r`. Pick a coordinate `i` with
`r(i) ≠ i`. For `x ∈ W_{m-1}` placed in coordinate `i`, the commutator `[g, x]` is supported on
the two coordinates `i` and `r(i)`, with entry `x^{±1}` in coordinate `i`. Commuting it with
`y ∈ W_{m-1}`, also placed in coordinate `i`, gives an element supported on coordinate `i` alone,
with entry a commutator of `x^{±1}` and `y`. Since `W_{m-1}` is perfect, these entries generate
`W_{m-1}`, so `N ⊇ W_{m-1}` in coordinate `i`. By transitivity of `A_5`, `N ⊇ W_{m-1}^5 = St(1)`.
Then `N/St(1)` is a nontrivial normal subgroup of the simple group `A_5`, so `N = W_m`.

*Case `k ≥ 1`.* Write `g = (g_1, …, g_5)`. After conjugating by a rooted element, which does not
change `N`, we may assume `g_1 ∈ St_{W_{m-1}}(k-1) ∖ St(k)`. For `x ∈ W_{m-1}` in coordinate 1,
`[g, x] = ([g_1, x], 1, 1, 1, 1)`. The normal subgroup of `W_{m-1}` generated by these entries is
`[⟨⟨g_1⟩⟩, W_{m-1}]`. By induction `⟨⟨g_1⟩⟩ = S := St_{W_{m-1}}(k-1) ≅ W_{m-k}^{5^{k-1}}`, which is
perfect, so `[S, W_{m-1}] ⊇ [S, S] = S`. Hence `N ⊇ S` in coordinate 1. Conjugating by rooted
elements gives `N ⊇ S^5 = St_{W_m}(k)`, and `N ⊆ St(k)` because `g ∈ St(k)` and `St(k)` is normal. ∎

## Proposition G (the known lower bound; attribution)

Put `θ := max{1, log diam(A_5)/log log 60}` in the conventions of Eberhard–Maini–Sabatini–Tracey
(arXiv:2604.15303, where `θ_2(G) = max({log diam(T)/log log|T| : T ∈ 𝒩(G)} ∪ {1})`), and
`β := 1/(20+10θ)`. Then no finitely generated dense subgroup `Γ ≤ W` has growth
`γ_Γ(n) ⪯ exp(n^β)`.

*Proof.*
1. Put `N_i := Γ ∩ St_W(i)`. These are finite-index normal subgroups with trivial intersection,
   and `Γ/N_i = W_i` because `Γ` is dense.
2. All composition factors of `W_i` are `A_5`, so `θ_2(Γ/N_i) = θ`.
3. By EMST Theorem 1.10, read at source, growth `⪯ exp(n^β)` would make `Γ` virtually
   nilpotent. Take a nilpotent normal subgroup `M` of finite index `k` in `Γ`.
4. The image of `M` in each `W_i` is a nilpotent normal subgroup of index at most `k`. By
   Proposition N it is trivial, so `|W_i| ≤ k` for all `i`. This is absurd. ∎

Credit: this is an immediate consequence of EMST's gap theorem, and EMST appear to state it
themselves with `β = 1/40`. That is according to the search-engine summary; I could not find
the sentence in the HTML of v1. **No priority is claimed.**

## Proposition S (spinal groups over `A_5`)

Use the spinal groups of Bartholdi–Šunić (arXiv:math/0005113, Definition 3.1, read at
source), but with the level maps only required to be homomorphisms. Concretely:
- the root group is `A_5` acting on `Y = {1, …, 5}`;
- `G_B` is a finite group, and `ω = ω_1 ω_2 …` is a sequence of homomorphisms
  `ω_i : G_B → A_5`, with kernels `K_i`;
- `b ∈ G_B` acts as the directed automorphism `b_ω = (ω_1(b), 1, 1, 1, b_{σω})`, with the spine
  along the letter 5;
- `G_ω := ⟨A_5, B_ω⟩`.

In Bartholdi–Šunić, `ω_i ∈ Epi(G_B, G_A)` and `ω ∈ Ω̂`, meaning that for every `i`,
`⋃_{j≥i} K_j = G_B` and `⋂_{j≥i} K_j = 1`. Their Theorem 5.1 says that `G_ω` then has
subexponential growth.

**(a) Dense ⇒ onto.** If `G_ω` is dense in `W`, then every `ω_i` is surjective.

*Proof.*
- The section of `G_ω` at a level-`i` vertex is a word in the rooted letters `ω_i(G_B)` and the
  directed letters `B_{σ^iω}`. This holds by induction on `i`: rooted letters have trivial
  sections, and the sections of `b_{σ^{i-1}ω}` are `ω_i(b)`, `b_{σ^iω}` or `1`.
- Directed letters act trivially on the children of the vertex. So `St_{G_ω}(i)` acts on
  level `i+1` through `ω_i(G_B)^{5^i}`.
- Density forces this action to be all of `A_5^{5^i}`, so `ω_i(G_B) = A_5`. ∎

**(b) Onto ⇒ the growth hypothesis fails.** If every `ω_i` is surjective, some `g ∈ G_B` lies in
no `K_i`. In particular `ω ∉ Ω̂`, so Bartholdi–Šunić Theorem 5.1 never applies to a dense spinal
group over `A_5`. The same holds for any nonabelian simple root group.

*Proof.*
1. `G_B` is finite, so only finitely many kernels occur; let `N_0` be their intersection.
2. `G_B/N_0` is a subdirect product of copies of `A_5`, hence isomorphic to `A_5^k`, since
   subdirect products of nonabelian simple groups are direct products of diagonals.
3. Each `ω_i` factors through `A_5^k`. The kernel of an epimorphism `A_5^k → A_5` is a maximal
   normal subgroup, which is the product of all factors but one.
4. Take `g ∈ G_B` mapping to `(s, …, s)` with `s ≠ 1`. Then `ω_i(g) ≠ 1` for every `i`. ∎

**(c) Onto and `K_i ⊄ K_{i+1}` ⇒ dense.** If every `ω_i` is surjective and `K_i ⊄ K_{i+1}` for
all `i ≥ 1`, then `G_ω` is dense in `W`.

*Proof.* Induction on `n`, for all shifts `σ^jω` at once (the hypothesis is shift-invariant):
`π_n(G_ω) = W_n`. The case `n = 1` is the rooted group.

*Case `n = 2`.* `St(1)` is generated by the conjugates `b_ω^a` with `a ∈ A_5`. These act on
level 2 as `ω_1(b)` placed in coordinate `a(1)`. So the image is `A_5^5`, and `π_2(G_ω) = W_2`.

*Case `n ≥ 3`.* Let `ψ` be the section map on `St(1)`, and let `L ⊴ St_{G_ω}(1)` be the elements
whose sections are supported on coordinate 5.
- The coordinate-5 projection of `St(1)` is `⟨ω_1(G_B), B_{σω}⟩ = G_{σω}`, using surjectivity
  of `ω_1`. So the coordinate-5 projection `L_5` of `L` is normal in `G_{σω}`.
- For `x ∈ K_1`, `ψ(x_ω) = (1, 1, 1, 1, x_{σω})`, so `x_{σω} ∈ L_5`. Pick `x ∈ K_1 ∖ K_2`. Then
  `x_{σω}` acts trivially on level 1 and as `ω_2(x) ≠ 1` below vertex 1, so
  `π_{n-1}(x_{σω}) ∈ St(1) ∖ St(2)` in `W_{n-1}`.
- By induction `π_{n-1}(G_{σω}) = W_{n-1}`, so `π_{n-1}(L_5)` is normal in `W_{n-1}`. By
  Proposition N it contains `St_{W_{n-1}}(1)`.
- Conjugating `L` by rooted elements shows that `π_{n-1}^{×5}ψ(St(1))` contains
  `∏_j St_{W_{n-1}}(1)`. Modulo that product it is `A_5^5`, by the case `n = 2`. Hence it equals
  `W_{n-1}^5`, and so `π_n(G_ω) = W_n`. ∎

**What this says about 21.44.**
- Spinal groups with directed sections at a single spine are the family behind Grigorchuk's
  and Šunić's dense intermediate-growth subgroups of iterated wreath products of cyclic groups.
- Over `A_5` they are dense essentially exactly when every level map is onto (by (a) and (c)).
- In that regime there is always a "mixed" directed letter whose section is nontrivial at
  every level (by (b)). The η-contraction of Bartholdi–Šunić §6 uses letters that vanish, and
  so gives no bound there.
- So the spinal route to a YES answer is not closed. It moves entirely outside the proved
  regime.

## The smallest test object (open)

Let `P := G_ω` with `G_B = A_5 × A_5` and `ω = (p_1 p_2)^∞`, the two coordinate projections
alternating. Then `K_1 = 1 × A_5` and `K_2 = A_5 × 1`, so `P` is dense by (c).
- **Size:** `P` is generated by four elements, two for the rooted `A_5` and two for the directed
  `A_5 × A_5`.
- **Automaton structure:** it is finite-state with bounded activity, a bounded automaton group.
  So it is amenable, by Bartholdi–Kaimanovich–Nekrashevych (Duke 2010; recalled, not re-read).
- **Mixed letters:** the letters `(s, t)` with `s, t ≠ 1` are exactly the letters that never
  vanish.

**Question.** Does `P` have subexponential growth? If it does, Kourovka 21.44 has answer YES.
If it has exponential growth, then so does every dense spinal group over `A_5` with periodic
`ω`, and the spinal route is closed. The second claim is a conjecture, not proved here.
