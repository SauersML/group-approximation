**ESTABLISHED as stated (lane proof, not independently reviewed).** Input: the Milnor–Wolf
theorem, cited below. Kourovka 21.44 itself stays **OPEN**.

## Statement

Let `H = ⟨A_5, d_s : s ∈ A_5⟩ ≤ Aut(T_5)` with `d_s = (s, 1, 1, 1, d_s)`. Here the section of `d_s` at
vertex 1 is the rooted `s`, at vertex 5 it is `d_s`, and elsewhere it is trivial. Then `H` has
exponential word growth. So does the spinal group `P ⊇ H` of
`kourovka-21-44-spinal-groups-over-a5-dense-regime`, since `H ≤ P` (Proposition D, referee PASS
15b37f96f8). Neither is a witness for 21.44.

## Proof

**The subgroup.** Let `G_0 = Stab_{A_5}({1,5})`, the setwise stabilizer, of order 6. It is generated
by `s = (1 5)(2 3)` and `s' = (1 5)(2 4)`, and `r := s s' = (2 3 4)` (up to orientation). Its
restriction to `{2,3,4}` is an isomorphism `G_0 ≅ S_3`, and `g ∈ G_0` swaps 1 and 5 iff it is odd
there. Put `K = ⟨G_0, d_t : t ∈ G_0⟩ ≤ H`, generated by `s, s', d_s, d_{s'}` since `t ↦ d_t` is a
homomorphism.

**Step 1 (K is self-similar on two letters).** By induction on word length, every `g ∈ K` has
level-1 permutation in `G_0`, sections at 1 and 5 in `K`, and trivial sections at 2, 3 and 4. This
holds for the generators, and the section rule `(gh)|_x = g|_x · h|_{x^g}` preserves it, because `G_0`
preserves both `{1,5}` and `{2,3,4}`. Hence `K` preserves the binary subtree `B = {1,5}*`.

**Step 2 (the kernel is abelian of exponent 3).** Let `F : K → Aut(B)` be restriction to `B`, and
let `N = ker F`.
- Every vertex outside `B` has the form `u x w` with `u ∈ B` and `x ∈ {2,3,4}`.
- Take `g ∈ N`, let `ρ_u` be the level-1 permutation of `g|_u`, and recall that sections at 2, 3, 4
  are trivial. Then `g` acts by `u x w ↦ u x^{ρ_u} w`.
- Since `g` fixes `u1` and `u5`, each `ρ_u` lies in `⟨r⟩ ≅ ℤ/3`.
- So `g ↦ (ρ_u)_{u ∈ B}` embeds `N` into the abelian group `(ℤ/3)^B`, with composition pointwise.

**Step 3 (the quotient is D_∞).** `F(G_0) = ⟨σ⟩`, where `σ` swaps 1 and 5. `F(d_t)` is trivial for
even `t`, and equals `b = (σ, b)` for odd `t`. So `F(K) = ⟨σ, b⟩` is generated by two involutions
(`b² = (1, b²)`, so `b² = 1`). The product `bσ` has wreath recursion `(σ, b)σ`, and its two sections
multiply to `σb = (bσ)^{-1}`. By the standard criterion (`(g_1, g_2)σ` is transitive on level
`n+1` iff `g_1 g_2` is transitive on level `n`), `bσ` acts transitively on every level of `B`. So it
has infinite order, and `F(K) ≅ D_∞`.

**Step 4 (N is infinite).**
- `d_r ∈ N`, and as a function on `B` its support is the ray `S = {5^k 1 : k ≥ 0}`. It meets each
  level once.
- Pick `z ∈ K` with `F(z) = bσ`, for example `z = d_s s` in a suitable order.
- Conjugating an element of `N` by `z^n` moves its support by `F(z)^{±n}`. Any values that change
  under the `S_3` labels stay nonzero.
- Level-transitivity puts the level-`(k+1)` vertex of `S` in an orbit of size `2^{k+1}`. So the
  elements `z^{-n} d_r z^n` (`n ∈ ℤ`) are pairwise distinct, and `N` is infinite.

**Conclusion.**
- `K` is finitely generated and solvable, being abelian-by-`D_∞`.
- Its subgroup `N` is infinite of exponent 3, hence not finitely generated. So `K` is not
  virtually nilpotent, since finitely generated virtually nilpotent groups have all subgroups
  finitely generated.
- By Milnor–Wolf (J. Milnor, J. Differential Geom. 2 (1968) 447–449; J. A. Wolf, ibid. 421–446),
  `K` has exponential growth.
- Since `K ≤ H ≤ P` are finitely generated, `H` and `P` do too. ∎

## Remarks

- **Why merge-counting was never going to settle H.** For a positive word in
  `Z = {r d_s : r(5) ↦ 1, s ∈ S*}`, the element depends only on:
  - the level-1 permutation;
  - the labels `s_i`;
  - the streams `σ_i = 5^{P_i^{-1}}`, where `P_i` is the prefix product.
  
  This is because the section at `x` collects `s_i` when `x^{P_i} = 1`, `d_{s_i}` when
  `x^{P_i} = 5`, and nothing otherwise, and `r_i(5) = 1` forces `x^{P_i} = 1 ⟺ x = σ_{i−1}`. So the
  `144^k` merge-free words of the companion node represent at most `3·48^k` elements, and
  `Z⁺ → H` is **not** injective. The exponential lower bound above comes from the solvable
  subgroup `K`, not from that family.
- **Mechanism.** The rooted group has a 2-point setwise stabilizer whose pointwise stabilizer
  (`ℤ/3`) is abelian and acts only on leaves off the spine. That yields an abelian-by-dihedral
  subgroup, a lamplighter-type obstruction.
  - Any spinal subgroup of `W` containing such a configuration has exponential growth: rooted
    `Stab({i,j})` together with `d_t` for `t` in it, with sections `t` at `i` and `d_t` at `j`.
  - Future candidates for 21.44 must avoid this configuration, and not only merge-freeness.
- **Credit.** `H` is the constant-sequence group of Neumann–Segal type (Juschenko–Nekrashevych–de
  la Salle, arXiv:1305.2637, §4.3.2, "a general version of Segal's construction"); see the
  companion node. Whether
  its exponential growth is already recorded in the literature was not checked. No priority is
  claimed.
