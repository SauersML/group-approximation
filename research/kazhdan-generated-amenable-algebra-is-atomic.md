Let `Gamma` be a countable group with Kazhdan's property (T), let `(N, tau)` be
a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state, and let
`pi : Gamma -> U(N)` be a group homomorphism -- not necessarily injective, with
no condition on the character `tau o pi`.  Put `P = pi(Gamma)''`.

**Theorem.**  If `P` is amenable (injective; for instance if `N` is itself
amenable, e.g. `N = R`, since amenability passes to von Neumann subalgebras of
finite amenable algebras), then `P` is atomic of type I:

```text
P  ~=  (+)_(i in I) M_(n_i)(C),        I countable,
tau|_P = sum_i lambda_i tr_(n_i),      lambda_i > 0,  sum_i lambda_i = 1.
```

Equivalently, `pi` is unitarily a direct sum `(+)_i sigma_i` of pairwise
inequivalent finite-dimensional irreducible representations `sigma_i` of
`Gamma`, each appearing with positive trace weight `lambda_i`.  Conversely
every such direct sum generates an amenable (atomic) algebra.

**Two remarks on scope.**  (a) Nothing is assumed about how `Gamma` sits in a
larger group, nor about the character: the conclusion is purely a consequence
of property (T) plus amenability of the generated algebra.  (b) The theorem is
sharp in the sense that the regular character is excluded exactly: `L(Gamma)`
is not amenable for infinite Kazhdan `Gamma`, and the atomic algebras are the
only amenable ones a Kazhdan group can generate.

**Why it matters here.**  The abstract-embedding equivalence
`abstract-unitary-embedding-upgrades-to-hyperlinear` opens the possibility of
modelling a Kazhdan subgroup `Gamma < G` by an algebra `P` that is amenable --
the hypothesis under which Popa's relative free independence and the
Brown--Dykema--Jung amalgamation permanence apply.  This theorem says that such a
`P` is necessarily atomic, and `atomic-tracial-algebra-endomorphisms-are-automorphisms`
then makes atomic `P` rigid under compression
(`amenable-kazhdan-models-absorb-compression`).
