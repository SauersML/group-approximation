Throughout `[a,b]=a b a^-1 b^-1`.  The Steinberg group `St_n(R)` over a unital
associative ring `R` is generated by `x_ij(r)`, `i!=j`, `r in R`, subject to

```text
(S1) x_ij(r) x_ij(s) = x_ij(r+s);
(S2) [x_ij(r), x_jk(s)] = x_ik(rs)          for i,j,k distinct;
(S3) [x_ij(r), x_kl(s)] = 1                 for j!=k and i!=l.
```

Everything below uses only these relations; the script in the artifact merely
confirms the signs in the quotient `EL_3(M_2(Z))`.

## Two conjugation rules

From (S2), for distinct `i,j,k`,

```text
(C1) x_ij(a) x_jk(b) x_ij(a)^-1 = x_ik(ab) x_jk(b),
(C2) x_jk(b) x_ij(a) x_jk(b)^-1 = x_ik(-ab) x_ij(a),
```

the second because `[x_jk(b),x_ij(a)]=[x_ij(a),x_jk(b)]^-1=x_ik(-ab)`.

## Step 1: conjugation by `w=w_12` in `St_3(R)`

`w=x_12(1)x_21(-1)x_12(1)`, so conjugation by `w` is conjugation by
`x_12(1)`, then by `x_21(-1)`, then by `x_12(1)`.  Pairs that commute by (S3)
are marked `(S3)`.

- `x_13(r)`: fixed by `x_12(1)` (S3); `x_21(-1)` gives `x_23(-r)x_13(r)` (C1);
  `x_12(1)` gives `x_13(-r)x_23(-r)x_13(r)` (C1 and S3), which equals
  `x_23(-r)` since `x_13,x_23` commute (S3).
- `x_23(r)`: `x_12(1)` gives `x_13(r)x_23(r)` (C1); `x_21(-1)` gives
  `x_23(-r)x_13(r)x_23(r)=x_13(r)` (C1, S3); `x_12(1)` fixes `x_13(r)` (S3).
- `x_31(r)`: `x_12(1)` gives `x_32(-r)x_31(r)` (C2); `x_21(-1)` gives
  `x_31(-r)x_32(-r)x_31(r)=x_32(-r)` (C2, S3); `x_12(1)` fixes it (S3).
- `x_32(r)`: fixed by `x_12(1)` (S3); `x_21(-1)` gives `x_31(r)x_32(r)` (C2);
  `x_12(1)` gives `x_32(-r)x_31(r)x_32(r)=x_31(r)` (C2, S3).

Hence

```text
(P1) w x_13(r) w^-1 = x_23(-r),     (P2) w x_23(r) w^-1 = x_13(r),
(P3) w x_31(r) w^-1 = x_32(-r),     (P4) w x_32(r) w^-1 = x_31(r).
```

By (S2), `x_12(r)=[x_13(r),x_32(1)]` and `x_21(r)=[x_23(r),x_31(1)]`, so

```text
(P5) w x_12(r) w^-1 = [x_23(-r),x_31(1)] = x_21(-r),
(P6) w x_21(r) w^-1 = [x_13(r),x_32(-1)] = x_12(-r).
```

Iterating, `w^2` sends each of `x_13(r),x_23(r),x_31(r),x_32(r)` to the same
root with `-r`, and fixes `x_12(r),x_21(r)`.  Therefore `w^4` commutes with
every generator and

```text
z := w^4 is central in St_3(R).                                  (Z1)
```

## Step 2: an element inverting `w`

The relations (S1)--(S3) are invariant under relabelling indices by any
permutation, so `tau=(2 3)` defines an automorphism `theta` of `St_3(R)` with
`theta(x_ij(r))=x_(tau i)(tau j)(r)`.  Put
`v=theta(w)=x_13(1)x_31(-1)x_13(1)` and `h=v^2=theta(w^2)`.  Applying `theta`
to Step 1, `h` sends `x_12(r),x_32(r),x_21(r),x_23(r)` to the same roots with
`-r`.  In particular

```text
h w h^-1 = x_12(-1) x_21(1) x_12(-1) = w^-1.
```

Hence `h z h^-1 = z^-1`.  Since `z` is central, `z=z^-1`, so `z^2=1` and

```text
w^8 = 1.                                                          (W8)
```

## Step 3: one trivial Weyl word kills everything

Let `f:St_3(R)->H` be a homomorphism with `f(w)=1`.  By (P2),
`f(x_13(r))=f(x_23(r))`.  By (S2), `x_13(r)=[x_12(1),x_23(r)]`, so

```text
f(x_13(r)) = [f(x_12(1)), f(x_23(r))] = [f(x_12(1)), f(x_13(r))] = 1,
```

because `x_12` and `x_13` commute (S3).  Thus `f` kills `x_13(R)` and
`x_23(R)`.  Then (S2) gives, in order,

```text
x_21(r)=[x_23(r),x_31(1)],   x_12(r)=[x_13(r),x_32(1)],
x_31(r)=[x_32(r),x_21(1)],   x_32(r)=[x_31(r),x_12(1)],
```

each a commutator with one factor already killed.  So `f` is trivial.

If `H` is torsion-free, (W8) forces `f(w)=1`, and `f` is trivial.

## Step 4: `n>=3`

For distinct `i,j` choose `k` outside `{i,j}`.  The assignment
`x_ab(r) |-> x_(sigma a)(sigma b)(r)` with `sigma(1,2,3)=(i,j,k)` sends
relations of `St_3(R)` to relations of `St_n(R)`, hence is a homomorphism
`St_3(R)->St_n(R)` carrying `w` to `w_ij`.  Therefore `w_ij^8=1`.

Let `f:St_n(R)->H` satisfy `f(w_12)=1`.  For each `k>=3`, the composite of
`f` with the copy on indices `(1,2,k)` sends `w` to `f(w_12)=1`, so it is
trivial by Step 3.  Hence `f` kills `x_ab(R)` for all distinct
`a,b in {1,2,k}`, for every `k>=3`.  For distinct `i,j` outside `{1,2}`,
(S2) gives `x_ij(r)=[x_i1(r),x_1j(1)]`, a commutator of killed elements.  So
`f` is trivial.  If `H` is torsion-free, (W8) for `w_12` forces `f(w_12)=1`.
Since `EL_n(R)` is the quotient of `St_n(R)` by `x_ij(r)|->e_ij(r)`, and a
quotient of a group all of whose torsion-free images are trivial has the same
property, (STF1) is proved.

## Proof of (STF2)

1. For matrices over `Z+I`, `e_ij(a)e_ij(b)=e_ij(a+b)`, so
   `e_ij(a)^m=e_ij(ma)=1`.
2. In the `(1,2)` block,
   `e_12(e)e_21(-e)=[[1-e,e],[-e,1]]` and multiplying by `e_12(e)` gives
   `u=[[1-e,e],[-e,1-e]]` because `e(1-e)=0`.  Squaring,
   `u^2=[[(1-e)^2-e^2, 2e(1-e)],[-2e(1-e), (1-e)^2-e^2]]=(1-2e)I_2`, and
   `(1-2e)^2=1-4e+4e^2=1`, so `u^4=1`.  If `2e!=0` then `u^2!=1`, so `u` is a
   nontrivial torsion element.  If `2e=0` then `e_12(e)` has order `2` by
   item 1, and is nontrivial because `e!=0`.  Either way `EL_n(I)` has
   nontrivial torsion.
3. `eIe` is a unital ring with unit `e`.  Matrices over any rng satisfy
   (S1)--(S3): e.g. `(1+rE_ij)(1+sE_jk)(1-rE_ij)(1-sE_jk)=1+rsE_ik`.  So
   `x_ab(r)|->e_(sigma a)(sigma b)(r)` defines a homomorphism
   `St_3(eIe)->EL_n(I)` for every injective `sigma`.  By (STF1) its composite
   with `f` is trivial, so `f(e_ij(e))=1` for all `i!=j`.  Then, for `n>=3`
   and `k` outside `{i,j}`,
   `e_ij(ea)=[e_ik(e),e_kj(a)]`, `e_ij(ae)=[e_ik(a),e_kj(e)]`,
   `e_ij(aeb)=[e_ik(ae),e_kj(b)]` and `e_ij(me)=e_ij(e)^m` are killed, and by
   (S1) so is `e_ij(a)` for every `a` in `Ze+eI+Ie+IeI`, the ideal generated by
   `e`.

## Proof of (STF3)

*Finite generation of `I`.*  Let `g_1,...,g_s` generate `EL_3(I)` and let
`I_0` be the subring generated by the entries of all `g_i-1` and
`g_i^-1-1`, which lie in `I`.  Since `(1+A)(1+B)=1+(A+B+AB)`, every group
element is `1+A` with `A in M_3(I_0)`.  As `e_ij(b)` lies in the group for every
`b in I`, `b in I_0`.  So `I=I_0` is finitely generated.

*`I=I^2`.*  The map `1+A |-> A mod M_3(I^2)` is a homomorphism
`EL_3(I)->(M_3(I/I^2),+)` by the same product formula, and its image contains
`aE_ij` for all `a in I`, `i!=j`.  The image is a finitely generated abelian
group, nonzero unless `I=I^2`, and a nonzero finitely generated abelian group
has a nontrivial finite quotient.

*Additive torsion and idempotents* follow from (STF2).1 and (STF2).2.

*Noncommutativity.*  Suppose `I!=0` is commutative with `I=I^2`, generated as
a ring by `a_1,...,a_k`.  With `A=Z+I` (commutative), every monomial in the
`a_l` lies in some `A a_l`, so `I=sum A a_l`.  Since `I=I*I=sum_l I a_l`,
write `a_k=sum_l b_kl a_l` with `b_kl in I`.  Then `(1-B)a=0` for the column
`a=(a_l)` and the matrix `B=(b_kl)` over `A`; multiplying by the adjugate,
`det(1-B)a_l=0` for all `l`.  Expanding, `det(1-B)=1-e` with `e in I`.  Thus
`(1-e)I=0`, i.e. `ex=x` for all `x in I`; in particular `e^2=e`, and `e!=0`
because `I!=0`.  This contradicts idempotent-freeness.

## Proof of (STF4)

1. `Gamma` is generated by conjugates of images of groups `St_n(R)`.  A
   homomorphism to a torsion-free group kills each such image by (STF1),
   hence kills `Gamma`.  A subgroup of a torsion-free group that is an image of
   `Gamma` is a torsion-free image of `Gamma`, hence trivial.
2. If `M<=Gamma` has finite index, its normal core `N` has finite index and
   `Gamma/N` is a finite quotient, hence trivial; so `M=Gamma`.  The only
   finite-index subgroup is `Gamma`, and `Gamma` is torsion-free only if the
   identity map is a trivial homomorphism, i.e. `Gamma=1`.
3. `iota(Gamma)` is a torsion-free image of `Gamma`, so `iota` is trivial, the
   transported root `d` commutes with `iota(gamma)=1`, every pointwise defect
   `[d,iota(gamma)]` is `1`, and the normal closure of the defect set is
   trivial.

## The parabolic `H(R)` is normally elementary

For `h in EL_3(R)`, row vectors `u`, column vectors `v`, and `s in R`, write
`g(h)=diag(1,h,1)`, `a(u)`, `b(v)`, `c(s)` for the matrices with the displayed
single block.  Every element of `H(R)` is `a(u)b(v)c(s)g(h)`: this product is
`[[1,uh,uv+s],[0,h,v],[0,0,1]]`.  Direct multiplication gives

```text
[g(h),a(u)] = a(u(h^-1 - 1)),   [g(h),b(v)] = b((h-1)v),   [a(u),b(v)] = c(uv).
```

With `h^-1=1+rE_kl` and `u=e_k^T` the first is `a(r e_l^T)`; with
`h=1+rE_lk` and `v=e_k` the second is `b(r e_l)`; and
`c(r)=[a(e_1^T),b(r e_1)]`.  So the normal closure of `g(EL_3(R))` contains all
generators, and `H(R)` and each of its quotients are normally elementary.
