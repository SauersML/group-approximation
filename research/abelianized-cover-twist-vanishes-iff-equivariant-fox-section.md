**ESTABLISHED.**

## Setting

- `F` is free on `x_1,...,x_n`, `R` is normal in `F`, `G = F/R`,
  `E = F/[R,R]` and `M = R/[R,R]`, written additively, with `G` acting by
  conjugation.
- **Fox coordinates.**  `Fox : M -> ZG^n`, `r |-> (bar(dr/dx_i))_i`, where
  `bar : ZF -> ZG` is induced by `F -> G`.  This is an injective map of left
  `G`-modules (Lyndon; the relation sequence `0 -> M -> ZG^n -> I_G -> 0`).
  Additivity holds because `d(rr')/dx_i = dr/dx_i + r dr'/dx_i` and
  `bar(r) = 1`.  Equivariance follows from
  `d(w r w^-1)/dx_i = dw/dx_i + w dr/dx_i - w r w^-1 dw/dx_i`, whose image
  in `ZG` is `bar(w) bar(dr/dx_i)`.
- **Dual Fox projection.**  Put `Omega = Hom(ZG^n, T) = (T^n)^G`, the
  Bernoulli shift with the product topology and `(g.chi)(a) = chi(g^-1 a)`.
  Put `M^ = Hom(M, T)`.  The restriction
  `pi = Fox^* : Omega -> M^` is a continuous, `G`-equivariant, surjective
  homomorphism of compact abelian groups.  It is surjective because `T` is
  divisible.
- **A model.**  `rho : E -> U(A)` is a unitary representation in a unital
  C*-algebra.  The two cases of interest are a norm matrix corona and
  `B(l^2 E)`.  Let `B = C*(rho(M)) = C(X)`, a commutative algebra.
- **Joint spectrum.**  The map `j : X -> M^`, `j(x)(m) = rho(m)(x)`, is a
  continuous injection, since `B` is generated by `rho(M)`.  It is
  `G`-equivariant for the action `g.b = rho(gt) b rho(gt)^*` on `B`.
  Identify `X` with the closed `G`-invariant set `j(X)`.
- **Pushed class.**  `phi = rho|_M : M -> U(B) = C(X,T)` is `G`-equivariant.
  Let `xi in H^2(G; M)` be the class of `1 -> M -> E -> G -> 1`, and let
  `phi_* xi in H^2(G; C(X,T))` be the pushed class.  This is the class
  `rho_* xi` of the pushout
  `E'_phi = (C(X,T) x| E)/{(phi(m)^-1, m)}`.

## Statement

**(FS1)  Criterion.**  The following are equivalent:

1. `phi_* xi = 0`, that is, `E'_phi -> G` splits;
2. `phi` extends to a `G`-equivariant homomorphism `Phi : ZG^n -> C(X,T)`
   with `Phi o Fox = phi`;
3. the dual Fox projection has a continuous `G`-equivariant section over the
   joint spectrum, i.e. there is a continuous equivariant `s : X -> Omega`
   with `pi o s = id_X`.

Concretely, item 2 asks for `n` unitaries `f_1,...,f_n in U(B)` such that,
for every relator `r` in a normal generating set of `R`,

```text
rho(r)  =  prod_i prod_(g in G) (g . f_i)^(a_(i,g)),     dr/dx_i |-> sum_g a_(i,g) g.   (FS2)
```

It is enough to check `(FS2)` on normal generators of `R`, because both
sides are equivariant homomorphisms on `M`.

**(FS3)  Consequence for full-radical bases.**  Suppose `Rad_MF(G) = G`,
`A` is a norm matrix corona, and the joint spectrum of `rho` has an
equivariant section as in item 3.  Then `rho([E,M]) = 1`.  This combines
(FS1) with the splitting argument of
`abelianized-cover-injective-corona-model-carries-nonzero-twist`: a section
`sigma : G -> E'_phi` composed with `Psi(b,e) = b rho(e)` is a homomorphism
`G -> U(A)`, hence trivial, so each `rho(gt)` lies in `U(B)` and commutes
with `rho(M)`.  Hence the joint spectrum of any corona model of `E` that
does not kill `[E,M]` carries no equivariant continuous section of `pi`.

**(FS4)  Faithful calibration.**  Suppose `G` has a nontrivial torsion
element and `n >= 1`.  For the left regular representation
`lambda : E -> U(B(l^2 E))`:

- `lambda` is faithful;
- `C*(lambda(M)) = C(M^)`, so its joint spectrum is all of `M^`;
- the `G`-action on `M^` is nontrivial when `G` is infinite;
- `pi` has **no** continuous equivariant section over `M^`, so the pushed
  class is nonzero.

So an injective model with nonzero twist, whose joint spectrum is the whole
dual group, is realized by an honest unitary representation.

## Proof of (FS1)

**(2) <=> (3).**  An equivariant homomorphism `Phi : ZG^n -> C(X,T)` is the
same datum as a continuous equivariant map `s : X -> Omega`, via
`s(x)(a) = Phi(a)(x)`.
- `s` is continuous because each coordinate `x |-> Phi(a)(x)` is.
- `s` is equivariant because
  `s(gx)(a) = Phi(a)(gx) = (g^-1 . Phi(a))(x) = Phi(g^-1 a)(x) = (g.s(x))(a)`.
- Conversely, any continuous equivariant `s` defines such a `Phi`.

Under this dictionary, `Phi o Fox = phi` says `s(x)|_M = j(x)`, that is,
`pi o s = id_X`.

**(2) => (1).**
- Define a crossed homomorphism `c : F -> C(X,T)` (written additively) by
  `c(x_i) = f_i := Phi(e_i)`.  Free groups admit crossed homomorphisms with
  arbitrary values on a basis.
- By the fundamental formula of Fox calculus,
  `c(w) = sum_i bar(dw/dx_i) . f_i = Phi(Fox(w))` for `w in R`.  So on `R`,
  `c` equals `phi o (R -> M)`.
- `R` acts trivially on `C(X,T)`, so `c|_R` is a homomorphism into an abelian
  group.  It therefore vanishes on `[R,R]`, and the crossed homomorphism
  identity lets `c` descend to `c : E -> C(X,T)` with `c|_M = phi`.
- Let `iota : E -> E'_phi`, `e |-> [(1,e)]`.  Then
  `sigma(bar e) = [(-c(e), e)]` is well defined: for `m in M`,
  `[(-phi(m), m)]` is the identity in `E'_phi`.
- `sigma` is a homomorphism by the crossed homomorphism identity, and it is a
  section of `E'_phi -> G`.

**(1) => (2).**
- Let `sigma : G -> E'_phi` be a homomorphic section.  Every element of
  `E'_phi` has the form `[(b,1)] sigma(g)` for unique `b` and `g`, so define
  `c : E -> C(X,T)` by `iota(e) = [(c(e),1)] sigma(bar e)`.
- From `iota(ee') = iota(e) iota(e')`, and since conjugation by `sigma(bar e)`
  acts on `C(X,T)` through `bar e`, we get `c(ee') = c(e) + bar e . c(e')`.
- For `m in M`, `iota(m) = [(phi(m),1)]` and `sigma(1) = 1`, so
  `c|_M = phi`.
- Pull `c` back to `F`.  By the fundamental formula, for `w in R`,
  `phi(bar w) = c(w) = sum_i bar(dw/dx_i) . c(x_i)`.
- Put `Phi(e_i) = c(x_i)`, extended `G`-linearly.  Then `Phi o Fox = phi`.

The implications (1) <=> (2) use nothing about `C(X,T)`.  They hold verbatim
for any `G`-module `A` and any equivariant `phi : M -> A`, with `E'_phi` the
pushout.

## Proof of (FS4)

- **Faithful.**  The regular representation is faithful.
- **Joint spectrum.**  Restricted to `M`, `lambda` is a multiple of the
  regular representation of `M`: decompose `l^2 E` over the cosets `Me`.
  `M` is abelian, so `C*(lambda(M)) = C*_r(M) = C(M^)`, with `lambda(m)`
  corresponding to the evaluation character `ev_m`.  So `j(X) = M^`.
  Conjugation by `lambda(gt)` induces the dual of the module automorphism of
  `M`.
- **Action.**  When `G` is infinite, the action on `M^` is nontrivial.  Here
  `M != 0`, because `G` has torsion and so is not free, and `M^G = 0`,
  because `M <= ZG^n` and `(ZG^n)^G = 0`.  So `G` acts nontrivially on `M`,
  hence on `M^`.  For finite `G` this bullet can fail (`F = Z`, `G = Z/k`),
  and it is not used below.
- **Degree retraction.**  `R` is a subgroup of a free group, so it is free,
  and `M = R^ab` is free abelian of countable rank `J`.  Then `M^ = T^J` is
  compact and connected, and
  - `[M^, T] = H^1_Cech(M^; Z)` (Bruschlinsky);
  - `H^1_Cech(T^J; Z)` is the colimit of `H^1(T^K; Z) = Z^K` over finite
    `K <= J` (continuity of Cech cohomology for inverse limits), which is
    `(+)_J Z`, and it is realized exactly by the characters.

  So every `f in C(M^, T)` is uniquely `f = chi exp(ih)` with `chi` a
  character and `h in C(M^, R)`.  A nontrivial character is not
  null-homotopic, since its Cech class is nonzero.  This defines a group
  homomorphism `deg : C(M^,T) -> M`, `f |-> chi`, that is the identity on
  characters.  It is `G`-equivariant, because homeomorphisms induced by
  group automorphisms carry characters to characters and null-homotopic
  maps to null-homotopic maps.
- **No section.**  Suppose `s : M^ -> Omega` were a continuous equivariant
  section.
  - By (FS1), `ev` extends to `Phi : ZG^n -> C(M^,T)`.
  - Then `deg o Phi : ZG^n -> M` is a `G`-map with `deg o Phi o Fox = id_M`.
  - By the module-coefficient form of (2) => (1), with `A = M` and
    `phi = id_M`, there is a crossed homomorphism `c : E -> M` with
    `c|_M = id`.
  - So `bar e |-> c(e)^-1 e` is a well-defined homomorphic section
    `G -> E`.  Indeed `c(m)^-1 m = 1`, and
    `c(e)^-1 e c(e')^-1 e' = c(e)^-1 (bar e . c(e'))^-1 e e' = c(ee')^-1 e e'`.
  - This embeds `G` in `E`.  `E` is torsion-free by
    `free-abelianized-cover-is-torsion-free`, and `G` has a nontrivial torsion
    element, a contradiction.
- **Nonzero class.**  So there is no section, and by (FS1) the pushed class
  of `lambda` is nonzero.  In fact `C(M^,T) = M (+) U_0(C(M^))` as
  `G`-modules, and the class is `(xi, 0)`.

## What this kills, and where it stops

**Named invariant.**  The obstruction is the equivariant lifting problem of
the dual Fox projection `pi : (T^n)^G -> M^` over the joint spectrum
`X <= M^` of a model.  Every corona model of `E` has such an `X`: a closed
`G`-invariant subset of the compact group `M^`.  By (FS3), `(DF)` of
`full-mf-radical-abelianized-cover-is-not-mf` follows from the purely
dynamical statement

```text
(Sec)  for every norm-matrix-corona model of E, pi admits a continuous
       G-equivariant section over the joint spectrum X.
```

`(Sec)` can fail on its own.  It is sufficient for `(DF)`, not equivalent to
it.

**Class killed.**  Consider any argument for `(Sec)`, or for vanishing of
the twist, whose only inputs are:
- the compact `G`-space `X` with its embedding in `M^`;
- faithfulness of the model;
- commutativity of `rho(M)`;
- implementation of the `G`-action by unitaries in a unital C*-algebra.

Every such argument dies at (FS4).  The regular representation satisfies all
of these inputs, has `X = M^`, and admits no section.  A proof of `(DF)` must
therefore use a feature of norm matrix coronas that `B(l^2 E)` lacks, namely
finite-dimensional lifts, beyond the full MF radical of `G`.  That radical
enters (FS3) only through the splitting step, which needs the section
first.

**Positive boundary cases.**
- **Fixed points.**  Over a `G`-fixed point `chi in M^G = Hom(M_G, T)`, a
  section exists if and only if `chi` factors through the exponent-sum map
  `M_G = R/[F,R] -> Z^n`.  The constant elements `T^n <= Omega` are the only
  fixed points, and `pi(c)(m) = prod_i c_i^(eps(dm/dx_i))`.  The kernel of
  that map is `H_2(G)`.  So for a superperfect base such as `St_20` of a
  Leavitt ring, every fixed point lifts.  This recovers `H^2(G;T) = 0`.
- **Where it stops.**  Nothing here bounds the joint spectra that norm matrix
  corona models of `E` can produce.  `(Sec)` remains open, and (FS1) makes
  no claim that injective corona models exist or do not exist.
