# Proof

Notation is as in the target. Let `rho` be the representation of `J` on
`V = F_2^(N)` given by `S e_i = e_{i+1}`, `T e_i = e_{i-1}` and
`T e_0 = 0`. Two facts about it are used below.

1. `rho` is faithful. The normal forms `S^a T^b` send `e_b` to `e_a`
   and kill `e_i` for `i < b`, so they are linearly independent.
2. `rho(S^a Q T^b)` is the matrix unit `E_{a,b}`, because `rho(Q)` is
   the projection onto `e_0`. So `rho(I)` is the algebra of finite
   matrices, and `J/I = F_2[z,z^-1]` with `S -> z` and `T -> z^-1`.
   This is standard, and the script checks it on windows.

## Step 1. The symbol kernel is locally finite

Let `G_I = ker(GL_n(J) -> GL_n(J/I)) = {1 + X invertible : X in M_n(I)}`.
Let `g_1, ..., g_k` be elements of `G_I`, with `g_j = 1 + X_j`. Under
`rho^n` on `V^n`, each `X_j` is a finite matrix. So there is an `M` such
that every `X_j` maps `V^n` into
`W = span{e_i^(c) : i <= M, c <= n}` and kills every basis vector
`e_i^(c)` with `i > M`.

Write `V^n = W (+) W'`, where `W'` is spanned by the remaining basis
vectors. Each `g_j` then has the block form `[[A_j, 0], [0, 1]]`, and
`A_j` is invertible on `W` because `g_j` is invertible. Since `rho` is
faithful, `<g_1, ..., g_k>` embeds in `GL(W)`, which is finite (of
order at most `|GL_{n(M+1)}(F_2)|`).

So every finitely generated subgroup of `G_I` is finite. Hence `G_I`,
and every subgroup of it, is locally finite and amenable. In particular
`N_n = EL_n(J) cap G_I` is locally finite.

## Step 2. Theorem A

Let `H <= GL_n(J)` be countable, with `sigma(H)` amenable. Then
`H cap G_I` is the kernel of `sigma|_H`, and it is amenable by Step 1.
An extension of an amenable group by an amenable group is amenable, so
`H` is amenable.

By [[amenable-implies-operator-mf]] (Tikuisis–White–Winter; also
Schafhauser, arXiv:2306.02498, Theorem 2.3), `H` has an injective
norm-corona representation `pi : H -> U(prod M_{k_m} / (+) M_{k_m})`.

Let `h in H` satisfy `h^2 = 1` and `h != 1`. Then `pi(h)` is a
self-adjoint unitary other than `1`, so its spectrum contains `-1` and
`||pi(h) - 1|| = 2`.

The head `x_13(Q)` is an involution in characteristic 2, since
`x_13(Q)^2 = x_13(2Q) = 1`. It is nontrivial because `Q != 0`.

## Step 3. Corollary A1

`H0` is generated by `U = M_2(J)` (the upper-right block of `EL_4(J)`),
`g = diag([[S,Q],[0,T]], 1_2)`, `e = x_12(1)`, `a = x_34(1)` and
`b = x_43(S)`. Here `g^-1 = diag([[T,0],[Q,S]], 1_2)`.

The Levi part `<g, e>` acts on `U` on the left and `<a, b>` acts on the
right. These two parts commute and both normalize `U`, so
`H0 = U semidirect (<g,e> x <a,b>)`.

The symbol images are as follows.

* `sigma(U) = M_2(F_2[z,z^-1])`, which is abelian.
* `sigma(g) = diag(z, z^-1, 1, 1)` and `sigma(e) = x_12(1)`. Both are
  upper triangular, so they lie in the metabelian Borel subgroup of
  `SL_2(F_2[z,z^-1])` (left block).
* `sigma(a)` and `sigma(b)` are involutions. A group generated by two
  involutions is a quotient of `Z/2 * Z/2 = D_inf`, which is virtually
  cyclic.

So `sigma(H0)` lies in `M_2 semidirect (solvable x virtually cyclic)`
and is amenable. By Theorem A, `H0` is amenable and has a faithful MF
model in which the head sits at distance 2.

The product `sigma(ab)` has symbol block `[[1+z, 1], [z, 1]]` of trace
`z`, so `<a,b>` really is `D_inf`. This is not needed for the argument.

**The pigeonhole runs inside `H0`.** Let
`A = {x_13(r_3) x_14(r_4) : r_3, r_4 in F_2[S]}`. This is an abelian
subgroup of `U`, and it contains `u = x_13(1)`. (The w12 bus certificate,
p22-double-parabolic-is-not-lef, obtains `A` as the group generated by
the `<a,b>`-conjugates of `u`. That description is not needed here.) Checked by hand and in the script:

* `g x_1c(r) g^-1 = x_1c(S r)` for `c = 3, 4`, so `g A g^-1 <= A`;
* `e` commutes with every `x_1c(r)`;
* `g^-1 u g = x_13(T) x_23(Q)`;
* `[e, g^-1 u g] = x_13(Q)`.

Let `phi : H0 -> F` be a homomorphism to a finite group. Then
`phi(g) phi(A) phi(g)^-1 <= phi(A)`, and conjugation is injective, so
this is an equality. Hence `phi(g^-1 u g)` lies in
`phi(g)^-1 phi(A) phi(g) = phi(A)`. Since `phi(e)` centralizes
`phi(A)`, `phi(x_13(Q)) = [phi(e), phi(g^-1 u g)] = 1`.

So `H0` is amenable and not residually finite, and it keeps the head in
some MF model but in no finite quotient. The pigeonhole relations hold in
`H0`, so they alone cannot imply norm-corona head collapse.

## Step 4. Theorem B

Let `B_n^+` and `B_n^-` be the preimages in `EL_n(J)` of the upper and
lower triangular matrices of `SL_n(F_2[z,z^-1])`.

* Their symbol images are triangular groups over a commutative ring,
  which are solvable. By Step 1 their kernels are locally finite. So
  `B_n^+` and `B_n^-` are countable and amenable.
* `N_n` is normal in `EL_n(J)`, hence in both `B_n^+` and `B_n^-`, and
  `N_n` lies in their intersection.

Schafhauser, *Finite dimensional approximations of certain amalgamated
free products of groups* (arXiv:2306.02498; Groups Geom. Dyn. 20 (2026)),
Theorem 1.3, verbatim:

> "If G and H are amenable groups with a common normal (necessarily
> amenable) subgroup N, then G *_N H is MF."

His abstract, verbatim: "This paper provides a new class of MF groups by
showing that given two amenable groups with a common normal subgroup,
the amalgamated free product is MF."

The paper's MF notion (Definition 1.1) has three conditions:

* approximate multiplicativity in operator norm;
* `|tr(u_g)| < eps` for `g != 1`;
* approximate regular norms.

This is stronger than the repository's operator-MF convention. As
recorded in `amenable-implies-operator-mf-citation`, forgetting the extra
conditions gives an injective norm-corona representation.

Hence `Lambda_n = B_n^+ *_{N_n} B_n^-` is MF. The factors embed in the
amalgam, so the head (an element of `N_n`, since `sigma(Q) = 0`) is a
nontrivial involution of `Lambda_n`. By the Step 2 argument it sits at
distance 2 in a faithful model.

Every `x_ij(r)` with `i < j` lies in `B_n^+`, and every one with `i > j`
lies in `B_n^-`. So the natural map `Lambda_n -> EL_n(J)` is onto.

**The P22 version.** Let `B^+` and `B^-` be the Borel preimages in
`EL_2(J)` and `N_2` the symbol kernel there. Let
`A_+ = U semidirect (B^+ x B^+)` and `A_- = U semidirect (B^- x B^-)`,
inside `P22(J)` acting by `X -> L X R^-1`.

* Their symbol images are block triangular, inside the triangular Borel
  subgroups of `SL_4(F_2[z,z^-1])`. So `A_+` and `A_-` are amenable by
  Theorem A.
* `C0 = U semidirect (N_2 x N_2)` is normal in both, because `U` is
  normal and `N_2 x N_2` is normal in `B^+- x B^+-`.
* `B^+ x B^+` contains `B^+ x 1` and `1 x B^+`, and `<B^+, B^-> = EL_2(J)`.
  So `A_+` and `A_-` generate `P22(J)`.

By Schafhauser Theorem 1.3, `A_+ *_{C0} A_-` is MF, keeps the head
(`x_13(Q)` lies in `U`, which is contained in `C0`) and surjects onto
`P22(J)`.

## Step 5. Scope

Let `Gamma` be a group containing the head, and `P` a proof that
`pi(x_13(Q)) = 1` for every norm-corona representation `pi` of `Gamma`.

If `P` only uses that `pi` restricts to a homomorphism on a subgroup `H`
containing the head, with `sigma(H)` amenable, then `P` would prove the
same statement for `H`. That contradicts Theorem A.

If `P` only uses that `pi` is a homomorphism on `B^+` and on `B^-` that
agree on `N_n`, then `P` would prove the statement for `Lambda_n`. That
contradicts Theorem B.

This is stated at the EL level. HC implies its restriction HC_EL to
representations that factor through `EL_5(J)`, and for those
representations every relation of `B_5^+` and of `B_5^-` holds.

So any proof of HC, read on the representations of HC_EL, must use an
element of `ker(Lambda_5 -> EL_5(J))`, that is, a relation that is not a
consequence of the relations of the two Borel preimages glued along
`N_5`. In P22-local form, the relation must lie in
`ker(A_+ *_{C0} A_- -> P22(J))`.

Examples of relations that do mix the two Borels:

* the Steinberg relations `[x_ij(r), x_jk(s)] = x_ik(rs)` with `i < j`
  and `j > k`, whose two factors lie in opposite Borels;
* the Weyl element relations;
* relations inside `EL_2(J)` between `x_12(J)` and `x_21(J)`.

The statement is not lifted to `St_5(J)`. Doing so would need
amenability of the Borel preimages in `St_5(J)`, which involves
`K_2(5,J)`. That was not checked.

Nothing here shows that `P22(J)` or `EL_5(J)` is MF, because quotients of
MF groups need not be MF. So HC stays OPEN.

## Script

`python3 experiments/jacobson-amenable-symbol-kill-2026-09-17/check_amenable_symbol.py`
works with exact normal-form arithmetic in `J`. It checks:

* the ring rules and the symbol homomorphism;
* the certificate identities in Step 3;
* the triangular symbols and `D_inf` of Step 3;
* that random 4-generated subgroups of `E_2(J,I)` supported on a
  two-point window close up as subgroups of `GL_4(F_2)`, with orders
  12, 4, 192, 576, 36 and 48;
* the numerical `Gamma_BS` toy.
