**ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** Let `q` be a prime power and `A = SL_3(F_q[t,1/t])`. Let `Δ <= A`
contain a principal congruence subgroup `A(f)`. Under the congruence subgroup property for
`SL_3(F_q[t,1/t])` (Bass--Milnor--Serre; import, not re-read) this is every finite-index subgroup.

1. `H^1(Δ;Q) = H^2(Δ;Q) = H^3(Δ;Q) = 0`, `H_2(Δ;Z)` is a torsion group, and `H^2(Δ;Z)` is finite.
2. **Central covers.** Let `Δ~` be a Kazhdan group with a central subgroup `N` and
   `Δ~/N ≅ Δ`. Then `N` is a torsion group. If `Δ~` is finitely generated and `Δ` is finitely
   presented (hypothesis (FP) below), `N` is finite.
3. **Rounding.** Assume (FP).
   - Every sequence of models `ρ_k∘s` pulled back from a central Kazhdan cover as in
     `kazhdan-cover-models-round-iff-kernel-fixed-mass-one` rounds strictly.
   - Every asymptotically projective model `α_n(g)α_n(h) = c_n(g,h)α_n(gh)`, `c_n -> 1`
     pointwise, rounds strictly in operator norm (`finite-multiplier-projective-models-round-strictly`).
   - Dogon's Theorem 1.3 has no input at `Δ`: every central extension of `Δ` by a torsion-free
     abelian group has infinite abelianization, so it is not Kazhdan.

**Hypothesis (FP).** `A` is finitely presented, hence so is `Δ`. This is Behr's theorem on
finite presentation of `S`-arithmetic groups over function fields, where the local ranks of `SL_3`
at the two places add to `4`. It is also the case `m = 4` of the Bux--Köhl--Witzel rank theorem,
arXiv:1102.0428 (Ann. of Math. 177 (2013)). Its abstract, read on 2026-09-13, says: "the
finiteness length of an S-arithmetic subgroup Γ in a noncommutative isotropic absolutely almost
simple group G over a global function field is one less than the sum of the local ranks of G
taken over the places in S". For `SL_3` and `S = {0,∞}` the sum is `4`, so `A` is of type `F_3`,
hence finitely presented. Only the abstract was read; the numbered theorem was not.

**Reading for `sl3-laurent-f2-is-flexibly-hs-stable`.**
- **The one-place contrast.** In `SL_3(F_q[t])` the congruence subgroups have thousands of real
  degree-two classes, and the instability route
  `sl3-polynomial-finite-index-z-extension-center-survives` stays open there. Adding the second
  place kills all of them (`laurent-sl3-real-cohomology-from-chamber-quotient`, item 3).
- **What instability of `SL_3(F_2[t,1/t])` would need.** Descent to finite index
  (`flexible-hs-stability-descends-to-retracts-finite-index`) lets instability come from any
  finite-index subgroup. By this claim it cannot come from a scalar cocycle, and it cannot come
  from a central Kazhdan cover. A witness needs genuinely non-scalar defects: a non-central
  Kazhdan cover whose kernel has almost-trivial representations without fixed vectors, or a model
  pulled back from no Kazhdan cover at all.
- **Non-central kernels generated by relative-(T) elements are also void.** Take a ring `R`
  mapping onto `F_q[t,1/t]` with kernel `I`, and the cover `EL_3(R) -> A`. Its kernel is normally
  generated by elementary matrices `e_ij(x)`, `x ∈ I`, which sit inside `R^2 ⋊ EL_2(R)`. There
  the trace of a model gives an `EL_2(R)`-invariant spectral measure concentrating at the trivial
  character, and relative property (T) forces fixed mass 1. This bullet is a sketch, not part of
  the established statement.

**Model tests.**
- *A group failing the hypothesis.* `Z^(2g) ⋊ Sp_(2g)(Z)` has a non-torsion `H^2` class, and there
  the conclusion fails (`rf-kazhdan-group-not-flexibly-hs-stable`).
- *A group satisfying it.* `A(t+1)` is a congruence subgroup; items 1--3 apply.
