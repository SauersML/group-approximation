Full proof: artifact Section 4, Corollary 2. It uses Lemmas C1, C2 and C4 of part 3,
which are integral exact-sequence arguments.
- C1 is restriction to a closed set, with kernel `C_c` of the complement.
- C2 is the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for a union of two closed sets.

Strong induction on `j >= 1` shows that `C(C^j, Z)` is V-acyclic.

1. **`j = 1`.** `Conf_1(C) = C`, so `C(C, Z) = C_c(Conf_1(C), Z)`. It is V-acyclic by
   `cantor-configuration-function-modules-are-v-acyclic`.
2. **Strata of the fat diagonal.** Let `j >= 2`. For a nonempty set `E` of pairs
   `{a, b}`, put `Δ_E = {x : x_a = x_b for all {a, b} ∈ E}`.
   - `Δ_E` is V-equivariantly homeomorphic to `C^i`, where `i` is the number of
     classes of the equivalence relation generated by `E`.
   - Since `E ≠ ∅`, `i < j`, so `C(Δ_E, Z)` is V-acyclic by induction.
3. **The fat diagonal `D = ∪ Δ_{ab}`.** Induct on the number `k` of sets in a union
   `Y = Δ_{E_1} ∪ ... ∪ Δ_{E_k}` with every `E_i ≠ ∅` (C4).
   - Write `Y = Y' ∪ Δ_{E_k}`. Then `Y' ∩ Δ_{E_k} = ∪_{i<k} Δ_{E_i ∪ E_k}` is a union
     of `k − 1` such sets, again with nonempty `E`.
   - Apply the long exact homology sequence of C2 to
     `0 → C(Y) → C(Y') ⊕ C(Δ_{E_k}) → C(Y' ∩ Δ_{E_k}) → 0`.
   - So `C(D, Z)` is V-acyclic.
4. **Assembly.** C1 gives the short exact sequence of V-modules
   `0 → C_c(Conf_j(C), Z) → C(C^j, Z) → C(D, Z) → 0`.
   - The outer terms are V-acyclic, by the required claim and by step 3.
   - The long exact sequence, including its right-exact end in degree 0, makes
     `C(C^j, Z)` V-acyclic. ∎
