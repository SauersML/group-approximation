Throughout, `S` is a nontrivial finitely presented simple group.

**Pair-orbits of a transitive action.** Let `S` act transitively on a set `O`,
fix `ω ∈ O`, and put `H = Stab_S(ω)`, so that `O ≅ S/H`.

- Every ordered pair `(xH, yH)` of distinct points is moved by `x^-1` to
  `(H, x^-1 y H)`, and `x^-1 y ∉ H`.
- Two pairs `(H, aH)` and `(H, bH)` lie in one orbit iff some `s` satisfies
  `sH = H` and `saH = bH`. That means `s ∈ H` and `bH ⊆ HaH`, i.e. `HaH = HbH`.

So the orbits of ordered pairs of distinct points are in bijection with the
double cosets `HaH ≠ H`. Each orbit of two-element subsets is the image of one or
two orbits of ordered pairs, so there are at most `|H \ S / H| - 1` orbits of
two-element subsets. Conversely, each ordered-pair orbit maps onto one
subset-orbit, and each subset-orbit has at most two ordered preimages. Hence
`|H \ S / H| < infinity` iff there are finitely many orbits of two-element
subsets of `O`.

**(2) ⟹ (1).** Let `H < S` be proper, finitely generated, with finitely many
double cosets, and let `S` act on `S/H` by left multiplication.

- *Faithful.* The kernel is `⋂_g gHg^-1`, a normal subgroup of `S` contained in
  `H ≠ S`, so it is trivial by simplicity.
- *Finitely presented.* By hypothesis.
- *Finitely generated stabilizers.* `Stab(gH) = gHg^-1`.
- *Finitely many orbits of two-element subsets.* By the count above.

**(1) ⟹ (2).** Let `S` act on `Ω` with type (A). Since the action is faithful
and `S ≠ 1`, some orbit `O = Sω` has `|O| ≥ 2`. Put `H = Stab_S(ω)`.

- `H` is finitely generated by clause 3.
- `H` is proper, because `|O| ≥ 2`.
- Two-element subsets of `O` form finitely many orbits, since there are finitely
  many in `Ω` (clause 4). By the count, `|H \ S / H| < infinity`.

**Consequences.** Under (1), `S` embeds (identically) in a group admitting an
action of type (A), which is clause (i) of BFFHZ Theorem C. `S` has solvable word
problem by `simple-envelope-forces-solvable-word-problem`, so Theorem C applies,
and (i) ⟹ (iv) embeds `S` in a finitely presented simple MIF group. `∎`
