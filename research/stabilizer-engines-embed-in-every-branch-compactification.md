**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-invent-13, 2026-09-18; elementary; not reviewed): parts 1–3.
Part 4 is a dictionary, and all of it is proved (4(c) revised 09-18: the classifiability remark is withdrawn).

**Notation.** `S` is a countable infinite group, `D = Z/2 * S`, and `T` is the Bass–Serre tree
(S-vertices `gS`, Z/2-vertices `g(Z/2)`, edges `g ∈ D`). The edges at `v = gS` are `gs`, `s ∈ S`.
An **S-compactification of S** is a compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected S-space `K` that
contains `S`, with left translation, as an open, dense, discrete orbit. Write `∂ = K \ S`.
The one-point compactification `S⁺ = S ⊔ {∞}` is the smallest one, and βS is the largest.

**The engine of K.** For `v = gS`, let `K_v = (g, K)/((gt,k) ~ (g,tk))`, and `∂_v` the image of `∂`.
Put `Ω^K = ∂T ⊔ ⊔_{v} ∂_v`. For a half-tree `H`, its shadow `Sh(H)` is the set of ends of `H` together
with the `∂_w`-points of the S-vertices `w` in `H`. Take as a basis the shadows, together with the sets
`N(v,U) = (U∩∂)_v ∪ ⋃_{s ∈ U∩S} Sh(half-tree at v through edge gs)` for `U ⊆ K` clopen.
`D` acts by homeomorphisms. Write `F_S^K = [[D ⋉ Ω^K]]`.
For `K = S⁺` this is the tree compactified by the S-vertices, so `F_S^{S⁺} = F_S` is the
stabilizer engine of `free-product-boundary-groupoids-carry-the-vertex-group-homology`.
Its vertex point `∞_v` is an infinite emitter with isotropy `S`.

## Statement

1. **Monotonicity.** Let `π : K' → K` be an S-equivariant continuous map that is the identity on `S`.
   Then it induces an injective homomorphism `F_S^K → F_S^{K'}`, carrying `D(F_S^K)` into `D(F_S^{K'})`.
   Every `K` maps onto `S⁺`, so the stabilizer engine embeds in every blow-up:
   `S ⊆ D ⊆ F_S ↪ F_S^K` for every S-compactification `K`.
2. **Finite remainders are ends.** Let `S` be finitely generated with at most one end and no proper
   finite-index subgroup. Then `S⁺` is the only S-compactification of `S` with finite remainder.
   This covers every infinite Kazhdan group without finite quotients, and every infinite fg simple
   group with one end.
3. **Finite type.** Call `K` of finite type if it is conjugate to the orbit closure `\overline{Sx} ⊆ A^S`
   of a point `x`, with `A` finite, such that `Sx` is open and discrete, `s ↦ sx` is injective, and
   `\overline{Sx}` is a subshift of finite type. In other words, `x` is an isolated point of an S-SFT
   with dense orbit.
   - (a) For infinite fg `S`, `S⁺` is never of finite type.
   - (b) For `S = Z`, the two-point compactification `Z ⊔ {±∞}` is of finite type.
   - (c) For `S = Z²`, the "corner" compactification below is of finite type. Its remainder is
     infinite, with orbits `Z, Z` and two fixed points.
4. **Operator-algebraic dictionary.**
   - (a) S-compactifications of `S` correspond to S-invariant unital C*-subalgebras `𝔄`, generated by
     projections, with `c_0(S) + C1 ⊆ 𝔄 ⊆ ℓ^∞(S)`. The correspondence reverses order: a larger `𝔄`
     means a finer `K`. Part 1 says `𝔄 ↦ F_S^K` is monotone.
   - (b) The isotropy of `D ⋉ Ω^K` at `ξ_v ∈ ∂_v` is a conjugate of `Stab_S(ξ)`. At an end it is
     trivial or infinite cyclic. So the input `S` is isotropy only when `∂` has an S-fixed point.
     If `S ↷ ∂` has torsion-free stabilizers, all isotropy is torsion-free.
   - (c) The projection `ρ_v : Ω^K → K` is continuous and S-equivariant. It is the identity on `∂_v`
     and sends the shadow through the edge `gs` to `s`, and `ρ_v^{-1}(U) = N(v,U)`. So if `S ↷ K` is
     amenable, then so is `S ↷ Ω^K`.
     In the other direction, an amenable étale groupoid has amenable isotropy. The isotropy of
     `D ⋉ Ω^{S⁺}` at `∞_v` is `S`, so for non-amenable `S` the one-point engine is non-amenable, and its
     reduced C*-algebra is non-nuclear (Anantharaman-Delaroche–Renault).
     No classifiability claim is made for blow-up engines. Revision 09-18: an earlier version stated,
     as plausible, that amenability of `S ↷ K` makes the engine algebra a Kirchberg algebra. That
     statement is withdrawn.

## Proof

**Part 1.** `π` is surjective, since its image is compact and contains the dense set `S`.
- *π maps `∂'` into `∂`.* If `π(ξ) = s ∈ S`, then `π^{-1}(s)` is open, and `π^{-1}(s) \ {s}` is an
  open set containing `ξ`. It therefore meets `S` at some `t ≠ s` with `π(t) = t = s`. Contradiction.
- *The map `Π`.* Define `Π : Ω^{K'} → Ω^K` as the identity on ends and as `π` on each `∂'_v`.
  - Preimages of basic sets are basic: `Π^{-1}(Sh(H)) = Sh'(H)` and `Π^{-1}(N(v,U)) = N'(v, π^{-1}U)`,
    because `π^{-1}U ∩ S = U ∩ S`. So `Π` is continuous.
  - `Π` is D-equivariant and surjective.
- *`D` acts locally uniquely.* A nonempty open subset of `Ω^K` contains a shadow, since every
  nonempty open `U ⊆ K` meets `S`. An element of `D` fixing a shadow pointwise fixes infinitely many
  ends of a half-tree, hence the half-tree, hence an edge. Edge stabilizers are trivial, so the
  element is trivial.
- *Cocycles.* So each `f ∈ F_S^K` has a unique locally constant cocycle `c_f : Ω^K → D` with
  `f(x) = c_f(x)·x`, and `c_{fg}(x) = c_f(gx) c_g(x)`.
- *The homomorphism.* Set `c_{f'} = c_f ∘ Π`. Then `f'` is a homeomorphism: its pieces are
  `Π^{-1}` of pieces, and `Π` intertwines. The cocycle identity passes through `Π`, so `f ↦ f'` is a
  homomorphism. It is injective because `Π` is onto. Commutators go to commutators.
- *Everything maps onto `S⁺`.* Send `∂ ↦ ∞`. This is continuous: a neighbourhood of `∞` is `S⁺`
  minus a finite set `F`, and its preimage `K \ F` is open.
- *The chain.* `D` sits in each `F_S^K` as the constant cocycles.

**Part 2.** Let `∂ = {p_1, …, p_k}`. `S` permutes `∂` through a finite quotient, so it fixes `∂`
pointwise.
- Choose disjoint clopen `W_i ∋ p_i` covering `K` up to a finite set, and put `A_i = W_i ∩ S`.
- For `s ∈ S`, the set `sW_i \ W_i` is clopen and contains no `p_j`. (It misses `p_i ∈ W_i`, and
  `p_j ∈ sW_i` would force `p_j = s^{-1}p_j ∈ W_i`.) So it is a compact subset of the discrete set
  `S`, hence finite.
- Thus `S = A_1 ⊔ … ⊔ A_k ⊔ (finite)`, and `sA_i Δ A_i` is finite for every `s`. After inversion, the
  `A_i^{-1}` are infinite, pairwise disjoint and almost invariant under right multiplication. So
  `k ≤ e(S)`, and `k = 1` when `e(S) ≤ 1`.
- With `k = 1`, `K = S ⊔ {p}` has the cofinite neighbourhoods of `p`, so `K = S⁺`.
- *Kazhdan groups.* Property (T) implies (FA), so `S` does not split over a finite subgroup. By
  Stallings, `e(S) ≤ 1`.

**Part 3.**
- *(a)* `\overline{S δ_1}` is the set of configurations with at most one `1`. An SFT with window `F`
  admits configurations with two `1`s farther apart than `diam F`, since every `F`-pattern of such a
  configuration occurs in a one-`1` configuration. Any finite-type `K` conjugate to `S⁺` would make
  this subshift an SFT, which is impossible.
- *(b)* The step `x = 1_{[0,∞)}`. Its orbit closure is `{σ^n x} ∪ {0^Z, 1^Z}`, which is the SFT
  forbidding `10`, and `x` is isolated.
- *(c)* Tiles `in, out, H, V, C`. The nearest-neighbour rules:

  | tile | left | right | below | above |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `H` | `H` or `C` | `H` | `out` | `in` |
  | `V` | `out` | `in` | `V` or `C` | `V` |
  | `C` | `out` | `H` | `out` | `V` |
  | `in` | `in` or `V` | `in` | `in` or `H` | `in` |
  | `out` | `out` | `out`, `V` or `C` | `out` | `out`, `H` or `C` |

  - *At most one corner.* The `in`-cells form an up-right closed set, and `in`/`out` meet only across
    a ray. For two corners `(a,b)` and `(a',b')`:
    - `a'<a, b'<b`: the H-ray of the second corner reaches `(a,b')`, which lies in the `out`-column
      below the first corner;
    - `a'<a, b'>b`: `(a,b')` would be both `H` and `V`;
    - `a'=a`: a `V`-cell would lie below `C`.
    The remaining cases are symmetric.
  - *The subshift.* Rays cannot cross and cannot end except at `C`. So the SFT consists of the
    corners (one orbit, isolated), the horizontal lines (orbit `≅ Z`), the vertical lines, and
    `in^{Z²}` and `out^{Z²}`. It is the orbit closure of a corner.

**Part 4.**
- *(a)* This is Gelfand duality. Restriction to the dense set `S` embeds `C(K)` in `ℓ^∞(S)`, and it
  contains `c_0(S)` because the points of `S` are isolated.
- *(b)* `Stab_D(ξ_v)` fixes `v` (the only S-vertex whose remainder copy contains `ξ_v`), so it is
  `g Stab_S(ξ) g^{-1}`. Elliptic torsion fixes no end.
- *(c)* This is the expected analogue of relative exactness for tree compactifications; it is not
  proved here. If `S` is non-amenable, the non-amenable isotropy `S` at `∞_v` rules out amenability
  of the one-point engine.

## Consequences for the gates (SYNTHESIS v5)

- **(IE) is sufficient, not necessary, for Track C.**
  - BH for a decidable simple `S` follows from finite presentation of `D(F_S^K)` for ANY `K`.
    Minimality holds for every `K`, since every basic open set contains shadows and hence ends.
    Pure infiniteness is checked in the parent node only for `K = S⁺`; it must be rechecked for each
    `K`. Given it, `D(F_S^K)` is simple by `matui-purely-infinite-minimal-full-groups-have-simple-d`,
    and it contains `S = [S,S]` by part 1.
  - So a NEGATIVE answer for Reid's group `F_Z` kills only the bottom member of the family, not "the
    whole engine family". For `S = Z`, the next member is the two-point blow-up `F_Z^{±}`, where
    `F_Z ↪ F_Z^{±}`: finite type, with two ordered emitters per vertex.
- **Why emitters fail finite type.** Finite-type finiteness engines (Boolean inverse monoids, Li
  Garside categories, Stein–Farley) see the compactification of each branch set. At an emitter that
  compactification is `S⁺`, which is never an SFT (part 3a). The finite-type repair is a blow-up to
  an SFT compactification.
- **One-ended inputs pay with a boundary.**
  - For one-ended `S`, which includes every Kazhdan input, the only finite-remainder compactification
    is the emitter (part 2).
  - So a finite-type repair needs an **isolated dense orbit in an S-SFT** whose remainder carries a
    nontrivial S-action. Call this gate **(IE′)**.
  - `Z` and `Z²` have one. For one-ended groups without product structure it is open.
- **Ring route.**
  - A blow-up whose remainder has torsion-free stabilizers removes `S` from isotropy (4b), which
    lifts the v5 (K′) warning about inputs in isotropy.
  - The identification of finite-type blow-ups with SFT compactifications, and the hyperbolic case, are
    in `finite-type-branch-blow-ups-are-sft-compactifications`.

## Lesson for general BH

- An infinite emitter is the one-point compactification of a branch set, and that compactification
  is never of finite type. This is the whole reason finite-type finiteness theorems stall there.
- The emitter is the bottom of a lattice of blow-ups. The lattice corresponds to S-invariant
  C*-subalgebras between `c_0(S) + C` and `ℓ^∞(S)`, and the engine groups increase along it.
- So Track C and Track A′ are two ends of one family. The design variable is the branch
  compactification `K`, not the emitter itself, and "is Reid's group finitely presented?" is only the
  bottom test.
- For one-ended inputs, the cheapest nontrivial `K` must carry a genuine S-boundary. The real gate is
  an SFT over `S` with an isolated dense orbit, (IE′).
