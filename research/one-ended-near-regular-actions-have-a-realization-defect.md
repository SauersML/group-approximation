**ESTABLISHED** by `one-ended-near-regular-actions-have-a-realization-defect-proof`. This is a lane proof: elementary, not independently reviewed, with no priority claimed. The general theory of near actions is Cornulier's (arXiv:1901.05065), and an equivalent invariant may be implicit there. That has not been checked.

## Setting

- `P` is an infinite group generated by a finite symmetric set `S`, and `P` is one-ended.
- `nu: P -> N` is a bijection, `lambda_g(nu(h)) = nu(gh)`, `s: n ↦ n+1`, and
  `c_k = s^k lambda(P) s^-k` for `k ∈ Z`, all as in `shell-germ-group-has-index-two-fiber-product`.
- Every infinite group with property (T) is one-ended, so all inputs of
  `boone-higman-iff-simple-kazhdan-decidable-inputs` and `St_N(R_L)` qualify.

A *realization* of a near action `alpha` of `P` on `N` is a genuine action of `P` on a set `Y`, where `Y △ N` is finite, that agrees with `alpha(g)` at all but finitely many points for each `g ∈ S`. Write `y(Y) = |Y \ N| - |N \ Y|`. Call `alpha` *near-regular* if it has a realization in which `P` acts freely and transitively. For such a free transitive realization `Z`, put

    delta(alpha) = y(Z) = |Z \ N| - |N \ Z|.

## Statement

1. **Well defined.** `delta(alpha)` does not depend on the choice of `Z`.
2. **Realizations.** Every realization `Y` of a near-regular `alpha` is one free orbit `O` together with a finite set `F`, and
   `y(Y) = delta(alpha) + |F|`. So `alpha` is realizable on `N` itself only if `delta(alpha) <= 0`, and then with exactly `-delta(alpha)` points outside the free orbit.
3. **Invariance.** `delta` is unchanged under conjugation by any index-zero near permutation `tau` (the class of a bijection of `N`) and under precomposition with any automorphism of `P`. Conjugation by `s` lowers `delta` by one.
4. **Shell conjugates.** `c_k` is near-regular with `delta(c_k) = -k`. So `c_k` is realizable on `N` exactly when `k >= 0`, with `k` points outside the free orbit.
5. **Copies cannot be conjugated into one another.** Let `tau` be an index-zero near permutation and `j ≠ k`. Then `tau c_j tau^-1` is not contained in `c_k`.
   - With `tau = 1` and `k = 0` this recovers item 1 of `one-ended-shell-inputs-admit-no-shift-normalizing-enumeration`.
   - The `c_k`, for `k ∈ Z`, are pairwise non-conjugate in `ker(eta) <= R_nu`, and even in the group of index-zero near permutations.
   - In the finite-window gate `s^-eps lambda(P) s^eps <= W_N^eps` of `shell-ascending-gate-is-a-finite-window-inclusion`, `c_(-eps)` is never contained in a `ker(eta)`-conjugate of a single copy `c_(eps j)`, `0 <= j <= N`. A witness has to generate `c_(-eps)` by genuinely mixing several copies.
6. **Witnesses with eps = 1 are not realizable.** If the gate holds with `eps = 1` and window `N`, then the near action of `W_N^1 = <c_0, ..., c_N>` has no realization on any `Y` with `y(Y) <= 0`, and in particular none on `N`. Each generating copy `c_j`, `0 <= j <= N`, is realizable on `N`, by its canonical realization fixing `0, ..., j-1`. So the canonical lifts generate a subgroup `G_hat <= Sym(N)` that meets the finitary permutations nontrivially, and `G_hat -> W_N^1` is not injective. For `eps = -1` the same count gives no constraint, because `W_N^-1` contains `c_-N`.

## Calibration

The square-spiral witness for `Z^2` (`square-spiral-z2-enumeration-passes-the-finite-window-gate`) has `eps = 1`, `N = 2`, and is consistent with items 5 and 6.
- Its corrections `delta_j(h)` are products of one-step shifts along diagonal rays.
- Each such shift is a non-realizable near permutation.
- The witness generates `c_-1` by mixing the copies `c_1` and `c_2`, not by conjugating `c_-1` into one copy.

## Scope

- Property (T), FA and FW enter only through one-endedness. The artifact records why they give nothing further for the gate.
- Nothing is claimed about finite presentation of `R_nu`, `F_nu`, `Q_nu` or `E_nu`.
- Inputs with more than one end are not treated. For `P = Z` the zigzag enumeration of the one-ended node shows that items 4–5 fail there.
