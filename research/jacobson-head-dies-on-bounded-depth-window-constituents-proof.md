## 1. The finite-dimensional sector of `E` is blind to `L`

**Lemma 1.** Every finite-dimensional unitary representation `σ` of `E` is trivial on `L`. Hence, for any unitary
representation `Π` of `E`, `Π(g) Q_fd = Q_fd` for all `g ∈ L`.

*Proof.*
1. `L ≅ GL(∞,2) = ∪_m GL_m(F_2)` is infinite. Over `F_2`, `GL_m = SL_m`, which is simple for `m >= 3`. An increasing
   union of simple groups is simple: a nontrivial normal subgroup meets some `SL_m` nontrivially, hence contains it and
   every later `SL_(m')`. So `L` is infinite and simple.
2. `σ(E)` is a finitely generated linear group, so it is residually finite (Mal'cev).
3. `σ(L)` is a quotient of the simple group `L`, so it is `1` or `σ|_L` is injective.
4. Suppose `σ|_L` is injective. Pick `g ≠ 1` in `σ(L)` and a finite quotient `f : σ(E) -> Φ` with `f(g) ≠ 1`. Then
   `f∘σ|_L` is a nontrivial homomorphism from the simple group `L`, hence injective, into the finite group `Φ`. This
   contradicts `|L| = ∞`. So `σ(L) = 1`.
5. `Q_fd` projects onto the closed span of finite-dimensional invariant subspaces. `L` fixes each of them pointwise by
   steps 1–4, hence fixes their closed span pointwise. ∎

## 2. Tuples and isotypic blocks

Fix `D >= 1` with `D <= n`.
* `Y = X^(D)` is the set of injective `D`-tuples of `X`, with the coordinatewise genuine action `Π` of `E` on
  `ℓ²(Y)`.
* `F^(D) ⊆ Y` has `N = n!/(n-D)!` elements.
* `π̃(q)` is the unitary on `ℓ²(Y)` that permutes `F^(D)` coordinatewise by `π(q)` and is the identity on
  `ℓ²(Y ∖ F^(D))`. For a word `v`, `π̃(v)` is the corresponding word in these unitaries. It preserves `ℓ²(F^(D))` and
  acts there as the permutation `π(v)`.

As a `Sym(F)`-module, `ℓ²(F^(D)) = Ind_(Sym(n-D))^(Sym(n)) 1`, because the stabilizer of a tuple is the symmetric group
on the remaining `n-D` points. By the branching rule, the multiplicity of `λ` is the number of standard skew tableaux
of shape `λ/(n-D)`. This is positive iff `(n-D) ⊆ λ`, that is iff `λ_1 >= n-D`. So every irreducible `λ` of depth
`<= D` occurs.

Fix such a `λ`. Let `V ⊆ ℓ²(F^(D))` be its isotypic block, `P` the orthogonal projection onto `V`, and `d = dim V`.
Then `V ≅ λ^(⊕ mult)`, so for every `g ∈ Sym(F)`

```text
‖λ(g) - 1‖_2 = ‖(π̃(g) - 1)|_V‖_(2,d).                                      (WP1)
```

**Constant diagonal.** `P` commutes with the permutation unitaries of `Sym(F)`, and `Sym(F)` is transitive on
`F^(D)`. So `⟨P δ_c, δ_c⟩` does not depend on `c`, and it equals `tr P / N = d/N`. Consequently, for any
`B ⊆ F^(D)`, with `R_B` the projection onto `ℓ²(B)`,

```text
‖R_B P‖_HS² = Σ_(c∈B) ⟨P δ_c, δ_c⟩ = (|B|/N) d.                             (WP2)
```

## 3. Truncated and genuine words agree off a small set of tuples

Let `v = q_k ⋯ q_1` be a word over `Q`. For `c ∈ F^(D)` put `c_0 = c` and `c_i = π(q_i) c_(i-1)`. Let `B_v` be the set of
`c` such that, for some `i`, some coordinate `x` of `c_(i-1)` has `π(q_i)x ≠ q_i x`.

**Lemma 2.** `|B_v| <= k D ε N`, and `π̃(v) δ_c = Π(v) δ_c` for every `c ∈ F^(D) ∖ B_v`.

*Proof.*
1. For a fixed coordinate position `j`, the `j`-th coordinate of a uniformly random injective tuple is uniform on `F`.
   By `(WD1)`, at most `εN` tuples have a bad `j`-th coordinate for `q_i`. A union over the `D` positions gives at most
   `DεN` tuples with some bad coordinate for `q_i`.
2. `c ↦ c_(i-1)` is the permutation `π(q_(i-1) ⋯ q_1)` of `F^(D)`. So the set of `c` that fail at step `i` is the
   preimage of the set in step 1, and it has at most `DεN` elements. Summing over `i = 1..k` gives the bound.
3. If `c ∉ B_v`, then by induction on `i`, `c_i = q_i ⋯ q_1 c`, because the genuine action on tuples is coordinatewise.
   So `π(v)c = vc` and `π̃(v) δ_c = δ_(vc) = Π(v) δ_c`. ∎

**Corollary 3.** `‖P (π̃(v) - Π(v)) P‖_HS <= 2 (k D ε d)^(1/2)`.

*Proof.* `(π̃(v) - Π(v)) P = (π̃(v) - Π(v)) R_(B_v) P` by Lemma 2, since `P` is supported in `ℓ²(F^(D))`. The
difference of two unitaries has operator norm at most `2`, and `(WP2)` gives `‖R_(B_v) P‖_HS² <= k D ε d`. ∎

## 4. The block is a Kazhdan corner

Let `W : V -> ℓ²(Y)` be the inclusion, and `U_q = π̃(q)|_V`. This is a unitary of `V`, because `V` is `Sym(F)`-invariant.
Then `W^* Π(q) W - U_q = P (Π(q) - π̃(q)) P` on `V`, so Corollary 3 with `k = 1` gives

```text
max_(q∈Q) ‖W^* Π(q) W - U_q‖_(2,d) <= 2 (D ε)^(1/2).                        (WP3)
```

`(KFM1)` applied to the genuine representation `Π` of `E` on `ℓ²(Y)` then gives

```text
(1/d) Tr(P (1 - Q_fd) P) <= 8 (D ε)^(1/2) / κ².                              (WP4)
```

## 5. Assembly

Let `z` be the value of `w` in `L`, so `Π(w) = Π(z)`. By Lemma 1, `(Π(z) - 1) Q_fd = 0`, hence

```text
‖P (Π(z) - 1) P‖_HS <= ‖(Π(z) - 1)(1 - Q_fd) P‖_HS <= 2 ‖(1 - Q_fd) P‖_HS
                     = 2 Tr(P (1 - Q_fd) P)^(1/2) <= (4√2/κ) (D ε)^(1/4) d^(1/2),   by (WP4).
```

Corollary 3 with `v = w` and `k = ℓ` gives `‖P (π̃(w) - Π(w)) P‖_HS <= 2 (ℓ D ε d)^(1/2)`. On `V`,
`P π̃(w) P - P = (π̃(w) - 1)|_V`. The triangle inequality and `(WP1)` give

```text
‖λ(π(w)) - 1‖_2 <= 2 (ℓ D ε)^(1/2) + (4√2/κ) (D ε)^(1/4)                     (WP5)
```

for every irreducible `λ` of depth `<= D`.

For a general `ρ` whose constituents all have depth `<= D`, `‖ρ(g) - 1‖_2²` is the dimension-weighted average of
`‖λ(g) - 1‖_2²` over its constituents. So `(WP5)` gives `(WD2)`.

## 6. Subgroups and alternating groups

`ρ(π(w))` is one matrix, so `(WD2)` holds verbatim for `ρ` restricted to any subgroup of `Sym(F)` containing `π(Q)`,
in particular to `Alt(F)` when every `π(q)` is even.

If `λ = λ'` then `λ_1` equals the number of rows, so `n >= 2λ_1 - 1`. For depth `<= D < (n-1)/2` we have
`λ_1 > (n+1)/2`, so `λ ≠ λ'` and `Res^(Sym)_(Alt) λ` is irreducible. The irreducible representations of `Alt(F)` are
these restrictions together with the two halves of `Res λ` for self-conjugate `λ`. So the restrictions cover every
`Alt(F)`-irreducible whose label has depth `<= D`. ∎

## 7. Where the route stops

Nothing here uses the finite presentation or the Toeplitz structure. The mechanism is only property (T) plus the
`L`-blindness of the finite-dimensional sector, which is why it is uniform over `E`-sets.

Its two hypotheses are exactly the survivors listed on the target:
* **Windowness.** `(WD1)` needs the generator permutations to be `ε`-truncations of a genuine action. A generic sofic
  approximation need not be one.
* **Bounded depth.** The bound degrades as `(Dε)^(1/4)`. Constituents of depth `D >= c/ε` are untouched. The corner
  defect `(WP3)` is then of order one, and `(KFM1)` is vacuous.
