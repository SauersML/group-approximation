Write `e_d=s_d t_d`.  Maximality and prefix-freeness give

```text
sum_(d in D)e_d=1,       t_d s_e=delta_(d,e).            (MLO4)
```

Consequently the right-module map

```text
Phi:R^D direct_sum R^(n-1) -> R^n,
Phi((a_d)_(d in D),b_2,...,b_n)
  =(sum_(d in D)s_d a_d,b_2,...,b_n)                    (MLO5)
```

is an isomorphism.  Its inverse applies `t_d` to the first coordinate.

For a standard prefix permutation `f in F`,

```text
f=sum_(d in D)s_(f(d))t_d.
```

Thus, in the chart `Phi`, the block unit `D_n(f)` permutes the
`D`-coordinates by `d |-> f(d)` and fixes the remaining coordinates.
Let `star` denote coordinate `2`.  Direct substitution in `(MLO5)`
gives

```text
Phi^(-1)e_12(s_(d_0))Phi = 1+E_(d_0,star),
Phi^(-1)e_21(t_(d_0))Phi = 1+E_(star,d_0).              (MLO6)
```

Here the nonzero entries on the right are the scalar `1_R`.  The scalar
matrix map `M_(m+1)(F_2)->M_(m+1)(R)` is injective: a zero scalar matrix
endomorphism has every entry `c_(u,v)1_R=0`, hence every
`c_(u,v)=0`.  Equivalently, its `D`-by-`D` corner is the faithful
matrix-unit algebra spanned by the `s_d t_e`.  Since `Phi` is an
isomorphism, conjugating this scalar copy back to `R^n` also preserves
injectivity.  Thus it is an actual finite subgroup, not a quotient or a
coefficient specialization.  Therefore all generators of `H` lie in the scalar copy of
`GL_(m+1)(F_2)` on `D union {star}`, acting identically on coordinates
`3,...,n`.

Transitivity supplies the reverse inclusion.  Conjugating the two
transvections in `(MLO6)` by `F` gives

```text
1+E_(d,star),        1+E_(star,d),             d in D.  (MLO7)
```

For distinct `d,e in D`, the elementary commutator relation gives

```text
[1+E_(d,star),1+E_(star,e)]=1+E_(d,e).                  (MLO8)
```

Hence `H` contains every off-diagonal scalar transvection on
`D union {star}`.  These generate `EL_(m+1)(F_2)`.  Since the only
nonzero scalar of `F_2` is `1`,

```text
EL_(m+1)(F_2)=SL_(m+1)(F_2)=GL_(m+1)(F_2).              (MLO9)
```

Together with the preceding containment this proves `(MLO2)`.  Taking
`m=27` proves `(MLO3)`.  Before the packet conjugates are added, the
two roots in `(MLO6)` generate
`EL_2(F_2)=GL_2(F_2)`, the six-element group `S_3`; equivalently their
squares and the cube of their product are all `1`.

For `n>=5`, let `Gamma` be generated by the lifted packet and the two
standard Steinberg roots.  Its image under the Steinberg projection is the
finite group just computed.  The exact sequence

```text
1 -> Gamma intersect ker(pi) -> Gamma -> GL_(m+1)(F_2) -> 1
```

has central left term: the projection maps the displayed generators onto
all of `H`, so its image is exactly the computed scalar group.  Thus `Gamma` is central-by-finite, amenable,
and hyperlinear.  Finite-support localization then excludes every finite
no-CE atlas supported in `C[H]` or `C[Gamma]`.

Finally, when `d_0` is nonempty, the coefficient algebra generated by
`s_(d_0),t_(d_0)` is genuinely infinite-dimensional: it contains all
distinct pure prefix monomials `s_(d_0)^k`, `k>=0`, which are linearly
independent by the standard Leavitt normal form.  Thus this computation is
not an instance of finite coefficient closure.  Its finiteness comes from
the simultaneous module chart `(MLO5)`: the one-sided inverse pair becomes
a matched pair of scalar arrows.  Any next opposite-root candidate must
destroy that simultaneous scalarization.
