Fullness is immediate and is worth separating from weak or strong operator
topology.  If `0!=a in Z`, then `a` generates the simple unital algebra `Z`
as a closed two-sided ideal.  Applying a unital embedding `phi` to finite
sums approximating `1_Z` shows that the norm-closed ideal generated by
`phi(a)` contains `1_M`.  Thus `phi(a)` lies in no proper norm-closed ideal
of `M`.  The same holds for `psi(a)`.

For rank, let `z_f` be the largest central projection such that `z_f M` is
finite, and put `z_i=1-z_f`.  This canonical split exists in every von
Neumann algebra and `z_i M` has no nonzero finite central summand.

On `z_f M`, the center-valued trace composed with either compressed
embedding is forced by the unique trace of `Z`.  Normality of the
center-valued trace and functional calculus then give equal center-valued
traces for the range projections of `z_f phi(a)` and `z_f psi(a)`.
Projection comparison in a finite von Neumann algebra makes those range
projections Murray--von Neumann equivalent.

On `z_i M`, let `p` be the range projection of `z_i phi(a)` for `a!=0`.
Finite-sum fullness in `Z`, after applying `phi` and passing to range
projections, gives an integer `n` such that

```text
z_i <=_MvN p directSum ... directSum p.                       (1)
```

The projection `p` has no nonzero finite central compression.  Indeed, if
`0!=z<=z_i` were central and `zp` finite, then (1) would make the unit `z`
of `zM` subequivalent to the finite projection `(zp)^directSum n`.  This
would make `zM` finite, contradicting the definition of `z_i`.

The central finite/properly-infinite decomposition of a projection therefore
makes `p` properly infinite.  It absorbs its `n`-fold sum, so (1) gives
`z_i<=_MvN p`.  Since `p<=z_i`, projection Schroeder--Bernstein yields
`p~z_i`.  The same argument applies to the support of `z_i psi(a)`.  Joining
the finite and properly infinite central pieces proves equal `M`-rank for
every nonzero `a`; both ranks are zero for `a=0`.

The argument deliberately applies only to supports of full image elements.
It makes no assertion that arbitrary nonzero projections in a
non-countably-decomposable factor are equivalent to the unit.
