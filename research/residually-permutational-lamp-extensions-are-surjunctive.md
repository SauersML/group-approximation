**ESTABLISHED** by `residually-permutational-lamp-extensions-proof`.

Let `Q` be surjunctive and `V` an abelian group with a `Q`-action. Suppose that for every
nonzero `v in V` there are a `Q`-set `Y`, a residually finite group `A`, and a `Q`-equivariant
homomorphism `phi : V -> direct_sum_Y A` with `phi(v) != 0`. Then `V x| Q` is surjunctive.

**For elementary abelian 2-groups** the targets can be the permutation modules `F_2[Y]`
(Lemma 3.1). For a quotient `V = P/U` of `P = F_2[G/Gamma]`, the maps are `phi_c(e_(g Gamma)) = g c`
with `c` a finitely supported `Gamma`-fixed vector (Lemma 3.2). So `V` qualifies iff `U` equals its
permutation closure `U-bar`, the intersection of the kernels of the `phi_c` that vanish on `U`.

**Consequences for the Kun--Thom wreath `W`.** Take `U <= K_even`.
- For every closed `U`, the lamp quotient `W/U` is surjunctive. Closed examples are `0`, the
  permutation kernels `U_K` with `Gamma <= K <= N`, and all intersections of kernels.
- In particular every intermediate coset wreath `W_K` with `Gamma <= K <= N` is surjunctive,
  and nonsofic when `K != N`.
- A submodule generated by one fibre pair `e_Gamma + e_(n Gamma)` equals `U_<Gamma,n>`, so it
  is closed (Example 3.4).

So a counterexample among lamp quotients needs `U != U-bar`: a lamp relation that no permutation
image sees.

Proof: Theorem B, Lemmas 3.1--3.2, Corollary 3.3 and Example 3.4 of the artifact.
