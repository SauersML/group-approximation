**Statement.** Fix a finite alphabet `X`, the free group `F = F(X)` and its
word-length balls `B_rho`. Let `E = F/K` be a finitely generated group with a
central subgroup `C = R/K`, where `K <= R <= F`, such that the base
`Q = E/C = F/R` is hyperlinear. A *finitely presented cover* of `Q` is
`P = F/S` with `S <= R` the normal closure of a finite set. Say that `Q` is
*approximable by hyperlinear covers* (AHC) if for every `rho` there is a
hyperlinear finitely presented cover `P = F/S` with
`S intersect B_rho = R intersect B_rho`.

1. **(Cover transport.)** For every normal `S <= R`, the diagonal image
   `E_S` of `F` in `E x F/S`, that is `E_S = F/(S intersect K)`, is a central
   extension

   ```text
   1 --> A_S --> E_S --> F/S --> 1,      A_S = SK/K <= C.          (CT1)
   ```

   If `S` is the normal closure of a finite set `Sigma`, then `A_S` is
   generated by the images of `Sigma`, so it is a finitely generated abelian
   group. If `S intersect B_rho = R intersect B_rho` then `E_S` and `E` agree
   on `B_rho`. So `E_S --> E` in the space of marked groups as `S` runs along
   any sequence of covers converging to `Q`.
2. **(Gap theorem.)** If `E` is not hyperlinear and `Q` is AHC, then some
   hyperlinear finitely presented group `P` has a non-hyperlinear central
   extension by a finitely generated subgroup `A <= C`. Consequently
   (item 4 of `hyperlinear-shadow-schur-cokernel-criterion`) some finitely
   generated hyperlinear group has a non-hyperlinear central extension by
   `Z/p`. That is item (a') there.
3. **(Biography of the divisible gap.)** Suppose some hyperlinear group has
   a non-hyperlinear central extension, but no finitely generated hyperlinear
   group has one with finite kernel. Then every pair `(E, Q)` as above with
   `E` non-hyperlinear has the following properties.
   - `Q` is infinitely presented.
   - There is a radius `rho_0` such that **every** finitely presented cover
     `F/S` of `Q` with `S intersect B_rho_0 = R intersect B_rho_0` is
     non-hyperlinear.
   - `Res(E)` is a nonzero divisible group. By (CT1) all the transported
     witnesses `E_S` over those covers have finitely generated central kernels
     but non-hyperlinear bases.

   Every countable non-hyperlinear central extension of a hyperlinear group
   contains such a finitely generated pair, since some finitely generated
   subgroup `E_0` is non-hyperlinear and `E_0/(E_0 intersect C)` is a
   subgroup of the base.

**Exact failing step of every cover-based central compiler.** The
transport (CT1) is unconditional. The kernel is finitely generated, and the
transported group converges to the witness, hence is itself a witness
eventually. The only step that can fail is hyperlinearity of the finitely
presented cover `F/S`. In the gap world it fails for all sufficiently close
covers at once.

**Why this changes the object.** The recorded dead ends for the divisible
gap push the base *up*: enlargements `Q <= Q'`, split pullbacks, and pullbacks
along finite-kernel extensions. They need the infinite-order Schur class
either to extend to a bigger group or to be killed by a finite group, and
transgression from a finite `Hom(N, P)` blocks both. Covers move the base
*down*, in the quotient direction. The class is pulled back rather than
extended, and upstairs the transported central kernel `SK/K` is generated by
finitely many relator images. The divisible part of `H_2(Q)` simply does not
exist there. What is paid is a new invariant: the marked-space position of
`Q` relative to the hyperlinear finitely presented groups above it. This is
not a cohomological invariant of `Q`, so it is independent of the
Schur-obstruction-subgroup formulation of the gap (every such subgroup
divisible and some nonzero), which is a property of `H_2(Q)` alone.

**First falsifiable step and calibration.**
- The first falsifiable step is (CT1): that `SK/K` is central in `F/(S intersect K)`
  and finitely generated, and that `S intersect K` agrees with `K` on `B_rho`.
- If `Q` is finitely presented, take `S = R` for every `rho`. Item 2 then
  reproduces the finitely presented case of
  `hyperlinear-shadow-schur-cokernel-criterion`.
- If `E` is a finitely generated central extension of hyperlinear `Q` by a
  finite `C`, every `A_S` is finite, and item 2 is item 4 of the shadow
  criterion.
- Deligne's central extension `Sp_4(Z)~` over the finitely presented
  `Sp_4(Z)`: `S = R` is allowed, `A_S = C = Z` and `E_S = E`, so the transport
  is the identity. The Deligne arithmetic candidate therefore lives entirely
  in the non-gap part, where finite central witnesses are already equivalent.
- Whether a hyperlinear finitely generated group can have a whole marked
  neighbourhood of finitely presented covers that are non-hyperlinear (item 3,
  second bullet) is open even assuming the root. It needs a witness whose
  hyperlinear residual is not approximable from above by finitely many
  relators with hyperlinear quotients.

**Scope.** This is a one-directional strict reduction of the divisible gap
(gap implies a non-AHC base carrying a non-hyperlinear central extension).
The converse is not claimed: a non-AHC hyperlinear base need not carry any
non-hyperlinear central extension. Known infinitely presented examples with
hyperlinear finitely presented truncations, such as infinite `C'(1/6)`
presentations whose truncations are cubulated and hence linear, have free
abelian `H_2`. They are already covered by torsion density, so no new class
of bases is claimed to be killed beyond what item 2 states.
