## The group

- **The lamplighter part.** `L = ⟨a, b⟩ ≅ Z/2 ≀ Z` is the lamplighter automaton group on the binary tree:
  `a = σ(a, b)`, `b = (a, b)`. It is faithful and finite-state, and its relator kernel is
  `N_L = K_∞(F(a,b))`.
- **The infinite-state part.** `Z = ⟨t⟩` acts on the binary tree by the virtual endomorphism `φ(2k) = 3k` of `2Z`.
  This action is faithful: a `φ`-invariant subgroup `mZ ⊆ 2Z` would need `m | 3m/2`, which is impossible. It is
  infinite-state: the sections of `t^n` are `t^(3n/2)`, or `t^(3(n±1)/2)` when `n` is odd.
- **The product.** `G = L × Z` acts on the 4-ary tree over the alphabet `{0,1}^2`, coordinatewise on pairs of
  letters: `(g, h)` sends `(x_1, x_2)w` to `(g(x_1), h(x_2)) · (g|_(x_1), h|_(x_2))(w)`.
  - It is faithful, since both factors are.
  - It is self-similar, since sections are pairs of sections.
  - It is infinite-state, through `t`.
  - `G` is **not finitely presented**, because `L` is a retract of `G` and is not finitely presented.
  - Its word problem is solvable.

## Statement

With the generating set `S = {a, b, t}`, free group `F = F(a, b, t)` and relator kernel `N = ker(F -> G)`:

`N = ⟨⟨[a,t], [b,t]⟩⟩_lift`.

So `V_4(G)` is finitely presented, by `lift-ideal-criterion-sufficiency-holds-for-infinite-state`.

## Proof

- **`⊇`.** Both commutators are trivial in `G`, and `N` is a lift ideal.
- **`⊆`.**
  - **The relators of `G`.** `G = L × Z`, so `N` is the normal closure in `F` of `N_L` together with `[a,t]` and
    `[b,t]`.
  - **`N_L` lies in `M`.** A word in `a, b` has the same word-level sections in the product tree as in the binary
    tree, because `a` and `b` act on the first coordinate only. So `N_L = K_∞(F(a,b)) ⊆ K_∞(F)`.
  - **Every lift ideal contains `K_∞`.** Hence `K_∞(F) ⊆ M := ⟨⟨[a,t],[b,t]⟩⟩_lift`.
  - **Conclusion.** `M` contains the two commutators and is normal, so `N ⊆ M`. ∎

## What the test shows

- **The criterion has reach.** Here `G` is neither finitely presented (Scott's FP1) nor contracting (Nekrashevych's
  FP2), and it is not finite-state (Theorem A). Still `V_4(G)` is finitely presented.
- **This is the decoupling principle at work.**
  - All infinitely many states live on the `Z`-coordinate, which carries no relators except the commutation.
  - All the non-finitely-presented relators live on the contracting-like finite-state coordinate, where they
    collapse into `K_∞`.
- **What the test does not show.**
  - It gives no word-problem hardness: `G` is easy.
  - It gives no free-product version. A faithful self-similar `A * B` needs a self-similar free-product
    construction (Bartholdi–Sidki), which is not attempted here.
  - A test where the criterion **fails** would also be informative. It needs a faithful infinite-state group whose
    relators expand under sections. The naive lamplighter of the parent node is not faithful.

## Lesson for general BH

**Relators and states can live on different coordinates.** A host for hard inputs can be assembled so that:
- a contracting or finite-state coordinate carries the relators, which then collapse into `K_∞`;
- an infinite-state coordinate carries the growth that hardness needs, with only commutation relators;
- the Röver–Nekrashevych group stays finitely presented by finitely many lift generators.

**The open question.** Can the infinite-state coordinate carry **word-problem** hardness while its relators stay
finitely lift-generated? A product with `Z` cannot, because the hard part must itself have hard relators.
