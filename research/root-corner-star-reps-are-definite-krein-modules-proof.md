## (K1) Hecke basis and relations

For `g in Gamma`,

```text
P g P = (1/4) sum_(a,b in {0,1}) (-1)^(a+b) z^a g z^b.
```

**Nonvanishing.** The four words `z^a g z^b` cover the double coset `<z> g <z>`. The stabilizer of `g` under
`(a,b) . g = z^a g z^b` is either trivial or `{(0,0),(1,1)}`, because `z g = g` and `g z = g` are impossible. The sign
`(-1)^(a+b)` is trivial on the stabilizer, so the coefficients do not cancel. Hence `P g P != 0`.

**Basis.** Distinct double cosets have disjoint supports, so the elements `P h P`, one per double coset, are linearly
independent. They span `B`, since `B = P A P` is spanned by the `P g P`.

**Relations.**
- `P z = z P = -P` gives `P z h P = P h z P = -P h P`.
- `P* = P` gives `(P h P)* = P h^(-1) P`.
- `(P g P)(P h P) = P g ((1 - z)/2) h P = (1/2)(P g h P - P g z h P)`.

**Correspondence with kernels.** A linear map on `B` is the same as a linear map `E` on `A` with `E(x) = E(P x P)`. On
group elements this is exactly the sign rule. Multiplicativity, the star property and unitality of `rho` translate into
the three remaining conditions on `phi`.

## (K2) Induction

Let `rho : B -> D` be a unital star homomorphism, and let `B` act on `D` on the left through `rho`. Put
`M = A P (x)_B D`, with `Gamma` acting on the left factor.

**The form is well defined.** Define `[a P (x) d, b P (x) e] = d* rho(P a* b P) e`. For `x in B` we have `a P x in A P`
and `(a P x)* = x* P a*`, so

```text
[a P x (x) d, b P (x) e] = d* rho(x)* rho(P a* b P) e = [a P (x) rho(x) d, b P (x) e].
```

The form is:
- hermitian, since `rho(P b* a P) = rho(P a* b P)*`;
- `D`-linear in the second variable;
- `Gamma`-invariant, since `(g a)* (g b) = a* b`.

**The evaluation map.** The map `ev : M -> D`, `a P (x) d -> rho(P a P) d`, is well defined. It is balanced because
`rho(P a P x) = rho(P a P) rho(x)` for `x = P x P`. It sends `P (x) d` to `d`.

**The odd part.** `P . (a P (x) d) = P a P (x) d = P (x) rho(P a P) d`. So `sigma(P) M = P (x) D`, and `ev` identifies
it with `D`. Hence it is free of rank one, generated by `xi = P (x) 1`, and `[P (x) d, P (x) e] = d* e`.

**Orthogonality.** `[Q a P (x) d, P (x) e] = d* rho(P a* Q P) e = 0`, because `Q P = 0`.

**Recovering `rho`.** `[xi, sigma(g) xi] = rho(P g P)`. Also `M` is spanned by `sigma(g) xi d`. So `(M, sigma, xi)`
satisfies (KM), and the construction returns `rho`.

**Converse.** Let `(M, sigma, xi)` be a definite-corner Krein module and set `rho(P x P) = [xi, sigma(x) xi]`.

- *Well defined.* `sigma(z)` preserves the form and `sigma(z)^2 = 1`, so `sigma(P)` is self-adjoint for the form. Since
  `sigma(P) xi = xi`, we get `[xi, sigma(x) xi] = [xi, sigma(P x P) xi]`, which depends only on `P x P`.
- *Multiplicative.* By (KM), `sigma(P) sigma(h) xi = xi d_h` for some `d_h`. Pairing with `xi` gives
  `d_h = [xi, sigma(P) sigma(h) xi] = [xi, sigma(h) xi]`. Therefore
  `rho((P g P)(P h P)) = [xi, sigma(g) sigma(P) sigma(h) xi] = [xi, sigma(g) xi] [xi, sigma(h) xi]`.
- *Star and unital.* `[xi, sigma(g^(-1)) xi] = [sigma(g) xi, xi] = [xi, sigma(g) xi]*`, and `rho(P) = [xi, xi] = 1`.

**The universal surjection.** The map `a P (x) d -> sigma(a) xi d` from `M_rho` to `M` is balanced, because
`sigma(P g P) xi = sigma(P) sigma(g) xi = xi rho(P g P)`. It is `Gamma`-equivariant, form-preserving, and surjective
by (KM).

## (K3) Positivity

Every element of `M_rho` is a finite sum `m = sum_i g_i P (x) d_i`. Its square is

```text
[m, m] = sum_(i,j) d_i* rho(P g_i^(-1) g_j P) d_j = sum_(i,j) d_i* E_rho(g_i^(-1) g_j) d_j.
```

**(1) iff (2).** Two standard facts combine here.

- *Lance, Lemma 4.2.* A matrix `[a_ij]` in `M_m(D)` is positive if and only if `sum_(i,j) d_i* a_ij d_j >= 0` for all
  `d_1, ..., d_m` in `D`. So (1) says exactly that every kernel matrix `[E_rho(g_i^(-1) g_j)]` is positive in
  `M_m(D)`, that is, `g -> E_rho(g)` is a `D`-valued positive-definite function.
- *Positive-definite functions and complete positivity.* For `D` inside `B(H)`, see Brown--Ozawa, Theorem 2.5.11 and
  the discussion around it. Write `x in M_n(C[Gamma])` as `x = sum_g x_g g` with `x_g in M_n(C)`. Then
  `E_rho^(n)(x* x) = sum_(g,h) x_g* E_rho(g^(-1) h) x_h`. Paired with a vector `eta`, this is the kernel sum on the
  vectors `x_g eta`.

So a positive-definite function has a completely positive linear extension, and conversely the kernel matrices are
values of `E_rho^(n)` on elements `x* x`.

**(2) iff (3).** Complete positivity says `rho(P x* x P) >= 0` for all matrix `x`. Now

```text
rho(P x* x P) = rho(P x P)* rho(P x P) + rho(P x* Q x P).
```

So (3) implies (2). Conversely, applying (2) to `Q x` gives `rho(P x* Q x P) >= 0`.

**(1) implies (4).** Separate and complete `M_rho` in the norm `||[m,m]||^(1/2)`. This is the KSGNS construction, and
it gives a Hilbert `D`-module `X`. Each `sigma(g)` preserves the form and has inverse `sigma(g^(-1))`, so it extends to
a unitary `pi(g)` on `X`. The map `V d = [P (x) d]` is an isometry, because `[P (x) d, P (x) e] = d* e`.

- `pi(P)` is a bounded self-adjoint idempotent, that is, a projection.
- `pi(P) X` is the closure of `pi(P)` applied to the image of `M_rho`. That image is `V(D)`, and `V(D)` is closed
  because `V` is isometric.

So `pi(P) X = V(D)`, and `V* pi(g) V = [xi, sigma(g) xi] = rho(P g P)`.

**(4) implies (2).** `E_rho(x) = V* pi(x) V`, since `pi(P) V = V`, and this is completely positive.

**(4) is multiplicative.** In (4), `V V* = pi(P)`, so `V* pi(g) V V* pi(h) V = V* pi(g P h) V`. This recovers the
product rule, so the data of (4) always define a positive corner representation.

## (K4) The infinite dihedral group

**Double coset representatives.** The double cosets of `<z>` in `<z> * <y>` are represented by `h_0 = 1` and
`h_m = (y z)^(m-1) y` for `m >= 1`. Put `e_m = P h_m P` and `T = e_1 = P y P`.

**Recursion.** From `y h_m = z h_(m-1)` for `m >= 2`, `y h_1 = 1` and `y z h_m = h_(m+1)`, the product rule (K1) gives:

```text
T e_1 = (1/2)(P - e_2),        T e_m = -(1/2)(e_(m-1) + e_(m+1))  for m >= 2.
```

**The corner is a polynomial ring.** So `e_m` is a polynomial in `T` of exact degree `m`, with leading coefficient
`(-1)^(m-1) 2^(m-1)`. By the basis of (K1), `B = C[T]` is a polynomial ring and `T = T*`. Therefore `T -> t` defines a
unital star homomorphism to `C` for every real `t`.

**Positivity fails for `|t| > 1`.** `rho(P y Q y P) = rho(P) - rho(T)^2 = 1 - t^2`, so (3) fails when `|t| > 1`.

**Positivity holds for `|t| <= 1`.** In that case take the unitary representation on `C^2` with `z = diag(1,-1)` and
`y` a reflection with `<xi, y xi> = t`, where `xi` is the second standard basis vector. Its compression is a star homomorphism `C[T] -> C` sending
`T` to `t`, so it equals `rho`.

**Explicit Krein model for `|t| > 1`.** Use the basis `u, xi` with form `diag(-1, 1)`, and set

```text
z = diag(1, -1),        y = [[-t, b], [-b, t]],        b = sqrt(t^2 - 1).
```

- `y^2 = (t^2 - b^2) I = I`.
- The form-adjoint of `y` is `J y^T J = y = y^(-1)`, with `J = diag(-1, 1)`.
- `[xi, y xi] = t`.
- The `z`-odd line is `C xi`, and `y xi = b u + t xi` with `b != 0`, so (KM) holds.

The form has signature `(1,1)`, and by the converse in (K2) it realizes `rho`.

## (K5) Finite dimension

Let `rho : B -> M_n(C)`, `n >= 1`, be a unital algebra homomorphism. The construction of (K2) needs no star map for the
module part.

**Rank of the odd part.** `M = A P (x)_B C^n` is a `Gamma`-module, possibly infinite-dimensional. The map `ev` of (K2)
shows that `sigma(P)` is an idempotent of rank exactly `n`. Hence `sigma(z) - 1 = -2 sigma(P)` has rank `n`.

**Finite-rank elements form a normal subgroup.** Let `N = {g : rank(sigma(g) - 1) < infinity}`. The identities

```text
sigma(g h) - 1 = sigma(g)(sigma(h) - 1) + (sigma(g) - 1),
sigma(g^(-1)) - 1 = -sigma(g^(-1))(sigma(g) - 1),
sigma(x g x^(-1)) - 1 = sigma(x)(sigma(g) - 1)sigma(x)^(-1)
```

show that `N` is a normal subgroup. It contains `z`, so it contains the normal closure `K`.

**A finite-codimension fixed space.** Let `S` be a finite generating set of `K` and put
`U = intersection_(s in S) ker(sigma(s) - 1)`. Then `U` has codimension at most `sum_s rank(sigma(s) - 1)`, which is
finite, and `U` is fixed pointwise by `K`. So `K` acts on the finite-dimensional space `M / U`.

**Contradiction.** By hypothesis `z` acts trivially on `M / U`, so `(sigma(z) - 1) M` lies in `U`. Also
`(sigma(z) - 1) U = 0`. Therefore `(sigma(z) - 1)^2 = 0`, that is, `4 sigma(P)^2 = 4 sigma(P) = 0`. This contradicts
rank `n >= 1`.

**Instance.** By `leavitt-steinberg-map-iso-from-rank-three` and `binary-leavitt-elementary-group-is-simple`,
`Delta = St_20(R) = EL_20(R)` is simple.

- It is finitely generated, since `R` is a finitely generated ring.
- It is infinite, since `x_12(R)` is an infinite subgroup.
- `z = x_13(s_1 t_1) != 1` and `z^2 = x_13(2 s_1 t_1) = 1` in characteristic two.

So `K = Delta`. A finite-dimensional representation of `Delta` has finitely generated linear image, which is
residually finite by Malcev. The image is a quotient of a simple group, so it is trivial or isomorphic to `Delta`. In
the second case `Delta` would be residually finite and simple, hence finite, which is false. So every
finite-dimensional representation is trivial, and the hypothesis of (K5) holds.

## (C1) to (C4)

**(C1).** This is (K2) with `D = Q_k`, together with the dichotomy "positive or not" of (K3). In the positive case,
(K3.4) gives `X`, whose odd part `pi(P) X = V(Q_k)` is free of rank one. The translates of `xi` span `M_rho`, which is
dense in `X`. Conversely, such an `X` defines a positive `rho` through (K3.4).

**(C2).** Suppose `X` in (R+) is finitely generated projective. Then `X = p Q_k^m` and `L(X) = p M_m(Q_k) p`.

- Lift `p` to projections `p_n` of rank `r_n`. Then `L(X)` is the norm corona of the `r_n`, after discarding the
  indices with `r_n = 0`.
- `leavitt-st20-t-free-holes-hold-via-kazhdan`, item 2, says every homomorphism `Delta -> U(Q_r)` is trivial.
- Hence `pi(z) = 1` and `pi(P) = 0`, which contradicts `pi(P) X = Q_k`.

**What that argument cannot do.** It uses unitarity in two places: the codomain must be the unitary group of a norm
corona, and the Kazhdan projection must be positive.

- A countably generated `X` has `L(X)` a corner of `M(K (x) Q_k)`. No MF or Kazhdan statement in the graph applies
  there.
- In the non-positive case of (K3) there is no Hilbert module at all. `Gamma` acts on a `Q_k`-module preserving an
  indefinite form, and the norms of the lifted matrices `sigma(g)` can grow along words. The positive-definite
  spectral-gap inequality `pi(Lap) >= epsilon (1 - pi(p))` has no meaning without a positive form.

These are statements about what the recorded argument covers, not claims that (R+) or (R-) fail.

**(C3)** is (C1) together with (K4): no general principle makes corner representations positive.

**(C4)** follows from two facts.

- By (K5), the corner `P_z C[Delta] P_z` has no finite-dimensional representation. So no exact finite model can exhibit
  a corner representation (which would refute MF-nullity), or be one of the maps that MF-nullity forbids.
- By `lin-explicit-fixed-bcs-gap-via-generic-conversion`, `omega_q^s(B_loop) < 1`. So `A(B_loop)` has no
  finite-dimensional representation either, since such a representation is a perfect finite-dimensional strategy.

Thus the map of `agent-leavitt-not-bcs-negative-root-corner` has neither domain nor target testable on an exact finite
model. Only asymptotic, corona-valued objects can decide either hole.
