**ESTABLISHED.**  Put

```text
J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>,       Q=1-ST,       E=EL_5(J),
A=F_2[Q]={0,1,Q,1+Q}.
```

Then `Q` is a nonzero proper idempotent and

```text
A ~= F_2 x F_2.                                           (JCQ1)
```

Consequently the literal subgroup

```text
H_Q=<x_ij(1),x_ij(Q):1<=i!=j<=5>
    =EL_5(A) <= E                                         (JCQ2)
```

is finite, and its element

```text
w=x_13(Q)                                                 (JCQ3)
```

is nontrivial.  In particular `H_Q` has a faithful finite-dimensional
unitary representation in which the Jacobson head survives.

The group `H_Q` contains the native two-qubit packet

```text
x_12(Q), x_23(1), x_14(Q), x_43(1), w,
```

every constant elementary root, every constant Weyl word, and every
constant-Weyl translate of every `Q`-labelled root.  Hence no relation whose
literal occurrences stay inside this entire constant/`Q` envelope can
authenticate the common four-Pauli intertwiner required by
`opnorm-common-intertwiner-pi-gap-is-sparse-terminal`: its faithful finite
model has an exact nonzero head cut and would contradict that terminal PI
inequality.

Thus adding more constant coordinate transports to the native two-qubit
packet is not the missing Jacobson occurrence.  The first eligible package
must simultaneously contain

1. an `S`- or `T`-bearing occurrence leaving `F_2[Q]`; and
2. a nonmonomial constant-root incidence leaving the amenable monomial-symbol
   preimage.

Either ingredient separately has a marked MF countermodel.  The seam is
their joint occurrence semantics, not target Pauli fullness or another
constant Weyl alignment.
