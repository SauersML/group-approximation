## Setting

Let `B = V_{d,r}` act on its Cantor space `X`. Let `B <= G <= Homeo(X)` be a
finite germ extension of `B` (Belk--Hyde--Matucci, arXiv:2407.03149v1). For
`p in X`, `(G)_p` is the group of germs at `p` of elements of `G` fixing `p`.

At a point `p` with eventually periodic address, `(B)_p` is infinite cyclic,
generated by the local contraction `delta_p` (the setting of
`normal-germ-extensions-of-thompson-v-contain-no-sl3z`). A subgroup
`Gamma <= (G)_p` **commensurates** `(B)_p` if every `g in Gamma` satisfies
`g delta_p^m g^-1 = delta_p^n` for some integers `m, n >= 1`.
- Normalizing `(B)_p`, as `VA` does, is the case `m = n`.
- Dilation-type germs have `m != n`. These allow `BS(1,2)`.

## Statement

Let `K <= Q^x_{>0}` be a subgroup that is not finitely generated. Let
`p_1, ..., p_r` be points with eventually periodic addresses, and let
`Gamma_i <= (G)_{p_i}` be finitely generated subgroups that commensurate
`(B)_{p_i}`. Then `Gamma_1 x ... x Gamma_r` contains no subgroup isomorphic to
`Aff_K(Q) = Q x| K`. In particular it contains none of:
- `Aff(Q)`, `SL_2(Q)`, `PSL_2(Q)`;
- `GL_n(Q)`, `SL_n(Q)`, `B_n(Q)` (`n >= 2`).

**Consequence (SingFix).** Let `M ⊆ M'` be finite subsets of `sing(G)`. Suppose
every `p in M` has an eventually periodic address and `(G)_p` is finitely
generated and commensurates `(B)_p`. Then `SingFix_G(M, M')` contains no copy of
`Aff(Q)`.
- **Hypothesis in the Theorem 2.1 regime.** `(G)_p` is finitely generated whenever
  `SingFix_G({p},{p})` is, as in the regime of arXiv:2407.03149v1 Theorem 2.1.
- **The germ map is injective.** On such a copy the map
  `Aff(Q) -> prod_{p in M} (G)_p` has as kernel a normal subgroup of the copy
  consisting of elements with no singular points. That kernel lies in `B`, and
  `B <= V` contains no `(Q,+)` (Higman, survey Theorem 4.4). Every nontrivial
  normal subgroup of `Aff(Q)` contains `Q`, so the kernel is trivial.

## What this leaves

Together with a germ-carrier theorem for `Aff(Q)` (every copy in `G` conjugate
into some `SingFix_G(M,M')`, the analogue of Theorem A of
`normal-germ-extensions-of-thompson-v-contain-no-sl3z`, which is not proved here),
this would exclude every finite germ extension of `V_{d,r}` whose singular points
have eventually periodic addresses and finitely generated commensurating germ
groups. The open escapes are:
- singular points whose addresses are not eventually periodic, where `(B)_p = 1`;
- germ groups that do not commensurate `(B)_p`;
- copies of `Aff(Q)` that lie in no `SingFix_G(M,M')`.
