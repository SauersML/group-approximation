**ESTABLISHED** for items 1–3 (lane proof, not independently reviewed). The inputs are Matte Bon
(arXiv:1801.10133v2, Thm `t-asdim`, its proof, and Cor. `c-asdim-obstruction`, read at source, TeX
in `gq/src/bh-free-57/`) and the Bell–Dranishnikov Hurewicz theorem for asymptotic dimension
(Trans. AMS 358 (2006); recalled, not re-read). **No priority claimed.**

**The question behind this node is still OPEN:** does `2V` embed in `CT(Z)`? Items 1–3 answer it for
`CT_∅(Z) = V` (no, recovering Matte Bon's `2V ↛ V`). For `CT_{3}(Z)` they reduce any embedding to a
spatial form. They leave `CT_P(Z)` with `|P| ≥ 2` untouched. Kourovka 17.58 ("Does CT(ℤ) have
subgroups of intermediate (word-) growth?", S. Kohl) stays OPEN.

## Setting

- `P` is a finite set of odd primes, `P' = P ∪ {2}`, and `k = |P'|`.
- `G_P` is the one-vertex `k`-graph groupoid on `Ẑ_P`, with `[[G_P]] = CT_P(Z)`
  (`ct-p-z-is-a-one-vertex-k-graph-full-group`).
- `G_P` is compactly generated by the single-edge pushes `x ↦ qx + t` (`q ∈ P'`, `0 ≤ t < q`). Every
  germ is `push_λ ∘ pop_μ`, of degree `d(λ) − d(μ) ∈ Z^k`.

## Statement

1. **Leaf dimension.** Every leaf of `G_P` has asymptotic dimension at most `k = |P| + 1`.
2. **Dimension count.** Let `G` be a product of `n` groupoids of irreducible non-periodic one-sided
   shifts of finite type (for example Brin's `nV`). If `n > |P| + 1`, then every homomorphism
   `A(G) → CT_P(Z)` is trivial.
   - In particular `nV` does not embed in `CT_P(Z)` for `n > |P| + 1`, so `3V ↛ CT_{3}(Z)`.
   - Since `nV` is finitely generated and `CT(Z) = ∪_P CT_P(Z)`, any copy of `nV` inside `CT(Z)`
     uses moduli with at least `n − 1` distinct odd primes.
3. **Any 2V in CT_{3}(Z) is spatial.** Let `ρ : 2V → CT_{3}(Z)` be an embedding.
   - Its support `Z` is clopen, and `ρ` extends to a continuous pseudogroup morphism
     `ρ̃ : G̃_{2V} → G̃_{3}|Z`. This is the case `r = 1`: no symmetric power is needed.
   - The spatial component `q : Z → C × C` is a continuous equivariant surjection.
   - The pushes of the two stacks go to maps `s_i = ρ̃(S^{(1)}_i)` and `t_j = ρ̃(S^{(2)}_j)` on `Z`.
     These form two commuting full binary Cuntz families of piecewise-canonical injections:
     `Z = s_0Z ⊔ s_1Z = t_0Z ⊔ t_1Z` and `s_i t_j = t_j s_i`.
   - Moreover:
     - (a) the `s_i` are not single canonical maps of a box onto sub-boxes, by the commutant lemma
       (item 3 of `ct-p-z-is-not-a-product-shift-full-group`), since otherwise `t_j = id`;
     - (b) the fixed slice is blown up: on `K = q^{-1}({0^∞} × C)`, the map `s_0` is not the
       identity.

## Proof

**Item 1.** Fix a leaf, the germs with source `y`, with the word metric in pushes and pops. Let
`δ(γ) ∈ Z^k` be the degree of the germ `γ`. It is 1-Lipschitz.

- **Normal form.** A pop of one colour after a push of another commutes past it with relabelled
  edges, by unique factorization: `pop_f(ez) = e′ pop_f(z)`. A pop after a push of the same colour
  cancels. So a word with at most `r` letters has germ `push_λ ∘ pop^κ` with `|κ|, |d(λ)| ≤ r`.
- **Ancestors.** For a level `L ∈ Z^k` and a germ `γ` with `δ(γ) ≥ L`, set
  `A_L(γ) = pop^{δ(γ) − L}(γ)`. Pops are defined at every infinite path. If `γ′ = push_λ pop^κ γ`
  and `δ(γ′) − L ≥ d(λ)`, then `A_L(γ′) = A_L(γ)`, because `pop^{d(λ)} ∘ push_λ = id`.
- **Uniformly bounded pieces.** Let `W` be an `R`-box of degrees and take `L = min W − r`. Any
  `r`-chain inside `δ^{-1}(W)` has a common `A_L`. The germs with a given `A_L` and degree in `W`
  are the `push_λ(A_L)` with `d(λ) ∈ W − L`: a bounded number, of bounded diameter. So the family
  `{δ^{-1}(B_R(m))}` has asymptotic dimension `0` uniformly, for every `R`.
- **Conclusion.** Bell–Dranishnikov gives
  `asdim(leaf) ≤ asdim(Z^k) + 0 = k`.

**Item 2.** This is Matte Bon, Cor. `c-asdim-obstruction`: if `sup_y asdim(H_y) < inf_x asdim(G_x)`
then there is no nontrivial homomorphism `A(G) → F(H)`.
- Here `H = G_P` has `sup ≤ k` by item 1.
- The leaves of `G` are products of `n` infinite branching trees (Matte Bon, proof of Cor.
  `c-SFT-prod`), so `inf = n`.
- `A(G) = D(G)` is simple, so every nontrivial homomorphism is injective. For `nV`, `A = nV`.

**Item 3.**
- **The extension.** Theorem `t-asdim` applies, since the leaves of `G_{3}` have `asdim ≤ 2` by
  item 1. Its proof yields an injective coarse map from a product of `r` leaves of `G_{2V}`, each a
  product of two trees, into a leaf of `G_{3}`. So `2r ≤ 2`, i.e. `r = 1`.
- **Surjectivity of q.** `q(Z)` is closed, nonempty and `2V`-invariant in `C × C`, hence everything.
- **The families.** A pseudogroup morphism preserves composition, inverses and disjoint unions. So
  the relations of the two push families transfer: domains `Z`, ranges `q^{-1}(C_i × C)` and
  `q^{-1}(C × C_j)`, and commutation.
- **(a).** Apply the commutant lemma to `c = t_0`, which would force `t_0 = id`. But `t_0 Z ≠ Z`.
- **(b).** Suppose `s_0 = id` on `K`.
  - For a piecewise-canonical `h`, `Fix(h) = U ⊔ E`, with `U` clopen (the pieces where `h = id`)
    and `E` finite. A slope-`1` piece is the identity or has no fixed point, and a piece of slope
    `≠ 1` has at most one fixed point.
  - For `z ∈ U`, `q(z) = q(s_0 z) = S^{(1)}_0 q(z)`, so `q(z) ∈ {0^∞} × C`. Hence `U ⊆ K`, and
    `K ∖ U ⊆ E` is finite.
  - But `Z ∖ U` is clopen, and `q(Z ∖ U) ⊇ q(Z ∖ K) = (C ∖ {0^∞}) × C`, which has closure
    `C × C`.
  - Compactness then puts `{0^∞} × C` inside `q(Z ∖ U) ∩ q(K) = q(K ∖ U)`. That set is finite,
    while `{0^∞} × C` is uncountable. Contradiction. `∎`

## What is left, sharply

- **For P = {3}.** `2V ≤ CT_{3}(Z)` iff (only-if proved above; if expected, by the normal-form
  argument for Cuntz families) some clopen `Z ⊆ Z_2 × Z_3` carries two commuting full binary Cuntz
  families of piecewise-canonical maps.
- **Forced shape of such families.**
  - The first family is genuinely piecewise.
  - Its fixed slices blow up to fibres on which the pushes act nontrivially.
  - The fibre points fixed by these pushes, apart from finitely many, lie in the interior of their
    fixed sets.
- **For |P| ≥ 2.** The dimension count allows symmetric powers `r ≤ ⌊(|P|+1)/2⌋`, so more room.

**Lesson for general BH.**
- **Dimension count.** The degree cocycle of a k-graph groupoid has ultrametric fibres, so its leaves
  have asymptotic dimension at most `k`. By Matte Bon, a full group can therefore host Brin's `nV`
  only if `k ≥ n`.
- **For CT(Z).** A copy of `nV` must use at least `n − 1` odd primes, and at the threshold every copy
  is spatial.
- **Writer questions become Cuntz questions.** For hosts of rank `k`, "does the host contain a
  writer" reduces to explicit commuting Cuntz families of its own partial maps. This is a finite
  combinatorial object that can be searched for, or excluded by a commutant computation. It is the
  right target for the open cases (`2V ≤ CT_{3}(Z)`, and 17.58's full-group route).
