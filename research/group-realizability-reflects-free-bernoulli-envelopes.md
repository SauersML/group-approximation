Call a separable finite tracial von Neumann algebra `Q` group-realisable if
it embeds unitally and trace preservingly into `L(Lambda)` for some
countable group `Lambda`.  Let `Gamma` be countable and let `I` be a
nonempty countable `Gamma`-set.  Then

```text
Q is group-realisable
 iff Q * L(Gamma) is group-realisable,                         (GRB1)

Q is group-realisable
 iff Q^(tensor I) rtimes_shift Gamma is group-realisable.      (GRB2)
```

Thus adjoining free group labels while retaining the whole payload, or
placing the payload in an untwisted Bernoulli/wreath envelope, cannot create
a group-factor host.  Each construction succeeds exactly when the original
payload already satisfies the Parseval criterion.

For the fixed non-`R^U` BCS, choose any tracial GNS algebra `Q_tau`.
Equations `(GRB1)--(GRB2)` show that free products and ordinary Bernoulli
crossed products are not special-case solutions of
`non-ru-bcs-is-satisfiable-in-a-group-factor`: group-realising either
envelope is equivalent to group-realising `Q_tau` itself.  A surviving
crossed-product construction must therefore use extra structure not
equivariantly compiled by a coordinatewise group-factor embedding, such as
a genuinely operator-valued cocycle action; but since the input coordinate
still embeds in that envelope, proving the envelope group-realisable would
already settle the original hole.

The reflection implication also holds verbatim for the weaker positive-
corner notion used by the BCS target: restricting an envelope embedding
`P->pL(Omega)p` to its payload coordinate embeds `Q` in the same corner.
The forward free/wreath compilers above are asserted only for unital
group-factor realizability; no claim that arbitrary group-factor corners
are themselves group factors is being used.
