Let `L=L_2(F_p)` have its standard grading `deg(s_i)=1`, `deg(t_i)=-1`.
For any fixed integer row weights `a_1,...,a_m`, the unital matrix ring

```text
B(a)={X in M_m(L):X_ij in L_(a_i-a_j)}                 (WOP1)
```

is locally finite. Consequently every finite subgroup-generator inventory
in `B(a)^*` belongs to a finite group. Its regular representation preserves
every nonidentity marked element and all literal group identities among
that inventory.

In particular, fix prefix depth `r>=1`, let `k=2^r`, and use weights
`(0,r,0)`. With `s_u=s_(u_1)...s_(u_r)` and
`t_u=t_(u_r)...t_(u_1)`, the following whole packet has a finite model:

```text
X_u=x_12(t_u),      Y_u=x_23(s_u),      Z=x_31(1),
x_21(s_u),         x_32(t_u),          w=x_13(1),
c_g=sum_(u,v)g_(u,v)s_u t_v,
D_g=diag(1,c_g^(-1),c_g)              (g in GL_k(F_p)). (WOP2)
```

The subgroup generated by just `X_u,Y_u,Z` is isomorphic to
`SL_(k+2)(F_p)`. Adding every displayed `D_g` still embeds the packet into
a finite matrix group, for example `GL_(k(k+2))(F_p)`. The mark `w` remains
nonidentity, with canonical regular squared HS distance two.

All asserted relations are the literal identities of these matrices.
In particular, the family `D_g` is not assumed to be a representation of
`GL_k`, and no undeclared linear action on the `Y_u` packet is imposed.

The same marked conclusion applies to an affine active corner when
`s_i,t_i` lie in `ARA`, satisfy the Leavitt relations with unit `A!=0`, and
the matrices are extended by the identity on `1-A`. It concerns the
elementary image, not local finiteness of the whole Steinberg preimage.

Thus leaving the upper triangular subgroup through the opposite mark root
alone does not close the character-PVM interface. A fixed inventory whose
homogeneous coefficient degrees admit consistent row weights still has
these exact finite marked models. Raw occurrences of incompatible degree,
or depth-changing mixed cycles, are outside the fixed-weight conclusion.

There is an exact boundary at the first two depths. In `EL_3(L)`, put

```text
G_12=<x_12(t_i), x_23(s_i),
      x_12(t_j t_i), x_23(s_i s_j), x_31(1):i,j in {0,1}>.
```

Then

```text
G_12=EL_3(L_2(F_p)).                                    (WOP4)
```

Thus the same opposite mark root is compatible with a finite group at
either single depth, but coupling depths one and two already reaches the
complete rank-three coefficient core. This is an elementary-group
generation theorem, not a stability theorem or a claim about its entire
Steinberg preimage. In particular, it does not supply a uniformly accurate
matrix-size-dependent character table for that core.
