Let `H <= GL_n(K)` be generated by `h_1, ..., h_m`, with `K` algebraic over `Q`.

1. **A number field.** The finitely many entries of the `h_i` and of their
   inverses generate a subfield `F <= K` that is finite over `Q`, of degree
   `d = [F:Q]`, and `H <= GL_n(F)`.
2. **Restriction of scalars.** Fix a `Q`-basis of `F`. Left multiplication gives
   a unital `Q`-algebra homomorphism `mu: F -> M_d(Q)`. It is injective, because
   `mu(a)` sends `1` to `a`.
3. **Matrices.** Applying `mu` entrywise gives an injective unital ring
   homomorphism `M_n(F) -> M_n(M_d(Q)) = M_(nd)(Q)`. It maps invertible matrices
   to invertible matrices, so it restricts to an injective group homomorphism
   `GL_n(F) -> GL_(nd)(Q)`.
4. **Conclusion.** `H` is isomorphic to a finitely generated subgroup of
   `GL_(nd)(Q)`. By `rational-linear-groups-satisfy-boone-higman` that subgroup
   embeds in a finitely presented simple group, and composing the embeddings
   embeds `H`. QED
