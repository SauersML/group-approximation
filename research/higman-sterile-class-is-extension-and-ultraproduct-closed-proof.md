Notation as in the target. `Hig = H4` is generated by `a, b, c, d`, and
`r_1, ..., r_4` are its four relator words. `S` is the class of groups `G` such
that every homomorphism `Hig -> G` is trivial. Since `Hig` is generated by
`a, b, c, d`, a homomorphism `psi` is trivial iff `psi(a) = psi(b) = psi(c) =
psi(d) = 1`. Every image `psi(Hig)` is a quotient of `Hig`, and it is nontrivial
iff `psi` is.

**Standard imports.**
* (I1) Higman: `Hig` is nontrivial and has no nontrivial finite quotient. This is
  also contained in `nontrivial-higman-quotients-keep-all-four-bs-subgroups`,
  since the generators of a nontrivial quotient have infinite order.
* (I2) Malcev: every f.g. subgroup of `GL_n(k)`, for any field `k`, is residually
  finite.
* (I3) For groups `G, H`, the kernel of the natural map `G * H -> G x H` is a free
  group (the Cartesian subgroup; Serre, *Trees*, I.1.3, Prop. 4).
* (I4) Hyperbolic groups and CAT(0) groups contain no `BS(1,2)`. These are the
  standard facts that `nontrivial-higman-quotients-have-tower-distorted-generators`
  already imports at statement level.
* (I5) Higman's decomposition `Hig = A *_C B`, where
  `A = <a,b,c | b^a=b^2, c^b=c^2>`, `B = <c,d,a | d^c=d^2, a^d=a^2>`, and
  `C = <a,c>` is free of rank two in both. Also `A = <a,b> *_<b> <b,c>`.

## 1. Closure

**(a) Subgroups.** If `H <= G` and `G` lies in `S`, a map `Hig -> H` composed
with the inclusion is a map to `G`, hence trivial.

**(b) Extensions.** Let `N` be normal in `G`, with `N` and `K = G/N` in `S`, and
let `psi : Hig -> G`.
* `pi o psi : Hig -> K` is trivial, so `psi(Hig) <= N`.
* Then `psi` is a map to `N`, hence trivial.

**(c) Directed unions.** Let `G = union G_lambda` be a directed union of groups
in `S`. The four elements `psi(a), ..., psi(d)` lie in a common `G_lambda`, so
`psi(Hig) <= G_lambda` and `psi` is trivial.

**(d) Direct products.** Let `G = prod_i G_i` with every `G_i` in `S`. Each
`p_i o psi` is trivial, so every coordinate of every `psi(x)` is `1`. A
residually-`S` group embeds in a product of `S`-groups, so by (a) it lies in `S`.

**(e) Algebraic ultraproducts.** Let `G = prod_omega G_i`, the quotient of
`prod G_i` by the normal subgroup of sequences equal to `1` on an
`omega`-large set, where `omega`-almost every `G_i` lies in `S`. Let
`psi : Hig -> G`.
1. Lift the four generator images to sequences `(a_i), (b_i), (c_i), (d_i)`.
2. For each relator `r_j`, the word `r_j(a_i, b_i, c_i, d_i)` equals `1` on an
   `omega`-large set `E_j`.
3. On the `omega`-large set `E = E_1 cap ... cap E_4 cap {i : G_i in S}`, the
   assignment `a -> a_i, ..., d -> d_i` defines a homomorphism `Hig -> G_i`.
4. That homomorphism is trivial, so `a_i = ... = d_i = 1` for `i` in `E`.
5. Hence `psi(a) = ... = psi(d) = 1` in `G`.

**(f) Local embeddings.** Say `G` is locally embeddable into `S` if, for every
finite `F <= G`, there are `H` in `S` and an injective `phi : F -> H` with
`phi(xy) = phi(x) phi(y)` whenever `x, y, xy` are in `F`. Let `psi : Hig -> G`.
Put `x_1 = psi(a), ..., x_4 = psi(d)`. Let `F` contain:
* `1`;
* the `x_k^(+-1)`;
* the values in `G` of all prefixes of the four relators.

Choose `phi : F -> H` as above.
* `phi(1) = phi(1) phi(1)` gives `phi(1) = 1`.
* `phi(x) phi(x^-1) = phi(1) = 1` gives `phi(x^-1) = phi(x)^-1`.
* Induction on prefix length shows that the value in `H` of a prefix `p` at
  `(phi(x_1), ..., phi(x_4))` is `phi(psi(p))`. Write `p = p' y`, with `y` a
  generator or inverse; the elements `psi(p')`, `psi(y)` and `psi(p)` all lie
  in `F`.
* So each relator evaluates to `phi(psi(r_j)) = phi(1) = 1` in `H`.
* Hence `a -> phi(x_1), ..., d -> phi(x_4)` is a homomorphism `Hig -> H`, which
  is trivial.
* So `phi(x_k) = 1 = phi(1)`, and injectivity gives `x_k = 1`.

**(g) Wreath products.** `L wr_X K = (+)_X L x| K`.
* The base `(+)_X L` is the directed union of the finite products `L^Y`, `Y`
  finite. These lie in `S` by (d), so the base lies in `S` by (c).
* Then (b) applies.

**(h) Free products.** Let `G, H` lie in `S`. There is an exact sequence
`1 -> W -> G * H -> G x H -> 1`.
* `G x H` lies in `S` by (d).
* `W` is free by (I3). Free groups are residually finite, and so lie in `S`
  by the finite case of Section 3 and (d, a).
* Then (b) applies.

## 2. Non-closure

* **Quotients.** `F_4` lies in `S`: it is residually finite, so apply Section 3
  and (d, a). But `F_4 ->> Hig`, and `Hig` is not in `S`, because the identity
  map is nontrivial by (I1).
* **Amalgams.** `BS(1,2)` is metabelian, hence elementary amenable, hence in `S`
  (Section 3). By (I5), `A = BS(1,2) *_Z BS(1,2)` and `Hig = A *_(F_2) B`, with
  `B` isomorphic to `A` by the cyclic shift of generators.
  * If `A` is not in `S`, the first amalgam leaves `S`.
  * If `A` is in `S`, then so is `B`, and the second amalgam leaves `S`.

  Either way `S` is not closed under amalgamated products over free subgroups.

## 3. Base members

* **The class `D`.** By item 2 of
  `nontrivial-higman-quotients-are-not-locally-elementary-amenable` applied to
  `Q = Hig`, every map from `Hig` to a group in `D` is trivial. That claim also
  proves that `D` contains every finite, residually finite and elementary amenable
  group.
* **`BS(1,2)`-free groups.** Let `G` have no subgroup isomorphic to `BS(1,2)`.
  If `psi : Hig -> G` were nontrivial, then `psi(Hig)` would be a nontrivial
  quotient of `Hig`. By `nontrivial-higman-quotients-keep-all-four-bs-subgroups`
  it contains four copies of `BS(1,2)`, a contradiction. This covers several
  cases.
  * *Periodic groups.* `BS(1,2)` has elements of infinite order: the
    homomorphism `BS(1,2) -> Aff(Q)`, with `x -> (t -> t+1)` and
    `s -> (t -> t/2)`, sends `x` to an element of infinite order.
  * *Groups of locally subexponential growth,* meaning that every f.g. subgroup
    has subexponential growth. This includes nilpotent groups and groups of
    intermediate growth, because a f.g. subgroup `H` of a f.g. group `G`
    satisfies `B_H(r) <= B_G(Cr)`. `BS(1,2)` has exponential growth:
    * `u = s^-1` and `v = s^-1 x` act on `Q` as `t -> 2t` and `t -> 2t + 2`;
    * a positive word of length `n` in `u, v` acts as `t -> 2^n t + k`;
    * the binary digits of `k/2` record the word, so distinct words give
      distinct elements;
    * so the free monoid on `u, v` injects.
  * *Abelian groups.* `BS(1,2)` is not abelian. Directly, `Hig` is perfect,
    since `a^d = a^2` gives `a = [a, d]`.
  * *Hyperbolic and CAT(0) groups,* by (I4).
* **Low-distortion groups.** Let every f.g. subgroup `Gamma` of `G` have the
  property that each infinite cyclic subgroup has distortion bounded by an
  iterated exponential of fixed height. By the Consequence in
  `nontrivial-higman-quotients-have-tower-distorted-generators`, applied with
  `Gamma = <psi(a), ..., psi(d)>`, every `psi` is trivial. Finite-order
  generators are covered by the same statement.
* **Linear groups.** Let `psi : Hig -> GL_n(k)`. Then `psi(Hig)` is f.g. and
  linear, hence residually finite by (I2). It is a quotient of `Hig`, so it has
  no nontrivial finite quotient by (I1). A residually finite group with no
  nontrivial finite quotient is trivial.

## 4. Consequences

* **LEF groups.** Each is locally embeddable into finite groups, so it lies in
  `S` by (f). The same holds for groups locally embeddable into elementary
  amenable groups.
* **Residually linear groups and `prod U(k_n)`** lie in `S` by (d, a).
* **Ultraproducts of finite or linear groups** lie in `S` by (e).
* **Juschenko--Monod groups.** By Grigorchuk--Medynets, as recorded in
  `residually-finite-minimal-actions-have-lef-full-groups`, `[[T]]` is LEF for
  every Cantor minimal system. So `[[T]]` and its subgroups lie in `S`, including
  `[[T]]'`. Those are the f.g. infinite simple amenable groups of Juschenko--Monod
  when `T` is a minimal subshift.
* **Ma's theorem and the LRF claim.** For `[[alpha]]` with `alpha` minimal,
  topologically free and residually finite, `[[alpha]]` is LEF by Ma's Theorem C
  (same citation). The groups of
  `lrf-cantor-actions-give-lef-full-and-elementary-groups` are LEF by that claim.
* **Nekrashevych's simple groups.** Nekrashevych (*Palindromic subshifts and
  simple periodic groups of intermediate growth*, Ann. of Math. 187 (2018)) gives
  f.g. infinite simple groups that are periodic. They lie in `S` by the periodic
  case, and so does every group of intermediate growth.

## 5. (NAQ) and (HMF5) as sterility statements

* **(NAQ).** Amenability passes to quotients.
  * If some amenable `G` is not in `S`, a nontrivial `psi : Hig -> G` has an
    amenable, nontrivial image, which is a quotient of `Hig`.
  * Conversely, a nontrivial amenable quotient `Q` is an amenable group not in
    `S`, through the quotient map.
* **(HMF5).** By item 1 of `hmf5-forces-higman-to-have-no-amenable-quotient`,
  (HMF5) is equivalent to (HMF4): every homomorphism from `Hig` to
  `U_(k) = prod_n U(k_n) / N_(k)` is trivial, for every `(k_n)`. That is the
  statement that `U_(k)` lies in `S`.
  * `prod_n U(k_n)` lies in `S` by the linear case and (d).
  * `N_(k)` is a subgroup of it, so it lies in `S` by (a).
* **Sofic quotients.**
  * A countable nontrivial sofic quotient of `Hig` embeds into some metric
    ultraproduct `prod_omega (Sym(n_i), d_Hamming)` (Elek--Szabo). So that group
    is not in `S`.
  * Conversely, a nontrivial map into such an ultraproduct has a countable image.
    Countable subgroups of these ultraproducts are sofic, so the image is a
    nontrivial sofic quotient.
  * Each such ultraproduct is a quotient of `prod Sym(n_i)`, which is in `S` by
    the finite case and (d).

## 6. Residual (NAQ)

Let `Q_0` be a nontrivial amenable quotient of `Hig`.
* `Q_0` is f.g. By Zorn's lemma it has a maximal proper normal subgroup. The
  quotient `Q` by it is f.g., simple and amenable, and it is infinite by (I1).
* `Q` is not in `S`, since the quotient map `Hig -> Q` is nontrivial.
* Let `S_0` be the smallest class that contains the base members of Section 3 and
  is closed under (1a)-(1h). Then `S_0 <= S`, because `S` is such a class.
* By (f), `Q` is not locally embeddable into `S`, hence not into `S_0`. By (a),
  `Q` is not a subgroup of any group listed in Section 4.
* The four `BS(1,2)` copies and the tower-distorted generators are the two cited
  claims applied to `Q`.
* The Juschenko--Monod exclusion already follows from Grigorchuk--Medynets and
  item 3 of `nontrivial-higman-quotients-are-not-locally-elementary-amenable`,
  since a nontrivial Higman quotient is not LEF. This route makes that explicit
  and extends it to the non-LEF members of `S_0`, such as periodic and
  intermediate-growth simple groups and ultraproducts.

## 7. The class kill

This follows the operation tree of a target `G` in `S_0`.
* A nontrivial `psi : Hig -> G` at an extension node gives, by the proof of (b),
  a nontrivial map to the normal subgroup or to the quotient.
* At a product node it gives a nontrivial map to one factor (d). At an
  ultraproduct node, to `omega`-almost every factor (e). At a directed union, to
  one member (c).
* At a local-embedding node it gives a nontrivial map to one approximating group
  (f). At a wreath or free-product node, to the base, the top or the free
  Cartesian subgroup (g, h).
* The closure `S_0` is reached by transfinite iteration of these operations.
  Minimality of `S_0` together with the closure of `S` formalizes the
  induction: `S_0 <= S`.
* At the leaves, Section 3 kills the map.

The operations excluded in Section 2, together with metric ultraproducts and
coronas (Section 5), are exactly the quotient steps. Section 5 shows that (NAQ),
sofic quotients and (HMF5) each live at such a step.
