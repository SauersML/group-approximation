Let `p` be a prime. For a group `G` generated by a finite set, let
`def_p(G)` be the supremum over presentations `(X,R)` of `G` with `X` finite
and `R` arbitrary of `|X| - 1 - sum_(r in R) p^(-nu_p(r))`. Here `nu_p(r)` is
the largest `k` such that `r` is a `p^k`-th power in `F(X)`. This is the
normalization of `fpbs-power-p-deficiency-schreier-bound`.

**Theorem.** If `G` is finitely presented and `def_p(G) > 0`, then `G` is
`p`-large and contains elements of infinite order. So every finitely
presented torsion group has `def_p(G) <= 0` for every prime `p`. In
particular every finite presentation `(X,R)` of a torsion group satisfies
`sum_(r in R) p^(-nu_p(r)) >= |X| - 1`.

**What it fences for Zaremsky Problem 1.11.** Positive power `p`-deficiency is
a known source of infinite torsion groups: Schlage-Puchta's infinite finitely
generated `p`-groups, which use infinitely many relators. No finitely
presented group with positive power `p`-deficiency is torsion, even counting
presentations with infinitely many relators. So those constructions give only
infinitely presented groups, and a finitely presented torsion answer to 1.11
has nonpositive `p`-deficiency at every prime.

**Credit and prior work.** Button–Thillaisundaram, *Applications of
p-deficiency and p-largeness*, arXiv:1007.2845, abstract: "a group having a
finite presentation with p-deficiency greater than 1 is large, which implies
that Schlage-Puchta's infinite finitely generated p-groups are not finitely
presented". So the fence against Schlage-Puchta's groups is theirs. Their
normalization of `p`-deficiency was not checked here, so the threshold `> 0`
above is not compared with their `> 1`. This node relies only on the two
imports named in its proof route, as recorded on main. No novelty is claimed.

**Model tests.**
- `Z/p * Z/p = <a, b | a^p, b^p>` with `p >= 3`: `def_p >= 2 - 1 - 2/p > 0`.
  The group is virtually nonabelian free, so it is `p`-large and not torsion.
- `Z/2 * Z/2` at `p = 2`: this presentation gives `1 - 1/2 - 1/2 = 0`. The
  theorem is silent, and the infinite dihedral group is not large.
- `A_5 = <a, b | a^2, b^3, (ab)^5>` at `p = 2`: this presentation gives
  `1 - 1/2 - 1 - 1 < 0`, consistent with a finite group.
