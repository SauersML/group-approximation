title: "Type F_infinity does not force a solvable word problem, so unsolvability alone does not rule out an F_infinity host"
---

There is a group of type `F_∞`, indeed of type `F`, whose word problem is
unsolvable. So "`G` has unsolvable word problem" does not imply "`G` has no
host of type `F_∞`" (or of type `F_{n+1}`). Such a group hosts itself.

Source: Zaremsky's list, note to Problem 1.2: "Xiaolei Wu pointed out a result
of Collins and Miller producing a group of type F with unsolvable word
problem." The Collins--Miller paper was not re-read at the source on
2026-09-13.

**What this does not exclude.** If `H` hosts `G`, the word problem of `G`
many-one reduces to that of `H`. A finer obstruction by degree is still open:
a type-`F_n` group whose word problem has a degree no group of type `F_{n+1}`
reaches. The sharp form is the route
`no-type-fn-plus-1-host-via-word-problem-degree`, whose premise is
`some-type-fm-level-has-no-complete-word-problem`.

Correction note (2026-09-13): the first version said that word-problem
complexity cannot obstruct such an embedding at all. The proof gives only the
weaker statement above (review
