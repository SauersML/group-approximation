**ESTABLISHED** (route `hilbert-hotel-radical-smallest-quotient-proof`, artifact section 1).

Let `R` be a unital associative ring. Call `(s,t)` a one-sided pair when `ts = 1`,
and `e = 1 - st` its defect; `R` is directly finite exactly when every defect is `0`.
Let `d(R)` be the two-sided ideal generated by all defects, and define

```text
hh_0 = 0,     hh_(a+1)/hh_a = d(R/hh_a),     hh_l = union of hh_a for a < l.
```

The chain increases, so it stabilises; call its value `hh(R)`, the **Hilbert-hotel radical**.

1. `R/hh(R)` is directly finite, and `hh(R) = 0` iff `R` is directly finite.
2. `hh(R) <= I` for every two-sided ideal `I` with `R/I` directly finite. So `hh(R)` is the
   smallest such ideal.
3. If `f : R ->> R'` is surjective then `f(hh(R)) <= hh(R')`. (`hh` is not functorial for
   injections: directly finite rings embed in rings that are not.)
4. `hh(R) = R` iff `R` has no nonzero directly finite quotient.
5. Taking defects in every `M_m(R)` gives `shh(R)`, the smallest ideal with stably finite
   quotient, and `hh(R) <= shh(R)`.

The point of the definition is that it is intrinsic: no dynamics, no groupoid, and no choice of a
one-sided pair. Its two extreme values are the two calibrating rings of the UN program, `hh = R`
for `L_k(1,m)` and `hh = 0` for `LC(X,F_q) ⋊ Z`.
