## 1. The faithful half-line model

Let `V` have basis `e_0,e_1,...` over `F_2`.  Represent `J`
faithfully by

```text
S e_r=e_(r+1),
T e_0=0,             T e_(r+1)=e_r.
```

Then `TS=1`, `Q=1-ST` is the projection onto `F_2 e_0), and

```text
E_(r,s)=S^r Q T^s,          r,s>=0,                    (JWS2)
```

are the matrix units on `V).  Their span `I=JQJ` is the ring of
finite-support matrices.  The standard normal form in the
Toeplitz--Jacobson algebra gives

```text
J/I isomorphic to F_2[z,z^(-1)],                        (JWS3)
```

with `S |-> z` and `T |-> z^(-1)`.

Apply `EL_28`.  Surjectivity follows because every Laurent coefficient
has a lift in `J), so every elementary generator downstairs has a lift.
If `g` is in the kernel, every entry of `g-I_28` belongs to `I).
In the faithful action on `V^(28)), the operator `g-I` is therefore
supported on a finite-dimensional coordinate subspace.  Hence

```text
ker(EL_28(J)->EL_28(A)) <= GL_fin(V^(28)).               (JWS4)
```

Conversely, every finitary transvection on the flattened basis belongs to
`EL_28(J)`.  If its source and target have different outer coordinates,
this is immediate from the matrix units in `(JWS2)`.  If they have the
same outer coordinate, choose a third flattened basis vector in another
outer coordinate and use

```text
[I+E_(alpha,gamma), I+E_(gamma,beta)]
   = I+E_(alpha,beta).                                  (JWS5)
```

Over `F_2`, every finite invertible matrix has determinant one, and the
stable finite general linear group is generated by these transvections.
This proves the reverse inclusion in `(JWS4)` and hence the exact sequence
`(JWS1)`.  It also proves local finiteness of `L): a finite subset has
one common finite coordinate support and lies in one finite general linear
group.

## 2. The Laurent quotient is residually finite

The balanced-actor generation theorem says that `G` is generated by the
finite constant group `GL_28(F_2)` and one actor.  Its image therefore
finitely generates `EL_28(A)`.  This quotient embeds in

```text
GL_28(F_2(z)).
```

A finitely generated linear group over a field is residually finite:
put the finitely many matrix coefficients and inverse determinants in a
finitely generated coefficient ring, and reduce modulo a finite-index
maximal ideal which does not kill the chosen nonzero matrix entry.  Thus
the quotient in `(JWS1)` is residually finite.

## 3. Weak soficity

A locally finite group is amenable and therefore weakly sofic.  Theorem 1.1
of Lev Glebsky, *Extension of a residually finite group by a weakly sofic
group is weakly sofic*, states precisely that if

```text
1 -> H -> E -> R -> 1
```

has weakly sofic `H` and residually finite `R`, then `E` is weakly
sofic.  Applying it to `(JWS1)` proves that `G` is weakly sofic.

Primary source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08631

This theorem deliberately lands in weak soficity.  It does not turn the
extension into a permutation approximation or a normalized-HS
approximation.

## 4. Exact finite-dimensional representations kill the kernel

The finite-quotient invisibility theorem kills one nonidentity head root
in every homomorphism from `G` to a finite group.  Its scalar permutation
conjugates and Steinberg commutators generate all finitary transvections.
Equivalently, the nontrivial intersection of a finite-quotient kernel with
the simple stable group `L` is all of `L`.  Therefore

```text
every finite quotient of G kills L.                     (JWS6)
```

Let `rho:G->U(d)` be an exact finite-dimensional unitary representation.
Its image is a finitely generated linear group over `C`, hence is
residually finite by the same specialization argument.  If
`rho(ell)!=1` for some `ell in L`, a finite quotient of `rho(G)`
would separate `rho(ell)` from the identity.  Composing with `rho`
would contradict `(JWS6)`.  Thus

```text
rho(L)=1.                                                (JWS7)
```

In particular, every exact finite-dimensional representation factors
through the Laurent symbol quotient.

## 5. Why the standard closure argument stops

The extension in `(JWS1)` has amenable kernel and residually finite
quotient.  This is the amenable-by-sofic direction, not the established
sofic-by-amenable permanence direction.  Section 4.4 of Arzhantseva,
Berlai, Finn-Sell and Glebsky, *Unrestricted wreath products and sofic
groups*, records that amenable-by-sofic metric approximation is open and
that even a finite cyclic kernel is unresolved.

Primary source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04688

Accordingly neither `(JWS1)`, local finiteness of `L`, nor `(JWS7)`
decides hyperlinearity.  They locate the exact discontinuity a successful
proof must exploit: the regular group trace sees every nonidentity head
root with trace zero, while every exact finite-dimensional representation
sets that same root equal to the identity.
