Let `R_0` be a finitely presented unital ring with decidable equality.  There
is a finitely presented unital ring `R_0 <= L` with `R_0 -> L` injective and
`L` simple.

Equivalently, in the form the construction wants: an effective process that
enumerates nonzero elements `r_i` of the successively enlarged rings `R_i`
and makes each one full can be compiled into **one** finitely presented
extension of `R_0`, with the original coefficient ring provably still
embedded.

Open.  This is the ring-side analogue of Higman's embedding theorem, and no
such theorem is known to this audit.

## Why the two halves of the statement are the same problem

Each sandwich adjunction is cheap on its own — that is
`universal-sandwich-ring-is-finitely-presented-and-injective`, one relation
per element.  The difficulty is that the requirements are not a fixed list.
Adjoining `p, q` with `p r_i q = 1` enlarges the ring, and the enlarged ring
has nonzero elements that were not in the original enumeration; simplicity
demands those be made full too.  So the process is a genuine recursion, not a
finite conjunction, and "finitely many relations in total" is a compression
claim about the recursion itself.

## Why this repository's compressor machinery does not already supply it

`GroupApproximation/Leavitt/ShiftEndomorphism.lean`,
`RankFourCompressors.lean` and `UniversalRankFour.lean` formalize a
self-similar shift/compressor pattern in which one fixed endomorphism moves a
piece of algebraic data through infinitely many disjoint corners.  That looks
tailor-made for this.  It is not, for a precise reason:

* a compressor finitely controls a family **already generated by a fixed
  self-similar rule**, `x, sigma(x), sigma^2(x), …`;
* an arbitrary recursive enumeration `r_0, r_1, …` does not satisfy
  `r_{i+1} = sigma(r_i)` for any pre-existing algebra endomorphism `sigma`,
  and *declaring* such identities encodes the enumeration algorithm into the
  algebra rather than compressing it.

This is a statement about the scope of the existing machinery, **not** a
theorem that no compression exists.  The gap it names is exactly a
Higman-style simulation theorem for rings, which is why the node is stated
positively and left open rather than recorded as an obstruction.

## Attempts

1. **Iterated universal sandwiches.**  *Dies on the recursion*, as above: the
   ring grows under the construction and each round creates fresh
   requirements.  Nothing here bounds the number of rounds or the relations
   they consume.
2. **The shift/compressor as a fullness compressor.**  *Dies at the
   self-similarity hypothesis*, as above.  Deferred rather than refuted; a
   construction that first *arranges* the requirements into a self-similar
   family and then applies the compressor is not excluded by anything found
   here, and is the most concrete thing to try next.
3. **Take a maximal ideal of the finitely presented sandwich ring.**  *Dies
   at the same place as the group version.*  A maximal two-sided ideal
   `I ⊂ U` gives a simple `U/I`, but finite presentability of `U/I` is
   equivalent to finite generation of `I` as a two-sided ideal, which is the
   ring analogue of
   `fp-quotient-iff-kernel-finitely-normally-generated` and
   is no more available here than there.
4. **Deny that effectivity is the obstacle** — worth recording because it
   removes a plausible excuse.  By Clapham's theorem a finitely generated
   group with solvable word problem embeds in a *finitely presented* group
   with solvable word problem, so the coefficient ring can be taken to be
   `Z[H]` with `H` finitely presented and decidable.  Then `R_0` is finitely
   presented as a unital ring and has decidable equality, and the simple core
   already lies in `[H,H]`.  So the difficulty is not recognizing which
   elements are nonzero; it is that new ones keep appearing.

## A near miss worth knowing about

The Chatterji--Kassabov device does reach a simple ring containing the
coefficients: for `W` of infinite dimension `c`, the quotient of `End_F(W)`
by the operators of rank `< c` is simple, by the same sandwich argument
applied to any rank-`c` representative, and the amplified coefficient algebra
survives in it because its nonzero elements act with rank `c`.  What that
quotient lacks is finite generation and finite presentation — which is this
claim, again.
