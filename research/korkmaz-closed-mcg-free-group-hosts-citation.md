Citation import. Source read on 2026-09-16 from the arXiv PDF by text extraction,
which drops some symbols; they are restored by hand below. M. Korkmaz,
*Low-dimensional linear representations of mapping class groups*,
arXiv:1104.4816v2 (2 Aug 2011), Section 1:

> Theorem 5. Let g ≥ 2 and let S be a closed orientable surface of genus g. Let n be
> a positive integer with n ≤ 2g − 1. Let H denote either of Aut(F_n) or Out(F_n)
> and let φ : Mod(S) → H be a homomorphism. Then the image of φ is (1) trivial if
> g ≥ 3, and (2) a quotient of Z_10 if g = 2.

Referee recheck (2026-09-16): the quotation matches the statement `thm:autF-n` in the
arXiv e-print TeX source of v2 (`LinRepMCG.tex`) word for word. For `g ≥ 3` the proof
in Section 6 there is short: Theorem 1 of the paper (for `g ≥ 3` and `n ≤ 2g − 1`
every homomorphism `Mod(S) → GL(n, C)` is trivial; for `g ≥ 3` this is due to
Franks--Handel, arXiv:1102.4584, whose abstract states it) makes the composite to
`GL(n, Z)` trivial; the kernels `IA_n` of `Aut(F_n) → GL(n, Z)` and `IA_n/Inn(F_n)` of
`Out(F_n) → GL(n, Z)` are torsion-free (Baumslag--Taylor, as cited there); and `Mod(S)`
is generated by torsion elements (McCarthy--Papadopoulos and others, as cited there).
The journal publication status of Korkmaz's paper was not rechecked.

The proof is Section 6 of that paper. The same page defines `Mod(S)` as "the group
of isotopy classes of orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphisms of S", which is
`MCG(S_g)` here, and records "the classical representation of Mod(S) onto the
symplectic group Sp(2g, Z)".

**Items 1 and 2.** Theorem 5 for `n ≥ 1`. For `n = 0`, `Aut(F_0)` and `Out(F_0)` are
trivial.

**Item 3.** `Sp(2g, Z)` is infinite: with symplectic form `ω` and integral vectors
`e, f` satisfying `ω(e, f) = 1`, the transvection `τ(v) = v + ω(e, v) e` preserves
`ω` and the lattice, and `τ^k(f) = f + k e`, so `τ` has infinite order. Hence
`MCG(S_g)` is infinite and an injective homomorphism has infinite image, which items
1 and 2 exclude when `n ≤ 2g − 1`.
