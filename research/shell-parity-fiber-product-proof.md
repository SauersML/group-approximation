Every generator of Q either preserves parity eventually (rho_g) or
swaps it eventually (tau). These alternatives are disjoint and
compose by addition modulo two. They give a surjective homomorphism
Q->Z/2. Its kernel Q_0 has index two. Splitting N into evens 2n and
odds 2n+1 identifies the parity-preserving near permutation group
with NearSym(N) times NearSym(N), using n as coordinate on each part.
Composition is from right to left.

In these coordinates the Schreier generators of Q_0 are

    rho_g=(lambda_g,1),
    tau*rho_g*tau^-1=(1,lambda_g),
    tau^2=(s,s).

These formulas hold as near permutations; the finite missing domain
of a shift inverse does not affect a germ. Both factors lie in R_nu,
and their indices are equal for every displayed generator. Thus
Q_0 lies in F_nu.

Let L be the normal closure of lambda(P) in R_nu. Since R_nu is
generated by lambda(P) and s, the quotient R_nu/L is cyclic, generated
by sL. Near index vanishes on L and sends s to 1, so this cyclic
quotient is infinite and L=ker(eta). More explicitly L is generated
by all s^k*lambda_g*s^-k, k in Z, g in P: their subgroup contains
lambda(P), is invariant under s and s^-1, and hence is normal in
the generated group R_nu.

Conjugating the first two Schreier families by powers of (s,s)
shows that Q_0 contains L times {1} and {1} times L. Every pair
(a,b) with eta(a)=eta(b)=k has the form

    (a*s^-k,b*s^-k)*(s,s)^k,

where the first pair belongs to L times L. Hence Q_0=F_nu.
The identification is faithful because a parity-preserving near
permutation is trivial precisely when both restrictions are trivial.

For completeness the remaining generator acts by

    tau*(a,b)*tau^-1=(s*b*s^-1,a),       tau^2=(s,s).

Thus the index-two extension is specified, not merely its subgroup.
Finite presentation passes both to finite-index subgroups and to
finite-index overgroups, proving Q finitely presented iff F_nu is.

Suppose now P has a finite generating set S. The first-coordinate
projection F_nu->R_nu is onto, since every (r,r) is in F_nu. Its
kernel is {1} times L. It is normally generated in F_nu by the
finite family (1,lambda_g), g in S: conjugation by the diagonal
copy of R_nu produces the whole normal closure {1} times L.
Consequently a finite presentation of F_nu yields one of R_nu by
adding these finitely many relators. The required simple-kernel
claim supplies A_1 finitely presented implies Q finitely presented,
giving the final chain of necessary conditions.

## An exact finite set of mixed commutators

Assume P is generated by a finite S and R_nu is finitely presented.
The shift generates an infinite cyclic subgroup, so the amalgam

    T = R_nu *_<s> R_nu

is finitely presented: take two finite presentations of R_nu and
one relation identifying the words representing s. Since
R_nu=L semidirect <s>, the amalgam identifies with

    (L_1 * L_2) semidirect <s>,

where s acts by the corresponding conjugation on both factors.
One can verify the identification by the universal properties:
each R_nu=L_i semidirect <s> maps into this group, agreeing on s;
conversely the two L_i and the common s generate T with exactly
these conjugation relations.

The map T->F_nu takes the first factor element r to
(r,s^eta(r)) and the second to (s^eta(r),r). Its kernel is the
normal closure of all cross-factor commutators [L_1,L_2]. The
quotient is (L_1 times L_2) semidirect <s>, exactly F_nu.
Each L_i is generated by the s^j*lambda_g^(i)*s^-j, g in S,
j in Z. Conjugating commutators of two such generators by s^-j
shows that this kernel is normally generated by the set D of

    [lambda_g^(1),s^k*lambda_h^(2)*s^-k], g,h in S, k in Z.

Here normal closure supplies commutation of arbitrary products and
inverses once all these pairs of generators commute.

If some finite band |k|<=B normally generates the kernel, adding
those finitely many relators to T gives a finite presentation of
F_nu. Conversely, if F_nu is finitely presented, the kernel of
the epimorphism from the finitely presented T is finitely normally
generated. Express each of its finitely many normal generators
as a finite product of conjugates of elements of D and their
inverses. Only finitely many integers k occur in these expressions.
Choose B bounding their absolute values. The relators in that band
normally generate the full kernel, proving the equivalence.

Thus the fiber-product condition asks for finite normal control of
all mixed commutators. It cannot be replaced by testing their images
in the actual near action: every one of them already vanishes in
that action, regardless of whether a finite band implies the rest.
