**ESTABLISHED.**  Let `G=<X|R>` be finitely presented, let `b in G` have
finite order at least two, fix a word `beta` representing `b`, and let

```text
H <= C_G(b).                                             (UCF1)
```

The following are equivalent.

1. `H` is finite.
2. There is a choice of word `eta_h` representing every `h in H` and a
   constant `C` such that

```text
Area_R([beta,eta_h])<=C             for every h in H.   (UCF2)
```

In fact, if `H` is infinite, then for **every** choice of representatives,

```text
sup_(h in H) Area_R([beta,eta_h])=infinity.             (UCF3)
```

Thus a bounded-stabilizer filling hypothesis is not a consequence of finite
generation, bounded generation, or Kazhdan property `(T)`.  These control
how elements or almost-invariant vectors are assembled; `(UCF2)` asks for a
uniform number of ambient defining-relator cells, which is strictly stronger.

For a concrete simultaneous firewall, take

```text
G=C_m times SL_3(Z),       b=(generator of C_m,1),
H={1} times SL_3(Z).                                  (UCF4)
```

The stabilizer `H` is infinite, finitely presented, has property `(T)`, and
is boundedly generated by its elementary root subgroups.  Nevertheless
`(UCF3)` holds for every finite presentation of `G` and every word section of
`H`.  Consequently neither a Kazhdan constant nor a bounded-generation width
can replace the uniform centralizer-area certificate needed by a literal
van-Kampen-to-Hilbert--Schmidt transport argument.

There is no conflict with analytic site coherence.  Same-dimension stability
can first replace a microstate by an exact representation; property `(T)` can
then project a lamp into the exact stabilizer commutant, as in
`hs-stable-kazhdan-finite-bi-index-pair-gives-site-coherence`.  That mechanism
does not fill all words `[beta,eta_h]` with bounded area and therefore evades
`(UCF3)`.
