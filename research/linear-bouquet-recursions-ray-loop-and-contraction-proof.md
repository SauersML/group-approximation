**Status: ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** Items 1–5 and Lemma R are proved by hand from finite
data. The finite data (item 1's closure, the nucleus of `B`, the loop set `L`, and the
finiteness and infinite-order certificates) are exact computations, explained in §6.
Notation is that of the claim. Actions are on the right, and `(gh)|_v = g|_v · h|_{v^g}`.

## 0. The recursions in the Nielsen basis

- `s = π_s (A s B, α, β, t, 1, …)` and `t = π_t (C t D, 1, 1, 1, α, 1, …)`, with rooted
  words `A, B, C, D ∈ {1, α^{±1}, β^{±1}}`.
- In the bouquet class, `z := 0^{π_A^{-1}} = 0^{π_C^{-1}}`. The words `S = A s B` and
  `T = C t D` have roots `σ = π_A π_s π_B` and `τ = π_C π_t π_D`, both fixing `z`. Also
  `S|_z = S` and `T|_z = T` (item 5 of the parent).
- Rooted letters have trivial sections. So for `y ≠ z` we have `S|_y = s|_{y^{π_A}}`, which
  lies in `{1, α, β, t}`, and `T|_y = t|_{y^{π_C}}`, which lies in `{1, α}`.
  - The nontrivial ones sit at `y_1, y_2, y_3 = 1^{π_A^{-1}}, 2^{π_A^{-1}}, 3^{π_A^{-1}}`
    (carrying `α, β, t`) and at `y_4 = 4^{π_C^{-1}}` (carrying `α`).
- `G = ⟨α, β, s, t⟩ = ⟨α, β, S, T⟩`, and `B := ⟨α, β, t⟩ = ⟨α, β, T⟩`.
  - `B` is generated by a bounded automaton, because `t` has rooted non-loop sections.
    So `B` is contracting (Bondarenko–Nekrashevych). Its nucleus is also certified in §6.
  - Put `F = ⟨σ, τ⟩ ≤ Stab_{M_11}(z)`.

## 1. Hypotheses and the loop subgroup (item 1)

**(F)** holds because every generator is finite-state.

**(Ω) and (E).** Let `W` be the letterwise section closure of the words
`a^{±1}, e^{±1}, S^{±1}, T^{±1}`. It is finite by unit weight.
- Along a ray `v c^∞`, the word sections of a generator eventually follow the map
  `u ↦ u|_c` on `W`.
- (E) at the class of `c^∞` holds iff every cycle of this map is a fixed word. (Ω) holds iff
  every fixed word fixes `c`. The eventual sections are then the fixed words, and the
  trivial word means a trivial section.
- `ray_loop_hyp.py` computes this map exactly for every `c ∈ X` and all 472 recursions:
  - at `c = z`, (Ω) and (E) hold, and the nonempty fixed words are exactly
    `S^{±1}, T^{±1}`;
  - at `c ≠ z`, (Ω) and (E) hold and there is no nonempty fixed word.

So `C = {S^{±1}, T^{±1}}` and `H := H_{z^∞} = ⟨S, T⟩`. The loop subgroups at `c^∞` with
`c ≠ z` are trivial. So are those at nonconstant periodic rays: by item 2 of the parent,
`Fix_v = 1` for every nonconstant `v`. The loop obstruction therefore only constrains `H`.

## 2. The embedding (item 2)

Every `h ∈ H` fixes `z` and has `h|_z = h`. This holds for `S` and `T`, and it is preserved
under products, because `(gh)|_z = g|_z h|_{z^g} = g|_z h|_z`. For `y ≠ z`, `y^g ≠ z`, so
`(gh)|_y = g|_y h|_{y^g}`. By §0, induction on word length gives `h|_y ∈ B`. Hence
`ι(h) = (π_h ; (h|_y)_{y ≠ z})` is a homomorphism `H → B ≀_{X∖z} F`.

It is injective. Suppose `π_h = 1` and `h|_y = 1` for all `y ≠ z`. Then `h` acts on `X^n`
trivially, by induction on `n`:
- on a word `y v` with `y ≠ z`, it acts as `h|_y = 1`;
- on `z v`, it acts as `h|_z = h` on `v`, which is trivial at level `n − 1`.

So `H ≅ ⟨σ a_S, τ a_T⟩` with `a_S = (α@y_1, β@y_2, t@y_3)` and `a_T = α@y_4`.

`ray_loop_hyp.py` finds that `y_4 ∉ {y_1, y_2, y_3}` in all 472 cases, so the supports of
`a_S` and `a_T` are disjoint. If `σ = τ = 1`, then `S` and `T` commute, and
`H = ⟨S⟩ × ⟨T⟩`. Moreover `S^n = 1` iff `α^n = β^n = t^n = 1`, so `ord S = lcm(55, ord t)`,
and `ord T = 11`.

## 3. Lemma C (item 3)

**Lemma C.** Let `B ≤ Aut(X*)` be self-similar, finite-state and contracting, and let `N_B` be
a finite, section-closed set that contains `b|_v` for all `b ∈ B` and all long enough `v`.
Let `S` fix `z` with `S|_z = S`, and suppose `S^{±1}|_y ∈ B` for `y ≠ z`. Put `G = ⟨B, S⟩`,
`L = {c ∈ N_B : c fixes z^p and c|_{z^p} = c for some p ≥ 1}` and `H_L = ⟨S, L⟩`.
Then `G` is contracting iff `H_L` is finite.

*Proof.* (⇒) Let `P` be a common period of the loops in `L`. Each generator of `H_L`
fixes `z^P` and is its own section there, and so, as in §2, is every `h ∈ H_L`. So
`h = h|_{z^{mP}}` for all `m`, and `H_L` lies in the nucleus of `G`. The nucleus is finite
when `G` is contracting.

(⇐) Suppose `H_L` is finite.

*A finite section-closed set.* The sections of `h ∈ H_L` at a prefix `z^j` lie in `H_L`,
because `S|_z = S` and `L|_z ⊆ L`. Its sections at other vertices lie in `B`. Let
`M = N_B · H_L · N_B`, which is finite. The sections of an element `n h n'` at a letter `x`
are products `n|_x · h|_{x'} · n'|_{x''}`, so they lie either in `M` or in a finite subset of
`B`. Since `B` is finite-state, the section closure `𝒩` of `M` is finite, and it contains
`N_B`.

*Every element eventually has sections in `𝒩`.* Write `g ∈ G` as
`g = b_0 S^{e_1} b_1 ⋯ S^{e_k} b_k` with `b_i ∈ B` and `e_i = ±1`. We use induction on
`k`, and prove that `g|_v ∈ 𝒩` for all long enough `v`. The case `k = 0` holds since
`N_B ⊆ 𝒩`.
- Computing `g|_v` letterwise gives again such a word. In it, the `i`-th `S`-letter survives
  exactly when it is read at `z^{|v|}`; otherwise it becomes an element of `B` and merges.
- All `k` letters survive iff `v^{b_0} = z^{|v|}` and `b_1, …, b_{k−1}` fix `z^{|v|}`. So at
  each level at most one such **persistent** vertex exists, namely
  `v_n = (z^n)^{b_0^{-1}}`, and these vertices form a path.
- **Case A: the path stops at some level `m`.** Every section at level `m` has fewer than
  `k` surviving `S`-letters. There are finitely many of them, so induction applies to each.
- **Case B: the path is infinite.** Then each middle `b_i` fixes `z^∞`. The sections
  `c_i(n) = b_i|_{z^n}` lie in `N_B` for large `n`, fix `z`, and satisfy
  `c_i(n+1) = c_i(n)|_z`.
  - A sequence in a finite set under a map is eventually periodic. So for large `n`,
    `c_i(n)` fixes `z^p` and returns to itself there, which means `c_i(n) ∈ L`.
  - Hence, for large `n`,
    `g|_{v_n} = (b_0|_{v_n}) · S^{e_1} c_1(n) ⋯ c_{k−1}(n) S^{e_k} · (b_k|_{z^n})`, which
    lies in `N_B · H_L · N_B = M`.
  - Choose such an `n`. The off-path vertices at level `n` have fewer surviving letters, so
    induction applies to them.
  - Every vertex below `v_n` has its section in `𝒩`, because `𝒩` is section-closed.

So all sections of `g` at long enough vertices lie in the finite set `𝒩`, and `G` is
contracting. ∎

**For the bouquets.** Take `B = ⟨α, β, t⟩` and `S = ℓ_s`; then `G = ⟨B, S⟩` by §0.
`loops_B.py` certifies a nucleus superset `N_B` (Lemma N in §6), and it finds `L = ⟨T⟩` in
all 472 cases. So `H_L = ⟨S, T⟩ = H`, and **`G` is contracting iff `H` is finite.**

## 4. The kills (item 4)

**Finite `H` kills `G`.** If `H` is finite, then `G` is contracting by Lemma C. By Lemma S of
`same-point-loop-recursions-reduce-to-loop-graph-words`, every recursion of the family is fully
self-replicating: `G = G^X ⋊ M_11` through the section map. So `G` is a contracting group that is
regular branch over itself, and it is not finitely presented (Bartholdi, as cited in
`fully-self-replicating-tree-groups-give-fg-tits-hosts`).

`H` is certified finite in two ways, and `classify.py` records which applies.
- **(a) Commuting, with `ord S < ∞`.** `R.trivial([S, T])` is exact (§6), and so is
  `exact_order` of `same_point_screen.py`. If `S` and `T` commute and both have finite order,
  then `H` is a quotient of `C_{ord S} × C_{ord T}`, so it is finite.
- **(b) A presentation certificate** (`h_present.py`). GAP computes the image `H_n` of `H` on
  level `n` together with a presentation `⟨x, y | r_1, …, r_k⟩` of `H_n` on the images of `S`
  and `T`. The script checks, by the exact test, that every `r_i(S, T)` is trivial in `G`.
  - Then `x ↦ S`, `y ↦ T` defines a surjection `φ: H_n → H`.
  - Composing with the level-`n` quotient `π_n: H → H_n` gives `π_n ∘ φ = id`, because it fixes
    the generators.
  - So `φ` is injective, `H ≅ H_n`, and `|H| = |H_n|`.

A level-`n` certificate can only succeed when `H = H_n`, so the candidates are selected first.
- `h_orders.py` (GAP orders `|H_1|, |H_2|, |H_3|`, run on part of the undecided cases) selects
  those with `|H_2| = |H_3|`.
- `h_growth.py` (orders of all cyclically reduced words of length at most 4 in `S, T` on levels
  2, 3, 4) runs on all of them. A word whose order grows from level `n` to `n + 1` proves
  `H_n ≠ H_{n+1}`, so it rules out level `n`. The cases whose screened orders are constant from
  level 2 on go to `h_present.py` at level 2. Those constant only from level 3 on, and the level-2
  failures, go to `h_present.py` at level 3, with a time limit (900 s of GAP per case), so not
  all of them were reached.

**Result** (`classify.out`).
- (a) applies to 27 recursions, with `|H|` dividing 605 (17), 2420 (9) or 9680 (1).
- (b) certifies 81 more: 50 at level 2 and 31 at level 3. Their orders are 13310, 212960,
  3221020, 12884080, 206145280, 824581120 and 103072640000.
- So 108 recursions are killed.
- Of the 132 that stay undecided:
  - 58 have `S` growing from level 3 to level 4;
  - 55 are stable from level 3 (9 failed at level 3, 46 were not reached);
  - 19 are stable from level 2 (all failed at level 2; at level 3, 9 failed and 10 were not
    reached).

The selection is a heuristic and proves nothing. Neither does a failed relator.

## 5. Infinite `H`, the germ character and the commuting survivors (item 5)

**Infinite certificates.** `h_finite.py` (words of length at most 4) and `h_inf6.py` (length 5
and 6) look for a word `w(S, T)` of infinite order. They use the power-section-graph certificate
of `same_point_screen.py`, justified in §6 of `same-point-loop-recursions-reduce-to-loop-graph-words-proof`:
a strongly connected component of the graph of power sections that contains an edge of weight
`l > 1`. For these recursions `H` is infinite, so `G` is **not contracting** (Lemma C). The
contraction kill therefore does not apply, and the loop obstruction reduces to the finite
presentability of `H`.

**Lemma G (germ character).** For all 472 recursions, `b_1(G) = b_1(H) ≤ 1`. Moreover
`b_1(G) = 1` iff `S` has infinite order in `H^{ab}`.

*Proof.*

*Upper bound.* `G^{ab}` is generated by the images of `α, β, S, T`. `M_11` is perfect, so the
images of `α` and `β` vanish. `T` has finite order (11, 22 or 44), so its image is torsion.
Hence `b_1(G) ≤ 1`. By the same argument `b_1(H) ≤ 1`.

*A homomorphism `ψ: G → Z` with `ψ(S) ≠ 0` restricts to `H`.* Since `S ∈ H`, such a `ψ` makes
the image of `S` in `H^{ab}` have infinite order.

*Conversely, suppose `φ: H → Z` has `φ(S) ≠ 0`.* Then `φ(T) = 0`, because `T` is torsion.
- **The set of rays.** Let `Ω = X* z^∞` be the set of rays cofinal with `z^∞`. It is countable
  and `G`-invariant.
- **Germs.** For `g ∈ G` and `ξ = v z^∞ ∈ Ω` there is `n_0` such that `g|_{v z^n}` is a single
  element `c(g, ξ) ∈ H` for all `n ≥ n_0`.
  - For the generators this is read off §0. `α` and `β` have germ 1.
  - For `T`: the germ is `T` at `z^∞` and 1 elsewhere. Its sections off `z` are `α` or 1, and
    those have trivial sections.
  - For `t`: the germ is `T` at `0 z^∞` and 1 elsewhere. Indeed `t|_0 = T`, the other sections
    of `t` are `α` or 1, and `T|_x ∈ {T, α, 1}`, equal to `T` only for `x = z`.
  - For `S`: the germ is `S` at `z^∞`. At rays `y_3 u` it is `c(t, u) ∈ {1, T}`, and at every
    other ray it is 1.
  - Because `c(g, ξ)` fixes `z` and satisfies `c(g, ξ)|_z = c(g, ξ)`, the sections along `ξ` stay
    equal to it. Moreover `(v z^n)^g = v' z^n` for large `n`.
  - The rule `(gh)|_u = g|_u h|_{u^g}` then gives germs for all products, together with the
    cocycle identity `c(gh, ξ) = c(g, ξ) c(h, ξ^g)`. The identity also shows that
    `{ξ : c(g, ξ) ≠ 1}` is finite for every `g`.
- **The character.** Put `λ(g) = Σ_{ξ ∈ Ω} φ(c(g, ξ))`. Since `ξ ↦ ξ^g` is a bijection of `Ω`,
  `λ(gh) = λ(g) + λ(h)`. So `λ: G → Z` is a homomorphism.
- **Its value on `S`.** By the list of germs, `λ(S) = φ(S) + (a multiple of φ(T)) = φ(S) ≠ 0`.

So `b_1(G) = 1`. ∎

**The commuting survivors.** Suppose `S` and `T` commute and `S` has infinite order.
- `H` is abelian, generated by `S` of infinite order and `T` of finite order, so
  `H ≅ Z × C_k` for some `k`. When `σ = τ = 1`, §2 gives `H = ⟨S⟩ × ⟨T⟩ ≅ Z × C_11`.
- `H` is finitely presented. So the ray-loop obstruction of
  `fp-self-similar-groups-have-fp-ray-loop-subgroups` holds and gives nothing. In particular the
  R''-type splitting `H ≅ L × C_5` with `L` of finite index in a non-fp group (as in
  `m11-odometer-free-host-is-not-finitely-presented`) does not occur.
- By Lemma G, `b_1(G) = 1`. `G` is fully self-replicating with root image `M_11 ≤ Alt(11)`
  (Lemma S of the parent). So item 3(d) of `fully-self-replicating-fp-infinity-groups-are-acyclic`,
  with `d = 11`, gives two things.
  - **`G` is not of type `FP_10`**, since that would force a finite abelianization.
  - `b_1(G) = 1` is the largest value that item allows for a finitely presented `G`. So finite
    presentability is **not** excluded.
- `G` is not contracting (Lemma C), so Bartholdi's theorem does not apply.
- `G` is amenable, so no free-subgroup argument is available either. The reason is
  activity growth:
  - `s` has one looping section, at `0`, and exits to the bounded letter `t` at `3`;
  - `t` has one looping section and otherwise rooted sections.

  So the automaton has linear activity growth, and such automaton groups are amenable
  (Amir–Angel–Virág; cited, not reproved).

These recursions survive every obstruction currently on the graph. They are candidates for
a finitely presented fully self-replicating `R`, and any such `R` has `b_1(R) = 1` and is
not `FP_10`. The non-commuting recursions with infinite `H`, and those still undecided,
remain candidates as well.

## 5a. Lemma R: the tool for the non-commuting infinite class (item 6)

**Setup.** Let `K = ker(H → F)`. Through `ι`, `K ≤ B^{X∖z}`. For `y ≠ z`, put
`Q_y = p_y(K) ≤ B`, the image under projection to coordinate `y`, and
`K_y = {k ∈ K : k|_{y'} = 1 for all y' ≠ y}`. Then `K_y ⊴ Q_y`, since conjugation by
`k ∈ K` acts on coordinate `y` by `p_y(k)`.

**Lemma R.** If `[Q_y : K_y] < ∞` and `H` is finitely presented, then `Q_y` is finitely
presented. Consequently, by `fp-self-similar-groups-have-fp-ray-loop-subgroups`, if `G` is
finitely presented then every such `Q_y` is finitely presented.

*Proof.*
- Let `K' = K ∩ p_y^{-1}(K_y)`. It has finite index in `K`, and hence in `H`.
- The map `r: K' → K_y` given by `r(k) = p_y(k)`, placed at coordinate `y`, is a homomorphism,
  because `p_y` is. It is the identity on `K_y`. So `K_y` is a retract of `K'`.
- A retract of a finitely presented group is finitely presented. So `K_y` is, and so is the
  finite-index overgroup `Q_y`. ∎

For the R''-type kill of `m11-odometer-free-host-is-not-finitely-presented`, one needs a
coordinate `y` for which `[Q_y : K_y] < ∞` and `Q_y` is known not to be finitely presented.
Both conditions concern subgroups of the contracting group `B`. Neither has been computed
here for the non-commuting infinite class, and that class is left open.

## 6. The computations

**Exact identification** (`nucleus.py`, `Elements`). Elements of `G` are reduced words.
- Two words are declared equal only when `R.trivial(u v^{-1})` holds. This test forms the
  letterwise section closure of the word, which is finite by unit weight, and checks that
  every word in it has trivial root permutation. That is a sufficient condition for
  triviality. It is also necessary, because a nontrivial root at a section is a nontrivial
  action.
- Words with different level-`L` fingerprints act differently, so they are distinct.
- Hence the section graphs built from `Elements` are the true section graphs of group
  elements.

**Lemma N (nucleus certificate).** Suppose:
- `N` is a finite section-closed set of elements containing 1;
- `Y` is a finite section-closed set containing the generators and their inverses;
- for every `n ∈ N` and `y ∈ Y`, every vertex that lies on a cycle of the (finite) section
  graph of `n y` belongs to `N`.

Then every `g ∈ G` has `g|_v ∈ N` for all long enough `v`. So `G` is contracting, with nucleus
contained in `N`.

*Proof.* Let `ℓ` be the largest number of vertices of the section graphs of the products
`n y`. We induct on the length of `g` as a product of elements of `Y`. The empty product is
`1 ∈ N`.

Suppose `g = g' y` and `g'|_v ∈ N` whenever `|v| ≥ m`. Fix `v` with `|v| = m`.
- Then `g|_v = n y'`, where `n = g'|_v ∈ N` and `y' = y|_{v^{g'}} ∈ Y`.
- Take `u` with `|u| ≥ ℓ`. The path from `n y'` along `u` visits `|u| + 1 > ℓ` vertices, so
  it repeats one, and that vertex lies on a cycle.
- So `g|_{vu} = (n y')|_u` is a section of a cycle vertex. The cycle vertex lies in `N`, and
  `N` is section-closed, so `g|_{vu} ∈ N`.

Hence `g|_w ∈ N` for all `|w| ≥ m + ℓ`. ∎

`contraction_certificate` builds `N` by saturation: it starts from the cycle vertices over `Y`
and adds the closure of any new cycle vertex until the hypothesis holds. `loops_B.py` runs it
on `B = ⟨α, β, t⟩` and then lists
`L = {c ∈ N_B : c fixes z^p and c|_{z^p} = c for some p}` by following the `z`-sections inside
`N_B`. It compares `L` with the powers of `T`.

**Tiers.**
- *Exact, and proved.* The following are exact finite computations whose correctness rests on
  the lemmas above:
  - the hypothesis check (`ray_loop_hyp.py`);
  - the supports of `S` and `T`;
  - `N_B` and `L` (`loops_B.py`);
  - commutation and finite orders;
  - the presentation certificates (`h_present.py`, given GAP's `Size` and
    `IsomorphismFpGroupByGenerators`);
  - the infinite-order certificates.
- *Heuristic.* The level orders of `h_orders.py` and `h_growth.py` only choose which recursions
  to feed to `h_present.py`. They are not used in any proof.
