**Lemma.** Let `G` be generated by finite subgroups `A_1,...,A_r`. Assume each is
simple (nonabelian simple, or cyclic of prime order), and the graph with an edge
`i -- j` whenever `A_i ∩ A_j != 1` is connected. Then every homomorphism from `G` to
any group is either trivial or injective on every `A_i`.

**Corollary.** If moreover every finite subgroup of `G` is conjugate into some `A_i`,
then

```text
G virtually torsion-free  <=>  G has a nontrivial finite quotient.
```

If `G` is infinite and has no nontrivial finite quotient, then its torsion is
invisible in every finite quotient, and `G` is neither virtually torsion-free nor
residually finite.

**Where it applies.** Take a triangle of groups with finite simple vertex groups,
nontrivial edge groups and trivial face group, over a simply connected triangle.
Assume it is nonpositively curved in the Gersten–Stallings sense: the angle sum
`π/m_1 + π/m_2 + π/m_3 <= π`, where `2 m_i` is the girth of the coset graph at vertex
`i`. It is then developable. Its fundamental group `G` is generated by the vertex
groups, which pairwise meet in the edge groups. `G` acts on a CAT(0) 2-complex with a
triangle as strict fundamental domain. A finite subgroup fixes a point and so lies in
a conjugate of a vertex group (Bridson–Haefliger, Part II, Ch. 2 fixed point theorem
and Ch. 12). When the angle sum is `< π`, `G` is word-hyperbolic.

So in this class Lück's question is a pure existence question: one nontrivial finite
quotient, no separation needed. One infinite member without nontrivial finite quotients
decides `hyperbolic-groups-virtually-torsion-free` and
`non-residually-finite-hyperbolic-group` negatively
(`hyperbolic-simple-triangle-group-without-finite-quotients`).

**Calibration.**
- `PSL(2,7) *_{S_4} PSL(2,7)` satisfies the hypotheses, is virtually free, and has
  nontrivial finite quotients.
- Dropping connectivity breaks the Lemma: `A_5 * A_5 -> A_5`, killing one factor, is
  nontrivial but not injective on both factors.
- Dropping simplicity breaks it as well: `Z/4 *_{Z/2} Z/4 -> Z/2`.

is claimed. Unreviewed.
