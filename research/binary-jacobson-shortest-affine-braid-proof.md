The Steinberg relation in characteristic two gives

```text
[g,u]=[x_31(1),x_12(1)]=x_32(1),                         (JBP1)
```

so `(JB1)` is the usual constant Weyl swap of rows `2,3`.  In the standard
row order,

```text
h=[[Q,S,0],[T,0,0],[0,0,1]],
r=[[1,0,0],[0,0,1],[0,1,0]],
h r=[[Q,0,S],[T,0,0],[0,1,0]].                           (JBP2)
```

Using

```text
Q^2=Q,       QS=0,       TQ=0,       ST+Q=1,       TS=1,
```

direct multiplication gives

```text
(h r)^2=[[Q,S,0],[0,0,1],[T,0,0]],
(h r)^3=I_3.                                              (JBP3)
```

The relation is not already an edge relation of the complete one-step
envelope.  Indeed

```text
h r h=[[Q,0,S],[0,1,0],[T,0,0]],                         (JBP4)
```

which is not in `F`: its displayed `S,T` entries do not lie in
`F_2[Q]`.  Thus `r` is not in `K=F cap hFh`.  Bass--Serre normal form now
shows that `(hr)^3` is a nontrivial cyclically reduced word in `Gamma_F`.
It has three `h`-syllables, and the cited minimum-length theorem excludes
nontrivial kernel words with fewer.

For the finite-model fence, put `B=<h,r>`.  The two generators are distinct
involutions and `hr` has order three by `(JBP2)--(JBP3)`, so

```text
B ~= S_3.                                                   (JBP5)
```

Let `L` be the finitary kernel of the Jacobson symbol map.  It is normal in
`E` and locally finite, and `C_N<=L`.  Since `B` is finite,

```text
D_N=<b C_N b^(-1):b in B>                                  (JBP6)
```

is generated by finitely many elements of the locally finite group `L`, so
it is finite.  It is normalized by `B`, and

```text
H_N=D_N B                                                   (JBP7)
```

is finite.  The packet contains the literal nontrivial head root
`w=x_13(Q)`, so the inclusion `H_N<=E` and its finite regular representation
give the advertised exact marked model.  This proves the fence without any
Property `(T)` input.
