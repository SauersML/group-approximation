## Step 1: remove all matrix sizes without changing dimension

In `(MP2)`, write `f^(j)_(ab)` for the image under the section of standard
matrix units and put

```text
p=sum_j f^(j)_(11),            B=pEp,        J=pIp.
```

Let the index `alpha=(j,a)` run through all matrix rows and set
`w_alpha=f^(j)_(a1)`.  Then

```text
w_alpha p=w_alpha,
sum_alpha w_alpha w_alpha^*=1.                           (R1)
```

The usual finite-frame map

```text
Phi:E -> M_N(B),
Phi(x)=(w_alpha^* x w_beta)_(alpha,beta)                 (R2)
```

is a *-isomorphism onto the corner `Phi(1)M_N(B)Phi(1)`.  Indeed, `(R1)`
makes `(R2)` multiplicative, and

```text
x=sum_(alpha,beta) w_alpha Phi(x)_(alpha,beta) w_beta^*
```

gives injectivity and the inverse on the range.  Hereditary-subalgebra and
matrix-amplification permanence therefore give

```text
dim_nuc(E)<=dim_nuc(B).
```

The reverse inequality holds because `B=pEp` is a hereditary subalgebra of
`E`.  Thus the dimensions are equal.  The quotient corner is

```text
p(direct_sum_j M_(r_j))p=direct_sum_j C e_j,
```

where `e_j=f^(j)_(11)`, proving `(MP3)`.

## Step 2: finite-dimensional quotients are iterated projection adjunctions

For `J_m` as in the claim, multiplication by `e_m` preserves `J_(m-1)` on
both sides: it annihilates the earlier `e_i` and sends `J` into `J`.
Consequently `e_m` is a projection in `M(J_(m-1))`, and

```text
J_m=J_(m-1)+C e_m.                                      (R3)
```

If `(MP1)` holds, induction in `(R3)` gives
`dim_nuc(B)=dim_nuc(J)`.  Since `J` is hereditary in `I`,
`dim_nuc(J)<=dim_nuc(I)`; combined with Step 1 and ideal monotonicity this
proves equality for every finite-dimensional split quotient.

If the quotient `D` is AF with a dense directed union of unital
finite-dimensional subalgebras `D_lambda`, set

```text
E_lambda=I+s(D_lambda).
```

The finite-dimensional result gives
`dim_nuc(E_lambda)=dim_nuc(I)`.  The union of the `E_lambda` is dense in
`E`, so local permanence yields `dim_nuc(E)<=dim_nuc(I)` when the latter is
finite; ideal monotonicity handles the reverse inequality and the infinite
case.  This proves `(1)=>(3)=>(4)`.  The implications `(4)=>(3)=>(2)` follow
because finite-dimensional algebras, and `C direct_sum C` in particular,
are AF.

## Step 3: two quotient points recover an arbitrary multiplier projection

Let `p in M(A)` be a projection and `K=A+C p`.  If `p in A`, then `K=A`.
If `1-p in A`, then `K=A+C1` is the minimal unitization of `A`.  Both cases
satisfy `(MP1)` by unitization invariance.

In the remaining case, the minimal unitization of `K` is

```text
K~=A+C p+C(1-p),
```

and quotienting by `A` gives `C direct_sum C`.  The two coordinate
projections lift to `p` and `1-p`, so this extension is unital and split.
Assertion 2 gives

```text
dim_nuc(K~)=dim_nuc(A).
```

Unitization invariance gives `dim_nuc(K)=dim_nuc(K~)`, proving `(MP1)`.
This closes the cycle of implications.

## Step 4: full multiplier projections satisfy the formula

Assume `A` is sigma-unital and `closure(ApA)=A`.  Put `K=A+C p`.  If
`p in A`, then `K=A` and there is nothing to prove, so assume `p notin A`.
The ideal
of `K` generated by `p` contains both `C p` and `ApA`, so it is all of `K`.
Thus `pKp` is a full hereditary corner of `K`.  Strong Morita invariance of
nuclear dimension and unitization invariance give

```text
dim_nuc(K)=dim_nuc(pKp)
          =dim_nuc(pAp+C p)
          =dim_nuc(pAp)
          <=dim_nuc(A).                                (R4)
```

Since `A` is an ideal of `K`, ideal monotonicity reverses the last comparison
between `K` and `A`, proving equality.  If `L=closure(ApA)` is proper, the
same argument still computes the projection-generated ideal `L+C p`, but
the residual quotient of `K` is `A/L`; gluing those two pieces is exactly an
extension problem again.  This explains why fullness closes the argument
and nonfullness is the genuine remaining case.

## Step 5: locate the exact stage-centre obstruction

Every `x in J` has Peirce decomposition

```text
x=sum_(j,l) e_j x e_l.
```

If every off-diagonal corner vanishes, this is a diagonal sum and all `e_j`
commute with `J`.  Conversely, if `e_j` commutes with `J` and `j!=l`, then

```text
e_j x e_l=e_j x e_l-x e_j e_l=0.
```

This proves `(MP4)`.  For norm-one `x in e_j J e_l`, `j!=l`, one has
`e_jx=x` and `xe_j=0`, hence `[e_j,x]=x`.  The direct-sum splitting used by
the stage-centre theorem therefore fails with the largest possible
commutator norm as soon as one linking corner is present.
