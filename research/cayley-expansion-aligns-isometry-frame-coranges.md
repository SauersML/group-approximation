Let \(G\) be a finite group generated by a symmetric marked set \(S\).
Assume its left Cayley graph has Hilbert-valued Poincare constant
\(\gamma>0\), in the normalization
\[
 \frac1{|G|}\sum_g\|f_g-\overline f\|_{\mathcal H}^2
 \leq\frac1{\gamma|S||G|}\sum_{s,g}
       \|f_{sg}-f_g\|_{\mathcal H}^2                       \tag{CEA1}
\]
for every finite-dimensional Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\) and every
\(f:G\to\mathcal H\).

Let \(B<G\), let \(\beta:B\to U(d)\), and let
\(V_g:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^R\) be isometries satisfying
\[
 V_{bg}=V_g\beta(b)^*.                                    \tag{CEA2}
\]
For prescribed \(U_s\in U(d)\), put
\[
 e(V)^2=\frac1{|S||G|}\sum_{s,g}
 \|V_{sg}U_s-V_g\|_{2,d}^2.                               \tag{CEA3}
\]
There is a relative unitary zero-cochain
\[
 W:G\to U(d),\qquad W_e=1,\qquad W_{bg}=\beta(b)W_g,       \tag{CEA4}
\]
such that
\[
 \mathcal E_U(W)
 \leq \left(1+2\sqrt{\frac2\gamma}\right)e(V).             \tag{CEA5}
\]

The mechanism is the exact principal-angle identity. If
\(P_g=V_gV_g^*\) and
\(\theta_1(s,g),\ldots,\theta_d(s,g)\) are the principal angles between
\(\operatorname{Ran}V_{sg}\) and \(\operatorname{Ran}V_g\), then
\[
 \frac1d\|P_{sg}-P_g\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =\frac2d\sum_{j=1}^d\sin^2\theta_j(s,g)
 \leq2\|V_{sg}U_s-V_g\|_{2,d}^2.                          \tag{CEA6}
\]
Moreover one can choose one rank-\(d\) projection \(P\), independent of
\(g\), with
\[
 \frac1{|G|d}\sum_g\operatorname{Tr}((1-P)P_g)
 \leq\frac2\gamma e(V)^2.                                 \tag{CEA7}
\]
Polarizing the compressions of \(V_g\) to this plane gives (CEA5).

Consequently, if \(e_{\rm unit}(U)\) is the infimum of (RCO3) and
\(e_{\rm iso}(U)\) is the infimum in (RTF7), then
\[
 e_{\rm iso}(U)\leq e_{\rm unit}(U)
 \leq\left(1+2\sqrt{\frac2\gamma}\right)e_{\rm iso}(U).    \tag{CEA8}
\]
Together with (RTF7)--(RTF8),
\[
 \Delta(U)\leq e_{\rm unit}(U)
 \leq\left(1+2\sqrt{\frac2\gamma}\right)
      \sqrt{\Delta(U)^2+2\Delta(U)}.                       \tag{CEA9}
\]
Thus, on a uniformly expanding family, the unitary Cayley cosystole and
the full relative positive-definite gate vanish simultaneously. In
particular, property \((T;FD)\) supplies one uniform \(\gamma\) for the
Cayley graphs of all finite quotients of a fixed marked group: (CEA1) is
the spectral-gap inequality for the finite-dimensional regular
representations. This applies to the authenticated finite quotients
\(A_N\).

Uniform expansion is essential for aligning an arbitrary low-energy
frame. Take \(G_L=\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\), \(S=\{1,-1\}\), trivial core,
\(d=1\), \(U_{\pm1}=1\), and
\[
 V_j=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\pi j/L)\\ \sin(2\pi j/L)\end{pmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{CEA10}
\]
Then \(e(V)=2\sin(\pi/L)\to0\), but
\[
 \frac1L\sum_jV_jV_j^*=\frac12I_2,
\]
so every fixed line has average leakage \(1/2\). This is an obstruction
to common-plane rounding of arbitrary frames without a spectral gap, not
a unitary-cosystole counterexample: the constant scalar table already has
zero unitary energy.

The theorem closes the target-dimension gap between unitary and
multi-Kraus selectors on the concrete expanding quotient family. It does
not prove that the two Iwahori cubic defects force either energy to
vanish. That nonlinear presentation-to-Cayley estimate remains open.
