Notation as in the target. The artifact carries the same argument with every
germ computation written out and an independent finite-model check of the key
identity (`experiments/v-times-ample-full-group-swindle-2026-09-16/`).

**Inputs.**

- (V1) `V` is generated by transpositions of `G_V`, that is, by elements
  `τ_B = B ⊔ B^{-1} ⊔ id` with `B` a compact open bisection of `G_V` and
  `s(B) ∩ r(B) = ∅`. This follows from Higman's theorem that `V` is simple
  (G. Higman, *Finitely presented infinite simple groups*, 1974; Cannon–Floyd–Parry,
  Enseign. Math. 42 (1996), Theorem 6.9): the subgroup these elements generate is
  normal, since `g τ_B g^{-1} = τ_{gBg^{-1}}` (and each `τ_B` is a homeomorphism
  of `C` that is a prefix replacement on each cylinder of a finite partition, so
  it lies in `V`), and nontrivial, since
  `[0] ↔ [1]` is one of them. Independently, it is Matui's Lemma 6.10 with §6.7
  (`matui-sft-full-groups-generated-by-transpositions`).
- (M) Nekrashevych's definitions of multisections, `S(T)` and `A(T) = A_3(T)`
  (`nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups`). Nothing else from that
  paper is used.

**Conventions.**

- For a finite word `u`, `[u] ⊆ C` is its cylinder. `B_{u→v}` is the compact open
  bisection of `G_V` of germs of the prefix replacement `uw ↦ vw` on `[u]`. These
  sets and their restrictions `B_{us→vs}` form a basis of `G_V`, and
  `B_{v→w} B_{u→v} = B_{u→w}`.
- For a clopen `R ⊆ Q`, also write `R` for the bisection of units over `R`.
  Products `B × b`, with `b` a compact open bisection of `H`, are compact open
  bisections of `T`, and they form a basis of `T`.
- Products of bisections are composed right to left, as maps.

**Step 1: normal form.** Every `h ∈ F(T)` can be written as a finite disjoint
union

`h = ⊔_{i=1}^n B_{u_i→v_i} × b_i`, with `s(b_i) = Q_i` and `r(b_i) = R_i`,

where the source rectangles `D_i = [u_i] × Q_i` partition `Ω` and the range
rectangles `E_i = [v_i] × R_i` partition `Ω`.

*Proof.* `h` is a compact open bisection with `s(h) = r(h) = Ω`. Each `γ ∈ h` lies
in a basic bisection `B_{u→v} × b ⊆ h`. Finitely many of these cover `h`, and
their source rectangles cover `Ω`. The partition of `Ω` generated by finitely many
clopen rectangles consists of finite unions of clopen rectangles, since
`[u] ∖ [u']` is a finite union of cylinders. So there is a partition of `Ω` into
rectangles `D_i`, each inside the source of one chosen basic bisection. Restrict
that bisection to source `D_i`; the restriction of `B_{u→v} × b` to a source
rectangle `[us] × Q'` is `B_{us→vs} × (b restricted to Q')`. The ranges partition
`Ω` because `h` is a bijection of `Ω`. ∎

Refining further never hurts: replacing a piece by its restrictions to
`[u_i a] × Q_i`, `a ∈ {0,1}`, gives the pieces `B_{u_i a→v_i a} × b_i`.

**Step 2: elements with trivial `H`-part lie in `S(T)`.** Let `ρ ∈ F(T)` have a
normal form in which every `b_i` is a unit bisection `Q_i`. Refine the `Q_i` to a
clopen partition `{P_j}` of `Q`. For fixed `j`, the cylinders `[u_i]` with
`P_j ⊆ Q_i` partition `C`, and so do the matching `[v_i]`. So
`v_j = ⊔ B_{u_i→v_i}` is an element of `V`, and
`ρ = ∏_j (v_j × P_j ⊔ id)`, a product of commuting factors. By (V1),
`v_j = τ_{B_1} ⋯ τ_{B_m}` with `s(B_k) ∩ r(B_k) = ∅`. Then
`v_j × P_j ⊔ id = τ_{B_1 × P_j} ⋯ τ_{B_m × P_j}`, and each `B_k × P_j` is a
compact open bisection of `T` with disjoint source and range. So `ρ ∈ S(T)`. In
particular `V × id ⊆ S(T)`. ∎

**Step 3: copies.** For a word `w`, `ι_w = B_{∅→w} × Q` is a compact open
bisection with source `Ω` and range `[w] × Q`, and `ι_a ι_w = ι_{aw}`. For
`h ∈ F(T)` put

`h^(w) = ι_w h ι_w^{-1} ⊔ (Ω ∖ [w] × Q)` (the identity off `[w] × Q`).

This is in `F(T)`, supported in `[w] × Q`, and `(h^(w))^(a) = h^(aw)`.

*Conjugation rule.* If `g ∈ V` restricts on `[w]` to the prefix replacement
`w → w'`, then `(g × Q) h^(w) (g × Q)^{-1} = h^(w')`.

- On `[w'] × Q` the product is
  `(B_{w→w'} × Q) ι_w h ι_w^{-1} (B_{w'→w} × Q) = ι_{w'} h ι_{w'}^{-1}`.
- Off `[w'] × Q`, `(g × Q)^{-1}` lands off `[w] × Q`, where `h^(w)` is the
  identity, so the product is the identity.

Three instances are used:

- `g_0`: `0w ↦ 00w`, `10w ↦ 01w`, `11w ↦ 1w`. It sends `h^(0)` to `h^(00)`.
- `g_1`: `0w ↦ 10w`, `10w ↦ 0w`, `11w ↦ 11w`. It sends `h^(0)` to `h^(10)`.
- `g_2`: `0w ↔ 1w`. It sends `h^(0)` to `h^(1)`.

All three lie in `V × id ⊆ S(T)` (Step 2).

**Step 4: the rotation identity.** Take a normal form of `h` as in Step 1 and
define

`ρ = ⊔_{i, a} B_{u_i a→a u_i} × Q_i` and `ρ' = ⊔_{i, a} B_{v_i a→a v_i} × R_i`,

with `a ∈ {0,1}`.

- The sources of `ρ` are the rectangles `[u_i a] × Q_i`, which partition `Ω`.
- The ranges of `ρ` are `[a u_i] × Q_i = ι_a(D_i)`, which partition `Ω` since
  `[0] × Q` and `[1] × Q` are each partitioned by the `ι_a(D_i)`.
- The same holds for `ρ'` with the `E_i`.

So `ρ, ρ' ∈ F(T)`, and both have trivial `H`-part, so `ρ, ρ' ∈ S(T)` by Step 2.

*Claim:* `ρ' h ρ^{-1} = h^(0) h^(1)`. Restrict both sides to the source rectangle
`[a u_i] × Q_i`; these rectangles partition `Ω`.

- `ρ^{-1}` restricts to `B_{a u_i→u_i a} × Q_i`, with range `[u_i a] × Q_i`.
- `h` restricted to that source is `B_{u_i a→v_i a} × b_i`, with range
  `[v_i a] × R_i`.
- `ρ'` restricted there is `B_{v_i a→a v_i} × R_i`.

The composite is `B_{a u_i→a v_i} × b_i`. On the same rectangle, `h^(a)` is
`ι_a h ι_a^{-1}`, that is `(B_{v_i→a v_i} × R_i)(B_{u_i→v_i} × b_i)(B_{a u_i→u_i} × Q_i)`,
which is `B_{a u_i→a v_i} × b_i`. `h^(1-a)` is the identity there. The bisections
agree piece by piece, hence are equal. ∎

**Step 5: `F(T) = S(T)`.** `S(T)` is normal in `F(T)`. Let
`π: F(T) -> F(T)/S(T)` be the quotient map. Elements of `S(T)` act trivially by
conjugation in the quotient, so by Step 3,
`π(h^(00)) = π(h^(10)) = π(h^(1)) = π(h^(0))` for every `h ∈ F(T)`.

By Step 4, applied to any `k ∈ F(T)`, `π(k) = π(ρ')π(k)π(ρ)^{-1} = π(k^(0) k^(1))`.

- With `k = h^(0)`, using `(h^(0))^(0) = h^(00)` and `(h^(0))^(1) = h^(10)`:
  `x = π(h^(0))` satisfies `x = π(h^(00))π(h^(10)) = x·x`. So `x = 1`.
- With `k = h`: `π(h) = π(h^(0))π(h^(1)) = x·x = 1`.

So `h ∈ S(T)`. ∎

**Step 6: `S(T) = A(T)`, and `A(T)` is perfect.**

*Halving.* Every nonempty clopen `U ⊆ Ω` is a finite disjoint union of rectangles
`[w_j] × P_j`. Put `U_a = ⊔ [w_j 0] × P_j`, `U_b = ⊔ [w_j 1] × P_j` and
`D = ⊔ B_{w_j 0→w_j 1} × P_j`, a bisection from `U_a` onto `U_b`.

*Transpositions are in `A(T)`.* Let `τ_B` be a transposition, `U = s(B)`,
`B(U) ∩ U = ∅`. Halve `U`. The four sets `U_a`, `U_b`, `B(U_a)`, `B(U_b)` are
pairwise disjoint. They carry a degree-4 multisection with base bisections
`id_{U_a}`, `D`, `B|_{U_a}` and `B D` out of `U_a`, and all others obtained by
composition. `τ_B` is its permutation `(1 3)(2 4)`, which lies in `A_4`. `A_4` is
generated by 3-cycles, and the 3-cycle on indices `{i, j, k}` is the 3-cycle of
the degree-3 sub-multisection on those indices. So `τ_B ∈ A_3(T) = A(T)`. Hence
`S(T) ⊆ A(T) ⊆ F(T) = S(T)`.

*Perfect.* Let `τ` be the 3-cycle of a degree-3 multisection with pieces
`U_0, U_1, U_2` and bisections `F_{i0}`. Halve `U_0` by `D: U_{0a} -> U_{0b}`. The
six pieces `F_{i0}(U_{0a})` and `F_{i0}(U_{0b})` carry a degree-6 multisection
with base bisections `F_{i0}|_{U_{0a}}` and `F_{i0} D` out of `U_{0a}`.

- On `F_{i0}(U_{0a})`, its permutation `(0a 1a 2a)(0b 1b 2b)` acts by
  `F_{π(i)0} F_{i0}^{-1}`.
- On `F_{i0}(U_{0b})` it acts by `(F_{π(i)0} D)(F_{i0} D)^{-1}`, the same
  bisection.

So it equals `τ`. That permutation lies in `A_6`, which is simple and nonabelian,
hence perfect. So `τ` is a product of commutators of elements of the image of
`A_6`, which lies in `A(T)` by the sub-multisection remark. The generators of
`A(T)` are products of commutators of elements of `A(T)`, so `A(T)` is perfect. ∎

**Scope check.**

- No minimality, effectiveness or comparison hypothesis on `H` was used.
- `Q` compact is used only for the finiteness of the normal form.
- `Q` may be empty or finite.
- The argument never uses a homology computation, so it is not an instance of an
  AH-conjecture theorem. Li's proof of the AH conjecture (arXiv:2209.08087)
  assumes minimality and comparison, which fail for the non-minimal restrictions
  this is applied to.
