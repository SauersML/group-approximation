## 1. Exact finite-group carriers

For a finite subgroup `F<=K`, put

```text
p_F=|F|^(-1) sum_(g in F) lambda_g,
e_F=1-p_F.                                               (FAC4)
```

The projection `p_F` is the central support of the trivial representation in
`C*(F)`.  Hence `e_F in I intersection C*(F)`, and

```text
e_F a=a e_F=a       for every a in I intersection C*(F). (FAC5)
```

The projections `e_F`, over the directed set of finite subgroups, form an
approximate unit for `I`: the algebraic augmentation ideal is spanned by
`lambda_g-1`, and `(FAC5)` is exact once `g in F`.

## 2. Følner-average without leaving a finite group algebra

Fix finite `Omega subset C[K] intersection I` and `N>=1`.  Only finitely many
group elements occur in the supports of its members.  By local finiteness,
there is a finite subgroup `F<=K` containing the inverse translates under
`alpha^j` of all those elements for `0<=j<N`.  Therefore

```text
Omega subset I intersection C*(alpha^j(F))
       for every 0<=j<N.                                (FAC6)
```

Set

```text
h=N^(-1) sum_(j=0)^(N-1) alpha^j(e_F).                  (FAC7)
```

The subgroup generated by the finitely many finite groups
`alpha^j(F)`, `0<=j<N`, is finite.  Thus `h` lies in one finite-dimensional
group algebra, is a positive contraction with finite spectrum, and belongs
to `I`.  Equations `(FAC5)--(FAC6)` give `ha=ah=a` for `a in Omega`.
Telescoping `(FAC7)` gives

```text
||alpha(h)-h||
 =N^(-1)||alpha^N(e_F)-e_F||
 <=1/N,
```

and a second telescoping argument gives `(FAC1)`.

Diagonalize over an increasing sequence of finite algebraic subsets dense in
`I` and integers `N->infinity`.  The resulting `h_n` is an approximate unit
for `I`, commutes asymptotically with `B`, and satisfies
`||alpha(h_n)-h_n||->0`.  If `U` is the crossed-product unitary, then

```text
||U h_n U^*-h_n||=||alpha(h_n)-h_n||->0.
```

For a Fourier monomial `aU^k`, the left approximate-unit relation follows
from `h_n a->a`; the right relation follows from

```text
(aU^k)h_n=a alpha^k(h_n)U^k
```

and `(FAC1)`.  Hence `(h_n)` is a quasicentral approximate unit for `J`.

## 3. Support corners and local nuclear dimension

Because `h` lies in a finite-dimensional algebra, its support projection
`s=1_(0,infinity)(h)` belongs to that algebra.  Functional calculus with a
function vanishing at zero shows `s in I`.  Since `h` is invertible in the
corner `sBs`, it generates the same hereditary subalgebra of `J` as `s`,
proving `(FAC2)`.  Also `(1-s)a=(1-s)ha=0` and similarly on the right, so
`s` retains the exact carrier property on `Omega`.

For a finite subset of `J`, first approximate it by Fourier polynomials with
algebraic augmentation coefficients, then choose `h` as above with large
`N`.  The elements `hxh` lie in `sJs` and approximate the prescribed finite
set.  Nuclear dimension is local with respect to approximation by
subalgebras of uniformly bounded nuclear dimension.  Therefore `(FAC3)`
implies `dim_nuc(J)<=d`.

The support map is discontinuous at zero.  The smallest nonzero eigenvalue
of the averages `(FAC7)` may tend to zero, so the estimate on
`alpha(h)-h` gives no estimate on `alpha(s)-s`.  This is why the argument
stops at the carrier corners instead of producing invariant AF stages.
