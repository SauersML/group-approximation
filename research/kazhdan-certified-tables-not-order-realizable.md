**ESTABLISHED (proposed; unreviewed).**  Derivation: `kazhdan-certified-tables-not-order-realizable-proof`.

**Setting.**  Tables, embeddings and forbidden tables are as in
[[orderable-nonsofic-iff-order-realizable-forbidden-table]].  Let `Gamma` be generated by a finite symmetric set
`S` not containing `1`, with Laplacian `Delta = |S| - sum_(s in S) s`.  An *Ozawa certificate of radius `R`* is an
identity

```text
Delta^2 - lambda Delta = sum_i xi_i^* xi_i     in R[Gamma],   lambda > 0,
```

in which every `xi_i` is supported in the ball `B_(R/2)(Gamma)`.  Call a table *Kazhdan-certified* if it contains
the ball `B_R(Gamma)` (with its products that land in the ball) for some such `Gamma`, `S` and certificate.

**Theorem.**  Let `T` be Kazhdan-certified through `(Gamma, S, R)`, and let `f` embed `T` in a group `H`.
1. `<f(S)> <= H` has property (T), and it is nontrivial.
2. So `T` embeds in **no locally indicable group**, hence in no bi-orderable group.  By compactness it has a finite
   refutation by order clauses.
3. If `T` embeds in a **left-orderable** group, then `<f(S)>` is an infinite left-orderable Kazhdan group, which is
   Navas's Question 3 ([[infinite-left-orderable-kazhdan-group]]).

**Every Kazhdan subgroup yields such tables.**  By Ozawa's theorem (J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 15 (2016)), every Kazhdan
group has a certificate of some radius.  So any table of a group `G` containing a nontrivial Kazhdan subgroup can be
enlarged to a Kazhdan-certified table of `G`.  Enlarging a forbidden table keeps it forbidden.

**The class this kills.**  Call a nonsoficity proof *table-uniform* if it reads its hypotheses off a finite table,
so that it proves the table forbidden.  Kun--Thom's rigid-defect criterion needs (T) of a subgroup with quantitative
control.  A table-uniform version of it can take that input only from a Kazhdan-certified table.  Then:
- *LI and BO:* no table-uniform Kazhdan certificate can witness the locally indicable, bi-orderable (and so
  Conradian) cells.  This is unconditional.
- *LO:* such a certificate can witness the left-orderable cell only through Navas's Question 3.

The step where the approach fails is (1): the certificate identity survives every realization of the table.

**What stays open, and is now sharply posed.**  The obstruction is about tables that *contain* their Kazhdan
certificate, not about groups.  A Kun--Thom group `G` may still have a forbidden sub-table that omits the certificate
ball and embeds in a locally indicable group.  By the reduction, that would give a locally indicable nonsofic group.
Any such sub-table would have to be forbidden for a reason that does not consume (T) of a subgroup inside the table.
