**OPEN.** There is a finitely presented infinite simple group `G` with `β_1^(2)(G) > 0`.

## Consequences

1. **Osin's problem.** `G` would refute `fp-positive-l2-betti-groups-are-acylindrically-hyperbolic`, since
   simple groups are never acylindrically hyperbolic (Minasyan–Osin, arXiv:1310.6289, TeX l.241–242).
2. **Lodha's question.** If `β_1^(2)(G) > 1`, then `G` needs at least three generators, because
   `d(G) >= 1 + β_1^(2)(G)`. That answers `fp-infinite-simple-group-not-two-generated` affirmatively. A value
   in `(0, 1]` does not suffice.

## The finitely generated case is known

Osin–Thom, arXiv:1108.2411, TeX l.84, read at source: "For every integer n ≥ 2 and every ε > 0 there exists
an infinite simple group Q generated by n elements such that β_1^(2)(Q) ≥ n−1−ε." Their groups are limits of
hyperbolic groups: quotients of free products of finite groups by infinitely many high-power relators. They
are not finitely presented. (Their l.92 also gives simple groups of every rank `d(Q) = n`.)

## Structural constraints on G

By Peterson–Thom (arXiv:0708.4327, source l.865–867, Corollary `coromoon`), a group with `β_1^(2) > 0` has no
infinite, finitely generated, ws-normal subgroup of infinite index. For a simple `G` this means that every
infinite finitely generated ws-normal subgroup is `G` itself. In particular `G`:
- is not locally moving (`locally-moving-groups-have-vanishing-first-l2-betti-number`);
- has no fixed-point-free action on a tree with a finitely generated edge stabilizer
  (`simple-groups-on-trees-with-fg-edge-groups-have-b1-zero`), and so is no Burger–Mozes, Wise or Rattaggi
  product lattice;
- has no Kazhdan property (T);
- contains no infinite finitely generated commensurated subgroup of infinite index;
- is not vigorous, and is not a circle group containing `T`. These classes are locally moving; separately,
  they are 2-generated by `fg-simple-circle-groups-with-t-are-two-generated` and the vigorous-group theorem
  of Attempt 1 of `fp-infinite-simple-group-not-two-generated`.

So `G` would be a finitely presented simple group of a new kind. Each infinite finitely generated proper
subgroup must have some conjugate meeting it finitely, and more strongly must fail ws-normality.

## Lesson for general BH

All known finitely presented simple groups have `β_1^(2) = 0`, for three different structural reasons:
splittings, local mobility and products. Every one of these reasons produces an infinite finitely generated
ws-normal subgroup of infinite index. That common feature is the real invariant, and it is also what the
Boone–Higman host constructions on main produce: rigid stabilizers, germ stabilizers and commensurated
point stabilizers of type (A) actions. A host with no such subgroup would be the first of its kind, and would
be needed both here and for any "hyperbolic-like" finitely presented simple host.
