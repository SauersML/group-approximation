Notation is as in the claim. `[p, q] = p q p^(-1) q^(-1)`, and `rho(p, q) = rk_omega(p - q)` on
`U(M_omega)`.

## Step 0. Rank one means invertible, and units lift to invertible matrices

Let `A in M_n(K)` over a field `K` have rank `r`. Choose `P, P' in GL_n(K)` with
`P A P' = diag(1_r, 0)`. Then `A^+ = P^(-1) P'^(-1)` is invertible, and
`A^+ - A = P^(-1) diag(0, 1_(n - r)) P'^(-1)` has rank `n - r`.

- Take `m = [A_i] in M_omega` with `rk_omega(m) = 1`, so `rk(A_i)/n_i -> 1` along `omega`.
  Then `[A_i^+] = m`, so `m` is a unit.
- Every unit `v` of `M_omega` has `rk_omega(v) = 1`: `1 = rk(v v^(-1)) <= rk(v)`.
  So `v` has an invertible lift `[A_i^+]` by the previous bullet.

## Step 1. The group `Q`

- `Pi` is unital and `w w^(-1) = 1` in `k[W]`. So `sigma = Pi|_W` is a group homomorphism
  `W -> U(M_omega)`, and `Q = sigma(W)` is a group.
- `G` is Kazhdan, hence finitely generated. `L` is generated as a `G`-group by `x_o`.
  So `W`, and with it `Q`, is finitely generated.
- For `w in W`, the rank distance is `rho(sigma(w), 1) = rk_omega(Pi(w - 1)) = rk(w - 1)`.

## Step 2. Specialization: `Q` is `F_p`-linear sofic (or `C`-linear sofic)

**Lemma.** Let `k_i` be fields of one characteristic `c`, and let `Q <= U(prod_omega M_(n_i)(k_i) / {null})`
be countable. If `c = p > 0`, `Q` embeds in a rank ultraproduct of the `M_m(F_p)`. If `c = 0`, it embeds
in one of the `M_m(C)`. So `Q` is `F_p`-linear sofic, resp. `C`-linear sofic.

*Proof.*

1. **Finite data.** Enumerate `Q = {q_1, q_2, ...}` and lift each `q_j` to invertible matrices
   `A_(j,i) in GL_(n_i)(k_i)` (Step 0). Fix `N`. Let `Sigma_N` be the finite list of matrix expressions
   `A_j A_j' - A_(m(j,j'))` and `A_j - 1`, for `j, j' <= N`, where `q_j q_j' = q_(m(j,j'))`.
   The normalized ranks of these expressions at index `i` have ultralimits `0` and `rho(q_j, 1)`.
   Pick an index `i = i_N` at which every normalized rank is within `1/N` of its ultralimit.
2. **Rank data are polynomial.** Let `A` be the subring of `k_i` generated by the entries of the
   `A_(j,i)`, `j <= N`, and the inverses of their determinants. `A` is a finitely generated domain over the
   prime field. For each expression in `Sigma_N`, of rank `r`, choose one nonzero `r x r` minor. Let `s`
   be the product of these minors. Now `rank = r` is witnessed by two facts:
   - that minor is nonzero;
   - all `(r+1) x (r+1)` minors vanish.
   Both are polynomial identities over the prime field in the entries.
3. **Characteristic `p`.**
   - `A[1/s]` is a nonzero finitely generated `F_p`-algebra. Take a maximal ideal `m`.
   - By Zariski's lemma, `A[1/s]/m` is a finite field `F_q`.
   - Reduction mod `m` preserves every vanishing minor. It keeps every chosen minor, and every
     determinant, nonzero, because they divide the unit `s`.
   - So the reduced matrices `B_j in GL_(n_i)(F_q)` have exactly the ranks of the `A_(j,i)` on `Sigma_N`.
   - Restriction of scalars along `F_q = F_p^e` embeds `GL_n(F_q)` in `GL_(ne)(F_p)` and multiplies every
     rank by `e`, so normalized ranks do not change.
4. **Characteristic `0`.** `Frac(A)` is a finitely generated extension of `Q`, so it embeds in `C`.
   Field embeddings preserve rank.
5. **Assembly.** Take a nonprincipal ultrafilter on `N`. The map sending `q_j` to the class of its stage-`N`
   matrices is multiplicative in the limit. It keeps `rho(q_j, 1) > 0` for `q_j != 1`, so it is an
   injective homomorphism into the rank ultraproduct over `F_p`, resp. `C`. `[]`

Countable groups embedding in such an ultraproduct are exactly the linear sofic ones
(Arzhantseva--Paunescu, with amplification). This is the form used in
`kt-wreath-rank-radical-is-rank-fixed-algebra-rigidity` and
`rigid-compression-defect-normalization-dichotomy`.

## Step 3. The rigid pair survives in `Q`

`kun-thom-nonsofic-wreath` (Theorem E) gives the following. `Gamma` and `G` are Kazhdan, and
`Gamma` is infranormal: its compression semigroup `P_Gamma = {g : g Gamma g^(-1) <= Gamma}`
generates `G`.

- `sigma(Gamma)` and `sigma(G)` are quotients of Kazhdan groups, hence Kazhdan.
- `sigma(P_Gamma) <= P_(sigma(Gamma))`, and `sigma(P_Gamma)` generates `sigma(G)`. So `sigma(Gamma)` is
  infranormal in `sigma(G)`.

Hence `sigma(Gamma) <= sigma(G) <= Q` is a rigid pair in the sense of
`rigid-compression-defect-normalization-dichotomy`.

## Step 4. The defect has rank distance `rk(x_a - x_b)`

**Choice of `u` and `gamma`.**

- If every element of `P_Gamma` normalized `Gamma`, then `G = <P_Gamma>` would normalize `Gamma`, but
  `Gamma` is not normal. So some `u in P_Gamma` has `u Gamma u^(-1) < Gamma` strictly.
- Pick `gamma in Gamma \ u Gamma u^(-1)`. Then `gamma u Gamma != u Gamma`, so `a = u o != b = gamma u o`.

**The centralizing element.**

- For `gamma' in Gamma`, `gamma' x_o gamma'^(-1) = x_(gamma' o) = x_o`.
- So `z = sigma(x_o)` commutes with `sigma(Gamma)`, that is, `z in C_Q(sigma(Gamma))`.

**The defect in `W`.**

- `u x_o u^(-1) = x_a`.
- `delta_W = [x_a, gamma] = x_a (gamma x_a gamma^(-1))^(-1) = x_a x_b^(-1)`.
- So `delta = [sigma(u) z sigma(u)^(-1), sigma(gamma)] = sigma(delta_W)`.

**Its rank distance.** By Step 1 and the invariance of `rk` under multiplication by the unit `x_b`:

```text
rho(delta, 1) = rk(x_a x_b^(-1) - 1) = rk((x_a - x_b) x_b^(-1)) = rk(x_a - x_b).
```

- If `rk` sees one lamp difference, this is positive, so `delta != 1`.
- If `rk` is base-faithful, it equals `1`, because `x_a - x_b` is a nonzero element of `S_k`: `a != b`,
  so the two monomials are distinct.

## Step 5. Conclusions of part 1

- **`Q` is not sofic.** `Q` is a countable group with the rigid pair of Step 3 and a nontrivial rigid
  defect, since `delta in D_Q(sigma(G), sigma(Gamma))`. `sofic-groups-kill-rigid-compression-defects`
  says a sofic group has no such defect.
- **`DD` fails for the class.** `Q` is `F_p`-linear sofic, resp. `C`-linear sofic, by Step 2. Hence
  `DD(F_p-linear sofic)` fails, resp. `DD(C-linear sofic)`.
- **`NORM` fails.** `sigma(u) z sigma(u)^(-1)` does not commute with `sigma(gamma)`, so `sigma(G)` does not
  normalize `C_(U(M_omega))(sigma(Gamma))`. Transfer to an ultraproduct over `F_p` via Step 2. Then `NORM`
  fails, as `rigid-compression-defect-normalization-dichotomy` predicts from the failure of `DD`.
- **Separation.** Since `DD(sofic)` holds, `Q` separates sofic from `F_p`-linear sofic (resp. `C`-linear
  sofic). In characteristic 0 this gives a positive answer to `linear-sofic-nonsofic-group`.

## Step 6. Part 2 (barrier)

- Assume `DD(F_p-linear sofic)`. By Step 5 no approximable `rk` on `F_p[W]` sees the lamp difference. So
  `rk(x_a - x_b) = 0` for every admissible pair `(u, gamma)`.
- Translates: `x_(g a) - x_(g b) = g (x_a - x_b) g^(-1)`, and Sylvester rank functions are invariant
  under conjugation by units. So `rk(x_(g a) - x_(g b)) = 0` for all `g in G`.
- A base-faithful `rk` would give `rk(x_a - x_b) = 1`, so none is approximable.
- Matrix models are handled in Step 8.

## Step 7. Part 3 (monomial models are sofic)

**Rank comparison.** Let `m in Mon_n(F_q)` have permutation `pi` and scalars `c_j`, and put
`h(m) = #{j : pi(j) != j or c_j != 1}`. Then

```text
h(m)/2 <= rank(m - 1) <= h(m).
```

On a `pi`-cycle of length `k`, the block of `m` is a weighted cycle matrix `C` with `C^k = c I`, where
`c` is the product of the weights.

- `C` is cyclic, with characteristic polynomial `t^k - c`. So `rank(C - 1) = k` if `c != 1`, and `k - 1` if
  `c = 1`.
- A cycle of length `k >= 2` contributes `k` to `h`.
- A fixed point `j` contributes `1` to both `h` and the rank iff `c_j != 1`, and `0` to both otherwise.
- `k - 1 >= k/2` for `k >= 2`.

**Conclusion.**

- `Mon_n(F_q)` acts faithfully on `F_q^x x [n]`, and the normalized Hamming distance of `m` from `1` is
  exactly `h(m)/n`.
- So the rank length and the Hamming length on `prod_omega Mon_(n_i)(F_(q_i))` have the same null
  subgroup. Its image in `U(M_omega)` embeds in `prod_omega Sym((q_i - 1) n_i) / {Hamming-null}`.
- So `Q` is sofic, and `delta = 1` by `sofic-groups-kill-rigid-compression-defects`. Step 4 then gives
  `rk(x_a - x_b) = 0`, and the translates die as in Step 6.

This holds with no hypothesis on `DD(F_p)`. It covers every model built from permutations of finite sets
twisted by scalars, including point models and permutation models. A sofic `W` would have provided such a
model; part 5 excludes that directly.

## Step 8. Part 4 (matrix models of `R_p`)

- **From a matrix model to a rank function.** Let `phi : R_p -> M_omega` be a unital ring homomorphism.
  Then `rk_omega o phi` is a Sylvester rank function on `R_p`. Its restriction to `F_p[W]` is approximable
  with model `phi|_(F_p[W])`. It is base-faithful, because every `s in S` is a unit of `R_p`, so `phi(s)` is
  a unit (Step 0).
- **From a rank function to a matrix model.** Let `Pi` be the model of a base-faithful approximable `rk`.
  By Step 0, `Pi(S)` consists of units. `R_p` is the Ore localization `F_p[W] S^(-1)`
  (`coset-field-rank-certificates-need-base-faithful-lamp-ranks`). By the universal property of Ore
  localization, `Pi` extends uniquely to `R_p -> M_omega`.
- **Both conclusions.** A matrix model gives the rank condition for `R_p`, by part 2 of
  `coset-field-rank-certificates-need-base-faithful-lamp-ranks`, or directly because `M_omega` has a
  faithful rank function. Step 5 applies to the restriction.

## Step 9. Part 5 (`W` is not sofic)

Apply Steps 3 and 4 inside `W` itself, with `sigma = id`:

- `z = x_o` centralizes `Gamma`;
- `[u z u^(-1), gamma] = x_a x_b^(-1)`, which is nontrivial because `a != b` and `L` is free abelian.

By `sofic-groups-kill-rigid-compression-defects`, `W` is not sofic. This is the argument of
`left-orderable-non-sofic-via-integer-lamp-rigid-pair`, step 2, specialized to the Theorem E pair.
