**ESTABLISHED.**  Let `G = F/R` with `F` free and `R` normal in `F`, and set

```text
E = F/[R,R].
```

Then

```text
1 -> R/[R,R] -> E -> G -> 1                                             (FA1)
```

is exact, the kernel `R/[R,R]` is free abelian, and **`E` is torsion-free**.
If `F` has rank `n` then `E` is generated by `n` elements, so a finitely
generated `G` has a finitely generated torsion-free cover of the same rank.

The construction is canonical given the presentation and needs no hypothesis
whatever on `G`: no finiteness, no residual property, no torsion condition.
It is not finitely presented in general, and no claim of finite
presentability is made or needed.

**What it is for.**  It reduces "does a torsion-free group with property P
exist" to "does P lift from `F/R` to `F/[R,R]`" for whatever P is being
transferred.  For P = "not MF" that lifting statement is
`free-abelianized-cover-reflects-mf`, which is open; see
`torsion-free-countable-non-mf-from-abelianized-cover` for the route it
would complete.  Nothing about MF is asserted here.

**A free choice worth making (2026-09-08).**  Nothing above constrains the
generating set, so one may always adjoin a dummy generator `z` mapping to the
identity of `G`.  This costs one unit of rank and buys a presentation-free
detector inside the cover: `[fbar, zbar] != 1` whenever `q(f) != 1`.  The
representation-theoretic consequences are
`free-abelianized-cover-has-nontrivial-fd-radical` and
`free-abelianized-cover-fd-radical-is-commutator`; both are about exact
finite-dimensional representations and neither decides MF.
