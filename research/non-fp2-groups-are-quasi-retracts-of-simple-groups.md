**ESTABLISHED** by `non-fp2-groups-are-quasi-retracts-of-simple-groups-proof`
(a lane proof composing three published theorems, not independently reviewed). No priority is
claimed. It is a direct corollary of Belk--Zaremsky, but it is not stated in LISW or in FWZ.

## Statement

Let `H` be a finitely generated group that is not of type `FP_2(k)` for any field `k`.
Equivalently, `H` is not of type `FP_2(R)` for any nonzero commutative ring `R`. Let `SV_H` be the
Belk--Zaremsky twisted Brin--Thompson group of the left regular action of `H` on itself. Then:

1. `SV_H` is simple and finitely generated;
2. `H` is a subgroup of `SV_H`, via `ι_∅`;
3. `SV_H` admits a quasi-retraction onto `H`;
4. `SV_H` has the same finiteness properties as `H`. Both are of type `F_1` and `FP_1(R)` for
   every `R`. Neither is of type `F_n`, `F`, `FP_n(R)` or `FP(R)` for any `n >= 2` and any
   `R ≠ 0`.

**For LISW Question 1.10.** The statement applies to every finitely generated `H <= GL_n(C)` of type
`FP_2` over no field, so Question 1.10 has a positive answer for all of them. This case includes groups
that no construction on main or in LISW reaches.

- **Example: `Z wr Z`.** Fix a transcendental `s ∈ C`. The matrices `a = [[1,1],[0,1]]` and
  `t = [[s,0],[0,1]]` generate `{[[s^m, f(s)],[0,1]] : m ∈ Z, f ∈ Z[s^{±1}]} ≅ Z[s^{±1}] ⋊ Z = Z wr Z`
  inside `GL_2(C)`.
- **It is not linear over `Q-bar`.** It contains the free abelian group `A = Z[s^{±1}]` of infinite
  rank. Suppose `Z wr Z` embeds in `GL_m(Q-bar)`.
  - The image is finitely generated, so it lies in `GL_m(K)` for a number field `K`. Restricting
    scalars puts it in `GL_{m'}(Z[1/N])` for some `m'` and `N`.
  - The `Q`-span of `A` is a commutative subalgebra `C ⊆ M_{m'}(Q)`, so `A` lies in the unit group
    of the `Z[1/N]`-order `C_0 = C ∩ M_{m'}(Z[1/N])`.
  - That unit group has finite torsion-free rank. Its image in `(C_0/nil(C_0))^×` lies in a group of
    `S`-units of a product of number fields, which is finitely generated by Dirichlet's theorem. The
    kernel `1 + nil(C_0)` has a finite filtration by the subgroups `1 + nil^i`. Each quotient embeds in
    the finitely generated `Z[1/N]`-module `nil^i/nil^{i+1}`.
  - This contradicts the infinite rank of `A`. So `Z wr Z` lies outside LISW Theorem 1.4 and
    Remark 1.5.
- **It is of type `FP_2` over no field.** Let `k` be a field. In the split extension
  `Z[s^{±1}] ⋊ Z`, the Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre spectral sequence has only two columns. So
  `H_0(Z; H_2(Z[s^{±1}]; k))` is a direct summand of `H_2(Z wr Z; k)`. That summand is
  `(Λ^2 k[s^{±1}])_Z`. It has a basis indexed by `j - i ≥ 1`, from the orbits of `e_i ∧ e_j`, so
  it is infinite-dimensional. A group of type `FP_2(k)` has finite-dimensional `H_2(-; k)`.
- **Other examples.** Direct products `(Z wr Z) × L` with any finitely generated `L <= GL_m(C)`. Such
  a product retracts onto `Z wr Z`, and `FP_2(R)` passes to retracts.

**What remains of Question 1.10.** It stays open for the groups that are of type `FP_2` over some
field. Beyond finite generation, a host needs finiteness properties that the regular action cannot
supply: `SV_G` is finitely presented only when `G ↷ S` has finitely many orbits on pairs
(`twisted-brin-thompson-finite-presentation-criterion`). The self-similar method of LISW §6 cannot be
used over non-algebraic rings; see `rigid-affine-hosts-are-linear-over-number-fields`.

## Lesson for general BH

- **The quasi-retraction is free.** In a Thompson-like full group, a germ cocycle `c(g, ξ) ∈ Λ` into
  a group `Λ` gives a coarse Lipschitz map `g ↦ c(g, ξ_0)`, provided two things hold:
  - `c` is locally constant and takes finitely many values on each generator;
  - `c(λ, ξ_0) = λ` on a subgroup `Λ` of global elements.

  This map is a quasi-retraction onto `Λ`. Belk--Zaremsky's `SV_G` is the case `Λ = G`, with the twist
  cocycle. LISW's `ρ` is the case where `c` is the linear part of affine germs.
- **So only finiteness is hard.** Once the input sits among the global elements of such a host, the
  host is never more finite than the input (Alonso). "Same finiteness" then means "at least as
  finite", and every question of LISW type is a question about building finite hosts.
- **The same cocycle is an obstruction tool.** Suppose a finitely generated host has all its germs in
  a group `Λ` that it also contains as global elements. Then the host is no more finite than `Λ`.
  - **Example.** Take the homeomorphisms of `P^1(Q_p)` that fix `∞` and are piecewise in
    `Λ = Z[1/p][λ^{±1}] ⋊ ⟨p, λ⟩` (affine maps), with `λ ∈ Z_p^×` transcendental. Here `Λ` is not
    finitely presented: valuations of `Q(λ)` that are trivial on `Q` put an antipodal pair into
    `Σ^c`. So that host, if finitely generated, is not finitely presented.
  - **Test for any Thompson-like BH host.** Its globally realized germ group must already be as
    finite as the target.
