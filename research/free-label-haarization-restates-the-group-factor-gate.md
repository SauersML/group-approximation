Let `(M,tau)` be a finite tracial von Neumann algebra, let
`u_1,...,u_n in U(M)`, and put `A=W^*(u_1,...,u_n)`.  Let
`s_1,...,s_n` be the free generators of `F_n` and define

~~~text
v_i=u_i tensor lambda(s_i)
    in M tensor L(F_n).                                  (FLH1)
~~~

Then the map `lambda(s_i) maps_to v_i` extends to a trace-preserving
normal star-isomorphism

~~~text
L(F_n) isomorphic to W^*(v_1,...,v_n).                   (FLH2)
~~~

Thus the free labels do not encode the joint distribution of the
`u_i`.  They replace every source tuple by the same free Haar tuple.

Adding canceling labels does recover the source algebraically.  Put

~~~text
b_i=1 tensor lambda(s_i).                                (FLH3)
~~~

Then

~~~text
v_i b_i^*=u_i tensor 1,
W^*(v_1,...,v_n,b_1,...,b_n)=A tensor L(F_n).            (FLH4)
~~~

However, this does not provide the canonical trace of an abstract group.
Let `H` be the actual subgroup of `U(A)` generated by the `u_i`, and let
`Gamma` be the actual subgroup of `U(A tensor L(F_n))` generated by all
`v_i,b_i`.  There is an exact abstract-group identification

~~~text
Gamma=H times F_n,                                       (FLH5)
~~~

under which the ambient trace is

~~~text
(tau tensor tau_F)(h,s)=tau(h) 1_(s=1).                 (FLH6)
~~~

It is the canonical group trace on `Gamma` if and only if

~~~text
tau(h)=0 for every h in H minus {1}.                     (FLH7)
~~~

Condition (FLH7) is equivalent to the original inclusion of `H` in
`U(A)` extending to a trace-preserving normal isomorphism

~~~text
L(H) isomorphic to A.                                    (FLH8)
~~~

Consequently the canceling-label construction has canonical trace exactly
when the source was already a group von Neumann algebra in its given
trace.  It does not weaken the group-factor realization problem.

For the finite non-CE SynchBCS source, take finitely many generating
symmetries in any tracial GNS algebra.  The `v_i` alone generate the
Connes-embeddable free group factor and erase the source.  The enlarged
tuple `(v_i,b_i)` recovers the non-CE algebra, but its abstract group
inherits the canonical trace only under (FLH8).  If (FLH8) holds, `H` is
already a nonhyperlinear group and the root problem is solved; if it
fails, non-CE of the ambient representation says nothing about
hyperlinearity of `Gamma`.

Hence universal Haarization is a sharp no-go, not a dilation theorem.
The source-specific Pauli, groupoid, or non-Cartan compiler must preserve
multiplication and the canonical group trace simultaneously.  The global
route remains **OPEN**.
