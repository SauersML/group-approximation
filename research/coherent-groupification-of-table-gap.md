**Target (second half of Target C of the source dossier).**  Given a finite
table `T` with the robust quantum/classical gap of
`finite-table-quantum-classical-hamming-gap`, there is a group `G` that is
hyperlinear and contains a nested sequence of finite windows whose tables
realize the quantum optimum of `T` asymptotically — coherently, meaning that
the unitary models of the successive windows are compatible enough to assemble
into a single hyperlinear approximation of `G`, while the classical obstruction
persists at every stage.

**Why this is the hard half.**  A gap is a local phenomenon and a group is a
global object, and the passage between them is where every version of this
attack has failed.  Two specific hazards:

1. *Linearity.*  Finite-dimensional perfect quantum strategies are very often
   realized by matrices generating a finitely generated linear group, and
   finitely generated linear groups are sofic.  So the gadget must be embedded
   in a way that provably does not land in a linear group, or the construction
   proves nothing.
2. *Coherence.*  Hyperlinearity of `G` requires models of *all* finite windows
   simultaneously accurate, not one window at a time.  A tower of groups each
   realizing the gap on its own window can easily fail to have a single
   hyperlinear approximation, and conversely forcing coherence tends to force
   the models to become genuine representations, which destroys the gap.

This is the same difficulty every candidate lane in this graph meets from the
other side; what is different here is that the local obstruction is a conic
dual certificate rather than a rigidity property, so the coherence question is
about preserving a linear functional's value along the tower rather than about
preserving a spectral gap.

## Attempts

- **Take `G` to be the group generated by the gadget's unitaries.**  Dies on
  hazard 1: that group is linear by construction, hence sofic, hence the table
  it realizes has permutation models — contradicting the gap it was supposed to
  witness.  The lesson is that the gap must be realized only in the limit, by
  approximate models, never by a genuine finite-dimensional representation.
- **Amalgamate copies of the gadget along a common subgroup, iterating.**  The
  standard groupification move, and the point where it is currently stuck:
  permanence of hyperlinearity under amalgamation over an amenable subgroup is
  available, but the gap functional's value is not known to be preserved by the
  amalgam — the amalgamated models can average the certificate away.
- **Use a wreath product over the gadget, as in the nonsofic constructions.**
  Deferred: this is exactly the shape of the known nonsofic groups, and the
  open question is whether the conic certificate survives the coset action.  It
  is worth attempting only after `finite-table-quantum-classical-hamming-gap`
  has an actual instance, since the construction must be tailored to the
  certificate.
