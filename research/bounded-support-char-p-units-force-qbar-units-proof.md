**Setup.** Fix `G` and finite `S, T in G`. For distinct `g, h in S` let
`I_(g,h)` be the ideal of `Z[u_s (s in S), v_t (t in T), w]` generated by

- the coefficient of every group element in `(sum_s u_s s)(sum_t v_t t) - 1`,
- the coefficient of every group element in `(sum_t v_t t)(sum_s u_s s) - 1`,
- `w u_g u_h - 1`.

All generators have integer coefficients. For a field `k`, a ring map
`Z[u, v, w]/I_(g,h) -> k` is exactly a pair `u, v in k[G]` with
`supp u in S`, `supp v in T`, `uv = vu = 1`, and `u_g u_h != 0` (then `w` is
determined). A unit of `k[G]` is nontrivial exactly when its support has at
least two elements, so the nontrivial units on `(S,T)` over `k` are the union of
the `k`-points of the finitely many schemes `B_(g,h) = Spec Z[u,v,w]/I_(g,h)`.

**Step 1 (no Qbar-point gives finitely many characteristics).** Suppose
`B_(g,h)(Qbar)` is empty. By Hilbert's Nullstellensatz over the algebraically
closed field `Qbar`, `1` lies in `I_(g,h) Qbar[u,v,w]`, hence in
`I_(g,h) Q[u,v,w]` (linear algebra over `Q` on the finitely many coefficients of
a certificate `1 = sum c_i f_i`). Clearing denominators gives an integer
`N_(g,h) >= 1` with `N_(g,h) in I_(g,h)`. If `k` is a field of characteristic
`p` not dividing `N_(g,h)`, then `N_(g,h)` is a unit of `k` lying in the ideal
generated by `I_(g,h)` in `k[u,v,w]`, so `B_(g,h)(k)` is empty. Hence if every
`B_(g,h)(Qbar)` is empty, `Pi(S,T)` is contained in the finite set of primes
dividing `prod N_(g,h)`.

**Step 2 (a Qbar-point gives almost all characteristics).** Suppose some
`B_(g,h)` has a `Qbar`-point. Its coordinates generate a number field `K` and
lie in `O_K[1/M]` for some integer `M >= 1`. For a prime `𝔭` of `O_K` not
dividing `M`, reduction `O_K[1/M] -> O_K/𝔭` is a ring map, so it carries the
point to an `O_K/𝔭`-point of `B_(g,h)`: the generators have integer
coefficients, and `w u_g u_h = 1` reduces to `wbar ubar_g ubar_h = 1`, so the
reduced unit still has two nonzero coefficients. Every rational prime `p`
not dividing `M` has a prime `𝔭` of `O_K` above it, so `Pi(S,T)` contains
every prime not dividing `M`.

**Items.**
- Item 1: Steps 1 and 2 are exhaustive. A `Qbar`-point exists or it doesn't;
  the two cases give "finite" and "cofinite", and cofinite is not finite.
- Item 2: Step 2 with the given `K`.
- Item 3 is the contrapositive of Step 1.
- Item 4 is the contrapositive of item 2 for `K = Q`, whose residue fields at
  `p` are `F_p`.
