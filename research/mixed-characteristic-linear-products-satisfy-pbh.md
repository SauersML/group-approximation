## Statement

Let `G_0` be a finitely generated subgroup of `GL_m(K_0)` for a field `K_0` of characteristic
zero. Let `G_2` be a finitely generated subgroup of `GL_n(K_2)` for a field `K_2` of
characteristic two and transcendence degree at most one over `F_2`. Then `G_0 × G_2`, every
group containing it with finite index, and every subgroup of these embed in a finitely
presented group admitting an action of type (A). In particular they satisfy Boone--Higman.

## A concrete group

`Δ = SL_3(Z) × SL_3(F_2[t])` is such a product: `SL_3(F_2[t])` is generated by the elementary
matrices `e_ij(1)`, `e_ij(t)`, through the Steinberg relation `[e_ij(a), e_jk(b)] = e_ik(ab)`.

**Remark (not part of the established claim): `Δ` is linear over no field.**
- *Characteristic zero.* A finitely generated linear group in characteristic zero is virtually
  torsion-free (Selberg's lemma, standard, not re-read here). So its torsion subgroups are
  finite. But `Δ` contains `{e_13(a) : a ∈ F_2[t]} ≅ (F_2[t], +)`, an infinite elementary
  abelian `2`-group.
- *Characteristic `p > 0`.* `Δ` contains the integer Heisenberg group of `SL_3(Z)`, whose central
  commutator `c = [a, b]` has infinite order and commutes with `a` and `b`. The argument of
  Attempt 1 on `kun-thom-wreath-embeds-in-leavitt-unit-group` shows that such a `c` has finite
  order in every `GL_N(K)` with `char K = p > 0`:
  - `a` and `b` preserve every generalized eigenspace `E` of `c`;
  - `a b a^(-1) = c b` gives `det(b|E) = λ^(dim E) det(b|E)`, so every eigenvalue `λ` of `c` is a
    root of unity;
  - the unipotent part of `c` has `p`-power order.

So neither the characteristic-zero host of `char-zero-linear-groups-satisfy-permutational-boone-higman`
nor the Leavitt host of `char-two-curve-linear-groups-satisfy-permutational-boone-higman`
contains `Δ` through a linear representation. Only the permanence of the permutational class
combines them.
