Let `G` be a finitely generated virtually free group, let `p` be prime, and
let

```text
K <= (F_p^m)^G                                             (VFL1)
```

be a closed `G`-invariant subgroup defined by finitely many linear local
constraints and all their translates.  Put `A=K-hat`.  Then:

1. `A` is a finitely presented `F_p[G]`-module;
2. `A` is residually finite as a `G`-module: every nonzero `a in A` survives
   in a finite `G`-module quotient;
3. the discrete semidirect product

   ```text
   A rtimes G                                               (VFL2)
   ```

   is residually finite, hence sofic and hyperlinear;
4. consequently

   ```text
   L^infinity(K,Haar) rtimes G ~= L(A rtimes G)             (VFL3)
   ```

   is Connes embeddable and cannot contain a trace-preserving copy of a
   non-Connes-embeddable tracial von Neumann algebra.

Thus the finite-type branch of the preferred Manzoor algebraic-envelope
architecture is impossible when it retains the virtually free acting group.

## Proof

### Step 1: the dual module is finitely presented

The Pontryagin dual of the full shift `(F_p^m)^G` is the finite-rank free
`F_p[G]`-module `F_p[G]^m` (up to the harmless left/right convention).  A
linear local constraint is an element of its algebraic dual finite-support
module.  Imposing finitely many constraints at every translate says exactly
that the annihilator `K^perp` is generated by finitely many module elements.
Pontryagin duality therefore gives

```text
A ~= F_p[G]^m / K^perp,                                    (VFL4)
```

with finitely many generators and finitely many module relations.

### Step 2: finitely presented modules over `F_p[G]` are residually finite

First take `G=F` finitely generated free.  Garrido--Jaikin-Zapirain,
*Free factors and profinite completions*, arXiv:2207.00912, Proposition 5.3,
prove as the key claim in their argument that every finitely presented
`F_p[F]`-module is residually finite.  Their proof is explicit: if
`M=R^n/I`, `R=F_p[F]`, `I` is finitely generated, and `z notin I`, they
construct a finite-index submodule `L >= I` with `z notin L`.  It uses that
`F_p[F]` is a fir/coherent and the finite-codimension ideal theorem for free
group algebras.  The paper states the result for right modules; the involution
`g -> g^(-1)` gives the left-module version.

Now let `G` be virtually free.  Choose a free NORMAL subgroup `F normal G`
of finite index.  If `M` is finitely presented over `F_p[G]`, then its
restriction to `F_p[F]` is finitely presented, because `F_p[G]` is finite
free as an `F_p[F]`-module.  Given `0 != a in M`, choose a finite-index
`F`-submodule `N` not containing `a`.  Set

```text
N_G = intersection_(g in G/F) g.N.                         (VFL5)
```

There are only finitely many terms.  Since `F` is normal, `N_G` is a
`G`-submodule; it has finite additive index; and `N_G <= N`, so it still does
not contain `a`.  Hence `M` is residually finite as a `G`-module.  Apply this
to `(VFL4)`.

### Step 3: residual finiteness of the semidirect product

The virtually free group `G` is residually finite.  Let `(a,g)` be a
nonidentity element of `A rtimes G`.

- If `g != 1`, a finite quotient of `G` separates its image; kill `A`.
- If `g=1` and `a != 0`, choose a finite `G`-module quotient `A/N` in which
  `a` survives.  The action homomorphism
  `G -> Aut(A/N)` has finite image, and the finite semidirect product

  ```text
  (A/N) rtimes image(G -> Aut(A/N))
  ```

  separates `(a,1)`.

This proves residual finiteness of `(VFL2)`.  The remaining conclusions follow
from residual finite `=>` sofic `=>` hyperlinear and the trace-preserving
Fourier isomorphism `(VFL3)`.

## Exact scope

The finite-type hypothesis is load-bearing for the **residual-finiteness**
conclusion: a recursively enumerable linear subshift has a finitely generated
dual module with possibly infinitely many relations, so the
Garrido--Jaikin-Zapirain module theorem does not apply.

It is not load-bearing for the weaker conclusion needed to kill this route.
`abelian-by-virtually-free-groups-are-sofic` proves that `A rtimes G` is sofic
for every countable abelian `G`-module whenever `G` is virtually free.  Thus
neither infinite r.e. relations nor a non-Cartan embedding repair the native
Manzoor actor: its whole compact-abelian crossed product is CE.  The surviving
algebraic-envelope route must change to a non-virtually-free acting group.

In particular, merely making the finite local constraint table larger cannot
work: every finite table still produces a finitely presented module and hence
a residually finite output group.
