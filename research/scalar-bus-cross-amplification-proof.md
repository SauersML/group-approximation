For the abstract graph-of-groups statement, take a common multiple `D` of
all vertex-representation dimensions and amplify every vertex representation
to dimension `D`.  Each edge group acts at both endpoints by the same scalar
character, so the amplified representations agree literally on a maximal
tree.  They therefore extend over its iterated amalgam.  Send every remaining
HNN stable letter to `I_D`; scalarity makes both associated edge actions
equal.  This proves `(SBP1)--(SBP3)` by the universal properties of amalgams
and HNN extensions.

For the packet application, the assignment-coordinate representations give
the marked sector for allowed assignments.  For a forbidden assignment, the
selector subgroup and predicate word are central and the predicate word is
not in the selector subgroup for a nonlinear predicate.  Extend the desired
selector character to the central group generated by it and the predicate
word with predicate value `-1`, induce to the finite packet group, and take
an irreducible constituent.  Central elements retain the prescribed scalar
values, giving `(CWA9)` and hence `(SBP4)`.  Applying the abstract
cross-amplification construction to these local sectors proves `(SBP5)` for
an arbitrary global assignment.
