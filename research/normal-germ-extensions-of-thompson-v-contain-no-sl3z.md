independently reviewed.

**Setting.** `B = V_{d,r}` (a Higman–Thompson group, e.g. Thompson's `V`) acts on its Cantor space `X`, and
`B <= G <= Homeo(X)` is a finite germ extension of `B` in the sense of Belk–Hyde–Matucci (arXiv:2407.03149;
`germ-complex-and-bux-gonzalez-morse-inputs`). For `p in X`, `(G)_p` is the group of germs at `p` of elements
fixing `p`, and `(B)_p <= (G)_p` the germs of `B`. At a point with eventually periodic address `(B)_p` is
infinite cyclic, generated by the local contraction `δ_p`; at every other point it is trivial.

**Theorem A (germ carrier, no hypothesis on germs).** If a finite-index subgroup of `SL_3(Z)` embeds in `G`,
then for some finite sets `M ⊆ M' ⊆ sing(G)` and some finite-index subgroup `Γ_1` of `SL_3(Z)`,
a conjugate of `Γ_1` lies in `SingFix_G(M,M')` and the germ map `Γ_1 -> ∏_{p in M} (G)_p` is injective. So a
finite product of germ groups at singular points must contain a finite-index subgroup of `SL_3(Z)`.

**Theorem B (obstruction).** Suppose every `p in sing(G)` satisfies
- (a) `|(G)_p : (B)_p| < ∞`, or
- (b) `p` has an eventually periodic address and `(B)_p` is normal in `(G)_p`.

Then no finite-index subgroup of `SL_3(Z)` embeds in `G`.

**Corollaries.**
1. For `n >= 3`, none of `GL_n(Z)`, `SL_n(Z[1/m])`, `GL_n(Q)`, `SL_n(Q)`, `PGL_n(Q)` embeds in such a `G`: each
   contains `SL_3(Z)` (for `PGL_n(Q)`, `n >= 3`, via `SL_3(Z)`, whose image is isomorphic to it since
   `SL_3(Z)` has trivial centre).
2. The hypotheses hold for:
   - `VA`: its singular points are the eventually constant addresses, and near them the elements of Brin's
     group satisfy `f(2x) = 2f(x)`, so their germs commute with `δ_p` (case (b));
   - every Röver–Nekrashevych group `V_{d,r}G` of a bounded automata group `G` (case (a), Belk–Hyde–Matucci);
   - every finite germ extension of `V_{d,r}` to which Belk–Hyde–Matucci's Corollary 2.10 applies through its
     germ condition (either alternative), provided its singular points have eventually periodic addresses.

   So for `n >= 3` the "VA pattern" (finite germ extensions whose germs commute with the local dilation, the
   route by which `Q <= VA`) can never host `GL_n(Q)`, whatever germ groups are chosen. This extends to that
   whole regime the argument of the survey's Remark 4.12 for `VA` (arXiv:2306.16356v3).
3. A host for `GL_n(Q)`, `n >= 3`, built as a finite germ extension of `V_{d,r}` needs, at some singular point,
   germs that do not normalize the local dilation (as in the shell envelopes of
   `shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`), or an aperiodic singular point with infinite germ
   group. By Theorem A a finite product of its germ groups contains a finite-index subgroup of `SL_3(Z)`, and
   such germ extensions fall outside Corollary 2.10, so finite presentation has to go through the
   `SingFix` clause of Belk–Hyde–Matucci's Theorem 2.1.

**Remark (n = 2, BS(1,2)).** In case (a) or (b), every homomorphism `BS(1,2) = <a,t | t a t^{-1} = a^2> ->
∏_{p in M} (G)_p` sends `a` to an element of finite order (proof, Lemma 5). So the exponential distortion that
`GL_2(Q) >= BS(1,2)` needs (root obstruction O4) cannot live in the germ groups of this regime. This does not
exclude `BS(1,2) <= G`, since `BS(1,2)` has no fixed-point property; the case `n = 2` stays open for this regime
beyond `VA` itself.

**Scope.** No claim is made for other bases (`nV`, twisted Brin–Thompson groups), for case (b) at aperiodic
points, or for germ extensions outside the hypotheses of Theorem B. No priority is claimed beyond the
generalization of the survey's Remark 4.12.
