**ESTABLISHED.**  Let a finite two-player game be torically determined by

```text
r_i=(beta_i,a_i,b_i) in B x G_A x G_B,
```

where `B` is the finite scalar phase group and `G_A,G_B` are the one-player
question groups.  If `N=normalClosure({r_i})` satisfies

```text
N intersect B={1},                                      (NPS1)
```

then

```text
omega_qa=1.                                             (NPS2)
```

In particular no finite two-player torically determined game can have both
normal phase safety and `omega_qc=1>omega_qa`.

By the normal toric quotient formula, the images `abar_i` and `bbar_i` in
the one-player centralization quotients are central.  Phase safety is exactly
the assertion that the prescription

```text
(abar_i,bbar_i) |-> conjugate(beta_i)                   (NPS3)
```

is a well-defined character on the subgroup `C` generated by these central
pairs.  Since the circle group is divisible, this character extends from
`C` to the abelian group generated separately by all `abar_i` and all
`bbar_i`.  The extension splits as player characters `lambda_A lambda_B`.

Pull those characters back to the subgroups generated by the original
player words `a_i` and `b_i`.  Full group C-star algebras of subgroups embed
in those of the ambient groups, so positive Hahn--Banach extends the two
character states to states `phi_A` on `C*(G_A)` and `phi_B` on `C*(G_B)`.
Their GNS vectors are eigenvectors of every clause word, with

```text
beta_i phi_A(a_i) phi_B(b_i)=1.                         (NPS4)
```

Consequently the product state is fixed by every toric clause.  Since the
clauses determine the game, it is a perfect product strategy.

Finally, each one-player algebra is a free product of finite-dimensional
commutative answer algebras and is residually finite dimensional.  Its
finite-dimensional vector states are weak-star dense in its whole state
space.  Approximate `phi_A` and `phi_B` separately and take tensor products.
The resulting finite-dimensional tensor strategies converge on the finitely
many game coefficients to the perfect product state, proving `(NPS2)`.
