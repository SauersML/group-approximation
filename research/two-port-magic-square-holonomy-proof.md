# Compare row-major and column-major products in the transverse two-port cell

## Setup

Let `K` be generated by nine involutions `x_{ij}` (`1\leq i,j\leq 3`), subject only to the commutations inside every row and every column.  Write

``
R_i=x_{i1}x_{i2}x_{i3},
\qquad
C_j=x_{1j}x_{2j}x_{3j}.
``

Thus every `R_i` and `C_j` is itself an involution.  The two distinguished, transverse ports are the first-row and first-column products.  In a perfect representation impose

``
R_1=Z,qquad C_1=W,qquad
R_2=\alpha _2 I,quad R_3=\alpha _3 I,qquad
C_2=\beta _2 I,quad C_3=\beta _3 I,
``

where `Z,W` are arbitrary involutions a priori and
`\alpha _2,\alpha _3,\beta _2,\beta _3\in\{\pm1\}`.

The point of the calculation below is not to assume that `Z` and `W` commute.  They are products of two intersecting commuting contexts, but the two products need not commute merely because their lines meet in one cell.

## 1. The ordering holonomy is a derived word

Define the row-major and column-major words

``
P_R=(x_{11}x_{12}x_{13})(x_{21}x_{22}x_{23})(x_{31}x_{32}x_{33})
    =R_1R_2R_3,
``

``
P_C=(x_{11}x_{21}x_{31})(x_{12}x_{22}x_{32})(x_{13}x_{23}x_{33})
    =C_1C_2C_3,
``

and define the ordering holonomy

``
\Omega=P_C^{-1}P_R.
``

In the abelianization `K_{\mathrm{ab}}`, both `P_R` and `P_C` are the product of every cell generator exactly once.  Therefore

``
[P_R]_{\mathrm{ab}}=[P_C]_{\mathrm{ab}},
\qquad
[\Omega]_{\mathrm{ab}}=1,
``

so `\Omega\in K'=[K,K]`.  This conclusion uses only the incidence pattern; it does not use any scalar line-product equation.

## 2. Exact transverse-port identity

After imposing the six line products,

``
P_R=Z\alpha _2\alpha _3,
\qquad
P_C=W\beta _2\beta _3.
``

The four signs are central and square to one.  If

``
\kappa=\alpha _2\alpha _3\beta _2\beta _3,
``

then

``
\Omega
=(W\beta _2\beta _3)^{-1}(Z\alpha _2\alpha _3)
=\kappa WZ.
``

Use the commutator convention `[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}`.  Since `W` and `Z` are involutions,

``
\boxed{\ [W,Z]=WZWZ=\Omega^2\ }.
``

This is the exact universal relation supplied by the single square.  It does not by itself say that the ports commute: their commutator is carried by the square of a specific nonabelian ordering defect.

## 3. Class-two and Pauli completions necessarily quotient the ports

Let `\rho` be a representation in which the image of the derived subgroup is central:

``
\rho(K')\subseteq Z(\rho(K)).
``

This includes every representation whose image is nilpotent of class at most two, in particular the fixed Pauli/extraspecial representations normally used for the Mermin--Peres square.  Since `\Omega\in K'`, the operator `\rho(\Omega)` is central.  The identity
`WZ=\kappa\Omega` then makes `WZ` central as well.  Commuting `WZ` past `W` gives

``
(WZ)W=W(WZ)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
WZW=Z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
ZW=WZ.
``

Hence a fixed Pauli or, more generally, class-two completion cannot be a transparent two-port absorber for arbitrary source involutions.  It extends only the quotient in which the two ports commute.

This also marks the limit of the conclusion: in a general representation `\Omega` may be noncentral, and the calculation does not prove that `W` and `Z` commute in the full universal group.

## 4. Robust form

Let `\|\cdot\|_2` be any unitarily invariant Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Suppose the six line-product equations have defects

``
\begin{aligned}
\|R_1-Z\|_2&\leq\eta_{R_1},&
\|R_2-\alpha _2I\|_2&\leq\eta_{R_2},&
\|R_3-\alpha _3I\|_2&\leq\eta_{R_3},\\
\|C_1-W\|_2&\leq\eta_{C_1},&
\|C_2-\beta _2I\|_2&\leq\eta_{C_2},&
\|C_3-\beta _3I\|_2&\leq\eta_{C_3}.
\end{aligned}
``

Put

``
\eta=\eta_{R_1}+\eta_{R_2}+\eta_{R_3}
     +\eta_{C_1}+\eta_{C_2}+\eta_{C_3}.
``

Repeated use of
`\|AB-A'B'\|_2\leq\|A-A'\|_2+\|B-B'\|_2`
for unitaries gives

``
\|P_R-Z\alpha _2\alpha _3\|_2
\leq\eta_{R_1}+\eta_{R_2}+\eta_{R_3},
``

and

``
\|P_C-W\beta _2\beta _3\|_2
\leq\eta_{C_1}+\eta_{C_2}+\eta_{C_3}.
``

Inversion is an isometry on unitaries, so another telescoping step yields

``
\boxed{\ \|\Omega-\kappa WZ\|_2\leq\eta\ }.
``

Finally, for unitaries `A,B`,

``
\|A^2-B^2\|_2
\leq
\|A(A-B)\|_2+\|(A-B)B\|_2
=2\|A-B\|_2.
``

Taking `A=\Omega` and `B=\kappa WZ` gives

``
\boxed{\ \|\Omega^2-[W,Z]\|_2\leq2\eta\ }.
``

Thus the exact identity has a dimension-free stability estimate, but only for the holonomy relation; no unjustified near-centrality conclusion is available.

## 5. Consequence for the search

A one-square transverse two-port gadget is qualitatively different from the one-port and common-row absorbers:

- its fixed Pauli sector is not transparent, because it forces the source ports to commute;
- any genuinely transparent completion of arbitrary noncommuting ports must realize the derived word `\Omega` noncentrally, with `\Omega^2=[W,Z]`;
- a decoder based on this cell would have to control that noncentral holonomy, not merely the six line signs.

The remaining exact question is whether the full universal cell group admits finite-dimensional extensions for every pair of involutions with the required noncentral holonomy.  The calculation above neither constructs those extensions nor rules them out; it isolates the only commutator channel that such an extension must use.
