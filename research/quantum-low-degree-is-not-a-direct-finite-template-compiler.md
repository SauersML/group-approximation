ESTABLISHED.  The test `QLD^(2)(m,d,q)` in arXiv:1801.03821 has two features which prevent
a direct application of `relative-coset-pauli-incidence-compiler`.

First, its mixed component samples uniform

```text
(x,z,u,u') in F_q^m x F_q^m x F_q x F_q.                       (QD1)
```

It substitutes the point questions `(X,x)` and `(Z,z)` into a commutation or
generalized Magic-Square test according to the bilinear phase.  Thus every X
point has growing mixed degree and the contracted X--Z incidence contains
the complete point-pair rectangles.  Giving these incidences finitely many
offset labels repeats adjacent relative syllable pairs on eight-syllable
rectangles, exactly the obstruction in
`finite-offset-pauli-rectangles-violate-relative-gr-sixth`.

Second, the same-basis agreement component uses every point/two-plane
incidence.  For `m>=3`, two distinct points on an affine line lie in many
two-planes, so the point-plane incidence graph has four-cycles and growing
degree.  These pure agreement cycles are not themselves fatal to the
balanced Greendlinger theorem, but ordinary bounded-degree occurrence
replacement duplicates each mixed point-pair test inside connected decoded
fibers.  Then `equality-synchronized-lifts-create-two-pair-contours` produces
a mixed contour with only two pair letters.

The low-degree theorem therefore supplies the right **analytic decoder** but
not the required **finite orbit geometry**.  Neither of the following is a
valid direct completion:

- fixing `q`, because `fixed-field-plane-point-loses-uniform-low-degree-robustness` applies;
- applying ordinary equality-cloud degree reduction, because it recreates
  the two-pair relative contour.

The remaining conversion has to sparsify agreement and mixed sampling
inside the algebraic code itself, before occurrences are split, or recursively
compile the growing plane packet by finite templates.
