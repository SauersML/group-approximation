Let `(G, w)` be a counterexample, `w in G * <t>` with `deg_t(w) = m != 0` and
`1 != a in G` dying in `G_w`, and let `A` be countable.

Since `w` lies in the factor `G * <t>` of `G * A * <t> = A * (G * <t>)`,

    (G * A * <t>)/<<w>>  =  A * G_w ,

so `a` still dies, and `a != 1` in `G * A`.  Hence `(G * A, w)` is a
counterexample whose coefficient group contains `A`.

Now apply the prerequisite to `(G * A, w)`.  Reading its proof
(`kl-simple-envelope-via-finite-presentation`), the envelope is produced by
embedding a countable coefficient group into a two-generator nonabelian
simple group, and any subgroup of the coefficient group is carried along by
that embedding.  So the resulting simple counterexample contains `A`.

## The one place care is needed

The prerequisite is applied to `G * A`, not to `G`, and its own first step
replaces the coefficient group by the subgroup generated by the coefficients
of `w` — which lies inside `G` and loses `A` again.  The embedding must
therefore be taken of `G * A` itself, which is legitimate because the
localization step is a *sufficiency* reduction: it says a counterexample
exists over the coefficient subgroup, not that one must pass to it.  Any
countable overgroup of a counterexample's coefficient subgroup that still
kills a nontrivial element — and `G * A` does, by the displayed
decomposition — may be fed to the embedding theorem directly.
