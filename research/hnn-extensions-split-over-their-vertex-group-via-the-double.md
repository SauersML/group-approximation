**ESTABLISHED** (elementary; no novelty claimed for item 1, which is standard Bass--Serre theory;
lane proof, not yet refereed).

## Statement

Let `G = A*_φ = ⟨A, t | t c t^-1 = φ(c) (c ∈ C_1)⟩` with `φ : C_1 → C_2`, `C_i ≤ A`.
1. **The double.** `D = ⟨A, tAt^-1⟩ ≤ G` is the amalgam `A *_(C_2) tAt^-1`, where `C_2 ≤ A` is
   identified with `tC_1t^-1 ≤ tAt^-1`. `G` is the HNN extension of `D` along
   `ψ = c_t : A → tAt^-1`, via `s ↦ t`. It is non-ascending iff the original extension is
   (`C_1 ≠ A ≠ C_2`).
2. **Permanence form.** Suppose `Γ` acts on `X` with type (A), and:
   - `D ≤ Γ`;
   - `A` has finitely many orbits on `X`;
   - a permutation `π` of `X` satisfies `π a π^-1 = t a t^-1` for all `a ∈ A`;
   - `Γ*_ψ → Sym(X)`, `s ↦ π`, is injective.

   Then `G ∈ B_A`, so `G` embeds in a finitely presented simple group.
3. **No orbit-finiteness obstruction.** The regular-orbit invariant of
   `type-a-actors-have-no-proper-power-cyclic-edge-splittings` constrains the edges of the given
   splitting. It does not constrain the actor `Γ*_ψ`, whose edge is the vertex group `A`.

## Lesson for general BH

The orbit-finite-edge condition for HNN permanence is a property of a chosen splitting, not of the
group. Moving to the double fattens every edge to a whole vertex group. So thin, proper-power, or
infinite-index edges are never the obstruction by themselves.

What remains is symmetric amalgam permanence: an actor for `A *_(C_2) A^t` in which the two copies
of `A` act permutation-isomorphically, together with one Britton-type faithfulness condition. In
Cantor hosts this symmetry is exactly where the dynamical obstructions sit. For `BG` the two copies
are `⟨a_0, a_1⟩` and `⟨a_1, a_2⟩`, and entropy, equicontinuity and germ rigidity forbid them to act
alike inside a finite-entropy brick host
(`bg-in-isometric-brick-hosts-needs-unbounded-exponents`,
`equicontinuous-square-conjugators-are-trivial-in-brick-hosts`). Permutation actors carry no such
invariants.

## Proof

**Item 1.**
- *The amalgam.* In the Bass--Serre tree of `G`, the vertices `A` and `tA` are adjacent, and by
  Britton's lemma `A ∩ tAt^-1 = C_2 = tC_1t^-1`. The subgroup generated by the stabilizers of two
  adjacent vertices is their amalgam over the edge stabilizer (Serre, *Trees*, §I.4). So
  `D = A *_(C_2) tAt^-1`.
- *The HNN extension.* Let `H = ⟨D, s | s a s^-1 = t a t^-1 (a ∈ A)⟩`. Then `s ↦ t` defines
  `H → G`, onto because `A` and `t` generate `G`. In the other direction, `A ↦ A` and `t ↦ s`
  respects the relations of `G`: `s c s^-1 = t c t^-1 = φ(c)` in `D`, since there
  `tc t^-1 ∈ tC_1t^-1` is identified with `φ(c) ∈ C_2`. The two maps are mutually inverse on
  generators.
- *Non-ascending.* `A = D` iff `tAt^-1 ≤ A` iff `C_1 = A`, and similarly `tAt^-1 = D` iff
  `C_2 = A`.

**Item 2.** Apply `stable-permutation-hnn-of-type-a-actor-is-type-a` with `D_1 = A`,
`D_2 = tAt^-1` and the given `ψ`. Part (a) gives type (A) for `Γ*_ψ`. Part (b), with the input
`D` in place of `A` there, has `D ∩ D_1 = A` and `D ∩ D_2 = tAt^-1`. So `⟨D, s⟩ ≅ D*_ψ ≅ G`, which
lies in `Γ*_ψ` and hence in `B_A`.

**Item 3.** Item 2 concerns type (A) actions of `Γ*_ψ`. The splittings of `Γ*_ψ` used by
`type-a-actors-split-only-over-orbit-finite-edges` have edge groups conjugate to `A`, which is
orbit-finite by hypothesis. The thin splitting of `G ≤ Γ*_ψ` is not a splitting of the actor. `∎`
