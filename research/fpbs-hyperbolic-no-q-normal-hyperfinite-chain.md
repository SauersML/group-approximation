**OPEN (obstruction target).** Let `Gamma` be a nonelementary hyperbolic group
and `a` any free p.m.p. action with orbit relation `R`. Use the notation of
`fpbs-q-normal-hyperfinite-chain-forces-cost-one`: `T^g`, `QN(T)`, and the
chain `T_(k+1) = T_k v R_(<QN(T_k)>)`. Then no aperiodic hyperfinite
`T_0 <= R` has a chain (indexed by a countable ordinal) with union `R`.

**Stakes both ways.**

* If it holds, every mechanism of hyperfinite-spine type dies on every
  hyperbolic group with property (T). That covers wq-normal amenable subgroups,
  the normal commuting-direction routing of Section 8 of
  `research/artifacts/fpbs/docs/cost-cycle-structure.md`, and their measured
  q-normal analogues. Cocompact lattices in `Sp(n,1)` and `F_4^(-20)` are
  hyperbolic Kazhdan groups, and `fpbs-kazhdan-sparse-spines` records that
  they are outside every published fixed-price-one class. For them the
  cycle-tail compactness target cannot come from this mechanism.
* If it fails for the Bernoulli action of one hyperbolic Kazhdan group,
  `fpbs-q-normal-hyperfinite-chain-forces-cost-one` gives that group fixed
  price one. That group lies in the class left open by Question 2.67 of
  Gaboriau's lecture notes, as recorded in
  `fpbs-bernoulli-cycle-tail-compactness`.

## Attempts

**Groups with positive first L2 Betti number: settled.** A chain forces
`C(R)=1`, and hence `beta_1^(2)(Gamma)=0`, by
`fpbs-q-normal-hyperfinite-chain-forces-cost-one`. That lemma is currently
OPEN in the graph: referees found its `C(R)=1` and `beta_1^(2)=0` parts sound
and refuted only the import scope of its fixed-price consequence. So the claim holds for every
nonelementary hyperbolic group with `beta_1^(2)>0`, for instance free and
surface groups. That calibrates the statement: it is true wherever cost can
see it. It gives nothing when `beta_1^(2)=0`, because the only available cost lower
bound is then `1`. In particular it gives nothing for Kazhdan groups, which
have cost-one actions by Hutchcroft--Pete
(`fpbs-kazhdan-groups-have-cost-one`).

**Spines generated by subgroups: dies at the start.** If `T_0 = R_H`, then `H`
is an infinite amenable subgroup and hence virtually cyclic. Freeness gives
`QN(R_H) = {g : H cap g^(-1)Hg infinite}`. For `H` virtually cyclic in a
hyperbolic group this is the maximal elementary subgroup `E(H)`. This is the
standard commensurator fact (Olshanskii), recalled here and not re-verified.
So `T_1 = R_(E(H))` is again generated by a virtually cyclic group, and the
chain stops there. That rules out group-generated spines but says nothing
about genuinely measured `T_0`.

**Normal spines: killed modulo standard facts.** Suppose `T_0^s = T_0` for
every generator `s`, and hence for every `g`. This is exactly the shape of
Section 8. In `M = L^infinity(X) rtimes Gamma`, conjugation by `u_g` carries
the partial isometry of a partial map with graph in `T_0^g` to one with graph
in `T_0`. So every `u_g` normalizes `A = L(T_0)`, and
`N_M(A)'' = M`. Popa--Vaes, arXiv:1201.2824 (Crelle 2014), source of v9
fetched 2026-09-17, Theorem 1.4 (label `thm.main-two`), verbatim:

> Let $\Gamma$ be a weakly amenable, bi-exact group and let
> $\Gamma \actson (B,\tau)$ be an arbitrary trace preserving action on the
> tracial von Neumann algebra $(B,\tau)$. Put $M = B \rtimes \Gamma$.
> If $q \in M$ is a projection and $A \subset qMq$ is a von Neumann subalgebra
> that is amenable relative to $B$, then $\cN_{qMq}(A)\dpr$ remains amenable
> relative to $B$, or $A \prec_M B$.

Their Lemma `lem.exam-wa-bi-exact` states, verbatim: "The following groups are
weakly amenable and bi-exact~: (\cite{Oz03,Oz07}) non-elementary hyperbolic
groups". Take `B = L^infinity(X)` and `q = 1`. The deduction also needs three
standard facts that are recalled and not re-verified here:

* (a) `L(T_0)` is hyperfinite (Connes--Feldman--Weiss) and therefore amenable
  relative to `B`;
* (b) `L(T_0)` does not intertwine into `B` when `T_0` is aperiodic;
* (c) `B rtimes Gamma` is amenable relative to `B` only when `Gamma` is
  amenable.

Given these, `M` would be amenable relative to `B`, so `Gamma` would be
amenable. That is a contradiction. So no free p.m.p. action of a nonelementary
hyperbolic group, Kazhdan or not, has a normal aperiodic hyperfinite spine,
and the literal Section 8 mechanism dies on the whole class. The same argument
applies to lattices in rank-one simple Lie groups with finite center, which
the same lemma lists.

**One-sided q-normalizers: where it dies.** When `T_0 cap T_0^g` is only
aperiodic, `u_g` does not normalize `L(T_0)`. It carries only the subalgebra
`L(T_0 cap T_0^g)` into `L(T_0)`. Theorem 1.4 above is stated for
`N_(qMq)(A)''` only, and no q-normalizer version was located.

The boundary route also stops. It uses Adams' theorem that an amenable
subrelation of a hyperbolic-group relation carries an invariant measurable
field of at most two boundary points, containing the support of every
invariant field of measures. That form of the theorem is recalled from memory
and not verified against Adams (Topology 1994). Apply it to
`S = T_0 cap T_0^g` with the fields of `T_0` and of `T_0^g`: the union
`phi(x) cup g^(-1) phi(gx)` has at most two points. Where `|phi|=2` almost
everywhere this forces `phi(gx) = g phi(x)` for every generator. The result is
a `Gamma`-equivariant map to unordered boundary pairs. Averaging gives a
`Gamma`-invariant probability measure on `boundary(Gamma)`, which is
impossible for nonelementary `Gamma`.

**Where it dies:** single-point fields. Neighbouring values `{a}`, `{a,b}`,
`{b}` satisfy the at-most-two-points constraint without any global
equivariance, so neither Popa--Vaes nor the union bound excludes a
horocyclic-type spine in which every class of `T_0` and every aperiodic
overlap `T_0 cap T_0^g` selects one boundary point. The next test is whether
unimodularity of the orbit relation rules out a measurable horocyclic spine
carrying a Busemann-type height cocycle. The AN-subgroup pattern in `Sp(n,1)`,
where `N` is normal in `AN`, is the non-unimodular model that has to be
excluded.
