> `GL_fin(F_2^(V/L)) x| Q` is finitely generated, sofic, not MF, and has no

From its route `ascending-hnn-finitary-linear-family-proof`:

> `V = N x| <u>` with `u` implementing it has the displayed presentation

> `L_j = u^-j L u^j` increase with union `N`.

> `L_j/B` acts faithfully with finite orbits, so it is residually finite and sofic.
> Every finitely generated subgroup of `N/B` sits in some `L_j/B` and has finite
> orbits, and `Q/(N/B)` is `Z`.

From `elek-szabo-sofic-permanence` (Elek–Szabó, Theorem 1):

> direct products, subgroups, inverse limits, direct limits, ... if `N ◁ G`, `N`
> is sofic and `G/N` is amenable then `G` is also sofic.

Here `N` is the direct limit, `B = ker(V -> Sym(V/L))` and `Q = V/B`. The
coefficient group is `K_0 = GL_fin(F_2^(X))`, `X = V/L`: the invertible
operators that are the identity on all but finitely many basis vectors `e_x`.

## Step 1. The Z-splitting

`V = N x| <u>` gives a homomorphism `V -> Z` with `u -> 1` and kernel `N`. Its
kernel contains `B`, since `B <= L_0 = L <= N`. So it descends to `Q -> Z`,
with kernel `N/B`. This matches the quoted `Q/(N/B) = Z`.

Compose with `G -> Q` to get `G -> Z`. It is split by `u`, and its kernel is

```text
K = K_0 x| (N/B).
```

So `G = K x|_phi Z`, where `phi = Ad u` restricted to `K`.

## Step 2. K is locally residually finite (item 1)

Let `H <= K` be generated by `(k_1, h_1), ..., (k_r, h_r)`.
- Let `H_0 = <h_1, ..., h_r> <= N/B`. By the import, `H_0` lies in some `L_j/B`,
  and all its orbits on `X` are finite.
- Let `F ⊂ X` be the finite set of basis indices outside which some `k_i` is not
  the identity. Put `Y = H_0 F`. It is finite, being a finite union of finite
  orbits.
- Let `K_Y ≤ K_0` be the finite group of operators that preserve `F_2^Y` and fix
  every `e_x` with `x ∉ Y`. `H_0` preserves `Y`, so it normalizes `K_Y`. Hence
  `H <= K_Y x| H_0`.

The map `(k, h) -> ((k, h|_Y), h)` from `K_Y x| H_0` to
`(K_Y x| Sym(Y)) x H_0` is a homomorphism. It is injective, because the second
coordinate recovers `h` and the first then recovers `k`.
- `K_Y x| Sym(Y)` is finite.
- `H_0` is residually finite, as a subgroup of the residually finite group `L_j/B`.

So `H` is residually finite.

**Locally RF implies LEF.** Given a finite `F' ⊂ K`, let `H = <F'>`. Take a
finite quotient of `H` that is injective on the finite set `F' F'`. Its
restriction to `F'` is a partial homomorphism into a finite group, injective on
`F'`.

## Step 3. The doubling lemma

**Involutions.**
- `alpha^2 = 1` is clear.
- `beta^2(x, y) = beta(phi y, phi^-1 x) = (x, y)`.

**Conjugacy.** For `theta(x, y) = (x, phi y)`:
- `alpha theta (x, y) = (phi y, x)`;
- `theta^-1 alpha theta (x, y) = (phi y, phi^-1 x) = beta(x, y)`.

So `(n, beta) -> (theta n, alpha)` is an isomorphism `N^2 x| <beta> -> N^2 x| <alpha>`.

**The product.** `alpha beta (x, y) = alpha(phi y, phi^-1 x) = (phi^-1 x, phi y)`.

**Amalgam.** Let `C = A * B` act on `N`, with `A` and `B` subgroups of `Aut(N)`.
The groups `N x| C` and `(N x| A) *_N (N x| B)` have the same universal property.
- A homomorphism from either group to a group `P` is a triple `(f_N, f_A, f_B)`
  of homomorphisms with `f_c(c) f_N(n) f_c(c)^-1 = f_N(c . n)` for `c in A ∪ B`.
- For `N x| C` this uses that the set of `c in C` satisfying the equivariance
  is a subgroup, so it suffices to check it on the generators `A ∪ B`.
- Both groups are generated by the images of `N`, `A` and `B`.

So the two groups are isomorphic, compatibly with `N`, `A` and `B`. The
experiment's check 6 verifies the three displayed identities on a toy.

## Step 4. Items 2 and 3

**Item 2.** A finitely generated subgroup of `K wr Z/2 = K^2 x| <alpha>` lies in
`H^2 x| <alpha>` for some finitely generated `H ≤ K`. Take `H` generated by both
coordinates of the `K^2`-parts of the generators.
- `H^2` is residually finite by Step 2.
- `H^2` has index at most two in `H^2 x| <alpha>`.
- A group with a residually finite subgroup of finite index is residually finite.
  Separate the cosets by the finite quotient. For `1 != g` in the subgroup, take
  a finite-index normal subgroup `U` of the subgroup with `g ∉ U`; the
  intersection of the conjugates of `U` is normal of finite index and misses `g`.

**Item 3, not MF.** By Step 3,
`(K x 1) x| <alpha beta> ≅ K x|_{phi^-1} Z ≅ K x|_phi Z = G`,
the last isomorphism sending the generator to its inverse. `G` is not MF, by the
import. MF passes to subgroups: restrict the injective homomorphism into the norm
ultraproduct. So `Gamma` is not MF.

**Item 3, finitely generated.** `G` is finitely generated. Replacing generators
`k_i u^(n_i)` by `k_i` and `u`, it is generated by `u` and finitely many
`k_i ∈ K`.
- Via `u -> (alpha beta)^-1`, the subgroup generated by `alpha`, `beta` and the
  `(k_i, 1)` contains `K x 1`.
- Conjugating by `alpha` gives `1 x K`.
- So `Gamma = <alpha, beta, (k_i, 1)>`.

**Item 3, sofic.** The index-two subgroup `K^2 x| <alpha beta>` embeds in
`(K x|_{phi^-1} Z) x (K x|_phi Z) ≅ G x G`, by
`(x, y, (alpha beta)^n) -> ((x, t^n), (y, u^n))`.
- `G x G` is sofic (Elek–Szabó, products), so the subgroup is sofic (subgroups).
- Then `Gamma` is sofic, as a sofic normal subgroup with finite quotient
  (Elek–Szabó, amenable extensions).

## Step 5. The finite-generation obstruction (remark in the target)

Let `N` be finitely generated and residually finite, and `phi ∈ Aut(N)`.
- `N` has only finitely many subgroups of each finite index. So
  `N_m = ∩{U : [N : U] <= m}` is characteristic, of finite index, and
  `∩_m N_m = 1`.
- `phi` induces an automorphism of finite order `o_m` on `N/N_m`. Hence
  `N x| Z -> (N/N_m) x| Z/o_m` is a finite quotient, and these quotients
  separate the points of `N x| Z`.
- The index-two subgroup `N^2 x| <alpha beta>` of `Gamma(N, phi)` embeds in a
  product of two such groups, so it is residually finite. By the finite-index
  argument of Step 4, `Gamma(N, phi)` is residually finite.
