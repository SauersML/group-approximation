source as stated in BBMZ. Part 2 uses standard circle dynamics, which were not re-read. Part 3
is elementary. Not independently reviewed. No priority is claimed.

## Setting

`E = B/C` is Hall's group, as in `compact-skew-products-cannot-carry-divisible-fiber-subgroups`:
- `E = ⟨a, b, t⟩`;
- `a(α) = e_12(α)`, `b(β) = e_23(β)`, `c(γ) = e_13(γ)` modulo `C = c(Z)`, and
  `t = diag(1,p,1)`;
- `A = c(Z[1/p]) ≅ C_(p^∞)` is central, and `c(αβ) = [a(α), b(β)]` up to sign.

## Statement

1. **Not in V.** `E` does not embed in Thompson's group `V`.
2. **Not in circle groups.** `E` does not embed in `Homeo(S^1)`, so not in `T` or `F`. It does
   not embed in `Homeo(R)` either.
3. **No eventually periodic ends.** Let `Γ = ⟨a(Z[1/p]), b(Z[1/p]), A⟩` act on a compact space
   `Y` by skew products `Ψ_n` on `Y × F_S`, one copy `Y_n` for each `n ∈ Z`, with `A` in the
   fiber. Let `t` map `Y_n` to `Y_(n+1)` by a homeomorphism, with a locally constant fiber
   shift, so that `E` acts on `⊔_n Y_n × F_S`. This is the induced space of Shapiro's lemma,
   a Houghton-type host with two ends. Then the realizations cannot be eventually periodic:
   there are no `m >= 1`, `N`, and skew-product homeomorphisms
   `ι_n: Y_(n+m) × F_S → Y_n × F_S` with locally constant fiber shifts, such that `ι_n`
   conjugates the `Γ`-action on copy `n+m` to the `Γ`-action on copy `n` for all `n >= N`.
   The same holds at the other end.

## Proof

1. **V.** `a(Z[1/p]) ≅ Z[1/p]` is a non-cyclic subgroup of `Q`. BBMZ, arXiv:2306.16356v3,
   Theorem `thm:NoSL3ZInV` (Higman 1974), read at source: "every torsion-free nilpotent group
   that embeds into `V` is free abelian. In particular, `Q` does not embed into `V`, and
   neither does any non-cyclic subgroup of `Q`".
2. **Circle.**
   - **Reduction to orientation-preserving maps.** Let `E^+ = E ∩ Homeo^+(S^1)`, of index at
     most 2. `A` is divisible, so `A ≤ E^+`. Squares lie in `E^+`, so
     `[a(α)^2, b(1)^2] = c(4α)`, and these fill `A` as `α` ranges over `Z[1/p]`. Hence
     `A ≤ [E^+, E^+]`.
   - **Rotation numbers.** A finite-order orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism with a
     fixed point is the identity. So every `z ≠ 1` in `A` acts freely, and the rotation
     number is injective on the abelian group `A`. Its image is infinite, hence dense.
   - **Semiconjugacy (standard; Ghys, not re-read).** An abelian action with dense rotation
     numbers has a unique minimal set `K`. It is semiconjugate to the rotation action by a
     monotone degree-one map `h`.
   - **The contradiction.** Each `g ∈ E^+` commutes with `A`, so it preserves `K`, and `h`
     carries it to a map commuting with a dense set of rotations, that is, a rotation. So
     `g ↦ (rotation)` is a homomorphism, and `[E^+, E^+]` fixes `K` pointwise. But
     `A ≤ [E^+, E^+]` and no `z ≠ 1` in `A` has a fixed point. Contradiction.
   - **The line.** For `Homeo(R)`, note that `A` has elements of order `p^k`, while torsion in
     `Homeo(R)` has order at most 2.
3. **Ends.**
   - Suppose such `ι_n` exist. For `n >= N`, `T' = ι_n ∘ t^m` is a homeomorphism of the single
     compact bundle `Y_n × F_S` with locally constant fiber shift. It satisfies
     `T' ĝ T'^-1 = (t^m g t^-m)^` on copy `n`.
   - So `E_m = ⟨Γ, t^m⟩` acts on the compact bundle `Y_n × F_S` by skew products with
     locally constant cocycles, with `A` in the fiber.
   - `E_m` is generated by `a, b, t^m`, since `t^(mk) a t^(-mk) = a(p^(-mk))` and `b(β)`
     likewise, and these generate `Γ` together with the commutators. So `E_m` is finitely
     generated and contains `A`.
   - This contradicts `compact-skew-products-cannot-carry-divisible-fiber-subgroups`. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

The minimal non-residually-finite soluble case rules out four host shapes at once:
- compact bundles with the center in the fiber;
- `V`, which has no `Z[1/p]`;
- circle groups, where central torsion inside commutators is impossible;
- Houghton-type ends that repeat.

What survives is a host whose divisibility comes from odometers (as in `2V_τ` or `H_P`, which
contain `BS(1,p)`). Its structure at infinity must be aperiodic: the end cocycles `Ψ_n` never
become periodic up to locally constant gauges.

So a germ-extension host needs a germ group at the end that sees this aperiodic family. In
the natural `Q_p × F_S` model the family is `E` itself. By `shell-envelope-fp-forces-fp-germ-group`
and its analogues, that points to a finitely presented overgroup as germ group, such as
Abels' `A_p/C`. That is the next step to try.
