Setting and notation of `kl-p-power-index-reduces-to-p-nonsingular-square-systems`:
`H <| G`, `Q = G/H` a finite `p`-group of order `n`, `w in G * <t>` reduced
with `p` not dividing `deg_t(w)`, root `c in Q`, unknowns `y_q`, equations
`rho_q`.

1. **Gauge to root `1`.** Pick `g in G` with `gH = c`. The substitution
   `t -> t g` is an automorphism of `G * <t>` fixing `G`, so it preserves
   injectivity of `G`. The new word `w'` has root `1`. Rotate `w'` to begin with
   a letter `t^(±1)`:

       w' = t^(eps_1) a_1 t^(eps_2) a_2 ... t^(eps_k) a_k,     a_j in G.

   Let `R <= Q` be generated by the images `phi(a_j)`.
2. **Localization shrinks the quotient.** If `R != Q`, injectivity of
   `G_R = phi^(-1)(R)` at `w'` implies injectivity of `G` at `w`. `G_R` contains
   `H` with quotient `R`, a smaller `p`-group. So one may assume `R = Q`.
3. **Occurrence sets.** With root `1`, the letter `t^(eps_j)` of `w'` sits at
   level `L_j = phi(a_1 ... a_(j-1))` for both signs. Let `Lambda = {L_j}`. Then
   the reduced word `rho_q` contains exactly the unknowns `y_x` with
   `x in q Lambda`, and no free cancellation between them occurs.
4. **Connected and regular.** If `R = Q != 1` then `|Lambda| >= 2`:
   - every equation contains exactly `|Lambda|` unknowns;
   - every unknown occurs in exactly `|Lambda|` equations;
   - the incidence graph is connected.

   Consequently any `k` equations with `0 < k < n` contain more than `k`
   unknowns. The system has no proper square subsystem. No ordering of the
   equations lets each one introduce a single new unknown.
5. **The degenerate case.** `|Lambda| = 1` iff every `phi(a_j) = 1`, iff
   `w' in H * <t>`. Then the system is `n` translates of one equation over `H`,
   and `G -> G_w` is injective iff `H -> H_(w')` is. When `H` is torsion-free
   and the degree is `±1`, Klyachko's theorem gives injectivity.

## Why it matters

The reduction route for torsion-free-by-finite-`p`-group coefficients ends in a
connected `p`-nonsingular square system with at least two unknowns per
equation. Single-equation theorems (Klyachko, Forester--Rourke) cannot be
applied equation by equation, so a proof has to solve a genuinely coupled
system over a torsion-free group. That is Howie's conjecture, "neither proved
nor disproved" (pinned in the artifact).
