Notation is as in the target. Write `G_Z = C(C,Z) ⋊ V`, with `V` acting by
`(h·f)(w) = f(h^{-1}w)`, and write the group `C(C,Z)` additively.

**Step 0 (two inequalities).** `ℓ(ab) ≤ ℓ(a) + ℓ(b)`, since
`abA Δ A ⊆ a(bA Δ A) ∪ (aA Δ A)` and `|a(bA Δ A)| = ℓ(b)`. Also `ℓ(a^{-1}) = ℓ(a)`. Hence:
- if `a` and `b` commute and are elliptic, then `ab` is elliptic, as
  `ℓ((ab)^N) ≤ ℓ(a^N) + ℓ(b^N)`;
- conjugates of elliptic elements are elliptic, as `ℓ(k a^N k^{-1}) ≤ 2ℓ(k) + ℓ(a^N)`;
- finite-order elements are elliptic.

Precomposing a commensurating action with an automorphism of the group gives a commensurating
action. So "elliptic in every commensurating action" is invariant under automorphisms, for
example coordinate permutations of `nV`.

**Step 1 (`G_Z` is perfect and has no proper finite-index subgroup; `1_C` is central).**
- *Centre.* Constant functions are `V`-invariant, so `1_C` commutes with `V` and with
  `C(C,Z)`, hence is central.
- *Coinvariants vanish.* Let `D ≤ C(C,Z)` be the subgroup generated by all `h·f − f`. In `G_Z`
  one has `h f h^{-1} f^{-1} = h·f − f`, so `D ≤ [G_Z, G_Z]`.
  - `C(C,Z)` is generated by the indicators `1_{C(u)}` of cylinders, since a locally constant
    function on the compact space `C` is constant on the cylinders of some fixed length.
  - For nonempty words `u, u'`, the proper clopens `C(u)` and `C(u')` have nonempty complements,
    so some `h ∈ V` maps `C(u)` onto `C(u')`, by `u w ↦ u' w`, completed on the complements. The
    complements are nonempty finite unions of cylinders, which can be refined to the same number
    of cylinders and matched by prefix replacements. Then `h·1_{C(u)} = 1_{C(u')}`, so
    `1_{C(u)} ≡ 1_{C(u')} (mod D)`.
  - In particular `1_{C(0)} = 1_{C(00)} + 1_{C(01)} ≡ 2·1_{C(0)}`, so `1_{C(0)} ∈ D`. Hence
    every `1_{C(u)}` with `u` nonempty lies in `D`, and so does `1_C = 1_{C(0)} + 1_{C(1)}`.

  Thus `D = C(C,Z)`.
- *Perfect.* By `thompson-v-finitely-presented-infinite-simple`, `V` is infinite and simple,
  and it is nonabelian, so `V = [V,V]`. With `D = C(C,Z)` this gives `[G_Z, G_Z] = G_Z`.
- *No finite quotients.* Let `H ≤ G_Z` have finite index, and let `N` be its normal core, which
  is normal of finite index. Then `N ∩ V` is a normal subgroup of `V` of finite index. Since `V`
  is infinite, `N ∩ V ≠ 1`, and since `V` is simple, `N ⊇ V`. For `h ∈ V` and `f ∈ C(C,Z)`,
  `h·f − f = h (f h^{-1} f^{-1}) ∈ N`, because `f h^{-1} f^{-1} ∈ N` by normality. So
  `N ⊇ D = C(C,Z)`, hence `N = G_Z` and `H = G_Z`.

Quotients inherit all three properties: a finite-index subgroup of a quotient pulls back to a
finite-index subgroup, and images of central elements are central in the image.

**Step 2 (a copy of `G_Z` centralizing a single-brick fibre element).** Let `n ≥ 2`, let
`B = C(u_1) × ⋯ × C(u_{n−1}) ⊆ C^{n−1}` be a brick, and let `s ∈ V`. Let `g_{B,s} ∈ nV` be the
map `(x', y) ↦ (x', s(y))` for `x' ∈ B` and the identity off `B × C`. Define
`Ψ : G_Z → nV` on generators, for points `(u_1 w, x_2, …, x_{n−1}, y) ∈ B × C` (both maps are the
identity off `B × C`):

```text
Ψ(f) : (u_1 w, x_2, …, y) ↦ (u_1 w,    x_2, …, s^{f(w)}(y))     f ∈ C(C,Z)
Ψ(h) : (u_1 w, x_2, …, y) ↦ (u_1 h(w), x_2, …, y)               h ∈ V
```

- *Elements of `nV`.* `Ψ(h)` is `h` conjugated into the cylinder `C(u_1)` by the prefix map and
  extended by the identity, so it is a finite union of brick maps. `Ψ(f)` is `id × s^k` on each
  brick `C(u_1 v) × C(u_2) × ⋯ × C(u_{n−1}) × C` on which `f` equals `k`. It is the identity
  off `B × C`, so it is also a finite union of brick maps.
- *Homomorphism.* `f ↦ Ψ(f)` is a homomorphism, since `s^{f(w)} s^{f'(w)} = s^{(f+f')(w)}` on
  each fibre, and so is `h ↦ Ψ(h)`. The semidirect relation holds: `Ψ(h)Ψ(f)Ψ(h)^{-1}` sends
  `(u_1 w, …, y)` through `(u_1 h^{-1}w, …, y)` and `(u_1 h^{-1}w, …, s^{f(h^{-1}w)} y)` to
  `(u_1 w, …, s^{(h·f)(w)} y)`, which is `Ψ(h·f)`. So `Ψ` extends to `G_Z`.
- *Centralizer.* `Ψ(1_C) = g_{B,s}`. Let `S = Ψ(G_Z)`, a quotient of `G_Z`. By Step 1, `S` is
  perfect with no proper finite-index subgroup, and `g_{B,s}` is central in `S`, so
  `S ≤ C_{nV}(g_{B,s})`.

By item 2 of `commensurating-loxodromics-split-their-centralizers` (with `z = g_{B,s}`), every
`g_{B,s}` is elliptic in every commensurating action of `nV`.

**Step 3 (items 1 and 2).**
- *`K_n`.* Let `g ∈ K_n` with locally constant `F`. By compactness, `C^{n−1}` is a finite
  disjoint union of bricks `B_1, …, B_m` with `F ≡ s_i` on `B_i`. Then `g = g_{B_1,s_1} ⋯ g_{B_m,s_m}`,
  a product of commuting elements with disjoint supports. Each factor is elliptic by Step 2, so
  `g` is elliptic by Step 0.
- *Conjugates and coordinate permutations.* These are elliptic by Step 0.
- *`V^n`.* `(x_1, …, x_n) ↦ (v_1 x_1, …, v_n x_n)` is the product of the commuting coordinate
  elements `c_i`, where `c_i` applies `v_i` in coordinate `i`. Now `c_n ∈ K_n` (with `F ≡ v_n`),
  and `c_i` is the image of an element of `K_n` under the coordinate transposition `(i n)`,
  which is an automorphism of `nV`. So each `c_i` is elliptic, and so is their product by Step 0.

**Step 4 (item 3).** `⟨γ⟩ ≅ Z`, and `f ↦ γ^f` gives
`G_Z ≅ C(C,⟨γ⟩) ⋊ V ≤ C(C,Γ) ⋊ V`, taking `1_C` to the constant map `γ`. By Step 1 and item 2 of
`commensurating-loxodromics-split-their-centralizers`, the constant map `γ` is elliptic.

For a clopen `U`, write `U = ⊔_j C(u_j)`. The map `γ·1_{C(u)}` is the image of `1_C` under the
cylinder copy `f ↦ (u w ↦ γ^{f(w)})`, `h ↦ (u w ↦ u h(w))`. Both maps are extended by `1` off `C(u)`. This
copy is a homomorphism `G_Z → C(C,Γ) ⋊ V`, by the same conjugation computation as in Step 2:
`h' f' h'^{-1} = h'·f'`, where `h'` is the cylinder copy of `h`. So `γ·1_{C(u)}` is elliptic,
and `γ·1_U = Π_j γ·1_{C(u_j)}` is a commuting product, hence elliptic.

**Step 5 (item 4).** A subgroup `L` containing an infinite-order elliptic element `z` contains
the infinite set `{z^N}`, on which `ℓ` is bounded. So `ℓ|_L` is not proper. Examples:
- `V × id^{n−1} ∋ c_1` with `v_1` of infinite order;
- `V^n, K_n, T_n ∋ c_n`;
- the subgroup `Ψ(G_Z) ≤ T_n` of Step 2 with `B = C^{n−1}` and `s` of infinite order, which
  contains `g_{B,s} = id × s`;
- `C(C,Γ) ⋊ V`, by Step 4.
