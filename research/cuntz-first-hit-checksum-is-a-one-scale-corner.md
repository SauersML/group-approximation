Suppose a proposed binary first-hit hierarchy is generated by two decoded
partial isometries `S_0,S_1` with common initial projection `Q`:

```text
S_i^*S_j=delta_(ij)Q.                                  (CFC1)
```

There are only two Cuntz-like placements of the first children.

1. **Internal children.**  If

   ```text
   S_0S_0^*+S_1S_1^*=Q,                               (CFC2)
   ```

   then cyclic finite trace already forces `tau(Q)=0`.  Quantitatively, if
   the three displayed diagonal/range equations have normalized-HS errors
   `epsilon_0,epsilon_1,epsilon_Sigma`, then

   ```text
   tau(Q)<=epsilon_0+epsilon_1+epsilon_Sigma.          (CFC3)
   ```

   This is the relative Leavitt trace checksum at depth one.

2. **External children.**  If the two final projections are merely
   orthogonal outside the root, then `(S_0,S_1,Q)` is exactly the
   common-source orthogonal partial-swap star.  Julia reflections of the two
   partial isometries give the one-scale canonical trace breaker.

The deeper first-hit projections

```text
S_0^n S_1S_1^*(S_0^*)^n                              (CFC4)
```

may provide a useful square-function decomposition, but they do not remove
the need to authenticate `(CFC1)` in ordinary group microstates.  Once that
authentication is available, `(CFC2)` is already a trace-cyclic
contradiction, while the external case is already the live swap-star
endpoint.  Thus literal Cuntz/Leavitt self-similarity is not an easier
realization of the multiscale compiler; it reduces to the existing one-scale
proper-corner/common-source problem.
