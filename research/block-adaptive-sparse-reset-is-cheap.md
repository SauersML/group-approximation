ESTABLISHED.  Let the original Tanner family have `M=Theta(L)`, fixed row
width and bounded column degree, with the repeated-original, pair-sum,
complete-pair, and equality layers of the balanced presentation.  Let
`(z_omega)` be a finite central PVM for the algebra generated by a shared
reflection tuple `(Q_i)`.  On each block choose an arbitrary coordinate set
`U_omega` and define

```text
 Q_i'=sum_omega z_omega
        (1_(i in U_omega) 1+1_(i notin U_omega)Q_i).     (BAS1)
```

Make the same replacement on every equality copy.  Then `(Q_i')` are
reflections in the same algebra, equality remains exact on reset clouds,
and

```text
 avg_i ||Q_i-Q_i'||_2^2
 <=4 sum_omega tau(z_omega)|U_omega|/L,                  (BAS2)

 E_bal(Q')
 <=2E_bal(Q)+C sum_omega tau(z_omega)|U_omega|/L.        (BAS3)
```

The constant depends only on the fixed incidence and balancing parameters.
The statement extends verbatim to a central direct integral.

Indeed a reset reflection moves squared distance at most four.  For a
width-`w` face, telescoping and `t^2<=wt` charge the change by its number
`t` of reset positions.  Each coordinate has `O(M)` incidences among
`Theta(M^2)` repeated and pair-sum faces, while at most `2L|U_omega|`
ordered complete pairs meet the reset set.  Prove the estimate on each
central block and integrate.

Thus block-dependent sparse support is not a coherence problem when the
blocks are genuinely reducing.  The unresolved issue is converting
vector-state or anchor-dependent supports into such a common reducing
decomposition without paying a product-cut loss.
