**OPEN.** Let `Y` be a countable group with `H_n(Y; Z) = 0` for all `n >= 1`,
let `1 != a in Y`, and put

    Q = Y *_(<a>) (<a> x <s>).

For every `w in Y * <t>` with `deg_t(w) != 0`, the map
`H_2(Q; Z) -> H_2((Q * <t>)/<<w>>; Z)` is injective.

By `kl-iff-acyclic-torus-amalgam-schur-injectivity`:
- `H_2(Q)` is cyclic, generated by the torus class of `a` and `s`;
- this statement is equivalent to `kervaire-laudenbach-nonsingular-conjecture`.

It is a special case of `universal-schur-injectivity-for-nonsingular-adjunctions`.
Unlike that claim, it is known not to be stronger than the conjecture.

So the question is whether one cyclic torus class survives adjoining a
nonsingular root. The equation's coefficients lie in an acyclic group, and the
only homology comes from the torus glued along `<a>`.
