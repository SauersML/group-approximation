**OPEN.** Let `p` be an odd prime and `U = L_(F_p)(1,2)^x`. Then:

1. `U` is finitely presented;
2. `U = <V, x_1, ..., x_r>`, where `V` is the standard copy of Thompson's group and each `x_j`
   is `1 + μ s_a t_b` with incomparable words `a`, `b` and `μ ∈ F_p`, or `λ s_a t_a + (1 - s_a t_a)`
   with `λ ∈ F_p^x`.

By `prime-field-leavitt-pbh-via-covered-module-amalgam` this gives
`prime-field-leavitt-unit-groups-satisfy-pbh`.

## What is known

- **`p = 2`.** Both items hold: finite presentation (`leavitt-unit-group-finitely-presented`,
  Khanh Theorem 6.1) and generation by `V` and one transvection
  (`thompson-steinberg-presentation-of-leavitt-unit-group`).
- **Odd `p`.** `K_1(L_(F_p)(1,2))` is trivial (`leavitt-unit-max-mf-quotient-is-power-cokernel`).
  Khanh's criteria as quoted on main are stated for rings of characteristic two: Theorem 5.1
  for "every nonzero unital ring B of characteristic two", as quoted in
  `research/artifacts/bh-leavitt-path-steinberg-hosts-2026-09-12.md`. No odd-characteristic
  analogue of Khanh's Theorems 5.4 and 6.1 is on main.
