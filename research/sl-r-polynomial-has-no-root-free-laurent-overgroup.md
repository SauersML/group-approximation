**Setting.** Let `A` be a UFD, `0 != f in A`, `R = A[1/f]`, and `r >= 3`. Write
`E_r(A)` for the subgroup generated by the elementary matrices `e_ab(c)`, with `a != b`
and `c in A`. Write `U_ab(S) = {e_ab(c) : c in S}`.

**Theorem.** Let `H` be a subgroup with `E_r(A) <= H <= GL_r(R)` and

```text
H ∩ U_12(R) = U_12(A).
```

Then `H <= R^× · GL_r(A)`. In particular `H ∩ SL_r(R) <= SL_r(A)`.

Equivalently, if `h in SL_r(R)` has an entry outside `A`, then the group
`<E_r(A), h>` contains a root element `e_ab(g)` with `g in R \ A`. The proof builds that
element as an explicit word. The word has three nested commutators in `h^(±1)` and in
elementary matrices over `A`, followed by one commutator that extracts a root. Its
length is bounded in terms of `r` alone.

**Laurent case.** For `A = k[x_1,…,x_d]` with `k` a field and `R = A[1/(x_1⋯x_d)]` the
Laurent ring, Suslin gives `SL_r(A) = E_r(A)` for `r >= 3`. So every subgroup `H` with
`SL_r(A) <= H <= SL_r(R)` and `H ∩ U_12(R) = U_12(A)` equals `SL_r(A)`. This holds for
every `d >= 1`, and no compressor or other stability hypothesis is needed.

**Consequences.**

* `kt-compressor-stable-kernel-overgroups-are-polynomial` holds, and in a stronger form
  (route `kernel-polynomiality-from-no-root-free-overgroup`).
* The third alternative of `kt-hyperlinear-floor-is-root-invisible-laurent-overgroup`
  (a root-invisible Laurent overgroup) never occurs. The Kun–Thom floor is `Gamma` or
  `E` (`kt-hyperlinear-floor-is-gamma-or-normal-closure`).
* A whole class of approaches is closed. No construction can produce a subgroup strictly
  between `SL_r(A)` and `SL_r(R)` whose root groups stay polynomial, whether it uses
  lattice stabilisers, unipotent radicals, tori, or anything else.

**Certificate.** `experiments/root-free-overgroups-2026-09-17/root_extraction.py`
implements the word from the proof. It runs over random non-polynomial `h`, with
`(p,d,r)` in `{(2,2,3),(2,3,3),(3,3,3),(2,3,4),(3,2,4)}`. It evaluates every commutator
as an actual matrix product and checks two things: that the double commutator equals
the predicted matrix, and that the final matrix is a single non-polynomial root element.
