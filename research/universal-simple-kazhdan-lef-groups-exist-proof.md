**(a)** Let `D = ⊕_(C∈𝒞) C` be the restricted direct sum.
- `D` is countable, since it is a countable union of finite products of countable groups.
- `D` is residually finite. An element `d ≠ 1` has some coordinate `d_C ≠ 1`. The projection `D -> C` is a
  homomorphism, and `C` has a finite quotient in which `d_C` survives.
- Every `C ∈ 𝒞` is a subgroup of `D`.
- By `countable-rf-groups-embed-in-simple-kazhdan-lef-groups`, `D ≤ U` with `U` infinite, f.g., simple, Kazhdan, LEF.

**(b)** Countability of the class:
- A recursively presented finitely generated group is `⟨s_1,…,s_k | Rel⟩`, with `Rel` a recursively enumerable subset
  of the free group `F_k`.
- There are countably many `k` and countably many r.e. subsets. So up to isomorphism these groups form a countable
  set, and (a) applies to the residually finite ones.

The examples:
- **Finitely presented** groups are recursively presented.
- **Finitely generated linear** groups are residually finite (`finitely-generated-linear-groups-are-residually-finite`)
  and recursively presented:
  - such a group lies in `GL_n(A)`, where `A = Z[x_1,…,x_m]/I` is generated by the matrix entries and the inverse
    determinants of the generators;
  - `I` is finitely generated (Hilbert's basis theorem), and ideal membership in `Z[x_1,…,x_m]` is decidable
    (Gröbner bases over `Z`; recalled, not re-read);
  - so equality of words is decidable, and the relators form an r.e. (even recursive) set. ∎
