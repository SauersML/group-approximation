The full proof is Section 3 of the artifact. Here is an outline.

**Input.** Failure of Problem I gives, by the classical reduction, a finite
AW*-factor `M` whose dimension quasitrace `q` is a nonadditive normalized
2-quasitrace. Because `q` is not additive, `M` is not a matrix algebra, so it is of
type `II_1`. Fix self-adjoint `x,y` with `q(x+y) != q(x)+q(y)`. By halving, fix a unital
CAR subalgebra `B` of `M` with dyadic projections `p_r`, so `q(p_r) = r`.

**Construction.** Let `A_0 = C*(1,x,y,B)`. Given a separable `A_k` with a countable
dense subset `Q_k`, let `A_{k+1}` be generated by `A_k` and the following elements,
for each `h` in `Q_k` and each `m >= 1`:

- (i) a finite-spectrum self-adjoint `t_{h,m}` within `1/m` of `Re h`. It exists
  because maximal abelian AW*-subalgebras are Stonean.
- (ii) an invertible within `1/m` of `h`, and its inverse. Polar decomposition plus
  finiteness gives stable rank one in `M`.
- (iii) the fullness coefficients of the separable-simple lemma of
  `stw01-separable-simple-reduction-proof` for `h*h` and its nonzero rational
  cutdowns. `M` is simple.
- (iv) for each projection `e` in `C*(1,t_{h,m})` and each dyadic `r` in `(0,1)`:
  - if `q(e) > r`, a partial isometry from `p_r` into `e`;
  - if `q(e) < r`, a partial isometry from `e` into `p_r`.

  These exist by comparability of projections in AW*-factors.

Let `A` be the closure of the union of the `A_k`.

**Properties.**

- (i) makes finite-spectrum self-adjoint elements dense, so `A` has real rank zero.
- (ii) makes invertibles dense, so `A` has stable rank one.
- (iii) gives simplicity, exactly as in the cited route.
- Stable finiteness and non-elementarity follow as there.

**Uniqueness.** Let `sigma` be a normalized 2-quasitrace on `A`.

1. On each `M_{2^k}` inside `B`, `sigma` is the normalized trace, so `sigma(p_r) = r`.
2. For a scheduled projection `e`, the partial isometries of (iv) give
   `sigma(e) >= r` whenever `q(e) > r`, and `sigma(e) <= r` whenever `q(e) < r`. These
   use only `sigma(v*v) = sigma(vv*)` and additivity on commuting projections. So
   `sigma(e) = q(e)`. The edge cases `q(e)` in `{0,1}` use faithfulness of `q`.
3. By linearity on `C*(1,t_{h,m})`, `sigma(t_{h,m}) = q(t_{h,m})`.
4. 2-quasitraces are monotone on self-adjoint elements (Blackadar-Handelman), hence
   1-Lipschitz there. The `t_{h,m}` are dense in `A_sa`, so `sigma = q|A`.

So `q_A = q|A` is the unique normalized 2-quasitrace. It is faithful, and it is not
additive because `x,y` lie in `A`. A tracial state would be a 2-quasitrace equal to
`q_A`, so there is none. Haagerup's theorem then makes `A` nonexact.

**Comparison.** Any projection is within distance `1/2` of a scheduled projection,
and hence unitarily equivalent to it in `A`. If `q(e') < r < q(f')`, composing the two
partial isometries of (iv) gives `e' <~ f'`.

**Trust boundary.** The external inputs are:

- the classical equivalence between failure of Problem I and a finite AW*-factor with
  a nonadditive dimension quasitrace (the boundary of
  `stw01-separable-simple-reduction-proof`; Gow arXiv:2601.04431v2 Theorem 4.11 is a
  checked modern form);
- standard AW*-factor facts: comparability, halving, the faithful dimension function,
  polar decomposition, and Stonean maximal abelian subalgebras;
- monotonicity of 2-quasitraces (Blackadar-Handelman);
- the Brown-Pedersen characterization of real rank zero by finite-spectrum elements.

The scheduling, uniqueness and comparison arguments are internal.
