Notation is as in the target claim. The one outside input is Nekrashevych's Theorem `th:simple`
(arXiv:1511.08241, read at source). For a minimal groupoid of germs, every nontrivial subgroup of
`[[𝒢]]` normalized by `A` contains `A`, and `A` is simple. We also use that `A` is normal in
`[[𝒢]]` (op. cit., just after the definition: conjugating a multisection gives a multisection).

**Item 1.** Let `U`, `σU` and `τU` be pairwise disjoint, and let `h, k ∈ Γ_U`.
- **The 3-cycle.** The restrictions of `σ` and `τ` to `U` are bisections. They form a degree-3
  multisection with components `U`, `σU` and `τU`. Let `c` be its 3-cycle
  `U → σU → τU → U`: `c = σ` on `U`, `c = τσ^(−1)` on `σU`, `c = τ^(−1)` on `τU`, and the
  identity elsewhere. Then `c ∈ A`.
- **Conjugating by it.** Since `c` restricted to `U` is `σ`, we get `c h c^(−1) = σ h σ^(−1)`,
  which is supported in `σU`.
- **An element of A.** `e := h (σhσ^(−1))^(−1) = h c h^(−1) c^(−1)` lies in `A`, because
  `h c h^(−1) ∈ A` by normality and `c^(−1) ∈ A`.
- **Its commutator with k.** `σhσ^(−1)` is supported in `σU`, which is disjoint from `U`. So it
  commutes with both `h` and `k`, and `[e, k] = [h, k]`.
- **Conclusion.** `[e, k] = e · (k e^(−1) k^(−1))`, and both factors lie in `A`. So `[h,k] ∈ A`. ∎

**Item 2.** `L ⊆ A` by Item 1.
- **L is normal.** For `g ∈ [[𝒢]]`, `g Γ_U g^(−1) = Γ_(gU)`, and `gU` is 3-displaceable, using
  `gσg^(−1)` and `gτg^(−1)`. So the generating set of `L` is conjugation-invariant, and `L` is
  normal in `[[𝒢]]`.
- **L is nontrivial.** Take any point `x`. Its orbit is dense, hence infinite, so there are
  `σ, τ ∈ [[𝒢]]` with `x`, `σx` and `τx` distinct. Choose products of transpositions of small
  multisections, which lie in `[[𝒢]]`. By continuity, a small clopen neighbourhood `U` of `x` is
  3-displaceable. The orbit of `x` meets `U` in infinitely many points. So by Nekrashevych's
  Lemma `lem:smallmsections` there is a degree-5 multisection with domain inside `U`, and its group
  `A(F) ≅ A_5 ≤ Γ_U` has nontrivial commutators.
- **L = A.** `L` is nontrivial and normalized by `A`, so Nekrashevych's theorem gives `L ⊇ A`.
  Hence `L = A`. ∎

**Item 3.**
- **If D is simple.** Each 3-cycle of a degree-3 multisection is a commutator of two of its
  transpositions, since `(123) = ((12)(13))^2 = [(12),(13)]`. So `A ⊆ D`. `A` is normal and
  nontrivial, so simplicity of `D` forces `D = A = L`.
- **If D = L.** Then `D = A`, which is simple. ∎

**Item 4.**
- **The radius r.**
  - Let `F` be a set of at most four points. The orbits are infinite, so choose for each `x ∈ F`
    two points `y_x` and `z_x` in its orbit, with all these points and `F` pairwise distinct.
  - Let `σ` be the product of commuting transpositions exchanging small clopen neighbourhoods of
    each `x` with neighbourhoods of `y_x`, taken along bisections that carry `x` to `y_x`. Define
    `τ` in the same way using the points `z_x`.
  - Then `F`, `σF` and `τF` are pairwise disjoint. By uniform continuity there is `δ_F > 0` such
    that the `δ_F`-neighbourhood of `F` and its images under `σ` and `τ` are pairwise disjoint.
  - The sets of at most four points form a compact space in the Hausdorff metric. Covering it by
    the balls of radius `δ_F/2` and taking a finite subcover gives `r = min δ_(F_i)/2`.
- **Proof of item 4.** Suppose `[[𝒢]]` is generated by a set `Σ` of elements `s`, each supported
  in the `r`-neighbourhood of a set `E_s` of at most two points.
  - `D` is the normal closure of the commutators `[s, s']` with `s, s' ∈ Σ`.
  - The supports of `s` and `s'` are closed subsets of the open `r`-neighbourhood of
    `E_s ∪ E_(s')`, a set of at most four points. So they lie in a clopen set `W` inside that
    neighbourhood, and `W` is 3-displaceable.
  - Item 1 gives `[s, s'] ∈ A`. Since `A` is normal, `D ⊆ A`. We also have `A ⊆ D`, so `D = A`,
    which is simple. ∎
