**ESTABLISHED.**  Use the torsion Baumslag metabelian group

```text
Lambda_2=<a,b,c | a^2=1, [b,c]=1, [a^b,a]=1, a^c=a^b a>,           (BQA1)
```

with conjugation convention `x^y=y^(-1)xy`.  The subgroup generated by
`a,b` is the lamplighter group `C_2 wr Z`.  Hence the words

```text
B_n=b^n a b^(-n),                 n in Z,                            (BQA2)
```

are independent commuting involutions.

Moreover `Lambda_2` has quadratic Dehn function.  There is therefore a
constant `C` depending only on `(BQA1)` such that, for every pair `i,j`, the
null word `[B_i,B_j]` has a van Kampen proof with at most

```text
C(|i-j|+1)^2                                                     (BQA3)
```

defining-relator cells.  Consequently every unitary tuple `rho(a),rho(b),rho(c)`
with total normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt presentation energy

```text
E(rho)=sum_(r in R)||rho(r)-1||_2^2
```

obeys

```text
||[rho(B_i),rho(B_j)]-1||_2
 <= C(|i-j|+1)^2 sqrt(E(rho)).                                    (BQA4)
```

Thus one fixed finite presentation authenticates arbitrarily many Boolean
address signs with polynomial certificate cost.  For any fixed terminal depth
`N`, the signs `B_0,...,B_(N-1)` may replace the separately named destination
signs in `finite-depth-prefix-preserving-hard-sign-hnn`; adjoining independent
source signs and the finitely many HNN rows then gives the same exact first-hit
prefix ledger.

**Boundary.**  `(BQA1)` contains no contextual payload.  Tensoring any BCS
tuple with any representation of `Lambda_2` leaves the address distribution
independent of the BCS forbidden mass.  The theorem therefore solves finite
authentication and naming, not the open task of making the retained Schur
child into the next common shared-variable carrier.

Primary sources: Cornulier--Tessera, *Metabelian groups with quadratic Dehn
function and Baumslag--Solitar groups*, Theorem 2 and Example 3.3; the subgroup
description also appears in Kassabov--Riley, *The Dehn function of Baumslag's
metabelian group*, for the torsion quotients `Gamma_m`.
