title: Fixed paired finite-group relations are one ordinary subgroup intertwiner problem
---

Let `G_1,G_2` be finite groups and fix pairs

```text
(a_s,b_s) in G_1 x G_2,       s in S.
```

Let `K<=G_1 x G_2` be the subgroup generated by these pairs, with coordinate
projections `p_1,p_2`.  For unitary representations

```text
rho:G_1->U(W),       sigma:G_2->U(V),
```

put

```text
E(T)=sum_(s in S) ||rho(a_s)T-T sigma(b_s)||_2^2.       (PFGI1)
```

Then

```text
ker E = Hom_K(sigma o p_2, rho o p_1).                  (PFGI2)
```

Moreover there is a positive spectral gap off this kernel depending only on
the finite representation types occurring in the two `K`-representations and
on the fixed generator pairs, not on their external multiplicities.  In
particular, when `rho` and `sigma` range through amplifications of fixed finite
representations, the gap is uniform in the amplification.

The exact maximal shrunk-space deficiency of the zero-energy matrix space is
therefore

```text
sum_lambda d_lambda (m_lambda-n_lambda)_+,              (PFGI3)
```

where `m_lambda,n_lambda` are the `K`-isotypic multiplicities of
`sigma o p_2` and `rho o p_1` respectively.

Thus a collection of two-term equations

```text
rho(a_s)T = T sigma(b_s)
```

contains no more noncommutative-rank information than the multiplicity table
of one finite subgroup `K<=G_1 x G_2`.  To obtain genuinely new singular
geometry one must leave this paired-group form (for example through multi-term
linear constraints or microstate-dependent coefficient selection).
