**OPEN.** Let `H, K ≤ F` with `F = HK` and `K` abelian. Then `H` contains a subgroup isomorphic to
`F`.

**Why it matters.**
- *If true.* A factorization `F = HK` with `H` amenable and `K` abelian can exist only if `F` is
  amenable. So the last survivor of `liouville-action-criteria-cannot-decide-thompson-f-amenability`
  cannot supply a proof of `thompson-f-is-amenable`. The only remaining way to produce a LAT `F`-set
  with abelian stabilizers is then to already know that `F` is amenable.
  - *Invariant:* containment of `F` in the cofactor.
  - *Dying step:* amenability of `H`.
- *If false.* Some `H ≤ F` containing no copy of `F` has an abelian complement-like cofactor. By item
  1 of `thompson-f-abelian-cofactors-fixing-an-interval-force-f-into-h`, `F` then acts faithfully on the
  abelian group `K ≅ F/H`, extending the translation action. This would be a new, exotic transitive
  `F`-set. `H` is `F`-free, so by `every-f-free-subgroup-of-thompson-f-is-amenable` it is expected to be
  amenable, which would feed the Juschenko–Zheng survivor directly.

**Status of the pieces.**
- ESTABLISHED when `int Fix(K) ≠ ∅`: `thompson-f-abelian-cofactors-fixing-an-interval-force-f-into-h`.
- Reduced below, under `## Attempts`, to a problem about `F ≀ Z` alone, namely (SA).

## Attempts

- **2026-09-18 (swarm-0917-w8-w8-f-pull), orbital reduction to shift actions of `F ≀ Z`.**
  Assume `core_F(H) = 1`; otherwise the claim holds by item 1 of
  `thompson-f-abelian-cofactors-fixing-an-interval-force-f-into-h`. Then `X = F/H ≅ K` with `K` acting
  by translations, and `K ≠ 1` unless `H = F`.
  - **Lemma A (orbitals of an abelian subgroup; proved).** Let `J` be a component of the set of points
    moved by `K`. Then every `f ∈ K` either fixes `J` pointwise or has no fixed point in `J`, and
    `K|_J` is infinite cyclic.
    - *Proof.* Let `f ∈ K`. The moved set of `f` is a finite union of open intervals, because `f` has
      finitely many breakpoints. Each `k ∈ K` commutes with `f`, so it maps this set to itself and
      permutes its components preserving order. Hence it fixes each component and its endpoints.
    - For `x ∈ J`, pick `f ∈ K` with `f(x) ≠ x`, and let `I` be the component of the moved set of `f`
      containing `x`. Then `I ⊆ J`, and the endpoints of `I` are fixed by all of `K`, so they are not in
      `J`. Hence `I = J`. This gives the first assertion.
    - Let `a` be the left endpoint of `J`. Every `f ∈ K` fixes `a` and is linear on some `[a, a+ε)`,
      with slope `2^(m(f))`. The chain rule makes `m : K → Z` a homomorphism. If `m(f) = 0`, then `f`
      fixes points of `J`, so `f|_J = id`. Hence `K|_J ≅ m(K) ≤ Z`, and it is nontrivial. ∎
  - **Reduction.** Let `K_J = {k ∈ K : k|_J = id}`, and pick `g ∈ K` with `g|_J` generating `K|_J`.
    Then `K/K_J ≅ Z`, generated by the class of `g`.
    - Pick dyadic `c < d` in `J` with `d < g(c)`, replacing `g` by `g^(-1)` if needed. Then the intervals
      `g^n[c,d]` are pairwise disjoint and dyadic.
    - Let `L = F[c,d] ≅ F`. The groups `L_n = g^n L g^(-n) = F[g^n c, g^n d]` pairwise commute, so
      `W = ⟨L, g⟩ ≅ L ≀ Z`, with base `B = ⊕_n L_n` and top generator `g`.
    - `K_J` fixes `J ⊇ [c,d]` pointwise, so `L` commutes with `K_J`, by Step 4 of
      `thompson-f-abelian-cofactors-fixing-an-interval-proof`. Also `g` commutes with `K_J`. So
      `W ⊆ C_F(K_J)`.
    - Hence `W` permutes the `K_J`-orbits in `X ≅ K`, which are the cosets of `K_J`. So `W` acts on
      `Y = K/K_J ≅ Z`, and `g` acts on `Y` as the shift `y ↦ y+1`.
  - **Transfer lemma (proved).** If `Stab_L(y)` contains a subgroup `Φ ≅ F` for some `y ∈ Y`, then `H`
    contains a subgroup isomorphic to `F`.
    - *Proof.* Write `y = kK_J`. The group `Φ′ ≅ F′` is simple and nonabelian, hence perfect, and it
      contains `F[1/4,1/2] ≅ F`.
    - `Φ′` preserves the coset `kK_J ⊆ X` and commutes with `K_J`. So, exactly as in Step 3 of the
      proof route, it acts on that coset by translations through a homomorphism `Φ′ → K_J`.
    - That homomorphism is trivial, because `Φ′` is perfect. So `Φ′` fixes `kH`, and
      `k^(-1) Φ′ k ⊆ H`. ∎
    - *Special case.* A finite `L`-orbit on `Y` suffices. A finite-index subgroup of `L` then fixes a
      point, and it contains a normal finite-index `N ◁ L`. `N ∩ L′` is normal of finite index in the
      infinite simple group `L′` (fact (F1)), so `N ⊇ L′ ⊇` a copy of `F`.
  - **Further constraints on the `W`-action on `Y` in a counterexample (proved).**
    - (C1) `L` acts faithfully on `Y`. Otherwise the kernel is a nontrivial normal subgroup of `L ≅ F`,
      so it contains a copy of `F` by `nontrivial-normal-subgroups-of-thompson-f-contain-a-copy-of-f`,
      and the transfer lemma applies. In particular `L` does not act through an abelian quotient.
    - (C2) The `B`-orbits are blocks for the shift, so they are the cosets of `dZ` for some `d ≥ 0`.
      If `d = 0`, then `B` acts trivially, against (C1). So `d ≥ 1`, and all `B`-orbits are infinite.
    - (C3) *The base does not act freely.* Suppose `B` acts freely on `Y_0 = dZ`.
      - Write `σ` for conjugation by `g^d`, and `T` for the action of `g^d` on `Y_0`. With
        `T(0) = c·0` for some `c ∈ B`, we get `T(b·0) = σ(b)c·0`.
      - Then `T^n(0) = P_n·0`, where `P_n = σ^(n-1)(c)⋯σ(c)c` for `n ≥ 1`, `P_0 = 1`, and
        `P_(-n) = σ^(-n)(c^(-1))⋯σ^(-1)(c^(-1))`. The shift is transitive and `B` is free, so every
        element of `B` is some `P_n`.
      - `c ≠ 1`, since `T` has no fixed point. Let `c` occupy the coordinates `m..M` of
        `B = ⊕ L_n`, with the coordinates indexed so that `σ` raises them by `d`.
      - For `n ≥ 1`, `P_n` has the nontrivial entry `c_M` at coordinate `M + (n−1)d`. For `n ≤ −1`, it
        has the nontrivial entry `c_m^(-1)` at coordinate `m + nd`.
      - So at most two nontrivial elements of `L_0` are of the form `P_n`, while `L_0` is infinite.
        Contradiction. ∎
  - **Where it dies: problem (SA).** Does `F ≀ Z = (⊕_n F_n) ⋊ ⟨t⟩` admit a transitive action on `Z`
    in which `t` acts as the shift and no point stabilizer of `F_0` contains a copy of `F`?
    Equivalently: is there `S ≤ F ≀ Z` with `S⟨t⟩ = F ≀ Z` and `S ∩ ⟨t⟩ = 1` such that no conjugate
    of `S` meets `F_0` in a copy of `F`?
    - The reduction shows that a **negative answer to (SA) proves the claim for every abelian `K`**.
      Lemma A makes it apply to every nontrivial `K`, whether or not its support is dense. The fixed-interval
      theorem is then a special case.
    - A positive answer does not refute the claim, because the reduction discards everything in `F`
      outside `C_F(K_J)`.
    - A counterexample to (SA) must satisfy (C1)–(C3): `F_0` acts faithfully with only infinite orbits,
      and `B` acts with infinite orbits but not freely. Since `F_n` commutes with `F_0`,
      `Stab_(F_0)` is constant along the orbits of `⊕_(n≠0) F_n`.
    - *Not tried:*
      - exploiting the self-similarity `F_0 ⊇ F[c′,d′] ≀ Z` inside `L`, which could recurse (C3)
        into `S ∩ B`;
      - the recurrent theorem `thompson-f-recurrent-f-sets-factor-through-the-abelianization`. When `K`
        has rank at most 2 and every generator of `F` moves points of `X ≅ K` a bounded distance, `X` is
        recurrent, so `H ⊇ F′`. Hence a counterexample needs unbounded displacement.
    - *Calibration question, open.* Does any nonabelian group `L` admit an action of `L ≀ Z` on `Z` with
      `t` the shift, `L` acting faithfully, and `B` not acting through an abelian quotient? For abelian
      `L`, translation actions `x ↦ x + k + Σ_n χ(b_n)` exist, with `χ : L → Z`.
