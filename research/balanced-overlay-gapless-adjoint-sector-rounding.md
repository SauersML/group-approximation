OPEN.  Work on the retained low raw-square-function block after bounded local
face exactification and equality-cloud averaging/signing.  Let
`R_1,...,R_L` be the resulting shared reflections, with balanced parity and
complete-pair energy tending to zero, and put `A=W*(R_1,...,R_L)`.

If the generated-adjoint Poincare constant

```text
 lambda(R)=inf_(T in A, T perpendicular A')
   ((1/L)sum_i||[T,R_i]||_2^2)/||T||_2^2               (BGA1)
```

is bounded below, `uniform-generated-adjoint-gap-rounds-to-central-code`
finishes the same-Hilbert correction.  Prove that the complementary sector
`lambda(R)->0` is cheaply roundable or admits a common reducing split whose
total discarded/reset trace and average generator movement tend to zero,
uniformly in length and matrix dimension.  Iterating the split must either
exhaust the gapless mass at summable cost or leave a block with a fixed
positive value of `(BGA1)`.

The witness to `(BGA1)` is an almost-central element **inside** the algebra
generated by the tuple, rather than an arbitrary low-band vector in
`L_2(M)`.  This extra membership is the available leverage: its spectral
cuts belong to `A`, while small conjugation Dirichlet energy gives coarea
control of their boundaries against all generators.  What is not yet proved
is a cardinality-free stopping/decomposition theorem that prevents the
successive cuts from repeating the same boundary mass.

This is strictly narrower than generic operator-valued cosystolic rounding.
The high-gap case, all scalar/central mixtures, exact central Clifford
sectors, and bounded-type blocks are already excluded.  Any remaining far
countermodel must carry positive trace in gapless noncentral generated
blocks and supply almost-central spectral cuts which nevertheless cannot be
assembled into a cheap central decomposition.

The single-cut mechanisms are already available.  A bounded witness gives a
balanced spectral cut by `bounded-low-gap-witness-gives-balanced-cut`; an
unbounded witness gives either such a cut or a small-trace spike by
`low-gap-witness-spike-or-balanced-cut`.  On exact parity blocks,
`adjoint-parity-cone-lifts-to-loewner-order` makes the boundary of **every**
such physical cut uniformly spread over the coordinates, and the established
compression/sign ledger makes the cut reducing at comparable average cost.
The unresolved issue is only global summability: successive low-gap cuts can
expose fresh orthogonal spike supports or repeat boundary through a deep cut
tree.  `balanced-parity-multicut-boundary-is-summable` records the exact
potential/decomposition statement which would finish this branch.

## Attempts

1. **Generic expander decomposition.**  This cannot be enough.  Central
   direct sums of translated sparse Clifford cycles can make every fixed
   pair commutator small while keeping constant average distance from all
   commuting tuples.  The balanced parity layer excludes those examples,
   so it must enter the multicut potential rather than being restored only
   after a generic decomposition.
2. **One coarea cut.**  The bounded and clipped coarea theorems produce the
   required physical projection, and `(BPM3)` makes its boundary safe for
   every parity coordinate.  They do not bound the sum over a recursive cut
   tree; entropy pays a factor equal to the tree depth.
3. **Quarantine every spike.**  One known small-trace spike is cheap to
   shatter into rank-one blocks.  Iteration can reveal fresh orthogonal
   spikes whose total trace is order one.  A common support or monotone
   energy budget is still required.
