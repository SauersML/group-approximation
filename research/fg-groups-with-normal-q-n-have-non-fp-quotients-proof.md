Fix an isomorphism `N ≅ Q^n`, write `N` additively, and let `μ(h) ∈ GL_n(Q)` be the matrix with
`h x h^{-1} = μ(h) x` for `x ∈ N`.

**Part 1.** Let `s_1, ..., s_k` generate `H`. Let `m >= 1` be a common denominator of all entries of the `2k`
matrices `μ(s_i)^{±1}`. The matrices over `Z[1/m]` whose inverse is also over `Z[1/m]` form a group
`GL_n(Z[1/m])`. It contains every `μ(s_i)^{±1}`, hence contains `μ(H)`. For a prime `p ∤ m` the matrix
`diag(1/p, 1, ..., 1)` lies in `GL_n(Q)` but not in `GL_n(Z[1/m])`, because `1/p ∉ Z[1/m]`. So
`μ(H) ≠ GL_n(Q)`.

**Part 2.** Suppose `H/N` is finitely presented.
- **Finitely many normal generators.** Let `F` be free on `x_1, ..., x_k`, let `φ: F -> H` send `x_i` to `s_i`,
  and let `M` be the kernel of the composite `F -> H -> H/N`.
  - A finitely presented group is finitely presented on every finite generating set. Take a finite presentation
    `<y_1, ..., y_r | u_1, ..., u_t>` of `H/N`. Write each `y_j` as a word `α_j` in the images of the `x_i`, and
    each image of `x_i` as a word `β_i` in the `y_j`.
  - By Tietze transformations `M` is the normal closure in `F` of the finitely many words `u_q(α_1, ..., α_r)` and
    `x_i^{-1} β_i(α_1, ..., α_r)`.
  - Since `φ` is onto and `φ(M) = N`, the subgroup `N` is the normal closure in `H` of finitely many elements
    `v_1, ..., v_l ∈ N`.
- **Module bound.** `N` is abelian and normal, so this normal closure is the subgroup generated by the conjugates
  `h v_j h^{-1} = μ(h) v_j`, `h ∈ H`. Hence `N = Σ_j Z[μ(H)] v_j`, where `Z[μ(H)]` is the subring of
  `M_n(Q)` generated by `μ(H)`. By part 1, `Z[μ(H)] ⊆ M_n(Z[1/m])`.
- **Contradiction.** Choose `d >= 1` with `d v_j ∈ Z^n` for every `j`. Then `N ⊆ (1/d) Z[1/m]^n`. Take a prime
  `p ∤ m`. The vector `(1/(dp)) e_1` lies in `Q^n = N`. But `d · 1/(dp) = 1/p ∉ Z[1/m]`, so the vector is not in
  `(1/d) Z[1/m]^n`.

So `H/N` is not finitely presented. ∎
