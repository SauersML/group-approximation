Conjugation by the signed Weyl words permutes root positions up to sign.
The depth description of `D` therefore gives `(NPC4)`, `(NPC6)`, `(NPC7)`,
and `(NPC8)`.  Translating `(NPC2)` by `d_2`, respectively `d_1`, leaves the
whole product and one of its two factors fixed.  Left, respectively right,
cancellation supplies the two missing positive simple-root stabilizers.
The four positive/negative simple-root subgroups generate `SL_3(Z)`, after
which `z=z^2` proves the exact result.

For the metric statement, insert the original triangle between its
`d_i`-translate and use unitary bi-invariance.  Each cancellation costs at
most `2 epsilon_tri+2 epsilon_D`.  Each signed Weyl word contains three root
letters, giving `(NPC14)`.  Comparing the original triangle with `Z^2` then
gives `(NPC15)`.  All word lists are fixed, so approximate actor relations
only change the displayed numerical constant by another fixed amount.
