Lane proof, elementary, not independently reviewed. Notation as in the claim. `C_w` is the cone of the finite word
`w`, `V_[U]` is the set of elements of `V` supported in `U`, `λ|_U ∈ C(C, Λ)` is the label `λ` on `U` and `1`
elsewhere, and `v^[P]` is `v` applied to the `C` coordinate over the clopen set `P ⊆ X`.

## 1. Generation

By `v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions`, `F(T)` is generated by transpositions `t_U` of
compact open bisections `U : W -> W'` with `W ∩ W' = ∅`. Refine `U` into basic pieces
`(wc, x) -> (w'c, λx)` on `C_w × P`, with `w, w'` nonempty (split once more on the first digit if needed) and `P` a
cylinder. Then `t_U` is the product of the commuting piece transpositions, so it suffices to treat one piece `t`.
Disjointness gives two cases.
- **Case `w ⊥ w'`** (incomparable). Let `m = λ|_(C_w) ∈ M`. Then `m t m^-1` swaps `C_w × λP` and `C_(w') × λP` by
  `(wc, y) -> (w'c, y)`. This is the cone transposition `(w w') ∈ V` conditioned on `λP`, an element of
  `LC(X, V) ≤ N_X`. So `t ∈ <M, N_X>`.
- **Case `w, w'` comparable.**
  - The two sources and ranges meet unless `P ∩ λP = ∅`, since `C_(w')` or `C_w` lies inside the other cone.
  - Choose `u ⊥ w` and `v ∈ V` with `v(w'c) = wc` on `C_(w')`. A prefix replacement exists, since both cones are
    proper.
  - Then `n = v^[λP]` fixes `C_w × P` and carries `C_(w') × λP` onto `C_w × λP`, so `s = n t n^-1` swaps
    `C_w × P` and `C_w × λP` by `(wc, x) -> (wc, λx)`.
  - Let `τ_1` swap `C_w × P` and `C_u × λP` by `(wc, x) -> (uc, λx)`, which is the first case, and let
    `τ_2 = (u w)^[λP] ∈ N_X`. Then `τ_2 τ_1 τ_2 = s`. So `t = n^-1 s n ∈ <M, N_X>`.

## 2. Intersection

- Suppose `(v(c), f(c)x) = (u_x(c), λx)` for all `c, x`.
  - Then `u_x = v` for all `x`.
  - Also `f(c)^-1 λ` fixes a point of `X`, so `f(c) = λ` by freeness.
- So the element is `(v, λ) ∈ V × Λ`. Freeness also makes `M -> F(T)` injective.

## 3. The pieces

- *M.* This is item 1 of `cantor-integer-maps-by-v-are-of-type-fp2` with `L = Λ`. Also `M = < V, s|_(C_0) : s ∈ S >`:
  - `λ|_U` for a proper cone union `U` is a `V`-conjugate of `λ|_(C_0)`;
  - `λ|_(C_0)` is the product of the `s|_(C_0)`;
  - every `f ∈ C(C, Λ)` is a product of such terms.
- *N_X, finite generation.*
  - `t_q v^(a) t_q^-1 = v^[x(q) = a]`.
  - `[v^[P_1], w^[P_2]] = [v, w]^[P_1 ∩ P_2]`, and `V` is perfect. So by induction on the number of sites, every
    `v^[P]` with `P` a cylinder lies in `< Λ, v^(a) >`.
  - Every element of `LC(X, V)` is a product of the commuting `v_π^[π]` over a cylinder partition.
- *Rigidity (item 4).*
  - `e_a` is the 3-cycle `h: 00w -> 01w -> 1w -> 00w` of `V` conditioned on `x(1) = a`, so `e_a = h^(a) ∈ N_X`.
  - The constant elements give `V × {1} ≤ N_X`, and `t_s ∈ Λ ≤ N_X`.
  - Both parts of `fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity` apply with `Γ = N_X`.

## 4. The gluing group Γ~

- **Finitely many relations.** Let `R_0 = { [g, h^(a)] : g ∈ gens(M_0), h ∈ gens(V_[1]), a ∈ A }`, where
  `M_0 = < V_[0], s|_(C_00) : s ∈ S >` is the copy of `M` on `C_0`.
  - Conjugating by `t_q ∈ V × Λ` sends `λ|_(C_00)` to `(qλq^-1)|_(C_00)` and `h^(a)` to `h^[x(q)=a]`.
  - Every `λ|_(C_00)` is a product of the `s|_(C_00)`. So `R_0` gives
    `[λ|_(C_00), h^[x(q)=a]] = 1` for all `λ, q`, and `V_[0]` commutes with every `h^[x(q)=a]` already in `N_X`.
  - The `h^[x(q)=a]` generate `N_1`, by the commutator induction of §3. Hence `[M_0, N_1] = 1` follows from `R_0`.
- **The map onto F(T).** Pieces with disjoint supports commute in `F(T)`, so `Γ~ -> F(T)` is defined, and it is
  onto by §1.
- **Copy endomorphisms.** Let `σ ∈ V` swap `C_0` and `C_1` by prefix, and `M_1 = σ M_0 σ^-1`, `N_0 = σ N_1 σ^-1`.
  Then `[M_1, N_0] = 1` in `Γ~`.
  - Define `φ_0` on `M` as the copy map onto the elements supported in `C_0 × X`. Define it on `N_X` by
    `copy_0 : LC(X, V) -> LC(X, V_[0]) = N_0` and `λ -> λ|_(C_0) ∈ M`.
  - The only relation of `N_X` to check is `λ f λ^-1 = λ.f`. Write `λ|_(C_0) = λ · (λ^-1)|_(C_1)`, with
    `(λ^-1)|_(C_1) ∈ M_1` commuting with `copy_0(f) ∈ N_0`. Then
    `λ|_(C_0) copy_0(f) λ|_(C_0)^-1 = λ copy_0(f) λ^-1 = copy_0(λ.f)`.
  - The two definitions agree on `V × Λ`.
  - `φ_0` sends the relation `[M_0, N_1]` to `[M_00, LC(X, V_[01])]`. This is a consequence of `[M_0, N_1] = 1`:
    conjugate by `τ ∈ V` with `τ(C_0) = C_00` and `τ(C_1) = C_01 ∪ C_1`.
  - `φ_1` is defined symmetrically. Their images commute:
    - `[M_0, M_1] = 1` and `[N_0, N_1] = 1` hold in `M` and in `N_X`;
    - `[M_0, N_1] = [M_1, N_0] = 1` hold by `R_0`;
    - `[λ|_(C_0), N_1] ⊆ [M_0, N_1]`. ∎
