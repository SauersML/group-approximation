  graph-path-almost-automorphism-proper-power-is-equicontinuous: that is the zero-dimensional statement for almost automorphisms of path spaces, where a proper-power conjugate generates an equicontinuous group; this is the one-dimensional statement for piecewise real-analytic circle homeomorphisms, where such elements can be parabolic and not equicontinuous, as x ↦ x+1 on RP^1 is.

**ESTABLISHED** (elementary; no novelty claimed).

**Setting.**
- `S^1 = R/Z`. `PA(S^1)` is the set of homeomorphisms `g` of `S^1` with a
  finite set `B_g ⊂ S^1` such that, on the closure of each component of
  `S^1 \ B_g`, `g` agrees with a real-analytic diffeomorphism defined on an open
  neighbourhood of that closed arc. It is a group. `PA_+(S^1)` is its
  orientation-preserving subgroup, of index at most 2.
- `PA_+(S^1)` contains every group of PL circle homeomorphisms with finitely many
  breakpoints, and every group of piecewise-`PSL_2(R)` homeomorphisms of
  `RP^1 = R ∪ {∞}` with finitely many breakpoints. The second family includes
  Thompson's `T` in its piecewise-`PSL_2(Z)` model, and Lodha's finitely
  presented infinite simple group `S` (arXiv:1710.06220v2, Theorem 1.1), which is
  generated by `T` and one piecewise projective homeomorphism.
- A *gap* of `g` is a component of `S^1 \ Fix(g)`.

**Statement.** Let `g, h ∈ PA_+(S^1)` satisfy `h g h^-1 = g^n` for an integer
`n ≥ 2`, and suppose `g` has a fixed point. When `n = 2` this is automatic.
1. `Fix(g)` has finitely many components and `h` permutes them. Some `h^N` with
   `N ≥ 1` maps every gap of `g` onto itself and fixes every endpoint of every
   gap.
2. At every fixed point of `g`, both one-sided derivatives of `g` equal `1`.
3. Suppose `g ≠ 1`, let `p` be an endpoint of a gap `J`, and take an analytic
   coordinate `x` at `p` with `x > 0` pointing into `J`. Then the germ of `g` at
   `p` from `J` is `x ↦ x + c x^(m+1) + O(x^(m+2))` with `m ≥ 1` and `c ≠ 0`, and
   the one-sided derivative of `h^N` at `p` from `J` is `n^(-N/m)`. In
   particular it is not `1`.

**Sharpness.** In `PSL_2(R)` acting on `RP^1`, `g(x) = x+1` and `h(x) = 2x`
satisfy `h g h^-1 = g^2`. Here `Fix(g) = {∞}`, `m = 1`, and `h` has derivative
`1/2` at `∞`. So hyperbolic conjugators occur, and `BS(1,2)` embeds in `PA_+(S^1)`.
The obstructions for the Baumslag--Gersten group and Higman's group
(`baumslag-gersten-embeds-in-no-piecewise-analytic-circle-group`,
`higman-group-embeds-in-no-piecewise-analytic-circle-group`) come from a conjugator
that is itself conjugate to its square.

**Scope.**
- The argument needs finitely many pieces (so that fixed-point sets have finitely
  many components) and analytic germs of finite tangency order. It says nothing
  about `C^0` actions or `C^∞` germs flat at a fixed point.
- It covers the interval `[0,1]` by gluing the endpoints, and the line when
  elements are projective near `±∞`.

The proof is `piecewise-analytic-parabolic-parameter-scaling-proof`.
