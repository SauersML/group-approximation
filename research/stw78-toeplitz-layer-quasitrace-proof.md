Let `E=T_n` be the Toeplitz--Cuntz algebra generated by isometries
`s_1,...,s_n` with orthogonal ranges.  Its standard extension is (1), with
compact-operator ideal `K` and simple quotient `O_n`.  It is separable and
nuclear, and its ideal lattice is `{0,K,E}`.

There is no tracial state on `E`.  Indeed, a tracial state `tau` would give

```text
tau(s_i s_i^*)=tau(s_i^*s_i)=tau(1)=1
```

for every `i`, whereas orthogonality and
`sum_i s_i s_i^*<=1` would give `n<=1`.  Exactness turns every bounded
2-quasitrace into a trace, so `E` has no normalized 2-quasitrace.

The ideal `K` has the usual densely finite lower semicontinuous trace, which
is unbounded on an approximate unit of finite-rank projections.  Its Cuntz
semigroup is unperforated.  The quotient `O_n` is simple purely infinite, so
its Cuntz semigroup is almost unperforated as well.  The compact ideal
prevents `E` from being strongly purely infinite, since ideals of a strongly
purely infinite algebra are strongly purely infinite.

This example distinguishes two notions that coincide in a unital layer but
not in `K`: absence of a bounded normalized quasitrace on the ambient
algebra does not exclude an unbounded lower semicontinuous quasitrace on a
nonunital ideal.
