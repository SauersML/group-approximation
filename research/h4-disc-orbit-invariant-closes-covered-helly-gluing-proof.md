**1. The invariant.** Fix `σ ∈ PB_46` and an arc `γ_a ⊂ Δ_F` from `m` to `F_a`.
- For each outside `j`, the pure braid `σ` changes the linking of the pair `(F_a, j)` and of the pair `(m, j)`.
  So the winding of `j` around the closed curve `σ(γ_a)·γ_a^{-1}` is `e_{F_a j}(σ) − e_{m j}(σ)`.
  - To check this, compute on generators `A_{pq}`: only `A_{F_a j}` and `A_{m j}` move the arc around `j`, with
    opposite signs.
- The stabiliser of `Δ_F` in `PB_46` is generated by three kinds of braid:
  - braids supported in `Δ_F`, where all mixed `e` vanish;
  - braids supported off `Δ_F`, where again all mixed `e` vanish;
  - the disc moved as one fat strand around outside punctures, where `e_{F_a j} = e_{m j}` for every `a`.
- So `ι` is a homomorphism `PB_46 → ℤ^{126}` that kills the stabiliser, and it descends to discs.
- Two arcs from `m` to `F_a` inside the same disc `D` differ by a loop in `D`. This loop has winding 0 around each
  outside `j`. So `ι(D)_{a,j}` can be read off any arc from `m` to `F_a` inside `D`.
  - A point-push of `m` along a loop in `D` moves `m` only inside `D`, so it does not change `ι(D)`.

**2. The orbit lattice.**
- `B°` is the complement of the 121 collision planes in `V/L_X` (w17). So `π_1(B°)` is generated by conjugates of
  meridians.
- Near a generic point of a plane `Q`, the colliding punctures form clusters. Every pair in a cluster collides on `Q`:
  `lattice.py` asserts that each cluster is a clique.
- The punctures move affinely in the base, so the local monodromy of a meridian is the product of the full twists
  `Δ_C^2` of the clusters. This product has `e_{pq} = 1` on colliding pairs and `0` elsewhere.
- `m` never collides with a puncture on `B°`, because the walls `y_h = m` are the removed hyperplanes of the fibre.
  Along a loop in a compact part of the base, choose the section `s` with `m` outside a disc containing all 45
  punctures. Then `m` can only wind around all of them at once, which contributes a multiple of `(−1, …, −1)` to `ι`.
- Conjugation does not change exponent sums, and `ι` is a homomorphism. So `ι(μ̂(s(β))Δ_F) ∈ Λ` for every `β ∈ B°`.
- `full.py` builds the 122 generators and finds rank 60. The Smith normal form has all invariant factors `0` or `1`,
  so `Λ` is saturated (`full-H4.log`). `lattice.py` gives the 84-coordinate form: rank 57, saturated, 63 F-planes
  (`lattice-H4.log`).
- Independent check: `calib.py` runs 60 random pure words `∏ w s_i^{±2} w^{-1}` through the w17 closed-form
  monodromy, reads the exponent sums from the crossing words, and finds every vector in the rational span
  (`calib.log`).
  - Only the 84-coordinate form is calibrated this way. Brieskorn words carry point-push components, which the
    126-coordinate form sees.

**3. Gluing.**
- Adjacency in the lifted model (w16): `x_0 = P_X` and `t x_0` are both adjacent to the y-vertex `γΔ_F` if and only if
  `t ∈ γFγ^{-1}`. That is, `t` is a point-push of `m` along a loop inside `γΔ_F`.
- `D_{12}` and `D_{13}` are adjacent to `x_0`, so by item 2 their `ι` lies in `Λ`.
- Write `D_{23} = t_2 D'`, where `D'` is adjacent to `x_0` and to `t_2^{-1}t_3x_0`.
  - Here `t_2` is a point-push inside `D_{12}`, and `D_{12}` contains no outside puncture. So by item 1,
    `ι(D_{23}) = ι(D') ∈ Λ`.
- The Z/2 hull `D` contains the pair hull `E` of `x_i, x_k`, and `E ⊂ D_{ik}`.
  - For `a ∈ S_E`, take the arc from `m` to `F_a` inside `E`. It lies in both `D` and `D_{ik}`.
  - `D` and `D_{ik}` contain no outside puncture, so item 1 gives `ι(D)_{a,·} = ι(D_{ik})_{a,·}`.
  - Hence `π_{S_E} ι(D) ∈ π_{S_E} Λ`.

**4. Patterns.**
- `full.py` lists the patterns `(S_{12}, S_{13}, S_{23})` whose homology is feasible:
  - `supp[t_2] ⊂ S_{12}`, `supp[t_3] ⊂ S_{13}` and `supp([t_3] − [t_2]) ⊂ S_{23}`;
  - the classes have entries in `{−1, 0, 1}`;
  - a singleton `S_E` means a nonzero power of a simple loop.
- For each pattern it computes `Λ^{glue}` over `ℚ`, by adding the annihilator constraints of the three projections.
- Result, for the 38 patterns with full hull F-set:
  - rank 60 in the 23 covered patterns;
  - rank 62 or 67 in the 15 uncovered ones;
  - no exceptions (`full-H4.log`).
- In a covered pattern, `Λ^{glue} ⊂ Λ_ℚ ∩ ℤ^{126} = Λ` because `Λ` is saturated. So the equality holds over `ℤ`.
- The runs `full.py A4|B4|D4` give item 5 (`full-small-types.log`).

**What this does not prove.**
- A hull disc can pass the abelian test and still lie outside the orbit.
- The uncovered patterns are homologically feasible, but they have not been realised by arcs.
- The next steps are:
  - the pair-covering lemma (the Consequence paragraph of the claim);
  - non-abelian shadows, for example the forgetful image in `PB_7` on `{m, F_0, F_1, F_2, r_{29}, r_{33}, r_{35}}`.
    The 5-strand shadow loses `ζ`.
