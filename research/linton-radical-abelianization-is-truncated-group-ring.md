ESTABLISHED.  Let `G = F/<<w>>` be a one-relator group.  Take `r ∈ F` and
`k >= 1` from Linton's Theorem 1.1 (arXiv:2407.09272), so that

    N = <<r>>_F ,   W = <<w>>_F ,   w ∈ r^k[N,N] ,
    R = G_Q^(ω) = <<r>>_G = N/W ,   H = F/N ≅ G/R .

Then, as `ZH`-modules,

    R_ab  ≅  ZH / kZH  ≅  (Z/kZ)[H] .

In particular `k = 1` forces `R` to be **perfect**, and for `k > 1` the radical
has bounded exponent `k` abelianization, free as a `Z/kZ`-module over the
quotient `H = G/R`.

## Proof

`H = F/<<r>>` is residually rationally solvable by Linton's corollary.  A
residually rationally solvable group is torsion-free: each finite rational
derived quotient has torsion-free abelian factors, an extension of torsion-free
groups by torsion-free groups is torsion-free, and a residual intersection of
such quotients cannot acquire torsion.  So `H` is torsion-free, and by the
torsion theorem for one-relator groups `r` is **not a proper power** (a proper
power would put torsion in `H`).

Lyndon's identity theorem for the one-relator presentation `H = F/<<r>>` then
says the relation module is free of rank one:

    N/[N,N]  ≅  ZH ,  generated by the class [r] .

Because `w ∈ r^k[N,N]`, the class of `w` is `[w] = k[r]`.  The image of
`W = <<w>>_F` in `N/[N,N]` is the `ZH`-submodule generated by `[w]`, i.e.
exactly `kZH`.  Hence

    R_ab = (N/W)_ab ≅ N/(W[N,N]) ≅ (N/[N,N]) / kZH ≅ ZH/kZH .   ∎

(`W ⊆ N` holds because `w ∈ r^k[N,N] ⊆ N`, so `R = N/W` is meaningful.)

## What it is good for

This computation alone is not a soficity proof, but its relation module is now
the first layer of the stronger theorem
[[one-relator-fox-magnus-descent-to-derived-linton-radical]].  The distinction
matters: this node only identifies an amenable quotient of `R`, whereas that
theorem uses purity inside the full Magnus module to embed `G/R'` itself.

Three consequences remain useful here.

- **It splits the unresolved case into two regimes.**  `k = 1` gives a perfect
  radical; `k > 1` gives one whose abelianization is `k`-torsion.  In the
  second regime nonsoficity of `R` would have to survive to `R'`, since `R/R'`
  is amenable and sofic-by-amenable is a theorem.
- **It makes the radical computable from the Linton data.**  `(r, k)` are
  extracted from `w` with `|r| <= |w|`, so `R_ab` is available for any explicit
  primitive-rank-2 relator one wants to test.
- **It kills the Cohen--Lyndon shortcut it suggests.**  The diagonal form
  `[w] = k[r]` invites eliminating orbit relators by Tietze moves, one per
  conjugate generator.  That is false: the identity holds only in `N/[N,N]`,
  while in `N` itself `w` may contain the relevant conjugate of `r` repeatedly
  and entangled with others.  Abelian diagonalization is not a nonabelian
  elimination theorem, and the residue of that attempt is this computation and
  nothing more.
