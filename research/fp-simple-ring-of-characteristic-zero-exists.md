**OPEN.** There is a unital ring `R`, finitely presented as a ring (a finitely presented
`Z`-algebra), that is simple and of characteristic `0`.

**Equivalent forms.** The following are equivalent:
- (a) the statement above;
- (b) `Q` embeds unitally in a finitely presented simple ring;
- (c) `Q` embeds, possibly non-unitally, in a finitely presented simple ring.

Proof of the equivalence:
- (a) ⇒ (b): the centre of a simple unital ring is a field, and in characteristic 0 it
  contains `Q·1`.
- (b) ⇒ (c): trivial.
- (c) ⇒ (a): let `e` be the image of `1 ∈ Q`, a nonzero idempotent, and `r` the image of
  `1/p`. If `p·1_R = 0`, then `e = p r = (p e) r = 0`. So `R` has no prime
  characteristic, and a simple ring has characteristic `0` or a prime.

**Why it matters for `gl-n-q-embeds-in-fp-simple-group`.**
- It is the characteristic-0 case of the algebra form of Boone--Higman for the single
  decidable input `Q`. Evans--Mandelberg--Neff (1975) handle rings of characteristic `p`
  in the weak form (BBMZ survey arXiv:2306.16356v3, §3).
- A positive answer supplies the ring for the `E_n` / unit-group route of lane
  `gq-en-ring`: `GL_n(Q) <= E_(n+1)(R)`, exactly as in
  `gl-n-q-embeds-in-e-n-plus-1-of-a-fp-ring`.
- Simplicity of `R` is the natural source of simplicity for `PE_N(R)`. The group-level
  finite presentation (`St_N`, `K_2`) would still need its own proof.

## Attempts

1. **Scalar extensions of free abelian rings (dead).** By
   `integral-form-algebras-have-no-fg-subring-containing-q`, no finitely generated subring
   of `M_n(A ⊗ K)` contains `Q` when `A` has free abelian additive group. This rules out
   finitely generated subrings of every Leavitt, Leavitt path, Cohn, group, Weyl and
   Steinberg algebra over any field, and of Leavitt--Nekrashevych completions with
   integral data. In a completion `O_ψ` of a finitely presented `B`, the scalars of the
   core `colim M_(d^k)(B)` are the scalars of `B`. So such completions create no new
   denominators.
2. **Vacuum and resolvent (finitely presented, not simple).**
   `q-embeds-in-a-corner-of-a-fp-toeplitz-ring` gets all of `Q` from four relations. Its
   mechanism has two parts:
   - a number operator `N` with a vacuum (`N E = 0`) and a shift (`NS = S(N+1)`), so that
     `(N+m)E = mE`;
   - one resolvent `A = (N+1)^(-1)`, transported along the shift.

   The vacuum makes `E` a minimal idempotent. In the concrete model the ideal it generates
   is the simple ring of finitary matrices, which has zero annihilator. So every nonzero
   ideal of the model contains `E`, and no simple quotient contains `Q`. *Dies* for
   simplicity. It locates the requirement: a purely infinite version needs an operator
   that acts as the integer `m` on a non-minimal idempotent, for every `m`, with finitely
   many relations.
3. **Weyl algebra with resolvents (same death).** In `End_Q(Q[x])`:
   - the Euler operator `θ = x∂` acts as a number operator, and `x(θ+1)^(-1)∂ = 1 - E_0`,
     with `E_0` the projection onto constants;
   - the ring `Z<x, ∂, (θ+1)^(-1)>` contains `Q E_0`, using
     `∂^(m-1)(θ+1)^(-1)x^(m-1) E_0 = ((m-1)!/m) E_0` together with Bezout for primes;
   - it is not simple, for the same vacuum reason.

   The simple Ore localization of `A_1(Q)` at `{θ + c}` does not act on `Q[x]` and is not
   finitely generated as a ring (Attempt 1).
4. **Binary Leavitt with a number operator (only `Z[1/2]`).**
   - Adjoin to `L_Z(1,2)` the operator `N` with `N S_1 = S_1(2N)`, `N S_2 = S_2(2N+1)`,
     and its resolvent `(N+1)^(-1)` (the permutative model on `Q^(N)`).
   - The transported resolvents are `(2^k N + j)^(-1)` with `1 <= j <= 2^k`. Their
     contents are powers of `2`. So by Gauss's lemma, every constant in the subring of
     `Q(N)` generated by `N` and these resolvents lies in `Z[1/2]`.
   - Each further prime needs its own finite-state isometry (multiplication by `p`), so
     infinitely many generators. *Dies* for finite generation.
5. **Secondary literature, not re-read at the source** (details in the artifact):
   - Bokut: finitely generated simple envelopes over fields;
   - Belyaev (Algebra i Logika 17, 1978): Higman's theorem for rings and algebras;
   - Evans--Mandelberg--Neff: weak Boone--Higman in characteristic `p`.

   None of these, as recorded, gives finite presentation and simplicity together. The
   Cairn root `algebra-boone-higman-conjecture` records the same wall.
