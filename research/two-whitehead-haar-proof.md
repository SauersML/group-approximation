Write `p=s_0t_0` and `q=s_1t_1`.  Prefix cancellation gives

```text
t_i s_j=delta_(ij),        p+q=1,        pq=qp=0.       (1)
```

The three-root multiplication from the first prerequisite, applied once to
each branch, gives

```text
W_0=[[q,s_0],[t_0,0]],            W_0^2=1,
W_1=[[p,s_1],[t_1,0]],            W_1^2=1.              (2)
```

Using `(1)`,

```text
W_1W_0
 =[[pq+s_1t_0, p s_0],[t_1q,t_1s_0]]
 =[[s_1t_0,s_0],[t_1,0]]=C.                             (3)
```

Regard the two rank-one module copies as the binary cylinders `[0]` and
`[1]`.  The nonzero prefix entries of `(3)` give the complete table

```text
[00] -> [01],             [01] -> [1],
[1]  -> [00].                                             (4)
```

The suffix after each displayed prefix is unchanged.  Hence `C^3=1`, while
`C` and `C^2` are nonidentity.  The group generated by the two involutions
in `(2)` is therefore the quotient of

```text
<u,v | u^2=v^2=(vu)^3=1>=S_3
```

in which the product has order three.  No proper quotient of `S_3` retains
two involutions with product of order three, so the actor is exactly `S_3`.

The elementary row module is faithful: its matrix entries recover every
coefficient.  Conjugation by `W_i` right-multiplies the two row coordinates
`(a_2,a_3)` by `W_i^(-1)=W_i` and fixes the remaining coordinates.  Thus it
gives an action of this `S_3` on `(R,+)^(n-1)`.  The actor block meets the
strict first-row subgroup trivially, proving the split normal form `(CWH2)`.
The Steinberg subgroup maps onto `(CWH2)`, and the quotient remains injective
on `N`.

For a finite row window `F`, put

```text
V=span_(F_2){g.f:g in S_3, f in F}.
```

This is a finite-dimensional `S_3`-invariant vector space.  Hence
`V semidirect S_3` is a finite subgroup of `(CWH2)`.  These finite groups
exhaust `(CWH2)`, proving local finiteness and LEF.  Their left regular
characters vanish on every retained nonidentity row element.  Passing along
an exhaustion and pulling back through the elementary quotient gives the
full Haar restriction on `N`; `lef-implies-operator-mf` gives the required
operator-norm matrix origin.

Finally `z=x_13(q)` is a nonidentity row involution.  Once its coefficient is
included in `F`, left translation by its image is a union of two-cycles, so
its trace is zero and its operator-norm distance from the identity is two.
