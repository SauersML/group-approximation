**Novelty.** No novelty is claimed with confidence.
- The countable case (a mapping telescope of graphs) and the cohomological bound (Auslander's lemma) are standard.
- A literature search on 2026-09-17 (two web searches plus a graph search) found no statement of the geometric bound for uncountable chains.
- It may be folklore.

## Statement

Let `G = ⋃_{α<λ} G_α` be a continuous well-ordered chain of subgroups, with `G_0 = 1` and `G_μ = ⋃_{α<μ} G_α` at limit ordinals `μ`. Suppose each successor step `G_α ≤ G_{α+1}` is **free-based**, meaning one of the following holds:

- **(i)** `G_α` is free, and `gd G_{α+1} ≤ 2`; or
- **(ii)** `G_{α+1}` is the fundamental group of a graph of groups in which `G_α` is a vertex group, every edge group is free, and every other vertex group has `gd ≤ 2`.

Then `gd G ≤ 2`.

The engine is the **free-base extension lemma**. Let `H ≤ G` with `H` free and `gd G ≤ 2`. Then *every* 2-dimensional `K(H,1)` is a subcomplex of some 2-dimensional `K(G,1)`, and the inclusion induces `H ≤ G`.

## Corollaries

- **Chains of free groups.** Suppose `G` is the union of a continuous chain of free subgroups. Then `gd G ≤ 2`, because every step is of type (i).
  - **(C1)** In particular, every `κ`-free group of cardinality `κ` (every subgroup generated by fewer than `κ` elements is free; for uncountable `κ` this is the same as every subgroup of size `< κ` being free, while for `κ = ℵ_0` it means locally free) has `gd ≤ 2`. This includes every `ℵ_1`-free group of cardinality `ℵ_1`.
- **(C2) Non-free groups.** If such a `G` is not free, then `cd G = gd G = 2`.
  - `cd G ≥ 2` holds by Stallings–Swan, which is valid for arbitrary groups.
  - So the Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture holds for every non-free `ℵ_1`-free group of size `ℵ_1`.
  - Such groups exist in ZFC (G. Higman, *Almost free groups*, Proc. LMS 1951; Eklof–Mekler, *Almost Free Modules*; both cited from memory, not re-read).
  - These groups are not covered by the usual EG classes: they are not finitely generated, not countable, and not one-relator.
- **(C3) Hierarchy.** The recursion is exactly the special case of `relative-countable-eg-gives-eg-for-aleph-one-groups` in which the relative step is provable, namely when the smaller group is free.

## Why the free base matters

A 2-dimensional `K(H,1)` need not extend into a `K(G,1)` for non-free `H`.
- **Example.** Take `T^2` inside a Klein-bottle group containing `Z^2` with index 2: `H_2` injectivity fails in the double cover.
- **Necessary condition.** If `K ⊆ X` with both complexes 2-dimensional, then `pd_{ZG} I_{G/H} ≤ 1`. Here `I_{G/H}` is the augmentation kernel of `Z[G/H]`.

Freeness of `H` makes the extension unconditional. `K` is then homotopy equivalent to a graph, so it can be glued to `X` along a 2-dimensional mapping cylinder.

For countable chains, mapping telescopes already give 2-complexes. At uncountable limit ordinals there is no telescope, so one needs *nested subcomplexes*, and nesting is exactly what the lemma supplies.
