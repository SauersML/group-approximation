The universal deletion scheme H_{R,M} does not approach optimal graphing cost, and it fails on a group already known to have fixed price one. For Gamma = F_2 x Z with the standard Cayley graph T_4 x Z, every free action has cost one, while p_c < p_u holds by the nonuniqueness theorem for products of a regular tree with an infinite quasi-transitive graph. The free and wired minimal spanning forests therefore differ, the wired forest has expected root degree two, and delta_G = (1/2) E deg_F(o) - 1 is strictly positive. Since F is contained in every H_{R,M}, half the expected root degree of H_{R,M} is at least 1 + delta_G for all R and M.

So the completion "exhausting all finite-cycle priority deletions approaches action cost" is false. The outputs are valid connected graphings at every finite stage, which is the point: a valid generating graphing can stay uniformly more expensive than the infimum defining cost. The counterexample concerns identically distributed independent edge priorities and does not rule out priority constructions that use an asymmetric starting relation and selected alternatives.

Status records a written deduction from the cited spanning-forest and percolation theorems, not independent validation.
