We use normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norms throughout.

### 1. A commutant gauge cannot hide a transvection

Let `F=GL_M(F_2)` with `M>=3`, let `b` be a transvection, let
`rho:F->U(H)` be arbitrary, and let `Z in rho(F)'` be unitary. Then

```text
||rho(b)Z-I||_2 >= (1/2)||rho(b)-I||_2.                 (CCE1)
```

It suffices first to work in an irreducible representation `pi`. Choose
transvections `a,c` with `[a,c]=b`. All three are conjugate involutions.
If `t` is the normalized dimension of the negative eigenspace of
`pi(b)`, it is also the negative-eigenspace fraction for `pi(a)` and
`pi(c)`. Put `A=pi(a)`, `B=pi(b)`, and `C=pi(c)`. Then

```text
2 sqrt(t)
 = ||B-I||_2
 = ||[A,C]-I||_2
 = ||AC-CA||_2
 <= 2||C+I||_2
 = 4 sqrt(1-t).                                        (CCE2)
```

The middle equality follows by multiplying `[A,C]-I` on the right by
the unitary `CA`. Consequently `t<=4/5`.

On the isotypic block `V_pi tensor C^m`, a commutant unitary has the
form `I tensor z`. Since `tr_pi(B)=1-2t`,

```text
||B tensor z-I||_2^2
 = 2-2 Re((1-2t)tr_m(z))
 >= 2-2|1-2t|
 = 4 min(t,1-t)
 >= t
 = (1/4)||B-I||_2^2.                                  (CCE3)
```

The penultimate inequality is immediate for `t<=1/2`, and for
`t>=1/2` it is exactly `t<=4/5`. Squared normalized norms on an
arbitrary representation are the dimension-weighted sums of the
isotypic squared norms. Thus `(CCE3)` proves `(CCE1)`, with no bound on
the multiplicities.

### 2. Every fixed Toeplitz relator has bounded endpoint rank

Let `theta_N` be the standard finite-section substitution. Every named
generator is a matrix over fixed finite-propagation words in `S_N,T_N`.
For a fixed presentation relator `r`, evaluation on coordinates farther
than a constant distance from the top endpoint agrees with evaluation in
the unilateral infinite model. The latter is exact because `TS=1`.
Hence there is a number `D_r`, independent of `N`, such that

```text
rank(theta_N(r)-I) <= D_r.                              (CCE4)
```

We record the elementary bounded-filling consequence of `(CCE4)`. If
`g in GL_M(F_2)` and `rank(g-I)<=D`, set `K=ker(g-I)` and choose a
complement `C`, so `dim(C)<=D`. Relative to `K directSum C`, let
`K_0` be the span of all `K`-components of `g(C)`. Then
`dim(K_0)<=D`, the space

```text
W=K_0 directSum C
```

is `g`-invariant, and `g` fixes pointwise a complement to `K_0` in
`K`. Thus `g` is the identity off a space of dimension at most `2D`.
The finite group `GL_d(F_2)` is generated by transvections for every
`d>=2`. Taking the maximum of its transvection diameter over
`d<=2D` gives a constant `C(D)` such that `g` is a product of at
most `C(D)` transvections. All nonidentity transvections in `GL_M(F_2)`
are conjugate.

It follows from `(CCE4)` that, for every fixed relator `r`,

```text
||rho(theta_N(r))-I||_2
 <= C(D_r)||rho(b_N)-I||_2,                             (CCE5)
```

where `b_N` is either endpoint rank-one transvection.

### 3. The distinguished endpoint relation strips off the carrier

The fixed Tietze-expanded presentation has a fixed consequence word
`q_0` expressing the Steinberg relation that uses `TS=1`. Its
Toeplitz value is the top endpoint transvection `b_N`. A fixed
van Kampen filling for `q_0` gives a constant `A_0`, depending only
on the presentation, such that

```text
||q_0(U)-I||_2 <= A_0 delta(U).                         (CCE6)
```

Because every `Z_s` commutes with the entire carrier `rho(F_N)`, word
evaluation separates without reordering the gauges among themselves:

```text
q_0(U)=rho(b_N)Z_(q_0),       Z_(q_0) in rho(F_N)'.
```

Apply `(CCE1)` and `(CCE6)`:

```text
||rho(b_N)-I||_2 <= 2A_0 delta(U).                      (CCE7)
```

The finite-section head value `h_N=theta_N(w)` is the bottom endpoint
transvection and is conjugate to `b_N`. Therefore `(CCE7)` also holds
with `h_N` in place of `b_N`.

For each defining relator, write

```text
r(U)=rho(theta_N(r))Z_r.
```

Equations `(CCE5)--(CCE7)` and the triangle inequality give

```text
||Z_r-I||_2
 <= ||r(U)-I||_2+||rho(theta_N(r))-I||_2
 <= (1+2A_0 C(D_r)) delta(U).                           (CCE8)
```

Thus the gauge tuple `Z=(Z_s)` is itself a Jacobson microstate, with a
dimension-free linear loss. This conclusion did not use a multiplicity
bound.

### 4. Bounded coherence collapses the remaining gauge head

In the isotypic decomposition, every gauge has the form

```text
Z_s=directSum_lambda I_(V_lambda) tensor z_(s,lambda).
```

If `m_lambda<=K`, the C-star algebra generated by the gauge tuple has
irreducible degree at most `K`; the factors `dim(V_lambda)` merely
change the normalized trace weights. Apply
`bounded-block-jacobson-microstates-also-collapse` to `(CCE8)`. There is
a modulus `omega_K(epsilon)->0` such that, with

```text
A_1=max_(r in R)(1+2A_0 C(D_r)),
```

one has

```text
||Z_w-I||_2 <= omega_K(A_1 delta(U)).                   (CCE9)
```

Finally,

```text
w(U)=rho(h_N)Z_w,
```

so `(CCE7)` and `(CCE9)` imply

```text
||w(U)-I||_2
 <= 2A_0 delta(U)+omega_K(A_1 delta(U)).                (CCE10)
```

This is `(BCT1)`, with the right side as `Omega_K`.

The argument pinpoints its own boundary. Inequality `(CCE1)` already
works for arbitrary multiplicity, so an unbounded commutant gauge cannot
hide the carrier endpoint. The missing estimate is precisely a
dimension-free head modulus for the gauge tuple in `(CCE8)`. A perturbation
outside the commutant is different: then the carrier/gauge word separation
used before `(CCE7)` fails, and isotype mixing is a genuinely new case.
