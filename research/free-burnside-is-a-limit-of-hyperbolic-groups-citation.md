This is a literature import, not a derivation.

Source: R. Coulon, *Small cancellation theory and Burnside problem*,
arXiv:1302.6933. The TeX source was read on 2026-09-13
(`notes.tex`, `0_introduction.tex`, `3_small_cancellation.tex`). The notes
say: "In these notes we detail the geometrical approach of small cancellation
theory used by T.~Delzant and M.~Gromov to provide a new proof of the
infiniteness of free Burnside groups and periodic quotients of torsion-free
hyperbolic groups."

- **Theorem `res : SC - periodic quotient`** (item 4): "Let $G$ be a
  non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic group. There exists a critical
  exponent $n_0$ such that for every odd integer $n \geq n_0$, the quotient
  $G/G^n$ is infinite."
- **Proposition `res: SC - induction lemma`** (the induction step, item 1): "Let
  $G$ be a group acting properly co-compactly by isometries on a geodesic
  proper $\delta_1$-hyperbolic space $X$." Its conclusion reads: "Then there
  exists a geodesic proper $\delta_1$-hyperbolic space $\bar X$ on which
  $\bar G$ acts properly, co-compactly by isometries. The action of $\bar G$
  on $\bar X$ satisfies also Points ..."
  - Point 1 of that proposition is: "$G$ is non elementary."
- **Proof of the theorem, induction** (item 2): "Let $K_k$ be the normal
  subgroup of $G_k$ generated by $\{r^n, r \in R_k\}$ and $G_{k+1}$ the
  quotient of $G_k$ by $K_k$." The initialization is "We put $G_0 = G$".
- **Direct limit** (item 3): "We denote by $G_\infty$ the direct limit of the
  sequence $(G_k)$. We claim that $G_\infty$ is isomorphic to $G/G^n$." The
  paragraph ends: "It follows that the kernel of the map
  $G \twoheadrightarrow G_\infty$ is exactly $G^n$." For a direct limit of
  surjections, that kernel is the increasing union of the kernels of
  `G -> G_k`.
- **Hyperbolicity of the stages** (item 1): the conclusion of the proof says
  "This contradicts the fact that $G_k$ is a non-elementary hyperbolic group."
  The introduction says "It forces the groups $G_k$ to be non-virtually cyclic
  and hyperbolic."

The bound `n_1(G)` in the claim is the odd exponent threshold fixed in the
proof: "there exists an integer $n_1 \geq n_0$ such that ... From now on we fix
an odd integer $n \geq n_1$." It depends on `G` through the rescaled Cayley
graph.
