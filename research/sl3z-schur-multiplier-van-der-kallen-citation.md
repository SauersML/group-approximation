**Source.** W. van der Kallen, *The Schur Multipliers of SL(3,Z) and SL(4,Z)*, Math. Ann. 212
(1974), 47--49. Crossref metadata for DOI `10.1007/BF01343979` was fetched on 2026-09-16 and
matches this title, journal, volume and page range.

**Pin.** The author's own scan is linked from his publication page
(`webspace.science.uu.nl/~kalle101/publications.html`, entry "The Schur multipliers of SL(3,Z) and
SL(4,Z), PDF"). The file `schur.pdf` was fetched on 2026-09-16 and its page 47 image read
directly. Verbatim:

> "If G is a perfect group, then we will denote its universal central extension by G~ → G. The
> kernel of G~ → G is the Schur multiplier M(G) of G."

> "Theorem. (i) M(SL(3,Z)) is generated by two elements of order 2: w_12^4 and w_12 w_21. They
> are distinct."

> "Corollary. M(SL(3,Z)) ≃ M(SL(4,Z)) ≃ Z/2 × Z/2."

Here `w_ij = x_ij x_ji^(-1) x_ij`. The same page recalls Milnor's facts that `K_2(n,Z)` has
order 2 for `n >= 3`, and that the Schur multiplier of `SL(n,Z)` equals `K_2(n,Z)` for `n >= 5`.

**From M to H_2.** For a perfect group, the kernel of the universal central extension is
`H_2(G; Z)` (Milnor, *Introduction to Algebraic K-Theory*, §5). This is classical and was not
re-read this session.

**Trust surface.**
- The primary paper was read at the statement level only; the three-page proof was not checked.
- Only finiteness of `H_2(SL_3(Z); Z)` is load-bearing downstream.
