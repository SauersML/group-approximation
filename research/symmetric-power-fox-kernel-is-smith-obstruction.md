Put `R=Z[1/2]` and let `S_n=Sym^n(R^3)`, of rank

```text
d_n=(n+1)(n+2)/2.                                      (SPM1)
```

Let

```text
T_n:S_n -> S_n directSum S_n,             P |-> (XP,YP) (SPM2)
```

be the stacked torus/authenticated Fox operator.  Then the following are
equivalent:

1. `T_n` has zero kernel after base change to every field of odd
   characteristic;
2. the ideal generated by the `d_n x d_n` minors of `T_n` is the unit ideal
   in `R`;
3. `T_n` is a split injection of finite free `R`-modules; and
4. the Smith cokernel of `T_n` has no odd torsion and `T_n` has full column
   rank over `Q`.

The two rows admit explicit functional equations `(SPR1)--(SPR2)` in every
degree.  The established degree-one through degree-four claims prove that
the maximal-minor ideal is the unit ideal for `n<=4` on the defining side.

This is the exact boundary of the present polynomial method.  A proof in all
degrees must show that the determinantal ideal remains a unit, or produce a
uniform left inverse over `R`.  Merely finding one nonzero rational minor is
insufficient: degree four already has a natural generic minor with four odd
prime divisors, all removed only by complementary minors.
