Direct proof (2026-09-17, lane sw-010). Setting (L_Z) as in the target: `A`, `ν`,
`Γ_0 <= Γ`.

## Step 0: pass to `Γ_0`

- `A x| Γ_0` has finite index in `A x| Γ`, so `H_1 = H ∩ (A x| Γ_0)` has finite index
  in `H`. Let `H_2` be the normal core of `H_1` in `H`.
- `H_2` is finitely generated by Reidemeister--Schreier, with Schreier generators `B_2`.
- **Rewriting.** A word `w` over `B` is read through the finite permutation action of
  `H` on `H/H_2`.
  - If `w` does not fix the coset `H_2`, then `w != 1`.
  - Otherwise `w` rewrites, in linear time, into a word over `B_2` of length `|w|`
    representing the same element.
- So it suffices to solve the word problem of `H_2 <= A x| Γ_0` in time `C*l^C + C`.
  From now on `Γ_0` commutes with `ν`, and `B` is a finite subset of `A x| Γ_0`,
  closed under inverses, that generates `H`.

## Step 1: the completion along `ν`

Let `e` be the exponent of the finite group `A/νA`.

- **Powers of `e`.** `eA ⊆ νA`. By induction,
  `e^k A = e(e^(k-1)A) ⊆ e ν^(k-1)A = ν^(k-1)(eA) ⊆ ν^k A`, since `ν` is additive.
- **Finite levels.** `ν^j A/ν^(j+1)A ≅ A/νA` because `ν` is injective, so `A/ν^k A` is
  finite of order `|A/νA|^k`.
- **The completion.** Put `Â = lim_k A/ν^k A`, a profinite abelian group. Since
  `∩_k ν^k A = 0`, the map `A -> Â` is injective.
- **Module structure.** On `A/ν^k A` both `e^k` and `ν^k` act as zero. So it is a
  module over `Λ_k = (Z/e^k)[t]/(t^k)`, with `t` acting by `ν`, compatibly in `k`.
  Hence `Â` is a topological module over `Λ = lim_k Λ_k = Z_e-hat[[t]]`, where
  `Z_e-hat = lim Z/e^k`.
- **Linearity.** Each `γ in Γ_0` is an automorphism commuting with `ν`, so
  `γ(ν^k A) = ν^k γ(A) = ν^k A`. So `γ` induces automorphisms of every `A/ν^k A`
  commuting with `t` and with `Z`, hence `Λ_k`-linear. The limit `γ-hat` is a
  `Λ`-linear automorphism of `Â` that extends `γ`.
- **Finite generation.** Pick `a_1, ..., a_r in A` lifting generators of `A/νA`, and
  let `π: Λ^r -> Â`, `π(λ) = Σ λ_i a_i`.
  - `π` is continuous, and `Λ^r` is compact, so `π(Λ^r)` is closed.
  - Every `a in A` satisfies `a = Σ n_i a_i + ν a'`. Iterating gives
    `a ≡ Σ p_i(ν) a_i (mod ν^k A)` with `p_i in Z[t]`. So `π(Λ^r)` meets every coset of
    every `ν^k`-level, i.e. it is dense.
  - Hence `π` is onto: `a_1, ..., a_r` generate `Â` as a `Λ`-module.

## Step 2: a finitely generated coefficient ring

- For each `b = (α_b, γ_b) in B`, acting on `A` by `x -> γ_b(x) + α_b`, choose
  - `λ_b in Λ^r` with `π(λ_b) = α_b`, and
  - a matrix `C_b in M_r(Λ)` with `γ_b-hat(a_j) = Σ_i (C_b)_(ij) a_i`.
  Both exist by surjectivity of `π`.
- Let `S <= Λ` be the `Z`-subalgebra generated by all entries of all `λ_b` and `C_b`.
  So `S` is a finitely generated commutative ring, `S = Z[x_1, ..., x_m]/I`, where the
  `x_k` map to those entries.
- Let `M_S = Σ_i S a_i <= Â`.
  - `Λ`-linearity gives `γ_b-hat(Σ s_j a_j) = Σ_(i,j) s_j (C_b)_(ij) a_i in M_S`, and
    `α_b in M_S`.
  - So every `b in B`, and therefore every element of `H`, maps `M_S` into itself by
    the affine map `x -> γ-hat(x) + α`.
- **Faithfulness on `{0, a_1, ..., a_r}`.** Suppose `h = (α, γ) in H` fixes `0` and
  every `a_j`. Then `α = h(0) = 0`. Also `γ-hat` fixes a set of `Λ`-module generators of
  `Â` and is `Λ`-linear, so `γ-hat = id`, so `γ = id` on `A ⊆ Â`. Hence `h = 1`.

## Step 3: lifts and the identity test

- Let `P_0 = Z[x_1, ..., x_m]`. The surjection `P_0^r -> M_S`, `u -> Σ ū_i a_i`, has
  kernel a submodule `K ⊆ P_0^r`. It contains `I P_0^r` and is finitely generated
  because `P_0` is noetherian. Fix generators `k_1, ..., k_c`.
- Write `≡` for congruence modulo `K`.
- **Lifts.** For `b in B` let `v_b in P_0^r` and `W_b in M_r(P_0)` have entries equal
  to the corresponding variables `x_k`, or `0`. By Step 2, `ξ -> W_b ξ + v_b` lifts the
  action of `b` on `M_S`.
- **Composition.** For a word `b_1 ... b_l`, set `(v, W)` recursively by
  `(v_b, W_b)·(v', W') = (v_b + W_b v', W_b W')`. By induction, `ξ -> Wξ + v` lifts the
  action of the word.
- **Identity criterion.** By the faithfulness in Step 2, the word is trivial iff
  - (i) `v ≡ 0`, and
  - (ii) `W e_j - e_j ≡ 0` for `j = 1, ..., r`.

  - **(⇒)** Evaluate at `0` and at `a_j`.
  - **(⇐)** The word fixes `0` and every `a_j`, so it is trivial by Step 2.

## Step 4: size of the lifted data

Let `‖f‖_1` be the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients of `f in P_0`, and
`‖W‖ = max_i Σ_j ‖W_(ij)‖_1`. Then `‖fg‖_1 <= ‖f‖_1 ‖g‖_1` and the matrix norm is
submultiplicative.

- Every `W_b` has `‖W_b‖ <= r`, and every entry of `v_b` has `‖·‖_1 <= 1`.
- For a word of length `l`:
  - `‖W‖ <= r^l`;
  - every entry of `v` has `‖·‖_1 <= l r^l`;
  - all entries have total degree `<= l`.
- A polynomial of degree `<= D` in `m` variables has at most `(D+1)^m` monomials.
- So `(v, W)` is computed with `poly(l)` arithmetic operations on integers of `O(l)`
  bits, i.e. in time `C*l^C + C`.

## Step 5: module membership by strong Gröbner reduction over `Z`

- **Degree-one encoding.** Let `P = Z[x_1, ..., x_m, y_1, ..., y_r]` and
  `λ(u) = Σ u_i y_i` for `u in P_0^r`. Let `J` be the ideal generated by
  `λ(k_1), ..., λ(k_c)` and all `y_i y_(i')`.
  - **Claim:** `u in K` iff `λ(u) in J`.
  - **(⇒)** This is clear.
  - **(⇐)** Suppose `λ(u) = Σ f_s λ(k_s) + Σ g_(ii') y_i y_(i')`. Compare the parts of
    `y`-degree one. The `g`-terms contribute only in degree `>= 2`, and `λ(k_s)` is
    homogeneous of degree one. So `λ(u) = Σ f_s^(0) λ(k_s)`, where `f_s^(0)` is the
    `y`-degree-zero part of `f_s`. Hence `u = Σ f_s^(0) k_s in K`.

  This is Step 3 of `commutative-algebra-affine-groups-polynomial-word-problem-proof`
  with `F_q` replaced by `Z`; there the relations `I` are absorbed into `K`.
- **Strong Gröbner basis.** Fix a graded monomial order on `P`. A finite
  `G = {g_1, ..., g_n} ⊆ J` is a *strong Gröbner basis* if every nonzero `f in J` has
  some `g in G` whose leading term (coefficient times monomial) divides `LT(f)` in
  `P`. Such `G` exist for every ideal of a polynomial ring over a PID (trust surface).
  `G` is fixed finite data attached to `(H, B)`. Let `K_G` be the maximum absolute
  value of a coefficient of any `g in G`.
- **Reduction.** Given `f`:
  - while `f != 0`, look for `g in G` with `LT(g) | LT(f)`;
  - if there is none, answer `f ∉ J`;
  - otherwise replace `f` by `f - (LT(f)/LT(g)) g`.
- **Correctness.**
  - Each step preserves the class of `f` modulo `J` and kills `LT(f)` exactly.
  - If `f in J`, every intermediate `f` is in `J`. So a divisor always exists, and the
    loop ends at `0`.
  - If `f ∉ J`, no intermediate `f` is `0`. So the loop stops with "no divisor", and the
    answer is `f ∉ J`.
- **Degree.** All monomials of `(LT(f)/LT(g)) g` are `<= LM(f)`. The order is graded,
  so if `f` has degree `<= D`, every intermediate `f` has degree `<= D`.
- **Termination.** `LM(f)` strictly decreases at each step, within the monomials of
  degree `<= D` in `m + r` variables. So there are at most `T = (D+1)^(m+r)` steps.
- **Coefficient growth.** Let `M` bound the absolute values of the coefficients of `f`
  before a step. The quotient `LT(f)/LT(g) = q·μ` has `|q| <= |LC(f)| <= M`, since
  `LC(g)` divides `LC(f)` in `Z`. The new coefficients are bounded by `M + M K_G`. So
  after `T` steps the bound is `M_0 (1 + K_G)^T`, i.e.
  `log_2 M_0 + T log_2(1 + K_G)` bits.
- **Cost.** Each step touches at most `(D+1)^(m+r)` monomials, with integers of that
  bit length, so it costs `poly(D, log M_0)`.
- **Application.** The tests (i) and (ii) are `r + 1` reductions with `D <= l + 1` and
  `log M_0 = O(l)` (Step 4). The total time is `C*l^C + C`. With Step 0 this proves
  conclusion 1.

## Step 6: one bound for the class

- `l^C <= C^C 2^l`, so `C*l^C + C <= C'*2^(C'*l) + C'*l + C'`.
- The constant depends on `(H, B)`; the function `g(n) = 2^n` does not. So every such
  word problem lies in `F(2^n)` of `(UWB1)`. This proves conclusion 2. ∎

## Independent route to conclusion 2 (no strong Gröbner bases)

- **Aschenbrenner's bound.** If `f_0 in (f_1, ..., f_n) ⊆ Z[X_1, ..., X_N]`, then
  `f_0 = Σ g_i f_i` with `deg g_i <= (2d)^(2^(O(N^2)))(h+1)`. Here `d` is the maximum
  total degree and `h` the maximum height of the coefficients of `f_0, ..., f_n`.
- **Size.** Apply it to `λ(u) in J` with `N = m + r` fixed, `d <= l + d_0` and
  `log(max |coefficient|) = O(l)`. Under either reading of "height" (bit size or
  absolute value), the cofactor degree is `D' <= exp(C l)`.
- **Truncation.** Taking `y`-degree-zero parts as in Step 5 keeps the degree bound. So
  `u in K` iff `u = Σ f_s k_s` for some `f_s in P_0` of degree `<= D'`.
- **Linear algebra.** That is an integer linear system with `exp(C l)` unknowns and
  equations and entries of `O(l)` bits. Its solvability over `Z` is decided in
  polynomial time in its size by Hermite normal form (Kannan--Bachem 1979), hence in
  time `C*2^(C*l) + C`.

This proves conclusion 2 directly.

## Trust surface

- **Strong Gröbner bases over `Z`.** Every ideal of `R[X_1, ..., X_N]`, for `R` a PID,
  has a finite strong Gröbner basis for any monomial order. Quoted from
  Adams--Loustaunau, *An Introduction to Gröbner Bases* (AMS GSM 3, 1994), Ch. 4, §4.5;
  see also D. Lichtblau, *Effective computation of strong Gröbner bases over Euclidean
  domains*, Illinois J. Math. 56 (2012). Neither was re-read at source. Only
  conclusion 1 depends on this.
- **Aschenbrenner.** M. Aschenbrenner, *Ideal membership in polynomial rings over the
  integers*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 17 (2004), arXiv:math/0305172. The degree bound
  `(2d)^(2^(O(N^2)))(h+1)` was read from the arXiv v2 abstract today. The body was not
  re-read.
- **Hermite normal form in polynomial time.** R. Kannan, A. Bachem, SIAM J. Comput. 8
  (1979). Quoted, not re-read.
- **Unused.** The linearization of `char-p-untwisted-self-similar-affine-groups-are-linear`
  is not used. Step 1 is the pro-`e` completion sketched in artifact §9.2, written out
