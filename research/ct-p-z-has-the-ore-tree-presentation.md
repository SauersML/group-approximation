presentation (1609.07058, eq. `infinitepresentation`); that theorem is not used here.

## Statement

Let `P` be a finite set of odd primes and `P' = P ∪ {2}`. An *ordered natural basis* of size `n`
is a tuple `(E_1, …, E_n)` of boxes forming the leaf set of a tree grown from `Z` by `p`-splits,
`p ∈ P'`.

Let `𝒞` be the category with objects `n ≥ 1`, generated by:
- *splits* `x_{n,i,p}: n → n+p−1`, which replace the `i`-th cell by its `p` children in order;
- *permutations* `σ ∈ Sym(n)`, acting as automorphisms of `n`.

Its relations are:
- (a) the multiplication of `Sym(n)`;
- (b) far commutation: splitting two different cells in either order, with the induced index
  shifts;
- (c) colour exchange: `x_{n,i,p}` followed by `q`-splits of its `p` children equals `x_{n,i,q}`
  followed by `p`-splits of its `q` children, followed by the permutation of the `pq`
  grandchildren matching `j + pl ≡ j' + ql' (mod pq)`;
- (d) naturality: `σ` followed by a split of cell `σ(i)` equals the split of cell `i` followed by
  the induced permutation.

Then `PC` (= `CT_P(Z)`, by `piecewise-canonical-permutations-are-transposition-products`) is
isomorphic to the group presented by these generators and relations together with
`x_{n,n,2} = 1` for all `n` (the comb spanning tree), based at the object 1.

## Proof

**O1 (validity).** `Hom_𝒞(1, n)` is in bijection with the ordered natural bases of size `n`: a
word is sent to the basis obtained from `(Z)` by applying its splits and permutations. The map is
surjective by definition. For injectivity, use (d) to push the permutations to the end; it then
suffices to show that two trees with the same leaf set are equal modulo (b), (c) and a final
permutation. Induct on the number of leaves.
- **Different root primes `p ≠ q`.** The leaf set refines both root splits, hence (CRT) the
  `pq`-split, whose cells inside the `p`-child `A_a` are exactly the `q`-children of `A_a`.
  1. By induction, each `p`-child subtree is equal to one whose first split is `q`.
  2. Use (b) to bring these `q`-splits directly after the root.
  3. Apply (c) at the root.
- **Equal root primes.** Recurse into the children, using (b) to separate the independent
  subtrees.

The same argument, based at any fixed ordered natural basis `b_m`, identifies `Hom_𝒞(m, n)` with
the ordered natural refinements of `b_m` of size `n`. So `𝒞` is *thin* relative to its
realization.

**O2 (Ore).** Any two morphisms out of `m` have a common right multiple: realize them as
refinements of `b_m`, and refine every cell to the full `M`-split, `M` the lcm of all moduli. This
is reachable from each by splits, because the full split of a box is a tree below it. Left and
right cancellation hold because the realization in O1 is injective and composition is
refinement.

**O3 (fractions = PC).** By Ore's theorem the groupoid of fractions `𝒢` has
`Hom_𝒢(1,1) = {g^{-1} f : f, g ∈ Hom_𝒞(1, n)}`. Here `g^{-1} f = g'^{-1} f'` iff the pairs agree after
a common right multiple. Send `g^{-1} f` to the element of `PC` that maps the basis of `g` onto
the basis of `f`, cell by cell and canonically.
- *Well defined, and a homomorphism:* simultaneous refinement does not change the element, and
  composition is by common refinement of the middle basis.
- *Injective:* two pairs with the same element become equal after refining the source to a
  common natural basis. The targets are then determined, and O1 applies.
- *Surjective:* every `g ∈ PC` maps some natural partition onto a natural partition (Lemma 1 of
  the generation node).

**O4 (presentation).** Two standard facts:
1. The groupoid of fractions of an Ore category is its universal groupoid (every functor to a
   groupoid factors uniquely), so it is presented, as a groupoid, by the category's generators
   and relations.
2. The vertex group of a connected groupoid presented by `(S | R)` is presented by `S` and `R`
   with the edges of a spanning tree set to 1.

`𝒢` is connected (every size is reached from 1 by binary splits), and the comb edges `x_{n,n,2}`
form a spanning tree. With O3 this gives the statement. ∎

## O1 in full (pass 5, 09-19)

The O1 paragraph above asserts that "each `p`-child subtree is equal to one whose first split is
`q`". This rests on the following lemma, which O1 did not state.

**Restriction lemma.** Let `M` be the leaf set of a tree rooted at a box `Q`, and suppose `M`
refines the `p`-split of `Q`. Then for each `p`-child `P_c` of `Q`, the set
`M|P_c = {E ∈ M : E ⊆ P_c}` is the leaf set of a tree rooted at `P_c`. Hence `M` is the leaf set
of a tree whose root split is `p`.

*Proof.* Induct on `|M|`. The case `|M| = 1` is vacuous. Let `r` be the root prime of the tree.
- If `r = p`, the restrictions are its subtrees.
- If `r ≠ p`, let `R_b` be the `r`-children of `Q`.
  1. By CRT, `R_b ∩ P_c` is a single class mod `pr·m_Q`. It is both a `p`-child of `R_b` and an
     `r`-child of `P_c`.
  2. So `M|R_b`, the leaf set of the subtree at `R_b`, refines the `p`-split of `R_b`. It has
     fewer leaves than `M`.
  3. By induction, each `M|(R_b ∩ P_c)` is a tree leaf set.
  4. `M|P_c` is their union over `b`, so it is the leaf set of the tree that splits `P_c` by `r`
     and then grows those trees. ∎

**O1, the tree case, in full.** Claim: two tree words (splits only) with the same leaf set, rooted
at the same cell, are equal in `𝒞` up to a final permutation. Induct on the number of leaves.
1. Relation (b) (far commutation) rewrites a tree word as its root split followed by the
   subtree words of the children, one after another.
2. **Same root prime.** The subtrees at each child have the same leaf set, namely the
   restriction. Apply induction to each, then move the resulting block permutations to the end
   by (d).
3. **Root primes `p ≠ q`.**
   - The common leaf set refines both root splits. So its restriction to each `p`-child `A_a`
     refines the `q`-split of `A_a`: a `q`-child of `A_a` is `A_a ∩` a `q`-child of `Q`, by CRT.
   - By the restriction lemma, there is a tree rooted at `A_a` with root split `q` and the same
     leaves as the subtree at `A_a`.
   - Induction (fewer leaves) makes the two subtrees equal up to a permutation.
   - After that, the word is: split by `p`, then every child by `q`, then subtrees at the
     grandchildren. Relation (c) at the root turns it into: split by `q`, then every child by `p`,
     then a permutation. The grandchild subtrees are carried along by (d).
   - The result has root prime `q`, the same as the other word, so case 2 applies.

The general case of O1 (words with permutations) reduces to this one: by (d), push the
permutations to the end. Two words with the same realization then differ by a permutation of an
ordered basis with distinct cells. That permutation is determined, and by (a) the two words agree.

## Lesson for general BH

For full groups of box-type Cantor algebras, "presented by local moves" is a formal consequence
of three finite checks:
- validity: equal leaf sets are connected by exchange squares;
- Ore: common refinements;
- the free action.

So a host's infinite presentation is never the hard part. The hard part is comparing it with
another generating set, such as the class transpositions, and that comparison lives entirely in
the central defects of the 2-cells.
