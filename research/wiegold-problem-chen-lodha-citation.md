Citation import, not reproved. Both sources were fetched as arXiv e-prints on MSI
on 2026-09-13 and read at the lines given. TeX macros are stripped from the quotes.

**Chen–Lodha**, *The Wiegold problem and free products of left-orderable groups*,
arXiv:2510.26073 (v1 30 October 2025, v2 1 December 2025), file `wiegold.tex`:
- **l.181:** "A longstanding open problem posed by Wiegold in 1976 (Problem FP14 in
  [MR1921705], Problem 5.52 in [Kourovka]), asks if there exist finitely generated
  perfect groups with normal rank greater than one, for which we give a positive
  answer:"
- **l.182–184, Theorem A** (`thmA: Wiegold`): "There exist finitely generated
  (even finitely presented) perfect groups that have normal rank greater than 1."
- **l.208:** "Theorem A is a corollary of Theorem B by taking A and B to be
  finitely generated perfect left-orderable groups. Some prominent examples of
  finitely presentable perfect left-orderable groups include: the commutator
  subgroup of the braid group B_n for n ≥ 5 [...], a central extension of
  Thompson's group T [...] and a central extension of the (2,3,7)-triangle group
  [...], and Higman's group [...]."
- **l.253–255, Theorem C** (`thmA: Dehn surgery`): "There are infinitely many
  different hyperbolic integer homology 3-spheres M such that π_1(M) has normal
  rank at least 2, [...]". l.257: "First, this gives infinitely many solutions to
  the Wiegold problem that are linear groups."

**Kourovka Notebook No. 21** (2026), arXiv:1401.0300, file `21tkt.tex`:
- **l.1352–1356:** "5.52. It is not hard to show that a finite perfect group is the
  normal closure of a single element. Is the same true for infinite finitely
  generated groups? J. Wiegold"
- **l.1358–1360** (the answer): "There exist finitely presented perfect groups that
  are not normal closures of a single element (L. Chen, Y. Lodha, Preprint, 2025,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26073)."
