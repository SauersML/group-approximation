Let `Gamma` contain a central involution `J` and, for one context, commuting
involutions `z_1,...,z_k`.  Assume that the subgroup generated by
`J,z_1,...,z_k` is the full elementary abelian group `(C_2)^(k+1)`.  For each
selector assignment `a in {+1,-1}^k`, put

```text
e_a^-=(1-J)/2 product_i (1+a_i z_i)/2 in C[Gamma].       (RTL1)
```

Then the canonical trace of `L(Gamma)` satisfies

```text
tau_Gamma(e_a^-)=2^(-(k+1))>0.                            (RTL2)
```

Consequently no exact group-algebra construction retaining this local
selector subgroup can do any of the following for a designated forbidden
atom `e_a^-`:

1. force `e_a^-=0` in every tracial representation;
2. make `e_a^-` properly infinite in `C*(Gamma)`; or
3. prove an inequality `c tau(e_a^-)<=E_rel` with `c>0` in every tracial
   representation, where `E_rel` is a sum of defining-relator energies.

Indeed, apply the proposed conclusion to the left regular representation.
All defining relators have zero energy, while `(RTL2)` is positive, and the
finite von Neumann algebra `L(Gamma)` contains no nonzero properly infinite
projection.

This does **not** refute a nonhyperlinearity compiler.  It proves that the
compiler must distinguish finite matrices from the regular finite von
Neumann algebra.  In the Schur--Clifford/S3 program that distinction can come
from integer packet multiplicities, finite-dimensional rank, or a
matrix-specific exactification theorem.  A triangular S3 holonomy may impose
incompatible finite-dimensional type flows while the regular representation
absorbs the same flow in infinite multiplicity; it may not be replaced by an
exact trace identity.

If a proposed local atlas avoids `(RTL2)` by collapsing the subgroup generated
by `J,z_1,...,z_k`, then it has instead deleted selector characters
algebraically.  That returns to the affine selector barrier and must be
audited separately.
