**ESTABLISHED.** Let `m, N >= 2`, and let `G` be generated by `m` elements
with `g^N = 1` for every `g in G`. Let `K` be the finite residual of `G` (the intersection
of its finite-index normal subgroups), and put

```text
c(m,N) = |R(m,N)| (m - 1) + 1,        eta_N = |1 - e^(2 pi i/N)| = 2 sin(pi/N).
```

Let `rho : G -> U(Q_d)` be any homomorphism into a norm matrix corona
`Q_d = prod_n M_(d_n) / directSum_n M_(d_n)`. Exactly one of the following
holds.

1. **`rho(K) = 1`.** Then `rho` factors through the finite group `G/K`,
   which is a quotient of `R(m,N)`. In particular `|rho(G)| <= |R(m,N)|`.
2. **`rho(K) != 1`.** Then `rho(G)` is infinite, `K` is generated by
   `m' <= c(m,N)` elements, and `K` has a quotient `H` with all of the
   following properties.
   - `H` is **hyperlinear**. It is the image of `K` in the unitary group of
     a tracial matrix ultraproduct `prod_omega M_(k_n)`, obtained from an
     operator-norm asymptotic representation `sigma_n : K -> U(k_n)` that
     represents the restriction of `rho` to `K` after reblocking.
   - `H` is **infinite**, has exponent dividing `N`, is generated by
     `m' <= c(m,N)` elements, and has **no proper finite-index subgroup**
     (so it is perfect).
   - Some generator `a` of `K` stays **quantitatively visible**:
     `||sigma_n(a) - 1||_(2,tr)^2 >= eta_N^2/(8 m') - o(1)` along `omega`.
     Hence the limit trace satisfies
     `Re tau(a) <= 1 - eta_N^2/(16 c(m,N))`.

Moreover `rho(G)` is infinite exactly in case 2.

## Consequences

3. **Per-parameter reduction.** If every hyperlinear group generated by at
   most `c(m,N)` elements, of exponent dividing `N`, is finite, then for
   every matrix-size sequence `d` every homomorphism `B(m,N) -> U(Q_d)`
   factors through `R(m,N)`. That is clause `(A)` of
   `corona-restricted-burnside-equals-power-law-moduli` for `(m,N)`, so it
   also gives clause `(B)`, the dimension-free moduli of
   `matrix-restricted-burnside-gap` for `(m,N)`.
4. **Hilbert--Schmidt Burnside implies corona Burnside.**
   `hyperlinear-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-finite` implies
   `operator-norm-restricted-burnside`. The route is
   `corona-burnside-from-hyperlinear-burnside-finiteness`, and the existing
   routes then carry it to `infinite-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-non-mf`
   and `osin-periodic-quotient-is-non-mf`. In contrapositive form: an
   infinite, finitely generated, bounded-exponent subgroup of a norm matrix
   corona produces an infinite, finitely generated hyperlinear group with no
   finite quotients, whose exponent divides that of the subgroup.
5. **The MF radical.** Always `Rad_MF(G) <= K`. The finite group `G/K` has a
   faithful regular representation in `U(k)`, `k = |G/K|`, and constant
   sequences embed `M_k` in the corona with `d_n = k`, so `G -> G/K` gives a
   corona homomorphism with kernel `K`. If `K` has no infinite hyperlinear quotient,
   then `Rad_MF(G) = K = Res_fin(G)`. For an infinite simple `G`, the
   quotient `H` equals `G`. So a nontrivial corona homomorphism makes `G`
   hyperlinear, in agreement with
   `simple-torsion-groups-have-a-macroscopic-mf-dichotomy`.

## Model tests

- `G` finite, or `N in {2,3,4,6}` (where `B(m,N)` is finite): `K = 1` and
  case 1 always holds. This is consistent, because no infinite finitely
  generated group of exponent `N` exists for these `N`.
- `G` an infinite simple group of exponent `N` (a Tarski monster of prime
  exponent): `K = G`. Case 2 means `G` is hyperlinear.
- `G = B(m,N)` infinite: case 2 is exactly what a counterexample to
  `operator-norm-restricted-burnside` must look like, and the hyperlinear
  quotient `H` is then a counterexample to
  `hyperlinear-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-finite`.

## Scope

- **No converse.** Hilbert--Schmidt models do not become operator-norm models
  (see attempt 3 of `operator-norm-restricted-burnside`), so nothing here
  shows that corona Burnside implies Hilbert--Schmidt Burnside.
- **Possibly proper quotient.** `H` may be a proper quotient of `K`. Neither
  `K` nor `G` is claimed to be hyperlinear, and `H` is not claimed to be MF.
- **Novelty.** The ingredients are established nodes: finite residuals via
  restricted Burnside, and span-of-active-ranges reblocking. The new content
  is the combination. Bounded exponent supplies the spectral gap uniformly,
  and the absence of finite quotients of `K` forces the tracial image to be
  infinite.
