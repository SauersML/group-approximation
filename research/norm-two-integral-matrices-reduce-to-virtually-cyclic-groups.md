Let `G` be any group and `A in M_n(Z[G])` self-adjoint with `||r_A|| <= 2` on
`l^2(G)^n = l^2(G x [n])`. Let `Gamma_A` be the graph on `G x [n]` whose edges are
the nonzero off-diagonal matrix entries of `r_A`.

1. **Linear growth.** Every ball of radius `r` in `Gamma_A` has at most
   `max(16, 4r+2)` vertices.
2. **Virtually cyclic stabilizers.** For each component `C`, its stabilizer `K_C`
   under left translation is finite or virtually infinite cyclic. It acts freely
   on `C` with one orbit on `C cap (G x {j})` for each `j` in a set
   `J_C <= [n]`.
3. **Decomposition.** Choose one component `C` in each `G`-orbit. The sets `J_C`
   partition `[n]`, there are `A_C in M_(J_C)(Z[K_C])`, and

   ```text
   mu_A = sum_C (|J_C| / n) mu_(A_C).
   ```

4. **Consequences.**
   - `det_(N(G)) r_(Q(A)) >= 1` for every nonzero `Q in Z[x]`.
   - Every eigenvalue of `A` is `2cos(2 pi r)` for a rational `r`, and conjugate
     eigenvalues have equal multiplicity.
   - `mu_A` is a limit of integer-matrix spectra.

**Reading.** A self-adjoint integral matrix whose spectral measure fails a Serre
inequality has norm above two, over every group. Below two the graph is finite in
every direction (`integral-operator-norm-below-two-is-quantized`). At two it is a
periodic line, and the operator never leaves amenable groups. Monsters need room.
