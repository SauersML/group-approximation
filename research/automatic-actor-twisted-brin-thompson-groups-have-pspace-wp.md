## Setting

Twisted Brin--Thompson groups are due to Belk--Zaremsky. Let `Γ` act faithfully on a countable set
`S`, and let `C = {0,1}^N`.
- `SV_Γ` acts on `C^S`. It is generated by `Γ`, which permutes coordinates, and by the
  Brin--Thompson group `BV_S`. An element of `BV_S` acts by dyadic brick maps on finitely many
  coordinates and by the identity on the others.
- The actor is **automatic** if `S` is a regular language over a finite alphabet `B` and each
  generator `γ_i` of `Γ` has a synchronous rational graph `{s ⊗ γ_i(s)}` (padded by `#`), as does
  its inverse.

Examples:
- Thompson-type actors on dyadic points: `F`, `T` or `V` acting on finite binary words ending
  in `1`, by prefix replacement.
- Houghton-type actors on `N × {1..n}`.
- Automaton groups acting on the levels of their tree.

As recalled, not re-read here, these include the standard finitely presented examples of
Belk--Zaremsky and Zaremsky.

## Statement

Let `Γ ↷ S` be automatic.

1. **Actor problems.** Computing `γ(s)` from a word `γ` of length `L` and `s ∈ S` takes
   polynomial time, and `|γ(s)| ≤ |s| + cL`. Deciding `γ = 1` in `Γ` is in PSPACE.
2. **Hosts.** Every finitely generated subgroup of `SV_Γ` has word problem in PSPACE.
3. **Refinement.** If identity in `Γ` is decidable in polynomial time, as for Thompson-type actors,
   then every finitely generated subgroup of `SV_Γ` has word problem in coNP.

## Lesson for general BH

In a twisted Brin--Thompson host, the hardness of the host is the hardness of the actor's
**permutational** problems: computing `γ(s)` and deciding whether `γ` acts trivially. The
Brin--Thompson part only adds a coNP layer. So the root's Attempt 1 ("complex actors") needs an
actor whose permutation action is not automatic. The rule that permutes coordinates must itself be
computationally rich. It is not enough for `Γ` to have a hard abstract word problem while acting
by a tame rule. That case cannot occur anyway: a faithful automatic action puts `WP(Γ)` in PSPACE
by item 1. Together with `definable-parameter-hosts-cap-simple-subgroups-at-pspace`, this places the
"minimal non-definable ingredient" of every classical host shape in the rule by which it moves
points, never in constants.

## Not claimed

- Finite presentability or simplicity of any particular `SV_Γ`. Those are Zaremsky's type (A)
  conditions, which are independent of automaticity.
- Any lower bound. Thompson-type automatic actors give coNP, matching Birget's `2V`.
