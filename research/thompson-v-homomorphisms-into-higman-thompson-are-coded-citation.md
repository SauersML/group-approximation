## The groups (Example 11.1, Section 11)

> Example 11.1 (Higman-Thompson groups). For d ≥ 2, denote by Ω_d the a graph
> consisting of one vertex and d loops, that we label by {1,...,d}. The
> corresponding map S : X_Σ → X_Σ the full one-sided shift on {1,d}^N. The
> group F(G_{Ω_d}) is the group of all homeomorphism of {1,d}^N which are
> locally given by a prefix change. This is a well studied group, namely the
> Higman-Thompson group V_{d,1}. The group V_{2,1} is more simply known as
> Thompson's group V.

The standing assumption of Section 11.1:

> We will always assume that Σ is irreducible, i.e. for any vertices x,y ∈ V
> there is a directed path going from x to y, and that Σ is not a cycle

`Ω_d` has one vertex and `d >= 2` loops, so it satisfies both conditions.

## Tables (Section 11.2)

> Every table {(v_i,w_i)}_{i∈I} defines an element F ∈ ~G_Σ with
> S(F) = ⨆_i C_{w_i} and r(F) = ⨆_i C_{v_i}, given by:
> (5) F = ⨆_{i∈I} S_{v_i}^{-1} S_{w_i}

Here `S_v^{-1} S_w` is the prefix replacement `C_w -> C_v`.

## Omega-systems (Definition 11.5)

> Definition 11.5. Let ~H be a pseudogroup over a space Y. A Σ-system in ~H is
> a family {T_e}_{e∈E} of non-zero elements of ~H with the following
> properties:
> (i) the sets s(T_e), e ∈ E are clopen and pairwise disjoint;
> (ii) for every e ∈ E we have r(T_e) = ⨆_{o(e)=t(f)} s(T_f).
> The set Z = ⨆_{e∈E} s(S_e) is called the domain of the Σ-system.

The coding map is defined just below it:

> Define a continuous map T : Z → Z by T(y) = T_{e_y}(y). Further define a map
> q : Z → X_Σ by coding the dynamical system (T,Z), namely
> q(y) = e_y e_{T(y)} e_{T^2(y)} · · ·.

## Theorem 11.11

> Theorem 11.11. Let G_1 be any minimal effective étale groupoid over a Cantor
> space X_1, and let G_{Σ_2} be a groupoid associated to irreducible one sided
> shifts of finite type. Then for every non-trivial group homomorphism
> ρ : A(G_1) → F(G_{Σ_2}), the support of ρ(A(G_1)) is a clopen subset
> U ⊂ X_{Σ_2} and ρ extends to a continuous morphism of pseudogroups
> ~ρ : ~G_1 → ~G_{Σ_2}|U.

## Theorem 11.12

> Theorem 11.12. Let ~G_{Σ_1} and ~G_{Σ_2} be groupoids associated to
> irreducible one-sided-shifts of finite type. Then non-trivial group
> homomorphisms ρ : D(G_{Σ_1}) → F(G_{Σ_2}) are in natural one-to-one
> correspondence with Σ_1-systems in ~G_{Σ_2}.
> This correspondence is explicitly described as follows: a Σ_1-system
> {T_e}_{e∈E_1} ⊂ ~G_{Σ_2} with domain U gives rise to a homomorphism
> ρ : D(G_{Σ_1}) → F(G_{Σ_2}|Z) given by
> ρ(g) = ⨆_{i=1}^n T_{v_i}^{-1} T_{w_i}
> where {(v_i,w_i)}_{i=1}^n is a table which defines g, and for every finite
> path w = e_1 · · · e_k we set T_w = T_{e_k} · · · T_{e_1}. In particular, the
> ρ-action of D(G_{Σ_1}) on Z factors onto X_{Σ_1} via the coding map
> q : Z → X_{Σ_1} associated to {T_e}_{e∈E}.

## Lemma 11.8 and the idempotent identity (proof of Proposition 11.6)

> Lemma 11.8. For every path w we have s(T_w) = q^{-1}(C_w), and
> r(T_w) = q^{-1}(D_{t(w)}).

A line of the same proof uses `T_e^{-1} T_e = s(T_e)`:

> ⨆_{e∈t(v)} T_{ve}^{-1} T_{we} = ⨆_{e∈t(v)} T_v^{-1} T_e^{-1} T_e T_w =
> T_v^{-1} ( ⨆_{e∈t(v)} s(T_e) ) T_w

## Equivariance of the spatial component (Proposition 4.9)

> For every F ∈ ~G with U = s(F) and V = r(F) we have s(ϕ(F)) = q^{-1}(U) and
> r(ϕ(F)) = q^{-1}(V), and the homeomorphisms τ(F), τ(ϕ(F)) verify
> τ(F) ◦ q = q ◦ (τ(ϕ(F)).

Proposition 11.6 says (quote) "the spatial component q : Z → X_Σ of ϕ
coincides with the coding map of {T_e}_{e∈E}".

## How the claim's items follow

**Item 1.** Theorem 11.11 applies with `G_1 = G_{Ω_2}` and `A(G_1) = V`.
- `A(G)` is "the subgroup of F(G) generated by 3-cycles" (Section 1). It is
  normal, and it is non-trivial because `V` contains 3-cycles, for example
  `ac` of the census.
- `V` is simple, as Example 11.13 recalls, so `A(G_{Ω_2}) = V`.
- `F(G_{Σ_2}|U)` fixes every point off `U`.

**Items 2 and 3.**
- Items 2 and 3 are Theorem 11.12 with `Σ_1 = Ω_2`, `Σ_2 = Ω_n`, and
  `D(G_{Ω_2}) = V` (Example 11.13).
- The domain formula is Lemma 11.8. That lemma is stated for the coding map of
  the system. Proposition 11.6 identifies it with the spatial component,
  which is the map of Theorem 11.12.

**The consequence.** A table of `g` with the pair `(p, p)` gives the piece
`T_p^{-1} T_p = s(T_p) = q^{-1}(C_p)` of `rho(g)`, which is an identity map.
