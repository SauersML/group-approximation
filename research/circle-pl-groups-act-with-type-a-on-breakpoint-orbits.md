**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, not reviewed; no novelty claimed. The mechanism is the classical one for Thompson's `T` on dyadic points.)

## Setting

Let `P ≤ R_{>0}` be a subgroup and `A ⊆ R` a `Z[P]`-submodule. `P` must be nontrivial, so `A` is dense. Let `l ∈ A` with `l > 0`, and write `X = A/lZ ⊆ S^1 = R/lZ`.
- `T(l;A,P)` is the group of orientation-preserving PL homeomorphisms of `S^1` with finitely many breakpoints, all in `X`, slopes in `P`, and mapping `X` onto `X`.
- `G([0,l];A,P)` is the Bieri–Strebel group of PL homeomorphisms of `[0,l]` with breakpoints in `A` and slopes in `P`.
- `IP·A` is the subgroup generated by the `(p−1)a`, for `p ∈ P` and `a ∈ A`.

## Theorem

Assume:
- (H1) `T(l;A,P)` is finitely presented;
- (H2) `G([0,l];A,P)` is finitely generated;
- (H3) `A/(IP·A)` is finite, of order `d`.

Then the action of `T(l;A,P)` on `X` is of type (A):
- it is faithful;
- the group is finitely presented;
- point stabilizers are finitely generated;
- there are exactly `d` orbits of ordered pairs of distinct points, hence at most `d` orbits of two-element subsets.

When `d = 1` the action is 2-transitive. Consequently every subgroup of `T(l;A,P)`, in particular every simple subgroup, has solvable word problem, satisfies permutational Boone–Higman, and embeds in the finitely presented simple twisted Brin–Thompson group `SV_{T(l;A,P)}` (Zaremsky's Corollary B, as imported in `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`). So BFFHZ Question 3.4 holds for every finitely presented simple subgroup, by `bh-implies-pbh-iff-fp-simple-groups-satisfy-pbh`.

## Proof

- **Faithful.** `X` is dense in `S^1`.
- **Transitive on `X`.** The rotation by `a ∈ A` has slope `1 ∈ P`, has no breakpoints, and preserves `X`, so it lies in `T(l;A,P)`.
- **Stabilizer of `0`.** Cutting the circle at the fixed point `0` identifies it with `G([0,l];A,P)`, which is finitely generated by (H2). Every point stabilizer is conjugate to it.
- **Pair orbits.** Every ordered pair `(x,y)` with `x ≠ y` can be rotated to `(0, y')` with `y' ∈ A ∩ (0,l)`. So the pair orbits are in bijection with the orbits of `G([0,l];A,P)` on `A ∩ (0,l)`.
  - By Bieri–Strebel Corollary A1, these orbits are the sets `(IP·A + a) ∩ (0,l)`.
  - `IP·A` is dense in `R`, as used in the proof of their Theorem B7, so every coset meets `(0,l)`.
  - Hence there are exactly `d` orbits. ∎

## Instances

1. **Thompson and Higman–Thompson circle groups.** Take `A = Z[1/n]`, `P = ⟨n⟩`, and `l = r ∈ N`. Then `IP·A = (n−1)A` and `d = n−1`. For `n = 2` this recovers Thompson's `T` acting 2-transitively on dyadic points, with stabilizer `F`. These are calibration cases, already known to satisfy PBH.
2. **Stein's groups.** Take `A = Z[1/(n_1⋯n_k)]` and `P = ⟨n_1, …, n_k⟩`. Then `IP·A = gcd(n_i − 1)·A`, and `d = gcd(n_1−1, …, n_k−1)`; for `⟨2,3⟩` this is `d = 1`.
   - (H1) and (H2) are Stein's finiteness theorem (Trans. AMS 332, 1992): the `F`- and `T`-versions are `F_∞`. It is cited, **not read at source** here.
   - So every simple subgroup of Stein's circle groups `T(l; Z[1/m], P)` satisfies PBH. That includes their simple derived subgroups, whatever their index.
3. **The golden-ratio groups (Cleary; Burillo–Nucinkis–Reeves).** Take `A = Z[τ]` with `τ = (√5−1)/2` and `P = ⟨τ⟩`.
   - `τ − 1 = −τ²` is a unit, so `IP·A = A` and `d = 1`: the action is 2-transitive.
   - (H2): `F_τ = G([0,1];Z[τ],⟨τ⟩)` is `F_∞` by Cleary (`irrational-slope-f-tau-f-infinity-citation`, quoting arXiv:1806.00108 l.137).
   - (H1): BNR, arXiv:2006.02401 l.101, state that `T_τ` is `F_∞` "by directly applying the methods of Stein". That is their assertion, not re-derived.
   - BNR prove the index-2 subgroup `T_{xz}` simple (Thm. at l.319). So `T_{xz}`, a finitely presented simple group, satisfies PBH, and it acts with type (A) itself on `X`, with at most two orbits of pairs.

## Scope and what is not claimed

- **Sources.** The Bieri–Strebel notes (arXiv:1411.2868, `chaptA_Construction.tex` l.462–470) and both BNR TeX files were read on MSI. The finiteness inputs (Stein; the `T_τ` part of BNR l.101) are cited, not verified.
- **`V`-versions not treated.** The groups `V_τ`, `V_{xz}` and Stein's `V`-groups are not covered. A point stabilizer in a `V`-type group is not an interval group, and its finite generation needs a separate argument.
- **Beyond (H2).** For general `(A,P)` the finite generation of `G([0,l];A,P)` for compact intervals is subtle (Bieri–Strebel Chapter B). The theorem is stated under (H2) and does not decide it.
