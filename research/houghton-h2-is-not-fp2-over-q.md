**ESTABLISHED** by the elementary proof in `houghton-h2-not-fp2-over-q-proof`
(not independently reviewed).

Let `H = FSym(Z) x| <t>`, the group of permutations of `Z` generated by the
translation `t(x) = x + 1` and the transposition `(0 1)`. Here `FSym(Z)` is
the group of finitary permutations of `Z`, and it is normal with quotient
generated by `t`. This is Houghton's group `H_2`.

**Theorem.** `H` is not of type `FP_2` over `Q`.

Over `Z`, `H_2` is classically known not to be finitely presented, and Brown's
work on Houghton groups gives more. That was not re-read here. The proof here
is self-contained and works over `Q`, which is what
`cycle-transposition-lef-group-is-not-finitely-presented` needs: over `Q`,
locally finite kernels are invisible
(`fp2-over-q-descends-along-locally-finite-kernels`).

**What the proof actually shows.** The rational homology `H_1(H; prod_N Q[H])`
is nonzero, while the product of the `H_1(H; Q[H])` is zero. The nonzero class
comes from averaging idempotents of disjoint transpositions that the shift
carries further and further apart. The same argument applies to any group
`K x| <t>` with `K` locally finite that contains a nontrivial finite subgroup
whose shifts eventually have disjoint support from it and from any given
finite subgroup, such as the lamplighter groups `F x| Z` with `F` finite and
`F != 1`.
