## Why sufficient

Suppose, for a contradiction, that `SL_4(Z)` is point-norm matricially stable.

1. **Weak matricial stability.** `dadarlat-matricial-stability-obstruction` §1 records that
   matricial stability implies weak matricial stability. So `SL_4(Z)` is weakly matricially
   stable.
2. **Finitely generated and linear.** `SL_4(Z) ⊂ GL_4(Q)`. Since `Z` is Euclidean, row
   reduction gives `SL_4(Z) = E_4(Z)`, generated by the twelve elementary matrices `x_{ij}(1)`.
3. **Torsion element with a nonzero even class.** `g = [[0, I], [I, 0]]` has order 2, and
   `H^2(Z_{SL_4(Z)}(g); Q) ≅ Q` (`sl4z-involution-centralizers-have-rational-h2`, part 2).
4. **Contradiction.** By steps 1–2, `weak-matricial-stability-kills-centralizer-even-cohomology`
   applies with `G = SL_4(Z)`, `K = Q`, this `g` and `k = 1`, and gives
   `H^2(Z_{SL_4(Z)}(g); Q) = 0`. This contradicts step 3.

So `SL_4(Z)` is not point-norm matricially stable.

## Remark

Dadarlat's own theorems are silent here: `H^{2k}(SL_4(Z); Q) = 0` for `k ≥ 1` (recalled,
Lee–Szczarba). The class lives in a torsion sector. By
`sl4z-delocalized-bott-lives-in-odd-central-sector`, any realization of the needed
statement for this group must put it on the part where `−I` acts by `−1`.
