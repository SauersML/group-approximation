**ESTABLISHED** (written proof, unreviewed) by [[commutator-root-frontier-kills-pl-and-bi-ordered-targets-proof]].
Item 1 is a computation with a built-in back-substitution check. Item 2 is a strict reduction. Items 3 to 5 are
class-killing obstructions. Item 6 records two bounded searches that found nothing. Nothing is new for the
zero-divisor conjecture.

**Setting.** As in [[malcev-root-steps-put-commutator-root-one-relators-in-h]]:
- `𝓗` is the class of [[vertex-injective-quotients-give-hughes-free-division-rings]]. It is closed under
  subgroups, extensions and directed unions. It contains free, bi-orderable, locally indicable amenable,
  residually torsion-free nilpotent and free-by-cyclic groups. Every group in it is locally indicable.
- `𝒮` is the class of groups with a finite normal series with torsion-free abelian factors (item 3 there).
- **The 26 words** are the open frontier through length 15 left after rule S
  ([[pro-unipotent-magnus-steps-put-one-relators-in-h]]): the 18 length-14 words with `e = (0, ±2, 0)` and the 8
  length-15 words with `e = (0, -3, 0)` (`open-26.txt`).
- Conventions: `y_i = t^{-i} y t^i` and `[g, h] = g^{-1} h^{-1} g h`.

## Theorem

1. **Six groups** (`commutator_normal_form.py`, output `normal-form-26.txt`). Each of the 26 words is carried by
   an automorphism of `F_2` (a Nielsen basis `{t^{±1}, t^i y^{±1} t^j}` of length at most 3) to the relator

   `y_1^K = [y_0^α, y_2^β]`, with `K > 0` and `α, β = ±1`.

   So the 26 groups are, up to isomorphism, among the six groups `G(K; α, β) = <y, t | y_1^K = [y_0^α, y_2^β]>`.
   Reversing `t` identifies `(α, β)` with `(-β, -α)`, and the tally is:

   | `(α, β)` | `K = 2` (length 14) | `K = 3` (length 15) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `(1, 1)` | 8 | 4 |
   | `(1, -1)` | 4 | 2 |
   | `(-1, 1)` | 6 | 2 |

   Each rewrite is checked by expanding the normal form back and comparing it with a rotation of the substituted
   relator or its inverse, up to conjugation by a power of `t`.
2. **One map suffices (strict reduction).** Let `N` be the normal closure of `y`, so `G = N ⋊ <t>`. Then
   `G(K; α, β) ∈ 𝓗` if and only if some `Q ∈ 𝓗` receives a homomorphism `φ: G → Q` with `φ(y) ≠ 1`.
   - The segments `N_[-m, n] = <y_{-m}, …, y_n>` form a tower of cyclic HNN extensions over the free group
     `<y_0, y_1>`, with stable letters `y_{n+1}` (edge `<y_{n-1}^α> → <y_{n-1}^α y_n^K>`) and `y_{-m-1}`.
   - The edge groups are generated by conjugates of the `y_i`, so one map that keeps `y` is injective on every
     edge of every step, and item 1 of [[edge-injective-quotients-give-hughes-free-division-rings]] climbs the
     whole tower at once. Conversely, the identity map of `G` keeps `y`.
   - So the question for the 26 words is no longer one target per step, of growing complexity, but one map.
3. **PL obstruction.** Let `Q` be a group of orientation-preserving piecewise-linear homeomorphisms, with finitely
   many breakpoints, of a real interval (compact, half-line or `R`), and let `α, β, K` be nonzero integers with
   `K ∉ {α, -β}`. Then every homomorphism `G(K; α, β) → Q` kills `y`.
   - This covers all 26 words, since `K ≥ 2` and `α, β = ±1`.
   - It kills Thompson's group `F`, `PL_+([0, 1])`, `PL_+(R)`, the Stein–Thompson groups and all their subgroups.
     These are bi-orderable, so in `𝓗`, and `F` is not residually solvable. So they are exactly the kind of
     target that item 3 of the Mal'cev node leaves.
   - The mechanism: the right slope `λ ≠ 1` of `y` at the left end of its support is preserved by conjugation.
     The left ends `p_n` of the supports of `y_n` move monotonically, and the relation then forces `K = 0`,
     `K = α` or `K = -β`, according as `p_n` is constant, decreasing or increasing.
4. **Bi-order obstruction.** Let `Q` be bi-orderable and `φ: G(K; α, β) → Q` with `φ(y) ≠ 1`. The smallest convex
   subgroups `v_n` containing `φ(y_n)` then form a strictly monotone chain, and:
   - if it increases (`v_0 ⊊ v_1`), then `sign K = sign α`;
   - if it decreases (`v_0 ⊋ v_1`), then `sign K = -sign β`.

   So `G(K; -1, 1)` has no bi-orderable target that keeps `y`. That covers 8 of the 26 words (6 of length 14,
   2 of length 15). For `(1, 1)` a bi-ordered target must have increasing classes, and for `(1, -1)` either
   direction is allowed.
5. **Fibre obstruction.** `N^ab = (Z/K)[t^{±1}]` is torsion. So `φ(N)` lies in the kernel of every homomorphism
   from `Q` to a torsion-free abelian group. If `φ(N)` lies in a subgroup of `Q` all of whose finitely generated
   subgroups are residually 𝒮, then `φ(y) = 1`. This uses item 3 of the Mal'cev node, whose hypotheses all 26 words
   satisfy.
   - This kills, besides residually-𝒮 groups, every Baumslag–Solitar group `BS(m, n)`: the kernel of its
     `t`-exponent map is a directed union of finite chains of infinite cyclic groups, each free-by-`Z`, hence
     residually 𝒮.
   - It does not cover the Baumslag–Gersten groups `G(1, n)`. The kernel of `G(1, n) → Z` contains a nontrivial
     forward Baumslag–Gersten chain, so it is not residually solvable
     ([[bg-shift-chains-die-in-residually-linear-alphabets]]). Whether its finitely generated subgroups are
     residually 𝒮 is not settled here.
6. **Searches, all negative.**
   - A Nielsen search, depth 8 with slack 10 (`nielsen_deep.py`, `nd810.out`), finds no image of any of the 26
     that the landed rules, rule R or rule N certify.
   - A search for maps to `G(1, n)` with `a, b` sent to words of length at most 4 and nontrivial on the Magnus
     kernel (`bg_search2.py`, exact word problem by Britton's lemma; `bg2-4.out`) finds none for `n = 2, -1, -2` (the runs for `n = 3, 4, -3` hit the time cap).
   - Neither search is an obstruction.

## Where it stops

- Whether `G(K; α, β) ∈ 𝓗` is open for all six groups. By items 2 to 5, a witness is one map `φ` keeping `y` into
  a group of `𝓗` that satisfies all of the following:
  - it is not a PL group of an interval;
  - it is not bi-orderable when `(α, β) = (-1, 1)`;
  - the image of `N` is not locally residually 𝒮, and it lies in the kernel of every map from the target to a
    torsion-free abelian group.
- The remaining named candidates are the Baumslag–Gersten groups `G(1, n)`, their elementary amenable quotients
  that keep `a`, and bi-orderings of `G(K; 1, ±1)` itself, which would put it in `𝓗` directly and must have
  strictly increasing classes when `(α, β) = (1, 1)`.
- **Next step.** Decide the bi-orderability of `G(2; 1, 1)`, the largest class (8 words), using the forced
  increasing chain `v_0 ⊊ v_1 ⊊ …` of item 4. On each convex jump the relation reads `y_1^2 ≡ y_2^{-1} y_0 y_2`,
  which describes the leading terms of a candidate order. Either build the order from that recursion, or show the
  recursion is inconsistent. Inconsistency would extend item 4 to 12 more words.
