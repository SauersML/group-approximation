Let `X` be a countable set with a finitely presented group `G` acting so that
there are finitely many orbits on `X^k` for every fixed `k`, and stabilizers
of finite tuples are finitely generated.  Thompson's action on dyadic points
and the standard Houghton actions are concrete choices.  Put

```text
W=F_2^(X),
L=SL_fin(W) semidirect G.                              (OLA1)
```

Here `SL_fin(W)` is generated by elementary transvections `e_(xy)`, for
distinct `x,y in X`.  Its Steinberg presentation has only the bounded-index
relations

```text
e_(xy)^2=1,
[e_(xy),e_(yz)]=e_(xz),
[e_(xy),e_(zw)]=1                                    (OLA2)
```

in the usual noninteracting-index cases.  The `G`-action has finitely many
orbits on all index configurations occurring in `(OLA2)`.  Choose one
transvection generator per orbit, impose the finitely many stabilizer
relations, and one relator per orbit of `(OLA2)`.  The standard
orbit-compression presentation proves

```text
L is finitely presented.                              (OLA3)
```

Over `F_2`, stable `K_2(F_2)=0`, so the stable Steinberg and finitary special
linear groups agree; no hidden central kernel changes the action on `W`.

The normal subgroup `SL_fin(W)` is transitive on finite tuples with a fixed
linear-relation kernel.  Hence its orbits on `W^k` are classified by subspaces
of `F_2^k`, of which there are finitely many.  For the two-sorted space

```text
W disjoint_union W^circ,
```

where `W^circ` is the finite-support restricted dual, tuple orbits are
classified by the two linear-relation kernels and the finite evaluation
matrix `(phi_j(v_i))`.  Consequently every bounded-arity BLR/Pauli incidence

```text
u+v=w,        phi+psi=chi,        phi(v)=epsilon       (OLA4)
```

is a finite union of `L`-orbits.  This removes the unbounded linear-code
incidence obstruction from the occurrence compiler.

There is also an explicit finitely presented Pauli extension.  Let `P(W)` be
generated by

```text
J, X_v (v in W), Z_phi (phi in W^circ)
```

with

```text
J^2=1, J central,
X_v X_w=X_(v+w),
Z_phi Z_psi=Z_(phi+psi),
[X_v,Z_phi]=J^(phi(v)).                               (OLA5)
```

All relation families in `(OLA5)` have bounded arity and finitely many
`L`-orbits.  The stabilizers of one nonzero vector or covector are generated
by finitely many parabolic transvection orbits together with the corresponding
tuple stabilizer in `G`.  The same orbit-compression argument therefore gives

```text
Gamma_lin=P(W) semidirect L  is finitely presented.   (OLA6)
```

On `ell^2(W)`, let `X_v` translate basis vectors, let `Z_phi` multiply by
`(-1)^(phi(w))`, and let `L` permute the basis through its natural action.
This is an exact representation with

```text
J=-I.                                                 (OLA7)
```

Thus `J!=1`.  For every `N`, choosing dual families
`v_1,...,v_N` and `phi_1,...,phi_N` embeds the extraspecial Pauli group `E_N`
with the same central `J`.  Every exact finite-dimensional unitary
representation consequently kills `J`, since a nonzero `J=-1` sector would
have dimension divisible by `2^N` for every `N`.

This is a concrete finitely presented group with a nontrivial word invisible
to every exact finite-dimensional unitary representation, plus an
oligomorphic addressing action adapted to linear-code tests.

## Quantitative firewall

The theorem is not a nonhyperlinearity proof.  With an amenable Houghton
choice of `G`, the successive kernels in `(OLA1)` and `(OLA6)` are locally
finite/amenable and the quotient is amenable; `Gamma_lin` is therefore
amenable and hyperlinear.  Its canonical microstates keep `J` alive while no
exact finite-dimensional representation does.

Hence finite presentation, finite orbit types, exact infinite Pauli capacity,
and exact finite-dimensional invisibility still do not provide the required
normalized-HS floor.  The remaining ingredient is genuinely quantitative:
an occurrence-consistency/PCP mechanism must prevent the microstate from
changing coordinate gauges between orbit representatives.  This example is
a reusable exact countermodel to any claim that finite-orbit code incidence
alone solves that problem.
