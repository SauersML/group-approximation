**Setting.** `T̄` is the group of homeomorphisms `g` of `R` that commute with `z(x) = x + 1`, are
piecewise linear with finitely many breakpoints mod 1, all in `Z[1/2]`, have slopes in `2^Z`, and
map `Z[1/2]` onto itself. Let `p : T̄ → T` be the projection, so `ker p = ⟨z⟩`. Write `t_k` for the
translation `x ↦ x + 2^{-k}` of `R`, which lies in `T̄`, and `r_k = p(t_k)` for the rotation by
`2^{-k}`.

**Statement.** For each `k ≥ 0`, the map

`ι_k : T̄ → T`, `ι_k(g) = p(λ^k(g))`, where `λ^k(g)(x) = 2^{-k} g(2^k x)`,

is a homomorphism onto the centralizer `C_T(r_k)`. Its kernel is `⟨z^{2^k}⟩` and it sends
`z ↦ r_k`. Hence

`T̄ / ⟨z^{2^k}⟩ ≅ C_T(r_k)`.

**Descriptions.**
- `ι_k(t_j) = r_{j+k}`.
- If `f̃ ∈ T̄` fixes `Z` pointwise, so that it lifts some `f ∈ F`, then `ι_k(f̃)` is the
  `2^{-k}`-periodic copy of `f`. It acts on each arc `[j 2^{-k}, (j+1) 2^{-k}]` as `f` rescaled.

**Consequences.**
- Every representation of `T̄` in which `z^{2^k}` acts trivially is a representation of a
  subgroup of `T`.
- `⋂_k ⟨z^{2^k}⟩ = 1`, so `g ↦ (ι_k(g))_k` embeds `T̄` in the unrestricted product `T^N`. (This
  alone says nothing about the Haagerup property. Every residually finite group embeds in a product
  of finite groups.)
