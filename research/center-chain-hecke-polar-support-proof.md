Write

```text
u=x_47(a_1),  v=x_57(a_1),  w=x_67(a_1),
r=x_58(a_2),  s=x_68(a_2).
```

The five displayed root groups commute and have order two.  Their product
`V=<u,v,w,r,s>` is `F_2^5`: Steinberg collection gives at most one binary
normal form, while their elementary matrices have five independent root
coordinates.  Conjugation by `H` preserves `V`.  The only orbit differences
of `u` are `v,w`, and the only additional orbit difference of `ur` is `s`.
The three coordinates are independently attained by the `54,64,65`
coordinates of `H`.  Therefore

```text
H.u=uW_1,       W_1=<v,w>,
H.(ur)=urW_2,   W_2=<v,w,s>.                           (CSP1)
```

This also proves that `W_1,W_2` are `H`-invariant and that `W_1<=W_2`.
The group generated by `H` and `V` has the collected form `VH`; the
elementary image separates all such forms, so no hidden Steinberg-kernel
identification affects the following finite calculation.

For any element `g` whose `H`-orbit is finite, expand the real character
atom

```text
e=|H|^(-1)sum_(h in H)chi(h)h.
```

Since `eh=chi(h)e` and `gh=h(h^(-1)gh)`, the two character factors cancel:

```text
ege=|H|^(-1)sum_(h in H)e(h^(-1)gh)
    =e |H.g|^(-1)sum_(y in H.g)y.                     (CSP2)
```

Apply `(CSP2)` to the two affine orbits in `(CSP1)`.  If
`z_W=|W|^(-1)sum_(x in W)x`, their orbit averages are `uz_1` and `urz_2`.
They are `H`-invariant, hence commute with `e`.  Because `V` is elementary
abelian,

```text
(uz_1)^2=z_1,                 (urz_2)^2=z_2.           (CSP3)
```

Equations `(CSP2)--(CSP3)` prove `(CHS4)--(CHS5)`.  Also
`z_1z_2=z_2`.  The supports of `e` and `z_i` lie in subgroups with trivial
intersection, so the identity coefficient gives

```text
tau(ez_i)=1/(|H||W_i|),                                (CSP4)
```

which is `(CHS6)`.  Since `T_i=T_i^*` and `T_i^2=p_i`, the projections
`(p_i+/-T_i)/2` are its `+/-1` spectral projections.  The group-basis
coefficient of the identity in `T_i` is zero, so the two traces are equal.
This proves the full asserted singular and signed spectra.

It remains to verify reduction and the cross-Gram.  A general elementary
observation applies here: if `T=eae` is self-adjoint, `T^2=p` is a
projection, and `p<=e`, then

```text
||(1-e)ap||_2^2=tau(p)-tau(paea p)=tau(p)-tau(T^2)=0.
```

Faithfulness gives `ap=pa=T`.  Thus `p_1` reduces `A_1=u`, and `p_2`
reduces `A_2A_1=ru`.  The `H`-invariant averages `uz_1,urz_2` commute in
`C[V]`, so `T_1` commutes with `p_2`.  Since `p_2<=p_1`, it follows that
`p_2` reduces `A_1`; combining this with reduction of `A_2A_1` shows that it
also reduces `A_2`.

For `B_1=x_72(b_1)`, conjugation fixes `H` pointwise and sends

```text
v |-> v x_52(q),       w |-> w x_62(q),       s |-> s. (CSP5)
```

Both added roots are derived roots of `H`, on which `chi=+1`.  Averaging
`(CSP5)` over `W_2` therefore gives

```text
B_1(ez_2)B_1=ez_2.                                    (CSP6)
```

So `B_1` also reduces `p_2`.  The simple root `x_42(q)` acts on `e`, hence
on `p_2`, by `-1`, proving the Pauli assertion `(CHS7)`.

Finally, commutativity in `V` and `z_1z_2=z_2` give

```text
T_1^*T_2=e(uz_1)(urz_2)=er z_2=A_2p_2.                (CSP7)
```

This operator squares to `p_2`.  The roots `A_2` and `A_1` commute, and
`A_2` also commutes with `B_1`; all three reduce `p_2`.  Thus `(CSP7)` has
the two zero Pauli commutators in `(CHS9)`.  Its squared norm is
`tau(p_2)=tau(e)/8`, completing the proof.
