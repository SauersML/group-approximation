**ESTABLISHED** by `bijective-designs-force-presentations-proof`.

**Theorem.** For every finite presentation `P` there is a design
`D = (F_2^T, S, M, nu, mu)` such that:
- its forward-sufficient partitions form a principal up-set generated by one
  partition `E`;
- the canonical table group `U_E` is isomorphic to `P`, with one independent cycle of
  the decoder difference graph per relator;
- over every group, and for every realization, the encoder is bijective.

**The design.**
- **Tracks.** One data track, plus two key tracks `a_r`, `b_r` for each relator
  `u_r v_r^-1 = w_r z_r^-1` of a holonomy-shape presentation. Every finite
  presentation has one.
- **Encoder.** It translates `a_r` by `v_r` and `b_r` by `z_r`. It adds
  `sum_r x_(a_r)(g u_r) x_(b_r)(g w_r)` to the data track.
- **Decoder.** It reads both key tracks of relator `r` at the single address
  `h_r = u_r v_r^-1`, and that shared address is what forces the relator.

The encoder is a key-track translation followed by a shear, hence invertible over any
group.

**Consequences.**
1. Forcing a nonsofic or Kazhdan core, with any number of independent cycles, never
   yields strictness. Examples are the nine-leaf compression configuration with its
   nesting relators, or any finitely presented nonsofic group.
2. The recorded death point of `defect-window-automaton-over-leavitt-units`, "no rule
   pair is known whose forward sufficiency needs a Kazhdan presentation", is resolved
   by bijective rule pairs. The missing ingredient is a reverse hinge that survives in
   the forced core, not the forcing itself.
3. Composing with bijective gadgets enlarges a forced core arbitrarily and leaves
   strictness unchanged over every group. So the decisive coincidences of a
   counterexample must come from the irreversible part of its rules.

Proof and consequences: Sections 1 to 5 of the artifact. Section 6 lists open design
directions (left–right transposition, linear rules), not results.
