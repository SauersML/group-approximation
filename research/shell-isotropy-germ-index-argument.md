The imported claim supplies the model used here:
- `rho_g` transfers the left regular action of `P` to the even
  integers and fixes every odd integer;
- `hat(g)(0^n 1 z) = 0^(rho_g(n)) 1 z`;
- `Q` is generated by the germs of `rho(P)` and the cofinite shift
  `tau: n -> n+1`, and `(V)_p = <tau>`;
- every nonidentity `g` in `P` has nonidentity germ.

**Index.** A near permutation of `N` is a bijection `sigma` between
cofinite subsets. Taken up to agreement on a cofinite set, these form
the near permutation group. Put
`ind(sigma) = |N - dom(sigma)| - |N - im(sigma)|`.
- It is well defined on germs: shrinking the domain by one point also
  shrinks the image by one point.
- It is additive under composition, so it is a homomorphism to `Z`.
- It is invariant under conjugation.
- `ind(tau) = 0 - 1 = -1`.

**1. Normality fails.** Let `g != 1` in `P`, and suppose
`rho_g tau rho_g^-1 = tau^j` as germs for some integer `j`. Applying
`ind` gives `-j = -1`, so `j = 1`, and `rho_g` commutes with `tau` near
infinity: `rho_g(n+1) = rho_g(n) + 1` for all large `n`. Every odd `n`
is fixed by `rho_g`, so for large odd `n` we get `rho_g(n+1) = n+1`.
Hence `rho_g` fixes all large even numbers as well, so it agrees with
the identity near infinity and its germ is trivial. That contradicts
germ faithfulness. So no germ of `rho(P - {1})` normalizes `<tau>`.
Those germs lie in `Q`, and `P` is infinite, so `<tau>` is not normal
in `Q`.

**2. Infinite index.** The map `g -> germ(rho_g)` is a homomorphism
`P -> Q`, and by germ faithfulness it is injective. If `<tau>` had finite
index in `Q`, then `P ∩ <tau>` would have finite index in the image of
`P`. It would also be cyclic, as a subgroup of `<tau>`, so `P` would be
virtually cyclic.

**Consequence.** In Belk–Hyde–Matucci's notation `(G)_p = Q` and
`(B)_p = (V)_p = <tau>`. By 1 and 2, neither alternative of hypothesis
(3) of their Corollary 2.10 holds at `p` when `P` is not virtually
cyclic. The corollary quantifies over every singular point, so its
hypothesis fails.
