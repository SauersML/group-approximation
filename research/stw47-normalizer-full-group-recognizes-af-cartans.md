Let `A` be a separable unital C-star algebra and let `D subset A` be a
C-star diagonal with Cantor spectrum.  Write

```text
N_U(A,D)={u in U(A):uDu*=D},
F(A,D)=N_U(A,D)/U(D).
```

Then `D` is an AF Cartan subalgebra of `A` if and only if the abstract
countable group `F(A,D)` is locally finite: every finitely generated subgroup
of `F(A,D)` is finite.  This is also equivalent to the following local
C-star-algebraic condition: for every finite tuple
`u_1,...,u_k in N_U(A,D)`, the intermediate algebra

```text
C*(D,u_1,...,u_k)
```

is subhomogeneous (the degree bound may depend on the tuple).

Equivalently, `D` fails to be an AF Cartan exactly when there is one finite
packet of normalizing unitaries whose words induce infinitely many distinct
homeomorphisms of the Cantor spectrum; equivalently, the intermediate algebra
generated by this packet and `D` is not subhomogeneous.  Thus any
counterexample to STW Problem XLVII must already contain such a finite
normalizer packet.

No simplicity, nuclearity, amenability, minimality, real-rank, stable-rank, or
ambient AF hypothesis is used in the criterion.  The C-star-diagonal
(unique-extension) hypothesis is essential: it makes the Weyl groupoid a
principal etale equivalence relation, which is the setting in which local
finiteness of the full group detects AF-ness.
