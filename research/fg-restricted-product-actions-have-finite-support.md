## Statement

Let `(Ω_i, o_i)_(i∈I)` be pointed sets and `Ω = ∏'_i (Ω_i, o_i)` their restricted
product: families `x` with `x_i = o_i` for almost all `i`. A bijection `h` of `Ω` is
**monomial** if there are a permutation `σ_h` of `I` and bijections
`h_i : Ω_i -> Ω_(σ_h(i))` with `(h x)_(σ_h(i)) = h_i(x_i)`. It is **coordinatewise**
if `σ_h = id`. For `h` monomial put `D(h) = {j ∈ I : (h o)_j ≠ o_j}`, a finite set.

Let `H` be a group of monomial bijections generated by a finite set `F`, and let
`s = max_(f∈F) |D(f)|` and `S = ⋃_(f∈F) (D(f) ∪ D(f^-1))`.

1. **Word length.** `|D(h)| <= s |h|_F` for all `h ∈ H`, and
   `D(h) ⊆ σ(H) · S`, the union of the `σ(H)`-orbits of the finite set `S`.
2. **Coordinatewise case.** If `H` acts coordinatewise, then every element of `H`
   fixes `o_i` for every `i ∉ S`. So every subgroup of `H` fixes these base
   coordinates, and `H` preserves the finite product
   `Ω_S = ∏_(i∈S) Ω_i × {(o_i)_(i∉S)}`. For restricted products of graphs or
   complexes acting by automorphisms, `Ω_S` is a convex subcomplex.
3. **Restricted product groups.** Every finitely generated subgroup of a restricted
   product of groups `∏'_i (G_i, K_i)` lies in `∏_(i∈S) G_i × ∏_(i∉S) K_i` for a
   finite `S`. For example, every finitely generated subgroup of `SL_n(A_f)` lies
   in `SL_n(Q_S) × ∏_(p∉S) SL_n(Z_p)`.

## Consequences for the root

- **The natural adelic configurations have no finitely generated coordinatewise
  host.** Let `Γ` be `SL_n(Q)`, `GL_n(Q)` (`n >= 2`) or `Aff(Q)`, acting diagonally
  on the restricted product of its Bruhat–Tits buildings (vertices), `∏'_p T_p` for
  `n = 2`, or on any coordinatewise thickening `Ω_p ⊇ T_p^0` with the same base
  points. For every prime `p`, the root group moves `o_p`: `u(1/p)` does. So no
  finitely generated group `H` of coordinatewise bijections of such an `Ω`
  contains `Γ` acting in this way. Otherwise `u(1/p)` would fix `o_p` for all
  `p ∉ S`.
- **Automorphisms of the median graph.** Every automorphism of `∏'_p T_p` is
  coordinatewise (`adelic-median-graph-automorphisms-are-restricted-products`,
  item 1, lane gq-sl2q). So part 2 recovers its item 4: no finitely generated
  subgroup of `Aut(∏'_p T_p)` contains the root group `U(Q)`. The point of the
  present node is that the same holds for every coordinatewise *thickening*,
  meaning any sets `Ω_p ⊇ T_p^0` and arbitrary bijections, not only graph
  automorphisms. That covers coordinatewise almost-automorphism and
  rigid-permutation hosts.
- **Monomial hosts must merge prime directions.** If a finitely generated
  monomial host extends the action, then by part 1 all prime factors lie in
  finitely many `σ(H)`-orbits of factors, and `|u(1/m!)|_F >= π(m)/s`. So the
  host must identify infinitely many prime directions with each other through
  its factor permutations. The prime-shift overgroups of root attempt 18
  (`gl-n-q-lies-in-prime-shift-permutation-group`) merge the primes in the same
  spirit, though not on this space: `σ` conjugates every prime dilation to
  `x -> 2x`. They are the surviving shape.
- **Permanence theorems.** Suppose a permanence theorem for complexes of groups
  over restricted products of trees (vertex groups `SL_2(Z)`, finite-index cube
  groups) builds its host as a finitely generated group acting coordinatewise on
  the vertex set, or on any coordinatewise thickening, extending the given
  action. BLIW-style rigid permutations of each factor, or finite products of
  per-prime hosts, are examples. Then it cannot apply to `SL_2(Q)`. A valid
  theorem must use a host that mixes infinitely many factors. By
  `sl-2-q-root-group-is-parabolic-in-every-prime-factor`, such a host also pays at
  least `2 Ω(m!)/C` word length for `u(1/m!)` whenever the action on the median
  graph survives isometrically.
