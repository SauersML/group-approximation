Write the central deck group of `E_6` additively as `Z/6`.  The subgroup
generated by `2` has order three, and quotienting by it leaves `Z/2`; hence

```text
1 -> <z^2> -> E_6 -> E_2 -> 1.
```

Deligne's finite-residual theorem identifies the intersection of the kernels
of all finite quotients of `E_6` with `2(Z/6)=<z^2>`.  The quotient `E_2` is
residually finite by `metaplectic-half-parameter-is-ce` and its proof.

By `maslov-ce-subgroup-classifies-finite-deligne-covers`,

```text
E_q hyperlinear  iff  1/q belongs to E_CE.              (M6P1)
```

The subgroup `E_CE` contains `1/2`.  Therefore

```text
1/3 in E_CE  =>  1/2-1/3=1/6 in E_CE,
1/6 in E_CE  =>  2(1/6)=1/3 in E_CE.                    (M6P2)
```

Equations `(M6P1)`--`(M6P2)` prove the group-level equivalence.

For the quantitative presentation statement, choose a finite-dimensional
exact projective representation `S_g` with the metaplectic half multiplier.
Such a representation exists because residual finiteness of `E_2` gives a
finite quotient detecting its central involution; a nontrivial central
spectral summand of the finite regular representation has the required scalar
central character.  If `U_g` has multiplier `1/3` and projective defect
`delta`, then

```text
conj(U_g tensor S_g)
```

has multiplier `-(1/3+1/2)=1/6` and the same defect, by tensor invariance of
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Conversely `V_g tensor V_g` has multiplier
`2(1/6)=1/3` and defect at most `2 delta` by the tensor telescoping inequality.
These fixed tensor operations preserve vanishing defect in both directions,
so a positive uniform gap exists in one sector exactly when it exists in the
other.
