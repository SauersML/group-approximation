OPEN.  Let `E = G_Q^(n) ≅ R ⋊_α F₀` be the Linton shell of
[[one-relator-soficity-localizes-at-rational-derived-stage]], with the splitting
obtained by lifting a free basis of `E/R`.  Is

    α : F₀ --> Aut(R)

a **sofic action** in the sense of Alekseev--Bradford (arXiv:2601.18742), the
hypothesis their semidirect-product theorem consumes?

With [[linton-radical-sofic]] this gives [[linton-shell-sofic]] and hence all of
[[one-relator-groups-sofic]]; that is the route
`linton-shell-sofic-via-sofic-action`.

## Why the automatic criterion does not apply

Alekseev--Bradford have a criterion that makes an action automatically sofic
when the normal group is **finitely generated and residually finite**.  A
nontrivial Linton radical in the torsion-free case is **infinitely generated**
(Linton), and residual finiteness of `R` is not available either — a residually
finite `R` would make `G` far more tractable than the unresolved case allows.
So the criterion misses precisely the regime this claim is about.  That is not
an accident of statement: it is the same gap that makes
[[linton-shell-sofic]] hard.

## Attempts

1. **Transfer soficity from free-group actions on graphs.**  *Dies on a notion
   mismatch, and this is the trap worth labelling.*  Recent results make all
   free-group actions on sets/graphs sofic in an action-on-a-set sense.  The
   semidirect-product theorem needs soficity of an action **by automorphisms of
   a group**, i.e. finite models compatible with the group structure of `R`, not
   merely with a `F₀`-set.  No implication between the two notions is known here,
   and the fact that `α` happens to be a free-group action supplies nothing on
   its own.  This is the same shape of error as reading a permanence theorem's
   hypothesis off a similarly named property.
2. **Build finite models from the one-relator origin.**  *The intended attack,
   not carried out.*  `R` is generated by `G`-conjugates of one word `r`, and
   `α` permutes those conjugates through `F₀ <= E/R`.  The hope is that the
   one-relator origin forces enough coherence on the orbit that finite models of
   `R` compatible with the conjugation action can be assembled even though `R`
   is infinitely generated.  This is step 3 of the artifact's proposed program.
   Nothing has been computed; the first honest test is an explicit `π(w) = 2`
   family with its Linton data `(r, k)` extracted.
3. **Amenable point stabilizers.**  *Unavailable.*  In the wreath setting this
   is the one known sufficient condition for a sofic action, and in
   [[sofic-action-permanence]] it is what fails for the Kun--Thom coordinate
   action (stabilizers are conjugates of a Kazhdan group).  Here it is not so
   much refuted as meaningless: `α` is an action by automorphisms with no
   distinguished point set, so the criterion has no hypothesis to check.
4. **Reality check, and why failure here is interesting.**  Because generic
   semidirect closure is now known to fail
   ([[sofic-semidirect-product-closure-fails]]), a demonstrated failure of this
   claim for a specific relator would not be a mere technical annoyance — it
   would be a candidate mechanism for [[nonsofic-one-relator-group]], and a new
   failure of semidirect closure with a **free** acting group, which nothing in
   the literature currently exhibits.  Both outcomes are worth having, which is
   the argument for spending effort here rather than on the radical alone.
