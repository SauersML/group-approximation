Proof: [[bernoulli-collapse-is-null-or-eventually-total-proof]], with full details in the artifact.

**Setting.** `G` is countably infinite, `s = h^Rok_sup(G)`, and `X_h` is the Bernoulli shift over `G` with base
entropy `h`, well defined by `bernoulli-shifts-with-equal-base-entropy-are-isomorphic`. `D(G)` and its witness
sets are as in `bernoulli-isomorphism-collapse-set-is-a-subgroup`. For `a, b ∈ (0, ∞)` write `a ~ b` when
`X_a ≅ X_b`. Put:
- `S(G) = {h > 0 : X_h ≅ X_h × X_h}`, the own-square levels;
- `R(G) = {a > 0 : X_a ≅ X_b for some b ≠ a}`, the non-rigid levels;
- `r(G) = inf R(G)`, with `inf ∅ = ∞`.

**Theorem.**
1. **Analyticity.** `~` is an analytic subset of `(0, ∞)^2`. So `D(G)`, `S(G)`, `R(G)`, every class and every
   witness set are Lebesgue measurable and have the Baire property.
2. **Dichotomy.** The following are equivalent:
   - `D(G) = R`;
   - `D(G)` is not Lebesgue-null, or equivalently not meager;
   - some isomorphism class of levels is not null, or not meager;
   - `S(G)` is not null, or not meager;
   - there is `M < ∞` with `X_h ≅ X_(h')` for all `h, h' ≥ M`.

   When they fail, `D(G)`, `S(G)` and every class are null and meager.
3. **Non-rigid levels.**
   - `R(G)` is upward closed and contained in `[s, ∞)`.
   - It is non-empty iff `D(G) ≠ {0}`.
   - Under the conditions of item 2, `R(G)` is a single isomorphism class, and every level below `r(G)` is rigid.
4. **Collapse above the supremum.** Let `s < ∞`. The following are equivalent:
   - **(T1)** all `X_h` with `h ∈ (s, ∞)` are isomorphic, which is
     `bernoulli-shifts-above-rokhlin-supremum-are-isomorphic` for `G`;
   - **(T2)** `D(G) = R` and `r(G) = s`;
   - **(T3)** for every `ε > 0`, `S(G) ∩ (s, s + ε)` is not null, or equivalently not meager;
   - **(T4)** there is `η > 0` with `X_h ≅ X_h × X_h` for all `h ∈ (s, s + η)`.
5. **Countable seeds.** Take countably many isomorphisms `X_(a_i) ≅ X_(b_i)`, and close under composition,
   inversion and multiplication by Bernoulli factors. Every level is then related only to levels in `a + Γ`, where
   `Γ` is the countable group generated by the `b_i − a_i`. So such constructions stay in the null case of
   item 2, and they give neither eventual collapse nor (T1).

**Reading.**
- Item 2 answers part of the structure question on `bernoulli-isomorphism-collapse-set-is-a-subgroup`. A proper
  `D(G)` is a null meager subgroup, and `D(G) = R` is eventual total collapse.
- Item 4 splits the open claim into two halves: `eventual-bernoulli-collapse-at-finite-rokhlin-supremum` and
  `bernoulli-rigidity-threshold-equals-rokhlin-supremum`.
- Neither half follows from the other by the product calculus. The artifact (Section 5) gives two formal models:
  - `a ≈ b` iff `a = b`, or `a, b > s` and `b − a` lies in a countable dense subgroup;
  - `a ≈ b` iff `a = b` or `a, b ≥ M`, with `M > s`.

  Both satisfy every property of Items 1–4 and 6 there, together with Seward's threshold. They are models of the
  calculus, not group actions.
- Nothing here produces an isomorphism over any group, or excludes a non-trivial null `D(G)`.
