The following statements are equivalent.

1. Every finitely generated group with solvable word problem embeds in a
   finitely presented simple group.
2. Every infinite finitely generated computably presented simple group embeds
   in a finitely presented simple group.

Consequently, if Boone--Higman fails, it has a counterexample that is itself
infinite, finitely generated, computably presented and simple. By Higman's
embedding theorem that counterexample is already a subgroup of a finitely
presented group; what fails is precisely the upgrade to a finitely presented
simple overgroup.
