**ESTABLISHED** through `finite-nilpotent-local-restricted-burnside-proof`.

**Statement.** Let `m, N >= 2`.
1. There is `R_nil = R_nil(m,N)` with the following property. Let `Q` be a
   finite nilpotent group generated by `s_1, ..., s_m`, with `w(s)^N = 1` for
   every `w in F_m` of length `<= R_nil`. Then `Q` has exponent dividing `N`, so
   `Q` is a quotient of `R(m,N)` and `|Q| <= |R(m,N)|`.
2. Let `N` be odd with `N >= n_1(F_m)`, and let `G_k` be the stages of
   `free-burnside-is-a-limit-of-hyperbolic-groups`. For all sufficiently large
   `k`:
   - every finite nilpotent quotient of `G_k` has exponent dividing `N`;
   - for every prime `p`, the pro-p completion of `G_k` is finite (trivial when
     `p` does not divide `N`).
3. So finite `p`-groups can refute the local principle `LRB(R, m, N)` of
   `local-restricted-burnside-iff-partial-burnside-finite-quotients` only at
   radii `R < R_nil(m,N)`. Refuting `LRB(m,N)` at every radius needs
   non-nilpotent finite groups, and if some `G_k` with large `k` is residually
   finite, its large finite quotients are non-nilpotent.

`R_nil` comes from a stabilizing chain inside a finite group and is not
effective. No novelty is claimed.
