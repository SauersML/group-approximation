## Setting

- `𝒢` is a **groupoid of germs** with unit space the Cantor set `X`, in the sense of Nekrashevych
  (arXiv:1511.08241): an effective étale groupoid, possibly non-Hausdorff.
- `[[𝒢]]` is its topological full group, and `D = [[𝒢]]′` its derived subgroup.
- `A = A(𝒢)` is Nekrashevych's alternating group: the subgroup generated by the groups `A(F)` over
  all multisections `F` of degree 3 (op. cit., Definition in §"Definition of A(G) and S(G)").
- `𝒢` is **minimal**: every orbit is dense. For a clopen `U`, let `Γ_U` be the set of
  `g ∈ [[𝒢]]` that are the identity outside `U`.
- A clopen set `U` is **3-displaceable** if there are `σ, τ ∈ [[𝒢]]` such that `U`, `σ(U)` and
  `τ(U)` are pairwise disjoint.

A discrete piecewise full group `G ≤ Homeo(X)` in the sense of Garrido–Reid (arXiv:2501.00908v1,
l.200–201) is exactly `[[𝒢_G]]` for its groupoid of germs `𝒢_G`. Their `Al(G)` (l.1770–1796) is
Nekrashevych's `A(𝒢_G)`.

## Statement

Let `𝒢` be a minimal groupoid of germs with Cantor unit space.

1. **Local commutators are alternating.** If `U` is 3-displaceable, then `[h, k] ∈ A` for all
   `h, k ∈ Γ_U`.
2. **The characterization.** Let `L` be the subgroup generated by the commutators `[h,k]` with
   `h, k ∈ Γ_U` for some 3-displaceable `U`. Then `A = L`.
3. **The reformulation.** `D` is simple if and only if `D = L`, that is, if and only if every
   commutator `[α, β]` with `α, β ∈ [[𝒢]]` is a product of commutators of pairs supported in a
   common 3-displaceable clopen set. For `G` discrete, this is exactly Garrido–Reid's question
   `minimal-piecewise-full-group-derived-subgroup-is-simple`.
4. **A sufficient condition.** Fix a metric on `X`. There is `r > 0` such that every clopen set
   inside the `r`-neighbourhood of a set of at most four points is 3-displaceable. If `[[𝒢]]` is
   generated by elements each supported inside the `r`-neighbourhood of a set of at most two
   points, then `D = A`, and `D` is simple.

**What item 4 covers.** Every transposition of a degree-2 multisection is a product of such
elements: refine its domain into small clopen pieces. So item 4 applies whenever `[[𝒢]]` is
generated by Nekrashevych's `S(𝒢)` together with elements supported near two points, such as
first-return maps on small neighbourhoods of two points. When `[[𝒢]]` is generated by torsion,
Matte Bon's theorem `[[𝒢]]_t′ = A` (arXiv:1512.02133, Theorem `T: main 2`) already gives `D = A`.
Item 4 is aimed at generating sets that are not torsion.

**The non-discrete case.** Item 3 is a statement about the abstract group, and the groupoid of
germs is defined for any `G ≤ Homeo(X)`. So it applies to Garrido–Reid's non-discrete piecewise
full groups too, provided Nekrashevych's simplicity theorem holds for uncountable groupoids of
germs. His proof uses only bisections and compactness, but that extension is not checked here.

## Lesson for general BH

Simplicity of full groups is a fragmentation problem, not a dynamics problem. `A(𝒢)` is simple for
every minimal `𝒢` and finitely generated when `𝒢` is expansive (Nekrashevych). It is exactly the
subgroup generated by local commutators. So a Boone–Higman host built as a topological full group
should be taken to be `A(𝒢)`, and the only remaining gate is its finite presentation. That gate
follows from finite presentation of `[[𝒢]]` whenever `[[𝒢]]/A(𝒢)` is finite. The Garrido–Reid and
Matui question, whether `D = A`, affects only the abelianization bookkeeping, never whether a simple
host exists.
