**OPEN.** Placement prerequisite of `nonhyperlinear-via-q1-and-free-host-of-nonce-relation`.

**Statement.** Let `R` be the Manzoor relation `R_mu` of `non-ce-relation-is-not-treeable` item 3, or the
property-(T) relation of `non-ce-property-t-equivalence-relation-exists`. Then `R` has a finite free host, in the
sense of `nonce-relation-hosts-force-root-or-q1-failure`. That is, there are:
* a countable group `Lambda` with an essentially free pmp action on a standard probability space `(Z, zeta)`;
* a Borel subrelation `S ⊆ R_Lambda(Z)` (restricting to a positive-measure `A ⊆ Z` is also allowed);
* a measure-preserving Borel map `r : Z -> X` that maps a.e. `S`-class bijectively onto an `R`-class.

**Sufficient condition** (item 2 of `nonce-relation-hosts-force-root-or-q1-failure`): `R` has a Borel cocycle `c`
into some group `Lambda` acting pmp on `X`, with `c(x, x') · x = x'`.

**Parallel input.** If `R` is generated by a pmp action of some group with finite stabilizers, item 3 of the same
claim gives the conclusion the route needs (a non-CE crossed product of that actor). It does so without producing a
relation-level host, so it is an alternative to this claim rather than a proof of it.

**What is excluded.** By item 5 of `nonce-relation-hosts-force-root-or-q1-failure`, the actor can be neither
amenable-by-(finitely generated virtually free) nor free. The finite-stabilizer and splitting versions are excluded
for those actors too. So a host must use an actor outside every class where Q1 is currently known.

**How it can fail.** A proof that `R` has no free host with any actor would be a new orbit-equivalence rigidity
statement. It would be stronger than non-treeability, and it would have to be invisible to Connes embeddability:
Bernoulli relations of non-hyperlinear groups, if such groups exist, have hosts. No such rigidity mechanism for
class-bijective placements is recorded in the graph. `kazhdan-bernoulli-product-seed-stabilizers-embed-in-host` is the
nearest tool, but it constrains only the actor's subgroups, and only for Bernoulli product seeds of Kazhdan groups.

## Attempts

- **Regeneration from the native action (fails as stated).** The native action of the free group `F` has infinite
  stabilizers (`manzoor-isotropy-has-no-positive-finite-type-corner`), so item 3 of the hosting claim does not apply
  to it. Replacing `F` by another generating group changes nothing unless the new stabilizers are finite.
- **Normalized-section extension (partial, swarm-0917 w18, 2026-09-19).** Let `Gamma ↷ X` generate `R`.
  * Take the space of pairs `(x, sigma)`, where `sigma(x') ∈ {g : g x = x'}` for `x' ∈ [x]` and `sigma(x) = 1`.
    Re-rooting `(x, sigma) -> (x', sigma sigma(x')^(-1))` defines a class-bijective extension `R~` of `R`.
  * `c((x, sigma), (x', sigma')) = sigma(x')` is a cocycle into `Gamma` with `c · x = x'`, and it is class-injective.
  * The product of the uniform measures on the stabilizer cosets is invariant under re-rooting, but it exists only
    when stabilizers are finite. That case is already covered by the finite-isotropy corner.
  * For infinite stabilizers one needs a re-rooting-invariant random normalized section. None is known.
  * Even when one exists, a class-injective cocycle on `R~` gives only an infinite-measure host (by the Mackey
    range). By item 5 of the hosting claim, amenable and free actors are excluded even there.
