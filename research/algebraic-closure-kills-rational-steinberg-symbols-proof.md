**Inputs.** Pinned at source by gq-referee-b (report fad9054a8) in the Dennis--Stein survey (LNM 342) and in
van der Kallen (LNM 551).
- **(T) Tate.** `K_2(Q) ≅ Z/2 ⊕ ⊕_(p odd) (Z/p)^x`, a torsion group. Milnor, *Introduction to Algebraic
  K-Theory*, p. 101, as quoted in Dennis--Stein §11(iii), p. 253. (The theorem number 11.6 was not seen at source.)
- **(S) Bass--Tate.** "K_2 of an algebraically closed field is a torsion free divisible group": Dennis--Stein
  §11(ii), p. 253, citing Bass--Tate, *The Milnor ring of a global field*, LNM 342, (1.2). The stated reason is that
  `K_2(F)` is uniquely divisible by `m` when every `X^m - a` splits over `F`.
- **(IS) Injective stability for fields.** For a field `F` and `N >= 3`, `K_2(N, F) -> K_2(F)` is injective.
  van der Kallen, *Injective stability for K_2*, LNM 551 (1976), Theorem 1, p. 77, stated in terms of the dimension
  `d` of the maximal spectrum; for a field `d = 0` and it gives `K_2(n+1, F) ≅ K_2(F)` for `n >= 2`. Also
  Dennis--Stein §12, pp. 253--254, and Matsumoto's theorem.
- **Functoriality.** A unital ring map `A -> B` induces `St_N(A) -> St_N(B)`, `x_ij(a) -> x_ij(a)`. It maps
  `K_2(N, A)` into `K_2(N, B)` and commutes with the stabilization maps `K_2(N, -) -> K_2(-)`.

**Item 1.**
- Let `x in K_2(N, Q)`, and let `y` be its image in `K_2(N, F)`.
- The image of `y` in `K_2(F)` equals the image of `x` under `K_2(N,Q) -> K_2(Q) -> K_2(F)`, and that is `0` by
  hypothesis.
- So `y = 1` by (IS). The map `Q -> R` factors as `Q -> F -> R`, because a unital map out of `Q` is unique. So the image of `x` in `St_N(R)` is the image of
  `y`, which is `1`.
- For `λ, μ in Q^x` the symbol `{λ, μ}_N = h_12(λμ) h_12(λ)^-1 h_12(μ)^-1`, with `h_12(u) = w_12(u) w_12(-1)` and
  `w_12(u) = x_12(u) x_21(-u^-1) x_12(u)`, maps to `1` in `SL_N(Q)`. So it lies in `K_2(N, Q)`, and its image in
  `St_N(R)` is the symbol `{λ, μ}_N` of `R`.

**Item 2.** `K_2(Q) -> K_2(Q̄)` maps a torsion group, by (T), into a torsion-free group, by (S). So it is zero.
For `F ⊇ Q̄` the map factors through `K_2(Q̄)`.

**Item 3.**
- `R_L` is finitely presented over `Z` and simple, with `Q ⊆ R_L` central and unital
  (`leavitt-resolvent-ring-is-fp-simple-of-char-zero`).
- By `field-envelopes-pass-to-algebraic-closures`, item 2, `Q̄` embeds unitally in `R_L`, through the ultramatricial
  algebra `colim M_(d_i)(Q) ⊆ L_Q(1,2) ⊆ R_L`. It restricts to the given `Q -> R_L`, since a unital ring map out of `Q` is unique
  (gq-referee-a, N1).
- Items 1 and 2 then apply. For `S ⊗_Q L_Q(1,2)`, use `Q̄ ⊆ 1 ⊗ L_Q(1,2)`.

**Item 4.**
- Fix `λ in Q̄^x`, `x in R^x` and `m >= 1`, and pick `ν in Q̄` with `ν^m = λ`. Since `Q̄ ⊆ Z(R)` unitally, `ν` is a central unit, and the subring
  `C` generated by `ν^(±1)` and `x^(±1)` is commutative.
- In `K_2(C)` symbols are bimultiplicative: Dennis--Stein §9(a), relation (S1), p. 249, pointing to Milnor, p. 74. So `{λ, x} = m {ν, x}` in
  `K_2(C)`, hence in `K_2(R)`.
- Suppose `E_N(R)` is finitely presented. By `infinitely-generated-symbols-block-fp-elementary-groups`, item 1, the
  image `A` of `K_2(N, R)` in `K_2(R)` is a finitely generated abelian group containing every symbol of commuting
  units, in particular `{ν, x}`. So `{λ, x} in mA` for every `m`.
- In a finitely generated abelian group `∩_m mA = 0`: take `m` a multiple of the torsion exponent, then note that
  a free abelian group has no nonzero element divisible by every integer. So `{λ, x} = 0`.

**Scope.** Only item 4 uses a finiteness hypothesis. Items 1--3 say nothing about the symbols `{λ, x}` for
non-rational units `x`, or about `K_1`.
