In the binary Jacobson ring put

```text
Q=1-ST,                    A=QT.
```

The unital coefficient algebra generated by `Q,A` is exactly

```text
A_QT=F_2*1 direct-sum F_2*Q direct-sum F_2*A,            (GQF1)
|A_QT|=8,
```

with multiplication table

```text
Q^2=Q,             QA=A,             AQ=0,             A^2=0.   (GQF2)
```

Consequently the full long-router sign group is finite:

```text
K_QT=<C,g_2,H,C_1,W> <=GL_28(A_QT).                     (GQF3)
```

Its left regular representation is an exact finite-dimensional model in
which the head `H` is nonidentity and

```text
||lambda(H)-I||_2=sqrt(2).                              (GQF4)
```

It simultaneously realizes every sign projection

```text
G_(sigma,tau)=E(1+sigma C_1)(1+tau W)/4,
```

every polar support of `G_(sigma,tau)R`, every power of `g_2`, and
every cross term made from them. In particular the exact ranks `5/72`
and `1/16` coexist with this finite head-retaining model.

Thus no `g_2`-conditioned recombination confined to
`C,H,C_1,W` can yield a uniform Jacobson head-collapse modulus. This
includes all constructions which use the sign polars as black boxes and
route them only by scalar long-router powers.

The firewall is sharp at the coefficient level. Although
`C_1=[H,U]` carries the label `QT`, the head factor `Q` traps it in
the finite algebra `A_QT`. A surviving long-router relation must expose
a raw letter

```text
U=x(T)          or          V=x(S)
```

outside that head-masked envelope in the same operator which claims source
preservation. Merely using the returned roots `C_1,W` does not do so.
