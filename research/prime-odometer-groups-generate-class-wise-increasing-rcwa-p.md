simplicity of `CT_P(Z)` and Jordan's theorem. It has not been reviewed, and no priority is
claimed. The `F_∞` input is Li's theorem, rechecked at source for `Λ_P` without the extra
colour; see `class-wise-increasing-rcwa-p-groups-are-abelian-cofinite-hosts`.

## Statement

Let `P′ = P ∪ {2}` be finite, and let `B_p = V_(p,1)(⟨a⟩)` act on `Z`, the eventually constant
base-`p` sequences, with `a(n) = n + 1`. Then
`⟨B_p : p ∈ P′⟩ = R_P`,
the group of class-wise increasing elements of `RCWA_P(Z)`.

**Consequences.**
- *Mixed-radix gluing.* In particular `G_(2,3) = ⟨B, B_3⟩ = R_{3}`, which is of type `F_∞`.
- *Kernel removal.* The map `B *_⟨a⟩ B_3 → R_{3}` is onto a finitely presented group, so its
  kernel (free, and nontrivial by `port-amalgams-with-a-locally-moving-factor-are-never-faithful`)
  is finitely normally generated.
- *The same for any `P`.* The iterated port amalgam of the `B_p` along `⟨a⟩` maps onto `R_P`
  with a finitely normally generated kernel.

## Proof

Write `G = ⟨B_p⟩`. *Boxes* are `B(r, m) = r + mẐ_P`, with `m` a `P′`-smooth integer. Take `P′ = {2, 3}`;
the general case is identical, with `|P′|` coordinates in step 1.

**Step 1. `G` contains the odometer full group `O`.** `O` is the group of piecewise translations
`x ↦ x + c(x mod M)`. For `M = 6^k`, `O_M` is the centralizer of `a^M`, isomorphic to
`Z ≀ Sym(M)`, with translation lattice `T = Z^M`.
- **The generators.** The piecewise translations with pieces mod `2^k` lie in `B`, and those
  mod `3^k` lie in `B_3`.
  - On `Z/M = Z/2^k × Z/3^k` (points `(e, ρ)`), the first induce every map
    `(e, ρ) ↦ (π(e), ρ + t_e)`, with `π ∈ Sym(2^k)` and `t_e` arbitrary, by the Chinese
    remainder theorem. Call this group `W_1`.
  - The second induce every `(e, ρ) ↦ (e + u_ρ, ψ(ρ))`. Call this group `W_2`.
- **A 3-cycle.** A translation of one fibre `{e_0} × Z/3^k` and a translation of one column
  `Z/2^k × {ρ_0}` are cycles meeting in one point, so their commutator is a 3-cycle.
- **Primitivity.** Let `Δ` be a nontrivial block.
  - If `Δ` meets two fibres, translating one fibre while fixing the other forces `Δ` to
    contain both fibres. Then `Sym(2^k)` forces all of them, so `Δ` is everything.
  - So `W_1`-blocks lie in fibres. Symmetrically, `W_2`-blocks lie in columns.
  - A common block lies in one point.
- **All permutations.** By Jordan's theorem the induced group contains `Alt(M)`. A fibre swap
  is a product of `3^k` transpositions, which is odd, so the induced group is `Sym(M)`.
- **The lattice.** `T ∩ G` is `Sym(M)`-invariant. It contains:
  - the powers of translating a class mod `2^k` by `2^k`, which are indicator vectors of
    `3^k`-sets;
  - symmetrically, indicator vectors of `2^k`-sets.

  Differences give the root lattice, and `gcd(3^k, 2^k) = 1` gives the sum. So `T ⊆ G`, and
  `O_M ⊆ G`.

**Step 2. `R_P = ⟨CT_P(Z), O⟩`.** Let `f ∈ R_P` map boxes `P_i → Q_i`.
- The map `Q_i → P_i` by the normalized canonical maps `s + nz ↦ r + mz` lies in
  `[[G_Λ]] = CT_P(Z)` (`ct-p-z-is-a-one-vertex-k-graph-full-group`, item 1).
- Composing it with `f` gives slope `1` on every piece, which is an element of `O`.

**Step 3. `G` contains every box 3-cycle.** A *box 3-cycle* `(A, B, C)` permutes three disjoint
boxes by canonical affine maps.
- **`O` transports configurations.** `O` acts transitively on such configurations of a fixed
  modulus type `(m_A, m_B, m_C)`. It translates boxes, and moving a box by multiples of its
  modulus adjusts the shifts. In particular `O ⊆ G` normalizes shifts.
- **Type `(m, 2m, 2m)`, `m = 2^a 3^b`, `a ≥ 2`.**
  - A 2-adic cone transposition `τ_0 ∈ V ≤ B` swaps `B(r_0, 2^a)` with `B(s_0, 2^{a+1})`. It
    is a product of `3^b` commuting transpositions `τ_j`, one for each sub-box of modulus
    `m`.
  - Choose `λ ∈ O` that fixes one sub-box `A` pointwise, moves its partner `B` to a fresh
    box `C`, and moves the other components off `supp τ_0 ∪ C`. The counting gives room once
    `a ≥ 2`.
  - Every other component commutes with everything else in sight. So
    `[τ_0, λ τ_0 λ^-1] = [τ_A, τ_A′]`, where `τ_A` swaps `A, B` and `τ_A′` swaps `A, C`.
  - This is the box 3-cycle `(A, C, B)`.
- **Type `(m, 3m, 3m)`.** The same construction with `B_3` and 2-adic sub-boxes.
- **Small moduli.** A box 3-cycle is the product of the commuting 3-cycles on corresponding
  halves, so small moduli follow from large ones.
- **Changing one modulus.** If `(A, B, C)` and `(C, D, E)` are box 3-cycles with `D, E` fresh,
  then conjugating the first by the second gives `(A, B, D)`. Rotations of the types
  `(z, 2z, 2z)` and `(z, 3z, 3z)` multiply or divide one modulus by `2` or `3`.
- **All types.** Starting from type `(m, m, m)` in `O`, every type is reached.

**Step 4. Conclusion.**
- The normalized box 3-cycles lie in `CT_P(Z) = [[G_Λ]]` and generate a nontrivial normal
  subgroup of it.
- `CT_P(Z)` is simple (same node, item 2), so `CT_P(Z) ⊆ G`.
- With steps 1 and 2, `G = R_P`. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

**Kernel removal can be proved by generation, without writing down relators.**
- Glue groupoid-coded factors along a common port. The image is then a subgroup of the full
  group of the generated groupoid.
- Once the image *is* that full group, Li's theorem makes it finitely presented. The free
  gluing kernel is then finitely normally generated, even though no disjoint-support relator
  between the factors exists (2-adic and 3-adic boxes always meet).

**Where generation comes from.** It follows from three cheap facts:
- a Jordan-type argument on finite quotients, which gives the odometer part;
- commutators of a transposition with its conjugates by that odometer part, which give
  3-cycles;
- simplicity of the port-free part (Matui), which absorbs the 3-cycles.

**The general principle.** One-prime Thompson-type hosts glued along the translation generate
the full mixed-radix host. That is a template for building finitely presented hosts from
finitely presented pieces whenever their groupoids generate a Li-finite groupoid. It gives the
positive side of the gate in `port-amalgams-with-a-locally-moving-factor-are-never-faithful`.
