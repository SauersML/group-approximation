If Thompson's `V` is hyperlinear and not sofic, it is a hyperlinear nonsofic
group.

## Why this candidate is recorded separately

Every other separation route in this graph (`hyperlinear-nonsofic-from-leavitt-unit-group`,
`hyperlinear-nonsofic-from-fournier-facio`, the Kun--Thom splices) starts from
a group whose nonsoficity comes from the Kazhdan compression mechanism. By
`hyperlinear-nonsofic-group`, that mechanism may leave Question 3.4
inaccessible until a second mechanism exists. `V` is the opposite kind of
candidate. It has the Haagerup property, so it contains no infinite Kazhdan
subgroup. Both premises are open, and the nonsoficity premise needs exactly
the second mechanism.

It is also the one candidate whose hyperlinear branch is not merely a lower
bound. The Leavitt, Steinberg and Fournier-Facio routes each need
hyperlinearity of a group containing `V` or another famous simple group. This
route needs hyperlinearity of `V` itself.
