Let `F` be a field, `M` a rank ultraproduct over `F`, `sigma : V -> M^x` a homomorphism, `p` an odd prime
different from `char F`, and `a_p(sigma)` the constant of `v-rank-models-are-trivial-plus-free-on-odd-cycle-trees`.
Let `f M` be the fixed right ideal of `sigma(V)`.

**Theorem.** `a_p(sigma) = rk(f)`.

**Corollaries.**
1. `a_p(sigma)` is the same for every odd prime `p != char F`.
2. **Fixed spaces do not localize.** For every nonempty clopen `O != X`, the rigid stabilizer `V_O` fixes
   exactly `f M`.
3. **Reduced quotient.** If `sigma` is nontrivial, the corner compression
   `sigma'(g) = (1 - f) sigma(g) (1 - f)` is a nontrivial rank model with no nonzero fixed vectors and
   `a_p(sigma') = 0`.
4. **Reduced models.** In a model with `Fix(V) = 0`, every clopen-cycle subgroup acts regularly, and every
   clopen `p`-cycle has `rk(sigma(g) - 1) = (p - 1)/p`.

**Proof idea.** The upper bound comes from dyadic tree groups of unbounded order. For the lower bound:
- `V` is generated by the rigid stabilizers of `X \ [00]` and `X \ [11]` (a transposition fragmentation).
- Each is generated by finitely many clopen `p`-cycles.
- Long tails of disjoint `p`-cycles in `[00]` and in `[11]` have fixed ideals of rank close to `a`. The
  iterated modular law shows that almost all of those tail-fixed vectors are fixed by the generators, and
  that the two tails share them.

## Attempts

- **What it does not give.** A reduced model looks, on torsion, exactly like a sofic approximation of `V`:
  regular on every clopen-cycle subgroup, with supports that never localize. So this theorem cannot force
  triviality alone. It removes the constant `a_p` from the gate: the gate is equivalent to "no reduced
  nontrivial model exists".
