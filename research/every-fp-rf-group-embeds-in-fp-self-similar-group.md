**OPEN.** For every finitely presented residually finite group `G` there are
`d >= 2` and a finitely presented self-similar group `H <= Aut(T_d)` with
`G <= H`.

## Necessary conditions on any overgroup

**(N1) Bounded composition factors.** Every subgroup of `Aut(T_d)` is
residually `W_d`, where `W_d` is the class of finite groups each of whose
composition factors is a composition factor of a subgroup of `S_d`. Indeed
the quotient by the level-`n` stabilizer embeds in the automorphism group of
the first `n` levels, the `n`-fold iterated wreath product of `S_d`, and the
level stabilizers intersect trivially because the action is faithful. So `G`
must be residually `W_d` for the chosen `d`. No finitely presented residually
finite group failing this for every `d` is known here; the condition is
recorded as a screen, not an obstruction.

**(N2) Infinitely many states for complex inputs.** An automaton group, i.e.
a self-similar group generated by finite-state automorphisms, is a group of
rational homeomorphisms of the boundary. For the input of
`decidable-fp-groups-need-not-embed-in-rational-group` no such host exists, so
a positive answer must use self-similar groups with infinitely many states.

**(N3) Complex word problem.** By
`uniform-wp-bound-excludes-bh-universal-targets`, whose excluded input is itself
finitely presented and residually finite, the overgroups for all inputs of this
claim cannot share one recursive time bound on their word problems. The
two-generated form `complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal` is not
enough here, because its witness is not residually finite. Combined
with `fp-self-similar-groups-embed-in-fp-simple-groups`, this claim applied to
algorithmically complex inputs would produce finitely presented simple groups
with arbitrarily complex word problem, the open root
`fp-simple-groups-with-arbitrarily-complex-word-problem`.

## Attempts

1. **Affine self-similar groups.** For rings `R` as in Zaremsky's Example 4.7
   (principal left ideals of finite index generated by non-zero-divisors),
   `R^n x| GL_n(R)` is self-similar, and Theorem 1.2 there handles finitely
   generated subgroups of `GL_n(Q)`. This covers linear inputs over such
   rings. It does not reach coefficient rings of positive transcendence
   degree by the same formula: for `R = Z[t]` embedded in `Z_p`, the section
   of `x -> Ax + v` at a depth-`k` vertex `u` has translation part
   `(Au + v - u')/p^k` with `u'` the integer residue, which need not lie in
   `Z[t]`. No finitely generated self-similar closure is exhibited, and none
   is excluded.
2. **Sections of the profinite translation action.** Along a chain of normal
   subgroups `N_i` of finite index, the section of translation by `g` at the
   vertex `xN_i` is translation on the coset tree of `N_i`. Self-similarity
   requires identifying that tree with the tree of `G` itself, i.e. a virtual
   endomorphism carrying `N_i` onto `G` with trivial core. A general
   residually finite group has no such endomorphism; producing one for an
   overgroup is exactly the construction problem.
3. **Self-similar closure inside `Aut(T_d)`.** Any residually `W_d` group
   `G <= Aut(T_d)` lies in the group generated by all sections of its
   elements, which is self-similar and countable. It is finitely generated
   exactly when the generators have finitely many sections, i.e. are
   finite-state, which (N2) forbids for complex inputs. Finite presentation
   is not addressed.
