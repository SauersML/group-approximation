**OPEN.** For every finitely presented residually finite group `G` there are
`d >= 2` and a finitely presented self-similar group `H <= Aut(T_d)` with
`G <= H`.

## Necessary conditions on any overgroup

**(N1) Bounded composition factors.** Every subgroup of `Aut(T_d)` is
residually `W_d`, where `W_d` is the class of finite groups each of whose
composition factors is a composition factor of a subgroup of `S_d`. Indeed:
- the quotient by the level-`n` stabilizer embeds in the automorphism group of
  the first `n` levels, the `n`-fold iterated wreath product of `S_d`;
- the level stabilizers intersect trivially, because the action is faithful.

So `G` must be residually `W_d` for the chosen `d`.

**Torsion form of (N1).** Every torsion element of `Aut(T_d)` has order with
all prime factors at most `d`, by
`regular-tree-torsion-orders-have-primes-at-most-degree`. An input with
elements of prime order for infinitely many primes therefore has no overgroup
of this kind.

**Status of (N1) as an obstruction.**
- *Finitely generated inputs.* (N1) is a genuine obstruction there. The
  two-generated group of
  `cycle-transposition-lef-group-has-no-regular-tree-action` has all of these
  properties:
  - it is residually finite;
  - its word problem is solvable in polynomial time;
  - it contains `Alt(p)` for every prime `p >= 5`;
  - it fails (N1) for every `d`.
- *Finitely presented inputs.* No finitely presented residually finite group
  failing (N1) is known here. One would refute this claim; that is the open
  claim `some-fp-rf-group-acts-on-no-regular-rooted-tree`, listed under
  `refuted_by`.
- *Consequence.* This claim implies a negative answer to
  `rf-higman-embedding-for-decidable-groups` for that two-generated group.

**(N2) Infinitely many states for complex inputs.** An automaton group, i.e.
a self-similar group generated by finite-state automorphisms, is a group of
rational homeomorphisms of the boundary. For the input of
`decidable-fp-groups-need-not-embed-in-rational-group` no such host exists, so
a positive answer must use self-similar groups with infinitely many states.

**(N3) Complex word problem.**
- By `uniform-wp-bound-excludes-bh-universal-targets`, the overgroups for all
  inputs of this claim cannot share one recursive time bound on their word
  problems. The input excluded there is itself finitely presented and
  residually finite.
- The two-generated form `complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal` is
  not enough here, because its witness is not residually finite.
- Combined with `fp-self-similar-groups-embed-in-fp-simple-groups`, this claim
  applied to algorithmically complex inputs would give finitely presented simple
  groups with arbitrarily complex word problem. That is the open root
  `fp-simple-groups-with-arbitrarily-complex-word-problem`.

## Attempts

1. **Affine self-similar groups.** For rings `R` as in Zaremsky's Example 4.7
   (principal left ideals of finite index generated by non-zero-divisors),
   `R^n x| GL_n(R)` is self-similar, and Theorem 1.2 there handles finitely
   generated subgroups of `GL_n(Q)`. This covers linear inputs over such rings.
   The same formula does not reach coefficient rings of positive transcendence
   degree. For `R = Z[t]` embedded in `Z_p`, the section of `x -> Ax + v` at a
   depth-`k` vertex `u` has translation part `(Au + v - u')/p^k`, with `u'` the
   integer residue, and this need not lie in `Z[t]`. No finitely generated
   self-similar closure is exhibited, and none is excluded.
2. **Sections of the profinite translation action.** Along a chain of normal
   subgroups `N_i` of finite index, the section of translation by `g` at the
   vertex `xN_i` is translation on the coset tree of `N_i`. Self-similarity
   requires identifying that tree with the tree of `G` itself, i.e. a virtual
   endomorphism carrying `N_i` onto `G` with trivial core. A general residually
   finite group has no such endomorphism, and producing one for an overgroup is
   exactly the construction problem.
3. **Self-similar closure inside `Aut(T_d)`.** Any residually `W_d` group
   `G <= Aut(T_d)` lies in the group generated by all sections of its elements,
   which is self-similar and countable. That group is finitely generated exactly
   when the generators have finitely many sections, i.e. are finite-state, which
   (N2) forbids for complex inputs. Finite presentation is not addressed.
4. **Refutation through regular-tree obstructions.** This is the open claim
   `some-fp-rf-group-acts-on-no-regular-rooted-tree`, listed under `refuted_by`.
   - *Sufficient input.* A finitely presented residually finite group with
     torsion of infinitely many prime orders
     (`fp-rf-group-with-unbounded-prime-torsion`). One example would be a
     finitely presented residually finite overgroup of the cycle-transposition
     group.
   - *Classes that cannot supply it.* Each of these has only boundedly many
     torsion primes:
     - linear groups;
     - abelian-by-polycyclic groups;
     - groups acting properly and cocompactly on CAT(0) spaces;
     - the Kharlampovich--Myasnikov--Sapir groups.
   - *Lesson for a positive proof.* It must use finite presentation of the input
     in an essential way, since (N1) already fails for finitely generated
     residually finite inputs with polynomial-time word problem.
