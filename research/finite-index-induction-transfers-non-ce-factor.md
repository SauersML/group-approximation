Let `H<Lambda` have finite index `n`, and let

```text
pi:H -> U(Q),              pi(H)''=Q,                 (FII1)
```

where `Q` is a non-Connes-embeddable finite factor.  Form the ordinary
induced representation

```text
rho=Ind_H^Lambda(pi):Lambda -> U(M_n(Q))             (FII2)
```

in coset coordinates, and put `A=rho(Lambda)''`.  Then `A` is not Connes
embeddable.

Consequently, if every extremal character of `Lambda` is either the regular
character or finite dimensional, then `Lambda` is nonhyperlinear.  Indeed,
if `L(Lambda)` were Connes embeddable, the central decomposition of the
induced character would consist only of Connes-embeddable factors, contrary
to the first assertion.

Thus finite-index induction is a valid factor/character compiler.  It does
not apply to the proposed acylindrically hyperbolic-to-simple-Kac--Moody
transfer.  An infinite simple group has no proper finite-index subgroup, so
an acylindrically hyperbolic subgroup of such a host is necessarily in the
infinite-index regime unless the host itself is acylindrically hyperbolic.
The latter is excluded by the proper infinite quotient consequences already
recorded in `ah-groups-fail-regular-fd-character-rigidity`.

The external universality input remains exactly Chifan--Drimbe--Ioana,
Theorem A(1), arXiv:2205.07442: every separable tracial algebra embeds in a
factor generated by a representation of any prescribed acylindrically
hyperbolic group.  This claim proves what finite-index induction would do to
that representation; it does not manufacture the absent finite-index
embedding into the rigid host.
