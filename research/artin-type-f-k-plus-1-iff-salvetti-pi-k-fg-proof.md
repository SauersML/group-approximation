Notation as in `artin-type-f-k-plus-1-iff-salvetti-pi-k-fg`: `X` finite, `π_1(X) = G`,
`k ≥ 2`, `π_i(X) = 0` for `2 ≤ i ≤ k − 1`, and `X~` the universal cover. Then `X~` is
`(k − 1)`-connected, so by the Hurewicz theorem `H_i(X~) = 0` for `1 ≤ i ≤ k − 1` and
`π_k(X) = π_k(X~) ≅ H_k(X~)`, an isomorphism of `ZG`-modules (the deck action on both
sides).

**`G` is of type `F_k`.** Attach cells of dimensions `≥ k + 1` to `X` to kill `π_i` for
all `i ≥ k`. This does not change the `k`-skeleton, and the result is a `K(G, 1)` with
finite `k`-skeleton.

**(1) ⇒ (2).** The cellular chain complex of the universal cover of a `K(G,1)` with
finite `(k+1)`-skeleton is a free resolution of `Z` that is finitely generated in
degrees `≤ k + 1`.

**(2) ⇒ (3).** Let `C_* = C_*(X~)`, a complex of finitely generated free `ZG`-modules.
By the vanishing above,

`C_k → C_{k−1} → ⋯ → C_0 → Z → 0`

is exact except at `C_k`: a partial resolution of finite type of length `k`. Put
`Z_k = ker(∂_k : C_k → C_{k−1})`. Since `G` is of type `FP_{k+1}`, there is a projective
resolution `P_*` of `Z` with `P_0, …, P_{k+1}` finitely generated, and then
`Z'_k = ker(P_k → P_{k−1}) = im(P_{k+1} → P_k)` is finitely generated. The generalized
Schanuel lemma, applied to the two exact sequences
`0 → Z_k → C_k → ⋯ → C_0 → Z → 0` and `0 → Z'_k → P_k → ⋯ → P_0 → Z → 0`, gives

`Z_k ⊕ P_k ⊕ C_{k−1} ⊕ P_{k−2} ⊕ ⋯ ≅ Z'_k ⊕ C_k ⊕ P_{k−1} ⊕ C_{k−2} ⊕ ⋯`.

The right side is finitely generated, so its direct summand `Z_k` is finitely generated.
Hence `H_k(X~) = Z_k / im(∂_{k+1})` is finitely generated, and so is `π_k(X)`.

**(3) ⇒ (1).** Choose cellular maps `α_1, …, α_r : S^k → X~` whose classes generate
`π_k(X~)` as a `ZG`-module, and attach `r` cells of dimension `k + 1` to `X` along the
projections of the `α_j` to `X`. The new finite complex `Y` has `π_1(Y) = G`, and its
universal cover `Y~` is obtained from `X~` by attaching the `G`-orbits of these cells, so
`Y~` is still `(k − 1)`-connected. In the exact sequence of the pair,

`H_{k+1}(Y~, X~) → H_k(X~) → H_k(Y~) → H_k(Y~, X~) = 0`,

the first group is the free `ZG`-module on the new cells and its image is the submodule
generated by the classes `[α_j]`, which is all of `H_k(X~)`. So `H_k(Y~) = 0`, and by the
Hurewicz theorem `Y~` is `k`-connected. Attaching cells of dimensions `≥ k + 2` to `Y`
gives a `K(G, 1)` with finite `(k + 1)`-skeleton.

**Artin groups.** `Sal(Γ)/W` is a finite CW complex with fundamental group `A_Γ`
(Paris 2014, Theorem 3.10), so the statement applies with `X = Sal(Γ)/W`. ∎

Only standard algebraic topology and the generalized Schanuel lemma are used; no
property of Artin groups beyond the finiteness of the Salvetti complex and its
fundamental group.
