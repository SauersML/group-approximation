**ESTABLISHED BY CITATION.**  Lemma 20 of
Culf--Mastel--Paddock--Spirig, *The quantum smooth label cover problem is
undecidable*, arXiv:2510.03477v3, gives a constant `s<1` and an RE-hard
perfect-versus-`s` family of finite `3SAT-10` clause-variable games.  On a
value-one instance one can choose binary observables `A_x` satisfying every
clause and

```text
[A_x,A_y]=0  if x,y occur in one clause,                  (C2O1)
[A_x,A_y]=0  if x,z and y,z occur in two clauses.         (C2O2)
```

The second quantifier ranges over the whole radius-two incidence relation;
it is not restricted to pairs sampled by the game distribution.  The paper
calls this property `2`-oracularizability.  In its proof of Theorem 19 it is
used exactly in this strength: two smooth-label-cover questions having a
possible common partner have commuting answer PVMs.

In particular, fix a source variable `z`.  Every variable lying with `z` in
any clause commutes with every other such variable.  Since variables within
each clause commute as well, the finite-dimensional abelian algebras
generated by all clauses incident to `z` commute elementwise and admit one
joint assignment PVM.

This statement concerns the cited `3SAT-10` completeness model only.  It
does not say that an arbitrary perfect model has the property, and it does
not by itself show that a later fixed-language gadget reduction preserves
the property.
