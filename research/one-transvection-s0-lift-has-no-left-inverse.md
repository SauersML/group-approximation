**Setting.**
- `k` is a field of characteristic not 2, `G` a group, and `z in G` central of order 2.
- `eps_- = (1 - [z])/2` and `S_- = eps_- k[G]`.
- For an involution `d in G \ <z>`, put `P_d = eps_-(1 - [d])/2`.

**Theorem 1.** Let `d in G \ <z>` be an involution and `h in G`. If `P_d[h]P_d` has a left inverse in
`P_d S_- P_d`, then it has an inverse there.

**Corollary 2.** Let `u0, u1 in G`, `P1 = P_(d1)` and `P0 = eps_- - P1`. If `a = [u0]P0 + [u1]P1` is left
invertible, then it is invertible.
- So no two-piece lift of `s0` over the involution partition of `d1 = 1 + s1 t1` in `eps_- F_3[L_(F_3)(1,2)^x]`
  has a left inverse.
- In particular the one-transvection lift `a = -[u0] - [u0 d1] - [u1] + [u1 d1]` of
  `one-transvection-ternary-s0-lift-is-left-invertible` has none.
- Equivalently, `Y_h = P_(d1)[h]P_(d1)` with `h = [[0,1],[-1,s0]]` has no left inverse in the corner.

**Why.**
- Put `c = h^(-1) d h` and `Q = P_c`. Then `v P[h]P = P` rewrites as `P = P x Q P` with `x = v[h]`.
- Projecting coefficients onto `W = <z, d, c>` keeps the equation, with `x` now supported in `W`.
- `W` is a quotient of `C_2 x D_inf`, so it is residually finite.
- In a finite quotient separating the relevant elements, `e = QPxQ` is equivalent to `P`, lies under `Q`, and has
  the same dimension. So `e = Q`.
- Then `P x [h^(-1)] P` is a two-sided inverse.
- For the one-transvection data, `W` is dihedral of order 12.

**Independent check** (artifact Section 2). The representation `s0 -> 0` of `<z, d1, h> <= GL_2(F_3[s0])` sends
`Y_h` to 0 and `P_(d1)` to `diag(0,1)`. Projecting onto that subgroup, as in
`subgroup-supported-one-sided-inverse-rigidity`, gives a contradiction.

**Consequence once verified.** Route `one-transvection-s0-lift-refutes-ternary-surjunctivity` is invalidated. A
two-piece witness for `J_1(S_-) != 0` then needs an idempotent of a finite subgroup `A` with `|A/<z>| >= 3`, and an
`h` for which `<A, h^(-1) A h>` is not residually finite (artifact Remark 1.2).
