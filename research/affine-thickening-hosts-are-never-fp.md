## Statement

Let `N ≥ 1`, `A = Z[1/N]`, `d ≥ 1`, and let `Q ≤ Aff(A^d) = A^d ⋊ GL_d(A)` be finitely
generated and contain the translation `τ` by `e_1`. Let `S` be an infinite set of integers
`m ≥ 5` prime to `N`. Then `Q` acts on `X_m = (A/mA)^d = (Z/m)^d` by reduction. Let `α` be
the 3-cycle `(0, e_1, 2e_1)` in every `X_m`, and put

    Γ = ⟨Q, α⟩ ≤ ∏_{m∈S} Sym(X_m).

**Theorem.** `Γ` is residually finite and **not finitely presented**.

**Examples.**
- `d = 1`, `Q = ⟨x+1⟩`: Neumann's group itself (another proof that it is not finitely
  presented).
- `d = 1`, `Q = ⟨x+1, 2x⟩`: the doubling host.
- `Q = ASL_d(Z)` with `S` the odd `m ≥ 5`: `⟨α, τ⟩ ≤ Γ` is Neumann's exact group,
  because the line through `0` is the Neumann coordinate and the other lines see only
  `Γ → Z`. So these hosts contain Neumann's group, and none of them is finitely presented.
- `ASL_d(Z[1/N])`, and every other affine choice.

## Proof

**1. Structure.** The normal closure `N_Γ` of `α` is generated by the conjugates `qαq^{-1}`
(`q ∈ Q`), which are 3-cycles on the reductions of `q{0, e_1, 2e_1} ⊂ A^d`.
- `N_Γ` is **locally finite.** `r` conjugates generate a finitely generated subgroup of
  `∏_m Alt(3r points)`. A finitely generated residually-(order `≤ C`) group is finite,
  since it has only finitely many such quotients.
- `Q ≅ Q̄` and `N_Γ ∩ Q̄ = 1`. A non-identity affine map of `A^d` stays non-identity mod
  large `m` and then moves more than any fixed number of points.
- So `Γ = N_Γ ⋊ Q`.

**2. Truncations.** For `K ≥ 1`, let `B_K` be the `K`-ball of `Q`. Let `F_K ⊂ A^d` be the
union of the triples `q{0, e_1, 2e_1}` with `q ∈ B_K`, and let `H_K ≤ N_Γ` be generated by
the `α_q = qαq^{-1}`, `q ∈ B_K`. Put

    Γ_K = ⟨gens(Q), α | relators of Q, all words in the α_q (q ∈ B_K) trivial in Γ⟩.

Write a relator of `Γ` as `n·v`, with `n` in the normal closure of `α` in the free group
and `v` a word in `gens(Q)`. By Step 1, `v` is a relator of `Q`. Modulo relators of `Q`,
`n` is a word in finitely many `α_q` that is trivial in `N_Γ`, hence a relator of some
`H_K`. So `Γ = lim Γ_K`. If `Γ` were finitely presented, then `Γ = Γ_K` for some `K`.

**3. The periodized fake.** Fix `K`. Choose `k` prime to `N`, `k ≥ 3`, with
`(F_K − F_K) ∩ kA^d = {0}`. This is possible because every nonzero vector of the finite
set `F_K − F_K` has a coordinate `a/N^j` with `a ≠ 0`; take `k` a prime above all `|a|`.
On `A^d` let `Q` act affinely, and put

    O = kA^d,   α_k = ∏_{o∈O} (o, o+e_1, o+2e_1).

The triples are disjoint, because `e_1, 2e_1 ∉ kA^d`.
- Every linear part `L ∈ GL_d(A)` satisfies `LO = O`. So for `q = τ_b L`,
  `q α_k q^{-1} = ∏_{o∈O} τ_o (qαq^{-1}) τ_o^{-1}`: each `α_q` acts as the
  **`O`-periodization** of the honest 3-cycle `qαq^{-1}` on `A^d`.
- The translates `F_K + o` (`o ∈ O`) are pairwise disjoint. So a word `W` in the `α_q`
  acts on each translate as `τ_o W_hon τ_o^{-1}`, where `W_hon` is its honest action on
  `F_K`.
- If `W` is trivial in `Γ`, it is trivial in every coordinate `m ∈ S`. For large `m`,
  reduction is injective on `F_K` and `Q`-equivariant, so `W_hon = 1`.
- Relators of `Q` hold because `Q` acts on `A^d`. Hence `(gens(Q), α_k)` is an action of
  `Γ_K` on `A^d`.

**4. It is not a Γ-action.** Let `a_i = τ^iατ^{-i}`. In `Γ`, `⟨a_0,…,a_{k-1}⟩` is finite by
Step 1. On the fake, `a_c` maps `(nk+c)e_1 ↦ (nk+c+1)e_1` (take `o = nk e_1 ∈ O`). So the
orbit of `0` contains every `je_1` with `j ≥ 0`, which is infinite. A finite group has
finite orbits. So `Γ_K ≠ Γ` for every `K`. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

**Periodization kills linear dynamics.** Suppose a host is built as `N ⋊ Q`, with `N`
locally finite and generated by `Q`-translates of one finitary element, and residual
finiteness comes from finite coordinates. Then any subgroup `O` of the limit set that is
invariant under the linear parts of `Q` and misses all finite difference sets carries a
fake of every truncated presentation. Two things then happen:
- the fake places one copy of the finitary element on each point of `O`;
- the local window groups, finite in the host, acquire infinite orbits on the fake.

Every affine action over a residually finite ring has such an `O` (`kA^d`). So a finitely
presented residually finite host of this kind needs **non-linear** point-stabilizer
dynamics on its limit set, with no invariant separated sublattice.

This is the residually finite counterpart of why Houghton-type fp hosts work: there `Q`
acts transitively on the limit set, and no proper invariant subset exists. The same test
applies unchanged to any candidate fp host `Alt_fin(X) ⋊ Q`, or to its thickenings, in the
BH program.
