## Imports

**Source.** J. Brieussel, T. Zheng, *Speed of random walks, isoperimetry and
compression of finitely generated groups*, arXiv:1510.08040v2 (7 Jul 2016). It
was read at source on 2026-09-18. Quotes are verbatim up to PDF math
extraction.

**(BZ1) Diagonal products** (Section 2.1).
> "Let A = {a1,...,a|A|} and B = {b1,...,b|B|} be two finite groups. Let {Γs} be
> a sequence of groups such that each Γs is marked with a generating set of the
> form A(s)∪B(s) where A(s) and B(s) are finite subgroups of Γs isomorphic
> respectively to A and B. [...] Fix a sequence (ks)s≥0 of strictly increasing
> integers. Take the wreath product ∆s = Γs ≀ Z, and mark it with generating
> tuple Ts = (τ(s), α1(s),...,α|A|(s), β1(s),...,β|B|(s)) where τ(s) = (e, +1)
> and αi(s) = (ai(s)δ0, 0), βi(s) = (bi(s)δks, 0) [...] The diagonal product ∆
> of the, possibly finite, sequence of marked groups {∆s} is the quotient group
> G/∩s ker(πs) [...] A word in T represents e∆ if and only if the same word in
> Ts represents e∆s for each s."

The product rule is "(f,i)(g,j) = (f(·)g(· − i), i + j)". Assumption 2.1
imposes "k0 = 0 and Γ0 = A(0) × B(0) ≃ A × B".

**(BZ2) Expander factors** (Example 2.3).
- The lattice: "Λ = SL(3, Fp[X]), generated by the union of the two following
  finite subgroups", with `A ≃ F_p^2` and `B ≃ Z/3Z`.
- The quotients: "the 'congruence subgroups' Λm = SL(3, Fp[X]/(X^m − 1))
  satisfy cm − c1 ≤ log|Λm| ≤ cm + c1 for all m ≥ 1".
- The groups used: "we take Γ (resp. Γm) to be the diagonal product of Λ
  (resp. Λm) with A × B".
- The Cheeger bound, from the proof of Theorem 4.6: "Since the infinite group Γ
  in Example 2.3 has Property (T), there exists a constant δ > 0 such that the
  Cheeger constants h(Γs, νs) ≥ δ for all s ≥ 1."

**(BZ3) One factor** (Lemma 4.1 and Remark 4.2, with `p = 1`). Let `q_s` be
"the uniform distribution on {τ±1} ∪ A(s) ∪ B(s) in ∆s". There is an absolute
`C > 1` with:
- "(Slow phase) for 1 ≤ v ≤ 2^(ks/2), Λp,∆s,qs(v) ≥ (log2 v)^−p / (C(|A(s)| + |B(s)|))";
- by Remark 4.2, "Λp,∆s,qs(v) ≥ h(Γs,ν)^p / (C(|A(s)| + |B(s)|) ks^p), for
  v ∈ (2^(ks/2), |Γs|^ks)".

**(BZ4) Quotients and profiles** (Section 4.1).
- "Λ1,G,φ(v) = inf{φ(∂Ω)/|Ω| : |Ω| ≤ v}", where
  "φ(∂Ω) = Σ_{x∈Ω, xy∈G\Ω} φ(y)".
- "Let H be a quotient group of G, φ̄ be the projection of φ on H, then by
  [Tes13, Proposition 4.5], for all 1 ≤ p < ∞, Λp,G,φ ≥ Λp,H,φ̄."

**(BZ5) Amenability** (Fact 2.10(i)).
> "Assume (ks) is unbounded. (i) If all the groups in the family {Γs} are
> elementary amenable (e.g. finite), then the diagonal product ∆ is also
> elementary amenable."

Example 2.3 also says: "To make sure the second part of Assumption 2.1 holds,
we take Γ (resp. Γm) to be the diagonal product of Λ (resp. Λm) with A × B."
So every family drawn from `{Γ_m}`, with `Γ_0 = A × B` and `k_0 = 0`, meets
Assumption 2.1.

## Construction

Fix a prime `p`, and `A`, `B`, `Γ_m` as in (BZ2). Let `U` be recursive and
non-decreasing, with `U >= 1`.
- Put `k_0 = 0`, `Γ_(0) = A × B`, and for `s >= 1`
  - `k_s = 2 · 3^s`;
  - `m_s` = the least `m >= 1` with `|Γ_m| > s · U(s^2 k_s)`;
  - `Γ_(s) = Γ_(m_s)`.
- `m_s` exists because `Γ_m` surjects onto `Λ_m` (it is generated by the
  diagonal images of the generators), so `|Γ_m| >= |Λ_m| >= e^(cm − c1)`.
- `m_s` is computable. `Γ_m` is an explicit subgroup of
  `SL(3, F_p[X]/(X^m − 1)) × A × B`, and its order is found by closing the
  generators under multiplication.
- `Δ = Δ_U` is the diagonal product of the marked groups
  `Δ_s = Γ_(s) wr Z` (BZ1), with generating tuple `T`. Let `S` be the set of
  `τ^(±1)` and the images of `A` and `B`, a finite symmetric generating set.

## (1) Finitely generated and elementary amenable

`Δ` is generated by `T`. `(k_s)` is unbounded, every `Γ_(s)` is finite, and
Assumption 2.1 holds. So `Δ` is elementary amenable by (BZ5).

## (2) Solvable word problem

Let `w` be a word in `T` of length `ℓ`. By (BZ1), `w = e` in `Δ` iff
`w = e` in every `Δ_s`.

**Large `s` behave alike.** Suppose `k_s > 2ℓ`. By the product rule, the
cursor of every prefix of `w` lies in `[−ℓ, ℓ]`. In `Δ_s` the image of `w` is
`(f_s, i)`, where `i` is the exponent sum of `τ`, and `f_s(x)` is the ordered
product of:
- the `a`-letters read while the cursor is at `x`;
- the `b`-letters read while the cursor is at `x − k_s`.

The first kind occur only for `x` in `[−ℓ, ℓ]`, and the second only for `x` in
`k_s + [−ℓ, ℓ]`. These ranges are disjoint. So `f_s(x)` is a word in `A(s)`, or
a word in `B(s)`. Since `A(s) ≅ A` and `B(s) ≅ B` by the fixed
identifications, `w = e` in `Δ_s` iff all of the following hold:
- `i = 0`;
- for each `x`, the `a`-letters read at cursor `x` multiply to `e` in `A`;
- for each `x`, the `b`-letters read at cursor `x` multiply to `e` in `B`.

This condition does not depend on `s`. It is the condition in
`Δ_0 = (A × B) wr Z`. The paper's Fact 2.9 states the same.

**Algorithm.**
- Compute the finitely many `s >= 1` with `k_s <= 2ℓ`.
- For each such `s`, compute `m_s` and evaluate `w` in `Γ_(m_s) wr Z` by
  explicit matrix arithmetic.
- Evaluate `w` in `(A × B) wr Z`.
- Answer "`w = e`" iff every evaluation is trivial.

Every step is uniform in an index of `U`.

## (3) The Følner function is not `≼ U`

Let `q` be uniform on `S`. For `s >= 1`, `π_s` is injective on `S`: the `α`s sit
at position `0`, the `β`s at `k_s ≠ 0`, `A(s) ≅ A` and `B(s) ≅ B`. So the
projection of `q` is `q_s`.

Put `C' = C(|A| + |B|)`, with `δ <= 1` from (BZ2).
- Every `Λ_1` is at most `1`, since `φ(∂Ω) <= |Ω|`, so `h <= 1`.
- For `v <= 2^(k_s/2)` the slow phase of (BZ3) gives
  `Λ_(1,Δ_s,q_s)(v) >= 2/(C' k_s) >= δ/(C' k_s)`.
- Remark 4.2 gives the same bound on `(2^(k_s/2), |Γ_(s)|^(k_s))`.

With (BZ4) this yields

```text
Λ_(1,Δ,q)(v)  >=  δ / (C' k_s)      for all v < |Γ_(s)|^(k_s).     (P)
```

**From left Følner sets to BZ's boundary.**
- Let `Ω` be an `n`-Følner set of `Δ` for `X = S` in the sense of the target,
  so `|Ω \ xΩ| <= |Ω|/n` for every `x in S`.
- Put `Ω' = Ω^(−1)`. For `u in S`,
  `{y in Ω' : yu ∉ Ω'} = Ω' \ Ω'u^(−1) = (Ω \ uΩ)^(−1)`, which has at most
  `|Ω|/n` elements.
- So at most `|S||Ω'|/n` points `y` of `Ω'` have some `yu ∉ Ω'`. Each such
  point contributes at most `1` to `q(∂Ω')`, and every other point contributes
  `0`. Hence `Λ_(1,Δ,q)(|Ω'|) <= q(∂Ω')/|Ω'| <= |S|/n`.
- If `n > |S| C' k_s / δ`, this is `< δ/(C' k_s)`, so by (P)
  `|Ω| >= |Γ_(s)|^(k_s) >= |Γ_(s)|`.

**Conclusion.**
- Let `N_s = ⌊|S| C' k_s / δ⌋ + 1`, and `D = |S| C'/δ + 1`, so `N_s <= D k_s`.
  Then `F_(Δ,S)(N_s) >= |Γ_(m_s)| > s U(s^2 k_s)`.
- Given `C`, take `s >= max(C, D)`. Then `C N_s <= s · s k_s`, and `U` is
  non-decreasing, so

  ```text
  F_(Δ,S)(N_s)  >  s U(s^2 k_s)  >=  C U(C N_s).
  ```

- The `N_s` are unbounded, so this holds for infinitely many `n`.
- `≼` is insensitive to the choice of finite generating set, up to changing
  `C`. So the claim holds for every finite generating set `X`. `∎`

**Note.** The constants `C` and `δ` are not computed. They are absorbed
because `m_s` is chosen against `s^2 k_s`, not against `N_s`. Only Lemma 4.1,
Remark 4.2 and the expander bound are used. Proposition 4.4 and the
approximation machinery of Appendix B are not needed, and neither is exact
equivalence to a prescribed function (Corollary 4.7).
