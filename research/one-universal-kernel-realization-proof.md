The full-radical and perfectness assertions in `(UK1)` follow from the
regular-star theorem.  Since the copies of `D` are identified pointwise, the
element `d` is the same element in every vertex group `B_i`.  Its normal
closure in `K_I` contains its normal closure inside every `B_i`, which is all
of `B_i`.  The vertex groups generate `K_I`, proving the third assertion in
`(UK1)`.

If `I` is finite, finite generating sets for its finitely many vertex groups
generate `K_I`.  Conversely, every element of a generalized free product
has a reduced normal form involving only finitely many vertex groups.  A
finite subset of `K_I` is therefore contained in the subamalgam indexed by
some finite `F subset I`.  When `I` is infinite, choose `i in I\F` and
`b in B_i\D`.  Generalized amalgam normal form gives

```text
b notin *_(D,j in F) B_j,
```

so the finite subset does not generate `K_I`.  This proves the finite-
generation criterion.

A bijection of index sets induces an isomorphism of the corresponding star
amalgams, so the isomorphism type depends only on `|I|`.  For countably
infinite `Q`, choose a bijection `Q~=N` in the kernel description `(RSA1)`;
this identifies `K_Q` with the single group `(UK2)`.  Under this
identification, the splitting action remains the left-regular permutation
of the vertex factors, giving `(UK3)`.

If `Q` is finitely generated, `C_d(Q)` is generated by one finite generating
set of `B` together with lifts of a finite generating set of `Q`.  The final
claims now follow from semantic-closure transplantation and the
finite-generation criterion.
