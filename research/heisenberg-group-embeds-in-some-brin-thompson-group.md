There is `m` such that `H_3(Z) = ⟨a, b | [a,[a,b]], [b,[a,b]]⟩` is isomorphic to
a subgroup of `mV`. Necessarily `m >= 2`, by
`thompson-v-has-no-heisenberg-subgroup`.

**Why it is the next target.** It is the smallest subgroup of `SL_3(Z)` on which
the `V` obstruction acts, and its only metric requirement is a drift-free
central commutator (`heisenberg-in-nv-forces-drift-free-central-element`). That
requirement is satisfiable in `2V` (`brin-thompson-mv-contains-a-distortion-element`).
A no here gives a no to Zaremsky 2.19(c), through
`heisenberg-group-embeds-in-no-brin-thompson-group`.

## Attempts

- 2026-09-13 (lane z2-19-glnz-q-2v, unreviewed): **counter encodings fail.**
  - Let `H_3(Z)` act on `Z^3` or `Z^2` with unit-counter coordinates stored as
    prefix lengths or chain positions in the Cantor coordinates.
  - One generator must add an unbounded multiple of one counter to another,
    e.g. `(a, b) ↦ (a + b, b)` in the affine action on `Z^2`.
  - An element of `mV` reads and writes only bounded prefixes, so it cannot do
    this in one step.
- 2026-09-13, same lane: **candidate route through reversible Turing machines.**
  - In `2V`, orbits behave like configurations of a one-tape reversible machine:
    two stacks, bounded rewrites at the tops, transfers through baker's maps.
  - The SMART machine is drift-free and, by Callard–Salo, distorted. The natural
    attempt is to find `a, b` in a group generated by reversible Turing machines
    whose commutator is a SMART-like counter commuting with both.
  - Callard and Salo (arXiv:2208.00685, p. 2) record that embedding `H_3(Z)` or
    `BS(1,2)` in the automorphism group of a full shift is open.
