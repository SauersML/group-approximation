**ESTABLISHED (written proof; see `research/fpbs-rank-one-homogeneous-bb-certificates-fail-proof.md`).**

**Why this matters for the flagship.** The only methods that prove fixed price one for groups with no infinite
commensurated amenable structure are Mellick's criterion and the Bevilacqua--Bowen criterion (arXiv:2510.05459,
Theorem 8.1):
- **Mellick** (arXiv:2307.11728, as quoted in Bevilacqua--Bowen): if a unimodular lcsc group `G` has a closed
  unimodular amenable subgroup `H` with `G` acting conservatively on `(G/H)^2`, then `G` and all its lattices have
  fixed price one. This is how higher-rank lattices are handled (`H` a split torus of `SL(3,R)`).
- **Bevilacqua--Bowen**: a countable group with an amenable, partially doubly recurrent (PDR) imp action with
  non-atomic infinite components has fixed price one.

The rank-one groups `SO(n,1)`, `SU(n,1)`, `Sp(n,1)`, `F_4^(-20)` carry the lattices where fixed price is most in
doubt: Kazhdan lattices of `Sp(n,1)` and `F_4^(-20)`, and closed hyperbolic 3-manifold groups
(`fpbs-hyperbolic-3-manifold-groups-fixed-price-one`). The natural transfer of the higher-rank method is
to look for a better amenable subgroup `H`. This claim proves that no such `H` exists, and that nothing mapping
onto a space `G/H` works either.

**Setting.** `G` is a connected simple Lie group of real rank one with finite centre. `X = G/K` is its symmetric
space, which is CAT(-1) after rescaling, with visual boundary `dX = G/P` and `P = M A_0 N` the stabilizer of a
boundary point `xi`. `b : P -> R`, `b(p) = beta_xi(p o, o)`, is the Busemann character; its kernel is `MN`, the
stabilizer of every horosphere centred at `xi`.

**Theorem A (classification).** Let `H` be a closed amenable subgroup of `G`. Then `H` is of one of three types:
- **(C)** `H` is compact;
- **(L)** `H` preserves a geodesic line of `X`;
- **(F)** `H` fixes a point `xi` of `dX`, and `b(H) != 0`.

If `H` is unimodular, type (F) collapses into (L) unless `b(H) = 0`. So every closed **unimodular** amenable
subgroup is compact, lineal, or **horospherical** (type **(Hor)**: `H <= MN_xi`, preserving each horosphere at `xi`).

The new step is the unimodular collapse. Take `a` in `H` with `b(a) != 0`. Conjugation by `a` contracts `MN`
modulo the compact group `M`. Unimodularity of `H` together with abelianness of `H/(H ∩ MN)` forces that
conjugation to preserve Haar measure on `L = H ∩ MN`. So `L` has finite Haar measure and is compact. Its fixed set
is then a convex `H`-invariant set containing the axis of `a`, and every element of `H` preserves that axis.

**Theorem B (obstruction).** Let `H` be a closed unimodular amenable subgroup, with invariant measure `m` on `G/H`.
Let `c : G/H -> Omega` be its anchor map:
- `c(gH) = g p` in `X` for type (C);
- `c(gH) = g ell` in the space of geodesic lines for type (L);
- `c(gH) = g xi` in `dX` for type (Hor).

Then:
1. For every closed subgroup `Gamma <= G` (in particular `G` itself and every lattice), the crossing set
   `D = {(x,y) : c(x) != c(y)}` of `(G/H)^2` is `Gamma`-dissipative. The failing step is an equivariant compact
   "midpoint" set in `X`:
   - the point `c(x)` itself for (C);
   - the quasi-centres of the three or four endpoints for (L);
   - the midpoint of the segment that the geodesic between the two centres cuts out between the two horospheres,
     for (Hor).
2. `m x m(D^c) = 0`, because the fibres of `c` are submanifolds of positive codimension.
3. **So Mellick's hypothesis fails for `G`.** `G` acting on `(G/H)^2` is dissipative off a null set, not
   conservative.
4. **So the Bevilacqua--Bowen hypothesis fails for every lattice.** For every lattice `Gamma` (uniform or not),
   `Gamma` acting on `G/H` is not PDR: the relation generated by `Con((G/H)^2)` lies inside the null equivalence
   relation `{c(x) = c(y)}`.
5. **Extensions.** Let `(Z, mu)` be any imp `Gamma`-space, `Gamma` a lattice, with a measurable equivariant map
   `pi : Z -> G/H`. Then `Z` is not PDR. Otherwise `{c pi(z) = c pi(z')}` would be conull, forcing the push-forward
   class of `mu` on `Omega` to be a Dirac mass at a `Gamma`-fixed point of `X`, of `dX`, or of the space of lines,
   and a lattice has none (Borel density). This covers finite-measure extensions, products with pmp actions, and
   Maharam or Poisson-type constructions built over `G/H`.

**What is killed.** Every homogeneous certificate `G/H` for fixed price one in real rank one, both for `G` (Mellick)
and for every lattice (Bevilacqua--Bowen), and every imp action of a lattice that factors onto such a `G/H`.
Consequently a BB certificate for an `Sp(n,1)` lattice or a hyperbolic 3-manifold group must be an amenable imp
action whose Zimmer boundary map is a Dirac mass at `xi(x)` (as for every amenable action) and which admits **no**
equivariant measurable map to any space `G/H` with `H` unimodular amenable. In particular its fibres over `dX`
carry no horosphere coordinate and no invariant line.
- Non-unimodular `H` (for example `H = P` or `H = A_0 N`) give no `G`-invariant measure on `G/H`, and every
  amenable action already maps to `G/P = dX`. So this is the exact boundary of Mellick's method.
- For **uniform** lattices nothing homogeneous remains. A `Gamma`-invariant Radon measure on `G/H` gives a Radon
  measure on the compact space `Gamma\G` that `H` scales by `Delta_H`. That measure is finite, so `Delta_H = 1`
  and Theorem B.5 applies. This covers closed hyperbolic 3-manifold groups and cocompact `Sp(n,1)` lattices.
- For **non-uniform** lattices, the one homogeneous case not covered is an infinite `Gamma`-invariant Radon
  measure on `G/H` for a non-unimodular focal `H` that is not cocompact in `P`.

**Calibration.**
- `SL(3,R)` with `H` the diagonal torus satisfies Mellick's hypothesis. The proof uses rank one at three places,
  all false there:
  - the minimal set of a hyperbolic element is a single line;
  - three distinct boundary points have a compact quasi-centre;
  - a horosphere meets a geodesic from its centre in one point.
  Maximal flats in general position have no compact equivariant centre.
- `SO(2,1)` lattices (surface groups, virtually free groups) have cost above one, so every certificate must fail;
  the theorem agrees.
- Amenable `G` (not simple) is excluded: there `H = G` is a valid certificate.

**Novelty status.** Neither Mellick (arXiv:2307.11728, whose Theorem 1.2 reaches rank one only in products with
p-adic groups) nor Bevilacqua--Bowen states that rank-one groups admit no admissible `H`. As far as this graph
records, the classification of unimodular amenable subgroups into (C), (L), (Hor), and the resulting uniform
failure for all lattices including non-uniform ones, are new here. A referee should check this against the
literature.
