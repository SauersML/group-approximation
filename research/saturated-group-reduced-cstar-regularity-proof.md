## Why sufficient

`Q` is countable, so `A=C*_r(Q)` is separable, and it is unital and
generated by the two canonical unitaries of a two-generating set.

`Q` is torsion-free, so its finite radical `K(Q)` is trivial, and it is
acylindrically hyperbolic.  Dahmani--Guirardel--Osin, Theorem 2.35, together
with Breuillard--Kalantar--Kennedy--Ozawa, Theorem 1.4, then make `A` simple
with a unique tracial state; this is the chain used by Gerasimova--Osin,
arXiv:1910.14524, in the sentence preceding their main theorem.  Their
Theorem 1.1 states that acylindrically hyperbolic `G_1,...,G_k` with
`K(G_i)=1` satisfy `sr(C*_r(G_1 x ... x G_k))=1`; the case `k=1` gives
stable rank one for `A`.

The canonical trace on `A` is faithful, so `A` is stably finite.

If `A` were MF it would embed in a norm matrix corona, and the embedding may
be taken unital because the image of `1` lifts to a sequence of projections
and the corresponding corner is again a norm matrix corona.  The canonical
unitaries would then give an injective homomorphism from `Q` into the unitary
group of that corona, making `Q` an MF group, against `Res_MF(Q)=Q`.  This is
the manuscript's reduced `C*` consequence, applied to `Q` in place of the
binary Leavitt group.
