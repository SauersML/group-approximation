This proof is complete. It uses only the *statements* of Choi–Seo's
Definitions 2.11 and 2.13 and Theorem 2.17 (arXiv:2508.08932v2, PDF
pp. 13–16), and not the truth of any of them. The TeX line numbers are
recorded in `research/artifacts/fpbs-acylindrical-l2-gap-2026-09-16.md`.

> **Definition 2.11.** B is a dS-barrier between A and C if every dS-path
> (g0,...,gn) with g0∈A and gn∈C has some gi∈B.

> **Definition 2.13.** B⊆G is r-roughly branching if there is B′⊆G with
> (1) B ⊆ r-neighborhood of B′; (2) for every k≥1, if g1,...,gk and
> h1,...,hk are distinct sequences of elements of B′, then g1···gk ≠ h1···hk.

> **Theorem 2.17 (hypothesis).** There is r>0 and, for each D,E>0, sets
> S_D = ⊔_{i≥1} S_{D;i}, G_{D,E} and a family H_D such that (1) S_D is
> r′_D-roughly branching; (2) for each H∈H_D there is an r-roughly branching
> B = B1⊔...⊔BD with each Bi a dS-barrier between id and H; (3) ⊔_{i≥E}
> S_{D;i} is a dS-barrier between id and G_{D,E}. Moreover for each ϵ,D,E
> there is N = N(ϵ,D,E) such that every finite A⊆G has A′⊆A with
> #A′ ≥ (1−ϵ)#A and, for each a∈A′, some H1,H2∈H_D with
> #(A \ a(H1∪H2∪G_{D,E})) ≤ N.

(These are condensed from the verbatim quotations in the wave-19 route for
the normal-cyclic case, which carries them word for word; the quantifier order
is unchanged, and in particular `r` is chosen before `D`.)

**Notation.** `|g| = ||g||_S`, `B(ρ)` is the `ρ`-ball about `id`, and
`N_ρ(X) = X·B(ρ)`. `K ◁ G`, and `K_0 ≤ K` is infinite and generated by a
finite set `T_0`. Put `c = max_{τ ∈ T_0} |τ|`. For `t ∈ G`, let
`φ_t(v) = t v t^(-1)`, an automorphism of `K`. Free subsets and `b(X)` are as
in the claim.

## Step 1. The twisted product identity

For `v_1, …, v_k ∈ K` and `t ∈ G`,

```text
(v_1 t)(v_2 t) ⋯ (v_k t) = v_1 φ_t(v_2) φ_t^2(v_3) ⋯ φ_t^(k-1)(v_k) · t^k.     (1)
```

The proof is by induction on `k`. Given (1) for `k`, multiply on the right by
`v_{k+1} t` and use `t^k v_{k+1} = φ_t^k(v_{k+1}) t^k`. ∎

So if `Y = {v_1 t, …, v_n t} ⊆ Kt` is free, then for each `k` the `n^k`
elements `w(i_1,…,i_k) = v_{i_1} φ_t(v_{i_2}) ⋯ φ_t^(k-1)(v_{i_k})` of `K` are
pairwise distinct. The factor `t^k` is common to all of them and cancels.

## Step 2. Bounds on `b(Kt)`

**Lemma A (virtually nilpotent `K`).** Let `K` be generated by a finite set
`T`, with word length `|·|_K` and growth `γ_K(m) = #{v : |v|_K <= m} <= C m^d`
for `m >= 1`, which holds for virtually nilpotent `K` by Bass–Guivarc'h. Put
`L = max(1, max_{τ ∈ T} |φ_t(τ)|_K)`. Then `b(Kt) <= L^d`.

*Proof.* `φ_t` is a homomorphism, so `|φ_t(v)|_K <= L |v|_K`, and
`|φ_t^j(v)|_K <= L^j |v|_K`. Let `Y = {v_i t}` be free with `n` elements, and
put `R = max(1, max_i |v_i|_K)`. Every `w(i_1,…,i_k)` has
`|w|_K <= R(1 + L + ⋯ + L^(k-1)) <= R k L^k`. Step 1 gives
`n^k <= γ_K(R k L^k) <= C (R k)^d L^(dk)`. Take `k`-th roots and let
`k → ∞`: `n <= L^d`. The bound does not depend on `Y`. ∎

For `K = Z` this gives `L = 1` and `b = 1`, which recovers wave-19 Lemma 1.

**Lemma B (inner action, no free subsemigroup).** Suppose `t = κ c` with
`κ ∈ K` and `c ∈ C_G(K)`, and suppose `K` has no free subsemigroup of rank 2.
Then `b(Kt) = 1`. (`b >= 1`, since a singleton is free.)

*Proof.* `Kt = Kc`, so the elements of `Y ⊆ Kt` are `u_i c` with `u_i ∈ K`.
Since `c` centralises `K`, `(u_{i_1} c) ⋯ (u_{i_k} c) = u_{i_1} ⋯ u_{i_k} c^k`.
So `Y` is free iff `U = {u_i}` satisfies: distinct equal-length sequences
from `U` have distinct products in `K`.

Suppose `#U >= 2`, and take letters `x ≠ y` in `U`. We show that some two
distinct equal-length positive words in `x, y` are equal in `K`, which
contradicts freeness.

`K` has no free subsemigroup, so some two distinct positive words `W_1 ≠ W_2`
in `x, y` are equal in `K`. If `|W_1| = |W_2|`, we are done. Otherwise say
`|W_1| < |W_2|`.

* The words `W_1 W_2` and `W_2 W_1` have equal length, and in `K` both equal
  `W_1^2`. If they differ as words, we are done.
* Otherwise `W_1 W_2 ≡ W_2 W_1` as words. By Lyndon–Schützenberger, both are
  powers of one word `Z`: `W_1 ≡ Z^a` and `W_2 ≡ Z^b` with `a < b`.
  Cancelling in the group gives `Z^m = 1` in `K`, where `m = b - a >= 1`.
  Choose a letter `u ∈ {x, y}` such that `Z` is not a power of the one-letter
  word `u`. Such a `u` exists: `Z` is nonempty, and it cannot be both a power
  of `x` and a power of `y`. The words `Z^m u` and `u Z^m` have equal length,
  and in `K` both equal `u`. They differ as words, because if
  `Z^m u ≡ u Z^m`, Lyndon–Schützenberger again makes `Z^m` a power of the
  primitive word `u`, so `Z` is a power of `u`, a contradiction.

So `#U <= 1`. ∎

**Lemma C (calibration: free subsemigroups give infinite capacity).** If
`x, y ∈ K` freely generate a free subsemigroup, then
`U_n = {x y^i : 0 <= i < n}` is free for every `n`, so `b(K) = ∞`.

*Proof.* Two sequences with equal products give the same positive word in
`x, y`, by freeness. That word parses uniquely into blocks `x y^i`: each block
starts at an occurrence of `x` and runs to the next one. So the sequences
coincide. ∎

## Step 3. A sharp example: `b = 2` in `Z^2 x|_M Z`

Let `M = [[2,1],[1,1]]` and let `t` act on `K = Z^2` by `M`. By (1), in
additive notation the `K`-part of a product of `v_{i_1} t, …, v_{i_k} t` is
`Σ_{j<k} M^j v_{i_{j+1}}`.

*Pairs are free.* Two sequences from `{v_1 t, v_2 t}` with equal products
give `p(M) δ = 0`, where `δ = v_1 - v_2 ≠ 0` and `p = Σ e_j x^j ≠ 0` has
`e_j ∈ {-1,0,1}`. So `p(M)` is singular and `p` vanishes at an eigenvalue of
`M`. The eigenvalues are the roots of the irreducible `x^2 - 3x + 1`, so `p`
vanishes at `λ = (3+√5)/2`. Removing a power of `x` leaves
`±x^n + Σ_{j<n} e_j x^j` with the same nonzero roots. For `|x| >= 2` this
has no root, since `|x|^n > (|x|^n - 1)/(|x| - 1) >= |Σ_{j<n} e_j x^j|`. But
`λ > 2`, a contradiction. So `b(Z^2 t) >= 2`.

*Triples are not free.* Let `ℓ_+` and `ℓ_-` be left eigenvectors for `λ` and
`1/λ`. The map `v ↦ (ℓ_+(v), ℓ_-(v))` takes `Z^2` injectively onto a lattice
`Λ ⊂ R^2`. The `K`-parts of length-`k` products from a finite `Y` satisfy
`|ℓ_+| <= A λ^k` and `|ℓ_-| <= A/(1 - 1/λ)`. A box of area `O(λ^k)` holds
`O(λ^k)` points of `Λ`, since balls of radius half the minimal norm of `Λ`
about them are disjoint. So a free `Y` of size `n` has `n^k = O(λ^k)`, hence
`n <= λ < 3`, and `b(Z^2 t) = 2` exactly.

`check_free_capacity.py` confirms both halves. Five pairs are free through
`k = 16`. Four triples collide, at `k = 2, 4, 4, 5`. For `{0, e_1, e_2}`
the number of distinct `K`-parts is the Fibonacci number `F_(2k+2)`, whose
ratio to `λ^k` stays at `1.17`.

The script also checks that central twists give `b = 1` (`Z^2` and
Heisenberg pairs collide at `k = 2` or `4`), and it checks Lemma C in
`BS(1,2)` through the tested lengths.

This example shows the wave-19 core lemma `#(B' ∩ Kt) <= 1` is false beyond
the cyclic case. Only the counting bound survives, and it is all Step 4
needs.

## Step 4. Roughly-branching sets meet `N_ρ(K)` boundedly

**Lemma 2′.** Suppose `b(Kt) < ∞` for every `t`. If `B` is `r`-roughly
branching and `ρ >= 0`, then

```text
#(B ∩ N_ρ(K)) <= Q(r,ρ) := #B(r) · Σ_{t ∈ B(r+ρ)} b(Kt) < ∞.
```

*Proof.* Take `B'` as in Definition 2.13. Every finite subset of `B' ∩ Kt`
is free, so `#(B' ∩ Kt) <= b(Kt)`.

Let `β ∈ B ∩ N_ρ(K)`, and write `β = κ u` with `κ ∈ K` and `|u| <= ρ`. Pick
`β' = β v ∈ B'` with `|v| <= r`. Then `β' = κ (uv) ∈ K t` with
`t = uv ∈ B(r+ρ)`. So every such `β'` lies in `∪_{t ∈ B(r+ρ)} (B' ∩ Kt)`,
which has at most `Σ_{t ∈ B(r+ρ)} b(Kt)` points. Each `β'` is within `r` of
at most `#B(r)` points `β`. ∎

**Lemma 3′ (fibre paths).** For every `κ ∈ K_0` there is a `d_S`-path from
`id` to `κ` whose vertices all lie in `N_c(K_0) ⊆ N_c(K)`.

*Proof.* Write `κ = τ_1 ⋯ τ_m` with `τ_j ∈ T_0^(±1)`, and write each `τ_j` as
an `S`-word of length at most `c`. Concatenate these words. Each vertex has
the form `τ_1 ⋯ τ_{j-1} · s`, where `s` is a prefix of the word for `τ_j`,
so it lies within `c` of `τ_1 ⋯ τ_{j-1} ∈ K_0`. ∎

## Step 5. The hypothesis of Theorem 2.17 fails

Assume `b(Kt) < ∞` for every `t`. Suppose `r` and families
`S_D, G_(D,E), H_D` satisfy conditions (1)–(3). We show the magic-lemma
condition fails. The argument follows wave-19 Steps 2a–2c, with `Z`
replaced by `K_0` and the core lemma replaced by Lemma 2′.

**(5a) No `H ∈ H_D` meets `K_0` once `D > Q(r,c)`.** Suppose `κ ∈ H ∩ K_0`.
Condition (2) gives an `r`-roughly branching `B = B_1 ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ B_D` in which
every `B_i` is a barrier between `id` and `H`. The fibre path of Lemma 3′
from `id` to `κ` meets every `B_i`. It lies in `N_c(K)`, and the `B_i` are
disjoint, so `#(B ∩ N_c(K)) >= D`. By Lemma 2′, `D <= Q(r,c)`. The bound
`Q(r,c)` does not depend on `D`, because `r` is fixed first.

**(5b) `G_(D,E) ∩ K_0 = ∅` once `E >= E_0(D)`.** By condition (1) and
Lemma 2′, `#(S_D ∩ N_c(K)) <= Q(r'_D, c) < ∞`. The pieces `S_(D;i)` are
disjoint, so only finitely many of them meet `N_c(K)`. Let `E_0(D)` exceed
all their indices. For `E >= E_0(D)`, the tail `⊔_(i>=E) S_(D;i)` misses
`N_c(K)`. By condition (3) it is a barrier between `id` and `G_(D,E)`, but
the fibre path from `id` to a point of `G_(D,E) ∩ K_0` would avoid it. So
there is no such point.

**(5c) Contradiction.** Take `ε = 1/2`, `D > Q(r,c)` and `E >= E_0(D)`, and
let `N = N(1/2, D, E)`. Since `K_0` is infinite, we can choose `A ⊆ K_0` with
`#A = N + 1`. For `a ∈ A` and any `H_1, H_2 ∈ H_D`, we have `a^(-1) A ⊆ K_0`,
which misses `H_1 ∪ H_2 ∪ G_(D,E)` by (5a) and (5b). So

```text
#(A \ a(H_1 ∪ H_2 ∪ G_(D,E))) = #(a^(-1)A \ (H_1 ∪ H_2 ∪ G_(D,E))) = N + 1 > N.
```

Then `A' = ∅`, but the condition requires `#A' >= #A/2 > 0`. ∎

As in wave 19, the argument uses only `A' ≠ ∅`, so even the weakened "some
`a ∈ A`" form of the condition fails.

## Step 6. The listed classes

* **(a)** If `K` is finitely generated, infinite and virtually nilpotent,
  take `K_0 = K`. Lemma A gives `b(Kt) <= L_t^d < ∞` for every `t`.
  Examples:
  - `Z^d x| Q` for any `Q`, and so `Z^2 x| SL(2,Z)` and `Z^2 x| F_2`;
  - `H_3(Z) x| F_2`, for any action;
  - any `G` with an infinite finitely generated virtually nilpotent normal
    subgroup.

  None of these groups is acylindrically hyperbolic, since their amenable
  radical is infinite.
* **(b)** If `G = K · C_G(K)`, `K` contains an infinite finitely generated
  `K_0`, and `K` has no rank-2 free subsemigroup, then Lemma B gives
  `b(Kt) = 1` for every `t`. This holds for `G = H x K`, where
  `C_G(K) ⊇ H`, with `K` of any of these kinds:
  - infinite torsion `K`, where `x^n = 1` gives `x^(n+1) = x`, so there is
    no free subsemigroup;
  - `K` of subexponential growth, since a free subsemigroup forces
    exponential growth.

  In particular, `B(m,n) x B(m',n')` and `T x T'` are covered. These are the
  explicit members of `fpbs-residual-price-one-non-ah-class-nonuniqueness`
  listed in `fpbs-residual-class-has-torsion-products`.
* **Boundary.** Lemma C shows `b = ∞` as soon as `K` has a free subsemigroup,
  so neither Lemma 2′ nor Step 5 applies to `F_2 x F_2`, `F_2 x BS(1,2)` or
  `F_2 x (Z/2 wr Z)`.

  For finitely generated elementary amenable `K`, not being virtually
  nilpotent already forces a free subsemigroup (Rosenblatt 1974 for solvable
  groups, Chou 1980 in general). So under (b) the amenable part of the
  boundary is exactly the non-virtually-nilpotent elementary amenable
  factors.

  Locally finite normal subgroups have no infinite finitely generated
  subgroup, so Lemma 3′ is unavailable there too. Paths within a bounded
  neighbourhood reach only a bounded finite subgroup, while `A` in (5c) must
  grow with `N(ε,D,E)`.

## What is not proved

* Nothing about Theorem 2.9. The set-family obstruction exists only for
  infinite centre (`fpbs-cs-set-family-hypothesis-fails-infinite-centre`).
  For `H x K` with `K` torsion, the witness `A ⊆ {1} x K` does not obstruct
  Theorem 2.9, since any `h` with nontrivial `H`-coordinate conjugates
  outside `A A^(-1)`.
* Nothing about `p_c` or `p_u`. Choi–Seo's Theorem 2.17 is one sufficient
  condition for (Γ2), and other routes are untouched.
