## Why sufficient

Let `sigma : H -> U(s)` be a unitary representation with
`(C^s)^{iota(Gamma)} = 0`, and put `V_n = sigma^{oplus n}` on dimension
`s n`.  The dimensions increase, and `V_n` is an exact homomorphism, so its
multiplicative defect vanishes identically and `V` is an operator-norm
asymptotic representation — a corona model in any of the equivalent
formulations of `countable-group-mf-conventions`.

Its coordinate ultraproduct is `prod_omega C^{s n}`, and the
`iota(Gamma)`-action on it is the ultraproduct of `sigma^{oplus n}|`.  A
vector fixed by `iota(Gamma)` is represented by a sequence `(x_n)` with
`‖sigma^{oplus n}(iota gamma) x_n - x_n‖ -> 0` for each `gamma`.  Since
`sigma` has no invariant vectors and `iota(Gamma)` is generated by finitely
many elements for the purposes of a Kazhdan pair, the operator
`(1/|S|) sum_{gamma in S} sigma(iota gamma)` has norm bounded by some
`1 - eta < 1` on each summand, uniformly in `n` because the summand is
always the same finite-dimensional `sigma`.  Hence
`‖x_n‖ <= (1 - eta)^{-1}` forces `x_n -> 0`, and the invariant subspace is
zero.  So `P = 0`, the subspace generated by `ran P` is zero, and
`kazhdan-invariant-subspace-generates` fails for this model.

The restriction stated in the claim follows: the demand can only be made of
corona models satisfying some further hypothesis, and detecting the mark is
the hypothesis the route actually needs, since a model with `Theta(z) = 1`
is harmless to the route no matter what its invariant subspace does.
