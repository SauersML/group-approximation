**OPEN.** Let `k` be a field, `D = k(<x_0, x_1>)` the free skew field, and `r >= 1`. Every commutative subfield
`F ⊆ M_r(D)` containing `k · 1` has `trdeg_k F <= 1`.

**Known.**
- `r = 1` holds: the centralizer of a non-scalar element of `D` has transcendence degree 1. This is Cohn 1978
  with Schofield 1985, Thm 11.6, as quoted in `leavitt-nonnegative-part-is-matrix-union-over-free-algebra-proof`
  §4.
- The same bound holds in the free algebra: by Bergman's centralizer theorem, centralizers there are `k[t]`.

**Equivalent form.** A subfield `F ⊆ M_r(D) = End_D(D^r)` is the same as an `F`-`D`-bimodule structure on `D^r`.
So the claim says the following, where `x, y` are central in `D[x, y]`:
- every `D[x, y]`-module of finite `D`-dimension on which all of `k[x, y] ∖ 0` acts invertibly is zero.

Two remarks on this form.
- Sides can be switched, since `D^op ≅ D`.
- By Schur's lemma on a simple submodule, it is equivalent to this: some nonzero element of `k[x, y]` annihilates
  a nonzero submodule of every nonzero `D[x, y]`-module of finite `D`-dimension.

For a commutative field `E` in place of `D`, the analogous bound is `trdeg F <= trdeg_k E`. That bound is useless
here, because `D` contains free fields on infinitely many generators. So any proof must use the free,
noncommutative structure of `D`, as the `r = 1` centralizer theorem does.

**Use.** Together with `leavitt-polynomial-pairs-move-to-the-nonnegative-part`, this gives
`leavitt-algebras-contain-no-two-variable-polynomial-ring` for all fields
(route `leavitt-rank-one-from-free-field-matrices-and-degree-reduction`).

Only the subfields generated by images of `M_(2^N)(k<x_0, x_1>)` under the minimal-rank restriction are needed.
So a proof for matrix rings over the free algebra `P` would also suffice.

## Attempts

**w16: small `r` by hand (not a proof).**
- For `r = 2` and `A = [[0, 1], [u, 0]]` with `u ∈ D ∖ k`, we have `A^2 = u · 1`.
- The commutant of `A` is `{[[p, q], [q u, p]] : p, q ∈ C_D(u)}`, which is `C_D(u)[A]`, of transcendence degree 1.
- So companion-type constructions over one centralizer do not produce transcendence degree 2.
- A general `r = 2` argument would need to control `F`-`D`-bimodules of `D`-dimension 2 on which no subfield of
  `F` of transcendence degree 1 fixes a `D`-line. A fixed line puts that subfield inside `D`.
