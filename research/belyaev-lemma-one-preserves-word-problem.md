**OPEN.** Let `B` be a finitely generated associative `K`-algebra with solvable
word problem over a decidable field `K`, let `A ≤ B` be a finitely generated
subalgebra that is a `K`-module direct summand of `B`, and let `φ : A → B` be a
`K`-algebra homomorphism given computably on generators (`φ(a_i) = b_i`, with
`b_i` explicit elements of `B`). Then the overalgebra produced by Belyaev's
Lemma 1 / Remark 2, in which the system `Hom_n(ā, b̄, θ̄)` becomes solvable, has
solvable word problem.

Concretely (Lemma 1's `K[X]/I` form) the overalgebra is
`A_1 = K[X ∪ {x, y, z, β_1, …, β_n}] / I_1`, with `I_1` generated by `I` and the
families

- `x w z β y - φ[w β(ā)]` for `w ∈ L[X] ∪ {∅}`, `β ∈ L[β_1, …, β_n] ∪ {∅}`,
  `w β ≠ ∅`;
- `a_i z - z β_i`;
- `x z β_i β_j - b_i x z β_j`.

The first family is infinite, so `A_1` is not finitely presented over `B`; the
claim is only that its word problem stays solvable.

**Why it is a gap and why it is plausible.** Belyaev proves `I_1 ∩ K[X] = I`
(faithfulness) by a rank induction on the depth of `x … y` parenthesis pairs.
Read as a rewriting system — `x w z β y → φ[w β(ā)]`, `a_i z → z β_i`,
`x z β_i β_j → b_i x z β_j` — that induction is a confluence-flavoured argument,
and `φ[w β(ā)]` is computable when `φ` is computable and `B` is decidable. So a
normal form, hence a decision procedure, is expected. What is not done here is a
complete termination-and-confluence proof of this specific infinite system, in
particular the overlaps between the three rules. Until that is supplied, the
claim stands open.

**Consumers.** The embedding theorem applies Lemma 1 three times (systems (2),
(4), (6) in the proof), and Lemma 3 applies it once more; each application must
preserve solvable word problem. This claim states exactly that preservation.
See `decidable-clapham-via-effective-belyaev`.
