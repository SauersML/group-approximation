**ESTABLISHED.** Let `P = <a, b | b^-1 a^2 b = a^-2, a^-1 b^2 a = b^-2>` be the
Promislow group, with `x = a^2`, `y = b^2`, `z = (ab)^2` as in
`promislow-group-ring-units-are-constant-reduced-norm`. For a commutative ring
`R` let

    D(R) = R[t^(+-1)] <u | u^2 = -1, u f(t) = f(t^-1) u>,

a free `R`-module with basis `t^m, t^m u` (`m in Z`). Write `fbar(t) = f(t^-1)`
and, for `X = l_0 + l_1 u` with `l_0, l_1 in R[t^(+-1)]`,

    N(X) = l_0 l_0bar + l_1 l_1bar.

There are three ring homomorphisms `R[P] -> D(R)`, the **definite shadows**:

| shadow | `a ->` | `b ->`      | `x, y, z ->`    | kernel               |
|--------|--------|-------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| `C`    | `u`    | `-t^-1 u`   | `-1, -1, t^2`   | `I_C = (x+1, y+1)`   |
| `A`    | `t`    | `u`         | `t^2, -1, -1`   | `I_A = (y+1, z+1)`   |
| `B`    | `u`    | `t`         | `-1, t^2, -1`   | `I_B = (x+1, z+1)`   |

Each is surjective, and its kernel is the two-sided ideal generated by the
displayed pair.

1. **Integral units.** If `v` is a unit of `Z[P]`, its image under each shadow
   is `+-t^m` or `+-t^m u`. Equivalently, for `i = A, B, C` there are
   `epsilon_i in {+-1}` and `g_i in P` with `v - epsilon_i g_i in I_i`.
2. **Rational units.** Every unit of `Q[P]` maps under each shadow to
   `lambda t^m` or `lambda t^m u`, with `lambda in Q^x`.
3. **Trivial residues.** If `v in Z[P]^x` and `v = 1 mod 2Z[P]`, then
   `v in epsilon + (I_A cap I_B cap I_C)` for a single sign `epsilon`.
4. **Lifting obstruction.** Let `d` be a prime and `w in F_d[P]`. If some shadow
   of `w` in `D(F_d)` is not of the form `+-t^m` or `+-t^m u`, then no unit of
   `Z[P]` reduces to `w` modulo `d`. Nothing is assumed about the support, word
   radius or coefficient height of the integral unit.
5. **Failure over `Z[zeta_8]`.** Gardam's unit of `Z[zeta_8][P]`
   (`unit-conjecture-fails-over-complex-numbers`) has images of norm `N = 1`
   that are not monomials under all three shadows, with 7, 10 and 10 terms
   (computed exactly in the artifact).

**What the proof uses.** The constant term of `N(X)` over `Z` is
`sum_m (l_(0,m)^2 + l_(1,m)^2)`, a sum of squares. Tabei (arXiv:2608.02982v1,
Section 6.1) observes that an argument using only reduction modulo 2, reduction
modulo 3 and embedding into `C` cannot prove Higman's conjecture for `P`,
because `Z[zeta_8]` passes all three. The input here is positivity, which
`Z[zeta_8]` lacks, as item 5 shows.

**The quotients.** `D(R)` is the twisted group ring of `P/<x, y> = C_2 * C_2`
in which both involutions square to `-1`. Over `Q(t)` it is the cyclic algebra
`(Q(t)/Q(t + t^-1), t -> t^-1, -1)`, whose reduced norm is `N`. On the lines
where `a^2` or `b^2` goes to `+1` instead, the norm form is
`l_0 l_0bar - l_1 l_1bar`, which is indefinite, and item 1 has no analogue.

**Credit.** The argument is a relator check, a basis count and a sum of squares.
No novelty is claimed. Literature was not searched beyond Gardam
(arXiv:2312.05240), Tabei (arXiv:2608.02982, arXiv:2608.00103) and the repo;
Craven--Pappas (J. Algebra 394, 2013) was not read.

**Scope.** This proves no triviality. A unit is constrained only modulo each
`I_i`. Units in `+-g + (I_A cap I_B cap I_C)` are not reached; that ideal
contains `(x+1)(y+1)(z+1)`. The consequences for the known nontrivial residues
are recorded in `promislow-classical-nontrivial-units-have-no-integral-lifts`.
