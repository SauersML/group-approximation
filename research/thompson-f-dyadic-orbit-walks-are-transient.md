**ESTABLISHED by citation.** Let `D` be the set of dyadic rationals in `(0,1)`, with the standard action of `F`.
Let `ρ` be any probability measure on `F` such that the semigroup generated by `supp ρ` is all of `F`. Then the
Markov chain on `D` with transitions `x ↦ h(x)`, `h ~ ρ` (that is, `P(x,y) = ρ{h : h(x) = y}`), is transient.
No moment, support or symmetry condition is imposed.

**Source.** V. A. Kaimanovich, *Thompson's group F is not Liouville*, arXiv:1602.02971v2 (Groups, Graphs and
Random Walks, CUP 2017), §3.C, Theorem 23, verbatim:

> Theorem 23. Let µ be a strictly non-degenerate probability measure on the group F̃. Then the random walk (Γ, µ)
> on Γ with the transitions γ ⇝ γ.g (µ(g)) induced by the random walk (F̃, µ) is transient.

"Strictly non-degenerate" is defined in §1.F as `sgr µ = G` (the semigroup generated by the support). The proof
is the comparison criterion of Woess (*Random Walks on Infinite Graphs and Groups*, Theorem 3.1): the induced walk
preserves counting measure and is uniformly irreducible, and the simple random walk on `Γ` is transient
(Theorem 21 there).

**Transfer to the unit interval.** In the source, `F̃ ⊂ PL_F(R)` is the image of `F` under the change of coordinates
`τ` of §2.C, and Remark 12 there states that `τ` is a bijection from `Z[1/2] ∩ (0,1)` onto `Z[1/2]`. The vertex set
of `Γ` is `Z[1/2]`, and `γ.g = g(γ)` (formula (18)). Conjugation by `τ` is an isomorphism `F → F̃` intertwining the
action on `D` with the action on `Γ`. The semigroup generated by a support is the same set whether products are
written in prefix or postfix order. So the statement above is the source theorem transported by `τ`.
