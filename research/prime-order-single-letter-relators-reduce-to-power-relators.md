**ESTABLISHED** by `prime-order-single-letter-relators-reduction-proof` (elementary; no
novelty claimed, and not independently reviewed).

**Statement.** Let `A` and `H` be groups, `α ∈ A` of prime order `p`, and `W ∈ H`. Put
`G = (A * H)/<<αW>>` and `R = H/<<W^p>>`.
1. If the image of `W` in `R` has order `p`, then `G ≅ A *_{α = W^{-1}} R`, so `A` and
   `R` embed in `G`.
2. If the image of `W` in `R` is trivial, then `G ≅ (A/<<α>>) * R`.
3. So `G` is trivial exactly when `<<α>>_A = A` and `<<W^p>>_H = H`.

**Application.** A free product `A * B * C` of nontrivial groups is normally generated by
an element with exactly one `A`-letter `α` of prime order `p` (up to conjugacy) exactly
when `A = <<α>>` and `B * C` is the normal closure of `W^p`, where `W ∈ B * C` is the rest
of the relator. For `A_5 * A_5 * A_5`, every letter has order 2, 3 or 5, so a
single-letter factor reduces the question to `A_5 * A_5 = <<W^p>>` with `p ∈ {2,3,5}`.
Chinyere–Howie use a version of this reduction, with a length bound, in their proof of
`short-relator-three-factor-one-relator-products-are-nontrivial` (arXiv:1509.02717,
l.436–440).
