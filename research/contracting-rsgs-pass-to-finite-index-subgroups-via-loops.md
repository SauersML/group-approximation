## Statement

Let `G ≤ R_{Γ,E}` be a contracting RSG (BBMZ arXiv:2309.06224v3, Defs. 2.32, 2.41), with irreducible core `Γ_0`. Let
`Γ' ≤ G` have finite index.
- **Labels.** Fix, for every vertex `u` of `Γ` that is the type of a proper cone in `E`, a base cone `[α_u] ⊊ E`. For
  every edge `e : u → u'`, fix `k_e ∈ G` restricting to the canonical similarity `[α_{u'}] → [α_u e]`.
- **Path labels.** Every path `β` from a root cone gets a label `ℓ_β ∈ G`, the product of the `k_e` along it after a root
  label. `ℓ_β` restricts to the canonical similarity `[α_{t(β)}] → [β]`.
- **The loop group.** For a core vertex `v`, `Λ_v ≤ G` is the group generated by the labels of closed walks at `v`.

Then:
1. On the coset-refined graph `Γ̃ = Γ × (Γ'∖G)`, with edges `(u, c) → (u', c·k_e)`, the group `Γ'` is an RSG with finite
   nucleus. Its nucleus is contained in `Nuc_G × (Γ'∖G)²`. This part needs **no condition**.
2. `Γ'` is a contracting RSG there **iff (T)**: all reachable cosets `Γ'ℓ_β` with `t(β) = v` lie in one `Λ_v`-orbit.
3. **(T) holds whenever `Γ'Λ_v = G`**, in particular when `Λ_v = G`. In that case every finite-index subgroup of `G` is a
   contracting RSG, and by `contracting-rsgs-closed-under-finite-index-overgroups` so is every group commensurable with
   `G`.

## Instances

- **Hyperbolic RACGs with spread nerves.** `Λ = W` always holds there: loops along the complement graph give `W⁺`, and
  an odd loop exists (see `hyperbolic-racgs-with-spread-nerves-are-contracting-rsgs`, repaired Step 6). So the
  commensurability condition (c) of that node is not needed.

## What this does and does not give for Question 1.1

- **It gives:** a positive answer for `G` passes to every group commensurable with `G` whenever the loop labels of one
  contracting coding generate `G`.
- **It does not give** Question 1.1 for all cubulated hyperbolic groups via Agol. Virtual specialness puts a finite-index
  subgroup of `G` into a right-angled Artin or Coxeter group as a quasiconvex subgroup of **infinite** index, and the
  ambient group is usually not hyperbolic. Closure under finite-index subgroups does not reach that.
- **The gap is quasiconvex subgroups.** For those the relevant partial result is
  `convex-cores-inherit-greedy-coding-na`: (NA) passes to convex cores, and only (IRR) remains.

## Lesson for general BH

- **What goes down to finite-index subgroups.** The RSG property and a finite nucleus always do, by labelling cones with
  a cocycle and refining by cosets.
- **What can fail.** Only irreducibility can, and exactly when the loop group of the coding misses cosets. A parity or
  grading that every loop respects is the typical obstruction; an odd loop kills a parity.
- **So finite index is a monodromy question.** Passing contracting RSGs to finite-index subgroups is a question about
  the monodromy of one finite graph, not about the group.
