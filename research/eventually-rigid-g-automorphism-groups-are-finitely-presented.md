**OPEN.** This is the single gate between main and the permutational Boone–Higman property for graph Houghton groups
(and, via the trust notes on `graph-houghton-groups-embed-in-eventually-rigid-g-automorphisms`, for surface and
braided Houghton groups).

## Structure

Let K ≤ P_G(r) be the elements whose germ is finitary. Then:
- K is normal, and P_G(r) = K · H_r with K ∩ H_r = FSym(X), so P_G(r)/K ≅ Z^{r−1};
- K is the directed union, over finite E, of the groups A_E generated by Aut_G(G * F(E)) and the slides
  slide_j^E(G * F(E)).

So P_G(r) is a Houghton-type extension of a direct limit of finitely presented groups. Each A_E is a product of
Aut_G(G * F(E)) with the slide groups (G * F(E))^r. Aut_G(G * F(E)) is finitely presented by BFFHZ `prop:fp` (Carette,
G finitely presented and centerless). This is the same shape as HKUW's PB_r, with Aut(F_n) replaced by
Aut_G(G * F_n).

## Attempt 1 (bh-graph-houghton, 09-18): a sufficient condition (lane sketch, not a proof)

**(BSP) Bounded-support presentations.** There is a k such that for every finite E, Aut_G(G * F(E)) has a
presentation with these properties:
- the generators are FSym(E)-relabelings of the finitely many local generators from the finite-generation list;
- the relators are FSym(E)-relabelings of finitely many relator shapes, each supported on at most k letters.

For G = 1 this is exactly what the Armstrong–Forrest–Vogtmann presentation of Aut(F_n) provides, and HKUW use it
(Theorem `thm:PBrPresentation`) to present PB_r.

**Claim (sketch).** (BSP) implies that P_G(r) is finitely presented for r ≥ 3. Take the following generators and
relations:
- generators: finite generators of H_r, the local generators, and one slide of each of the listed kinds;
- relations of H_r (finitely presented for r ≥ 3);
- one representative of each relator shape;
- local generators commute with generators of the pointwise stabilizer in H_r of their support (finitely generated,
  since finite-set stabilizers of H_r are H_r-like);
- slide relations: slides commute with local generators supported away from their ray, and the recursion
  slide^E = (partial conjugation) · slide^{E∪{y}}.

The argument follows HKUW §3. Relabeled generators are defined as H_r-conjugates, and they are well defined by the
stabilizer relations. Every relabeling of a bounded-support relator is realized by H_r, because FSym ≤ H_r is highly
transitive. So every relation of every A_E, and of K, follows, and P_G(r) = K·H_r follows from the Houghton
relations. What has not been checked: the slide relations for words w_j that mix G and F(E), and that K's
presentation is just the union of the A_E presentations (the directed-union step).

## Routes to (BSP) or to the gate

1. Read Gilbert (1987, presentations of Aut of free products) and Carette (2011) at source, and check whether their
   relators can be rewritten with bounded support uniformly in n.
2. Presentation stability via the Brown–Tits colimit lemma. (BSP) follows once, for large n, the complex of "G-partial
   bases" of G * F_n rel G is simply connected. That is a G-relative version of the Hatcher–Vogtmann complexes.
3. Brown's criterion directly, as in HKUW §4, with a G-vertex at the core.
   - Stabilizers: finite G-graphs rel boundary, which are finitely presented by Carette plus the slides.
   - Descending links: complete joins over the piece complex.
   - What it needs: simple connectivity of G-tethered-handle complexes. Hatcher–Wahl (2010) supply this when G is a
     3-manifold group, but our G must be simple, so a new algebraic proof is needed.

## Lesson for general BH

Houghton-type Boone–Higman questions have a single finiteness gate: a bounded-support ("stable") presentation of the
finite-rank actor, with all transitivity inherited from finite rank. Finiteness of a directed union extended by a
highly transitive shift group reduces to relabeling-invariance of a local presentation. The same template (local
presentation plus a highly transitive shift group) should apply to any asymptotically rigid host.
