Notation as in symbol-blind-automata-reduce-to-defect-counting. G is any
group, M a finite subset containing e, sigma a class selector, and psi_P(g) =
g sigma(pi_g P), T(P) = psi_P^* P on set partitions of G. For finite F, E = FM,
a partition pi of F and j >= 0,

    N_j^T(F,pi) = #{partitions P of E with defect j : T(P)|_F = pi},

where P is extended by singletons and the defect is the sum of (|C|-1) over
classes C.

**Claim (open).** If tau_q is injective for every q, then N_j^T(F,pi) =
N_j^{id}(F,pi) for all j >= 2, all finite F and all pi.

Orders j = 0 and j = 1 hold over every group. By
symbol-blind-automata-reduce-to-defect-counting, the identities of all orders
are equivalent to bijectivity of tau_q at every q.

Known inputs, under injectivity at every q:

- T is a defect-preserving injection of the finitely-supported partitions.
- psi_P induces a bijection of classes.
- T is bijective on defect-1 partitions.

The two missing inputs are:

- surjectivity of T onto finitely-supported partitions of defect at least 2;
- cancellation of the boundary terms in the regularized count, where a class
  of P inside E maps to a class of T(P) that leaves F.

Over a finite group both hold trivially, since T permutes a finite set.

## Attempts

- **Pointed memory: settled.** See
  symbol-blind-automata-on-pointed-memory-are-permutive. If the semigroup
  generated by M \ {e} misses e, pre-injectivity at one q >= |M|+|M^{-1}M|
  forces permutivity at e. Solving in reverse order then gives the uniform
  counts, and hence the identities at every order.
- **Where that attack dies.** On non-pointed memory the order g ≺ g m has
  cycles. Both steps then fail.
  - The minimal unhit singleton needed to force P1 may not exist. R29 on
    {0,-1,1,2} over Z is pre-injective at every q and permutive in no
    coordinate.
  - The maximal changed cell may not exist. The centred minority rule on
    {0,-1,1} is e-permutive, yet not pre-injective at q = 3.
- **Finite-defect data do not decide injectivity.** Pre-injectivity alone
  already gives the three known inputs above: see the remark in
  symbol-blind-automata-on-pointed-memory-are-permutive-proof. So no
  finite-defect argument separates injective rules from merely pre-injective
  ones. The minority rule on {0,1,2} is pre-injective and injective at no
  q >= 2. The surjectivity of T in defect >= 2 has to come from somewhere
  else.
