Let `(M,tau)` be a finite tracial von Neumann algebra and let `X,Z in M` be
Pauli involutions:

```text
X^2=Z^2=1,
XZ=-ZX.                                                    (MPL1)
```

Write

```text
B={X,Z}' cap M                                             (MPL2)
```

for their common commutant.  In a matrix packet
`M ~= M_2 tensor M_m`, this is exactly `I_2 tensor M_m`, the smaller
multiplicity reservoir after one Clifford doubling.

### Dimension-free gauge-lock theorem

For every unitary `V in M`, put

```text
delta_X=||VX-XV||_2,
delta_Z=||VZ-ZV||_2.                                      (MPL3)
```

Then there is a unitary `U in B` such that

```text
||V-U||_2 <= delta_X+delta_Z.                              (MPL4)
```

Thus two mixed return relations lock the relative gauge with a constant
independent of the ambient dimension and the reservoir multiplicity.

### Proof

The four conjugations generated by `Ad_X` and `Ad_Z` form a Klein four group,
because their central sign disappears under conjugation.  Its Reynolds
operator is the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto `B`:

```text
E_B(T)=1/4 (T+XTX+ZTZ+(XZ)T(XZ)^*).                        (MPL5)
```

Unitary invariance and one telescoping step give

```text
||V-XVX||_2=delta_X,
||V-ZVZ||_2=delta_Z,
||V-(XZ)V(XZ)^*||_2<=delta_X+delta_Z.                      (MPL6)
```

Hence

```text
||V-E_B(V)||_2 <= (delta_X+delta_Z)/2.                     (MPL7)
```

Put `A=E_B(V)`.  Conditional expectation is the orthogonal projection in
`L^2`, so

```text
1-||A||_2^2=||V-A||_2^2.                                  (MPL8)
```

Also `A` is a contraction.  Extend the partial isometry in its polar
decomposition to a unitary `U in B`; this is possible in a finite von Neumann
algebra because the two complementary kernel projections have equal trace.
Pointwise on the singular values,

```text
(1-s)^2 <= 1-s^2  for 0<=s<=1,
```

and therefore

```text
||A-U||_2^2 <= 1-||A||_2^2=||V-A||_2^2.                   (MPL9)
```

The triangle inequality and `(MPL7)` prove `(MPL4)`.

### Exact nested-Pauli interpretation

Suppose two extensions of one exact `E_n` packet select next Pauli pairs

```text
(X_(n+1),Z_(n+1)),
(V X_(n+1) V^*, V Z_(n+1) V^*)                            (MPL10)
```

inside the old multiplicity algebra.  If the two mixed return comparisons
have errors at most `epsilon`, then `(MPL4)` gives

```text
dist_2(V, I_2 tensor U(m_(n+1))) <= 2 epsilon.             (MPL11)
```

This is precisely the compatibility missing from the nested uniqueness
audit: the relative gauge in `U(m_n)` is forced into the smaller commutant
`I_2 tensor U(m_(n+1))`.  Once the fixed packet has been exactified and a
Gowers--Hatami partial intertwiner has been made unitary on its large common
corner, no larger mixed packet is analytically necessary.

Each test is an ordinary scalar word moment.  For example

```text
||VX-XV||_2^2
 = 2-2 Re tau(V^* X V X),                                 (MPL12)
```

and similarly for `Z`.  Thus the lock consumes only two mixed commutator
words after decoding.

### One commuting test is insufficient

The two-test result is minimal among packet-commutation locks.  In
`M_2 tensor M_m`, take

```text
V=X tensor I_m.                                           (MPL13)
```

Then `[V,X tensor I_m]=0`, but `V` is orthogonal in `L^2` to every
`I_2 tensor U` because `tr_2(X)=0`.  Consequently

```text
dist_2(V, I_2 tensor U(m))=sqrt(2).                        (MPL14)
```

One normal commuting test leaves a full non-scalar direction in the label
factor and cannot lock the pointwise reservoir gauge.

### Claim boundary and regular-trace firewall

The theorem is analytic and valid in every finite tracial von Neumann
algebra.  It does **not** expose `V` as a common word on the two extension
routes, and it does not by itself create a finite-dimensional contradiction.
Literal global group relators `[V,X]=[V,Z]=1` merely impose the same lock in
the regular representation.

The remaining matrix-only theorem is now narrower: fixed packet
exactification and the mixed cycle must decode one relative gauge `V` on one
positive-density common corner, with the two return discrepancies equal to
the commutators in `(MPL3)`.  If it instead produces two unrelated gauges or
two unrelated corners, rectangular escape remains.  If it supplies the one
common gauge, `(MPL4)` closes the reservoir compatibility with only two word
tests; the later PI or multiplicity-cycle endpoint supplies the actual
finite-matrix obstruction.
