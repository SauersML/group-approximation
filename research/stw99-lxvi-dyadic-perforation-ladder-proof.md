Write `u=[1_B]` in `Cu(B)`.  The existence of an infinite projection makes
the unit infinite: adding the complement of an infinite projection to a
proper equivalent subprojection produces a proper subprojection equivalent
to the unit.  Since `B` is simple,
`stw74-simple-infinite-projections-are-properly-infinite` then makes the unit
properly infinite.  Thus

```text
ku=u                                                       (1)
```

for every positive integer `k`.  Moreover, `u` is the largest element of
`Cu(B)`: every positive element in a finite matrix algebra over `B` is
dominated by a finite multiple of `u`, which equals `u` by (1), and an
arbitrary stabilized class is the supremum of such cutdowns.

Now use the CAR copy.  The corner `pDp` is a UHF corner with an infinite
2-primary tail.  Concretely, approximate `p` closely enough by a finite
tensor stage and use
the standard close-projections unitary to conjugate it into that stage.
Every further tail matrix factor then splits it into equally equivalent
subprojections; conjugating the split back puts those subprojections under
the original `p`.  Hence,
for every `n>=1`, there is a nonzero projection `q_n<=p` such that

```text
[p]=2^n[q_n]                                                (2)
```

already in `V(D)`, and therefore in `Cu(B)`.  Each `q_n` is finite in `B`,
because finiteness passes to subprojections.

Put `x_n=[q_n]`.  The nonzero projection `q_n` is full because `B` is simple.
The ideal of `Cu(B)` generated by `x_n` is therefore all of `Cu(B)`.  Since
the projection class `u` is compact, there is a finite integer `r` with
`u<=r x_n`.  Let `m_n` be the least such integer.  As `x_n<=u`, equation (1)
gives

```text
u<=m_n x_n<=m_n u=u,
```

so

```text
m_n x_n=u.                                                  (3)
```

The integer `m_n` is larger than `2^n`.  Indeed, if `m_n<=2^n`, then (2)
and (3) imply

```text
u=m_n x_n<=2^n x_n=[p]<=u.
```

Thus `[p]=u`.  Cuntz equivalence of projections is stable
Murray--von Neumann equivalence, so `p` would be properly infinite because
the unit is, contradicting the assumed finiteness of `p`.

Finally, `u not<=x_n`: otherwise `x_n<=u` would make `q_n` Cuntz equivalent
to the properly infinite unit and hence properly infinite.  Combining this
with (1) and (3) gives

```text
(m_n+1)u=u=m_n x_n,          u not<=x_n.                    (4)
```

Equation (4) is exactly a failure of almost unperforation with coefficient
`m_n`, and `m_n>2^n` makes these coefficients unbounded.  Therefore no LXVI
witness can have almost unperforated Cuntz semigroup.
