## Proof

**`B` is a countable nonzero unital `F_2`-algebra.**  The four displayed
operators are honest linear maps on a nonzero space and `(NF1)` holds by
evaluating on basis vectors: `Y_iX_jv_k` is `v_k` when `i = j` and `0`
otherwise, and `X_0Y_0 + X_1Y_1` is the identity because every index is
even or odd.  `B` is spanned by the finitely many words in four
generators of each length, hence countable.

**`(NF2)` is a ring.**  `M_fin(N,B)` is an ideal of the ring generated by
`S`, `T` and the finite matrices: shifting a finite matrix on either side
leaves finitely many nonzero entries.  So the displayed sum is closed
under multiplication and is a countable unital ring.

**`(NF3)`.**  `TS = 1` and `1 - ST` is the matrix unit `E_00(1_B)` by the
usual one-sided shift computation.  It is nonzero, and `2e = 0` because
`B` is an `F_2`-algebra.

**`(NF4)`.**  For the ideal, `E_ij(b) = S^i E_00(b) e T^j` exhibits every
finite matrix in `ReR`; conversely `M_fin(N,B)` is an ideal containing
`e`, so `ReR <= M_fin(N,B)`.  For the quotient, `S` and `T` become
mutually inverse.  A scalar Laurent polynomial acts on all sufficiently
far basis vectors along fixed shifted diagonals; if it were a finite
matrix, each of those diagonals would be eventually zero, forcing every
coefficient to vanish.  So no relation beyond invertibility of `z`
survives and `R/M_fin(N,B) = F_2[z,z^(-1)]`.  The corner identity
`eRe = E_00(1_B) R E_00(1_B) = B` is immediate from matrix
multiplication.

**Nonfullness and the absence of any full pair.**  `R/ReR != 0` gives the
first.  For the second, let `t's' = 1` in `R`.  The quotient
`F_2[z,z^(-1)]` is commutative and nonzero, so the image of `s'` is a
unit and `1 - s't'` maps to `0`; an element of a proper ideal cannot
generate the unit ideal.

**The certificate.**  Take `p = e`, and let `x_i, y_i` be `X_i, Y_i` read
inside `eRe = B`.  Then `y_ix_j = delta_(ij) e` is `(NF1)`.  The first
prerequisite applies with this `p != 0`, giving non-MF of `EL_n(R)` for
every `n >= 2`.

**Centre.**  A central element commutes with every matrix unit
`E_ij(1_B)`, which forces it to be a matrix `bI` for a single `b in B`
commuting with all of `B`; but the sum in `(NF2)` then forces `bI` to
have eventual Laurent form, so `b in F_2`.  A nontrivial central
idempotent would be a central element other than `0,1`, so there is none.

**Point 5.**  The quotient map of `(NF4)` induces a surjection
`EL_n(R) ->> EL_n(F_2[z,z^(-1)])`, whose target is residually finite by
the second prerequisite and therefore MF.  It is nontrivial because the
target is nontrivial.  So `Rad_MF(EL_n(R))` is proper, while the
conclusion of `full-defect-ring-non-mf-at-rank-two` would make it
everything.
