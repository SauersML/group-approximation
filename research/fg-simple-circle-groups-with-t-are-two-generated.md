**Credit.** The mechanism is the commutator-extraction argument of Bleak–Elliott–Hyde (arXiv:2008.04791),
in the circle form that Belk–Hyde–Matucci use for `TA` (arXiv:2407.03149v1, Theorem `thm:TA-2-gen`, TeX
l.1103–1128, read at source). BHM remark (l.1101) that their proof "can be adapted to the case of TA (as well
as many other groups of homeomorphisms of the circle)". What is added here is a general hypothesis that
covers every finitely generated simple circle group containing `T`, and a fragmentation lemma for `T` that
removes the need for a perfect arc group. No priority is claimed beyond this. Bounded arXiv searches
(2026-09-18: "Lodha simple group piecewise projective generated", "two-generated homeomorphisms circle simple
group") found no general statement of this kind.

## Statement

Let `T` be Thompson's group acting on `S^1 = R/Z` by piecewise-linear homeomorphisms with dyadic breakpoints
and slopes in `2^Z`. Let `G ≤ Homeo_+(S^1)`, and suppose some homeomorphism of `S^1` conjugates a subgroup
of `G` onto `T`. Replace `G` by that conjugate, so that `T ≤ G`. For a closed arc `I ≠ S^1`, write `G_I` for
the rigid stabilizer `{g ∈ G : g = id on S^1 ∖ I}`.

1. **Main theorem.** Assume
   - **(G)** `G` is generated by `T` together with finitely many elements, each a product of elements of the
     commutator subgroups `[G_I, G_I]`, for closed arcs `I ≠ S^1`.

   Then `G = <t, f>` for two elements: `t ∈ T` is a rotation of order `2^(k+2)` for some `k`, and `f ∈ G`.
2. **Simple groups.** If `G` is finitely generated and simple, (G) holds. The subgroup `N` generated by all
   `[G_I, G_I]` is normal, because `g G_I g^(-1) = G_(gI)`. It is nontrivial, because `T_I ≅ F` for a dyadic
   arc `I`, so `[G_I, G_I] ⊇ [F, F] ≠ 1`. Hence `N = G`, and finitely many elements of `N` generate `G`.
3. **Corollaries.**
   - Lodha's finitely presented simple group `S` is 2-generated (`lodha-simple-group-s-is-two-generated`).
   - This recovers BHM's theorem that `TA` is 2-generated, and the 2-generation of `T` itself.

## Why it matters here

`fp-infinite-simple-group-not-two-generated` (Attempt 1) lists "groups acting on the circle or the line, such
as Lodha's `S`" among the candidate classes left open by the vigorous-group theorem. This removes every
finitely generated simple circle group containing `T`, whatever its finiteness properties. What survives on
the circle is a finitely presented simple circle group that contains no conjugate of `T`.

## Lesson for general BH

Commutator extraction is a packing principle, not a fact about the circle. A shift `t` that permutes a Sidon
family of disjoint pieces lets one element, spread over the family, carry any finite list of piece-supported
elements: all their commutators come back as `[t^(-u_a) f t^(u_a), t^(-u_b) f t^(u_b)]`. So in any host that
has such a shift and whose piece stabilizers generate, finitely many local data collapse into two generators.
- For Boone–Higman, generation rank is never an obstruction inside dynamical hosts. Circle hosts containing
  `T` and vigorous Cantor hosts are 2-generated.
- The finite data needed to embed an input group (images of its generators placed in one piece) can be packed
  into a single element of a fixed finitely presented host. This is the in-host analogue of the
  Higman–Neumann–Neumann and Schupp two-generator embeddings.

## Scope

- Finite presentability plays no role. The theorem needs `T ≤ G` only through three facts:
  - a finite-order rotation `t ∈ T` that cycles a family of `2n` arcs;
  - the fragmentation lemma of the proof, Step 1;
  - the transitivity of `T` on cyclically ordered pairs of dyadic points.
- It says nothing about groups of homeomorphisms of the line. There is no finite-order element there, and the
  trick would need a translation-like replacement for `t`.
