Put `Gamma_m=SL_{2m+1}(Z)`.  Each `Gamma_m`, for `2m+1>=3`, has trivial
amenable radical.  Indeed, the Margulis normal subgroup theorem makes an
amenable normal subgroup finite (a finite-index subgroup would be
nonamenable), and a finite normal subgroup of this higher-rank lattice is
central.  The center of `SL_n(Z)` is trivial for odd `n`.  Vigdorovich's
linear-group theorem therefore makes every `C*_r(Gamma_m)` selfless.

The subgroup inclusions give unital trace-preserving embeddings of the
reduced group algebras, and their directed union is dense in
`C*_r(SL_infinity(Z))`.  Robert's directed-union theorem proves selflessness
of the limit.  His tracial regularity theorem gives simplicity, stable rank
one, uniqueness of normalized 2-quasitrace, and strict comparison.

For nonlinearity, fix a rank-two free subgroup `L<=SL_2(Z)` (for example the
one generated by the two standard Sanov unipotents).  Placing copies of `L`
in disjoint `2 x 2` diagonal blocks embeds

```text
direct_sum_N F_2 <= SL_infinity(Z).                    (1)
```

The algebra-chain argument in
`stw91-countable-free-direct-sum-is-selfless-proof` proves that the group in
(1) has no faithful representation in `GL_d(k)` for any finite `d` and any
field `k`.  A subgroup of a linear group is linear, so (1) proves that
`SL_infinity(Z)` is nonlinear.

Finally, every element `g` of `SL_infinity(Z)` is supported in a finite
matrix block.  Its centralizer contains the full stable special linear group
on the complementary coordinates, and in particular contains a nonabelian
free group.  Thus no element has virtually cyclic centralizer.  A
non-virtually-cyclic acylindrically hyperbolic group has a loxodromic element,
and the centralizer of such an element is virtually cyclic.  This
contradiction proves that `SL_infinity(Z)` is not acylindrically hyperbolic.

**Trust boundary.**  The imported inputs are the Margulis normal subgroup
theorem (including the standard finite-normal-subgroup conclusion for these
lattices), Vigdorovich's linear selflessness theorem, Robert's two permanence
and regularity theorems, and the virtually-cyclic-centralizer theorem for
loxodromic elements.  Reduced-algebra density, the embedded restricted direct
sum, nonlinearity transfer, and the complementary-block centralizer argument
are explicit above.
