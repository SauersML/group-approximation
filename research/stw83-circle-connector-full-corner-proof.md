## The first-return graph

Write `U={v_0,w_0}`.  Removing these two vertices breaks both simple cycles
into directed paths.  Together with acyclicity of the connector and the
absence of backward edges, this shows that the induced graph on `F^0 minus U`
is finite and acyclic.

Let `Gamma` be the finite set of nontrivial paths `gamma` in `F` whose source
and range lie in `U` and which have no intermediate vertex in `U`.  There is
exactly one element of `Gamma` from `v_0` to itself: one traversal of the
upstream cycle.  There is exactly one from `w_0` to itself, coming from the
downstream cycle, and none from `w_0` to `v_0`.

The paths from `v_0` to `w_0` are counted by `N`.  Indeed, before returning to
`v_0`, there is one route along the upstream cycle to the source of each exit.
After that exit, the recursion defining `n(r(e))` counts precisely the paths
through the acyclic connector to their first downstream-cycle vertex.  From
there there is a unique downstream-cycle segment ending at `w_0` without an
intermediate visit to `w_0`.  This gives a bijection

```text
{gamma in Gamma: s(gamma)=v_0, r(gamma)=w_0}
    <-> the N weighted connector paths.                 (4)
```

Consequently the graph with vertex set `U` and edge set `Gamma` is `E_N`.

## Identify the full corner

Inside `C*(F)`, put `Q_u=p_u` for `u in U` and `T_gamma=s_gamma` for
`gamma in Gamma`.  Distinct first-return paths cannot properly extend one
another, since the endpoint of the shorter path would be an intermediate
vertex in `U` for the longer one.  Their range projections are therefore
orthogonal.  Expanding the Cuntz--Krieger relations through the finite acyclic
graph `F^0 minus U` gives

```text
Q_u=sum_{gamma in Gamma, s(gamma)=u} T_gamma T_gamma*
                                                            (u in U).       (5)
```

Thus `(Q,T)` is a Cuntz--Krieger `E_N`-family and induces

```text
Phi:C*(E_N) -> P C*(F)P.                                  (6)
```

The vertex projections are nonzero.  The only cycle of `E_N` without an exit
is the loop at `w_0`; its image is the path around the original downstream
cycle and has full circle spectrum in the cycle corner.  Szymanski's general
Cuntz--Krieger uniqueness theorem therefore makes `Phi` injective.

For surjectivity, the corner is densely spanned by monomials
`P s_mu s_nu* P`.  A nonzero such monomial has `s(mu),s(nu) in U` and a common
range.  If the common range lies in `U`, split both paths at successive visits
to `U`; they become words in `Gamma`.  If it lies outside `U`, repeatedly use
the Cuntz--Krieger relations in the finite acyclic complement to expand its
vertex projection as the finite sum of `s_alpha s_alpha*` over paths `alpha`
which first return to `U`.  Then

```text
s_mu s_nu* = sum_alpha s_(mu alpha) s_(nu alpha)*,
```

and every path on the right is a word in `Gamma`.  Hence (6) is onto and
proves (1).

The projection `P` is full.  Its ideal contains every vertex of the two cycles.
It also contains every connector vertex: order the acyclic connector backwards
from the downstream cycle and use the Cuntz--Krieger relation at each vertex,
just as in the `K_0` recursion of `stw83-circle-circle-boundary-index`.  Thus
the ideal generated by `P` contains every vertex projection and is all of
`C*(F)`.  Brown's full-corner theorem and Morita invariance of nuclear
dimension now give (2).

## A concrete Toeplitz-module model

Let `A=C(T)`, with coordinate unitary `z`, and let `(e_(i,j))` be the standard
matrix units of `K` on `ell^2(N_0)`.  In `M(K tensor A)`, define

```text
q_w=e_(0,0) tensor 1,                q_v=1-q_w,
t_b=e_(0,0) tensor z,
t_(f_j)=e_(j,0) tensor 1             (1<=j<=N),
t_a=(S^N tensor 1)q_v.
```

Direct calculation gives

```text
t_a* t_a=q_v,
t_a t_a*=q_v-sum_(j=1)^N t_(f_j)t_(f_j)*,
t_b* t_b=t_b t_b*=q_w,
t_(f_j)*t_(f_j)=q_w.
```

These are exactly the Cuntz--Krieger relations for `E_N`.  The downstream
loop has spectrum `T`, so the same uniqueness theorem makes the induced map
from `C*(E_N)` injective.

The image contains `K tensor A`.  Indeed, `t_a^r t_(f_j)` gives
`e_(j+rN,0) tensor 1`; multiplying two such terms and inserting powers of
`t_b` gives `e_(k,l) tensor z^m` for every `k,l` and every integer `m`.
Their span is dense in `K tensor A`.  Conversely all displayed graph
generators belong to `C*(S^N tensor 1,K tensor A)`, while

```text
S^N tensor 1=t_a+e_(N,0) tensor 1
```

belongs to the graph-generated image.  This proves (3).

The model is a genuine algebraic/full-corner reduction, not a classification
from the boundary index.  Although its quotient symbol is generated by
`z^N`, its ideal is `K tensor C(T)`, not `K`; the existing Toeplitz
nuclear-dimension-one theorem therefore does not by itself compute (3).
