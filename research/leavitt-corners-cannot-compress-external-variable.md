Inside a Leavitt matrix group, two elementary-root errors on distinct matrix
entries can be packed into one product: equality to the identity reads each
entry separately.  This does not compile two KL transport equations.  Their
errors contain the stable letter, which is an element of an arbitrary group
extension and has no Leavitt matrix entries or prefix corners.

The binary Leavitt compressions use addition and multiplication by the
nonunits `s_i,t_i`; they are not group words which can be applied to an
external stable letter.  Fixed unit conjugations likewise do not force that
letter to preserve a matrix block decomposition.  Therefore orthogonal
corner support cannot justify splitting a single relative group relation
into the two desired transport relations.  A successful compiler needs a
genuinely group-theoretic test word or extra relative cells.
