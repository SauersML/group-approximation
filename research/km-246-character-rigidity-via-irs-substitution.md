Fix a prime power `q >= 4`. Write `S = S_q`, and take `L_±` and `Λ_+` as in
`km-246-completions-are-kazhdan-howe-moore-simple`, with `K_-` the stabilizer in `L_-` of the
standard negative chamber, which is compact open. Items (CP-a) to (CP-g) are those of
`creutz-peterson-factor-representation-rigidity-steps`.

**Step 1: an escaping element.** Caprace--Remy, arXiv:math/0607664v2 (`SimplSuperrigid.tex`,
§2, fetched on MSI on 2026-09-13), say: "We also set N := T.⟨m(u) | u ∈ U_α − {1}, α ∈ Π⟩. A basic
fact is that the subquotient N/T is isomorphic to W". They also say that a group with a twin
root datum "admits two structures of BN-pairs", with twinned buildings whose "apartments are
modelled on the Coxeter complex of (W,S)", standard twin apartment `(A_+, A_-)` and standard
positive chamber `c_+`. In the building of a BN-pair, `N` stabilizes the standard apartment and
`n` maps the standard chamber to `w c_+`, where `w = nT` (standard; Abramenko--Brown Ch. 6,
not re-read).
- `W`, the `(2,4,6)` triangle group, is infinite, finitely generated and linear (Tits'
  geometric representation). By Schur's theorem (a finitely generated periodic linear group is
  finite; classical, not re-read) it contains an element `w` of infinite order. Take `n in N`
  with `nT = w`, and let `γ_0` be its image in `S`.
- The chambers `γ_0^k c_+ = w^k c_+`, for `k in Z`, are pairwise distinct, since `W` acts
  simply transitively on the chambers of the standard apartment.
- A compact `C ⊂ L_+` moves `c_+` to finitely many chambers, because the stabilizer of `c_+` is
  open and `C` is covered by finitely many of its cosets. So only finitely many `γ_0^k` lie in
  `C`, i.e. `γ_0^k -> infinity` in `L_+`. The same holds in `L_-`, since `N` also stabilizes
  `A_-` and acts on it through `W`.

**Step 2: proper outerness in both completions.** Apply
`irs-rigid-simple-dense-subgroups-act-properly-outer` with `G = L_+` and with `G = L_-`. Take
`Λ = S`, identified with its injective dense image (KHM3).
- `G` is second countable and topologically simple (KHM1), and Howe--Moore (KHM2).
- (PO1) and (PO2), simplicity and IRS rigidity of `S`, come from
  `km-246-lattice-fp-simple-kazhdan-irs-rigid-for-q-ge-4`.
- (PO3) is Step 1.

So every continuous ergodic trace-preserving action of `L_±` on a finite von Neumann algebra
`≠ C` with separable predual is properly outer on `S`.

**Step 3: the lattice side is precompact.** Let `π: S -> U(M)` be a factor representation that
does not extend to an isomorphism `L(S) -> M`. `M` has a faithful normal trace and is
generated by the countable set `π(S)`, so `L^2(M)` is separable and `M` has separable predual.
- Apply (CP-c) with `G = L_+`, `Γ = Λ_+` (the subgroup `S ∩ (L_+ x K_-)` of `S`) and `Λ = S`.
  - `Λ_+` is a lattice in `L_+` commensurated by `S` (KHM4), and `S` is dense (KHM3).
  - The only proper closed normal subgroup of `L_+` is `{e}` (KHM1).
  - Proper outerness is Step 2.

  So `N = π(Λ_+)''` is injective.
- Apply (CP-d): `L_+` has (T) (KHM2), so `π(Λ_+)` is precompact in the strong operator
  topology.

**Step 4: `M` is finite-dimensional.**
- Apply (CP-e) with:
  - ambient group `L_+ x L_-`;
  - closed normal subgroup `H = L_+ x {e}`, so the quotient is `L_-`;
  - lattice `S` (KHM3), whose image in `L_-` is dense;
  - `U = K_-`, a compact open neighbourhood of the identity.

  Then `Γ_U = S ∩ (L_+ x K_-)` is the subgroup `Λ_+` of Step 3, so `π(Γ_U)` is precompact.
  The `L_-`-algebra of `π` (for `S -> L_-`) therefore contains `N' ∩ M` for a
  finite-dimensional `N ⊂ M`.
- By Step 2 for `L_-`, the hypothesis of (CP-a) holds for `G = L_-` and `Λ = S`. The proof of
  Lemma 4.3 applies that hypothesis only to the `L_-`-algebra of `π`, a subalgebra of `M` with
  separable predual. So the `L_-`-algebra is `C`, and `N' ∩ M = C`.
- Hence `Z(N) ⊂ N' ∩ M = C`, `N ≅ M_k(C)`, and `M ≅ N ⊗ (N' ∩ M) = N` is finite-dimensional.
  This is the step "we must have M_0 = C, and hence M is finite dimensional" in the proof of
  Creutz--Peterson's Proposition 6.2.

**Step 5: finite-dimensional factor representations are trivial.** If `M = M_k(C)`, then
`π(S) ⊂ U(k)` is a finitely generated linear group, hence residually finite
(`finitely-generated-linear-groups-are-residually-finite`). `ker π` is normal in the simple
group `S`.
- If `ker π = {e}`, then `S` is residually finite. But an infinite simple group is not: a
  finite quotient separating `g ≠ e` has a proper normal kernel of finite index, which must be
  trivial, so `S` would be finite.
- So `ker π = S`, `π` is trivial, and `M = C`. This proves (CR1).

**Step 6: characters.** `S` is infinite and simple, hence ICC.
- If `g` had a finite conjugacy class, its centralizer would have finite index. Its normal core
  would then be a normal subgroup of finite index, hence `S`, so `g` would be central and
  therefore trivial.
- So `L(S)` is a II_1 factor, and its normalized trace is unique.

Now let `χ` be an extreme normalized character. By (CP-g), its GNS representation generates a
finite factor `M_χ`, with `χ = τ_{M_χ} ∘ π_χ`. By (CR1), either `π_χ` extends to an isomorphism
`L(S) -> M_χ`, and uniqueness of the trace gives `χ = δ_e`, or `M_χ = C` and `χ = 1`.
- The characters form a Choquet simplex with these two extreme points (CP-g). So every
  character is `t δ_e + (1-t) 1`, which is (CR2).
- The tracial states of `C*(S)` are exactly the normalized characters, which gives (CR3).
