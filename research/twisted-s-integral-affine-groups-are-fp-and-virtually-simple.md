with its parent `twisted-integral-affine-full-group-is-finitely-presented`. No priority claimed.

**The group.** Fix a finite set of primes `P`, a prime `ℓ ∉ P`, `R = Z[1/P] ⊆ Z_ℓ`, and
`X = Z_ℓ^D`, where `F` acts on the dyadics `D`. `W_{P,ℓ}` is the group of homeomorphisms of `X`
with a finite partition into pieces (cosets of open subgroups of `Z_ℓ^{T_i}` times the other
coordinates) on which the map is
- affine `x ↦ M_i x + b_i` on `Z_ℓ^{T_i}`, with `M_i ∈ GL_{T_i}(Z[1/ℓP])` and `b_i ∈ Z[1/ℓP]^{T_i}`,
- composed with a coordinate permutation by some `f_i ∈ F`.

The global elements form exactly `Γ^P` of `s-integral-affine-structure-group-is-finitely-presented`:
a matrix over `Z[1/ℓP]` that preserves `Z_ℓ^T` has entries and inverse entries in
`Z_ℓ ∩ Z[1/ℓP] = R`. `W_{∅,2}` is the group `W` of the parent node.

**Claim.**
1. `W_{P,ℓ}` is finitely presented.
2. The abelianization of `W_{P,ℓ}` is finite of exponent dividing `ℓ − 1`. It is trivial for
   `ℓ = 2`.
3. The commutator subgroup `D(W_{P,ℓ})` is simple and finitely presented.
4. `D(W_{P,ℓ})` contains `E(R; D) ⋊ F'`, and hence `GL_n(Z[1/P])` for every `n`.

## Proof

**1.** Route `twisted-integral-affine-group-fp-via-stein-complex`, with these substitutions.
- **Splits are `ℓ`-ary.** Directions are lines in `F_ℓ^(D)`. The orbit count of
  `integral-affine-splitting-types-have-finitely-many-tuple-orbits` holds verbatim over `F_ℓ`:
  the image of `GL(R; D)` contains finitary `SL(F_ℓ; D)`, and orbits of tuples of lines are
  determined by finitely many dependency data.
- **Stabilizers.** Vertex stabilizers are `Γ^P ≀ S_m`, which is finitely presented. Short-edge
  stabilizers are finitely generated by `s-integral-splitting-stabilizers-are-finitely-generated`.
- **Merges.** In BZ Lemma 7.2, merges join `ℓ` copies, so the nerve is the matching complex of
  the complete `ℓ`-uniform hypergraph. Its connectivity grows linearly in `m`: this is the input
  of Brown's `F_∞` proof for the Higman–Thompson groups `V_{ℓ,r}` (Brown 1987, recalled).
- **Morse function.** In `μ` omit `μ_1` and `μ_ℓ`, a root split once. A root is fat if its
  weight is at least `2ℓ − 1`.
- **BZ Lemma 7.6.** The nerve is the complex of sets of lines not spanning `U_i ≤ F_ℓ^(D)`. It is
  `(dim U_i − 3)`-connected by Solomon–Tits over `F_ℓ`; the proof of
  `non-spanning-subsets-of-an-f2-space-are-highly-connected` never uses `q = 2`. A full tree has
  `ℓ^{dim U_i}` leaves, so the bound reads `log_ℓ(m − μ_1) ≤ Σ dim U_i`.

**2.**
- `W` is generated by the untwisted `ℓ`-ary `DV_ℓ` and the elements `ι_B(γ)` ("`γ` on a brick `B`"),
  by the germinal-twist argument of BZ Prop 3.1.
- Every such generator is a product of elements that act trivially on some coordinate `d'`. For
  `DV_ℓ` elements, finitely many coordinates are touched. For `γ ∈ Γ^P`, write it as a product of
  finitary elementary, diagonal and translation factors and of `F`-elements that fix a dyadic
  point; the latter generate `F`, since they form a conjugation-invariant set that generates a
  normal subgroup containing `F'` and mapping onto `F/F' = Z^2`.
- For `g` trivial on `d'`, splitting along `d'` gives `[g] = ℓ c(g)`, where `c(g)` is the class of
  `ι_B(g)` for any proper brick `B`. All such `ι_B(g)` are conjugate by canonical maps in `DV_ℓ`.
  Splitting `B` along a second untouched coordinate gives `c(g) = ℓ c(g)`.
- So `(ℓ−1) c(g) = 0`. The finitely generated group `W^ab` is generated by torsion, so it is
  finite, and trivial for `ℓ = 2`.

**3.** Let `𝒢` be the germ groupoid of `W` acting on `X`. It is:
- étale and ample;
- Hausdorff: the interior of each fixed-point set is a union of pieces on which the local affine
  map is the identity, so it is clopen;
- effective, hence essentially principal;
- minimal, since `DV_ℓ` orbits are dense;
- purely infinite: every clopen set is a finite union of pieces, and canonical `ℓ`-adic maps
  embed it twice disjointly into itself.

`W` is closed under piecewise gluing, so `W = [[𝒢]]`. Matui (arXiv:1210.5800, Theorem 4.16,
`\label{simple2}`, read from the local TeX source, 2026-09-18) states: if `G` is purely infinite
and minimal, then `D([[G]])` is simple. By 2, `D(W)` has finite index, so it is finitely presented.

**4.** For `n ≥ 3`, `E_n(R)` is perfect, so `E(R; D) ≤ D(W)`. `GL_n(R)` embeds in
`SL_{n+1}(R) = E_{n+1}(R)` by `g ↦ diag(g, det g^{-1})`, using `SK_1(R) = 0`. `F' ≤ D(W)`.
For `ℓ = 2`, `W` itself is simple, by the parent's simplicity node. ∎
