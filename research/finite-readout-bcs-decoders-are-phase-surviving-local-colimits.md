## Setting

`B` is a finite Boolean constraint system with variables `v`, contexts `V_c`
(every variable lies in some context) and constraint polynomials in the
context variables. `A(B)` is its synchronous algebra. For a group `Lambda`
with a central element `J` of order dividing `n` and a faithful character
`omega` of `Z/n`, put

```text
p_omega = (1/n) sum_(k=0)^(n-1) conj(omega^k) J^k  in C[Lambda].        (LC0)
```

An **exact finite-readout decoder** of `B` into `(Lambda, J, omega)` is a
unital *-homomorphism `A(B) -> C[Lambda] p_omega`, `x_v` the images of the
variables, such that `J` has order exactly `n` and every context `c` has a
finite subgroup `A_c <= Lambda` with `x_v in C[A_c] p_omega` for `v in V_c`.

**Finite local data** `D` for `B` and `n` consist of:
- finite groups `A_c` (one per context) and `H_v` (one per variable), each
  containing a central copy of `Z/n = <J>`;
- injective homomorphisms `iota_(v,c) : H_v -> A_c` for `v in V_c`, fixing `J`;
- self-adjoint unitaries `y_v in C[H_v] p_omega` such that for every context
  `c` the elements `iota_(v,c)(y_v)`, `v in V_c`, commute pairwise and satisfy
  the constraint polynomials of `c` in `C[A_c] p_omega`.

The **local colimit** `Lambda(D)` is the colimit of the diagram formed by the
`A_c`, the `H_v`, one vertex `<J> = Z/n` mapping into all of them, and the
maps `iota_(v,c)`. The **incidence graph** `Inc(D)` is the bipartite graph on
contexts and variables with an edge `v - c` whenever `v in V_c`.

## Statement

**(LC1) Equivalence.** `B` has an exact finite-readout decoder into some group
corner if and only if some finite local data `D` for `B` and `n` has `J` of
order exactly `n` in `Lambda(D)`. In that case the images of the `y_v` form an
exact finite-readout decoder into `C[Lambda(D)] p_omega`, and every exact
finite-readout decoder into any `(Lambda, J, omega)` factors through the
colimit of its own local data by a homomorphism `Lambda(D) -> Lambda`.

**(LC2) Finite presentation.** Every local colimit `Lambda(D)` is finitely
presented and is generated by the images of the finitely many finite groups
`A_c`.

**(LC3) Finite-dimensional blindness.** Suppose `B` has no finite-dimensional
perfect model, i.e. no unital *-homomorphism `A(B) -> M_k(C)` with `k >= 1`.
If `B` has an exact finite-readout decoder into `(Lambda, J, omega)`, then:
1. every finite-dimensional unitary representation `pi` of `Lambda` has
   `pi(p_omega) = 0`;
2. in every finite quotient of `Lambda` the image of `J` has order `< n`;
3. some prime `p | n` has `J^(n/p)` in the finite residual of `Lambda`; for
   `n` prime, `J` itself lies in the finite residual, so `Lambda` is not
   residually finite.

**(LC4) Acyclic gluings are finite-dimensional.** If `Inc(D)` is a forest,
then `Lambda(D)` has a finite-dimensional unitary representation `pi` with
`pi(p_omega) != 0`. So `J` has order `n` in `Lambda(D)` and `B` has a
finite-dimensional perfect model. Consequently, for a source with no
finite-dimensional perfect model, the incidence graph of every finite local
data is cyclic, whether or not its phase survives.

## Consequence for the compilation route

By (LC1) the nonabelian-readout sector of
`group-corner-decoders-split-into-coset-and-matrix-readouts` is, for
finite readouts, the single question of **phase survival in a finitely
presented colimit of finite local models**. For the tracial non-`R^U` source
`B_*` this is `tracial-nonru-bcs-has-phase-surviving-local-colimit`; a
positive answer puts `A(B_*)` in a corner of `L(Lambda(D))` and so solves
`non-ru-bcs-is-satisfiable-in-a-group-factor` with a finitely presented,
non-residually-finite host (LC2, LC3). Certificates of survival cannot be
finite-dimensional (LC3.1), and acyclic gluings never survive for such
sources (LC4); all the difficulty sits in cycles of `Inc(D)`.
