V. Nekrashevych, *Simple groups of dynamical origin*, arXiv:1511.08241
(submitted 25 Nov 2015, last revised 7 Feb 2017). Let `F(G)` denote the
topological full group of an etale groupoid `G` with Cantor unit space.

**Definition 3.1 (verbatim).** "A *multisection of degree d* is a collection of
`d^2` bisections `{F_{i,j}}_{i,j=1}^d` such that (1)
`F_{i2,i3} F_{i1,i2} = F_{i1,i3}` for all `1 <= i1, i2, i3 <= d`; (2) the
bisections `F_{i,i}` are disjoint subsets of `G^{(0)}`."

So a degree-`d` multisection is `d` pairwise disjoint clopen sets
`U_i = F_{i,i}` together with bisections `F_{i,j} : U_j -> U_i` composing
consistently. Permuting the `U_i` by these bisections and acting as the
identity elsewhere embeds `S_d` in `F(G)`; write `S(F)` and `A(F)` for the
image of `S_d` and of `A_d`.

**Definition 3.2 (verbatim).** "Denote by `S_d(G)` (resp. `A_d(G)`) the
subgroup of `F(G)` generated by the union of the subgroups `S(F)` (resp.
`A(F)`) for all multisections `F` of degree `d`. Denote `S(G) = S_2(G)` and
`A(G) = A_3(G)`."

So `A(G)` is generated by the **3-cycles of degree-3 multisections**, and
`S(G)` by the **transpositions of degree-2 multisections**. Both are normal
in `F(G)`.

**Theorem 4.1 (verbatim).** "Suppose that `G` is a minimal groupoid of germs.
Then every non-trivial subgroup of `F(G)` normalized by `A(G)` contains
`A(G)`. In particular, `A(G)` is simple."

**Theorem 5.6 (verbatim).** "Suppose that `G` is expansive and every `G`-orbit
has at least 5 points. Then the group `A(G)` is finitely generated."

**Abstract (verbatim), for the last statement.** "We associate with every etale
groupoid `G` two normal subgroups `S(G)` and `A(G)` of the topological full
group of `G`, which are analogs of the symmetric and alternating groups. We
prove that if `G` is a minimal groupoid of germs (e.g., of a group action),
then `A(G)` is simple and is contained in every non-trivial normal subgroup of
the full group. We show that if `G` is expansive (e.g., is the groupoid of
germs of an expansive action of a group), then `A(G)` is finitely generated. We
also show that `S(G)/A(G)` is a quotient of `H_0(G, Z/2Z)`."

Three points that matter for the consumers of this node:

1. **Finite generation does not need minimality.** Theorem 5.6 asks for
   expansiveness and orbits with at least 5 points. This is what supports the
   finite generation of `A(G_V x (Z |x Sigma))` asserted in
   `v-times-shift-alternating-group-restrictions-are-simple`, whose groupoid is
   *not* minimal. What still has to be checked there is that the product of the
   two expansive groupoids is expansive in Nekrashevych's sense.
2. **Simplicity does need minimality** (Theorem 4.1), and it is stated for
   groupoids of germs, that is, effective groupoids.
3. `S(G)/A(G)` is a quotient of `H_0(G, Z/2Z)`, so `H_0(G, Z/2Z) = 0` forces
   `S(G) = A(G)`. See `halvable-groupoid-alternating-full-groups-are-perfect`,
   where this is applied.
