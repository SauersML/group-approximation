**Route.** The implication below is complete; the route fires once the two OPEN
premises (P1) and (P2) are proved.

**How this differs from nearby routes.**
- `boone-higman-via-permutational-boone-higman` places the input in a type (A)
  actor and takes a twisted Brin--Thompson group. This route places the input in
  the acting group of a symbolic dynamical system and takes a Nekrashevych
  alternating full group. Neither premise here mentions finitely generated
  stabilizers.
- `input-encoded-sft-groupoid-hosts-are-not-universal` kills full groups of
  one-sided SFTs over N, which contain no infinite Kazhdan group. Here the SFT
  lives over the input's own overgroup `Λ`, and `Λ` sits inside the host.
- `boone-higman-via-rover-nekrashevych-hosts` is refuted because Kazhdan subgroups
  of Rover--Nekrashevych groups are residually finite. `T` below is not eventually
  self-similar over an SFT on N.

## Setting

- `C = {0,1}^N`, and `G_V` is the groupoid of germs of Thompson's group `V`
  acting on `C`.
- `Λ` is a finitely generated group, `A` a finite alphabet, and `X ⊆ A^Λ` a
  nonempty subshift on which `Λ` acts freely and minimally.
- `H = Λ ⋉ X` is the transformation groupoid and `T = G_V × H`, on unit space
  `C × X`.
- `F(T)` is the topological full group; `A(T)` is Nekrashevych's alternating
  full group, generated by 3-cycles of degree-3 multisections.

## Premises

- **(P1)** `decidable-groups-embed-in-fp-groups-with-minimal-free-sft`: every
  finitely generated group with solvable word problem embeds in a finitely
  presented group `Λ` that admits a nonempty minimal free subshift of finite type.
- **(P2)** `v-times-minimal-free-sft-alternating-full-groups-are-fp`: for every
  finitely presented `Λ` and nonempty minimal free subshift of finite type
  `X ⊆ A^Λ`, the group `A(G_V × (Λ ⋉ X))` is finitely presented.
- Imports already ESTABLISHED in the graph:
  - Nekrashevych, Theorem 4.1, quoted in
    `nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups`: "Suppose that `G` is a
    minimal groupoid of germs. Then every non-trivial subgroup of `F(G)`
    normalized by `A(G)` contains `A(G)`. In particular, `A(G)` is simple."
  - `v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions`: for every ample
    Hausdorff groupoid `H` with compact totally disconnected unit space,
    `F(G_V × H) = S(G_V × H) = A(G_V × H)`.

## Proof that (P1) and (P2) give Boone--Higman

Let `K` be finitely generated with solvable word problem. By (P1) choose a
finitely presented infinite `Λ >= K` and a nonempty minimal free subshift of
finite type `X ⊆ A^Λ`. Put `H = Λ ⋉ X` and `T = G_V × H`.

1. **`X` is a Cantor space.** `X` is compact, metrizable and totally disconnected.
   It is infinite, since `Λ` is infinite and acts freely. Suppose `x` were
   isolated. Then every point of `Λx` is isolated, so `Λx` is open, and
   `X \ Λx` is closed and invariant. By minimality it is empty, so `X = Λx` is
   compact and discrete, hence finite. This is a contradiction. So `C × X` is a
   Cantor space.
2. **`T` is ample and Hausdorff.** Both factors are ample Hausdorff groupoids
   with compact totally disconnected unit spaces. Products of compact open
   bisections are compact open bisections of `T` and form a basis.
3. **`T` is effective, hence a groupoid of germs.**
   - An arrow `(g, h)` of `T` is isotropy exactly when `g` and `h` are.
   - `H` is principal because the action is free, so its isotropy is its unit
     space.
   - Let `W` be an open subset of `T` inside the isotropy. Every point of `W` has
     a basic neighbourhood `U × U'` inside `W`.
     - `U'` consists of units.
     - `U` lies in the isotropy of `G_V`, whose interior is the unit space
       because `G_V` is a groupoid of germs.
     - So `W` consists of units.
   - An effective ample Hausdorff groupoid is the groupoid of germs of the
     pseudogroup of its compact open bisections. This is the standard
     identification (Nekrashevych, arXiv:1511.08241, Section 2, on groupoids of
     germs; not re-read at source for this node).
4. **`T` is minimal.** The `T`-orbit of `(c, x)` is `V c × Λ x`. `V` acts
   minimally on `C`, and `Λ x` is dense by minimality of `X`. So every orbit is
   dense in `C × X`.
5. **`Λ` embeds in `F(T)`.** For `λ` in `Λ`, the set
   `B_λ = {(1_c, (λ, x)) : c ∈ C, x ∈ X}` is a compact open bisection with source
   and range the whole unit space. It defines `φ_λ(c, x) = (c, λ x)` in `F(T)`.
   - `λ ↦ φ_λ` is a homomorphism.
   - It is injective, because `X` is nonempty and the action is free.
6. **`Λ` lies in `A(T)`.** By
   `v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions`,
   `F(T) = A(T)`.
7. **`A(T)` is simple.** By steps 3 and 4 and Nekrashevych's Theorem 4.1. It is
   nontrivial because it contains `Λ`.
8. **`A(T)` is finitely presented.** By (P2).

So `K <= Λ <= A(T)`, a finitely presented simple group. ∎

## Where each premise can fail

- **The implication uses only minimality and freeness.** Finite type of `X` and
  finite presentation of `Λ` appear in the premises only to make (P2) plausible.
  `fp-alternating-full-groups-of-free-subshifts-force-sft` shows that finite
  presentation of the full group of `Λ ⋉ X` alone forces `X` to be of finite
  type, and a version of (P2) over all minimal free subshifts is expected to fail
  for the same reason.
- **(P1) is where decidability is consumed.** A finitely presented group with a
  nonempty strongly aperiodic subshift of finite type has solvable word problem
  (Jeandel, "Aperiodic subshifts of finite type on groups", arXiv:1501.06831;
  context only, not re-read at source and not used above). A nonempty minimal free
  subshift of finite type is strongly aperiodic, so (P1) cannot hold for an input
  with unsolvable word problem, as it must not. (P1) also needs `Λ` one-ended, by
  Cohen's theorem that groups with at least two ends carry no strongly aperiodic
  SFT (context only). One-endedness is harmless, since `Λ × Z` is one-ended.
- **(P2) is a pure finiteness statement.** It mentions no input and no
  computability. It fails as soon as one finitely presented `Λ` with a minimal
  free SFT gives a non-finitely-presented `A(T)`.
- **Hard inputs.** By `boone-higman-iff-simple-kazhdan-decidable-inputs` it is
  enough to have (P1) for infinite simple Kazhdan inputs. Such an input sits
  inside `Λ`. The commensurated-marking obstructions that kill Rover--Nekrashevych,
  eventually self-similar and graph-shift hosts
  (`fw-subgroups-of-eventually-similar-groups-virtually-embed`,
  `input-encoded-sft-groupoid-hosts-are-not-universal`) therefore do not reach
  this host.
- **Relation to the permutational route.** `A(T)` is a full Cantor group
  containing `V` through `v ↦ v × id_X`. When it is finitely presented,
  `fp-full-binary-cantor-groups-have-type-a-actions` should give it a type (A)
  action, so (P1) and (P2) would also give `permutational-boone-higman-conjecture`.
  The transport of the binary model along `C × X ≅ C` was not checked against that
  node's exact hypotheses, so this corollary is not claimed.
