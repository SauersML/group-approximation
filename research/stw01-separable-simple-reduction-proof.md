Only `(2) => (1)` needs proof.  Use the classical AW*-factor reduction of
Kaplansky's problem (the same literature input used in
`stw01-free-group-qt-invariance-equivalence-proof`).  Failure of `(1)` gives
a finite AW*-factor `M` with its faithful normalized `2`-quasitrace `q`
nonadditive.  Choose self-adjoint `x,y in M` such that

```text
q(x+y) != q(x)+q(y).                                      (1)
```

First observe that `M`, regarded only as a C*-algebra, is simple.  If
`0 != a in M_+`, choose `epsilon>0` for which the AW*-spectral projection

```text
p=1_[epsilon,infinity)(a)
```

is nonzero.  Since `pap >= epsilon p`, functional calculus in the corner
shows that `p` belongs to the closed two-sided ideal generated by `a`.
The dimension quasitrace is faithful, so `q(p)>0`.  Comparison of projections
in the finite AW*-factor gives an integer `n` for which `1` is subequivalent
to the direct sum of `n` copies of `p`.  Hence the ideal generated by `p`,
and therefore by `a`, contains `1`.

We use the standard separable-simple-subalgebra lemma:

> If `D` is a unital simple C*-algebra and `S subset D` is separable, there
> is a separable unital simple C*-subalgebra `B subset D` containing `S`.

For completeness, build increasing separable unital subalgebras.  At each
stage take a countable dense family of positive contractions and all rational
cutdowns which are nonzero.  Simplicity of `D` supplies finitely many
coefficients making each such cutdown full; adjoining the coefficients and
the inverse square root of the resulting strictly positive finite sum makes
the corresponding fullness equation exact at the next stage.  In the
closure, any nonzero positive element Cuntz-dominates one of the scheduled
cutdowns.  Its ideal therefore contains `1`, proving simplicity.

Apply the lemma to `D=M` and `S=C*(1,x,y)`.  The resulting algebra `B` is
separable, unital, and simple.  The restriction `q|B` is a normalized
`2`-quasitrace, remains faithful, and is nonadditive by (1).  Faithfulness of
the matrix extension of `q|B` rules out a proper isometry in every matrix
algebra over `B`: if `v*v=1` and `vv*<1`, quasitracial equality on equivalent
projections and additivity on the orthogonal projections `vv*` and
`1-vv*` contradict faithfulness.  Thus `B` is stably finite.  It is
non-elementary because quasitraces on finite-dimensional algebras are
traces.

This produces a counterexample to `(2)`.  The converse implication is
immediate, and Haagerup's exact-algebra theorem forces every witness just
constructed to be nonexact.

**Trust boundary.**  The only external structural input is the classical
equivalence between a counterexample to Kaplansky's problem and a finite
AW*-factor with nonadditive dimension quasitrace, already isolated by STW
and by `stw01-free-group-qt-invariance-equivalence-proof`.  The
separable-simple cutdown and stable-finiteness arguments are internal.
