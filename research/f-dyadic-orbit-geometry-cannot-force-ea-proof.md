**Imports (trust surface).**
- (J1) Juschenko–Matte Bon–Monod–de la Salle (JMBMdlS), Definition 1.1, quoted verbatim in
  `affine-lamplighter-amenable-iff-extensively-amenable-citation`: "The action of a group G on a set X is
  extensively amenable if there is a G-invariant mean on P_f(X) giving full weight to the collection of
  subsets that contain any given element of P_f(X)."
- (J2) JMBMdlS Lemma 2.1, same citation route: "Every action of an amenable group is extensively amenable,
  and every extensively amenable action on a nonempty set is amenable."
- (J3) The JMBMdlS inverted-orbit Proposition, quoted verbatim in
  `f-dyadic-ea-iff-inverted-orbit-confinement-proof`. It is stated for any finitely generated `G` acting
  transitively on `X`, with `μ` symmetric of generating support. It is used only for item 3(c).
- (BS) `pl-plus-interval-has-no-non-abelian-free-subgroups` (Brin–Squier). It is used only for item 4.

Everything else is elementary. `F` is the group of orientation-preserving PL homeomorphisms of `[0,1]`
with finitely many dyadic breakpoints and slopes in `2^Z`. It is generated by `x_0, x_1` with the
formulas in the script. `F` acts transitively on `D`. This is step 1 of
`jones-subgroup-coamenable-from-extensive-amenability-proof`, recorded in
`f-dyadic-ea-iff-inverted-orbit-confinement-proof`.

**Step 0 (coding).**
- Every block `0 1^m` (`m = 1, 2, 3`) starts with its only `0`.
- So in `w = u1` the zeros mark the block starts, and the runs of ones between zeros give `m_1, …, m_{k-1}`.
  The final run is `1^{m_k + 1}`.
- A dyadic has a unique finite binary expansion ending in `1`. So `φ(u) = .u1` determines `u`, and `φ` is
  injective. The script also checks this exhaustively on the 3280 block words of at most 7 blocks.
- A nonempty `u` begins with `01`, so `φ(u) ∈ [1/4, 1/2)`. Also `φ(∅) = .1 = 1/2`. Hence
  `Y ⊂ [1/4, 1/2]`.

**Step 1 (affine transport lemma).** Let `a, b` be binary words, each containing both letters. Let
`I_w = [.w, .w + 2^{−|w|}]`. Then there is `g ∈ F` that is affine from `I_a` onto `I_b`. So
`g(.aξ) = .bξ` for every binary word `ξ`.

*Proof.*
- The siblings of the prefixes of `a` form a standard dyadic subdivision of `[0,1]` that contains `I_a`.
  It has `ℓ_a ≥ 1` intervals left of `I_a` (because `a` contains a `1`) and `r_a ≥ 1` intervals right of
  it (because `a` contains a `0`). The same holds for `b`.
- Halve the largest intervals on each side until both subdivisions have `max(ℓ_a, ℓ_b)` intervals on the
  left and `max(r_a, r_b)` on the right. They remain standard dyadic subdivisions.
- Let `g` map the `i`-th interval of the first subdivision affinely onto the `i`-th interval of the
  second. Each piece carries a standard dyadic interval onto a standard dyadic interval, so its slope is
  a power of `2` and its breakpoints are dyadic. Hence `g ∈ F`, and `g|I_a` is the affine map onto
  `I_b`. ∎

The script implements this construction (`element(a, b)`). For random tails it checks the slopes and
`g(.aξ) = .bξ` exactly.

**Step 2 (item 1, the Lipschitz tree).** A tree edge joins `u` and `cu`, where `u` and `cu` are reduced.

- **Case `u ≠ ∅`.** Write `u = c' u''` with `c' ≠ c`, and put `ξ = u''1`. Then `φ(u) = .c'ξ` and
  `φ(cu) = .cc'ξ`. The words `a = c'` and `b = cc'` both contain both letters. Step 1 gives
  `g_{c',c} ∈ F` with `g_{c',c} φ(u) = φ(cu)` for all such `u` at once. So
  `d(φ(u), φ(cu)) ≤ |g_{c',c}|`, the word length in `{x_0^{±1}, x_1^{±1}}`. There are six such
  elements, one for each ordered pair `c' ≠ c`.
- **Case `u = ∅`.** The three root edges `1/2 — φ(c)` have finite length by transitivity of `F` on `D`.
- **Constant.** Let `L` be the maximum of the six word lengths and the three root distances. `L` is
  finite.
- **Measurement.** The script's bidirectional BFS measures the distances actually realised:
  - `A→BA`: 7; `A→CA`: 9; `B→AB`: 5; `B→CB`: 9; `C→AC`: 5; `C→BC`: 7 (on 40 random tails each);
  - root edges: 3, 5, 7;
  - 60 random long edges: all at most 9.

  The proof uses only `L < ∞`. The value 9 describes the embedding but no step depends on it.

**Step 3 (the involutions and the free orbit).**
- Prepending `c` to a reduced `u` that does not begin with `c` gives a reduced word that begins with `c`.
  Stripping `c` from a reduced `cu'` gives a reduced `u'` that does not begin with `c`. So `σ_c` is an
  involution of `D`, and it preserves `Y`.
- By Step 2, `d(t, σ_c t) ≤ L` for every `t ∈ D`. The stripping move `φ(cu') ↦ φ(u')` is the tree edge
  between `u'` and `cu'`, and points off `Y` do not move.
- Identify the reduced word `c_1 ⋯ c_k` with the normal form `s_{c_1} ⋯ s_{c_k} ∈ Γ`. Then
  `ψ : Γ → Y`, `γ ↦ φ(normal form of γ)`, is a bijection (Step 0). It satisfies
  `ψ(s_c γ) = σ_c ψ(γ)`, because left multiplication by `s_c` prepends `s_c` or cancels a leading `s_c`.
- Hence `s_c ↦ σ_c` defines a homomorphism `π : Γ → Sym(D)`. Any three involutions define one, since `Γ`
  is a free product of copies of `Z/2`.
- Through `ψ`, `π(Γ) ↷ Y` is the left regular action of `Γ` on itself. That action is free, so `π` is
  injective. Put `H = π(Γ) ≅ Γ`.
- **`Γ` is nonamenable.** Its Cayley graph for `{s_A, s_B, s_C}` is the 3-regular tree. A finite vertex
  set `S` spans at most `|S| − 1` edges, so it has at least `3|S| − 2(|S| − 1) = |S| + 2` boundary
  edges. There are no Følner sets, so there is no invariant mean.

**Step 4 (item 2: `G ↷ D` is not extensively amenable).**
- Suppose `m` is a `G`-invariant mean on `P_f(D)` that gives full weight to `{E : E ⊇ E_0}` for every
  finite `E_0 ⊆ D` (J1).
- Then `m` is `H`-invariant, since `H ≤ G`.
- Let `r : P_f(D) → P_f(Y)`, `E ↦ E ∩ Y`. Because `hY = Y` for `h ∈ H`, we have `r(hE) = h r(E)`, so
  `r_* m` is an `H`-invariant mean on `P_f(Y)`.
- For finite `E_0 ⊆ Y`, `r^{-1}{E' : E' ⊇ E_0} = {E : E ⊇ E_0}` has full weight.
- So `H ↷ Y` is extensively amenable, and `Y ≠ ∅`. By (J2), `H ↷ Y` is amenable: it has an
  `H`-invariant mean on `Y`.
- Transported by `ψ`, this is a left-invariant mean on `Γ`. That contradicts Step 3.

**Step 5 (item 2: transitivity, amenability, wobbling and bi-Lipschitz).**
- **Transitive.** `G ⊇ F` and `F` is transitive on `D`.
- **Amenable.** Take `S_N = {2^{−k}/8 : 0 ≤ k < N} ⊂ (0, 1/8]`.
  - Every `σ_c` fixes `S_N` pointwise, because `Y ⊂ [1/4, 1/2]`.
  - `x_1` is the identity on `[0, 1/2]`.
  - `x_0(t) = 2t` on `[0, 1/4]`, so `x_0 S_N Δ S_N = {1/4, 2^{−(N−1)}/8}`.
  - So `|s S_N Δ S_N| ≤ 2` for every generator `s`, while `|S_N| = N`. A weak-* limit of the uniform
    probability measures on `S_N` is a `G`-invariant mean on `D`. The script checks the boundary count
    (2 edges) for `N = 200`.
- **Wobbling.** Bounded-displacement bijections form a group:
  - `d(t, gh t) ≤ d(t, ht) + d(ht, g ht)`;
  - `d(t, g^{-1} t) = d(g s, s)` with `s = g^{-1} t`.

  The generators of `G` have displacement at most `max(1, L)`. So `G ≤ W(Γ_D)`.
- **Bi-Lipschitz.** Let `d_G` be the Schreier metric of `G` for `{x_0^{±1}, x_1^{±1}, σ_A, σ_B, σ_C}`.
  Every edge of `Γ_D` is an edge of that graph, so `d_G ≤ d`. Every edge of that graph has `d`-length at
  most `L`, so `d ≤ L d_G`.

**Step 6 (item 3).**
- **Main statement.** If `F ↷ D` had a property `P` that depends only on the Schreier graph up to a
  bi-Lipschitz bijection of vertex sets, then `G ↷ D` would have `P`, by Step 5. If `P` implied extensive
  amenability, `G ↷ D` would be extensively amenable, contradicting Step 4.
- **(a)** A `K`-invariant mean is invariant under every subgroup of `K`. So if `K ⊇ G` acts extensively
  amenably on `D`, so does `G`, which Step 4 excludes. This applies in particular to `K = W(Γ_D)`.
- **(b)** Each invariant listed there is a bi-Lipschitz invariant of bounded-degree graphs, up to
  multiplicative constants in space and time:
  - recurrence (rough-isometry invariance, Kanai and T. Lyons);
  - growth type;
  - isoperimetric and Følner profiles;
  - resistance growth;
  - the return-probability decay class (Pittet–Saloff-Coste).

  So any criterion whose hypothesis is such an invariant, taken up to those constants, and whose
  conclusion is extensive amenability, does not apply to `Γ_D`: if it did, it would also apply to
  `G ↷ D`. The Liouville property is deliberately left off, because it is not a rough-isometry invariant
  (T. Lyons). This argument does not touch it.
- **(c)** `G` is finitely generated and transitive on `D`, and `μ_G` is symmetric with generating
  support. By (J3), condition (ii) fails for `G ↷ D`: there is `ε > 0` with `P(|O_n| < εn) ≤ e^{−εn}` for
  all large `n`. Condition (i) fails as well.

**Step 7 (item 4, the order gate).** Let `h ∈ W^+(Γ_D)`, `R = sup_t d(t, ht)`, and `B = B_F(R)`, the
finite set of elements of word length at most `R`.

- **Local pieces.** For each `t ∈ D`, a path of length at most `R` from `t` to `ht` in `Γ_D` is labelled
  by a word, so `ht = gt` for some `g ∈ B`.
- **Extension.** `h` is an order-preserving bijection of the dense set `D`. It extends uniquely to an
  increasing homeomorphism `Ĥ` of `[0,1]`:
  - `Ĥ(x) = sup{h t : t ∈ D, t ≤ x}` for `x > 0`, and `Ĥ(0) = 0`;
  - `Ĥ` has no jumps because its image contains the dense set `D`;
  - `Ĥ` is strictly increasing because `h` is and `D` is dense.
- **Covering by closed sets.** Put `C_g = {x : Ĥ(x) = g(x)}`, a closed set. Then `D ⊆ ⋃_{g∈B} C_g`. A
  finite union of closed sets is closed, so `⋃_{g∈B} C_g = [0,1]`.
- **Coincidence sets.** For `g ≠ g'` in `B`, the set `E_{g,g'} = {g = g'}` is the fixed set of the
  nontrivial PL map `g^{-1}g'`. On each affine piece that fixed set is empty, a point or the whole piece,
  so `E_{g,g'}` is a finite union of points and closed intervals. Let `Z` be `{0,1}` together with the
  boundary points of all the `E_{g,g'}`. `Z` is finite.
- **One element per component.** Let `J` be a component of `[0,1] \ Z`.
  - Each `E_{g,g'} ∩ J` is closed in `J`. It is also open in `J`, since a non-interior point of
    `E_{g,g'}` lies in `Z`. As `J` is connected, `J ⊆ E_{g,g'}` or `J ∩ E_{g,g'} = ∅`.
  - Group `B` into classes of equality on `J`. For `g, g'` in different classes,
    `C_g ∩ C_{g'} ∩ J ⊆ E_{g,g'} ∩ J = ∅`.
  - So the sets `⋃_{g∈κ} C_g ∩ J`, one for each class `κ`, are finitely many pairwise disjoint relatively
    closed sets that cover the connected set `J`. Exactly one of them is nonempty, and it equals `J`.
    Hence `Ĥ = g` on `J` for any `g` in that class.
- **Conclusion.**
  - `Ĥ` is a homeomorphism that agrees with a single element of `B` on each of the finitely many
    components of `[0,1] \ Z`. Its breakpoints lie in the finite set `Z ∪ ⋃_{g∈B} bp(g)`. So
    `Ĥ ∈ PL_+([0,1])`.
  - `h ↦ Ĥ` is an injective homomorphism, since extensions from a dense set are unique. So
    `W^+(Γ_D) ≤ PL_+([0,1])` and, by (BS), it has no non-abelian free subgroup.
- **The gate.** If `H' ≤ W^+(Γ_D)` acts on `D` but not extensively amenably, then by the first half of
  (J2) `H'` is not amenable. So `PL_+([0,1])` has a nonamenable subgroup, which is alternative (b) of item 4
  of `pl-ea-calibrators-are-nonamenable-interval-groups`.
- **`G` is not order-preserving.** An order-preserving involution of a totally ordered set is the
  identity: if `σ t > t`, then `t = σσ t > σ t`. Since `σ_A ≠ id`, `σ_A ∉ W^+(Γ_D)`. More strongly, `G`
  contains `H ≅ Γ` and so non-abelian free subgroups, which `W^+(Γ_D)` cannot contain. ∎

**What the script does not prove.** The script certifies:
- Step 0 on a finite range;
- the explicit elements of Step 1 for the six edge types;
- the measured distances;
- the Følner count.

It does not certify the word lengths of the `g_{c',c}`, and none are needed: Step 2 uses only
finiteness.
