Let a countable group `H` act essentially freely by probability-preserving
transformations on a standard probability space `(K,m)`, put

```text
D=L^infinity(K),       M=D rtimes H,
```

and let `T subset H minus {e}` be countable. For every `t in T` there are countably
many three-grade Hadamard cells `W_(t,n)` of
`three-actor-grades-support-an-exact-hadamard-cell` such that

```text
D vee {W_(t,n):t in T,n>=1}
      = D rtimes <T>.                                  (CTG1)
```

More explicitly, for each `t` there is a measurable partition
`(q_(t,n))_n` of `1` with

```text
q_(t,n) alpha_(t^(-1))(q_(t,n))=0.                    (CTG2)
```

Set

```text
p_(t,n)=alpha_(t^(-1))(q_(t,n)),
v_(t,n)=q_(t,n)U_t,
r_(t,n)=1-p_(t,n)-q_(t,n),

W_(t,n)=r_(t,n)
 +2^(-1/2)(p_(t,n)-q_(t,n)+v_(t,n)+v_(t,n)^*).        (CTG3)
```

Then compression by the named base projections recovers the actor arrow:

```text
q_(t,n) W_(t,n) p_(t,n)=2^(-1/2)v_(t,n).              (CTG4)
```

Since the `q_(t,n)` partition `1`, strong summation gives

```text
U_t=sum_n v_(t,n).                                    (CTG5)
```

Thus the base together with the cells recovers every `U_t`, proving
`(CTG1)`.

This has two consequences for the Manzoor route.

1. A countable three-grade-cell assembly contains the specific non-CE
   relation factor if and only if the crossed product generated by its base
   and actor directions contains that factor. The cells remove finite-grade
   syntax as an obstruction but do not weaken the algebraic-envelope theorem.
2. If `<T>` is amenable, the right side of `(CTG1)` is amenable and cannot
   contain the non-CE Manzoor factor. For Manzoor's native virtually-free
   actor, `abelian-by-virtually-free-groups-are-sofic` makes the corresponding
   Haar algebraic crossed product Connes embeddable, so no such assembly can
   work there either. Combined with
   `haagerup-actors-cannot-host-property-t-algebraic-envelope`, any successful
   cell directions must generate a non-Haagerup replacement actor and solve
   the original global envelope problem inside its crossed product.
