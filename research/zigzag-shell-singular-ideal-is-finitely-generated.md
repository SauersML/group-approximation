**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, not reviewed; no priority claimed).

## The shell

Take `P = Z` with generator `1`, and let `nu` be the zigzag `nu(k) = 2k - 1` for `k >= 1` and
`nu(k) = -2k` for `k <= 0`. With the parity convention of
`shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`, `rho_1` acts on `N` as follows:
- `n |-> n + 4` if `n ≡ 2 (mod 4)`;
- `n |-> n - 4` if `n ≡ 0 (mod 4)` and `n >= 4`, and `0 |-> 2`;
- the odd `n` are fixed.

Let `A = A_K(G_nu)` over any field `K`, and let `J` be its singular ideal. By
`shell-germ-essential-algebras-are-central-simple`, `A/J` is central simple and contains
`E_nu`.

## Statement

1. **Germ group.** Put `x = r_1`, `y = τ x τ^(-1)` and `t = τ^4`. Then `N_0 = <x, y, t>` is
   free abelian of rank 3 and normal in `Q = <τ, x>`. Conjugation by `τ` acts by
   `x |-> y |-> x^(-1)` and fixes `t`, and `Q/N_0 ≅ Z/4`. So `K[Q] = ⊕_(j<4) R τ^j` with
   `R = K[x^(±1), y^(±1), t^(±1)]`.
2. **Local part.** Let `I_* = (y-1, xt-1) ∩ (y-1, x-t) ∩ (x-1, yt-1) ∩ (x-1, y-t)` in `R`.
   Then `J_p = ⊕_j I_* τ^j = I_* K[Q]`.
3. **Finite generation.** `J` is finitely generated as a two-sided ideal of `A`.
4. **Not by the four-germ elements.** The ideal generated by
   `f_(g,h) = (1 - u_g) s_0 (1 - u_h) t_0`, `g, h ∈ {±1}`, is strictly smaller than `J`. A
   witness is the element of `J_p` given by `(x - t)(xt - 1)(y - t)(yt - 1)`.

## Proof

**1.** Split `N` into the residue classes `N_i`, `i ∈ Z/4`, and write `n = 4m + i`. Every
element of `Q` acts at infinity by a permutation of the classes followed by a translation on
each class. On the four classes the translations are:
- `x`: `(-4, 0, +4, 0)`;
- `τ x τ^(-1)`, i.e. `n |-> x(n-1) + 1`: `(0, -4, 0, +4)`, which is `y`;
- `τ^2 x τ^(-2)`: `(+4, 0, -4, 0)`, which is `x^(-1)`;
- `t`: `(4, 4, 4, 4)`.

The vectors for `x`, `y` and `t` are independent, and `τ, τ^2, τ^3` permute the classes
nontrivially.

**2.** `M = K^N / K^(N)` splits as `⊕_i M_i`, where `M_i` is the corresponding module on
`N_i`. Let `S` be the shift `m |-> m + 1` on `N_i`. Then `t` acts as `S` on every `M_i`, and:
- `x` acts as `S^(-1)` on `M_0`, as `S` on `M_2`, and trivially on `M_1` and `M_3`;
- `y` acts as `S^(-1)` on `M_1`, as `S` on `M_3`, and trivially on `M_0` and `M_2`.

A nonzero Laurent polynomial in `S` acts nonzero on `M_i`, by the sparse-sum test of
`shell-germ-singular-ideal-is-generated-at-the-singular-point`. So `Ann_R(M_i)` is the kernel
of the corresponding map `R -> K[S^(±1)]`:
- `(y-1, xt-1)` for `i = 0`;
- `(x-1, yt-1)` for `i = 1`;
- `(y-1, x-t)` for `i = 2`;
- `(x-1, y-t)` for `i = 3`.

Now take `X = Σ_j X_j τ^j`. The term `X_j τ^j` maps `M_i` to `M_(i+j)`, and `τ^j` is a
bijection there. So `X ∈ Ann(M) = J_p` iff every `X_j` lies in all four kernels, i.e. in
`I_*`.

**3.** `K[Q]` is Noetherian, since `Q` is virtually `Z^3`. Apply part 4 of
`shell-germ-singular-ideal-is-generated-at-the-singular-point`.

**4.** The `(p,p)` entries of the `f_(g,h)` are units times `(1-x)(1-y)`, because
`τ(1-x)(1-y)τ^(-1) = (1-y)(1-x^(-1))`. So by part 3 of the same node, the ideal they generate
has local part `(1-x)(1-y) K[Q] = ⊕_j (1-x)(1-y) R τ^j`. The witness lies in each of the four
prime ideals, since one of its factors does. It is not divisible by `1 - x`: setting `x = 1`
gives `-(t-1)^2 (y-t)(yt-1) ≠ 0`. `∎`

## Scope

- `P = Z` is not an input the Boone--Higman route needs. The point is that for one explicit
  shell, the first gate of the repaired route (`J` finitely generated) holds.
- The singular ideal is not generated by the obvious four-germ elements. The extra generators
  come from the four arithmetic progressions on which the near action is a translation.
- For infinite simple `P` the germ group ring is not Noetherian, and the question is open.
