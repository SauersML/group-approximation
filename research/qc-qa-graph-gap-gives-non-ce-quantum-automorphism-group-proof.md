**Sources, read at source.**  BCEHPSW = Brannan, Chirvasitu, Eifler, Harris,
Paulsen, Su, Wasilewski, *Bigalois extensions and the graph isomorphism game*,
arXiv:1812.11474v2, Comm. Math. Phys. 375 (2020).

- **(B1)** Theorem 2.1(3): a synchronous game has a perfect `qa`-strategy iff
  its game algebra has a unital *-homomorphism into an ultrapower of the
  hyperfinite II_1 factor.
- **(B2)** Remark 4.3: for classical graphs `A(Iso(X,Y))` is the universal
  unital *-algebra generated by the entries of a magic unitary `V=(v_(xa))`,
  `x in V(X)`, `a in V(Y)`, with `A_X V = V A_Y`.  For `X = Y = Z` it is the
  Hopf *-algebra `O(G_Z)` of the quantum automorphism group, with
  `Delta(u_(cd)) = sum_e u_(ce) (x) u_(ed)`.
- **(B3)** Theorem 4.9: for classical graphs, `X ≅_qc Y` iff
  `A(Iso(X,Y)) != 0`.
- **(B4)** Theorem 4.7, Example 3.14 and Theorem 3.15(1): the Haar state `h`
  of `O(G_Z)` is the unique bi-invariant state.  It is tracial for a classical
  graph `Z` (Kac type).

**Credit.**  Lupini--Mančinska--Roberson, arXiv:1712.01820v2, read at source.
Steps 2--6 re-derive the corner construction from the proof of their
Theorem 4.4:
- connectivity through complements and their Corollary 4.7;
- the projection `p = sum_(y in Y) u_(xy)` in `C(Qut(X ⊔ Y))`;
- `p != 0` through a representation;
- the normalized Haar state on the corner, with `h(p) > 0` from their
  Corollary 4.3.

They obtain the vanishing `u_(xy) u_(x'x'') = 0` from the quantum orbital
configuration; Steps 1 and 3 here obtain it from Schur closure of intertwining
pairs.  Step 5 (`h(P) = 1/2` by invariance) and Step 7 (non-embeddability) are
the additions.

Assume `(QG1)`: `A(Iso(X,Y)) != 0`, and there is no unital *-homomorphism
`A(Iso(X,Y)) -> R^U`.

**Step 1: intertwining pairs are Schur closed.**  Let `V` be a magic unitary
over a unital *-algebra, with rows indexed by `V(X)` and columns by `V(Y)`.
Let `M`, `N` be complex matrices of matching sizes.  Then

```text
MV = VN   iff   M_(xx') v_(xa) v_(x'a') = N_(aa') v_(xa) v_(x'a')   for all x, x', a, a'.   (S1)
```

- **(=>)** Suppose `MV = VN`.  The `(x,a')` entry is
  `sum_(x'') M_(xx'') v_(x''a') = sum_b v_(xb) N_(ba')`.  Multiply on the left
  by `v_(xa)`, using `v_(xa) v_(xb) = delta_(ab) v_(xa)`.  Then multiply on the
  right by `v_(x'a')`, using `v_(x''a') v_(x'a') = delta_(x''x') v_(x'a')`.
  The result is (S1).
- **(<=)** Write `v_(x'a') = sum_a v_(xa) v_(x'a')` (row `x` sums to one) and
  apply (S1).  Then sum over `x'`, using `sum_(x') v_(x'a') = 1`.

So the set `W(V)` of pairs `(M,N)` with `MV = VN` has these properties:

- it contains `(I,I)`, and `(J,J)`, since rows and columns sum to one;
- it is closed under sums, scalar multiples and products `(MM', NN')`;
- it is closed under entrywise products `(M o M', N o N')`: apply (S1) twice.

**Step 2: sizes, complements, connectivity.**  Let `V` be a magic unitary
with `A_X V = V A_Y` over a nonzero algebra.

1. **Sizes.**  Summing all entries by rows and by columns gives
   `|V(X)| 1 = |V(Y)| 1`, so both graphs have `n` vertices.
2. **Complements.**  `(J-I-A_X, J-I-A_Y) in W(V)`, and the converse also holds.
   So `A(Iso(X,Y))` and `A(Iso(X^c,Y^c))` are the same presented algebra, and
   `(QG1)` passes to the complements.
3. **Connectivity passes from `X` to `Y`.**
   - Let `K = sum_(k=0)^(n-1) A^k`, with entries in `{0,...,L}` for both
     graphs.  Let `p` be a polynomial with `p(0)=0` and `p(1)=...=p(L)=1`.
   - Applying `p` entrywise, through Schur powers and `J`, gives the
     same-component matrix `R`.  The same operations act on both coordinates,
     so `(R_X, R_Y) in W(V)`.
   - If `X` is connected, then `R_X = J` and `(JV)_(xa) = 1`.  But
     `(V R_Y)_(xa)` is the sum of `v_(xb)` over the component of `a`.  So
     each component of `Y` carries row mass one.  The row sums to one, so `Y`
     has a single component.

Either `X` or `X^c` is connected, so we may assume `X` and `Y` connected.

**Step 3: the central projection.**  Put `Z = X ⊔ Y`, `G = G_Z`, and let `U`
be the magic unitary of `O(G)`.

- Step 1 for the pair `(A_Z, A_Z) in W(U)` gives `(R_Z,R_Z) in W(U)` with
  `R_Z = J_X (+) J_Y`.
- Read `U R_Z = R_Z U` entrywise on the four blocks and use that rows and
  columns sum to one.  For `x, x' in X` and `y, y' in Y` this gives

  ```text
  sum_(d in Y) u_(xd) = sum_(c in X) u_(cy) = sum_(c in X) u_(yc) = sum_(d in Y) u_(dx') =: P,
  sum_(c in X) u_(xc) = sum_(c in X) u_(cx') = sum_(d in Y) u_(yd) = sum_(d in Y) u_(dy') = 1-P.   (S2)
  ```

- So `u_(cd) <= P` when `c` and `d` lie in different parts, and
  `u_(cd) <= 1-P` otherwise.  Hence `P` commutes with every generator and is
  central.

**Step 4: `P != 0`.**  By (B3) there is a magic unitary `V` over
`A(Iso(X,Y)) != 0` with `A_X V = V A_Y`.

- Define the block matrix `U' = [[0, V], [V^t, 0]]`, where `(V^t)_(ax) = v_(xa)`.
  It is a magic unitary.
- Transposing entrywise gives `V^t A_X = A_Y V^t`, since adjacency matrices
  are symmetric with scalar entries.  So `A_Z U' = U' A_Z`.
- By (B2) there is a unital *-homomorphism `O(G) -> A(Iso(X,Y))` with
  `U |-> U'`.  It sends `P` to `sum_(b in Y) v_(xb) = 1 != 0`.

**Step 5: `h(P) = 1/2`.**  Apply right invariance `(id (x) h) Delta = h(.) 1`
to `P = sum_(d in Y) u_(xd)`, and use (S2):

```text
h(P) 1 = sum_e u_(xe) h( sum_(d in Y) u_(ed) ) = (1-P) h(P) + P h(1-P).
```

Also `h(P) 1 = (1-P) h(P) + P h(P)`.  Subtracting gives
`P (h(1-P) - h(P)) = 0`, so by Step 4 `h(P) = h(1-P) = 1/2`.

**Step 6: the corner homomorphism.**

- In the corner `P O(G)`, whose unit is `P`, the block `(u_(xa))_(x in X, a in Y)`
  is a magic unitary by (S2).
- The `(X,Y)` block of `A_Z U = U A_Z` reads `A_X (u_(xa)) = (u_(xa)) A_Y`.
- By (B2), `psi: v_(xa) |-> u_(xa)` is a unital *-homomorphism
  `A(Iso(X,Y)) -> P O(G)`.  By (B4) and Step 5, `tau = 2 h o psi` is a
  tracial state.

**Step 7: non-embeddability.**  Suppose `L^infinity(G) = pi_h(O(G))''`, with
trace `h`, has a trace-preserving embedding into `R^U`.

- `pi_h(P)` has trace `1/2`.  The corner `pi_h(P) L^infinity(G) pi_h(P)`, with
  trace `2h`, embeds trace-preservingly into a corner `q R^U q` with
  `tau(q) = 1/2`.
- `q R^U q ≅ R^U`: lift `q` to projections of trace `1/2` in `R`, whose
  corners are copies of `R`.
- Composing with `pi_h o psi` gives a unital *-homomorphism
  `A(Iso(X,Y)) -> R^U`.  This contradicts `(QG1)` through (B1).

So `L^infinity(G_Z)` is not Connes embeddable.  The proof uses only invariance
and traciality of `h`: no faithfulness, coamenability or residual finite-
dimensionality.
