Let `Φ: R_Y → R_X` be a ring isomorphism.

**1. Strong orbit equivalence.** This is `cantor-crossed-product-matrix-ring-iso-forces-soe` with `m = 1` and `k = k' = F_2`.
Infinite minimal subshifts are minimal Cantor systems.

**2. Growth.** Let `V_X ⊆ R_X` and `V_Y ⊆ R_Y` be the finite-dimensional generating subspaces of
`subshift-crossed-product-growth-is-n-times-complexity`, each containing `1`. That claim identifies the algebra of the
shift groupoid with the crossed product.
- Since `1 ∈ V_X`, the powers `V_X^k` increase, and their union is the subalgebra generated by `V_X`, which is `R_X`.
- `Φ` is `F_2`-linear, because every ring isomorphism of `F_2`-algebras is: `F_2` is the prime field. So `Φ(V_Y)` is a
  finite-dimensional subspace of `R_X`, and some `C ≥ 1` has `Φ(V_Y) ⊆ V_X^C`.
- Then `Φ(V_Y^n) ⊆ V_X^{Cn}` for all `n`, so `dim V_Y^n ≤ dim V_X^{Cn}`.
- The two sides of the imported estimate give
  `⌊n/2⌋·p_Y(⌊n/2⌋) ≤ dim V_Y^n ≤ dim V_X^{Cn} ≤ (2Cn+1)·p_X(2Cn)`.
- Apply the same argument to `Φ^{-1}` and take the larger constant.

**3. Entropy.** For a subshift, `h_top = lim_n (1/n) log p(n)`. Put `n = 2m` in item 2:
`m·p_Y(m) ≤ (4Cm+1)·p_X(4Cm)`. Take logarithms, divide by `m`, and let `m → ∞`. This gives
`h_top(Y) ≤ 4C·h_top(X)`, and the symmetric inequality holds. In particular `h_top(X) = 0 ⟺ h_top(Y) = 0`.

**4. GK dimension.**
- For an infinite subshift `p` is nondecreasing, since every word of `X` extends to a longer word of `X`.
- The upper estimate gives `limsup log dim V_X^n / log n ≤ 1 + limsup log p_X(2n)/log n = 1 + limsup log p_X(m)/log m`.
  Monotonicity lets the limsup over even `m` equal the limsup over all `m`.
- The lower estimate gives the reverse inequality in the same way.
- So `GKdim R_X = 1 + limsup log p_X(n)/log n`. GK dimension is independent of the generating subspace and invariant
  under isomorphism, so the exponent agrees for `X` and `Y`.
