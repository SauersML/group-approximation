All complexes are CW complexes, and all maps between them are cellular.

**Lemma 1 (free-base extension).** Let `H ≤ G` with `H` free, let `X` be a 2-dimensional `K(G,1)`, and let `K` be any 2-dimensional `K(H,1)`. Then there is a 2-dimensional `K(G,1)`, `M`, that contains `K` as a subcomplex, with `K ↪ M` inducing the inclusion `H ≤ G` on `π_1`.

*Proof.*
1. **Graph models.** Let `Γ` be a wedge of circles indexed by a free basis of `H`.
   - Define `g: Γ → K` by sending each circle to a based edge-loop in `K^1` representing that basis element. This uses cellular approximation relative to the vertex.
   - `g` is a `π_1`-isomorphism between aspherical CW complexes, so it is a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem.
   - Likewise choose a cellular `h: Γ → X` with `h_* = (H ≤ G)` after composing with `g_*^{-1}`.
2. **The complex.** Let `M = K ∪_g (Γ × [0,1]) ∪_h X` be the double mapping cylinder, with `Γ×0` glued to `K` by `g` and `Γ×1` glued to `X` by `h`.
   - `M` is a CW complex of dimension `max(dim K, 2, dim X) = 2`, and `K` is a subcomplex of it.
3. **Homotopy type.** `M` is the pushout of `X ←h− Γ×1 ↪ Mcyl(g)`.
   - The inclusion `Γ×1 ↪ Mcyl(g)` is a cofibration and a homotopy equivalence, because `g` is. So it is the inclusion of a strong deformation retract.
   - Strong deformation retracts are preserved under pushout, so `X ↪ M` is a homotopy equivalence.
   - Hence `M` is a `K(G,1)`.
4. **Fundamental group.** The inclusion `K ↪ M` factors up to homotopy as `K ≃ Γ −h→ X ≃ M`. So on `π_1` it induces `H ≤ G`. ∎

**Lemma 2 (graph-of-groups step).** Let `G_+` be the fundamental group of a graph of groups whose vertex `v_0` carries `G_0`, whose edge groups are free, and whose other vertex groups `G_v` have `gd ≤ 2`. Let `K` be any 2-dimensional `K(G_0,1)`. Then some 2-dimensional `K(G_+,1)` contains `K` as a subcomplex, inducing `G_0 ≤ G_+`.

*Proof.* Build a graph of spaces.
- **Vertex spaces.** `K` at `v_0`, and a 2-dimensional `K(G_v,1)` at each other vertex.
- **Edge spaces.** For each edge, `Γ_e × [0,1]`, where `Γ_e` is a wedge of circles with `π_1 = G_e`. Its two ends are attached by cellular maps realizing the two edge monomorphisms, as in Lemma 1, step 1.
- **Result.**
  - The total space is 2-dimensional and contains `K` as a subcomplex.
  - Its fundamental group is `G_+` (van Kampen for graphs of spaces).
  - It is aspherical because it is a graph of aspherical spaces with `π_1`-injective attaching maps (Scott–Wall, *Topological methods in group theory*, 1979, Prop. 3.6). ∎

**Lemma 3 (limits).** Let `(K_α)_{α<μ}` be an increasing family of aspherical complexes, each a subcomplex of the next, with a common base vertex. Then `K_μ = ⋃ K_α` is aspherical and `π_1 K_μ = colim π_1 K_α`.

*Proof.* Spheres, and homotopies between them, have compact images. Each image lies in a finite subcomplex, hence in some `K_α`. So `π_n K_μ = colim π_n K_α`, which is `0` for `n ≥ 2`. ∎

**Theorem.** In the situation of the claim, `gd G ≤ 2`.

*Proof.* By transfinite recursion, build 2-dimensional complexes `K_α` with `K_α` a subcomplex of `K_β` for `α ≤ β`. Each `K_α` is a `K(G_α,1)`, and each inclusion induces `G_α ≤ G_β`.
- **Start.** `K_0` is a point.
- **Successor, type (i).** Apply Lemma 1 with `H = G_α`, `G = G_{α+1}`, `K = K_α`, and any 2-dimensional `K(G_{α+1},1)` as `X`.
- **Successor, type (ii).** Apply Lemma 2.
- **Limit `μ`.** Set `K_μ = ⋃_{α<μ} K_α`. Lemma 3 and continuity `G_μ = ⋃ G_α` show that it is a 2-dimensional `K(G_μ,1)`.

Then `K_λ = ⋃ K_α` is a 2-dimensional `K(G,1)`. (Choose the attaching maps based, so that the identifications of `π_1` are compatible. Lemma 1 lets `h` be based.) ∎

**Corollary C1.** Let `G` be `κ`-free of cardinality `κ`.
- Enumerate `G = {g_ξ : ξ < κ}` and put `G_α = ⟨g_ξ : ξ < α⟩` for `α ≤ κ`. This is a continuous chain with `G_0 = 1` and `G_κ = G`.
- For `α < κ`, `G_α` is generated by fewer than `κ` elements, so it is free. (Do not use "subgroups of size `< κ`" when `κ = ℵ_0`: that only says `G` is torsion-free, and `Z^3` shows the conclusion would fail.)
- Each successor step `G_α ≤ G_{α+1}` with `α+1 < κ` is between free groups, so it is of type (i) (a free `G_{α+1}` has a 1-dimensional `K(G_{α+1},1)`).
- `κ` is an infinite cardinal, hence a limit ordinal, so `G = ⋃_{α<κ} G_α` and the theorem (with `λ = κ`) gives `gd G ≤ 2`.

**Corollary C2.** If `G` is as in C1 but not free, then `cd G ≥ 2`. Otherwise `cd G ≤ 1`, and Stallings–Swan (valid without finite generation, Swan 1969) makes `G` free. So `cd G = gd G = 2`.

*Cross-check with homological algebra.* Auslander's lemma recovers `cd G ≤ 2` independently.
- `I_G` is the continuous union of the `ZG ⊗_{ZG_α} I_{G_α}`.
- The successive quotients are induced from `I_{G_{α+1}} / ZG_{α+1} I_{G_α} ≅ I_{G_{α+1}/G_α}`.
- That module has `pd ≤ 1`, as the quotient of a free module (`I_{G_{α+1}}`, since `G_{α+1}` is free) by a free submodule.

So the geometric statement is consistent with, and strictly refines, the known algebra.

*Calibration.*
- For a countable chain of free groups the recursion is the mapping-telescope argument.
- For a free group `F` (a trivial chain), it yields the 2-dimensional `K(F,1)` obtained by gluing mapping cylinders, which collapses to a graph.
- The Klein-bottle example in the claim shows that the free hypothesis in Lemma 1 cannot simply be dropped.
