If `gamma` is order zero, apply it to the orthogonal positive elements
`(1_F,0)` and `(0,1_G)`.  Their images are `alpha(1_F)` and `beta(1_G)`, so
their product is zero.

Conversely, set `h=alpha(1_F)` and `k=beta(1_G)`.  Every value of an
order-zero map belongs to the hereditary C*-algebra generated by the image
of the unit: this follows directly from the supporting-homomorphism form

```text
alpha(x)=pi_alpha(x)h,       beta(y)=pi_beta(y)k.
```

If `hk=0`, the hereditary algebras generated by `h` and `k` are orthogonal.
Consequently every value of `alpha` annihilates every value of `beta` in
both orders.  Orthogonality is already preserved within each summand, since
`alpha` and `beta` are order zero.  Hence `gamma` preserves orthogonality on
all of `F directSum G`, which is precisely the order-zero condition.
