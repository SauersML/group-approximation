Direct proof. Not independently reviewed.

## Step 1. Divisible groups have no nontrivial finite quotient

Let `D` be a divisible abelian group and `phi : D -> F` a homomorphism to a
finite group of order `N`. For `x in D` write `x = N y`. Then
`phi(x) = phi(y)^N = 1`. So `phi` is trivial.

For `i != j` and `q in Q` let `e_ij(q)` be the elementary matrix with `q` in
position `(i,j)`. The root group `U_ij = {e_ij(q) : q in Q}` is isomorphic to
`(Q,+)` by `q -> e_ij(q)`, so it is divisible.

## Step 2. `SL_n(K)` is generated by elementary matrices, for every field `K`

Left multiplication by `e_ij(t)` adds `t` times row `j` to row `i`; right
multiplication adds `t` times column `i` to column `j`. Let `g in SL_n(K)`,
`n >= 2`.

- If `g_21 = 0`, some `g_i1 != 0`; adding row `i` to row 2 (or, if only
  `g_11 != 0`, row 1 to row 2) makes `g_21 != 0`.
- Adding `(1 - g_11)/g_21` times row 2 to row 1 makes `g_11 = 1`.
- Row operations with row 1 clear the rest of column 1, and column operations with
  column 1 clear the rest of row 1.

The result is `diag(1, g')` with `g' in SL_(n-1)(K)`. Repeating on `g'` reaches
`diag(1,...,1,d)`, and `d = det g = 1`. So `g` is a product of elementary
matrices.

## Step 3. Finite-index subgroups (part 1)

Let `L <= H` have finite index and let `N` be its normal core, the intersection
of the finitely many conjugates of `L`. Then `N` is normal of finite index, and
`H -> H/N` restricts to a homomorphism from each `U_ij` to a finite group. By
Step 1 it is trivial, so every `U_ij <= N`. By Step 2, `SL_n(Q) <= N <= L`.

## Step 4. Homomorphisms to residually finite groups (part 2)

Let `psi : H -> R` with `R` residually finite. For each finite-index `M <= R`,
`psi^-1(M)` has finite index in `H`, so it contains `SL_n(Q)` by Step 3. Hence
`psi(SL_n(Q))` lies in the intersection of all finite-index subgroups of `R`,
which is trivial. Since `SL_n(Q) = ker(det)` and `det : GL_n(Q) -> Q^x` is onto,
`psi` restricted to `GL_n(Q)` factors through `det`.

## Step 5. The finite residual of `GL_n(Q)` (part 3)

Step 3 with `H = GL_n(Q)` gives that the finite residual contains `SL_n(Q)`.

Conversely, by unique factorization `Q^x ~ {±1} x ⊕_p Z`, a direct sum of
residually finite groups, hence residually finite. If `g in GL_n(Q)` has
`det g != 1`, some finite-index subgroup `M` of `Q^x` misses `det g`, and
`det^-1(M)` is a finite-index subgroup of `GL_n(Q)` missing `g`. So the finite
residual is contained in `ker(det) = SL_n(Q)`.

The last sentence of part 3 follows. The kernel of `H -> Ĥ` (profinite
completion) is the finite residual of `H`, which contains `SL_n(Q)` by part 1.
So the image of `GL_n(Q)` is a quotient of `GL_n(Q)/SL_n(Q) ~ Q^x`.
