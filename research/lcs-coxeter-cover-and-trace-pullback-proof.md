Let `X` be the finite BCS variable set. For each variable let

```text
s_x=s_x*=s_x^(-1)
```

be its Boolean symmetry in `A_B`. Variables which occur in one context
commute. Let `E` be the set of unordered co-context pairs and define

```text
W_B=<s_x (x in X) | s_x^2=1,
                       [s_x,s_y]=1 ({x,y} in E)>.       (LCPB1)
```

For each `{x,y} in E`, form one LCS row with a fresh variable
`a_(x,y)`:

```text
x+y+a_(x,y)=0.                                         (LCPB2)
```

The solution-group relations for this row say that `x,y,a_(x,y)`
commute and

```text
x y a_(x,y)=1.                                         (LCPB3)
```

Thus `a_(x,y)=xy`. Eliminating the ancilla from (LCPB3) leaves exactly
`[x,y]=1`; conversely commuting involutions extend uniquely by
`a_(x,y)=xy`. Performing these Tietze eliminations for every pair gives

```text
Gamma_(L_B)=W_B times <J | J^2=1>,                     (LCPB4)
```

with `J` central. No equation uses `J), since every row has parity
zero.

Put `p_-=(1-J)/2). From (LCPB4),

```text
C*(Gamma_(L_B))=C*(W_B) tensor_max C*(C_2),
p_-C*(Gamma_(L_B))p_-=C*(W_B) tensor C p_-,
```

so the LCS solution algebra, whose unit is `p_-`, is canonically
isomorphic to `C*(W_B)).

The tuple `(s_x)_(x in X)` in `A_B` satisfies (LCPB1). The universal
property therefore gives a unital star-homomorphism

```text
q:C*(W_B)->A_B.                                        (LCPB5)
```

It is surjective because the Boolean symmetries generate the BCS algebra.
Under the LCS presentation, the ancilla in (LCPB2) maps to `s_xs_y).
The additional kernel of (LCPB5) is precisely generated by the BCS
forbidden joint spectral projections, together with any redundancies among
them. This proves (LBQ1) as an actual unital map of solution algebras, not
only a correspondence of representations.

Let `tau` be any trace on `A_B` and set `tau_L=tau after q`. This is
a trace and carries the specified BCS moments exactly. Its trace-null ideal
is

```text
N_(tau_L)=q^(-1)(N_tau).                                (LCPB6)
```

Indeed both sides are the zero set of
`tau(q(a)*q(a))`. Since `q` is surjective, the first isomorphism
theorem gives

```text
C*(W_B)/N_(tau_L) isomorphic to A_B/N_tau.              (LCPB7)
```

The isomorphism preserves the traces, and taking weak closures proves that
the two trace-GNS von Neumann algebras are isomorphic. Hence `tau_L` is
non-CE exactly when `tau` is non-CE.

On the other hand, assign

```text
J=-1,       s_x=1 for every x,       a_(x,y)=1.         (LCPB8)
```

Every row (LCPB2) is satisfied, so (LCPB8) is a one-dimensional unital
representation of the negative central-sign LCS algebra. Thus this LCS is
`R^U)-satisfiable regardless of the pulled-back trace.

Finally, `W_B` is a finitely generated right-angled Coxeter group. Its
standard integral linear representation and Malcev's theorem make it
residually finite; equivalently one may use residual finiteness of graph
products of finite groups. The direct product with `C_2` in (LCPB4) is
also residually finite. Therefore the solution group is sofic and
hyperlinear.

This exact example separates the two arrow directions. The quotient
`A_(L_B)->A_B` pulls a pathological trace backward but does not turn an
arbitrary `R^U)-model of `A_(L_B)` into one of `A_B`. The terminal
criterion needs a unital map `A_B->A_L`, so that every target
`R^U)-model restricts to the source, together with a trace extending in
that same direction.
