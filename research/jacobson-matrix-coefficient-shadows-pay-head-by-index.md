**ESTABLISHED** (route `jacobson-matrix-coefficient-shadows-pay-head-by-index-proof`).

**Setting.** `J = F_2<S,T | TS = 1>`, `Q = 1 - ST`, `n >= 3`, `G = EL_n(J)` generated by the root elements
`x_ij(1), x_ij(S), x_ij(T)` (`i != j`). The head is

```text
z = [x_12(T), x_23(S)] . [x_12(S), x_23(T)] = x_13(TS + ST) = x_13(1 + ST) = x_13(Q).
```

The TS relator is `r = [x_12(T), x_23(S)] . x_13(1)^(-1)`, which is trivial in `G` because `TS = 1`.

**Matrix-coefficient shadow.** Take a field `F` of characteristic `2`, an integer `N`, and **arbitrary**
matrices `A, B in M_N(F)`. Define the generator assignment

```text
rho(x_ij(1)) = X_ij(1),   rho(x_ij(S)) = X_ij(A),   rho(x_ij(T)) = X_ij(B)   in GL_(nN)(F) = GL_n(M_N(F)),
```

where `X_ij(c) = 1 + E_ij (x) c`. No multiplicativity of `S |-> A`, `T |-> B` is assumed. Let `sigma` be any
unitary representation of `GL_(nN)(F)` on a finite-dimensional space, with normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
`||.||_2`, or any permutation action with normalized Hamming distance. Extend `sigma rho` to words.

**Theorem (MCS).** For every such `(F, N, A, B, sigma)`,

```text
||sigma rho(z) - 1||_2  <=  2 ||sigma rho(r) - 1||_2.                          (MCS)
```

Consequently, along any sequence of such models whose defect on the one relator `r` tends to `0`, the head
tends to `1`. In particular, no sofic or hyperlinear approximation of `EL_n(J)` of matrix-coefficient-shadow
form keeps the head, for any field of characteristic two and any post-composition.

**Invariant and death step.**
- *Invariant:* the finite-dimensional Fredholm index. For square `A, B`, `rk(1 - AB) = rk(1 - BA)`.
- *Death step:* `z` maps exactly to `X_13(e_1) X_13(e_2)`, with `e_1 = 1 - BA` and `e_2 = 1 - AB`.
  Here `X_13(e_1)` is the image of `r`. Equal rank makes `X_13(e_2)` conjugate to `X_13(e_1)` in `GL_(nN)(F)`,
  so the second factor costs exactly what the relator costs.
- In `J` itself the index of `S` is `-1`: `TS = 1` and `rk(1 - ST) = 1`. This is precisely what no finite
  square matrix pair can reproduce.

**Scope (what survives).** A head-seeing approximation must stop sending the root generators to elementary
matrices over one common coefficient matrix per letter. Concretely, at least one of the following must fail:
- the images of `x_12(S)` and `x_23(S)`, and of `x_12(T)` and `x_23(T)`, are elementary with shared coefficients;
- `x_13(1)` goes to `X_13(1)`.

Periodic shifts, one-sided sections, compressions, and any other coefficient choice all die here. The class
already contains the truncated shifts of `toeplitz-folner-compressions-cannot-retain-jacobson-head`, where
`e_1 = P_(N-1)` and `e_2 = P_0` are the two endpoint projections.

**Where the idea stops.** The bound uses only the Steinberg commutator identity inside `GL_n(M_N(F))`. So it
says nothing about models of `EL_n(J)` into `U(d)` or `Sym(d)` that are not of elementary shape on the roots,
for example the permutation models of the amenable subgroups `<GL_fin, u>`. In those models no coefficient
pair `(A, B)` exists and the index is undefined. Turning a general `eps`-homomorphism into a coefficient
shadow is a stability statement for Steinberg relations, and that stability is exactly what the hole lacks.
