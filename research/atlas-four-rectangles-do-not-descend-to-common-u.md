Write the packet incidence graph as

```text
G=K_(2,2) disjointUnion K_(2,4).                       (FRD1)
```

It has `12` edges, `10` vertices and `2` components, so its cycle rank is

```text
12-10+2=4.                                             (FRD2)
```

Choose the first column in each component as a tree column.  If the edge
messages are `M_(i,j)`, a free cycle basis is

```text
H_j=M_(1,j)M_(0,j)^*M_(0,0)M_(1,0)^*,                 (FRD3)
```

with one `j=1` in `K_(2,2)` and `j=1,2,3` in `K_(2,4)`.
Because `G` is simple and bipartite, it has no reduced closed walk of length
two or three.  Thus `(FRD3)` are the lowest-degree vertex-gauge holonomy
words.  Every reduced closed edge word is generated by these four fundamental
cycles (separately in the two components), and its spectrum or cyclic traces
are invariant under the ordinary vertex gauges.

These invariants do **not** descend to the exact common-frame quotient.  In
the twelve-double-coset system, each edge has witnesses

```text
L_e=X_e R Y_e=U,                                      (FRD4)
```

and an independent right-regular gauge

```text
(X_e,Y_e)->(X_eW_e,W_e^(-1)Y_e).                      (FRD5)
```

The canonical product edge assignment is `M_e=L_e`.  By `(FRD4)` every edge
then has the same label `U`, and all four words `(FRD3)` equal `I` exactly.
This remains true in matrices, under arbitrary amplification, and in the
known positive-energy finite-tracial quotient solution.

On the other hand, choosing one factor such as `X_e` or `Y_e` as the edge
message makes `(FRD3)` depend on the independent gauges `(FRD5)`.  A gauge on
one non-tree edge occurs only once in its fundamental rectangle, so the
rectangle word changes while every product `L_e`, the common `U`, and
`q_19243(U)` remain fixed.  Already the scalar choice
`W_e=exp(it)I` rotates that rectangle word by `exp(plusOrMinus it)`, so its
spectrum and trace moments are not functions on the quotient parameter space.

The remaining lowest-degree **local** invariants are double-coset invariants
of one edge.  But exact packet membership puts

```text
U in U(rho(C_3)') R U(rho(C_2)'),                     (FRD6)
```

so every such principal-angle or singular-value invariant is exactly its
fixed reference value at `R`.  It carries no collision-dependent mismatch.

Consequently collision 19243 cannot force a nontrivial invariant merely by
acting on the four graph cycles: with the only canonical edge lift all cycles
are flat, while with factor-level lifts their spectra are gauge artifacts.
Any surviving obstruction must be a mixed common-`U` quantity coupling at
least two different packet subgroups directly--for example the established
projection-Gram coordinates--and must be invariant under all twelve gauges
`(FRD5)`.  It cannot be a closed alternating product of independently chosen
edge witnesses.
