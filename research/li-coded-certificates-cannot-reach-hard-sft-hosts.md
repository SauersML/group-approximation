## Setting

- `Λ`, `X ⊆ A^Λ`, `T = G_V × (Λ ⋉ X)` and the finitely generated subgroup `Q ≤ A(T)` are as in
  `alternating-v-full-group-wp-computes-sft-language`: `Q` is generated by the translations `φ_s` and
  the site-conditioned `A_5`-moves `ρ_{U_a}`.
- `𝒦_fs` is the class of effective Li-coded groupoids with `N^k` degrees and finite-state unit data,
  from `finite-state-li-coded-full-groups-have-exponential-word-problem`.

## Statement

1. **Complexity transfer.** If some finitely generated `Γ ⊇ Q` embeds in `F(𝒢)` for some
   `𝒢 ∈ 𝒦_fs`, then the pattern language of `X` (patterns on `m` sites given by words of length
   `≤ r`, size `n = m + r`) is decidable in time `C · 2^{C n^8}`.
2. **The bridge fails for hard SFTs.** For every recursive `R` there is a nonempty minimal free
   `Z^2`-SFT `X` (from `minimal-free-z2-sfts-have-arbitrarily-hard-row-languages`, applied to
   `R(n) = 2^{n^9}`) such that no finitely generated group containing `Q_X` embeds in the full group of
   any `𝒢 ∈ 𝒦_fs`. In particular `A(T)` itself is not such a subgroup. So "`X` rigid ⇒ `T`, or a host
   containing `Q`, is finitely coded with finite-state data" is false for these `X`, whatever
   quantum rigidity they have. The obstruction is complexity, not rigidity.
3. **Inputs.** If a finitely generated `Λ` embeds in `F(𝒢)` for some `𝒢 ∈ 𝒦_fs`, then `Λ` has
   word problem in time `C · 2^{C l^2}`. So for a Boone–Higman input `G` whose word problem is not in
   `F(2^{l^2})`, no finitely presented `Λ ⊇ G` carrying any SFT gives a host through a finite-state
   Li coding.
4. **What a Li-type bridge must use instead.** Any Li-coded certificate for a hard SFT host needs
   non-finite-state unit data. In an effective coding those units act faithfully on locally finite
   rooted trees of cylinders and are residually finite
   (`finitely-coded-groupoid-unit-groups-are-residually-finite`), and Li's theorem also
   wants them of type `F_2`. So the bridge for hard SFTs is exactly as hard as producing finitely
   presented residually finite groups with a faithful, non-finite-state self-similar action on a
   finite-object `N^k`-graded category and arbitrarily hard word problem. That is the multi-object
   form of `fp-self-similar-groups-with-arbitrarily-hard-word-problem`.

## Proof

1. Let `Γ ⊇ Q` embed in `F(𝒢)`, `𝒢 ∈ 𝒦_fs`. By item 1 of
   `finite-state-li-coded-full-groups-have-exponential-word-problem`, the word problem of `Γ`
   (finitely generated) is in `F(R)` with `R(l) = 2^{l^2}`. By clause 3 of
   `alternating-v-full-group-wp-computes-sft-language`, the pattern language of `X` is in `F(R_4)`,
   `R_4(n) = R(n^4) = 2^{n^8}`.
2. Take `X` with `L_row(X) ∉ F(2^{n^9})`. The row language is a special case of the pattern language
   with `m = n`, `r ≤ n`, so it would be in `F(2^{(2n)^8}) ⊆ F(2^{n^9})` by item 1. Contradiction.
3. A subgroup of a group with word problem in `F(R)` has word problem in `F(R)` for its own
   generators, up to constants.
4. Items 1–3 exclude finite-state data. With non-finite-state data, the unit groups of an effective
   coding are residually finite by the cited node, and the host's word problem is at least that of
   `Q`, which computes `L(X)`. ∎

## Where rigidity does enter (remark, not claimed)

For hierarchical SFTs (substitutive or self-simulating tilings), the natural Li category is the
*inflation category*: objects are tile types, degree-1 morphisms are "type `v` sits at position `p`
of the supertile of type `w`", and units are supertile symmetries. There:
- (UFP*) is exactly recognizability, i.e. the unique composition property;
- `(t<d)` is primitivity after a power;
- (F) holds by right cancellation.

So Li's theorem applies to the **inflation groupoid**. That groupoid contains the translations of
`X` only away from the *fault set*, the points whose origin and translate never share a finite-level
supertile. Every aperiodic hierarchical tiling has faults. So the inflation category does not code
`Λ ⋉ X`; a coding of `T` must add fault moves. Items 1–3 show that for hard `X` this cannot be done
with finite-state data, and quantum rigidity (a statement about the faults) cannot change that. For
easy `X`, e.g. Labbé's shift, whose language is elementary, items 1–3 say nothing: the Z²
calibration of G2/G3g stays open along this bridge.

## Lesson for general BH

The master route's hard hosts can never be certified finitely presented by a Li/Garside-type
theorem with finite-state data, because such certificates compute normal forms and so bound the
word problem. Rigidity is necessary for P2′ (`fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`),
but it cannot supply the certificate through a finite-state coding. P2′ for hard SFTs needs either
- non-finite-state residually finite units carrying the hardness (the self-similar root), or
- a finiteness theorem that produces no normal form: the "non-deterministic finite type ⇒ finite
  presentation" theorem (FTR, group side of `board/SYNTHESIS.md`).

The bridge G3g(j) is therefore not a corollary of Li. It is at best a reduction of the easy
(elementary-language) cases, and the hard cases must go through G3g(i) with a non-Garside argument.
