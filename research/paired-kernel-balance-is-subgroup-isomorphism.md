title: Balanced coordinate kernels mean the relation pairs are the graph of a subgroup isomorphism
---

Let `G_1,G_2` be groups, let `K<=G_1 x G_2`, and write

```text
H_i=p_i(K)<=G_i
```

for the coordinate images.  Then the following are equivalent:

1. `ker(p_1:K->H_1)=ker(p_2:K->H_2)`;
2. both coordinate projections are injective;
3. there is an isomorphism `phi:H_1->H_2` such that

   ```text
   K={(h,phi(h)):h in H_1}.                             (PKB1)
   ```

If `K` is generated by pairs `(a_s,b_s)`, the isomorphism necessarily sends
`a_s` to `b_s`.  Thus kernel balance is exactly **path independence of a
finite-state generator transducer**: every word relation among the `a_s` must
be the corresponding word relation among the `b_s`, and conversely.

For finite groups this gives a bounded exact decision procedure.  Traverse the
Cayley graph of `H_1=<a_s>` while propagating the corresponding `b`-value.  A
state reached with two different propagated values certifies a word in
`ker p_1` but not `ker p_2`.  If propagation is consistent, it defines the
surjective homomorphism `H_1->H_2`; checking injectivity (or symmetrically
propagating from `H_2`) decides balance after visiting at most `|H_1|+|H_2|`
states.

This is the compiler form of the paired-relation no-go: exact regular-trace
two-chart equations can only encode an honest subgroup isomorphism.  They
cannot encode a one-sided finite-index/compression phenomenon.
