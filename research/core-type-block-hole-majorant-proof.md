## Scalar commutant

If `beta` is irreducible, Schur's lemma gives `D_beta=C`. If
`L_epsilon` contains a nonzero `V`, then

```text
V*V=a1,       a=||V||_(2,d)^2>0.                      (CTP1)
```

Thus `a^(-1/2)V` is an isometry still in `L_epsilon`, and its one-element
frame covers the unit. Hence `c_(epsilon,beta)=1`. If the band is zero,
the only Gram carrier is zero and `c_(epsilon,beta)=0`. This proves
(CTB3), and the almost-full coverage estimate with `delta=0` gives (CTB4).

If `Delta>=eta` and `epsilon<=eta^2/4`, (CTB4) rules out a nonzero band.
Since `Q_epsilon` is the closed spectral projection for
`[1-epsilon,1]`, its vanishing gives
`lambda_max(T_X)<1-epsilon`. This proves (CTB5).

## Multiplicity-free effects

Suppose every `m_alpha=1`. Then `D_beta` is the algebra generated by its
minimal central projections `e_alpha`, and every positive dual variable is

```text
Z=sum_alpha z_alpha e_alpha,       z_alpha>=0.         (CTP2)
```

Its normalized trace is `sum_alpha w_alpha z_alpha`. On
`L_epsilon`,

```text
Q_epsilon R_Z Q_epsilon
 =sum_alpha z_alpha A_alpha.                           (CTP3)
```

The central projections sum to one, so the compressed effects are positive
and sum to the identity of `L_epsilon`. Substitution of (CTP2)--(CTP3)
into the exact hole dual (ACN5) proves (CTB7). Compression by
`Q_epsilon` need not preserve products, so the effects need not commute
even though the original `e_alpha` do. If they do commute, simultaneous
diagonalization turns the operator inequality into finitely many scalar
linear inequalities, proving the linear-program assertion.

## Full right-module invariance

Now allow arbitrary multiplicities and assume `L_epsilon D_beta` is
contained in `L_epsilon`. Since `D_beta` is star closed, the band is
reducing for every right multiplier and `Q_epsilon` commutes with
`R_(e_alpha)`. The central compressions are therefore orthogonal
projections onto `L_epsilon e_alpha`.

Let

```text
s=sum_(alpha:L_epsilon e_alpha !=0) e_alpha.           (CTP4)
```

Every carrier `sum_j V_j*V_j` formed from the band is supported under
`s`, so its trace is at most `tr_d(s)`. Conversely, a nonzero right
`M_(m_alpha)` submodule contains a copy of the standard row module.
Choosing one unit vector in its left multiplicity space and all
`m_alpha` coordinate rows gives finitely many `V_j` with

```text
sum_j V_j*V_j=e_alpha.                                 (CTP5)
```

Do this independently in every active block. Their union covers `s`,
so the optimal trace equals

```text
tr_d(s)=sum_(alpha active) d_alpha m_alpha/d.          (CTP6)
```

This proves (CTB8)--(CTB9).

## BS packet specialization

In a multiplicity-one fourth-power packet, the distinct `S` eigenvalues
force every commutant operator to be diagonal across the orbit, while
cyclic transitivity of `R` forces all diagonal entries equal. Hence the
packet `(O,v)` is irreducible. Its `S` spectrum determines `O`, and
`R^|O|=v` determines the return phase, so two packets are equivalent
exactly when both labels agree. Schur decomposition therefore gives
(CTB10)--(CTB11), with `m_(O,v)` equal to the number of repeated copies.

Finally, for any finite group `B_N`, the left regular representation has

```text
lambda_(B_N)=direct_sum_(alpha in Irr(B_N))
               pi_alpha tensor 1_(d_alpha).            (CTP7)
```

Thus its multiplicities are `m_alpha=d_alpha`; substituting in (CTB2)
gives the central block weights `d_alpha^2/|B_N|`, proving (CTB12).
These are exactly the Plancherel weights. Pointwise convergence of a
moving character to the regular character controls aggregate fixed-word
moments, not the individual moving multiplicities `m_(O,v)`. Therefore it
does not justify replacing the actual coverage problem by the irreducible
case.
