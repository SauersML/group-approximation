**ESTABLISHED** (elementary; lane-proved, not independently reviewed; no novelty
claimed for the analytic-continuation argument itself).

## Setting

Let `X` be a Hausdorff space and `L <= Homeo(X)` a group with the **identity
principle**: an element of `L` that is the identity on a nonempty open subset of
`X` is trivial. Examples, all used below:
- `PGL_n(R)` on `RP^{n-1}`, `n >= 2` (a nonempty open set contains `n+1` points
  in general position, and an element fixing them is scalar);
- `PGL_n(Q_p)` on `P^{n-1}(Q_p)`, and the affine groups `K^n x| GL_n(K)` on `K^n`
  for `K = R, Q_p`;
- affine maps `x -> Ax + b` of the finite adeles `A_f^n` (or of `A_f^n ∪ {∞}`)
  with `A in GL_n(Q)`, `b in A_f^n`: an open set contains `x_0 + N Ẑ^n`, and
  `A = I` on `N Ẑ^n` forces `A = I`.

For `h in Homeo(X)` let `P(h)` be its **pieces**: the elements of `L` that agree
with `h` on some nonempty open set. Say `h` is **piecewise-`L` with pieces in
`Δ`** (`Δ <= L`) if the points near which `h` agrees with some element of `L`
form a dense set, and `P(h) ⊆ Δ`.

## Statement

Let `Δ <= L`, and let `B <= Homeo(X)` be a group of piecewise-`L`
homeomorphisms with pieces in `Δ`. Let `G <= Homeo(X)` be any group such that
every `g in G` agrees with some element of `B` near each point outside a closed
nowhere dense set `sing(g)`. Then:

1. every `g in G` is piecewise-`L` with pieces in `Δ`;
2. `G ∩ L ⊆ Δ`;
3. if `B` is generated by `b_1, ..., b_k` with each `P(b_i)` finite, one may take
   `Δ = <P(b_1), ..., P(b_k)>`, a finitely generated subgroup of `L`.

**Corollaries.** Let `B` be as in 3, with `L` one of the examples, and `n >= 2`.
- **(a) Finite germ extensions.** If `X` has no isolated points, every finite
  germ extension `G` of `B` in the sense of Belk--Hyde--Matucci
  (arXiv:2407.03149v1, Notation 2.2: every element has finitely many singular
  points, and outside them it agrees locally with elements of `B`) satisfies
  1--2, whatever its germs at the singular points, and whether or not `G` is
  finitely generated. The same holds for towers of finite germ extensions over
  `B`: the points where an element of the tower is not locally in `B` form a
  closed set, which is nowhere dense because off a finite set it is locally
  finite.
- **(b) No standard `GL_n(Q)`.** For `L = PGL_n(R)` (or `PGL_n(Q_p)`, or the
  affine examples), `Δ` is a finitely generated linear group, hence residually
  finite (Mal'cev). So `G ∩ L` has no nontrivial divisible subgroup. In
  particular `G` contains neither the standard unipotent copy of `(Q,+)` nor
  the standard copy of `PGL_n(Q)` (resp. `GL_n(Q)` acting linearly or
  affinely), for every such `G`.
- **(c) Rational data.** If the pieces of the `b_i` lie in `PGL_n(Q)`, then
  `G ∩ PGL_n(Q)` lies in the image of `GL_n(Z[1/m])` for one `m`, which misses
  `diag(p,1,...,1)` for every prime `p ∤ m`.
- **(d) Thurston's PIP question over `Q`.** The group of all homeomorphisms of
  `RP^{n-1}` that are piecewise `PGL_n(Q)` with finitely many pieces contains
  `PGL_n(Q)`, so by (c) it is not finitely generated. (Survey Remark 5.5 asks
  about integral PIP groups; nothing is claimed about them here.)

## Proof

*Products and inverses.* Let `b, c` be piecewise-`L` with pieces in `Δ`. The
sets `D(c)` and `c^{-1}(D(b))`, where `D(h)` is the open dense set of points
near which `h` agrees with an element of `L`, are open and dense, so their
intersection `W` is open and dense. Near `x in W`, `bc` agrees with `βγ`, where
`γ` is the piece of `c` near `x` and `β` the piece of `b` near `c(x)`. If
`ℓ in L` agrees with `bc` on a nonempty open `O`, choose `x in O ∩ W`; on a
smaller nonempty open set `ℓ = βγ` as maps, so `ℓ^{-1}βγ` is the identity there
and `ℓ = βγ in Δ` by the identity principle. Similarly `b^{-1}` agrees with
`β^{-1}` near `b(x)` for `x in D(b)`. So the piecewise-`L` homeomorphisms with
pieces in `Δ` form a group, which gives 3.

*Item 1.* Let `g in G`. Its complement `W_g = X \ sing(g)` is open and dense. For
`x in W_g` choose `b_x in B` and an open `U_x ∋ x` with `g = b_x` on `U_x`. The
open set `E = ⋃_x (U_x ∩ D(b_x))` is dense: a nonempty open `O` meets `W_g` at
some `x`, and `O ∩ U_x` is a nonempty open set, so it meets the dense set
`D(b_x)`. Near each point of `E`, `g` agrees with a piece of some `b_x`, which
lies in `Δ`. If `ℓ in L` agrees with `g` on a nonempty open `O`, pick
`y in O ∩ E`; near `y` both `ℓ` and some `δ in Δ` agree with `g`, so `ℓ = δ` by
the identity principle.

*Item 2.* If `g in G ∩ L`, then `g in P(g) ⊆ Δ`.

*Corollaries.* (a) A finite set in a Hausdorff space without isolated points is
closed and nowhere dense. (b) `PGL_n(R)` embeds in `GL_{n^2}(R)` (adjoint
action), so `Δ` is a finitely generated linear group, residually finite by
Mal'cev. A divisible group has no nontrivial finite quotient, so it has trivial
image in every finite quotient of `Δ`; hence no nontrivial divisible subgroup
lies in `Δ`. The standard unipotent `(Q,+)` is divisible. (c) Lift each
generator of `Δ` to `GL_n(Q)` and let `m` clear the denominators of the lifts
and of their inverses. If `diag(p,1,...,1) = λM` with `M in GL_n(Z[1/m])` and
`p ∤ m`, then the `p`-adic valuation `v = v_p(λ^{-1})` satisfies `v >= 0`
(entries of `M`) and `nv + 1 = v_p(det M) = 0`, which is impossible for `n >= 2`.
(d) is (c) applied to a finite generating set. ∎

## What this leaves open

- **Nonstandard copies.** The divisible part of `GL_n(Q)` must be carried by
  elements that are not globally in `L`. That is how `(Q,+)` sits in
  Belk--Hyde--Matucci's `T-bar <= A <= VA`: the element for `1/k` is a dyadic
  piecewise-linear lift of an order-`k` element of `T`, not the translation by
  `1/k`.
- **Bases with infinitely generated piece groups.** The claim needs a base `B`
  whose piece group is finitely generated. A standard copy of `PGL_n(Q)` forces
  pieces with infinitely many primes into the base itself, so the base must have
  elements with infinitely many pieces. With finitely many pieces outside every
  neighbourhood of a singular set, those pieces accumulate at singular points.
- **Relation to other lanes.** The special case `G = B` finitely generated and
  finitely piecewise (no singular points) is the lemma being landed by lane
  `gq-solenoid` as `piecewise-commutative-linear-hosts-miss-linear-gl-n-q`. This
  claim adds arbitrary groups over such a base, in particular every finite germ
  extension.
