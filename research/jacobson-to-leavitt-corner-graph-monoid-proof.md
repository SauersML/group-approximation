## 1. The Jacobson algebra is the algebraic Toeplitz graph

Let `E_T` have vertices `v,w`, a loop `e` at `v`, and one edge
`f:v -> w`, where `w` is a sink. In the Leavitt path algebra
`L_(F_2)(E_T)` put

```text
S=e+f,                    T=e^*+f^*.
```

The path and ghost-path relations give

```text
TS=e^*e+f^*f=v+w=1,
ST=ee^*+ff^*=v,
Q=1-ST=w.                                                (JLC5)
```

The cross terms vanish. Conversely, inside
`J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>` put

```text
v=ST,     w=Q,     e=S v,     f=S w,
e^*=v T,            f^*=w T.                             (JLC6)
```

The idempotents `v,w` are orthogonal and sum to one. Moreover

```text
e^*e=v,       f^*f=w,       e^*f=f^*e=0,
ee^*+ff^*=S(v+w)T=ST=v.
```

Thus `(JLC6)` is a Leavitt `E_T`-family. It is inverse to `(JLC5)`,
because `e+f=S` and `e^*+f^*=T`. Hence

```text
J isomorphic to L_(F_2)(E_T),              Q corresponds to w.   (JLC7)
```

This also identifies the general corner theorem concretely:
`QJQ` is the Leavitt path algebra of the one-vertex, no-edge graph.

## 2. The sink ideal is exactly the finite-dimensional coefficient radical

The elementary identities

```text
QS=0,                     TQ=0
```

show that

```text
E_(r,s)=S^r Q T^s,             r,s>=0,
E_(r,s)E_(k,l)=delta_(s,k) E_(r,l).                     (JLC8)
```

Consequently

```text
I=JQJ=span_(F_2){E_(r,s):r,s>=0}
```

is the finite-support matrix ideal. Sandwiching a reduced word by `Q`
kills it unless the word is the identity, so

```text
QJQ=F_2 Q.                                               (JLC9)
```

Killing `Q` makes `ST=1` as well as `TS=1`. Therefore

```text
J/I isomorphic to F_2[z,z^(-1)],
S |-> z,                   T |-> z^(-1).                 (JLC10)
```

Let `pi:J -> End_(F_2)(V)` be unital with `V` finite-dimensional.
The equation `pi(T)pi(S)=1` says that the square matrix `pi(S)` has a
left inverse, hence is invertible and `pi(T)=pi(S)^(-1)`. It follows
that `pi(Q)=0` and `pi(I)=0`.

Conversely, if `a notin I`, its Laurent symbol is a nonzero Laurent
polynomial `p(z)`. Choose `alpha` in a finite extension of `F_2` with
`alpha!=0` and `p(alpha)!=0`; this is possible because the algebraic
closure is infinite and `p` has only finitely many roots. Multiplication
by `alpha` on that finite extension gives a finite-dimensional
representation of `J/I` which does not kill `a`. Hence

```text
Rad_fd^alg(J)=I.                                         (JLC11)
```

The graph monoid says the same thing. Its only relation is

```text
[v]=[v]+[w].
```

A finite-dimensional module supplies a dimension function to `N`, so
`d_v=d_v+d_w` and therefore `d_w=0`. Nonzero dimension can remain on
`v` and factors through the Laurent cycle quotient.

There is an important relative-versus-intrinsic distinction. The abstract
corner `QJQ=F_2Q` has a one-dimensional module. That module does not
extend to a finite-dimensional unital `J`-module, because `Q` is not
full and every ambient module kills it. Thus “the head corner lies in
the finite-dimensional radical” refers to its embedding in `J`, not to
the abstract algebra `QJQ`. A corner theorem alone cannot erase the
corner.

## 3. The second branch makes the same idempotent full

Let

```text
L=L_(F_2)(1,2)
 =F_2<s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1 |
       t_i s_j=delta_(ij), s_0t_0+s_1t_1=1>.
```

The subalgebra generated by `S=s_0,T=t_0` is the Jacobson algebra above,
and its defect is

```text
q=1-ST=s_1t_1.
```

The identity

```text
t_1 q s_1=t_1(s_1t_1)s_1=1                             (JLC12)
```

puts `1` in `LqL`, so `q` is full. In fact the corner is not merely
Morita equivalent to `L`. The maps

```text
Phi:L -> qLq,          Phi(a)=s_1 a t_1,
Psi:qLq -> L,          Psi(b)=t_1 b s_1                 (JLC13)
```

are inverse unital ring homomorphisms, where the unit of `qLq` is `q`.
Indeed `t_1s_1=1` proves multiplicativity and `q b q=b` proves
`Phi(Psi(b))=b`.

There is no nonzero finite-dimensional unital `L`-module. If `L` acts
on a finite-dimensional `V`, each `s_i` is injective with left inverse
`t_i`. The two images are disjoint because `t_i s_j=0` for `i!=j`,
and the completeness relation gives

```text
V=s_0V direct-sum s_1V.
```

If `d=dim(V)`, both summands have dimension `d`, so `d=2d` and
`d=0`. Equivalently, the graph is the two-loop rose and its graph
monoid has

```text
[u]=2[u],
```

whose only finite dimension function is zero. By `(JLC13)` the same
statement holds for the full corner `qLq`.

This proves every assertion in `(JLC2)` and the graph-monoid table.

## 4. Location of the qutrit mixed packet

In the half-line matrix units `(JLC8)`,

```text
Q=E_(0,0),                  QT=E_(0,1),                 (JLC14)
```

so the coefficients of `H,C_1,W` in `(JLC3)` all lie in `I`. The raw
letters `T` and `S` do not lie in `I`; their Laurent symbols are
`z^(-1)` and `z`. This exactly separates the head-polar envelope from
the two raw actors which created it.

For completeness, let `G=EL_28(J)` and let `N` be the kernel of its
Laurent symbol map. The established Jacobson calculation identifies
`N` with the simple finitary group on
`N times {1,...,28}` and proves that every finite quotient of `G` kills
`N`. If an exact unitary representation `rho:G -> U(d)` retained one
of `H,C_1,W`, then `rho(N)` would be nontrivial. The finitely generated
linear group `rho(G)` is residually finite, so a finite quotient of
`rho(G)` would separate a nonidentity element of `rho(N)`. Composing
would give a finite quotient of `G` nontrivial on `N`, a contradiction.
Thus every exact finite-dimensional unitary representation sends

```text
H, C_1, W |-> 1,              E=(1-H)/2 |-> 0.          (JLC15)
```

This is the group-level exact collapse used by the qutrit packet.

Adjoining the composite coefficient `Q=s_1t_1` has not exposed the
second branch: `Q` was already present in `J`. Only adjoining the
individual letters `s_1,t_1` supplies `(JLC12)--(JLC13)` and changes
the normal coefficient ideal generated by `Q` from `I` to all of `L`.

## 5. What the corner theorems do and do not supply

Abrams--Nam's theorem that unital corners of finite-graph Leavitt path
algebras are again Leavitt path algebras, the corresponding Steinberg
reduction-corner results, and the finite-dimensional classification of
Koc--Ozaydin are consistent with the calculation above. The
Ara--Moreno--Pardo identification of the projective-module monoid with
the graph monoid gives the two displayed dimension equations. None of
these general results is load-bearing here: `(JLC5)--(JLC13)` prove the
needed special cases directly.

They also do not solve the microstate problem. Equation `(JLC13)` is a
rectangular Morita equivalence at the coefficient level. An arbitrary
normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt approximate representation of the abstract
elementary group does not automatically decode `s_1,t_1` as coefficient
operators on a common matrix reservoir. Fullness of `q` therefore
does not imply that a qutrit polar source is transported without loss
into `Ee_2`.

The first viable continuation must use a bounded group word containing
both literal complementary-branch letters, authenticate the two inverse
rows on the selected sign-polar source, and close their ranges inside one
qutrit target. Without that group-word authentication, the graph-monoid
transition is an exact finite-dimensional obstruction but not a
nonhyperlinearity certificate.
