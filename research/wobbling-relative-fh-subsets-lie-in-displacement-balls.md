## Setting

- `W(Z)` is the group of bijections `g` of `Z` with finite displacement
  `disp(g) = sup_z |gz − z|`.
- `Γ` is a group generated by a finite symmetric set `Q`, with a homomorphism `ρ: Γ → W(Z)`.
  Faithfulness is not needed. Put `r = max_{q ∈ Q} disp(ρ(q))`.
- A subset `Y ⊆ Γ` has **relative FH** if every cnd function on `Γ` is bounded on `Y`. For a
  subgroup `H`, this is the same as relative (T) of `(Γ, H)`.
- `X = {0,1}^Z`, and `[[X]]` is its topological full group, with `f(x) = σ^{k_f(x)} x`.
  `B_K = {f ∈ [[X]] : ‖k_f‖_∞ ≤ K}`.

## Theorem W

Let `Y ⊆ Γ` have relative FH. Then there is `n`, depending only on `(Γ, Q, Y)` and `r`, such that
`disp(ρ(y)) < 4n` for every `y ∈ Y`. Explicitly, `n` is any integer `> max(2Cr², 2r)`, where `C`
is the constant of Lemma Q (`sup_Y ψ ≤ C·max_Q ψ` for all cnd `ψ`). The constant `C` does not
depend on `ρ`.

## Corollaries

1. **Subgroups of `W(Z)`.** If `(Γ, H)` has relative (T), then every `ρ(H)`-orbit lies in an interval
   of length `< 8n`. So `ρ(H)` is locally finite of bounded exponent: its f.g. subgroups embed in
   finite products of groups of order at most `(8n)!`.
2. **The full shift.** Let `Γ ≤ [[X]]` be f.g., and let `Y ⊆ Γ` have relative FH. Then
   `Y ⊆ B_K` with `K = 4n − 1`. The same `n` works on every aperiodic `σ`-orbit, because `C` does not
   depend on `ρ`. Aperiodic points are dense, and `k_y` is continuous.
3. **Relative-(T) subgroups of `[[X]]`.** If `H ≤ Γ ≤ [[X]]` and `(Γ, H)` has relative (T), then
   `H ⊆ B_K`. Hence `H` is locally finite, and its exponent divides `(2K+1)!`.

## Class-kill

- **Killed class.** Refutations of `full-shift-topological-full-group-is-a-t-menable` by a
  relative-(T) pair `(Γ, H)`, or a relative-FH subset `Y`, whose witness is not contained in one
  displacement ball `B_K`. That covers:
  - every `Y` containing elements of unbounded `‖k‖_∞`;
  - every `H` that contains an element of infinite order, or has unbounded exponent;
  - every non-normal witness of this kind, so normality is no longer needed. This closes the
    survivor "non-normal relative-(T) subsets" recorded by w17 in the target's Attempts, for
    subsets with relative FH.
- **Invariant.** Recurrence of `Z`: a point has zero capacity. This is the cut-space mechanism
  behind extensive amenability. Cuts can be smeared over a window of width `n` at Dirichlet cost
  `~ 1/n`.
- **Death step.** Take the scale-`n` smeared cut measure `μ_{m,n}` on the countable space of cuts
  `C = {A ⊆ Z : A Δ N finite}`. Each generator moves `√μ` by at most `4r²/n`. An element that
  carries a point across the cut `m` from distance `≥ 2n` on one side to distance `≥ 2n` on the
  other makes `μ` and its image mutually singular, so it moves `√μ` by exactly `√2`.
  Lemma Q turns this into the uniform bound.

## What survives

- A relative-(T) witness that sits inside **one** ball `B_K`, of bounded exponent. The open char-p
  candidate `EL_2(F_p[t,t^{-1}]) ⋉ F_p[t,t^{-1}]²` of
  `full-shift-full-group-has-infinite-normal-relative-t-subgroup` has exponent `p`, so it is not
  excluded. But any realization must put its entire base in a single `B_K`. Then every base orbit
  on each `σ`-orbit has size `≤ 2K+1`.
- A remaining test, which is not a full criterion: `Γ`-almost-invariant vectors in the monomial
  block representation `⊕_B ℓ²_0(B)` over the `H`-orbit blocks `B`. Cutting blocks costs
  `Γ`-boundary, so this test is unresolved.
- **Not asserted.** "Haagerup ⟺ no infinite relative-FH subset" is not known. So nothing here
  proves `[[X]]` Haagerup. Theorem W only says where a relative-(T) refutation has to live.

## Relation to the w19/w20 lanes (text only)

- w19 obtained local finiteness of non-normal relative-(T) subgroups through Cornulier's
  wobbling results. Theorem W is a stronger, self-contained form, with a uniform ball and bounded
  exponent.
- w20 showed that cnd functions on `W(Z)` are bounded on displacement balls. Theorem W is the
  complementary direction, inside any f.g. `Γ` acting on `Z`: relative-FH subsets are confined to
  a displacement ball. Neither direction decides the Haagerup property.
