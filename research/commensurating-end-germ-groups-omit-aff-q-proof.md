This applies Corollary (ii) of `aff-q-forces-distortion-in-abelian-kernels`, with
the germ of `z` as the commensurated element.

**Cited inputs.**
- **(C1) Burillo--Felipe**, arXiv:2605.09763v1 (10 May 2026), Theorem 1: "Let
  f∈V𝒜 have infinite order. Then ⟨f⟩ is undistorted in V𝒜." Corollary 5:
  "V𝒜 does not contain any group with cyclic subgroups that are distorted." Their
  `V𝒜` is the group of Belk--Hyde--Matucci (their reference [2]).
- **(C2) T-bar <= VA**, survey arXiv:2306.16356v3 §4.3: "The group $\overline{T}$
  is contained in a larger finitely presented group $\mathcal{A}$ introduced by
  Brin, ... consider the group $V\!\A$ of homeomorphisms of the Cantor set
  generated by $\mathcal{A}$ and Thompson's group~$V$." `T-bar` is the group of
  dyadic PL homeomorphisms of `R` commuting with `z` (Belk--Hyde--Matucci,
  arXiv:2005.02036v4, as recorded in `aff-q-does-not-embed-in-aut-thompson-f`).

**Step 0 (no distortion in T-bar).** Let `H <= T-bar` be finitely generated and
`c in H` of infinite order. By (C2), `H <= VA`. Then
`l_H(c^N) >= l_VA(c^N)/C`, where `C` bounds the VA-lengths of the generators of `H`.
By (C1) the right side is at least linear in `N`. So `<c>` is undistorted in `H`.

**Step 1 (T-bar_M is isomorphic to T-bar).** For `M >= 1`, let `T-bar_M` be the group
of dyadic PL homeomorphisms of `R` commuting with `z^M`.
- Let `psi: [0, M] -> [0, 1]` map `[j, j+1]` affinely onto
  `[1 - 2^-j, 1 - 2^-(j+1)]` for `0 <= j <= M-2`, and `[M-1, M]` onto
  `[1 - 2^-(M-1), 1]`. Its slopes are powers of 2 and its breakpoints are dyadic.
- Let `Psi(x + kM) = psi(x) + k`. This is an increasing dyadic PL homeomorphism of
  `R` with `Psi z^M = z Psi`.
- Conjugation by `Psi` preserves the form `x -> 2^n x + d`, dyadic breakpoints
  and local finiteness. So `h -> Psi h Psi^-1` is an isomorphism `T-bar_M -> T-bar`.

With Step 0, no finitely generated subgroup of any `T-bar_M` has a distorted
infinite cyclic subgroup.

**Step 2 (periodic extension).** Let a germ `h in Comm_inf` commute with `z^m`
near `+inf`, say `h(x + m) = h(x) + m` for `x >= c`.
- **Definition.** Put `h~(x) = h(x + km) - km` for any `k` with `x + km >= c`.
  This does not depend on `k`.
- **It lands in T-bar_m.** `h~` is an increasing dyadic PL homeomorphism of `R`
  commuting with `z^m`.
- **Injective homomorphism.** `h -> h~` is a homomorphism on germs commuting with
  `z^m`, and it is injective, because the germ determines `h~`.

**Step 3 (the contradiction).** Let `Gamma <= Comm_inf` be finitely generated and
contain a copy of `Aff_K(Q)`. Adjoining the germ of `z` keeps `Gamma` finitely
generated.
- **Commensuration.** Each element commensurates `<z>` by definition.
- **The kernel is covered by T-bar_M's.** A finitely generated subgroup of
  `U_m C_Gamma(z^m)` has finitely many generators centralizing
  `z^(m_1), ..., z^(m_r)`. It therefore centralizes `z^M` with
  `M = m_1 ... m_r`, and embeds in `T-bar_M` by Step 2. By Step 1 it has no
  distorted infinite cyclic subgroup.
- **Conclusion.** Corollary (ii) of the lemma says `Aff_K(Q)` does not embed in
  `Gamma`, which contradicts the assumption.

**Whole-line version.** The same steps, without germs, apply to the group of
dyadic PL homeomorphisms `h` of `R` with `h(x + a) = h(x) + b`: the centralizer of
`z^m` there is `T-bar_m` itself.

**Normal subgroups of Aff(Q)** (used in the consequence). Let `N` be a nontrivial
normal subgroup of `Aff(Q)`.
- **If N meets Q trivially.** Then `[N, Q] <= N ∩ Q = 1`. The centralizer of `Q`
  in `Aff(Q)` is `Q`, so `N <= Q`, and `N = 1`, a contradiction.
- **Otherwise.** `N ∩ Q` is a nonzero subgroup of `Q` stable under multiplication
  by `Q^x`, so it is `Q`.
