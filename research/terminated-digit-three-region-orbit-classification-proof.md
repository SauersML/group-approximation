Put m=h-1. For a boundary set U, define its label to be EMPTY
if U is empty, and otherwise b(U) in Z/mZ. There are m+1=h
possible labels, including a separate label for a nonempty set
of residue zero.

Every translate S=g(F) is a boundary set of residue r modulo m.
Conversely the global prefix-transport theorem takes F onto every
nonempty boundary set with that residue, identifying the orbit.
Its intersection with F is clopen in F: a tile meets F only
when its prefix has no terminator, in which case it is a digit
cylinder of F. Attach to S the three labels of

    A=F intersect S,       B=F minus S,       C=S minus F.

## Equal triples give the same K orbit

Suppose S_1,S_2 have equal triples. There is a prefix element
of V_(h,r) on F sending A_1 onto A_2: refine the finite digit
partitions of the nonempty A regions to equal leaf counts using
their matching residues modulo m, and do the same independently
for their complements B. If a region is empty, omit it. Matching
the resulting leaves gives the required boundary prefix map.

The restriction theorem lifts this map to u in K. Then u(A_1)=A_2
and u(C_1) is a boundary set off F with the same label as C_2.
If both are empty, no further change is needed. Otherwise the
supported transport theorem gives v, supported in a clopen set
off F, taking u(C_1) onto C_2. In particular v belongs to K and
fixes A_2. Thus v*u(S_1)=S_2.

The labels are also invariant under K, because its elements
preserve F setwise and preserve boundary residues. They therefore
classify K orbits among the translates of F. There are at most
h^3 such orbits, hence at most h^3 K double cosets in H. This
bound does not assert that every possible triple is realized.

## Faithfulness of the action on fiber images

Let Omega_digit be the set of paths with only finitely many #
edges. It is dense in C_X. Given x in Omega_digit and a clopen
neighborhood U of x, choose a boundary tile alpha*T contained
in U and containing x. By subdividing it sufficiently far,
choose r disjoint subtiles including the one containing x.
Their union R lies in U, contains x, and has residue r modulo m.

The global transport theorem gives g in H with g(F)=R. If k is
in the kernel of the H action on the orbit of F, it preserves
every such R setwise. Hence k(x) belongs to every clopen
neighborhood U of x, forcing k(x)=x. Density of Omega_digit
and continuity give k=1. Thus core_H(K)=1.

The host H is finitely presented by its nucleus theorem, and K
is finitely generated by its restriction-and-kernel theorem.
Together with the finite pair-orbit count and faithfulness,
these are exactly the four defining conditions of type (A).

## The enlarged stabilizer retains the original subaction

For a lifted shell group, the equivariant collapse pi sends F
to p and gives e(F)=pi^-1(e(p)). Thus e preserves F exactly
when e(p)=p, proving K intersect E_nu=S. The E_nu orbit is
therefore identified with E_nu/S as asserted.

If S had finite index d in K, every K double coset in H would
split into at most d^2 S double cosets, by writing its left K
factor in S cosets and its right K factor in cosets of S.
Finite bi-index of K would then give finite bi-index of S,
contradicting the earlier fiber-count theorem. Thus [K:S] is
infinite. The old and new stabilizer conclusions are consistent.
