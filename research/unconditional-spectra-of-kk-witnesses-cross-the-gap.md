**ESTABLISHED** by `unconditional-spectra-cross-the-gap-proof`.

**Setting.**
- `Γ` is a torsion-free discrete subgroup of a reductive Lie group. Examples: every
  torsion-free finite-index subgroup of `SL_3(Z)`, such as `Γ(3)` or `E_3(3)`.
- `A(Γ)` is an unconditional completion of `C[Γ]` with a continuous injective unital
  `*`-homomorphism `i : A(Γ) → C*_r(Γ)` extending the identity on `C[Γ]`. Example: `ℓ¹(Γ)`.
- `τ` is the canonical trace, and `L_c = {z : Re z = c}`.

**Conclusions.**
1. **Integer traces.** Every idempotent `e ∈ M_n(A(Γ))` has `τ(i(e)) ∈ Z`.
2. **No idempotents.** `A(Γ)` has no idempotent other than `0` and `1`.
   - In particular `ℓ¹(Γ)` and `C[Γ]` have none.
   - Kaplansky's idempotent conjecture therefore holds for `Γ` over every field of
     characteristic zero.
3. **Gap crossing.** Let `a ∈ M_n(A(Γ))` with `i(a)` self-adjoint, and `c ∈ R` outside the
   spectrum of `i(a)`. If `L_c` misses the spectrum of `a` in `M_n(A(Γ))`, then
   `τ(χ_(-∞,c)(i(a))) ∈ Z`.
4. **Witness shape.** Let `a ∈ M_n(Z[i][Γ])` be self-adjoint, and suppose
   `τ(χ_(-∞,c)(λ(a))) ∉ Z` for some `c` outside the reduced spectrum. Then for every such
   `A(Γ)`, in particular `ℓ¹(Γ)`, the spectrum of `a` in `M_n(A(Γ))` meets `L_c`.
   - For `n = 1` every reduced gap with spectrum on both sides qualifies.
   - So a counterexample to `kadison-kaplansky-torsion-free` on `Γ` is an element whose
     reduced spectrum is disconnected while its spectrum in every unconditional completion
     connects across the gap.
5. **Capacity.** Every Kadison–Kaplansky witness `a ∈ Z[i][Γ]` has `cap(σ_r(a)) >= 1`.
6. **Rapid decay subgroups.** For `a ∈ Z[i][Γ]` self-adjoint: if the subgroup generated by
   the support of `a` has property (RD) for some length function, then the reduced spectrum
   of `a` is connected. Witness supports generate subgroups without (RD).

**Where this stops.** `sl3z-has-no-spectral-unconditional-completion`: for `SL_3(Z)` no
unconditional completion is dense in `C*_r` and closed under holomorphic calculus.
- Items 3 and 4 do not exclude witnesses there.
- They say a witness must live in the spectral excess of unconditional completions over the
  reduced algebra.
- That excess already shows up on the amenable subgroup `Z^2 ⋊ Z`, where
  Kadison–Kaplansky holds.

**Scope.**
- Torsion-free finite-index subgroups of `EL_3(Z[x_1, ..., x_d])`, `d >= 1`, are not covered:
  no embedding as discrete subgroups of reductive Lie groups is used or known here.
- Novelty: none claimed. Items 1–2 for `C[Γ]` are credited to Lafforgue in Fuchs
  (arXiv:math/0612023, p. 4). The rest is packaging for this graph.
