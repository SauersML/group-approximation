**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed). It is elementary: it uses only the Chinese
remainder theorem and the cocycle identity for canonical maps. No priority is claimed.

## Statement

A *box* is a class `r(m) = {r + tm : t ∈ Z}` with `0 ≤ r < m`. For boxes `A = r(m)` and
`B = s(n)`, the *canonical map* `κ(A,B) : A → B` is `r + tm ↦ s + tn`. A permutation
`g` of `Z` is *piecewise canonical* if there are box partitions `{C_i}` and `{D_i}` of
`Z` with `g|C_i = κ(C_i, D_i)`. Write `PC` for these permutations, and `CT(Z)` for the
group generated by the class transpositions `t(A,B)` of disjoint boxes. Then

    PC = CT(Z),

and for a finite set `P` of odd primes the same holds with every modulus `P'`-smooth,
where `P' = P ∪ {2}`. The inclusion `CT(Z) ⊆ PC` is immediate, so the content is
`PC ⊆ CT(Z)`.

## Setup

- **Canonical maps.** They satisfy `κ(B,C) κ(A,B) = κ(A,C)` and `κ(A,B)^(-1) = κ(B,A)`.
  The restriction of `κ(A,B)` to a sub-box of `A` is the canonical map onto its image,
  which is a box.
- **Trees.** The *`k`-split* of `r(m)` is its `k` children `r + jm (km)`, `0 ≤ j < k`.
  A *tree* is built from a root box by repeatedly splitting a leaf by a prime.
  - Its leaves partition the root.
  - Its *type* is the prime at the root together with the multiset of the primes at the
    other internal nodes.
  - A *natural partition* is the leaf set of a tree rooted at `Z`.
  - For a tree `u` rooted at `Z` and a box `N`, `κ(Z,N)(u)` is `u` placed in `N`.
- **Equivalence.** For partitions `P` and `Q`, write `P ≈ Q` if some `h ∈ CT(Z)` maps
  every piece of `P` canonically onto a piece of `Q`. This is an equivalence relation
  by the cocycle identity.
- **Refinement.** `u^x` is `u` with the tree `x` placed in every leaf.

## Lemmas

1. **Natural representatives.** Each `g ∈ PC` maps some natural partition canonically
   onto a natural partition.
   - Refine `{C_i}` to `Π_L`, all residues mod `L = lcm(m_i)`; it is natural. The
     images form a box partition `D'`.
   - Split each piece `Y` of `D'` fully down to `L' = lcm` of the moduli of `D'`, and
     split its preimage by the same integer. The target becomes `Π_{L'}`, the source
     becomes a refinement of `Π_L`, and both are natural.
   - Splitting by `k = ab` is splitting by `a` and then by `b`.
2. **Free bijection.** If `P ≈ Q`, then every `g` mapping `P` canonically onto `Q` lies
   in `CT(Z)`.
   - With `h` witnessing `P ≈ Q`, `σ = g h^(-1)` permutes the pieces of `Q`
     canonically.
   - `π ↦` (the canonical permutation of the pieces by `π`) is a homomorphism from
     `Sym(Q)` to `PC` that sends transpositions to class transpositions.
3. **Transport.** For a box `N`, conjugating by `κ(Z,N)` and extending by the identity
   is an injective homomorphism `φ_N` with `φ_N(t(A,B)) = t(κ(Z,N)A, κ(Z,N)B)`. So if
   `u ≈ v`, a tree with pattern `u` at a node `N` is `≈` to the tree with `v` at `N`,
   where the subtree at each leaf of `u` moves along to its image leaf.
4. **Exchange.** Two trees rooted at `Z` of the same type are `≈`.
   - If two nodes `N` and `N'` are not in ancestor relation, then `t(N,N')` is a class
     transposition. It swaps their subtrees canonically, by the cocycle identity.
   - Such swaps bring every tree to a *chain*: the root has one internal child, at
     position 0, and so on down, with the primes in a fixed order.
   - By induction on the number of internal nodes: move a node `Q` of the first prime to
     a root child. If the root has no leaf child, first swap another root child with a
     leaf under `Q`. Then swap every other internal root child into a leaf of `Q`, and
     recurse inside `Q`.
5. **Commutation.** For primes `p ≠ q`, a node split by `p` whose children are all split
   by `q` gives the same partition as the node split by `q` with all children split by
   `p`. Both are the full `pq`-split, and every grandchild subtree stays on its own box.
   - As a change of type at a non-root node, going from `q` over `q` copies of `p` to
     `p` over `p` copies of `q`: remove one `q` and `q` copies of `p`, add one `p` and
     `p` copies of `q`. For `q = 2` this removes `{2, p, p}` and adds `{p, 2^p}`.
   - At the root: root `q` over `q` copies of `p` becomes root `p` over `p` copies of
     `q`, and conversely.
6. **Cancellation.** If `u^x ≈ v^x`, then `u ≈ v`.
   - Let `h` witness this. Compose it with a canonical permutation `σ` of the leaves of
     `v^x` (Lemma 2) so that `σh` sends the `λ`-th leaf of `x` under the leaf `u_ℓ` to
     the `λ`-th leaf of `x` under `v_(π ℓ)`.
   - By restriction and uniqueness of canonical maps, `σh|u_ℓ = κ(u_ℓ, v_(π ℓ))`.
7. **Base.** For an odd prime `p`, the `p`-split of `Z` is `≈` to a binary tree with `p`
   leaves.

## Proof of Lemma 7

Write `2` and `p` for the node types. Let `a` be the `p`-split, `b` the `2`-split, and
`x = b(a, b)`, a tree of type `(2, {p, 2})`. Let `u` be the `p`-split of `Z`, and `v` a
binary tree with `p` leaves.

1. `u^x` has type `(p, {2p·2, p·p})`, that is, root `p` and non-root multiset
   `{2,…,2 (2p times), p,…,p (p times)}`. Its root children are the roots of the
   `x`'s, all split by `2`. Root commutation (Lemma 5) gives type `(2, {p·2, (p+2)·p})`.
2. By Lemma 4 choose a tree of this type in which some non-root `2`-node has two
   `p`-children, then commute there: type `(2, {(2p−1)·2, (p+1)·p})`.
3. Repeat once: type `(2, {(3p−2)·2, p·p})`.
4. `v^x` has type `(2, {(p−2)·2 + p·{2, p, 2}}) = (2, {(3p−2)·2, p·p})`. Lemma 4 gives
   `u^x ≈ v^x`, and Lemma 6 gives `u ≈ v`.

Only the moduli `2` and `p` occur.

## Proof of the theorem

- **Every natural partition is `≈` to a binary one.** Replace the `p`-split at an odd
  node by a binary tree with `p` leaves (Lemmas 7 and 3), and induct on the number of
  odd nodes.
- **All binary partitions of one size are `≈`.** They have the same type (Lemma 4).
- **Conclusion.** Let `g ∈ PC`. Lemma 1 gives natural `C`, `D` with `g` mapping `C`
  onto `D`, so `C ≈ D`. Lemma 2 then gives `g ∈ CT(Z)`.
- **Smooth moduli.** Every step only splits by primes already present, together with
  `2`. So the proof stays inside `CT_P(Z)` when all moduli are `P'`-smooth.

  The rewriting uses exchanges, commutations and cancellations, and there it would still
  have to be shown that the loops they create are null in the presented group.
- **Why cancellation is essential.** Swaps never change the root prime, and on a fixed
  partition a `p`-divisible modulus cannot disappear. So the `p`-split and a binary tree
  of size `p` are not related by moves at their own size; the refinement by `x` is what
  makes room.
