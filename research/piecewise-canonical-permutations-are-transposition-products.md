**OPEN.** Two lemmas below are proved (elementary, lane proofs, not reviewed); the
induction they were meant to drive has a gap, stated precisely in **The gap**. No
priority is claimed.

## Statement

Call a class `r(m) = {r + tm : t ∈ Z}` with `0 ≤ r < m` a *box*, and for boxes
`A = r(m)`, `B = s(n)` let `κ_(A,B) : A → B` be the *canonical map*
`r + tm ↦ s + tn`. A permutation `g` of `Z` is *piecewise canonical* if there are box
partitions `{C_i}`, `{D_i}` of `Z` (`i ≤ M`) with `g|C_i = κ_(C_i,D_i)`. Write `PC` for
the set of these, and `CT(Z)` for the group generated by the class transpositions
`t(A,B)` of disjoint boxes (`κ_(A,B)` on `A`, `κ_(B,A)` on `B`, the identity elsewhere).

    PC = CT(Z).

The inclusion `CT(Z) ⊆ PC` is immediate, since the complement of `A ⊔ B` is a finite
union of boxes on which the identity is canonical. The claim is `PC ⊆ CT(Z)`.

## Why it matters

`ct-p-z-is-a-one-vertex-k-graph-full-group` item 3 asserts `PC = CT_P(Z)` by
identifying `CT_P(Z)` with `[[G_Λ]]`, so it inherits the generation theorem for
topological full groups of purely infinite minimal groupoids. That is the only
non-elementary input to the answer to Kourovka 17.59 in
`ct-z-is-the-rcwa-group-fixing-the-nonnegative-integers`. An elementary proof of
`PC ⊆ CT(Z)` would make 17.59 self-contained, and would leave 17.61 depending on the
groupoid literature only for `F_∞`, not for the identification.

## Proved

Both are elementary; they use only the Chinese remainder theorem and the cocycle
identity `κ_(B,C) κ_(A,B) = κ_(A,C)`.

1. **`PC` is a group, and boxes pull back to boxes.** Boxes are closed under nonempty
   intersection, and a canonical map carries boxes to boxes in both directions. So for
   `g, h ∈ PC` the sets `C_i ∩ g^(-1)(C'_j)` are boxes and partition `Z`, and `hg` is
   canonical on each. Hence `PC` is a group.
2. **Transitivity on boxes by two transpositions.** For proper boxes `A, B` there is
   `w ∈ CT(Z)`, a product of at most two class transpositions, with
   `w|A = κ_(A,B)`.
   - If `A ∩ B = ∅`, take `w = t(A,B)`.
   - Otherwise `A ∪ B ≠ Z`. Indeed if `A = r(m)`, `B = s(n)` meet and cover `Z`, then
     the densities give `1/m + 1/n − 1/lcm(m,n) = 1`, which forces `m = 1` or `n = 1`,
     i.e. `A = Z` or `B = Z`, against properness. So some box `C` lies in the
     complement of `A ∪ B`, and `w = t(C,B) t(A,C)` satisfies
     `w|A = κ_(C,B) κ_(A,C) = κ_(A,B)`.
3. **Free choice of the bijection.** Transpositions of two pieces of one partition are
   class transpositions, and they carry that partition onto itself. So if some
   `g ∈ PC` with source `{C_i}` and target `{D_i}` lies in `CT(Z)`, then so does every
   piecewise-canonical permutation with the same two partitions and any other
   bijection between them: the two differ by an element of the copy of `Sym(M)`
   generated by the `t(D_i, D_j)`, and that copy changes neither partition.

## The gap

The intended induction is on `M`. Given `g` with `M ≥ 2` pieces, item 2 supplies
`w ∈ CT(Z)` with `w|C_1 = g|C_1`, so `h = w^(-1) g` fixes `C_1` pointwise and `h ∈ PC`
by item 1. One would then recurse on `Z ∖ C_1` with `M − 1` pieces.

The step that fails is that `h` need not have `M − 1` pieces. The new target partition
is `{w^(-1)(D_j)}_(j ≥ 2)`, and `w^(-1)` splits any `D_j` that meets the support of `w`
without being contained in it, so `h`'s minimal piece count can exceed `M`. Item 3
removes the freedom in the bijection but not this refinement blow-up, and the same
obstruction appears in each variant tried:

- recursing on a common refinement `T` of the two partitions: refining the source along
  `T` replaces the target by the canonical images of the `T`-pieces, which again need
  not refine `T`;
- choosing the moved piece by maximal or minimal modulus: boxes of a partition need not
  be comparable under divisibility, for example `{0(2), 1(4), 3(4)}` against
  `{0(3), 1(3), 2(3)}`, so no piece of one partition need contain or be contained in a
  piece of the other;
- restricting the helper transpositions to the support: item 2 needs room outside
  `C_1 ∪ D_1`, which exists in `Z` but not always inside the remaining support.

What is needed is a terminating measure on `PC` that the reduction of item 2 decreases,
or a choice of `w` whose inverse preserves the rest of the target partition. This is the
same coherence statement recorded as the precise gap in the Attempts of
`class-transposition-relations-present-ct-p-z`, so closing either should close both.

## Scope

- Nothing here depends on `P`; the same argument and the same gap apply to `CT_P(Z)`
  with `P'`-smooth moduli throughout, since item 2's helper box `C` can be chosen with
  `P'`-smooth modulus (take it inside the complement, which is a union of boxes of
  modulus `lcm(m, n)`).
- No Lean work was started, following the standing rule that formalization comes after
  the informal proof closes.
