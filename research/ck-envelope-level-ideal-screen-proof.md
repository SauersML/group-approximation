Put `I=lev(M)`.  Additivity of elementary matrices and the Steinberg
commutator identities, for pairwise distinct indices,

```text
[e_ki(x),e_ij(a)] = e_kj(xa),
[e_ij(a),e_jk(x)] = e_ik(ax),
```

show that `I` is an additive subgroup closed under multiplication on either
side by arbitrary elements of `U`; elementary signed permutation matrices
change the pair of indices.  Thus `I` is a two-sided ideal.

If `1 in I`, normality and the same commutators put every `e_ij(u)` in `M`,
so `M=EL_n(U)`.  A proper `M` therefore has proper level.

For `1 != s in S`, simplicity gives `g in <<s>>_S`.  The identities

```text
1-ab=(1-a)+a(1-b),
1-a^{-1}=-a^{-1}(1-a),
1-hah^{-1}=h(1-a)h^{-1}
```

put `1-g` in the two-sided ideal generated by `1-s`.  Since `1-g` is full
in `U`, so is `1-s`.  If `1-s in I`, the commutators put
`e_ij(a(1-s)b)` in `M` for all `a,b` and all `i != j`; fullness and
additivity then force `M=EL_n(U)`, a contradiction.  Hence no such `1-s`
lies in `I`.

Finally, equal images of `s,t in S` modulo `I` give
`1-s^{-1}t in I`; the preceding paragraph forces `s=t`.  Thus `S` embeds
in `(U/I)^x`, proving all four assertions.
