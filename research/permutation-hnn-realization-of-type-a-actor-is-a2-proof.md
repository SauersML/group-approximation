Notation is as in the target.

## (a)

**Finite presentation.** `L` is presented by:
- the finitely many generators and relators of `Γ`;
- one letter `t`;
- one relator `t d_j t^-1 = ψ(d_j)` for each of the finitely many generators `d_j` of
  `D_1`. Here `ψ(d_j)` is written as a word in the generators of `Γ`.

These relators hold for `π` in `Sym(X)`, so `ρ` is a well-defined homomorphism. Its
image is generated by `ρ(Γ)` and `π`.

**Pair orbits.** First, `Γ` has finitely many orbits on `X`. If `|X| <= 1` this is
trivial. Otherwise every point lies in a two-element subset, and the orbit of the
subset determines the orbit of the point up to two choices.

The orbits of `Γ` on `X x X` are:
- the diagonal, whose orbits correspond to point orbits;
- off-diagonal ordered pairs, at most two orbits over each orbit of two-element
  subsets.

So `Γ`, and hence `L`, has finitely many orbits on `X x X`.

**Stabilizers.** Fix `x in X` and let `P = Stab_L(x)`, taken for the action through
`ρ`.

Let `T` be the Bass--Serre tree of `L`:
- vertex set `L/Γ`;
- edge set `L/D_2`, where the edge `gD_2` joins `gΓ` and `gtΓ`.

By Britton's lemma, `Γ ∩ tΓt^-1 = tD_1t^-1 = D_2`. So this is the standard tree, `L`
acts on it without inversions, and the edge stabilizers are the conjugates of `D_2`.

By Serre, *Trees*, §I.5.4, `P` is the fundamental group of a graph of groups on the
connected graph `P\T`. Its vertex groups are the stabilizers in `P` of vertices of
`T`.

- *Vertices.* The vertices of `P\T` are the double cosets `P\L/Γ`. Inversion matches
  them with `Γ\L/P`. Since `L/P ≅ ρ(L).x` as `L`-sets, `Γ\L/P` is the set of
  `Γ`-orbits on `ρ(L).x ⊆ X`. There are finitely many.
- *Edges.* The same argument matches the edges with the `D_2`-orbits on
  `ρ(L).x`. The relation `π(D_1.y) = D_2.π(y)` shows that `π` maps `D_1`-orbits
  bijectively onto `D_2`-orbits. So there are finitely many.
- *Vertex groups.* The vertex group at `gΓ` is
  `P ∩ gΓg^-1 = g (Stab_L(g^-1.x) ∩ Γ) g^-1 = g Stab_Γ(g^-1.x) g^-1`. This is
  finitely generated, because `Γ ↷ X` has finitely generated point stabilizers.

The fundamental group of a finite connected graph of groups is generated by its
vertex groups and one letter for each edge outside a maximal tree. So `P` is finitely
generated.

Faithfulness of `ρ` was never used, so `L ↷ X` is of type `[A_2]`.

**Kernel.** `Γ ↷ X` is faithful, so `K ∩ Γ = 1`. As `K` is normal,
`K ∩ gΓg^-1 = g(K ∩ Γ)g^-1 = 1`. So `K` acts on `T` without inversions and with
trivial vertex stabilizers. A group acting freely and without inversions on a tree is
free (Serre, *Trees*, §I.3.3).

**Word problem.** On the generators of `Γ` together with `π`, the word problem of
`<Γ, π>` is exactly membership in `K` for words in the generators of `Γ` and `t`. ∎

## (b)

**Step 1: every `g in L` commensurates `Γ`.** Let `Comm` be the set of `g in L` such
that `Γ ∩ gΓg^-1` has finite index in both `Γ` and `gΓg^-1`.

*`Comm` contains `Γ` and is closed under inverses.* The inverse `g^-1` conjugates the
pair `(Γ ∩ gΓg^-1 <= gΓg^-1)` onto `(g^-1Γg ∩ Γ <= Γ)`.

*`Comm` is closed under products.* Let `g, h in Comm`, and put

```text
B = (Γ ∩ gΓg^-1) ∩ g(Γ ∩ hΓh^-1)g^-1   ⊆   Γ ∩ ghΓh^-1g^-1.
```

Both terms of `B` have finite index in `gΓg^-1`, so `B` does too. The index
`[Γ ∩ gΓg^-1 : B]` is at most `[gΓg^-1 : B]`, which is finite. Since
`[Γ : Γ ∩ gΓg^-1]` is finite, `[Γ : B]` is finite.

For the other side, put `F = g^-1Γg ∩ Γ` and `H' = Γ ∩ hΓh^-1`. Then:
- `g^-1 B g = F ∩ H'`;
- `F` has finite index in `Γ`, by conjugating the finite-index inclusion
  `Γ ∩ gΓg^-1 <= gΓg^-1` by `g^-1`;
- `H'` has finite index in `hΓh^-1`, because `h in Comm`.

So `[H' : F ∩ H'] <= [Γ : F]` is finite. Hence `F ∩ H'` has finite index in
`hΓh^-1`, and conjugating by `g`, `B` has finite index in `ghΓh^-1g^-1`.

So `Comm` is a subgroup.

*`t in Comm`.* We have `Γ ∩ tΓt^-1 = D_2`, of finite index in `Γ`, and
`t^-1 D_2 t = D_1`, of finite index in `Γ`. So `Comm = L`.

**Step 2: kernel elements centralize a finite-index subgroup.** Let `k in K` and
`E_k = Γ ∩ k^-1Γk`, of finite index in `Γ` by Step 1. For `e in E_k`, both `k e k^-1`
and `e` lie in `Γ` and have the same image under `ρ`. So `(k e k^-1) e^-1` lies in
`Γ ∩ K = 1`. Hence `k` commutes in `L` with every element of `E_k`.

**Step 3: certificates.** Apply `a2-kernel-recursion-ascends-normal-orbit-finite-chains`
(1) to `L ↷ X`, which is of type `[A_2]` by (a), with:
- `W` = all words;
- `K_0 = {1}`, whose word set is r.e. since `L` is finitely presented.

For `C`, fix `Γ`-orbit representatives `z_1, ..., z_r`, each written as a word applied
to an `L`-orbit representative. Enumerate all `n >= 1` and all assignments of
permutations in `Sym(n)` to the generators of `Γ`. Keep those that satisfy the
relators of `Γ` and act transitively; each kept assignment is a homomorphism
`θ : Γ -> Sym(n)`. From `θ`:
- compute a Schreier transversal `c_1, ..., c_n`, with `θ(c_j)(1) = j`;
- compute the Schreier generators `A_θ` of `E_θ = Stab_θ(1)`;
- put `Y_θ = {c_j^-1.z_l}`.

Each pair `(A_θ, Y_θ)` is valid. Indeed `Γ = ⋃_j c_j E_θ`, hence
`Γ = ⋃_j E_θ c_j^-1`, and so `E_θ.Y_θ = Γ.{z_l} = X`.

For `k in K`, take `θ` to be the action of `Γ` on `Γ/E_k`. By Step 2, `[k, a] = 1`
for every `a in A_θ`. So (1) gives that `K` is r.e.

By `a2-kernel-removal-forces-recursive-kernel` (a), `K` is co-r.e. Hence `K` is
recursive, and by (a) `<Γ, π>` has solvable word problem. ∎

## (c)

`γ t γ^-1 t^-1 in K` iff `ρ(γ) π ρ(γ)^-1 π^-1 = id` iff `γ in C_Γ(π)`. The map on
words is computable. So a decision procedure for `K` decides `C_Γ(π)`. ∎
