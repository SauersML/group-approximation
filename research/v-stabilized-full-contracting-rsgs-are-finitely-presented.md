## Setting

- `Σ_Γ` is an SFT with irreducible core, and `E ⊆ Σ_Γ` is a nonempty clopen set.
- `L ≤ R_(Γ,E)` is a **full contracting RSG** (BBMZ Defs. `def:full`, `def:rsg`, `def:contracting`).
  - `L` is finitely presented (BBMZ Thm. `thrm:fin_pres`).
  - `L ⊇ V_(Γ,E)` (BBMZ Prop. `prop:RSGsAndV`).
  - **Mapping cones (BBMZ Cor. `cor:MappingConesWithV`).** For disjoint cones `C_(α_i)` and `C_(β_i)` with
    `t(α_i) = t(β_i)` and neither union equal to `E`, some `f ∈ V_(Γ,E)` maps each `C_(α_i)` onto `C_(β_i)` by the
    canonical similarity.
- `G_L` is the germ groupoid of `L` on `E`. `T' = G_V × (L ⋉ E)` is the transformation form, and `F(T')` is its
  full-bisection group.
- `A = V(L) = C(C, L) ⋊ V` and `B = LC(E, V) ⋊ L`, as in `v-times-gluing-presentations-are-exact` with `Λ = L`.
- For each node type `τ`, fix a proper cone `R_τ ⊊ E` of type `τ`.

## Theorem

`F(G_V × G_L)` is finitely presented.

**Proof outline.**
- `A` is finitely presented, because `L` is (`cantor-integer-maps-by-v-are-of-type-fp2`, item 1 as used in the
  split node).
- `B` is finitely presented by Lemma 1.
- Hence `Γ~ = A *_(V×L) B / ⟨⟨[A_0, B_1]⟩⟩` is finitely presented. `V × L` is finitely generated, and
  `[A_0, B_1] = 1` follows from finitely many relators `[g, h^[R_τ]]`, by the split node's argument: `B_1` is
  generated by `L`-conjugates of the `h^[R_τ]`, and `L` normalizes `A_0`.
- By `v-times-gluing-presentations-are-exact` (any group, any space), `Γ~ ≅ F(T')`.
- `F(G_V × G_L) = F(T') / 𝔈`, where `𝔈` is the group of full bisections acting trivially. By Lemma 2, `𝔈` is
  the normal closure of finitely many elements.
- A quotient of a finitely presented group by finitely many relators is finitely presented. ∎

## Lemma 0: rigid stabilizers are full contracting RSGs

For a nonempty clopen `U ⊆ E`, let `L_U` be the elements of `L` supported in `U`.
- `L_U` is a full contracting RSG on `U`:
  - it contains `V_(Γ,U)`, so it is an RSG by BBMZ Prop. `prop:RSGsAndV`;
  - it is full on `U`, since local agreement is local;
  - its nucleus lies in `Nuc_L`, which is finite.
- Hence `L_U` is finitely presented, and in particular finitely generated.
- `Stab_L(U) = L_U × L_(E∖U)` by fullness.

## Lemma 1: the Boolean power over a full group is finitely presented

`B = LC(E, V) ⋊ L` has the following finite presentation.

**Generators.** Those of `L`, and `x_(v,τ)` for `v` in a finite generating set of `V` and each type `τ`. Here
`x_(v,τ)` stands for `v^[R_τ]`.

**Relations.**
- (a) The relators of `V` in each family `x_(·,τ)`.
- (b) `[κ, x_(v,τ)] = 1` for `κ` in finite generating sets of `L_(R_τ)` and of `L_(E∖R_τ)` (Lemma 0).
- (c) **One-step splits.** For each edge `e` out of `τ`, the child cone `C_(α_τ e)` equals `κ_e R_(t(e))`, with
  `κ_e` given by mapping cones. The relation is `x_(v,τ) = ∏_e κ_e x_(v,t(e)) κ_e^(-1)`.
- (d) **Disjoint pairs.** For each ordered pair of types `(τ, τ')`, fix disjoint cones `R_τ, R*_(τ')` whose union
  is not `E`, with `R*_(τ') = θ R_(τ')`. The relation is `[x_(v,τ), θ x_(w,τ') θ^(-1)] = 1`.
- (f) **Nucleus.** `Nuc_L` is finite. For each `n ∈ Nuc_L` fix `λ_n ∈ L` and a cone `S_n = κ_n R_τ` with
  `λ_n|_(S_n) = n`. Its image `λ_n S_n` is a clopen `⊔_j R_j^(n)` of cones `R_j^(n) = κ_(n,j) R_(τ_j)`. The
  relation is `λ_n κ_n x_(v,τ) κ_n^(-1) λ_n^(-1) = ∏_j κ_(n,j) x_(v,τ_j) κ_(n,j)^(-1)`.

**Proof that it presents `B`.** Call the presented group `B̃`.
- *Conditioned elements.* For a proper cone `R = κ R_τ`, where `κ` exists by mapping cones, put
  `v^[R] := κ x_(v,τ) κ^(-1)`. By (b) this does not depend on `κ`. For a clopen set, take the product over a cone
  decomposition. Refinements are handled by (c), so this does not depend on the decomposition.
- *(P1)* `v ↦ v^[R]` is a homomorphism, by (a).
- *(P2)* `λ v^[R] λ^(-1) = v^[λR]` for `λ ∈ L`.
  - Split `R` into cones `S = C_α` so deep that `λ|_α = n ∈ Nuc_L`. This is possible because `λ|_α ∈ Nuc_L`
    for all but finitely many `α`.
  - Let `κ` be the canonical similarity `S_n → S`, from mapping cones. Then `λκ` agrees with `λ_n` on `S_n`, so
    `μ = (λκ)^(-1) λ_n ∈ L_(E∖S_n) ⊆ Stab(S_n)`.
  - Hence `λ v^[S] λ^(-1) = λ_n μ^(-1) v^[S_n] μ λ_n^(-1) = λ_n v^[S_n] λ_n^(-1)`, using (b). By (f), this is
    `v^[λS]`.
- *(P3)* `v^[R]` and `w^[R']` commute for disjoint cones.
  - If `R ∪ R' ≠ E`, mapping cones gives one `κ` with `κ(R_τ, R*_(τ')) = (R, R')`. Conjugate (d).
  - If `R ∪ R' = E`, split `R'` by (c) and apply the first case.
- *Directed union.*
  - For a finite partition `𝒫` of `E` into proper cones, `W̃_𝒫 = ⟨v^[R] : R ∈ 𝒫⟩` is a quotient of `V^𝒫`, by
    (P1) and (P3).
  - It maps onto `V^𝒫 ≤ LC(E, V)`, and the composite `V^𝒫 → W̃_𝒫 → V^𝒫` is the identity. So `W̃_𝒫 ≅ V^𝒫`.
  - Refinement embeds `W̃_𝒫` into `W̃_(𝒫')`, by (c). So `W̃ = ⋃ W̃_𝒫 ≅ colim V^𝒫 = LC(E, V)`.
  - By (P2), `W̃` is normal and `B̃ = W̃ · L`. Killing the `x`'s gives a retraction `B̃ → L`, so `W̃ ∩ L = 1`.
  - Hence `B̃ ≅ LC(E, V) ⋊ L = B`. ∎

## Lemma 2: the trivially acting bisections are finitely normally generated

**Structure of `𝔈`.** An element of `𝔈` is a locally constant label map `(c, x) ↦ λ(c, x) ∈ L`, where each
`λ(c, x)` fixes a neighbourhood of `x`. It multiplies pointwise. Refining it to a product grid gives
`e = ∏_i ε_([w_i], σ_i)`. Here `ε_([w], σ)` is the label `σ(x)` on `[w] × E` and `1` elsewhere, and `σ` is a
germ-trivial section `E → L`.
- **Cones.** `V ≤ F(T')` has trivial labels, so conjugation by `v ∈ V` moves `ε_([0], σ)` to `ε_(v[0], σ)`. So
  `𝔈 = ⟨⟨ε_([0], σ)⟩⟩`.
- **Equivariance.** `σ ↦ ε_([0], σ)` is a homomorphism. It is `L`-equivariant for global labels:
  `κ ε_([0], σ) κ^(-1) = ε_([0], κ·σ)`, where `(κ·σ)(x) = κ σ(κ^(-1)x) κ^(-1)`.
- **Sections.** A germ-trivial section is a product of elementary sections `σ_(λ,R)`: `λ` on a clopen `R`, and
  `1` elsewhere, with `λ` fixing `R` pointwise, so `λ ∈ L_(E∖R)`.
  - Split `R` into cones.
  - For a cone `R = κ R_τ`, `σ_(λ,R) = κ·σ_(κ^(-1)λκ, R_τ)`, with `κ^(-1)λκ ∈ L_(E∖R_τ)`.
  - `μ ↦ σ_(μ, R_τ)` is a homomorphism on `L_(E∖R_τ)`, which is finitely generated by Lemma 0.
- **Conclusion.** `𝔈` is the normal closure in `F(T')` of the finitely many elements `ε_([0], σ_(s, R_τ))`, for
  each type `τ` and each `s` in a finite generating set of `L_(E∖R_τ)`. ∎

## Corollaries

1. **Calibration: Brin's theorem.** For `L = V = V_(2,1)`, the full shift on two letters, `G_L = G_V` and
   `F(G_V × G_V) = 2V`. So 2V is finitely presented (Brin). This is a consistency check, not a new result.
2. **Matui–Matsumoto groups.** For `L = V_(Γ,E)`, `F(G_V × G_(Σ_Γ)|_E)` is finitely presented.
3. **E3′ for hyperbolic seeds.**
   - Let `Γ` be hyperbolic and act faithfully and topologically freely on `∂_h Γ`, and let `[[Γ | ∂_h Γ]]` be a
     full contracting RSG. BBMZ prove this whenever `Γ` has a proper `Z` free factor, in particular for every
     `Γ ∗ Z`, which is the tree-gluing carrier.
   - Then `G_L = Γ ⋉ ∂_h Γ`, and `F(G_V × (Γ ⋉ ∂_h Γ))` is finitely presented.
   - This closes the statement of `v-stabilized-horofunction-hosts-need-upstream-certification`, with no finite
     shadow basis.
4. **Rigidity for free.** By `topologically-free-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`, the seedless
   horofunction SFT of such a `Γ` is quantum rigid and of finite type. That closes the rigidity question left open
   in `hyperbolic-groups-have-sft-horofunction-compactifications`, for these `Γ`.

## Lesson for general BH

**Fullness is room in the space, and room kills the Boolean-power obstruction.**
- Over a bare group `Λ`, the conditioned elements `v^[R]` at far sites satisfy relations that only rigidity can
  certify. That is P2′a, still open.
- Over a full contracting group `L`, any configuration of disjoint cones can be moved to a standard one by an
  element of `L`, and the finite nucleus limits how `L` distorts cones. So finitely many relations present
  `LC(E, V) ⋊ L`.
- The trivially acting labels, which are the price of passing from `L ⋉ E` to its germ groupoid, are also
  generated by finitely many rigid-stabilizer types.

With exact gluing (22a3770d9), V-stabilization preserves finite presentation for every full contracting RSG. So the
algebraic route succeeds exactly where the topological route (finite shadow bases) is blocked by the lack of
upstream certification. For carriers the recipe is: **replace the acting group by its full closure, then glue.**
The remaining question for the master route is whether the (★′) carriers over non-hyperbolic `Λ₀` have full
closures that are finitely presented and contracting.
