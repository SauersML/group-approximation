Let `R` be a unital associative ring and `r in R`.  Suppose there are an
injective unital homomorphism `phi : R -> B` and elements `P, Q in B` with

    P phi(r) Q = 1_B .

Let `U = R<p,q>/(p r q - 1)` be the universal unital ring obtained from `R`
by adjoining two noncommuting elements subject to that single relation.
Then

1. the canonical map `R -> U` is injective;
2. `r` is full in `U`, that is `U r U = U`;
3. if `R` is finitely presented as a unital associative ring, so is `U`.

## Why this is an improvement on the construction it comes from

Chatterji--Kassabov's Remark 5 says that their extension `L` need not be
finitely presented even when `R` is, and that is why their proof interposes a
Shalom finitely presented cover.  Remark 5 is a statement about *their
particular* subring of `End(W)`, and it does not transfer to the universal
ring with the same defining relation.

Their own proof of Lemma 4 supplies the hypothesis above.  Read from source
2026-08-17: "Since the element r is nonzero the linear transformation φ(r): W
→ W has rank exactly 𝔠 which is the same as the dimension of the vector space
W. Hence we can find linear transformations P,Q ∈ End(W) such that P ∘ φ(r) ∘
Q = Id", and then "we can take L to be the subring of End(W) generated by
φ(R) and α and β".  So `L` is generated by `phi(R)` together with the two
sandwich elements, which makes `L` a quotient of `U`, and the composite
`R -> U ->> L ⊆ End(W)` is the original injection.  The surjection `U ->> L`
is what certifies that no extra relation has been imposed that could collapse
the embedded copy of `R`.

## What it buys downstream

Take `R = Z[G~]` for the finitely presented group `G~` produced by the
Chatterji--Kassabov chain and `r = 1 - g`.  Then `R` is finitely presented as
a unital ring — group generators plus formal inverses, the inverse relations,
and the finitely many group relators — and has torsion-free additive group,
so their Lemma 4 applies and supplies the sandwich.  Hence

    U = Z[G~]<p,q>/(p(1-g)q - 1)

is a finitely presented ring containing `R` with `1 - g` full. The
Steinberg relations together with fullness put every elementary generator
of `EL_4(U)` into the normal closure of the corner element `g`.

**Correction, September 8:** Krstić--McCool supplies finite presentation
of `St_4(U)`, not automatically of `EL_4(U)`. The previous assertion that
all group covers could be removed is withdrawn. The exact verified input
is [[steinberg-finite-presentation-and-kazhdan-theorem]].

[[ck-steinberg-marked-cover]] supplies a finitely presented property-(T)
quotient of `St_4(U)` that still maps onto `EL_4(U)`, embeds the prescribed
finitely presented marking, and is normally generated by `g`. This makes
the source of the cover explicit. It does not remove its kernel, and the
maximal-kernel route must retain the finitely presented cover as ambient
group unless finite presentation of the elementary group is separately proved.

## What it does not buy

Simplicity.  See `one-sided-sandwich-leaves-an-ideal-missing-the-core`: the
relation `prq = 1` makes the prescribed element full and leaves room for a
proper nonzero ideal disjoint from `R`.
