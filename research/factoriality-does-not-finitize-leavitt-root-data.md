The exact finite-dimensional proof for `St_n(L_(F_2)(1,2))` cannot be
extended by replacing "finite-dimensional representation" with "finite-factor
representation".

Indeed, finite-dimensionality is used to make every elementary-abelian root
image finite.  A finite factor can contain infinite elementary abelian
two-groups whose generated abelian von Neumann algebra is diffuse.  In the
canonical regular representation this happens literally:

```text
pi(x_ij(L))''=L((L,+))
```

is diffuse.  By `finite-root-algebra-forces-steinberg-triviality`, every
nontrivial representation is actually injective on every root subgroup, so
there is no nonzero common root kernel from which to form a finite coefficient
quotient.

Factoriality only says that the algebra generated by **all** group elements
has scalar center.  It neither makes the individual abelian root algebra
atomic nor bounds its dimension.  The Steinberg commutator relations encode
coefficient multiplication between root groups, but they do not turn the
root unitaries into additive coefficient operators in the factor.

Thus a theorem saying that every nonregular factorial character extends to a
finite ring representation would require a new character-rigidity input; it
does not follow from the Steinberg presentation, simplicity of the coefficient
ring, property `(T)`, or factoriality.  The known higher-rank character theorem
for `EL_d(R)` assumes a commutative Noetherian coefficient ring and therefore
does not apply to the noncommutative, non-Noetherian binary Leavitt algebra.
