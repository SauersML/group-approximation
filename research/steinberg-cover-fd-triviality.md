**Theorem.**  Let `L = L_{𝔽₂}(1,2)` (the binary Leavitt algebra — a
simple, infinite-dimensional `𝔽₂`-algebra) and `r ≥ 3`.  Every
finite-dimensional unitary representation of the Steinberg group
`St_r(L)` (and of `EL_r(L)`) is trivial.

**Proof (four lines, the char-p root-torsion mechanism plus
simplicity).**  Char 2 makes each root subgroup `x_ij(L) ≅ (L,+)` an
elementary abelian 2-group, so under a finite-dimensional unitary
representation its image is a simultaneously diagonalizable
2-torsion abelian group — finite — and `A_ij = ker ∩ x_ij(L)` has
finite additive index; put `A = ⋂ A_ij`.  The Steinberg relations
`[x_ik(a), x_kj(b)] = x_ij(ab)` and normality of the kernel put
`x_ij` of the two-sided ideal generated by `A` inside the kernel
(iterate for `L·A·L`).  That ideal contains a finite-codimension
subgroup, hence is nonzero; `L` is SIMPLE, so it is all of `L`, and
every root subgroup dies.  The roots generate `St_r(L)`.  ∎

**Corollary (exact solutions of the criterion do not exist).**  The
regular-`A₈` criterion (`atlas-steinberg-rank-five-translation`)
seeks `U ∈ U(20160·k)` making the `T_St` relators exact/approximate
while the charts act by the FIXED faithful regular representation of
`A₈`.  An exact solution at any finite `k` would be a
finite-dimensional unitary representation of the presented group —
`St₅(L)` by the established rank-five translation — nontrivial on
the charts; the theorem forces every such representation trivial,
contradicting faithfulness on `A₈`.  Hence **the criterion's defect
is strictly positive at every point of every finite dimension**; the
HS-stability fork is genuinely and exclusively about the infimum as
`k → ∞`, and any approximate-solution family for the hyperlinear
side is automatically far from every genuine representation —
the Becker–Lubotzky phenomenon is forced structurally on this fork,
not just guaranteed abstractly.

**Cross-fork remark.**  Both of the program's forks now run on the
same mechanism with opposite ring inputs: commutative Laurent rings
make root torsion produce finite congruence quotients
(`fd-invisibility-of-compression` — collapse, rigidity-side); the
simple Leavitt algebra makes it produce nothing at all (this node —
emptiness, criterion-side).  Finite-dimensional representation
theory sees the compression not at all, and the Leavitt Steinberg
cover not at all: in both cases the action is entirely in the
approximate/asymptotic regime.

Fence: this does not select a branch of the fork, does not bound the
infimum away from zero (that would refute hyperlinearity of `Q` and
is exactly the open stable branch), and says nothing about
infinite-dimensional or asymptotic representations.
