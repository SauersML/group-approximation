## Step 1: each relator lies in some `U_pi`

The Steinberg relators involve one root position (additivity), two
non-adjacent positions `(i,j),(k,l)` with `j!=k`, `i!=l`, or three
positions `(i,j),(j,k),(i,k)` with `i,j,k` distinct.  In every case there is
a total order `pi` in which all positions involved are positive: for the
triple take `i <_pi j <_pi k`; for two positions with a common first index
put it first, with a common second index put it last, and otherwise order
the four indices freely.  Hence each relator is an identity in `U_pi`,
which is a locally finite `p`-group by
`finite-positive-root-fragments-have-marked-regular-models`.

## Step 2: the colimit over the `U_pi` is `St_n(R)`

The argument of `steinberg-group-is-the-colimit-of-its-finite-root-subgroups`
applies verbatim with the diagram restricted to the `U_pi` and their
intersections: the colimit is generated by roots, every Steinberg relator
holds in it by Step 1, and the maps in both directions are identities on
roots.  So `(SPG2)` holds.

## Step 3: the complex of groups and its two-cells

Orderings `pi` and `s pi` differing by an adjacent transposition share all
roots but one opposite pair, so `U_pi cap U_(s pi)` is the subgroup
generated by the `n(n-1)/2-1` common positive roots.  The permutohedron is
the Cayley graph of `S_n` on adjacent transpositions; its two-cells are the
commuting squares `(s_i s_j)^2` (`|i-j|>=2`) and the braid hexagons
`(s_i s_(i+1))^3`, and it is simply connected with these cells (it is the
Coxeter presentation complex).  A compatible family of representations of
the vertex groups agreeing along edges defines a representation of the
colimit exactly when the compatibility gauges have trivial holonomy
around each two-cell, and the square holonomy is trivial because the two
transpositions move disjoint root pairs, so the four vertex groups share a
common subgroup containing both moved pairs' complements and the gauges
can be chosen in its commutant consistently.

## Step 4: corona representations

By `corona-representations-are-exact-on-locally-finite-subgroups` each
`U_pi^((r))` carries an exact lift at large coordinates, and two lifts of
the same corona representation agree up to `o(1)` on the finite
intersection.  If the hexagon holonomies can be gauged away, the glued
representation is an exact finite-dimensional representation of
`St_n(R)`, and the compression defect dies there.  Each hexagon is the
rank-two configuration of one `A_2` subsystem, i.e. the six orderings of
three indices with vertex groups the corresponding Heisenberg-type
unipotent groups over `R`.
