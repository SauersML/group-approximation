Artifact §1, Lemma 1.

Enumerate the words `w_1, w_2, …` on `S ∪ S^{-1}` in length-lex order. For each `i`, decide `w_i = e` with the word
problem; when `w_i ≠ e`, run the effectiveness algorithm on `w_i`. It halts and returns a finite group `F_i` and the
tuple `φ_i(S) ∈ F_i^S` of a homomorphism `φ_i : Γ → F_i` with `φ_i(w_i) ≠ 1`. Put

```text
K_m = ∩ { ker φ_i : i ≤ m, w_i ≠ e }.
```

- **Finite index and normal.** A finite intersection of kernels of homomorphisms to finite groups.
- **Descending, computable.** `K_{m+1} ⊆ K_m`, and the finitely many tuples `φ_i(S)` are computed as above.
- **Trivial intersection.** If `w ≠ e`, then `w = w_i` for some `i`, and `φ_i(w) ≠ 1`, so `w ∉ K_i`.
- **Decidable membership.** Evaluate `φ_i(w)` by multiplying the images of the letters of `w` in `F_i`; then
  `w ∈ K_m` iff `φ_i(w) = 1` for the finitely many `i ≤ m` with `w_i ≠ e`.
- **Computable quotients.** `Γ/K_m` is the subgroup of `∏_{i≤m} F_i` generated by the images of `S`, computed by
  closing a finite generating tuple inside a finite group.

**Converse.** Given such a chain: if `w ≠ e` then `w ∉ K_m` for some `m`, found by search, and the quotient map
`Γ → Γ/K_m` is the separating homomorphism, so `Γ` is effectively residually finite. If moreover `Γ` is recursively
presented, then `w = e` is semi-decidable from the presentation and `w ≠ e` is semi-decidable by that search, so the
word problem is solvable. ∎
