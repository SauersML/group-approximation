Write `F=F(x_1,...,x_m)`, let `N` be the normal closure of the finite relator
set `{r_1,...,r_k}`, and let `p:F->Q=F/N` be the quotient.  The fiber product

```text
P_Q={(u,v):p(u)=p(v)}
```

is generated by `(x_i,x_i)` and `(r_j,1)`.  Indeed `(v,v)` is generated by
the diagonal pairs.  If `p(u)=p(v)`, then `uv^(-1)` is a product of conjugates
of the `r_j` and their inverses; conjugating `(r_j,1)` by diagonal words
generates `(uv^(-1),1)`, and

```text
(u,v)=(uv^(-1),1)(v,v).
```

This also gives `P_Q cap ({1}xF)={1}xN`.

Because residual finiteness implies soficity, the nonsofic group `Q` is not
residually finite.  Choose `q!=1` in its finite residual and a lift `w in F`.
Then `(w,1)` is outside `P_Q`.  It cannot be separated from `P_Q` in a finite
quotient of `F x F`.  One clean proof is the diagonal criterion: the diagonal
`Delta Q` is separable in `Q x Q` if and only if `Q` is residually finite,
because `(q,1)` can be separated from `Delta Q` exactly when some finite
quotient of `Q` detects `q`.  Since `P_Q` is the inverse image of `Delta Q`,
the same finite-residual element witnesses nonseparability of `P_Q`.  Here is
the small pullback detail.  If a finite-index subgroup `K<F x F` contained
`P_Q` but excluded `(w,1)`, its normal core `C` would still have finite index.
The normal subgroup `N x N` lies in `P_Q`, hence in every conjugate of `K`,
so `N x N <= C`.  The finite quotient by `C` would therefore factor through
`Q x Q` and separate `(q,1)` from `Delta Q`, contradicting the choice of `q`.

For completeness, this remains true after embedding `F x F` in the ambient
actor.  If a finite-index subgroup of `Gamma_0 x SL_3(Z)` contained `P_Q`
but excluded `(w,1)`, its intersection with `F x F` would give the forbidden
finite-index separator there.

The required free embeddings are effective.  The source actor is an explicit
nonamenable virtually-free group: amenability would make every tracial GNS
algebra generated by it amenable/Connes embeddable, contradicting the fixed
non-CE source trace.  Bass--Serre normal forms therefore compute a rank-two
free subgroup, and Nielsen--Schreier conjugate bases give an embedded `F_m`.
In `SL_3(Z)`, embed the classical free subgroup of `SL_2(Z)` generated by

```text
U=[[1,2],[0,1]],       V=[[1,0],[2,1]]
```

in the upper-left block; the conjugates `U^i V U^(-i)`, `0<=i<m`, freely
generate an explicit `F_m`.

A transitive coset action is residually finite exactly when its stabilizer is
separable, so the action in `(MFP4)` is not residually finite.  Finally `W`
has the actor generators and one base lamp as generators.  A recursive
presentation consists of the actor relators, `a^2=1`, the stabilizer
relations `a^p=a` for the finite generating set of `P`, and the recursively
enumerable commuting relations `[a,a^g]=1`; hence the effectiveness claim.

The tempting extra inference from nonsoficity of `Q` is invalid: soficity of
an action by a free cover need not descend through its kernel to soficity of
the quotient group.  More concretely, Gao--Kunnawalkam Elayavalli--Patchell,
Theorem 2.19 of
[*Soficity for group actions on sets and applications*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04945),
prove that every action of a free group on a countable set is sofic.  Hence
the two coordinate `F_m` actions on `(Q x Q)/Delta Q` are sofic even though
`Q` is nonsofic.  Their combined action is the one detected by the
nonseparable fiber product, and the argument proves only that this combined
action is not residually finite.  The proof stops at exactly that
commuting-actions obstruction.
