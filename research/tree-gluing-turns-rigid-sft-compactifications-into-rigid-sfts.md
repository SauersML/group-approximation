## Definitions

Quantum families and `D`-quantum rigidity over a field `k` are as in
`fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity` (Q1–Q3; Q3 kills every pattern on a
`D`-ball that does not occur in the subshift). The action is `(g.x)(h) = x(g^(-1) h)`.

**SFT compactification.** Let `Λ_0` be finitely generated. An *SFT compactification* of `Λ_0` is an
SFT `Y ⊆ A^(Λ_0)` together with a pattern `π` on a ball `B_(R_0)`, the *seed*, such that:
- (C1) `{y ∈ Y : y|_(B_(R_0)) = π} = {y_*}` is a single point, and `Stab(y_*) = 1`;
- (C2) `Y` is the orbit closure of `y_*`.

Say `y` *has a seed at* `s` if `y(sf) = π(f)` for all `f ∈ B_(R_0)`. By (C1) this happens iff
`y = s.y_*`. So every point has at most one seed: two seeds at `s ≠ s'` would give `s.y_* = s'.y_*`.
The orbit `Λ_0.y_*` is open, discrete and dense, and `∂Y = Y ∖ Λ_0.y_*` is the set of seedless points.
Write `𝒞` for the class of groups with an SFT compactification that is `D`-quantum rigid over every
field for all large `D`.

**Basic members of 𝒞.**
- **Finite groups.** Take `Y = {y : y(hs) = y(h)s for all s ∈ S_0}` over the alphabet `Λ_0`, with seed
  `y(1) = 1`. Then `Y` is a single orbit, and it is rigid once `2D` exceeds the diameter.
- **`Z = <t>`.** `C_Z ⊆ {+, *, −}^Z`, with allowed adjacent pairs `(+,+), (+,*), (*,−), (−,−)` and seed
  `*`. Its points are `…++*−−…`, `+^Z` and `−^Z`. `C_Z` is `1`-quantum rigid over every field. Put
  `M_n = E_−(n)`. The forbidden pairs give `M_n ≤ M_(n+1)`, `E_*(n) ≤ M_(n+1)` and
  `M_(n+1) E_+(n) = 0`, hence `M_(n+1) = M_n + E_*(n)`. So `E_−(n) = M_n`, `E_*(n) = M_(n+1) − M_n` and
  `E_+(n) = 1 − M_(n+1)` all lie in the algebra generated by the chain `(M_n)`, which is commutative.
- **Direct products.** Take `Y_0 ⊠ Y_1` over `Λ_0 × Λ_1` with seed `π_0 × π_1`. It is rigid by
  `quantum-rigidity-is-product-stable`, and (C1), (C2) hold coordinatewise.
- **Free products.** See part (4) of the theorem.

## Theorem

Let `Λ_0, Λ_1 ∈ 𝒞` via `(Y_0, π_0)`, `(Y_1, π_1)`. Assume both are nontrivial and not both of order 2.
Let `Λ = Λ_0 * Λ_1` with generating set `S_0 ⊔ S_1`. There is an SFT `X` over `Λ` such that:
1. `Λ ↷ X` is minimal;
2. `Λ ↷ X` is topologically free;
3. `X` is `D`-quantum rigid over every field over which `Y_0` and `Y_1` are, for all large `D`;
4. a variant `X^+ ⊇ X` is an SFT compactification of `Λ` that is rigid in the same range. So `𝒞` is
   closed under free products.

`Λ` is finitely presented iff `Λ_0` and `Λ_1` are. In particular `Λ_0 ∈ 𝒞` gives `Λ_0 * Z` (with
`Y_1 = C_Z`), which carries a minimal, topologically free, rigid SFT.

## Proof

**Pieces and tree.**
- A *piece of kind i* is a left coset `λΛ_i`. Every element lies in exactly one piece of each kind, and
  every Cayley edge lies in one piece.
- Pieces are isometrically embedded: by normal forms, `d_Λ(λh, λh') = d_(Λ_i)(h, h')`.
- `T` is the bipartite graph on pieces ⊔ elements, joining `λ` to its two pieces. It is a tree, the
  Bass–Serre tree subdivided, and `Λ` acts freely on its element nodes.

**The SFT.** Take `x = (x_0, x_1)` with `x_i(λ) ∈ A_i`, subject to three rules:
- (R1) for every `λ` and `i`, `h ↦ x_i(λh)` (`h ∈ Λ_i`) lies in `Y_i`;
- (R3) at every `λ`, exactly one of the two pieces of `λ` is seeded at `λ`;
- (R3+) the same with "at least one".

These rules are local (radius `max(r_i, R_0)`). `X` is defined by R1 and R3, and `X^+` by R1 and R3+.

**Step A (structure).**
- **Orientation.** Orient `T` so that `P -> λ` if piece `P` is seeded at `λ`, and `λ -> P` otherwise.
  - Element nodes have out-degree 1 under R3, and 0 or 1 under R3+ (0 means both pieces are seeded).
  - Piece nodes have out-degree ≤ 1, since each piece has at most one seed.
- **Consequence.** In a tree where every out-degree is ≤ 1 there is at most one sink. Along the path
  between two sinks the arrows are forced toward the first sink, which contradicts the second. Likewise
  there is no bi-infinite path whose arrows point toward both of its ends.
- So every `x` has exactly one of the following:
  - a sink piece `P`, seedless, with `x|_P ∈ ∂Y_i`;
  - a sink element (only in `X^+`);
  - an end of `T`, to which all out-paths converge.
- **Determination.** Every non-sink piece is seeded at its *exit*, the element through which its
  out-path leaves it. So its configuration is `e.y_*` for that exit `e`.
- **Existence.** Every datum (end; sink piece with a seedless configuration; for `X^+`, sink element)
  occurs. Seed each non-sink piece at its element nearest the sink or end. R3 holds at `λ`: exactly one
  of its pieces lies on `λ`'s side away from the sink.

**Step B (minimality of X).** Fix `η ∈ X` and `R`.
- **Structure inside `B_R`.** Let `T_R` be the finite subtree of pieces meeting `B_R`. The element nodes
  on tree paths between pieces of `T_R` lie in `B_R`, since the ball is connected and elements are cut
  points. So the flow of `η` leaves `T_R` from one piece `P_last`, through an exit `e ∉ B_R`, or
  `P_last` is the sink.
- **What determines `η|_(B_R)`.** Every other piece of `T_R` exits toward `P_last` along `T_R`. So
  `η|_(B_R)` is determined by `P_last` and by `η|_(P_last ∩ B_R)`.
- **Choosing a far seed.** Pick `e' ∈ P_last ∖ B_R` with `(e'.y_*)|_(P_last ∩ B_R) = η|_(P_last ∩ B_R)`:
  - if `P_last` is not the sink, take `e' = e`;
  - if `P_last` is the sink, its configuration lies in `∂Y_i`, which is in the orbit closure (C2) and
    seedless, so far seeds approximate it.
- **Translating an arbitrary point.** Given any `ξ ∈ X`, choose a non-sink piece `Q` of kind `i` with
  exit `e_Q`. All but at most one piece is a non-sink. Put `g = e' e_Q^(-1)`. Then `gQ = P_last`, the
  exit of `P_last` in `g.ξ` is `e'`, and every other piece of `T_R` exits toward `P_last`. Hence
  `(g.ξ)|_(B_R) = η|_(B_R)`.

**Step C (topological freeness of X).** A nonempty open set contains a cylinder around some `η` as in
Step B.
- That cylinder contains every `x` whose `P_last` has exit `e'`, in particular every end of `T` beyond
  `e'`.
- Beyond `e'` the tree branches. Pieces have at least 2 elements, some kind has at least 3 or is
  infinite, and the kinds alternate. So there are three such ends whose median is a piece node `P'`,
  with rays leaving `P'` through distinct elements `e_1, e_2, e_3`.
- If `g` fixed all three ends, it would fix `P'` and each ray, hence `e_1`, so `g = 1`.
- So no `g ≠ 1` fixes an open set.

**Step D (rigidity).** Let `D ≥ max(r_i, R_0, 1)` be a scale at which `Y_0` and `Y_1` are rigid, and let
`E_c(q)` be a `D`-quantum family for `X` (or `X^+`) on `W_0`.
- **Piece letters.** Put `E^i_a(q) = Σ_(c : c_i = a) E_c(q)`. Then `E_c(q) = E^0_(c_0)(q) E^1_(c_1)(q)`.
  So it suffices that any two piece letters commute.
- **Same piece.** On a piece `λΛ_i`, the family `h ↦ E^i_a(λh)` is a `D`-quantum family for `Y_i`:
  - Q1 is inherited;
  - Q2 holds because pieces are isometric;
  - Q3 holds because letters in a `D`-ball commute. Expanding a non-occurring `Y_i`-pattern into
    `X`-patterns on the `Λ`-ball gives only non-occurring patterns, by R1.

  So it commutes, and piece letters on one piece commute.

*Lemma 1.* In a commuting family for `Y_i`, let `S_e = ∏_(f ∈ B_(R_0)) E_(π(f))(ef)` be the seed idempotent.
Then:
- `S_e ≤ E_(y_*(e^(-1)u))(u)` for every `u`;
- `S_e S_(e') = 0` for `e ≠ e'`.

*Proof.* `[seed at e] ∩ [y(u) = b] = ∅` for `b ≠ y_*(e^(-1)u)`. By König's lemma some ball `B_n(e)`
has no pattern that has a seed at `e`, has `b` at `u`, and whose `D`-subpatterns all occur. Expand
`S_e E_b(u)` over patterns on `B_n(e)`: each term contains a non-occurring `D`-pattern, so it vanishes by
Q3. The second statement is the same argument with a second seed. `∎`

- **Different pieces.** Let `f` be a letter at `q` of piece `P` and `f'` a letter at `q'` of piece
  `P' ≠ P`. Take the tree path `P = P_0, e_1, P_1, …, e_m, P_m = P'`, with `m ≥ 1` and
  `e_i ≠ e_(i+1)`.
  - **Junction events.** Let `A_i` be the seed idempotent of `P_(i−1)` at `e_i`, and `B_i` that of
    `P_i` at `e_i`. They commute, because their letters lie within `R_0` of `e_i`. R3+ and Q3 give
    `(1 − A_i)(1 − B_i) = 0`, i.e. `1 − A_i ≤ B_i`. (Under R3 also `A_i B_i = 0`.)
  - **The chain.** Lemma 1 in `P_i` gives `B_i A_(i+1) = A_(i+1) B_i = 0`, so `B_i ≤ 1 − A_(i+1)`.
    Transitivity of `≤` for idempotents needs no further commutation. So
    `1 − A_1 ≤ B_1 ≤ 1 − A_2 ≤ B_2 ≤ … ≤ 1 − A_m ≤ B_m`
    is a chain, hence commutative.
  - **Partition.** Its successive differences, together with `1 − A_1` first and `1 − B_m` last, are
    orthogonal idempotents `Π_0, …, Π_(2m)` summing to `1`.
  - **Where each `Π_j` sits.**
    - Every `Π_j` except `Π_0` is `≤ A_1`, and every `Π_j` except `Π_(2m)` is `≤ B_m`.
    - `Π_0 = 1 − A_1` commutes with `f`, since both are letters of `P_0`.
    - `Π_(2m) = 1 − B_m` commutes with `f'`.
  - **Scalars from Lemma 1.** In `P_0`, `A_1 ≤ E_(c)(q)` with `c = y_*(e_1^(-1)q)`, so
    `f A_1 = A_1 f = δ A_1` with `δ ∈ {0, 1}`. Symmetrically `f' B_m = B_m f' = δ' B_m`. Hence:
    - `f Π_j = Π_j f = δ Π_j` for `j ≥ 1`;
    - `f' Π_j = Π_j f' = δ' Π_j` for `j ≤ 2m − 1`.
  - **Conclusion.** Each `j` has `f` or `f'` scalar on `Π_j`, and the other commutes with it. So
    `f Π_j f' = f' Π_j f` for every `j`, and summing over `j` gives `f f' = f' f`.

**Step E (X^+ is a compactification).**
- Interior points are those whose sink is an element. The cylinder "both pieces seeded at 1" is one
  point, with trivial stabilizer.
- Its orbit is dense: the proof of Step B works verbatim, with `ξ` interior.
- Rigidity is Step D, which used only `1 − A_i ≤ B_i`. `∎`

## Consequences for the master route

- **Minimality and topological freeness are free.** They come from the locally infinite tree. The
  compactification `Y` needs neither: it has an isolated orbit.
- **The input sits in a vertex group.** A decidable input `G ≤ Λ_0` fixes the piece node `Λ_0` and
  permutes its elements freely. This is the only way a Kazhdan simple input can meet a tree, since it
  acts trivially on locally finite trees (`kazhdan-simple-groups-act-only-on-exotic-2d-buildings`).
  All of its geometry must then be coded by `Y_0`.
- **Isotropy.** Finite subgroups fix no end. The ring side needs torsion-free isotropy, which here
  holds iff finite subgroups of `Λ_0` act freely on `∂Y_0` (with `Λ_1 = Z`).
- **Decidability.** `Λ_0 * Z` carries a rigid minimal topologically free SFT. For finitely presented
  `Λ_0`, `rigid-topologically-free-sfts-force-solvable-word-problem` then forces WP(`Λ_0`) solvable, as
  it must.

## Lesson for general BH

**Minimality and topological freeness are free; glue along a locally infinite tree.**
- Free products turn any rigid SFT *compactification* into a rigid, minimal, topologically free SFT. The
  compactification has one isolated seed orbit and no dynamics of its own.
- Rigidity crosses the tree by the monotone chain `1 − A_i ≤ B_i ≤ 1 − A_(i+1)`: a piece seeded
  backward forces the next one backward. This is the rank-one compression mechanism with arbitrary
  vertex groups.
- So the "minimality gap" of gate E1 disappears. Gates E1′ + E2 reduce to one deterministic object: a
  rigid, locally checkable coordinate system with a unique origin on a finitely presented overgroup of
  the input (`decidable-groups-embed-in-rigid-sft-compactification-groups`).
