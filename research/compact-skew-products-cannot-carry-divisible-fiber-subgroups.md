## Setting

Let `Φ` be a discrete abelian group acting freely on a set `F`, written `θ ↦ θ + φ`. A group
`E` acts on `Y × F` **by skew products** if

~~~text
g·(y, θ) = (ḡ y, θ + κ(g, y)),        κ(gh, y) = κ(g, h̄y) + κ(h, y),        (SK)
~~~

for some action `g ↦ ḡ` of `E` on `Y` and some cocycle `κ: E × Y → Φ`. The **fiberwise
subgroup** is `N = ker(E → Sym Y)`. Its elements act by `θ ↦ θ + κ(n, y)`.

## Theorem

Let `E` be generated by a finite set `S`, acting faithfully on `Y × F` by skew products.
Suppose each `κ(s, ·)`, `s ∈ S`, takes finitely many values. Let `Φ_0 ≤ Φ` be the subgroup
generated by these values. Then:

1. `κ(g, ·)` takes values in `Φ_0` for every `g ∈ E`.
2. `n ↦ κ(n, ·)` is an injective homomorphism `N → Map(Y, Φ_0)`. So `N` is abelian.
3. `N` contains no subgroup isomorphic to `Z[1/p]` or to the quasi-cyclic group `C_(p^∞)`,
   for any prime `p`.
4. **Topological form.** Suppose `Y` is compact and `E` acts on `Y × F` by homeomorphisms,
   where `F` is either `Φ` with the discrete topology, or the Cantor circle `F_S` with
   `Φ = Z[1/p]/Z` acting by rotation. (Here `F_S = {0,…,p−1}^N`, read as the most significant
   digits of a point of `R/Z`, and rotation adds a `p`-adic fraction with carries toward the
   first digit, discarding the carry out.) Then every `κ(g, ·)` is locally constant, so the
   hypothesis holds and items 1–3 follow.

## Proof

1. By (SK), `κ(gh, ·)` is a sum of translates of `κ(g, ·)` and `κ(h, ·)`, and
   `κ(g^-1, y) = −κ(g, ḡ^-1 y)`. Induction on word length gives the claim.
2. For `n, m ∈ N`, (SK) gives `κ(nm, y) = κ(n, y) + κ(m, y)`, since `n̄ = 1`. The action on
   fibers is free, so `κ(n, ·) = 0` forces `n` to act trivially, and then `n = 1` by
   faithfulness.
3. The p-divisible elements of `Φ_0` are `D = ∩_k p^k Φ_0`. `Φ_0` is finitely generated, so
   `D` is its prime-to-`p` torsion, a finite group of exponent `e` prime to `p`.
   - Suppose `C_(p^∞) ≅ Z ≤ N` or `Z[1/p] ≅ Z ≤ N`, with `Z` nontrivial.
   - Every element of `Z` is divisible by every `p^k` inside `Z`. Hence every element of
     its image in `Map(Y, Φ_0)` takes values in `D`. So the image has exponent dividing `e`.
   - Then `Z` would be a nontrivial group of exponent `e` prime to `p`. This is impossible:
     `C_(p^∞)` is a `p`-group, and `Z[1/p]` is torsion-free.
4. **Discrete `F`.** Continuity at `(y_0, θ)` forces `κ(g, y) = κ(g, y_0)` near `y_0`.
   - **`F = F_S`.** Every nonzero rotation `a` moves some point of the first-digit cylinder
     `[p−1]` into the cylinder `[0]`. Take the representative `a_r ∈ (0, 1)` of `a` and a
     `p`-adic rational `θ` slightly above `max(1 − a_r, (p−1)/p)`. Then `θ + a_r − 1` is
     small and positive.
   - `F_S` is compact, so continuity of `g` gives a neighbourhood `U` of `y_0` on which
     `θ ↦ θ + κ(g, y) − κ(g, y_0)` moves no point from `[p−1]` to `[0]`. Hence
     `κ(g, ·)` is constant on `U`.
   - `Y` is compact, so a locally constant function takes finitely many values. ∎

## Corollaries

Let `A = Z[1/p]/Z ≅ C_(p^∞)`.
- **Hall's group.** Let `B = ⟨e_12(1), e_23(1), diag(1,p,1)⟩ ≤ GL_3(Z[1/p])` and
  `C = ⟨e_13(1)⟩`. `B/C` is finitely generated, and its center contains `A = e_13(Z[1/p])/C`.
  So `B/C` has no faithful skew-product action over any compact base in which the center acts
  fiberwise, with discrete fibers or with the Cantor circle `F_S`.
- **Abels' quotient.** The same holds for `A_p/⟨e_14(1)⟩`, which contains `B/C`.
- **Every finitely generated group with a central `C_(p^∞)` or `Z[1/p]`.** The same holds.
  For `Z[1/p]` this includes Hall's group `B` itself, with its center `e_13(Z[1/p])`.
- **Attempt 2 of `fg-soluble-minimax-groups-satisfy-boone-higman` cannot be completed.** Its
  "one-digit-pair-per-weight defect" of Hall's `t` is not a gauge accident. The whole
  finitely generated group is excluded, on every compact base.

## The two designs that do work (lane computations)

1. **Unbounded cocycles over a locally compact base.** Let `B/C` act on `Q_p × F_S` by
   ~~~text
   a(α)(y,θ) = (y + α, θ),   b(β)(y,θ) = (y, θ + (βy mod Z_p)),
   c(γ)(y,θ) = (y, θ + γ),   t(y,θ) = (y/p, θ).
   ~~~
   Here `Q_p/Z_p ≅ A`.
   - This is a faithful action by locally constant skew products.
   - `κ(b(1), y) = (y mod Z_p)` is unbounded as `|y|_p → ∞`, so the center lives at infinity.
   - Equivalently: `Z[1/p] = Q/Q_0` with `Q_0 = ⟨b(β), t⟩` is the induced space, `Q_p` is its
     completion, and the class dies on `Q_0` (Shapiro's lemma).
2. **Connected fibers over a compact base.** Let `S_p = (R × Q_p)/Z[1/p]` be the `p`-adic
   solenoid, whose Pontryagin dual is `Z[1/p]`, with characters `χ_β`. Then `B/C` acts
   faithfully on `S_p × R/Z` by continuous affine maps of this compact abelian group:
   ~~~text
   a(α): (s,θ) ↦ (s + α, θ),   b(β): (s,θ) ↦ (s, θ + χ_β(s)),
   c(γ): (s,θ) ↦ (s, θ + γ),   t: (s,θ) ↦ (s/p, θ).
   ~~~
   - The relations check directly: `χ_β(s + α) − χ_β(s) = βα mod 1`, and
     `χ_β(p s) = χ_(pβ)(s)`.
   - The theorem does not apply: the fiber group `R/Z` is not discrete, and `S_p` is
     connected, so the cocycles are continuous but not locally constant.
   - The shear `b(β)` couples the flow direction of `S_p` with the fiber circle, so it is
     not of Brin–Thompson product type.

## Lesson for general BH

A finitely generated group sees only a finitely generated coefficient group in any compact
bundle with locally constant cocycles. So a divisible central (or fiberwise) subgroup cannot
sit in the fiber of a Cantor-type host. The obstruction is finite generation alone: it needs
no stabilizer condition, and it holds even when the base action is free.

Hosts for non-residually-finite inputs with divisible centers must do one of three things:
- move the center on the base;
- carry it at infinity, on a non-compact base with ends;
- carry it in a connected coordinate, as a circle cover, where a shear couples the base
  with the fiber (compare Thompson's `T` and its central extensions on covering circles).

In the compact totally disconnected hosts on main (self-similar affine groups, RN groups,
`H_P` and `2V_τ`), a skew-product design must take the first option. No construction of that
kind is known for Hall and Abels-type groups, and that is the design gap. The theorem only
constrains skew-product embeddings; it does not rule out other embeddings of these groups in
Cantor hosts.
