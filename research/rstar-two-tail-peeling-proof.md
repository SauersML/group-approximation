Fix a deletion order `c_1,...,c_r` for all contexts outside the terminal
kernel, where `c_i` has at least two unpinned degree-one variables in the
subinstance on `K_2,c_i,...,c_r`.  Process the contexts in reverse order.

When `c_i` is processed, every variable already fixed belongs either to the
kernel or to some `c_j` with `j>i`.  A variable of `c_i` which had degree one
at its deletion occurs in none of those contexts.  Therefore at most two
coordinates of `c_i` are already fixed.  Retain them and apply
`rstar-two-retained-coordinates-admit-a-fixed-cap-section` to define all
remaining coordinates as functions of their joint spectral projections.
This preserves the already processed rows and makes `c_i` cap-supported.
Variables so defined may occur in later reconstruction steps, but they are
then among the at most two retained coordinates and are never redefined.

Induction constructs the desired representation.  Every new observable lies
in the algebra generated by retained observables, so all modifications stay
inside the original CE algebra.  The same argument starting with scalar
values when the unpinned kernel is empty gives a scalar assignment to every
variable.

The terminal kernel is independent of deletion order.  Eligibility is
monotone: deleting other contexts can only lower active variable degrees, so
an eligible context remains eligible.  Equivalently, simultaneous deletion
rounds compute the greatest terminal subinstance in which every context has
at most one unpinned degree-one variable.
