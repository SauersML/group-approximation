Source: David A. Craven and Peter Pappas, *On the Unit Conjecture for
Supersoluble Group Rings, I*, arXiv:1010.1144v1 (6 October 2010), Theorems
2.1 and 3.1, published in J. Algebra 394 (2013) 310-356. The theorem numbers
here are those of arXiv v1, read from the PDF.

**The cited statement.** Section 2 fixes a group `G` and a normal subgroup
`N` such that `G/N` is infinite dihedral, generated by involutions `Nx` and
`Ny`. Put `X = <N, x>` and `Y = <N, y>`. The alternating words `W` in `x` and
`y` are a transversal for `N` in `G`. For `g in Nw` with `w in W`, `L(g)` is
the number of letters of `w`, and `L(alpha)` is the maximum of `L` over
`supp(alpha)`. Theorem 2.1 assumes that `KG` has no proper divisors of zero
and that `KN` is an Ore domain. Theorem 3.1 is proved in the same setting: if
`sigma, tau in KG \ {0}` and `sigma tau in KN`, then `L(sigma) = L(tau)`.
Its proof uses Steps 1 to 3 of the proof of Theorem 2.1. The first two steps
are those of Passman, *The Algebraic Structure of Group Rings*, Theorem
13.3.7, and the paper does not reproduce them.

**The hypotheses for `P`, `N_i` and the pair `r_m, r_(m+1)`.**

1. `pi_i` is a surjective homomorphism. The `i`-th coordinates of
   `(s, t)(s', t')` are `(s_i s'_i, t_i + s_i t'_i)`, which is the law of
   `Z x| Z/2` on `(t_i, [s_i = -1])`. The images of `a` and `b` are
   `(1, 0), (0, 1)` for `i = 0`, `(1, 1), (1, 0)` for `i = 1`, and
   `(0, 1), (1, 1)` for `i = 2`. Each pair generates `D_infty`.
2. `K[N_i]` is an Ore domain. In `P` the parity of `t` is fixed by `s`:
   `000` for `(1,1,1)`, `110` for `(1,-1,-1)`, `011` for `(-1,1,-1)` and
   `101` for `(-1,-1,1)`. The one non-identity `s` with `s_i = 1` forces
   `t_i` to be odd. So `N_i = {(1, t) : t in 2Z^3, t_i = 0}`, which is
   isomorphic to `Z^2`, and `K[N_i]` is a commutative domain.
3. `K[P]` has no proper divisors of zero, for every field `K`. `P` is
   torsion-free, and the elements `(1, t)` with `t in 2Z^3` form a normal
   subgroup isomorphic to `Z^3` of index 4. So `P` is polycyclic-by-finite,
   and the zero-divisor theorem for torsion-free polycyclic-by-finite groups
   applies. Craven and Pappas use this for `P` in the proof of their
   Theorem 4.3.
4. `r_m r_(m+1) = (-1, 0)` generates the translations. So `r_m` and
   `r_(m+1)` generate `D_infty`, which is their free product, and they lift
   to elements `x, y` of `P`. The alternating words are
   `(r_m r_(m+1))^k = (-k, 0)` and `(r_(m+1) r_m)^k = (k, 0)`, of length
   `2k`, and `r_m (r_(m+1) r_m)^k = (m - k, 1)` and
   `r_(m+1) (r_m r_(m+1))^k = (m + 1 + k, 1)`, of length `2k + 1`. So `L`
   for this decomposition is `l_i^m`.

Theorem 3.1 then gives `L_i^m(sigma) = L_i^m(tau)`. For a unit `u`,
`u u^(-1) = 1` lies in `K[N_i]`. If `supp(u)` lies in `S`, every
`g in supp(u^(-1))` has
`l_i^m(g) <= L_i^m(u^(-1)) = L_i^m(u) <= max_(h in S) l_i^m(h)`. QED
