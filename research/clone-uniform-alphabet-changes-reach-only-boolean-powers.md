**ESTABLISHED** by `clone-uniform-alphabet-changes-boolean-power-proof`.

Let `A` be a finite set with `k = |A| >= 2` and `M` a nonempty finite set. Suppose each operation `p: A^n -> A`
in the finite list

    c_a (a in A),   s,   and,   not,   chi_a (a in A),   t

(defined in the proof) is assigned an operation `p^M: M^n -> M` of the same arity, such that the finitely many
identities (E1)–(E5) of the proof, all true in `A`, are also true in `M`. Then `|M| = k^r` for some `r >= 0`.

**Corollary (uniform alphabet changes).** Call an alphabet change *clone-uniform* if it assigns to every
finitary operation `p` on `A` an operation `p^M` on `M`, sends projections to projections and commutes with
substitution: `p(q_1, ..., q_n)^M = p^M(q_1^M, ..., q_n^M)`. That is a clone homomorphism
`Clo(A) -> Clo(M)`. It carries every identity of `A` to `M`, so `|M| = k^r`. Then `k^r` has the same prime
divisors as `k` (for `r >= 1`), and it is a power of two only if `k` is.

Such a change transports every strict pair: the local rules `f`, `g` of `tau`, `sigma` go to `f^M`, `g^M`, and
the identity `sigma o tau = id` is an identity in the clone, so `tau^M` is injective on `M^G`. What cannot
come out of it is a new size. So from a failing size that is not a power of two, **no clone-uniform alphabet
change reaches a power of two**, whether or not it keeps strictness.

**What it covers.** Every construction that is defined for all local rules at once and is functorial in them:
- regrouping `A^m` (sitewise powers), which is the case `r = m`;
- Boolean powers `A[B]` over a finite Boolean algebra `B`;
- quotients and subalgebras of powers (the finite members of the variety of `A`);
- any transport of polymorphisms that is built from coordinatewise evaluation.

This generalizes the attempt "Regrouping moves stay inside the radical" on
`some-nonsurjunctive-group-gives-a-binary-power-one` from regroupings to every uniform encoding. It is the
finite, elementary case of Hu's theorem on primal algebras: the finite algebras in the variety generated by a
primal algebra `A` are its finite powers. The proof below is self-contained and imports nothing.

**Where every member dies.** At the reconstruction identity (E4) `x = R(chi_{a_1}(x), ..., chi_{a_{k-1}}(x))`
together with the decoding identity (E5). In `M` they make `m -> (chi_a(m))_a` a bijection from `M` onto the
`k`-labelled partitions of unity of the finite Boolean algebra `{m : s(m) = m}`. There are `k^r` of these,
where `r` is the number of atoms.

**What escapes, and so what a proof of the bridge must be.**
- **Lax transports.** A construction that respects composition only up to inclusion is not covered. The
  power-set lift is the natural example. It is treated in `power-set-lifts-never-give-binary-power-witnesses`,
  and it dies there for a different reason.
- **Rule-specific transports.** One can transport only the two rules `f`, `g` of one strict pair. Take a finite
  set `M` with operations `f^M`, `g^M` satisfying the single decoder identity of the pair (its table of
  compositions over `T S`), and a surjection `pi: M -> A` that is a homomorphism for `f` and `g`. Then `tau^M` is
  strict on `M^G`:
  - it is injective by the decoder identity;
  - it is not surjective, because `pi` acts sitewise and is onto, so `tau(A^G) = tau(pi(M^G)) = pi(tau^M(M^G))`.
    If `tau^M` were surjective, this would be all of `A^G`.

  With `M = A` this is trivially equivalent to the bridge. The claim shows that such an `M` of size `2^j`,
  starting from odd `k`, **cannot** carry operations satisfying (E1)–(E5): the structure has to be
  rule-specific and non-functorial.
