Write `K*G = ⊕_g K u_g` with `u_g f = σ_g(f) u_g` and `u_g u_h = α(g,h) u_(gh)`, where `α` takes values in `K^×`.

## 1. Rank condition passes to subrings and matrix rings

- **Subrings.** If `R' ⊆ R` is a unital subring and `AB = I_d` over `R'` with `A` of size `d x r`, the same holds in
  `R`.
- **Matrix rings.** Over `M_n(R)`, a `d x r` matrix is an `nd x nr` matrix over `R`, and `AB = I_d` becomes
  `AB = I_(nd)`. The rank condition for `R` gives `nr >= nd`, so `r >= d`.

## 2. Finite-index lift (A)

1. **Free module.** Let `N ≤ H` with left transversal `t_1..t_n`, so `H = ⊔ t_i N`. `R_N = K*N` is the span of the
   `K u_x`, `x ∈ N`, and is closed under multiplication, so it is a unital subring of `R_H = K*H`.
   - **Generation.** For `x ∈ N` and `f ∈ K`: `u_(t_i) (f u_x) = σ_(t_i)(f) α(t_i,x) u_(t_i x)`. As `f` ranges over `K`,
     these span `K u_(t_i x)`, since `α(t_i, x)` is a unit. So `u_(t_i) R_N = ⊕_(x ∈ N) K u_(t_i x)`.
   - **Freeness.** `R_H = ⊕_i u_(t_i) R_N`, and `u_(t_i) r = 0` forces `r = 0` (left multiplication by `u_(t_i)` is
     invertible with inverse `α(t_i^(-1), t_i)^(-1) u_(t_i^(-1))`). So `R_H` is a free right `R_N`-module with basis
     `u_(t_1)..u_(t_n)`.
2. **Embedding.** Left multiplication by `r ∈ R_H` is right `R_N`-linear, giving a unital ring homomorphism
   `R_H -> End_(R_N)(R_N^n) = M_n(R_N)`. It is injective because `r·1 = r`.
3. **Conclusion.** Section 1 gives (A).

## 3. Virtually bi-orderable groups (B)

1. **Finite data.** Let `AB = I_d` over `K*G`. Let `H` be the subgroup generated by the supports of all entries, so
   `AB = I_d` already holds in the sub-crossed product `K*H`.
2. **Division ring.** By hypothesis `H` has a finite-index subgroup `N` with a bi-invariant order. By the
   Malcev–Neumann construction for crossed products (D. S. Passman, *Infinite Crossed Products*, Academic Press 1989,
   Chapter 13), the formal sums `sum_x f_x u_x` over `N` with well-ordered support form a division ring `D ⊇ K*N`
   under the crossed-product multiplication.
3. **Conclusion.** `D` has the rank condition by vector-space dimension, so `K*N` has it by Section 1, and so does
   `K*H` by (A). Hence `r >= d`.

## 4. Examples and non-examples (imported facts)

- **Bi-orderable groups.**
  - Free groups and RAAGs (Duchamp–Krob; RAAGs are residually torsion-free nilpotent).
  - Pure braid groups (Kim–Rolfsen 2003).
  - Torsion-free nilpotent groups.
- **Virtually special groups.**
  - Special groups embed in RAAGs (Haglund–Wise 2008), and subgroups of bi-orderable groups are bi-orderable.
  - Cubulated hyperbolic groups are virtually special (Agol 2013).
- **Not virtually torsion-free.** If `N ≤ H` has finite index and is torsion-free, every finite subgroup of `H`
  injects into the finite set `H/N`. So a group with finite subgroups of unbounded order, such as the unit group of
  `L_(F_3)(1,2) ≅ M_(2^k)(L_(F_3)(1,2))` containing `GL_(2^k)(F_3)`, is not virtually bi-orderable.
- **Not left-orderable.** Finite-index subgroups of `SL_n(Z)` with `n >= 3` are not left-orderable (Witte 1994), so
  they are not virtually bi-orderable.

## 5. Calibration

- `LC(X,k) ⋊ F_2` with a paradoxical action fails the rank condition, and `F_2` is bi-orderable.
- Step 3.2 needs coefficients in a division ring. `LC(X,k)` has idempotents, and the series ring over it is not a
  division ring, so nothing is certified there.
