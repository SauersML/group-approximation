**ESTABLISHED** (lane bh-invent-02, 2026-09-18). This is a lane proof, elementary. It has not
been reviewed, and no priority is claimed.

**Terms.**
- A *relative host* `(P, X, A)` is: `P` finitely presented; `P ↷ X` with finitely generated
  point stabilizers; `A ≤ P` with `A ≅ Z^n` and finitely many orbits on `X`. The action need
  not be faithful.
- `Γ ≤ P` is *sharp* if `Γ ∩ ker(P ↷ X) = 1`.
- A *host* is a faithful relative host.

By `cofinite-abelian-subgroups-give-relative-pbh`, every sharp subgroup of a relative host
satisfies relative PBH. That proof never used faithfulness beyond sharpness.

## Statement

1. **The odometer host.** Let `B = V_(2,1)(⟨a⟩)` act on `Z`, viewed as the eventually constant
   binary sequences.
   - `B` is finitely presented (Nekrashevych; the odometer group is contracting and
     self-replicating).
   - The action is faithful, with `a` a single free orbit.
   - `Stab_B(0^∞)` is finitely generated.

   So `(B, Z, ⟨a⟩)` is a host, and `V ≤ B` and every subgroup of `B` lie in hosts. It follows
   that `FSym(Z) ⋊ B` is a host too, which settles the point left unchecked in
   `cofinite-abelian-subgroups-give-relative-pbh`.
2. **Amalgams and HNN along the port.** Let `D_1, D_2` be finitely presented, acting on one
   set `X` and containing a common `A ≅ Z^n` that has finitely many orbits there, with the
   same action. Let their stabilizers be finitely generated. Then `D_1 *_A D_2` is a relative
   host on `X`. The same holds for an HNN extension `D *_{φ}`, `φ: A → A′`, whenever the two
   `A`-sets are isomorphic through `φ` (realize the stable letter as a bijection of `X`).
3. **Port disjunction.** Let `(P, X, A)` be a relative host and `Γ` a sharp subgroup. Then
   there is a relative host `(P × Q, X ⊔ Y, A_Δ)` in which `Γ × 1` is sharp and meets `A_Δ`
   trivially.
4. **Free products.** If `Γ_i` is sharp in a relative host (`i = 1, 2`), then `Γ_1 * Γ_2`
   embeds in a relative host `P = D_1 *_A D_2 ↷ Z`, compatibly with the normal form. It is
   sharp there exactly when `Γ_1 * Γ_2` acts faithfully on `Z`.
5. **Port rigidity.** Let `A ≅ Z^n` act on `X` with orbit types `A/L_τ` of multiplicity
   `m_τ`. Then its centralizer in `Sym(X)` is `∏_τ (A/L_τ) ≀ Sym(m_τ)`, which is countable and
   virtually abelian. So the gluing in items 2–4 is fixed up to this small group, and no Baire
   generic choice is available to make the result faithful.

## Proof

**Item 1.**
- **Tables.** Elements of `B` are tables `u_i → v_i a^{k_i}`. These preserve eventual
  constancy, and `a` acts on `Z` as `n ↦ n+1`, so `B ↷ Z` is transitive. It is faithful
  because `Z` is dense.
- **Germs at `0^∞`.** An element fixing `0^∞` acts near it as `0^k w ↦ 0^m a^j(w)`, and
  `a^j(0^∞) = 0^∞` forces `j = 0`. So the germ group is `Z`, generated by the germ of
  `s = (0w ↦ 00w, 10w ↦ 01w, 11w ↦ 1w) ∈ V`.
- **The kernel of the germ map** is the union of the rigid stabilizers of `C ∖ 0^kC`.
  - `s^k` carries `C ∖ 0C = 1C` onto `C ∖ 0^{k+1}C`, so these rigid stabilizers are the
    conjugates `s^k Rist(1C) s^{-k}`.
  - The rigid stabilizer of the cone `1C` is isomorphic to `B`, through `w ↦ 1w`: tables
    `1u → 1v a^k` correspond to tables `u → v a^k`.
- **Conclusion.** `Stab_B(0^∞) = ⟨Rist(1C), s⟩` is finitely generated.
- **For `FSym(Z) ⋊ B`.** Its point stabilizer is generated by three finite sets:
  - generators of `B_0`;
  - one transposition for each of the finitely many `B_0`-orbits on 2-subsets of
    `Z ∖ {0}`, which are finite because `B` has finitely many orbits on `Z^4`
    (`fsym-by-odometer-group-on-the-integers-is-fp`);
  - the lifts `(0 b(0))·b` of generators of `B`.

**Item 2.** Apply the Bass–Serre count of `amalgams-of-orbit-finite-actions-are-type-a2`.
- Edges correspond to `A`-orbits, which are finitely many, and vertex groups are
  finitely generated stabilizers.
- So stabilizers are finitely generated, and finite presentation is clear.
- For HNN, use one vertex and one edge orbit type.

**Item 3.** Let `Q ⊇ A` be the Houghton block group of item 1 of
`cofinite-abelian-subgroups-give-relative-pbh`, acting on `Y ≅ X × [2^n]`, with embedding
`ι: A → Q`.
- Let `P × Q` act on the disjoint union `X ⊔ Y`, each factor on its own part.
- Put `A_Δ = {(a, ι(a))}`. It has finitely many orbits on `X ⊔ Y`, and point stabilizers are
  a stabilizer times the other factor.
- `(γ, 1) = (a, ι(a))` forces `ι(a) = 1`, hence `a = 1`. The kernel is
  `ker(P ↷ X) × ker(Q ↷ Y)`.

**Item 4.** Apply item 3 to both relative hosts, getting ports `A_1, A_2` on
`W_i = X_i ⊔ Y_i`.
- **The two factors.** Put `A = A_1 × A_2`. Let `D_1 = (P_1 × Q_1) × Q_2′`, where `Q_2′` is
  the Houghton block group realizing `W_2 × [2^{n_2}]`. Let `D_2 = Q_1′ × (P_2 × Q_2)`
  symmetrically.
- **Matching the sets.** As `A`-sets, both act on copies of `W_1 × W_2 × [2^{n_1+n_2}]`,
  after a trivial factor. Identify the copies `A`-equivariantly.
- **Normal form.** `Γ_i ∩ A = 1` in `D_i`. So alternating products of nontrivial elements of
  `Γ_1` and `Γ_2` are reduced in `D_1 *_A D_2`, and they generate `Γ_1 * Γ_2`.
- **Sharpness** means exactly that `Γ_1 * Γ_2` meets the free kernel trivially.

**Item 5.** An `A`-equivariant bijection maps orbits to isomorphic orbits, and
`Aut_A(A/L) = A/L`. Two gluings of the same factors differ by an element of this centralizer. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

Each of Higman's operations on hosts is cheap except for one clause, *faithfulness*:
- finite presentation, finitely generated stabilizers and a cofinite port all pass through
  amalgams, HNN extensions and free products (items 2–4);
- the gluing leaves a free Bass–Serre kernel, which is the same object as in FFWZ Question 5.9.

So the one-dimensional Higman problem and Q3.4 meet at a single gate: **faithful gluing along a
cofinite port.**

There is also no room to get faithfulness by genericity (item 5). The classical Sym(N) trick,
where generic conjugates generate free products, needs a large centralizer, and a cofinite
port leaves only a virtually abelian one.

Faithfulness must therefore come from the factors' own dynamics. It has to be ping-pong on a
set whose Schreier graphs have finitely many ends, i.e. Cantor-type rather than tree-type
dynamics, as in the odometer host `B`, which contains `V` and hence many free products. The
next test is to glue two copies of `B` along `⟨a⟩`, twisted by a Thompson-type element, and
decide faithfulness.
