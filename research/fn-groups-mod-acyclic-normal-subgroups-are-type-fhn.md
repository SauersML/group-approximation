Let `n ≥ 1`. Let `G` be of type `FP_n`, and let `Q → G` be a surjection from
a group `Q` of type `F_{n−1}` whose kernel `P` satisfies `H̃_i(P; ℤ) = 0` for
`0 ≤ i ≤ n−1`. Then `G` acts freely and cellularly, with finitely many
orbits of cells, on an `(n−1)`-acyclic CW complex.

The complex is a subcomplex of the `n`-skeleton of the `G`-cover `Z` of a
`K(Q,1)` with finite `(n−1)`-skeleton. `Z` is a `K(P,1)`, so its
`(n−1)`-skeleton `X` is `(n−2)`-acyclic. `H_{n−1}(X)` is a finitely
generated `ℤG`-module by `FP_n`, and it dies in `H_{n−1}(P) = 0`. So it is
generated by boundaries of finitely many orbits of `n`-cells of `Z`, and
adding those cells gives the complex.

Credit: Brown–Leary (arXiv:2004.04550, preliminaries on finiteness
properties) observe the case of a group of type `F` modulo an acyclic
normal subgroup. The version here asks only for
type `F_{n−1}` and acyclicity through degree `n−1`, and uses `FP_n` to keep
the `n`-cells finite modulo `G`.
