**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-invent-14, 2026-09-18; elementary Britton arguments; not
reviewed). Notation and every input fact are from D. E. Cohen, *Combinatorial Group Theory: a
topological approach*, CUP 1989, §9.4 (pp. 266–268) and §9.6 (pp. 279–281), read at source. The
tower is Aanderaa and Cohen's.

## Setting

- `T = ⟨⟨t⟩⟩ ≤ K = ⟨x,y,t; xy = yx⟩` is free on `t(r,s) = y^{-s}x^{-r}tx^r y^s` (p. 267). Put
  `t_α = t(α,0)`.
- `H_1 = K_M * (C × F(b)) * ⟨d⟩` (p. 280), and `U = {t, r_i, l_j} ⊂ K_M`.
- `I` is the set of `m`-ary numerals with digits in `{1, …, 2n}`. Put
  `I_0 = {α ∈ I : (α,0) ∈ H_0(M)}`.
- `⟨U⟩ ∩ K = ⟨t(α,β) : (α,β) ∈ H_0(M)⟩` (p. 280).
- For `α ∈ I`, `w_α(c) = 1` in `C` iff `α ∈ I_0` (p. 279).
- Put `e_α = t_α w_α(b) d`, `P = ⟨t_α : α ∈ I⟩` and `P' = ⟨e_α : α ∈ I⟩`.
  - The retraction `ρ : H_1 → K_M` that kills `C`, `b` and `d` maps `P'` isomorphically onto `P`,
    with `e_α ↦ t_α`. So both are free on these bases (p. 280).
- **The tower.**
  - `B_1 = HNN(H_1; a_i : A° → A_i°)`, where `A° = ⟨t,x,d,b_j⟩`, `A_i° = ⟨t_i, x^m, b_i d, b_j⟩`,
    and `φ_i : t, x, d, b_j ↦ t_i, x^m, b_i d, b_j`. This is an HNN extension by
    `the-aanderaa-cohen-rope-is-the-digit-letter-orbit-of-one-element` §4.
  - `B_2 = ⟨B_1, p | p^{-1}tp = td, [p, a_i] = 1⟩`.
  - `B_3 = ⟨B_2, k | k^{-1}uk = u (u ∈ U ∪ {d, p}), k^{-1}b_jk = b_jc_j⟩`.
  - `B_3` has Cohen's presentation (I) of `H_3` (p. 281), so `B_3 ≅ H_3`.

## Subgroup lemma

Let `B = ⟨H, s_i | s_i^{-1} a s_i = φ_i(a), a ∈ A_i⟩` be an HNN extension, and let `P ≤ H`
satisfy `φ_i(P ∩ A_i) = P ∩ φ_i(A_i)` for all `i`. Then:
- `⟨P, s_i⟩` is the HNN extension of `P` along `P ∩ A_i → P ∩ φ_i(A_i)`;
- `⟨P, s_i⟩ ∩ H = P`.

*Proof.* Take a word that is reduced for the HNN extension of `P`. Suppose it has a pinch in `B`.
- If the pinch is `s_i^{-1} q s_i` with `q ∈ P ∩ A_i`, it is already a pinch for `P`.
- If the pinch is `s_i q s_i^{-1}` with `q ∈ P ∩ φ_i(A_i) = φ_i(P ∩ A_i)`, it is again a pinch
  for `P`.

So the word is Britton-reduced in `B`. That gives injectivity. It also shows that a word with a
stable letter never lies in `H`. ∎ (Cohen uses this principle as his Proposition 1.34 on p. 280.)

## (R1) holds: `⟨t, a_i⟩ ≅ ⟨td, a_i⟩` in `B_1`, via `t ↦ td`, `a_i ↦ a_i`

**1. `⟨t, a_i⟩ = ⟨P, a_i⟩` and `⟨td, a_i⟩ = ⟨P', a_i⟩`.** For a digit word `W`,
`W^{-1} t W = t_{α(W)}` and `W^{-1} td W = e_{α(W)}` (rope node, §1).

**2. The lemma's hypothesis for `P`.**
- `P ⊂ ⟨t,x⟩ ∩ T ⊂ A°`, so `P ∩ A° = P` and `φ_i(P) = ⟨t_{mα+i}⟩`.
- `A_i° ∩ K_M = ⟨t_i, x^m⟩`, whose intersection with `T` is `⟨t(mr+i, 0) : r ∈ Z⟩`.
  Intersections of subgroups spanned by subsets of the free basis of `T` are spanned by the common
  basis elements. So `P ∩ A_i° = ⟨t_β : β ∈ I, β ≡ i mod m⟩`.
- For `1 ≤ i ≤ 2n < m` this is exactly `{mα + i : α ∈ I}`. So `P ∩ A_i° = φ_i(P)`.

**3. The lemma's hypothesis for `P'`.**
- `P' ⊂ A°` and `φ_i(e_α) = t_{mα+i} w_α(b) b_i d = e_{mα+i}`.
- If `g ∈ P' ∩ A_i°`, then `ρ(g) ∈ P ∩ ⟨t_i, x^m⟩ = ⟨t_{mα+i}⟩`. Since `ρ|_(P')` is injective,
  `g ∈ ⟨e_{mα+i}⟩ = φ_i(P')`.
- The reverse inclusion is clear.

**4. Conclusion.**
- By the lemma, `⟨t, a_i⟩` is the HNN extension of `P` along `P → φ_i(P)`, and `⟨td, a_i⟩` is the
  HNN extension of `P'` along `P' → φ_i(P')`.
- The isomorphism `P → P'`, `t_α ↦ e_α`, intertwines the `φ_i`. So the two HNN extensions are
  isomorphic by `t_0 ↦ e_0 = td`, `a_i ↦ a_i`.
- Both groups are free on `{t, a_i}` and `{td, a_i}`: Tietze moves eliminate each `t_{mα+i}` by
  its defining relation.

**So `B_2` is an HNN extension of `B_1` along free groups of rank `2n+1`.** Also
`⟨t,a_i⟩ ∩ H_1 = P` and `⟨td, a_i⟩ ∩ H_1 = P'`, by the lemma.

## (R2) holds: `A_+ = ⟨U,d,b_j,p⟩ ≅ A_- = ⟨U,d,b_jc_j,p⟩` in `B_2`

**The bases.** Put `P_+ = ⟨U⟩ * ⟨d⟩ * F(b)` and `P_- = ⟨U⟩ * ⟨d⟩ * ⟨b_jc_j⟩`, both in `H_1`.
- `⟨b_jc_j⟩ ≅ F_n`, because it maps onto `F(b)` by `c ↦ 1` and is generated by `n` elements.
- Both `P_+` and `P_-` meet `K_M` in `⟨U⟩`.

**Intersections with the associated subgroups of `B_2`.** Since `⟨t, a_i⟩ ∩ H_1 = P`:
- `P_± ∩ ⟨t, a_i⟩ = ⟨U⟩ ∩ P = ⟨t_α : α ∈ I_0⟩`, as spans of basis subsets of `T`.
- `P_+ ∩ ⟨td, a_i⟩ = P_+ ∩ P' = ⟨e_α : α ∈ I_0⟩`.
  - `⊆`: apply `ρ`, which is injective on `P'`.
  - `⊇`: `t_α ∈ ⟨U⟩` for `α ∈ I_0`.
- `P_- ∩ P' = ⟨e_α : α ∈ I_0⟩`.
  - The map `σ` that kills `C` fixes `P'`, sends `P_-` isomorphically onto `P_+`, and gives `⊆`.
  - `⊇` holds because for `α ∈ I_0`, `w_α(c) = 1` and `[b, c] = 1` give `e_α = t_α w_α(bc) d ∈ P_-`.

**Conclusion.**
- `ψ(t_α) = e_α` maps `P_± ∩ ⟨t, a_i⟩` onto `P_± ∩ ⟨td, a_i⟩`. So the lemma, applied to `B_2`,
  shows that `A_±` is the HNN extension of `P_±` along `⟨t_α⟩_(I_0) → ⟨e_α⟩_(I_0)`. This matches
  Cohen's description of `A_+` in `H_2` (p. 280).
- `θ : P_+ → P_-` (`U, d` fixed, `b_j ↦ b_jc_j`) is an isomorphism of free products. It fixes
  `t_α`, and it fixes `e_α` for `α ∈ I_0`, since `w_α(c) = 1`.
- So `θ` intertwines the HNN data, and `θ ∪ {p ↦ p}` is an isomorphism `A_+ → A_-`. ∎

## What this gives the seed tower

`H_3 ≅ B_3` is obtained from `H_1` by three genuine HNN steps.
- **Associated subgroups.** They are `A°, A_i°` (free of rank `n+3`), `⟨t,a_i⟩, ⟨td,a_i⟩`
  (free of rank `2n+1`) and `A_±`. **All are finitely generated.** The infinite-rank rope is gone.
- **Relative seeds still needed.**
  - `(H_1, A°)` and `(H_1, A_i°)`: subgroups spanning several free factors, so a Kurosh gluing.
  - `(B_1, ⟨t, a_i⟩)`: its induced splitting has vertex group `P`, the regular infinitely generated
    subgroup. So a relative seed for `(H_1, P)` enters again as a vertex-level input, not as an edge.
    - By `relative-seeds-on-free-groups-have-fg-stabilizers` (bh-g1-universal-point, 25f6d41ba),
      no relative seed over the free group `⟨x,t⟩` has stabilizer `P`.
    - So this seed must be built on `B_1` directly, where the finitely generated `⟨t, a_i⟩` is the
      target and the digit letters carry the transport. Gluing through `P` is not available.
  - `(B_2, A_±)`: the halting HNN, which is CAP.
  - `C × F_n ∈ 𝒞`: the input.

## Lesson for general BH

**The subgroup lemma turns orbit presentations into genuine splittings.** A family of relations
indexed by a regular set, here the rope, becomes one relation plus commutation with the digit
letters. The lemma certifies that the result is still a chain of honest HNN extensions with
finitely generated associated subgroups.
- For the seed tower this settles the Higman–Clapham shape: everything outside the halting HNN
  and the input is a finitely generated splitting.
- What is left are relative seeds for subgroups that are regular, or span several free factors.
  Those are input-free, finite-state constructions.
- CAP is concentrated in one pair `(B_2, A_±)`, whose trace on `K` is the halting set.
