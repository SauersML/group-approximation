**ESTABLISHED** by `surjunctive-decoder-subgroups-force-surjectivity-proof`.

**Theorem.** Let `G` be any group, `A` a finite alphabet, and `tau`, `sigma` cellular automata
on `A^G` with `sigma tau = id`. Let `S` be the memory of `sigma` and `H` the subgroup generated by
`S^-1 S`. If `H` is surjunctive at alphabet size `|A|`, then `tau` is surjective, and so bijective.

The memory of `tau` is arbitrary and `H` need not be normal, amenable or of finite index.

**Descent (proved in the same route).** If `(tau, sigma)` is strict (`tau` not surjective), freezing
the input to a constant symbol off `H` gives a strict pair `(tau_H, sigma_H)` over `H` itself. The
decoder `sigma_H` has the same rule and a translate of `S` as memory. The encoder `tau_H` has the
old rule with the addresses outside `H` read as the frozen symbol. So every strict pair descends to
a strict pair over a group generated by its decoder's memory differences.

**Consequences.**
- **Filter.** In every strict pair over every group, `S^-1 S` generates a nonsurjunctive subgroup.
  It is therefore nonsofic, not locally embeddable into surjunctive groups, and outside every
  landed surjunctivity permanence closure. This replaces "nonamenable" in
  `amenable-decoder-memory-forces-surjectivity` by "nonsurjunctive", and "normal closure" in
  `normal-surjunctive-decoder-memory-forces-surjectivity` by the subgroup itself. It is not vacuous
  on simple hosts: on the Leavitt unit groups, Thompson's `V` or a simple Kazhdan group, a strict
  design must have a decoder whose difference set generates a nonsofic subgroup.
- **Three-point decoder memory.** For `N = {e, s, t}`, `S^-1 S` generates `<s, t>`. So
  `three-point-decoder-memory-injective-ca-are-surjective` holds whenever `<s, t>` is sofic, in
  particular in the free case `<s, t> = F_2`, which that claim recorded as open. Its remaining content
  is the case of a nonsurjunctive two-generated group `<s, t>`, and by descent it may be assumed that
  `G = <s, t>`.
- **Two-point decoder memory** `{e, a}` gives `H = <a>`, cyclic, and recovers the two-point case with
  no Garden of Eden theorem for the decoder.

This is a class theorem on automaton pairs. It decides Gottschalk's conjecture for no new group.
