Full details are in Sections 1--3 of the artifact.

1. **Hull.**  Gabe's Theorem A gives an embedding `ρ : I ↪ D = C_0((0,1], O_2)`.
   `D` is quasidiagonal because it is a cone.  Let `J` be the hereditary
   subalgebra of `D` generated by `ρ(I)`.  Then `ρ : I -> J` is approximately
   unital (Brown--Dadarlat, after Definition 3.1), and `J` is quasidiagonal and
   separable.  `J` is full in the ideal `J'` of `D` it generates, and
   `J' = C_0(U) ⊗ O_2` for some open `U ⊆ (0,1]`.
2. **Push forward.**  With `γ^s : K⊗B -> Q(K⊗I)` the stabilised Busby invariant,
   `id_K ⊗ ρ` induces `η : K⊗B -> Q(K⊗J)` and embeddings
   `E ↪ E(γ^s) ↪ E(η)` (Definitions 2.4 and 3.1).
3. **Ext vanishes.**  `B` is separable and nuclear, so Kasparov's theorem
   identifies the Brown--Dadarlat Ext group with `KK^1`:
   `Ext(K⊗K⊗B, K⊗K⊗J) ≅ KK^1(B, J)`.  Brown's stable isomorphism gives
   `≅ KK^1(B, C_0(U) ⊗ O_2)`, and this is `0` because `1_(O_2) = 0` in
   `KK(O_2, O_2)` (`O_2` is UCT with `K_*(O_2) = 0`).  So `[η^s] = 0`.
4. **Conclude.**  Brown--Dadarlat Proposition 2.5 makes `E(η)` quasidiagonal,
   hence also its subalgebra `E`.

Lemma 1 (closure of `N(E)`): smaller open sets inherit the absence of compact
open subsets.  For extensions, a compact open `W ⊆ Prim(L)` meets the closed set
`Prim(L/J)` in a compact open subset of `Prim(L/J)`, which is empty, so `W` sits
in `Prim(J)` and is empty.  For unions, a compact `W` lies in finitely many,
hence one, member of a chain.

Consequence 1 is the theorem with `I = J`.  In 2, maximality forbids a nonzero
compact-free ideal of `E/J`, so each nonzero ideal of `E/J` has a compact open
piece.  For 3, if `B` is a counterexample then `C([0,1], B)` is separable,
nuclear, stably finite (isometries are fibrewise unitary) and not quasidiagonal
(it contains `B`).  Its two ideals have spectra `[0,2/3) × Prim B` and
`(1/3,1] × Prim B`.  A compact open subset of either would project to a
nonempty compact open subset of a connected noncompact interval.  Consequence 4
is the theorem for `I = C_0(R^2) ⊗ K`, whose spectrum is `R^2`.
