## 1. The problem text, at source

The notebook LaTeX reads, verbatim: "Is it algorithmically decidable whether a group generated by three given
class transpositions (for the definition, see 17.57) a) has only finite orbits on ℤ? b) acts transitively on the
set of nonnegative integers in its support? A difficult case is the group ⟨τ_{1(2),4(6)}, τ_{1(3),2(6)},
τ_{2(3),4(6)}⟩, which acts transitively on ℕ ∖ 0(6) if and only if Collatz' 3n+1 conjecture is true."

## 2. The proofs

- **Lemma 1: correct.**
  - For a set T of involutions, the orbits of ⟨T⟩ are the components of the graph with edges {x, t(x)}.
  - Within each family S_j the supports are disjoint. So at most one element of S_j moves a given x, and then
    σ_j(x) = s(x).
  - So the two graphs have the same non-loop edges.
  - σ_j itself does not have to lie in ⟨S⟩ for the lemma, and the lemma never uses that it does.
- **Lemma 2: correct.** Every element of the dihedral group ⟨σ₁, σ₂⟩ is g^k or g^kσ₁, where g = σ₂σ₁. So the
  orbit of x is ⟨g⟩x ∪ ⟨g⟩σ₁x.
  - All orbits are finite iff all g-cycles are.
  - Orbits are bounded iff g has finite order.
- **Lemma 3: correct.** A finitely generated group of permutations with orbits of size at most B lies in the
  variety generated by S_B, which is locally finite.
- **The two families: correct.** The refereed node (lines 111–119) defines
  s₁ = ∏_{c∈K} τ_{c(L),2c(2L)} and s₂ = ∏_{c∈K} τ_{2c(2L),(λ_c c)(λ_c L)}.
  - Both products have disjoint supports: c(L) ⊆ X is odd, 2c(2L) is even, and the classes f(c(L)) partition X.
  - On c(L), f(n) = λ_c n. So the class transposition τ_{2c(2L),f(c(L))} is exactly 2n ↔ f(n) there.
- **Property 1: correct.** Lemmas 1 and 2 give that the orbits are finite iff all cycles of F_M are finite. Part 1
  of the refereed Theorem gives that this holds iff every configuration of M is periodic. So Kari–Ollinger
  Theorem 3 makes the property undecidable.
- **Property 2: correct.**
  - The group is finite iff its orbits are bounded (Lemma 3, since S_M is finite), iff F_M has finite order.
  - By Claim 1 of the refereed node, the finite cycles of f are the codes of periodic configurations, and on them
    f is one machine step.
  - So F_M has finite order iff all configurations are periodic with bounded period, i.e. M is uniformly periodic.
  - The side claim is decidable because Kari–Ollinger Theorem 4 makes uniform periodicity decidable. I took that
    from the source record, which says "Theorems 2 and 4 show that uniform mortality and uniform periodicity of
    k-CM are decidable". I did not reread Theorem 4, since the PDF is not readable here. It is used only for this
    side claim, and the node says "decidable on this family", not in general.
- **Remark 1: correct.** With τ_{1(2),2(4)} in place of S₁:
  - every 2c(2L) lies in 2(4), because c is odd and 4 | 2L;
  - the product is f on X and 2f⁻¹ on 2X;
  - it swaps n ↔ 2n for odd n ∉ X, and fixes 0(4).
- **Remark 2: correct.** A triple in which two of the transpositions have disjoint supports reduces to the single
  element τ_k τ_i τ_j. Kohl's Collatz triple has pairwise overlapping supports, so it is not of this kind.
- **Remark 4: correct.** The group acts faithfully with finite orbits, so it is residually finite. F_M has
  infinite order when M is not uniformly periodic.
