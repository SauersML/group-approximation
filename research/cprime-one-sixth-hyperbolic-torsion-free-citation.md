`/scratch.global/sauer354/hl-zd-amplification/src/1210.0178/`).

(1) **Classical as graphical, `1introduction2.tex` l.71.** "For any classical $C(n)$ or
$C'(\lambda)$ presentation $\langle S|R\rangle$, we can construct a graph as the disjoint
union of cycle graphs, each labelled by (the class of cyclic conjugates and their
inverses) of a relator of the presentation. If $\langle S|R\rangle$ satisfies the
classical $C(n)$ or $C'(\lambda)$ condition, the constructed graph satisfies the $Gr(n)$
or $Gr'(\lambda)$ condition, respectively. If no relator is a proper power, the graph
satisfies the $C(n)$ or $C'(\lambda)$ condition, respectively."

(2) **`2basics.tex` l.54 and the lemma after l.62.** "Thus the $C(n)$ condition implies
the $Gr(n)$ condition, and the $C'(\lambda)$ condition implies the $Gr'(\lambda)$
condition." and "\begin{lem} Let $\Gamma$ be a labelled graph, $n\in\N$ and
$\lambda>0$. Then the following are equivalent: \begin{itemize} \item[i)] $\Gamma$
satisfies $C(n)$. \item[ii)] No reduced cycle is the concatenation of fewer than $n$
pieces. \item [iii)] No simple cycle is the concatenation of fewer than $n$ pieces.
\end{itemize} Moreover, if $\Gamma$ satisfies $C'(\lambda)$, then $\Gamma$ satisfies
$C(\lfloor \frac{1}{\lambda} \rfloor +1)$. \end{lem}"

(3) **Theorem `thm:linear_isoperimetry`, `2basics.tex`.** "Let $\Gamma$ be a
$Gr(7)$-labelled graph. Let $R$ be the set of words read on all simple cycles of
$\Gamma$. Then the presentation $\langle S|R\rangle$ satisfies the linear isoperimetric
inequality: $$\operatorname{Area}_R(w)\leq 8|w|.$$ In particular, if $\Gamma$ is finite,
then the group $G(\Gamma)$ is Gromov-hyperbolic."

(4) **Theorem `thm:asphericity` and Corollary, `3asphericity.tex` l.7–12.** "Let $\Gamma$
be a $C(6)$-labelled graph. Let $R$ be the set of cyclic reductions of words read on free
generating sets of the fundamental groups of the connected components of $\Gamma$. Then
the presentation complex associated to $\langle S|R\rangle$ is aspherical." and "The
group $G(\Gamma)$ defined by $\Gamma$ has an at most 2-dimensional $K(G(\Gamma),1)$
space and hence cohomological dimension at most 2. Therefore, $G(\Gamma)$ is
torsion-free."

**Deduction.**
- *The graph.* By (1) with no proper powers, the disjoint union `Gamma` of one cycle
  per relator is `C'(1/6)`-labelled. By the lemma in (2) it is `C(7)`-labelled; by
  item iii) of that lemma no simple cycle is a concatenation of fewer than `7`, hence
  fewer than `6`, pieces, so it is `C(6)`-labelled; and by the first quote of (2) it
  is `Gr(7)`-labelled. It is finite. Each component is a cycle, whose fundamental group
  is free of rank one generated by the loop reading the relator, so the words of
  (4) are the relators `R`, and the simple cycles of (3) read exactly the relators.
  (The abstract of arXiv:1408.4488, `main.tex` l.83, likewise speaks of "classical
  $C(7)$-groups and, hence, classical $C'(\frac{1}{6})$-groups".)
- *Conclusions.* (4) gives asphericity and torsion-freeness; (3) gives hyperbolicity.
