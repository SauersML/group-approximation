Let `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)`, let `D` be a finite maximal binary prefix code
with `|D|>=2`, choose `d_+,d_- in D`, and let
`F<=Sym(D)` be a finite transitive subgroup in the standard
prefix-permutation copy.  For `n>=2`, put

```text
H=<D_n(F),e_12(s_(d_+)),e_21(t_(d_-))> <= EL_n(R),
D_n(f)=diag(f,1,...,1).                                 (MLO1)
```

Whether the return is matched or crossed-zero,

```text
t_(d_-)s_(d_+)=delta_(d_-,d_+),
```

one has, for `m=|D|`,

```text
H isomorphic to GL_(m+1)(F_2),                           (MLO2)
```

acting trivially on the last `n-2` module coordinates.  In particular, for
the transitive order-`3^5` qutrit packet on `D_27`, every such one-chart
opposite-root bridge is exactly

```text
H isomorphic to GL_28(F_2).                              (MLO3)
```

The two roots alone generate `GL_2(F_2) isomorphic to S_3` when
`d_+=d_-`, and the dihedral group `D_8` when `d_+!=d_-`.

For `n>=5`, the subgroup of `St_n(R)` generated by the lifted qutrit
packet and the two standard root lifts is central-by-finite, amenable, and
hyperlinear.  Hence arbitrary additive overlaps supported by this package
cannot complete a no-CE atlas.

This is stronger than finite coefficient closure.  The unital
`F_2`-algebra generated by `s_(d_+),t_(d_-)` is
infinite-dimensional, but the common source/range module chart turns the
root package into a finite scalar matrix group.  The first surviving
opposite-root bridge must break this common chart: the two coefficients may
not both be leaf arrows for one finite prefix decomposition.
