## Setting

We use the minimal loop-label designs of `loop-labels-reduce-t2-to-two-sided-locality`.
- Level-`n` loops carry labels from finite sets `O_n`.
- **Transports** relate the labels of two loops of the same level. They generate `H_n ≤ Sym(O_n)`, which is
  transitive by minimality.
- **Lifts** are automorphisms acting on every loop label by the same local rule. They commute with `H_n`.

Call a design **regular** when `H_n` acts regularly on `O_n`. Then `O_n ≅ Q_n := H_n`, transports are left
multiplications, and the lifts act through the centralizer, which is `Q_n` acting by right multiplication.

A group `Γ` has a **two-sided cellular encoding** if all of the following hold:
- `Γ` has finite quotients `Γ → Q_n` along a residual chain (each `g ≠ 1` survives in all large `Q_n`);
- there are injective encodings `e_n: Q_n → (loop words of length ℓ_n, with skeleton decorations)`;
- for each generator `s` of a fixed finite generating set there are two local rules `λ_s` and `ρ_s`, of
  radius bounded independently of `n`, with `λ_s(e_n(q)) = e_n(s q)` and `ρ_s(e_n(q)) = e_n(q s)`;
- every `λ_s` commutes with every `ρ_{s'}`.

## Statement

1. **Pointer encodings.** Let `F` be a finite group and `Q_n = F wr Z/ℓ_n = F^(Z/ℓ_n) ⋊ Z/ℓ_n`. A loop
   label has three parts:
   - a value track `v: Z/ℓ_n → F`;
   - a pointer track with one marked cell `k`;
   - the loop's origin cell `0` (its NW corner), which the skeleton marks.

   Encode `(f, k)` as `v = f` with the pointer at `k`. Then:
   - right multiplication by `a ∈ F` (in coordinate 0) is `v(k) ↦ v(k)·a` at the pointer cell (radius 0);
   - right multiplication by the shift generator moves the pointer one cell along the loop (radius 1);
   - left multiplication by `a` is `v(0) ↦ a·v(0)` at the origin cell (radius 0, reads the skeleton);
   - left multiplication by the shift generator rotates the value and pointer tracks together one cell along
     the loop (radius 1).

   Every left rule commutes with every right rule, as maps on all configurations, including those with any
   number of pointers. So `F wr Z` has a two-sided cellular encoding. The lift group generated by the right
   rules acts faithfully on single-pointer labels as `F wr Z`.

   For `F = A_5`, the group `F wr Z` is **not virtually linear**. This answers the first test of the
   `gq-bh-invent-16-ra-free-state` handoff: a commuting pair can lift a group that is not virtually linear.
   With single-pointer labels, the label layer is sofic, not of finite type (see the proof).
2. **Closure, and the plaquette condition is free.**
   - Groups with a two-sided cellular encoding are closed under finite direct products, using parallel tracks
     and padding to common loop lengths. The class contains `GL_2(F_2[t^(±1)])` (the linear pair) and every
     `F wr Z`. For example it contains `GL_2(F_2[t^(±1)]) × (A_5 wr Z)`, which is non-amenable and not
     virtually linear.
   - For any such `Γ`, the 2×2 plaquette cocycle condition of the linear node (item 4(c)) costs nothing. Use
     two tracks `Q_n × Q_n`, with horizontal transports as left multiplications on track 1 and vertical
     transports as left multiplications on track 2. These commute, and together they act regularly. Lifts are
     right multiplications, diagonally or on one track.
3. **Quotient escape.**
   - Each lift rule is the restriction of a cellular automaton `ρ̃_s` of the full shift over the label
     alphabet, possibly with skeleton decorations. The lifted group on `Y` is the image of `Γ̃ = ⟨ρ̃_s⟩`
     restricted to the admissible data. It is a quotient of `Γ̃`, and can be a proper one: in item 1, `Γ̃` on
     multi-pointer data is Salo's larger variant, while the lift group is `F wr Z`.
   - So T2 does **not** need a non-exact subgroup of `Aut(B^Z)`. By Salo's universality theorem, that would
     be the open question of whether one explicit three-involution group is non-exact. Every finitely
     generated group is a quotient of a free group, and free groups lie in `Aut(B^Z)`. The obstruction to
     T2 lies in two-sidedness on the admissible data, not in the ambient group of cellular automata.
4. **Linear transports force linear, hence exact, lifts (regular designs).**
   - Suppose a design is regular and its transport groups are uniformly linear:
     `H_n ≤ GL_d(R_n)` with `R_n` commutative and `d` fixed.
   - Suppose also every lift `g ≠ 1` acts nontrivially on `O_n` for all large `n`.
   - Then the lift group `Γ` is linear over a commutative ring, hence exact.

   This strengthens item 3 of the linear node from "linear lifts are exact" to "**all** lifts commuting with
   linear regular transports are exact". It covers any non-linear lift rule. So T2 needs non-linear transports
   as well as non-linear lifts, and item 1's pair is non-linear on both sides.
5. **The sharpened crux (open).** In regular plaquette designs, T2 comes down to two independent problems.
   - (a) **The group problem.** Find a finitely generated, residually finite, **non-exact** group `Γ` with a
     two-sided cellular encoding. By item 4 its quotients `Q_n` cannot lie uniformly in `GL_d` of
     commutative rings.
   - (b) **The design problem**, shared with T1′ and independent of `Γ`:
     - seam-driven transport cocycles of unbounded level (the linear node, 4(a)–(b)) that make the label
       skew product minimal;
     - freeness, which the skeleton already gives;
     - quantum rigidity of the whole system.
   - For T1 (`F_2`, measure-preserving), (a) is already solved by the linear pair. Only (b) remains.
6. **Heuristics (not proved).** These record what the known mechanisms can and cannot reach.
   - (a) **Carries.** Two-sided locality in the known examples comes from exactly three mechanisms:
     convolution in characteristic `p` (the linear pair), pointers over lamps (item 1), and products. Groups
     like `SL_3(Z)` need integer arithmetic on the encoding, and carries propagate without bound. Redundant
     signed-digit encodings make each side local, but they break exact commutation of the left and right
     rules, since the encodings of `(sx)t` and `s(xt)` differ.
   - (b) **Displacement encodings.** Permutation-matrix encodings in a square's interior make row and
     column operations local and exactly commuting. But bounded displacement on a one-dimensional index set
     forces linear growth of the Schreier graphs, hence virtually cyclic lifts. So non-exactness needs a
     mixing mechanism that is neither convolution nor displacement. None is known.

## Proof

**1.**
- *Multiplication.* In `Q_n`, `(f, k)(f′, k′) = (f · σ^k f′, k + k′)`, with `(σ^k f′)(i) = f′(i − k)`.
  - Right multiplication by `(δ_a, 0)` gives `(f · σ^k δ_a, k)`: position `k` is multiplied by `a` on the
    right.
  - Right multiplication by `(1, 1)` gives `(f, k + 1)`.
  - Left multiplication by `(δ_a, 0)` multiplies position 0 by `a` on the left.
  - Left multiplication by `(1, 1)` gives `(σ f, k + 1)`, i.e. both tracks rotated by one relative to the
    fixed origin.

  So the four rules implement the stated multiplications, and `e_n` is a bijection onto single-pointer
  labels.
- *Commutation, on arbitrary configurations.*
  - Rotating both tracks commutes with shifting the pointer track, and with multiplying at pointer cells,
    because the pointers move with the values.
  - Multiplying at the origin commutes with shifting the pointer track, since they act on different tracks.
  - Multiplying at the origin on the left commutes with multiplying at pointer cells on the right, by
    associativity: `(a v) b = a (v b)`, including when the origin carries a pointer.
- *Faithfulness.* If `g = (f, k) ≠ 1` in `F wr Z`, choose `ℓ_n > 2(diam supp f + |k|) + 1`. The image of
  `g` in `Q_n` is nontrivial, so right multiplication moves some single-pointer label of level `n`.
- *Not virtually linear.* Let `N ≤ A_5 wr Z` be normal of finite index. Then `N ∩ A_5^(Z)` is shift-invariant,
  so `K = N ∩ A_5^((i))` is independent of `i` and is `1` or `A_5`.
  - If `K = 1`, the coordinate copies `A_5^((i))` embed into the finite group `A_5^(Z)/(N ∩ A_5^(Z))`. Their
    images commute pairwise, and they intersect trivially because `A_5` has trivial center. So `k` copies
    give order `60^k`, which is impossible for all `k`.
  - Hence `K = A_5` and `N ⊇ A_5^((Z))`. In `A_5^((Z))`, the centralizers of `A_5^((1)) × … × A_5^((m))`
    strictly decrease in `m`. That violates the minimal condition on centralizers, which every linear group
    satisfies. So no finite-index subgroup is linear.
- *Soficity.* "Exactly one pointer per closed loop" is enforced by an auxiliary prefix-OR track `b`:
  - `b(0) = p(0)` at the origin, and `b(i) = b(i−1) ∨ p(i)` elsewhere;
  - `p(i) = 1` forces `b(i−1) = 0` away from the origin;
  - `b = 1` at the last cell.

  Forgetting `b` is a factor map, so the single-pointer layer is sofic. The rules of item 1 are defined on
  labels alone, without `b`, and preserve single-pointer labels, so they are automorphisms of the sofic
  layer. Rather than trying to update `b`, which changes globally when the pointer passes the origin, we
  drop it from the lifted system.

**2.**
- *Products.* Run the encodings on parallel tracks and pad the loop words to a common length with a blank
  symbol that every rule fixes. Rules on different tracks commute, and each track's pair commutes.
- *Plaquettes.* Horizontal transports lie in `Q_n × 1` and vertical ones in `1 × Q_n`, so they commute
  elementwise. With the linear node's rule (a horizontal transport depends only on the vertical boundary
  crossed, and conversely), the plaquette identity holds. `Q_n × Q_n` is regular on itself. Right
  multiplications commute with both factors.

**3.** The rules `ρ_s` are given by finitely many local maps, and each extends verbatim to the full shift
over the label and decoration alphabet. The admissible set is invariant, so restriction is a homomorphism
from `Γ̃` onto the lift group. In item 1 on multi-pointer data, the value at a cell collects the right factors
of the word in time order from several pointers. So `Γ̃` does not factor through `F wr Z`, while its
restriction to single-pointer data does.

**4.**
- *Regular centralizers.* For a regular `H` on `O`, the centralizer of `H` in `Sym(O)` is `H` acting on the
  right, isomorphic to `H` via `h ↦ h^(-1)`. So the image `Γ_n` of `Γ` embeds in `H_n ≤ GL_d(R_n)`.
- *Ultraproduct.* Take a nonprincipal ultrafilter `U` on levels. Since each `g ≠ 1` is nontrivial for all
  large `n`, the map `Γ → ∏_U GL_d(R_n) = GL_d(∏_U R_n)` is injective. Here `∏_U R_n` is a commutative ring.
- *Exactness.* The entries of the finitely many generators generate a finitely generated, hence Noetherian,
  subring `R_0`. Reducing modulo the nilradical has nilpotent kernel `1 + M_d(nil)`. `R_0/nil` embeds in a
  finite product of fields, one for each minimal prime. So `Γ` is (nilpotent)-by-(a subgroup of a finite
  product of `GL_d(fields)`), which is exact by Guentner–Higson–Weinberger and closure under extensions by
  amenable groups.
- *Caveat.* Regularity is used. For a merely transitive `H`, the centralizer `N_H(H_x)/H_x` is a section.
  For example, `U_3(F_q)` acting on the cosets of an index-`p` subgroup of its center has extraspecial
  centralizers, whose minimal faithful degree is unbounded. So the ultraproduct step fails as stated. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

**Two-sidedness is a property of the lifted group, not of the ambient automata.**
- Pointers over lamps give commuting local left and right regular actions of `F wr Z`. So two-sided locality
  is not confined to linear algebra.
- Admissibility constraints can pass to quotients. The group of cellular automata the rules generate is
  irrelevant, and the exactness of `Aut(B^Z)` is no obstruction.
- On the other side, a linear regular transport group already forces exact lifts, however non-linear the lift
  rules are.
- What remains of T2 is one clean group-theoretic property: a residually finite **non-exact** group whose
  finite quotients carry left and right multiplications as commuting bounded-radius cellular automata.
- The three known mechanisms (convolution in characteristic `p`, pointers over lamps, products) all produce
  exact groups. A fourth mechanism is needed.
