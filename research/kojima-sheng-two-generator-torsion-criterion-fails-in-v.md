## Statement

In Thompson's group `V`, and hence in every Brin–Thompson group `nV` (`n ≥ 1`, via
`V ≤ nV` acting on the first coordinate), there are `g, h` with
`g^3 = h^3 = (gh)^3 = 1` such that `⟨g, h⟩ ≅ Z^2 ⋊ Z/3` is infinite. Here `Z/3`
acts on `Z^2` by rotation of order 3; this is the Euclidean triangle group `Δ(3,3,3)`.

Consequently the following claim of Kojima–Sheng, arXiv:2603.18410v3 (Kojima, Sheng,
"On subgroups of Brin–Thompson groups nV", v3 of 23 May 2026; TeX read on MSI, file
`20260522XS.tex`, md5 `4a3489f524643f173b87367080a5e987`) is false for every `n ≥ 1`:

> Corollary (label `Cor:TwoGenerator`). Let `g, h` and `gh` be torsion elements in `nV`.
> Then the group `⟨g, h⟩` generated by `g` and `h` is a finite group.

So is the conclusion of their Proposition `Prop:Identity`, that `R^p` is a dyadic block
invariant under both `g` and `h` (which would give `⟨g,h⟩ ≤ S_{|R^p|}`), since its proof uses
only that `g`, `h` and `gh` are torsion.

## Proof

1. **Three commuting copies of `Z`.** Let `x_0 ∈ F ≤ V` be the standard generator
   `0w ↦ 00w, 10w ↦ 01w, 11w ↦ 1w`; it has infinite order. Let `z_1 ∈ V` act as `x_0` on the
   cone `00` (`00w ↦ 00·x_0(w)`) and as the identity elsewhere.
2. **A cyclic permutation of cones.** Let `c ∈ V` be the prefix replacement
   `00w ↦ 01w, 01w ↦ 1w, 1w ↦ 00w`. Then `c^3 = 1`. Put `z_2 = c z_1 c^{-1}` and
   `z_3 = c^2 z_1 c^{-2}`. They are copies of `x_0` on the cones `01` and `1`. They have
   disjoint supports, so they commute, and they generate `Z^3`. Conjugation by `c` permutes
   them cyclically, `c z_i c^{-1} = z_{i+1}` (indices mod 3). So `⟨z_1, c⟩ ≅ Z ≀ Z/3`.
3. **The elements.** Put `g = c` and `h = z_1 z_2^{-1} c`. With `u = z_1 z_2^{-1}` we get
   `h^3 = u · (c u c^{-1}) · (c^2 u c^{-2}) · c^3 = (z_1 z_2^{-1})(z_2 z_3^{-1})(z_3 z_1^{-1}) = 1`.
   Also `gh = c u c = (c u c^{-1}) c^2 = z_2 z_3^{-1} c^2`, and the same telescoping gives
   `(gh)^3 = (z_2 z_3^{-1})(z_1 z_2^{-1})(z_3 z_1^{-1}) = 1`.
4. **Infinite.** `g^{-1} h = c^{-1} z_1 z_2^{-1} c = z_3 z_1^{-1}` has infinite order. In
   fact `⟨g, h⟩` is the subgroup `A ⋊ ⟨c⟩` of `Z^3 ⋊ Z/3`, where
   `A = {z_1^a z_2^b z_3^e : a+b+e = 0} ≅ Z^2` and `c` acts as a rotation of order 3.

Any composition convention works: the relations above hold in the abstract group `Z ≀ Z/3`.

## Consequence for the literature

- The proof of Kojima–Sheng's Theorem `Thm:TorsionLocallyFinite` ("nV is torsion locally
  finite for n ≥ 1", their Theorem 1.1) is by induction on the number of generators. Its base
  case is `Cor:TwoGenerator`, and each step applies `Prop:Identity` to pairs `(g, h_1)`,
  `(g h_1, h_2)`, … inside a torsion group. Since `Prop:Identity` is proved from the torsion
  of `g`, `h`, `gh` alone and its conclusion fails for the pair above, the argument as written
  is invalid. I did not locate the individual faulty step among Lemmas `Lem:TandR`,
  `Prop:TandR`, `Lem:Flip`.
- The theorem itself is not refuted: `Δ(3,3,3)` is not a torsion group, and for `n = 1` the
  statement is Röver's theorem. For `n ≥ 2`, torsion local finiteness of `nV` has no valid
  published proof known to this graph, and it remains open
  (`brin-thompson-2v-is-torsion-locally-finite`, `brin-thompson-groups-contain-infinite-periodic-groups`).
- Main previously cited the Kojima–Sheng torsion theorem as a fact in
  `every-gl-n-z-embeds-in-some-brin-thompson-group`; that citation is corrected in the same
  landing. The "torsion is no obstruction" conclusion there does not depend on it.
