Suppose a factor map of free actions has C(Y) < C(X). Take epsilon below the gap in the localization theorem and adjoin the resulting finitely many reuse flags to the base one at a time, X = Z_0, Z_1, ..., Z_m = Z. Costs are nonincreasing along the chain and the last is strictly below the first, so some single step strictly decreases cost while adjoining the translates of exactly one measurable set. For a fixed finitely generated group the following are therefore equivalent: all free actions have the same cost; every factor map between free actions preserves cost; every free extension generated over its base by one binary orbit observable preserves cost. An equivalent third form is that every free factor map admits source-near-optimal plans whose signed traffic measures lose asymptotically no total variation under projection.

This reduces the alphabet of the unresolved information, not its group-wide structure. A binary symbolic extension reveals the whole orbit process of one set; its fibers can be infinite and its bits can satisfy complicated compatibility constraints, so it is not a two-point extension.

Status records a written deduction, not independent validation.
