## Why sufficient

Full argument: §1 (Corollary 1.1') and §3 (Theorems 2' and 2) of the
artifact.

Let `Gamma = <A_1, ..., A_m, S>` be countably infinite, with the `A_i`
amenable and `S` finite.

1. **Absorb the finite generators.** Move every finite `A_i` into `S`. Now
   every remaining `A_i` is infinite. If none remains, `Gamma` is an infinite
   finitely generated group, and the hypothesis gives fixed price.
2. **Hosts.** For each remaining `A_i`, the embedding claim
   (`countable-group-embeds-in-two-generator-simple-group`) gives a finitely
   generated group `B_i = <T_i>` containing a copy of `A_i`. Only "embeds in a
   finitely generated group" is used.
3. **Tower.** Put `G_0 = Gamma` and `G_i = G_(i-1) *_(A_i) B_i`. By the
   normal form theorem each `G_(i-1)` embeds in `G_i`. Since `A_i` lies in
   `B_i`, `G_i` is generated by `A_(i+1), ..., A_m`, `S` and
   `T_1, ..., T_i`. So `G_m` is finitely generated by
   `S cup T_1 cup ... cup T_m`, and it is infinite because it contains
   `Gamma`.
4. **Finite cost of the factors.** Corollary 1.1' of the artifact: if a
   countable group is generated by `k` amenable subgroups and a finite set
   `F`, then every free p.m.p. action of it has cost at most `k + |F|`. The
   proof joins cost-1 graphings of the amenable orbit relations ([GL] Thm
   2.20, and Cor 2.14 for finite subgroups) with the generator maps. So every
   free action of `G_(i-1)` has cost at most
   `(m - i + 1) + |S| + |T_1| + ... + |T_(i-1)|`, and every free action of
   `B_i` has cost at most `|T_i|`.
5. **Descent.** By the hypothesis, `G_m` has fixed price. Suppose `G_i` has
   fixed price. Then `fpbs-amalgam-over-amenable-descends-fixed-price` applies
   to `G_i = G_(i-1) *_(A_i) B_i`: `A_i` is an infinite amenable common
   subgroup, and both factors have only finite-cost free actions by step 4.
   So `G_(i-1)` has fixed price. Downward induction from `i = m` gives fixed
   price for `G_0 = Gamma`.

The hypothesis is used exactly once, at `G_m`.
