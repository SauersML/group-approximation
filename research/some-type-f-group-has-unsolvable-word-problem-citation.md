Citation import, not a reproof. D. J. Collins and C. F. Miller III, *The word
problem in groups of cohomological dimension 2*, in *Groups St Andrews 1997 in
Bath, I* (C. M. Campbell, E. F. Robertson, N. Ruškuc, G. C. Smith, eds.),
London Math. Soc. Lecture Note Ser. 260, Cambridge University Press, 1999,
pp. 211--218, doi:10.1017/CBO9781107360228.013.

The chapter abstract on cambridge.org (read 2026-09-13) says three things:

1. finitely presented groups with unsolvable word problem given by the
   Boone--Britton construction have cohomological dimension 2;
2. these groups are obtained from free groups by successive HNN extensions
   with finitely generated free associated subgroups, and the presentations
   obtained are aspherical;
3. consequently there is no algorithm to decide whether a presentation, or a
   finite 2-complex, is aspherical.

The chapter body was not read here, so no internal theorem number is recorded.

**Type F.** A finite presentation is aspherical when its presentation complex
`X` has contractible universal cover, that is, when `X` is a `K(G,1)`. `X` is
a finite 2-complex, so `G` has type `F`. The HNN description gives the same
conclusion directly. Start with a finite graph as a `K(F_r,1)`, and at each of
the finitely many steps glue a cylinder (a finite graph `× [0,1]`) along maps
inducing the injective associated subgroup inclusions. By Scott--Wall this
graph of aspherical spaces with π_1-injective edge maps is aspherical, and it
is a finite 2-complex.
