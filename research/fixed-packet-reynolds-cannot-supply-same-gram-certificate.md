**ESTABLISHED FENCE.**  The positive mass clause `(SMG1)` of
`fixed-mass-same-gram-mixed-word-certificate` cannot be forced by a fixed
finite family of semisimple packet algebras, their internal and mutual
relations, and fixed Reynolds/functional-calculus localization alone.

Indeed put all fixed packet actions on

```text
V tensor C^N
```

as operators in `End(V) tensor I_N`.  By
`two-fixed-packet-reynolds-actions-leave-a-gram-spectator`, every projection

```text
I_V tensor Q_N                                             (FSG1)
```

commutes with every packet and is fixed by every associated Reynolds
operator.  All fixed packet multiplication, restriction, covariance, and
mutual-incidence data are therefore identical as `Q_N` ranges from `0` to an
arbitrary-rank projection.  No consequence of those data can give

```text
||G||_2^2 >= m_0 tau((1-J)/2)-o(1),       m_0>0,           (FSG2)
```

for a Gram coordinate whose only authenticated property is membership in
the common packet fixed algebra: take the spectator coordinate in `(FSG1)`
to be zero while retaining the same nonzero marked label packet.

Nor can fixed finite-group averaging, spectral cuts, polar/support
projections, conditional expectations, and fixed matrix compressions repair
this by manufacturing a trace-cyclic proper return.  Those operations are
tracially functorial, so `functorial-packet-return-firewall` applies them in
the left regular finite factor.  Any two-product return charging the positive
marked carrier then forces its faithful regular trace to vanish.

Thus a same-Gram compiler has exactly two surviving kinds of input at this
stage:

1. a scalable packet/actor whose algebra grows through the multiplicity
   coordinate; or
2. an ordinary mixed word whose action is nontrivial on every non-scalar
   spectator gauge and whose fixed-mass estimate is proved only on the
   pointed canonical face.

The theorem does not refute the target, which explicitly allows the second
alternative.  It rules out treating the phrase “mixed word” as a fixed
packet Reynolds or polar-localization calculation in disguise.
