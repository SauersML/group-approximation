Let `S` be the class of surjunctive groups. We prove both permanence
properties needed by
[[universal-all-group-subgroup-colimit-class-tester]], including the
noninjective part of the colimit statement.

## 1. Subgroups and finitely generated locality

Let `H<=G` and let a cellular automaton on `A^H` have memory
`M subset H`. Use the same local rule on `A^G`. The resulting automaton is
the product, over the appropriate cosets of `H`, of copies of the original
automaton. Therefore an injective nonsurjective automaton over `H` would
give one over `G`. Hence surjunctivity passes to subgroups.

The same coset decomposition also shows

~~~text
G is surjunctive
  <=>
every finitely generated subgroup of G is surjunctive.
~~~

Indeed the finite memory of any cellular automaton lies in a finitely
generated subgroup.

## 2. The marked-limit lemma with both finite witnesses

Let `(G_i,s_i)` converge to the finitely generated marked group `(G,s)`,
and suppose every `G_i` is surjunctive. Assume toward contradiction that
`G` has an injective nonsurjective cellular automaton `tau` on `A^G`, with
finite memory `M` and local rule `mu`.

Injectivity has a finite detector. The compact set

~~~text
X={(x,y) in A^G x A^G : x(1)!=y(1)}
~~~

is covered by the open sets on which `tau(x)` and `tau(y)` differ at a
specified coordinate. Compactness supplies a finite `N subset G` such that

~~~text
tau(x)|N = tau(y)|N  =>  x(1)=y(1).        (1)
~~~

Nonsurjectivity has a finite missing cylinder. The image of `tau` is
compact and hence closed. Choose `z` outside it. A basic open neighborhood
of `z` disjoint from the image supplies a finite `F subset G` such that

~~~text
there is no x with tau(x)|F=z|F.            (2)
~~~

Choose one finite marked ball containing `M,N,F` and all products needed
to evaluate the local rule on `N` and `F`. For all sufficiently large `i`,
the equality table of that ball is identical in `G_i` and `G`. Transport
`mu` to obtain a cellular automaton `tau_i` on `A^(G_i)`.

Equation (1) makes `tau_i` injective. If `tau_i(x)=tau_i(y)`, transport the
restrictions of `x,y` on the finite rule ball back to `G` and extend them
arbitrarily to configurations there. Equality of the outputs on the
transported copy of `N` and (1) give equality at the identity. Translating
this argument gives `x=y` at every coordinate.

Equation (2) makes `tau_i` nonsurjective. Transport the pattern `z|F` to
`G_i` and extend it to a full target configuration. A preimage would, by
transporting its restriction on the finite rule ball back to `G`, produce
an `x` with `tau(x)|F=z|F`, contradicting (2). Thus a sufficiently large
`G_i` has an injective nonsurjective cellular automaton, contrary to its
surjunctivity.

This finite-witness proof is the marked-group closure theorem used in the
literature. See Glebsky--Gordon, *On surjunctivity of the transition
functions of cellular automata on groups*, Taiwanese J. Math. 9 (2005),
511--520, [doi:10.11650/twjm/1500407858](https://doi.org/10.11650/twjm/1500407858),
especially their approximation statement and ultraproduct proof.

## 3. Arbitrary directed colimits

Let

~~~text
G=colim_i G_i
~~~

where every `G_i` is surjunctive and the structure maps are arbitrary.
Take a finitely generated subgroup `H=<h_1,...,h_d>` of `G`. Lift its
marked generators to some stage `G_i`. For every later stage `j`, let
`H_j<=G_j` be the subgroup generated by their images. Section 1 gives
surjunctivity of every `H_j`.

The marked groups `(H_j,(h_(1,j),...,h_(d,j)))` converge, as a directed
net, to `(H,(h_1,...,h_d))`. For any finite list of marked words, an
equality holding in the colimit is witnessed at one later stage; finitely
many such equalities are witnessed at one common stage. An inequality in
the colimit cannot become an equality at any stage, since it would then
remain an equality in the colimit. Hence each finite marked ball eventually
has exactly the equality table of `H`. Section 2 shows that `H` is
surjunctive. Section 1 then shows that `G` is surjunctive.

This proves closure under directed colimits with genuinely noninjective
structure maps, not merely directed unions.

## 4. Apply the fixed all-group host

The class `S` is now subgroup-closed and closed under arbitrary directed
colimits. Applying
[[universal-all-group-subgroup-colimit-class-tester]] to `S` gives

~~~text
U is surjunctive  <=>  every group is surjunctive.
~~~

QED
