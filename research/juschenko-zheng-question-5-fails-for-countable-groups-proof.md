Notation is as in the claim. `B` is the group of upper triangular matrices in `G` with positive diagonal entries.

**Step 1 (G = BK).** Let `M ∈ G` have columns `m_1, m_2`. Since `R` is real closed, positive elements have square
roots. Gram–Schmidt therefore works over `R`:
- set `q_1 = m_1/√(m_1·m_1)`;
- set `q_2 = (m_2 − (m_2·q_1)q_1)/√(·)`, where the vector under the root is nonzero because `M` is invertible.

Let `Q = [q_1 q_2]`. It is orthogonal, and `U = QᵀM` is upper triangular with positive diagonal. Since
`det U > 0` and `det M = 1`, `det Q > 0`, so `Q ∈ K` and `det U = 1`, so `U ∈ B`. Hence `M = QU` and `G = KB`.
Applying this to `M⁻¹` gives `G = BK`.

**Step 2 (the action).** Every coset is `gK = bkK = bK`, so `B` acts transitively on `G/K`. In particular every
finite subset of `G/K` lies in the single orbit `B·K`. `B` is solvable, since it is an extension of the
diagonal group by the unipotent group. Hence `B` is amenable, and the action is locally amenably transitive.
- Theorem A of `amenably-coupled-actions-admit-symmetric-liouville-measures` gives a symmetric non-degenerate
  Liouville measure. `G` is countable, so any enumeration of `G` serves as the generating sequence.
- The stabilizer of `gK` is `gKg⁻¹`, and `K ≅ {a + bi : a² + b² = 1} ⊆ R(i)^×` is abelian.

**Step 3 (nonamenability).**
- `G ⊇ SL_2(Z)`, and `SL_2(Z)` contains the free group generated by `[[1,2],[0,1]]` and `[[1,0],[2,1]]`
  (Sanov). So `G` is not amenable.
- If `G ↷ G/K` had an invariant mean `m`, then `G` would be amenable, which it is not. To see this, fix a left
  invariant mean `m_K` on the abelian group `K`. For `f ∈ ℓ^∞(G)`, set `f̄(gK) = m_K(k ↦ f(gk))`. This does not
  depend on the representative `g`, by left invariance of `m_K`. Then `f ↦ m(f̄)` is a left invariant mean on
  `G`, because translating `f` by `h` translates `f̄` by `h`. ∎

**Relation to Question 5.** The hypotheses of Question 5 hold with the measure of Step 2, and `G` is not
amenable. The question as stated says only that `G` acts on a set. The paper's standing assumption of a finite
generating set is not met, so the finitely generated question stays open.
