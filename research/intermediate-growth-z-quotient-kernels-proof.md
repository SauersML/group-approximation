Elementary growth facts used (proved inline): (i) a free subsemigroup on two elements of word
length `<= l` gives at least `2^n` elements in the ball of radius `l n`; (ii) a finitely generated
subgroup `K <= G` satisfies `gamma_K(n) <= gamma_G(C n)`, `C` the maximal `G`-length of `K`'s
generators; (iii) a finite-index subgroup has growth equivalent to the whole group (it is
finitely generated, and every element of `G` is a coset representative times an element of the
subgroup, which bounds `gamma_G` by a constant times `gamma_{G_1}` of a rescaled radius).

1. By (i), `G` has no free subsemigroup on two generators. `Z` is elementary amenable, so `N` is
   finitely generated by `no-free-semigroup-ea-quotient-kernels-are-fg`.
2. By (ii), `N` has subexponential growth.
3. Suppose `N` has polynomial growth. By Gromov, `N` has a nilpotent subgroup of some finite index
   `d`. As `N` is finitely generated it has finitely many subgroups of index `d`; their
   intersection `M` is characteristic in `N`, of finite index, and nilpotent. So `M` is normal in
   `G`, and `N/M` is finite.
4. Pick `t in G` with `pi(t) = 1`. Conjugation by `t` permutes the finite set `N/M`, so some
   `t^k` centralizes `N/M`, and `G_1 = <M, t^k>` has finite index in `G` (at most `k |N/M|`).
   `G_1/M` is cyclic and `M` is nilpotent, so `G_1` is solvable.
5. By Milnor–Wolf, `G_1` has polynomial or exponential growth; by (iii), so does `G`. This
   contradicts intermediate growth. Hence `N` is not of polynomial growth, so by step 2 it has
   intermediate growth, and it is not virtually nilpotent.
6. A finite-index subgroup of `G` is finitely generated of intermediate growth by (iii), so the
   argument applies to it.
