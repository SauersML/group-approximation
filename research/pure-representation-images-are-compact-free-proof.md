title: Pull a compact image ideal back to an elementary ideal-subquotient

Suppose `J=rho(A) intersect K(H)` is nonzero.  Since `K(H)` is an ideal of
`B(H)`, `J` is an ideal of `rho(A)`.  Put `L=rho^(-1)(J)` and `N=ker(rho)`.
Then `N` is an ideal of `L`, `L` is an ideal of `A`, and

```text
L/N isomorphic J.                                    (R2)
```

Choose `0<k in J`.  A nonzero spectral value of the compact operator `k` is
isolated, so continuous functional calculus produces a nonzero finite-rank
spectral projection `p in C*(k) subset J`.  The finite-dimensional algebra
`pJp` has a minimal projection `e`.  It is also minimal in `J`, because for
every `x in J`,

```text
e x e = e(pxp)e in e(pJp)e = C e.
```

The ideal of `J` generated by a minimal projection is elementary: its
standard representation on the Hilbert completion of `Je` identifies it
with the compact operators.  Thus `J`, and by (R2) the algebra `A`, has a
nonzero elementary ideal-subquotient.

But a pure C-star algebra is nowhere scattered, and nowhere scatteredness is
equivalent to having no nonzero elementary ideal-subquotient.  This
contradiction proves (R1).  The argument also covers finite-dimensional `H`,
where `K(H)=B(H)` and hence every representation of a pure algebra is zero.
