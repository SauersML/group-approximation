**Problem LXXIX of Schafhauser--Tikuisis--White, arXiv:2506.10902.**
For nowhere scattered `A` with finitely many ideals and finite nuclear
dimension, characterise finite decomposition rank in terms of
quasidiagonality-type conditions.

## Attempts

* The simple case is the TWW-era theorem: finite nuclear dimension +
  quasidiagonality (with faithful trace machinery) gives finite dr,
  and dr forces strong quasidiagonality of every quotient.  With
  finitely many ideals the natural conjecture-shaped answer: dr < ∞
  iff every subquotient in the ideal filtration is strongly
  quasidiagonal — necessity is classical (dr passes to ideals and
  quotients and forces strong QD); sufficiency needs the extension
  step, i.e. gluing completely positive QD approximations across one
  extension at a time, which is Problem LXXXII's dimnuc-of-extensions
  question in its dr form with QD hypotheses to rule out the
  Toeplitz-type failures.  Recorded: LXXIX = (LXXXII for dr) +
  strong-QD bookkeeping; a counterexample must be an extension of
  strongly QD pieces with dr jumping to ∞, and the Eckhardt-McKenney
  style nilpotent-group examples (dr vs dimnuc separation territory,
  cf. this lane's LXXXVI Berend frontier) are the candidate quarry.
