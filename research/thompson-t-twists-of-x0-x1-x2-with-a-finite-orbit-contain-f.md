**OPEN.** Notation as in `thompson-t-dyadic-rotation-cosets-are-not-amenably-transitive`;
`x_2 = x_0^{-1}x_1x_0`, supported in `(3/4, 1)`.

**Statement.** For all dyadic `a, b, c`, if `H = ⟨x_0ρ_a, x_1ρ_b, x_2ρ_c⟩ ≤ T` has a finite orbit on `S¹`, then
`H` contains a subgroup isomorphic to `F`.

**Consequence (proved here).** If the statement holds, then `thompson-t-dyadic-rotation-cosets-are-not-amenably-transitive`
is equivalent to `thompson-f-is-not-amenable`.
- *Proof.* One direction is `jz-question-5-for-thompson-t-implies-f-nonamenable` (item 3). For the other, let
  `F` be nonamenable and take `S = {x_0, x_1, x_2}`. Consider a twist `H`. If `H` has no finite orbit it is
  nonamenable by `fg-amenable-subgroups-of-thompson-t-have-finite-orbits` (every element of `T` has a periodic
  point, by Ghys–Sergiescu; alternatively check the three generators). If it has a finite orbit it contains
  `F` by the statement, so again it is nonamenable. So `S` is a witness. ∎

So a proof would show that the route `thompson-f-not-amenable-via-rotation-cosets-of-thompson-t` is circular:
its hard prerequisite is the root, restated. A counterexample (a twist with a finite orbit and no `F`) is an
F-free subgroup of `T` with a finite orbit. Its finite-index point stabilizer lies in `PL_o(I)`, so it would be
a candidate test case for `every-subgroup-of-thompson-f-is-ea-or-contains-f`; it is still not known to be amenable.

**Evidence (exact census, `experiments/rotation-twist-finite-orbits-2026-09-17/classify_twists.py`).**
- For all `(a, b, c) ∈ (2^{-4}Z/Z)³` (4096 triples), 4085 twists have no finite orbit, so they are nonamenable
  unconditionally. The other 11 contain `F`, certified by Brin's one-sided criterion on a pair of words of
  length at most 3 (`classify_x0x1x2_K4.json`).
- At `2^{-3}` (512 triples) the split is 506 and 6 (`classify_x0x1x2_K3.json`).
- The twist `(1/2, 0, 0)`, which extends the amenable twist of `{x_0, x_1}`, now contains `F`: it is
  `⟨x_1, x_2⟩ ≀ Z/3`, and `⟨x_1, x_2⟩ ≅ F`.

**Why the statement is plausible and where a proof must work.** In the census, every twist with a finite
orbit has most twist parameters equal to 0 or small. The generic twist has no finite orbit at all, because
the three periodic sets `Per(x_iρ_{θ_i})` do not share an invariant subset. A proof has to control the
infinitely many twists whose generators are all torsion. For those, `Per` is the whole circle and the
finite-orbit condition is a genuine group-theoretic constraint, not a local one.

**Tool that closes the hard census cases.** Suppose two elements of a twist fix a common point and each
has a single orbital, and the two orbitals cross (`r' < r < s' < s`). Then the first element approaches
exactly one end of the common orbital `(r', s)`, so the pair generates a group containing `F` by
`brin-plo-i-one-sided-approach-gives-a-copy-of-f`. Short words miss this configuration. For the
`{x_0, x_1}` twist `(21/32, 0)` the crossing pair has lengths 3 and 9 (see
`thompson-generators-x0-x1-have-an-amenable-rotation-twist`, census). A proof would plausibly produce such a
crossing pair from the finite orbit: conjugate a generator's single-orbital piece by an element that moves an
endpoint of it but not the whole orbital.

  needs a reason why `x_1ρ_b` and `x_2ρ_c` cannot both sit inside single arcs of one torsion arc
  permutation. That is what `x_2` adds over `{x_0, x_1}`, and it is unproved. Deferred; the census is the
