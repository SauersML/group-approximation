Since `O_2` is simple and nuclear, the ideals of
`A_X=C_0(X) tensor O_2` correspond to open subsets of `X`; hence its
primitive spectrum is `X`, and every quotient is of the form
`C_0(F) tensor O_2` for a closed subset `F` of `X`.

Suppose a quotient contains a nonzero commutative hereditary subalgebra
`C`, and let `J` be the ideal generated by `C`.  The algebra `C` is full
hereditary in `J`, so strong Morita equivalence makes `J` type I.  But
`J=C_0(V) tensor O_2` for a nonempty open subset `V` of `F`, and evaluation
at any point of `V` gives a quotient `J -> O_2`.  This contradicts
permanence of type I under quotients.  Thus `C` cannot exist.

Finally, `A_X` is separable, nuclear, and O-infinity-stable.  The
Bosa--Gabe--Sims--White nuclear-dimension theorem gives
`dim_nuc(A_X)<=1`.  It is not AF, since it has the non-AF quotient `O_2`,
so its nuclear dimension is not zero.  Therefore it is exactly one.
