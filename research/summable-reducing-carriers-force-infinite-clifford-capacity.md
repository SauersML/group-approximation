Let `H=C^d`, with normalized trace `tau`, and let `A<=M_d(C)` be generated by
Pauli candidates

```text
X_1,Z_1,X_2,Z_2,... .
```

Suppose that for every `N>=1` there is a projection `C_N` central in `A` such
that

```text
sum_(N>=1) tau(1-C_N) < 1,                                  (SRC1)
```

and on `C_N H` the first `N` pairs obey

```text
X_i^2=Z_i^2=1,             X_i Z_i=-Z_i X_i,
[X_i,X_j]=[X_i,Z_j]=[Z_i,Z_j]=1       (i!=j).                (SRC2)
```

Then no such finite-dimensional tuple exists.

Indeed, the meet `C=meet_N C_N` has

```text
tau(C) >= 1-sum_N tau(1-C_N)>0.                              (SRC3)
```

Centrality makes `CH` a reducing subspace for every cell.  For each `N`, the
restriction of the first `N` cells to `CH` is therefore a unital
representation of `M_(2^N)(C)`.  Consequently

```text
2^N divides rank(C)                                         (SRC4)
```

for every `N`, contradicting `0<rank(C)<infinity`.

The word **reducing** is essential.  Large contextwise projections with a
large set-theoretic intersection do not suffice: a Pauli word for one context
may move that intersection out of another context's carrier.  Central
carriers are a convenient sufficient condition; it is enough more generally
that their common meet reduce every cell.

This is a finite-matrix-only endpoint and is therefore compatible with
`regular-trace-blocks-exact-local-predicate-return`.  The group regular trace
may absorb the same tower in infinite multiplicity, while no fixed matrix
algebra can.
