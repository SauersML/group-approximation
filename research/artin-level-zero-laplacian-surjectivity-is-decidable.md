**OPEN.** There is an algorithm with the following input and output.
- *Input:* `(Γ, χ)`, where `Γ` is a finite labelled graph and `χ : A_Γ -> R` is dominant.
- *Output:* whether the level-0 Laplacian `L_0` of
  `artin-sigma1-equals-dead-cut-laplacian-surjectivity` is onto `K_r(Z[ker χ])`.

An explicit graph-theoretic or arithmetic rule would also do.

**Proved reduction (to a finitely generated subgroup).** Let `H' ≤ H = ker χ` be the subgroup
generated by the supports of all the `σ_ρ`. Then `L_0` is onto over `Z[H]` iff it is onto
over `Z[H']`.
- Write `Z[H] = ⊕_s s·Z[H']`, where `s` runs over left coset representatives with `s_0 = 1`.
  This is a direct sum of right `Z[H']`-modules.
- A coefficient `λ = Σ_s s μ_s` gives `λ σ = Σ_s s (μ_s σ)` for `σ ∈ Z[H']`.
- So the `s_0`-component of an identity `Σ_ρ λ_ρ σ_ρ (e_a - e_b) = f_i` is an identity with
  coefficients `μ_{ρ,s_0} ∈ Z[H']`.
- `H'` is generated by explicit words: `pq` for dead edges `{p,q}`, and the dead vertices.
  The prefixes of dead-dead relators are words in dead vertices.
- So the question is a unimodular-row question over the group ring of an explicit, finitely
  generated subgroup of `A_Γ`.
- By part 6 of `artin-sigma1-equals-dead-cut-laplacian-surjectivity`, the input can be
  replaced by the *type* of `χ`: its sign vector and its set of edges with
  `χ(u) + χ(v) = 0`. Each `Γ` has finitely many types.
- So the claim is equivalent to this: a finite list of explicit unimodularity questions per
  `Γ` can be decided uniformly in `Γ`.

**Known partial answers.**
- If `r = 1`, the answer is always yes.
- There are necessary tests: augmentation, and the finite abelian and representation
  witnesses (`artin-sigma1-dead-edge-ring-obstruction`).
- There are sufficient tests: explicit identities, as for `K_q` and the mixed `K`.

## Attempts

**w17 z-follow (2026-09-17): two semi-decision procedures. They do not meet.**
- *"Onto" is semi-decidable wherever the word problem of `A_Γ` is solvable.* This covers
  every class in which it is known.
  - Enumerate coefficient vectors over `Z[H'_τ]`, written as words.
  - Check each candidate identity `Σ c_ρ σ_ρ (e_a - e_b) = f_i` using the word problem.
  - The `K_q` and mixed-`K` identities were found this way, by hand.
- *"Not onto" is certified by any ring homomorphism `Z[G] -> R ≠ 0` that kills the
  crossing `σ_ρ` of some cut.* Examples are finite abelian twists, `2 x 2` representations,
  and augmentation mod `p`.
  - Enumerating finite-dimensional representations over finite fields gives a
    semi-procedure.
  - It is complete only if non-surjectivity is always witnessed by a finite-dimensional
    representation. That is a residual-finiteness-type property of the module `coker L_0`,
    and it is not known.
- *Where it stops.* A test that always terminates would need one of two things:
  - a bound on the coefficient supports of an identity;
  - residual finiteness of `coker L_0` as a module.
  Neither is available.
- *Why it may fail.* A unimodular-row problem over the group ring of a subgroup of an Artin
  group can be as hard as the word problem there, which is itself open for general Artin
  groups. The subgroups `H'_τ` that occur (for example `<ae, ab^{-1}, ac, ...>` inside
  `K_q`) are generated by products of pairs of generators, and their structure is not known
  in general.
