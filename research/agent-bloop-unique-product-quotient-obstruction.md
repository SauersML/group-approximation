Let `B` be a unital star algebra generated by projections and assume that
`B` has no Connes-embeddable tracial state.  Suppose that `G` is a group,
`J` is a nontrivial central involution, and, with

```text
q=(1-J)/2,
```

there is a unital star homomorphism

```text
Phi:B -> q C[G]q,                  Phi(1)=q.              (UPQ1)
```

Let `Lambda` be the subgroup generated by `J` and the finite supports of the
images of one finite projection-generating set of `B`.  Then

```text
Lambda/<J> does not have the unique-product property.                (UPQ2)
```

More concretely, one of the displayed generator projections has image
`0<p<q`.  If `A` and `D` are the supports of `p` and `q-p`, respectively,
in the canonical twisted basis of `q C[Lambda]q`, then `A,D` are finite and
nonempty and

```text
no element of AD has a unique expression ad with a in A, d in D.     (UPQ2')
```

Thus the failure of unique product is witnessed by coefficients already
occurring in one BCS generator image; it is not merely an abstract property
of the ambient quotient.

In particular this applies to `B=A(B_loop)` in
`nonhyperlinear-bloop-central-sign-corner`.  The countable ambient host and
all generators outside the displayed finite supports are irrelevant: every
successful authenticated corner already contains a finitely generated
central extension

```text
1 -> <J> -> Lambda -> H -> 1
```

whose quotient `H` fails unique product.  Consequently the quotient cannot
belong to any class for which unique product is known, and in particular it
cannot be a free group or a bi-orderable group.  A construction with a free,
bi-ordered, or other unique-product coefficient quotient cannot be repaired
by adding the no-CE diagram only in a larger ambient group.

## Proof

Put `H=Lambda/<J>` and choose a section `s:H->Lambda` with `s(1)=1`.
For `h,k in H` there is a unique `omega(h,k) in {+1,-1}` such that

```text
s(h)s(k)=J^epsilon s(hk),          omega(h,k)=(-1)^epsilon.
```

The elements

```text
u_h=q s(h),                        h in H,
```

form a complex vector-space basis of `q C[Lambda]q`.  Indeed each `u_h` is
one half of the difference of the two distinct group elements in the
`<J>`-coset over `h`, and different cosets have disjoint supports.  Their
multiplication is

```text
u_h u_k=omega(h,k)u_(hk).                                  (UPQ3)
```

Thus, algebraically and unitally,

```text
q C[Lambda]q ~= C_omega[H],                                (UPQ4)
```

where the corner unit `q` corresponds to `u_1`.

We use the following elementary twisted-domain lemma.  If a group `H` has
the unique-product property, then `C_omega[H]` is a domain for every scalar
two-cocycle `omega`.  To see this, take nonzero finite sums

```text
x=sum_(a in A)c_a u_a,       y=sum_(b in D)d_b u_b
```

with all displayed coefficients nonzero.  Unique product gives an element
`h in AD` having exactly one expression `h=a_0d_0`.  The coefficient of
`u_h` in `xy` is

```text
c_(a_0)d_(d_0)omega(a_0,d_0) != 0,
```

so `xy!=0`.

Assume for contradiction that `H` has unique product.  By `(UPQ4)`, the
corner `q C[Lambda]q` is a domain.  Every projection `p` in a domain is
trivial, since

```text
p(q-p)=0
```

forces `p=0` or `p=q`.  Therefore `Phi` sends every projection in the chosen
generating set of `B` to either `0` or `q`.  Its whole image is consequently
contained in `Cq`, and, after identifying `q` with `1`, `Phi` is a unital
star homomorphism

```text
B -> C.                                                    (UPQ5)
```

But `(UPQ5)` is a character, and its positive functional is a
one-dimensional, hence Connes-embeddable, tracial state on `B`.  This
contradicts the hypothesis.  Hence `H` is not unique product, proving
`(UPQ2)`.

The same argument extracts the promised finite witness.  Some chosen
generator projection has image `p` different from both `0` and `q`, since
otherwise all generator images would be scalar and would give `(UPQ5)`.
Write `p` and `q-p` in the basis `(u_h)` and let their nonzero supports be
`A` and `D`.  Both are finite and nonempty.  The projection identity gives

```text
p(q-p)=0.                                                   (UPQ6)
```

If an element of `AD` had a unique expression `a_0d_0`, its coefficient in
the left side of `(UPQ6)` would be the nonzero product of the two displayed
coefficients and `omega(a_0,d_0)`.  This is impossible.  Hence `(UPQ2')`
holds.

For the finite-support assertion, every `Phi(p)` belongs to
`q C[Lambda]q` by construction, and the defining identities of `B` remain
identities in that subalgebra.  Nontriviality of `J` also persists on passing
from `G` to its subgroup `Lambda`.  Thus the preceding argument applies
inside the finitely generated support group itself.  End proof.

## Exact effect on the Bloop gate

The result does not construct `(UPQ1)` and does not prove that failure of
unique product is sufficient.  It does rule out a broad class of seemingly
natural property-`(T)`-free coefficient hosts before any analytic stability
question begins.  The missing block-escaping chord cannot live over a
unique-product quotient and cannot be delegated to an ambient wrapper: its
own finite support must create both the non-unique-product algebraic zero
divisors needed for proper BCS projections and the cross-context identities
of `B_loop`.
