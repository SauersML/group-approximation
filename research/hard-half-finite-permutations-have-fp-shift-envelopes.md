**OPEN.** For every recursive non-decreasing `R : N -> N` there are
`gamma_1, ..., gamma_k in Sym(N)` with three properties.
1. Each `gamma_i` has half-finite support and `gamma_i(x) <= 4x` for all `x`.
2. For some `i`, the map `n -> gamma_i(n)` (with `n` in unary) is not in `F(R)`.
3. `G = < gamma_1, ..., gamma_k, sl, flip, (1 2) >` is finitely presented.

`hard-type-a-actors-via-hard-half-finite-shift-envelopes` derives
`type-a-actors-with-arbitrarily-hard-word-problem` from this. The hardness of
the actor comes from evaluating its own generators, via
`half-finite-shift-envelope-wp-computes-generators`.

## Attempts

1. **Eventually periodic generators.** *Dies as a hardness source.* If every
   `gamma_i` lies in some `E_m`, evaluation takes linear time, so all such
   families sit under one recursive bound. These are exactly the known positive
   cases: the zigzag envelope `E_nu(Z)` of index 12 in `E_4`
   (`zigzag-cyclic-shift-envelope-is-finitely-presented`), and the finite-ray
   envelopes of virtually cyclic inputs
   (`finite-ray-shift-envelopes-require-virtually-cyclic-input`).
2. **Single-input envelopes `E_nu(P)`.** A special case: the `gamma_i` are the
   transported left-regular generators of `P` on the even numbers. Their
   hardness is the hardness of the enumeration `nu`. For `P = Z` the
   finite-presentation locus is countable and dense
   (`shift-envelope-fp-enumerations-are-countable`,
   `cyclic-shift-finite-prefix-cannot-decide-fp`), but nothing on main bounds
   the complexity of successful enumerations from either side.
3. **A necessary condition: finitely presented germs.** `Sym_fin(N) <= E_2 <= G`,
   and `Sym_fin(N)` is normally generated in `G` by the single transposition
   `(1 2)`, since all transpositions are conjugate under `Sym_fin(N)`. So if `G`
   is finitely presented, the germ group `G / Sym_fin(N)` (Mallery--Zaremsky,
   Definition `def:germs`) is finitely presented too.
   - *Heuristic kill for minimal patterns, unclear.* Take a bounded-displacement
     involution whose infinite half acts by a pattern read from a set `X`. Its
     conjugates by powers of `sl` shift the pattern, so the germ group resembles
     a topological full group over the orbit closure of `X`. For minimal
     aperiodic subshifts, commutator subgroups of such full groups are
     infinitely presented (Grigorchuk--Medynets, arXiv:1105.0719, abstract). The
     germ group is not literally that full group, so this is not a proof.
4. **Rich patterns.** *Dies with Attempt 5 (2026-09-13).* Patterns whose orbit closure is a shift of
   finite type, such as a hard decidable set containing every finite word,
   escape the heuristic of Attempt 3. Next test: compute the germ group of
   `< gamma_X, E_2 >` for such an `X` and decide its finite presentation.
5. **Bounded displacement.** *Dies, for every pattern (2026-09-13).*
   - *The theorem.* `fp-bounded-displacement-e2-envelopes-are-eventually-periodic`:
     if every `gamma_i` moves points a bounded distance and `G` is finitely
     presented, every `gamma_i` lies in some `E_m`, so evaluation takes linear
     time. This covers Attempt 4 and any other bounded-displacement pattern,
     whatever its orbit closure, including sparse hard defects and Toeplitz-type
     non-minimal patterns. The germ group of Attempt 3 is never needed.
   - *The mechanism.* `fp-alt-fin-envelopes-are-unique-solutions-of-their-relators`:
     a finite presentation makes `gamma` the only tuple satisfying its relators.
     With bounded displacement the relators are a one-sided shift of finite type
     on fold indices, and pumping a unique solution makes it periodic.
   - *Exact gap.* A witness needs a generator of unbounded displacement, up to
     `gamma(x) <= 4x`. There one relator at `x` reaches points near `4^t x`, the
     conditions couple scales and are not a one-dimensional shift of finite
     type, so pumping fails. Uniqueness still makes each witness computable
     (clause 2 of the rigidity claim), but gives no complexity bound.
