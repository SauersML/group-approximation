Conventions.  For non-unital `A`, real rank zero of `A` means real rank zero of
its unitization `A~`, and K1-surjectivity of `A` means that of `A~`.

**Part (1), weak cancellation.**  Let `A` have real rank zero.  If `A` is
unital, it is separative by `rr0-c-star-algebras-are-separative`, and part 2 of
`agor-separative-rr0-algebras-are-k1-bijective` with `n = 1` gives weak
cancellation of `A`.

If `A` is not unital, `A~` is unital of real rank zero, hence separative, hence
weakly cancellative.  Let `p, q` be projections in `A` generating the same
closed ideal `I` of `A` with `[p]_0 = [q]_0` in `K_0(I)`.  The closed ideal of
`A~` generated by `p` is the closure of `A~ p A~`.  Since `p = p p p` and
`a p = a p p`, `p a = p p a` for `a` in `A`, this is the closure of `A p A`,
i.e. `I`.  Likewise for `q`.  So `p, q` generate the same ideal `I` of `A~` with
the same class in `K_0(I)`, and weak cancellation of `A~` gives a partial
isometry `v` in `A~` with `v*v = p` and `vv* = q`.  Then `v = v p` lies in `A`,
so `p ~ q` in `A`.

**Part (2), K1-surjectivity.**  If `A` is unital of real rank zero, it is
separative, and part 1 of `agor-separative-rr0-algebras-are-k1-bijective`
(AGOR Theorem 3.1) gives that `U(A)/U(A)° -> K_1(A)` is an isomorphism, in
particular surjective.  If `A` is not unital, apply this to `A~`.

So the target holds.  In fact the argument gives K1-bijectivity of every unital
real rank zero algebra, which is `rr0-algebras-are-k1-bijective`
(`rr0-k1-bijectivity-via-separativity`).
