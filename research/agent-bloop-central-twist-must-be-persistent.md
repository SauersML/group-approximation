Let `B` be a unital star algebra generated by finitely many projections and
assume that `B` has no Connes-embeddable tracial state.  Suppose that `G` has
a nontrivial central involution `J` and that, for

```text
q=(1-J)/2,
```

there is a unital star homomorphism

```text
Phi:B -> q C[G]q,                  Phi(1)=q.                 (CTP1)
```

Let `Lambda` be the subgroup generated by `J` and the finite supports of the
images of a finite projection-generating set of `B`, put

```text
H=Lambda/<J>,
```

and let

```text
alpha in H^2(H;C2)                                          (CTP2)
```

classify the central extension

```text
1 -> <J> -> Lambda -> H -> 1.                               (CTP3)
```

Then `alpha` is **persistent**: for every finite-index subgroup `H_0<=H`,

```text
res^H_(H_0)(alpha) != 0.                                   (CTP4)
```

Equivalently, `(CTP3)` does not split over any finite-index subgroup.  This
conclusion does not assume that `H` or `Lambda` is residually finite.

Consequently `H` is not good in Serre's sense.  In particular, `H` cannot be
hyperbolic virtually special.  Thus no group defined by a finite classical
`C'(1/6)` presentation, and no hyperbolic cubulated group, can be the
coefficient quotient of an authenticated Bloop corner.  More elementary
special cases are:

* `H` cannot be finite;
* `H` cannot have a finite-index subgroup with `H^2(H_0;C2)=0`, so in
  particular it cannot be virtually free;
* a generalized Rips--Segev quotient is not made viable merely by its
  explicit failure of unique product.  Its chosen central double-cover class
  must additionally be persistent.

Applied to `B=A(B_loop)` in
`nonhyperlinear-bloop-central-sign-corner`, this is a support-local
obstruction.  Enlarging the ambient group outside `Lambda` cannot turn a
virtually split twist into a valid model.

## Proof

All images of the chosen generators lie in `q C[Lambda]q`, so `(CTP1)`
restricts to that corner.  Suppose, contrary to `(CTP4)`, that

```text
res^H_(H_0)(alpha)=0
```

for a finite-index subgroup `H_0<=H`.  Classification of central extensions
by `H^2` gives a homomorphic section over `H_0`.  Its image is a subgroup
`K<=Lambda` satisfying

```text
K intersect <J> = 1,        [Lambda:K]=2[H:H_0]<infinity.  (CTP5)
```

Let

```text
N=intersection_(lambda in Lambda) lambda K lambda^(-1)
```

be the normal core.  It is normal and finite-index in `Lambda`.  Since
`<J>` is central, `(CTP5)` gives

```text
N intersect <J> = 1.                                      (CTP6)
```

Therefore the finite quotient `rho:Lambda->F=Lambda/N` does not kill `J`.
Its linear extension sends

```text
q |-> q_F=(1-rho(J))/2 != 0.                              (CTP7)
```

Because `rho(J)` is still a central involution, `q_F` is a nonzero central
projection in `C[F]`.  Composing `(CTP1)` with `rho` gives a unital star map

```text
B -> q_F C[F]q_F.                                         (CTP8)
```

The target is a nonzero finite-dimensional star algebra.  The canonical
group trace restricted to this corner, normalized by
`tau_F(q_F)=1/2`, is a tracial state.  Pulling it back through `(CTP8)` gives
a finite-dimensional, hence Connes-embeddable, tracial state on `B`, a
contradiction.  This proves `(CTP4)`.

If `H` were good, `good-groups-have-no-persistent-finite-cohomology` applied
to `alpha` in degree two would make its restriction vanish on a finite-index
subgroup, contradicting `(CTP4)`.  Hyperbolic virtually special groups are
good, giving that exclusion.  If `H` is finite, take `H_0=1`; if `H` has a
finite-index subgroup with vanishing degree-two `C2`-cohomology, restrict to
that subgroup.  These prove the listed special cases.  End proof.

## Rips--Segev boundary

Arzhantseva--Steenbock's generalized Rips--Segev construction
(arXiv:`1407.2441`) supplies torsion-free hyperbolic non-unique-product
groups, but its non-unique-product sets do not supply a persistent class
`(CTP2)`.  Martin--Steenbock (arXiv:`1409.3678`) explicitly distinguish
their cubulation theorem for classical small cancellation over free products
from the then-open problem of cubulating generalized Rips--Segev groups.
Accordingly, the present obstruction rules out every such candidate once it
is known to be hyperbolic cubulated, but it does not silently apply the
classical `C'(1/6)` cubulation theorem to the graphical Rips--Segev
presentations.

This is still only necessary.  A persistent central class supplies no
projection in the twisted group algebra and no cross-context BCS identities.
