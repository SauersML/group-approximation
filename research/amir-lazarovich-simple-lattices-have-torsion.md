**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed; route
`amir-lazarovich-simple-lattices-have-torsion-proof`). The proof is by hand, for every
`d >= 9`. A computer run of the combinatorial core (artifact) agrees for `9 <= d <= 12`.
No priority claimed.

## Setting

Amir–Lazarovich (AL), arXiv:2605.09493; the definitions are recorded in
`gq-bh-bh-free-extensions-cd3-sources.md`.

- **Input.** `Γ` is an involutive BMW group of degree `(m, n)`, with generators
  `x_1..x_m, a_1..a_n` and alternating local actions. `𝓔` is the explicit `n`-scaffolding
  of AL's Lemma "existence of scaffoldings", with parameter `d`.
- **The lattice `Λ'`.** It is the group of the interlacing pair AL build from `(Γ, 𝓔)`:
  - generators `Z = {x_i} ∪ {y_1..y_{2d-1}}` and `V(O_d)`;
  - relators `z²`, `D²`, `[D, D']` for edges of `O_d`, and `z·D·δ_D(z)·ζ_z(D)`.
- **Parity.** `Λ'^+` is the kernel of the parity map `Λ' → (Z/2)²` (`z ↦ (1,0)`,
  `D ↦ (0,1)`), the colour-preserving subgroup of index 4.
- **`Λ`** is the finite residual of `Λ'`. When `Γ` is not residually finite, AL prove
  (Theorem A) that `Λ` is a finitely presented simple lattice of finite index.

**Hypothesis (H).** `a_j a_k` lies in the finite residual `FR(Γ)` for some `j ≠ k`.

## Theorem

Assume `d >= 9` and (H). Then:
1. every homomorphism from `Λ'` to a finite group is constant on `V(O_d)` and constant
   on `Z`, so it factors through the parity map;
2. `Λ = Λ'^+`;
3. `Λ` contains the involution `DD'` for every edge `{D, D'}` of `O_d`.

So `Λ` has torsion and `cd Λ = ∞`. AL's simple lattices give no answer to their
Question ("finitely presented simple groups of cohomological dimension other than 2 or
∞?") or to Kourovka 21.46. As a by-product, `Λ'^+` is itself simple.

## When (H) holds

- **`FR(Γ) = Γ^+`.** Then every pair satisfies (H). Lazarovich–Levcovitz–Margolis,
  arXiv:2202.00378, Lemma "Finite residual", prove this for every involutive BMW group
  whose structure set contains their set `S_0`.
- **Radu's `Δ = Γ_{4,5,9}`.** Suppose `Γ` contains `Δ` as the subgroup generated by some of
  its own generators. Radu's Theorem 5.5 (arXiv:1712.01091, as quoted by LLM) gives
  `Δ/FR(Δ) ≅ (Z/2)²`. So at least four of the `a_j` coincide in every finite quotient of
  `Γ`, on either side.

These are the two sources AL cite for their input, and both prove non-residual finiteness
through `Δ`. An input violating (H) would need `Γ/FR(Γ)` to separate all of `a_1, …, a_n`.
None is known to us. Note also that LLM's family has full symmetric local actions, while
AL ask for alternating ones.

## Erratum to AL's explicit scaffolding (d <= 8)

- **The failure.** AL's condition (E4) asks for `∪_{j=i}^{i+3} supp(υ_j) ⊆ C_i`. With
  `υ_j = (j, j+1)(j+2, j+3)`, that union is `{i, …, i+6}`, which has 7 elements, while
  `|C_i| = d-1`. Their own proof of the interlacing condition (D4) at `C_i` uses exactly
  this inclusion.
- **Computer check.** The script in the artifact finds that (E4), (D4) and (D5) fail on
  the `y`-part for `d = 7, 8`, and hold for `d = 9, …, 12`.
- **The fix.** With `d = max{n+1, 6}` the Lemma is correct only for `n >= 8`. Their (E1)
  only asks `d >= max{n+1, 6}`, so taking `d >= max{n+1, 9}` restores it. This is why the
  Theorem assumes `d >= 9`.

## Scope

- **Only AL's explicit scaffolding.** The proof uses two features of it:
  - every vertex at which some `y_i` is twisted (`B` and all `C_i`) contains the point 1;
  - `2d - 1 ∈ A'`.

  General scaffoldings satisfying (E1)–(E4) are not covered.
- **Not ruled out.** Torsion-free simple lattices in `Aut(T_c) × Aut(X_{O_d})` built by
  other means. For AL's explicit data the proof shows that any finite quotient identifying
  two of the `A_j` is the colour quotient. So a torsion-free simple subgroup there needs a
  seed `Γ` violating (H).
- **Compatible with `tree-by-complex-lattices-with-faithful-tree-factor-satisfy-pbh`.**
  That node (bh-testcases, a2b4d86ae) puts `Λ'` in `B_A`. So `Λ = Λ'^+` and its products
  embed in finitely presented simple groups. The two statements are independent:
  membership in `B_A` says nothing about torsion.

## Lesson for general BH

**Mechanism.** A lattice with one vertex orbit on a product `T × X` is a matched pair
`Π · W`, with relations `z D = ζ_z(D) δ_D(z)`. In any finite quotient, "`φ(D) = φ(D'')`" is
an equivalence relation that moves along every untwisted letter.

**Consequence.** In AL's lattices, one collapsed pair of seed generators forces the whole
quotient down to the colouring quotient `(Z/2)²`. The proof needs only a region free of
twisted vertices on which the local action is still alternating. So the same should hold
whenever the twisted vertices are few and the local actions are primitive.

**What this buys.**
- The finite residual of such constructions is computed by a finite orbit closure, with no
  coset enumeration.
- Simple lattices glued from BMW seeds are forced to be exactly the colour-preserving
  subgroup, so they inherit the seed's involutions.
- Involutive square data put involutions `DD'` in the colour-preserving subgroup. So a
  torsion-free simple subgroup exists only if no finite quotient identifies seed generators.
  Every seed in the sources AL cite identifies some, since both sources build seeds that
  contain Radu's `Δ`.
