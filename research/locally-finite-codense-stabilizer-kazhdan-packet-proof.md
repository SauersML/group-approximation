Notation as in the target. `[x,y]=xyx^(-1)y^(-1)`. Characters of `M=direct_sum_X F_2` are
functions `X -> {+1,-1}`, and `A` acts on them by `(g chi)(m)=chi(g^(-1)m)`.

## Part I: the general form (LF0)

**Step 1 (aperiodic seed on M).** Let `chi in M^` have `chi(m_0)=-1` and suppose its orbit is
finite. The kernel `N` of the action of `A` on that orbit is a finite-index normal subgroup fixing
`chi`. By `(LF1)`, `a=nc` with `n in N`, `c in C`. So `ax=ncC=nC=nx`, and

```text
chi(delta_(ax)) = chi(delta_(nx)) = (n^(-1) chi)(delta_x) = chi(delta_x),
```

because `n^(-1)` fixes `chi`. Hence `chi(m_0)=chi(delta_x)chi(delta_(ax))=+1`, a contradiction.
Also `m_0 != 0`, because `a notin C` gives `ax != x`. So `(ICA1)` holds.

**Step 2 (aperiodic seed on M_0).** Let `M_0` be the augmentation submodule (even-size supports),
which contains `m_0`. Let `chi in M_0^` have `chi(m_0)=-1` and finite orbit, with finite-index
normal kernel `N` as before. Extend `chi` to `chi~ in M^`; the extensions of `chi` are exactly
`chi~` and `-chi~` (the constant sign `-1` is the only nontrivial character killing `M_0`). For
`n in N`, `n chi~` extends `n chi=chi`, so `n chi~ = eps(n) chi~` with `eps(n) in {+1,-1}`, and
`eps:N -> {+1,-1}` is a homomorphism. Its kernel has index at most 2 in `N`, hence finite index in
`A`. Its normal core `N'` in `A` is finite-index normal and fixes `chi~`. Step 1 applied to `chi~`
and `N'` gives `chi~(m_0)=+1`, a contradiction.

**Step 3 (finite-dimensional invisibility).** `A` is finitely generated by hypothesis, as
`infinite-character-actor-kills-fd-mark` requires. Steps 1 and 2 are `(ICA1)`
for `M` and for `M_0`. That node then gives: every finite-dimensional unitary representation of
`P(M rtimes A, m_0)` or `P(M_0 rtimes A, m_0)` kills `J`, and `J != 1` there. This is item 1.

**Step 4 (sofic set action and sofic base).** `A` is sofic and `C` is locally finite, so Theorem
2.14 of `gkp-sofic-action-toolkit` makes the left multiplication action `A curvearrowright A/C=X`
sofic. Theorem 3.6 of `gkp-wreath-permanence-theorem`, with lamp group `F_2` (finite, hence
sofic), gives that `M rtimes A = F_2 wr_X A` is sofic. `M_0 rtimes A` is a subgroup of it, hence
sofic by `elek-szabo-sofic-permanence` clause 1.

**Step 5 (hyperlinear packet, negative mass 1/2).** Sofic groups are hyperlinear, so
`central-d8-seed-preserves-hyperlinearity` (CDS3), applied with the involution `b=m_0`, makes
`P(M rtimes A, m_0)` and `P(M_0 rtimes A, m_0)` hyperlinear, with `J != 1` (CDS2). A hyperlinear
group has a trace-preserving embedding of its group von Neumann algebra in a matrix ultraproduct.
So there are unitaries `u_n` in `U(d_n)` representing `J` along the ultrafilter, which may be taken
to be exact involutions by functional calculus, with `tr_(d_n)(u_n) -> tau(J)=0`. Then
`tr_(d_n)(1_(-1)(u_n))=(1-tr_(d_n)(u_n))/2 -> 1/2`. This is item 2.

**Step 6 (Kazhdan gap).** Assume `A` has property (T) with Kazhdan pair `(S,eps)`, `S` finite
symmetric. Let `chi` be seed-visible, so its orbit `O` is infinite by Step 1 or 2. On
`l^2(O)` an invariant vector is constant on `O`, hence zero. So every unit vector `v` has
`max_(s in S)||sv-v|| >= eps`, and with `Q=(1/|S|) sum_(s in S) s`,

```text
<(I-Q)v,v> = (1/(2|S|)) sum_(s in S) ||sv-v||^2 >= eps^2/(2|S|) =: gamma.
```

This `gamma` does not depend on `chi`, which is `(ISC1)` for `M` and for `M_0`.

**Step 7 (no modulus).** Steps 4 and 6 are exactly the hypotheses of
`sofic-chart-quotient-forces-character-energy-floor`, for `M` and for the invariant submodule
`M_0`. That node produces, for every finite actor/module word window, exact representations of the
module and approximate actor unitaries with defect tending to zero on the window, seed-negative
mass tending to `1/2` (SCQ1), transport energy at least `gamma-o(1)` (SCQ2), and a moving module
word with covariance defect at least `sqrt(gamma)-o(1)` (SCQ3). So no dimension-independent
function of word defect bounds `(ISC2)` or the supremum in `(IWE6)`, on this packet. This is
item 3.

## Part II: the explicit instance (LF2)

Write `R=F_2[t]` and `A=EL_3(R)`, generated by the elementary matrices `e_ij(f)`, `i != j`,
`f in R`. For distinct `i,j,k` the Steinberg relation is

```text
[e_ik(f), e_kj(g)] = e_ij(fg),        e_ij(f)e_ij(g)=e_ij(f+g).        (ST)
```

**Step 8 (finite generation).** By `(ST)`, `e_ij(t^(r+1))=[e_ik(t^r),e_kj(t)]`, so induction on `r`
puts every `e_ij(t^r)` in the subgroup generated by the twelve matrices `e_ij(1)`, `e_ij(t)`.
Additivity then gives every `e_ij(f)`.

**Step 9 (residual finiteness and soficity).** For `k >= 1` let `pi_k:A -> SL_3(R/(t^k))` reduce
entries mod `t^k`; the targets are finite. If `g != I`, some entry of `g-I` is a nonzero
polynomial, which survives mod `t^k` once `k` exceeds its degree. So `A` embeds in the direct
product of the finite groups `pi_k(A)`. Finite groups are sofic, so `A` is sofic by
`elek-szabo-sofic-permanence` clause 1 (direct products and subgroups).

**Step 10 (property T).** `R=F_2[t]` is the free algebra on one generator over the finite field
`F_2`, so `elementary-group-property-t-over-free-algebras` (`finiteFieldElementaryThree_hasKazhdanPropertyT`)
gives property (T) for `A`.

**Step 11 (normal subgroups of finite index contain an ideal).** Let `N` be finite-index normal
in `A`. The set `W={f in R : e_13(f) in N}` is an additive subgroup, and `f -> e_13(f)N` embeds
`R/W` into `A/N`, so `W` has finite index in the infinite group `R` and `W != 0`. For `f in W`
and `g in R`, `(ST)` gives

```text
e_12(fg) = e_13(f) * ( e_32(g) e_13(f)^(-1) e_32(g)^(-1) ) in N,
```

since the bracketed factor is a conjugate of `e_13(f)^(-1) in N`. By additivity `e_12(I) <= N`,
where `I=W R` is a nonzero ideal of `R`.

**Step 12 (lambda kills no nonzero ideal).** Let `0 != p in I` have degree `d`, so its top
coefficient is `1`. Choose `m` with `2^(m-1) > d` and put `j=2^m-d >= 0`. Then `p t^j` has exponents
in the window `[2^m-d, 2^m]`. The only power of 2 there is `2^m`, since `2^(m-1) < 2^m-d` and
`2^(m+1) > 2^m`. So `lambda(p t^j)=1`, and `p t^j in I`.

**Step 13 (profinite condition LF1).** `lambda(t)=1` because `1=2^0`. By Step 12 there is `i in I`
with `lambda(i)=1`, so `v=t-i in V=ker lambda`. Then

```text
a = e_12(t) = e_12(i) e_12(v)  in  N C,
```

using Step 11 for the first factor. As `N` was arbitrary, `(LF1)` holds.

**Step 14 (the stabilizer).** `e_12:(R,+) -> A` is injective, so `C=e_12(V)` is isomorphic to the
codimension-one subspace `V` of the countably infinite-dimensional `F_2`-space `R`. So `C` is an
infinite elementary abelian 2-group, and every finitely generated subgroup of it is finite, i.e.
`C` is locally finite. `a notin C` since `lambda(t)=1`.

**Step 15 (nonseparability).** The closure of `C` in the profinite topology of `A` is the
intersection of the sets `NC` over finite-index normal `N`. By Step 13 it contains `a`, and
`a notin C`. So `C` is not closed, i.e. not separable.

Steps 8, 9, 10, 13 and 14 verify the hypotheses of `(LF0)` together with property (T), so Part I
applies to `(LF2)`, including items 1, 2 and 3 with `(ISC1)`. QED
