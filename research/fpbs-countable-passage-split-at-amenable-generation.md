## Why sufficient

Assume every infinite finitely generated group has fixed price, and let
`Gamma` be a countably infinite group. Either `Gamma` is generated by finitely
many amenable subgroups, or it is not.
- In the first case, `fpbs-fixed-price-amenably-generated-from-fg` gives
  fixed price.
- In the second case, `fpbs-fixed-price-non-amenably-generated-from-fg` gives
  fixed price.

Both prerequisites assume the same hypothesis as the target, and their
conclusions cover the two cases. So they give the target. See §4 of the
artifact.

**Honest scope.** The first prerequisite has a complete route
(`fpbs-fixed-price-amenably-generated-from-fg-proof`, via
`fpbs-amalgam-over-amenable-descends-fixed-price` and the Schupp embedding
claim). The second implies the target by restriction. So this route does not
weaken the hypothesis. What it does is shrink the class in which a
counterexample can lie: it removes every finitely generated group, every
countable amenable group, and groups such as `Q * Z` and `SL(2,Q)`. Artifact
§5 explains why amalgamation descent over amenable subgroups cannot reach
further in one step.
