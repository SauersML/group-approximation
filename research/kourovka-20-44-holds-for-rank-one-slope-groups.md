**ESTABLISHED** for the cases stated (lane proofs). The V case and the integer-ray case have been refereed PASS (table below).
Kourovka 20.44 itself stays **OPEN**.

## The problem

Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue (2026), Problem 20.44 (S. Kohl), verbatim:

> The definition of CT(Z) is given in 17.57. Is it true that a finitely generated subgroup of
> CT(Z) either has only finitely many orbits on Z or there is a set of representatives for its
> orbits on Z which has positive density?

- **Source.** First printed in the 20th issue. bh-ref-kourovka-a checked it at source there
  (p. 153).
- **Density.** The positive results give positive *lower* density, which is at least as strong
  as any reading except "natural density exists". The hard side gives *upper* density 0 for
  every transversal. So the picture below does not depend on how "positive density" is read.

## Statement

Let `G ≤ CT(Z)` be finitely generated, so `G ≤ CT_P(Z)` for a finite set `P` of odd primes.
Let `Γ ≤ Q_{>0}` be the group generated by the slopes of `G`'s affine pieces.

| Slope group `Γ` | Kourovka 20.44 for `G` | Node | Referee |
|---|---|---|---|
| `G ≤ CT_∅(Z) ≅ V` (all moduli powers of 2; then `Γ ≤ 2^Z`) | **true** | `ct-empty-z-subgroups-satisfy-the-orbit-transversal-dichotomy` (2676555d5) | **PASS**, bh-ref-kourovka-a (ab9d63e16) |
| `Γ ≤ γ^Z`, `γ ≥ 2` an integer (positive ray; includes `Γ = 1`), any finite `P` | **true** | `ct-p-z-ray-cocycle-subgroups-satisfy-kourovka-20-44` (50b7f0bf8) | **PASS**, bh-ref-kourovka-a (0de1f29ea) |
| `Γ = γ^Z`, `γ` and `1/γ` non-integral (mixed sign, e.g. `3/2`), some point of `Ẑ` has a frozen tail | **true** | `ct-p-z-mixed-ray-frozen-tails-force-finite-orbits` (59f44f3c5) | not yet reviewed |
| `Γ = γ^Z` mixed sign, totally consumable (no frozen tail anywhere) | **OPEN**, of Collatz type | same node, item 3 | — |
| rank `Γ = 2`: Kohl's `G_T = ⟨τ_{0(2),1(2)}, τ_{1(2),2(4)}, τ_{1(4),2(6)}⟩` | **⟺ the 3x+1 map has finitely many components on Z** | `ct-z-orbit-transversal-dichotomy-is-collatz-hard` (7b84a747f) | not yet reviewed |
| any `Γ`, infinitely many orbits of size `≤ s` | **true** | `ct-z-orbit-transversal-counterexamples-need-unbounded-orbits` (1c4a8a43c) | not yet reviewed |

- **Consequence.** 20.44 holds whenever `Γ` has rank at most 1, except in the totally
  consumable mixed-sign case. An affirmative answer in general would prove that the 3x+1 map
  has only finitely many cycles and divergent classes on Z.
- **Constants.** The positive cases are effective. In the positive-ray case `G` has at most
  `2Π^D` orbits, with `Π` the product of the primes in `P ∪ {2}` and `D` the depth of the
  generators' tables, or else the minima have lower density at least `δ/(2A)`.

## One mechanism: frozen tails

Write `Ẑ` as the path space of the one-vertex `k`-graph of `CT_P(Z)`
(`ct-p-z-is-a-one-vertex-k-graph-full-group`). A piece is then a prefix replacement
`λz ↦ μz`, and it carries the tail at a degree `n ≥ d(λ)` unchanged to `n + d(μ) − d(λ)`.
A tail is *frozen* if no word ever reads it. The slope group is the image of this degree
cocycle, and its shape decides everything.
- **Positive ray.** Tails drift along a line through the positive cone. They can be read only
  near the corner `D·1`, where configurations are bounded. So either every orbit meets a fixed
  finite set, or a frozen tail is an orbit invariant with positive-density minima.
  - The one-prime case is the V node. There the tracking must follow individual letters,
    because a word can fix `0^∞` and still shift its tail. The referee tested exactly this.
- **Mixed-sign ray.** A line of mixed direction meets `N^k` in a finite segment. A frozen tail
  is trapped there, and the orbit is finite, with a size bounded by the frozen degree alone.
  - What is left is the regime where every orbit reads every depth.
  - There, reading at the 2-adic end is a descent, and escape to the 3-adic end is a drift
    walk of Collatz type.
- **Rank two.** Carries reach every depth. Kohl's maps `r`, `s` identify the orbits of `G_T`
  with the 3x+1 components. The Terras–Everett parity-vector count makes every transversal
  sparse.

## What remains open

1. **The no-freezing mixed-sign case.** Example: slopes in `(3/2)^Z`, every depth read. Is
   there a counterexample, or a descent argument that forces reading at the contracting end?
2. **General rank two.** Only `G_T` is pinned to a named problem. A proof for any rank-two
   family would have to go beyond stopping times.
3. **Natural density.** This is not addressed on the positive side.

## Lesson for general BH

**The tame/wild line of an arithmetic host is the shape of the degree (slope) cocycle.** It is
not the number of primes, and it is not the carries.
- **Rank one, positive.** Frozen tails are invariants of infinite orbits: tame.
- **Rank one, mixed sign.** Frozen tails force finite orbits: tame wherever anything freezes.
- **Rank two.** Carries read every depth: Collatz.

The same line governs torsion (the ray-cocycle pumping node) and automorphisms (Cobham
rigidity in `out-ct-p-z-is-c2`). So a host construction that must keep an embedded subgroup's
orbits or torsion under control should keep its cocycle rank one. Hardness should be looked
for exactly where every depth is read.
