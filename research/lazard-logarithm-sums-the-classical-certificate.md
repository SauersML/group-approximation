Since `Ax=b` has no solution over the field `Z_p`, linear algebra gives a
row vector `y` with `y^T A = 0` and `y^T b = s != 0` in `Z_p`.

Let `phi: Gamma -> N` be onto, where `N` is finite nilpotent of class `< p`.

**Step 1: reduce to a `p`-group.**
- `N` is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups.  Let `rho: N -> P` be the
  projection onto the Sylow `p`-subgroup, a homomorphism.
- `phi(J)^p=1`, so `phi(J)` lies in `P` and `rho(phi(J))=phi(J)`.
- `P=rho(N)` has class `< p`.

So it is enough to treat `psi=rho o phi: Gamma -> P`, where `P` is a finite
`p`-group of class `c < p`.

**Step 2: the Lazard correspondence.** For a finite `p`-group of class
`< p`, the Lazard correspondence (Lazard 1954; see Khukhro, *p-Automorphisms
of Finite p-Groups*, Ch. 10) gives a nilpotent Lie ring `L` of class `< p`
and a bijection `log: P -> L` with these properties:

- (L1) `log(g^k) = k log g` for all integers `k`;
- (L2) if `gh=hg`, then `[log g, log h]=0` and `log(gh)=log g + log h`.

For (L2): the inverse Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula expresses `[log g,
log h]` as a rational Lie series in group commutators whose denominators are
prime to `p`.  Since `g` and `h` commute, `[log g, log h]=0`.  The truncated
BCH formula then reduces to `log(gh)=log g+log h`.

Moreover, `(L, +)` has exponent dividing that of `P`.  In particular
`p log J' = log(J'^p) = 0` for `J'=psi(J)`.

**Step 3: sum the equations.**
- In equation `i` the factors `psi(x_j)^{A_ij}` pairwise commute, because
  those variables occur together in equation `i`.
- Applying (L2) repeatedly, then (L1), gives
  `sum_j A_ij log psi(x_j) = b_i log J'` in `L`, where coefficients in `Z_p`
  make sense because `p log psi(x_j)=0`.
- Multiply by `y_i` and sum over `i`.  The left side is
  `sum_j (y^T A)_j log psi(x_j) = 0`, so `s log J' = 0`.
- Since `s` is invertible mod `p` and `p log J'=0`, we get `log J'=0`, hence
  `J'=1`.

**Step 4: back to `phi`.** `phi(J)=rho(phi(J))=psi(J)=1`.

**Step 5: consequence 1.** Let `pi` be a finite-dimensional operator solution
with `pi(J)=omega I != I`.
- The generated group `U=<pi(x_j)>` need not be finite.
- Suppose `U` is nilpotent of class `<p`.  It is generated by finitely many
  unitaries, so it is a finitely generated nilpotent group, hence residually
  finite.
- Because `pi(J)` is a nontrivial central element, some finite quotient of `U`
  keeps `pi(J)` nontrivial.
- That quotient is nilpotent of class `<p` and is a quotient of `Gamma` with
  `J` surviving, contradicting the claim.

(The claim is stated for finite quotients; the reduction just given covers
arbitrary finite-dimensional nilpotent images.)

**Calibration.**
- `p=2`: "class `<2`" means abelian.  Abelian images of a classically
  unsatisfiable system kill `J`, and the magic-square Pauli solution has
  class 2.  Consistent.
- `p=3`: GAP finds `J` dead in the class-`<=2` 3-quotients of the Heawood,
  Moebius-Kantor, Pappus and Desargues incidence systems, and alive at class 3
  (`z3-heawood-incidence-system-has-a-finite-dimensional-solution`).
  Consistent.
