**ESTABLISHED** through `thompson-f-x1x0inv-and-x2x0inv-generate-a-copy-of-f-proof` (exact
computation plus two imported standard facts; not independently reviewed; no priority claimed).

Let `x_0, x_1` be the standard generators of `F`, with `x_(n+1) = x_0^-n x_1 x_0^n`, so that
`x_j x_i = x_i x_(j+1)` for `i < j`. Put

`p = x_2 x_0^-1 = x_0^-1 x_1` and `q = x_1 x_0^-1`.

Then `x_0 -> p`, `x_1 -> q` extends to an injective homomorphism `F -> F`. So `<p, q>` is a copy of
`F`, generated by two elements of degree zero for the homomorphism `deg : F -> Z`, `deg x_i = 1`.

As piecewise-linear maps (standard Cannon–Floyd–Parry maps for `x_0, x_1`, product `f·g = f∘g`):
- `p` has breakpoints `(0,0), (1/4,1/2), (1/2,3/4), (3/4,13/16), (7/8,7/8), (1,1)`;
- `q` has breakpoints `(0,0), (1/4,1/2), (3/4,3/4), (1,1)`.

**Use.** If a finite `Z ⊂ F` satisfies `|p Z \ Z| < δ|Z|` and `|q Z \ Z| < δ|Z|`, restricting `Z` to a
right coset of `<p,q>` and pulling back along the isomorphism gives a finite set that is `8δ`-Følner
(in Moore's sense) for `{x_0^±1, x_1^±1}`. This is the step used in
`thompson-f-cardinality-certificates-need-tower-degree`.
