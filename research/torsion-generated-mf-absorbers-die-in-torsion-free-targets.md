**ESTABLISHED.**  For a group `G`, let

```text
TorNor(G)=normalClosure_G({g : g has finite order}).
```

Every homomorphism from `G` to a torsion-free group kills `TorNor(G)`.
Consequently:

1. if `G` is generated by finite-order elements, every homomorphism from
   `G` to a torsion-free group is trivial;
2. if `j:A->G`, `A` is generated by finite-order elements, and

   ```text
   N=normalClosure_G(j(A)),
   ```

   then every torsion-free quotient of `G` kills `N`;
3. this remains true even when `N` contains elements of infinite order:
   being an infinite-order product of torsion generators does not make an
   element survive a homomorphism which kills every generator.

Apply this to `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)`.  Every elementary root satisfies

```text
x_ij(a)^2=x_ij(2a)=1,
```

and `EL_n(R)` is generated by these roots.  Since the established all-ranks
identification gives `R^x ~= GL_n(R)=EL_n(R)` for `n>=2`, every binary-Leavitt
unit, GL, and elementary black hole currently used in Cairn is
torsion-generated.  Thus every homomorphism from any of these groups to a
torsion-free group is trivial.

The same conclusion holds after inserting such a group as an MF absorber:
the normal closure of every homomorphic image of the absorber lies in
`TorNor` of the ambient group and vanishes in every torsion-free quotient.
In particular, Hull saturation cannot turn the binary-Leavitt full-radical
core into the nonzero normal radical required by
`property-t-free-torsion-free-full-mf-radical`: a torsion-free Hull quotient
kills that core before the saturation argument begins.  Free products,
regular star amalgams, graph products, and camouflage attachments do not
evade the obstruction when their invisible kernel is generated by copies of
the same absorber.

This does not obstruct a genuinely torsion-free invisible seed.  It shows
that the independent torsion-free target cannot be obtained by routing the
present characteristic-two black hole, or any normal closure generated by
its images, through a torsion-free quotient.
