title: No infinite finite-index subgroup of an elementary group EL_n(R), n >= 3, is left-orderable
---

**ESTABLISHED.**  Let `R` be an associative unital ring and `n >= 3`.  Let
`EL_n(R)` be the subgroup of `GL_n(R)` generated by the elementary matrices
`e_ij(a)`.  Then no infinite subgroup of finite index in `EL_n(R)` is
left-orderable.

**Why it matters.**  The Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain universal lattices `EL_n(R)`,
with `n >= 3` and `R` finitely generated, are the repository's largest supply
of Kazhdan groups, and property (T) passes to finite-index subgroups.  This
claim rules out every infinite finite-index subgroup of them as a witness for
`infinite-left-orderable-kazhdan-group`.  It says nothing about Kazhdan
subgroups of infinite index.

Derivation: `finite-index-subgroups-of-el-n-are-not-left-orderable-proof`.

## Sharpness

The proof uses two different mechanisms, a lattice in characteristic zero and
torsion in positive characteristic.  The obvious torsion-free relative groups
escape both, but they contain no Kazhdan subgroups at all.

Let `K = ker(SL_n(Z[x]) -> SL_n(Z))` for `x -> 0`, and let
`K_k = ker(SL_n(Z[x]) -> SL_n(Z[x]/(x^k)))`.  Then:
- `K = K_1`, and `[K_a, K_b] <= K_(a+b)`;
- `I + x^k B + O(x^(k+1)) -> B` embeds `K_k/K_(k+1)` in the additive group of integer matrices;
- the intersection of all `K_k` is trivial.

So each `K/K_k` is torsion-free nilpotent, and `K` is residually torsion-free
nilpotent.  A residually torsion-free nilpotent group is bi-orderable, hence
locally indicable.  A locally indicable group has no nontrivial Kazhdan
subgroup, because every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup maps onto `Z`.
