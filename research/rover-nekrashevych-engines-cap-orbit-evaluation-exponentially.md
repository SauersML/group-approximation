**ESTABLISHED** (lane bh-invent-03, 2026-09-19; elementary; not reviewed; no priority claimed). This answers the
coordinator's question on the escape of `finite-type-farley-hughes-groups-are-exponentially-capped`.

## Statement

**Setting.** A Farley–Hughes structure as in that node, with (N) nested domains and (T) finitely many domain types,
but with the structure groups `G_t = 𝕊(R_t, R_t)` now **finitely generated self-similar**: the restriction of a
state to a child, in canonical coordinates, lies again in the structure group of the child's type. Examples are
`V_d(G)` and its vertex version `QV_d(G)` for self-similar `G ≤ Aut(T_d)`, contracting or not, finite-state or not.

1. **Bounded sections.** There is a `c` such that every state word of length `m` has restrictions of length `≤ cm`.
2. **Orbits in exponential time.** For finitely generated `Γ ≤ Γ_S`, a point `x` with a finite address (a singleton
   domain), and a finite union of domains `U`, the relation `w·x ∈ U` is decidable in time `2^(O(|w| + |addr(x)|))`.
3. **Seeds.** For a seed certified by such a structure, as in item 4 of the finite-type node, `w ↦ y_*(w)` is
   computable in time `2^(O(|w|))`. So condition 1 of `hard-evaluation-seeds-with-finitely-presented-full-groups`
   fails for every recursive bound `T` beyond a fixed exponential.
4. **The word problem is not capped.** `WP(Γ)` contains the word problem of each structure group. For a faithful
   self-similar action that is only co-r.e. in general, so this is where hardness can live.

**Scope (corrected after `farley-hughes-complexity-caps-review`, 508b190dbb).** The first version said FH's axioms
force this setting. They do not.
- **What FH's axioms give.** Restrictions of S-maps are S-maps (FH Cor. 3.8). But the structure sets **need not be
  closed under restriction**: FH Remark 4.28 treats that as an extra property.
- **A counterexample in FH.** Their Röver structure (Ex. 4.35) is not closed under restriction, yet it proves Röver's
  group `F_∞` (Prop. 6.24, Ex. 8.4).
- **What item 2 does cover.**
  - Certificates whose structure sets are closed under restriction.
  - Certificates whose self-similar closure, the group generated by all canonical-coordinate sections of
    structure-group elements, is finitely generated. This includes every maximal structure with finitely generated
    maximal structure groups.
- **What stays open.** Certificates with finitely generated but non-restriction-closed structure groups whose
  sections generate an infinitely generated group. There the bound `c` is missing.
- **Also required.** The seed must be a domain with a finite address, as in qualifier 1 of the review.
- **The merger is conditional.** The statements "no hard seed can be certified" and "the seed branch merges into
  `fp-self-similar-groups-with-arbitrarily-hard-word-problem`" are proved only in this corrected scope.

## Proof

1. Let `A` be a finite generating set for the structure groups. For each `a ∈ A` and child index `i`, the restriction
   `a|_i` is a word in `A`. Let `c` be the maximum of their lengths. A word of length `m` restricts to the product of
   the letters' restrictions, which has length `≤ cm`.
2. As in the finite-type node, each generator of `Γ` acts on addresses by swapping a prefix of bounded length and
   then applying a state word of bounded length to the tail.
   - **Cost of one state.** Applying a state word of length `m` to a tail of length `ℓ` means reading the tail letter
     by letter. That permutes each letter and replaces the word by its restriction, so the cost is at most
     `Σ_(k<ℓ) c^k m = O(c^ℓ m)`.
   - **Lengths.** After `j` generators the address has length `≤ |addr(x)| + dj`.
   - **Total.** `Σ_(j≤|w|) O(c^(|addr(x)|+dj)) = 2^(O(|w|+|addr(x)|))`. Membership in `U` is a test on a bounded
     prefix.
3. `y_*(w) = (w^(-1)·y_*)(1)`. Apply 2.
4. `G_t ≤ Γ_S` acts faithfully on the domain tree, and triviality must be checked at every depth. ∎

## What this does to the routes

- **Contracting structure groups.** These are finite-state, so the finite-type node already makes orbits
  quadratic. Their word problems are polynomial too (known; recalled).
- **Non-contracting automaton groups.** These are finite-state, so orbits are quadratic. Their word problem is
  decidable in PSPACE and can be PSPACE-hard (recalled), which is still under the `2^(O(n))` cap of the finite-type
  node.
- **Infinite-state, finitely generated self-similar structure groups.**
  - Orbits are exponential by item 2.
  - `WP(G)` is the only place a super-exponential cost can sit.
  - For a finitely presented `G` it is decidable (`G` is residually finite, by McKinsey), with no uniform bound
    known.
- **Conclusion.**
  - **Seeds.** No hard seed can be certified by any engine with finitely many domain types and finitely generated
    self-similar structure groups: its evaluation is at most exponential.
  - **The hard-actor route.** It must obtain hardness from the word problem of the actor, not from the position of a
    seed point. With Röver–Nekrashevych engines that means from `WP(G)` for finitely presented self-similar `G`,
    i.e. exactly `fp-self-similar-groups-with-arbitrarily-hard-word-problem`.
  - **Conditionally, the two branches merge.** Within the corrected scope, the seed branch of
    `type-a-actors-with-arbitrarily-hard-word-problem` reduces to that branch.

## Lesson for general BH

**Where a point sits is always cheap; only triviality can be hard.**
- **Why position is cheap.** In any finite-type engine, or self-similar engine in the corrected scope, locating a point after `n`
  moves costs at most `2^(O(n))`. Moves act on addresses with bounded prefix changes, and state words whose
  restrictions grow at most geometrically.
- **Why triviality can be hard.** Deciding that an element is trivial requires every depth.
- **The consequence.** Designs that put the input's complexity into a configuration, such as seeds, hierarchies or
  checked sequences, are capped. Designs that put it into the word problem of a structure group are not.
- **So** the live form of the hard-actor question is finitely presented self-similar groups with arbitrarily hard
  word problems.
