Notation as in the target. Let `F_d ⊆ Y` be the union of `|I|` over `I ∈ 𝓘` of length `≤ d`,
so `F_0` is the vertex set and `F_L = Y`.

**Step 1. Pieces.** A simplex (chain) `σ` lies in `F_d \ F_{d−1}` iff `[min σ, max σ]` has
length `d`, because `𝓘` is closed under sub-intervals and length is strictly monotone.
Faces omitting the min or the max have shorter intervals. Hence
`C_*(F_d)/C_*(F_{d−1}) ≅ ⊕_{[I]} Ind_{Γ_I}^{Γ} C_*(|I|, ∂I)`, with
`∂I = |[v,w)| ∪ |(v,w]| ≅ Σ|(v,w)|`.
- Simplex stabilizers fix chains pointwise, since the action is order-preserving and the
  rank separates the vertices of a chain. So there are no orientation signs.
- `|I|` is a cone, so `H_k(|I|, ∂I) ≅ H̃_{k−2}(|(v,w)|)`.

**Step 2. FP_n.**
- By hypothesis 2, each summand is quasi-isomorphic to `Ind Z_χ[e(I)]` (essential) or to `0`
  (inessential). The character `χ` comes from the action of `Γ_I` on the homology.
- By hypothesis 3, `Ind Z_χ` is `FP_{n−e}` when `e ≤ n` (argument of Step 4 of
  `twisted-bt-type-fn-interval-filtration-proof`).
- Step 0 of that route defines `n`-finite complexes and proves the needed facts: modules,
  quasi-isomorphism invariance, sums, extension closure, and the conclusion. With them,
  `C_*(F_0)` and every quotient are `n`-finite by hypotheses 3–4, so `C_*(Y)` is `n`-finite.
- By hypothesis 1 the augmentation `C_*(Y) → Z` is bijective on `H_i` for `i < n` and
  surjective at `n`. So `Γ` is `FP_n`.

**Step 3. Finite presentation.**
- *The cover graph.* Let `C ⊆ Y` be the union of the vertices and the edges `|I|` of the
  cover intervals (those with `(v, w) = ∅`, of any length). Let `F'_d` be `C` together with
  `|I|` for the non-cover intervals of length `≤ d`. Each `F'_d` is a subcomplex, because
  `∂I` of a non-cover interval consists of shorter sub-intervals.
- *Components.* For a non-cover `I`, `(v,w) ≠ ∅`, so `∂I = Σ|(v,w)|` is connected.
  Attaching does not merge components. Since `Y` is connected, `C` is connected.
- *Van Kampen.* The cones `|I|` of one length meet only inside `F'_{d−1}`, so
  `π_1(F'_d) = π_1(F'_{d−1}) / ⟨⟨ π_1(∂I) : ℓ(I) = d ⟩⟩`.
- *Generators of `π_1(∂I)`.* Let `A_1, ..., A_k` be the path components of `|(v,w)|`. Then
  `π_1(ΣA)` is free of rank `k − 1`, because each `ΣA_i` is simply connected. It is
  generated by loops `v → u_1 → w → u_j → v` with `u_i ∈ A_i`. Each leg is a maximal chain
  of the finite interval, hence a chain of covers.
  - Such a leg stays in `|[v,w)|` or in `|(v,w]|`.
  - The class of such a loop depends only on the pair of components.
  - So these loops lie in `C` and generate `π_1(∂I)`.
- *Presentation complex.* Hence `π_1(Y) = π_1(C) / ⟨⟨ ℓ ⟩⟩`, over the finitely many loops
  of this form of each non-cover interval with disconnected open part. Attach a 2-cell to `C` along
  each such oriented based loop, `Γ`-equivariantly. The result is a simply connected
  `Γ`-CW complex. The action is rigid: an element fixing a based oriented loop fixes its
  vertices.
- *Conclusion.* It has finitely many orbits of cells, finitely presented vertex stabilizers
  and finitely generated edge stabilizers. By Brown's presentation theorem (K. S. Brown,
  J. Pure Appl. Algebra 32 (1984); not re-read at source), `Γ` is finitely presented.
