Self-contained proof.

## Step 1. Finite section tables enumerate FR_D

A **table** over `Y_D` is a finite set `Sigma` with, for each `t in Sigma`, a
permutation `pi_t` of `Y_D` and positive words `w_(t,y) in Sigma^*` for
`y in Y_D`.

**It defines automorphisms.** Put `t(y v) = pi_t(y) w_(t,y)(v)`, where a word
acts by composition and the empty word acts trivially. By induction on `n`:

- each `t` restricted to `Y_D^n` is a well-defined bijection of `Y_D^n`;
- it lies over its restriction to `Y_D^(n-1)`.

At level `n` the map is `y v -> pi_t(y) w_(t,y)(v)`. Here `pi_t` is a bijection,
and `w_(t,y)` acts on level `n-1` as a composite of bijections by induction. So
every word over `Sigma` is an automorphism of `T_D`, and the group generated by
`Sigma` is self-similar, since the sections of generators are words.

**Every finitely generated self-similar group arises this way.** Let
`G <= Aut(T_D)` be self-similar and generated by a finite set `Sigma` closed
under inverses. Each section `t|_y` lies in `G`, so it is a positive word over
`Sigma`. Choosing such words gives a table whose automorphisms are the elements
of `Sigma`, and positive words over `Sigma` give all of `G`.

So `FR_D` is the set of automorphisms named by pairs (table over `Y_D`, positive
word). These pairs are computably enumerable. Given a pair and a vertex `v`, the
recursion computes the image of `v`. Fix an enumeration `(D_n, s_n)` of all such
pairs over all `D >= 2`, with uniformly computable portraits.

## Step 2. The construction

Let `sigma_u` be the rigid swap at vertex `u`: it sends `u 0 w -> u 1 w` and
`u 1 w -> u 0 w`, and fixes every other vertex. Swaps at pairwise incomparable
vertices commute and are involutions.

Fix a computable enumeration of the pairs `(e, n)` with `D_e = D_n`. We build
`g = sigma_0 · prod_(k in K) sigma_(1^(m_k) 0)` with `1 <= m_1 < m_2 < ...`, in
stages. The vertices `0` and `1^(m_k) 0` are pairwise incomparable, so `g` is an
involution. It is nontrivial because of `sigma_0`.

**Stage k,** handling the `k`-th pair `(e, n)`.

1. Let `g_(k-1)` be the finite product built so far, with `g_0 = sigma_0` and
   `m_0 = 0`. A swap `sigma_(1^m 0)` fixes every vertex of length `<= m + 1`.
2. Put `P_(k-1) = max over j < k of mu_(e_j)(L_j)`, the protected depth, with
   `P_0 = 0`. Choose `m_k > max(m_(k-1), P_(k-1))`. Let `g' = g_(k-1)` and
   `g'' = g_(k-1) · sigma_(1^(m_k) 0)`.
3. `g' != g''` and `iota_e` is injective, so `iota_e(g')` and `iota_e(g'')`
   differ on some level. Search `L = 0, 1, 2, ...`, computing both portraits on
   `Y_(D_e)^{<=L}` with the functional. The search terminates at some `L_k`.
4. Compute the action of `s_n` on `Y_(D_e)^{<=L_k}`. It differs there from at
   least one of `iota_e(g')`, `iota_e(g'')`. Let `g_k` be that one, taking `g'`
   when both differ, so that `k in K` iff `g_k = g''`.

**Protection.** Every later stage changes `g` only by swaps at vertices
`1^(m_j) 0` with `m_j > mu_e(L_k)`, and those swaps fix `{0,1}^{<=mu_e(L_k)}`
pointwise. So the final `g` agrees with `g_k` on `{0,1}^{<=mu_e(L_k)}`. By level
locality, `iota_e(g)` agrees with `iota_e(g_k)` on `Y_(D_e)^{<=L_k}`, which is
where `iota_e(g_k)` differs from `s_n`. Hence `iota_e(g) != s_n`.

## Step 3. Conclusion

**Computability.** To compute `g(v)`, run the stages until `m_k >= |v|`. This
terminates, because every stage terminates by Step 2.3 and `m_k` is increasing.
The swaps added afterwards fix `v`, so `g(v) = g_k(v)`.

**Escape.** Fix `e`. Every element of `FR_(D_e)` is some `s_n` with
`D_n = D_e`, and the pair `(e, n)` is handled at some stage. So
`iota_e(g) notin FR_(D_e)`.

**The listed members form one uniformly computable family.** For the members
listed in the claim:

- **Level locality and injectivity.** Alphabet enlargement has `mu(N) = N`.
  Padding along `f` has `mu(N) = #{i : f(i) <= N}`. Conjugation by a
  finite-state automorphism `c` has `mu(N) = N`. Blocking into `T_(2^k)` has
  `mu(N) = kN`. Each is injective.
- **Uniformity.** Finite composites of maps from computable lists are uniformly
  computable. The increasing primitive recursive schedules are enumerated as
  `f(i) = i + h(1) + ... + h(i)`, with `h` ranging over an enumeration of the
  primitive recursive functions.

The theorem applies to the union of all these lists.

## What the proof does not reach

The proof needs a computable list of total injective re-encodings, because Step
2.3 waits for `iota_e` to separate `g'` from `g''`. A partial list, such as
"pad by the running time of the given portrait algorithm", can diverge at that
step. That is exactly the room left for input-dependent re-encodings.
