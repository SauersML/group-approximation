Exactness of a countable discrete group is equivalent to the existence of a
topologically amenable action on a compact metrizable space.  For
completeness, metrizability can be retained in the usual Stone--Cech proof:
choose amenability witnesses for an increasing sequence of finite subsets of
`G` and errors tending to zero, and take the separable unital
`G`-invariant subalgebra of `ell_infinity(G)` generated by all their
coefficient functions.  Its spectrum is a compact metrizable amenable
`G`-space `X`.

We record directly why a nonamenable group leaves no invariant measure on
such a space.  Let

```text
m_i:X -> Prob(G)
```

be continuous amenability witnesses, so for each `g in G`,

```text
sup_(x in X) ||g m_i(x)-m_i(gx)||_1 ->0.               (R1)
```

If `mu` were a `G`-invariant probability measure on `X`, put

```text
M_i=integral_X m_i(x) dmu(x) in Prob(G).
```

Invariance of `mu` and `(R1)` give

```text
||gM_i-M_i||_1
 <=integral_X ||g m_i(x)-m_i(gx)||_1 dmu(x) ->0.       (R2)
```

Thus `(M_i)` is a Reiter net and `G` is amenable, a contradiction.  Hence
the amenable compact action has no invariant probability measure.  Taking

```text
A=C(X)
```

gives a commutative unital separable nuclear algebra.  Its tracial states
are exactly probability measures on `X`, and invariance agrees on both
sides, proving `(1)=>(2)=>(3)=>(4)` for nonamenable `G`.

Conversely, if `G` acts amenably on a unital nuclear algebra `A`, then
`A crossed_r G` is nuclear.  The canonical unitaries give an embedding of
`C*_r(G)` into this nuclear algebra, so `C*_r(G)` is exact and therefore
`G` is exact.  This proves `(4)=>(1)`.
