**Essential freeness.** `(γ,λ) != 1` fixes `Ah` iff `h(γ,λ)h^(-1) ∈ A`. For fixed
`a ∈ A` and `λ'' ∈ Λ'' \ {1}`, the set `{h : hλ''h^(-1) = a}` is empty or a coset of the centralizer
of `λ''`, a closed subgroup of positive codimension, hence Haar null. There are countably many
pairs. The same argument handles `A ↷ H/Λ''`.

**Item 1, duality.** On `H` with Haar measure, let `E` be the countable Borel equivalence relation
`h ~ a h λ''` with `a ∈ A`, `λ'' ∈ Λ''`. It preserves Haar measure, because `H` is unimodular. Let
`D_A` be a Borel fundamental domain for left `A`-translation and `D''` one for right
`Λ''`-translation. Both have finite measure, since `A` and `Λ''` are lattices, and each meets every
`E`-class. On `D_A ≅ A\H`, two points are `E`-equivalent iff they differ by right `Λ''`-translation
modulo `A`, so `E|_(D_A)` is the orbit relation of `(FF1)`. Likewise `E|_(D'')` is the orbit relation
of `A ↷ H/Λ''`. Put `W = D_A ∪ D''`, a finite-measure set, and `R = E|_W`. Item 1 of
`sofic-free-actions-are-soe-invariant` (amplification, with Cordeiro's restriction) gives

```text
R|_(D_A) sofic   ⇔   R sofic   ⇔   R|_(D'') sofic.
```

Both actions are essentially free. By item 2 of the same node, soficity of each action equals
soficity of its orbit relation, so `(FF1)` is sofic iff `A ↷ H/Λ''` is sofic. The projection
`H/Λ'' → PSL_2(Q_2)/Λ` is `A`-equivariant and measure preserving, so the frame dynamics is a
factor.

**Factors.** If `L^∞(Z) <= L^∞(X)` is an invariant subalgebra, the von Neumann algebra generated by
`L^∞(Z)` and the `u_g` inside `L^∞(X) ⋊ A` is trace-preservingly `L^∞(Z) ⋊ A`. Restricting a sofic
embedding (Păunescu Definition 1.4) keeps the base diagonal and the `u_g` permutation matrices. So
factors of sofic actions are sofic. This is the argument recorded in
`kun-thom-free-nonsofic-action-proof`, Step 2.

**Item 2.** Suppose `(FF1)` is sofic. Then `A ↷ H/Λ''` is sofic, and so is its factor
`A ↷ PSL_2(Q_2)/Λ`. By item 3 ⇒ item 1 of `dyadic-vertex-soficity-equals-frame-dynamics-soficity`,
the vertex action is sofic. ∎

**Item 3.** This is the contrapositive of item 2, together with essential freeness. For `F_2 × F_2`:
if `F_2 × F_r` sits with finite index in `F_2 × F_2` and `Z` is a nonsofic essentially free action
of it, then the induced action `F_2 × F_2 ×_(F_2 × F_r) Z` is essentially free. Its restriction to
`F_2 × F_r` contains `Z` as an invariant non-null subset. A sofic action restricts to sofic actions
on invariant non-null subsets, by Păunescu's Definition 1.4 cut down by the projection. So the
induced action is nonsofic. ∎
