In **every** ring `A` carrying a binary Leavitt family the additive subgroup
generated by the values of the units is all of `A`.  No characteristic
hypothesis, no countability, no coefficient field.

Specialized to `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)` and its unit group `U = R^x`, signs vanish
and multiplicities reduce mod 2, so every element of `R` is an `F_2`-linear
combination of units, and the evaluation map

    pi : A = F_2[U] -> R,     pi([u]) = u

is a **surjective** unital `F_2`-algebra homomorphism.

## Why this is more than "the units sit inside the algebra"

It is the one statement that puts the whole of the non-directly-finite algebra
(`binary-leavitt-algebra-not-directly-finite`) in the image of a *group*
algebra, which is where Kaplansky's conjecture lives.  Everything else in this
lane is about the distance between that image and the group algebra itself:
`direct-finiteness-not-inherited-by-quotients` says the surjection alone
transports nothing, and `leavitt-corner-idempotent-unital-surjection` sharpens
the surjection to a unital one out of an explicit corner.

The kernel-checked form is `LeavittFamily.unitAdditiveSpan_eq_top`.  Note that
it makes the *coefficient* field irrelevant to the spanning statement — only
the passage from an additive combination of units to an `F_2`-combination uses
characteristic two, and only to drop signs.
