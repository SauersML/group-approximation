## Setting

- **Rule.** A translation-invariant binary linear rule `L` on `Z^d` has a finite variable alphabet `V` and finitely many context types `c`. Each type is a finite set `S_c ⊂ V × [0,R]^d`, for a fixed `R >= 1`, with a parity `b_c in F_2`. Its placements are `S_c + a` for `a in Z^d`.
- **Solution group.** `Sol(L)` is generated by involutions `X_(v,y)` and a central involution `J`. Variables in a common placement commute, and their product is `J^(b_c)`.
- **Host.** `Gamma(L) = Sol(L) ⋊ Z^d`, with `Z^d` acting by translation. It is finitely presented by `x_v = X_(v,0)`, `j = J` and commuting `t_1,...,t_d`, with `X_(v,y) := t^y x_v t^(-y)` and `t^y = t_1^(y_1)...t_d^(y_d)`. The relators come from the origin placements (list (H1)--(H5) in the route).
- **Box system.** `L_N` is the finite linear system on the sites `B_N = [0,N)^d` whose placements are those contained in `B_N`. Its solution group is `P_N`.
- **Models.** An `(eps,J)`-model of a finitely presented group with a marked central involution `J` assigns unitary matrices to the generators. Every defining relator must be within `eps` of `I`, and `J` within `eps` of `-I`, in the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
- **R^U-satisfiable at `J`.** `(eps,J)`-models exist for every `eps > 0`. Equivalently, there is a unital *-homomorphism into `R^U` sending `J` to `-1` (see the route). For a finite linear system this is Paddock--Slofstra's R^U-satisfiability of the linear system game.

## Theorem

Let `L_c >= 4` be the maximal length of a relator of type (H2)--(H5), as a word in the `X_(v,y)` and `j`.

1. **Box to host, exactly periodic.** An `(eps,J)`-model of `P_N` gives an `(eps + 2 sqrt(dR/N), J)`-model of `Gamma(L)`. In it the `t_i` commute exactly and `t_i^N = I`, so it is also a model of the torus quotient `Gamma(L)/<<t_i^N>>`.
2. **Host to box.** An `(eps,J)`-model of `Gamma(L)` gives a `((1 + 3 L_c d^2 N R) eps, J)`-model of `P_N`.
3. **Equivalence.** `Gamma(L)` is R^U-satisfiable at `J` iff every `P_N` is. R^U-satisfiability of `P_N` is inherited by every smaller box.
4. **Exact side.** `J != 1` in `Gamma(L)` iff `J != 1` in `Sol(L)` iff `J != 1` in every `P_N`.
5. **Gap localization.** The following are equivalent:
   - `J != 1` in `Gamma(L)` but `Gamma(L)` is not R^U-satisfiable at `J`;
   - `J != 1` in every `P_N`, and some `P_(N_0)` is not R^U-satisfiable at `J`.

   In that case every box system `L_N` with `N >= N_0` has `J != 1` in its solution group, yet is not R^U-satisfiable at `J`.

## Consequences

- **A tiling gap is already a finite-system gap.** For finite linear systems, `J != 1` is tracial satisfiability (Cleve--Liu--Slofstra, as quoted by Paddock--Slofstra; see `research/artifacts/ex-nh-mipstar-quantum-symmetry-rung-2026-09-12.md` §1). So a gap in a tiling host hands over `P_(N_0)`, a finite linear system with free boundary that is tracially but not R^U-satisfiable. By `lcs-tracial-ru-gap-equiv-nonhyperlinear` that is a finitely presented nonhyperlinear group. The host adds nothing.
- **Aperiodicity is invisible to approximation.** Item 1 turns box models into exactly periodic torus models, at defect `eps + 2 sqrt(dR/N)`. So the absence of exact periodic modules is not a source of non-approximability.
  - The round-2 criterion "no finite-dimensional periodic module with `J = -I`" (`research/artifacts/ideas-2026-09-13/nh/nh-games.md`, Approach 4) is necessary for a gap, but it carries none of it.
  - A gap must be a uniform approximation failure on one finite box.
- **Compactness picture.** This is the approximate-model form of compactness in the domino problem. Aperiodic tile sets matter classically only because exact periodic models are demanded. Approximate models give up exactness at the seam of the torus, and that loss costs `O(sqrt(R/N))`.
