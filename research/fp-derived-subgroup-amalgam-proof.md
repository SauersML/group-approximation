Choose a finite presentation of H and discard any identity generators.
For each generator h choose a finitely presented group B_h containing an
element b_h in [B_h,B_h] of the same order.

If h has infinite order, use the integral Heisenberg group and its
infinite-order central commutator. If h has finite order m>=2, use
the alternating group A_(m+3), which is perfect. For odd m an m-cycle
has order m and is even; for even m multiply an m-cycle by a disjoint
transposition. The resulting even permutation has order m.

Form the iterated amalgamated free product of H with the finitely many
B_h by identifying the cyclic subgroup generated by h with that generated
by b_h. The cyclic subgroups are isomorphic by the order choice, so the
normal form theorem for amalgamated free products keeps all vertex groups
embedded. A finite presentation is the union of the vertex presentations
plus one identification per h. Each h is now a commutator-subgroup element
in its B_h, so the embedded H lies in [T,T]. For trivial H take trivial T.

This is an existence construction; no algorithm deciding generator orders
from an arbitrary finite presentation is asserted.
[Chatterji--Kassabov Lemma 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22907v1)
uses the same finite graph-of-groups construction.
