## Why sufficient

Suppose `phi(zeta)` has finite order.  Put `Delta' = phi(Delta)`, a
countable subgroup of `M`, hence MF, and `L' = phi(L)`, a quotient of a
Kazhdan group, hence Kazhdan.  Homomorphisms carry compressors of `L`
to compressors of `L'`, centralizers to centralizers, and elements of
`L` to elements of `L'`, so

```text
phi( D_Delta(L) ) <= D_(Delta')(L').
```

Hence `K = <phi(zeta)>` is a finite subgroup of `Delta'`, central since
`zeta` is central, so normal and Kazhdan, and `K <= D_(Delta')(L')`.
The prerequisite, applied to `Delta'` with the inclusion `Delta' -> M`
as the homomorphism to an MF group, kills `K`.  So `phi(zeta) = 1`.
