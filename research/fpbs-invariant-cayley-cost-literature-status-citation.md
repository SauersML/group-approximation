Citation import. Nothing is re-proved. Both papers were read on 2026-09-17 from arXiv HTML, fetched with curl and
stripped of tags, with math given as alttext.

- **Item 1.** T. Hutchcroft, G. Pete, *Kazhdan groups have cost 1*, arXiv:1810.11015.
  - Theorem 1.2: "Let $\Gamma$ be a countably infinite Kazhdan group. Then $\Gamma$ has cost $1$ ."
  - Introduction: "The proof of Theorem 1.2 establishes that for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every finite symmetric
    generating set $S$ of $\Gamma$ , there is a $\Gamma$ -invariant measure on connected, spanning subgraphs of the
    associated Cayley graph with average degree at most $2+\varepsilon$ ."
  - Introduction: "Our construction is very far from being a factor of i.i.d., and therefore seems unsuitable to study
    $\operatorname{cost}^{*}(\Gamma)$".
- **Items 2 and 3.** R. Beringer, G. Pete, Á. Timár, *On percolation critical probabilities and unimodular random
  graphs*, arXiv:1609.07043.
  - "The cost of a group $\mathbb{G}$ is defined as half of the infimum of the expected degrees of its invariant
    connected spanning graphs. The $\mathbb{G}$ -cost of a transitive graph $G$ may be defined similarly, over
    $\mathbb{G}$ -invariant random connected spanning sub graphs of $G$ , where $\mathbb{G}\leq Aut(G)$ is a
    vertex-transitive subgroup of graph-automorphisms. It is not known in general that, if we first fix a Cayley graph
    $G$ of $\mathbb{G}$ , then the $\mathbb{G}$ -cost of $G$ is always as small as the cost of $\mathbb{G}$ (which is
    the cost of the complete graph on $\mathbb{G}$ ). Nevertheless, we have seen that cost 1 can be achieved inside any
    Cayley graph of any amenable group (since the expected degree of an infinite unimodular tree with at most two ends
    is 2)."
