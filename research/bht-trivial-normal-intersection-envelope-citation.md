Fournier-Facio--Wu--Zaremsky, [*Abstract twisted Brin--Thompson
groups*](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24687v2), revision dated 24 August 2026,
prove exactly this in Appendix A: Lemma A.1 gives the three equivalent normal-
intersection formulations, Proposition A.4 gives the equivalence with
solvability of the word problem, and Corollary A.5 gives the intermediate
finitely generated simple group `G`.

Their forward construction starts with Boone--Higman--Thompson
`Gamma <= G <= P_0`, chooses `1 != g in G`, and uses Lemma A.2 to embed the
finitely presented `P_0` in a finitely presented `P` normally generated by
`g`.  Simplicity of `G` makes every nonidentity element of `G` normally
generate `P`; hence every proper normal subgroup of `P` misses `G`, and in
particular misses `Gamma`.

Conversely, for `w in Gamma`, enumerate proofs of `w=1` in a finite
presentation of `P` while simultaneously enumerating consequences of the
presentation with the extra relator `w=1`.  If `w != 1`, it normally
generates `P`, so the second enumeration eventually proves every generator
trivial.  Exactly one search terminates, deciding the word problem.  `\u220e`
