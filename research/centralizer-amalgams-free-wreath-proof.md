Standard facts used, not re-read: universal properties of free products, amalgams
and semidirect products; a subfamily of free factors generates their free product.

**(W).** Let `W = P ≀*_{A/C} A`, `ω_0 = C`.
- `Φ: A *_C (C × P) → W`. On `A` take the inclusion. On `C × P` take
  `(c, p) ↦ p_{ω_0} c`. This is a homomorphism: `c` fixes `ω_0`, so
  `c p_{ω_0} c^{-1} = p_{ω_0}` and the two factors commute. The two maps agree on
  `C`, so `Φ` is defined.
- `Ψ: W → A *_C (C × P)`. On `A` take the inclusion. On `P_{aC}` take
  `p_{aC} ↦ a p a^{-1}`. This does not depend on the representative: if `a' = ac`
  with `c ∈ C`, then `a' p a'^{-1} = a (c p c^{-1}) a^{-1} = a p a^{-1}`, because `P`
  commutes with `C` in `C × P`. The maps on the factors extend to the free product
  `*_ω P_ω`. They are compatible with the `A`-action:
  `Ψ(b · p_{aC}) = Ψ(p_{baC}) = (ba) p (ba)^{-1} = b Ψ(p_{aC}) b^{-1}`. So `Ψ` extends
  to the semidirect product.
- `ΨΦ` is the identity on `A` and sends `(c,p)` to `p c = (c,p)`. `ΦΨ` is the identity
  on `A` and sends `p_{aC}` to `a p_{ω_0} a^{-1} = p_{aC}`. Both composites are the
  identity on generators, so `Φ` is an isomorphism.

**(S).** Let `O = M ω_0`. The map `M/C_M → O`, `mC_M ↦ mω_0`, is an `M`-equivariant
bijection. The factors `P_ω` (`ω ∈ O`) generate their free product `F_O`, a free
factor of `*_Ω P_ω`, and `M` permutes them, so `F_O ⋊ M` is a subgroup of `P ≀*_Ω A`
(it meets `*_Ω P_ω` in `F_O`, and `M` meets `*_Ω P_ω` trivially). It contains `M` and
`P_{ω_0}`, and it is generated by them since `m P_{ω_0} m^{-1} = P_{mω_0}`. The
equivariant bijection identifies it with `P ≀*_{M/C_M} M`, and (W) finishes.
