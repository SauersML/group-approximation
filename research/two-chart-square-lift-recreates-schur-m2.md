# A two-chart square lift exposes raw holonomy only by recreating the Schur M2

ESTABLISHED. Let `J` be central, let `q^2=1`, and work on the marked sector
`J=-1`. Suppose a chart transport `t` crosses the two Schur children:

```text
t q t^-1=Jq=-q.                                         (TCL1)
```

Put `p_+=(1+q)/2` and `p_-=(1-q)/2`. Then

```text
v=p_- t p_+,     v^*v=p_+,     vv^*=p_-.               (TCL2)
```

Thus `p_+,p_-,v,v^*` are matrix units. The operator algebra generated by
`q,t` already contains the full `M_2` joining the two child multiplicity
lines. This conclusion does not require `t` to be an involution.

With a reverse edge `t_1`, the closed word

```text
h=t_1^-1 t                                               (TCL3)
```

commutes with `q` and its two diagonal compressions are the raw relative
transport holonomies. If the finite packet part of the return is a known
intertwiner `c`, then `g=c^-1h` is the corresponding raw multiplicity word,
and `g^2=J` is exactly the phase-orienting clause. Equivalently a single
swap can be written, after choosing child coordinates, as

```text
t = [ 0  A ] ,       t^2=[ AB  0 ],                     (TCL4)
    [ B  0 ]              [ 0  BA ]
```

so `t^4=J` imposes the required square relation on both conjugate return
blocks, up to any explicitly known finite-packet scalar.

The construction is algebraically consistent. On `C^2`, take

```text
J=-I,       q=diag(1,-1),       t=[0 1; i 0].            (TCL5)
```

Then `(TCL1)` holds, `t^2=iI`, and `t^4=J`. Hence failure is not collapse of
the mark or loss of exact completeness. It is precisely Morita cancellation:
`(TCL2)` recombines the two child lines into one spin block before the square
clause can charge one child as an exit. This is the same factor-two surplus
consumed by a branch flip, now without assuming the flip has order two.

There is an exact dichotomy. If a stable letter satisfies `(TCL1)`, it exposes
the desired child-relative raw loop but generates the forbidden `M_2`. If it
does not exchange the `q` sectors, an auxiliary two-chart loop may safely
expose a raw holonomy, but it does not compare the two coordinate embeddings
whose relative class is the Schur datum `(BRH4)`. Identifying it with that
datum requires a child-crossing intertwiner and returns to `(TCL2)`.

The existing Atlas rectangle stable-letter loops do not evade the dichotomy.
On the canonical common-`U` lift every such rectangle is exactly `1`; on a
factor-level lift it changes under an independent right gauge. Hence it is
not a canonical raw representative of the Schur relative holonomy. A viable
raw-lift compiler must therefore expose phase without a unitary equivalence
between the two child central projections; a two-chart HNN square cannot do
so.
