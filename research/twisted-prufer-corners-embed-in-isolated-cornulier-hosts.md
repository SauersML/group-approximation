## Imports, read at source

**Abels, LNM 1261 (1987), Introduction** (PDF via MSI, institutional access):
- **0.2.5 (Behr).** "If G is reductive every S-arithmetic subgroup is finitely presented."
- **0.2.8 (Kneser 1964).** The S-arithmetic subgroup of a linear algebraic `Q`-group `G` is
  finitely presented iff `G_{Q_p}` is compactly presented for every `p ∈ S`.

**Cornulier, arXiv:math/0502140v4** (pp. 1–9 read):
- **Definition 2.4.** `G` is the algebraic group of upper triangular matrices with blocks
  `(n_1, n_2, n_3, n_4)`, diagonal blocks `I_(n_1)`, `SL_(n_2)`, `SL_(n_3)`, `I_(n_4)`, and
  `n_2, n_3 >= 3`. The prime `p` is arbitrary.
- **Centre.** The centre of `G` is the corner block `(*)_14`.
- **Conjugation.** `GL_(n_1)` acts on `G` by conjugation, with `A_12 ↦ uA_12`,
  `A_13 ↦ uA_13`, `A_14 ↦ uA_14`.
- **Theorem 3.1 (Abels).** For unipotent-by-split-semisimple `G` with no rank-one factor,
  `G(Q_p)` is compactly presented iff `H_1(u)^S = 0` and `H_2(u)^S = 0`.
- **Lemmas 3.2, 3.6.** These verify the two conditions. The weights lie in
  `Z^(n_2+n_3)/P`, independent of `p`. Conclusion: "Γ is finitely presented".
- Hence `G(Q_q)` is compactly presented for every prime `q`.

## Statement

**Notation.**
- Let `𝐇` be Cornulier's group with blocks `(r, 3, 3, 1)`.
- For `R = Z[1/m]`, let `H = 𝐇(R)` and `V = (*)_14 ≅ R^r`, the centre of `H`.
- For `Δ ≤ GL_r(R)`, let `Δ` act on `H` by conjugation with `diag(Ã, I, I, 1)`, `Ã ∈ Δ`, and
  put `P = H · Δ ≤ GL_(r+7)(R)`. Then `H ∩ Δ = 1`, and `Δ` acts on `V` by `v ↦ Ãv`.

**Datum.** A *twisted Prüfer datum* is `(m, r, Δ, L)` with:
- `Δ ≤ GL_r(R)` finitely presented;
- `L ⊆ R^r` a `Δ`-invariant subgroup, satisfying:
  - (i) `L` is finitely generated as a `Z[Δ]`-module;
  - (ii) `L` contains no nonzero `R`-submodule of `R^r`;
  - (iii) `R^r/L` is finitely cogenerated (a finite group times finitely many Prüfer groups).

Its *radical* is `D = R^r/L` with the `Δ`-action.

**Theorem A (hosts).** For every twisted Prüfer datum, `Γ = P/L` is finitely presented and
finitely discriminable, hence isolated. It contains `D` as the normal subgroup `V/L`, on which
`Δ` acts as prescribed.

**Corollary A′ (one universal host per prime).** Let `p` be a prime, `m'` coprime to `p`, and
`r >= 1`. Then
`Γ_(r,p,m') = (𝐇(Z[1/pm']) ⋊ GL_r(Z[1/m'])) / Z[1/m']^r`
is isolated.
- It contains `(Q_p/Z_p)^r = Z[1/pm']^r / Z[1/m']^r` twisted by all of `GL_r(Z[1/m'])`, and so
  by every finitely generated subgroup of it.
- `GL_r(Z[1/m'])` is finitely presented by Behr, and `Z[1/m']^r` is generated by `e_1` as a
  module.
- **What this covers:**
  - (a) scalar S-units;
  - (b) units of any number field `K` acting on `O_K[1/m'] ≅ Z[1/m']^d` by their
    multiplication matrices. This is Weil restriction, and works for split, inert or ramified
    `p` alike;
  - (c) Jordan blocks and any other non-semisimple integral twists.

**Theorem B (reduction for soluble minimax groups).** Let `G` be finitely generated soluble
minimax, `τ = τ(G)`, `D ≤ τ` its divisible part, `D = ⊕_p D_p`. Then `G` embeds in an
isolated group iff for each of the finitely many primes `p` there is a homomorphism `f_p`
from `G` to an isolated group that is injective on `D_p[p]`. It suffices that `f_p` maps to
some `Γ` of Theorem A.

**Examples.** Each is a Heisenberg group `N ⋊ Q` modulo a corner lattice. Each embeds in a
host of Theorem A: `x` is the first column of `A_12`, `y` the first entry of `A_24`, and
`z = xy` lies in `V`.
- **`G_2`:** `r = 1`, `R = Z[1/2p]`, `Δ = ⟨2⟩`, `L = Z[1/2]`.
- **`G_λ`:** `r = 2`, `R = Z[1/11]^2 ≅ O[1/11]` with `O = Z[(1+√5)/2]`,
  `Δ = ⟨M_λ, M_(π')⟩`, `L = O[1/π']`. Here `D ≅ C_(11^∞)` is twisted by an irrational
  11-adic unit.
- **Inert prime:** `K = Q(√2)` and `p = 3`, which is inert. `r = 2`, `Δ = ⟨M_(1+√2)⟩`,
  `L = Z[√2]`, and `D = K_3/O_3 ≅ (Q_3/Z_3)^2`, twisted by a unit with no invariant rank-one
  Prüfer subgroup.
- **Jordan block:** `r = 2`, `R = Z[1/p]`, `Δ = ⟨J⟩` with `J = [[1,1],[0,1]]`, `L = Z^2`.

In each case:
- `s ↦ diag(1, A, I, 1)` with `A = diag(q^{-1}, q, 1) ∈ SL_3(R)` for the relevant prime `q`
  (`q = p` for `G_2`, `q = 11` for `G_λ`), which scales `x` by `q` and `y` by `q^{-1}`;
- the twist generator maps to `diag(Ã, I, I, 1)` with `Ã ∈ Δ`;
- the Heisenberg relation holds because `A_12 A_24 = A_14`.

## Proof

**Theorem A, finite presentation.**
- `𝐇(Q_q)` is compactly presented for every prime `q` (Cornulier). So `𝐇(R)` is finitely
  presented by Kneser (0.2.8), with `S` the primes dividing `m`.
- `P` is an extension of `H` by the finitely presented `Δ`.
- `L ⊆ V` is central in `H` and `Δ`-invariant, so it is normal. Its normal closure is its
  `Z[Δ]`-span, so by (i) `Γ` is finitely presented.

**Theorem A, every nontrivial normal `M ◁ Γ` meets `D = V/L`.**
- **Blocks.** Write `U` for the unipotent radical, with blocks `A_12, A_13, A_14, A_23, A_24,
  A_34`. Then `U' = u_13 ⊕ u_24 ⊕ u_14` (Cornulier). The upper central series is
  `Z_1 = u_14 = V` and `Z_2 = u_13 ⊕ u_24 ⊕ u_14`.
- **(A) `M` meets the image of `U`.**
  - An element `g ∈ M` outside it has Levi part `(Ã, B_2, B_3, 1) ≠ 1`.
  - This part acts on `U/U' = u_12 ⊕ u_23 ⊕ u_34` by `X ↦ ÃXB_2^{-1}`, `B_2XB_3^{-1}`,
    `B_3X`.
  - Trivial action forces `Ã = λI`, `B_2 = B_3 = λI` and `λ = 1`, from the last block. So some
    elementary `u` has `[g, u] ∉ U'`. Since `L ⊆ V ⊆ U'`, this commutator is a nontrivial
    element of `M ∩ Ū`.
- **(B) Descent.**
  - If `h ∈ U − Z_2`, one of `A_12, A_23, A_34` is nonzero, and a commutator with an
    elementary matrix has a nonzero `u_13` or `u_24` component. It lies in `Z_2 − Z_1`,
    hence outside `L`.
  - If `h ∈ Z_2 − Z_1`, then `A_13 ≠ 0` or `A_24 ≠ 0`, and `[h, e_34(Y)] = A_13Y` or
    `[e_12(Y), h] = YA_24`.
  - As `Y` varies, these fill a nonzero `R`-submodule of `V`. By (ii) it is not inside `L`,
    so `M ∩ D ≠ 0`.
- **(C) Conclusion.** `M ∩ D` is a nonzero subgroup of the finitely cogenerated group `D`
  (iii). So it contains an element of a fixed finite cogenerating set, which therefore
  discriminates `Γ`.

**Corollary A′.**
- (i) holds with the generator `e_1`.
- (ii) holds because `Z[1/m']^r` has no nonzero `Z[1/pm']`-submodule, as `p`-divisibility
  fails.
- (iii) holds because `Z[1/pm']^r / Z[1/m']^r ≅ (Q_p/Z_p)^r`.
- `Δ = GL_r(Z[1/m'])` is finitely presented by Behr.

**Theorem B.**
- **Only if.** An embedding into an isolated group is one of the `f_p`.
- **Injectivity on `D`.** A nonzero subgroup of `D` contains an element of prime order `q`,
  lying in `D_q[q]`. So `(f_p)_p` is injective on `D`.
- **The finite part.** `K = ker(f) ∩ τ` meets `D` trivially, so it is finite, and it embeds in
  `G/D`. Now `τ(G/D) = τ/D` is finite, so `G/D` is residually finite (Pyber–Segal §3, as in
  `isolated-embedding-of-soluble-minimax-groups-via-max-n-hosts`). Some finite quotient
  `q : G → F` is therefore injective on `K`.
- **The rest.** `G/τ` is residually finite, hence linear, so it embeds in an isolated `I'`
  (`linear-and-metabelian-groups-embed-in-isolated-groups`).
- **Conclusion.** `g ↦ ((f_p(g))_p, q(g), gτ)` is injective into a product of isolated
  groups, which is isolated (`ascending-hnn-of-isolated-groups-embed-in-isolated-groups`,
  item 1).

**Examples.**
- Each map is injective on `N`, since `N` embeds in `U`, and on the torus part, and it sends
  the defining lattice exactly onto `L`.
- The data satisfy (i)–(iii):
  - for `G_λ`, see `partial-torus-abels-quotients-are-isolated`;
  - for the inert case, `O[1/3]/O ≅ K_3/O_3` is `(Q_3/Z_3)^2`, and `Z[√2]` contains no
    `Z[1/3]`-submodule;
  - for the Jordan case, (i)–(iii) are as in A′. `∎`

## Where the inputs are needed

- **The only non-elementary input is finite presentation of the core:**
  - Cornulier's verification of Abels' Theorem 3.1 at one prime;
  - Kneser's criterion to pass to several primes;
  - Behr's theorem, only for the universal twist group in A′.
- **Everything else is elementary:** isolation by commutator descent, the Weil restriction,
  the Jordan case, and the finite part.
- **Not needed:** compact presentability over any `K_v ≠ Q_q`, strong approximation, and
  max-n.

## What remains

**Corner realizability.** For each prime `p`, a homomorphism from `G` to some `Γ` of
Theorem A (or to any isolated group) that is injective on `D_p[p]`.
- It holds for every finitely generated subgroup of a `Γ`, and hence for every generalized
  Hall group, whose Prüfer radical is the corner quotient of a Heisenberg pairing `z = xy`.
- A proof for all `G` would have to show that the divisible radical of a finitely generated
  soluble minimax group is always reached by such a pairing, up to maps that stay injective
  on `D_p[p]`. That is not proved here.

## Lesson for general BH

**One universal isolated host per prime.**
- `Γ_(r,p,m')` carries the `p`-Prüfer radical `(Q_p/Z_p)^r` twisted by the whole S-arithmetic
  group `GL_r(Z[1/m'])`.
- Weil restriction turns every number-field twist into an integral matrix twist, and Jordan
  blocks are just more integral matrices.

**What Question 4 needs, for inputs whose non-residually-finite part is a minimax radical.**
- For each prime, one arithmetic homomorphism reaching that radical.
- The finite part is free (residual finiteness of `G/D`).
- Finite presentation is paid once, by Kneser and Abels, in the core.
- Boone–Higman additionally needs these explicit isolated groups to embed in simple groups.
