Run the exact packet audit.  It works entirely with `4 x 4` matrices over
`F2`, so subgroup closure, intersections, centers and generated orders are
finite exact computations rather than numerical tests.

For the two connected components it returns the signatures

```text
(number of involutions, number of order-three labels,
 packet occurrences, distinct pairs,
 generated subgroup order, center order,
 involution-generated subgroup order)

(2,2,7,4,  6,1,6),
(2,4,23,8, 18,3,6).
```

The maximum possible number of distinct involution/order-three pairs in those
two vertex sets is respectively `2*2=4` and `2*4=8`.  Since the audit attains
both maxima, the underlying simple pair graphs are exactly

```text
K_(2,2) and K_(2,4).
```

The audit also checks that the two generated matrix subgroups have trivial
intersection and that the subgroup generated by their union has order `20160`,
the order of `GL_4(F2) ~= A8`.  Hence they intersect in `1` and generate all
of `A8`.

It remains to identify the two small groups.  The order-six subgroup `H_6` is
generated by involutions.  A cyclic group of order six has a unique involution,
so its involutions generate only its order-two subgroup.  Therefore `H_6` is
the nonabelian group of order six:

```text
H_6 ~= S3.
```

For `H_18`, let `K` be the subgroup generated by its two involution labels.
The audit gives `|K|=6`, so the same argument gives `K ~= S3`.  Let
`Z=Z(H_18)`.  The audit gives `|Z|=3`.  Since

```text
K intersect Z <= Z(K)=1,
```

we have `|K Z|=6*3=18`.  Thus `K Z=H_18`; `Z` centralizes `K`, and

```text
H_18 ~= K x Z ~= S3 x C3.
```

Finally a connected graph `K_(2,m)` has first Betti number

```text
2m-(m+2)+1 = m-1.
```

The two components therefore contribute `1` and `3` independent rectangle
cycles, for four total.  This proves every assertion in the claim.
