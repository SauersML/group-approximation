For a unital `F_2`-algebra `A`, write

```text
Rad_fd^alg(A)
 = intersection ker(pi),
pi:A -> End_(F_2)(V),  0<dim_(F_2)(V)<infinity.         (JLC1)
```

Put

```text
J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>,          Q=1-ST,          I=JQJ,
L=L_(F_2)(1,2),             S=s_0, T=t_0,    q=s_1t_1=Q.
```

Then the coefficient radical and the two relevant corners are exactly

```text
Rad_fd^alg(J)=I,
QJQ=F_2 Q,                  J/I isomorphic to F_2[z,z^(-1)],

qLq isomorphic to L,        LqL=L,
L has no nonzero finite-dimensional unital module.       (JLC2)
```

The distinction between `Q` and the actual letters `s_1,t_1` is
load-bearing.  The element `Q=1-s_0t_0` already belongs to the
one-branch Jacobson subalgebra, but it is not full there.  Adjoining
`s_1,t_1` makes it full, since

```text
t_1 q s_1=1,
a |-> s_1 a t_1 : L -> qLq
```

is an isomorphism with inverse `b |-> t_1 b s_1`.

This is the concrete graph-monoid transition

| coefficient algebra | finite graph | graph-monoid relation | finite dimension vectors | head corner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `J` | a loop at `v` and an edge `v -> w` to a sink | `[v]=[v]+[w]` | `(d_v,d_w)=(d,0)` | `QJQ isomorphic to F_2`, nonfull |
| `L` | one vertex `u` with two loops | `[u]=2[u]` | only `d_u=0` | `qLq isomorphic to L`, full |

Thus every finite-dimensional coefficient representation of `J` kills
`I` but may survive through the Laurent cycle.  By contrast, exposing
both binary branches removes every nonzero finite-dimensional coefficient
representation, not merely the head.

For the qutrit mixed packet,

```text
H=x_(c_2,c_1)(Q),
C_1=x_(c_2,c_0)(QT),
W=x_(c_2,c_3)(QTS)=x_(c_2,c_3)(Q),                     (JLC3)
```

all three labels lie in `I`.  Hence the packet is entirely supported in
the ambient finite-dimensional coefficient radical.  The established
group-kernel theorem upgrades this exact coefficient statement:
every exact finite-dimensional unitary representation of
`EL_28(J)` sends `H,C_1,W` to the identity.  In particular it erases
the sign-polar carrier `E=(1-H)/2`.

Adding the literal letters `s_1,t_1` changes this algebraic obstruction:
the normal coefficient ideal generated by `Q` becomes all of `L`.  It
does **not** by itself prove a normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt gap.  The full
corner equivalence is implemented by the rectangular pair `s_1,t_1`,
whereas arbitrary unitary microstates of the elementary group do not
come with decoded coefficient operators.  Neither the general corner
theorem nor the graph-monoid equation authenticates a word which preserves
one of the qutrit polar sources and returns it to `Ee_2`.

Consequently the corner route gives a sharp no-go and an exact next object:

```text
A successful bounded packet must expose s_1 and t_1 separately
and authenticate their two branch returns on the same selected
qutrit polar carrier.                                           (JLC4)
```

Merely using `Q=s_1t_1`, or any word whose nonconstant labels remain in
the one-branch ideal `I`, stays on the Jacobson side of the graph-monoid
transition and cannot use full-corner cancellation.
