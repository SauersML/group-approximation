The binary relations give

```text
wq=b_1,             wa_i=s_0t_(0^i),
a_iw=s_1t_(0^(i-1))t_1.                               (1)
```

The ordinary non-opposite Weyl conjugation formulas applied at the target
or source endpoint of `F_i` now give `(BHS2)--(BHS4)`.  The last coefficient
in `(1)` satisfies

```text
(a_iw)b_(i+1)=0,                                     (2)
```

because its last `t_1` meets the first `s_0` of `b_(i+1)`.  On the other
hand `a_(i+1)b_(i+1)=q`.  The ordinary composable-root commutator law turns
these two products into `(BHS5)`.  On the `B_(i+1)=+1` atom, a commuting
root has positive-space range while a root anticommuting through the
central sign `C_(i+1)=-1` has negative-space range.  Their restrictions
are therefore orthogonal, not equal.

The movement in `(BHS3)` is also genuine, not just a change of names.  In
the finite labelled-atom elementary image, a q-root and the corresponding
`b_1` root are distinct transvections with different target atoms.  Their
signed subgroup idempotents have different supports in the finite group
algebra, so the left regular representation does not identify the original
q-star projection with its outer-branch-flip conjugate.

For the conjugacy fence, let `v=rwr^(-1)` with `r` a prefix-code monomial
unit.  It is again an involution.  If an outer placement returned `F_i`
exactly to `A_i`, coefficient comparison would give `va_i=a_i`.
Right-multiplication by `b_i` gives `vq=q`.  For a monomial unit this says
that its Cantor prefix permutation fixes the cylinder `1*` pointwise.
The permutation of `w` exchanges `0*` and `1*` and has no fixed point, as
does every conjugate of it.  This is impossible.

If a middle placement returned `F_i` to `A_(i+1)`, coefficient comparison
would give `a_iv=a_(i+1)`.  Taking algebraic adjoints and multiplying gives

```text
v e_i v=e_(i+1),
e_j=s_(0^j)t_(0^j).                                  (3)
```

But `e_(i+1)<e_i` strictly.  Since conjugation by `v` has order two,
applying it to this inclusion would give `e_i<e_(i+1)`, a contradiction.
This proves the stated prefix-code-conjugacy scope.

For the localized flip, put `r_p=s_0wt_0`.  Cross-zero gives

```text
r_p^2=p,             qr_p=r_pq=0.
```

Hence `w_p=q+r_p` is an involution.  Also `q a_j=a_j` and
`r_pa_j=0`, proving `(BHS7)`.  The outer Weyl formula then swaps the two
q-centers, fixes every `Z` arm, and gives `(BHS9)`, so the whole positive
star flag is preserved.

In characteristic two, the elementary matrices of the two outer Weyls are

```text
L_i=[[0,1],[1,0]],             Theta_i=[[0,w_p],[w_p,0]].
```

Their product is `(BHS10)`.  Conjugation by this diagonal matrix fixes an
outer-root coefficient `a_j` because `w_pa_j=a_j`, fixes `q`, and is
distant from every middle/terminal `B` arm.  Thus `T_i` centralizes the
entire anchored Pauli packet and its positive star idempotent.

To see that this is a real reservoir freedom, use the finite labelled-atom
elementary image of the displayed packet.  On each of the two relevant
outer coordinates, split the complementary `p` atom into `00` and `01`.
The unit `w_p` fixes the q atom and swaps those two p atoms.  All remaining
displayed prefix coefficients are finite matrix units between their named
labelled coordinate summands, so the generated image is a finite subgroup
of some `SL_N(F_2)`.  Let `K` be its anchored Pauli subgroup, let `K_0<=K`
be the commuting subgroup generated by the q-centers and retained `Z`
arms, and take the left regular representation of the finite image.

The signed q-center and positive-Z character idempotent `E_+` is nonzero in
the regular representation and is supported in `C[K_0]`.  The element
`T_i` commutes with `K` but does not belong to `K`, since `K` fixes the two
complementary p atoms and `T_i` swaps them.  Therefore the nonzero vectors

```text
E_+delta_1,             T_iE_+delta_1=E_+delta_(T_i)
```

have disjoint supports `K_0` and `K_0T_i`.  Thus `T_i` is a nonidentity
operator in the Pauli commutant on the selected star space.  This proves
the finite-model gauge fence and completes the route.
