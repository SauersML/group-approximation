Let `R = Z[t,t^{-1}]` and let `U_2(R)` be the subgroup of `SL_2(R)` generated by
unipotent matrices. Then `SL_2(Z[t]) ⊂ U_2(R)`. Equivalently
(`sl2-laurent-elementary-iff-three-unipotent-conditions`, item 4),
`SL_2(R) = U_2(R)`.

This is condition (U) of the three-way split of
`sl2-laurent-integers-equals-elementary-subgroup`. It is necessary for that claim.

## Attempts

- **Fricke compatibility refutes it.** If `polynomial-unipotent-subgroup-fricke-compatible`
  holds, the derivation in `sl2-laurent-integers-not-fg-via-fricke-compatibility`
  gives `SL_2(R)/U_2(R) ≅ SL_2(Z[t])/U_2(Z[t])`. This quotient is nontrivial, and has
  free quotients of every rank by `gmv-sl2-zx-mod-unipotents-has-free-quotients`, so
  this claim fails. Conversely, a proof of this claim refutes Fricke compatibility.
- **Valuation-tree quotients are simply connected (a class of approaches killed).**
  Let `v` be any valuation of `Q(t)` with `v(t) ≥ 0`. Then `v(Z) ≥ 0`, so
  `SL_2(Z[t]) ⊂ SL_2(O_v)` fixes the standard vertex of the Bruhat–Tits (R-)tree
  `T_v` of `SL_2` over the completion, and `D SL_2(Z[t]) D^{-1}` fixes its
  `D`-translate. By `sl2-laurent-integers-splits-over-gamma0-t`, these two subgroups
  generate `G = SL_2(R)`. If `v(t) < 0`, use the `t ↦ t^{-1}` form of the same
  splitting. So for every valuation `v`, `G` is generated by vertex-fixing subgroups
  of `T_v`. Hence every `G`-invariant quotient of `G` obtained from `T_v` through the
  fundamental group of the quotient graph is trivial: in Bass–Serre theory
  `π_1(G\T_v)` is `G` modulo the normal closure of the vertex stabilizers. So no
  obstruction to (U), or to finite generation, comes from a free fundamental group of
  a valuation-tree quotient of `G` itself. GMV-type free quotients must enter through
  a subgroup such as `SL_2(Z[t])`, as in the Fricke route. Specializations
  `R → O_K` into imaginary quadratic rings also do not transfer them: `t` must go to
  a root of unity, so the image is `Z`, `Z[i]` or `Z[ζ_3]`, all Euclidean.
