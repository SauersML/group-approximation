Let `Gamma` be generated by `d` elements, let `1!=y in Gamma`, and assume
`Gamma` is residually finite.  Choose a finite quotient separating `y`, and
let `N` be its kernel.  Put `m=[Gamma:N]`.

There are only finitely many subgroups of `Gamma` of index at most `m`.
Indeed, a subgroup of index `k` is a point stabilizer in the transitive coset
action on `k` points.  After labeling those points, this action is a
homomorphism `Gamma -> Sym(k)`.  A homomorphism out of a `d`-generated group
is determined by the images of the `d` generators, so for each `k<=m` there
are at most `|Sym(k)|^d` such homomorphisms and only finitely many possible
point stabilizers.

Let

```text
C = intersection {H<=Gamma : [Gamma:H]<=m}.
```

This is a finite intersection of finite-index subgroups, hence has finite
index.  The family contains `N`, so `C<=N` and `y` is not in `C`.  Every
automorphism of `Gamma` permutes the subgroups in the displayed family,
because it preserves index.  It therefore preserves their intersection:
`C` is characteristic.

Consequently `Q=Gamma/C` is finite and the quotient map `q:Gamma->Q` keeps
`q(y)!=1`.  For any action `alpha:B->Aut(Gamma)`, characteristicity gives a
well-defined descended action

```text
bar alpha:B -> Aut(Q),
bar alpha(b)(q(g))=q(alpha(b)(g)).
```

Its image is finite because `Q` is finite.  The map

```text
Gamma semidirect_alpha B
  -> Q semidirect image(bar alpha),
(g,b) |-> (q(g),bar alpha(b))
```

is therefore a finite quotient and still detects `(y,1)`.  This proves the
finite marked-quotient formulation of the claim.

Finally, the left regular representation of `Q` is faithful and decomposes
as a finite direct sum of irreducibles.  Since its value at `q(y)` is not the
identity, at least one irreducible constituent `theta` satisfies
`theta(q(y))!=1`.  Every `B`-translate of `theta o q` factors through the same
finite group `Q`; hence its orbit lies in the finite dual of `Q`, and some
member (already `theta o q`) detects `y`.  This is exactly the equivalent
finite marked-type-orbit statement.
