## Statement

Let `A` be finitely generated with a left order `≺` whose positive cone `P` is generated as a semigroup by a
finite set `S⁺`. Then `A ∈ 𝒞`. The seed is the sign function `y(g) = cmp(g, 1)`, with generating set
`S = S⁺ ∪ (S⁺)^{-1}`.

*Proof.* Apply `order-seeds-are-rigid-relative-seeds` with `Ω = A`, `p = 1` and `C = 1`. Hypothesis (M) holds:
`g ≺ g′` gives `g^{-1}g′ = s_1 ⋯ s_m` with `s_i ∈ S⁺`, and each partial product increases. A left order is either
dense (no least positive element, so (D) holds) or discrete.
- **The discrete case.** Let `s_min` be the least positive element.
  - `s_min ∈ S⁺`, since a product of two or more positive elements exceeds each factor.
  - The gaps of `A` are exactly the pairs `g ≺ g s_min`, since `g s_min` is the successor of `g`.
  - The rule forbids `<>` along `s_min`, because nothing lies strictly between `1` and `s_min`. For every other
    `s ∈ S⁺`, `s_min` lies strictly between `1` and `s`, so `<>` is allowed.
  - So a cut without `=` cannot sit at a gap. Every remaining cut has no endpoints on either side, or is
    constant, and is a limit of point cuts.
  - The proofs of (RS1) and (RS3) are unchanged; forbidding a pattern only helps. `∎`

## Instances

- **Braid groups `B_n`, for every n.** The Dubrovina–Dubrovin ordering has positive cone generated by
  `a_i = (σ_{n−i} σ_{n−i+1} ⋯ σ_{n−1})^{±1}`, `i = 1, …, n−1`, with alternating signs (the exact
  convention is in Ito). This is as stated in Ito, arXiv:1107.0545,
  §1, read 2026-09-18, citing Dubrovina–Dubrovin. So `B_n ∈ 𝒞`.
- **Torus-knot groups `⟨x, y | x^m = y^n⟩`.** `{x y^{1−n}, y}` defines an isolated ordering (Navas for `m = 2`, Ito
  in general, as quoted by Ito, §1). These were already in 𝒞 as graphs of free abelian groups.
- **Ito's partially central cyclic amalgams.** Ito's Theorem 1.1, read 2026-09-18, assumes:
  - `G`, `H` finitely generated, with isolated orders defined by finite generating sets `g_1 < ⋯ < g_m` and
    `h_1 < ⋯ < h_n`;
  - `z_G` central in `G`, and `z_H ∈ H`;
  - the cofinality conditions `g_i < z_G` and `h_i < z_H`, and `<_H` right-invariant under `z_H`.

  Then `X = G *_{z_G = z_H} H` has an isolated order defined by `{g_i z_H^{-1} h_1} ∪ {h_j}`, and the construction
  iterates. So every iterate lies in 𝒞.
- **Where the route stops.** Free products and direct products do not preserve isolated orders. By Rivas (as
  quoted by Ito), free products have none, and neither does `Z²`. So `F_2`, `Z²` and `F_2 × F_2` have no absolute
  order seed, although they lie in 𝒞 by other seeds.

## Question (O), restated, and what the constructions can do

- **CAP predicts a bound.** Since every group with a finitely generated positive cone lies in 𝒞, CAP (with bound
  EXP) predicts that every finitely presented group with a finitely generated positive cone has its word problem
  in EXP. So one finitely presented group with a finitely generated positive cone and word problem outside EXP
  refutes CAP.
- **Ito's amalgamation transports but does not create** (a lane sketch, not a proof).
  - Ito's proof is constructive: `<_X` is computable from `<_G`, `<_H` and the reduced standard factorization
    (Ito, §2.7, as announced in §1).
  - The word problem of `X` reduces to those of `G` and `H` and to membership in `⟨z⟩`. Membership reduces to order
    comparisons with the cofinal central `z`.
  - So the only new cost is the height of an element measured in powers of `z`. That is a distortion, and the
    Baumslag–Gersten node shows distortion certifies nothing.
  - Starting from `Z`, braid groups and torus-knot groups (Garside-type, easy word problems), iteration gives no
    hard word problem that we can see.
- **What an answer needs.** A *primary* construction of a finitely generated positive cone on a finitely presented
  group with a hard word problem. By the sketch above, Ito amalgams of known examples do not supply
  one.
  - The Bludov–Glass and Darbinyan–Steenbock embeddings produce computable orders, but not finitely generated
    cones.
  - Question (O) stays **open**.

## Lesson for general BH

Every finitely generated positive cone is a rigid seed. So braid groups and Ito's amalgams join 𝒞, and CAP now
makes a concrete prediction about left-orderable groups: finitely generated positive cones on finitely presented
groups have tame word problems.
- **The known constructions.** Ito's amalgamation, Dubrovina–Dubrovin and Navas's Hecke-type examples only
  transport complexity.
- **Where a refutation must come from.** A new, primary way to make an isolated order carry a computation.
