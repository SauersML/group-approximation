Use the arithmetic representatives
\[
 t=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 r=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 x=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
in \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(A)\), and put \(v=(0,1)\). The stabilizer
of \([v]\) among unimodular rows modulo \(A^\times\) is the full
upper triangular subgroup. It is generated by \(r,t\): powers of \(r\)
give every diagonal unit and their conjugates of \(t\) give every
dyadic upper-root parameter. Thus it is \(P_\infty\). The unordered
pair
\[
 [v],\quad[vx]=[(1,0)]
\]
has the full monomial stabilizer \(N_\infty=\langle r,x\rangle\).
Consequently
\[
 P_\infty g\longmapsto[vg],\qquad
 N_\infty g\longmapsto\{[vg],[vxg]\}                 \tag{DUP1}
\]
are equivariant bijections on vertices and edges.

It remains to audit the triangles. A third vertex adjacent to both members
of the base edge has a representative \((\alpha,\beta)\) with
\(\alpha,\beta\in A^\times\). Scaling by \(\beta^{-1}\) gives a
unique representative
\[
 (u,1),\qquad u\in A^\times.                           \tag{DUP2}
\]
Right multiplication by the two generators of the edge stabilizer acts on
this ratio by
\[
 r:u\longmapsto4u,\qquad x:u\longmapsto-u^{-1}.        \tag{DUP3}
\]
Since \(A^\times=\mathord\pm2^{\mathbb Z}\), there are exactly two
orbits:
\[
 \mathord\pm4^{\mathbb Z},\qquad
 \mathord\pm2\,4^{\mathbb Z}.                        \tag{DUP4}
\]
The face of \(a=xt\) through the base edge has third row
\[
 va=vxt=(1,1),
\]
so it represents the first orbit. The face of \(b=xt^2r\) has third row
\[
 vb=vxt^2r=(2,1)
\]
so it
represents the second orbit. Equivalently, the two normalized ratios are
\(1\) and \(2\).

There is no hidden multiplicity over \(A\). Embed
\(\operatorname{PSL}_2(A)\) in \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\).
A setwise stabilizer of three distinct real projective points acts by a
cyclic permutation, because projective determinant-one transformations
preserve the cyclic order on \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb R)\). The elements
\(a\) and \(b\) have order three and cyclically permute their respective
triples. Hence their order-three subgroups are the full triangle
stabilizers. Equations (DUP1)--(DUP4) therefore identify the face sets as
well and prove (DUC1).

Hutchinson proves
\[
 \pi_1(Y(A))\cong K_2(2,A)/C(2,A),
\]
where \(C(2,A)\) is generated by symbols, and records that
\(A=\mathbb Z[1/2]\) is universal for \(\mathrm{GE}_2\); see
K. Hutchinson, *GE2-rings and a graph of unimodular rows*, J. Pure Appl.
Algebra 226 (2022), Theorem 6.9 and Example 6.13. Thus \(Y(A)\) is simply
connected. Fundamental groups depend only on the two-skeleton, proving
(DUC2).

Now reduce modulo \(m=4^n-1\). Reduction is onto on
\(\operatorname{SL}_2\), since elementary row reduction generates
\(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\), and the image of
\(A^\times\) is
\[
 L=\mathord\pm\langle2\rangle.
\]
The same normalization as (DUP2) says that the common neighbors of a
fixed refined edge are parametrized by \(u\in L\), and (DUP3) remains
valid. The order of \(2\) modulo \(m\) is exactly \(2n\): for
\(0<j<2n\), the positive integer \(2^j-1\) is smaller than \(m\).
Moreover \(-1\notin\langle2\rangle\), since
\(0<2^j+1<m\) for \(0\le j<2n\). It follows that
\[
 L=(\mathord\pm\langle4\rangle)
   \sqcup(\mathord\pm2\langle4\rangle),             \tag{DUP5}
\]
which proves exhaustion and disjointness in (DUC3).

Finally put \(q=2^n\). Since \(q^2=4^n=1\pmod m\),
\[
 c=r^n=qI
\]
in \(G_m\). It is a nontrivial central involution: \(q\not\equiv
\mathord\pm1\pmod m\). It belongs to \(P_m\cap N_m\), fixes every
\(L\)-projective vertex, and is in neither order-three face subgroup.
Thus \(A_mg,A_mcg\) are distinct cells with the same three boundary
edges, and likewise for \(B_m\).

These are the only duplicates. For either representative triangle, its
setwise stabilizer acts on its three vertices. The kernel of this action
consists of diagonal elements from \(L\) whose square is one, hence is
exactly \(\{1,c\}\) in the global-sign quotient. A transposition cannot
occur: reduction modulo \(3\mid m\) sends the three vertices to three
distinct points of \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_3)\), while
\(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\cong A_4\) has cyclic order-three
stabilizer on any three-point subset. The stabilizer is therefore
\(A_m\times\langle c\rangle\), or
\(B_m\times\langle c\rangle\), and every geometric triangle has
exactly two face cells.

Duplicating a two-cell duplicates a column of the cellular boundary (or a
row of cellular coboundary) and changes neither its image nor the kernel
of \(\delta_1\). The one-skeleton and vertex coboundaries are already
the same. This proves (DUC4).
