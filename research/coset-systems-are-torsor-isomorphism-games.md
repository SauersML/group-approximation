**Coset systems.**

- A *coset system* `S = (Q, K, E, D, c)` consists of:
  - a finite set `Q`;
  - finite groups `K_q` for `q in Q`;
  - a symmetric set `E` of ordered pairs `q != q'`;
  - for `(q,q') in E`, a subgroup `D_(qq') <= K_q x K_(q')` and an element
    `c_(qq') in K_q x K_(q')`.

  Both `D_(q'q)` and `c_(q'q)` are the transposes of `D_(qq')` and `c_(qq')`.
- Its algebra `A(S)` is the universal unital *-algebra generated by PVMs
  `(p_q^s)`, `s in K_q`, one for each `q`, with

  ```text
  p_q^s p_(q')^(s') = 0   whenever (q,q') in E and (s,s') not in c_(qq') D_(qq').   (CS)
  ```

- `S_1` is the homogeneous system, with every `c_(qq') = 1`.
- `S` is *tracial* when `A(S)` has a tracial state, and *`R^U`* when there is
  a unital *-homomorphism `A(S) -> R^U`.
- Binary linear systems are the case where every `K_q` is elementary
  abelian and `D` says "agree on shared variables".

**The torsor digraph `X(S)`.**  Vertices `(q,x)`, `x in K_q`.  Colours:

- inside a fibre, `col((q,x),(q,x')) = (in, q, x^(-1) x')`;
- on `(q,q') in E`, `col((q,x),(q',x')) = (edge, q, q', D_(qq') c_(qq')^(-1) (x,x'))`,
  a right coset of `D_(qq')`;
- other pairs in distinct fibres get `(free, q, q')`.

Diagonal colours `(in, q, e)` are never off-diagonal colours, and transposes
of colour classes are colour classes.

**Theorem.**

- **(T1) Dictionary.**  `A(Iso(X(S_1), X(S)))` is *-isomorphic to `A(S)` via

  ```text
  v_((q,x),(q',a))  |->  delta_(qq') p_q^(a x^(-1)),        p_q^s |-> v_((q,e),(q,s)).
  ```

  So `X(S_1) ≅_qc X(S)` iff `S` is tracial, and `X(S_1) ≅_qa X(S)` iff `S`
  is `R^U`.
- **(T2) Quantum symmetry.**  `O(Qut X(S_1)) ≅ A(S_1)`, with

  ```text
  Delta(p_q^s) = sum_(r in K_q) p_q^r (x) p_q^(s r^(-1)),   epsilon(p_q^s) = delta_(s,e),   S(p_q^s) = p_q^(s^(-1)).
  ```

- **(T3) Not a group dual.**  `L_q = {s : p_q^s != 0 in A(S_1)}` is a
  subgroup of `K_q`.  It contains the projection to `K_q` of the classical
  solution group

  ```text
  Sol_0 = {(s_q) : (s_q, s_(q')) in D_(qq') for all (q,q') in E}.
  ```

  If some `L_q` is nonabelian, then `O(Qut X(S_1))` is not cocommutative, so
  `Qut X(S_1)` is not dual to a discrete group.
- **(T4) Rung.**  Suppose `S` is tracial and not `R^U`, and some `L_q` is
  nonabelian.  Let `Z = X(S_1) ⊔ X(S)`, with a new cross colour between the
  two parts.  Then:
  - `X(S_1) ≅_qc X(S)` but they are not `qa`-isomorphic;
  - `Qut Z` is a Kac quantum group whose Haar von Neumann algebra is not
    Connes embeddable (`closure-safe-support-is-exactly-the-colored-iso-rung`);
  - `Qut Z` is not a group dual, since it has `Qut X(S_1)` as a Hopf quotient.

  As on every colored isomorphism game, C*-satisfiability of `A(S)` already
  gives a trace (corner of the Haar state), so there is no trace-selection
  step.

**Why this is the square completion.**  Given a synchronous game, make each
answer set a torsor of a group `K_q` (possibly after padding answers by
coarse-graining maps `beta_q: K_q -> A_q`).  Replace each accepting set by a
coset inside it.  The result is a coset system, and its perfect models push
forward to perfect models of the game.  In the torsor digraph, the rows of each
fibre form a square block that is literally a left translation.  So this is
the bijection that the independent-set injection lacks
(`independent-set-injection-splits-as-question-bijection`).  What it costs is
the lifting of the source's answers to coset-valued answers.

**Model tests.**

- **All `K_q` elementary abelian with shared-variable `D`.**  (T1) and (T2)
  are Roberson--Schmidt, arXiv:2111.12362v1, Lemma 3.6, Theorem 3.8 and
  Theorem 6.7 (pp. 5--10 read at source).  There the symmetry is a group dual,
  consistent with (T3), since every `L_q` is abelian.
- **`K_q = S_3` and `E` empty.**  `A(S_1)` is the free product of copies of
  `C(S_3)`, whose symmetry is a genuinely quantum free product and not a group
  dual, as (T3) says.

**Credit and novelty.**

- The mechanism is Roberson--Schmidt's for elementary abelian fibres: the
  fibre block depends only on `alpha △ beta` (their Lemma 3.6), and the
  linking algebra is the linear-system algebra (their Theorem 6.7).
- New here:
  - the extension to coset constraints over arbitrary finite groups;
  - the left-translation form for nonabelian fibres;
  - the non-cocommutativity criterion (T3).
- **Bounded check.**  The novelty check covers Roberson--Schmidt,
  Lupini--Mančinska--Roberson, BCEHPSW, AMRSSV and Paddock--Slofstra.  No web
  search was possible in this session, so the check is bounded to those five
  papers.
- **Scope of (T4).**  A nonhyperlinear group would trivially give a pair as in
  (T4), by adjoining an unconstrained nonabelian fibre.  The value of this
  syntax is the converse direction: a tracial non-`R^U` coset system is not
  known to give a nonhyperlinear group.
