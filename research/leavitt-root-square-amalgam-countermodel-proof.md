The root `x_13(q)` is nontrivial and normally generates the rank-twenty
Steinberg group by `full-leavitt-idempotent-defect-saturation`; normal
generation pushes through the canonical surjection to `EL_20(R)`.  The root
law is additive, so characteristic two gives `x_13(q)^2=x_13(2q)=1`.
Transport along `EL_20(R) ~= R^x` gives `(LRS1)`.

Let `C_4=<c | c^4=1>`, and identify its order-two subgroup `<c^2>` with
`<r> <= G`.  The amalgamated free product

```text
H=G *_<r>=<c^2> C_4
```

has presentation obtained from a presentation of `G` by adjoining `c` and
the single relation `c^2=r` (the consequence `c^4=1` already follows from
`r^2=1`).  Since `r=r^(-1)`, this is precisely

```text
H ~= <G,t | r t^2=1>,
```

which proves `(LRS2)`.  The normal-form theorem for amalgamated free products
embeds both vertex groups, in particular the original coefficient group `G`.
Hence `w` is a nonsingular equation with a solution in an overgroup, not a
Kervaire--Laudenbach violation.

Map `H->C_2` trivially on `G` and by `c |-> 1 mod 2`.  Under `(LRS2)` this is
the degree map, so its kernel is exactly `Q_w`.  It is generated by the two
vertex groups

```text
G,             cGc^(-1).
```

Their intersection is `<r>`: in the Bass--Serre tree of
`G *_<r> C_4`, the corresponding `G`-vertices are distance two apart through
the `C_4`-vertex, and their stabilizer intersection is
`<r> intersection c<r>c^(-1)=<r>` because `<c^2>` is central in `C_4`.
The quotient graph for the index-two kernel is therefore one edge with these
two `G` vertex groups and edge group `<r>`.  Bass--Serre normal form gives

```text
Q_w ~= G *_<r> G,
```

proving `(LRS3)`.  It is infinite because either vertex group embeds, perfect
because `G` is perfect and the abelianization of an amalgam of two perfect
groups is trivial, and has no nontrivial finite quotient because each vertex
copy of `G` has none.  This is an explicit nonsolvable countermodel, not a
finite-quotient screen.
