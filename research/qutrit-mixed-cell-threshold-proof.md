## The two-cell quiver

Write the active coordinates as `0,1,2,3` for
`c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3`. The two adjacent cells `N_0,N_1` are generated by

```text
X_(0,r)=x_(2,0)(t_r),        Y_(1,r)=x_(1,2)(s_r),
X_(1,r)=x_(3,1)(t_r),        Y_(2,r)=x_(2,3)(s_r).
                                                               (QMT6)
```

As maps between coordinate columns, their incidence quiver is

```text
0 --t--> 2 --s--> 1 --t--> 3 --s--> 2.                 (QMT7)
```

Thus there is already a directed three-cycle on `2,1,3`. The group is
not finite: the scalar root `x_(3,2)(1)` belongs to it, and

```text
[x_(1,2)(s_0^n),x_(2,3)(s_0)]
   =x_(1,3)(s_0^(n+1)),
[x_(1,3)(s_0^(n+1)),x_(3,2)(1)]
   =x_(1,2)(s_0^(n+1)).                                 (QMT8)
```

Starting with `x_(1,2)(s_0)` gives all
`x_(1,2)(s_0^n)`. The coefficients are distinct Leavitt normal forms,
so `G_2` is infinite.

## A positive shifted grading

Use the standard Leavitt grading

```text
deg(s_r)=1,                 deg(t_r)=-1.
```

Assign coordinate potentials

```text
phi(0)=0, phi(1)=0, phi(2)=1, phi(3)=1
```

and give a homogeneous coefficient matrix the shifted degree

```text
Deg(E_(ij) a)=deg(a)+phi(i)-phi(j).                      (QMT9)
```

The four generator entries in `(QMT6)` have degrees

```text
Deg(X_0)=0,       Deg(Y_1)=0,
Deg(X_1)=0,       Deg(Y_2)=1.                            (QMT10)
```

Let `mathcal A` be the unital `F_2`-subalgebra of
`M_4(L_(F_2)(1,2))` generated by those eight entries. Since the
Leavitt relations are homogeneous, `mathcal A` is nonnegatively graded:

```text
mathcal A=direct-sum_(n>=0) mathcal A_n.                 (QMT11)
```

The zero-degree generators have the acyclic incidence path

```text
0 -> 2 -> 1 -> 3.
```

Consequently `mathcal A_0` is finite dimensional. More generally, if
`W` is the two-dimensional span of the two degree-one `Y_2` entries,
then

```text
mathcal A_n subset
 mathcal A_0 W mathcal A_0 W ... W mathcal A_0
```

with `n` copies of `W`. Every homogeneous piece is therefore finite
dimensional.

For `N>=1` put

```text
I_N=direct-sum_(n>=N) mathcal A_n.
```

This is a two-sided ideal and `mathcal A/I_N` is a finite ring. The
intersection of the `I_N` is zero. If `1!=g in G_2`, choose `N` above
the largest shifted degree occurring in `g-1`. The unit `g` remains
nonidentity in the finite group

```text
(mathcal A/I_N)^times.
```

Hence `G_2` is residually finite.

The same argument includes the qutrit torus. Replace each active
coordinate by its finite-dimensional `C`-invariant summand and assign
the same potential to the whole summand. The two nontrivial coordinates
`1,2` lie in distinct corresponding copies, so `C` is block diagonal
for these potentials. Adjoining the finite degree-zero algebra generated
by `C` leaves the zero-degree incidence acyclic and every graded piece
finite dimensional. Its degree truncations prove that `<C,G_2>` is
residually finite as well.

## Exact scalar intersection

The two cross-cell returns and the middle matched row give

```text
D_3=x_(1,0)(1) in N_0,
D_0=x_(2,1)(1) in N_1,
D_1=[X_(1,r),Y_(1,r)]=x_(3,2)(1) in G_2.                (QMT12)
```

These three adjacent scalar transvections generate all of
`U_4^-(F_2)` by path commutators.

For the reverse containment, inspect scalar entries in the shifted
grading. A scalar matrix unit `E_(ij)` has shifted degree
`phi(i)-phi(j)`. Negative degrees cannot occur in `mathcal A`.
At degree zero, the only nontrivial paths use the acyclic quiver
`0->2->1->3`. Its scalar reductions can occur only in the forward
positions, such as

```text
E_(1,2)(s_r) E_(2,0)(t_s)=E_(1,0)(s_rt_s),
E_(3,1)(t_r) E_(1,2)(s_s)=delta_(rs)E_(3,2),
```

never in a reverse position. Degree one scalar positions have a row in
`{2,3}` and a column in `{0,1}`. Thus

```text
mathcal A intersect M_4(F_2)
 subset {lower triangular scalar matrices}.             (QMT13)
```

Every invertible lower triangular matrix over `F_2` has all diagonal
entries equal to one. Combining `(QMT12)` and `(QMT13)` proves

```text
G_2 intersect GL_4(F_2)=U_4^-(F_2).
```

The actor `P=(0 1 2 3)` is not lower triangular, so `P notin G_2`.

## The third cell closes the scalar cycle

Adjoin `N_2`. Its generators include `X_(2,r)` and `Y_(3,r)`.
The cell return gives `D_1` again. More importantly, `Y_(2,r)` was
already present in `N_1`, so the matched same-row relation gives the
missing edge

```text
[X_(2,r),Y_(2,r)]=D_2=x_(0,3)(1).                       (QMT14)
```

Together `D_0,D_1,D_2,D_3` are the constant transvections around a
directed four-cycle. Their commutators give every constant root, hence

```text
GL_4(F_2) subset G_3.
```

In particular `P in G_3`. The group also contains raw roots with each
coefficient `s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1`. Scalar permutation conjugation places
them at every ordered root position, while additivity and three-index
commutators generate their whole `F_2`-algebra. Therefore

```text
G_3=EL_4(L_(F_2)(1,2)).
```

The established Leavitt theorem makes this group nonsofic, and hence not
residually finite.

## The first head word and its trace

Set

```text
S=s_0,        T=t_0,        Q_0=1-ST=s_1t_1.
```

The second branch of the `N_1` return is

```text
H=[X_(1,1),Y_(2,1)]
  =x_(2,1)(s_1t_1)=x_(2,1)(Q_0).                        (QMT15)
```

Thus the Jacobson head already occurs in `G_2`. Residual finiteness
shows why that occurrence alone is harmless: finite quotients of `G_2`
can retain it.

After adjoining the third cell, the scalar group moves `H` to every
root position. The subalgebra generated by `S,T` is the Jacobson
algebra `F_2<S,T | TS=1>`, and the normal closure of the `Q_0` roots
is its finitary locally finite radical. The established Jacobson theorem
says that every exact finite-dimensional unitary representation kills
this radical.

Finally, `H` acts between corresponding coordinates `2,1` in two
copies of the same nontrivial `C`-module, so it commutes with `C`.
Let `p_chi` be a Fourier atom of `C`. In the canonical group trace,

```text
tau(p_chi)=1/9,                 tau(p_chi H)=0.           (QMT16)
```

The second equality holds because no product of a `C` element with the
nonidentity root `H` is the identity. Since `H` is an involution,

```text
||p_chi(H-1)||_2^2=2 tau(p_chi)=2/9,
||(1-H)p_chi/2||_2^2=tau(p_chi)/2=1/18.
```

These are the promised atomwise canonical energies. They identify a
macroscopic finite-representation-invisible carrier, but it is diagonal
on `C`. A nonhyperlinearity proof still needs a third-cell occurrence
which authenticates a one-complement transport of this same carrier;
the grading proof gives exact finite models for everything visible before
that occurrence.
