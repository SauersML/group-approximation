Write `pi:G->A` for the quotient map and `T=tor(A)`.

## 1. Absorb all quotient torsion

Put `K=pi^(-1)(T)`.  To prove that `K` is locally finite, take a finitely
generated subgroup `H<=K`.  Its image `pi(H)` is a finitely generated
subgroup of the torsion abelian group `T`, hence is finite.  Therefore
`H intersection L` has finite index in `H` and is finitely generated by
Schreier's theorem.  It is a finitely generated subgroup of the locally
finite group `L`, so it is finite.  Thus `H` is finite.  This proves that
`K` is locally finite and gives `(TAL2)`.

In particular, if `r=0`, then `A=T`, so `G=K` is locally finite.  Its group
C-star algebra is AF, proving `(TAL4)`.

## 2. Exhaust the torsion-free quotient by lattices

Enumerate `A/T` and let `B_j` be the subgroup generated by the first `j`
elements.  Since `A/T` is torsion-free abelian, each

```text
B_j isomorphic to Z^(q_j),       q_j<=r.
```

Put `E_j=pi^(-1)(B_j)`.  These groups increase to `G`.  Choose a set-theoretic
section `s_j:B_j->E_j` with `s_j(0)=1`, and define

```text
alpha_b(k)=s_j(b) k s_j(b)^(-1),
u(b,c)=s_j(b)s_j(c)s_j(b+c)^(-1) in K.                (TAL9)
```

The associativity law in `E_j` gives the Busby--Smith identities

```text
alpha_b alpha_c = Ad(u(b,c)) alpha_(b+c),
u(b,c)u(b+c,d)=alpha_b(u(c,d))u(b,c+d).               (TAL10)
```

Conversely, multiplication in `E_j` is recovered from `(TAL9)--(TAL10)`.
After applying the canonical group unitaries `lambda_K(u(b,c))`, the
universal properties give

```text
C*(E_j) isomorphic to C*(K) rtimes_(alpha,u) B_j.      (TAL11)
```

All groups involved are amenable, so there is no full-versus-reduced issue.
Also `C*(K)` is AF because `K` is an increasing union of finite groups.
Equations `(TAL3)` and the asserted uniform-bound reduction now follow from
`stw84-directed-union-uniform-bound`.

## 3. The rank-one core

If `r=1`, each nonzero `B_j` is infinite cyclic.  Choose a generator and a
lift `t_j in E_j`.  The map `n |-> t_j^n` splits `E_j->B_j`, so

```text
E_j=K rtimes_(Ad(t_j)|K) Z.                            (TAL12)
```

This proves the reduction to `(TAL5)`.  Conversely, every group in `(TAL5)`
is itself an extension of the required kind with rank-one quotient, so this
is the minimal unrestricted positive-rank family.

For fixed `K rtimes_alpha Z`, take increasing finite subgroups `F_j` whose
union is `K` and set `H_j=<F_j,t>`.  Each `H_j` is finitely generated, its
kernel is the locally finite subgroup generated by all `alpha^n(F_j)`, the
groups increase, and their union is the full semidirect product.  The
directed-union theorem therefore reduces a universal cyclic estimate to the
finitely generated case.

This also identifies the kernel-finite boundary exactly.  For finite
`E subset K`,

```text
<E,t> intersection K = <alpha^n(E):n in Z>.            (TAL13)
```

Thus kernel-finiteness implies that every displayed orbit hull is finite.
Conversely, if every such hull is finite, multiplication in semidirect
normal form puts the kernel intersection of every finitely generated
subgroup inside the finite invariant hull of its finitely many kernel
coordinates.  Hence the extension is kernel-finite.  Finally, the finite-
hull condition is equivalent to an increasing invariant finite-subgroup
exhaustion: enumerate `K` and take the orbit hull of each finite initial
segment.

## 4. Lower bound and the finite-data firewall

The induced map `G_ab->A` is onto.  Its kernel is the image of `L`, hence is
torsion.  Tensoring with `Q` yields

```text
Q tensor G_ab isomorphic to Q tensor A.
```

The abelianization-rank theorem gives `(TAL8)`.

Although a lattice presentation uses only finitely many lifts, the subgroup
of `K` generated by the lattice orbit of the finitely many commutators and
kernel seeds need not be finite.  In rank one there are no cocycle
commutators at all, yet the orbit hull of one finite subgroup can already be
the infinite simple group `Alt_fin(Z)`.  Thus replacing `(TAL11)` by finite-
kernel crossed products, or by commuting tensor coordinates, is not a valid
finite-stage argument.
