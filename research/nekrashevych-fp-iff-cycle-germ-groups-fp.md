## Setting

`G = ⟨S⟩ <= Aut(X^*)`, `S` finite and closed under sections and inverses, `d = |X|`.
- A letter `y` is **stable** if for every `s in S` the sections `s|_{y^k}` are eventually constant in `k`. The
  limit `e_y(s)` then satisfies `e_y(s)|_y = e_y(s)`, and `y ↦ e_y(s)(y)` is its eventual output.
- A family `𝒪` of stable letters is **closed** if eventual outputs of letters of `𝒪` lie in `𝒪`.
- The **germ group** `G_(ξ)` of `G` at a point `ξ` is the group of germs at `ξ` of the elements fixing `ξ`.
- **Regrouping.** Suppose `S` has subexponential activity (or only (P1): each nontrivial state has at most one
  letter keeping it in its strongly connected component). Then every component is a simple cycle. At level
  `r = lcm(cycle lengths)` every cycle becomes a loop, and every letter of `X^r` is stable. The constant rays of
  `X^r` that carry loops are the **cycle rays**: the periodic rays `ℓ(s) ℓ(νs) ℓ(ν^2 s) …` of the cycles.
  There are finitely many. Regrouping changes neither the Cantor action, nor the germs, nor finite presentation
  of the Nekrashevych group (`lift-presentability-is-invariant-under-level-regrouping`).

## Statement

1. **Necessity (every automaton).** If `V_d(G)` is finitely presented, then `G_(y^ω)` is finitely presented for
   every letter `y` of every closed family of stable letters.
2. **Criterion (subexponential activity).** `V_d(G)` is finitely presented if and only if the germ groups of `G`
   at its finitely many cycle rays are finitely presented.
3. **Descent.** Under (P1), after regrouping, let `y` be a loop letter. Then
   `G_(y^ω) ↪ B ≀ Sym(X^r ∖ {y})`, where `B` is generated by the sections `s|_z` (`s` a loop state,
   `z ≠ ℓ(s)`). Each of these lies strictly below `s`, so if `S` has activity degree `m` in Sidki's sense, `B` is
   generated by an automaton of degree `<= m - 1`.

## Consequences

1. **Bounded activity** (degree 0): `B` is finitary, hence finite, so every cycle germ group is finite and
   `V_d(G)` is finitely presented. This recovers Nekrashevych's FP2 for bounded automata (bounded automaton
   groups are contracting).
2. **Linear activity** (degree 1): `V_d(G)` is finitely presented iff the cycle germ groups, which are
   f.g. subgroups of (bounded automaton group) `≀` (finite group), are finitely presented. Instances on main:
   - spine-Grigorchuk: germ group `Γ`, not fp, so `V_3` is not fp;
   - odometer automaton `a = σ(1,a)`, `b = (a,b)`: germ group `⟨b⟩ ≅ Z`, so `V_2` is fp.
3. **Spine-mover is an equivalence.** In `spine-mover-automata-over-fp-bases-are-lift-presented`, the letters `2, 3`
   form a closed stable family and the germ group at `2^ω` is `⟨ŝ_p⟩ ≅ P`. By 1, `M(P)` is lift-presented
   **iff** `P` is finitely presented. This answers the converse left open there. In particular, the four-letter
   test case `M(Γ)` of `spine-and-odometer-mover-generate-a-2-adic-lamplighter` has non-fp `V_4`.
4. **Loop retraction and SWZ are the case "every state fixes `y`".** Then `y` is stable after regrouping by the
   period of the section map at `y`, and `G_(y^ω)` is the eventual section group
   (`loop-retraction-forces-fp-eventual-section-groups`). For persistent actions it is all of `G`.

## Lesson for general BH

Finite presentation of a Nekrashevych host is a **local** property, decided at finitely many singular orbits,
the cycle rays. For subexponential activity it is exactly "the cycle germ groups are fp". Those germ groups
live one activity degree lower, inside `(lower group) ≀ finite`. So BH for subexponential automaton groups
through self-similar hosts is an induction on degree with one step: **germ enlargement**. Enlarge each cycle
germ group to a finitely presented group that is again a cycle germ group of an automaton.

Tree automorphisms can realize, as germs at a ray, only subgroups of `(lower group) ≀ finite`. They cannot
realize the prefix shift. That is why this step needs finitely presented *self-similar* overgroups
(`decidable-fg-self-similar-groups-embed-in-fp-self-similar`). Germ-extension hosts of `V` get the shift for
free: the Grigorchuk germ group there is `W ⋊ Z`, as in
`grigorchuk-groups-lie-in-one-point-germ-extensions-of-v`. The two host families differ exactly by the
shift germ.
