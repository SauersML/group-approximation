## Setting

Let `C = {0,1}^N` be the Cantor set with its standard cylinders `[w]`, `w` a finite word.
Let `Γ <= Homeo(C)` be finitely generated by `g_1, ..., g_m` and faithful, with

- **(CT) cylinder transitivity:** the `Γ`-orbit of `[0]` contains every cylinder `[w]`.

Let `G_Γ` be the germ groupoid of the action: the quotient of `Γ ⋉ C` by the equivalence
identifying `(g, x)` with `(h, x)` when `g` and `h` agree on a neighbourhood of `x`. It is
an ample groupoid with compact Hausdorff unit space `C`, it is effective by construction,
and it is minimal iff the action is minimal. Write `A = A_(F_2)(G_Γ)`.

(CT) holds whenever `Γ` contains Thompson's `V`, since `V` maps `[0]` onto every cylinder.
Minimality also follows, since `V` is already minimal.

## Statement

1. **Finite generation as a ring.** `A` is generated, as a ring, by the `m + 1` elements
   `1_([0])` and `1_([g_1]), ..., 1_([g_m])`, where `[g]` is the germ class of the full
   bisection of `g`. In particular `A` is a finitely generated `F_2`-algebra, with no
   scalars needed beyond the prime field.
2. **Units.** `Γ` embeds in `A^x` by `g |-> 1_([g])`.
3. **Simplicity and centre.** If the action is minimal and `G_Γ` is Hausdorff, then `A` is
   simple with `Z(A) = F_2`, so `A` is a central simple `F_2`-algebra. Without
   Hausdorffness the same conclusion holds under the hypotheses of
   `cepss-nonhausdorff-steinberg-simplicity`.

## Proofs

**1.** `A` is spanned over `F_2` by the indicators `1_B` of compact open bisections of
`G_Γ`, and every such `B` is a finite disjoint union of germ classes `[U, g] = U · [g]`
with `U` clopen and `g in Γ`. So `A` is generated as a ring by `C(C, F_2)` together with
`{1_([g]) : g in Γ}`. Now:
- `1_([g]) 1_([h]) = 1_([gh])`, so the finitely many `1_([g_i^(±1)])` generate all `1_([g])`;
- `1_([g]) 1_(U) 1_([g])^(-1) = 1_(g(U))`, so the subring generated by `1_([0])` and the
  `1_([g_i])` contains `1_(gamma([0]))` for every `gamma in Γ`, hence by (CT) the indicator
  of every cylinder;
- indicators of cylinders generate `C(C, F_2)` as a ring: every clopen set is a finite
  disjoint union of cylinders, and over `F_2` a disjoint union is a sum.

**2.** `Γ -> [[G_Γ]]`, `g |-> [g]`, is a homomorphism, and it is injective: if `[g] = [h]`
then `g^(-1)h` has trivial germ at every point, so every point has a neighbourhood on
which `g^(-1)h` is the identity, and compactness of `C` gives `g = h`. Compose with
`full-group-units-embed-in-steinberg-algebras`, applied to the ample groupoid `G_Γ` with
`R = F_2`.

**3.** Simplicity of `A_k(G)` for `G` ample, Hausdorff, minimal and effective with compact
unit space is the standard Steinberg-algebra criterion. For the centre, let `z in Z(A)`.
Commuting with every `1_(U)`, `U` clopen, forces the support of `z` to lie in the interior
of the isotropy, which is `C` by effectiveness; so `z in C(C, F_2)`. Commuting with every
`1_([g])` makes `z` a `Γ`-invariant function, and minimality makes it constant. Hence
`Z(A) = F_2`.

## Scope

- (CT) is what makes `A` finitely generated; without it the clopen algebra need not be
  generated by finitely many `Γ`-orbits of idempotents. The profinite translation hosts of
  `profinite-translation-hosts-preserve-levels` are the opposite extreme: there every
  finitely generated subring sits at one level.
- Nothing here asserts that `A` is finitely PRESENTED. That is the gate of
  `shell-germ-steinberg-algebra-is-finitely-presented`.
- For `Γ = V` the germ groupoid is the Cuntz groupoid and `A = L_(F_2)(1,2)`, which is
  finitely presented. So the presentation gate is satisfiable in the base case.
