**ESTABLISHED.**  Let `A` be any finite family of operators on `H`, let `K`
be a nonzero finite-dimensional spectator space, and let `Q` be a projection
on `H`.  If `Hull_A(Q)` denotes the smallest `A`-reducing projection whose
range contains `Ran(Q)`, then

```text
Hull_(A tensor I_K)(Q tensor I_K)=Hull_A(Q) tensor I_K.       (CRH1)
```

Indeed, the right side is reducing and contains the amplified seed.  Conversely,
the cyclic reducing space generated by the amplified seed is the closed span
of

```text
{w(A,A^*) Q xi tensor z : w a word, xi in H, z in K},
```

which is exactly `Ran(Hull_A(Q)) tensor K`.

The same identity holds inductively for the canonical BTB tree.  Suppose each
child seed is the join of its parent carrier and finitely many returned ranges,
and the child carrier is the smallest reducing hull for a prescribed finite
packet family.  Amplifying every group letter by `I_K` amplifies every word,
source projection, returned range, finite join, and then the reducing hull.
Thus

```text
Q_sigma^(K)=Q_sigma tensor I_K                         (CRH2)
```

at every reached vertex `sigma`.

In particular, when `K=C^2`, the invariant half
`I tensor diag(1,0)` is a proper reducing subprojection of a reached carrier,
not the reached carrier itself.  Spectator amplification therefore refutes a
return estimate quantified over all reducing subcarriers, but it does not by
itself refute the path-specific overlap estimate on the canonical reached
hulls.
