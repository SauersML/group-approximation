Notation is as in the target. Write `A = alpha(Gamma_0)`, `X_0 = Gamma_0 x_0`, and let `S_0` be a
finite generating set of `Gamma_0`. For a finite `F subset X` let `M_F = M_2(k)^(tensor F)`. The
restricted tensor product `B = M_2(k)^(tensor X)` is the direct limit of the `M_F` under
`a -> a tensor 1`. `Gamma` acts on `B` by permuting sites, written `gamma . b`, and
`R = B x| Gamma` is the skew group ring, with `u_gamma b u_gamma^(-1) = gamma . b`. Write `a_(x)` for
`a in M_2(k)` placed at site `x`.

## Step 0: the site map

`t(gamma H) = alpha(gamma) H` is well defined and bijective because `alpha(H) = H`. We have
`t(gamma x) = alpha(gamma) t(x)` and `t(x_0) = x_0`. It follows that `t(X_0) = A x_0`.

## Step 1: (R2)

Let `psi_B` be the automorphism of `B` moving site `x` to `t(x)`, and put `psi(u_gamma) = u_(alpha(gamma))`.

- *Covariance.* `psi_B(gamma . b)` moves the factor at `x` to `t(gamma x) = alpha(gamma) t(x)`.
  This equals `alpha(gamma) . psi_B(b)`.
- *Extension.* So `psi` extends to a ring endomorphism of `R`.
- *Inverse.* The same construction with `(alpha^(-1), t^(-1))` is its inverse.

## Step 2: (R3)

- `psi(a_(x_0)) = a_(t x_0) = a_(x_0)`.
- `psi(u_s) = u_(alpha(s))`, and `alpha(s) in A <= Gamma_0`.

So `psi(R_0) <= R_0`. Since `R_0` contains `u_gamma` for every `gamma in Gamma_0`, it contains
`a_(gamma x_0) = u_gamma a_(x_0) u_gamma^(-1)`, that is, `M_2(k)` at every site of `X_0`.

## Step 3: the finite orbit, and (R4)

Put `q = g x_0` and `y = t^(-1)(q)`, and let `O = Gamma_0 y`.

- *`O` is finite.* `t(O) = alpha(Gamma_0) t(y) = A q`, which is finite by (HH). Since `t` is
  injective, `O` is finite.
- *`O` misses `X_0`.* `t(X_0) = A x_0`, and `q notin A x_0` by (HH). So `y notin X_0`. Since `O` and
  `X_0` are both `Gamma_0`-orbits, they are disjoint.

Let `z = w^(tensor O)`, with `w = [[0,1],[1,0]]`. Then `z^2 = 1`, so `z` is a unit.

- `z` commutes with `M_2(k)` at `x_0`, because `x_0 notin O`.
- For `gamma in Gamma_0`, `u_gamma z u_gamma^(-1) = w^(tensor gamma O) = z`.

So `z` commutes with `R_0`.

## Step 4: (R5)

We have `psi(z) = w^(tensor t(O))`, and `q = t(y) in t(O)`. Also `q in X_0`, since `g in Gamma_0`.
Take `p = (e_12)_(q)`, which lies in `R_0` by Step 2. Every factor of `psi(z)` except the one at `q`
sits at another site and commutes with `p`, so

```text
psi(z) p psi(z)^(-1) - p = (w e_12 w - e_12)_(q) = (e_21 - e_12)_(q)  !=  0.
```

This is a nonzero element of the copy `M_2(k)_(q)`, which is a simple ring with the same unit as `R`.
So the ideal it generates in `M_2(k)_(q)` already contains `1_R`, and hence `I = R`.

## Step 5: finite generation

`X = Gamma/H` is one `Gamma`-orbit. Let `S` be a finite generating set of `Gamma`. The ring generated by
`M_2(k)` at `x_0` (which is finite, since `k` is finite) and by `u_s^(+-1)` for `s in S` contains the
following:

- every `u_gamma`;
- every `a_(gamma x_0) = u_gamma a_(x_0) u_gamma^(-1)`, which covers every site;
- hence every `M_F` and all of `B`.

So it is `R`, and `R` is a finitely generated ring. The subring `R_0` is finitely generated by definition.

## Step 6: `R` is simple

`B` is simple, because it is a direct limit of the simple rings `M_F`. Let `I != 0` be an ideal of `R`.

1. *A minimal element.* Choose `r = sum_(gamma in T) b_gamma u_gamma` in `I \ {0}` with `|T|`
   minimal, all `b_gamma != 0`. Multiplying on the right by a `u_gamma^(-1)`, we may assume `1 in T`.
2. *Normalizing.* Since `B` is simple, `sum_i c_i b_1 d_i = 1` for some `c_i, d_i in B`. Then
   `r' = sum_i c_i r d_i` has coefficient `sum_i c_i b_gamma (gamma . d_i)` at `gamma`. So its support
   lies in `T`, and its coefficient at `1` is `1`. By minimality its support is exactly `T`. Rename it
   `r`.
3. *Commutators.* For `c in B`, `c r - r c` has coefficient `c b_gamma - b_gamma (gamma . c)` at `gamma`,
   and coefficient `0` at `1`. By minimality it is `0`. So `c b_gamma = b_gamma (gamma . c)` for all
   `c in B` and `gamma in T`.
4. *Contradiction.* Suppose `gamma in T \ {1}`, and let `b_gamma in M_F` with `F` finite. By (IS),
   `gamma` moves some `y'` outside the finite set `F u gamma^(-1) F`. Then `y'` and `gamma y'` are
   distinct and lie outside `F`. For `c = (e_11)_(y')`, Step 3 gives
   `b_gamma tensor e_11 tensor 1 = b_gamma tensor 1 tensor e_11` in
   `M_F tensor M_2(k)_(y') tensor M_2(k)_(gamma y')`. This is impossible for `b_gamma != 0`.

So `T = {1}` and `r = 1 in I`.

## Step 7: (R1), the embedding

Choose representatives `gamma_x` with `x = gamma_x H` and `gamma_(x_0) = 1`. Let `X_n = Gamma/H_n`, a
finite set, and define `phi_n(x) = gamma_x H_n`.

- *Local agreement.* Let `F subset X` and `E subset Gamma` be finite. For all large `n`:
  - `phi_n` is injective on `F u E F`. For `x != x'`, `gamma_(x')^(-1) gamma_x notin H`, hence
    `notin H_n` for large `n`.
  - `phi_n(e x) = e phi_n(x)` for `e in E` and `x in F`. The element `gamma_(ex)^(-1) e gamma_x` lies
    in `H`, hence in `H_n` for large `n`.
- *Target.* Put `N_n = 2^(|X_n|)`, so that `M_2(k)^(tensor X_n) = M_(N_n)(k)`. Let `P_n(gamma)` be the
  permutation matrix that permutes the tensor factors by the action of `gamma` on `X_n`. Then
  `gamma -> P_n(gamma)` is an exact group homomorphism into `GL_(N_n)(k)`, and `Ad P_n(gamma)` moves
  site `phi` to `gamma phi`.
- *Covariant pair.* Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter `omega` and let `Q = prod_omega M_(N_n)(k)`.
  - For `b in M_F`, let `rho(b)_n` be `b` transported along `phi_n` when `phi_n` is injective on `F`,
    and `0` otherwise. This is compatible with `b -> b tensor 1` and multiplicative for all large `n`,
    so it gives a unital ring homomorphism `rho : B -> Q`.
  - `U(gamma) = [P_n(gamma)]` is a group homomorphism into the units of `Q`.
  - `U(gamma) rho(b) U(gamma)^(-1)` puts the factor of `b` at `x` on `gamma phi_n(x)`, while
    `rho(gamma . b)` puts it on `phi_n(gamma x)`. These agree for all large `n`, by local agreement.
- *Conclusion.* By the universal property of the skew group ring, `(rho, U)` induces a unital ring
  homomorphism `R -> Q`. It is nonzero, and `R` is simple (Step 6), so it is injective. This is
  (R1) with `k_n = k`.

If `H` is profinitely closed, enumerate `Gamma \ H = {delta_1, delta_2, ...}`. Choose finite-index
`K_i >= H` with `delta_i notin K_i`, and put `H_n = K_1 meet ... meet K_n`. Then `H_n -> H`.

Steps 1-7 prove the Statement.

## Step 8: (N1), residual finiteness

- *`Gamma` is residually finite.* Given `gamma != 1`, (IS) gives `x = gamma_x H` with `gamma x != x`.
  Then `gamma_x^(-1) gamma gamma_x notin H`, hence `notin H_n` for large `n`. So `gamma` acts
  nontrivially on the finite set `X_n`.
- *`Gamma x|_alpha Z` is residually finite.* This is Mal'cev's argument.
  - The intersection `K_m` of all subgroups of index at most `m` is characteristic and of finite
    index, because `Gamma` is finitely generated. So `alpha` induces an automorphism of finite order
    `e` on `Gamma/K_m`.
  - `(Gamma/K_m) x| Z` contains `(Gamma/K_m) x eZ` with finite index, so it is residually finite.
  - An element `(gamma, j)` with `j != 0` is detected by the map to `Z`. If `j = 0`, it is detected in
    `(Gamma/K_m) x| Z` for some `m`.

## Step 9: (N2), the extended action is not approximable

Let `Lambda = Gamma x|_alpha <t>` act on `X`, with `t` acting by the site map. This is consistent, since
`t gamma x = alpha(gamma) t x`. The stabilizer of `x_0` is `L = H x| <t>`.

Suppose finite `Lambda`-sets `Y_n` and points `y_n` have `Stab(y_n) -> L` in the Chabauty topology.

- *The finite orbit survives.* For `o in O` choose `delta_o in Lambda` with `o = delta_o x_0`, taking
  `delta_y = t^(-1) g`. For large `n`:
  - the map `o -> delta_o y_n` is injective;
  - it avoids `y_n`, since `delta_o notin L`;
  - `s delta_o y_n = delta_(so) y_n` for all `s in S_0^(+-1)`.

  So `O_n = {delta_o y_n}` is `Gamma_0`-invariant and does not contain `y_n`. Hence `O_n` is disjoint
  from `Gamma_0 y_n`.
- *The finite orbit is swallowed.* For large `n`, `t y_n = y_n`. Then
  `t(Gamma_0 y_n) = A y_n <= Gamma_0 y_n`. These finite sets have the same size, so they are equal,
  and `t^(-1)(Gamma_0 y_n) = Gamma_0 y_n`. Since `g in Gamma_0`, it follows that
  `delta_y y_n = t^(-1) g y_n` lies in `Gamma_0 y_n`.

This contradicts the first bullet. So no such approximation exists. This is the combinatorial form of
`matricial-rings-rigidify-compression-centralizers`: in a finite model, `t` cannot map the
`Gamma_0`-orbit of `x_0` onto a proper subset of itself. Every (HH) witness must therefore have a
residually finite `Lambda` whose particular action on `X` is not a limit of finite actions. ∎
