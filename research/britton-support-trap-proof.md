Write `x=sum_b lambda_b b`.  If `b notin A`, Britton's lemma says that
`t b t^(-1)` is reduced and does not represent an element of the base group
`B`.  Distinct such `b` give distinct elements: equality of
`t b t^(-1)` and `t b' t^(-1)` cancels to `b=b'`.  If `b in A`, the HNN
relation reduces the term to `phi(b) in B`.  Since group elements are a
linear basis of `C[G]`, no reduced term coming from `b notin A` can cancel
with a base-group term or with another coefficient.  Therefore `(BST2)`
forces every `b` in `supp(x)` into `A`, and termwise reduction gives `(BST3)`.

If `(BST4)` holds, `A` contains the subgroup generated by `supp(x)`, hence
`A=B`.  Applying the same argument to `t^(-1)y t=x` gives `C=B`.  The HNN is
therefore the mapping torus of an automorphism of `B`.  Its height kernel is
a directed union of copies of `B`; it is hyperlinear when `B` is, and the
quotient is `Z`, so hyperlinear-by-amenable permanence proves the last
assertion.

It remains to prove `(BST8)`.  Let `N=(C_2)^D` be the diagonal-sign subgroup
of `K_D`.  In the tautological block, Fourier inversion gives

```text
E_(ij)=(D/|K_D|) sum_(g in K_D) rho(g^(-1))_(ji) g.      (BST9)
```

The matrix `rho(g^(-1))` is monomial.  A phase word may coincide for several
fixed coordinates of `sigma`, so one must retain the finite Fourier sums
rather than asserting termwise support without checking cancellation.

For the identity permutation, the coefficient of the single-coordinate sign
flip `z_r` is a nonzero scalar multiple of

```text
Tr(Y)-2y_(rr).                                           (BST10)
```

Thus `(BST7a)` puts every `(z_r,1)` in the support, and these elements
generate `N times {1}`.

Now take a transposition `sigma=(r s)`.  On its fixed coordinates the phase
is `1`.  By choosing the diagonal sign `n` independently on those fixed
coordinates, at least one of the resulting signed sums of the nonzero
numbers `y_(jj)` is nonzero.  Hence the support contains `(n sigma,1)` for
some `n`, and the already available `N` removes `n`.  Transpositions generate
`S_D`, so `K_D times {1}` is available.

On the moved coordinate `j=r`, the corresponding phase is

```text
a_s a_r^(-1).                                           (BST11)
```

This phase is distinct from `1` and from the phase on the other moved
coordinate, so its coefficient cannot cancel with another matrix entry; it
is nonzero by `(BST7)`.  Removing the available signed transposition leaves

```text
(1,a_s a_r^(-1)).                                       (BST12)
```

The differences `(BST12)` generate `L_D` (take `r=1`).  Hence the support
generates `K_D times L_D`, proving `(BST8)`.

Equivalently, before collecting possible collisions, each monomial entry
contributes an element of the form

```text
(n sigma, a_(sigma(j)) a_j^(-1)).                       (BST13)
```

Finally, for `0<r<D`, conjugating a signature-`(r,D-r)` diagonal involution
by a generic unitary makes every entry nonzero.  Each additional equation
`Tr(Y)-2y_(rr)=0` is also a proper real-algebraic condition: the diagonal
entry of a conjugated signature matrix is not constant as its `r`-th basis
vector moves between the positive and negative spectral subspaces.  Finitely
many such proper conditions cannot cover the unitary group.  Algebraic
unitary matrices are dense, so an algebraic choice exists.  This proves the
density assertion and completes the route.
