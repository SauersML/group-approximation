```text
F=G_Q x G_P,              Gamma_F=F *_K (K x <h>)
```

from `binary-jacobson-one-step-finite-type-envelope-is-marked-mf`, and impose

```text
(h w_23)^3=1,

(rho^2 h w_12 rho^(-2))(rho h w_12 rho^(-1))(h w_12)
  =(w_23,1),                 rho=w_12 w_23.             (JFM1)
```

The resulting joint quotient has a homomorphism to a finite group which is
nontrivial on the head mark `w=x_13(Q)`.  More generally, every finite list
of relations in the generators `F union {h}` which holds in the literal
binary Jacobson group has a finite model retaining `w`.

Indeed the whole literal subgroup generated by `F` and `h` lies in the
marked LEF group

```text
H=<L,a>=L semidirect Z
```

of `binary-jacobson-finitary-whitehead-shift-is-marked-lef`: the finite
packet `F` is finitary and hence lies in `L`, while the balanced involution
satisfies `h=a w_12` with `w_12 in L`.  Apply the LEF property to the finite
multiplication table containing the packet generators, every relator prefix,
and the marked word.  The resulting partial embedding assigns exact finite
group values to the generators, satisfies all selected relators, and keeps
the mark nonidentity.

Consequently the two shortest cells cannot close the Property-(T)-free
operator-MF obstruction, even through arbitrary complex packet types.  The
natural-module and tail-trivial exclusions remain useful descriptions of
what the finite countermodel cannot look like, but the joint quotient itself
is definitively marked finite.  A load-bearing relation must leave the
one-Whitehead LEF subgroup, equivalently use further Laurent-symbol
elementary incidence absent from `<L,a>`.
