**ESTABLISHED** (proposed) by `higman-edge-closure-absorbs-b-powers-proof`.

**Setting.** `H4(n) = < a, b, c, d | b^a = b^n, c^b = c^n, d^c = d^n, a^d = a^n >` with `n >= 2` and
`x^y = y^-1 x y`. For a quotient `Q` write `A = <a, c>`, `A_M = <a^M, c^M>`. For `w` in the free group
`F(a, c)` let `e_a(w)`, `e_c(w)` be its exponent sums.

**Statement.**
1. **Finite absorption.** Let `Q` be a finite quotient of `H4(n)`, `M >= 1`, and let `w` be a word in
   `a^M, c^M` with `w = 1` in `Q`. Then
   `b^(n^|e_a(w)| - 1)` and `d^(n^|e_c(w)| - 1)` lie in `A_M`.
   Equivalently, `[<b> : <b> ∩ A_M]` divides `n^|e_a(w)| - 1`.
   This is the relative form of the target's Lemma 3.1 ("torsion goes around the cycle": `a^E = 1`
   implies `b^(n^E - 1) = 1`). Here `a^E` is only known to lie in `A'`, or more generally some word
   has nonzero `a`-exponent sum, and `b^(n^E - 1)` lands in the edge group instead of at `1`.
2. **Profinite absorption.** Let `Q` be any quotient of `H4(n)` and `w` a relation of `A_M` as in 1.
   Then `b^(n^|e_a(w)| - 1)` and `d^(n^|e_c(w)| - 1)` lie in the profinite closure of `A_M` in `Q`:
   in every finite quotient of `Q` they lie in the image of `A_M`. The same holds in every profinite
   group `K` receiving `H4(n)`, with the closure taken in `K`.
3. **Edge-closure criterion.** Let `pi : Q -> K` be a homomorphism from a quotient of `H4(n)` to a
   profinite group, for example an action on a locally finite rooted tree (`K = Aut(T)`); it need not
   be injective. If no power `pi(b)^m`, `m >= 1`, lies in the closure of `pi(A)` (or no power of
   `pi(d)` does), then `A` is free on `a, c`.
4. **Uniform absorption for power-perfect pairs.** If `A` is not free (for example `A` amenable),
   then by the base-uniform dichotomy `a^Y` and `c^Y'` lie in `A'` for some `Y, Y' >= 1`. So with the
   **fixed** exponents `m = n^Y - 1`, `m' = n^Y' - 1`, in every finite image `Q_f` of `Q`:
   `b^m, d^m'` lie in `A_f = <a, c>`, and `(a, b^m, c, d^m')` satisfies the Higman relations with
   bases `(n, n^m, n, n^m')`. So `A_f` is a finite quotient of the mixed-base Higman group
   `H(n, n^m, n, n^m')` generated by its edge pair alone.

**What it kills.** Take question (a) of `higman-power-pair-collapse-is-base-uniform-up-to-torsion`:
in an odd base, an amenable power-perfect edge image with infinite-order generators.
- **Matched-pair and exact-factorization quotients.** Suppose a profinite image of `Q` factors as
  `cl(A) cl(B)`, with `B = <b, d>` and `cl(A) ∩ cl(B) = 1`, and `b` has infinite order. Then `A` is
  free. The universal pro-2 image of `H4(n)` for odd `n` is of this shape, as far as it has been
  computed (below). Such a construction can only produce free edge images.
- **Independent tree actions, including the odometer candidate.** Let `a, b, c, d` act on a rooted
  tree `T` (a spherically homogeneous one, say) with `A` amenable. Then the closure of `A` in
  `Aut(T)` contains `b^(n^Y - 1)` and `d^(n^Y' - 1)`. In a pro-2 setting with `n` odd, it contains
  `b^(2^s)` with `s = v_2(n^Y - 1)`. So `b` and `d` cannot be built separately from `a` and `c`
  (for example level-separated, or with coprime orders at each level). A tree counterexample must
  be "diagonal": `b` and `d` must be approximated by words in `a, c` at every level, up to a
  bounded cyclic defect.
- **Coprime-order families.** Suppose `A` is not free and `Q` has finite images in which `|A_f|` is
  coprime to `ord(b_f)`. Then `ord(b_f)` divides `n^Y - 1`, which is bounded.

**Reduction (one direction).** Suppose a residually finite `Q` answers (a) positively. Then some
finite quotients of the fixed mixed-base group `H(n, n^m, n, n^m')` (with `m = n^Y - 1`) satisfy
all of the following, with `ord(a)` unbounded along the family:
- the quotient is generated by the images of `a` and `c` alone;
- `a^Y` lies in the derived subgroup;
- the index `[<b> : <b> ∩ A_f]` is bounded.

The converse is not claimed.

**Calibration.**
- `D_oo x D_oo`, odd `n`: `Y = 2` and `b^2 = 1`, so this is consistent.
- `(Z/(n-1))^4`: consistent.
- The universal pro-2 quotients of `H4(3)`, `H4(5)`, `H4(7)` up to class 6:
  - `|G_k| = |A_k| |B_k|` and `A_k ∩ B_k = 1`;
  - `|A_k| = |F_2 / P_k(F_2)|` (the sequence `2^2, 2^5, 2^10, 2^18, 2^32, 2^55`);
  - this is consistent with a free pro-2 edge closure, which nothing forces to absorb `b`-powers.
    That the full pro-2 completion is an exact product `F^_2(a,c) F^_2(b,d)` is conjectured, not
    proved.
- `H4(3) / <<[a,c]^2>>`, pro-2 classes 1 to 10:
  - `A_k^ab = (Z/16)^2` from class 4 on, so `Y = 16` 2-adically.
  - Item 1 predicts `b^(2^s) ∈ A_k` with `s = v_2(3^16 - 1) = 6`. The computed least such `s` is
    `1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 6, 6, 6, 6`: it stabilizes exactly at 6, while `ord(b)` reaches 128.
  - This is a quantitative check of the dichotomy together with absorption.

**Open, with evidence only.** In the same tower `ord(a)` keeps growing (`16, 16, 32, 32, 64, 64` at
classes 5 to 10) while `A^ab` stays `(Z/16)^2`. This suggests that the residually-2 image of
`H4(3)/<<[a,c]^2>>` has a non-free edge image with finite abelianization and infinite-order
generators. That would show that item 2 of the target needs `A ∈ X`, not just non-freeness. It is
not proved here, and nothing here decides whether that edge image is amenable.
