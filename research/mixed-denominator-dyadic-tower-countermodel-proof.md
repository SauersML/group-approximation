Fix an odd `m>=3`, put `Q=SL_3(Z/mZ)`, and let bars denote reduction
modulo `m`.  Let

```text
D_6=<a,z:a^3=z^2=1, zaz=a^(-1)>                       (MDC1)
```

and work first in the finite direct product `D_6 times Q`.  Use the gauge
coordinates

```text
E_12=(1,xbar_12(1)),             E_23=(1,xbar_23(1)),
A_12=(1,xbar_12(2)),             A_23=(1,xbar_23(2)),
B_21=(z,xbar_21(-1)),            B_32=(z,xbar_32(-1)),
W_12=(z,wbar_12(1)),             W_23=(z,wbar_23(1)),
H=(a,hbar).                                             (MDC2)
```

Here `hbar=diag(2,1,2^(-1))`.  Let `G_0` be the finite group generated by
the elements in `(MDC2)`, and let `L_0` be the subgroup generated by all
the displayed lattice coefficients, excluding `H`.

This is the abstract finite-group version of the packet in
`closed-a2-loop-fold-countermodel-proof`.  The positive-root gauges are
one and the two negative-root and Weyl gauges are `z`, so the two
rank-one packages and both closed returns hold coordinatewise.  Put

```text
v=aza^(-1)z=a^2.                                       (MDC3)
```

Both torus factors have gauge coordinate `v`; hence their product has
gauge `v^2=a`.  Their `Q`-coordinates multiply to `hbar`.  Therefore the
unbalanced torus relation is exact in `G_0`.

Now put `C_13=[E_12,E_23]=(1,xbar_13(1))`.  The gauge coordinate of
`H B_21 H^(-1)` commutes with the trivial gauge coordinate of `C_13`, so
the Chevalley formula in `Q` gives

```text
[H B_21 H^(-1),C_13]
 =(1,xbar_23(-1/2))
 =H^(-1)E_23^(-1)H.                                   (MDC4)
```

This proves the mixed relation `(MDT1)` as an equality of actor elements.
It is important that `(MDC4)` is not merely a scalar trace identity.

The first-coordinate image of `L_0` is contained in `<z>`, while the
first coordinate of `H` is `a notin <z>`.  Thus

```text
H L_0 != L_0                                             (MDC5)
```

in the finite coset set `X=G_0/L_0`.

For a prescribed `R`, form

```text
K_R=(*_(x in X) C_(2^R,x)) semidirect G_0,              (MDC6)
```

where `G_0` permutes the free factors through its action on `X`.  This
group is virtually free and residually finite.  Choose a finite quotient
which preserves every nontrivial lamp power and every reduced two-lamp
word needed below, and take its left regular representation.  If `t_x`
is the generator of the factor at `x`, set

```text
T=lambda(t_(L_0)).                                      (MDC7)
```

The element `T` has exact order `2^R` and commutes with `lambda(L_0)`.
Its consecutive spectral blocks give nested PVMs `q_(r,j)` of trace
`2^(-r)`.  Actor conjugation gives

```text
lambda(g)q_(r,j)^x lambda(g)^*=q_(r,j)^(gx).            (MDC8)
```

Because distinct lamp factors form a free product, the Fourier expansion
of two spectral blocks has only its constant term at the identity.  The
chosen finite quotient preserves all other words in that finite expansion,
so for `x!=y`

```text
tr(q_(r,i)^x q_(s,j)^y)=2^(-r-s)                       (MDC9)
```

for every `r,s<=R`.  Equations `(MDC4)` and `(MDC8)` show that the two
mixed-loop paths send each physical atom to exactly the same atom.
Finally `(MDC5)` makes the lamp commutator `[T,lambda(H)]` a nontrivial
reduced two-lamp word; preserving it in the finite quotient gives regular
trace zero.  This proves all assertions of the claim.
