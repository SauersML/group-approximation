Notation: `L^2(R)` is `L^2` of the counting-times-`mu` measure on `R`; `L(R)` acts on the left,
`(u_phi xi)(x,y) = xi(phi^-1 x, y)` and `(f xi)(x,y) = f(x) xi(x,y)` for `f in L^infinity(X)`. `L(R)` is the von
Neumann algebra generated by `L^infinity(X)` and `{u_phi : phi in [R]}`. Put `N = W*(u_g : g in Gamma)`.

**Imported input (full-group cut and paste).** A. Carderi, A. Giraud, F. Le Maître, *Classification of non-free
p.m.p. boolean actions of ergodic full groups and applications*, arXiv:2304.01536v3, source-read through the arXiv
HTML on 2026-09-17, quoted as retrieved: Proposition 3.4, "Let 𝔾⩽Aut(X,μ) be an ergodic full group. Then for every
A,B∈MAlg(X,μ) such that μ(A)=μ(B), there is an involution T∈𝔾 such that T(A)=B and supp(T)=A∪B." We use it only
for disjoint `A, B`, and only for `𝔾 = [R]` with `R` ergodic, which the paper treats as an ergodic full group.
`[R]` is closed under cutting and pasting: an automorphism that agrees on each piece of a countable partition with an
element of `[R]` is in `[R]`.

**Step 1 (uniform to strong).** By the trace formula `tau_R(v_g)=mu(Fix(g))` of
`full-group-subgroup-trace-generation-dichotomy`, `||u_phi - u_psi||_2^2 = 2 d_u(phi, psi)`. For `x in L(R)`,
`||(u_phi - u_psi) x-hat|| = ||(u_phi - u_psi) x||_2 <= ||u_phi - u_psi||_2 ||x||`, and `L(R)-hat` is dense in
`L^2(R)` while `||u_phi - u_psi|| <= 2`. So `d_u(g_n, phi) -> 0` implies `u_(g_n) -> u_phi` strongly. Since
`Gamma` is dense, `u_phi in N` for every `phi in [R]`.

**Step 2 (dyadic involution groups).** Fix a measurable `B` with `0 < mu(B)`. We build commuting involutions
`T_1, T_2, ... in [R]`, supported in `B`, such that `G_k = <T_1, ..., T_k>` is isomorphic to `(Z/2)^k` and permutes
simply transitively a partition `B = ⊔_(w in {0,1}^k) B_w` into sets of measure `2^-k mu(B)`. Start with `G_0 = {e}`
and `B_empty = B`. Given level `k`, split `B_(0^k)` into disjoint `C_0, C_1` of equal measure (`mu` is nonatomic)
and take, by Proposition 3.4, an involution `S in [R]` with `S(C_0) = C_1` and support `C_0 ∪ C_1 = B_(0^k)`. For
`w` let `s_w in G_k` be the unique element with `s_w(B_(0^k)) = B_w`. Put `B_(w i) = s_w(C_i)` and define
`T_(k+1) = s_w S s_w^-1` on `B_w`, the identity off `B`. It is in `[R]` by cut and paste and is an involution. For
`s in G_k`, on `B_(s w)` we get `s T_(k+1) s^-1 = s s_w S s_w^-1 s^-1 = s_(sw) S s_(sw)^-1 = T_(k+1)`, because
`G_k` is abelian and acts simply transitively. So `T_(k+1)` commutes with `G_k`, swaps `B_(w0)` and `B_(w1)`, and
`G_(k+1)` acts simply transitively on level `k+1`.

**Step 3 (averaging).** `P_k = 2^-k sum_(s in G_k) u_s` lies in `N` (Step 1) and is the orthogonal projection onto
the `G_k`-invariant vectors of `L^2(R)`. The `P_k` decrease and converge strongly to the projection `P` onto the
vectors invariant under `G_infinity = ∪ G_k`. Let `xi` be invariant. For `mu`-a.e. `y`, `x -> xi(x, y)` is in
`l^2([y]_R)` and is invariant under the countable group `G_infinity`. For `x in B` the `G_k`-orbit of `x` has `2^k`
points, one in each `B_w`, so the `G_infinity`-orbit of `x` is infinite and `xi(x, y) = 0`. Conversely a vector
supported on `(X \ B) x X` is invariant, because `G_infinity` fixes `X \ B` pointwise. Hence `P = 1_(X \ B)`, and
`1_B = 1 - P in N`.

**Step 4 (generation).** `N` contains every projection `1_B`, hence `L^infinity(X)`, and every `u_phi`
(Step 1). So `N = L(R)`.

**Step 5 (character).** `tau_R(u_g) = chi_R(g)`, and `N = L(R)` is cyclic for the trace vector, so the GNS
construction of `chi_R` on `Gamma` is `(L(R), tau_R)` with `g -> u_g`. The center of `L(R)` lies in
`L^infinity(X)' ∩ L(R) = L^infinity(X)` (the Cartan subalgebra is maximal abelian) and commutes with every `u_phi`,
so it consists of `R`-invariant functions, which are constant by ergodicity. A factorial GNS algebra means `chi_R` is
extremal. CE of `chi_R` means a trace-preserving embedding of its GNS algebra into `R^U`, which is CE of `L(R)`.
