Let `H`, `M` and `Omega` be as in the claim, with `dim M = n in {2, 3}`.

1. **Passing to subgroups.** Let `K <= H` be finitely generated. `K` preserves
   `Omega`, and it acts properly discontinuously on `Omega`, because properness
   restricts to subgroups. `K` is virtually torsion-free, because `H` is.
   Choose a torsion-free subgroup `K' <= K` of finite index. A group containing
   a finitely presented subgroup of finite index is finitely presented, so it
   suffices to show that `K'` is finitely presented.

2. **Effectiveness.** Suppose `g in SL_3(R)` acts trivially on a nonempty open
   subset of `M`.
   - For `M = RP^2`: `g` fixes four points in general position, so `g` is a
     scalar. A real scalar of determinant one is `1`.
   - For `(RP^2)^*`: the same argument applies to `g^{-T}`.
   - For `F`: an open set of flags projects onto an open subset of `RP^2`, and
     each of those points is fixed, so again `g = 1`.
   - For `R^3 \ {0}`: `g` fixes an open set of vectors, so `g = 1`.

   So `K'` acts faithfully on `Omega`.

3. **Free proper action.** `K'` is torsion-free and acts properly
   discontinuously, so every stabilizer is finite and hence trivial. The
   action is free and properly discontinuous. The quotient `N = Omega / K'` is
   therefore a connected `n`-manifold, and `Omega -> N` is a covering map.
   `Omega` is connected and simply connected, so it is the universal cover
   and `pi_1(N) = K'`. Here `n = 2` for `RP^2` and `(RP^2)^*`, and `n = 3` for
   `F` and `R^3 \ {0}`.

4. **Dimension two.** `N` is a connected surface, possibly noncompact or
   non-orientable, with `pi_1(N) = K'` finitely generated.
   - If `N` is noncompact, `pi_1(N)` is free, since every noncompact surface
     deformation retracts onto a graph. It is free of finite rank because it
     is finitely generated.
   - If `N` is compact, `pi_1(N)` has a one-relator presentation, or is
     trivial or `Z/2`.

   Either way `K'` is finitely presented.

5. **Dimension three.** `N` is a connected 3-manifold with finitely generated
   fundamental group. By Scott's compact core theorem (P. Scott, *Compact
   submanifolds of 3-manifolds*, J. London Math. Soc. (2) 7 (1973) 246–250;
   see also *Finitely generated 3-manifold groups are finitely presented*,
   same volume, 437–440), `N` contains a compact submanifold `C` such that
   inclusion induces an isomorphism `pi_1(C) -> pi_1(N)`. The fundamental
   group of a compact manifold is finitely presented. So `K'` is finitely
   presented.

   This proves the claim.

6. **Consequence (i).** Let `Omega ⊂ RP^2` be properly convex and open, and
   let `K <= SL_3(Z)` preserve it.
   - `Aut(Omega)` is a closed subgroup of `SL_3(R)` and acts properly on
     `Omega`, because it preserves the Hilbert metric and that metric is
     proper.
   - `K` is discrete, so it acts properly discontinuously on `Omega`.
   - `Omega` is convex in an affine chart, hence contractible.

   Now apply the claim with `M = RP^2`.

   For the second sentence of (i), let `Omega` be a convex open subset of an
   affine chart that is not properly convex. Up to projective equivalence it
   is the whole chart, an open half-plane or an open strip (a convex open
   planar set containing a full affine line is one of these). In `RP^2` the
   chart is the complement of one projective line, and a half-plane or a strip
   is a component of the complement of two distinct projective lines (for a
   half-plane, the line at infinity and the boundary line). So the boundary of
   `Omega` in `RP^2` is a union `D` of one or two projective lines. A
   projective map preserving `Omega` preserves `D`, and every projective line
   contained in `D` is one of its (at most two) constituent lines, so the map
   permutes them. So a subgroup of index at most `2` in the
   stabilizer of `Omega` fixes a projective line, and its Zariski closure lies
   in a proper parabolic subgroup. Zariski density passes to finite-index
   subgroups, so `K` is not Zariski dense. By
   `non-zariski-dense-subgroups-of-sl3z-are-coherent`, `K` is coherent, and in
   particular it is not a witness. Properness of the action is not needed
   here.

7. **Consequence (ii).** Under Vinberg's hypotheses (E. B. Vinberg, *Discrete
   linear groups generated by reflections*, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Ser. Mat. 35
   (1971) 1072–1112), the reflection representation `W -> GL_3(R)` is
   faithful, and `W` acts properly discontinuously on the interior `int C` of
   the Tits cone. `int C` is a convex open cone in `R^3 \ {0}`, hence simply
   connected. The claim applies with `M = R^3 \ {0}` and `Omega = int C`, after
   passing to the index-at-most-two subgroup `W cap SL_3(R)`. Coherence
   passes to finite-index overgroups: a finitely generated subgroup `L <= W`
   meets `W cap SL_3(R)` in a finitely generated subgroup of finite index in
   `L`, and that subgroup is finitely presented. So `W` is coherent.

8. **Consequence (iii).** This is the case `M = F` of the claim, stated in
   contrapositive form.
