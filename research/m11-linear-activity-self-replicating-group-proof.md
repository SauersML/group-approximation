The notation is that of the target. Maps act on the right and words are read left to right.
For `g ∈ Aut(X*)` and `x ∈ X`, `g@x` acts as `g` below `x` and trivially elsewhere. For
`y ∈ X`, fix `p_y ∈ M_11` with `1^{p_y} = y`, and `p_1 = 1`. Then:

- **(C)** `p_y^{-1} (g@1) p_y = g@y`. Elements of `Stab_{M_11}(1)` commute with `g@1`.
- **(W)** Every `g` with root permutation `π` satisfies `g = (∏_y g|_y@y) · π`, and the
  factors `g|_y@y` commute.

So `a = (a@1) α`, `b = (b@1)(α@2)(β@3)` and `c = (c@1)(a@2)`.

## 0. The rigid-stabilizer certificate and the section words

For `q ∈ Stab_{M_11}(1)` put `h_q = b^{-1} (q^{-1} b q) ∈ ⟨M_11, b⟩`. The root permutation
of `q^{-1} b q` is trivial and its section at `y` is `b|_{y^{q^{-1}}}`. So `h_q` has
trivial root permutation, trivial section at `1`, and section
`e_y(q) = b|_y^{-1} b|_{y^{q^{-1}}} ∈ {1, α^{±1}, β^{±1}}^2 ⊆ M_11` at `y ≠ 1`. The
elements of `M_11` act at the root only, so `h_q` is determined by its tuple
`(e_y(q))_{y=2..11} ∈ M_11^{10}`. The script builds these tuples for **all**
`q ∈ Stab_{M_11}(1)`. It finds that they generate the whole of `M_11^{10}` (check (2)).

So for each generator `p` of `M_11` there is a word `W_p` in the `h_q` equal to `p@2`.
Fix one. Define the endomorphism `σ : F → F` on generators by

- `σ(p) = p_2 W_p p_2^{-1}`, which represents `p@1` by (C);
- `σ(a) = a α^{-1}`, which represents `a@1`;
- `σ(c) = c · (p_2^{-1} σ(a) p_2)^{-1}`, which represents `c(a@2)^{-1} = c@1`;
- `σ(b) = b · (p_3^{-1} σ(u_β) p_3)^{-1} (p_2^{-1} σ(u_α) p_2)^{-1}`, which represents
  `b (β@3)^{-1}(α@2)^{-1} = b@1`.

Here `u_α` and `u_β` are fixed words in the generators of `M_11` for `α` and `β`, and `α`
also denotes `u_α` inside `σ(a)`. Since `g ↦ g@1` is a homomorphism `R → R`, **`σ(w)`
represents `w@1` for every `w ∈ F`**. Script check (1) verifies the three level identities
on `X^4`.

**Exponent sums.** Let `e_a` and `e_c` be the exponent sums of `a` and `c`. For integers
`s` and `t`, put `χ_{s,t} = s e_a + t e_c : F → ℤ`. This is the homomorphism with
`a ↦ s` and `c ↦ t` that kills `b` and the generators of `M_11`.

The words `σ(p)`, `σ(u_α)` and `σ(u_β)` use only `b` and generators of `M_11`. So
`χ_{s,t} σ` kills `b` and the generators of `M_11`. It sends `a ↦ χ_{s,t}(a α^{-1}) = s`
and `c ↦ t − s`, since the conjugate of `σ(a)` in `σ(c)` enters inverted. Hence

- **(E)** `χ_{s,t} ∘ σ = χ_{s, t−s}`, and so `χ_{s,t} ∘ σ^n = χ_{s, t−ns}`.

## 1. Item 1

**Self-similar.** The sections of the generators are `a, b, c, α, β, 1`, all in `R`.

**Closed under `@1`.** `R@1` is generated by the `s@1` for `s ∈ S`. Each of these is
represented by `σ(s)`, so it lies in `R`. By (C), `R@y = p_y^{-1} (R@1) p_y ⊆ R` for every
`y`. So `R ⊇ R^X ⋊ M_11 = R ≀_X M_11`, and self-similarity gives the reverse inclusion.
The section map is the restriction of the isomorphism
`Aut(X*) ≅ Aut(X*) ≀_X Sym(X)`, so `ψ : R ≅ R ≀_X M_11`. The root image is `M_11`, since
`a` has root permutation `α ∈ M_11` and `b` and `c` have trivial root permutation. ∎

## 2. Items 2 and 3

**Orders.**

- `a^{11}` has root permutation `α^{11} = 1`. Its section at `y` is the product of the
  sections of `a` along the `α`-cycle through `y`: one `a` and ten `1`'s. So
  `a^{11} = (a, …, a)`, and `ord_n(a) = 11 · ord_{n−1}(a) = 11^n`.
- `c` fixes the root, with `(c^m)|_1 = c^m`, `(c^m)|_2 = a^m` and all other sections `1`.
  So `ord_n(c) = lcm(ord_{n−1}(c), ord_{n−1}(a)) = 11^{n−1}` by induction.
- The same computation gives `ord(b) = lcm(ord(b), 11, 5) = 55`, since `α` has order `11`,
  `β` has order `5`, and `ord_1(b) = 1`.

All of these match check (3). In particular `a` and `c` have infinite order.

**Not contracting.** `(c^m)|_{1^k} = c^m` for all `k`. A finite nucleus `𝒩` would contain
`g|_v` for all long `v`, so it would contain every `c^m`. These are pairwise distinct.

**Activity.** Let `A_n(g) = #{v ∈ X^n : g|_v ≠ 1}`.

- `A_n(a) = 1`, since the only nontrivial section is `a` at `1^n`.
- `A_n(b) = 3`, from `b` at `1^n`, `α` at `1^{n−1}2` and `β` at `1^{n−1}3`, since finitary
  sections vanish below.
- `A_n(c) = n + 1`, from `c` at `1^n` and `a|_{1^{n−k−1}} = a` at `1^k 2 1^{n−k−1}` for
  `0 ≤ k < n`.

These match check (4). So `R` is generated by the states of a finite automaton of linear
activity. By Amir--Angel--Virág (J. Eur. Math. Soc. 15 (2013); cited), `R` is amenable, so
it has no non-abelian free subgroup. ∎

## 3. Item 4

This is `tits-hosts-for-transitive-local-actions` item 4. Its hypotheses are item 1 here
together with the setting of item 6 there: `M_12` on the 12 colours, `F_0 = M_11`. The
edge groups `R × R` are amenable by item 3. ∎

## 4. Item 5: the canonical L-presentation

**Lemma W (presentation of a permutational wreath product).** Let `Γ = F/M` be any
quotient of `F` in which the generators of `M_11` generate a copy of `M_11`. Then
`Γ ≀_X M_11` has a presentation with:

- **generators:** the generators of `M_11` and symbols `t_s` for `s ∈ S`, where `t_s`
  stands for `s@1`;
- **relators:**
  - the relators of `M_11`;
  - `w(t)` for `w ∈ M`;
  - `[q, t_s]` for `q` in a generating set of `Stab_{M_11}(1)`;
  - `[t_s, p_y^{-1} t_{s'} p_y]` for `y ≠ 1`.

*Proof.* These relators hold, and the named elements generate. So the presented group
`H'` maps onto `Γ ≀ M_11`. Let `T ≤ H'` be generated by the `t_s`, and let
`T_y = p_y^{-1} T p_y`.

- `Stab(1)` centralizes `T`. So `T_y` depends only on `y`, and `M_11` permutes the `T_y`
  as it permutes `X`.
- For `y ≠ y'`, conjugating by `p_y` turns `[T_y, T_{y'}]` into `[T_1, T_{y''}]` with
  `y'' ≠ 1`, and these are trivial.
- `T` is a quotient of `Γ`, via `w(t) = 1`.

So `H' = (∏_y T_y) ⋊ M_11` is a quotient of `Γ ≀ M_11`, and the two surjections are
inverse. ∎

**The formal section map.** Define `Ψ : F → Γ ≀_X M_11` by:

- `p ↦ p` on the generators of `M_11`;
- `a ↦ (a@1) α`;
- `b ↦ (b@1)(α@2)(β@3)`;
- `c ↦ (c@1)(a@2)`.

Here `r@y` means the image of `r ∈ F` in `Γ`, placed at coordinate `y`.

**Claim.** `Ψ(σ(s)) = s@1` for every `s ∈ S`.

*Proof.* Take the generators in turn.

- **`a`.** `Ψ(a α^{-1}) = a@1`.
- **`c`.** `Ψ(σ(c)) = (c@1)(a@2) · (p_2^{-1} (a@1) p_2)^{-1} = c@1`, using (C) in the
  wreath product.
- **The generators of `M_11`.** `Ψ(h_q) = Ψ(b)^{-1} q^{-1} Ψ(b) q`. Its coordinate at `1`
  is `(b@1)`-part times its inverse, which is trivial. Its coordinate at `y ≠ 1` is
  `e_y(q)`, computed in the copy of `M_11` inside `Γ`. So `Ψ(W_p)` is the element of
  `M_11^{10}` that `W_p` evaluates to in §0, namely `p@2`. Then (C) gives
  `Ψ(σ(p)) = p@1`.
- **`b`.** The previous case gives `Ψ(σ(u)) = u@1` for every word `u` in the generators of
  `M_11`. Then `Ψ(σ(b)) = (b@1)(α@2)(β@3)(β@3)^{-1}(α@2)^{-1} = b@1`. ∎

**The key step: one presentation shape for both `R ≀ M_11` and `G ≀ M_11`.** Take `Γ` as
in Lemma W. Adjoin `a, b, c` to its presentation with the relations `s = Ψ(s)`, written in
the `t`'s. Adjoin also `t_s = σ(s)`, which holds by the Claim. Now eliminate the `t`'s by
Tietze moves.

The relations `s = Ψ(s)[t := σ]` become free tautologies:

- `a = a α^{-1} α`;
- `c = c (p_2^{-1} σ(a) p_2)^{-1} p_2^{-1} σ(a) p_2`;
- `b = b (p_3^{-1}σ(u_β)p_3)^{-1}(p_2^{-1}σ(u_α)p_2)^{-1} · (p_2^{-1}σ(u_α)p_2)(p_3^{-1}σ(u_β)p_3)`.

What remains is

`Γ ≀_X M_11 = ⟨S | Q_0 ∪ σ(M)⟩`, via `s ↦ Ψ(s)`.   (†)

**(a).** Apply (†) with `Γ = R` and `M = N`. `Ψ(s)` is then `ψ(s)`, and `ψ` is injective.
So the kernel of `F → R ≀ M_11` is `N`, and by (†) it is also `⟨⟨Q_0 ∪ σ(N)⟩⟩`.

**(b).** `σ(⟨⟨Y⟩⟩) ⊆ ⟨⟨σ(Y)⟩⟩`, so `⟨⟨Q_0 ∪ σ(L)⟩⟩ = ⟨⟨⋃_{n≥0} σ^n(Q_0)⟩⟩ = L`.
Also `L ⊆ N`, since `Q_0 ⊆ N` and `σ(N) ⊆ N`.

The generators of `M_11` generate `M_11` in `G = F/L`. They satisfy its relators, and they
map onto `M_11 ⊆ R`. So (†) applies with `Γ = G` and `M = L`. It shows that `s ↦ Ψ(s)`
induces an isomorphism `ψ_G : G ≅ G ≀_X M_11`. With `π : G ↠ R`, the relation
`(π ≀ id) ∘ ψ_G = ψ ∘ π` holds on generators, so it holds everywhere.

**(c).** `g ∈ K` iff `π(g) = 1`. Since `ψ` is injective, this holds iff
`ψ_G(g) ∈ ker(π ≀ id) = K^X`. So `ψ_G(K) = K^X`. The action of `G` on `X*` factors through
the faithful action of `R`, so its kernel is `K`. If `K` were finite, then `|K| = |K|^{11}`
would force `K = 1`. ∎

## 5. Items 6 and 7

**Item 6.** By (E), each `χ_{s,t} ∘ σ^n` is again of the form `χ_{s',t'}`. It therefore
kills:

- the relators of `M_11`, which are words in generators that it kills;
- every commutator in `Q_0`, since its target is abelian.

So `χ_{s,t}` kills `⋃_n σ^n(Q_0)`, hence `L`, and it descends to `G`. `χ_{1,0}` and
`χ_{0,1}` are independent, so `dim_ℚ H_1(G; ℚ) ≥ 2`.

`G` is finitely generated, and `G ≅ G ≀_X M_11` by (b). Moreover `M_11 ≤ Alt(11)`, since it
is simple, and `d = 11 ≥ 4`. The acyclic node applies to any group with such an
isomorphism, and its item 3(d) with `k = ℚ` says that a finitely presented such group has
`dim H_1 ≤ 1`. So `G` is not finitely presented. ∎

**Item 7.**

- If `K = 1`, then `R = G`, which is not finitely presented.
- Suppose `R` is finitely presented. Item 3(d) of the acyclic node gives
  `dim Hom(R, ℚ) ≤ 1`. `Hom(R, ℚ)` is the space of characters of `G` that vanish on `K`.
  So some nonzero `χ_{s,t}` does not vanish on `K`. Pick `k ∈ K` with `χ_{s,t}(k) ≠ 0`,
  and lift it to `w ∈ N`. Then `s e_a(w) + t e_c(w) ≠ 0`. Also `w ∉ L`, since `χ_{s,t}`
  kills `L`.

Finally, `b_1(R) ≤ 2`. The generators of `M_11` die in `H_1(R)` because `M_11` is perfect,
and `b` has finite order, so only `a` and `c` can have infinite image. ∎

## 6. What was not done

The brief asked for a test of Bartholdi's L-presentation signature, meaning infinitely many
independent relators. Item 6 establishes this for the canonical cover `G`, by homology and
not by counting relators. Whether `G = R` is exactly the question of whether `K = 1`. No
computation here decides it:

- every finite congruence quotient is `M_11 ≀ ⋯ ≀ M_11`, which is perfect, so it sees
  neither the characters nor `K`;
- this environment has no FR package for words in non-contracting groups.
