OPEN, and this is the single checkable question on which the prize now turns.

After the reductions of [[b-shift-strong-freeness-linearization-pff]], MF of
the rope (hence finite-presentation `Pi^0_2`-completeness of MF) holds if:

```text
a uniformly random N-cycle sigma_N is strongly asymptotically free from a
fixed strongly convergent PFF family { u_g }, where the amalgamation corner
B = L(F_r) is generated by the fixed family, NOT by sigma_N.            (Q)
```

Two readings:

* **Scalar suffices.**  The `L`-corner `B` is generated by the fixed PFF
  family `{u_g}`; `sigma_N` is a *scalar* random permutation.  Bordenave--
  Collins (Ann. Math. 2019 / Cambridge 2020) prove that independent uniform
  random permutations are strongly asymptotically free, and the strong
  convergence holds jointly with any fixed strongly convergent family.  If the
  amalgamation is only used to *define* the shifted moments and is carried
  entirely by `{u_g}`, then `(Q)` is a direct corollary: `sigma_N` strongly
  free from `{u_g}` gives all mixed norms, and the `B`-corner structure is a
  book-keeping of the `{u_g}`-moments already controlled.  This would CLOSE
  the prize.  `[PLAUSIBLE, not verified]`.

* **Operator-valued needed.**  If the shift genuinely acts on
  `l^2(L)`-valued Fock space so that the amalgamation couples `sigma_N` to
  `B` non-trivially, the scalar theorem is insufficient and one needs an
  operator-valued / relative Bordenave--Collins (Magee--de la Salle style),
  which is heavier and may not be in the literature in the exact form.
  `[POSSIBLE]`.

Resolving `(Q)` -- reading the Bordenave--Collins statement against the exact
corner structure of the rope model -- is the concrete remaining task, and it
is a *literature-checking / careful-statement* task, not obviously a new
theorem.  This is the sharpest and most likely-closable form of the prize's
last step.
