Put `K=E_T=ker(E->Z)`.  The split normal form gives `E=K rtimes_alpha Z`,
hence

```text
C*_(max)(E)=C*_(max)(K) rtimes_(alpha,max) Z.
```

For a finite group-ring packet and a large interval `F subset Z`, precompose
the regular representation with the quotient from the full to the reduced
crossed product and compress to `F`.  This is a u.c.p. map

```text
kappa_F:C*_(max)(E)->M_F(C*_(max)(K)).
```

All entries needed for the packet, its pairwise products, and the compression
boundary contain only finitely many elements of `K`; this includes every
varying coefficient `alpha^(-j)(a)` for the finitely many `j` that occur.
Let `H<=K` be the single finitely generated subgroup generated by all of them.
By `literal-telescope-core-lef`, `H` is residually finite.  One finite quotient
`q:H->Q` separates every nonidentity group element occurring in the diagonal
coefficients.  The quotient regular representation
`pi:C*_(max)(H)->M_|Q|` has the correct canonical trace on those coefficients.
The maximal subgroup inclusion

```text
C*_(max)(H) -> C*_(max)(K)
```

is isometric (induce arbitrary representations of `H` to `K`), so Arveson's
theorem extends `pi` to one u.c.p. map
`phi:C*_(max)(K)->M_|Q|`.  On every collected entry, this one map is still
the exact homomorphism `pi`.

Set `Phi=(id_(M_F) tensor phi) kappa_F`.  For Fourier width at most `R`,

```text
kappa_F(xy)-kappa_F(x)kappa_F(y)
 =P_F Lambda(x)(1-P_F)Lambda(y)P_F.
```

After `pi`, this matrix has at most `2R|Q|`-dimensional initial support, while
its operator norm is at most `||x|| ||y||`.  Therefore

```text
||Phi(xy)-Phi(x)Phi(y)||_(2,tr)
 <=||x|| ||y|| sqrt(2R/|F|),
```

which tends to zero.  On `a u^r`, the compressed shift has zero diagonal for
`r!=0`; for `r=0`, quotient separation makes the normalized trace of every
diagonal block equal to the identity coefficient of `a`.  Thus
`tr compose Phi=tau_E` exactly on the chosen group-ring packet.

Fourier density, contractivity, and a diagonal sequence now give u.c.p.
matrix maps asymptotically multiplicative in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
and recovering the maximal/full canonical trace `tau_E`.  Brown's Theorems
3.1.6 and 4.1.9 give Kirchberg's factorization property.  The boundary
estimate is only Hilbert--Schmidt; its operator norm need not go to zero, so
this does not conflict with the proved failure of quasidiagonality/operator
MF.
