The Steinberg relation gives `[z,k]=x_21(1)`.  Hence the standard
characteristic-two Weyl word

```text
omega=x_12(1)x_21(1)x_12(1)
```

has the `(1,2)` block `[[0,1],[1,0]]` and fixes the other coordinates.
This proves `(JWS2)` and the coordinate-permutation formulas
`(JWS4)--(JWS5)`.

For `(JWS3)`, put `A=f omega f`.  Since `rf=-f`, right multiplication gives

```text
Ar=-A.                                                     (1)
```

But `omega r=c omega` by `(JWS2)`, and `cf=+f`, so also

```text
Ar=f omega r f=f c omega f=+A.                            (2)
```

Thus `A=0` over the complex representation space.

It remains to show that this is genuinely beyond the finite envelope `P`.
Apply the Laurent symbol map

```text
J -> F_2[t,t^(-1)],       Q|->0, S|->t, T|->t^(-1).
```

The image of `P` is generated by

```text
x_13(t),       x_21(t^(-1)),       x_41(t^(-1)),           (3)
```

because all its other listed generators have a factor `Q`.  Conjugating
`(3)` by

```text
Delta=diag(t^(-1),1,1,1)
```

turns all three into constant elementary matrices.  Therefore
`Delta symbol(P) Delta^(-1)` is contained in `GL_4(F_2)`.

On the other hand the `(1,2)` block of
`Delta symbol(omega) Delta^(-1)` is

```text
[[0,t^(-1)],[t,0]],                                       (4)
```

which is not constant.  Hence `omega` is not in `P`.  This proves both the
finite-envelope separation and the stated boundary.
