**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-major-product, 2026-09-18; not independently reviewed; no
priority claimed). It is conditional exactly where its inputs are:
- finite presentation of `R^×` and `GL_5(R) = E_5(R)`: the audit
  `khanh-ara-cortinas-hypotheses-hold-for-leavitt-tensor-hosts`, which rests on Khanh's
  unrefereed preprint arXiv:2609.08428;
- Martínez-Pérez–Matucci–Nucinkis, arXiv:1309.7858, Corollary 4.11, applied to Brin's `2V`
  (see step G1).

## Statement

Let `p` be a prime, `L_p = L_(F_p)(1,2)`, `R = L_p ⊗ L_p` and `M_p = C(C × C, F_p)`, the
locally constant `F_p`-valued functions on the Cantor square. `R` acts on `M_p` through the
tensor square of the Cantor representation:
`(s_α t_β ⊗ s_γ t_δ) f (α w, γ v) = f(β w, δ v)`, and `0` off `[α] × [γ]`.
Then:

1. `M_p ⋊ R^×` is finitely presented.
2. Its affine action on `M_p` is of type (A). So `R^×`, and every subgroup of `R^×`, lies in
   `B_A`. By BFFHZ Theorem C (`fp-simple-highly-transitive-groups-satisfy-pbh`), each such
   group embeds in a finitely presented simple highly transitive group.
3. For `p = 2`, `R^×` is itself finitely presented and simple, so this finitely presented
   simple group is in `B_A`. For odd `p`, the simple group `R^×/F_p^×` of
   `prime-field-leavitt-tensor-hosts-fp-and-simple-mod-centre` is *not* covered: `B_A` is not
   quotient-closed, and scalars act non-trivially on `M_p`. Every subgroup of `R^×` is
   covered, including the explicit hard groups of
   `leavitt-square-units-have-conp-parity-p-complete-word-problem` and
   `leavitt-square-units-mod-p-complete-word-problem`, which are generated by units of `R`.

## Proof

**Setup.**
- The representation is faithful: `R` is simple (audit item 3) and the representation is
  nonzero.
- **Brin's `2V` inside `R^×`.** An element `g ∈ 2V` is a bijection between two brick partitions
  `{[β_k] × [δ_k]}` and `{[α_k] × [γ_k]}` of `C²`, made of brick maps
  `(β_k w, δ_k v) ↦ (α_k w, γ_k v)`. The element `u_g = Σ_k s_(α_k) t_(β_k) ⊗ s_(γ_k) t_(δ_k)`
  acts on `M_p` by `f ↦ f ∘ g^(-1)`. So by faithfulness `u_g u_h = u_(gh)` and
  `u_(id) = 1`, which gives an injective homomorphism `2V → R^×`.

**G1: `M_p ⋊ 2V` is of type `F_∞`.**
- **The finite subgroup.** Take a `p`-leaf prefix code `u_0, …, u_(p−1)` of `C` (for example
  `0, 10, …, 1^(p−1)`), bricks `B_k = [u_k] × C`, and brick maps `β_k : B_0 → B_k`. Let
  `ρ ∈ 2V` act by `β_(k+1) β_k^(-1)` on `B_k`, with indices mod `p` and `β_p = β_0 = id`.
  It has order `p` and acts freely.
- **Its centralizer.** For `g ∈ C_(2V)(ρ)` and `x ∈ B_0`, write `g(x) ∈ B_(k(x))` and
  `ḡ(x) = β_(k(x))^(-1) g(x) ∈ B_0`. Then `g(β_j x) = β_(j+k(x)) ḡ(x)`.
  - The map `k : B_0 → Z/p` is locally constant.
  - `ḡ` lies in `2V(B_0)`: refine the brick partition of `g` so that each piece lies in one
    `B_k`. It is a bijection by the injectivity and surjectivity argument of Part (i) of
    `cantor-module-thompson-v-product-fp-via-mmn-centralisers`.
  - Conversely, every pair `(k, h)` defines such a `g`.
  - Multiplication is `(k_1, ḡ_1)(k_2, ḡ_2) = (k_2 + k_1 ∘ ḡ_2, ḡ_1 ḡ_2)`.
  - So `C_(2V)(ρ) ≅ C(B_0, Z/p) ⋊ 2V(B_0)`, with the precomposition action. Conjugating by a
    brick bijection `C² → B_0` identifies this with `M_p ⋊ 2V` and the Leavitt action.
- **Type `F_∞`.** MMN Corollary 4.11 (arXiv:1309.7858, quoted verbatim in
  `research/artifacts/leavitt-module-fp-2026-09-13.md`): "Suppose U_r(Σ) is valid, bounded
  and complete. Then V_r(Σ) is of type quasi-F∞. In particular, centralisers of finite groups
  are of type F∞."
  - Brin's `sV_n` has a valid and bounded algebra (their Example 2.7(ii), with `s` colours
    of arity `n`), which is complete (Remark 2.15).
  - Their companion presentation paper (TeX at `gqsrc/mmn/main.tex` on MSI, l.314–320 and
    l.1110) restates this for `sV`: "under some further minor restrictions, being complete,
    these groups are of type F∞ and ... centralisers of finite subgroups are of type F∞".
    Its l.419 treats `s = 2`, `r = 1` explicitly.
  - With `s = 2`, `n = 2`, `C_(2V)(ρ)` is `F_∞`.

**G2: `R^×` is generated by finitely many brick transvections.**
- By the audit (items 8–9), `R^× ≅ GL_5(R) = E_5(R)`. The isomorphism is conjugation by the
  Leavitt isometries `σ_i = 1 ⊗ s_(u_i)` of a 5-leaf prefix code in the second factor, so it
  sends `e_ij(r) ↦ 1 + σ_i r τ_j` with `τ_j = 1 ⊗ t_(u_j)`.
- `E_5(R)` is generated by the `e_ij(r)` with `i ≠ j` and `r` in the ring generating set
  `Γ = {1, s_a ⊗ 1, t_a ⊗ 1, 1 ⊗ s_a, 1 ⊗ t_a : a = 0, 1}`. This uses
  `e_ij(r + r') = e_ij(r) e_ij(r')` and `e_ij(rr') = [e_ik(r), e_kj(r')]`.
- So `R^×` is generated by the 180 elements `x = 1 + y` with `y = σ_i r τ_j`, `r ∈ Γ`. Each `y`
  is a monomial `s_α t_β ⊗ s_γ t_δ`, i.e. the brick bisection `φ : E = [α]×[γ] → D = [β]×[δ]`
  acting by `(y·f)(e) = f(φ(e))` on `E` and by `0` off `E`.
- `E ∩ D = ∅`, since the second coordinates lie under the distinct leaves `u_i` and `u_j`. So
  `y² = 0`, `x^(-1) = 1 − y = x^(p−1)`, and `Y = C² ∖ (D ∪ E) ⊇ C × [u_k]` for `k ≠ i, j` is
  nonempty.

**G2': finitely many relators make each transvection normalize `M_p`.**
- **The commuting subgroup.** Put `C_x = {c ∈ 2V : c(D) = D, c(E) = E, c|_E = φ^(-1) c|_D φ}`.
  - For `c ∈ C_x` the actions of `c` and `y` on `M_p` commute. Both sides send `f` to
    `f ∘ φ ∘ c^(-1)` on `E`, and to `0` off `E`.
  - By faithfulness, `c` and `x` commute in `R^×`.
- **Finitely many orbits.** `M_p` is additively generated by `𝒢 = {1_A : ∅ ≠ A clopen, A ⊆ D
  or A ⊆ E or A ⊆ Y}`, because `D, E, Y` partition `C²`. `C_x` contains
  `2V(D) × 2V(Y)`, acting by `h` on `D`, by `φ^(-1) h φ` on `E`, and by `k` on `Y`.
  - The full groups `2V(D)` and `2V(Y)` of purely infinite minimal groupoids with `H_0 = 0`
    are transitive on the proper nonempty clopen subsets of `D`, resp. `Y`.
  - So `C_x` has exactly six orbits on `𝒢`, with representatives `1_D`, `1_A` (`A ⊊ D`), `1_E`,
    `1_(A')` (`A' ⊊ E`), `1_Y`, `1_(A'')` (`A'' ⊊ Y`).
- **The relators.** For each `x` and each representative `a`, impose `x a x^(-1) = x·a`, where
  `x·1_A = 1_A + 1_(φ^(-1)(A ∩ D))` is written in `M_p ⋊ 2V`.
  - Conjugating by `c ∈ C_x`, which commutes with `x`, gives `x(c·a)x^(-1) = x·(c·a)`.
  - So these 6 relators force the correct conjugation on all of `𝒢`, hence on `M_p`. Since
    `x^(-1) = x^(p−1)`, `x` normalizes `M_p`.

**Assembly.** Let `G' = ((M_p ⋊ 2V) *_(2V) R^×) / ⟨⟨ the 1080 relators ⟩⟩`.
- **Finite presentation.** `M_p ⋊ 2V` is finitely presented (G1), `R^×` is finitely
  presented (the audit), and `2V` is finitely generated. So the amalgam is finitely
  presented, and so is `G'`.
- **The image of `M_p` is normal.** `2V` and every generator of `R^×` normalize it.
- **The quotient by it is `R^×`.** Modulo `M_p` the relators become trivial.
- **`M_p` injects.** The natural map `G' → M_p ⋊ R^×` is onto, and it is injective on `M_p`
  and on `R^×`. Its kernel meets `M_p` trivially and maps trivially to `R^×`, so it is
  trivial.
- So `G' ≅ M_p ⋊ R^×`, which proves item 1.

**Type (A).** By `orbit-finite-affine-actions-are-type-a` item 3 we need three things:
- `R^× ≤ Aut(M_p)`, which is faithfulness;
- `R^×` finitely generated;
- finitely many `R^×`-orbits on `M_p`. Already `2V` has at most `2^p − 1` orbits: `f` is an
  ordered partition of `C²` into its level sets, and `2V` is transitive on ordered clopen
  partitions with a given pattern of nonempty pieces, by gluing bisections between nonempty
  clopens, all equivalent since `H_0 = 0`.

So item 2 holds. Item 3 is bookkeeping. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

The one-factor argument for `L^×` has two parts:
- a finite subgroup of the Thompson-type group whose centralizer carries the module;
- local commuting subgroups that make finitely many relators suffice.

Both parts transfer word for word to any Leavitt-type unit group that contains a
Brin–Thompson-type group acting transitively on clopens with `H_0 = 0`, such as `nV` inside
`L^(⊗n)`. So on the ring side, Boone–Higman comes in its permutational form for free as soon
as finite presentation of the unit group is known. The scalars are the only obstruction left
there: they act on `M_p` and block the projective quotients at odd `p`.
