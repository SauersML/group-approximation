Notation is as in the target and in the imported criterion
(`artin-sigma1-equals-dead-cut-laplacian-surjectivity`). All modules are left modules. `N =
N_χ`. `N_{≥s}` is the set of elements supported at levels `≥ s`, and
`N_{≥s} N_{≥s'} ⊆ N_{≥s+s'}`. The imported criterion gives:
- (C) `[χ] ∈ Σ^1` iff `L_χ` is onto `K_r(N)`, for `χ` dominant (its parts 2–3);
- (S) `g_ρ = ε_ρ σ_ρ + δ_ρ`, `σ_ρ ∈ Z[H]`, `δ_ρ ∈ N_{>0}`, each `g_ρ` a finite sum
  (Step 7 of its proof).

**Step 1 (R_m implies membership).**
- Let `c^{(i)} ∈ P_m` satisfy `R_m`. Then `v_i := Σ_ρ c^{(i)}_ρ g_ρ (e_{a_ρ} - e_{b_ρ})` lies
  in `K_r(ZG)`, since every `e_a - e_b` does.
- Its part at levels `≤ 0` is `f_i`. So `v_i = f_i + Σ_j M_{ij} f_j`, where the `f`-coordinates
  `M_{ij}` of `v_i - f_i` lie in `N_{≥1}`.
- Exactly as in Step 7 of the imported proof, every entry of `M^n` lies in `N_{≥n}`, so
  `Σ_n (-M)^n` converges and `I + M` is invertible in `M_{r-1}(N)`.
- The image of `L_χ` contains the rows of `I + M`, hence every `f_i`. So `L_χ` is onto, and
  `[χ] ∈ Σ^1` by (C).

**Step 2 (membership implies R_m for some m).**
- By (C), for each `i < r` there is `λ^{(i)} ∈ N^{Rel}` with
  `Σ_ρ λ^{(i)}_ρ g_ρ (e_{a_ρ} - e_{b_ρ}) = f_i`.
- By the Novikov finiteness condition, each of the finitely many `λ^{(i)}_ρ` has only
  finitely many group elements at levels `≤ 0`. Let `-m` be the least level occurring,
  or `m = 0` if all levels are positive.
- Split `λ = c + λ_+`, where `c` is the part of `λ` at levels `≤ 0` and `λ_+ ∈ N_{≥1}`. Then
  `c ∈ P_m` is a finite element of `(ZG)^{Rel}`.
- Since `g_ρ ∈ N_{≥0}` by (S), `λ_+ g_ρ ∈ N_{≥1}`. Taking parts at levels `≤ 0` gives
  `(Σ_ρ c_ρ g_ρ (e_{a_ρ} - e_{b_ρ}))_{≤0} = (f_i)_{≤0} = f_i`. So `R_m` holds.
- Steps 1 and 2 prove part 1.

**Step 3 (part 2).**
- `P_m ⊆ P_{m+1}`, and the condition in `R_m` does not mention `m`. So `R_m ⇒ R_{m+1}`.
- For `m = 0`, `c ∈ Z[H]^{Rel}`. The level-0 part of `c_ρ g_ρ` is `c_ρ ε_ρ σ_ρ`, since
  `δ_ρ ∈ N_{≥1}`. So `R_0` says that the image of `L_0` (with the signs `ε_ρ`, which do not
  change the image) contains every `f_i`, that is, `L_0` is onto `K_r(Z[H])`.

**Step 4 (part 3).**
- The open claim for `(Γ, χ)` is: `L_χ` onto `⇒` `L_0` onto. By parts 1 and 2 this is:
  `(∃ m : R_m) ⇒ R_0`, which is the same as `R_m ⇒ R_0` for every `m`.

**Step 5 (the depth-1 criterion, part 4).** Take `r = 2`. The cut map `K_2(R) -> R` is an
isomorphism (Step 9 of the imported proof), and it turns the system into the left ideal
generated by the scalars `g_ρ`, up to signs that are absorbed into `ε_ρ`.
- Write `λ_ρ = μ_ρ + ν_ρ t^{-1}` with `μ_ρ, ν_ρ ∈ Z[H]`, the general element of `P_1`.
- `g_ρ = ε_ρ σ_ρ + δ_ρ`. For `ρ ∈ D_1`, `δ_ρ = σ_ρ h_ρ t` at level `1`. For the other
  D-generators, `δ_ρ` sits at level `χ(p) ≥ 2`. For V and W, `δ_ρ = 0`.
- *Level -1.* The only contribution is `Σ_ρ ν_ρ t^{-1} ε_ρ σ_ρ = Σ_ρ ν_ρ θ^{-1}(ε_ρ σ_ρ) t^{-1}`.
  So the level-(-1) condition reads `Σ_ρ θ(ν_ρ) ε_ρ σ_ρ = 0`, that is, `ν' := θ(ν) ∈ Syz_0`.
- *Level 0.* The contributions are `μ_ρ ε_ρ σ_ρ`, and `ν_ρ t^{-1} σ_ρ h_ρ t =
  ν_ρ θ^{-1}(σ_ρ h_ρ)` for `ρ ∈ D_1`. A D-generator with `χ(p) ≥ 2` reaches only level `≥ 1`
  from level `-1`. The condition is `Σ_ρ μ_ρ ε_ρ σ_ρ + Σ_{D_1} ν_ρ θ^{-1}(σ_ρ h_ρ) = 1`.
  Applying `θ`, it becomes `Σ_ρ θ(μ_ρ) ε_ρ θ(σ_ρ) + d^1(ν') = 1`.
- `μ` is arbitrary, and so is `θ(μ)`. So `R_1` holds iff `1 ∈ θ(J_0) + d^1(Syz_0)`.
- `d^1` is left `Z[H]`-linear and `Syz_0` is a left submodule. So the right-hand side is a
  left ideal, and it contains `1` iff it is `Z[H]`.
- Finally, `R_0` fails iff `J_0 ≠ Z[H]` (Step 3 for `r = 2`). Together these give the last
  sentence of part 4.

**Remarks.**
- In the spectral-sequence language of the w17 attempt on the open claim, `d^1` is the first
  differential of the level filtration, from level-0 syzygies to the `θ`-twisted cokernel
  `Z[H]/θ(J_0)`. Part 4 says that `R_1 ∧ ¬R_0` is exactly the statement that `d^1` hits a
  unit of `Z[H]/θ(J_0)` while that ring is nonzero.
- Everything above is exact algebra in `ZG` and `N`, plus the imported criterion.
- `R_m` is a finite system. Its data are the finitely many `g_{ρ,k} ∈ Z[H]` and their
  `θ`-twists, which all lie in a finitely generated subgroup of `H`.
