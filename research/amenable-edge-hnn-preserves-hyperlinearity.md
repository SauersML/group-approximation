Let `G` be a countable hyperlinear group, let `A<=G` be amenable, and let
`theta:A->G` be injective.  Then the HNN extension

```text
H=<G,t | t a t^(-1)=theta(a), a in A>                   (AEH1)
```

is hyperlinear.

Consequently an HNN chord cannot be the first source of nonhyperlinearity
when its base is hyperlinear and its associated subgroup is amenable.  This
excludes not only finite-edge resets, but also infinite cyclic edges,
locally finite edges, and amenable proper-infinite compression groups.  A
decisive Bass--Serre return for the signed-Hecke atlas must therefore use a
nonamenable associated subgroup; alternatively, the return must leave the
HNN/Bass--Serre framework.

The conclusion is about the whole HNN group, not merely the stable-letter
orbit of one packet.  Thus replacing finite character transports by an
amenable infinite packet does not evade the permanence obstruction.
