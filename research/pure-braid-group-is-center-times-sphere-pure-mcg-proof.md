Let `f: P_n → P_2 ≅ Z` delete strands `3, …, n`. Deleting strands sends full twists to full twists, so
`f(Δ_n^{2m}) = σ_1^{2m}`. Hence `f` is injective on `⟨Δ²⟩`, so `ker f ∩ ⟨Δ²⟩ = 1`. For `g ∈ P_n` with
`f(g) = σ_1^{2m}`, the element `g Δ^{−2m}` lies in `ker f`, so `ker f · ⟨Δ²⟩ = P_n`. Since `Δ²` is central,
both factors are normal, and `P_n = ker f × ⟨Δ²⟩`.

For `n ≥ 3`, `Z(P_n) = ⟨Δ²⟩` (Chow 1948). Identify `B_n` with `Mod(D_n)`. Capping the boundary of `D_n` with a
once-punctured disk gives a surjection `P_n → PMod(S_{0,n+1})` whose kernel is generated by the boundary
Dehn twist, which is `Δ²` (the capping homomorphism; Farb–Margalit, *A Primer on Mapping Class Groups*, §3.6
and Chapter 9). So `ker f ≅ P_n/Z(P_n) ≅ PMod(S_{0,n+1})`.
