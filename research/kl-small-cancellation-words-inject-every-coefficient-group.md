Let `G` be any group and let

```text
w = g_1 t^(e_1) g_2 t^(e_2) ... g_k t^(e_k),     g_i in G \ {1},  e_i != 0,
```

be cyclically reduced in `G * <t>`, of free-product length `2k`, and not a
proper power.  Let `sigma(w)` be the largest number of consecutive syllables
that agree exactly, syllable by syllable, in two different cyclic positions
of `w`, or in one cyclic position of `w` and one of `w^(-1)`.  If

```text
sigma(w) < k/3 - 2,                                          (SC1)
```

then `G -> (G * <t>)/<<w>>` is injective.  No exponent-sum hypothesis is
used.

## What it does to a violation search

A repeated `t`-exponent gives `sigma >= 1`, so the theorem says nothing for
`k <= 9`.  From `k = 10` on, a Kervaire--Laudenbach violation over any group
needs an exact repeated block of at least `k/3 - 2` syllables, about `k/6`
variable occurrences with the same interior coefficients, inside `w` or
between `w` and `w^(-1)`.  Long random or generic coefficient words are
excluded at every length.  Violations have to be nearly periodic without
being proper powers; the self-referential cyclic-cover shapes already in the
Leavitt lane are exactly of that kind.
