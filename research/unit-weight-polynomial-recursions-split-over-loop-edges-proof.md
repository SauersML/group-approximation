**Status: ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** §1–§4 are proofs by hand. §5 is a finite exact check.
The outside facts are standard Bass–Serre theory (Serre, *Trees*, I.4–I.6) and Sidki's
polynomial-activity subgroups, and they are stated where they are used. Notation is that of
the claim.

## 1. Lemma T (item 1)

**`G' = ⟨⟨P⟩⟩`.**
- `P` is perfect, so `P ≤ G'`, and hence `⟨⟨P⟩⟩ ≤ G'`.
- For the converse, work in `Ḡ = G/⟨⟨P⟩⟩`. Since `G = G^X ⋊ P`, the group `Ḡ` is generated
  by the images of the `ι_x(G)`.
- For `π ∈ P` we have `π^{-1} ι_x(g) π = ι_{x^π}(g)`. So the image of `ι_x(g)` in `Ḡ` does
  not depend on `x`, because `P` is transitive.
- Take `x ≠ z`. The subgroups `ι_x(G)` and `ι_z(G)` commute. So every `ι_x(g)` commutes with
  every `ι_z(h)`, and these images coincide with those of `ι_x(h)`.
- Hence the image of `ι_x(G)` is abelian. It generates `Ḡ`, so `Ḡ` is abelian and
  `G' ≤ ⟨⟨P⟩⟩`.

**Conjugates of coordinate copies.** Write `h = (h_0, …, h_10)π` with `h_y ∈ G`. Then
`h^{-1} ι_y(g) h = ι_{y^π}(h_y^{-1} g h_y)`. Since `G'` is normal, `h^{-1} ι_y(G') h = ι_{y^π}(G')`.

**Case 1: some `a ∈ ι_x(G)` is hyperbolic.** Let `L` be the axis of `a`, fix `y ≠ x`, and
put `B = ι_y(G)`.
- `B` commutes with `a`, and `b(L)` is the axis of `b a b^{-1} = a`. So `B` preserves `L`.
- The action of `B` on `L` factors through `Aut(L) ≅ D_∞`.
- The finite perfect group `ι_y(P) ≅ P` maps into `D_∞`, whose finite subgroups have
  order at most `2`. So it maps trivially.
- Hence the kernel of `B → Aut(L)` contains `⟨⟨ι_y(P)⟩⟩_B = ι_y(⟨⟨P⟩⟩_G) = ι_y(G')`.
- This kernel fixes `L` pointwise, so `ι_y(G')` lies in the stabilizer of every edge of
  `L`.

**Case 2: every element of every `ι_x(G)` is elliptic.**
- Each `ι_x(G) ≅ G` is finitely generated and consists of elliptic elements, so it fixes a
  vertex (Serre I.6.5, Cor. 3).
- Put `T_0 = Fix(ι_0(G))`, a nonempty subtree. `ι_1(G)` commutes with `ι_0(G)`, so it
  preserves `T_0`.
- An elliptic element that preserves a subtree fixes a point of it: take the projection of
  one of its fixed points. So `ι_1(G)` acts on `T_0` by elliptic elements, and it fixes a
  vertex of `T_0`.
- Continuing over `x = 2, …, 10`, the common fixed set `T_{10}` of all the `ι_x(G)` is
  nonempty, since each new factor commutes with the earlier ones. So `G^X` fixes a vertex.
- `G^X` has finite index `|P|` in `G`, so `G` has a bounded orbit. Hence `G` fixes a vertex
  or the midpoint of an edge, and since there are no inversions, it fixes a vertex.

**Non-perfect `P`.** Drop perfectness in Case 1. The finitary automorphisms
`⋃_n P ≀ ⋯ ≀ P` placed at `y` form an infinite locally finite subgroup of `B`. Its image in
`D_∞` is locally finite, so it has order at most `2`. The kernel restricted to this subgroup
is still infinite and locally finite, and it fixes `L`. ∎

One-endedness is not used anywhere. This is why the edge groups in item 2 may be
infinite.

## 2. The tree-of-factors obstruction (item 2)

Suppose `G` is finitely presented, and let `τ_N`, `ρ_N` be as in §1 of the proof of
`linear-m11-bouquet-recursions-are-not-finitely-presented`. That construction is item 1 of
`pure-loop-self-replicating-groups-are-not-fp`: for every normal `N` with
`⟨⟨Q^∞⟩⟩ ⊆ N ⊆ K` there is `τ_N : G → F/N` with `ρ_N τ_N = id`.

**The tree of groups.** `F` is the fundamental group of the tree of free groups
`(F(B_v), F(E_e))`, with the inclusions as edge maps. This is the subtree condition on the
letters, by induction on `|Y|`, peeling off a leaf with Serre I.1.
- For each edge let `R_e = ker(ρ|_{F(E_e)})`. This is possibly infinitely generated, which
  is allowed, because only `Q^∞` has to be finite in the retract criterion.
- By (i), choose conjugates `Q_v ⊆ F(B_v)` of the words of `Q^∞`.
- Put `N = ⟨⟨Q^∞ ∪ ⋃_e R_e⟩⟩_F`. Then `⟨⟨Q^∞⟩⟩ ⊆ N ⊆ K`.
- Let `Γ_v = F(B_v)/⟨⟨Q_v ∪ ⋃_{e ∋ v} R_e⟩⟩` and `D_e = F(E_e)/R_e ≅ ρ(F(E_e))`.
- The maps `D_e → Γ_v` are injective, because their composites with `Γ_v → G` are the
  isomorphism `D_e ≅ ρ(F(E_e))`.
- `N` is normally generated by subsets of the vertex groups. So `F/N` is the colimit of
  the tree of groups `(Γ_v, D_e)`, which has injective edge maps. Hence `F/N` is its
  fundamental group (Serre I.4), and it acts on the Bass–Serre tree without inversions,
  with edge stabilizers the conjugates of the `D_e`.
- Also `ρ_N(Γ_v) = ρ(F(B_v))` and `ρ_N(D_e) = ρ(F(E_e))`.

**Fixed vertex.** `G` acts on the tree through `τ = τ_N`.
- If some edge stabilizer contained `ι_y(G')`, then `τ(ι_y(G')) ≤ g D_e g^{-1}`. Applying
  `ρ_N` would give `ι_y(G') ≤ h ρ(F(E_e)) h^{-1}` with `h = ρ_N(g)`.
- By §1 this means `ρ(F(E_e)) ⊇ ι_z(G')` for some `z`, contradicting (ii).
- So by Lemma T, `τ(G) ≤ g Γ_v g^{-1}` for some `v`. Applying `ρ_N` gives
  `G ≤ h ρ(F(B_v)) h^{-1}`, so `ρ(F(B_v)) = G`, contradicting (iii). ∎

With two vertices and `ρ(F(E))` finite, this is the bouquet node's item 1. A finite group
contains no `ι_y(G')`, which is infinite because it contains every `ι_y ι_0^k(P)`. So (ii)
holds there.

## 3. The polynomial loop star (item 3)

Assume (U), (Poly), (L1_p), (N) and `d ≥ 1`.

**(3.1) Level `p` behaves like (L1).** Consider a signed cycle letter `s^ε`, lying on a
cycle `Z` of length `L | p`.
- A weight-one section path of length `mp` from `s^ε` back to `s^ε` cannot leave `Z`,
  because the SCC DAG is acyclic. Inside `Z` each node has exactly one out-edge in `Z`. So
  the path visits `s^ε` at every multiple of `L`, and hence at every multiple of `p`.
- By (L1_p), each block of `p` steps is the unique level-`p` loop, read at the current
  point. So the level-`mp` loop states are the level-`p` loops placed on constant words
  `c^m`, with `c ∈ X^p`.
- A level-`p` section `s^ε|_z` with `z` not the loop word stays inside `Z` for all `p`
  steps only if it ends at `s^ε` (since `L | p`). It would then be a second level-`p` loop.
  So it has left `Z`, and it is a rooted word or a word whose state letter lies in `D`.
- `D` is closed under sections: letters reachable from `D` are in `D`. So `U := F(R ∪ D)`
  is closed under level-1 sections.

**(3.2) Where the stable relators live.** Let `q ∈ Q^∞`. By item 2 of
`same-point-loop-recursions-reduce-to-loop-graph-words`, `q = q''|_u` for a relator `q''` on
a cycle of the section digraph, with `q''|_{v} = q''` and `v^{q''} = v`, `|v| = m`.
- Going round the cycle `p` times, we may replace `q''` by the cycle word `X` at a position
  whose distance to `q` along the digraph is `≡ 0 mod p`. Then `X|_y = X`, `y^X = y`,
  `|y| = pm`, and `q = X|_{u'}` with `|u'| ≡ 0 mod p`.
- By Lemma A of the same node (a level-free statement under (U)), `X` labels a closed path
  at `y` in `Δ_{pm}`. By (3.1), `y = c^m` and `X` is a word in the loops `ℓ_s^{±1}` of one
  component `Λ` of the level-`p` loop graph. So `X ∈ V_Λ := F(ℓ_s : s ∈ Λ)`.
- Read `u'` in blocks of length `p`. At a block equal to the path vertex, `X|_c = X`,
  because each loop is read at its own loop word.
- At any other block `c' ≠ c`, the tracked point differs from the path vertex at every
  letter. For `ℓ_s^{-1}`, the loop section occurs exactly at the head `w_s` of the edge. So
  every piece is an off-loop section, and `X|_{c'} ∈ U` by (3.1).
- `U` is section-closed. So `q ∈ V_Λ` or `q ∈ U`, and (i) holds without conjugation.

**(3.3) The star.**
- Nielsen-replace each cycle letter by its loop: `B = R ∪ {ℓ_s} ∪ {other letters}`.
- Sub-bases: the centre `B_U = R ∪ {ℓ_t : t ∈ D cycle} ∪ (D ∖ cycle letters)`; the leaves
  `B_Λ = {ℓ_s : s ∈ Λ}`; and a leaf `{n}`, joined by an empty edge, for each non-cycle
  letter `n ∉ D`.
- `ℓ_s ∈ F(B_U)` if and only if `s ∈ D`. The components are disjoint, so each letter spans a
  subtree. The edges are `E_Λ = {ℓ_s : s ∈ Λ ∩ D}`.

**(3.4) Activity.** For an automorphism `g` let `θ_g(n)` be the number of `v ∈ X^n` with
`g|_v ≠ 1`, and let `Pol(k)` be the set of `g` with `θ_g(n) = O(n^k)`.
- `(gh)|_v = g|_v h|_{v^g}`, so `θ_{gh} ≤ θ_g + θ_h` and `θ_{g^{-1}} = θ_g`. Hence `Pol(k)` is
  a group (Sidki).
- *Upper bound.* By induction on the SCC DAG, a letter of degree `k` lies in `Pol(k)`.
  - A non-cycle letter has boundedly many nontrivial sections at level 1, all of them lower.
  - A cycle letter at level `n` has at most `L` weight-one sections on its cycle. The
    sections that leave the cycle at level `j` contribute `θ_{lower}(n − j) = O(n^{k−1})`.
    Summing over `j ≤ n` gives `O(n^k)`.
  - Rooted letters lie in `Pol(−1)`, with eventually no nontrivial sections.
- *Lower bound.* Let `s` be a cycle letter of degree `k ≥ 0`, with a chain
  `s = s_k, …, s_0` of cycle letters, `s_{i−1}` reached from `s_i` after leaving its cycle.
  - For `n` large there are at least `c n^k` vertices `v ∈ X^n` (choose when to leave each
    cycle) with `s|_v = a ℓ_{s_0}^{±1} b`, where `a` and `b` are rooted.
  - Such a section is nontrivial. Otherwise `ℓ_{s_0}` would be rooted, and then
    `ℓ_{s_0} = ℓ_{s_0}|_{x_{s_0}} = 1`, contradicting (N).
  - So `θ_s(n) ≥ c n^k`. The same holds for any element with a section `a s'^{±1} b`, `s'` of
    degree `k`, with `n` shifted.

**(3.5) Checking (ii) and (iii) of item 2.**
- *Centre.* Every letter of `D` has degree `≤ d − 1`: it is reached from a cycle letter
  after leaving its cycle, and the degree drops by at least one. So
  `ρ(F(B_U)) ⊆ Pol(d − 1)`, while a top-degree cycle letter is not in `Pol(d − 1)` by (3.4).
  So `ρ(F(B_U)) ≠ G`.
- *Free leaves.* They are cyclic, and `G ⊇ P = M_11` is not.
- *Leaves `V_Λ`.* (iii) is condition (b). A leaf without top-degree letters maps into
  `Pol(d − 1)`. A proper root image is proper. So is a subgroup that is intransitive on
  some level, because `G` is level-transitive (`P` is transitive and `G ⊇ ι_v(P)` for all
  `v`).
- *Edges.* (ii) is condition (a).
  - If `|E_Λ| ≤ 1`, the edge image is cyclic, while `ι_y(G') ⊇ ι_y(P) ≅ M_11`.
  - Otherwise every letter of `E_Λ` has degree `< d`, so `ρ(F(E_Λ)) ⊆ Pol(d − 1)`. Now `G'`
    contains an element `g` with a section `a s'^{±1} b`, `s'` of degree `d`. So
    `θ_{ι_y(g)}(n) ≥ c (n−2)^d`, and `ι_y(G') ⊄ Pol(d − 1)` for every `y`.

**(3.6) Components satisfying (free).** Let `Λ` satisfy (free).
- Choose a spanning tree `T` of `Λ` that contains the forest of `D`-loops, and a base vertex
  `c_0`. Since `rank π_1(Λ) ≤ 1`, at most one edge `f` lies outside `T`, and `f ∉ D`.
- For an edge path `π` let `λ(π)` be its word in the `ℓ_e^{±1}`. Then `λ` is a morphism of
  the path groupoid (backtracks cancel).
- Put `γ_f = λ(π_0 f π_1)`, where `π_0, π_1` are the tree paths `c_0 → tail f` and
  `head f → c_0`. It is `ℓ_f^{±1}` times words in tree letters. So replacing `ℓ_f` by
  `γ_f` is a Nielsen move, and `{ℓ_e : e ∈ T} ∪ {γ_f}` is a basis of `V_Λ`.
- *New sub-bases.* The `ℓ_e` with `e ∈ T ∩ D` stay in the centre. Each `ℓ_e` with
  `e ∈ T ∖ D` gets a free leaf with empty edge. `C_c := F(γ_f)` is a leaf with empty edge,
  and it is absent if `Λ` is a tree. These sets are disjoint from each other and from the
  other sub-bases.
- *Relators.* By (3.2) a stable relator `q ∈ V_Λ` equals `λ(π)` for a closed path `π` at
  some `c ∈ Λ`. Conjugating by `λ` of the tree path `c_0 → c` gives a closed path at `c_0`.
  Its `λ`-image lies in `λ(π_1(Λ, c_0)) = F(γ_f)`, or is trivial for a tree. So (i) holds up
  to conjugation, which item 2 allows.
- *(ii) and (iii).* These hold because the new edges are empty and the new leaves are
  cyclic.

Mixing (3.6) components with (a)(b) components keeps the star shape: every leaf is still
attached only to the centre. Item 2 now applies. ∎ The case `d = 0` is the bounded case of
`same-point-loop-recursions-reduce-to-loop-graph-words`, item 5.

## 4. The escape routes (item 4)

Items 3(a), 3(b) and (N) are the only hypotheses beyond (U) and (Poly).
- The failure of (N) is a finite condition that the screen checks.
- A component escapes only if it fails (free) and also fails (a) or (b). Failing (a) is
  (α) or (β). Failing (b), when (free) also fails, is (γ).
- (Poly) fails exactly when some SCC has a node with two out-edges inside it. This gives
  exponential activity.
- In the bouquet node the obstruction ran over a finite edge `⟨T⟩`. Here a nontrivial loop
  `ℓ` is never finitary, since `ℓ|_{x_ℓ^k} = ℓ ≠ 1`. The edge is killed by cyclicity or by
  degree instead of by finiteness, so "finite-order loop blocks" play no role.

## 5. The screen (item 5)

`poly_star_screen.py` imports the exact word and permutation arithmetic of `loopgraph.py`
from the loop-graph screen. For each recursion it decides every hypothesis of item 3
exactly:
- (U) and (Poly), from the signed weight-preserving digraph and its SCCs;
- `p`, the degrees and `D`;
- (L1_p), by listing the level-`p` loops;
- the components, by union-find on the loop edges;
- (free), from the forest test on the `D`-edges and `rank = #edges − #vertices + 1`;
- (a1) and (a2), where (a2) takes the level-1 sections of `[x, s]` for `x ∈ {a, e}`;
- (b), from the root image order (`< 7920`) and from orbit counts on levels 2 and 3;
- (N), from `Rec.trivial` in `loopgraph.py`. A section with nonidentity root permutation
  certifies that the loop is nontrivial. A finite section closure with all roots trivial
  certifies that it is trivial. No run hit the closure cap.

Each check that is used is a sufficient condition proved in §3, so every `star` verdict is
a proof that the recursion is not finitely presented. A `survivor` verdict only records that
these sufficient conditions failed. For printed survivors the script also computes the
level-2 image of `⟨loops⟩` with sympy.

Results, with the tallies in the claim: 15,011 of the 16,384 recursions are killed and 1373
survive. Every survivor fails only (b) in a non-(free) component containing `s, t, u`. Each
of the 48 inspected survivors has full level-2 image `M_11 ≀ M_11`. The run used
`nice -n 10 timeout 1200`, and every family exited with code 0.
