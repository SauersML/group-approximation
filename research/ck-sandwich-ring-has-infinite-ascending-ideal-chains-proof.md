**Item 1 (basis over any k).** Work in the free `k`-algebra on the alphabet `(T \ {1}) ∪ {p,q}` with the
reduction rules `h h' -> (hh')` (group letters; the empty word when `hh' = 1`) and `p g q -> p q - 1`.
Order words by (number of letters `p,q`, total length) lexicographically; this is a semigroup order with
DCC, and every rule strictly decreases it. Ambiguities: two group rules overlap only through a group
letter and resolve by associativity in `T`. The rule `p g q` has no overlap with itself (its suffixes `q`,
`g q` are not prefixes `p`, `p g`), none with a group rule (its first and last letters are not group
letters, and its middle letter `g` has letter neighbours), and no inclusions. So all ambiguities
resolve, and Bergman's diamond lemma (valid over any commutative base ring) gives the basis. For
`k = Z` this is `ck-sandwich-ring-diamond-normal-form` item 1.

**Item 2.** Call a basis word *sealed* if it begins with the letter `p` and ends with the letter `q`
(`t_0 = t_m = 1`). Let `w` be sealed and `u = ... x t`, `v = t' y ...` basis words. In the
concatenation `u w v` the flanking group letters are unchanged (`t·1 = t`, `1·t' = t'`), the internal
segments of `u`, `w`, `v` are unchanged, and the two new segments are `x t p` and `q t' y`. Neither is
`p g q`: the first ends in `p`, the second begins with `q`. So `u w v` is again a basis word. Since `U_k`
is spanned by basis words, the two-sided ideal generated by a set of sealed words is the `k`-span of the
basis words that contain one of them as a block in this sense.

**Item 3.** Each `w_j = p q p^j q` is a basis word, because its only group letters are `1` and `g != 1`,
and it is sealed. A block `p q p^i q` inside `w_j` must start at a letter `p` immediately followed by `q`.
In `w_j` these are the first letter and the last `p`; the last `p` is followed only by the final `q`, so
the block starts at the first letter and then its length forces `i = j`. Hence `w_j` contains `w_i` as a
block only for `i = j`. By item 2, `I_A` is spanned by basis words containing some `w_i`, `i in A`, and
basis words are `k`-linearly independent (`k != 0`), so `w_j in I_A` iff `j in A`. The empty word `1`
contains no block, so `1 notin I_A`.

**Consequence.** Distinct `A` give distinct `I_A`, and `A ⊊ B` gives `I_A ⊊ I_B`. The chain
`I_{1} ⊊ I_{1,2} ⊊ ...` is an infinite strictly monotone chain of proper two-sided ideals, which is the
hypothesis of item (B) of `sandwich-ring-envelopes-have-infinite-core-bi-index`.
