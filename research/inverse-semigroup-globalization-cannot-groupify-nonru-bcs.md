Let `B_*` be the finite Paddock--Slofstra BCS with a tracial model but no
model in `R^U`, and fix any tracial model in a finite von Neumann algebra
`M`.  For a variable `v`, let `p_v^0,p_v^1` be its two spectral projections.
For a context `c` and an allowed answer `a`, let

```text
q_(c,a) = product_(v in c) p_v^(a_v).                   (ISG1)
```

Within each context these are orthogonal projections summing to `1`, and the
shared-variable identities recover `p_v^b` as sums of the appropriate
`q_(c,a)`.

There is no inverse semigroup `S` of partial isometries in any finite
tracial extension of this model for which every nonzero `q_(c,a)` occurs as
the source or range projection of an element of `S`.  Indeed, source and
range projections are idempotents of `S`, and all idempotents in an inverse
semigroup commute.  Hence all context atoms would commute.  Their sums then
make every `p_v^b` commute globally.  The finite-dimensional abelian algebra
generated by these projections has a nonempty spectrum, and evaluation at
any spectral point gives a classical assignment satisfying every BCS
constraint.  That is a one-dimensional, hence `R^U`, model of `B_*`, a
contradiction.

Equivalently, the finite Boolean inverse semigroups belonging to the
individual contexts cannot be amalgamated after their shared coarse
variable projections are identified.  The first obstruction occurs for
atoms `e=q_(c,a)` and `f=q_(d,b)` from incompatible contexts: closure in an
inverse semigroup would require `ef` to be an idempotent, while for
projections

```text
ef is a projection  iff  ef=fe.                         (ISG2)
```

If every such first cross-context product succeeded, the preceding
classical-assignment contradiction would apply.

Two standard globalization operations cannot remove this obstruction.

1. A semigroup homomorphism from an inverse semigroup to a group sends every
   idempotent to the group identity.  Thus the maximal group image erases
   exactly the support lattice `(ISG1)` carrying the context outcomes.
   Dilating the partial isometries to unitaries does not give an exact
   semigroup embedding: compression by the dilation projection inserts an
   extra projection between products, unless the corner reduces all the
   unitaries, in which case the nontrivial supports have again disappeared.
2. Compact-open or measurable groupoid bisections form an inverse semigroup
   whose idempotents are diagonal characteristic projections.  Encoding
   `(ISG1)` as their sources/ranges therefore makes all BCS atoms diagonal
   and triggers the same classical contradiction.  Encoding the BCS
   projections by arbitrary non-diagonal linear combinations is outside the
   inverse-semigroup compiler and is precisely the still-open arbitrary
   group-factor problem.

Canonical trace supplies a second, independent failure after passage to a
relation full group.  If partial bisections are extended to transformations
and `Lambda` is the countable subgroup they generate, its canonical
unitaries satisfy

```text
tau_R(u_g) = mu(Fix(g)).                                (ISG3)
```

When the action is not essentially free, `(ISG3)` is not the regular group
trace, so the representation does not extend trace-preservingly from
`L(Lambda)`.  When it is essentially free, it does extend, but
`full-group-subgroup-trace-generation-dichotomy` gives

```text
W*(u_g:g in Lambda) intersect L^infinity(X) = C.        (ISG4)
```

It therefore cannot recover any nontrivial diagonal context atom.  The
isotropy which remembers the partial supports is exactly what spoils the
regular trace; deleting that isotropy deletes the supports from the group
algebra.

This is a sharp no-go for the proposed inverse-semigroup/topological-full-
group route.  It does not refute an embedding of `A(B_*)` by genuinely
non-Cartan elements of a group factor, nor a finite central twisted corner;
those constructions would have to introduce a projective group basis not
obtained by globalizing the context support partial isometries.
