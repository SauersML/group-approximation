**Setting.**
- `B` is a finite Boolean constraint system, with contexts `c`, allowed sets
  `Allowed_c ⊆ Z_2^c`, and every variable in some context.
- `G_cv(B)` is its constraint-variable game, as in Corollary 3 of
  `forbidden-pair-splits-are-central-rectangle-splittings`.
  - Questions are the contexts, with answers `Allowed_c`, and the variables,
    with answers `Z_2`.
  - The pair `(c,v)` with `v in c` accepts `(a,b)` iff `a_v = b`.
  - Every other pair accepts everything (`V = 1` on unasked pairs).
- `R` is a coset refinement of `G_cv(B)` in the sense of
  `coset-refinement-forces-rectangular-central-splitting`:
  - groups `K_q` with padding maps `beta_q`;
  - a coset `c_e D_e` on every pair `e = {q,q'}`.

  Relations between non-incident questions are allowed.

**Edge-label systems.**  An edge-label system `N = (L_e, T_q, psi_e)` consists of:
- a finite group `L_e` for every pair `e` of questions;
- for every question `q`, a nonempty left coset `T_q` of a subgroup of
  `prod_(e ∋ q) L_e`;
- a readout `psi_e : L_e -> Z_2` on every incidence `e = (c,v)`.

Its algebra `A(N)` is generated by PVMs `(ell_e^lambda)_(lambda in L_e)`, one for
each pair.  The relations: for each `q`, the `ell_e` with `e ∋ q` commute, and
their joint spectral projections vanish off `T_q`.  Every coordinate is shared
by exactly two questions.

**Theorem.**  Suppose `A(R) != 0`.

- **(NF1) Normal form.**  There is an edge-label system `N(R)` with readouts such
  that:
  - sending each label PVM to the corresponding block sum of `R` defines a unital
    *-homomorphism `A(N(R)) -> A(R)`;
  - every representation of `A(N(R))` in a tracial von Neumann algebra `M` lifts
    to a representation of `A(R)` in `M (x) M_n`, with `n` fixed by the padding;
  - both maps commute with the pushforwards of `A(B)`.

  Consequently:
  - `A(R)` and `A(N(R))` are both tracial or neither, both `R^U` or neither, and
    both have a finite-dimensional representation or neither;
  - a perfect trace of `B` factors through `R` iff it factors through `N(R)`.
- **(NF2) Readouts.**  For `tau in T_c` write
  `psi(tau) = (psi_((c,v))(tau_((c,v))))_(v in c)`.
  - `psi(T_c) ⊆ Allowed_c` for every context `c`.
  - For `tau in T_v`, `psi_e(tau_e)` is the same for all incidences `e ∋ v`.
  - The pushed-forward variable is `X_v = sum_lambda (-1)^(psi_e(lambda)) ell_e^lambda`,
    for any `e ∋ v`.  This is an algebraic identity.
- **(NF3) Converse.**  Let `N` be an edge-label system with readouts satisfying
  (NF2), such that `pi_e(T_q) ∩ pi_e(T_(q'))` is nonempty on every pair.  Then
  `N = N(R)` for a coset refinement `R` without padding, and `A(R) ≅ A(N)`.

**What this says.**
- **Label-exact refinements.**  These are the case `L_e = Z_2` with bijective
  readouts.  There, `exact-label-coset-refinements-are-affine` confines the used
  allowed sets to affine ones.
- **Hidden labels on the variable questions.**  These are edge groups
  `L_((c,v))` with non-affine readouts, carried from `p_c` to `p_v` and on to
  other contexts.
- The syntax is general in three ways: arbitrary edge groups, arbitrary readouts,
  and relations between non-incident questions.

**Model tests.**
- **A classical solution.**  All `L_e` are trivial and every readout is constant.
  `A(N)` is `C`.
- **The magic square in constraint-variable syntax.**
  - `L_((c,v)) = Z_2` with identity readouts.
  - `T_c` is the solution set of the equation `c`, and `T_v` is the diagonal.
  - `A(N)` is the linear-system algebra, which is nonzero.
- **A non-affine readout.**  Take a one-hot context `c = {v_0, v_1, v_2}` with:
  - `L_((c,v_a)) = Z_3` and `T_c` the diagonal `Z_3` in `Z_3^3`;
  - readouts `psi_((c,v_a))(lambda) = [lambda = a]`.

  The readout is not affine, since `Z_3` has no nontrivial character to `Z_2`.
  `psi(T_c)` is the whole one-hot set, whose affine hull contains `(1,1,1)`.
