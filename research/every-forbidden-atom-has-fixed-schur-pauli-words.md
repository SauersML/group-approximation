Let a Schur rank gate have the block form

```text
M(x)=[[U(x),v],[w^T,0]],                               (SPW1)
```

where `U(x)` is invertible on the Boolean cube.  Fix a forbidden assignment
`a` with

```text
w^T U(a)^-1 v=1.
```

In the associated class-two packet write the noncentral generators as
`p_1,...,p_N,p_*` and `q_1,...,q_N,q_*`.  Define the constant binary vectors

```text
alpha=U(a)^-1 v,
beta^T=w^T U(a)^-1,                                   (SPW2)
```

and the two fixed group words

```text
P_a=p_* product_i p_i^(beta_i),
Q_a=q_* product_j q_j^(alpha_j).                      (SPW3)
```

On the central selector atom `a`, `P_a,Q_a` commute with the entire baseline
packet generated by the first `N` p- and q-generators, while

```text
P_a Q_a=-Q_a P_a.                                     (SPW4)
```

They therefore name the relative `M_2` multiplicity coordinate by
assignment-independent group words.  No measurable choice of Gaussian
elimination and no commutant projection is needed.  If a predicate has
several forbidden atoms, use the finite list `(P_a,Q_a)`; the selector atoms
are orthogonal, so their violation masses add.

For Fanizza's fixed nonlinear menu this produces respectively `2,4,20`
literal word pairs.  Their lengths are bounded by the fixed packet sizes and
are independent of machine input and computation depth.  For the optimized
gates in `(FSG1)`, the maximum `(P_a,Q_a)` generator weights are respectively
`(5,5)`, `(5,5)`, and `(8,9)`.
