*"0.1. Theorem (Cocycle superrigidity). Let Γ ↷^σ X be a s-malleable action (e.g. a generalized Bernoulli
Γ-action) and assume Γ has an infinite rigid subgroup H such that either H is wq-normal with σ mixing, or
that H is w-normal with σ|H weak mixing. Let 𝒱 be a Polish group of finite type. Then any 𝒱-valued
cocycle for σ is cohomologous to a group morphism of Γ into 𝒱. More generally, if σ′ is an action of the
form σ′_g = σ_g × ρ_g ∈ Aut(X × Y, μ × ν), g ∈ Γ, where ρ is an arbitrary Γ-action on a standard
probability space (Y, ν), then any 𝒱-valued cocycle w for σ′ is cohomologous to a 𝒱-valued cocycle w′
which is independent on the X-variable (i.e. w′ comes from a cocycle of ρ)."*

Same page: *"Countable discrete groups and separable compact groups are of finite type"*. p. 3--4:
*"A typical example of s-malleable action is the Bernoulli Γ-action on (X, μ) = Π_(g∈Γ) (X_0, μ_0)_g,
with (X_0, μ_0)_g identical copies of a standard probability space (X_0, μ_0)"*, and *"Bernoulli actions
are always (strongly) mixing"*.

Internal counterpart, Section 3 of the same PDF: *"3.1. Theorem. Assume σ is weakly mixing and let ρ be
another action of Γ on a standard probability space (Y, ν). Let 𝒱 ∈ 𝒰_fin and w ∈ Z^1(σ × ρ, 𝒱) ... Then
w is equivalent to a 𝒱-valued cocycle which is independent on the X-variable."* That theorem has an
additional hypothesis (`w_l ∼ w_r`), which the deformation/rigidity argument supplies under the
hypotheses of 0.1.

**Matching the claim.** An infinite property (T) group `Γ` is a rigid subgroup of itself. It is normal,
hence w-normal, and the diffuse-base Bernoulli action is mixing, so both alternatives of the hypothesis
hold with `H = Γ`. A countable group is of finite type by the quoted sentence. The definitions of
"rigid", "w-normal" and "s-malleable" (Section 4.3 of the paper) were not reread here. The reading that a
property (T) group is rigid in Popa's sense, meaning relative property (T) of `H ⊂ Γ`, follows the
paper's introduction and the peer import `popa-bernoulli-cocycle-superrigidity`.
