Notation as in the target. `D(Delta, L)` is normally generated by the
elements `w = [x, l]` with `x = s c s^-1`, `s in G_comp(L)`,
`c in C_Delta(L)` and `l in L`. It suffices to control each such `w`.

**Step 1 (a linear representation of `H`).** Let `pi` be one of:
- **(defining, only when `C <= ker p`)** `pi_0 : H -> Sp_(2g)(R) <= GL_(2g)(R)`,
  induced by `p`. Its kernel is `(ker p)/C`.
- **(adjoint)** `pi_1 = Ad o p : H -> GL(sp_(2g)(R))`. It is defined for
  every central `C`, since `Ad` kills `{+-1} = p(Z^)`. Its kernel is
  `p^-1(ker Ad)/C = p^-1({+-1})/C = Z^/C`, because `ker Ad = Z(G) = {+-1}`
  for connected `G`.

**Step 2 (finite-dimensional kill).** Restrict `pi` to `Delta`. By
`compression-defect-dies-in-finite-dimensions` (field `R`,
`V = R^(2g)` or `sp_(2g)(R)`), `D(Delta, L) <= ker(pi|Delta)`. So every
generator `w` lies in `ker pi_1 = Z^/C`. In particular `w` is central in `H`.

**Step 3 (lift).** Write `x = aC`, `l = bC` with `a, b in G^`. Then
`[aC, bC] = w` is central in `H`. By step 5 of
`sp2g-central-commutators-of-universal-lifts-are-torsion-proof`, `[a,b]` is
central in `G^`. By (CT1), `[a,b] in Tors(Z^)`, so
`w in (Tors(Z^) C)/C`. This proves (CD1). `Tors(Z^)` injects into
`Z^/ker p = Z/2`, so the bound has order at most 2.

**Step 4 ((CD2)).**
- **If `C >= Tors(Z^)`.** The group in (CD1) is trivial.
- **If `C <= ker p`.** Use `pi_0` in step 2. Then `p(w) = 1`, so `p(a)` and
  `p(b)` commute in `G`. So `[a,b] in ker p cap Tors(Z^) = 1`, because
  `ker p = Z` is torsion-free.

In both cases every generator is 1, so `D(Delta, L) = 1`.

**Step 5 ((CD3)).** `Gamma_n <= G^/<zeta^n>` and `<zeta^n> <= ker p`.
Apply (CD2) to `Delta` and every subgroup of `Gamma_n`.

**Step 6 ((CD4)).** Take `g = 2`, `H_3 = G^/<zeta^3>` and `E_3 = Gamma_3`.
- **Setup.** `Z^` maps onto `{+-1} <= Sp_4(Z)`, so `Z^/<zeta^3> <= E_3`. It
  is abelian of order `[Z^ : ker p] * 3 = 6`, hence cyclic, and it contains
  `<z> = (ker p)/<zeta^3>`.
- **Where central subgroups live.** A central `N <= E_3` maps into
  `Z(Sp_4(Z)) = {+-1}` (standard, not re-read), so `N <= Z^/<zeta^3>`.
- **Order of `N`.** If `z not in N`, then `N cap <z> = 1`, so `|N|` is 1 or 2.
  - `|N| = 1`: this is (CD3).
  - `|N| = 2`: `N` is the unique subgroup of order 2 of the cyclic group of
    order 6. The image of `Tors(Z^)` there has order at most 2, so it lies
    in `N`.
- **Conclusion.** Let `C'` be the preimage of `N` in `Z^`. Then
  `C' >= Tors(Z^)` and `E_3/N <= G^/C'`. So (CD2) gives `D(E_3/N, L) = 1`.

## Remark (not part of the established statement)

By the Margulis normal subgroup theorem for `Sp_4(Z)` (recalled, not
re-read), a normal subgroup of `Sp_4(Z)` is either finite or of finite
index. Finite normal subgroups are central.

Let `M` be a normal subgroup of `E_3` with `z not in M`. Then either:
- `E_3/M` is finite, and `compression-defects-die-in-rigid-targets` kills
  every defect image; or
- `M` is central, which is (CD4).

So applying a compression criterion inside any image of `E_3` that keeps `z`
also dies. This is the image-side form of
`central-defect-element-mf-image-is-trivial-or-infinite-proof`.
