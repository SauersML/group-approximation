Let `G` be any group and `w = t^5 y t^(-1) x` with `x, y in G`. This is the
normalized residue shape `(1,1,1,1)` of
`kl-length-six-coincidence-saturated-torsion-residue`.

If the subgroup `<x, y>` is hyperlinear, then `G -> (G * <t>)/<<w>>` is
injective.

In particular this holds when `x` and `y` are involutions. Then `<x, y>` is a
quotient of the infinite dihedral group, hence amenable, hence hyperlinear. So
the shape `(1,1,1,1)` carries no violation with involution loops, the case of the
Leavitt search.

**Unnormalized words.** A word `t u t u t u t u t y t^(-1) x` of this shape is
carried to `s^5 (u^(-1) y u) s^(-1) x` by the automorphism `t -> s u^(-1)` of
`G * <t>` fixing `G`. That automorphism does not change injectivity, so the
hypothesis to check is that `<u^(-1) y u, x>` is hyperlinear. With involution
loops it is dihedral.

**Not covered.** For loops of orders `2` and `3`, `<x, y>` is a quotient of
`C_2 * C_3`. Such quotients include every group generated by an involution and
an element of order three, so hyperlinearity is not automatic.
