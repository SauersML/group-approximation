Let `K` be a field, `R = L_K(1,2)`, and fix `a, b > 0`. For a word `μ` put `ω(μ) = a·#_0(μ) + b·#_1(μ)`.
- `R^ω_≤ = span{ s_μ t_ν : ω(μ) <= ω(ν) }` is a unital subalgebra, and it is stably finite.
- The anti-involution `s_i <-> t_i` carries it onto `R^ω_≥ = span{ s_μ t_ν : ω(μ) >= ω(ν) }`, which is
  stably finite too.
- If `K` is finite, every finitely generated subgroup of `(R^ω_≤)^x` or `(R^ω_≥)^x` is residually finite, so
  both unit groups are sofic.

**Proof sketch.**
- `R` acts faithfully on the locally constant functions on `{0,1}^N` (artifact Lemma 1.1).
- The functions constant on the pieces of the weight-`r` stopping partition form a finite-dimensional
  subspace `F_r`. It is invariant under `s_μ t_ν` whenever `ω(μ) <= ω(ν)` and `ω(μ) <= r` (Lemma 1.2).
- Restriction to the `F_k` embeds `R^ω_≤` in `∏_k End(F_k) / ⊕_k End(F_k)`, which is stably finite
  (Theorem 1). The same restrictions embed finitely generated unit subgroups in `∏_k GL(F_k)` (Corollary 2).

**Scope.**
- `R` itself is not weakly finite: `t_0 s_0 = 1 != s_0 t_0`, where `t_0` lies in `R^ω_≤` and `s_0` only in
  `R^ω_≥`.
- For the standard grading (`a = b`) the degree-zero part is the locally finite union of `M_(2^n)(K)`.
  `R^1_≤` contains `t_0`, so it is not locally finite; the theorem goes past that case.
