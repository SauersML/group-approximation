Citation import, not a reproof. A. Jaikin-Zapirain, *The universality of Hughes-free division
rings*, Selecta Math. (N.S.) 27 (2021), no. 4, Paper No. 74, DOI 10.1007/s00029-021-00691-w.

The abstract was fetched from `https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/s00029-021-00691-w` on
2026-09-18. It is verbatim, with the TeX formulas rendered as plain text:

> Let E*G be a crossed product of a division ring E and a locally indicable group G. Hughes
> showed that up to E*G-isomorphism, there exists at most one Hughes-free division E*G-ring.
> However, the existence of a Hughes-free division E*G-ring D_{E*G} for an arbitrary locally
> indicable group G is still an open question. Nevertheless, D_{E*G} exists, for example, if G
> is amenable or G is bi-orderable. In this paper we study, whether D_{E*G} is the universal
> division ring of fractions in some of these cases. In particular, we show that if G is a
> residually-(locally indicable and amenable) group, then there exists D_{E[G]} and it is
> universal. In Appendix we give a description of D_{E[G]} when G is a RFRS group.

"Universal" is read in Cohn's standard sense for division rings of fractions (P. M. Cohn,
*Skew Fields*, CUP 1995, Ch. 4; *Free Ideal Rings and Localization in General Rings*, CUP 2006,
Ch. 7). An epic `R`-field `U` is universal if it specializes to every epic `R`-field.

**Trust surface.**
- The body of the paper was not read. The printed theorem number was not verified.
- The reading of "universal" as Cohn universality is the standard meaning of the phrase
  "universal division ring of fractions". The same paper's Prop. 4.1, as quoted in
  `universal-rings-iteration-obstruction-proof`, argues through ranks against *every* division
  `Q[G]`-ring, including the augmentation. That is consistent with this reading.

This route asserts only that the source states the theorem as recorded.
