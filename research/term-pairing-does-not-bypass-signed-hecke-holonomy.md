Let `K` be finite, let

```text
q C[K] q = M_D(C)
```

with matrix units `E_ij`, and consider the standard algebraic block escape

```text
W=sum_i h_i E_ii,             P=W Y W^*,                (TPH1)
```

where `Y=(y_ij)` is a projection in the root block and the phase words `h_i`
commute with that block.  Then

```text
P=sum_(i,j) y_ij h_i h_j^(-1) E_ij.                    (TPH2)
```

Thus the support labels are not independent.  They are vertex potentials

```text
ell_ij=h_i h_j^(-1)
```

and obey

```text
ell_ji=ell_ij^(-1),            ell_ij ell_jk=ell_ik.    (TPH3)
```

Equations `(TPH3)` are precisely what makes star, multiplication, and the
projection identity survive the tagging:

```text
P^*=P,                         P^2=P.                   (TPH4)
```

This exposes the limitation of a coefficient term-pairing compiler.  A
finite group-algebra identity is indeed a finite signed sum of group words,
and after clearing rational denominators one may split coefficients and pair
positive terms with negative terms.  Imposing equality of the paired words
makes that **one linear identity** true.  But assigning a fresh padding word
to each occurrence in `(TPH2)` destroys `(TPH3)`, so the resulting sum is no
longer automatically self-adjoint or idempotent.  Adding pairings for
`P^*-P` and `P^2-P` forces the occurrence labels back into a coherent
groupoid/cocycle system.  Around a spanning forest the phase potentials can
be chosen successively; the first cycle leaves a word equation in the
coefficient group.  This is the same relative holonomy equation as the first
non-tree signed-Hecke chord.

There is no general injectivity theorem for imposing those remaining word
equalities.  Already a two-term group-ring equality

```text
x g x^(-1)-1=0                                      (TPH5)
```

forces the coefficient element `g` to be trivial.  Hence the slogan “pair
opposite coefficients by group relators” does not imply that the finite
subgroup supporting `q` embeds in the quotient or that `q` remains nonzero.
A universal preservation theorem even for standard nonsingular one-variable
equations would enter the Kervaire--Laudenbach problem; arbitrary singular
systems such as `(TPH5)` are plainly false.

Nor does long small-cancellation padding automatically help.  Padding both
sides by the same words merely conjugates the original equality and cancels
from the quotient.  Giving the two sides independent long tags changes the
group-ring projection; restoring `(TPH3)--(TPH4)` adds the cycle equations
back.  A graphical small-cancellation construction remains possible only
after proving a relative injectivity theorem for the **specific coherent
cycle presentation**.  That theorem is the missing block-escaping holonomy,
not a formal consequence of term pairing.

Consequently the signed-Hecke corner route already has addition,
multiplication, star, and the corner unit inside each context.  Its sole
unsolved algebra relation is the additive equality of shared signed
marginals.  Termwise cancellation can impose that equality only by either
breaking the context projections or solving the same coherent cycle
holonomy while preserving `K`.  Merely adjoining nilpotent-root syntax or a
finite central-character packet to this term-pairing construction does not
remove that compatibility requirement.
