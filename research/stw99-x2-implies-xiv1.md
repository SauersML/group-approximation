Let `D` be finite strongly self-absorbing.  `D` is simple, separable,
nuclear, unital, with a unique tracial state `τ` (Toms--Winter); `τ` is
faithful by simplicity, and `π_τ(D)''` is an injective (nuclearity) II₁
(infinite-dimensionality) factor (uniqueness makes `τ` extreme), hence
`≅ R` by Connes.  So `D` embeds unitally into `R`.  Under the premise
that `R` is quasidiagonal, so is its C\*-subalgebra `D` (quasidiagonality
passes to subalgebras).  The equivalence with `Q ≅ Q ⊗ D` is the
McDuff-type characterisation recorded by STW: a quasidiagonal `D` embeds
into `Q_ω`, strong self-absorption upgrades the embedding to
`Q_ω ∩ Q'`, and this is equivalent to `Q` being `D`-stable.
