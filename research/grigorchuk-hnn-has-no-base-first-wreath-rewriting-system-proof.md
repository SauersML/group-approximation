## Setting

- `Σ` is a finite alphabet, `π : Σ* → Γ` a monoid homomorphism onto a group `Γ`.
- A rewriting system `R ⊆ Σ* × Σ*` is **complete for `Γ`** if:
  - it is finite;
  - it terminates;
  - it is confluent;
  - `u ↔*_R v` holds exactly when `π(u) = π(v)`.
- Let `ρ : Σ → {1, …, |Σ|}` be a bijective ranking of the letters, and let `Σ_k` be the set of the
  `k` lowest letters.
- A reduction ordering `>` on `Σ*` is **alphabet-monotone for `ρ`** if, for every `k`, every word
  containing a letter outside `Σ_k` is greater than every word in `Σ_k*`.
- `R` is **compatible** with `>` if `l > r` for every rule `l → r`.

Sims' wreath ordering built from `ρ` is alphabet-monotone. Suppose `w` contains a letter of rank
`m > k` and `u ∈ Σ_k*`. The wreath comparison first compares the number of occurrences of the
highest letter present in `u` or `w`. That letter has rank at least `m`, and it occurs at least
once in `w` but not in `u`. So `w > u`. The recursive path ordering with precedence `ρ` is also
alphabet-monotone, since a term whose root symbol has the highest precedence dominates every term
built from smaller symbols. So is any ordering that first compares the multiset of ranks of the
highest letters. Shortlex is not alphabet-monotone.

## Lemma 1 (restriction to an initial segment)

Let `R` be complete for `Γ` and compatible with an alphabet-monotone ordering for `ρ`. Fix `k`, and
let `R_k = {(l → r) ∈ R : l ∈ Σ_k*}`. Then `R_k` is a finite complete rewriting system over `Σ_k`
for the submonoid `M_k = π(Σ_k*)` of `Γ`. In particular `M_k` is a finitely presented monoid.

*Proof.*

1. *Rules of `R_k` stay inside `Σ_k*`.* Let `l → r` be in `R_k`. If `r` contained a letter outside
   `Σ_k`, monotonicity would give `r > l`, contradicting `l > r`. So `r ∈ Σ_k*`.
2. *Reductions of words in `Σ_k*` use only `R_k`.* Suppose a rule `l → r` of `R` applies to
   `w ∈ Σ_k*`, so `w = x l y`. Then `l` is a subword of `w`, so `l ∈ Σ_k*` and the rule lies in
   `R_k`. By step 1, `x r y ∈ Σ_k*`. By induction, every `R`-reduction sequence starting in
   `Σ_k*` is an `R_k`-reduction sequence and stays in `Σ_k*`. The converse is trivial.
3. *Termination and confluence.* `R_k` is a subset of `R`, so it terminates. Let `w ∈ Σ_k*`.
   By step 2, the `R_k`-descendants of `w` are exactly its `R`-descendants. Since `R` is complete,
   `w` has a unique `R`-irreducible descendant `nf(w)`. So `R_k` is confluent on `Σ_k*`.
4. *Presentation.* Let `u, v ∈ Σ_k*`.
   - If `u ↔*_{R_k} v`, then `π(u) = π(v)`.
   - Conversely, if `π(u) = π(v)`, then `nf(u) = nf(v)`, because `R` is complete for `Γ`. Both
     normal forms are reached by `R_k`-reductions (step 2), so `u ↔*_{R_k} v`.

   Hence `π` induces an isomorphism `Σ_k*/↔*_{R_k} ≅ M_k`, and `⟨Σ_k | l = r (l → r ∈ R_k)⟩` is a
   finite monoid presentation of `M_k`. ∎

## Lemma 2 (torsion submonoids)

If a submonoid `M` of a group is generated by elements of finite order, then `M` is the subgroup
they generate. If `M` is moreover a finitely presented monoid, then `M` is a finitely presented
group.

*Proof.*

1. `M` is a subgroup. If `x^n = 1` with `n ≥ 1`, then `x^(−1) = x^(n−1) ∈ M`. So `M` contains the
   inverses of its generators, and hence it is the subgroup they generate.
2. `M` is finitely presented as a group. Take a finite monoid presentation `⟨X | U_i = V_i⟩` of
   `M`. Every element of a group is invertible, so the natural map from the monoid presentation to
   the group presentation `⟨X | U_i V_i^(−1)⟩` is an isomorphism. The inverse of a generator `x` is
   already represented by a positive word, namely the positive word that represents `x^(−1)` in
   `M`. ∎

## Theorem

Let `𝔊^` be Grigorchuk's HNN extension with presentation

`⟨a, b, c, d, t | a², b², c², d², bcd, t^(−1) x t = σ(x) (x ∈ {a,b,c,d}), (ad)⁴, (adacac)⁴⟩`,

where `σ(a) = aca`, `σ(b) = d`, `σ(c) = b` and `σ(d) = c`. Let `χ : 𝔊^ → Z` be given by
`χ(t) = 1` and `χ(a) = χ(b) = χ(c) = χ(d) = 0`, and let `N = ker χ`. Let `R` be a finite complete
rewriting system for `𝔊^` over a finite alphabet `Σ`. Suppose `R` is compatible with an ordering
that is alphabet-monotone for a ranking `ρ`. If the `k` lowest letters all map into `N`, then they
generate a **finite** subgroup of `𝔊^`.

*Proof.*

1. *`N` is a 2-group.* `N` is the increasing union `⋃_(n ≥ 0) t^n 𝔊 t^(−n)` of conjugates of the
   base `𝔊`. This is the standard normal form for ascending HNN extensions: every element of
   `𝔊^` is `t^p g t^(−q)` with `g ∈ 𝔊` and `p, q ≥ 0`, and it lies in `N` exactly when `p = q`.
   Each conjugate is a 2-group (`grigorchuk-group-is-just-infinite-torsion-not-fp`), so every
   element of `N` has finite order.
2. *`M_k` is a finitely presented group.* By Lemma 1, `M_k = π(Σ_k*)` is a finitely presented
   monoid. By step 1 and Lemma 2, it is the subgroup `H` generated by the `k` lowest letters, and
   `H` is a finitely presented group.
3. *`H` is a subgroup of a copy of `𝔊`.* `H` is finitely generated and contained in the increasing
   union of step 1. So `H ≤ t^n 𝔊 t^(−n) ≅ 𝔊` for some `n`.
4. *`H` is finite.* Grigorchuk and Wilson proved that every infinite finitely generated subgroup
   of `𝔊` is abstractly commensurable with `𝔊`: R. I. Grigorchuk and J. S. Wilson, "A structural
   property concerning abstract commensurability of subgroups", J. London Math. Soc. (2) 68
   (2003) 671–682. This theorem is recalled from the literature and was not re-read in this
   session.
   - Finite presentability passes to finite-index subgroups and to finite-index overgroups. So it
     is an invariant of abstract commensurability.
   - `𝔊` is not finitely presented (`grigorchuk-group-is-just-infinite-torsion-not-fp`).
   - So an infinite, finitely generated, finitely presented subgroup of `𝔊` cannot exist.
   - By steps 2 and 3, `H` is therefore finite. ∎

## Corollaries

- **(C1) Base-first wreath orderings on Grigorchuk's presentation fail.** On the alphabet
  `a A b B c C d D t T` with wreath ordering, the 8 lowest letters generate `𝔊`, which is infinite.
  So no finite complete rewriting system is compatible with this ordering. The same holds for
  every alphabet-monotone ordering in which some infinite subgroup of `N` is generated by letters
  ranked below all letters of nonzero `χ`. An example is any alphabet containing letters for
  `a, b, c, d` ranked below `t^(±1)`.
- **(C2) What survives.** Let `R` be such a system and `k_0` the largest `k` for which `Σ_k` maps
  into `N`. Then `Σ_(k_0)` generates a finite 2-subgroup `F`. The lowest letter `s` of nonzero
  `χ` comes next.
  - Every initial segment generates a finitely presented monoid with a finite complete rewriting
    system (Lemma 1).
  - A segment closed under inverses generates a subgroup. That subgroup is finitely presented and
    of type `FP_∞`, because a monoid with a finite complete rewriting system is of type `FP_∞`
    (Anick; Groves; Squier; Kobayashi), and for a group the monoid and group notions agree.

  So a monotone certificate for `𝔊^` must build `𝔊^` from a finite 2-group through a chain of
  finitely presented monoids, whose inverse-closed stages are `FP_∞` subgroups.
- **(C3) The same argument for the Basilica extension.** Let `B^ = ⟨a, t | t^(−2) a t^2 = a²,
  [a, a^b], b = t a t^(−1)⟩`. Suppose some initial segment of `Σ` maps into `ker χ` and generates
  a subgroup that is not finitely presented, for instance a conjugate of the Basilica group `B`.
  Then no finite complete rewriting system compatible with an alphabet-monotone ordering exists.
  - Examples: the alphabets `a A b B t T` and `a b A B t T` with wreath ordering, where `b` is a
    letter for `t a t^(−1)`.
  - Finite generation of the segment is automatic, and `B` is not finitely presented
    (`basilica-hnn-extension-is-fp-infinity`, Attempts, citing Erschler through
    `fp-covers-of-grigorchuk-groups-are-large`).
  - Here the conclusion is weaker than "finite". Infinite finitely generated subgroups of `B` can
    be finitely presented, such as `Z^n`, so C3 kills only the orderings whose initial segment
    generates a non-finitely-presented subgroup.
- **(C4) Scope.** The theorem says nothing about shortlex or other non-monotone orderings, and
  nothing about orderings where a letter of nonzero `χ` comes early. For those, see the bounded
  computations recorded in the target claim.

## Calibration

- `BS(1,2) = ⟨a, t | t a t^(−1) = a²⟩` with wreath ordering `a A t T` has an 8-rule complete system,
  found by the completer `experiments/hnn-rewriting-2026-09-17/kb.cpp` and re-verified there by checking all
  critical pairs. Its lowest segment `{a, A}` generates `Z`, which is finitely presented. This is
  consistent with Lemma 1.
- The Knuth–Bendix run on `𝔊^` with the base-first wreath ordering overflows, as C1 predicts.
