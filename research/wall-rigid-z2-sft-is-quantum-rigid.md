**OPEN.** Let `Ω` be a `Z^2`-SFT that is `D`-wall-rigid, meaning every difference set of two of its points is
`2D`-connected. Then `Ω` is `D'`-quantum rigid for some `D'`. Equivalently
(`sft-wall-rigidity-iff-idempotent-commutator-ideal`), the idempotent commutator ideal `J_(D')` of the local
tiling algebra vanishes.

**Payoff.** With `labbe-shift-is-wall-rigid`, it gives
`labbe-wang-shift-crossed-product-is-finitely-presented`, and hence gate (a) of the ring route.

**Refutation.** A wall-rigid SFT with a contextual operator family: noncommuting, with no two-dimensional
composition factor. Examples would be a periodic quantum tiling of an aperiodic wall-rigid shift
(`periodic-quantum-tilings-refute-quantum-rigidity`), or a non-abelian solution group below.

## Attempts

- **First order: done.** Walls are exactly the extension-type obstructions
  (`sft-wall-rigidity-iff-idempotent-commutator-ideal`). What remains is idempotent noncommutativity, visible
  only in irreducible families of dimension `>= 3` or in modules without finite filtrations.
- **Group shifts: reformulated, open.** For a group shift over `F_2` and `char k != 2`, families are
  representations of the solution group `Γ_D`. It is generated by involutions `X_z`, commuting at distance
  `<= 2D`, with parity relators. Rigidity is `Γ_D` abelian; wall rigidity is `[Γ_D, Γ_D]^ab ⊗ k = 0`, via
  relative augmentation ideals (recalled). A counterexample here is a non-abelian `Γ_D` with `k`-perfect
  commutator subgroup.
  - Heuristic for: this matches Bieri–Strebel for `F_p[Z^2]/(f) ⋊ Z^2`. Finite presentation is tameness (no
    parallel edges of the Newton polygon), which is where supports stop having moats. Not checked in
    detail.
- **Dimension 3: partial.** Frames of a three-dimensional family change only through bands where two
  eigenlines carry equal letters.
  - Loops of such rotations through `(2,1)`-bands close trivially (checked by hand in the generic case).
  - Triangles of frames glued through a scalar ray do not close trivially. So dimension 3 is not excluded in
    general. For those configurations the extracted classical tilings do have connected difference sets.
- **Labbé's shift as the test case (heuristic).** It has fault lines in four directions, so half-plane
  determinism fails antipodally in four directions. Yet it has no walls
  (`research/artifacts/labbe-shift-wall-rigidity-2026-09-13.md`, Section 5).
  - For group shifts the two conditions coincide, through superposition. Labbé's shift separates them. So
    it decides whether walls or antipodal indeterminism is the right invariant for tiling algebras.
- **Where a proof must go.** A noncommuting family must avoid constant-frame regions containing half-strips
  with different torus points. For rotation codings, such regions pin the torus point. A proof would have to
  control families whose local frames drift, or have no infinite constant-frame region.
