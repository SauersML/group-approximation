## The five coefficients generate the whole group

In characteristic two a transposition matrix is elementary:

```text
[[0,1],[1,0]]=x_12(1)x_21(1)x_12(1).
```

Hence the 20-cycle permutation matrix `p` belongs to `EL_20(R)`.  Conjugating
`x_12(q)` by powers of `p` supplies `x_(i,i+1)(q)` on every edge of the
directed 20-cycle, for each `q in {s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1}`.  Steinberg commutators

```text
[x_ij(r),x_jk(q)]=x_ik(rq)
```

and alternating among three indices produce arbitrary products of the four
ring generators in every root.  Root additivity produces arbitrary sums.
Finally `1=s_0t_0+s_1t_1`, so the (not a priori unital) subring generated by
the four coefficients is all of `R`.  Therefore the five displayed elements
generate every `x_ij(r)`, hence `EL_20(R)=G`.

Each `x_ij(q)` has order two because
`x_ij(q)^2=x_ij(2q)=1` in characteristic two.  Nontriviality follows from
the faithful matrix realization and `s_0,t_1 != 0`.  Thus `a` and `e` are
involutions.

## Why torsion is exactly load-bearing

The cyclic sign sequence of `(L6.1)` is `++++--`; its two sign changes occur
across `e` and, cyclically, across `a`.  Stallings' sign-index-two theorem
says that such an equation is solvable over an arbitrary coefficient group
when both sign-change coefficients have infinite order.  More generally,
Clifford--Goldstein, *Equations with torsion-free coefficients*, Proc.
Edinburgh Math. Soc. **43** (2000), 295--307, Theorem 1.1, derives
injectivity from the absence of the corresponding short positive relations.
Here the relative star graph has square loops labelled `a^2=1` and `e^2=1`.
Those are exactly the degree-two regions excluded by their hypotheses.

This does not prove failure of injectivity; it proves that the Leavitt torsion
is used at the precise two places where the general relative-picture proof
needs torsion-freeness.  Ivanov--Klyachko's theorem for all equations of
length at most six likewise assumes a torsion-free coefficient group.

The identity is not an accidental internal root at `t=1`.  The factors
`a,b,c` change only row 1, while right multiplication of the 20-cycle matrix
`p` by `e=x_12(t_1)` changes only column 2.  An off-diagonal `1` of `p` lying
outside row 1 and column 2 survives in `abcpe`; hence `abcpe != I`.

## Reidemeister--Schreier form

The prefix depths before `a,b,c,d,e,f` are `(0,1,2,3,4,3)`, and the terminal
power is `t^2=z`.  Therefore

```text
W=a_0 b_1 (z c_0 z^(-1))(z d_1 z^(-1))
            (z^2 e_0 z^(-2)) z
 =a_0 b_1 z c_0 d_1 z e_0 z^(-1).
```

Since `G` is perfect, `N_2^ab=Z[z]`, and `(L6.2)` maps to its generator, as
every degree-two candidate must.  The abelian/Fox test is therefore passed
but supplies no normal-generation conclusion.

Conjugation by `t` acts by

```text
tau(g_0)=g_1,       tau(g_1)=z g_0 z^(-1),       tau(z)=z.
```

Applying this to `(L6.2)` and cancelling adjacent `z^(-1)z` pairs gives
`(L6.3)`.  Since `t^2=z` lies in `N_2`, every further even translate is an
inner conjugate of one of these two relators.  Hence the ambient normal
closure intersects `N_2` exactly as stated in `(L6.4)`.

Finally, viewed in `(G_0*G_1)*<z>`, each of `(L6.2)` and `(L6.3)` contains
three occurrences of `z`, with signs `++-`.  Howie's universal length-three
equation theorem injects `G_0*G_1` into either one-relator quotient.  The
two-relator interaction is therefore load-bearing.

## The obvious retractions fail

First kill `G_1` and ask for a map of `(L6.4)` back to `G_0` which fixes
`G_0`.  Then `(L6.3)` becomes

```text
z b_0 z d_0 z^(-1)=1,
```

and cancellation forces `z=b_0^(-1)d_0^(-1)=b_0p^(-1)`.  The other relator
becomes

```text
F=a_0 z c_0 z e_0 z^(-1)
 =a b p^(-1)c b p^(-1)e p b.                         (L6.5)
```

This is not the identity.  With the convention `p epsilon_j=epsilon_(j+1)`,
direct matrix multiplication leaves, for example, an off-diagonal constant
entry `F_(3,4)=1`.  The artifact verifies the same entry in the faithful
infinite-word representation.  Hence no retraction can fix `G_0` while
killing `G_1`.

For the most obvious diagonal maps, send `G_1` to `G_0` either by
`Ad(p^k)`, or by `Ad(p^k)` after the automorphism
`g |-> (g^*)^(-1)`, where the Leavitt anti-involution swaps `s_i,t_i` and
matrix adjunction swaps roots 12 and 21.  If `z` is a constant permutation, the
permutation skeleton of `(L6.3)` forces `z=p^(-1)`.  The skeleton of `(L6.2)`
then forces the image of `p_1` to be `p`; in the inner case this restricts to
`Ad(p^k)`, and the duality already fixes `p`.  The
artifact evaluates all 40 cases on the faithful infinite-word module and
finds a nonfixed basis vector in every case.  Thus none is a retraction.

These failures are only a fence around the easiest embeddings.  They neither
prove that `(L6.4)` is trivial nor exhibit a coefficient kernel.
