**Conventions.**
- `R_X = LC(X,F_q) ⋊_T Z`, with `(Tx)_t = x_{t+1}` and `u f u^{-1} = f∘T^{-1}`, so `u^{-t} e_U u^t = e_{T^{-t}U}`.
- `e_a` is the indicator of `{x : x_0 = a}`.

**Step 1: one Kazhdan group.**
- Let `F = F_q⟨t_+, t_-, t_a : a ∈ A⟩` be the free associative algebra.
- The ring homomorphism `π_X: F → R_X`, with `t_± ↦ u^{±1}` and `t_a ↦ e_a`, is onto, because `R_X` is generated by `u^{±1}` and the letter indicators.
- So `EL_3(π_X): Λ := EL_3(F) → EL_3(R_X)` is onto: its image contains every `e_ij(π_X(r)) = e_ij(s)` with `s ∈ R_X`.
- `F` is finitely generated by `t_±, t_a` over the prime field (`F_q` needs one more generator when `q` is not prime; add it). So `Λ` has property (T) by Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain (`ejz-elementary-groups-explicit-kazhdan-constant`).
- Composing with the quotient by the centre gives `ρ_X: Λ → S_X`. Let `N_X = ker ρ_X`.

**Step 2: simple quotients.**
- `S_X` is infinite and simple (`simple-kazhdan-lef-group-from-minimal-subshift`).
- For `q = 2` the centre is trivial (`subshift-f2-ring-indicator-ideals-and-centreless-el3`), so `S_X = G_X`.

**Step 3: distinct kernels.**
- Let `X ≠ Y` be subshifts of `A^Z`. A subshift is determined by its language, so after swapping `X` and `Y` there is `v = v_0⋯v_{n−1} ∈ L(X) ∖ L(Y)`.
- Put `c_v = ∏_{t<n} t_-^t t_{v_t} t_+^t ∈ F`. Then:
  - `π_Z(c_v) = ∏_t u^{-t} e_{v_t} u^t`, the product of the indicators of `{x : x_t = v_t}`, which is the cylinder indicator `e_{[v]}`;
  - `e_{[v]} = 0` in `R_Y` and `e_{[v]} ≠ 0` in `R_X`.
- So `ρ_Y(e_12(c_v)) = 1`.
- Suppose `e_12(r)`, with `r = e_{[v]}`, were central in `EL_3(R_X)`. It would commute with `e_21(1)`. But `e_12(r)e_21(1) = I + rE_12 + E_21 + rE_11` and `e_21(1)e_12(r) = I + E_21 + rE_12 + rE_22`, whose `(1,1)` entries are `1 + r` and `1`. So `r = 0`, a contradiction.
- So `ρ_X(e_12(c_v)) ≠ 1`, and `N_X ≠ N_Y`.

**Step 4 (item 1).** Apply `kazhdan-simple-quotients-countable-in-separable-factors` to `Λ` and the family `{N_X}`.

**Step 5 (item 2).**
- If `L(S_Y) ≅ L(S_X)`, then `U(L(S_X))` contains a copy of `S_Y`, and `L(S_X)` is separable because `S_X` is countable. By item 1, only countably many `Y` occur.
- The `L(G_X)` are (T) factors in `R^ω` (`simple-kazhdan-group-gives-property-t-factor-in-r-omega`: infinite simple ⇒ ICC; Connes–Jones; hyperlinear by LEF).
- There are continuum many infinite minimal `X ⊆ {0,1}^Z` (`continuum-many-infinite-minimal-binary-subshifts`). Each isomorphism class contains countably many of them, so there are continuum many classes.

**Step 6 (item 3).** A separable `M` containing every `L(G_X)` would have every `G_X` in `U(M)`, contradicting item 1. ∎
