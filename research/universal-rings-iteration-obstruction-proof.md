**Imported.**
- **JZ** = A. Jaikin-Zapirain, *The universality of Hughes-free division rings*, Selecta Math. 2021.
  - Prop. 4.1: if `G` is not locally indicable, `Q[G]` has no universal division ring of fractions. A universal ring
    must be epic and injective, and its ranks must be at least those of every division `Q[G]`-ring, including
    non-injective ones such as the augmentation.
  - Thm 1.1: amenable locally indicable groups are Lewin. Both statements are quoted as in arXiv:2603.26580, §1.
- **Grater**, Cor. 8.3: for locally indicable `G`, a Hughes-free division ring is Linnell.
- **FSP** (arXiv:2303.08165), via `fsp-graph-of-rings-and-linnell-extension-imports`:
  - Cor. locIndGraphDivRing: the graph-of-rings embedding `k[G] -> U(DG_Gamma)` for Hughes-free vertex rings.
  - Lemma HF_amalg: if `D_(kG)` exists, then `U(DG_Gamma) = D_(kG)`, so q:HF is equivalent to (b).
- **Jaikin-Zapirain--Lopez-Alvarez**: for locally indicable `G`, `D_(Q[G])` exists, as does `D_(kG)` for `k` a
  subfield of `C`.
- **Tamari and Kropholler--Linnell--Moody**: for a torsion-free elementary amenable group, `k[G]` is an Ore domain.

**1. The amalgam.**
- Let `K1 = <x, b | x^-1 b x = b^-1>`, the Klein-bottle group `Z semidirect Z`.
  - `x^2` acts trivially on `<b>`, so `<x^2, b> = Z x Z`, of index 2.
- Let `K2 = <y, a | y^-1 a y = a^-1>`, with `<a, y^2> = Z^2`.
- Amalgamate along `a = x^2`, `b = y^2`.
  - The relation `[a, b] = 1` holds in each factor, so the identification is an isomorphism of `Z^2` subgroups.
- The presentation is `<x, b, y, a | x^-1 b x = b^-1, y^-1 a y = a^-1, a = x^2, b = y^2>`. Eliminating `a, b` gives
  `<x, y | x^-1 y^2 x = y^-2, y^-1 x^2 y = x^-2> = P`.

**2. `P` is torsion-free, amenable and not locally indicable.**
- **Torsion-free.** A finite-order element of an amalgam is conjugate into a factor, and the factors are
  torsion-free.
- **Not locally indicable.** Abelianising the relations gives `4y = 0` and `4x = 0`. So `H_1(P) = Z/4 x Z/4` is
  finite and `P` has no surjection onto `Z`, while `P` is non-trivial and finitely generated.
- **Amenable.** `P` is the Promislow (Hantzsche--Wendt) Bieberbach group, virtually `Z^3`.
- So `k[P]` is an Ore domain, and `Ore(k[P])` is its unique epic division ring of fractions: any injective map from an
  Ore domain to a division ring factors through the Ore field.

**3. Vertex and edge hypotheses hold.**
- `K1` and `K2` are amenable and locally indicable, so they are Lewin (JZ Thm 1.1).
- Their Hughes-free rings are `Ore(k[K_i])`, which are universal and, by Grater, Linnell.
- The edge group `Z^2` is locally indicable, and its closure in each vertex is `Ore(k[Z^2])`.

**4. The output is Linnell but not universal.**
- By the universal property of the ring coproduct, `R = D_K1 *_(D_Z^2) D_K2` maps to `Ore(k[P])`.
- FSP gives an injective map `k[P] -> U(R)`. Since `R` is generated by the closures of `k[K_i]`, `U(R)` is epic over
  `k[P]`.
- By step 2, `U(R) = Ore(k[P])`.
- It is Linnell. For `H <= P`, `H` is amenable and torsion-free, so `Div(k[H]) = Ore(k[H])`. A left relation over
  `Ore(k[H])` among right `H`-coset representatives clears to a common left denominator. This gives a relation over
  `k[H]` in the free `k[H]`-module `k[P]`, which is impossible.
- It is not universal for `Q[P]`, by JZ Prop. 4.1 and step 2.
- The rank function of `U(R)` dominates every division `R`-ring, but the augmentation `Q[P] -> Q` does not extend to
  `R`, since `x^2 - 1` is invertible in `D_K1`. So universality is not inherited.

**5. The equivalence (a) <=> (b) <=> (c) for locally indicable `G`.**
- **(a) => (b).** Let `N` be normal in a finitely generated `H <= G`, with `H/N = <tN> = Z`.
  - `{t^n}` is part of a right transversal of `N` in `G`.
  - Linnell independence over `Div(k[N])` gives the Hughes condition, so the Linnell ring is Hughes-free.
- **(b) => (a).** This is Grater, Cor. 8.3.
- **(b) <=> (c).** This is FSP Lemma HF_amalg. If `D_(kG)` exists, then `U(DG_Gamma) = D_(kG)`, which is Hughes-free.
  Conversely, a Hughes-free `U(DG_Gamma)` is `D_(kG)`.
- **Characteristic 0.** (b) holds by Jaikin-Zapirain--Lopez-Alvarez.

**6. Scope.**
- Steps 1--4 falsify the "universal" clause of the proposed iteration, with vertex groups as nice as possible
  (amenable, Lewin) and abelian edges.
- Step 5 shows the "Linnell" clause, for locally indicable `G`, is neither weaker nor stronger than q:HF. It is q:HF.
- No claim is made about non-locally-indicable `G` beyond the example.
