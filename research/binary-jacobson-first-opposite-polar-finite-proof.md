The Jacobson identities `Q^2=Q`, `QS=0`, `TQ=0`, `ST+Q=1` and `TS=1`
give

```text
h^2=1.                                                       (JPF5)
```

The root `g` is an involution in characteristic two.  Direct conjugation by
the displayed block, already computed in `(J3GP4)`, gives

```text
h g h=x_31(Q)x_32(S).                                       (JPF6)
```

The two factors on the right have the same source row.  They therefore
commute with `g=x_31(1)`, and consequently

```text
[g,hgh]=1.                                                  (JPF7)
```

For two involutions `(JPF7)` is equivalent to `(gh)^4=1`: the commuting
involutions `g` and `hgh` have product

```text
g(hgh)=(gh)^2.
```

It follows that

```text
B=<h,g>
```

is a quotient of the dihedral group of order eight and is therefore finite.

Let `L` be the finitary kernel in the symbol sequence.  The prerequisite
identifies it with `GL_fin(N x {1,2,3},F_2)` (inside the selected block), so
it is normal and locally finite.  The packet `C_N` is a finite subgroup of
`L`.  Since `B` is finite, the normal subgroup

```text
D_N=<b C_N b^(-1):b in B>                                  (JPF8)
```

is generated by finitely many elements of the locally finite group `L` and
is therefore finite.  It is normalized by `B`, and

```text
H_N=D_N B.                                                  (JPF9)
```

Thus `H_N` is finite.  The head `w=x_13(Q)` belongs to every nonempty
Clifford packet and is nontrivial in the faithful standard Jacobson action,
so it remains nontrivial in `H_N`.

It remains only to justify that the advertised polar compression is not
zero.  In the left regular representation,

```text
4 P lambda(g) P
 =lambda(g)-lambda(wg)-lambda(gw)+lambda(wgw).                (JPF10)
```

The four group elements in `(JPF10)` are distinct.  The first three
equalities that could identify two of them would force `w=1`; the remaining
one would force `wg=gw`, which is false in the faithful standard action
(on the `Q`-head, the two opposite elementary transvections do not commute).
Linear independence of the regular basis therefore gives `A!=0`.

Finally, multiplication of the displayed block with the coefficient column
of `x_13(Q)` gives

```text
h x_13(Q) h=x_13(Q),                                       (JPF11)
```

because `Q^2=Q` and `TQ=0`.  Hence `h` commutes with `w` and with `P`, which
proves `(JPF4)`.  The exact faithful finite model now supplies the claimed
polar-selector fence.
