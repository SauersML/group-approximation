**REFUTED** (2026-09-14, unreviewed) by
`amplified-roots-fail-in-odd-characteristic`: in every odd characteristic `p`,
the permutation matrices of two reflections of the `p`-gon and the word
`x^((p+1)/2) a x^(-(p-1)/2) b` have defect at least `(p-1)/(p^2 K_p)` at every
amplification (`1/225` for `p = 3`). The characteristic-zero case is split off
as `amplified-algebraic-roots-in-characteristic-zero` (OPEN).

The refuted statement: for every algebraically closed field `L`: for every `n >= 1`, every
tuple `a` in `GL_n(L)^k`, every `w` in `F_k * <t>` with nonzero exponent sum
in `t`, and every `epsilon > 0`, there are `r >= 1` and `T` in `GL_{nr}(L)`
with `rho(w(T ; a (x) 1_r) - 1) <= epsilon`, where `rho` is the normalized
rank. This is `ARR(L)` of `linear-sofic-kl-from-amplified-algebraic-roots`,
and it would give `kervaire-laudenbach-holds-for-linear-sofic-groups`.

**Known instances and walls.**
- **Unitary constants over `C`.** Exact roots with `r = 1` exist by the
  Gerstenhaber--Rothaus degree argument, as recorded in
  `kervaire-laudenbach-holds-for-hyperlinear`. This covers exactly the tuples
  that generate relatively compact subgroups.
- **Amplification is needed for exact roots.** In characteristic 2,
  `t^2 = J_2` has no root in `GL_2` but has one after doubling
  (`char-two-jordan-square-root-needs-amplification`).
- **Root existence is not an algebraic condition on constants.** For fixed
  `w` and `r`, the set of tuples admitting an exact root is the projection of
  an algebraic set, hence constructible. Over `C` it contains the Zariski-dense
  set of unitary tuples, so it contains a dense open set, but nothing forces it
  to be everything. Gordeev--Kunyavskii--Plotkin (arXiv:1801.00379, abstract
  l.189--190): "We present results on the images of such maps, in particular,
  we prove a theorem on the dominance of “general” word maps with constants";
  and (l.476--478) "there are words with constants $w_\Sigma$ such that
  $\Imm \w_\Sigma = 1$ (so-called identities with constants". Dominance does
  not put `1` in the image.
- **Module form.** For a finitely generated group `H` generated by the
  constants and `ρ : H --> GL_n(L)` the inclusion, an exact root with
  amplification `r` is the same as an `H_w`-module structure on `L^{nr}`
  restricting to `ρ (x) 1_r`. So the exact premise asks that every
  finite-dimensional module of every finitely generated group extend, after
  amplification, to every nonsingular adjunction. The approximate premise
  allows a rank-small defect.
