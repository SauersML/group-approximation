**ESTABLISHED** through `gl-n-q-embeds-in-e-n-plus-1-of-a-fp-ring-proof` (lane proof, not
independently reviewed; no priority claimed).

**Statement.** Let `R_T` and `E = 1 - ST` be as in
`q-embeds-in-a-corner-of-a-fp-toeplitz-ring`, and let `n >= 2`.
- `GL_n(Q)` embeds in `E_(n+1)(R_T)`.
- `E_(n+1)(R_T)` is generated by the finitely many elementary matrices `e_ij(x)`,
  `x ∈ {1, S, T, N, A}`, `i ≠ j`.
- The same holds in the concrete model: `GL_n(Q)` embeds in the finitely generated group
  `E_(n+1)(ρ(R_T))` of explicit invertible `(n+1) x (n+1)` block matrices over the operator
  ring `ρ(R_T) ⊂ End_Q(Q^(N))`. That group has solvable word problem.

**Relevance.**
- This is a concrete finitely generated overgroup of `GL_n(Q)` with solvable word problem,
  defined over a finitely presented ring. It is not finitely presented as far as is known,
  and it is not simple.
- It gives Problem 2.7 (`gl-n-q-embeds-in-fp-simple-group`, Sources) a target: if some
  group such as `E_(n+1)(R_T)` or `St_(n+1)(R_T)` were finitely presented and contained
  `GL_n(Q)`, that would be an explicit finitely presented overgroup. This needs `K_2`
  control of `R_T`, and lane `gq-en-ring` owns that question.
- Every proper quotient of the concrete model ring kills `E`
  (`q-embeds-in-a-corner-of-a-fp-toeplitz-ring`, Limits). So simplicity cannot come from
  a ring quotient here. It has to come from the group side, or from a different ring.
