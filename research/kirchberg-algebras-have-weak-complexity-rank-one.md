**ESTABLISHED (literature).**  Jaime--Willett, *Complexity rank for
C\*-algebras*, arXiv:2205.04704v2, Theorem 1.4: a separable unital simple
C\*-algebra has weak complexity rank at most one if and only if it has nuclear
dimension at most one and real rank zero.  Weak complexity rank at most one
(their Definition 1.3) asks, for every finite `X ⊂ A` and `ε > 0`, for
finite-dimensional C\*-subalgebras `C, D ∋ 1_A` and a positive contraction `h`
with `||[h, x]|| < ε`, `hx ∈_ε C` and `(1 − h)x ∈_ε D` for `x ∈ X`.

Kirchberg algebras have nuclear dimension one (Bosa--Brown--Sato--Tikuisis--
White--Winter, Mem. Amer. Math. Soc. 257 (2019), Theorem G) and real rank zero
(Zhang, as cited in Jaime--Willett).  So every unital Kirchberg algebra, with or
without the UCT and whatever its K-theory, has weak complexity rank at most one.
Jaime--Willett record this consequence directly after Theorem 1.4.
