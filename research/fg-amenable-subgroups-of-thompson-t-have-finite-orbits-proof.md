Let `H = ⟨h_1, …, h_n⟩ ≤ Homeo+(S¹)` be amenable, each `h_i` with a periodic point. Write `S¹ = R/Z` and
`p : R → S¹` for the projection.

**Step 1 (invariant measure).** `H` is amenable and acts continuously on the compact metrizable space `S¹`,
so it preserves a Borel probability measure `ν`. Let `ν̃` be the lift to `R`, invariant under `x ↦ x + 1`,
with `ν̃[x, x+1) = 1`.

**Step 2 (rotation number is a homomorphism with finite image).** For `h ∈ H` and a lift `h̃`, put
`d(h̃, x) = ⟨x, h̃x⟩`, where `⟨a, b⟩ = ν̃[a, b)` if `a ≤ b` and `−ν̃[b, a)` otherwise (so
`⟨a, b⟩ + ⟨b, c⟩ = ⟨a, c⟩`). For any `x, y`,
`d(h̃, y) − d(h̃, x) = ⟨h̃x, h̃y⟩ − ⟨x, y⟩ = 0`, because `h̃` maps `[x, y)` onto `[h̃x, h̃y)` and preserves
`ν̃`. So `d(h̃) := d(h̃, x)` does not depend on `x`. It is additive,
`d(g̃h̃) = ⟨x, h̃x⟩ + ⟨h̃x, g̃h̃x⟩ = d(h̃) + d(g̃)`, and changing a lift adds an integer. Hence
`r(h) := d(h̃) mod 1` is a homomorphism `H → R/Z`.

If `h_i` has a periodic point of period `q_i`, pick a lift `x̃` of it; then `h̃_i^{q_i}(x̃) = x̃ + k` for some
`k ∈ Z`, so `q_i·d(h̃_i) = d(h̃_i^{q_i}, x̃) = ⟨x̃, x̃ + k⟩ = k`. Thus `r(h_i) ∈ (1/q_i)Z/Z`. The image `r(H)`
is generated by the `r(h_i)`, so it is finite cyclic, of order `m` say. Put `H_1 = ker r`, of index `m`.

**Step 3 (a finite orbit).** *Case: `ν` has an atom.* The atoms of maximal mass form a nonempty finite
`H`-invariant set. It is a finite union of finite orbits.

*Case: `ν` has no atom.* We claim `H_1` fixes `K = supp ν` pointwise. Let `h ∈ H_1` and choose the lift
with `d(h̃) = 0`. Let `x ∈ p^{-1}K` and suppose `h̃x > x` (the case `h̃x < x` is symmetric, using `h^{-1}`).
Then `ν̃[x, h̃x) = 0`, so `(x, h̃x)` lies in a component `(x, y)` of the complement of `p^{-1}K`, with
`h̃x ≤ y`. Since `K` is `H`-invariant and `h̃x ∈ p^{-1}K`, `h̃x = y`. Now `y` is the right end of a gap and
lies in `p^{-1}K`. It is not also the left end of a gap: then `p(y)` would be an isolated point of `K`, and an
isolated point of the support of a measure is an atom. So `ν̃[y, y + ε) > 0` for every `ε > 0`. But
`d(h̃, y) = 0`, and `h̃y > h̃x = y`, so `ν̃[y, h̃y) = 0`, a contradiction. Hence `h̃x = x`, i.e. `h` fixes
`K` pointwise.

For `x ∈ K` the stabilizer of `x` contains `H_1`, so the orbit `Hx` has at most `[H : H_1] = m` points. `K` is
nonempty, so `H` has a finite orbit.

**Step 4 (items 2 and 3).** For a finite orbit `O`, the action of `H` on `O` has kernel `H_0` of index at most
`|O|!`. A group is amenable iff a finite-index subgroup is. Item 3 is the contrapositive of item 1. ∎

**Remark (why the periodic-point hypothesis matters).** An irrational rotation generates an amenable
subgroup of `Homeo+(S¹)` with no finite orbit. So the hypothesis cannot be dropped for general circle
groups. For `T` it holds by Ghys–Sergiescu, and in every computation below it is checked directly on the
generators.
