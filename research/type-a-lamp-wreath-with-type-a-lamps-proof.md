Direct proof (2026-09-13, lane `solve-bh-free-solvable`). Not independently reviewed.

**Imported theorem (Cornulier, arXiv:math/0509090).** As pinned verbatim in
`bh-type-a-lamp-wreath-closure-proof` (TeX l.147--157, not re-read here): "If
$W\neq \{1\}$, the wreath product $W\wr_X G$ is finitely presented if and only if
the following conditions are satisfied (i) $W$ and $G$ are finitely presented,
(ii) $G$ acts on $X$ with finitely generated stabilizers, and (iii) the product
action of $G$ on the cartesian square $X^2$ has finitely many orbits." Zaremsky
restates it (arXiv:2405.18354, TeX l.126, read 2026-09-13 on MSI): "By
\cite{cornulier06}, the permutational wreath product $W\wr_S G \coloneqq
\bigoplus_S W \rtimes G$ is finitely presented if and only if the action of $G$ on
$S$ is of type~(A) and $W\ne\{1\}$ is finitely presented."

Notation as in the target. Write `λ_t` for the function supported at `t` with value
`λ`. Then `(1, γ)(λ_t, 1)(1, γ)^(-1) = (λ_(γt), 1)`.

**0. (LW) is an action.**
- Acting first by `(f', γ')` and then by `(f, γ)` sends `(s, y)` to
  `(γγ's, f(γγ's) f'(γ's) y)`.
- The product `(f·(γ·f'), γγ')` sends `(s, y)` to
  `(γγ's, f(γγ's) f'(γ^(-1)γγ's) y)`, which is the same point.
- The identity acts trivially.

**1. Faithful.** Suppose `(f, γ)` fixes every `(s, y)`.
- Then `γ` fixes every `s`, so `γ = 1` because `Γ` acts faithfully.
- Then `f(s)` fixes every `y`, so `f(s) = 1` because `Λ` acts faithfully.
- So `f = 1`.

**2. Finitely presented.** `Λ ≠ 1` and `Γ` are finitely presented. `Γ` has finitely
generated point stabilizers on `S`, and finitely many orbits on `S × S`. Cornulier's
theorem applies with `W = Λ`, `X = S`, `G = Γ`.

**3. Stabilizers finitely generated.**
- The stabilizer of `(s, y)` is `{(f, γ) : γs = s, f(s) ∈ Stab_Λ(y)}`, which is the
  displayed semidirect product.
- `Stab_Γ(s)` has finitely many orbits on `S ∖ {s}`. Two ordered pairs `(s, t)`
  and `(s, t')` lie in one `Γ`-orbit exactly when `t` and `t'` lie in one
  `Stab_Γ(s)`-orbit, and there are finitely many orbits of ordered pairs. Pick
  representatives `t_1, ..., t_m`.
- The stabilizer is generated by three families:
  - `(1, γ)` for `γ` in a finite generating set of `Stab_Γ(s)`;
  - `(λ_s, 1)` for `λ` in a finite generating set of `Stab_Λ(y)`;
  - `(λ_(t_i), 1)` for `λ` in a finite generating set of `Λ`.
- The last family gives all of `Λ` at each `t_i`, since `λ ↦ λ_(t_i)` is a
  homomorphism.
- For `t = γ t_i` with `γ ∈ Stab_Γ(s)`, conjugating by `(1, γ)` gives `Λ` at `t`.
  So all of `Λ^(S∖{s})` is generated.
- An element `(f, γ)` of the stabilizer factors as
  `(f(s)_s, 1) · (f|_(S∖{s}), 1) · (1, γ)`.

**4. Finitely many orbits of ordered pairs of `S × Y`.** Take `((s, y), (s', y'))`.
- **`s ≠ s'`.** Some `γ ∈ Γ` moves `(s, s')` to one of finitely many representative
  pairs `(r, r')`. A lamp function supported on `{r, r'}` then acts by arbitrary
  independent elements of `Λ` on the two `Y`-coordinates. So the orbit is determined
  by the representative and the `Λ`-orbits of the two `Y`-coordinates. Finitely many.
- **`s = s'`.** Move `s` to one of finitely many point representatives `r`.
  Functions supported at `r` act on `(y, y')` by the diagonal action of `Λ`, which
  has finitely many orbits on `Y × Y`. Finitely many.

`Λ` has finitely many point orbits because it has finitely many orbits on `Y × Y`
(the diagonal). So (LW) is of type (A) in the BFFHZ form, hence in Zaremsky's form
(see the target). The consequence is `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`
(Zaremsky, arXiv:2405.18354, Corollary B, quoted there verbatim). `∎`
