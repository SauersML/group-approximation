**ESTABLISHED** by `orbit-preserving-germ-corollary-rationality-proof`.

**Setting.** `C = {0,1}^N` and `V` is the standard binary Thompson group,
acting by prefix replacements. For `h ∈ Homeo(C)` the singular set `sing(h)` is
the set of points near which `h` agrees with no element of `V` (Belk–Hyde–Matucci,
arXiv:2407.03149, §1). `G <= Homeo(C)` is a **finite germ extension of `V`**:
every element has finitely many singular points, and `V` is exactly the set of
elements with none. For `p ∈ C`, `(G)_p` is the group of germs at `p` of elements
of `G` fixing `p`, and `(V)_p <= (G)_p` the germs of elements of `V`.

**Theorem.** Let `G` be a finitely generated finite germ extension of `V`.
Suppose that for every `p ∈ sing(G)`:

- **(H2)** `Fix_V({p})` is finitely generated;
- **(H3)** `|(G)_p : (V)_p| < ∞`, or `(V)_p` is normal in `(G)_p`;
- **(T)** `g(p) ∈ V·p` for every `g ∈ G`.

Then every element of `G` is a rational homeomorphism (realized by a finite
asynchronous transducer), and the word problem of `G` is decidable in time
`2^(O(n))`.

(H2) is the case `|M| = 1` of hypothesis (2) of Belk–Hyde–Matucci Corollary 2.10
for any `n >= 1`, and (H3) is the either-or of its hypothesis (3) with the `F_n`
clause dropped. (T) holds whenever `sing(G)` is a single `V`-orbit, since
`sing(G)` is `G`-invariant.

**Intermediate facts proved in the route.**
1. Under (H2) every singular point is eventually periodic: for a point that is
   not, `Fix_V({p})` is a strictly increasing union of pointwise stabilizers of
   shrinking cones.
2. For `p = u w^∞` with `w` primitive, `(V)_p` is infinite cyclic, generated by
   the germ `τ_p` of `u y -> u w y`.
3. Under (H3) some power `τ_p^N` commutes with every germ in `(G)_p`. The
   conjugate of an attracting germ is attracting, so it cannot equal `τ_p^(-N)`.
4. A germ commuting with `τ_p^N` is determined by one fundamental annulus, on
   which it is a finite prefix replacement, so it is rational near `p`.

**Consequences.**
- Every such `G`, and every subgroup of it, has word problem in `F(2^n)` (notation
  of `uniform-wp-bound-excludes-bh-universal-targets`). By
  `complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal` these groups form a bounded
  host class, so none of them, and no subgroup of one, witnesses
  `fp-simple-groups-with-arbitrarily-complex-word-problem`.
- **What a hard witness in the finite-germ branch needs.** Either Belk–Hyde–Matucci
  Theorem 2.1 with germ groups that do not normalize any finite-index subgroup of
  their `V`-germs (as for shell envelopes of non-virtually-cyclic inputs), or a
  singular transport `g(q) = q'` between different `V`-orbits. There the relation
  `g τ_q^N = σ g` only makes `σ` rational, and `g` near `q` may apply an
  unbounded iterate of a fixed annulus map. No such group is known to be finitely
  presented, and no complexity bound is claimed for it.

No novelty is claimed; the argument is elementary once the Belk–Hyde–Matucci
definitions are in place.
