Put

```text
c=(a-epsilon/2)_+,       b=(a-epsilon)_+.
```

Then `b=(c-epsilon/2)_+`.  If `c=0`, take the one-element packet
`x_1=0`.  Assume henceforth that `c` is nonzero.

### Local exact square-zero separation

Fix a primitive ideal `P` not containing `c`, and choose an irreducible
representation `pi:A -> B(H)` with kernel `P`.  The hypothesis on `A` makes
`H` infinite-dimensional (dimension at least two is all that is used).
Choose orthonormal vectors `xi,eta in H`.

By the self-adjoint form of Kadison transitivity, there is a self-adjoint
`h in A` for which

```text
pi(h)xi=xi,       pi(h)eta=0.
```

The self-adjoint form applies because the prescribed operator on
`span{xi,eta}` is the self-adjoint diagonal operator with eigenvalues `1,0`.
It is valid for a nonunital `A` as stated: the representation is nondegenerate,
and no unit of `A` is used.

Choose nonnegative continuous functions `f,g` on the spectrum of `h` with
disjoint supports, `f(1)=1`, and `g(0)=1`, and put `e=f(h)`, `d=g(h)`.
Thus `ed=0`, `pi(e)xi=xi`, and `pi(d)eta=eta`.

Every nonzero vector is cyclic for an irreducible representation: the closed
subspace generated by `pi(A)eta` is nonzero and reducing.  Hence some `y in A`
satisfies

```text
<pi(y)eta,xi> != 0.
```

For `x_P=eyd` we consequently have

```text
x_P^2=ey(de)yd=0,
<pi(x_P)eta,xi>=<pi(y)eta,xi> != 0.
```

In particular, `x_P` does not belong to `P`.

### The whole local family generates `c`

Let

```text
J=closed sum { Ideal(x_P) : P primitive and c notin P }.
```

We claim that `c in J`.  Otherwise the nonzero positive element `c+J` in
`A/J` is avoided by some primitive ideal of `A/J`.  Its inverse image `Q` is
a primitive ideal of `A` with `c notin Q`.  But `J` contains `x_Q`, while the
local construction gives `x_Q notin Q`, contradicting `J subset Q`.

### The spectral gap makes the packet finite

Since `c in J`, choose an element `z` in the algebraic sum of finitely many
of the ideals `Ideal(x_P)` such that

```text
||c-z|| < epsilon/2.
```

Let those finitely many elements be `x_1,...,x_n` and put
`K=Ideal(x_1)+...+Ideal(x_n)`.  In the quotient `A/K`,

```text
||c+K|| < epsilon/2.
```

The finite sum `K` is closed.  Indeed, the sum of two closed C-star ideals is
closed because its image modulo either ideal is the C-star algebra
`I/(I intersect J)`; induction handles a finite sum.

Functional calculus in the quotient therefore gives

```text
b+K=(c-epsilon/2)_+ + K=0.
```

Thus `b in K`, as required.  Notice that finiteness comes from the positive
spectral gap between `c` and `b`, not from quasi-compactness of the whole
primitive ideal space.

For completeness, both cutoff identities used above are exact scalar
functional-calculus identities:

```text
((a-epsilon/2)_+-epsilon/2)_+=(a-epsilon)_+,
q((c-epsilon/2)_+)=(q(c)-epsilon/2)_+.
```

Since `q(c)` is positive with norm strictly below `epsilon/2`, the last
positive part is exactly zero, not merely small.
