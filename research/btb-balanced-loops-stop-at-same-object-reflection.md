**ESTABLISHED TWO-CELL FENCE.**  Fix one protected BTB--E5 packet type and
its selected Reynolds cut.  Consider a proposed mixed corner cell assembled
from the following finite language:

1. rectangular coefficient arrows and their adjoints;
2. rank-five or rank-six Steinberg multiplication triangles, distant-root
   commutations, and their exponent-balanced closed pastings; and
3. at most a fixed finite packet projective phase on a closed pair of edge
   intertwiners.

This language does not force a fixed fraction of the detected fiber outside
its current carrier.

For clauses 1--2, take a nonzero finite space `K`, put `E=K`, `F=K`, and
evaluate every coefficient arrow as a rectangular map between separately
named source and target copies.  Block transvections satisfy every Steinberg
triangle exactly, and every exponent-balanced pasted loop is an associativity
diagram.  The source and target minimal corners have equal rank, while their
ambient object identities remain independently placeable.  Choose those
copies inside one common multiplicity carrier.  The BTB outer coordinates
and E5 spectator cut tensor with this construction and therefore do not
orient the carrier ownership.

If the extra two-cell contributes a scalar phase `zeta` of fixed order `m`
on the selected packet simple, put the inverse phase on external
multiplicity.  For the basic closed pair use the `m`-dimensional clock and
shift matrices

```text
D S=zeta^(-1) S D.                                    (BCL1)
```

They cancel the packet phase exactly.  A fixed finite family of binary
projective squares is handled by its fixed Clifford module.  Arbitrary
multiplicity is obtained on complete copies of that fixed module, and an
incomplete remainder has total squared normalized-HS cost `O(1/d)`.
Consequently a fixed projective loop supplies a divisibility condition, not
a positive-density escape.

This is the smallest exact enemy to the proposed exponent-balanced repair:
one typed source copy, one typed target copy on the same carrier, and one
fixed inverse Weyl module.  It preserves every multiplication triangle and
the closed two-cell while the returned selected fiber remains wholly in the
carrier.  It is a local specialization countermodel, not a representation of
the full non-CE endpoint, and therefore fences precisely this decoder
language rather than the complete CBR3 claim.

The first relation outside the countermodel is a **same-object support row**.
Writing `e` and `f` for the source and target cuts and `W` for the Morita
swap, it has the analytic form

```text
(1-2f) W (1-2e) W^*=1                                (BCL2)
```

on one common carrier identity.  This row detects support leakage rather
than coefficient associativity or a scalar cocycle.  The known Clifford
completion does not yet authenticate `(BCL2)`: the target reflection is
represented as `(1-2f) tensor V` with an arbitrary multiplicity involution
`V`, and `(BCL2)` is exactly where that twist fails to cancel.

Thus an exponent-balanced mixed loop closes compressed antiphase only if it
supplies the same-object carrier reflection (or an equivalent matrix-only
support-leakage estimate).  More Steinberg triangles or one fixed projective
holonomy leave the finite rectangular--Weyl countermodel intact.
