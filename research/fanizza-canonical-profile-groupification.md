Totally computably from `m`, construct a finite generating set, a uniformly
recursively enumerable ordinary group presentation `Gamma_m`, and a finite
rational group-algebra mark `p_m` such that

```text
m halts      => p_m != 0 in Q[Gamma_m],
m nonhalts   => every canonical-delta microstate sequence for Gamma_m
                satisfies ||p_m(U_n)||_2 -> 0.              (FCP1)
```

The mark may be a selected finite-packet corner such as `q_rho D_m`; it need
not be a single group word. The canonical trace restriction to a fixed finite
subgroup is its regular character, so noncanonical forbidden-sector
representations do not refute `(FCP1)`.

This avoids the trivial-homomorphism obstruction
`unconditional-bcs-ultraproduct-decoder-forces-ce` and the finite central-sign
counterrepresentations of
`finite-packet-central-sign-cannot-enforce-nonlinear-support`. The remaining
obligation is nevertheless substantive: on canonical microstates, the
selected packet carriers must be aligned across all noncommuting Fanizza
contexts and must pay the native nonlinear BCS soundness estimate.

## Attempts

- A mark-relative decoder may be defined only on the positive-density selected
  type corner of canonical finite-packet microstates. The trivial group
  homomorphism has the wrong trace profile and is outside the quantifier.
- Exact finite forbidden-sector representations are harmless when their
  characters are noncanonical; a compiler still fails if such sectors can
  occur at positive Plancherel density in canonical microstates without a
  common-carrier return.
- The stronger quantitative and unconditional Fanizza groupifiers imply this
  target, but preserve unnecessary universal quantifiers. The live content is
  the canonical selected-type alignment, not a computable modulus.
