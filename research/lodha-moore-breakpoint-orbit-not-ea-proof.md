## Step 1. Singular points

Write pieces as integer matrices `(p q; r s) : t ↦ (pt + q)/(rt + s)`.
- `b` has pieces `(1 0; 0 1)`, `(1 0; −1 1)`, `(3 −1; 1 0)` and `(1 1; 0 1)`, meeting at `0`, `1/2` and `1`.
- `c` has pieces `(1 0; 0 1)`, `(2 0; 1 1)` and `(1 0; 0 1)`, meeting at `0` and `1`.

Adjacent pieces are non-proportional matrices, so they are distinct projective maps. Two distinct
projective maps agree at no more than two points. Suppose a single projective map `γ` agreed with `b` near a
junction. Then `γ` would equal both adjacent pieces on intervals, which is impossible.

So `sing(b) = {0, 1/2, 1}`, `sing(c) = {0, 1}` and `sing(a) = ∅`. The script
`experiments/lodha-moore-ea-calibrator-2026-09-17/orbit_of_zero.py` checks the matrices, the inverse
formulas and the non-proportionality.

## Step 2. The orbit of 0 is Q

*`Q` is invariant.* Every piece of `a`, `b`, `c` and `t ↦ t + 1/2` lies in `PGL_2(Q)`, and the junctions are
rational. So `F`, `G_0` and `G_{1/2}` map `Q` onto `Q`.

*`Q ⊆ F·0`.* Induct on the reduced denominator `q` of `x = p/q`.
- If `q = 1`, then `a^{−x}(x) = 0`.
- If `q ≥ 2`, put `x' = a^{−⌊x⌋}(x) = p'/q`, with `0 < p' < q` and `gcd(p', q) = 1`.
  - If `x' ≤ 1/2`, then `b(x') = p'/(q − p')`. This is reduced, since `gcd(p', q − p') = gcd(p', q) = 1`,
    and its denominator is `q − p' < q`.
  - If `x' > 1/2`, then `b(x') = 3 − q/p' = (3p' − q)/p'`. This is reduced, since
    `gcd(3p' − q, p') = gcd(q, p') = 1`, and its denominator is `p' < q`.
- Either way the denominator drops, so `x ∈ F·0` by induction.

The script runs this descent on 20000 random rationals and checks that the denominator drops at every
`b`-step.

*Breakpoint sets.* `F ≤ G_0` (`thompson-f-is-a-subgroup-of-lodha-moore-group`), and
`F ≤ G_{1/2}` because `a = (t ↦ t + 1/2)^2`. For each of the three groups, the union of the generators'
singular sets is `{0, 1/2, 1} ⊂ Q`. By item 1 of `piecewise-projective-groups-amenable-iff-breakpoint-action-ea`,
`B = G·{0, 1/2, 1} = Q`, and this is a single orbit. This proves item 1.

## Step 3. Item 2

- *Hereditarily amenable.* `G_0 ≤ H` (Lodha–Moore l.128), so item 3 of
  `piecewise-projective-groups-amenable-iff-breakpoint-action-ea` applies to `G_0` and to its orbit `Q`.
- *Not extensively amenable.* `G_0` is nonamenable (Lodha–Moore l.124–127) and `B(G_0) = Q`. Item 2 of
  `piecewise-projective-groups-amenable-iff-breakpoint-action-ea` then says `G_0 ↷ Q` is not extensively
  amenable.
- *Lamplighter form.* The action is transitive, so `affine-lamplighter-amenable-iff-extensively-amenable`
  applies.
- *Quantitative form.* `G_0 = ⟨a, b, c⟩` acts transitively on `Q`. Condition (ii) of JMMdlS l.511–518
  fails for every admissible `μ` and `x_0`: some `ε > 0` has `P(|O_n| < εn) > e^{−εn}` for only finitely
  many `n`. That is the stated bound for all large `n`.

## Step 4. Item 3

`F ↷ Q` is the restriction of `G_0 ↷ Q` to `F`, and `B(F) = Q` by Step 2. Then:
- `F` is amenable iff `F ↷ Q` is extensively amenable, by item 2 of the localized theorem;
- iff `F ↷ D` is extensively amenable, by `thompson-f-amenable-iff-dyadic-action-extensively-amenable`;
- iff the confinement of `f-dyadic-inverted-orbit-is-subballistic-on-rare-events` holds, by
  `f-dyadic-ea-iff-inverted-orbit-confinement`.

## Step 5. Item 4

*`C^1`.* `(t/(1−t))' = (1−t)^{−2}` takes the value `1` at `0` and `4` at `1/2`. `(3 − 1/t)' = t^{−2}` takes the
value `4` at `1/2` and `1` at `1`. So `b` is `C^1`. Translations are `C^1`, and `C^1` homeomorphisms whose
derivative never vanishes form a group. So every element of `G_{1/2}` is `C^1`.

*Integrality.* The pieces lie in `PSL_2(Z[1/2])`, because `(1 1/2; 0 1)` has determinant 1.

*The calibrator.* By item 2 of the localized theorem with `B(G_{1/2}) = Q`, `G_{1/2} ↷ Q` is extensively
amenable iff `G_{1/2}` is amenable. Lodha–Moore l.136–138 assert that `G_{1/2}` is nonamenable, but give no
proof. So item 4 is conditional on that assertion.

## Step 6. The shared list in the class kill

Each property listed in the claim holds for `F ↷ Q` and for `G_0 ↷ Q`:
- *Hereditary amenability.* Step 3, and the same argument for `F ≤ H`.
- *JNS germ data.* Step 0 of `piecewise-projective-breakpoint-ea-proof`, and
  Step 1 here.
- *Finite presentation.* For `G_0`, Lodha–Moore l.124–127. For `F`, its standard two-relator
  presentation.
- *No free subgroups.* Both groups lie in `H` (Lodha–Moore l.128).
- *Translation germ at `±∞`.* This is `a`, which lies in both groups.

`G_0 ↷ Q` has all of these properties and is not extensively amenable. So no implication from these
properties to extensive amenability is valid. That is the precise content of the kill. ∎

## Scope and honesty

- The nonamenability of `G_0` is imported. It rests on Monod's method via Carrière–Ghys and
  Connes–Feldman–Weiss. The same trust surface appears in
  `thompson-f-not-co-amenable-in-known-hosts-proof`.
- Item 4 rests on an unproved remark in Lodha–Moore and is flagged as conditional.
- The kill is a statement about implications from the listed properties. It does not bear on whether `F`
  is amenable.
