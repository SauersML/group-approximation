Fix a shared variable `x` and incident contexts `c,d`.  By `(FFC1)` and
`(FFC2)`,

```text
p_x in C[N_c] intersection C[N_d]
    = C[N_c intersection N_d].                         (FFP1)
```

Both `N_c` and `N_d` have prime order `p`.  Hence either they are equal or
their intersection is trivial.  In the latter case `(FFP1)` says that
`p_x` is a scalar projection, hence is zero or one.  But `p_x<=q_c<1`, so it
cannot be one.  Therefore

```text
p_x!=0  implies  N_c=N_d.                              (FFP2)
```

Partition the contexts by the equivalence relation generated by incidences
whose common negative carrier is nonzero.  Equation `(FFP2)` gives one
literal prime cyclic subgroup `N` for every equivalence class.  Choose one
nontrivial character `chi_N` of each such subgroup.  For a context `c` in
that class, the Fourier PVM selects a unique local assignment

```text
a_c=ell_c(chi_N).
```

It is allowed because `chi_N` lies under `q_c` and every nontrivial Fourier
atom in `(FFC1)` is predicate-allowed.

It remains to check consistency.  If a shared variable `x` has `p_x!=0`,
all its incident contexts lie in the same equivalence class.  Evaluating the
common projection `(FFC2)` at `chi_N` says that the negative-value indicator
of `x` is identical in every incident context, so their signs agree.  If
`p_x=0`, `(FFC2)` says the negative indicator vanishes at every nontrivial
character in every incident context; all those contexts assign the positive
sign to `x`, even if they lie in different equivalence classes.  Thus the
allowed local assignments `(a_c)` agree on every shared variable and combine
to a classical satisfying assignment.
