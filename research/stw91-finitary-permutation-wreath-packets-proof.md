Fix a faithful representation `P <= GL_d(k)`.  For a finite subgroup
`K <= L`, write

```text
supp(K)=union_(h in K) {x in X : hx != x}.
```

This set is finite.  Let `(S,K)` range over pairs for which `S subset X` is
finite, nonempty, `K`-invariant, and contains `supp(K)`, and put

```text
W_(S,K)=P^S rtimes K.                                (1)
```

## 1. The packets form a directed exhaustion

Every finitely generated subgroup of `Sym_fin(X)` is finite: the union of
the supports of finitely many generators is finite, and the generated group
embeds in the symmetric group of that finite set.  In particular every
finitely generated subgroup of `L` is finite.

Given two packet pairs, let `K` be the finite subgroup generated by their two
finite permutation groups and enlarge the union of their coordinate sets to
its finite `K`-orbit together with `supp(K)`.  This gives a third packet
containing both.  Conversely, any finite set of elements of `W` uses only
finitely many base coordinates and finitely many permutations, so it lies in
one of the packets.  Hence

```text
W = union_(S,K) W_(S,K)                              (2)
```

as an upward directed union.

## 2. Every packet is linear

The direct power `P^S` acts by block diagonal matrices on `k^(d|S|)`, and
`K` acts by permutation of the `d`-dimensional coordinate blocks.  Their
combined monomial-block representation realizes (1) inside
`GL_(d|S|)(k)`.  It is faithful: because `S` contains `supp(K)`, the action
of `K` on `S` is faithful, so an identity block-permutation matrix has trivial
permutation part and then trivial diagonal part.

## 3. The amenable radical of every packet is trivial

Set `N=P^S` and `G=N rtimes K`.  First `Rad_am(N)={e}`.  Indeed, the image of
an amenable normal subgroup of `N` under every coordinate projection is an
amenable normal subgroup of `P`, hence trivial.

Let `R=Rad_am(G)`.  Then `R intersect N` is an amenable normal subgroup of
`N`, so it is trivial.  Since `G/N=K` is finite, `R` injects into `K` and is
therefore finite.  For `r in R` and `m in N`, normality gives

```text
[m,r] in R intersect N = {e};
```

thus every element of `R` centralizes `N`.

Write `r=(n,h)`.  If `h` moves a coordinate `s` to a distinct coordinate
`hs`, choose a nontrivial element `m` supported only at `s`.  Conjugation by
`r` sends `m` to an element supported only at `hs`: the permutation moves the
support, while conjugation by the base element `n` acts coordinatewise and
cannot change or erase it.  This contradicts centrality.  Thus `h` fixes all
of `S`; faithfulness of the `K`-action gives `h=e`.  Now `r=n` belongs to
`R intersect N`, so `r=e`.  Therefore

```text
Rad_am(W_(S,K))={e}.                                 (3)
```

This is the point at which an arbitrary finite extension would be unsafe:
C*-simplicity is not being assumed to pass through a finite extension.  The
faithful coordinate-permutation action proves (3) directly.

By `vigdorovich-linear-selflessness`, (1)--(3) imply that every packet has
selfless reduced C*-algebra.  The subgroup inclusions in (2) induce
isometric, canonical-trace-preserving inclusions of reduced group algebras.
Applying `stw91-directed-unions-of-selfless-groups` proves that `C*_r(W)` is
selfless and hence simple.

## 4. Separation from the known geometric classes

The group `P` is nonabelian: a nontrivial abelian group is amenable and hence
cannot have trivial amenable radical.  Choose distinct coordinates
`x_1,x_2,...`.  If `P^(X)` embedded in `GL_q(F)` over any field, let `H_i` be
the image of the `x_i`-coordinate copy of `P`, and let `A_n` be the unital
`F`-subalgebra of `M_q(F)` generated by `H_1,...,H_n`.  If
`A_(n+1)=A_n`, then `H_(n+1)` lies in `A_n` and commutes with `A_n`, because
different coordinate groups commute.  Hence `H_(n+1)` lies in the
commutative center of `A_n`, contradicting nonabelianness of `P`.  The
strict chain

```text
A_1 proper A_2 proper ...
```

cannot occur in the `q^2`-dimensional vector space `M_q(F)`.  Thus
`P^(X)`, and therefore `W`, is nonlinear over every field.

Finally take `g=(f,h) in W`.  The union of the support of `f` and the support
of the finitary permutation `h` is finite.  Every coordinate copy of `P`
outside this union commutes with `g`; consequently `C_W(g)` contains a copy
of `P^(Y)` for an infinite set `Y`, and is not virtually cyclic.  A
non-virtually-cyclic acylindrically hyperbolic group has a loxodromic element
whose centralizer is virtually cyclic.  No element of `W` can have that
property, so `W` is not acylindrically hyperbolic.

**Trust boundary.**  The selflessness inputs are precisely Vigdorovich's
linear theorem and Robert's directed-union permanence through the two named
prerequisites.  The packet exhaustion, linear representation, amenable
radical computation, nonlinearity, and centralizer calculation are proved
above.  The only geometric input is the standard virtually-cyclic
centralizer theorem for loxodromic elements of acylindrical actions.
