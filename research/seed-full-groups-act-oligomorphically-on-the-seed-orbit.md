## Setting

These are the SEED objects of the v6 synthesis.
- `Γ` is finitely generated by `S`, and `Y ⊆ A^Γ` is an SFT.
- `y_* ∈ Y` is a **seed**:
  - (S1) `y_*` is isolated in `Y`;
  - (S2) `Stab_Γ(y_*) = 1`;
  - (S3) `O = Γ·y_*` is dense in `Y`.
- `F = [[Γ ⋉ Y]]` is the topological full group: homeomorphisms `h` of `Y` with a locally constant
  `c: Y → Γ` such that `h(y) = c(y)·y`. It contains `Γ` through constant cocycles.

## Statement

1. **FSym.** `F ⊇ FSym(O)`, the finitary permutations of `O` extended by the identity on
   `Y ∖ O`.
2. **The action.** `F ↷ O` is faithful and highly transitive, so it has finitely many orbits on
   `k`-subsets for every `k`.
3. **Orbit problem ≥ two-cell patterns.** Fix `s ∈ S` and letters `a ≠ b`. Let
   `P_(ab) = [y(1) = a, y(s) = b]`, and let `E_s(y_*)` be the set of triples `(g, a, b)`, with `g`
   a word, such that `g^(-1)·y_* ∈ P_(ab) ∪ s·P_(ab)`. Then `E_s(y_*)` reduces to the orbit problem
   `OP(F, O)`, with words of length `2|g| + O(1)`.

## Corollary: the SEED route to hard actors

Suppose also that `F` is finitely presented and `Stab_F(y_*)` is finitely generated. All point
stabilizers are then conjugate and finitely generated, so `F ↷ O` is of type (A). Hence:
- `SV_F` is finitely presented (`twisted-brin-thompson-finite-presentation-criterion`) and simple;
- its word problem computes `OP(F, O)` (`twisted-brin-thompson-wp-equals-actor-orbit-problem`),
  and so, by item 3, computes `E_s(y_*)`.

**Where hardness can come from.** It can come from the seed configuration even when `Γ` has an
easy word problem. It always includes `Γ`'s: by (S1) and (S2), `g = 1` iff `g^(-1)·y_*` lies in
the seed cylinder.

## Proof

**(1).** A point of `O` is isolated, so its singleton is clopen. A transposition of
`o_1 = g_1·y_*` and `o_2 = g_2·y_*` is continuous. Its cocycle takes the value `g_2 g_1^(-1)` on
`{o_1}`, `g_1 g_2^(-1)` on `{o_2}`, and `1` elsewhere, so it is locally constant.

**(2).** If `f ∈ F` fixes `O` pointwise, it fixes the closure `Y` by (S3), so `f = 1`. `FSym(O)`
is highly transitive on the infinite set `O`, and so is `F`.

**(3).** Since `a ≠ b`, the cylinders `P = P_(ab)` and `sP` are disjoint:
- a point of `P ∩ sP` has `y(s) = b`;
- lying in `sP` means `s^(-1)y ∈ P`, which forces `y(s) = a`.

Let `ρ ∈ F` swap `P` and `sP` by `s^(±1)` and fix the rest. For a word `g ∈ Γ ⊆ F`, the element
`gρg^(-1)` has length `2|g| + O(1)` in any generating set of `F` containing `S` and `ρ`. It fixes
`y_*` iff `ρ` fixes `g^(-1)y_*`, iff `g^(-1)y_* ∉ P ∪ sP`, because `ρ` moves every point of
`P ∪ sP`. So membership of `gρg^(-1)` in `Stab_F(y_*)` decides whether `(g, a, b) ∈ E_s(y_*)`. ∎

## Calibration and what is left

- **Γ = Z gives nothing hard.** In a `Z`-SFT, an isolated point has eventually periodic tails. The
  proof is pumping: a repeated window state beyond the central word lets one cut or repeat a
  segment without leaving the cylinder. So `E_s` is regular.
  - This is why the half-finite route (Attempt 6 of `type-a-actors-with-arbitrarily-hard-word-problem`)
    loses its eventually periodic generators.
  - Example: `C_Z`, whose full group is Houghton's `H_2`, not finitely presented.
- **Dimension >= 2 or non-amenable Γ.** A seed can force a non-periodic, computing configuration.
  Example: a start row plus a deterministic machine above it. Then the difficulty moves entirely
  to (S3) and to finite presentation of `F`:
  - (S3) needs `Y` to be exactly the orbit closure, i.e. the SFT must forbid every pattern that
    the seeded computation never shows;
  - finite presentation of `F` is P2′-type rigidity, as in the master route.

## Lesson for general BH

**Isolation buys oligomorphy.** A dense orbit of isolated points puts all its finitary
permutations into the full group, so the Houghton mechanism, not the clopen one, supplies the
type (A) pair-orbit condition for free. The orbit problem is then the evaluation problem of the
seed configuration.

**Consequence for the hard-actor claim.** The hard-actor claim, and with it the root
`fp-simple-groups-with-arbitrarily-complex-word-problem`, reduces to the SEED gates. We need seeds
whose forced configuration is hard to evaluate, and whose full group is finitely presented with
one finitely generated stabilizer.

**Consequence for CAP.** Here the seed's configuration, not `Γ`, carries the hardness. So a CAP
statement that bounds only the word problems of `Γ ∈ 𝒞` does not bound these actors. The relevant
cap is on the evaluation complexity of rigid seeds.

## Referee (bh-ref-misc, 2026-09-18): PASS for items 1–3 and the Corollary; the title and Lesson overstate item 3

Internal referee lane, not an external review.

**Checked.**
- **Item 1.** Orbit points are isolated, so finitary permutations have locally constant cocycles.
- **Item 2.** Faithfulness holds by density (S3), and high transitivity comes from `FSym(O)`.
- **Item 3.** `P_(ab) ∩ sP_(ab) = ∅` for `a ≠ b`, and `ρ` moves every point of `P ∪ sP`. So
  `gρg^(-1) ∈ Stab(y_*)` iff `g^(-1)y_* ∉ P ∪ sP`.
- **The Corollary.** A 2-transitive faithful action of a finitely presented group with one finitely
  generated stabilizer is of type (A).

**Gaps.**
1. **What item 3 reduces.** It reduces the disjunctive set `E_s(y_*)`:
   `[y_*(g) = a ∧ y_*(gs) = b] ∨ [y_*(gs) = a ∧ y_*(gs²) = b]`. It does not reduce the evaluation
   `g ↦ y_*(g)`.
   - The title's "computes the two-cell patterns along words" and the Lesson's "the orbit problem is
     the evaluation problem of the seed configuration" say more than is proved.
   - Along one `s`-line, the alternating patterns `…abab…` and `…baba…` give the same `E_s` data, so
     `E_s` alone does not fix the phase.
   - Decoding `y_*` from `E_s` together with the known seed values is plausible, but it is not given.
2. **Finite generation of `F`.** For item 3 to be a reduction on words, `F` must be finitely
   generated, or the reduction must be read in `⟨S, ρ⟩`. The Corollary assumes `F` is finitely
   presented, so it is not affected.
