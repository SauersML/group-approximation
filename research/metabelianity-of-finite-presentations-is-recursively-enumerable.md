**OPEN.** `METABELIAN_fp` is recursively enumerable. Adian--Rabin already gives
`Sigma^0_1`-hardness: the trivial group is metabelian and `F_2` embeds in no
metabelian group. So the claim is equivalent to `METABELIAN_fp` being
`Sigma^0_1`-complete, strictly below the `Pi^0_2`-completeness that holds on
recursive presentations.

**Why it is plausible.**
- **Finitely presented in the variety.** Every finitely generated metabelian group
  satisfies max-n (P. Hall), so it is finitely presented *within the variety*.
  By the Bieri--Strebel theorem, as reported in the literature this lane read
  (the primary source was not re-read), it is finitely presented *as a group*
  exactly when its module is tame. That is a condition with a finite cover of
  the character sphere.
- **Metabelian laws propagate.** On finite presentations the metabelian law has
  to follow from finitely many relators. The Baumslag--Remeslennikov lemma is
  the prototype certificate.

**What would suffice.** Every finitely presented metabelian group is the quotient,
by finitely many extra relators, of a finite presentation carrying a finite
metabelianity certificate. Quotients of metabelian groups are metabelian, and the
Tietze search is `Sigma^0_1`.

**Why this lane records it.** If the claim holds, then by the certificate screen
(attempt 1 of `amenability-of-finite-presentations-is-pi1-hard`) no computable
family can have
- a metabelian positive branch,
- a non-amenable negative branch, and
- a non-`Sigma^0_1` amenable locus.

In particular, a compiler that keeps the total branch inside Baumslag--Remeslennikov
envelopes of the lamplighter group (`lamplighter-fp-amenable-interface-is-directional`)
cannot prove `amenability-of-finite-presentations-is-pi2-complete`.

The promise version would *not* follow. That is Cannonito's question (M3) in
Baumslag--Myasnikov--Shpilrain, *Open problems in combinatorial group theory*,
2nd ed., read from the PDF 2026-09-12: "Is there an algorithm which decides
whether or not a given finitely presented solvable group is metabelian?"

## Attempts

1. **Certificate classes already available.**
   - *Baumslag--Remeslennikov lemma* (Kharlampovich--Myasnikov--Sapir,
     arXiv:1204.6506v5, Lemma 4.1, verbatim). "Suppose that a group H is
     generated by three sets X, F = {a_i | i = 1, ..., m}, F' = {a'_i | i = 1,
     ..., m} such that (1) The subgroup generated by F ∪ F' is Abelian; (2) For
     every a ∈ F and every x ∈ X we have x^{f(a)} = x^{a'} for some monic
     polynomial f of a which has at least two terms (in all our applications
     f(t) = t−1); (3) [x_1^{a_1^{α_1}...a_m^{α_m}}, x_2] = 1, for every x_1, x_2 ∈
     X, and every α_1, ..., α_m ∈ {0, 1, −1}. Then the normal subgroup generated
     by X in the group H = ⟨X ∪ F ∪ F'⟩ is Abelian, and H is metabelian." All
     hypotheses are finitely many relations.
   - *Honest polycyclic presentations*, which cover polycyclic metabelian groups
     (`polycyclic-type-classes-of-fp-groups-are-sigma1-complete`).
   - *Ascending HNN extensions of finitely generated abelian groups*. The relators
     `[b_i,b_j]` and `t^-1 b_i t = w_i(b)` certify an ascending union of abelian
     conjugates.

   A proof needs one certificate family, closed under finite quotients, that
   covers every tame module: a tame propagation lemma generalizing (1)--(3) over a
   finite cover of the character sphere. *Open*.
2. **Metabelianization.**
   - *Setup.* The metabelianization `M = F / ⟨⟨R⟩⟩F''` is computable with solvable
     word problem, and `P` is metabelian iff `P -> M` is injective, i.e. iff
     `F'' ⊆ ⟨⟨R⟩⟩`. That condition is `Pi^0_2`.
   - *The partial step.* If `M` is finitely presented, finitely many elements of
     `F''` normally generate `F''` modulo `⟨⟨R⟩⟩`.
   - *Where it dies.* Certifying that killing them yields a metabelian group is
     the original problem. *Circular.*
3. **Baumslag--Cannonito--Miller stabilization** (Math. Z. 178 (1981), proof of
   Theorem 3.1, section 3.3, read from the PDF).
   - *What it does.* It detects when groups presented *in the variety* agree,
     using their solvable word problems.
   - *Where it dies.* An ordinary finite presentation carries no metabelian law,
     so the stabilization has nothing to start from. *Dies as stated.*
