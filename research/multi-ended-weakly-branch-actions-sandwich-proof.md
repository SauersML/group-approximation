This adapts Matte Bon–Nekrashevych–Zheng (MNZ), *Commensurating actions and self-similar groups*, arXiv:2607.13776. TeX labels `l-commensurating-branch`, `t-commensurating-product-discrete`, `l-reduction-transitive` and `l-double-comm` were read at source. MNZ prove the statement for branch groups. Their proof uses branchness twice:
- to see that `rist(n)` acts with infinite image;
- through Lemma `l-quotient-rist`, to rule out virtually abelian images.

Both uses are replaced below by faithfulness and by fact (F1). That is what lets the argument run for weakly branch groups satisfying (R).

## Imported (read at source)

- **(I1) Reduction lemma (MNZ `l-reduction-transitive`).** Let `G` act transitively on `X` with a commensurated subset `A` that is not transfixed. Let `N ⊴ G` be finitely generated with infinite image. Then `N` has finitely many orbits on `X`, and for every `N`-orbit `Y` both `Y ∩ A` and `Y \ A` are infinite.
- **(I2) Product theorem (MNZ `t-commensurating-product-discrete`).** Let `G_1, G_2` be finitely generated, and let `G_1 × G_2` act transitively on `Y` with Schreier graph having at least two ends. Then either the image of `G_1` or of `G_2` is finite, or the image of `G_1 × G_2` is virtually abelian.
- **(I3) Double commutator lemma (MNZ `l-double-comm`, after Grigorchuk).** Let `L ≤ Aut(T)`, `g ∈ L` and `g(u) ≠ u`. Then the normal closure of `g` in `L` contains `rist_L(u)'`.
- **(I4) Ends versus commensurated sets (MNZ §2).** A transitive `G`-set of a finitely generated `G` has a commensurated, non-transfixed subset if and only if its Schreier graph has at least two ends. The degrees of `T` are assumed `≥ 2`, as usual.

## (F1) Rigid stabilizers are infinite and not virtually abelian

- **`rist(y)' ≠ 1` for every vertex `y`.** Pick `g ∈ rist(y)` moving some `z ∈ T_y`, and `1 ≠ h ∈ rist(z)`. Then `g h g^{-1}` is supported in `T_{g z}`, which is disjoint from `T_z`, so `[g,h] ≠ 1`. Both `g` and `h` lie in `rist(y)`.
- **`rist(u)'` is non-abelian.**
  - Take `1 ≠ c ∈ rist(u)'`; it moves some `w ∈ T_u`.
  - Take `1 ≠ d ∈ rist(w)' ≤ rist(u)'`.
  - Then `c d c^{-1}` is supported in `T_{cw}`, disjoint from `T_w`, so `[c,d] ≠ 1`.
- **`rist(v)` is infinite.** It contains the direct product of the nontrivial groups `rist(w)` over the descendants `w` of `v` at every depth.
- **`rist(v)` is not virtually abelian.**
  - Suppose `A ⊴ rist(v)` is abelian of finite index. Then `A ≠ 1`, so some `a ∈ A` moves some `u ∈ T_v`.
  - Apply (I3) with `L = rist(v)`. Since `rist_L(u) = rist_G(u)` for `u ∈ T_v`, this gives `rist_G(u)' ≤ A`, contradicting the previous step.
- **Consequence.** No `rist(n)`, and no group containing some `rist(v)`, is virtually abelian.

## Proof

Let `X = G/H`, `x = eH`, and let `A ⊂ X` be commensurated and not transfixed, which exists by (I4). Fix `n ≥ 1`.

**Step 1.** `rist(n)` is normal in `G`, because `g rist(v) g^{-1} = rist(gv)`, and it is finitely generated by (R). Since `G` acts faithfully, `rist(n)` embeds in `Sym(X)`, and it is infinite by (F1). So (I1) applies with `N = rist(n)`:
- there are finitely many `rist(n)`-orbits;
- each orbit `Y` is infinite;
- `A ∩ Y` is commensurated by `rist(n)`, infinite and co-infinite in `Y`.

So each Schreier graph of `rist(n)` on an orbit `Y` has at least two ends. Each factor `rist(v)` is finitely generated, being a quotient (direct factor) of `rist(n)`.

**Step 2 (no virtually abelian image).** Suppose the image of `rist(n)` on one orbit `Y` were virtually abelian.
- `G` permutes the `rist(n)`-orbits transitively, since `rist(n)` is normal and `G` is transitive on `X`.
- The image on `gY` is conjugate to the image on `Y`, so every image is virtually abelian.
- Faithfulness on `X` embeds `rist(n)` in the finite product of these images, so `rist(n)` would be virtually abelian, contradicting (F1).

**Step 3 (one active vertex per orbit).** Fix an orbit `Y` and `v` with `|v| = n`, and write `rist(n) = rist(v) × R_v` with `R_v = ⊕_{w≠v} rist(w)`. By (I2) and Step 2, either `rist(v)` or `R_v` has finite image on `Y`.
- Not every `rist(v)` can have finite image: then `rist(n)` would have finite image on `Y`, so `Y` would be finite, contradicting Step 1.
- If `rist(v)` has infinite image, then `R_v` has finite image, so every other `rist(w)` has finite image.

So there is exactly one vertex `v_n(Y)` at level `n` whose rigid stabilizer acts on `Y` with infinite image.

**Step 4 (the active vertices form a ray).** Let `Y_n` be the `rist(n)`-orbit of `x`. Since `rist(n+1) ≤ rist(n)`, we have `Y_{n+1} ⊆ Y_n`. Suppose `v_{n+1} := v_{n+1}(Y_{n+1})` were not below `v_n := v_n(Y_n)`.
- Then `v_{n+1}` lies below some `w ≠ v_n` at level `n`, and `rist(v_{n+1}) ≤ rist(w)`.
- `rist(w)` has finite image on `Y_n`.
- The action on the invariant subset `Y_{n+1}` is a quotient of the action on `Y_n`, so `rist(v_{n+1})` has finite image on `Y_{n+1}`. That is a contradiction.

So `(v_n)` is a ray `ξ_x ∈ ∂T`.

**Step 5 (equivariance).** For `g ∈ G`:
- the `rist(n)`-orbit of `gx` is `gY_n`;
- `rist(gv) = g rist(v) g^{-1}` acts on `gY_n` as `rist(v)` acts on `Y_n`.

So `v_n(gY_n) = g v_n(Y_n)`, hence `ξ_{gx} = g ξ_x`, and `H = G_x ≤ G_{ξ_x}`.

**Step 6 (the lower bound).**
- For `v ∈ N(ξ_x)` with `|v| = n`, we have `v ≠ v_n`. So `K_v := ker(rist(v) → Sym(Y_n))` has finite index in `rist(v)` and fixes `x ∈ Y_n`.
- Groups `rist(v)` for pairwise incomparable `v` commute and intersect trivially.

Hence `⊕_{v∈N(ξ_x)} K_v ≤ G_x = H`, with `ξ = ξ_x`. ∎

## Checks

- **Branch case.** For branch `G` this is MNZ's `l-commensurating-branch` verbatim. The only change there is that Step 2 replaces their appeal to `l-quotient-rist`.
- **Where (R) is used.** Only in Step 1, for (I1), and for the finite generation that (I2) needs. Without (R), the argument would need a version of (I1) for normal subgroups that are infinitely generated.
