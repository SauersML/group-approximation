title: Direct proof that SL_3(Z) is a non-simple Kazhdan marked limit of simple SL_3(F_p)
target: finite-simple-kazhdan-limits-need-not-be-simple
requires: []
---

1. **(T) and generation.** `SL_3(Z)` has property (T) (Kazhdan 1967) and is generated by the elementary matrices
   `e_ij(1)`.
2. **The finite groups are simple.** For a prime `p`, the centre of `SL_3(F_p)` is `{cI : c^3 = 1}`, which is trivial
   exactly when `3 ∤ p−1`. So for `p ≡ 2 mod 3`, `SL_3(F_p) = PSL_3(F_p)` is simple. By Dirichlet there are infinitely
   many such primes.
3. **Convergence.** A word `w` in the elementary generators has integer entries. If `w ≠ I` in `SL_3(Z)`, some entry of
   `w − I` is a nonzero integer, and it stays nonzero mod `p` for all large `p`. If `w = I`, it is trivial mod every `p`.
   There are finitely many words of length `≤ r`, so for large `p` the reduction map is injective on the `r`-ball and a
   word of length `≤ 2r` is trivial in `SL_3(F_p)` iff it is trivial in `SL_3(Z)`. So the marked groups converge.
4. **Not simple.** The kernel of `SL_3(Z) → SL_3(Z/2)` is a normal subgroup. It is proper, since the map is onto, and
   nontrivial, since it contains `e_12(2)`. ∎
