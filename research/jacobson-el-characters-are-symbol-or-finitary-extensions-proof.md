## Step 0. Conventions and imported facts

* `J`, `Q = 1 - ST`, `n >= 2`, `E = EL_n(J) <= GL(V)` and `L = GL_fin(N x {1..n}, F_2)` are as in
  `leavitt-corner-trace-gate-collapses-to-jacobson-hyperlinearity`. Its proof
  (`leavitt-corner-trace-gate-jacobson-proof`, Step 2) establishes the following.
  - (S1) `L <= E`.
  - (S2) `L` is simple and perfect.
  - (S3) `C_E(L) = 1`.
  - (S4) For `g in GL_n(J)`, if `g - 1` has finite rank on `V` then `g in L`.
* It also gives the decomposition used there: every `a in J` is `f + p`, with `f` a finite `F_2`-combination of
  matrix units `e_ij = S^i Q T^j` (`e_ij e_j = e_i`) and `p` a finite `F_2`-combination of `S^i` (`i >= 1`), `1`
  and `T^j` (`j >= 1`).
* `L` is the kernel of the symbol map `p : E -> EL_n(F_2[x,x^(-1)])`, by the symbol sequence `(JFQ1)` of
  `binary-jacobson-elementary-mark-is-finite-quotient-invisible`, as recorded in Step 3 of the same proof. Only
  normality of `L` and the name of `Q = E/L` are used below.
* `E` is countable, being generated by the elementary matrices over the countable ring `J`.
* **Indices.** Write indices of `V` as `alpha = (i,a)`, with *level* `|alpha| = i in N` and column
  `a in {1..n}`. Write `e_alpha` for basis vectors and `E_(alpha beta)` for the matrix unit with
  `E_(alpha beta) e_beta = e_alpha`.
* **Rank.** For an operator `X` on `V`, `rank X = dim X V`.
* **Commutators.** `[g,x] = g^(-1) x^(-1) g x`, the DLV convention.
* **Characters.** `Tr`, `Ch`, `e psi`, "locally inner" and "inducing" are as in
  `dlv-locally-inner-inducing-character-classification` (items 1--7 there).

## Step 1. Band structure

**Lemma 1.** For every `g in M_n(J)` there is `R = R(g) >= 0` with `<e*_alpha, g e_beta> = 0` whenever
`| |alpha| - |beta| | > R`.

*Proof.*
* **Scalars.** Let `a = f + p in J` as in Step 0.
  - `S^i` shifts levels by `+i`, `T^j` by `-j` (or kills the vector), and `1` fixes them. So `p` changes levels
    by at most `r`, the largest exponent occurring in `p`.
  - `f` is a finite combination of `e_ij`, whose only nonzero entry is at `(i,j)`. So the entries of `f` lie in
    a box `[0,r']^2`.
  - Hence every nonzero entry of `a` sits at levels differing by at most `max(r,r')`.
* **Matrices.** For `g in M_n(J)`, the `(alpha,beta)` entry with `alpha = (i,a)`, `beta = (j,b)` is the
  `(i,j)` entry of the scalar `g_ab in J`. Take `R` to be the maximum of the `n^2` scalar bounds. ∎

## Step 2. L is locally inner in E

**Lemma 2.** For every `g in E` and every finitely generated `F = <x_1,...,x_r> <= L` there is `h in L` with
`h x_j h^(-1) = g x_j g^(-1)` for `j = 1..r`.

*Proof.*
* **Notation.** For a set `B` of indices, put `U_B = span{e_alpha : alpha in B}` and `C_B = U_(complement of B)`.
  Then `V = U_B ⊕ C_B`.
* **A block for `F`.** Each `x_j - 1` has finitely many nonzero entries. Let `A_0` be the finite set of all row and
  column indices of these entries, over all `j`. Then each `x_j` preserves `U_0 := U_(A_0)` and is the identity on
  `C_0 := C_(A_0)`.
* **The target elements.** Put `y_j = g x_j g^(-1)`. Then `y_j - 1 = g(x_j - 1)g^(-1)` has finite rank, so
  `y_j in L` by (S4). Also `y_j` is the identity on `g C_0` and preserves `g U_0`.
* **A block for everything.** Choose a finite index set `B ⊇ A_0` such that
  - `g U_0 ⊆ U_B` (possible because `U_0` is finite-dimensional and `V` consists of finitely supported vectors),
    and
  - every `y_j` preserves `U_B` and is the identity on `C_B` (add the row and column indices of `y_j - 1`).
* **Modular law.** `U_B = g U_0 ⊕ (U_B ∩ g C_0)`.
  - Let `u in U_B`. Since `V = g U_0 ⊕ g C_0`, write `u = a + c` with `a in g U_0` and `c in g C_0`.
  - Then `c = u - a in U_B`, because `a in g U_0 ⊆ U_B`.
  - So `dim(U_B ∩ g C_0) = |B| - |A_0| = dim U_(B \ A_0)`.
* **The implementing element.** Define `h in GL(V)` on the three pieces of `V = U_0 ⊕ U_(B \ A_0) ⊕ C_B`:
  - `h = g` on `U_0`, mapping it onto `g U_0`;
  - `h` is any linear isomorphism from `U_(B \ A_0)` onto `U_B ∩ g C_0`;
  - `h = id` on `C_B`.

  By the modular law `h` maps `U_B` bijectively onto `U_B` and is the identity on `C_B`. So `h - 1` is finitary
  and `h in L`.
* **Check** `h x_j h^(-1) = y_j` on the decomposition `V = g U_0 ⊕ (U_B ∩ g C_0) ⊕ C_B`:
  - On `g U_0 = h U_0`, both sides equal `g x_j g^(-1)`.
  - On `U_B ∩ g C_0 = h U_(B \ A_0)`: `h^(-1)` maps it into `U_(B \ A_0) ⊆ C_0`, where `x_j = id`. So the left side
    is the identity. The right side is the identity because the space lies in `g C_0`.
  - On `C_B`: `h = id` and `x_j = id`, since `C_B ⊆ C_0`. So the left side is the identity, and so is `y_j`,
    by the choice of `B`. ∎

**Matching DLV Definition 2.7.**
* The automorphism `n -> g n g^(-1)` of `L` agrees with the inner automorphism `Ad h` of `L` on `F`, with `h in L`.
  Since `g` ranges over the group `E`, the convention `n^g = g^(-1) n g` gives the same condition, applied to
  `g^(-1)`.
* `L` is normal in `E`, being a kernel. Hence `L` is a locally inner subgroup of `E`.

## Step 3. Commutators with finitary involutions have unbounded rank

**Lemma 3.** Let `g in E \ L` and `k >= 1`. There is an involution `x in L` with `rank([g,x] - 1) >= k`.

*Proof.*
* **Many moved indices.** By (S4), `g - 1` has infinite rank.
  - Let `P = {alpha : g e_alpha != e_alpha}`. If `P` were finite, `g - 1` would vanish on `C_P` and have rank at
    most `|P|`.
  - So `P` is infinite, and since there are only `n` columns, the levels of elements of `P` are unbounded.
* **Choice of indices.** Let `R = R(g)` be as in Lemma 1.
  - Choose `alpha_1,...,alpha_k in P` whose levels are pairwise more than `2R` apart.
  - Choose indices `beta_1,...,beta_k` whose levels are pairwise more than `R` apart and distinct from all the
    `alpha_i`.
* **The involution.** Put `x = 1 + X` with `X = sum_i E_(alpha_i beta_i)`.
  - `X^2 = sum_(i,j) E_(alpha_i beta_i) E_(alpha_j beta_j) = 0`, because `beta_i != alpha_j` for all `i,j`.
  - So `x^2 = 1 + 2X = 1` over `F_2`, and `x in L`.
* **The key vectors.** Put `D = g x - x g = g X - X g`. For each `j`:

```text
D e_(beta_j) = g e_(alpha_j) - sum_i <e*_(beta_i), g e_(beta_j)> e_(alpha_i) = g e_(alpha_j) - c_j e_(alpha_j),
```

  with `c_j = <e*_(beta_j), g e_(beta_j)> in F_2`.
  - The terms with `i != j` vanish by Lemma 1, since the levels of `beta_i` and `beta_j` differ by more than `R`.
  - If `c_j = 1` the vector is `(g - 1) e_(alpha_j)`, which is nonzero because `alpha_j in P`.
  - If `c_j = 0` it is `g e_(alpha_j)`, which is nonzero because `g` is invertible.
* **Rank bound.** By Lemma 1, `D e_(beta_j)` is supported on indices whose levels are within `R` of `|alpha_j|`.
  - These level windows are pairwise disjoint.
  - So the `k` nonzero vectors `D e_(beta_j)` are linearly independent, and `rank D >= k`.
  - Finally `[g,x] - 1 = g^(-1) x^(-1) (g x - x g) = g^(-1) x^(-1) D`, and `g^(-1) x^(-1)` is invertible, so
    `rank([g,x] - 1) = rank D >= k`. ∎

Since `L` is normal, `[g,x] = (g^(-1) x^(-1) g) x in L`.

## Step 4. L is inducing in E

**Lemma 4.** Let `phi in Ch(E)` with `phi|_L` not identically `1`. Then `phi(g) = 0` for every `g in E \ L`.

*Proof.*
* **Restriction is a nontrivial character.** `E` is countable and `L` is locally inner (Step 2). By DLV
  Proposition 6.1(2), `psi := phi|_L in Ch(L)`, and `psi != 1_L` by hypothesis.
* **Identification with `SL_fin(F_2)`.** `L` consists of the invertible finitary matrices over the countably
  infinite index set `N x {1..n}`: matrices whose entries differ from `delta_(alpha,beta)` for finitely many pairs.
  - Over `F_2` every invertible matrix has determinant `1`.
  - So `L = SL_fin(F_2)` in the sense of DLV Section 8, and `rank(w - I)` is `rank(w - 1)` on `V`.
  - By DLV Proposition 8.5 with `F = F_2`, `q = 2`: for every `eps > 0` there is `M` with `|psi(w)| <= eps`
    whenever `w in L` and `rank(w - 1) >= M`.
* **The sequence.** Fix `g in E \ L`. By Lemma 3 choose `x_k in L` with `y_k := [g,x_k] in L` and
  `rank(y_k - 1) >= k`. For fixed `m` put `r_m = rank(y_m - 1) < ∞`. Then

```text
rank(y_k^(-1) y_m - 1) = rank(y_m - y_k) >= rank(y_k - 1) - rank(y_m - 1) >= k - r_m,
```

  using that `y_k^(-1)` is invertible, and subadditivity of rank for `y_k - 1 = (y_m - 1) - (y_m - y_k)`.
* **Conclusion.** Hence `phi([g,x_k]^(-1)[g,x_m]) = psi(y_k^(-1) y_m) -> 0` as `k -> ∞`, for every `m`.
  DLV Lemma 5.10 gives `phi(g) = 0`. ∎

So `L` is an inducing subgroup of `E` in the sense of DLV Definition 6.2, with `N ∩ ker phi ⪇ N` meaning
exactly that `phi|_L` is not identically `1`.

## Step 5. Theorem A

Items 1 and 2 are Lemmas 2 and 4. For item 3, apply DLV Theorem 6.4 to the countable group `E` and its
locally inner inducing subgroup `L`, with `Q = E/L`. Its proof, quoted in the citation route, states:
* `f_1 : Ch(Q) -> Ch(E)`, `sigma -> sigma o p`, and `f_2 : Ch(L) \ {1_L} -> Ch(E)`, `psi -> e psi`, are well
  defined and injective;
* `Im(f_1) ∩ Im(f_2) = ∅`;
* `Ch(E) = Im(f_1) ∪ Im(f_2)`.

This is (A1)/(A2) together with "exactly one" and "every such function is a character". The characters in
`Im(f_1)` are identically `1` on `L`. Those in `Im(f_2)` restrict to `psi != 1_L` and vanish off `L`.

On `ICC`: `e 1_L = delta^Q_e o p`, which lies in `Im(f_1)` exactly when `delta^Q_e in Ch(Q)`, i.e. when `Q` is
ICC. It is excluded from (A2) in any case. ∎

## Step 6. Corollary B

Let `tau in Tr(E)`, and let `mu` be its Choquet--Thoma measure: a Borel probability measure on `Tr(E)` with
`mu(Ch(E)) = 1` and `tau = ∫ chi dmu(chi)` pointwise (DLV Section 5, quoted).

* **Splitting.** Put `C_1 = {chi in Ch(E) : chi(l) = 1 for all l in L}`, the intersection of `Ch(E)` with a
  closed subset of `Tr(E)`, and `C_2 = Ch(E) \ C_1`. Both are Borel. Put `lambda = mu(C_1)`.
  - By Theorem A, `C_1 = Im(f_1)` and `C_2 = Im(f_2)`.
  - So every `chi in C_2` vanishes off `L` and has `chi|_L in Ch(L) \ {1_L}`.
* **(B2).** For `g notin L`, `tau(g) = ∫_(C_1) chi(g) dmu`, so `|tau(g)| <= lambda`.
* **(B1).** For `m >= 1` and `chi in Tr(E)`, put `s_m(chi) = sup{|chi(w)| : w in L, rank(w - 1) = m}`.
  - `s_m` is a supremum of countably many continuous functions, hence Borel, and `0 <= s_m <= 1`.
  - For `chi in C_2`, `s_m(chi) -> 0` by DLV Proposition 8.5, applied to the nontrivial character `chi|_L` of
    `L = SL_fin(F_2)` (Step 4).
  - For `w in L`, `tau(w) = lambda + ∫_(C_2) chi(w) dmu`. So
    `sup{|tau(w) - lambda| : rank(w - 1) = m} <= ∫_(C_2) s_m dmu -> 0` by dominated convergence.
  - So the limit `(B1)` exists uniformly in `w` and equals `lambda in [0,1]`.
* **Decomposition.**
  - If `lambda > 0`, `tau_1 = lambda^(-1) ∫_(C_1) chi dmu` is a trace of `E` (positivity, normalization and
    conjugation invariance survive pointwise integration) that is identically `1` on `L`.
  - A trace `tau_1` with `tau_1|_L == 1` factors through `Q`. In its GNS triple,
    `||pi(l) xi - xi||^2 = 2 - 2 Re tau_1(l) = 0`. So `tau_1(g l) = <pi(g) pi(l) xi, xi> = tau_1(g)`.
    Hence `tau_1 = sigma o p` with `sigma in Tr(Q)`, since positivity, normalization and invariance descend
    along the surjection `p`.
  - If `lambda < 1`, `tau_2 = (1 - lambda)^(-1) ∫_(C_2) chi dmu` is a trace of `E` vanishing off `L`. So
    `tau_2 = e psi` with `psi = tau_2|_L in Tr(L)`.
  - In the degenerate cases `lambda in {0,1}`, take the missing component to be any trace.
  - Then `tau = lambda (sigma o p) + (1 - lambda) e psi`. ∎

## Step 7. Corollary C

* **CE characters.** A CE character is a character, so Theorem A applies. By definition of `S_CE`, a CE character
  outside (A1) is `e psi` with `psi in S_CE`.
* **Microstates.** Its microstates satisfy `|tr U_g - (e psi)(g)| < eps` on the chosen finite sets, and
  `(e psi)(g) = 0` off `L`. This refines alternative (b) of `(JH1)`, which only asserted a trivial scalar kernel.
* **`L` is ICC.** Let `1 != w in L` and `W = (w - 1)V`, of dimension `r` with `1 <= r < ∞`. Fix `0 != v in W`.
  - For every index `alpha` there is `h_alpha in L` with `h_alpha v = e_alpha`, because `GL(U_B)` acts
    transitively on the nonzero vectors of `U_B` for any finite `B` containing `alpha` and the support of `v`.
  - Then `h_alpha w h_alpha^(-1) - 1 = h_alpha (w - 1) h_alpha^(-1)` has image `h_alpha W`, which contains
    `e_alpha`.
  - An `r`-dimensional subspace contains at most `r` basis vectors. So each subspace occurs as `h_alpha W` for at
    most `r` indices `alpha`, and `w` has infinitely many conjugates.
* **`delta^L_e` is a character.** The DLV criterion quoted in the citation route, with `N = {e}`, says
  `delta_e = e 1_({e})` is a character iff the group is ICC. So `delta^L_e in Ch(L)`, and `delta^L_e != 1_L`
  because `L != 1`. Clearly `e delta^L_e = delta^E_e`.
* **`E` is ICC.** By Theorem A (A2), `delta^E_e = e delta^L_e in Ch(E)`. The same criterion, with `N = {e}`,
  makes `E` ICC.
* **(C1).**
  - *`E` hyperlinear => `delta^L_e in S_CE`.* By (F4) of `leavitt-corner-trace-gate-jacobson-proof` with
    `N = {e}`, `delta^E_e = e delta^L_e` is CE.
  - *`delta^L_e in S_CE` => `S_CE` nonempty.* Trivial.
  - *`S_CE` nonempty => `E` hyperlinear.* Let `psi in S_CE`. Since `psi != 1_L`, there is `w in L` with
    `psi(w) != 1`, and `w != 1` since `psi(1) = 1`. Then `e psi` is a CE character of `E` with
    `(e psi)(w) != 1`. Theorem JH, (3) => (1), gives that `E` is hyperlinear. ∎

## Step 8. Corollary D and Remark R

* **`L ≅ GL(∞,2)`.** Enumerate the index set as `c_1, c_2, ...`. Every element of `L` lies in the corner copy of
  `GL_m(F_2)` on `span{e_(c_1),...,e_(c_m)}` for some `m`. The copies are nested by the corner embeddings of the
  Gorin--Kerov--Vershik definition. So `L` is their inductive limit `GL(∞,2)`.
* **Countability.** `f_2` is a bijection from `Ch(L) \ {1_L}` onto `Ch(E) \ Im(f_1)`, and `Ch(GL(∞,2))` is
  countable by the quoted Thoma--Skudlarek statement.
* **Test functions.** By (C1), `EL_20(J)` is hyperlinear iff `e psi` is CE for some `psi` in this countable set.
* **The Haar characters.** By `(JH2)`, `phi_k` equals `2^(-k rank(g-1))` on `L` and `0` off `L`. So
  `phi_k = e(phi_k|_L)`, as functions.

**Remark R (the GNS algebra of `e psi`).** Let `psi in Ch(L) \ {1_L}`, let `(H, pi, xi)` be the GNS triple of
`e psi`, and put `M = pi(E)''` and `M_0 = pi(L)''`, with trace `tau(x) = <x xi, xi>`.
1. **`M_0` is hyperfinite.** `L` is the increasing union of the finite groups `GL_m(F_2)` (corner copies).
   So `M_0` is the weak closure of an increasing union of finite-dimensional algebras.
2. **`M_0` is infinite-dimensional.** `(M_0, tau)` is the GNS algebra of `psi`, since the traces agree on `L` and
   `tau` is faithful on `M`.
   - `pi|_L` is nontrivial, since `psi != 1_L`. Its kernel is a proper normal subgroup of the simple group `L`,
     so it is trivial.
   - The involutions `1 + E_(alpha_i beta_i)` of Step 3, over infinitely many disjoint pairs, generate an infinite
     elementary abelian 2-subgroup `A <= L`.
   - In a finite-dimensional representation of dimension `d`, commuting unitary involutions diagonalize
     simultaneously with eigenvalues `±1`. So the image of `A` has order at most `2^d`, and the representation is
     not faithful on `A`.
   - If `M_0` were finite-dimensional, it would embed unitally in some `M_d`. Composing gives a faithful
     `d`-dimensional unitary representation of `L`, hence of `A`, which is impossible. So `M_0` is
     infinite-dimensional.
3. **`M_0` is a factor.** `psi` is extreme. By the correspondence between indecomposable characters and finite
   type factor representations (quoted in `dudko-medynets-compressible-action-character-theorems`), `M_0` is a
   finite factor. By items 1--2 and the classical uniqueness of the hyperfinite `II_1` factor, `M_0 ≅ R`.
4. **Crossed product structure.** Choose a section `s : Q -> E` with `s(1) = 1`, and put `u_q = pi(s(q))`.
   - Each `u_q` normalizes `M_0`, since `L` is normal.
   - `u_q u_(q') = pi(l(q,q')) u_(q q')` with `l(q,q') in L`.
   - `M` is generated by `M_0` and the `u_q`.
   - For `q != 1` and `l in L`, `tau(pi(l) u_q) = (e psi)(l s(q)) = 0`, since `l s(q) notin L`. By linearity and
     strong continuity of `tau` on bounded sets (Kaplansky density), `tau(x u_q) = 0` for all `x in M_0`.

   So `M` is a cocycle crossed product of `R` by `Q = EL_n(F_2[x,x^(-1)])`, with twisting unitaries `pi(l(q,q'))`
   in `M_0`. By (F1) of `leavitt-corner-trace-gate-jacobson-proof`, `e psi in S_CE` implies that `(M, tau)`
   embeds trace-preservingly in `R^omega`. Conversely, lifting the unitaries `u_g` from `R^omega` to matrix
   unitaries gives microstates. So Connes embeddability of this one cocycle crossed product is the open step
   recorded in the claim. ∎
