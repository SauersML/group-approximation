Let `B` be a unital star algebra with tracial states but with no
Connes-embeddable tracial state.  Let `K<=G` be finite, let

```text
0!=q=q^*=q^2 in C[K],
```

and suppose there is a unital star homomorphism

```text
Phi:B -> q C[G] q,              Phi(1)=q.              (FVI1)
```

Then every finite-dimensional unitary representation

```text
pi:G -> U(n)                                           (FVI2)
```

annihilates the selected corner:

```text
pi(q)=0.                                               (FVI3)
```

Equivalently, every irreducible `K`-type on which `q` has a nonzero matrix
block is absent from the restriction of every finite-dimensional
representation of `G`.

Consequently `G` cannot be maximally almost periodic.  More locally, no
finite-dimensional representation family whose restrictions separate `K`
can host `(FVI1)`.  In particular no residually finite group can contain such
a model in any nonzero finite-subgroup corner, regardless of whether the
images of the BCS projections are literal double-coset terms or arbitrary
finite Hecke sums.

For signed-Hecke construction this is an exact first-cycle gate.  The local
finite packet and every residually finite Bass--Serre stage have plentiful
finite-dimensional evaluations.  A successful block-escaping chord must
make the chosen `K`-type globally finite-dimensional-invisible at the same
stage where it installs the compatible projection atlas.  Merely adding
noncommuting multiplicity holonomy inside a MAP ambient group cannot work.

This condition is necessary, not sufficient.  Exact finite-dimensional
invisibility has no automatic normalized-HS robustification and does not
construct the overlap identities in `(FVI1)`.
