For any idempotent `e`, write

```text
x(e)=[[1,e],[0,1]],             y(e)=[[1,0],[e,1]].
```

Over characteristic two, direct multiplication gives

```text
w(e)=x(e)y(e)x(e)
    =[[1+e,e],[e,1+e]],         w(e)^2=1.              (1)
```

If `e,f` are orthogonal idempotents, another multiplication gives

```text
w(e)w(f)
 =[[1+e+f,e+f],[e+f,1+e+f]]
 =w(e+f).                                               (2)
```

Apply `(2)` to `e=q_0`, `f=q_1` and use `q_0+q_1=A`.  This proves `(RWF3)`
and shows explicitly that completeness, not only the two matching inverse
rows, is used.

For the finite model, `z,ell` are the two elementary transvections in the
`(1,4)` block over `F_2`.  Equation `(1)` with `e=1` proves `w^2=1`.
Coordinatewise multiplication in `(RWF5)` now gives

```text
W_0W_1=(w^2,w,w)=(1,w,w)=W_A.                          (3)
```

The payload extension is the support construction from
`full-completeness-opposite-menu-has-finite-gl5-fusion`: for each named
base occurrence in the path producing `x_14(s_it_i)`, use the same support
as `C_i`, and use its fixed root-position copy for `D_i`.  All multiplication,
cross-zero, associator, and transport words then hold coordinatewise in the
finite base group.  Hence the displayed `C_i,D_i` are the outputs of those
shared paths, not freely adjoined signs.

Finally `J=(1,z,z)` is nonidentity.  In the left regular representation of
the finite group generated by the cell, every discrepancy is zero and
`||lambda(J)-1||_op=2`.
