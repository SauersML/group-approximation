**ESTABLISHED.** Let `Σ` be a closed orientable surface of genus `g ≥ 3`, let
`G ≤ Mod(Σ)` have finite index, and let `G` act on a set `S` with finitely many
orbits on `S × S`. Equivalently, for `|S| ≥ 2`, there are finitely many orbits of
two-element subsets, which is the pair clause of type (A). No finite generation
of stabilizers is assumed.

(a) **Infinite orbits.** Let `S_∞` be the union of the infinite orbits. If the
    action is faithful, then `S_∞` is nonempty, and `G` acts faithfully on `S_∞`
    with finitely many orbits of pairs.

(b) **Stabilizers.** For every `x ∈ S_∞`, `H = Stab_G(x)` has none of the
    following properties:
    - `H` is finite;
    - `H` is normal in `G`;
    - `H` preserves the isotopy class of some essential multicurve;
    - `H` is virtually infinite cyclic.

    Faithfulness is not needed for (b).

**Why it matters.** A type (A) action is faithful, has a finitely presented
actor and finitely generated stabilizers, and has finitely many orbits of
two-element subsets. Such an action makes the twisted Brin--Thompson group
`SV_G` finitely presented and simple, and every subgroup of `G` embeds in a
finitely presented simple group. See `twisted-brin-thompson-finite-presentation-criterion`
and `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`, both quoting Zaremsky,
arXiv:2405.18354.

So a type (A) action of a finite-index subgroup of `Mod(Σ)` would settle
`closed-surface-mapping-class-groups-satisfy-boone-higman`, through
`boone-higman-type-a-class-closed-under-finite-extensions`. By (a) and (b) the
natural actions all fail:
- on curves, multicurves, or vertices of the curve or pants graph, because
  stabilizers are reducible;
- on cosets of finite subgroups, of normal subgroups, or of cyclic subgroups
  generated by a pseudo-Anosov.

A qualifying action needs finitely generated, infinite-index stabilizers that
fix no multicurve and are not virtually cyclic. Ivanov's trichotomy says a
subgroup is finite, reducible, or contains a pseudo-Anosov. It was not re-read
in this pass, but given it, such a stabilizer contains a pseudo-Anosov.

**Scope.**
- **Self-actions only.** It does not constrain type (A) actors that merely
  contain a finite-index subgroup of `Mod(Σ)`. BFFHZ's actor for `Aut(F_n)` is
  of that kind.
- **Not excluded.** Irreducible stabilizers that are not virtually cyclic, for
  example convex cocompact or Veech-type subgroups, or subgroups of larger rank.
- **Finite generation is untouched.** Part (b) never uses it, and the pair
  clause alone is satisfiable. A faithful highly transitive action has one
  orbit of two-element subsets. Hull--Osin produce such actions for countable
  acylindrically hyperbolic groups with no nontrivial finite normal subgroup,
  which covers `Mod(Σ)` for `g ≥ 3`; this was not re-read in this pass. So any
  obstruction to a type (A) self-action must use finite generation of the
  stabilizers.

The derivation is `closed-mcg-pair-orbit-stabilizer-proof`.
