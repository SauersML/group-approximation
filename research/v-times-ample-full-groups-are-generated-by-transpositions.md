Let `G_V` be the groupoid of germs of Thompson's group `V` acting on
`C = {0,1}^N`. Let `H` be an ample Hausdorff groupoid whose unit space `Q` is
compact and totally disconnected. Put `T = G_V × H`, on the unit space
`Ω = C × Q`. Write `F(T)` for the topological full group. `S(T)` and
`A(T) = A_3(T)` are Nekrashevych's symmetric and alternating full groups
(`nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups`), generated respectively
by the transpositions of degree-2 multisections and the 3-cycles of degree-3
multisections. Nekrashevych states these definitions for groupoids with Cantor
unit space; they make sense verbatim for any ample groupoid, and `C × Q` is a
Cantor set whenever `Q` is nonempty and metrizable, as in every application
below.

**Theorem.** `F(T) = S(T) = A(T)`, and this group is perfect.

No minimality is assumed, so the theorem applies to every clopen restriction
`(G_V × H)|_{C×P} = G_V × H|_P`. The case this was written for is
`H = (Z ⋉ Σ)|_P` with `Σ = {0,1}^Z` and `P ⊆ Σ` clopen. There it shows that every
element of `F(G_V × (Z ⋉ Σ))` which is the identity outside `C × P` lies in the
alternating group of the restriction to `C × P`
(`v-times-shift-restriction-kernels-are-perfect-proof`).

**Mechanism.** For `h ∈ F(T)`, let `h^(a)` be the copy of `h` on `[a] × Q`
through the prefix map `w ↦ aw`. Refining `h` into product pieces
`[u_i] × Q_i -> [v_i] × R_i` gives two elements `ρ`, `ρ'` of `F(T)` with trivial
`H`-part, `ρ: u_i a w ↦ a u_i w` and `ρ': v_i a w ↦ a v_i w`, such that
`ρ' h ρ^{-1} = h^(0) h^(1)`. Elements with trivial `H`-part are locally constant
maps `Q -> V`, and `V` is generated by transpositions, so they lie in `S(T)`.
Hence `[h] = [h^(0)][h^(1)]` in `F(T)/S(T)`. The copies `h^(0)`, `h^(00)`,
`h^(10)`, `h^(1)` are conjugate by elements of `V × id ⊆ S(T)`, so the class
`x = [h^(0)]` satisfies `x = x·x`, hence `x = 1`, and then `[h] = 1`. The
homological content is `[C] = 2[C]` in `H_0(G_V)`, realised at the level of the
group.
