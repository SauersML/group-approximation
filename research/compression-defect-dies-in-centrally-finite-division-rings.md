Call a division ring `D` **centrally finite** if `[D : Z(D)] < oo`.

**Claim.**  If `D` is a centrally finite division ring and
`rho : H -> D^x` is a homomorphism, then `D(H,L) <= ker rho` for every
`L <= H`.

Proof in one move: put `F = Z(D)`, a field, and regard `D` as an `F`-vector
space, finite-dimensional by hypothesis.  Left multiplication
`lambda_d(v) = dv` is `F`-linear because `F` is central, and
`lambda : D^x -> GL_F(D)` is an injective group homomorphism.  Apply
`compression-defect-dies-in-finite-dimensions` to `lambda . rho`, then use
injectivity of `lambda`.

The content is entirely in the observation that a centrally finite division
ring **is** a finite-dimensional representation of its own unit group.  No
new analysis of commutants is needed.

This is the first of the two steps by which the repository's finite-dimensional
theorem reaches division rings, and it is also the step that produces the
contradiction in `linnell-skew-field-can-be-centrally-infinite`: applied
there to the division subring generated by three elements, it would force a
nontrivial group element to be trivial.
