**ESTABLISHED (written proof; not Lean-verified).** Parts (A1), (A2),
(A4) and (B1) are proved in-graph. They use only the elementary halves of
Connes' theorem, and the route reproves both. The other parts rest on
imports quoted verbatim in the route:
- (A3) uses Theorem 6 of Ozawa (Acta Math. 2004), "(AO) ⇒ solid".
- (B2) uses the factoriality of L(F) from Jolissaint (Ann. Inst. Fourier
  1998).
- (B3) uses Jolissaint's McDuff theorem.

Two points are recalled rather than quoted. The converse "amenable ⇒
(AO)" in (A3) relies on amenable ⇒ C*_λ nuclear. The bi-exactness
sub-item of (C) relies on bi-exact ⇒ (AO). Neither is needed for the
kill.

Let `F` be Thompson's group acting on `[0,1]`, with `M = L(F)`.

**(A) Solidity family ⇔ root.** Each of the following is *equivalent* to
"F is amenable":

1. `M` is solid, i.e. `A' ∩ M` is injective for every diffuse von Neumann
   subalgebra `A ⊆ M` (Ozawa's definition).
2. `M` is strongly solid (weaker version used here): the von Neumann
   algebra generated by the normalizer of any diffuse *abelian* `A ⊆ M` is
   injective.
3. `M` satisfies Ozawa's condition (AO).
4. `F ∈ P` for any group property `P` that is closed under subgroups,
   holds for every amenable group, and is **product-rigid**: `Z × H ∈ P`
   implies `H` amenable. An example is solidity of `L(·)`.

The invariant is the **commuting pair** `Z × F ≤ F`. Pick
`c ∈ F \ {1}` supported in `[0,1/2]`. Then `c` commutes with
`F_{[1/2,1]} ≅ F`, and `A = L(⟨c⟩)` is diffuse. Hence
`A' ∩ M ⊇ L(F_{[1/2,1]}) ≅ L(F)`. Every item in (A) makes that algebra
injective, which forces `F` to be amenable. Conversely, if `F` is
amenable then `M` is injective, and injectivity gives items 1–3. So
none of these properties can *certify* nonamenability. Proving any of
them for `F` is exactly as hard as the root.

**(B) Indecomposability properties are false for F unconditionally.**

1. `F` is **inner amenable**: there is a conjugation-invariant mean on
   `F \ {1}`. It is the limit of uniform measures on
   `{x_0^k g x_0^{-k} : 0 ≤ k < n}`, with `supp g ⊆ [0,1/2]`.
2. `M` has **property Γ**, so it is not full. Asymptotically central
   unitaries are `u_n = exp(2πi Φ(S_n))`, where `S_n` is a normalized sum
   of the disjointly supported commuting conjugates `x_0^k g x_0^{-k}` and
   `Φ` is the Gaussian CDF.
3. `M` is McDuff (Jolissaint, cited), `M ≅ M ⊗̄ R`, so `M` is **not
   prime**.

These properties hold whether or not `F` is amenable.

**(C) Class killed.** Consider any route to the amenability problem for
`F` whose certificate is a von Neumann-algebraic rigidity or
indecomposability property of `L(F)`, or a group property in the classes
above. Bi-exactness / class S also falls in this class, through the
standard implication "bi-exact ⇒ L(G) satisfies (AO)" (Brown–Ozawa,
Ch. 15). That implication is *recalled, not verified verbatim here*,
so only this sub-item is conditional. Every such route dies at one of
two steps:

- it tries to verify a solidity-family property, which by (A) is the
  root restated (the step dies at `L(F_{[1/2,1]}) ⊆ L(⟨c⟩)' ∩ L(F)`);
- it tries to use primeness, fullness / non-Γ, or non-inner-amenability
  as a nonamenability witness, but by (B) these fail for `F` in both
  worlds (the step dies at the `x_0`-conjugate central sequence).

The only von Neumann invariant of `L(F)` that sees the root is
injectivity itself: `L(F) ≅ R` ⇔ `F` amenable.
