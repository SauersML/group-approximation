**ESTABLISHED.**  Put

```text
b=x_12(1),       z=x_23(1),       k=x_31(1),
y=[z,k]=x_21(1),
omega=b y b.                                                (JWS1)
```

Thus `omega` is the constant Weyl involution swapping coordinates `1,2`.
It consumes all three directed roots `b,z,k` of the active constant cycle.
Let

```text
r=x_23(ST)=x_23(1)x_23(Q),
c=x_13(ST)=x_13(1)x_13(Q).
```

Then

```text
omega r omega=c.                                           (JWS2)
```

On the last native residual

```text
f=e_res=e(1-x_13(1))(1-r)/4
```

one has `r=-1` and `c=+1`; the latter follows because both `x_13(1)` and
`x_13(Q)` act as `-1`.  Consequently

```text
f omega f=0.                                               (JWS3)
```

So `omega` changes exactly the missing product sign, but it is an
**orthogonal carrier switch**, not a return inside `f`.

The occurrence semantics are completely literal.  Conjugating the
established degree-two tuple on `e=F_+` gives

```text
(x_12(S^2),0,x_14(SQ),x_43(1))
 |->
(x_21(S^2),0,x_24(SQ),x_43(1)),                           (JWS4)
```

on the transported carrier `omega e omega`.  Conjugating the native target
tuple gives

```text
(x_12(Q),x_23(1),x_14(Q),x_43(1))
 |->
(x_21(Q),x_13(1),x_24(Q),x_43(1)),                        (JWS5)
```

whose center is the conjugate marked root `x_23(Q)`.

Moreover `omega` does not lie in the finite marked envelope
`P=<w,v,d,X,u,C,l>` of
`binary-jacobson-balanced-center-fold-data-is-finite-marked`.  Hence
`(JWS2)--(JWS5)` are the first explicit occurrence and covariance identities
across that exact finite-envelope fence.

This does not yet give the common PI return: `(JWS3)` moves the residual off
itself, and the transported degree-two and degree-four tuples move together.
The new sharply typed continuation is to combine this orthogonal switch with
a second occurrence returning only one of the two packet degrees, or to fold
`f` and `omega f omega` while retaining an asymmetric target covariance.
