The high-girth picture theorem proves that no finite exact van Kampen diagram
collapses the central phase.  It does not bound normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
relator energy in finite-dimensional maps which keep `J` separated.

In particular, Slofstra--Zhang explicitly leave open whether their odd-`p`
incidence examples have finite-dimensional operator solutions.  Therefore
the theorem is useful for protecting an exact marked element while adding
incidence cells, but cannot be substituted for the quantitative return or
payment theorem required by a nonhyperlinearity proof.

## Attempts

- Counting cells in a minimal exact picture has no direct approximate
  analogue: normalized-HS defect may be spread over the ambient dimension,
  and an almost representation need not produce a finite diagram.  A useful
  upgrade would need a stability theorem converting small HS energy into an
  exact phase picture or into a positive curvature density.
