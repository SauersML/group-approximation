Let `f_i=e_(H_i,chi_i)` be finitely many nonzero orthogonal
finite-character projections in `C[G]`, joined by a connected graph
of literal partial transports `v_e=g_e f_s` with
`g_e f_s g_e^-1=f_t`. Choose tree paths `h_i` from a root and put

```text
ell_e=h_t^-1 g_e h_s,
L=<H_0,ell_e>,             Gamma=L/H_0,
p=sum_i f_i.
```

Every return word preserves `(H_0,chi_0)`. The algebra generated by
the vertex projections and partial transports is exactly

```text
M_m(f_0 C[L]f_0) ~= M_m(C_omega[Gamma]),
```

where `m` is the number of vertices, with the normalized canonical
matrix trace. This includes all finite additive expressions in the
associated partial rotations on `p`. If they are extended by the
identity to an augmentation unit `q>=p`, their algebra lies in this
matrix algebra direct-summed with `C(q-p)`. A proposed cycle identity
holds exactly when its matrix coefficients, grouped by `H_0`-cosets
with their character phases, equal the identity coefficients.

For `q=1-e_K`, an individual atom `e_(H,chi)` lies below `q`
exactly when `chi` is nontrivial on `K intersect H`. For an
involution `K=<J>`, this means `J in H` and `chi(J)=-1`.
Under the stated orthogonality and containment, exhaustion of `q`
is equivalent to `m/h=(|K|-1)/|K|`, hence implies `|K|` divides
`h`. Numerical data alone do not construct such atoms.

In a triangle `0->1->2->0`, compare the rotated source projections
built from `a=v_20^*` and `b=v_12 v_01`. They agree exactly when

```text
w=g_20 g_12 g_01 in ker chi_0.
```

If `h=|H_0|`, their squared canonical HS distance is `1/h` when
`w` lies outside `H_0`, and `(1-Re chi_0(w))/h` otherwise.
This is an exact group-algebra statement, not a gap in all matrix
representations.

Closing every fundamental return to a scalar leaves only a finite
matrix algebra. More generally finite `Gamma` gives a finite algebra;
hyperlinear `L` gives a CE canonical trace. Neither can host the fixed
no-CE BCS. If these operations act as scalars on a nonzero leftover
`q-p`, their algebra also has a scalar quotient and cannot host that
BCS. Arbitrary overlapping noncommuting cuts lie outside this theorem.
