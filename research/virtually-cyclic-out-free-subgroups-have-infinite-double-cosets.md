- **Cited inputs, not read at source this session:**
  - Bestvina–Feighn, arXiv:1107.3308: the free factor complex `FF_n` is
    hyperbolic, fully irreducibles act loxodromically, and they satisfy WPD. So
    `Out(F_n)` acts non-elementarily, with two fully irreducibles whose fixed
    pairs in `∂FF_n` are disjoint.
  - Handel–Mosher, arXiv:0908.1255: the subgroup alternative.
  - Bestvina–Feighn–Handel, GAFA 1997: the stabilizer of the attracting
    lamination of a fully irreducible element is virtually cyclic. Used only for
    the corollary.

**Statement.** Let `n ≥ 3` and `H ≤ G = Out(F_n)` virtually cyclic. Then `H\G/H`
is infinite, and so is `L\G/L` for every `L` with `L ∩ H` of finite index in `L`.

**Proof.**
- **H finite.** Trivial, since `G` is infinite.
- **H virtually fixes a proper free factor class.** Then `H` has a finite orbit
  on the vertices of `FF_n`. That is excluded by item 2 of
  `out-free-pair-finite-stabilizers-contain-fully-irreducibles`, whose proof
  uses only that finite orbit.
- **Otherwise.** By Handel–Mosher, `H` contains a fully irreducible `φ`, and
  `⟨φ⟩` has finite index in `H`.
  - Write `H = ⋃_{i≤m} ⟨φ⟩h_i` and fix a vertex `v`. Put `A = H·v`, which is
    `H`-invariant, so `R = 0`.
  - `A` lies within Hausdorff distance `max_i d(h_iv, v)` of `⟨φ⟩·v`, a
    quasi-geodesic line with endpoints `φ^±`.
  - Choose a fully irreducible `χ` whose fixed points `χ^±` avoid `{φ^+, φ^−}`
    (non-elementarity).
  - Then `χ^k A` is a quasi-line with endpoints `χ^kφ^±`, which converge to
    `χ^+` by north–south dynamics. So `χ^k A` eventually lies in every
    neighbourhood of `χ^+`, with uniform quasi-geodesic constants.
  - If `δ(χ^k) = d(A, χ^kA)` stayed bounded along a subsequence, points `b_k` of
    `χ^kA` would stay within bounded distance of points `a_k ∈ A`. Since
    `b_k → χ^+`, the `a_k` would leave every ball. They would then converge to
    `φ^+` or `φ^−`, and so would the `b_k`, contradicting `χ^+ ∉ {φ^±}`. This is
    a standard hyperbolic-geometry step.
  - So `δ` is unbounded, and `escaping-invariant-sets-force-infinitely-many-double-cosets`
    applies.
- **Subgroups L.** Handled exactly as in the surface-word node.

**Corollary.** For a fully irreducible `φ`, the stabilizer of its attracting
lamination `Λ_φ^+` is virtually cyclic (BFH, cited). So it is never a type (A)
point stabilizer, and neither is anything virtually contained in it. The same
holds for the stabilizer of any point of `∂CV_n`, or any lamination, whose
stabilizer is virtually cyclic.

**What a type (A) point stabilizer of `Out(F_n)` must avoid, so far**, together
with the three sibling nodes:
- bounded orbits on `FF_n`;
- virtually fixing a free factor class, free factor system or free splitting;
- non-filling conjugacy classes;
- surface peripheral structures, orientable or not;
- virtually cyclic subgroups.

It must also be finitely generated, of infinite index and not commensurated.
Not treated: large non-geometric subgroups, such as convex cocompact free
subgroups. There the escaping argument would need a limit-set statement in
`∂FF_n` that is not proved here.
