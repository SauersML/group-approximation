## Why sufficient

Let `X, Y` be infinite minimal subshifts and `φ: R_X → R_Y` a ring isomorphism, where `R_X = LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Z`.

1. `M_3(φ): M_3(R_X) → M_3(R_Y)`, applying `φ` entrywise, is a ring isomorphism with inverse `M_3(φ^(−1))`. It
   therefore restricts to a group isomorphism `GL_3(R_X) → GL_3(R_Y)`.
2. `EL_3(R)` is the subgroup of `GL_3(R)` generated by the elementary matrices `e_ij(r) = I + r E_ij` with `i ≠ j` and
   `r ∈ R`. Since `φ(0) = 0` and `φ(1) = 1`, `M_3(φ)(e_ij(r)) = e_ij(φ(r))`. The image of `EL_3(R_X)` is the subgroup
   generated by the `e_ij(φ(r))`, `r ∈ R_X`. Because `φ` is surjective, that subgroup is `EL_3(R_Y)`.
3. Hence `M_3(φ)` restricts to an isomorphism `G_X = EL_3(R_X) → EL_3(R_Y) = G_Y`. A yes to
   `subshift-el3-group-isomorphism-forces-flip-conjugacy` then gives that `(X,T)` and `(Y,S)` are flip conjugate.

Together with `subshift-el3-flip-rigidity-from-ring-rigidity` this makes the group question and the ring question
equivalent.
