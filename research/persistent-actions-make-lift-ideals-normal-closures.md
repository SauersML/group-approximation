**Credits.** Persistence and the non-`F_n` transfer are due to Skipper–Witzel–Zaremsky, *Simple groups separated by
finiteness properties*, arXiv:1712.05361. Read at source: Definition 5.1, Lemma 5.3, Theorem 5.8 and Theorem 7.1.
Their Theorem 5.8 already gives item 2 for finite-state `G`, by a quasi-retraction. What is added here is the
elementary lift-ideal explanation, valid for any finitely generated persistent recursion, and the two-action
dichotomy on one group.

## Statement

Let `G` be generated by a finite set `S` with a self-similar recursion on `T_d` that is **`d`-persistent**:
`s|_d = s` and `ρ(s)(d) = d` for every `s ∈ S` (SWZ Def. 5.1). Let `F = F(S)` and `N = ker(F -> G)`.

1. **Lift ideals are normal closures.** For every `R ⊆ F`, `⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift = ⟨⟨R⟩⟩_F`. In particular `K_∞ = 1`.
2. **Hence, for finite-state `G`:** `V_d(G)` is finitely presented iff `N` is finitely normally generated, iff `G` is
   finitely presented. This uses `rover-nekrashevych-fp-iff-finite-lift-presentation` and agrees with SWZ
   Thm. 5.8.
3. **The dichotomy on one group.** Let `L = Z/2 ≀ Z`, which is not finitely presented.
   - Its standard automaton `a = σ(a,b)`, `b = (a,b)` on `T_2` has `N = K_∞`, and `V_2(L)` **is** finitely
     presented (`rover-nekrashevych-group-of-lamplighter-automaton-is-fp`).
   - SWZ's persistent re-action on `T_3`, namely `a = ι(σ)(a, b, a)` and `b = (a, b, b)` (Lemma 5.3), is again
     faithful and finite-state. There `V_3(L)` is **not** finitely presented. This is a direct corollary of SWZ
     Lemma 5.3 and Theorem 5.8. The contribution here is only the lift-ideal explanation and the pairing with
     `V_2(L)`.

## Proof

1. **A word is its own section at `d`.** Each letter fixes `d` and restricts to itself there. By the product rule
   for sections, every word `w` satisfies `w(d) = d` and `w|_d = w`.
   - **So normal closures are lift-closed.** Let `M = ⟨⟨R⟩⟩_F`, and let `w` have trivial root permutation with
     every `w|_x ∈ M`. In particular `w = w|_d ∈ M`.
   - **So they are the lift ideals.** `M` is the smallest lift ideal containing `R`, since any lift ideal is normal.
   - **`K_∞ = 1`.** A word that becomes freely trivial at level `n` equals its own level-`n` section at `d…d`,
     so it is freely trivial.
2. **Combine with Theorem A.** `N` is a finitely generated lift ideal iff it is a finitely generated normal subgroup,
   iff `G` is finitely presented (B.H. Neumann).
3. **Apply item 2 to both actions.** SWZ Lemma 5.3 shows the `T_3` action is faithful, finite-state and persistent,
   and `L` is not finitely presented. ∎

## What this settles

- **The criterion has teeth.** One and the same group can have a finitely presented and a non-finitely-presented
  Röver–Nekrashevych group, depending only on the self-similar action.
  - **In the standard action,** relators die by splitting: they lie in `K_∞`, a lift shortcut.
  - **In the persistent action,** every relator survives as its own section, so no finite set of relators
    lift-generates the rest.
- **Persistence is anti-contraction** (SWZ Remark 5.2). Every lift shortcut, whether nucleus relators or `K_∞`,
  needs relators whose sections become simpler. Persistence forbids exactly that.

## Lesson for general BH

**The action decides, not the group.** Whether `V_d(G)` is finitely presented is a property of the recursion: how
relators behave under sections, measured by lift ideals.
- **What a host needs.** A Röver–Nekrashevych host for a non-finitely-presented input must use an action in which
  relators shrink under sections: contracting, lamplighter-like, or decoupled as in `L × Z`. It can never use a
  persistent action.
- **What persistence does.** It turns lift ideals into normal closures, and with them the host's finite
  presentability back into that of the input.
- **The design constraint on hard hosts.** Infinite states for hardness, together with shrinking relators for
  finite presentation. Persistence shows the second is a genuine condition.

## Referee (bh-ref-q12, 2026-09-19): PASS with two scope repairs; the non-fp half is SWZ

Checked against Skipper–Witzel–Zaremsky, arXiv:1712.05361, read in the arXiv PDF on 09-19.
- **Definition 5.1:** `g` is `i`-persistent if `g_i = g` in its wreath recursion.
- **Lemma 5.3:** from a faithful self-similar action on `T_(d−1)`, the recursion `g = ιρ(g)(g_1, …, g_(d−1), g)`
  gives a faithful, persistent action on `T_d`, finite-state if the original is.
- **Theorem 5.8:** "Let G ≤ Aut(T_d) be a persistent, finite-state self-similar group. If V_d(G) is of type F_n
  (or FP_n) then so is G."
- The stated purpose of their §5 is exactly that `V_d(G)` fails `F_n` whenever `G` does, for this action.

**Item 1: correct.**
- `w(d) = d` and `w|_d = w` hold letter by letter, including inverse letters, and then for words by the product
  rule.
- So normal closures are lift-closed, `⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift = ⟨⟨R⟩⟩_F`, and `K_∞ = 1`.

**Item 2: correct**, with a **scope repair**.
- It needs a finite generating set that is **section-closed and persistent**. Theorem A is stated for a
  section-closed `S`, and the collapse needs the same `S` persistent.
- SWZ's lamplighter recursion `{a, b}` is both.
- The direction "`G` finitely presented ⇒ `V_d(G)` finitely presented" is Scott's, or Theorem A's sufficiency. The
  converse is SWZ Theorem 5.8 with `n = 2`.

**Item 3: correct.**
- The faithfulness of the `T_3` action is SWZ's argument: the `T_2` subtree is invariant, with the standard action.
- **Attribution.** "`V_3(L)` is not finitely presented" is an **immediate corollary of SWZ Lemma 5.3 and Theorem
  5.8**, applied to the lamplighter automaton. It is not new. SWZ do not single out the lamplighter in the pages
  read (§5).
- **The finitely presented half** (`V_2(L)`) comes from the unreviewed lamplighter node.
- **The two-action dichotomy on one group** is a correct and useful observation. Credit its non-fp half to SWZ.

**Scope repair 2 (Lesson).**
- "A Röver–Nekrashevych host for a non-finitely-presented input … can never use a persistent action" is proved
  only for **finite-state** persistent actions.
- For infinite-state persistent actions, the lift-ideal argument gives nothing beyond "`G` fp ⇒ `V_d(G)` fp",
  since the necessity half of the criterion needs finite state. SWZ Theorem 5.8 is also stated for finite-state
  `G`.
- So also restrict "valid for any finitely generated persistent recursion" to item 1.

**Verdict: PASS**, with the two scope repairs and the attribution of the non-fp half to SWZ.
