Every subgroup `H` of Richard Thompson's group `F` is elementary amenable, or has a
subgroup isomorphic to `F`. This is the affirmative answer to Zaremsky Problem 2.3
(`zaremsky-2-03-brin-sapir-conjecture`).

By closure of elementary amenable groups under directed unions, it is enough to
prove it for finitely generated `H`.

## Attempts

- **Uniform bound on EA-class (dead).** Brin (arXiv:math/0501085, Question 2) asked
  whether `ω²+1` is the largest elementary class of an elementary amenable subgroup of
  `PL_o(I)`. `f-has-ea-subgroups-of-every-class-below-epsilon-zero` realizes EA-class
  `α+2` for every `α < ε_0` among finitely generated subgroups of `F`. Any induction
  must run through all of these ordinals; no structure theorem of bounded depth can
  prove the conjecture.
- **Germ-kernel descent (z2-03-brin-sapir, 2026-09-13).** By
  `plo-i-group-is-ea-iff-its-orbital-germ-kernels-are`, it suffices to show that a
  finitely generated `F`-free `H ≤ PL_o(I)` has no infinite descent `(D_i, A_i)` with
  `D_{i+1} ≤ K_{A_i}(D_i)`, the closure of `A_{i+1}` inside `A_i`, and every
  `(D_i)_{A_i}` not elementary amenable. By
  `f-free-plo-i-groups-have-equal-end-germ-kernels` every step has synchronized end
  germs. Where it stands: "no such descent" is only a restatement of the conjecture;
  a proof needs an ordinal rank on pairs `(D, A)` that drops along descent steps,
  and by the firewall above its values exceed every ordinal below `ε_0`. No candidate
  rank yet.
- **Laminar orbitals (idea, unproved).** Conjectured lemma: in an `F`-free
  `H ≤ PL_o(I)`, the orbitals of any two finitely generated subgroups are disjoint,
  equal, or properly nested without a shared endpoint. It holds in `ℤ ≀ ℤ` and for
  two one-bump elements with different supports
  (`two-one-bump-pl-maps-unequal-supports-ea-or-contain-f`: one shared endpoint gives
  `F` by Brin's criterion, overlap gives `F` by Kim–Koberda–Lodha). Where it dies:
  for elements with several bumps, a partial overlap of two orbitals can sit inside
  a larger orbital of the generated group in which every element approaches both
  ends, so Brin's criterion does not fire directly, and the Kim–Koberda–Lodha theorem
  needs one-bump supports.
- **Two one-bump generators with equal support (open).** Let `f, g` be one-bump with
  the same support `A = (a,b)`, with slopes at `a` multiplicatively independent. Then
  the exponent sums are well defined, `⟨f,g⟩^{ab} ≅ ℤ²`, the germ kernel at `a` is
  `[G,G]` (the normal closure of `[f,g]`), and balance at `A` holds iff the slopes at
  `b` are multiplicatively independent too. Deciding elementary amenability then needs
  control of the compactly supported products in `[G,G]`. The high-class examples are
  already 2-generated: Bleak–Brin–Moore's Figure 1 shows generating pairs of
  oscillation 4 and 5 generating groups of EA-class `ω^ω+2` and `ω^{ω^ω}+2`.
- **Fast families.** Bleak–Brin–Kassabov–Moore–Zaremsky (arXiv:1701.08321) decide
  the isomorphism type of groups generated by fast families from finite dynamical
  data. Extending that to arbitrary generating sets would need a normal form for
  arbitrary generating sets, which is not available.
