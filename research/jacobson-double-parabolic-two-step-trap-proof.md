## Step 0. Partial-model bookkeeping

**Group facts.** Let `x, y in B_*`. Then `xy`, `xy^(-1)` and `x y x^(-1)` all
lie in `B_0 = B_*^3`, because `B_*` is closed under inverses:
- `N_0` is closed under `Y -> -Y`;
- `L_0^(-1)` is included in `B_*`.

**Consequences for rho.**
- `rho(1) = rho(1)^2`, and `rho(1)` is invertible, so `rho(1) = 1`.
- For `x in B_*`, `rho(x) rho(x^(-1)) = rho(1) = 1`.
- So `rho(x n x^(-1)) = rho(x) rho(n) rho(x)^(-1)` for `x in L_0` and
  `n in N_0`.
- If `x, y in B_*` commute in `P`, then
  `rho(x) rho(y) = rho(xy) = rho(yx) = rho(y) rho(x)`.
- For `Y, Y' in M_2(E)` with `Y + Y' in M_2(E)`:
  `rho(n(Y + Y')) = rho(n(Y)) rho(n(Y'))`.

## Step 1. The leading symbol is additive and Levi-equivariant

**The Levi action on H_d.** Every `rho(l)`, `l in L_0`, preserves the
filtration, and so does `rho(l)^(-1) = rho(l^(-1))`. So `rho(l)` induces an
invertible graded map `gr(l)` of degree 0. Put

```text
c_l(X) = gr(l) X gr(l)^(-1),   X in H_d.
```

**Additivity.** Write `a = rho(n(Y)) - 1` and `b = rho(n(Y')) - 1`. Both have
degree at least `d`, and

```text
rho(n(Y + Y')) - 1 = a + b + ab.
```

Here `ab` has degree at least `2d > d`. So `eps(Y + Y') = eps(Y) + eps(Y')`
whenever `Y, Y', Y + Y' in M_2(E)`. In particular `eps(0) = 0` and
`eps(-Y) = -eps(Y)`.

**Equivariance.** Let `l = diag(A, B) in L_0` and `Y, AYB^(-1) in M_2(E)`. By
Step 0,

```text
rho(n(A Y B^(-1))) - 1 = rho(l) (rho(n(Y)) - 1) rho(l)^(-1).
```

Taking degree-`d` parts gives `eps(A Y B^(-1)) = c_l(eps(Y))`.

## Step 2. Four conjugation identities

Every matrix below has entries in `E`.

**(a) Left, `A = x_21(1) = [[1,0],[1,1]]`.**
- `A Y_x = E_11 (x) x + E_21 (x) x` for `x in {1, s, t}`.
- By Step 1, `eps(E_21 (x) x) = (c_(x_21(1)) - 1) eps(Y_x)`.

**(b) Left, `A = x_12(b) = [[1,b],[0,1]]`.**
- `A (E_21 (x) a) = E_21 (x) a + E_11 (x) ba` for
  `(b, a) in {(s,1), (t,1), (t,s), (s,t)}`. The products `ba` are
  `s, t, ts = 1, st`.
- So `eps(Y_(ba)) = (c_(x_12(b)) - 1) eps(E_21 (x) a)`.

Combining (a) and (b), with

```text
Lambda(b) = (c_(x_12(b)) - 1)(c_(x_21(1)) - 1),
```

we get `eps(Y_(ba)) = Lambda(b) eps(Y_a)` for the four pairs.

**(c) Right, `B = [[1,1],[0,1]]` (this is `x_34(1)`).**
- `B^(-1) = [[1,-1],[0,1]]` and `Y_1 B^(-1) = E_11 - E_12`.
- So `eps(E_12 (x) 1) = -(c_(x_34(1)) - 1) v`, where `v = eps(Y_1)`.

**(d) Right, `B = [[1,0],[b,1]]` (this is `x_43(b)`), for `b in {s, t}`.**
- `(E_12 (x) 1) B^(-1) = E_12 - E_11 (x) b`.
- So `eps(Y_b) = -(c_(x_43(b)) - 1) eps(E_12 (x) 1)`.

Combining (c) and (d), with

```text
M(b) = (c_(x_43(b)) - 1)(c_(x_34(1)) - 1),
```

we get `eps(Y_b) = M(b) v`. The two minus signs cancel. In characteristic 2
there are no signs at all, and the artifact checks all identities of this
step exactly over `J`.

## Step 3. The relations satisfied by v

From Step 2:

```text
Lambda(s) v = eps(Y_s) = M(s) v,        Lambda(t) v = eps(Y_t) = M(t) v,     (R1)
Lambda(t) Lambda(s) v = Lambda(t) eps(Y_s) = eps(Y_(ts)) = eps(Y_1) = v,       (R2)
Lambda(s) Lambda(t) v = Lambda(s) eps(Y_t) = eps(Y_(st)).                      (R3)
```

## Step 4. Left and right Levi commute on the symbol

- In `P`, `diag(A, 1)` commutes with `diag(1, B)`.
- So each of `x_12(s), x_12(t), x_21(1)` commutes with each of
  `x_34(1), x_43(s), x_43(t)`.
- By Step 0, their images under `rho` commute. Hence their graded parts
  `gr(l)` commute, and the conjugations `c_l` commute.
- Therefore each `Lambda(b)` commutes with each `M(b')`.

## Step 5. The trap

**The trap space.** Let `𝓜 ⊂ End(H_d)` be the unital algebra generated by
`M(s)` and `M(t)`, and let `Z = 𝓜 v`. Take `m in 𝓜`.
- By Step 4 and (R1), `Lambda(s) m v = m Lambda(s) v = m M(s) v`, which lies
  in `Z`. The same holds for `t`. So `Z` is invariant under `Lambda(s)` and
  `Lambda(t)`.
- By (R2), `Lambda(t) Lambda(s) m v = m Lambda(t) Lambda(s) v = m v`. So
  `Lambda(t) Lambda(s)` is the identity on `Z`.

**Closing the inverse.** `Z ⊂ H_d` is finite-dimensional.
- `Lambda(s)|_Z` has a left inverse, so it is injective, and hence bijective.
- Its inverse is `Lambda(t)|_Z`, so `Lambda(s) Lambda(t)|_Z = 1`.
- Since `v in Z`, (R3) gives `eps(Y_(st)) = v`.

**The head.** `Y_q = Y_1 + (-Y_(st))`, and all three matrices lie in
`M_2(E)`. By additivity,

```text
eps(Y_q) = v - eps(Y_(st)) = 0.
```

This proves the theorem.

**Why the right Levi matters.** The left operators alone give only
`Lambda(t) Lambda(s) v = v` on the cyclic space of `Lambda(s), Lambda(t)`,
where `Lambda(s)` may be a nonsurjective isometry-like shift. The right Levi
supplies a second presentation `M(b) v` of the same vectors, and the space it
generates is both finite-dimensional and invariant under the left operators.
This is where `P_{2,2}` differs from `P_{2,1}`: there the right Levi is
`EL_1 = 1`, and `M(b)` is not available.

## Step 6. The two-step corollary and sharpness

**The corollary.** Take the filtration `W ⊃ W_1 ⊃ 0` and `d = 1`.
- The hypotheses make it `rho(L_0 ∪ N_0)`-stable, with every `n in N_0` of
  degree at least 1.
- For such `n`, `rho(n) - 1` equals the composite
  `W -> W/W_1 -> W_1 ⊂ W`, where the middle map is its degree-1 part.
- For `n = x_13(q)` that part is `eps(Y_q) = 0`, so `rho(x_13(q)) = 1`.

For `R = J` the head `x_13(Q)` is a nonidentity element of `P_{2,2}(J)`, and
the artifact checks this. So no such model separates it from `1`. Finite
group models linearized by a representation with a two-step Levi-stable flag
are a special case.

**Sharpness 1: the right Levi is needed.**
- `jacobson-rank-three-parabolics-are-lef` gives LEF models
  `Psi_(M,d)(g, X) = [[Phi_M(g), ev_d(X)], [0, 1]]` of `P_{2,1}(J)`. They are
  two-step for `W_1 = ` the first block, and they keep the head.
- Take `H = Hom(F^d, W_1)`, `c_l(X) = Phi_M(l) X` and `v = ev_d(e_1)`.
  Step 1 and Steps 2(a) and 2(b) go through verbatim, and give (R2). Steps
  2(c) and 2(d) have no analogue, because the right Levi is trivial.
- The artifact computes, for `M = 60` and `d = 6`:
  - `Lambda(T) Lambda(S) v = v`;
  - `Lambda(S) Lambda(T) v = ev_d(ST)`;
  - `v - Lambda(S) Lambda(T) v = ev_d(Q) != 0`.
- There is no second algebra `𝓜`, so the trap space does not exist.
  `Lambda(S)` acts on the cyclic space of `v` like the shift of the Toeplitz
  model, which stays non-surjective at the scale of the ball.

**Sharpness 2: finite dimension is needed.** `P_{2,2}(J)` acts on columns
`J^4`, and `W_1 = J^2 ⊕ 0` is stable under the whole group. On it,
`n(Y) - 1 : (u, w) -> (Y w, 0)` is two-step. The model is exact and faithful,
and `rho(x_13(Q)) != 1`. Here `Z` is infinite-dimensional and `Lambda(S)`
is the non-surjective shift.

**Sharpness 3: leading order only.** The argument controls only `eps`, the
degree-`d` part. A degree-`d` symbol of `n(Y_(st))` equal to `v` says nothing
about the higher-order terms of `rho(n(Y_q))`. These are not additive, since
the `ab` term of Step 1 enters them, so the trap cannot be iterated degree by
degree without new input.

## Step 7. General parabolics

Let `P <= EL_n(R)` be a standard parabolic with a unipotent-radical root
`(i,k)`. Suppose `i` and `j` lie in one Levi block, and `k` and `l` lie in
another.
- The elementary matrices supported on rows `{i,j}` and columns `{k,l}`
  commute pairwise, and they are additive in the coefficient.
- Conjugation by `x_ji(1)`, `x_ij(b)`, `x_kl(1)` and `x_lk(b)` acts on them by
  the formulas of Step 2, via the commutator relations of `St_n(R)`.
- The left and right generators commute, because their index sets are
  disjoint.

So Steps 0 to 5 apply word for word to `x_ik(q)`, with `N_0` replaced by the
products of the `x_(rows {i,j}, columns {k,l})(e)`, `e in E`. Such products
lie in `P` because `(i,k)`, `(i,l)`, `(j,k)` and `(j,l)` are all roots of the
unipotent radical.
