Notation as in `double-ascending-hnn-strong-atiyah-reduces-to-kernel-tree`.
Bass--Serre theory is used in the form of Serre, *Trees* (Springer, 1980),
Chapter I. Three facts are used:
- the structure theorem for groups acting on trees without inversion;
- the normal form theorem, so a subtree of groups injects;
- a finite group acting on a tree fixes a vertex.

**(F1) Subgroups and directed unions.** For `L <= L'` and a matrix `A` over
`K[L]`, `dim_(N(L')) ker r_A = dim_(N(L)) ker r_A`. This is the induction formula
for von Neumann dimension, as in Step 1 of `algebraic-atiyah-determinant-colimit-proof`.
So Strong Atiyah over `K` passes to subgroups. A matrix involves finitely many
group elements, so the conjecture holds for a directed union of subgroups iff it
holds for each member.

## Step 1: splitting and torsion

`pi` is well defined, since each relation maps to `s s^-1 = 1`. The subgroup
`<t_s : s in S>` maps onto `F(S)` by a homomorphism sending each generator to a
free generator. The map `s -> t_s` is a section, so the restriction of `pi` is an
isomorphism, `pi` is a retraction, and `E = N semidirect <t_s>`. Killing `Gamma`
in the presentation leaves the free group on the `t_s`. So `N` is the normal
closure of `Gamma`.

`E` is the fundamental group of a graph of groups with one vertex, carrying
`Gamma`, and one loop for each `s`. A nontrivial element of finite order
generates a finite group, which fixes a vertex of the Bass--Serre tree. So it
lies in a conjugate of `Gamma`, which is impossible. Hence `E` and `N` are
torsion-free.

## Step 2: the Bass--Serre tree and its quotient by N

Let `T` be the Bass--Serre tree of `E`. Its vertices are `E/Gamma`, and for each
`s` its `s`-edges are `E/Gamma`. The `s`-edge `g Gamma` joins `g Gamma` to
`g t_s^-1 Gamma`.

* **Stabilizers.** The edge `g Gamma` has stabilizer `g Gamma g^-1`. It lies in
  `Stab(g t_s^-1 Gamma) = g t_s^-1 Gamma t_s g^-1`, because
  `t_s Gamma t_s^-1 = phi_s(Gamma) <= Gamma`. The inclusion is
  `g gamma g^-1 = (g t_s^-1) phi_s(gamma) (g t_s^-1)^-1`.
* **Quotient graph.** `N` is normal and contains `Gamma`, so
  `N \ E / Gamma = E/N ≅ F(S)` on vertices and on `s`-edges. The `s`-edge `w`
  joins `w` and `w s^-1`. So `N \ T` is the Cayley graph of `F(S)` with respect
  to `S`, which is a tree.
* **A lift.** The map `w -> t_w Gamma` on vertices, with `s`-edge `w` sent to
  `t_w Gamma`, lifts this quotient tree into `T`. The edge `t_w Gamma` joins
  `t_w Gamma` and `t_w t_s^-1 Gamma = t_(w s^-1) Gamma`, since `w -> t_w` is a
  homomorphism on the free group. A lift of a tree maps it isomorphically onto a
  subtree of `T`.
* **Structure theorem.** `N` acts on `T` without inversion, with quotient tree
  `N \ T`. The structure theorem identifies `N` with the fundamental group of the
  quotient graph of groups, computed along the lifted subtree. Since the quotient
  is a tree, this is the colimit of the stabilizers along it:
  - vertex `w` carries `Stab_N(t_w Gamma) = t_w Gamma t_w^-1 = Gamma_w`;
  - the edge `{w, w s^-1}` carries `Gamma_w`;
  - the inclusion into `Gamma_(w s^-1)` is the one in the first bullet, with
    image `t_(w s^-1) phi_s(Gamma) t_(w s^-1)^-1`.

  This proves items 1 and 2.

## Step 3: finite subtrees

For a finite subtree `Y`, the subtree of groups on `Y` has fundamental group
`N_Y`, the subgroup generated by the `Gamma_w` with `w in Y`. By the normal form
theorem it injects into `N`. `N` is the directed union of the `N_Y`, since each
element is a finite product of elements of vertex groups.

* **Item 3, first equivalence.** (F1) gives `SA(N) <=> SA(N_Y)` for all finite `Y`.
* **Item 3, second equivalence.** `N` is torsion-free and `E/N ≅ F(S)` is free,
  hence locally indicable. If `N` satisfies Strong Atiyah over `K`, then
  `strong-atiyah-passes-to-locally-indicable-extensions` gives it for `E`.
  Conversely `SA(E) => SA(N)` by (F1).

## Step 4: growth by leaves

Every finite subtree containing a given vertex is reached from that vertex by
adding leaves one at a time. Let `Y` be finite, `w in Y`, and `l` a neighbour of
`w` outside `Y`, joined by the edge `e`. Adding a leaf to a tree of groups gives
`N_(Y + l) = N_Y *_(G_e) G_l`.

* If `l = ws`, the edge `{ws, w}` carries `Gamma_(ws)`, which is all of `G_l`.
  So the amalgam is `N_Y`.
* If `l = w s^-1`, the edge carries `Gamma_w <= N_Y`, embedded in `G_l = Gamma_l`
  as `t_l phi_s(Gamma) t_l^-1`. Conjugation by `t_l` identifies this with
  `phi_s(Gamma) <= Gamma`.

This is item 4.

## Step 5: the consequence

Assume `Gamma` satisfies Strong Atiyah over `K` and the permanence of item 5.
Induct on `|Y|`, starting from `N_({w}) = Gamma_w ≅ Gamma`.
* A child leaf leaves `N_Y` unchanged.
* A parent leaf forms `N_Y *_(Gamma_w) Gamma_l`. Here `N_Y` satisfies Strong
  Atiyah by induction and contains `Gamma_w ≅ Gamma`, and `Gamma_w` sits in
  `Gamma_l ≅ Gamma` as `phi_s(Gamma)`. The permanence applies.

So every `N_Y` satisfies Strong Atiyah over `K`, and Step 3 gives it for `E`. QED
