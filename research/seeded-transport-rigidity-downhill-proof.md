Notation as in the target. Fix a `D`-quantum family `E_(a,s,c)(z)` on `W`, with
`D >= max(R, r_Y, 1)`, and assume (B). Its marginals are:
- `Q_a(z)`, the `H`-letter marginal;
- `P_s(z)`, the flow marginal;
- `C_c(z)`, the computation marginal.
They pairwise commute at a common site, and `E_(a,s,c)(z) = Q_a(z) P_s(z) C_c(z)`. `𝒜` is the
commutative algebra generated by the base marginals.

## Step 0. Tools

- **Ball sums** (Step 0 of `quantum-rigidity-product-stability-proof`). Operators at sites of one
  ball `zB_D` commute. A product prescribing a partial pattern there that no point of `Z` realizes is
  `0`.
- **Base events.** As in Step 1 of `causal-depth-localization-proof`, there is a homomorphism
  `ι : LC(Y_D, k) → 𝒜` onto `𝒜`, where `Y_D` is cut out by the `B_D`-language of the base projection.
  - That projection lies in `B`, and `B` is an SFT with rules of radius `<= max(r_Y, 1) <= D`. So
    `Y_D ⊆ B`.
  - Hence every implication between cylinder events that holds on `B` holds in `𝒜`. In particular,
    `[e] [e'] = [e]` whenever `e ⊆ e'` on `B`.
- **Constancy** (Step 3 of `quantum-rigidity-product-stability-proof`, same argument). For `s' ∈ S_H`
  and a time generator `t`, `P_s(λ s', v') = P_s(λ, v')` and `Q_a(λ, v' t) = Q_a(λ, v')`. So
  `P_s(·, v')` does not depend on the `H`-coordinate, and `Q_a(λ, ·)` does not depend on the
  `F_m`-coordinate.
- **Transport identities.** For a base pattern `p` on the rule window at `z = (λ, v')`, write `[p]`
  for its product of base marginals. By ball sums, as in Step 2 of `causal-depth-localization-proof`:
  - (Sp\*) `[p] C_c(λ, v') = [p] C_(α_s(p,c))(λs, v')`, for `s ∈ S_H`;
  - (Ti\*) if `p` makes `v'` a rank-2 child with parent `v' s_0`, then
    `[p] C_c(z) = [p] C_(β(p,·)^(-1)(c))(λ, v' s_0)`;
  - (Se\*) if `p` makes `v'` a seed, then `[p] C_c(z) = [κ(p) = c] [p]`.

  In each identity all factors commute, since they lie in `zB_R`. Summing over `p` expresses
  `C_c(z)` through one neighbour.

## Step 1. Item 1: downhill centrality

Fix `b = P_s(w)` with `w = (μ, w')`. By constancy, `b = P_s(λ, w')` for every `λ`. We prove
`[b, C_c(λ, v')] = 0` for all `λ`, `v'`, `c`, by induction on `d = d_(F_m)(v', w')`.
- **`d = 0`.** Then `b = P_s(λ, v')` sits at the same site as `C_c(λ, v')`.
- **`d >= 1`.** Let `a_1` be the neighbour of `v'` toward `w'`. Expand `C_c(z)` over the time-window
  patterns `p` by (Ti\*) and (Se\*). There are three kinds of term.
  - *Seed terms* `[κ(p) = c][p]` lie in `𝒜`, so they commute with `b`.
  - *Toward terms* have parent `v' s_0 = a_1`. They are `[p] C(λ, a_1)`. Here `[p] ∈ 𝒜`, and
    `C(λ, a_1)` commutes with `b` by induction, since `d(a_1, w') = d - 1`.
  - *Away terms* have `v' s_0 ≠ a_1`. They are `[p] X`, with `X = C(λ, v' s_0)`.
    - *The target letter is forced.* On `B`, the arrow at `v'` points away from `a_1`. So `w'` lies
      below `v'`, and its arrow points along the geodesic toward `v'`. Hence `[p] b = b [p] = δ [p]`
      with `δ ∈ {0, 1}`.
    - *Commuting.* `[p]` and `X` commute (ball sums), so
      `[p] X b = X [p] b = δ X [p] = δ [p] X = b [p] X`.

## Step 2. Item 2: space centrality

Fix `b = Q_a(w)` with `w = (μ, w')`. By constancy, `b = Q_a(μ, v')` for every `v'`. We prove
`[b, C_c(λ, v')] = 0` by induction on `d_H(λ, μ)`.
- **Distance `0`** is a common site.
- **The step.** Pick `s ∈ S_H` with `d_H(λ s, μ) = d_H(λ, μ) - 1`. By (Sp\*),
  `C_c(λ, v') = Σ_p [p] C_(α_s(p,c))(λ s, v')`. Each `[p] ∈ 𝒜` commutes with `b`, and each
  `C(λ s, v')` does by induction.

So every base marginal is central in the algebra `𝔅` generated by the family.

## Step 3. Item 3: computation letters commute

**Reduction to one space coordinate.** Iterate (Sp\*) along a path in `H` from `λ` to `μ`. This gives
`C_c(λ, v') ∈ span(𝒜 · C(μ, v'))`. Since `𝒜` is central, it suffices to show
`[C_c(μ, v'), C_(c')(μ, u')] = 0` for all `v'`, `u'`, `c`, `c'`.

**Induction on `d = d(v', u')`.** For `d = 0` the two lie at one site. For `d >= 1`, let `a_1` be the
neighbour of `v'` toward `u'`, and expand `C_c(μ, v')` over time-window patterns `p`.
- *Seed terms* lie in `𝒜`, which is central.
- *Toward terms* are `[p] C(μ, a_1)`. Here `[p]` is central, and `C(μ, a_1)` commutes with
  `C(μ, u')` by induction.
- *Away terms* are `[p] X`, with `X = C(μ, v' s_0)` and `v' s_0 ≠ a_1`. Write `u' = a_d`, …, `a_1`,
  `a_0 = v'` for the geodesic.
  - *The chain from `u'` is forced onto `v'`.* On `B`, the arrow at `v'` forces the parent of `a_j`
    to be `a_(j-1)` for `j >= 1`. So in `𝒜`, `[p] = [p] ∏_j [parent(a_j) = a_(j-1)]`.
  - *Expanding upward.* Expand `C(μ, a_d)` by (Ti\*) or (Se\*) at `a_d`, then `a_(d-1)`, and so on.
    Patterns with another parent are killed by `[p]`. All coefficients are central. Each branch
    either stops at a seed `a_j`, giving an element of `𝒜`, or reaches `a_0 = v'`, giving a
    `C_(c'')(μ, v')`.
  - *So* `Y' := [p] C_(c')(μ, u')` lies in the algebra generated by `𝒜` and the `C(μ, v')`.
  - *Commuting.* `X` sits at the neighbour `(μ, v' s_0)` of `(μ, v')`, so it commutes with every
    `C(μ, v')` (ball sums), and with `𝒜`. Hence it commutes with `Y'`, and
    `[p] X C(μ, u') = X Y' = Y' X = C(μ, u') [p] X`.

## Step 4. Conclusion

- Every `E_(a,s,c)(z) = Q_a(z) P_s(z) C_c(z)` is a product of central base marginals and a
  computation marginal.
- The computation marginals pairwise commute by Step 3.
- So the family is commutative, and `Z` is `D`-quantum rigid over `k`. Nothing used the field or
  `dim W`. ∎

## Remark: where each hypothesis enters

- **Bijectivity of `α` and `β`.**
  - Bijectivity of `α` is used in (Sp\*), which expresses `C(λ, v')` through its space neighbour.
    That feeds Step 2 and the Reduction of Step 3.
  - Bijectivity of `β` is not used. For a general map `β`, (Ti\*) reads
    `[p] C_c(z) = Σ_(g : β(p,g) = c) [p] C_g(λ, v' s_0)`, and every step goes through unchanged.
    So rigidity holds for **any** time rule `c(λ, v') = β(p, c(λ, parent v'))`, bijective or not.
  - Bijectivity matters for the explicit count of spine completions (`<= |C|`), and to avoid dead
    configurations on the spine.
- **Where the proof would break.** It breaks only if a rule reads a time neighbour other than the
  parent, or if a space rule is not a bijection between neighbouring letters. A space rule of the
  second kind is a *relational* coupling.
