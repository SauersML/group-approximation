Notation: `Rad_MF(G)` is the intersection of the kernels of all homomorphisms from `G` to unitary
groups of norm-matrix C*-coronas (`universal-mf-quotient`). The group `G` is MF exactly when
`Rad_MF(G) = 1`.

## Two radical facts, from the definition

- **(R1) Monotonicity.** If `Y ≤ L`, then `Rad_MF(Y) ≤ Rad_MF(L)`. Every homomorphism
  `L → U(corona)` restricts to `Y`, so it kills `Rad_MF(Y)`.
- **(R2) Images.** If `φ : L → Q` is onto, then `φ(Rad_MF(L)) ≤ Rad_MF(Q)`. For every
  `ψ : Q → U(corona)`, the composite `ψ ∘ φ` kills `Rad_MF(L)`, so `ψ` kills `φ(Rad_MF(L))`.

## (a) ⇒ (b)

Let `H_0` be torsion-free, hyperbolic and not MF. Fix a finite set `M ⊂ P`.

**Step 1 (a radical element).** By `universal-mf-quotient`, `Rad_MF(H_0) ≠ 1`. Pick
`r ∈ Rad_MF(H_0)` with `r ≠ 1`. It has infinite order, since `H_0` is torsion-free.

**Step 2 (the free product).** Put `L = H_0 * P`.
- `L` is word-hyperbolic. A free product is hyperbolic relative to its factors, and a finitely
  generated group hyperbolic relative to hyperbolic subgroups is hyperbolic. Both statements are
  quoted verbatim from Osin, arXiv:math/0510195v3, pp. 2--3, in
  `residual-prime-torsion-via-free-factor` (item 1).
- `L` is torsion-free. A finite subgroup of a free product is conjugate into a factor (Kurosh),
  and both factors are torsion-free.
- `L` is non-elementary, since it contains the non-elementary group `P`.
- `E(L) = 1`, and `E(X) = 1` for every non-elementary `X ≤ L`. These are finite subgroups of the
  torsion-free group `L`.

**Step 3 (a free radical subgroup).** Fix `p ∈ P` with `p ≠ 1`, and put

```text
X = ⟨ r, p r p⁻¹ ⟩ ≤ L.
```

- **`X` is free of rank 2, hence non-elementary.** Let `w` be a nonempty freely reduced word in
  `a, b`, written as an alternating product of nonzero powers of `a` and of `b`, beginning with
  either letter. Substitute `a = r` and `b = p r p⁻¹`. The result is an alternating product of
  syllables `r^{n}` in `H_0` and `p^{±1}` in `P`, all nontrivial. `r^n ≠ 1` holds because `r` has
  infinite order, and `p^{±1} ≠ 1` by choice. Consecutive `b`-blocks never meet in a reduced word,
  so no `p⁻¹ p` cancellation occurs. By the normal form theorem for free products, the image is
  `≠ 1`.
- **`X ≤ Rad_MF(L)`.** First, `r ∈ Rad_MF(H_0) ≤ Rad_MF(L)` by (R1). Second, `Rad_MF(L)` is
  normal in `L`, so `p r p⁻¹ ∈ Rad_MF(L)`.

**Step 4 (one Olshanskii quotient).** Use clause 1 of `olshanskii-g-subgroup-quotient-theorem` in
`L`. For `Y ∈ {X, P}`:
- `Y` is non-elementary;
- `E(Y) = 1 = E(L)` by Step 2;
- `K(Y) = C_Y(1) = Y` and `K(L) = L`, so both indices equal `1`.

So `X` and `P` are G-subgroups of `L`. Clause 2, applied with `H_1 = X`, `H_2 = P` and the finite
set `M ⊂ P ⊂ L`, gives a non-elementary hyperbolic group `Q` and an epimorphism `φ : L → Q` with:
- `φ` injective on `M`;
- `φ(X) = φ(P) = Q`;
- a bijection induced by `φ` between the conjugacy classes of finite-order elements.

`L` has exactly one such class, `{1}`. So `Q` has only the trivial element of finite order, and
`Q` is torsion-free.

**Step 5 (the conclusions of (b)).**
- **Full radical.** `Q = φ(X) ≤ φ(Rad_MF(L)) ≤ Rad_MF(Q)`, by Step 3 and (R2).
- **The host surjects.** `π = φ|_P : P → Q` is onto, because `φ(P) = Q`. It is injective on `M`.
- **Generation.** Put `q = φ(r)` and `s = φ(p)`. Then `Q = φ(X) = ⟨q, s q s⁻¹⟩`. Both generators lie
  in the normal closure of `q`, so `Q` is normally generated by `q`.

## (b) ⇒ (c) ⇒ (a)

- **(b) ⇒ (c).** A non-elementary group is nontrivial.
- **(c) ⇒ (a).** `Q` is torsion-free and hyperbolic, and `Rad_MF(Q) = Q ≠ 1`. So `Q` is not MF by
  `universal-mf-quotient`.

## Part 2 (Kazhdan host)

Let `P` be given by `torsion-free-hyperbolic-kazhdan-partner-exists`.
- **Kazhdan.** Property (T) passes to quotients. This is the same standard fact used in
  `aut-f4-has-property-t-citation`. So `Q = π(P)` is Kazhdan.
- **Infinite.** `Q` is non-elementary.
- **Finitely presented.** Word-hyperbolic groups are finitely presented (standard: the Rips complex
  of a hyperbolic group is contractible for a large parameter, and the group acts on it
  cocompactly).
- **No finite quotients.** A finite group is countable and residually finite, so it is MF by
  `mf-positive-controls`. Every homomorphism `Q → F` to a finite group therefore kills
  `Rad_MF(Q) = Q`.
- **Not residually finite.** `Q` is infinite and has no nontrivial finite quotient.

## Part 3 (the product pair)

Put `G = Q × Z` and `K = Q × 0`.
- `G` is finitely presented, being a product of two finitely presented groups.
- `G` is torsion-free: `(g, n)` of finite order forces `n = 0` and `g = 1`.
- `K ≅ Q` is nontrivial, Kazhdan and finitely presented. Its normal closure of `(q, 0)` in `G`
  contains the normal closure of `q` in `Q`, which is all of `Q`.
- **`Rad_MF(G) ≤ K`.** `G/K ≅ Z` is residually finite, hence MF by `mf-positive-controls`.
- **`K ≤ Rad_MF(G)`.** Every homomorphism `G → U(corona)` restricts to `K ≅ Q`. There it kills
  `Rad_MF(Q) = Q` by definition.

So `Rad_MF(G) = K` and `G/K ≅ Z`, whether or not `Q` is sofic. Part 2 of
`finite-outer-order-radical-witnesses-cross-alekseev-thom` says `(K × Z, K)` is a witness exactly
when `K` is nontrivial, finitely presented, torsion-free, sofic and Kazhdan with `Rad_MF(K) = K`.
Every clause except soficity has just been verified. So `(G, K)` is a witness exactly when `Q` is
sofic, and it lies in region (AT) because `K` is finitely presented.

## Part 4 (trichotomy)

Suppose (T1) fails. Then (a) holds. Take `P` as in Part 2, and let `Q` come from (b) with any `M`.
- If `Q` is not sofic, (T2) holds with `Q`.
- If `Q` is sofic, Part 3 gives (T3).

In case (T3):
- part 3 of `finite-outer-order-radical-witnesses-cross-alekseev-thom` shows that `Q` answers
  Alekseev--Thom 6.1;
- `Q` is an infinite torsion-free hyperbolic group without nontrivial finite quotients, so it is not
  residually finite. QED.
