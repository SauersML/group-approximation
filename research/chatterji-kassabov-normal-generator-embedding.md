For every finitely generated recursively presented group `G` and every
`1 != g in G` there is an embedding `G <= Gamma` with `Gamma` finitely
presented, with property (T), and normally generated by `g`:

    Gamma = <<g>>_Gamma .

**Verification status.**  Read from source 2026-08-17.  Chatterji--Kassabov,
*Examples of finitely presented groups with strong fixed point properties and
property (T)*, arXiv:2601.22907 (submitted 30 January 2026), **Theorem 3**,
verbatim: "For any finitely generated recursively presented group G and any
non-trivial element g∈G, there exists an embedding of the group G into a
finitely presented property (T) group Γ that is normally generated by g."

A warning for anyone re-verifying this: the paper's **abstract does not
announce this theorem**.  It advertises the construction of a single finitely
presented property-(T) group with strong fixed point properties, and an
abstract-only check will report that the theorem is absent.  It is in the
body.

## How it is built, and which parts this graph re-uses

Their Lemma 4 is a Hall-type ring statement: for a finitely generated
associative ring `R` embeddable in an algebra over a field, and `0 != r in
R`, there is an extension `R <= L` in which the two-sided ideal generated by
`r` is all of `L`.  They apply it with `r = 1 - g` in a group ring, pass to
an elementary matrix group (rank four where rank three is insufficient), get
property (T) from the elementary-group input, and then replace the possibly
non-finitely-presented matrix group by a **Shalom finitely presented cover**.

Their Remark 5 records why that cover is needed: "Note that even if the ring
R is finitely presented we can not guarantee that the ring L is finitely
presented."

`universal-sandwich-ring-is-finitely-presented-and-injective` removes that
step.  Their own proof of Lemma 4 produces a *single sandwich*
`P φ(r) Q = Id` in `End(W)` and defines `L` as the subring generated by
`φ(R), P, Q` — so `L` is a quotient of the universal one-relation ring, and
the universal ring is finitely presented whenever `R` is.  This claim is
recorded in the form Chatterji--Kassabov state it; the improvement is a
separate node, and nothing here depends on it.
