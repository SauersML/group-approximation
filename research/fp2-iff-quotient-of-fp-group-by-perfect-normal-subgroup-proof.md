**Standard inputs.**

- (a) A group of type FP_2 is finitely generated.
- (b) Let `F` be free on a finite set `X`, let `F -> G` be onto, and let `R` be
  its kernel. The relation module `R_ab = R/[R,R]`, with `G` acting by
  conjugation, fits into the exact sequence of `ZG`-modules
  `0 -> R_ab -> ⊕_{x∈X} ZG -> ZG -> Z -> 0` (Lyndon; K. S. Brown, *Cohomology
  of Groups*, Chapter II, Section 5).
- (c) If `P_1 -> P_0 -> Z -> 0` is exact with `P_0`, `P_1` finitely generated
  projective, then `G` is of type FP_2 if and only if the kernel of `P_1 -> P_0`
  is finitely generated (Brown, Chapter VIII, Section 4).

By (b) and (c), a finitely generated group `G = F/R` is of type FP_2 if and
only if `R_ab` is finitely generated as a `ZG`-module.

**(⇐)** Let `H` be finitely presented, `N` a perfect normal subgroup, and
`G = H/N`. Choose a finite generating set of `H` and the free group `F` on it.
Then `R_0 = ker(F -> H)` is the normal closure of finitely many `r_1, ..., r_k`,
and `R = ker(F -> G)` contains `R_0` with `R/R_0 ≅ N`. Perfectness of `R/R_0`
gives `R = [R,R] R_0`. So `R_ab` is spanned as an abelian group by the images of
the `F`-conjugates of the `r_i`. Conjugation by `F` acts on `R_ab` through `G`,
so the images of `r_1, ..., r_k` generate `R_ab` as a `ZG`-module. Hence `G` is of
type FP_2.

**(⇒)** Let `G` be of type FP_2. By (a) it is finitely generated; write
`G = F/R` with `F` free of finite rank. By the criterion, choose `r_1, ..., r_k`
in `R` whose images generate `R_ab` as a `ZG`-module. Let `R_0` be their normal
closure in `F`, `H = F/R_0` (finitely presented) and `N = R/R_0`. The
`ZG`-submodule of `R_ab` generated by the images of the `r_i` is
`R_0[R,R]/[R,R]`, so `R = R_0[R,R]`. Therefore
`[N,N] = [R,R]R_0/R_0 = R/R_0 = N`, so `N` is perfect and `G ≅ H/N`.

**Consequence 1.** Write `Q = H/N` as above, and let `P` be the preimage of `M`
in `H`. Then `P/N ≅ M` is perfect, so `P = [P,P]N`. Also `N = [N,N] ≤ [P,P]`.
Hence `P = [P,P]` is perfect, and `Q/M ≅ H/P` is of type FP_2 by (⇐).

**Consequence 2.** If `K_j = [K_j, K_j]` for every `j`, then every `K_j` lies in
`[K, K]` for `K = ⟨K_j⟩`, so `K` is perfect. The normal closure of a family of
perfect subgroups is generated by their conjugates, which are perfect.

**Model test.** `Z` is finitely presented: take `H = Z`, `N = 1`. The theorem
also recovers Leary's FP_2 statement for `G_L(S)` when `π_1(L)` is perfect,
since passing from `G_L({0})` (finitely presented, Theorem 1.2(3)) to
`G_L(S)` adds long cycle relators, whose normal closure is generated by the
level-`n` images of the perfect group `π_1(L)` (Lemma 19.1 there).
