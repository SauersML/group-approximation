ESTABLISHED.  Let `Omega` be a countable set with a symmetric `D`-regular
action generated by `s_1,...,s_D`.  Assume its normalized adjacency operator
`A` has a positive bottom gap on `ell^2(Omega)`:

```text
<f,(1-A)f> >= gamma ||f||_2^2                               (ISP1)
```

for every finitely supported scalar `f`, with `gamma>0`.  Let `(P_v)` be
pairwise orthogonal projections in `M_d(C)`, only finitely many nonzero, and
let `W_s in U(d)`.  Put

```text
mu=sum_v tr_d(P_v),
E_PVM=(1/D) sum_s sum_v ||W_s P_v W_s^*-P_(s v)||_(2,d)^2.   (ISP2)
```

Then

```text
E_PVM >= 2 gamma mu,              hence mu<=E_PVM/(2 gamma). (ISP3)
```

Consequently a finite-dimensional approximate model cannot retain positive
mass on an infinite uniformly nonamenable character orbit while its actor
covariance defect tends to zero.  If one D8 seed places a fixed fraction of
a central marked sector in a union of such orbits, the marked sector itself
collapses with a dimension-independent modulus.  There is no challenge-size
parameter and no need to choose a virtual size after seeing `d`.

The remaining compiler input is genuinely global: finite presentation
defect must decode the infinite additive/module action to one finite-support
spectral PVM and must make the actor relators control `(ISP2)`.  Merely
rounding every fixed finite coefficient window does not supply that PVM.
