It rests on imports read at source, from Bodart–D'Angeli–Perego–Rodaro (BDPR, arXiv:2608.02111), Matte Bon–Nekrashevych–Zheng (MNZ, arXiv:2607.13776) and Juschenko (arXiv:1504.00610). It also uses two recalled inputs, listed in the route.

## Setting

`G ≤ Aut(T)` is finitely generated and satisfies one of the following:
- (B) `G` is branch; or
- (W) `G` is weakly branch, every level rigid stabilizer `rist(n)` is finitely generated, and `G` is not elementary amenable. The Basilica group is an example.

For `ξ ∈ ∂T`:
- `Σ_ξ = Γ_{G/G_ξ}` is the orbital Schreier graph;
- `Γ̃_ξ = Γ_{G/G^0_ξ}` is the graph of germs;
- `N(ξ)` is the set of vertices hanging off the ray `ξ`.

A subgroup `H` is **boundary-sandwiched** at `ξ` if `⊕_{v∈N(ξ)} K_v ≤ H ≤ G_ξ` for some finite-index subgroups `K_v ≤ rist(v)`.

## Statement

**(a) Only boundary actions reach V.** If `G` embeds in Thompson's group `V`, then fix any finite generating set. There are `ξ ∈ ∂T` and a subgroup `H` boundary-sandwiched at `ξ` such that `G` acts faithfully on `G/H`, and `Γ_{G/H}` is context-free with exponential growth and infinitely many ends.

**(b) Converse.** If `Γ_{G/H}` is context-free for some `ξ ∈ ∂T` and some `H ≤ G_ξ`, then `G` embeds in `V`. This needs only level-transitivity.

So `G ↪ V` holds exactly when some subgroup of some boundary stabilizer `G_ξ` has a context-free Schreier graph. By (a), such a subgroup can always be chosen boundary-sandwiched.

**(c) Finite germs.** Suppose in case (B) that the groups of germs `G_ξ/G^0_ξ` are finite. Then `H` in (a) can be taken of finite index in `G_ξ`, and three things follow:
- the orbital graph `Σ_ξ` has exponential growth;
- `Γ̃_ξ` has infinitely many ends;
- `G ↪ V` holds exactly when some finite-index subgroup of some `G_ξ` has a context-free Schreier graph.

Consequently, a finitely generated branch group with finite groups of germs, all of whose boundary orbital graphs have subexponential growth, does not embed in `V`.

**(d) Named consequences.** Every finitely generated branch group generated by a **bounded automaton** fails to embed in `V`. More generally, so does every finitely generated branch group generated by a polynomial-activity automaton whose groups of germs are finite. This covers:
- the Hanoi towers groups `H^(m)` (m ≥ 3) and Skipper's `G_n` (n ≥ 3), which recovers BDPR's corollary in §7 without the bottleneck computation;
- the Grigorchuk, Gupta–Sidki and Fabrykowski–Gupta groups (already excluded, by torsion or growth);
- every other bounded branch group, self-replicating or not.

## Calibration

- **Basilica, case (W).** Here (a) gives a context-free Schreier graph covering `Σ_ξ`. BDPR cite that `Σ_ξ` is never a quasi-tree. So BDPR's Lemma `lem:free_in_cover` forces a free subgroup, which the contracting Basilica group lacks. This recovers BDPR's Basilica corollary, with hypothesis (R) in place of their finite generation of `RStab(ℓ)'`.
- **Literal question.** The group `Fin(T) ≤ V` is branch but not finitely generated. It is consistent: finite generation is used in (a), in the reduction to a single faithful orbit, and in (R).

## What a positive example would have to look like

By (c), a finitely generated branch group with finite germs in `V` must:
- have a boundary orbit `Σ_ξ` of exponential growth, a finite cover of which is context-free, i.e. tree-like with finitely many cone types;
- by BDPR's `lem:free_in_cover`, either contain `F_2` or have `Σ_ξ` a quasi-tree.

So the only candidates are non-contracting groups with exponential-growth boundary orbits, necessarily generated by automata of super-polynomial activity or by non-automaton branch groups. Conversely, any branch group whose boundary orbit graph (or a finite cover of it) is context-free already embeds in `V`, by (b). The question is now one about boundary orbital graphs, not about `V`.

## Lesson for general BH

Rigidity of commensurating actions converts a host question into a question about the input's own dynamics. For a branch-type input, every tree-like faithful action lives at a boundary point, up to the sandwich (`multi-ended-branch-actions-are-boundary-sandwiched`). A host whose subgroups are cut out by a Schreier-graph class can therefore contain the input only if its boundary orbital graphs lie in that class. Examples of such hosts:
- `V`: context-free graphs;
- Houghton groups: context-free graphs of linear growth.

Here is the transferable pipeline:
1. faithful single orbit, by the normal-subgroup intersection property;
2. elementary-amenable (EA) or exponential-growth dichotomy for the host's graph class;
3. the MNZ sandwich;
4. growth or ends of boundary orbits.

It shows, for instance, that bounded-automaton branch groups, which do satisfy Boone–Higman via their Röver–Nekrashevych groups `V_d(G)` (BBMZ), can never be hosted by `V` itself. The right host must carry the input's germ dynamics. This supports germ-extension and Röver–Nekrashevych hosts over "universal" `V`-type hosts for branch and self-similar inputs.
