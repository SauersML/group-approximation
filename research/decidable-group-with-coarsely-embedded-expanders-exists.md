## Statement

1. There is a finitely generated group `M` with solvable word problem whose Cayley graph
   contains an expander family `(Θ_n)` isometrically. In particular `M` does not coarsely
   embed into Hilbert space.
2. There is an infinite, finitely generated, simple group with property (T) and solvable
   word problem that contains `M`, hence does not coarsely embed into Hilbert space either.

## Sources (read 2026-09-18)

Osajda, *Small cancellation labellings of some infinite graphs and applications*,
arXiv:1406.5015v3 (Acta Math.), read from the PDF:
- §2 standing hypotheses (p. 5–6): `Θ = (Θ_n)` finite connected graphs of degree `<= D`,
  `girth Θ_n → ∞`, `diam Θ_n <= A girth Θ_n`, and `1 < ⌊λ girth Θ_n⌋ < ⌊λ girth Θ_(n+1)⌋`
  ("this can be fulfilled by passing to a subsequence").
- Theorem 2.7 (p. 14): the product labelling `(Θ, m)`, over finitely many labels, "is reduced
  and no `m_n`-labelling of a path of length at least `λ girth Θ_n` in `Θ_n` appears as the
  `m`-labelling of some other path in `Θ`".
- Remark 2.8 (p. 14): each finite stage "can be extended … by using the brute force
  algorithm", so "if the sequence Θ of finite graphs is recursive, the small cancellation
  labelling `(Θ,m)`, as well as the resulting small cancellation presentation, are recursive".
- p. 15: "Recursive sequences of finite graphs Θ (satisfying our assumptions from the beginning
  of Section 2) exist"; §2.4 names the LPS Cayley graphs `X^(p,q_n)` of `PGL_2(q_n)`, an
  expander of degree `p+1` with girth → ∞ satisfying (1).
- §3.2 (p. 17) uses `λ <= 1/24`. Theorem 3.2: each `Θ_n` embeds isometrically into the Cayley
  graph of `G = ⟨Γ | Θ⟩`. Corollary 3.3 (p. 18): if `Θ` is expanding, `⟨Γ | Θ⟩` "is not coarsely
  embeddable into a Hilbert space".

Gruber–Sisto, *Infinitely presented graphical small cancellation groups are acylindrically
hyperbolic*, arXiv:1408.4488v3, read from the PDF:
- §2.2 (p. 5): `G(Γ) = ⟨S | labels of simple closed paths in Γ⟩`. A piece is a path with two
  essentially distinct lifts. The labelling is Gr'(λ) if it is reduced and every piece `p` that
  is a subpath of a simple closed path `γ` has `|p| < λ|γ|`.
- Lemma 2.4 and the paragraph after it (p. 6): for Gr(6)-labelled `Γ`, `w` represents the
  identity iff there is a Γ-reduced diagram for `w`. Such a diagram is a (3,6)-diagram, and
  under Gr'(λ) every interior arc `a` of a face `Π` has `|a| < λ|∂Π|`.

## Proof

**The group.** Take `Θ` recursive as on p. 15 (LPS graphs, subsequence chosen greedily, which is
computable because girths of finite graphs are computable). Fix `λ = 1/24`, and let `m` be the
labelling of Theorem 2.7, over a finite set `S`. It is recursive by Remark 2.8. Put `Γ = ⊔ Θ_n`
and `M = G(Γ)`. This is Osajda's `⟨Γ | Θ⟩`: the normal closure of the image of `π_1(Θ_n)` is
normally generated by the labels of the fundamental cycles of a spanning tree, which are simple
closed paths.

**Gr'(λ).** Let `p` be a piece with essentially distinct lifts `p_1 ⊂ Θ_n` and `p_2 ⊂ Θ_(n')`.
- If `|p| >= λ girth Θ_n`, Theorem 2.7 at `n` says the labelling of `p_1` appears on no other
  path, so `p_2 = p_1`, which is not essentially distinct.
- The same holds at `n'`.

So `|p| < λ min(girth Θ_n, girth Θ_(n'))`, which is at most `λ|γ|` for every simple closed path
`γ` containing a lift. The labelling is reduced by Theorem 2.7. Hence `Γ` is Gr'(1/24), so
Gr(7) and Gr(6).

**Greendlinger step (trust surface).** Let `D` be a Γ-reduced diagram for a nonempty cyclically
reduced word `w` with `w = 1`. By Gruber–Sisto p. 6, `D` is a (3,6)-diagram whose interior arcs
satisfy `|a| < λ|∂Π|`. By the combinatorial core of Greendlinger's lemma for (3,6)-maps
(Lyndon–Schupp, Ch. V, Thm 4.3, used as in the proof of Thm 4.4; not re-read), in its two-face
form, some face `Π` has one exterior arc `e` and at most three interior arcs. Singular `D` (N1):
- take an extremal disk component `D_0`, meeting the rest of `D` in at most one vertex `v`;
- if `D_0` has at least two faces, the two-face form gives two such faces, and one of them has its
  exterior arc avoiding `v`;
- if `D_0` is a single face, its whole boundary, read from `v` back to `v`, is traversed contiguously
  by `∂D`, and it serves as `e` with `|e| = |∂Π|`. Hence `|e| > (1 − 3λ)|∂Π| = (7/8)|∂Π|`.
- The interior vertices of `e` have degree 2 in `D`, so `e` is traversed contiguously by `∂D`.
  So its label is a cyclic subword of `w`.
- `∂Π` lifts to a simple closed path `γ` in some `Θ_i`, and `e` lifts to a subpath of `γ`.
- `|γ| = |∂Π| >= girth Θ_i`, so `girth Θ_i < (8/7)|w|`.

**Dehn algorithm.** Given `w`, freely and cyclically reduce it. Compute
`N = max{i : girth Θ_i < (8/7)|w|}`; this is computable since `Θ` and `m` are recursive and girths
increase. Search all `i <= N`, all simple closed paths `γ` in `Θ_i`, and all subpaths `p` of `γ`
with `|p| > |γ|/2` whose label is a cyclic subword of `w`.
- If there is none and `w` is nonempty, output `w ≠ 1`.
- Otherwise replace that subword by the label of `γ ∖ p` and repeat. The free and cyclic reduction
  and the bound `N` are recomputed from the current word in every round (N2).

Each replacement preserves the element (`γ` is a relator) and strictly shortens the word, so the
process halts. It reaches the empty word iff `w = 1`, because the Greendlinger step supplies a
match at every nonempty trivial stage, and `N` only decreases. So `M` has solvable word problem.

**Expanders and coarse embedding.** `Θ` is expanding, so item 1 is Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.3.

**Item 2.** By `fg-groups-have-simple-kazhdan-hosts-of-same-wp-degree`, `M <= K` with `K` infinite,
finitely generated, simple, Kazhdan, and `WP(K) <=_T WP(M)` solvable. The inclusion of a finitely
generated subgroup is a coarse embedding of word metrics: it is Lipschitz, and word balls of `K`
are finite. So a coarse embedding of `K` into Hilbert space would restrict to one of `M`. `∎`
