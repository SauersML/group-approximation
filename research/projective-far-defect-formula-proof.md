**Part 1 (the phase character).**  Write `c(lambda) 1 = U pi(lambda)
U^* pi(lambda)^*`.  For `lambda_1, lambda_2 in Lambda`,

```text
c(lambda_1 lambda_2) 1
 = U pi(lambda_1) [ U^* U ] pi(lambda_2) U^* pi(lambda_2)^* pi(lambda_1)^*
 = U pi(lambda_1) U^* ( c(lambda_2) 1 ) pi(lambda_1)^*
 = c(lambda_1) c(lambda_2) 1,
```

so `c` is a character of `Lambda`.  `SL_n(Z)` is perfect for
`n >= 3` (it is generated by elementary matrices, each a commutator
for `n >= 3`), so `c = 1` there; `SL_2(Z)^ab = Z/12`, so for `n = 2`
the kernel of `c` has index at most `12`.

**Part 2 (the doubled coefficient).**  Let `H = L^2(M, tau)`, let
`conj(H)` be its conjugate Hilbert space, and on `H otimes conj(H)`
define the unitary representation `sigma(g) = Ad pi(g) otimes
conj(Ad pi(g))` and the unit vector `v = U otimes conj(U)`.  Since
`<Ad pi(x) U, U>_H = tau(U^* pi(x) U pi(x)^*) = psi_U(x)`, the
coefficient of `sigma` at `v` is

```text
<sigma(x) v, v> = psi_U(x) conj(psi_U(x)) = phi_U(x),
```

so `phi_U` is positive definite (Gram matrix of the orbit).  From
`(PFD1)`, `pi(lambda) U pi(lambda)^* = conj(c(lambda)) U`, hence

```text
sigma(lambda) v = conj(c(lambda)) U otimes conj( conj(c(lambda)) U )
              = conj(c(lambda)) c(lambda) v = v :
```

the phases cancel in the double and `v` is EXACTLY `Lambda`-fixed.
Consequently `phi_U(lambda g mu) = <sigma(g) sigma(mu) v,
sigma(lambda)^(-1) v> = phi_U(g)` and `phi_U|_Lambda = 1`.  Being
constant on `Lambda`-double cosets, `phi_U` is continuous for the
Hecke topology and extends, exactly as in
`hecke-far-commutator-defect-formula-proof` (Schlichting completion,
dense image, finite central kernel inside `Lambda` acting trivially),
to a `K`-bi-invariant positive definite function on `G`.

**Part 3 (far limit and its identification).**  Let `W` be the closed
`sigma(Gamma)`-cyclic subspace of `v`.  As in the exact case, `W` is
`Gamma`-equivariantly identified with the GNS space of the extended
function, so it carries a continuous `G`-representation extending
`sigma|_Gamma`, and Howe--Moore applies: writing `v = v_0 + v_1` with
`v_0 in W^G`, the coefficient `phi_U` tends to `||v_0||^2` along the
cofinite double-coset filter (a compact subset of `G` meets finitely
many `K`-double cosets).  Under the canonical isometry `H otimes
conj(H) iso HS(H)`, `a otimes conj(b) |-> <., b> a`, the
representation `sigma` becomes `X |-> Ad pi(g) X Ad pi(g)^(-1)` and
`v` becomes the rank-one operator `P_U = |U><U|`; the
`sigma(Gamma)`-invariant vectors are exactly the Hilbert--Schmidt
operators commuting with every `Ad pi(gamma)`, and the orthogonal
projection onto them is `E_2`.  By the von Neumann mean ergodic
theorem `E_2(v)` lies in the closed convex hull of the orbit
`{sigma(gamma) v}`, hence in `W`; since `v - E_2(v)` is orthogonal to
all invariant vectors and `E_2(v)` is invariant, `v_0 = E_2(v)` and
`||v_0||^2 = || E_2(P_U) ||_HS^2 = s_proj`.  This is `(PFD2)`; the
energy form follows from `phi_U = |tau(U^* pi(.) U pi(.)^*)|^2`.

**Part 4 (rigidity of the extreme value).**  If `s_proj = 1` then
`E_2(v) = v` (unit vector), so `sigma(gamma) v = v` for all `gamma`:
`(Ad pi(gamma) U) otimes conj(Ad pi(gamma) U) = U otimes conj(U)`.
Equality of the associated rank-one projections forces
`Ad pi(gamma) U = omega(gamma) U` with `|omega(gamma)| = 1`, and
`omega` is then a character of `Gamma`.  For `n >= 3`,
`SL_n(Z[1/p])` is perfect (elementary generation plus the commutator
identities available from rank two on), so `omega = 1` and `U in
pi(Gamma)' cap M`.  If `s_proj < 1`, then `phi_U(gamma) <=
(1 + s_proj)/2 < 1` outside finitely many double cosets, giving the
uniform lower energy bound.

**Part 5 (doubled Hecke contraction, `n >= 3`).**  The contraction
argument of `hecke-averaging-realizes-commutant-expectation-proof`
Part 2 is abstract: it uses only a continuous unitary
`G`-representation on the cyclic space of a `K`-fixed unit vector,
the Kazhdan pair `(K cup K gamma_0 K, epsilon_0)`, the symmetry of
the double coset, and self-adjointness of the Hecke operator on
`K`-fixed vectors.  All hypotheses hold verbatim for `(W, sigma, v)`,
so the lazy doubled average `S_2` satisfies `|| S_2^N(P_U) -
E_2(P_U) ||_HS <= (1 - delta_0)^N || P_U - E_2(P_U) ||_HS` with the
same representation-independent `delta_0 = epsilon_0^2 / (4L)`.
