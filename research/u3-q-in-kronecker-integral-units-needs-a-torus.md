**ESTABLISHED** through `u3-q-in-kronecker-integral-units-needs-a-torus-proof`
(lane proof, not independently reviewed; elementary, no novelty claimed).

**Setting.** Let `r_1 | r_2 | ...` be positive integers and let
`GL_Kr = colim_k GL_(r_k)(Z)`, the direct limit along the Kronecker maps
`M -> I_(r_(k+1)/r_k) ⊗ M`. Each element of `GL_Kr` has a *level* `k` and a matrix
there; its eigenvalues are those of that matrix and do not depend on the level.

**Example in the Leavitt units.** Fix the configuration chain `Gamma_k` of the
proof route of `integral-leavitt-unit-group-contains-q`, so `r_k = 2·k!`. Then
`M -> u(Gamma_k, M)` identifies `GL_Kr` with a subgroup `Fin_0` of
`<V, t> <= L_Z(1,2)^x`. This is because `u(Gamma, e_ab(1))` is `V`-conjugate to
`t`, permutation matrices lie in `V`, and `GL_r(Z)` is generated by `E_r(Z)` and
the permutation matrices. `Fin_0` contains that node's copy of `(Q,+)`.

**Statement.**
1. **Unipotent roots are bounded.** If `u in GL_Kr` is unipotent and `u != 1`, then
   the `q in Q` for which `exp(q log u)` lies in `GL_Kr` form a subgroup `(1/N)Z`.
   So `u` has unipotent roots in `GL_Kr` of only finitely many orders.
2. **Torsion quotients of `U_3(Q)` kill the centre.** If `K` is normal in `U_3(Q)`
   and `U_3(Q)/K` is torsion, then `K` contains the centre `z^Q`, and
   `U_3(Q)/K` is abelian.
3. **A torus is necessary.** Let `psi: U_3(Q) -> GL_Kr` be injective. Then some
   `psi(g)` has an eigenvalue that is not a root of unity. Equivalently, it is not
   true that every `psi(g)` is quasi-unipotent.

## Consequences for `u3-q-embeds-in-fp-simple-group`

- **Kronecker root towers of transvections are not enough.** Consider towers that
  realize every element of `U_3(Q)` by a root tower of a unipotent seed with
  block-permutation twists, the pattern that gives `(Q,+)` in
  `integral-leavitt-unit-group-contains-q`. They make every element
  quasi-unipotent, so by part 3 they cannot embed `U_3(Q)` in `Fin_0`. The
  obstruction is the centre: by part 2 its image would consist of unipotent
  elements, and by part 1 a unipotent element has only boundedly many unipotent
  roots.
- **The rational model has no integral form.** `g -> lambda(g-bar) ⊗ rho(g)`, where
  `lambda` is the regular representation of `U_3(Q)^ab / (Z^2) = (Q/Z)^2` and
  `rho` is the standard representation, is compatible with the Kronecker maps over
  `Q`. But its centre acts unipotently, so by part 1 no invariant integral
  structure fits the Kronecker maps.
- **What would have to happen.** Some element must act with an eigenvalue that is
  not a root of unity, and the semisimple parts of central elements must lie in the
  torus of the identity component. The algebraic pattern is a *clock and shift*
  pair `X = t·C`, `Y = S`, with `S` a cyclic shift, `C` the matching clock
  (`C S = zeta S C`) and `t` a semisimple scalar of infinite order: then `[X, Y]` is
  the root of unity `zeta`. Whether such pairs fit the Kronecker maps integrally,
  compatibly for all denominators, is open.
- **Outside `Fin_0`.** The theorem says nothing about elements of `<V, t>` or of
  `L_Z(1,2)^x` that are not finite-type (with genuine `V`-dynamics). There the
  induced module `Z[U_3(Q)] ⊗_(Z[H_3(Z)]) Z^3` gives an integral faithful action,
  but its restrictions to the groups `H_(k!)` are not multiples of one lattice.
