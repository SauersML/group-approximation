**Source read 2026-09-13.** R. Meyer, *On the classification of group actions
on C\*-algebras up to equivariant KK-equivalence*, arXiv:1906.11163v3
(2 Nov 2020), Annals of K-Theory 6 (2021). Text was extracted on MSI with
ghostscript `txtwrite`.

**Section 4, setting, for `G` of prime order.** "Thus the equivariant bootstrap
class B^G for G is generated by two objects, namely, C = C(G/G) with the
trivial action of G and C(G) with the translation action of G." "Given a
separable G-C∗-algebra A, its little invariant is defined by
L_*(A) := KK^G_*(C, A) ⊕ KK^G_*(C(G), A) ≅ KK^G_*(C ⊕ C(G), A)."

**Lemma 4.2.** "Let A, B ∈ B^G. Then L_*(A) ≅ 0 if and only if A is
KK^G-equivalent to 0. And f ∈ KK^G_0(A, B) is invertible if and only if
L_*(f): L_*(A) → L_*(B) is invertible."

The proof there is the localising-subcategory argument. The class of `D` with
`KK^G_*(D, C) = 0` is localising and contains `C` and `C(G)`, hence all of
`B^G`. Right after it the text says "Next we relate L_*(A) to K-theory. We use
the Green–Julg isomorphism KK^G_*(C, A) ≅ KK_*(C, A⋊G) ..., the
induction–restriction adjunction KK^G(C(G), A) ≅ KK_*(C, A)".
