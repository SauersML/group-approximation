**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof below, elementary apart from one imported theorem; not reviewed). No
priority claimed: the contraction argument is Grigorchuk's.

## Setup

`T` is the binary rooted tree, `a(0x) = 1x`, `a(1x) = 0x`. For `ω ∈ {0,1,2}^N` and a generator
`s ∈ {b,c,d}`, let `u^s_i ∈ {1, a}` be the usual table: `u^s_i = 1` for exactly one `i ∈ {0,1,2}`,
a different `i` for each of `b, c, d`. Then

    s_ω(0x) = 0 u^s_(ω_0)(x),        s_ω(1x) = 1 s_(σω)(x),

where `σ` is the shift, and `G_ω = ⟨a, b_ω, c_ω, d_ω⟩`. Equivalently, `s_ω` flips bit `k+1` of
every point `1^k 0 y` with `u^s_(ω_k) = a`, and fixes everything else.

## Statement

Let `ω` be eventually periodic, so `Ω = {σ^n ω : n >= 0}` is finite. Put

    K_ω = ⟨ a, b_η, c_η, d_η : η ∈ Ω ⟩ <= Aut(T).

Then `K_ω` is finitely generated, self-similar and contracting. So `G_ω <= K_ω` embeds in a finitely
presented simple group (Belk–Matucci, arXiv:2405.10234, Theorem 1: "Every contracting self-similar
group embeds into a finitely presented simple group"; statement as recorded in the bh-lit survey
`research/artifacts/gq-bh-bh-lit-survey.md`, proof not re-read). Hence every eventually periodic
Grigorchuk group satisfies Boone–Higman.

## Proof

1. **Self-similar.** The sections of `a` at `0, 1` are trivial. The sections of `s_η` are
   `u^s_(η_0) ∈ {1, a}` at `0` and `s_(ση)` at `1`, and `ση ∈ Ω`. Sections of products are products
   of sections, so every section of every element of `K_ω` lies in `K_ω`.
2. **The group `D`.** Every `s_η` has the form `f_A`: flip bit `k+1` on `1^k 0 y` for `k ∈ A`, identity
   elsewhere. Any two such maps commute and square to `1`. So `D = ⟨ s_η : s ∈ {b,c,d}, η ∈ Ω⟩` is a
   finite elementary abelian 2-group, of order at most `2^(3|Ω|)`. It is closed under sections:
   `f_A` has sections `1` or `a` at `0` and `f_(A-1)` at `1`, with `A - 1 = {k-1 : k ∈ A, k >= 1}`.
3. **Normal form.** Every element of `K_ω` is a word alternating between `a` and elements of
   `D \ {1}`. Length `ℓ(g)` is the least number of letters.
4. **Contraction.** Let `g` be alternating of length `n`. It has at most `⌈n/2⌉` letters from `D`,
   and `a` contributes only a swap of subtrees. Each `D`-letter contributes one letter (`1`, `a`, or
   an element of `D`) to each of `g|_0` and `g|_1`, so `ℓ(g|_x) <= ⌈n/2⌉`. This is `< n` for `n >= 2`.
   Iterating, every section at depth `>= log_2(ℓ(g)) + 2` has length `<= 1`, so it lies in the finite
   set `N = {1, a} ∪ D`. So `K_ω` is contracting, with nucleus contained in `N`.
5. `G_ω` is generated by `a, b_ω, c_ω, d_ω ∈ K_ω`.

## Scope

- This covers the first Grigorchuk group, `ω = (012)^∞`, which Röver had already placed in a
  finitely presented simple group, and every other eventually periodic `ω`.
- For `ω` not eventually periodic, `D` is infinite and `K_ω` is not finitely generated, so the argument
  stops. `grigorchuk-groups-lie-in-one-point-germ-extensions-of-v` records the natural host there and
  why its obvious finiteness certificate fails.
