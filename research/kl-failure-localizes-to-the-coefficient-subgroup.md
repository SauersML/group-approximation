Let `w in G * <t>` with `deg_t(w) = m != 0`, let `A <= G` be the subgroup
generated by the finitely many coefficients occurring in `w`, and let

    A_w  =  (A * <t>)/<<w>>_{A*<t>} .

If `A -> A_w` is injective then `G -> G_w` is injective.  Equivalently: if
`(G, w)` violates `kervaire-laudenbach-nonsingular-conjecture` then so does
`(A, w)`, and `A` is finitely generated.

The mechanism is the pushout description `G_w = G *_A A_w`, which comes from
`G * <t> = G *_A (A * <t>)` followed by imposing `w = 1` inside the second
factor.  When `A -> A_w` is injective this is an ordinary amalgamated free
product and the normal form theorem embeds `G`.

## Why it is worth a node of its own

It is the step that turns a statement quantified over all groups into a
statement about finitely generated ones without any coding, embedding or
recursion-theoretic apparatus — those enter only at the next step,
`kl-counterexample-can-be-finitely-presented`.  It is also the reason the
whole reduction chain is available to the falsification lane: a violating
equation over any group is, verbatim, a violating equation over the finitely
generated subgroup its own coefficients generate.
