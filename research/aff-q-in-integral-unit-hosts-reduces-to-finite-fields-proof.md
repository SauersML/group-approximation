Lane proof, not independently reviewed. Notation as in the target.

**Part 1.** By part 1 of `integral-unit-hosts-for-gl-n-q-reduce-to-finite-fields`
there are:
- a finitely generated subring `D ⊆ K`, the ring generated by the basis coordinates
  of the generators of `S` and their inverses, with `S <= GL_m(A_D)`;
- for each maximal ideal `𝔪` of `D`, a ring homomorphism
  `rho_𝔪: A_D -> A ⊗ D/𝔪`, where `D/𝔪` is a finite field,

such that `∩_𝔪 ker(rho_𝔪|S) = 1`.

Let `h in H` be the translation `t_1`. Since `h != 1`, some `𝔪` has
`rho_𝔪(h) != 1`. By `gl-n-q-embeddings-detected-by-nontrivial-homomorphisms`,
part 3, a homomorphism from `Aff(Q)` is injective as soon as it does not kill
`t_1`. So `rho_𝔪|H` is injective. ∎

**Part 2.** Apply part 1 with `A = L_Z(1,2)` and `m = 1`. `L_Z(1,2)` is free abelian
on its normal-form monomials, by the diamond lemma over `Z`, as in part 0 of the
proof route of `integral-unit-hosts-for-gl-n-q-reduce-to-finite-fields`. Also
`L_Z(1,2) ⊗ K = L_K(1,2)`. This gives `Aff(Q) <= L_F(1,2)^x` for a finite field
`F = F_(p^r)`.

The regular representation `F -> M_r(F_p)` gives a unital injection
`L_F(1,2) = F ⊗_(F_p) L_(F_p)(1,2) -> M_r(F_p) ⊗ L_(F_p)(1,2) = M_r(L_(F_p)(1,2))`.
It is injective because tensor products over a field are exact. Also
`M_r(L_(F_p)(1,2)) ≅ L_(F_p)(1,2)`, since `L ≅ L^2`, hence `L ≅ L^r`, as modules.
So `Aff(Q) <= L_(F_p)(1,2)^x`, and its translation subgroup is a copy of `(Q,+)`
there.

In `Aff(Q)`, `t_1` has infinite order and roots `t_(1/k)` of every order. Over the
finite field `F_p`, every unit of infinite order is transcendental, so
`unit-roots-divide-centralizer-rank`, parts 2 and 3, give the image of `t_1`
infinite centralizer rank. ∎

**Part 3.** Let `d in S` with `d t d^(-1) = t^p`, and suppose `p` is not a unit of
`D`.
- Then `pD` is a proper ideal, so it lies in a maximal ideal `𝔪`, and `D/𝔪` is a
  finite field of characteristic `p`.
- `rho_𝔪` maps `x_01 y_00` to the normal-form monomial `x_01 y_00` of
  `L_(D/𝔪)(1,2)`, which is nonzero. Since `(x_01 y_00)^2 = 0`, the element
  `rho_𝔪(t) = 1 + x_01 y_00` has order exactly `p`.
- Applying `rho_𝔪` to `d t d^(-1) = t^p` gives
  `rho_𝔪(d) rho_𝔪(t) rho_𝔪(d)^(-1) = rho_𝔪(t)^p = 1`, so `rho_𝔪(t) = 1`. This is
  a contradiction.

So `p` is a unit of `D`. `D` is a finitely generated domain of characteristic 0.
By Step 2 of `fg-subrings-of-integral-form-algebras-omit-q-proof`, applied to the
element `1`, we get `1 in pD` for only finitely many primes `p`.

For the first consequence, a family `d_p` for infinitely many primes would make
infinitely many primes units of `D`.

For the second, suppose `H <= S` is a copy of `Aff(Q)` and `t` is the translation
by some `c != 0`. For each prime `p`, the dilation `x ↦ p x` in `H` conjugates
translation by `c` to translation by `p c`, which is `t^p`. This gives a family
`d_p` for all primes.

**Sharpness.** Take `Gamma_1 = (00, 01)` and `T_1 = [[1,0],[1,1]]`, so
`t = u(Gamma_1, T_1)`. The map `u(Gamma_1, ·)` of the proof route of
`integral-leavitt-unit-group-contains-q` is a homomorphism on `GL_2(Z[1/p])` into
`L_(Z[1/p])(1,2)^x`, by the same computation as there. Then
`diag(1,p) T_1 diag(1,p)^(-1) = [[1,0],[p,1]] = T_1^p`. ∎

**Part 4.** Let `Gamma = (g_1, g_2, g_3)` be three pairwise incomparable words
with nonempty complement, and use `u(Gamma, ·)` from the proof route of
`integral-leavitt-unit-group-contains-q`.
- For `a != b`, the element `u(Gamma, e_ab(1))` is the transvection from cone `g_b`
  to cone `g_a`, and the identity elsewhere. It equals `u((g_b, g_a), e_21(1))`,
  which is `V`-conjugate to `t` (Step 3 there). So it lies in `G = <V, t>`.
- `u(Gamma, ·)` is a homomorphism on `GL_3(Z)`, and
  `[e_23(1), e_31(1)] = e_21(1)`. So `u(Gamma, e_21(1))` lies in `G'`, and hence so
  does its `V`-conjugate `t`, since `G'` is normal in `G`.

Reduction modulo `p` is a ring homomorphism `rho_p: L_Z(1,2) -> L_(F_p)(1,2)`.
- `rho_p(t^p) = 1 + p x_01 y_00 = 1`, while `t^p = 1 + p x_01 y_00 != 1` because
  `L_Z(1,2)` is torsion-free.
- `rho_p(t) != 1`.

So `ker(rho_p) ∩ G'` is a normal subgroup of `G'` that contains `t^p` and misses
`t`: it is proper and nontrivial.

For the embedding into `∏_p L_(F_p)(1,2)^x`, take `g != h` in `G`. Then `g - h` is
a nonzero element of the free abelian group `L_Z(1,2)`, so it has a nonzero
coordinate `c in Z`. For any prime `p` not dividing `c`, `rho_p(g) != rho_p(h)`. ∎
