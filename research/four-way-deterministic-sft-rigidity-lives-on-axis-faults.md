## Setting

`Y ⊆ A^(Z^2)` is a nearest-neighbour SFT that is **four-way deterministic**: `y(z)` is determined by
the pair `(y(z+u), y(z+u'))` for each of the corner pairs
`(u, u') ∈ {(e_1,e_2), (e_2,−e_1), (−e_1,−e_2), (−e_2,e_1)}`. Example: Wang tile sets in which any two
adjacent edges determine the tile (Kari–Papasoglu; Lukkarila; Le Gloannec–Ollinger for `2×2`
substitutions). Quantum families (Q1)–(Q3) and the closure `C` are as in
`determinism-closure-certifies-quantum-rigidity`, with sup-norm balls `B_R` and `D >= 1`.

## Theorem

1. **One anti-diagonal carries everything.** Let `𝒟_n` be the algebra generated by the `P_a(z)` with
   `z_1 + z_2 = n`. In every `D`-family, `𝒟_n = 𝒟_0` for all `n`. So `Y` is `D`-rigid iff the
   generators on one anti-diagonal pairwise commute. The same holds for one main diagonal.
2. **Only the axes can be non-expansive.** Every line of nonzero finite slope is expansive: a wide
   strip along it determines the configuration. If `Y` is infinite, the horizontal or the vertical
   direction is non-expansive (Boyle–Lind, recalled). Two points that agree on the half-plane below a
   row and differ above it differ on the **whole** next row.
3. **Certificate: one-sided axis rules.** Suppose there are `ρ` and two rules:
   - a row rule `F_h ⊆ [−ρ,ρ] × [1,ρ]` (or its negative): the band above determines the row below;
   - a column rule `F_v ⊆ [1,ρ] × [−ρ,ρ]` (or its negative).

   Then `C = Z^2` for `D >= ρ`, so `Y` is `D`-quantum rigid over every field and `LC(Y,k) ⋊ Z^2` is
   finitely presented. Only the NE and SE corner rules are used. By compactness, the row rule exists
   iff every horizontal band of some fixed width determines the adjacent row on one **fixed** side.
4. **Kill: two-sided band freedom.** Suppose that, for arbitrarily large `w`, some horizontal (or
   vertical) band state of width `w` has two different extensions above and two different extensions
   below. Then `Y` is not `D`-quantum rigid for any `D`.
5. **Robinson-type tile sets fall between 3 and 4 (heuristic).** In a Robinson-type hierarchical
   tiling:
   - a band away from faults sees supertile boundaries of every order crossing it, so it determines
     the odometer coordinates and hence the tiling, and 4 never fires;
   - a band next to an infinite horizontal fault, but not containing it, does not see the choice on
     the fault row, such as its arrow direction. So the free side switches from below to above
     across the fault, and 3 fails;
   - the two points of a fault fibre differ on one row, so `e_2` (and `e_1`) is a fault normal. By
     item 3 of `determinism-closure-certifies-quantum-rigidity`, no determinism argument in any
     recoding proves such a tile set rigid.

   So Kari–Papasoglu's rigidity, and with it Corollary (b) of `a2-busemann-transplants-preserve-quantum-rigidity`,
   is a **contextual** question located on the fault lines, like Labbé's
   (`labbe-shift-is-quantum-rigid-iff-rigid-at-scale-two`).

## Proof

**Item 1.** Lemma 1 of `determinism-closure-certifies-quantum-rigidity`, with the NE rule `{e_1, e_2}`
(inside a `D`-ball for `D >= 1`), gives
`P_a(z) = Σ_(p ∈ L(a)) P_(p_1)(z+e_1) P_(p_2)(z+e_2)`. So `𝒟_n ⊆ 𝒟_(n+1)`. The SW rule gives
`𝒟_(n+1) ⊆ 𝒟_n`. The NW and SE rules give the same for main diagonals.

**Item 2.** Let `S = {c <= ⟨t, ν⟩ <= c + w}` with `ν = (m, 1)`, `m > 0`, and `w >= m`. If `⟨z, ν⟩ = c − 1`,
then `z + e_2` and `z + e_1` lie in `S`, so the NE rule determines `z`. Inducting layer by layer
determines the side below. The SW rule determines the side above. For `ν = (−m, 1)` use the NW and SE
rules. Boyle–Lind supplies a non-expansive direction for infinite `Y`.

For the last claim, let the two points agree on row `j` and let `r, r'` be their rows `j + 1`. The SW
rule (west and south neighbours) makes `r(i+1)` a function of `r(i)` and the letter at `(i+1, j)`. The
SE rule (east and south neighbours) makes `r(i−1)` a function of `r(i)` and the letter at `(i−1, j)`. So agreement at one position propagates along the
whole row.

**Item 3.** Suppose `B_R ⊆ C` with `R >= 2D >= 2ρ`. Rule (z) means: if `v − F ⊆ C` for a rule `F`, then
`v ∈ C`.
- **Axis segments.** `v = (R+1, y)` with `|y| <= R − ρ` has `v − F_v ⊆ B_R`. `v = (x, R+1)` with
  `|x| <= R − ρ` has `v − F_h ⊆ B_R`. By `C = −C` the opposite segments are added too. For the negated
  rules use rule (w) instead.
- **NE corner, by rule (z) with `{e_1, e_2}`.** First climb the column `x = R+1` from `y = R−ρ+1` to
  `R`: here `v − e_1 ∈ B_R` and `v − e_2` was just added. Then run along the row `y = R+1` from
  `x = R−ρ+1` to `R+1`.
- **SE corner, by rule (z) with the SE rule `{e_1, −e_2}`.** It needs `v − e_1` and `v + e_2`.
  - Descend the column `x = R+1` from `y = −(R−ρ+1)` to `−(R+1)`.
  - Run along the row `y = −(R+1)` from `x = R−ρ+1` to `R`. It starts from the bottom segment given
    by `C = −C`.
- **NW and SW corners.** These are the negatives of the SE and NE corners.

So `B_(R+1) ⊆ C`. Starting from `B_(2D)`, induction gives `C = Z^2`.

For the compactness claim: if every band state on rows `[1, w]` has a unique extension to row `0`, the
letter at `(0,0)` is a continuous function on a compact space. It therefore depends on finitely many
coordinates, which gives `F_h`.

**Item 4.** This is Corollary (a) of `quantum-rigid-sfts-over-products-have-a-single-free-branch`
(`H = Z`). In a `D`-rigid shift, every slab state of width `w >= 2D` has all its extensions agreeing
outside one side. ∎

## What this decides and what it leaves

- **No finite computation decides Kari–Papasoglu here.** Three things are missing:
  - its tile set was not available;
  - rigidity is at best semi-decidable in each direction: ideal membership certifies it, and a
    non-commuting finite-dimensional periodic family refutes it;
  - item 5 predicts that the first-order tests of items 3 and 4 are both silent.

  So no MSI job was run.
- **A scale-collapse pointer.** If the Kari–Papasoglu or Le Gloannec–Ollinger tile sets satisfy the
  hypotheses of `recognizable-morphisms-collapse-quantum-rigidity-to-one-scale`, their rigidity is one
  finitely presented algebra, as for Labbé. This was not checked.
- **Superseded as a construction target.** Co-location (`crossing-wire-fixed-point-tile-sets-are-quantum-rigid`)
  certifies hierarchical sets contextually, with faults handled by side-local margins. So neither
  four-way determinism nor one-sided axis rules are needed: `sw-crossing-wire-tiles-give-free-rigid-sfts-on-kazhdan-lattices`
  gets transplantable free rigid fibres from one-way flow. The determinism-only route below stays a
  second, independent option.
- **Determinism-only construction target.** Build a strongly aperiodic
  four-way deterministic SFT whose axis faults are **one-sided**: every horizontal band determines the
  row below, and every vertical band determines the column to its left. By item 3 it is rigid; it is
  free and NE-deterministic. So by `tree-product-busemann-transplants-preserve-quantum-rigidity` and
  `a2-busemann-transplants-preserve-quantum-rigidity`, it yields free quantum-rigid SFTs over `F_m × F_n`,
  every BMW group and every Kazhdan Ã2 lattice of that Setting.
  - *Sufficient substitution design (heuristic).*
    - Take a `2×2` substitution whose level-`n` supertiles are determined by their top rows and by
      their right columns.
    - Across infinite faults, the upper supertile row should determine the lower one, and the right
      column the left.
    - Realise it by a four-way deterministic tile set whose decoration layers add no fault choices of
      their own. A fault-row arrow, for example, must be forced from above.
  - *Obstruction to keep in mind.* The row shift of such a `Y` has no periodic points, since a
    `σ^a`-periodic row would generate a periodic point through the total row map. So it is not sofic.
    This is consistent: row shifts of SFTs need only be effective.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Determinism moves the rigidity question to the axis fault lines.** In four-way deterministic
  shifts the corner rules cover every non-axis direction, and one anti-diagonal generates everything.
- **A clean dichotomy on those lines.** One-sided faults are certified by determinism, and two-sided
  faults are killed.
- **Hierarchical tile sets such as Robinson and Kari–Papasoglu sit in the mixed case.** There, a
  contextual proof on the fault line is unavoidable.
- **Where G2 should aim.** Aim at a *design* rather than at analysing existing sets. The co-location
  design (complete crossings, one-way flow) is the one carried out in
  `sw-crossing-wire-tiles-give-free-rigid-sfts-on-kazhdan-lattices`.
