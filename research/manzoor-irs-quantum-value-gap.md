There exists a finite nonlocal game `G` for which

```text
omega_IRS(G) > omega*(G).                              (MIG1)
```

Here an IRS strategy is the stabilizer IRS of a probability-measure-preserving
action of the free product generated by the game's involutive answer-bit
variables and a free central involution `J`; its correlation is read from the
negative `J` sector.  Finite permutation strategies are special IRS strategies.

For every game, Manzoor's invariant-random-pseudo-subgroup linear programs give
computable decreasing upper bounds on `omega_IRS`, while exhaustive rational
finite-dimensional strategies give computable increasing lower bounds on
`omega*`.  If `(MIG1)` failed for every game, these two semidecision procedures,
combined with TailoredMIP*=RE, would decide the halting problem.
