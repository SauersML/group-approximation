Let `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)`, and let `H <= GL_n(K)` be generated by finitely many
matrices, their inverses included.

**Step 0, trivial case.** If `K` is algebraic over `F_2`, the entries lie in a
finite field, `H` is finite, and it embeds in `R^x` through `GL_N(F_2) <= GL_N(R)`.

**Step 1, reduce to F_2(t).** Otherwise the subfield generated by the entries
is a finite extension `K_0` of some `F_2(t)`, of degree `m`. The regular
representation `K_0 -> M_m(F_2(t))` is a unital ring embedding, so
`H <= GL_(nm)(F_2(t))`.

**Step 2, clear denominators.** Let `f in F_2[t]` be the product of `t` and all
denominators of the finitely many entries. Then `H <= GL_(nm)(F_2[t, 1/f])`,
because this ring contains every generator entry and is closed under products.

**Step 3, realize F_2[t,1/f] over Laurent polynomials.** Put `d = deg f >= 1`.
The map `F_2[g,g^-1][T] -> F_2[t,1/f]`, `T |-> t`, `g |-> f(t)` is onto, since
`f(t)^-1` is the image of `g^-1`, and its kernel is `(f(T) - g)`: substituting
`g = f(T)` identifies the quotient with `F_2[T][f(T)^-1]`. Since `f` is monic,
`F_2[t,1/f] ≅ F_2[g^±][T]/(f(T) - g)` is a free `F_2[g^±]`-module with basis
`1, T, ..., T^(d-1)`. Multiplication therefore gives a unital injective ring map
`F_2[t,1/f] -> M_d(F_2[g,g^-1])`, and `H <= GL_(nmd)(F_2[g,g^-1])`.

**Step 4, into the Leavitt algebra.** `char-two-laurent-linear-groups-proof`
constructs a unital injection `F_2[g,g^-1] -> R`, `g |->` the Thompson unit
`s_0 t_0 t_0 + s_1 s_0 t_1 t_0 + s_1 s_1 t_1`. Its image `A` is a unital
subalgebra, so `H <= GL_(nmd)(A)`, and
`leavitt-subalgebra-linear-groups-satisfy-boone-higman` embeds `H` in the
finitely presented simple group `R^x`. `∎`
