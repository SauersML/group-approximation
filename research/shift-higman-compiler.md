Let `P` be finitely presented with decidable word problem.  Then a bijection

    nu : P -> N

can be chosen so that the Mallery--Zaremsky envelope `E_nu(P)` -- the
subgroup of `Sym(N)` generated by the `nu`-transport of the left regular
representation of `P` onto the even numbers together with the eventually
2-periodic permutations -- is **finitely presented**.

The notation `E_nu(P)` is local to this graph; see
`mallery-zaremsky-shift-similar-envelope`.

## Why this single statement closes the conjecture

`boone-higman-via-shift-higman-compiler`.  In one line: Clapham makes the
input finitely presented and decidable, this statement makes the envelope
finitely presented, the envelope is strongly shift-similar by construction,
Zaremsky's Proposition 5.3 turns that into a type (A) action, and Corollary B
carries the conclusion down to every subgroup -- in particular to the group
one started with.

Everything except this statement is a theorem in print.  That is the entire
content of the reduction: the Boone--Higman conjecture is equivalent to, or
at least implied by, a finite-presentability question about one explicit
family of permutation groups, with no simple group, no normal lattice and no
ring anywhere in it.

## Decidability is not decoration

By `compilers-cannot-drop-the-decidability-hypothesis` the statement is
**false** without its hypothesis: for a finitely presented `P` with
unsolvable word problem -- one exists by
`novikov-boone-fp-group-undecidable-word-problem` -- no `nu` makes `E_nu(P)`
finitely presented.  So any proof must use the word-problem algorithm for
`P` to choose `nu`, and a proof that only reads a finite presentation of `P`
cannot exist.  This is the strongest structural information available about
what a proof has to look like, and it is what distinguishes the statement
from the many finite-presentability arguments that are uniform in the
presentation.

## Attempts

1. **Choose `nu` to make the germ maps `psi_j` eventually periodic.**  The
   point stabilizers of the natural action are already finitely generated
   for any strongly shift-similar group; what a finite presentation needs is
   a finite set of relations among the `psi_j`-conjugates, and the natural
   attempt is to use the word-problem algorithm to enumerate `P` in an order
   that makes the germ structure repeat.  Deferred at an identified step: no
   argument was found that bounds the relators, and the difficulty is that
   the eventually 2-periodic part `E_2` is infinitely related in the obvious
   generating set unless the transported copy of `P` interacts with it.
2. **Drop the decidability hypothesis and quantify over finitely presented
   `P`.**  *Dies* at `compilers-cannot-drop-the-decidability-hypothesis`;
   recorded as `boone-higman-via-hypothesis-free-shift-compiler`.
3. **Replace `E_2` by a larger finitary-plus-shift group to buy relations.**
   Deferred, not attempted in depth: any enlargement must preserve strong
   shift-similarity, which is what Proposition 5.3 consumes, and the source's
   construction is already the minimal enlargement that achieves it.
4. **Deduce it from finite presentability of the Houghton-like group
   `H_n(E_nu(P))`.**  Not pursued: the Houghton-like groups of
   Mallery--Zaremsky are the objects that motivate shift-similarity, but
   their finiteness properties are stated in terms of the shift-similar
   group's, so the implication runs the wrong way.
