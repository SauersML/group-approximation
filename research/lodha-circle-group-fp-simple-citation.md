Citation import. Y. Lodha, *A finitely presented infinite simple group of homeomorphisms of
the circle*, arXiv:1710.06220v2. It was read from the TeX source `FPSimple.tex`, fetched
into the lane scratchpad on 2026-09-16. Line numbers refer to that file.

**Source.**
- **Part 1.** l.89--92, Theorem 1.1: "The group $S$ satisfies the following: ... $S$ is a
  finitely presented, infinite, simple group."
- **Part 2.**
  - l.109: "Our group $S$ is generated by $T$ together with the following piecewise
    projective homeomorphism of the circle". The map is `s(t) = t` for `t ≤ 0`,
    `2t/(1+t)` on `[0,1]`, `2/(3−t)` on `[1,2]` and `t` for `t ≥ 2` (l.110--117).
  - l.141--143: "The group $F$ is the group of piecewise $PSL_2(\mathbf{Z})$ homeomorphisms
    of the real line with breakpoints in the set $\mathbf{Q}$. This is generated by the
    following:" with `a(t) = t + 1`, and `b(t) = t` for `t ≤ 0`, `t/(1−t)` on `[0,1/2]`,
    `(3t−1)/t` on `[1/2,1]` and `t + 1` for `t ≥ 1` (l.144--153).
  - l.155--157: "The group $T$ is the group of piecewise $PSL_2(\mathbf{Z})$
    homeomorphisms of $\mathbf{S}^1=\mathbf{R}\cup \{\infty\}$ with breakpoints in the set
    $\mathbf{Q}\cup \{\infty\}$. This is generated by $F$ together with the involution:
    $$l(t)=-\frac{1}{t}$$"
- **Part 3.**
  - l.327--344 define `φ(0ξ) = 1/(1 + 1/φ(ξ))`, `φ(1ξ) = 1 + φ(ξ)`,
    `Φ(0ξ) = −φ(~ξ)`, `Φ(1ξ) = φ(ξ)`. The primitive maps (l.347--362) are
    `x: 00η ↦ 0η, 01η ↦ 10η, 1η ↦ 11η` and
    `y: 00η ↦ 0 y(η), 01η ↦ 10 y^{-1}(η), 1η ↦ 11 y(η)`. The localized maps `x_s`, `y_s`
    are defined at l.364--385. The maps `p_n` are defined at l.275--289.
  - l.388--395: "Next, we have the following result (Proposition 3.1 in \cite{LodhaMoore}):
    ... For all $\xi$ in $2^\mathbf{N}$ we have
    $\Phi(\xi)\cdot a=\Phi(\xi\cdot x)\qquad \Phi(\xi)\cdot b=\Phi(\xi \cdot x_{\mathtt1})
    \qquad \Phi(\xi)\cdot c=\Phi(\xi\cdot y_{\mathtt{10}})$".
  - l.402--405: "For all $\xi\in 2^{\mathbf{N}}$ we have:
    $$\Phi(\xi)\cdot {\bf s}=\Phi(\xi\cdot (y_{10}y_{110}^{-1}))$$"
  - l.407--411: "Finally, it is an easy exercise to show the following: ...
    $\Phi(\xi)\cdot l=\Phi(\xi\cdot p_0)$".
  - l.413: "It follows that the group $S$ is generated by $x,x_1,p_0, y_{10}y_{110}^{-1}$."

**Scope.** Only the statements are imported. The intertwining for `a` and `b` is quoted by
Lodha from Lodha--Moore; the one for `s` is derived in two lines at l.397--401; the one for
`l` is left as an exercise. None is re-proved here.
