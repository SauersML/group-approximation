Lane proof from classical PI theory, not independently reviewed. Let `A` be generated by
`a_1..a_r` and satisfy a polynomial identity of degree `2m`.

1. **Semiprime means subdirect product of primes.** The prime radical of `A` is the
   intersection of all prime ideals, and it is `0` because `A` is semiprime. So
   `A -> Π_P A/P` is injective, the product running over all prime ideals `P`.
2. **Each prime quotient is in bounded matrices.** `A/P` is a prime PI ring satisfying the
   same identity.
   - By Posner's theorem, in the form of Formanek and Rowen, its centre `Z_P` is a domain
     whose nonzero elements are regular. The central localization
     `Q_P = (A/P) ⊗_(Z_P) F_P`, with `F_P = Frac(Z_P)`, is a central simple `F_P`-algebra of
     dimension `n_P^2`, and `A/P -> Q_P` is injective.
   - By the Amitsur--Levitzki and Kaplansky bound, a central simple algebra satisfying an
     identity of degree `2m` has `n_P <= m`.
   - Over an algebraic closure, `Q_P ⊗ F̄_P ≅ M_(n_P)(F̄_P)`. So `A/P` embeds unitally in
     `M_(n_P)(F̄_P)`.
3. **One matrix size.** Let `N = m!`. Each `n_P` divides `N`, and `x ↦ diag(x, ..., x)`, with
   `N/n_P` blocks, is a unital embedding `M_(n_P)(F̄_P) -> M_N(F̄_P)`. So
   `A -> Π_P M_N(F̄_P) = M_N(C)`, with `C = Π_P F̄_P` a commutative `Q`-algebra, and the
   map is unital and injective by step 1.
4. **Finitely many entries.** Let `C_0 ⊆ C` be the unital `Q`-subalgebra generated by the
   finitely many matrix entries of the images of `a_1..a_r`. It is a finitely generated
   commutative `Q`-algebra, and `A ⊆ M_N(C_0)`, which is part 1.
5. **The host.** By `char-zero-representable-rings-embed-in-fp-simple-rings` with `C = C_0`,
   `D = N` and `m = 1` (via `M_N(C_0) ⊆ M_N(C_0 ⊗ Q<x_1>)`), `A` embeds in a simple ring finitely presented over `Z`. That is
   part 2.

**GK dimension at most one.** A finitely generated algebra of GK dimension `1` is PI by
Small--Stafford--Warfield (1985). GK dimension `0` means finite dimension over `Q`, and the
regular representation embeds such an algebra in `M_e(Q)`. ∎
