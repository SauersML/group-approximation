**Residual finiteness of the packet.**  Let `(rho_j : A -> M_(d_j)(k_j))` be
finite-dimensional representations separating `A`.  Applying them entrywise
gives an injective ring map `M_n(A) -> prod_j M_(n d_j)(k_j)`, hence an
injective group map `GL_n(A) -> prod_j GL_(n d_j)(k_j)`.  A finitely generated
subgroup `K <= GL_n(A)` has image generated by finitely many matrices in each
factor, so each coordinate image is a finitely generated linear group, and is
residually finite by Malcev.  A nontrivial `g in K` is detected by one
coordinate and then by a finite quotient of that coordinate image.  Thus `K` is
residually finite; since each finite window lies in a finitely generated
subgroup, `GL_n(A)` is locally residually finite, hence LEF.

**Roots.**  The subgroup `K` generated by the coefficients of `w` is countable
and LEF, hence operator MF by `lef-implies-operator-mf`.  By
`kervaire-laudenbach-holds-for-mf`, `K -> (K * <t>)/<<w>>` is injective.  By
`kl-failure-localizes-to-the-coefficient-subgroup`, so is the coefficient map
of `R^x`.

**The instances.**  In the Leavitt normal form the monomials `s_mu` (empty
`t`-part) are distinct basis elements, so `A_s` is the free algebra on
`s_0, s_1`, and symmetrically for `A_t`.  A free algebra is residually
finite-dimensional: for a nonzero `f` of degree at most `D`, left
multiplication on `F_2<X>/I_D`, where `I_D` is spanned by the monomials of
length greater than `D`, is a finite-dimensional representation not killing
`f`.  Finitely generated commutative algebras are residually
finite-dimensional by the Krull intersection theorem applied at maximal
ideals, and finite algebras are trivially so.

**The chart obstruction.**  If `x y = 1` in `A` then `rho(y) rho(x) = 1` in
every finite-dimensional representation, so a residually finite-dimensional
`A` is directly finite.  Hence an entry algebra containing `t_0` and `s_0`
is never residually finite-dimensional, which is the contrapositive stated on
the claim.
