**The coding.** Lodha's map `Φ: 2^N → R ∪ {∞}` (arXiv:1710.06220v2, §2.4, read from the PDF;
recorded in `lodha-circle-group-is-finitely-presented-and-simple`, part 3) is defined by
`Φ(0ξ) = -φ(ξ̃)` and `Φ(1ξ) = φ(ξ)`, with `φ(0ξ) = φ(ξ)/(1+φ(ξ))` and `φ(1ξ) = 1 + φ(ξ)`.

1. **Continued fractions.** From the two rules, `φ(1^a ξ) = a + φ(ξ)` and
   `φ(0^b ξ) = 1/(b + 1/φ(ξ))`. So
   `φ(1^{a_0} 0^{a_1} 1^{a_2} ⋯) = [a_0; a_1, a_2, …]`.
   - A sequence that is not eventually constant is sent to an irrational. Its run lengths are its
     continued fraction digits.
   - By Lagrange's theorem, `φ(ξ)` is a quadratic irrational iff `ξ` is eventually periodic and not
     eventually constant.
   - The flip `ξ ↦ ξ̃` preserves eventual periodicity, and `x ↦ -x` preserves quadratic
     irrationality. So `Φ^{-1}` of the real quadratic irrationals is the set `Ω` of eventually
     periodic sequences that are not eventually constant. Each such sequence has primitive period
     length `>= 2`.
   - `Φ` is order-preserving (lexicographic order), and it is injective on irrationals.
2. **`T` is prefix replacement.** Lodha's `T`, the piecewise `PSL_2(Z)` group with breakpoints in
   `Q ∪ {∞}`, is generated by `a, b, l`. It corresponds under `Φ` to the Cantor-model `T`, generated
   by `x, x_1, p_0` (his Props 2.3 and 2.5). So every `γ ∈ PSL_2(Z) ≤ T` lifts through `Φ` to a
   homeomorphism of `2^N` that is locally a prefix replacement everywhere.
3. **Lifting `PP_Z`.** Let `g ∈ PP_Z`, with pieces `γ_i ∈ PSL_2(Z)` on the arcs between its
   breakpoints.
   - On the `Φ`-preimage of each closed arc with rational endpoints, `g` lifts as the `T`-lift of
     `γ_i`. At a rational breakpoint the two sides are distinct points of `2^N`, so no gluing is
     needed there.
   - At a quadratic irrational breakpoint `ξ`, with single preimage `p`, the two lifts extend
     continuously to `p`.
   - This gives the unique homeomorphism `ĝ` with `Φĝ = gΦ`, and `g ↦ ĝ` is an injective
     homomorphism. Uniqueness holds because `Φ` is injective on the dense set of irrationals.
4. **Germs at quadratic breakpoints.** Let `p = uv^∞ ∈ Ω` lie over a breakpoint `ξ`, with left
   piece `γ_-` and right piece `γ_+`, and `γ_-(ξ) = γ_+(ξ)`.
   - The element `h = γ_-^{-1}γ_+ ∈ PSL_2(Z)` fixes `ξ`. By step 2, its lift near `p` is one prefix
     replacement `uv^K ↦ uv^{K+m}`, a diagonal shift.
   - Since `Φ` is order-preserving, the left side of `ξ` corresponds to the side cones
     `D_{k,j}(p)` with `v_j = 1`, because `v̄_j < v_j` there. The right side corresponds to those
     with `v_j = 0`.
   - So near `p`, `ĝ` is the prefix-replacement germ of `γ_-` composed with the germ that shifts the
     right families by `m` and fixes the left ones.
   - That is admissible in the sense of `periodic-germ-extensions-of-v-are-virtually-simple`, with
     `π = id`. Also `ĝ(p)` is tail equivalent to `p`, since `T` preserves tail classes.
5. **Finitely many classes.** Let `H = ⟨g_1, …, g_s⟩`. Every breakpoint of every element of `H`
   lies in the `H`-orbit of a generator's breakpoint, hence in finitely many `PSL_2(Z)`-orbits. So
   the preimages lie in finitely many tail classes. Let `Π` be their set of primitive periods, each
   of length `>= 2`.
   - Then every `ĥ`, for `h ∈ H`, lies in `G_Π`. It is a prefix replacement away from finitely many
     points of `Ω_Π`, and has admissible germs there.
6. **Conclusion.** `H ≅ Ĥ ≤ G_Π`. By part 6 of `periodic-germ-extensions-of-v-are-virtually-simple`,
   `H` embeds in the finitely presented simple group `G_Π'`. ∎
