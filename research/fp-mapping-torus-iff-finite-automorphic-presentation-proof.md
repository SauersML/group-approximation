Choose words `u_x,v_x in F(X)` representing `phi(x)` and `phi^(-1)(x)` for
every `x in X`, and let `Phi_+,Phi_-` be the corresponding free-group
endomorphisms.

Suppose first that `(FAP1)` holds.  The finite presentation

```text
< X,t | R,
        t x t^(-1)=u_x,
        t^(-1) x t=v_x                 (x in X) >       (FAP2)
```

presents `G`.  Indeed conjugating `r in R` by arbitrary powers of `t`
imposes every relation in `(FAP1)`, while the conjugation relations realize
the two mutually inverse actions.  The evident maps between `(FAP2)` and
`K semidirectProduct_phi Z` are inverse.

Conversely suppose that `G` is finitely presented.  Start with the finite
generating set `X union {t}`.  Add the finitely many conjugation relators in
`(FAP2)`; they hold in `G`.  Since `G` is finitely presented on this finite
generating set, the remaining kernel of the map `F(X union {t})->G` is
normally generated by a finite set `S`.

Every `s in S` has exponent sum zero in `t`, because it is trivial in `G`
and the exponent map `G->Z` sends `t` to one and `X` to zero.  Using the
conjugation relators, move every occurrence of `t` to the right.  The zero
exponent sum leaves a word `r_s in F(X)`.  Let

```text
R={r_s : s in S}.
```

Reidemeister--Schreier rewriting for the kernel of the exponent map, with
transversal `{t^n:n in Z}`, says that all kernel relators are precisely the
conjugates `t^n r_s t^(-n)`.  Rewriting those conjugates through the action
gives the words obtained from `R` by the monoid generated by
`Phi_+,Phi_-`.  Hence these words normally generate all relations of `K`,
which is `(FAP1)`.  End proof.
