## Setting

Let `I` be an interval of `R` (open, closed or half-open, possibly `R` itself). A *piecewise
projective homeomorphism* of `I` is an increasing homeomorphism `g` of `I` with finitely many
breakpoints, such that on each piece `g(t) = (αt+β)/(γt+δ)` with a matrix in `GL_2(R)` that has no
pole on the closed piece. PL maps are the case `γ = 0`. The *data* of `g` are its breakpoints and
the entries of chosen matrix representatives. The *support* is `supp g = {t : g(t) ≠ t}`.

## Theorem

Let `G` be a finitely presented group of piecewise projective homeomorphisms of `I`. Let
`H ≤ G` be generated by finitely many elements whose data are real algebraic numbers. Let `u ∈ G`
and let `e` be a point of the interior of `I` such that, for some `ε > 0`:

1. `u` moves every point of some interval `(e - δ, e)` and fixes `[e, e+ε)` pointwise, and
2. `e` is an accumulation point of `L = {inf supp h : h ∈ H, ∅ ≠ supp h ⊆ (e, e+ε)}`.

Then `e` is an algebraic number. The mirror statement also holds: `u` fixes `(e-ε, e]` and moves
`(e, e+δ)`, and suprema of supports in `(e-ε, e)` accumulate at `e`.

So in a finitely presented piecewise projective group, transcendental numbers can occur as
support endpoints only away from the region where an algebraic subgroup has dense support endpoints.

## Corollaries

- **Thompson's `F`.** Take `H = F` on a dyadic interval `D`. Then `L` accumulates at every interior
  point of `D`. So in a finitely presented piecewise projective group containing this `F`, every
  endpoint in `int D` of a support component is algebraic. This contains
  `fp-pl-groups-with-f-have-no-transcendental-support-endpoints`, since `Q(λ) ∩ Q̄ = Q`.
- **Two copies of `F`.** For transcendental `λ > 0`, the group `⟨F, F^λ⟩ ≤ PL(R)` is not
  finitely presented. Here `F` acts on `[0,1]` and `F^λ` is its conjugate by `t ↦ λt`, acting on
  `[0,λ]`. Proof: `F^λ` contains bumps supported on `(0, λq)` for every dyadic `q ∈ (0,1)`; pick `q`
  with `λq ∈ (0,1)`. The same holds with `F^λ` replaced by the conjugate of `F` by any piecewise
  projective map that sends some dyadic point in `(0,1)` to a transcendental point of `(0,1)`.
- **Lodha--Moore.** The subgroup `⟨a, b⟩` of the Lodha--Moore group `G_0` (arXiv:1308.4250) is the
  piecewise-`PSL_2(Z)` copy of `F` on `R`. Its support endpoints are all rationals, and it has bumps
  on `(r,s)` for all rationals `r < s`. (These facts are from that paper and are not re-read here.)
  So a finitely presented piecewise projective overgroup of `⟨a, b⟩` has no transcendental endpoint
  of a support component anywhere on `R`. For example, `⟨G_0, G_0^λ⟩` is not finitely presented for
  transcendental `λ > 0`, where `G_0^λ` is the conjugate by `t ↦ λt`.
- **Circle.** The same holds for piecewise projective homeomorphisms of `RP^1`, applied in an affine
  chart around `e`. For example, it applies to overgroups of Thompson's `T` realized as `PPSL_2(Z)`.

## Not claimed

- The theorem constrains only the endpoints in the accumulation region. It says nothing about
  groups without an algebraic subgroup of this kind.
- Other analytic families, for example pieces `t ↦ c·t^λ`, are not covered. The proof needs relator
  identities to be algebraic in the data; for exponential families this would need a Schanuel-type
  genericity.
- `V`-type maps with discontinuities should go through with right-open pieces. This is not
  written out.

## Lesson for general BH

A finitely presented group sits at a point of its parameter variety where the relator locus is a
neighborhood of a Zariski-generic parameter. So it can "see" a transcendental parameter only
through finitely many open conditions. Whenever an algebraic subgroup lets the group compare a
parameter with a dense set of algebraic points, finite presentation forces that parameter to be
isolated, hence algebraic. This is a transferable **deformation test** for finite presentation:
move the parameters and look for a relation that breaks. Together with
`definable-parameter-hosts-cap-simple-subgroups-at-pspace`, it says that continuous parameters
never carry information into a finitely presented host.
