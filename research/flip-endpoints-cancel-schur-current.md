Let `C_f=B_f^Q`, let `theta` be the corrected child-sign automorphism, and
write

```text
R_f=C_f semidirect_<theta> <eta>.
```

On every forbidden selector sector the two child types `sigma_+` and
`sigma_-` satisfy

```text
sigma_- is equivalent to sigma_+ composed with theta,
```

and `eta` exchanges their isotypic spaces.  Let `E<=R_f` be any intermediate
subgroup containing `eta`, including every subgroup in the proposed interval

```text
<J,q_*,eta> <= E <= R_f.                                        (FCE1)
```

Put `K=E intersect C_f`.  Then `K` is `theta`-invariant.  Exactly one of the
following occurs:

1. `sigma_+|K` and `sigma_-|K` have the same isotypic data, so restriction to
   `K` has already forgotten the oriented child current; or
2. their isotypic data differ, in which case conjugation by `eta` pairs the
   two data and forces equal multiplicity in every `E`-module.

In the second case the paired `K`-types form one `E`-orbit and are counted as
one returned module of doubled dimension, exactly cancelling the factor-two
restriction multiplicity.  Therefore no subgroup satisfying `(FCE1)` can
both retain the signed/oriented Schur surplus and use the branch flip as part
of the returned packet.

This closes the entire intermediate-subgroup search suggested by the two
known endpoint calculations.  A surviving finite-depth bridge must be
one-sided: transport a proper child corner before adjoining the flip, or use
a proper-corner/projective return whose output algebra does not contain the
swapping implementer.
