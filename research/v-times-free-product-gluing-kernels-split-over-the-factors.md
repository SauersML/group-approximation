**ESTABLISHED** (lane bh-invent-16, 2026-09-18; lane proof, elementary given the cited nodes; not
reviewed; no priority claimed). It addresses gate 5 of the v5 synthesis (P2′b) in the non-amenable case.
**FPG in general, FSG(F_2, 2) and FSG(Z, 2) all stay OPEN.**

## Setting

- Notation is that of `gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-free-group-full-shift`, and full bisections are
  used throughout, so no freeness is assumed.
- `Λ = Λ_1 * Λ_2` with each `Λ_i` finitely generated, `X` is any compact zero-dimensional `Λ`-space, and
  `Ω = C × X`.
- `T = G_V × (Λ ⋉ X)`, and `T_i = G_V × (Λ_i ⋉ X)` is an open subgroupoid with the same unit space, so
  `F(T_i) ≤ F(T)`.
- `B = LC(X, V) = F(G_V × X)` is the full group with trivial labels. It lies in `F(T_1) ∩ F(T_2)`.
- For `Λ_i` the pieces are `M_i = V(Λ_i)` and `N_i = B ⋊ Λ_i`, with gluing group `Γ~_i`,
  `π_i : Γ~_i → F(T_i)` and `K_i = ker π_i`.
- The **support** of a full bisection is the clopen set of `ω` where its arrow is not a unit. `F(T_i)_W`
  consists of the elements supported in `W`.
- `A = F(T_1) *_B F(T_2)`, with natural map `π_A : A → F(T)` and `K_* = ker π_A`.
- **FPG(Λ_1, Λ_2; X)** (free-product gluing) is the statement that `K_*` is the normal closure of the
  commutators `[a_1, a_2]` with `a_i ∈ F(T_i)` of disjoint supports.

## Theorem

1. **The pieces split.**
   - `N = N_1 *_B N_2`, since `B ⋊ (Λ_1 * Λ_2)` is the amalgam of the `B ⋊ Λ_i` over `B`.
   - `M = ⟨M_1, M_2⟩` and `F(T) = ⟨F(T_1), F(T_2)⟩`.
   - The inclusions `M_i ≤ M` and `N_i ≤ N` induce homomorphisms `ι_i : Γ~_i → Γ~` with `π ∘ ι_i = π_i`,
     so `ι_i(K_i) ⊆ K`.
2. **Splitting.** Put `R = ⟨⟨ι_1(K_1) ∪ ι_2(K_2)⟩⟩ ⊴ Γ~` and `Γ~' = Γ~ / R`.
   - There is a surjection `ψ : A → Γ~'` with `π' ∘ ψ = π_A`, where `π' : Γ~' → F(T)` is induced by `π`.
   - `K / R = ker π' = ψ(K_*)`.

   So `K` is normally generated by `ι_1(K_1)`, `ι_2(K_2)` and lifts of normal generators of `K_*`.
3. **Disjoint commutation dies in the gluing group.** If `a_i ∈ F(T_i)` have disjoint supports, then
   `ψ([a_1, a_2]) = 1`.
4. **Reduction.** Assume FPG(Λ_1, Λ_2; X). Then `K = ⟨⟨ι_1(K_1) ∪ ι_2(K_2)⟩⟩`. So:
   - `K_1 = K_2 = 1` implies `K = 1`;
   - if each `ι_i(K_i)` is finitely normally generated in `Γ~` (for instance if `K_i` is finitely normally generated in `Γ~_i`), then
     `K` is finitely normally generated.
5. **Calibration: FPG holds on a point.** For `X` a point, `F(T_i) = V(Λ_i)` and `B = V`, and
   `V(Λ_1 * Λ_2) = V(Λ_1) *_V V(Λ_2) / ⟨⟨[V(Λ_1)_U, V(Λ_2)_(U')] : U ∩ U' = ∅⟩⟩`.
6. **Free groups.** Write `F_n = ⟨s_1⟩ * ⋯ * ⟨s_n⟩` and let `X = A^(F_n)`.
   - As a `⟨s⟩`-space, `X` is conjugate to the Cantor-alphabet full shift `C^Z`. The orbits of `⟨s⟩` on
     `F_n` are the right cosets `⟨s⟩h`, so `A^(F_n) = (A^J)^Z` with `J` countably infinite, and it is
     topologically free.
   - If FPG holds for each splitting `⟨s_1, …, s_(j−1)⟩ * ⟨s_j⟩` on `X`, then by induction
     `K_(F_n, A^(F_n))` is normally generated by the images of the cyclic kernels `K_(⟨s_j⟩, X)`.
   - Each cyclic kernel is an image of `K_(Z, C^Z)`, by item 1 of the parent descent node: `X` embeds in
     `C^Z` equivariantly (corollary (f) there).
   - Hence **EX(Z): `K_(Z, C^Z) = 1`, together with FPG, gives `K_(F_n, A^(F_n)) = 1`.** By rank
     universality (`gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-free-group-full-shift`, item 3) that gives exactness
     over every `n`-generated group and alphabet `A`, the Track A group `Λ₁ × Λ₁` included.
   - EX(Z) also gives exactness for every topologically free `Z`-Cantor system, in particular the exact
     form of FSG(Z, 2).
7. **No Brin–Thompson shortcut in the non-amenable case.** Suppose `Λ` is non-amenable and `X` carries a
   `Λ`-invariant probability measure on which the action is essentially free, as the full shift `A^Λ`
   does for `|A| ≥ 2`. Then there is no Borel isomorphism `Ω ≅ C^m` that carries `T`-orbits into orbits
   of Brin–Thompson `mV`, or into orbits of any groupoid whose orbit relation is Borel hyperfinite. For
   `Λ = Z` the baker's map does carry `T_(2^Z)`-orbits into `3V`-orbits (`F(T_(2^Z)) ≤ 3V`); for `F_n`
   that route has no analogue.

## Proof

**1.**
- The amalgam decomposition of `N` holds by the universal property of free products.
- `V(Λ) = C(C, Λ) ⋊ V`. A label `λ|_U` with `λ = ℓ_1 ⋯ ℓ_k` (letters from `Λ_1 ∪ Λ_2`) is the product
  `ℓ_1|_U ⋯ ℓ_k|_U`, and each `ℓ|_U` lies in some `M_i`.
- Every arrow of `Λ ⋉ X` is a composite of arrows of `Λ_1 ⋉ X` and `Λ_2 ⋉ X`. Also `F(T) = ⟨M, N⟩`
  (`v-times-sft-full-groups-split-into-two-wreath-pieces`, item 1, read with full bisections as in
  `gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-free-group-full-shift`), and `M, N ⊆ ⟨F(T_1), F(T_2)⟩`.
- `ι_i` is well defined: `M_i → M` and `N_i → N` agree on `V × Λ_i`, `M_(i,0) ⊆ M_0`, and the
  subgroup `N_1 = LC(X, V_[1])` is the same for `Λ_i` and `Λ`.
- `π ∘ ι_i = π_i` on generators.

**2.**
- *Definition of `ψ`.* For `a ∈ F(T_i)`, choose `ã ∈ Γ~_i` with `π_i(ã) = a` (`π_i` is onto, by the
  descent node's Setting). Put `ψ_i(a) = ι_i(ã) R`. Two lifts differ by `K_i`, and `ι_i(K_i) ⊆ R`, so this
  is a well-defined homomorphism.
- *Agreement on `B`.* For `b ∈ B ⊆ N_1 ∩ N_2`, take `ã = b` for both `i`, so `ψ_1(b) = ψ_2(b)`. Hence
  `ψ` is defined on `A`.
- *Surjectivity.* `ψ(π_i(m)) = mR` for `m ∈ M_i ∪ N_i`, so the image contains `⟨M, N⟩R`.
- *Kernel.* `π'(ψ_i(a)) = π(ι_i ã) = a`, so `π' ∘ ψ = π_A`. Since `ψ` is onto,
  `ker π' = ψ(ψ^-1(ker π')) = ψ(ker π_A)`.

**3.** *(i) Generation inside a support.* Let `W` be clopen and `P = pr_X(W)`.
- On each atom `Q` of the partition of `P` generated by the `X`-sides of the pieces of `W`, the fibre
  `W_x = U_Q` is a fixed nonempty clopen of `C`.
- Choosing a prefix bijection `U_Q → C` for each atom gives a compact open bisection `β` of `G_V × X`
  from `W` onto `C × P`.
- Conjugation by `β` identifies `F(T_1)_W` with `F(G_V × (Λ_1 ⋉ X)|_P)`.
- That group is generated by transpositions (`v-times-ample-full-groups-are-generated-by-transpositions`,
  valid for full bisections of any ample groupoid, as noted in the free-group descent node). So
  `F(T_1)_W` is generated by transpositions supported in `W`.

*(ii) Splitting off full fibres.* Let `τ` be a transposition along a bisection `γ : E → E'`. For
`a, b ∈ {00, 01, 10, 11}`, put `E_(ab) = E ∩ (C_a × X) ∩ γ^-1(C_b × X)`.
- The transpositions along `γ|_(E_(ab))` have pairwise disjoint supports, commute, and multiply to `τ`.
- Each support lies in `(C_a ∪ C_b) × X`, so none of its fibres is all of `C`.

*(iii) Separating two such supports.* Let `τ ∈ F(T_1)` and `τ' ∈ F(T_2)` be transpositions with
disjoint supports `S, S'`, neither having a full fibre.
- For each `x`, choose `v_x ∈ V`: with `v_x(S_x) = C_0` and `v_x(C ∖ S_x) = C_1` if `S_x ≠ ∅`, and with
  `v_x(C ∖ S'_x) = C_0` if `S_x = ∅ ≠ S'_x`, and `v_x = 1` if both are empty. The choice can be made locally constant in `x`, so it defines
  `g = (v_x)_x ∈ B`.
- Then `g S ⊆ C_0 × X` and `g S' ⊆ C_1 × X`.
- `F(T_1)_(C_0×X) = ⟨M_(1,C_0), N_(1,C_0)⟩` inside `F(T_1)`. It is the copy of `F(T_1) = ⟨M_1, N_1⟩`, and
  the copy of `fλ` is `copy_0(f) · λ|_(C_0)`.
- So a word in these letters lifts `gτg^-1`, and `ψ(gτg^-1)` lies in the image of `⟨M_(C_0), N_(C_0)⟩`.
  Likewise `ψ(gτ'g^-1)` lies in the image of `⟨M_(C_1), N_(C_1)⟩`.
- These subgroups of `Γ~` commute:
  - `M_(C_0)` with `M_(C_1)` in `M`, and `N_(C_0)` with `N_(C_1)` in `N`;
  - `M_(C_0)` with `N_(C_1)` by the defining relation;
  - `M_(C_1)` with `N_(C_0)` by its `V`-conjugate, which is (R1) of
    `v-times-sft-gluing-kernel-is-relatively-perfect`.
- Hence `ψ([τ, τ']) = ψ(g)^-1 [ψ(gτg^-1), ψ(gτ'g^-1)] ψ(g) = 1`.

*(iv) General case.* Write `a_1` and `a_2` as products of transpositions supported in their supports
(i), and split them (ii). Every factor of `ψ(a_1)` then commutes with every factor of `ψ(a_2)` (iii).

**4.** By 3, `ψ` kills the normal generators of `K_*`, so `ψ(K_*) = 1` and `K = R`. The two bullets
follow.

**5.** Let `G` be the presented group, and let `W_G ≤ G` be generated by the labels `λ|_U` with
`λ ∈ Λ_1 ∪ Λ_2`.
- `V` normalizes `W_G`, by relations inside the `V(Λ_i)`, and `G = W_G V`.
- *Reduction to one partition.* Given a product of labels, refine to a common clopen partition `𝒫`. Each
  `f ∈ C(C, Λ_i)` equals `∏_(U∈𝒫) f(U)|_U` in `V(Λ_i)`. Labels on distinct cells commute: inside
  `V(Λ_i)`, or by disjoint commutation. So the product equals `∏_U w_U|_U` with
  `w_U|_U ∈ L_U = ⟨Λ_1|_U, Λ_2|_U⟩`.
- *Each `L_U` is free.* `L_U` is a quotient of `Λ_1 * Λ_2`, because `λ ↦ λ|_U` embeds each `Λ_i`. It maps
  onto `Λ|_U ≅ Λ_1 * Λ_2`, generators to generators, so `L_U ≅ Λ_1 * Λ_2`.
- *Injectivity on `W_G`.* So `W_G → C(C, Λ)` is injective: an element mapping to 1 has every `w_U = 1`.
- *Injectivity on `G`.* `C(C, Λ) ∩ V = 1` in `V(Λ)`, so `W_G ∩ V = 1`, and `G → V(Λ)` is injective.

**6.** The coset computation is as stated.
- *Induction.* Apply 4 to `F_j = F_(j−1) * ⟨s_j⟩` on the same `X`.
- *Cyclic factors.* `⟨s⟩ ↷ X` embeds in `C^Z` equivariantly by the formula of corollary (f), which needs no
  freeness. So
  `K_(⟨s⟩, X)` is an image of `K_(Z, C^Z)` by item 1 of `gluing-kernels-descend-from-the-free-group-full-shift`.
- The last two claims are rank universality and the descent corollaries (a) and (f).

**7.**
- The orbit relation `R_T` of `T` contains `R' = {((c, x), (c, λx))}`.
- *Assume `R_T` is Borel hyperfinite.* Then so is `R'`, since subrelations of hyperfinite relations are
  hyperfinite (Jackson–Kechris–Louveau).
- *The slice.* So is the restriction of `R'` to one slice `{c} × X`, which is the orbit relation of
  `Λ ↷ X`. It is then amenable for the invariant measure, and an essentially free p.m.p. action with
  amenable orbit relation forces `Λ` to be amenable (Zimmer; Connes–Feldman–Weiss). This contradicts the
  hypothesis on `Λ`.
- *The Brin–Thompson orbit relation.* The orbit relation of `mV` on `C^m` lies in the m-fold product of
  tail equivalence, which is hyperfinite (Dougherty–Jackson–Kechris; finite products of hyperfinite
  relations are hyperfinite).
- These inputs are recalled, not re-read. ∎

## Status of the open residue

**FPG** is a statement about full groups only: no gluing group and no `M`/`N` pieces enter it. A proposed
attack, not carried out here, is Brown's presentation theorem applied to the natural action of `F(T)` on
the **Boolean power of the Bass–Serre tree**. This is the median graph `LC(Ω, 𝒯)`, where `𝒯` is the tree
of `Λ_1 * Λ_2`, and the action is `(g·s)(ω) = λ_g(g^-1 ω) · s(g^-1 ω)`. Its stabilizers are:
- `F(T_1)` and `F(T_2)` for the constant sections `v_1` and `v_2`;
- `B` for the edge between them;
- the commuting products `F(T_1)_E × F(T_2)_(Ω∖E)` for the mixed sections equal to `v_1` on `E` and `v_2`
  off `E`.

These are exactly the relations of FPG. FPG would follow if the orbit of these sections spans a simply
connected subcomplex and Brown's 2-cells reduce to such squares. The calibration (item 5) is the case
where `Ω` has one point in the `X` direction.

## Lesson for general BH

**Free products split the gluing layer.** For `V ×` a free product action, every relation of the full
group is one of three kinds:
- a relation of one factor's full group;
- an identification over the trivial-label group `LC(X, V)`;
- a commutation of disjointly supported factor elements (FPG).

The last kind holds automatically in the gluing group, because the trivial-label group can always push
two disjoint supports into complementary cones.

So the non-amenable case of P2′b, `F_n` and hence every Track A group by rank universality, reduces to
the cyclic case on the Cantor-alphabet shift, EX(Z), plus the dynamics-free FPG. That cyclic case is the
one the amenable programme (bh-p2b-exact) already attacks. Non-amenability cannot be handled by embedding
in a Brin–Thompson group, since hyperfinite orbit relations forbid it. It is handled instead by the free
product structure, which is the tree that witnesses non-amenability.
