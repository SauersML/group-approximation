ESTABLISHED.  Use the notation

```text
M=(A R)^5,                         H=St_5(R),
K=M semidirect H,                  m_0=x_16(A)!=1.          (DAS1)
```

The group von Neumann algebra `L(K)` contains an exact representation of
the whole module `M` and exact covariance by every actor in `H`.  Its
restriction to the abelian algebra generated by `M` is

```text
L(M)=L^infinity(M_hat, Haar).                              (DAS2)
```

The spectral measure is diffuse because `M` is infinite.  The canonical
trace satisfies

```text
tau(m_0)=0,                ||m_0-1||_2^2=2,                (DAS3)
```

while actor covariance has exactly zero defect.

Consequently, merely passing a canonical microstate sequence to an exact
tracial ultraproduct representation and exposing the resulting whole-module
spectral measure cannot prove active-mark collapse.  The gap in
`affine-leavitt-active-root-character-orbits-are-aperiodic` is a bottom gap
for the counting-measure representation on each discrete character orbit.
It does not give a Poincare inequality for the invariant Haar measure in
`(DAS2)`: the constant function `1` is actor-invariant and Haar gives the
trivial character measure zero.

The coordinate hypothesis in
`affine-leavitt-global-root-pvm-collapses-active-mark` is therefore
load-bearing.  One needs finite atomic coordinate PVMs together with actor
transport energy before taking the ultralimit, or an equivalent
matrix-coordinate boundary/multiplicity estimate.  An exact diffuse PVM in
the limit is strictly too weak.

This claim does not assert that the exact model `L(K)` embeds into a tracial
matrix ultraproduct; that would assume the very approximation property under
investigation.  It proves the required falsification statement: no argument
valid for arbitrary exact finite tracial representations can be the missing
decoder.
