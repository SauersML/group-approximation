Notation as in the target. Write `E = SL_X(Q)`.

## Imported standard facts

Milnor, *Introduction to Algebraic K-Theory*, Ann. of Math. Studies 72 (1971). The theorem
numbers below are recalled, not re-read; the statements are the standard ones.
- **(K1)** `K_2(Λ) = ker(St(Λ) -> E(Λ))` is precisely the centre of the Steinberg group
  `St(Λ)` (§5, Theorem 5.1).
- **(K2)** `St(Λ) -> E(Λ)` is a universal central extension (Kervaire; §5, Theorem 5.10). So every
  automorphism of `E(Λ)` lifts uniquely to `St(Λ)`.
- **(K3)** Tate: `K_2(Q) ≅ Z/2 ⊕ ⊕_(p odd) (Z/p)^x` (§11, Theorem 11.6). It is not finitely
  generated.

Two elementary facts:
- **(K4)** `St_n(Λ)` is perfect for `n >= 3`, since `x_ij(a) = [x_ik(a), x_kj(1)]`.
- **(K5)** Whitehead: `diag(g, g^-1) ∈ E(Λ)` for `g ∈ GL_k(Λ)`.

Let `St_X = St_X(Q)` be the Steinberg group on generators `x_pq(c)`, `p ≠ q ∈ X`, `c ∈ Q`. It is
`St(Q)`, and `π : St_X -> E` sends `x_pq(c) ↦ 1 + c e_pq`. For finite `F ⊆ X`, `St_F` is the
subgroup generated by the `x_pq(c)` with `p, q ∈ F`.

## Step 1. Monomial matrices act on `St_X`

For `m ∈ Mon(X)` set `λ_m(p,q) = mu_m(p)/mu_m(q)` and
`m · x_pq(c) = x_(σ_m p, σ_m q)(λ_m(p,q) c)`.
- This respects the Steinberg relations: additivity is clear, the commutator relation uses
  `λ(p,q)λ(q,r) = λ(p,r)`, and the disjointness relation holds because `σ_m` is a bijection.
- It is multiplicative in `m`, so it gives a homomorphism `Mon(X) -> Aut(St_X)`.
- `π(m · s) = m π(s) m^-1`, by the conjugation formula in the target.
- By (K2), `m · (-)` is the unique lift of conjugation by `m`.

## Step 2. `Mon(X)` acts trivially on `K_2(Q)`

Let `k ∈ K_2(Q) ⊆ St_X`, and let `F` be a finite set containing every index of a word for `k`, so
`k ∈ St_F`. Let `m ∈ Mon(X)`.
- Choose a finite `F' ⊇ F ∪ σ_m(F)` with `|F'| >= 3`, and a finitary monomial `m'` supported in
  `F'` with `σ_(m') = σ_m` and `mu_(m') = mu_m` on `F`. The formula of Step 1 depends only on the
  values at the indices involved, so `m · k = m' · k`.
- Pick `F''` disjoint from `F'` with a bijection `F' -> F''`, and put `g = m' ⊕ m'^-1`, acting as
  `m'` on `span(F')`, as `m'^-1` on `span(F'')`, and trivially elsewhere. By (K5), `g ∈ E`.
- `g` and `m'` induce the same conjugation on elements of `E` supported in `F'`. Their lifts
  `g̃` and `m' · (-)` to `St_X` therefore differ on `St_(F')` by a homomorphism
  `St_(F') -> K_2(Q)`, into the centre, by (K1).
- `St_(F')` is perfect by (K4), since the image of `St_n -> St_X` is a quotient of `St_n`.
  So that homomorphism is trivial, and `g̃ = m' · (-)` on `St_(F')`.
- As `g ∈ E = π(St_X)`, pick `ĝ` with `π(ĝ) = g`. Conjugation by `ĝ` lifts conjugation by `g`, so
  by uniqueness (K2) `g̃ = ĝ (-) ĝ^-1`.
- Hence `m · k = m' · k = ĝ k ĝ^-1 = k`, because `k` is central (K1).

## Step 3. The central extension

Put `Ĝ = St_X x| M`, with `M` acting as in Step 1, and `ρ : Ĝ -> Γ`, `(s, m) ↦ π(s) m`.
- `ρ` is a homomorphism by Step 1, and it is onto since `Γ = E M`.
- `ρ(s, m) = 1` forces `m = π(s)^-1 ∈ M ∩ E = 1`, so `ker ρ = K_2(Q) × 1`.
- `ker ρ` is central in `Ĝ`: it commutes with `St_X` by (K1) and with `M` by Step 2.

## Step 4. `Ĝ` is finitely generated

Let `γ_1, ..., γ_r` generate `Γ`, choose `γ̂_i ∈ Ĝ` with `ρ(γ̂_i) = γ_i`, and put
`Ĥ = ⟨γ̂_1, ..., γ̂_r⟩`.
- `Ĝ = Ĥ · ker ρ` with `ker ρ` central, so `[Ĝ, Ĝ] = [Ĥ, Ĥ] <= Ĥ`.
- `St_X` is perfect by (K4), so `St_X = [St_X, St_X] <= [Ĝ, Ĝ] <= Ĥ`.
- In particular `ker ρ <= St_X <= Ĥ`, so `Ĥ = Ĝ`.

## Step 5. Conclusion

Suppose `Γ` is finitely presented.
- Since `Ĝ` is finitely generated and `Γ = Ĝ / ker ρ` is finitely presented, `ker ρ` is the
  normal closure in `Ĝ` of finitely many elements. This is standard: finite presentability does
  not depend on the finite generating set (B. H. Neumann).
- `ker ρ` is central, so its normal closure equals the subgroup it generates. So `ker ρ` is
  finitely generated.
- But `ker ρ ≅ K_2(Q)` is not finitely generated by (K3). This contradiction shows that `Γ` is
  not finitely presented. ∎

## Remarks

- Only (K1), (K2), (K4), (K5) and "`K_2(Λ)` is not finitely generated" are used, so the general
  form in the target follows word for word.
- Steps 1–4 need `M ∩ E = 1`. A non-split `Γ` needs a lift of `M ∩ E` to `St_X`. Finitary
  monomials of determinant 1 lift only up to Steinberg symbols, so this proof does not cover
  that case.
