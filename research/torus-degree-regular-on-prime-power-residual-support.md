Let `tau` be an injective continuous `G`-equivariant self-map of `(T^d)^G`, with degree
matrix `D in M_d(Z[G])`. Let `H = <supp D>`. Call `H` **RPP** if every finite subset of
`H` embeds in some finite `p`-group quotient of `H`, the prime allowed to depend on the
subset.

**Claim.**
- (a) **Positive half.** If `H` is RPP, then `xi -> xi D` is injective on `Q[G]^d`, so
  `tau` is onto. Hence every group whose finitely generated subgroups are RPP is
  torus-surjunctive at every `d`. Examples: locally finite `p`-groups, all torsion-free
  abelian groups, and abelian groups with `p`-primary torsion (`Q`, `Z(p^inf)`,
  `Q (+) Z(p^inf)`). Also locally free and locally torsion-free-nilpotent groups.
- (b) **Sharpness.** If a group `H` contains a finite subgroup `K` whose order is not a
  prime power, pick `s, t in K` of distinct prime orders `p, q` and `ap + bq = 1`. Then
  `D = a(1 + s + ... + s^(p-1)) + b(1 + t + ... + t^(q-1))` has `epsilon(D) = 1` and is a
  zero divisor in `Q[K] subset Q[H]`.

So a proof of C2 that uses only `epsilon(D) in GL_d(Z)` works exactly on RPP support and
dies on the Bezout witness of (b). Any counterexample to C2 has non-RPP support. The
smallest open host is the divisible, locally finite abelian group `Z(2^inf) x Z(3^inf)`
(or `Q/Z`). It is amenable, and finite-alphabet surjunctivity over it is elementary.
