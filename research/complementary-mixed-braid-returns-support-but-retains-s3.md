**ESTABLISHED SYMMETRIC-CELL FENCE.**  Let the notation `W_79(a,b)` in
this node mean the literal symmetric word
`x_79(a)x_97(b)x_79(a)`, and put

```text
A=W_79(1,q_1),                 B=W_79(1,q_0),
C=W_79(q_0,q_0),               D=W_79(q_1,q_1),
F=W_79(1,1),                   q_0+q_1=1.             (CMR1)
```

Direct binary-Leavitt matrix multiplication first gives

```text
A=D,                  B=C.                            (CMR2a)
```

Indeed the complementary part of the two outer `x_79(1)` factors cancels.
Consequently

```text
ABA=C,                 BAB=D,                 CD=F.   (CMR2)
```

Thus `C` is the literal `W_1=W_79(e_0,e_0)` used by
`support-return-gives-gauged-native-loop`, but the apparent braid is only
the tautology `ABA=B=C` between two commuting support cells.

Most importantly, `A` is **not** the residual word from the mixed-braid
comparison.  That word is

```text
P=x_79(q_1)x_97(q_1)x_79(1),
A=[[q_0,q_1],[q_1,q_0]],
P=[[q_0,1],  [q_1,q_0]]                               (CMR2b)
```

on `(7,9)`.  Their top-right entries differ by the nonzero idempotent
`q_0`, so `A!=P`.  The identities `(CMR2)` therefore do not return the
actual mixed-braid chart.

Independently, the symmetric support table does not authenticate the gauge
of `A`.  The complete table
`(CMR2)`, the support-return identity

```text
J_1=C Khat_1 C,
```

and the factorization of `C,D,F` admit the exact reservoir assignment

```text
a=(1 2),             b=(2 3),
c=aba=(1 3),         d=bab=(1 3),             f=cd=1. (CMR3)
```

Every displayed relation is satisfied in `S_3`, while `a` and `b` remain
nontrivial and `ab` has order three.  The two occurrences of `C` in the
native support-return identity cancel its reservoir factor.  Hence the
table types the complementary factor and the support output, but it does
not force the support gauge `a` to be trivial or identify it with the
native Whitehead gauge on a signed Hecke carrier.

Exact full-signature tests give an additional carrier fence: none of
`A,B,AB,BA,C,D,F` preserves the signed Hecke projection `Q`.  Therefore
`(CMR2)` is not itself an `(ESB1)` bridge.  More basically, it is not a
continuation of `P`; a successful continuation must retain the asymmetric
first factor in `(CMR2b)`.

This remains true after closing **all** exact relations with the native
Whitehead.  Faithful matrix closure gives

```text
H=<A,B,J_1>,                    |H|=12.                (CMR4)
```

The parity which is odd on `A` and even on `B,J_1` is a well-defined
character `H->C_2`; exhaustive equality testing finds no parity collision.
Moreover the only element of `H` which preserves the signed Hecke pair is
the identity, and the only element carrying `Q` to `J_1QJ_1^(-1)` is
`J_1` itself.  Both have even `A` parity.  Hence no word in this symmetric
support/native subgroup supplies the missing odd Hecke bridge.  This is a
valid scoped fence for replacing `P` by the two symmetric support cells;
it is not a closure calculation for the subgroup generated by the actual
residual `P`.

No Property T, Kazhdan input, trace hypothesis, or literature theorem is
used.  The two small exact MSI artifacts verify the symmetric identities
and the twelve-element subgroup closure; they do not test the asymmetric
residual `P`.
