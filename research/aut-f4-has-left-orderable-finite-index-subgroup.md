**Statement (OPEN).**  Some subgroup of finite index in `Aut(F_4)` is
left-orderable.

**Why it matters.**  By `aut-f4-has-property-t` (Nitsche), every finite-index
subgroup of `Aut(F_4)` is an infinite Kazhdan group.  So this claim answers
Navas's Question 3 positively; see the route
`infinite-left-orderable-kazhdan-group-from-aut-f4`.

`n = 4` is the weakest case.  Extending an automorphism of `F_4` by fixing
`x_5, ..., x_n` embeds `Aut(F_4)` in `Aut(F_n)`.  A left-orderable finite-index
subgroup of `Aut(F_n)` meets this copy in a left-orderable finite-index subgroup
of `Aut(F_4)`.

**Necessary condition.**  The same argument with `Aut(F_3) <= Aut(F_4)` shows
that this claim forces some finite-index subgroup of `Aut(F_3)` to be
left-orderable.  `Aut(F_3)` is not Kazhdan, so that weaker question is not
Navas's question.

**A witness can be taken torsion-free and of level 3.**  Let
`Gamma_4(3) = ker(Aut(F_4) -> GL_4(Z/3))`.  It is torsion-free:
- A finite-order element of `Gamma_4(3)` maps to a finite-order integer matrix congruent to the identity mod 3.
- By Minkowski's lemma that matrix is the identity, so the element lies in `IA_4`.
- `IA_4` is residually torsion-free nilpotent (Andreadakis filtration), hence torsion-free.

Left-orderability passes to subgroups.  So the claim holds iff some
finite-index subgroup of `Gamma_4(3)` is left-orderable.

## Attempts

**The classical obstructions do not reach Aut(F_4).**  Let `Delta` be a
finite-index subgroup of `Aut(F_4)`.  None of the following excludes it:

- **Torsion.**  `Delta cap Gamma_4(3)` is torsion-free.
- **Local indicability.**  Neither for nor against.  `Delta` is Kazhdan, so its abelianization is finite.  Every left order on it is therefore non-Conradian.  By the root's trichotomy, any faithful action of it on the line without global fixed points is of Navas type (iii) on each irreducible component.
- **Nilpotent subgroups, Witte Morris's mechanism.**  `Aut(F_n)` embeds in `Out(F_(n+1))`: extend an automorphism by fixing a new letter `t`.  If the extension is inner, the conjugator commutes with `t`, so it is a power of `t`; since it preserves `F_n`, that power is trivial.  Bestvina--Feighn--Handel (arXiv:math/9712219, abstract) prove: "Every solvable subgroup of Out(F_n) is finitely generated and virtually abelian."  So `Aut(F_4)` contains no Heisenberg group, and the nilpotent configurations that kill `SL_3(Z)` do not exist inside it.
- **Higher-rank lattices.**  Bridson--Wade (arXiv:1004.3222, abstract) show that every homomorphism from an irreducible lattice in a real semisimple group of real rank at least two to `Out(F_n)` has finite image.  So `higher-rank-real-lattices-are-not-left-orderable` cannot be brought in through a subgroup of `Delta`.
- **Random groups, piecewise groups, smoothness.**  These constrain other families of groups, or the regularity of an action; see the root's Attempts.

**Blind-ball certificate search.**  Run on MSI with kissat.  This is evidence, not proof.
- *Encoding.*  Take the word ball `B_r`, with one boolean per pair `{g, g^-1}`.  Add the valid clauses `g > 1, h > 1 => gh > 1` for `|g| + |h| <= r`.  UNSAT proves non-orderability; SAT proves nothing.
- *Calibration.*
  - A group with an order-4 generator is UNSAT at `r = 4`.
  - The bi-orderable group generated by the six partial conjugations of `F_3` is SAT at `r = 4`.
  - The group generated by the six `e_ij(3)` in `SL_3(Z)` is UNSAT at `r = 5` (68,941 elements, 416,616 clauses).  So Witte Morris's theorem already has a radius-5 certificate.
- *Aut(F_3), right transvections.*  Take the group generated by the cubes `x_i -> x_i x_j^3` of the six right Nielsen transvections.  It maps onto that `SL_3(Z)` group, yet stays SAT at `r = 5` (129,493 elements) and at `r = 6` (1,281,853 elements, 7,639,608 clauses).  So the radius-5 certificate of the image does not lift within radius 6.
- *Aut(F_3), both sides.*  Adding the six left transvection cubes gives SAT at `r = 4` (161,329 elements).
- *Where it dies.*  A SAT ball says nothing about orderability outside the ball.  These runs are in `Aut(F_3)`, which bears only on the necessary condition.  Radius 7 is out of reach of the Python ball builder.
