For the abstract criterion, define on `ran(F)+ran(H)` the formal swap

```text
J(F xi+H eta)=H xi+F eta.
```

The Gram matrix of `(F,H)` is invariant under this swap exactly when
`F^*F=H^*H` and `F^*H=H^*F`.  Hence the formula is well-defined and
isometric, and is an involution.  Extend it by either sign on the orthogonal
complement.  Necessity follows immediately from `JF=H` and `J=J^*`.

For the support estimate, let `L_0=ran(F)+ran(H)` and
`L=L_0+J_0L_0`.  Then `L` is `J_0`-invariant and has dimension at most
`8 dim(E)`.  Use the Gram-swap reflection on `L_0`, any reflection on
`L minus L_0`, and `J_0` on `L^perp`.  This proves `(GS3)`.

For `(GS7)`, expand the square.  The identities

```text
C^2+D^2=1,          (-VCV^*)^2+VD^2V^*=VV^*,
CDV^*-DV^*VCV^*=0
```

give `X~^2=1`.  For `q in E`, `(GS5)` and
`G=C(1+C)^(-1)` give

```text
X~ U~_i X~q=U~_i^(-1)X~q.
```

The bottom component is exactly the exit equation `JF_i=H_i`; the top
component is `C^2+DGD=C`.  Therefore `(X~U~_i)^3q=q`, and commutation with
`X~U~_i` gives the identity on the cyclic subspace generated by `E`.

Finally, in the BS specialization use `RSV=S^4V` and `R^(-1)V=V` to obtain
`(GS10)`.  Write every Gram block using `P=1-VV^*`, use
`M_n=M_(4n)` and `(GS9)`, and cancel the common `G^2` terms.  All blocks of
`(GS2)` then agree except the second individual diagonal
`M_(-5)=M_(-5)^*` and the mixed block `M_(-3)=M_6`, proving `(GS11)`.
