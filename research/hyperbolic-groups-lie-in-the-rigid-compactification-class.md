## Theorem

Let `Γ` be hyperbolic with finite symmetric `S`, and fix `δ ≥ 1` as in
`hyperbolic-groups-have-sft-horofunction-compactifications`. Let `k ≥ 13δ+4`, and let `Y_k` be the
distance-gradient SFT. Then `Y_k` is `D`-quantum rigid over every field for all large `D`. With (C1) and (C2)
from that node, `Γ` lies in the class `𝒞` of `tree-gluing-turns-rigid-sft-compactifications-into-rigid-sfts`.

In particular this holds for uniform lattices in `Sp(n,1)` and for Żuk's random Kazhdan groups. So `Γ * Z`
carries a minimal, topologically free, quantum-rigid SFT for every hyperbolic `Γ`.

## 1. The window-chain lemma (abstract)

- **Data.** `X` is a subshift over `Λ`, `E` is a `D`-family, and `q, q'` are sites.
- **Windows and events.** `W_0 ∋ q, W_1, …, W_n ∋ q'` are finite windows. Consecutive windows and their
  events lie in one ball of diameter `≤ 2D`. The events `C_i, D_i` (`1 ≤ i ≤ n`) are cylinder events on
  `W_(i−1) ∪ W_i`.
- **Classical hypotheses.** Each must hold for every point of `X`:
  - (K1) `C_(i+1) ⇒ C_i` and `D_i ⇒ D_(i+1)`;
  - (K2) `¬C_i ⇒ D_(i+1)`, and `C_(i+1) ∧ D_i` is impossible;
  - (K3) on `C_i`, the letter at `q` is a function `Φ_i` of `x|_(W_i)`; on `D_i`, the letter at `q'` is a
    function `Ψ_i` of `x|_(W_(i−1))`.

Then `E_a(q)` and `E_b(q')` commute.

*Proof.*
- **Operators.** Letters in one `2D`-ball commute. So every cylinder event on such a ball is an idempotent,
  and non-occurring patterns vanish by (Q3).
- **Chains.**
  - (K1) and (K2) become `C_(i+1) ≤ C_i`, `D_i ≤ D_(i+1)`, `1 − C_i ≤ D_(i+1)` and `C_(i+1) D_i = 0`.
  - Hence all `C_i, D_j` commute pairwise:
    - for nearby indices, by locality;
    - for `j ≥ i+1`, by comparability `1 − C_i ≤ D_j`;
    - for `i ≥ j+1`, `C_i D_j = C_i C_(j+1) D_j = 0 = D_j C_i`.
- **Transport.** Put `Φ_i^a = Σ` of the pattern idempotents on `W_i` with `C_i` true and `Φ_i = a`.
  - Base: `E_a(q) C_1 = Φ_1^a` (take `W_1 ∋ q`).
  - Step: `E_a(q) C_i = E_a(q) C_(i−1) C_i = Φ_(i−1)^a C_i`. Expanding over patterns on `W_(i−1) ∪ W_i` and
    using (K3) for occurring patterns gives `= Φ_i^a`.
  - The same computation on the other side gives `C_i E_a(q) = Φ_i^a`. So `E_a(q)` commutes with every `C_i`.
- **Commutation with the `D`'s.** Write `E_a(q) = E_a(q) C_(i−1) + E_a(q)(1 − C_(i−1))`.
  - The first term is `Φ_(i−1)^a`, which is local to `D_i`.
  - In the second, `1 − C_(i−1) ≤ D_i`, and `E_a(q)` commutes with `C_(i−1)`.

  So `E_a(q)` commutes with every `D_i`. Symmetrically, `E_b(q')` commutes with all `C_i, D_i`, and
  `E_b(q') D_i = Ψ_i^b`.
- **Atoms.** Take an atom `π` of the commutative algebra generated by the `C`'s and `D`'s. Let `J` be the largest
  `i` with `π ≤ C_i` (or `0`, with `Φ_0 = E_a(q)`), and `J'` the least `i` with `π ≤ D_i` (or `n+1`). Then
  `J ≤ J' ≤ J+1`.
  - `E_a(q)π = πE_a(q) = Φ_J π`, and `E_b(q')π = Ψ_(J') π`.
  - `Φ_J` and `Ψ_(J')` live on windows at most two steps apart, so they commute.
  - Summing `E_a(q)πE_b(q') = πΦ_JΨ_(J')` over atoms gives `E_a(q)E_b(q') = E_b(q')E_a(q)`. `∎`

## 2. The hyperbolic instance

- **Reduction to the boundary.** By `hyperbolic-seed-rigidity-is-a-horofunction-boundary-question` (seed
  locally forced) and Corollary A of `quantum-rigidity-is-decided-on-the-derived-subshift`, it suffices to
  treat `X = ∂Y_k`. Its points are genuine horofunctions `f`, with centres `ξ`.
- **The Busemann formula.** For all `x, y`,
  `f(y) − f(x) = d(x,y) − 2(y|ξ)_x + err`, with `|err| ≤ K = 4δ`.
  - It follows from `f = lim(d(·,p_n) − d(1,p_n))` with `p_n -> ξ`, and from Gromov products at a boundary
    point being defined up to `2δ`.
  - The four-point condition with one boundary point holds with constant `2δ`.
- **Constants.** `ε_0 = 8δ+1`, `s = 30δ`, `r = 2s + 30δ`, and `D ≥ 2(r + 2s)` (and `D` beyond the threshold
  of Corollary A).

**Windows and events.**
- For `q, q'` with `d(q,q') > 2D`, take a geodesic `σ`, and points `q = c_0, c_1, …, c_n = q'` on it with
  consecutive distances in `[s, 2s)`.
- Put `W_i = B_r(c_i)`.
- `C_i` means `f(c_(i−1)) − f(c_i) ≥ d(c_(i−1),c_i) − ε_0`, a forward drop.
- `D_i` means `f(c_i) − f(c_(i−1)) ≥ d(c_(i−1),c_i) − ε_0`, a backward drop.

Both are read from gradients along `σ`, so they are cylinder events.

**(K1).** Let `a = c_(i−1)`, `b = c_i`, `e = c_(i+1)`.
- `C_(i+1)` gives `(e|ξ)_b ≥ d(b,e) − (ε_0 + K)/2 > 2δ`.
- Since `(a|e)_b = 0`, the four-point condition gives `(a|ξ)_b ≤ 2δ`.
- So `f(a) − f(b) ≥ d(a,b) − 4δ − K ≥ d(a,b) − ε_0`, which is `C_i`.
- The constant does not degrade, because each step is referred to `ξ` and not to the previous step.
- `D_i ⇒ D_(i+1)` is the same statement for the reversed geodesic.

**(K2).**
- `¬C_i` gives `(a|ξ)_b > (ε_0 − K)/2 > 2δ`. So `(e|ξ)_b ≤ 2δ` and `f(e) − f(b) ≥ d(b,e) − ε_0`, which is
  `D_(i+1)`.
- `C_(i+1)` gives `f(a) − f(b) ≥ s − 4δ − K > 0`, while `D_i` needs `f(b) − f(a) ≥ s − ε_0 > 0`. So they
  cannot both hold.

**(K3), the target squeeze.** Assume `C_i` with `i ≥ 2`.
- **`q` sees `ξ` through `c_(i−1)`.** `C_i` gives `(c_(i−2)|ξ)_(c_(i−1)) ≤ 2δ`. Also
  `(c_(i−2)|q)_(c_(i−1)) = d(c_(i−1),c_(i−2)) > 4δ`. The four-point condition gives
  `(q|ξ)_(c_(i−1)) ≤ 4δ`, and so `(x|ξ)_(c_(i−1)) ≤ 4δ + 1` for every `x ∈ B_1(q)`.
- **The descending rays pass near `c_(i−1)`.** The descending ray from each such `x` converges to `ξ`
  (`k-local-distance-functions-on-hyperbolic-groups-are-global`, part 2). So it passes within `ρ_0 = 9δ+1`
  of `c_(i−1)`, with room upstream.
- **Squeeze.** Let `q_W` be any target fitting `x|_(W_i)`. Occurring patterns are restrictions of limits of
  distance functions, so `q_W` exists. The squeeze of `hyperbolic-groups-have-sft-horofunction-compactifications`
  runs inside `B_(ρ_0+14δ+1)(c_(i−1)) ⊆ W_i`. It gives `f − d(·,q_W) = const` on `B_1(q)`.
- **Conclusion.** The letter at `q` is the gradient of `d(·,q_W)` at `q`. It is the same for every point with
  the given `x|_(W_i)`, so `Φ_i` is well defined. The event `C_i` is read inside `W_i`. `D_i` and `Ψ_i` are
  symmetric, and `i = 1` is trivial.

Section 1 now applies to every pair of far sites, and (Q2) covers the near ones. `∎`

## Remarks

- **Lanes are harmless.** The endpoint letters are never scalar. They are carried window to window as
  functions (`Φ_i`), and non-Busemann horofunctions (Webster–Winchester) need no special treatment.
- **Why this is not the degrading chain.** A naive chain "drop on window `i+1` ⇒ drop on window `i`" loses
  about `8δ` per step when derived from 4δ-convexity. Measuring every drop against Gromov products with the
  centre, and applying the four-point condition at the middle point, makes the threshold a fixed point.
- **Consistency with (RA′).** Hyperbolic groups are exact, and their boundary actions are amenable
  (recalled). So this is no counterexample to `quantum-rigid-minimal-topfree-subshift-actions-are-amenable`.

## Lesson for general BH

**Rigidity without walls: Gromov products replace walls.**
- In a hyperbolic group, "the centre lies ahead at this window" is a local event.
- The four-point condition makes these events a monotone chain with no loss. Forward at `i+1` implies forward
  at `i`, and not forward at `i` implies backward at `i+1`.
- The target squeeze makes each window determine the letters upstream.
- Transporting letters through the windows proves quantum rigidity for every hyperbolic group, Kazhdan ones
  included. So property (T) is no obstruction to (★𝒞) on the dynamics side.

For the canonical inputs `H_Γ`, which are not hyperbolic, the question is now purely geometric. It asks for an
fp overgroup whose Cayley graph has a unique-sink compactification with this "anchored drop" structure.
Relatively hyperbolic, or acylindrically hyperbolic, overgroups in which `H_Γ` sits as a parabolic subgroup are
the natural next candidates. There the tree-gluing case is the model, and the parabolic compactification is what
remains.

**§1, the window-chain lemma: correct, with one index off by one.**
- The chain relations, the transport `E_a(q)C_i = C_iE_a(q) = Φ_i^a` by induction, and the commutation of
  `E_a(q)` with each `D_i` are all correct.
- For the last of these, split `E_a(q) = Φ_(i−1)^a + E_a(q)(1 − C_(i−1))` and use `1 − C_(i−1) ≤ D_i`.
- The symmetric statements for `E_b(q')` go through `¬D_(i+1) ⇒ C_i`, the contrapositive of (K2).
- **Atoms.** From `π ≤ 1 − C_(J+1) ≤ D_(J+2)` one gets only `J ≤ J' ≤ J + 2`, not `J + 1`. For example, the
  centre can sit beside the geodesic near `c_J`.
  - `Φ_J^a` then lives on `W_(J−1) ∪ W_J`, and `Ψ_(J+2)^b` on `W_(J+1) ∪ W_(J+2)`.
  - Every pair of their sites is at distance `< 6s + 2r ≤ 2D`, since `D ≥ 2(r + 2s)`. So they still commute,
    and the conclusion stands.
  - Also, `Φ_i^a` is a sum of pattern idempotents on `W_(i−1) ∪ W_i`, where `C_i` is read, not on `W_i`
    alone.
- `E_a(q)E_b(q') = Σ_π Φ_J π Ψ_(J') = Σ_π Ψ_(J') Φ_J π = E_b(q')E_a(q)`, using that `E_a, E_b, Φ, Ψ` all
  commute with the atoms. This is correct over every field.

**§2, the hyperbolic instance: correct, with sharper constants.** Let the four-point condition hold with
constant `δ` in `Γ`, and let `(x|ξ)_w` be the inf over sequences `z_n → ξ` of `liminf (x|z_n)_w`. Then:
- **(a)** For `x, y ∈ Γ`: `(x|y)_w ≥ min((x|ξ)_w, (y|ξ)_w) − δ` and `(x|ξ)_w ≥ min((x|y)_w, (y|ξ)_w) − δ`. To
  see this, take `liminf` of the interior inequality along any `z_n → ξ`, then the inf. So the node's `2δ` is
  safe.
- **(b)** The Busemann formula holds with `err ∈ [−2δ, 0]`.
  - Write `f(y) − f(x) = d(x,y) − 2 lim(y|p_n)_x`.
  - Then `lim (y|p_n)_x ≥ (y|ξ)_x` by the definition as an inf.
  - Also `lim (y|p_n)_x ≤ (y|ξ)_x + δ`: apply (a) with `(p_n|z_m)_x → ∞`.
  - So `K = 4δ` is safe. Seedless points of `Y_k` are limits of normalized `d(·, p_n)` with `p_n → ∞`. Pass
    to a subsequence that converges to some `ξ ∈ ∂Γ`; this does not change `f`.
- **(c)** For a geodesic ray from `v` to `ξ`, `d(c, ray) ≤ (v|ξ)_c + 5δ`. Apply the stipulated segment bound
  to `[v, y_n]`, and use `(v|y_n)_c ≤ (v|ξ)_c + δ` for large `n`, by (a).
- **Checks.**
  - (K1), (K2) and `C_i ∧ D_i = ∅` follow exactly as written, and the thresholds are fixed points. I redid the
    arithmetic with `ε_0 = 8δ+1`, `s = 30δ` and `K = 4δ`.
  - In (K3), `(q|ξ)_(c_(i−1)) ≤ 3δ` by (a), so `(x|ξ) ≤ 3δ + 1` on `B_1(q)`. By (c), the descending ray from
    each such `x` passes within `8δ + 1 ≤ ρ_0` of `c_(i−1)`. It converges to `ξ` by (b), since
    `(y_n|ξ)_x ≥ n − δ` along the ray.
  - The squeeze is `hyperbolic-groups-have-sft-horofunction-compactifications` run in the window
    `B_(ρ_0+14δ+1)(c_(i−1)) ⊆ B_(30δ)(c_(i−1)) ⊆ W_i`. I rechecked its three steps (lower bound, the point
    `v`, the geodesic meeting the window) with these radii.
  - So `f − d(·, q_W)` is constant on `B_1(q)`, and `Φ_i` is a function of the `W_i`-pattern.
  - A shorter equivalent: `f(x) = min_(y ∈ B_(ρ_0)(c_(i−1))) (f(y) + d(x,y))` on `B_1(q)`.
- (Q2) covers pairs at distance `≤ 2D`. The constants `s, r` do not depend on `D`, so "all large `D`" is
