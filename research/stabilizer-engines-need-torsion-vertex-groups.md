**Notation.** As in `free-product-boundary-groupoids-carry-the-vertex-group-homology`: `k ≥ 2`,
`A = Z/k`, `B` is infinite with finite generating set `S`, `D = A * B`, `T` is the Bass–Serre
tree (edges `D`, the edge `d` joins `dA` and `dB`), `X_B` is its ends together with its
`B`-vertices, `F_B = [[D ⋉ X_B]]`. `v = 1B`, `u = 1A`, and `h(ε)` is the half-tree of a directed
edge `ε` (points of `X_B` on its head side). For the base edge `1`, `H = h(1 → u)`, and the
branches at `v` are the pairwise disjoint sets `bH`, `b ∈ B`. A subgroup `K ≤ B` is
**`R`-deep** if `K ∩ Ball_S(R) = {1}`.

## Theorem

Suppose `F_B` or `D(F_B)` is finitely presented. The second implies the first, by
`fp-stabilizer-engines-need-decidable-vertex-groups`. Then:
1. **Deep subgroups are normal.** There is `R` such that every `R`-deep subgroup of `B` is normal.
2. **No deep subgroups.** There is `R'` such that `B` has no nontrivial `R'`-deep subgroup, i.e.
   the finite set `Ball_S(R') \ {1}` meets every nontrivial subgroup of `B`.
3. **Consequently** `B` is a finitely presented torsion group with solvable word problem and only
   finitely many elements of prime order, and `B` is not simple.

## Corollaries

- (a) **Isolated groups do not suffice.** `F_V`, `F_T`, `F_F` and their derived groups are not
  finitely presented, although `V`, `T`, `F` are isolated. So
  `isolated-vertex-groups-have-fp-stabilizer-engines` is false, and the route
  `boone-higman-via-isolated-stabilizer-engines` has a false premise: any isolated overgroup of an
  input containing `Z` has an element of infinite order.
- (b) `F_B` is infinitely presented whenever `B` has an element of infinite order, or infinitely
  many elements of prime order, or is an infinite simple group. This includes Reid's group
  (`B = Z`, already in `stabilizer-engines-are-fp-only-over-isolated-groups`) and every `B`
  containing a free group.
- (c) **What is left.** An infinite `B` with `F_B` finitely presented would be an infinite finitely
  presented torsion group, whose existence is a long-standing open problem. No such `B` is known.
  So stabilizer engines in free-product form give no finitely presented group beyond the finite
  vertex groups (Higman–Thompson type), and they cannot carry Boone–Higman.

## Proof

**0. Tables and transport.** Measure `d ∈ D` by `‖d‖`, the sum over its free-product normal form
of `1` per `A`-syllable and `|b|_S` per `B`-syllable. As in part 1 of
`stabilizer-engines-are-fp-only-over-isolated-groups`, every `f ∈ F_B` has a *table*: a partition
of `X_B` into atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by half-trees of finitely many edges, with
labels in `D`. It has *radius `r`* if those edges and labels have `‖·‖ ≤ r`. Composing with a
table of radius `r_0` adds at most `r_0` to the radius. Since `D ↷ X_B` is effective (every
`d ≠ 1` has at most two fixed points), `f = 1` iff every nonempty piece has label `1`.

**1. The collapsed engine.** For `K ≤ B` put `D_K = B *_K (K × A)`, which is `D/⟨⟨[A,K]⟩⟩`. Let
`π : D → D_K` be the quotient map. The Bass–Serre tree `T_K` has edges `D_K/K`, `B`-vertices of
valence `[B:K]` and `(K × A)`-vertices of valence `k`. Let `X_K` be its ends together with its
`B`-vertices of infinite valence, and `F_{B,K} = [[D_K ⋉ X_K]]` the homeomorphisms of `X_K` that
are locally given by elements of `D_K`.

**Transport lemma.** Suppose `K` is `4r`-deep. Then:
- `π` is injective on `{‖d‖ ≤ 4r}`, and `d ↦ π(d)K` is injective on edges with `‖d‖ ≤ 2r`;
- the subtree of `T` spanned by those edges maps isomorphically into `T_K`, and `π` is
  equivariant on it;
- the map `h(ε) ↦ h(π ε)` extends to an isomorphism of the Boolean algebras generated by the
  half-trees of at most `2r` such edges, and this isomorphism preserves emptiness of atoms.

*Proof.*
- **Injectivity.** Let `δ = d^{-1}d'` with `‖δ‖ ≤ 4r`. Every `A`-syllable of its `D`-normal form
  lies in `(K × A) \ K`. Every `B`-syllable is nontrivial of length `≤ 4r`, so it lies in `B \ K`
  by depth. So the normal form is reduced in the amalgam `B *_K (K × A)`. A reduced word lies in
  `K` only if it is a single syllable in `K`, which is excluded. So `π(δ) ∈ K` iff `δ = 1`.
- **Emptiness.** An atom is empty exactly when it is a component of the tree minus the chosen
  edges that is a single `A`-vertex with all `k` edges chosen. Every `B`-vertex keeps unchosen
  edges: the cosets `bK` with `|b|_S ≤ 2r` are distinct, so the valence `[B:K]` is at least
  `|Ball_S(2r)| > 2r`. So the atom patterns agree in `T` and `T_K`. `∎`

**Consequence.** Let `F_B = ⟨Y | R⟩` with `Y` and `R` finite.
- Let `r_0` bound the radii of the tables of the `y ∈ Y`, so a word of length `≤ n` has a table of
  radius `≤ n r_0`.
- Let `n` bound the relator lengths and the lengths of fixed words for each `s ∈ S` and each
  `τ_b` below.

For `K` that is `4 n r_0`-deep, the transported tables `π(y)` are elements of `F_{B,K}`. The computation of a word's table is carried
along verbatim. So each relator, having labels `1` on all nonempty pieces, maps to `1`. There is
therefore a homomorphism

    ρ_K : F_B → F_{B,K},   y ↦ π(y).

For each `s ∈ S` (as a word in `Y`), `ρ_K(s)` is the global element `π(s)`. Hence
`ρ_K|_B = π|_B`, for **every** element of `B`, whatever its length.

**2. Test 1: deep subgroups are normal.** Fix `b ∈ S ∪ S^{-1}`. Let `τ_b ∈ F_B` be the involution
that swaps the branches `H` and `bH` at `v`, with labels `b` and `b^{-1}`, and is the identity
elsewhere.
- **In `F_B`.** Let `k ∈ B \ {1, b, b^{-1}}`. Then `kτ_bk^{-1}` swaps `kH` and `kbH`, and these are
  disjoint from `H ∪ bH`. So `[kτ_bk^{-1}, τ_b] = 1`.
- **In `F_{B,K}`.** Take `K` deep enough that `ρ_K(τ_b) = π(τ_b)` for all such `b`, and let
  `k ∈ K \ {1}`. Then:
  - `π(τ_b)` swaps `P = h(K)` and `Q = h(bK)`, the branches at `v' = 1B` through the edges `K`
    and `bK`;
  - `π(k)` fixes `v'` and the edge `K`, so `π(k)P = P`, and `π(k)Q = h(kbK) =: R`;
  - so `ρ_K(kτ_bk^{-1})` swaps `P` and `R`;
  - `R ≠ P`, since `b ∉ K`.
- **The contradiction.** If `R ≠ Q`, then `P, Q, R` are three disjoint nonempty branches. The
  swaps `(P Q)` and `(P R)` do not commute: one product sends `R` to `Q`, the other sends it to
  `P`. That contradicts `ρ_K` being a homomorphism.
- **So `R = Q`**, that is `kbK = bK`, i.e. `b^{-1}kb ∈ K`. This holds for all `k ∈ K` and all
  `b ∈ S^{±1}`, so `K` is normal. This proves item 1.

**3. Item 2.** By `stabilizer-engines-are-fp-only-over-isolated-groups`, `B` is finitely
discriminable: there is `R''` with no nontrivial normal `R''`-deep subgroup. With `R' = max(R, R'')`,
an `R'`-deep subgroup is normal by item 1, hence trivial.
- A self-contained version of that step is the same transport, applied to `A * (B/K)` for a deep
  normal `K`. The map `ρ` kills `β ∈ K \ 1` and the nontrivial commutator `[β, h]`, where `h ≠ 1`
  is supported in `H`. Then simplicity of `D(F_B)` makes the image abelian, while it contains
  `A * (B/K)`.

**4. Item 3.**
- **Torsion.** If `g` has infinite order, then `|g^j| ≥ R'` for all `|j| ≥ J`. So `⟨g^J⟩` is a
  nontrivial `R'`-deep subgroup, which item 2 forbids.
- **Prime order.** A subgroup of prime order is generated by any of its nontrivial elements, so it
  is `⟨φ⟩` for some `φ ∈ Ball(R') \ 1`. So there are finitely many such subgroups.
- **Presentation and word problem.** `B` is finitely presented by
  `fp-stabilizer-engines-need-fp-vertex-groups`, and decidable by
  `fp-stabilizer-engines-need-decidable-vertex-groups`.
- **Not simple.** Conjugation permutes the finitely many subgroups of prime order. If `B` were
  infinite and simple, the kernel of that action would be all of `B`. Each such subgroup would
  then be normal and proper, a contradiction. `∎`

**Corollary (a).** `V`, `T` and `F` have elements of infinite order. Isolation of `F` is Cannon–Floyd–Parry, as recorded in
`stabilizer-engines-are-fp-only-over-isolated-groups` item 3(d).

## Why the earlier transfer missed this

Approximating `B` by nearby marked groups only detects deep **normal** subgroups (kernels), so it
stops at isolated groups. Collapsing a deep subgroup `K` into the stabilizer of the base edge
changes no bounded pattern of the engine: balls of `B`, balls of the tree, and which atoms are
empty all stay the same. Yet it turns "`k` moves the branch `H` far away" into "`k` fixes `H`".
Every deep subgroup is visible this way, not only the normal ones.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Collapse transfer.** At a point whose isotropy acts **regularly** on its local branches, any
  deep subgroup can be collapsed onto a branch stabilizer without changing bounded patterns. So
  finite presentation detects every deep subgroup of the isotropy, and the isotropy must have none,
  which means it is essentially finite. For free-product engines this is the theorem above:
  an infinite emitter with regular branch action certifies nothing.
- **The same principle** explains the known finiteness criterion for twisted Brin–Thompson groups.
  `SV_G` is finitely presented only if `G ↷ S` has finitely many orbits on pairs: a
  regular-like action has "deep pairs" to collapse.
- **What survives.** Emitter designs survive only if the branch compactification carries global
  constraints that the collapsed amalgam cannot satisfy: an SFT with a nontrivial extension
  problem, as in the finite-type blow-ups. Otherwise the input's hardness must sit in an acting
  group with finite local branching (Track A).

## Referee (bh-ref-engines, 2026-09-18): PASS, with one lemma statement repaired

I checked this node together with `stabilizer-engines-are-fp-only-over-isolated-groups` and
`free-product-emitter-full-groups-need-isolated-vertex-groups`, since all three rest on the same
table transport. I checked every step of the transport lemma, the construction of `ρ_K`, Test 1 and
items 2–3 line by line. The theorem and corollaries (a)–(c) hold.

**What holds exactly as written.**
- *Amalgam normal form.* `D/⟨⟨[A,K]⟩⟩ = B *_K (K × A)`. For `1 ≠ δ` with `‖δ‖ ≤ 4r`, the
  `D`-normal form is reduced in the amalgam, since `A`-syllables lie in `(K×A) \ K` and short
  `B`-syllables lie in `B \ K` by depth. So `π(δ) ∉ K`, and also `π(δ) ∈ B` iff `δ ∈ B` and
  `π(δ) ∈ K×A` iff `δ ∈ A`.
- *Subtree.* The edges of norm `≤ 2r` form a prefix-closed set containing the base edge, hence a
  subtree. `π` maps it isomorphically, equivariantly for labels of norm `≤ r`.
- *`ρ_K` is a homomorphism,* and `ρ_K|_B = π|_B` on **all** of `B`: two homomorphisms that agree on
  `S` agree everywhere. This is the key point, and it is correct. Long elements `k ∈ K` are
  never transported by tables. They enter only through the homomorphism.
- *Test 1.* The relation `[kτ_bk^{-1}, τ_b] = 1` holds in `F_B` for every `k ∈ K \ 1`, since
  `|k| > 1` gives `k ∉ {1, b, b^{-1}}`. Its image is `[(P R), (P Q)] = 1`, where
  `(P Q)(P R)` sends `R` to `Q` and `(P R)(P Q)` sends `R` to `P`. So `R = Q`. `P, Q, R` are
  nonempty because `T_K` has no leaves (valences `k ≥ 2` and `[B:K] ≥ 2`).
- *Items 2–3.* The deep cyclic subgroup `⟨g^J⟩`, the count of prime-order subgroups and the
  non-simplicity argument are all correct.

**Gap (repaired; conclusions unaffected).** The transport lemma claims that emptiness of atoms is
preserved in **both** directions. The proof counts `[B:K] ≥ |Ball_S(2r)| > 2r ≥` (number of chosen
edges at a vertex). That count is wrong:
- A table of radius `r` may choose up to about `|Ball_S(4r)|` edges at one `B`-vertex, not `2r`.
- For a finite-index deep `K` (for example `B = Z`, `K = (4r+1)Z`, and a table using every branch
  `b` with `|b| ≤ 2r` at `v`), the atom `{v} ∪ (other branches)` is nonempty in `X_B` but empty in
  `X_K`.

The argument only ever uses the direction **empty in `T` ⇒ empty in `T_K`**, and that direction
holds unconditionally. An empty atom is a single `A`-vertex with all `k` edges chosen, and its
image is a `(K×A)`-vertex with the same `k` distinct edges chosen. That direction is what is
needed for each of the following:
- the transported tables being partitions, since disjointness, covering and containment are
  emptiness identities;
- relators mapping to `1`, since every nonempty piece in `T_K` is nonempty in `T`, where its
  label is `1`;
- `ρ_K(s) = π(s)` and `ρ_K(τ_b) = π(τ_b)`.

Repair: either state the lemma one-directionally, or require `K` to be `(8r+2)`-deep. Then
`[B:K] ≥ |Ball_S(4r+1)| > |Ball_S(4r)|` bounds the chosen edges at any vertex, since balls of an
infinite group grow strictly. Also, "`π` injective on `{‖d‖ ≤ 4r}`" should read "on
`{‖d‖ ≤ 2r}`". What is proved is `π(δ) ∉ K` for `1 ≠ δ`, `‖δ‖ ≤ 4r`. The corollaries for `V, T, F`
use cyclic `K = ⟨g^J⟩` of infinite index, where no atom at a `B`-vertex can empty out, so they
need no repair.

**Counterexample search.** I looked for an infinite `B` with `F_B` finitely presented, and for a
known finitely presented group of this form that the theorem would forbid. I found neither.
- The finite-`B` case (Higman–Thompson type) is consistent.
- Brown's theorem that Houghton's `H_2` is not finitely presented is recovered by the same scheme
  (calibration in the parallel node).
- The theorem's conclusion (an infinite finitely presented torsion group with finitely many
  elements of prime order) is a long-standing open existence question, so it does not contradict
  any known theorem.

**Credit.** The collapse is in the spirit of Zaremsky's finiteness criterion for twisted
Brin–Thompson groups (finitely many orbits on pairs), as the node says. The bounded-configuration
transfer is the standard "a finitely presented group's marked neighbours are its quotients"
argument of Cornulier–Guyot–Pitsch (J. Algebra 2007).
