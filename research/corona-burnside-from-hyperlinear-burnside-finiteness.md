## Why sufficient

Let `Q_d` be a norm matrix corona, and let `Gamma <= U(Q_d)` be generated by
`m` elements with `g^N = 1` for every `g in Gamma`. Suppose `Gamma` is
infinite. Apply `bounded-exponent-corona-image-gives-hyperlinear-quotient`
with `G = Gamma` and `rho` the inclusion. Since `rho(G) = Gamma` is infinite,
clause 2 holds: the finite residual `K` of `Gamma` has a quotient `H` which is
infinite, finitely generated (by at most `|R(m,N)|(m-1)+1` elements),
hyperlinear, and of exponent dividing `N`.

That contradicts `hyperlinear-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-finite`. So
`Gamma` is finite. As `Q_d`, `m`, `N` and `Gamma` were arbitrary, this is
`operator-norm-restricted-burnside`.

## What the route spends

The Hilbert--Schmidt hypothesis is used once, at the same exponent `N` and
for groups with at most `|R(m,N)|(m-1)+1` generators; clause 3 of the claim
records that per-parameter form. The group `H` it is applied to has no
finite quotients and is perfect, so the requirement is only ever tested on
that class.

The corona input consists of the spectral gap `eta_N` of finite-order
unitaries and the span-of-active-ranges reblocking. The reblocking turns an
operator-norm asymptotic representation into one with a macroscopic
Hilbert--Schmidt defect at a generator. No dimension-free power-law modulus
enters. So this route is independent of `corona-burnside-from-power-law-moduli`.

## What it does not give

The converse, corona Burnside implies Hilbert--Schmidt Burnside, is not
claimed. Normalized Hilbert--Schmidt models of periodic groups do not become
operator-norm models; attempt 3 of `operator-norm-restricted-burnside`
explains why.

The frontmatter of `hyperlinear-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-finite`
remarks that neither form implies the other on its face. This route settles
one direction. Once the finite residual is used, the Hilbert--Schmidt form
implies the operator-norm form, and through
`bounded-exponent-non-mf-from-corona-burnside` it implies
`infinite-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-non-mf`.
