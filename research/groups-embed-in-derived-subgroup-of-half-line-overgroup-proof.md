Products are compositions: `fg = f ∘ g`, so the right factor acts first. For `n ∈ Z` and `γ ∈ Γ` let `c_(n,γ)` be left
translation by `γ` on the levels `m ≥ n` of `X = Γ × Z`, and the identity on the levels `m < n`.

**1. Homomorphisms.** Left multiplication is an action, so `h_γ h_(γ') = h_(γγ')` and `δ_0(γ)δ_0(γ') = δ_0(γγ')`. The map
`δ_0` is injective because `Γ` acts freely on itself.

**2. The commutator.** `σ^n h_γ σ^(-n)(x,m) = σ^n h_γ(x, m-n)`, which translates by `γ` exactly when `m - n ≥ 0`. So
`σ^n h_γ σ^(-n) = c_(n,γ)`, and

    [σ, h_s] = σ h_s σ^(-1) h_s^(-1) = c_(1,s) ∘ c_(0,s^(-1)).

On level 0 only the right factor acts, by `s^(-1)`. On levels `m ≥ 1` it is `s s^(-1) = e`, and below 0 both factors are
the identity. So `[σ, h_s] = δ_0(s^(-1))`. The elements `δ_0(s^(-1))`, `s ∈ S`, generate `δ_0(Γ)`, and hence
`δ_0(Γ) ≤ [C(Γ), C(Γ)]`.

**3. Structure.** Every generator carries each level `Γ × {m}` onto a level and acts there by a left translation. So
`C(Γ)` lies in the Cartesian wreath product `Γ^Z ⋊ ⟨σ⟩`, which acts faithfully on `X`. Let `E ≤ Γ^Z` be the sequences
trivial for `m ≪ 0` and constant for `m ≫ 0`.
- `E` is a subgroup: pointwise products of such sequences are again such. It is `σ`-invariant and contains every `h_s`.
- So the normal closure `K` of `{h_s}` in `C(Γ)` lies in `E`, and `C(Γ) = K⟨σ⟩` with `K ∩ ⟨σ⟩ = 1`, since `σ` moves
  levels.
- Conversely let `f ∈ E` change value at the levels `m_1 < … < m_k`, and put `x_j = f(m_j - 1)^(-1) f(m_j)`. Then
  `c_(m_1,x_1) ∘ … ∘ c_(m_k,x_k)` acts on level `m` by left multiplication by `x_1 ⋯ x_(j(m))`, where `j(m)` is the
  largest `j` with `m_j ≤ m`. That product is the telescoping `f(m)`. Each `c_(n,γ) = σ^n h_γ σ^(-n)` lies in `K`, so
  `K = E` and `C(Γ) = E ⋊ ⟨σ⟩`.
- `E` is the union of the nested subgroups `E_M = {f : f(m) = e for m < -M, f(m) = f(M) for m ≥ M}`, and
  `f ↦ (f(-M), …, f(M))` is an isomorphism `E_M ≅ Γ^(2M+1)`.
- `σ` has infinite order and `C(Γ)` is generated by `σ` and the `h_s`.

**4. Permanence.**
- Finite direct powers, directed unions and extensions by `Z` preserve amenability and elementary amenability.
- Soficity: `Γ^(2M+1)` is sofic, a direct limit of sofic groups is sofic, and an extension of a sofic group by the
  amenable quotient `Z` is sofic, all by Theorem 1 (clauses 1 and 3) of `elek-szabo-sofic-permanence`.
- `E ≤ Γ^Z` is torsion-free if `Γ` is, and `C(Γ)/E ≅ Z` is torsion-free.
- If `Γ` is solvable of derived length `d`, so are the `E_M` and their union `E`, and `C(Γ)/E ≅ Z` gives length at most
  `d + 1`.

**5. Not residually finite for non-abelian `Γ`.** Choose `a, b ∈ Γ` with `[a,b] ≠ e`, and write `a_0 = δ_0(a)` and
`b_N = σ^N δ_0(b) σ^(-N)` (translation by `b` on level `N` only). For `N ≠ 0`, `a_0` and `b_N` act on different levels,
so `[a_0, b_N] = 1`.
- Let `π : C(Γ) → F` be a homomorphism to a finite group and `N = |F|!`. Then `π(σ^N) = 1`, so `π(b_N) = π(δ_0(b))`, and
  `π([a_0, δ_0(b)]) = π([a_0, b_N]) = 1`.
- But `[a_0, δ_0(b)] = δ_0([a,b]) ≠ 1`, and it lies in every finite-index normal subgroup.

**Model tests.**
- `Γ = Z`: `C(Z)` is the group of integer sequences trivial at `−∞` and eventually constant, extended by the shift.
  `δ_0(1)` is a commutator, while `σ` itself is not in the derived subgroup. That matches
  `shift-unit-of-subshift-crossed-product-is-not-elementary` for the unit of `σ`.
- `Γ = S_3`: `C(Γ)` is infinite, elementary amenable, not residually finite (item 5), and `δ_0(S_3) ≤ [C,C]`, although
  `S_3` is not perfect.
- `Γ = F_2`: `C(F_2)` is sofic and not amenable, as it must be.
