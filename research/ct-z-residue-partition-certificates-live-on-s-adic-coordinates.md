**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed). Elementary. No priority is claimed.
**Kourovka 21.74(a) itself remains OPEN.** This node carries out the next step recorded in
`ct-z-residue-partition-question-finite-vs-infinite-reading`: a lemma turning "the machine is
non-periodic" into "no partition". The literal lemma is false (Theorem 5). What is true is a
certificate that needs control of only finitely many primes (Theorems 1–4). The last section
states exactly what an undecidability proof still needs.

## Setting

- **The elements.** `g ∈ CT(Z)` is piecewise canonical, `r + mt ↦ s + nt`, on finitely many pieces
  `r(m) → s(n)`.
- **Primes.** `S` is a finite set of primes dividing every modulus `m, n` that occurs (enlarge it
  freely), and `S′` is its complement.
- **Coordinates.** `Ẑ = Ẑ_S × Ẑ_{S′}`, with `Ẑ_S = ∏_{p∈S} Z_p` and `Ẑ_{S′} = ∏_{ℓ∉S} Z_ℓ`.
  - A box `r + MẐ` is the product of the balls `r + MZ_p`. It is proper iff some prime divides `M`.
  - A proper ball of `Z_p` lies inside one residue class mod `p`.
- **YES.** `g` is YES if it permutes a partition of `Z` into at least two (finitely many) residue
  classes, equivalently a partition of `Ẑ` into at least two boxes.
- **Blocks.** A *block* is the affine map `y ↦ Uy + B` of one piece of some `ĝ^K`, with
  `U ∈ Z[1/S]^×_{>0}` and `B ∈ Z[1/S]`. It is *trivial mod `ℓ`* (`ℓ ∉ S`) if `U ≡ 1` and `B ≡ 0`
  mod `ℓ`. Otherwise it induces a non-identity affine map of `F_ℓ`, which fixes at most one residue.
- **Defect.** The *defect* of a set of blocks is the ideal of `Z[1/S]` generated by all their
  `U − 1` and `B`.

## Statement

**Theorem 1 (skew product).** There are a homeomorphism `g_S` of `Ẑ_S` and, for `x ∈ Ẑ_S`,
affine bijections `β_x` of `Ẑ_{S′}` such that `ĝ(x, y) = (g_S(x), β_x(y))`.
- `β_x` is the block of the piece containing `x`, so it depends only on that piece.
- For `K ≥ 1`, `ĝ^K(x, y) = (g_S^K(x), β^{(K)}_x(y))`, where `β^{(K)}_x` is the block of `ĝ^K` at `x`.

**Theorem 2 (localization of splitting outside `S`).**
- **Hypothesis.** `g` permutes a partition `𝒫` of `Ẑ` into `k` boxes. Put `K = k!`.
- **Claim.** Let `C = C_S × C_{S′} ∈ 𝒫`, and let `ℓ ∉ S` divide the modulus of `C`. Then
  `β^{(K)}_x` is trivial mod `ℓ` for every `x ∈ C_S`.
- **Corollary.**
  - Suppose that for every `K` and every `ℓ ∉ S` the set `{x : β^{(K)}_x trivial mod ℓ}` has empty
    interior in `Ẑ_S`.
  - Then `g` is YES iff `g_S` permutes a partition of `Ẑ_S` into at least two boxes, i.e. iff `g`
    permutes a partition into classes with `S`-smooth moduli.

**Theorem 3 (orbit-hull certificate).**
- Let `K ≥ 1` and `z ∈ Ẑ`, and suppose that for every prime `p` the forward orbit `{ĝ^{Kj}(z)}_{j≥0}`
  meets at least two residue classes mod `p`.
- Then `ĝ^K` fixes no partition of `Ẑ` into at least two boxes part by part.
- **Consequence.** If such a `z_K` exists for every `K = k!`, then `g` is not YES.

**Theorem 4 (the primes outside `S` are free).**
- Let `K ≥ 1` and `x ∈ Ẑ_S`, and let `β_j = β^{(K)}_{g_S^{Kj}(x)}` (`j ≥ 0`) be the blocks along
  the forward `g_S^K`-orbit of `x`.
- If their defect is the unit ideal of `Z[1/S]`, then some `y ∈ Ẑ_{S′}` makes the forward
  `ĝ^K`-orbit of `(x, y)` meet two residue classes mod every prime `ℓ ∉ S`.
- **Corollary (the working certificate).** Suppose that for every `K = k!` there is
  `x_K ∈ Ẑ_S` such that
  - (i) for each `p ∈ S`, its forward `g_S^K`-orbit meets two classes mod `p`, and
  - (ii) its `K`-blocks have unit defect.

  Then `g` is not YES. Condition (i) involves only the finitely many primes of `S`.

**Theorem 5 ("non-periodic ⇒ NO" fails).** For every reversible 2-counter machine `M`, periodic or
not, the element `F_M` of `ct-z-finite-cycle-problems-are-undecidable` is YES. So no lemma of the
form "the machine has a non-periodic configuration ⇒ `g_M` is NO" can hold for valuation codings.
Nor can it hold for a coding whose machine has a bit that flips at every step and is read as the
parity of the code: that element exchanges `0(2)` and `1(2)`.

## Proofs

**Theorem 1.**
- **One piece.** A piece `D = r(m) → s(n)` has `m` `S`-smooth, so `D̂ = (r + mẐ_S) × Ẑ_{S′}`.
  On `D̂`, `ĝ(x) = s + q(x − r)` with `q = n/m ∈ Z[1/S]^×`.
- **Both coordinates.** In each `Z_ℓ` with `ℓ ∉ S`, `q` is a unit. So the same formula gives
  - `g_S(x) = s + q(x − r)` on `r + mẐ_S`;
  - `β_D(y) = s + q(y − r)`, an affine bijection of `Ẑ_{S′}`.
- **Bijectivity.** `ĝ` is bijective and every `β_D` is bijective, so `g_S` is bijective.
- **Powers.** Iterating gives the formula for `ĝ^K`. On each of its pieces, `ĝ^K` is one rational
  affine map `x ↦ Ux + B`, and that is `β^{(K)}_x` in the second coordinate. ∎

**Theorem 2.** Here `ĝ^K` fixes every part.
- **Step 1: fibre maps preserve fibres of parts.** Let `C′ ∈ 𝒫` and `x ∈ C′_S`.
  - The image fibre `ĝ^K({x} × C′_{S′}) = {g_S^K(x)} × β^{(K)}_x(C′_{S′})` lies in `C′`, and
    `g_S^K(x) ∈ C′_S`. So `β^{(K)}_x(C′_{S′}) ⊆ C′_{S′}`.
  - Affine maps with unit slopes preserve Haar measure. Both sides are clopen boxes of equal
    measure, so `β^{(K)}_x(C′_{S′}) = C′_{S′}`.
  - Since `β^{(K)}_x` acts on each `Z_ℓ` by the same formula, it maps each ball `C′_ℓ` onto itself.
- **Step 2: a non-trivial block forces full `ℓ`-components.** Fix `x ∈ C_S`, put `h = β^{(K)}_x`,
  and suppose `h` is not trivial mod `ℓ`.
  - By Step 1, `C_ℓ` is an `h`-invariant proper ball, inside a class `c` mod `ℓ` that `h` fixes.
    So `c` is the only residue `h` fixes. Pick a residue `d ≠ c`.
  - Let `y ∈ C_{S′}`, and change its `ℓ`-coordinate to one `≡ d`. The part `C″ ∋ (x, y′)` is
    different from `C`, and its ball `C″_ℓ` is `h`-invariant (Step 1, since `x ∈ C″_S`) and meets
    `d + ℓZ_ℓ`. A proper such ball would make `h` fix `d`, so `C″_ℓ = Z_ℓ`.
  - Then `(x, y)` lies in both `C` and `C″`: the `ℓ`-coordinate is in `Z_ℓ = C″_ℓ`, and the other
    coordinates agree with `y′`. This contradicts disjointness.
- **Corollary.**
  - Each part has `C_S` open and nonempty. So under the hypothesis every modulus is `S`-smooth,
    i.e. every part is `C_S × Ẑ_{S′}`.
  - Such a partition is permuted by `ĝ` iff `{C_S}` is permuted by `g_S`. ∎

**Theorem 3.**
- Let `C ∋ z` be the part, `C = r + MẐ`. It is closed and `ĝ^K`-invariant, so the whole forward
  orbit lies in `C`, hence in one class mod every prime dividing `M`.
- By hypothesis no prime divides `M`, so `C = Ẑ` and the partition is trivial.
- For the consequence: if `g` permutes `k ≥ 2` boxes, then `ĝ^{k!}` fixes each of them. ∎

**Theorem 4.**
- **The fibre recursion.** `ĝ^{Kj}(x, y) = (g_S^{Kj}(x), y_j)`, with `y_0 = y` and
  `y_{j+1} = β_j(y_j)`.
- **One prime at a time.** Fix `ℓ ∉ S`.
  - Not all `β_j` are trivial mod `ℓ`, since otherwise the defect would lie in the proper ideal
    `ℓZ[1/S]`. Let `j_ℓ` be the first non-trivial index; then `y_{j_ℓ} ≡ y_0 (mod ℓ)`.
  - Choose `y_ℓ ∈ Z_ℓ` whose residue is not the (at most one) residue fixed by `β_{j_ℓ}`. Then
    `y_{j_ℓ+1} ≢ y_0 (mod ℓ)`.
- **All primes at once.** The choices are independent, because `Ẑ_{S′} = ∏ Z_ℓ` and the itinerary
  depends only on `x`. Put `y = (y_ℓ)_ℓ`.
- **Corollary.** Residues mod `p ∈ S` of `ĝ^{Kj}(x, y)` are those of `g_S^{Kj}(x)`, so (i) handles
  `p ∈ S`. Apply Theorem 3 to `z_K = (x_K, y)`. ∎

**Theorem 5.**
- `F_M` moves only points of `X ∪ 2X ⊆ 0(7)`, so it is YES by Theorem 1 of
  `ct-z-residue-partition-question-finite-vs-infinite-reading`, whatever `M` is.
- For the parity coding: if every step flips the parity of the code, then `g(0(2)) = 1(2)`. ∎

**Checks on known elements.**
- `g_0 = τ_{1(2),0(4)}τ_{0(2),1(2)}` has `S = {2}`, with blocks `2y`, `(y + 1)/2` and `y − 1`.
  The block `y − 1` is non-trivial mod every odd `ℓ`. That matches the direct proof there, where the
  part containing `0` is killed by a point of `3(4)`, which uses the block `y − 1`.
- For `ψ`, the forward block `4^K y + 2(4^K − 1)/3` near the attractor `−2/3` is trivial mod every
  prime `ℓ | 4^K − 1` with `ℓ ≠ 3`, on an open set. So the corollary of Theorem 2 does not apply.
  Theorem 2 still confines any splitting at such `ℓ` to where the blocks are trivial. The piece
  `n ↦ n − 3` is a translation block for `K = 1`, and it is non-trivial mod every `ℓ ≠ 3`.

## What an undecidability proof still needs

**Theorem 6 (conditional reduction).**
- **Setup.** Let `Φ` be a Σ₁ undecidable property of the machines of a class `𝓜`, and let
  `M ↦ g_M ∈ CT(Z)` be computable, with one fixed `S`.
- **Hypotheses.**
  - **(Y)** `Φ(M)` implies that `g_M` has finite order.
  - **(N)** `¬Φ(M)` implies that for every `K = k!` some `x_K ∈ Ẑ_S` satisfies (i) and (ii) of the
    corollary of Theorem 4.
- **Conclusion.** Then `g_M` is YES iff `Φ(M)`, so 21.74(a) is undecidable.

*Proof.* Elements of finite order are YES (Theorem 2 of
`ct-z-invariant-residue-partitions-are-not-conjugacy-invariant`). The corollary of Theorem 4 gives
NO. ∎

**Three gaps remain. None is claimed here.**
- **(G1) The source of (Y).**
  - (Y) needs an undecidable torsion problem for a computable family in `CT(Z)`.
  - Valuation codings of counter machines cannot supply it. Finite order of `F_M` is uniform
    periodicity, and uniform periodicity and uniform mortality of `k`-counter machines are decidable
    (Kari–Ollinger Thms 4, 2).
  - Whether the torsion problem of `CT(Z)` is decidable is open here. For `2V` it is reported
    undecidable (Belk–Bleak); that paper was not read at source.
  - Finite order (Theorem 2 of `ct-z-invariant-residue-partitions-are-not-conjugacy-invariant`) is
    the only general route to YES we have, besides the support lemma.
    So some such input is unavoidable.
- **(G2) The embedding.**
  - A piece acts on every `p`-adic coordinate by the same rational affine map, so there is no
    independent two-tape digit coding. A push `x ↦ 2x + d` on the 2-adic stack multiplies the 3-adic
    stack by the unit `2` and adds `d`.
  - What is needed is a reversible universal model realized directly as `g_S`, for instance
    digit-coded generalized Collatz permutations. Its uniform mortality or periodicity should be
    undecidable and Σ₁. For moving-head Turing machines, mortality is uniform by König's lemma.
- **(G3) Condition (N).**
  - Theorem 5 shows that (N) must be designed in; it cannot be inherited.
  - One candidate: a marker digit mod some `p ∈ S` that the machine raises only at a sparse
    self-timed set of steps. Any `K`-subsampling started at a raised step then sees both values.
  - A second need: a translation piece with `S`-unit shift in every `K`-block family, which gives
    (ii).

## Lesson for general BH

**Skew-product principle for arithmetic hosts.**
- In `CT_P(Z)`-type hosts, every element is a skew product: finite-type dynamics on the finitely
  many *active* coordinates `Ẑ_S`, over which the *passive* coordinates `Ẑ_{S′}` move by affine
  unit maps (Theorem 1).
- The passive coordinates cannot carry computation. Coordinate structure there is confined to where
  the cocycle is trivial (Theorem 2). Certificates on them are free: choose the fibre point
  generically, subject to one ideal condition (Theorem 4).
- So every coordinate or rigidity question about such a host reduces to its active part plus a
  cocycle ideal. Hardness, and the constraints on coordinates, both live on the active coordinates.
- **For host design.** Put the input's hard content in the active coordinates, and treat the
  passive ones as free genericity, the same resource the orbit-hull certificate uses.
