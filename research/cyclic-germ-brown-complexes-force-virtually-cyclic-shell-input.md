## Setting

- Let P be finitely generated and infinite, nu an enumeration, and E_nu the shell group.
- Let A_1 be its one-singularity stabilizer at p = 0^inf, and d: A_1 -> Q = <rho(P), tau> the
  germ map.
- For H <= Q put A(H) = d^-1(H) and K = ker d.

## Statement

1. **K is not finitely generated.** Hence A(H) is not finitely generated for finite H.
2. **Coarse orbit condition.** If A(H) is finitely generated, then N is the orbit of a finite set
   under the pseudogroup generated by representatives of finitely many elements of H. For
   example, A(rho(P)) is not finitely generated.
3. **Obstruction.** Suppose Q acts on a connected graph with:
   - every vertex stabilizer virtually infinite cyclic;
   - some vertex stabilizer containing a nonzero power of a conjugate of tau;
   - every edge stabilizer infinite.

   Then some tau^a is central in Q, and P is virtually cyclic.

## Consequence: the dying step

Consider a Brown-criterion proof of finite presentation of A_1, with A_1 acting through d on a
simply connected cocompact complex.
- Its vertex pieces must be FP and its edge pieces A(Q_e) finitely generated.
- Take the vertex pieces commensurable with Fix_V(p) = A(<tau>).
- Then every Q_e is infinite (item 1), and item 3 forces P to be virtually cyclic.

So for route inputs P_0 x Z, and for every enumeration, this approach class dies at the edge
stabilizers. Any surviving decomposition needs vertex pieces A(H) with H not virtually <tau>, and
edge pieces with the coarse orbit property of item 2.

Brown's criterion is only sufficient. Nothing here says that A_1 is not finitely presented.
