Notation as in the target.

**Imported inputs.** All four are nodes in the graph, with their sources read
there.

- **(I1)** `lcs-tracial-ru-gap-equiv-nonhyperlinear`: Paddock--Slofstra,
  arXiv:2310.07901, Proposition 5.8 and Lemma 5.7.
  - (E1) holds iff some binary linear system is tracially satisfiable but not
    `R^U`-satisfiable.
  - Tracial satisfiability is equivalent to `J != 1` in `Gamma(A,b)`.
- **(I2)** `paddock-slofstra-bcs-forbidden-projection-dictionary`, from the same
  paper (Definition 3.1, Lemma 3.4, Corollary 3.5, Theorem 3.11).
  - `A(B)` is the universal C*-algebra generated by self-adjoint involutions
    that commute within each context, with every forbidden joint spectral
    projection `Pi_(U,phi)`, `phi notin R`, set to zero.
  - The synchronous BCS game has a perfect quantum-approximable strategy iff
    there is a unital *-homomorphism `A(B) -> R^U`.
- **(I3)** `standard-form-group-separation-collapses-to-corner-model`, (SC4)
  and its proof.
  - Let `M` act on `L^2(M,tau)`, let `z in M` be a projection, and let
    `{R_a}_a` be PVMs in `zMz`.
  - Put `P_a = R_a + delta_(a,a_0)(1-z)`, `Q_b = J P_b J`, and
    `xi = z Omega/||z Omega||`.
  - Then `<P_a Q_b xi, xi> = tau(R_a R_b)/tau(z)`, using
    `J t J (s Omega) = s t^* Omega`.
  - Only this computation is used, not the game-algebra formulation of (SC4).
- **(I4)** `ce-standard-form-strategies-are-quantum-approximable`.

**(E3) implies (E2).** Solution groups of finite systems are finitely
presented, and (E3) is a special case of (E2).

**(E2) implies (E1).** If `Lambda` were hyperlinear, `L(Lambda)` with its
canonical trace would be Connes embeddable. Then (I4) would put the witness
in `C_qa`, a contradiction. This is the route
`non-hyperlinear-from-group-standard-form-correlation`.

**(E1) implies (E3).**

*Step 1 (the system).* By (I1) there is a binary linear system `Ax=b` that is
tracially satisfiable but not `R^U`-satisfiable. By Lemma 5.7, `J != 1` in
`Gamma = Gamma(A,b)`. This group is finitely presented, by generators `x_j`
and `J` with these relations:
- `x_j^2 = J^2 = 1`, and `J` is central;
- `x_j x_k = x_k x_j` whenever `x_j` and `x_k` share an equation;
- `prod_(j in U_i) x_j = J^(b_i)` for each equation `i` with support `U_i`.

*Step 2 (the corner).* Let `tau` be the canonical trace on `M = L(Gamma)`,
acting on `l^2(Gamma) = L^2(M,tau)` with `Omega = delta_e`.
- Since `J != e`, `tau(lambda(J)) = 0`.
- `lambda(J)` is a central self-adjoint unitary, so `z = (1-lambda(J))/2` is a
  central projection with `tau(z) = 1/2`, and `lambda(J) z = -z`.
- Put `u_j = lambda(x_j) z`. Each `u_j` is a self-adjoint involution in the
  unital algebra `zMz`, whose unit is `z`.
- The `u_j` commute within each equation.
- For each equation, `prod_(j in U_i) u_j = lambda(J^(b_i)) z = (-1)^(b_i) z`.

*Step 3 (the measurements).*
- For equation `i` and a local assignment `phi : U_i -> {+1,-1}`, put
  `R_(i,phi) = prod_(j in U_i) (z + phi(j) u_j)/2`. The factors commute.
- `{R_(i,phi)}_phi` is a PVM in `zMz`, the joint spectral measure of the
  commuting involutions of equation `i`.
- We have `prod_j u_j = sum_phi (prod_j phi(j)) R_(i,phi)`. Since this product
  equals `(-1)^(b_i) z`, `R_(i,phi) = 0` for every `phi` violating equation `i`.
- Rule 1: equal questions with different answers. If `phi != phi'` on the
  same equation, then `R_(i,phi) R_(i,phi') = 0`.
- Rule 2: different equations `i, i'` with local assignments that disagree on
  a shared variable `j`.
  - Every factor of `R_(i,phi)` commutes with `u_j`. Move the `j`-factor to
    the right end of `R_(i,phi)` and to the left end of `R_(i',phi')`.
  - The product then contains `(z + phi(j)u_j)(z - phi(j)u_j)/4 = 0`.
  - Hence `R_(i,phi) R_(i',phi') = 0`.

Apply (I3) with `a_0 = phi_0`. The correlation
`p(phi,phi'|i,i') = 2 tau(R_(i,phi) R_(i',phi'))` is realized by the data
listed in (E3).
- Its entries are nonnegative. For each pair `(i,i')` they sum to
  `2 tau(z) = 1`.
- By the two rules and the vanishing of violating atoms, `p` gives
  probability zero to every answer pair that loses the synchronous BCS game:
  - a violating local assignment;
  - different answers to the same equation;
  - answers that disagree on a shared variable.
- So `p` is a perfect synchronous strategy for that game, with Alice in
  `L(Gamma)`, Bob in `L(Gamma)'`, and `xi = sqrt(2) z delta_e`.

*Step 4 (not quantum approximable).* Suppose `p in C_qa`. Then the synchronous
BCS game of `Ax=b` has a perfect quantum-approximable strategy. By (I2) there
is a unital *-homomorphism `A(B) -> R^U`, so the system is
`R^U`-satisfiable. This contradicts Step 1. Hence `p notin C_qa`, and (E3)
holds.

**Scope.**
- Step 1 uses the existence statement of Paddock--Slofstra 5.8, and nothing
  about how explicit it is.
- The game rules in Step 3 are the usual synchronous BCS rules. Nothing is
  used beyond "violating atoms vanish" and "agreement on shared variables",
  which (I2) identifies with perfect strategies.
