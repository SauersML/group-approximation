Put

```text
omega=exp(2 pi i/3),                 x=diag(1,-1).
```

For `0<theta<pi/4`, let `p_theta` be the rank-one projection onto

```text
(cos(theta),sin(theta))
```

and define

```text
a_theta=omega p_theta+omega^2(1-p_theta).             (CRF1)
```

Then `x^2=1` and `a_theta^3=1`, so

```text
rho_theta(x)=x,                 rho_theta(a)=a_theta   (CRF2)
```

is an exact two-dimensional representation of `V=C_2*C_3`.  Since
`Tr(x)=0` and

```text
Tr(x p_theta)=cos^2(theta)-sin^2(theta)=cos(2 theta),
```

the ordinary character on the edge parabolic is

```text
Tr(rho_theta(t))
 =Tr(x a_theta)
 =(omega-omega^2) cos(2 theta)
 =i sqrt(3) cos(2 theta).                              (CRF3)
```

This is strictly varying on `(0,pi/4)`.  Hence the restrictions
`rho_theta|H` are pairwise inequivalent and give uncountably many elements
of the restriction image `(CIR1)`.

If that positive image were generated by finitely many restriction types,
then its value under the additive character functional

```text
[rho] -> Tr(rho(t))                                    (CRF4)
```

would lie in the additive monoid generated by finitely many complex numbers,
which is countable.  Equation `(CRF3)` gives an uncountable image, a
contradiction.  The same argument rules out a finitely generated
Grothendieck image.

The point is not merely that `H` has infinitely many abstract characters.
The continuum in `(CRF3)` consists of restrictions of exact representations
of the adjacent modular vertex itself.  Thus restricting attention to
extendible edge types does not recover the finite integer-flow setting.
