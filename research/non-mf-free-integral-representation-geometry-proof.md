Take `Q=F_r` and put `Gamma_r=C_d(F_r)`.  Free groups are finitely presented
and MF, so the camouflage construction makes `Gamma_r` finitely presented and
non-MF.  Its quotient onto `F_r` has a section and a nontrivial kernel normally
generated by the single element `d`.

A representation of `F_r` on an `A`-module is precisely a choice of `r`
automorphisms of that module, with no relations among them.  The integral
representation-geometry theorem transfers this description verbatim to
`Gamma_r`.  On a free module of rank `n`, the choices form `GL_n(A)^r`,
naturally in `A`; Yoneda gives `(FRG1)`.

The group scheme `GL_n` is smooth over `Z`, geometrically integral, and has
relative dimension `n^2`.  Products preserve these properties, proving the
first conclusion.  Smoothness gives the infinitesimal lifting property across
nilpotent extensions, hence unobstructed framed deformations.  Conjugation
equivariance and the tensor-category conclusions are inherited from the
integral representation-geometry theorem.

When `r=0`, the free group is trivial and `GL_n^0=Spec(Z)`.  The construction
still contains the nonidentity element `d`, but every finite-dimensional
linear representation over every commutative ring factors through the trivial
quotient.  This proves the final specialization.
