## Why sufficient

Complete written argument in the artifact.  The established form is the
extreme one: not finite index, but `<<pi(S)>>^G = G`, so property (T) of the
normal closure is property (T) of `G` and no inheritance step is needed.

The one new ingredient is a substitution inside Hull's own proof of
[Hul16, Corollary 7.4].  That proof picks the two infinite-order elements
`h_1, h_2` that generate the suitable subgroup by applying [Hul16, Corollary
5.7] to the free factor `G_2`.  Pick them instead from a prescribed infinite
normal subgroup `D` of `G_2`.  The picking is licensed by

- **Lemma A**: an infinite normal subgroup of a torsion-free `G in AH` is a
  suitable subgroup.  Proof: it is s-normal (`D^g = D` is infinite), so it
  acts non-elementarily by the published [Osi16, Lemma 7.1]; the
  finite-subgroup clause of Hull's Definition 1.4 is vacuous in a
  torsion-free group.

and nothing else in Hull's proof uses any property of `h_1, h_2` beyond
"infinite order, in `G_2`, non-commensurable, with `{<h_1>, <h_2>} ,->_h G_2`",
all of which [Hul16, Corollary 5.7] delivers for `D` once `D` is suitable.
The output is **Theorem B** of the artifact: a common quotient `Q` of `G_1`
and `G_2`, injective on prescribed finite sets, torsion-free if the inputs
are, finitely presented if the inputs are, with the extra clause
`alpha_2(D) = Q`.

Feeding `G_1 = H`, `G_2 = E`, `D = <<S>>^E` gives `G = pi(<<S>>^E)`, hence
`G = <<pi(S)>>^G`.  The artifact also records `defectNormal = <<pi(S)>>^G`
exactly (Lemma 0: `[t c t^{-1}, -]` generates the simple factor because
`pi(S)` is non-abelian simple and `t_1 c t_1^{-1}` is a nontrivial element of
it), so the defect is the whole group and `K = G` satisfies every hypothesis
of `normal-kazhdan-defect-non-mf`.

## Source-verified inputs

All quoted verbatim in §6 of the artifact, read from the arXiv PDFs:

- [Hul16] Hull, *Small cancellation in acylindrically hyperbolic groups*,
  arXiv:1308.4345v2: Definition 1.4, Lemma 3.5, Theorem 3.12, Corollary 5.7,
  Lemma 5.5, Lemma 5.8, Theorem 7.1 (including the torsion clause (e) and the
  relator shape in its proof), Corollary 7.4 (including the finitely
  generated case, which bypasses the direct limit of Corollary 7.3 and is
  what makes finite presentability available).
- [Osi16] Osin, *Acylindrically hyperbolic groups*, arXiv:1304.1246,
  Lemma 7.1 (s-normal subgroups act non-elementarily).
- [Osi10] Osin, *Small cancellations over relatively hyperbolic groups and
  embedding theorems*, arXiv:math/0411039, Theorem 2.4 clause 5 — the
  torsion-preservation clause Fournier-Facio cites as "Theorem 2.4.5" for the
  construction of `P`.  Verified to say what he uses it to say.
- Fournier-Facio, arXiv:2608.02025v1, §2, read in full.

## Caveat carried forward

The group is a *modification* of the published one: the artifact does not
claim that Fournier-Facio's `G` as published is normally generated by
`pi(S)`, and §3 of the artifact shows the property cannot be inherited from
`E` (always `E/<<S>>^E ->> F_2`), so it has to be created inside the
small-cancellation step.  Nothing in this route is formalised; the analytic
half it feeds is.
