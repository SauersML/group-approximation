Exhibit a triangle of finite groups with:
- trivial face group and nontrivial edge groups;
- every vertex group generated by its two edge groups;
- half-girths `m_v` of the coset graphs with `Σ_v 1/m_v < 1`;
such that no homomorphism from its colimit `G` to a finite group is injective on all
three vertex groups.

By Caprace–Conder–Kaluba–Witzel, arXiv:2011.09276, Theorem 3.1 (i), (ii), (iv), (v) (read
at source 2026-09-13), `G` is then infinite, non-elementary hyperbolic and not virtually
torsion-free. Equivalently, the triangle embeds in no finite group, meaning there is no
finite group containing the vertex groups with the given edge identifications and
pairwise intersections.

**OPEN.** This is the hyperbolic case of the embeddability problem of B. H. Neumann and
H. Neumann (Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 3 (1953), 243–256). Wise, *The residual
finiteness of negatively curved polygons of finite groups*, Invent. Math. 149 (2002),
p. 607 (read at source), says: "the embeddability problem is equivalent to the problem
of whether π_1P is virtually torsion-free", and "it seems likely that there exist
nonpositively curved polygons of finite groups that are not virtually torsion-free, but
no examples are currently known."

Literature check, bounded: Wise 2002 and Caprace–Conder–Kaluba–Witzel were read, and the
repo graph was searched. Web search was unavailable this session, so no search after
2020 was made.

## Attempts

- **More sides are residually finite (Wise).** Wise's Theorem 9.1 takes an `n`-gon of
  finite groups with every angle `<= π/2`. It is residually finite, and by his Theorem
  9.3 virtually torsion-free, when:
  - `n = 4` with two acute angles;
  - `n = 5` with one acute angle;
  - `n >= 6`.

  The proof splits `π_1 P` along a segment `J` whose endpoints lie in two disjoint edges
  and which has corners of `P` on both sides. The splitting is an amalgam of two
  virtually free groups over a finitely generated almost malnormal subgroup, and
  Theorem 1.3 of that paper applies.

  In a triangle, any such `J` leaves a single vertex `v` on one side. That side is the
  finite group `A_v`. The would-be amalgamated subgroup `E_e * E_e'` does not inject
  into `A_v`: it maps onto `A_v` with a free kernel of finite index. So `G` is the
  quotient `G_0/<<N>>` of the virtually free group `G_0` by that kernel
  (`triangle-colimit-llp-reduces-to-relator-local-splitting`, (TC2)), not an amalgam,
  and the separability argument has no foothold. Triangles are exactly the polygons
  Wise's method cannot reach.

- **Simple vertex groups.** `simple-vertex-triangles-are-universal-for-triangle-vtf`
  shows this claim is equivalent to
  `hyperbolic-simple-triangle-group-without-finite-quotients`. There, having one
  nontrivial finite quotient is the whole test. Explicit `(A_7, A_7, A_8)` candidates and
  the Caprace–Conder–Kaluba–Witzel search data are recorded in that node.

- **Order-2 edge groups never work.** By (U6) of the same node, if all three edge
  groups have order 2 the core is a Coxeter group. It is linear, so the triangle embeds
  in a finite group whatever the curvature.

- **Where it dies.** Showing that no vertex-injective finite quotient exists means
  excluding every finite overgroup of the three vertex groups with the prescribed
  intersections. That is the large-quotient barrier recorded in
  `hyperbolic-simple-triangle-group-without-finite-quotients`.
