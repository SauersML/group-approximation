Notation as in the target. Write `K4 = {1, a, b, ab}` for the coset
representatives of `L` in `P`, and `f -> f^c` for conjugation by `c` on `Z[L]`,
so that `c f = f^c c`. By `promislow-group-ring-units-are-constant-reduced-norm`
(table there), `a` inverts `y` and `z`, `b` inverts `x` and `z`, and `ab`
inverts `x` and `y`.

**Step 1 (item 1).** Let `m = (Y, Z) Z[L]` and `M = { sum_c f_c c : f_c in m }`.

- `m` is stable under every conjugation `f -> f^c`: each generator goes to a unit
  multiple of itself, since `y^-1 + 1 = y^-1 Y` and `z^-1 + 1 = z^-1 Z`, while
  `x` is fixed or inverted without touching `Y, Z`.
- `M` is a left `Z[L]`-submodule. For `c, d in K4` there are `l in L` and
  `e in K4` with `c d = l e`. Then `d (f c) = f^d (d c)`, with `d c = l' e'`
  and `f^d l' in m`, so `d M` is in `M`. Also `(f c) d = f (c d) = (f l) e`,
  with `f l in m`, so `M d` is in `M`. For `l in L`, `(f c) l = f l^c c` is in
  `M`. So `M` is a two-sided ideal.
- `M` contains `Y` and `Z`, so it contains the two-sided ideal `I_A` they
  generate. Conversely, if `f = g Y + h Z` with `g, h in Z[L]`, then `f c` is in
  `I_A`, so `M` is contained in `I_A`.

Hence `I_A = M`. The same argument, with `(X, Z)` and `(X, Y)`, gives `I_B` and
`I_C`.

**Step 2.** The decomposition `Z[P] = sum_c Z[L] c` is direct, so
`I_A cap I_B cap I_C = { sum_c f_c c : f_c in (Y,Z) cap (X,Z) cap (X,Y) }`.

**Step 3 (item 2).** `Z[L] = Z[x, y, z][x^-1, y^-1, z^-1]` is the localization
of `R = Z[x, y, z] = Z[X, Y, Z]` at the multiplicative set generated by
`x, y, z`. In `R`, the ideals `(Y, Z)`, `(X, Z)`, `(X, Y)` and `(XY, XZ, YZ)` are
monomial ideals in the variables `X, Y, Z`. A polynomial lies in a monomial
ideal exactly when each of its terms does, because the ideal is spanned over `Z`
by the monomials it contains. A monomial `X^i Y^j Z^k` lies in:

- `(Y, Z)` iff `j + k >= 1`;
- `(X, Z)` iff `i + k >= 1`;
- `(X, Y)` iff `i + j >= 1`.

All three hold iff at least two of `i, j, k` are positive, i.e. iff the monomial
is divisible by `XY`, `XZ` or `YZ`. So in `R`,
`(Y, Z) cap (X, Z) cap (X, Y) = (XY, XZ, YZ)`. Localization is exact, and the
intersection of finitely many ideals is the kernel of `R -> sum R / I_i`, so it
commutes with localization. The equality therefore holds in `Z[L]`.

The lines: `X, Y, Z` vanish on `x = -1`, `y = -1`, `z = -1`. So `(XY, XZ, YZ)`
vanishes on the union of `{Y = Z = 0}`, `{X = Z = 0}` and `{X = Y = 0}`. Over
`C` it is the (radical) ideal of that union, since the three monomial ideals are
the ideals of the three lines.

**Step 4 (item 3).** Item 3 of
`promislow-integral-units-have-monomial-definite-line-shadows` gives
`v - epsilon in I_A cap I_B cap I_C` for a unit `v` with `v = 1 mod 2`. Apply
Steps 2 and 3.

QED
