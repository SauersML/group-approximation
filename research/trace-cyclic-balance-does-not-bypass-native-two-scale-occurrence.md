**ESTABLISHED TYPING FENCE.**  The scalar lemmas
`two-oriented-trace-cyclic-returns-cancel-the-gauge` and
`trace-cyclic-triangle-balances-independent-return-gauges` do not close the
native binary-Leavitt two-scale compiler.

Write the two controlled selector outputs as

```text
T_i=C_(q_i) tensor V_i,       G_i=(1+V_i)/2,
(r_1,q_1)=(1/2,1/4),         (r_2,q_2)=(1/4,1/8).     (TCB1)
```

The affine rank contradiction needs equality of the normalized gauge ranks

```text
theta_i=rank(G_i)/dim(M_i).                             (TCB2)
```

A proposed third cyclic edge consists of block maps `A,B` and estimates

```text
tr(BA) approx tr(G_0),       tr(AB) approx tr(G_1).     (TCB3)
```

Cyclicity only gives equality of the two traces in the **one ambient finite
matrix algebra in which `(TCB3)` has been decoded**.  If the multiplicity
reservoirs have not first been identified with equal normalized carrier
mass, this equates raw ambient ranks and does not imply `(TCB2)`.  If they
have been so identified, then `(TCB3)` is already the required same-occurrence
rank coupling: it is a scalar replacement for the cross-Gram theorem, not a
consequence of cyclicity.

The exact version makes the obstruction transparent.  If

```text
BA=G_0,                  AB=G_1,                       (TCB4)
```

then the two gauge corners are Morita-equivalent through `A,B`, and finite
matrix cyclicity forces their ranks to agree.  Constructing `(TCB4)` across
the depth-one and next-refinement gauges is precisely an object-erasing
return after a net binary refinement.  The mixed Steinberg triangles do not
provide it: `mixed-steinberg-loops-admit-morita-rectangular-model` realizes
all such triangles in a finite linear category with distinct source,
intermediate, and target identities.  There, cyclic products close inside
their own typed objects while the two external multiplicity gauges remain
independent.

The native direct/composite identity

```text
(s_(000)t_(00))(s_(00)t_0)=s_(000)t_0                (TCB5)
```

does not change this conclusion.  It authenticates associativity of the
coefficient arrows, not a pair `(A,B)` whose two cyclic products are the
positive spectral projections of `V_1,V_2` on one prescribed packet
carrier.  Compressing `(TCB5)` by those spectral projections is exactly the
nonfunctorial selector-occurrence step still missing from the presentation.

The two-orientation lemma is stronger, not weaker, at this interface: using
one literal hidden projection `G` in both returns assumes that the two native
occurrences have already selected the same gauge corner.  Allowing independent
`G_0,G_1` moves that assumption to `(TCB3)` but does not remove it.

Nor does `same-core-involution-cubic-gauge-coercivity` apply.  Its relative
gauge has the form `x_1=cx_0` for two endpoints over one fixed BS/Iwahori core
and requires `[c,t]=0`.  The binary-Leavitt scale transports have different
typed prefix objects before the missing return, and no common core operator
`t` or involution/cubic endpoint pair is present.  Supplying such a common
core would again be an object-erasing occurrence theorem.

Consequently a trace-cyclic attack can replace `(NCG1)` only by proving a
new native finite-coordinate balance row with the same load-bearing content:
two proper returns plus `(TCB3)` on the actual selector occurrences.  The
abstract scalar triangle identities, coefficient associativity, and the
same-core cubic estimate provide no shorter route to that row.
