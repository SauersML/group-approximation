This is a citation import followed by a written assembly. The assembly reruns the
imported induction under a weaker hypothesis.

**Source.** E. Godelle and L. Paris, *Basic questions on Artin-Tits groups*,
arXiv:1105.1048v1 (2011-05-05). It was read on 2026-09-16 from the arXiv LaTeX source.

- **Parts read:**
  - the abstract;
  - the paragraph defining standard parabolic subgroups;
  - the definitions of syllabic expressions and reductions;
  - Theorem 3.3;
  - Corollary 3.4 and its proof;
  - Theorem 3.5;
  - the proof of Theorem C.
- **Not read:**
  - a proof of Theorem 3.3, which the survey states without proof as "well-known" and
    credits to Serre;
  - the proof of Theorem 3.5, which the survey credits to Godelle–Paris, arXiv:1007.1365
    (Math. Z. 272 (2012)). There I read Proposition 2.7 and its proof, but not Lemma 2.6
    or the construction of the retraction `π_T` that the proof uses.
  - I did not look for a journal version of the survey.

**Verbatim from the survey.**

- Parabolic subgroups: "For X⊂S, we set M_X = (m_{s,t})_{s,t ∈ X}, we denote by Γ_X the
  Coxeter graph of M_X, […] we set Σ_X = { σ_s; s ∈ X}, and we denote by A_X the subgroup
  of A = A_Γ generated by Σ_X. It is known that (W_X,X) is the Coxeter system of Γ_X (see
  [Bourb1]), and that (A_X,Σ_X) is the Artin-Tits system of Γ_X (see [Lek1, Paris1,
  GodPar1])." Here [Lek1] is H. van der Lek's thesis.
- Amalgam: "Let Γ be a Coxeter graph which is not free of infinity. Let s,t ∈ S, s ≠ t,
  such that m_{s,t} = ∞. Set X=S∖{s}, Y=S∖{t}, and Z = S∖{s,t}. Recall that (A_X, Σ_X)
  (resp. (A_Y, Σ_Y), (A_Z, Σ_Z)) is an Artin-Tits system of type Γ_X (resp. Γ_Y, Γ_Z), and
  that the natural homomorphisms A_Z → A_X and A_Z → A_Y are injective. Then the following
  equality is a direct consequence of the presentation of A. A = A_X *_{A_Z} A_Y."
- Before Corollary 3.4: "we will assume for the next corollary that H_1,H_2, and K are
  finitely generated, and we take finite generating sets S_1,S_2, and T, for H_1,H_2, and
  K, respectively."
- Corollary 3.4: "Assume that, for each j ∈ {1,2}, (a) H_j has a solution to the word
  problem; and (b) there exists an algorithm which, given a word w ∈ (S_j ⊔ S_j^{-1})*,
  decides whether β=[w]_{S_j} belongs to K and, if yes, determines a word
  u ∈ (T ⊔ T^{-1})* which represents β. Then G=H_1 *_K H_2 has a solution to the word
  problem."
  - Its proof reduces a syllabic pre-expression, deleting trivial syllables and merging a
    syllable in `K` into its neighbour. It then uses Theorem 3.3: `α = 1` if and only if
    the reduced expression is empty.
- Theorem 3.5 (Godelle, Paris): "Let Γ be a Coxeter graph, let S be be its set of
  vertices, let X ⊂ S, and let (A,Σ) be the Artin-Tits system of Γ. If A has a solution to
  the word problem, then there is an algorithm which, given a word
  w ∈ (Σ ⊔ Σ^{-1})*, decides whether α = [w]_Σ belongs to A_X, and, if yes, determines a
  word w' ∈ (Σ_X ⊔ Σ_X^{-1})* which represents α."
- Theorem C: "If all free of infinity Artin-Tits groups have solutions to the word
  problem, then all Artin-Tits groups will have solutions to the word problem."
  - Its proof is an induction on `|S|`. The free-of-infinity case is the hypothesis.
  - Otherwise the proof splits along `m_{s,t} = ∞` as above and applies the inductive
    hypothesis to `A_X` and `A_Y`.
  - It then uses Theorem 3.5 "for T=X or Y" to decide membership in `A_Z`, and concludes
    "by Corollary 3.4".

**Assembly for one graph.** For `n ≥ 1`, let `P(n)` be the statement:

> for every Coxeter graph `Γ` on a set `S` with `|S| = n`, if `A_{Γ_U}` has solvable word
> problem for every free-of-infinity `U ⊆ S`, then `A_Γ` has solvable word problem.

We prove `P(n)` by induction on `n`. Fix `Γ` on `S` with `|S| = n` satisfying the
hypothesis.

1. If `Γ` is free of infinity, take `U = S`.
2. Otherwise choose `s ≠ t` with `m_st = ∞`, and set `X = S∖{s}`, `Y = S∖{t}` and
   `Z = S∖{s,t}`.
   - By the amalgam quote, `A_Γ = A_X *_{A_Z} A_Y`.
   - By the parabolic quote, `A_X`, `A_Y` and `A_Z` are the Artin groups of `Γ_X`, `Γ_Y`
     and `Γ_Z` on the generators `Σ_X`, `Σ_Y` and `Σ_Z`.
3. Every free-of-infinity subset `U` of `X` is a free-of-infinity subset of `S`, and the
   graph induced on `U` from `Γ_X` is `Γ_U`. So `Γ_X` satisfies the hypothesis of
   `P(n − 1)`, and `A_X` has solvable word problem. The same holds for `Y`.
4. Apply Theorem 3.5 to the graph `Γ_X` and its vertex subset `Z`. It gives an algorithm
   that decides, for a word over `Σ_X^{±}`, whether the element lies in the subgroup
   generated by `Σ_Z`, and if so writes a word over `Σ_Z^{±}`. This is hypothesis (b) of
   Corollary 3.4 with `H_1 = A_X`, `S_1 = Σ_X`, `K = A_Z` and `T = Σ_Z`. The same holds
   for `H_2 = A_Y`.
5. All three groups are finitely generated, and hypothesis (a) is step 3. Corollary 3.4
   gives a solvable word problem for `A_Γ`.

The survey's proof uses its global hypothesis only in step 1, for the graph `Γ` itself. In
steps 3–5 it uses only the word problems of `A_X` and `A_Y`. So the induction goes through
unchanged under the hypothesis of `P(n)`.

**Maximal subsets.** Suppose `A_{Γ_X}` has solvable word problem for every maximal
free-of-infinity `X ⊆ S`. Let `U ⊆ S` be free of infinity.

1. Since `S` is finite, `U` lies in a maximal free-of-infinity `X`.
2. Apply the parabolic quote to the graph `Γ_X` and its subset `U`. The subgroup of
   `A_{Γ_X}` generated by `Σ_U` is the Artin group of `Γ_U`. So the homomorphism
   `A_{Γ_U} → A_{Γ_X}` sending each generator to itself is injective.
3. A word over `Σ_U^{±}` is therefore trivial in `A_{Γ_U}` if and only if it is trivial in
   `A_{Γ_X}`, and the latter is decidable.

So the hypothesis of `P(|S|)` holds, and `A_Γ` has solvable word problem. ∎

**Independent account.** J. Gálvez Mateos, F. Gavazzi and L. Paris, *Parabolic subgroups
and word problem in virtual Artin groups*, arXiv:2602.23819v1 (2026-02-27), read
2026-09-16, gives the same two ingredients. Its proofs were not checked in detail.

- Proposition 2.13: "Suppose that the word problem in A[Γ] is solvable. Let X ⊆ S. Then
  the strong membership problem for A[Γ_X] in A[Γ] is solvable."
- Proposition 2.18: "Let G_1, G_2, and H be finitely generated groups. Suppose that H is
  a subgroup of G_1 and G_2, and set G = G_1 *_H G_2. If the word problem in G_j is
  solvable for each j ∈ {1,2}, and the strong membership problem for H in G_j is solvable
  for each j ∈ {1,2}, then the word problem in G is solvable."
- Its introduction says that "Artin groups often satisfy the principle that, if a
  property holds for all subgroups A_X[Γ] ≃ A[Γ_X] with X free of infinity, then it holds
  for A[Γ] itself (see [GP12])".
