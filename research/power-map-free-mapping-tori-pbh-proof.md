Let `G = G_φ` with `φ(x_i) = x_π(i)^(m_i)`, and let `k` be the order of `π`.

## Step 1. The diagonal index-k subgroup

**The power of φ.** Induction on `r` gives
`φ^r(x_i) = x_(π^r(i))^(m_(π^(r-1)(i)) ··· m_(π(i)) m_i)`. So
`φ^k(x_i) = x_i^(M_i)` with `M_i = ∏_(j=0)^(k-1) m_(π^j(i)) ≠ 0`.

**Standard facts** (HNN normal form):
- `F_n` embeds in `G` (Britton's lemma).
- Every element of `G` has the form `t^-p w t^q` with `p, q >= 0` and `w ∈ F_n`.
  The proof is the closure computation in `pbh-relative-holomorph-closure-proof`,
  Step 5, with `J = F_n`.

**Index.** Let `χ : G -> Z` send `t -> 1` and each `x_i -> 0`, and let
`G' = <x_1, ..., x_n, t^k>`. Then `G' ⊆ χ^-1(kZ)`. For the converse, take
`g = t^-p w t^q` with `k | q - p`, and choose `r >= 0` with `k | p + r`. Then
`g = t^-(p+r) φ^r(w) t^(q+r)`, and both exponents are multiples of `k`, so
`g ∈ G'`. So `G' = χ^-1(kZ)` has index `k`.

**Presentation.** Let `G_k = < x_1, ..., x_n, s | s x_i s^-1 = x_i^(M_i) >`. The
map `x_i -> x_i`, `s -> t^k` is well defined, since `t^k x_i t^-k = φ^k(x_i)`,
and it is onto `G'`. It is injective: an element `s^-p w s^q` of `G_k` maps to
`t^-kp w t^kq`. If that is trivial, `χ` forces `p = q`, and then `w = 1` in
`F_n <= G`. So `G' ≅ G_k`.

## Step 2. An affine model

**The host group.** For each `i` let `Aff_i <= GL_2(Q)` be generated by

    u_i = [[1, 1], [0, 1]]   and   σ_i = [[M_i, 0], [0, 1]].

Here `σ_i u_i σ_i^-1 = [[1, M_i], [0, 1]] = u_i^(M_i)`, and `u_i` has infinite
order. Put `M = Aff_1 × ... × Aff_n`, a finitely generated subgroup of
`GL_2n(Q)` (block diagonal). By
`char-zero-linear-groups-satisfy-permutational-boone-higman`, `M ∈ B_A`. Write
`u_i` also for its copy in the `i`-th factor of `M`, and put
`σ = (σ_1, ..., σ_n) ∈ M`. Then `σ u_i σ^-1 = u_i^(M_i)` in `M`.

**The automorphism.** Let `Q = M * <z>`, and let `β ∈ Aut(Q)` be `c_σ` on `M` and
the identity on `z`. Its inverse is `c_(σ^-1) * id`. It restricts on `M` to
conjugation by `σ ∈ Q`.

**The subgroup.** Put `y_i = z^-(i-1) u_i z^(i-1)` and `J = <y_1, ..., y_n>`.
- `β(y_i) = z^-(i-1) σ u_i σ^-1 z^(i-1) = y_i^(M_i)`, so `β(J) ⊆ J`.
- **J is free on the y_i.** Take a product `c_1 ··· c_r` with
  `c_j = y_(i_j)^(a_j)`, `a_j ≠ 0` and `i_j ≠ i_(j+1)`. Put `e_j = i_j - 1`. The
  product equals

      z^(-e_1) u_(i_1)^(a_1) z^(e_1 - e_2) u_(i_2)^(a_2) ··· u_(i_r)^(a_r) z^(e_r).

  The syllables `u_(i_j)^(a_j)` lie in `M \ {1}`, and the inner syllables
  `z^(e_j - e_(j+1))` in `<z> \ {1}`. So this is a reduced word of positive length
  in the free product `M * <z>`, hence nontrivial.
- **Conjugacy.** The isomorphism `F_n -> J`, `x_i -> y_i`, intertwines `φ^k` with
  `β|_J`. So `G_k ≅ J *_β`.

## Step 3. Conclusion

- **The subgroup.** By part (T) of `pbh-class-closed-under-relative-holomorphs`,
  applied with `M`, `m = 1`, `Q`, `β`, `J` and `k = σ`, `G' ≅ J *_β` lies in `B_A`.
- **The whole group.** `G` contains `G'` with index `k`, so part 2 of
  `boone-higman-type-a-class-closed-under-finite-extensions` puts `G` in `B_A`.
- **Envelope.** `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups` gives solvable
  word problem and a finitely presented simple envelope. ∎
