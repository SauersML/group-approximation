Notation is as in the target. The only imported analytic facts are textbook ones:
- the spectral theorem for a unitary operator, with its Borel functional calculus;
- the von Neumann mean ergodic theorem, `(1/N) Σ_{M<N} U^M ξ → P_inv ξ`.

**Step 0 (G is perfect).** The argument is Step 1 of
`brin-thompson-nv-fibre-elements-are-cubically-elliptic-proof`, repeated here.
- Let `D ≤ A` be generated by the elements `h·f − f`. Since `h f h^{-1} f^{-1} = h·f − f`,
  `D ≤ [G,G]`.
- `A` is generated by the cylinder indicators `1_{C(u)}`.
- For nonempty words `u, u'`, some `h ∈ V` maps `C(u)` onto `C(u')`, so
  `1_{C(u)} ≡ 1_{C(u')} mod D`. Then `1_{C(0)} = 1_{C(00)} + 1_{C(01)} ≡ 2·1_{C(0)}`, so
  `1_{C(0)} ∈ D`, and so is every `1_{C(u)}`, including `1_C = 1_{C(0)} + 1_{C(1)}`.
- Hence `D = A`.
- `V` is infinite, simple and nonabelian (`thompson-v-finitely-presented-infinite-simple`), so
  `V = [V,V]`.

Therefore `[G,G] = G`, and every homomorphism from `G` to an abelian group is trivial.

**Step 1 (the commutation identity).** The cocycle identity `b(gh) = b(g) + π(g)b(h)` and
`zg = gz` give `b(z) + π(z)b(g) = b(g) + π(g)b(z)`, that is,

```text
(π(z) − 1) b(g) = (π(g) − 1) b(z)      for all g ∈ G.          (★)
```

**Step 2 (the decomposition).**
- Every Borel function of `π(z)` commutes with the operators commuting with `π(z)`, and
  `π(z)` is central in `π(G)`. So the spectral projections `Q_1` (onto `H_1`) and `P_j`
  commute with `π(G)`.
- The shells `{e(t) : 2^{-j-2} < |t| ≤ 2^{-j-1}}`, `j ≥ 0`, together with `{1}` partition the
  circle (take `t ∈ (−1/2, 1/2]`). So `Q_1 + Σ_j P_j = 1` strongly.
- Each component `Q_1 b`, `P_j b` is a cocycle for the corresponding subrepresentation, and
  `ψ(g) = ‖Q_1 b(g)‖² + Σ_j ‖P_j b(g)‖²`.

**Step 3 (the shells are coboundaries).**
- On `P_jH` the spectrum of `π(z)` lies in `{e(t) : |t| > 2^{-j-2}}`, where
  `|e(t) − 1| = 2|sin πt| ≥ 4|t| > 2^{-j}`. So `π(z) − 1` is invertible on `P_jH`, with inverse
  of norm `< 2^j`.
- Apply `P_j` to (★) and invert: `b_j(g) = (π(g) − 1) ξ_j` with
  `ξ_j = (π(z) − 1)^{-1} b_j(z)`.

This proves item 2 of Theorem A.

**Step 4 (the fixed part vanishes at z).**
- Apply `Q_1` to (★): the left side is `0`, since `π(z) = 1` on `H_1`. So `(π(g) − 1) b_1(z) = 0`
  for all `g`, i.e. `b_1(z)` lies in the space `H_1^G` of `G`-invariant vectors of `H_1`.
- Let `R` be the orthogonal projection onto `H_1^G`. It commutes with `π(G)`, and `π(G)` acts
  trivially on its range. So `Rb_1` satisfies `Rb_1(gh) = Rb_1(g) + Rb_1(h)`: it is a
  homomorphism from `G` to the additive group of `H_1^G`.
- By Step 0 it is zero. Hence `b_1(z) = R b_1(z) = 0`.

This proves item 1.

**Step 5 (growth along <z>).**
- By Step 4 and the cocycle identity, `b_1(z^M) = Σ_{0≤k<M} π(z)^k b_1(z) = 0` for `M ≥ 0`,
  and `b(z^{-M}) = −π(z^{-M}) b(z^M)`.
- By Step 3, for `M ≥ 0`: `ψ(z^{±M}) = Σ_j ‖(π(z)^M − 1) ξ_j‖²`, which is at most `4S`.
- Lower bound. On `P_jH` the unitary `U = π(z)` has no nonzero fixed vector. By the mean
  ergodic theorem,

  ```text
  (1/N) Σ_{M<N} ‖(U^M − 1)ξ_j‖² = 2‖ξ_j‖² − 2 Re⟨(1/N) Σ_{M<N} U^M ξ_j, ξ_j⟩ → 2‖ξ_j‖².
  ```

  So for every `J`, the Cesàro means of `ψ(z^M)` have liminf at least `2 Σ_{j≤J} ‖ξ_j‖²`, and
  `sup_M ψ(z^M) ≥ 2S`. This proves item 3.
- If `S < ∞`, then `ξ = Σ_j ξ_j` converges, and `b` restricted to `H_1^⊥` is `(π(g) − 1)ξ`.
  Conversely, if that restriction equals `(π(g) − 1)η`, then `ψ(z^M) ≤ 4‖η‖²` for all `M`,
  because the `H_1` part of `b(z^M)` vanishes.

This proves Theorem A.

**Step 6 (Theorem B, (a) ⇒ (b)).**
- Let `ψ` be unbounded on `<z>`. By Theorem A, `S = ∞`.
- Put `ξ_{≤J} = Σ_{j≤J} ξ_j` and `η_J = ξ_{≤J}/‖ξ_{≤J}‖` (for large `J`). By Step 3, and since
  `Σ_{j≤J} P_j` commutes with `π(g)`,

  ```text
  ‖(π(g) − 1) η_J‖ = ‖Σ_{j≤J} P_j b(g)‖ / ‖ξ_{≤J}‖ ≤ ψ(g)^{1/2} / ‖ξ_{≤J}‖ → 0.
  ```

- So the subrepresentation on `H_1^⊥` has almost invariant vectors and no nonzero
  `π(z)`-invariant vector.

**Step 7 (Theorem B, (b) ⇒ (a)).**
- `G` is countable. Let `F_1 ⊆ F_2 ⊆ ⋯` be finite sets exhausting `G`, and pick unit vectors
  `ξ_k` with `‖(π(g) − 1)ξ_k‖ ≤ 2^{-k}` for `g ∈ F_k`.
- Put `ψ(g) = Σ_k ‖(π(g) − 1)ξ_k‖²`. For fixed `g`, all but finitely many terms are at most
  `4^{-k}`, so `ψ` is finite.
- It is cnd: each term is the cnd function of the coboundary `(π(g) − 1)ξ_k`, and a pointwise
  convergent sum of cnd functions is cnd.
- Since `π(z)` has no nonzero fixed vector, the computation of Step 5 gives Cesàro means of
  `‖(π(z)^M − 1)ξ_k‖²` tending to `2`. So the first `K` terms have Cesàro means tending to
  `2K`, and `sup_M ψ(z^M) ≥ 2K` for every `K`.

**Step 8 (the spectral laws).**
- `A` is a countable discrete abelian group. By the spectral theorem for its unitary
  representation `π|_A` (SNAG; e.g. Folland, *A course in abstract harmonic analysis*,
  Thm 4.44), there is a projection-valued measure `E` on the compact dual `Â` with
  `π(f) = ∫ χ(f) dE(χ)`.
- Let `V` act on `Â` by `(vχ)(f) = χ(v^{-1}·f)`. Then `π(v)π(f)π(v)^{-1} = π(v·f)` gives
  `π(v) E(B) π(v)^{-1} = E(vB)`. So the law `μ_η(B) = ⟨E(B)η, η⟩` of a unit vector satisfies
  `μ_{π(v)η} = v_* μ_η`.
- For projections `E` and unit vectors `x, y`:
  `|⟨Ex,x⟩ − ⟨Ey,y⟩| = |⟨Ex, x−y⟩ + ⟨E(x−y), y⟩| ≤ 2‖x − y‖`. Hence
  `sup_B |v_*μ_η(B) − μ_η(B)| ≤ 2‖π(v)η − η‖ → 0`.
- `E({χ : χ(z) = 1})` is the projection `Q_1` onto `H_1`, so for vectors in `H_1^⊥` the law of
  `{χ(1_C) = 1}` is `0`.
- `∫ |χ(f) − 1|² dμ_η = ‖(π(f) − 1)η‖² → 0` for every `f ∈ A`. The functions `χ ↦ χ(f)`
  separate points of `Â` and span a dense *-subalgebra of `C(Â)` (Stone–Weierstrass), so
  `μ_η → δ_0` weakly.

This proves Theorem B.
