**Imported theorem (Cornulier).** Y. de Cornulier, *Finitely presented wreath
products and double coset decompositions*, arXiv:math/0509090, TeX lines
147--157, read 2026-09-13 from the source on MSI, verbatim:

> "If $W\neq \{1\}$, the wreath product $W\wr_X G$ is finitely presented if and
> only if the following conditions are satisfied (i) $W$ and $G$ are finitely
> presented, (ii) $G$ acts on $X$ with finitely generated stabilizers, and (iii)
> the product action of $G$ on the cartesian square $X^2$ has finitely many
> orbits."

**Two remarks** (as in `type-a-products-finite-wreaths-proof`). Since `|S| >= 2`,
every point lies in a two-element subset, so finitely many orbits of two-element
subsets give finitely many point orbits. Then finitely many orbits on ordered
pairs of distinct points follow, and with the point orbits (the diagonal),
finitely many orbits on `S^2`.

**(LW) is an action.** Apply `(f', gamma')` and then `(f, gamma)`:
`(s, b) -> (gamma' s, f'(gamma' s) b) -> (gamma gamma' s, f(gamma gamma' s) f'(gamma' s) b)`.
The product `(f, gamma)(f', gamma') = (f . (gamma . f'), gamma gamma')` sends
`(s, b)` to `(gamma gamma' s, f(gamma gamma' s) f'(gamma^-1 gamma gamma' s) b)`,
the same point.

**1. Faithful.** If `(f, gamma)` fixes every `(s, b)`, then `gamma` fixes every
`s`, so `gamma = 1` by faithfulness of `Gamma`. Then `f(s) b = b` for all `b`, so
`f(s) = 1` for every `s`, and `f = 1`.

**2. Finitely presented.** `B` is finite, hence finitely presented, and `Gamma`
is finitely presented. Point stabilizers of `Gamma` on `S` are finitely
generated, and by the remarks `Gamma` has finitely many orbits on `S^2`.
Cornulier's theorem applies with `W = B != 1`, `X = S`, `G = Gamma`.

**3. Stabilizers finitely generated.** The stabilizer of `(s, b)` is
`{(f, gamma) : gamma s = s, f(s) = 1} = B^(S \ {s}) x| Stab_Gamma(s)`.
- `Stab_Gamma(s)` is finitely generated.
- `Stab_Gamma(s)` has finitely many orbits on `S \ {s}`: the ordered pairs
  `(s, t)`, `t != s`, lie in finitely many `Gamma`-orbits, and two such pairs are
  in one orbit exactly when `t` and `t'` are in one `Stab_Gamma(s)`-orbit. Let
  `t_1, ..., t_m` be representatives.
- Every function in `B^(S \ {s})` is a finite product of functions supported at
  one point `t = gamma t_i` with `gamma in Stab_Gamma(s)`, and such a function
  is `gamma . (c at t_i)`. So the stabilizer is generated by generators of
  `Stab_Gamma(s)` and the finite groups `B` at `t_1, ..., t_m`.

**4. Finitely many orbits of two-element subsets of `S x B`.** Take
`{(s, b), (s', b')}`.
- *If `s != s'`.* Move `{s, s'}` by `Gamma` to one of finitely many
  representative two-element subsets `{r, r'}`. A lamp function supported on
  `{r, r'}` then changes the two lamp values independently to `1`. So the orbit
  is determined by the `Gamma`-orbit of `{s, s'}` (and which point of the pair
  is which, a factor of at most 2): finitely many.
- *If `s = s'`, `b != b'`.* Move `s` to one of finitely many point
  representatives `r`. The remaining freedom only matters inside `{r} x B`, and
  there are finitely many two-element subsets of `B`: finitely many orbits.

So (LW) is of type (A). By `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`
(Zaremsky, arXiv:2405.18354, Corollary B, quoted there verbatim), every subgroup
of `W` has solvable word problem and embeds in a finitely presented simple group.
`∎`

**Scope.** Finiteness of `B` is used twice: for finite presentation (any finitely
presented `B` would do there) and in the case `s = s'` of clause 4. With
`B = Z` that case gives infinitely many orbits `{(r, 0), (r, n)}`, so the
statement does not extend to infinite lamps in this form.
