**ESTABLISHED** through `rf-resolvent-ring-has-z-localized-corner-proof`. Lane proof,
not independently reviewed. No priority is claimed.

**Construction.** Fix a prime `l`. For `r >= 1` put `P_r = l^(r-1)` and let
`M_r` be the free `Z/l^r`-module with basis `f_j`, `j in Z/P_r`. Define
`Z/l^r`-linear maps of `M_r` by

    u f_j = l j f_j,   x f_j = f_(j-1),   x' f_j = f_(j+1),
    B f_j = (1 + l j)^(-1) f_j,   e f_j = δ_(j,0) f_j .

The elements `l j` and `(1 + l j)^(-1)` of `Z/l^r` depend only on `j mod l^(r-1)`,
and `1 + l j` is a unit. Let `R̄_l` be the subring of `∏_(r>=1) End(M_r)` generated by
the five elements `u, x, x', B, e`, each taken componentwise.

**Statement.**
1. **Relations.** In `R̄_l`:
   `x x' = x' x = 1`, `x u = (u + l) x`, `B (u+1) = (u+1) B = 1`, `u e = e u = 0`,
   `e^2 = e`. So `R̄_l` is a quotient of the finitely presented ring
   `R_l = Z<u, x, x', B, e | these eight relations>`.
2. **Residually finite.** Each `End(M_r)` is finite, and the projections to them
   separate the points of `R̄_l`.
3. **Resolvents.** For every integer `m ≡ 1 (mod l)`, write `m = 1 + k l`. Then `u + m`
   is a unit of `R̄_l`, with `(u + m)^(-1) = x^k B x^(-k)` (where `x^(-1) = x'`).
4. **The corner contains Z_(l).** Put `y_m = (u+m)^(-1) e` for `m ≡ 1 (mod l)`. Then
   `y_m in e R̄_l e` and `m y_m = e`. The ring homomorphism `Z -> e R̄_l e`, `n -> n e`,
   extends to a unital ring homomorphism `θ : Z_(l) -> e R̄_l e`, and `θ` is injective.
   In the component `r`, `θ(c)` acts on `f_0` by `c mod l^r` and kills every other `f_j`.
5. **Not reachable by the usual reductions.**
   - `R̄_l / l R̄_l` is infinite: `x` has order exactly `P_r` modulo `l` in the
     component `r`.
   - If `C` is a commutative quotient ring of `R̄_l` in which the image `ē` of `e` is
     nonzero, then `ē C` has characteristic a power of `l`. A characteristic-0 `ē C`
     would be a finitely generated commutative ring of characteristic 0 in which
     infinitely many primes are units, which is impossible. (The component `r = 1`,
     `End(M_1) = F_l`, is such a quotient.)
   - `R̄_l` has no Leavitt pair or any one-sided inverse that is not two-sided: finite
     rings are Dedekind finite, and so is a subring of a product of finite rings.

**Reading.** `R̄_l` shows that "residually finite" does not stop a finitely generated
ring from inverting every prime but one. This is the ring counterpart of
`sl-n-z-localized-embeds-in-fg-rf-elementary-group`. Whether the finite presentation
`R_l` already presents `R̄_l`, i.e. whether `R_l` is residually finite, is open; see
`rf-resolvent-elementary-group-is-fp`.
