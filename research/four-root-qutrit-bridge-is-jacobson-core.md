Let `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)`, let `F` be the transitive qutrit packet on
`D_27`, and put

```text
H_J=<D_n(F),
      e_12(s_0),e_21(t_0),
      e_12(s_00),e_21(t_00)> <= EL_n(R),       n>=2.     (FQJ1)
```

Let

```text
J=F_2<S,T | TS=1>.
```

Under the outer 27-leaf module chart, `S |-> s_0` and `T |-> t_0),
and

```text
H_J isomorphic to EL_28(J),                              (FQJ2)
```

acting trivially on the last `n-2` module coordinates.  In particular the
subgroup contains every Jacobson head root

```text
x_ij(Q),             Q=1-ST,             i!=j.          (FQJ3)
```

For `n>=5`, the subgroup `Gamma_J<=St_n(R)` generated by the lifted
qutrit packet and the four standard Steinberg roots maps onto `H_J` with
finite central kernel.  Thus no uncontrolled Leavitt `K_2) group remains
inside this finite package; the first nonpolynomial bridge is, up to a finite
central cover, exactly the Jacobson elementary group.

This is a reduction, not a nonhyperlinearity theorem.  The exact
finite-quotient and operator-MF behavior of the Jacobson head is recorded
elsewhere, and no known result in this atlas excludes the regular
hyperlinear representation of `EL_28(J)`.  The next load-bearing object is
therefore a genuinely additive overlap coupling the qutrit proper
three-phase marginal to a head-root occurrence `x_ij(Q)`; merely adjoining
the four roots has reached the hard coefficient core but has not supplied
that overlap.
