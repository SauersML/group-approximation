title: "SL_2(Z[t,t^-1]) is generated by elementary matrices"
---

`SL_2(Z[t,t^{-1}]) = E_2(Z[t,t^{-1}])`: every 2×2 integral Laurent-polynomial matrix
of determinant 1 is a product of elementary matrices `E_12(r)`, `E_21(r)`.

Equivalently (`sl2-laurent-integers-fg-iff-gamma0-t-in-fg-subgroup`, item 3): every
matrix in `SL_2(Z[t])` with lower-left entry divisible by `t` is a product of
elementary matrices over the Laurent ring. Equivalently again, every unimodular row
`(a,b)` over `Z[t]` can be reduced to `(1,0)` by elementary column operations with
Laurent-polynomial multipliers.

This is stronger than the affirmative answer to Zaremsky Problem 3.3; see route
`sl2-laurent-integers-fg-via-elementary-generation`. The zbMATH review of Knudson,
AGT 8 (2008), Zbl 1167.20026, records it as unknown.

## Attempts

- **Cohn's matrix reduces.** `[[1+2t, 4],[-t^2, 1-2t]]` is not in `E_2(Z[t])` (Cohn,
  1966), but over the Laurent ring `4 + (1+2t)(t^{-2} - 2t^{-1}) = t^{-2}` is a unit,
  so the row `(1+2t, 4)` reduces to `(1,0)`. Laurent units let one cancel the lowest
  terms as well as the highest.
- **Division algorithm.** For a row `(a,b)` with `w(b) ≤ w(a)` (width = top degree
  minus bottom degree), subtracting a monomial multiple of `b` lowers the width of
  `a` whenever the leading coefficient of `b` divides that of `a` (cancel from the
  top) or the trailing coefficient of `b` divides that of `a` (cancel from the
  bottom). A potential non-elementary row therefore has all four end coefficients of
  `a` and `b` non-units, with no divisibility among them, and this must survive every
  integer recombination. Rows whose coefficient matrix has small support reduce by
  integer column operations (e.g. `(2+3t, 3+5t)` goes to `(-t, 1+2t)` in two steps).
  No candidate obstruction has been found yet.
- **One-dimensional specializations see nothing.** Under `t ↦` a unit of a ring of
  `S`-integers, `t ↦` an element of a finite field, or reduction mod `p`, the image
  ring has `SL_2 = E_2`. So an obstruction, if any, is genuinely two-dimensional (as
  for Cohn's and Grunewald–Mennicke–Vaserstein's rings `Z[x]`, `k[x,y]`).
