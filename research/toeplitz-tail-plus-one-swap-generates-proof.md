The elements

```text
e_0=f,       e_1=xfy,       a=xf,       b=fy
```

are the matrix units `e_(00),e_(11),e_(10),e_(01)` in the unilateral-shift
model. Their multiplication rules also follow directly from `yx=1`,
`f^2=f`, `yf=0`, and `fx=0`. Hence `(TSG1)` is the transposition of the first
two coordinates and the identity on the complement, proving `(TSG2)`.

Since `e_1=sigma(f)` belongs to the tail image, `(TSG2)` first recovers

```text
e_0=s e_1 s.
```

It then recovers the off-diagonal head units by

```text
a=e_1 s e_0,                 b=e_0 s e_1.                     (1)
```

Finally, direct expansion of `(THT1)` at the two generators gives

```text
sigma(x)=x^2 y+f,            sigma(y)=x y^2+f.
```

Using `x=x(xy+f)=x^2y+a` and `y=(xy)y+b=xy^2+b`,

```text
x=sigma(x)+e_0+a,            y=sigma(y)+e_0+b.                (2)
```

Thus both defining generators of `T` lie in `<sigma(T),s>`, proving
`(TSG3)`. The elementary-group generation statement follows from the
Steinberg addition and commutator identities, which build root elements over
the subring generated by a fixed coefficient set using finitely many spare
indices.
