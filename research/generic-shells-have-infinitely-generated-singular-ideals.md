**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, not reviewed; no priority claimed).

## Setting

`P` is infinite and generated by the finite symmetric set `S`. `nu : P -> N` is a bijection,
and `succ(a) = nu^(-1)(nu(a) + 1)`. The shell uses the parity convention of
`shell-cantor-embeddings-are-finite-germ-extensions`. Even shell `2 nu(a)` is identified with
`(a, e)` and odd shell `2 nu(a) + 1` with `(a, o)`. Then:
- `r_g` sends `(a, e)` to `(ga, e)` and fixes the odd shells;
- `τ` sends `(a, e)` to `(a, o)`, and `(a, o)` to `(succ a, e)`.

**Generic.** Call `nu` *generic* if the partial action of `P * <t>` on `P` (left multiplication,
and `t = succ`) is free at infinity: every nontrivial `w ∈ P * <t>` fixes only finitely many
points.

## Statement

1. **Existence.** Every infinite finitely generated `P` has a generic enumeration.
2. **Germ group.** Let `P^(k)` (`k ∈ Z`) be copies of `P`. Put `F_e = *_j P^(2j)` and
   `F_o = *_j P^(2j+1)`, and let `Q_gen = (F_e × F_o) ⋊ <τ>`, where `τ` shifts the index by 1.
   Then `Q_gen = <P, τ | [P, τ^D P τ^(-D)] = 1 for D odd>`. For generic `nu`,
   `Q_nu ≅ Q_gen` via `r_g |-> g ∈ P^(0)`, and the boundary `𝓛` is the conjugacy class of `F_o`.
3. **Singular ideal.** `J_p = ω(F_e) ω(F_o) K[Q]`, for every field `K`.
4. **Not finitely generated.** For generic `nu`, `J_p` is not finitely generated. So `J` is
   not finitely generated as an ideal of `A_nu`, and if `A_nu` is finitely presented then
   `A_nu/J` is not. The witnesses are
   `f_(g,h,D) = (1 - u_g) s_0^D (1 - u_h) t_0^D` with `g, h ∈ S ∖ {1}` and `D` odd. Each is a
   four-germ singular element; `D = 1` is the `f_g` of
   `shell-germ-steinberg-algebras-are-never-simple`. None of them lies in the ideal generated
   by those with smaller `D`.

## Proof of 1

Fix radii `R_j -> ∞` and lengths `L_j -> ∞`. Build the chain `a_0, a_1, ...` (`nu(a_i) = i`) in
stages. At stage `j`, let `q` be the first element of a fixed enumeration of `P` not yet used.
- Append fresh points `f_1, ..., f_(L_j)`, then `q`.
- Each `f_i` must be at distance `> R_j` from `q` and from every point already used.

Such points exist because `P` is infinite and balls are finite. Every element is eventually
used, so `nu` is a bijection.

**Freeness.** Take a nontrivial cyclically reduced `w = g_0 t^(e_1) ⋯ t^(e_k)`, of length `ℓ`,
with `k >= 1`; the case `k = 0` is free left multiplication. Suppose `w` fixes a point used at
a late stage, so that `R_j > ℓ max|g_i|` and `L_j > ℓ`. The closed path of `w` is a cyclic
sequence of points joined by nontrivial `P`-moves and single `t^(±1)`-edges. There are no two
consecutive `P`-moves and no backtracking `t t^(-1)`. Let `y` be the point of the path used last.
- **`y` is fresh.** A `P`-move from `y` reaches an earlier point within distance `R_j`, which is
  impossible. `succ(y)` is used later. So both edges at `y` are the edge to `pred(y)`, which is
  backtracking.
- **`y` is a forced point `q`.** Both of its edges cannot be `P`-moves, so one is the edge to
  `f_(L_j) = pred(q)`. That point is fresh and far from `q` and from all earlier points, so its
  other edge goes to `f_(L_j - 1)`, and so on down the fresh block. The path cannot leave the
  block without backtracking, and the block is longer than `ℓ`.

Both cases are impossible, so only early points can be fixed. `∎`

## Proof of 2

**The presentation.** The relations `[P, τ^D P τ^(-D)]` with `D` odd, together with their
`τ`-conjugates, say that `P^(k)` and `P^(m)` commute exactly when `k - m` is odd. That is the
graph product over the complete bipartite graph on `Z` (even against odd indices), which is
`F_e × F_o`. Adding `τ` gives `Q_gen`.

**The model.** Let `X = Q_gen / F_o`.
- The even vertices `x τ^(2k) F_o` (`x ∈ F_e`) form the regular set of
  `F_e ⋊ <τ^2> ≅ P * <τ^2>`. So they carry the Cayley graph of `P * <t>`, with `P^(0)` acting by
  left multiplication and `τ^2` as `t`.
- The odd vertices are `τ` applied to the even ones. `P^(0)` fixes them, and `τ` carries odd to
  even as `τ^2 τ^(-1)`.

The shell is built from `(P, S, succ)` by the same recipe. So if `nu` is generic, then for
every `ℓ`, the `ℓ`-balls of the shell's Schreier graph (generators `τ`, `S`) at large `n` are
the `ℓ`-balls of `X` at vertices of the same parity.

**Consequences.** A word in `τ` and `S` is trivial in `Q_nu` iff it fixes all large `n`, iff
it fixes all of `X`, iff it is trivial in `Q_gen`. The last step holds because `X` is faithful:
its kernel lies in `F_o ∩ τ F_o τ^(-1) = F_o ∩ F_e = 1`. The limit stabilizers are the vertex
stabilizers of `X`, which are the conjugates of `F_o`, since `τ F_o τ^(-1) = F_e`. `∎`

## Proof of 3

By `shell-germ-singular-ideal-is-the-boundary-annihilator`, `J_p = Ann(K[X])`. Put
`Γ = F_e × F_o`, so `K[Q] = ⊕_k K[Γ] τ^k`.
- As a `Γ`-set, `X` is the disjoint union of the orbits `Γ τ^k F_o`, one for each `k ∈ Z`.
  These are distinct because `Q/Γ ≅ Z` is generated by the image of `τ`.
- So `Σ_k X_k τ^k` kills `K[X]` iff every `X_k` does.
- `K[Γ/F_o]` is the regular `F_e`-module with `F_o` acting trivially, and similarly for
  `K[Γ/F_e]`. So `X_k` must lie in `(K[F_e] ⊗ ω(F_o)) ∩ (ω(F_e) ⊗ K[F_o]) = ω(F_e) ⊗ ω(F_o)`. `∎`

## Proof of 4

**The witnesses are singular.** On shell `n >= D`, `s^D hat(h) s^(-D)` acts by
`n |-> rho_h(n - D) + D`, so it moves only odd shells, and `hat(g)` moves only even ones. The
four-term cancellation of `shell-germ-steinberg-algebras-are-never-simple` then applies. The
`(p,p)` entry of `f_(g,h,D)` is `x_D = (1 - r_g)(1 - τ^D r_h τ^(-D)) ∈ ω(P^(0)) ω(P^(D))`. This
part holds for every parity shell, generic or not.

**No finite generating set.** Suppose `J_p` were finitely generated.
- Each generator is a finite sum `Σ y_k τ^k`, with `y_k ∈ ω(F_e) ⊗ ω(F_o)` involving only the
  factors `P^(i)` with `|i| <= R`.
- Each such `y_k` lies in the ideal generated by the products `(1 - a)(1 - b)` with
  `a ∈ P^(i)`, `b ∈ P^(m)`, `|i|, |m| <= R`. So `J_p` lies in the ideal of `K[Q]` generated by
  these products.
- Meeting that ideal with `K[Γ]` gives the ideal of `K[Γ]` generated by all their
  `τ`-conjugates, which is graded bookkeeping. Every one of those conjugates involves two
  indices at distance at most `2R`.
- Take `D > 2R` odd, and let `π : Γ -> P^(0) × P^(D)` be the retraction that kills every other
  free factor. It sends every such generator to `0`, because at least one factor has an index
  outside `{0, D}`. But it sends `x_D` to `(1 - g)(1 - h) ≠ 0`.

So `x_D` is not in the ideal generated by the finitely many generators, although `x_D ∈ J_p`
by part 3. That is a contradiction. The same retraction shows that `x_D` is not in the ideal
generated by the `x_(D')` with `D' < D`. By part 3 of
`shell-germ-singular-ideal-is-generated-at-the-singular-point`, `J` is not finitely generated.
If `A_nu` is finitely presented, then a finite presentation of `A_nu/J` would make `J` finitely
generated. So `A_nu/J` is not finitely presented. `∎`

## Lesson for general BH

- **The same obstruction in two guises.** In a generic shell, the germ group and the singular
  ideal are both "parity wreaths", indexed by an odd shift distance `D`:
  - the group has the relations `[P, τ^D P τ^(-D)]`;
  - the algebra has the singular elements `(1 - P)(1 - τ^D P τ^(-D))`.
  The unbounded `D` is the Bieri--Strebel shift exponent of
  `shell-germ-algebra-fp-forces-bounded-germ-presentation`.
- **Consequence for the algebra route.** Passing from `E_nu` to `A_nu/J` does not remove the
  shift-exponent obstruction; it moves it into `J`.
- **What `nu` must do.** An enumeration for which `A_nu` is finitely presented and `A_nu/J`
  is too must be far from generic. Its Schreier boundary needs long-range coincidences that
  make every `x_D` a consequence of boundedly many.
- **Not claimed.** That such `nu` exist, or cannot exist, for infinite simple `P`. That
  `A_nu` is or is not finitely presented for generic `nu`.
