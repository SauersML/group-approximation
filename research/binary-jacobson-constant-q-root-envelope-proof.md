The Jacobson relations give

```text
Q^2=(1-ST)^2=1+STST=1+S(TS)T=1+ST=Q                  (1)
```

in characteristic two.  Also `Q!=0`: otherwise `ST=1`.  And `Q!=1`:
otherwise `ST=0`, while

```text
S=S(TS)=(ST)S=0,
```

contradicting `TS=1`.  Thus `1,Q` are linearly independent over `F_2`, and
the subring they generate is

```text
A={0,1,Q,1+Q} ~= F_2[x]/(x^2-x) ~= F_2 x F_2.          (2)
```

In particular `A` is finite.  The inclusion `A -> J` induces an injective
entrywise map `M_5(A)->M_5(J)`, so it identifies `EL_5(A)` with a finite
subgroup of `EL_5(J)`.

Root addition gives

```text
x_ij(1+Q)=x_ij(1)x_ij(Q).                              (3)
```

Therefore the elementary generators over every coefficient of `A` are
generated by the two displayed families, proving

```text
<x_ij(1),x_ij(Q)>=EL_5(A).                              (4)
```

The matrix `x_13(Q)=I+QE_13` is not the identity because `Q!=0`; hence the
mark survives in this finite subgroup and in its left regular unitary
representation.

The four words in the native two-qubit packet have coefficients `1` or `Q`,
so they lie in `EL_5(A)`.  Constant Weyl words are products of constant
roots and also lie there.  Conjugating a `Q`-labelled elementary matrix by a
constant permutation matrix merely moves its root position (signs disappear
in characteristic two), so all such translates remain in `EL_5(A)`.

Suppose relations internal to this occurrence envelope forced, whenever the
head survived, the nonzero common intertwiner required by the operator-norm
PI terminal.  Apply the asserted deterministic extraction to the exact left
regular representation of the finite group `EL_5(A)`.  Its head survives,
the native target tuple has the exact two-qubit Pauli residual, and all named
relations have zero defect.  The extracted nonzero common intertwiner would
contradict `opnorm-common-intertwiner-pi-gap-is-sparse-terminal`.  Hence no
such internal occurrence theorem exists.
