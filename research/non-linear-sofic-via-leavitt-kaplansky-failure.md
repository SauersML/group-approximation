## Why sufficient

The first prerequisite gives `alpha, beta in F_2[Q]`, `Q = L_(F_2)(1,2)^x`,
with `alpha beta = 1 != beta alpha`. So `F_2[Q]` is not directly finite, and
hence not stably finite. By the second prerequisite, `Q` is not `F`-linear
sofic for any field `F` containing `F_2`. With `F = F_2`, `Q` is a group that
is not linear sofic over some field.

The failure localizes. The subgroup generated by `supp alpha` and
`supp beta` already has a non-directly-finite group algebra, so it is a
finitely generated non-`F_2`-linear-sofic subgroup. Moreover
`subgroup-supported-one-sided-inverse-rigidity` applies to `alpha` and to
`beta` separately. Each of `<supp alpha>` and `<supp beta>` must fail to have
a directly finite group algebra, so each is non-`F_2`-linear-sofic.

## Calibration

The Kaplansky target over `Q` is therefore at least as hard as exhibiting a
non-linear-sofic group over `F_2`. That is Arzhantseva--Paunescu's open
question, which OpenAI's Hamming-metric theorem does not settle. The route
gives nothing over characteristic zero, where Kaplansky proved every group
algebra stably finite. So it cannot touch `non-hyperlinear-group` or any
complex approximation question.
