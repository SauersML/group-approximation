Reidemeister--Schreier rewriting, exactly as in Berlai,
arXiv:2502.05064v1, Theorem A, gives

    R = <a_i (i in Z) |
           a_(i+n)^(-1) a_i^l a_(i+n) = a_i^(l+1)>.

The indices split by their residue modulo `|n|`, and no relation mixes two
residue classes.  Hence `R` is the free product of `|n|` copies of

    K = <x_j (j in Z) |
           x_(j+1)^(-1) x_j^l x_(j+1) = x_j^(l+1)>.

The group `K` is the kernel of the exponent map to `Z` in
`B_(1,l)`.  Introducing `t=b^(-1)ab` exhibits `B_(1,l)` as an HNN extension
of `BS(l,l+1)` over two infinite cyclic associated subgroups.  Baumslag--Solitar
groups are residually solvable and hence sofic, and HNN extension over an
amenable subgroup preserves soficity.  Thus `B_(1,l)`, its subgroup `K`, the
finite free product `R`, and finally the extension `B_(n,l)` of `R` by `Z` are
all sofic.

The displayed relation also gives, with
`[x,y]=x^(-1)y^(-1)xy`,

    a_i = [a_i^l,a_(i+n)].

Every generator of `R` lies in `[R,R]`, so `R` is perfect.  Bass--Serre normal
form embeds the cyclic base generated by each `a_i`, so `R` is nontrivial.
The exponent sum of `a` in the defining relator is `-1`, so that relator is
not a proper power.  The one-relator torsion theorem makes `B_(n,l)`
torsion-free, and Brodskii--Howie then makes it locally indicable.  A nontrivial
finitely generated perfect subgroup of a locally indicable group would both
surject onto `Z` and have trivial abelianization, an impossibility.  Therefore
`R` is infinitely generated.

Perfectness and normality imply `R<=B_(n,l)_Q^(j)` for every finite `j`.
Conversely `B_(n,l)/R=Z` is residually rationally solvable, so the intersection
of the rational derived series lies in `R`.  Therefore

    R=B_(n,l)_Q^(omega).

The same perfectness induction for the ordinary derived series puts the
nontrivial element `a` in every derived term of `B_(n,l)`.  Hence the group is
not residually solvable, completing all assertions.
