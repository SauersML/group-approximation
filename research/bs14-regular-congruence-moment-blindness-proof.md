Every nonidentity `g in A` is represented by a rational matrix.  After
clearing its power-of-two denominators, the condition that `g` become
projectively trivial modulo an odd prime is a finite collection of integer
divisibilities; unless `g=1`, only finitely many primes satisfy them.  Apply
this to the union of supports of the finitely many expanded tests.

The normalized character of a finite left regular representation is one at
the identity and zero elsewhere, proving `(BML2)`.  Direct-sum amplification
does not change normalized characters, and `(BML3)` equalizes dimensions.
The kernels remain different because the two quotient maps have distinct
prime levels.  Exact group representations kill every presentation
residual, completing the residual-polar assertion.
