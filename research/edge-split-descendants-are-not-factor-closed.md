## Setting

Notation of `edge-splits-cut-every-rational-hyperplane`.
- `T_{ab} = I + E_{ab}`, and `𝕄` is the monoid generated by the `T_{ab}`,
  `a ≠ b`.
- The descendants of `Δ` are the cells `cone(K)` with `K ∈ 𝕄`. A cell
  determines its frame up to a column permutation `P`.
- The children of `Δ` are `C_{ab} = cone(T_{ab}) = {x_b ≤ x_a}`.
- For a nonnegative unimodular matrix `X`, `cone(X) ⊆ Δ`, and `cone(X) ⊆ C_{ab}`
  if and only if `row_a(X) ≥ row_b(X)` entrywise. In that case
  `cone(X) = cone(T_{ab} X')` with `X' = T_{ab}^{-1} X` (row `a` minus row `b`),
  which is again nonnegative.
- **Test.** `cone(X)` is a descendant of `Δ` if and only if `X` can be reduced to
  a permutation matrix by such row subtractions.
  - Call `X` *stuck* if it is not a permutation and has no pair of rows with
    `row_a ≥ row_b`.
  - A stuck matrix is not in `𝕄·Perm`. Every `K ∈ 𝕄` other than `I` has the
    form `T_{ab}K'` with `K'` nonnegative, so `row_a(K) ≥ row_b(K)`, and column
    permutations preserve row dominance.

## Statement

Take `m = 2`.
1. **A unimodular cell that is not a descendant.**
   `M = [[3,1,0],[1,1,1],[0,1,2]]` has determinant `1` and is stuck. So
   `cone(M) ⊂ Δ` is a unimodular cell, lies in no child of `Δ`, and is not a
   descendant of `Δ`. Hence `𝕄·Perm ⊊ SL_3(Z) ∩ Mat_3(Z_{≥0})·Perm`.
2. **Descendance is not inherited by children.** Let
   `K = [[3,2,2],[0,1,2],[1,1,1]]` (determinant `−1`), and `e = cone(K)`, with rays
   `(3,0,1), (2,1,1), (2,2,1)`.
   - `e` is a descendant of `Δ`: `K = T_{02} T_{02} T_{20} T_{12} T_{21} P`, where
     `P` swaps the last two coordinates. Each factor is checked by one row
     addition.
   - `e ⊆ C_{01}`, since `row_0(K) ≥ row_1(K)`.
   - `e` is not a descendant of `C_{01}`: `T_{01}^{-1}K = [[3,1,0],[0,1,2],[1,1,1]]`
     is `M` with two rows swapped, which is stuck.
   So the greedy row reduction is terminating but not confluent. From `K`, the
   step `(0,2)` reaches a permutation, and the step `(0,1)` gets stuck.
3. **Depth one is confluent as a dissection** (any `m`). Let `C_{ab} ≠ C_{cd}`
   be children of `Δ` whose intersection has nonempty interior.
   - If `a ≠ c`, then `C_{ab} ∩ C_{cd}` is a single cell and a common descendant
     of depth at most 2:
     - if `{a,b} ∩ {c,d} = ∅`, or if `b = d`, then `T_{ab}` and `T_{cd}` commute
       and the cell is `cone(T_{ab}T_{cd})`;
     - if `d = a`, the cell is `{x_b ≤ x_a ≤ x_c} = cone(T_{ca}T_{ab}) = cone(T_{ab}T_{ca}T_{cb})`;
       the case `c = b` is symmetric.
   - If `a = c` and `b ≠ d`, then `C_{ab} ∩ C_{ad} = {x_b, x_d ≤ x_a}` is not
     simplicial. It is the union of the two chambers `{x_b ≤ x_d ≤ x_a}` and
     `{x_d ≤ x_b ≤ x_a}`, each a common descendant of depth 2 by the previous
     case.
   The witness `e` of part 2 lives in exactly this non-simplicial case
   (`a = 0`, `{b,d} = {1,2}`): `e` straddles `x_1 = x_2`.
4. **Synchronization still holds for both witnesses, after one split.**
   - Split `cone(M)` along its first and third rays. Both children are descendants
     of `Δ`. The two reductions are `[[3,1,3],[1,1,2],[0,1,2]]` in six steps and
     `[[3,1,0],[2,1,1],[2,1,2]]` in five, each ending at a permutation matrix.
   - Split `e` along its first and third rays. In `C_{01}` coordinates the children
     are `[[3,1,0],[2,1,2],[2,1,1]]` and `[[3,1,3],[0,1,2],[1,1,2]]`. They reduce to
     permutation matrices in five and six steps. So `e` is the union of two cells that
     descend from both `e` and `C_{01}`.

## Consequences

- Expansions of `𝒯_m` by edge splits cannot be modelled by divisibility in a
  monoid in which a descendant lying in a child descends from that child.
  Frameworks that model expansions this way (Garside-type categories, X. Li
  arXiv:2110.04505, recalled and not re-read) do not apply as stated.
- Cofinality (`edge-splits-cut-every-rational-hyperplane`) and depth-one
  confluence (part 3) together still don't give synchronization. The remaining
  statement is `unimodular-cells-synchronize-under-edge-splits`.

## Lesson for general BH

For higher-rank refinement hosts, local diamonds do not make a Garside
structure. The right-LCM property can fail harmlessly at depth one, since the
non-simplicial intersection is a union of two common descendants. What really
fails is inheritance of descendance, and the witness here has depth five.
- A Stein–Farley proof for full groups of lattice-type boundary groupoids
  (`𝒯_m`, and the Lagrangian and Deligne hosts routed through it) has to treat
  expansions as dissections, not as prefixes.
- It has to prove synchronization as a separate, global statement.

The row-reduction test is an exact, finite way to decide descendance. So
synchronization questions for such hosts can be tested by hand, or by a small
bounded search, before any complex is built.
