Let `d=2^N` with `N>=2`, put `m=d/2`, and let `P,Q` be matrix unitaries,
of arbitrary common matrix size. Suppose their commutator `Z=[P,Q]` is
central in the algebra generated by `P,Q` and satisfies

```text
Z^m=-I.                                                  (SWD1)
```

This includes a direct sum of arbitrary primitive `d`-th central-character
fibers: every primitive character is an odd power of a fixed primitive root,
so its `m`-th power is `-1`. No choice of one scalar character is required.

For every unitary `V`, put

```text
epsilon_P=||V P V^*-P^2||_2,
epsilon_Q=||V Q V^*-Q^2||_2,                            (SWD2)
```

with normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm. Then

```text
epsilon_P+m epsilon_Q>=1,
m epsilon_P+epsilon_Q>=1.                               (SWD3)
```

Consequently

```text
epsilon_P+epsilon_Q>=2/(m+1),
epsilon_P^2+epsilon_Q^2>=2/(m+1)^2.                     (SWD4)
```

The constants depend only on the decoded conductor depth `N`, not on the
ambient dimension or on the multiplicity of the Weyl representation.

This is the minimal same-reservoir seam missing from independent cyclic
doubling matchings. On one root clock, eigenvalues can be matched to their
squares at cost tending to zero. Once a transverse root shares the same
primitive Heisenberg central-character band, doubling both coordinates changes
the half-period commutator from `-I` to `I`, which no common conjugating
unitary can do.

For the arithmetic hexagon route, `(SWD4)` says that after a primitive
central-character band and its two simple-root Weyl operators have been
decoded on one carrier, a single denominator transition already pays a
positive fixed-depth edge energy. The remaining extraction problem is to
prevent the two root matchings from occupying different rectangular
multiplicity reservoirs; no further six-edge holonomy is needed on such a
successfully decoded primitive band.
