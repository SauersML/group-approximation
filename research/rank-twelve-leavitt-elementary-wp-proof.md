Write `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)` and `H=EL_12(R)<=GL_12(R)`.

## Decidability

Orient the defining relations of `R` as in
`leavitt-normal-form-decides-unit-words-proof`:

```text
t_i s_j -> delta_(i,j),
s_1 t_1 -> 1 + s_0 t_0.                                      (RTP1)
```

These rules are confluent, and the Diamond lemma gives the `F_2`-basis

```text
s_alpha t_beta,                                              (RTP2)
```

with `alpha, beta` finite binary words not both ending in the letter `1`,
together with the usual empty-word conventions. So every element of `R` is a
finite `F_2`-linear combination of basis monomials; two elements are equal
exactly when those finite sets of monomials coincide; and the product of two
basis monomials is computed by applying `(RTP1)` until no rule applies, which
terminates. Hence the ring operations of `R` and equality in `R` are
computable.

`H` is finitely generated by `rank-twelve-leavitt-simple-non-mf`. Fix the
finite generating set

```text
X = { e_ij(g) : i != j in {1,...,12}, g in {1, s_0, s_1, t_0, t_1} },   (RTP3)
```

which generates `EL_12(R)` because the Steinberg identities
`e_ij(a) e_ij(b) = e_ij(a+b)` and `[e_ij(a), e_jk(b)] = e_ik(ab)` for
pairwise distinct `i,j,k` produce `e_ij(a)` for every `a` in the subring
generated by `{s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1}`, which is all of `R`; distinct indices are
available because the rank is at least three. In characteristic two
`e_ij(a)^(-1) = e_ij(-a) = e_ij(a)`, so every letter of `X^(±1)` is one of
the explicit matrices in `(RTP3)`.

Given a word `w` over `X^(±1)`, multiply out the corresponding `12 x 12`
matrices in order, keeping every entry in the normal form `(RTP2)`. This is a
finite computation by the previous paragraph. Since `H` is by definition a
subgroup of `GL_12(R)`, equality in `H` is equality of matrices, so

```text
w = 1 in H   iff   the computed product is I_12,             (RTP4)
```

and the right-hand side is decided by comparing `144` normal forms. No
faithfulness hypothesis is needed: the elements of `H` *are* matrices.

## Infiniteness

Every finite group is MF, since a faithful finite-dimensional unitary
representation is in particular a faithful corona representation. By
`rank-twelve-leavitt-simple-non-mf`, `H` is nontrivial and
`Rad_MF(H) = H`, which by the equivalent formulation in
`mf-radical-functoriality` says that every homomorphism from `H` to an MF
group is trivial. If `H` were finite, the identity map `H -> H` would be a
nontrivial homomorphism to an MF group. Hence `H` is infinite.

## Recursive presentation

A finitely generated group whose word problem is decidable is recursively
presented: the decidable set of identity words is recursively enumerable and
is a defining set of relators. `∎`

This route is a written proof. It is not a Lean certificate, and it imports
no statement beyond the two claims named in `requires`.
