Retain the packet `(MIH1)` and the literal center-chain arms

```text
A_1=x_47(a_1), B_1=x_72(b_1),
A_2=x_58(a_2), B_2=x_84(b_2),
A_3=x_69(a_3), B_3=x_95(b_3).                         (MIC1)
```

It is not normalized by these occurrences.  Already

```text
[A_1,Y_1]=x_43(a_1b_1)=x_43(q),
[X_1,B_1]=x_12(a_1b_1)=x_12(q),                       (MIC2)
[A_2,Y_2]=x_53(q),       [X_2,B_2]=x_14(q),
[A_3,Y_3]=x_63(q),       [X_3,B_3]=x_15(q).           (MIC3)
```

Every displayed output root is outside the subgroup generated by
`X_1,X_2,X_3,Y_1,Y_2,Y_3,z`; its elementary image has a root position absent
from that subgroup.  The failure persists for the mixed Hecke polar words.
For example, with

```text
u_1=B_2A_1,                    u_2=B_3A_2A_1,          (MIC4)
```

the conjugate `u_1Y_1u_1^(-1)` still contains the nonzero `x_43(q)`
coordinate, and `u_2Y_1u_2^(-1)` does as well; the later factors in `(MIC4)`
have no root incidence that can cancel that coordinate.

This gives the exact fence for the middle-index repair.  The same finite
packet supplies arbitrary-profile uniformity and a common Gram for the two
prefix Whiteheads, but it cannot also be the reducing packet for the
center-chain/Hecke words that expose the unequal selector coefficients.
Keeping `(MIC1)` inside this seven-generator extraspecial packet would force
the nonzero equations `a_mb_m=q` to vanish.  Enlarging the packet by the
ejected roots is a new occurrence construction and no longer gives the
three-pair extraspecial factor or the conditional atom balance used in
`(MIH6)`.

Moreover, even before the center-chain words are introduced, both
Whitehead label actions have negative fraction `1/4`.  Hence the positive
arbitrary-profile Gram `(MIH7)` cannot by itself prove the native
quarter/eighth selector cross-Gram.  No Property `(T)`, Kazhdan input,
canonical trace, or literature theorem is used.
