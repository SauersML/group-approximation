Put

```text
C=SL_3(Z),       A=SL_3(Z[1/2]),
N=L(C),          M=L(A).
```

Then `N subset M` is quasi-regular: the von Neumann algebra generated by its
one-sided quasi-normalizer is all of `M`.  More concretely, every group
unitary `u_a`, `a in A`, generates a bifinite `N-N` correspondence because
`C cap aCa^(-1)` has finite index in both groups.

The inclusion is not mixing in Popa's sense.  For the dyadic diagonal
`h=diag(2,1,1/2)`, there are weakly null unitaries `v_j in N` and fixed
`x,y in M minus N` such that

```text
||E_N(x v_j y)||_2=1       for every j.
```

Thus neither a relative-mixing theorem nor a theorem asserting that a
quasi-normalizer must return to `N` can obstruct `RE/N`: the arithmetic
commensurator is exactly the opposite regime.
