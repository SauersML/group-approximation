From J. Sánchez, *Free group algebras in division rings with valuation II*,
arXiv:1810.12449v1:
- §2: "A group G is called orderable if its elements can be given a strict
  total ordering < which is left and right invariant. That is, g1 < g2 implies
  that g1h < g2h and hg1 < hg2 for all g1, g2, h ∈ G. We call the pair (G, <)
  an ordered group."
- §7: "Let R be a ring and (G, <) be an ordered group. … We define a new ring,
  denoted R((G; <)) and called Malcev-Neumann series ring, in which R[G]
  embeds. As a set, R((G; <)) = { f = Σ_{x∈G} a_x x : a_x ∈ R, supp(f) is
  well-ordered } … It was shown, independently, in [31] and [34] that if R is a
  division ring, then R((G; <)) is a division ring."

From A. Clay and D. Rolfsen, *Ordered groups and topology*, arXiv:1511.05088:
- §1: "We will say that G is bi-orderable if it admits a total ordering which
  is simultaneously left and right invariant".
- "Theorem 3.4. Every free group is bi-orderable."

**Use.** Bi-orderable in Clay-Rolfsen's sense is orderable in Sánchez's sense
(an order invariant on both sides). Together they give the target.
