# Fell-absorb the regular representation, then apply Shulman's amalgam theorem

Internal proof from `notes/WEAK_MF_NONSOFIC_DOUBLE.md` sections 1-2, resting on
one external theorem that this repository has read in the primary source.

## Trust surface, stated up front

The only non-internal input is:

> **Shulman**, arXiv:2603.13564v2, **Theorem 10**: `A *_C A` is MF when `A` is
> separable MF and `C <= A`.

Its role and its limits are audited separately in
`notes/FALSE_SHULMAN_TRACE_AUDIT.md`, which records the important caveat that
*"Theorem 10 only proves that the algebra `B` is MF"* — it supplies no trace,
which is why this route reaches weak MF and nothing stronger. Shulman v2 is on
the repository's deep-verification list in `notes/CREDIT_AND_PRIORITY_AUDIT.md`
("Deep-web verification with PDFs in hand (CDE, Dadarlat, Shulman v2, TWW, …)").
Everything else below is internal.

## Step 1 — the MF algebra and its fixed subalgebra

`G` residually finite gives a norm-matrix-corona representation `rho` whose GNS
trace `tau` makes `pi_tau ∘ rho` the **left regular representation** of `G`.
Let `A` be the generated C-star algebra — separable, and MF because it sits
inside a norm matrix ultraproduct — and let `C <= A` be the subalgebra
generated by `rho(Gamma)`.

The separation lemma is the part that does real work:

> Only the reverse implication needs proof.  If \(g\notin\Gamma\), then for
> every finite sum \(c=\sum_{\gamma\in F}a_\gamma\rho(\gamma)\),
> \[ \tau(\rho(g)^*c)=0 \]
> by (3).  The same is true for every \(c\in C\) by norm continuity.  If
> \(\rho(g)\) belonged to \(C\), taking \(c=\rho(g)\) would give \(1=0\).

with the sharpened form that matters for the compression setting:

> The argument proves a little more: in the GNS Hilbert space of \(\tau\),
> \(\rho(g)\) is orthogonal to \(C\) whenever \(g\notin\Gamma\).  This remains
> true even if \(g\) lies in the relative profinite closure of \(\Gamma\), so
> finite-quotient collapse does not collapse the *fixed* C-star subalgebra
> \(C\).

That last sentence is the reason the route survives at a Kun--Thom pair, where
`Gamma` has large relative profinite closure.

## Step 2 — amalgamate

Shulman's Theorem 10 gives `B := A *_C A` MF. Let `i_1, i_2 : A -> B` be the
two canonical embeddings and define `iota : G *_Gamma G -> U(B)` by
`iota(g) = i_1(rho(g))` on the first vertex group and `i_2(rho(g))` on the
second.

## Step 3 — injectivity

> **Theorem.**  The map (7) is injective.  Hence every symmetric double of a
> countable residually finite group over an arbitrary subgroup is weak-MF.

The proof identifies the von Neumann completions,
`pi_tau(A)'' = L(G)` and `pi_tau(C)'' = L(Gamma)`, using that `pi_tau ∘ rho`
is the regular representation, and then reads injectivity off the reduced
tracial amalgam `L(G) *_{L(Gamma)} L(G)`, whose Bass--Serre normal form
separates the two vertex copies.

## Step 4 — the finite-lamp family

> Let \(K\neq1\) be finite and put \(H_K=G *_\Gamma(\Gamma\times K)\).
> The retraction \(H_K\to K\), trivial on \(G\) and equal to the second
> coordinate on \(\Gamma\times K\), has kernel
> \[ \ker(H_K\to K) \cong \underbrace{G *_\Gamma\cdots *_\Gamma G}_{|K|\text{ copies}}. \]
> This is the Bass--Serre covering with one central \(\Gamma\)-vertex and
> \(|K|\) outer \(G\)-vertices.  Its kernel is weak-MF by (11), and \(H_K\)
> is weak-MF by finite-extension closure.
