**ESTABLISHED** by `commensurating-actions-have-no-finite-helly-number-proof`.

**Statement.** Let `k ≥ 1`. There are:
- a group `W = ⟨s_0, …, s_k⟩`;
- an action of `W` on a set `X`;
- a commensurated subset `A ⊆ X`.

For every `j`, the subgroup generated by `{s_i : i ≠ j}` is finite. Yet
`ℓ(g) = |gA Δ A|` is unbounded on `W`. Here `W` is the affine Weyl group of type
`Ã_k`, acting on the half-spaces of its affine reflection arrangement.

**Consequence (class kill).** Suppose a proof of property FW for a group `G` shows
only that `G = ⟨H_0, …, H_m⟩` and that every join of at most `k` of the `H_i` has
bounded orbits, for some fixed `k`. Such a proof is invalid as it stands. With
`H_i = ⟨s_i⟩` and `m = k`, the hypothesis holds and the conclusion fails. In
particular, pairwise Helly arguments (`k = 2`) fail already for the `(3,3,3)`
triangle group `Ã_2`. The step where every such proof dies is the passage from
"each subfamily fixes a vertex" to "the fixed sets meet". Fixed-vertex sets in the
median graph of `A` are median-closed but not convex, so median Helly does not apply
to them. A finite Helly number comes back only with a dimension bound, as in `FW_∞`
statements for finite-dimensional cube complexes.

**Novelty.** None claimed. It is standard Coxeter-group geometry, recorded because
fixed-point arguments for `2V` (`brin-thompson-2v-has-property-fw`) keep proposing
Helly reductions.
