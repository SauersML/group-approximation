Notation is that of the target claim.  The only imported facts are the
prerequisite's equivalence

```text
gamma_Xi>0  iff  every homomorphism Xi->U(prod M_(d_n)/directSum M_(d_n))
                 kills w_Q                                        (E)
```

and its statement that the literal Jacobson action is a homomorphism
`pi:Xi->Lambda` with `pi(w_Q)!=1`.  Everything else is proved here.

## 1. The gate (G1)

Write `Q=prod_n M_(d_n)/directSum_n M_(d_n)`.

*If `gamma_Xi=0`.*  By (E) there is a homomorphism `phi:Xi->U(Q)` with
`phi(w_Q)!=1`.  Let `B` be the C*-subalgebra of `Q` generated by
`phi(Xi)`.  Its inclusion into `Q` is an injective *-homomorphism, so `B`
is MF, and `sigma=phi` is a unitary representation of `Xi` on `B` retaining
the mark.

*Conversely.*  Let `sigma:Xi->U(B)` have `sigma(w_Q)!=1`, where `B` is the
generated C*-algebra, and let `iota:B->Q` be an injective *-homomorphism.
Then `iota sigma:Xi->U(Q)` is a group homomorphism, and
`iota sigma(w_Q)!=1` because `iota` is injective.  By (E),
`gamma_Xi=0`.

*Consequences.*  If `gamma_Xi>0` and `Xi->Lambda'` retains `w_Q`, compose
with any representation of `Lambda'` retaining the image of the mark; the
generated algebra is not MF by the converse direction.  For
`Lambda'=Lambda` and the left regular representation, `lambda` is
faithful (it moves `delta_e` to `delta_g`), so `lambda pi(w_Q)!=1`, and
the generated algebra is `C*_r(Lambda)`.

## 2. The test object (G2)

`Lambda` is countable, being generated by the finite images of `F,J`, so
`C*_r(Lambda)` is separable.

*Admissibility and exactness.*  `lambda pi` is a group homomorphism on
`Xi`, so its restrictions to `F` and `J` are exact representations that
agree on `L'` (the amalgam identifies them), and both mixed words evaluate
to `lambda pi((h b)^3)=1` and `lambda pi(W)=1`.

*Norm of the mark.*  `u=lambda pi(w_Q)` is a unitary involution different
from `1`, so `p=(1-u)/2` is a nonzero projection and `u-1=-2p` has norm
`2`.

*Faithful trace.*  Put `tau(x)=<x delta_e,delta_e>`.  On the group algebra
`tau(lambda(g)lambda(k))=1_(gk=e)=tau(lambda(k)lambda(g))`, so `tau` is
tracial by linearity and norm continuity.  If `tau(x^*x)=0` then
`x delta_e=0`.  Right translations `R_g delta_k=delta_(k g^(-1))` commute
with every `lambda(k)`, hence with `x`, so
`x delta_g=R_(g^(-1)) x delta_e=0` for every `g`, and `x=0`.

*Stable finiteness.*  `tau tensor tr` is a faithful tracial state on
`M_n(C*_r(Lambda))`.  If `v^*v=1` there, then `1-v v^*>=0` has trace
`tau tensor tr(v^*v)-tau tensor tr(v v^*)=0`, so `v v^*=1`.

*Full support profiles.*  Let `H<=Xi` be finite with `pi|H` injective.
Decompose `Lambda` into right cosets `pi(H) g`.  Each
`l^2(pi(H) g)` is `lambda pi(H)`-invariant and is isomorphic to
`l^2(pi(H))=l^2(H)` with the left regular action.  Thus `lambda pi|H` is a
direct sum of copies of the regular representation of `H`, one for each
coset, and each copy contains every irreducible representation of `H` with
multiplicity equal to its dimension.  Restricting further to an abelian
normal subgroup `N<=H`, every character of `N` occurs, since the regular
representation of `N` does.  Only one copy is needed for (G3).

## 3. The class kill (G3)

Let `C` be any class of unital C*-algebras containing `C*_r(Lambda)`.
Suppose some `eps>0` has the property that every admissible pair in every
algebra of `C` with both mixed errors below `eps` satisfies
`rho_F(w_Q)=1`.  Apply it to `(lambda pi|F,lambda pi|J)` from Section 2:
the errors are `0<eps`, but `lambda pi(w_Q)!=1`.  Contradiction.

Suppose instead a modulus `omega` with `omega(t)->0` bounds
`||rho_F(w_Q)-1||` on `C`, in the sense that errors at most `t` force
`||rho_F(w_Q)-1||<=omega(t)`.  The same pair has errors `0<=t` for every
`t>0`, so `2<=inf_(t>0) omega(t)=0`.  Contradiction.

The class of unital C*-algebras with a faithful tracial state, and the class
of stably finite unital C*-algebras, both contain `C*_r(Lambda)` by
Section 2.

*Where each listed technique dies.*  A proof built from steps (1)--(5) of
the claim produces, for admissible pairs in any algebra of `C`, the final
implication "errors small `==>` head trivial or small".  Each step is
asserted from the relators, from algebraic identities, from averaging over a
finite group and polar decomposition of an invertible element (both
available in every unital C*-algebra), from the faithful trace, from stable
finiteness, or from support profiles, all of which hold for
`lambda pi` in `C*_r(Lambda)` by Section 2.  The conclusion step is then
refuted by that pair.  This is the invariant shared by the killed class:
validity in the literal reduced algebra, where the head has distance two.

*What is not killed.*  Arguments that use the dimension `d` of the target,
for example compactness of `U(d)` for fixed `d`, integrality of
multiplicities, or exclusion of a character orbit with a bounded fiber,
are not valid for `C*_r(Lambda)` and survive.  Any uniform-in-`d` version
must use a property `P` of matrix algebras with dimension-free constants
whose implication `P ==> head trivial` refutes `P(C*_r(Lambda))`.  If `P`
passes to C*-subalgebras of norm coronas, `P(C*_r(Lambda))` fails exactly
when the implication is nonvacuous, and then Section 1 shows the argument
proves `C*_r(Lambda)` non-MF.
