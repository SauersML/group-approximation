**ESTABLISHED by citation** (`cantor-z-crossed-product-embeds-in-continuous-factor-citation`). P. Ara and J. Claramunt,
*Sylvester matrix rank functions on crossed products*, arXiv:1902.06476, read from the TeX source on MSI on 2026-09-13.

Setting (abstract): A = C_K(X)⋊_T Z, where K is an arbitrary field, T a homeomorphism of the Cantor set X, and μ a full
ergodic T-invariant probability measure.
- **Approximating subalgebras.** Def `definition-approx.alg`: "Let $E$ be a nonempty clopen subset of $X$, and let $\calP$
  be a partition of $X \backslash E$. Define $\calB := \calA (E, \calP)$ as the unital $*$-subalgebra of $\calA$ generated by
  the elements $\chi_Z \cdot t$ for $Z \in \calP$." The approximating rings are $\gotR_n = \prod_{W \in \V_n} M_{|W|}(K)$,
  with connecting maps from a generalized Bratteli diagram (Prop `proposition-embed.Rn`).
- **Embedding.** Thm `theorem-completion.A.Ay`: "[...] We then have an embedding $\calA \hookrightarrow \gotR_{\rk}$ that induces
  a faithful Sylvester matrix rank function, denoted by $\rk_{\calA}$, on $\calA$." Here $\gotR_{\rk}$ is the rank completion
  of $\gotR_\infty$.
- **Uniqueness.** Prop `proposition-unique.rank`(ii): "$\rk_{\calA}$ [...] is a faithful Sylvester matrix rank function on $\calA$,
  and it is uniquely determined by the same property as in $(i)$, that is, for every clopen subset $U$ of $X$,
  $\rk_{\calA}(\chi_U) = \mu(U)$."
- **Continuous factor.** Thm `theorem-vN.cont.factor`: "There is an isomorphism of algebras $\gotR_{\rk} \cong \calM_K$, the von
  Neumann continuous factor over $K$."
- **All rank functions regular.** Thm `theorem-regular.Sylvester.space`: "Let $T$ be a homeomorphism on a totally disconnected
  compact metric space $X$ and set $\calA = C_K(X)\rtimes _T \Z$. Then we have $\Ps(\calA)= \Ps_{{\rm reg}}(\calA)$."

**Scope for the UN program.** This is a rank-metric (linear-sofic type) matricial approximation of the Pestov ring. It is
weaker than the exact local embedding into an algebraic ultraproduct that the Pestov 9.1 answer uses for LEF, which Ara and
Claramunt do not state.
