Fix a prime `ell in Sigma` and a certificate

```text
H=<h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3>,
alpha in Aut(H),
ord(alpha)=ell,
alpha(h_i)=[h_i,h_(i-1)].                                  (1)
```

By `periodic-higman-finite-images-are-perfect-or-trivial`, `H` is perfect.
Choose a maximal proper `alpha`-invariant normal subgroup `N` of the finite
group `H`, and put

```text
Q=H/N,       beta=induced automorphism,       q_i=h_i N.     (2)
```

The quotient is nontrivial, is generated by the four `q_i`, is perfect, and
satisfies the recurrence in `(1)`.  It has no nontrivial proper
`beta`-invariant normal subgroup by maximality of `N`.  The order of `beta`
divides the prime `ell`.  If `beta=1`, the trivial-twist part of
`periodic-higman-finite-images-are-perfect-or-trivial` makes `Q=1`, a
contradiction.  Hence

```text
ord(beta)=ell.                                               (3)
```

It remains to identify the structure of `Q`.  Form the finite semidirect
product

```text
Gamma=Q semidirect <beta>.
```

Normal subgroups of `Gamma` contained in `Q` are exactly the
`beta`-invariant normal subgroups of `Q`.  Hence `Q` is a minimal normal
subgroup of `Gamma`.  The standard structure theorem for finite minimal
normal subgroups writes `Q` as a direct product of mutually isomorphic finite
simple groups.  Since `Q` is nontrivial and perfect, those simple groups are
nonabelian.

Therefore

```text
Q=S^m
```

for one nonabelian finite simple group `S`.  Minimal normality also makes the
conjugation action of `Gamma` transitive on the simple factors.  Inner
automorphisms from `Q` fix each direct factor, so this transitivity is already
the transitivity of `beta`.  Its permutation has order dividing `ell`;
transitivity and `(3)` give

```text
m divides ell,       hence       m=1 or m=ell.
```

All relations and generation survive the quotient, proving the claim. QED
