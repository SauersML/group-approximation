Fix a Fanizza BCS `B_m`.  Let `Gamma_m^var` be generated by its involution
variables, with only the within-context commutators and any already native
multiplicative word relations retained.  Form

```text
Lambda_m=Gamma_m^var,L times Gamma_m^var,R.             (TCH1)
```

The two factors commute.  For a context `c`, let `P_(c,a)^L` be the usual
joint spectral projection of its left variables at assignment `a`, and let
`F_c^L` be the sum over forbidden assignments.  For every question pair
`(c,x)`, define the losing projection

```text
L_(c,x)
 = F_c^L
   + sum_(a allowed) P_(c,a)^L (1-a_x X_x^R)/2.        (TCH2)
```

The summands in `(TCH2)` are orthogonal and the right factor commutes with
the left context, so `L_(c,x)` is a projection.  With the rational BCS game
question weights `mu_(c,x)`, put

```text
H_m=sum_(c,x) mu_(c,x) L_(c,x),
D_R=(1-X_D^R)/2.                                      (TCH3)
```

Then `0<=H_m<=1`, and both elements lie in the rational group algebra
`Q[Lambda_m]`.

If `m` is a nonmember in the Fanizza family, then in every unitary
representation `pi` of `Lambda_m`,

```text
pi(D_R) <= C_m pi(H_m).                               (TCH4)
```

## Proof

Take any representation `pi:Lambda_m->U(H)` and any unit vector `xi`.  Use
the joint PVMs of the left context variables for Alice and the binary PVM of
`X_x^R` for Bob.  The two group factors commute, so this is a commuting
operator strategy.  Its losing probability is exactly

```text
<xi,pi(H_m)xi>.
```

Its associated distinguished signal state is

```text
<xi,pi(D_R)xi>.
```

Fanizza nonmember soundness gives the latter at most `C_m` times the former.
This holds for every unit vector in every representation, which is precisely
the positive-operator inequality `(TCH4)`.

No tracial postselection or synchronous rounding is used.  The right copy is
what turns an arbitrary vector state into a legal Bob algebra commuting with
all of Alice's context measurements.

## HALT side

A perfect commuting strategy with positive distinguished signal is already
a representation of the two commuting copies with a vector `xi` satisfying

```text
pi(H_m)xi=0,       <xi,pi(D_R)xi>>0.                  (TCH5)
```

Thus the same explicit Hamiltonian simultaneously has a nonzero perfect
ground signal on the HALT side and dominates that signal as an operator on
the nonmember side.

This theorem does not make `H_m=0` a group relation.  It instead supplies an
explicit positive group-algebra interface for a finite-dimensional-only
compressor.
