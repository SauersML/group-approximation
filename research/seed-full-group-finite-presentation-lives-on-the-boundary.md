## Setting

`(Γ, Y, y_*)` is a seed: (S1) `y_*` is isolated, via `[π] ∩ Y = {y_*}`; (S2) its stabilizer
is trivial; (S3) its orbit `O` is dense. Put `∂ = Y ∖ O`.
- `F = [[Γ ⋉ Y]]` is the plain full group.
- `F^V = [[G_V × (Γ ⋉ Y)]]` is its V-stabilization, as in the master route.

## Statement

1. **The boundary is an SFT.** `∂` is the SFT obtained from `Y` by also forbidding `π`.
2. **Seeds are topologically free.** `Γ` acts topologically freely on `Y`. Hence, by
   `topologically-free-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`, if `F^V` is finitely
   presented then `Y` is a quantum rigid SFT over every field, at all large scales.
3. **Finite presentation passes to the boundary.** Let `𝒢_∂` be the germ groupoid of `Γ ⋉ Y`
   restricted to `∂`.
   - The kernel of the germ-restriction map `F → [[𝒢_∂]]` is `FSym(O)`. It is normally generated
     by one transposition.
   - The kernel of `F^V → [[G_V × 𝒢_∂]]` is the group of elements supported on finitely many
     columns `C × {o}`. It is normally generated by the finitely generated copy of `V` on one
     column.
   - So if `F` (respectively `F^V`) is finitely presented, so is its image `F_∂`
     (respectively `F^V_∂`) in the boundary full group.
   - That image contains the alternating full group `A(𝒢_∂)` (respectively `A(G_V × 𝒢_∂)`).

## Proof

**(1).** A point `z ∈ ∂` containing `π` at `g` would give `g^(-1)z ∈ [π] ∩ Y = {y_*}`, so
`z ∈ O`, which is impossible. Conversely, every point of `O` contains `π`.

**(2).** Stabilizers of points of `O` are conjugate to `Stab(y_*) = 1`, so for `g ≠ 1` we have
`Fix(g) ⊆ ∂`. Now `O` is open, as a union of isolated points, and dense. So `∂` is closed with
empty interior, and `Fix(g)` has empty interior.

**(3).** Let `f ∈ F` have trivial germ at every point of `∂`.
- *The finitary part.* Then `f` is the identity on an open `U ⊇ ∂`, which we may take clopen,
  since `f` is given on a finite clopen partition. `Y ∖ U` is compact and consists of isolated
  points, so it is finite, and `f` permutes it. So `f ∈ FSym(O)`. The reverse inclusion is clear.
- *One transposition generates.* `F` is 2-transitive on `O`, so all transpositions are conjugate,
  and they generate `FSym(O)`.
- *The V-stabilized case.* The same argument works with the columns `C × {o}`: a compact subset of
  `C × O` meets finitely many columns. The full group of `G_V` on finitely many columns is
  generated by conjugates of `V` on one column and by column transpositions.
- *Quotient.* The quotient of a finitely presented group by a finitely normally generated normal
  subgroup is finitely presented.
- *Alternating part.* Take a 3-cycle of `A(𝒢_∂)`, given on a clopen `W ⊆ ∂` by bisections from
  `Γ`. Extend `W` to a clopen of `Y`. Disjointness of the three translates holds on `∂`, so it
  holds on a clopen neighbourhood after removing finitely many isolated points. The extended
  3-cycle lies in `F` and restricts to the given one. ∎

## What this means for SEED vs CAP

**Two locations.** The two demands on a hard fp seed sit in different places.
- **Hardness at the core.** By `seed-evaluation-is-bounded-by-the-forcing-radius`, the admissible
  patterns around the core `π` must stay undetermined out to super-polynomial distance.
  Anticipation layers (`anticipation-layers-convert-totality-into-isolation`) are the known
  mechanism.
- **Finiteness everywhere.** By (2), the V-stabilized route needs **all of `Y`** to be quantum
  rigid. By (3), the plain route needs a finitely presented boundary full group over the SFT `∂`.

**Where they collide.** The collision happens on `∂`. The limits at infinity of the
non-terminating walks of an anticipation layer lie in `∂`. Two far basins there give independent
label flips, which kill rigidity (the clustering lemma of bh-invent-18).

**The consequence.** A hard fp seed with an anticipation layer needs all non-terminating basins
to converge, which is exactly lemma CB. Conversely, a proof that CB fails for rigid SFTs would
rule out non-local forcing by anticipation layers in rigid seeds. That caps this mechanism, not every
conceivable non-local forcing.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Where each requirement lives.** Finite presentation of a seed full group is a property of the
  boundary: the core contributes only finitary permutations, or finitely many V-columns, which are
  finitely normally generated. Seeds are automatically topologically free, so the master-route
  necessity theorem applies unchanged. Hardness lives at the core, as forcing radius; rigidity
  lives on the boundary SFT, where the unforced tails of the core's forcing mechanism end up.
- **The decisive question.** On the anticipation route, SEED vs CAP comes down to whether a
  rigid SFT can carry convergent non-terminating basins, that is, lemma CB. That is where hardness
  and rigidity meet. The exact orbit closure (S3), the finitely generated stabilizer and finite
  presentation of the boundary full group itself remain as separate gates.
