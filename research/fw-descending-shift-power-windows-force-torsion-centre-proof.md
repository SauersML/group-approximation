Notation as in the target. "Near equal" means equal off a finite set.

**Step 1: exact equivariance.** Let `P` act on `N × N` by `g·(x,y) = (ρ_g x, ρ_(ψ(g)) y)`.
The graph of `s^m` is commensurated, since `ρ_(ψ(g)) s^m ρ_g^-1` is near equal to `s^m`.
Steps 2 and 3 of `fw-inputs-admit-no-twisted-shell-normalization-proof`, run for `s^m`,
give:
- a cofinite `ρ(P)`-invariant set `N_1`;
- an injection `s' : N_1 -> N`, near equal to `s^m`, with `s'(ρ_g x) = ρ_(ψ(g)) s'(x)`;
- an orbit graph `x -> s'(x)` consisting of `m` forward rays `R_1, …, R_m`, which contain
  all large `n`, and finitely many finite components.

**Step 2: labels.** For `x ∈ N_1`, `s'(P·x) = ψ(P)·s'(x) ⊆ P·s'(x)`. So
`f(P·x) = P·s'(x)` is well defined, and along each ray the labels
`λ_j = P·s'^j(k_i)` satisfy `λ_(j+1) = f(λ_j)`.
- If a label repeats on a ray, the labels on that ray are eventually periodic. Call the
  ray periodic.
- A label that occurs infinitely often occurs infinitely often on some ray, which is then
  periodic. So the set `𝓛` of labels occurring infinitely often is finite: it is the union
  of the label cycles of the periodic rays.
- Every other orbit meeting the rays meets each non-periodic ray at most once and the
  periodic rays only in their pre-periodic parts. So it has at most `c` points, for a
  bound `c` independent of the orbit.
- Hence, off finitely many points, `N` is the union of the finitely many infinite orbits
  in `𝓛` and of orbits of size at most `c`.

**Step 3: self-similarity of an orbit `O ∈ 𝓛`.** Let `p` be the least common multiple of
the periods and put `σ = s'^p`.
- Then `σ(O) = ψ^p(P)·σ(x) ⊆ f^p(O) = O`, and `σ` is near equal to `s^(mp)` on `O`.
- Let `Y = O − σ(O)`. It is finite, because `σ` shifts each strand of `O` along its ray.
- `Y` is nonempty. Let `x_(j*)` be the first point of some periodic ray with label `O`.
  - If `j* >= p`, its unique `σ`-preimage `x_(j*-p)` has a different label, by minimality,
    so it is not in `O`.
  - If `j* < p`, it has no `σ`-preimage.
  - Either way `x_(j*) ∈ Y`.
- `σ` is an isomorphism of `P`-sets `(O, ρ) -> (O − Y, ρ∘ψ^p)`.
- Put `Λ_k = O − σ^k(O) = ⊔_(i<k) σ^i(Y)`. `σ^k` moves points backward only finitely often,
  so `⋃_k Λ_k = O`.

**Step 4: fixed sets.** `σ^k(O)` is a `ψ^(kp)(P)`-orbit, infinite, and `ψ^(kp)(P)`
preserves the finite set `Λ_k`.
- Let `H_k` be the pointwise stabilizer of `Λ_k` in `ψ^(kp)(P)`. It has finite index in
  `ψ^(kp)(P)`.
- So its orbits on the infinite orbit `σ^k(O)` are finitely many and infinite, and
  `Fix(H_k) ∩ O = Λ_k`.

**Step 5: the centre.** Let `z ∈ Z(P)`.
- `ρ_z` preserves `O`, and it commutes with `ρ(H_k)` because `H_k <= P`. So it preserves
  `Fix(H_k) ∩ O = Λ_k` for every `k`.
- Hence it preserves each level `σ^k(Y) = Λ_(k+1) − Λ_k`, which has `|Y|` points. By Step 3
  these levels exhaust `O`.
- So `ρ_(z^(|Y|!))` is trivial on `O`.
- On an orbit of size at most `c`, `ρ_(z^(c!))` is trivial.
- Let `e` be the least common multiple of `c!` and the numbers `|Y_O|!` for `O ∈ 𝓛`. Then
  `ρ_(z^e)` has finite support, so `z^e = 1`.

**Consequences.** The commuting reduction is the argument of
`fw-inputs-fail-commuting-shell-windows-proof`, Steps 1 to 3, with `c_(-j)` in place of
`c_j`.
- The centre of `St_N(R_L)` contains `Q^x`. `St_N(R_L)` is perfect, being generated by the
  commutators `x_ij(a) = [x_ik(a), x_kj(1)]`.
- (T) for `N >= 4` comes from `steinberg-finite-presentation-and-kazhdan-theorem`, and
  (T) implies FW.
