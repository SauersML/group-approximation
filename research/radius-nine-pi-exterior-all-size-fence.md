Let `p` be an ordinary noncommutative polynomial of degree at most nine.  If
`p` vanishes under every substitution in every complex matrix algebra, then
`p=0` in the free algebra.  More sharply, `M_5(C)` has no nonzero polynomial
identity of degree at most nine: the least degree of a polynomial identity of
`M_n(C)` is `2n`.

Consequently an Amitsur--Levitzki or exterior-power identity visible inside a
radius-nine word window can constrain only a bounded initial set of matrix
sizes.  The standard identities available in that window stop at `s_8`, hence
at `M_4(C)`.  For `M_5(C)` and above there is no nonzero ordinary
degree-at-most-nine polynomial identity to invoke.

This bounded-size information cannot be promoted to the required all-size
outer bound by splitting into dimensions.  For every matrix tuple `U` and
every `k>=1`,

```text
m_9(U tensor I_k)=m_9(U),
D(U tensor I_k)=D(U).                                  (R9-PI-1)
```

Thus the same point of the radius-nine moment body has representatives of
arbitrarily large ambient dimension.  No relation defect or normalized
radius-nine moment can supply an upper bound on the dimension at which one
may stop the dimension-indexed identities.  Likewise an identity
`wedge^(n+1)=0` changes its exterior degree with `n`; it is not one fixed
rational radius-nine functional and amplification moves the same moment point
past every fixed exterior cutoff.

Therefore a proof of the all-dimension outer bound in
`matricial-radius-nine-dual-extraction` cannot consist solely of bounded-degree
polynomial identities, fixed exterior-power vanishings, or a finite case
split over matrix sizes.  It must instead provide a universal matrix trace
**inequality** that remains valid in every dimension but fails in the larger
tracial body, or use the atlas relations to obtain some different
amplification-invariant finite-dimensional restriction.

This is only a method fence.  It does not show that a matrix-only rational
separator is absent: finite-degree trace inequalities can contain information
not expressible as ordinary polynomial identities, and excluding all such
inequalities would amount to deciding the remaining matricial moment gap.
