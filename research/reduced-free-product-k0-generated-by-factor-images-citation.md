Source: Pierre Fima and Emmanuel Germain, *The KK-theory of amalgamated free products*,
arXiv:1510.02418v3 (Adv. Math. 2020), read from the arXiv PDF on 2026-09-12. Take the amalgam
`B = C`, and use the states `E_l = φ_l` as the conditional expectations onto `B`. Write `A_f`
for the full free product, `S = C_0(]-1,1[)`, and `A_v` for the vertex-reduced free product.

**Verbatim inputs.**
- *Section 4, p. 19:* "Let D be the subalgebra of S⊗A_f consisting of functions f such that
  f(]−1,0]) ⊂ A_1, f([0,1[) ⊂ A_2 and f(0) ∈ B. This algebra is of course isomorphic to the cone
  of i_1 ⊕ i_2 from B to A_1 ⊕ A_2. We call j the inclusion of D in the suspension of A_f."
- *Theorem 4.1:* "Suppose that there exist unital conditional expectations from A_l to B for
  l = 1, 2, then the map j, seen as an element [j] of KK^0(D, S⊗A_f), is invertible."
- *Proof of Theorem 4.1, p. 25:* `x ∈ KK^1(A_f, D)` is built in §4.1, and `y = (1_(A_f) ⊗ a) ⊗ x`,
  with `a` the inverse of the invertible `b ∈ KK^1(C, S)`, is shown to be the inverse of `[j]`.
  The same page records the exact sequence
  "0 → A_1⊗S ⊕ A_2⊗S → D → B → 0", with maps `κ̄_1 ⊕ κ̄_2` and `ev_0`.
- *Lemma 4.10:* "For all l = 1, 2, one has [j_l] ⊗_(A_f) x = ([Id_(A_l)] ⊗ b) ⊗_(A_l⊗S) [κ̄_l]
  ∈ KK^1(A_l, D)", where `j_l` is the inclusion of `A_l` in `A_f`.
- *Theorem 3.1:* "[λ] ∈ KK(A_f, A) is invertible". Here `λ : A_f → A_v` is the canonical
  surjection, the identity on `A_1 ∪ A_2`.
- *Proposition 2.15:* "... if E_k is GNS-faithful, λ_(k,v) is an isomorphism". This identifies the
  `k`-vertex-reduced product with the edge-reduced one. `A_v` carries the norm
  `max(||π_1(x)||, ||π_2(x)||)` (Definition 2.7), and the edge-reduced product is the classical
  reduced free product (Proposition 2.11). So for GNS-faithful states `A_v = A`.

**Derivation.**
1. `x = (1 ⊗ b) ⊗ y` is a product of invertibles, so `x_* : K_0(A_f) → K_1(D)` is an isomorphism.
2. The six-term sequence of `0 → S A_1 ⊕ S A_2 → D → C → 0` is exact at `K_1(D)`, and
   `K_1(C) = 0`. So `(κ̄_1)_* + (κ̄_2)_* : K_1(S A_1) ⊕ K_1(S A_2) → K_1(D)` is onto.
3. Lemma 4.10 gives `x_* ∘ (j_l)_* = (κ̄_l)_* ∘ β_l`, where `β_l : K_0(A_l) → K_1(S A_l)` is the
   isomorphism given by `b`. With steps 1 and 2, `(j_1)_* + (j_2)_*` maps onto `K_0(A_f)`.
4. By Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 2.15, `λ_* : K_0(A_f) → K_0(A)` is an isomorphism with
   `λ ∘ j_l` the inclusion of `A_l` in `A`. So `K_0(A)` is generated by the images of `K_0(A_l)`.

**Group case.** The canonical trace is GNS-faithful, and
`(C*_r G, τ) * (C*_r H, τ) ≅ C*_r(G * H)` trace-preservingly. This is the identification used in
`fkop-avitzour-free-product-classification`. Every element of `K_0(C*_r(G * H))` is
`(j_1)_* y_1 + (j_2)_* y_2`, whose trace is `τ_*(y_1) + τ_*(y_2)`. Conversely every such sum
occurs. This gives the trace formula.

**Model test.** Pimsner--Voiculescu's `K_0(C*_r F_2) = Z` and the classical `K_0(C*(C_n)) = Z^n`,
with minimal projections of trace `1/n`, give the two values in the claim.
