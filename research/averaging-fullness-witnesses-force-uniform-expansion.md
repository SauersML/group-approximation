**ESTABLISHED.**

**Setting.**
- `G` is any group, and `h ∈ G` has order 3.
- `e = 1 + h + h²` in `F_2[G]` is idempotent, because `3 = 1` in `F_2`.
- Suppose `c e b = 1` for some `b, c ∈ F_2[G]`. Let `S_b` be the support of `b`, and `H_b = ⟨S_b, h⟩`.
- For a `G`-set `Ω` and a finite `F ⊆ Ω`, write:
  - `Fix h` for the fixed set of `h`;
  - `S F = ⋃_(s∈S) sF`;
  - `∂_S F = SF \ F`.

**Theorem (exact inequality).** For every `G`-set `Ω` and every finite `F ⊆ Ω`:

```text
|F \ Fix h|  <=  |hF \ F|  +  (3/2) |S_b F \ F| .
```

The same holds with `S_b` replaced by `S_c^(-1)`, because `b* e c* = 1` under `g ↦ g^(-1)`. The
matrix `c` never enters the inequality for `b`.

**Corollaries.**
1. **Invisibility of `h`.** Let `Ω` be any `G`-set carrying a mean invariant under `H_b` alone. Then
   `m(Fix h) = 1`. In particular:
   - `h` acts trivially on every finite `H_b`-set, so `h` lies in every finite-index subgroup of `H_b`;
   - `H_b` is not amenable;
   - no quotient of `H_b` in which `h` survives is amenable or residually finite.

   All of this also holds for `H_c = ⟨S_c, h⟩`.
2. **Uniform expansion on `h`-free sets.** Suppose `h` has no fixed point on `Ω`. Then every finite
   `F ⊆ Ω` satisfies `|(S_b ∪ {h})F \ F| >= (2/5)|F|`. Examples of such `Ω`:
   - the regular `G`-set;
   - the Cantor set `C` for `G = V`, where the element `h` of the V claim cycles `[00] → [01] → [1]`;
   - `C²` for `G = 2V`, where `g ⊗ 1` cycles `[0] → [10] → [11]` in the first coordinate.

   So every orbit of `H_b` there is a Schreier graph with vertex Cheeger constant at least `2/5`
   with respect to `S_b ∪ {h}`.
3. **Fullness forces Property FM.** Let `G` be finitely generated, infinite and simple. If `e` is full,
   then every `G`-set with a `G`-invariant mean has that mean concentrated on `Fix(G)`. So `G` has
   Cornulier's Property FM. This applies to:
   - `G = V` with the order-three element `h` of `thompson-v-order-three-averaging-idempotent-is-full`;
   - `G = 2V` with `g ⊗ 1` (`brin-thompson-2v-order-three-averaging-idempotent-is-full`).

   Cornulier asks, as Question 1.19(1) of arXiv:1308.1318: "Consider R. Thompson's groups T and V of the
   circle and the Cantor set. Do T and V have Property FM?"
4. **Conditional exclusion.** Suppose `thompson-v-has-a-fixed-point-free-amenable-action` holds. Then no
   witness for either fullness claim has `S_b` or `S_c` inside the product `V ⊗ 1 · 1 ⊗ V ≅ V × V` of
   the two coordinate copies in `2V`.

**What it changes.**
- The only concrete Gottschalk counterexample route (`brin-thompson-2v-nonsurjunctive-from-averaging-fullness`)
  now needs, at a minimum, that `2V` has Property FM. That is open for `V` itself, as Cornulier's question.
- A witness search can be pruned before computing any product. Only `S_b` needs testing, and only
  against Følner-type sets for `H_b`.
