The prefix identities make `sigma_m` an injective unital ring map.
The ring `M_2(R)` is generated by its units: off-diagonal matrix
units with arbitrary coefficients are differences of a transvection
and identity, and their products give the diagonal matrix entries.
Transporting through `R~=M_2(R)` shows that `R` is generated by
its units. Thus a matrix commuting with `diag(H,...,H)` has
entries in `Z(R)=F_2`, and its invertible possibilities are
exactly `F_m=GL_m(F_2)`.

The infinite simple group `sigma_m(H)` has no nontrivial finite
conjugation orbit. Fourier coefficients therefore give
`N_m' intersect N=L(F_m)`. Conditional expectation from an
extension `Q` to `N` sends `N_m' intersect Q` into this algebra.
Since that commutant commutes with `B_m`, it sends `D_Q` into
`L(sigma_m(K)F_m)`, and the reverse inclusion already lies in
`D_Q intersect N`.

The matrix `diag(k,1,...,1)` centralizes `sigma_m(L)`. It is
outside `sigma_m(K)F_m`: an equality with `diag(h,...,h)T`,
`T in GL_m(F_2)`, first forces all off-diagonal entries of `T`
to vanish, hence `T=I`; the second diagonal entry then forces
`h=1`, contradicting `k!=1`. Fourier orthogonality and conditional
expectation prove the distance-one conclusion in every extension.

The artifact specifies the quantifier over embeddings, the same-
dimension finite-word substitution interpretation, and the boundary
between endomorphism functoriality and a choice of special embedding.
