## Setting

For each prime `p` let `T_p` be the `(p+1)`-regular Bruhat–Tits tree of
`SL_2(Q_p)`, with base vertex `o_p = [Z_p^2]`. Let `X = ∏'_p (T_p, o_p)` be the
restricted product: vertices are families `x = (x_p)` with `x_p = o_p` for almost
all `p`, and two vertices are adjacent when they differ in one coordinate by an
edge of that tree. `X` is a median graph (the 1-skeleton of a CAT(0) cube complex)
whose distance is `d(x, y) = Σ_p d_p(x_p, y_p)`. `x ↦ (x_p)` identifies its
vertices with the lattices `L ⊂ Q^2` up to homothety by `Q^x`, and `SL_2(Q)` acts
diagonally. Write `u(t) = [[1, t], [0, 1]]`, `U = u(Q)`, `U_m = u((1/m!)Z)`, and
`B(Q)` for the upper triangular group.

## Statement

1. **Orbits.** The action is not cocompact: `SL_2(Q)` has infinitely many vertex
   orbits, and `PGL_2(Q)` has one edge orbit per prime
   (`cocompact-fg-stabilizer-actions-force-finite-generation`, item 3). The
   stabilizer of `o` is `SL_2(Z)`.
2. **One end in every factor.**
   - For every prime `p`, `U` fixes no vertex of `T_p` and exactly one end, the
     end `ξ_p` of the line `Q_p e_1`.
   - So `U` fixes no vertex of `X`, and exactly one point of `∏_p (T_p ∪ ∂T_p)`,
     namely `ξ = (ξ_p)_p`. Every coordinate of `ξ` is at infinity.
   - `Stab_(SL_2(Q))(ξ_p) = B(Q)` for every `p`.
3. **Busemann data.** The Busemann characters `β_p : B(Q) -> Z` at `ξ_p` give
   `β = (β_p)_p : B(Q) -> ⊕_p Z`, with
   `β([[a, b], [0, a^-1]]) = (2 v_p(a))_p`. The image is `2 ⊕_p Z`, of infinite
   rank, and `ker β = ±U`. So the divisible root group is exactly the joint kernel
   of infinitely many independent Busemann characters.
4. **Depth along the chain.**
   - `Fix_X(U_m)` is nonempty. Its vertex nearest to `o` is
     `x_m = [Z e_1 ⊕ m! Z e_2]`, and `d(o, x_m) = Ω(m!)`, where
     `Ω(m!) = Σ_(p≤m) v_p(m!) = Σ_(k=2)^m Ω(k)` is the number of prime factors of
     `m!` counted with multiplicity.
   - `d(o, u(1/m!) o) = 2 Ω(m!)`, and `u(1/m!)` moves `o` in exactly the `π(m)`
     prime coordinates `p <= m`.
   - `U ∩ Stab(x_m) = U_m`, and the vertices `x_1, x_2, ...` lie in order on one
     geodesic ray from `o`, with `d(x_(m-1), x_m) = Ω(m)`.
   - Bounds: `m - 1 <= Ω(m!) <= m log_2 m`. The asymptotic
     `Ω(m!) = m log log m + O(m)` is the classical estimate for the summatory
     function of `Ω` (Hardy–Wright, Theorem 430). It is cited from memory and not
     checked at the source here; nothing below depends on it.

## What it says about hosts (MT-C growth invariant)

- **Word length lower bound.** Let `H` be generated by a finite set `F`, contain
  `SL_2(Q)`, and act by isometries on a metric space `M`. Suppose there is an
  `SL_2(Q)`-equivariant isometric embedding `ι : X^0 -> M`, and put
  `C = max_(f∈F) d_M(ι o, f ι o)`, which is positive. Then
  `|u(1/m!)|_F >= 2 Ω(m!)/C >= 2(m - 1)/C`. If `ι` is only an equivariant coarse
  embedding with lower control `ρ`, then `|u(1/m!)|_F >= ρ(2 Ω(m!))/C`.
- **Coherence data.** In the complex of groups, the chain `U_m` is carried by the
  vertex groups `Stab(x_m)` along one geodesic ray. Its `m`-th step has length
  `Ω(m)` and turns in the prime directions of `m`. A host of the complex of
  groups must embed these vertex groups compatibly along the whole ray
  (`ascending-unions-embed-along-coherent-chains`).
- **One end per factor kills nothing by itself.** The configuration of part 2 is
  realized by `SL_2(Q)`, whose vertex groups `SL_2(Z)` are residually finite. What
  it forces is breadth: the divisibility is spread over infinitely many
  independent end directions. `fg-restricted-product-actions-have-finite-support`
  shows that no finitely generated coordinatewise host can do this.
