**ESTABLISHED (referee bh-ref-d, 2026-09-19).** No priority claimed.

## (1) Merge-freeness: PASS

- **Streams.** With `π_(i+1) = π_i u_i` and `u_i ∈ S* = {σ : σ(1) = 5}`, we get
  `c_(i+1) = π_i(u_i(1)) = π_i(5) = e_i`.
  - A stream `j` receives `R` at step `i′ ≥ 2` only if `e_(i′−1) = j`, and at step 1 only if `c_1 = j`.
  - It receives `D` at step `i` only if `e_i = j`.
  - `c_i ≠ e_i`, since `1 ≠ 5` and `π_i` is a bijection.
  - So each stream reads: an optional initial `R`, then `D(t_i) R(t_(i+1))` blocks. The initial `R` can
    only be followed by a `D`, because `R`s arrive only right after a `D` in the same stream.
  - Every stream therefore alternates, and there is no merge at depth 1.
- **Sections.** The section at `j` is `[t_1] d_(t_(i_1)) t_(i_1+1) d_(t_(i_2)) t_(i_2+1) ⋯`, with every
  label and connector in `S*`. It is again of the lemma's form, with an arbitrary optional prefix. So
  induction gives merge-freeness at every depth.
- **Reduced words.** `1 ∉ S*`, so every letter is nontrivial, and positive words in `Z` are reduced words
  in `A_5 * D` with distinct letter sequences. That gives `144^k` of them, i.e. 12 per letter.
- **Scope.** The node's "What this does not show" is accurate. The family defeats merge-only length
  reduction. It says nothing about geodesic length or injectivity into `H`.

## (2) Fabrykowski–Gupta: PASS

For the regular `Z/3` on 3 points, `{σ : σ(1) = 3}` has one element, so there is one word per length.
The "principle" gives `(|A|/d)² = |A_x|²` choices per step for this spinal shape. It is correct as a
statement about this family. The "can only succeed when `A` acts regularly" part is a heuristic
reading of it.

## (3) Attribution: PASS with one softening (checked at source)

**Source.** JNS, arXiv:1305.2637, LaTeX e-print read on MSI (`amenable.tex`, subsubsection "Groups of
Neumann-Segal type").
- JNS define the construction: `(G_i, X_i)` transitive, generating sequences `a_(i,j)`, points
  `x_i, y_i`, `α_(i,j)(xw) = a_(i,j)(x)w`, and
  `β_(i,j)(xw) = x_i β_(i+1,j)(w)` if `x = x_i`, `= y_i α_(i+1,j)(w)` if `x = y_i`, `= xw` otherwise.
- With `G_i = A_5`, `x_i = 5`, `y_i = 1` and `a_j` generating `A_5`, this gives
  `β_j = (α_j at 1, β_j at 5) = d_(a_j)`. Since `s ↦ d_s` is a homomorphism,
  `G = ⟨α_j, β_j⟩ = H`. So the identification is exact.

**Amenability.** Proposition `pr:neumansegaltype`: "The group `G = ⟨α_(0,1), …, β_(0,k)⟩` is amenable if
and only if the group generated by the sequences `(a_(1,j), a_(2,j), …) ∈ ∏ G_i` … is amenable." For
constant sequences that group is the diagonal `A_5`, so `H` is amenable. PASS.

**Softening needed.**
- JNS call this "a general version of D. Segal's construction". So `H` is its constant-sequence case.
- JNS say of Neumann: "The examples considered by P. Neumann … are similar, but they are finite-state".
  They do not identify Neumann's groups with this `H` exactly. Neumann's 1986 paper could not be fetched
  (Project Euclid blocks scripted access).
- JNS say of Segal: "The main examples of D. Segal … are non-amenable (… `G_i` equal to `PSL(2, p_i)` for
  an increasing sequence of primes)". That is about his *main examples*, not the construction.
- **Repair:**
  - Say "`H` is the constant-sequence group of Neumann–Segal type (JNS)".
  - Say "it is finite-state, like Neumann's examples". Do not say "it is Neumann's group" until Neumann's
    paper is read.
  - Replace "Segal is not `H`'s source" with "Segal's main examples are different (non-amenable); whether
    his paper treats constant sequences is unchecked".
  - The Proposition D density credit should go to that construction, as Neumann/Segal, pending the
    primary sources.
