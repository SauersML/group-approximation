Let `S` be a finite alphabet, `X` a finite set, and `sigma_s in Sym(X)` for
`s in S`.  For each tested multiplication relation `r=(g,h,k)` choose a good
root set `G_r subset X`.  Let `E` be the least equivalence relation on `X`
which is invariant under every `sigma_s` and `sigma_s^(-1)` and contains

```text
sigma_g sigma_h(i)  E  sigma_k(i)                      (DCE1)
```

for every `i in G_r`.

Then every `sigma_s` descends to a permutation of `X/E`.  Moreover, on the
cardinality-preserving weighted blow-up

```text
Y = { (C,t) : C in X/E, 1 <= t <= |C| },
```

the formula

```text
hat_sigma_s(C,t) = (sigma_s C,t)                       (DCE2)
```

defines a permutation of `Y`, with `|Y|=|X|`, and for each relation
`r=(g,h,k)`

```text
d_H(hat_sigma_g hat_sigma_h,hat_sigma_k)
 <= |X\G_r|/|X|.                                       (DCE3)
```

For any two words `u,v` in the alphabet,

```text
d_H(hat_sigma_u,hat_sigma_v)
 = |{i in X : sigma_u(i) not E sigma_v(i)}|/|X|.       (DCE4)
```

Thus invariant congruence closure can merge arbitrarily large classes without
amplifying the exceptional-root mass of the relations.  Its only possible
cost to a sofic model is the explicit separation collapse measured in
`(DCE4)`.
