The theorem is Thumann's. The statement of Theorem `41762` and the definitions were
read in the LaTeX source of arXiv:1409.1085. The specialization to dissection
operads below is lane reasoning and not independently reviewed.

## Statement

**Data.**
- A finite set of compact open *tiles* `X_1, …, X_c`, the colors, in some Cantor
  space.
- For each tile, a finite group `G_i` of homeomorphisms of `X_i`, and finitely many
  identifications between tiles. Together these form a groupoid `𝒢` with finite
  automorphism groups.
- A set `S` of *subdivisions*. Each is a homeomorphism `X_{i_1} ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ X_{i_k} → X_j`
  (`k ≥ 2`), injective on each part, with disjoint images covering `X_j`.

**The operad.** Let `𝒪` be the suboperad of `End(TOP, ⊔)` generated by `𝒢` and `S`.
Its operations are the iterated subdivisions (*dissections*) with labels. Its
transformation classes are the unlabelled dissections, ordered by refinement *inside
the operad*.

**Assumptions.**
1. **(Square filling.)** Any two dissections of the same tile have a common
   refinement that is a dissection of the operad over each of them.
2. **(Finite type.)** The spine of the refinement poset of nontrivial dissections is
   finite. The spine is the smallest set containing the minimal dissections such that
   every other dissection has a greatest spine element below it. It is generated by
   iterating minimal common refinements of pairs (arXiv:1409.1085, Definition
   `79040`, Construction `45209`).
3. **(Color-tame.)** Automatic if `c = 1` and some subdivision exists; in general,
   Definition `77101` of the source.

**Conclusion.** Every operad group `π_1(𝒪, X)` is of type `F_∞`.
- These are the Thompson-like groups of fractions `θ'∘θ^{-1}` of dissections of a
  finite union `X` of tiles.
- Under 1, `π_1(𝒪, X)` is exactly the group of such fractions acting on `X`.

## Why this is the non-LCM finiteness theorem

- **No least common multiples.** Nothing asks two dissections to have a *least*
  common refinement. Minimal common refinements may be several: that is the
  non-determinism, or non-LCM behaviour, in the master synthesis. They are all put
  into the spine, and only the finiteness of the spine is needed.
- **Brin–Thompson calibration.** For `nV` the source computes the spine exactly (the
  cube-cutting products). Here minimal common refinements are unique, so this is a
  lattice case.
- **Unimodular dissections.** For the edge-split operad on unimodular simplices,
  minimal common refinements of two first splits come in flop pairs
  (`edge-split-operad-satisfies-ore`). So that host is genuinely non-LCM, and the
  theorem still applies once 1 and 2 hold.
- **Faithfulness.** Cancellation is automatic, since operations are mono and epi
  (remark in the source). The equalization part of the calculus of fractions follows
  from injectivity. So assumption 1 is the whole of the calculus of fractions, and
  `π_1(𝒪, X)` acts faithfully on `X`.

## Proof sketch of the specialization

- **Hypotheses of Theorem `41762`.**
  - finitely many colors (`c`);
  - color-tame (assumption 3);
  - `ℐ(𝒪) = 𝒢`, a groupoid with finite automorphism groups, hence `F_∞^+`
    (remark in the source);
  - cancellative calculus of fractions (as explained in the previous section);
  - finite type (assumption 2, Definition `26536`).
- **Identification of the group.** Arrows of the fundamental groupoid of a category
  with a calculus of fractions are fractions (Gabriel–Zisman). A fraction is
  determined by the homeomorphism it induces, because operations are the maps
  themselves.

## Lesson for general BH

- **The top gate needs no new theorem.** The master synthesis's top gate, "Li
  without lcms", already exists in operad form. For every dissection-type host
  (`𝒯_m` for closed MCG, `2V_Λ` for Deligne, and any extension-move category that is
  an operad with transformations), finite presentation reduces to two checkable
  combinatorial conditions: square filling and a finite spine.
- **A split task.** This separates a theorem-type task, now discharged, from a case
  task: verify 1 and 2 for the specific host.
- **For the P2′ bridge.** The question becomes whether rigid-SFT extension moves
  compose as an operad with transformations. If they do, Thumann's theorem is the
  group-side FTR for them. If they do not, the missing structure is exactly that
  operad composition.
