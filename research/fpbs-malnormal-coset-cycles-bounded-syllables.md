Let `F` be a free group with a free basis `S`, let `H <= F` be finitely
generated and malnormal, and let `t in F \ H` with `F = <H, t>`. Let `m` be the
number of vertices of the based Stallings graph of `H` over `S`, and put
`D = m^2`.

**Coset graph.** Its vertices are the left cosets `gH`. It has one `t`-edge
`e(g)` from `gH` to `gtH` for each `g in F`. A *detour* for `e(g)` is a
finite path in this graph from `gH` to `gtH` that does not use `e(g)`.
Equivalently, it is a word `h_0 t^{ε_1} h_1 ... t^{ε_k} h_k` equal to `t` in
`F`, with `h_i in H`, whose `t`-letters sit at edges other than `e(g)`. This is
exactly the kind of detour used by the detour-packing lemma of
`research/artifacts/fpbs/small-cancellation-family.md` §2.2, and by
[[fpbs-first-return-torsion-detours]], with `a` there replaced by `t` here.

1. **Bounded coset overlap.** Let `T` be the Cayley tree of `(F, S)`. For
   distinct cosets `gH != g'H`, the convex hulls of `gH` and `g'H` in `T` meet
   in a subtree of diameter less than `D`.
2. **Syllable bound.** Take a simple cycle in the coset graph with `k` `t`-edges
   and reduced syllables `h_i`. Then `|h_i|_S <= (k-1) D + k |t|_S` for every
   `i`.
3. **Finiteness.** For each `R`, every edge has only finitely many simple
   cycles with at most `R` `t`-edges. So any family of pairwise edge-disjoint
   detours for `e(g)` contains only finitely many detours with at most `R`
   `t`-edges.
4. **Consequence for detour packing.** In the packing lemma (artifact §2.2–2.3),
   the additional measure is `q + (1-q) prod_i (1 - q^{k_i})`, where `k_i` is
   the number of retained new-generator edges in detour `i` and the detour
   supports are disjoint. Its proof reads the same for arbitrary detour words.
   This consequence is only a remark and is not part of the established
   content. By item 3, over such an `H` the product can tend to zero at a fixed
   `q` only if the packing count `N(R)` is finite for each `R` and
   `sum_R N(R) q^R` diverges. For every `q > 0`, that requires superexponential
   growth. The bounded-length regime (all `k_i <= N`), which is the one used by
   [[fpbs-first-return-torsion-detours]] and
   [[fpbs-torsion-finite-repair-relative-cost]], is dead at every malnormal
   free stage.

For `Gamma_mal` (`a -> a`, `b -> b a b^{-2}`), every stage pair
`(H_{n+1}, H_n)`, and every composite pair `(H_{n+j}, H_n)`, is of this form:
`F` has rank two, `H` is generated by two elements, and malnormality holds by
the Stallings-graph certificate cited in
[[fpbs-malnormal-subgroup-relations-finite-contacts]] item 5, since a
composite of malnormal inclusions is malnormal.

**The invariant and the step.**
- **Invariant.** Bounded coset overlap `D`, which comes from malnormality plus
  cocompactness of the hull.
- **Step.** Items 2 and 3 kill the "N(R) = infinity for some R" divergence step
  of the packing lemma (`research/artifacts/fpbs/small-cancellation-family.md`
  (2.4)–(2.5)).

**Not claimed.**
- No lower bound on `relC(E_F; E_H)` is claimed.
- It is not decided whether `h` is finite. Item 2 bounds the total cycle length
  only by `O(k^2 D + k^2 |t|)`, which allows superexponential counts.
