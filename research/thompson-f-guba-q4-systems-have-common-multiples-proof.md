**Imports (trust surface).** All from V. Guba, *Amenability problem for Thompson's group F: state of
the art*, arXiv:2305.07113v4 (Groups Complexity Cryptology 15:1), read in full from the arXiv PDF on
2026-09-17 (text extracted with pypdf; typos are the author's).

- (G1) Definition of density, §2: "By the density of a subgraph Y we mean its average vertex degree.
  [...] It is denoted by δ(Y ). [...] By the density of the Cayley graph Γ = C(G;A) we mean the number
  ¯δ(Γ) = sup_Y δ(Y ), where Y runs over all nonempty finite subgraphs."
- (G2) Theorem 2.7 ([33] = arXiv:2210.12304): "The density of the Cayley graph of Thompson's group F
  in the standard set of generators {x0,x 1} is strictly greater than 3.5. Equivalenly, the Cheeger
  isoperimetric constant of F in the same set of generators is stricltly less than 1/2." The proof
  sketch works "in the left Cayley graph" and "leads to finite subgraphs in the Cayley graph of F with
  density exceeding 3.5004." (`thompson-f-evacuation-schemes-need-capacity-three` cites the same
  theorem as survey Theorem 2.8; in the v4 text it is numbered 2.7.)
- (G3) Lemma 3.8: "For any g1,...,g n∈F there exists g∈F such that g1g,...,g ng∈M."
- (G4) Problem `Q_k`, §3: "Given k+1 linear combinations of elements x0,x1,x2, consider a system of k
  equations with k + 1 unknowns: (α0x0 +β0x1 +γ0x2)u0 = (α1x0 +β1x1 +γ1x2)u1 =··· =
  (αkx0 +βkx1 +γkx2)uk. Find a nonzero solution of this system, where u0,u 1,...,u k∈K[M], or prove
  that it does not exist."

Notation: `x_(n+1) = x_0^-n x_1 x_0^n`, so `x_j x_i = x_i x_(j+1)` for `i < j`. In particular
`x_2 = x_0^-1 x_1 x_0`, and `M` is the monoid generated by the `x_i`.

**Step 0 (the convention does not matter).** Take the left Cayley graph `Γ_L`: vertices `F`, an edge
`z — s z` for `s ∈ S = {x_0, x_1}`. `F` is torsion-free (`thompson-f-is-bi-orderable`), and
`x_0 ≠ x_1^±1`. So `Γ_L` is a simple 4-regular graph, and the four neighbours of `z` are
`x_0^±1 z, x_1^±1 z`. The map `z -> z^-1` is a graph isomorphism from `Γ_L` to the right Cayley
graph (`z — z s`). It preserves induced degrees, so `δ̄` is the same for both conventions, and (G2)
applies to `Γ_L`.

**Step 1 (union bound).** Let `Z ⊂ F` be finite and nonempty. The induced degree of `z ∈ Z` is the
number of `s ∈ {x_0^±1, x_1^±1}` with `s z ∈ Z`. Summing over `z`,

`δ(Z)|Z| = Σ_s |sZ ∩ Z|`, hence `Σ_s |sZ \ Z| = (4 - δ(Z))|Z|`.

For each `s`, left multiplication by `s` is a bijection, so `|s^-1 Z ∩ Z| = |Z ∩ sZ|`, and therefore
`|s^-1 Z \ Z| = |sZ \ Z|`. So `|x_0 Z \ Z| + |x_1 Z \ Z| = (4 - δ(Z))|Z| / 2`. Since
`{1, x_0, x_1} Z = Z ∪ (x_0 Z \ Z) ∪ (x_1 Z \ Z)`,

`|{1, x_0, x_1} Z| <= |Z| + (4 - δ(Z))|Z|/2 = (3 - δ(Z)/2)|Z|`.    (1)

**Step 2 (transfer to A = {x_0, x_1, x_2}).** Put `p = x_2 x_0^-1` and `q = x_1 x_0^-1`. Since
`x_2 = x_0^-1 x_1 x_0`, also `p = x_0^-1 x_1`. By `thompson-f-x1x0inv-and-x2x0inv-generate-a-copy-of-f`,
`x_0 -> p`, `x_1 -> q` extends to an injective homomorphism `ψ : F -> F`. Let

`Y = x_0^-1 ψ(Z)`.

Then `|Y| = |Z|`, since `ψ` is injective. Also
`A Y = {x_0 x_0^-1, x_1 x_0^-1, x_2 x_0^-1} ψ(Z) = {1, q, p} ψ(Z) = ψ({1, x_1, x_0} Z)`, so
`|AY| = |{1, x_0, x_1} Z|`. With (1):

`|AY| <= (3 - δ(Z)/2)|Y|`.    (2)

**Step 3 (K[F] is a domain).** Fix the bi-invariant order `<` of `thompson-f-is-bi-orderable`. For
nonzero `a, b ∈ K[F]` let `g, h` be the largest elements of their supports. If `g' ∈ supp a`,
`h' ∈ supp b` and `(g', h') ≠ (g, h)`, then `g'h' <= g h' <= g h` by bi-invariance, with at least
one inequality strict. So `gh` occurs exactly once among the products, the coefficient of `gh` in
`ab` is `a_g b_h ≠ 0`, and `ab ≠ 0`.

**Step 4 (dimension count).** Let `k >= 1`. Let `t_0, ..., t_k ∈ K[M]` be nonzero with supports in
`A`. Let `Y ⊂ F` be finite with `|AY| < (1 + 1/k)|Y|`. Consider the `K`-linear map

`Φ : K[Y]^(k+1) -> K[AY]^k`, `(u_0, ..., u_k) -> (t_0 u_0 - t_i u_i)_(i = 1..k)`,

where `K[Y]` is the space of elements of `K[F]` supported in `Y`. It is well defined because
`supp(t_i u) ⊆ A · supp(u) ⊆ AY`. The domain has dimension `(k+1)|Y|`, and the target has dimension
`k|AY| < (k+1)|Y|`. So `Φ` has a nonzero kernel vector `(u_0, ..., u_k)`.

Pick `j` with `u_j ≠ 0`. By Step 3, `w = t_j u_j ≠ 0`. Every `t_i u_i` equals `w`, so every `u_i` is
nonzero.

By (G3) there is `g ∈ F` with `(supp u_0 ∪ ... ∪ supp u_k) g ⊆ M`. Then `u_i g ∈ K[M]` is nonzero,
and `t_i (u_i g) = w g` for all `i`. This is a solution of `Q_k` in the sense of (G4), with every
unknown nonzero.

**Step 5 (conclusions).**
- *Item 1 of the claim* is (1) together with Step 2. Taking the infimum over `Z` gives
  `inf_Y |AY|/|Y| <= 3 - δ̄/2`.
- *Item 2.* Suppose `δ̄ > 4 - 2/k`. By (G1), `δ̄` is a supremum over finite sets, so some finite `Z`
  has `δ(Z) > 4 - 2/k`. By (2), `|AY| < (3 - 2 + 1/k)|Y| = (1 + 1/k)|Y|`, and Step 4 applies.
- *Item 3.* By (G2) and (G1), some finite `Z` has `δ(Z) > 3.5 = 4 - 2/4`. Item 2 with `k = 4` gives
  `Q_4`. Since `5/4 <= 1 + 1/k` for `k <= 4`, the same `Y` gives `Q_1`, ..., `Q_3`. Translating `Y`
  on the right by (G3) keeps `|AY|/|Y|` and puts `Y` inside `M`.
- *Obstruction.* Read item 2 backwards. If some `Q_k` has only the zero solution for some nonzero
  forms, then `δ̄ <= 4 - 2/k`, that is, `ι*(F; {x_0, x_1}) = 4 - δ̄ >= 2/k`. This uses
  `δ(Y) + ι*(Y) = 2m` from §2 of the survey.

**What the route does not give.**
- No degree bound for the `u_i`: they live on sets the size of Guba's density witnesses.
- Nothing on `Q_5`. The union bound in Step 1 throws away the overlap of `x_0 Z \ Z` and
  `x_1 Z \ Z`, so `Q_5` may already follow from Guba's sets. Checking that means computing
  `|{1, x_0, x_1} Z|` on those sets, which are far too large to enumerate. It is not claimed.
