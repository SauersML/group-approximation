Culf and Mastel, *RE-completeness of entangled constraint satisfaction
problems*, arXiv:2410.21223v2, Theorem 4.14, prove fixed-language
`RE`-completeness with completeness one and constant soundness whenever the
finite language is NP-complete and is Boolean or non-TVF.  Corollary 4.17
includes the assignment algebra with commutation, and Corollary 4.18 expands
the succinct output to a finite uniformly weighted instance by a total
exponential-time procedure while preserving a constant gap.

For any two fixed coordinates of `(CRS1)` and any prescribed pair of bits,
the parity equation leaves two choices for the remaining pair.  Since only
one global tuple is deleted, one choice survives.  This proves non-TVF and
also exhibits the visible-pair projection of the elementary commutativity
gadget.  Proposition 6.2 of the same paper gives weighted-algebra maps in
both directions for replacing empty binary constraints by this gadget, with
constants depending only on the fixed arity four.

Finite-dimensional defect zero in the limit is equivalent to a perfect CE
trace by taking, or lifting from, a tracial matrix ultraproduct.  Therefore
the promise gap has the form `(CRS2)` required by the machine-indexed route.

Finally, the paper's definition of `A_(a+comm)` assigns commutator weight only
when the two variables lie in a common `V_i`, giving `(CRS3)`.  The three
matrix algebras generated by `Z tensor 1`, `1 tensor Z`, and `1 tensor X`
prove that commutation of one center with two neighbors has no
neighbor--neighbor consequence.  This is exactly the extra hypothesis used
by `commuting-neighborhood-pruning-pays-atom-mass`, so that pruning step does
not follow from the cited fixed-language theorem.
