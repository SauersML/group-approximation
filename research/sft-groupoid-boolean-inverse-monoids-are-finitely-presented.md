## Setting

- `Λ = <S | R>` is finitely presented, `A` a finite alphabet, and `X ⊆ A^Λ` a nonempty SFT, all of
  whose forbidden patterns lie on translates of `B_r`. `Λ` acts on `X` freely.
- `S(H)` is the Boolean inverse monoid of compact open bisections of `H = Λ ⋉ X`, with orthogonal
  joins and relative complements (Wehrung's signature).
- `k[H] = LC(X, k) ⋊ Λ` is its Steinberg algebra. `T = G_V × H`, and `F(T)` is its full group.

## Theorem

1. **Monoid level (always).** Let `D >= r`. The Boolean inverse monoid presented by:
   - generators: the elements of `S` (declared units) and idempotents `e_a`, `a ∈ A`;
   - relators of `Λ` on `S`;
   - (Q1) `e_a e_b = 0` for `a ≠ b`, and `⋁_a e_a = 1`;
   - (Q3) `∏_(f ∈ B_D) w_f e_(α(f)) w_f^(-1) = 0` for every pattern `α ∈ A^(B_D)` not occurring in `X`,
     with `w_f` a fixed word for `f`;

   is isomorphic to `S(H)`, via `e_a ↦ 1_{x(1) = a}` and `s ↦` the global bisection of `s`. There is
   no commutation relation (Q2): idempotents of an inverse monoid commute by axiom.
2. **Ring level.** `k[H]` is finitely presented as a `k`-algebra iff the same presentation, read in
   `k`-algebras, needs only finitely many extra relations. By
   `quantum-rigid-sfts-over-fp-groups-have-fp-crossed-products` (and, over `Z^2`,
   `sft-crossed-product-fp-iff-quantum-rigid`), the missing relations are exactly the long-range
   commutations (Q2 at all distances). Quantum rigidity says they follow from short ones.
3. **Group level.** If `F(T)` is finitely presented, `X` is quantum rigid over every field
   (`fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`).
4. **The levels separate.** For the golden–silver product `Ω_1 × Ω_2` over `Z^2`, which is minimal,
   free and of finite type, part 1 gives a finitely presented Boolean inverse monoid. Its `V`-full
   group is not finitely presented (`v-times-golden-silver-wang-product-full-group-is-not-fp`).

## Proof of part 1

- **The idempotents.** Let `B` be the Boolean algebra generated by symbols `[a@q]` (`q ∈ Λ`)
  subject to (Q1) at each `q` and to (Q3) at every translate. By Stone duality `B = Clopen(Y)`,
  where `Y ⊆ A^Λ` is the set of configurations whose `B_D`-windows all occur in `X`. Since `X` is
  an SFT with window `B_r ⊆ B_D`, and every `B_D`-pattern of `X` avoids the forbidden patterns,
  `Y = X`.
- **Covariance.** In the presented monoid `M`, put `e_a(q) = w_q e_a w_q^(-1)`. Units conjugate
  idempotents to idempotents, and the relators of `Λ` make `q ↦ w_q` well defined up to units that
  act trivially. So `E(M)` receives a Boolean homomorphism `β : Clopen(X) → E(M)`, equivariant for the
  conjugation action of `Λ`. `β` is onto, since the `e_a(q)` generate `E(M)` as a Boolean algebra
  (a product `m e m^(-1)` with `m` a join of idempotent-times-unit terms is again in the Boolean
  algebra generated by translates).
- **Normal form.** Every element of `M` is a finite orthogonal join `⋁ β(K_i) w_(λ_i)` with the `K_i`
  pairwise disjoint clopens. The same holds in `S(H)`, where `1_K λ` is the bisection
  `{(λ, x) : x ∈ λ^(-1)K}`.
- **Injectivity.** Two such joins are equal in `S(H)` iff, after common refinement of the `K_i`,
  the pieces carry the same `λ` on each nonempty piece. Arrows of the transformation groupoid are
  pairs `(λ, x)`, so a nonempty bisection `1_K λ` determines `λ`; part 1 does not use freeness,
  which enters only in parts 3–4, where `H` must be a groupoid of germs. Refinement is a Boolean
  operation, valid in `M` through `β`. So
  `S(H) → M`, `⋁ 1_(K_i) λ_i ↦ ⋁ β(K_i) w_(λ_i)`, is well defined. It is inverse to the canonical
  map `M → S(H)`, because both are identities on generators.

## Proof of parts 2–4

Part 2 is the cited ring theorem plus the observation that the ring presentation is the monoid
presentation with (Q2) removed. In a ring, idempotents need not commute. Part 3 is cited. Part 4
combines part 1 with the cited refutation.

## Lesson for general BH

- **The classical data is always finitely presented.** For an SFT host, every Boolean or classical
  presentation of the dynamics is finitely presented for free, by Stone duality plus finite type.
  Every obstruction to finite presentation of the ring, and of the `V`-full group, is
  noncommutative: the "supports" of short relations must be forced to commute globally.
- **Where P2′ lives.** Both the ring and the group know only local commutation, and have to derive
  global commutation. The ring does it iff `X` is quantum rigid. The open implication P2′
  (`v-times-quantum-rigid-sft-full-groups-are-fp`) asks whether the group can do it whenever the
  ring can.
- **What cannot work.** No theorem of the shape "finitely presented Boolean inverse monoid ⇒
  finitely presented `V`-full group" exists: golden–silver refutes it. Any proof of P2′ has to use
  the ring-level (quantum) rigidity, not merely finite type.
