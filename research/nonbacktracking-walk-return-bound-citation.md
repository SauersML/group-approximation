**Source.** G. Kozma and A. Lubotzky, *Linear representations of random groups*,
arXiv:1810.01529. The arXiv e-print was fetched on MSI on 2026-09-13 (`nosl3.tex`, md5
`b9aa69e9ea17bdfd753a26dec3c898ad`).

**Verbatim.**
- **`lem:half` (l.490–499):** "Let $G$ be any $d$-regular connected multigraph with $d\ge4$
  and more than 2 vertices, and let $x$ be some vertex of $G$. Let $t>1$. Then
  $\mathbb{P}^{x}(N(t)=x)\le\frac{d-2}{d-1}$ where $N(t)$ is a nonbacktracking random walk on
  $G$ at the $t^{\textrm{th}}$ step and $\mathbb{P}^{x}$ denotes the probability when
  $N(0)=x$."
- **Conventions (l.501–512):** self-loops count twice, the walk may not traverse an edge and
  then the same edge backwards, and later steps choose uniformly among $d-1$ possibilities.
- **Application to words (l.593–604):** "$\omega(A)$ is distributed like a nonbacktracking
  random walk of length $l$ on the Cayley graph generated by $A$ … This Cayley graph is a
  $2m$-regular multigraph, and by assumption it has more than 2 vertices. Hence we may use
  Lemma \ref{lem:half} to get … $\le\frac{2m-2}{2m-1}$."

**Match.** The target restates the lemma. The paragraph "Use for words" repeats the
source's application and checks the letter-to-edge correspondence by hand:
- a directed edge labelled `x_i^{±1}` leaves each vertex once for each sign and each `i`;
- the reverse of the edge just traversed carries the inverse label;
- so "no immediate inverse letter" is the nonbacktracking rule.

The source's proof (from l.513) was read, not re-derived. The application on main uses only
finite multigraphs, the Cayley multigraphs of finite quotients.
