**ESTABLISHED** (lane bh-invent-16, 2026-09-18; lane proof, elementary; not reviewed; no priority
claimed). This is gate 5 of the v5 synthesis (P2′b), in the non-amenable case. FPG for nontrivial actions
stays **OPEN**, and so do FSG(F_2, 2) and EX(Z).

## Setting

Notation is that of `v-times-free-product-gluing-kernels-split-over-the-factors`, and full bisections are
used throughout:
- `Λ = Λ_1 * Λ_2` acts on `X`, and `F_i = F(T_i)` for `T_i = G_V × (Λ_i ⋉ X)`;
- `B = LC(X, V)` and `A = F_1 *_B F_2`;
- `G_* = A / ⟨⟨[a_1, a_2] : a_i ∈ F_i, supports disjoint⟩⟩`, and FPG says `θ : G_* → F(T)` is injective;
- `M_i = V(Λ_i)` and `N_i = B ⋊ Λ_i`, with gluing groups `Γ~_i`, `Γ~` and kernels `K_i`, `K`;
- `cc` is the set of label commutators `[M_(1,U), M_(2,U')]`, for disjoint clopen `U, U' ⊆ C`, where
  `M_(i,U) ≤ V(Λ_i)` consists of the elements supported in `U × X`.

## Theorem

1. **Gluing groups of free products.** `Γ~ ≅ (Γ~_1 *_B Γ~_2) / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩`, as a pushout over `B` (which lies
   in `N_i ≤ Γ~_i`).
2. **Quotient by the factor kernels.** Let `R = ⟨⟨ι_1 K_1 ∪ ι_2 K_2⟩⟩`. Then
   `Γ~ / R ≅ A / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩ ≅ G_*`, compatibly with the maps to `F(T)`. In particular, in `A` every
   disjoint-support commutator is a consequence of the label commutators `cc`.
3. **Equivalence.** `K / R ≅ ker θ`. So:
   - FPG holds iff `K = ⟨⟨ι_1 K_1 ∪ ι_2 K_2⟩⟩`;
   - exactness for `Λ` on `X` (`K = 1`) implies FPG, and implies `ι_i(K_i) = 1`;
   - conversely, FPG together with `ι_1(K_1) = ι_2(K_2) = 1` gives `K = 1`.
4. **Free groups.** Put `X = A^(F_n)` and `F_n = ⟨s_1⟩ * ⋯ * ⟨s_n⟩`.
   - Exactness for `F_n` follows from FPG at every stage `⟨s_1, …, s_(j−1)⟩ * ⟨s_j⟩` together with EX(Z)
     (`K_(Z, C^Z) = 1`). Here `X` is a closed `⟨s⟩`-invariant copy inside `C^Z`, so each cyclic kernel
     vanishes.
   - Conversely, exactness for `F_n` forces FPG at the top stage `F_(n−1) * ⟨s_n⟩`.
5. **FPG for trivial actions.** If `Λ` acts trivially on `X`, then FPG holds. In that case
   `F(T) = LC(X, V(Λ))` and `F_i = LC(X, V(Λ_i))`. The case where `X` is a point is item 5 of the parent
   node.

## Proof

**1.** Compare the universal properties.
- *Maps out of `Γ~`.* A homomorphism `Γ~ → H` is a pair `α : M → H`, `β : N → H` that agree on
  `I = V × Λ` and satisfy `[α(M_0), β(N_1)] = 1`. Here:
  - `M = (M_1 *_V M_2) / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩` (the point case of FPG, parent item 5) and `N = N_1 *_B N_2`;
  - `I = ⟨V, Λ_1, Λ_2⟩`;
  - `M_0 = ⟨M_(1,C_0), M_(2,C_0)⟩`, because it is the copy of `M = ⟨M_1, M_2⟩` into `C_0`.

  So the data are:
  - `α_i : M_i → H` agreeing on `V` and killing `cc`;
  - `β_i : N_i → H` agreeing on `B`;
  - `α_i = β_i` on `V` and on `Λ_i`;
  - `[α_i(M_(i,0)), β_i(N_1)] = 1`, where `N_1 = LC(X, V_[1]) ≤ B`.
- *Maps out of the pushout.* A homomorphism `(Γ~_1 *_B Γ~_2) / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩ → H` is a pair `γ_i : Γ~_i → H`
  agreeing on `B` and killing `cc`. Each `γ_i` is a pair `(α_i, β_i)` agreeing on `V × Λ_i` and killing
  `[M_(i,0), N_1]`. Agreement on `B ⊇ V` makes the `α_i` agree on `V`.
- The two lists of data coincide.

**2.**
- Quotienting a pushout by normal subgroups of its factors gives the pushout of the quotients. Here
  `Γ~_i / K_i = F_i`, and `B` embeds in `F_i`, so `Γ~ / R ≅ (F_1 *_B F_2) / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩ = A / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩`.
- Under this isomorphism, the map `ψ : A → Γ~/R` of the parent node is the quotient map. Parent item 3
  shows that `ψ` kills every disjoint-support commutator, so these lie in `⟨⟨cc⟩⟩`.
- Conversely `cc` consists of disjoint-support commutators. Hence `A / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩ = G_*`.

**3.** By item 2, `Γ~/R → F(T)` is `θ`, so `K / R = ker θ`. For the second bullet, `R ⊆ K`. The third is
parent item 4.

**4.** Induct on `j` with 3, applied to `F_j = F_(j−1) * ⟨s_j⟩` on the same `X`: the factor `F_(j−1)` is
exact by induction, and `K_(⟨s_j⟩, X)` is an image of `K_(Z, C^Z) = 1` (parent item 6). The converse is the
second bullet of 3 at the top stage.

**5.**
- *Pieces on a partition.* Every element of `F_i = LC(X, V(Λ_i))` is a product of commuting elements
  `c^[P]` (the constant `c ∈ V(Λ_i)` on `C × P`, the identity elsewhere) over a clopen partition of `X`.
  These have disjoint supports.
- *The atom groups.* For clopen `P`, let `S_P ≤ G_*` be generated by `V(Λ_1)^[P]` and `V(Λ_2)^[P]`.
  - The relations of the point case hold in `S_P`: the copies of `V(Λ_i)` and the amalgamation over
    `V^[P] ≤ B`. Disjoint cones `U, U'` give disjoint supports `U × P` and `U' × P`.
  - So `S_P` is a quotient of `V(Λ_1) *_V V(Λ_2) / ⟨⟨cc⟩⟩ ≅ V(Λ)` that maps onto `V(Λ)^[P] ≅ V(Λ)`.
    Hence `S_P ≅ V(Λ)` and `θ` is injective on it.
- *Commuting atoms.* For disjoint `P, P'`, `S_P` and `S_(P')` commute: inside each `F_i`, or by disjoint
  commutation.
- *Conclusion.* Given a word in `G_*`, refine to one partition `𝒫` of `X` and rewrite it as `∏_(P∈𝒫) s_P`
  with `s_P ∈ S_P`. If `θ` kills the word, each `θ(s_P)` is trivial, so each `s_P = 1`. ∎

## The Boolean power of the Bass–Serre tree (the geometric form of FPG; open)

Let `𝒯` be the Bass–Serre tree of `Λ_1 * Λ_2`, with base edge `v_1 – v_2`.
- **The complex.** The **Boolean power** `𝒬` has as vertices the locally constant sections
  `s : Ω → V(𝒯)`.
  - Edges change `s` on one clopen set by tree edges.
  - Cubes are independent such changes on disjoint clopens.
  - Over a finite clopen partition with `r` atoms, `𝒬` restricts to the product cube complex `𝒯^r`, and
    `𝒬` is the direct limit of these. So `𝒬` is simply connected, indeed contractible.
- **The action.** `F(T)` acts by `(g·s)(gω) = λ_g(ω) · s(ω)`, where `λ_g(ω)` is the `Λ`-label of the
  arrow of `g` at `ω`. The stabilizers are:
  - `F_1` and `F_2` for the constant sections `v_1` and `v_2`;
  - `B` for the edge between them, since `Λ_1 ∩ Λ_2 = 1`;
  - `F_1(E) × F_2(Ω ∖ E)` for the mixed section `s_E`, equal to `v_1` on `E` and `v_2` off `E`.
- **What `G_*` records.** Its relations are exactly the stabilizers of the Boolean cube
  `𝒞 = {s_E : E clopen}` and its faces.
- **The obstacle.** `𝒞` is not a strict fundamental domain. `s_E` and `s_(E′)` lie in one orbit iff some
  `g` carries `E` onto `E′` by `T_1`-germs and `Ω ∖ E` onto `Ω ∖ E′` by `T_2`-germs.
- **What FPG then needs.** By Brown's presentation theorem applied to `F(T) · 𝒞`, FPG comes down to two
  things:
  - that `F(T) · 𝒞` is simply connected;
  - that each such type-changing `g`, factored in `G_*` as a product of `F_1` and `F_2` elements, gives
    the same element of `G_*` for every factorization.

## Lesson for general BH

**For free products, gluing exactness is exactly free-product gluing.** The gluing group of `V ×`
`(Λ_1 * Λ_2)` is the amalgam of the two factor gluing groups. So the free product's gluing kernel is
generated by the factor kernels iff the full group is the amalgam of the factor full groups modulo
disjoint commutation (FPG).
- Exactness forces FPG, so FPG is a genuine necessary step, not an artifact of the method.
- FPG holds whenever the action does not mix fibres (trivial actions).
- What remains is precisely the type-changing elements, which carry a clopen set by one factor's germs
  and its complement by the other's.

For P2′b over `F_n`, and hence over the Track A group, the content is therefore two pieces:
- the cyclic case EX(Z) on the Cantor-alphabet shift;
- type-changing coherence in the Boolean power of the Bass–Serre tree.
