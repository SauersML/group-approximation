Notation as in the claim. `D_Γ` is the diagonal subgroup of `Γ` (for `Aff`, `D_Γ = C = Q^x`).

**Inputs.**
- **(NF) Normal form.** In an amalgam `X *_Z Y`, a product `g_1 g_2 ... g_k` with `k >= 1` whose factors lie
  alternately in `X \ Z` and `Y \ Z` is not `1`. Serre, *Trees*, Ch. I, §1.2, Theorem 1 and its
  corollary; Lyndon--Schupp, Ch. IV, Theorem 2.6. The factors `X` and `Y` embed in `X *_Z Y`.
- **(FP) Finitely presented quotients.** If `G` is generated by a finite set `X` and `G/N` is finitely
  presented, then `N` is the normal closure of finitely many elements. Proof: finite presentability does
  not depend on the finite generating set (Tietze), so `G/N = <X | R_0>` with `R_0` finite. The kernel of
  `F(X) -> G/N` is `<<R_0>>`, and its image in `G` is `N`. So `N = <<R_0>>_G`.
- **(DC) Directed colimits.** A directed colimit of groups is computed on underlying sets. So an element
  of one term that becomes `1` in the colimit is already `1` in some later term. Colimits commute with
  pushouts.

**Step 0: `Γ = <Γ(Z), D_Γ>`.**
- `GL_n`, `SL_n`: `Γ` is generated by the elementary matrices `x_ij(a)`, `a in Q`, together with `D_Γ` for
  `GL_n`. For `t in D_Γ`, `t x_ij(c) t^-1 = x_ij(c t_i/t_j)`.
  - For `GL_n` take `t_i = 1/b` and `1` elsewhere; then `x_ij(a/b) = t x_ij(a) t^-1`.
  - For `SL_n` take `t_i = 1/b`, `t_j = b` and `1` elsewhere; then `x_ij(a/b) = t x_ij(ab) t^-1`.
  - `x_ij(c) = x_ij(1)^c in Γ(Z)` for `c in Z`.
- `Aff`: `m_r τ^c m_r^-1 = (x -> x + rc)`, and `Aff(Q) = Q ⋊ Q^x`.

**Step 1: the filtration.** Put `L = Γ(Z) *_(C(Z)) B`, where `C(Z) = C ∩ Γ(Z)` and `C(Z) -> B` is the
restriction of `C -> B`. Let `φ : L -> Γ *_C B` be induced by the inclusions.
- `L` is finitely generated, since `Γ(Z)` and `B` are.
- `φ` is onto. Its image contains `Γ(Z)` and `B`, hence `D_Γ ⊆ C ⊆ B`, hence `Γ` by Step 0.
- For finite `S`, `E_S = G_S *_C B` with `G_S = Γ_S *_(C_S) C`. For `S ⊆ S'` the inclusions
  `Γ_S ⊆ Γ_(S')`, `C_S ⊆ C_(S')` and the identities of `C` and `B` induce `E_S -> E_(S')`. The maps
  `Γ_S ⊆ Γ` and `C ⊆ Γ` induce `G_S -> Γ`, and then `E_S -> Γ *_C B`. `L -> E_S` is induced by
  `Γ(Z) ⊆ Γ_S` and the identity of `B`. It is well defined: `c in C(Z)` lies in `C_S`, where `G_S` identifies it
  with `c in C`, and `E_S` identifies that with its image in `B`, which is where the `B`-route sends it.
  The same check covers `G_S -> G_(S')` and `G_S -> Γ`. All these maps commute.
- By (DC), `colim_S G_S = (∪_S Γ_S) *_(∪_S C_S) C = Γ *_C C = Γ`, and `colim_S E_S = Γ *_C B`. Here
  `∪_S Γ_S = Γ` since every matrix over `Q` has entries in some `Z[1/S]`.
- So `ker φ` is the increasing union of the kernels `ker(L -> E_S)`, by (DC).

**Step 2: the relation `(BS_p)` fails in `E_S`.** Let `p ∉ S`. Write the relator as a word in `L`:
- `GL_n`: `w_p = d_p^-1 · x_21(1) · d_p · x_21(1)^-p`;
- `SL_n`: `w_p = t_p^-1 · x_21(1) · t_p · x_21(1)^-(p^2)`;
- `Aff`: `w_p = m_p · τ · m_p^-1 · τ^-p`.

The dilation factors lie in `D_Γ ⊆ C ⊆ B` and the root factors in `Γ(Z)`. The conjugation
identities `d_p^-1 x_21(c) d_p = x_21(pc)`, `t_p^-1 x_21(c) t_p = x_21(p^2 c)` and
`m_p τ m_p^-1 = (x -> x+p)` show `w_p = 1` in `Γ`, so `φ(w_p) = 1`.

The image of `w_p` in `E_S` lies in `G_S = Γ_S *_(C_S) C`.
- `d_p^(±1)`, `t_p^(±1)`, `m_p^(±1)` lie in `C \ C_S`: an entry is `p^(±1)`, which is not in `Z[1/S]`.
- `x_21(1)` and `x_21(1)^-p`, `x_21(1)^-(p^2)` lie in `Γ_S \ C_S`, because `C` meets `x_21(Q)` trivially.
  `τ` and `τ^-p` lie in `Aff(Z[1/S])` and are not dilations.
- So `w_p` is an alternating product of length 4 as in (NF), and it is not `1` in `G_S`. `G_S` embeds in
  `E_S` by (NF). So `w_p ∉ ker(L -> E_S)`.

**Step 3: item 1.** Suppose `Γ *_C B` is finitely presented. By (FP) and Step 1, `ker φ = <<k_1,...,k_r>>`.
By Step 1 each `k_i` lies in `ker(L -> E_(S_i))`. Take `S = S_1 ∪ ... ∪ S_r`. Then
`ker φ ⊆ ker(L -> E_S)`. Pick a prime `p ∉ S`. Then `w_p in ker φ` by Step 2, and `w_p ∉ ker(L -> E_S)`,
a contradiction. Item 2 is Steps 1--2.

**Item 3.** `N_n(Q)` is generated by the signed permutation matrices and the `diag(1,...,q,...,1)`, `q`
prime. With `h·q_i = q` for representatives `q_1, ..., q_s` of the `H`-orbits on `P`,
`(1,h) diag(q_i,1,...,1) (1,h)^-1 = diag(q,1,...,1)` in `N_n(Q) ⋊ H`. So `N_n(Q) ⋊ H` is generated by
the signed permutation matrices, the `diag(q_i,1,...,1)` and generators of `H`. The same holds for
`Q^x ⋊ H`. `N_n(Q)` contains `D_Γ` and meets `x_21(Q)` trivially, so item 1 applies with `C = N_n(Q)`.
For `n = 1`, `C = Q^x`.

**Item 4.** This is the argument of `prime-shift-hnn-groups-are-not-finitely-presented-proof` ("The
quotient"), with `<s>` replaced by `H`.
- Let `M = <<Γ(Z)>>_Γ`. For `n >= 2`, `M = {g : det g = ±1}`: it contains `SL_n(Q)`, the normal closure of
  `x_12(1)` in `GL_n(Q)` (loc. cit.), and `diag(-1,1,...,1)`, and it lies in that normal subgroup. So
  `|det| : Γ/M ≅ Q_>0`. For `n = 1`, `M = Q ⋊ {±1}` and `Γ/M ≅ Q_>0`.
- `C -> Γ/M` is onto. By the universal property of amalgams, `Π^H / <<M>> ≅ (C ⋊ H) / <<C ∩ M>>`. Here
  `C ∩ M = ker(|det|)` on `C`. It is `H`-invariant because `det(h·c) = σ_h(det c)` and `σ_h(±1) = ±1`.
  So it is normal in `C ⋊ H`.
- The quotient is `Q_>0 ⋊ H`, with `h` acting by `σ_h`. `Q_>0` is the free abelian group on `P`, and `σ_h`
  permutes that basis as `h` permutes `P`. So the quotient is `Z ≀_P H`.
- **Cornulier.** Y. de Cornulier, *Finitely presented wreath products and double coset decompositions*,
  Geom. Dedicata 122 (2006), 89--108, arXiv:math/0509090v2. Read through the arXiv abstract and HTML
  rendering, not the PDF.
  - Theorem 1.1: "If W≠{1}, the wreath product W≀ₓG is finitely presented if and only if the following
    conditions are satisfied (i) W and G are finitely presented, (ii) G acts on X with finitely
    generated stabilizers, and (iii) the product action of G on the cartesian square X² has finitely
    many orbits."
  - Example 3.5: Thompson's group `T` "acts two-transitively on T/F = ℤ[1/2]/ℤ."
  - With `W = Z`, `G = T` and `X = Z[1/2]/Z ≅ P`: `T` is finitely presented (Cannon--Floyd--Parry), the
    stabilizers are conjugates of `F`, and `X^2` has two orbits. So `Z ≀_P T` is finitely presented.

**Item 5.**
- For `m in N_n(Q)`, `σ_(h,n) m σ_(h,n)^-1` applies `σ_h` to the diagonal entries of `m`, because `σ_h` is
  multiplicative with `σ_h(0) = 0` and commutes with coordinate permutations and signs
  (`gl-n-q-lies-in-prime-shift-permutation-group`, item 2, for `H = Z`). And `h -> σ_(h,n)` is a
  homomorphism, because `H` acts on `P` on the left, so `σ_(hh') = σ_h σ_(h')`. So the two maps of item 5 agree on `N_n(Q)` and define `Π_n^H -> K_n^H`, onto by
  definition of `K_n^H`.
- If `K` is finitely presented and `Π_n^H -> K` were injective, `Π_n^H` would be finitely presented,
  against item 3.
- For `H = Z`, the relation required by `prime-shift-kn-fp-reduces-to-a-metabelian-quotient`, item 4,
  comes from the non-finitely presented quotient `Z ≀ Z`. For `Z ≀_P T` that quotient is finitely
  presented, so a relation of that form is not forced. Item 5 still forces some relation outside
  `Π_n^H`.

**Item 6.** Conjugating `d_p^-1 x_21(1) d_p = x_21(1)^p` by `f` gives `d_q^-1 v d_q = v^p`. If
`v = x_21(t)`, then `x_21(qt) = x_21(pt)`, so `t = 0`.

**Scope.** Baumslag: "Wreath products of finitely presented groups", Math. Z. 75 (1961), 22--28 (title
per gq-referee-b). Items 1--3 use only (NF), (FP) and (DC). No action of the amalgam is used, so the conclusion
holds for every finitely generated `B` and every embedding of `C`. The argument needs infinitely many
primes: for `Γ = SL_n(Z[1/p])` the filtration is finite and nothing follows.
