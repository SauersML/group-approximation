**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, written below, not reviewed; no priority claimed).

## Notation

For a group `G` let `J_1(G) ⊴ G * <x>` be the one-variable mixed identities: the words
`w` with `w(g) = 1` for every `g ∈ G`. Let `E(G) ≤ G^G` be the group generated by the
constant maps and the identity map. As recorded in
`e-of-finitely-presented-group-is-recursively-presented`, `E(G) ≅ (G * Z)/J_1(G)`, with
`g ↦` (constant map `g`) and `x ↦ id`.

## Statement

There is a group `Γ` with three generators such that:
1. `Γ` has a recursive presentation and a solvable word problem;
2. `J_1(Γ)` is not recursively enumerable, though it is co-r.e.;
3. `E(Γ)` is a 4-generator group that is not recursively presented, whose word problem
   is co-r.e. but not decidable. By Higman's embedding theorem, `E(Γ)` embeds in no
   finitely presented group.

## Construction

1. **The base group `G = N ⋊ <t>`.**
   - `N` is the free nilpotent group of class 2 on generators `a_i`, `i ∈ Z`.
   - `t` acts by the shift `t^{-1} a_i t = a_{i+1}`.
   - `G` is generated by `a = a_0` and `t`. It has the recursive presentation: generators
     `a, t`; relators `[[a_i, a_j], a_k]` for all `i, j, k`, where `a_i := t^{-i} a t^i`.
   - Put `c_{i,j} = [a_i, a_j]` for `i < j`. The derived subgroup `N' = Z(N)` is free
     abelian with basis `{c_{i,j} : i < j}`, and `t^{-1} c_{i,j} t = c_{i+1,j+1}`.
   - Normal forms in `N` are computable, so `G` has solvable word problem.
2. **Rows.** For `d ≥ 1` let `R_d = span_Z{c_{k,k+d} : k ∈ Z}`, the Z-span of one
   `t`-orbit of basis elements. Each `R_d` is normal in `G`: `N` centralizes `N'`, and
   `t` permutes the orbit. `N' = ⊕_d R_d`.
3. **The recursive set `H`.** Fix a standard enumeration of Turing machines `φ_d`, and let
   `H = {(d,k) : d ≥ 1, k ∈ Z, φ_d(d) does not halt within |k| steps}`. Then `H` is
   recursive, and `{d} × Z ⊆ H` iff `φ_d(d)` never halts. The set
   `S = {d : φ_d(d) never halts}` is co-r.e. and not r.e.
4. **The amalgam `Γ`.** Let `C = span_Z{c_{k,k+d} : (d,k) ∈ H} ≤ N'`, a direct summand
   of `N'` spanned by basis elements. Put `Γ = G *_C (C × <c>)`, amalgamating the two
   copies of `C`.
   - **Generators:** `a, t, c`.
   - **Presentation:** the relators of `G`, plus `[c, t^{-k} [a, t^{-d} a t^d] t^k]` for
     `(d,k) ∈ H`. This is a recursive set.
   - **Word problem:** both factors have solvable word problem. Membership in `C` is
     decidable in each factor: an element of `G` lies in `C` iff it lies in `N'` and its
     coordinates in the basis `c_{i,j}` are supported on `H`. The amalgamating maps are
     the identity. So `Γ` has solvable word problem, by the normal form theorem for
     amalgamated free products.

## The mixed words

For `d ≥ 1` let `w_d(x) = [c, x^{-1} c_{0,d} x] ∈ Γ * <x>`, where
`c_{0,d} = [a, t^{-d} a t^d]`. The map `d ↦ w_d` is computable.

**Claim.** `w_d ∈ J_1(Γ)` iff `d ∈ S`.

- **(⇐)** Suppose `{d} × Z ⊆ H`. Then `R_d ⊆ C`. The group `Γ` is generated by `G` and
  `c`. `R_d` is normal in `G`, and `c` centralizes `C ⊇ R_d`. So `R_d ⊴ Γ`, and every
  conjugate `x^{-1} c_{0,d} x` lies in `R_d ⊆ C`, where it commutes with `c`. Hence
  `w_d(x) = 1` for all `x ∈ Γ`.
- **(⇒)** Suppose `(d,k) ∉ H` for some `k`. Take `x = t^k`, so that
  `x^{-1} c_{0,d} x = c_{k,k+d}`. This basis element lies outside the span `C` of the
  other basis elements, so `c_{k,k+d} ∈ G ∖ C`. Also `c ∈ (C × <c>) ∖ C`. So
  `c · c_{k,k+d} · c^{-1} · c_{k,k+d}^{-1}` is an alternating word with no letter in `C`,
  and it is nontrivial by the normal form theorem. Hence `w_d(t^k) ≠ 1`.

## Proof of the statement

- **Part 2.** `J_1(Γ)` is co-r.e.: `w ∉ J_1(Γ)` iff some `g` has `w(g) ≠ 1`, and this
  can be found by search, since `Γ` has solvable word problem. If `J_1(Γ)` were r.e.,
  then `S` would be r.e.: enumerate `J_1(Γ)` and compare each element with `w_d`, using
  the solvable word problem of `Γ * Z`. But `S` is not r.e.
- **Part 3.** `E(Γ)` is generated by the constant maps at `a, t, c` and by `id`. Its
  trivial words over these generators are exactly `J_1(Γ)`, which is not r.e. Recursive
  presentability of a finitely generated group does not depend on the finite generating
  set, so `E(Γ)` is not recursively presented. Its word problem is `J_1(Γ)`: co-r.e. but
  not decidable. By Higman's embedding theorem, a finitely generated group embeds in a
  finitely presented group iff it is recursively presented. So `E(Γ)` embeds in none.
- **Π₂ variant.** Replace `H` by the r.e. set
  `{(d,k) : φ_d(|k|) halts}`. Then `Γ` is still finitely generated and recursively
  presented, and `{d : w_d ∈ J_1(Γ)}` is the Π₂-complete set of total machines.

## Scope

- `Γ` is finitely generated but not finitely presented: the base `G` is not finitely
  presented. So this does not answer the finitely presented question in
  `e-of-finitely-presented-group-is-recursively-presented`. It shows that finite
  presentation is the only hypothesis of that question that could force `J_1(G)` to be
  r.e.
- The gap to finitely presented `G` is recorded on that node.
- Relevance to Boone–Higman: `E(G)` is a natural functorial companion of `G`. Here a
  decidable input `G` has an `E(G)` that no Boone–Higman host can contain, since `E(G)`
  is not recursively presented. That is consistent, because `E(G)` itself is not a
  decidable group.
