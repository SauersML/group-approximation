**Lane proof, not reviewed.** It transfers two published proofs step by step.
- Belk–Zaremsky, arXiv:2001.04579v2, §§5–7: the Stein complex, contractibility, and
  descending links.
- Zaremsky, arXiv:2405.18354v2, §§3–4: finite presentation from 2-elementary subcomplexes,
  and Proposition 3.1 on bad edge stabilizers.

Both papers were read from the arXiv PDFs on 2026-09-18. Coordinates `s ∈ S` become halving
directions, and the twist group `G` becomes the structure group `Γ_X`. Only two places
need genuinely new input: the vertex and edge stabilizers, and the F_2-linear nerve in
their Lemma 7.6. **Load-bearing: referee review requested.**

## Setting

`X = Z_2^D`. A **piece** is a coset `c + L`, where `L` is an open subgroup defined by
integral congruences on finitely many coordinates. Every piece is affinely `≅ X` by maps
whose linear parts lie in `GL(Z[1/2])`, and two such identifications differ by an element of
`Γ_X` (`integral-affine-structure-group-is-finitely-presented`). A **halving** of a piece is
its split into the two cosets of an index-2 subgroup. `W` is the group of homeomorphisms of
`X` that are piecewise "identification ∘ `γ` ∘ identification^{-1}" with `γ ∈ Γ_X`. The
groupoid `𝒲` of such maps `X(m) → X(n)` between disjoint unions of copies plays the role of
`SV_G`'s groupoid; its structure maps `G(m) = Γ_X ≀ S_m` play the role of twisted permutations.

## Dictionary: each step, with its justification

1. **Forests and partitions (BZ22 Lemma 2.3, Prop 5.3).**
   - Forests are composites of halvings, and a forest up to target structure is the same as
     its partition of the domain.
   - Refinement is `≤`. Two forest partitions have a common refinement that is again a forest
     partition: restrict one tree's halvings to each piece of the other. The restriction of a
     halving direction to a piece is either constant or a halving of the piece.
   - `f γ = γ' f'`: pull the halvings back along `γ`. Restrictions of `γ` to pieces, read
     through identifications, are affine with linear part in `GL(Z[1/2])` preserving the
     lattice, hence lie in `Γ_X`.
2. **Poset `P_1` and directedness (BZ22 Lemmas 5.1, 2.2, Prop 5.2).** Antisymmetry and
   transitivity follow from 1 verbatim. Every `h ∈ 𝒲` is `f_2^{-1} t f_1`: refine the domain to
   cosets of a common lattice `L_0` on which `h` is affine, then refine the codomain to a lattice
   `L_1` inside every image lattice, and pull back. So `P_1` is directed and `|P_1|` is contractible.
3. **Elementary objects (BZ22 §5).**
   - A piece is *elementary* if it is a coset of some `L ⊇ 2X`.
   - An *elementary forest* is a forest all of whose leaves are elementary.
   - Common refinements of elementary forests are elementary, since the lattices intersect in
     some `L ⊇ 2X`.
   - This gives elementary cores (Prop 5.5) and the Stein complex `X`, contractible (Prop 5.6)
     by the same Quillen argument.
4. **Stabilizers (BZ22 Lemma 6.3, Z24 Citation 4.8).**
   - The stabilizer of a vertex of rank `m` is `Γ_X ≀ S_m`, finitely presented by
     `integral-affine-structure-group-is-finitely-presented`.
   - A short edge splits one copy once along a direction `χ`. Its stabilizer is commensurable
     with `Stab_{Γ_X}(χ) × Γ_X ≀ S_{m−1}`, which is finitely generated by
     `integral-affine-halving-stabilizers-are-finitely-generated`.
5. **k-elementary subcomplexes and cocompactness (Z24 Def 4.3, Lemma 4.5).**
   - The *spectrum* of an elementary tree is the smallest subspace `V` of `F_2^(D)` (directions)
     such that every leaf is a union of cosets of `V^⊥ = ⋂_{χ∈V} ker χ`.
   - A forest is `k`-elementary if every tree has `dim V ≤ k`.
   - The join of `l` simple halvings has every spectrum of dimension `≤ l`, which is what
     Z24 Prop 4.4 uses.
   - `Γ_X` has finitely many orbits on subspaces of each dimension, since finitary
     `GL(F_2; D)` is transitive on them (`integral-affine-splitting-types-have-finitely-many-tuple-orbits`),
     and a fixed `V` has finitely many coarsenings of its coset partition. So `X_m(2)` is
     `W`-cocompact.
6. **Long edges (Z24 Lemma 4.9, Prop 3.1).** Closed intervals `[v,w]` are finite: they are
   intermediate partitions. The fixer of a short-edge path in `[v,w]` has finite index in the
   stabilizer of `v < w`. Proposition 3.1 is a statement about any cocompact action on a
   simply connected complex, so it applies verbatim.
7. **Descending links (BZ22 §7).**
   - `VE_m` (merges by single halvings) is `(ν(m) − 1)`-connected by the matching-complex
     argument of Lemma 7.2, which never uses the color set.
   - The Morse function `μ`, the merge link (Lemma 7.4) and the first split-link case
     (Lemma 7.5, pruning) transfer verbatim.
   - **The only change, in Lemma 7.6.**
     - For a fat root `R_i` with tree `f_i`, let `U_i` be the directions constant on every
       leaf of `f_i`, a subspace. The atoms below `f_i` are the halvings along `χ ∈ U_i ∖ 0`.
     - A set of atoms has join equal to `[f]` iff every `f_i` is *full* (its leaves are exactly
       the cosets of `U_i^⊥`) and the atoms span each `U_i`.
     - If some `f_i` is not full, the nerve is a simplex. Otherwise the nerve is
       `⋃_i Δ_1 ∗ … ∗ K(U_i) ∗ … ∗ Δ_q ≃ Σ^{q−1}(∗_i K(U_i))`, which is
       `(Σ_i dim U_i − 3)`-connected by `non-spanning-subsets-of-an-f2-space-are-highly-connected`.
     - A full `f_i` has `2^{dim U_i}` leaves, so `log_2(m − μ_1) ≤ Σ dim U_i`, exactly as in BZ22.
   - Corollary 7.8 and Proposition 7.9 then give descending links of connectivity
     `≥ min(ν(m/2) − 2, log_2(m/2) − 2)`, so `X_m` is simply connected for large `m`.
8. **Conclusion (Z24 Prop 4.4 and proof of Theorem A).**
   - `X_m(2)` is simply connected for large `m`.
   - `W` acts cocompactly on it (5), with finitely presented vertex stabilizers and finitely
     generated short-edge stabilizers (4), and long edges are handled by (6).
   - So `W` is finitely presented. ∎

## Consistency check

Zaremsky's necessary condition comes from a quasi-retraction `SV_G → Z ≀_S G`. Here the
germ at `0` of the linear part lands in `GL(Z[1/2]; D) ⋊ F`, which is finitely presented by
the same orbit-counting argument. So the analogous obstruction does not bite. Unlike
`E(Z)`'s action on vectors, the combinatorics of halvings is `F_2`-linear and oligomorphic.
