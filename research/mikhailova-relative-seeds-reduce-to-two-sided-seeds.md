`mikhailova-relative-seeds-two-sided-proof`. Part 4 is analysis, not a theorem.

## Setting

- `G = ⟨S | R⟩` is finitely presented, `F` is free on `S`, and `π : F -> G`. `M_G = {(u, v) : πu = πv} ≤ F × F`
  is the Mihailova subgroup, generated by the `(s, s)` and the `(r, 1)`.
- Membership in `M_G` is `WP(G)`: `(w, 1) ∈ M_G` iff `w =_G 1`.
- A *two-sided seed* on `G` is a relative seed for `(G × G, Δ_G)`, that is, (RS1) of
  `relative-seeds-glue-graphs-of-groups-into-class-c`.
- *Local forcing* is as in `quantum-rigidity-is-decided-on-the-derived-subshift`. The *forcing-radius profile*
  `R(n)` is the least `m` such that the forcing closure, run with windows inside `B_m`, already contains `B_n`.

## Statement

1. **Coset structure.** `(F × F)/M_G ≅ G`, with `(g_1, g_2).c = π(g_1) c π(g_2)^(-1)`.
   - A configuration with stabilizer `M_G` has the form `y(u, v) = Y(π(u)^(-1) π(v))` for a unique `Y : G -> Σ`.
   - Moving along the first coordinate multiplies the argument of `Y` on the left; moving along the second
     multiplies it on the right.
   - So a Mihailova seed is a coding of `G` that is checkable from **both** sides.
2. **Pullback.** A locally forced two-sided seed `(Z, z_*)` on `G` pulls back to a locally forced (RS1) relative
   seed for `(F × F, M_G)`, namely `x̂ = z_* ∘ π`. With descent (bh-free-56), locally forced two-sided seeds on `G`
   and locally forced Mihailova seeds exist together, up to (RS2) and (RS3) (see Scope). So the Mihailova detour adds
   no construction: the object is the two-sided seed.
3. **Evaluation bound.** If `(F × F, M_G)` has a locally forced (RS1) relative seed with forcing-radius profile
   `R(n)`, then `WP(G) ∈ DTIME(2^(O(R(n + c))))`, with no oracle.
   - So if `WP(G) ∉ DTIME(2^(O(f(n))))`, then `R(n) ≥ f(n) − c` infinitely often.
   - Hard inputs need forcing that travels superlinearly far from the ball it decides, as a van Kampen diagram
     does. Forcing length, the number of steps, is irrelevant, which matches the Baumslag–Gersten rung.
4. **Collapse (analysis).**
   - **Reduction.** By `quantum-rigidity-is-decided-on-the-derived-subshift`, rigidity of a locally forced
     Mihailova seed is decided on its seedless limits. These are the limits of the two-sided neighbourhood
     patterns `{Y(a c b) : |a|, |b| ≤ D}` at far elements `c ∈ G`.
   - **No conflict with hardness.** The forcing radius `R(n)` is a property of the seed orbit, which is classical.
     So hardness does not by itself prevent collapse.
   - **Calibrations (sketch, not proved here).** For `G = Z^d`, the limits collapse to one sign per coordinate.
     For `G = F_k`, they collapse to a pair of ends.
   - **The live question.** Is there a hard `G` with a locally forced two-sided coding whose far two-sided
     neighbourhoods are classified by tree data plus finitely many global parameters?

## Scope

- **(RS2) is not claimed for the pullback SFT.** It may contain configurations that are locally but not
  globally pulled back (not `N × N`-invariant), and those are not limits of translates of `x̂`.
- The orbit closure of `x̂` consists of pullbacks of points of `Z`. Whether it is of finite type is open.

## Lesson for general BH

**Mihailova relocates, and splits the bill.**
- On the construction side, the Mihailova route is the two-sided seed on `G`, nothing more. Seeding
  `M_G ≤ F × F` means coding `G` so that it is checkable under left and right multiplication at once.
- On the cost side:
  - the word problem of `G` is paid by the **forcing radius**, which is at least `log` of `WP(G)`'s time;
  - rigidity is paid by the **far two-sided neighbourhoods**, which never see that radius.
- So ¬CAP through Mihailova needs a coding whose deductions reach superlinearly far, through van Kampen diagrams,
  while its asymptotic two-sided patterns collapse.
