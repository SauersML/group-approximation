## Item 1: membership

Put `b = a^t = t^{-1} a t` and `B = <a, b | b^{-1} a b = a^n> = BS(1,n)`. The relator of `G(1,n)` is
`b^{-1} a b a^{-n}`. So `G(1,n)` is the HNN extension of `B` with stable letter `t` and `θ: A = <a> -> C = <b>`,
`a ↦ b`. This is the Magnus–Moldavanskii rewriting over the generator `t`, which has exponent sum zero. Here
`A` and `C` are infinite cyclic Magnus subgroups of `B`.

- **`B ∈ 𝓗`.** `B = Z[1/n] ⋊ <b>`, where `b` acts by multiplication by `n`. This is locally indicable (an
  extension of torsion-free abelian groups) and solvable, so `B ∈ 𝓗` by item 1 of
  [[vertex-injective-quotients-give-hughes-free-division-rings]].
- **Retraction.** `a ↦ 1`, `b ↦ b` respects the relation, since `b^{-1} b = 1`. So `B` retracts onto `C`.

By item 6 of that node (the retract-HNN case), `G(1,n) ∈ 𝓗`. So every crossed product over `G(1,n)` has a
Hughes-free division ring.

## Item 2: Lewin–Lewin, q:HF and compatibility

`G(1,n)` is a torsion-free one-relator group. An HNN extension of the torsion-free group `B` is torsion-free.
So, by Karrass–Magnus–Solitar, its relator is not a proper power. By item 1, `k[G(1,n)] ⊆ D_{k G(1,n)}`. FSP Theorem 7.8 (item 4 of
[[hughes-extension-lemma-and-fsp-universal-field-imports]]) says the Lewin–Lewin ring is `D_{kG}`. The q:HF and
compatibility statements are item 7 of [[vertex-injective-quotients-give-hughes-free-division-rings]].

## Item 3: `BG` lies outside the known classes

Take `n = 2`, and let `α` and `β` be the images of `a` and `b` in a finite quotient.

- **Not residually finite (Baumslag's argument).** Let `o = ord(α)`. Since `β = α^t` is conjugate to `α`,
  `ord(β) = o`. From `β^{-1} α β = α^2`, induction gives `β^{-j} α β^j = α^{2^j}`, so `α = α^{2^o}` and
  `2^o ≡ 1 mod o`. In particular `o` is odd.

  Suppose `o > 1`, and let `p` be its least prime factor. The order of `2` modulo `p` divides `o`, because
  `2^o ≡ 1 mod p`. It also divides `p - 1`. Every prime factor of `o` is at least `p`, so `gcd(o, p-1) = 1`.
  Hence `2 ≡ 1 mod p`, which is impossible. So `α = 1` in every finite quotient, while `a ≠ 1` in `BG`, since
  `B` embeds in the HNN extension.
- **Primitivity rank 2.** The relator `w` is not a proper power (item 2), so `π(w) ≠ 1`. If `π(w) = ∞`, then `w` is
  primitive and `BG` is free. If `π(w) ≥ 3`, then `BG` has negative immersions and is virtually compact special
  (Louder–Wilton, Linton). Both would make `BG` residually finite. This is the case analysis in the proof of
  [[nonsofic-one-relator-relator-has-primitivity-rank-two]]. So `π(w) = 2`.
- **`N` is perfect.** The kernel `N` of `t ↦ 1, a ↦ 0` is generated by `a_i = t^{-i} a t^i`, with relations
  `a_{i+1}^{-1} a_i a_{i+1} = a_i^2`. These say `a_i = [a_i, a_{i+1}]`.

  If every `a_j` lies in `N^{(m)}`, then every `a_i` lies in `N^{(m+1)}`. So every `a_i` lies in every derived
  subgroup, and `N` is perfect. Consequently:
  - every homomorphism from `BG` to a solvable group kills `N`, and has cyclic image;
  - the lower central series satisfies `γ_2(BG) = N = γ_m(BG)` for all `m ≥ 2`, so `BG` is not residually
    nilpotent, and in particular not residually torsion-free nilpotent.

  This is the obstruction noted in the class theorem: no single solvable target is vertex-injective on the whole
  line, and only its finite segments map into solvable groups.
- **Not amenable.** `A` and `C` are proper in `B`, so the HNN extension is non-ascending and contains `F_2`.
- **No finite-index free-by-cyclic subgroup.** Suppose `H ≤ BG` has finite index and `H = F ⋊ Z` with `F` free
  of any rank. Then `S = H ∩ B` has finite index in `B = BS(1,2)`, so it is solvable and not polycyclic
  (`S ∩ Z[1/2]` has finite index in `Z[1/2]`, which is not finitely generated).

  But `S ∩ F` is free and solvable, so it is trivial or `Z`. Then `S` is an extension of a cyclic group by a
  subgroup of `Z`, which is polycyclic. This is a contradiction.
- **Consequences.** Virtually compact special groups, finitely generated 3-manifold groups and virtually
  (finitely generated free)-by-cyclic groups are residually finite. So `BG` is none of these. It is not
  amenable, not RTFN, and not virtually free-by-cyclic in any rank.

## Item 4: examples that are not bi-orderable

`B' = BS(1,2) × Kl` is locally indicable and amenable, since it is poly-(torsion-free abelian). So `B' ∈ 𝓗`.
Killing `a` and `Kl` retracts `B'` onto `<b>`. The retract-HNN case gives `G_Kl ∈ 𝓗`.

- **It contains `BG`.** The associated subgroups `<a>` and `<b>` lie in `BS(1,2) ≤ B'`. By Britton's lemma, the
  HNN extension of `BS(1,2)` along the same `θ`, which is `BG`, embeds in `G_Kl`.
- **It is not bi-orderable.** It contains `Kl`, where `x` is conjugate to `x^{-1}`. That is impossible in a
  bi-ordered group.
- **It is not residually finite,** since it contains `BG`.

`BG * Kl ∈ 𝓗` follows from item 4 of the class theorem, given `BG ∈ 𝓗` and `Kl ∈ 𝓗`.

## Item 5: retractive Magnus hierarchies

`G = B *_θ` is the Magnus–Moldavanskii splitting. `B` is one-relator on the rewritten generators
`x_{j}`, for indices in `[m_x, M_x]`. `A` is generated by the `x_j` with `j < M_x`, and `C` by those with
`j > m_x`. Both are free on these generators by the Freiheitssatz. The retract-HNN case of the class theorem
applies directly.

**The retraction criterion.** A retraction `ρ: B -> C` is the identity on the free generators of `C`, so it is
determined by the images `u_x` of the generators `x_{m_x}` that are not in `C`. It is a homomorphism exactly when
the rewritten relator becomes `1` in `F(C)` after `x_{m_x} ↦ u_x`. Conversely, any such substitution defines a
retraction, since `C ≤ B` is free on those generators. The case of `A` is symmetric. □
