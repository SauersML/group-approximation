**ESTABLISHED.**  Put

```text
A=rho(x_13(1)),             k=rho(x_31(1)),
e=(1-A)/2,                  K=e k e.                     (JOP1)
```

The two root involutions generate a quotient of

```text
<A,k | A^2=k^2=(Ak)^3=1> = S_3.                         (JOP2)
```

In every finite- or infinite-dimensional unitary representation, the
compression `K` obeys in the `e` corner

```text
(K+e)(K-(1/2)e)=0,
K^2+(1/2)K-(1/2)e=0.                                   (JOP3)
```

Consequently `K` is invertible on `e`, its singular values belong to
`{1,1/2}`, and its polar unitary is the fixed polynomial

```text
polar(K)=(4K+e)/3.                                      (JOP4)
```

On the positive-tail part of the negative Jacobson head one has

```text
x_13(Q)=-1,   x_13(ST)=+1  ==>  A=x_13(1)=-1,           (JOP5)
```

so this is the natural constant opposite-root correction available after
the degree-two fold of
`binary-jacobson-tail-phase-cannot-correct-closing-fold`.

However `(JOP4)` lies entirely in the finite constant subgroup generated by
`x_13(1),x_31(1)`, and hence in the finite constant/`Q` envelope retaining
the head.  It supplies no new occurrence relation with the raw range cut
of `x_13(SQ)`.  In particular the first load-bearing use of `x_31(1)` must
compress it jointly with that raw cut (or an equivalent S/T-bearing
occurrence); its constant `S_3` polar cannot authenticate the literal
degree-four target by itself.
