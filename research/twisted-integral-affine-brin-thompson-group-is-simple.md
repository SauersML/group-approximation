**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed). It uses the Bleak–Elliott–Hyde criterion, as quoted
in Belk–Zaremsky arXiv:2001.04579 p. 13 (read from source): a group of homeomorphisms of a
Cantor space is simple if it is (i) vigorous, (ii) generated by elements of small support,
and (iii) perfect.

`W` is as in `twisted-integral-affine-full-group-is-finitely-presented`. It contains the untwisted
`DV` and the structure group `Γ_X`.

- **(i) Vigorous.** `DV ≤ W` is vigorous: for clopen `A` and proper clopen `B, C ⊂ A`, some
  element supported on `A` maps `B` into `C`. Vigor passes to overgroups.
- **(ii) Small support.** Every `h ∈ W` is `f_2^{-1} t f_1` (step 2 of
  `twisted-integral-affine-group-fp-via-stein-complex`).
  - A halving along any direction is a coordinate halving composed with a structure map on
    the copy. So `h` is a product of `DV` elements (generated by small-support elements) and
    structure maps `γ ∈ Γ_X` acting on one copy.
  - A global `γ ∈ Γ_X` fixes some coordinate `d_1` near `0`, outside its finite linear and
    translation support. The `F`-part of `γ` is the identity near `0`. So `γ` commutes with the
    `d_1`-halving and restricts to `γ` on each half: `γ = (γ on H_0)(γ on H_1)`, a product of
    two small-support elements.
- **(iii) Perfect.** `DV` is simple (Brin; Belk–Zaremsky), so `DV ≤ [W, W]`. For `γ ∈ Γ_X` and
  a proper brick `B`, let `c(γ) ∈ W^ab` be the class of "`γ` on `B`". It does not depend on `B`,
  since `DV` moves proper bricks to proper bricks by canonical maps. Splitting `B` along a
  coordinate `d_1` untouched by `γ` gives `c(γ) = 2c(γ)`, so `c(γ) = 0`. By (ii), `W` is generated
  by `DV` and such elements, so `W^ab = 0`. ∎
