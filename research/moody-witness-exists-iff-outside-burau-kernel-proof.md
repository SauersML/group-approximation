**(2) ⇒ (3).** Take `k = 1`.

**(3) ⇒ (1).**
- By `moody-polynomial-change-certifies-burau-nonkernel` with `n = 5`, `f(Φ_0) ∉ ker ρ_5`.
- By `burau-kernel-restricts-along-strand-inclusion`, `Φ_0 ∉ ker ρ_4`.
- `ker ρ_4` is a normal subgroup, so `Φ^k ∉ ker ρ_4`, and hence `Φ ∉ ker ρ_4`.

**(1) ⇒ (2).** This is the new direction. Assume that (2) fails.

1. **Linear form of the witness difference (§3–§5).**
   - Write `H = Z[t^{±1}]^5` with the Fox basis `e_j = [x̃_j]` (§4.2). Mapping classes act by
     rows, `h̃_*(v) = v J(h)`, with `J_{h∘g} = J_g J_h`.
   - The intersection form `I` is `Z[t^{±1}]`-linear (§2).
   - By Lemma 3.1, `(1 − t^s) 𝕄_Γ = I([c̃_{(β_*^3)Γ}])`.
   - In the standard picture, `c_β = x_3`, so `[c̃_β] = e_3 =: r`. Also `I(v) = v·c` with
     `c = ε(1, −t^{±1}, 0, 0, 0)^T`: the segment `α` meets only the circles of `x_1` and `x_2`, once
     each (§10.3).
   - By naturality (§5, proof of Theorem A), `[c̃_{(β)ΨΓ}]` is `r J(Ψ) J(Γ)` or `r J(Γ) J(Ψ)`,
     depending on the composition order.
   - So `(1 − t^s)(𝕄_{ΨΓ} − 𝕄_Γ)` equals `r (J(Ψ) − I) J(Γ) c` (order 1) or
     `r J(Γ)(J(Ψ) − I) c` (order 2).
   - `Z[t^{±1}]` is a domain and `1 − t^s ≠ 0`. So `𝕄_{ΨΓ} = 𝕄_Γ` exactly when this bilinear
     expression vanishes.
2. **Conventions (§4.3–4.4).** As `g` runs over a subgroup `S ∈ {f(B_4), K_5}`, the set
   `{J(g)}` is one of two sets, possibly with `t ↦ t^{-1}` throughout:
   - `ρ_5(S)`, from the words `w` and `mirror w`;
   - `D^{-1} ρ_5(S)^T D`, from the words `rev w` and `rev mirror w`.

   Both `f(B_4)` and `K_5` are closed under `mirror`: `mirror` preserves `P_5` and commutes with
   deleting `p_5`. Also `t ↦ t^{-1}` is a field automorphism of `Q(t)`, so it preserves every rank
   statement below.
3. **Spanning lemmas** (certificate `spanning.py`, log `spanning.log`, all 80 variants of sign,
   exponent `v = ±t^e` for `e ∈ [−2, 2]`, kind `rho`/`rev`, and specialization `t_0`, for
   `n = 3, 4, 5`). Put `Fix_col` and `Fix_row` for the common fixed columns and rows of `J(f(B_4))`:
   - `e_5` on both sides;
   - the all-ones column, or the row `(1, t, ..., t^4)`, depending on the variant.

   These vectors are fixed by hand. Each block `[[1−t, t],[1, 0]]` has row sums `1`, `(1, t)` is
   a fixed row of it, and `f(B_4)` does not touch coordinate `5`. Then, over `Q(t)`:
   - (a) `span{J(Γ)c : Γ ∈ K_5} + Fix_col = Q(t)^5`;
   - (b) `span{r J(y) : y ∈ f(B_4)} + Fix_row = Q(t)^5`;
   - (c) `span{r J(Γ) : Γ ∈ K_5} + Fix_row = Q(t)^5`;
   - (d) `span{J(y)c : y ∈ f(B_4)} + Fix_col = Q(t)^5`.

   Why the certificate is valid:
   - The span of an orbit of the group generated by `g_1, ..., g_m` is the smallest subspace
     containing the start vector and invariant under the `g_i`, since invariance under `g_i`
     implies invariance under `g_i^{-1}` in finite dimension. `spanning.py` computes that closure,
     using the generators `A_{j5}` of the free group `K_5` and `σ_1, σ_2, σ_3` of `f(B_4)`.
   - Orbit vectors have Laurent polynomial entries. If `5` of them specialize at `t_0 ≠ 0` to
     linearly independent vectors, the corresponding `5 × 5` minor is a nonzero rational function.
     So full rank at one rational `t_0` certifies full rank over `Q(t)`.
4. **Conclusion.** Put `M = J(f(Φ)) − I`. Every conjugate satisfies
   `J(f(yΦy^{-1})) − I = J(y')^{-1} M J(y')` for some `y' ∈ f(B_4)`, and `y'` runs over all of
   `f(B_4)` as `y` does. Also `M u = 0` for `u ∈ Fix_col` and `u M = 0` for `u ∈ Fix_row`, because
   `J(f(Φ))` fixes them.
   - **Order 1.** The expression `r J(y')^{-1} M J(y') J(Γ) c` is `0` for all `y'` and `Γ`.
     - `J(y')` preserves `Fix_col`, and `M` kills `Fix_col`. So by (a),
       `r J(y')^{-1} M J(y') = 0`, and hence `r J(y')^{-1} M = 0` for all `y'`.
     - `Fix_row · M = 0`, so by (b), `M = 0`.
   - **Order 2.** The expression `r J(Γ) J(y')^{-1} M J(y') c` is `0` for all `Γ` and `y'`.
     - Fixed rows satisfy `u J(y')^{-1} M = u M = 0`. So by (c), `J(y')^{-1} M J(y') c = 0`, that
       is `M J(y') c = 0` for all `y'`.
     - By (d), `M = 0`.

   So `J(f(Φ)) = I`. `J(f(Φ))` is `ρ_5(f(w')) = ρ_4(w') ⊕ 1`, possibly with `t ↦ t^{-1}`, for some
   `w' ∈ {w, rev w, mirror w, rev mirror w}` (§4.3, and `burau-kernel-restricts-along-strand-inclusion`
   for the block form). By §4.4, `ρ_4(w') = I` implies `ρ_4(Φ) = I`, contradicting (1). ∎
