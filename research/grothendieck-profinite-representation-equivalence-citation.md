**Established by citation.**  Both statements are imported as published.
Grothendieck's equivalence is the theorem his 1970 paper proves and which
Bridson--Grunewald recall in their introduction; the Grothendieck pair
construction is the main theorem of the Annals paper.

Neither internal argument is re-derived here.  The import is deliberately
narrow: what the region downstream needs is (a) the *existence* of the
classical phenomenon, to position an analogue correctly, and (b) the
equivalence of the profinite and representation-categorical formulations, which
is what makes "invisible to finite quotients" a statement about representation
theory rather than only about finite images.

The Jaikin-Zapirain--Lubotzky attribution recorded in the target is explicitly
NOT imported; it is carried as an unverified scope note so that no downstream
node leans on it.
