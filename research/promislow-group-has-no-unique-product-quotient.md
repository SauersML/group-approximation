Let

```text
P = <a, b | b^(-1) a^2 b = a^(-2), a^(-1) b^2 a = b^(-2)>
```

be the Promislow group. Every homomorphism from `P` to a group with unique
products is trivial. So `P` is a finitely generated torsion-free group with no
nontrivial unique-product quotient, and it lies in the class of
`integral-units-trivial-on-groups-without-up-quotients`.

In particular, `P` has no nontrivial left-orderable quotient, and every
homomorphism from `P` to a torsion-free nilpotent group or to a free group is
trivial.

The proof uses two facts from `unit-conjecture-fails-over-complex-numbers`:
`P` is torsion-free, and `C[P]` has a nontrivial unit. Everything else is
derived from the two relators (route
`promislow-no-up-quotient-from-complex-units`).
