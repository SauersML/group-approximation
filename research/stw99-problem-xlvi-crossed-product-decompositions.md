**Problem XLVI of Schafhauser--Tikuisis--White, arXiv:2506.10902.**
When is a classifiable C\*-algebra of the form `A ⋊ G` for an outer
amenable action of a NONTRIVIAL countable discrete group on a
classifiable algebra?

Known: stable UCT Kirchberg algebras are `Z`-crossed products of
stable AT algebras of real rank zero (Rordam's book, Prop 4.3.3; AF
base when `K_1` is torsion-free); `Z ⊗ K ≅ B ⋊ Z` (Elliott--Sato--
Thomsen), hence every STABLE classifiable `A` has
`A ⊗ K ≅ (A ⊗ B) ⋊ Z`; Jacelon: `KK`-contractible stably finite
classifiable algebras with compact trace space are `Z`-crossed
products.

## Attempts

* The stable case is CLOSED by the tensor trick, so the problem lives
  entirely in the unital case, where `[1_A]` bookkeeping bites exactly
  as in XLV.  Sharp open cell after Jacelon: unital, nontrivial
  `K`-theory, `G = Z` — the first test family is unital UHF-absorbing
  algebras with `K_0 = Z[1/n]`, where a positive answer needs an
  outer `Z`-action on a classifiable base with prescribed
  Rokhlin-type `K`-data and a negative answer needs a new unital
  obstruction beyond Pimsner--Voiculescu (none known once the base is
  allowed to vary).
