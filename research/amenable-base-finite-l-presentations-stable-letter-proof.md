Let

```text
B = < X | Q >
```

be amenable. For finite sets `R subset F(X)` and
`Sigma subset End(F(X))`, the associated finite L-presentation presents

```text
L = F(X) / << Q union { sigma(r) : sigma in Sigma*, r in R } >>.
```

The canonical map from `B=F(X)/<<Q>>` onto `L` is surjective. Hence
`L` is amenable, independently of the substitution orbits.

Now consider the raw stable-letter simulator

```text
T = < X,t_1,...,t_s |
      Q,R, t_i^-1 x t_i = sigma_i(x)
      (x in X, 1 <= i <= s) >.
```

If `s=0`, then `T` is a quotient of `B`, hence amenable.

If `s=1`, let `A` be the image of `B` in `T`. It is amenable and the
defining relations imply `t^-1 A t <= A`. Therefore

```text
N = union_{n>=0} t^n A t^-n
```

is an increasing directed union of amenable groups. It is normal: conjugation
by `t` shifts the union forward, while conjugation by `t^-1` contributes
`t^-1 A t <= A` and shifts every other term backward. The quotient `T/N`
is generated by the image of `t`, so it is cyclic. Thus `T` is amenable.

If `s>=2`, killing every generator in `X` defines an epimorphism

```text
T -> F(t_1,...,t_s).
```

All relators from `Q` and `R` vanish, and every conjugation relator becomes
trivial. Since the free group of rank at least two is nonamenable and
amenability passes to quotients, `T` is nonamenable.

Consequently `T` is amenable exactly when `s<=1`; this criterion is read
directly from the finite presentation data. The L-presented groups above are
always amenable. Neither family can encode the nonrecursive totality set. QED
