**ESTABLISHED INTERNAL ODD-PARITY FENCE.**  On the common active carrier
`F` of `same-center-prefix-whiteheads-are-uncontrolled-adjacent-swaps`, let
`E` be the extraspecial group generated by the three same-center Pauli pairs
and their common central involution.  The two literal native Whiteheads

```text
J_1=W_1,                 J_2=W_2                       (SQS1)
```

normalize `E` by the two adjacent transpositions of its three Pauli pairs.
Consequently the full fixed packet actor group has a quotient

```text
<E,J_1,J_2> -> S_3 -> {+1,-1},
E |-> +1,              J_1,J_2 |-> -1.                (SQS2)
```

Every ordinary group relation which remains inside this actor group has
even total Whitehead parity.  In particular, no word involving one
individual `J_i` oddly and only same-center Pauli actors can be the identity.
This is stronger than an audit of a chosen relator list: it follows from an
actual quotient of the whole packet group.

The quotient gives an exact finite operator-norm fence.  On

```text
H_0=(C^2)^(tensor 3)
```

use the standard Pauli representation for `E` and the canonical adjacent
swaps `S_12,S_23`.  Tensoring this representation by the sign character is
equivalently

```text
J_1'= -S_12,             J_2'= -S_23,                 (SQS3)
```

with every Pauli actor unchanged.  All Pauli relations, both Whitehead
involution laws, every covariance row, and the Coxeter braid remain exact.
For example, both sides of the braid acquire the same scalar `(-1)^3`.
Yet

```text
||J_i'-J_i||=2                                           (SQS4)
```

on the whole carrier.  Arbitrary amplification preserves this gap and the
active marked/central spectral projections, so the fence is uniform over
operator-norm active profiles and does not use a canonical trace.

The known order-seven words do not contradict `(SQS2)`.  Their Hecke
factors use the center-chain actors

```text

B_3A_2=x_95(b_3)x_58(a_2),                              (SQS5)
```

not the same-center Pauli arms.  These actors eject the middle-index packet
into the nonzero roots `x_43(q),x_12(q),x_53(q),x_14(q),...` computed in
`middle-index-heisenberg-packet-is-not-center-chain-invariant`.  Thus the
odd order-seven anchors escape the sign quotient precisely by leaving the
carrier on which the unequal selector comparison is supposed to occur.

On a genuinely reducing occurrence packet the seventh-power relation is
useful: it locks the Whitehead gauge to the Hecke-return gauge.  On an
arbitrary active same-center carrier, however, obtaining that conclusion
requires paying every ejected return boundary.  The exact zero-cut and
moving-stage fences show that this is not supplied by the fixed packet or
by one-step compression.  Hence the smallest presently available odd
anchor does not yet yield a finite-coordinate estimate toward `(SNA2)`;
the surviving input is still a coefficient-sensitive moving return, not an
additional same-center relation.

No Property `(T)`, Kazhdan input, stability theorem, canonical trace, or
literature result is used.
