# Pull the Kazhdan spectator through the terminal amalgam

## 1. The edge is genuinely Kazhdan

Let \(P\) and \(c\) be as in the claim. The one-copy terminal theorem gives
\[
 c\in Z(P),\qquad c^2=1,\qquad c\ne1.                    \tag{1}
\]
Take
\[
 T=SL_3(\mathbb Z),\qquad H=T\times\langle c\rangle.
\]
The group \(T\) has property \((T)\), and property \((T)\) is preserved
under direct products with finite groups. Hence \(H\) has property
\((T)\).

Let \(\alpha\) be the Q8 automorphism interchanging \(x,y\) and fixing
\(z\). The homomorphism
\[
 H\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(K)
\]
is trivial on \(T\) and sends \(c\) to \(\alpha\). It is well defined
because \(c^2=1\) and \(\alpha^2=1\).

In
\[
 B=K\rtimes H,
\]
the subgroup generated by \(H\) and \(z\) is
\[
 C_B=H\times\langle z\rangle.
\]
In
\[
 D=(T\times P)\times\langle z\rangle,
\]
the same abstract group embeds as
\[
 C_D=(T\times\langle c\rangle)\times\langle z\rangle.
\]
The copy of \(c\) is nontrivial by (1), so both edge maps are injective.
Their common edge is
\[
 C\cong SL_3(\mathbb Z)\times C_2\times C_2,             \tag{2}
\]
which is nonamenable and has property \((T)\).

All groups in the construction are finitely presented. If desired, an
explicit presentation of \(\Omega\) is obtained by adjoining to finite
presentations of \(T\) and \(P\) the three Q8 generators \(x,y,z\), making
\(T\) commute with \(x,y,z\), imposing
\[
 cxc^{-1}=y,\qquad cyc^{-1}=x,
\]
and making \(z\) commute with the generators of \(P\). This is exactly the
standard presentation of the amalgam (KES1).

## 2. The new sign survives

The normal-form theorem for amalgamated free products embeds both \(B\)
and \(D\) into
\[
 \Omega=B*_C D.
\]
In particular the subgroup \(K\le B\) embeds. Hence its central involution
\(z\) remains nontrivial.

The element \(z\) is central in \(B\): it is central in \(K\) and fixed by
the \(H\)-action. It is central in \(D\), and the two copies are identified
in the edge. Since \(B,D\) generate \(\Omega\),
\[
 z\in Z(\Omega),\qquad z\ne1.                            \tag{3}
\]

## 3. Every exact finite-dimensional image kills the new sign

Let
\[
 \rho:\Omega\to U(d)
\]
be a genuine finite-dimensional representation. Its restriction to the
embedded copy of \(P\) is a genuine finite-dimensional representation.
The terminal theorem therefore gives
\[
 \rho(c)=I.                                              \tag{4}
\]

Write
\[
 X=\rho(x),\qquad Y=\rho(y),\qquad Z=\rho(z).
\]
Apply \(\rho\) to \(cxc^{-1}=y\). Equation (4) gives \(X=Y\). The Q8
relation \(yx=zxy\) then becomes
\[
 X^2=ZX^2,
\]
and unitarity gives
\[
 Z=I.                                                    \tag{5}
\]

Thus the old invisible sign acts as the exact coequalizer which kills the
new sign.

## 4. The Kazhdan edge is a direct-product disguise

Because \(T\) acts trivially on \(K\),
\[
 B\cong T\times(K\rtimes_\alpha\langle c\rangle).        \tag{6}
\]
Also
\[
 D\cong T\times(P\times\langle z\rangle),\qquad
 C\cong T\times(\langle c\rangle\times\langle z\rangle). \tag{7}
\]

For arbitrary inclusions \(C_0\le B_0,D_0\), the universal property gives
\[
 (T\times B_0)*_{T\times C_0}(T\times D_0)
 \cong T\times(B_0*_{C_0}D_0),                           \tag{8}
\]
provided the displayed copies of \(T\) commute with both vertex groups.
The two maps are obtained by including the factors, and they are inverse
on the generating vertex groups.

Apply (8) to (6)--(7). This proves
\[
 \Omega\cong T\times\Omega_0,
\]
where
\[
 \Omega_0=
 (K\rtimes_\alpha\langle c\rangle)
 *_{\langle c\rangle\times\langle z\rangle}
 (P\times\langle z\rangle).                             \tag{9}
\]

The first vertex in (9) is finite. The second is hyperlinear because \(P\)
is hyperlinear, and the edge \(C_2\times C_2\) is finite. Amenable-edge
permanence implies that \(\Omega_0\) is hyperlinear. The group
\(T=SL_3(\mathbb Z)\) is residually finite and therefore hyperlinear, so
\[
 \Omega\text{ is hyperlinear}.                           \tag{10}
\]

Since \(z\ne1\), its canonical group trace is zero. Matrix lifts of a
trace-preserving embedding of \(L(\Omega)\) give almost-representations of
the fixed finite presentation with
\[
 \|Z-I\|_2^2\longrightarrow2.                            \tag{11}
\]

## 5. Architectural conclusion

The displayed Bass--Serre edge (2) is genuinely Kazhdan, and it clears the
two exact gates: the new sign survives in the abstract group and dies in
every exact finite-dimensional representation. Nevertheless the entire
Kazhdan factor acts trivially on the new selector, so (8) pulls it outside
the amalgam and exposes the finite edge in (9).

This rules out a common shortcut: tensoring a terminal invisible-word
construction with a property-\((T)\) group does not turn exact
finite-dimensional collapse into normalized-HS collapse. The Kazhdan packet
must participate nontrivially in the selector relations. For a finite packet
normalized by the pinned Kun--Thom vertex, the constant-root
normal-generation theorem forbids that design; an infinite moving packet
is the remaining structural escape.
