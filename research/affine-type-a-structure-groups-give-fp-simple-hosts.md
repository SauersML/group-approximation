**Definition (affine type (A)).** Fix a prime `ℓ`, an infinite countable set `D`, a subring
`R ⊆ Z_ℓ` and a group `G ≤ Sym(D)`. The pair `(R, G)` is of **affine type (A)** if:
- **(R)** `R` is a finitely presented ring and **ℓ-saturated**: `R ∩ ℓZ_ℓ = ℓR`, equivalently
  `R[1/ℓ] ∩ Z_ℓ = R`. Moreover `R^×` and `K_2(R)` are finitely generated, and `SK_1(R) = 0`.
- **(G)** `G` is finitely presented. It has finitely many orbits on ordered `k`-tuples of distinct
  points for `k ≤ 5`. Pointwise stabilizers of at most three points are finitely generated, and
  `G` is generated by its point stabilizers.

`W(R, G)` is the group of homeomorphisms of `X = Z_ℓ^D` that are piecewise, on cosets of open
subgroups, of the form `x ↦ Mx + b` on finitely many coordinates composed with a permutation of
coordinates by some `g ∈ G`. Here `M` and `b` have entries in `R[1/ℓ]`.

**Theorem.** If `(R, G)` is of affine type (A), then:
1. `W(R, G)` is finitely presented and `W(R, G)^ab` is finite.
2. `D(W(R, G))` is simple and finitely presented.
3. `D(W(R, G))` contains `E(R; D)`, hence `GL_n(R)` for every `n` (via `g ↦ diag(g, g^{-1}) ∈ E_{2n}(R)`,
   Whitehead). It also contains the ℓ-ary Brin–Thompson group `DV_ℓ`.

So every subgroup of every `GL_n(R)`, for all `n` at once, lies in one finitely presented simple
group.

**Where each hypothesis is used** (steps of `twisted-integral-affine-group-fp-via-stein-complex`).
- **Saturation.** It makes the global germs exactly `Γ = (R^(D) ⋊ GL(R; D)) ⋊ G`, the structure
  group. A structure map restricted to a piece and read through the `GL(Z[1/ℓ])` identifications
  has linear part in `GL(R[1/ℓ]) ∩ GL(Z_ℓ)`. Without saturation the structure group is
  `GL(R[1/ℓ] ∩ Z_ℓ)` and is not finitely generated; see `saturated-l-adic-subrings-are-arithmetic`.
- **`R/ℓR = F_ℓ`.** This follows from saturation. The mod-`ℓ` image of `GL(R; D)` contains
  finitary `SL(F_ℓ; D)`, so `Γ` has finitely many orbits on tuples of splitting directions (the
  analogue of the type (A) pair-orbit count).
- **Finite presentation of `Γ`.** It uses (R) and (G) as in
  `s-integral-affine-structure-group-is-finitely-presented`:
  - `St(R; D) ⋊ G` is finitely presented by the ≤5-tuple lemma;
  - `K_2(R)` is finitely generated and central;
  - `SK_1 = 0` and `R^× ≀_D G` is finitely presented (Cornulier);
  - the translations need the stabilizer of a point and of pairs to be finitely generated.
- **Split stabilizers are finitely generated.** The congruence kernel mod `ℓR` is relative
  elementary: `SK_1(R, ℓR)` embeds in `SK_1(R) = 0`, because `K_2(R/ℓR) = K_2(F_ℓ) = 0` in the
  relative exact sequence. Then use relative elementary generation (Vaserstein–Suslin), and
  finiteness of orbits under the stabilizer of three points.
- **Descending links.** These depend only on `F_ℓ`: an `ℓ`-uniform matching complex and
  Solomon–Tits.
- **Finite abelianization.** It needs `G` generated by point stabilizers. Every generator is then
  trivial on some coordinate, and `(ℓ−1)c = 0`.
- **Simplicity.** Matui 1210.5800 Thm 4.16, since the germ groupoid is minimal and purely
  infinite, because `DV_ℓ ≤ W`.

**Instances.** `(Z[1/P], F)` for any finite `P` and any `ℓ ∉ P`, with `F` Thompson's group on
the dyadics. `R^×` and `K_2` are finitely generated, and `SK_1 = 0` by Bass–Milnor–Serre. The
same holds for `O_{K,S}` with a degree-one unramified place `λ | ℓ` outside `S` and all other
places above `ℓ` in `S`. By restriction of scalars this adds nothing beyond `Z[1/P]`.

**Reach.** `saturated-l-adic-subrings-are-arithmetic`: a finitely generated ℓ-saturated subring of
`Z_ℓ` lies in a number field. So affine type (A) hosts reach exactly the S-arithmetic linear class
(`Z[1/P]`-linear groups). Groups such as `GL_n(Z[t])` cannot be reached this way, and need non-affine
local models: Zaremsky's self-similar affine groups, or Leavitt units in positive characteristic.

## Lesson for general BH

Stein–Farley finiteness sees the structure group only through the **finite-field geometry of its
splittings**, meaning the action of `Γ` on configurations of index-`ℓ` subgroups, which factors
through `R/ℓR = F_ℓ`. So a structure group that is hopeless as a permutation group can still yield
a finitely presented simple host. For example, `E(Z)` acting on vectors has `Z`-valued pair
invariants, which no type (A) action can have. It works as soon as its reduction mod `ℓ` is
oligomorphic on split directions, which strong approximation supplies for arithmetic groups.

This is a **linear type (A)** criterion, parallel to Zaremsky's permutational one. The concrete
lesson for BH hosts: put the "hard" group into the structure group and make it act linearly, and
check finiteness on the mod-`ℓ` split geometry, not on points. The limit is sharp. Saturation
forces the coefficient ring to be arithmetic, so the unresolved inputs, positive transcendence
degree and all primes at once, must come from other local models.
