**Setting.**
- A classical base configuration of a mark-type seed space has unterminated components `X` (from
  `modular-machine-mortality-at-infinity-is-uniform`). Each carries one bit.
- Rules between components are forbidden two-site patterns:
  - an *implication* `X → Y` forbids `(1 at a site of X, 0 at a site of Y)` at bounded distance;
  - exact transport is an implication in both directions.
- `≤` is the preorder generated by the implications.
- In a quantum family over the fixed base, let `P_X` be the bit projection of `X`. Exact transport makes
  it constant on `X`.

## Statement

1. **Implications are transitive.** An implication `X → Y` gives `P_X ≤ P_Y`, since
   `P_X(1 − P_Y) = 0`. So `X ≤ Y` gives `P_X ≤ P_Y` at any distance, and then `P_X` and `P_Y` commute.
   Mere proximity gives only `[P_X, P_Y] = 0` for components within `2D`. Commutation is not transitive,
   so proximity chains force nothing at a distance.
2. **Incomparable components can be split.** Let `A`, `C` be `≤`-incomparable. Put `U_A = {X : A ≤ X}`
   and `U_C = {X : C ≤ X}`. Suppose no component of `U_A ∖ U_C` lies within `2D` of a component of
   `U_C ∖ U_A`. Then there is a `D`-family with `P_A P_C ≠ P_C P_A`, so the space is not `D`-quantum
   rigid.
3. **Design consequence.** In a coupling built from transport and implications, rigidity forces the bits
   of any two separated unterminated components into comparison through directed chains, unless their
   exclusive up-sets touch. In the forced point, the bits are the true fates.
   - So the fates of genuine configurations must be **monotone** along the directed chains that the
     coupling uses.
   - The machine must then accept along a threshold in the geometry of the tower, not arbitrarily.
   - Hardness can live in the threshold, which is compatible with the zero-entropy constraint of
     `machine-sheet-rigid-layers-force-zero-entropy-fates`.

## Proof

1. By Q3, the forbidden pattern gives `P_X(1 − P_Y) = 0`, so `P_X = P_X P_Y`. Taking adjoints,
   `P_X = P_Y P_X`. Then `P_X ≤ P_Y`, and comparable projections commute. The relation `≤` on
   projections is transitive.
2. Choose non-commuting projections `P`, `Q`. Set:
   - `P_X = 1` on `U_A ∩ U_C`;
   - `P_X = P` on `U_A ∖ U_C`;
   - `P_X = Q` on `U_C ∖ U_A`;
   - `P_X = 0` elsewhere.

   Base projections are the scalars of the base configuration. Terminated components carry their forced
   scalar bits.
   - **The implications hold.** `U_A` and `U_C` are up-sets. So `X ≤ Y` moves `X` only to the same or a
     higher class, and the values are ordered `0 ≤ P, Q ≤ 1`.
   - **`A` and `C` get `P` and `Q`.** `A ∈ U_A ∖ U_C` and `C ∈ U_C ∖ U_A`, by incomparability.
   - **Q2 holds.** The only non-commuting pairs are `P`-sites against `Q`-sites, which are more than
     `2D` apart by hypothesis. Everything else is a scalar, or two equal projections.
   - **Q3 holds.** The forbidden patterns are implications and pins, respected in each classical
     specialization.
3. Contrapositive of 2. In the forced point, `P_X` is the scalar fate, so every implication used by the
   coupling must hold between the true fates. `∎`

## What this says for gate 4

A coupling that only relates nearby bits cannot rigidify separated `m`-adic tails. Only two couplings
can:
- **Order couplings.** Directed implication chains, in the style of the atom and chain lemmas of
  `relative-seeds-glue-graphs-of-groups-into-class-c` §2, where pointer orientation supplies the order.
  These require monotone fates.
- **Permutive couplings.** Rules that are not two-site implications, e.g. Ledrappier-type sums, where a
  bit is a function of several others.

So there are two candidate designs for the tower's machine:
- a threshold (monotone) acceptance along the tree order;
- a permutive carrier modulated by a reversible machine (`machine-sheet-rigid-layers-force-zero-entropy-fates`).

## Lesson for general BH

Commutation does not propagate, and order does. A rigid seed can couple far-apart computations only
through relations that are transitive in the operator sense:
- implications, which give `≤`;
- genuinely multi-site deterministic rules.

Either the answers are monotone along an order that the geometry supplies, which is the pointer or tree
mechanism that made gluing work, or they are determined by a permutive carrier. Couplings between
neighbours that merely check consistency cannot rigidify computations at infinity.
