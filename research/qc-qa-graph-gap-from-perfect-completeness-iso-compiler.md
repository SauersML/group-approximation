Assume a total computable `M |-> (X_M, Y_M)` with `(IC1)` and `(IC2)` of
`perfect-completeness-isomorphism-compiler`.

**Step 1: `A(Iso(X,Y)) = 0` is recursively enumerable.**
- `A(Iso(X,Y))` is the free unital *-algebra on the `v_(xa)` modulo finitely
  many polynomial relations: projections, row and column sums, and
  `v_(xa) v_(x'a') = 0` for non-matching adjacency.
- It is zero iff `1` lies in the two-sided *-ideal generated by these
  relations.
- Membership is witnessed by a finite combination with noncommutative
  polynomial coefficients.  Such combinations can be enumerated and checked.

**Step 2: the fixed point.**  By Kleene's recursion theorem there is a
machine `M_*` that:
- computes its own pair `(X_(M_*), Y_(M_*))` with the compiler;
- searches for a witness that `1` lies in the defining ideal of
  `A(Iso(X_(M_*), Y_(M_*)))`;
- halts when it finds one.

**Step 3: `M_*` does not halt.**  If it halted, `A(Iso(X_(M_*),Y_(M_*))) = 0`.
This algebra then has no nonzero finite-dimensional representation,
contradicting `(IC1)`.

**Step 4: the pair.**  Since `M_*` does not halt, no witness exists, so
`A(Iso(X_(M_*), Y_(M_*))) != 0`.
- For finite simple graphs, a nonzero isomorphism algebra is equivalent to
  `X ≅_qc Y` (BCEHPSW, arXiv:1812.11474v2, Theorem 4.9, as recorded on the
  target claim).
- `(IC2)` gives `X_(M_*)` not `qa`-isomorphic to `Y_(M_*)`.

So `(X_(M_*), Y_(M_*))` witnesses the target.  Only the weak completeness
`M halts => X_M ≅_qc Y_M` was used in Step 3.  The finite-dimensional
completeness of `(IC1)` is what separates the compiler from the constant
map (see the claim).
