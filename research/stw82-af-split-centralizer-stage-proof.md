Write the unital AF algebra `D` as the directed closure of unital
finite-dimensional subalgebras `D_lambda`, and put

`E_lambda = I + s(D_lambda)`.                                  `(ASP1)`

These are C*-subalgebras with dense directed union in `E`.  Fix one stage
and write

`D_lambda = direct_sum_{j=1}^m M_{r_j}`.                       `(ASP2)`

If `z_j` is the central unit of the `j`th block and `p_j=s(z_j)`, then
`p_1,...,p_m` are central projections in `E_lambda`: they commute with
`s(D_lambda)` by centrality of the `z_j`, and with `I` by `(ASC2)`.  Thus

`E_lambda = direct_sum_j p_j E_lambda`,
`I = direct_sum_j p_j I`.                                      `(ASP3)`

We claim that

`p_j I = 0` whenever `r_j >= 2`.                               `(ASP4)`

Set `J=p_jI` and identify the corresponding section block with `M_{r_j}`.
For `x in J` and `a,b in M_{r_j}`, the element `x s(a)` lies in `I`, so
commutation with `s(b)` gives

`s(b) x s(a) = x s(a) s(b)`.

Commuting `s(b)` past `x` on the left shows

`x s([b,a]) = 0`.                                               `(ASP5)`

The closed ideal generated by commutators in the simple noncommutative
algebra `M_{r_j}` is all of `M_{r_j}`.  Moving arbitrary section factors
past `x` in `(ASP5)` therefore gives `x s(1)=x=0`, proving `(ASP4)`.

For `r_j=1`, on the other hand,

`p_jE_lambda = p_jI + C p_j = unitization(p_jI)`.               `(ASP6)`

Combining `(ASP3)`--`(ASP6)`, invariance under minimal unitization, and
the finite-direct-sum formula gives

`dim_nuc(E_lambda)
 = max({dim_nuc(p_jI): r_j=1} union {0})
 = dim_nuc(I)`.                                                  `(ASP7)`

If `dim_nuc(I)` is finite, local/inductive-limit permanence applied to the
dense union of the `E_lambda` gives `dim_nuc(E)<=dim_nuc(I)`; the reverse
inequality follows from ideal monotonicity.  If `dim_nuc(I)=infinity`, that
monotonicity already forces `dim_nuc(E)=infinity`.  Since an AF quotient
has nuclear dimension zero, `(ASC3)` follows in all cases.

When `D` is commutative, `(ASC2)` automatically makes `s(D)` central in
`E=I+s(D)`, recovering
`stw82-central-af-split-extensions-obey-maximum`.  The matrix-block
annihilation argument is the new ingredient needed for noncommutative AF
quotients.
