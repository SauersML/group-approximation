The CE traces form a weak-star compact set.  The scalar equations

```text
phi(overline(chi(k)) k)=1,       k in K,               (APP1)
```

are weak-star closed faces.  If no finite subset separated them from
`T_CE(G)`, compactness would give a CE trace satisfying all of them,
contrary to `(APA2)`.  Choose a finite separating list
`F={k_1,...,k_m}`.

Let `L=normalClosure_G(F)`.  Conjugation invariance of `chi` shows that

```text
C_chi(L)={(chi(l)^(-1),l):l in L} <= C x G             (APP2)
```

is normal.  The normal closure used in `(APA4)` lies in this graph, and the
graph meets `C x {1}` trivially.  Hence `C` embeds in `H`.  The group `C` is
a finitely generated subgroup of the circle, so it is a finitely generated
abelian group and is finitely presented.  Formula `(APA4)` therefore gives a
finite presentation of `H`.

Let `beta:C->T` be the faithful inclusion character from `(APA3)`.  If `H`
were hyperlinear, `thom-central-corner-criterion` would make the
`beta`-twisted group von Neumann algebra of `H/C` Connes embeddable.  Its
canonical twisted regular representation lifts to a representation of `H`
in which every `c in C` acts as `beta(c)I`.  Pull it back along

```text
G -> H,          g |-> image(1,g).                     (APP3)
```

The relators in `(APA4)` give

```text
k_j=beta(chi(k_j)) I=chi(k_j)I                         (APP4)
```

in this representation.  Its canonical trace is CE, and its restriction to
the von Neumann algebra generated by `G` remains CE.  The pulled-back trace
therefore belongs to the finite face `(APP1)`, contradicting its choice.
Thus `H` is nonhyperlinear.
