Lane proof (bh-g2-abh, 2026-09-18). It is elementary and algebraic, and it has not been reviewed.

## Setting

- **Group and metric.** `Λ = F_n × Z`, with `n >= 1`. `S` is the symmetric free basis, `t` generates `Z`,
  and the word metric is taken for `S ∪ {t^(±1)}`. So `d(w t^i, w' t^(i')) = d_T(w, w') + |i - i'|`, where
  `T` is the tree of `F_n`.
- **The fold.** `X ⊆ A^(Z^2)` is horizontally expansive with thickening 0, and `Z ⊆ (A × S)^Λ` is its ABHT
  path-fold (arXiv:2204.11492v3, §5).
- **Two facts from ABHT (Prop. `configsJuntas` and its proof).**
  - (F1) Every `z ∈ Z` has the form `x ⊗ y`, with `x ∈ X` and `y` in the flow shift, and
    `z(w t^i) = (x(i, h_y(w)), y(w))`. Here `h_y(w) = 2 max{|w'| : w' ⊑ w, w' ⊑ W(y)} - |w|`, and `y` is
    constant on `t`-cosets.
  - (F2) If `y(g) = s`, then `h_y(gs) = h_y(g) + 1`: following the flow raises the height by one.
- **Height of an end.** For an end `ξ`, `h_ξ = -b_ξ` is the negative Busemann function, normalized by
  `h_ξ(1) = 0`. For `y ↔ ξ` this is exactly `h_y`.
- **Row rule.** Row `j` of a point of `X` determines row `j - 1`. That map is continuous and commutes with
  the horizontal shift. So by Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon there are `R` and `φ: A^([-R,R]) → A` with
  `x(i, j-1) = φ(x([i-R, i+R], j))` for all `x ∈ X`.
- **Parameters.** `X` is `D_X`-quantum rigid over `k`. The sup and `ℓ^1` conventions on `Z^2` differ by a
  factor of at most 2. We fix `D >= max(2 D_X, R + 1)`.

Let `E_(a,s)(g)` be a `D`-quantum family for `Z` on a `k`-space `W`, with conditions (Q1)–(Q3) as in
`fp-v-times-subshift-full-groups-force-quantum-rigidity`.
- **Marginals.** `P_s(g) = Σ_a E_(a,s)(g)` and `T_a(g) = Σ_s E_(a,s)(g)`. At each site these are two
  commuting partitions of unity, and `E_(a,s)(g) = T_a(g) P_s(g)`.
- **Ball sums.** Let `π` be a partial pattern on a set inside some `gB_D`, prescribing a letter, a flow, or
  both at each site, such that no point of `Z` realizes `π`. Then the product of the corresponding
  marginals vanishes. To see this, extend `π` to `gB_D`, sum over the extensions, commute inside the ball
  (Q2, diameter `2D`), and apply (Q3) to each term. This is Step 1(a) of
  `free-group-boundary-shift-rigidity-proof`.

## Step 1. The flow algebra

- (a) `P_s(gt) = P_s(g)`. The pattern `{g ↦ flow s, gt ↦ flow s' ≠ s}` does not occur. So
  `P_s(g) = P_s(g) P_s(gt)`, and symmetrically `P_s(gt) = P_s(gt) P_s(g)`. The two commute, being adjacent.
- (b) By ball sums, `(P_s(w))_(w ∈ F_n)` is a `D`-family for the end shift `X_∂(F_n)`. So by
  `free-group-boundary-shift-rigidity-proof` (Steps 1–3):
  - all `P`'s commute;
  - `P_(s^(-1))(ws) = I - P_s(w)`;
  - along any geodesic `w_0, …, w_m` of `T`, the backward arrows `Q_k = [flow at w_k points to w_(k-1)]`
    form a chain: `Q_k ⊑ Q_l` for `k <= l`, where `A ⊑ B` means `AB = BA = A`.
- (c) Let `C` be the commutative algebra generated by all the `P`'s. Sending a cylinder of `∂F_n` to the
  product of the corresponding `P`'s defines a homomorphism `LC(∂F_n, k) → C`. The argument is the
  compactness step of `quantum-rigid-sfts-over-fp-groups-have-fp-crossed-products-proof`. So every
  identity between finite Boolean combinations of flow events that holds for ends also holds in `C`.
  For such an event we write `[·] ∈ C`.

## Step 2. Downward determinism

Let `g' = gs` be a tree neighbour of `g`, and put `Q = [flow at g' points to g] = P_(s^(-1))(g')`. For all
`i` and `b`,

```text
Q T_b(g' t^i) = Q Φ^b_(g,i),      Φ^b_(g,i) = Σ_(w ∈ A^([-R,R]), φ(w) = b) ∏_(|ℓ|<=R) T_(w_ℓ)(g t^(i+ℓ)),
```

and `Q` commutes with `T_b(g' t^i)` and with `Φ^b_(g,i)`.
- **Why the identity holds in `Z`.** By (F1) and (F2) at `g'`, a point with flow `s^(-1)` at `g'` has
  `h(g') = h(g) - 1`. So its letter at `g' t^i` is `φ` of its letters on `g t^(i+[-R,R])`.
- **Ball sums.** Hence `Q T_b(g' t^i) ∏ T_(w_ℓ) = 0` whenever `φ(w) ≠ b`. The support lies in the
  `R`-ball about `g t^i`, plus the point `g' t^i`.
- **Summation.** Sum over `w`, using `Σ_w ∏ T_(w_ℓ) = I`, and use
  `Q ∏ T_(w_ℓ) = Q T_(φ(w))(g' t^i) ∏ T_(w_ℓ)`.
- **Commutation.** Every site involved is within `R + 1 <= D` of every other.

## Step 3. Centrality (C): every `P_s(g)` commutes with every `T_a(h t^i)`

Fix the edge `{g, g' = gs}`. Every vertex `h` lies on the `g'`-side or on the `g`-side.

**The `g'`-side.**
- **Setup.** Let `Q = P_(s^(-1))(g') = I - P_s(g)`. Let `g = h_0, h_1 = g', …, h_m = h` be the geodesic,
  and `Q_k` the backward arrow at `h_k`. So `Q_1 = Q`, and `Q ⊑ Q_k` for `k >= 1` by Step 1(b).
- **Claim, by induction on `k`.** `Q` commutes with every `T_c(h_k t^(i'))`, for all `c` and `i'`.
- **Base (`k <= 1`).** `Q = P_(s^(-1))(g' t^(i'))` by Step 1(a). That site is at distance `<= 1` from
  `h_k t^(i')`.
- **Induction step (`k -> k + 1`).** By Step 2 on the edge `(h_k, h_(k+1))`,
  `Q_(k+1) T_b(h_(k+1) t^i) = Q_(k+1) Φ = Φ Q_(k+1)`, with `Φ = Φ^b_(h_k,i)`. Then:

```text
Q T_b = Q Q_(k+1) T_b = Q Q_(k+1) Φ = Q Φ,
T_b Q = T_b Q_(k+1) Q = Q_(k+1) T_b Q = Φ Q_(k+1) Q = Φ Q,
```

  where `T_b = T_b(h_(k+1) t^i)`. `Φ` is a polynomial in row-`h_k` letters, so `QΦ = ΦQ` by induction.
  Hence `Q T_b = T_b Q`.

**The `g`-side.** Run the same argument on the reversed edge, with `Q' = [flow at g points to g'] = P_s(g)`.

So `P_s(g)` commutes with all letters, and by Step 1(b) with all flows. It is **central** in the algebra
generated by the family.

## Step 4. Coherence

- **Statement.** Let `d(u, v) = 2r` with midpoint `q`, and put `A = [ξ projects to q on [u, v]]`. Then
  `A T_a(u t^i) = A T_a(v t^i)` for all `a` and `i`.
- **Proof.** Write `u = u_0, …, u_r = q` and `v = v_0, …, v_r = q`.
  - `A` lies under the flow arrows `u_k → u_(k+1)` and `v_k → v_(k+1)`, since `q` is the projection.
  - Argue by downward induction on `k`, for all `i` at once. Step 2 gives
    `A T_b(u_k t^i) = A Φ^b_(u_(k+1), i)`.
  - `A` is a central idempotent, so `A Φ` is `Φ` evaluated on the operators `A T(u_(k+1) t^(i'))`. By
    induction these equal `A T(v_(k+1) t^(i'))`.
  - Hence `A T_b(u_k t^i) = A T_b(v_k t^i)`.
- **Height form.** For ends, `h_ξ(u) = h_ξ(v)` holds exactly when `ξ` projects to the midpoint of
  `[u, v]`. So, by Step 1(c), `[h(u) = h(v)] T_a(u t^i) = [h(u) = h(v)] T_a(v t^i)`.

## Step 5. The section family

- **The section.** Fix an order on `S`. Put `σ(0) = 1`. For `j >= 0`, let `σ(j+1) = σ(j) y(σ(j))`. For
  `j <= 0`, let `σ(j-1) = σ(j) s*(σ(j))`, where `s*(u)` is the least `s ≠ y(u)`.
- **Its geometry.** For every end:
  - `σ` is a bi-infinite geodesic along which the flow runs from `σ(j)` to `σ(j+1)`. Moving by `s*` goes
    against the flow, and `y(u s*) = s*^(-1)`, so the path never backtracks.
  - `h(σ(j)) = j`.
- **The idempotents `c_(j,v)`.** Put `c_(j,v) = [σ(j) = v] ∈ C`. For fixed `j`, these are orthogonal,
  finitely many are nonzero, and they sum to `I`.
- **The family.** Define

```text
F_a(i, j) = Σ_v c_(j,v) T_a(v t^i).
```

**`F` is a `D_X`-quantum family for `X` on `W`.** The coefficients are central, which is used throughout.
- **(Q1).** Holds because the `c_(j,v)` are orthogonal central idempotents summing to `I`.
- **(Q2).** Take `(i, j)` and `(i', j')` within the rigidity distance `2 D_X` of `X`, so within `ℓ^1`
  distance `4 D_X <= 2D`.
  - Only terms with `c_(j,v) c_(j',v') ≠ 0` survive, that is, with `v = σ(j)` and `v' = σ(j')` jointly.
  - Then `d(v t^i, v' t^(i')) = |j - j'| + |i - i'| <= 2D`, so the two `T`'s commute by (Q2) for `Z`.
- **(Q3).** Let `α` be a pattern on a `D_X`-ball of `Z^2` about `p = (i_p, j_p)` that does not occur in
  `X`. Expanding the product gives terms `e ∏_f T_(α(f))(v_f t^(i_f))`, with `e = ∏_f c_(j_f, v_f)`.
  - Suppose `e ≠ 0`. Then `e <= e'`, where `e'` is the product of the flow arrows `σ(j) → σ(j+1)` over
    the heights in the ball.
  - The operators in `e' ∏_f T` sit inside the `2D_X`-ball about `v_(j_p) t^(i_p)`, and they form a
    partial `Z`-pattern: a flow line carrying the letters `α`.
  - That pattern does not occur in `Z`. If `x' ⊗ y'` realized it, then by (F2) the path would be a
    flow line of `y'`, with heights `j_f + c`. So `α` would occur in `x'`.
  - By ball sums, `e' ∏ T = 0`, and hence `e ∏ T = e e' ∏ T = 0`.

Since `X` is `D_X`-rigid, all the `F_a(i, j)` commute.

## Step 6. Conclusion

- **Letters as combinations of `F`.** Take `v ∈ F_n`. By Step 4 applied to `(v, σ(j))` under
  `c_(j,u) [h(v) = j]`, and summing over `u`:

```text
T_a(v t^i) = Σ_(|j| <= |v|) [h(v) = j] F_a(i, j).
```

- **Commutation of letters.** Every letter is therefore a combination of pairwise commuting `F`'s with
  orthogonal central coefficients, so all letters commute.
- **Commutation of flows.** By Steps 1(b) and 3, all flows commute with everything.
- **Conclusion.** Since `E_(a,s)(g) = T_a(g) P_s(g)`, the whole family commutes. `Z` is `D`-quantum rigid
  over `k`. ∎

## Remarks

- **Why this breaks the stall recorded on the target.**
  - Attempt 1 tried to show that `P_s(g)` commutes with a far letter by expanding the letter back to the
    flow's neighbourhood. That widens the `t`-window by `R` per tree step.
  - Here only **commutation** is transported, one tree step at a time.
  - Each step uses a radius-`R` window of a row with which `Q` already commutes **entirely**, because `Q`
    is `t`-invariant (Step 1(a)). The chain `Q ⊑ Q_k` carries `Q` under the determinism of every later
    edge.
  - No far letter is ever expanded.
- **Hypotheses used.** Nothing uses `dim W` or `char k`. `X` enters only through (F1), (F2), the one-step
  rule `φ`, and its rigidity at scale `D_X`.
- **Single-free-branch calibration (`quantum-rigid-sfts-over-products-have-a-single-free-branch`,
  `Λ_0 = Z`, `H = F_n`).** Passes. A slab `Z × N_w(S)` contains a full row, which fixes the fibre `x`.
  So extensions differ only in the flow, and only in the branch containing the end.
- **Other slice groups (same proof, not written out separately).**
  - Replace `⟨t⟩` by any finitely generated `H`. Take `X ⊆ A^(H × Z)` whose `H`-slice at height `j`
    determines the slices at `j ± 1` by local rules over `H`, and fold it over `H × F_n` with the flow
    constant on `H`-cosets.
  - Steps 1–6 go through verbatim, with "row" read as "`H`-slice". So the fold is rigid iff `X` is,
    granted the analogue of (F1). ABHT's proof of (F1) uses only slice expansivity; this was not
    re-checked.
- **Busemann transplants.** Condition (C) of `busemann-transplants-of-rigid-z2-sfts-are-quantum-rigid` has
  the same shape. The analogue of Step 3 needs two things, neither checked:
  - germ idempotents that are invariant along the direction in which the tiles are transported
    deterministically;
  - monotone germ chains along cone directions.
