View `(QHR1)` as the HNN extension of `C` with associated subgroups

```text
A=<z>,          B=<j>,          U^(-1) z U=j^(-1).
```

If `c in C`, the word `U c U^(-1)` has a Britton pinch exactly when
`c in B`. Therefore

```text
C intersect U C U^(-1)=A=<z>.                          (QHR5)
```

The character compatibility on its generator is

```text
chi_b(z)=chi_a(U^(-1)zU)=chi_a(j^(-1))=omega^2.
```

This is equivalent to `b=2` and proves `(QHR2)`. The usual
double-coset norm is still

```text
||p_2 U p_a||_2^2=|A|/|C|^2=1/27.                      (QHR6)
```

The small norm does not imply a small support. Put `Y_a=p_2Up_a`. Its
initial Gram operator is

```text
Y_a^*Y_a=p_a U^(-1)p_2U p_a.                           (QHR7)
```

Britton normal form identifies the subgroup generated by `C` and
`U^(-1)CU` with the amalgam of two copies of `C` over `B=<j>`.
Because both vertex groups are abelian and split as `B times C_3`, this
amalgam is

```text
B times (C_3 * C_3).                                   (QHR8)
```

The restrictions of `p_a` and `U^(-1)p_2U` to `B` agree: the first
has `j`-value `omega`, while the second has

```text
chi_2(z^(-1))=omega
```

on `j=U^(-1)z^(-1)U`. Compress to this common central character sector
and normalize its trace. The remaining parts of the two projections in
`(QHR7)` are character projections from the two free `C_3` factors in
`(QHR8)`. They are free projections `r,s` with

```text
tau_bar(r)=tau_bar(s)=1/3.
```

The standard meet formula for free projections gives

```text
tau_bar(r meet (1-s))
 =max(tau_bar(r)+tau_bar(1-s)-1,0)
 =max(1/3+2/3-1,0)
 =0.                                                    (QHR9)
```

Hence the product `sr` has full initial support `r`. Translating back,
the support of `Y_a^*Y_a` is all of `p_a`. If
`Y_a=W_a|Y_a|`, then

```text
W_a^*W_a=p_a.
```

Its final support lies below `p_2` and has the same trace as its initial
support. Since `tau(p_a)=tau(p_2)=1/9`, faithfulness of the trace forces

```text
W_aW_a^*=p_2.
```

This proves `(QHR3)`. Notice the distinction from the finite router:
`Y_a^*Y_a` is no longer a finite character projection. Its spectrum
accumulates at zero, allowing squared norm `1/27` while its support has
trace `1/9`.

Now attach the HNN stable letter to the full D8 base along the same finite
line. The source identity in `(QHR3)` immediately gives

```text
||W_aV_eta||_2^2
 =tau(V_eta^*p_aV_eta)
 =1/288.
```

For `a=0,1` both final supports are the same projection `p_2`, so the
two polar branches are not orthogonal. Finally, the associated group
`A=<z>` is finite and amenable. The amenable-edge HNN permanence theorem
therefore preserves hyperlinearity whenever the base is hyperlinear. This
proves both the positive full-source statement and its exact firewall.
