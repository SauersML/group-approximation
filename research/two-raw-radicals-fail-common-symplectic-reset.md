Fix a forbidden atom `a`.  Its raw radical plane is

```text
R_a=<r_(a,0),r_(a,1)>=rad(A_f(a)).                       (RRC1)
```

For a nonzero chart functional `ell`, the odd chart form is

```text
B_ell=[[A_f(a),ell],[ell^T,0]].                          (RRC2)
```

Its radical is the one line `R_a intersect ker(ell)`.  If `W` is any
codimension-one complement on which `B_ell` is nondegenerate, then

```text
W^perp=rad(B_ell).                                       (RRC3)
```

Consequently only the single line in `(RRC3)` can commute with the reset
packet generated by `W`.  The two independent words in `(RRC1)` can never
both do so.

For the endpoint ordering `(XRS1)`, the crossed chart has
`ell=ell_3=ell_1+ell_2`, hence

```text
rad(B_(ell_3))=<r_(a,0)+r_(a,1)>.                       (RRC4)
```

Neither `r_(a,0)` nor `r_(a,1)` centralizes its reset subgroup.  Explicitly,
`W` must contain a vector with odd-coordinate value one: otherwise
`W` would be the old even space, whose restriction is `A_f(a)` and is
degenerate.  Pairing that vector with either raw word gives one.

Conjugating by a context transport cannot repair this.  It preserves
commutators, so the transported raw-word projections still fail to commute
with the transported reset packet.  Thus the assertion in
`three-chart-return-from-cross-context-relators` that both projections `P,Q`
lie in the fresh multiplicity commutant is false for every admissible reset
hyperplane.  The route cannot establish
`three-chart-affine-clifford-return-incidence` as stated, even though the
separate completeness-side word conjugacies have exact S3 alignments.
