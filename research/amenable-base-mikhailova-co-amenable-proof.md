Write `Q = <X | R>` (finite), `F = F(X)`, `N` the normal closure of `R` in
`F`, `H = F x F`, `M = M_Q = {(u,v) : u =_Q v}` and `X_M = H/M`.

## Co-amenable

By [[mikhailova-coset-action-is-two-sided-q-action]], `X_M ~= Q` as an `H`-set.
Here `H` acts through `phi x phi : H -> Q x Q`, and `Q x Q` acts on `Q` by
two-sided translation.  `Q` is amenable, so `Q x Q` is amenable and has an
invariant mean on `ell^infinity(Q)` for this action.  Pulled back along
`phi x phi`, it is an `H`-invariant mean on `ell^infinity(H/M)`.  That is
co-amenability of `M` in `H`.

## Non-amenable

For `u in F`, `phi(u) = phi(u)`, so `Delta = {(u,u) : u in F} <= M`.  Once
`|X| >= 2`, `Delta ~= F` is a nonabelian free group, so `M` is non-amenable.

## Finitely generated

`M` is generated by `(x,x)` for `x in X` and `(r,1)` for `r in R`.  These lie
in `M`.  Conversely, let `(u,v) in M`.  Then `(u,v) = (u v^(-1), 1)(v,v)`.
`(v,v)` is a word in the `(x,x)`, and `u v^(-1) in N` is a product of
conjugates `w r^(+-1) w^(-1)`.  Each factor `(w r w^(-1), 1)` equals
`(w,w)(r,1)(w,w)^(-1)`.

## Non-separable when `Q` has undecidable word problem

`(u,1) in M` iff `u =_Q 1`.  So decidable membership in `M` would decide the
word problem of `Q`.  A finitely generated separable subgroup of the finitely
presented group `H` has decidable membership.  Membership is recursively
enumerable: enumerate products of the finite generators and compare them in
`F x F`, which has solvable word problem.  Non-membership is recursively
enumerable too: enumerate homomorphisms from `H` to finite groups and look for
one that separates the element from the image of `M`.  So if `Q` has
undecidable word problem, `M` is not separable.

Such an amenable `Q` exists: [[finitely-presented-amenable-undecidable-word-problem]]
gives a fixed finitely presented solvable group with undecidable word problem
(Kharlampovich).  So all three properties hold at once for that choice.  `∎`

## Remark

Co-amenable and non-amenable are compatible because `H = F x F` is
non-amenable.  Co-amenability says only that `H/M` carries an invariant mean,
and the two-sided action of the amenable `Q` supplies one.  Co-amenability puts
Følner sets in the lamp index space, which helps approximation.
Non-separability makes the centralizing HNN fail residual finiteness, and that
is why the compiler needs it.
