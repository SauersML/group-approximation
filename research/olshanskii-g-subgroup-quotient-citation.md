Literature import.  A. Yu. Ol'shanskii, *On residualing homomorphisms and
G-subgroups of hyperbolic groups*, Internat. J. Algebra Comput. 3 (1993),
no. 4, 365--409, DOI `10.1142/S0218196793000251`, Theorems 1 and 2 and
Proposition 1.

Verification boundary.  The IJAC paper itself was not read.  The statements
were read on 2026-09-11, pages 1--3 of the arXiv PDF, in the verbatim
restatement by A. Minasyan, *On residualizing homomorphisms preserving
quasiconvexity*, arXiv:math/0406126v2 (4 Dec 2005), which attributes them to
Ol'shanskii [16]:

> Using the terminology from [16], a non-elementary subgroup `H` of `G` will
> be called a G-subgroup if for any finite subset `M ⊂ G` there is a
> non-elementary hyperbolic quotient `G_1` of the group `G`, such that the
> natural homomorphism `G → G_1` is surjective on `H` and injective on `M`.

> Denote `E(H) = ∩_{x∈H^0} E(x)`. If `H` is a non-elementary subgroup of `G`,
> then `E(H)` is the unique maximal finite subgroup of `G` normalized by `H`
> ([16, Prop. 1]). ... The kernel of that homomorphism is `C_H(E(H))` which
> sometimes will be denoted by `K(H)`.

> **Lemma 1.1.** A non-elementary subgroup `H` of a hyperbolic group `G` is a
> G-subgroup if and only if `E(H) = E(G)` and `|H : K(H)| = |G : K(G)|` ...
> ([16, Thm. 1])

> **Theorem 1.** Let `H_1, H_2, ..., H_k` be G-subgroups of a non-elementary
> hyperbolic group `G` ... Then there exist a group `G_1` and an epimorphism
> `φ : G → G_1` such that 1) `G_1` is a non-elementary hyperbolic group; 2)
> ... `φ` is injective on `Q`; 3) `φ` is surjective on each of the subgroups
> `H_1, ..., H_k`, i.e. `φ(H_i) = G_1` for each `i = 1, 2, ..., k`; ... 6)
> `ker φ` is a torsion-free subgroup; 7) `φ` induces a bijective map on sets
> of conjugacy classes of elements having finite orders in `G` and `G_1`
> respectively ...

> Parts 1)-8) of Theorem 1 were proved by A. Ol'shanskii in [16, Thm. 2] for
> the case when the subset `Q` is finite ... If `card(Q) < ∞`, ... the
> condition (*) becomes trivial and can be omitted.

The claim imports exactly the finite-`Q` case attributed to Ol'shanskii.
