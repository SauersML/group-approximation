## 0. Conventions and standard facts

`F(Y)` is the free group on a finite set `Y`, and `|w|` is the reduced length of `w ∈ F(Y)`. For a
subset `A` of a group, `⟨⟨A⟩⟩` is its normal closure. `K = F(X)/N`, and `N_{≤R}` is as in the
claim. Every `N_{≤R}` is contained in `N`, and `N_{≤R} ⊆ N_{≤R'}` for `R ≤ R'`.

Standard facts used:
- **(S1)** *B. H. Neumann.* Let `P = F(Y)/𝒩` be finitely presented with `Y` finite, and let
  `𝒩 = ⟨⟨A⟩⟩`. Then `𝒩 = ⟨⟨A_0⟩⟩` for some finite `A_0 ⊆ A`.
- **(S2)** If `K = F(X)/N` is finitely presented, then `N = N_{≤R}` for some `R`. This is (S1)
  with `A = N`.
- **(S3)** Hyperbolic groups are finitely presented (Bridson--Haefliger III.Γ.2.2).
- **(S4)** *Gromov.* A hyperbolic group is virtually cyclic or contains a free subgroup of rank 2.
- **(S5)** Countable Kazhdan groups are finitely generated
  (`GroupApproximation/Kazhdan/KazhdanFiniteGeneration.lean`).

**Lemma 0 (semidirect presentations).** Let `M ◁ F(X)` and `G = F(X)/M`. Let `α` be a
substitution `x ↦ w_x` with `α(M) ⊆ M`, such that the induced endomorphism `ᾱ` of `G` is an
automorphism. Put `T = {t x t^{-1} w_x^{-1} : x ∈ X} ⊆ F(X ⊔ {t})`. Then
`F(X,t)/⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩ ≅ G ⋊_ᾱ Z` via `x ↦ (x̄, 0)` and `t ↦ (1, 1)`, and

```text
⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩ ∩ F(X) = M.
```

*Proof.* The map `π: F(X,t) → G ⋊_ᾱ Z`, with `x ↦ (x̄,0)` and `t ↦ (1,1)`, is onto. It kills `M`,
and it kills `T` because `π(t x t^{-1}) = (ᾱ(x̄), 0) = π(w_x)`. So `⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩ ⊆ ker π`.

Conversely, the relators `T` give `t u t^{-1} ≡ α(u)`. Since `ᾱ` is onto, each `x` is congruent to
some `α(u_x)`, which gives `t^{-1} x t ≡ u_x`. So modulo `⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩` every element can be written
`u t^k` with `u ∈ F(X)`. If `π(u t^k) = 1`, then `k = 0` and `ū = 1` in `G`, so `u ∈ M`. Hence
`ker π = ⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩`.

For `w ∈ F(X)` with `w ∈ ⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩`, we get `π(w) = (w̄, 0) = 1`, so `w ∈ M`. ∎

## 1. Part A: the gap lemma

Fix `ψ`, the words `w_x`, `v_x`, and `L = max(1, max_x |w_x|, max_x |v_x|)`. Let `α: x ↦ w_x` and
`β: x ↦ v_x` be the corresponding substitutions of `F(X)`.

- Since `α` induces the endomorphism `ψ` of `K`, it maps `N` into `N`. The same holds for `β` with
  `ψ^{-1}`.
- `|α(w)| ≤ L|w|` and `|β(w)| ≤ L|w|` for all `w ∈ F(X)`.
- For each `x`, the words `α(v_x) x^{-1}` and `β(w_x) x^{-1}` lie in `N`, because they represent
  `ψψ^{-1}(x) x^{-1}` and `ψ^{-1}ψ(x) x^{-1}`. Both have length at most `L² + 1`.

**Step 1: a finite subpresentation.** Let `T = {t x t^{-1} w_x^{-1}}` and `𝒩 = ⟨⟨N ∪ T⟩⟩`.
Lemma 0, applied to `(M, α) = (N, α)`, gives `F(X,t)/𝒩 ≅ K ⋊_ψ Z = P`. Since `P` is finitely
presented, (S1) gives a finite `A_0 ⊆ N ∪ T` with `⟨⟨A_0⟩⟩ = 𝒩`. Put `R_fin = A_0 ∩ N`; then
`⟨⟨R_fin ∪ T⟩⟩ = 𝒩` as well. Set

```text
R_1 = max( max{|r| : r ∈ R_fin},  L² + 1 ).
```

**Step 2: a gap scale gives an invariant truncation.** Let `R ≥ R_1` with `N_{≤LR} = N_{≤R}`, and
put `M = N_{≤R}`.
- As a normal subgroup, `M` is generated by the `r ∈ N` with `|r| ≤ R`.
- For such `r`, the word `α(r)` lies in `N` and has length at most `LR`. So `α(r) ∈ N_{≤LR} = M`.
- Since `α` is a homomorphism, `α(u r u^{-1}) = α(u) α(r) α(u)^{-1} ∈ M`. Hence `α(M) ⊆ M`, and in
  the same way `β(M) ⊆ M`.

So `α` and `β` induce endomorphisms `ᾱ`, `β̄` of `G = F(X)/M`. The words `α(v_x) x^{-1}` and
`β(w_x) x^{-1}` lie in `N` and have length at most `L² + 1 ≤ R`, so they lie in `M`. Hence
`ᾱβ̄ = β̄ᾱ = id` on generators, and `ᾱ ∈ Aut(G)`.

**Step 3: the sandwich.** Every `r ∈ R_fin` lies in `N` and has length at most `R`, so
`R_fin ⊆ M ⊆ N`. Therefore

```text
𝒩 = ⟨⟨R_fin ∪ T⟩⟩ ⊆ ⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩ ⊆ ⟨⟨N ∪ T⟩⟩ = 𝒩.
```

Intersect with `F(X)` and use Lemma 0 twice, once for `(M, α)` and once for `(N, α)`:

```text
M = ⟨⟨M ∪ T⟩⟩ ∩ F(X) = 𝒩 ∩ F(X) = N.
```

This proves (GAP).

**Step 4: consequences.**
- If `K` is not finitely presented, then `N_{≤R} ≠ N` for every `R`: a normal closure of finitely
  many words would give a finite presentation. So (GAP) gives `N_{≤LR} ≠ N_{≤R}` for all
  `R ≥ R_1`. These are `L`-bounded gaps.
- (UG) over `X` would give `R ≥ R_1` with `N_{≤LR} = N_{≤R} ≠ N`, contradicting (GAP). So (UG)
  forbids a finitely presented `K ⋊_ψ Z`.
- `X` was an arbitrary finite generating set, and `ψ` was arbitrary. So (UG) over one finite
  generating set forbids every finitely presented mapping torus. ∎

## 2. Part B: split extensions by finitely presented groups

Let `H = ⟨Y | S_H⟩` with `Y` and `S_H` finite, let `φ: H → Aut(K)`, and let `P = K ⋊_φ H` be
finitely presented.
- For each `y ∈ Y`, choose substitutions `α_y` and `β_y`, lifting `φ(y)` and `φ(y)^{-1}`. Let `L`
  be the maximum of their stretch constants, and at least 1.
- For `s = y_1^{e_1} ... y_k^{e_k} ∈ S_H`, put `γ_s = σ_1 ∘ ... ∘ σ_k`, where `σ_i = α_{y_i}` if
  `e_i = 1` and `σ_i = β_{y_i}` if `e_i = -1`.
- `γ_s` lifts `φ(s) = id`. So `γ_s(x) x^{-1} ∈ N`, with length at most `L^k + 1`.

Let `T = {y x y^{-1} α_y(x)^{-1}}`. Then `P = F(X ⊔ Y)/⟨⟨N ∪ T ∪ S_H⟩⟩`, by the analogue of Lemma 0:
map onto `G ⋊ H`, and use `T` together with surjectivity of each `ᾱ_y` to normalize every word to
`u · h(Y)`.

- Step 1 goes through verbatim and gives a finite `R_fin ⊆ N`.
- Take `R_1` at least `max |r|` over `R_fin`, at least `L² + 1`, and at least `L^k + 1` for every
  relator of `S_H` of length `k`.
- At a scale `R ≥ R_1` with `N_{≤LR} = N_{≤R} = M`, Step 2 shows that every `α_y` and `β_y`
  preserves `M` and induces mutually inverse automorphisms of `G = F(X)/M`.
- Each `γ_s` induces the identity on `G`, because `γ_s(x) x^{-1} ∈ M`. So `y ↦ ᾱ_y` defines a
  homomorphism `H → Aut(G)`.
- The analogue of Lemma 0 gives `⟨⟨M ∪ T ∪ S_H⟩⟩ ∩ F(X) = M`. The sandwich of Step 3 then gives
  `M = N`. ∎

## 3. Part C: graded small cancellation gives (UG)

Let `⟨X | ⋃_{i≥0} R_i⟩` be a graded small cancellation presentation of `K`, with `X` finite, and
assume `K` is not finitely presented. Put `N_n = ⟨⟨R_0 ∪ ... ∪ R_n⟩⟩` and `G_n = F(X)/N_n`. Then
`N_0 ⊆ N_1 ⊆ ...` and `N = ⋃ N_n`. Import (A1)-(A4) from `oos-graded-small-cancellation-scales-separate`,
with `r_n = r_X(G_n → G_{n+1})`.

**Step 1: no stage is the limit.** If `N = N_n`, then `K = G_n` is hyperbolic by (A4), hence
finitely presented by (S3). That is excluded, so `N ≠ N_n` for every `n`.

**Step 2: upper scale of a stage.**
- `G_0` is hyperbolic by (A4), so it is finitely presented by (S3). By (S2) there is `a` with
  `N_0 = ⟨⟨N_0 ∩ {|w| ≤ a}⟩⟩`.
- Put `m_n = max(a, max_{1≤i≤n} max_{R∈R_i} |R|)`. This is finite by (A1).
- Every generator of `N_n` given above is an element of `N` of length at most `m_n`. So
  `N_n ⊆ N_{≤m_n}`.

**Step 3: lower scale of the next new relation.** Put `λ_n = min{|w| : w ∈ N \ N_n}`. The set is
nonempty by Step 1.
- Given `w ∈ N \ N_n`, let `k + 1` be the least index with `w ∈ N_{k+1}`. Then `k ≥ n`, and `w`
  represents an element `g ≠ 1` of `Ker(G_k → G_{k+1})`.
- The homomorphism `G_k → G_{k+1}` is injective on the ball of radius `r_k` about `1`, and it
  identifies `g` with `1`. So `|g|_{G_k} > r_k`. (Balls of equal radius are left translates of one
  another, so any center may be used.)
- Hence `|w| ≥ |g|_{G_k} > r_k`. If `G_k → G_{k+1}` is injective there is no such `w` for this `k`.

So `λ_n ≥ inf_{k≥n} r_k`.

Every element of `N` of length below `λ_n` lies in `N_n`. So for `m_n ≤ R < λ_n`:

```text
N_n ⊆ N_{≤m_n} ⊆ N_{≤R} ⊆ N_n,   that is   N_{≤R} = N_n ≠ N.            (PLATEAU)
```

**Step 4: the plateaus are unboundedly long.**
- By (A2), there is `n_0` with `ρ_{k+1} > ρ_k` for all `k ≥ n_0`. So `ρ_k ≥ ρ_n` whenever
  `k ≥ n ≥ n_0`.
- Put `τ_n = inf_{k≥n} r_k/ρ_k`. By (A3), `τ_n → ∞`. For `n ≥ n_0` this gives
  `λ_n ≥ inf_{k≥n} r_k ≥ τ_n ρ_n`.
- By (A1), with `D ≥ 1`, we have `m_n ≤ max(a, D·max_{i<n_0} ρ_i, D ρ_n)`. Since `ρ_n → ∞` by
  (A2), this is `D ρ_n` for all large `n`.

Hence `λ_n / m_n ≥ τ_n / D → ∞`, and `λ_n → ∞`.

**Step 5: (UG).** Given `C ≥ 1` and `R_0`, choose `n` so large that `λ_n > (C+1) m_n` and
`λ_n > (C+1) R_0`. Put `R = λ_n/(C+1)`. Then `m_n ≤ R` and `R ≤ CR < λ_n`. So (PLATEAU) gives
`N_{≤CR} = N_{≤R} = N_n ≠ N`, with `R ≥ R_0`. ∎

## 4. Part D: the monster

Let `Q = F(S)/N` be the quotient of the claim `kazhdan-torsion-free-tarski-monster-satisfies-kk`
(ESTABLISHED). By items 1 and 2 of its theorem, `Q` has these properties:
- it is infinite, non-abelian and torsion-free;
- every proper subgroup of `Q` is cyclic;
- it has a graded small cancellation presentation over the finite generating set `S` of the
  hyperbolic group `G = G(0)`;
- it is the direct limit of the hyperbolic stages `G(i)`.

**`Q` is not finitely presented.** Suppose it were.
1. By (S2), `N` would be the normal closure of finitely many words.
2. Those words lie in one stage kernel `N_i`. So `Q = G(i)` would be hyperbolic.
3. By (S4), `Q` would then be virtually cyclic or contain `F_2`.
4. Suppose `Q` is virtually cyclic. A torsion-free virtually cyclic group is trivial or `Z`, so `Q`
   would be abelian. That is a contradiction.
5. Suppose `F_2 ≤ Q`. `F_2` contains non-cyclic proper subgroups such as `⟨a, b²⟩`, so `Q` would
   have a non-cyclic proper subgroup. That is a contradiction.

So Part C gives (UG) over `S`. Parts A and B then give the rest of D. No hypothesis on `ψ`, on
`(RF_Γ)` or on the lattice is used. For the Kazhdan case, `G = Γ` is a torsion-free cocompact
`Sp(n,1)` lattice, as in the Corollary of the imported claim.

## 5. Part E: flagship witnesses

Let `(G, K)` witness `torsion-free-sofic-exact-mf-radical-over-z`.
- `K` has (T) and is countable, so it is finitely generated by (S5).
- `G/K ≅ Z` is free, so `G = K ⋊_ψ Z` for some `ψ`.
- `G` is finitely presented. So Part A applies to every finite generating set `X` of `K`: either
  `K` is finitely presented, or `K` has `L_{ψ,X}`-bounded gaps over `X`.
- In the second case `K` is not finitely presented. Part C, used in contrapositive form, then rules
  out a graded small cancellation presentation over any finite generating set. ∎

The (AT) case is `finite-outer-order-radical-witnesses-cross-alekseev-thom`. Nothing about the MF
radical, soficity or torsion is used anywhere. The obstruction is purely combinatorial, at the
level of presentations.

## 6. Remark: the L-presentation form, and the relation with (FAP1)

The same Step 2 proves an intrinsic statement. Let `K = ⟨X | ⋃_{σ∈Φ*} σ(R_0)⟩` be a finite
`L`-presentation whose substitutions `σ ∈ Φ` each map `N` into `N`. Let `L = max_{σ,x} |σ(x)|`, and
let `R ≥ max_{r∈R_0} |r|` with `N_{≤LR} = N_{≤R}`.
- `M = N_{≤R}` is `Φ`-invariant, by Step 2.
- `M` is normal and contains `R_0`.
- Hence `M ⊇ σ(R_0)` for all `σ ∈ Φ*`, so `M = N`.

So a finitely `L`-presented kernel with invariant substitutions also has bounded gaps. By
`fp-mapping-torus-iff-finite-automorphic-presentation`, (FAP1) is such a presentation with
`Φ = {Φ_+, Φ_-}`. This gives a second proof of Part A for the lifts `Φ_±` used there.

## Checked against

- **Finitely presented kernels.** `N_{≤R} = N` for large `R`, so (GAP) is vacuous. This is
  consistent with the (AT) region, where finitely presented tori abound.
- **Infinitely presented kernels with finitely presented tori exist.** An example is the
  Stallings--Bieri kernel of `F_2 × F_2 → Z`, which is finitely generated but not finitely
  presented, and whose torus is `F_2 × F_2`. Part A says such kernels have bounded gaps over every
  finite generating set, and that is not a contradiction.
- **Where the monster's lacunarity enters.** Only (A1)-(A3) are used: `max |R_n| = O(ρ_n)` and
  `ρ_n = o(r_n)`. The hyperbolicity constants and the `(1 - o(1))ρ_{n+1}` bound in the proof of
  Lemma `asc` are not used.
- **Why the conclusion is not already in `tarski-monster-tori-evade-compression-and-stage-lifts`.**
  Its item (iv) needs `ψ` to lift to infinitely many stages. Here the stages do not appear in
  Part A at all. The argument lifts `ψ` only to the truncation `F/N_{≤R}` at a gap scale, and that
  truncation is a stage `G_n` precisely because of (PLATEAU). So finite presentation of `P` would
  force `ψ` to lift to a stage `G_n` whose kernel is already all of `N`, which is impossible.
