**ESTABLISHED** through `fg-groups-have-fg-derivative-images-proof`. Not
independently reviewed. The argument is elementary and no priority is claimed.

**Statement.** Let a group `Γ` act on a set `X`, let `L` be a group, and let
`c : Γ × X -> L` be a cocycle:

```text
c(γδ, x) = c(γ, δx) · c(δ, x)      for all γ, δ in Γ and x in X.
```

Suppose `Γ` is generated by a finite set `S` and that for each `s in S` the map
`x -> c(s, x)` takes only finitely many values. Put

```text
Λ = < c(s, x) : s in S, x in X >  <=  L,
```

a finitely generated subgroup. Then:

1. `c(γ, x) in Λ` for every `γ in Γ` and `x in X`;
2. for every `x in X` the restriction of `c(-, x)` to the stabilizer `Γ_x` is a
   homomorphism `Γ_x -> Λ`.

**Where such cocycles come from.** In each case the finiteness hypothesis holds
for an element with finitely many pieces, and also for periodic lifts with
finitely many distinct pieces.

- **Derivative / linear part.** `Γ` acts on a space by homeomorphisms that are
  locally affine, `v -> A v + b` with `A in GL_N(K)`, in a common affine atlas
  (examples: `Q_p^N`, `A_f^N`, `R^N`, disjoint unions of these). `c(γ, x)` is
  the linear part of `γ` at `x`, and the chain rule is the cocycle identity.
- **Projective piece.** `Γ` acts on `RP^1` (or on `R` by lifts) by piecewise
  projective homeomorphisms. `c(γ, x)` is the Möbius piece of `γ` on the right
  of `x`. It is well defined because two Möbius maps that agree on an interval
  are equal.
