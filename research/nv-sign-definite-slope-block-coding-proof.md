Notation is as in the target. `S` is a finite symmetric generating set of `K`.

**Step 0 (tables and refinement).**
- A table of `g ∈ nV` is a finite partition of `C^n` into bricks
  `B(u) = u_1 C × ... × u_n C`, together with bricks `B(u')` forming another
  partition, such that `g(u_1 y_1, ..., u_n y_n) = (u'_1 y_1, ..., u'_n y_n)`.
- **Refinement.** Split one table brick in coordinate `j` by appending a bit
  `b` to both `u_j` and `u'_j`. This gives another table of `g`, with the same
  slopes. Iterating, any table brick can be refined so that `u_j` has any
  prescribed length `>= |u_j|`, and `u'_j` grows by the same amount.
- **Cocycle.** By the cocycle identity, every slope of every element of `K` lies
  in the subgroup generated by the slopes of `S`. So every slope of every
  `g ∈ K` is at every point of the form `Σ_i m_i v^(i)`, with `m_i ∈ Z`. In
  particular it is `0` on `Z_0`.

**Step 1 (freeze the zero-slope coordinates).**
- Let `D` be the largest length `|u_j|`, `j ∈ Z_0`, over fixed tables of the
  elements of `S`. Refine these tables so that `|u_j| = D` for every `j ∈ Z_0`.
  Then `|u'_j| = D` too, because the slope vanishes on `Z_0`.
- Write a point of `C^n` as `(a, t, p)`:
  - `a ∈ F = ({0,1}^D)^{Z_0}` is the first `D` bits of the `Z_0` coordinates;
  - `t` is the remaining tails of those coordinates;
  - `p ∈ C^P` is the `P = P_1 ⊔ ... ⊔ P_k` coordinates.
- Each `g ∈ S` then satisfies `g(a,t,p) = (ρ_g(a,p), t)` for a homeomorphism
  `ρ_g` of `F × C^P` that does not depend on `t`.
- The set `G_D` of homeomorphisms of this form is a group. Restricting to `t`
  constant is an isomorphism `G_D -> Homeo(F × C^P)`. So `K <= G_D`, and `ρ`
  embeds `K` into `Homeo(F × C^P)`.
- **Each `ρ_g`, `g ∈ K`, is a piecewise prefix replacement.**
  - Take a table of `g` and refine it so that every `Z_0` prefix has a common
    length `D_g >= D`.
  - A table brick is then `{a} × uC × B_P(w)`, with `u` the bits `D+1..D_g`. Since
    `g ∈ G_D` fixes tails, its image is `{a'} × uC × B_P(w')`.
  - Fix `t = 0^ω` in every `Z_0` coordinate. The bricks with `u = 0^{D_g - D}`
    give a finite partition `{a} × B_P(w) -> {a'} × B_P(w')` of `F × C^P`, with the
    slopes of `g` on `P`.

**Step 2 (align each block to its slope vector).**
- Write `v^(i)_j = c_j > 0` for `j ∈ P_i`, and `A_i = Σ_{j ∈ P_i} c_j`.
- Take a piece `{a} × B_P(w) -> {a'} × B_P(w')` of `ρ_g`. On `P_i` its slope is
  `m_i v^(i)`, so `|w'_j| = |w_j| + m_i c_j` for `j ∈ P_i`.
- Choose `T_i >= max_{j ∈ P_i} ⌈|w_j| / c_j⌉`, and refine coordinate `j ∈ P_i` by
  `e_j = c_j T_i - |w_j| >= 0` bits.
  - The source lengths become `c_j T_i`.
  - The image lengths become `|w_j| + m_i c_j + e_j = c_j (T_i + m_i)`. They are
    nonnegative, so `T_i + m_i >= 0`.
- Hence after refinement every piece of every `ρ_g` is **block aligned**. In each
  block `P_i`, source prefixes have lengths `c_j T_i` and image prefixes have
  lengths `c_j T'_i`, with common integers `T_i, T'_i >= 0`.

**Step 3 (interleave).**
- Fix an order of each `P_i`. Define `Φ_i : C^{P_i} -> C` by writing, for
  `r = 1, 2, ...`, the block `x_j[(r-1)c_j + 1 .. r c_j]` of every `j ∈ P_i` in that
  order. This is a homeomorphism.
- `Φ_i` carries the brick `B_{P_i}(w)` with `|w_j| = c_j T` onto the cylinder of
  the length `A_i T` word `Φ_i(w)`, the interleaving of the first `T` blocks.
  - The tails `y_j` start at block boundaries both before and after a
    block-aligned prefix replacement.
  - So `Φ_i(w y) = Φ_i(w) Φ_i(y)` and `Φ_i(w' y) = Φ_i(w') Φ_i(y)`, where `Φ_i(y)`
    interleaves the tails.
- For `k >= 1`, define `Ψ : F × C^P -> C^k` by
  `Ψ(a, p) = (a Φ_1(p_{P_1}), Φ_2(p_{P_2}), ..., Φ_k(p_{P_k}))`. Here `a` is read as
  a binary word of length `D |Z_0|`, and `Ψ` is a homeomorphism.
- A block-aligned piece `{a} × B_P(w) -> {a'} × B_P(w')` is carried to the brick
  prefix replacement
  `(a Φ_1(w_{P_1}) z_1, Φ_2(w_{P_2}) z_2, ...) -> (a' Φ_1(w'_{P_1}) z_1, Φ_2(w'_{P_2}) z_2, ...)`.
- A finite partition of `F × C^P` into such pieces goes to a finite brick
  partition of `C^k`, and likewise for the images. So `Ψ ρ_g Ψ^{-1} ∈ kV` for
  every `g ∈ K`, and `g -> Ψ ρ_g Ψ^{-1}` is an injective homomorphism `K -> kV`.
- For `k = 0`, `P` is empty and `ρ` embeds `K` into `Sym(F)`, so `K` is finite.
  This proves part 1.

**Step 4 (one sign-definite line).**
- Suppose `L(K) <= Z v` with the nonzero entries of `v` of one sign. Replace `v`
  by `-v` if needed, so they are positive.
- Take `P_1 = {j : v_j > 0}`, `Z_0` its complement, and `v^(1) = v`. If
  `v = 0`, use `k = 0`.
- Part 1 gives `K <= V` up to isomorphism, or `K` finite.
- `thompson-v-has-haagerup-property`: subgroups of `V` are a-T-menable, and their
  Kazhdan subgroups are finite.
- Part 2 of `fw-subgroups-of-eventually-similar-groups-virtually-embed` applies
  to `V = V_{2,1}`, the canonical similarities of the full binary shift. It gives
  finiteness of FW subgroups. This proves part 2.

**Step 5 (consequence).**
- A group with property (T) is finitely generated (Bekka–de la Harpe–Valette,
  *Kazhdan's Property (T)*, Theorem 1.3.1).
- So an infinite Kazhdan `Q <= nV` falls under Step 4 unless `L(Q)` lies on no
  sign-definite line. Then either `rank L(Q) >= 2`, or `L(Q) = Z v` with `v`
  having a positive and a negative entry.
- **Unimodular remark.** If every slope has coordinate sum `0`, then a brick of
  shape `(|u_j|)` and its image have equal product Lebesgue measure
  `2^{-Σ|u_j|}`. So Lebesgue measure is `Q`-invariant, and part 1 of
  `kazhdan-subgroups-of-nv-finite-iff-invariant-sets-carry-measures` gives
  `λ(U_Q) = 1`.
