The Steinberg commutator relations put every word in
`H=<x_65(q),x_54(q),x_42(q)>` into the six-bit positive-root normal form on
the ordered indices `(6,5,4,2)`.  Hence `|H|<=2^6`.  The elementary matrix
image contains all sixty-four unitriangular matrices with entries in
`{0,q}`; they are distinct because `q!=0`.  Thus `H~=UT_4(F_2)` and
`|H|=64`.

The abelianization of `UT_4(F_2)` is generated by its three simple roots.
Assigning `-1` to all three therefore defines `chi`, while every commutator
root has character `+1`.  Finite-group character orthogonality makes
`e_chi` a projection, and the canonical group trace reads only its identity
coefficient, proving `(CCG2)`.

The Steinberg commutator criterion says that `x_ij(r)` commutes with
`x_kl(s)` unless `j=k` or `i=l`.  The selector index sets `{8,7}` and
`{9,8}` are disjoint from `{6,5,4,2}`, so both `g_i` centralize `H` and
`e_chi`.  Therefore `S_i^*S_i=S_iS_i^*=e_chi`, and cancellation gives
`S_1^*S_2S_2^*S_1=e_chi`, proving `(CCG3)`.

Finally `qa_m=a_m` for `m=1,2`, because
`s_1t_1s_1t_(0^m)=s_1t_(0^m)`.  The composable-root relation gives `(CCG4)`.
The conjugate of `C_2` by `A_1`, respectively of `C_3` by `A_2`, consequently
has a nonzero root coordinate outside the six root positions of `H`.
Elementary matrices show that the conjugate subgroup is not `H`.  In the
left regular representation the Fourier supports of `e_chi` and its
conjugate are therefore different, so the corresponding arm does not reduce
`e_chi`.  This proves the asserted scope boundary.
