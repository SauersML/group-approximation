## Statement

Let `X` be a compact metrizable space and give `Homeo(X)` the uniform topology. For `S ≤ Homeo(X)` let `S̄` be its closure and `J_k(S) ⊆ S * F_k` its `k`-variable mixed identities.

1. **Closure principle.** `J_k(S) = {w ∈ S * F_k : w(x) = 1 for all x ∈ S̄^k}`. The mixed identities depend only on the closure, with constants in `S`.
2. **Cantor case.** If `X` is a Cantor space and `S` is dense in `Homeo(X)`, then `S` is MIF: `J_k(S) = 1` for all `k`.
   - This covers `V`, `nV`, `V_{n,r}`, every Röver–Nekrashevych group `V_d(G)`, every group containing one of these in its standard action (e.g. full BBMZ rational similarity groups containing `V_d`, Belk–Hyde–Matucci's `VA`), and twisted Brin–Thompson groups. The groups containing Brin–Thompson subgroups are dense because those subgroups carry any dyadic brick onto any nonempty clopen set.
3. **One-dimensional tame case.** Let `X = [0,1]` with `S ≤ Homeo_+(X)`, or `X = S^1` with `S ≤ Homeo_+(S^1)`. Suppose:
   - `S` is dense in `Homeo_+(X)`;
   - `S` is generated by finitely many homeomorphisms with semialgebraic graphs defined over real algebraic numbers (piecewise-linear with algebraic breakpoints and slopes, piecewise projective, and so on).

   Then every `J_k(S)` is recursive, uniformly in `k` and the defining data.
   - Every group containing `F` (on the interval) or `T` (on the circle) in its standard action is dense.
   - This covers `F` and `T` again, and also every dense Higman–Thompson, Stein or algebraic-irrational-slope group of this kind, and every dense piecewise-projective group with algebraic coefficients. Density of the groups not containing `F` or `T` is recalled, not re-checked.
4. **Consequence.** No finitely presented simple group in the classes of 2 or 3 witnesses `fp-simple-group-with-nonrecursive-mixed-identities`. For each of them the kernel of the BFFHZ Theorem C action is recursive. An automorphism acts trivially iff it changes each free generator by an `n`-variable mixed identity, as in `thompson-t-relative-automorphism-kernel-recursive-proof`. Recursive `J_n` therefore gives a recursive kernel, and for MIF groups the kernel is trivial. Compare `a2-kernel-removal-forces-recursive-kernel` (c). So the computability obstruction to BFFHZ Question 3.2 and to the first part of FFWZ Question 5.9 does not arise.

## Proof

**1.** `Homeo(X)` is a topological group, so `x ↦ w(x)` is continuous on `Homeo(X)^k`, and `{x : w(x) = 1}` is closed. It contains `S^k` iff it contains `S̄^k`.

**2.** Write `w = c_0 x^{n_1} c_1 ⋯ x^{n_m} c_m` in reduced form, with `n_i ≠ 0` and `c_i ≠ 1` for `0 < i < m`. Expand each power into single letters. By 1 and density, it suffices to find `x ∈ Homeo(X)` and `p_0` with `w(x)p_0 ≠ p_0`.
- Follow the trajectory of `p_0` from the right, building a finite partial injection `φ` for `x`. Each `x^{±1}`-step lands at a point outside the current domain, resp. image, of `φ`; there we choose the image freshly.
- At the last step of each block `x^{n_i}`, choose the new point inside the nonempty open set `supp(c_{i-1})`. Also avoid the finitely many points that would make the next point `c_{i-1}(r)` hit `dom φ ∪ im φ ∪ {p_0}`.
- The Cantor space has no isolated points, so every avoidance is possible. The trajectory never closes, and the final point differs from `p_0`.
- Any finite partial injection of a Cantor space extends to a homeomorphism, by exchanging small disjoint clopen neighbourhoods. For `k` variables, build `k` partial injections the same way.

**3.** By 1 and density, `w ∉ J_k(S)` iff some `x ∈ Homeo_+(X)^k` and `p_0` have `w(x)p_0 ≠ p_0`.
- **Unknowns.** Write `w` over the generators of `S` and the `x_i^{±1}`, of length `L`. Take the trajectory points `p_0, …, p_L` as unknowns.
- **Conditions.**
  - Each generator step imposes `(p_{j-1}, p_j) ∈ graph(g^{±1})`, which is semialgebraic.
  - For each `i`, the pairs attached to `x_i`-steps must form a finite partial injection that preserves the order (on `[0,1]`, fixing `0` and `1` if they occur) or the cyclic order (on `S^1`, parametrized by `[0,1)`).
  - Finally, `p_L ≠ p_0`.
- **Extension.** A finite partial injection preserving the order, resp. the cyclic order, extends to an element of `Homeo_+(X)` by piecewise-linear interpolation. So the conditions exactly describe non-identities.
- **Decision.** The conditions form an existential sentence in `(R, +, ·, <)` with real algebraic parameters, computable from `w`. Tarski–Seidenberg decides it. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

- **What mixed identities see.** The mixed identities of a host are an invariant of its closure in a homeomorphism group, not of the group itself.
- **Where a non-recursive J_k could live.** Flexible closures (Cantor, or any closure extending finite partial injections freely) give no identities. Order-type closures give only identities decided by real quantifier elimination. So a finitely presented simple group with non-recursive mixed identities must act rigidly, with closure-level structure beyond order, or have non-tame generators.
- **Consequence for BH ⇒ PBH.** Along the FFWZ Theorem C route, BH ⇒ PBH meets no computability obstruction from any Thompson-type host or from the MIF lattice hosts (`burger-mozes-simple-lattices-are-highly-transitive`, `fp-simple-kac-moody-lattices-are-mif`). This matches `fp-decidable-group-whose-e-is-not-recursively-presented`: universal statements over a whole group are fragile, and their only robust content lives in the closure of the host.
