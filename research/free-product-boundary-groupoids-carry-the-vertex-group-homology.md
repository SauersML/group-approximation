## Setting

Fix `k >= 2` and a countably infinite group `B`. Put `A = Z/k` and `D = A * B`.
- **The tree.** Let `T` be the Bass–Serre tree: vertices `D/A ⊔ D/B`, edges `D`, and the
  edge `d` joins `dA` and `dB`. Edge stabilizers are trivial. `A`-vertices have valence
  `k`; `B`-vertices have infinite valence.
- **The space.** `X` is the set of ends of `T` together with its `B`-vertices. For a
  directed edge, its half-tree is the set of points of `X` on its head side. Half-trees
  and their complements form a subbasis of clopen sets.
  - Equivalently, `X` is the closure of the rays in the product, over all edges, of the
    two complementary half-trees. That is Reid's definition.
  - A-vertices are not limits of rays, because they have finite valence.
  - A basic neighbourhood of a `B`-vertex `w` is `w` together with all but finitely many
    branches at `w`.
- **Base pieces.** Let `v = 1B`, `u = 1A`, and let `e` be the edge `1`. Let `H` be the
  half-tree of `e` on the `u` side and `K = X \ H`. Then:

      (i)  K = {v} ⊔ ⊔_{b ∈ B\1} bH,        (ii)  H = ⊔_{a ∈ A\1} aK.

- **Groupoid.** `G = D ⋉ X` is the transformation groupoid, and `F = [[G]]` is its
  topological full group, with commutator subgroup `D(F)`.

## Theorem

1. **Dynamics.**
   - `X` is a Cantor set.
   - `G` is essentially principal, and equals its groupoid of germs.
   - `G` is minimal and purely infinite minimal in Li's sense.
   - If `B` is finitely generated, the action is expansive.
2. **Homology.** `H_0(G) ≅ Z` is generated by `[1_K]`, with `[1_H] = k-1` and `[1_X] = k`.
   For every `n >= 1`, the isotropy `D_v = B` induces `H_n(G) ≅ H_n(B; Z)`.
3. **The full group.**
   - `D(F)` is simple, and `D(F) = A(G)`. It is finitely generated if `B` is.
   - `B ≤ F` as the isotropy of `v`. Every perfect subgroup of `B` lies in `D(F)`.
   - By Li there is an exact sequence

         H_2(D(F)) → H_2(B; Z) → Z/2 → F_ab → B_ab → 0.

     So `F_ab` maps onto `B_ab` with kernel of order at most 2. If `H_2(B; Z) = 0`, then
     `F_ab` is an extension of `B_ab` by `Z/2`.
4. **Rational homology.** This is Li, arXiv:2209.08087, Corollaries `cor:RatHom` and
   `cor:DRatHom`, with the intro statement read at source (TeX l.682–690, sha256 `8e04cdb5…0a6a56`):
   - `H_*(F; Q) ≅ Ext(H^odd_*(B; Q)) ⊗ Sym(H^even_{*>0}(B; Q))`;
   - `H_*(D(F); Q)` is the same with odd degrees `> 1` only.

## Proof

**1. Dynamics.**
- **Cantor.** `X` is compact and metrizable, because the edge set is countable. It is
  totally disconnected by the subbasis. It has no isolated points, since every half-tree
  is infinite.
- **Essentially principal.**
  - A nontrivial element conjugate into `A` fixes an `A`-vertex. It permutes that
    vertex's `k` branches freely, so it fixes no point of `X`.
  - A nontrivial element conjugate into `B` fixes exactly one `B`-vertex and permutes the
    branches there freely.
  - A hyperbolic element fixes exactly two ends.
  - So every `d ≠ 1` has at most two fixed points. Hence `G` is effective, equals its
    groupoid of germs, and its points with trivial isotropy are dense.
- **Minimal.** Every orbit meets `K` and `H`:
  - a point of `aK ⊂ H` is sent into `K` by `a^{-1}`;
  - `a·v ∈ H` for `a ≠ 1`;
  - a point of `bH ⊂ K` is sent into `H` by `b^{-1}`.

  Every half-tree is a translate of `H` or `K`, since there is one edge orbit. So every
  orbit meets every half-tree. Every nonempty open set contains a half-tree. So every
  orbit is dense.
- **Purely infinite minimal.** Write `U ≼ V` if a compact open bisection has source `U`
  and range inside `V`.
  - `K ≼ H` by `aK ⊂ H`, and `H ≼ K` by `bH ⊂ K`, for `a ≠ 1`, `b ≠ 1`.
  - `X = ⊔_{a∈A} aK ≼ K`. Choose distinct `b_1, …, b_k ∈ B \ 1` and `a' ≠ 1`, and send
    the piece `a_i K` to `b_i a' K ⊂ b_i H ⊂ K` by `b_i a' a_i^{-1}`. The ranges are
    disjoint.
  - So `X` is `≼` every half-tree, and every clopen `U ⊆ X` is `≼` every nonempty clopen
    `V`. This is Li's definition. It implies Matui's: take disjoint nonempty
    `V_1, V_2 ⊂ U` and use `U ≼ V_1` and `U ≼ V_2`.
- **Expansive** (`B` finitely generated). The map `x ↦ (1_H(dx))_{d∈D}` is continuous and
  equivariant. It is injective, because two points of `X` are separated by some edge and
  half-trees are the sets `dH` and `dK = X \ dH`. So `(D, X)` is a subshift, which is
  item 6(4) of `nekrashevych-expansive-groupoid-criteria`.

**2. Mayer–Vietoris.** Let `M = C(X, Z)`, with `(d·f)(x) = f(d^{-1}x)`. By
`matui-groupoid-homology-transformation-and-reduction`, `H_n(G) = H_n(D; M)`.
- **The sequence.** The tree gives an exact sequence of `Z[D]`-modules
  `0 → Z[D] → Z[D/A] ⊕ Z[D/B] → Z → 0`.
- **Tor.** Apply `Tor^{Z[D]}_*(−, M)`. Shapiro gives `Tor_n(Z[D/P], M) = H_n(P; M)`, and
  `Tor_n(Z[D], M) = 0` for `n >= 1`. So:
  - `H_n(D;M) = H_n(A;M) ⊕ H_n(B;M)` for `n >= 2`;
  - there is an exact sequence
    `0 → H_1(A;M) ⊕ H_1(B;M) → H_1(D;M) → M →∂ M_A ⊕ M_B → H_0(D;M) → 0`.
- **As an A-module.** By (ii), `M = ⊕_{a∈A} C(aK, Z)` is induced from the trivial group.
  So `H_n(A; M) = 0` for `n >= 1`, and `f ↦ Σ_{a∈A} f(a·)|_K` identifies `M_A` with
  `C(K, Z)`.
- **As a B-module.** A locally constant `f` equals `f(v)` on all but finitely many
  branches `bH`. So by (i), `M = Z·1 ⊕ ⊕_{b∈B} C(bH, Z)`. Hence `H_n(B; M) = H_n(B; Z)`
  for `n >= 1`. Also `f ↦ (f(v), Σ_b (f − f(v))(b·)|_H)` identifies `M_B` with
  `Z ⊕ C(H, Z)`.
- **Conclusion.** Item 2 for `n >= 2` follows, and `H_1(B; Z) ⊆ H_1(G)` with cokernel
  `ker ∂`.

**3. `ker ∂ = 0`.** Let `f ∈ ker ∂`.
- **Equations.** `[f]_B = 0` gives `f(v) = 0` and `Σ_{b∈B} f(bx) = 0` for `x ∈ H`.
  `[f]_A = 0` gives `Σ_{a∈A} f(ay) = 0` for `y ∈ K`. The `b`-sum is finite, because `f`
  vanishes near `v`.
- **Recursion.** Combining the two, for every `x ∈ H`:

      f(x) = Σ_{b≠1} Σ_{a≠1} f(a b x).        (*)

- **Coordinates.** By (i) and (ii), a point of `H` is a sequence of pairs
  `(a_1, b_1), (a_2, b_2), …` with `a_i ∈ A \ 1` and `b_i ∈ (B \ 1) ∪ {stop}`. It is
  either infinite (an end) or ends with `stop` (a `B`-vertex). The map `x ↦ abx`
  prepends the pair `(a, b)`.
- **Descent.**
  - `f` is locally constant, so it is constant on the pieces of a finite partition of `H`
    into vertex neighbourhoods and half-trees. Membership in each piece is read from
    boundedly many pairs. So for some `m`, `f(x)` depends only on the first `m` pairs of
    `x`.
  - By (*), `f(x)` then depends only on the first `m − 1` pairs.
  - Descending to `m = 0`, `f` is constant on `H`, say equal to `κ`. The `A`-equation at
    `y = v` gives `0 = f(v) = −(k−1)κ`. So `κ = 0`, and then `f|_K = 0` as well.

**4. `H_0 ≅ Z`.**
- **Generation.**
  - Every clopen set is a finite disjoint union of vertex neighbourhoods `C(w; F)` (the
    `B`-vertex `w` with a finite set `F` of branches removed).
  - Translating `w` to `v` gives `[C(w; F)] = [1_X] − |F|·[1_H]`.
  - By (ii), `[1_H] = (k−1)[1_K]`, so `[1_X] = k[1_K]`.
  - So `[1_K]` generates `H_0`.
- **An invariant functional.** Define `λ_K : C(K, Z) → Z` and `λ_H : C(H, Z) → Z` by

      λ_K(φ) = φ(v) + Σ_{b≠1} ( λ_H(φ(b·)|_H) − (k−1)φ(v) ),     λ_H(ψ) = Σ_{a≠1} λ_K(ψ(a·)|_K).

  - The sums are finite.
  - The recursion stops, by local constancy, at the base values `λ_K(c) = c` and
    `λ_H(c) = (k−1)c`, which are self-consistent.
  - Put `μ(f) = λ_K(f|_K) + λ_H(f|_H)`.
  - Then `μ(f) = Σ_{a∈A} λ_K(f(a·)|_K)`, which is `A`-invariant. Also
    `μ(f) = k f(v) + Σ_{b∈B} (λ_H(f(b·)|_H) − (k−1)f(v))`, which is `B`-invariant.
  - So `μ` is `D`-invariant, with `μ(1_K) = 1`. On clopens it gives
    `μ(C(w; F)) = k − |F|(k−1)`. This is a signed "Euler measure", negative for large
    `|F|`.

**5. The full group.**
- **Simplicity.** `D(F)` is simple by `matui-purely-infinite-minimal-full-groups-have-simple-d`.
- **Equality with `A(G)`.** A 3-cycle is a commutator in `S_3`, so `A(G) ≤ D(F)`.
  `A(G)` is normal in `F`, so by the same import `A(G) ⊇ D(F)`.
- **Finite generation.** `A(G)` is finitely generated when the groupoid is expansive
  (`nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups`, Theorem 5.6; orbits are infinite).
- **Perfect subgroups.** The image of a perfect subgroup of `B ≤ F` in the abelian group
  `F_ab` is trivial.
- **The exact sequence.** It is `li-ah-exact-sequence-for-minimal-comparison-groupoids`
  with `H_1(G) = H_1(B)`, `H_2(G) = H_2(B)` and `H_0(G; Z/2) = H_0(G) ⊗ Z/2 = Z/2`. The
  last equality holds because the chain groups `C_c(G^(n), Z)` are free abelian. `∎`

## Lesson for general BH

- **Vertex groups and homology.** Infinite vertex groups of a free product become points
  of the compactified boundary and carry their whole homology into the groupoid. Finite
  vertex groups are induced and contribute nothing: with `B` finite this is Reid's
  Higman–Thompson family, which is acyclic in positive degrees.
- **An isotropy host.** So `Z/2 * B` is a universal isotropy host. Every countable `B` is
  the full isotropy of a purely infinite minimal Cantor groupoid, expansive when `B` is
  finitely generated. Its simple derived full group contains every perfect subgroup of
  `B`, and its homology is `Z ⊕ H_{>=1}(B)`.
- **Consequence for Boone–Higman.** Finite presentation of such hosts cannot be read off
  from the isotropy or from groupoid homology.
  - If `B` is finitely generated, perfect and has unsolvable word problem, then `D(F)` is a
    finitely generated simple group containing `B`. It has unsolvable word problem, so it
    is not finitely presented. Every invariant computed above depends only on `H_*(B)`.
  - Li's `F_n` theorem (arXiv:2110.04505, `intro:Gars`, `intro:ZS`) needs a locally
    finite Garside family. In the natural category of paths of this groupoid, a
    `B`-vertex is an infinite emitter, so that hypothesis fails.
  - So a BH host built on infinite emitters must prove finite presentation by an argument
    that sees the word problem of the isotropy.
- **Necessary condition (added 2026-09-18).** If `F` or `D(F)` is finitely presented, then
  `B` is *isolated*: finitely presented, and every nontrivial normal subgroup meets a fixed
  finite set. This is `free-product-emitter-full-groups-need-isolated-vertex-groups`.
  - So `B = Z` (Reid's group) fails, and so does every infinite residually finite or
    hyperbolic `B`.
  - It remains open whether some infinite isolated `B`, for example a finitely presented
    simple group, gives a finitely presented `D(F)`.
