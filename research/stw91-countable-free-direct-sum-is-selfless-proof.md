Write `G_n=F_2^n`, embedded as the first `n` coordinates of `G`.  The free
group `F_2` is acylindrically hyperbolic with trivial finite radical, so it
has Ozawa's PHP property.  PHP is closed under finite direct products;
hence every `G_n` has PHP and `C*_r(G_n)` is selfless.  Since
`G=union_n G_n`, `stw91-directed-unions-of-selfless-groups` makes
`C*_r(G)` selfless and therefore simple.

We prove nonlinearity without a representation-theoretic classification.
Suppose that an injective homomorphism

```text
rho:G -> GL_d(k)                                       (2)
```

exists.  Let `H_j` be the image under `rho` of the `j`-th copy of `F_2`, and
let `R_n` be the unital `k`-subalgebra of `M_d(k)` generated by
`H_1,...,H_n`.  Distinct `H_j` commute elementwise.  If `R_{n+1}=R_n`, then
the algebra generated by `H_{n+1}` lies both in `R_n` and in its commutant,
hence in the commutative center `Z(R_n)`.  This would make `H_{n+1}`
abelian, contradicting injectivity of (2) on that copy of `F_2`.  Therefore

```text
R_1 proper_subset R_2 proper_subset ... .              (3)
```

But (3) is an infinite strictly increasing chain of vector subspaces inside
the `d^2`-dimensional space `M_d(k)`, an impossibility.  This proves that `G`
is not linear over any field.

Finally, every `g in G` has finite support.  Its centralizer contains the
direct sum of all coordinate copies of `F_2` outside that support, and so is
not virtually cyclic.  A non-virtually-cyclic acylindrically hyperbolic group
has a loxodromic element for a non-elementary acylindrical action, while the
centralizer of every loxodromic element in an acylindrical action is
virtually cyclic.  Thus `G` cannot be acylindrically hyperbolic.

**Trust boundary.**  Selflessness uses Ozawa's PHP results and Robert's
directed-union theorem through the two prerequisite nodes.  The nonlinear
and non-acylindrically-hyperbolic separations are proved directly, except for
the standard virtually-cyclic-centralizer theorem for loxodromic elements of
acylindrical actions.
