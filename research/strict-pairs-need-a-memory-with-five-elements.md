**ESTABLISHED** by `strict-pairs-need-a-memory-with-five-elements-proof` (computer-assisted; the separation
witnesses are replayed by an independent checker).

**Theorem.** Let `G` be any group, `A` any finite alphabet, and `tau`, `sigma` cellular automata on `A^G`
with memories `M` and `S` such that `sigma tau = id`. If `|M| <= 4` and `|S| <= 4`, then `tau` is surjective.

Equivalently, a counterexample to Gottschalk's conjecture has an injective automaton or a left inverse with a memory
of at least five elements. The same then holds for every left inverse of that automaton.

**Method (rule-free).** After normalizing `1` into both memories and padding, a strict pair of this shape has a
four-by-four forward table `T`. It lives on the table group `P(T)`, whose reverse table refines the ambient one.
If finite quotients of `P(T)` separate every pair of reverse products that are distinct in `P(T)`, strict data
at `T` would give an injective, non-surjective automaton over a finite group, which is impossible. This holds
whatever the alphabet and the rules. The census enumerates all 17,427,192 rook-independent four-by-four tables,
reduces them to 246,595 orbits under 72 symmetries (relabeling and inversion), and removes unrealizable tables by
a sound difference closure. Every surviving table is then either unrealizable or has its reverse table
separated by explicit permutation representations.

**What is new.**
- Every alphabet at once. The earlier censuses solve rules over two or three symbols by SAT and stop at shape
  `3x3` for three symbols and at `3x4` for two.
- Shape `4x4`, which no earlier census enumerated
  (`research/artifacts/gk3-strict-census-4-2026-09-14.md`, table in section 5).

**What it rules out, precisely.** Every strict pair, over any group and alphabet, with both memory sets of size at
most four. With `surjunctive-decoder-subgroups-force-surjectivity`, a counterexample needs a nonsurjunctive group
generated by the differences of a decoder memory, and a memory of at least five points on one side.

**Gap tables.** A table is a *gap table* when some pair of reverse products is distinct in `P(T)` but merged in
every finite quotient. Only gap tables can host counterexamples. There are none at shape `4x4` or below.
