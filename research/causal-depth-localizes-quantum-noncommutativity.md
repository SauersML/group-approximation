## Setting

- `Λ = <S>` is finitely generated, with balls `B_r`.
- `X ⊆ (A × C)^Λ` is an SFT, with base letters in `A` and computation letters in `C`.
- For a `D`-quantum family `E_(a,c)(z)`, the marginals are `B_a(z) = Σ_c E_(a,c)(z)` and
  `C_c(z) = Σ_a E_(a,c)(z)`.
- `Y_D ⊆ A^Λ` is the SFT whose forbidden patterns are the `B_D`-patterns absent from the base
  projection `π_A(X)`.

**Causal rule (radius `R`).**
- **Window and predecessors.** There is a finite `W ⊆ B_R` containing `1`. For each pattern `p` on
  `W` there is a set of *predecessors* `P(p) ⊆ W \ {1}` and a map `Θ_p : C^(P(p)) → C`.
- **The rule.** For every `x ∈ X` and every `z`: `x_C(z) = Θ_p(x_C|_(zP(p)))`, where `p` is the base
  pattern of `x` on `zW`.
- **Seeds.** Sites with `P(p) = ∅` are *seeds*. Their computation letter is a function of the base.

**Causal depth.** For a base configuration `ω ∈ Y_D`, draw an arrow `z → zu` for each
`u ∈ P(ω|_(zW))`. Then `depth_ω(z) ∈ {0, 1, …, ∞}` is the supremum of the lengths of arrow paths
from `z`. For each `n`, `{ω : depth_ω(z) <= n}` is clopen, since it depends on `ω` on `zB_((n+1)R)`.
On it the letter `val_ω(z)` computed by the rule is well defined.

**Commuting-base hypothesis (B).** The base marginals `B_a(z)` pairwise commute in every `D`-quantum
family. This holds, for example:
- if `π_A(X)` is a `D`-quantum rigid SFT and the computation layer adds no base constraint;
- in plane-constant designs, by `copy-constraints-make-simulation-skeleton-and-data-commute`.

## Theorem

Let `D >= R` and assume (B). Fix a `D`-quantum family on `W_0`, and let `𝒜` be the commutative
algebra generated by the base marginals. Then `𝒜` is a quotient of `LC(Y_D, k)`. Write `ι(U)` for
the image of a clopen `U`, and `e_n(z) = ι{depth(z) <= n}`.

1. **Finite depth is classical.** For all `z`, `c` and `n`,

   ```text
   e_n(z) C_c(z) = C_c(z) e_n(z) = ι{ω : depth_ω(z) <= n, val_ω(z) = c}  ∈ 𝒜 .
   ```

2. **Commutators live at infinite depth.** For all sites `z, z'`, all letters, and every `n`,
   `e_n(z) e_n(z')` annihilates, on both sides, the commutator of any two family members at `z`
   and `z'`.
3. **Bounded depth gives rigidity.** Suppose every `ω ∈ Y_D` has finite depth at every site. By
   compactness the depth is then bounded by some `N`. So `e_N = I`, and every `C_c(z)` lies in `𝒜`.
   The family commutes: `X` is `D`-quantum rigid, given (B).
4. **Finite-dimensional splitting.** If `dim W_0 < ∞`, then `W_0 = W_fin ⊕ W_∞` with two properties.
   - Both summands are invariant under the whole family, and the family commutes on `W_fin`.
   - The base spectrum of `W_∞` consists of points `ω ∈ Y_D` with `depth_ω(z) = ∞` at some site
     `z`. The spectrum is the finitely many points `ω_π` attached to the minimal idempotents `π` of
     `𝒜`.

## Reading

- **Where E2 lives in any seeded simulation.** Computations started at seeds are classical, whatever
  the module. Every obstruction to rigidity of a seeded coding sits on configurations with a site of
  infinite depth. There, a computation reaches back forever without meeting a seed. These are the
  bi-infinite paths of Barbieri–Sablik–Salo's obstacle (a), in operator form.
- **Relation to forced ambiguity.** Item 4 of `bounded-determination-simulations-only-realize-sft-shadows`
  says a useful simulation cannot have bounded determination. In a causal coding, the undetermined
  sites are exactly the infinite-depth sites. This node shows that nothing else can fail
  operator-theoretically.
- **Kill test at infinite depth.** Take `ω` with an infinite-depth region, and suppose the
  computation there admits two completions that differ on sets at distance `> 2D`. Then the
  far-modification Lemma of `minimal-sfts-with-a-finite-modification-are-not-quantum-rigid` refutes
  rigidity. So rigid seeded codings need **unsplittable infinite-depth computation**.
- **Hypotheses not used.** Neither `char k` nor amenability of `Λ` is used. `dim W_0` is used only in
  item 4.

## Lesson for general BH

Determinism does not need to be bounded, or single-sourced, to transport rigidity. Any causal
determination that terminates at seeds is classical at every finite depth, as an operator identity,
and it needs no centrality argument. So in every coding built from seeded computations, gate E2 is
exactly the question at infinite depth. It is the operator form of the classical fact that simulation
of a non-SFT input must leave computation undetermined along infinite backward chains. Designs should
therefore be judged by one invariant: the structure of their infinite-depth locus.
- A thin locus, such as one spine per end, is good.
- Independent far-apart infinite-depth regions are fatal.
- Unbounded but finite depth is harmless.
