**ESTABLISHED** through `q-free-normal-towers-omit-aff-q-proof`. Not
independently reviewed. The arguments are elementary and no priority is
claimed.

Write *Q-free* for "contains no subgroup isomorphic to `(Q,+)`". Let `𝒯` be the
class of groups `E` that have a chain

    E = E_0 >= E_1 >= ... >= E_r = 1

in which **every `E_i` is normal in `E`** (not merely in `E_{i-1}`) and every
factor `E_i / E_{i+1}` is Q-free.

## Statement

1. **Closure.** `𝒯` contains every Q-free group and is closed under
   - subgroups and finite direct products;
   - extensions with Q-free kernel: if `K` is a Q-free normal subgroup of `E` and
     `E/K ∈ 𝒯`, then `E ∈ 𝒯`;
   - the continuous `V`-wreath `H ↦ LC(C,H) ⋊ V`. Here `C = {0,1}^N`,
     `LC(C,H)` is the group of locally constant maps `C -> H` under pointwise
     product, and Thompson's group `V` acts by precomposition. Equivalently,
     `LC(C,H) ⋊ V` is the group of bijections `(x,y) ↦ (v(x), f(x)·y)` of `C × H`.

   In particular `LC(C,H)` is Q-free whenever `H` is.
2. **Exclusion.** No group in `𝒯` contains `Aff(Q) = Q ⋊ Q^x`, the Borel group
   `B_2(Q)` of upper triangular matrices in `SL_2(Q)`, or the image of `B_2(Q)`
   in `PSL_2(Q)`. Hence no group in `𝒯` contains `GL_n(Q)`, `SL_n(Q)`,
   `PGL_n(Q)` or `PSL_n(Q)` for any `n >= 2`.
3. **Sharpness.** `(Q,+) ∈ 𝒯`, through `Q >= Z >= 0`. Also `U_3(Q) ∈ 𝒯`, through
   `U_3(Q) >= U_1 >= U_2 >= 1`, where
   - `U_1` is the group of unitriangular matrices with integer `(1,2)` and `(2,3)`
     entries and rational `(1,3)` entry, and
   - `U_2 = e_13(Z)`.

   The factors are `(Q/Z)^2`, a central extension of `Z^2` by `Q/Z`, and `Z`, all
   Q-free. So the two-step exclusion of `U_3(Q)` in
   `lifts-add-no-unipotent-divisibility` fails for towers of length three.

## Examples in 𝒯

- Every Q-free group: residually finite groups, `V`, `T`, `F`, and groups whose
  abelian subgroups are finitely generated.
- Central lifts of groups in `𝒯` by a Q-free centre, e.g. `T-bar`, a central
  extension of `T` by `Z`.
- `LC(C, Z) ⋊ V` and its variants `LC(C, <h>) ⋊ V`. These are the groups that
  carry the copies of `(Q,+)` in `twisted-bt-point-fixing-actor-elements-are-divisible`
  and in Kojima--Sheng-type roots: the root of order `N` is a free rotation of
  `N` clopen pieces in `V` together with one application of `h` per cycle.
- Every iterate `LC(C, LC(C, ... LC(C,H) ⋊ V ...) ⋊ V) ⋊ V` over a Q-free `H`,
  and every subgroup of a finite product of such groups.

## Consequence for the root and its stepping stones

A host for `Aff(Q)`, and so for `GL_n(Q)`, cannot be assembled from Q-free
groups by any finite tower of normal extensions, subgroups, finite products and
continuous `V`-wreaths. The translation subgroup's divisibility has to live in
a group that admits no such tower.

Consider a twisted Brin--Thompson group `SV_G` and fix a coordinate `*`. The
elements that act as `v` on `C_*` and, on the other coordinates, by a
homeomorphism from a group `Π` depending locally constantly on `x_*`, form a
copy of `LC(C, Π) ⋊ V` inside `SV_G`. Such a subgroup is in `𝒯` whenever `Π` is.

So the cocycle devices produce copies of `(Q,+)` but never `Aff(Q)`, when the
fibre group is Q-free: for instance when `Π` is generated by coordinate
permutations from a residually finite actor, which is the situation of O1.
Constructions of `Aff(Q)` in `nV` or `SV_G` must use elements that entangle at
least two coordinates carrying `V`. This matches the gates recorded in
`rational-stepping-stones-in-nv-need-bs12-or-heisenberg`.

For `U_3(Q)` the tower obstruction stops at two steps.

**Remark (the first two levels of the U_3(Q) tower occur in SV_G).** Let
`x, y ∈ G` generate a copy of `H_3(Z)` with `z = [x,y]`, and suppose `x` and `y`
fix a point `* ∈ S`. Then `U_1`, the central product of `H_3(Z)` and `(Q,+)`
over `z`, embeds in `SV_G`. Proof:
- Item 1 of `twisted-bt-point-fixing-actor-elements-are-divisible` puts `z` in a
  copy `R ≅ (Q,+)` of `SV_G`, whose elements act as `V` on `C_*` and by powers of
  `z` on the other coordinates, the exponent depending only on `x_*`.
- `x` and `y` fix `*`, so they commute with the `V` part on `C_*`. They commute
  with the powers of `z` because `z` is central in `<x,y>`. So `R` centralizes
  `<x,y>`, and `H_3(Z) × Q -> SV_G` factors through the central product `U_1`.
- A nontrivial normal subgroup of the nilpotent group `U_1` meets its centre
  `e_13(Q)`, and `R` is a faithful copy of `Q`. So the map is injective.

Whether all of `U_3(Q)` embeds in `SV_G` or `nV` stays open
(`u3-q-embeds-in-fp-simple-group`,
`heisenberg-group-embeds-in-some-brin-thompson-group`).
