Notation as in the claim: `𝒫` is the smallest class containing the LEA groups and closed under
(P1) subgroups, (P2) directed unions, (P3) extensions with amenable quotient, (P4) fundamental
groups of graphs of groups, and (P5) finite direct products.

**Standard facts used.** Let `K` be a group with property (T).
- (T1) `K` is finitely generated, and every quotient of `K` has (T).
- (T2) A countable amenable group with (T) is finite.
- (T3) Every finite-index subgroup of `K` has (T). It is finitely presented if `K` is.
- (T4) `K` has Serre's property FA: every action of `K` on a tree without inversions fixes a
  vertex (Watatani 1982).
- (R) A group containing a residually finite subgroup of finite index is residually finite.

**Ranks.** Put `𝒫_0 = LEA`. `𝒫_{β+1}` is `𝒫_β` together with every group obtained from members of
`𝒫_β` by one application of (P1)–(P5). For limit `λ`, `𝒫_λ = ∪_{β<λ} 𝒫_β`. Then `𝒫 = ∪_β 𝒫_β`,
since this union is closed under (P1)–(P5): each operation uses at most a set of members, and
their ranks are bounded. The *rank* of `G ∈ 𝒫` is the least `β` with `G ∈ 𝒫_β`. It is never a
limit ordinal, so it is `0` or a successor.

**Stronger statement.** For every finite family `G_1, …, G_m` of groups in `𝒫`, every finitely
presented subgroup `K ≤ G_1 × ⋯ × G_m` with (T) is residually finite. The claim is the case
`m = 1`.

To a family attach `(α, k)`, where `α` is the largest rank of the `G_i` and `k` is the number of
`i` of rank `α`. Induct on `(α, k)` in the lexicographic order, which is a well-order.

**Base `α = 0`: all `G_i` are LEA.**
1. A finite product of LEA groups is LEA: combine the local embeddings coordinatewise into a
   product of amenable groups, which is amenable. A subgroup of an LEA group is LEA, so `K` is LEA.
2. Let `K = ⟨S | R⟩` with `S` and `R` finite, and let `g ∈ K ∖ {e}`. Fix words over `S` for `g`
   and for each relator. Let `F ⊆ K` be the finite set of all elements represented by prefixes
   of these words, together with `S ∪ S^{-1} ∪ {e}`.
3. Take a local embedding `φ : F → A` into an amenable group. It is injective on `F` and
   multiplicative on pairs whose product stays in `F`. From `φ(e)² = φ(e)` we get `φ(e) = e`.
4. Define `ψ(s) = φ(s)` for `s ∈ S`.
   - Along a relator `r = s_1 ⋯ s_k` every prefix lies in `F`, so by induction on the prefix
     length `ψ(s_1 ⋯ s_j) = φ(s_1 ⋯ s_j)`. So `ψ(r) = φ(e) = e`.
   - So `ψ` extends to a homomorphism `K → A`. The same prefix argument gives
     `ψ(g) = φ(g) ≠ φ(e) = e`.
5. `ψ(K)` is amenable (a subgroup of `A`) and has (T) by (T1), so it is finite by (T2). So finite
   quotients of `K` separate `g` from `e`, and `K` is residually finite.

**Inductive step `α ≥ 1`.** Reorder so that `G_1` has rank `α = β + 1`. Then `G_1` arises from
members of `𝒫_β` by one operation.
- **(P1)** `G_1 ≤ H` with `H ∈ 𝒫_β`. Then `K ≤ H × G_2 × ⋯ × G_m`, and the invariant drops.
- **(P2)** `G_1 = ∪_i H_i` directed, `H_i ∈ 𝒫_β`.
  - The projection `π_1(K)` is finitely generated by (T1), so `π_1(K) ≤ H_i` for some `i`.
  - Then `K ≤ H_i × G_2 × ⋯ × G_m`.
- **(P3)** `N ⊴ G_1`, `N ∈ 𝒫_β`, `G_1/N` amenable.
  - The image of `K` in `G_1/N` is amenable and has (T), so it is finite by (T1) and (T2).
  - So `K_0 = K ∩ (N × G_2 × ⋯ × G_m)` has finite index in `K`. By (T3) it is finitely presented
    with (T).
  - By induction `K_0` is residually finite, and by (R) so is `K`.
- **(P4)** `G_1 = π_1(𝔾, Y)` with vertex groups `G_v ∈ 𝒫_β`.
  - `K` acts on the Bass–Serre tree of `(𝔾, Y)` through `π_1`, without inversions.
  - By (T4) it fixes a vertex, whose stabilizer is a conjugate `x G_v x^{-1}`. So
    `π_1(K) ≤ x G_v x^{-1}`, and `K ≤ x G_v x^{-1} × G_2 × ⋯ × G_m ≅ G_v × G_2 × ⋯ × G_m`.
- **(P5)** `G_1 = H_1 × ⋯ × H_r` with `H_j ∈ 𝒫_β`. Then `K ≤ H_1 × ⋯ × H_r × G_2 × ⋯ × G_m`.

In every case the new family has all its new factors of rank at most `β < α`. So either `α` drops,
or `α` stays and `k` drops by one. By induction `K` is residually finite. ∎

**Remarks on the proof.**
- **Where (T) is used.** Only through (T1)–(T4), so the argument proves the claim for every finitely
  presented group whose finite-index subgroups have FA and no infinite amenable quotients.
- **Why amenable-kernel extensions are not included.** In (P3) the amenable part is the quotient,
  and (T) makes it finite. With an amenable kernel `N ⊴ G_1` and `G_1/N ∈ 𝒫_β`, the image of `K`
  in `G_1/N` can be infinite, and nothing forces `K ∩ N × ⋯` to have finite index. Deligne's triple
  cover shows the claim really fails for that closure (see the claim, Sharpness).
