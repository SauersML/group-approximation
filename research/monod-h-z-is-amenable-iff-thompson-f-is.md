**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed) by `monod-h-z-amenable-iff-f-proof`. The embedding step
imports Kim–Koberda–Lodha. The other direction is the established local conjugacy into F.

**Setting (Monod, arXiv:1209.5229, read at source).**
- `P_A` is the set of fixed points of hyperbolic elements of `PSL_2(A)`.
- `G(A)` consists of the homeomorphisms of `P^1(R)` that are piecewise in `PSL_2(A)`, with finitely
  many interval pieces, all of whose endpoints lie in `P_A`.
- `H(A) = G(A) ∩ H` is the stabilizer of `∞`.
- Monod's **Problem 12** reads verbatim: "Is H(Z) amenable?"

**Theorem.**
1. **(F inside H(Z).)** Let `γ(t) = (2t+1)/(t+1)`. Its fixed points are `a = (1-√5)/2` and
   `b = (1+√5)/2`. Put `f = γ` on `[a,b]` and `f = id` elsewhere, and `g(t) = f(t-1) + 1`.
   - Then `f, g ∈ H(Z)`.
   - Their supports are `(a,b)` and `(a+1,b+1)`, with `a < a+1 < b < b+1`.
   - Both move points to the right.
   - Hence `⟨f^N, g^N⟩ ≅ F` for all sufficiently large `N`.
2. **(Equivalence.)** `H(Z)` is amenable iff `F` is. More generally, for every group `Γ` with
   `⟨f^N, g^N⟩ ≤ Γ ≤ G_Z`, and every property that passes to subgroups and to directed unions of
   countable groups, `Γ` has the property iff `F` has it. Here `G_Z` is the group of all
   finitely-piecewise `PSL_2(Z)` homeomorphisms of `R`.
3. **(Problem 12.)** Monod's Problem 12 has the same answer as the amenability problem for `F`.
   A solution of either is a solution of the other.

**What this changes.**
- The literature lists "Is H(Z) amenable?" as a problem separate from F: Monod's Problem 12, and
  Maruyama, arXiv:2302.10653, which says "its amenability is unknown". The two are one problem.
- **Class kill (host enlargement to H(Z)).** Every route that proves `F` amenable or nonamenable by
  way of `H(Z)` is circular. Examples are a Følner, Reiter or Ramsey argument, a Monod-type
  orbit-relation argument, or an EA argument on `P_Z`. Each such route dies at the transfer step.
  - The copy of `F` in part 1 makes `H(Z)` nonamenable whenever `F` is.
  - Local conjugacy into `F` makes every finitely generated subgroup of `H(Z)` a subgroup of `F` up
    to a homeomorphism.
  - The invariant is *mutual local embeddability*: `F ↪ H(Z)` and each finitely generated
    `H ≤ H(Z)` embeds in `F`.
  - This extends C4 of `integral-piecewise-projective-groups-are-locally-subgroups-of-f` from
    overgroups of Thurston's `F` to `H(Z)`, which contains no such overgroup.
- **Free consequences for H(Z).** These are remarks, not part of the established statement. They
  follow from the local-embeddability sandwich plus standard facts about `F`, which are not
  imported into the graph here:
  - `H(Z)` satisfies no law, since `F` satisfies none;
  - `H(Z)` is not elementary amenable;
  - `H(Z)` has the Haagerup property. `F` has it (Farley), and the property passes to subgroups and
    to directed unions of countable groups. This last point was already implied by the local
    conjugacy node.

**Scope.** This says nothing about whether `F` is amenable. The copy of `F` is abstract: `⟨f^N, g^N⟩`
is not conjugate in `Homeo(R)` to Thurston's model, since its breakpoints are irrational. No
priority is claimed. The embedding is a two-line application of Kim–Koberda–Lodha and may be known
to experts. The literature checks found no statement of it: Monod's PNAS paper and Maruyama 2023.
