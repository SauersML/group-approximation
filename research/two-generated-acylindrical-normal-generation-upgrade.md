**OPEN RESIDUAL ROUTER.**  Let `P` be a finitely presented torsion-free group
and `1!=p in P` with `normalClosure_P(p)=P`.  Construct in-repository (no
cited small-cancellation theorem as a premise) a homomorphism `g:P->Q` such
that `Q` is two-generated, finitely presented, torsion-free and acylindrically
hyperbolic, `g(p)!=1`, and `normalClosure_Q(g(p))=Q`.

Together with `torsion-free-embedding-normal-generation-router` (applied to
any torsion-free finitely presented `E` and `d`, then composed) and
`mf-radical-saturation-along-any-homomorphism`, this is exactly what the
torsion-free manuscript endpoint needs from routing.  It is weaker than
`internal-torsion-free-single-word-router` restricted to acylindrically
hyperbolic `P`.

Two sub-clauses can fail on their own:

1. **(2G)** two generation, finite presentation, torsion-freeness and
   `g(p)!=1`, all with `normalClosure_Q(g(p))=Q`;
2. **(AH)** acylindrical hyperbolicity of such a `Q`.

## Attempts

**SOLVED by a single amalgam with a two-elliptic generating pair (swarm-0917 w5, host-geometry).**
See `two-generated-acylindrical-normal-generation-upgrade-proof`.  Pad
the generators of `P` to pairwise distinct nontrivial `p_1,...,p_m` with
`m>=3`, and put `N=m+1`.  Then

```text
Q = (P * <s>) *_{C} F(a,b),
  s^N <-> a,   p_k s^k <-> b^{-k} a b^{k+1}   (1<=k<=m).
```

Both sides are free of rank `m+1`, by syllable counting.  `Q=<s,b>`, because
`a=s^N` gives `B`, and `B` gives every `p_k`.  `Q/normalClosure(P)` is
`<s,b | b^{-k}s^N b^{k+1}=s^k>`, which is trivial: from `r_1,r_2,r_3`,
`s^k b = b s^{k+1}` gives `bs^4=s^2b=bs^3`.  Torsion-freeness and finite
presentation come from amalgam permanence.  Acylindrical hyperbolicity comes
from Minasyan--Osin, since `C cap (bab)C(bab)^{-1}=1`: every element of `C`
begins with `a^{±1}` or `b^{-1}`, and every element of the conjugate begins
with `b`.

This evades both recorded obstructions.  The last step is an amalgam, not an
HNN extension, as perfectness forces.  The generating pair `(s,b)` consists
of elliptic elements at adjacent vertices, linked through the edge relation
`a=s^N`.  The argument proves more than the target: any nontrivial finitely
presented torsion-free `P` embeds in such a `Q` with
`normalClosure_Q(P)=Q`.

**Neumann/Higman HNN two-generation dies for the whole class (2026-09-17).**
Every classical two-generation embedding (B. H. Neumann; Higman--Neumann--Neumann;
the Chiodo/Rotman Higman embedding) ends with an HNN extension
`Q = B *_{s:A->A'}` with `g(P) <= B`.  Sending `B` to `0` and `s` to `1`
defines `epsilon:Q->>Z`.  Its kernel contains `normalClosure_Q(g(p))`, so
normal generation fails at that final step, for any choice of associated
subgroups.  The invariant is the stable-letter exponent sum.  More generally,
no valid `Q` has an epimorphism to `Z` that kills `g(p)`.

**Gadget repair regresses.**  One can kill the stable letter `s` by
amalgamating the Baumslag--Solitar chain gadget `K` of
`torsion-free-embedding-normal-generation-router` over a free pair
`<g(p),s>`.  This adds the generator `z` of `K`, and re-two-generating
reintroduces a stable letter.  In a nontrivial amalgam the edge group cannot
contain both generators, and `K` cannot be generated by a free pair `<x,y>`
with `K=normalClosure(x)`, since `K` would then be free.  So every
Bass--Serre construction that alternates "two-generate by HNN" with
"normal-generate by a gadget amalgam" never terminates.

A successful construction must therefore end with a step that is not an HNN
extension over a base containing `g(P)`.  Two possibilities are a genuine
quotient, where small cancellation or a formalized Greendlinger theory
reappears, or an amalgam whose generating pair includes an element that is
hyperbolic on the Bass--Serre tree.

**Perfection forces trees of groups.**  The embedding output `P` of `(EMB)`
is perfect.  In `P^{ab}`, `e_i=2e_i`, `t_i=2t_i`, `z=2z` and `c=2c`, so every
generator vanishes.  If `P` is perfect and `normalClosure_Q(g(p))=Q`, then
`Q^{ab}` is generated by the image of `g(p) in g([P,P]) <= [Q,Q]`, so `Q` is
perfect.  A perfect group has no epimorphism onto `Z`.  Hence every splitting
of a valid `Q` as a finite graph of groups has a tree as its underlying
graph: no HNN step of any kind can be the last one, whatever the base.  A
successful (2G) construction for this input must present `Q` as an iterated
amalgam, or as a quotient.
