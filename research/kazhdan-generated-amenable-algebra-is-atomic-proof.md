**Step 1: `P` has property (T) in the bimodule sense.**  Let `H` be a normal
`P`-`P`-bimodule and suppose `H` has unit vectors `xi_n` that are almost
central for the finite set `pi(S)`, `S` a finite generating set of `Gamma`:
`||pi(s) xi_n pi(s)^* - xi_n|| -> 0`.  The map
`gamma |-> (xi |-> pi(gamma) xi pi(gamma)^*)` is a unitary representation of
`Gamma` on `H` with almost invariant vectors, so property (T) gives a nonzero
invariant vector `xi_0`: `pi(gamma) xi_0 = xi_0 pi(gamma)` for all
`gamma in Gamma`.  The set `{x in P : x xi_0 = xi_0 x}` contains the
`*`-algebra generated by `pi(Gamma)` and is closed in the `sigma`-weak topology
of `P`, because for each `eta in H` the functionals `x |-> <x xi_0, eta>` and
`x |-> <xi_0 x, eta>` are normal (the bimodule is normal).  Hence `xi_0` is
`P`-central.  This is the Connes--Jones property (T) for `P` (A. Connes and
V. Jones, *Property T for von Neumann algebras*, Bull. London Math. Soc. 17
(1985), 57--62), with the generating set `pi(S)` as the critical set.

**Step 2: amenability gives almost central vectors in the coarse bimodule.**
A finite von Neumann algebra `P` with faithful normal trace is amenable
(injective) if and only if the trivial bimodule `L^2(P)` is weakly contained
in the coarse bimodule `L^2(P) (x) L^2(P)`, i.e. the coarse bimodule has almost
central unit vectors for every finite subset of `P` (Connes' characterization
of injectivity; see e.g. Anantharaman--Popa, *An introduction to II_1 factors*,
the chapter on amenable von Neumann algebras, where injective `<=>` almost
central vectors in the coarse bimodule is proved).

**Step 3: a finite-dimensional summand.**  By Steps 1 and 2 the coarse
bimodule has a nonzero `P`-central vector `T`.  Identify
`L^2(P) (x) L^2(P)` with the Hilbert--Schmidt operators on `L^2(P)`, the
bimodule actions being `x . T . y = x T y` (left multiplication operators on
both sides, `P` acting on `L^2(P)` in the standard way).  Centrality says
`x T = T x` for all `x in P`, so `T` is a nonzero Hilbert--Schmidt, hence
compact, operator in the commutant `P'` of `P` on `L^2(P)`.  Then `T^* T` is a
nonzero positive compact element of `P'`, and any spectral projection `e` of
`T^* T` for an interval `[c, infinity)`, `c > 0`, is a nonzero finite-rank
projection in `P'`.  Let `z` be the central support of `e` in `P'`; `z` lies
in `Z(P') = Z(P)`.  The map `P z -> P e`, `x z |-> x e`, is a `*`-isomorphism
(standard: `P z ~= P e` when `z` is the central support of `e in P'`), and
`P e` acts faithfully on the finite-dimensional space `e L^2(P)`, so `P z` is a
nonzero finite-dimensional direct summand of `P`; with a faithful trace it is a
finite direct sum of full matrix algebras.

**Step 4: exhaustion.**  Let `z_infty` be the supremum of all central
projections `z` of `P` with `P z` finite-dimensional; it is a countable
supremum (separable predual) and `P z_infty` is a countable direct sum of full
matrix algebras.  If `z_infty != 1` then `P (1 - z_infty)` is amenable (a
corner of an amenable algebra), carries the faithful normal trace
`tau(. (1-z_infty))/tau(1-z_infty)`, and is generated by the unitary
representation `gamma |-> pi(gamma)(1 - z_infty)` of the Kazhdan group
`Gamma`; Steps 1--3 produce a nonzero finite-dimensional central summand of it,
i.e. a finite-dimensional summand of `P` orthogonal to `z_infty`, contradicting
maximality.  Hence `z_infty = 1` and `P = (+)_i M_(n_i)(C)`.

**Step 5: the representation.**  Write `z_i` for the minimal central
projections and `sigma_i(gamma) = pi(gamma) z_i in U(M_(n_i))`.  Since `pi(Gamma)`
generates `P`, each `sigma_i(Gamma)` generates `M_(n_i)`, so `sigma_i` is
irreducible; if `sigma_i` and `sigma_j` were equivalent for `i != j`, the
diagonal image of `Gamma` in `M_(n_i) (+) M_(n_j)` would generate a proper
subalgebra, contradicting generation.  The weights are `lambda_i = tau(z_i) > 0`.
Conversely a direct sum of finite-dimensional representations generates a
subalgebra of `prod_i M_(n_i)`, which is atomic, hence amenable.
