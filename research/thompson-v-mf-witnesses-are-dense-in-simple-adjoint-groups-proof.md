Notation is as in the target. `sigma : G0 -> U(n)` is a nontrivial irreducible unitary
representation, `Gamma = sigma(G0)`, `K` is its closure, `K^0` the identity component,
`l = Lie(K)`, `z` the centre of `l`, `m = [l, l]`, `w_i = sigma(r_i)` for `5 <= i <= 8`, and
`D = D(sigma) < 1/2`. The inner product on `u(n)` is `<X, Y> = Re tr(X^* Y)`. Operator norms are
taken for this inner product.

## Inputs

- **[R]** `thompson-v-mf-iff-opnorm-s4-s3-defect-gap-vanishes`, items 1 to 3.
  - Every nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary representation `rho` of dimension `<= N` has
    `D(rho) >= delta_N > 0`. It has a nontrivial irreducible summand, and the defect of an orthogonal
    sum is the maximum over its summands.
  - `V` is MF iff `delta_N -> 0`.
- **[S]** `thompson-v-finitely-presented-infinite-simple`: `V` is infinite and simple. The *transfer
  tool* of `thompson-v-gap-witnesses-need-unbounded-lie-closure-proof` follows. A homomorphism from `G0`
  to a finite group that kills `r5, ..., r8` factors through `V`, and so it is trivial.
- **[LC]** `thompson-v-gap-witnesses-need-unbounded-lie-closure`, item 2. If `sigma` is irreducible and
  `K^0` is abelian, then `D >= 1/2`. From the proof route of that claim, this route also uses:
  - the standard facts (L1)-(L3), which that route recalls with textbook references;
  - Lemma O (orthogonal moves);
  - Lemma A (adjoint contraction, and the complexification of an orthogonal operator keeps its norm);
  - Lemma Z (`l = z (+) m` orthogonally, `z` is the centre of `l`, and `ad X` is skew for `<.,.>`).
- **[CL]** Only the corollary uses this: the classification of compact simple Lie algebras. It is

  > The compact Lie algebras are classified and named according to the compact real forms of the
  > complex semisimple Lie algebras. These are: A n: s u n + 1, corresponding to the special unitary
  > group B n: s o 2 n + 1, corresponding to the special orthogonal group C n: s p n, corresponding
  > to the compact symplectic group D n: s o 2 n, corresponding to the special orthogonal group
  > Compact real forms of the exceptional Lie algebras E 6, E 7, E 8, F 4, G 2.

  The quote uses only one thing: there are finitely many exceptional compact simple Lie algebras up to

## Step 1: the simple ideals of `m`

**(1.1) `m` is nonzero and has trivial centre.** `D < 1/2`, so [LC] item 2 makes `K^0` nonabelian.
Since `K^0` is generated by `exp(l)` (L2), `l` is nonabelian and `m != 0`. Now let `W` in `m`
commute with `m`. It also commutes with `z`, which is central in `l`. So `W` lies in the centre `z`
of `l`, and also in `m`, which is `z^perp` inside `l` (Lemma Z). Hence `W = 0`.

**(1.2) Orthogonal decomposition into simple ideals.**
- Let `I` be an ideal of `m`. Then `I^perp`, taken inside `m`, is an ideal as well. Indeed, for
  `X` in `m`, `Y` in `I^perp` and `W` in `I`, skewness of `ad X` gives
  `<[X, Y], W> = -<Y, [X, W]> = 0`.
- Hence `m` is an orthogonal sum `h_1 (+) ... (+) h_r` of minimal nonzero ideals, by induction on
  dimension. For `a != b`, `[h_a, h_b]` lies in `h_a ∩ h_b = 0`.
- **Each `h_a` is nonabelian.** If `h_a` were abelian, it would commute with every `h_b`, so it
  would lie in the centre of `m`, which is `0` by (1.1).
- **Each `h_a` is simple.** An ideal `J` of `h_a` satisfies
  `[m, J] = [h_a, J] ⊆ J`, so `J` is an ideal of `m`. By minimality, `J = 0` or `J = h_a`.

**(1.3) The simple ideals are canonical.** Let `J` be any simple ideal of `m`. Then
`[m, J] = sum_a [h_a, J]`, and `[h_a, J]` lies in `h_a ∩ J`. `J` is nonabelian and `[m, J] ⊇ [J, J] != 0`,
so some `h_a ∩ J` is nonzero. It is an ideal of both simple algebras `h_a` and `J`, so `h_a = J`.
Consequently every Lie algebra automorphism of `m` permutes the set `{h_1, ..., h_r}`.

**(1.4) The Killing form of each `h_a` is negative definite.**
- Let `B(X, Y) = tr_(h_a)(ad X ad Y)` for `X, Y` in `h_a`. The restriction of `<.,.>` to `h_a` is
  invariant and `ad X` is skew for it, so `B(X, X) = -||ad X||_HS^2 <= 0`.
- Equality forces `ad X = 0` on `h_a`. Since `X` also commutes with every `h_b`, `b != a`, it is
  central in `m`, and so `X = 0` by (1.1).

**(1.5) Every derivation of `h = h_a` is inner.**
- Let `M` be the Lie algebra of derivations of `h`, and `I = ad(h)`. The map `ad` is injective
  because `h` has trivial centre. `I` is an ideal of `M`, because `[Delta, ad X] = ad(Delta X)`.
- Put `beta(Delta, E) = tr_h(Delta E)` on `M`. Then `beta(ad X, ad Y) = B(X, Y)`, which is
  nondegenerate by (1.4).
- For `Delta` in `M`, the functional `X -> beta(Delta, ad X)` equals `B(Y, .)` for a unique `Y` in
  `h`, and `Delta' = Delta - ad Y` satisfies `beta(Delta', ad X) = 0` for all `X`.
- For such a `Delta'` and all `X, Y` in `h`,

  ```text
  B(Delta' X, Y) = beta(ad(Delta' X), ad Y) = beta([Delta', ad X], ad Y)
                 = tr(Delta' ad X ad Y - ad X Delta' ad Y) = tr(Delta' [ad X, ad Y])
                 = beta(Delta', ad [X, Y]) = 0 ,
  ```

  using cyclicity of the trace. Since `B` is nondegenerate, `Delta' X = 0` for every `X`. So
  `Delta' = 0` and `Delta = ad Y`.

**(1.6) `Ad(exp Y) = exp(ad Y)` on `u(n)`.** For `Y, X` in `u(n)`, put
`F(t) = exp(tY) X exp(-tY)`. Then `F'(t) = exp(tY) [Y, X] exp(-tY) = [Y, F(t)]`, which is
`(ad Y) F(t)`. So `F(t) = exp(t ad Y) X` by uniqueness for linear ODEs. Take `t = 1`.

## Step 2: every simple ideal is `G0`-invariant

- For `k` in `K`, `Ad(k)` is an orthogonal Lie automorphism of `l` (L3). It preserves `m` (Lemma Z).
  By (1.3) it permutes `{h_1, ..., h_r}`. This gives a homomorphism `p : K -> Sym(r)`.
- By Lemma A, `||Ad(w_i)|_m - 1|| <= 2 ||w_i - 1|| <= 2D < 1 < sqrt 2`. Now suppose
  `p(sigma(r_i))` moves some `h_a` to `h_b` with `b != a`. Then `Ad(w_i)|_m` maps the nonzero
  subspace `h_a` into `h_b`, which is orthogonal to `h_a` by (1.2). Lemma O, applied to the unitary
  `(Ad(w_i)|_m)_C` on `m_C` with `E = (h_a)_C` and `E' = (h_b)_C`, gives `||Ad(w_i)|_m - 1|| >= sqrt 2`.
  That is a contradiction.
- So `p o sigma : G0 -> Sym(r)` kills `r5, ..., r8`, and the transfer tool makes it trivial.
- For each `a`, the set `{k in K : Ad(k) h_a = h_a}` is closed, because `Ad` is continuous and `h_a`
  is a closed subspace. It contains the dense subgroup `Gamma`, so it is all of `K`.

Hence `tau_a(k) = Ad(k)|_(h_a)` is a continuous homomorphism from `K` to the group
`O(h_a) ∩ Aut(h_a)` of orthogonal Lie automorphisms of `h_a`. Put `tau = tau_a o sigma` on `G0`.
Since `||tau(g) - 1|| <= ||Ad(sigma(g))|_m - 1||`,

```text
||tau(r_i) - 1|| <= 2 D < 1          for 5 <= i <= 8.                     (2.1)
```

## Step 3: small orthogonal automorphisms are inner

Fix `a` and write `h = h_a`. Let `Inn(h)` be the subgroup of `Aut(h)` generated by
`{exp(ad Y) : Y in h}`.

**Lemma 3.1 (principal logarithm).** Let `phi` be an orthogonal Lie automorphism of `h` with
`||phi - 1|| < sqrt 3`. Then `phi = exp(ad Y)` for some `Y` in `h`. In particular `phi` is in `Inn(h)`.

*Proof.*
- **Eigenangles.** Let `phi_C` be the complex-linear extension of `phi` to `h_C = h + i h`, with the
  Hermitian extension of `<.,.>`. It is unitary and is an automorphism of the complex Lie algebra
  `h_C`. By Lemma A (last sentence), `||phi_C - 1|| = ||phi - 1|| < sqrt 3`.
  - Write the eigenvalues as `e^(i theta)` with `theta` in `(-pi, pi]`. Then
    `|e^(i theta) - 1| = 2 |sin(theta/2)| < sqrt 3`, so `|theta| < 2 pi/3`.
  - Let `Theta` be the set of eigenangles. `h_C` is the orthogonal sum of the eigenspaces `E_theta`,
    with orthogonal projections `P_theta`.
- **Brackets add angles.** Let `X` be in `E_theta` and `Y` in `E_theta'`. Then
  `phi_C [X, Y] = e^(i(theta + theta')) [X, Y]`. Suppose `[X, Y] != 0`. It is then an eigenvector,
  with eigenangle some `theta''` in `Theta` satisfying `theta'' ≡ theta + theta'` mod `2 pi`. But
  `|theta + theta' - theta''| < 4 pi/3 + 2 pi/3 = 2 pi`, so `theta'' = theta + theta'`. Hence
  `[E_theta, E_theta'] ⊆ E_(theta + theta')`, which is read as `0` when `theta + theta'` is not in
  `Theta`.
- **The logarithm is a derivation.** Put `Delta = sum_theta i theta P_theta`. For `X` in `E_theta`
  and `Y` in `E_theta'`,
  `Delta [X, Y] = i(theta + theta') [X, Y] = [Delta X, Y] + [X, Delta Y]`. By bilinearity, `Delta`
  is a derivation of `h_C`, and `exp(Delta) = sum e^(i theta) P_theta = phi_C`.
- **`Delta` is real.** Let `c` be the complex conjugation of `h_C` fixing `h`. Since `phi_C`
  commutes with `c` and `c` is conjugate-linear, `c E_theta = E_(-theta)`, so `-theta` is in `Theta`
  whenever `theta` is, and `c P_theta c = P_(-theta)`. Hence
  `c Delta c = sum_theta (-i theta) P_(-theta) = Delta`. So `Delta` maps `h` into `h`, and
  `Delta|_h` is a derivation of `h` with `exp(Delta|_h) = phi`.
- **Conclusion.** By (1.5), `Delta|_h = ad Y` for some `Y` in `h`, so `phi = exp(ad Y)`. QED.

By (2.1), `tau(r_i)` lies in `Inn(h)` for `5 <= i <= 8`.

**Lemma 3.2. `Ad(K^0)|_h = Inn(h)`, and this group is compact and connected.**
- `K^0` is generated by `exp(X)`, `X` in `l` (L2). Write `X = Z + sum_b X_b`, with `Z` in `z` and
  `X_b` in `h_b` (Lemma Z and (1.2)). The summands commute pairwise, because `z` is central and
  `[h_b, h_c] = 0` for `b != c`. So `exp(X) = exp(Z) prod_b exp(X_b)`.
- By (1.6), `Ad(exp W)|_h = exp(ad W)|_h` for every `W` in `l`. For `W = Z`, and for `W = X_b` with
  `b != a`, `ad W` vanishes on `h`. Hence `Ad(exp X)|_h = exp(ad_h X_a)`.
- So `Ad(K^0)|_h` is the group generated by `{exp(ad Y) : Y in h}`, which is `Inn(h)`.
- It is compact, as the continuous image of the compact group `K^0`. It is connected, as the continuous
  image of the connected group `K^0`.

## Step 4: the closure of `tau(G0)` is exactly `Inn(h)`

- Put `L = tau_a(K)` and `L_0 = tau_a(K^0) = Inn(h)` (Lemma 3.2). `L` is compact.
- `K^0` is normal of finite index in `K` (L2). So `L_0` is normal of finite index in `L`.
- The composite `G0 -> L -> L/L_0`, given by `g -> tau(g) L_0`, is a homomorphism to a finite group.
  It kills `r5, ..., r8` by Step 3, so the transfer tool makes it trivial. Hence `tau(G0) ⊆ L_0`.
- `Gamma` is dense in `K` and `tau_a` is continuous, so `tau(G0) = tau_a(Gamma)` is dense in `L`.
  `L_0` is compact, hence closed, so `L = closure(tau(G0)) ⊆ L_0`.

Therefore `L = L_0 = Inn(h)`. `tau` is a homomorphism from `G0` into `Inn(h)` with dense image,
and it is orthogonal for the `Inn(h)`-invariant inner product `<.,.>|_h`.

## Step 5: nontriviality and the dimension bound

- **`tau` is nontrivial.** Otherwise `Inn(h)`, the closure of `tau(G0)`, would be `{1}`. Then
  `exp(t ad Y) = 1` for all real `t` and all `Y` in `h`. Differentiating at `t = 0` gives `ad Y = 0`,
  so `h` would be abelian, which contradicts (1.2).
- **The bound.** `tau_C : G0 -> U(h_C)` is a unitary representation of complex dimension
  `d = dim_R h`. By Lemma A, `||tau_C(r_i) - 1|| = ||tau(r_i) - 1|| <= 2 ||w_i - 1||`. So
  `D(tau_C) <= 2D`, and `tau_C` is nontrivial. By [R] item 1,

  ```text
  2 D(sigma) >= D(tau_C) >= delta_(dim h).                                  (5.1)
  ```

- **Growth.** For every `N`, if `D(sigma) < min(1/2, delta_N / 2)`, then `delta_(dim h) < delta_N`.
  Since `delta` is nonincreasing, `dim h > N`. So along any sequence of nontrivial irreducibles with
  `D -> 0`, every simple ideal of `[l, l]` has dimension tending to infinity.

## Step 6: the converse

Let `h_j` be compact simple Lie algebras, each with an inner product invariant under `Inn(h_j)`.
Let `tau_j : G0 -> Inn(h_j)` be homomorphisms, not necessarily with dense image, such that
`tau_j != 1` and `eps_j = max_i ||tau_j(r_i) - 1|| -> 0`. Each `tau_j(g)` is orthogonal, so
`(tau_j)_C` is a nontrivial unitary representation with defect `eps_j` (Lemma A, last sentence). By
[R] item 1, it has a nontrivial irreducible summand `rho_j` with `D(rho_j) <= eps_j`. So
`delta_(dim rho_j) <= eps_j`.

Suppose `delta_N` stayed at least some `c > 0` for all `N`. Then `eps_j >= c` for all `j`, which is
impossible. So `inf_N delta_N = 0`. Since `delta` is nonincreasing, `delta_N -> 0`, and `V` is MF by
[R] item 2.

## Step 7: the corollary (the only use of [CL])

- Assume `V` is MF. By [R], there are nontrivial irreducibles `sigma_n` with `D(sigma_n) -> 0`.
- For large `n`, `D(sigma_n) < min(1/2, delta_(N_exc) / 2)`. By Steps 2 to 5, `sigma_n` produces
  a simple `h` with `dim h > N_exc`, and a homomorphism `tau_n : G0 -> Inn(h)` with dense image.
  `tau_n` is orthogonal for an `Inn(h)`-invariant inner product, and its defect is at most
  `2 D(sigma_n)`.
- By [CL], `h` is not exceptional. So `h` is isomorphic to `su(k)`, `so(k)` or `sp(k)`.
- Transport `tau_n` and the inner product along the isomorphism `psi : h -> X(k)`. The transported
  map is `Ad(psi) o tau_n`, which lands in `Inn(X(k))` because `psi exp(ad Y) psi^(-1) = exp(ad psi Y)`.
  Its image is still dense, the inner product is still invariant, and the defect is unchanged.
- One of the three families occurs for infinitely many `n`, so the corresponding `Gap(X)` fails.
- Conversely, if some `Gap(X)` fails, Step 6 shows that `V` is MF.

So `V` is not MF iff `Gap(SU)`, `Gap(SO)` and `Gap(Sp)` all hold.
