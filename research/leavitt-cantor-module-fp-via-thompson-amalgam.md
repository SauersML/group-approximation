Notation as in the target: `R = L_(F_2)(1,2)`, `U = R^×`, `M = C(C, F_2)` with the
prefix-operator representation, `G = M ⋊ U`. Write `χ_B` for the indicator of a clopen
set `B`, `[w]` for the cone of a word `w`, and `s_w`, `t_w` for prefix products.

## Inputs

1. **The copy of `V`.** The units `Σ s_(a_i) t_(b_i)` over bijections of complete prefix
   codes form a copy of Thompson's `V` in `U`. They act on `M` by `f ↦ f ∘ φ^(-1)`, where
   `φ` is the prefix replacement `b_i w ↦ a_i w`, since `(s_a t_b f)(a w) = f(b w)`. So
   `H := M ⋊ V ≤ G` is the group of `cantor-module-thompson-v-product-is-finitely-presented`,
   which is finitely presented.
2. **Generation of `U`.** By `thompson-steinberg-presentation-of-leavitt-unit-group`, `U` is
   generated by this copy of `V` together with the transvection `x = 1 + s_(00) t_(01)`, and
   `x^2 = 1` (the presented relator `x^2`; directly, `(s_(00) t_(01))^2 = 0`).
3. **Finite presentations.** `U` is finitely presented (`leavitt-unit-group-finitely-presented`),
   and `V` is finitely generated (`thompson-v-finitely-presented-infinite-simple`).
4. **Transitivity.** `V` is transitive on proper nonempty clopen subsets of `C`. Write two
   such sets `A`, `B` and their complements as disjoint unions of cones. Splitting a cone
   into its two children raises the count by one, so both pairs can be brought to the same
   numbers of cones. A bijection of the pieces is a prefix replacement taking `A` to `B`.

## The action of `x` on six indicators

`x f = f + s_(00) t_(01) f`, i.e. `(x f)(00 w) = f(00 w) + f(01 w)` and `x f = f` off `[00]`.
Hence

- `x·χ_[00] = χ_[00]`, `x·χ_[000] = χ_[000]`, `x·χ_[1] = χ_[1]`, `x·χ_[10] = χ_[10]`;
- `x·χ_[01] = χ_[01] + χ_[00]`, `x·χ_[010] = χ_[010] + χ_[000]`.

## A subgroup of `V` commuting with `x`

Here `s_w = s_(w_1) ⋯ s_(w_n)` prepends `w` and `t_w = t_(w_n) ⋯ t_(w_1)` strips it:
`(s_w f)(w y) = f(y)`, `s_w f = 0` off `[w]`, and `(t_w f)(y) = f(w y)`.

For `h, k ∈ V` (as units of `R`) let `c(h,k) = s_(00) h t_(00) + s_(01) h t_(01) + s_1 k t_1`.
This is the prefix replacement acting by `h` on the tails of `[00]` and `[01]` and by `k`
on the tails of `[1]`, so `c(h,k) ∈ V`. Since `t_(01) s_(00) = t_(01) s_1 = t_1 s_(00) = 0`
and `t_(00) s_(00) = t_(01) s_(01) = 1`,

`c x = c + s_(00) h t_(01) = x c`.

Let `C_x = {c(h,k)} ≤ V`.

## The presentation

Let `P = H *_V U`, amalgamating the two copies of `V` in item 1. It is finitely presented:
take the finite presentations of `H` and `U` and identify a finite generating set of `V`
in both. Let `G'` be `P` modulo the six relators

`x χ_w x^(-1) = x·χ_w`,  for `w ∈ {00, 000, 01, 010, 1, 10}`,

where `χ_w = χ_[w]` and the right sides from the table above are elements of `M ≤ H`. So
`G'` is finitely presented. The relators hold in `G`, where conjugation by `u ∈ U`
restricts to the module action on `M`. So there is a natural surjection `π : G' → G`.

**Step 1: `M` is normal in `G'`.**
- As a group, `M` is generated by `χ_([a]A)` with `a ∈ {00, 01, 1}` and `A ⊆ C` clopen,
  because `χ_B = χ_(B∩[00]) + χ_(B∩[01]) + χ_(B∩[1])`.
- Given such a generator with `A` nonempty, let `A' = C` if `A = C`, and `A' = [0]`
  otherwise. By item 4 choose `h` (for `a = 00, 01`) or `k` (for `a = 1`) in `V` with
  `h(A') = A` or `k(A') = A`, and let `c = c(h,1)` or `c(1,k)`. Then
  `c χ_([a]A') c^(-1) = χ_([a]A)` in `H`, and `[a]A'` is one of the six cones above.
- In `G'` the relation `c x = x c` holds, because it holds in `U`. So
  `x χ_([a]A) x^(-1) = c (x χ_([a]A') x^(-1)) c^(-1) = c (x·χ_([a]A')) c^(-1) ∈ M`.
- So `x M x^(-1) ⊆ M`, and `x = x^(-1)` gives equality. The copy of `V` normalizes `M`
  inside `H`. Since `U = ⟨V, x⟩` (item 2) and `G'` is generated by `H` and `U`, `M` is
  normal in `G'`.

**Step 2: `G'/M ≅ U`.** Modulo the normal subgroup `M`, the group `H` becomes `V`, the
amalgam becomes `V *_V U = U`, and each relator becomes trivial.

**Step 3: `π` is injective.**
- `π` maps `M ≤ H` identically onto `M ≤ G`, so `ker π ∩ M = 1`.
- The composite `G' → G → G/M ≅ U` agrees on generators with `G' → G'/M ≅ U`: both kill
  `M` and are the identity on `V` and `U`. So `ker π ⊆ M`, and hence `ker π = 1`.

So `G ≅ G'` is finitely presented.
