Let `f:G->T` with `T` torsion-free.  If `g in G` has finite order `m`, then

```text
f(g)^m=f(g^m)=1,
```

so `f(g)=1`.  The kernel of `f` is normal, hence it contains the normal
closure of all finite-order elements of `G`.  This proves the general
statement and clause 1.

For clause 2, if `a in A` has finite order then so does `j(a)`, so every
torsion-free-target homomorphism kills `j(a)`.  If finite-order elements
generate `A`, it kills `j(A)`, and normality of its kernel makes it kill
`normalClosure_G(j(A))`.  The proof concerns the generators, not the orders
of their products, so clause 3 follows at the same time.

In characteristic two the Steinberg relation in an elementary linear group
gives

```text
x_ij(a)^2=x_ij(a+a)=x_ij(0)=1.
```

The elementary group is generated by the `x_ij(a)`.  The established
all-ranks theorem identifies the binary-Leavitt unit and general linear
groups with such elementary groups.  They are therefore generated by
involutions, and the general statement applies.

Finally, every vertex copy used by an absorber or regular star amalgam is a
homomorphic image of the same torsion-generated group.  The union of its
torsion generators generates the invisible kernel, so a homomorphism to a
torsion-free group kills every vertex copy and hence their normal closure.
No MF-radical input is needed for this obstruction; full MF radical only
explains why this killed normal closure was the proposed invisible core.
