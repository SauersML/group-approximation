## Definitions

- `X = {0,1}^N` is Cantor space, with points `w = w_1 w_2 ...`, shift `σ`, and
  cylinders `[α]` for finite words `α`. `F = Q^X` is the Q-vector space of all
  functions `X -> Q`.
- **Cuntz operators** on `F`: `(s_i u)(w) = [w_1 = i] u(σw)` and
  `(t_i u)(w) = u(iw)` for `i = 0, 1`. They satisfy `t_i s_j = δ_ij` and
  `s_0 t_0 + s_1 t_1 = 1`, so they form a binary Leavitt family.
- **Harmonic functions.** `k(w)` is the number of leading `1`s of `w`, with
  `k(1^∞) = ∞`. Put `η(w) = 1/(1 + k(w))` (so `η(1^∞) = 0`) and
  `ζ(w) = 1/(1 + η(w)) = (1 + k(w))/(2 + k(w))` (so `ζ(1^∞) = 1`). Write `m_f` for
  multiplication by `f`.
- **The harmonic Cuntz ring** `C_h` is the unital subring of `End_Q(F)` generated
  by the six operators `s_0, s_1, t_0, t_1, m_η, m_ζ`.

In the Cuntz algebra: on `L^2(X)` with the Bernoulli measure, the same formulas
(with the factor `√2`) give the Cuntz isometries of `O_2`, and `η, ζ` lie in the
canonical diagonal `C(X) ⊂ O_2`, because they are continuous. So `C_h` is the
algebraic shadow of a dense subring of `O_2`. Unlike the Leavitt diagonal,
`η` is not locally constant: it takes infinitely many values near `1^∞`.

## Statement

1. **Q inside.** For every `k >= 0`, `t_0 t_1^k m_η s_1^k s_0 = (1/(k+1)) · 1`.
   So `C_h` contains `Q`, as central scalars, and is a Q-algebra.
2. **Normal form.** Every element of `C_h` is a finite Q-combination of operators
   `s_α m_f t_β`, where `α, β` are words and `f ∈ 𝒜`. Here `𝒜` is the Q-algebra of
   functions `f` that have a level `n` and rational functions `r_γ ∈ Q(t)`
   (`|γ| = n`), each with no pole in `N` and a finite limit `r_γ(∞)` at infinity,
   such that `f(γz) = r_γ(k(z))` for all `z`.
3. **Two-sided division.** For every nonzero `a ∈ C_h` there are `b, c ∈ C_h` with
   `b a c = 1`. In particular `C_h` is a simple ring. This is Cuntz's criterion for
   pure infiniteness, holding algebraically.
4. **Centre.** The centre of `C_h` is `Q · 1`, so its central units are `Q^x`.
5. **Matrix stability.** For every complete prefix code `c_1, ..., c_r`,
   `M ↦ Σ s_(c_i) M_ij t_(c_j)` is a unital ring isomorphism `M_r(C_h) -> C_h`. It
   restricts to a unital embedding `M_r(Q) -> C_h`.
6. **Decidability.** Equality in `C_h` is decidable from words in the six
   generators.

## Consequences

- `C_h` has the three inputs of the repository's root-detection theorem for
  elementary groups: a binary Leavitt family, two-sided division, and a known centre
  (`harmonic-cuntz-el3-is-fg-simple-over-all-gl-n-q`).
- The rational Leavitt algebra `L_Q(1,2)` embeds unitally in `C_h`, since `C_h` is a
  Q-algebra with a Leavitt family and `L_Q(1,2)` is simple.
