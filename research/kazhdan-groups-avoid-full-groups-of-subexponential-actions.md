**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-algebra, elementary, not reviewed; no priority claimed).

**Claim.** Let `P` be a finitely generated group of subexponential growth and `X` a compact Hausdorff
`P`-space. Every Kazhdan subgroup of the topological full group `[[P ⋉ X]]` is finite. In particular this
holds for `P = Z^2`, whatever the action, free or not.

**Proof.** Let `G <= [[P ⋉ X]]` be generated by a finite `S`, with Kazhdan pair `(S, ε)`. Each `s ∈ S` acts
by `x ↦ p_i x` on the pieces of a finite clopen partition. Let `D` bound the word lengths of these `p_i`.
- *Growth.* `G x ⊆ P x`, and a Schreier ball `B^G_r(x)` lies in the `P`-orbital ball of radius `Dr`, so
  `|B^G_r(x)| <= |B_P(Dr)|`, which is subexponential in `r`.
- *No infinite orbits.* An infinite orbit gives a connected infinite Schreier graph of subexponential
  growth. That graph is amenable (balls contain a Følner sequence), so `ℓ²(G x)` has almost invariant
  vectors. By property (T) it has a nonzero invariant vector, which is impossible on an infinite
  transitive `G`-set.
- *Bounded finite orbits.* Let an orbit have size `N`. By (T), `ℓ²_0(G x)` has spectral gap depending only
  on `(S, ε)`. So the Schreier graph is an expander, with diameter at most `C log N`. The whole orbit
  lies in `B^G_(C log N)(x)`, so `N <= |B_P(DC log N)| = N^(o(1))`. Hence `N <= N_0` for a constant `N_0`.
- *Conclusion.* Every point stabilizer has index at most `N_0`. A finitely generated group has finitely
  many subgroups of index at most `N_0`, so their intersection `K` has finite index. `K` lies in every
  stabilizer, so it acts trivially on `X`. `G` acts faithfully, so `K = 1` and `G` is finite. ∎

**Consequence for gate U.** In `(B ⊗ L)^x` with `B = LC(X, F_2) ⋊ Z^2`, the unit group contains the full
group `[[Z^2 ⋉ X]]` as its "permutational" units supported in `B`. The canonical inputs
`H_Γ = EL_3(R_Γ)` of `decidable-host-classes-reduce-to-canonical-kazhdan-groups` are infinite Kazhdan, so
they never lie there. An embedding of `H_Γ` must use either the Leavitt tree (the groupoid
`𝒪_2 × (Z^2 ⋉ X)`, whose orbits have exponential growth) or genuinely non-monomial units, such as
elementary matrices over an embedded ring.

**Lesson for general BH.** Fixing an amenable acting group to get `K`-theory for free
(`free-crossed-leavitt-k-gate-is-a-farrell-jones-instance`) costs all permutational capacity for Kazhdan
inputs, once the growth is subexponential. Since Boone--Higman reduces to Kazhdan inputs, any fixed-`P`
host must place its inputs through the Leavitt tree or through ring embeddings, never through the
`P`-dynamics alone. Complexity comes from the subshift's language. Kazhdan rigidity must come from
matrix (Steinberg) structure over rings built from that language.
