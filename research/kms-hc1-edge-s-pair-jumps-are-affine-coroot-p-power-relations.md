Notation of `kms-hc1-kassel-relation-is-a-first-order-triangle-s-pair`.

- `A = F_p<<Y_a,Y_b,Y_c>>`, with `Y_i = log(1+X_i)`.
- `I` is the relator ideal. `A/I` is the completed group algebra of the pro-p
  completion `Gamma^` of `Gamma^A2_p`.
- `J` is spanned by the `Y_i^p` and the six Serre words. `Z` is spanned by the
  three vertex relations `(XY-YX)^p`.
- `L_p = n^+(sl_3) + t sl_3[t]` is the restricted Lie algebra of the pro-p
  Iwahori `I_1`, and `u(L_p) = gr F_p[[I_1]]`.
- In multidegree `(p,p,p)`, put `V = (gr I)/(J+Z)` and
  `W = ker(A -> u(L_p))/(J+Z)`.

Since `Gamma^` maps onto `I_1`, `gr I` lies in `ker(A -> u(L_p))`, so `V` is a
subspace of `W`.

## (R) Reduction, every p >= 5 (proved)

**(R1) The hidden space.** Through degree `3p`, `A/(J+Z)` agrees with
`u(L_p)` except in multidegree `(p,p,p)`, where `dim W <= 3`.

Proof. `A/(J+Z)` is `u(Lambda')`. Here `Lambda'` is the restricted Lie algebra
presented by the three generators with these relations: the Serre relations,
the restricted relations `x^[p] = 0` for the generators, and
`[x,y]^[p] = 0` for the three edges.

By `kms-hc1-iwahori-h2-serre-plus-kassel-all-degrees` (Open 1), `H_2(L_p)`
consists of the Serre classes plus one Kassel class at each `n delta` with
`p | n`. So the ordinary Lie algebra `G` presented by the Serre relations
agrees with `L_p` below height `3p`. At `delta`-degree `p`, that is at height
`3p`, it has `dim G_(p,p,p) = dim L_(p,p,p) + 1 = 3`.

Now compare `Lambda'` with `L_p` below height `3p`. In `L_p`, the p-power map
sends height `h` to height `ph`. For a generator or an edge root vector `x`
it gives `x^[p] = 0`. So below height `3p` the restricted closure adds
nothing new, and `Lambda'` agrees with `L_p`.

At `(p,p,p)` there are two more elements, `(H_1)^[p]` and `(H_2)^[p]`, where
`H_i` spans the Cartan part at `delta`-degree 1. In `u(L_p)` both lie in the
2-dimensional `(L_p)_(p,p,p)`. So `u(Lambda')` exceeds `u(L_p)` at
`(p,p,p)` by at most `3 + 2 - 2 = 3`, and nowhere else below degree `3p+1`,
by PBW. QED.

For `p=5`, `A/(J+Z)` at `(5,5,5)` has dimension `1905` against `1902`
(the `COUNT` option of `fo_sb.cpp`). So `dim W = 3` there.

**(R2) Criterion for the first Kassel direction.** The following are
equivalent:

1. `V = W`;
2. `gr I = ker(A -> u(L_p))` through degree `3p`;
3. the Zassenhaus quotients of `Gamma^` and `I_1` have the same orders
   through class `3p`;

and all three hold if the images of the three edge jumps of (F) below are
linearly independent in `W*`-coordinates.

1 and 2 are equivalent by (R1). 2 and 3 are equivalent by Jennings' formula,
which recovers the dimensions of the Zassenhaus quotients from the Hilbert
function of `gr F_p[[G]]` degree by degree.

Condition 3 is the `n=1` case of Open 2 in
`kms-hc1-a2-pro-p-completion-is-iwahori-criterion`: the Kassel direction at
`p delta` dies in `Gamma^`.

## (F) First-order coordinates on W

**(F1) The Rees evaluation.** By the torus lemma, every eigencomponent `f`
of a relator has the form `f = f0 + f1 + ...`, with `f_k` of degree
`d + k(p-1)`. Let `I_t` be the ideal of `A[t]/(t^2)` generated by the
elements `f0 + t f1`.

Take a combination `sum c u f v`. If its `t^0`-part `sum c u f0 v` vanishes,
the true element of `I` has lowest form `T = sum c u f1 v` in degree
`d + p - 1`. The torus grading rules out degrees strictly between `d` and
`d + p - 1`. So `T` lies in `gr I`. This is a **jump**.

The program stores `T` only through its image `rho_alpha(T)` in `u(N_alpha)`,
where:

- `rho(Y_a) = E12`, `rho(Y_b) = E23` and `rho(Y_c) = E31 t`;
- `N_alpha = L_p + F z` is the Kassel extension, with cocycle
  `[x t^i, y t^j] = [x,y] t^(i+j) + i delta_(i+j,p) tr(xy) z`;
- the restricted structure has root vectors with `^[p] = 0`, together with
  `(hA t)^[p] = hA t^p + alpha_A z` and `(hB t)^[p] = hB t^p + alpha_B z`;
- `N_alpha` is truncated to multidegrees `<= (p,p,p)`.

`N_alpha` is restricted for every `alpha` by Jacobson's criterion, as in
`kms-hc1-kassel-classes-survive-restricted-initial-forms`. The map `rho_alpha`
kills `J+Z`. So each `rho_alpha` induces a linear functional
`v_alpha : W -> F_p`, the coefficient of `z`.

The three lifts of `kms-hc1-kassel-relation-is-a-first-order-triangle-s-pair`
are the Kassel lift `K` and the restricted Cartan relations `C1` and `C2`.
They are evaluated inside `u(N_alpha)` by the program's self-test. Write
`coord = (v_00, v_10 - v_00, v_01 - v_00)`. For both `p=5` and `p=7`:

| lift | coord |
|---|---|
| `K`  | `( 2, 0, 0)` |
| `C1` | `(-2, 1, 1)` |
| `C2` | `( 1,-1, 0)` |

The same values hold for `p=11` and `p=13` (`selftest_lifts.out`). The
determinant is `2`, which is nonzero. So for these `p`, `dim W = 3`,
`W = <K,C1,C2>`, and `coord` is an isomorphism `W -> F_p^3`.

In words:

- `v_00` is the Kassel cocycle;
- `lambda_A = v_10 - v_00` and `lambda_B = v_01 - v_00` read off the
  restricted parts of `hA t` and `hB t`.

**(F2) Soundness.** The following hold:

- `t^0` decisions are exact in `A`, because they are made with polynomial
  arithmetic in `A`.
- Restricting to a multidegree box `<= S` is exact. Multiplication never
  lowers multidegree, so the words outside the box span an ideal and the box
  is an algebra quotient.
- By (R1), every jump below degree `3p` has `T` in `J+Z`, so it does not
  change the `t^0` basis. The program checks that `rho(T) = 0` for all such
  jumps (`sanity-fails=0` in every run), and the check passes.
- Two reduction paths of the same S-pair therefore differ by `rho`-zero terms.

For an extra check, `PERM` relabels the generators and repeats the run
through a different reduction path. The values agree.

**(F3) The three edge jumps (exact, `p=5` and `p=7`).** Run the slices
`(p,1,p)`, `(1,p,p)` and `(p,p,1)`, each for
`alpha in {(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)}` (`run_fo.sh`, `fo_p5.out`, `fo_p7.out`).

Each slice has exactly one `rho`-visible jump. It lands in `(p,p,p)` and is
pure `z`:

| edge | S-pair (lead words) | jump | coord, `p=5` | coord, `p=7` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ca` | `(Y_cY_a)^p` with `g = Y_cY_aY_cY_aY_cY_aY_b + ...` | `2p+1 -> 3p` | `(0,0,-1)` | `(0,0,-1)` |
| `bc` | `(Y_cY_b)^p` with `Y_cY_bY_cY_bY_a + ...` | `2p+1 -> 3p` | `(0,1,0)` | `(0,1,0)` |
| `ab` | `Y_cY_bY_aY_b + ...` with `(Y_bY_a)^p` | `2p+1 -> 3p` | `(-1,-1,-1)` | `(-1,-1,-1)` |

The determinant of the three rows is `1`. So the jumps span `W`, and by (R2)
the first Kassel direction dies for `p=5` and `p=7`. This was already known
from the towers (T), and it is a consistency check of the method.

The new content is the decomposition. In `W`:

- `S_ca = -K/2 - C1 - C2`,
- `K = 2 (S_ca - S_ab - S_bc)`.

**Correction to the predicted step.** The brief predicted that "the
first-order tail of the S-pair of `[c,a]^p` with the triangle word equals `K`
modulo `J+Z`". This is false for `p=5` and for `p=7`: `S_ca` has Kassel
coordinate `0`, and it is not proportional to `K = (2,0,0)`.

It is also not a word of multidegree `(p-2,1,p-2)`. For both `p`, the triangle
word `g` has the `p`-independent multidegree `(3,1,3)` and degree 7. Its lead
is `Y_cY_aY_cY_aY_cY_aY_b` (for `p=7`: `GB[9]` in the run). The overlap
`(Y_cY_a)^p Y_b`, of degree `2p+1`, and the shift `(0,p-1,0)` are as
predicted.

There is no contradiction with (D) of
`kms-hc1-kassel-relation-is-a-first-order-triangle-s-pair`. That claim's `h3`
is reduced against the hidden elements `h1` and `h2` found earlier in the
same degree. In the coordinates above, for `p=5`,
`h1 = C1 - 4C2 + 2K = (3,0,1)` and `S_ca = -K + 4 h1`. So the statement
"`h3 = K`" holds modulo `h1`, as that claim computed it, but not as an
absolute first-order value.

**Interpretation.** Up to a scalar, each edge jump is the restricted p-power
relation attached to the coroot of the root of its Heisenberg commutator:

| edge | commutator | root | `lambda` part of the jump |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ab` | `[a,b] = E13` | `alpha_1 + alpha_2` | `hA + hB` |
| `bc` | `[b,c] ~ E21 t` | `delta - alpha_1` | `hA` |
| `ca` | `[c,a] ~ E32 t` | `delta - alpha_2` | `hB` |

The Kassel coordinate is carried by the `ab` edge alone.

Consequently, no single edge can kill `K`. `K` dies only through the
combination `S_ca - S_ab - S_bc`, which matches the gluing statement (E) of
the previous claim.

## (H) Where the symbolic all-p argument breaks

By (R2), the `n=1` case of Open 2 for every `p >= 5` follows from one
statement:

> **(E_p)** The three edge jumps have coordinates `(-1,-1,-1)`, `(0,1,0)` and
> `(0,0,-1)`, or any other independent triple.

Here is what is uniform in `p` and what is not.

1. **Group-level form of the t-parts (uniform).** `rho` extends to the group.
   Take `a -> E(E12)`, `b -> E(E23)` and `c -> E(E31 t)`, with `E` the
   truncated exponential. These are units of `u(N_alpha)_box`, and they
   satisfy `x^p = 1` because root vectors have `^[p] = 0`.

   The torus acts on `u(N_alpha)` by the multigrading. So for each
   commutator relator `r`, `rho(f)` is the `chi`-component of
   `rho(r) - 1`. In particular, `rho(f1)` is the degree-`(d+p-1)` part of that
   component.

   So every `t`-part is a component of the **Heisenberg defect**
   `rho(r) - 1` of the truncated exponentials. If `rho(r) = 1` for all six
   `r`, every jump would be `rho`-invisible. The defects are nonzero, and
   they have one source. For `X` and `Y` in the edge subalgebra,
   `E(X)E(Y)` differs from the product `E(Z)` of the Heisenberg law by
   carry terms `sum_(i+j>=p) X^i Y^j/(i! j!)`. Their degree-`p` part is the
   Witt-vector carry `sum_(0<i<p) (-1)^i X^i Y^(p-i)/i`, since
   `1/(i!(p-i)!) = (-1)^i/i` mod `p`. That carry is where the restricted
   structure `(h t)^[p]` of the edge's coroot enters. This is the heuristic
   reason for the interpretation in (F3), not a proof of it.

2. **The reduction chain (uniform length).** The triangle word `g` is built
   by three S-pair steps, `S([b,c,c],[a,b,a])`, then with `[a,c,a]`, then
   with `[a,c,c]`. It has multidegree `(3,1,3)`. For both `p=5` and `p=7`,
   the ca-slice standard basis has the same leads: `baa, ccb, caa, cca`,
   `cbaca, cbcaca, cacaba, cacacab`, together with `a^p`, `c^p` and
   `(ca)^p`. Only the last three depend on `p`. The expected uniformity for
   all `p` is not proved.

3. **The break.** The `t^0`-reduction of the overlap `(Y_cY_a)^p Y_b` to 0
   has about `p` steps. Each step multiplies a `t`-part by words
   `u, v` of length up to `2p`. The value is then
   `sum c zeta(rho(u) rho(f1) rho(v))`, a sum over the reduction path.

   Two ingredients are missing.
   - A closed form for the cofactors `(c,u,v)` of that reduction as `p`
     varies. They are known only numerically: the runs record the step
     counts, but not a formula.
   - A closed form for the degree-`(3p-1)` Heisenberg defect of the `ca`
     edge, the `t`-part of `Z_ca`, inside `u(N_alpha)` at multidegree
     `(p,p-1,p)`.

   At that multidegree `u(N_alpha)` is large. Already for `p=11` the
   program exceeds 2 GB there while storing `rho`-images: it reached degree
   21 of 23 in the ca-slice, then ran out of memory reducing the
   `(11,0,11)` eigencomponent of `[a,c,a]`. So neither the computation nor
   the symbolic evaluation reaches `p=11` in this form.

   The natural fix works dually. Store each `t`-part as its pairing
   `(u,v) -> zeta(rho(u) X rho(v))` against complementary word pairs. At
   `(p,p-1,p)` the complement is `(0,1,0)`, a single letter. That makes the
   top of the computation tiny, and may make the defect computable by hand.
