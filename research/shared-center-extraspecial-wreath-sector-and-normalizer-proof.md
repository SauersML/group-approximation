# Shared-center extraspecial normal forms, spin corners, and the surviving multiplicity cocycle

All matrix Hilbert--Schmidt norms are normalized.

## 1. The restricted central product and its finite generation

For a finite §F subset X§, let §E(F)§ have the presentation inherited from
§(SCW1)§.  Every word can be collected into the form

~~~text
J^c product_(x in F) A_x^(a_x) B_x^(b_x),
c,a_x,b_x in {0,1}.                                     (1)
~~~

The form is unique.  One concrete verification acts on
§H_F=(C^2)^(tensor F)§: send §J§ to §-I§ and send §A_x,B_x§ to the two
standard Pauli involutions on tensor leg §x§.  The
§2^(1+2|F|)§ matrices obtained from (1) are distinct.  Hence
§|E(F)|=2^(1+2|F|)§ and the inclusions for nested windows are injective.
Define §E(X)§ as their directed union.  In particular §J§ remains
nontrivial.

Let §G§ act transitively on §X§.  The formulas

~~~text
g A_x g^(-1)=A_(gx),      g B_x g^(-1)=B_(gx),          (2)
g J g^(-1)=J
~~~

define an action on §E(X)§.  If §S§ generates §G§ and §o in X§, then

~~~text
Pi(X)=<S,A_o,B_o>.                                       (3)
~~~

Indeed §J=[A_o,B_o]§, and every site pair is a §G§-conjugate of the root
pair.  This proves finite generation.  The commuting subgroup generated by
the §A_x§ is §direct_sum_X C_2§ and is normalized by §G§, so

~~~text
(direct_sum_X C_2) semidirect G < Pi(X).                 (4)
~~~

For the pinned Kun--Thom action, (4) is the pinned nonsofic wreath.

## 2. The common center is killed by every finite-dimensional representation

Let §pi:Pi(X)->U(d)§ be exact.  Since §J§ is a central involution,

~~~text
C^d=H_+ direct_sum H_-,
pi(J)|H_+=I,              pi(J)|H_-=-I.                 (5)
~~~

Both spaces are invariant under the entire representation.  Fix a finite
window §F§ of size §m§.  The finite group §E(F)§ has §2^(2m)§
one-dimensional representations, all with §J=1§, and one further
irreducible representation §rho_F§, of dimension §2^m§, on which
§J=-1§.  This follows either from the usual extraspecial character table or
directly from the Pauli model: the image of the negative central summand of
the group algebra is §M_(2^m)§, whose dimension §2^(2m)§ already exhausts
that whole summand.

It follows that, as an §E(F)§-module,

~~~text
H_- = rho_F^(direct_sum r_F)                             (6)
~~~

for some integer §r_F§.  Therefore §2^m§ divides
§dim(H_-)§.  Since §X§ is infinite, §m§ is arbitrary.  The only finite
integer divisible by every §2^m§ is zero.  Thus §H_-=0§ and
§pi(J)=I§.

The same proof works for an odd prime §p§.  Use site Heisenberg generators
of order §p§, share their central element §J§ of order §p§, and impose
cross-site commutation.  For each nontrivial central character, the
§m§-site group has a unique irreducible representation of dimension
§p^m§.  Every finite-dimensional representation therefore kills §J§.
The binary version is singled out because it contains the pinned
§C_2§-lamp wreath.

## 3. The canonical negative corner is a tensor factor

Let §tau§ be the canonical group trace and §e_-=(1-J)/2§.  Since
§J!=1§,

~~~text
tau(e_-)=1/2.                                            (7)
~~~

The negative central summand of §C[E(F)]§ has vector-space dimension
§2^(2m)§.  Its Pauli representation is onto §M_(2^m)§, also of dimension
§2^(2m)§, and hence is an isomorphism:

~~~text
e_- C[E(F)] isomorphic_to M_(2^m).                       (8)
~~~

Under (8), the site algebra

~~~text
C^*(e_-A_x,e_-B_x) isomorphic_to M_2                    (9)
~~~

is the full algebra on tensor leg §x§.  Different site algebras commute,
and their product is the algebra in (8).  The normalized corner trace

~~~text
tau_-(T)=tau(T)/tau(e_-)
~~~

is the unique normalized trace on (8), hence is exactly the tensor-product
trace.  This proves tensor independence, not merely vanishing of a selected
list of moments.

Because a finitely generated transitive action has countable §X§, the
norm closure of the directed union in (8) is the §2^infinity§ UHF algebra
and its tracial weak closure is the hyperfinite §II_1§ factor §R§.  The
actor permutes its named tensor legs.  Since §e_-§ is central in
§L(Pi(X))§, the group relations and canonical trace identify

~~~text
e_- L(Pi(X)) isomorphic_to R crossed_product G           (10)
~~~

for this tensor-shift action.

There is an important contrast with independent Heisenberg packets.  A
separate nontrivial central-character cut at each of §m§ sites has mass
decaying exponentially in §m§.  Here there is one shared cut of mass
§1/2§ for every window.  What does grow is the irreducible spin dimension,
and that growth reappears as the multiplicity calculation below.

## 4. Exact and quantitative actor-normalizer factorization

Fix §F§ of size §m§ and an exact negative-sector representation on
§H_F tensor C^r§.  By uniqueness of the spin representation, after one
unitary conjugacy it is

~~~text
rho_F(E(F))=M_(2^m) tensor I_r.                          (11)
~~~

Let §beta§ be a permutation of §F§ and let §P_beta§ be the tensor-leg
permutation, so

~~~text
P_beta rho_F(A_x) P_beta^*=rho_F(A_(beta x)),
P_beta rho_F(B_x) P_beta^*=rho_F(B_(beta x)).            (12)
~~~

If §U§ has the same exact covariance, then
§V=P_beta^*U§ commutes with the algebra in (11).  Its commutant is
§I_(2^m) tensor M_r§, and hence

~~~text
U=P_beta tensor W,                 W in U(r).             (13)
~~~

This has no site-dependent inner gauge: naming both Pauli generators fixes
the entire spin factor.

The fixed-window quantitative version is equally elementary.  Suppose the
largest error in the §2m§ equations (12) is §epsilon§ and set
§V=P_beta^*U§.  Let §mathcal W_F§ be the §4^m§ Pauli words modulo scalar
center.  Every such word is a product of at most §2m§ named Pauli
generators, so telescoping gives

~~~text
||[V,W]||_2<=2m epsilon          for W in mathcal W_F.   (14)
~~~

Pauli twirling is the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto the
commutant:

~~~text
E(V)=4^(-m) sum_(W in mathcal W_F) W^* V W
    =I_(2^m) tensor T.                                   (15)
~~~

Orthogonality of §E§ and unitarity of §V§ give the exact identity

~~~text
4^(-m) sum_W ||[V,W]||_2^2
 =2||V-E(V)||_2^2.                                       (16)
~~~

Thus, for §delta=||V-E(V)||_2§,

~~~text
delta<=sqrt(2)m epsilon.                                 (17)
~~~

The contraction §T§ has a unitary polar completion §W§.  If
§s_1,...,s_r§ are its singular values, then §0<=s_i<=1§ and

~~~text
||T-W||_2^2
 =average_i (1-s_i)^2
 <=average_i (1-s_i^2)
 =1-||T||_2^2
 =delta^2.                                               (18)
~~~

Combining (17)--(18) proves

~~~text
||U-P_beta tensor W||_2<=2sqrt(2)m epsilon.              (19)
~~~

If the finite Pauli packet is initially only approximate, one first applies
ordinary normalized-HS stability of the fixed finite group §E(F)§.  That
adds the fixed-packet rounding error to (19); it does not alter the
multiplicity conclusion.

## 5. The external reservoir and the logarithmic scale

Formula (13) leaves all of §U(r)§.  This is not just a symmetry count.
Let §F_r§ be the normalized Fourier unitary and take

~~~text
U=P_beta tensor F_r.                                     (20)
~~~

It has exact covariance.  In the tensor-product basis every column of
§U§ has exactly §r§ nonzero entries, each of modulus §1/sqrt(r)§.  A
monomial matrix selects only one entry per column.  Therefore, for every
monomial §M§,

~~~text
|tr(M^*U)|<=1/sqrt(r),
||U-M||_2^2>=2-2/sqrt(r).                                (21)
~~~

Adding §q=o(2^m r)§ arbitrary dimensions changes the normalized overlap by
at most §q/(2^m r+q)§, so the same asymptotic gap survives sublinear
padding.

Now consider a sequence of canonical group microstates and fix §F§.
Round the central involution and the fixed finite packet.  Since
§tau(J)=0§, the negative spectral dimension §D_-§ satisfies

~~~text
D_-/D ->1/2.                                             (22)
~~~

The exactified packet on that sector has the form (11), so

~~~text
D_-=2^m r.                                               (23)
~~~

For fixed §m§, §r->infinity§.  Making §r<=K§ requires

~~~text
m>=log_2(D_-)-log_2(K).                                  (24)
~~~

This is also the quantitative reason that finite-dimensional invisibility
does not by itself imply approximate invisibility.  Exact representations of
the whole infinite group have to satisfy (6) for every §m§ simultaneously
and hence have no negative sector.  A microstate at one fixed stage only sees
a finite §m§ and may devote a macroscopic dimension §2^m r§ to the exact
spin representation.  Reaching the contradiction needs a window beyond the
matrix logarithm, while hyperlinear convergence gives no such diagonal
rate.

## 6. The sharply two-transitive presentation interface

Assume §G§ acts sharply two-transitively in characteristic different from
two.  The action set is equivariantly the conjugacy class of involutions.
For a root involution §j§,

~~~text
Gamma=Stab_G(j)=C_G(j).                                  (25)
~~~

The stabilizer is transitive on the complement of the root, so its orbits on
§X§ are §{j}§ and §X minus {j}§.  Equivalently,

~~~text
|Gamma backslash G/Gamma|=2.                             (26)
~~~

Suppose in addition that §G§ is finitely presented and §Gamma§ is finitely
generated.  Start with finite generators for §G§ and one root pair §A,B§.
Relations fixing §A,B§ under generators of §Gamma§ make their conjugates
depend only on cosets.  Choose one §t notin Gamma§.  The four relators

~~~text
[A,tAt^(-1)]=[A,tBt^(-1)]
 =[B,tAt^(-1)]=[B,tBt^(-1)]=1                           (27)
~~~

generate every distinct-site commutator by (26).  Add the finite local
extraspecial relations, put §J=[A,B]§, and require §J§ to be central and
actor-fixed.  This is a finite presentation of §Pi(X)§.

Both finiteness assumptions matter.  Independently of the standard wreath
criterion, if §Pi(X)§ were finitely presented, adjoining the two relations

~~~text
A_o=1,       B_o=1                                       (28)
~~~

would kill the whole base by transitivity and leave §G§.  Hence §G§ would
be finitely presented.  Finite generation of §C_G(j)§ is what turns the
root-site stabilizer laws into finitely many relations in the direct
presentation.

The André--Guirardel theorem supplies a two-generated simple
property-(T) sharply two-transitive group of characteristic zero.  Their
published construction is an iterated/direct-limit small-cancellation
construction.  It does not assert finite presentation of §G§ or finite
generation of §C_G(j)§.  General sharp two-transitivity shows that
§C_G(j)§ is malnormal and is the point stabilizer, but gives no finite
generating set.  These source facts are audited in
§andre-guirardel-sharp-t-actor-audit§.

Since §G§ itself has property (T), the pair §(G,C_G(j))§ has relative
property (T) in the automatic formal sense enjoyed by every subgroup of a
property-(T) group.  That statement does not say §C_G(j)§ is finitely
generated or property (T), and it cannot be applied to a root packet vector:
that vector is supposed to move under §G§ and is not almost
§G§-invariant.  Thus it supplies neither the missing stabilizer relations nor
a way to remove §W§ in (13).

Sharp two-transitivity is therefore a real algebraic improvement: it reduces
all off-diagonal packet commutation to (27).  It does not presently provide
the finite-presentation interface, prove that its coset action is the pinned
nonsofic action, or solve normalized-HS multiplicity classicalization.  The
shared-center construction supplies a strong exact central wall, but the
global nonhyperlinearity route remains open.
