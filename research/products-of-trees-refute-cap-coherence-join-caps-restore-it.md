## Setting

- `Λ = F_m × F_n` with `m, n >= 2`, generated by free bases and their inverses, `S = S_1 ∪ S_2`. The
  metric is `d((g,h),(g',h')) = d_1(g,g') + d_2(h,h')`. `T_1` and `T_2` are the Cayley trees.
- `X = ∂T_m ⊠ ∂T_n` (`free-group-boundary-shifts-are-quantum-rigid`, item 3). A point `x = (ξ, η)` has
  symbol `x(g,h) = (a_ξ(g), a_η(h))`, where `a_ξ(g)` is the arrow at `g` toward `ξ`.
- `X` is a quantum-rigid SFT without isolated points. `Λ ↷ X` is minimal, topologically free, and
  topologically amenable (a product of the amenable boundary actions of the factors; recalled).
- Notation as in the parent: `E_r^c(x) = {y ∈ X : y = x on B_r(c)}` and
  `V_r^c(x) = ⋃_(y ∈ E_r^c(x)) Δ(x,y)`; the caps `Cap_r^c` use a fixed `L >= 0`, and
  `ν_R^c = (1/R) Σ_(r=R)^(2R−1) u(Cap_r^c)`.
- For `c ∈ Λ`, `p_1^c(i)` is the vertex at distance `i` from `c_1` on the ray `[c_1, ξ)`, and
  `p_2^c(j)` is defined in the same way on `[c_2, η)`. `L(ξ,ξ')` is the vertex set of the line
  `(ξ,ξ')`, which is empty if `ξ' = ξ`.

## The modification structure (used throughout)

1. `Δ(x,y) = (L(ξ,ξ') × F_n) ∪ (F_m × L(η,η'))` for `y = (ξ',η')`.
   - The arrows toward `ξ` and `ξ'` differ exactly on the line `(ξ,ξ')`. A symbol changes when either
     of its coordinates does.
2. `E_r^c(x) = Sh_1 × Sh_2`, where `Sh_1` is the set of ends whose ray from `c_1` passes through
   `p_1^c(r+1)`, and `Sh_2` is defined in the same way.
   - `B_r(c)` contains `B_r(c_1) × {c_2}` and `{c_1} × B_r(c_2)`.
   - The arrows on `B_r(c_1)` toward `ξ'` and toward `ξ` agree iff `ξ'` does not branch off `[c_1,ξ)`
     within distance `r`.
3. The nearest point of `L(ξ,ξ')` to `c_1` is the branch point of `ξ'` from the ray `[c_1, ξ)`. For
   `ξ' ∈ Sh_1`, it is `p_1^c(i)` with `i >= r+1`, and every such `i` occurs, because the degree is
   at least 3.

So a modification in one factor has a **slab** as support: a line times the whole other factor.
Every slab comes back to within distance `r+1` of `c`, at the **axis points** `(p_1^c(r+1), c_2)` and
`(c_1, p_2^c(r+1))`.

## Theorem 1 (cap coherence fails)

For every `L >= 0`, every `R > L`, every `x ∈ X` and every `s ∈ S_2`,

    ‖ ν_R^1(x) − ν_R^s(x) ‖_1  >=  2 (2m−1)^L / N(m,n,L),

where `N(m,n,L) = Σ_(j=0)^L [ (2m−1)^j |B_(L−j)^(F_n)| + (2n−1)^j |B_(L−j)^(F_m)| ]`. The same holds
for `s ∈ S_1`, with `m` and `n` exchanged. So `X` violates (CC) uniformly, at every point, for every
`L`.

**Consequence.** (CC′) of the parent node (minimal + topologically free + rigid ⇒ (CC)) is **false**,
and (CC) is not necessary for amenability even for minimal, topologically free, rigid SFTs. The
parent's reduction of (RA′) to (CC′) is void.

*Proof.*
1. **The caps.**
   - By items 1–3, `V_r^c(x) = (U_1 × F_n) ∪ (F_m × U_2)`, where `U_1` is the subtree of `T_1` hanging
     beyond `p_1^c(r+1)`, and `U_2` is defined in the same way.
     - A vertex of that subtree lies on the ray, or hangs off it at some `p_1^c(t)` with `t >= r+1`. In
       either case it lies on a line `(ξ, ξ')` with `ξ' ∈ Sh_1`.
   - So `ρ_r^c(x) = r+1`.
   - For `r >= L`, `Cap_r^c = P_1^c ⊔ P_2^c`, with
     `P_1^c = {(u,h) : u ∈ U_1, d_1(c_1,u) + d_2(c_2,h) <= r+1+L}`, and `P_2^c` defined in the same way.
     - The two parts are disjoint: a point of both would be at distance `>= 2r+2 > r+1+L`.
   - `U_1` has `(2m−1)^j` vertices at depth `j` below `p_1^c(r+1)`. So `|Cap_r^c| = N(m,n,L)`, for
     every `r >= L`, every `c` and every `x`.
2. **A test set.**
   - Take `c = 1` and `c' = s = (1,s)`. They have the same first coordinate, so `U_1` and the
     `p_1(t)` are the same for both.
   - Let `A = {(u,1) : d_1(1,u) > L}`. Points of `P_2` have first coordinate within `L` of `1`, so
     `A` meets only the `P_1` parts.
   - Write a vertex `u ∈ T_1` as projecting to `p_1(t)` on `[1,ξ)`, at excursion `e = |u| − t`.
     Then `(u,1) ∈ Cap_r^1` iff `r <= t−1` and `r >= t + e − L − 1`. Also `(u,1) ∈ Cap_r^s` iff
     `r <= t−1` and `r >= t + e − L`, since `d_2(s,1) = 1`.
   - So the set of admissible `r` for `s` is the set for `1` minus the single value
     `r = t + e − L − 1`. That value is admissible for `1` iff `e <= L` and `R <= t+e−L−1 <= 2R−1`.
3. **Counting.**
   - For each `e ∈ [0, L]` there are exactly `R` admissible values of `t`.
   - For each `(t, e)` there are `(2m−2)(2m−1)^(e−1)` vertices `u` with projection `p_1(t)` and
     excursion `e >= 1`, and one with `e = 0`. The sum over `e <= L` is `(2m−1)^L`.
   - All the differences have the same sign. So `ν_R^1(x)(A) − ν_R^s(x)(A) = R (2m−1)^L / (R N)`.
   - Finally, `‖μ − μ'‖_1 >= 2|μ(A) − μ'(A)|` for probability measures. ∎

**Why.**
- A slab in factor 1 is constant along factor 2. So its nearest point to `c` sits at the transverse
  coordinate `c_2`, and it moves with every step of the centre in factor 2.
- Half the cap mass lives on such transverse axes, and non-amenability of `F_n` stops the `L`-balls
  around `c_2` and `c_2 s` from overlapping in proportion `1 − o(1)`.
- Scale averaging does not help, because every scale has the same defect.
- Nearest points see each independent direction **separately**. Reiter functions for a product must
  see the directions **jointly**.

## Theorem 2 (join caps give amenability over any group)

Let `Λ` be finitely generated and `X ⊆ A^Λ` any subshift. For `k >= 1`, `R >= 1`, `c ∈ Λ` and
`x ∈ X`, define the **`k`-fold join cap**

    J_R^(k),c(x) = A_R^(k)(c) ∩ ⋃ { NP_c( Δ(x,y_1) ∩ … ∩ Δ(x,y_k) ) : y_1, …, y_k ∈ E_R^c(x) },

where `A_R^(k)(c) = {p : kR < d(c,p) <= 2kR}` and `NP_c(S)` is the set of points of `S` nearest to
`c`. Taking `y_i` equal shows that `J^(1)` is the set of nearest points of single supports.

1. `J_R^(k),c(x)` depends only on `x|B_(2kR)(c)` and the language of `X`, so it is locally constant
   in `x`.
   - The nearest points that lie in `B_(2kR)(c)` are the nearest points of the truncated
     intersection.
   - The truncated supports range over the finitely many patterns on `B_(2kR)(c)` that occur in `X`
     and agree with `x` on `B_R(c)`.
2. `J_R^(k),gc(g.x) = g J_R^(k),c(x)`, because `Δ(g.x, g.y) = g Δ(x,y)` and the metric is
   left-invariant.
3. **(JC_k)** Suppose that for all large `R`, every `J_R^(k),1(x)` is nonempty, and that
   `sup_x ‖u(J_R^(k),1(x)) − u(J_R^(k),s(x))‖_1 → 0` for every generator `s`. Then `Λ ↷ X` is
   topologically amenable, so `Λ` is exact.

*Proof of 3.* This is item 4 of the parent node, with the maps `x ↦ u(J_R^(k),1(x))` in place of
`ν_R`. They are locally constant and supported in `B_(2kR)`, and by 2 they satisfy the same
equivariance identity. ∎

## Theorem 3 (products of trees: join caps cohere at level 2, and not at level 1)

For `X = ∂T_m ⊠ ∂T_n`:
1. `(JC_2)` holds, with `sup_x ‖u(J_R^(2),1(x)) − u(J_R^(2),s(x))‖_1 <= 4/R` for every
   `s ∈ S_1 ∪ S_2`.
2. `(JC_1)` fails: for `s ∈ S_2`, the defect is at least `1`.

*Proof.* Let `y = (ξ',η')` and `y' = (ξ'',η'')` lie in `E_R^c(x)`, and write `L_1 = L(ξ,ξ')`,
`L_1' = L(ξ,ξ'')`, and so on. By item 1 of the modification structure,

    Δ(x,y) ∩ Δ(x,y') = ((L_1∩L_1') × F_n) ∪ (F_m × (L_2∩L_2')) ∪ (L_1 × L_2') ∪ (L_1' × L_2).

- When both lines are nonempty, `L_1 ∩ L_1'` contains a ray toward `ξ`. Its nearest point to `c_1`
  is the later of the two branch points, which lies on `[c_1, ξ)`.
- So every nearest point to `c` has one of three forms:
  - an **axis point** `(p_1^c(i), c_2)`;
  - an axis point `(c_1, p_2^c(j))`;
  - a **corner** `(p_1^c(i), p_2^c(j))`;

  in each case with `i, j >= R+1`.
- Every corner occurs: take `y` flipping factor 1 at `p_1^c(i)` and `y'` flipping factor 2 at
  `p_2^c(j)`. Every axis point occurs: take `y = y'`.

Hence `J_R^(2),c(x)` consists of:
- the corners with `i, j >= R+1` and `2R < i+j <= 4R`, of which there are `R(2R−1)`;
- the `2R + 2R` axis points in the annulus.

Now take `c = 1` and `s ∈ S_2`, so `s = (1,s)`.
- If `s` points toward `η`, then `p_2^s(j) = p_2^1(j+1)`. Otherwise `p_2^s(j) = p_2^1(j−1)`.
- In the first case, the corners for `s` are `{(p_1(i), p_2(k)) : i >= R+1, k >= R+2, 2R+1 < i+k <= 4R+1}`.
  - This differs from the corner set for `1` in the row `k = R+1` and the diagonal `i+k = 4R+1`: at
    most `4R` points.
  - The axis points `(p_1(i), 1)` and `(p_1(i), s)` are disjoint: `4R` points.
  - The other axis shifts by one: 2 points.
- Both sets have size `2R² + 3R`, so the defect is at most `(8R+2)/(2R²+3R) <= 4/R`.
- The second case, and the case `s ∈ S_1`, are symmetric.

For `(JC_1)`, `J^(1)` consists of the axis points alone. The half of its mass on `(p_1(i), c_2)` moves
to a disjoint set under `c ↦ cs`. ∎

**Remark (k factors).** For `∂T_(m_1) ⊠ … ⊠ ∂T_(m_k)`, the same computation shows:
- `(JC_k)` holds, with defect `O(k²/R)`;
- `(JC_(k−1))` fails, because the `(k−1)`-fold nearest points fill coordinate faces, and a step of the
  centre in the missing coordinate moves a face bodily.

The computation was written out for `k = 2` only.

## 4. What the Reiter side needs (for (RA′))

**The join dimension.**
- Call modifications `y_1, …, y_k ∈ E(x)` *independent* if their supports pairwise meet and all
  `2^k` combinations are points of `X`. Examples: flips in different factors, or flips of pairwise
  crossing hyperplanes in a cube complex.
- Theorem 3 says the Reiter functions of a product need joins of as many independent directions as
  there are. Fewer joins leave lower-dimensional faces, which the centre drags transversally.
- **Expected, not proved.** For the cube Roller SFTs of `roller-compactifications-of-cube-complexes-are-quantum-rigid`
  in dimension `d`, `(JC_d)` holds.
  - There the `d`-fold join caps should be the corners of median intervals `[c, x]`, and interval
    measures are the Reiter functions behind property A for finite-dimensional cube complexes
    (Brodzki–Campbell–Guentner–Niblo–Wright; recalled, not re-read).
  - The two wall properties this uses are:
    - **finite dimension**: a uniform bound on pairwise-crossing families;
    - **Helly**: pairwise-crossing hyperplanes have a common cube, so the join is realized near `c`.

**Corrected form of the reduction.**
- (RA′) follows from **(JC′)**: every minimal, topologically free, rigid subshift satisfies `(JC_k)`
  for some `k`.
- So a proof must derive from minimality a *finite join dimension*, or some substitute for it.
- A counterexample must have independent modification families of unbounded size with no nearby
  common corner. That is exactly the geometry of infinite-dimensional cubings.
- The Arzhantseva–Osajda Haagerup non-exact wall spaces have infinite-dimensional dual cube complexes
  (recalled). So if their wall-orientation subshift has a rigid minimal subset, every level of join
  caps fails there, as non-exactness requires.

**Minimal subsets of the Osajda wall boundary: two facts for the flippable-wall test.**
- (a) **Flippable walls.** At an ultrafilter `o`, a wall `H` is flippable iff `o(H)` is minimal among
  the chosen halfspaces.
  - Reversing `H` fails to give an ultrafilter iff some chosen `o(K)` misses `o(H)*`, that is,
    `o(K) ⊆ o(H)`.
  - At the points `(ξ,η)` of the minimal set `∂T_m × ∂T_n`, no chosen halfspace is minimal, so the
    two-far-flippable-walls kill (item 5 of the parent) never fires there. This is the calibration.
- (b) **A correction to part 4 of the cube node.** In a graphical small-cancellation Cayley graph,
  every edge lies on a translate of every relator that carries its label, the shortest ones included.
  So walls also acquire short links.
  - Whether a wall's edge set is uniformly `2D`-connected, which is (W1) of the cube node, is
    therefore **undecided**, not "expected to fail".
  - Which way it goes decides whether single-wall flips can kill the full compactification.

## Lesson for general BH

**Rigid dynamics over products are amenable for a joint reason, which nearest points cannot see.**
- A modification in one direction is constant along the others.
- So the first variation seen from `c` sits on an axis through `c`, and it moves with the centre.
- Reiter functions need the *join* of first variations in all independent directions: the corner of
  the interval, not the nearest point.

**What this means for (RA′).**
- (RA′) cannot be proved from caps, because the product of two trees is a rigid, minimal, topologically
  free, amenable counterexample to cap coherence.
- A proof via joins needs a bounded number of independent directions (finite dimension) and nearby
  corners (Helly).
- Those are precisely the two properties that infinite-dimensional, non-exact wall spaces lack.
- So the next test is concrete: at Osajda's input, are rigid minimal subsets forced to have finite join
  dimension, or can they carry unboundedly many independent modification directions?
