Put `B=F_2[T]` and `r=28`.  The finite-rank Steinberg projection

```text
theta:St_r(B) -> E_r(B)                                 (PQS4)
```

is an isomorphism.  Here are the two inputs, including the stability point.
The ring `B` is Euclidean, so Steinberg stability identifies
`ker(theta)` at rank `r>=5` with stable algebraic `K_2(B)`.
Quillen homotopy invariance for the regular ring `F_2` gives

```text
K_2(F_2[T]) isomorphic to K_2(F_2),
```

and Quillen's finite-field computation gives `K_2(F_2)=0`.  Hence
`ker(theta)=0`; surjectivity onto `E_r(B)` is its definition.  The use
of rank `28`, rather than an unstable rank-two assertion, is essential.

Let `N=n+26`.  Splitting the first copy of `R` along the 27 prefix
leaves gives the module isomorphism

```text
Phi:R^N -> R^n                                           (PQS5)
```

from the depth-mismatch proof.  Conjugation by `Phi` is an isomorphism

```text
alpha:E_N(R) -> E_n(R),                                  (PQS6)
```

because it is an isomorphism of the corresponding general linear groups and
`GL=EL` at both ranks over `R`.

At ranks at least five, the Steinberg groups are the universal central
extensions of their elementary groups.  Therefore `alpha` has a unique
lift to an isomorphism

```text
alpha_tilde:St_N(R) -> St_n(R)                           (PQS7)
```

commuting with the Steinberg projections.  One obtains the inverse by lifting
`alpha^(-1)), so this is an isomorphism, not merely a map modulo the
central kernels.

Let `H_B<=E_r(B)` be generated by the qutrit permutation matrices on the
27 leaf coordinates together with

```text
1+E_(0,star),              1+E_(star,0)(T).              (PQS8)
```

Its image under block stabilization to rank `N`, coefficient inclusion
`B->R` with `T |-> t_0`, and `alpha` is exactly the downstairs group
`H`.  This map is injective: `B->R` is injective by Leavitt normal form,
the matrix and block maps are entrywise injective, and `alpha` is an
isomorphism.

Now compose

```text
H_B -> St_r(B) -> St_N(B) -> St_N(R) -> St_n(R),         (PQS9)
```

where the first arrow is `theta^(-1)), the middle arrows are stabilization
and coefficient inclusion, and the last arrow is `alpha_tilde`.  Naturality
of Steinberg generators makes the composite project to the preceding
injective copy of `H_B`.  Identifying that copy with `H` defines the
section `s` in `(PQS2)`.

It remains to compare this polynomial section with the generators specified
in `(PQS1)`.  For `f in F`, put

```text
delta(f)=iota_n(f)s(D_n(f))^(-1).                        (PQS10)
```

Both factors lift the same element, so `delta(f)` lies in the central
Steinberg kernel.  Because `iota_n|_F` and `s|_F` are homomorphisms and
their discrepancies are central, `delta:F->ker(pi_n)` is a homomorphism.
Its image `C_F` is finite.

Let `y_+,y_-` be the two standard root lifts in `(PQS1)), and let
`y'_+,y'_-` be their lifts under `s`.  The discrepancies

```text
c_+=y_+(y'_+)^(-1),             c_-=y_-(y'_-)^(-1)      (PQS11)
```

are central.  Each standard root has order two in characteristic two, and
each section lift has order two because its downstairs root does.  Since the
discrepancy is central, `c_+^2=c_-^2=1`.

Let

```text
C=<C_F,c_+,c_->.
```

It is a finite central group: `C_F` is finite and the other two commuting
generators have order at most two.  Every generator in `(PQS1)` is a
central element of `C` times the corresponding generator of `s(H)).
This proves

```text
Gamma <= C s(H).                                         (PQS12)
```

The group `s(H)` is isomorphic to the residually finite group `H`, and
it has finite index in `C s(H)`.  Hyperlinearity is preserved under finite
extensions and under subgroups.  Consequently `Gamma` is hyperlinear,
which closes the restricted Steinberg-kernel seam for the first polynomial
depth return.
