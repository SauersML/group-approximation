First suppose that `M` is finite; no countability hypothesis is needed in
this case.  Let `Phi:M->Z(M)` be its center-valued trace.  Every state
`omega` on `Z(M)` makes `omega Phi phi` and `omega Phi psi` tracial states
on `Z`, so uniqueness of the trace of `Z` gives

```text
Phi phi(a)=tau_Z(a)1=Phi psi(a)                 (a in Z).    (0)
```

The precise imported result here is Li--Hadwin--Liu, *Operators and
Matrices* 17 (2023), Theorem 4: if `A` is relatively LF in `A**`, `M` is an
arbitrary finite von Neumann algebra, and two unital star homomorphisms
`A->M` have the same center-valued trace, then they are approximately
unitarily equivalent.  An ASH algebra is relatively LF in its bidual, so it
applies to `Z`.  The theorem's formulation permits a net of unitaries; since
`Z` is separable, the usual finite-set diagonal extraction gives a sequence.

It remains to treat a semifinite `M` acting on a separable Hilbert space.
Let `z_f` be the largest central projection for which `z_f M` is finite.
Write

```text
M = M_f directSum M_infinity,
M_f=z_f M,                 M_infinity=(1-z_f)M.              (1)
```

Both central compressions of a unital embedding of the simple algebra `Z`
are unital embeddings whenever the summand is nonzero.

On `M_f`, let `Phi:M_f->Z(M_f)` be the center-valued trace.  For every state
`omega` on the center, the scalar maps

```text
omega Phi phi,             omega Phi psi
```

are tracial states on `Z`, and hence both equal its unique trace `tau_Z`.
States separate the center, so

```text
Phi phi(a)=tau_Z(a)1=Phi psi(a)                 (a in Z).    (2)
```

The Jiang--Su algebra is ASH and therefore relatively LF in its bidual.
Li--Hadwin--Liu Theorem 4 turns (2) into approximate unitary equivalence in
`M_f`.

Now work in `M_infinity`, which has no nonzero finite central summand.  Fix
`0!=a in Z` and let `p` be the range projection of `phi(a)`.  The positive
element `a*a` is full in the simple unital algebra `Z`.  Compactness of the
unit, or the elementary finite-sum fullness criterion, gives a finite `n`
such that

```text
1_Z <=_Cu (a*a) directSum ... directSum (a*a)   (n copies).
```

After applying `phi` and passing to support projections,

```text
1_(M_infinity) <=_MvN p directSum ... directSum p.           (3)
```

Relation (3) makes the central support of `p` equal to one.  Moreover `p`
has no nonzero finite central part: if `0!=z` were central and `zp` finite,
then `n(zp)` would be finite, while (3) would make the unit `z` of `zM`
subequivalent to it.  This would make `zM` a finite central summand, contrary
to (1).

The standard central finite/properly-infinite decomposition for a projection
now says that `p` is properly infinite: its finite central part is zero by
the preceding paragraph.  This structural step is not packaged in the
Li--Hadwin--Liu theorem.  Proper infiniteness absorbs every finite number of
copies, so

```text
p directSum ... directSum p <=_MvN p.                          (4)
```

Combining (3) with (4) gives `1 <=_MvN p`; the reverse subequivalence follows
from `p<=1`.  Cantor--Schroeder--Bernstein comparison for projections yields

```text
M_infinity-rank(phi(a))=[p]=[1].                            (5)
```

Thus countable decomposability is not used in the projection-comparison
step.  The same argument gives (5) for `psi(a)`; the zero element has rank
zero.  Therefore `phi` and `psi` have the same von Neumann rank on every
element of `Z`.

The precise imported result now is Li--Hadwin--Liu, Theorem 8: for a
separable unital ASH algebra `A`, a semifinite von Neumann algebra
`M subset B(H)` with `H` separable, and unital star homomorphisms `A->M`
having equal `M`-rank on every element, the maps are norm approximately
unitarily equivalent (indeed modulo the ideal generated by finite
projections).  It applies to `M_infinity`.

Taking the direct sum of the implementing unitaries in the two summands of
(1), with one common finite-set tolerance, proves approximate unitary
equivalence in `M`.

The imported results are exactly Li--Hadwin--Liu Theorems 4 and 8, with the
hypotheses stated above, plus standard von Neumann projection decomposition
and comparison.  The support-rank collapse (3)--(5), including its avoidance
of any countable-decomposability hypothesis, and the central splitting
application to `Z` are internal.
