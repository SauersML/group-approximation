Modules are left modules, and `⊗` is `⊗_{Z[G]}`. `U = U(G)`.

## Imports (recalled)

- **(J1) Dimension.** `dim_U` is defined on all `U`-modules. It is additive on short exact
  sequences and monotone on submodules and quotients. It is **cofinal**: if `M` is the directed
  union of submodules `M_λ`, then `dim_U M = sup_λ dim_U M_λ`. A finitely generated projective `U`-module
  of dimension 0 is zero. Also `dim_U(U ⊗_NG N) = dim_NG N`. (W. Lück, *L²-invariants*, Springer
  2002, Theorem 6.7 and its `U`-version Theorem 8.29; recalled, as import (J1) of
  `amenable-fp-iff-affiliated-top-homology-vanishes-proof`.)
- **(J2) Regularity.** `U` is von Neumann regular. Over a von Neumann regular ring, every finitely
  generated submodule of a projective module is a direct summand (Goodearl, *Von Neumann regular
  rings*, Theorem 1.11; import (J2) there). The theorem is stated for finitely generated projective
  modules. It extends: a finitely generated `M ⊆ Q`, with `Q ⊕ Q' = F` free, lies in a finitely
  generated free summand `F_0` of `F`. So `M` is a summand of `F_0`, hence of `F`. If `F = M ⊕ C`
  and `M ⊆ Q ⊆ F`, then `Q = M ⊕ (C ∩ Q)`.
- **(J7) Traces of idempotents.** For an idempotent `E ∈ M_n(NG)`, `dim_NG(NG^n E) = tr(E) =
  Σ_i ⟨E_ii δ_1, δ_1⟩`. This holds because `E` is similar to a projection with the same trace
  (Lück, *op. cit.*, Chapter 8; recalled).
- **Dual basis lemma.** `P` is projective iff there are `x_i ∈ P` and `f_i ∈ Hom(P, Z[G])`
  (`i ∈ I`) such that, for every `x ∈ P`, `f_i(x) = 0` for all but finitely many `i`, and
  `x = Σ_i f_i(x) x_i`.

For `f ∈ Hom(P, Z[G])` write `f^U = U ⊗ f : U ⊗ P → U`, so `f^U(u ⊗ p) = u f(p)`. Its image is
`U f(P)`, the left ideal of `U` generated by `f(P)`.

## Item 1

Let `D = dim_U(U ⊗ P) < ∞` and fix a dual basis `(x_i, f_i)_{i ∈ I}`. For finite `S ⊆ I` let
`M_S ⊆ U ⊗ P` be the `U`-span of `{1 ⊗ x_s : s ∈ S}`. The `x_i` generate `P`, so the `1 ⊗ x_i`
generate `U ⊗ P`, and the directed union of the `M_S` is `U ⊗ P`. By cofinality (J1) there is a
finite `S ⊆ I` with `dim_U M_S > D − ε`.

`U ⊗ P` is a projective `U`-module, since it is a summand of `U ⊗ F` for `F` free. `M_S` is
finitely generated, so by (J2) `U ⊗ P = M_S ⊕ N_S`. By additivity, `dim_U N_S < ε`.

Let `B_S = { i ∈ I : f_i(x_s) ≠ 0 for some s ∈ S }`. It is finite by the dual basis lemma. For
`i ∉ B_S`, `f_i^U` vanishes on `1 ⊗ x_s` for `s ∈ S`, hence on `M_S`. So
`U f_i(P) = f_i^U(U ⊗ P) = f_i^U(N_S)`, a quotient of `N_S`. Therefore
`dim_U(U f_i(P)) <= dim_U N_S < ε`.

If `P` is not finitely generated, then `f_i(P) ≠ 0` for some `i ∉ B_S`. Otherwise every
`x = Σ_{i ∈ B_S} f_i(x) x_i` lies in the span of the finitely many `x_i` with `i ∈ B_S`. Pick
`0 ≠ a ∈ f_i(P) ⊆ T(P)`. Then `U a ⊆ U f_i(P)`, so `dim_U(U a) < ε` by monotonicity. ∎

## Item 2

Take `ε = c` in item 1. For `i ∉ B_S`, a nonzero `a ∈ f_i(P)` would have `dim_U(U a) < c`,
against the hypothesis. So `f_i = 0` for `i ∉ B_S`, and `P` is generated by
`{x_i : i ∈ B_S}`, a finite set. ∎

## Item 3

*Integrality and positivity.* Let `Q` be finitely generated projective, say `Q ≅ Z[G]^n E` for an
idempotent `E ∈ M_n(Z[G])`. Then `U ⊗ Q ≅ U^n E = U ⊗_NG (NG^n E)`. By (J1) and (J7),
`dim_U(U ⊗ Q) = Σ_i (E_ii)(1)`, the sum of the coefficients of the identity in the diagonal
entries. It is an integer. If `Q ≠ 0`, then `Q → U ⊗ Q` is injective, being a summand of the
injection `Z[G]^n → U^n`. So `U ⊗ Q` is a nonzero finitely generated projective `U`-module, and
its dimension is positive by (J1), hence at least 1.

*Splitting.* Let `Q` be a finitely generated direct summand of `P`. Then `U ⊗ Q` is a summand of
`U ⊗ P`, so `dim_U(U ⊗ Q) ∈ {0, 1, …, ⌊D⌋}`. Choose such a `Q = P_0` of maximal dimension and
write `P = P_0 ⊕ P'`. Suppose `P'` had a nonzero finitely generated summand `Q'`. Then
`P_0 ⊕ Q'` would be a finitely generated summand of `P` of dimension at least
`dim(U ⊗ P_0) + 1`, a contradiction. If `P'` = 0 then `P = P_0` is finitely generated.
Conversely, if `P` is finitely generated, so is its quotient `P'`, and then `P'` is a finitely
generated summand of itself, so `P' = 0`. ∎

## Over `Q[G]`

Items 1 and 2 use only `Q[G] ⊆ U`, the dual basis lemma and (J1)–(J2). So they hold verbatim for
projective `Q[G]`-modules and `a ∈ Q[G]`. The integrality in item 3 used integer coefficients.
Over `Q[G]` it needs the trace conjecture for `G`.
