## 1. Common-interface absorption

Let `G_+` and `G_-` be the two scalar-port specializations of one finite
involution gadget.  Suppose they have representations `pi_+` and `pi_-` on
the same finite-dimensional space `K` and that, for every interface
generator `q in Q`,

```text
pi_+(q)=pi_-(q).                                        (CGP1)
```

Let `Z` be any self-adjoint involution on `H` and put
`P_plus=(I+Z)/2`, `P_minus=(I-Z)/2`.  For every internal gadget
generator `x` define

```text
Pi_Z(x)=P_plus tensor pi_+(x)+P_minus tensor pi_-(x).    (CGP2)
```

Orthogonality of `P_plus,P_minus` makes every relation block diagonal.
Thus `Pi_Z` satisfies the positive-port relations on the positive spectral
sector and the negative-port relations on the negative sector.  It also
commutes with `Z tensor I`, as required in the port equation.

On the interface, `(CGP1)` gives

```text
Pi_Z(q)=I_H tensor pi_+(q),                              (CGP3)
```

which is independent of `Z`.  Therefore for arbitrary involutions
`Z_1,...,Z_k`, commuting or not, the constructions `Pi_(Z_t)` may be made
on the same `H tensor K` and their interface generators identified.  There
are no cross terms to compare between different copies: only the operators
in `(CGP3)` are shared.

If the two interface restrictions are merely unitarily equivalent, conjugate
one whole gadget representation first; exact equality `(CGP1)` is then
restored.  Hence the actual obstruction to this construction is inequivalence
of the two amplified interface representations, not noncommutation of the
ports.

## 2. Two Magic Squares with the same first row

Put

```text
Q_1=I tensor X,       Q_2=X tensor I.                   (CGP4)
```

The odd Magic Square `A^-` is

```text
Q_1,          Q_2,          Q_1 Q_2,
Z tensor I,   I tensor Z,   Z tensor Z,
Z tensor X,   X tensor Z,   Y tensor Y.                 (CGP5)
```

Every displayed row and column is a commuting triple.  The three row
products and the first two column products are `+I`, while the last column
product is `-I`.

Now define the even square `A^+` by

```text
Q_1,          Q_2,          Q_1 Q_2,
Q_1,          I,            Q_1,
I,            Q_2,          Q_2.                        (CGP6)
```

All entries in `(CGP6)` belong to the abelian group generated by
`Q_1,Q_2`.  Every row and column product is `+I`.  Most importantly,

```text
A^+_(1j)=A^-_(1j) for j=1,2,3.                          (CGP7)
```

Thus the full first row is a common interface in the sense of `(CGP1)`.

For a source representation `rho` and ports `z_t`, set

```text
P_(t,+)=(I+rho(z_t))/2,       P_(t,-)=(I-rho(z_t))/2,

B_(t,ij)=P_(t,+) tensor A^+_(ij)
        +P_(t,-) tensor A^-_(ij).                       (CGP8)
```

Within each row or column, multiplication in `(CGP8)` is blockwise, so the
operators commute and their products are

```text
all rows:                         I,
columns 1 and 2:                  I,
column 3 of copy t:               rho(z_t) tensor I.    (CGP9)
```

Each `B_(t,ij)` is a self-adjoint involution.  Equation `(CGP7)` yields

```text
B_(t,1j)=I_H tensor A^-_(1j),                           (CGP10)
```

independently of `t`, so those three variables may be identified across all
copies.  No commutation of the spectral projections for different `z_t` was
used.

Keep every old source generator as `rho(x) tensor I_4`.  Equations
`(CGP8)--(CGP10)` give the promised extension on `H tensor C^4`.  It
preserves finite dimensionality and preserves tracial or commuting models
after tensoring the state with the normalized trace on `M_4`.  Restriction
to the old variables proves the converse existence implication.

The shared-row bouquet is connected, cyclic, and has a nontrivial common
Pauli context, yet all of its ports are representation-theoretically free.
A candidate decoder must therefore make the shared interface vary with the
port sector in a way that cannot be aligned after finite amplification.
The next bounded audit is the two-port single square, where no decomposition
into copies with a common row is available.
