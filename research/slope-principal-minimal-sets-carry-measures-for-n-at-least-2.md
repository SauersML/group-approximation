Let `n >= 2` and `G <= nV`. Let `Z ⊆ C^n` be a minimal closed `G`-invariant set that is
slope-principal (`s(g,z) = 0` whenever `g ∈ G`, `z ∈ Z`, `gz = z`). Then `Z` carries a
`G`-invariant Borel probability measure.

## Why this branch is different from n = 1

By `slope-principal-sets-of-v-are-locally-of-finite-order`:

- at `n = 1` slope-principality forces every element of `G` to have a power that is the
  identity on a neighbourhood of `Z`, so the whole question becomes the Burnside-type
  claim `locally-torsion-subgroups-of-v-have-measured-minimal-sets`;
- at `n >= 2` that rigidity is **false**. Brin's baker map `b ∈ 2V` has constant slope
  `(1,-1)` and countable periodic set, so over any aperiodic minimal subshift `S` every
  `⟨b⟩`-minimal subset of `Y = ⋂_{k >= 0} b^k(S × C)` — the natural extension of `(S,σ)`,
  on which `b` is the two-sided shift — is slope-principal vacuously while `b` acts on it
  with infinite order and without periodic points. (`S × C` itself is only forward
  `b`-invariant; the correction is recorded in Section 4 of the artifact.)

Both bullets are quoted from `slope-principal-sets-of-v-are-locally-of-finite-order`,
which was demoted to OPEN at landing on 2026-09-18 after two of three referee lenses
refuted it at the `b`-invariance step now corrected. Theorem A survived all three lenses,
but neither statement is established graph state, so this node's motivation is not
certified.

So here the hypothesis gives no information about elements that have no periodic point in
`Z`, and the compressing/saddle/locally-trivial trichotomy of
`nv-point-germ-groups-embed-in-zn-by-slope` is vacuous on baker-type minimal sets.
