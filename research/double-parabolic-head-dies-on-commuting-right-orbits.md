## Setting

- `J = F_2<S,T | TS=1>`, `Q = 1 + ST`, `P = P_{2,2}(J) <= EL_4(J)`, head
  `h = x_13(Q)`.
- For elements `g, x` of any group put `d_g(x) = x g x g`. For an
  involution `g` this is `x (g x g)`.
- Seven named elements (in `P` they are the involutions below):
  - left: `as = x_12(S)`, `at = x_12(T)`, `c = x_21(1)`;
  - right: `b1 = x_34(1)`, `bs = x_43(S)`, `bt = x_43(T)`;
  - unipotent: `u = x_13(1)`.
- Composite maps: `Lambda_s = d_as d_c`, `Lambda_t = d_at d_c`,
  `M_s = d_bs d_b1`, `M_t = d_bt d_b1` (rightmost applied first).
  In `P`: `Lambda_s u = x_13(S)`, `M_s u = x_13(S)`, and similarly for `t`;
  `Lambda_t Lambda_s u = x_13(TS) = u`; `Lambda_s Lambda_t u = x_13(ST)`, so
  `h = u . Lambda_s Lambda_t u`.

## Theorem (orbit trap)

Let `G` be any group containing involutions `as, at, c, b1, bs, bt, u` such that

- (LR) each of `as, at, c` commutes with each of `b1, bs, bt`;
- (R1) `Lambda_s u = M_s u`;
- (R2) `Lambda_t u = M_t u`;
- (R3) `Lambda_t Lambda_s u = u`.

Let `Z` be the smallest subset of `G` containing `u` and closed under
`d_b1, d_bs, d_bt`. Assume

- (F) `Z` is finite;
- (C1) `d_c d_b z = d_b d_c z` for all `z in Z` and `b in {b1, bs, bt}`;
- (C2) `d_a d_b z' = d_b d_a z'` for all `z' in d_c(Z)`, `a in {as, at}`,
  `b in {b1, bs, bt}`.

Then `Lambda_s Lambda_t u = u`, hence `h := u . Lambda_s Lambda_t u = 1`.

For commuting involutions `a, b` and any `z`,
`d_a d_b z = d_b d_a z` iff `b z b` and `a z a` commute. So (C1) and (C2) say
that certain conjugates of orbit elements commute.

No commutation among unipotent words (the `UU` relations of `Gamma_trap`) and
no linear structure are used.

## Corollaries

1. **Abelian-orbit models of any Loewy length die.** Let `G` be generated by
   the seven elements and satisfy (LR), (R1)-(R3). Every element of `Z`, and
   every conjugate of it, lies in the normal closure `<<u>>`. So if `<<u>>` is
   abelian and `G` is finite, (F), (C1), (C2) hold and `h = 1`. This covers:
   - every finite quotient of `P` in which the image of `n(M_2(J))` is
     abelian (a uniform reproof, from seven relations, that finite
     quotients of `P` kill the head);
   - every two-step or block-affine model `[[Phi(A), X], [0, Psi(B)]]` over a
     finite field that is multiplicative on the ball `B_trap` of item 2 (the
     finite-target case of `jacobson-double-parabolic-two-step-models-kill-head`,
     on a larger ball than that node's `B_0`). Conjugates `1+N`, `1+N'` of the
     image of `u` there satisfy `N W ⊂ W_1`, `N W_1 = 0`, so `N N' = 0` and
     they commute;
   - every finite linear model whose unipotent image is abelian, whatever its
     Loewy length, and every permutation model in which `<<u>>` is abelian.
2. **LEF witnesses must break (C).** All words in (LR), (R1)-(R3) lie in a
   fixed finite ball `B_trap` of `P` (checked exactly in `trap_group.py`). Any
   map from a ball of `P` containing `B_trap` and its prefixes to a finite
   group that is multiplicative there gives elements satisfying the theorem's
   hypotheses except (C). If it keeps `h != 1`, then (C1) or (C2) fails at
   some `z in Z`: two conjugates of orbit elements fail to commute in the
   target, although in `P` all of them lie in the abelian group `n(M_2(J))`.
   In particular the target's `<<u>>` is nonabelian.
3. **In `P` the orbit is infinite.** In `P` (C1), (C2) hold because
   `Z <= n(M_2(J))` is abelian, and `h != 1`. So by the theorem `Z` is
   infinite in `P`. The script lists the 10 elements up to depth 4.
4. **Loewy length is not the invariant.** The node
   `jacobson-double-parabolic-two-step-models-kill-head` left open "models
   where the unipotent radical has Loewy length at least 3". The theorem
   shows that Loewy length is irrelevant. The obstruction is the pair (finite
   orbit, commuting conjugates on it), and a head-keeping finite model must
   fail (C) somewhere on `Z`.

## Where every member of the class dies

- *Invariant:* the finite right `d`-orbit `Z` of `u` together with the
  commutation condition (C).
- *Step:* the pigeonhole. (C) transports `Lambda_a` through right words, so
  `Lambda_s, Lambda_t` preserve `Z` and `Lambda_t Lambda_s = id` on `Z`. On a
  finite set a one-sided inverse is two-sided, so `Lambda_s Lambda_t u = u`.
- Every model class in which the target's `<<u>>` is abelian meets this step
  and dies.

## Open reduction (not established)

Let `Gamma_trap` be the finitely presented group on the seven involutions
with relations (LR), (R1)-(R3) and the `UU` relations of `trap_group.py`
(the eight unipotent words pairwise commute). All its relators lie in a finite
ball of `P`, and `h != 1` in `P`.

- If every finite quotient of `Gamma_trap` kills `h`, then `P_{2,2}(J)` is not
  LEF. Reason: an LEF approximation on a ball containing the relators is a
  finite quotient of `Gamma_trap` that keeps `h`.
- By the theorem, a finite quotient that keeps `h` must fail (C) on `Z`. So
  the missing step is: in finite quotients of `Gamma_trap`, the `UU`
  relations (commutation of eight explicit orbit words) force commutation on
  the whole orbit `Z`. This is exactly where the reduction is open. A priori
  the `UU` relations constrain only the first few layers of `Z`.

  - 200 random-layer trials: 0 hits.
