**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not reviewed). No priority claimed. Three classical inputs are
recalled, not re-read:
- `SK_1(Z[1/P]) = 0` and `SL_n(Z[1/P]) = E_n(Z[1/P])` for `n ≥ 3` (Bass–Milnor–Serre);
- `K_2(Z[1/P])` is finite (Tate's computation, or the localization sequence
  `K_2(Z) → K_2(Z[1/P]) → ⊕_{p∈P} F_p^×`);
- `Z[1/P]^n ⋊ Z^P`-type metabelian relations, handled directly below.

Put `R = Z[1/P]`, `R^× = {±1} × ⟨p : p ∈ P⟩`, `D` the dyadic rationals in `(0,1)`, and `F`
Thompson's group acting on `D`. Let

    Γ^P = (R^(D) ⋊ GL(R; D)) ⋊ F,

acting on `R^(D)` by finitary affine maps and by `F` on indices.

**Claim.** `Γ^P` is finitely presented.

## Proof

It extends `integral-affine-structure-group-is-finitely-presented` in three steps. Every
relation below involves at most four indices, so one representative per `F`-orbit of ordered
tuples suffices, as in `finitary-steinberg-extensions-by-oligomorphic-actions-are-fp`.

1. **`E(R; D) ⋊ F`.** `R` is a finitely presented commutative ring:
   `Z[u_p : p ∈ P] / (p u_p − 1)`. So `St(R; D) ⋊ F` is finitely presented by the lemma of
   `finitary-steinberg-extensions-by-oligomorphic-actions-are-fp`. Its kernel onto
   `E(R; D) ⋊ F` is the stable `K_2(R)`: central and finite, with each element a word on
   finitely many indices. Divide by those finitely many words.
2. **`GL(R; D) ⋊ F`.** `GL(R; D) = E(R; D) · T`, where `T` is the finitary diagonal group,
   because `GL/E = K_1(R) = R^×` (`SK_1 = 0`). `T ⋊ F = R^× ≀_D F` is finitely presented: `R^×`
   is, and `F ↷ D` has finitely many orbits of pairs and finitely generated point stabilizers
   (Cornulier's criterion, as quoted in Zaremsky arXiv:2405.18354, Citation 2.2). Form
   `E(R; D) ⋊ (T ⋊ F)`. Its action relations are
   `δ_{u,a} x_bc(r) δ_{u,a}^{-1} = x_bc(u^{[b=a]−[c=a]} r)`, finitely many up to conjugacy.
   Then impose `δ_{u,a} δ_{u,b}^{-1} = h_ab(u)` for one pair `a ≠ b` and `u` in a basis of `R^×`,
   which identifies `T ∩ E`. The result is `GL(R; D) ⋊ F`.
3. **Translations.** Add one generator `τ = τ_{d_0}(1)` with these relations:
   - `τ` commutes with a finite generating set of the stabilizer of the vector `e_{d_0}` in
     `GL(R; D) ⋊ F`. That stabilizer is `(R^(D∖d_0) ⋊ GL(R; D∖d_0)) ⋊ F_{d_0}` in transposed
     form. It is finitely generated by steps 1–2 applied to `D ∖ d_0`, on which `F_{d_0}`
     acts with the same orbit properties.
   - `[τ, τ'] = 1` for `τ' = τ_{d_1}(1)`, a conjugate of `τ`.
   - `δ_{p,d_0} τ δ_{p,d_0}^{-1} = τ^p` for `p ∈ P`, and `δ_{−1,d_0} τ δ_{−1,d_0}^{-1} = τ^{-1}`.

   From these: `x_ab(r) τ_b x_ab(−r) = τ_b τ_a(r)` defines `τ_a(r)`. Commutativity of all
   `τ_a(r)` follows. For `a ≠ b` it is conjugate to the imposed commutator. For `a = b` the
   group `⟨τ_a, δ_{p,a} : p ∈ P⟩` is a quotient of `R ⋊ R^×`, because the `δ`'s commute (step 2)
   and act on `τ_a` by `τ_a ↦ τ_a^p`. In that group the normal closure of `τ_a` is abelian:
   `[τ_a, g τ_a g^{-1}] = 1` for `g = δ^{-k}` follows by conjugating `[τ_a, τ_a^{p^k}] = 1`.
   Additivity `τ_a(r)τ_a(s) = τ_a(r+s)` then follows from `x_ab(r)x_ab(s) = x_ab(r+s)` and
   `[x_ab(s), τ_a] = 1`, since `x_ab(s)` fixes `e_a`. So the presented group is the semidirect
   product `R^(D) ⋊ (GL(R; D) ⋊ F)`. ∎
