The notation is that of the target. In the chart `φ = φ_{e,f}` of the standard frame,
three facts are used:
- `φ(S) ∈ Σ(φ(R))` iff `det(S − R) = 0`;
- `φ(S) ∈ Σ(L_e)` iff `det S = 0`;
- `Σ(L_f)` is the complement of the chart.

## Step 0. Walls and vertices of a region

Let `B ∈ 𝔅`.
- **B is a union of chambers.** `∂B` lies in a finite union `𝒜` of rational Maslov
  cycles. So `B ∖ ∪𝒜` is open and closed in `Λ ∖ ∪𝒜`, hence a union of chambers of `𝒜`,
  and `B` is the interior of the closure of that union.
- **Walls.** `W(B)` is the set of rational Maslov cycles `Σ` such that `∂B ∩ Σ` has
  nonempty interior in `Σ`. It is intrinsic, so `W(γB) = γ W(B)`.
- **Local structure.** Near any point `q`, `B` is a union of local chambers of the members
  of `W(B)` through `q`.
  - Two local chambers of `𝒜` adjacent across a 2-dimensional face in a wall
    `Σ ∉ W(B)` are both in `B` or both outside it.
  - Local chambers of the sub-arrangement are connected through such faces, because
    removing sets of codimension `≥ 2` does not disconnect.
- **Vertices.** `V(B)` is the set of isolated points of triple intersections
  `Σ ∩ Σ' ∩ Σ''` of three distinct members of `W(B)`.
  - A real algebraic set has finitely many isolated points, so `V(B)` is finite.
  - Its points are real algebraic, defined over number fields.
  - It is equivariant: `V(γB) = γV(B)`.
- **Fields.** For an algebraic point `v ∈ Λ`, `Q(v)` is the field generated by the ratios
  of its Plücker coordinates. Every `γ ∈ Sp_4(Q)` has `Q(γv) = Q(v)`.

## Step 1. Walls through a point with no rational vector

Let `q ∈ Λ` be defined over a real quadratic field `K`, and suppose `q` contains no nonzero
rational vector. Let `σ` be the nontrivial automorphism of `K`.

1. **Smoothness.** Every rational Maslov cycle `Σ(L)` through `q` is smooth at `q`.
   Indeed `Σ(L)` is singular only at `L` itself, and `q ≠ L`. Moreover `ℓ = L ∩ q` is a
   line.
2. **Tangent planes.** Identify `T_qΛ` with the quadratic forms on `q` (textbook). The
   conormal line of `Σ(L)` at `q` is spanned by `v ⊗ v`, where `v` spans `ℓ`.
   - Chart check: at `S` with `rank(S − R) = 1` and `ker(S − R) = span(u)`, the gradient
     of `det(· − R)` is `adj(S − R) ∝ u u^T`.
3. **Distinct walls have distinct lines.** The line `ℓ` is defined over `K` and is not
   rational, because `q` has no rational vector. So `σ(ℓ) ≠ ℓ`. Both lines lie in the
   rational `L`, so `L = ℓ + σ(ℓ)`, and `L` is determined by `ℓ`. Hence distinct rational
   walls through `q` have distinct lines `ℓ`, and so distinct conormals.
4. **Independence.** Squares `v ⊗ v` of pairwise non-proportional `v` in the plane `q` lie
   on a smooth conic in the projective plane of quadratic forms. Three distinct points of
   a smooth conic are not collinear, so any three distinct rational walls through `q` have
   linearly independent conormals.
5. **Consequences.**
   - (a) Any three distinct rational walls through `q` meet transversally at `q`. So `q`
     is an isolated point of their intersection.
   - (b) With one or two walls through `q`, every local chamber has closure containing a
     `C^1` curve through `q`. For one wall this is the wall; for two it is their
     transverse intersection curve.
   - (c) With exactly three walls, each local chamber is locally diffeomorphic to an
     octant. Its closure has a pointed (simplicial) tangent cone at `q`, which contains no
     line.

## Step 2. The corners

- **The point.** Fix an odd prime `p`. Put `t = 1/(p+1)` and `R_p = diag(−1, t)`, and let
  `S_p = [[p, √(2p)], [√(2p), 2]] / (p+2)` and `q_p = φ(S_p)`.
- **It lies on three walls.** Write `s = √(2p)`.
  - `det S_p = (2p − s^2)/(p+2)^2 = 0`.
  - `det(S_p − I) = ((−2)(−p) − s^2)/(p+2)^2 = 0`.
  - `det(S_p − R_p) = (2(p+1)/(p+2)) · (p/((p+2)(p+1))) − 2p/(p+2)^2 = 0`.

  So `q_p` lies on `Σ(L_e)`, `Σ(G)` and `Σ(φ(R_p))`, where `G = φ(I) = span(e_i + f_i)`.
- **Field of definition.** `Q(q_p) = Q(√(2p))`. These fields are distinct for distinct
  `p`.
- **No rational vector.** A vector `a(e_1 + s_11 f_1 + s_12 f_2) + b(e_2 + s_12 f_1 + s_22 f_2)`
  is rational only if `a` and `b` are rational and `a s_12`, `b s_12` are rational. Since
  `s_12` is irrational, this forces `a = b = 0`. So Step 1 applies.
- **The three walls are distinct.** The kernels of `S_p`, `S_p − I` and `S_p − R_p` are
  pairwise distinct, since `I`, `R_p` and `R_p − I` are invertible. So by Step 1 their
  conormals are independent.
- **Frames.** Take the unimodular frames `(e, f)`, `(e + f, f)` and
  `e' = (e_1 − f_1, (p+1)e_2 + f_2)`, `f' = (f_1, −e_2)`.
  - For the third, check `ω(e'_i, f'_j) = δ_ij`, and `span(e') = φ(R_p)`.
  - The partners `L_f` (twice) and `span(f_1, e_2)` do not contain `q_p` in their Maslov
    cycles. For `span(f_1, e_2)`: a vector of `q_p` in it has `a = 0` and `b s_22 = 0`,
    so `b = 0`.
- **The region.** So near `q_p`, each frame's cells are the two sides of one wall. Choosing
  one cell per frame gives a region `R ∈ 𝔅_u ⊆ 𝔅` whose germ at `q_p` is a chosen local
  octant. In particular `q_p ∈ ∂R` and `q_p ∉ R`.

## Step 3. Some tile has a vertex at q_p (item 1)

- **Setup.** Suppose `𝒯` witnesses FDT, and dissect `R = ⊔_{i ≤ m} P_i` with
  `P_i = γ_i T_{j_i}`.
  - Each `P_i ⊆ int cl R = R`.
  - `cl R = ∪ cl P_i`, so some `P = P_i` has `q_p ∈ cl P`.
- **At least three walls.** Let `𝒲` be the set of members of `W(P)` through `q_p`. By
  Step 0, `P` is near `q_p` a union of local chambers of `𝒲`.
  - Suppose `|𝒲| ≤ 2`. Then by Step 1(b), `cl P` contains a `C^1` curve through `q_p`.
    The curve has tangent vectors `±τ` in the tangent cone of `cl R` at `q_p`.
  - That cone is pointed by Step 1(c), so it cannot contain both `±τ`. This is a
    contradiction.
  - The case `|𝒲| = 0` is included: then `P` would contain a neighbourhood of `q_p`, but
    `P ⊆ R ∌ q_p`.
- **A vertex.** So `|𝒲| ≥ 3`. By Step 1(a), `q_p` is an isolated point of the
  intersection of three members of `W(P)`. Hence `q_p ∈ V(P) = γ_i V(T_{j_i})`.

## Step 4. Contradiction

- The set `ℱ = {Q(v) : v ∈ V(T), T ∈ 𝒯}` is finite.
- By Step 3, `Q(√(2p)) = Q(q_p) = Q(γ_i^{-1} q_p) ∈ ℱ` for every odd prime `p`.
- There are infinitely many distinct such fields. So FDT fails. ∎

## Step 5. Pair diagrams force FDT (item 2)

- **The test element.** Let `E ∈ 𝔅` be nonempty, fix `v_0 ∈ F`, and put `E' = E × {v_0}`.
  - Let `g ∈ G` exchange `E' × C_0` and `E' × C_1` by `(y, 0x) ↔ (y, 1x)`, and fix the
    rest.
- **No mixed pieces.** Take a partition of `Y × F × C` into rectangles `Q × C_w`,
  `Q = γA`, on each of which `g` acts by one `γ'` on `Y × F` and one map `c` on `C_w`.
  - If `Q` met both `E'` and its complement, then on `(Q ∩ E') × C_w` the map `c` would be
    the letter flip.
  - On `(Q ∖ E') × C_w`, `c` would be the identity. These are the same `C_w`, so this is
    impossible.
  - So every `Q` lies in `E'` or misses it.
- **The dissection.** Fix `x ∈ C`. The rectangles with `x ∈ C_w` partition the slice
  `Y × F × {x}`. So `E'` is the disjoint union of the `Q = γA ⊆ E'` among them.
- **Reading off FDT.** Write `A = ⊔_v A_v × {v}`. Then
  `E = ⊔ γ A_{γ^{-1} v_0}`, with each `A_v ∈ 𝔅`.
  - So `𝒯 = {A_v : A ∈ 𝒜, v ∈ F} ∖ {∅}` witnesses FDT. ∎
