Let `theta:R->T` be a unital ring homomorphism and suppose `T` is
finite. Put `u_i=theta(x_i)`. Since `x_i` is invertible in `R`,
`u_i` belongs to the finite group `T^times`. Let `m_i` be its
multiplicative order. Then

~~~text
theta(x_i^(-1))=u_i^(-1)=u_i^(m_i-1)=theta(x_i^(m_i-1)).
~~~

Thus the images of all Laurent generators and their inverses already belong
to `theta(R_+)`. This proves

~~~text
theta(R) subset theta(R_+).
~~~

The reverse inclusion is tautological, proving (KFR1).

The elementary group `EL_r(S)` is generated by `e_ij(s)` with
`s in S`. Applying `theta` entrywise and using (KFR1) gives (KFR2).

For the pinned return,

~~~text
theta(h)
 =e_12(theta(x_1)^(-1)theta(x_2))
 =e_12(theta(x_1)^(m_1-1)theta(x_2)),
~~~

which is visibly in `theta(EL_r(R_+))`. Therefore, in any unitary
representation of this finite congruence image,

~~~text
X in pi(theta(Gamma))'  implies  [X,pi(theta(h))]=0.
~~~

This is exact and independent of matrix dimension or character estimates.
It covers all finite-ring specializations, including products and
nonreduced finite rings. It does not cover arbitrary asymptotic unitary maps
of the Kun--Thom actor, which need not be close to any ring specialization;
that noncongruence multiplicity sector is exactly the surviving problem.
