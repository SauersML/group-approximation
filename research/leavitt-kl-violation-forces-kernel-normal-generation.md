Let `w in R^x * <t>` have `deg_t(w) = m != 0` and kill some nontrivial
coefficient.  Replacing `t` by `t^(-1)` if necessary, take `m >= 1`.  The unit
group `R^x` is nonabelian simple (`binary-leavitt-unit-group-is-simple`), so
part (a) of `kl-simple-failure-leaves-only-the-cyclic-quotient` applies:
the relative quotient is `C_m` and `<<w>>_(R^x * <t>) = N_m`.  That is the
target.

## The equivalence, and the cycle warning it draws

`leavitt-kl-violation-via-total-cyclic-collapse` proves the converse from
part (b).  The two routes form the loop
`kl-violating-equation-over-leavitt-unit-group ->
leavitt-degree-kernel-normally-generated-by-one-word -> ...`, which `check`
reports as a dependency cycle.  The loop is correct: it records that over the
simple Leavitt unit group the existential form (some coefficient dies) and the
maximal form (the degree kernel is normally generated by one word) are the same
problem.  A least fixpoint never fires a cycle, so neither claim establishes
itself through it.  Do not delete either route to silence the warning.

The practical consequence for searches: a candidate identity that kills one
coefficient while visibly preserving another is impossible, so no partial or
localized collapse is a meaningful intermediate target.
