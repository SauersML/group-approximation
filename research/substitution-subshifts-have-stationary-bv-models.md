**ESTABLISHED (literature import plus a standard telescoping).** Source: F. Durand,
B. Host, C. Skau, *Substitutional dynamical systems, Bratteli diagrams and dimension
groups*, Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 19 (1999), arXiv:0807.3621 (TeX read at
source, local copy `$GQ/src/bh-free-03/dhs/`).

**Statement.** Let `tau` be a primitive aperiodic substitution with subshift
`(X_tau, S)`. Then `(X_tau, S)` is isomorphic to the Bratteli--Vershik system
`(X_B, V_B)` of a stationary, properly ordered Bratteli diagram `B` such that:
- there is exactly one edge from the top vertex to each vertex of level 1; and
- for levels `>= 1`, with a stationary labeling of vertices by an alphabet `A`, the
  substitution `sigma` read on `B` (the ordered list of source labels of the edges
  ranging at `V(n,a)`) satisfies: `sigma` is primitive, `|sigma(a)| >= 2` for all
  `a`, and there are letters `l, r in A` with `sigma(a)` beginning with `l` and ending
  with `r` for every `a`.

**Sources.**
- DHS Proposition 20: "The system associated to any (primitive, aperiodic)
  substitution is isomorphic to the system associated to some stationary, properly
  ordered Bratteli diagram."
- DHS Lemma 9 (Forrest): a stationary properly ordered diagram is equivalent to one
  with no multiple edges between levels 0 and 1; by DHS Proposition 8 [HPS, Thm 4.7]
  equivalent diagrams (generated by isomorphism and telescoping) give (pointedly)
  isomorphic systems.
- DHS Lemma 15: the substitution read on a stationary properly ordered diagram is
  primitive and proper, i.e. for some `p` the first and last letters of `sigma^p(a)`
  do not depend on `a`.
- Telescoping `B` to the levels `0 < 1 < 1+p < 1+2p < ...` keeps it stationary and
  properly ordered, keeps the simple top edges, and replaces the read substitution by
  `sigma^p`; taking `p` a large multiple also gives `|sigma^p(a)| >= 2` (primitivity).
