**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-algebra, elementary, not reviewed; no priority claimed, likely folklore
for subshift algebras).

**Claim.**
1. Let `Y ⊆ Σ^Z` be a subshift over a finite alphabet `Σ`, with language `ℒ(Y)`, and `K` a field. Send each
   letter `a ∈ Σ` to `ξ_a = 1_([a]) u ∈ LC(Y, K) ⋊ Z`. Here `[a]` is the cylinder `y_0 = a` and `u` is the
   shift unit. The kernel of `K<Σ> -> LC(Y, K) ⋊ Z` is spanned by the words not in `ℒ(Y)`. So the monomial
   algebra `M_Y = K<Σ>/(Σ^* \ ℒ(Y))` embeds unitally.
2. **The benchmark.** Let `X ⊆ ℕ` and `S = ℕ \ X`. The *S-gap shift* `Y_S` over `{A, B}` consists of the
   sequences all of whose factors `A B^n A` have `n ∈ S`. Its language is the set of words avoiding every
   `A B^n A` with `n ∈ X`. So `A_X = K<x, y>/(x y^n x : n ∈ X)` embeds in `LC(Y_S, K) ⋊ Z`, by
   `x ↦ 1_([A]) u` and `y ↦ 1_([B]) u`.
3. **Over Z^2.** If a `Z^2`-subshift `Z_S` has a layer whose rows have language exactly `ℒ(Y_S)`, then the
   same formulas, with `u = u_(e_1)`, embed `A_X` in `LC(Z_S, K) ⋊ Z^2`. By the one-dimensional
   simulation theorems of Durand--Romashchenko--Shen and Aubrun--Sablik (cited, not read here), an SFT
   with this property exists whenever `X` is decidable, since `Y_S` is then effective.

**Proof of 1.** Write `C_w` for the cylinder "`w` is read from position `0`". By induction,
`ξ_(w_1) ⋯ ξ_(w_m) = 1_(C_w) u^m`. The `u^m` form a basis of the crossed product as a left
`LC(Y,K)`-module. Distinct words of the same length give disjoint cylinders, and a cylinder is nonempty iff
its word lies in `ℒ(Y)`. So `Σ c_w (image of w) = 0` iff `c_w = 0` for every `w ∈ ℒ(Y)`: indicator functions
of disjoint nonempty sets are linearly independent. The span of `Σ^* \ ℒ(Y)` is a two-sided ideal, since
`ℒ(Y)` is factor-closed. ∎

**Proof of 2.** `ℒ_S`, the words avoiding `A B^n A` for `n ∈ X`, is factor-closed and bi-extendable:
appending or prepending `B` creates no new gap. So it is the language of `Y_S`. Its complement generates
the ideal `(x y^n x : n ∈ X)`. ∎

**Status of gate U (Track B) at the algebra level.** Every monomial benchmark algebra lies in a crossed
product over the fixed amenable, Farrell--Jones group `Z^2`, with all complexity in the subshift language.
This is exactly the Track B design. What remains is shared with gate G2:
- the row-language realization must be done by a *free, minimal, quantum-rigid* SFT, so that
  `LC(Z_S, F_2) ⋊ Z^2` is finitely presented and central simple;
- `Y_S` itself contains the fixed point `B^∞` and is not minimal. So a minimal `Z_S` must have row
  language `ℒ_S` without having `Y_S` as its row set. Minimality forces every word, including every
  `B^n`, to recur syndetically in two dimensions.
Given that, algebra Boone--Higman holds for the benchmark through Track B.

**Lesson for general BH.** "Complexity from the language" is literal. Conditioned translations turn a
language into a monomial algebra with no cancellation, because distinct words of the same length occupy
disjoint cylinders. So the algebra complexity barrier is met by one fixed acting group, and the whole
difficulty is moved to realizing prescribed languages by rigid minimal SFTs (G2). The group-level version
cannot be permutational (`kazhdan-groups-avoid-full-groups-of-subexponential-actions`). It has to build
Kazhdan inputs from rings generated by conditioned translations, for example `EL_3` of such rings.
