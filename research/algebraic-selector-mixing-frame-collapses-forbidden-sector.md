**ESTABLISHED.**  Let `Gamma` be a group and work in `C^*(Gamma)`.  Let
`Q,p` be commuting projections and put

```text
F=Q(1-p).                                               (ASM1)
```

For `1<=i<=N`, suppose there are partial isometries `S_i`, target support
projections `p_i`, and carrier projections `q_i` satisfying

```text
S_i^*S_i=Q,                 q_i<=p_i.                  (ASM2)
```

Assume the carrier family is an **algebraic operator frame**,

```text
sum_i S_i^*q_iS_i >= kappa Q          (kappa>0),       (ASM3)
```

and that the selector-mixing rows are exact identities,

```text
p_iS_i=S_ip                         for every i.       (ASM4)
```

Then

```text
F=0.                                                       (ASM5)
```

Thus `(ASM2)--(ASM4)` cannot coexist with a phase-safe embedding of a finite
marked selector packet for which `F` is a nonzero character projection.

## Proof boundary for ordinary words

The conclusion applies when `(ASM4)` is wordized rather than written as an
additive defining relation.  If fixed packet identities and an ordinary
commutator relator `r_i=1` imply

```text
r_i=1  iff  p_iS_i=S_ip,                               (ASM6)
```

as in the exact partial-swap calculation, then `(ASM4)` holds in the full
group C-star algebra of the quotient presentation.  It does not hold merely
in one selected tracial witness: defining group relators vanish in every
representation.

Likewise, a tight frame manufactured inside a complete finite Weyl,
Clifford, controlled-Fourier, or finite Morita packet is algebraic.  Once the
packet table embeds, its positive operator inequality `(ASM3)` holds in every
representation, not only in canonical matrix microstates.  Combining that
finite packet with the exact mixing words therefore forces `(ASM5)`.

For the D8 common-swap compiler, take `Q` to be the marked source half and
`p` the allowed-support projection of one context.  If the finite subgroup
generated by the D8 role cell and the Boolean selector subgroup embeds, then
every forbidden character atom below `Q(1-p)` is a nonzero group-algebra
projection; its canonical finite-subgroup trace is positive.  Equation
`(ASM5)` contradicts that embedding.  Avoiding the contradiction means that
the proposed quotient has collapsed part of the marked selector packet.  It
then loses the advertised nonlinear context semantics and returns to the
affine/finite-Morita induction barriers; in particular this is not a
phase-safe realization of CPS9.

## Architectural consequence

The positive mixing inequality does not admit a purely finite-packet
groupification.  A viable frame must be **matrix-only or canonical-trace-only**:
its lower bound may arise from finite cyclic trace, integer multiplicity, or
another approximation-specific return, but it may not be an operator
inequality in `C^*(Gamma)`.  The selector-mixing rows can still be ordinary
words—the obstruction is specifically to making their accompanying frame a
universal finite Weyl/Morita identity.

This is the exact zero-defect fork:

```text
universal algebraic frame + exact wordized mixing
    => forbidden marked projection is zero;

phase-safe full selector packet
    => the frame must be genuinely finite-matrix/canonical-trace-only.      (ASM7)
```
