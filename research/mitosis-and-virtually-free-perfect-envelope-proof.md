Let P=<x_1,...,x_r | R> have a given word-problem algorithm. Write
P times P using generators x_i for the first factor and y_i for
the second. Add stable letters s,d with relations

    s*x_i*s^-1=y_i,
    d*x_i*d^-1=x_i*y_i,          1<=i<=r.

Call the result M(P). These are two HNN extensions: the first-factor
copy of P is identified with the second-factor copy and with the
diagonal copy, respectively. All these maps are isomorphisms onto
their images. Britton's lemma therefore embeds P times P in M(P),
and in particular embeds P. The displayed presentation is finite:
two copies of R, the finitely many relations [x_i,y_j]=1, and the
two displayed families.

## Word problem and the free stable-letter subgroup

Membership in the associated subgroups of the base P times P is
decidable. For (u,v), the first-factor, second-factor, and diagonal
tests are respectively v=1, u=1, and u=v in P. The coordinates also
give effective expressions in the associated generators and compute
the edge isomorphisms and their inverses.

The multiple-HNN Britton reduction therefore decides the word
problem of M(P). Search for a pinch with two inverse stable letters
of the same type and a base word in the relevant associated subgroup,
replace it by the computable base word, and repeat. Each replacement
reduces the number of stable letters. A reduced word with stable
letters is nonidentity by Britton's lemma; otherwise use the word
problem of P times P. The two stable-letter types are handled as
two loop edges over the same base, so no membership problem in a
previous HNN extension is being assumed decidable without a test.

Killing the base gives a retraction

    pi:M(P)->F(s,d).

The subgroup generated by s,d is thus a free group of rank two.
Membership in it is decidable: on input w, compute the stable-letter
word pi(w) and use the word problem of M(P) to test w=pi(w).
When the test succeeds, pi(w) is the required edge expression.

In the abelianization, the HNN relations give x_i=y_i and
x_i=x_i+y_i, hence x_i=y_i=0. There are no relations on s,d
in the free quotient. Thus M(P)_ab=Z^2, generated by s,d.

## A perfect ambient group with a decidable free edge

Let K=A_5 * A_5. It is finitely presented, infinite, and perfect.
For an explicit presentation one may use two copies of the finite
multiplication-table presentation of A_5. Reduced free-product
normal forms decide its word problem.

Choose a=(1 2)(3 4) in the first A_5 and b=(1 2 3) in the second.
Normal forms identify J=<a,b> with C_2 * C_3. The subgroup

    H=ker(J->C_2 times C_3)

is freely generated by

    u=a*b*a*b^-1,
    v=a*b^-1*a*b.

Here is a direct Schreier check of this specific basis. Use the
transversal {a^i*b^j: i=0,1; j=0,1,2}. All b-edge Schreier
generators are trivial after b^3=1. For the a-edges, those at j=0
are trivial, those at j=1 are u and u^-1, and those at j=2 are
v and v^-1. Rewriting the a^2 relators just identifies the paired
inverses. No remaining relator involves u,v, proving that they
are a free basis. The same finite rewriting gives edge coordinates.

Membership in H inside K is decidable. Reduce a K-word into its
alternating A_5 syllables. It lies in J exactly when each syllable
lies in <a> or <b> in its respective factor. These are finite tests.
For such a J-word, it belongs to H exactly when its a-exponent
sum modulo 2 and b-exponent sum modulo 3 both vanish. Schreier
rewriting then expresses it as a word in u,v.

## The perfect envelope and its algorithm

Form the amalgamated free product

    W(P)=M(P) *_(F_2) K,

identifying s with u and d with v. Both edge maps are injective by
the free-subgroup proofs above. The amalgam normal form embeds
both factors, hence P. It also embeds the infinite group K, so
W(P) is infinite even when P is finite or trivial.

A finite presentation consists of the presentations of M(P) and
K together with s=u and d=v. It is perfect: the only generators
of M(P)_ab were s,d, and their images belong to the perfect group
K, so they too vanish in W(P)_ab. All base and K generators
already vanish there.

Finally, reduce a W(P)-word by the amalgam normal-form algorithm.
Each factor has decidable word problem and decidable edge membership
with effective edge coordinates, as proved above. An edge syllable
can therefore be transferred to the other factor and combined with
a neighboring syllable. Each such reduction shortens the alternating
word. A reduced alternating word of length at least two is nontrivial;
a remaining one-factor word is decided in that factor. This is a
terminating word-problem algorithm for W(P).

All finite presentations, embedding words, membership tests, and
rewriting procedures have been specified using the input presentation
and its supplied word-problem algorithm. The proof is an explicit
perfect-envelope construction, not a claim that the resulting group
is simple or a Lean formalization of its algorithms.
