Direct proof.

**Part 1.**
- **Setup.** Let `G` be countable and not finitely generated. Enumerate
  `G = {g_1, g_2, ...}` and put `G'_k = <g_1, ..., g_k>`. These subgroups
  increase, their union is `G`, and each is proper, since `G` is not finitely
  generated. Passing to the subsequence of strict increases gives
  `G_1 < G_2 < ...`, all proper, with `⋃ G_k = G`.
- **The tree `T`.**
  - Vertices: `⊔_{k>=1} G/G_k`. The coset `gG_k` has level `k`.
  - Edges: `gG_k — gG_{k+1}`, well defined because `G_k <= G_(k+1)`.
  - Every vertex has exactly one neighbour of higher level. So a cycle would have
    a vertex of minimal level with two distinct higher neighbours on it, which is
    impossible.
  - `T` is connected: `gG_k` joins upward to `gG_j = G_j` once `g in G_j`, and
    `G_1, G_2, ...` lie on one ray.
  - `G` acts by left multiplication and preserves levels, so it acts without
    inversions.
- **Distance.** Let `v_0 = G_1`, and for `g in G` let `j(g)` be the least `j` with
  `g in G_j`. The upward rays from `v_0` and `g v_0` first meet at level `j(g)`,
  since `gG_i = G_i` if and only if `g in G_i`. Hence
  `d(v_0, g v_0) = 2(j(g) - 1)`. This is unbounded on `G`, because no `G_k` is
  all of `G`.
- **The commensurated subset.** Let `X` be the `G`-set of oriented edges of `T`,
  and `M ⊆ X` the oriented edges pointing towards `v_0`. Then `gM` is the set
  pointing towards `g v_0`. The two sets differ exactly on the edges of the
  geodesic `[v_0, g v_0]`, each counted with both orientations. So
  `|gM Δ M| = 2 d(v_0, g v_0)`, which is finite: `M` is commensurated.
- **`M` is not transfixed.** Suppose `N` is `G`-invariant and `|N Δ M| = c < ∞`.
  Then `2 d(v_0, g v_0) = |gM Δ M| <= |gM Δ gN| + |N Δ M| = 2c` for all `g`,
  which contradicts unboundedness.
- **Conclusion.** `G` lacks FW. The orbit of `v_0` is unbounded and levels are
  preserved, so `G` fixes no vertex of `T`, and `G` lacks FA.

**Part 2.**
- The group generated by finitely many elements of `GL_n(Q)` has all matrix
  entries, and those of the inverses, in `Z[1/m]`, where `m` is the product of the
  denominators involved. So it lies in `GL_n(Z[1/m])`. For `n >= 2` and a prime
  `p ∤ m`, the element `e_12(1/p)` lies in `SL_n(Q)`, `B_n(Q)` and `U_n(Q)` but not
  in `GL_n(Z[1/m])`.
- So no finitely generated subgroup exhausts any of these groups, and their images
  modulo the finite centre are not finitely generated either. For `(Q,+)`,
  `Aff(Q)` and `PGL_n(Q)` the same denominator argument applies.
- Part 1 applies to each of them.

**Part 3.** This is cited, and standard.
- For `n >= 3` and every local field `K`, `SL_n(K)` has (T).
- A finite product of groups with (T) has (T).
- `SL_n(Z[1/m])` is a lattice in `SL_n(R) x ∏_{p|m} SL_n(Q_p)` (reduction
  theory), and lattices inherit (T).
- See Bekka--de la Harpe--Valette, *Kazhdan's Property (T)*, Ch. 1.
- (T) implies FW, as quoted in `fw-subgroups-of-eventually-similar-groups-virtually-embed`.
- Every finite subset of `SL_n(Q)` lies in some `SL_n(Z[1/m!])`. ∎
