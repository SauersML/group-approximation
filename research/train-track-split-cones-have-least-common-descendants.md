bh-major-mcg, 2026-09-18). Lane proof, not independently reviewed. It rests on three
standard train-track facts (F1 to F3 in the proof), recalled from Penner–Harer and not
re-read at source. Parts 3 and 4 are read at source in Agol, arXiv:1008.1606 (TeX, §3).
No priority is claimed.

## Setting

- `S` is a closed orientable surface of genus `g ≥ 2`.
- A *complete* train track `τ` is generic (trivalent), maximal and birecurrent.
- `P(τ) ⊂ ML(S)` is the cone of measured laminations carried by `τ`. It is the linear
  image of the weight cone `W(τ)` and has nonempty interior.
- For a large branch `b` of `τ`, `τ_b^L` and `τ_b^R` are its left and right splits.
- `D(τ)` is the set of *split descendants* of `τ`: tracks reached from `τ` by finitely
  many left or right splits, each with a cone of nonempty interior. Such tracks are
  complete (part 1 of the proof).

## Statement

1. **Wall locality and commutation.** Let `b ≠ b'` be large branches of `σ`.
   - `b'` is a large branch of both splits `σ_b^ε`.
   - The linear functional `ℓ_{b'}` that decides between the left and right split of `b'`
     is the same function on `P(σ_b^ε)` as on `P(σ)`.
   - `(σ_b^ε)_{b'}^{ε'} = (σ_{b'}^{ε'})_b^ε` whenever either side has a cone with nonempty
     interior.
   - Consequently `P(σ_b^ε) = P(σ) ∩ {ε·ℓ_b ≥ 0}`.
2. **Least common descendants.** Let `σ_1, σ_2 ∈ D(τ)`. Either `P(σ_1)` and `P(σ_2)` have
   disjoint interiors, or there is `ρ ∈ D(σ_1) ∩ D(σ_2)` with
   `P(ρ) = P(σ_1) ∩ P(σ_2)`. In the second case every common descendant `δ` has
   `P(δ) ⊆ P(ρ)`, so `ρ` is least at the level of cylinders.
3. **Generic Serret property** (literature). Let `λ` be a minimal measured lamination
   suited to two measured train tracks `(τ, μ)` and `(τ', μ')`. Then both reach a common
   train track `τ''` by maximal splittings (Agol, Corollary `spliteq`). This rests on
   Penner–Harer Theorem 2.3.1, that equivalent positively measured tracks have a common
   splitting, as quoted by Agol.
4. **Finite local data.** Up to `Mod(S)` there are finitely many complete tracks. Each has
   boundedly many large branches, and so finitely many single splits and finitely many
   "multisplits" (simultaneous splits of pairwise distinct large branches).

## Consequences

- **The LCM input to gate G4.** The cones `{P(σ) : σ ∈ D(τ)}` form a cylinder family
  closed under intersections with nonempty interior, with one generating cylinder for
  each such intersection. Together with part 4 this gives three of the standing
  hypotheses of Li's Garside-category finiteness theory (arXiv:2110.04505): finitely many
  objects, finitely aligned with principal intersections, and a finite Garside family
  (the multisplits). What is left for G4 is Li's conditions (F) and (t<d).
- **Rank of flats is native.** At a track with `k` large branches, the `k` binary splits
  commute and act on independent walls. So the local model is a `k`-fold product of
  binary splittings, as in Brin–Thompson `kV`, not `V`. Commuting Dehn twists supported
  in disjoint regions should act on independent coordinates. That last point needs the
  "twists are splits" fact of `train-track-trace-groupoid-hosts-closed-mcg`, Attempt 1,
  which is still recalled only. This is the structural reason rank-one codings of
  `Mod(S_g)` fail (`perfect-subgroups-are-locally-trivial-in-abelian-germ-hosts`), while
  this coding need not.
- **The right coding is the group's own moves.** The ambient unimodular dissection operad
  of `𝒯_m` is too flexible to have least common refinements. The subfamily that
  `Mod(S_g)`'s dynamics generates does have them.

## Lesson for general BH

A group's own canonical local moves, rather than an ambient linear subdivision
calculus, supply the right-LCM coding that Li's and Thumann's finiteness engines need.
The moves in question are splits of the combinatorial objects it permutes. Such moves
have two features that together give least common descendants automatically: they
commute when their supports are disjoint, and the walls that decide them are unchanged
by independent moves. The number of independent moves at a state is the local rank of
the host, so inputs with higher-rank flats get variable-rank Brin–Thompson local
models for free. The price moves to the Serret gate: every group element must be a
finite cylinder exchange for the coding. Here that gate holds on the generic set by
Penner–Harer and Agol, and stays open only at non-generic laminations.
