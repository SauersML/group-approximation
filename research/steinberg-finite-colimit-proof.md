## Step 1: the two presentations

`St_n(R)` is presented by generators `x_ij(a)` (`i!=j`, `a in R`) and the
relators

```text
x_ij(a)x_ij(b)x_ij(a+b)^-1,
[x_ij(a),x_jk(b)] x_ik(ab)^-1          (i,j,k distinct),
[x_ij(a),x_kl(b)]                      (j!=k, i!=l).                      (1)
```

Let `C=colim D`.  Its presentation has generators the elements of all
root-generated finite subgroups `F` and relators the multiplication tables
of each `F` together with the identifications along inclusions
`F' <= F`.  Since each root `x_ij(a)` lies in the finite subgroup
`x_ij(F_2 a)` of `D`, the roots are among the generators of `C`.

## Step 2: `C` maps onto `St_n(R)` and is generated by roots

The inclusions `F -> St_n(R)` are compatible, so `(SFC1)` exists and is
surjective because the roots generate.  Every generator of `C` is an
element of some root-generated `F`, hence a product of roots inside `F`,
and that product identity is a row of the multiplication table of `F`;
so `C` is generated by the roots.

## Step 3: the Steinberg relators hold in `C`

Each relator in `(1)` involves two or three roots in non-opposite
positions.  By `finite-positive-root-fragments-have-marked-regular-models`
(local finiteness of the positive unitriangular group over an
`F_p`-algebra, applied after a permutation of indices that makes the
positions involved positive), the group they generate in `St_n(R)` is a
finite `2`-group, root-generated, hence in `D`; the relator is a true
identity in that finite group, hence a relation of `C`.  Therefore the
assignment `x_ij(a) -> x_ij(a)` defines a homomorphism `St_n(R) -> C`.

## Step 4: the two maps are inverse

The composite `St_n(R) -> C -> St_n(R)` is the identity on roots, hence
the identity.  The composite `C -> St_n(R) -> C` is the identity on every
root, and `C` is generated by roots (Step 2), hence it is the identity.
So `(SFC1)` is an isomorphism.

## Step 5: corona representations as epsilon-cocycles

By `corona-representations-are-exact-on-locally-finite-subgroups`, each
finite `F_i` admits, at all large coordinates, an exact unitary
representation `rho_i` lifting `Theta`; since `rho_i` and `rho_j` both lift
`Theta` on the finite set `F_i cap F_j`, they agree there up to `o(1)`.
If they can be conjugated to agree exactly on all intersections of a
finite subdiagram whose colimit is already `St_n(R)`, the glued
representation is an exact finite-dimensional representation of
`St_n(R)`, and `compression-defect-dies-in-finite-dimensions` kills the
compression defect, hence the marked root that normally generates.  This
is `(SFC2)`.

## Step 6: finite subdiagram under finite presentation

If `St_n(R)=<X|W>` with `X`, `W` finite, take `r_0` so that every word in
`W` is a product of elements of root-generated finite subgroups contained
in the ball of radius `r_0` about `X` (possible: each relator lies in one
finite unipotent group by Step 3, and the finitely many generators lie in
cyclic groups).  The colimit `C_(r_0)` of that finite subdiagram is
generated by `X`, satisfies `W`, and maps onto `St_n(R)` compatibly with
the identity on `X`; so `C_(r_0)=St_n(R)`.
