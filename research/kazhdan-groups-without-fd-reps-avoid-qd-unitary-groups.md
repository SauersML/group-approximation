Let `Γ` be a countable group with property (T) whose only finite-dimensional unitary representations are multiples of the trivial one. Let `P` be a unital C*-algebra, and suppose the C*-subalgebra generated by the image of a homomorphism `ψ : Γ → U(P)` is quasidiagonal. For instance, `P` itself is quasidiagonal: AF, residually finite-dimensional, or `∏_n M_n(C)`. Then `ψ` is trivial.

**Consequences.**
- **Full algebra.** `C*(Γ)` is not quasidiagonal when `Γ` is nontrivial. More precisely, every quasidiagonal quotient of `C*(Γ)` is `C`, through the trivial representation.
- **The note's groups.** The hypothesis holds for every infinite simple Kazhdan group. A finite-dimensional unitary representation has a finitely generated linear image, which is residually finite by Mal'cev, while a simple infinite group is not; so the image is trivial. In particular it holds for `G_X = EL_3(LC(X,F_2)⋊Z)` (`subshift-elementary-groups-are-character-rigid` records the triviality of its finite-dimensional representations).
- **Contrast for `G_X`.** The note's LEF models give an injective homomorphism `G_X → U(∏_N M_{|Q_N|}/⊕_N M_{|Q_N|})` into a matrix corona, which is MF. So `G_X` sees MF targets and never QD targets. Any weakly dense QD subalgebra of `L(G_X)` (`separable-ii1-factors-have-weakly-dense-full-free-group-cstar`) meets `λ(G_X)`, and every nontrivial image of `G_X`, only trivially.

**Scope.**
- It is stronger than "`C*(G)` is not QD" for these groups, since it forbids nontrivial images in QD algebras.
- It is weaker than Rosenberg's theorem in one respect: it needs property (T) and the absence of finite-dimensional representations, whereas Rosenberg handles `C*_r` of every non-amenable group.
- For residually finite Kazhdan groups such as `SL_3(Z)` the hypothesis fails, and those groups do embed in `U(∏_n M_n)`.
