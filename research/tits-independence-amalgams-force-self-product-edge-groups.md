**ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** Proof: `tits-independence-amalgams-force-self-product-edge-groups-proof`.

**Setting.** Let `Γ = A *_C B` with `C ≠ A` and `C ≠ B`. Let `T` be its Bass--Serre
tree, with edge `e = (v, w)`, `Stab(v) = A`, `Stab(w) = B` and `Stab(e) = C`, and
assume `Γ` acts faithfully on `T`. Deleting the open edge `e` leaves the half-trees
`T_v ∋ v` and `T_w ∋ w`. Put

- `V = Fix(T_w)`, the elements of `C` that can move only vertices of `T_v`;
- `W = Fix(T_v)`, the elements of `C` that can move only vertices of `T_w`.

Say `Γ` has the **edge-product property** if `C = V W`. Then `C = V × W`. Tits'
independence property (P) implies it, and (P) is the hypothesis of Tits'
simplicity theorem: if a group with (P) acts minimally without a fixed end, the
subgroup generated by edge stabilizers is trivial or simple. For an amalgam that
subgroup is `⟨⟨C⟩⟩`. So Tits' theorem gives a finitely presented simple group
whenever `A` and `B` are finitely presented, `C` is finitely generated and
normally generates both `A` and `B`, and (P) holds with `C ≠ 1`. Nothing in that
scheme bounds the word problem, so it looks like a way around
`complexity-bounded-host-classes-are-not-universal`.

**Theorem.** Assume the edge-product property and `C ≠ 1`. Write `m = [A:C]`,
`n = [B:C]`, `W_x = aWa^{-1}` for `x = aC ∈ A/C`, and `V_y = bVb^{-1}` for
`y = bC ∈ B/C`.

1. `V ≠ 1` and `W ≠ 1`. Hence `C` is never one of the two factors.
2. `⊕_{x ∈ A/C, x ≠ C} W_x ≤ V` and `⊕_{y ∈ B/C, y ≠ C} V_y ≤ W`. The full sums
   `D_A = ⊕_{x ∈ A/C} W_x` and `D_B = ⊕_{y ∈ B/C} V_y` are normal in `A` and `B`,
   lie in the kernels of `A` acting on `A/C` and of `B` acting on `B/C`, and
   `C = D_A D_B`.
3. `m` and `n` are not both 2. `V` contains a subgroup isomorphic to `V × V`, and
   so does `W`. So `V` and `W` are infinite. For every nontrivial `h ∈ V ∪ W` and
   every `k`, the group `C` contains a subgroup isomorphic to `⟨h⟩^k`.
4. **Class-killing corollary.** Suppose `C` embeds in a group `H` in which finite
   subgroups have bounded order and free abelian subgroups have bounded rank.
   Then `C = 1`, and `Γ = A * B` is not simple. Such `H` include:
   - finite groups;
   - hyperbolic groups;
   - every group of finite virtual cohomological dimension, among them virtually
     polycyclic groups, arithmetic groups, mapping class groups, `Out(F_n)`,
     right-angled Artin and Coxeter groups, and 3-manifold groups;
   - proper cocompact CAT(0) groups (Bridson--Haefliger II.2.8 and the flat torus
     theorem).

   Every overgroup of `C`, in particular `A`, `B` and `Γ`, is excluded from those
   classes as well.

**What this kills.** It kills the geometric version of the Tits-(P) engine, where
the vertex groups are hyperbolic, small-cancellation, Rips-type, CAT(0) or finite
vcd. That is where hard membership problems for `C` in `A` would be cheapest to
arrange, and hard membership is what would make the amalgam's word problem hard.
The failing step is item 3: the independence splitting reproduces `V × V` inside
`V`.

The same step shows two more things:

- An amalgam whose edge group is a nontrivial free group, such as the free
  stabilizers of Burger--Mozes lattice actions, never has (P).
- A discrete action on a locally finite tree with finite stabilizers has (P) only
  when `C = 1`, which is a free product.

**What survives.** A (P)-host needs an edge group with self-similar direct
products, `V ↪ V × V`, as in Thompson's group `F` or branch groups. Its vertex
group `A` must be finitely presented and normalize the direct sum `D_A` of
`[A:C]` commuting copies of `W`. The live question is whether any finitely
presented group acts faithfully on a tree with (P), nontrivial finitely generated
edge group, and not both indices 2. That is the first step of any Tits-(P) route
to `boone-higman-conjecture`. This node records no example.
