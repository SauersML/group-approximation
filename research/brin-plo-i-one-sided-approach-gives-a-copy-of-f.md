title: "Brin 1999: a subgroup of PL_o(I) in which some element approaches exactly one end of an orbital contains a copy of F"
---

**Source.** M. G. Brin, *The ubiquity of Thompson's group F in groups of piecewise
linear homeomorphisms of the unit interval*, J. London Math. Soc. (2) 60 (1999)
449–460; arXiv:math/9705205 (preprint dated April 13, 1997), Theorem 1.

`PL_o(I)` is "the group of piecewise linear (finitely many changes of slope),
orientation preserving, self homeomorphisms of the unit interval" `I = [0,1]`
(§0 of the paper).

**Definitions, verbatim from §1** (for a subgroup `H` of `PL_o(I)`):
"The fixed point set F(H) of H is the set points fixed by all h ∈ H. The support of
H is the complement of F(H) in I." "It is easy to see that the orbitals of H are the
components of the support of H. If h ∈ H, then ⟨h⟩ is the cyclic subgroup of H
generated by h and the orbitals of ⟨h⟩ will be referred to as the orbitals of h."
"If h is in H and A is an orbital of H with an endpoint a, then we say that h
approaches a in A if some orbital B of h lies in A and has a as an endpoint."

**Theorem 1, verbatim.** "Let H be a subgroup of PL_o(I). Assume that H has an
orbital (a,b) and that some element of H approaches one end of a or b in (a,b) but
not the other. Then H contains a subgroup isomorphic to F."

The paper writes functions on the right of their arguments; the statement does not
depend on that convention.

**Scope.** Citation import. The definitions and Theorem 1 were read from pages 1
and 3 of the arXiv PDF on 2026-09-13. The proof (Lemma 2.1 and §2 of the paper) was
not re-derived here. Used by `f-free-plo-i-groups-have-equal-end-germ-kernels`.
