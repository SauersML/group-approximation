**OPEN.** For some `m >= 2` and some odd `N >= n_1(F_m)`, `LRB(m,N)` of
`local-restricted-burnside-iff-partial-burnside-finite-quotients` holds.
Equivalently (clause 3 there), for some radius `R*`, every finite quotient of the
partial Burnside group `Π_{R*}(m,N)` has exponent dividing `N`.

By clause 5 of that claim, this gives a non-residually-finite hyperbolic group
(route `non-rf-hyperbolic-group-via-local-restricted-burnside`). By the converse
in the same clause, a positive answer to the residual finiteness question for
hyperbolic groups refutes it. So proving this claim is at least as hard as
producing a non-residually-finite hyperbolic group.

## Attempts

1. **Small radii are refuted.** By
   `partial-burnside-presentations-are-golod-shafarevich`, a witness radius
   exceeds `(q - 5)/4` for every prime power `q` dividing `N`. The refuting
   groups are finite `p`-groups from the Golod--Shafarevich criterion.
2. **Nilpotent groups cannot refute large radii.** By
   `finite-nilpotent-groups-satisfy-local-restricted-burnside`, above an
   ineffective radius `R_nil(m,N)` every finite nilpotent group satisfying the
   law on short words has exponent dividing `N`. A refutation at every radius
   needs non-nilpotent finite groups of unbounded order.
3. **Zelmanov's method does not localize directly.** (Reasoning only; the
   papers were not re-read.) The Lie-ring step derives Engel-type identities in
   the associated graded Lie ring from the law on elements deep in the lower
   central or Zassenhaus series. Such elements are long words in the generators.
   For `p`-groups, clause 1 of the nilpotent claim recovers a finite radius by a
   compactness argument instead. For general finite groups, the reduction to
   prime-power exponent passes to Sylow subgroups (Hall--Higman with the
   classification of finite simple groups). Sylow subgroups are neither quotients
   nor generated by short words, so the local law does not reach them. That
   passage is the exact break.
4. **Linear refutations.** An infinite finitely generated linear group generated
   by `m` elements whose words of length `<= R` satisfy `h^N = 1` has finite
   quotients of unbounded order (Malcev), so it refutes `LRB(R, C)` for all `C`.
   The uniform Tits alternative (Breuillard--Gelander; not pinned here) suggests
   such linear groups need degree tending to infinity as `R` grows. No family
   was found.
