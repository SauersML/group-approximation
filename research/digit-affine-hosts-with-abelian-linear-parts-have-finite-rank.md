**ESTABLISHED.**

**Setting.**
- `O` is a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic zero with finite
  residue field. It has a uniformizer `pi` with `pi^e = p` for some `e >= 1`.
  Examples: `Z_p`, the unramified rings `W(F_q)`, and `Z_p[p^(1/e)]`.
- `T` is the rooted tree whose level-`k` vertices are the cosets of `pi^k O^n`
  in `O^n`. The subtree below `d + pi O^n` is identified with `T` through
  `w -> d + pi w`, for a fixed set of coset representatives `d`.
- `G <= O^n x| GL_n(O)` is a finitely generated group of affine maps
  `v -> Av + b`. These preserve `T`. Suppose `G` is *self-similar* for this
  structure: the section of every element of `G` at every first-level vertex
  lies in `G`.
- `L <= GL_n(O)` is the group of linear parts of `G`, and `N = G ∩ O^n` is the
  translation subgroup, the kernel of `G -> L`.

**Theorem.** If `L` is virtually abelian, then `N` has finite torsion-free rank:
`dim_Q (N ⊗ Q) < ∞`. So every subgroup `H <= G` is an extension of the
finite-rank abelian group `H ∩ N` by a virtually abelian group.

**Corollary (Baumslag's group).** `B = R x| Z^2`, with `R = Z[x^±1,(1+x)^-1]` and
`s, t` acting by `1+x` and `x`, embeds in no such `G`. Every normal subgroup `K`
of `B` with `B/K` virtually abelian meets `R` in a nonzero ideal, and such an
ideal has infinite rank.

**Which hypothesis does what.**
- *`pi^e = p`:* sections of translations give `N ∩ pO^n = pN`, so `N/pN` is
  finite.
- *`G` finitely generated and `L` virtually abelian:* `N` is a finitely generated
  module over the commutative ring generated by an abelian finite-index subgroup
  of `L`.
- *The commutative algebra:* a finitely generated faithful module of that kind,
  with `N/pN` finite and `∩ p^k N = 0`, has finite rank. The proof uses Krull's
  principal ideal theorem and the dimension formula over `Z`.

The derivation is `digit-affine-hosts-with-abelian-linear-parts-proof`.

**What it strengthens.** `affine-self-similar-coordinate-rings-are-one-dimensional`
bounds a section-closed commutative coordinate ring. Here no ring is assumed. The
translation subgroup may be any finitely generated module over the ring generated
by the linear parts, and it is still forced to have finite rank.

**What it does not exclude.**
- *Linear parts that are not virtually abelian.* Hosts such as `R^n x| GL_n(R)`
  for `n >= 2` are not covered. There `N` is a module over a noncommutative group
  ring, and Step 2 of the proof has no commutative ring to work over.
- *Other contractions:* uniformizers with `pi^e/p != 1`, and non-scalar
  contracting matrices.
- *Self-similar hosts that are not affine.*

**Novelty.** Modest. It is a module-level strengthening of the coordinate-ring
obstruction, built from textbook commutative algebra. No search was made for
this exact statement.
