Notation as in the target. For a marked group `(G, s)` with `s` an `m`-tuple,
`F = F_m` has basis `x_i` and words are evaluated at `s`. `(Q_n, s^(n)) -> (G, s)`
means: for each `r`, for all large `n`, the words of length `<= r` trivial in
`Q_n` are exactly those trivial in `G`.

**Lemma A (marked limits).**
- (a) *LEF groups are limits of finite groups.* Let `(G, s)` be LEF and `r >= 1`.
  Take `φ` on `B_r(G)`, injective and multiplicative, into a finite group, and
  put `Q_r = ⟨φ(s_1), ..., φ(s_m)⟩`. Then `φ(1) = 1`, `φ(s_i^{-1}) = φ(s_i)^{-1}`,
  and by induction on length `w(φ(s)) = φ(w(s))` for `|w| <= r`. Since `φ` is
  injective, `w` is trivial in `Q_r` iff it is trivial in `G`. So
  `(Q_r, φ(s)) -> (G, s)`.
- (b) *Limits of finite groups are LEF.* Suppose `(Q_n, s^(n)) -> (G, s)` with
  `Q_n` finite. Given finite `K_0 ⊆ B_r(G)`, fix representative words `u_x` of
  length `<= r`. For large `n`, agreement on words of length `<= 3r` makes
  `x -> u_x(s^(n))` injective and multiplicative on `K_0`: the words
  `u_x^{-1}u_y` and `u_x u_y u_{xy}^{-1}` are trivial in `G` iff trivial in `Q_n`.
- (c) *Compactness.* Every sequence of `m`-generated marked groups has a
  convergent subsequence. `F` is countable, so a diagonal extraction gives an
  infinite index set `I` along which every word is eventually always trivial or
  always nontrivial. The eventually trivial words form a normal subgroup `K_T`,
  and the marked group `F/K_T` is the limit along `I`.
- (d) *Finite limits.* If the limit `T` is finite, then `K_T` has finite index,
  so it is normally generated by finitely many words (Schreier). For large
  `n ∈ I` these are trivial in `Q_n`, so `Q_n` is a marked quotient of `T`.

**(iii) ⇔ (i).** This is clause 1 of
`local-restricted-burnside-iff-lef-burnside-groups-are-finite`, applied at each
`m`. An `m`-generated group is also `m'`-generated for `m' >= m` (repeat a
generator), so the failure of `LRB` is inherited by larger `m`.

**(ii) ⇒ (i).** By Lemma A(b), a limit of finite groups is LEF.

**(i) ⇒ (ii), with clauses 2 and 3.** Let `L` be infinite, generated by `m`
elements, LEF, with `g^N = 1` for all `g ∈ L`.

1. **The finite residual.** Let `K` be the intersection of all finite-index
   normal subgroups of `L`.
   - `L/K` is residually finite: any `gK ≠ K` avoids the image of some
     finite-index normal subgroup containing `K`.
   - `L/K` is finitely generated of exponent dividing `N`, so it is finite
     (`fg-rf-bounded-exponent-groups-are-finite`). It is then an `m`-generated
     finite group of exponent dividing `N`, hence a quotient of `R(m,N)`
     (`restricted-burnside-finiteness`), so `[L:K] <= |R(m,N)|`.
   - By Schreier, `K` is generated by `m' <= |R(m,N)|(m-1)+1` elements. `K` is
     infinite, since `L` is.
2. **`K` has no proper finite-index subgroup, and is perfect.** Take `H ≤ K` of
   finite index. `H` has finite index in `L`, so its normal core in `L` is a
   finite-index normal subgroup of `L`. That core contains `K`, so `H = K`. Every
   quotient of `K` therefore has no proper finite-index subgroup. `K/[K,K]` is a
   finitely generated abelian group of exponent dividing `N`, hence finite, hence
   trivial.
3. **Finite perfect approximants.** `K` is LEF: restrict the models of `L`.
   Fix a generating tuple `t` of `K` of length `m'`. By Lemma A(a) there are
   finite marked groups `(P_n, t^(n)) -> (K, t)`.
   - Since `K` is perfect, each `t_i = c_i(t)` for a word `c_i` that is a product
     of commutators. These finitely many relations hold in `P_n` for large `n`,
     so every generator of `P_n` lies in `[P_n, P_n]`: `P_n` is perfect.
   - `K ≠ 1`, so some `t_i ≠ 1`, and `P_n ≠ 1` for large `n`.
4. **Simple quotients.** A nontrivial finite perfect group `P_n` has a maximal
   normal subgroup `M_n`. The quotient `S_n = P_n/M_n` is simple and perfect,
   hence nonabelian. Mark `S_n` by the image `u^(n)` of `t^(n)`.
5. **The limit.** By Lemma A(c), `(S_n, u^(n))` converges along some infinite
   `I` to a marked group `(T, u)`.
   - *`T` is a marked quotient of `K`.* If `w(t) = 1` in `K`, then for large `n`,
     `w` is trivial in `P_n`, hence in `S_n`, hence in `T`.
   - So `T` has exponent dividing `N`, is perfect, and has no nontrivial finite
     quotient (step 2).
   - *`T` is infinite.* Otherwise, by Lemma A(d), `S_n` is a nontrivial finite
     quotient of `T` for large `n ∈ I`, which contradicts the previous bullet.
6. **Conclusion.** `T` is an infinite `m'`-generated group of exponent dividing
   `N` and a limit of the nonabelian finite simple groups `S_n`, `n ∈ I`. This
   gives (ii), clause 2 and the first part of clause 3.

**Clause 3, linear degree.** `(T, u)` is an infinite `m'`-generated group of
exponent dividing `N`, and `(S_n, u^(n))_{n ∈ I}` converges to it. Clause 4 of
`lef-burnside-approximants-are-nonlinear-and-nonsolvable` applies to this
sequence: for each `d`, only finitely many `S_n` have a faithful representation
of dimension `<= d` over a field.

**Clause 4.**
- **First bullet.** If no infinite finitely generated group of exponent dividing
  `N` is a limit of nonabelian finite simple groups, (ii) fails at `N`. So (i)
  fails, and every `m`-generated LEF group of exponent dividing `N` is finite.
  The first bullet of clause 4 of
  `local-restricted-burnside-iff-lef-burnside-groups-are-finite` then makes `G_k`
  non-residually-finite for all sufficiently large `k`.
- **Second bullet.** If infinitely many `G_k` are residually finite, the second
  bullet of that clause makes `B(m,N)` an infinite LEF group of exponent `N`.
  That is (i), hence (ii). ∎
