Let `Gamma = EL_r(R_+) < G = EL_r(R) x| SL_d(Z)` be the Kun--Thom Theorem E
pair with `R = F_q[x_1^(+-1), ..., x_d^(+-1)]`, `d >= 3`, and `r` large
enough that `H_2(EL_r(R); Z) ~= K_2(R)` (the stable range for `K_2` of the
elementary group; `r >= d + 3` suffices by van der Kallen's stability bound
`r >= sr(R) + 2` with `sr(R) <= d + 1`).  Kun--Thom's Theorem E allows any
`r >= 3`, so this is still a Kun--Thom pair.  Let `W = (+_(G/Gamma) C_2) x| G`
and `D = G *_Gamma G`.

**Theorem.**  `H^2(G; Q) = H^2(W; Q) = H^2(D; Q) = 0`.  Consequently every
central extension of `W` or of `D` by a finitely generated abelian group has
a torsion class, and by `sofic-central-covers-need-nontorsion-classes` (2)
**no central cover of `W` or `D` is sofic**: any such cover sits with finite
index inside `Z^k x W` (resp. `Z^k x D`) after dividing out finite central
torsion.  In particular Thom's central-quotient descent
(`thom-central-corner-criterion`) cannot produce hyperlinearity of `W` or
`D` from a sofic group.

**Inputs, at statement level** (numbers not re-verified against the PDFs
in this session):
- fundamental theorem of algebraic K-theory for the regular ring `F_q`
  (Bass--Heller--Swan, Quillen): `K_2(A[t, t^(-1)]) = K_2(A) (+) K_1(A)` for
  `A` regular, iterated over the `d` Laurent variables, with `K_2(F_q) = 0`
  (Steinberg/Milnor), `K_1(F_q) = F_q^*`, `K_0(F_q) = Z`; hence
  `K_2(R) ~= (F_q^*)^d (+) Z^(d choose 2)`, the free part generated by the
  Steinberg symbols `{x_i, x_j}`, `i < j`;
- the universal central extension of `E_r(R)` is the Steinberg group with
  kernel `K_2(r, R)` (Kervaire, Steinberg), and `K_2(r, R) -> K_2(R)` is an
  isomorphism for `r` in the stable range (van der Kallen);
- `H^2(SL_d(Z); Q) = 0` for `d >= 3` (Borel's stable computation, and
  Soule for `d = 3`).

**Why the free part of `K_2` carries no `SL_d(Z)`-invariants.**  Steinberg
symbols are bimultiplicative and antisymmetric, so on monomials
`{x^u, x^w} = (u wedge w) . {x_1, x_2}`-type: the free part of `K_2(R) (x) Q`
is `Lambda^2 Q^d` with `SL_d(Z)` acting through its natural action on
`Q^d` (the monomial substitution `x^v |-> x^(Av)`).  For `d >= 3`,
`Lambda^2 Q^d` is a nontrivial irreducible `SL_d`-module, so its
`SL_d(Z)`-invariants (Zariski density of `SL_d(Z)` in `SL_d`) vanish.  For
`d = 2` the determinant would be invariant; `d >= 3` is exactly the
Kun--Thom range.
