**ESTABLISHED (literature import plus a two-line translation)** by
[[free-group-minimal-overgroup-rank-is-fox-rank-citation]].

Let `F` be a free group with finite basis `f_1, …, f_q`, and let `H ≤ F` be
finitely generated, with basis `h_1, …, h_p` (`p = rk H`; `p = 0` if `H = 1`).
Let `A = (∂h_j/∂f_k) ∈ M_{p×q}(Z F)` be the Fox matrix, and `rk(A)` its von
Neumann rank over `N(F)` (trace of the left support projection). Put

```text
π̄(H ≤ F) = min{ rk L : H ≤ L ≤ F }.
```

Then

```text
rk(A) = π̄(H ≤ F).
```

In particular `rk(A) = rk H` iff `H` is compressed, which is the imported
`compressed-subgroups-of-free-groups-are-l2-independent` in rank form.

## Source statements (verbatim, arXiv:2403.09515v2, 3 Feb 2026)

Read from the arXiv PDF on 2026-09-19 (page numbers are the printed ones).

- **Theorem 3.12** (p. 15): "Let K be a field of characteristic 0 or 2, F a
  free group and H a finitely generated subgroup of F. Then
  (12) π̄(H≤F) = rk(H) − β^{K[F]}_1 (I_{K[F]}/ ^F I_{K[H]}).
  Moreover, if H̃ is the D_{K[F]}-closure of H, then rk(H̃) = π̄(H≤F)."
- **Definition of π̄** (p. 15, §3.5): "π̄(H≤F) = min{rk(L) : H≤L≤F}."
- **Definition of β** (p. 6, (P1)): "the embedding k[G] ↪ D is the universal
  division k[G]-ring of fractions. By definition, it is uniquely determined up
  to k[G]-isomorphism (see [Mal80]); to emphasize this, we denote D by
  D_{k[G]}. … We define β^{k[G]}_i(M) = dim_{D_{k[G]}} Tor_i(D_{k[G]}, M)."
- **Identification with the Linnell ring** (p. 7): "The result of Hughes
  implies that if G is locally indicable, then there exists at most one
  division K[G]-ring of fractions satisfying (P4). In the case of free group F
  this has two consequences. First, for any subgroup H of F, D_H is isomorphic
  to D_{K[H]} as a K[H]-ring and second that the division Q[F]-ring D(F)
  mentioned in the introduction is isomorphic to D_{Q[F]} as a Q[F]-ring."
- **D(F)** (p. 5): "the division closure D(G) of Q[G] in U(G)"; "In this paper
  we make use of Linnell's solution of the Strong Atiyah Conjecture for free
  groups [Lin93]."
- The notation `^F I_{K[H]}` is the left ideal of `K[F]` generated by the
  augmentation ideal `I_{K[H]}`.

## Translation into the Fox rank (not in the paper; checked here)

Take `K = Q` and write `D = D_{Q[F]} ≅ D(F)`.

1. `I_{Q[F]}` is a free left `Q[F]`-module on `f_k − 1`. `I_{Q[H]}` is free on
   `h_j − 1` over `Q[H]`, and `Q[F]` is free as a right `Q[H]`-module, so
   `^F I_{Q[H]} = Q[F] ⊗_{Q[H]} I_{Q[H]}` is free on `h_j − 1`. Under these
   bases, the inclusion `^F I_{Q[H]} → I_{Q[F]}` is `v ↦ vA`, because
   `h_j − 1 = Σ_k A_{jk}(f_k − 1)`.
2. The short exact sequence `0 → ^F I_{Q[H]} → I_{Q[F]} → I_{Q[F]}/^F I_{Q[H]} → 0`
   and `Tor_1(D, I_{Q[F]}) = 0` give
   `Tor_1(D, I_{Q[F]}/^F I_{Q[H]}) = ker( D^{1×p} → D^{1×q}, v ↦ vA )`.
   So `β_1 = p − rk_D(A)`, and (12) reads `π̄(H ≤ F) = rk_D(A)`.
3. By `linnell-dimension-equals-skew-field-rank`, applied to the cokernel of
   `A` and with `lueck-dimension-additivity-cofinality-and-induction`, the rank
   over `D(F) ≅ D` equals the `N(F)`-rank. The graph node uses the closure
   of `C[F]`; it contains the closure of `Q[F]` as a sub-division ring, and
   Gaussian elimination shows a matrix over a division ring keeps its rank in
   any larger division ring. (This is exactly steps 4–5 of §3 of
   `fpbs-sandwich-cost-fox-rank-lower-bound-proof`, with `q − p` replaced by
   `q − rk_D(A)`.)
