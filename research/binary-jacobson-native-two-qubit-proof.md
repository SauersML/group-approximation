Every root element in `(J2Q1)` is an involution because the coefficient ring
has characteristic two.  The Steinberg commutator relation through the
middle indices `2` and `4` gives

```text
[x_12(Q),x_23(1)]=x_13(Q),
[x_14(Q),x_43(1)]=x_13(Q).                              (1)
```

Roots in one common row commute, as do roots in one common column.  The two
remaining cross pairs are distant.  Hence

```text
[X_0,X_1]=[Z_0,Z_1]=[X_0,Z_1]=[X_1,Z_0]=1.             (2)
```

The root of `w=x_13(Q)` shares a row or column with each of the four roots,
so it commutes with all of them.  This proves the finite packet relations.

Now let `P=(1-rho(w))/2`.  Centrality makes `P` reducing, and `rho(w)|_P=-P`.
For involutions `X,Z`, the commutator equation `[X,Z]=-1` is equivalent to
`XZ=-ZX`; applying this to `(1)` gives `(J2Q4)`.  The universal complex
star-algebra generated by one anticommuting pair of self-adjoint unitaries is
`M_2(C)`.  The two pairs commute, so their joint algebra is a unital quotient
of `M_2 tensor M_2=M_4`.  Since `M_4` is simple and `P!=0`, the unital map is
injective.  Every finite-dimensional representation is therefore
`M_4 tensor I_m`, proving `(J2Q5)` and the divisibility of `rank(P)`.

The exact four-Pauli calculation in
`four-pauli-standard-polynomial-gives-a-fixed-rectangular-gap` gives

```text
||s_4(X_0,Z_0,X_1,Z_1)|_P||_op=8.                      (3)
```

Indeed its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm is `8`, so its operator norm is
at least `8`, while the explicit Pauli evaluation is the scalar `8` times a
unitary.  Tensoring with `I_m` changes neither value.

Finally the packet is fixed and finite.  Approximate involutions and its
matrix-unit combinations can be rounded with the dimension-free modulus of
`opnorm-packet-exactification-is-dimension-free`; fixed-word telescoping
transfers the correction back to `(J2Q1)`.  A nonzero rounded negative cut
then carries the exact packet just analyzed.
