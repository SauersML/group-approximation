**OPEN** (lane bh-free-54, 2026-09-18). The first `m ≥ 2` case is `m = 2`.

## Statement

Let `𝒪_E ⊂ 𝒪_m` be the operad generated by one edge split, with transformations
`S_{m+1}` (`transvections-are-single-carets-of-the-edge-split-operad`). An
`𝒪_E`-dissection is a dissection obtained from `Δ` by repeatedly splitting one
piece at an edge midpoint. `D ≥_E D'` means that `D` is obtained from `D'` by such
splits.

1. **(Ore.)** Any two `𝒪_E`-dissections `D_1, D_2` of `Δ` have a common upper bound
   `D ≥_E D_1, D_2`.
2. **(Finite type.)** The spine of the `≥_E`-poset of nontrivial `𝒪_E`-dissections is
   finite (arXiv:1409.1085, Definition `79040`).

Cancellation is automatic, since the operations are mono and epi. So by Thumann's
theorem (Theorem `41762` of the source, read in the LaTeX), 1 and 2 would make
`π_1(𝒪_E)` of type `F_∞`. By the transvection node, that group contains
`GL_{m+1}(Z)`.

## An equivalent monoid form of the local obstruction

- **Nodes.** Write `M_E ⊂ GL_{m+1}(Z)` for the monoid generated by the nonnegative
  elementary matrices `I + E_{ij}` and the permutation matrices. The pieces that
  can occur in `𝒪_E`-dissections of `Δ` (the *nodes*) are exactly the cones `gΔ`
  with `g ∈ M_E`.
- **Why.** An edge split multiplies a frame on the right by `I + E_{ij}`, up to a
  permutation. Conversely, a first split at `{x_i ≥ x_j}` is left multiplication by
  `(I + E_{ij})^{-1}`.
- **A necessary condition for 1.** Take nodes `qΔ` and `hΔ` with overlapping
  interiors. The common sub-nodes `qM_E ∩ hM_E` (as cones) must cover `qΔ ∩ hΔ` up
  to measure zero. This is a Serret-type condition on the positive monoid `M_E`.
  - At `m = 1`, `M_E` is the free monoid on `L, R` and the nodes form a tree. Two
    nodes are nested or disjoint, and 1 and 2 are trivial.
  - At `m = 2`, `M_E` is strictly smaller than the monoid of all nonnegative
    unimodular matrices. The cone `cone((0,1,3),(1,1,1),(2,1,0))` is not a node, so
    nodes can overlap without either containing the other.

## Attempts

1. **Atoms, by hand** (lane bh-free-54, 2026-09-18). Any two first splits `E_{ij}`
   and `E_{ik}` have exactly two minimal common upper bounds. They have 5 pieces
   each and are related by an Atiyah flop at `2e_i + e_j + e_k`. Both are
   `≥_E`-above both splits (checked piece by piece in the half frames). So local
   Ore holds on atoms.
2. **Bounded MSI tests** (lane bh-free-54, 2026-09-18; a few cores, minutes). The
   scripts `ore.py` and `chain.py` and their outputs are in
   `research/artifacts/gq-bh-free-54-edge-split-operad-tests.md`.
   - **Brute force (inconclusive).** Among the 12090 pairs of `𝒪_E`-dissections with
     2 to 4 pieces, 7266 have no common `≥_E`-upper bound with at most 8 pieces.
     - The first failing pair is `E_{02}` against `E_{01}` followed by a split at
       `2e_0 + e_1`.
     - Its obstruction dissolves by hand. The cut plane `x_0 = x_2` becomes
       `𝒪_E`-constructible in the frame of `cone(e_0, 2e_0 + e_1, e_2)` after three
       splits, which needs more than 8 pieces in total.
     - There are already 10^7 `𝒪_E`-dissections with at most 9 pieces, so brute
       force cannot go further.
   - **The canonical chain is not cofinal.** Let `U_k` be the `k`-fold iterated
     barycentric edge split (6, 36, 216, 1296, 7776 pieces; each `U_k` is an
     `𝒪_E`-dissection).
     - Of the 156 `𝒪_E`-dissections with at most 4 pieces, 36 are `≤_E` no `U_k`
       with `k ≤ 5`. The covered set stops growing after `k = 3`.
     - Pull back `U_k` restricted to the half `cone(e_0, e_0+e_1, e_2)` into that
       half's frame. The result is `≥_E U_1` but never `≥_E U_2`, for `k ≤ 5`.
     - The quarter `cone(e_0, 2e_0+e_1, e_2)` gives nothing even at `U_1`.
     - **Reading.** Barycentric pieces are generally not nodes relative to a
       sub-node's frame. So Ore's condition, if true, cannot be witnessed
       uniformly; common refinements must adapt to both frames.
   - **Spine (lower bound).** Starting from the three atoms, two rounds of minimal
     common upper bounds inside at most 9 pieces gave 6 new elements (all of size
     5, the flop pairs) and then 7 more (sizes 6–8). A third round found no new
     element within the window, but 27 bounds were truncated. So the spine has at
     least 16 elements, and its finiteness is undecided.
3. **A constraint from property (T), and where it leaves the route** (lane
   bh-free-54, 2026-09-18; recalled, not re-read).
   - **Premises.**
     - `SL_3(Z)` is infinite with property (T), so it is not a-T-menable. Nor is any
       group containing it.
     - Farley proved that `V` (and diagram groups) act properly on CAT(0) cube
       complexes built from expansions, so they are a-T-menable (recalled, not
       re-read). His argument uses the tree structure of expansions.
   - **Consequence.**
     - No host containing `SL_3(Z)` can be one to which that cubical construction
       applies. The non-tree features met here are forced: overlapping nodes, flop
       relations, and the failure of the barycentric chain to be cofinal.
     - At `m = 1`, `𝒯_1 ⊇ SL_2(Z)` is `V`-like, which is consistent, since
       `SL_2(Z)` is a-T-menable.
   - **What it does not show.** This does not refute 1. It shows that a proof of 1
     must use the non-tree structure and cannot come from a free or tree-like
     suboperad.
4. **Ore's condition reduced to one local rearrangement; the payoff made
   group-free** (lane bh-free-54, 2026-09-18).
   - **Plane lemma, proved for `m = 2`**
     (`edge-splits-resolve-every-rational-plane`). Edge splits resolve every
     rational plane, with a strictly decreasing crossing-complexity potential. So
     edge-split dissections are cofinal among all dissections. Greedy evidence
     supports ranks 4 to 6.
   - **Ore's condition reduces to LD_atom** (route
     `edge-split-ore-via-atom-plane-restriction`). LD_atom says that an edge-split
     dissection respecting a first-split plane can be rearranged to split there
     first (`edge-split-dissections-respecting-an-atom-plane-split-there`).
     - It holds with 0 violations in 106830 instances up to 7 pieces.
     - Its stronger form, restriction to any node that is a union of pieces, holds
       with 0 violations in 45075 instances up to 6 pieces.
     - The proof is done except for the far-edge sub-case, which closes on the
       family of unit-corner lines.
     - So the brute-force failures of Attempt 2 were artefacts of the size window.
   - **The payoff needs only Ore's condition and finite type**
     (`ore-operad-groups-contain-the-derived-full-group`,
     `ore-operad-groups-have-type-a-clopen-actions`,
     `closed-mcg-bh-via-edge-split-operad`).
     - Ore's condition puts `D(𝒯_m)` inside `G_E` (Matui's normal-subgroup theorem,
       recalled). So `Mod(S_g)` and `SL_{m+1}(Z)` lie in `G_E` because they are
       perfect.
     - `G_E` acts with type (A) on accessible clopens without being full.
5. **LD_atom is false; the correct split of the Ore half** (lane bh-free-54,
   2026-09-18, second pass).
   - **The Attempt 4 reduction is dead.** A 17-piece split tree respects `x_0 = x_1`
     but is not above `E_{(01)}`: it contains bh-free-61's non-inherited depth-five
     cell (`edge-split-order-is-not-geometric-refinement`). The route
     `edge-split-ore-via-atom-plane-restriction` is dead.
   - **The replacement, in every rank**
     (`edge-split-ore-iff-synchronization-and-tree-domination`):
     - Ore ⟺ (Sync′) ∧ (TD), and Ore ⟺ Ore against single atoms.
     - (Sync_m) ∧ (TD) gives saturation `π_1(𝒪_E) = 𝒯_m`. So Matui is not needed
       for `Mod(S_g) ≤ G_E`.
     - (Sync_m) is bh-free-61's gate. (TD),
       `edge-split-descendant-dissections-are-tree-dominated`, is new and purely
       combinatorial.
   - **Evidence.** The reversing search (MSI, single core, minutes) found no failure:
     - all 15129 path-tree/atom pairs up to entry sum 14, including the 72 pairs on
       the 24 non-inherited cells;
     - 414 random tree pairs with up to 40 pieces, with 26 more hitting the budget;
     - all four recorded full-operad primes are dominated.
   - **Finite type.** The spine is untouched by this pass. Reversing outputs are not
     minimal bounds, so they give no information on spine size.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Two kinds of host.** For a linear group, the Cantor-host route to
  Boone–Higman comes down to a Garside/Ore-type property of one positive monoid:
  the elementary-positive monoid `M_E`, whose nodes are the pieces of edge splits.
  - In rank 2 it is free, so the host is `V`-like and Farley-cubical.
  - In rank ≥ 3, property (T) forbids any cubical, tree-like host. The host must
    be genuinely higher-rank, as in the `nV` family.
- **Where the gate sits.** The finiteness gate is Ore's condition for a non-tree
  positive monoid. The same shape is known to be the crux for Brin–Thompson
  groups, where the cube-cutting lattice makes it hold.
- **What to look for next.** A transferable tool would be a positive monoid of
  `GL_3(Z)` with a lattice property: intersections of nodes are unions of nodes,
  as for dyadic bricks.
