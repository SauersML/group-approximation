## Setting

Notation of `closed-mcg-embeds-in-rational-projective-full-group`: `Ŝ_m` is the
Stone space of the Boolean algebra `B_m` of rational polyhedral regular open
subsets of `S^m`, and `Δ = int cone(e_0, …, e_m)`.

- A *cell* is `cone(F) = int cone(F e_0, …, F e_m)` for `F ∈ GL_{m+1}(Z)`. Its
  *slots* are the columns `w_k = F e_k`.
- The *edge split* of a cell along slots `a ≠ b` replaces `w_b` by `w_a + w_b`
  (frame `F T_{ab}`, where `T_{ab} = I + E_{ab}`) or `w_a` by `w_a + w_b` (frame
  `F T_{ba}`). The two children tile the cell. In the cell's coordinates `y`, they
  are `{y_b ≤ y_a}` and `{y_a ≤ y_b}`.
- *Descendants* of a cell are the cells reached by iterated edge splits, that is
  `cone(F M)` with `M` in the monoid `𝕄 = ⟨T_{ab} : a ≠ b⟩⁺`.

## Statement

1. **Cut lemma.** Let `C` be a cell and `ℓ` a nonzero integral linear form. Then
   `C` is a finite disjoint union, up to walls, of descendants of `C`, each lying
   in `{ℓ ≥ 0}` or in `{ℓ ≤ 0}`. The descendants are produced by an explicit
   Euclid-type algorithm.
2. **Basis.** Every nonempty element of `B_m` is a finite disjoint join of
   descendants of the `2^{m+1}` orthant cells `εΔ`. Equivalently, those
   descendants form a basis of clopen sets of `Ŝ_m`.

Part 2 gives §3 of `closed-mcg-rational-projective-full-group-proof` without
toric resolution.

## Proof of the cut lemma

- **Coefficients.** For a cell with slots `w_k`, put `a_k = ℓ(w_k) ∈ Z`. On the
  cell, `ℓ = Σ a_k y_k` with every `y_k > 0`. So the cell lies on one side of
  `ℓ = 0` unless it *straddles*: some `a_k > 0` and some `a_k < 0`.
- **Children.** Splitting along `(a, b)` changes a single coefficient: the child
  that replaces `w_b` has `a_b ↦ a_a + a_b`, and the other child has
  `a_a ↦ a_a + a_b`.
- **The rule.** Let `T = max |a_k|` and `c_T = #{k : |a_k| = T}`. Pick `i` with
  `|a_i| = T`, and `j` with `a_j` of the opposite sign. If the maximal entries
  have both signs, take `j` maximal too. Split along `(i, j)`. Put
  `a_i = σT`, `a_j = −σN` with `1 ≤ N ≤ T`. The new coefficient is
  `s = σ(T − N)`, with `|s| ≤ T − 1`.
  - The child replacing slot `i`: an entry of size `T` becomes `T − N < T`, so
    `(T, c_T)` drops lexicographically.
  - The child replacing slot `j`, when `N = T`: an entry of size `T` becomes `0`,
    so `(T, c_T)` drops.
  - The child replacing slot `j`, when `N < T`: the maximal entries are
    untouched, so `T`, `c_T` and their common sign `σ` are unchanged. Slot `j`
    goes from sign `−σ` to sign `σ`, so the number `n_{−σ}` of entries of sign
    `−σ` drops by one.
- **Termination.** Along any branch of straddling cells, `(T, c_T)` is
  lexicographically non-increasing, so it is eventually constant. From then on,
  `σ` is fixed and `n_{−σ}` strictly decreases. So every branch is finite. The
  splitting tree is binary, so by König's lemma it is finite. Its leaves do not
  straddle, as claimed.

## Proof of the basis statement

Let `u ∈ B_m` be generated by half-spaces `H_{ℓ_1}, …, H_{ℓ_r}`.
- The orthant cells tile `S^m`.
- Cut each orthant cell by `ℓ_1`, then each resulting cell by `ℓ_2`, and so on.
  The final cells are descendants of the orthants, and each has interior inside
  one open chamber of the arrangement `{ℓ_t = 0}`.
- In the regular open algebra, `u` is the join of the chambers it contains, so
  `u` is the disjoint join of the final cells lying in `u`.
- Conversely, every cell lies in `B_m`, since it is an intersection of `m+1`
  rational half-spaces.

## Remarks

- **Rank one.** For `m = 1`, this is the Stern–Brocot/Euclid algorithm on
  `(a_0, a_1)`.
- **The choice is adaptive.** The rule picks the pair from the signs and sizes
  of the coefficients. Whether some splitting schedule fixed in advance also
  cuts every rational hyperplane is not checked here. The descendants form a DAG,
  not a tree: a cell can have several parents. `edge-split-descendants-are-not-factor-closed`
  shows that for `m ≥ 2` this DAG is not factor-closed.

## Lesson for general BH

A Stein–Farley proof that a full group is finitely presented needs three inputs:
1. cofinality: the elementary cells cut out every clopen;
2. synchronization: any two expansions have a common expansion;
3. connectivity of descending links.

In `V`, `nV` and Röver–Nekrashevych groups, all three come from the tree (or
product of trees) of cells. This node shows that input 1 needs no tree. A finite list of
elementary moves, up to the structure group, together with a Euclid-type
potential on the coefficients of one linear form, is enough. So for hosts whose
cells are unimodular simplices, the real gates are inputs 2 and 3.
