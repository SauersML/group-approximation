**1. Quotients and isomorphic copies.** A discrete group `Gamma` has (T) iff
it has a Kazhdan pair: a finite `Q ⊆ Gamma` and `ε > 0` such that every
unitary representation with a `(Q, ε)`-invariant unit vector has a nonzero
invariant vector. By `elementary-groups-over-fg-rings-have-property-t`,
`EL_n(R)` has (T), so it has a Kazhdan pair `(Q, ε)`.

Let `p : EL_n(R) -> H` be onto. Let `π` be a unitary representation of `H`
with a unit vector `ξ` satisfying `||π(p(q))ξ - ξ|| < ε` for all `q ∈ Q`.
Then `π ∘ p` is a unitary representation of `EL_n(R)` for which `ξ` is
`(Q, ε)`-invariant. So there is `η != 0` with `π(p(g))η = η` for all `g`.
Since `p` is onto, `η` is `H`-invariant. Hence `(p(Q), ε)` is a Kazhdan pair
for `H`. An abstract isomorphism carries Kazhdan pairs to Kazhdan pairs.

**2. Elementary Leavitt hosts.** `B` is finitely presented, so it is
generated as an `F_2`-algebra by finitely many `b_1, ..., b_k`. `L` is
generated by `x_1, x_2, y_1, y_2`. The ring `B ⊗_(F_2) L` is generated as a
ring by `b_i ⊗ 1`, `1 ⊗ x_j` and `1 ⊗ y_j`, because `F_2` is the prime
subring and elementary tensors of monomials span. So `R = B ⊗ L` is finitely
generated and part 1 applies to `E_n(R) = EL_n(R)`.

**3. Unit-group hosts.** Under the stated hypotheses,
`central-simple-leavitt-tensor-unit-groups-are-simple`, part 2, gives an
isomorphism `R^x ≅ GL_3(R) = E_3(R)`. By step 2, `R` is finitely generated,
so `E_3(R)` has (T), and part 1 transfers (T) to `R^x`.

**4. Projective hosts.** `PEL_4(L)` is a quotient of `EL_4(L)`, and `L` is
finitely generated. Part 1 applies with `n = 4`.
