Notation as in the claim: `D = Z[1/p]`, `Lambda = F_0 x <pI>`,
`G = D^2 x| Lambda` acting affinely, `Gamma = Z^2`, `o = Gamma in G/Gamma`,
and `e_x` the lamp at `x`.

**1. Orderability.**  `F_0` is free and `<pI>` is infinite cyclic.  The scalar
`pI` is central in `GL_2(D)`, so `Lambda ~= F_0 x Z`.  This group is locally
indicable, since free groups and `Z` are and local indicability is closed under
extensions.  `D^2` is torsion-free abelian, hence locally indicable, so `G` is
locally indicable.  So is `H`, an extension of the torsion-free abelian group
`direct_sum Z` by `G`.  Locally indicable groups are left-orderable.

**2. Relative property (T).**  Navas, arXiv:1712.06462v2, p. 3, verbatim:
"Letting `F_2 ⊂ SL(2,Z)` be a finite-index subgroup, the semidirect product
`G := F_2 ⋉ Z^2` still has the relative property (T) (with respect to `Z^2`)."
`Z^2 x| F_0 <= G`.  Relative property (T) of a pair `(K, A)` passes to `(G, A)`
for every overgroup `G` of `K`: an affine isometric action of `G` restricts to
`K`, which has an `A`-fixed point.  `Gamma = Z^2` is abelian, hence amenable.

**3. Infranormality.**  Conjugation by a translation `v in D^2` fixes `Gamma`
pointwise, and conjugation by `A in Lambda` maps `Gamma` onto `A Z^2`.

- `F_0 <= SL_2(Z)` preserves `Z^2`.
- `pI` maps `Z^2` onto `pZ^2 < Z^2`.

So `D^2`, `F_0` and `pI` lie in `P_Gamma`.  They generate `G`, since `Lambda`
is generated by `F_0` and `pI`.  `Gamma` is not normal:
`(pI)^-1 Gamma (pI) = p^-1 Z^2` is not contained in `Z^2`.

**4. Soficity of `H`.**  `G` is finitely generated: `D^2` is the union of the
`p^-k Z^2`, so `e_1`, `e_2`, generators of `F_0` and `pI` generate `G`.  `G`
embeds in `GL_3(Q)` as affine matrices, so it is linear, hence residually
finite (Malcev), hence sofic.  The point stabilizers of `G -> G/Gamma` are the
conjugates `g Gamma g^-1`, which are amenable.

- Gao--Kunnawalkam Elayavalli--Patchell, *Sofic actions on graphs*,
  arXiv:2408.15470, Theorem 1.7, read from the arXiv HTML on 2026-09-12:
  "Let α:G↷Γ=(V,E) be an action of a sofic group such that G↷V is transitive
  and Stab(v) is amenable for some/all v∈V. Then α is sofic."
- Apply it to the graph with vertex set `G/Gamma` and no edges.
- Their Proposition 2.7(4): "If G↷Γ is sofic, then G↷V is sofic in the sense
  of [GKEP24]."  So the coset action is a sofic action in the sense of
  arXiv:2401.04945, Definition 2.1(5).
- Alekseev--Bradford, arXiv:2601.18742, Theorem 4.18, transcribed in the
  artifact, states the same set-level consequence and attributes it to that
  Theorem 1.7.

Then Gao--Kunnawalkam
Elayavalli--Patchell, arXiv:2401.04945, Theorem A (= Theorem 3.6), same
artifact: "Let G, H be sofic groups, α: H ↷ X be a sofic action. Then the
generalized wreath product G ≀_α H is sofic."  Apply it with lamp group `Z`,
acting group `G` and `α` the coset action.  So `H` is sofic.

**5. The defect.**

- For `gamma' in Gamma`, `gamma' o = o`, so `z = e_o` commutes with `Gamma`.
- `t = pI` gives `t z t^-1 = e_(t o)`, where `t o` is the coset `t Gamma`.
- For the translation `gamma = v_1 = (1,0)`, the commutator is
  `e_(t o) - e_(gamma t o)` in additive notation.
- `gamma t Gamma = t Gamma` iff `t^-1 gamma t in Gamma`.  But
  `t^-1 gamma t` is the translation by `p^-1 v_1`, which is not in `Z^2`.
- So the two lamps are distinct, and the commutator is nontrivial.

**6. The relative criterion fails.**  `H` is sofic, and
`Gamma <= G <= H` is infranormal with relative (T) of `(G, Gamma)`.  But
`t z t^-1` does not centralize `Gamma`, so `G` does not normalize `C_H(Gamma)`.  ∎
