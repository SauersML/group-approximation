H. Bass, J. Milnor, J.-P. Serre, *Solution of the congruence subgroup problem
for SL_n (n >= 3) and Sp_2n (n >= 2)*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 33 (1967), 59–137.
Read 2026-09-13 from the Numdam scan
(`numdam.org/item/10.1007/BF02684586.pdf`), pages 71, 94 and 95.

§4, p. 94:

> Let A be a commutative ring and let q be an ideal of A. E_n(A) denotes the
> subgroup of SL_n(A) generated by all "elementary" matrices, I + te_ij (t∈A,
> i≠j), and E_n(A, q) denotes the *normal* subgroup of E_n(A) generated by
> those with t∈q. This is a subgroup of SL_n(A, q) = ker(SL_n(A) → SL_n(A/q)).

Theorem 4.1 b), p. 94: "Write C_q(n) = SL_n(A, q)/E_n(A, q)".

p. 95:

> *Corollary 4.3.* — Suppose that A is of arithmetic type and that n≥3. a)
> SL_n(A) is equal to E_n(A) and it is a finitely generated group, equal to
> its own commutator subgroup. b) If A is not totally imaginary then C_q={1}
> for all q.

§3, p. 71:

> A denotes a Dedekind ring of arithmetic type defined by a finite set, S_∞,
> of primes in a global field k. [...] We shall call A totally imaginary if
> S_∞ consists of complex primes. This means that k is a totally imaginary
> number field, and that A is its ring of algebraic integers.

`Z` is the ring of integers of `Q`, with `S_∞` the real prime. It is of
arithmetic type (the standing case of the Introduction, p. 59) and not
totally imaginary. So with `n = 3`, part a) gives `SL_3(Z) = E_3(Z)` and part
b) gives `E_3(Z, q) = SL_3(Z, q)`. The Appendix definition of "arithmetic
type" was not re-read. The graph's
`bms-sln-z-representations-agree-with-algebraic-ones-citation` applies the
same corollary to `Z`.
