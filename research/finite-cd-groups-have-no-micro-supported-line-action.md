title: A group of finite cohomological dimension d has no faithful action on the line with more than d disjoint nontrivially supported intervals, so no micro-supported action
---

**ESTABLISHED (unreviewed).** Let `G` be a group with `cd G = d < ∞`, and let
`ρ : G → Homeo_+(R)` be injective. For an open interval `I ⊆ R` let
`G_I = {g ∈ G : ρ(g) fixes R ∖ I pointwise}` be its rigid stabilizer.

1. If `I_1, …, I_k` are pairwise disjoint open intervals with `G_{I_j} ≠ 1` for every `j`,
   then `Z^k ≤ G` and `k ≤ d`.
2. So `ρ` is not micro-supported: some nonempty open interval `I` has `G_I = 1`.
3. So a group of type F, which has finite cohomological dimension, has no faithful
   micro-supported action on the line.

**Model tests.**

- `Z` acting by a translation: `cd = 1` and every bounded rigid stabilizer is trivial. The
  action is not micro-supported, as item 2 requires.
- Thompson's group `F` acting on `(0,1) ≅ R`: micro-supported, and `cd F = ∞`, so item 2
  does not apply.
- `Z^2` generated by two homeomorphisms with disjoint supports: two intervals and
  `cd = 2`, so the bound in item 1 is attained.

**Use for Zaremsky Problem 1.22.** A left-orderable simple group of type F
(`some-left-orderable-simple-group-has-type-f`) cannot come from any group all of whose
faithful actions on `R` are micro-supported. The natural actions of the Hyde–Lodha groups
(arXiv:2302.04805) and of Thompson-like groups of homeomorphisms of the line are
micro-supported, matching their infinite geometric dimension. A torsion-free cocompact
lattice in a product of two trees has `cd ≤ 2`, so in any faithful action on `R` at most
two disjoint intervals carry nontrivial rigid stabilizers.

**Credit.** The argument is elementary and presumably folklore. It is not claimed as new.
