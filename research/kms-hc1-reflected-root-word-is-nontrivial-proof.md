The presentation is `G_HC^(1)_2(p)` in Caprace--Conder--Kaluba--Witzel,
*Hyperbolic generalized triangle groups, property (T) and finite simple
quotients*, Theorem 1.3. For `p>=7`, that theorem establishes infiniteness,
word-hyperbolicity, and property (T).

Index its simple roots as `1=a, 2=c, 3=b`. The pairs `{a,b}` and `{b,c}`
are of type `A_2`, so the reflected root groups `U_(s_b alpha_a)` and
`U_(s_b alpha_c)` are precisely the cyclic order-`p` commutator groups
generated by `r_p` and `s_p`. Any choice of Chevalley signs changes the
generators only to nonzero powers. The matrix has rank three, is
2-spherical, and is not spherical. Caprace--Marquis, *Amalgams of rational
unipotent groups and residual nilpotence*, Theorem 6.9, applies over every
integral domain, hence over `F_p`, and proves that these root groups
generate their free product. The reduced-word theorem for free products
now gives infinite order of `r_p s_p` and of its nonzero power `w_p`.

In the positive Kac--Moody group, the same two root groups are simultaneous
conjugates, by a representative of `s_b`, of the root groups for `a,c`.
Those generate the finite unipotent group of type `B_2`. It embeds in
`UT_4(F_p)`, which has exponent `p` for `p>=5`: if `N^4=0`, then
`(I+N)^p=I+N^p=I`. Hence the canonical image of `w_p` is trivial.
