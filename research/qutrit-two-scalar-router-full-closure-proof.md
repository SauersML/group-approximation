## The second complete scalar graph

Use the simultaneous `C`-basis from `(QSA9)`. Choose trivial coordinate
vectors `u_0,u_1` outside the active support. Recall that
`d_0 in W_[1,0]` and `d_1 in W_[1,-1]`. Complete the eight named
coordinates to a basis with `r_1,...,r_20` and define `g_2` by
`(QSR1)`.

Suppose

```text
c=g_2c'g_2^(-1),                    c,c' in C.           (QSR8)
```

Every element of `C` fixes `u_0,u_1`. Since
`g_2^(-1)u_i=d_i`, applying `(QSR8)` to `u_i` gives

```text
c'd_i=d_i,                         i=0,1.
```

A nonidentity order-three operator on a two-dimensional irreducible
`F_2` module has no nonzero fixed vector. Hence

```text
c' in ker[1,0] intersect ker[1,-1]={1}.
```

Thus `c'=c=1` and

```text
C intersect g_2Cg_2^(-1)={1}.                            (QSR9)
```

The same equality for `g_1` was proved in `(QSA11)`. The standard
finite-subgroup Hecke formula now gives `(QSR3)` for both actors.

## The first head-touching return

The first four entries of the cycle satisfy

```text
g_2c_1=c_2,                 g_2c_2=c_3.
```

Scalar conjugation changes only the root positions, so

```text
g_2x_(c_2,c_1)(Q_0)g_2^(-1)=x_(c_3,c_2)(Q_0)=H_1.
```

The two roots are composable. Since `Q_0^2=Q_0`, the Steinberg
commutator formula gives

```text
[H_1,H_0]=x_(c_3,c_1)(Q_0)=B.                           (QSR10)
```

The three displayed roots are involutions, `B` commutes with `H_0,H_1`,
and their distinct matrix entries recover the exponents in the collected
normal form

```text
H_0^epsilon H_1^delta B^gamma.
```

All eight forms are distinct. Therefore `<H_0,H_1>` is `D_8`.

## Scalar and coefficient closure

Let `K_4` be the scalar `GL_4(F_2)` on the active coordinates. It lies
in `G_3`. In particular `K_4` contains both roots on the first cycle
edge,

```text
x_(c_0,c_1)(1),                 x_(c_1,c_0)(1).
```

Conjugating these roots by `g_2^k`, `0<=k<28`, gives both directed
scalar transvections on every adjacent edge of the 28-cycle. Commutators
along a simple directed path give `x_(v,w)(1)` for every two distinct
cycle coordinates `v,w`. Hence

```text
<K_4,g_2>=EL_28(F_2)=GL_28(F_2).                         (QSR11)
```

The group `G_3=EL_4(L)` also contains elementary roots labelled by each
of `s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1`. The scalar group in `(QSR11)` moves any one of
their root positions to every ordered pair. Root additivity and a third
index generate sums and products of coefficients. These four
coefficients generate `L=L_(F_2)(1,2)` as a ring. Therefore

```text
<G_3,g_2>=EL_28(L).                                     (QSR12)
```

The reverse inclusion is immediate, and `C,g_1` are already scalar
elements of the right-hand side. This proves `(QSR5)`.

The binary Leavitt algebra is finitely presented as a unital associative
ring. The Krstic--McCool theorem therefore makes `EL_28(L)` finitely
presented. The established Leavitt results make it minimally almost
periodic and nonsofic. These conclusions do not imply nonhyperlinearity.

## The finite scalar-head absorber

Set

```text
A_Q=F_2[Q_0]={a+bQ_0:a,b in F_2}.
```

The idempotent `Q_0` is neither zero nor one. Evaluation at its two
idempotent values is an injective ring isomorphism

```text
A_Q -> F_2 times F_2,             a+bQ_0 |-> (a,a+b).
                                                               (QSR13)
```

Every scalar matrix lies in `EL_28(A_Q)`, because
`GL_28(F_2)=EL_28(F_2)`. The head `H_0` is an elementary matrix over
`A_Q`. It follows at once that

```text
Lambda=<C,g_1,g_2,H_0> <=EL_28(A_Q).
```

Using `(QSR13)` and compatibility of elementary groups with finite
products,

```text
EL_28(A_Q)
 isomorphic to GL_28(F_2) times GL_28(F_2),              (QSR14)
```

a finite group. This proves `(QSR7)`.

More generally, scalar conjugation of a matrix over `A_Q` remains a
matrix over `A_Q`, and products, inverses, and commutators never enlarge
the coefficient ring. Thus any number of scalar actors, scalar conjugates
of `Q_0`-roots, and commutators among those roots remain in the same
finite group `(QSR14)`. The returned root `B` in `(QSR10)` is the
first example, not an exception.

## Exact trace firewall

The subgroup generated by `C,g_1,H_0` is the finite direct product
computed in the support-three theorem. In its canonical trace,

```text
||e_2g_1E(e_0+e_1)||_2^2=1/81,
tau(E(e_0+e_1))=1/9,
tau(Ee_2)=1/18.                                         (QSR15)
```

Adjoining `g_2` and the relations `(QSR10)` embeds this entire packet in
the finite group `Lambda`. Its left regular representation is faithful
and preserves every nonidentity word, every Fourier atom, and every
projection in `(QSR15)`. In particular, the new head return cannot imply
that the polar source of the first operator is all of
`E(e_0+e_1)`: such an implication would already fail in this exact
finite-dimensional representation.

This does not give a finite-dimensional representation of all of
`Gamma=EL_28(L)` with its canonical trace. The raw `s_r,t_r` roots are
exactly what leave `A_Q` and make the full group minimally almost
periodic. The conclusion is narrower and load-bearing: no relation made
only from scalar actors and returned `Q_0` heads can supply the missing
Hall support. A surviving relation must use a raw positive or negative
Leavitt letter while coupling that letter to the polar source in
`(QSR15)`.
