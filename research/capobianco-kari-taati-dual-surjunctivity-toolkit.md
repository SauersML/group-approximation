**ESTABLISHED (literature import).** The route is
`capobianco-kari-taati-dual-surjunctivity-citation`. Every statement below is
quoted from text extracted from the sources.

**Capobianco–Kari–Taati**, *Post-surjectivity and balancedness of cellular
automata over groups*, DMTCS vol. 19:3, 2017, #4 (arXiv:1507.02472v5).
- **Definition 1:** "A cellular automaton A = ⟨S, N, f⟩ on X is
  post-surjective if, however given c ∈ X and a predecessor e ∈ X of c, every
  configuration c′ ∈ X asymptotic to c has a predecessor e′ ∈ X asymptotic to
  e."
- **Proposition 5 (excerpt):** "post-surjectivity of arbitrary CA is equivalent
  to post-surjectivity on the subgroup generated by the neighborhood."
- **Theorem 1:** "Every pre-injective, post-surjective cellular automaton on a
  full shift is reversible."
- **Corollary 2:** "A cellular automaton on an amenable group (in particular,
  a d-dimensional CA) is post-surjective if and only if it is reversible."
- **Theorem 2:** "Let G be a sofic group. Every post-surjective cellular
  automaton on G is pre-injective (and therefore reversible)." Followed by:
  "cellular automata which are post-surjective, but not pre-injective, could
  only exist over non-sofic groups!"

**Doucha–Gismatullin**, *On Dual surjunctivity and applications*,
arXiv:2008.10565 (TeX source).
- "A CA T: A^G → A^G is post-surjective if and only if it is strongly
  post-surjective" (their Observation, citing CKT Lemma 1).
- "All sofic groups are dual surjunctive [cap, Theorem 2]."
- Dual surjunctive groups are closed under subgroups and ultraproducts, and
  form a closed subset of the space of marked groups (their Proposition and
  Theorems in Section 3).
- **Theorem:** "Surjunctive and dual surjunctive groups satisfy Kaplansky's
  Direct Finiteness conjecture for fields of positive characteristic." Inside
  its proof, for `ab = 1` in `K[G]`: "The map T_b is post-surjective and T_a is
  injective", with `e′ = e + T_a(d) − T_a(c)`.

**Bartholdi**, *Amenability of groups is characterized by Myhill's Theorem*,
arXiv:1605.09133 (TeX source).
- **Theorem (main):** "Let G be a non-amenable group. Then there exists a
  cellular automaton carried by G that admits Gardens of Eden but no mutually
  erasable patterns."
