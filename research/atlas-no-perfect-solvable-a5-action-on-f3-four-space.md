Let `M ~= F3^4` be an irreducible `L`-chief factor carrying the image of `z`
in a finite completion with solvable kernel.  The induced action group `X` is
a perfect subgroup of `GL4(3)`, because it is an image of the perfect universal
seam group.  Its solvable radical `R` has quotient `A5`, and the fixed
complement in `P=N semidirect A5` gives

```text
X = R semidirect A5.                                      (Q1920-GL4-1)
```

The possible primes and exponents in `R` are finite and exact.  The 2-part of
`GL4(3)` bounds `|R|_2` by `2^7`, while its 3-part bounds `|R|_3` by `3^5`.
The 5-part is already exhausted by the quotient `A5`.

No factor 13 can occur.  If `O_13(R)` is nontrivial, it is cyclic of order 13;
the perfect group `X` centralizes it, and absolute irreducibility would make it
a scalar, impossible over `F3`.  If `13` divides `|R|` but `O_13(R)=1`, then
`F(R)=O_2(R)`: a faithful irreducible characteristic-three action has trivial
3-core.  The Fitting centralizer theorem embeds the remaining odd action into
`Aut(O_2(R))`.  For a 2-group of order at most `2^7`, every odd prime in its
automorphism group divides some `GL_d(2)`, `d<=7`; but `ord_13(2)=12`.  This is
again impossible.  Hence

```text
|X| = 60 * 2^a * 3^b,       0<=a<=7, 0<=b<=5.             (Q1920-GL4-2)
```

The complete perfect-group census at the orders `(Q1920-GL4-2)` contains 29
split solvable-by-`A5` candidates.  Twenty have nontrivial 3-core, which acts
trivially on every irreducible module in characteristic three and therefore
precludes a faithful action.  The remaining nine have 2-group radical.  Each
has exactly one four-dimensional irreducible representation, and in every
nontrivial-radical case its image has order 60: the entire radical is killed.
Thus

```text
R nontrivial  ==>  no faithful X -> GL4(3).                (Q1920-GL4-3)
```

If `M` carries `z`, the collision involution `c in R` must act by inversion on
that nonzero vector.  Formula `(Q1920-GL4-3)` instead kills `c`, a contradiction.
Therefore a finite solvable seam kernel cannot retain `z` in a
four-dimensional `L`-chief factor, regardless of how many even or odd derived
layers precede it.

## Attempts

Classifying only elementary or extraspecial 2-actions left open odd radical
layers; `(Q1920-GL4-2)` and the perfect-group census close them all in degree
four.  The remaining logical gap is not another `GL4(3)` overgroup: it is the
possibility that the `L`-chief factor generated from `z` has dimension greater
than four even though its `A5`-span has only one- and four-dimensional
composition factors.
