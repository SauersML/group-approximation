**ESTABLISHED** via `two-cell-core-deletions-need-a-unit-fox-coefficient-proof` (unreviewed). The
search part is a negative computation. It is evidence only, not part of the ESTABLISHED statement.

## Setting

This is the setting of `core-relation-elimination-bounds-relative-defect`.

- `G = ⟨S | w⟩`, with `w` cyclically reduced and not a proper power.
- `H ≤ G` is finitely generated, and `C ⊂ X_H` is a finite core with exactly two 2-cells `e_0, e_1`.
- `F = π_1(C^1, v)` is free on a basis `B` with `|B| = r`, and `φ: F → F(S)` is induced by
  `C^1 → X^1`.
- `u_j ∈ F` is the based boundary loop of `e_j`. It satisfies `φ(u_j) = t_j w^{ε_j} t_j^{-1}`,
  where `t_j` is the label of a path from `v` to the base vertex `v_j` of `e_j`.
- Put `ε = ε_0 ε_1` and `g = t_1 t_0^{-1}`. Then `φ(u_1)^{ε} = g φ(u_0) g^{-1}` in `F(S)`.

The **core relative presentation** is the one-vertex complex

`Y_1^B = ⟨B, S | u_0, u_1, r_x = x φ(x)^{-1} (x ∈ B)⟩`.

It contains the 2-dimensional `K(H,1)` `K_B = ⟨B | u_0, u_1⟩ ≃ C`, and it has relative defect 1.
For `y ∈ B`, the **one-cell deletion** `P_y^B = Y_1^B ∖ r_y` is the presentation `P_{B ∖ {y}}` of
the elimination node. It is `⟨S, y | λ(u_0), λ(u_1)⟩`, with `λ = φ` on `B ∖ {y}` and `λ(y) = y`.

For `x ∈ B`, write `∂_x` for the Fox derivative, evaluated in `ZH ⊂ ZG`. Put

`c_x = ε g ∂_x u_0 - ∂_x u_1 ∈ ZG`, and `c = (c_x)_{x∈B} ∈ ZG^B`.

## Statement

- **(U1) The sphere.** `π_2(Y_1^B) ≅ ZG`. It is freely generated by the identity
  `σ = ẽ_1 - εg ẽ_0 + Σ_x c_x r̃_x`. The element `g` does not lie in `H`, because `e_0 ≠ e_1`.
- **(U2) Admissibility is unimodularity.** `pd_{ZG} I_{G/H} ≤ 1` holds iff `c` is unimodular, that
  is, iff `Σ_x c_x d_x = 1` for some `d_x ∈ ZG`.
- **(U3) Deletion criterion.** For every free basis `B` and every `y ∈ B`, the following hold.
  - `P_y^B` presents `G` (with `H → G` the inclusion) iff `c_y` is a unit of `ZG` and the kernel
    `N_y` of `π_1(P_y^B) → G` is trivial.
  - `c_y` is a unit iff `N_y` is perfect.
  - When `P_y^B` presents `G`, it is aspherical and gives `RP(H,G)`.
- **(U4) Unit test.** `c_y` is a unit of `ZG` iff one of the following holds in `ZH`:
  - `∂_y u_0 = 0` and `∂_y u_1 = ±h` for some `h ∈ H`; or
  - `∂_y u_1 = 0` and `∂_y u_0 = ±h` for some `h ∈ H`.

  This uses that the units of `ZG` are trivial: `G` is locally indicable (Brodskii, Howie), so
  Higman's theorem applies. In degree-one form (`[u_0] = a p`, `[u_1] = b p`, with `p` primitive
  and `gcd(a,b) = 1`, as in (E5)), a unit `c_y` forces `ab = 0`. So if `ab ≠ 0`, no free basis and
  no `y` give a one-cell deletion presenting `G`. In particular the two-relator map
  `⟨S, y | λ(u_0), λ(u_1)⟩ → G` of the elimination node's open test is **never** an isomorphism.
  This covers every `y`, including basis elements that occur several times in both relators.
- **(U5) What is left.** Handle slides `r_x ↦ r_x · k r_{x'}^{±1} k^{-1}` change `c` by elementary
  column operations `c_{x'} ↦ c_{x'} ∓ c_x k`. Slides over `u_0` and `u_1` leave `c` unchanged.
  So a deletion after handle slides needs a unit coordinate in `c · E` for some `E ∈ E_r(ZG)`, and
  unit coordinates can appear only through such slides. The coset support argument of (U4) does
  not survive these column operations. RP by this route for `ab ≠ 0` is therefore a question about
  elementary completion of the unimodular row `c` over `ZG` (plus a perfect-kernel condition).
  It is not settled here.

## Calibration

- **Triangular case.** Suppose `y` does not occur in `u_0` and occurs once in `u_1`. Then
  `∂_y u_0 = 0` and `∂_y u_1 = ±h`, so (U4) holds. Magnus's theorem kills `N_y`, and this
  recovers (E3).
- **Torus.** Take `G = Z^2`, `H` of index 2, and the two-square core. Then `[u_0] = -[u_1] = p`
  (so `a = 1`, `b = -1`) and `c` has augmentation `ε(c_x) = ±2 p_x`. This row is not unimodular,
  since augmentation would give `2 Σ p_x ε(d_x) = 1` in `Z`. So by (U2) the pair is not
  admissible, which matches the known `H_2` failure.

## Negative computation (evidence, not proved)

The degree-one normal form of (E5) allows `(a,b) = (1,2)`. The scripts searched for a folded graph
carrying two distinct w-cycles with dependent classes `a p, b p`, `ab ≠ 0`, `|a| ≠ |b|`. None was
found. The search space was:

- exhaustive vertex identifications (up to 3) of two w-circles for all `w ∈ [F_2,F_2]` of length
  at most 8, and up to 2 identifications at length 10;
- surface-guided gluings (two `w`-discs and one `w^{-1}`-disc, 3000 samples per word, length 8);
- random identifications (see `RESULTS.md`).

Every dependent pair found (for example 353 pairs of type `(1,-1)` and 74 + 80 of type one-class-zero
among 20000 random trials) had one of two forms:
- `[u_1] = ±[u_0]`, which is never degree one; its `c` has augmentation `0` or `±2a p`, so it is
  not admissible by (U2);
- one class zero (`ab = 0`). Whether `ab ≠ 0` two-cell cores exist at
all is open. If they do not, then (E4), (U4) and (E3) together say that the two-cell case of
generator-preserving elimination is exactly the `ab = 0` Fox-singleton case.
