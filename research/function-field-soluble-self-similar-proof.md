Let `G <= GL_n(K)` be generated by `h_1, ..., h_m`.

1. **A finitely generated field.** The entries of the `h_i` and of their inverses
   generate a subfield `F <= K`. It is finitely generated of transcendence
   degree at most one over `F_p`, so it is a finite field or a global function
   field. A finite field `F_q` sits inside the global function field `F_q(t)`.
   So assume `G <= GL_n(F)` with `F` a global function field.

2. **Triangularize (Kolchin--Mal'cev).**
   - The Zariski closure `𝒢` of `G` in `GL_n` is a soluble algebraic group, and
     `G_1 = G ∩ 𝒢°` has finite index in `G`.
   - By the Lie--Kolchin theorem, `𝒢°` stabilizes a full flag in `Fbar^n`. Its
     finitely many spanning vectors have coordinates in some finite extension
     `K'` of `F`.
   - So some `x in GL_n(K')` conjugates `G_1` into `B_n(K')`. The field `K'` is
     again a global function field, and `G_1` is finitely generated, being of
     finite index in `G`.

3. **S-integers.** Choose finitely many generators of `x^-1 G_1 x` and list the
   entries of them and of their inverses. Each entry has finitely many poles.
   Let `S` be a finite set of places of `K'` containing all these poles, with
   `|S| >= 3`. Let `O_S` be the ring of elements of `K'` regular outside `S`.
   Then `x^-1 G_1 x <= B_n(O_S)`.

4. **Into the affine Borel group.** `B_n(O_S)` is the stabilizer of `0` in
   `A_S = O_S^n x| B_n(O_S)`.

5. **Finite presentation.** Map `[[b, v], [0, λ]]` in `B_(n+1)(O_S)`, with `b` in
   `B_n(O_S)`, `v` in `O_S^n` and `λ` in `O_S^x`, to the affine map
   `y -> λ^-1 (b y + v)`.
   - This is the action on the affine chart `(y, 1)` of projective space, so it
     is a homomorphism onto `A_S`: the preimage of `(v, c)` is `[[c, v], [0, 1]]`.
   - Its kernel is the scalar matrices `O_S^x I_(n+1)`.
   - So `A_S ≅ B_(n+1)(O_S) / O_S^x`.

   `B_(n+1)(O_S)` is finitely presented by
   `function-field-borel-groups-are-fp-for-three-places`, since `|S| >= 3`. The
   central subgroup `O_S^x ≅ F_q^x x Z^(|S|-1)` is finitely generated, by the
   S-unit theorem. A quotient of a finitely presented group by a finitely
   generated normal subgroup is finitely presented. So `A_S` is finitely
   presented.

6. **Self-similarity.**
   - `O_S` is a Dedekind domain with finite residue fields, and it is not a field.
   - Choose a nonzero nonunit `x_0 in O_S`. Then `O_S / x_0 O_S` is finite, `x_0`
     is not a zero divisor, and `∩_k x_0^k O_S = 0` by the Krull intersection
     theorem.
   - By `principal-ideal-affine-groups-are-self-similar`, applied with
     `R = O_S`, `J = O_S x_0` and `Γ = B_n(O_S) <= GL_n(O_S)`, the group `A_S`
     is self-similar.

So `G_1 ≅ x^-1 G_1 x` lies in the finitely presented self-similar group `A_S`.

**The example.** For `B_p`, the ring `F_p[x^±1, (1+x)^-1]` is `O_S` for
`S = {0, -1, ∞}`, and the S-units are `F_p^x × <x, 1+x>`. `B_p` is the subgroup
of `A_S = O_S x| O_S^x` whose linear parts lie in `<x, 1+x>`. It is already
upper triangular, so `G_1 = G`. ∎
