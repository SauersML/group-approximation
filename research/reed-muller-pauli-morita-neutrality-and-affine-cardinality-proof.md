# Separate the stable Pauli matrix factor from the uncontrolled occurrence algebra

## 1. Exact scope of the 2026 stability theorem

The primary source is Michael Chapman, Thomas Vidick, and Henry Yuen,
*Efficiently Stable Presentations from Error-Correcting Codes*,
arXiv:2311.04681v2, published in Discrete Analysis (2026):

- full text: <https://arxiv.org/html/2311.04681v2>;
- arXiv record and revision history:
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04681>;
- journal DOI: <https://doi.org/10.19086/da.154623>.

Theorem 4.1 states that their Reed--Muller/Hadamard presentation of
\(\mathbb Z_2^k\) is stable over the class of all tracial von Neumann
algebras, with modulus

\[
 \delta(\varepsilon)=
 \min\left\{
 C(mdt)^{c_1}\big(\varepsilon^{c_2}+(1/q)^{c_3}\big),
 C'_{t,d,m}\varepsilon
 \right\}.                                               \tag{1}
\]

The generators in that theorem are binary involutive coordinates, and the
nearby exact homomorphism is an exact representation of the finite abelian
group \(\mathbb Z_2^k\).

Theorem 5.10 applies a stable binary code presentation to the braided code
test and obtains a \(k\)-qubit test with error
\(O(\delta^{1/2}(6\varepsilon))\). In its proof, the rounded strategy is
placed, after the permitted stabilization, in an algebra of the form

\[
 M_{2^k}({\mathbf C})\mathbin{\overline\otimes}\mathcal N, \tag{2}
\]

and the controlled observables are the canonical Pauli matrices tensored
with \(I_{\mathcal N}\). This is also the exact structure asserted in the
Pauli braiding statement, Corollary 2.19.

Thus the new ingredient has two exact outputs:

1. an exact binary abelian coordinate table; and
2. after braiding, a canonical finite Pauli matrix factor with an arbitrary
   multiplicity algebra.

Neither theorem states a rounding result for an additional projection that
records which occurrence of a nonlinear source predicate is active.

## 2. The marked Pauli corner is a full matrix algebra

Let \(\mathsf P_k\) be the binary Pauli group. It is generated by a central
involution \(J\) and \(2k\) involutions \(X_i,Z_i\) with

\[
 [X_i,Z_i]_g=J,\qquad
 [X_i,X_j]_g=[Z_i,Z_j]_g=[X_i,Z_j]_g=1
 \quad(i\ne j).                                         \tag{3}
\]

Put \(d=2^k\) and \(p_-=(1-J)/2\). The standard spin representation

\[
 \sigma:\mathsf P_k\longrightarrow U({\mathbf C}^{d})   \tag{4}
\]

has \(\sigma(J)=-I_d\). It is the unique irreducible representation with
this central character.

For completeness, the uniqueness can be seen directly. Choose a joint
eigenvector for the commuting \(Z_i\). The \(2^k\) vectors obtained by
applying all products of the \(X_i\) have distinct \(Z\)-characters and
span an invariant copy of the standard spin module. Irreducibility leaves
only that copy.

The negative central corner of the finite group algebra has dimension

\[
 \dim_{\mathbf C}p_-{\mathbf C}[\mathsf P_k]
 =\frac{|\mathsf P_k|}{2}=2^{2k}=d^2.                  \tag{5}
\]

The representation (4) maps this corner onto \(M_d({\mathbf C})\).
Equation (5) makes the surjection an isomorphism:

\[
 p_-C^*(\mathsf P_k)p_-\cong M_d({\mathbf C}).           \tag{6}
\]

For any unital \(C^*\)-algebra \(A\), centrality of \(p_-\) and functoriality
of the maximal tensor product give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_-\big(C^*(\mathsf P_k)\otimes_{\max}A\big)p_-
 &\cong
 \big(p_-C^*(\mathsf P_k)p_-\big)\otimes_{\max}A\\
 &\cong M_d({\mathbf C})\otimes_{\max}A.                 \tag{7}
 \end{aligned}
\]

This proves (RPM1).

## 3. Traces, matrix models, and Connes embeddability

Let \(\tau\) be a tracial state on \(M_d\otimes A\), and define

\[
 \tau_A(a)=\tau(I_d\otimes a).                           \tag{8}
\]

For standard matrix units \(e_{ij}\), traciality gives

\[
 \tau(e_{ij}\otimes a)=0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
 \tau(e_{ii}\otimes a)=\frac1d\tau_A(a).                \tag{9}
\]

The first equality follows by conjugating with a diagonal unitary that
multiplies \(e_{ij}\) by a nontrivial phase. The second follows by
conjugating one diagonal matrix unit to another. Hence

\[
 \tau={\rm tr}_d\otimes\tau_A,                           \tag{10}
\]

and \(\tau_A\) is uniquely determined. Conversely every tracial state on
\(A\) gives (10).

A unital finite-dimensional representation of \(M_d\otimes A\) restricts
on \(M_d\otimes1\) to a multiple of its defining representation. After a
unitary identification its Hilbert space is
\({\mathbf C}^d\otimes{\mathbf C}^r\), and the commuting copy of \(A\)
acts as \(I_d\otimes\pi_A(A)\). Thus

\[
 M_d\otimes A\text{ has a unital finite-dimensional representation}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A\text{ does}.                                         \tag{11}
\]

The GNS von Neumann algebra of (10) is

\[
 M_d\mathbin{\overline\otimes}\pi_{\tau_A}(A)''.         \tag{12}
\]

Finite matrix amplification preserves Connes embeddability, and the
converse follows by passing to the normalized corner
\(e_{11}\otimes1\). Therefore

\[
 {\rm tr}_d\otimes\tau_A\text{ is Connes embeddable}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \tau_A\text{ is Connes embeddable}.                    \tag{13}
\]

Equations (10)--(13) are the precise sense in which the Pauli module is
Morita-neutral. It preserves a specified non-CE source trace, but it does
not convert the source algebra into group words.

## 4. Zero coordinate defect leaves occurrence data arbitrary

Fix any tracial \(A\) and any projection \(e\in A\). In (7), assign every
Reed--Muller/Pauli coordinate its canonical matrix value,

\[
 X(a)\longmapsto\sigma^X(a)\otimes1,\qquad
 Z(b)\longmapsto\sigma^Z(b)\otimes1,                    \tag{14}
\]

and assign an occurrence marker

\[
 P_{\rm occ}=1\otimes e.                                \tag{15}
\]

Every code, multiplication, commutation, and anticommutation relation in
the coordinate test holds exactly in (14). Its total defect is zero for
every choice of \(A\) and \(e\).

For the strongest elementary counterexample, take \(A={\mathbf C}\oplus
{\mathbf C}\). The two characters of \(A\) send the same coordinate table
(14) to the same Pauli matrices but send \(e=(1,0)\) to \(1\) and \(0\),
respectively. Hence a quantity depending nontrivially on the occurrence
marker cannot be bounded by any function of the coordinate defects that
vanishes at zero.

More generally, taking \(A\) to be the finite SynchBCS source algebra
places its nonlinear one-hot projections entirely in the multiplicity
algebra. The Chapman--Vidick--Yuen rounding controls (14) and says nothing
new about those projections. If instead the occurrence projections are
required to lie in \(M_d\otimes1\), then a perfect decoded source
configuration is itself a representation inside \(M_d\), which is the
finite absorber the construction was meant to exclude.

A mixed relation can of course couple (14) to (15), but such a relation is
not one of the hypotheses controlled by Theorem 4.1 or Theorem 5.10. Its
preservation under the rounding, and its finite-dimensional soundness,
must be proved separately. This is exactly the missing occurrence return.

## 5. Binary word rows cannot isolate three atoms

There is an additional exact obstruction to using the stable binary table
as a qutrit selector. Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) be commuting involutions.
Index their joint characters by \(u\in\mathbb F_2^k\):

\[
 \chi_u(x_i)=(-1)^{u_i}.                                \tag{16}
\]

An ordinary signed group-word row

\[
 x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_k^{a_k}=(-1)^b                       \tag{17}
\]

allows precisely the characters satisfying

\[
 a\cdot u=b\pmod2.                                      \tag{18}
\]

A finite family of such rows gives a linear system \(Bu=c\). Its solution
set is empty or is a coset of \(\ker B\); in the nonempty case its
cardinality is \(2^{\dim\ker B}\).

In particular, no such family has exactly three allowed characters. More
invariantly, any affine set containing three points \(u,v,w\) also contains

\[
 u+v+w,                                                  \tag{19}
\]

because \(u+v+w=u+(v-u)+(w-u)\) in characteristic two. For the usual
two-bit qutrit encoding this is the forbidden fourth binary character.

A pointed representation may simply assign multiplicity zero to the
fourth character. But the binary group presentation does not force this:
the omitted character remains another exact one-dimensional
representation. Conversely, the direct sum of the three retained
characters is already finite-dimensional. Thus trace support selection is
not supplied by binary group stability.

## 6. Consequence for the positive qutrit/Lin lane

The three-carrier hidden-line selector has positive source support above
one qutrit target capacity, and the Lin source forces a uniformly positive
nonaffine one-hot occurrence in every perfect trace. Those facts concern a
pointed nonlinear trace face. Sections 4 and 5 show that the stable
Reed--Muller/Pauli coordinate module does one of two things:

- it leaves that trace face in the arbitrary multiplicity algebra, where
  the code theorem cannot detect it; or
- it moves the data into the canonical finite matrix factor, producing a
  finite model.

The remaining possibility is neither reducing placement. One needs an
explicit mixed root-triangle, Hecke, or other nonnormal return whose trace
depends on the source occurrence and whose off-factor leakage is
quantitatively charged by additional relations. The 2026 stability theorem
may then stabilize the coordinate part of such a construction, but it does
not provide the mixed decoder. No such decoder is proved here, so the
nonhyperlinear-group root remains open.
