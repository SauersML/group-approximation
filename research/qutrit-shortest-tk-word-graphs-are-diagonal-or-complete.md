Put

```text
t=x_23(T),             k=x_31(1),
u=[t,k]=(tk)^2=x_21(T).
```

Then `D=<t,k>` is the dihedral group of order eight. Let
`C=<J,Z_1>` and `e_a=z_(C,chi_a)` be the three qutrit phase
projections, so `R=e_0+e_1` and `q-R=e_2`.

For every nonidentity word

```text
g in {t,k,u,tkt,ktk,tk,kt},
```

the intersection `H_g=C intersect gCg^(-1)` is either trivial or an
order-three point stabilizer. Its phase blocks satisfy exactly one of

```text
H_g={1}:
  ||e_b g e_a||_2^2=1/81 for every a,b;

H_g isomorphic to C_3:
  e_b g e_a=0 for a!=b,
  ||e_a g e_a||_2^2=1/27.                              (QDW1)
```

Thus every phase support graph in the complete alternating word ball is
either the complete three-by-three graph or the diagonal matching. No word
has a Hall-deficient graph sending the two phases of `R` only to the
single phase of `q-R`.

This is exhaustive for the smallest genuinely non-scalar Steinberg class:
it includes each raw root, both length-two products, both length-three
reflections, and the length-four alternating commutator. On the live raw
residual their analytic compressions have nontrivial geometry, including
orthogonal transport and square-root-half leakage, but no two-to-one qutrit
pattern is already forced by the underlying group-word phase graph. Any
successful selector must therefore arise from a joint compression or polar
which uses an additional residual cut, not from another word in `<t,k>`.
