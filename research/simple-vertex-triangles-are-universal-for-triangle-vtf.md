**Setting.** A triangle of finite groups `T` has vertex groups `A_1, A_2, A_3`,
nontrivial edge groups `E_12, E_13, E_23` with given injections `E_ij -> A_i`,
`E_ij -> A_j`, and trivial face group, so the two edge subgroups at a vertex meet
trivially. Its colimit is `Λ(T) = (A_1 * A_2 * A_3)/(identify the two images of each E_ij)`.
At the vertex `v` with edges `e, e'` put

```text
B_v = < E_e, E_e' > <= A_v,        Γ_v(T) = coset graph of A_v w.r.t. {E_e, E_e'},
```

and let `2 m_v` be the girth of `Γ_v(T)`. `T` is *hyperbolic* if `Σ_v 1/m_v < 1`.
The *core* `T°` is the triangle with vertex groups `B_v` and the same edge groups;
write `Λ° = Λ(T°)`.

**THEOREM (established; unreviewed).**

```text
(U1)  Γ_v(T) is the disjoint union of [A_v : B_v] copies of Γ_v(T°).
      So T and T° have the same half-girths m_v.
(U2)  Λ(T) is the pushout of  Λ° <- B_v -> A_v  (v = 1,2,3).
(U3)  Λ(T) has a finite quotient injective on every A_v
        <=>  Λ° has a finite quotient injective on every B_v.
(U4)  If every A_v is simple:
        Λ(T) has a nontrivial finite quotient
        <=>  Λ° has a finite quotient injective on every B_v.
      If moreover T° is hyperbolic, both are equivalent to
        Λ° virtually torsion-free,
      and Λ(T) is word-hyperbolic with every finite subgroup conjugate into some A_v.
(U5)  The following are equivalent:
      (a) some hyperbolic triangle of finite groups, with trivial face group, nontrivial
          edge groups and every vertex group generated by its two edge groups, has a
          colimit that is not virtually torsion-free;
      (b) some hyperbolic triangle of finite simple groups, with trivial face group and
          nontrivial edge groups, has a colimit with no nontrivial finite quotient.
      For (a) => (b), replace each B_v by A_v = Alt(|B_v| + 2) through the regular
      representation.
(U6)  If all three edge groups have order 2, Λ(T) has a finite quotient injective on
      every vertex group, whatever the A_v and whatever the curvature: Λ° is the
      Coxeter group < t_12, t_13, t_23 | t_e^2, (t_e t_e')^{m_v} >.
```

**Literature frame.** (U5)(a) is the hyperbolic case of the embeddability problem of
B. H. Neumann and H. Neumann, as restated by Wise (*The residual finiteness of negatively
curved polygons of finite groups*, Invent. Math. 149 (2002), p. 607, read at source):
"an old problem of B.H. Neumann and H. Neumann [25], asked whether every developable
triangle of finite groups is actually embeddable in a finite group". Wise continues:
"the embeddability problem is equivalent to the problem of whether π_1P is virtually
torsion-free", and "it seems likely that there exist nonpositively curved polygons of
finite groups that are not virtually torsion-free, but no examples are currently known."
His Theorem 9.1 proves residual finiteness for `n`-gons of finite groups with all angles
`<= π/2` when `n = 4` with two acute angles, `n = 5` with one, or `n >= 6`. Triangles
are exactly the polygons that theorem cannot reach, because its proof splits `π_1 P` as
an amalgam along a segment `J` with corners of `P` on both sides.

**Consequences for the target.**
- Simple vertex groups cost nothing:
  `hyperbolic-simple-triangle-group-without-finite-quotients` is equivalent to
  `hyperbolic-triangle-of-finite-groups-not-virtually-torsion-free`, the hyperbolic
  Neumann–Neumann problem.
- Every hyperbolic triangle of finite groups with generated vertex groups is a
  candidate. That includes the Caprace–Conder–Kaluba–Witzel triangle groups of girth
  types `(4,8,8)`, `(6,6,6)` and `(6,6,8)` for which no finite simple quotient was found.
  Each yields a simple-vertex member that is quotientless iff that group is not
  virtually torsion-free.
- A quotient search on a simple-vertex member with disconnected links runs on the
  smaller core `Λ°`, where it asks for injectivity on the `B_v`.
- A candidate needs an edge group of order at least 3 (U6).

**Model test.** With trivial edge groups (U4) fails: `A_5 * A_5 -> A_5`. With `A_v = B_v`
the statement reduces to `simple-vertex-groups-vtf-iff-some-finite-quotient`. The
Caprace–Conder–Kaluba–Witzel groups satisfy every hypothesis of (U5)(a) except the
unknown conclusion.

Proof: `simple-vertex-triangles-are-universal-for-triangle-vtf-proof`. Elementary apart
from Caprace–Conder–Kaluba–Witzel Theorem 3.1 for the core and standard facts on graphs
of groups. No novelty is claimed for amalgamating into simple groups. Unreviewed.
