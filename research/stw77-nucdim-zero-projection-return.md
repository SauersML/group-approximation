title: Zero-dimensional properly infinite-image maps admit finite-dimensional models with projection return
---

Let `phi:A->B` satisfy the hypotheses of STW LXXVII and suppose additionally
that `dim_nuc(phi)=0`.  For every finite `F subset phi(A)` and
`epsilon>0`, there is a unital finite-dimensional subalgebra

```text
D=direct_sum_j M_(n_j) subset B
```

which approximates `F` within `epsilon` and has matrix units such that each
`e^(j)_(11)` is within `1/16` of a positive element of `phi(A)`.

## Attempts

- `zero-dimensional-unital-maps-are-locally-fd` proves the approximation of
  `F` by `D`, but gives no reverse control on the model projections.
- If projection return holds, functional calculus produces actual nearby
  projections in `phi(A)`; `projection-return-models-force-central-splitting`
  then proves O-infinity-stability.
- An arbitrary one-sided model cannot be used.  More strongly,
  `one-sided-fd-models-allow-finite-junk-corners` uses the representations
  `ev_(t_n)` and `ev_0` of `C([0,1])`, with `t_n->0`, to produce models which
  are generated by their downward images yet retain a finite rank-one block.
  A proof must merge asymptotically indistinguishable representation blocks,
  not merely delete algebraically unused summands.
- The remaining selection issue is the map analogue of the double
  approximation problem: outgoing finite-dimensional ranges lie in `B`, not
  in `phi(A)`, so their matrix units cannot simply be placed into the next
  domain-side test set.
