**Statement (OPEN).**  Some infinite group with Kazhdan's property (T) is
left-orderable.

A Kazhdan group is finitely generated, hence countable, and a countable group
is left-orderable exactly when it embeds in `Homeo_+(R)`, equivalently in the
orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of `[0,1]`.  So this claim is Navas's
question, verbatim from *Group actions on 1-manifolds: a list of very concrete
open questions*, arXiv:1712.06462v2 (20 Apr 2018), p. 3:

> **Question 3.** Does there exist a nontrivial (hence infinite) Kazhdan group
> of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the interval ?

together with its circle companion on the same page:

> **Question 2.** Does there exist an infinite, finitely-generated Kazhdan
> group of circle homeomorphisms ?

**Status check (bounded).**  The 2018 survey states it open.  Vergara,
arXiv:2407.03579v3 (8 Dec 2025), p. 1, still writes: "It is an open problem to
determine whether a group with Property (T) can act faithfully on R by
orientation-preserving homeomorphisms."  A web search on 2026-09-12 found no
later resolution.  That check covers these two sources and the search only.

## Attempts

**Smoothness.**  Dead in low regularity.  Navas (survey p. 3): "If a
finitely-generated group of `C^{3/2}` circle diffeomorphisms satisfies property
(T), then it is finite."  The same restriction for piecewise-linear circle
homeomorphisms is attributed to Lodha--Matte Bon--Triestino and to Cornulier in
arXiv:2509.09856; the primary sources were not re-read here.  A witness
therefore acts by homeomorphisms that are neither `C^{3/2}` nor PL.

**Higher-rank lattices.**  Witte Morris (survey p. 3): for `n >= 3`, every
action of a finite-index subgroup of `SL(n,Z)` by orientation-preserving
homeomorphisms of the interval is trivial.  The proof uses nilpotent subgroups
of the lattice, and the survey's Question 1 asks about lattices without them.
Later work on that question was not re-read here.

**Dynamics forces a proximal model.**  The survey (pp. 3-4) records a
trichotomy for a finitely generated group acting on the line without global
fixed points:

1. there is an invariant sigma-finite measure;
2. the action is semiconjugate to a minimal action whose contracting intervals
   are cut off by a commuting homeomorphism, so it descends to a circle;
3. the action is semiconjugate to a minimal action that contracts every bounded
   interval.

Case 1 gives a nonzero translation-number homomorphism to `R`, which property
(T) forbids.  Case 2 gives a Kazhdan group of circle homeomorphisms, which is
Question 2.  So a witness to this claim with no circle shortcut acts on each
irreducible interval through a type 3 action.  No Kazhdan group is known to
admit one.

**Bi-Lipschitz actions and relative (T).**  Vergara's Proposition 1.2
(arXiv:2407.03579v3, p. 2): if a group generated by a finite `S` has injective
homomorphisms `theta_n` into the bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms with bounded
displacement and `lim BiLip(theta_n(g)) = 1` for all `g in S`, then it maps
onto an infinite abelian group.  So a Kazhdan witness keeps its bi-Lipschitz
constants away from 1.  The paper quantifies this through Kazhdan constants.
Relatively Kazhdan pairs such as `F_2 x| Z^2` do act on the line, as the survey
notes on p. 3, so relative (T) alone is not an obstruction.

**Locally indicable and bi-orderable groups.**  Dead.  Their nontrivial
finitely generated subgroups map onto `Z`.
