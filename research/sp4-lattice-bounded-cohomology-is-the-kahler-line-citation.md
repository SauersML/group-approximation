arXiv:0710.5361v1 (29 Oct 2007); J. reine angew. Math. 640 (2010). The PDF was fetched and pages 1--3 and 19--20
were read on 2026-09-20.

- **p. 2 (setting of Theorem 1.2).** "Let thus {Gα}α∈A be connected simply connected semi-simple kα-groups, where
  {kα}α∈A are local fields and A is a finite non-empty set. Consider the locally compact group G = ∏α∈A Gα(kα)
  defined using the Hausdorff kα-topologies. As usual, write rank(G) = ∑α∈A rankkα(Gα)."
- **p. 3, Corollary 1.4.** "Let G be as for Theorem 1.2 and let Γ < G be an irreducible lattice. Then the
  restriction map H^n_b(G; R) → H^n_b(Γ; R) is an isomorphism for all n < 2 rank(G)."

- **p. 223, Corollary 9.** "Let G be a compactly generated locally compact second countable group and α : E → F an
  injective adjoint morphism of coefficient modules. Assume F is separable. Then (i) H^1_cb(G,E) = 0. (ii) The
  induced map H^2_cb(G,E) → H^2_cb(G,F) is injective and both spaces are Banach spaces."
- **p. 226, Theorem 21.** "Let Γ be a lattice in G(k), where G is a connected, simply connected, almost simple
  k-isotropic group and k a local field. If G has k-rank at least two, then the natural map H^2_b(Γ, H) −→
  H^2(Γ, H) is injective for any unitary representation (π, H) of Γ." Followed by: "In order to dispose of the
  co-compactness assumption, we use notably the results of Lubotzky, Mozes and Raghunathan [LMR]".

  H^2_c(Gα(kα)) is injective for any α. On the other hand, the right-hand side is known: it vanishes unless the
  associated symmetric space is Hermitian, in which case it is one dimensional and generated by a bounded cocycle,
  see [GuW]."

**[GW]** The continuous homogeneous quasimorphism `f` on the universal cover `G^` of `Sp_(2g)(R)` with
`f(zeta) != 0`, imported verbatim from Ben Simon--Burger--Hartnick--Iozzi--Wienhard, arXiv:1305.2620v2,
pp. 8--10, in `sp2g-central-commutators-of-universal-lifts-are-torsion-proof` (section "Import (verbatim)"), with
item 1 of its elementary part: `f` is additive on commuting pairs.

Standard, not re-read: `Sp_4` is a connected, simply connected, almost simple `R`-split group of `R`-rank 2;
its symmetric space (Siegel upper half space) is Hermitian; finite-index subgroups of `Sp_4(Z)` are lattices in
`Sp_4(R)`; for discrete groups `H^*_cb = H^*_b`.

## Derivation

Let `Lambda <= Gamma = Sp_4(Z)` have finite index `m`.

**(BK1).** Take `A` a single index, `k = R`, `G = Sp_4(R)` in [M]. Then `rank(G) = 2`, and `Lambda` is a lattice
in `G`, irreducible because there is only one factor. Corollary 1.4 with `n = 2 < 4` gives
`H^2_b(Lambda) = H^2_cb(Sp_4(R))`. By [BM, proof of Cor. 24], `H^2_cb(Sp_4(R))` injects into
`H^2_c(Sp_4(R))`, which is one-dimensional. So `dim H^2_b(Lambda) <= 1`.

**(BK2).** `Lambda` is finitely generated and discrete, hence compactly generated, locally compact and second
countable. Apply [BM, Cor. 9 (ii)] with `E = F = R` and `alpha` the identity. Then `H^2_b(Lambda)` is a Banach space
for the quotient seminorm, so that seminorm is a norm.

**(BK3).** Let `s : Gamma -> Gamma~ <= G^` be the set-theoretic section with `s(e) = e` that defines the covering
cocycle, `s(g) s(h) = s(gh) zeta^(b(g,h))`. Any other normalized integral cocycle representing the covering class
differs from this one by `delta k` with `k` integral and normalized. Let `f` be the quasimorphism [GW], with defect
`D`. Since `zeta` is central, `f(x zeta^j) = f(x) + j f(zeta)`. Hence

```text
| f(s(g)) + f(s(h)) - f(s(gh)) - b(g,h) f(zeta) | = | f(s(g)) + f(s(h)) - f(s(g) s(h)) | <= D.
```

Put `a = f o s / f(zeta)`, so `a(e) = 0`, and `b' = b - delta floor(a)`. Then
`b' = (b - delta a) + delta {a}`, so `|b'| <= D / |f(zeta)| + 2` everywhere, and `b'` is integral and normalized.

*Nonzero on `Lambda`.* By (V3) of `deligne-parameter-group-is-a-virtual-invariant` (the case `n = 0`),
`[b|Lambda]` is not torsion in `H^2(Lambda;Z)`. `Lambda` is finitely presented, so `H_1(Lambda)` is finitely
generated. By the universal coefficient theorem, `H^2(Lambda;Z) = Hom(H_2 Lambda, Z) + Ext(H_1 Lambda, Z)`, where
the Ext part is torsion. So the real class `[b'|Lambda]_R`, which is the image of `[b|Lambda]` in
`Hom(H_2 Lambda, R)`, is nonzero. The comparison map sends `[b'|Lambda]_b` to it, so `[b'|Lambda]_b != 0`.

**(BK4).** With (BK1), the class `[b'|Lambda]_b` spans `H^2_b(Lambda)`.

*Independence of `b'`.* Apply [BM, Thm. 21] with `k = R`, the group `Sp_4`, `Gamma` itself and `H = R` trivial.
So two bounded integral representatives of `[b]` have the same bounded class on `Gamma`, hence also on `Lambda`.

*Level-freeness of the norm.* Restriction does not increase the norm, so `||[b']_b||_Lambda <= ||[b']_b||_Gamma`.
For the converse, use homogeneous cochains. The Gromov norm is the quotient sup-norm of the invariant complex
`l^inf(Lambda^(n+1))^Lambda`. Inhomogeneous cochains `phi(g,h)` and homogeneous cochains
`Phi(1, g, gh) = phi(g,h)` correspond isometrically.

- *Retraction.* Choose right coset representatives, `Gamma = disjoint union Lambda t_i`, and define
  `r(lambda t_i) = lambda`. This `r : Gamma -> Lambda` is `Lambda`-equivariant and restricts to the identity on
  `Lambda`.
- *Prism homotopy.* For the two `Lambda`-equivariant maps `id` and `r` of `Gamma`, put
  `(H Phi)(x_0, ..., x_(n-1)) = sum_(j<n) (-1)^j Phi(x_0, ..., x_j, r x_j, ..., r x_(n-1))`.
  This preserves boundedness and `Lambda`-invariance and gives `id - r^* = delta H + H delta` on
  `l^inf(Gamma^(n+1))^Lambda`.
- *Averaging.* For `Lambda`-invariant `Phi`, the translate `g.Phi` depends only on `g Lambda`. Put
  `A Phi = (1/m) sum_(g Lambda in Gamma/Lambda) g.Phi`. Then `A` has norm at most 1, commutes with `delta`, lands in
  the `Gamma`-invariant cochains and fixes them.

Now let `beta` be a `Gamma`-invariant bounded homogeneous 2-cocycle representing `[b']_b`. Suppose
`beta|Lambda^3 = phi + delta psi`, with `phi` a `Lambda`-invariant bounded cocycle on `Lambda^3` and `psi` bounded.
Pull back by `r`. Since `beta o r^3 = r^*(beta|Lambda^3)`,

```text
beta = r^* phi + delta( r^* psi + H beta ),
```

and averaging gives `beta = A r^* phi + delta A( r^* psi + H beta )`, with `||A r^* phi|| <= ||phi||`. So
`||[b']_b||_Gamma <= ||[b']_b||_Lambda`, and the two norms are equal.

`nu_b` is finite because `b'` is bounded. It is positive by (BK2) on `Gamma` and `[b']_b != 0`. ∎
