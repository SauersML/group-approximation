**ESTABLISHED** through `scalar-descendants-make-completions-simple-proof` (lane proof, bh-algebra,
elementary, not reviewed).

**Setting.** `K` a field, `A` a unital `K`-algebra, `d >= 2`, and `ψ : A -> M_d(A)` an injective unital
`K`-algebra homomorphism. Iterates `ψ_j : A -> M_(d^j)(A)` are indexed by pairs of words of length `j`
over `{1..d}`, with `ψ_(j+m)(a)_(ur, u'r') = ψ_m(ψ_j(a)_(uu'))_(rr')`.

**Scalar descendants.** `a ∈ A` has a *scalar descendant* if `ψ_j(a)_(uu') ∈ K^x` for some `j >= 0` and
words `u, u'`.

**Claim.** If every nonzero `a ∈ A` has a scalar descendant, then:
1. `A` is ψ-minimal;
2. `(A, ψ)` is aperiodic;
3. hence the completion `O_ψ(A)` is simple (`leavitt-nekrashevych-completion-simplicity-criterion`, part 3).

**Useful refinement.** Let `A` be generated by a finite set `G`, and suppose some state `p` satisfies
`ψ(u)_(pp) = 0` for every word `u` of positive length in `G`. Then any element `c + e`, with `c ∈ K^x`
and `e` a combination of words of positive length, has the scalar descendant `c` at `(p, p)`. So it is
enough to reach an entry with nonzero constant term. A "peel" state, which reads one letter and leaves
`p` for good, is of this kind.
