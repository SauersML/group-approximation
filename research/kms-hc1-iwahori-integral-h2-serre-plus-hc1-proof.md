Notation of the target claim. `F_Z` is the free Lie ring on `e_1,e_2,e_0`
(degree 1), mapped to `L_Z` by `e_1 -> E_12`, `e_2 -> E_23`, `e_0 -> tE_31`.
Degrees: `deg(t^a E_ij) = 3a + j - i`, `deg(t^a h_s) = 3a`.

## 1. (Z1) `L_Z` is generated in degree 1; `H_1(L_Z) = Z^3`

We show every basis element `x` of degree `d >= 2` equals `[y,z]` for basis
elements `y,z` of degrees `< d`. By induction on `d`, `x` then lies in the Lie
subring generated by `E_12, E_23, tE_31`. Use `[E_ik, E_kj] = E_ij` for
distinct `i,j,k` and `[E_ij, E_ji] = E_ii - E_jj`.

- `d = 3a+1`, `a >= 1`: `t^a E_12 = [t^{a-1}E_13, tE_32]`,
  `t^a E_23 = [t^a E_21, E_13]`, `t^{a+1}E_31 = [t^a E_32, tE_21]`.
- `d = 3a+2`, `a >= 0`: `t^a E_13 = [t^a E_12, E_23]`,
  `t^{a+1}E_21 = [t^a E_23, tE_31]`, `t^{a+1}E_32 = [tE_31, t^a E_12]`.
- `d = 3a`, `a >= 1`: `t^a h_1 = [t^{a-1}E_12, tE_21]`,
  `t^a h_2 = [t^{a-1}E_23, tE_32]`.

In each case both factors are basis elements of `L_Z` of positive degree
summing to `d`. Hence `[L_Z,L_Z] = (L_Z)_{>=2}` and
`H_1(L_Z) = L_Z/[L_Z,L_Z] = Z^3`, free.

## 2. Universal coefficients

The Chevalley--Eilenberg complex `C_*(L_Z) = Lambda^*_Z L_Z` is a complex of
free abelian groups, finite of each weight, and
`C_*(L_Z) (x) F_p = Lambda^*_{F_p} L_p = C_*(L_p)`. The universal coefficient
theorem gives
`H_2(L_p)_w = H_2(L_Z)_w (x) F_p + Tor(H_1(L_Z)_w, F_p)`. The Tor term is 0
by section 1. Writing `H_2(L_Z)_w = Z^r + sum_i Z/e_i` with `e_i > 1`, we get
`dim H_2(L_p)_w = r + #{i : p | e_i}`. This is (Z1).

Here `L_p` is the algebra of the deviation claim: its basis and degrees are
those of `L_Z (x) F_p`, so the `D_p` of that claim is
`{k : H_2(L_Z)_k (x) F_p != 0}`.

## 3. (Z2) The computation

`h2z.py K` lists the basis of `L_Z` in degree `<= K` with its weight, the
integral bracket (structure constants `0, +-1`; the diagonal part
`d_1E_11 + d_2E_22 + d_3E_33` of trace 0 is written `d_1 h_1 + (d_1+d_2) h_2`),
and buckets all pairs and triples of basis elements by total weight. For each
weight `w` it builds the integer matrices of

```text
d_2(x^y)   = -[x,y],
d_3(x^y^z) = -[x,y]^z + [x,z]^y - [y,z]^x,
```

and computes Smith normal forms with FLINT (`fmpz_mat.hnf`, then `snf` of the
nonzero rows; both exact over `Z`). Since
`Lambda^2_w / ker d_2 = im d_2` is a subgroup of the free group `L_w`,
`ker d_2` is a direct summand of `Lambda^2_w`, so
`Tors H_2(L_Z)_w = Tors(Lambda^2_w / im d_3)`, whose invariant factors are the
elementary divisors `> 1` of `d_3`. The free rank is
`rank ker d_2 - rank d_3`. The output `h2z90.out` has one line per weight with
nonzero `H_2` (and a per-degree summary). `check_pattern.py h2z90.out`
compares every weight line with the display (Z2) and checks that every
weight at which (Z2) predicts nonzero `H_2` is listed:
`max degree 90; 212 weights with nonzero H_2 listed, 212 predicted; 0 anomalies, 0 predicted weights missing`.
The run takes about one CPU minute.

Calibrations. (i) The free part is `Z^6` at the six weights of degree 3,
namely `rho - w rho` for the six `w` of length 2 in the affine Weyl group, as
Garland--Lepowsky predict over `Q`. (ii) Reducing mod `p` via section 2
reproduces every table entry of the deviation claim's independent `F_p`
computation (`h2loop.py`) in the overlap `k <= 90`: `(3,6)` and `(3mp,1)` for
`p = 5,...,23`, and `6,6,7,6,6,7,6,6` at degrees `3,...,24` for `p=3`.

## 4. (Z3) and (Z4)

Take `p >= 5`. By (Z1) and (Z2), in degrees `k <= 90` the only weights with
`H_2(L_Z)_w (x) F_p != 0` are the six Serre weights (rank 1 each, so
`dim H_2(L_p)_3 = 6`) and the weights `n delta` with `p | n` (one invariant
factor `n`, so dimension 1 in degree `3n`). The invariant factors 2 and 3 do
not contribute. Hence `D_p cap [1,90] = {3} + {3mp <= 90}` with dimensions
`6` and `1`.

For `p = 3`, degree `3n` with `2 <= n <= 30` gets `6` from the six weights
`n delta + alpha_i - alpha_j` (factor 3 each) and `1` more exactly when
`3 | n` (factor `n` at `n delta`). Degree 5 contributes nothing at `p = 3`.

## 5. (Z5) Towers, uniformly in p

Let `p` be odd. By (G) of the deviation claim, `gr(Gamma^) = F/J` with
`J <= I = ker(F -> L_p)`, where `F = F_Z (x) F_p`. We have
`dim F_1 = 3 = dim (L_p)_1`, `dim F_2 = 3 = dim (L_p)_2`, so `I_1 = I_2 = 0`.
`dim F_3 = 8` and `dim (L_p)_3 = 2`, so `dim I_3 = 6`. The six Serre elements
`[e_i,[e_i,e_j]]` (`i != j`) are linearly independent in `F_3` (they are six
of the eight Lyndon basis brackets of degree 3), they lie in `I_3`
(e.g. `[E_12,[E_12,E_23]] = [E_12,E_13] = 0`), and they lie in `J_3`:
in the vertex group `<x,y>` (`{x,y}` one of `{a,b}`, `{b,c}`, `{c,a}`), the
commutator `[x,y]` is central, so `[[x,y],x] = [[x,y],y] = 1` in `Gamma`. So
`J_3 = I_3` and `gr_k(Gamma^) = gr_k(I_1)` for `k <= 3`, i.e.
`|Gamma/P_4| = |I_1/P_4|`.

Now apply the deviation bound (B) of
`kms-hc1-a2-iwahori-towers-deviate-only-at-kassel-degrees` inductively for
`k = 4, 5, ...`: as long as `k` is not in `D_p`, the towers agree at `k`.
By (Z3), the least element of `D_p` above 3 is `3p` if `p <= 29`
(`3p <= 87`), and it is `> 90` if `p >= 31`. So
`dim gr_k(Gamma^) = dim gr_k(I_1)` for `k <= 3p-1`, respectively `k <= 90`,
which is `|Gamma/P_k| = |I_1/P_k|` for `k <= min(3p, 91)`. A p-group image of
p-class `c` factors through `Gamma/P_{c+1}(Gamma)`, which maps onto
`I_1/P_{c+1}(I_1)` (surjectivity of `phi^`) and has the same order, so it is
isomorphic to it; the image kills `w_p` because `phi(w_p) = 1`.

## 6. (Z6) The reduction and the Serre-ring form

Both directions of the equivalence are section 2: `dim H_2(L_p)_w` is
determined by the rank and the `p`-divisible invariant factors at `w`, and
conversely the invariant-factor count is `dim H_2(L_p)_w - rank`. The rank is
6 in degree 3 and 0 elsewhere (Garland--Lepowsky homology of the positive
part of the affine Kac--Moody algebra of type `A~2`, which is `L_Q`; checked
through degree 90 in section 3). So "`D_p = {3} + 3pN` with dimensions 6 and
1 for all `p >= 5`" holds iff the displayed torsion statement holds.

Hopf formula over `Z`. Let `R = ker(F_Z -> L_Z)`. Since `L_Z` and `R` are free
abelian and `H_2(F_Z) = 0` (`U(F_Z)` is a tensor algebra), the five-term
sequence of the Hochschild--Serre spectral sequence for `R <| F_Z` gives
`H_2(L_Z) = R/[F_Z,R]` (note `R <= [F_Z,F_Z]` by section 1). Let `S <= R` be
the ideal generated by the six Serre elements, `K_Z = F_Z/S` and
`J = R/S = ker(K_Z -> L_Z)`. Then `J/[K_Z,J] = R/(S + [F_Z,R])`, and in
degrees `>= 4` the image of `S` in `R/[F_Z,R]` is 0 (`S_{>=4} = [F_Z,S]_{>=4}`).
So `H_2(L_Z)_k = (J/[K_Z,J])_k` for `k >= 4`. By Gabber--Kac,
`K_Z (x) Q = L_Q`, so `J (x) Q = 0` and `J` is torsion; as `L_Z` is torsion
free, `J = Tors K_Z`. Thus (Z2) says: the Serre-presented Lie ring of type
`A~2` over `Z` has torsion, and its coinvariants in degree `>= 4` are
`Z/n` at `n delta`, `Z/3` at `n delta + alpha_i - alpha_j`, and `Z/2` in
degree 5, through degree 90.

## 7. What the pattern suggests (not claimed)

The `Z/n` at `n delta` is the integral Kassel class: the form
`omega_n(t^a X, t^b Y) = a tr(XY) [a+b = n]` is a `Z/n`-valued 2-cocycle on
`L_Z`, and for `n = mp` it reduces to the cocycle (K) of the deviation claim.
In terms of the Iwahori order `Lambda = Z^3[pi; sigma]`
(`pi = E_12 + E_23 + tE_31`, `pi^3 = t`, `pi`-degree = p-central degree), the
cyclic homology `HC_1(Lambda)` is `Z/n` in degree `3n`: `HH_1` is `Z` in each
degree `3n`, and Connes' `B` multiplies the generator by `n`. So the data fit
`H_2(L_Z) (x) Z[1/6] = (Serre) + HC_1(Lambda) (x) Z[1/6]`, a Kassel--Loday
statement for the Iwahori order. A proof of that statement, e.g. through the
torsion of `K_Z` via a Steinberg Lie ring of `Lambda`, would settle Open 1 in
all degrees.
