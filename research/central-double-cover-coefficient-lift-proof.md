## A torsion-free integral coefficient ring

Assume J!=1 in H. Since J is central and J^2=1, it has exact order two.
Put R=Z[H]/(J+1). Choose a representative h from each coset of <J>.
The relations in R identify [Jh]=-[h], so R is a free abelian group on
these representatives. To see there are no additional additive relations,
decompose Z[H] into the free rank-two summands Z[h] plus Z[Jh]; the
ideal (J+1) is exactly the sum of the subgroups generated by [h]+[Jh].

In C[H], let e=(1-[J])/2. This is a nonzero central idempotent. The map

    R -> e C[H] e,       [h] -> e[h]

is an injective unital map, where the target identity is e. Indeed the
chosen representative images ([h]-[Jh])/2 have pairwise disjoint group
supports and are linearly independent over Z.

The ordinary coefficient group ring R[G], with H-coefficients commuting
with external G-labels, therefore embeds coefficientwise into

    e C[H x G] e.

The complex group algebra C[H x G] embeds faithfully in its finite
group von Neumann algebra. Its corner at e is directly finite; so is
the displayed algebraic corner and its unital subring R[G]. This uses
the classical characteristic-zero direct-finiteness theorem only.
It applies to arbitrary H and G, irrespective of finite approximations.

## Pair cancellation and coefficient reduction

Write x_i,y_j for the images of [X_i],[Y_j] in R. Define in R[G]

    B_tilde=sum_i x_i [t_i],       A_tilde=sum_j y_j [s_j].

In a paired cell, t_i s_j=t_k s_l and x_i y_j=-x_k y_l, because
[J]=-1 in R. Hence the two corresponding summands cancel exactly.
The pivot contributes x_0 y_0[t_0 s_0]=1. Thus
B_tilde A_tilde=1. Direct finiteness of R[G] gives
A_tilde B_tilde=1 as well.

The homomorphism Z[H]->F_2 sending every group element to one kills
J+1 and factors through R. All x_i,y_j reduce to one. Applying this
map coefficientwise to the reverse identity gives AB=1 in F_2[G].
This proves the main assertion and its contrapositive for every pairing.

Scalar phase lifts are a special case. After setting the two coefficient
phases at the chosen pivot to one by the independent scalar row/column
normalizations, each relation maps J to -1 in a group of roots of unity.
Thus J survives in H. The D_8 model in the attached example proves that
the converse implication from central-sign survival to scalar phase
existence is false even for a valid F_2 inverse rectangle.

## Exact parity of identities among relators

Let F be free on the row and column generators other than X_0,Y_0,
and let N be the normal closure of the unsigned relators r_e in F.
Then Gamma=F/N is the ordinary normalized matching group. We prove

    J survives in H
      iff every identity among conjugates of r_e has even length.

If an odd identity (CSI1) exists, impose r_e=J in H. Each conjugated
relator or inverse becomes J, since J is central of order two. The
identity then becomes 1=J^q=J, so J collapses.

Conversely, suppose every identity in F expressed as a product of
conjugates of r_e or r_e^(-1) has even length. For n in N, choose any
such expression and define phi(n) in C_2 to be its length modulo two.
Two expressions for the same n give, on multiplying one by the inverse
of the other, an identity among relators. The assumed evenness makes
phi well-defined. It is a homomorphism N->C_2 and is invariant under
conjugation by F. Hence K=ker phi is normal in F and

    1 -> N/K ~= C_2 -> F/K -> Gamma -> 1

is central, provided there is at least one relator. The image of every
r_e is the same nonidentity central element j. Sending the presentation
generators of H to their images in F/K and J to j respects every
relation, so J survives in H. If there are no paired cells, H itself
has its free central C_2 factor and the assertion is immediate.

This establishes (CSI1) as an exact obstruction. Abelianizing F in
(CSI1) gives an odd integral relation among the pairing vectors, so
the diagram requirement strengthens the earlier lattice requirement.
The converse need not hold: the D_8 example has an odd integral
dependence but retains J.

## The aspherical-presentation corollary

Suppose the ordinary normalized presentation complex of Gamma is
aspherical. The relation module N/[N,N] is then the free Z[Gamma]-module
on the displayed relators r_e. One way to see this is to lift the
presentation complex to its contractible universal cover: its one-skeleton
is the covering of the free-generator bouquet corresponding to N, whose
first homology is N/[N,N]. The boundaries of the lifted two-cells give
an isomorphism from the free module on these cells to this first homology,
since the universal cover has zero first and second homology.

Taking coinvariants under F (equivalently Gamma on the relation module)
gives N/[F,N], free abelian on the r_e. The homomorphism sending every
such basis element to one in C_2 yields an F-conjugation-invariant
phi:N->C_2 with phi(r_e)=1. The preceding central-extension construction
therefore retains J and excludes a reverse defect.

The proof actually only needs this parity homomorphism on the relation
module; asphericity is a sufficient condition. An aspherical presentation
elsewhere for the same abstract group does not certify the displayed
relator identities. Nor does this corollary settle direct finiteness for
all groups admitting an aspherical presentation.
