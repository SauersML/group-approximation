Let `Gamma` be a countable group with a central involution `w`.  Suppose it
contains `B=N semidirect H`, where `N` is abelian and `H` is nonamenable.
Assume there are a finite subgroup `K <= Gamma` containing `w` and a subset
`V` of the normalizer of `K` such that:

1. with `e_-=(1-w)/2`, write the finite-dimensional block

   ```text
   e_- C*(K) e_- isomorphic to directSum_(j in J) M_(d_j);
   ```
2. for every `v in V`, conjugation by `v` induces an automorphism `alpha_v`
   of this block.  On every summand fixed by the induced permutation of `J`,
   an implementing unitary for `alpha_v|M_(d_j)` has normalized trace zero;
3. every `n in N\{e}` is conjugate in `Gamma` to an element of `V`;
4. every `h in H\{e}` has Haar-null fixed-point set for the dual action on
   `N_hat`.

Then every homomorphism from `Gamma` to the unitary group of a finite
injective von Neumann algebra kills `w`.

The trace condition in (2) is intrinsic: two implementers of the same
automorphism of a fixed matrix summand differ by a scalar.  Summands moved
by `alpha_v` require no trace test—the corresponding part of every
implementing unitary is off-diagonal and hence already traceless.  No
Clifford presentation, property (T), residual finiteness, invariant-measure
rigidity, or character classification is required.

The signed-Clifford criterion is a special case.  Take `K` to be the finite
extraspecial two-group generated by the central sign and an even Clifford
packet.  Its negative central-character block is the spin matrix block, and
the signed-cycle formula computes exactly when the normalizer implementer in
that block is traceless.
