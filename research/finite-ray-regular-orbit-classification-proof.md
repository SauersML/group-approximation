First suppose an infinite P<=E_m has a free orbit Omega. The permutation
of the m ray germs gives a homomorphism P->S_m. Its kernel P_0 has finite
index in P and preserves each ray eventually. For every ray i the
eventual integer translation defines a homomorphism chi_i:P_0->Z.

Some ray i meets the infinite set Omega in infinitely many points.
If chi_i(p)=0, p fixes the tail of that ray, hence some point of Omega.
Freeness of the orbit gives p=1. Thus chi_i is injective. P_0 is infinite
because it has finite index in infinite P, and its image is a nonzero
subgroup of Z. Hence P_0 is infinite cyclic and P is virtually cyclic.
This argument does not need P to be finitely generated.

The regular copy of P in E_nu(P) has the even numbers as a free orbit.
Conjugating the entire permutation action preserves freeness of that
orbit. The preceding paragraph therefore proves (3) implies (1);
(2) implies (3) is immediate.

For (1) implies (2), start with an infinite cyclic subgroup of finite
index in P. Intersect its finitely many conjugates to obtain an infinite
cyclic normal subgroup C=<t> of finite index d. Choose representatives
r_1,...,r_d so each element of P is uniquely t^k r_i. Define

    nu(t^k r_i)=2d*k+i                    if k>=0,
    nu(t^k r_i)=2d*(-k-1)+d+i             if k<0.

These are the positive and negative rays of each cyclic coset, and the
formula is a bijection P->N.

For p in P, write ptp^-1=t^epsilon with epsilon in {1,-1}, and
pr_i=t^(c_i) r_(sigma(i)). Then

    p(t^k r_i)=t^(epsilon*k+c_i) r_(sigma(i)).

On each of the 2d rays, for sufficiently large ray coordinate, the sign
of epsilon*k+c_i is constant. The displayed formula becomes a
permutation of rays followed by an integer translation of ray coordinate.
Thus the nu-transported regular action of P lies in E_(2d).

Transporting that action instead onto the even numbers, fixing odd
numbers, puts it in E_(4d). Also E_2<=E_(4d), since 2 divides 4d.
Consequently E_nu(P)<=E_(4d), proving (2).

For the Z^2 instance, no cyclic subgroup has finite index: a nonzero
cyclic subgroup generated by (a,b) is killed by the nonzero homomorphism
(x,y)->b*x-a*y, whose image is infinite. The trivial subgroup also has
infinite index. Thus the criterion excludes every finite-ray realization
of every E_nu(Z^2).

Only eventual translations and the regular action enter this proof.
No assumption about finite presentation of the envelope is used.
