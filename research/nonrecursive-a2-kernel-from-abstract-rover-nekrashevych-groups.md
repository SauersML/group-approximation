Lane proof by bh-ffwz-q59, 2026-09-18. One referee PASS (bh-ref-ffwz, see Referee below); no priority is claimed.

**Literature input.** L. Bartholdi and I. Mitrofanov, *The word and order problems for
self-similar and automata groups*, arXiv:1710.10109v4, Theorem A″ (p. 2, read from the arXiv
PDF). There is a finite alphabet `A`, a finite set `S` and a map `Φ : A × S → F_S × A`. These
define a group `⟨Φ⟩` acting faithfully and self-similarly on `A^*` by (1.1),
`(a_1 a_2 ... a_n)^s = a_1' (a_2 ... a_n)^{s'}`, where `(s', a_1') = Φ(a_1, s)`. The set
`{ f ∈ F_S : f = 1 in ⟨Φ⟩ }` is not recursive. Here `⟨Φ⟩` is the quotient of `F_S` acting on
`A^*`.

**Correction (2026-09-19).** As printed in the arXiv versions, Bartholdi–Mitrofanov's Theorem A″ rests on their Proposition 2.4, whose proof has a gap. There `[g,u] ≠ 1` for every machine. It is repaired by replacing `u` with a partner `p` supported on `0^*1A^ω`; see `bartholdi-mitrofanov-proposition-2-4-gap-and-repair` (referee bh-ref-f PASS, 6747a7e43d). Theorem A″ holds for the repaired table `G′_M = ⟨Φ_M, p, p′⟩`. Read `Φ` below as the repaired table, whose generators include `p` and `p′`. Steps 0–3 go through unchanged, and the kernel `N` is the repaired table's, which is not recursive.

## Step 0. A left recursion with the same kernel

Put `d = |A|` and identify `A = {1, ..., d}`.
- **The left action.** `(1.1)` is a right action by tree automorphisms. The map
  `g ↦ (u ↦ u^{g^{−1}})` turns it into a faithful left action with the same kernel on `F_S`.
  In it, each generator `s` acts as `i u ↦ π_s(i) g_{s,i}(u)` for a permutation `π_s` of `A`
  and states `g_{s,i}`. The states are elements of the group, since the action is
  self-similar in the sense of (1.1).
- **The recursion.** Choose words `ŝ_i ∈ F_S` representing them, and let
  `ψ : F_S → S_d ≀_d F_S` be the homomorphism with `ψ(s) = π_s ⟨ŝ_1, ..., ŝ_d⟩` on generators.
- **The kernel.** The tree action `ρ_ψ` of `abstract-rover-nekrashevych-groups-are-finitely-presented`
  satisfies the same recursion as the left action on generators, so the two agree by
  induction on word length. Hence `N := ker ρ_ψ = { f : f = 1 in ⟨Φ⟩ }`, which is not
  recursive.

## Step 1. The actor

Let `P = V_d(F_S, ψ)`. It is finitely presented, by the target of `requires` with `G = F_S`
(no relators). Let `P` act on the set `Ω` of proper nonempty clopen subsets of `C_d` through
`P → V_d(ρ_ψ(F_S)) ≤ Homeo(C_d)`.

## Step 2. P ↷ Ω is of type [A_2]

**(a) Finitely many orbits on Ω × Ω.**
- **Atoms and residues.** For a pair `(U_1, U_2)`, look at its four Boolean atoms, and at the
  residue `c(·) mod (d − 1)` of each nonempty atom (notation of §0 of
  `abstract-rover-nekrashevych-finite-presentation-proof`).
- **The count.** Homeomorphisms preserve which atoms are empty. Elements of `V_d` preserve
  residues. So at most `7 · (d − 1)^4` patterns occur.
- **One orbit per pattern, already under `V_d ≤ P`.** Take two pairs with the same pattern.
  Choose cone partitions of corresponding atoms of equal size, which is possible because
  their residues agree. The resulting partitions of `C_d` have equal size, and the
  prefix-replacement element they define maps atoms to atoms. This is step (5) of
  `twisted-btb-clopen-action-type-a-proof`, with residues added.

**(b) Point stabilizers are finitely generated.** For a clopen `Y`, let `D(Y) ≤ P` be the
classes with a representative whose cones outside `Y` carry `β(p) = p` and label `1`.
- **Splitting.** Let `x ∈ P` with `x(U) = U`. Refine a representative until its domain
  partition refines `{U, U^c}`. A table maps each domain cone onto its image cone, so the
  cones in `U` go to cones in `U`, and likewise for `U^c`. Cutting the table along
  `{U, U^c}` writes `x = x_U x_{U^c}` with `x_U ∈ D(U)` and `x_{U^c} ∈ D(U^c)`. Hence
  `P_U = D(U) × D(U^c)`: the factors commute and meet trivially.
- **Generation.** Fix a cone `w` with `C(w) ⊊ Y`. Then `D(Y)` is generated by the
  finitely generated group `V_d(Y) = {v ∈ V_d : supp v ⊆ Y}` (a copy of `V_(d,r)`) and by
  `ι_w(F_S)`, the labels on `C(w)` (L1 there).
  - Every element of `D(Y)` is `v_β ∏_{p ⊆ Y} ι_p(λ(p))`, with `v_β ∈ V_d(Y)` (§3 there).
  - For a cone `p ⊊ Y`, `ι_p(g) = a ι_w(g) a^{−1}` for some `a ∈ V_d(Y)` with
    `a(wu) = pu`. Such an `a` exists because `Y \ C(w)` and `Y \ C(p)` are nonempty with
    equal residues.
  - If `p = Y` is a single cone, first expand `ι_p(g)` into its `d` children by L4.

So `P` is finitely presented, its point stabilizers are finitely generated, and it has
finitely many orbits on `Ω × Ω`: type `[A_2]`.

## Step 3. The kernel is not recursive

- **The kernel.** A homeomorphism fixing every clopen set is the identity, so
  `K := ker(P ↷ Ω) = ker(P → Homeo(C_d))`.
- **The root copy of `F_S`.** Let `r : F_S → P` send `f` to the class of
  `({∅}, {∅}, id, f)`. It is a homomorphism, and `r(f)` acts on `C_d` as `ρ_ψ(f)`. So
  `r(f) ∈ K ⇔ f ∈ N`.
- **Computing `r`.** It is computable as a map from words to words in the generators of the
  presentation:
  - expanding once, `r(s) = e′_s · ∏_i a_i ŝ_i(x) a_i^{−1}`;
  - `e′_s ∈ V_d` is `iu ↦ π_s(i)u`, and `a_i ∈ V_d` is a fixed element with `a_i(1u) = iu`;
  - `r(f)` is the product along the word `f`.
- **Conclusion.** If `K` were recursive, `N` would be recursive, contrary to Step 0. ∎

## Consequences

1. **`some-a2-action-has-a-nonrecursive-kernel` is established.** So
   `a2-action-kernels-are-recursive` is refuted.
2. **No kernel removal for this pair.** By `non-removable-a2-kernel-from-nonrecursive-kernel`,
   `(P, K)` sharply embeds in no type `[A_2]` pair with finitely normally generated kernel.
   So `a2-pair-with-non-removable-kernel-exists` holds, and the first part of FFWZ
   Question 5.9 has answer **no**.
3. **The second part is also no:** `fp-relatively-simple-pair-with-unsolvable-simple-quotient`.
4. **The strongest form of FFWZ Question 5.8.** The image `P/K = V_d(⟨Φ⟩)` is not even
   recursively presented, and its point stabilizers are finitely generated but not finitely
   presented, as `a2-stabilizer-image-presentation-controls-kernel` (3) requires.
5. **The actor itself can be decidable.** Make `ψ` injective by the padding in Remark 2 of
   the claim used; `N` does not change. Then `P` has solvable word problem, and only its
   kernel is undecidable.

## Consistency checks against the recorded constraints

- **`a2-stabilizer-image-presentation-controls-kernel` (4).** Finitely generated
  `Γ ≤ P` with `Γ ∩ K = 1` have solvable word problem. The hardness here sits in the
  quotient `r(F_S)K/K = ⟨Φ⟩`. The root copy `r(F_S)` meets `K` in `r(N) ≠ 1`, so it is not
  sharply embedded.
- **`a2-kernel-recursion-ascends-normal-orbit-finite-chains` (5).** Its three constraints
  on counterexamples now hold for `P` automatically. They are forced by Step 3 and were not
  checked independently. For example, no finitely generated orbit-finite normal subgroup of
  `P` meets `K` trivially.

## Lesson for general BH

A finite presentation can hide an arbitrary co-r.e. kernel inside an oligomorphic action.
The mechanism is a recursion table over a free group:
- the table makes the group finitely presented;
- the Thompson part makes the clopen action oligomorphic;
- the kernel of the table's tree action is as undecidable as a Minsky machine.

So removing kernels is not a general tool. `(relPBH) ⇒ (PBH)` and `(relBH) ⇒ (BH)` cannot go
through FFWZ Question 5.9. Any route from BH to PBH (BFFHZ Q3.4) must use the special
structure of the actors it removes kernels from. For the Theorem C actors of finitely
presented simple groups, that structure is mixed identities: the question becomes whether
those kernels are recursive and their images lie in the permutational class. General actors
are out of reach.
