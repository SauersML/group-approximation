Let `S` be an infinite finitely generated simple group and `n >= 2`.  Put

    A = Aut_S(S * F_n) ,      X = Hom_S(S * F_n, S)  ~=  S^n ,

with `A` acting on `X` by precomposition.  Then the action is highly
transitive, and point stabilizers are finitely generated.

Faithfulness is **not** part of this claim.  It is known under a
mixed-identity-free hypothesis on `S`, and that hypothesis is used for
nothing else in the analysis of the action.

**Verification status.**  Read from source 2026-08-17.
Belk--Fournier-Facio--Hyde--Zaremsky, *Boone--Higman embeddings of Aut(F_n)
and mapping class groups of punctured surfaces*, arXiv:2503.21882v2
(submitted 27 March 2025, revised 14 April 2025).  Proposition 2.3: "the
action of Aut_G(G*F_n) on Hom_G(G*F_n,G) is highly transitive".  Proposition
2.6: "If G is finitely generated then Stab(φ₀) is finitely generated".  On
the MIF hypothesis: "this is the only place in all the analysis of the action
where we need G to be MIF".

## Why this graph carries it

Because the missing piece is unusually well isolated.  Let

    K = ker( A acting on X ) ,      Abar = A/K .

Then `Abar` acts faithfully and highly transitively on `X` with finitely
generated point stabilizers, and — this is the part that matters here — the
translation subgroup `S <= A` acts freely on `X`, so `K ∩ S = 1` and `S`
still embeds in `Abar`.  Every hypothesis of
`twisted-brin-thompson-finite-presentation-criterion` therefore holds for
`Abar` acting on `X` **except** finite presentability of `Abar`, which is
`bffhz-action-image-is-finitely-presented`.

## One effectivity remark that is easy to over-read

If `S` is finitely generated, computably presented and simple, then `A` is
finitely generated and has solvable word problem: `S` has solvable word
problem by the two-enumeration argument of
`simple-envelope-forces-solvable-word-problem-proof` run against a computable
presentation, the word problem of `S * F_n` follows, and a word in the
automorphism generators is decided by computing its effect on a finite
generating set.  So `A` is computably presented.

That says nothing about `Abar`.  The kernel `K` is an intersection of point
stabilizers and is not known to be recursively enumerable from this data, so
`A` computably presented does not make `A/K` computably presented, let alone
finitely presented.  The decidability remark is recorded because it is true
and cheap, and flagged because it is about the wrong group.
