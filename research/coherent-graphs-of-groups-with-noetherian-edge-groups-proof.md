Let `K <= G` be finitely generated. The full argument is Theorem 1.3 of the
artifact.

1. **Elliptic case.** If every element of `K` fixes a vertex, then `K` fixes
   a vertex (Serre, *Trees*, I.6.5). So `K` lies in a coherent stabilizer and
   is finitely presented.
2. **Hyperbolic case.** Otherwise `K` has a minimal invariant subtree with
   finite quotient graph (Bass 1993, Prop. 7.9). So `K` is the fundamental
   group of a finite graph of groups whose edge groups are subgroups of
   Noetherian stabilizers, hence finitely generated.
3. **Vertex groups are finitely generated.** Collapse one edge at a time. If
   `A *_C B` (or `A *_C`) is finitely generated and `C` is finitely generated,
   then `A` and `B` are finitely generated. The subamalgam generated by the
   finitely many letters used together with `C` embeds and is everything, and
   uniqueness of normal forms puts `A` inside it.
4. **Finite presentation.** The vertex groups are finitely generated subgroups
   of coherent groups, so they are finitely presented. A finite graph of
   finitely presented groups with finitely generated edge groups has a finite
   presentation.
