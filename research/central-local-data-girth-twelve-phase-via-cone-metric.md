## The simple complex of groups

Let `Q` be the poset with elements `A_c` (contexts), `H_v` (variables) and one
element `*`, ordered as a cone:

```text
* < H_v  for all v,     * < A_c  for all c,     H_v < A_c  whenever v in V_c.
```

Assign `P_* = J`, `P_(H_v) = H_v`, `P_(A_c) = A_c`, with the given inclusions
`J -> H_v -> A_c` and `J -> A_c`. The inclusions fix `J`, so the diagram commutes.
By (PR) every map is injective and non-surjective, so this is a simple complex
of finite groups. Its direct limit is by definition the local colimit
`Lambda(D)`. The realization `K = |Q|` is the cone with apex `*` over the
incidence graph. Its 2-simplices are the chains `* < H_v < A_c`, one per
incidence.

This uses the order in which larger elements carry larger groups, as in
Prytula's convention; the source records that this reverses Bridson--Haefliger's
order, and the constructions agree.

## The metric

Make each 2-simplex a Euclidean triangle with angle `pi/6` at `*`, `pi/2` at
`H_v` and `pi/3` at `A_c` (sum `pi`), scaled so that the shortest edge has length
1. There are finitely many shapes.

## Local developments and their links

Take the link of the central vertex of each local development, with the
angular metric.

1. **At `*`.** Nothing lies below `*`, so the local development is the star of
   `*` in `K`, and its link is the incidence graph `Inc(D)`. Every edge has
   length `pi/6`. By (G12) every embedded cycle has at least 12 edges, so it has
   length at least `2 pi`.
2. **At `H_v`.** Below `H_v` sits only `*`, with group `J`; it is replicated by
   the cosets `H_v/J`. Above `H_v` sit the contexts `A_c` with `v in V_c`, not
   replicated. So the link is the complete bipartite graph on
   `{c : v in V_c}` and `H_v/J`, with every edge of length `pi/2`. Embedded
   cycles have length at least 4 edges, i.e. at least `2 pi`.
3. **At `A_c`.** Everything below is replicated by cosets. The link has
   vertices `A_c/J` (chambers) and `A_c/H_v` for `v in V_c`, with an edge from
   `gJ` to `gH_v` of length `pi/3`. The graph is bipartite between chambers and
   subgroup cosets, so embedded cycles have even length, and it has no 2-cycles.
   A 4-cycle would have the form `gJ - X - g'J - Y - gJ` with `gJ != g'J` and
   distinct cosets `X`, `Y` each containing both chambers.
   - Two distinct cosets of the same `H_v` cannot contain a common chamber, so
     `X = gH_v = g'H_v` and `Y = gH_w = g'H_w` with `v != w`.
   - Then `g^(-1) g' in H_v cap H_w = J` by (CI), so `gJ = g'J`, a
     contradiction.

   So every embedded cycle has at least 6 edges, i.e. length at least `2 pi`.

So the link of the central vertex of every local development has angular girth
at least `2 pi`. This is exactly the check that Prytula's proof of Theorem 4.1
(arXiv:2004.08187, source lines 371--383, quoted in the artifact) performs to
conclude nonpositive curvature in the sense of Bridson--Haefliger, for the same
cone metric and the same three types of local development. The only
differences are that the cone group is `J` rather than trivial, and that
chambers are therefore cosets of `J` rather than elements. So the complex of
groups is nonpositively curved.

## Conclusion

By `nonpositively-curved-simple-complexes-are-developable`,
the complex of groups is strictly developable. So `P_(H_v) = H_v`, and with it
`J`, injects into `Lambda(D)`. Hence `J` has order exactly `n` in `Lambda(D)`.
The decoder statement is (LC1) of
`finite-readout-bcs-decoders-are-phase-surviving-local-colimits`.

## Calibration

- **Linear systems with at least three variables per equation.** Take `A_c`
  generated by `J` and commuting involutions `x_v`, `v in V_c`, subject only to
  `prod_(v in V_c) x_v = J^(b_c)`. For distinct `v, w in V_c`, `x_w in <J, x_v>`
  would put the two-term product `x_v x_w` in `J`. The single relation puts only
  the full product, of at least three generators, in `J`, so this does not
  happen. Hence `<J, x_v> cap <J, x_w> = J` and (CI) holds.
- **The two-variable cyclic system.** `x_(e_i) in x_(e_(i-1)) J`, so
  `H_(e_(i-1)) cap H_(e_i)` is larger than `J` and (CI) fails, matching the
  collapse of `J` there.
