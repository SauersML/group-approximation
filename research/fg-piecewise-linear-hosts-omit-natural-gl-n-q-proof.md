**1. Finite coefficients.** By the proof of `fg-groups-have-fg-derivative-images`,
every `c(γ, x)` and its inverse `c(γ^-1, γx)` is a product of matrices
`c(s, y)` and `c(s, y)^-1` with `s in S`. By definition all of these have entries
in `R`. So `c(γ, x)` is an invertible matrix over `R`, i.e. it lies in
`GL_N(R)`. `R` is
generated by finitely many elements, since each `c(s, -)` is finitely valued.

**`R` does not contain `Q`.** Suppose `Q ⊂ R` and take a maximal ideal `m`.
- `F = R/m` is a field that is finitely generated as a `Q`-algebra, so it is a
  number field (Zariski's lemma), and it is finitely generated as a ring.
- Write `F = Z[a_1..a_k]` and pick `d >= 1` with every `d a_i` integral. Then
  `F ⊂ O_F[1/d]`.
- For a prime `p ∤ d` we have `1/p in F`, so `d^j / p in O_F` for some `j`.
  Taking norms, `p^[F:Q]` divides `d^(j[F:Q])` in `Z`, so `p | d`.

This is a contradiction. The same argument shows that no finitely generated
commutative ring contains `Q`.

**2. Fixed-point rigidity.**
- By part 2 of the lemma, `c(-, x)` is a homomorphism `Γ_x -> GL_N(R)`. Its
  image lies in `Λ`, which is finitely generated and linear, hence residually
  finite (Mal'cev). Subgroups of residually finite groups are residually finite.
- The image of `P` is a quotient of `P` and a subgroup of a residually finite
  group, so it is residually finite. It is therefore trivial.
- **Examples.** A divisible group has only divisible quotients, and a finite
  divisible group is trivial. For `n >= 2` every normal subgroup of `SL_n(Q)` is
  central (so `<= {±1}`) or everything. So every nontrivial quotient of `SL_n(Q)`
  is `SL_n(Q)` or `PSL_n(Q)`. Neither has a nontrivial finite quotient, so
  neither is residually finite.
- If `γ in Γ_x` is affine `v -> A v + b` near `x` with `A = 1`, then `A x + b = x`
  forces `b = 0`, so `γ` is the identity near `x`. The projective right-germ
  case is the same.

**3.**
- The entries of `c(H, x)` lie in `R` by step 1, so the ring they generate is
  in `R` and does not contain `Q`.
- In (a) the entries of `GL_n(Q)` exhaust `Q`. In (b) the entries of the
  unipotents give every `q`. In (c) the dilations give `Q^x`, which generates
  `Q`. In (d) the upper-triangular lifts `[[α,β],[0,α^-1]]` give every `β`.

**Consequence.** For the chain `GL_n(Z[1/m!])` with the natural action, the
origin is fixed and `c(g, 0) = g`. A finitely generated overgroup with the
extended cocycle would give (a).
