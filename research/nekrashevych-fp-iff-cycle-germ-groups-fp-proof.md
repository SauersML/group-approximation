Notation of the claim. `F = F(S)`, `N = ker(F -> G)`. The stages `M_α` of a lift ideal `⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift` are as in
§1 of `loop-retraction-forces-fp-eventual-section-groups-proof`.

## 0. The eventual-state cocycle

Fix a closed family `𝒪` of stable letters.
- `Ω = {c y^ω : c in X^*, y in 𝒪}`, with `obj(c y^ω) = y`. It is `G`-invariant: for a state `t`,
  `t(y^ω) = t(y^k) e(y)^ω` with `e = e_y(t)`, and `e(y) in 𝒪`.
- `Π` is the free groupoid on objects `𝒪` with one arrow `[e,y]: y -> e(y)` for each state `e ≠ 1` and
  `y in 𝒪` with `e|_y = e`. Since `e^{-1}|_{e(y)} = (e|_y)^{-1}`, the arrow `[e^{-1}, e(y)]` is taken as the
  inverse of `[e,y]`.
- For a letter `t` and `ξ = c y^ω in Ω`, let `e` be the eventual value of `t|_{c y^k}`. Put `Φ_ξ(t) = [e, y]`,
  or the identity at `y` if `e = 1`. It is an arrow `obj(ξ) -> obj(tξ)`. For a word `w = t_1 ⋯ t_n`, put
  `Φ_ξ(w) = Φ_{t_2⋯t_n ξ}(t_1) ⋯ Φ_ξ(t_n)`.

**(Φ1) Cocycle.** `Φ_ξ(uv) = Φ_{vξ}(u) Φ_ξ(v)`. This is the definition.

**(Φ2) Free reduction.** The sections of `t` at `t^{-1}(c')` are `((t^{-1})|_{c'})^{-1}`. So if `t^{-1}` has
eventual state `e` at `ξ`, then `t` has eventual state `e^{-1}` at `t^{-1}ξ`, and `Φ_ξ(t t^{-1})` is the
identity. So `Φ` is defined on `F` and is a cocycle for the action of `F` on `Ω` through `G`.

**(Φ3) Sections.** `Φ_{zξ'}(w) = Φ_{ξ'}(w|_z)`, where `w|_z` is the word of letter sections. Each letter has the
same eventual state along its input ray `z_i ξ'_i` as its section `t_i|_{z_i}` along `ξ'_i`.

**(Φ4) Finiteness.** `Φ_{c y^ω}(r) = Φ_{y^ω}(r|_c)`. For a fixed word `r`, the reduced words `red(r|_c)` form a
finite set, since they have length `<= |r|`.

**(Φ5) Germs.** For a closed path `p = [e_1,y_1] ⋯ [e_n,y_n]` at `y`, let `ŵ(p) = e_1 ⋯ e_n in F`.
- **Paths to words.** Each `e_i` receives `y_i` and keeps itself, so `ŵ(p)` fixes `y` and `ŵ(p)|_y = ŵ(p)`. Also
  `Φ_{y^ω}(ŵ(p)) = p`. A reduced path gives a reduced word: consecutive letters `e_i = e_{i+1}^{-1}` together
  with composability `y_i = e_{i+1}(y_{i+1})` would make the two arrows mutually inverse. So `ŵ` is an
  injective groupoid morphism `Π -> F`.
- **The germ map.** Let `ev(p)` be the germ of `ŵ(p)` at `y^ω`.
  - It is a homomorphism `π_1(Π, y) -> G_(y^ω)`.
  - Its kernel is `K_y = {p : ŵ(p) in N}`. A trivial germ means `ŵ(p)` is trivial on some `y^k X^*`, and
    `ŵ(p)(y^k v) = y^k ŵ(p)(v)`.
  - It is onto. Let `g` fix `y^ω`, and let `w` be a word for `g`. For large `k`, every letter of `w` receives a
    long prefix of its input ray and has reached its eventual state. So `w|_{y^k} = ŵ(Φ_{y^ω}(w))`. Also
    `g(y^k v) = y^k g|_{y^k}(v)`, so the germs agree. `Φ_{y^ω}(w)` is closed at `y`, since `g y^ω = y^ω`.

So `G_(y^ω) ≅ π_1(Π,y)/K_y`. The union `K` of the `K_y` is a normal subgroupoid, and `𝒢 = Π/K` is the germ
groupoid of `G` on the rays `y^ω`. It has finitely many objects and arrows. So `𝒢` is finitely presented iff
each of its vertex groups `G_(y^ω)` is.

## 1. Necessity

Let `V_d(G)` be finitely presented. By Theorem A, `N = ⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift` with `R` finite. Put
`R_C = {Φ_{y^ω}(r') : y in 𝒪, r' = red(r|_c), r in R, c in X^*}`. It is finite by (Φ4). Its elements are
closed paths in `K`: `r'` is a relator, and by (Φ5) the germ of `ŵ(Φ_{y^ω}(r'))` is the germ of `r'`, which is
trivial. Let `D ⊆ K` be the normal subgroupoid generated by `R_C`.

**Claim.** `Φ_ξ(w) in D` for every `w in N` and every `ξ in Ω`. By induction along the stages `M_α`:
- Relators act trivially. So `Φ_ξ(uv) = Φ_ξ(u) Φ_ξ(v)` for `u, v in N`.
- By (Φ1), `Φ_ξ(g u g^{-1}) = Φ_ξ(g^{-1})^{-1} Φ_{g^{-1}ξ}(u) Φ_ξ(g^{-1})`. So products and conjugates stay in `D`
  once their factors do.
- `M_0`: for `r in R` and `ξ = c y^ω`, `Φ_ξ(r) = Φ_{y^ω}(r|_c) in R_C`, by (Φ4).
- Successor: for `u in L(M_α)` and `ξ = zξ'`, `Φ_ξ(u) = Φ_{ξ'}(u|_z)` by (Φ3), with `u|_z in M_α`.
- Limits: unions.

For `p in K_y`, `p = Φ_{y^ω}(ŵ(p)) in D` by (Φ5). So `K = D`, which is finitely normally generated, and every
`G_(y^ω)` is finitely presented. ∎(1)

## 2. The criterion under (P1)

Regroup at `r = lcm` of the cycle lengths. By `lift-presentability-is-invariant-under-level-regrouping`,
`V_d(G)` is fp iff `V_{d^r}(G)` is. At level `r`:
- Every letter is stable, since section sequences along `y` descend through the acyclic component order and
  stop at a loop.
- The arrows are the `[s, ℓ(s)]` for loop states `s`, since under (P1) `s|_y = s ≠ 1` forces `y = ℓ(s)`.
- A reduced word in which every letter is a loop state receiving its loop letter is exactly `ŵ(p)` for a path
  `p`. For loops, "cycling" in `polynomial-automata-reduce-to-cycling-relators` means "special", so the
  cycling relators are `ŵ(K)`.

`⇒` is (1) with `𝒪` = all letters. `⇐`: choose a finite `R_C ⊆ K` normally generating `K` (vertex-group
presentations plus a maximal tree). For every cycling relator `ŵ(p)` we have `p in ⟨⟨R_C⟩⟩_Π`, so
`ŵ(p) in ⟨⟨ŵ(R_C)⟩⟩_F`, since `ŵ` is a groupoid morphism. By `polynomial-automata-reduce-to-cycling-relators`,
`N = ⟨⟨ŵ(R_C)⟩⟩_lift`, and `V_{d^r}(G)` is finitely presented by Theorem A. ∎(2)

## 3. Descent

Let `w = ŵ(p)` for `p` closed at the loop letter `y`, and let `z ≠ y`.
- The rightmost letter `e_n` receives `z ≠ ℓ(e_n)`.
- It outputs `e_n(z) ≠ e_n(ℓ(e_n)) = ℓ(e_{n-1})`.
- Inductively every letter receives a letter other than its loop letter.

So `w|_z` is a product of sections `s|_{z'}` with `z' ≠ ℓ(s)`, which lie in `B`. Such a section is outside the
component `{s}`, by (P1). Any cycle chain from it extends by the loop of `s`, so its degree is `<= m - 1`.

`w` fixes `y` and `w = w|_y`, so `w ↦ (w|_{X^r∖y}; (w|_z)_{z≠y})` is an injective homomorphism into
`B ≀ Sym(X^r∖{y})`: if the image is trivial, then `w(y^k z v) = y^k z v` for all `k, z, v`. ∎(3)
