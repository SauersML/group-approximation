Write `X = Prim(A)`.  First consider a Hausdorff open subset `U` of `X`, and
let `B` be the corresponding ideal of `A`, so `Prim(B) = U`.  The ideal `B`
has real rank zero.  Its ideals therefore have approximate units of
projections.  If a point lies in an open subset of `Prim(B)`, choose a
projection in the corresponding ideal but outside that primitive ideal.  The
support of this projection is a compact open neighborhood contained in the
given open set: compactness follows by identifying that support with the
primitive spectrum of the ideal generated by the projection, which is Morita
equivalent to the unital corner cut out by the projection.  Thus `U` has a
basis of compact open sets and has covering dimension zero.

Since `A` is separable, `U` is second countable.  Being Hausdorff and locally
quasi-compact as a primitive ideal space, it is locally compact and metrizable.
The Dauns--Hofmann structure makes `B` a separable `C_0(U)`-algebra.  Its fibre
at a point of `U` is the quotient by the corresponding primitive ideal of
`B`.  Points of the Hausdorff space `Prim(B)` are closed, so these fibres are
simple.  They are nonzero simple ideal-quotients of `A`, hence are `Z`-stable
by hypothesis.  Hirshberg--Rordam--Winter, [Theorem
4.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610344), applies to the zero-dimensional
locally compact metrizable base `U` and shows that `B` is `Z`-stable.

Now use local Hausdorffness.  The second-countable space `X` is Lindelof, so
choose a countable cover `(U_n)` by Hausdorff open subsets and let `B_n` be the
corresponding ideals.  Each `B_n` is `Z`-stable by the preceding paragraph.
Set `J_n = B_1 + ... + B_n`.  Inductively, `J_n` is `Z`-stable: there is an
exact sequence

`0 -> J_(n-1) -> J_n -> B_n/(B_n intersect J_(n-1)) -> 0`,

the quotient is `Z`-stable because absorption passes to quotients, and
absorption passes from ideal and quotient to a separable extension by
Toms--Winter, [Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0502211).  The cover of
`X` says that the increasing union of the `J_n` is dense in `A`.  Hence `A` is
their sequential inductive limit and is `Z`-stable by Toms--Winter, Corollary
3.4.  Thiel, [Theorem 5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08404), then gives
generator rank one; under the identification `(x,y) -> x+iy` from
`A_sa x A_sa` to `A`, its dense `G_delta` of generating pairs is a generic set
of single generators.

For the consequence, nowhere scatteredness excludes nonzero elementary
ideal-quotients by Thiel--Vilalta, [Theorem
3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09877).  Every simple ideal-quotient is
separable and has finite nuclear dimension, since nuclear dimension passes to
ideals and quotients.  Tikuisis, [Corollary
8.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2237), therefore makes every such simple
quotient `Z`-stable.  The first part applies.

For the filtered version, each successor quotient has real rank zero, is
separable, and has only `Z`-stable simple ideal-quotients.  Applying the local
Hausdorff argument to that quotient makes every successor layer `Z`-stable.
The arbitrary-ordinal successor-and-limit induction in
`stw96-countable-zstable-filtrations-have-generators` then makes `A`
`Z`-stable and gives generator rank one.
