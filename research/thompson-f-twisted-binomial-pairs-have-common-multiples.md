**ESTABLISHED.** Let `K` be a field and `R = K[F]`. `R` is a domain (`F` is left-orderable).

1. **Twisted pairs.** For all `a, c` in `F \ {1}` and all `lambda, mu` in `K^x`,
   `(1 + lambda a) R ∩ (1 + mu c) R ≠ 0` and `R (1 + lambda a) ∩ R (1 + mu c) ≠ 0`.
2. **Binomial sides.** For every `g` in `F \ {1}`, every `lambda` in `K^x` and every nonzero
   `b` in `R` with `|supp b| <= 2`, `(1 + lambda g) R ∩ b R ≠ 0`. Equivalently, any two nonzero
   elements of `R` with at most two terms each have a nonzero common right multiple.
3. **Comaximal pairs.** If `x, y` in `R` are nonzero and `R x + R y = R`, then
   `R x ∩ R y ≠ 0`. The same holds for right ideals.
4. **Endpoint germs remove the twist.** Let `e_0(f)` and `e_1(f)` be the base-2 logarithms of
   the slopes of `f` at `0` and at `1`. Let `g` in `F` have `(e_0(g), e_1(g)) ≠ (0, 0)`, put
   `d = gcd(e_0(g), e_1(g))`, fix `lambda` in `K^x`, and let `L = K(gamma)` with
   `gamma^d = -1/lambda`. If `(1 - g) L[F] ∩ c L[F] ≠ 0` for every nonzero `c` in `L[F]`,
   then `(1 + lambda g) R ∩ b R ≠ 0` for every nonzero `b` in `R`.

**Consequences.**
- Item 2 adds to the established cases of
  `thompson-f-every-twisted-binomial-is-ore-with-every-element`: every `g ≠ 1`, with any number
  of bumps and any `lambda`, against binomials `b`, including `b` whose terms move bump
  endpoints of `g` and `g` with non-dyadic bump endpoints. It extends item 3 of
  `thompson-f-binomial-pairs-have-common-multiples` from `lambda = -1` to every `lambda`.
- So a non-Ore pair in `K[F]`, which would prove `thompson-f-is-not-amenable`, has at least
  three terms on one of its sides.
- Item 4: for `g` with a nonzero germ exponent at `0` or `1`, the twisted statement for `g`
  over every field is equivalent to its case `lambda = -1` over every field. Only `g` that are
  trivial near both `0` and `1` can make the twist matter. By Cannon--Floyd--Parry (§4, quoted,
  not re-fetched) these are exactly the elements of `F'`.

**Scope.**
- Item 1 splits in two. When `a -> -lambda`, `c -> -mu` extends to a character of `<a, c>`,
  it is the twist of item 1 of `thompson-f-binomial-pairs-have-common-multiples`, which uses
  Brin--Squier. Otherwise the right ideals `(1 + lambda a) R` and `(1 + mu c) R` are
  comaximal, and item 3 applies. The left version follows through `h -> h^-1`.
- Item 3 holds in every domain, for all nonzero comaximal right or left ideals, not only
  principal ones. That is standard, and novelty is not claimed for it or for item 1. Novelty
  was not checked beyond this graph.
- For three elements, item 3 gives `x_1 R ∩ x_2 R ∩ x_3 R ≠ 0` whenever
  `(x_1 R ∩ x_2 R) + x_3 R = R`. That hypothesis is not implied by pairwise comaximality.
- Three or more conjugates `1 + lambda t^-1 g t` remain open for multi-bump `g`. Comaximality
  of the right ideal they generate does not give a common multiple of three.
