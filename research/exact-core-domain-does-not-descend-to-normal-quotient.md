Let

\[
\Gamma=F(a,x),\qquad \overline\Gamma=\Gamma/\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle .
\]

There is a ucp map \(\Theta:C^*(\Gamma)\to\mathbb C\) which is exactly the
trivial representation on the core \(\langle a\rangle\), so \(u_a\) lies
in its multiplicative domain, but whose **minimal** Stinespring
representation does not factor through \(\overline\Gamma\).

Take

\[
\pi(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\pi(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V1=e_1,
\]

and put \(\Theta(y)=V^*\pi(y)V\).  For every \(k\in\mathbb Z\),

\[
\pi(a)^kV=V,\qquad \Theta(u_{a^k})=1.                  \tag{CMD1}
\]

Thus \(\Theta|_{C^*(\langle a\rangle)}\) is a character and the whole core
algebra is in the multiplicative domain.  On the other hand,

\[
\pi(xax^{-1})V=-V.                                     \tag{CMD2}
\]

The cyclic span of \(V\mathbb C\) under \(\pi(\Gamma)\) is all of
\(\mathbb C^2\), because \(\pi(x)e_1=e_2\).  Hence this Stinespring
representation is minimal, and (CMD2) proves that it does not annihilate
the normal closure of \(a\).

The general distinction is exact.  If
\(J=\langle\!\langle z\rangle\!\rangle\) is the closed ideal generated by
a relation \(z-1\), then

\[
\Theta(z)=1
\]

puts \(z\) in the multiplicative domain and gives
\(\pi(z)V=V\).  Descent of the minimal Stinespring representation through
\(C^*(\Gamma)/J\), however, requires

\[
(\pi(z)-1)\pi(w)V=0                                    \tag{CMD3}
\]

for every word \(w\) in a cyclic spanning window.  The \(w=x^{-1}\) row is
exactly what fails in (CMD2).  Core exactness controls the empty row of
this normal-closure table, not the translated rows.

The same obstruction occurs canonically in the actual Iwahori group, with
no free-group surrogate.  Put

\[
A=PSL_2(\mathbb Z[1/2]),\qquad B=\langle r,t\rangle=BS(1,4),
\]

and let \(\beta:B\to U(d)\) satisfy \(\beta(t)^N=1\).  In the induced
representation

\[
\rho=\operatorname{Ind}_B^A(\beta)
\]

let \(V:\mathbb C^d\to H_\rho\) be the canonical copy supported on the
identity coset.  Then

\[
\rho(b)V=V\beta(b)\quad(b\in B),\qquad
(\rho(t)^N-1)V=0.                                      \tag{CMD4}
\]

Nevertheless the translated row is maximally bad.  In the standard matrix
model, \(B\) is upper triangular, whereas

\[
xt^Nx^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-N&1\end{pmatrix}\notin B.         \tag{CMD5}
\]

Consequently \(\rho(xt^Nx^{-1})V\mathbb C^d\) and
\(V\mathbb C^d\) are supported on distinct cosets and are orthogonal.  Hence

\[
{1\over\sqrt d}
 \|(\rho(xt^Nx^{-1})-1)V\|_F=\sqrt2.                   \tag{CMD6}
\]

Thus the canonical relative induced dilation is exact on every core word
but misses the first extender-translated normal row by the largest generic
Hilbert-space amount.  This does not exclude a different positive window;
it proves that induction or a core expectation alone cannot be that window.

This fences the natural operator-algebraic shortcuts in the authenticated
Iwahori problem.  There \(z=t^N\) and the finite quotient is

\[
A_N=PSL_2(\mathbb Z[1/2])/\langle\!\langle t^N\rangle\!\rangle .
\]

An LLP or local-reflexivity lift which is exact on the finite-dimensional
core may satisfy \(\pi(t)^NV=V\) while failing (CMD3) on vectors generated
by the extending involution.  A conditional expectation onto the
finite-dimensional core algebra does not add those rows: such expectations
already exist in every matrix algebra, including the two-by-two example
above.  To obtain a finite-dimensional Stinespring corner one must prove
descent to \(C^*(A_N)\), equivalently produce the positive-definite matrix
window in
'finite-positive-definite-window-is-a-relative-corner-selector'.

Quasidiagonality of the full canonical trace does not supply this descent
for a prescribed microstate either.  Residual finiteness of
\(SL_2(\mathbb Z[1/2])\) already gives existential exact finite-quotient
models of that scalar trace.  The required window is operator-valued and
must approximate the supplied matrices, not merely their limiting scalar
character.  Calling this extra demand "relative quasidiagonality" or
"finite-dimensionality on the core-generated operator system" simply
restates that the prescribed ucp map is in the point-HS closure of
finite-dimensional representation corners.

Finally, the nonnegative restriction-ring fiber is downstream of (CMD3).
It lists the multiplicities of representations which already factor through
\(A_N\); it has no entry for an infinite Stinespring sector on which the
normal-closure ideal acts.  Finite-level restriction separation therefore
cannot select a lift before quotient descent.

For the authenticated \(SL_2(\mathbb Z[1/2])\) core, no available theorem
controls all translated rows (CMD3) or the equivalent finite positive
kernel.  The finite-corner lift and the root problem remain open.
