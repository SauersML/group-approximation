**ESTABLISHED (classical argument; no priority claimed; not independently
reviewed).**

**Statement.** Let `Γ` act properly by isometries on a CAT(0) space `X`, and
suppose every element of `Γ` acts as a semisimple isometry. Then no finitely
generated subgroup of `Γ` contains a distorted infinite cyclic subgroup. Hence
`Γ` contains none of the following, for any `n >= 2`: `BS(1,2)`, `GL_n(Q)`,
`SL_n(Q)`, `PSL_n(Q)`, `Aff(Q)`, `U_3(Q)`.

This applies in particular to:
- (a) every group acting properly by cellular isometries on a CAT(0)
  `M_κ`-polyhedral complex with finitely many shapes of cells. Bridson,
  *On the semisimplicity of polyhedral isometries*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 127
  (1999), 2143--2146, proves that such cellular isometries are semisimple.
  arXiv:1209.5804 (HTML) states it as: "Bridson proved in [B] that if X is an
  M_κ polyhedral complex where the number of isometry types of polygons
  (shapes) is finite, all isometries are semi-simple." Bridson's theorem is
  stated for cellular isometries, which is the case used here.
- (b) every group acting properly on a CAT(0) cube complex of any dimension.
  Haglund, arXiv:0705.3386, abstract: "We show that an automorphism of an
  arbitrary CAT(0) cube complex either has a fixed point or preserves some
  combinatorial axis. It follows that when a group contains a distorted cyclic
  subgroup, it admits no proper action on a discrete space with walls."

## Proof

Let `H <= Γ` be generated by a finite set `S`. Let `a in H` have infinite order
with `liminf_k |a^k|_S / k = 0`. By hypothesis `a` is semisimple.

- If `a` is elliptic, it fixes a point `x`. The action is proper, so `Stab_Γ(x)`
  is finite. Then `a` has finite order, a contradiction.
- If `a` is hyperbolic, it has an axis (Bridson--Haefliger, Theorem II.6.8(1)):
  a geodesic line `c` with `a·c(t) = c(t + l)`, where `l > 0`. Put `x_0 = c(0)`
  and `λ = max_{s in S} d(x_0, s x_0)`. The triangle inequality along a word for
  `a^k` gives `k l = d(x_0, a^k x_0) <= λ |a^k|_S`. So `|a^k|_S >= k l / λ`,
  which contradicts distortion.

So there is no such `a`.

**The groups listed are excluded.** In each case exhibit a finitely generated
subgroup with a distorted infinite cyclic subgroup.
- `BS(1,2) = <t, a | t a t^-1 = a^2>` realised as `a = [[1,1],[0,1]]`,
  `t = diag(2,1)` in `GL_2(Q)`. This is `Z[1/2] ⋊ <2>`, and `a^(2^k) = t^k a t^-k`
  has word length at most `2k+1`. It is contained in `Aff(Q)` and in
  `GL_n(Q) >= GL_2(Q)`.
- `SL_2(Q)` contains `<diag(2,1/2), [[1,1],[0,1]]>`, which is `BS(1,4)`, with
  `a^(4^k)` of length at most `2k+1`. It embeds in `SL_n(Q)` for `n >= 2`, and in
  `PSL_n(Q)`, because it is torsion-free and so meets the finite centre
  trivially.
- `U_3(Q)` contains the integral Heisenberg group, where `[x^k, y^k] = z^(k^2)`,
  so `z^(k^2)` has length at most `4k`.

## Consequence for the root

Any finitely presented simple group containing `GL_n(Q)`, `n >= 2`, has no
proper action by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space. So it acts properly on
no product of locally finite trees, no locally finite building of finite type,
and no CAT(0) cube complex. The survey (arXiv:2306.16356v3, §4.1) names only two
known families of infinite finitely presented simple groups outside the Thompson
world: the Burger--Mozes groups, which act properly and cocompactly on products
of two locally finite trees, and the Kac--Moody groups used by Caprace--Rémy.
The latter are discrete in the automorphism group of the product of the two
halves of their twin building over a finite field, so they act properly by
cellular isometries on the product of the Davis realizations, which has finitely
many shapes. These two applications are standard facts, not re-checked at the
source here. With them, both families are excluded as hosts: the host must be
of Thompson type or of a kind not yet constructed.

The exclusion already applies to the finitely generated subgroup `BS(1,2)`, so it
does not use divisibility. It is a calibration record, not a new obstruction to
the finitely generated subgroups, which do embed in finitely presented simple
groups (Zaremsky, arXiv:2405.09722, Theorem 1.2).
