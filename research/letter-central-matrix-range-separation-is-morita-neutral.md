**ESTABLISHED MORITA-NEUTRALITY.**  Let `A` be generated by a fixed unitary
tuple `u=(u_s)_(s in S)` and write

```text
MR_d(A,u)={(Phi(u_s))_s:Phi:A->M_d is ucp}.
```

Let

```text
T=direct-sum_(j=1)^r zeta_j I_(d_j),
```

where the `zeta_j` are distinct, and suppose `X_s,B_s in T'`.  Write
`X_s^(j),B_s^(j)` for their `d_j`-dimensional blocks and
`alpha_j=d_j/d`.  Then the matrix-range support function splits exactly:

```text
h_d(B)=sum_j alpha_j h_(d_j)(B^(j)).                     (MRS1)
```

Consequently the separation gap is

```text
Delta_d(B;X)
 =Re sum_s tr_d(B_s^*X_s)-h_d(B)
 =sum_j alpha_j Delta_(d_j)(B^(j);X^(j)).                (MRS2)
```

In particular a positive central gap is only a weighted collection of
ordinary smaller matrix-range gaps; at least one multiplicity block already
has a positive gap.  The Choi cones and Stinespring dilations on the blocks
are independent.  The letter supplies no coupling that could turn the
separator into a common lattice actor.

The converse is equally exact.  Given any `m`-dimensional tuple and
separator `(X,B)`, let `T_k` have the `k` distinct `k`th roots of unity, each
with multiplicity `m`, and put

```text
X~_s=I_k tensor X_s,       B~_s=I_k tensor B_s.          (MRS3)
```

Then `X~,B~` commute with `T_k`, their normalized operator and
Hilbert--Schmidt bounds are unchanged, and

```text
Delta_(km)(B~;X~)=Delta_m(B;X).                          (MRS4)
```

Moreover `tr(T_k^ell)=0` for every fixed nonzero `ell` once `k>|ell|`, so
the central letter is asymptotically Haar.  If a coordinate of `B` is
nonscalar, its distance from `W^*(T_k)` is exactly the centered
Hilbert--Schmidt norm of that coordinate and does not decay.  Thus neither
Haar moments nor the off-spectral multiplicity condition changes the
matrix-range problem.

This does not refute the large-prime central-dual claim: the amplification
in `(MRS3)` need not extend to the prescribed `SL_3(Z[1/p])` HNN
microstate.  It proves that any successful argument must use that extension
through an arithmetic relation involving an element outside `Lambda`—for
example the relative position of `T` and `phi(h)Tphi(h)^*`—rather than only
the spectral multiplicity, Choi cone, or Stinespring dilation of `T'`.
