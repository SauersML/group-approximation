## 1. Presentation

`Delta_(1,2) = C1,2 *_(B0,1) C1',2` with `B0,1 = <u_1,u_t,v'_t>`.  By Nagao over
`F_2`, `C1,2 = S_3 *_(Z/2) V_2 = <a,b,c,c' | a^2,b^2,(ab)^3,c^2,c'^2,[a,c],[a,c'],[c,c']>`.
Its `theta`-image is `C1',2 = S_3' *_(Z/2') theta(V_2)` with `S_3' = <w',v'_t>`,
`w' = theta(w) = [[0,1/t],[t,0]]`, and `theta(V_2) = <v'_t, v'_(t^2), v'_(t^3)>`.
The generators of `B0,1` sit in the two factors as

```text
in C1,2 :  u_1 = a,  u_t = c,  v'_t = b c b = y,
in C1',2:  u_1 = w' v'_(t^2) w',  u_t = w' v'_(t^3) w',  v'_t native,
```

since `theta(v'_(tq)) = u_q` and `theta(w u_(tq) w) = w' v'_(t^2 q) w'`.  Pushing
out eliminates `v'_t = y`, `v'_(t^2) = w' a w'` and `v'_(t^3) = w' c w'`.  The
remaining relators of `C1',2` are `w'^2`, `(w'y)^3`, and pairwise commutation
of `y, w'aw', w'cw'`; the last pair commutes automatically.  So

```text
Delta_(1,2) = < C1,2, w' | w'^2, (w'y)^3, [y, w'aw'], [y, w'cw'] >.
```

Put `f = w' y w'`.  With `(w'y)^3 = 1` one has `f = y w' y`, so `w' = y f y`,
`f^2 = 1` and `(yf)^3 = (w'y)^3`.  Conjugating by `w'` turns `[y, w'aw']` into
`[f,a]` and `[y,w'cw']` into `[f,c]`.  This gives item 1.  In
`SL_2(F_2[t,1/t])`, `f = w' v'_t w' = u_(1/t)`.

## 2. The kernel

The assignment `a -> (12)`, `b -> (23)`, `c,c',f -> 1` kills every relator
(`(bcbf)^3 -> (bb)^3`), and it is injective on `<a,b> = S_3`.  So
`Delta = W ⋊ <a,b>` with `W` the normal closure of `c, c', f`.

Reidemeister--Schreier for a normal closure with a complement `Q = <a,b>`:
`W` is generated by `x_g = g x g^(-1)` (`x in {c,c',f}`, `g in Q`), with
relators the `Q`-conjugates of the relators involving `x`, rewritten.

- `[a,x] = 1` for `x in {c,c',f}` gives `x_(ga) = x_g`.  So the index is the
  coset `g<a>`, identified with the point `g(3)`, since `a = (12)` fixes `3`.
- `x^2`, `[c,c']` and `[f,c]` give involutions, `[c_p,c'_p] = 1` and
  `[c_p,f_p] = 1`.
- `(bcbf)^3` conjugated by `g` is `(c_(gb) f_g)^3 = (c_(g(2)) f_(g(3)))^3`.  As
  `g` ranges over `S_3`, `(g(2),g(3))` runs over all ordered pairs of distinct
  points.

No other relator involves `c, c', f`.  This is exactly the Coxeter
presentation in item 2.  The restriction of the map to `C1,2 = <a,b,c,c'>`
is onto `S_3`, with kernel the normal closure of `c, c'` in `C1,2`.  That
kernel is generated by the `S_3`-conjugates `c_p, c'_p`, which is `W_T`.  So
`C1,2 = W_T ⋊ S_3`.

## 3. Separability

Let `V` have basis `e_s` (`s` in the nine generators), with
`B(e_s,e_t) = -cos(pi/m(s,t))` and `B = -1` when `m = infinity`.  The geometric
representation `rho(s) v = v - 2B(e_s,v) e_s` has matrix entries
`2B in {2, 0, -1, -2}`, so it is integral, and so is its contragredient
`rho*` on `V*`.  Let `phi_T in V*` have `phi_T(e_s) = 0` for `s in T` and `1`
otherwise.  It lies in the closed fundamental chamber.  By Tits' theorem, the
stabilizer in `W` of a point of the closed chamber is the standard parabolic
subgroup generated by the simple reflections fixing it (Humphreys,
*Reflection Groups and Coxeter Groups*, §5.13).  So `Stab_W(phi_T) = W_T`.

If `k in W \ W_T`, then `rho*(k) phi_T - phi_T` has a nonzero integer entry
`r`.  Choose a prime `p` not dividing `r`, and let `N` be the kernel of
`W -> GL(Z/p)`.  Every element of `kN` moves `phi_T` modulo `p`, so
`k notin W_T N`.  Thus `W_T` is separable in `W`.

For `g = k x notin C1,2` (`k in W`, `x in S_3`) we have `k notin W_T`.  Take
`N` as above, and replace it by the intersection of its `S_3`-conjugates,
which has finite index and is normal in `Delta`.  The decomposition
`Delta = W S_3` is unique and `N <= W`, so `W ∩ W_T N S_3 = W_T N`, and we get
`g notin C1,2 N`.  Hence `C1,2` is closed in `Delta_(1,2)`.
