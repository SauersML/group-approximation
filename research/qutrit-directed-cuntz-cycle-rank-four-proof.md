## The constant four-cycle group

Write the active coordinates in the order `(i,j,k,ell)` and let

```text
P:i |-> j |-> ell |-> k |-> i.
```

The orbit of `B=x_(j,ell)(1)` under `P` consists of the four forward
cycle roots

```text
x_(i,j)(1), x_(j,ell)(1), x_(ell,k)(1), x_(k,i)(1).
```

The orbit of `A=x_(i,k)(1)` consists of the four roots in the reverse
orientation

```text
x_(j,i)(1), x_(ell,j)(1), x_(k,ell)(1), x_(i,k)(1).
```

Thus `<A,B,P>` contains both directed elementary transvections on every
edge of a connected four-cycle. If `a,b,c` are distinct, the
commutator formula

```text
[x_(ab)(1),x_(bc)(1)]=x_(ac)(1)
```

adds a chord. Induction on path length gives every `x_(ab)(1)` with
`a!=b`. These transvections generate `EL_4(F_2)`, and
`EL_4(F_2)=GL_4(F_2)` because `F_2^x={1}`. This proves `(QCY1)`.

## The concrete subgroup is full EL4

The acyclic cell `M` contains

```text
x_(ij)(s_0), x_(ij)(s_1),
x_(jk)(t_0), x_(jk)(t_1).
```

By `(QCY1)`, scalar permutation matrices in `<A,B,P>` move each of
these four coefficient roots to every ordered root position. Root
additivity gives coefficient sums and a third root index gives
coefficient products. Since `s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1` generate the binary
Leavitt algebra, `<M,P>` contains every elementary root
`x_(ab)(r)` with `r in L`. Conversely all of its generators are
elementary. Hence

```text
<M,P>=EL_4(L).                                           (QCY2)
```

The established binary-Leavitt theorem makes this group nonsofic. A
residually finite group is sofic, so it is not residually finite either.
Nothing in this calculation decides hyperlinearity.

## The first cross-cycle word

The permutation sends the negative root to

```text
P V_s P^(-1)=x_(ell,i)(t_s).
```

Since `U_r=x_(ij)(s_r)`, the three-index relation and
`t_ss_r=delta_(sr)` give

```text
[P V_s P^(-1),U_r]
 =x_(ell,j)(t_ss_r)
 =x_(ell,j)(delta_(sr)).                                (QCY5)
```

Also

```text
P^2 A P^(-2)=x_(ell,j)(1)=D.
```

Together these are exactly `(QCY3)`. It is a genuine kernel word for
the finite-vertex amalgam shadow: one letter comes from the scalar
vertex and the other from the acyclic coefficient cell.

## The isolated cycle row is finite

Put

```text
X(t)=x_(ell,i)(t),        t in T=span{t_0,t_1},
Y(s)=x_(i,j)(s),          s in S=span{s_0,s_1}.
```

Their only nontrivial commutators are

```text
[X(t),Y(s)]=x_(ell,j)(ts).
```

The pairing `T times S -> F_2` is the standard perfect pairing

```text
(t_0,t_1)(s_0,s_1)^transpose=I_2.
```

Therefore the group generated by `X(t_0),X(t_1),Y(s_0),Y(s_1)` has
the unique class-two normal form

```text
X(t)Y(s)D^epsilon,
t in T, s in S, epsilon in F_2.                         (QCY6)
```

The three root entries recover `t,s,epsilon`, so

```text
|Q|=2^2 2^2 2=32.                                      (QCY7)
```

This finite group realizes all four delta rows `(QCY5)` exactly.

## The finite row plus the actor is already full

The scalar return `D` lies in `Q`. Its orbit under `P` is

```text
x_(ell,j)(1), x_(k,ell)(1), x_(i,k)(1), x_(j,i)(1).
```

These are the four transvections around the reverse directed coordinate
cycle. Commutators along paths give every constant root, exactly as in
the proof of `(QCY1)`. Hence `<P,D>=GL_4(F_2)`.

Moreover `Q` contains `Y(s_r)=x_(i,j)(s_r)` and
`X(t_s)=x_(ell,i)(t_s)`. Conjugating by the scalar group just obtained
places both coefficient families in every ordered root position.
Additivity and three-index commutators then generate every coefficient
in `L`. Consequently

```text
<P,Q>=EL_4(L).                                           (QCY4)
```

Thus the full nonsofic group is generated by an order-four element and
the explicit order-32 packet. The abstract free product `C_4*Q` is
virtually free and residually finite; the concrete matrix relations
between distinct `P`-translates of `Q` form the nonsofic quotient kernel.
This sharpens the location of the obstruction without proving a
normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt gap for that kernel.

## Qutrit trace audit

The roots `X(t_s)` and `Y(s_r)` join a nontrivial and a trivial
`C`-type in the `h`-fixed block. Neither normalizes `C`, and each
centralizes `h`. Hence

```text
C intersect vCv^(-1)=<h>,                v=X(t_s),Y(s_r).
```

Their joint-character incidence is

```text
2alpha'+beta'=2alpha+beta,
```

and every compatible block has squared canonical norm `1/27`. The
return `D=x_(ell,j)(1)` acts between corresponding coordinates in two
copies of the same `C`-type, so it commutes with `C` and is diagonal on
all nine atoms.

One can package this with the finite scalar head explicitly. Let
`K_0=GL_4(F_2)` on the active block, enlarge it by the finite qutrit
group `C`, and let `H=<h> times M`. Their common subgroup contains
`<h,A,B>`, and the corresponding finite-vertex amalgam has regular
microstates. The subgroup `Q` above gives an even smaller exact model
for every relation in `(QCY3)`. In both models the restriction to `C`
has the uniform weights `1/9`. Thus neither the delta selector nor its
scalar return yields a Hall inequality.

The passage from the finite shadow to `EL_4(L)` occurs already after the
complete covariance closure of `Q` by the single actor `P`. Proving nonhyperlinearity would
require a bounded family of those covariance words with a quantitative
effect on one common qutrit carrier. The isolated cross-cycle relation
has no such effect.
