## Step 0. Four standard facts about CE characters

A character here is a normalized central positive-definite function. CE is as in the claim: approximate, not genuine,
matricial microstates. Let `tau`, `sigma` be characters of a countable group `Gamma`.

**(F1) GNS embedding.**
* Let `U^(m)` be microstates for finite sets `F_m` increasing to `Gamma` with `eps_m -> 0`. They define a
  homomorphism `Gamma -> U(prod^omega M_(d_m))` whose trace is `tau`.
* The von Neumann algebra it generates is trace-isomorphic to the GNS algebra `(M_tau, tau)`. It sits inside
  `prod^omega M_(d_m)`, which embeds in `R^omega`.
* So `M_tau` is Connes-embeddable.

**(F2) Products and conjugates.**
* If `U`, `W` are `(F,eps)`-microstates for `tau`, `sigma`, then `U_g ⊗ W_g` has multiplicative defect at most
  `2 eps` in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, and trace `tr U_g · tr W_g`. So `tau·sigma` is CE.
* Entrywise conjugates give `conj(tau)`, so `|tau|^2` is CE.

**(F3) Limits.** If `tau_k` is CE and `tau_k -> tau` pointwise, then for finite `F` and `eps`, choose `k` with
`|tau_k-tau|<eps/2` on `F`. An `(F,eps/2)`-microstate of `tau_k` is an `(F,eps)`-microstate of `tau`.

**(F4) Regular characters of quotients.** For `N` normal in `Gamma`, `1_N` is CE iff `Gamma/N` is hyperlinear.
* **(If.)** Compose microstates of `Gamma/N` (with `tr ≈ delta_e`) with the quotient map.
* **(Only if.)** For `g in N ∩ F`, `||U_g-1||_2^2 = 2-2 Re tr U_g < 2 eps`. So `U` is constant up to `O(sqrt eps)`
  on `N`-cosets meeting `F`. Choosing one representative per coset gives microstates of `Gamma/N` with trace close
  to `delta_e`.

## Step 1. Theorem TG

**Corestriction.** By Step 1 of `leavitt-corner-bcs-support-coefficient-gate-proof`, `Phi` corestricts to a unital
star homomorphism `A(B_loop) -> P_z C[Gamma_X] P_z`.

**Setup.** Let `tau` be a CE character of `Gamma_X` with `tau(z)!=1`.
* Since `z^2=1`, `tau(z)` is real and `tau(z) in [-1,1)`. So `tau(P_z)=(1-tau(z))/2>0`.
* Extend `tau` linearly to `C[Gamma_X]`. Positive-definiteness means `tau(b^*b)>=0`.
* The GNS map `C[Gamma_X] -> M_tau` is a unital star homomorphism sending `P_z` to a projection `p` with
  `tau(p)>0`.

**Pull back.** Put `sigma = tau o Phi / tau(P_z)`. It is a tracial state on `A(B_loop)`, and its GNS algebra is the
von Neumann algebra generated by the image of `Phi` inside `p M_tau p`, with the normalized trace.

**Contradiction.**
* By (F1), `M_tau` is Connes-embeddable. Hence so are its corner `p M_tau p` and every tracial von Neumann subalgebra
  of that corner.
* These are exactly the two permanence facts used in `nonce-bcs-unital-algebraic-corner-map-forces-nonhyperlinear`.
  The only property of the canonical trace used there, beyond embeddability, is that it charges the corner
  projection, and here `tau(P_z)>0` does that.
* So `sigma` would be a Connes-embeddable tracial state on `A(B_loop)`. This contradicts Step 0 of
  `leavitt-corner-bcs-support-coefficient-gate-proof`.

Hence `tau(z)=1`.

The shadow gate is the special case `tau=1_N` from (F4), where `tau(z)=0` when `z notin N`. The case `tau(z)=-1` is
genuinely additional: it is a CE character in which `z` acts as the scalar `-1`.

## Step 2. The finitary kernel of E_n: inside, simple, perfect, self-centralizing

Let `n>=2`, `E=EL_n(J)`, `V=V_n`, and `F <= J` the ideal of finite-support matrices.

**Faithful action and matrix units.**
* `J` acts faithfully on `F_2^(N)` by `S e_m = e_(m+1)` and `T e_m = e_(m-1)` (with `T e_0 = 0`).
* Its matrix units are `e_ij = S^i Q T^j`.
* So `M_n(J)` acts faithfully on `V`, and `E <= GL(V)`.

**(S1) `L <= E`.**
* For distinct columns `a!=b`, the elementary matrix `x_ab(e_ij)` is the finitary transvection
  `1+e_((i,a),(j,b))`.
* For `(i,a)!=(j,a)`, pick `b!=a`. Then `[1+e_((i,a),(k,b)), 1+e_((k,b),(j,a))] = 1+e_((i,a),(j,a))`.
* Over `F_2`, the elementary transvections generate `GL_fin = SL_fin`.

**(S2) `L` is simple and perfect.** `L` is the increasing union of the groups `GL_m(F_2)=SL_m(F_2)`, `m>=3`, which
are simple and perfect. An increasing union of simple (respectively perfect) groups is simple (respectively perfect).
This is also recorded in `binary-jacobson-elementary-mark-is-finite-quotient-invisible` for `n=5`.

**(S3) `C_(GL(V))(L)=1`, so `C_E(L)=1`.** Let `g in GL(V)` commute with `L`, and let `0!=v in V`.
* Since `dim V>=2`, choose a finitely supported functional `phi!=0` with `phi(v)=0`. Put `t=1+v⊗phi in L`.
* Then `g t g^(-1) = 1 + gv ⊗ (phi o g^(-1)) = t`. Equality of rank-one operators gives `gv in F_2^× v`, so `gv=v`.
* Hence `g=1`.

**(S4) Finite rank forces membership in `L`.**
* Every `a in J` is `f+p` with `f in F` and `p` a finite `F_2`-combination of `S^i` (`i>=1`), `1`, and `T^j`
  (`j>=1`). The symbol of `a` is the corresponding Laurent polynomial.
* If `p!=0`, let `d` be its lowest exponent. For large `m`, `a e_m = p e_m` has lowest basis index `m+d`. So
  infinitely many images are linearly independent, and `a` has infinite rank.
* For `g in GL_n(J)`, suppose `g-1` has finite rank on `V`. Then every entry of `g-1` has finite rank on the
  corresponding summand, since a row-coordinate projection of a finite-rank operator has finite rank. So every entry
  lies in `F`, `g in 1+M_n(F)`, and `g in L`.

## Step 3. Theorem JH

**Setup.** Let `tau` be a CE character of `E`, and `K=K_tau`.
* By `character-diracization-limit`: in the GNS representation, `u_g` is the scalar `tau(g)1` exactly when `g in K`;
  `K` is normal; `lambda=tau|_K : K -> T` is a homomorphism; and `|tau|^(2k) -> 1_K` pointwise.
* By (F2)--(F3), `1_K` is CE. By (F4), `E/K` is hyperlinear.
* For `g in E` and `k in K`, `u_g u_k u_g^(-1) u_k^(-1) = 1`. So `lambda` is trivial on `[E,K]`.

**Case A: `K ∩ L = 1`.**
* Then `[K,L] <= K ∩ L = 1`, so `K <= C_E(L) = 1` by (S3).
* So `1_K = delta_e` is CE and `E` is hyperlinear by (F4).
* This is alternative (b) of `(JH1)`.

**Case B: `K ∩ L != 1`.**
* By (S2), `K ∩ L`, which is normal in `L`, equals `L`. So `L <= K`.
* By perfectness, `L=[L,L] <= [E,K]`, so `lambda` is trivial on `L`: `u_l=1` for `l in L`.
* Then `tau(gl) = <u_g u_l xi, xi> = tau(g)`, so `tau` factors through `E/L`.
* This is alternative (a).

**Exclusivity.** Case A gives `K=1`, and `L!=1`. So the two cases are exclusive.

**Equivalences.**
* (1) => (2): take the identity quotient.
* (2) => (3): the regular character `1_N` of a hyperlinear quotient `E/N` with `w notin N` is CE by (F4), and
  `1_N(w)=0`.
* (3) => (1): if `tau(w)!=1` with `w in L`, then `tau` is not trivial on `L`. Alternative (a) fails, so (b) holds
  and `E` is hyperlinear.
* **Addendum.** If `E` is not hyperlinear, alternative (a) holds for every CE `tau`.

**The quotient.** `E/L` is the Laurent elementary group. By the symbol exact sequence `(JFQ1)` of
`binary-jacobson-elementary-mark-is-finite-quotient-invisible` (stated there for `n=5`; the same symbol map works for
every `n>=2`), `E/L ≅ EL_n(F_2[x,x^(-1)])`. For the dichotomy itself only (S1)--(S3) are used.

## Step 4. The Haar fixed-point characters (JH2), (JH3)

**The action.**
* Let `Y=F_2^(N x {1..n})` with the product topology and Haar probability measure `mu`, paired with `V` by
  `<y,v> = sum_omega y_omega v_omega`. The sum is finite.
* `g in E` acts by `<gy,v> = <y,g^(-1)v>`. This is a continuous surjective automorphism of the compact group `Y`, so
  it preserves `mu` by uniqueness of Haar measure.
* `E` acts diagonally on `Y^k`.

**Character.** Put `phi_k(g) = mu^k({y in Y^k : gy = y})`.
* **Positive definite.** For `g_1..g_m in E` and `c in C^m`,
  `sum_ij c_i conj(c_j) phi_k(g_j^(-1)g_i) = ∫ sum_ij c_i conj(c_j) 1[g_i y = g_j y] dmu^k(y) >= 0`. The integrand is
  the Gram form of the vectors `delta_(g_i y)` in `l^2(Y^k)`.
* **Central.** `Fix(hgh^(-1)) = h Fix(g)` and `h` preserves `mu^k`.
* **Normalized.** `phi_k(e)=1`.
* **Multiplicative in k.** `phi_k = phi_1^k`, because the fixed set is a product.

**The value.**
* `y` is fixed by `g` iff `y` annihilates `W=(g^(-1)-1)V`, and `dim W = rank(g-1)`.
* If `r=rank(g-1)` is finite, `Ann(W)` is a closed subgroup of index `2^r`, so it has measure `2^(-r)`.
* If `W` is infinite-dimensional, `Ann(W)` lies in `Ann(W')` for subspaces `W' <= W` of every finite dimension, so it
  has measure `0`.
* By (S4), `rank(g-1)` is finite iff `g in L`. This proves `(JH2)`.

**(JH3).**
* `x_13(Q)-1 = Q e_13` has rank one, so `phi_k(x_13(Q))=2^(-k)`.
* `phi_k` is real with `0<=phi_k<=1`, and `phi_k(g)=1` iff `rank(g-1)=0`, i.e. `g=e`. So `K_(phi_k)=1`.
* If `phi_k` is CE, then alternative (a) of `(JH1)` fails (`phi_k(x_13(Q))<1`), so (b) holds and `E` is hyperlinear.
* Directly: `phi_(2km) = |phi_k|^(2m) -> delta_e` is CE by (F2)--(F3).

## Step 5. Corollary for the Toeplitz survivor class

**The Toeplitz copy.**
* In `R=L_(F_2)(1,2)`, `t_2 s_2 = 1` and `s_1 t_1 + s_2 t_2 = 1` with `s_1 t_1 != 0`, so `s_2 t_2 != 1`.
* By `prime-characteristic-defect-embeds-the-jacobson-algebra` (`p=2`), `S->s_2, T->t_2` is an injective unital ring
  map `J -> R` with image `T_2`, carrying `Q` to `1-s_2t_2 = q`.
* Entrywise, it gives an isomorphism `iota: EL_20(J) -> E_20(T_2) <= E_20(R)` with `iota(x_13(Q)) = pi(z)`.

**Kill direction.**
* Let `X` be an elementary Toeplitz support, so `pi(Gamma_X) <= E_20(T_2)`. Let `tau` be a CE character of
  `EL_20(J)` with `tau(x_13(Q))!=1`.
* Then `tau o iota^(-1) o pi|_(Gamma_X)` is a CE character of `Gamma_X`. Composing microstates with a homomorphism
  preserves CE, and restriction to a subgroup preserves it too.
* Its value at `z` is `!=1`, so Theorem TG forbids `LNC3` on `X`.
* By Theorem JH, such a `tau` exists iff `EL_20(J)` is hyperlinear.

**Construct direction.**
* If `LNC3` has elementary Toeplitz support and `EL_20(J)` were hyperlinear, then `pi(Gamma_X)` would be a hyperlinear
  quotient of `Gamma_X` retaining `z` (`pi(z)!=1`). This contradicts `leavitt-corner-bcs-support-coefficient-gate`.

**No weaker certificate.** This is the implication (3) => (1) of Theorem JH with `w=x_13(Q)`.

## Remark (not part of the established statement): non-elementary Toeplitz supports

If `pi(Gamma_X)` lies in `GL_20(T_2) ≅ GL_20(J)` but not in `E_20(T_2)`, the same corollary follows once one knows
that hyperlinearity of `EL_20(J)` passes to `GL_20(J)`.

**The index-quotient step.**
* The kernel of the symbol map on `GL_20(J)` is `L <= E`.
* By Step 3, `E` maps onto `EL_20(F_2[x,x^(-1)])`, which equals `SL_20` since `F_2[x,x^(-1)]` is Euclidean.
* So `E` is the preimage of `SL_20`, it is normal, and `GL_20(J)/E` embeds in `F_2[x,x^(-1)]^× / 1 ≅ Z`.

**Permanence.**
* Hyperlinear-by-amenable permanence then gives the transfer. That theorem is quoted inline in
  `compressor-subgroup-directed-union-proof`, but no node certifies it.
* Alternatively, a Toeplitz index computation (`∂[x] = -[Q] != 0` in `K_0(F)`) shows `GL_20(J)=EL_20(J)`.

Neither step is certified here.
