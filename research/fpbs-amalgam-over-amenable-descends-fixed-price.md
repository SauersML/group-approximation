title: Fixed price of an amalgam over an infinite amenable subgroup descends to both factors

**Statement.** Let `Gamma_1` and `Gamma_2` be countable groups with a common
infinite amenable subgroup `A`, and let `G = Gamma_1 *_A Gamma_2`. Assume
that every essentially free p.m.p. action of `Gamma_1`, and every essentially
free p.m.p. action of `Gamma_2`, has finite cost. If `G` has fixed price,
then:

- `C(b|Gamma_i) = C(Gamma_i)` for every free p.m.p. action `b` of `G` and
  `i = 1, 2`;
- `Gamma_1` and `Gamma_2` have fixed price;
- `c(G) = C(Gamma_1) + C(Gamma_2) - 1`.

Here `C(Gamma)` is the infimum of the costs of free p.m.p. actions of
`Gamma`, and `c(G)` is the common cost of all free actions of `G`.

**Relation to known results.** This is the converse of the "moreover"
clause of Gaboriau's lecture notes (version of October 3, 2025), Corollary
2.28: if both factors have fixed price, so does the amalgam. Under the
finite-cost hypothesis the two together say that `G` has fixed price if and
only if both factors do. The argument is a short consequence of Gaboriau's
amalgamation formula (Invent. Math. 139 (2000), Theoreme IV.15, used through
Theorem 2.27 of the lecture notes), Abert-Weiss and Tucker-Drob. It was not
found in the sources consulted on September 16, 2026, and no priority is
claimed.

**Use.** Applied along a tower of amalgams, it moves the hypothesis "every
infinite finitely generated group has fixed price" from a finitely generated
supergroup down to a group generated by finitely many amenable subgroups. See
`fpbs-fixed-price-amenably-generated-from-fg`.
