**ESTABLISHED** by `o-type-reversing-strips-the-minimum-proof` (lane bh-emitter-a, 2026-09-19; elementary apart
from Dehornoy's termination theorem, which is cited; not reviewed). The question it addresses, verbatim from
P. Dehornoy, arXiv:1204.3211, Question 10.11: *"If a triangular presentation defines a monoid of O-type, does the
associated reversing necessarily have a polynomial (quadratic?) complexity?"* It remains **open**.

## Setting

`(S ; R)` is a finite right-triangular presentation (Dehornoy, Definition 2.4), and `M = ⟨S | R⟩⁺` is of O-type. By
his Lemma 4.9 the letters then form a single chain.
`G` is its group, with left order `<` and positive cone `P = M − {1}`. For `s < s′` in `S`, `R̂` contains the
relation `s′ = s·C_{s,s′}`, with `C_{s,s′}` a positive word. Put `n = |u| + |v|`.

## Statement

1. **Reversing is stripping.** Right reversing of `u^{-1}v` is forced to act at the junction.
   - At each step, let `s` and `s′` be the first letters of the current words `x` and `y`, and let `t = min(s, s′)`.
     The step replaces `(x, y)` by `(t^{-1}x, t^{-1}y)`, written as positive words:
     - it deletes `t` from the word that begins with `t`;
     - if `s ≠ s′`, it replaces the larger first letter `s′` by `C_{t,s′}`.
   - It terminates, by Dehornoy §4, since O-type monoids have common multiples. The stripped letters
     `t_1, …, t_m` then satisfy `t_1 ⋯ t_m = min(u, v)`.
   - **So the number of steps equals the length of this "top-min" factorization of `min(u, v)`.** The output is a
     positive word for `min^{-1}max`, or its inverse.
2. **Convex subgroups are initial segments.** Every convex subgroup `C` is generated by `S ∩ C`, which is an
   initial segment of `(S, <)`.
   - `P ∩ C` is the semigroup generated by `S ∩ C`.
   - So there are at most `|S| + 1` convex subgroups, and each is a group with a finitely generated positive cone.
3. **Nonnegative homomorphisms bound strips linearly.** Let `φ : G → R` be a homomorphism with `φ ≥ 0` on `S`.
   - Its kernel is a convex normal subgroup.
   - At most `n · max φ / min{φ(s) : φ(s) > 0}` of the stripped letters lie outside it.
4. **Where super-polynomial reversing can live.**
   - Reversing is super-polynomial only if super-polynomially many stripped letters lie in the common kernel `K`
     of all nonnegative homomorphisms. Apply 3 to a finite sum of nonnegative homomorphisms with kernel `K`, which
     exists because `H^1(G; R)` is finite-dimensional. By 2, `K` is one of the convex subgroups, and no
     nonnegative homomorphism sees the order above it.
   - Every torus-knot presentation `(a, b ; a = b(a^p b)^q)` with `pq > 1` (Dehornoy, Proposition 8.1) has no
     nonzero nonnegative homomorphism. So its whole reversing lies in that regime.
5. **The two-letter family `a = b a^k b`.** This is the torus-knot group `⟨a, y | a^{k+1} = y^2⟩`, with
   `b = y^{-1}a`; the case `k = 2` is `B_3`. For it, with `U` unequal steps and `E_a` cancellations `a^{-1}a`:
   - unequal steps come in runs of one direction, each followed by a cancellation `a^{-1}a` or by termination;
   - the number of letters `b` never increases;
   - hence `U ≤ B_0 (E_a + 1)` and `E_a ≤ (A_0 + (k−1)U)/2`, where `A_0` and `B_0` count the letters `a` and `b` in
     `u` and `v`.
   - **`k = 1`, the Klein bottle.** Reversing takes at most `n²/8 + 2n` steps.
   - **`k = 2` with one `b`.** It takes `O(n)` steps.
   - **`k ≥ 2` in general.** The two inequalities do not close, and this is the precise gap.

## What this gives on Question 10.11

- **Proved.** Polynomial, in fact linear, control of every strip that a nonnegative homomorphism sees. A complete
  answer for the Klein bottle presentation.
- **Open core.** Dehornoy's torus-knot presentations, starting with `B_3 = (a, b ; a = ba²b)`.
  - A run of length `r` creates `(k−1)r` letters `a`, enough for `(k−1)r/2` further cancellations. Each
    cancellation can start a new run of length up to `B_0`.
  - So the letter count that settles `k = 1` cannot close for `k ≥ 2` and `B_0 ≥ 2`. A potential must see the order
    itself, for instance through the rotation quasimorphism of the central element `a^{k+1}`.

## Lesson for general BH

Subword reversing on an isolated order is a greedy factorization of the smaller element. Its length is bounded
linearly by any nonnegative homomorphism, so all the difficulty sits where no such homomorphism exists.
- **Why these are hard.** They include the circle-action orders of torus-knot and braid groups, where letter
  counts do not close.
- **What a proof needs.** A polynomial answer to Dehornoy's question, and hence CAP on isolated orders, cannot rest on
  homomorphisms alone; a quasimorphism potential is the natural candidate.
- **What a counterexample needs.** Its long strips must lie in that common kernel, above the conjugation-rigid bottom
  `⟨e⟩`.
