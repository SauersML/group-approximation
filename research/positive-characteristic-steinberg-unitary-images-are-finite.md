**Statement.** Let `A` be an associative unital ring with `m·1 = 0` for some integer `m >= 1`, let `n >= 3`, and let
`rho : St_n(A) -> U(d)` be a homomorphism. Then:

1. `I := {a in A : rho(x_12(a)) = 1}` is a two-sided ideal, and `rho(x_ij(a)) = 1` holds for every `i != j` iff `a ∈ I`.
2. `|A/I| <= m^d`.
3. `rho` factors through the natural map `St_n(A) -> St_n(A/I)`.
4. `rho(St_n(A))` is finite, and `|rho(St_n(A))| <= |E_n(A/I)| · d^d`.

The same holds for every homomorphism `EL_n(A) -> U(d)`, and for every group generated by root elements over `A` that
is a quotient of `St_n(A)`, by composing with `St_n(A) -> EL_n(A)`.

**Consequence.** For fixed `d` and finitely generated `A` there are only finitely many possible `I`, since a finitely
generated ring has finitely many ideals of index at most `m^d`. So the finite-dimensional unitary representation theory
of `St_n(A)` in dimension `d` is exactly that of the finitely many finite groups `St_n(B)`, where `B` runs over the
quotients of `A` of order at most `m^d`. Property (T), residual finiteness and similar soft properties of `St_n(A)` carry
no information about such representations beyond this congruence reduction.

The hypothesis `m·1 = 0` enters only in (2), the exponent bound. Without it the argument still gives (1) and (3),
but `A/I` need not be finite.
