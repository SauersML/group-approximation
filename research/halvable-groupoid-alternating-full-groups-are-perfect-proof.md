Throughout, `G` is ample, Hausdorff, with compact unit space, and halvable.
Multisection notation is Nekrashevych's Definition 3.1, imported in
`nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups`: a degree-`d`
multisection is `F = {F_{i,j}}`, with `U_i = F_{i,i}` pairwise disjoint clopen
and `F_{i,j} : U_j -> U_i` bisections satisfying `F_{i2,i3} F_{i1,i2} =
F_{i1,i3}`. Its symmetric and alternating groups `S(F) ≅ S_d` and
`A(F) ≅ A_d` act on `G^{(0)}` by permuting the `U_i` through the `F_{i,j}` and
fixing everything outside `U_1 ⊔ ... ⊔ U_d`, so every element of `S(F)` is
supported in that union. By Definition 3.2, `A(G) = A_3(G)` is generated by
the groups `A(F)` of degree-3 multisections.

**Step 0: sub-multisections.** If `F` has degree `d` and
`1 <= k_1 < k_2 < k_3 <= d`, then `{F_{k_a, k_b}}_{a,b=1}^3` is a degree-3
multisection, and the 3-cycle it gives is the corresponding 3-cycle of `A(F)`.
Since the finite alternating group `A_d` is generated by 3-cycles for every
`d`, this gives `A(F) ⊆ A_3(G) = A(G)` for a multisection `F` of any degree.

**Step 1: halving a 3-cycle.** Let `F` be a degree-3 multisection with sets
`U_1, U_2, U_3` and let `τ ∈ A(F)` be the 3-cycle `U_1 -> U_2 -> U_3 -> U_1`
given by the `F_{i,j}`. By halvability write `U_1 = A_1 ⊔ B_1` with `A_1, B_1`
nonempty clopen and a bisection `θ` with source `A_1` and range `B_1`. Put

    A_i = F_{i,1}(A_1),   B_i = F_{i,1}(B_1)      (i = 2, 3).

Since `F_{i,1}` is a bisection with source `U_1` and range `U_i`, the sets
`A_i, B_i` are clopen, nonempty, disjoint, and `A_i ⊔ B_i = U_i`. The families
`{A_i}` and `{B_i}` are each closed under the `F_{i,j}`, so `F` restricts to
degree-3 multisections `F^A` on `(A_1, A_2, A_3)` and `F^B` on
`(B_1, B_2, B_3)`, with 3-cycles `τ_A ∈ A(F^A)` and `τ_B ∈ A(F^B)`. These have
disjoint supports and

    τ = τ_A τ_B,   supp(τ_A) ∪ supp(τ_B) = supp(τ) = U_1 ⊔ U_2 ⊔ U_3.

**Step 2: a degree-5 multisection around `τ_A`.** The five sets

    A_1, A_2, A_3, B_1, B_2

are pairwise disjoint (the `U_i` are disjoint and `A_i ∩ B_i = ∅`). They are
pairwise `G`-equivalent: put `e_1 = A_1` (the unit bisection), `e_2 = F_{2,1}`
restricted to `A_1`, `e_3 = F_{3,1}` restricted to `A_1`, `e_4 = θ`, and
`e_5 = F_{2,1} θ` restricted appropriately, so `e_k` is a bisection with source
`A_1` and range the `k`-th set. Then `F'_{k,l} = e_k e_l^{-1}` is a degree-5
multisection `F'`: the identity `F'_{k2,k3} F'_{k1,k2} = F'_{k1,k3}` is
immediate from the definition, and the diagonal bisections are the five
pairwise disjoint clopen sets above. By Step 0, `A(F') ≅ A_5` is a subgroup of
`A(G)`, and every element of `A(F')` is supported in
`A_1 ⊔ A_2 ⊔ A_3 ⊔ B_1 ⊔ B_2 ⊆ supp(τ)`.

By construction `τ_A` is exactly the 3-cycle `(1 2 3)` of `F'`: its germ from
`A_1` to `A_2` is `e_2 e_1^{-1} = F_{2,1}|_{A_1}`, which is the germ of `τ_A`,
and likewise for the other two legs. The finite group `A_5` is perfect, so

    τ_A ∈ [A(F'), A(F')],

a product of commutators of elements of `A(G)` supported inside `supp(τ)`.
Symmetrically, using the degree-5 multisection on `(B_1, B_2, B_3, A_1, A_2)`,

    τ_B ∈ [A(F''), A(F'')]

with `A(F'') ⊆ A(G)` supported inside `supp(τ)`. Multiplying the two gives
item 1 of the theorem. (Ore's theorem for alternating groups sharpens this:
every element of `A_5` is a single commutator, so each of `τ_A`, `τ_B` is one
commutator and `τ` is a product of two.)

**Step 3: restrictions and perfectness.** Let `W ⊆ G^{(0)}` be clopen. A
halving `U = A ⊔ B` of a nonempty clopen `U ⊆ W` has `A, B ⊆ W`, and the
bisection realizing `A ~ B` has source and range in `W`, so it lies in
`G|_W = {g ∈ G : s(g), r(g) ∈ W}`: hence `G|_W` is halvable. Now `A(G|_W)` is
generated by the 3-cycles of degree-3 multisections of `G|_W`, and by items 1
and 2 applied inside `G|_W` each such 3-cycle `τ` is a product of commutators
of elements of `A(G|_W)` (they are supported in `supp(τ) ⊆ W` and are
alternating groups of multisections of `G|_W`). A group generated by products
of commutators of its own elements is perfect, so `A(G|_W)` is perfect. With
`W = G^{(0)}` this gives item 2.

**Step 4: `H_0` and `S = A`.** `H_0(G, Z/2Z)` is the quotient of
`C(G^{(0)}, Z/2Z)` by the subgroup generated by `1_{s(V)} - 1_{r(V)}` over
compact open bisections `V`. For a nonempty clopen `U` with halving
`U = A ⊔ B`, `A ~ B` gives `[1_A] = [1_B]`, so
`[1_U] = [1_A] + [1_B] = 2[1_A] = 0` in `Z/2Z` coefficients. Indicators of
clopen sets generate `C(G^{(0)}, Z/2Z)`, so `H_0(G, Z/2Z) = 0`. Since
`S(G)/A(G)` is a quotient of `H_0(G, Z/2Z)` (Nekrashevych, imported in
`nekrashevych-symmetric-and-alternating-full-groups`), `S(G) = A(G)`. The same
argument applies to each halvable `G|_W`. This is item 3.

**Proof of the corollary.** Let `G = G_V × H`. Every nonempty clopen subset of
`C × H^{(0)}` is a finite disjoint union of bricks `[u_i] × D_i` with `u_i` a
finite binary word and `D_i ⊆ H^{(0)}` clopen. Split each brick as
`[u_i 0] × D_i ⊔ [u_i 1] × D_i` and let `θ_i` be the bisection given by the
germ of the prefix replacement `u_i 0 ↦ u_i 1` in `G_V` times the unit
bisection `D_i` of `H`. The disjoint unions `A = ⊔_i [u_i 0] × D_i`,
`B = ⊔_i [u_i 1] × D_i` and `θ = ⊔_i θ_i` halve the given clopen set. So
`G_V × H` is halvable and the theorem applies. Only the `G_V` factor is used,
so `H` is arbitrary.
