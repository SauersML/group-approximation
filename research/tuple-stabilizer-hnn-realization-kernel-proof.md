Notation is as in the target. `ρ : L -> Sym(X)` extends the action of `Γ` by
`t -> π`. `L` is generated by the generators of `Γ` and `t`.

## Step 0. Effective ingredients

**(i) Points and stabilizers.** `Γ` has solvable word problem
(`type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`). Point stabilizers have decidable
membership (`finite-bi-index-subgroups-have-decidable-membership`, Consequence 1).
So equality of encoded points is decidable: `c.x_i = c'.x_i'` iff `i = i'` and
`c^-1 c' in Stab(x_i)`. Membership in `D_i = Stab_Γ(T_i)` is decidable, by testing
the finitely many fixed-point equations.

**(ii) `D_1` is orbit-finite.** UTS for `T_1` gives `<A_(T_1)>.Y_(T_1) = X` with
`<A_(T_1)> <= D_1`. So the hypotheses of `permutation-hnn-realization-of-type-a-actor-is-a2`
hold, and by its part (a) `L ↷ X` is of type `[A_2]`.

**(iii) `ρ` is computable on encoded points.** Fix, once and for all:
- representatives `y_1, ..., y_r` of the `D_1`-orbits;
- encoded values `π(y_j)`;
- words `ψ(d_l)` for the generators `d_l` of `D_1`.

Given a point `p`, search over words `d` in the generators of `D_1` and indices `j`
until `d.y_j = p`, which is decidable by (i). The search halts, since the `y_j`
represent all orbits. Output `ψ(d).π(y_j)`, which equals `π(p)`.

For `π^-1`, note that the `D_2`-orbits are the sets `π(D_1.y_j)`, with
representatives `π(y_j)`. Search for `d' in D_2` and `j` with `d'.π(y_j) = p`, and
output `ψ^-1(d').y_j`. Here `ψ^-1(d')` is found by enumerating words `d` in the
generators of `D_1` until `ψ(d) = d'` in `Γ`.

So `ρ(u).p` is computable for every word `u` in the generators of `L`.

**(iv) Britton reduction is computable.** A pinch `t h t^-1` with `h in Γ` is
detected by deciding `h in D_1`. It is rewritten by writing `h` as a word in the
generators of `D_1` (by enumeration, after membership is known) and applying `ψ`.
Pinches `t^-1 h t` are handled in the same way with `D_2` and `ψ^-1`. So every word
can be brought to reduced form `γ_0 t^(e_1) γ_1 ... t^(e_n) γ_n`.

## Step 1. The intersection is a tuple stabilizer

Let `k = γ_0 t^(e_1) γ_1 ... t^(e_n) γ_n` be reduced. Put
`v_j = γ_j t^(e_(j+1)) γ_(j+1) ... t^(e_n) γ_n`, with `σ_j = 1` if `e_j = +1` and
`σ_j = 2` if `e_j = -1`. Let `g in Γ`.

**Claim.** `k g k^-1 in Γ` iff `ρ(g)` fixes every point of
`T_k = ρ(v_1)^-1 T_(σ_1) ∪ ... ∪ ρ(v_n)^-1 T_(σ_n)`.

**Proof.** Put `h_n = γ_n g γ_n^-1 in Γ`.
- Suppose `h_j in Γ` for some `j`. Then `t^(e_j) h_j t^(-e_j)` lies in `Γ` iff
  `h_j in D_(σ_j)`.
  - If `h_j in D_(σ_j)`, it equals `ψ^(±1)(h_j)`. Then
    `h_(j-1) = γ_(j-1) ψ^(±1)(h_j) γ_(j-1)^-1 in Γ`.
  - If `h_j` is not in `D_(σ_j)`, the word
    `γ_0 t^(e_1) ... γ_(j-1) t^(e_j) h_j t^(-e_j) γ_(j-1)^-1 ... t^(-e_1) γ_0^-1` is
    reduced. Its middle is not a pinch, and every other junction is a junction of the
    reduced form of `k` or of its inverse. So by Britton's lemma it is not in `Γ`.
- Hence `k g k^-1 in Γ` iff `h_j in D_(σ_j)` for all `j`. Inductively,
  `h_j = v_j g v_j^-1` in `L`.
- Since `ρ` is faithful on `Γ` and `h_j in Γ`: `h_j in D_(σ_j) = Stab_Γ(T_(σ_j))` iff
  `ρ(v_j) ρ(g) ρ(v_j)^-1` fixes `T_(σ_j)` pointwise, iff `ρ(g)` fixes
  `ρ(v_j)^-1 T_(σ_j)` pointwise.
- The conditions are checked from `j = n` down to `j = 1`, and each one is
  meaningful once the previous ones hold. So the conjunction is exactly the claim. ∎

By Step 0 (iii) and (iv), `T_k` is computable from any word representing `k`,
after reduction.

## Step 2. Exact commutation

Let `k in K` and `g in Stab_Γ(T_k)`. By Step 1, `k g k^-1 in Γ`. Also
`ρ(k g k^-1) = ρ(g)`, because `ρ(k) = 1`. `ρ` is faithful on `Γ`, so `k g k^-1 = g`
in `L`, and `[k, g] = 1`.

## Step 3. Certificates

Apply `a2-kernel-recursion-ascends-normal-orbit-finite-chains` (1) to `L ↷ X` with:
- `W` = all words in the generators of `L`;
- `K_0 = {1}`, whose words are r.e. because `L` is finitely presented;
- `C = {(A_T, Y_T) : T a finite tuple of encoded points}`.

`C` is r.e.: enumerate the tuples and run the UTS algorithm. Every pair in `C`
satisfies `<A_T>.Y_T = X`.

For `w in K`, let `T = T_w` from Step 1. Every `a in A_T` represents an element of
`Stab_Γ(T_w)`, so `[w, a] = 1` by Step 2. So the hypothesis of (1) holds, and
`K = W ∩ K` is r.e.

`K` is co-r.e. (`a2-kernel-removal-forces-recursive-kernel` (a)), so `K` is
recursive. By (a) of `permutation-hnn-realization-of-type-a-actor-is-a2`,
`<Γ, π>` has solvable word problem. ∎

## Remark on necessity of the hypothesis

The only point where UTS is used is Step 3. Steps 0–2 hold for any tuple-stabilizer
realization. So a non-recursive kernel here requires that, for some kernel element
`k`, no r.e. family of true pairs `(A, Y)` contains a pair with
`A ⊆ Stab_Γ(T_k)`. If some finitely generated orbit-finite subgroup of `Stab_Γ(T_k)`
could be listed, principle (1) would still apply.
