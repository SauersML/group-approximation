**Precursor.** Baumslag's group `Γ = <a, s, t | [a, a^t], [s, t], a^s = a a^t>`
(G. Baumslag, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 35 (1972) 61--62) is finitely presented, so
its group ring `Z[Γ] = Z[V] ⋊ Z^2` is too, with `Z[V]` a Laurent ring in
countably many variables. Localizing gives `Q(u_v) ⋊ Z^2 ≅ K_∞ ⋊ Z^2`. So the
first two items are one step from Baumslag's theorem; the tape `T` below is
the additive variant, with `x ↔ t` and `y ↔ s` in his letters.

**Setting.**
- `V = Z[s, s^-1, (1+s)^-1]`, a cyclic module over `Z[x^(±1), y^(±1)]` with `x`
  acting as `s` and `y` as `1 + s`.
- `P = Sym_Z(V)`, generated by symbols `t_v`, `v ∈ V`, with `t_(v+w) = t_v + t_w`.
  `V` is free abelian: by partial fractions (`s` and `1+s` are comaximal),
  `V = Z[s^(±1)] ⊕ ⊕_(b>=1) Z (1+s)^(-b)`. So `P` is a polynomial ring over `Z`,
  and `P ⊗ Q` is a polynomial ring over `Q` in countably many variables. Write `t = t_v` for `v = 1`, and `t_k = t_(s^k)`. Its fraction field is `K_∞ = Q(t_1, t_2, ...)`, since
  `V ⊗ Q` has countably infinite dimension.
- `Z^2 = <x, y>` acts on `P` by `x·t_v = t_(sv)` and `y·t_v = t_((1+s)v)`.

**Statement.**
1. **The tape is finitely presented.** The crossed product `T = P ⋊ Z^2` is
   presented, as a ring, by the generators `t`, `x^(±1)`, `y^(±1)` and the
   relations
   - `x x^-1 = x^-1 x = 1`, `y y^-1 = y^-1 y = 1`, `x y = y x`;
   - `y t y^-1 = t + x t x^-1`;
   - `t · x t x^-1 = x t x^-1 · t`.

   The second relation carries the
   module relation `y = 1 + x`. The third, with the second, forces all
   conjugates of `t` to commute, by the induction behind Baumslag's group.
2. **Its localization is simple.** `S = P \ {0}` is `Z^2`-invariant, and
   `S^-1 T = K_∞ ⋊ Z^2`. It is simple, because `Z^2` acts faithfully on the
   field `K_∞` (for skew group rings over commutative rings see P. Öinert,
   arXiv:0904.4661, Theorem 6.13: simple iff `G`-simple and maximal commutative).
   It contains `K_∞`. Likewise the skew Laurent ring
   `K_∞[x^(±1); σ]` of the shift is simple.
3. **The crossed product is never the host.** Let `G` be any group acting on a
   polynomial ring `P` over `Q` in any set of variables (for the tape, `P ⊗ Q`) by automorphisms that
   preserve total degree, for example variable shifts or affine substitutions.
   Then no finitely generated subring of `Frac(P) ⋊ G` contains `Frac(P)`. In
   particular, no finitely presented subring of `K_∞ ⋊ Z^2` or of
   `K_∞[x^(±1); σ]` contains `K_∞`.

**Reading.**
- The shift alone gives the tape `Q[t_i][x^(±1); σ]`, which needs the relations
  `[t, x^k t x^-k] = 0` for all `k`. Adding the letter `y` with
  `y t y^-1 = t + t_1` reduces them to one, as `a^s = a a^t` does for
  Baumslag's group.
- By item 3, the inverses of `S` must come from elements outside the crossed
  product, such as registers, idempotents or Leavitt letters. For Leavitt--Nekrashevych completions, the
  diagonal-subfield bound of Attempt 3 on
  `countable-char-zero-fields-embed-in-fp-simple-rings` says a copy of `K_∞` in
  the diagonal needs a base of infinite Krull dimension. The alternative is a
  non-diagonal commutative subring. The tape of item 1 supplies such a base
  with finitely many relations.
- What is missing is a finite mechanism that inverts every element of `S`.
  Attempt 4 on the open node records why registers over this tape do not give
  one in the obvious ways.
