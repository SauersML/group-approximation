## Setting

- `R = F_p[t, t^{-1}]`, `E = SL_2(R)`, `H = R²` (column vectors), `Γ_p = E ⋉ H`.
- `R` is Euclidean for the width `f ↦ deg_max f − deg_min f`, so `E = EL_2(R)` is generated by
  elementary matrices, and `(Γ_p, H)` has relative (T) by `elementary-linear-semidir-pair-relative-t`.
- `W(Z)` is the group of bijections of `Z` with bounded displacement `sup_n |f(n) − n|`.
- `X = {0,1}^Z`, `[[X]]` its topological full group. Restricting to a dense `σ`-orbit gives an
  injective homomorphism `[[X]] → W(Z)`.

## Statement

Let `Γ` be finitely generated, `H ⊴ Γ`, and `ρ: Γ → W(Z)` a homomorphism. Let `r` be the largest
displacement of `ρ` on a fixed finite generating set. Write `Z_∞` for the union of the infinite
`ρ(Γ)`-orbits and `Z_fin` for its complement.

1. **Few infinite orbits.** There are at most `2r` infinite `Γ`-orbits, and every infinite orbit
   has gaps of length at most `r`.
2. **H-orbits are finite.** If `(Γ, H)` has relative (T), then every `H`-orbit in `Z` is finite.
   Moreover, for every interval `J ⊂ Z`, at most `8 r_Q / κ²` `H`-orbits meet both `J` and `Z \ J`.
   Here `(Q, κ)` is a uniform relative Kazhdan pair and `r_Q` is the largest displacement of `ρ(Q)`.
3. **Finite orbits are bounded.** Suppose also that `Γ/H` has property (τ) with respect to all its
   finite-index subgroups. Then there is `M` with `|O| ≤ M` for every finite `Γ`-orbit `O`. So `Γ`
   acts on `Z_fin` through the finite quotient `Γ/Γ_M`, where `Γ_M` is the intersection of all
   subgroups of index at most `M`.
4. **Congruence lemma.** For `Γ = Γ_p`, every infinite `Γ_p`-orbit `O` has a nonzero ideal
   `I_O ⊲ R` such that `I_O R²` acts trivially on `O`.
5. **Conclusion.** For `Γ = Γ_p`, every homomorphism `ρ: Γ_p → W(Z)` has `ker ρ ∩ H` infinite.
   It contains `(J R²) ∩ Γ_M`, where `J` is the product of the ideals `I_O` over the finitely many
   infinite orbits. In particular `Γ_p` embeds neither in `W(Z)` nor in `[[X]]`, and neither does
   any group containing a copy of `Γ_p`.

## Remarks (checked in outline, not load-bearing)

- `Γ_p` passes the two necessary tests listed in the target node.
  - It is residually finite, being linear (affine inside `SL_3(R)`).
  - An element `(e, h)` with `e` of finite order has finite order, since `H` has exponent `p`. So
    an infinite-order element has `e` of infinite order. Unipotents of `E` have order `p`, so `e`
    is semisimple with an eigenvalue `λ` that is not a root of unity. By the product formula,
    `|λ|_v ≠ 1` at `v = t` or `v = ∞`, so `e` is hyperbolic on that Bruhat–Tits tree. Hence `e`
    is undistorted in `E`, and `(e, h)` is undistorted in `Γ_p` because `Γ_p → E` is 1-Lipschitz.
  So the obstruction here is new relative to both tests.
- Cornulier's FW route (arXiv:1308.1318, Theorem 1.18) does not apply. `E` is dense in
  `SL_2(F_p((t)))`, and `diag(t, t^{-1})` is hyperbolic on its tree, so `E` fails FA and hence FW.
  Item 3 uses (τ) plus relative (T) on the finite orbits instead of FW.
- **Class kill.** Take any pair `(Γ, H)` with relative (T), `H ⊴ Γ`, and `Γ/H` with (τ) for all
  finite-index subgroups. In any bounded-displacement action, `H` is seen faithfully only on at
  most `2r` infinite orbits, modulo a finite-index subgroup, and on those orbits all `H`-orbits are
  finite. For module bases `R^n` over an elementary group with a contracting diagonal element and
  unipotents (item 4), those orbits see the base only through congruence quotients `(R/I)^n`.
  A surviving witness for `full-shift-full-group-has-infinite-normal-relative-t-subgroup` needs one
  of two things:
  - a quotient `Γ/H` without (τ) on its finite-index subgroups; or
  - a base whose characters on an infinite orbit avoid the exponential-growth argument of item 4.
