Let `B = C(M_-, F_3) x| V`, with Haar measure `lambda` on `M_-`. For elements of `B`, the *coefficient
graphing* `Gamma` joins `mu` to `g mu` (`g != 1`) whenever some coefficient `b_g(mu)` is nonzero, and
`R_Gamma` is the relation it generates.

**Case 1, Cohn families.** Let `sigma_1, sigma_2, tau_1, tau_2 in B` with `tau_i sigma_j = delta_ij eps_-`.
- *Expansion.* In every orbit module, every finite set `J` of points satisfies
  `|N_(sigma_1)(J) ∪ N_(sigma_2)(J)| >= 2|J|`, where `N` collects the points of the columns at `J`. This is
  pointwise and uses no measure.
- *Non-hyperfinite.* The relation generated by the coefficient graphing of `sigma_1, sigma_2` is not
  `lambda`-hyperfinite.
- *Degree two.* No such family has a graphing in which every point has at most two neighbours.

**Case 2, corner witnesses.** Let `b', c' in B` with `c' q_[0] b' = eps_-`.
- *Expansion.* `x = q_[0] b'` has `|N_x(J)| >= |J|` with `N_x(J) ⊆ U_[0]`.
- *Non-hyperfinite.* The relation generated by the coefficient graphing of `b'` is not
  `lambda`-hyperfinite.
- *Degree two.* No witness has a graphing in which every point has at most two neighbours.

**Why.** The orbit modules are `B`-modules, and a left inverse inside `B` makes the columns at `J`
injective. On the finite classes `C` of an exhaustion, with interior `C°` of small complement, this gives
`2|C°| <= |C|` in case 1 and `|C°| <= |C ∩ U_[0]|` in case 2. Mass transport turns these into
`lambda(interior) <= 1/2`, which is a contradiction.

**Scope.**
- *Partners.* By the `H`-support projection (Lemma 6.1 of the artifact), the `tau_i` and `c'` may lie
  anywhere in `S_-`. Only the isometries `sigma_j`, or `b'`, need to lie in `B`.
- *What it refines.* It sharpens the support-group no-go: a witness with amenable Thompson projection has a
  hyperfinite coefficient relation, and small coefficient pieces can have one even over a nonamenable
  support.
- *What it misses.* It says nothing about entries outside `B`. Remark 5.2 of the artifact records the
  non-measurable paradoxical matchings that case 1 forces.
