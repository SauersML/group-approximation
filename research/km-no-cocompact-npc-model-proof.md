**(a).**  Literature import, read at source this date: Caprace--Rémy
arXiv:math/0607664, Remark 2 following the normal-subgroup-property
theorem — no Kac--Moody group over a finite field is Gromov
hyperbolic (NSP contradicts the SQ-universality of non-elementary
hyperbolic groups; alternatively, such groups contain infinitely many
conjugacy classes of torsion elements, while a hyperbolic group has
finitely many conjugacy classes of finite subgroups).  The same
remark records the input used in (b): `Lambda` has infinitely many
conjugacy classes of torsion elements.  (Concretely, the subgroups
`V_+` generated by the positive root groups along a residue are
infinite with every element torsion, loc. cit.)

**(b).**  Suppose `Lambda` acts properly cocompactly by isometries on
a complete CAT(0) space `X`.  Pick a compact `K` with
`Lambda . K = X` and let `S = {gamma : gamma K cap K != emptyset}`, a
finite set by properness.  If `g` is torsion, the finite group `<g>`
has a bounded orbit, hence fixes the circumcenter `x` of that orbit
(Bridson--Haefliger II.2.8, classical).  Choose `h` with `h x in K`;
then `h g h^{-1}` fixes `h x`, so `h g h^{-1} in S`.  Thus every
torsion element is conjugate into the finite set `S`: at most `|S|`
conjugacy classes of torsion elements — contradicting (a)'s input.

**(c).**  Let a finite simple complex of finite groups present
`Lambda` and suppose some assignment of geodesic-simplex metrics to
its chambers is nonpositively curved (all local developments CAT(0)).
Then the complex of groups is developable (Bridson--Haefliger III.C,
classical) and its development `D` is a complete CAT(0) complex
(finitely many isometry types of cells), locally finite (finite local
groups, finite base), on which the colimit `Lambda` acts cellularly
with finite stabilizers (conjugates of the local groups), properly
(local finiteness plus finite stabilizers) and cocompactly (compact
base).  This contradicts (b).

For a triangle: metrize the chamber as a geodesic triangle with
angles `gamma_ij > 0` at the `ij`-corners; such a triangle exists in
the Euclidean or hyperbolic plane iff `sum gamma_ij <= pi`.  For a
2-complex, the CAT(0) link condition sits only at vertices
(Bridson--Haefler II.5.24, classical): the link at an `ij`-vertex is
the graph `L_ij` (coset graph of `P_ij` over `P_i, P_j` with edges
`P_ij/P_0`) with every edge of angular length `gamma_ij`, and it is
CAT(1) iff its systole is `>= 2 pi`, i.e. iff
`g_ij . gamma_ij >= 2 pi` where `g_ij` is the girth.  A
nonpositively curved metrization therefore exists iff there are
`gamma_ij` with `sum gamma_ij <= pi` and `gamma_ij >= 2 pi / g_ij`,
i.e. iff `2/g_12 + 2/g_13 + 2/g_23 <= 1`.  Since no NPC metrization
can exist when the colimit is `Lambda`, the girths of any triangle of
finite groups presenting `Lambda` satisfy the strict reverse
inequality.  This proves (NG3).

**(d).**  If the links are generalized `m_ij`-gon incidence graphs,
their girth is `2 m_ij` (Tits; the defining property of a generalized
polygon, classical), so `sum 1/m_ij <= 1` gives an NPC metrization by
the same criterion (hyperbolic-shape triangles when the sum is `< 1`,
Euclidean when `= 1`).  The colimit then acts properly cocompactly on
the complete CAT(0) development as in (c), so by the argument of (b)
run forwards it has at most finitely many conjugacy classes of
torsion elements; by (a)'s input it is not a Kac--Moody lattice over
a finite field.  When the sum is `< 1` the development is CAT(-1)
and the colimit is moreover Gromov hyperbolic — separated from
`Lambda` by (a) as well.
