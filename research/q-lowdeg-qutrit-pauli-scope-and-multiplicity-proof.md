# Audit the characteristic-three low-degree self-test and its central-character corner

## 1. Primary-source statement

The primary source is Anand Natarajan and Thomas Vidick,
*Low-degree testing for quantum states, and a quantum entangled games PCP
for QMA*, FOCS 2018:

- full arXiv text: <https://arxiv.org/html/1801.03821v2>;
- arXiv record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03821>;
- published DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1109/FOCS.2018.00075>.

Theorem 3.2 states the following. Let \(n\geq1\), let \(h,m\) satisfy
\(h^m\geq n\), put \(d=hm\), and let \(q=p^t\) be a prime power such
that \(\mathbb F_q\) has a self-dual basis over \(\mathbb F_p\). Then
their composed quantum low-degree test is a two-prover self-test for the
low-degree generalized-Pauli strategy on \(n\) qudits of local dimension
\(q\), with completeness one and robustness

\[
 \delta=\operatorname{poly}\!\left(
  \operatorname{poly}(p)\operatorname{poly}(\varepsilon)
  +\operatorname{poly}(d/q)
 \right).                                                \tag{1}
\]

The source labels the following paragraph Remark 3.3, not Theorem 3.3.
It explains the scalable parameter choice in which \(q\) grows with the
degree so that \(d/q\) is small.

The prime field \(\mathbb F_3\) satisfies the self-dual-basis hypothesis:
the field trace \(\mathbb F_3\to\mathbb F_3\) is the identity and the
one-element basis \(\{1\}\) has Gram matrix \((1)\). Thus \(q=3\) is
admissible in the formal theorem.

There are two scope cautions.

First, at fixed \(q=3\), the displayed estimate retains the additive
\(d/q\) term. It is not, as stated, a modulus tending to zero with
\(\varepsilon\) for a scalable fixed-q family. One may instead take
extension fields \(q=3^t\) with \(t\) growing, but then the tested matrix
factor grows as well.

Second, this is a game self-test, not a theorem about stability of a
finite group presentation in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm over all
tracial von Neumann algebras. Definition 2.5 of the paper explicitly
concludes only after local isometries and with an arbitrary state
\(|\mathrm{aux}\rangle\). In particular, neither Theorem 3.2 nor its proof
asserts a characteristic-three analogue of Chapman--Vidick--Yuen
Theorem 4.1.

## 2. The binary cardinality obstruction disappears

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) be commuting order-three coordinates. Their joint
characters are indexed by \(u\in\mathbb F_3^k\):

\[
 \chi_u(x_i)=\omega^{u_i},\qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.    \tag{2}
\]

A signed word row

\[
 x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_k^{a_k}=\omega^b                    \tag{3}
\]

allows exactly those characters for which

\[
 a\cdot u=b\pmod3.                                      \tag{4}
\]

A finite family of rows gives an affine linear system \(Bu=c\).
Every nonempty solution set is a coset of \(\ker B\), and hence has
cardinality \(3^{\dim\ker B}\). In particular a one-dimensional affine
line has exactly three points.

Thus a native qutrit coordinate may have exactly three affine outcomes.
The power-of-two argument for binary involutions does not apply. This is
a correction of scope, not a contradiction: the earlier result states
its affine-cardinality fence only for binary word rows.

The observation does not make an arbitrary three-point subset affine, and
it does not linearize the cross-context losing-pair predicate of the Lin
source. It removes only the local objection to a standard three-outcome
qutrit coordinate.

## 3. The generalized-Pauli central corner

Let \(\mathsf P_{3,k}\) be generated by \(J\), \(X_i\), and \(Z_i\),
\(1\leq i\leq k\), with

\[
 J^3=X_i^3=Z_i^3=1,\qquad J\text{ central},
\]

and commutators

\[
 [X_i,Z_i]_g=J,\qquad
 [X_i,X_j]_g=[Z_i,Z_j]_g=[X_i,Z_j]_g=1
 \quad(i\ne j).                                         \tag{5}
\]

Its order is \(3^{2k+1}\). Fix the central character \(J\mapsto\omega\)
and define

\[
 p_\omega=\frac13\left(1+\omega^{-1}J+\omega^{-2}J^2\right). \tag{6}
\]

The standard Schrödinger representation acts on
\(\ell^2(\mathbb F_3^k)\), has dimension \(d=3^k\), and sends \(J\) to
\(\omega I\).

It is the unique irreducible representation with that central character.
Indeed, choose a joint \(Z\)-eigenvector. Applying all \(X(a)\),
\(a\in\mathbb F_3^k\), produces \(3^k\) vectors with distinct
\(Z\)-characters. They span an invariant standard module; irreducibility
leaves one copy.

The central corner has dimension

\[
 \dim_{\mathbf C}p_\omega{\mathbf C}[\mathsf P_{3,k}]
 =\frac{|\mathsf P_{3,k}|}{3}
 =3^{2k}=d^2.                                           \tag{7}
\]

The standard representation maps it onto \(M_d({\mathbf C})\), so the
dimension identity proves

\[
 p_\omega C^*(\mathsf P_{3,k})p_\omega
 \cong M_d({\mathbf C}).                                \tag{8}
\]

For every unital \(C^*\)-algebra \(A\),

\[
 p_\omega\big(C^*(\mathsf P_{3,k})\otimes_{\max}A\big)
 p_\omega
 \cong M_d({\mathbf C})\otimes_{\max}A.                 \tag{9}
\]

## 4. Morita neutrality survives unchanged

Let \(\tau\) be a tracial state on \(M_d\otimes A\), and put

\[
 \tau_A(a)=\tau(I_d\otimes a).                          \tag{10}
\]

Traciality on matrix units gives

\[
 \tau(e_{ij}\otimes a)=\frac{\delta_{ij}}d\tau_A(a),
\]

so

\[
 \tau={\rm tr}_d\otimes\tau_A.                          \tag{11}
\]

Exactly as in the binary proof, a unital finite-dimensional
representation of \(M_d\otimes A\) restricts to a multiple of the defining
representation of \(M_d\), and its commuting factor is a
finite-dimensional representation of \(A\). Conversely, tensoring any
finite-dimensional representation of \(A\) with the defining matrix
representation gives one of \(M_d\otimes A\). Hence

\[
 M_d\otimes A\text{ has a finite-dimensional representation}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\text{ does}.            \tag{12}
\]

The GNS von Neumann algebra of (11) is
\(M_d\overline\otimes\pi_{\tau_A}(A)''\). Matrix amplification and
normalized corners preserve Connes embeddability, so

\[
 {\rm tr}_d\otimes\tau_A\text{ is CE}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tau_A\text{ is CE}.       \tag{13}
\]

Finally, for any projection \(e\in A\), the exact coordinate tuple

\[
 \tau_X(a)\otimes1,\qquad \tau_Z(b)\otimes1             \tag{14}
\]

satisfies every generalized-Pauli relation independently of the
occurrence marker \(1\otimes e\). In self-testing language, \(A\) is the
operator-algebraic form of the arbitrary auxiliary system in Definition
2.5.

## 5. Consequence for the qutrit/Lin compiler

The characteristic-three test can stabilize the correct native qutrit
coordinate geometry, at least in the game/state-dependent setting of the
paper. It also shows that no argument should reject a three-outcome
coordinate merely because three is not a power of two.

But the source obstruction is not the local number of outcomes. The Lin
projection belongs to a pointed nonaffine trace face spanning overlapping
contexts. If it remains as \(1\otimes e\), equations (9)--(14) leave it
arbitrary. If it is forced into \(M_d\otimes1\), its decoded source tuple
already has a finite-dimensional representation.

The moved-root calculation needs a third placement: a nonreducing raw
\(S/T\) coefficient whose exact initial polar support is the named Lin
projection and whose final support is the full trace-\(1/18\) qutrit
target. Q-LOWDEG supplies neither that coefficient nor a relation
identifying its initial support. Therefore the native qutrit test is a
valid coordinate-stability module, but not the missing occurrence
compiler.
