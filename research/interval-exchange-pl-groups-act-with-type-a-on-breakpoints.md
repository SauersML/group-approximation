**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary, not reviewed; no novelty claimed).

## Setting

Let `P ≤ R_{>0}` be a nontrivial finitely generated subgroup, and `A ⊆ R` a `Z[P]`-submodule; then `A` is dense. Let `l ∈ A` with `l > 0`. Put `X = A ∩ [0,l)` and let `IP·A` be as in Bieri–Strebel.

`V(l;A,P)` is the group of bijections `f` of `[0,l)` with a partition of `[0,l)` into intervals `[b_{i−1}, b_i)` with all `b_i ∈ A`, such that:
- on each interval, `f` is `x ↦ p_i x + c_i` with `p_i ∈ P`;
- the images are again intervals `[c, d)` with `c, d ∈ A`, forming a partition.

The mirror convention with pieces `(a,b]` gives an isomorphic group, via `x ↦ l − x`. Examples:
- Higman–Thompson `V_{n,r}`: `A = Z[1/n]`, `P = ⟨n⟩`, `l = r`;
- Stein's `V`-groups: `A = Z[1/m]`, `P = ⟨n_1, …, n_k⟩`;
- the golden-ratio group `V_τ` of Burillo–Nucinkis–Reeves (arXiv:2006.02401): `A = Z[τ]`, `P = ⟨τ⟩`, `l = 1`.

## Theorem

Assume `d := |A/(IP·A)| < ∞` and `V = V(l;A,P)` is finitely presented. Then the action of `V` on `X` is of type (A):
1. it is faithful and transitive;
2. there are at most `d` orbits of ordered pairs of distinct points;
3. `Stab_V(0) = ⟨K_{δ_0}, g, g_1, …, g_k⟩` is finitely generated. Here `K_{δ_0} ≅ V` is the subgroup fixing `[0,δ_0)` pointwise, and `g, g_1, …, g_k` are germ elements at `0`.

If `d = 1`, `V` is `k`-transitive on `X` for every `k`, i.e. highly transitive.

Consequently every subgroup of `V(l;A,P)` has solvable word problem, satisfies permutational Boone–Higman, and embeds in the finitely presented simple group `SV_{V(l;A,P)}` (`type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`). So BFFHZ Question 3.4 holds for its finitely presented simple subgroups.

## Proof

Throughout, the input is Bieri–Strebel **Theorem A** (arXiv:1411.2868, `chaptA_Construction.tex` l.235, read at source): for `a < c` and `a' < c'` in `A`, some PL homeomorphism of `R` with breakpoints in `A` and slopes in `P` maps `[a,c]` onto `[a',c']` if and only if `c' − a' ≡ c − a` modulo `IP·A`. Restricting such a map to `[a,c)` gives an admissible piece.

**(1) Faithful and transitive.**
- *Faithful:* `f` is affine on each piece and `X` is dense, so `f` is determined by `f|_X`.
- *Transitive:* for `x ∈ X` the rotation sends `[0, l−x)` to `[x,l)` and `[l−x, l)` to `[0,x)`, both by translations. Its breakpoint `l−x` lies in `A` and its slopes are `1`.

**(2) Pair orbits.** Rotate a pair `(x,y)` to `(0,y)`. Suppose `y, y' ∈ A ∩ (0,l)` satisfy `y ≡ y'` modulo `IP·A`.
- Theorem A gives admissible maps `[0,y) → [0,y')` and `[y,l) → [y',l)`; the second applies because `l−y ≡ l−y'`.
- Together they form an element of `Stab_V(0)` sending `y` to `y'`.

So there are at most `d` orbits on ordered pairs.

*Case `d = 1`.* Given two `k`-tuples of distinct points, add `0` to both and cut `[0,l)` at every listed point. Adding extra points if needed, both partitions have the same number of pieces. Map the piece starting at each `x_i` onto the piece starting at `y_i` with Theorem A, and pair the remaining pieces arbitrarily. All lengths are congruent modulo `IP·A = A`, so every piece map exists.

**(3) The stabilizer.** An element `f` fixing `0` is `x ↦ px` on its first piece `[0,b)`. The germ map `σ: Stab_V(0) → P`, `f ↦ p`, is a homomorphism. Its kernel is `K = ⋃_{δ} K_δ`, where `K_δ` is the subgroup acting as the identity on `[0,δ)`, for `δ ∈ A ∩ (0,l)`.
- *Germ elements.* Let `p_1, …, p_k` generate `P`. For each `i` pick `δ_i ∈ A` small with `p_i δ_i < l`. Let `g_i` be `x ↦ p_i x` from `[0,δ_i)` onto `[0,p_iδ_i)`, together with a Theorem-A map from `[δ_i,l)` onto `[p_iδ_i,l)`. The lengths differ by `(1−p_i)δ_i ∈ IP·A`, so the map exists. Then `σ(g_i) = p_i`, and `Stab_V(0) = ⟨K, g_1, …, g_k⟩`.
- *A contracting element.* Fix `p' ∈ P` with `0 < p' < 1`, and put `δ_0 = (1−p')l ∈ A ∩ (0,l)`. Let `g` be `x ↦ p'x` on `[0,δ_0)`, together with a Theorem-A map from `[δ_0,l)` onto `[p'δ_0,l)`. Since `g([0,δ)) = [0,p'δ)` for `δ ≤ δ_0`, we get `g^n K_{δ_0} g^{−n} = K_{p'^nδ_0}`. These subgroups increase and are cofinal, so `K = ⋃_n g^n K_{δ_0} g^{−n}`.
- *`K_{δ_0} ≅ V`.* Translating by `−δ_0 ∈ A` identifies `K_{δ_0}` with `V(l−δ_0;A,P) = V(p'l;A,P)`. Scaling by `1/p'` preserves `A` and `P`, so this is isomorphic to `V(l;A,P)`, which is finitely generated.

Hence `Stab_V(0) = ⟨K_{δ_0}, g, g_1, …, g_k⟩` is finitely generated. By transitivity, so is every point stabilizer. The action is faithful, `V` is finitely presented, point stabilizers are finitely generated, and pair orbits are finite, so it is of type (A). ∎

## Instances

1. **Calibration.** `V_{n,r}`: here `d = n−1`, recovering the known type (A) action of Higman–Thompson groups on `n`-adic points.
2. **The golden-ratio groups.** Here `τ − 1 = −τ²` is a unit, so `IP·A = A` and `d = 1`.
   - BNR (arXiv:2006.02401 l.101) assert that `V_τ` is `F_∞` by Stein's methods; this is cited, not re-derived.
   - So `V_τ` acts highly transitively on `Z[τ] ∩ [0,1)` with type (A).
   - The simple index-2 subgroup `V_xz` (their Theorem `thethm`, l.429–430) satisfies PBH. It even has its own type (A) action: restrict to `V_xz`, which has finite index, so it is still finitely presented, with finitely generated stabilizers and at most `2` pair orbits.
3. **Stein's `V`-groups** `V(l; Z[1/m], ⟨n_1,…,n_k⟩)`, with `d = gcd(n_i − 1)`: the theorem applies whenever the group is finitely presented. That is Stein's finiteness theorem, cited and not read here. With `circle-pl-groups-act-with-type-a-on-breakpoint-orbits`, this covers the "Stein groups, irrational-slope `T_τ`, `V_τ`" row of the Q3.4 census (`research/artifacts/q34-known-actors-coverage-2026-09-13.md`), modulo the cited finiteness theorems.

## Remark: why the V-version is easier than the T-version

For `T`-groups the stabilizer is an interval group, whose finite generation is a separate problem (Bieri–Strebel Chapter B). For `V`-groups the stabilizer contains a full copy of `V` living on `[δ_0,l)`, and a single contraction toward `0` sweeps that copy over the whole kernel. So finite generation of the stabilizer comes for free from finite generation of `V`.
