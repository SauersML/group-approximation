Notation as in the target. Group-ring coefficients sit on the left. In `D(R)`
we use `u f = fbar u`, in particular `u t^-1 = t u` and `t u t = u`.

**Step 1: the shadows are homomorphisms.** Assign the generators as in the
table and check both relators `b^-1 a^2 b = a^-2` and `a^-1 b^2 a = b^-2`.

- **Shadow C.** `a -> u`, `b -> beta = -t^-1 u`. Here `u^2 = -1` and
  `beta^2 = t^-1 u t^-1 u = t^-1 t u^2 = -1`. So `a^2` and `b^2` both go to the
  central element `-1`, and both sides of each relator go to `-1`. Also
  `ab -> -u t^-1 u = -t u^2 = t`, so `z -> t^2`, and `x, y -> -1`.
- **Shadow A.** `a -> t`, `b -> u`. Then
  `b^-1 a^2 b -> u^-1 t^2 u = -u t^2 u = -t^-2 u^2 = t^-2`, the image of
  `a^-2`, and `a^-1 b^2 a -> t^-1 (-1) t = -1`, the image of `b^-2`. Also
  `ab -> tu`, so `z -> t u t u = t t^-1 u^2 = -1`, `x -> t^2` and `y -> -1`.
- **Shadow B.** `a -> u`, `b -> t`. Then `b^-1 a^2 b -> -1`, the image of
  `a^-2`, and `a^-1 b^2 a -> -u t^2 u = t^-2`, the image of `b^-2`. Also
  `ab -> ut = t^-1 u`, so `z -> u t u t = u^2 t^-1 t = -1`, `x -> -1` and
  `y -> t^2`.

So each assignment extends to a ring homomorphism `R[P] -> D(R)`.

**Step 2: kernels.** Take shadow C. Let `J` be the two-sided ideal generated by
`x+1` and `y+1`; it lies in the kernel. In `R[P]/J` every group element
`x^i y^j z^k c`, with `c in {1, a, b, ab}`, equals `(-1)^(i+j) z^k c`, so
`R[P]/J` is spanned over `R` by the classes of `z^k c`. Their images are

    z^k -> t^(2k),   z^k a -> t^(2k) u,   z^k b -> -t^(2k-1) u,   z^k ab -> t^(2k+1),

which are, up to sign, the basis `t^m, t^m u` of `D(R)`, each exactly once. A
spanning family that maps bijectively onto a basis up to signs is a basis, so
`R[P]/J -> D(R)` is an isomorphism and the kernel is `J`. The same count works
for shadow A, where `x^k c` goes to `t^(2k), t^(2k+1), t^(2k) u, t^(2k+1) u`, and
for shadow B, where `y^k c` goes to `t^(2k), t^(2k) u, t^(2k+1), t^(2k-1) u`.
In particular every shadow is surjective, and every signed basis element is the
shadow of `+-g` for some `g in P`.

**Step 3: the norm is multiplicative.** `D(R)` is a free left
`R[t^(+-1)]`-module with basis `(1, u)`. For `X = l_0 + l_1 u`, right
multiplication `Y -> YX` is left `R[t^(+-1)]`-linear, with `1 X = l_0 + l_1 u`
and `u X = l_0bar u + l_1bar u^2 = -l_1bar + l_0bar u`. In row convention its
matrix is

    [  l_0      l_1   ]
    [ -l_1bar   l_0bar ],

with determinant `N(X)`. Since `Z(YX) = (ZY)X`, the matrix of `YX` is the
product of the matrices of `Y` and `X`, so `N(YX) = N(Y) N(X)`. Also
`N(X)` is invariant under `t -> t^-1`, and `N(1) = 1`.

**Step 4: units of `D(Z)`.** Let `X` be a unit of `D(Z)`. Then
`N(X) N(X^-1) = 1` in `Z[t^(+-1)]`, whose units are `+-t^m`. As `N(X)` is
invariant under `t -> t^-1`, `m = 0` and `N(X) = +-1`. The constant term of
`N(X)` is `sum_m (l_(0,m)^2 + l_(1,m)^2)`, a nonnegative integer. So `N(X) = 1`,
exactly one coefficient of `X` is `+-1` and all others are `0`:
`X = +-t^m` or `X = +-t^m u`.

**Item 1.** A ring homomorphism sends units to units, so each shadow of
`v in Z[P]^x` is a unit of `D(Z)`, hence a signed basis element. By Step 2 it is
the shadow of `epsilon_i g_i` for some sign and some `g_i in P`, and
`v - epsilon_i g_i` lies in the kernel `I_i`.

**Item 2.** By `rational-units-rescale-to-integral-units`, whose hypothesis
holds for `P` as recorded there, a unit of `Q[P]` is `c v` with `c in Q^x` and
`v in Z[P]^x`. Its shadows are `c` times those of `v`.

**Item 3.** The shadows are defined over `Z`, so they commute with reduction
modulo 2. If `v = 1 mod 2`, each shadow of `v` is a signed basis element that
is `1` modulo 2, hence `epsilon_i in {+-1}`, and `v - epsilon_i in I_i`. Then
`epsilon_C - epsilon_A` lies in `I_A + I_C`, inside the two-sided ideal `K`
generated by `x+1, y+1, z+1`. Under shadow C, `K` maps onto the ideal generated
by `t^2 + 1`, which is two-sided because `u (t^2 + 1) = t^-2 (t^2 + 1) u`. By
Step 3, `D(Z)/(t^2 + 1)` is free over `Z` with basis `1, t, u, tu`, so `2` is not
in `K` and `epsilon_C = epsilon_A`. In the same way `epsilon_B = epsilon_C`. So
`v - epsilon in I_A cap I_B cap I_C`.

**Item 4.** Reduction modulo `d` commutes with the shadows. If a unit
`v in Z[P]` reduces to `w`, each shadow of `w` is the reduction of a signed
basis element, which is again a signed basis element.

**Item 5 (exact computation).** The script
`research/artifacts/promislow-definite-shadows-gardam-complex.py` imports
`research/artifacts/gardam-support-pair-gsp.py`, which rebuilds Gardam's unit
`alpha` and its inverse `beta` in `R[P]`, `R = Z[s,t]/(s^4+1, t^4+1)`, and checks
`alpha beta = beta alpha = 1` exactly. It specializes Gardam's parameters
`s, t` both to `zeta_8`, applies the three shadows (with Laurent variable
renamed), checks multiplicativity of each shadow on 300 random pairs of words,
and checks that the shadows of `alpha` and `beta` multiply to `1`. Each shadow of
`alpha` has norm `1` and is not a monomial. Over `Z[zeta_8]` the constant term
of `N` is not a sum of squares of integers, so Step 4 does not apply. The log is
in the artifact.

QED
