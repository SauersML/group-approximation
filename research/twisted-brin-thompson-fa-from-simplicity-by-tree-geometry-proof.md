## Imports

J. Belk and M. C. B. Zaremsky, *Twisted Brin–Thompson groups*, arXiv:2001.04579,
Geom. Topol. 26 (2022). Quoted from the ar5iv rendering, read on 2026-09-17 with a
summarizing fetch tool.
- Bricks: "a function ψ:S→{0,1}* has finite support if ψ(s)=∅ for all but finitely
  many s∈S. Given such a function, the associated dyadic brick in S is the set
  B(ψ)={κ∈ℭ^S | ψ(s) is a prefix of κ(s) for each s∈S}."
- "h_ψ : ℭ^S → B(ψ) defined by h_ψ(κ)(s) = ψ(s)·κ(s)"; "τ_γ(κ)(s) = κ(γ^−1 s)".
- "Given any two dyadic bricks B(φ) and B(ψ) and an element γ ∈ G, the associated
  twist homeomorphism B(φ) → B(ψ) is the composition h_ψ ∘ τ_γ ∘ h_φ^−1".
- "Given any two partitions B(φ₁),...,B(φₙ) and B(ψ₁),...,B(ψₙ) of ℭ^S into the
  same number of dyadic bricks and γ₁,...,γₙ∈G, we can define a homeomorphism of
  ℭ^S by mapping each B(φᵢ) to B(ψᵢ) via the twist homeomorphism associated to γᵢ.
  The group of all homeomorphisms of ℭ^S defined in this fashion is the twisted
  Brin–Thompson group SV_G."
- Theorem A: "The group SV_G is finitely generated if and only if G is finitely
  generated and the action of G on S has finitely many orbits."
- Theorem 3.4: "The twisted Brin–Thompson group SV_G is simple."

Throughout, `G` acts faithfully on the nonempty set `S`, `X = ℭ^S` and `Γ = SV_G`.
A *branch* is a twist homeomorphism `h_ψ τ_γ h_φ^-1 : B(φ) -> B(ψ)`.

## Brick calculus

**B1 (identities).** `τ_γ h_φ = h_(γφ) τ_γ` with `(γφ)(s) = φ(γ^-1 s)`,
`h_ψ h_φ = h_(ψ*φ)` (coordinatewise concatenation) and `τ_δ τ_γ = τ_(δγ)`. Each is
checked by evaluating at a coordinate `s`.

**B2 (bricks and clopens).** Bricks form a basis of the product topology.
- Two bricks meet in a brick or not at all: `B(φ) ∩ B(ψ)` is nonempty iff
  `φ(s), ψ(s)` are prefix-comparable for all `s`, and then it is the brick of the
  longer prefixes.
- The complement of `B(ψ)` is the union, over the finitely many `s` in the support and
  the words `w != ψ(s)` of length `|ψ(s)|`, of the bricks prescribing `w` at `s`.
- So every clopen set is a finite disjoint union of bricks, and it has such a partition
  with `m` pieces for every `m` at least some `m_0`: splitting `B(ψ)` at one
  coordinate into the prefixes `ψ(s)0` and `ψ(s)1` adds one piece.
- Every nonempty clopen set has a proper nonempty clopen subset.

**B3 (branches restrict and compose).** Restrict the branch `b = h_ψ τ_γ h_φ^-1` to a
sub-brick `B(φ*φ')`. By B1,
`b h_(φ*φ') = h_ψ τ_γ h_(φ') = h_ψ h_(γφ') τ_γ = h_(ψ*γφ') τ_γ`. So the restriction is the branch
`B(φ*φ') -> B(ψ*γφ')` with the same `γ`. Similarly:
- the composite of branches `B(φ)->B(ψ)` (for `γ`) and `B(ψ)->B(χ)` (for `δ`) is the
  branch for `δγ`;
- the inverse of a branch is the branch `B(ψ)->B(φ)` for `γ^-1`.

**Admissible maps.** For clopen `U, U'`, let `𝒯(U, U')` be the set of homeomorphisms
`U -> U'` that are branches on the pieces of some finite brick partition of `U`.
- By the import, `Γ = 𝒯(X, X)`. The range pieces are automatically a partition.
- By B2 and B3, `𝒯` is closed under composition and inverses. For composition,
  intersect the range pieces of the first map with the domain pieces of the second,
  pull back through branches (inverse branches send bricks to bricks), and compose.
- Admissible maps on disjoint domains with disjoint ranges patch together.
- The identity of `U` is admissible.

**B4 (charts).** For nonempty clopen `U`, there is `h_U in 𝒯(X, U)`.
- Write `U = B(ψ_1) ⊔ ... ⊔ B(ψ_m)`.
- Partition `X` at one coordinate `s_0` into the `m` bricks with prefixes
  `1, 01, ..., 0^(m-2)1, 0^(m-1)`.
- Map the `i`-th brick canonically (`γ = 1`) onto `B(ψ_i)`.

For clopen `U`, let `Γ_U = {f in Γ : f = id on X∖U}`. Such an `f` satisfies `f(U) = U`.

## Four lemmas

**R (rigid stabilizers are copies).** Let `U` be nonempty clopen. Define
`ι_U(f) = h_U f h_U^-1` on `U` and the identity on `X∖U`.
- `ι_U : Γ -> Γ_U` is an isomorphism. Admissibility of `ι_U(f)` follows from
  composition and patching.
- It is injective.
- It is onto: for `f in Γ_U`, restrict a brick partition of `f` to `U`, using B2 and B3.
  Then `f|_U in 𝒯(U, U)`, so `h_U^-1 f|_U h_U in 𝒯(X, X) = Γ`.

So `Γ_U ≅ Γ`. By Theorem 3.4 it is simple. It is also nonabelian: `Γ` contains two
brick transpositions with overlapping, non-nested supports, which do not commute.
So `Γ_U` is perfect. When `G` is finitely generated with finitely many orbits, it
is finitely generated by Theorem A.

**C (conjugation and commuting).** For `x in Γ`, `x Γ_U x^-1 = Γ_(xU)`. If
`U ∩ U' = ∅`, then `Γ_U` and `Γ_(U')` commute: check pointwise on `U`, on `U'` and
on the rest, using `f(U) = U`.

**T (transitivity inside a clopen).** Let `W` be clopen, and let `K, K' ⊆ W` be clopen with
`K, K', W∖K, W∖K'` all nonempty. Then some `w in Γ_W` has `wK = K'`.
- By B2, choose partitions of `K` and `K'` into the same number `m` of bricks, and of
  `W∖K` and `W∖K'` into the same number `n`.
- Map piece to piece canonically, and use the identity off `W`.

**D (displacement).** Let `U` be nonempty clopen with `X∖U` nonempty. Choose a
proper nonempty clopen `K' ⊊ X∖U` (B2). By T with `W = X`, some `g in Γ` has
`gU = K'`, which is disjoint from `U`. By C, `g Γ_U g^-1 = Γ_(gU)` commutes with
`Γ_U`.

**J (join lemma).** Let `U, W` be nonempty clopen with `U ∩ W != ∅`, and put
`Y = U ∪ W`. Then `<Γ_U, Γ_W> = Γ_Y`.
- Put `H = <Γ_U, Γ_W> <= Γ_Y`. If one set contains the other, `H` is the larger
  stabilizer. So assume `A = U∖W`, `C = W∖U` and `D = U∩W` are all nonempty.
- Let `N` be generated by all `Γ_K` with `K ⊆ Y` clopen and `Y∖K != ∅`.
  - By C, `N` is normal in `Γ_Y`. For `x in Γ_Y`, `xK ⊆ Y` and `Y∖xK = x(Y∖K)`.
  - `N` is nontrivial.
  - `Γ_Y` is simple, by R or because `Y = X`. So `N = Γ_Y`.
- It remains to show `Γ_K <= H` for each such nonempty `K`. Choose a brick
  `E ⊆ Y∖K`. Then `E` meets `W` or `A`.
  - *`E ∩ W != ∅`.* If `K ∩ W = ∅`, then `K ⊆ A ⊆ U` and `Γ_K <= Γ_U`. Otherwise
    apply T in `W` to `K∩W` and `K' = D`. Both have nonempty complements in `W`:
    `E∩W` and `C`. This gives `w in Γ_W` with `w(K∩W) = D`. Since `w` fixes `A`
    pointwise, `wK = (K∩A) ∪ D ⊆ U`. So `Γ_K = w^-1 Γ_(wK) w <= H`.
  - *`E ∩ A != ∅`.* Symmetrically: if `K ∩ U = ∅`, then `K ⊆ C ⊆ W`. Otherwise
    apply T in `U` to `K∩U` and `D`, whose complements in `U` contain `E∩A` and `A`.
    This gives `u in Γ_U` with `uK = (K∩C) ∪ D ⊆ W`. So `Γ_K <= H`. `∎`

## The theorem

Assume `G` is finitely generated and acts with finitely many orbits.
- Partition `X = P_1 ⊔ P_2 ⊔ P_3 ⊔ P_4` into bricks (B2).
- Put `U_1 = P_3∪P_4`, `U_2 = P_1∪P_4` and `U_3 = P_2∪P_4`.

**Generation.** By J with intersection `P_4`, `<Γ_(U_1), Γ_(U_2)> = Γ_(P_1∪P_3∪P_4)`.
By J again with intersection `P_4`, `<Γ_(P_1∪P_3∪P_4), Γ_(U_3)> = Γ_X = Γ`. So
`Γ = <K_1, K_2, K_3>` with `K_i = Γ_(U_i)`.

**Hypotheses of the criterion.** For `i, j`, put `Q_ij = Γ_(U_i ∪ U_j)`.
- Each union `U_i ∪ U_j` is proper:
  - `U_1 ∪ U_2` misses `P_2`;
  - `U_1 ∪ U_3` misses `P_1`;
  - `U_2 ∪ U_3` misses `P_3`;
  - each `U_i` misses two bricks.
- By R, `Q_ij` is finitely generated and perfect, and it contains `K_i` and `K_j`.
- By D, it commutes with the conjugate `g Q_ij g^-1`.

By the (FA) criterion of `displaced-perfect-subgroups-are-elliptic-in-tree-actions`,
every action of `Γ` on a tree without inversions fixes a vertex. `∎`

**Specialisation.** For the translation action of a finitely generated `G` on itself,
there is one orbit. So `G·V_G` has FA.
