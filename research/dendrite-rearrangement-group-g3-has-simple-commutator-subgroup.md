**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, not independently reviewed). The proof is the one step
missing from Tarocchi's argument, added to results imported from his paper. No priority
is claimed beyond a bounded search: arXiv API searches on 2026-09-18 (author Tarocchi;
"dendrite" in math.GR; "Wazewski") found no later answer.

## The printed question

M. Tarocchi, *On Thompson groups for Ważewski dendrites*, arXiv:2310.14660v3
(8 May 2025), TeX `main.tex` l.1604–1605 (sha256 prefix b4546cad24e80130):

> Is $[G_3, G_3]$ simple?

It is repeated as Question `qst:3:comm:simple` in Tarocchi's thesis, *Rearrangement
groups of fractals: structure and conjugacy*, arXiv:2412.02339, l.5583. Theorem
`thm:comm:simple` (l.1533) proves simplicity for n ≥ 4. Remark `rmk:3:comm:simple`
(l.1595) says that for n = 3 "it would suffice to find a different way to show that
τ_2 τ_3 ∈ N".

## Setting (imported from arXiv:2310.14660v3)

- `G = G_3` is the rearrangement group of the replacement system `𝒟_3`, acting on the
  dendrite `D_3`. `p_0` is the central branch point. The cells `C(1), C(2), C(3)` meet
  at `p_0`, and the branches at `p_0` are `C(i) ∖ {p_0}`.
- `K ≅ Sym(3)` permutes `C(1), C(2), C(3)` canonically (Lemma `lem:K`). `τ_i ∈ K` is
  the transposition `(1 i)`. `H ≅ F` is generated by `g_0, g_1` (Lemma `lem:thomp`), and
  `g_1` is supported in `C(3)`.
- `Φ = (Π, Δ) : G → Z/2 ⊕ Z` is given by the parity and the endpoint derivative.
  Theorem `thm:commutator` (l.1463): `G' := [G,G] = Ker Φ`, and `Φ(τ_i) = (1,0)`.
- Remark `rmk:comm:gen` (l.1470): `G'` is generated by `τ_2τ_3`, `[τ_3, g_1]` and the
  elements `τ_2^ζ g_1^{-z} g_0 g_1^z τ_2^ζ`. This rests on Theorem `thm:gen` (l.970),
  `G_n = <H, K>`, which is stated and proved for every `n` with no restriction.
- **Facts from the proof of Theorem `thm:comm:simple`, valid for n = 3** (Remark
  `rmk:3:comm:simple`: "Every other step of the proof works"). Let `N ⊴ G'` be
  nontrivial. Then:
  - (E) `[h_1, h_2] ∈ N` for all `h_1, h_2 ∈ Rist_{G'}(B)` and every branch `B`
    (equation `eq:comm:simple`, l.1557). This is Epstein's double commutator trick,
    combined with the transitivity of `G'` on branches (Corollary `cor:comm:trans`,
    whose proof uses that `[K,K] = Alt(3)` is transitive on the three branches at
    `p_0`).
  - (R) `N` contains `[τ_3, g_1]`, `[H,H]` and every `τ_2^ζ g_1^{-z} g_0 g_1^z τ_2^ζ`.
    These are derived from (E) and equation `eq:g0` (l.1455).

## Statement

`[G_3, G_3]` is simple. With Theorem `thm:comm:simple`, the commutator subgroup
`[G_n, G_n]` is simple for every `n ≥ 3`.

## Proof

Let `N ⊴ G'` be nontrivial, and put `c = τ_2 τ_3 ∈ [K,K]`, a 3-cycle of order 3. By (R)
and the generating set above, `N` contains every generator of `G'` except possibly `c`.
So `G'/N` is generated by `cN`. In particular `G'/N` is cyclic, hence abelian. It
remains to show `c ∈ N`.

**Local transpositions.**
- For a cell `C(x)` in which the sub-cells `C(x2)`, `C(x3)` both end at free endpoints,
  let `ρ_x` be the rearrangement that swaps `C(x2)` and `C(x3)` canonically and fixes
  everything else.
- For `x = 12, 22, 312`: `ρ_x` is a graph automorphism of an expansion. Both edges run
  from the centre of `C(x)` to leaves.
- It is supported in `C(x)` and is an involution.
- Its local permutation is a transposition at the centre of `C(x)` and trivial at every
  other branch point, so `Π(ρ_x) = 1`.
- Its endpoint derivatives at the two moved leaves are `0 − 1` and `1 − 0`, since the
  words `x2` and `x3` end in zero and one letter `3`. So `Δ(ρ_x) = 0`, and
  `Φ(ρ_x) = (1, 0)`.

**Step 1: `Y := ρ_22 ρ_12` lies in `N`.**
- Let `q` be the centre of `C(3)`, and `B` the branch at `q` containing `p_0`. Then
  `D_3 ∖ B = C(32) ∪ C(33)`, so `B ⊇ C(1) ∪ C(2) ∪ C(31) ∖ {q}`.
- Put `σ = ρ_312`. Then `C(312) ⊂ C(31) ∖ {p_0, q}`.
- `τ_2`, `ρ_12` and `σ` all lie in `Rist_G(B)`. Here `τ_2` fixes `C(3)` pointwise.
- `σ` commutes with `τ_2` and `ρ_12`, since their supports are disjoint.
- So `a := τ_2 σ` and `b := ρ_12 σ` have `Φ = 0`, and lie in `Rist_{G'}(B)`.
- Since `σ` commutes with both and all four are involutions,
  `[a, b] = τ_2 ρ_12 τ_2 ρ_12 = (τ_2 ρ_12 τ_2) ρ_12 = ρ_22 ρ_12 = Y`.
  Here `τ_2` transports `C(1)` canonically onto `C(2)`.
- By (E), `Y ∈ N`.

**Step 2: a conjugate of `c` by an element of `G'`.**
- Put `g = τ_2 ρ_12`. Then `Φ(g) = 0`, so `g ∈ G'`.
- `c` maps `C(2)` canonically onto `C(1)`, since `τ_3` fixes `C(2)`. So
  `c^{-1} ρ_12 c = ρ_22`, and therefore `ρ_12 c ρ_12 = c · (c^{-1} ρ_12 c) · ρ_12 = c Y`.
- Hence `g c g^{-1} = τ_2 (c Y) τ_2 = (τ_2 c τ_2)(τ_2 Y τ_2) = c^{-1} Y`.
- Here `τ_2 c τ_2 = τ_3 τ_2 = c^{-1}`, and `τ_2 Y τ_2 = ρ_12 ρ_22 = Y`.

**Step 3: conclusion.**
- `G'/N` is abelian and `g ∈ G'`, so `g c g^{-1} N = c N`.
- By Steps 1 and 2, `g c g^{-1} N = c^{-1} Y N = c^{-1} N`.
- So `c^2 ∈ N`, and `c = (c^2)^{-1} · c^3 ∈ N`.
- Hence `N = G'`. So `G'` is simple.

## What changed relative to n ≥ 4

For `n ≥ 4`, Tarocchi writes the 3-cycle directly as a commutator of two elements of one
rigid stabilizer. For `n = 3` no rigid stabilizer contains both corrected
transpositions. Here that is avoided in two ways:
- the abelian quotient `G'/N` turns the conjugation `c ↦ c^{-1}` by the odd element
  `τ_2` into the congruence `c ≡ c^{-1}`;
- the error term `Y` of correcting `τ_2` to `g ∈ G'` is itself a commutator inside one
  rigid stabilizer.

## Consequences and scope

- With Theorem `thm:comm:fg` ("finitely generated, for every n ≥ 3") and the embedding
  `G_n ≤ V` (arXiv:2310.14660v3, Proposition `prop:dendrite:Thompson:comparison`), `[G_3, G_3]` is a finitely generated
  infinite simple subgroup of Thompson's `V`. It is dense in `Homeo(D_3)`, by Corollary
  `cor:comm:dns`.
- Finite presentation of `G_n` or `[G_n, G_n]` is not addressed; see
  `dendrite-rearrangement-groups-are-finitely-presented`. If `[G_3, G_3]` were finitely
  presented, it would be a new finitely presented simple group in the Thompson family.
- All imported statements were read from the TeX. The proofs of `thm:gen`,
  `thm:commutator` and `eq:g0` were not re-verified for `n = 3` beyond checking that
  their statements carry no restriction on `n`.
