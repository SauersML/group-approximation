## Statement

Let `g ≥ 3` and let `L ≤ Mod(S_g)` have finite index. Let `X` be a finite alphabet, and
suppose `L` acts faithfully on the rooted tree `X^*` and self-similarly. That is, for
every `h ∈ L` and `x ∈ X` there is `h|_x ∈ L` with `h(x w) = h(x) h|_x(w)`.

For `x ∈ X` let `H_x = Stab_L(x)`, which has finite index in `L`, and let
`φ_x: H_x → L`, `h ↦ h|_x`, be the section homomorphism. Then some `φ_x` is neither
injective nor of finite image.

**Equivalent form.** Suppose every homomorphism from a finite-index subgroup of
`Mod(S_g)` to `Mod(S_g)` is either injective or of finite image. Then no finite-index
subgroup of `Mod(S_g)` is self-similar, faithfully, over a finite alphabet.

## Why it matters for Boone--Higman

- By Zaremsky (`fp-self-similar-groups-embed-in-fp-simple-groups`), a finitely presented
  self-similar group embeds in a finitely presented simple group. Finite-index subgroups
  of `Mod(S_g)` are finitely presented.
- So this theorem says what that route needs for the open part of BBMZ Problem 5.3(2):
  a homomorphism from a finite-index subgroup of `Mod(S_g)` into `Mod(S_g)` with infinite
  image and nontrivial kernel.
- Such a homomorphism exists if some finite-index subgroup of `Mod(S_g)` surjects onto
  `Z`. Map `Z` onto the cyclic group generated by a Dehn twist. Whether such a surjection
  exists is Ivanov's question on virtual infinite abelianization, open for `g ≥ 3`. It is
  recalled here, not re-checked.
- The theorem does not exclude hosts `V_d(G)` in which `L` sits inside a larger
  self-similar group `G`. Then the sections of elements of `L` lie in `G`, not in `L`.

## Analogy

Compare Kapovich (arXiv:0809.0323, abstract only): an irreducible lattice in a
semisimple algebraic group admits a faithful self-similar action on a rooted tree of
finite valency if and only if it is virtually arithmetic.
- In that setting the Margulis normal subgroup theorem gives the dichotomy "injective or
  finite image" for homomorphisms of finite-index subgroups. The discreteness of the
  commensurator of a non-arithmetic lattice plays the role that Irmak rigidity plays here.
  This is an analogy only; Kapovich's proof was not read.
- For `Mod(S_g)` the normal subgroup property fails (for example, the Torelli group is an
  infinite normal subgroup of infinite index). So here the dichotomy is an open
  hypothesis rather than a theorem.
