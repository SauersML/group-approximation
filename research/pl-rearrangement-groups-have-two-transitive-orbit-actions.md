(elementary; not independently reviewed; no novelty claimed, since these are
the standard Thompson-group arguments written for a general slope group).

## Setting

Let `A ⊆ R` be a subring and `P ≤ R_{>0}` a finitely generated multiplicative
subgroup whose elements are units of `A`. Assume:

- **(S)** every positive element of `A` is a finite sum of elements of `P`;
- **(R)** `1 = q + q'` for some `q, q' ∈ P`.

Let `V(A,P)` be the group of bijections `g` of `[0,1)` for which there are
`0 = x_0 < x_1 < ... < x_n = 1` in `A`, `p_i ∈ P` and `c_i ∈ A` with
`g(x) = p_i x + c_i` for `x ∈ [x_{i-1}, x_i)`. Put `O = A ∩ [0,1)`.

## Statement

1. `V(A,P)` acts faithfully and 2-transitively on `O`.
2. The stabilizer of `0` is generated by finitely many elements together with a
   subgroup isomorphic to `V(A,P)`. So point stabilizers are finitely generated
   whenever `V(A,P)` is.
3. Hence the action on `O` is of type (A) exactly when `V(A,P)` is finitely
   presented. In that case every subgroup of `V(A,P)` satisfies permutational
   Boone--Higman, has solvable word problem, and embeds in a finitely presented
   simple group.

## Instances whose hypotheses are checked in the proof

- `A = Z[1/2]`, `P = <2>`: Thompson's `V` (a sanity case, already known).
- `A = Z[1/6]`, `P = <2,3>`: Stein's rearrangement group of `[0,1)`.
- `A = Z[τ]`, `τ = (√5 − 1)/2`, `P = <τ>`: the irrational-slope rearrangement
  group.

Whether these particular groups are finitely presented is not part of this
claim; it is imported separately where used.
