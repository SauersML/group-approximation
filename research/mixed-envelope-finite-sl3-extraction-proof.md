Put `K=A-hat`.  Fourier transform identifies `(MFE1)` with the corresponding
Haar algebraic crossed product.  By
`haagerup-factor-direction-strips-from-property-t-envelope`, the assumed
embedding forces

```text
A rtimes H
```

to be non-hyperlinear.

Enumerate `A={b_1,b_2,...}` and set

```text
A_n = sum_(i<=n) F_p[H] b_i.
```

Then `A_n rtimes H` is a directed increasing family with union `A rtimes H`.
Hyperlinearity is local: if every `A_n rtimes H` were hyperlinear, every
finite subset of `A rtimes H` would lie in a hyperlinear stage and would have
unitary microstates there, making the union hyperlinear.  Hence some stage
`A_n rtimes H` is non-hyperlinear.  Taking the nonzero/redundancy-free
generators among `b_1,...,b_n` gives `(MFE2)` and `(MFE3)`.

The semidirect product is generated by the chosen module generators together
with a finite generating set of `H`.  To see recursive presentability, map a
finite-rank free `F_p[H]`-module onto `A_0` by sending its basis to the chosen
`b_i`.  Because `A` is recursively presented, the module words mapping to
zero can be enumerated by enumerating formal consequences of the presentation
of `A`; this enumerates the kernel and hence a module presentation of `A_0`.
Adjoin the recursively enumerable module relations and the conjugation rules
to a recursive presentation of `H`.  This gives a finite generating set and
an r.e. relator set for `A_0 rtimes H`.

Higman's embedding theorem places every finitely generated recursively
presented group in a finitely presented group.  Since subgroups of
hyperlinear groups are hyperlinear, a finitely presented supergroup
containing `(MFE3)` cannot be hyperlinear.
