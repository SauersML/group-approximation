Complete proof: artifact Sections 1 and 3.

- **Local finiteness.** Products of `n` strictly upper triangular matrices vanish, so finitely many
  elements of `UT_n(R)` lie in `1 + B` for a finite-dimensional nilpotent `F_p`-algebra `B`, a finite
  group.
- **The model.** Exhaust `L` by finite subgroups `P_1 <= P_2 <= ...`, and take the left regular
  representations of the `P_k` in the rank ultraproduct of `M_(|P_k|)(F_p)`. They are eventually
  multiplicative on any pair of elements. `F_p[P_k]` is free over `F_p[Q]` for finite `Q <= P_k`, so
  normalized ranks equal the free profile of `Q`.
- **Value.** `<x_12(1), x_23(1)>` is the Heisenberg group over `F_p`. For `p = 2` the right ideal
  generated by `(y-1)(x-1)` in `F_2[D_8]` has dimension `3`, as in
  `finite-subgroup-rank-data-cannot-force-two-root-identity`.
- **Corner family.** From `T_i S_j = delta_ij e` and `S_0 T_0 + S_1 T_1 = e`, the `S_i T_i` are
  orthogonal idempotents, each of rank at least `rk(e)`, so `rk(e) >= 2 rk(e)`. This is the argument
  of `leavitt-algebra-has-no-unital-rank-model`. With `rk(e) = rk(N_23 N_12) = 3/8` in the regular
  model, no derivation from positive-root data can produce such a family.
