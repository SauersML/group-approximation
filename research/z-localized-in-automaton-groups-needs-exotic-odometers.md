## Setting

- **Tree and periods.** Notation as in `automaton-groups-see-only-smooth-periods`: the `m`-ary tree `T_m`, and
  `π(x)` for the eventual period of an eventually periodic ("rational") point `x`.
- **Period-smooth maps.** A homeomorphism `h` of `X^ω` is *`s`-period-smooth* if it maps rational points to rational
  points with `π(h x) | π(x)·N` for some `s`-smooth `N`. By that node, a finite-state automorphism with at most `s`
  states is `s`-period-smooth.
- **Rough part.** For a rational point `x`, `ρ_s(x)` is the part of `π(x)` coprime to every prime `<= s`.

## Theorem

1. **Rough-period invariant.** Let `Γ` be generated by `s`-period-smooth maps whose inverses are also
   `s`-period-smooth. Then `ρ_s(g x) = ρ_s(x)` for every `g ∈ Γ` and every rational `x`. The partition of rational
   points by the large primes of their periods is `Γ`-invariant.
2. **Additive embeddings die.** Let `Γ` be an automaton group and `M(Γ)` the orbit span of
   `adder-hosts-linearize-odd-odometer-roots`. For every nonzero `ψ ∈ M(Γ)`, `ψ/ℓ ∈ M(Γ)` holds for only finitely
   many primes `ℓ` with `ord_ℓ(m)` a prime `> s`. So no subgroup of `M(Γ)` is isomorphic to `Z_(S)`.
   - This holds for every embedding into `M(Γ)`, not only for constants.
   - So an adder host `M(Γ) ⋊ Γ` over an automaton group can contain `Z_(S)` only with nontrivial image in `Γ`.
3. **Odometers with a finite-state orbit coding die.**
   - **The orbit coding.** Let `a ∈ Γ` (an automaton group), and let `x` be a rational point whose `⟨a⟩`-orbit
     closure `Y` is infinite.
     - `a` permutes the level-`n` prefixes of `Y` in a single cycle of length `c_n`, with `c_n | c_(n+1)` and
       level radices `r_n = c_(n+1)/c_n ∈ {1, …, m}`.
     - So `C(λ) = a^λ x` is a homeomorphism from the mixed-radix odometer `Z_(r)` onto `Y`, with `C(0) = x`. It
       conjugates `+1` to `a` and maps level-`n` digit cylinders onto level-`n` cylinders of `Y`. Levels of radix 1
       carry the digit 0.

     This *canonical orbit coding* depends only on `(a, x)`.
   - **Hypothesis.** The radices are eventually periodic, with product `N` over one period, and `C` is
     finite-state: it has finitely many sections, counted together with the radix phase.
   - **Conclusion.** There is `t` such that `a^(1/ℓ) ∉ Γ` for every prime `ℓ` with `ord_ℓ(N)` a prime `> t`. By
     Zsigmondy there are infinitely many such `ℓ`, so `Z_(S)` does not embed in `Γ` with `1 ↦ a`.
   - **Cases covered.** Every `a` conjugate to the standard odometer by a finite-state automorphism: translations,
     odd affine maps and all their finite-state conjugates.
4. **The open shape.** Two facts hold for any `a`. The only `ℓ`-th root of `a` in `Aut(T_m)`, for a prime
   `ℓ > m`, is the profinite root `a^(1/ℓ)` in the closure of `⟨a⟩` (see the proof). And by item 1, `a^(1/ℓ)` keeps
   the rough period of every rational point. So an automaton-group witness of `Z_(S)` with `1 ↦ a` needs all of the
   following:
   - every `a^(1/ℓ)`, `ℓ ∉ S`, lies in the finitely generated `Γ`;
   - for every rational point with infinite orbit closure, the canonical orbit coding is infinite-state, or has
     radices that are not eventually periodic;
   - in that coding, `C(1/ℓ)` has the rough period of `C(0)`. In every finite-state coding the digits of `1/ℓ`
     have period divisible by `ord_ℓ(N)`.

   No example and no refutation are known.
5. **What a non-finite-state generator must look like** (translation-type witnesses, which are what
   `natural-q-rtimes-2-in-2v-h-iff-odd-odometer-roots` needs). Let a finitely generated `H <= Aut(T_m)` contain
   `τ_(1/ℓ)` for all primes `ℓ` outside a finite set, as a translation copy of `Z_(S)` does. For (a) a single
   `ℓ ∤ m` is enough. Then its generating set contains:
   - **(a) a generator of positive-density activity.** Some generator `h` has `α_h(n) >= m^n / C` for infinitely
     many levels `n`. This excludes bounded, polynomial and subexponential activity, and every activity that is
     `o(m^n)`, finite-state or not;
   - **(b) a prime-creating generator.** Some generator is `s`-period-smooth for no `s`: it sends a rational point
     to an irrational one, or multiplies periods by unboundedly large primes;
   - **(c) a generator whose pieces are not all Möbius over one finitely generated ring**
     (`automaton-groups-see-only-smooth-periods`, item 3).

## What this says about the known families

- **Automaton groups** (contracting, bounded, polynomial, Aleshin-type, lamplighters, affine) fail (b), and fail
  (a) or item 3 as well.
- **Nekrashevych's bounded non-finite-state groups** fail (a).
- **The rational Iwahori group and other Möbius hosts** fail (c).

So every known self-similar family is excluded as a translation-type (SL) witness. A witness needs a generator that
is dense, prime-creating and non-Möbius.

## Lesson for general BH

**Large primes in periods are a conserved quantity of local rules.**
- Finite-memory maps, and more generally period-smooth maps, cannot create a large prime in the period of a
  rational point.
- So a group generated by such maps preserves the rough period.
- Divisibility by a prime `ℓ` always needs one: some point must move to a point of period `ord_ℓ(m)`.

`GL_n(Q)`, and every input containing `Q`, therefore needs a host with a genuinely prime-creating move. That is
infinite data in the generators, not a clever arrangement of finite automata.
- Additive or linearly coded divisibility dies against the invariant.
- Only an exotic, infinite-state odometer coding could hide the primes, and item 4 is its exact shape.

This is the automaton-group case of the carrier principle, in which complexity must sit in the acting group's
infinite data.
