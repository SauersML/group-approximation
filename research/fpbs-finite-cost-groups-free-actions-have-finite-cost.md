**OPEN.** Let `Γ` be a countably infinite group with `C(Γ) < ∞`, that is, with
some free p.m.p. action of finite cost. Prove that every free p.m.p. action of
`Γ` has finite cost.

**Why it matters.**
- It is the condition (U) of Theorem B of the passage artifact, asked for all
  groups of finite cost at once.
- With `fpbs-countable-groups-have-fixed-pseudocost`, it gives
  `fpbs-fixed-price-universal` (route
  `fpbs-fixed-price-from-fixed-pseudocost-and-finite-costs`).
- It is implied by `fpbs-fixed-price-universal`.

**Where it can fail.**
- Finitely generated groups satisfy it trivially: a finite generating set `S`
  gives cost at most `|S|`.
- A counterexample is not finitely generated, and it has a free action `b`
  with `C(b) = ∞` although `C(Γ) < ∞`.
  - If `Γ` has fixed pseudocost, then `PC(b) = C(Γ) < C(b)` by Tucker-Drob
    Cor 6.8(1), applied to a near-optimal free action, and Prop 6.26(1). This answers Tucker-Drob Q 7.6 positively.
  - `E_b` is then not treeable (Cor 6.8(2)).
- By `fpbs-fixed-price-amenably-generated-from-fg`, under fixed price for
  finitely generated groups, a counterexample is not generated by finitely many
  amenable subgroups.

## Attempts

**Transfer of finite cost between free actions (September 17, 2026, b-t-nonamgen).**
- Tried: pass from a finite-cost free action `a` to an arbitrary free `b`
  through the diagonal `a × b`. Alternatively, go through weak containment:
  `s_Γ` is weakly contained in `b` (Abért–Weiss).
- Where it dies: `a × b` factors onto `b`, but cost does not descend along
  factor maps. A graphing of `E_{a×b}` pushes forward to a generating set of
  `E_b` only with multiplicities, and with no bound on its cost. Weak
  containment moves costs only through the upper semicontinuity of Tucker-Drob
  Cor 6.20, which bounds pseudocosts of `b` from above by that of `s_Γ`. It
  says nothing about `C(b)` once `E_b` is not treeable.
- Not tried in earnest: building a graphing of `E_b` directly from one of
  `E_a`. For a group that is not finitely generated, that needs infinitely
  many group elements carried on sets of summable measure in `b`, and nothing
  in `a` controls where `b` can place them.

**Stagewise tail transfer along weak containment (September 18, 2026,
swarm-0917-w8-w8-fp-follow, quantifier-shift).**
- **Result.** [[fpbs-finite-cost-transfers-up-weak-containment]] (route
  `fpbs-finite-cost-transfers-up-weak-containment-proof`, artifact
  `research/artifacts/fpbs-finite-cost-transfers-up-weak-containment-2026-09-18.md`).
  For every countable `Γ`, if `a` is free and `a ≼ b`, then `C(b) ≤ C(a)`,
  also relative to any subgroup. This answers Burton–Kechris Problem 7.4.
- **How the obstruction above is met.** Exhaust `Γ` by `H_n`. The pieces of a
  finite-cost graphing of `E_a` with labels outside `H_n` have mass tending to
  0. Each stage transfers only its tail, with its own conjugator, relative to
  the relation `E_{b|H_{n_k}}` already built in `b`. No single transfer of the
  infinite graphing is needed.
- **Reduction.** With Abért–Weiss, `C(a) ≤ C(s_Γ)` for every free `a`. So this
  node is **equivalent** to: `C(Γ) < ∞` implies `C(s_Γ) < ∞`, for the Bernoulli
  shift alone. A counterexample is exactly a group of finite cost whose
  Bernoulli shift (equivalently, every free action weakly contained in it) has
  infinite cost.
- **Test case.** For `Gamma_mal`, this node and fixed price are both
  equivalent to the failure of `fpbs-mal-bernoulli-single-stage-floor`
  ([[fpbs-mal-fixed-price-iff-bernoulli-finite-cost]]).
- **What stays open.** Finiteness of Bernoulli cost itself. Weak containment
  now carries finite cost upward only, from `s_Γ` to all free actions, and
  nothing here gives `C(s_Γ) < ∞`. The node stays OPEN.
