Let `U = L_(F_2)(1,2)^x`. `U` has no proper finite-index subgroup, so its profinite topology is
indiscrete and every proper subgroup is non-closed.

**Consequence, and its scope.** Closedness measured in the profinite topology of `U` itself filters
nothing: every proper subgroup fails it. That is all this claim shows. The localization theorems
constrain the subgroup generated by a witness, not the ambient group:
- `kun-thom-wreath-deficit-witnesses-need-nonclosed-stabilizers` measures closedness in the profinite
  topology of `pi(K)` for the subgroup `K` generated by a witness. Over `U` the relevant subgroups can
  be residually finite, where closedness is a genuine condition.
- `sofic-radical-localizes-bernoulli-deficit-witnesses` constrains `Rad(K)`, which is nontrivial only
  for nonsofic `K`. So invisibility is not automatic at the level of a witness, even though
  `Rad(U) = U`.

So this claim removes only the ambient version of the non-closed-stabilizer filter. The witness-level
filters still bite over `U`, in the same shape as the nonsoficity requirement on the combining window
in `single-maximal-stage-routed-codes-cannot-compress`.
