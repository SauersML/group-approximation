**Setting.**
- `Q` is the quiver with vertices `e, f`, a loop `α` at `e`, a loop `δ` at `f`, an arrow `β` from
  `e` to `f` (so `β = eβf`) and an arrow `γ` from `f` to `e`.
- `R_+ = F_2 Q` is its path algebra, and `Γ = E_20(R_+)`.
- Wave 22 (lane w22-nh-last1, node leavitt-st20-is-a-quotient-of-its-kazhdan-cone-half) shows
  that `Γ` surjects onto `Delta = St_20(L_(F_2)(1,2))`. Its item 6 says that flexible
  HS-stability of `Γ` (the "stable half") makes `Delta` non-hyperlinear.
- For a two-sided ideal `J` put `A = R_+/J` and `Γ_J = Γ / E_20(R_+, J)`. Here `E_20(R_+, J)` is
  the normal closure of `{x_ij(a) : a in J}`.
- The canonical map `St_20(A) ->> E_20(A)` factors through `St_20(A) ->> Γ_J ->> E_20(A)`.

Flexible HS-stability is Dogon's notion: normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm, `D_n >= d_n` and
`D_n/d_n -> 1`.

**Theorem.** Suppose `Γ` is flexibly HS-stable.

1. **Transfer to every finitely presented quotient.** For every finitely generated two-sided ideal
   `J` of `R_+`, `Γ_J` is flexibly HS-stable.
   - The proof uses a head bound: if `J` is generated by `m` elements, `F_J` is the finite set
     `{x_ij(a_k)}` and `λ` is any finite-dimensional unitary representation of `Γ`, then
     ```text
     max_(x in F_J) ||λ(x) - 1||_2 <= δ   ==>   codim V^(E_20(R_+,J)) <= (δ/κ_m)^2 dim V.
     ```
   - The constant `κ_m > 0` depends only on `m`. It is the Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain Kazhdan constant
     for `E_20` over a ring with `5 + m` generators.
2. **Dichotomy.** Consequently each `Γ_J` is residually finite or not hyperlinear.
3. **Coverage.** Let `B` be any finitely presented `F_2`-algebra generated by two elements `u, v`.
   - The map `e -> E_11`, `f -> E_22`, `β -> E_12`, `γ -> E_21`, `α -> u E_11`, `δ -> v E_22` is
     a surjection `R_+ ->> M_2(B)` with finitely generated kernel.
   - So `E_20(M_2(B)) = E_40(B)` is a quotient `E_20(A)` as in item 1.
   - Every finitely presented `F_2`-algebra `C` has `M_k(C)` 2-generated for large `k`, so every
     `E_(40k)(C)` occurs.
4. **Retract.** `t -> α + δ` and `α -> t`, `β, γ, δ, f -> 0` exhibit `SL_20(F_2[t]) = E_20(F_2[t])`
   as a retract of `Γ`. So `SL_20(F_2[t])` is flexibly HS-stable.
5. **Embedded non-RF groups (conditional on centrality).** Let `G` be finitely presented and
   superperfect (`H_1 = H_2 = 0`), and take `C = F_2[G]`, `B = C` if `C` is 2-generated
   (otherwise `B = M_k(C)`), and `A = M_2(B)` in item 3.
   - The map `g -> diag(g, 1, ..., 1)` embeds `G` into `E_20(A)` (Whitehead lemma).
   - Assume `(Z)`: the kernel of `St_20(A) -> E_20(A)` is central. Then the embedding lifts to
     `St_20(A)`, because a central extension of a superperfect group splits. Pushing the lift to
     `Γ_J` gives `G ↪ Γ_J`.
   - So under `(Z)`, `Γ_J` is non-hyperlinear whenever `G` is not residually finite. Examples:
     - Thompson's group `V` (simple and acyclic, Szymik–Wahl);
     - every finitely presented infinite simple acyclic group.
   - `(Z)` holds in the stable limit, where `K_2` is the centre of `St`. It is not verified here at
     level 20.

## What this changes

- **The stable half is not a statement about Delta.**
  - It proves non-hyperlinearity at once for:
    - `Delta`;
    - `E_N` of every other simple infinite finitely presented `F_2`-algebra, for example every
      `L_(F_2)(1,n)`;
    - under `(Z)`, the groups `Γ_J ⊇ V` over `F_2[V]`;
    - every other non-residually-finite `Γ_J`.
  - It also forces flexible stability of `SL_20(F_2[t])`.
  - So it is a universal characteristic-2 stability principle for finitely presented Steinberg
    quotients, not a Leavitt-specific input.
- **Opposite answers across routes.**
  - The Kun–Thom route to Pestov's question prefers instability of `SL_r(F_q[t])`, which is the
    one-variable reduction inside `el-r-polynomial-vertex-not-flexibly-hs-stable`.
  - The stable-half route needs stability of `SL_20(F_2[t])`.
  - At `(q, r) = (2, 20)` at most one of the two can hold.
- **Refutation target, made explicit.** One finitely presented `F_2`-algebra `C` with `Γ_J`
  hyperlinear and not residually finite kills the stable-half route (item 6 of wave 22).
  - The natural candidate is a Deligne-type phenomenon over characteristic-2 `S`-integers, such as
    `C = F_2[t, 1/(t^2+t+1)]`. There a Hilbert symbol at the degree-2 place gives a class in `K_2`
    that finite quotients may miss.
  - Both halves remain open here:
    - non-residual-finiteness of the central extension, which needs a function-field form of
      Deligne's theorem;
    - its hyperlinearity, since central extensions of linear groups by finite groups are not known
      to be hyperlinear in general.
- **What survives.** `(PR)` itself, rounding only of the pullbacks of asymptotic representations of
  `Delta`, is untouched. It is equivalent to non-hyperlinearity of `Delta`. What this node removes
  is the hope that the uniform sufficient condition "the half is stable" is cheaper than the
  endpoint: it is at least as strong as simultaneous non-hyperlinearity of the whole
  characteristic-2 family in item 5.
