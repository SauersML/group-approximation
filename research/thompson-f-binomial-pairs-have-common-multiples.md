**ESTABLISHED.** Let `K` be a field and `R = K[F]`.

1. **Augmentation binomials.** For all `a, c` in `F \ {1}`,
   `(1 - a) R ∩ (1 - c) R ≠ 0`.
2. **Intersections through conjugates.** For `h` in `F` and `b` in `R`, if `v` lies
   in `(1 - t^-1 h t) R` for every `t` in `supp b`, then `b v` lies in `(1 - h) R`.
3. **Corollary.** For every `h` in `F \ {1}` and every nonzero `b` in `R` whose
   support has at most two elements, `(1 - h) R ∩ b R ≠ 0`. The coefficients of `b`
   are arbitrary.

**Consequence for the roots.** By `thompson-f-amenable-iff-group-ring-is-ore`, a
proof of `thompson-f-is-not-amenable` through the Ore condition needs a pair with no
nonzero common right multiple. Item 1 excludes the pairs `(1 - a, 1 - c)`, and item 3
excludes `1 - h` paired with any binomial.

**Scope.**
- Item 1 follows directly from Brin--Squier and Lyndon's relation sequence. It is very
  likely folklore, and novelty is not claimed.
- Twisted binomials `1 + lambda a` and `1 + mu c` reduce to item 1 by the ring
  automorphism `g -> chi(g) g` whenever `a -> -lambda`, `c -> -mu` extends to a
  homomorphism `<a, c> -> K^x`. They are not claimed in general.
- Item 3 extends to supports of any size whenever the conjugates `t^-1 h t`
  (`t` in `supp b`) have augmentation binomials with a common right multiple. For three
  or more conjugates that is open; see
  `thompson-f-one-minus-x1-is-ore-with-every-element`.
