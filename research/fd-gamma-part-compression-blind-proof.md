Write `u = pi(t)`, `Gamma' = t^(-1) Gamma t >= Gamma`, and for a subgroup
`Lambda` of `G` let `K_Lambda` be the closure of the sum of all
finite-dimensional `L_Lambda`-invariant subspaces of `H = L^2(N)`.

**`e_(K_Lambda)` lies in `N` and in `pi(Lambda)' cap N`.**  If `F` is a
finite-dimensional `L_Lambda`-invariant subspace and `x in N`, then `F x`
(right multiplication) is finite-dimensional and `L_Lambda`-invariant, since
right multiplications commute with left multiplications.  So `K_Lambda` is a
closed right `N`-submodule of `L^2(N)`, and the orthogonal projection onto a
closed right `N`-submodule is left multiplication by a projection of `N`
(the commutant of the right action is the left action).  For `lambda in
Lambda` the unitary `L_lambda` maps `K_Lambda` into itself, and so does
`L_(lambda^(-1))`, hence `L_lambda K_Lambda = K_Lambda` and
`pi(lambda) e = e pi(lambda)`.  Likewise left multiplication by any
`x in pi(Lambda)' cap N` commutes with `L_Lambda` and maps finite-dimensional
invariant subspaces to finite-dimensional invariant subspaces, so
`x e = e x`.

**Monotonicity.**  `Gamma <= Gamma'`, and a finite-dimensional
`L_(Gamma')`-invariant subspace is `L_Gamma`-invariant; hence
`K_(Gamma') <= K_Gamma`.

**Transport by the compressor.**  Let `V xi = u^* xi u` on `H`, a unitary.
For `gamma in Gamma`, `V L_gamma V^* = L_(u^* pi(gamma) u) = L_(t^(-1) gamma t)`,
so `V` intertwines the action of `Gamma` with the action of `Gamma'` composed
with the isomorphism `Ad(t^(-1)) : Gamma -> Gamma'`.  Therefore `V` carries
finite-dimensional `L_Gamma`-invariant subspaces onto finite-dimensional
`L_(Gamma')`-invariant subspaces and `V^*` carries them back:
`V K_Gamma = K_(Gamma')`.  Combined with monotonicity,

```text
V K_Gamma = K_(Gamma') <= K_Gamma.
```

As elements of `N`, `V e V^* = u^* e u` (indeed `V L_e V^* xi = u^*(e (u xi u^*))u
= (u^* e u) xi`), so `u^* e u <= e`.  Both projections have trace `tau(e)`,
and in a finite von Neumann algebra a subprojection of equal trace is the
whole projection.  Hence `u^* e u = e`, i.e. `e` commutes with `pi(t)`, and
`K_(Gamma') = K_Gamma`, proving (1) and (2).

**The corner.**  Since `e` commutes with `pi(Gamma)` and with `pi(t)`, the
maps `g |-> pi(g) e` restricted to the group generated by `Gamma` and the
compressors form a homomorphism into `U(e N e)`, with `t Gamma t^(-1) <= Gamma`
unchanged.  The algebra `P_e = pi(Gamma)'' e` acts on its standard space
`L^2(P_e) <= e L^2(N) e <= K_Gamma`, which is a direct sum of
finite-dimensional `L_Gamma`-invariant, hence `P_e`-invariant, subspaces.  A
von Neumann algebra with a faithful family of normal finite-dimensional
representations is atomic: a diffuse central summand would map to zero in
every finite-dimensional normal representation (its image would be a
finite-dimensional algebra, which has minimal projections).  So `P_e` is
atomic, and `amenable-kazhdan-models-absorb-compression`, in its atomic
formulation (which invokes only
`atomic-tracial-algebra-endomorphisms-are-automorphisms`, not property (T)),
gives `pi(t^(-1) Gamma t) e <= P_e`.  This is (3).

**The wreath consequence.**  In a model `Phi` of `W`, `E` or `D`, the lamps
(respectively the free lamp, respectively the swapped copy of `Gamma`) lie in
`Phi(Gamma)' cap N`, so `e` commutes with them by (1); `e` also commutes with
`Phi(G)` when `G` is generated by `Gamma` and compressors (infranormality),
and with the second vertex copy of `G` in `D` because the elements
`i_1(t)` of that copy compress the common `Gamma` just as `i_0(t)` do, so
(1) applies to them as well.  Hence `e` is central
for the whole model, and on the corner `e` the commutators
`[h, a_Gamma]`, `h in t^(-1) Gamma t - Gamma`, are trivial by (3).
