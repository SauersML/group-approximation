## Statement

Let `P` be a finite set of odd primes and `P' = P ∪ {2}`. Following Kourovka
17.57 and 17.60, `CT_P(Z)` is the group of permutations of `Z` generated by the class
transpositions `τ_{r1(m1),r2(m2)}` whose moduli `m1, m2` have all prime factors in
`P'`. Here `r(m) = {r + tm : t ∈ Z}` with `0 ≤ r < m`, and `τ` interchanges
`r1 + t m1` and `r2 + t m2` for each `t`, fixing everything else.

1. `CT_P(Z) = [[G_Λ]]`, the topological full group of the groupoid `G_Λ` of the
   one-vertex `k`-graph `Λ = Λ_P` below (`k = |P'|`), acting on `Ẑ = ∏_{p∈P'} Z_p`.
2. `CT_P(Z)` is simple and of type `F_∞`. In particular it is **finitely
   presented**. This answers Kourovka 17.61 ("Is it always finitely presented if P is
   finite?") positively.
3. Concretely, `CT_P(Z)` is the group of all permutations `g` of `Z` for which there
   are two partitions of `Z` into residue classes `r_i(m_i)` and `s_i(n_i)`
   (i ≤ N), with `P'`-smooth moduli, such that `g(r_i + t m_i) = s_i + t n_i` for
   all `i` and `t`.

For `P = ∅` this recovers McDermott's `CT_∅(Z) ≅ V`, since `Λ_∅` is the binary
rose and `[[G_{Λ_∅}]]` is Thompson's `V`.

## The k-graph

- **The category.** `Λ = {(m, r) : m ∈ N_{P'}, 0 ≤ r < m}`, where `N_{P'}` is the
  monoid of positive `P'`-smooth integers, with composition
  `(m, r)(m', r') = (mm', r + m r')`. The degree is `d(m, r) = (v_p(m))_{p ∈ P'} ∈ N^k`.
- **Unique factorization.** If `d(m, r) = a + b`, write `m = m_a m_b`. Then
  `(m, r) = (m_a, r_1)(m_b, r_2)` forces `r_1 = r mod m_a` and `r_2 = (r − r_1)/m_a`,
  so `Λ` is a one-vertex `k`-graph.
- **Finiteness.** It is row-finite with no sources, and `|Λ^{e_p}| = p ≥ 2` edges of
  colour `p`.
- **Infinite paths.** An infinite path is a compatible sequence of residues mod
  `M^n` (`M = ∏ P'`). So `Λ^∞ ≅ lim Z/M^n = Ẑ`, and the cylinder `λΛ^∞` is the
  *box* `B(r, m) = r + mẐ` for `λ = (m, r)`, with `λz ↦ r + m z`.

  Boxes form a basis of clopen sets, and every clopen set is a finite disjoint union
  of boxes. Moreover `B(r, m) ∩ Z = r(m)`, and two boxes meet iff the residue
  classes do.
- **Bisections.** The basic bisection `Z(λ, μ) = {(λz, d(λ) − d(μ), μz)}` acts as the
  *canonical box map* `κ : B(μ) → B(λ)`, `κ(x) = r_λ + (m_λ/m_μ)(x − r_μ)`. On `Z` it
  is `r_μ + t m_μ ↦ r_λ + t m_λ`, which is exactly the class transposition's rule.

## Properties of G_Λ

- **Hausdorff, ample, Cantor unit space.** `G_Λ` is a Hausdorff ample groupoid with
  unit space `Ẑ`, a Cantor set (Kumjian–Pask; standard, not re-read).
- **Effective.** If `Z(λ, μ)` acts as the identity on an open set, the affine map `κ`
  is the identity. So `m_λ = m_μ` and `r_λ = r_μ`, hence `λ = μ`. The interior of the
  isotropy is therefore the unit space, i.e. `G_Λ` is essentially principal (standard
  equivalence for second countable Hausdorff étale groupoids).
- **Minimal.** For `x ∈ Ẑ` and a box `B`, `x ∈ B(x mod m, m)`, and `κ` maps that box
  onto `B`.
- **Purely infinite.** Let `U = ⊔_{i ≤ n} B_i` and let `V` contain a box `B`. Then
  `B` contains `n` disjoint sub-boxes `C_i` (split by 2-digits), and `⊔ κ_i` with
  `κ_i : B_i → C_i` is a compact open bisection with source `U` and range inside `V`.
  - This is Matui 1210.5800, Prop. `pim` (2), which is equivalent to purely infinite
    and minimal.
  - It is also Li 2209.08087's form (§2, the "comparison" paragraph), which gives
    comparison.

So `F := [[G_Λ]]` is the group of homeomorphisms `g` of `Ẑ` that are piecewise
canonical box maps from one box partition onto another. `g` maps `Z` onto `Z`, and
restricting to the dense orbit `Z` embeds `F` in `Sym(Z)` as the group described in
item 3.

## Proof

1. **`CT_P(Z) ≤ F`.** Take `U = B(r1, m1)` and `V = B(r2, m2)` disjoint. The class
   transposition is `κ ⊔ κ^{-1} ⊔ id` on `U ⊔ V ⊔ (Ẑ ∖ (U ∪ V))`, where the last set
   is clopen.
2. **`CT_P(Z)` is normal in `F`.**
   - Let `g ∈ F`, and let `τ` swap `U` and `V` via `κ`. Then `gτg^{-1}` is an involution
     swapping `g(U)` and `g(V)` via `gκg^{-1}`.
   - Compositions of basic bisections are finite unions of basic bisections (finite
     alignment), so `g(U) = ⊔_j C_j` with `gκg^{-1}|_{C_j}` the canonical box map onto
     a box `D_j ⊆ g(V)`.
   - Hence `gτg^{-1} = ∏_j τ_{C_j, D_j}`, a product of commuting class transpositions.
3. **`H_*(G_Λ) = 0`.**
   - Farsi–Kumjian–Pask–Sims, arXiv 1808.07807, Theorem `thm:H computation`: for a
     row-finite single-vertex k-graph with at least two edges of each colour,
     `H_n(G_Λ) ≅ (Z/gcd(N_1, …, N_k))^{binom(k−1, n)}` with `N_i = |Λ^{e_i}| − 1`.
   - Here `N_2 = 2 − 1 = 1`, so `gcd = 1` and every `H_n` vanishes. The theorem notes
     that the answer is independent of the factorization rules.
   - Elementary check of `H_0`: all boxes are equivalent via `κ`, and
     `B = B' ⊔ B''` (2-split) gives `[B] = 2[B]`. So `H_0(G; A) = 0` for every
     coefficient group `A`.
4. **`F` is perfect.**
   - Li, arXiv 2209.08087v2, Cor. `cor:AHConj` (intro form): for minimal ample
     groupoids with comparison and without isolated points there is an exact
     sequence `H_0(G, Z/2) → H_1(F(G)) → H_1(G) → 0`.
   - Both ends vanish by step 3, so `F^ab = H_1(F) = 0`.
5. **`CT_P(Z) = F`, and it is simple.**
   - Matui 1210.5800, Thm. `simple2`: for `G` purely infinite and minimal (essentially
     principal, étale, Cantor unit space), every nontrivial subgroup of `[[G]]`
     normalized by `D([[G]])` contains `D([[G]])`, and `D([[G]])` is simple.
   - By step 2, `CT_P(Z)` is such a subgroup, so `CT_P(Z) ⊇ D(F) = F ⊇ CT_P(Z)`.
     Hence `CT_P(Z) = F`, and it is simple.
   - This reproves Kohl's simplicity theorem for finite `P`.
6. **Type `F_∞`.**
   - Li, arXiv 2110.04505v2, Cor. `cor:OneVertex`: for a monoid given by a finite
     one-vertex higher rank graph with `d^{-1}(ε_j) ≥ 2` for all `j`,
     `F((I_l ⋉ X)_Y^Y)` is `F_∞` for closed invariant `X ⊆ Ω_∞` and
     `Y = X(1; d^{-1}(e_P))`.
   - Take `X = ∂Ω` and `e_P = ∅`, so `Y = X`.
   - `I_l ⋉ ∂Ω` is the k-graph groupoid `G_Λ` (Li [Li21a], as recalled in
     2209.08087 §`sss:HigherRankGraphGPD`; not re-read).
   - So `CT_P(Z) = F` is `F_∞`.
7. **Item 3.** This restates the description of `F` above.

## Referee (bh-ref-kourovka-b, 2026-09-18): PASS, conditional on the cited theorems

**Verdict: PASS.** Lane bh-kourovka's argument is correct. The route to 17.61 is clean: identify
`CT_P(Z)` with a k-graph full group, then apply Li's `F_∞` theorem. The two reading checks raised by
bh-pal-kourovka61 both come out in the node's favor. The result depends on published theorems,
all confirmed in the sources in `gq/src/kep/` and `gq/src/kourovka/`: Li 2110.04505v2
(`cor:OneVertex`), Li 2209.08087 (`cor:AHConj`), Matui 1210.5800 (`pim`, `simple2`) and
Farsi–Kumjian–Pask–Sims 1808.07807 (`thm:H computation`). The quoted statements match the sources.

**Check 1: `Y = X` in `cor:OneVertex` is right, with `𝔢_P = ∅`.**
- Li defines `X(e; 𝔣) = {χ ∈ X : χ(e) = 1, χ(f) = 0 for f ∈ 𝔣}` and writes `a` for `a𝔠`
  (§ "Groupoids arising from left cancellative small categories" and l.880–930).
- `cor:OneVertex` (l.2171) takes `Y = X(1_{P̂}; 𝕕^{-1}(𝔢_P))` for any subset `𝔢_P ⊆ S_P`, and
  `∅ ⊆ S_P` is allowed.
- With `𝔢_P = ∅` we get `Y = X(1) = X`, since the category has one object, so every character has
  `χ(𝔠) = 1`.
- The alternative `𝔢_P = {0}` has `𝕕^{-1}(0) = {1}`, so `Y = X(1; {1}) = ∅`, which gives the
  trivial group. That is not what is wanted.
- Li himself confirms it: for `X = ∂Ω`, `∂Ω(𝔳; 𝔢) = ∅` unless `𝔢 = ∅`, in which case it is
  `∂Ω(𝔳)` (the corollary at l.2082).
- The other hypotheses of `cor:OneVertex` hold:
  - finitely many edges in each degree;
  - `#𝕕^{-1}(ε_p) = p ≥ 2`, with the minimum 2 at colour 2;
  - trivial units, so condition (F) and the `F_∞` isotropy hypothesis are automatic.

**Check 2: `I_l ⋉ ∂Ω = G_Λ` holds.**
- **Source.** Li 2209.08087, `sss:HigherRankGraphGPD` (l.873–875), states it: k-graph groupoids
  "can be identified with boundary groupoids arising from left regular representations of higher
  rank graphs".
- **Direct check for `Λ_P`, needing only that `∂Ω = Λ^∞` for row-finite k-graphs without sources.**
  1. `Λ_P` is singly aligned: two boxes meet in the single box of lcm modulus, by the Chinese
     remainder theorem, or not at all. So every nonzero element of `I_l` is some `λμ^{-1}`, acting on
     `Λ^∞ = Ẑ` as the canonical box map `κ : B(μ) → B(λ)`.
  2. The action of `λμ^{-1}` on finite paths `(m, r) ⊆ B(μ)` is also determined by `κ`: the image has
     modulus `m · m_λ/m_μ` and residue `κ(r)`.
  3. So two elements of `I_l` whose actions agree on a box agree on the corresponding principal
     ideal. Li's germ relation is therefore equality of germs of homeomorphisms, and `I_l ⋉ ∂Ω` is
     effective.
  4. `G_Λ` is effective too (the node's "Effective" paragraph). Both groupoids are the germ groupoid
     of the pseudogroup of finite unions of canonical box maps, so they are isomorphic, and their
     topological full groups are one group `F`.
- The FKPS homology input (step 3) is stated for the same Kumjian–Pask groupoid, so the chain is
  consistent.

**Other steps.**
- Step 1 (inclusion), step 2 (normality) and the effective, minimal and purely infinite
  verifications are correct.
- Step 3: `N_2 = 1` forces `gcd = 1`, and FKPS's hypotheses hold (row-finite, one vertex, at least
  two edges of each colour).
- Step 4: Li 2209.08087 l.734 records that the AH exact sequence applies to all purely infinite
  minimal groupoids. Also `H_0(G; Z/2) = H_0(G) ⊗ Z/2 = 0`.
- Step 5: matches Matui's `simple2`, under Matui's standing assumptions (essentially principal,
  étale, Cantor unit space).
- Restriction to the dense orbit `Z ⊆ Ẑ` is injective, and canonical box maps preserve `Z`.
