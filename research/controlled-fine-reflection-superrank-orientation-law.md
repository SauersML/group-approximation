**ESTABLISHED.**  Let `P` be the two-qubit extraspecial Pauli group, let
`J` be its central involution, and let

```text
G=P semidirect_theta <t | t^2=1>                       (CFS1)
```

be the controlled-Z extension of
`controlled-pauli-corner-has-clifford-groupification`.  Write `S` for the
unique four-dimensional `J=-1` spin module and `C` for the literal
fine-atom controlled-Z reflection on `S`.

Every finite-dimensional representation of `G` on its `J=-1` carrier has
the form

```text
H_-=S tensor (M_+ direct_sum M_-),
rho(p)=S(p) tensor I,
rho(t)=C tensor (I_(M_+) direct_sum -I_(M_-)).           (CFS2)
```

Put `k_+=dim M_+`, `k_-=dim M_-`, `k=k_++k_-`, and define the orientation
correlation

```text
mu=tr_(H_-)(rho(t)(C tensor I))=(k_+-k_-)/k.            (CFS3)
```

Let `E_P,E_G` be the adjoint Reynolds projections onto the commutants of
`rho(P)` and `rho(G)`, respectively, and normalize their ranks by
`dim(H_-)^2`.  Then exactly

```text
c_P=rank(E_P)/(4k)^2=1/16,
c_G=rank(E_G)/(4k)^2=(1+mu^2)/32,
c_P-c_G=(1-mu^2)/32.                                   (CFS4)
```

Thus commutant superrank detects the *amount of orientation mixing*, but it
cannot distinguish the two literal orientations `C tensor I` and
`-C tensor I`: both have `|mu|=1` and zero rank drop.

Polynomial identities are weaker still.  For every multiplicity
involution in `(CFS2)`, the algebra generated by `rho(G)` on the marked
carrier is either `M_4(C)` or `M_4(C) direct_sum M_4(C)`.  Its PI ideal is
therefore always exactly `Id(M_4(C))`.  In particular every
Amitsur--Levitzki/standard-polynomial degree test gives the same answer for
`V=I`, `V=-I`, and a mixed `V`; it cannot authenticate the literal fine
reflection.

There is a sharper canonical-trace consequence.  If the finite subgroup
`G` embeds in an ambient group and the ambient canonical trace is restricted
to the marked Fourier corner `q=(1-J)/2`, then

```text
tr_q(rho(t)(C tensor I))=0.                             (CFS5)
```

Indeed `C` is a linear combination of Pauli words, so every term of `tC`
lies in the nontrivial stable-letter coset `tP`; multiplying by `q` only
replaces such a term by a linear combination of `tp` and `Jtp`, neither of
which is the identity.  Hence `mu=0` and

```text
c_P-c_G=1/32.                                          (CFS6)
```

After finite-group normalized-HS exactification, canonical microstates have
the same conclusion up to `o(1)`.  Consequently the native controlled
reflection packet does **not** authenticate `C tensor I` on canonical
microstates; it canonically selects the balanced mixture of the two
orientations.  The superrank gap is nevertheless a valid matrix-only
endpoint: if an additional Whitehead/mixed return identifies `E_G` with
`E_P` on the same reservoir, `reynolds-rank-gap-forces-hs-holonomy-energy`
charges at least the fixed gap `(CFS6)`.

What this does not provide is that return.  Standard polynomials see no
orientation at all, and superrank sees only `mu^2`; neither turns the native
odd Whitehead transport of one typed corner into an identification of these
two adjoint Reynolds ranges.  The remaining same-reservoir/selector seam is
therefore strictly a mixed-return theorem, not a missing PI calculation.

The exact endpoint and the main algebraic firewall for that theorem are now
isolated.  `balanced-controlled-whitehead-return-checksum` expresses the
return as one finite trace-square overlap and shows that canonical balanced
orientation forces `1/32-o(1)` leakage for **every** actuator.  Conversely,
`orientation-graded-mixed-returns-have-a-balanced-double` proves that any
larger exact presentation retaining the stable-letter `C_2` grading inherits
an exact balanced marked double.  Hence an even-grade Whitehead return cannot
select the missing range; the successful cell must break the orientation
grading or invoke genuinely finite-matrix-only information.
