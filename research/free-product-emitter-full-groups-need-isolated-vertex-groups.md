**Imported, read at source:** Y. de Cornulier, L. Guyot, W. Pitsch, *On the isolated
points in the space of groups*, J. Algebra 307 (2007), arXiv:math/0511714v2 (PDF read
2026-09-18).
- A group is *finitely discriminable* if a finite `Φ ⊆ G \ {1}` meets every nontrivial
  normal subgroup (Prop.-Def. 2.1).
- A group is *isolated* if it is an isolated point of the space of marked groups.
  Prop. 2.2: "A group G is isolated if and only if it is both finitely presentable and
  finitely discriminable."
- Prop. 8: "Infinite residually finite groups and infinite hyperbolic groups are not
  finitely discriminable."
- Cor. 5: an isolated group has solvable word problem. Finitely presented simple groups
  are isolated.
- Thm. 7: isolated groups are stable under extensions and finite-index overgroups.
- Question 4 (p. 12), verbatim: "Does every finitely generated group with solvable word
  problem embed into an isolated group?" The paper notes that the Boone–Higman question is
  stronger.

## Statement

The setting is that of `free-product-boundary-groupoids-carry-the-vertex-group-homology`:
- `k >= 2`, `A = Z/k = ⟨x⟩`;
- `B` infinite and generated by `y_1, …, y_q`;
- `D = A * B` with Bass–Serre tree `T`;
- `X` is the set of ends of `T` together with its `B`-vertices;
- `F = F_B = [[D ⋉ X]]`.

**Theorem.** If `F_B` or `D(F_B)` is finitely presented, then:
- **(a)** `B` is finitely presented;
- **(b)** `B` is finitely discriminable.

So `B` is isolated, and in particular has solvable word problem.

**Corollary 1 (Reid's question).** Reid's `d = 0` group `F_Z` and its simple
commutator subgroup are finitely generated but **not finitely presented**, since `Z` is
residually finite. The same holds whenever `B` is:
- infinite and residually finite, for example `Z^n`, free, surface or f.g. linear groups;
- infinite hyperbolic;
- not finitely presented.

**Corollary 2 (the engine conjecture).** `fp-decidable-vertex-groups-have-fp-stabilizer-engines`
is **false**: `B = Z` is infinite, finitely presented and decidable. The corrected
conjecture needs `B` to be isolated, and it is still open.

## Proof

**Nearby marked groups.** Let `B'` be generated by `y'_1, …, y'_q`. It *agrees with `B`
to radius `ρ`* if every word `w` of length `≤ ρ` satisfies `w = 1` in `B` ⟺ `w' = 1`
in `B'`.
- *The map ι.* Then `w ↦ w'` is a bijection `ι` of `(ρ/2)`-balls, with
  `ι(uv) = ι(u)ι(v)` when `|u| + |v| <= ρ/2`.
- *Extension to `D`.* Call `d ∈ D` *ρ-small* if its normal form has at most `ρ`
  syllables, and each `B`-syllable has length `≤ ρ/4`.
  - `ι` extends syllable by syllable to an injection from the ρ-small elements of `D`
    into `D' = A * B'`.
  - It satisfies `ι(uv) = ι(u)ι(v)` for ρ/4-small `u, v`. Only one junction merge
    survives cancellation, and each merge is decided inside the `(ρ/2)`-ball.
- *Trees and spaces.* Edges of `T` are the elements of `D`, and `ι` respects adjacency
  among small edges. `T'` is the tree of `D'`. `X'` is its ends, together with its
  `B'`-vertices when `B'` is infinite.
- *Two examples.*
  - `B/N` agrees with `B` to radius `ρ` iff `N` misses the `ρ`-ball minus `1`. Then `ι` is
    the quotient map on small elements.
  - `B̃_ρ = ⟨y | relators of B of length ≤ ρ⟩` agrees with `B` to radius `ρ`.

**Step 1 (bounded configurations transfer).** Let `Σ` be a finite set of edges of `T`,
spanning the finite subtree `S`. There is `ρ(Σ)` such that every `B'` agreeing with `B`
to radius `ρ(Σ)` satisfies `β = ∅ ⟺ ι(β) = ∅`. Here `β` is any Boolean combination of
the half-trees of `Σ`, and `ι(β)` is the same combination of the half-trees `h(ιε)` in
`X'`.

*Proof.*
- **Choice of ρ.** Take `ρ(Σ)` so large that:
  - every edge of `S` is ρ/4-small;
  - the `(ρ/2)`-ball of `B` has more than `r` elements, where `r` is the largest number
    of `S`-edges at a vertex.
- **`ι|_S` is an isometric embedding.** It preserves adjacency. It is injective on the
  edges at each vertex:
  - at `A`-vertices, because `A ≤ D'`;
  - at a `B`-vertex `dB`, whose `S`-edges `d b_i` go to `ι(d)ι(b_i)`, because each
    `b_i^{-1} b_j` is short and nontrivial.

  A locally injective map of trees is an isometric embedding.
- **Atoms.** The atoms of the Boolean algebra are the points of `X` in one component of
  `T` minus the open edges of `Σ`. A `B`-vertex endpoint lies on its own side, as in the
  parent's `K = {v} ⊔ ⊔ bH`.
  - Distinct components are separated by a `Σ`-edge.
  - Each component `C` meets `S` in a nonempty connected set, and its side pattern is
    read inside `S`.

  So atoms correspond under `ι`.
- **Nonempty atoms.** `C` contains no point of `X` iff `C` is a single `A`-vertex all of
  whose `k` edges lie in `Σ`.
  - If `C ∩ S` contains a `B`-vertex, then that vertex is a point.
  - Otherwise `C ∩ S` is one `A`-vertex. Each of its edges outside `Σ` leaves `S` and leads
    to ends.
  - The same holds in `T'`. The image `ι(w)` of a `B`-vertex of `S` has `|B'| > r` edges,
    so it has one outside `ι(S)`. `T'` has no leaves, so that edge leads to ends.
  - Edge counts at `A`-vertices are preserved. `∎`

**Step 2 (realized words).**
- **Data.** Each `g ∈ F` has finite data `(β_i, δ_i)`: a clopen partition `{β_i}` with
  `δ_i ∈ D` and `g|_{β_i} = δ_i`. Each `β_i` is a Boolean combination of half-trees,
  which form a subbasis of the compact space `X`.
- **Realization.** Fix such data for a finite `S_0 ⊂ F`, with edges `Σ_0`. Let `Σ_L` be
  the edges `dε`, for `ε ∈ Σ_0` and `d` a product of at most `L` of the `δ_i^{±1}`.
  - For a word `w` of length `≤ L` over `S_0^{±1}`, each itinerary `κ` of pieces gives a
    set `γ_κ`, a Boolean combination over `Σ_L`, on which `w` acts by a product `δ_κ`.
  - Let `ρ(L) >= ρ(Σ_L)`, with all such products ρ/4-small.
  - If `B'` agrees with `B` to radius `ρ(L)`, put `s' = (ι(β_i), ι(δ_i))`.
- **The claim.** Then:
  1. each `s'` is a homeomorphism of `X'`, because its partition relations are emptiness
     statements covered by Step 1, and `ι(δ)·h(ιε) = h(ι(δε))`;
  2. `w' = s'_1 ⋯ s'_m` acts on `ι(γ_κ)` by `ι(δ_κ)`. These sets partition `X'`, and
     `ι(γ_κ) = ∅ ⟺ γ_κ = ∅`;
  3. if `w = d` in `F` with `d ∈ D` small, then `w' = ι(d)`. A nonempty `γ_κ` is an
     infinite clopen set, and nontrivial elements of `D` fix at most two points (parent,
     item 1), so `δ_κ = d`.

  In particular every relation of `F` of length `≤ L` holds among the `s'`.

**Step 3.** Suppose `F = ⟨S_0 | R⟩` with `R` finite.
- **Setup.**
  - Fix words `u_j`, `u_x` over `S_0` representing `y_j`, `x`, and fix `b_0 ∈ B \ {1}`.
  - Let `L` bound the lengths of `R`, the `u_j` and `u_x`.
  - Let `ρ = ρ(L)`, enlarged so that the `ρ`-ball contains `b_0` and three distinct
    elements of `B`.
- **Every agreeing `B'` is a quotient of `B`.** Let `B'` agree with `B` to radius `ρ`.
  - By Step 2, `s ↦ s'` kills `R`, so it defines `ψ : F → Homeo(X')`, with
    `ψ(y_j) = y'_j` and `ψ(x) = x`.
  - `D'` acts faithfully on `X'`. Edge stabilizers are trivial, and `T'` has at least three
    ends and no leaves. So an element fixing every end fixes every line pointwise, hence an
    edge.
  - So `ψ|_B` is a homomorphism `B → B'` with `y_j ↦ y'_j`, and `B'` is a marked quotient
    of `B`.
- **(a) `B` is finitely presented.** Take `B' = B̃_ρ`. The marked maps
  `B → B̃_ρ → B` and `B̃_ρ → B → B̃_ρ` fix the generators, so `B ≅ B̃_ρ`.
- **(b) `B` is finitely discriminable.** Let `N ◁ B` miss the `ρ`-ball minus `1`, and
  take `B' = B/N`. Then `ψ|_D` is the quotient map `D → D'`, since both are homomorphisms
  agreeing on generators. Suppose `N ≠ 1`.
  - Some `1 ≠ n ∈ N` has `ψ(n) = 1`, so `ker ψ` is a nontrivial normal subgroup.
  - By `matui-purely-infinite-minimal-full-groups-have-simple-d` (applicable by parent,
    item 1), `ker ψ ⊇ D(F)`, so `ψ(F)` is abelian.
  - But `ψ(x) = x` and `ψ(b_0) = b̄_0 ≠ 1` lie in different free factors of the faithful
    `D'`, so they do not commute. This is a contradiction.

  Hence `N = 1`, and the `ρ`-ball minus `1` is a finite discriminating set.
- **`D(F)`.** `F_ab` is finitely generated abelian (parent, item 3), hence finitely
  presented. So `D(F)` finitely presented implies `F` finitely presented. `∎`

## What this decides for the emitter gate (IE) of SYNTHESIS v5

1. **Gate 1 is decided negatively.** Reid's group is not finitely presented, and neither is
   its derived group.
2. **The exact necessary condition is isolation.**
   - A finitely presented engine `D(F_B)` needs `B` to be isolated. That covers both of
     the known necessary conditions, finitely presented and decidable, and adds finite
     discriminability.
   - The live engine conjecture must read: every infinite *isolated* `B` gives a finitely
     presented `D(F_B)`.
   - Its first test is now an isolated group, for example Thompson's `V` or `T`, or
     Houghton's `H_3` (isolated, CGP §5.3). It is no longer `Z`.
3. **The route factors through a printed question.** `boone-higman-via-fp-stabilizer-engines`
   needs the vertex group to be isolated and to contain `G`. Clapham's group need not be
   isolated.
   - The corrected route is: `G ≤ B_0` isolated, then `B = B_0 ≀ Z/2`, which is isolated
     by CGP Thm. 7, then the engine.
   - The first step is Cornulier–Guyot–Pitsch Question 4 (2007), which is open and weaker
     than BH.
   - So, granted the corrected engine conjecture, Boone–Higman is **equivalent to CGP
     Question 4**, because BH gives Question 4 via fp simple groups.
4. **Emitter principle** (proved for free-product emitters, expected in general). Call a
   point an *infinite emitter* when its neighbourhoods are the point plus cofinitely many
   branches, permuted simply transitively by its isotropy.
   - An infinite emitter of a finitely presented purely infinite minimal full group has
     isolated isotropy.
   - The proof uses the finite/cofinite branch algebra (Step 1), ball agreement under
     replacing the isotropy by nearby marked groups, and Matui monolithicity. Finite-range
     codings over a free factor at an emitter keep these (not proved here).
   - So emitters transport isolated groups. They cannot convert decidability into finite
     presentation; the emitter half of v5's "scale or sit at an emitter" presupposes CGP
     Question 4.
5. **Track A′ over `Λ₁ ∗ Z`** (expected; `B_1 * B_2` needs the parent's dynamics
   rechecked). `Z` is not isolated, so `Z`-vertices must not be infinite emitters. Their
   ends must be visible, as at the cusps of `T` below.

## Calibration

- **Thompson's `T`.** In the piecewise-`PSL_2(Z)` model on `RP^1`, `T` has parabolic germs
  at cusps, but left and right germs are independent.
  - So one-sided pieces `[N, ∞]` exist, and the cusp is not an infinite emitter. Fillings
    `Z → Z/d` do not preserve those pieces. This is consistent with `T` being finitely
    presented.
  - Compare item 3 of `simple-inputs-have-finitely-presented-stabilizer-engines`: Reid's
    group embeds in an fp SFT full group once each vertex is doubled into `w_±`.
- **Houghton `H_2`.** The same scheme with `Sym(Z/d) ⋊ Z/d` recovers Brown's theorem that
  `H_2` is not finitely presented.
- **Reid's finite family.** The realizations with `B' = Z/d` land in Reid's groups for
  finite `d`. Each map `F_Z → F_{Z/d}` kills the nontrivial element `t^d`.
- **BBMZ.** The singular germs of hyperbolic groups on horofunction boundaries are
  contracting or expanding, never infinite emitters.

## Lesson for general BH

**Isolation principle.** An infinite emitter of a finitely presented full group carries an
*isolated* isotropy group.
- Bounded configurations at an emitter transfer to every nearby marked group of the
  isotropy. So a finite presentation realizes every nearby group as a quotient, which
  gives finite presentation.
- Matui monolithicity rules out proper nearby quotients, which gives finite
  discriminability.

**Consequences.**
- Emitters cannot manufacture finite presentation for a decidable input. They move
  isolated groups, and "every decidable group embeds in an isolated group" is exactly
  Cornulier–Guyot–Pitsch Question 4.
- **Test for every host.** Perturb each germ group in the space of marked groups. If
  bounded configurations persist, that germ group must be isolated.
