**ESTABLISHED.** Let `K` be a computable field (finite, or with decidable
equality and computable field operations). Let `A = K<x_1, ..., x_m> / I` be a
nonzero simple associative `K`-algebra, unital or not, where the two-sided ideal
`I` is generated by finitely many relators. Then there is an algorithm that
decides, for a noncommutative polynomial `w` in the `x_i`, whether `w = 0` in `A`.

This is the necessary condition for the algebra analogue of Boone--Higman
(`algebra-boone-higman-conjecture`). A finitely generated subalgebra of `A`
inherits a solvable word problem, so only algebras with solvable word problem
can have finitely presented simple envelopes.

The argument is Kuznetsov's for simple groups, transcribed to ideals. No
novelty is claimed.
