## Why sufficient

**Bisection calculus.** For a compact open bisection `V`, `1_V * 1_(V^(-1)) = 1_(r(V))` and
`1_(V^(-1)) * 1_V = 1_(s(V))`. So if `X ⊇ U_0 ⊔ U_1` and there are bisections `B_i` with
`s(B_i) = X` and `r(B_i) = U_i`, then `v_i = 1_(B_i)` and `w_i = 1_(B_i^(-1))` satisfy
`w_i v_j = delta_ij 1`, because `1_(B_i^(-1)) * 1_(B_j)` is supported on `B_i^(-1) B_j`, which is
empty for `i != j`. That is (PI), so `full-idempotent-ring-has-properly-infinite-unit` (2 ⇒ 1) and
`full-defect-ring-non-mf-at-rank-two` give triviality from rank 2, for every countable field `k`.

**(F1).** With `F_2 = <a, b>` and `C(g)` the words starting with `g`, `a^(-1) C(a) = ∂F_2 \ C(a^(-1))`.
So `[C(a)] + [C(a^(-1))] = [X]` in `S(𝒢)`, likewise for `b`, and hence `2[X] = [X]`. Assembling the
equidecompositions into two bisections gives (PI). For `d > 2`, the remaining cylinders contain the
`b`-halves, so `2[X] <= [X]`.
- As a `k`-algebra, `R` is generated by `u_(a^(±1))`, `u_(b^(±1))` and the four `1_(C(g))`.
- Every cylinder of a reduced word `g_1 ... g_m` is `g_1 · C(g_2 ... g_m)`.
- `1_(hU) = u_h 1_U u_h^(-1)`.

**(F2).** The germs `B_x` of `w ↦ xw` have `s(B_x) = X^ω` and `r(B_x) = C(x)`, and the `C(x)` partition
`X^ω`.
- `s_x = 1_(B_x)` and `t_x = 1_(B_x^(-1))` satisfy `t_x s_y = delta_xy` and `sum_x s_x t_x = 1`, a unital
  copy of `L_k(1,|X|)`.
- As a `k`-algebra, `R` is generated by the `s_x`, the `t_x`, and the germ indicators of a finite
  generating set of `G`.
- The germ of `(α, g, β)` is `s_α [g] t_β`.

**(T), finite `k` only.** Adding the finitely many elements of `k` to the generators makes `R` a
finitely generated ring. Then `elementary-groups-over-fg-rings-have-property-t` gives property (T) of
`EL_N(R)` for `N >= 3`. For infinite `k`, `EL_N(R)` is not finitely generated (claim node, correction
paragraph), so no (T) clause is asserted.
