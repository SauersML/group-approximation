Use the standard edge coordinates

```text
pi_+(u) ~= pi_-(u)^2,       pi_-(l) ~= pi_+(l)^2.     (SRP1)
```

Put `X=pi_-(u)` and `Y=pi_+(l)`.  The exact first-vertex block form makes
`P` commute with `pi_+(u)` and `Y`; `(SRP1)` therefore gives
`[P,X^2]->0` and `[P,pi_-(l)]->0`.  Since the second copy of `SL_2(Z)` is
generated by `pi_-(u),pi_-(l)`, a second-vertex commutator bounded below can
only remain on `X`, after choosing a subsequence.  The positive-density block
bounds were already retained by the first-block reduction.

Passing to the tracial matrix ultraproduct turns the vanishing edge errors
into equalities.  The two exact vertex representations become `(X^2,Y)` and
`(X,Y^2)`, while `P` commutes with `X^2,Y` but not `X`.  For `V=2P-1`,
`V` commutes with `X^2` and `Y^2`; hence `(VXV,Y^2)` is the conjugate of the
second modular representation by `V` and `(VXV)^2=X^2`.  This proves the
claimed square-root normal form.
