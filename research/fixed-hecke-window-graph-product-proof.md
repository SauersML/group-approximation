Graph-product normal forms imply that the subgroup generated by any vertex
set `F` has no relations beyond the vertex-group and edge relations in the
induced graph.  This proves the first assertion.  Formula `(FWG2)`, its
trace, and `(FWG3)` follow by multiplying complementary sign projections;
the overlap identity is just summing the omitted signs.

For `(FWG5)`, every lamp in `K` commutes with both `c_x` and `c_y`, so `e`
does too.  Put `a=c_xc_y-c_yc_x`.  Then

```text
a* a=2-(c_yc_x)^2-(c_xc_y)^2.                          (FWP1)
```

The Fourier support of `e` lies in the special subgroup on `K`.  Each of
the last two words in `(FWP1)` is a nontrivial reduced word in the disjoint
special subgroup on `{x,y}`.  The special-subgroup intersection theorem
therefore gives

```text
tau(e(c_yc_x)^2)=tau(e(c_xc_y)^2)=0.
```

Taking the canonical trace proves `(FWG5)`.  Since
`[P_x,P_y]_add=(1/4)(c_xc_y-c_yc_x)`, `(FWG6)` follows.

Finally, conjugation in the semidirect product transports every lamp and
every clique PVM exactly.  Enlarging `F` by any fixed collection of
translated shells therefore preserves all stipulated local covariance but
does not alter the special-subgroup calculation above.
