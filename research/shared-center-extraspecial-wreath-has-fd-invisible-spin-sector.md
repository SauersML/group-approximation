**ESTABLISHED ALGEBRAIC WALL, POSITIVE SPIN CORNER, AND FIXED-WINDOW
NORMALIZER THEOREM; NONHYPERLINEARITY OPEN.**

Let a finitely generated group `G` act transitively on an infinite set
`X`.  Define the restricted shared-center extraspecial group

~~~text
E(X)=<J,A_x,B_x (x in X) |
 J^2=A_x^2=B_x^2=1, J central,
 [A_x,B_x]=J,
 [A_x,A_y]=[A_x,B_y]=[B_x,B_y]=1 for x!=y>.             (SCW1)
~~~

Let `G` permute the pairs `(A_x,B_x)` and fix `J`, and put

~~~text
Pi(X)=E(X) semidirect G.                                 (SCW2)
~~~

If `o in X` is fixed, `Pi(X)` is generated by a finite generating set of
`G` together with `A_o,B_o`; indeed `J=[A_o,B_o]` and transitivity
produces every site pair.  The displayed finite-support normal form is
faithful, so `J!=1`.

The common center is invisible in every exact finite-dimensional unitary
representation:

~~~text
pi:Pi(X)->U(d)       implies       pi(J)=I_d.            (SCW3)
~~~

To see this, split into the `J=+1` and `J=-1` eigenspaces.  On the negative
space, every `m`-site subgroup is extraspecial of order `2^(1+2m)` and has
one irreducible `J=-1` representation, of dimension `2^m`.  Hence the
negative-space dimension is divisible by `2^m` for every `m`.  It must be
zero.  This gives a nontrivial central involution killed by every
finite-dimensional representation without any property-(T) hypothesis.

The canonical group trace nevertheless gives that involution a macroscopic
matrix sector.  Put

~~~text
e_-=(1-J)/2.                                             (SCW4)
~~~

Then `tau(e_-)=1/2`.  For every finite `F subset X` of size `m`,

~~~text
e_- C[E(F)] = tensor_(x in F) M_2 = M_(2^m),             (SCW5)
~~~

and the normalized corner trace is the tensor-product matrix trace.  Thus
distinct sites are exactly tensor independent.  For countable `X` the weak
closure of the increasing union in `(SCW5)` is the hyperfinite factor `R`,
and

~~~text
e_- L(Pi(X)) = R crossed_product G                       (SCW6)
~~~

for the tensor-shift action.  Sharing `J` removes the exponentially shrinking
central-sector mass of independent Heisenberg packets.

It also removes every internal actor gauge on a fixed full window.  In an
exact `J=-1` representation of `E(F)` one has

~~~text
H=(C^2)^(tensor F) tensor C^r,
rho(E(F))=M_(2^m) tensor I_r.                            (SCW7)
~~~

If a unitary `U` sends both Pauli generators at every site according to a
bijection `beta:F->F`, then

~~~text
U=P_beta tensor W,                 W in U(r),             (SCW8)
~~~

where `P_beta` is the canonical tensor-leg permutation.  Quantitatively, if
the largest covariance error on the `2m` Pauli generators is `epsilon`,
then some `W in U(r)` satisfies

~~~text
||U-P_beta tensor W||_2 <=2 sqrt(2) m epsilon.            (SCW9)
~~~

The estimate follows by Pauli-twirling onto the commutant and polar
decomposition.  It is independent of `r` for fixed `m`.

The factor `W` in `(SCW8)` is arbitrary.  Exact covariance permits
`P_beta tensor F_r`, where `F_r` is Fourier, and in the packet-product basis

~~~text
inf_(M monomial)||P_beta tensor F_r-M||_2^2
 >=2-2/sqrt(r).                                          (SCW10)
~~~

The same lower bound survives `o(2^m r)` padding.  In a canonical
matrix model of dimension `D`, the negative sector has dimension
`D_- approximately D/2`, and `(SCW7)` gives

~~~text
r=D_-/2^m.                                               (SCW11)
~~~

Every fixed `m` therefore leaves `r->infinity`.  Bounded multiplicity
requires

~~~text
m>=log_2(D_-)-O(1),                                      (SCW12)
~~~

a moving-window rate absent from pointwise hyperlinear convergence.
The shared center rigidifies the spin tensor factor but is Morita-neutral on
the external actor cocycle.

There is a precise sharply two-transitive presentation audit.  In
characteristic different from two, points are equivariantly the involutions.
For a root involution `j`,

~~~text
Gamma=Stab_G(j)=C_G(j),       |Gamma backslash G/Gamma|=2. (SCW13)
~~~

Hence all four off-site commutators among one Pauli pair and a distinct pair
form finitely many `G`-orbits.  If `G` is finitely presented and `Gamma` is
finitely generated, the standard permutational-wreath presentation gives a
finite presentation of `Pi(X)`.  Conversely,

~~~text
Pi(X) finitely presented       implies       G finitely presented, (SCW14)
~~~

because killing the two root packet generators kills their whole normal
`G`-orbit and leaves `G`.

André--Guirardel provide a two-generated simple property-(T) sharply
two-transitive `G`, but the published construction does not claim that `G`
is finitely presented or that `C_G(j)` is finitely generated.  It identifies
`C_G(j)` as a malnormal point stabilizer, not as a controlled
finite-presentation subgroup.  Property (T) of `G` makes the pair
`(G,C_G(j))` relatively property (T) in the formal automatic sense, but
does not give generators for the stabilizer or classicalize the factor
`W` in `(SCW8)`.  Thus sharp two-transitivity collapses the double-coset
relators, but the exact stabilizer-uniformity and multiplicity gates remain.

For the pinned Kun--Thom action, `Pi(X)` contains

~~~text
(direct_sum_X C_2) semidirect G
~~~

as the subgroup generated by the `A_x` and `G`.  Therefore it is nonsofic
whenever that pinned wreath is nonsofic.  Proving `Pi(X)` hyperlinear would
already imply hyperlinearity of the pinned subgroup; proving it
nonhyperlinear still requires a normalized-HS theorem which upgrades the
exact divisibility wall `(SCW3)` to approximate representations.  Fixed
windows cannot do so because `(SCW11)--(SCW12)` allow arbitrarily large
exact spin sectors.  No nonhyperlinear group is proved here.
