**ESTABLISHED** through `mod-s2-jones-rectangular-separating-twist-spectrum-proof`, with a runnable check in
`experiments/jones-genus-two-near-scalar-2026-09-17/jones_tl6.py`. Unreviewed; no novelty claimed for the
representation itself (it is Jones' genus-two rectangular representation), only for the calibration below.

**Setting.**
- `M = Mod(S_2)`, generated by the Dehn twists `s_1, ..., s_5` about the Lickorish chain `c_1, ..., c_5`, which satisfy
  the braid relations. `t_s = (s_1 s_2)^6` is the twist about the boundary of a regular neighbourhood of `c_1 u c_2`,
  a separating curve (2-chain relation).
- `V` is the 5-dimensional Temperley-Lieb module of `TL_6(delta)` on noncrossing perfect matchings of 6 points.
  `A = exp(i(pi/2 + x))`, `delta = -A^2 - A^(-2) = 2 cos 2x`, and `g_i = A + A^(-1) e_i`.
- The Gram form `<m, m'> = delta^(loops(m u m'))` has determinant `delta^5 (delta-1)^4 (delta+1)^4 (delta^2-2)`, so it is
  positive definite for `|x| < pi/8`, which is the *unitary arc*.

**Statement.**
1. **(J1) A family of honest unitary representations.** For every `|x| < pi/8` there are exactly 10 scalars `beta` with
   `rho_(x,beta)(s_i) = beta g_i` a unitary representation of `M`. They differ by tenth roots of unity, which are
   the twists by the characters of `H_1(M) = Z/10`. For generic `x` it has infinite image, because
   `rho(s_1)` has eigenvalue ratio `-A^(-4)`, of infinite order when `x/pi` is irrational.
2. **(J2) Closed-form spectrum.** `rho_(x,beta)(t_s)` has eigenvalues `zeta w^(-4)` (multiplicity 1) and `zeta w`
   (multiplicity 4). Here `w = exp(-24 i x/5)` and `zeta = beta^12 exp(-36 i x/5)` lies in `mu_5`, and all five fifth
   roots occur.
3. **(J3) Near-scalar with infinite image.** `dist(rho(t_s), T I) = 2 sin(gamma/4)`, where `gamma` is the distance from
   `24x` to `2 pi Z`. The nearest scalar is the midpoint of the two eigenvalues. It vanishes exactly at `x = 0` (image `S_6 = Sp_4(F_2)`) and at `x = +-pi/12` (`delta = sqrt 3`). It is
   `O(|x|)` near these points, so representations with infinite image carry an arbitrarily near-scalar separating twist.
4. **(J4) Sharp cube-root gap on the family.** For all `|x| < pi/8`, all 10 rescalings and both signs,
   `|| rho(t_s) - omega^(+-1) I ||_op >= 2 sin(pi/15) = 0.41582...`. Equality holds at `x = 0`. This number is the
   distance from `mu_5` to the primitive cube roots of unity.
5. **(J5) Uniform gap on the tensor closure, in unbounded dimension.** Let `rho` be any finite direct sum of
   representations of the form `chi (x) rho_(x_1) (x) ... (x) rho_(x_n)`, with the tensor factors taken from the family
   or its complex conjugates, `chi` a character, and `n` arbitrary. Then
   `|| rho(t_s) - omega^(+-1) I ||_op >= 2 sin(pi/24) = 0.261...`.
   - *Mechanism.* Scalar-locus attraction: the nearest scalar drifts from `mu_5` by at most `0.3` times the angular
     spread of the spectrum, that is `0.6` times its angular radius.
   - *Why this is not the determinant test.* The dimension here is `5^n`, unbounded, so the w9 determinant test gives
     nothing. A random scan (`tensor_closure_scan.py`, `n <= 4`) never goes below `0.4158`.
   - *Not covered.* Subrepresentations of the tensor products, such as symmetric powers, are not covered.

**What this changes.**
- *Heuristic refuted.* The gap node's evidence line "in quantum representations the separating twist is far from every
  scalar" is true for the tabulated SU(2) level-k representations, but it fails for Jones' rectangular family. Here
  infinite-image honest representations have `rho(t_s)` within any `eps` of a scalar.
- *Class kill (spectral-spread arguments).* Any proof of `mod-s2-exact-representations-have-an-opnorm-torelli-scalar-gap`
  (or of its HS version) that lower-bounds `dist(rho(t_s), T I)` on infinite-image representations is dead. The
  invariant is the distance to the scalar circle, which tends to 0 along `rho_x` as `x -> 0`. It dies at the step
  "infinite image implies Torelli acts non-scalarly by a definite amount".
- *What does work on the family.* The gap is arithmetic, not spectral. The approached scalar is pinned to `mu_5`,
  which is the value set of characters on `t_s`, and (J5) keeps this pinning uniform in dimension.
  - *Candidate invariant.* This points to a replacement for the dead approaches, the *scalar-locus attraction
    constant*: `C(rho) = dist(nearest scalar of rho(t_s), exact scalar values) / dist(rho(t_s), T I)`.
  - *Sufficient condition.* The gap node follows from `sup_rho C(rho) < infinity` over honest representations that
    are near-scalar at `t_s`, together with (ST5), which excludes cube roots as exact values. On the Jones tensor
    closure `C <= 0.6`.
  - *Conditional extension to subrepresentations (sketch, not claimed).* Assume the closure of `rho_x(M)` contains
    `SU(5)`. This is expected for generic `x`, but no citation has been read. Then every irreducible
    subrepresentation of a tensor word in `rho_x` and its conjugate is a Schur module `S_lambda(V)`, up to a character.
    - *Spectrum.* On `S_lambda(V)`, `t_s = zeta diag(a, b, b, b, b)` has eigenvalues `zeta^n a^k b^(n-k)`, with `k`
      filling the whole interval `[lambda_5, lambda_1]`.
    - *Estimate.* Since `n = |lambda|` lies in `[lambda_1 + 4 lambda_5, 4 lambda_1 + lambda_5]`, the drift of the
      midpoint from the pinned value is at most `0.3` times the spread. So the bound `2 sin(pi/24)` would hold on all
      of these subrepresentations too.
  - *Where it may fail.* A counterexample to the gap node therefore cannot come from the tensor category of a single
    Jones rectangular family. It has to mix unrelated families, for example Jones with the SU(2) level-`k` quantum
    representations, or other infinite-image representations whose `t_s` eigenvalue spacings are incommensurable.
    The pinning argument uses the fact that consecutive eigenphases differ by one fixed step (`24x`). That is the
    next place to look.
