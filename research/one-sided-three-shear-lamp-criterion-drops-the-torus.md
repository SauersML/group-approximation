## Setting

- `p` is any prime, `A = F_p[t]`, and `M_+ = A^2` (column vectors).
- `M^_+ = Hom(M_+, F_p)` is identified with `(F_p^2)^N` via
  `<(a,b),(α,β)> = Σ_(k≥0) a_k α_k + b_k β_k`, where `a = Σ a_k t^k`.
- `σ` is the one-sided shift `(σα)_k = α_(k+1)`. It is adjoint to multiplication by `t`, and it is
  not invertible.
- `γ ∈ SL_2(A)` acts by `γ·χ = χ∘γ^-1`. The three generators used here act by the one-sided linear
  cellular automata

| element | action on `(α, β) ∈ (F_p^2)^N` |
|---|---|
| `E_1 = e_12(1)` | `(α, β − α)` |
| `E_2 = e_12(t)` | `(α, β − σα)` |
| `E_3 = e_21(t)` | `(α − σβ, β)` |

- `Γ_0 = ⟨e_12(1), e_12(t), e_21(t)⟩ ≤ SL_2(A)`. It is finitely generated.
- A continuous `φ : C^n → M^_+` is **affinely spanning** if no nonzero `m ∈ M_+` makes
  `x ↦ φ(x)(m)` constant.

## Theorem P (relative (T) for three shears, every p)

The pair `(M_+ ⋊ Γ_0, M_+)` has relative property (T), for every prime `p`.

For `p ≥ 3` the two shears `e_12(1)` and `e_21(t)` already suffice (Remark 2 of the route). For
`p = 2` they do not: `⟨e_12(1), e_21(t)⟩` is then generated by two involutions, so it is infinite
dihedral (or finite) and amenable, and relative (T) fails for it. The third shear is exactly what
`p = 2` needs.

## Theorem Q (the torus-free criterion)

Suppose there are:
- `n ≥ 1` and a continuous affinely spanning `φ : C^n → (F_p^2)^N`;
- elements `g_1, g_2, g_3 ∈ nV` and vectors `v_i ∈ (F_p^2)^N` with
  `φ∘g_i = E_i∘φ + v_i` for `i = 1, 2, 3`.

Then `nV` is not Haagerup. More precisely, `nV` contains a subgroup `M' ⋊ Λ` with
`M' ≤ C(C^n, F_p)` an infinite elementary abelian `p`-group and `(M' ⋊ Λ, M')` with relative (T).
The same holds for any finite family of lifts whose linear parts generate a group containing a
conjugate of `Γ_0` in `GL_2(A)`, or, for `p ≥ 3`, of `⟨e_12(1), e_21(t)⟩`.

## Proposition L (Laurent realizations restrict)

Let `φ : C^n → (F_p^2)^Z` be affinely spanning and let lifts realize affine maps of `(F_p^2)^Z`
whose linear parts contain `e_12(1)`, `e_12(t)` and `e_21(t)`, as in the open claim
`brin-thompson-2v-lifts-el2-laurent-linear-ca-generators`. Then `φ_+ = res∘φ`, where `res` keeps the
entries of index `≥ 0`, satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem Q. The three lifts are words in the given elements of `nV`
(the words whose linear parts are `E_1`, `E_2`, `E_3`).

So Theorem Q's lifting problem is implied by the open Laurent claim. It asks for strictly less:
- three generators instead of five;
- no torus element `h`, so the kill `piecewise-baker-torus-lifts-force-monomial-lamp-linear-parts`
  does not apply (it needs a lift of `h`);
- no invertibility of the shift: `φ` may read only a one-sided window;
- `p = 2` is allowed, the natural prime for the binary group `2V`.

## What this changes

- **The survivor of wave 22 becomes finite.** `piecewise-baker-torus-lifts-force-monomial-lamp-linear-parts`
  left the one-sided variant open because `SL_2(F_p[t])` is not finitely generated (Nagao), so the
  imported relative (T) needed countably many lifts. Theorem P shows that three lifts suffice, for
  every `p`. The one-sided lamp route is now: **lift three explicit one-sided linear CAs through one
  `φ`**. There is no analytic step left and no torus.
- **It is a relaxation of the Laurent problem**, by Proposition L. Any future realization of
  `brin-thompson-2v-lifts-el2-laurent-linear-ca-generators` gives one of Theorem Q, and a kill of
  Theorem Q's lifting problem for a class of `φ` kills the Laurent problem for the same class.
- **`p = 2` is covered.** The two-shear witness of
  `free-linear-part-char-p-affine-pairs-have-relative-t` needs `p ≥ 3`. In `2V` the most natural
  encodings are binary (Proposition B's homeomorphism and the two-box baker are `p = 2`), so the
  prime that the known constructions use now has a finite criterion.
- **An elementary proof** of the instances used. The route is a finite degree count on
  `P^1(F_p((t^-1)))`, checked by the artifact
  `experiments/one-sided-shears-2026-09-17/check_three_shear_sectors.py` (all sector inclusions for
  `p = 2, 3, 5`, 0 failures). It does not depend on the proof file of the wave-20 claim, which is
  not in the graph.

## What is left

1. **The lifting problem of Theorem Q.** Nothing here constructs lifts. The kills that still apply:
   - `char-p-lamp-realizations-with-invariant-measures-die` (Theorem H) should transfer to the
     one-sided setting. Its proof uses only that the affine group is nonamenable (true here by
     Theorem P) and that nontrivial affine maps have Haar-null fixed sets. This transfer is not
     rechecked here. If it holds, the three lifts must jointly distort every measure whose image
     carries a free action, and no homeomorphism `C^n → (F_p^2)^N` can work.
   - The side-split kill of wave 22 does **not** apply: it needs a lift of `h`.
2. **A candidate class kill (spark, not proved).** A hyperbolic element such as
   `u = e_12(1)e_21(t)` (trace `2 + t`) plays the role of `h`. If some lift of `u` had a power that
   is `nV`-conjugate to a piecewise local baker map, one would expect the column rule of wave 22 to
   force every lift to preserve the pair of `u`-eigenlines over `F_p((t^-1))`, hence a virtually
   abelian linear group. The obstacle is that `M_+` is not a free `F_p[u^±]`-module with two
   one-sided halves, so the side split holds only modulo a finite-dimensional error, and the
   recurrence lemma must be run on open sets where lifts may match or swap sides.
