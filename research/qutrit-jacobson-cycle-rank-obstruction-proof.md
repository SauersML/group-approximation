The coefficient evaluation is well defined because the defining relation
`TS=1` maps to `1=1`. Entrywise evaluation is a unital matrix-ring
homomorphism

```text
epsilon_28:M_28(A)->M_28(F_2).
```

If `g` is invertible, then
`epsilon_28(g^(-1))=epsilon_28(g)^(-1)`. Every element of the finite
qutrit packet is a scalar zero-one permutation matrix and is fixed by this
map.

We first compute two similarity invariants. On a packet leaf,

```text
J^r Z_1^q:(u_1,u_2,s) |-> (u_1,u_2,s+r-q u_1).         (QJR1)
```

For `J` itself, the last coordinate is translated by one. For each of the
nine pairs `(u_1,u_2)` this is a three-cycle, and the extra matrix
coordinate is fixed. A three-cycle permutation matrix `P_3` over
`F_2` has a one-dimensional fixed space, so

```text
rank_(F_2)(P_3-I)=2.
```

Consequently

```text
rank_(F_2)(J-I)=9*2=18.                                 (QJR2)
```

Now let `q!=0`. Equation `(QJR1)` fixes a packet leaf exactly when

```text
u_1=r/q.
```

There are three choices of `u_2` and three choices of `s`, hence nine
fixed packet leaves. The remaining eighteen leaves form six three-cycles,
and the extra coordinate is again fixed. Therefore every nonidentity
generator of the noncentral line `<J^r Z_1^q>` satisfies

```text
rank_(F_2)(J^r Z_1^q-I)=6*2=12.                         (QJR3)
```

Suppose first that `H=<J>` and that `H'` is noncentral. Since
`H'=g^(-1)Hg`, the matrix `g^(-1)Jg` is one of the two nonidentity
generators `J^r Z_1^q` or `(J^r Z_1^q)^2` of `H'`, with `q!=0`.
Applying `epsilon_28` gives a similarity over `F_2`:

```text
epsilon_28(g)^(-1) J epsilon_28(g)=J^r Z_1^q
```

after possibly replacing the displayed generator by its square. Similar
matrices have equal ranks after subtracting the identity, contradicting
`(QJR2)--(QJR3)`. If `H` is noncentral and `H'=<J>`, apply the same
argument to `g^(-1)`. This proves preservation of centrality.

For completeness, `H` and `H'` always have the same order, and their only
possible orders are `1,3,9`. The phase-graph criterion handles them as
follows.

| intersection order | restriction pattern |
|---|---|
| `1` | complete graph |
| `3`, both central | complete or empty graph |
| `3`, both noncentral | partial matching |
| `9` | partial matching |

In the last row, the three characters `chi_a` already have distinct
restrictions to `C`. The asymmetric order-three row is the only row that
could be a star, and it has just been excluded for every
`g in GL_28(A)`.

Since `EL_28(A)<=GL_28(A)`, this applies to the entire Jacobson elementary
core generated by the finite packet and the balanced actor. In particular,
adding longer alternating words, additional `S`- or `T`-roots, or scalar
Weyl conjugates cannot change the conclusion. The obstruction is global in
the coefficient host, not a bounded-word exhaustion.
