## Setting

- **The point.** Let `p = α β^∞ ∈ C_2 = {0,1}^ω` be rational, with `β` primitive.
- **Levels.** Put `N_k = [α β^k]` and `A_k = N_k ∖ N_(k+1)`. Each `A_k` is clopen and nonempty,
  and `N_k ∖ {p} = ⊔_(j >= k) A_j`.
- **The shift.** `s : α β^k w -> α β^(k+1) w` is the one-period shift. Its germ generates the
  V-germ group `(V)_p`, which is infinite cyclic: a prefix replacement fixing `p` moves
  prefixes of `p` by multiples of `|β|`, by primitivity.
- **The germs.** Let `H` be a group of germs at `p` of homeomorphisms of `C_2` that fix `p` and
  are V-local on a punctured neighbourhood of `p`. Every germ group at `p` of a finite germ
  extension of `V` is of this kind (Belk--Hyde--Matucci arXiv:2407.03149, §1, conditions 1–2).
- **The spiral group.** `W = C(C_2, Z) ⋊ V` acts faithfully on `C_2 × Z` by
  `(φ, c)·(a, n) = (φ(a), n + c(a))`.

## Statement

1. **Normal germs commute with the shift.** If `(V)_p ⊴ H`, then `H` centralizes `(V)_p`.
2. **Spiral form.** Every germ `g ∈ H` that commutes with `s` is represented, for all large `K`,
   by a unique pair `(ḡ, c) ∈ V(A_K) × C(A_K, Z)` with
   `g(s^m a) = s^(m + c(a)) ḡ(a)` for `a ∈ A_K`, `m >= 0`.
   Here `V(A_K)` is the group of V-local homeomorphisms of `A_K`, which is isomorphic to `V`.
   The map `g -> (ḡ, c)` is an injective homomorphism `C_H(s) -> W` that sends `s` to `(1, 1)`.
3. **Into 2V.** Fix `z ∈ V` of infinite order. Then `(φ, c) -> [(a, y) -> (φ(a), z^(c(a)) y)]` is
   an injective homomorphism `W -> 2V`.
4. **Consequence.** Belk--Hyde--Matucci's germ condition (3) at a rational point, over the base
   `V`, allows exactly two kinds of germ group:
   - a virtually cyclic group (the finite-index case);
   - a subgroup of `W`, and hence of `2V`, containing `(V)_p` as a central subgroup (the
     normal case).

   `W` itself is realized. `C(C_2,Z) ⋊ V` is generated by `V` and one indicator function
   `1_U`, and it acts on the two-point compactification of `C_2 × Z` (a Cantor set) V-locally
   off the two ends, with germ group `W` at each end.

## Proof

- **(1)** Conjugation by a germ `g` fixing `p` preserves attraction to `p`. The germ `s` is
  attracting (`s^m(N_0) ⊆ N_m`, so `s^m(N_0)` shrinks to `p`), and `s^(-1)` is not. So
  `g s g^(-1)`, which lies in `(V)_p = <s>`, must be `s`.
- **(2)**
  - *Equivariance.* Choose `K` so that three things hold: `g s = s g` on `N_K`; `g` is V-local
    on `N_K ∖ {p}` (it has finitely many singular points); and `g(N_K) ⊆ N_0`. Then
    `g(s^m x) = s^m g(x)` for `x ∈ N_K`, `m >= 0`.
  - *The pair.* For `a ∈ A_K`, `g(a) ∈ A_j` for a unique `j`. Put `c(a) = j - K` and
    `ḡ(a) = s^(K-j) g(a) ∈ A_K`, using the inverse of `s` on `N_(k+1) -> N_k` when `j > K`.
    Then `c` is locally constant, since `g` is continuous and the `A_j` are clopen. And `ḡ` is
    V-local, being a composite of V-local maps.
  - *ḡ is a homeomorphism of A_K.*
    - Injective: `ḡ(a) = ḡ(a')` with `j >= j'` gives `g(a) = g(s^(j-j') a')`, so
      `a = s^(j-j') a' ∈ A_K ∩ A_(K+j-j')`, hence `j = j'` and `a = a'`.
    - Surjective: given `b ∈ A_K`, the point `g^(-1)(s^m b)` lies in `N_K ∖ {p}` for `m` large,
      say it is `s^i a`. Then `ḡ(a) = b`.
    - A continuous bijection of a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.
  - *Homomorphism.* Composition in the displayed formula gives
    `(ḡ_1 ḡ_2, c_1 ∘ ḡ_2 + c_2)`, which is the product in `W`.
  - *Injectivity.* `(ḡ, c) = (1, 0)` forces `g = id` on `N_K`.
  - *Independence of K.* Changing `K` conjugates by `s`, and `V(A_K) ≅ V(A_(K+1))` compatibly.
  - *The shift.* `s` goes to `(1, 1)`.
- **(3)**
  - *Homomorphism.* Writing `Φ_i` for the image of `(φ_i, c_i)`:
    `Φ_1 Φ_2 (a, y) = (φ_1 φ_2 a, z^(c_1(φ_2 a) + c_2(a)) y)`, which is the image of the product.
  - *Lands in 2V.* On a clopen partition on which `c` is constant and `φ` is a prefix
    replacement, `Φ` is a product of a prefix replacement with a power of `z`. That is a
    2V-local homeomorphism, and `2V` is full.
  - *Injective.* `Φ = id` forces `φ = id` and `z^(c(a)) = id` for every `a`. Since `z` has
    infinite order, `c ≡ 0`.
- **(4)**
  - *The two cases.* If `(V)_p` has finite index in `H`, then `H` is virtually `Z`. Otherwise,
    in the normal case, (1) gives `H = C_H(s)`, and (2)–(3) apply.
  - *Generation.* `V` acts on `C(C_2, Z)` through its transitive action on proper nonempty
    clopen sets. So the `V`-translates of one `1_U` are all the indicators `1_(U')`, which
    generate `C(C_2, Z)`.
  - *Realizing W.* Code `C_2 × Z ∪ {±∞}` by: `0^(n+1) 1 a` for level `n >= 0`, `1^m 0 a` for
    level `-m`, and `0^∞`, `1^∞` for the ends.
    - The translation `(a,n) -> (a,n+1)` is then V-local everywhere.
    - Each `(φ, c)` is V-local off `{0^∞, 1^∞}`.
    - Its germ at an end is trivial only if `φ = 1` and `c ≡ 0`. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

The germ machine over `V` is *Brin--Thompson-local*: at rational singular points the normal
case of the Belk--Hyde--Matucci germ condition admits only groups of spirals `(φ, c)`, which are
2V-local.

- **What it can reach.** It reaches groups outside `V`, such as `Z^2 * Z`
  (`z2-free-z-lies-in-an-fp-full-germ-extension-of-v`).
- **What it cannot reach.** Every germ ingredient is already in `2V`. To host inputs whose local
  pieces are not Brin--Thompson, a construction must do one of the following:
  - change the base (graph groupoids, contracting RSGs, twisted Brin--Thompson);
  - use irrational singular points, where `Fix_V(p)` must still pass condition (2);
  - use non-normal germ inclusions, which the Belk--Hyde--Matucci theorem does not cover.
- **The design rule.** A universal compiler cannot be a germ extension of `V` with rational
  singular points. It must take its germ groups, or its base, from the input.
