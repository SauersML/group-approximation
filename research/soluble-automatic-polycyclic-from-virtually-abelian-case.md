## Why sufficient

Let `G` be a soluble automatic group. `G` is finitely generated by definition. By the
prerequisite, `G` has an abelian subgroup `B` of finite index.

1. The normal core `N` of `B` in `G` is an intersection of finitely many conjugates of
   `B`. So `N` is a normal abelian subgroup of finite index.
2. `N` is finitely generated, being of finite index in a finitely generated group
   (Schreier). So `N` is a finitely generated abelian group, and in particular polycyclic.
3. `G/N` is finite, and soluble as a quotient of a soluble group. A finite soluble group
   is polycyclic, since its derived series has finite abelian, hence finitely generated,
   factors.
4. An extension of a polycyclic group by a polycyclic group is polycyclic: concatenate
   the subnormal series of `N` with the preimage in `G` of a cyclic series of `G/N`. So
   `G` is polycyclic.
