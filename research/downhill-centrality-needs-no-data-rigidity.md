## A. The abstract downhill lemma (portable)

**Setting.** `Z` is an SFT over a finitely generated `Λ`. Its alphabet has a *flow* layer, a
*data* layer and a *computation* layer. Fix a `D`-family, with flow, data and computation marginals
`P`, `Q` and `C`.
- **(F1)** The flow marginals commute pairwise.
- **(F2)** Every flow marginal commutes with every data marginal.
- **(F3) One-predecessor causal rule.** For each base pattern `p` (flow and data) on the window at
  `z`, either `c(z) = κ(p)`, or `c(z) = β(p, c(z u_p))`. The offset `u_p` depends only on the flow
  part of `p`, and the window lies in `zB_R ⊆ zB_D`.
- **(F4) Downhill potential.** For every flow letter `b` at a site `w` there is `δ_b : Λ → N` with
  two properties.
  - If `δ_b(z) = 0`, then `b` equals a flow marginal at a site of `zB_D`, as an operator. For
    example, by constancy identities.
  - If `δ_b(z) > 0`, then for each flow pattern `p` at `z` with a predecessor, either
    `δ_b(z u_p) < δ_b(z)`, or `p` forces the letter at `w`. The second means `[p] b = δ[p]` in the
    flow algebra, with `δ ∈ {0,1}`.

**Lemma.** Every flow marginal commutes with every computation marginal. With (F1) and (F2), the flow
marginals are central in the algebra generated by the family. **Nothing is assumed about the data
layer.**

*Proof.* Induction on `δ_b(z)`, exactly as Step 1 of `seeded-transport-rigidity-downhill-proof`.
Expand `C_c(z)` by (F3) into three kinds of term.
- **Seed terms `[p]`.** `[p]` is a product of flow and data marginals, and it commutes with `b` by
  (F1) and (F2).
- **Terms that decrease `δ_b`.** They are `[p] C(z u_p)`, and they commute with `b` by induction.
- **Forced terms.** Here `[p] = [data part][flow part]`, and `[flow part] b = δ [flow part]` holds in
  the flow algebra. By (F2), `[p] b = b [p] = δ [p]`. Since `[p]` commutes with `X = C(z u_p)`
  inside the ball, `[p] X b = δ [p] X = b [p] X`. ∎

**Instances of (F4).**
- *End flows.* For `Λ = H × F_m` with the end flow constant along `H`, take
  `δ_b(λ, v') = d(v', w')`. This is Step 1 of that proof, which never used the data.
- *Tree flows in general.* Suppose three things:
  - the predecessor steps `u_p` are edges of a tree `T` on which `Λ`, or the relevant factor, acts;
  - the flow letter at each site is its arrow toward one end of `T`;
  - `b` is constant along the directions that fix the `T`-coordinate.
  Then `δ_b = d_T` works: a step away from `w` puts `w` below `z`, and that forces its arrow.

## B. Seeded transport with input-reading rules

**Setting.** `Λ = H × F_m`, and the base is `Y ⊠ X_∂(F_m)` for an `H`-SFT `Y`, the *input*, with no
rigidity assumed. `Z` is as in `seeded-transport-codings-over-end-flows-are-quantum-rigid`, rules
(Sp), (Ti) and (Se) with bijective `α_s(p, ·)`, where `p` may read `Y` freely.
- **(Surj)** For every `y ∈ Y` and every end `η ∉ Σ_ν`, the pair `(y, η)` has a completion in `Z`.
  It is then unique, since chains end at seeds.

**Theorem.**
1. **The time direction is free of charge.** In every `D`-family (`D >= max(R, 1)`), the flow
   marginals are central. This holds for every input `Y`.
2. **Rigidity is the input's rigidity.**
   - If `Y` is `D`-quantum rigid over `k`, then so is `Z`, for `D >= max(R, r_Y, 1)`.
   - Conversely, under (Surj), if `Z` is `D`-rigid then `Y` is `(D + R)`-rigid.
3. **Minimality by boundary completeness.** Let `Y` be minimal, and suppose every completion over a
   spine end is a limit of anchored configurations. Then `Z` is minimal.

**Proof of 1.** (F1): the flow marginals form a `D`-family for `pt ⊠ X_∂`, which is rigid. (F2):
by constancy, `P_s(λ', w') = P_s(μ, w')` and `Q_a(μ, v'') = Q_a(μ, w')`, so the two sit at one site.
(F3) is (Ti)/(Se). (F4) is the end-flow instance. The Lemma applies.

**Proof of 2, rigidity of `Y` suffices.**
- The data marginals of a `Z`-family form a `D`-family for `Y`: a pattern outside `L(Y)` is the data
  part of no pattern of `Z`. So they commute.
- With item 1 this is hypothesis (B), and `seeded-transport-codings-over-end-flows-are-quantum-rigid`
  applies.

**Proof of 2, rigidity of `Z` forces it for `Y`.** Let `F_a(λ)` be a non-commuting `D'`-family for
`Y` on `W`, with `D' = D + R`, and fix `η ∉ Σ_ν`.
- *The chain reads a bounded window.* Every chain from `(λ, v')` keeps its `H`-coordinate, and each
  rule reads the input on `B_R(λ)`, which is constant along `F_m`. So the anchored letter is a
  function `val(q, v')` of the input pattern `q` on `B_R(λ)`.
- *The pulled-back family.* Put `P_s(λ, v') = [η-flow at v' = s]`, a scalar, `Q_a(λ, v') = F_a(λ)`,
  and `C_c(λ, v') = Σ_q F_q(λ) [val(q, v') = c]`. Here `F_q(λ)` is the product of the `F` over `q`,
  which commute inside `B_R(λ)`.
- *(Q1).* Holds at every site.
- *(Q2) at scale `D`.* Operators at sites within `2D` are polynomials in `F`'s inside one ball of
  diameter `<= 2D + 2R = 2D'`.
- *(Q3).* Expand a forbidden pattern's product into terms that fix an input pattern on a
  `(D + R)`-neighbourhood.
  - Terms whose input pattern is outside `L(Y)` vanish by (Q3) for `F`.
  - Terms whose computation letters disagree with `val` vanish, since those factors are the scalars
    `[val = c]`.
  - Any remaining term would realize the pattern in `Z`, by (Surj).
- *Conclusion.* `Q_a(λ, ·) = F_a(λ)` does not commute. ∎

**Proof of 3.** As in `relational-spine-couplings-are-splittable-or-not-minimal`, items 2–3.
- *Density.* By the completeness hypothesis, the anchored points are dense.
- *Continuity.* On anchored base points the completion is continuous in `(y, η)`: chains stop, and
  they read bounded input windows.
- *Orbits.* `Y ⊠ X_∂` is minimal, as a product of minimal actions of the two factors. So every orbit
  approaches every anchored point, and hence every point. ∎

## Message for the (★′) builders (bh-g1-simulation)

Seeds and slots may read the input freely. For any causal layer with a one-predecessor rule along a
flow that has a downhill potential (F4), the flow direction contributes nothing to rigidity. E2 is
then **exactly** the rigidity of what the rules read: the input or fibre coding in the other
directions.
- **Rigidity** for `(Λ_0 * Z) × Z^2`: check (F3) and (F4) for the keyed-slot layer relative to its
  tree direction. Rigidity then reduces to the crossing-wire fibre together with the input layer
  those slots read.
- **Minimality**: check boundary completeness, as in item 3.

## Lesson for general BH

Reading the input costs no rigidity in the direction that does the seeding. Downhill centrality uses
only three things: the flow's own algebra, its commutation with the input, and the tree geometry. So
in seeded codings E2 splits cleanly.
- The seeded direction is free.
- The input direction must be rigid on its own, and that is necessary as well as sufficient.

For v6's CAP question this is a sharp statement. A seeded transport coding carries exactly the
rigidity of its input layer, so seeding cannot manufacture rigidity for a complex input. The complex
input must come already rigid, as a rigid seed over `Λ_0`. The end-flow layer is then a free
minimality-and-freeness device on top of it.
