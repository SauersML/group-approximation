Let `alpha:L curvearrowright L/N` be the action in `(GSB1)`.  Theorem 3.8 of
Gao--Kunnawalkam Elayavalli--Patchell says that if `M` is Connes embeddable,
`H` is hyperlinear, and `H curvearrowright X` is a sofic action, then the
generalized wreath product `M wr_X H` is Connes embeddable.  Take
`M=L(C_2)`, `H=L=SL_3(Z)`, and `X=L/N`.  The first algebra is finite
dimensional and `L` is residually finite, hence hyperlinear.  Therefore
soficity of `alpha` implies Connes embeddability of `L(W_N)`, equivalently
hyperlinearity of `W_N`.

Now restrict `alpha` to `E`.  GKP Proposition 2.15(3) says this restriction
would be sofic if `alpha` were.  Its orbit through the base coset is

```text
E.N = {eN:e in E} = E/N.                                (GSB2)
```

Proposition 2.15(2) allows passage to this orbit.  But this conclusion has no
contradictory force: GKP Theorem 2.19 says every action of a free group is
sofic, so `E curvearrowright E/N` is sofic without assuming anything about
`alpha` or `Q`.

The kernel issue is substantive, not a missing lemma.  Choose a finitely
presented nonsofic group `Q` generated by `m` elements and an epimorphism
`pi:F_m->Q`.  Its kernel is finitely normally generated because `Q` is
finitely presented.  Nevertheless Theorem 2.19 makes the `F_m`-action on
`F_m/ker(pi)` sofic.  After factoring the actor through `pi`, the same
permutation action is the regular action `Q curvearrowright Q`.  A sofic
regular action yields the usual finite almost-free multiplication tables and
hence makes `Q` a sofic group; therefore this `Q`-action is not sofic.  Thus
even finite normal generation of the action kernel does not make action
soficity descend to the faithful quotient.

Finally `N<F_m` is free by Nielsen--Schreier.  Free groups have the Haagerup
property.  A discrete group with both Haagerup and property `(T)` is finite;
as `F_m` is torsion-free, a property-`(T)` subgroup `N` must be trivial.  This
degenerates the quotient to the sofic group `F_m`, so property `(T)` of the
stabilizer cannot close the intended case.  Property `(T)` of `Q` changes
none of the preceding free-action argument.

There is one newer positive theorem to check.  Gao--Patchell--Kunnawalkam
Elayavalli, *Sofic actions on graphs*, arXiv:2408.15470, Theorem 2.20 proves
that a transitive action of a sofic group with amenable stabilizer is sofic;
their Proposition 2.7(4) identifies the empty-graph specialization with the
earlier set-action notion.  Here `N` is nontrivial (otherwise `Q=F_m`) and
has infinite index because `Q` is infinite.  A nontrivial infinite-index
normal subgroup of a finite-rank nonabelian free group is free of infinite
rank, hence nonamenable.  Thus this improvement does not establish the
ambient action.  Their Proposition 2.10 gives only a normal `H` contained in
the total action kernel such that the actor quotient by `H` is sofic.  It
cannot force the whole kernel to be removed; the free-actor example above
shows why.

GKP Theorem 3.8 has no converse, and even its sofic-group analogue is posed
there as Question 4.4.  Hence nonsoficity of `alpha`, if later established,
would only block this permanence proof; it would not decide hyperlinearity of
`W_N`.
