This follows Magee–de la Salle (arXiv:2312.03220v2, §2) step by step. `E_ij` are matrix units.

0. **Reductions.**
   - `A` is a finite product of finite chain rings, and `SL_4(A)` and `SL_2(A)` split accordingly.
   - An irreducible representation of the product is a tensor product of irreducibles of the factors, and tensors
     of invariant vectors are invariant.
   - So take `A` local with maximal ideal `πA`, residue field `F`, `(πA)^e = 0 ≠ (πA)^{e-1}`, and `φ`
     irreducible and nontrivial.
   - Suppose `φ` factors through `SL_4(A/π^{e-1}A)`. That ring is again a chain ring, so replace `A` by it. Hence
     either `φ` is nontrivial on `G' = ker(SL_4(A) → SL_4(A/π^{e-1}A))` ("new"), or `e = 1`.
   - Fix a generating character `ψ` of `(A,+)`, one whose kernel contains no nonzero ideal. It exists since `A` is
     Frobenius. Every character of `A^3` is `u ↦ ψ(ξ·u)` for a unique `ξ ∈ A^3`.
1. **Root layers act nontrivially.** Put `C_ij = {I + xE_ij : x ∈ π^{e-1}A}` (for `e = 1`, `x ∈ F`).
   - **Case e = 1.** `SL_4(F)` is generated by root subgroups, so some `C_ij` is not in `ker φ`.
   - **Case e ≥ 2.** Since `(π^{e-1}A)^2 = 0`, `A ↦ I + A` identifies the trace-zero matrices over `π^{e-1}A`
     with `G'`, as groups. Suppose every off-diagonal `C_ij` is in the kernel.
     - The quotient of `G'` by them is spanned by trace-zero diagonal classes, so some `I + x·diag(1,-1,0,0)` is
       not in the kernel. (Permuting coordinates is conjugation by a permutation matrix; in characteristic 2 its
       sign is trivial, otherwise use signed permutations.)
     - Conjugating `I + xE_12` by `I + E_21` gives `I + x(E_12 - E_11 + E_22 - E_21)`, which is in the kernel.
       Multiplying by `I + x(-E_12 + E_21)`, also in the kernel, gives `I + x·diag(-1,1,0,0)`, a contradiction.
   - All `C_ij` are conjugate under permutation matrices, so none is in the kernel.
2. **A Heisenberg character.** Let `U_1 = {Υ(u) = I + Σ_i u_i E_i4} ≅ A^3`.
   - Suppose every character of `U_1` occurring in `φ` had `ξ ∈ πA^3`. Then `U_1 ∩ G'` would act trivially,
     since `ξ·u ∈ π^e A = 0`. That contradicts step 1 for `C_14`. So some occurring `ξ` is unimodular.
   - `SL_3(A)` acts transitively on unimodular vectors (a local ring), so by the dual action take `ξ = (0,0,1)`.
     Let `V_χ` be the isotypic subspace.
   - It is invariant under `G_1` (`SL_3` on coordinates 1–3 with third row `(0,0,1)`), under `U_1`, and so under
     `H = {[x;y;z] = I + xE_23 + zE_24 + yE_34}`.
   - `[0;0;z] = Υ(0,z,0)` acts trivially on `V_χ`, and `[0;y;0] = Υ(0,0,y)` acts by `ψ(y)`.
   - Decompose `V_χ` under `U_2 = {I + v_1E_13 + v_2E_23} ≅ A^2`.
     - **All of `U_2` acts trivially.** Then `H` acts on `V_χ` by `[x;y;z] ↦ ψ(y)`.
     - **Otherwise.** Some character `v ↦ ψ(ζ·v)` with `ζ ≠ 0` occurs. In a chain ring `ζ = π^R w` with `w`
       unimodular: take `R` with `(ζ_1,ζ_2) = π^R A`; some `ζ_i` has valuation exactly `R`, and its `w_i` is a unit.
       `SL_2(A) ≤ G_1` on coordinates 1–2 normalizes `U_2` and moves `w` to `(1,0)`. The new character
       `ψ(π^R v_1)` is trivial in `v_2`, and on its isotypic space `H` again acts by `ψ(y)`.
   - In both cases there is `v ≠ 0` with `φ([x;y;z])v = ψ(y)v`.
3. **Induction.** Let `G_2 = SL_2(A)` on coordinates 2–3, and `N = {I + nE_23}`. `N` fixes `v`, so
   `W = span φ(G_2)v` is a quotient of `Ind_N^{G_2} 1`.
   - For `g = (a b; c d) ∈ G_2`, `g[0;y;z]g^{-1} = [0; cz+dy; az+by]`. So `φ(g^{-1})v` has character
     `(y,z) ↦ ψ(dy + cz)` on `{[0;y;z]}`.
   - That character equals the character of `v` iff `ψ(cz) = 1` and `ψ((d-1)y) = 1` for all `y,z`, i.e. `c = 0`
     and `d = 1` (ψ generating), i.e. `g ∈ N`.
   - So the vectors `φ(g^{-1})v`, `g ∈ G_2/N`, carry distinct characters and are linearly independent. Then
     `dim W = |G_2/N|`, so `W ≅ Ind_N^{G_2} 1`, which contains the trivial representation by Frobenius reciprocity.
   - `G_2` is conjugate to the upper-left `SL_2(A)` by a permutation matrix of determinant one (signed if the
     characteristic is not 2), which finishes the proof.
