Notation as in the target. Imported inputs:
- Point 5 uses Theorem 1 of `elek-szabo-amalgams-over-amenable-subgroups-are-sofic`.
- The Consequence uses closure of soficity under direct products and subgroups (`elek-szabo-sofic-permanence`, item 1).
- Two textbook facts of Bass--Serre theory (J.-P. Serre, *Trees*, Springer 1980, Ch. I):
  - (T1) the amalgam `U = A_0 *_(C_0) B_0` acts without inversions on a tree whose vertex stabilizers are the
    conjugates of `A_0` and `B_0` (Ch. I, Section 4.1);
  - (T2) a group acting freely and without inversions on a tree is free (Ch. I, Section 3.3).
  These are quoted from memory of the book, not re-read in this session; the section numbers should be checked
  against a copy.

Everything else is manipulation of presentations.

## Step 0. The factors (point 0)

- **`A ∩ B = C`.** Clearly `C ⊆ A ∩ B`. If `x in A ∩ B`, then `π(x) in π(A_0) ∩ π(B_0) = π(C_0)` by (H), so
  `x in L C_0 = C`.
- **`A_0 ∩ C = C_0`.** Let `a = l c` with `a in A_0`, `l in L` and `c in C_0 ⊆ A_0`. Then
  `l = a c^(-1) in A_0 ∩ L = 1`, so `a = c`. The case `B_0 ∩ C` is the same.
- **Splittings.** `L` is normal in `E`, and `A_0 ∩ L = 1`, so `A = L ⋊ A_0`. Likewise `B = L ⋊ B_0`, and
  `C = L ⋊ C_0` because `C_0 ∩ L ⊆ A_0 ∩ L = 1`.

So `P = A *_C B` is the amalgam of two subgroups of `E` along their intersection, and `Φ` is the identity on `A` and
on `B`.

## Step 1. The split (point 1)

- **`L` is normal in `P`.** `L ⊆ C`, and `L` is normal in `A` and in `B` because it is normal in `E`. The group `P` is
  generated by `A ∪ B`, and conjugation by each generator preserves `L`. So `L ⊴ P`.
- **The quotient.** Adding the relations `l = 1` (`l in L`) to the presentation of `A *_C B` gives
  `P / L ≅ (A/L) *_(C/L) (B/L)`.
- **Identification.** By Step 0 the inclusions give isomorphisms `A_0 -> A/L`, `B_0 -> B/L` and `C_0 -> C/L`, and these
  agree on `C_0`. So `P / L ≅ A_0 *_(C_0) B_0 = U`.
- **Section.** The inclusions `A_0 -> A` and `B_0 -> B` agree on `C_0`, so they induce `s : U -> P`. The composite
  `U -> P -> P/L ≅ U` is the identity on the generators `A_0 ∪ B_0`, hence the identity. So `s` is injective,
  `s(U) ∩ L = 1`, and `P = L ⋊ s(U)`. From now on `U` means `s(U)`, and `p : P -> U` is the retraction with kernel `L`.
- **Action.** For `a in A_0` and `l in L`, the conjugate `a l a^(-1)` computed in `A ⊆ E` is the one in `P`. So
  conjugation by `u in U` on `L` is conjugation by `Φ(u)` in `E`, first on generators and then for all `u`.

Also `Φ|_L` is the inclusion `L ⊆ E`, hence injective, and `π Φ = λ p` on `P`, since both kill `L` and agree on `U`.

## Step 2. The graph (point 2)

- **`N ∩ L = 1`.** `Φ` is injective on `L`.
- **`[N, L] = 1`.** Both `N` and `L` are normal in `P`, so `[N, L] ⊆ N ∩ L = 1`.
- **`p(N) ⊆ K`.** For `n in N`, `λ(p(n)) = π(Φ(n)) = 1`.
- **`p|_N` is injective,** because its kernel is `N ∩ L = 1`.
- **`p(N) = K`.** Let `k in K`. Then `π(Φ(k)) = λ(k) = 1`, so `ℓ(k) = Φ(k) in L`. Put `n = ℓ(k)^(-1) k in P`, with
  `ℓ(k)` read in `L ⊆ P`. Then `Φ(n) = ℓ(k)^(-1) Φ(k) = 1`, so `n in N`, and `p(n) = k`.

So `k -> ℓ(k)^(-1) k` is the inverse of `p|_N`, and `N = {ℓ(k)^(-1) k : k in K}` is the graph of the homomorphism
`ℓ = Φ|_K : K -> L`.

## Step 3. Freeness (point 3)

- `λ|_(A_0) = π|_(A_0)`, since `Φ` is the inclusion on `A_0`. So `K ∩ A_0 = A_0 ∩ L = 1`, and likewise `K ∩ B_0 = 1`.
- `K` is normal in `U`, so `K ∩ g A_0 g^(-1) = g (K ∩ A_0) g^(-1) = 1` for every `g in U`, and the same for `B_0`.
- By (T1), `K` acts on the Bass--Serre tree of `U` without inversions and with trivial vertex stabilizers, that is,
  freely. By (T2), `K` is free. So is `N ≅ K` (Step 2).
- Free groups are residually finite, subgroups of free groups are free, and residually finite groups are sofic (the
  convention quoted in `elek-szabo-amalgams-over-amenable-subgroups-are-sofic`). This gives the last sentence of
  point 3.

## Step 4. The join (point 4)

- `U` is generated by `A_0 ∪ B_0`, so `Φ(U) = <A_0, B_0> = E_0`.
- **`N ∩ U = ker ℓ`.** If `u in U` and `Φ(u) = 1`, then `λ(u) = 1`, so `u in K` and `ℓ(u) = 1`. The converse is clear.
- **`ℓ(K) = E_0 ∩ L`.** `ℓ(K) ⊆ Φ(U) ∩ L`. Conversely, if `e = Φ(u) in L`, then `λ(u) = π(e) = 1`, so `u in K`.

Assume now `E_0 = E`, and put `M = N ∩ U = ker ℓ`.
- **`ℓ` is onto,** by the last bullet. `Φ|_U` is onto with kernel `M`, so `E ≅ U / M`.
- **`N / M ≅ L`.** The map `ν = ℓ p|_N : N -> L` is a homomorphism, onto because `p(N) = K`. If `ν(n) = 1`, then
  `n = ℓ(p(n))^(-1) p(n) = p(n) in U` by Step 2, so `n in M`. So `ker ν = M`.
- **`M ⊴ P`.** `M` is normal in `U`, as a kernel of `Φ|_U`. It centralizes `L` because `M ⊆ N` (Step 2). As
  `P = L U`, `M` is normal in `P`.
- **`P / M ≅ L ⋊ E`.** The map `l u -> (l, u M)` from `P = L ⋊ U` to `L ⋊ (U/M)` is well defined, because `M` acts
  trivially on `L`. It is a homomorphism and onto. Its kernel consists of the `l u` with `l = 1` and `u in M`, which is
  `M`. Via `U / M ≅ E` the action of `U/M` on `L` is conjugation in `E` (Step 1).
- **`L ⋊ E ≅ E ×_Λ E`.** Let `ψ(l, e) = (l e, e)`.
  - Homomorphism: `(l, e)(l', e') = (l e l' e^(-1), e e')` maps to `(l e l' e', e e') = (l e, e)(l' e', e')`.
  - Injective: `(l e, e) = (1, 1)` forces `e = 1` and then `l = 1`.
  - Onto: given `π(e) = π(e')`, put `l = e e'^(-1) in L`; then `ψ(l, e') = (e, e')`.
- **Normal generation.** Let `r in N` with `ν(r) != 1`. For `g in P`, `ν(g r g^(-1)) = Φ(p(g)) ν(r) Φ(p(g))^(-1)`,
  since `p` and `Φ` are homomorphisms and `ν = Φ p` on `N`. As `g` runs over `P`, `Φ(p(g))` runs over `Φ(U) = E`. So
  `ν(<<r>>_P)` is the normal closure of `ν(r)` in `E`, which is a nontrivial `E`-invariant normal subgroup of `L`.
  Under the hypothesis on `L` it is `L`. Since `<<r>>_P ⊆ N` and `ker ν = M`, this gives `N = M <<r>>_P`.

## Step 5. Soficity of the ambient amalgam (point 5)

This is Theorem 1 of `elek-szabo-amalgams-over-amenable-subgroups-are-sofic`, applied to `P = A *_C B`.

## The Consequence

- `e -> (e, e)` embeds `E` in `E ×_Λ E ≅ P / M`. So if `P / M` is sofic, then `E` is sofic (closure under subgroups).
- `E ×_Λ E` is a subgroup of `E x E`. So if `E` is sofic, then `P / M` is sofic (closure under direct products and
  subgroups, `elek-szabo-sofic-permanence`).

The remark that no property of `N` can obstruct soficity of `E` is a restatement: `N` is free by point 3, and the
equivalence above only involves the quotient. ∎
