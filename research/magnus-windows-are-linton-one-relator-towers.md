ESTABLISHED (a proof from Linton's definitions, unreviewed). Proof:
[[magnus-windows-are-linton-one-relator-towers-proof]].

This settles the caveat left open in the trust section of the length-19 hierarchy certificate
(c951f0d79, `length19-singular-relators-z-stable-hierarchy-special-proof`): "whether Linton's
definition of one-relator hierarchy allows the hierarchy to stop at a primitive relator after
two steps". It does.

## Statement

Let `X_0` be the presentation complex of `G = <a, t | w>`, where `w` is cyclically reduced and
the exponent sum of `t` in `w` is 0. Rewrite `w` along `t` (Magnus–Moldavanskii):
- `G = B_0 *_psi`, where `B_0 = <a_0, ..., a_n | W_0>`;
- `A = F(a_0..a_(n-1))`, `B = F(a_1..a_n)`, and `psi(a_i) = a_(i+1)`.

Let `s` be a letter of `W_0` with exponent sum 0. Rewrite `W_0` along `s`:
- `B_0 = V *_phi`, where `V = <u_h : u != s, m_u <= h <= M_u | R>`;
- `phi(u_h) = u_(h+1)`, between the subgroups generated by the copies with `h < M_u` and with
  `h > m_u`.

Iterate this `N` times, and suppose the last relator `R_N` is primitive in its free group.
Then:

1. **Tower.** There are one-relator complexes `X_N -> ... -> X_1 -> X_0` forming a one-relator
   tower in Linton's sense (arXiv:2202.11324v3, p. 15). Each `X_i` is a one-relator Z-domain of
   a Z-cover of `X_(i-1)`. The one-relator splitting that `X_i` induces is exactly the
   `i`-th Magnus–Moldavanskii HNN splitting above, with the same edge groups, up to replacing
   `psi_i` by `psi_i^(-1)`.
2. **Hierarchy.** Since `R_N` is primitive, `pi_1(X_N)` is free. So the tower is a
   *hierarchy of length N* (p. 15: "`pi_1(X_N)` splits as a free product of cyclic groups").
3. **Linton's theorem applies.** Suppose `sZ(psi_i) < infinity` and `sZ(psi_i^(-1)) < infinity`
   for all `i < N`, and `G` contains no Baumslag–Solitar subgroup (for example, `G` is
   hyperbolic). Then Linton's Theorem 7.1 (3)⇒(1) applies.
   - `G` is hyperbolic with a quasi-convex hierarchy.
   - `G` is virtually compact special, hence linear, residually finite and sofic.

   Checking both `psi_i` and `psi_i^(-1)` makes the conclusion independent of which
   orientation Linton's convention assigns.

The case `N = 1` is Linton's own Examples 1.2 (p. 4) and 6.12 (p. 28), where he calls the
length-one splitting ending in a free group "a one-relator hierarchy of length one". For
`N = 2` this is the shape used by the length-19 certificate and by its length-21 extension.

## Why this is not automatic

Linton's towers are defined geometrically: each step is a Z-domain of a cyclic cover, with the
three conditions of Definition 4.1. Magnus–Moldavanskii rewriting is defined algebraically. So
two things need checking:
- the algebraic windows (height ranges `[m_u, M_u]`) are Z-domains of the right covers of the
  previous *window complex*, not of the standard presentation complex of the vertex group;
- the termination condition allows a free group, not only a finite cyclic group. Theorem 4.13
  ends at a finite cyclic group, but that is the *maximal* tower.

The proof checks Definition 4.1 on the explicit windows. Every piece of a window is an integer
interval of heights, so conditions (2) and (3) reduce to facts about intervals. It then reads
the termination condition off the definition on p. 15.
