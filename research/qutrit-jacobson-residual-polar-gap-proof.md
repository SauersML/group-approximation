The residual calculation gives five commuting involutions

```text
w=x_13(Q),          v=x_13(SQ),
d=x_13(S^2T),       alpha=x_13(1),
r=x_23(ST).                                             (QJR4)
```

On `e=e_res` their signs are, respectively,

```text
(-1,-1,+1,-1,-1).                                      (QJR5)
```

The four coefficients `Q,SQ,S^2T,1` are linearly independent in the
Jacobson ring. Indeed `Q=1-ST` and `SQ=S-S^2T` rewrite a relation
among them as a relation among the distinct Jacobson normal forms
`1,ST,S,S^2T`. The fifth root has a different matrix position.
Consequently the involutions in `(QJR4)` generate

```text
N isomorphic to C_2^5.
```

The residual is exactly the character projection with the signs in
`(QJR5)`:

```text
e=(1-w)(1-v)(1+d)(1-alpha)(1-r)/32.                    (QJR6)
```

In particular its identity coefficient and canonical trace are `1/32`.

The qutrit support group `F_q` has order `3^5`. Hence
`F_q intersect N={1}`. If

```text
p=sum_(f in F_q) a_f f,             e=sum_(n in N)b_n n,
```

then `fn=1` forces `f=n=1`. It follows that

```text
tau(p e)=a_1 b_1=tau(p)tau(e).                          (QJR7)
```

For a projection `p in C[F_q]`, traciality gives

```text
||e p||_2^2=tau(p e p)=tau(e p)=tau(p)/32.              (QJR8)
```

Substitute the qutrit traces

```text
tau(q)=1/3,          tau(R)=2/9,          tau(q-R)=1/9
```

to obtain `(QJR1)`. The same proof applies to a scalar conjugate
`gF_qg^(-1)`: it still has odd order, so its intersection with the
two-group `N` is trivial.

Now use the coarse carrier

```text
F=(1-x_13(Q))(1+x_13(ST))(1-x_23(ST))/8,
```

which contains `e`. The opposite-root quarter-gap theorem, with
`k=x_31(1)`, says exactly that either `polar(e k e)` has both support
projections equal to `e`, or the leakage term in `(QJR2)` has
operator norm at least `1/4`. Suppose the first alternative holds and
write `V=polar(e k e)`. Then `V` is unitary in the `e`-corner.

Let `p'` be `q-R` or any scalar conjugate of it. For arbitrary
contractions `C,D`,

```text
||V e R||_2=||eR||_2=1/12,
||C e p' D||_2<=||e p'||_2=1/(12 sqrt(2)).              (QJR9)
```

The reverse triangle inequality proves `(QJR3)`. Thus a typed relation
which makes these two occurrences converge in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm is impossible.

Finally every coefficient in `(QJR4)` is one-sided:

```text
ST=1+Q,                 S^2T=S+SQ.
```

The qutrit packet and `k` are scalar, so the group generated by
`F_q,N,k` is a subgroup of `EL_28(A_+)`. The one-sided theorem makes
this ambient group residually finite. Choose finite quotients injective on
increasing balls and take their left regular representations. They converge
to the canonical trace, preserve `(QJR6)--(QJR8)`, and retain every
nonzero qutrit atom and the residual cut. These exact finite models prove
that the displayed package, without a separately evaluated `T`-bearing
occurrence, has no Hilbert--Schmidt obstruction.

The alternating identity which exposes `x_21(T)` is therefore
load-bearing at the occurrence level. The present theorem supplies its two
numerical endpoints: the leakage branch costs `1/4` in operator norm, and
a one-complement full-polar branch costs
`(sqrt(2)-1)/(12 sqrt(2))` in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
