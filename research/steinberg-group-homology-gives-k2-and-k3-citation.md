Citation import. C. Weibel, *The K-book* (Grad. Stud. Math. 145, AMS, 2013). Read 2026-09-13 from the author's chapter
PDFs `https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~weibel/Kbook/Kbook.III.pdf` and `Kbook.IV.pdf`. The text was extracted on MSI with
ghostscript (`/scratch.global/sauer354/sk/sk-open-4/KIII.txt` l.1892–1901 and `KIV.txt` l.312–319). Sub- and superscripts
that the extraction split off are restored and marked [·]. Every word is verbatim.

- **III, Theorem 5.5 (Kervaire, Steinberg), p. 38.** "The Steinberg group St(R) is the universal central extension of
  E(R). Hence K[_2](R) ≅ H[_2](E(R);Z)."
- **III, Proposition 5.5.1.** "If n ≥ 5, every central extension Y → St[_n](R) is split. Hence St[_n](R) is the universal
  central extension of E[_n](R)."
- **IV, Corollary 1.7.1, with the sentence that follows it.** "The group K[_2](R) = π[_2] BGL(R)[^+] is isomorphic to the group
  K[_2](R) ≅ H[_2](E(R);Z) of chapter III. In fact, we will see in Ex. 1.8 and 1.9 that K[_n](R) ≅ π[_n](BE(R)[^+]) for all n ≥ 2,
  and K[_n](R) ≅ π[_n](BSt(R)[^+]) for all n ≥ 3, with K[_3](R) ≅ H[_3](St(R);Z)."

Scope note: (iii) is stated in the K-book text with its proof delegated to exercises. Primary source (not read):
S. M. Gersten, *K_3 of a ring is H_3 of the Steinberg group*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 37 (1973), 366–368.
