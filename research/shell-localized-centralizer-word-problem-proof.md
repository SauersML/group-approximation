Put B_p=Fix_V(p). Its finite generation follows from the rational
prefix-stabilizer result. The shell germ group is generated by rho(S)
and the prefix shift germ, so the neighborhood-kernel argument gives
A_1=<B_p,hat(S)>. These are finite generating sets. A word-problem
algorithm can be transferred between finite generating sets using
fixed marking words; the statements below presume the markings are
correct and do not recognize correct markings or presentations.

## A decidable restriction test using A_1

Let U=0^N*C. Copy B_p inside U by prefixing 0^N and extending each
element by the identity outside U. Call this subgroup B_(U,p).
It is contained in B_p, is finitely generated, and contains every
prefix homeomorphism supported in a cone W subset U avoiding p.
Thus it is locally moving on U minus {p}.

For r in A_1,

    r is identity on U  iff  [r,b]=1 for every generator b of B_(U,p).

For the reverse implication, suppose r moves x in U minus {p}.
Choose a cone W around x contained in U minus {p}, with W and r(W)
disjoint. A nonidentity prefix homeomorphism b supported in W belongs
to B_(U,p); its conjugate by r has disjoint support and cannot equal b.
This contradicts centralization. The point p is fixed by definition.
For the forward implication, a homeomorphism fixing U pointwise also
preserves its complement and commutes with every map supported in U.

The generators of B_(U,p) are effective finite prefix tables. Express
them in a fixed finite generating set of B_p by enumerating words and
comparing finite prefix tables. Termination follows from membership.
Consequently a word-problem algorithm for A_1 decides the displayed
restriction test using finitely many commutators.

## Reduce a general envelope word to these restriction tests

Suppose A_1 has decidable word problem. Enumerating its trivial words
gives a recursive presentation, and enumeration extraction therefore
computes nu, its inverse, and all shell generator permutations and
their inverses. Hence their actions on eventually-zero points and
their local prefix tables away from p are effective.

For w=a_k*...*a_1 in the generators of E, compute

    F={(a_(j-1)*...*a_1)^-1(p): a_j is a shell letter}.

If w moves a member of F, it is nonidentity. Otherwise for each q
in F follow its trajectory z_j and choose prefix transporters t_z
with t_z(p)=z, using the same transporter for repeated points and
t_p=1. Normalize each letter as t_(z_j)^-1*a_j*t_(z_(j-1)).

If a_j is a shell letter at z_(j-1)=p, this normalized letter is
the shell letter itself. In every other case it is a known prefix
map near p fixing p, with germ a shift by an effectively computed
integer c. Choose the corresponding power of a fixed prefix shift
in B_p. This produces a word r_q in A_1 whose action agrees with
t_q^-1*w*t_q on an effectively computable cone 0^N*C.

Here computing N does not test triviality of any germ. Each replacement
already has an explicit cone of agreement. These cones can be pulled
back through the preceding chosen representatives effectively. For
a shell permutation rho and desired output cone 0^M*C, evaluate
rho^-1(j) for the finitely many j<M and take N larger than all these
indices. Then the shell map sends 0^N*C into 0^M*C. Prefix maps have
the analogous finite-table calculation. Repeating this for a finite
word gives all required neighborhood inclusions. Increase N until
t_q is a single prefix map on the cone as well.

Apply the preceding finite commutator test to r_q on this specified
cone. A failure proves w nonidentity. Success proves w identity on
the computable cone U_q=t_q(0^N*C). After all tests pass, remove their
union. The remaining compact clopen set D avoids every candidate
singularity of w. Before each shell letter its current image avoids
p, since the preimage of p was one of the removed points in F.
Each such compact clopen set lies in finitely many shells. Thus the
action of w on D is an effective finite prefix table, which decides
identity there. This completes a terminating algorithm for E.

Notice that no search for an unknown neighborhood of identity is
used: the cone is chosen first, from local agreement data, and
identity on that exact cone is then decided inside A_1.

## The equivalences and the germ-quotient consequence

The punctured-prefix discriminator proves that a recursive presentation
of either E or A_1 gives its own decidable word problem. Conversely
decidable word problem enumerates a recursive presentation. A_1 is a
finitely generated subgroup of E, so a word-problem algorithm for E
restricts to one for A_1. The construction above proves the remaining
direction, completing all four equivalences for arbitrary nu.

Finally A_1->Q is onto with simple nontrivial neighborhood kernel K.
Any fixed nonidentity k in K normally generates K in A_1, as in the
required kernel claim. Adjoining k=1 to a recursive presentation of
A_1 therefore gives a recursive presentation of Q. This implication
uses only enumeration of relators, not finite presentation. The
discriminator lies in K and is killed, so it supplies no decision
algorithm for the word problem of Q.
