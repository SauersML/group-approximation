Let X be the orbit closure of x.

**Step 1.** R_x ≅ LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Z (`sequence-operator-ring-is-subshift-crossed-product`), so EL_n(R_x) ≅ EL_n(LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Z).

**Step 2: X is minimal iff x is uniformly recurrent.** This is Birkhoff's classical fact; the proof is recorded because it is short.
- (⇐) Suppose x is uniformly recurrent, y ∈ X, and v is a word of x. Then v occurs in every window of length M(v) of x. Every word of y is a word of x of length at least M(v), so v occurs in y.
  - So L(y) = L(x). The orbit closure of y contains x, since each word of x is a word of y. So every orbit is dense.
- (⇒) Suppose some word v of x has unboundedly long gaps. A compactness limit of the windows of x avoiding v gives y ∈ X in which v never occurs. Then the orbit closure of y is a proper closed invariant subset, so X is not minimal.

**Step 3: X is finite iff x is periodic.** A finite orbit closure means that the orbit of x is finite, which means x is periodic. If x is not periodic, then X ⊇ {T^n x} is infinite.

**Step 4.** Combine the steps.
- Item 1: e_12(σ^j), j ∈ Z, are distinct elements of G.
- Item 2 follows from `subshift-elementary-group-simple-iff-infinite-minimal` (k = F_2) together with Steps 2 and 3.
- Item 3 follows from `simple-kazhdan-lef-group-from-minimal-subshift`:
  - Z(EL_3) = 1 over F_2, as in the note l.114-116; for n ≥ 3 the same computation applies.
  - The theorem node covers n = 3, and its proof applies verbatim for n ≥ 3: EJZ for n ≥ 3, towers of height n(2m+1).
- Finite generation: R_x is generated by σ^{±1} and the D_a.

**Model tests.**
- x = Fibonacci word: uniformly recurrent and not periodic, so G is simple. It is the note's group.
- x = (…0001000…): not uniformly recurrent. The ideal comes from the fixed point 0^∞, and G is not simple. This agrees with the example in the criterion node.
- x = (01)^∞: periodic, so G = SL_{2n}(F_2[t,t^{-1}]), which is not simple.
