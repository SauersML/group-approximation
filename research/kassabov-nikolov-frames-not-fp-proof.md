**Source pinned.** M. Kassabov and N. Nikolov, *Cartesian products as profinite
completions*, arXiv:math/0602446. The TeX source (`profinite18-02.tex`) was
fetched on MSI on 2026-09-13.
- Definition in §2 "Frame subgroups" (TeX l.195–207): "A finitely generated subgroup $G < \mathfrak{S}$ is \emph{a frame} for $\mathfrak{S}$ if the following hold: (a) $G$ contains $\bigoplus_{n=1}^\infty S_n$. (b) The natural surjection $\widehat G \rightarrow \mathfrak{S}$ is an isomorphism."
- Theorem `beginning` (TeX l.323–331): "For every odd prime $p$, there exists a 10-generated group $G_1$ which is a frame for the Cartesian product $$\prod_{n=3}^\infty \Alt( u_{n,p}),$$ where $u_{n,p}=(p^{3n}-1)(p-1)^{-1}$."
- Proof of Theorem `main1` (TeX l.459–476): "there exists a frame subgroup $\Gamma < \G$ generated by $(c+1)$ copies of $G_2$".

**Proof.**
1. Let `G <= S = prod_n S_n` contain `(+)_n S_n`, with every `S_n` alternating
   of degree at least `4` and infinitely many factors. A frame satisfies this by
   clause (a) of the definition.
2. By `alternating-groups-have-nonzero-schur-multiplier`, `H_2(S_n; Z) != 0` for
   every `n`.
3. By `products-of-finite-groups-with-schur-factors-not-fp`, `H_2(G; Z)` is not
   finitely generated and `G` is not finitely presented.

For Theorem `beginning` the degrees are `u_(n,p) >= (p^9 - 1)/(p - 1) >= 4`. `∎`
