**Lemma.** Let `φ : G -> Q` be a homomorphism and suppose `φ|_{A_i}` is not injective.
Its kernel is a nontrivial normal subgroup of the simple group `A_i`, so
`φ(A_i) = 1`. For a neighbour `j` of `i`, `φ` kills the nontrivial subgroup
`A_i ∩ A_j` of `A_j`. So `φ|_{A_j}` is not injective, and again `φ(A_j) = 1`. By
connectivity `φ` kills every `A_k`, and since these generate `G`, `φ` is trivial.

**Corollary.**
- (=>) Let `N` be a torsion-free normal subgroup of finite index. `A_1 ∩ N = 1`, so
  `G/N` is finite and nontrivial.
- (<=) A nontrivial finite quotient is injective on every `A_i` by the Lemma. Every
  finite subgroup is conjugate into some `A_i`, so the quotient is injective on
  every finite subgroup. By `finite-torsion-carrier-virtual-torsionfree-criterion`
  (4 => 1), with carriers `A_1,...,A_r`, `G` is virtually torsion-free.
- If `G` is infinite with no nontrivial finite quotient, every `A_i != 1` lies in the
  finite residual `R_f(G) = G`. So `G` is neither virtually torsion-free nor residually
  finite.

**Triangles of groups.** The facts used are standard for simple complexes of groups
over a simply connected triangle.
- The fundamental group is the colimit of the vertex, edge and face groups, so it is
  generated by the vertex groups.
- Gersten–Stallings: when the angle sum is `<= π` the triangle is developable, and `G`
  acts on the CAT(0) development with the triangle as strict fundamental domain and
  cell stabilizers conjugate to the local groups. So a finite subgroup, having a fixed
  point, is conjugate into a vertex group.
- When the sum is `< π` the development is CAT(-1) and `G` is hyperbolic.

These facts are used only for the (<=) direction and for hyperbolicity; the Lemma
needs none of them.
