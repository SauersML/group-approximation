**ESTABLISHED (unreviewed)** via `sequence-operator-ring-is-subshift-crossed-product-proof`.

**Setting.**
- A is a finite set and x ∈ A^Z is an arbitrary sequence.
- V = F_2[Z] is the space of finitely supported F_2-valued sequences, with basis δ_n.
- σδ_n = δ_{n+1} is the shift.
- For a ∈ A, D_a δ_n = [x_n = a] δ_n is multiplication by the indicator of the letter a.
- R_x ⊆ End_{F_2}(V) is the subring generated by σ, σ^{-1} and the D_a.
- X ⊆ A^Z is the orbit closure of x under (Ty)_n = y_{n+1}.
- R = LC(X,F_2) ⋊_T Z = {Σ_j f_j u^j} with u f u^{-1} = f∘T^{-1}. These are the conventions of simple_kazhdan_sofic_group.tex, l.72-77.

**Statement.** The rule π(f)δ_n = f(T^n x)δ_n, π(u) = σ defines a ring isomorphism π: R → R_x, with π(e_{[x_0=a]}) = D_a.

**Consequences.**
- R_x depends, up to isomorphism, only on the set L(x) of finite words occurring in x, since X = {y : L(y) ⊆ L(x)}.
- EL_n(R_x) ≅ EL_n(LC(X,F_2) ⋊ Z) for every n, so the group G_X of the note is EL_3(R_x) for any x ∈ X.
- No hypothesis on x is needed. For the note's theorem, x is uniformly recurrent and not periodic, which is exactly when X is an infinite minimal subshift.

**Why it matters for the note.** The headline can be stated with two operators on F_2[Z] and no subshifts, clopen sets or crossed products. A periodic sequence y = w^∞ on the circle F_2[Z/N] gives, by the same construction, the full matrix ring M_N(F_2) when w is primitive. So the note's finite models are the same construction applied to periodic sequences.

**Credit.** Orbit (regular) representations of crossed products are classical. The C*-analogue is the realization of C(X) ⋊ Z on ℓ²(Z) along a dense orbit. This node only records the algebraic statement in the form the note uses.
