Throughout, a tree action "has a fixed point" in Serre's sense. We use two
standard facts.

- **(S1)** A countable group with FA is finitely generated, and has no
  quotient isomorphic to `Z` (Serre, *Trees*, Ch. I §6.1, Theorem 15). FA
  passes to quotients.
- **(S2)** A finitely generated virtually free group is the fundamental
  group of a finite graph of finite groups (Karrass--Pietrowski--Solitar,
  J. Austral. Math. Soc. 16 (1973)). So it acts without inversions on its
  Bass--Serre tree with finite vertex stabilizers, and every FA subgroup of it
  is finite.

**Trees.** For a prime `p` let `X_p` be the tree of the discretely valued field
`(Q, v_p)`, with valuation ring `Z_(p) = Q ∩ Z_p` (Serre, *Trees*, Ch. II §1).
Its vertices are the homothety classes `[L]` of `Z_(p)`-lattices `L ⊆ Q^2`.
`GL_2(Q)` acts on `X_p` through `PGL_2(Q)`.

**Step 1 (PGL_2(Q)).** Let `H̄ ≤ PGL_2(Q)` have FA. By (S1) it is finitely
generated. Represent its generators and their inverses by matrices, clear
denominators, and invert the primes dividing their entries and determinants.
Then `H̄` lies in the image of `GL_2(Z[1/m])` for some `m >= 1`.

Fix `p | m`. Let `H̄` act on the barycentric subdivision of `X_p`, which it acts
on without inversions. FA gives a fixed point, that is, a vertex or the
midpoint of an edge of `X_p`. So `H̄` fixes a vertex `x_p` or stabilizes an edge
`e_p`, and a subgroup `H̄_p` of index at most 2 fixes a vertex `x_p = [L_p]`.

Let `K = ⋂_{p|m} H̄_p`, a subgroup of index at most `2^ω(m)` in `H̄`, where
`ω(m)` is the number of primes dividing `m`. Take `h ∈ K` with representative
`g ∈ GL_2(Z[1/m])`. For each `p | m` we have `g L_p = λ_p L_p` with
`λ_p = p^(a_p)`, `a_p ∈ Z`. Replace `g` by `g' = ∏_{p|m} p^(-a_p) g`. This changes only
the valuations at the primes `p | m`, so `g' ∈ GL_2(Z_(q))` for every prime
`q ∤ m`. It also gives `g' L_p = L_p` for every `p | m`.

Let `L = Q^2 ∩ ⋂_{p|m} L_p ∩ ⋂_{q∤m} Z_(q)^2`. This is a Z-lattice in `Q^2`,
since a Z-lattice is determined by its localizations and they equal `Z_(q)^2`
for almost all `q`. Every such `g'` satisfies `g' L = L`. So `K` lies in the
image of `GL(L) ≅ GL_2(Z)`, which is `PGL_2(Z)` up to conjugation in
`PGL_2(Q)`.

So `H̄` has the finite-index subgroup `K`, which lies in a conjugate of
`PGL_2(Z)`. `PGL_2(Z)` is virtually free: the image of the principal congruence
subgroup of level 3, which is free, has finite index. So `K`, and hence `H̄`, is
virtually free, and `H̄` is finitely generated by (S1). By (S2) applied to `H̄`
itself, `H̄` acts without inversions on a tree with finite vertex stabilizers.
FA gives a fixed vertex, so `H̄` is finite.

**Step 2 (GL_2(Q)).** Let `H ≤ GL_2(Q)` have FA. Its image `H̄` in `PGL_2(Q)` has
FA, so `H̄` is finite by Step 1. The group `det(H) ≤ Q^x` is an abelian quotient
of `H`, so it has FA. By (S1) it is finitely generated with no `Z` quotient,
hence finite, hence contained in `{±1}`. A scalar `λI ∈ H` has `λ^2 = det = ±1`,
so `λ = ±1`. So `H ∩ Q^x I ⊆ {±I}`, and `H` is an extension of the finite group
`H̄` by a group of order at most 2. Hence `H` is finite.

**Step 3 (FW).** A group with FW has a bounded orbit in every action on a
CAT(0) cube complex, in particular on every tree. In a tree acted on without
inversions, a bounded orbit gives a fixed vertex (the centre of the orbit's
convex hull), so FW implies FA.

`SL_2(Q) ≤ GL_2(Q)` and `PSL_2(Q) ≤ PGL_2(Q)` give the remaining cases.
