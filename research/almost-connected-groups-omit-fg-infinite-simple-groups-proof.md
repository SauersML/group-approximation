Let `φ : S → G` be a homomorphism. `S` is simple, so `φ` is trivial or
injective. Assume it is injective and derive a contradiction. The group `S` is
nonabelian, since an abelian simple group is finite cyclic.

**Step 1: `φ(S) ⊆ G^0` (item 1).**
- `G^0` is a closed normal subgroup (standard: the identity component is
  closed and invariant under every homeomorphism of `G` fixing `1`).
- So `G/G^0` is a compact Hausdorff group, by hypothesis.
- The composite `S → G → G/G^0` is a homomorphism to a compact group. By
  `fg-infinite-simple-groups-are-minimally-almost-periodic` it is trivial.
- So `φ(S) ⊆ G^0`.

This proves item 1, for any homomorphism.

**Step 2: a connected Lie quotient.** `G^0` is closed in `G`, hence locally
compact, and it is connected. By `gleason-yamabe-and-van-dantzig-structure-theorems`,
item 2, there is a compact normal subgroup `K ⊴ G^0` with `L = G^0/K` a
connected Lie group. Let `π : G^0 → L` be the quotient map.

**Step 3: `S` embeds in `L`.**
- The kernel of `π ∘ φ` is a normal subgroup of `S`, namely `φ^{-1}(K)`.
- If it were all of `S`, then `φ` would be an injective homomorphism from `S`
  into the compact group `K`. That is nontrivial, since `S ≠ 1`, which
  contradicts `fg-infinite-simple-groups-are-minimally-almost-periodic`.
- So the kernel is `1`, and `π ∘ φ : S → L` is injective.
- In particular `L ≠ 1`, so `n = dim L ≥ 1`. A zero-dimensional connected Lie
  group is trivial.

**Step 4: `S` is linear.**
- Let `Ad : L → GL(Lie L) ≅ GL_n(R)` be the adjoint representation.
- For connected `L`, `ker Ad` is the centre `Z(L)`. This is standard Lie theory:
  `L` is generated by `exp(Lie L)`, and `g exp(X) g^{-1} = exp(Ad(g) X)`.
- The kernel of `Ad ∘ π ∘ φ` is `(π∘φ)^{-1}(Z(L))`, a normal subgroup of `S`.
  Since `π ∘ φ` is injective, it is isomorphic to a subgroup of the abelian group
  `Z(L)`, so it is abelian.
- `S` is nonabelian, so this kernel is not `S`. Hence it is `1`.
- So `Ad ∘ π ∘ φ : S → GL_n(R)` is injective.

**Step 5: contradiction.**
- `S` is finitely generated and isomorphic to a subgroup of `GL_n(R)`. By
  `finitely-generated-linear-groups-are-residually-finite` it is residually
  finite.
- Pick `s ≠ 1` in `S`. A finite quotient `S → F` with `s ↦ ≠ 1` has a kernel
  that is a proper normal subgroup of finite index. By simplicity that kernel is
  `1`, so `S ≅` a subgroup of `F`, which is finite.
- This contradicts `S` being infinite.

Hence `φ` is trivial, proving item 2. ∎

**Trust surface.**
- The imports are:
  - Gleason–Yamabe, verbatim through Tao's notes;
  - Malcev, as imported in the graph;
  - minimal almost periodicity, established in the graph.
- Two standard facts are used without citation: the identity component is a
  closed normal subgroup, and `ker Ad = Z(L)` for a connected Lie group `L`.
- Only the normality of `ker Ad` and its inclusion in the centre are used. If
  the equality is doubted, Step 4 needs only `ker Ad ⊆ Z(L)`, which follows from
  the displayed formula.
