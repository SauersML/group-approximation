# Compute the Q8/BS(2,3) terminal mark and its unavoidable HS wall

## 1. One explicit finite-residual word in \(BS(2,3)\)

Write

\[
 A=\langle a,t\mid t a^2t^{-1}=a^3\rangle
\]

and set

\[
 b=tat^{-1},\qquad w=[b,a]=bab^{-1}a^{-1}.              \tag{1}
\]

We first prove directly that every homomorphism from \(A\) to a finite
group kills \(w\). Let \(\bar a,\bar b\) be the images in a finite group
and put \(r=|\bar a|=|\bar b|\). The defining relation says

\[
 \bar b^2=\bar a^3.
\]

Taking orders gives

\[
 \frac r{\gcd(r,2)}=\frac r{\gcd(r,3)}.
\]

Hence \(\gcd(r,2)=\gcd(r,3)\). The left side belongs to
\(\{1,2\}\), the right side to \(\{1,3\}\), so both equal \(1\).
Choose \(u\) with \(2u\equiv1\pmod r\). Then

\[
 \bar b=(\bar b^2)^u=(\bar a^3)^u\in\langle\bar a\rangle,
\]

and therefore \(\bar w=1\).

The word \(w\) is nevertheless nontrivial in \(A\). Regard \(A\) as the
HNN extension of \(\langle a\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\) which identifies
\(\langle a^2\rangle\) with \(\langle a^3\rangle\). Expanded in HNN
normal form,

\[
 w=t a t^{-1}a\,t a^{-1}t^{-1}a^{-1}.                  \tag{2}
\]

None of the three possible pinches occurs: the intervening exponents
\(1,1,-1\) belong neither to the required subgroup \(2\mathbb Z\) nor
to the required subgroup \(3\mathbb Z\). Britton's lemma makes (2)
nontrivial.

The group \(A\) is torsion-free. Indeed a finite-order element of an HNN
extension fixes a vertex of its Bass--Serre tree, hence is conjugate into
the torsion-free base group \(\mathbb Z\). Consequently the nontrivial
word \(w\) has infinite order.

Finally, every finite-dimensional unitary representation
\(\rho:A\to U(d)\) kills \(w\). The image \(\rho(A)\) is a finitely
generated complex linear group and is residually finite by Mal'cev's
lemma. If \(\rho(w)\ne I\), a finite quotient of \(\rho(A)\) would detect
it, producing a finite quotient of \(A\) in which \(w\) survives,
contrary to the first paragraph.

For completeness, the needed form of Mal'cev's lemma follows by putting
the entries of finitely many matrix generators and their inverses in a
finitely generated subring \(R\subset\mathbb C\). A nonzero entry of
\(\rho(w)-I\) survives a specialization of \(R\) to a finite field, and
the specialized generators lie in a finite general linear group.

## 2. The finite selector and its killed coinvariant

Use the presentation

\[
 Q_8=\left\langle x,y,z\ \middle|\
 z^2=1,\ x^2=y^2=z,\ [z,x]=[z,y]=1,\ yx=zxy
 \right\rangle .                                       \tag{3}
\]

The assignment

\[
 \alpha(x)=y,\qquad \alpha(y)=x,\qquad \alpha(z)=z       \tag{4}
\]

preserves (3) and defines an involutive automorphism. In the coinvariant
quotient obtained by imposing \(\alpha(k)=k\), one has \(x=y\). The last
relation in (3) then gives

\[
 x^2=zx^2,
\]

so \(z=1\). Thus \(z\) lies in the normal subgroup generated by the
coinvariant differences \(\alpha(k)k^{-1}\).

Take

\[
 G=A,\qquad \Lambda=1,\qquad \Gamma=\langle w\rangle.
\]

Section 1 says that every finite-dimensional representation of \(G\)
has the same image on \(\Lambda\) and \(\Gamma\). The terminal branch of
the finite-dimensional coequalizer theorem is therefore active.

## 3. Amalgam normal form preserves \(z\)

Let

\[
 B=Q_8\rtimes_\alpha\langle s\rangle,
 \qquad \langle s\rangle\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

Because \(\alpha(z)=z\), the subgroup

\[
 C_B=\langle s,z\rangle\leq B
\]

is isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\times C_2\). Let

\[
 D=A\times\langle z\rangle.
\]

Section 1 gives

\[
 C_D=\langle w,z\rangle\leq D
 \cong\mathbb Z\times C_2.
\]

Identify \(C_B\) with \(C_D\) by \(s\mapsto w\) and \(z\mapsto z\), and
form

\[
 P=B*_{\mathbb Z\times C_2}D.                            \tag{5}
\]

The standard presentation theorem for amalgamated free products, followed
by elimination of \(s\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P=\langle x,y,z,a,t\mid\;&
 z^2=1,\ x^2=y^2=z,\ [z,x]=[z,y]=1,\ yx=zxy,\\
 &t a^2t^{-1}=a^3,\ [z,a]=[z,t]=1,\\
 &w xw^{-1}=y,\quad w yw^{-1}=x\rangle .
                                                               \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]

This is exactly the group \(\Pi\) in the claim.

The normal-form theorem for amalgamated free products embeds both vertex
groups \(B\) and \(D\) into \(P\). In particular the nontrivial involution
\(z\in Q_8\leq B\) remains nontrivial in \(\Pi\). It is central in both
vertex groups and belongs to their common edge group, so it commutes with
every generator of the amalgam. Thus

\[
 z\in Z(\Pi),\qquad z\ne1.                               \tag{7}
\]

This is the exact perfect-survival half of the terminal program. It is a
group normal-form statement, not an appeal to a formal universal algebra.

## 4. Every exact finite-dimensional representation kills \(z\)

Let \(\pi:\Pi\to U(d)\) be a representation. Its restriction to \(A\)
kills \(w\) by Section 1. Applying \(\pi\) to the last line of (6) gives

\[
 \pi(x)=\pi(y)=:X.
\]

Apply \(\pi\) to \(yx=zxy\). Since \(X\) is unitary,

\[
 X^2=\pi(z)X^2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \pi(z)=I.                                               \tag{8}
\]

Equations (7) and (8) exhibit a central involution which survives in the
group but lies in the kernel of every exact finite-dimensional unitary
representation.

## 5. The quantitative terminal step fails maximally

The group \(A=BS(2,3)\) is itself the fundamental group of a one-vertex
one-loop graph of groups with vertex group \(\mathbb Z\) and edge group
\(\mathbb Z\), embedded as \(2\mathbb Z\) and \(3\mathbb Z\).
The vertex group is hyperlinear and the edge group is amenable.
Fima--Freslon, [arXiv:1307.5609](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5609),
Theorem 4.6(3), Proposition 6.9, and Corollary 6.10(1), therefore imply
that \(A\) is hyperlinear. Hence \(D=A\times C_2\) is hyperlinear.
The other vertex group \(B=Q_8\rtimes_\alpha\mathbb Z\) and the edge
group \(\mathbb Z\times C_2\) are amenable. Applying the same
amenable-edge permanence theorem to (5) proves

\[
 \Pi\ \text{is hyperlinear}.                             \tag{9}
\]

This conclusion is stronger than merely failing to prove an HS collapse.
The trace-preserving embedding of \(L(\Pi)\) into \(R^\omega\) sends the
canonical unitary \(u_z\) to a trace-zero involution, because \(z\ne1\).
Lift the five generator unitaries through the tracial ultraproduct to
unitary matrices. Along a representing subsequence, every relator in the
fixed presentation (6) has normalized Hilbert--Schmidt defect tending to
zero, while

\[
 \|Z_n-I\|_2^2
 =2-2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}_n(Z_n)
 \longrightarrow2.                                     \tag{10}
\]

Thus no dimension-independent function \(\omega(\delta)\to0\) can force

\[
 \|Z-I\|_2\leq\omega(\operatorname{Def}_{(6)}(X,Y,Z,A,T)).
\]

The exact finite-dimensional radical is discontinuous at zero. The
amenable-edge normal form simultaneously proves \(z\ne1\) and guarantees
the asymptotic matrix models which keep it a fixed distance from \(I\).

## 6. Residual gate

The presentation (6) settles the algebraic part of the terminal branch
with only one finite selector, one automorphism, and one
finite-dimensionally invisible actor word. But it also proves a sharp
architectural fence:

- an amenable-edge amalgam can preserve the central mark exactly;
- exact finite-dimensional image collapse can kill that mark in every
  matrix representation;
- their combination still cannot produce a nonhyperlinear group.

A successful replacement needs a nonamenable interface plus a genuinely
quantitative relative-commutant or stability theorem. Property (T) alone
does not supply that theorem, and exact image collapse cannot be
bootstrapped into it. No nonhyperlinear group is constructed here.
