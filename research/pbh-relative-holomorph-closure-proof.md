Automorphisms compose right to left. Let `M` be finitely generated and in `B_A`,
`m >= 1`, `Q = M * F(z_1, ..., z_m)`. Put
`P = M * F(z_0, z_1, ..., z_m) = Q * <z_0>`, and `A = Aut_M(P)`.

## Step 0. The input actor

By `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups`, `M` has solvable word
problem. Since `m + 1 >= 2`, direction (1) ⟹ (2) of
`pbh-passes-to-relative-free-group-automorphisms` puts `A` in `B_A`.

## Step 1. Left multiplication of the new letter

For `g ∈ Q` let `ι(g)` be the endomorphism of `P` that is the identity on `M` and
on `z_1, ..., z_m`, and sends `z_0` to `g^-1 z_0`. It exists by the universal
property of the free product.
- **Multiplicative.** `ι(g)` fixes `Q` pointwise, so
  `ι(g)(ι(h)(z_0)) = ι(g)(h^-1 z_0) = h^-1 g^-1 z_0 = (gh)^-1 z_0`. Hence
  `ι(g) ι(h) = ι(gh)`, since both sides agree on generators.
- **Invertible.** `ι(1) = id`, so `ι(g^-1)` is a two-sided inverse and `ι(g) ∈ A`.
- **Injective.** `ι(g)(z_0) = g^-1 z_0`, and `g^-1 z_0 = z_0` in `P` only for
  `g = 1`.

## Step 2. Extension of old automorphisms

For `α ∈ Aut_M(Q)` let `E(α)` act as `α` on `Q` and fix `z_0`. It is the free
product of `α` and the identity of `<z_0>`, so `E : Aut_M(Q) -> A` is an
injective homomorphism.

## Step 3. Conjugation formulas

For `α ∈ Aut_M(Q)` and `g, k ∈ Q`:
- **(C1)** `E(α) ι(g) E(α)^-1 = ι(α(g))`. On `z_0`, `E(α)^-1` fixes `z_0` and
  `E(α)(g^-1 z_0) = α(g)^-1 z_0`. On `Q`, both sides are the identity, because
  `ι(g)` fixes `Q` pointwise.
- **(C2)** `ι(k) ι(g) ι(k)^-1 = ι(k g k^-1)`, since `ι` is a homomorphism.

## Step 4. (H)

- `E(α)` fixes `z_0`, while `ι(g)` fixes `z_0` only for `g = 1`. So
  `ι(Q) ∩ E(Aut_M(Q)) = 1`.
- By (C1), `E(Aut_M(Q))` normalizes `ι(Q)` and acts on it through `α`.
- So the subgroup of `A` generated by the two images is `ι(Q) ⋊ E(Aut_M(Q))`,
  isomorphic to `Q ⋊ Aut_M(Q)`.

It lies in `B_A` because `A` does and `B_A` is closed under subgroups (part 1 of
`boone-higman-type-a-class-closed-under-finite-extensions`).

## Step 5. (T)

Let `β(x) = k x k^-1` for `x ∈ M`, and put `α = c_(k^-1) ∘ β`. Then `α` fixes
`M` pointwise, so `α ∈ Aut_M(Q)`. Put `T = ι(k) E(α) ∈ A`. By (C1) and (C2),

    T ι(g) T^-1 = ι(k) ι(α(g)) ι(k)^-1 = ι(k α(g) k^-1) = ι(β(g))    (g ∈ Q).

**Normal form.** Every element of `G = J *_β` has the form `t^-p j t^q` with
`p, q >= 0` and `j ∈ J`. This set contains the generators and is closed under
inverses, since `(t^-p j t^q)^-1 = t^-q j^-1 t^p`. It is also closed under
products. The relations give `t^r j' = β^r(j') t^r` and `j t^-r = t^-r β^r(j)`,
so for `q >= p'`

    t^-p j t^q · t^-p' j' t^q' = t^-p (j β^(q-p')(j')) t^(q-p'+q'),

and for `q < p'` it equals `t^-(p+p'-q) (β^(p'-q)(j) j') t^q'`.

**The map.** Define `ρ : G -> A × Z` by `ρ(j) = (ι(j), 0)` for `j ∈ J` and
`ρ(t) = (T, 1)`. It is a homomorphism: `ι` restricted to `J` respects the
relations of `J`, and `(T,1)(ι(j),0)(T,1)^-1 = (ι(β(j)), 0) = ρ(β(j))`.

**Injective.** `ρ(t^-p j t^q) = (T^-p ι(j) T^q, q - p)`. If this is trivial, then
`q = p` and `ι(j) = T^p T^-p = 1`, so `j = 1` and the element is trivial.

**Membership.** `Z = <2> <= GL_1(Q)` is in `B_A` by
`char-zero-linear-groups-satisfy-permutational-boone-higman`. `B_A` is closed
under finite direct products and subgroups (part 1 of
`boone-higman-type-a-class-closed-under-finite-extensions`). So `A × Z` is in
`B_A`, and so is `G`. Taking `J = Q` gives `Q ⋊_β Z = Q *_β`. ∎

No step uses that `M` is simple, finitely presented or MIF. Those properties
enter only through the envelope that Step 0 imports.
