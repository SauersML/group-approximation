**Source facts.** KMS, Bull. Math. Sci. 7 (2017), read again 2026-09-13 from the PDF
text (lane scratch `bhwp/kms-bms.txt`, lines 1319--1425 and 1443). These are the facts
(S1)--(S6) of `kms-lamp-base-obstruction-proof`:
- **(S1)** p. 334, (G1): `H_2 = <L_2>` is abelian.
- **(S2)** Lemma 4.1(2): `x f(a) = x a'`, with `f(t) = t - 1` throughout.
- **(S3)** Remark 4.2, p. 335: the `a'_i` are BR-conjoints of the `a_i` with respect to
  all `x(u)` whose `u` contains no `A_i`.
- **(S4)** (G8): `f ∗ a_i` is a product of conjugates of `f^(±1)` by `a_i` and `a'_i`.
- **(S5)** Theorem 4.3(c), pp. 335--336: `x(q_i) ∗ a_1^(m_1) ∗ ...` equals
  `x(q_j) ∗ a_1^(n_1) ∗ ...` exactly when the words coincide in `Ŝ`.
- **(S6)** Lemma 4.5: `T` is abelian of exponent `p`.

Write `T` additively as an `F_p`-space. `G` acts on it by conjugation; write the
action of `g` as `y |-> g . y`. Put `x = x(q_j)` and `V = V_j`.

**Step 1: the difference map is onto `V`.** The word `q_j` contains no `A_1`.
- By (S2) and (S3), `a' . x = (a - 1) . x`.
- `<a, a'>` is abelian by (S1). So for `y = g . x` with `g in <a, a'>`,
  `a' . y = g . (a' . x) = (a - 1) . y`. Hence `a' = a - 1` on `V`.
- `V` is `<a, a'>`-invariant and `a'` is invertible, so `a - 1` maps `V` onto `V`.
- Conjugation by `a^(-1)` acts on `V` by `a^(-1)`, and
  `a^(-1) - 1 = -a^(-1)(a - 1)` is onto as well.

**Step 2: the `a`-orbit of `x` is infinite.**
- By (S4) each `x ∗ a^(n)` lies in `V`. By (S5), applied with `q_i = q_j` and every
  exponent zero except `m_1 = n`, these elements are pairwise distinct.
- Let `U` be the span of `{a^m . x : m in Z}`. If `U` had finite dimension, `a - 1`
  (injective on `V`, since `a' = a - 1` there) would be bijective on `U`. Then `U`
  would be invariant under `a'^(±1)` and `a^(±1)`, so `U = V`. But `V` is infinite and
  `F_p` is finite, a contradiction.
- So `U` is infinite-dimensional, and the `a`-orbit of `x` is infinite.

**Step 3: transport and apply the lemma.**
- Let `psi : Gamma -> Sym(X)` be the action. Put `V_X = psi(iota(V))` and
  `alpha = psi(iota(a))`.
- `psi ∘ iota` is injective on `V` by hypothesis. So `V_X` is an elementary abelian
  `p`-group of permutations (S6), and `alpha` normalizes it.
- Conjugation by `alpha` on `V_X` corresponds to `v |-> a v a^(-1)` on `V`.
  - By Step 1, `c - 1` is onto, which is (H3).
  - By Step 2, `psi(iota(x))` has an infinite `c`-orbit.
- By `finite-orbit-actions-carry-no-invertible-difference-module`, (H1) or (H2) fails
  for `V_X`.
- If (H1) fails, alternative 1 holds.
- If (H1) holds and `psi(iota(x))` moved points in only finitely many `V_X`-orbits,
  then (H2) would hold.
  - Each generator `psi(iota(g x g^(-1)))`, `g in <a, a'>`, moves the image under
    `psi(iota(g))` of the orbits moved by `psi(iota(x))`. That image is a finite set of
    `V_X`-orbits, since `psi(iota(g))` normalizes `V_X` and permutes its orbits.
  - A product of elements each moving finitely many orbits moves finitely many.
  - So (H2) would hold, a contradiction, and alternative 2 holds. `∎`

**Consequence 1 (finitary).**
- If `psi(iota(x))` is finitary, so is every conjugate of it. Then every element of
  `V_X` is finitary.
- Placement 1 of the lemma then gives (H1) and (H2), contradicting the statement.

**Consequence 2 (finite lamps).** Let `W = B^(Omega) x| Q` act on `Omega x B` by
`(f, g) . (s, b) = (g s, f(g s) b)`, and suppose `iota(x)` lies in the base.
- The base is normal. `V` is generated by conjugates of `x`, so `iota(V)` lies in the
  base.
- The base acts faithfully on `Omega x B`, so the hypothesis of the statement holds
  whether or not `Q` acts faithfully.
- Placement 2 of the lemma gives (H1) and (H2), contradicting the statement. `∎`
