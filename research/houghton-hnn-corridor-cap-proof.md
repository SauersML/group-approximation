Conventions follow `houghton-h3-at-least-cubic-dehn-corridor-proof` (the route
of the cubic claim; "w13" below): right actions, `h^u = u^(-1) h u`, and
`λ_(a,b)` maps `(a,p) ↦ (a,p-1)` for `p >= 2`, `(a,1) ↦ (b,1)`,
`(b,p) ↦ (b,p+1)`. Fix `n >= 3`, `B = Stab(ray n)` and `t = λ_(1,n)`.

## Step 0. H_n is the ascending HNN extension B *_φ, and HNN-shaped presentations exist

This is w13 Step 1b with ray 3 replaced by ray `n`.

- **`φ` maps `B` into `B`.** Let `b ∈ B` and `z = (n,p)`. Then `z t = (n, p+1)`,
  which `b` fixes, and `(n,p+1) t^(-1) = z`. So `t b t^(-1)` fixes ray `n`.
- **`B` is finitely generated.** `B ≅ H_(n-1)` is generated by
  `λ_(1,j)` (`2 <= j <= n-1`) and `α = ((1,1),(1,2))`. The argument of w13 1b
  applies: the conjugates of `α` by powers of `λ_(1,j)` are the adjacent
  transpositions of the line `ray j ∪ ray 1`, these lines together connect all
  points, and the vectors of the `λ_(1,j)` span the sum-zero translation lattice.
  (For `n - 1 = 2` this is part (a) of
  `houghton-ray-stabilizer-distortion-quadratic-then-log`: `B_2 = <X, α>`.)
- **`H_n = <B, t>`.** The same argument, with `j` now allowed to be `n`.
- **Injectivity.** Pushing `t` to the right (`t b = φ(b) t`) and `t^(-1)` to
  the left (`b t^(-1) = t^(-1) φ(b)`) writes every element of the abstract
  HNN extension `G* = <B, t | t b t^(-1) = φ(b)>` as `t^(-a) b t^c`, with
  `a, c >= 0`. Let `τ` be the eventual translation on ray `n`, so `τ(t) = 1`
  and `τ(B) = 0`. If the image of `t^(-a) b t^c` in `H_n` is trivial, then
  `c = a`, so `b = 1` in `H_n`, and hence in `B`.

So `H_n = <S_B, t | R_B, t s t^(-1) W_s^(-1) (s ∈ S_B)>`, where `R_B` is the
set of all `S_B`-words trivial in `B` and `W_s` is any `S_B`-word for `φ(s)`.
`H_n` is finitely presented for `n >= 3`:

> "Corollary B. For n ≥ 2, H_n is of type FP_(n−1) but not FP_n, it is finitely
> presented for n ≥ 3." (S. R. Lee, *Geometry of Houghton's groups*,
> arXiv:1212.0257, Corollary B, as quoted in w13.)

By B. H. Neumann's theorem, a finite subset `R_0 ⊆ R_B` already suffices. That
gives an HNN-shaped presentation.

## Step 1. The arc lemma for an arbitrary HNN-shaped P

Let `D` be a van Kampen diagram over an HNN-shaped `P` for a null-homotopic
word `w`. w13 Step 2b uses only two facts about its presentation: relators in
`R_0` are `t`-free, and relators in `R_1` contain exactly one `t` and one
`t^(-1)` with `S_B`-word sides. So it applies verbatim.

- The `t`-letters of `w` are paired by an involution without fixed points.
- Each pair is either a tree edge, with corridor length 0, or the two ends of
  a corridor. Distinct corridors share no cell.
- Paired letters have opposite exponents.

w13 Step 2c, verbatim, gives the following. If letters `i < j` are paired by a
corridor of `L` cells, then one side of the corridor is a path in `D` from
`v_i` to `v_(j-1)`. Its label `U` is a word in `S_B^(±1)` with `|U| <= M L`,
since each cell contributes one side of length at most `M`. Because `D` is
simply connected, `w(i,j) = U` in `H_n`.

For a tree edge the arc is a closed loop at one vertex, so `w(i,j) = 1`.
Either way:

- **(A)** `w(i,j) ∈ B`, and `|w(i,j)|_(S_B) <= M · L(i,j)`.

## Step 2. Claim part 1

**Lower bound.** By (A), `L(i,j) >= |w(i,j)|_(S_B) / M` in every diagram, so
`b ↦ |b|_(S_B)/M` is a corridor cost.

**Upper bound: the one-strip diagram.** Let `c` be a corridor cost and
`b ∈ B`. Choose a geodesic `S_B`-word `U = s_1 … s_k` for `b`, with
`k = |b|_(S_B)` and `s_r ∈ S_B^(±1)`. For `s ∈ S_B` use the cell
`t s t^(-1) W_s^(-1)` of `R_1`. For `s^(-1)` use the same cell read in the
opposite orientation: its top side is `s^(-1)` and its bottom side is
`W_s^(-1)`. Glue `k` such cells in a row, identifying the right `t`-edge of
cell `r` with the left `t`-edge of cell `r+1`. The result is a planar disc
(a `t`-strip) with boundary word

`w_b = t U t^(-1) W^(-1)`, where `W = W_(s_1)^(±1) … W_(s_k)^(±1)`.

In this diagram, letter 1 (`t`) and letter `k+2` (`t^(-1)`) are joined by the
strip, a corridor of `k` cells, and the arc between them is `U`, which
represents `b`. A corridor cost must hold in every diagram, so
`c(b) <= k = |b|_(S_B)`.

(If `W_s` is empty, the cell's bottom side is a single vertex. The strip is
still a disc, and the count is unchanged.)

## Step 3. Claim part 2 (the cap)

Let `|w| = ℓ`, and let `D` be any diagram. There are at most `ℓ/2` pairs. For
each pair `(i,j)`:

- `w(i,j)` is a subword of `w` of length at most `ℓ - 2`, so
  `|w(i,j)|_(S_B ∪ {t}) <= ℓ`;
- by (A), `w(i,j) ∈ B`.

So `|w(i,j)|_(S_B) <= Dist_P(ℓ)`. By part 1, `c(w(i,j)) <= Dist_P(ℓ)`.
Summing over the pairs,

`Σ c(w(i,j)) <= (ℓ/2) Dist_P(ℓ)`. ∎

Nothing here needs the diagram to be minimal, or the pairing to be forced.
This is the step at which every corridor-cost argument stops: a single
corridor cannot certify more cells than the `B`-length of its arc, and an
arc is a subword of `w`.

## Step 4. Claim part 3

`Dist_P` changes only up to `≃` with the finite generating sets, and
`B ≅ H_(n-1)` is the stabilizer `B_(n-1)` of
`houghton-ray-stabilizer-distortion-quadratic-then-log`.

- **`n = 3`.** By part (a) there, `Dist_P(ℓ) ≼ ℓ^2`. So every corridor lower
  bound is `≼ x · x^2 = x^3`.
- **`n >= 4`.** Here `k = n - 1 >= 3`, and part (b) there gives
  `Dist_P(ℓ) ≼ ℓ log ℓ`. So every corridor lower bound is `≼ x^2 log x`.
  If `Dist_P` is linear, the bound is `≼ x^2`.

**Sharpness at `n = 3`.** Let `P` be any HNN-shaped presentation of `H_3`
with `t = Y = λ_(1,3)`. Put `I = max_(s ∈ S_B) inv(s)`. Then `I >= 1`: if
every generator had `inv = 0`, all of `B` would, by subadditivity (w13 2a),
but `inv(α) = 1`.

By (A), a corridor of `L` cells has a side `U` with `|U| <= M L`. By
subadditivity, `inv(w(i,j)) = inv(U) <= I M L`. So `inv / (I M)` is a corridor
cost for `P`.

Rewrite the w13 words `w_m` over `P`: replace `X` and `α` by fixed `S_B`-words
and `Y` by `t`. This multiplies the length by at most a constant `C_P`, keeps
the same `4m` `t`-letters in the same order, and keeps every arc the same
element of `B`.

w13 Step 3 uses only opposite signs and `arc ∈ B`, and Step 1 provides both
for every HNN-shaped `P`. So the matching is still forced, and it has
`Σ inv = m^2 (m-1)`. Hence every diagram of the rewritten `w_m` has corridor
cost at least `m^2(m-1)/(I M)`, while its length is at most `C_P (6m+2)`.
So the cap `x^3` is attained up to constants. ∎

`referee_and_cap.py` re-derives the forced matching and the value
`m^2(m-1)` independently for `m = 2..12`, by explicit permutation arithmetic
(`referee_and_cap.out`).

## Remark: no one-letter HNN over H_2 inside H_m for m >= 4

The translation homomorphism `H_m → Z^m` has image the sum-zero lattice, of
rank `m - 1`. `H_2 = Stab(rays 3..m)` maps into the rank-1 lattice spanned by
`e_2 - e_1`. For any `g`, the image of `<H_2, g>` lies in a lattice of rank
at most `2`. For `m >= 4`, `2 < m - 1`, so `H_m ≠ <H_2, g>`.

So the quadratic distortion of `H_2` (BCMR Theorem 5.1) cannot enter a
one-stable-letter corridor argument for `H_m`, `m >= 4`. The one-ray
stabilizer `H_(m-1)` is the only base of that shape, and its distortion is at
most `x log x`.

## What this leaves open

- Whether `δ_(H_3) ≃ x^3`. This needs area bounds for the `t`-free regions,
  which carry `R_0`-cells. `H_2` is not finitely presented, so these regions
  are not governed by a Dehn function of `B`.
- Whether `H_(n-1)` is distorted in `H_n` at all for `n >= 4`. If it is
  distorted by exactly `log`, one-ray corridors could still give
  `x^2 log x ≻ x^2` for some `n >= 4`, and this route does not exclude that.
