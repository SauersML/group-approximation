Let `I` be the two-sided ideal of `Z[S]` generated by `1 - t`, and set

    K = { s in S : 1 - s in I } .

`K` is a subgroup and is normal, by three identities that hold in every group
ring:

    1 - ab       = (1 - a) + a(1 - b)            (closure under products)
    1 - a^{-1}   = -a^{-1}(1 - a)                (closure under inverses)
    1 - h a h^{-1} = h (1 - a) h^{-1}            (normality)

In each case the right-hand side lies in `I` whenever the displayed factors
`1 - a`, `1 - b` do, because `I` is a two-sided ideal.  Also `1 - 1 = 0 in
I`, so `1 in K`.

By construction `t in K`.  If `t` normally generates `S`, then `K`, being a
normal subgroup containing `t`, is all of `S`.  Hence `1 - s in I` for every
`s in S`.

Finally, the elements `1 - s` span the augmentation ideal as an abelian
group: an element `sum_s n_s s` has augmentation `sum_s n_s`, and

    sum_s n_s s  -  (sum_s n_s) 1  =  - sum_s n_s (1 - s) .

So an element of augmentation zero is a `Z`-combination of the `1 - s`, and
`I` is exactly the augmentation ideal (the reverse inclusion holding because
each `1 - s` has augmentation zero and `I` is generated by such elements). ∎

## The identities are the whole proof

Worth noting for anyone formalizing this: nothing above uses commutativity,
finiteness, or any property of `Z` beyond being a commutative unital
coefficient ring, so the statement holds over any such coefficient ring.  The
only place a hypothesis on `S` is consumed is the single sentence "a normal
subgroup containing `t` is everything".
