## 1. Reflection groups of dimension ≥ 3

Let `W` act cocompactly on `H^d`, `d ≥ 3`, generated by reflections, with `∂W = S^{d−1}`. Let `s` be a
reflection with wall `H_s`, a totally geodesic `H^{d−1}` with `∂H_s ≅ S^{d−2}`.

- **The half-space identity.** `|sx| − |x| = ±1` is constant on each half-space of `H_s`.
  - So off `φ^{-1}(∂H_s)`, `s` maps deep atoms onto atoms, as in `gq-bh-finf-hyp-pentagon-three-checks.md`,
    item 2.
  - Hence `sing(s) ⊆ φ^{-1}(∂H_s)`.
- **At every point over `ξ ∈ ∂H_s` the mismatch persists.**
  - Near `ξ` both half-spaces of `H_s` are present. There the balls `B_n(s)` and `B_{n∓1}(1)` agree on one side
    and differ by 2 levels across `H_s`.
  - The walls of the tessellation that cross `H_s` meet it in a tessellation of `H_s ≅ H^{d−1}`. Their traces
    accumulate at **every** point of `∂H_s`.
  - A wall crossing `H_s` arbitrarily close to `ξ` belongs to one front and not the other at every level. It cuts
    `sD` for every deep atom `D` around a point over `ξ`, so `sD` is never an atom.
- **Calibration.** For the right-angled pentagon (`d = 2`), `∂H_s` is 2 points, and this predicts exactly the
  observed 4 persistent chains per generator (2 endpoints × 2 sides).
- **Conclusion (sketch).** For `d ≥ 3`, `sing(s) ⊇ φ^{-1}(∂H_s)`, which surjects onto a sphere of dimension
  `d − 2 ≥ 1` and so is **uncountable**. The horofunction RSG is not bounded: its activity is exponential.
- **Scope.** These groups are cubulated, so they already have `F_∞` simple hosts through 2V. What this shows is
  that the germ-extension route to Question 1.2 fails there, not that hosts are missing.

## 2. Kazhdan hyperbolic groups: two regimes

- **Boundary dimension ≥ 2** (for example cocompact lattices in `Sp(n,1)`, with `∂Γ = S^{4n−1}`).
  - **Conjecture U.** Some element of `[[Γ ∗ Z | ∂_h]]` has uncountably many singular points.
  - Reason: the branch locus of `∂_h Γ → ∂Γ` has dimension `≥ dim ∂Γ − 1 ≥ 1`
    (`horofunction-boundary-homology-reduces-to-the-branch-locus`), and Section 1 is the model mechanism.
- **Boundary dimension 1.** Kazhdan hyperbolic groups with Menger-curve boundary exist: random groups at density
  above 1/3 (Żuk; Dahmani–Guirardel–Przytycki; recalled).
  - Here the branch locus may be countable, and **boundedness is open**.
  - A heuristic points the other way. Boundedness would make the frontiers of atom shadows locally `Γ`-invariant
    off a countable set, and that is a candidate wall structure. If finite separation held, Sageev's construction
    would contradict (T). That step is not proved.
  - **So this is the decisive test case.** If the host of a Menger-boundary Kazhdan group is bounded, the `Z^r`
    level-set lemma would give the **first `F_∞` simple host of a Kazhdan hyperbolic group**. If it is not
    bounded, the heuristic is confirmed.

## Consequence for the F_∞ program

- **Where the bounded theory ends.** `F_∞` for bounded full contracting RSGs is now proved for finite defect
  groups and for defect group `Z` with interval defect sets (`level-set-join-complexes-are-highly-connected`,
  refereed). A `Z^r` version (vector level sets, e.g. through sumset saturation) would extend it to all bounded
  hosts, and would test Question 1.2 on higher-rank flux. The pentagon has rank 5.
- **What it can and cannot reach.** By Section 1 and Conjecture U, hosts with boundary of dimension `≥ 2` are not
  bounded, and BHM's germ complex needs finitely many singular points per element. So germ-extension arguments
  reach Kazhdan inputs only in the Menger-boundary regime, and only if those hosts are bounded.
- **The next target.** The Kazhdan frontier of Zaremsky's question is a finiteness theorem for full contracting
  RSGs with **uncountable singular sets**, i.e. a germ complex whose hidden data are clopen neighbourhoods of a
  singular *subshift* rather than finitely many points. No such complex is known.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Activity tracks boundary dimension.** A reflection shears along its whole wall sphere, and the sphere has
  dimension `dim ∂Γ − 1`. Finite singular sets, hence finite germ extensions, occur only when that sphere is
  finite, i.e. for 1-dimensional boundaries.
- **For property (T) inputs of boundary dimension `≥ 2`, the missing object is singular-subshift finiteness, not a
  better flux lemma.** For 1-dimensional boundaries the decisive question is whether a Kazhdan host can be
  bounded.
