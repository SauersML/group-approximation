**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, elementary; not reviewed; no priority claimed). bh-one-relator, 2026-09-18.
This is the first check of E3′ for the hyperbolic seed SFTs (b17ce0c51), as split with bh-p2b-exact.

## Setting

- `Λ` is a countable group, `Y` a compact zero-dimensional metrizable `Λ`-space, and `y_* ∈ Y` a **seed**. That
  is, `y_*` is isolated, its orbit `O = Λ y_*` is dense, and `Stab(y_*) = 1`.
- `∂Y = Y ∖ O` is closed and invariant. For a seed SFT it is the SFT obtained by forbidding the seed pattern.
- `F_Y = F(G_V × (Λ ⋉ Y))` is the group of full compact open bisections acting on `C × Y`, and similarly for
  `F_∂ = F(G_V × (Λ ⋉ ∂Y))`. Since `O` is dense and free, `Λ` acts topologically freely on `Y`, so `F_Y` is
  also the topological full group.

## Theorem

1. **Restriction is onto.** `r : F_Y → F_∂`, restriction to `C × ∂Y`, is a surjective homomorphism.
2. **The kernel is finitary.** `ker r = F_fin`, the elements that are the identity outside `C × S` for some
   finite `S ⊆ O`.
3. **Structure of the kernel.** `F_fin` is the increasing union over finite `S ⊆ O` of groups `F_S ≅ V_(2,|S|)`.
   So it is simple and not finitely generated (when `O` is infinite). It is the normal closure in `F_Y` of the
   copy `V_(y_*)` of `V` acting on the column `C × {y_*}`.
4. **E3′ passes to the boundary.** If `F_Y` is finitely presented, so is `F_∂`, since
   `F_∂ = F_Y / ⟨⟨finitely many elements⟩⟩`.

## Consequences

- **Hyperbolic seed SFTs.** Let `Y_k` be the distance-gradient SFT of a hyperbolic group, with boundary
  `∂Y_k = ∂_h Γ`. Then E3′ for `Y_k` implies that `F(G_V × (Γ ⋉ ∂_h Γ))` is finitely presented. This is the
  V-stabilized, germ-only relative of the BBMZ host `[[Γ | ∂_h Γ]]`.
  - bh-finf-hyp localizes the failures of square filling for the nucleus-tile operad to the branch locus
    (`hyperbolic-cone-matching-holds-off-the-branch-locus`).
  - Cone matching fails for the right-angled pentagon group
    (`pentagon-reflection-group-fails-cone-matching-at-wall-ends`).

  Neither shows non-finite-presentation. But any proof of E3′ for hyperbolic seed hosts must run through the
  boundary host, and hence through the branch locus.
- **Design rule for seed routes (synthesis v6).** A seed buys oligomorphy and a type (A) orbit. It adds a
  finitary, locally V normal subgroup, and it never repairs a boundary that fails finite presentation. So an
  E3′ proof for a seed SFT is an E3′ proof for its boundary SFT, plus the relative question below.
- **The converse is open: the seed-relative question.** Suppose `F_∂` is finitely presented. Then `F_Y` is
  finitely presented only if the relations of the finitary groups `V_(2,n)`, and of the label moves between seed
  columns, follow from finitely many relators modulo those of `F_∂`. This is the infinite-emitter gate (IE) in
  seed form. Reid's group is the cautionary example of that shape: an infinite-emitter host that is not
  finitely presented (`stabilizer-engines-are-fp-only-over-isolated-groups`).

## Proof

**Setup.**
- `O` is open (its points are isolated), so `U = C × O` is open and invariant, and `C × ∂Y` is closed and
  invariant.
- A clopen subset of `Y` that misses `∂Y` is a compact subset of the discrete set `O`, hence finite.

**Item 2.**
- `r` is a well-defined homomorphism: arrows over the invariant closed set `C × ∂Y` stay there, and the
  restriction of a full compact open bisection is one.
- The groupoid is Hausdorff and étale, so its unit space is clopen. Hence the set of points where `g` acts by a
  unit arrow is clopen.
- If `r(g) = 1`, that set contains `C × ∂Y`. Its complement is then a clopen set missing `C × ∂Y`, so it lies in
  `C × S` with `S` finite. So `g ∈ F_fin`.
- Conversely, `F_fin ⊆ ker r`.

**Item 1.**
- *Extend the pieces.* Let `h ∈ F_∂` have pieces `B_(u_i → u'_i) × (λ_i, P_i)`, with `P_i ⊆ ∂Y` clopen. Extend
  each `P_i` to a clopen `P̂_i ⊆ Y` with `P̂_i ∩ ∂Y = P_i`. The overlaps and gaps among the extended sources, and
  among the extended ranges, are clopen sets missing `C × ∂Y`. So they lie in `C × S_0` with `S_0 ⊆ O` finite.
- *Cut out finitely many columns.* Put `S = S_0 ∪ ⋃_i (λ_i S_0 ∪ λ_i^-1 S_0)`, and replace `P̂_i` by
  `P̂_i ∖ S`. The sources are now disjoint, and so are the ranges: a range overlap at a point of `S_0` would come
  from a source point in `λ_i^-1 S_0 ⊆ S`.
- *Fill the gaps.* The leftover source set `L_s` and range set `L_r` are clopen and contained in `C × S'`, with
  `S'` finite. Make both nonempty by also removing one small cone-piece `[u_i w] × {y}`, with `y ∈ P̂_i ∖ S` a seed
  point; such points exist because `O` is dense. The restriction of the groupoid to `C × S'` is
  `G_V × Pair(S')`, since `Λ` acts freely and transitively on `O`. So any two nonempty clopen subsets of `C × S'`
  are exchanged by a full bisection of it.
- Adding such a bisection from `L_s` onto `L_r` gives `ĥ ∈ F_Y` with `r(ĥ) = h`.

**Item 3.**
- `F_S` (support in `C × S`) is `F(G_V × Pair(S)) ≅ V_(2,|S|)`, which is simple (Higman). `F_fin = ⋃_S F_S`,
  a strictly increasing union when `O` is infinite, so it is simple and not finitely generated.
- The normal closure of `V_(y_*) ≤ F_({y_*})` meets every `F_S` with `y_* ∈ S` nontrivially, hence contains it.

**Item 4.** `V_(y_*)` is finitely generated. A quotient of a finitely presented group by the normal closure of
finitely many elements is finitely presented. `∎`

## Lesson for general BH

**Seeds cost finiteness; they never supply it.** The host of a seed SFT is an extension of its boundary host by
a simple, locally V finitary group. So:
- finite presentation of the seed host forces finite presentation of the boundary host;
- the seed can only add relations (the infinite-emitter part).

For the master route's seed objects, the E3′ work splits into two independent pieces:
- the boundary host, where rigidity and cone matching live (for hyperbolic groups, the horofunction boundary and
  its branch locus);
- a seed-relative presentation, the (IE) gate.

Neither can substitute for the other.
