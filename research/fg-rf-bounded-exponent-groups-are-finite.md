title: A finitely generated residually finite group of bounded exponent is finite
distinct_from:
  restricted-burnside-finiteness: that bounds the finite m-generated groups of exponent dividing N; this transfers the bound to every finitely generated residually finite group of bounded exponent, which is the form used against Zaremsky Problem 1.11
---

Let `G` be generated by `m` elements and satisfy `g^N = 1` for all `g in G`.
If `G` is residually finite, then `G` is finite. Its order is at most the
largest order of a finite `m`-generated group of exponent dividing `N`.

**For Zaremsky Problem 1.11.** A finitely presented infinite torsion group of
bounded exponent is not residually finite. A bounded-exponent answer must
live among groups whose finite quotients do not separate points, for example
groups with no nontrivial finite quotients.

**Model tests.**
- Infinite free Burnside groups `B(m,N)`, where they exist, have bounded
  exponent, so by this claim they are not residually finite. Their residually
  finite image `R(m,N)` is finite, as `restricted-burnside-finiteness` says.
- `Z` is residually finite and infinite, and it has no bounded exponent.
