Notation of `kms-hc1-kassel-classes-survive-restricted-initial-forms`.

- `Gamma = Gamma^A2_p`, and `I` is the two-sided ideal of `A = F_p<<a,b,c>>`
  generated by `rho - 1` for the nine relators.
- `J` is the ideal of the nine initial forms: `X_i^p` and the six Serre words.
- `Z` is the span of the three edge elements `(XY - YX)^p`.
- `gr I` is the ideal of all initial forms of `I`.
- `u(L_p)` is the restricted enveloping algebra of `L_p = n^+ + t sl_3[t]`.

By the towers (T) of `kms-hc1-a2-pro-p-completion-is-iwahori-criterion`, for
`p = 5` we have `A/(I + F_16) = F_5[I_1]/omega^16`. So
`gr I = ker(A -> u(L_5))` through degree 15, and the computation below matches
this exactly.

**(A) Lemma: torus eigen-splitting, for every odd `p`.** Put `Y_i = log(1+X_i)`,
truncated below degree `p`, so that `a = E(Y_a) = sum_{k<p} Y_a^k/k!`.

- `Y_i^p` is a unit times `a^p - 1`, so `Y_i^p` lies in `I`.
- For `l` in `F_p^*` we have `a^l = E(l Y_a)` modulo `(Y_a^p)`.
- Every vertex group is Heisenberg, so `[a^l, b^m] = [a,b]^{lm}` is central in
  `<a,b>`. Hence `a -> a^l, b -> b^m, c -> c^n` is an automorphism of `Gamma`.

So the torus `T = (F_p^*)^3` acts on `A` by `Y_i -> l_i Y_i` and maps `I` onto
itself. Since `|T| = (p-1)^3` is prime to `p`, `I` is the direct sum of its
`T`-eigenspaces. So `I` is graded by multidegree modulo `p-1`. It is generated
by the `Y_i^p` and the eigencomponents of the six commutator relators. Each
component involves only the two letters of its edge, and all its terms share
one multidegree class modulo `p-1`. Consequences:

- every tail is shifted from its initial form by a vector in `(p-1)Z^3`;
- every Gröbner-basis element is `T`-homogeneous;
- every jump in the standard basis (an S-pair of degree `s` whose reduction
  leads in degree `> s`) jumps by a multiple of `p-1`.

**Computed for `p = 5`, total degree `<= 15`.** This is an exact Mora standard
basis over `F_5` in eigen-coordinates (`hidden_sb.cpp`, `run_hidden15.sh`,
`hidden15.out`). Truncation by total degree is exact for this order.

**(B) Degree 10: the vertex relations `[x,y]^5`.** The graded ideal `J` misses
exactly three relations through degree 10, namely `(XY-YX)^5` for the three
edges. In eigen-coordinates each one is a generator: the `(1,1,0)`-class
component of `[a,b,a]` reduces to `(Y_bY_a)^5 - (Y_aY_b)^5`, with no tail
through degree 15. The other two edges behave the same way. In Magnus
coordinates the same relations appear as three jumps `6 -> 10`. They are
vertex-local, since `gr F_5[U_xy] = F<X,Y>/(X^5, Y^5, Serre, (XY-YX)^5)`.
So the model `F<X>/J = u(Lambda_p)` of the survival claim is already too
large at degree `2p`. The vertex-exact graded model is
`Lambda' = F<X>/(J+Z) = F<X>/(sum_e gr I_e)`.

**(C) Degree 15: the hidden space.** The Hilbert function of `A/(J+Z)` agrees
with that of `u(L_5)` through degree 14:

```text
1 3 9 21 48 96 189 348 630 1092 1863 3087 5043 8061 12726
```

At degree 15, `A/(J+Z)` has dimension 19764 while `A/I` (equal to `u(L_5)`)
has 19761. The difference sits entirely in multidegree `(5,5,5)`, where the
counts are `1905` and `1902`. So `V = (gr I)_15 / (J+Z)_15` has dimension 3.

The graded ideal generated by the lowest forms of all 96 eigencomponents equals
`J+Z` through degree 15. So `V` consists of true syzygy jumps, not of initial
forms of generators. The standard basis has three hidden elements `h1, h2, h3`,
with leads normal for `J+Z`:

| element | S-pair | jump | origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| `h1` | `Y_cY_aY_b^3` with `Y_b^5` | `7 -> 15` | two tail steps; vertex `b`, edges `bc`, `ab` |
| `h2` | `Y_cY_bY_aY_b` with `(Y_bY_a)^5` | `11 -> 15` | one tail step |
| `h3` | `(Y_cY_a)^5` with `g = Y_cY_aY_cY_aY_cY_aY_b + ...` | `11 -> 15` | one tail step |

The explicit Lie lifts from `kassel_lift.py`, reduced modulo `J+Z`
(`nfsolve.py`), satisfy:

```text
K  = [E12_1, E21_4] - [E12_4, E21_1]   ==  h3                (the Kassel lift; 2z in N)
K' = [E23_1, E32_4] - [E23_4, E32_1]   ==  h3
C2 = H2^5 + [E12_1, E21_4]             ==  h2 + h3           (restricted Cartan relation)
C1 = H1^5 - [E12_1,E21_4] - [E23_1,E32_4]  ==  h1 + 4 h2 + 2 h3
```

So `V = <K, C1, C2>`, and the Kassel line is `F_5 h3`.

**(D) The mechanism for `K`.** The degree-7 word `g` (lead
`Y_cY_aY_cY_aY_cY_aY_b`, multidegree `(3,1,3)`) is built as follows:

```text
g11 = S([b,c,c], [a,b,a])   at Y_cY_cY_bY_aY_a     (bc and ab, glued at b)
g16 = S(g11, [a,c,a])                              (then ca)
g   = S([a,c,c], g16)                              (then ca again)
```

It uses the initial forms of all three edges. Take its S-pair with the vertex
relation `Z_ca = (Y_cY_a)^5 - (Y_aY_c)^5` at the overlap `(Y_cY_a)^5 Y_b`.
This overlap has degree 11 and multidegree `(5,1,5)`.

- The degree-11 part reduces to 0 modulo lowest forms.
- The torus grading (A) forbids degrees 12 to 14.
- The degree-15 remainder has multidegree `(5,5,5) = (5,1,5) + (0,4,0)` and
  equals `K` modulo `J+Z`.

So `K` is produced by exactly one tail step, a `(0,4,0)`-shift. It is linear
in the degree-`+4` tails, that is, first order in the Rees parameter
`t = s^{p-1}`. In contrast, `C1` needs a jump of 8, two tail steps.

**(E) Gluing: the tails cancel only around the whole triangle.**

- **Degree 11.** Keep the tails on one edge `xy` only, with the other edges
  reduced to their initial forms plus `Z`. Then four hidden relations appear
  at degree 11, each from a jump `7 -> 11`. They sit at the multidegrees whose
  `x`- or `y`-degree is 5: for `ab`, at `(5,4,2), (5,2,4), (4,5,2), (2,5,4)`.
  With the two edges at a vertex `v`, the two obstructions at `v`'s
  multidegrees cancel. This is listed for `ab+bc`; the other two pairs give
  the same degree-11 count, 3083 against 3087. Only with all three edges do
  all six cancel. So the
  first-order tails satisfy a vertex cocycle condition.
- **Degree 15.** With tails on any proper subset of the edges, `K` is not in
  the resulting initial ideal: its normal form has 1170 to 1366 terms. With
  all three edges it is. Tails of degree `>= 14` are irrelevant: capping at
  13 gives the exact answer.

So the Kassel direction dies only by gluing the three edges' tails together.

**Consequence for Open 2.** A proof that the Kassel class dies at `3p` must
generalise three things:

1. the torus splitting (A);
2. the vertex relations `[x,y]^p` as genuine eigencomponents (B);
3. one first-order tail step `2p+1 -> 3p` of the S-pair of `Z_ca` with a
   triangle word of degree `2p-3` (D).

The prediction for general `p`, not tested, is this: the word has
multidegree `(p-2,1,p-2)`, the overlap `(Y_cY_a)^p Y_b` has degree `2p+1`, and
the shift is `(0,p-1,0)`. The lowest case `p = 7` needs degree 21, which is
out of reach of this program.

Calibration.

- The Hilbert functions reproduce `u(L_5)` exactly through degree 15. A
  Magnus-coordinate run agrees through degree 13.
- For `p = 3` (outside the claim), the group ideal and `J+Z` agree at
  `(3,3,3)` (both 162). Both exceed `u(L_3)` from degree 6 (141 against 135).
  This matches (T3) of the criterion, where the `p = 3` tower leaves the `I_1`
  pattern at class 6.
