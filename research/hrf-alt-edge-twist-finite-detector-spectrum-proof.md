Write the central kernel additively as `C=F_p`.

For a finite quotient `f:Λ ->> Q`, choose a central extension
`1 -> C -> F_β -> Q -> 1`. Pulling back along `A_v -> Q` splits because
`H^2(A_v;C)=0`. Its splitting is unique because two splittings differ by
a homomorphism `A_v -> C`, and `A_v` is perfect. Denote its lift by `s_v`.
On an oriented edge `e=ij`, both vertex lifts project to the same map, so

```
s_i(x) = z^(d_e(x)) s_j(x).
```

Centrality makes `d_e:E_e -> C` a homomorphism. Set `D_f(β)=(d_e)_e`.
Equivalence of extensions preserves the discrepancies. Baer addition adds
them, so `D_f` is linear.

The maps `(a,t) -> s_v(a)z^t` from the vertex groups of `Λ_c` agree on
the twisted edge maps exactly when `c=D_f(β)`. They give a homomorphism
`Λ_c -> F_β` which is the identity on `C`; its image is the required
finite detector.

Conversely let `ρ:Λ_c -> F` be a finite detector, replacing `F` by its
image, and identify `ρ(C)` with `C`. Then `Q=F/C` is a quotient of `Λ`.
The restrictions of `ρ` to the untwisted vertex factors are the unique
vertex lifts for the central extension `F -> Q`. The defining edge
relations give `D_f([F])=c`.

The group `Λ` is generated by its perfect vertex groups, so it and every
quotient `Q` are perfect. The universal coefficient sequence identifies
`H^2(Q;C)` with `Hom(H_2(Q;Z),C)`, since its `Ext(H_1(Q;Z),C)` term is
zero. This proves the Schur-multiplier description. Pulling back central
extensions along a common product-image refinement preserves every edge
discrepancy, proving the union assertion.

For clarity, twist coordinates are faithful under the hypotheses. If
`Λ_c -> Λ` splits globally, compare the section on each `A_v` with the
canonical lift. Uniqueness forces equality on every vertex. Agreement on
the edges then forces `c=0`. More generally, any central extension of `Λ`
splits uniquely on the vertices, and these lifts recover it as an edge
twist: the colimit map is an isomorphism since it is the identity on both
the central kernel and the quotient. Thus `V ≅ H^2(Λ;C)` here.

Finally take any central extension `E -> Λ` whose class restricts to zero
on `N`. Its section `s:N -> E` is unique when `H^1(N;C)=0`. For every
`e in E`, the conjugate section
`n -> e s(π(e)^(-1)nπ(e)) e^(-1)` is another section, hence equals `s`.
Thus `s(N)` is normal in `E`. The quotient `E/s(N)` is a central extension
of `Q` by `C`, and `E` is its pullback to `Λ`. If `H^2(Q;C)=0` that quotient
extension, hence `E`, splits. This proves restriction injectivity. Faithful
twist coordinates and, when `f` is injective on the vertices, the moment
criterion give the full-moment conclusion. Vertex injectivity supplies
the torsion-free kernel required by that criterion.

**Boundary:** if `H^1(N;C)` is nonzero, a splitting over `N` need not be
normal. The fixed-`Q` moment test allows wreath-product detectors that need
not be central extensions of this same `Q`. The union over *all* finite
quotients above remains exact; replacing it by a single `im D_f` does not.
