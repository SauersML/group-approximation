Call a group `K` *UP-null* if every homomorphism from `K` to a group with
unique products is trivial. Here `Q` has unique products if every pair of
finite nonempty subsets `A, B` of `Q` has an element of `AB` with exactly one
expression `ab`, `a in A`, `b in B`.

For every finitely generated torsion-free UP-null group `K`, every unit of
`Z[K]` is `+-k` with `k in K`.

**Relation to the root.** This claim together with
`connell-group-ring-primality` implies `integral-unit-conjecture-torsion-free`
(route `integral-unit-conjecture-from-no-up-quotient-case`). Conversely, the
root implies this claim, which is its special case on torsion-free UP-null
groups. So the two are equivalent. The reorganization says where a counterexample must
live: a nontrivial unit `u` of `Z[G]`, translated so that `1 in supp u`,
produces a UP-null group generated by finitely many elements. They are either
the supports of the unit and its inverse, or the supports of a support-minimal
zero divisor pair.

**What a nontrivial member looks like.** A nontrivial UP-null group has no
unique products, has finite abelianization, has no nontrivial left-orderable
quotient and is not locally indicable (artifact, Section 5.2). A torsion-free
group has unique products exactly when it contains no nontrivial finitely
generated UP-null subgroup (artifact, Proposition 5.2).

## Attempts

* **The Promislow group is in the class.** Every homomorphism from `P` to a
  group with unique products is trivial
  (`promislow-group-has-no-unique-product-quotient`). So this claim restricted
  to `P` is the root restricted to `P`. Every attack on `Z[P]` recorded under
  `integral-unit-conjecture-torsion-free` transfers here unchanged and dies at
  the same place: Case A of the mod-2 dichotomy, the fixed inverse support, the
  shape of the ansatz, the kernels of the definite shadows. The reduction removes
  nothing on `P`.
* **Proving the class empty of counterexamples by quotients alone.** The
  argument that removes groups with a UP quotient uses Strojnowski's two unique
  products to find a component `u_(a) v_(b)` of `uv = 1` that must vanish. The
  attack dies at coefficient cancellation. For a quotient `Q` without unique
  products, every component of `uv` is a sum over several expressions `q = ab`,
  and the sum can vanish with no single term vanishing. Replacing unique
  products by "`Z[Q]` is a domain with trivial units" gives no replacement lemma
  (artifact, Section 5.6).
* **Passing to finite-index subgroups.** `P` has an abelian normal subgroup `L`
  of index at most `4`, generated by `a^2`, `b^2` and `(ab)^2`, which maps onto
  `Z`. A reduction that could pass to finite index would remove `P`. The attack
  dies at the unit itself: no translate of a nontrivial unit of `C[P]` lies in
  `C[L]` (artifact, Section 5.3, last bullet). So the class is not closed under
  finite index, and units do not descend.
* **Reduction without Connell.** Without `connell-group-ring-primality`, the
  same lemmas derive the root from two statements over the class: trivial units
  in `Z[K]` whenever `Z[K]` is a domain, and no zero divisors in `Z[K]`
  (artifact, Section 5.5). The attack dies at the second statement, which is a
  zero-divisor conjecture on the class and is open. It trades the literature
  import for a second open hole, so the single hole with Connell's theorem is
  recorded instead.
* **Coefficient change does not help.** The analogue over `C` is false, because
  `P` is in the class and `C[P]` has nontrivial units with coefficients in
  `Z[zeta_8]` (`unit-conjecture-fails-over-complex-numbers`). A proof has to
  use integrality of the coefficients, exactly as for the root.
