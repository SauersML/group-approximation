## Statement

Let `B = ⟨a, b⟩` be the Basilica group with `a = (1, b)` and `b = (1, a)ε`, in main's right-action convention
(`basilica-group-is-torsion-free-and-contains-every-z-n`), and let `B~` be its finitely presented HNN extension.
1. `B` is not bi-orderable. Explicitly, `x = (a, a^b)` lies in `B` and commutes with `b^2`, but not with `b`.
2. So `B` and `B~` are subgroups of no diagram group and of no bi-orderable group, for example `PL_+[0,1]`.
3. `B` is not residually torsion-free nilpotent, since such groups are bi-orderable.

## Proof

- **Coordinates.** Elements of `St_B(1)` multiply coordinatewise, and `ε (g, h) ε = (h, g)`.
  - `b^(−1) = ε (1, a^(−1))`, and `b^2 = (1,a) ε (1,a) ε = (1,a)(a,1) = (a, a)`.
  - `a^b = b^(−1) a b = ε (1, a^(−1))(1, b)(1, a) ε = (a^(−1) b a, 1)`. It commutes with `a = (1, b)`, since the supports
    are disjoint. This is the Bartholdi–Virág relation `[a, a^b] = 1`.
- **`x ∈ B`.** `ψ(St_B(1))` is the fiber product `{(g, h) ∈ B × B : ε_a(g) = ε_a(h)}`.
  - It contains `B' × B'`, by item 3 of the torsion-free node.
  - Modulo `B' × B'` it is generated by `ψ(a) = (1, b)`, `ψ(b a b^(−1)) = (b, 1)` and `ψ(b^2) = (a, a)`.
  - Since `ε_a(a) = ε_a(a^b) = 1`, `x = (a, a^b)` lies in this fiber product, hence in `B`.
- **`[x, b^2] = 1`.** Both `x b^2` and `b^2 x` equal `(a^2, a^b a)`, using `a a^b = a^b a`.
- **`[x, b] ≠ 1`.** `b (g, h) b^(−1) = (1,a)(h, g)(1, a^(−1)) = (h, a g a^(−1))`. So `b x b^(−1) = (a^b, a)`, which equals
  `x` only if `a^b = a`. That would make `B` abelian, but `B` has exponential growth.
- **Not bi-orderable.** In a bi-ordered group, `u < v` gives `u^2 < u v < v^2`, so square roots are unique. Then
  `x b^2 x^(−1) = b^2` forces `x b x^(−1) = b`, a contradiction. ∎
- **Item 2.**
  - Guba–Sapir, *Diagram groups are totally orderable*, J. Pure Appl. Algebra 205 (2006) 48–73 (arXiv:math/0305153,
    read at source), define "orderable" as a total order with `a < b ⇒ ca < cb` and `ac < bc`, and prove that every
    diagram group of every directed 2-complex is orderable in this sense.
  - Bi-orderability passes to subgroups. `PL_+[0,1]` is bi-orderable by the sign of the right derivative at the
    leftmost point of the support (Brin–Squier, as cited there).
- **Item 3.** Residually torsion-free nilpotent groups are bi-orderable (standard: order the torsion-free lower
  central quotients lexicographically). ∎

## What this does to the crux (`basilica-hnn-extension-is-fp-infinity`)

- Thompson's `F` gets `F_∞` from a free action on Farley's CAT(0) cube complex for diagram groups, with a Morse
  filtration. **That route is closed for `B~`**, even by restriction, because `B~` is not a subgroup of any diagram
  group.
- **Scope.** This is no obstruction to free actions on other CAT(0) cube complexes. The Klein bottle group acts freely
  on the square tiling of the plane and is not bi-orderable. A free cube action for `B~` would have to come from
  walls that are not diagram-theoretic.
- The remaining certificates are: a finite complete rewriting system; a free action on a non-diagram cube complex
  (for example from a wallspace built from the branch structure); or the product-coefficient condition on the crux
  node.

## Lesson for general BH

Unique roots fail in weakly branch groups. Commuting with `b^2 = (a, a)` is a coordinatewise condition, while commuting
with `b` involves the coordinate swap. So branch groups carry an order-theoretic obstruction that keeps them out of
the whole Thompson and diagram-group world: diagram groups, `PL_+[0,1]`, and residually torsion-free nilpotent groups.
For BH this confirms that branch inputs need V-type (germ) hosts rather than `F`-type hosts. For finiteness it removes
the best-known free-action machine.
