title: Pauli incidence rectangles are the threshold case for relative small cancellation

Collapse every X-equality cloud to the factor `H_X` and every Z-equality
cloud to `H_Z`, and give each signed X--Z pair edge a letter in the free
factor generated by `P_0 union P_1`.  A rectangle of four pair incidences
then has a contour of free-product syllable length at most eight:

```text
p H_Z p^-1 H_X p H_Z p^-1 H_X.                                (PR8-1)
```

The complete Pauli commutation table, and its ordinary occurrence-cloud
degree reduction, contains such rectangles.  Therefore any direct use of
`relative-c-sixth-protects-sign-double-cosets` is a threshold construction:
every essential rectangle must have exactly eight syllables after free
reduction, and relative `C'(1/6)` permits no repeated piece of length two.
Any cancellation, two-pair bigon, or repeated adjacent relative pattern
breaks the hypothesis.

This does not refute the occurrence route or prove that the threshold cannot
be met.  It shows exactly what a terminal construction must verify: no mixed
contour shorter than a rectangle, and uniqueness of every adjacent relative
syllable pair on the eight-syllable rectangles.  Alternatively one can use a
finite **base address group** which absorbs the bounded templates while
keeping the sign double cosets separated, followed by relative small
cancellation only for long contours.
