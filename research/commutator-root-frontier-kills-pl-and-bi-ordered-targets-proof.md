## Inputs

- **(C)** `𝓗` is closed under subgroups, extensions and directed unions. It contains free groups and bi-orderable
  groups, and every group in it is locally indicable, hence torsion-free. Item 1 of
  [[vertex-injective-quotients-give-hughes-free-division-rings]].
- **(E)** Item 1 of [[edge-injective-quotients-give-hughes-free-division-rings]]: a graph of groups with vertex
  groups in `𝓗` lies in `𝓗` if it has a homomorphism to a group in `𝓗` that is injective on every edge group.
- **(F)** Item 3 of [[malcev-root-steps-put-commutator-root-one-relators-in-h]]: for the 26 words, every map
  from a Magnus segment `S_[0,n]`, `n ≥ 2`, to a residually-𝒮 group kills the interior letters.
- **(M)** Magnus–Moldavanskii: for a one-relator group with a letter `t` of exponent sum `0`, the kernel of the
  `t`-exponent map is the directed union of the segments, each obtained from the last by an HNN extension, and the
  bottom segment is free (Freiheitssatz).

Throughout, `G = G(K; α, β) = <y, t | y_1^K = [y_0^α, y_2^β]>`, with `y_i = t^{-i} y t^i`,
`[g, h] = g^{-1} h^{-1} g h`, and `α, β, K` nonzero integers. For a homomorphism `φ` write `g = φ(y)`,
`s = φ(t)` and `g_n = s^{-n} g s^n = φ(y_n)`. The relation reads

`(R)  g_1^K = g_0^{-α} g_2^{-β} g_0^α g_2^β`, and the same with every index shifted by `n`.

## Item 1: the normal form

`commutator_normal_form.py` takes each word `w ∈ F(a, b)`. It runs over pairs `(A, B)` of words of length at most 3
in `t, y` such that one of them is `t^{±1}` and the other has exactly one letter `y^{±1}`, so that `{A, B}` is a
basis of `F(t, y)`, and such that `y` has height `0`. For each pair it rewrites `w(A, B)` in the letters `y_i`,
merges syllables cyclically, and tests for the five-syllable shape `y_1^k y_i^{s_1} y_j^{s_2} y_i^{-s_1} y_j^{-s_2}`
with `{i, j} = {0, 2}` and `|s_1| = |s_2| = 1`.

A hit gives `y_1^K = [y_0^α, y_2^β]` after solving the relator for `y_1^k`. The script then normalizes:
- `y → y^{-1}` flips all three signs, which makes `K > 0`;
- `t → t^{-1}` sends `(α, β)` to `(-β, -α)`.

**Verification.** Each hit is verified by expanding the syllables back into a word in `t, y`. The expansion must
agree, up to conjugation by a power of `t`, with a cyclic rotation of `w(A, B)` or of its inverse. `{A, B}` is a
basis, so `<a, b | w> ≅ <t, y | w(A, B)> = G(K; α, β)`.

**Result.** All 26 words have a hit (`normal-form-26.txt`), with the tally of the claim.

In `G`, the abelianization is `Z ⊕ Z/K`. So `Hom(G, Z) = Z`, and the kernel `N` of the `t`-exponent map is
canonical. It is the Magnus kernel of the original presentation too, and it is the normal closure of `y`.

## Item 2: one map suffices

**The tower.** Shifting the relation gives `y_{j+2}^{-β} y_j^α y_{j+2}^β = y_j^α y_{j+1}^K` for every `j`.
- By (M), `N_[0,1] = <y_0, y_1>` is free.
- For `n ≥ 1`, `N_[0,n+1]` is the HNN extension of `N_[0,n]` with stable letter `y_{n+1}`. Since `β = ±1`, it
  conjugates `y_{n-1}^α` to `y_{n-1}^α y_n^K`.
- Downward, `y_{-m-1}` conjugates `y_{-m+1}^{-β}` into `N_[-m, n]`, by the same relation solved for its
  lowest letter, using `α = ±1`.
- `N` is the directed union of the `N_[-m, n]`.

Every associated subgroup is infinite cyclic and generated by a conjugate, inside the next segment, of some
`y_i^{±1}`.

**Proof.** Let `φ: G → Q ∈ 𝓗` with `φ(y) ≠ 1`.
- `Q` is torsion-free, so every `φ(y_i^{±1})` has infinite order. So does every conjugate of it.
- So `φ`, restricted to any segment, is injective on both associated subgroups of every step.
- By induction on `m + n`, using (E) with vertex group the previous segment and the free base as the start,
  every `N_[-m, n]` lies in `𝓗`.
- By (C), `N ∈ 𝓗` as a directed union, and `G = N ⋊ Z ∈ 𝓗` as an extension.

Conversely, if `G ∈ 𝓗` then `φ = id` works, because `y ≠ 1` in `G` (Freiheitssatz). ∎

## Item 3: the PL obstruction

Let `Q ≤ PL_+(I)`, where `I ⊆ R` is an interval and the elements have finitely many breakpoints. Let `φ: G → Q`,
and suppose `g ≠ 1`. We derive `K ∈ {α, -β}`.

**Germs at `-∞`.** Suppose first that `I` is unbounded below and the support of `g` is unbounded below.
- Every element of `Q` is affine, `x ↦ μx + c`, near `-∞`. The germ at `-∞` is a homomorphism
  `Q → Aff^+(R)`.
- Its composite with the slope `μ` kills `φ(N)`: `N^ab` is torsion (item 5) and `R_{>0}` is torsion-free.
- So `φ(N)` maps into the translation group `R`, which is torsion-free abelian. So it maps to `0`.
- So `g` is a translation of amount `0` near `-∞`, that is, the identity there. This contradicts the assumption.

**The left ends.** So `p_0 = inf supp(g)` is a real number, a point of `I` or its left endpoint. Here `g` fixes `p_0`
and is the identity on `I ∩ (-∞, p_0]`.
- `g` has finitely many breakpoints, and `p_0` is a limit of moved points from the right. So `g` is
  `x ↦ p_0 + λ(x - p_0)` on some `[p_0, p_0 + ε)`, with `λ ≠ 1`.
- Since `supp(g_n) = s^{-n}(supp g)`, the left end of the support of `g_n` is `p_n = s^{-n}(p_0)`.
- By the chain rule for right derivatives, `g_n` has right slope `λ` at `p_n`. Here
  `(s^{-n})'(p_0^+) (s^n)'(p_n^+) = 1`, because `s^n` is increasing and maps `p_n` to `p_0`.
- `s^{-1}` is increasing, so `(p_n)` is constant, strictly decreasing or strictly increasing.

Two facts are used below. For elements `x, u`:
- (i) `u^{-1} x u` has left support end `u^{-1}(inf supp x)`, with the same right slope there.
- (ii) Suppose `x, z` are the identity on `(-∞, q]`, `x` has right slope `μ ≠ 1` at `q`, and `z` is the identity
  on a neighborhood of `q`. Then `xz` and `zx` have left support end `q` and right slope `μ` there.

**Constant.** All `g_n` fix `p = p_0`.
- The right slope at `p` is a homomorphism from the stabilizer of `p` to `R_{>0}`, and it kills commutators.
- (R) gives `λ^K = 1`. So `λ = 1`, a contradiction.

**Decreasing,** `p_2 < p_1 < p_0`. Rewrite (R) as `g_0^α g_1^K = g_2^{-β} g_0^α g_2^β`.
- On the left, `g_1^K` has left end `p_1` and slope `λ^K ≠ 1` there. `g_0^α` is the identity on `(-∞, p_0]`,
  which is a neighborhood of `p_1`. By (ii), the left side has left end `p_1` and slope `λ^K` there.
- By (i), the right side has left end `g_2^{-β}(p_0)` and slope `λ^α` there.
- So `λ^K = λ^α`, and `K = α`.

**Increasing,** `p_0 < p_1 < p_2`. Rewrite (R) as `g_1^K g_2^{-β} = g_0^{-α} g_2^{-β} g_0^α`.
- `g_2^{-β}` is the identity near `p_1`. So by (ii) the left side has left end `p_1` and slope `λ^K` there.
- By (i), the right side has slope `λ^{-β}` at its left end.
- So `K = -β`.

If `K ∉ {α, -β}`, every case is contradictory, so `g = 1`. All 26 words have `K ≥ 2` and `α, β = ±1`. ∎

## Item 4: the bi-order obstruction

Let `Q` be bi-ordered. For `h ≠ 1`, let:
- `D_h` be the smallest convex subgroup containing `h`;
- `C_h` be the union of the convex subgroups not containing `h`.

Standard facts:
- Convex subgroups form a chain under inclusion.
- `C_h ◁ D_h`, there is no convex subgroup strictly between them, and `D_h / C_h` is order-isomorphic to a
  subgroup of `R` (Hölder), so it is abelian.
- The projection `D_h → D_h / C_h` is order-preserving. So if `a ∈ D_h ∖ C_h` and `b ∈ C_h`, then `ab` and `ba`
  both have the sign of `a`.
- Conjugation is an order automorphism, so `D_{u^{-1} h u} = u^{-1} D_h u`, and the sign of `u^{-1} h u` is the
  sign of `h`.
- `D_{h^k} = D_h` for `k ≠ 0`, and `h^k` has sign `sign(k)·sign(h)`.

Let `φ(y) = g ≠ 1`, and let `v_n = D_{g_n} = s^{-n} v_0 s^n`. Conjugation by `s^{-1}` preserves inclusion, so
`(v_n)` is constant, strictly decreasing or strictly increasing. All `g_n` have the sign `σ` of `g`.

**Constant.** Let `v_n = D`.
- Every convex subgroup strictly inside `D` misses every `g_n`, so `C_{g_n} = C` does not depend on `n`.
- In the abelian group `D/C`, (R) gives `K[g_1] = 0`. Since `D/C` is torsion-free, `g_1 ∈ C`, a contradiction.

**Decreasing,** `v_0 ⊋ v_1 ⊋ v_2`. Write (R) as `g_1^K = (g_0^{-α} g_2^{-β} g_0^α) · g_2^β`.
- The first factor has class `W = g_0^{-α} v_2 g_0^α`. The second has class `v_2`.
- If `W ⊆ v_2`, then `g_1^K ∈ v_2`, so `v_1 ⊆ v_2`, a contradiction.
- So `W ⊋ v_2`, hence `v_2 ⊆ C_h` for `h = g_0^{-α} g_2^{-β} g_0^α`. By the sign fact,
  `sign(g_1^K) = sign(h) = -sign(β)·σ`.
- Since `sign(g_1^K) = sign(K)·σ`, we get `sign K = -sign β`.

**Increasing,** `v_0 ⊊ v_1 ⊊ v_2`. Write (R) as `g_1^K = g_0^{-α} · (g_2^{-β} g_0^α g_2^β)`.
- The first factor has class `v_0`, and the second has class `W = g_2^{-β} v_0 g_2^β`.
- If `W ⊆ v_0`, then `v_1 ⊆ v_0`, a contradiction.
- So `v_0 ⊆ C_h` for `h = g_2^{-β} g_0^α g_2^β`, and `sign K · σ = sign α · σ`. So `sign K = sign α`.

For `(α, β) = (-1, 1)` and `K > 0`, the three cases are all impossible, so `g = 1`. ∎

In the increasing case the proof also gives `v_1 = W`: the relation is then `g_1^K ≡ g_2^{-β} g_0^α g_2^β`
modulo `C_{g_1}`. This is the recursion that the claim's next step proposes to use.

## Item 5: the fibre obstruction

**`N^ab`.** Abelianizing the presentation of `N` by the generators `y_i` and the relations `(R)_j` gives
`K y_{j+1} = 0` for all `j`. So `N^ab = ⊕_i Z/K = (Z/K)[t^{±1}]`, which is torsion. Hence `φ(N)` lies in the kernel
of every homomorphism from `Q` to a torsion-free abelian group.

**The kill.** Suppose `φ(N) ≤ L`, where every finitely generated subgroup of `L` is residually 𝒮.
- The Magnus segments `S_[0,2]` of the original words lie in `N` (item 1), and `φ(S_[0,2])` is finitely
  generated.
- So (F) applies to `φ|S_[0,2]`. It kills the conjugate `a_1` of the original generator, whose normal closure is
  `N ∋ y`.

**Baumslag–Solitar groups.** Take `Q = BS(m, n) = <a, u | u^{-1} a^m u = a^n>` and let `χ` be the
`u`-exponent map to `Z`.
- `φ(N) ≤ ker χ`, since `Z` is torsion-free.
- `ker χ` is the directed union of the finite chains `<a_i, …, a_j>`, with `a_i = u^{-i} a u^i` and
  `a_i^m = a_{i+1}^n`.
- Such a chain maps to the additive group of rationals, injectively on each vertex group, by `a_i ↦ (m/n)^i`. The kernel of
  that map acts freely on the Bass–Serre tree, so it is free, and the chain is free-by-`Z`.
- A free-by-`Z` group `F ⋊ Z` is residually 𝒮: `γ_c F` is characteristic, `(F/γ_c F) ⋊ Z ∈ 𝒮`, and
  `∩_c γ_c F = 1`.
- 𝒮 is closed under subgroups, by intersecting the series. ∎

## Item 6: the searches

- `nielsen_deep.py` does a breadth-first search over elementary Nielsen moves, to depth 8, with length at most
  `|w| + 10`. Each image is passed to the certifier of the Mal'cev node with rules R and N switched on.
  `nd810.out` records `none` for all 26 words.
- `bg_search2.py` sends `a, b` to reduced words of length at most 4 in `G(1, n)`. It decides the relator and the
  nontriviality of `φ` on the Magnus kernel exactly, by Britton reduction in the HNN extension of
  `BS(1, n) = Z[1/n] ⋊ Z`. `bg2-4.out` records `none` for every word and every `n` it lists.
- These are bounded searches and prove nothing about longer images.
