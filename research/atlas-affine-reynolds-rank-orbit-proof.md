List the distinct conjugates of `mu` under `K` as
`mu_1=mu,mu_2,...,mu_n`.  Then

```text
U-E_K(U)
=lambda(mu_1) [1-(1/n)sum_(i=1)^n lambda(mu_1^(-1)mu_i)]. (ARP1)
```

Let `L` be the subgroup of the translation module `M` generated by the
distinct elements `mu_1^(-1)mu_i`.  The bracket in `(ARP1)` is `1-T`, where
`T` is the average of these `n` left-translation unitaries.  Strict convexity
of Hilbert space gives

```text
Ker(1-T)=intersection_i Ker(lambda(mu_1^(-1)mu_i)-1)
        =l2(E)^L.                                    (ARP2)
```

In the left regular representation, the `L`-fixed space consists of
functions constant on left `L`-cosets, hence has normalized dimension
`1/|L|`.  Since `L` contains the `n` distinct orbit differences,
`|L|>=n`.  Multiplication by `lambda(mu_1)` does not change rank, so

```text
rank(U-E_K(U))/dim l2(E)
=1-1/|L| >=1-1/n,                                   (ARP3)
```

proving `(AFO2)`.

For sufficiently small `t`, `P_t` is the support projection of
`|U-E_K(U)|`, so its rank is the left side of `(ARP3)`.  The exact affine
audit gives zero for all five boundary residuals.  The collision residual is
`lambda(g)-1` for a nonidentity involution `g`, hence has singular value `2`
on the `-1` eigenspace of normalized dimension `1/2` and zero elsewhere.
Its polar initial and final high spaces coincide.  Therefore `rank Z_t=d/2`
for every small `t` with `kappa t<=2`.  If `n>=3`, `(ARP3)` makes
`rank P_t>d/2`, contradicting the injectivity required by `(PAI3)`.

Finally, a subgroup of `S3` containing neither named transposition has index
`2`, `3`, or `6`; the index-two case is necessarily the alternating subgroup
`<h_a h_b>`.  This proves `(AFO4)--(AFO5)`.

## Lightweight membership certificate

Here `M=F2[A8]/I` and `m=1+I`, where `I` is the left ideal generated by the
aligned derivatives of the 234 boundary words.  The archived file
`atlas-boundary-f2-bridge-ideal.json` was produced after
the boundary ideal reached rank `20005` with an empty closure queue.  It
contains four dual supports.  The generating replay verifies that each
support annihilates every ideal basis row before storing it.

`atlas_affine_raw_c3_membership_certificate.py` reconstructs the alignment
and the two raw letters from the authoritative word sources, enumerates only
the 20,160 group labels, and pairs `1+h_a h_b` with those already archived
dual supports.  All four pairings equal one.  A vector in the ideal is killed
by every such functional, so `1+h_a h_b` is not in the ideal.  This certifies
`n` is `3` or `6` without rebuilding the ideal or constructing the enormous
affine group.

For an independent proof that the archived support really annihilates `I`,
`atlas_boundary_f2_bridge_witness_verify.py` checks by direct convolution all
left translates of all 234 boundary derivatives.  It reports exact support
size `9408` and no failure.  Thus the small membership verifier uses a fully
replayable dual certificate, not merely metadata from the old echelon run.
