**Status.**
- Items 1 and 2 are elementary lane proofs, unreviewed. Item 2 is an explicit form of Hochman's embedding
  mechanism: positive entropy and dense minimal points put `Aut` of the full shift inside (ETDS 2010,
  Theorem 3). It is credited to him, and no priority is claimed.
- Item 3 is a lane proof for the stated class of designs.
- **T2 is not built.** The construction passes the `F_2` test and fails minimality and freeness.

## Statement

1. **The tower law is satisfiable by an explicit singular set.** Let `K = ∏_i K_i` and `Z = M × K` with any
   skew action. Pick closed sets `B_i ⊆ K_i` with nonempty complement, of Haar measure `β_i = 1 − 1/(i+1)`.
   Put `Z_1 = M × ∏_i B_i`. Then:
   - `Z_1` is closed, nowhere dense and null;
   - `ν(Z_1 · K_(>N)) = ∏_(i≤N) β_i = 1/(N+1)`.

   Since `log I_N ≥ N log 2`, this meets the tower law `ν(Z_1 · K_(>N)) ≳ (log I_N)^(-a)` for every
   repetitivity exponent `a ≥ 1`, in particular `a = d = 2`. So the inequality itself is never the
   obstruction.
2. **Loops make free cellular-automaton groups local at every scale.** Let `X` be Hochman's
   nested-square-path `Z^2`-SFT (ETDS 2010, §6), and let `X_B` be the SFT that adds a free label from `B`
   to every path cell. Let `Γ ≤ Aut(B^Z)` be free of rank 2; such subgroups exist for `|B| ≥ 2`
   (Boyle–Lind–Rudolph, recalled). Then:
   - applying `γ ∈ Γ` along every path is an automorphism `φ_γ` of `X_B`, as a cyclic word on closed
     paths and a bi-infinite word on infinite paths;
   - `γ ↦ φ_γ` is injective, so `F_2 ≤ Aut(X_B)`.

   `X_B` is not minimal and not free, and it has positive entropy. It passes only the `F_2` test. The
   point is **locality**: a loop carries a periodic configuration, and a cellular automaton acts on it with
   the radius it has on `B^Z`, at every level at once. This needs `d ≥ 2`; in `Z`, stored words have ends.
3. **What minimality costs: two-sided locality.**
   - *The class of designs.* Consider any minimal `Y` built as follows:
     - (i) a square tiling with the labels of every level-`n` loop drawn from a finite set `O_n`;
     - (ii) lifts `φ_γ` that act by the loop cellular automata;
     - (iii) consistency rules saying that the labels of two level-`n` loops in one point are related by a
       transport map `h ∈ Sym(O_n)`, which may depend on the tiling.
   - *The conclusion.* Let `H_n` be the group generated by the transports. Then:
     - (a) each transport commutes with the action of `Γ` on `O_n`, so `Γ` acts on `O_n` through the
       centralizer of `H_n`;
     - (b) minimality forces `H_n` to be **transitive** on the labels that occur;
     - (c) hence `Γ` acts on the level-`n` labels through a subgroup of `N_(H_n)(H_(n,x)) / H_(n,x)`.
   - *Consequences.*
     - This is abelian whenever `H_n` is abelian, in particular for **odometer-driven transports**
       (labels depending only on position modulo the level-`n` lattice). Then the commutator subgroup of
       `Γ` acts trivially at every level, and `Γ` is not faithful. This is the combinatorial face of the
       profinite collapse.
     - A faithful free action therefore needs **non-abelian transports that commute with the lifted
       automorphisms, both realized by local rules**. The model case is a regular two-sided action, with
       left multiplication as transport and right multiplication as lift.
   - *Two-sided locality* is this requirement. It is the open core of T2, and of T1 run through a product
     of `SU(n)`s.
     - Constant left and right multiplications on a group alphabet `B = Q` commute and are local (radius
       0), but they give only the fixed finite group `Q`.
     - Growth requires commuting non-abelian families of positive radius.
     - Two families of cellular automata on independent tracks commute, but each acts on its own track
       only, so the transport is not transitive on the lifted track. That fails (b).

## Proof

**1.** `Z_1` is closed, being a product of closed sets. It is nowhere dense: every basic open set
constrains finitely many coordinates, and `B_i ≠ K_i` for the others. `ν(Z_1) = ∏ β_i = 0`. Also
`Z_1 · K_(>N) = M × ∏_(i≤N) B_i × K_(>N)`, of measure `∏_(i≤N) β_i = 1/(N+1)`.

**2.**
- Arrows determine each path cell's successor, so `φ_γ` has a local rule of the radius of `γ`, read along
  the path. It maps `X_B` onto itself because labels are unconstrained, and `φ_(γ^(-1))` inverts it.
- *Faithfulness.* If `γ ≠ 1`, pick `x ∈ B^Z` with `γ(x)(0) ≠ x(0)`. Label an infinite path of an
  exceptional point by `x`, or label a closed path of length at least `2 rad(γ) + 1` by a periodic word
  agreeing with `x` near `0`. Then `φ_γ` moves that point.

**3.**
- *(a)* If a point satisfies `w_(L′) = h(w_L)`, its image under a lift uses the same tiling and so the
  same `h`. The rule must still hold: `γ w_(L′) = h(γ w_L)` for all occurring `w_L`.
- *(b)* In one point, all level-`n` labels lie in one `H_n`-orbit. A limit of such points has labels in
  the same finite orbit, so each orbit spans a closed invariant set. Minimality leaves one orbit.
- *(c)* The centralizer of a transitive permutation group `H` on `O` is `N_H(H_x)/H_x`. For abelian
  transitive `H`, the action is regular and the centralizer is `H` itself, which is abelian. ∎

## Lesson for general BH

**Loops give locality, and minimality demands two-sidedness.**
- In `Z^2`, closed loops let a free group of cellular automata act at every scale with bounded radius.
  This is the geometric reason the carrier question lives in dimension at least 2.
- Minimality ties all loop labels together by transports, and the lifts must commute with them. So the
  free commutant needs a second, commuting, non-abelian and equally local action on the same data. That
  is a two-sided locality problem for groups of cellular automata.
- Odometer-driven transports are abelian, which is the profinite collapse again.

T2 now reduces to one concrete question: **do there exist two commuting groups of cellular automata, one
of them free, acting on a common family of periodic configurations, with the other transitive on a
growing tower of orbits?** Both groups would be local. The measure side of the tower law is never the
obstacle.
