Let `G_1, G_2` be finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic groups with
trivial finite radical, let `D` be an **infinite normal subgroup of `G_2`**,
and let `F_i subset G_i` be finite.  Then there are `Q in AH_0` and
surjections `alpha_i : G_i ->> Q` with

1. `alpha_i|_{F_i}` injective;
2. **`alpha_2(D) = Q`** — the prescribed normal subgroup already surjects;
3. every finite-order element of `Q` lifts to one of the same order in
   `G_1 * G_2`, so `Q` is torsion-free when `G_1, G_2` are;
4. `Q` finitely presented when `G_1, G_2` are.

Clause 2 is the whole content; without it this is [Hul16, Corollary 7.4].
The proof is Hull's, with the two elements generating his suitable subgroup
drawn from `D` instead of from `G_2` — legitimate because an infinite normal
subgroup of a torsion-free acylindrically hyperbolic group is suitable, which
follows from [Osi16, Lemma 7.1] on s-normal subgroups.

The reusable content: **in Hull's machinery, the reservoir of
small-cancellation target words may be confined to any prescribed infinite
normal subgroup, at no cost.**  Any construction that needs a common quotient
to be normally generated by the image of a designated subgroup can take it
from here, by feeding `D` the normal closure of that subgroup.
