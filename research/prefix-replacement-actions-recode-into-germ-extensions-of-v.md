## Setting

- **The path space.** `E` is a finite directed graph, `v0` a vertex, and every vertex reachable
  from `v0` has out-degree at least 2. `X` is the space of infinite paths from `v0`, a Cantor
  space.
- **Prefix replacements.** A *state-preserving prefix replacement* is a map `uw -> u'w`, where
  `u, u'` are finite paths from `v0` ending at the same vertex. A path is *eventually periodic*
  if it is `u c^∞` for a cycle `c`.
- **The group.** `G <= Homeo(X)` is generated by a finite symmetric set `S`. Each `s ∈ S` agrees
  locally with a state-preserving prefix replacement at every point outside a finite set
  `Σ(s)`. Every point of `Σ(s)` and every point of `s(Σ(s))` is eventually periodic.

## Statement

1. **Recoding.** Choose, at every vertex, a bijection of its out-edges onto a complete binary
   prefix code. Concatenating codes gives a homeomorphism `φ : X -> C_2 = {0,1}^ω`. Under `φ`:
   - every state-preserving prefix replacement becomes a prefix replacement `αz -> α'z`, so it
     lies locally in Thompson's `V`;
   - eventually periodic paths go to rational points.
2. **The host.** Identify `G` with `φGφ^-1`. Let `𝒢` be the germ groupoid generated by the germs
   of `V` and of `G`, and let `Γ` be the set of homeomorphisms of `C_2` that agree locally with
   `V` off finitely many points and have all their germs in `𝒢`. Then:
   - `Γ` is a group containing `V` and `G`;
   - `Γ` is full and clopen transitive;
   - `Γ` is a finite germ extension of `V` in the sense of Belk--Hyde--Matucci
     (arXiv:2407.03149, §1);
   - `sing(Γ)` is a finite union of `V`-orbits of rational points.
3. **Finiteness.** Suppose that for every `p ∈ sing(Γ)` the subgroup `(V)_p` is normal in
   `(Γ)_p` and `(Γ)_p/(V)_p` has type `F_n`. Then `Γ` has type `F_n`.
4. **Consequence.** If (3) holds with `n = 2`, then `Γ` has a type (A) action, so `G` lies in
   `B_A` and embeds in a finitely presented simple group.

## Proof

- **(1)**
  - Write `c_v(e)` for the code of an edge `e` out of `v`. For a path `e_1 e_2 ...` put
    `φ(e_1 e_2 ...) = c_{v0}(e_1) c_{v1}(e_2) ...`.
  - Codes are nonempty, since out-degrees are at least 2, and complete, so `φ` is a
    homeomorphism.
  - A cylinder `[u]` of a path `u` ending at `v` maps to the cylinder `[φ(u)]`, and `φ(uw)` is
    `φ(u)` followed by the code of `w` read from `v`. So `uw -> u'w` becomes
    `φ(u)z -> φ(u')z`.
  - `φ(u c^∞) = φ(u) (φ_c)^∞`, where `φ_c` is the code word of the cycle `c`.
- **(2)**
  - *Group.* Composites and inverses keep finitely many singular points and germs in `𝒢`.
    `V ⊆ Γ` trivially. `G ⊆ Γ` by (1), since each generator is `V`-local off the finite set
    `φ(Σ(s))`.
  - *Full.* A homeomorphism that agrees locally with `Γ` agrees with finitely many elements on a
    clopen partition. So it has finitely many singular points and germs in `𝒢`.
  - *Clopen transitive.* `Γ` contains `V`, and any two nonempty clopen subsets of `C_2` are
    joined by a `V`-local map: split cylinders to equalize their numbers.
  - *Finite germ extension.*
    - Condition 1 holds by definition.
    - Condition 2: an element of `Γ` with no singular points is `V`-local everywhere, so it lies
      in `V`, because `V` is full.
    - Condition 3 (localization): given `f` and `p ∈ sing(f)`, choose a clopen `N ∋ p` meeting
      `sing(f)` only in `p`, with nonempty complements `C_2 ∖ N` and `C_2 ∖ f(N)`. Let `h = f` on
      `N` and a `V`-local map `C_2 ∖ N -> C_2 ∖ f(N)` elsewhere. Then `h ∈ Γ` and
      `sing(h) = {p}`.
  - *Singular set.*
    - A germ in `𝒢` is a product of basic germs of `V` and of `S` along a finite trajectory. It
      is not a `V`-germ only if the trajectory meets `P = ⋃_s φ(Σ(s))`.
    - Along a trajectory, a non-singular step keeps the `V`-orbit. A singular step starts at a
      point of `P`, which is finite, and ends at a rational point, by the hypothesis on
      `s(Σ(s))`.
    - So `sing(Γ)` lies in the `V`-orbits of `P ∪ ⋃_s φ(s(Σ(s)))`, finitely many orbits of
      rational points. It is `V`-invariant, so it is a union of some of them.
- **(3)** This is Belk--Hyde--Matucci `cor:MainFinitenessCorollary` (arXiv:2407.03149v1, read at
  source), with base `V`. Its three conditions:
  - **(i) Finitely many orbits on `sing(Γ)^n`.** Take two `n`-tuples of distinct points with
    `p_i` and `p'_i` in the same `V`-orbit. Write `p_i = α_i ζ_i` and `p'_i = α'_i ζ_i`.
    Lengthen the prefixes until the cylinders `[α_i]` are pairwise disjoint with nonempty
    complement, and likewise the `[α'_i]`. Then use `α_i z -> α'_i z` on the cylinders and a
    `V`-local map between the complements. So the orbits of `n`-tuples are classified by orbit
    labels and coincidence pattern, of which there are finitely many.
  - **(ii) `Fix_V(M)` has type `F_∞`** for every finite set `M` of rational points:
    Belk--Hyde--Matucci Appendix A, `thm:Stabilizers` (read at source).
  - **(iii) The germ condition** is the hypothesis.
- **(4)** By (2) and (3), `Γ` is a finitely presented, full, clopen-transitive Cantor group.
  `fp-clopen-transitive-full-cantor-groups-have-type-a-actions` then gives a type (A) action,
  and `type-a-action-gives-boone-higman-for-subgroups` finishes. ∎

## Computing the germ groups

- **The rational-point germ group.** For a rational point `p = α β^∞` with `β` primitive,
  `(V)_p ≅ Z` is generated by the one-period shift.
  - A `V`-germ fixing `p` is `γw -> γ'w` with `γ, γ'` prefixes of `p`.
  - Primitivity of `β` forces `|γ'| - |γ|` to be a multiple of `|β|` once both are past `α`.
- **A useful special case.** Suppose the singular points `P` lie in distinct `V`-orbits that no
  singular step leaves, and each `s` fixes its singular points. Then `(Γ)_p` is generated by
  `(V)_p` and the germs at `p` of the generators singular at `p`.
- **Choosing the codes.** The codes can be chosen to make cycle code words primitive and
  cyclically distinct. That fixes `(V)_p` and separates the orbits. This is how
  `z2-free-z-lies-in-an-fp-full-germ-extension-of-v` applies the theorem.
