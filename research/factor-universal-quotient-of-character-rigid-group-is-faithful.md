Let `q:G->K` be a surjection.  Suppose `K` is factor-representation
universal: every separable tracial von Neumann algebra embeds
trace-preservingly in a finite factor generated by a representation of `K`.
Then `G` is factor-representation universal.  Indeed, precomposing any such
representation with `q` leaves both its generated factor and the embedded
tracial algebra unchanged.

Now suppose every extremal character of `G` is either the regular character
or the character of a finite-dimensional factor representation.  Then

```text
ker(q)={e}.                                                (FUQ1)
```

Consequently `q` is an isomorphism and `G` is nonhyperlinear.  Thus quotient
pullback is a genuine way to place universality and character rigidity on one
source group, but it never produces a proper factor-universal quotient of a
group with this character rigidity.

The opposite direction has an exact kernel gate.  Factor universality of `G`
descends through this particular `q` precisely when, for every separable
tracial algebra `M`, its universal witness can be chosen as

```text
pi:G->U(Q),   pi(G)''=Q,   M subset Q,   ker(q) subset ker(pi).   (FUQ2)
```

The containment lets `pi` descend to `K`; conversely every witness for `K`
pulls back to a witness satisfying it.  Character rigidity of `K` alone says
nothing about `(FUQ2)`.  In particular, replacing the actual CDI image by an
unrelated character-rigid quotient still misses exactly this kernel
containment.

Finite-index induction and infinite-index coamenable induction are separate
questions, not exceptions to this quotient law.  The former already
transfers a single non-CE payload in
`finite-index-induction-transfers-non-ce-factor`; the latter kills every
localized coset payload in
`infinite-index-factor-induction-kills-every-coset-payload`.
