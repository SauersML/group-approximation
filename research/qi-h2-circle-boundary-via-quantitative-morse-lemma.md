Notation as in the claim. `X` is geodesic, `δ` is its four-point constant, and `o ∈ X`. Conventions:
`−log 0 = ∞` and `∞ − c = ∞`. A liminf "over `i, j → ∞`" is `lim_N inf_{i,j ≥ N}`, and it includes
`i = j`.

**Imported statement.**
- **(M)** S. Gouëzel, V. Shchur, *A corrected quantitative version of the Morse lemma*, arXiv:1810.04579v1
  (10 Oct 2018). The PDF was fetched and its text decoded through its ToUnicode maps on 2026-09-16.
  - Quotes are verbatim, except that relation glyphs from the Computer Modern math fonts are rendered as
    `≤`, `≥`, `−`, `|`, `λ` and `δ`.
  - Theorem 1.1: "Consider a (λ,C)-quasi-geodesic Q in a δ-hyperbolic space X, and G a geodesic segment
    between its endpoints. Then the Hausdorff distance HD(Q,G) between Q and G satisfies
    HD(Q,G) ≤ 92λ²(C + δ)."
  - Definitions there: "a (λ,C)-quasi-geodesic is the image of a map f from a compact interval to X
    satisfying for all x,y the inequalities λ^{-1}|y − x| − C ≤ d(f(x),f(y)) ≤ λ|y − x| + C." "We also
    require λ ≥ 1 and C ≥ 0 in the definition."
  - "We say that the space X is δ-hyperbolic if the Gromov product (x,y)_w = (d(x,w)+d(y,w)−d(x,y))/2
    satisfies for all points x,y,z,w the inequality (x,z)_w ≥ min((x,y)_w,(y,z)_w) − δ."
  - "the Hausdorff distance HD(Q,G) is the smallest number r such that G is included in the
    r-neighborhood of Q, and conversely."
  - So every point of `G` is within distance `HD(Q,G)` of `Q`, and every point of `Q` is within
    `HD(Q,G)` of `G`. Continuity of `f` is not required.

Everything else is proved below. The only facts about `H^2` used are the hyperboloid model and
transitivity of its isometry group.

**Step 0: base-point facts.**
- (F1) `|(x|y)_w − (x|y)_{w'}| ≤ d(w,w')`, (F2) `|(x|y)_w − (x'|y)_w| ≤ d(x,x')`, and
  (F3) `0 ≤ (x|y)_w ≤ min{d(x,w), d(y,w)}`. All three follow from the triangle inequality.
- By (F1), convergence at infinity and equivalence do not depend on the base point.
- Write `⟨u|v⟩_w` for the boundary product at base `w`. By (F1), each liminf in its definition moves by
  at most `d(w,w')`, so `|⟨u|v⟩_w − ⟨u|v⟩_{w'}| ≤ d(w,w')`.
- Hence `N^w_{t + d(w,w')}(u) ⊆ N^{w'}_t(u)`, and the neighbourhood filters, so also `T`, do not depend
  on the base point.
- `⟨u|v⟩_w = ⟨v|u⟩_w`, since `(y|w)` is symmetric.
- **Topologies from filters.** A topology is determined by its neighbourhood filters: a set is open iff
  it is a neighbourhood of each of its points. So a bijection `f` between two spaces is a homeomorphism
  iff, for every point `u`, `f` carries the neighbourhood filter at `u` onto the neighbourhood filter at
  `f(u)`.
  - For `T`, the filter at `u` is generated by the sets `N_t(u)`.
  - This also shows that `T` exists as soon as `∂X` is in bijection with a topological space in this
    filter-preserving way, as Step 8 provides.

**Step 1: Gromov products and distance to a geodesic.** Let `Y` be any metric space satisfying the
four-point inequality with constant `δ_Y`. Let `σ: [0,L] → Y` be a geodesic from `x` to `y`, and `w ∈ Y`.
Then `(x|y)_w ≤ d(w, σ) ≤ (x|y)_w + 2δ_Y`.
- For `p = σ(t)` we have `(x|y)_p = (t + (L − t) − L)/2 = 0`.
- **Lower bound.** `(x|y)_w = (d(x,w) + d(y,w) − L)/2 ≤ (d(x,p) + d(p,w) + d(y,p) + d(p,w) − L)/2 = d(w,p)`.
- **Upper bound.** `F(t) := (x|w)_{σ(t)} − (w|y)_{σ(t)}` is continuous. We have `F(0) = −(w|y)_x ≤ 0`
  and `F(L) = (x|w)_y ≥ 0`.
  - By the intermediate value theorem there is `p = σ(t)` with `(x|w)_p = (w|y)_p =: m`.
  - The four-point inequality at base `p` gives `0 = (x|y)_p ≥ min{(x|w)_p, (w|y)_p} − δ_Y = m − δ_Y`.
  - Expanding, `(x|w)_p + (w|y)_p = d(w,p) + (L − d(x,w) − d(y,w))/2 = d(w,p) − (x|y)_w`.
  - So `d(w,p) = (x|y)_w + 2m ≤ (x|y)_w + 2δ_Y`.

**Step 2: polar estimates in `H^2`.** Use the hyperboloid model
`H^2 = {v ∈ R^3 : v_0² − v_1² − v_2² = 1, v_0 > 0}` with `cosh d(v,v') = v_0v'_0 − v_1v'_1 − v_2v'_2`, and
base point `o_H := (1,0,0)`.
- Every `x ≠ o_H` is `(cosh a, sinh a cos α, sinh a sin α)` with `a = d(o_H,x) > 0` and a unique
  `α ∈ S^1 = R/2πZ`.
- Put `χ(α,β) := |sin((α − β)/2)| = |e^{iα} − e^{iβ}|/2`. This is a metric on `S^1` inducing its usual
  topology.
- For `x, y ≠ o_H` with data `(a,α)`, `(b,β)`, put `s := χ(α,β)` and `ρ := −log s ∈ [0,∞]`. Then
  `cos(α − β) = 1 − 2s²`, and the model gives
  `cosh d(x,y) = cosh a cosh b − sinh a sinh b (1 − 2s²) = (1 − s²) cosh(a − b) + s² cosh(a + b)`,
  using `2 sinh a sinh b = cosh(a+b) − cosh(a−b)`.
- **(P)** `min{a, b, ρ} − log 2 ≤ (x|y)_{o_H} ≤ min{a, b, ρ + (1/2)log 2}`.
  - **Upper.** `e^d ≥ cosh d ≥ s² cosh(a+b) ≥ s² e^{a+b}/2`. So `d ≥ a + b + 2log s − log 2`, and
    `(x|y)_{o_H} = (a + b − d)/2 ≤ ρ + (1/2)log 2`. Also `(x|y)_{o_H} ≤ min{a,b}` by (F3).
  - **Lower.** `e^d ≤ 2cosh d ≤ 2e^{|a−b|} + 2s² e^{a+b}`. So
    `e^{d − a − b} ≤ 2e^{−2min{a,b}} + 2s² ≤ 4 max{e^{−2min{a,b}}, s²}`.
    Hence `d − a − b ≤ log 4 − 2min{a, b, ρ}`, which is the claimed bound.
- **(H) `H^2` satisfies the four-point inequality with `δ_H := (5/2)log 2`.** The isometry group acts
  transitively and `(x|y)_w = (gx|gy)_{gw}`, so it suffices to take `w = o_H`.
  - If one of `x, y, z` is `o_H`, then `min{(x|y), (y|z)} = 0 ≤ (x|z)` by (F3), where the minimum is
    `0` because one of its entries has `o_H` as an argument.
  - Otherwise let `m := min{(x|y)_{o_H}, (y|z)_{o_H}}`, and write `a, b, c` for the radii and
    `ρ_{xy}, ρ_{yz}, ρ_{xz}` for the angular data.
  - By (P), `a ≥ m`, `c ≥ m`, and `ρ_{xy}, ρ_{yz} ≥ m − (1/2)log 2`.
  - Since `χ` is a metric, `s_{xz} ≤ s_{xy} + s_{yz} ≤ 2max{s_{xy}, s_{yz}}`. So
    `ρ_{xz} ≥ min{ρ_{xy}, ρ_{yz}} − log 2 ≥ m − (3/2)log 2`.
  - By (P), `(x|z)_{o_H} ≥ min{a, c, ρ_{xz}} − log 2 ≥ m − (5/2)log 2`.

**Step 3: a quasi-isometry coarsely preserves Gromov products.**
- **The map.** By hypothesis there are `K ≥ 1`, `C ≥ 0`, `R ≥ 0` and `φ: X → H^2` with
  `K^{-1}d(x,y) − C ≤ d(φx, φy) ≤ K d(x,y) + C` for all `x, y`, and with every point of `H^2` within `R`
  of `φ(X)`.
  - If one is given instead such a map `ψ_0: H^2 → X`, choose `φ(x)` with `d(ψ_0 φ(x), x) ≤ R`.
  - Then `d(φx,φy) ≤ K(d(x,y) + 2R + C)` and `d(φx,φy) ≥ K^{-1}(d(x,y) − 2R − C)`.
  - For `v ∈ H^2`, the point `v' := φ(ψ_0 v)` satisfies `d(ψ_0 v', ψ_0 v) ≤ R`, so `d(v', v) ≤ K(R + C)`.
  - So `φ` has the stated form, with `C` replaced by `K(2R + C)` and `R` replaced by `K(R + C)`.
- **(G3)** Put `M := 92K²(C + δ_H)` and `A := 2Kδ + C + M + 2δ_H`. Then for all `x, y ∈ X`:
  `K^{-1}(x|y)_o − A ≤ (φx|φy)_{φo} ≤ K(x|y)_o + A`.
  - Let `σ: [0,L] → X` be a geodesic from `x` to `y`, which exists because `X` is geodesic.
  - Then `φ∘σ` satisfies `K^{-1}|s − t| − C ≤ d(φσ(s), φσ(t)) ≤ K|s − t| + C`. So `Q := φ(σ([0,L]))`
    is a `(K,C)`-quasi-geodesic with endpoints `φx`, `φy`.
  - Let `G` be the geodesic segment from `φx` to `φy` in `H^2`. By (H), `H^2` is `δ_H`-hyperbolic in the
    sense of (M), so (M) gives `HD(Q,G) ≤ M`.
  - For `g ∈ G` and `q ∈ Q`: `d(φo, Q) ≤ d(φo, g) + d(g, Q)`. Hence `d(φo,Q) − M ≤ d(φo,G)`, and by
    symmetry `d(φo,G) ≤ d(φo,Q) + M`.
  - Since `σ([0,L])` is compact, `d(o,σ) = d(o,p_0)` for some `p_0 ∈ σ`. So
    `d(φo,Q) ≤ d(φo,φp_0) ≤ K d(o,σ) + C`.
  - Also `d(φo,Q) = inf_p d(φo,φp) ≥ K^{-1} d(o,σ) − C`.
  - **Lower bound.** Step 1 in `H^2`, then Step 1 in `X`:
    `(φx|φy)_{φo} ≥ d(φo,G) − 2δ_H ≥ K^{-1}d(o,σ) − C − M − 2δ_H ≥ K^{-1}(x|y)_o − A`.
  - **Upper bound.** Step 1 in `H^2`, then Step 1 in `X`:
    `(φx|φy)_{φo} ≤ d(φo,G) ≤ K d(o,σ) + C + M ≤ K((x|y)_o + 2δ) + C + M ≤ K(x|y)_o + A`.

**Step 4: the boundary bijection.** For each `v ∈ H^2` choose `ψ(v) ∈ X` with `d(φψ(v), v) ≤ R`.
- **Well defined.** If `y_i → u`, then by (G3) `(φy_i|φy_j)_{φo} ≥ K^{-1}(y_i|y_j)_o − A`, whose liminf
  is `∞`. The same bound with `w_j` in place of `y_j` shows that equivalent sequences have equivalent
  images. Put `∂φ(u) := [(φy_i)]`.
- **Injective.** If `(φy_i)` and `(φw_j)` are equivalent, then by (G3)
  `K(y_i|w_j)_o + A ≥ (φy_i|φw_j)_{φo}`, whose liminf is `∞`. So `(y_i)` and `(w_j)` are equivalent.
- **Surjective.** Let `(v_i)` converge at infinity in `H^2`.
  - By (F2) and (G3), `(v_i|v_j)_{φo} − 2R ≤ (φψv_i|φψv_j)_{φo} ≤ K(ψv_i|ψv_j)_o + A`. So `(ψv_i)`
    converges at infinity.
  - By (F2), `(φψv_i|v_j)_{φo} ≥ (v_i|v_j)_{φo} − R`. So `(φψv_i)` is equivalent to `(v_i)`, and
    `∂φ([(ψv_i)]) = [(v_i)]`.
- **(S)** If `(v_i)` represents `∂φ(u)`, then `(ψv_i)` represents `u`. It represents some `u'` with
  `∂φ(u') = ∂φ(u)`, and `∂φ` is injective.

**Step 5: boundary products.** For `u, v ∈ ∂X`:
`K^{-1}⟨u|v⟩_o − A − 2R ≤ ⟨∂φu|∂φv⟩_{φo} ≤ K⟨u|v⟩_o + A`.
- **Upper bound.** For representatives `y_i → u` and `w_j → v`, the sequences `(φy_i)` and `(φw_j)`
  represent `∂φu` and `∂φv`. So
  `⟨∂φu|∂φv⟩_{φo} ≤ liminf (φy_i|φw_j)_{φo} ≤ K liminf (y_i|w_j)_o + A`. Take the infimum over
  representatives.
- **Lower bound.** Let `(v_i)` and `(v'_j)` represent `∂φu` and `∂φv`. By (S), `ψv_i → u` and
  `ψv'_j → v`. By (F2) and (G3),
  `(v_i|v'_j)_{φo} ≥ (φψv_i|φψv'_j)_{φo} − 2R ≥ K^{-1}(ψv_i|ψv'_j)_o − A − 2R`.
  - The liminf of the right side is at least `K^{-1}⟨u|v⟩_o − A − 2R`, because `(ψv_i)` and `(ψv'_j)`
    are admissible in the infimum defining `⟨u|v⟩_o`.
  - Take the infimum over `(v_i)` and `(v'_j)`.

**Step 6: `∂φ` preserves neighbourhood filters.** Use base `φo` on `∂H^2`, which is allowed by Step 0.
- If `⟨v|u⟩_o > K(t + A + 2R)`, then by Step 5 `⟨∂φv|∂φu⟩_{φo} > t`. So
  `∂φ(N_{K(t+A+2R)}(u)) ⊆ N_t(∂φu)`.
- If `⟨∂φv|∂φu⟩_{φo} > Ks + A`, then by Step 5 `⟨v|u⟩_o > s`. So `∂φ^{-1}(N_{Ks+A}(∂φu)) ⊆ N_s(u)`.
- Since `∂φ` is a bijection, it carries the filter generated by `{N_t(u)}` onto the filter generated by
  `{N_t(∂φu)}`.

**Step 7: the boundary of `H^2` is the circle.** Use base `o_H`.
1. **Converging sequences.** Suppose `(x_i)` converges at infinity.
   - Its diagonal terms give `a_i = (x_i|x_i)_{o_H} → ∞`, so `x_i ≠ o_H` for large `i`.
   - By the upper bound in (P), `−log χ(α_i,α_j) + (1/2)log 2 ≥ (x_i|x_j)_{o_H} → ∞`. So `(α_i)` is
     `χ`-Cauchy and converges to some `α ∈ S^1`, by compactness.
   - Conversely, if `a_i → ∞` and `α_i → α`, then the lower bound in (P) tends to `∞`.
2. **The map Θ.** Let `x_i → u` and `y_j → v`, with angle limits `α` and `β`, and put `χ_0 := χ(α,β)`.
   - For large `i, j`, (P) puts `(x_i|y_j)_{o_H}` in
     `[min{a_i, b_j, −log χ(α_i,β_j)} − log 2, −log χ(α_i,β_j) + (1/2)log 2]`.
   - Both ends converge, to `−log χ_0 − log 2` and `−log χ_0 + (1/2)log 2`.
   - So `liminf_{i,j} (x_i|y_j)_{o_H} ∈ [−log χ_0 − log 2, −log χ_0 + (1/2)log 2]`. In particular it is
     `∞` iff `α = β`.
   - Hence `Θ(u) := lim α_i` is well defined and injective on `∂H^2`. It is surjective, since
     `x_i := (cosh i, sinh i cos α, sinh i sin α)` converges at infinity with angle `α`.
3. **Boundary products.** The interval bound of item 2 holds for every pair of representatives. So
   `⟨u|v⟩_{o_H} ∈ [−log χ(Θu,Θv) − log 2, −log χ(Θu,Θv) + (1/2)log 2]`.
4. **Neighbourhoods.** Consequently
   `{β : χ(Θu,β) < e^{−t}/2} ⊆ Θ(N_t(u)) ⊆ {β : χ(Θu,β) < √2 e^{−t}}`.
   - If `χ < e^{−t}/2`, then `⟨u|v⟩ ≥ −log χ − log 2 > t`.
   - If `⟨u|v⟩ > t`, then `−log χ + (1/2)log 2 > t`.
   - The `χ`-balls around `Θu` form a neighbourhood base of `Θu` in `S^1`. So `Θ` carries the filter
     generated by `{N_t(u)}` onto the neighbourhood filter of `Θu`.

**Step 8: conclusion.**
- By Steps 6 and 7, and the base-point independence of Step 0, the bijection `Θ∘∂φ: ∂X → S^1` carries
  the filter generated by `{N_t(u)}` onto the circle neighbourhood filter at `Θ∂φ(u)`, for every
  `u ∈ ∂X`.
- So the topology `T` with these neighbourhood filters is the pullback of the circle topology, and
  `Θ∘∂φ: (∂X, T) → S^1` is a homeomorphism. ∎

**Weakest points.**
- The import (M). Its statement was read verbatim, but its proof is not checked here. The paper reports
  a formalization in Isabelle/HOL.
- The decoding of relation glyphs in (M). The inequality directions are forced by the sense of the
  statements.
- Step 7.2 uses the convergence of `−log χ(α_i,β_j)` to `−log χ_0` in `[0,∞]`, including when
  `χ_0 = 0`.
