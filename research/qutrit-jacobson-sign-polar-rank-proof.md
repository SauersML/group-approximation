## The finite subgroup carrying the rank calculation

Let `B=<C_1,W>`. The two generators are commuting independent
involutions, so `B isomorphic to F_2^2`. Conjugation by
`C=<J,Z>` acts on the source coordinate `c_2` through the nontrivial
two-dimensional type `[1,1]` and fixes each of the two trivial target
coordinates `c_0,c_3`.

The kernel of that type is `<h>`, where `h=J^2Z`. For each target, the
three nonzero conjugates of its root form the nonzero vectors of one
irreducible two-dimensional `F_2[C/<h>]` module. The targets are
different, so their root spaces are independent and commute. Therefore
the normal closure of `B` under `C` is

```text
A=V direct-sum V isomorphic to F_2^4,                    (QPR6)
```

where `C/<h> isomorphic to C_3` acts diagonally and fixed-point-freely.

The head `H` commutes with `C` and `A` and is independent of them.
Also `C=<h> times <k>` for any complementary order-three line. It
follows that

```text
F_0=<C,H,C_1,W>
 isomorphic to <H> times <h> times (A semidirect <k>),
|F_0|=2*3*16*3=288.                                     (QPR7)
```

This proves `(QPR3)`.

## Reduction to a 48-dimensional Frobenius group

Choose `k=J`. If an element of `C` is written both as `J^rZ^s` and
`h^alpha k^beta`, then

```text
alpha=s,                 beta=r+s.
```

The selected character `chi_a=chi_(1,a)` has

```text
chi_a(h)=omega^(2+a),             chi_a(k)=omega.        (QPR8)
```

Thus the two atoms in `R=e_0+e_1` select two different central
`<h>` characters but the same `<k>` character. The head cut `E`
selects one character of the central `<H>` factor.

Consequently the rank of `G_(sigma,tau)R` in the regular representation
of `F_0` is twice the rank of

```text
f_(sigma,tau)e_omega
```

in the 48-dimensional regular representation of

```text
G_0=A semidirect <k>.                                   (QPR9)
```

Here `f_(sigma,tau)` is the sign-character projection of the
two-dimensional subgroup `B<=A`, and `e_omega` is the Fourier
projection of the indicated character of `<k>`.

## Irreducibles of the Frobenius group

The fixed-point-free action of `<k>` on `A-{0}` has five orbits of
size three. Hence `G_0` has

- three one-dimensional representations inflated from `<k>`; and
- five three-dimensional representations induced from the five nonzero
  character orbits of `A`.

Their squared dimensions sum to

```text
3*1^2+5*3^2=48,
```

so this is the complete list.

In a three-dimensional induced representation, use the basis indexed by
one orbit of characters of `A`. The projection `f_(sigma,tau)` is
diagonal: it keeps precisely those orbit characters whose restriction to
`B` is the requested sign. The projection `e_omega` has rank one and
its Fourier vector has three nonzero coordinates. Therefore

```text
rank(f_(sigma,tau)e_omega)=1
```

exactly when the character orbit contains at least one restriction equal
to the requested sign, and is zero otherwise.

It remains to count those orbits. Identify `V` and its dual with
`F_4`, let `alpha^2=alpha+1`, and let `<k>=F_4^times` act by common
scalar multiplication on `F_4^2`. The five nonzero dual orbits have
representatives

```text
(0,1), (1,0), (1,1), (1,alpha), (1,alpha^2).
```

Choose the two generators of `B` to be the `F_2`-lines generated by
`1` in the two coordinates. Restriction is then the pair of field
traces to `F_2`. Along the three scalar multiples, the restriction table
is

| orbit representative | three restrictions to B |
|---|---|
| `(0,1)` | `00,01,01` |
| `(1,0)` | `00,10,10` |
| `(1,1)` | `00,11,11` |
| `(1,alpha)` | `01,11,10` |
| `(1,alpha^2)` | `01,10,11` |

Every one of the four signs occurs in exactly three orbit rows. Thus the
five induced representations contribute regular rank

```text
3 representations * multiplicity 3 =9                       (QPR10)
```

for every sign.

Among the one-dimensional representations, `f_(+,+)` is the identity
and the other three sign projections are zero. Exactly one of the three
one-dimensional quotient characters is selected by `e_omega`. Hence

```text
rank_(C[G_0])(f_(+,+)e_omega)=9+1=10,
rank_(C[G_0])(f_(sigma,tau)e_omega)=9
                         for (sigma,tau)!=(+,+).         (QPR11)
```

## Normalized polar ranks

There are two central `<h>` blocks in `R`, so `(QPR11)` gives regular
ranks `20` and `18` inside `C[F_0]`. The initial support of the polar of
an operator has rank equal to the operator rank; its range support has
the same rank. Dividing by `|F_0|=288` yields

```text
20/288=5/72,                    18/288=1/16.
```

This proves `(QPR2)`. The Hilbert--Schmidt identity `(QPR1)` follows
independently from the identity coefficient:

```text
||G_(sigma,tau)R||_2^2
 =tau(RG_(sigma,tau))
 =2*(1/72)=1/36.
```

The calculation is intrinsic to the ambient group trace. For any larger
group containing `F_0`, its left regular Hilbert space restricts to a
direct sum of regular `F_0` modules, so the normalized support ranks are
unchanged.

Finally `tau(Ee_2)=1/18`. Subtracting it from the two source ranks gives
`(QPR5)`. This is a conditional terminal: a future relation must preserve
the computed polar source while placing its range in `Ee_2`. The present
proof does not assert that routing relation.
