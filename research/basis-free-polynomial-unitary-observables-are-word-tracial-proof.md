This is the unitary form of the first fundamental theorem of matrix invariant
theory.  Procesi's 1976 announcement states explicitly that for simultaneous
`U(d)` conjugation the invariants are generated by monomials

```text
Tr(V_1 ... V_r),
```

where each `V_j` is one of the matrix variables or its adjoint.  See
C. Procesi, *The invariants of n x n matrices*, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 82
(1976), 891--892, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9904-1976-14196-1`; the full treatment is
C. Procesi, *The invariant theory of n x n matrices*, Adv. Math. 19 (1976),
306--381.

The group-table consequence is immediate but the quantifiers matter.  Let
`F` be the current finite microstate window and let `P` be one fixed invariant
polynomial.  Choose a trace-word expression for `P`.  It involves only
finitely many `*`-words, of some bounded length `L`.  Enlarge `F` so that the
prefix products needed to evaluate those finitely many words are present.
For a normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt microstate whose multiplication defect on
that enlarged window tends to zero, each matrix word is asymptotic in
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm to the matrix assigned to its group-word
value.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives the corresponding convergence of normalized
traces.  Thus every generator `d^(-1)Tr(word(U))`, and hence `P` after the
fixed normalization appropriate to its degree, is determined asymptotically
by the ordinary word-trace/character data.

This does **not** say that arbitrary measurable or dimension-dependent
conjugacy invariants are trace-polynomial with a uniform degree bound.  It
rules out the much more tempting repair of the gauge-instability obstruction:
no bounded-degree polynomial conjugacy-averaging of the raw traffic moment can
supply the missing branching information.
