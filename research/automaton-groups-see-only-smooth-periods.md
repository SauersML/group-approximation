## Setting

- **The tree.** `m >= 2`, `X = {0, …, m−1}`, and `X^ω ≅ Z_m = ∏_(p | m) Z_p` (least significant digit first). `S` is
  the set of primes dividing `m`.
- **Periodic points.** A point is *eventually periodic with period `P`* if its digit sequence is eventually periodic
  with period `P`. These are exactly the points of `Z_m ∩ Q`. A point of period `P` lies in `(1/(m^P − 1)) Z`.
- **The group.** `Γ <= Aut(X*)` is generated by finitely many finite-state automorphisms. `Σ` is the union of the
  state sets of the generators and their inverses. It is finite and closed under sections, and `s = |Σ|`.
- **Smoothness.** An integer is *`s`-smooth* if all its prime factors are `<= s`.

## Theorem

1. **Smooth periods.** If `x` is eventually periodic with period `p`, then for every `g ∈ Γ` the point `g(x)` is
   eventually periodic with period dividing `p·N`, for some `s`-smooth `N`.
2. **Missing primes.** For every prime `n > max(s, 2)` there is a prime `ℓ ∉ S` with `ord_ℓ(m) = n` (Zsigmondy).
   For each such `ℓ`, `1/ℓ` is not in the `Z`-span of the orbit `Γ·0`. So:
   - **(a) No odometer roots at those primes.** `τ_(1/ℓ) ∉ Γ`, since `τ_(1/ℓ)(0) = 1/ℓ`. More precisely, if
     `τ_(1/ℓ) ∈ Γ` then `ord_ℓ(m)` is `s`-smooth. In particular Γ contains neither the translation copy
     `T ≅ Z_(S)` nor any group containing the odometer and all its roots at primes outside `S`.
   - **(b) No adder constants at those primes.** Every constant in the orbit span `M(Γ)` of
     `adder-hosts-linearize-odd-odometer-roots` is its value at `0`, so it lies in `Z`-span`(Γ·0)`. Hence
     `1/ℓ ∉ M(Γ)`, and the adder host `G_Γ`, itself an automaton group, contains no `Z_(2)`.
   - **(c) Röver–Nekrashevych envelopes inherit this.** Elements of `V_m(Γ)` and `dV_Γ` act near every point by a
     prefix replacement followed by an element of `Γ` on the tail, and prefix replacements do not change eventual
     periods. So item 1 holds in these groups too, and their orbits of `0` miss `1/ℓ`.
3. **Möbius coefficient groups (any ring).** Let `m = p` be prime, and let a finitely generated group `H` act on `Z_p`
   by homeomorphisms that are piecewise Möbius on finitely many cylinders. Suppose all pieces are represented in
   `GL_2(R)`, where `R ⊂ Q_p` is a finitely generated ring. If `τ_(1/ℓ) ∈ H` then `1/ℓ ∈ R`. So this happens for only
   finitely many `ℓ`. This holds for any coefficient ring, including transcendental ones, and needs no finite-state
   hypothesis.

## Consequences

- **The candidate host classes are all excluded.** The coefficient group of `natural-q-rtimes-2-in-2v-h-iff-odd-odometer-roots`,
  and every translation-type (SL) witness on a regular rooted tree, must have a generator that is **not
  finite-state** and whose pieces are not Möbius over a finitely generated ring. That rules out every automaton
  group, including all candidate classes named on the board: Aleshin-type free automaton groups, lamplighters,
  `F_2[[t]]`-affine groups, bounded and polynomial automata, and affine groups.
- **The coordinator's search is closed.** The target "odd constants in the adder span of Aleshin-type automaton
  groups" is closed negatively, in exact form: for `s` states the constants miss every prime `ℓ` with
  `ord_ℓ(2) = n` prime and `n > s`. The question of finite presentation does not arise.
- **Calibration.** `⟨τ_1, μ_3⟩ = BS(1,3)` contains `τ_(1/3^k)`, and `ord_(3^k)(2) = 2·3^(k−1)` is 3-smooth. This is
  consistent with item 2(a), which only forbids non-smooth orders.

## Lesson for general BH

**Finite-state generation is finite prime budget in disguise.**
- A finite-state automorphism reads a periodic input with a finite memory, so it multiplies the period by at most
  its number of states.
- Words in finitely many such automorphisms can therefore only reach rational points whose periods are smooth.
- Rational points with prime period `n` exist for every prime `n` (Zsigmondy), so every automaton group, and its
  Röver–Nekrashevych envelopes, misses infinitely many primes.

This is the automaton form of the carrier principle, and it sharpens the (SL) bottleneck for `GL_n(Q)`:
- divisibility by all primes cannot be compressed into finitely many finite automata;
- it cannot be compressed into finitely many Möbius maps over any finitely generated ring either;
- an (SL) witness of odometer type needs an infinite-state generator.

The complexity has to live in the acting group's infinite data, not in local finite-state rules. That is the same
conclusion the master route reached for general BH, now as a counting lemma.
