Let

```text
C=(F_p^I)^H,
D=F_p^(H x I)
```

where `D` denotes finitely supported arrays.  Pontryagin duality identifies
`D` with the character group of `C`; the character indexed by `a` is the
integrand in `(FLH1)` after composition with `Phi`.

If `nu` is Haar measure on a closed subgroup `K<=C`, character orthogonality
gives

```text
integral_K chi_a dnu = 1   if chi_a|_K=1,
                       0   otherwise.
```

This proves `(1)=>(2)` and identifies `S=K^perp`.

Conversely assume `(FLH2)`.  If `M(a)=1`, equality in the triangle inequality
for the unit-circle random variable `chi_a(Phi(x))` implies
`chi_a(Phi(x))=1` almost surely.  Therefore the set

```text
S={a:M(a)=1}
```

is a subgroup of `D`: products and inverses of characters which are one
almost surely remain one almost surely.  Shift invariance of `nu` makes `S`
an `F_p[H]`-submodule.  Put `K=S^perp`.  The preceding a.s. identities show
that `nu` is supported on `K`.  By `(FLH2)`, the Fourier transform of `nu` is
exactly the indicator `1_S`, which is also the Fourier transform of Haar
measure on `K`.  Characters determine probability measures on the compact
metrizable abelian group `C`, so `nu` is Haar on `K`.  Duality gives `(FLH3)`.

The name map always identifies the sigma algebra generated by its coordinates
with the sigma algebra generated by all translates of the `f_i`.  If that is
the full probability algebra, `Phi` is an isomorphism of standard probability
spaces modulo null sets onto `(K,nu)` and intertwines the actions.  This proves
the conjugacy and `(FLH4)`; Fourier transform identifies the right-hand
crossed product with the displayed group factor.

For `p=2`, write a finite linear combination as `v:X->F_2`.  Its moment is

```text
E[(-1)^v]=1-2 Pr[v=1].
```

Membership in `{0,1}` says either `v=0` almost surely or `Pr[v=1]=1/2`,
proving `(FLH5)`.  For general `p`, applying `(FLH2)` to every nonzero scalar
multiple of `a` and Fourier inversion says that every nonconstant linear
combination is uniformly distributed on `F_p`.
