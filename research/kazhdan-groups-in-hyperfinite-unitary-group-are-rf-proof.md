Write `τ(g) = tr_R(ρ(g))`, a character of `Γ`, and also `τ` for the induced
tracial state on `C*(Γ)`.  Faithfulness of `ρ` gives `τ(g) ≠ 1` for `g ≠ 1`
(a unitary `u` with `tr(u) = 1` is `1`).

1. The GNS closure `π_τ(C*(Γ))''` is the von Neumann algebra generated by
   `ρ(Γ)` inside `R`, a von Neumann subalgebra of `R`, hence injective
   (hyperfinite).  By Brown, Theorem 3.2.2 (N. P. Brown, *Invariant means
   and finite representation theory of C\*-algebras*, Mem. Amer. Math. Soc.
   184 (2006), no. 865: conditions (5) and (1) there), `τ` is a uniformly
   amenable trace, in particular an amenable trace on `C*(Γ)`.
2. `Γ` has property (T).  By Kirchberg's lemma (Brown, Lemma 4.1.11) and
   Brown, Proposition 4.1.12, `AT(C*(Γ)) = AT(C*(Γ))_LFD`: every amenable
   trace on `C*(Γ)` is a pointwise limit of characters of finite-dimensional
   unitary representations.  So there are finite-dimensional unitary
   representations `π_n` of `Γ` with `tr(π_n(g)) → τ(g)` for all `g`.
3. For `g ≠ 1`, `τ(g) ≠ 1`, so `π_n(g) ≠ 1` for large `n`.  Hence the
   finite-dimensional unitary representations of `Γ` separate points.
   `Γ` is finitely generated, so each image `π_n(Γ) ≤ U(d)` is a finitely
   generated linear group and is residually finite (Mal'cev); a finite
   quotient of `π_n(Γ)` separating `π_n(g)` from `1` separates `g` from `1`
   in `Γ`.  So `Γ` is residually finite.

Residually finite groups are MF (Carrión--Dadarlat--Eckhardt), which is
the form used by the obstruction.
