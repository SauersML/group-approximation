This is a complete proof. It is written out in full in Part I of the artifact,
§I.2--§I.5.

## Step 1: restrict to a certificate

Let `y` be a parity certificate with support `S`. The subsystem on the rows in
`S` has a subset of the relations of the whole system, so the identity on
generators gives `Gamma_S -> Gamma(A,b)` with `J |-> J`. Since `y^T A = 0`,
every column meets `S` in an even set `s_j`.

## Step 2: transfer from the incidence group

Let `C` be the set of nonempty `s_j`, and let `H(S,C)` be generated by
involutions `xi_c`, with two relations:
- `xi_c` and `xi_d` commute when `c cap d` is nonempty;
- `gamma_v = prod_(c ni v) xi_c` is central.

Sending `xi_c` to the product of the variables with `s_j = c` is a
homomorphism. These variables commute because they share a row, and row
products map to `J^(b_v)`. It sends `prod_v gamma_v` to `J^(y^T b) = J`. If
`H(S,C)` is abelian, then `prod_v gamma_v = prod_c xi_c^|c| = 1`, because every
`|c|` is even. So `J = 1`.

## Step 3: small patterns are abelian

Assume `|S| <= 5`, and when `|S| = 5` assume the size-2 members of `C` are not
all ten pairs.
- **Size-4 members are central.** Each meets every nonempty even subset of a
  set of size at most 5.
- **Closure rule.** If `xi_d` commutes with every factor of `gamma_v` except
  `xi_e`, then it commutes with `xi_e = gamma_v prod(other factors)`.
- **Reduction.** Killing generators is a surjective homomorphism that sends
  `gamma_v` to `gamma_v`, so abelianness passes to subfamilies. It suffices to
  treat `K_4` together with `S`, and `K_5 minus 45` together with all 4-subsets.
- **Explicit derivations** (artifact, Proposition 6):
  - `K_4`: `12~34` by row 3, then `13~24` and `14~23` by row 2;
  - `K_5 minus 45`: twelve steps, first through rows 4 and 5, then through
    rows 1 and 3, covering all twelve disjoint pairs.

This proves Theorem CERT.

## Step 4: the pentagram

The operators are

```text
01 XXX, 02 XYY, 03 YXY, 04 YYX, 12 XII, 13 IXI, 14 IIX, 23 IIY, 24 IYI, 34 YII
```

- They are involutions.
- At each vertex they pairwise commute: each pair differs in an even number of
  positions.
- The products are `-I` at vertex 0 and `+I` elsewhere.
- Negating `X_(0v)` toggles the signs at vertices 0 and `v`, which reaches
  every odd right-hand side.

Assign these operators to one column per pair, and `I` to every other column
(weight 0 or 4, or a duplicate). This is an 8-dimensional operator solution.

## Step 5: assembly

For `m <= 5`, suppose `J != 1` and there is no classical solution. A
certificate exists, and CERT forces `|S| = 5 = m`, `y = 1`, even columns, odd
`b` and all ten pairs realized, which is item 3 of the theorem. Conversely,
item 3 gives a solution of dimension 1 or 8 by Step 4. A solution with
`J |-> -I` gives `J != 1`.

The corollary follows. Composing a `U(8)` solution with a unital
trace-preserving `M_8 -> R^U` makes `J` nontrivial in an `R^U` representation.

## Machine checks

`verify.py` checks exactly, in Gaussian-integer arithmetic:
- Step 4, for all 16 odd right-hand sides;
- the closure derivations of Step 3 (3 and 12 steps, none for `K_5`);
- that size-4 columns meet every even column when `|S| <= 5`.

The proof above does not depend on the census part of that script.
