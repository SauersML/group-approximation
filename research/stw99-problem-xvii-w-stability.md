**Problem XVII of Schafhauser--Tikuisis--White, arXiv:2506.10902**,
three parts on the Razak--Jacelon algebra `W`: (1) is `W`-stability
preserved by extensions and hereditary subalgebras?  (2) is there a
central sequence characterisation of `W`-stability?  (3) find a
direct proof that `W ≅ W ⊗ W` in the spirit of Jiang--Su's
`Z ≅ Z ⊗ Z` or its modern reproofs (Schemaitat; Ghasemi).

## Attempts

* STW's own footnote records the structural warning for (2): `R^ω` is
  a quotient of `W_ω` yet is not separably `W`-stable, so no naive
  central-sequence criterion (which would pass to quotients) can
  characterise `W`-stability — any answer must be
  quotient-breaking, e.g. requiring central sequences WITH tracially
  large kernels-of-definition, unlike the `Z` case.  This makes (1)
  genuinely delicate for extensions (the `Z`-case proof runs through
  the central sequence test).
* For (3): Schemaitat's `Z`-proof runs on dimension-drop
  self-absorption; the `W`-analogue building block is the
  Razak block with trivial `K`-theory and the missing ingredient is
  an unsuspended `E`-theory-free uniqueness for maps between Razak
  blocks — precisely what Szabo's 2026 KK-uniqueness now supplies in
  black-box form; a direct (3) is flagged as newly plausible
  post-Szabo, by rerunning Schemaitat with the new uniqueness
  theorem replacing classification.
