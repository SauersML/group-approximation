The natural first restriction of a coefficient search: build the equation from
elementary roots and matrices over only one coefficient chart,
`F_2<s_0, s_1>` or `F_2<t_0, t_1>`, where normal forms are free-algebra words
and products are cheap to evaluate.

Dead: `leavitt-rfd-entry-packets-satisfy-kl`.  Both charts are free algebras,
hence residually finite-dimensional, and every nonsingular equation over the
resulting matrix groups injects its coefficients at every length.  A candidate
must mix the charts through a one-sided inverse pair or some other
non-residually-finite-dimensional entry behaviour.
