We use the notation of `compressor-enemies-need-trace-invisible-hnn-defects`.
Throughout:

- `M = prod_omega (M_(d_n), tr)`, where `tr` is the normalized trace on each
  `M_(d_n)` and `||a||_2 = tr(a^* a)^(1/2)`.
- A model `sigma : G -> U(M)` is an arbitrary group homomorphism. Nothing is
  assumed about `tr ∘ sigma`, so "canonical trace" is not used anywhere.
- For a subgroup `H <= G`, `sigma(H)'` denotes the relative commutant
  `sigma(H)' ∩ M`.

## 0. Two standard facts

**(K) Kazhdan projection inequality.** Let `(S, kappa)` be a Kazhdan pair for
a group `H`. Let `pi` be a unitary representation of `H` on a Hilbert space
`K`, and `P` the orthogonal projection onto the `pi(H)`-invariant vectors.
Then for every `xi` in `K`,

```text
||xi - P xi|| <= kappa^-1 max_(s in S) ||pi(s) xi - xi|| .
```

*Proof.*

- `(1 - P)K` is invariant and has no invariant vectors. By the definition of
  `kappa`, `max_s ||pi(s) eta - eta|| >= kappa ||eta||` for
  `eta = (1 - P) xi`.
- Also `pi(s) eta - eta = pi(s) xi - xi`, because `P xi` is fixed. ∎

A Kazhdan pair of a group is a Kazhdan pair of each quotient, with the same
constant: pull the representation back.

**(CE) Trace-preserving conditional expectation.** Let `B` be a unital
`*`-subalgebra of `M_m`. The orthogonal projection of `L^2(M_m, tr)` onto `B`
is the trace-preserving conditional expectation `E_B`. It is completely
positive and unital, so `||E_B(a)|| <= ||a||` in operator norm.

## 1. Proof of Theorem 1

Fix representatives `U_n(s)` (`s` in `S_Gamma`) and `T_n` of `sigma(s)` and
`sigma(t)` that satisfy every word of `F = R_0 ∪ C_t` exactly for `n` in a set
`N_0` in `omega`. For `n` outside `N_0` the choices do not matter. Fix `n` in
`N_0`.

**Step 1 (uniform Kazhdan pair).** Since every relator of `R_0` holds exactly,
`s -> U_n(s)` extends to a homomorphism `Gamma~ = <S_Gamma | R_0> -> U(d_n)`.
Its image `Gamma_n = <U_n(S_Gamma)>` is a quotient of `Gamma~`. So
`(U_n(S_Gamma), kappa)` is a Kazhdan pair for `Gamma_n`, with `kappa`
independent of `n`.

**Step 2 (compression inside Gamma_n).** For each `s`, the compression relator
`t s t^-1 = w_s` holds exactly, so `T_n U_n(s) T_n^* = w_s(U_n)` lies in
`Gamma_n`. Hence `T_n Gamma_n T_n^* <= Gamma_n`.

**Step 3 (dimension count).** Put `A_n = Gamma_n' ∩ M_(d_n)` and
`B_n = (T_n Gamma_n T_n^*)' ∩ M_(d_n)`.

- Step 2 gives `A_n ⊆ B_n`.
- `B_n = T_n A_n T_n^*`, so `dim B_n = dim A_n`.
- Hence `A_n = B_n`.

This is the mechanism of `ccr-holds-for-all-genuine-fd-coordinate-models`.
Here it is run with exactness only on `F`.

**Step 4 (Kazhdan lift).** Let `x` be in `sigma(Lambda)'`. Since
`Lambda = t Gamma t^-1` is generated by the `t s t^-1`, `x` commutes with every
`sigma(t) sigma(s) sigma(t)^*`. Choose representatives `x_n` with
`||x_n|| <= ||x||`. Then

```text
eps_n := max_(s in S_Gamma) || [x_n, T_n U_n(s) T_n^*] ||_2  ->  0   along omega.
```

Let `Gamma~` act on `L^2(M_(d_n), tr)` by
`gamma -> Ad(T_n U_n(gamma) T_n^*)`. This is a unitary representation, and it
factors through `Gamma_n`.

- By Step 1, `(S_Gamma, kappa)` is a Kazhdan pair for it.
- `|| Ad(V) a - a ||_2 = || [V, a] ||_2` for unitary `V`.
- The invariant vectors are exactly `B_n`, and the projection onto them is
  `E_(B_n)`.

By (K),

```text
|| x_n - E_(B_n)(x_n) ||_2 <= eps_n / kappa .
```

Put `y_n = E_(B_n)(x_n)`.

- By (CE), `||y_n|| <= ||x||`, so `y = (y_n)` defines an element of `M`.
- `||x_n - y_n||_2 -> 0`, so `y = x`.
- By Step 3, `y_n` lies in `A_n`, so it commutes exactly with every `U_n(s)`.

Hence `x` commutes with `sigma(S_Gamma)`, so `x` is in `sigma(Gamma)'`.

**Step 5 (conclusion).**

- Step 4 gives `sigma(Lambda)' ⊆ sigma(Gamma)'`. The reverse inclusion holds
  because `Lambda <= Gamma`. So condition 1 of
  `kt-compressor-commutant-collapse-is-all-or-nothing` holds for
  `pi = sigma : G -> U(M)` and our `t`.
- By that theorem (condition 1 implies condition 4),
  `sigma(Gamma)' = sigma(E)'`.
- Since `u = e_12(x_1^-1)` lies in `E`, every element of `sigma(Gamma)'`
  commutes with `sigma(u)`. So there is no enemy. ∎

**Remark 1.1 (what was used).** Exactness of `R_0` enters only through the
uniform Kazhdan constant of `Gamma_n`. Exactness of `C_t` enters only through
`T_n Gamma_n T_n^* <= Gamma_n`. So Theorem 1 holds under the weaker hypothesis:

- the `Gamma_n = <U_n(S_Gamma)>` have a uniform Kazhdan constant for
  `U_n(S_Gamma)`, and
- `T_n Gamma_n T_n^* <= Gamma_n`.

The other relations of `G`, including the lamps `u` and the `SL_d(Z)` part, are
used only in Step 5, through the all-or-nothing theorem, and there only as
relations in `M`.

**Remark 1.2 (the genuine case).** If `sigma|_(V_T)` is an ultraproduct of
genuine representations of `V~_T = <S_Gamma, t | F>` of dimensions `d_n`, the
hypothesis holds. In particular it holds for ultraproducts of genuine
representations of `G`, which recovers the no-enemy consequence of
`ccr-holds-for-all-genuine-fd-coordinate-models`.

## 2. Proof of Theorem 2

Let `sigma_n = rho_n ∘ phi_n` define `sigma`. This means: for each `g` in `S`,
`sigma(g)` is the class of `(rho_n(phi_n(g)))_n`, and these classes satisfy
every relator of `G` in `M`. Use the representatives

```text
U_n(s) = rho_n(phi_n(s)),   T_n = rho_n(phi_n(t)) .
```

For a word `w` over `S_Gamma ∪ {t}`, `w(U_n, T_n) = rho_n(z_(n,w))`, where
`z_(n,w)` is the value of `w` on `phi_n` in `Q_n`.

**Every word in F is a relator of G.** The words in `R_0` are relators of
`Gamma <= G`, and the words `t s t^-1 w_s^-1` hold in `G` by the choice of
`w_s`. Since `sigma` is a homomorphism, `w(sigma) = 1` in `M`, so
`||rho_n(z_(n,w)) - 1||_2 -> 0` along `omega`. For a unitary `V`,

```text
||V - 1||_2^2 = 2 - 2 Re tr V ,
```

so `Re tr rho_n(z_(n,w)) -> 1` for **every** `w` in `F`.

**Some defect is nontrivial.** For `w` in `F`, let
`N_w = { n : rho_n(z_(n,w)) = 1 }`.

- Suppose every `N_w` is in `omega`. Since `F` is finite, `N_0 = ∩_w N_w` is in
  `omega`. On `N_0` the representatives satisfy `F` exactly, so Theorem 1
  applies and `sigma` has no enemy.
- So if `sigma` has an enemy, some `N_w` is not in `omega`. Since `omega` is an
  ultrafilter, its complement `{ n : rho_n(z_(n,w)) != 1 }` is in `omega`. On
  that set also `z_(n,w) != 1`.

Together with the previous paragraph, this proves Theorem 2. ∎

**Corollary 2.1 (general gap criterion).** Suppose there is `c < 1` such that,
for omega-almost every `n`, every `q` in `Q_n` with `rho_n(q) != 1` satisfies
`Re tr rho_n(q) <= c`. Then the transported model has no enemy.

*Proof.* If there were an enemy, Theorem 2 would give `w` with
`rho_n(z_(n,w)) != 1` on an `omega`-set and `Re tr rho_n(z_(n,w)) -> 1`.
That contradicts `Re tr rho_n(z_(n,w)) <= c`. ∎

Only the finitely many elements `z_(n,w)` (`w` in `F`) need to satisfy the
bound. The lamp images, and the defects of all other relators of `G`, are
unconstrained.

## 3. Proof of Corollary 3

Cases (a)-(d) check the hypothesis of Corollary 2.1. Case (e) needs a
reduction first.

**(a) Regular transports.** If `rho_n(q) != 1`, then `q != 1`, so
`tr rho_n(q) = 0`. Take `c = 0`.

**(b) Fixed-point-ratio permutation transports.** Here
`tr rho_n(q) = |Fix(q)| / |Y_n|`, and `rho_n(q) != 1` means that `q` moves a
point. The hypothesis gives `c`.

- For `GL(V)` acting on `V = F_q^m`: the fixed set of `g != 1` is the proper
  subspace `ker(g - 1)`, of size at most `q^(m-1)`, so the ratio is at most
  `1/q`.
- For `AGL(V)`: the fixed set of a nontrivial map `v -> Av + b` is empty or a
  coset of `ker(A - 1)`. Since the map is nontrivial, this coset is proper, so
  the ratio is at most `1/q`.

**(c) Tensor-permutation transports.** A permutation `pi` of `X_n` acting on
`(C^k)^(⊗X_n)` has trace `k^(c(pi))`, where `c(pi)` is its number of cycles.
If `pi != 1`, then `c(pi) <= |X_n| - 1`. So `tr <= 1/k <= 1/2`.

**(d) Weil/Clifford transports.** Let `U != 1` lie in the n-qubit Clifford
group with scalars in `mu_8`. Let `{P_a : a in F_2^(2n)}` be the Hermitian
Pauli operators. They form an orthogonal basis of `M_(2^n)` with
`Tr(P_a P_b) = 2^n delta_(ab)`, and conjugation by `U` permutes them up to
sign: `U P_a U^* = eps(a) P_(g a)` with `eps(a) = ±1`, where `g` is the image of
`U` in `Sp_(2n)(F_2)`.

- Compute the trace of `Ad U` on `M_(2^n)` in this basis:

  ```text
  |Tr U|^2 = Tr(Ad U) = sum_a 2^-n Tr(P_a U P_a U^*) = sum_(a : g a = a) eps(a) .
  ```

  So `|Tr U|^2 <= |ker(g - 1)|`.
- **If `g != 1`:** `dim ker(g - 1) <= 2n - 1`. So `|tr U|^2 <= 2^(2n-1) / 4^n`,
  that is, `|tr U| <= 2^(-1/2)`.
- **If `g = 1`:** `a -> eps(a)` is a character of `F_2^(2n)`, hence of the
  form `eps(a) = (-1)^(<a, b>)` for the symplectic form and some `b`. Then
  `U P_b^*` commutes with every `P_a`, so it is a scalar, and `U = lambda P_b`.
  - If `b != 0`, then `Tr U = 0`.
  - If `b = 0`, then `U = lambda` is a scalar of the group, so `lambda` is in
    `mu_8 \ {1}` and `Re lambda <= cos(pi/4) = 2^(-1/2)`.

So `c = 2^(-1/2)` works.

**(e) Bounded products of simple groups, any representation.**

Let `Q_n = S_(n,1) x ... x S_(n,k)`, with `k` fixed. Each factor is a finite
simple group of Lie type, a sporadic group, or an alternating group of degree
at most a fixed `m_0`. Let `rho_n` be arbitrary. The constant we use is

```text
c_0 = max(19/20, c_fin) < 1 .
```

- `19/20` is Gluck's uniform bound `|chi(x)/chi(1)| <= 19/20`, for `x` not
  central in a quasisimple group of Lie type and `chi != 1` irreducible
  (Gluck, J. Algebra 174 (1995) 229-266, as stated in Liebeck's survey,
  Thm 2.4).
- `c_fin` is the maximum of `|chi(x)|/chi(1)` over the finitely many sporadic
  groups, the alternating groups of degree at most `m_0`, and any finitely many
  small exceptions to that statement, taken over `x != 1` and `chi != 1`. Each
  ratio is `< 1`: for `G` simple and `chi != 1`, the centre of `chi` is
  `Z(G) = 1`, and `|chi(x)| = chi(1)` would put `x` in it.

So `|chi(x)/chi(1)| <= c_0` for every nontrivial `x` in a factor and every
nontrivial irreducible `chi` of that factor.

*Step 1 (drop asymptotically trivial factors).*

- Let `P_(n,i)` be the projection onto the `rho_n(S_(n,i))`-fixed vectors,
  which is the average of `rho_n` over `S_(n,i)`.
- Since `S_(n,i)` is normal in `Q_n`, `P_(n,i)` is a central projection of the
  algebra `rho_n(C Q_n)`. So the `P_(n,i)` commute with `rho_n(Q_n)` and with
  each other.
- Put `w_i = lim_omega tr P_(n,i)`, `I_1 = { i : w_i = 1 }`, and
  `P_n = prod_(i in I_1) P_(n,i)`.
- Then `tr(1 - P_n) <= sum_(i in I_1) tr(1 - P_(n,i)) -> 0`.

Put `d_n' = rank P_n`, so `d_n'/d_n -> 1`. Then

```text
[a_n] -> [P_n a_n P_n] :  prod_omega M_(d_n) -> prod_omega M_(d_n')
```

is a trace-preserving `*`-isomorphism, because `||a - P a P||_2 -> 0` for
bounded `a` and the normalized traces differ by the factor `d_n/d_n' -> 1`.
Under it, `sigma` becomes the transport `rho_n' ∘ phi_n'`, where:

- `phi_n'` is `phi_n` followed by the projection onto
  `Q_n' = prod_(i not in I_1) S_(n,i)`;
- `rho_n'` is `rho_n` restricted to `P_n C^(d_n)`, which factors through
  `Q_n'` because the `S_(n,i)`, `i in I_1`, act trivially there.

Enemies correspond under the isomorphism. For `i not in I_1`, the fixed
projection of `S_(n,i)` in the new space is `P_(n,i) P_n`, and its normalized
trace tends to `w_i' <= w_i < 1`.

If `I_1` is everything, `rho_n'` is trivial and `F` holds exactly, so there is
no enemy by Theorem 1.

*Step 2 (the ratio bound at a defect).*

- Suppose `sigma'` has an enemy. Theorem 2 gives `w` in `F` with
  `q_n := z'_(n,w) != 1` on an `omega`-set and `Re tr rho_n'(q_n) -> 1`.
- Choose `j(n)` with the `j(n)`-th coordinate of `q_n` nontrivial. There are
  finitely many choices, so `j(n) = j` on an `omega`-set.
- Decompose `rho_n'` into irreducibles `psi = ⊗_i chi_i` of `Q_n'`. Then
  `psi(q_n)/psi(1) = prod_i chi_i((q_n)_i)/chi_i(1)`.
- If `chi_j != 1`, this has modulus at most `c_0`, since the `j`-th factor is
  bounded by `c_0` and the others by 1.
- The isotypic part with `chi_j = 1` is exactly the range of the fixed
  projection of `S_(n,j)`, of normalized trace `tau_n -> w_j' < 1`.

Hence

```text
Re tr rho_n'(q_n) <= tau_n + (1 - tau_n) c_0  ->  w_j' + (1 - w_j') c_0 < 1 ,
```

a contradiction. So `sigma` has no enemy. ∎

**Remark 3.1 (why alternating groups of unbounded degree escape).** In `A_m`,
a 3-cycle has ratio `(m - 4)/(m - 1) -> 1` on the standard character. Neither
Step 2 nor any other uniform bound applies. The same holds for `Sym(Y_n)` in
its permutation representation (the Hamming transport) and for products with
unboundedly many factors. These are exactly the survivors named in the claim.
This is consistent with the sofic Pauli wall models of `P_X ⋊ V_T` in
`monomial-kazhdan-hnn-is-rf-but-non-rfd-and-not-vfhs-stable`, where the
Kazhdan relators hold only in Hamming measure.

## 4. Calibration

- **Genuine representations of G.** Theorem 1 applies (Remark 1.2). This
  reproduces the credited (CCR) result for genuine coordinate models.
- **Dyadic analogue, without (T).** Step 1 fails, since there is no Kazhdan
  pair, and the corresponding enemies in sofic models are not excluded. This
  is correct.
- **Sofic Pauli models of `P_X ⋊ V_T`.** These have `Ad sigma(e) != 0` for
  `e = t p_Gamma t^-1 - p_Gamma`, and their defects are Hamming-small. They fall
  outside Corollary 3, as they must, since they exist.

## 5. What this leaves

A matrix model with an enemy, if one exists, restricts on `V_T` to a
non-liftable almost representation of `V~_T`: no representatives satisfy `F`
exactly, and not even up to a uniform Kazhdan quotient (Remark 1.1). Any
finite-carrier realization of such a model has trace-invisible defects at `F`.

The sharpest open step is the **extension problem**: extend one sofic wall
model of `V_T` with `Ad sigma(e) != 0` to an HS-almost representation of all of
`G`. That means adjoining `sigma(u)` and the `SL_d(Z)` action compatibly with
the relations of `E ⋊ SL_d(Z)`. By the all-or-nothing theorem, such an
extension would carry an enemy automatically.
