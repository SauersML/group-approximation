## Level-set complexes

- `D_1, …, D_n` are integer intervals `[−a_i, b_i]` with `a_i, b_i ≥ 0`. Let `I = [t_1, t_2]` be an interval of
  **width** `w = t_2 − t_1 ≥ 0`.
- `Λ(D; I)` is the simplicial complex on vertices `(i, v)`, `v ∈ D_i`, whose faces are the partial assignments `F`
  (at most one value per coordinate) that complete to a full assignment with sum `s` for **every** `s ∈ I`.
- For a partial `F` with assigned sum `s_F` and unassigned set `R`, the completion sums form the interval
  `[s_F − a_R, s_F + b_R]`, where `a_R = Σ_R a_i` and `b_R = Σ_R b_i`. So `F ∈ Λ(D; I)` iff
  `I ⊆ [s_F − a_R, s_F + b_R]`.
- For `w = 0`, `Λ(D; {t})` is the pure complex generated by the level set `{Σ x = t}`.

## Theorem L

Suppose `|D_i| ≥ 2` for all `i`, and `∅ ∈ Λ(D; I)`. Then `Λ(D; I)` is `(⌊(n−w)/2⌋ − 2)`-connected.

If some `D_i = {0}`, then `Λ` is a cone on `(i, 0)` whenever `i` is unassigned, hence contractible.

**Proof,** by induction on `n`. The bound is `≤ −2`, hence vacuous, when `n − w ≤ 1`. So assume `n − w ≥ 2`.
Split off coordinate `n`, with `D' = (D_1, …, D_{n−1})`.

1. **Cones.** For `v ∈ D_n`, put `A_v = Λ(D'; I − v)`. Faces containing `(n,v)` are exactly `(n,v) ∗ G` with
   `G ∈ A_v`. Let `Y = ⋃_v C_v`, where `C_v = (n,v) ∗ A_v`, over the `v` with `∅ ∈ A_v`.
   - These `v` form an interval `V = D_n ∩ [t_2 − b', t_1 + a']`, with `a' = a_{[n−1]}` and `b' = b_{[n−1]}`.
2. **Nesting.** For `u < v < u'` in `V`, `A_u ∩ A_{u'} ⊆ A_v`, because completion sums form an interval.
   - So, listing `V = v_1 < v_2 < ⋯`, `Y_k = ⋃_{i≤k} C_{v_i}` meets `C_{v_{k+1}}` exactly in
     `Z_k = A_{v_k} ∩ A_{v_{k+1}} = Λ(D'; [t_1 − v_{k+1}, t_2 − v_k])`.
   - `Z_k` has width `w + 1` and contains `∅`.
3. **Connectivity of `Y`.**
   - `Y_1` is a cone.
   - `Y_{k+1} ≃ Y_k ∪ C(Z_k)`, since `C_{v_{k+1}}` is contractible. So
     `conn Y_{k+1} ≥ min(conn Y_k, conn Z_k + 1)`.
   - By induction, `conn Z_k ≥ ⌊(n−1−w−1)/2⌋ − 2`. So `conn Y ≥ ⌊(n−w)/2⌋ − 2`.
4. **Uncovered faces.**
   - A face `F` avoiding coordinate `n` lies in some `A_v` iff the interval `[t_2 − s_F − b_R, t_1 − s_F + a_R]`
     meets `D_n`.
   - Given `F ∈ Λ(D; I)`, that happens iff `w ≤ a_R + b_R`.
   - So an uncovered face has `|R| ≤ a_R + b_R ≤ w − 1`, using `|D_i| ≥ 2`. It therefore has dimension
     `≥ (n−1) − (w−1) − 1 = n − w − 1`.
   - Adding faces of dimension `≥ d` in increasing order preserves `(d−2)`-connectivity. So
     `conn Λ(D; I) ≥ min(conn Y, n − w − 3) ≥ ⌊(n−w)/2⌋ − 2`, since `n − w ≥ 2`. ∎

**Check against hand computations** (earlier version of this node).
- `D_i = {−1,0,1}`, `h = 2`, `t = 0`: three disjoint edges, `(−1)`-connected, equal to the bound `⌊2/2⌋ − 2`.
- `h = 3`, `t = 0`: 0-connected, above the bound −1.

## Proposition 1: the zero-sum germ complex is contractible

`K_0 ⊆ K = ∏'_p T_p` is spanned by partial portraits that complete to total defect 0. By the referee's form of
`wall-flux-is-not-a-houghton-obstruction`, item 4, it is the `L`-orbit of the non-singular cells.
- **The cover.** For a zero-sum portrait `π`, let `Q_π` be the set of partial portraits that agree with `π` off
  their hidden set. It is a restricted product of intervals `{π(p), ∗}`, which is contractible.
- **`K_0 = ⋃_π Q_π`.** A cube lies in `K_0` iff its bottom vertex is completable, because completability is
  monotone under hiding. A completion `π` of the bottom vertex contains the whole cube in `Q_π`.
- **Intersections.** Every finite intersection `⋂ Q_{π_i}` hides the points where the `π_i` disagree and agrees
  with them elsewhere. It is a nonempty restricted product of intervals, hence contractible.
- **Conclusion.** The nerve of the cover is a full simplex. By the nerve theorem, `K_0` is contractible. ∎

## Proposition 2: descending links are inflations of level-set complexes

Let `γ` be a vertex of `K_0` with hidden set `M`, `|M| = h`. By item 4 of the parent it may be taken
non-singular, so the revealed total is 0. Put:
- `D_p` = the set of defects of B-germs at `p`, a finite subset of `⟨Δ⟩ ≅ Z` containing 0;
- `X_p^v` = the set of B-germs at `p` with defect `v`. It is finite, since `(L)_q/(B)_q` is finite and `B` is
  transitive on each singular orbit.

Then:
- `lk↓(γ)` is the inflation of `Λ = Λ(D_M; {0})` by the sets `X_p^v`.
- The inflation formula gives `lk↓(γ) ≃ ⋁_{F∈Λ} (Σ^{|F|} lk_Λ F)^{∨ν(F)}`, with `lk_Λ F = Λ(D_{M∖S}; {−s_F})`.
- By Theorem L (width 0), and treating coordinates with `D_p = {0}` as cone points,
  `conn lk↓(γ) ≥ min_F (⌊(h − |F|)/2⌋ − 2 + |F|) ≥ ⌊h/2⌋ − 2`. ∎

## The F_∞ Theorem

Let `L` be a bounded full contracting RSG over an SFT whose irreducible core is not a single cycle. Suppose its
defect group `⟨Δ⟩` is either finite, or infinite cyclic with every defect set `D_p` an integer interval. Then:
- `L` is `F_∞`;
- the twisted Brin–Thompson envelope `SV_L` is an `F_∞` simple group, conditional on
  `twisted-bt-of-full-contracting-rsg-inherits-f-n-from-rsg-family`, as in the parent nodes.

*Proof.*
- The finite case is `torsion-defect-costs-nothing-in-bhm-germ-complexes`.
- In the cyclic case, `L` acts on the contractible `K_0` (Proposition 1). Its sublevel sets `K_{0,≤n}` are
  cocompact with `F_∞` cell stabilizers (parent, item 4, per the referee).
- Descending links of vertices with `h` hidden points are `(⌊h/2⌋ − 2)`-connected (Proposition 2). This tends to
  infinity with `h`.
- By Bestvina–Brady, `K_{0,≤N}` is `(⌊(N+1)/2⌋ − 1)`-connected, and Brown's criterion gives `F_n` for every `n`. ∎

## What remains for surface groups

The right-angled pentagon RSG `[[P | ∂_h P]]` satisfies the hypotheses if three finite checks hold:
- (i) boundedness (evidence so far: 4 non-branching singular rays per generator at depths ≤ 5);
- (ii) its true defect group is `Z`, with no torsion in the kernel of the map to the coarse `H_0 ≅ Z`;
- (iii) each `D_p` is an interval, e.g. `⊆ {−1, 0, 1}`.

If they hold, `SV_{[[P|∂_h P]]}` is an `F_∞` simple group containing `P` undistortedly. Every closed surface group
of genus ≥ 2 is commensurable into `P`, so they all get `F_∞` simple hosts, and this would be a partial answer to
BBMZ Question 1.2 for a full contracting RSG with nontrivial integer flux.

## Lesson for general BH

- **Linear connectivity of level-set joins is enough.**
  - Level-set complexes of integer interval sums gain connectivity at half the rate of the full join. That is
    enough for Brown's criterion.
  - The proof is a cone-and-nest induction: the completion sums of a partial assignment always form an interval, so
    the cones over different last coordinates are glued along width-one-larger complexes, and the faces left
    uncovered are high-dimensional.
- **What changed.** Bounded germ extensions with an integer flux are therefore `F_∞`. The flux, which looked like a
  Houghton obstruction, costs only a factor 2 in connectivity.
