## The conjecture

**Conjecture C.** Let `D_1, …, D_h` be finite sets of integers, and let `t` be an integer such that
`Z = {x ∈ D_1 × ⋯ × D_h : Σ x_i = t}` is nonempty. Let `Λ = Λ(D; t)` be the pure `(h−1)`-dimensional complex on
vertices `(i, v)`, `v ∈ D_i`, generated by the facets `{(i, x_i)}` for `x ∈ Z`. Then `Λ` is `(h−3)`-connected.

A weaker form suffices: `(h − 2 − C)`-connected, with `C` depending only on `max |D_i|`.

**General coefficients.** When the defect group has torsion (the true `⟨Δ⟩` may; referee bh-ref-q12), read `D_i` as
finite subsets of a finitely generated abelian group `A`, and `t ∈ A`. The same conjecture is expected.

**Links are of the same kind.** The link of a face `F`, a partial assignment on `S` with sum `s`, is
`Λ(D_{[h]∖S}; t − s)`. So Conjecture C is closed under links.

## Proposition 1: the zero-sum germ complex is contractible

`K_0 ⊆ K = ∏'_p T_p` is the germ complex of `wall-flux-is-not-a-houghton-obstruction`, spanned by partial
portraits that complete to total defect 0.
- **The cover.** For a zero-sum portrait `π`, let `Q_π` be the set of partial portraits that agree with `π` off
  their hidden set. It is a restricted product of intervals `{π(p), ∗}`, a contractible cube complex.
- **`K_0 = ⋃_π Q_π`.**
  - A cube of `K` lies in `K_0` iff its bottom vertex (fewest hidden points) is completable, because
    completability is monotone under hiding.
  - If `π` completes the bottom vertex, the whole cube lies in `Q_π`.
- **Intersections.** `⋂_i Q_{π_i}` consists of the portraits that hide every point where the `π_i` disagree and
  agree with them elsewhere. That is a nonempty restricted product of intervals, hence contractible.
- **Conclusion.** By the nerve theorem, `K_0` is homotopy equivalent to the nerve of the cover, which is a full
  simplex. So `K_0` is contractible. ∎

## Proposition 2: descending links are inflations of level-set complexes

Let `γ` be a vertex of `K_0` with hidden set `M` (`|M| = h`) and revealed total `c`. For `p ∈ M` let `D_p` be the
set of defects of B-germs at `p`, and `X_p^v` the (finite, nonempty) set of B-germs at `p` with defect `v`.
- The descending link `lk↓(γ)` has one vertex for each revealed germ `(p, ξ)`. A set of such vertices spans a
  face iff the points are distinct and revealing them keeps `γ` completable.
- So `lk↓(γ)` is the **inflation** of `Λ = Λ(D_M; −c)`, in which each vertex `(p, v)` is replaced by the set
  `X_p^v`.
- **Finiteness of `X_p^v`.** It is finite because `(L)_q/(B)_q` is finite and `B` is transitive on each singular
  orbit.
- **The inflation formula.**
  `lk↓(γ) ≃ ⋁_{F∈Λ} (Σ^{|F|} lk_Λ F)^{∨ ν(F)}`, with `ν(F) = ∏_{(p,v)∈F} (|X_p^v| − 1)`.
  Hence `conn lk↓(γ) ≥ min_F (conn lk_Λ(F) + |F|)`.
- **Only `c = 0` is needed at the top.** Every vertex of `K_0` is `L`-equivalent to a non-singular one
  (`wall-flux-is-not-a-houghton-obstruction`, item 4, per referee), so only `Λ(D_M; 0)` occurs as a top link. Its
  face links `Λ(D_{M∖S}; −s)` still need all targets.
- **Consequence.** By closure under links, Conjecture C gives `conn lk↓(γ) ≥ h − 3`. ∎

## Theorem (conditional on Conjecture C)

Let `L` be a bounded full contracting RSG whose defects are bounded. Then:
- `L` is `F_∞`, and so is `SV_L`.

*Proof.*
- `K_0` is contractible (Proposition 1).
- `L` acts on `K_0` with cocompact sublevel sets `K_{0,≤n}` and `F_∞` cell stabilizers (items 2 and 4 of the
  parent).
- Descending links of vertices with `h` hidden points are `(h−3)`-connected (Proposition 2 and Conjecture C).
- By Bestvina–Brady, `K_{0,≤n}` is `(n−2)`-connected, and Brown's criterion gives `F_(n−1)` for every `n`.
- `SV_L` follows as in `bounded-defect-free-full-contracting-rsgs-are-f-infinity`. ∎

**Application.** Assume Conjecture C, and boundedness of the right-angled pentagon RSG `[[P | ∂_h P]]` (evidence:
4 non-branching singular rays per generator at depths ≤ 5, `gq-bh-finf-hyp-pentagon-activity-and-defect-check.md`).
- Then `SV_{[[P | ∂_h P]]}` is an `F_∞` simple group containing `P` undistortedly.
- Every closed orientable surface group of genus ≥ 2 has finite index in the genus-2 group, which has finite index
  in `P`. So all of them get `F_∞` simple hosts by one mechanism.
- These groups were already covered through 2V. The value is a partial answer to BBMZ Question 1.2 for a full
  contracting RSG with nontrivial flux.

## 3. Evidence: small cases (hand computation)

Take `D_i = {−1, 0, 1}`.
- `h = 2, t = 0`: three disjoint edges, so `(−1)`-connected, which is `h − 3`. This is sharp: `(h−2)` fails.
- `h = 2, t = ±1`: two disjoint edges. `t = ±2`: one edge.
- `h = 3, t = 0`: 9 vertices, 21 edges and 7 triangles. It is connected, with `χ̃ = −6`, so `b_1 − b_2 = 6`: it is
  0-connected, which is `h − 3`.

Take `D_i = {0, 1}`.
- `h = 3, t = 2`: three triangles meeting pairwise in single vertices. It is connected with `H_1 = Z`: 0-connected,
  which is `h − 3`.
- `t = h`: a simplex.

Every case checked attains exactly `h − 3` or better.

## Routes to a proof

- **Cone decomposition.** `Λ(D; t) = ⋃_{v∈D_1} (1,v) ∗ Λ(D_{≥2}; t − v)`, together with the faces avoiding
  coordinate 1 that complete to different `t − v`.
  - A nerve-type induction needs the interval-completable complexes `⋂_{s∈I} Λ(D; s)` to gain connectivity with
    the length of the interval `I`.
  - That strengthened statement is the natural inductive hypothesis.
- **Shelling.** Order the facets `x ∈ Z` lexicographically and test shellability of the resulting pure complex;
  a shellable pure `(h−1)`-complex with no `(h−2)`-homology beyond the obvious part would suffice.
- **Discrete Morse theory.** Match faces by toggling the last coordinate's value within `D_h`.

## Lesson for general BH

- **For bounded germ extensions with integer flux, all remaining topology is numerical.**
  - The germ complex restricted to zero-sum-completable portraits is contractible, by one nerve argument.
  - Its descending links are inflations of **level-set complexes of integer sums**.
  - So `F_∞` for surface-type BBMZ hosts, and more generally for bounded full contracting RSGs, hangs on a single
    statement about subsets of `ℤ^h`: level sets of `Σ x_i` generate `(h−3)`-connected complexes.
