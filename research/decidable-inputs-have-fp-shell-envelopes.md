For every infinite finitely presented group P with decidable word
problem, some bijection nu:P->N gives a finitely presented group

    E_nu=<V,hat(P)>

in the exact shell construction. This is OPEN. It suffices for
Boone--Higman because finite point stabilizers of E_nu are already
finitely generated for every nu. Any successful nu must be computable,
by `shell-recursive-presentations-compute-enumerations`, but no uniform
algorithm constructing it from P is included in this assertion.

The earlier three-stabilizer hypothesis implies this one. Its necessary
germ and ascending HNN tests apply to that stronger hypothesis, since
they pass through finite presentation of A_1. They have not been shown
necessary for finite presentation of E_nu alone. In particular this
claim does not require a finitely presented isotropy germ group Q.

The restricted hypothesis
`perfect-decidable-inputs-have-fp-shell-envelopes` already suffices
for Boone--Higman: every input embeds in an infinite perfect
finitely presented decidable group, and its shell envelope is
simple for every enumeration. That does not establish the present
claim about a shell envelope of the original input itself.

## Attempts

The finite-ray construction proves this for virtually cyclic inputs.
The general finite-germ construction supplies faithfulness, finite
generation, and the point-stabilizer condition. The independent-germ
proof does not make the neighborhood kernel finitely generated and
does not establish a finite presentation of any general S(M,F).
No finite presentation of E_nu for all decidable inputs has been found.
