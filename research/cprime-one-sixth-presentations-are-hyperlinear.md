Let

```text
G = F_k / <<R>>
```

be given by a finite symmetrized classical `C'(1/6)` presentation.  Then `G`
is hyperlinear.

The reason is structural, not probabilistic.  Martin--Steenbock's cubulation
theorem for `C'(1/6)` quotients of free products applies to
`F_k = Z * ... * Z`, so `G` is cubulated.  Classical finite `C'(1/6)` gives
word-hyperbolicity.  Agol--Groves--Manning then make a hyperbolic cubulated
group virtually special; in particular it is linear and residually finite.
Residual finiteness implies soficity, and soficity implies hyperlinearity.

Thus any attempted nonhyperlinear construction that finishes by certifying
its finite relator set with classical `C'(1/6)` has certified the opposite of
its desired conclusion.
