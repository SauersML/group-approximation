Avoid the normal-lattice problem entirely by solving the corresponding
problem one category down, where the objects are explicit.

Let `G` be finitely generated with solvable word problem.  Run the
Chatterji--Kassabov chain to a finitely presented group `G~` containing `G`
with the simple core inside `[G~, G~]`, and set `R = Z[G~]`, a finitely
presented unital ring.  By
`universal-sandwich-ring-is-finitely-presented-and-injective` the sandwich
extension

    U = R<p,q>/(p(1-g)q - 1)

is a finitely presented ring containing `R`, so the coefficient ring at the
start of the process is finitely presented and, by Clapham, may also be taken
to have decidable equality.  By
`finite-fullness-simulation-theorem-for-rings`, compile the remaining
fullness requirements into a single finitely presented simple extension
`U <= L` with `R` still embedded.  Then `EL_4(L)` is finitely presented by
Krstić--McCool, is normally generated by `g` by the Steinberg computation
that `chatterji-kassabov-normal-generator-embedding` already performs, and
simplicity of `L` removes the ideals that
`one-sided-sandwich-leaves-an-ideal-missing-the-core` exhibits for `U`.

## What this route actually trades

It does not make the problem easier by making it smaller; it makes it
concrete.  The open prerequisite is a statement about explicitly presented
rings and an explicitly enumerated list of requirements, with decidable
equality available throughout, whereas
`ck-envelope-has-a-finitely-normally-generated-maximal-kernel` is a statement
about a normal lattice with no handle on its elements.  Whether that is
progress is a judgement about which category is more tractable, not a
theorem.

## Two things this route must not be read as claiming

**It is not the Leavitt route.**  `boone-higman-via-universal-leavitt-host`
is dead, and this route deliberately does not cite a fixed simple host.  It
asks for a bespoke extension of the specific coefficient ring, which is the
form that `weyl-algebra-blocks-a-universal-leavitt-host` leaves open.

**Simplicity of `L` is not by itself enough for the final group, and the
route uses more.**  The last step needs `EL_4(L)` finitely presented, which
comes from `L` being finitely presented, not from `L` being simple; and it
needs the elementary group of a simple ring to be simple, which is the part
supplied by the Steinberg/normal-generation computation rather than by ring
simplicity alone.  Both are inside the open prerequisite's conclusion or the
imported theorem, and neither is free.
