Let `p=(p_ij) in M_n(B)` and `C=C*(p_ij)`.  Ideals of `M_n(C)`
correspond to ideals of `C`, and the ideal generated by `p` corresponds to
the ideal generated by all its entries.  Since those entries generate `C`,
the projection `p` is full in `M_n(C)`.  Therefore the unital corner

```text
p M_n(C) p
```

is Morita equivalent to `C`.  Its primitive spectrum is compact, and hence
so is `Prim(C)`.

If `C` is stable, choose an isomorphism `M_n(C) ~= C`.  The image of `p` is
already a nonzero level-one projection.  This is the first descent
mechanism.

For the second, let `K subset Prim(C)` be nonempty, compact and open, and
suppose the corresponding ideal `I=C(K)` is homogeneous of degree `r`.
The standard structure theorem for homogeneous C*-algebras gives a locally
trivial C*-bundle `E` over `K`, with fibre `M_r(C)`, such that

```text
I ~= Gamma_0(K,E).
```

Because `K` is compact, the fibrewise identity is a section of `I`:

```text
0!=q=1_E in Gamma(K,E)=I subset C.
```

Thus `q` is the unit of `I`, hence a central projection in `C`.  Notice that
no regularity is required of `C` off `K`.  If `C` itself is homogeneous,
fullness of `p` says that its nonzero fibre support is all of `Prim(C)`; the
compactness proved above recovers the old homogeneous theorem.  Equivalently,
one can see compactness directly from the projection section:

```text
Prim(C)={x:||p(x)||>=1/2}.
```

When `r=1`, the island projection is also the positive support of the
integer-valued ordinary matrix trace `sum_i p_ii` there.

Apply either mechanism with `B=H` in `(CI)`.  It puts a nonzero projection
inside every nonzero hereditary subalgebra `H` of `A`, which is property
(SP).  The established exact LXXIV property-(SP) theorem then makes `A`
purely infinite.  The finite-unit-root theorem explains why `(CI)` adds no
existence assumption at the stabilized level: for `0!=a in H_+`, some
finite multiple of `[a]` is the compact unit class, so a nonzero projection
occurs in a finite matrix algebra over `Her_A(a) subset H`.

Here is the sharp obstruction to extending the theorem to subhomogeneous,
CCR, or type-I coefficients.  Fix `N>=3`, choose projections `e_2,e_3 in
M_N(C)` of ranks two and three, and set

```text
D={f in C([0,1],M_N): f(0) in C e_2 and f(1) in C e_3}.
```

The algebra `D` is subhomogeneous.  It is projectionless: if `q in D` is a
projection, then `t |-> rank(q(t))` is locally constant and hence constant
on `[0,1]`.  A nonzero `q` would have rank two at zero and rank three at one,
which is impossible.

Nevertheless `M_3(D)` contains a projection.  In `M_3(M_N)` put

```text
P_0=1_3 tensor e_2,
P_1=r_2 tensor e_3,
```

where `r_2 in M_3(C)` has rank two.  Both projections have rank six, so a
unitary path in the connected finite-dimensional unitary group joins them
by a projection path `P(t)`.  Its endpoint entries lie respectively in
`C e_2` and `C e_3`, hence `P in M_3(D)`.

Let `C_P=C*(P_ij) subset D`.  Subhomogeneity passes to C*-subalgebras, so
`C_P` is subhomogeneous, and it is projectionless because `D` is.  The
opening fullness argument applied to `P` shows that `P` is full in
`M_3(C_P)` and `Prim(C_P)` is compact.  Thus the failure remains even for a
full stabilized projection with compact-open support equal to the whole
primitive spectrum.  Since subhomogeneous algebras are CCR and type I, this
also refutes those two proposed blanket extensions.  The example pinpoints
what homogeneity prevented: endpoint multiplicities can balance only after
matrix amplification.

Finally, if `D` is a projectionless LXXIV corner, the two positive
mechanisms rule out stable coefficients and every compact-open homogeneous
coefficient ideal for every nonzero stabilized projection over `D`.  For a
scalar unitary `U in M_n(C)`, each coefficient of `U* p U` is a complex
linear combination of the coefficients of `p`, and applying `U` instead of
`U*` gives the reverse inclusion.  Hence the generated coefficient algebra
is unchanged by scalar basis rotation.

No comparison, cancellation, property (QQ), algebraicity, separability, or
nuclear regularity is used.  General continuous-trace coefficients with
varying finite ranks are not asserted here: the proof covers precisely a
compact-open constant finite-rank island, while stable continuous-trace
coefficients fall under the stable case.  The subhomogeneous example shows
that compact primitive spectrum and finite-dimensional fibres, without the
continuous-trace/local-homogeneity control, are insufficient.
