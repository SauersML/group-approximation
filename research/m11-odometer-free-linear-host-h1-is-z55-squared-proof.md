**Conventions.**

- Right actions, with `(x w)^g = x^{π_g} w^{g|_x}` and `(gh)|_x = g|_x h|_{x^{π_g}}`.
- `g@y` is the element that acts as `g` below the vertex `y` and trivially elsewhere.
- `α`, `β`, `M_11` and `b = (b, α, β, 1, …, 1)` are as in item 6 of
  `tits-hosts-for-transitive-local-actions`, where `R_0 = ⟨M_11, b⟩` is shown to satisfy
  conditions (S), (B) and (A) of `fully-self-replicating-tree-groups-give-fg-tits-hosts`,
  with `A_d` replaced by `M_11`.
- `c = (c, bα, 1, …, 1)` and `u = c b^{-1}`. Since `b` and `c` have trivial root,
  `u|_x = c|_x (b|_x)^{-1}`, so `u = (u, b, β^{-1}, 1, …, 1)`.
- `R'' = ⟨M_11, b, c⟩ = ⟨M_11, b, u⟩`.

## 1. Full self-replication, activity, non-contraction

**(S).** Every section of every generator lies in `R''`. These sections are `c`, `bα`, `b`,
`α`, `β` and `1`. So every section of every element of `R''` lies in `R''`.

**(A).** The root image is generated by the roots of the generators, which is `M_11`.

**(B).** `R_0` satisfies (B), so `R_0@x ⊆ R_0` for every `x`. In particular `M_11@1`,
`b@1` and `(bα)@2` lie in `R_0 ⊆ R''`. The element `(bα)@2` has support below `2`,
and `c@1` has support below `1`. So `c = c@1 · (bα)@2`, and hence
`c@1 = c · ((bα)@2)^{-1} ∈ R''`. Then `R''@1 = ⟨M_11@1, b@1, c@1⟩ ⊆ R''`. Conjugating by
powers of `α ∈ M_11` gives `R''@x ⊆ R''` for all `x`. So (S), (B) and (A) hold, and the
section map is an isomorphism `R'' ≅ R'' ≀_X M_11`.

**Finite state and linear activity.**

- The states are `c, bα, b, u, α, β, β^{-1}` and `1`, together with their inverses, and the
  finitary elements of `M_11`.
- The states `b` and `u` have bounded activity: they have `3` nontrivial sections at each
  level.
- `c` has `c` at `1^n`, and its other nontrivial sections at level `n` come from the
  copies of `bα` at the vertices `1^k 2` with `k < n`. Each such copy contributes at most
  `3` nontrivial sections at each level.
- So `c` has at most `1 + 3n` nontrivial sections at level `n`, and its activity is linear.
- By Amir–Angel–Virág (linear-activity automaton groups are amenable; cited), `R''` is
  amenable.

**Lemma 1 (`bα` has infinite order).** For `π ∈ M_11`, let `L(π)` be the length of the
`π`-cycle through `1`, and define `T(π)` by `(bπ)^{L(π)}|_1 = b T(π)`.

- The sections of `bπ` are those of `b`, and the orbit of `1` under `bπ` is its orbit
  under `π`. So `(bπ)^{L}|_1` is the product of `b|_y` over this orbit, starting at `y = 1`.
  That product is `b` followed by a product of copies of `α` and `β`, so `T(π) ∈ M_11` is
  well defined.
- If `bπ` has finite order `n`, then `L | n`, and `(bπ)^L` fixes the letter `1`. So
  `(b T(π))^{n/L} = ((bπ)^L)^{n/L}|_1 = 1`. Hence `bT(π)` has finite order dividing
  `n / L(π)`.
- The script computes `T(α) = αβ`, `T(αβ) = β` and `T(β) = αβ`, with `L(αβ) = L(β) = 5`.
  If `bαβ` had finite order `n`, going once around the 2-cycle would give
  `n | n / 25`, which is impossible.
- So `bαβ` has infinite order. So do `bβ` and `bα`, since `b T(π)` having infinite order
  forces the same for `bπ`.

**Non-contraction.** Since `c` has trivial root, `c^m = (c^m, (bα)^m, 1, …, 1)`. So
`c^m ≠ 1` for all `m ≠ 0`, by Lemma 1. Each `c^m` is its own section at `1`, so it lies in
the nucleus. A contracting group has a finite nucleus. `R''` has the infinitely many
distinct elements `c^m` in its nucleus, so it is not contracting.

## 2. Two elements of order 55

**Lemma 2.** `b` and `u` have order exactly `55`.

*Proof.* Both have trivial root. So `b^n = (b^n, α^n, β^n, 1, …, 1)` and
`u^n = (u^n, b^n, β^{-n}, 1, …, 1)`.

- `b^{55} = (b^{55}, 1, …, 1)`. An element `g = (g, 1, …, 1)` with trivial root fixes every
  vertex, by induction on the level. So `b^{55} = 1`.
- If `55 ∤ n`, then `α^n ≠ 1` or `β^n ≠ 1`, so `b^n ≠ 1`.
- The same argument applies to `u`, using `b^{55} = 1` for the second coordinate and
  `b^n ≠ 1` or `β^{-n} ≠ 1` when `55 ∤ n`. ∎

`M_11` is perfect, so `H_1(R''; Z)` is generated by `[b]` and `[u]`. By Lemma 2, it is a
quotient of `(Z/55)^2`. It remains to construct two independent characters onto `Z/55`.

## 3. Words, sections and the descent lemma

Let `F` be the free product of `M_11` with the free group on `b, u`. Let `N` be the kernel
of `F → R''`. A word is an alternating product of elements of `M_11` and letters in
`{b, u}^{±1}`. `nf(w)` counts its letters in `{b, u}^{±1}`. The section `w|_x` is computed
letter by letter with the rule of the conventions.

- Letter sections of `b^{±1}` are `b^{±1}` at `1`, `α^{±1}` at `2`, `β^{±1}` at `3`, and
  empty elsewhere.
- Letter sections of `u^{±1}` are `u^{±1}` at `1`, `b^{±1}` at `2`, `β^{∓1}` at `3`, and
  empty elsewhere.
- Elements of `M_11` have empty sections. Both `b` and `u` have trivial root, so the point
  at which a letter is read is moved only by the `M_11` letters.

**Lemma 3.**

- (i) `nf(w|_x) ≤ nf(w)`.
- (ii) If `w ∈ N`, then `w|_x ∈ N` for every `x`.
- (iii) For every word, `Σ_x e_u(w|_x) = e_u(w)` and `Σ_x e_b(w|_x) = e_b(w) + e_u(w)`.

*Proof.* (i) holds because each letter contributes at most one letter.

(ii) holds because the section of the identity element is the identity.

(iii) A letter `u^{±1}` contributes `u^{±1}` at exactly one point and `b^{±1}` at exactly
one point. A letter `b^{±1}` contributes `b^{±1}` at exactly one point. The script checks
(iii) on all `37448` words of length `≤ 5` over `{b, u, α, β}^{±1}`. ∎

A pair `(w, x)` is **stable** if every letter of `w` in `{b, u}^{±1}` contributes a letter
in `{b, u}^{±1}` to `w|_x`. A **stable cycle of relations** is a sequence
`w_0, w_1, …, w_p = w_0` in `N` with `w_{i+1} = w_i|_{x_i}`, each `(w_i, x_i)` stable, and
`w_{i+1}` taken freely reduced, which does not change `nf`.

**Lemma 4 (descent, mod `n`).** Let `λ : F → Z/n` be a homomorphism that vanishes on
`M_11`. Suppose `Σ_x λ(w|_x) = λ(w)` for all `w ∈ N`, and `λ` vanishes on every relation
lying on a stable cycle. Then `λ(N) = 0`.

*Proof.* This is Lemma 2 of `m11-linear-activity-group-b1-two-not-finitely-presented-proof`,
with `Z` replaced by `Z/n`. The proof only uses that a nonzero value of `λ(w)` forces a
nonzero value of `λ(w|_x)` for some `x`. So it goes through with values in `Z/n`.

Start from `w_0 ∈ N` with `λ(w_0) ≠ 0`, and pass each time to a section with `λ ≠ 0`.

- By Lemma 3(i) and (ii), `nf` is non-increasing along this sequence. It is never `0`,
  since `λ ≠ 0` on every term. So it is eventually constant.
- From then on every pair is stable.
- The words of a fixed `nf` whose `M_11`-letters are merged form a finite set, so the
  sequence repeats.
- The part between two repeats is a stable cycle of relations with `λ ≠ 0`, which
  contradicts the hypothesis. ∎

**Lemma 5 (shape of stable cycles).** Let `w_0, …, w_p = w_0` be a stable cycle of
relations. Along the cycle:

- the number of `u`-letters is non-increasing, since only `u^{±1}` read at `1` gives a
  `u`-letter. It returns to its start, so it is constant, and every `u`-letter is read at
  point `1`;
- every `b`-letter is read at point `1`, since that is its only non-finitary section.

So in `w = w_0`, read from `x = x_0`, every letter of `{b, u}^{±1}` is read at point `1`.
The `M_11`-letters between the first and last of them fix `1`. The section is
`w|_x = W`, the word `w` with its `M_11`-letters deleted. In particular:

- `W` is a word over `{b, u}^{±1}`;
- `W ∈ N`;
- `e_b(W) = e_b(w)` and `e_u(W) = e_u(w)`.

The same holds for `R_0` with only the letter `b`. There, `W = b^{e_b(w)}`. ∎

The script checks the local part of Lemma 5 on `5586` stable pairs of length `≤ 6`. Every
stable pair reads all letters at point `1`, or is an all-`u` word read at point `2`. The
second kind drops the `u`-count, so it cannot lie on a cycle.

## 4. The characters

**Proposition 6.** `e_b` mod `55` descends to a surjection `R_0 → Z/55`. Hence
`H_1(R_0; Z) ≅ Z/55`.

*Proof.* On words over `M_11 ∪ {b}^{±1}`, `Σ_x e_b(w|_x) = e_b(w)`. By Lemma 5, a relation
on a stable cycle has `e_b(w) = e_b(W)` with `W = b^{e_b(w)} = 1`. So `55 | e_b(w)` by
Lemma 2. Lemma 4 applies. ∎

**Proposition 7.** `e_u` mod `55` descends to a surjection `λ : R'' → Z/55`, with
`λ(u) = 1`, `λ(b) = 0` and `λ(M_11) = 0`.

*Proof.* The mass hypothesis of Lemma 4 is Lemma 3(iii). Let `w` be a relation on a
stable cycle, and let `W` be as in Lemma 5.

- `W|_2` is `W` with `b ↦ α` and `u ↦ b`. It is a relation of `R_0`, by Lemma 3(ii).
- Its `b`-exponent is `e_u(W)`.
- By Proposition 6, `55 | e_u(W) = e_u(w)`. ∎

**Proposition 8.** `e_b` mod `55` descends to a surjection `ν : R'' → Z/55`, with
`ν(b) = 1`, `ν(u) = 0` and `ν(M_11) = 0`.

*Proof.* For `w ∈ N`, Lemma 3(iii) and Proposition 7 give
`Σ_x e_b(w|_x) = e_b(w) + e_u(w) ≡ e_b(w) (mod 55)`. So Lemma 4 applies. Let `w` be a
relation on a stable cycle, with `W` as in Lemma 5.

- The root of `W|_2 = W(α, b)` is `α^{e_b(W)}`. It is trivial, so `11 | e_b(W)`.
- `W|_3` is `W` with `b ↦ β` and `u ↦ β^{-1}`. It equals `β^{e_b(W) − e_u(W)} = 1`. So
  `5 | e_b(W) − e_u(W)`.
- By Proposition 7, `55 | e_u(W)`. Hence `5 | e_b(W)`.
- So `55 | e_b(W) = e_b(w)`. ∎

**Theorem (item 3).** `(ν, λ) : R'' → (Z/55)^2` is onto, and `H_1(R''; Z)` is a quotient of
`(Z/55)^2` by §2. So `H_1(R''; Z) ≅ (Z/55)^2`, with basis `[b]`, `[u]`. Also `[c] = [b] + [u]`.
∎

## 5. The obstructions (item 4) and the host (item 5)

- **Acyclicity bounds.** Let `k` be a field with `char k ∤ 7920`. Then `H_1(R''; k) = 0`.
  So items 3(a) with `a = 1`, 3(c) in degree 1, and 3(d) of
  `fully-self-replicating-fp-infinity-groups-are-acyclic` are all met. `M_11 ≤ Alt(11)` and
  `d = 11`.
- **Primes.** `R''^{ab}` is finite, of order `55²`, and `5` and `11` divide `|M_11|`. This
  is the `FP_{d−1}` condition of item 3(d).
- **Odometer obstruction.** It requires a state `a` with root moving `x_a` and section `a` at
  `x_a`. The states of `R''` with nontrivial root are finitary or of the form `bπ`. The
  section of `bπ` at its moved letter `1` is `b`, not `bπ`. So the obstruction does not
  apply.
- **Contraction.** `R''` is not contracting by §1.
- **Blind invariants.** Characters, Σ-invariants, L² invariants and congruence quotients are
  blind for every `R ≅ R ≀_X P` with `P` perfect. This is the setting of the acyclicity node
  and of `fully-self-replicating-tree-groups-give-fg-tits-hosts`. For congruence quotients:
  `G_n = G_{n−1} ≀ M_11`, and `M_11` is perfect.
- **Host.** `tits-hosts-for-transitive-local-actions` applies to any `R` satisfying (S), (B)
  and (A) with root image `M_11 = Stab_{M_12}(12)`. It gives a finitely generated simple
  `Γ⁺`, which is finitely presented, or `F_∞`, if and only if `R''` is. ∎
