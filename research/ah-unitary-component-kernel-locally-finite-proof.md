Notation as in the target. F_k is the skeletal filtration of Pi(E) from
Steps 1-3 of `component-group-class-bound-by-excess-2026-09-12.md`:
F_k = ker(Pi(E) -> Pi(E|_{X^{(k-1)}})). That artifact proves
N(E) ⊆ F_{2r} (Step 3, from the stable-range isomorphism
Pi(F) ≅ K^1(Y) for dim Y < 2 rank F) and F_{d+1} = 1.

**(a).** Let S_k be the group, under pointwise product, of unitary sections
equal to 1 on X^{(k-1)}. By homotopy extension, S_k -> Pi(E) has image F_k.
Over each closed k-cell e the bundle is trivial, and a trivialization is
unique up to conjugation by U(r). Conjugation acts trivially on
pi_k(U(r), 1), because U(r) is connected. So s in S_k gives a well-defined
class h_e(s) in pi_k U(r), and h = (h_e): S_k -> C^k := prod_e pi_k U(r) is
a homomorphism: pointwise product of maps (D^k, ∂D^k) -> (U(r), 1) represents
the sum in pi_k. If h(s) = 0, each cell map is null rel boundary, so s is
homotopic over X^{(k)} rel X^{(k-1)} to 1, and s lies in F_{k+1}. Hence
F_k/F_{k+1} is a quotient of h(S_k) ⊆ C^k. For k >= 2r the group pi_k U(r)
is finite, since pi_*U(r) ⊗ Q is concentrated in degrees 1, 3, ..., 2r-1.
So F_{2r} ⊇ F_{2r+1} ⊇ ... ⊇ F_{d+1} = 1 has finite quotients, F_{2r} is
finite, and N(E) ⊆ F_{2r} is finite. The order bound follows from the
filtration. N(E) is nilpotent as a subgroup of Pi(E), which is nilpotent by
block-component-group-class-at-most-excess-plus-two. For a finite direct
sum of blocks, Pi and K_1 split as products, so the kernel is the product of
the N(E_j) and is again finite.

**(b).** Put G_i = Pi(A_i). The canonical map lim G_i -> U(A)/U_0(A) is onto:
given u in U(A), pick a in A_i with ||phi_{i,∞}(a) - u|| < 1/4. Since
phi_{i,∞} is isometric, a is invertible and its polar part v = a|a|^{-1}
satisfies ||phi_{i,∞}(v) - u|| < 2, so the two unitaries lie in one
component. It is injective by Toms, arXiv:2609.09535v1, Lemma 7.1 (imported
in toms-trapped-spin-bordism-class): a unitary whose image lies in U_0(A)
lies in U_0 at a finite stage. Likewise K_1(A) = lim K_1(A_i), and kappa is
the limit of the kappa_i. Direct limits of groups are exact, so
ker kappa = lim N(A_i). Any finitely generated subgroup of a direct limit is
the image of a finitely generated subgroup of some stage. Here that subgroup
lies in the finite nilpotent group N(A_i), so ker kappa is locally finite and
locally nilpotent. In a locally finite, locally nilpotent group, elements of
coprime prime-power orders commute (they generate a finite nilpotent group),
so the group is the direct sum of its Sylow p-subgroups. K_1(A) is abelian,
so the commutator subgroup lies in ker kappa.

**(c).** Let H be generated by x_1, ..., x_n. Lift the generators to some
G_i and let H_i be the subgroup they generate. Then H_i' ⊆ N(A_i) is finite,
and H' is its image, so H' is finite. Each x_j has conjugacy class inside the
finite set H' x_j, so its centralizer has finite index. Z(H) is the
intersection of the n centralizers of generators, so it has finite index. A
torsion-free group with finite commutator subgroup is abelian.

**(d).** By exactness choose j and x_j in N(A_j) representing x. For a block
E of rank r over X of dimension d, diag(x_j, 1_m) is a unitary of
Gamma(End(E ⊗ C^{1+m})), a bundle of rank r(1+m). Once 2r(1+m) > d, the
stable-range isomorphism gives Pi(E ⊗ C^{1+m}) ≅ K^1(X), and the K_1 class
of diag(x_j, 1_m) is kappa(x_j) = 0. Take m large for all blocks of A_j. □
