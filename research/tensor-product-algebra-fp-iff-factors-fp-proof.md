All algebras are unital associative `k`-algebras, and all tensor products are over `k`. `k⟨x⟩` is the free
algebra on a finite set `x`.

**Lemma 1 (independence of generators).** If `R` is finitely presented, then for every finite generating set
`y` the kernel `K_y` of `k⟨y⟩ -> R` is a finitely generated ideal.

*Proof.* Let `k⟨x⟩ -> R` have kernel `K_x` generated by finitely many `ρ_1, …, ρ_t`. Choose homomorphisms
`α : k⟨x⟩ -> k⟨y⟩` and `β : k⟨y⟩ -> k⟨x⟩` over `R`, by lifting each generator to a word. Let `I` be the ideal
generated by the `α(ρ_i)` and by `y_j - αβ(y_j)`, all of which lie in `K_y`.
- The endomorphism `αβ` agrees with the identity modulo `I` on generators, hence on all of `k⟨y⟩`.
- For `z ∈ K_y`, `β(z) ∈ K_x`, so `αβ(z)` lies in the ideal generated by `α(K_x)`. That ideal is generated by
  the `α(ρ_i)`, because `α(Σ a ρ_i b) = Σ α(a) α(ρ_i) α(b)`.
- So `z ≡ αβ(z) ≡ 0` modulo `I`, and `K_y = I`. ∎

**Lemma 2 (presentations of tensor products).** Let `A = k⟨a⟩ / I` and `C = k⟨c⟩ / J`. In `k⟨a, c⟩`, the ideal
`⟨I, J, [a_i, c_j]⟩` is the kernel of `k⟨a, c⟩ -> A ⊗ C`, `a_i |-> a_i ⊗ 1`, `c_j |-> 1 ⊗ c_j`.

*Proof.* Put `T = k⟨a, c⟩ / ⟨I, J, [a_i, c_j]⟩`. The map `T -> A ⊗ C` exists. Conversely, the images of
`k⟨a⟩` and `k⟨c⟩` in `T` commute, because their generators do, and they factor through `A` and `C`. So
`x ⊗ z |-> xz` is a well-defined bilinear and multiplicative map `A ⊗ C -> T`. The two maps are mutually inverse
on generators, hence inverse. ∎

**Proof of the claim.**

*If.* Take finite presentations `k⟨a⟩ / ⟨ρ⟩` and `k⟨c⟩ / ⟨σ⟩`. Lemma 2 presents `A ⊗ C` with the finitely
many relators `ρ, σ, [a_i, c_j]`.

*Only if.* Fix finite generating sets `a` of `A` and `c` of `C`, with kernels `I ⊆ k⟨a⟩` and `J ⊆ k⟨c⟩`.
Enumerate generators of `I` and `J` as ideals, and let `I_r` and `J_s` be the ideals generated by the first `r`
and `s` of them. Put `A_r = k⟨a⟩ / I_r` and `C_s = k⟨c⟩ / J_s`.
1. The elements `a ⊗ 1` and `1 ⊗ c` generate `A ⊗ C`. By Lemma 1 its kernel `K ⊆ k⟨a, c⟩` is a finitely
   generated ideal.
2. By Lemma 2, `K = ⟨I, J, [a, c]⟩ = ⋃_(r,s) K_(r,s)` with `K_(r,s) = ⟨I_r, J_s, [a, c]⟩`, an ascending union.
   An element of the ideal generated by a set uses finitely many of its elements.
3. Each of the finitely many generators of `K` lies in some `K_(r,s)`. Taking maxima, `K = K_(r,s)` for some
   `r, s`.
4. By Lemma 2 again, `k⟨a, c⟩ / K_(r,s) ≅ A_r ⊗ C_s`. So the canonical surjection
   `π_A ⊗ π_C : A_r ⊗ C_s -> A ⊗ C` is an isomorphism.
5. It factors as `(id ⊗ π_C)(π_A ⊗ id)`, so `π_A ⊗ id : A_r ⊗ C_s -> A ⊗ C_s` is injective.
6. Over a field, `ker(π_A ⊗ id) = (I / I_r) ⊗ C_s`. Now `C_s` surjects onto `C ≠ 0`, so `C_s ≠ 0`. Hence
   `I / I_r = 0`, and `A = A_r` is finitely presented.
7. By symmetry, using `A ≠ 0`, `C = C_s` is finitely presented. ∎

**Consequence 1 (product hosts).**
- `LC(Ω × Y, k) ≅ LC(Ω, k) ⊗ LC(Y, k)`. A locally constant function on a compact product is a finite sum of
  products of indicators of clopen rectangles, and the multiplication map is injective because products of
  linearly independent indicators of atoms of partitions are independent.
- The crossed product by `P × Q`, with basis `v_(p,q) = v_p ⊗ v_q`, satisfies the covariance relations of the
  tensor product. So `LC(Ω × Y, k) ⋊ (P × Q) ≅ (LC(Ω, k) ⋊ P) ⊗ (LC(Y, k) ⋊ Q)`: both are free over
  `LC(Ω × Y, k)` on `{v_(p,q)}` with the same relations.
- Both factors are nonzero when `Ω` and `Y` are nonempty. Finite generation passes to the factors.
  - Suppose `A ⊗ C` is generated by finitely many `z_i = Σ_l a_(il) ⊗ c_(il)`, and let `A_0 ⊆ A` be the
    subalgebra generated by the `a_(il)`.
  - Then `A ⊗ C` lies in the subalgebra generated by `A_0 ⊗ C`, which is `A_0 ⊗ C`, so `A_0 ⊗ C = A ⊗ C`.
  - Over a field `(A / A_0) ⊗ C = 0`, and `C ≠ 0`, so `A = A_0`.
- So the claim applies with no separate finite generation hypothesis on the factors.

**Consequence 2 (Leavitt).** `L_k(1, n)` is finitely presented and nonzero.
