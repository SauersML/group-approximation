Fix a prime `p`.  There exist:

- a finite-rank free group `F`;
- a cyclic recursively presented, but not finitely presented,
  `F_p[F]`-module `A`;

such that `A` is not residually finite as an `F`-module and

```text
G_A = A rtimes F                                             (NRM1)
```

is not residually finite, but `G_A` is sofic (hence hyperlinear) and
`L(G_A)` is Connes embeddable.

Thus the surviving infinite-type branch of the algebraic-envelope route is
genuine, but the condition “dual module is non-residually-finite” is only a
necessary screen.  It does not by itself create a non-CE crossed product.

## Construction

Choose a finitely generated, recursively presented, infinite simple amenable
group `Q`.  Concrete choices are available from topological full groups: for
an effective minimal subshift, the commutator subgroup is finitely generated,
infinite and simple (Matui), amenable (Juschenko--Monod), and has decidable
word problem when the subshift language is recursive
(Grigorchuk--Medynets, arXiv:1508.04454), hence is recursively presented.

Choose an epimorphism

```text
pi:F -> Q                                                   (NRM2)
```

from a finite-rank free group.  Let

```text
A = F_p[Q]                                                  (NRM3)
```

with its left regular `Q`-action, pulled back along `pi` to an `F_p[F]`-module.
It is cyclic, generated by the basis vector `delta_1`.

If `N=ker(pi)`, a recursive presentation is obtained by enumerating the
relations

```text
(n-1) delta_1 = 0,             n in N.                     (NRM4)
```

Indeed the kernel of `F_p[F] -> F_p[Q]` as a left-module map is the left ideal
generated by all `n-1`, `n in N`.  Since `Q` is recursively presented, `N` is
recursively enumerable.  The module cannot be finitely presented by
`finite-type-linear-vfree-envelope-is-residually-finite`, because it is not
residually finite, as proved next.

## The module and semidirect product are not residually finite

Choose `1 != q in Q` and put

```text
a = delta_q-delta_1 in A.                                  (NRM5)
```

This is nonzero.  Let `A -> V` be any finite `F_p[F]`-module quotient.  The
`F`-action on `V` factors through `Q`, so it gives a homomorphism

```text
Q -> GL(V).
```

The target is finite.  Since `Q` is infinite and simple, the homomorphism is
trivial: a nontrivial homomorphism would be injective.  Hence `q` acts
trivially on `V`, and the image of `(NRM5)` is zero.  Thus `a` dies in every
finite module quotient and `A` is not residually finite.

The same element `(a,1)` dies in every finite group quotient of `A rtimes F`.
Indeed the intersection of the kernel of such a quotient with the normal
elementary-abelian subgroup `A` is an `F`-invariant finite-index subgroup, so
the image of `A` is a finite `F`-module quotient.  Therefore `(NRM1)` is not
residually finite.

## Nevertheless the semidirect product is sofic

The map `(a,f) -> pi(f)` gives an exact sequence

```text
1 -> A x N -> A rtimes F -> Q -> 1.                         (NRM6)
```

Here `N=ker(pi)` acts trivially on `A`, so the kernel really is the direct
product `A x N`.  The group `A` is amenable, and `N` is a subgroup of a free
group, hence free and sofic.  Therefore `A x N` is sofic.  The quotient `Q` is
amenable.  Closure of sofic groups under extensions with sofic kernel and
amenable quotient applied to `(NRM6)` proves that `A rtimes F` is sofic.

## Consequence for the envelope search

The finite-type theorem forces the Manzoor-native linear route into
infinitely related modules, but this example shows that merely arranging an
element invisible in every finite invariant quotient is not enough.  The
remaining module must also make the associated coset/action extension
non-sofic at the action level, or directly force its group factor to be
non-CE.  Modules obtained by pulling back a regular module from an amenable
quotient are a broad barren family even though their finite invariant
quotients fail to separate points maximally.
