The group-theoretic corollary in item 3 has not been checked against the literature. Its case `n = 1`
is Bieri–Strebel (recalled).

## Setting

- `Λ` is finitely presented. `A = F_p^m`, and `X ⊆ A^Λ` is a *group subshift*: a closed, invariant
  subgroup.
- `M = X^` is its Pontryagin dual, a finitely generated `F_p[Λ]`-module, and `L = M ⋊ Λ` is its
  *lamp group*.
- `k` is a field of characteristic `≠ p` containing a primitive `p`-th root of unity, for example `C`.
- `X_D` is the group SFT cut out by the relations of `M` that are supported in a `D`-ball; it has
  lamp group `L_D`. Since `X` and `X_D` have the same `B_D`-patterns, they have the same `D`-quantum
  families.
- `Q_D` is the group generated by `U_χ(q)`, for `q ∈ Λ` and `χ ∈ A^*`, subject to:
  - (i) `U_χ(q) U_(χ')(q) = U_(χχ')(q)`;
  - (ii) `[U(q), U(q')] = 1` whenever `d(q, q') <= 2D`;
  - (iii) `∏_f U_(χ_f)(pf) = 1` for every relation `(χ_f)` of `M` supported in a ball `pB_D`.

## Theorem

1. `X` is `D`-quantum rigid over `k` iff `Q_D` is abelian, that is, iff `Q_D ⋊ Λ = L_D`: the lamp group
   of `X_D` is defined by its radius-`D` relations.
2. A group SFT is quantum rigid (for some `D`) iff its lamp group is finitely presented.
3. **Free groups.** Over `F_n`, `n >= 2`, no infinite group subshift is quantum rigid. Hence
   `M ⋊ F_n` is not finitely presented for any infinite finitely generated `F_p[F_n]`-module `M`.
4. **Products with a free group.** Over `Λ_0 × F_n`, `n >= 2`, a quantum-rigid group subshift is a
   *uniformly tight spacetime*. For some `R`, restriction to the slab `Λ_0 × B_R` is injective, and
   `F_n` acts on the slab shift by automorphisms commuting with `Λ_0`.
5. **Coset shifts.** Let `Δ ≤ Λ` be finitely generated and `|A| >= 2`. The full coset shift of
   right-`Δ`-invariant configurations is quantum rigid iff `Δ\Λ/Δ` is finite.

## Proof

1. **From families to `Q_D`.** Given a `D`-family `E`, put `U_χ(q) = Σ_a χ(a) E_a(q)`.
   - (i) and (ii) follow from (Q1) and (Q2).
   - For (iii), the operators on a ball commute. Expand the product over patterns `α`. Illegal `α`
     contribute 0 by (Q3). A relation `(χ_f)` takes the value 1 on every legal `α`, so the product is
     `Σ_α ∏ E = I`.
   - So every `D`-family is a representation of `Q_D`, and `E_a(q)` is a polynomial in the `U_χ(q)`.
     If `Q_D` is abelian, all families commute.

   **From `Q_D` to families.** Conversely, in `k[Q_D]` put
   `E_a(q) = |A|^(-1) Σ_χ χ(a)^(-1) U_χ(q)`.
   - (Q1) and (Q2) hold.
   - For (Q3): on a ball `B`, the `U`'s generate a quotient of the dual of `X|B`, since
     `(X|B)^⊥` is exactly the relations of `M` supported in `B`. The idempotent of an illegal pattern
     is therefore 0.
   - This family commutes only if `Q_D` is abelian.

   **Abelian means `M_D`.** If `Q_D` is abelian, it is the module `M_D = X_D^` presented by the
   radius-`D` relations.
2. **(⇐)** Let `L` be finitely presented, on the generators of `Λ` and the `U_χ(1)`.
   - Each of its finitely many relators is a consequence of the relations of `Q_D ⋊ Λ` for large `D`,
     which include commutation at every distance up to `2D` and every relation of `M` inside `B_D`.
   - So `Q_D ⋊ Λ ≅ L`, and `Q_D ≅ M` is abelian.

   **(⇒)** If `X = X_D` is `D`-rigid, then `L = Q_D ⋊ Λ`. This group has finitely many relators up to
   conjugation by `Λ`, and `Λ` is finitely presented.
3. **No rigid infinite group subshift over `F_n`.** An infinite compact group has no isolated point. So
   by item 4(a) of `rigid-subshifts-over-free-factors-are-tight-almost-everywhere`, the fixed point
   `0` is tight at no scale, and `ξ(0)` is defined.
   - `ξ` is equivariant and `0` is `F_n`-fixed, so `ξ(0)` is an end fixed by all of `F_n`. There is
     none for `n >= 2`.
   - **The corollary.** A finitely generated module `M` is the dual of a group subshift of
     `(F_p^m)^(F_n)`. Apply item 2 (⇐).
4. The same argument over `Λ_0 × F_n`, with slab tightness, shows that `0` is tight at some scale `R`.
   So the only point vanishing on `Λ_0 × B_(R+w)` is `0`. Restriction to the slab is a continuous
   injective homomorphism, and the spacetime structure is item (d) of
   `quantum-rigid-sfts-over-products-have-a-single-free-branch`.
5. **(⇐)** is `cornulier-regime-coset-sfts-are-quantum-rigid`. **(⇒)** The full coset shift is the
   coinduction from `Δ` of the constant subshift, so Corollary 3 of
   `quantum-rigid-subshifts-cluster-the-modifications-of-each-point` kills it when `Δ\Λ/Δ` is
   infinite. ∎

Item 5 and Cornulier's criterion agree. That is a check on the whole chain: necessity theorem,
clustering, and the equivalence in item 1.

## What this says about the question (RA)

- **Minimality cannot be dropped.** The rigid full coset shifts of the Cornulier regime (for example
  Thompson's `V` on the dyadic points) carry Bernoulli measures. So they are rigid, topologically free,
  **non-amenable** actions, and they are not minimal.
- **Where the tight case of (RA) now sits.** Over `Λ_0 × F_n`, measure-preserving rigid examples are
  tight, and every algebraic one is uniformly tight. Its rigidity is exactly finite presentation of
  `N ⋊ (Λ_0 × F)`, where `N` is a finitely generated `F_p[Λ_0]`-module and `F` a free group of
  module automorphisms.
- **Open test.** Is `F_p[t^±]^2 ⋊ (⟨t⟩ × F)` finitely presented for some free `F ≤ SL_2(F_p[t^±])`?
  Group subshifts are never minimal (`0` is fixed), so a yes would not refute (RA) as stated.

## Lesson for general BH

For algebraic dynamics, quantum rigidity *is* finite presentation of the lamp group. The whole
rigidity theory of group subshifts is the classical theory of finitely presented
(abelian of exponent `p`)-by-`Λ` groups: Baumslag, Bieri–Strebel, Cornulier.

Our dynamical theorems then give new group theory. Over a free group, rigidity produces an
invariant end, so no infinite exponent-`p` module over `F_n` has a finitely presented semidirect
product. The dictionary also runs the other way: every finitely presented lamp group over a
non-amenable `Λ` is a rigid, measure-preserving, non-amenable action. So (RA) is a statement about
**minimal** dynamics only.

### Referee addendum (bh-ref-hl, 2026-09-19): the item 3 corollary is known (Dunwoody), not new

This withdraws my "looks new" recommendation above. The corollary follows in a few lines from Dunwoody's resolution, and it holds more
generally.
- **Claim.** Let `M` be an infinite locally finite group, e.g. of exponent `p`. Let `Q` be finitely generated and virtually free,
  e.g. `F_n` with `n ≥ 1`, acting on `M`. Then `M ⋊ Q` is not finitely presented.
- **Proof.**
  1. `G = M ⋊ Q` acts on a Bass–Serre tree `T` of `Q`. Its vertex stabilizers are (conjugates of) `M ⋊ K` with `K` finite, so they
     are locally finite.
  2. Suppose `G` is finitely presented. By Dunwoody's resolution there is a `G`-tree `S` with finitely generated vertex and edge
     stabilizers, and a morphism `S → T` (Dunwoody, Invent. Math. 81 (1985); stated as the "standard result" corollary of
     Prop. "Dunwoody's accessibility" in Guirardel–Levitt, *JSJ decompositions of groups*, Astérisque 395 (2017),
     arXiv:1602.05139, read at source).
  3. Stabilizers in `S` lie in stabilizers of their images, and those images are locally finite. Being finitely generated, the
     stabilizers are finite.
  4. Pass to the minimal subtree, which is cocompact since `G` is finitely generated. Then `G` is the fundamental group of a finite
     graph of finite groups, hence virtually free (Karrass–Pietrowski–Solitar).
  5. The torsion subgroup `M` is therefore finite. `∎`
- **Consequences for this node.**
  - The group-theoretic half of item 3 recovers a known fact, and the Bieri–Strebel case `n = 1` in the calibration table is the
    same argument.
  - The dynamical half ("no infinite group subshift over `F_n` is rigid") also follows from item 2 (⇒) plus Dunwoody. So the
    dynamical proof and the classical one cross-check each other.
  - The node's new content is the dictionary in items 1–2 and its dynamical proof of item 3, not the group-theoretic corollary.
  - Credit Dunwoody for the corollary.
