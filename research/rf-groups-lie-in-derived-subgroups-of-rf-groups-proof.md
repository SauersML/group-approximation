Artifact §2, Lemma 2.

1. **Residually finite `Γ`.**
   - Choose normal subgroups `Γ_n` of finite index with `∩ Γ_n = {e}`, and put `Ω_n = Γ/Γ_n × {1,2}`.
   - Left multiplication `λ_n(γ)(gΓ_n, i) = (γgΓ_n, i)` has its cycles in pairs of equal length, so it is even.
   - `ρ = (λ_n)_n : Γ -> P = ∏_n Sym(Ω_n)` is an injective homomorphism.
   - By `ore-even-permutations-are-commutators`, choose for each `s ∈ S`, coordinate by coordinate, `a_s, b_s ∈ P`
     with `ρ(s) = [a_s, b_s]`.
   - Put `Δ = ⟨ρ(S), a_s, b_s : s ∈ S⟩ ≤ P`. It is finitely generated, and residually finite because the coordinate
     projections onto the finite groups `Sym(Ω_n)` separate the points of `P`.
   - Since `ρ(S) ⊆ [Δ,Δ]`, `ρ(Γ) ≤ [Δ,Δ]`. If `Γ` is infinite, then `Δ ⊇ ρ(Γ)` is infinite.
2. **LEF `Γ`.**
   - A finitely generated LEF group embeds in an algebraic ultraproduct `∏_ω F_n` of finite groups: send `γ` to the
     class of its images under injective partial models on growing balls.
   - Composing with the doubled regular representations `F_n -> Alt(F_n × {1,2})` gives `ρ : Γ -> ∏_ω Sym(Ω_n)`.
     Choose `a_s, b_s` coordinatewise as in 1 and let `Δ` be generated by `ρ(S)` and the `a_s, b_s`.
   - Finitely many products and inequalities among elements of `Δ` hold in `ω`-almost every coordinate. So some
     coordinate gives an injective partial homomorphism into a finite group, and `Δ` is LEF. ∎
