Use throughout

```text
Q^2=Q,       QS=0,       TQ=0,       Q+ST=1,       TS=1.  (JFDP1)
```

The roots `x_13(A_13)`, `x_23(A_23)` and `x_43(A_43)` all have target
column three.  Roots in a common target column commute, and root addition
identifies each coefficient span with an elementary abelian group.  Their
product therefore has rank at most `3+2+2=7`.

The two opposite-coordinate generators act on the three source-cut roots by

```text
u x_13(a) u=x_23(Ta)x_13(a),
l x_13(a) l=x_43(Ta)x_13(a).                              (JFDP2)
```

For the three coefficients in `A_13`,

```text
TQ=0,             T(SQ)=Q,             T(S^2T)=ST.        (JFDP3)
```

Thus `u,l` send `x_13(A_13)` into the root product in `(JFD2)`.  They
commute respectively with `x_23(A_23)` and `x_43(A_43)`, and with the other
new root group by disjointness.

Conversely the Q-labelled generators give

```text
X x_23(b) X=x_13(Qb)x_23(b),
C x_43(b) C=x_13(Qb)x_43(b).                              (JFDP4)
```

Here

```text
QQ=Q,                    Q(ST)=0,                         (JFDP5)
```

so `(JFDP4)` introduces at most the already listed root `w=x_13(Q)`.
The generator `X` commutes with the `(1,3)` and `(4,3)` root groups, while
`C` commutes with the `(1,3)` and `(2,3)` groups.  This audits every
root-generator conjugation.

Direct multiplication of the two rank-one transvections gives

```text
[X,u]=I+QT E_11=c_T=[C,l].                                (JFDP6)
```

Indeed the reverse products vanish because `TQ=0`, as do all higher terms.
Also `(QT)^2=Q(TQ)T=0`, so `c_T^2=1`.  Conjugation by `c_T` fixes all roots
in positions `(2,3)` and `(4,3)`, while on the first root space it sends

```text
x_13(a) |-> x_13((1+QT)a).                                (JFDP7)
```

The only nontrivial shear on the chosen coefficients is

```text
QT(SQ)=Q,
QTQ=0,
QT(S^2T)=QTS^2T=QST=0.                                   (JFDP8)
```

Hence `c_T` sends `v` to `wv` and fixes `w,d`, proving that it normalizes
the rank-at-most-seven root group.  Equations `(JFDP6)--(JFDP8)` prove
`(JFD3)`.

The four target generators also fix `c_T` itself:

```text
X c_T X=u c_T u=C c_T C=l c_T l=c_T.                    (JFDP8a)
```

This follows directly from `TQ=0`, `QTQ=0` and `TQT=0`; equivalently,
`c_T` is the involutory commutator of each opposite pair and the two pairs
cross-commute.

It remains to audit the cross commutators among `X,u,C,l`.  The same-source
and same-target pairs commute:

```text
[X,C]=1,                  [u,l]=1.                        (JFDP9)
```

The other cross pairs would have coefficients `TQ`, and therefore also
commute:

```text
[X,l]=1,                  [u,C]=1.                       (JFDP10)
```

Each of `X,u,C,l` is an involution.  Equations
`(JFDP2)--(JFDP10)` show both that `N` is normal in `P` and that the quotient
is generated by four commuting involutions, proving `(JFD4)`.

Finally `Q` is the nonzero head idempotent in the faithful unilateral-shift
model of `J`, so `w=x_13(Q)` is nonidentity in the literal subgroup `P`.
The finite group `P` itself, or its left regular representation, is the
claimed exact marked finite model.
