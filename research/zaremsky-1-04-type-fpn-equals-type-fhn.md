M. C. B. Zaremsky, *Some open problems* (version of July 12, 2026,
https://zaremsky.github.io/open_problems.pdf), Section 1, Problem 4, verbatim:
"Are type FP_n and type FH_n equivalent? That is, does every group of type
FP_n admit a proper, cocompact action on an (n − 1)-acyclic complex?"

This claim is the question. It is established only through an answer route:

- **Yes**: `zaremsky-1-04-by-yes` requires `every-type-fpn-group-is-of-type-fhn`.
- **No**: `zaremsky-1-04-by-no` requires `some-type-fpn-group-is-not-of-type-fhn`.

Never write a `requires: []` route into this claim.

## Reading

- Coefficients are `ℤ`. A group `G` is of type `FP_n` if the trivial
  `ℤG`-module `ℤ` has a projective resolution that is finitely generated in
  degrees `≤ n`; equivalently a free resolution with finitely generated free
  modules in degrees `≤ n`.
- A CW complex `X` is `(n−1)`-acyclic if it is nonempty and
  `H̃_i(X; ℤ) = 0` for `0 ≤ i ≤ n−1`.
- Printed reading of `FH_n`: `G` acts cellularly on an `(n−1)`-acyclic CW
  complex with finite cell stabilizers and finitely many orbits of cells.
  Bestvina–Brady and Leary use free actions instead. The two readings define
  the same class (proof in progress in this region), so either may be used.
- Cocompactness may be weakened to "finitely many orbits of cells of
  dimension `≤ n`": the `n`-skeleton of such a complex is still
  `(n−1)`-acyclic and has finitely many orbits of cells.
- `FH_n ⇒ FP_n` is elementary (cellular chains give a partial resolution by
  finitely generated permutation modules with finite stabilizers, and those
  are of type `FP_∞`). So the problem is the implication `FP_n ⇒ FH_n`.
- A Yes answer needs every `n`. A No answer needs one `n` and one group.
  For `n ≤ 2` the implication holds: attach finitely many orbits of 2-cells
  to a Cayley graph along loops generating the relation module. So a No
  answer lives at some `n ≥ 3`.

## Attempts

- 2026-09-13 (lane z1-04-fp-fh). Reduction at `n = 3`, proof being written
  up: `G` is of type `FH_3` iff `G` is of type `FP_3` and `G ≅ Q/P` with `Q`
  finitely presented and `P` superperfect (`H_1(P) = H_2(P) = 0`). Forward:
  the orbit space of a free cocompact 2-acyclic 3-complex `Y` has finitely
  presented `π_1 = Q`, and `P = π_1(Y)` has `H_1(P) = H_1(Y) = 0` and
  `H_2(P) = 0` because Hopf's theorem makes `H_2(Y) → H_2(P)` onto. Backward:
  in the `G`-cover `X` of a presentation complex of `Q`, `H_2(X)` is finitely
  generated by `FP_3` and is entirely spherical because
  `H_2(X)/h(π_2 X) ≅ H_2(P) = 0`; attach finitely many orbits of 3-cells.
- Direct attack on `FP_3 ⇒ FH_3`: start from any finitely presented `Q ↠ G`
  with perfect kernel `P` (this exists by `FP_2`). The obstruction to
  attaching 3-cells is exactly `H_2(P)`, a finitely generated `ℤG`-module.
  Killing a perfect, finitely normally generated `N ◁ Q` inside `P` removes
  `im(H_2(N) → H_2(P))`. Amalgamating a finitely presented superperfect group
  along a finitely generated `A ≤ P` removes `im H_2(A)` but adds
  `⊕_{g ∈ G} H_1(A)` to `H_2`. Gluing acyclic groups along surface subgroups
  representing classes adds `⊕_{g ∈ G} H_1(Σ)`. No construction closes the
  regress yet.
- Known `FH` examples: Bestvina–Brady kernels over finite acyclic flag
  complexes act freely and cocompactly on acyclic level sets. Leary's groups
  `G_L(S)` (arXiv:1512.06609) act freely and cocompactly on level sets; the
  exact hypotheses under which those are acyclic are being checked from the
  source.
