Source: Benjamin Bachner, *Linear sofic representations of amenable
algebras*, arXiv:2602.05039v3, Theorem 1.2, equivalently Theorem 6.4(2).

The theorem states exactly: if `Gamma` is torsion-free amenable and
`K[Gamma]` has no zero divisors, then `K[Gamma]` is weakly stable if and
only if `Gamma` is residually finite.

The forward direction uses weak stability to replace a maximally separating
linear-sofic representation by genuine finite-dimensional representations,
which make `Gamma` residually linear; Malcev's theorem then gives residual
finiteness. For the reverse direction, residual finiteness supplies genuine
matrix representations forming a linear-sofic representation; the
unique-conjugacy theorem for the amenable domain shows that every
linear-sofic representation is rank-conjugate to such a genuine sequence.

The torsion-free and domain hypotheses are retained exactly as printed; no
Kaplansky zero-divisor conjecture is silently invoked.
