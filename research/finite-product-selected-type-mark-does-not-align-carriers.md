Let

```text
Gamma=C_2*C_2=<s,t | s^2=t^2=1>
```

and put

```text
q_s=(1+s)/2,                 q_t=(1+t)/2.              (FPM1)
```

These are the trivial-type projections of the two finite packet subgroups.
In the canonical group trace,

```text
||q_t q_s||_2^2
 =tau(q_s q_t q_s)
 =tau(q_s q_t)
 =1/4.                                                   (FPM2)
```

Nevertheless

```text
q_s meet q_t=0                                            (FPM3)
```

in the left-regular von Neumann algebra.  Thus the product mark has fixed
positive canonical norm while there is no nonzero projection lying under
both selected-type carriers.

The same failure occurs along canonical finite-matrix models.  Let `Gamma`
act by its regular representation through the finite dihedral quotient

```text
D_(2n)=<s,t | s^2=t^2=(st)^n=1>.
```

As `n->infinity` these representations form canonical-delta microstates for
`Gamma` on every fixed word window.  Equation `(FPM2)` remains exact for
`n>1`, whereas

```text
tau_(D_(2n))(q_s meet q_t)=1/(2n)->0.                    (FPM4)
```

Consequently neither canonical trace conditioning nor finite-dimensional
Plancherel exactification promotes survival of a finite product

```text
q_m u_(m-1) q_(m-1) ... u_1 q_1                        (FPM5)
```

to a positive-density common selected carrier.  Cross-Gram polar factors
obtained from `(FPM5)` provide only sequential partial correspondences; they
need not have a nonzero joint reducing subspace.

## Proof

Expanding `q_s q_t` gives

```text
q_s q_t=(1+s+t+st)/4.
```

The four displayed group elements are distinct in `C_2*C_2`, so canonical
trace leaves only the identity coefficient and proves `(FPM2)`.

The range of `q_s` consists of the vectors fixed by the left action of `s`,
and similarly for `q_t`.  Their intersection is therefore the invariant
subspace for the group generated by `s,t`, namely `Gamma`.  The left regular
representation of an infinite group has no nonzero invariant vector, proving
`(FPM3)`.

In the regular representation of `D_(2n)`, the common invariant subspace is
the one-dimensional constant subspace.  Normalized trace gives `(FPM4)`.
For every fixed radius the quotient map is injective on the corresponding
ball once `n` is large, so the normalized regular characters converge to the
canonical character of `Gamma` and all fixed relators hold exactly.

## Consequence for a proof-carrying Fanizza mark

A mark such as

```text
p=q_(c_m)t_(m-1)q_(c_(m-1))...t_1q_(c_1)D_m           (FPM6)
```

may vanish when two local packet carriers are orthogonal, but its survival
does not certify the common carrier needed to interpret every Fanizza
context in one state.  To make `(FPM6)` sound one needs an additional
full-overlap, common-reducing-corner, or source-specific mixed-moment theorem.
The product itself is not that theorem.

## Claim boundary

This does not rule out a mark containing a genuine algebraic corner partial
isometry, nor a matrix-only actuator which upgrades sequential overlap to a
common reducing carrier.  It rules out the qualitative inference
`nonzero product mark => aligned selected types`, even under canonical
finite-packet microstates.
