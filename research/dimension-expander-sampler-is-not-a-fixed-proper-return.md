Fix `n` and any finite two-matching/dimension-expander Pauli sampler on
`V_n`.  Its intended Weyl strategy is an exact finite-dimensional model.
Tensor it with the active D8 spin packet and with an arbitrary spectator
space.  The D8 projection `H` has fixed positive conditional density, while
every sampler relation has zero defect in every spectator amplification.

Consequently the fixed sampler cannot imply either proper-return estimate
`(TCT2)` or `(TCT3)`: at zero defect, cyclicity would force a nonzero
subprojection of `H` to have zero trace, contradicting the exact Weyl--D8
model.  Equivalently, finite dimension-expander maps are invertible square
transports.  Their exact packet algebra is stably finite and contains no
one-sided rank loss.

The sampler becomes relevant only as an **unbounded family**.  If one finite
presentation realizes the samplers for all `n` with one dimension-independent
local HS modulus, robust soundness can force a block of dimension
`2^(c n)` for every `n`; choosing `n` after seeing the matrix dimension then
contradicts finiteness.  That is the occurrence-game/capacity route.  It
does not manufacture a fixed trace-cyclic proper corner.

Thus there are exactly two ways a two-matching sampler could help the native
Fanizza lane:

1. the prescribed machine words themselves provide a uniform all-`n`
   occurrence compiler on their multiplicity coordinates, in which case the
   sampler bypasses the trace-cyclic actuator and proves mark collapse by
   capacity; or
2. an additional genuinely one-sided matrix-coordinate relation converts
   the sampler's square transports into `(TCT2)--(TCT3)`, in which case that
   additional relation—not dimension expansion—is the missing actuator.

Repeating or expanding the fixed separator triangle is not option 1, by
`flat-fanizza-triangle-realizes-every-separator-distribution`.  The current
open dimension-expander square-function and occurrence-labeling theorems are
therefore a separate global route, not an available realization of the two
native proper returns.
