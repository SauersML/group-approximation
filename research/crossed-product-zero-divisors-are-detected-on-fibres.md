Let `B = C(M_-, F_3) x| V` (`signed-thompson-algebra-is-odd-measure-crossed-product`). For `mu in M_-`,
the fibre representation `rho_mu` on `F_3[V]` is `rho_mu([g]) delta_k = delta_(gk)` and
`rho_mu(f) delta_k = f(k mu) delta_k`. For `x = sum_g [g] b_g` the fibre operator is

    x_mu delta_k = sum_g b_g(k mu) delta_(gk).

The following are equivalent:
1. `x` is a left zero divisor in `B`;
2. `x_mu` is not injective for some `mu in M_-`;
3. `x_mu` is not injective for some `mu` in any fixed dense subset, such as sums of an odd number of Dirac
   measures.

`x` is a right zero divisor iff some `(x*)_mu` is not injective, with `* : [g] -> [g^-1]`.

**Consequences for witnesses.** Left-invertible elements are never left zero divisors, and a zero divisor
stays one after multiplication by units on either side. So:
- every Cohn-family isometry `sigma_j` in `S_-`, and every corner witness `q_[0] b'`, that lies in `B` has
  injective fibre operators at every `mu`;
- the adjoints of the co-isometries `tau_i` and of `c' q_[0]`, when they lie in `B`, have injective fibre
  operators at every `mu`.

This holds whatever the partners are, including partners outside `B`. Lemma 2.3 of the artifact.
