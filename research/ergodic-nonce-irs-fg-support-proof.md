Let `Sub_fg(Gamma)` denote the finitely generated subgroups.  It is invariant
under conjugation.  It is countable, because every member is generated by a
finite tuple and the union of the finite powers of a countable set is
countable.  Ergodicity and `(EFG1)` therefore give

```text
mu(Sub_fg(Gamma))=1.
```

Write the countable set as `{H_1,H_2,...}`.  If every singleton had measure
zero, countable additivity would give

```text
1=mu(Sub_fg(Gamma))=sum_i mu({H_i})=0,
```

a contradiction.  Thus `mu` has an atom.  The finite-index normalizer and
regular-quotient construction in
`atomic-noncohyperlinear-irs-forces-nonhyperlinear` now yields a group `K`
with non-Connes-embeddable group factor `L(K)`, so `K` is non-hyperlinear.

For the profinite warning, if `N` is finite-index normal then
`H |-> HN/N` has finite range and is conjugation equivariant.  Its fibers
are precisely the finite invariant-partition setting already analyzed in
`finite-invariant-partition-only-reduces-irs-to-fiber-irs`.  Equality of all
such images determines the profinite closure of `H`, not necessarily `H`;
residual finiteness separates elements from the identity, while subgroup
separability for an infinitely generated subgroup is a strictly stronger
condition.  Hence no atomicity conclusion follows from those partitions
alone.
