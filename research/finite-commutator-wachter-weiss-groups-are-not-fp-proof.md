## Facts from the construction

- **(W1) How the TM mode changes letters** (l. 1313, before the definition of `T_2`).
  In `T'`, "the symbols `#, $` and `γ_i ∈ Γ` are not changed by any transition and
  `0` and `1` are either also not changed or they are toggled". Only the
  generalized check-mark part toggles digits (Proposition `prop:TMmode` and its
  proof). The checking states store information in the state and output their input.
  The states `checksub{g}` for the various `g` act identically before the first `$`
  (l. ~1000). `T_2` reads encodings over `W` and never outputs a letter outside `W`
  on `W`-input.
- **(W2) What happens outside `W`.**
  - Every word of `W^*` is a prefix of an encoded word (Fact
    `fct:encodingIsSurjective`).
  - A word leaving the encoded format is `u a v` with `a ∈ Σ̃`
    (Lemma `lem:malformedWords`).
  - On such a letter `a`, every TM-mode state and every delayed-`R` state outputs
    `a` and passes to `id`. This is the missing-transition convention of `T'` (an
    implicit `a/a` transition to `id`, proof of `prop:TMmode`), which the encodings
    `T_2` and `R_0` (l. 1768ff.) inherit. Fact `fct:malformedWords` (l. 1417)
    records the result for the checking sequences `p_i`. The copies `T_{2,r}`,
    `r ∈ R`, replace the place-holder `r` by the `R`-state `r` (l. 1766).
- **(W3) The commutator mode.** It consists of the states of `R`, and of copies of
  `R` delayed until the encoded `$`. Here `R` acts on the first letter only, by a
  permutation of `Σ̃` fixing `W`, with trivial sections.

## 1. The map onto Z and its kernel

- **The action on `W^*`.** By (W1)–(W3), every state maps `W`-letters to `W`-letters,
  so `W^*` is invariant, and restriction gives `ρ: G_R -> Aut(W^*)`.
  - A TM-mode state acts on `W^*` either trivially or as the encoded generalized
    check-mark adder `c`, which is the same map for all copies.
  - An `R`-state, or a delayed `R`-state, acts trivially on `W^*`, because `R` fixes
    `W`. After an encoded `$` the TM-mode states become `R`-states or `id`, which fix
    `W`-letters.
  - So `ρ(G_R) = <c>`.
- **`c` has infinite order.** On the single-configuration words `0^k γ` (encoded) the
  check-mark part acts as the binary adding machine on the `k`-digit block (the
  automaton of Figure `fig:checkmarkingAutomaton`). So `c` has orbits of size `2^k`
  for every `k`, and `<c> ≅ Z`.
- **The kernel is a power of `R`.** Let `K = ker ρ`, take `g ∈ K`, and consider a
  point not in `W^N`, written `u a v` with `u ∈ W^*`, `a ∈ Σ̃`. Then
  `g(u a v) = u · g|_u(a v)`. The section `g|_u` is a product of states. On the
  letter `a`, TM-mode states output `a` and pass to `id` (W2), and `R`-states
  permute `a` and pass to `id` (W3). So `g|_u(a v) = π_u(g)(a) v` for some
  `π_u(g) ∈ R`, and `g` fixes `W^N`. Thus `g ↦ (π_u(g))_{u ∈ W^*}` is an injective
  homomorphism `K -> R^{W^*}`.
- **Consequences.** The unrestricted power of a finite group lies in the variety
  generated by that group, which is locally finite. So `K` is locally finite, and
  `G_R` (locally finite by `Z`) is amenable.

## 2. The kernel is infinite

Let `z` be the encoded format-checking state of `T'`: it loops on `0/0`, `id_Γ` and
`#`, sends every `1` to `id`, and passes to `r` after `$`. In the copy where `r` is
an `R`-state `ρ != 1`:
- `z ∈ K`, and `π_w(z) = ρ` exactly for `w = u$` with `u` well formed and all digit
  blocks zero; `π_w(z) = 1` otherwise.
- For `h = c^n` one gets `π_w(h z h^-1) = π_{h^{-1} w}(z)`, because `c` passes to
  `id` on `Σ̃`-letters. So the support of `c^n z c^{-n}` is `c^n(supp z)`.
- On single-block words, `c^n(0^k γ $) = bin(n) γ $` (reverse binary), and
  `0^k γ $` is the only `k`-digit single-block word in `supp z`.
- So the conjugates `c^n z c^{-n}`, `0 <= n < 2^k`, are pairwise distinct. As `k` is
  arbitrary, `K` is infinite.

## 3. Not finitely presented

- **Setup.** Suppose `G` is finitely generated and finitely presented, with
  `χ: G ->> Z` and `ker χ` locally finite. By the Bieri--Strebel theorem (cited; for
  example Bieri--Strebel 1978, and Brown's account through Σ-invariants), `G` is an
  HNN extension `<B, t | t A t^-1 = A'>` with `B` finitely generated,
  `A, A' <= B` and `B <= ker χ`.
- **The base is finite.** `B` is finitely generated and locally finite, so it is
  finite, and `|A| = |A'|`.
- **Case `A = B`.** Then `A' = B`, and `G = B ⋊ <t>` is virtually `Z`.
- **Case `A != B`.** The Bass--Serre tree is locally finite with vertex degree
  `[B:A] + [B:A'] >= 4`. The action has finite stabilizers and is cocompact, so `G`
  is virtually free of rank at least 2 and contains `F_2`. That is impossible for
  the amenable group `G`.
- **Conclusion.** So such a `G` is virtually cyclic. Applied to `G_R`, with `K`
  locally finite (step 1) and infinite (step 2), this is a contradiction, so `G_R`
  is not finitely presented. `∎`
