# Proof

The notation is that of the target. `F = F(x,y)`, `π : F → G` is the quotient, `N = ker π`,
`F' = F(a,…,e)`, `R' = ⟨⟨u_0, u_1⟩⟩ ≤ F'`, and `φ_0 : F' → F` substitutes the basis words
`a = XyxYx`, `b = Xyyyx`, `c = XYxyx`, `d = y`, `e = xyX`.

**Inputs.**
- (I1) `F'/R' → G` induced by `φ_0` is injective with image `H`
  (`rank-five-degree-one-two-cell-configuration-is-a-core`). So `R'` is exactly the kernel of
  `π φ_0 : F' → H`.
- (I2) `φ_0(u_0) = w` and `φ_0(u_1) = g w g^{-1}` as reduced words in `F`, with `g = Xy`
  (`lift.py`, `basics()`).
- (I3) `e(u_0) = (−1,0,1,0,0)` and `e(u_1) = (−2,0,2,0,0)`, as exponent sums over `a,…,e`
  (`basics()`).
- (I4) Lyndon's identity theorem: `w` is not a proper power, so `N_ab` is a free `ZG`-module on
  `[w]`. Here `G` acts by conjugation, `[v n v^{-1}] = π(v)[n]`.
- (I5) Hopf's formula: for any presentation `K = F_X / R_X`, `H_2(K) ≅ (R_X ∩ [F_X,F_X])/[F_X,R_X]`.
  This is natural. A map `F_X → F_Y` over the identity of `K` induces the identity of `H_2(K)`
  under the two identifications.

Write `Φ : N_ab → Z[H\G]` for `λ[w] ↦ Hλ`. It is well defined by (I4), additive, and satisfies
`Φ(μλ[w]) = ε(μ) Φ(λ[w])` for `μ ∈ ZH`, where `ε` is the augmentation. The last point holds
because `Hh = H` for `h ∈ H`.

## Step 1. Change of lift (Fox chain rule)

**Lemma 1.** Let `X` be a set, and let `α, β : F(X) → F` be homomorphisms with
`α(z) = n_z β(z)`, where `n_z ∈ N`. Then for every `u ∈ F(X)`, `α(u) β(u)^{-1} ∈ N` and
`[α(u) β(u)^{-1}] = Σ_z (∂_z u)^{πβ} [n_z]`. Here `∂_z` is the Fox derivative and `^{πβ}` applies
`πβ = πα` to coefficients.

*Proof.* Induct on the length of `u`, using `∂_z(uv) = ∂_z u + u ∂_z v`. For `u = z` the claim is
`[n_z]`. For `u = z^{-1}`, we have `α(z^{-1}) β(z^{-1})^{-1} = β(z)^{-1} n_z^{-1} β(z)`, whose class
is `−π β(z)^{-1} [n_z] = (∂_z z^{-1})^{πβ} [n_z]`. For a product,
`α(uv) β(uv)^{-1} = α(u)β(u)^{-1} · β(u) (α(v)β(v)^{-1}) β(u)^{-1}`, and the class is additive
with `β(u)` acting by `πβ(u)`. ∎

**Corollary 2.** Let `⟨Z | r⟩` be a presentation of `H` with `e(r) = 0`, and let `f, f'` be two
lifts. Then `f(r), f'(r) ∈ N` and `Φ[f(r)] = Φ[f'(r)]`.

*Proof.* `πf = πf'` is the map `F(Z) → H ⊂ G` of the presentation, which kills `r`. So
`f(r), f'(r) ∈ N` and `n_z = f(z) f'(z)^{-1} ∈ N`. By Lemma 1,
`[f(r)] − [f'(r)] = Σ_z (∂_z r)^{π f'} [n_z]` (the classes add because `N_ab` is abelian and
`f(r) = (f(r) f'(r)^{-1}) f'(r)`). Each coefficient lies in `ZH` and has augmentation `e_z(r) = 0`,
so `Φ` kills it. ∎

## Step 2. The coinvariant map on the cells

**Lemma 3.** `Ψ : R' → Z[H\G]`, `v ↦ Φ[φ_0(v)]`, is a homomorphism that kills `[F', R']`. It has
`Ψ(u_0) = H` and `Ψ(u_1) = Hg`.

*Proof.* `φ_0(R') ⊂ N` by (I1), and `n ↦ [n]` is a homomorphism. For `s ∈ F'` and `v ∈ R'`,
`[φ_0(s v s^{-1} v^{-1})] = (π φ_0(s) − 1)[φ_0(v)]`, with `π φ_0(s) ∈ H`, so `Φ` gives 0. By (I2),
`[φ_0 u_0] = [w]` and `[φ_0 u_1] = [g w g^{-1}] = g[w]`. ∎

So `Ψ` factors through `R'/[F', R']`. That group is abelian (central in `F'/[F',R']`) and generated
by the images of `u_0, u_1`. So every `v ∈ R'` has `v ≡ u_0^{m_0} u_1^{m_1} mod [F',R']` for some
`m_0, m_1 ∈ Z`, and then `Ψ(v) = m_0 H + m_1 Hg`.

## Step 3. Homology of H (O1)

- `H_ab = Z^5 / ⟨e(u_0), e(u_1)⟩ = Z^5 / Z e(u_0) ≅ Z^4`, by (I1) and (I3), since `e(u_0)` is
  primitive.
- *`H_2(H)` is generated by `u_0^{-2} u_1`.* If `v ≡ u_0^{m_0} u_1^{m_1}` lies in `[F',F']`, then
  `0 = m_0 e(u_0) + m_1 e(u_1) = (m_0 + 2m_1) e(u_0)`, so `(m_0, m_1) ∈ Z(−2, 1)`. By (I5), `H_2(H)`
  is the cyclic group generated by the class of `u_0^{-2} u_1`.
- *`H_2(H) ≅ Z`.* The inclusion `H → G` induces a map on Hopf formulas, given by `φ_0` (which lies
  over it). It sends `u_0^{-2} u_1` to `w^{-2} g w g^{-1} ≡ w^{-1} mod [F, N]`. Now `w ∈ [F,F]` (both
  exponent sums of `w` are 0), and `H_2(G) = N/[F,N]` is infinite cyclic on `w`, by (I4): it is
  `Z ⊗_{ZG} N_ab = Z`. So the generator of `H_2(H)` has infinite order, and `H_2(H) ≅ Z`. Its image
  in `H_2(G)` is `−[w]`.
- *One-relator presentations.* Let `H ≅ ⟨Z | r⟩`, with `R = ⟨⟨r⟩⟩ ≤ F(Z)`. Then `R/[F(Z), R]` is
  cyclic, generated by `r`.
  - If `e(r) ≠ 0`, the map `R/[F(Z),R] → F(Z)_ab` sends the generator to `e(r) ≠ 0`, so it is
    injective. Then `H_2(H) = 0`, a contradiction.
  - So `e(r) = 0`, and `H_ab = Z^{|Z|}`, which gives `|Z| = 4`.
  - Also `H_2(H) = R/[F(Z),R]` is generated by `r`.
- *Examples.* `u_0 = dEbcBADDed` contains `c` and `a` once each. Solving `u_0 = 1` for one of them
  and substituting into `u_1` is a Tietze transformation. It gives the two relators of (O1),
  checked by `elim()` in `lift.py`.

## Step 4. The class of every lift (O2)

Let `H ≅ ⟨Z | r⟩` be a one-relator presentation, identified with `H` by `θ : F(Z) → H`, and let
`f` be a lift. Choose `σ : F(Z) → F'` with `π φ_0 σ = θ`, i.e. a word in `a,…,e` for each `θ(z)`.
Then `φ_0 σ` is also a lift, and `σ(R) ⊂ R'` by (I1).

1. By Step 3, `e(r) = 0`, so Corollary 2 gives `Φ[f(r)] = Φ[φ_0 σ(r)] = Ψ(σ(r))`.
2. `σ` lies over the identity of `H`. By (I5), it carries the generator `r` of
   `H_2(H) = R/[F(Z),R]` to a generator of `(R' ∩ [F',F'])/[F',R']`, which is infinite cyclic on
   `u_0^{-2} u_1` by Step 3. Hence `σ(r) ≡ (u_0^{-2} u_1)^{ε} mod [F', R']`, with `ε = ±1`.
3. By Lemma 3, `Ψ(σ(r)) = ε(Hg − 2H)`.

So `Φ[f(r)] = ±(Hg − 2H)` for every one-relator presentation and every lift. ∎

*Explicit form for eliminations.* Let `z_0` occur once in `u_0`, and let `ψ : F' → F(Z)`,
`Z = {a,…,e}∖z_0`, substitute the solution `γ` of `u_0 = 1` for `z_0`. Then `r = ψ(u_1)`, and
`f_0 = φ_0|F(Z)` is a lift.
- Put `m = φ_0(γ) φ_0(z_0)^{-1} ∈ N`. Lemma 1, applied to `α = φ_0ψ` and `β = φ_0`, gives two
  identities:
  - `0 = [φ_0 ψ(u_0)] = [w] + (∂_{z_0}u_0)[m]`;
  - `[f_0(r)] = g[w] + (∂_{z_0}u_1)[m]`.
- `∂_{z_0} u_0 = ±h_0` is a unit of `ZH`, since `z_0` occurs once. Eliminating `[m]` gives
  `[f_0(r)] = (g − (∂_{z_0}u_1)(∂_{z_0}u_0)^{-1})[w]`.
- The middle factor lies in `ZH`, with augmentation `e_{z_0}(u_1)/e_{z_0}(u_0) = 2`. So `Φ` gives
  `Hg − 2H`, consistent with Step 4 with `ε = 1`.
- `check_all()` in `lift.py` verifies the identity for `z_0 = c` and `z_0 = a`. It compares the Fox
  derivatives `∂_x, ∂_y` of `f_0(r)` with the predicted multiple of `∂_x w, ∂_y w` in the exact
  normal form of `G = F_4 ⋊ Z`. By the injectivity of `N_ab → ZG^2` (the relation sequence), this
  is a proof of the identity, not a test.

## Step 5. Obstruction (O3)

`g ∉ H`. Otherwise `x^{-1} = g d^{-1} ∈ H` (since `d = y`), so `H = G`, which is impossible because
`H_ab ≅ Z^4` and `G_ab ≅ Z^2`. So `H ≠ Hg`, and `Hg − 2H` has coefficients `1` and `−2` on two
distinct cosets. It is not of the form `±Hv`.

If `f(r) = v w^{±1} v^{-1}` in `F`, then `[f(r)] = ±π(v)[w]` and `Φ[f(r)] = ±Hπ(v)`, a
contradiction. So no lift of any one-relator presentation of `H` sends `r` to a conjugate of
`w^{±1}`. ∎

## Step 6. Relative presentations (O4)

Suppose `G ≅ ⟨H, x, y | z^{-1} f(z) (z ∈ Z)⟩` with `f(z) ∈ F`. That is, the quotient of
`H ∗ F = ⟨Z, x, y | r⟩` by the relators `z^{-1} f(z)` maps isomorphically to `G`, by the inclusion
on `H` and by `x ↦ x_1`, `y ↦ y_1` for a generating pair `(x_1, y_1)` of `G`.

- *Standard pair.* Let `(x_1, y_1) = (x, y)`. Tietze moves delete each `z` using `z = f(z)`,
  leaving `⟨x, y | f(r)⟩`, and the map to `G` becomes the quotient map `F/⟨⟨f(r)⟩⟩ → F/N`.
  - Compatibility on `H` says `f(z) = z` in `G`, so `f` is a lift.
  - The map is an isomorphism iff `⟨⟨f(r)⟩⟩ = N = ⟨⟨w⟩⟩`. By Magnus' theorem, that holds iff
    `f(r)` is conjugate to `w^{±1}`, which Step 5 excludes.
- *Nielsen-equivalent pair.* If `(x_1, y_1)` is Nielsen-equivalent to `(x, y)` in `G`, lifting the
  Nielsen moves gives `α ∈ Aut F` with `π α(x) = x_1` and `π α(y) = y_1`.
  - Precomposing with `α^{-1}` turns the presentation into `⟨H, x, y | z^{-1} α(f(z))⟩` over the
    standard pair.
  - `α ∘ f` is a lift, since `π α f(z) = z`.
  - The standard case applies.
- *Count.* `|T| = 2` and `|R| = |Z| = 4` by Step 3. So `|R| − |T| = 2 = χ(G) − χ(H)`, with
  `χ(G) = 0` and `χ(H) = −2` as in `admissible-ab-nonzero-two-cell-base-exists`. By
  `relative-realization-is-relative-efficiency`, this is exactly the shape that would certify
  `RP(H,G)`. ∎
