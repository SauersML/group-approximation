**ESTABLISHED.**  Let

```text
q:Gamma_6 -> Lambda
```

be a nontrivial hyperlinear quotient.  Exactly one of the following two
alternatives is forced.

1. `Lambda` is minimally almost periodic: every finite-dimensional unitary
   representation of `Lambda` is trivial.
2. The six literal relators have a marked realization in a nonabelian finite
   simple group `S`, generated by two injected `A8` charts, with

   ```text
   [S:A8] > 48960.                                     (MAP6)
   ```

Thus all finite-dimensional shadows of a hypothetical Atlas hyperlinear
escape are already covered by the finite-simple search.  If no such enormous
finite-simple model exists, every surviving hyperlinear quotient must be
minimally almost periodic and can only be witnessed through dimensions
tending to infinity.

In character language, any nontrivial factorial Connes-embeddable character
of `Gamma_6` either has an infinite-dimensional finite-factor GNS closure or
produces the finite-simple branch above.  Hence after the finite-simple branch
is excluded, the only possible factorial witnesses are diffuse type-II1
characters.

This is not the desired exclusion: minimally almost periodic hyperlinear
groups may exist.  It sharply identifies the only analytic enemy left after
the finite Atlas screens.
