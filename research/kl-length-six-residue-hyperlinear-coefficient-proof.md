**(a).** In a normalized residue word every P-label lies in `{1, g}`, the N-label
of a `++++--` word is `1`, and the only other coefficients are the loops `y` and
`x`. So the subgroup `A_w` generated by the coefficients that occur in `w` is
contained in `A = <g, x, y>`. A subgroup of a hyperlinear group is hyperlinear,
because an embedding into a metric ultraproduct of unitary groups restricts. The
degree is `deg_t(w) = 4` for `+++++-` and `2` for `++++--`, so `w` is
nonsingular.

By `kervaire-laudenbach-holds-for-hyperlinear` (Nitsche--Thom, Theorem 1.2,
credited to Gerstenhaber--Rothaus and Pestov), `A_w -> (A_w * <t>)/<<w>>` is
injective. By `kl-failure-localizes-to-the-coefficient-subgroup`,
`G -> (G * <t>)/<<w>>` is injective.

For an unnormalized word the same two steps apply verbatim to the subgroup its
own coefficients generate. The shift automorphism `t -> s c^(-1)` of
`kl-even-syllable-reduction-proof` fixes `G` and does not change injectivity, so
the hypothesis may be checked on either form.

**(b).** If `x^2 = y^2 = 1`, the homomorphism `C_2 * C_2 -> <x, y>` sending the
free generators to `x` and `y` is onto. `C_2 * C_2` is the infinite dihedral
group, virtually infinite cyclic, so it and all its quotients are amenable.
Amenable groups are sofic, and sofic groups are hyperlinear. If `g in <x, y>`
then `A = <x, y>`, so (a) applies.

**(c).** Finite groups are amenable. If `g in <x, y>` and `<x, y>` is amenable,
then `A = <x, y>` is hyperlinear as in (b), and (a) applies.

**Involution table.**
- `kl-three-equal-residue-shapes-inject-for-every-loop-order` settles the four
  three-equal shapes, for involution loops, outside exactly these relations:
  - `(1,1,1,g)`, `(1,g,1,1)`: `g = y`, `g = y x`, `g x g^(-1) = y`;
  - `(g,1,1,1)`, `(1,1,g,1)`: `g = x`, `g = y x`, `g x g^(-1) = y`.
- In each case `g = y`, `g = x` and `g = y x` put `g` in `<x, y>`, so (b)
  settles them. The relation `g x g^(-1) = y` is settled by (b) when
  `g in <x, y>`. What remains is `g x g^(-1) = y` with `g` outside `<x, y>`.
- For the two-pair `+++++-` shapes and all six `++++--` shapes, (b) settles
  every word with `g in <x, y>`. Nothing is claimed when `g` is outside `<x, y>`.
- `(1,1,1,1)` has no label, and `A_w = <x, y>` is dihedral; this is
  `kl-two-coefficient-residue-word-with-involution-loops-injects`.

**Substitution remark (checked in the free group on `t, g, x, v`).** With
`y = g x g^(-1)`, the automorphism `t -> s g^(-1)` sends
`t^4 g t y t^(-1) x` to `(s g^(-1))^3 s^2 x s^(-1) x`:
`t^4 g t = (s g^(-1))^3 s s g^(-1)`, then `g^(-1) (g x g^(-1)) = x g^(-1)`, and
`g^(-1) t^(-1) = s^(-1)`. The image has the same shape with both loops `x`. The
shift `s -> r g` returns labels `(1,1,1,g)` with loop `g x g^(-1)` at `r^-` and
`x` at `r^+`, i.e. the original relation. So this substitution is circular and
is recorded only as a checked dead end.
