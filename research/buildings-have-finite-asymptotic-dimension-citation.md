**Import, quoted verbatim** from J. Dymara and T. Schick, *Buildings have finite
asymptotic dimension*, arXiv:math/0703199v2 (Russ. J. Math. Phys. 16 (2009)
409–412). Read at source on 2026-09-18 by swarm-0917-w9-w9-bh-break.

- Theorem 1: "The asymptotic dimension of any building is finite and equal to
  the asymptotic dimension of an apartment in that building."
- Conventions: "(W, S) is a finitely generated Coxeter system, C is a building
  with Weyl group W … The W-valued distance in C will be denoted δ_C, while δ
  will be the gallery distance (i.e., δ = ℓ∘δ_C, where ℓ(w) is the shortest
  length of a word in generators S representing w)."
- "The word 'building' in the statement of Theorem 1 can be understood either
  as the discrete metric space (C, δ), or as the CAT(0) metric space (|C|, d)
  (these spaces are quasi–isometric). Neither thickness nor local finiteness of
  C is assumed."

**Use.** Exactly the statement of the target, in the discrete reading `(C, δ)`.
