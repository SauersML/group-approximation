**ESTABLISHED.** Proof: `jnds-criterion-is-inert-on-one-ended-principal-orbits-proof`.

**Setting.**
- `X` is a compact zero-dimensional space and `G` is a group of
  homeomorphisms of `X`, generated by a finite symmetric set `S`.
- `[[G]]` is the group of homeomorphisms of `X` that agree near every point with
  an element of `G`. This is the convention of JNS, arXiv:1305.2637,
  `amenable.tex` l.92–97.
- *Principal:* if `g ∈ G` and `g(x) = x`, then `g` is the identity on a
  neighbourhood of `x`.
- *One-ended amenable orbits:* every `G`-orbit is infinite, and its orbital
  Schreier graph (with respect to `S`) is one-ended and has Følner sets.
- For finite `F ⊂ X`, `R_F` is the group of `k ∈ [[G]]` that are the identity on
  a neighbourhood of `F`.

**Theorem.**
1. *(Rigid stabilizers are co-amenable.)* For every finite `F`, `R_F` is
   co-amenable in `[[G]]`. So `[[G]]` is amenable iff `R_F` is.
2. *(Finite exceptional sets force `R_F` into `[[H]]`.)* Let `H` be any group of
   homeomorphisms of `X`. Suppose each `s ∈ S` agrees with an element of `H` near
   every point outside a finite set `F_s`, which is clause (2) of JNS
   `th:amenhomeo1` (l.104–117) for the generators. Then there is a finite `F̂`
   containing `∪ F_s` with `R_F̂ ≤ [[H]]`.
3. *(Inertness.)* Hence clauses (1) and (2) of `th:amenhomeo1` already imply
   that `[[G]]` is amenable, without clauses (3) (recurrence) or (4) (germs).
   Conversely, if `[[G]]` is amenable then `H = G` satisfies (1) and (2). On this
   class the theorem is logically equivalent to its conclusion.
4. *(No half-orbits.)* A subset `A` of an orbit with `|sA Δ A| < ∞` for all
   `s ∈ S` is finite or cofinite. So the Juschenko–Monod half-orbit mechanism
   (a commensurated set with amenable stabilizer) only offers finite or cofinite
   `A`. The stabilizer of such an `A` in `[[G]]` contains `R_A` or `R_{O \ A}`,
   which is amenable iff `[[G]]` is, by item 1.

**Scope.**
- *Covered.* Take a free Cantor `Z^d`-action (`d >= 2`), such as Labbé's `Omega_U`
  or the Elek–Monod subshift, and a finitely generated `G ≤ [[Z^d ~ X]]` that
  contains the standard translations.
  - `[[G]] = [[Z^d ~ X]]`, since translation germs are everywhere.
  - `G` is principal, since the cocycle is locally constant and the action is
    free.
  - Its orbital graphs are bi-Lipschitz to `Z^d`, hence one-ended and amenable.
  - Every finitely generated subgroup of `[[Z^d ~ X]]` lies in such a `G`.

  The full group is amenable iff its derived subgroup is, since the quotient is
  abelian. So for `labbe-shift-derived-full-group-is-amenable`, no choice of `H` whatsoever
  makes `th:amenhomeo1` on `Omega_U` weaker than the claim itself. This strengthens
  the recorded "relative to translations the walls are infinite".
- *Calibration.*
  - **Juschenko–Monod, minimal `Z`-subshifts.** Orbits are two-ended, and item 2
    fails exactly there: removing one point disconnects `Z`, which is why the
    half-orbit stabilizer works.
  - **Elek–Monod.** `[[G]]` contains `F_2`, and item 3 correctly predicts that no
    `H` satisfies (1) and (2).
  - **Rank-2 interval exchange groups realized as `Z^2` full groups (ChJN l.88).**
    They are amenable, and item 3 is consistent with that. Their proof escapes this
    class in two ways: it works in the circle topology, where interval exchanges
    are not homeomorphisms and germs are not principal, and it uses a cocycle
    rather than the orbit `G`-set.
- *Not covered (the escape routes).*
  - Non-principal coarser topologies. For polygon exchanges on `T^2`, the
    exceptional set relative to translations is one-dimensional.
  - `G`-sets that are not orbits of a principal Cantor action, such as the
    JMMdlS cocycle criterion `prop:functor:amenable:intro`.
