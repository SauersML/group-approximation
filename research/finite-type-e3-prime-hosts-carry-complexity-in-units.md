`nonascending-hnn-end-shifts-satisfy-e3-prime-proof` from graphs to finite `k`-graphs. Item 3 is
`finitely-coded-unit-groups-act-residually-finitely` plus faithfulness. Item 6 is elementary Bass–Serre
theory. Item 5 names an open gate.

## Setting

- `C` is a finite `k`-graph with no sources. `𝔊` is a groupoid with object set `C^0`, generated by a
  finite set `S`. It acts self-similarly on `C` (Li, `ss:ZS`), preserving degrees, and the action is
  pseudo-free.
- **Finite data.** `s·e` and `φ(s, e)` are given words, for `s ∈ S` and `e` an edge of `C`. `M` bounds
  their lengths.
- `D = C ⋈ 𝔊`, and `F` is the full group of Li's groupoid of `D` reduced to a union `Y` of vertex
  path spaces. The V-stabilized version `G_V × ...` is the same with `C_2 × C` in place of `C`.
- **Identity detection (ID).** A row `μ ν ↦ μ'(u·ν)` acts as the identity on a cylinder only if
  `μ = μ'` and `u = 1`.
  - (ID) holds for trivial `𝔊` on aperiodic finite `k`-graphs (the identity-detection lemma of
    `finite-k-graph-full-groups-have-exponential-word-problem`).
  - It holds for the non-ascending HNN codings under their half-tree condition (proof node, Step 5).
- `T_K(ℓ)` is the time to solve the word problem of a finitely generated group `K` on words of length
  `ℓ`, and `T_𝔊` the maximum over the isotropy groups of `𝔊`.

## Statement

1. **Upper bound.** Every finitely generated `K <= F` has `T_K(ℓ) <= 2^(cℓ) (1 + T_𝔊(M^(cℓ)))` for a
   constant `c = c(K)`.
2. **Lower bound.** Each unit group `St(𝔊, v) = D^*(v, v)` embeds in `F`. So its word problem is at
   most `T_F(c·)` plus linear time.
3. **Units are residually finite.** Under (ID), each unit group acts faithfully on its vertex path
   space by permuting the finitely many cylinders of each degree. So it is residually finite.
4. **The finite-type regime of E3′.**
   - When Li's Example `ex:ZS` applies, `F` is of type `F_n` as soon as the stabilizers are.
   - The host's complexity is then exactly that of a residually finite self-similar groupoid, up to
     an exponential change of variables.
   - With finite `𝔊` this is the single-exponential cap. With infinite `𝔊` there is no cap other
     than the units' own.
5. **What finite-type Track A needs.** Suppose a master-route host `F ⊇ Λ ⊇ G` is finitely coded in
   this sense, with `G` an infinite simple Kazhdan group (a reduced input of
   `boone-higman-iff-simple-kazhdan-decidable-inputs`). Then:
   - `T_G(ℓ) <= 2^(cℓ)(1 + T_𝔊(M^(cℓ)))`, with residually finite unit groups;
   - `k >= 2`: in rank one, `rank-one-cone-hosts-put-fw-groups-in-local-map-groups` puts `G` inside
     a unit group, which is residually finite;
   - so, since reduced inputs of arbitrarily high complexity exist, the route needs the following
     gate.

   **(SS-hard)_k.** For every recursive bound `f`, there is a finitely generated residually finite
   groupoid `𝔊` with the following properties:
   - it has a faithful, pseudo-free, degree-preserving self-similar action on a finite `k`-graph with
     at least two loops of each colour at each vertex;
   - its stabilizers are of type `F_2`;
   - its word problem is not solvable in time `f`.

   **OPEN**, also for `k = 1`. In HNN form, `k = 1` is equivalent to the open attempt 5 of
   `fp-simple-groups-with-arbitrarily-complex-word-problem`: a hard group of type `F_2` with a
   faithful HNN extension over finite-index subgroups (`faithful-finite-index-hnn-upgrades-to-half-tree-free`).
   Its self-similar form is `fp-self-similar-groups-with-arbitrarily-hard-word-problem`.
   - All examples on main (finite `𝔊`, `Z^d`, the Katsura odometers, `BS(m,k)`) have isotropy word
     problems in polynomial time.
   - Hard finitely presented residually finite groups exist (`kms-arbitrarily-hard-fp-rf-groups`).
     What is missing is the commensuration: for the KMS groups, every available shape is dead (see
     the upgrade node).
6. **Emitter lemma (tree hosts).** Let `Γ` act faithfully on a tree `T` without inversions. Let
   `1 ≠ G <= Γ` have property FA and no nontrivial finite quotient. Then `G` fixes a vertex `w` of
   infinite valence at which it has an infinite orbit of edges. So some edge stabilizer at `w` meets
   `G` in a subgroup of infinite index. In particular, when `T` is the tree of a finite graph of
   groups, `G` lies in a vertex group with an incident edge group of infinite index. That is an
   infinite emitter of the boundary coding, outside Li's local finiteness.

## Proof sketches

**Items 1–2.** An element of `F` is a finite table of rows `(μ', u, μ)`: the `Z(μ)` and the `Z(μ')`
partition `Y` into cylinders, `u` is a unit, and the row acts by `μν ↦ μ'(u·ν)`.
- **Refinement.** Two cylinders `Z(μ)` and `Z(ν)` meet in a finite union of cylinders of degree
  `d(μ) ∨ d(ν)`, since finite `k`-graphs are finitely aligned. Refining a row by all `α` of degree
  `δ` replaces it by the rows `(μ'(u·α), φ(u, α), μα)`.
  - This multiplies the number of rows by at most `B^(|δ|)`, where `B` bounds the number of edges.
  - It multiplies label lengths by at most `M^(|δ|)`: `φ(s_1⋯s_r, α)` is computed generator by
    generator from the tables.
- **Composition.** A product of `ℓ` generators of `K` needs refinements of total degree `O(ℓ)`. So the
  normalized table has `2^(O(ℓ))` rows with labels of length `M^(O(ℓ))`.
- **Triviality.** By (ID), the product is trivial iff every row is `(μ, 1, μ)`. That is `2^(O(ℓ))`
  word problems in `𝔊`.
- **Item 2.** A unit `u ∈ D^*(v, v)` extended by the identity off `∂Ω(v) ∩ Y` is a global bisection.

**Item 3.** This is `finitely-coded-unit-groups-act-residually-finitely`, since units preserve degree.
By (ID) the action is faithful, so the permutation representations on cylinders of degree `n`
separate elements.

**Item 6.**
- By FA, `G` fixes a vertex.
- Suppose that at every `G`-fixed vertex every `G`-orbit of edges is finite. A finite orbit gives a
  homomorphism of `G` to a finite symmetric group, which is trivial, so `G` fixes all those edges and
  their far vertices.
- By induction along paths, `G` then fixes `T` pointwise, contradicting faithfulness. So some
  `G`-fixed vertex `w` has an infinite `G`-orbit of edges, and hence infinite valence.
- The stabilizer in `G` of an edge in that orbit has infinite index in `G`.
- In a graph of groups whose edge groups all have finite index, `T` is locally finite. So some
  incident edge group of the vertex group containing `G` must have infinite index.

## Lesson for general BH

**Finite type caps nothing; residual finiteness caps the units, and the emitter is forced.**

In any Li-type coding, the complexity of the host is the complexity of its unit groupoid, up to an
exponential. So E3′ in finite type is limited by which residually finite groupoids admit finite
self-similar structures of type `F_2` (gate (SS-hard)_k), not by finite type itself.

For hard simple inputs the two exits are exactly the two gates on the board:
- a rank-at-least-2 finite-type coding over hard residually finite units, with the input in a
  piecewise slot;
- or an infinite emitter, where the input is a vertex group with an infinite orbit of edges. In tree
  hosts the emitter is not a design choice: item 6 proves that FA inputs without finite quotients
  can only sit there.
