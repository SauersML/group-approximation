## Setting

Let `M` be the set of odd integers `m ≥ 5`. In `Sym(Z/m)` put `α_m = (0 1 2)` and
`β_m = (x ↦ x+1)`. Both are even because `m` is odd. **Neumann's group** is
`G = ⟨α, β⟩ ≤ ∏_{m∈M} A_m`, with `α = (α_m)` and `β = (β_m)` (B. H. Neumann 1937, the
group in MO 491978). Write `a_i = β^i α β^{-i}`, which is the 3-cycle `(i, i+1, i+2)` in
every coordinate, and `W_k = ⟨a_0, …, a_k⟩`.

## 1. The windows are explicit finite groups

**Proposition 1.** For every `k ≥ 0`,
`W_k ≅ A_{k+3} × ∏_{m∈M, m≤k+2} A_m`. Here the first factor is `Alt({0,…,k+2})`, acting
identically in every coordinate `m ≥ k+3`, and the factor `A_m` is the full coordinate
`m`. In particular `G` contains every coordinate `A_m`, hence `⊕_{m∈M} A_m`. This
recovers Neumann's containment.

*Proof.* For `m ≥ k+3`, the window `{0,…,k+2}` embeds in `Z/m`, and the `a_i` act on it as
the consecutive 3-cycles `(i,i+1,i+2)`, which generate `Alt({0,…,k+2})`. So `W_k` maps
into the diagonal copy of `A_{k+3}` in `∏_{m≥k+3} A_m`, onto it and injectively. For
`m ≤ k+2`, the images of `a_0,…,a_{m-3}` are consecutive 3-cycles covering `Z/m`, so they
generate `A_m`. Hence `W_k` is a subdirect product of `A_{k+3}` and the `A_m` with
`m ≤ k+2`. These are nonabelian simple and pairwise non-isomorphic, because they have
different degrees. A subdirect product of such groups is their full direct product. ∎

## 2. Structure and presentation

**Proposition 2.** Every relator of `G` has `β`-exponent sum `0`. So
`G = G_0 ⋊ ⟨β⟩`, where `G_0 = ⟨a_i : i ∈ Z⟩ = ⋃_k β^{-k} W_{2k} β^k` is **locally finite**.

*Proof.* A word `w` equals `π·β^σ`, where `σ` is its `β`-exponent sum and `π` is a
product of at most `|w|` of the cycles `a_i^{±1}` with `|i| ≤ |w|`. Take `m > 2|w|+3`.
Then `π_m` moves at most `2|w|+3 < m` points, while `x ↦ x+σ` moves all `m` points unless
`σ ≡ 0 (mod m)`. So `w_m = 1` forces `σ ≡ 0 (mod m)`, and hence `σ = 0` because
`|σ| ≤ |w| < m`. Local finiteness follows from Proposition 1. ∎

**Corollary 3.** `G` is not finitely presented. The same proof works for the subgroup
generated by `α, β` in `∏_{m∈S} A_m` for any infinite `S ⊆ M`.

*Proof.* Suppose `G = ⟨α, β | R⟩` with `R` finite. By Proposition 2 each `r ∈ R` is, up
to conjugation by a power of `β`, a word in `a_0,…,a_{2N}` for some `N`. So `G` is a
quotient of `Q = ⟨α, β | all relations of W_{2N} in a_i := β^iαβ^{-i}⟩`, and `Q` maps
onto `G`, so `Q = G`. Now `Q` is the HNN extension of the finite group `W_{2N}` along
the isomorphism `W_{2N-1} → ⟨a_1,…,a_{2N}⟩`, `a_i ↦ a_{i+1}`, given by conjugation by
`β` in `G`. The two presentations define mutually inverse maps. So every finite subgroup
of `Q` is conjugate into `W_{2N}`, by Bass–Serre theory. But `G ⊇ A_m` for all
`m ∈ M`. ∎

**Proposition 4 (limit and effectivity).** Let `L = Alt_fin(Z) ⋊ Z`, generated by
`(0 1 2)` and the shift. Then `α ↦ (0 1 2)`, `β ↦ shift` defines an epimorphism `G → L`
with kernel `⊕_{m∈M} A_m`. If `w ≠ 1` in `G`, then `w_m ≠ 1` for some `m ∈ M` with
`m ≤ 2|w|+5`. So `G` has solvable word problem and is **effectively residually finite**,
with depth at most `|A_{2|w|+5}|`. Neither McKinsey's obstruction (solvable word problem)
nor Rauzy's (effective residual finiteness, arXiv:2002.02540) rules out a host.

*Proof.* Keep the notation of Proposition 2 with `σ = 0`. For `m > 2|w|+3`, reduction
mod `m` is injective on `[-|w|, |w|+2]`. So `w_m` is the reduction of `π ∈ Alt_fin(Z)`,
and `w_m = 1` iff `π = 1` iff `w = 1` in `L`. So the map is well defined, its kernel is
the set of elements trivial in all large coordinates, and that set is `⊕A_m` by
Proposition 1. If `w ≠ 1` in `L`, the least odd `m > 2|w|+3` works. Otherwise `w` is
already nontrivial in some coordinate `m ≤ 2|w|+3`. ∎

## 3. Constraints on any host

**Proposition 5.** (a) If a group `H` contains nonabelian finite simple subgroups of
unbounded order, then no finite-index subgroup of `H` is residually solvable.
(b) Suppose a group `H` has a separating family of finite quotients whose nonabelian
composition factors all have order `≤ N`. Then every nonabelian finite simple subgroup
of `H` has order `≤ N`.
(c) So `G` embeds in no group acting faithfully on a rooted tree in which every vertex
has at most `d` children, for any `d`. This covers every self-similar group, every
automaton group and every iterated monodromy group. It also embeds in no finitely
generated linear group.

*Proof.* (a) Let `[H:H'] = d`. A finite simple `S ≤ H` with `|S| > d!` has no proper
subgroup of index `≤ d`: such a subgroup would give an injective `S → Sym(i)` with
`i ≤ d`. So `S ≤ H'`, and `S = [S,S] ≤ H'^{(k)}` for all `k`.
(b) Given a finite simple `S ≤ H`, the product of finitely many quotients from the
family is injective on `S`, so `S` embeds in a finite group `Q` whose composition factors
are sections of the family's factors. Intersecting a composition series of `Q` with `S`
embeds `S` in one composition factor.
(c) The quotients by level stabilizers separate points, and they are iterated wreath
products of subgroups of `Sym(d)`. Apply (b) with `N = d!`, and use `A_m ≤ G` for all
`m ∈ M`. Finitely generated linear groups are virtually residually `p` (Mal'cev,
Platonov), so (a) excludes them. ∎

**Consequence for MO 491978.** By (a), Minasyan's title question (a finitely presented
residually finite group that is not virtually residually solvable) already follows from
any finitely presented residually finite group containing `A_m` for infinitely many `m`.
That is weaker than hosting `G`. By (c), the finitely presented self-similar hosts used
elsewhere in the campaign cannot host `G`. A host's profinite completion must have
alternating sections of unbounded degree.
