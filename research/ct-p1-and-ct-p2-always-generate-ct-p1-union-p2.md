## Source

Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue (2026), Problem **21.75** (S. Kohl), verbatim:

> Given two distinct sets `𝒫_1` and `𝒫_2` of odd primes none of which is a subset of the
> other, is it true that `⟨CT_{𝒫_1}(Z), CT_{𝒫_2}(Z)⟩ ⪇ CT_{𝒫_1 ∪ 𝒫_2}(Z)`?
> See the definition of `CT_𝒫(Z)` in 17.60.

Problem 17.60 defines `CT_𝒫(Z)` as the subgroup of `CT(Z)` generated by all class
transpositions that interchange residue classes whose moduli have only prime factors in
`𝒫 ∪ {2}`.

## Statement

Let `P1` and `P2` be arbitrary sets of odd primes: finite or infinite, comparable or not.
Then

`⟨CT_{P1}(Z), CT_{P2}(Z)⟩ = CT_{P1 ∪ P2}(Z)`.

Every class transposition of `CT_{P1∪P2}(Z)` is a product of at most 60 class
transpositions, each lying in `CT_{P1}(Z)` or in `CT_{P2}(Z)`. So the answer to 21.75 is
**no**: the inclusion is never proper.

## Conventions

- `r(m) = {r + tm : t ∈ Z}` with `0 ≤ r < m`, called a *box*. For disjoint boxes, the
  class transposition `τ_{r(m), s(n)}` swaps `r + tm ↔ s + tn` for every `t` and fixes
  everything else.
- The *canonical map* `r(m) → s(n)` is `r + tm ↦ s + tn`.
- Canonical maps compose to canonical maps.
- Restrictions of canonical maps are canonical in the following sense. If `κ : r(m) → s(n)`
  is canonical and `0 ≤ i < q`, then `κ` maps the sub-box `(r + mi)(qm)` canonically onto
  `(s + ni)(qn)`. Both representatives are normalized, since `0 ≤ r + mi < qm`.
- Write `S = P1 ∪ P2`. A modulus is *`Q`-smooth* if all its prime factors lie in `Q ∪ {2}`.

Three identities are used repeatedly. `A`, `B`, `E` denote pairwise disjoint boxes.

- **(I1) Splitting.** Put `C_i = (r + mi)(qm)` and `D_i = (s + ni)(qn)` for `0 ≤ i < q`. Then
  `τ_{r(m), s(n)} = ∏_{i<q} τ_{C_i, D_i}`, a product of commuting factors. This is the
  restriction rule above.
- **(I2) Routing.** `τ_{A,B} = τ_{A,E} τ_{E,B} τ_{A,E}`.
  - A point of `A` goes to `E` by the canonical map, then to `B` by the canonical map, then
    stays in `B`.
  - A point of `B` goes back the same way.
  - A point of `E` goes to `A`, is fixed by `τ_{E,B}`, and returns.
  - The composite `A → E → B` of canonical maps is the canonical map `A → B`.
- **(I3) Transport.** Suppose `g` is a permutation of `Z` mapping the box `X'` canonically
  onto a box `X`, and fixing the box `E` pointwise, with `X' ∩ E = ∅`. Then
  `g τ_{X',E} g^{-1} = τ_{X,E}`.
  - On `X`, the conjugate is `g^{-1}` (canonical `X → X'`), then canonical `X' → E`, then
    `g = id` on `E`.
  - Outside `X ∪ E`, `g^{-1}` avoids `X' ∪ E`, so the conjugate is the identity there.

## Proof

**Step 1 (2-adic separation).** Let `τ_{r(m), s(n)}` be a class transposition with
`S`-smooth moduli. Apply (I1) with `q = 4`.
- Each factor `τ_{C,D}` has `C ⊆ c(4)` and `D ⊆ d(4)` for residues `c, d mod 4` (possibly
  `c = d`), since `4 | 4m` and `4 | 4n`.
- Choose `e ∈ {0,1,2,3} ∖ {c, d}` and put `E = e(4)`, a box of modulus 4 disjoint from
  `C` and `D`.
- By (I2), `τ_{C,D} = τ_{C,E} τ_{E,D} τ_{C,E}`.

So it suffices to prove the following.

**Step 2 (key lemma).** Let `C = r(m)` with `m` `S`-smooth and `4 | m`, and let `E = e(4)`
with `e ≢ r (mod 4)`. Then `τ_{C,E}` is a product of at most five class transpositions
from `CT_{P1}(Z) ∪ CT_{P2}(Z)`.

*Proof.*
1. **Factor the modulus.** Write `m = m_1 m_0 m_3`, where:
   - `m_1` is the part of `m` supported on `P1 ∖ P2`;
   - `m_3` is the part supported on `P2 ∖ P1`;
   - `m_0` is the part supported on `{2} ∪ (P1 ∩ P2)`.

   So `4 | m_0`, `m_0 m_1` is `P1`-smooth, and `m_0 m_3` is `P2`-smooth. If `m_3 = 1`, then
   `τ_{C,E} ∈ CT_{P1}(Z)` already. Assume `m_3 > 1`.
2. **The boxes `A`, `A'` and `F`.**
   - Let `A = ρ(m_0)` be the box of modulus `m_0` containing `C`, and `A' = ρ'(m_0 m_3)` the
     box of modulus `m_0 m_3` containing `C`. Then `C ⊆ A' ⊆ A ⊆ c(4)` with `c = r mod 4`.
   - Choose `γ ∈ {0,1,2,3} ∖ {c, e}` and put `F = γ(4)`. So `A` (hence `A'`), `E` and `F`
     are pairwise disjoint.
3. **The transporter `g`.** Put `g = τ_{F,A'} τ_{A,F}` (apply `τ_{A,F}` first).
   - Both factors lie in `CT_{P2}(Z)`: the moduli are `m_0`, `4` and `m_0 m_3`.
   - On `A`, `g` is canonical `A → F` followed by canonical `F → A'`. That is the canonical
     map `ρ + m_0 t ↦ ρ' + m_0 m_3 t` of `A` onto `A'`.
   - `g` fixes `E` pointwise, since `E` misses `A`, `A'` and `F`.
4. **The preimage `C'`.** By the restriction rule, the canonical map `A → A'` sends the
   sub-boxes of `A` of modulus `m_0 m_1` bijectively and canonically onto the sub-boxes of
   `A'` of modulus `m_0 m_1 m_3 = m`.
   - So `C' := g^{-1}(C)` is a box of modulus `m_0 m_1`, and `g|_{C'}` is the canonical map
     `C' → C`.
   - Explicitly, `C' = (ρ + m_0 j)(m_0 m_1)`, where `r mod m = ρ' + m_0 m_3 j` with
     `0 ≤ j < m_1`.
5. **Conclude.** `C' ⊆ A` is disjoint from `E`, and `m_0 m_1` and `4` are `P1`-smooth. So
   `τ_{C',E} ∈ CT_{P1}(Z)`. By (I3),

   `τ_{C,E} = g τ_{C',E} g^{-1} = τ_{F,A'} τ_{A,F} τ_{C',E} τ_{A,F} τ_{F,A'}`,

   a palindrome of five class transpositions: four from `CT_{P2}(Z)` and one from
   `CT_{P1}(Z)`. ∎

**Step 3 (conclude).** In Step 1, `τ_{C,E}` and `τ_{E,D} = τ_{D,E}` are both of the form
covered by the key lemma. So each of the four factors `τ_{C_i,D_i}` is a product of at most
`5 + 5 + 5` class transpositions from the two subgroups, and `τ_{r(m),s(n)}` of at most 60.
- Class transpositions with `S`-smooth moduli generate `CT_S(Z)` by definition. Hence
  `CT_S(Z) ⊆ ⟨CT_{P1}(Z), CT_{P2}(Z)⟩`.
- The reverse inclusion is trivial.

Nothing in the argument uses finiteness of `P1` or `P2`, or their incomparability. ∎

## A minimal mixed case, fully explicit

Take `P1 = {3}` and `P2 = {5}`. The class transposition `τ_{0(6),1(10)}` moves `6t ↦ 10t+1`,
so it lies in neither `CT_{3}(Z)` nor `CT_{5}(Z)`. It is still a product of six class
transpositions:

`τ_{0(6),1(10)} = [τ_{0(12),2(4)} τ_{2(4),1(20)} τ_{0(12),2(4)}] · [τ_{6(12),0(4)} τ_{0(4),11(20)} τ_{6(12),0(4)}]`.

- `τ_{0(12),2(4)}` and `τ_{6(12),0(4)}` lie in `CT_{3}(Z)`.
- `τ_{2(4),1(20)}` and `τ_{0(4),11(20)}` lie in `CT_{5}(Z)`.
- Splitting by parity of `t`, `τ_{0(6),1(10)} = τ_{0(12),1(20)} τ_{6(12),11(20)}` (I1).
- Each bracket is (I2) with a routing box of modulus 4. In the first, `0(12) ⊆ 0(4)`,
  `1(20) ⊆ 1(4)`, and the route goes through `2(4)`. In the second, `6(12) ⊆ 2(4)`,
  `11(20) ⊆ 3(4)`, and the route goes through `0(4)`.
- Spot checks:
  - `0 → 2 → 1 → 1`;
  - `12 → 6 → 21 → 21`;
  - `−12 → −2 → −19 → −19`;
  - `6 → 0 → 11 → 11`;
  - `18 → 4 → 31 → 31`;
  - `6 ↦ 6` under the first bracket and `0 ↦ 0` under the second, as required.

**Why Step 1 is needed.** Routing (I2) applied directly to `τ_{0(6),1(10)}`, without
splitting first, fails. Any box `E` with `τ_{0(6),E} ∈ CT_{3}(Z)` and
`τ_{E,1(10)} ∈ CT_{5}(Z)` would need a pure 2-power modulus. It would also need to be odd,
to miss `0(6)`, and even, to miss `1(10)`. Refining to modulus 4 first removes this parity
clash.

## Consequences

- **All odd primes.** `CT(Z) = ⟨CT_{P1}(Z), CT_{P2}(Z)⟩` whenever `P1 ∪ P2` is the set of all
  odd primes. By induction on the number of primes in a pair of moduli,
  `CT(Z) = ⟨CT_{{p}}(Z) : p odd prime⟩`.
  - So `CT(Z)` is generated by the class transpositions whose two moduli are of the form
    `2^a p^b` for a single odd prime `p`.
  - More generally, `⟨CT_{P_j}(Z) : j ∈ J⟩ = CT_{∪_j P_j}(Z)` for any family of sets of odd
    primes.
- **Lattice map.** `P ↦ CT_P(Z)` is a join-homomorphism from sets of odd primes under union
  to subgroups under join.
  - Meets are a separate question: is `CT_{P1}(Z) ∩ CT_{P2}(Z) = CT_{P1 ∩ P2}(Z)`?
  - The inclusion ⊇ is clear. For finite `P`, ⊆ plausibly follows from the description of
    `CT_P(Z)` as all piecewise-canonical permutations with `P`-smooth moduli
    (`ct-p-z-is-a-one-vertex-k-graph-full-group`, item 3). Not treated here.
- **Finite generation.** For finite `P1` and `P2`, `CT_{P1∪P2}(Z)` is generated by two simple
  subgroups of type `F_∞` (`ct-p-z-is-a-one-vertex-k-graph-full-group`), with explicit
  60-letter words for its standard generators.

## Lesson for general BH

**Mechanism: shared-colour routing.** In a multi-colour full group (the one-vertex
`k`-graph behind `CT_P(Z)`), the full groups on two colour sets that share a colour with at
least four edges (here colour 2, with the modulus-4 boxes) generate the full group on the
union of the colour sets.
- The shared colour supplies spare disjoint boxes, which is Step 1.
- It also supplies canonical stretches that move one colour's refinement past the other's,
  which is Step 2.
- So a finitely presented simple multi-colour host is the join of its single-colour hosts.
  To show a group lands in `CT_P(Z)`, it is enough to write its generators through
  single-prime pieces `CT_{p}(Z)` and route them through 2-power boxes.
- The same argument should apply to any `k`-graph full group whose colours all factor
  through one common colour.

## Referee (bh-ref-kourovka-b, 2026-09-18): PASS

**Verdict: PASS.** I checked every step by hand. The proof uses nothing beyond the definitions.

**Source.** arXiv:1401.0300v46 (1 Sep 2026): Problems 21.75 and 17.60 read verbatim.
- The quotes under Source are exact, including "P ∪ {2}" in the definition in 17.60.
- 21.75 has no asterisk.

**Step checks.**
- **Restriction rule and (I1).** The sub-box representatives are normalized. The `C_i` partition
  `r(m)`, and the `D_i` partition `s(n)`. Correct.
- **(I2).** It is the conjugate of `τ_{E,B}` by `τ_{A,E}`, and canonical maps compose. Correct. The
  word is a palindrome, so it does not depend on the composition convention.
- **(I3).** Checked on `X`, on `E`, and off `X ∪ E`. It needs only that `g` maps `X'` canonically
  onto `X` and that `g` is the identity on `E`. Correct.
- **Step 1.** Splitting mod 4 puts `C` and `D` each inside one class mod 4, and a third class mod 4
  is always free. Correct.
- **Step 2.** Correct, including the explicit index `j`.
  - `m = m_1 m_0 m_3`, with `4 | m_0`.
  - `C ⊆ A' ⊆ A ⊆ c(4)`, and `A`, `E`, `F` are pairwise disjoint.
  - `g = τ_{F,A'} τ_{A,F}` lies in `CT_{P2}(Z)`, maps `A` canonically onto `A'` and fixes `E`.
  - By the restriction rule, `C' = g^{-1}(C)` has the `P1`-smooth modulus `m_0 m_1`, and `g` maps
    it canonically onto `C`.
  - (I3) gives the five-letter palindrome, which reads the same in either composition convention.
- **Step 3.** The count is `4 · (5 + 5 + 5) = 60`. Correct.
- **Explicit example.** I recomputed the six-letter word on `0, 12, −12, 6, 18`, and class by class;
  it equals `τ_{0(6),1(10)}`. The claim that `τ_{0(6),1(10)}` lies in neither `CT_{3}(Z)` nor
  `CT_{5}(Z)` is also right.
  - Along any product of generators of `CT_Q(Z)`, each affine piece has a multiplier whose
    numerator and denominator are `Q`-smooth.
  - The pieces of `τ_{0(6),1(10)}` are `n ↦ (5n + 3)/3` and `n ↦ 3(n − 1)/5`, with multipliers
    `5/3` and `3/5`.
- **Consequences.** Both follow by induction on the finitely many primes in each generator:
  - the join property;
  - `CT(Z) = ⟨CT_{p}(Z) : p odd⟩`.
