Notation as in `kazhdan-nv-measure-criterion-2026-09-17.md`, Section 0: bricks
`[v] ⊆ C^n`, canonical charts, and the slope cocycle `s(g,x) = |w| - |w'|` for `x` in a
brick `[w]` on which `g` is canonical with image `[w']`.

Let `G <= nV` and `x ∈ C^n`. The **germ group** `Germ_G(x)` is `Stab_G(x)` modulo the
normal subgroup of elements that are the identity on some neighbourhood of `x`. For a
closed `G`-invariant `Z ∋ x`, `Germ_{G,Z}(x)` is defined the same way with
neighbourhoods taken in `Z`.

**Theorem.**

1. *(Slope is a germ invariant.)* `h_x: Stab_G(x) -> Z^n`, `g -> s(g,x)`, is a
   homomorphism. If `g ∈ Stab_G(x)` and `s(g,x) = 0`, then `g` is the identity on a
   neighbourhood of `x` in `C^n`. So `Germ_G(x) ≅ h_x(Stab_G(x))`, a free abelian
   group of rank at most the number of eventually periodic coordinates of `x`.
2. *(Subsystems.)* Let a group `Γ` act on a compact space `X`, let `ρ: Γ -> nV` be a
   homomorphism, and let `ι: X -> Z ⊆ C^n` be a `Γ`-equivariant homeomorphism onto a
   closed `ρ(Γ)`-invariant set. Then for every `x ∈ X`, `Germ_{Γ,X}(x)` is a quotient
   of a subgroup of `Z^n`, so it is abelian and generated by at most `n` elements.
3. *(Separation from SL_3.)* The action of `SL_3(Z[1/p])` on `P^2(Q_p)` has a
   non-abelian point germ group (it contains `SL_2(Z[1/p])` at `[1:0:0]`). So no
   minimal set of any subgroup of any `nV` is conjugate to it, and the same holds for
   every action with a non-abelian point germ group.
4. *(Compression.)* Let `g ∈ nV` be canonical on a brick `B = [w]` with `gB = [wu] ⊊ B`,
   and let `Z` be closed, `g`-invariant, with `Z ∩ B ≠ ∅`. Then `g` fixes a point
   `p ∈ Z ∩ B` with `s(g,p) = -|u| ≠ 0`, where `|u| ∈ N^n \ {0}`.
5. *(Germ types.)* Let `g` fix `z ∈ Z` with `d = s(g,z)`. If `d = 0`, `g` is the
   identity near `z`. If `d ≠ 0` and `d <= 0` coordinatewise, then `g` is canonical on
   every deep brick `B ∋ z` with `gB ⊊ B`. If `d >= 0`, the same holds for `g^{-1}`. The
   remaining case, `d` with coordinates of both signs (a **saddle**), needs `n >= 2`.
6. *(Property (T) stabilizers.)* If `L <= Stab_{nV}(x)` has property (T), then every
   element of `L` is the identity on a neighbourhood of `x`.

**What this changes for the Kazhdan hole.** Call a closed invariant `Z` **slope-principal**
for `G` if `s(g,z) = 0` whenever `g ∈ G`, `z ∈ Z`, `gz = z`. By items 4 and 5, `Z` is
slope-principal iff no `g ∈ G` fixing a point of `Z` has a nonzero slope there. Then no
element compresses a brick meeting `Z` into itself, and in `n = 1` there are no saddles.
Item 3 names the invariant behind "it must use nV" in
`minimal-sets-of-kazhdan-subgroups-of-nv-carry-invariant-measures`: germs at points are
slope vectors. It does not separate `nV` from actions whose point stabilizers are
virtually cyclic, such as boundary actions of hyperbolic groups.

### 2026-09-18, adversarial referee audit (`e2-w2-audit-nv1`, calibration) — SURVIVES

This claim landed ESTABLISHED in the previous wave with no referee stage. Full record:
`research/artifacts/nv-cnd-and-germ-referee-audit-2026-09-18.md`, Section B. Three
lenses, no step refuted.

- **Proof steps.** Items 1–6 were re-derived line by line; each follows. The mechanism
  was also checked computationally in 2V on an independent implementation
  (`experiments/nv-cnd-audit-2026-09-17/audit_germ_slope.py`, which reuses only
  `audit_brick_lemma.py`): the cocycle identity `s(gh,x) = s(g,hx) + s(h,x)` on 300
  random triples (0 violations); zero slope with image brick = domain brick forces the
  identity there (1707 bricks, 0 violations); item 4's compression realised by
  Thompson's `x_0` in one and in both coordinates, with the predicted fixed point and
  slopes `(-1,0)` and `(-1,-1)`; and item 5's saddle exhibited, `s(baker, (0^∞,0^∞)) =
  (1,-1)`, which is impossible for `n = 1`.
- **Imports.** The single import, Delorme–Guichardet (BdlHV Thm 2.12.4), is used in the
  safe direction ((T) ⇒ (FH)), which needs no σ-compactness hypothesis, and `L ≤ nV`
  is countable discrete anyway. **It is also unnecessary:** a group with (T) has finite
  abelianisation (BdlHV Cor. 1.3.6), so `Hom(L, Z^n) = 0` at once, and item 6 follows
  with no citation. The notation reference
  `research/artifacts/kazhdan-nv-measure-criterion-2026-09-17.md` Section 0 exists and
  supplies the sub-brick rule, the deep-brick rule and the slope cocycle as used.
- **Statement looser than the proof (not a refutation).** The title says germ groups
  are "abelian of rank at most `n`"; item 2's conclusion is a *quotient* of a subgroup
  of `Z^n`, which may have torsion (an element can be locally trivial on `Z` without
  being locally trivial on `C^n`). The body's "abelian and generated by at most `n`
  elements" is exact, and item 3 uses only abelianness. Read "rank" as torsion-free
  rank.
- **Item 3, a step worth spelling out.** A conjugacy between `G|_Z` and `Γ ↷ X` gives a
  surjection `G → Γ`, not a homomorphism `Γ → nV`. The conclusion still follows because
  a germ group of an action depends only on the image of `G` in `Homeo(Z)`: the kernel
  of `G → Homeo(Z)` is locally trivial. So apply item 2 with `Γ = G ≤ nV`, `X = Z`,
  `ι = id`.
- **Calibration (proves too much?).** No. `1V`: germs are slopes, the classical
  statement. SFT full groups: the groupoid isotropy at a point is generated by the
  return-time shift, so germs are cyclic. Nekrashevych/self-similar groups are the
  sharp test, and they are consistent: a group whose sections never become trivial
  (Grigorchuk, and any non-eventually-trivial automaton group) is not a subgroup of
  `V` at all because elements of `nV` are finite brick maps; the Grigorchuk group's own
  germ group at `1^∞` is the Klein four group, abelian, so it is not a candidate
  counterexample. The theorem bites only where a slope element conjugates finite germ
  decorations non-trivially (Röver-type), and no such action is known inside `nV`.
