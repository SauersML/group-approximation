Put `u = pi(t)`.  Conjugation `Ad u` is a trace-preserving automorphism of `N`,
and it carries the von Neumann algebra generated by `pi(Gamma)` onto the von
Neumann algebra generated by `pi(t Gamma t^(-1))`:

```text
u P u^* = pi(t Gamma t^(-1))''  <=  pi(Gamma)'' = P,
```

the inclusion because `t Gamma t^(-1) <= Gamma`.  Thus
`theta = Ad u |_P : P -> P` is a unital, injective, trace-preserving
`*`-homomorphism of the atomic tracial algebra `(P, tau|_P)` into itself.  By
`atomic-tracial-algebra-endomorphisms-are-automorphisms` it is surjective:
`u P u^* = P`.  Conjugating by `u^*` gives `u^* P u = P`, and therefore

```text
pi(t^(-1) Gamma t) = u^* pi(Gamma) u <= u^* P u = P.
```

Iterating with `t^k` (also a compressor) gives `pi(t^(-k) Gamma t^k) <= P` for
all `k`, hence the generated subgroup.  When `Gamma` has property (T) and `P`
is amenable, `kazhdan-generated-amenable-algebra-is-atomic` supplies
atomicity, so the hypothesis of the theorem is met.

The consequences listed in the claim are immediate from (AKM1): in each
group the adjoined element (lamp, free lamp, or the swapped copy) commutes
with `Gamma`, hence with the algebra `P` it generates, hence with `pi(h)` for
`h in t^(-1) Gamma t - Gamma`; the displayed group relations show it does not
commute with `h` in the group.  For the double, the induced representation
`Phi'(d) = diag(Phi(d), Phi(s d s))`, `Phi'(s) = [[0,1],[1,0]]` is a faithful
representation of `E = D x|_alpha C_2 = G *_Gamma (Gamma x C_2)` on `M_2(N)`,
and since `s` centralizes `Gamma`, `Phi'(Gamma)'' = P (x) 1`, which is amenable
iff `P` is.  Normalization of the centralizer (consequence 5) is
`u (P' cap N) u^* = (u P u^*)' cap N = P' cap N`.
