Use the notation of the target. A neighborhood basis at p consists
of p together with all C_n for n>=k. Every permutation of N sends
cofinite sets to cofinite sets. Hence the displayed formula defines
a continuous map at p, with continuous inverse obtained from the
inverse permutation. On each shell it is a prefix homeomorphism.
Products agree with permutation products, and the free regular
action on the even integers makes the homomorphism faithful.

The map hat(g) is locally in V at every point other than p. Indeed
any one of its shell prefix maps extends to V. It also preserves
every V orbit, including that of p. Therefore words in V and
hat(P) have finitely many singularities, all in Omega=V*p, and
preserve every V orbit. V is full, so an element of E_nu with no
singularities belongs to V. The set Omega consists of the eventually
zero binary sequences and is countable and dense.

## Near permutations and the isotropy group

A near permutation is a bijection between cofinite subsets of N,
where two such bijections are identified if they agree on a
cofinite subset. Composition, after restriction, gives a group.
Define its index by

    I(f)=|N minus range(f)| - |N minus domain(f)|.

Removing the same finite number of domain and range points leaves
this value unchanged. Restricting two maps to make their intermediate
sets agree proves I(fh)=I(f)+I(h). In particular I(tau)=1, while
every actual permutation has index zero.

An element of V fixing p agrees, near p, with
0^k*z -> 0^l*z. On all sufficiently deep shells this is n->n+l-k.
Conversely such prefix germs are realizable in V. Thus (V)_p is
the infinite cyclic group generated by tau.

To compute an E_nu isotropy germ, follow p through a word in V and
hat(P). Choose V transporters t_q with t_q(p)=q for its intermediate
points q in Omega, choosing t_p=1. A letter a taking q to q' gives
the isotropy germ of t_(q')^-1*a*t_q at p. A V letter contributes
a prefix germ. So does a hat(P) letter at q!=p, where it is locally
V. At q=p the letter fixes p and contributes the near permutation
rho_g. These germs telescope to the original word's germ.

Consequently all isotropy germs are generated by <tau> and rho(P).
Each of their products acts on all sufficiently deep whole shells
by a cofinite permutation and leaves the suffix inside each shell
unchanged. Two such germs are equal precisely when their shell
indices agree eventually. This proves the claimed identification
with a subgroup of the near permutation group.

A permutation pi of N which is eventually a translation n->n+c
has c=0. For sufficiently large k, its image of {n>=k} is
{n>=k+c}; the remaining domain and range have sizes k and k+c,
and bijectivity makes those sizes equal. Thus pi is finitary.
For g!=1, rho_g moves every even integer and is not finitary.
It is therefore neither trivial as a germ nor a V germ. This
shows that hat(g) has precisely the one singularity p and that
rho(P) embeds in the isotropy germ group.

## Single-singularity realization

Every isotropy germ at p is represented by a word in hat(P) and
elements of V fixing p, by the preceding normalization. All these
representatives fix p and have singularities contained in {p}.
The same is true of their products. If the germ is not a V germ,
the representative is singular at p, so its singular set is {p}.

Given an arbitrary e singular at q, compose on the left by a V
map carrying e(q) to q, and conjugate by a V transporter between
p and q. Realize the resulting isotropy germ as above and undo
these V maps. The result has just the one singularity q and agrees
with e near q. Together with finite singular sets and relative
fullness this verifies all three parts of the definition of a
finite germ extension in
[Belk--Hyde--Matucci](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03149v1#S1).

V is finitely generated, so E_nu is finitely generated whenever P
is. There is no claim here that it is finitely presented, even
when P is finitely presented and has decidable word problem.
