OPEN. Let `G = <a,t | w>` be torsion-free, and suppose that for every free
basis `(a,t)` of `F_2` in which `t` has exponent sum zero in `w`, both extreme
letters of the Magnus rewriting are unpeelable. Such an extreme letter forms
at least three syllables, or two syllables with different absolute exponents.
Claim: `G` is sofic.

Together with [[peelable-extreme-one-relator-groups-are-sofic]] this is
exactly [[torsion-free-two-generator-one-relator-groups-sofic]], and hence all
of Pestov's Question 4.10.

This class is not the real boundary. [[amenable-envelope-magnus-piece-one-relator-sofic]]
also closes unpeelable relators whose Magnus piece embeds over a Magnus
subgroup into an amenable-edge envelope. An example is the nested conjugator
`a_n U a_n^(-1) X a_n U^(-1) a_n^(-1) Z`, with four top syllables. The smaller
remainder is [[no-magnus-envelope-one-relator-groups-sofic]], routed by
`unpeelable-extreme-sofic-via-magnus-envelope`.

## Attempts

1. **Peel anyway.** *Dies at the third syllable.* With syllables
   `y^(e_1) U_1 y^(e_2) U_2 y^(e_3) U_3 ...`, the new letter couples to the free
   subgroup generated by several coefficients at once. The window step
   `S_(j-1) *_(F_j) B_j` is then an amalgam over a free group of rank
   `n-m >= 2` that no cyclic rearrangement exposes. No amenable-edge
   permanence theorem applies.
2. **Unequal two-syllable exponents by substitution.** *Moves the complexity.*
   For `y^2 U y^(-3) V`, the substitutions `s = y^2`, `u = y^3` force
   `y = u s^(-1)`. Resubstituting gives a relator with four `s`-syllables.
3. **Relative Magnus rewriting over the previous window.** *Makes the edge
   larger.* If the `y`-exponent sum is zero, rewriting over the conjugates
   `y^(-i) S_(j-1) y^i` produces edges that are free products of copies of
   `S_(j-1)`. These are nonamenable, and worse than the original free edge.
4. **Choose another basis.** *Not a proof, only a sharper search filter.*
   When `w` lies in the commutator subgroup, every primitive class `chi` in
   `H^1(G;Z)` gives zero-exponent bases, and its extreme letters are the
   syllables of the relator path running along the two supporting lines
   `chi = max` and `chi = min`. A group in this class must be unpeelable at
   both supporting lines in every direction and for every choice of basis.
   When `b_1(G) = 1` there is a single class up to sign, and only changes of
   basis preserving it remain.
