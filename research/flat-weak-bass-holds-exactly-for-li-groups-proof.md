Notation as in the target. Modules are left modules and elements of `Z[G]^k` are row vectors. A
matrix `A ∈ M_{k×n}(Z[G])` acts by right multiplication, `r_A(x) = xA`. `rk_U(A)` is
`dim_U` of the image of `r_A : U^k → U^n`. Equivalently, it is the von Neumann dimension of the
closure of the image of `r_A : ℓ²(G)^k → ℓ²(G)^n`. `rk_ε(A) = rank_Q ε(A)`. `A^*` is the
conjugate transpose, with `g ↦ g^{-1}`.

Recalled facts about `dim_U` (Lück, *L²-invariants*, §6): additivity on short exact sequences and
over arbitrary direct sums; `dim_U` of a directed union of submodules is the supremum; for a
directed system, `dim_U(colim M_i) = sup_i inf_{j >= i} dim_U im(M_i → M_j)`; induction from
`H ≤ G` preserves ranks of matrices over `Z[H]`; for `N ≤ G` of index `m`,
`dim_{U(N)} res M = m · dim_{U(G)} M`.

## 1. (i) ⇒ (ii): Cohn specialization

Let `G` be locally indicable and `D = D(G)`, the division closure of `Q[G]` in `U`. By
Jaikin-Zapirain–López-Álvarez, `D` is a division ring and a Hughes-free `Q[G]`-ring. By
Jaikin-Zapirain (*The universality of Hughes-free division rings*, 2021), `D` is the universal
division `Q[G]`-ring of fractions. Universality in Cohn's sense means the following. For every
ring homomorphism `φ : Q[G] → E` into a division ring, and every matrix `A` over `Q[G]`,
`rank_E φ(A) <= rank_D A`. For a non-epic `φ`, replace `E` by the division subring generated by
`φ(Q[G])`, which does not change ranks.

Apply this to `ε : Q[G] → Q`, which is onto a field. It gives `rk_ε(A) <= rank_D(A)`. Also
`rank_D(A) = rk_U(A)`. To see this, note that `U` is a free `D`-module, since `D` is a division
ring. So `U ⊗_D −` is exact, and the image of `r_A` on `U^k` is `U ⊗_D (image of r_A on D^k)`.
That image is `U ⊗_D D^ρ` with `ρ = rank_D A`, and its `U`-dimension is `ρ`. Hence
`rk_ε(A) <= rk_U(A)`.

*Commutative check.* If `G` is abelian and torsion-free, `Z[G]` is a domain and `D` is its field
of fractions. Here (ii) says that if some `ρ × ρ` minor of `A` has nonzero augmentation, then the
minor is nonzero. That is clear. It is the lower semicontinuity of fibre rank between the closed
point `ker ε` and the generic point.

## 2. (ii) ⇒ (iii): Lazard

This step holds for every group. Let `M` be flat. By Lazard–Govorov, `M = colim_{j ∈ J} F_j`
over a directed set, with each `F_j = Z[G]^{n_j}` finitely generated free.

Let `r` be a finite integer with `r <= rank_Q(Q ⊗ M)`. Pick `m_1, …, m_r ∈ M` whose images
`1 ⊗ m_i` are `Q`-independent. All the `m_i` lift to some `F_{j_0}`. For `j >= j_0`, let
`A_j ∈ M_{r×n_j}(Z[G])` be the matrix whose rows are the images of the lifts in `F_j`. Then
`Q ⊗ M = colim Q^{n_j}`. Since the images in the colimit are independent, the rows of `ε(A_j)`
are independent, so `rk_ε(A_j) = r`.

By (ii), `rk_U(A_j) >= r`. So `r_{A_j} : U^r → U^{n_j}` has image of dimension `r`, and by
additivity its kernel has dimension `0`. Let `φ : U^r → U ⊗ M = colim U^{n_j}` send `e_i` to
`1 ⊗ m_i`. Its kernel is the directed union over `j >= j_0` of the kernels of `r_{A_j}`, so it has
dimension `0`. Hence `dim_U(U ⊗ M) >= dim_U im φ = r`. Letting `r` grow gives (iii).

(iii) ⇒ (iv) is a special case.

## 3. (iv) ⇒ (i): a non-indicable subgroup gives a flat module of positive defect

Suppose `G` is not locally indicable. Then there is a nontrivial finitely generated `H ≤ G` with no
surjection onto `Z`, so `H_1(H; Q) = 0`.

**A matrix with `rk_ε > rk_U`.**

- *`H` finite.* Take `A = (N_H)` with `N_H = Σ_{h ∈ H} h`. Then `ε(A) = |H|`, so `rk_ε(A) = 1`.
  Also `N_H = |H| p` for a projection `p` of trace `1/|H|`, so `rk_U(A) = 1/|H|`.
- *`H` infinite.* Choose generators `x_1, …, x_n` of `H` and relators `r_1, …, r_k`. Since
  `H_1(H; Q) = 0`, the exponent-sum vectors of all relators span `Q^n`, and finitely many of
  them already do. Let `J_{ji}` be the image of the Fox derivative `∂r_j/∂x_i` in `Z[H]`. Then
  `ε(J_{ji})` is the exponent sum of `x_i` in `r_j`, so `rk_ε(J) = n`.

  Let `c` be the column with entries `c_i = x_i − 1`. The fundamental formula
  `Σ_i (∂r/∂x_i)(x_i − 1) = r − 1` gives `Jc = 0`. So `im r_J ⊆ ker(r_c : U^n → U)`.

  To compute the rank of `c`, use `ℓ²`. The adjoint of `r_c` is `w ↦ (w(x_i^{-1} − 1))_i`. Its
  kernel is the set of vectors of `ℓ²H` invariant under right multiplication by `H`, and that is
  `0` because `H` is infinite. So `rk_U(c) = 1`, `dim ker r_c = n − 1`, and
  `rk_U(J) <= n − 1 < n = rk_ε(J)`.

Induction from `H` to `G` preserves both ranks. So in both cases there is a matrix `A` over
`Z[G]` with `s := rk_ε(A) > t := rk_U(A)`.

**The flat module.** Put `B = A^* A ∈ M_n(Z[G])`. Then `B^* = B`, and
`r_B = r_A ∘ r_{A^*} = T T^*` for `T = r_A` on `ℓ²`. So `r_B` is a positive operator. Its powers
all have the same kernel and the same closure of the image, and
`rk_U(B^m) = rk_U(T^*) = rk_U(T) = t` for all `m >= 1`.

Likewise `ε(B) = ε(A)^T ε(A)` is a positive semidefinite rational matrix, and
`rank ε(B)^m = rank ε(A) = s` for all `m >= 1`.

Let `M_B = colim(Z[G]^n →r_B Z[G]^n →r_B ⋯)`. It is countably generated and flat, being a
directed colimit of free modules. Then:

- `Q ⊗ M_B = colim(Q^n →ε(B) Q^n → ⋯)` has dimension `lim_m rank ε(B)^m = s`.
- `U ⊗ M_B = colim(U^n →B U^n → ⋯)`. By Lück's colimit formula, its dimension is
  `inf_m rk_U(B^m) = t < ∞`.

So `D(M_B) = s − t > 0`, which contradicts (iv).

If `G` is torsion-free, the subgroup `H` is infinite. Then `s = n` and `t <= n − 1`, so
`D(M_B) >= 1`. For the counterexample `K` of item 4 of the target, take `H = K`: its
abelianization is finite, so `b_1(K) = 0`.

## 4. Item 3: weak Bass for locally indicable groups, subgroups and finite extensions

*(a) Locally indicable `G`.* A projective module is flat, so (i) ⇒ (iii) gives WBB. Neither
amenability nor countable generation is needed.

*(b) Subgroups.* Let `H ≤ G` and let `C` be a projective `Z[H]`-module. Induction gives
`Q ⊗_{Z[G]} (Z[G] ⊗_{Z[H]} C) = Q ⊗_{Z[H]} C`, and
`U(G) ⊗_{Z[G]} Z[G] ⊗_{Z[H]} C = U(G) ⊗_{U(H)} (U(H) ⊗_{Z[H]} C)` has the same dimension
(Lück; recorded in `l2-finite-projectives-over-amenable-groups-satisfy-weak-bass`). So
WBB for `G` implies WBB for `H`.

*(c) Finite extensions (Swan).* Let `N ⊴ G` have finite index, put `F = G/N`, and suppose WBB
holds for `N`. Let `C` be a countably generated projective `Z[G]`-module with
`d = dim_{U(G)}(U(G) ⊗ C) < ∞`.

- *Restriction to `N`.* `res_N C` is a countably generated projective `Z[N]`-module.
  `U(G) = U(N) ⊗_{Z[N]} Z[G]`, so `U(G) ⊗_{Z[G]} C ≅ U(N) ⊗_{Z[N]} C` as `U(N)`-modules. Its
  `U(N)`-dimension is `|F| d`. WBB for `N` gives `rank_Q(Q ⊗_{Z[N]} C) <= |F| d`.
- *The module `P_F`.* As right `Z[G]`-modules, `Q ⊗_{Z[N]} Z[G] = Q[F]`. So
  `Q ⊗_{Z[N]} C = Q ⊗_Z P_F` with `P_F := Z[F] ⊗_{Z[G]} C`, which is a projective
  `Z[F]`-module.
- *`P_F` is finitely generated.* Restricted to `Z`, `P_F` is a projective abelian group, hence
  free. By the previous step its rank is at most `|F| d < ∞`. So `P_F` is finitely generated.
- *Swan's theorem.* A finitely generated projective `Z[F]`-module is locally free. Hence
  `Q ⊗ P_F ≅ Q[F]^ρ` with `ρ = rank_Z(P_F)/|F|`.
- *Conclusion.* `rank_Q(Q ⊗_{Z[G]} C) = rank_Q(Q ⊗_{Z[F]} P_F) = ρ`. So

  > `rank_Q(Q ⊗_{Z[G]} C) = rank_Q(Q ⊗_{Z[N]} C) / [G:N] <= d.`

If `N` is only of finite index, not normal, apply (b) to its normal core, which has finite index
in `N`, and then apply (c).

*(d) Virtually locally indicable groups.* Combine (a)–(c). Virtually polycyclic groups are
virtually poly-`Z`, and poly-`Z` groups are locally indicable. Hantzsche–Wendt is virtually
`Z³`: flat WBB fails for it by §3, but WBB holds by (d). So on this group, WBB depends on
projectivity. What separates the two here is exactly Swan's theorem (§4(c)). Over `N = Z³`,
flat WBB holds, so `rank_Q(Q[F] ⊗ M_B) <= |F|(n − 1)`, while its trivial isotypic part
`Q ⊗_G M_B` has rank `n`. So in `Q[F] ⊗ M_B` the trivial part is larger than its `1/|F|`
share. For a projective `C`, Swan's theorem forces `Q[F] ⊗ C` to be `Q[F]`-free, so the trivial
part is exactly the `1/|F|` share. A flat module has no such constraint.

## 5. Item 4

By §3, a group `G` that is not locally indicable carries a countably generated flat `M_B` with
`D(M_B) > 0`, and `D(M_B) >= 1` if `G` is torsion-free. By §2, every argument that proves
`D(M) <= 0` from the flatness of `M` alone proves (ii). By §3, (ii) forces local indicability.

Now take the counterexample `K` of item 3 of
`amenable-cd-two-bass-defect-equals-euler-characteristic`. It has finite abelianization, so it
is not locally indicable. It is also not virtually locally indicable: a finite-index locally
indicable subgroup would be an amenable locally indicable group of cd 2, hence elementary
amenable by `amenable-cd-two-kropholler-iff-locally-indicable`, and then `K` itself would be
elementary amenable. So neither §4(a) nor §4(d) reaches `K`, and the flat module `M_B` over `K`
has the same sign of defect as the core `P'`. ∎
