Because `A` is a unital subalgebra containing `F`, each block matrix

```text
D(f)=diag(f,1,...,1),                  f in F,
```

belongs to `GL_n(A)); its inverse is `D(f^(-1))).  For every selected
coefficient `r_alpha in S<=A`, both positive and negative root positions
give

```text
e_(i_alpha,j_alpha)(r_alpha)
 =1+r_alpha E_(i_alpha,j_alpha) in GL_n(A),             (FCQ4)
```

with inverse `1-r_alpha E_(i_alpha,j_alpha)`.  No ordering of the root
positions is used.

If `d=dim_(F_2)(A)`, then `A` has exactly `2^d` elements.  Hence
`M_n(A)` is finite and its subset `GL_n(A)` is finite.  The literal
binary-Leavitt subgroup therefore satisfies

```text
H <= GL_n(A) <= GL_n(R),                                (FCQ5)
```

so `H` is finite.  This argument includes all alternating products,
opposite-root commutators, and Weyl words made from the chosen generators;
they do not have to remain triangular.

Now pass to `St_n(R)` with `n>=5`.  Let `Gamma` be generated by the
established lift of the qutrit packet and by the standard lifts of the
selected roots.  Under the Steinberg projection `pi`,

```text
pi(Gamma)<=H.
```

Thus `pi(Gamma)` is finite and

```text
1 -> Gamma intersect ker(pi) -> Gamma -> pi(Gamma) -> 1. (FCQ6)
```

The kernel of `pi` is central in the stable Steinberg range used here.
Consequently `Gamma intersect ker(pi)` is abelian and central in
`Gamma`, proving that `Gamma` is central-by-finite.  It is amenable and
therefore hyperlinear.

For the first advertised special case, the linear span

```text
A_F=span_(F_2)(F)
```

is a unital algebra: products of basis elements remain in the finite group
`F`.  Its dimension is at most `|F|=3^5`.

For the fixed-code case, let `D` be a finite maximal prefix code whose
matrix chart contains the qutrit packet, and set

```text
m_(d,e)=s_d t_e,                    d,e in D.
```

The prefix relations give

```text
m_(d,e)m_(d',e')=delta_(e,d')m_(d,e'),
sum_(d in D)m_(d,d)=1.                                (FCQ7)
```

Therefore

```text
A_D=span_(F_2){m_(d,e):d,e in D} isomorphic to M_|D|(F_2)
```

is a finite unital coefficient algebra.  The standard prefix permutation
`sigma in Sym(D)` is the unit

```text
u_sigma=sum_(d in D)m_(sigma(d),d),                     (FCQ8)
```

so the qutrit packet `F<=Sym(D)` lies in `A_D^x).  Taking
`S<=A_D` makes the algebra in `(FCQ1)` a subalgebra of `A_D`, hence
finite-dimensional.  This covers arbitrary mixed signs and all same-chart
Weyl returns, not just same-code conjugation.

Finally, every finite additive overlap supported by these generators lies in
`C[H]` downstairs or `C[Gamma]` upstairs.  Finite-support localization
cannot extract a nonhyperlinear subgroup from a finite or amenable group.
Thus any surviving opposite-root construction must fail `(FCQ2)`: its
packet-normalized coefficient words must span infinitely many
`F_2`-directions.  This is why the next unresolved object uses both literal
Leavitt one-sided inverses rather than another scalar or fixed-code root.
