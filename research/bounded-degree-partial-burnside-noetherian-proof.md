Notation as in `bounded-degree-partial-burnside-reps-factor-through-burnside`.

**The varieties.** Identify `Hom(F, GL_d(C))` with `GL_d(C)^m`, and realize it as
the affine variety

```text
V_d = { (A_1, ..., A_m, t_1, ..., t_m) in M_d(C)^m x C^m : det(A_i) t_i = 1 for all i } .
```

For a word `h`, the entries of `rho(h)` are polynomials in the entries of the
`A_i` and of the `A_i^-1 = t_i adj(A_i)`, hence polynomials in the coordinates of
`V_d`. So `X_R(d)`, defined by the vanishing of the entries of `rho(h)^N - 1` for
the finitely many `h` with `1 <= |h| <= R`, is Zariski closed in `V_d`. If
`R <= R'` then `X_R'(d) <= X_R(d)`.

**Clause 1.** The coordinate ring of `V_d` is a quotient of a polynomial ring in
finitely many variables, hence Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem. So `V_d`
satisfies the descending chain condition on Zariski closed subsets, and the chain

```text
X_1(d) >= X_2(d) >= X_3(d) >= ...
```

is constant from some index on. Every `g in F` has finite length, so the
intersection of the chain is `Hom(B(m,N), GL_d(C))`. A chain that is constant
from `R_0` on equals its intersection from `R_0` on. Let `R_0(m,N,d)` be the
least such index.

**Clause 2.** Let `rho in X_R(d)` with `R >= R_0(m,N,d)`. Then `rho(g)^N = 1`
for all `g in F`, so `Gamma = rho(F)` is a finitely generated periodic subgroup
of `GL_d(C)`, finite by Schur's theorem, of exponent dividing `N`. By Jordan's
theorem `Gamma` has an abelian normal subgroup `A` of index `<= f(d)`. The
elements of `A` commute and satisfy `a^N = 1`, so they are simultaneously
diagonalizable with `N`-th roots of unity on the diagonal. Hence `|A| <= N^d`
and `|Gamma| <= f(d) N^d`.

**Clause 3, forward.** Assume `LRB(m,N)`. By clause 3 of
`local-restricted-burnside-iff-partial-burnside-finite-quotients` there is `R*`
such that every finite quotient of `Π_{R*}(m,N)` has exponent dividing `N`. Fix
`d` and `rho in X_{R*}(d)`.
- `rho` factors through `Π_{R*}(m,N)`, since it kills `h^N` for `|h| <= R*`.
- `Gamma = rho(F)` is finitely generated and linear, hence residually finite by
  `finitely-generated-linear-groups-are-residually-finite`.
- Every finite quotient of `Gamma` is a finite quotient of `Π_{R*}(m,N)`, so it
  has exponent dividing `N`.
- For `g in F`, the element `rho(g)^N` therefore dies in every finite quotient of
  `Gamma`, and residual finiteness gives `rho(g)^N = 1`.

So `X_{R*}(d) = Hom(B(m,N), GL_d(C))`, that is `R_0(m,N,d) <= R*`, for every `d`.

**Clause 3, converse.** Assume `R_0(m,N,d) <= R` for every `d`. Let `Q` be a
finite quotient of `Π_R(m,N)`, generated by the images `s_1, ..., s_m` of the
`x_i`. The left regular representation `Q -> GL_|Q|(C)` is faithful. Composed with
`F -> Π_R(m,N) -> Q` it lies in `X_R(|Q|)`, which equals
`Hom(B(m,N), GL_|Q|(C))` because `R >= R_0(m,N,|Q|)`. Faithfulness then gives
`q^N = 1` for every `q in Q`. So every finite quotient of `Π_R(m,N)` has exponent
dividing `N`, and clause 3 of the LRB reformulation, with `R* = R`, gives
`LRB(m,N)`. ∎
