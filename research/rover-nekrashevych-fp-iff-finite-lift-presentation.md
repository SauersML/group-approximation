## Setting

- `X = {0, ..., d-1}` with `d >= 2`. Sections follow `g(xw) = g(x) g|_x(w)` and `(gh)|_x = g|_{h(x)} h|_x`.
- `G <= Aut(X^*)` is generated by a finite set `S` closed under sections and inverses (a finite-state, or
  automaton, group). `F` is the free group on `S` (the identity, if it lies in `S`, is sent to `1`).
- `ψ: F -> F ≀ Sym(X)` is the recursion homomorphism: on letters it is the wreath recursion of `S`, and on words it
  computes the root permutation and the word-level sections `w|_x`.
- `N = ker(F -> G)` is the **relator kernel**. Every `w in N` has trivial root permutation and `w|_x in N`.
- A subgroup `M <= F` is a **lift ideal** if it is normal and **lift-closed**: whenever `w` has trivial root
  permutation and every `w|_x` lies in `M`, then `w in M`. Intersections of lift ideals are lift ideals, so every
  set `R` generates a smallest lift ideal `⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift`.
- `K_∞ = ⟨⟨∅⟩⟩_lift` is the set of words that become freely trivial: `w in K_∞` iff for some `n` the word `w` has
  trivial permutation on `X^n` and all its word-level sections at level `n` are freely trivial. `K_∞ ⊆ N`.

## Statement

**Theorem A.** `V_d(G)` is finitely presented if and only if `N = ⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift` for some finite `R ⊆ N`.

When it holds, `V_d(G)` has the explicit finite presentation of Nekrashevych's proof of his Theorem 5.9, with his
nucleus relations (N) replaced by `L(r) = 1` for `r in R`:

- a finite presentation of the Higman–Thompson group `V_d` on generators `Σ`;
- generators `L(s)`, `s in S`, standing for `s` acting on the cone `0X^ω`;
- **(C)** commutation relations of `L(s)` with a finite generating set of the pointwise fixer of `0X^ω` in `V_d`,
  and of cone copies on disjoint cones `x ≠ y` and `v_1 ≠ v_2 in X^2`;
- **(S)** splitting relations `L(s) = h_s · L_{00}(s|_0) ··· L_{0,d-1}(s|_{d-1})`, with `h_s in V_d` permuting the
  cones `0x` as `s` permutes `X`;
- `L(r) = 1` for `r in R`.

**Consequences.**

1. **Scott (FP1) and Nekrashevych (FP2) are the two ends.** A finite presentation of `G` gives finite `R` with
   `N = ⟨⟨R⟩⟩ ⊆ ⟨⟨R⟩⟩_lift`. For contracting `G`, Nekrashevych's Theorem 5.9 and the necessity half show that `N`
   is a finitely generated lift ideal.
2. **When the recursion alone suffices.** If `N = K_∞`, then `V_d(G)` is finitely presented by `V_d`, (C) and (S)
   alone. Every relator of `G` then dies after finitely many free splittings.
3. **Invariance.** Whether `N` is a finitely generated lift ideal does not depend on the finite self-similar
   generating set `S`, since `V_d(G)` does not.
4. **Decidability of `K_∞`.** Word-level sections of `w` have length at most `|w|`, so the set of reduced words
   reachable from `w` is finite. Then `w in K_∞` iff every reachable word has trivial root permutation and the
   section graph on the nonempty reachable words is acyclic. `w in N` iff every reachable word has trivial root
   permutation. Both are implemented in `experiments/nonc-rn-fp-2026-09-17/autom.py`.

**First application.** `rover-nekrashevych-group-of-lamplighter-automaton-is-fp`: for the lamplighter automaton
`a = σ(a, b)`, `b = (a, b)`, the group `G ≅ Z/2 ≀ Z` is neither finitely presented nor contracting, and
`N = K_∞`. So `V_2(Z/2 ≀ Z)` is finitely presented, which neither FP1 nor FP2 covers.
