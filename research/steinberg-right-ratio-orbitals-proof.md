Every matrix in the first-five-coordinate actor acts on `M=B^5` by a
right-`B`-linear map: associativity gives

```text
h(mb)=(hm)b.                                             (RRP1)
```

In particular, if `hv=v`, then `(RRP1)` gives `(RRO2)` for every `b`.

Suppose one ordered `H`-orbit contains both `(v,vb)` and `(v,vc)`.  Choose
`h_1,h_2 in H` carrying the same prototype pair to these two points.  Then
`h=h_2 h_1^(-1)` fixes `v` and carries `vb` to `vc`.  But `(RRO2)` says
`h(vb)=vb`, so `vb=vc`.  Comparing first coordinates and using that `A` is
the identity of `B` gives `b=c`.  This proves that each ordered orbital
meets `(RRO3)` at most once.  An unordered orbital is the union of at most
two ordered orbitals, proving the corresponding bound two.

The active corner `B` is infinite.  For example its binary Leavitt words
`s_0^n` are nonzero and distinct: `t_0^n s_0^n=A`, while the natural Leavitt
grading places them in different degrees.  Remove the finitely many `b`
whose pair `{v,vb}` lies in one of the finitely many unordered orbitals
generated by `P`, and also remove `0,A`.  An admissible `b` remains.

The graph defining `K_P` is `H`-invariant by construction, so `H` acts on
`K_P` by permuting its vertex groups and the semidirect product `(RRO4)` is
defined.  Each vertex has order `p`, and every checked commutator is an edge
relation.  The vertices `v` and `vb` are distinct and nonadjacent, hence the
standard graph-product normal form makes their commutator nontrivial.  This
proves `(RRO5)`.

Notice the exact scope.  Replacing the graph product by the elementary
abelian group `M` would additionally impose every additive triangle.  The
argument shows that commutator relations and actor transport do not force
that replacement; it does not claim that `(RRO4)` is a representation of
the full Steinberg presentation.  Indeed exact finite-dimensional
representations of that full presentation kill the active mark.  The
remaining possible source of a normalized-HS decoder is therefore the
quantitative interaction of same-root addition with mixed root
multiplication, not actor transitivity.
