Notation as in `kms-hc1-reflected-root-word-is-nontrivial`: `[x,y]=x^-1y^-1xy`,
left-normed, and `Gamma_p=<a,b,c | a^p,b^p,c^p,[a,b,a],[a,b,b],[b,c,b],[b,c,c],
[a,c,a],[a,c,c,a],[a,c,c,c]>`. Let `F` be any group, `A,B,C in F` a triple
satisfying these relators, and put

```text
R=[B,A],  S=[B,C],  H=<R,S>,  w=(RS)^p.
```

Let `Delta_p=<x,y | x^p,y^p,[x,y,x],[x,y,y,x],[x,y,y,y]>`. This is the vertex
group `<a,c>` of `Gamma_p`, i.e. the positive unipotent group of type `B_2`
over `F_p`.

## 0. Two standard facts

**(F1)** Suppose `t=[x,y]` commutes with `x`. Then `[x^n,y]=t^n` for every
`n>=0`. Proof: `[x^{n+1},y]=[x,y]^{x^n}[x^n,y]=t\cdot t^n`.

**(F2)** Let `K` be a nilpotent group of class `<p` generated by elements of
order dividing `p`. Then `K` has exponent `p`.
Proof: `K` is nilpotent and generated by finitely many torsion elements, so it
is a finite `p`-group. A finite `p`-group of class `<p` is regular (P. Hall),
and in a regular `p`-group the elements of order dividing `p` form a subgroup
`Omega_1`. That subgroup contains the generators, so `Omega_1=K`.

## 1. Lemma (the reflected pair)

`R^p=S^p=1`, and `B` commutes with `R` and with `S`.

Proof. `[A,B]` commutes with `A` and `B`. By (F1), `[A,B]^p=[A^p,B]=1`, and
`R=[A,B]^-1`. `[B,R]=1` holds by the relator `[a,b,b]`. The same argument
applies to `S` and the pair `(b,c)`.

## 2. Theorem A (regularity gate)

**(A1)** If `H` is nilpotent of class `<p`, then `w=1`.

**(A2)** Let `p>=5`. If `[R,S,R]=[R,S,S,R]=[R,S,S,S]=1`, i.e. `x->R, y->S`
defines a homomorphism `Delta_p -> F`, then `w=1`.

**(A3)** If `F` is nilpotent of class `<=2p-1`, then `w=1`. In particular,
every nilpotent quotient of `Gamma_p` of class `<=2p-1` kills `w_p`.

Proof.

- (A1) is (F2) combined with Lemma 1.
- (A2): it suffices to show that `Delta_p` has class `<=3`. Put `t=[x,y]` and
  `u=[t,y]`. The relators say `[t,x]=1`, `[u,x]=1` and `[u,y]=1`, so `u` is
  central. Then `t^x=t` and `t^y=tu`. Hence the normal closure of `t` is the
  abelian group `<t,u>`, `gamma_3(Delta_p)=<u>` and `gamma_4(Delta_p)=1`.
  Since `3<p`, (A1) applies to the image `H` of `Delta_p`. (By (F1), `t^p=1`
  and `u^p=1`, so `|Delta_p|<=p^4`.)
- (A3): `R,S in gamma_2(F)`. From `[gamma_i F,gamma_j F]<=gamma_{i+j}F` we get
  `gamma_i(H)<=gamma_{2i}(F)` by induction, so `gamma_p(H)<=gamma_{2p}(F)=1`.
  Apply (A1).

**Sharpness at `p=3`.** The 3-quotient computation
(`experiments/kms-coprime-extensions-2026-09-17/pq.out`) gives:

```text
p=3 class<=5 |Q|=3^19 nilclass=5 w order 1
p=3 class<=6 |Q|=3^31 nilclass=6 w order 3
```

So `w_3` is detected first in class `6=2p`, exactly at the bound of (A3). At
the same class, `[r,s,r]` and `[s,r,s]` become nontrivial and `rs` gets order
9 (`refl3.out`). The gate opens precisely when the reflected `B_2` relators
fail. (A2) needs `p>=5` because `Delta_3` has class `3=p`.

**What Theorem A kills.** Two dead families on the hole are instances of (A2)
and (A1):

- **Positive Kac--Moody images.** `r,s` map into a finite unipotent group of
  class `<p`.
- **Block matrices `I+E_12(X), I+E_23(Y), I+E_31(Z)`.** `H` lies in `I+N`
  with `N^3=0`, of class `<=2`.

Any detecting finite image must make `H` fail both (A1) and (A2). In a
`p`-group this forces nilpotency class `>=2p`.

## 3. Theorem B (root-element images)

Let `Phi` be a reduced irreducible finite root system and `k` a field of
characteristic `p>=7`. Let `G` be a Chevalley group `G_Phi(k)` of any isogeny
type, with root subgroups `X_delta={x_delta(t)}`. We use three standard facts
(Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley groups*): the Chevalley commutator
formula

```text
[x_alpha(t),x_beta(u)] = prod_{i,j>=1, i alpha + j beta in Phi} x_{i alpha+j beta}(c_ij t^i u^j)
```

for `alpha != -beta`, with `c_11=±(q+1)` (where `q` is maximal with
`beta-q alpha in Phi`) and all `c_ij in {±1,±2,±3}`; `<X_alpha,X_-alpha>` is a
quotient of `SL_2(k)` by a central subgroup; and for a set `D` of roots that is
closed under addition within `Phi` and lies in an open half-plane, the product
`prod_{delta in D} X_delta` (in any fixed order) is a subgroup in which every
element has a unique expression. The unique-expression fact is what makes the
"`!= 1`" conclusions below valid, so the theorem is stated only for Chevalley
groups, not for arbitrary groups satisfying the commutator formula.

Suppose `A=x_alpha(t_1)`, `B=x_beta(t_2)`, `C=x_gamma(t_3)` with `t_i != 0`
satisfy the HC1 relators. Then `(RS)^p=1`.

Proof. All `c_ij` are nonzero in `k`, since `p>=5`.

**Step 1: `alpha != -beta` and `beta != -gamma`.** `<A,B>` is a quotient of
the Heisenberg group of order `p^3`, so it is a `p`-group. A finite `p`-subgroup
of a central quotient of `SL_2(k)` in characteristic `p` lifts (since `p` is
odd and the centre has order `<=2`) to a finite `p`-subgroup of `SL_2(k)`, which
is conjugate into the upper unitriangular group and so fixes a point of
`P^1(k)`; a nontrivial unipotent element fixes exactly one point. But
`x_alpha(t_1)` and `x_-alpha(t_2)` are nontrivial and fix the distinct points
`infty` and `0`. The same argument applies to `(B,C)`.

**Step 2: `R` is `1` or a single root element.**

- If `alpha=beta`, then `R=1`.
- **Descent fact.** Let `delta=i alpha+j beta in Phi` with `i,j>=1` and
  `delta != alpha,beta`. Since `(delta,delta)=i(delta,alpha)+j(delta,beta)>0`,
  one of `(delta,alpha),(delta,beta)` is positive, so `delta-alpha` or
  `delta-beta` is a root (or zero, excluded by non-proportionality). Hence every
  such `delta` of height `i+j=m>=3` has a root of the same form of height `m-1`
  below it, and every `delta` of height `>=2` descends to `alpha+beta`.
- If `alpha+beta` is not a root, then by the descent fact no `i alpha+j beta`
  with `i,j>=1` is a root, so `R=1` by the commutator formula.
- Otherwise put `D={i alpha+j beta in Phi : i,j>=1}` and let `V_m` be the
  product of the `X_delta` with `delta in D` of height `i+j>=m`. The commutator
  formula gives `[V_m,X_alpha] <= V_{m+1}` and `[V_m,X_beta] <= V_{m+1}`.
  Modulo `V_3`, `R=x_{alpha+beta}(±c_11 t_1t_2)`.
- Suppose `2alpha+beta in Phi`. Then modulo `V_4`,

  ```text
  [R,A] = x_{2alpha+beta}(c' c_11 t_1^2 t_2) != 1,
  ```

  where `c'` is the structure constant for `(alpha+beta, alpha)`. This
  contradicts `[a,b,a]=1`. Symmetrically, `alpha+2beta in Phi` contradicts
  `[a,b,b]=1`.
- Hence `D` has no element of height 3, and by the descent fact none of
  height `>=3`. So `D={alpha+beta}`, `V_3=1`, and `R=x_theta(u)` with `theta=alpha+beta` and
  `u != 0`. Likewise `S=1` or `S=x_phi(v)` with `phi=beta+gamma` and `v != 0`.

If `R=1` or `S=1`, then `(RS)^p` is `S^p` or `R^p`, which is `1`. So assume
both are root elements.

**Step 3: `phi != -theta` (the exact failing step).** Suppose
`phi=-theta=-alpha-beta`. The relator `[b,c,b]` says `S=x_{-alpha-beta}(v)`
commutes with `B=x_beta(t_2)`. The roots `beta` and `-alpha-beta` are not
proportional, and their sum `-alpha` is a root. By the filtration argument of
Step 2 for the pair `(beta,-alpha-beta)`,

```text
[x_beta(t_2), x_{-alpha-beta}(v)] = x_{-alpha}(c'' t_2 v) mod higher terms, with c'' != 0.
```

This is `!= 1`, a contradiction.

**Step 4: the product.**

- If `theta=phi`, then `RS in X_theta`, which has exponent `p`.
- Otherwise `theta` and `phi` are non-proportional. Let `Psi=Phi cap
  (Z theta+Z phi)`, a rank-2 root system. Choose a linear form `f` with
  `f(theta),f(phi)>0` that does not vanish on `Psi`. Then
  `Psi^+={f>0}` is a positive system, and `U=prod_{delta in Psi^+} X_delta` is
  a nilpotent group of class at most the height of the highest root of `Psi`,
  which is `<=5`. So `H<=U` has class `<=5<p` and is generated by the elements
  `R,S` of order `p`. By (F2), `(RS)^p=1`.

**Remark (where the loophole must live).** In a Kac--Moody root system,
Step 4 fails exactly when `{theta,phi}` is not prenilpotent: then
`<X_theta,X_phi>=X_theta * X_phi`. This is how `w_p` survives in `Gamma_p`
and in `Gamma^A2_p` (`kms-hc1-root-word-infinite-in-a2-quotient`). A finite
root system has no non-prenilpotent pairs other than `{theta,-theta}`, and
Step 3 excludes that pair. So a detecting finite image cannot send `a,b,c` to
root elements of any Chevalley group over a field of characteristic `p>=7`.
Twisted groups of Lie type are not covered by this argument.

## 4. Proposition C (abelian layers over a finite image)

Let `rho_0:Gamma_p->G_0` be finite with `rho_0(w_p)=1`, and let `N=ker rho_0`.
Then the following are equivalent:

1. some finite image `F` of `Gamma_p` with an abelian normal subgroup `M` and
   `F/M=G_0` (compatibly with `rho_0`) detects `w_p`;
2. the class `[w_p]` is nonzero in `N^ab`.

For `M` of exponent `l` coprime to `|G_0|`, detection happens exactly when
`[w_p]` is not in `l N^ab`. It is witnessed by the explicit affine lift
`a->(rho_0 a,0)`, `c->(rho_0 c,0)`, `b->(rho_0 b,(rho_0 b-1)x)`. Here
`x in M^a`, `y in M^c` and `x-y in M^b`, and the translation part of `w` is
`N_g(R-1)(x-y)` with `g=rho_0(rs)` and `N_g=1+g+...+g^{p-1}`.

Proof.

- (1)⇔(2). The finite images `F` with such an `M` are the quotients
  `Gamma_p/N_0` with `[N,N]<=N_0<=N` and `N/N_0` finite. `N^ab` is finitely
  generated, hence residually finite.
- The coprime statement and the lift. By inflation-restriction and
  `H^i(G_0,M)=0` for `i>0`, `H^1(Gamma_p;M)=Hom_{G_0}(N^ab,M)`.
- The cochain formula. A cocycle with `m_ac=0` is determined by the vertex
  translations. Differentiating `w=(rs)^p` gives `delta(rs)=(RS-1)y+(R-1)(x-y)`,
  and then `delta(w)=N_g(R-1)(x-y)`, using that `(RS)^p=1` in `G_0`.

**Metabelian images.** For `F` metabelian, `H<=[F,F]` is abelian of exponent
`p`, so `w=1`. The corrected computation `N^ab=(Z/p)^7` for
`N=ker(Gamma_p->C_p^3)` with `p=3,5,7` (`kerab.out`) agrees with this.

## 5. Computations (runnable; `experiments/kms-coprime-extensions-2026-09-17/`)

**(C1) 5-quotients** (`pq.g`, `pq.out`). `w_5` is trivial in the maximal
5-quotient of 5-class `c` for every `c<=12`, with `|Q|=5^286` at `c=12`. This
rules out detection of `w_5` in every 5-group of 5-class `<=12`, which goes
three classes beyond Theorem A3.

**(C2) 7-quotients** (`pq7.g`, `pq7.out`). `w_7` is trivial in the maximal
7-quotient of 7-class `c` for `c=12,13,14`, with `|Q|=7^274, 7^432, 7^685`.
Classes `<=13` are covered by A3, so they serve as a consistency check. Class
14 is the first class beyond A3, and there `w_7=1` as well: every 7-group of
7-class `<=14` kills `w_7`.

**(C3) Reflected relators** (`refl.g`, `refl.out`, `refl3.g`, `refl3.out`).

- For `p=5` and 5-class `4..10`: `[r,s,r]=[r,s,s,r]=[r,s,s,s]=1`, while
  `[s,r,s]` has order 5 and `rs` has order 5. The pair `(r,s)` satisfies the
  `Delta_5` relators (with `r` in the role of `a`) but not the transposed ones,
  so it does not collapse.
- For `p=3`: all trivial through 3-class 5. At class 6, `[r,s,r]` and
  `[s,r,s]` have order 3, `w` has order 3 and `rs` has order 9.

**(C4) Commuting-edge quotient** (`gp2.g`, `gp2.out`). For
`Gamma'_p=Gamma_p/<<[a,c]>>` with `p=3,5,7`, the maximal `p`-quotient
stabilises at order `p^6` and nilpotency class 3, which is `U_4(F_p)`. In it
`rs` has order `p` and `w=1`. So every `p`-group image with `[A,C]=1` kills
`w_p`.

**(C5) Serre algebra** (`serre.py`, `serre_q{3,5,7}.out`). In the Serre
algebra of the non-symmetrizable HC1 matrix over `F_q`, take `x=[e_b,e_a]` and
`y=[e_b,e_c]`. The dimension of the Lie span in bidegree `(i,j)` modulo the
Serre ideal is:

| field | (1,1) | (1,2) | (2,1) | degree 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `F_5`, `F_7` | 1 | 1 | 0 | all 0 |
| `F_3` | 1 | 1 | 1 | all 1 |

The associated graded Lie algebra of the lower central series of `Gamma_p` is
a quotient of this algebra. So for `p=5,7` the initial forms of `[r,s,r]`,
`[r,s,s,r]` and `[r,s,s,s]` vanish (for example `[r,s,r] in gamma_7`), while
for `p=3` they do not.

**(C6) Coprime abelian layers over `U_4(F_p)`** (`coprime_u4.py`,
`coprime_p{3,5,7}.out`). Take `rho_0:a,b,c->x_12,x_23,x_34`. Every irreducible
`F_l U_4(F_p)`-module is a quotient of some `Ind_A chi`, where
`A={[[1,X],[0,1]]}`. Testing all `p^4` characters `chi` gives no detecting
module for `(p,l)=(3,7),(3,13),(5,11),(5,31),(7,29)`. By Proposition C,
`[w_p]` lies in `l N^ab` for these `l`.

**(C7) GAP pitfall (affects other lanes).** Let `H` be a finite-index subgroup
of an fp group given by `PreImage` or `Kernel`, and `q:=MaximalAbelianQuotient(H)`.
In GAP 4 at this site, `Image(q,w)` returned nonzero classes for words `w`
that are trivial in `H^ab`. The counterexample: `p=5`,
`S=<x12,x13,x34,x14> <= U_4(F_5)`, where `w` acts trivially on the cosets of
`ker q` but `q(w)=f1*f4*f5^2*f6^2*f7^2`. Route through
`IsomorphismFpGroup(H)` before `MaximalAbelianQuotient`, and confirm with
`FactorCosetAction`.

**(C8) Complex representations in dimension `p+1`** (`repsearch.py`,
`rep_p7_n8.out`; numerical, not decisive). Fix `A` as a diagonal matrix with
spectrum all 7th roots plus `1`, and conjugate it to get `B` and `C`. Impose
the relators together with `tr[B,A]=tr[B,C]=7 zeta+1`. Levenberg--Marquardt
finds a solution with residual `1e-14` and `[A,C] != I`, and in it
`(RS)^7=I` with RS eigenvalues `{1, zeta^2 (x7)}`. This cross-characteristic
family has not produced a detection. The unipotent case, where `RS` has all
eigenvalues `1`, was not searched.

## 6. Open: Conjecture RB and the gate

**Conjecture RB.** For `p>=5`, the reflected relators `[r,s,r]`, `[r,s,s,r]`
and `[r,s,s,s]` lie in the kernel of `Gamma_p -> hat Gamma_p^(p)`. By (A2) this
implies that every finite `p`-group image kills `w_p`. Evidence: C1, C3 and C5.
Falsifier: one `p`-quotient where one of the three is nontrivial.

The remaining loophole for the hole is a finite image where `<R,S>` is neither
a quotient of `Delta_p` nor nilpotent of class `<p`. By Theorem B, it cannot be
a root-element image of a Chevalley group in characteristic `p>=7`. The
natural place to look is cross-characteristic images, where `R` and `S` are
semisimple of order `p` and `RS` need not be unipotent.
