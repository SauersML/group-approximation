Let `M` be an infinite elementary abelian two-group, let `T subset M` be
finite, and let two distinct characters `chi_0,chi_1` agree on `T`.  Put
equal-rank spectral blocks on those characters and let `S` exchange the two
blocks.  Then every `rho(m)`, `m in T`, is scalar on their sum, and hence

```text
[S,rho(m)]=0                    for every m in T.        (FCM1)
```

It follows that **every** finite noncommutative word formed from `S,S^*`
and the tested module evaluations reduces on this carrier to the same word
with scalar module coefficients.  In particular every conjugation-balanced
mixed covariance check--arbitrary products, commutators, sums of squares, or
matrix amplifications built from

```text
S rho(m) S^*, rho(m),       m in T,
```

has exactly the same value as on a correctly transported block, while the
fine two-atom transport energy is `2`.

Thus a noncommutative mixed-word square function does not evade the finite
restriction obstruction merely by inserting the actor transporter between
diagonal module words.  It must additionally authenticate the two domains
using relations not contained in the finite tested module algebra--most
naturally other actor generators plus a matrix-only rigidity theorem.

The claim is marginal: it does not assert that the swap extends to a
representation of a property-`(T)` actor.  That extension is exactly where
a surviving positive argument must use actor relations.
