**Read at source, 09-18:**
- Ara–Li–Lledó–Wu, arXiv:1607.00328v3 (Bull. Math. Sci. 2018).
  - Definition 6.1: the translation algebra `K_u(X)` is the unital algebra generated by the operators `V_t` of
    the partial translations `t`. Over a finite field it is exactly the algebra of finite-propagation matrices
    `C_u[X; k]` of `linear-gate-u-reduces-to-algebraic-uniform-roe-embeddings`.
  - Theorem 6.3: for locally finite `X` the following are equivalent:
    - `X` is amenable;
    - `K_u(X)` is algebraically amenable;
    - `K_u(X)` is not properly infinite;
    - `K_u(X)` contains no unital copy of `L_K(1,n)`.
- Brownlowe–Sørensen, arXiv:1603.03618 (J. Algebra 2016).
  - For a field `K`, whether `L_(2,K) ⊗ L_(2,K)` embeds in `L_(2,K)` is stated as an open problem of the
    subject; it is listed on the Graph Algebra Problem Page.
  - They show that `L_(2,Z) ⊗ L_(2,Z)` has no unital *-embedding into `L_(2,Z)`.
  - They also rule out, over any `R`, unital *-embeddings sending `u ⊗ v` into Thompson's `V` for full-spectrum
    unitaries `u, v`.
  - Over a field, every embedding into `L_(2,K)` can be made unital, because all nonzero idempotents of
    `L_(2,K)` are equivalent.

`K` is a field. `L = L_K(1,2)` with `y_i x_j = δ_ij` and `x_1y_1 + x_2y_2 = 1`, and `φ(a) = x_1 a y_1 + x_2 a y_2`.

## Theorem 1 (bilateral shift: R_1 ↪ L ⊗ L)

`Γ = 1` gives `Δ = C(1) = Z` and
`R_1 = LC(2^Z, K) ⋊ (Z/2 ≀ Z) ≅ (⊗_(k∈Z) M_2(K)) ⋊ Z`, with `Z` shifting the factors. Put
`u = x_1 ⊗ y_1 + x_2 ⊗ y_2` and `u^(-1) = y_1 ⊗ x_1 + y_2 ⊗ x_2`. Then the following define a unital embedding
`R_1 ↪ L ⊗ L`:
- site `0` of `M_2^(⊗Z)` goes to `1 ⊗ span{x_p y_q}`;
- the shift goes to `u`.

**Proof.**
1. `u` is a unit: `u·u^(-1) = Σ_i x_i y_i ⊗ 1 = 1` and `u^(-1)·u = 1 ⊗ Σ_i x_i y_i = 1`.
2. Compute the conjugates of the site-0 matrix units:
   - `u(1 ⊗ x_p y_q)u^(-1) = Σ_(i,j) x_i y_j ⊗ y_i x_p y_q x_j = x_p y_q ⊗ 1`;
   - `u^(-1)(1 ⊗ b)u = 1 ⊗ φ(b)` and `u(a ⊗ 1)u^(-1) = φ(a) ⊗ 1`.
3. So for `e ∈ M_2 = span{x_p y_q}`:
   - `u^k(1 ⊗ e)u^(-k) = φ^(k−1)(e) ⊗ 1` for `k ≥ 1`;
   - `u^(-k)(1 ⊗ e)u^k = 1 ⊗ φ^k(e)` for `k ≥ 0`.
4. These unital copies of `M_2` pairwise commute:
   - within one leg they are distinct sites `φ^j(M_2)` of the UHF core, and `φ(L)` is the commutant of
     `span{x_p y_q}`;
   - across the two legs they commute trivially.
5. Commuting unital copies of `M_2(K)` induce a unital map `M_2^(⊗Z) → L ⊗ L`, and `u` implements the shift.
   So there is a unital map from the algebraic crossed product, which is universal for covariant pairs.
6. `R_1` is simple. Its groupoid is minimal and effective, by the proof of Theorem B in the required node. So
   this nonzero unital map is injective. ∎

Consequences:
- `L ⊗ L ⊆ LC(X_par × X_par) ⋊ (F_2 × F_2) ⊆ K_u(F_2 × F_2)`, by Theorem 1(a) of the absorb node, applied in
  each leg.
- So **`R_1` embeds in the translation algebra of `F_2 × F_2`** by an explicit Leavitt formula with no
  `ℓ^∞`-coefficients, over every field.
- `u` is the algebraic form of the bilateral shift on `O_2 ⊗ O_2`.

## Theorem 2 (no monomial module of K[Z²] over a tree)

Let `X` be a locally finite tree of bounded degree, or any bounded-geometry graph with `sep_X(m) = o(√m)`.

Call a module over `X` **monomial** for a set of units if:
- it has a basis `B` and an at most `n`-to-1 map `ι : B → X`;
- each of the units acts by a permutation of `B`, with no scalars (over `F_2` this covers every monomial matrix);
- each of these permutations has displacement at most `R` along `ι`.

Then `K[Z²] = K[s^(±1), t^(±1)]` has no faithful module over `X` that is monomial for `s` and `t`.

**Proof.**
1. `Z²` permutes `B`. For an orbit `O` with stabilizer `H`, the kernel of `K[Z²] → End(K·O)` contains the
   augmentation ideal `I(H) = (h − 1 : h ∈ H)`.
2. The orbit's Schreier graph for `{s, t}` maps into `X` with Lipschitz constant `R` and fibres at most `n`, so
   the map is regular.
3. The Schreier graph of `Z²/H` contains an embedded grid ball of radius `(r(H) − 1)/2`, where `r(H)` is the length
   of a shortest nonzero vector of `H` (∞ for `H = 1`). Grid balls of radius `ρ` have separation `≍ ρ` on
   `≍ ρ²` vertices.
4. Separation is monotone under regular maps (Benjamini–Schramm–Timár, recalled). With `sep_X(m) = o(√m)`,
   `r(H) ≤ C(R, n, X)` for every orbit, so `H ≠ 1` contains some `(a, b) ≠ 0` with `|a| + |b| ≤ C`.
5. Let `f = Π (s^a t^b − 1)` over the finitely many `(a, b) ≠ 0` with `|a| + |b| ≤ C`. Then `f ≠ 0`, since
   `K[Z²]` is a domain, and `f ∈ I(H)` for every orbit. So `f` acts as zero, and the module is not faithful. ∎

**Corollaries.**
1. **`R_Γ` over one tree must be non-monomial.**
   - Let `Γ` have an element `γ` of infinite order, for example `Γ = Z`. The levels `0` and `1` of `Δ = C(Γ)`
     give `Z² ≅ ⟨δ_0(γ), σδ_0(γ)σ^(-1)⟩ ≤ Δ`, and `K[Z²] ⊆ K[Δ] ⊆ R_Γ`.
   - Take any unital embedding of `R_Γ` into a coarsely dense corner of `M_n(K_u(F_2))`, the open gate.
   - Then the two lamp shifts are not both monomial over `F_2`. Every such embedding, and every Kirchberg-type one, is
     genuinely linear on the lamps.
2. **A constraint on the Brownlowe–Sørensen problem, over every field.**
   - Let `g ∈ V ⊆ L^x` have infinite order. Some tail-class orbit of `g` is infinite, so
     `K[⟨g⟩] ⊗ K[⟨g⟩] ≅ K[Z²]` sits in `L ⊗ L` as `⟨g ⊗ 1, 1 ⊗ g⟩`.
   - In a unital embedding `ψ : L ⊗ L → L`, the elements `ψ(g ⊗ 1)` and `ψ(1 ⊗ g)` are never both in `V`.
   - Reason: `V` acts monomially, with bounded displacement, on the orbit module `K[F_2]` of
     `L ⊆ LC(X_par) ⋊ F_2`, so Theorem 2 applies.
   - This is a companion to their `V`-obstruction for `u ⊗ v`. It holds for all embeddings, not only
     *-embeddings. Its hypothesis is different, and neither result implies the other.

## Theorem 3 (reductions for one tree)

1. **Corners are free.** Let `P` be non-amenable and `A ⊆ P × [n]` coarsely dense.
   - Hall's theorem, using the expansion of `P`, gives bounded-displacement injections `P → A` and `A → P`.
   - Schröder–Bernstein then gives a bounded-displacement bijection `P ≅ A`.
   - So `1_A M_n(K_u(P)) 1_A ≅ K_u(P)`, and the open gate reads simply "`R_Γ` embeds unitally in `K_u(F_2)`".
2. If `L ⊗ L` embeds unitally in `K_u(F_2)`, then so do `R_1` and `K[Z²]`, by Theorem 1. The hypothesis is
   implied by a positive answer to the Brownlowe–Sørensen problem over `F_2`, since `L ⊆ K_u(F_2)`.
3. By Corollary 2 above, such an embedding cannot send both legs' `V`-shifts into `V`.

## Lesson for general BH

The first canonical input `R_1`, the lamplighter crossed product, has an explicit algebraic Kirchberg-type
embedding over two trees: the bilateral shift `Σ x_i ⊗ y_i` in `L ⊗ L`. Moving from two trees to one is
precisely the algebraic form of `O_2 ⊗ O_2 ≅ O_2`. That form is open over fields (Brownlowe–Sørensen), and it
is provably not monomial.

So the exactness question for gate U now has a concrete first instance: `L_2 ⊗ L_2` versus the translation
algebra of one tree.
- A positive answer there, meaning a unital embedding into `K_u(F_2)`, gives the one-tree gate for `R_1` and
  for `K[Z²]`.
- Any proof must scramble commuting Thompson shifts into non-monomial units.
