## Setting

- **The space.** `X` is a Cantor space and `p ∈ X` a point.
- **The annuli.** `A_0, A_1, ...` are pairwise disjoint clopen sets with `{p} ∪ ⋃_{d >= D} A_d` a clopen
  neighbourhood basis of `p`.
- **The model and the contraction.** `θ_d : A -> A_d` are homeomorphisms from a model clopen `A`, and a
  homeomorphism `z`, defined near `p` and fixing it, satisfies `z θ_d = θ_(d+1)`. The standard example is
  `X = {0,1}^N`, `p = 0^∞`, `A_d = 0^d 1 C` and `z : 0w ↦ 00w`, a germ of `V`.
- **The machine.** `m` is a homeomorphism of `A` of infinite order. It may be any element of `nV` acting on the
  model, for instance a reversible Turing machine.
- **The block-power germ.** For `f : N -> Z`, `h_f` is `θ_d m^(f(d)) θ_d^(-1)` on each `A_d`, fixes `p`, and is
  the identity elsewhere. It is a homeomorphism, continuous at `p` because the annuli shrink to `p`.
- **The germ group.** `W_f` is the group of germs at `p` generated by the germs of `z` and `h_f`.

## Statement

1. **Structure.** `W_f = M ⋊ <z>`, where `M` is the abelian group of germs of `h_g`, for `g` in the `Z`-span
   of the shifts `f(· - k)`, `k ∈ Z`. `M` is torsion-free.
2. **Finite presentation forces a recurrence.** If `W_f` is finitely presented, there are integers
   `c_0, ..., c_r`, not all zero, and a sign `ε`, with `sum_j c_j f(d + ε j) = 0` for all large `d`.
3. **Exponential schedule.** Hence `|f(d)| <= C K^d` for constants `C, K`, where `K` can be taken to be
   `sum |c_j|`.
4. **Where it applies.** For `Γ = <V, h_f>` at `p = 0^∞` with the standard annuli, the germ group of `Γ` at `p`
   is `W_f`. So if `Γ` is certified finitely presented by Belk–Hyde–Matucci Theorem 2.1,
   `bhm-singfix-condition-forces-fp-germ-groups` makes `W_f` finitely presented, and items 2–3 apply.

## Proof

1. **Conjugation shifts the schedule.** `z^k h_f z^(-k)` equals `θ_d m^(f(d-k)) θ_d^(-1)` on `A_d`, by
   `z θ_d = θ_(d+1)`.
   - **They commute.** All `h_g` are powers of `m` on each annulus, so they commute and `h_g h_(g') = h_(g+g')`.
     The germ of `h_g` is trivial iff `m^(g(d)) = 1` for all large `d`, iff `g` is eventually `0`, since `m` has
     infinite order. So `M ≅ L / L_0`, with `L` the span of the shifts and `L_0` its eventually-zero part.
   - **Torsion-free.** If `n g` is eventually `0`, so is `g`.
   - **The semidirect product.** Elements of `M` preserve every `A_d`, while `z^k` with `k ≠ 0` moves `A_d` to
     `A_(d+k)`. So `M ∩ <z> = 1`, `M` is normal, and `W_f = M ⋊ <z>` with `π : W_f -> Z` sending `z ↦ 1`.
2. **Bieri–Strebel.** By `bieri-strebel-splitting-over-kernel-of-map-to-z`, `W_f` is an HNN extension with
   finitely generated base `H ≤ ker π = M`.
   - **It is ascending.** `W_f` is metabelian, so contains no free subgroup of rank 2, while a non-ascending HNN
     extension does (standard ping-pong). So the stable letter `t` satisfies `t H t^(-1) ⊆ H` or
     `t^(-1) H t ⊆ H`, and `M = ⋃_k t^(∓k) H t^(±k)`.
   - **Replace `t` by `z`.** `t = z^(±1) μ` with `μ ∈ M`, and `M` is abelian, so conjugation by `t` on `M` is
     conjugation by `z^(±1)`.
   - **A dependence.** `H ≅ Z^r` is finitely generated torsion-free. The class of `f` lies in some
     `t^(∓k_0) H t^(±k_0)`. So one of the families `{f(· - k) : k >= k_0}` or `{f(· + k) : k >= k_0}` lies in
     `H`, as germs. Any `r + 1` of its members are `Z`-linearly dependent, giving the relation of item 2.
3. **Solve for the extreme index.** Let `c_j` be the nonzero coefficient at the extreme index in the direction
   the relation runs. Solving the relation for that term expresses `f(n)`, for large `n`, as a rational
   combination of at most `r` earlier values, with coefficients `c_i / c_j` bounded by `sum |c_i|`. Induction
   gives `|f(n)| <= C K^n`.
4. Near `0^∞`, every germ of `V` is a power of the germ of `z`, and `h_f` contributes the germ of `h_f`. So the
   germ group of `Γ` at `p` is `W_f`.

## What it kills

This is Attempt 3 on `kourovka-7-19-explicit-fp-simple-group-with-non-pr-word-problem`.

- **The design.** Place a reversible machine `m` on the annuli around a singular point, and run it `f(d)` steps
  on the `d`-th annulus, with `f` an Ackermann-type diagonal, so that evaluating the host is not primitive
  recursive.
- **Why it fails.** With a finitely presented germ group, as Belk–Hyde–Matucci certification requires, `f` is at
  most exponential. A word of length `l` then reaches annuli of depth `O(l)`, where the schedule is at most
  `K^(O(l))`.
- **What that leaves the host.** This is a hand remark, not proved here. Evaluating such a word takes elementary
  time, and together with Birget-type witness bounds on the shallow annuli, so does the word problem. So the
  host stays primitive recursive.
- **The lesson.** An Ackermann schedule needs non-commuting germs, whose germ group is not metabelian.
  - For example: machines whose step counts depend on each other's outputs, or a hard finitely presented group
    realized as the singular germ group.
  - The latter is the shell route of `perfect-decidable-inputs-have-fp-shell-envelopes`, run on one explicit hard
    input.
