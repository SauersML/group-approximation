`Γ` is nontrivial, torsion-free, quotientless and Kazhdan. Since `Γ` is countable, choose Borel
representatives of `ρ(γ)`. The identities `ρ(γ)ρ(γ') = ρ(γγ')` fail only on a null set, so discard its
`ρ(Γ)`- and `R`-saturation, which is null because `R` has countable classes and preserves `μ`. After
this, `ρ` is a genuine Borel action by elements of `[R]`.

**Clause 1.**
1. Let `O` be a finite `ρ(Γ)`-orbit. The action on `O` is a homomorphism `Γ → Sym(O)`, so its image
   is a finite quotient of `Γ`, hence trivial.
2. So every point of `O` is fixed by every `ρ(γ)`.
3. Consequently `Supp ρ` is `ρ(Γ)`-invariant, and all its orbits are infinite.

**Clause 2.**
1. Let `Γ ↷ (Z, λ)` be ergodic p.m.p. and not weakly mixing. The Koopman representation on
   `L^2_0(Z)` then has a nonzero finite-dimensional invariant subspace `V`: weak mixing is equivalent
   to the absence of such subspaces.
2. The representation `π : Γ → U(V)` has finitely generated image, since `Γ` is finitely generated
   by (T). The image is a subgroup of `GL_n(C)`, so it is residually finite by Malcev.
3. A residually finite quotient of a quotientless group is trivial, so `π` is trivial.
4. Then `V` consists of `Γ`-invariant functions of mean zero. These vanish by ergodicity, a
   contradiction.
5. Strong ergodicity of ergodic actions of Kazhdan groups is Connes--Weiss (Schmidt, 1980--81).
6. A nontrivial ergodic action with discrete spectrum is not weakly mixing, so there is none.

**Clause 4.**
1. `ρ(Γ) ≤ [R] ≤ [R_G]`.
2. By `haagerup-hosts-blind-full-group-kazhdan-certificate`, clause 1 of that node, almost every
   `ρ(Γ)`-orbit is finite. Only property (T) of `Γ` is used there, not freeness of the `Γ`-action.
3. By clause 1 here, almost every point is fixed by all of `ρ(Γ)`. So `ρ(γ) = id` in `[R]` for every
   `γ`.

**Clause 3.**
1. Discard an `R`-invariant null set so that `R` is Borel hyperfinite.
2. Every hyperfinite countable Borel equivalence relation is generated by a Borel `Z`-action, by
   Slaman--Steel and Weiss (Dougherty--Jackson--Kechris 1994, Thm 5.1). Let `T` be a generating
   automorphism.
3. Split `X = X_fin ⊔ X_∞` into the `R`-invariant sets of points with finite and with infinite
   `R`-class. Both are `[R]`-invariant.
4. On `X_fin`, `ρ(Γ)`-orbits lie in finite `R`-classes, hence are finite, hence are fixed by clause 1.
5. On `X_∞`, every `T`-orbit is infinite, so `Z` acts freely there. `R|X_∞ = R_T` is the orbit
   relation of an essentially free action of the Haagerup group `Z`, and clause 4 gives
   `ρ(Γ)|X_∞ = 1`.

**Clause 5.**
1. **Cocycle.** Define `c(γ, y)` as the unique `h ∈ H` with `θ(γy) = h·θ(y)`.
   - This is well defined a.e. by containment and essential freeness of `H ↷ X`.
   - It is measurable, since the sets `{y : θ(γy) = hθ(y)}` are measurable.
   - It satisfies the cocycle identity `c(γ_1γ_2, y) = c(γ_1, γ_2y)c(γ_2, y)` a.e., by uniqueness.

   This is step 1 of `simple-kazhdan-bernoulli-host-via-popa-superrigidity`, verbatim.
2. **Untwisting.** `Γ` is an infinite normal subgroup of itself with property (T), hence w-rigid.
   `β` is s-malleable and weakly mixing, since `Γ` is infinite. `H` is countable discrete, hence of
   finite type.
   - By `popa-bernoulli-cocycle-superrigidity`, there are a homomorphism `δ : Γ → H` and a measurable
     `φ : Y → H` with `c(γ, y) = φ(γy)δ(γ)φ(y)^(-1)` a.e.
   - Put `ψ(y) = φ(y)^(-1)·θ(y)`. Then `ψ(γy) = φ(γy)^(-1)c(γ,y)θ(y) = δ(γ)ψ(y)` a.e.
   - Note `ψ(y) ∈ H·θ(y)`.
3. **The kernel is finite.** Let `N = ker δ`, which is normal, and suppose `N` is infinite.
   - The restriction of `β` to `N` is the Bernoulli shift of `N` with base `[0,1]^(N\Γ)`, so it is
     mixing, hence ergodic.
   - `ψ` is `N`-invariant, hence essentially constant, equal to some `x_0`.
   - Then `θ(y) ∈ H·x_0` for a.e. `y`, so the countable set `H·x_0` carries all of `μ_A`.
   - But `μ` has no atoms. `H` is infinite, since it contains infinite `Γ`-orbits inside its orbits.
     So by essential freeness an atom would have an infinite orbit of atoms of equal mass.
   - This is a contradiction, so `N` is finite.
4. **The kernel is trivial.** A finite subgroup of the torsion-free group `Γ` is trivial. So `δ` is
   injective.

**Clause 6.**
1. If `H` is residually finite, then so is `δ(Γ) ≅ Γ`. A residually finite quotientless group is
   trivial, contradicting `Γ ≠ 1`.
2. Finitely generated linear groups are residually finite (Malcev).
3. An infinite Kazhdan subgroup of an amenable or Haagerup group does not exist. Kazhdan subgroups of
   Haagerup groups are finite, and `Γ` is infinite, being torsion-free and nontrivial.

∎
