Let

```text
B = C<y_1,...,y_m,y_1^*,...,y_m^*>/(f_1,...,f_s)
```

be a finitely presented unital star algebra, and let

```text
Gamma=<X | R>
```

be an arbitrary group presentation (the sets `X,R` may be infinite).  Suppose
that `q,A_1,...,A_m` are finite sums of words in `X` and that in `C[Gamma]`

```text
0 != q=q^*=q^2,
q A_i q=A_i,
f_j(A_1,...,A_m,A_1^*,...,A_m^*)=0                 (FDC1)
```

for every `i,j`.  Then there are finite sets `X_0 subset X` and
`R_0 subset R`, containing every letter and every relator needed below, such
that all the identities `(FDC1)` already hold in

```text
Gamma_0=<X_0 | R_0>,
```

and the same group-ring element `q` is nonzero in `C[Gamma_0]`.
Consequently the assignment `y_i -> A_i` already defines a unital star
homomorphism

```text
B -> q C[Gamma_0] q.                                (FDC2)
```

In particular, if `B` is the finite constraint algebra of a no-CE-trace BCS,
then any successful finite-support corner realization in an effective
recursive signed-Hecke host already occurs in a finitely presented group.
An infinite sequence of auxiliary relators, wrong-type eliminators, or
Bass--Serre packet stages cannot be essential to the exact realization.

This does **not** say that arbitrary representation-theoretic properties of
recursive presentations are finitely witnessed.  Infinite relator families
can eliminate finite-dimensional representations noncompactly.  The result
applies here because the desired signed-Hecke output is a finite algebraic
diagram: a finite presentation of `B`, a finite-support corner, and finitely
many finite-support generator images.

For the first-cycle chord this leaves exactly the previous alternatives.  A
recursive tower may be useful as a search or bookkeeping device, but if its
limit closes the fixed finite BCS atlas then one finite stage already closes
it while preserving `q`.  Hence the load-bearing stage must itself solve the
finite coherent holonomy/term-pairing problem; recursive wrong-type
elimination does not bypass the affine finite-edge or finite-support fences.
