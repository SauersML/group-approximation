**Literature input, quoted from the PDF.** V. Nekrashevych, *Simple groups of
dynamical origin*, arXiv:1511.08241v2 (7 Feb 2017). Read from the arXiv PDF,
pages 1–14, on 2026-09-13.

- Standing assumptions (p. 3): "All groupoids in this paper are étale (see
  below) and the unit space 𝔊^(0) is homeomorphic to the Cantor set." Topological
  groupoids are not assumed Hausdorff.
- Minimal (p. 3): "A groupoid 𝔊 is said to be minimal if all its orbits are dense
  in 𝔊^(0)." Restriction (p. 3): "For U ⊂ 𝔊^(0) we denote by 𝔊|_U the subgroupoid
  {γ ∈ 𝔊 : s(γ), r(γ) ∈ U}".
- Definition 2.1: "A 𝔊-bisection is a compact open subset F ⊂ 𝔊 such that
  s : F → s(F) and r : F → r(F) are homeomorphisms."
- Definition 2.2: "An étale groupoid 𝔊 is said to be a groupoid of germs (or
  effective) if for every non-unit element γ ∈ 𝔊 and every bisection F containing
  γ, there exists δ ∈ F such that s(δ) ≠ r(δ)."
- Definition 2.3: the full group F(𝔊) "is the set of bisections F ⊂ 𝔊 such that
  s(F) = r(F) = 𝔊^(0)."
- Definition 3.1: "A multisection of degree d is a collection of d² bisections
  {F_{i,j}}_{i,j=1}^d such that (1) F_{i2,i3}F_{i1,i2} = F_{i1,i3} for all
  1 ≤ i1, i2, i3 ≤ d; (2) the bisections F_{i,i} are disjoint subsets of 𝔊^(0)."
- p. 6: "Let ℱ = {F_{i,j}}_{i,j=1}^d be a multisection with the domain U, and let
  π ∈ S_d be a permutation ... Then ⋃_{i=1}^d F_{i,π(i)} ∪ (𝔊^(0) \ U) is an element
  of F(𝔊), which we will denote ℱ_π. It is easy to see that π ↦ ℱ_π is an embedding
  of S_d into F(𝔊). Let us denote by S(ℱ) the image of this embedding, and denote by
  A(ℱ) the image of the alternating subgroup A_d < S_d."
- Definition 3.2: "Denote by S_d(𝔊) (resp. A_d(𝔊)) the subgroup of F(𝔊) generated
  by the union of the subgroups S(ℱ) (resp. A(ℱ)) for all multisections ℱ of degree
  d. Denote S(𝔊) = S_2(𝔊) and A(𝔊) = A_3(𝔊)."
- Theorem 4.1 (= Theorem 1.1): "Suppose that 𝔊 is a minimal groupoid of germs.
  Then every non-trivial subgroup of F(𝔊) normalized by A(𝔊) contains A(𝔊). In
  particular, A(𝔊) is simple and is contained in every non-trivial normal subgroup
  of F(𝔊)."
- Theorem 5.6: "Suppose that 𝔊 is expansive and every 𝔊-orbit has at least 5
  points. Then the group A(𝔊) is finitely generated."
- Proposition 5.5: for a finitely generated group G acting on a Cantor set 𝒳 with
  groupoid of germs 𝔊, "(2) The groupoid 𝔊 is expansive" is equivalent to "(3) The
  action of G on 𝒳 is expansive, i.e., there exists a neighborhood W ⊂ 𝒳 × 𝒳 of the
  diagonal such that if x, y ∈ 𝒳 are such that (g(x), g(y)) ∈ W for all g ∈ G, then
  x = y."

**Degree.** By Definition 3.2, `A(𝔊) = A_3(𝔊)` is generated by the 3-cycle groups
`A(ℱ)` of degree-3 multisections. That is all the argument below uses; no degree-5
step is needed. **Indexing.** Below, `F_{ij}` has source `U_j` and range `U_i`, with
`F_{ij}F_{jk} = F_{ik}`. This is Nekrashevych's `F_{j,i}`, the transpose.

**The groupoid.** `G = G_V × (Z ⋉ Σ)` on `X = C × Σ`. A germ of `(g, σ^k) ∈ V × Z`
at `(c, x)` is determined by the germ of `g` at `c` and by `k`: two powers of `σ`
agreeing on an open set are equal, since periodic points of the full shift are
nowhere dense. So `G` is the groupoid of germs of the action of the finitely
generated group `V × Z` on the Cantor set `X`.

**0. Finite generation.** The action of `V × Z` on `X` is expansive. Let `W` be the
set of pairs with equal first letter in `C` and equal coordinate 0 in `Σ`. If
`c ≠ c'`, they differ first after a common prefix `u`, say `c ∈ [ua]`,
`c' ∈ [ub]`. An element of `V` mapping `[ua]` to `[0]` and `[ub]` into `[1]` by
prefix replacement (the complement is a finite union of cylinders, which can be
refined to fill `[1]`) separates their first letters. If `x ≠ x'`, some `σ^k`
separates coordinate 0. So `G` is expansive by Proposition 5.5, (3) ⇒ (2). Every
orbit is a `V`-orbit times a shift orbit and is infinite, so `A(G)` is finitely
generated by Theorem 5.6.

**1. Invariant sets.** Let `Z ⊆ X` be closed and invariant, with `(c, x) ∈ Z`.
Germs of `V × id` lie in `G`, so `(c', x) ∈ Z` for every `c'` in the `V`-orbit of
`c`. That orbit is dense in `C` and `Z` is closed, so `C × {x} ⊆ Z`. Germs of
`id × σ^k` give `(c, σ^k x) ∈ Z`. Hence `Z = C × Y` with `Y = {x : (c_0, x) ∈ Z}`
closed and shift-invariant. Conversely every such product is closed and invariant.

**2. Surjectivity.** Let `Y_X = C × Y` (closed, invariant). Restricting bisections
to `Y_X` is a homomorphism `ρ_Y : F(G) -> F(G|_{Y_X})`.

- **Well defined into `A`.** Let `ℱ` be a degree-3 multisection of `G`. The
  restricted components `U_i ∩ Y_X = r(F_{i0}(U_0 ∩ Y_X))` are all empty or all
  nonempty, since `Y_X` is invariant.
  - If they are all empty, `ρ_Y(ℱ_π) = id`.
  - Otherwise the traces `F_{ij} ∩ G|_{Y_X}` form a degree-3 multisection of
    `G|_{Y_X}` and `ρ_Y(ℱ_π)` is its `π`.

  So `ρ_Y(A(G)) ⊆ A(G|_{Y_X})`.
- **Onto.** Let `F` be a degree-3 multisection of `G|_{Y_X}` with pieces
  `U_0, U_1, U_2`.
  - Each compact open bisection `B` of `G|_{Y_X}` is the trace of a compact open
    bisection `B̃` of `G`. Cover `B` by finitely many basic bisections of `G`,
    refine to disjoint pieces, shrink each piece's source to a clopen subset of `X`
    whose trace is that piece's source, and separate sources and ranges by
    disjoint clopen sets.
  - Lift `F_{10}, F_{20}` to `F̃_{10}, F̃_{20}`. Choose a clopen `Ũ_0 ⊆ s(F̃_{10}) ∩ s(F̃_{20})`
    with trace `U_0`.
  - The sets `Ũ_0, F̃_{10}(Ũ_0), F̃_{20}(Ũ_0)` have pairwise disjoint traces, so their
    pairwise intersections are clopen and avoid `Y_X`. Remove from `Ũ_0` the
    preimages of those intersections under `F̃_{10}` and `F̃_{20}`. They avoid `Y_X`
    by invariance, and afterwards the three images are pairwise disjoint.
  - Put `F̃_{00} = id_{Ũ_0}` and `F̃_{ij} = F̃_{i0}F̃_{j0}^{-1}`. This is a multisection
    of `G` whose trace is `F`.

  Hence `ρ_Y` maps `A(G)` onto the generators of `A(G|_{Y_X})`, and so onto
  `A(G|_{Y_X})`.

**3. Simplicity.** Let `Y` be an infinite minimal subshift. Then `C × Y` is a Cantor
set (compact, metrizable, totally disconnected, and perfect because an infinite
minimal subshift has no isolated points). `G|_{C×Y} = G_V × (Z ⋉ Y)` is étale.

- **Minimal.** The orbit of `(c, x)` is (the `V`-orbit of `c`) × (the shift orbit
  of `x`). The `V`-orbit of `c` contains `u w` for every finite word `u` and every
  tail `w` of `c`, so it meets every cylinder and is dense in `C`. The shift orbit
  of `x` is dense in `Y` by minimality of `Y`. A product of dense sets is dense in
  `C × Y`.
- **Groupoid of germs (Definition 2.2).** Let `γ = (η, (k, x))` be a non-unit and
  `F ∋ γ` a bisection.
  - If `k ≠ 0`: `σ^k x ≠ x`, since `Y` has no periodic points, so `δ = γ` has
    `s(δ) ≠ r(δ)`.
  - If `k = 0`: `η` is a non-unit germ of `G_V`, and `F` contains a basic bisection
    `B_1 × ({0} × P)` with `η ∈ B_1` and `x ∈ P` clopen. `G_V` is the groupoid of
    germs of `V`, hence effective, so some `δ_1 ∈ B_1` has `s(δ_1) ≠ r(δ_1)`. Then
    `δ = (δ_1, (0, x)) ∈ F` has `s(δ) ≠ r(δ)`.

So `A(G|_{C×Y})` is simple by Theorem 4.1. It is nontrivial: three pairwise
disjoint cylinders `[a], [b], [c']` of `C` of equal length, with prefix-replacement
bisections, times `{0} × Y`, form a degree-3 multisection whose 3-cycle moves points.

**4. Distinct kernels.** Distinct minimal subshifts are disjoint: their intersection
is closed, invariant, and proper in each. Choose a clopen `P ⊆ Σ` with
`P ∩ Y_1 ≠ ∅` and `P ∩ Y_2 = ∅`. Take the degree-3 multisection of step 3 with
`{0} × Y` replaced by `{0} × P`, and let `t ∈ A(G)` be its 3-cycle. `t` is
supported in `C × P`, so `t ∈ ker ρ_{Y_2}`. It moves `(a w, x)` for `x ∈ P ∩ Y_1`,
so `t ∉ ker ρ_{Y_1}`.

**5. Many minimal subshifts.** For irrational `α ∈ (0, 1)`, the Sturmian subshift
`X_α` (the orbit closure of `n ↦ ⌊(n+1)α⌋ − ⌊nα⌋`) is minimal and aperiodic. Its
unique invariant measure gives the cylinder `[x_0 = 1]` measure `α`, so
`X_α ≠ X_β` for `α ≠ β` (Morse–Hedlund; Lothaire, *Algebraic Combinatorics on
Words*, Chapter 2).
