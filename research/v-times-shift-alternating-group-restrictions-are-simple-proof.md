**Input (literature).** Nekrashevych, *Simple groups of dynamical origin*,
arXiv:1511.08241, abstract: "if G is a minimal groupoid of germs (e.g., of a
group action), then A(G) is simple". Also "if G is expansive ... then A(G) is
finitely generated." In the body of the paper, `A(G)` is the subgroup of the
topological full group generated by the images of alternating groups under
multisections. That definition and the exact theorem numbers are still to be
quoted from the PDF.

The argument below uses only 3-cycles. If the definition admits only degrees
`n ≥ 5`, a 3-cycle of a degree-3 multisection still lies in `A(G)`: choosing
two further disjoint pieces with bisections extends it to a degree-5
multisection. This works here because every cylinder of `C` splits into
disjoint subcylinders related by prefix bisections.

A *multisection* of degree `n` is a family of compact open bisections
`F_{ij}` (`0 ≤ i, j < n`) with `F_{ii}` the identity on a clopen set `U_i`,
the `U_i` pairwise disjoint, `s(F_{ij}) = U_j`, `r(F_{ij}) = U_i`, and
`F_{ij} F_{jk} = F_{ik}`. It gives a homomorphism `S_n -> [[G]]`.

**1. Invariant sets.** Let `Z ⊆ X` be closed and invariant, with `(c, x) ∈ Z`.
Germs of `V × id` lie in `G`, so `(c', x) ∈ Z` for every `c'` in the `V`-orbit
of `c`. That orbit is dense in `C` and `Z` is closed, so `C × {x} ⊆ Z`. Germs of
`id × σ^k` give `(c, σ^k x) ∈ Z`. Hence `Z = C × Y` with
`Y = {x : (c_0, x) ∈ Z}` closed and shift-invariant. Conversely every such
product is closed and invariant, since germs are products of prefix germs and
shift powers.

**2. Surjectivity.** Let `Y_X = C × Y` (closed, invariant) and let `F` be a
multisection of `G|_{Y_X}` with pieces `U_0, ..., U_{n−1}`.

- Each compact open bisection `B` of `G|_{Y_X}` is the trace of a compact open
  bisection `B̃` of `G`. Cover `B` by finitely many basic bisections of `G`,
  refine to disjoint pieces, and shrink each piece's source to a clopen subset
  of `X` whose trace on `Y_X` is exactly that piece's source (compact open
  subsets of `Y_X` are traces of clopen subsets of `X`). Then separate sources
  and ranges of the pieces by disjoint clopen sets.
- Lift `F_{10}, ..., F_{n−1,0}` this way to `F̃_{i0}`. Choose a clopen
  `Ũ_0 ⊆ ∩_i s(F̃_{i0})` with trace `U_0`.
- The sets `Ũ_0, F̃_{10}(Ũ_0), ..., F̃_{n−1,0}(Ũ_0)` have pairwise disjoint traces
  on `Y_X`, so their pairwise intersections are clopen and avoid `Y_X`. Remove
  from `Ũ_0` their preimages under the `F̃_{i0}`. These preimages also avoid
  `Y_X`, because `Y_X` is invariant. The pieces are now pairwise disjoint.
- Put `F̃_{ij} = F̃_{i0} F̃_{j0}^{-1}` on these pieces. This is a multisection of
  `G` whose restriction to `Y_X` is `F`, so `ρ_Y` maps its alternating image onto
  that of `F`.

Since `A(G|_{Y_X})` is generated by such images, `ρ_Y` is onto. Restriction
maps multisections of `G` to multisections of `G|_{Y_X}` (possibly with empty
pieces), so `ρ_Y` is well defined into `A(G|_{Y_X})`.

**3. Simplicity.** For an infinite minimal subshift `Y`, the groupoid
`G|_{C×Y} = G_V × (Z ⋉ Y)` is:

- minimal: orbits are `V`-orbit × shift-orbit, both dense;
- effective: a germ `(g, σ^k)` fixing a neighbourhood in `C × Y` pointwise
  forces `σ^k = id` on an open subset of `Y`, so `k = 0` because infinite minimal
  subshifts have no periodic points, and then `g` is the identity germ;
- Hausdorff.

So it is a minimal groupoid of germs, and `A(G|_{C×Y})` is simple by
Nekrashevych. It is nontrivial: three disjoint cylinders of `C` with prefix
bisections, times `Y`, give a nontrivial 3-cycle.

**4. Distinct kernels.** Distinct minimal subshifts are disjoint: their
intersection is closed, invariant and proper in each. Choose a clopen `P ⊆ Σ`
with `P ∩ Y_1 ≠ ∅` and `P ∩ Y_2 = ∅`. Take a degree-3 multisection of `G_V`
(disjoint cylinders `[a], [b], [c]` of `C` with prefix bisections) times the
identity bisection on `P`. Its 3-cycle `t ∈ A(G)` is supported in `C × P`. So
`t ∈ ker ρ_{Y_2}`, while `t` moves `(a c', x)` for `x ∈ P ∩ Y_1`, so
`t ∉ ker ρ_{Y_1}`.

**5. Many minimal subshifts.** For irrational `α ∈ (0, 1)`, the Sturmian
subshift `X_α` (the orbit closure of `n ↦ ⌊(n+1)α⌋ − ⌊nα⌋`) is minimal and
aperiodic. Its unique invariant measure gives the cylinder `[x_0 = 1]` measure
`α`, so `X_α ≠ X_β` for `α ≠ β` (Morse–Hedlund; Lothaire, *Algebraic
Combinatorics on Words*, Chapter 2).
