Fix `C>0`, let `K<=C r^2`, and choose independent uniform labels
`v_1,...,v_K in F_2^r`.  With probability tending to one, every nonempty
linear dependency

```text
sum_(t in S) v_t=0
```

has

```text
|S| > r/(8 log_2 r).                                      (IPD1)
```

Equivalently, the binary character-evaluation code generated by these
columns has dual distance `Omega(r/log r)`.  In particular its exact scalar
kernel cannot be presented by bounded-arity parity checks on the selected
coordinates themselves.

The private Weyl sampler may be chosen to have `(IPD1)` simultaneously with
its matrix-dimension gap, and the choice remains recursive because dual
distance is a finite decidable predicate.  Therefore a bounded-occurrence
same-basis compiler cannot merely expose the direct parity checks of the
independently sampled label code.  It must either redesign the sampler as
the coordinate functionals of a very-low-rate LDPC kernel or introduce a
local auxiliary encoding whose uniform operator soundness is proved.
