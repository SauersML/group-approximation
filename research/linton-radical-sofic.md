OPEN.  For every one-relator group `G = F/<<w>>`, is the maximal rationally
perfect normal subgroup

    R = G_Q^(ω) = <<r>>_G      (Linton, arXiv:2407.09272, Theorem 1.1)

sofic?

This is one of the two halves of `linton-shell-sofic-via-sofic-action`, the
other being [[linton-radical-free-action-sofic]].  It is **not** sufficient on
its own: [[sofic-semidirect-product-closure-fails]] means a sofic `R` and a free
`F₀` still do not give a sofic `R ⋊ F₀`.

## What is known about `R`

- It is normally generated in `G` by a single element `r`, with `|r| <= |w|`.
- `R = 1` exactly when `G` is residually rationally solvable, in which case
  [[residually-rationally-solvable-one-relator-sofic]] closes everything; so the
  interesting case is `R ≠ 1`.
- In that case, in the torsion-free regime that a counterexample must inhabit,
  Linton shows `R` is **infinitely generated**.
- `R_ab ≅ (Z/kZ)[G/R]` by
  [[linton-radical-abelianization-is-truncated-group-ring]].  So `k = 1` makes
  `R` perfect, and for `k > 1` the abelianization is `k`-torsion.
- `R` inherits local indicability from `G` whenever `G` is torsion-free, since
  local indicability passes to subgroups.

## Attempts

1. **Reduce to the derived subgroup in the `k > 1` regime.**  *Correct but only
   a reduction.*  For `k > 1`, `R/R'` is abelian hence amenable, so `R'` sofic
   would give `R` sofic by sofic-by-amenable.  Nonsoficity of `R`, if it
   happened, would therefore already live in `R'`.  This narrows the target and
   proves nothing; in the `k = 1` regime `R` is perfect and the step is vacuous.
2. **Directed union of finitely generated pieces.**  *Deferred for want of a
   structure theorem.*  Soficity passes to directed unions, so it would suffice
   that every f.g. subgroup of `R` lie in a sofic subgroup of a class closed
   under such unions.  `R` is a subgroup of a one-relator group, so its f.g.
   subgroups inherit local indicability and the Freiheitssatz constraints — but
   nothing in that package produces a sofic certificate, and the natural
   candidates (free, residually finite) are exactly what fails for a rationally
   perfect radical.
3. **Read `R` off the Magnus decomposition.**  *Not carried out.*  `R` and the
   Magnus kernel are two descriptions of the same complexity, and a proof here
   should probably explain why the nonamenable free-edge obstruction of
   [[magnus-staggered-chain-sofic]] is harmless in the radical picture.  That
   comparison is step 4 of the artifact's proposed program and has not been
   attempted.
4. **Reality check.**  Recorded because this claim reads more tractable than it
   is.  No technique is known that certifies soficity of an infinitely
   generated, rationally perfect, locally indicable group from the fact that it
   is normally generated by one element in a one-relator group.  The honest
   status is that this is a *component* of the open question, isolated for
   clarity, not a lemma with a foreseeable proof.
