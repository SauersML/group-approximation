Notation as in the target. The input supplies `θ` and its elementary properties in
`R_l`. Write `≡` for equality in `R_l`.

**Step 1. D.**
- Let `U` be the ring presented by `u, x, x', B` and the first four relations. In `U`,
  `x^k u x^(-k) = u + kl`, so `b_k := x^k B x^(-k)` is a two-sided inverse of
  `u + 1 + kl`. `u` and all `b_k` commute, being `u` and inverses of polynomials in
  `u`.
- So the subring `C` they generate is commutative, and localization gives a
  surjection `A -> C`. `x^j C x^(-j) = C`, hence `U = Σ_k C x^k`.
- `D` satisfies the relations, so `U -> D` is onto. It restricts to `A -> C -> A`,
  the identity, and it respects the grading by powers of `x`. So `U ≅ D`.
- The composite `D -> R_l -> R_l/(e) = U = D` is the identity (setting `e = 0` in the
  presentation gives `U`). So `D ⊆ R_l` and `D ∩ (e) = 0`.

**Step 2. The modules.**
- `V = D/Du = ⊕_a x^a (A/uA)`, since `D = ⊕_a x^a A` and `Du = ⊕_a x^a A u`.
- `W = D/uD = ⊕_b (A/uA) x^b`, similarly.
- `A/uA = Z_(l)` through `f -> f(0)`, since the denominators become `1 + kl`.
- `A` acts on the right of `V` and on the left of `W`, since `A` is commutative.
- `M = W ⊗_D V = D/(uD + Du) = ⊕_k x^k A/(uA + klA)`, using
  `u x^k = x^k (u - kl)`. So `M = Z_(l) ⊕ N`, with `A/(uA + klA) = Z/l^(m_k)` for
  `k != 0`.
- On `N` the left and right `Z_(l)`-actions agree, because
  `f(-kl) - f(0) in kl Z_(l)` for every `f in A`.

**Step 3. The ring R'.**
- Let `T = T_(Z_(l))(N)` be the tensor algebra, and `μ : W × V -> M ⊆ T` the
  pairing `(w, v) -> w ⊗ v`, with `Z_(l) = T^0` and `N = T^1`.
- On `R' = D ⊕ (V ⊗ T ⊗ W)`, all tensor products over `Z_(l)`, define:
  - the `D`-bimodule action on the second summand through `V` and `W`;
  - `(v⊗t⊗w)(v'⊗t'⊗w') = v ⊗ t μ(w,v') t' ⊗ w'`.
- Associativity reduces to `μ(wd, v) = μ(w, dv)`, which holds because `μ` is defined
  over `D`. So `R'` is a unital associative ring.
- Put `e' = v_0 ⊗ 1 ⊗ w_0`, with `v_0, w_0` the classes of 1. Then:
  - `e'e' = v_0 ⊗ μ(w_0,v_0) ⊗ w_0 = e'`;
  - `u e' = 0`, since `u v_0 = 0` in `V`;
  - `e' u = 0` likewise.
- So `φ : R_l -> R'`, fixing `D` and sending `e -> e'`, is a ring homomorphism. It is
  onto: `d e' d' = (d v_0) ⊗ 1 ⊗ (w_0 d')`, and `e' x^k e' = v_0 ⊗ z_k ⊗ w_0`, whose
  products give all of `T`.

**Step 4. The inverse ψ : R' -> R_l.**
- `ψ_V(d v_0) = d e` is well defined, since `d u e = 0`. It is left `D`-linear, and
  right `Z_(l)`-linear for right multiplication by `θ(Z_(l))`, because `f e = θ(f(0))`
  for `f in A`. (`A -> eR_le`, `f -> f e`, is a ring map killing `u`, so it factors
  through `Z_(l)`, and it equals `θ` by uniqueness of localization maps.)
- `ψ_W(w_0 d) = e d`, similarly.
- `ψ_T : T -> e R_l e`, extending `z_k -> e x^k e`, is a map of `Z_(l)`-algebras.
  - `θ(Z_(l))` is central in `e R_l e`. That ring is generated by `θ(Z_(l))` and the
    `e x^k e`, since `e f x^k e = θ(f(-kl))·e x^k e`, and
    `θ(c) e x^k e = e x^k e θ(c)`: the two sides differ by `θ(kl c')` times
    `e x^k e`, and `kl · e x^k e = 0`.
  - `l^(m_k) e x^k e = 0`, since `k/l^(v_l(k))` is invertible on the corner.
  - The universal property of `T` applies.
- `ψ = id_D ⊕ (v⊗t⊗w -> ψ_V(v)ψ_T(t)ψ_W(w))` is a ring homomorphism. The one nontrivial
  check is `ψ_W(w)ψ_V(v') = e d d' e = ψ_T(μ(w,v'))`, which holds by
  `e x^k f e = e x^k e θ(f(0))`.

`ψ∘φ` fixes the generators of `R_l`, so `ψ∘φ = id` and `φ` is an isomorphism.

**Step 5. Reading off 2–4.**
- `V ⊗ T ⊗ W = ⊕_(a,b) x^a e · T · e x^b`.
- `T = ⊕_s N^(⊗s)`, and
  `Z/l^(m_(k_1)) ⊗ ... ⊗ Z/l^(m_(k_s)) = Z/l^(min m_(k_i))`.
- `φ^(-1)` maps the basis tensor `x^a v_0 ⊗ z_(k_1) ⊗ ... ⊗ z_(k_s) ⊗ w_0 x^b` to
  `[a|K|b]`. This gives 2.
- 3 is the multiplication of `R'`, where `μ(w_b, v_a)` is the class of `x^(a+b)`. It
  equals 1 if `a+b = 0`, else `z_(a+b)`. The `A`-actions use `f x^a = x^a f(u - al)`.
- 4 is the case `a = b = 0`. ∎
