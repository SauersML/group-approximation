Every finitely generated unital C\*-subalgebra of the hyperfinite II₁
factor `R` is an MF algebra: it admits a norm ultraproduct embedding
`B ↪ ∏_ω M_{k_n} / ⊕ M_{k_n}`.  (Local formulation because `R` is not
norm-separable, matching the finitely generated witness reduction for
X(2).)

An Oberwolfach C\*-algebras problem session poses this alongside
X(2), verbatim: "Problem 16.  Is the hyperfinite II₁-factor R
quasidiagonal?  Is it MF?" (TIB open-access report; see the artifact's
verification log).  Quasidiagonality asks in addition that the models
be u.c.p., asymptotically multiplicative, and recover `τ_R`; MF asks
only for the norm embedding.  So
`stw99-problem-x2-hyperfinite-factor-quasidiagonal` implies this claim
(`x2-implies-hyperfinite-factor-mf`), and refuting this claim refutes
X(2).

The graph's group route already targets this weaker claim: an MF
embedding of a subalgebra generated by a subgroup `Γ ≤ U(R)` restricts
to asymptotic norm unitary representations of `Γ`, so a countable
non-MF group inside `U(R)` refutes local MF-ness of `R` directly, not
just quasidiagonality — the leaf
`non-mf-subgroup-of-hyperfinite-factor-unitary-group` refutes both at
once.

## Attempts

* Positive side: every known MF-certificate for a class of subalgebras
  of `R` factors through quasidiagonality of the trace (exact + UCT
  via Tikuisis--White--Winter/Gabe, RFD algebras, amenable-group
  patterns), so no route is currently known that would establish MF
  without establishing X(2) itself — but the converse implication is
  not proved either, so the two are carried separately: a refutation
  strategy may distinguish them (a witness could conceivably be MF
  while its trace fails quasidiagonality; the trace condition is
  strictly extra data).
* Negative side: same exclusions as X(2) — group-shaped witnesses are
  dead (`x2-witnesses-are-never-group-shaped`,
  `literal-group-mark-invisible-to-hyperfinite-representations`), so a
  refutation needs a non-group norm obstruction inside `R`.
