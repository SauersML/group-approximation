**ESTABLISHED** (lane proof, bh-gln-centralizer; elementary, modulo the recalled V-dynamics inputs (D1)–(D3);
not reviewed; no priority claimed).

**Setting.** As on the parent node.
- `F` is any field and `L = L_F(1,2) ≅ A_F(𝒢_2)`, the Steinberg algebra of the Cuntz groupoid
  `𝒢_2 = {(αw, |α|−|β|, βw)}` on `C = {0,1}^ℕ`.
- `Ψ(a) = x_0 a y_0 + x_1 a y_1` is the doubling endomorphism.
- A **monomial unit** is `n = f·u_g`, where `g ∈ V`, `u_g` is the indicator of the germ bisection of `g`, and
  `f ∈ LC(C, F^×)`. So `n` is a unit supported on a global compact open bisection. Over `F_2` the monomial units
  are exactly `V`.

## Theorem

Let `n = f·u_g` be a monomial unit whose homeomorphism `g` has infinite order. Then `n` commutes with no unital copy
of `L`. Equivalently (parent Theorem 1), `n` is not conjugate in `L^×` to its double `Ψ(n)`.

## Inputs

**Dynamics of V, recalled.** These are the same inputs as the parent's Theorem 4: Brin's revealing pairs (Brin,
*Higher dimensional Thompson groups*, 2004) and Salazar-Díaz, *Thompson's group V from a dynamical viewpoint*
(2010); not re-read.
- **(D1)** The locally periodic set `C_per` is clopen and `g`-invariant, and `g^m = id` on it for some `m ≥ 1`.
  Here `C_per` is the set of points with a neighbourhood on which some power of `g` is the identity; it is the union
  of the cones of the neutral leaf cycles.
- **(D2)** Outside `C_per`, `g` has only finitely many periodic points: the attractors and repellers of the
  revealing chains.
- **(D3)** If `g` has infinite order, it has a periodic point outside `C_per`.

**(E) Lags of periodic germs, self-contained.**
- Let `g^d(y) = y`. On a table piece of `g^d` through `y`, `g^d(αw) = βw` and `y = αw_0 = βw_0`.
- If the lag `|β| − |α|` is 0, then `α = β` and `g^d = id` near `y`, so `y ∈ C_per`.
- If the lag is nonzero, `β` is a proper prefix of `α` or the reverse, and `y = αγ^∞` is eventually periodic.
- Conversely, for `y ∈ C_per` of period `d` we have `d | m`. The germ of `g^d` at `y` has trivial `(m/d)`-th
  power, and isotropy groups of `𝒢_2` are torsion-free, so that germ is the identity.
- **So:** periodic points in `C_per` have lag 0, and those outside `C_per` have nonzero lag ("hyperbolic").

## Proof

**Step 1 (twisted induced modules).**
- Let `x ∈ C` be eventually periodic, tail-equivalent to `ρ^∞` with `ρ` primitive. Its isotropy group `Iso_x ≅ Z`
  is generated by the arrow `ζ` of lag `|ρ|`.
- For an `F[ζ^(±1)]`-module `σ`, put `M = F𝒢_x ⊗_(F Iso_x) σ`. Here `F𝒢_x` is the span of the arrows with source
  `x`, and `A_F(𝒢_2)` acts on it by convolution on the left.
- For `σ = F[ζ]/(φ)` these are the twisted Chen modules; we use only the construction.
- Choose arrows `γ_y : x → y`, one for each `y` in the tail class `O` of `x`. Then `M = ⊕_(y∈O) γ_y ⊗ σ`, and
  `n(γ_y ⊗ v) = f(gy) γ_(gy) ⊗ c(g,y)v` with `c(g,y) = γ_(gy)^(−1) [g,y] γ_y ∈ Iso_x`. Here `[g,y]` is the germ
  of `g` at `y`.

**Step 2 (splitting `M_n`, with `z` acting by `n`).** `g` permutes `O`.
- An infinite `g`-orbit in `O` spans a free module `F[z^(±1)] ⊗ σ`.
- A finite cycle `Γ = (y, gy, …, g^(d−1)y)` spans a finite-dimensional module whenever `σ` is. Re-choose
  `γ_(g^i y) = [g^i, y] γ_y` for `i < d`. Then the module is `F[z] ⊗_(F[z^d]) σ`, with `z^d` acting on `σ` by
  `λ_Γ ζ^(k_Γ)`.
  - `λ_Γ = ∏_(i<d) f(g^(i+1)y)` is the weight around the cycle.
  - `k_Γ = s_Γ/|ρ|`, where `s_Γ` is the lag of `g^d` at `y`. `γ_y^(−1)[g^d,y]γ_y` is an isotropy arrow at `x`, so
    `|ρ|` divides `s_Γ`.
- So the torsion part of `M_n` is the direct sum of the cycle modules.

**Step 3 (choosing `x` and `σ`).**
- **The point.** By (D3) and (E), take a periodic point `q ∉ C_per`, with period `d_0`, weight `λ_0` and
  `k_0 = s_0/|ρ| ≠ 0`. Put `x = q`.
- **The lag-0 cycles are pinned.** They lie in `C_per`, by (E), and have length `d | m`.
  - `λ_Γ^(m/d)` lies in `Λ = {∏_(i<m) f(g^(i+1)y) : y ∈ C_per}`. This is a finite subset of `F^×`, being the
    values of a locally constant function on a compact set.
  - So every root `β ∈ F̄` of their annihilator `z^d − λ_Γ` lies in `B_0 = {β : β^m ∈ Λ}`, which is finite and
    Galois-stable.
- **The twist.** Take `σ = E = F[ζ]/(φ)`, with `φ` irreducible and root `α`, such that no root `β` of
  `z^(d_0) − λ_0 α'^(k_0)` lies in `B_0`, for every conjugate `α'`.
  - Such a root would give `(λ_0 α'^(k_0))^m = β^(m d_0) ∈ Λ^(d_0)`, so it suffices to avoid that.
  - *`F` infinite:* take `φ = ζ − a` with `a ∈ F^×` outside the finite set `{a : (λ_0 a^(k_0))^m ∈ Λ^(d_0)}`.
    Each value of `a^(k_0 m)` has at most `|k_0 m|` preimages.
  - *`F = F_q`:* take `α` generating `F_(q^k)^×` and `φ` its minimal polynomial. Membership would force
    `α'^(k_0 m) ∈ F^×`, i.e. `(q^k−1)/(q−1)` divides `k_0 m`. That is false for large `k`.
- **The polynomial π.** The `Γ_0` module is `E[z]/(z^(d_0) − λ_0 α^(k_0))` as an `F[z]`-module. Its minimal
  polynomial over `F` has the same roots as `∏_(α')(z^(d_0) − λ_0 α'^(k_0))`. Let `π` be an irreducible factor.
  Its roots lie outside `B_0`.

**Step 4.** The `π`-primary torsion `T_π(M_n)` is finite-dimensional and nonzero.
- It is nonzero, since it contains the `π`-part of the `Γ_0` module.
- Lag-0 cycles contribute nothing to it, since `π` is coprime to every `z^d − λ_Γ`.
- Free parts have no torsion.
- The hyperbolic cycles in `O` are finitely many, by (D2), and each is finite-dimensional.

**Step 5.** Suppose `n` commutes with a unital copy of `L`. Then parent Theorem 2(b) applies to the `L`-module `M`
and gives `M_n ≅ M_n ⊕ M_n` as `F[z^(±1)]`-modules. So `T_π ≅ T_π ⊕ T_π`, which is impossible in finite nonzero
dimension. ∎

## Remarks

1. **The `F_2` witness that parent Theorem 4 missed.** It is `t = x_0y_0 + x_1Ψ(c)y_1`: the identity on `[0]` and
   the doubled north–south unit on `[1]`. Here `C_per = [0]`, `m = 1`, `Λ = {1}` and `B_0 = {1}`. The attracting
   fixed points of `Ψ(c)` have lag `k_0 ≠ 0`, and a twist of large order separates their blocks from the absorbing
   identity part. Parent Theorem 4 could not see this, because its traces use the trivial twist.
2. **Why the twist works.** Lag-0 cycles have trivial isotropy holonomy, so they cannot see `σ`. Hyperbolic cycles
   carry `ζ^(k)` in their monodromy. A generic isotropy character therefore makes the hyperbolic spectrum disjoint
   from everything the periodic part can absorb. Lefschetz traces are the one-dimensional, untwisted shadow of this
   invariant: they vanish mod `p`, and the untwisted Cantor module is absorbed by `C_per`.
3. **Self-doubling forces infinite rank directly.** Under `M_2(L) ≅ L`, `Ψ(t) ↔ diag(t,t)`, and
   `C_(M_2(L))(diag(t,t)) = M_2(C_L(t))`, which is `C_L(t)^4` as an `F[t]`-module. So `ρ(Ψt) = 4ρ(t)`. A
   self-doubling transcendental `t` has `ρ(t) = ∞`. So `leavitt-unit-centralizers-have-finite-rank` excludes every
   such `t` without going through `(Q,+)`, as parent Corollary 5 did.
4. **Diagonal-preserving embeddings.** The Theorem also excludes unital embeddings `A_F(𝒢_2 × K) ↪ L` that carry
   the diagonal onto the diagonal, for any ample `K` whose full group has an element of infinite order:
   - such an embedding sends normalizing units to monomial units (the support of a normalizing unit is a
     bisection, since `𝒢_2` is effective);
   - `1 ⊗ u_k` would then commute with the copy of `L ⊗ 1`.

   At the level of groupoids this is already forced by isotropy, as in Nyland–Ortega (arXiv:1806.11087, §11.4,
   p. 50). Uncountably many units of `𝒢_2 × K` have isotropy `Z`, but only countably many points of `𝒢_2` do. So
   Remark 4 adds nothing beyond the Theorem.

## Exact status of route R2 (finite-field Leavitt units for GL_n(Q))

The commuting-copy mechanism needs a transcendental `t ∈ L_(F_2)(1,2)` conjugate to `t ⊕ t`. Equivalently, it needs
a unital embedding `L ⊗ F_2[u^(±1)] ↪ L`, a case of Bilich–Hazrat–Nam's Question 2.8. What is now known about
such a `t`:
- it has `ρ(t) = ∞` (Remark 3);
- it has vanishing twisted spectral invariants for every tail class and every isotropy character;
- by the Theorem, it is **not in `V`**, nor in any monomial unit.

So the witness must be a genuinely linear unit. No construction is known, and a unital `L ⊗ L ↪ L` over `F_2`
(open) would supply one. Even with it, `GL_n(Q)` needs the dilations `r_k ↦ r_k^q` on top of `(Q,+)`. R2 is
therefore parked at an open problem in Leavitt-algebra embedding theory.

## Lesson for general BH

Isotropy-twisted induced modules turn every hyperbolic periodic orbit of a full-group element into a
finite-dimensional spectral block, and no doubling can absorb it. So absorption ("commutes with a copy of the host",
`t ≅ t ⊕ t`, the engine of infinite divisibility) can never be fed by topological-full-group or monomial elements of
a groupoid algebra, over any field. The divisible part of an input like `GL_n(Q)` has to be carried by the ACTING
GROUP of the host (the carrier principle; Track A with `St_10(R_L) ⊆ Λ₁`), never by combinatorial units inside a
fixed Cuntz–Leavitt host.
