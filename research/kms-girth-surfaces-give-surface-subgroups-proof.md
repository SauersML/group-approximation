## Step 0. The vertex groups

Let `U` be `<x,y | x^p, y^p, [x,y,x], [x,y,y]>` (`U_3`) or `<x,y | x^p, y^p, [x,y,x], [x,y,y,x], [x,y,y,y]>`
(`U_4`), with `[x,y] = x^{−1}y^{−1}xy` and left-normed commutators.

**(a) Normal forms.** As in Step 0(a) of the reference proof:
- in `U_3`, every element is `y^β x^α u^μ`, where `u = [x,y]` is central;
- in `U_4`, every element is `y^β x^γ u^μ v^ν`, where `u = [x,y]` and `v = [u,y]` is central;
- all exponents are read mod `p`.
So `|U_3| ≤ p^3` and `|U_4| ≤ p^4`.

**(b) Orders.**
- *`U_4`.* Let `φ` be the automorphism `x ↦ xu`, `u ↦ uv`, `v ↦ v` of `(Z/p)^3 = <x,u,v>`. Then
  `φ^n(x) = x u^n v^{C(n,2)}` and `φ^n(u) = u v^n`. Since `p` is odd, `p | C(p,2)`, so `φ^p = id`.
- In `H = (Z/p)^3 ⋊_φ Z/p`, take `X = x` and `Y` a generator of `Z/p` acting by `φ`. They satisfy
  `X^p = Y^p = 1` and the three relators, with `[X,Y] = u` and `[[X,Y],Y] = v`.
  - These are the laws of `verify.log` item 8, reduced mod `p`.
  - `Y^{−1}XY = φ(X) = Xu` gives `[X,Y] = u`. `[u,Y] = u^{−1}φ(u) = v`. `u` commutes with `X`, and `v`
    commutes with `X` and `Y`.
- So `U_4 → H` is onto, and `|U_4| ≥ p^4`.
- *`U_3`.* The same argument works with `(Z/p)^2 = <x,u>` and `φ(x) = xu`, `φ(u) = u`.
- So `|U_3| = p^3` and `|U_4| = p^4`, normal forms are unique, `x` and `y` have order `p`, and
  `<x> ∩ <y> = 1`.

**(c) Link girths.** Let `Γ(U)` be the coset graph with respect to `<x>` and `<y>`. Its girth is 6 for `U_3` and
8 for `U_4`.
- This is Step 0(f) of the reference proof, which uses only the normal-form law of `U`.
- The graph does not change when the roles of `<x>` and `<y>` are exchanged.
- `Γ(U)` has `|U|` edges and two parts of `|U|/p` vertices each. It is connected because `x` and `y`
  generate `U`.

## Step 1. Local structure of `Y`

The presentation of `G` is the union of the presentations of its vertex groups, amalgamated along `<a>`, `<b>`
and `<c>`. So `G` is the fundamental group of the triangle of groups with:
- vertex groups `U_{m_i}`, each generated by its two edge groups;
- cyclic edge groups of order `p`;
- trivial face group.

By Step 0(c) the half girths are `m_0, m_1, m_2`, and `Σ 1/m_i < 1` for the three types `(3,3,4)`, `(3,4,4)`,
`(4,4,4)`. Theorem 3.1(i),(v) (item 1 of `cckw-kms-triangle-groups-structure`, in the reading recorded there)
then gives:
- a CAT(−1) simplicial complex `Y` on which `G` acts by simplicial isometries;
- a strict fundamental domain `C = (v_0, v_1, v_2)`, a hyperbolic triangle with angles `π/m_i`;
- `Stab(v_i) = X_i`, of order `|U_{m_i}|`;
- `Lk(v_i, Y) ≅ Γ_i := Γ(U_{m_i})`.

Let `e_k` be the side of `C` opposite `v_k`. The vertices of `C` lie in distinct orbits, so the action preserves
vertex types.

(a) *Every simplex of `Y` is `gF` for a face `F` of `C`.* Let `y` be an interior point of a simplex `σ`, and
write `y = g y_0` with `y_0 ∈ C`. Then `g^{−1}σ` is a face of `C`.

(b) *Chamber stabilizers.*
- If `gC ∋ v_i`, then `g^{−1}v_i` is the vertex of `C` of type `i`. So `g ∈ X_i`, and `X_i` is transitive on
  the chambers at `v_i`.
- These chambers are the edges of `Lk(v_i) ≅ Γ_i`, so there are `|X_i|` of them.
- Hence `Stab_{X_i}(C) = 1`. Since `Stab(C) ⊆ Stab(v_i)`, also `Stab(C) = 1`.

(c) *Edge stabilizers and intersections.* Take the side `e_k`, joining `v_i` and `v_j`.
- *Counting at `v_i`.* The edges at `v_i` are the vertices of `Lk(v_i)`.
  - Two edges of the same type never lie in a common chamber. So the link is bipartite by edge type, and
    `X_i` is transitive on each type, by the argument of (b).
  - `Γ_i` is connected and bipartite, so its bipartition is unique, and each part has `|X_i|/p` vertices.
  - So `X_i·e_k` has `|X_i|/p` elements, and `|Stab_{X_i}(e_k)| = p`.
  - An element stabilizing `e_k` fixes its type-`i` endpoint. So `|Stab(e_k)| = p`.
- *The intersection fixes `e_k`.*
  - `e_k` is a local geodesic: at an interior point, the two directions along `e_k` are at angle `π`. In the
    CAT(−1) space `Y` it is therefore the unique geodesic from `v_i` to `v_j` (BH Proposition II.1.4).
  - Every element of `X_i ∩ X_j` fixes `v_i` and `v_j`, hence `e_k`.
- *Conclusion.* `g_k ∈ X_i ∩ X_j` has order `p` by Step 0(b). So
  `<g_k> ⊆ X_i ∩ X_j ⊆ Stab(e_k)`, all of order `p`, and `X_i ∩ X_j = Stab(e_k) = <g_k>`.
- An element of `X_0 ∩ X_1 ∩ X_2` fixes the three vertices of `C`. Simplices are determined by their vertices,
  so it fixes `C` and is trivial.
- `Y` is locally finite: `|X_i|` chambers meet at a vertex, and `p` at an edge.

(d) *The link as a coset graph.*
- The chambers at `v_i` are the `xC` with `x ∈ X_i`, all distinct.
- The edges at `v_i` are the `x e_j` and the `x e_k`. By (c), `x e_j = x' e_j` if and only if
  `x^{−1}x' ∈ <g_j>`.
- So `Lk(v_i, Y)` has vertex set `X_i/<g_j> ⊔ X_i/<g_k>` and one edge of length `π/m_i` for each `x ∈ X_i`,
  joining `x<g_j>` to `x<g_k>`. Its combinatorial girth is `2m_i`.
- The chambers containing `e_k` are the `hC` with `h ∈ <g_k>`.

## Steps 2–7

Steps 2–7 of the reference proof, including the quasiconvexity argument of Step 7 via BH I.8.19, II.1.4(1),
III.H.1.7 and III.H.1.9, apply with the angles `π/m_i`. They use only:
- Step 1(a)–(d);
- the girths `2m_i`;
- the order `p` of each `g_k`, with labels not divisible by `p`;
- the CAT(−1) metric and the hyperbolicity of `G` (Theorem 3.1(v));
- local finiteness.

In Step 2, a vertex of type `i` has `2m_i` corners, hence total angle `2π`. The counts
`V = Σ_i T/(2m_i)`, `E = 3T/2` and `F = T` give `χ(S) = T(Σ_i 1/(2m_i) − 1/2)`, which is negative because
`Σ 1/m_i < 1`. An orientable connected double cover of `S` gives an orientable closed surface of genus at least 2.
