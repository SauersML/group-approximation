Notation as in the target. Maps compose as functions: `(gh)(x) = g(h(x))`. The support `supp(g)` is the
closure of `{x : g(x) ≠ x}`. For an open arc or interval `U`, `T_c(U)` is the set of elements of `T` whose
support is a compact subset of `U`. Standard facts about `F` used:
- (F1) For dyadic `a < b`, the elements of `T` supported in `[a, b]` form a copy `F_[a,b]` of `F`. Its
  commutator subgroup is the set of elements that are the identity near `a` and near `b`, i.e.
  `T_c((a, b))` (Cannon–Floyd–Parry, Theorem 4.1: `F' = {f : f'(0) = f'(1) = 1}`).
- (F2) `T_c((a, b))` acts transitively on the dyadic points of `(a, b)`.
- (F3) For a dyadic `x` in an open arc `V`, every germ at `x` of an element of `T` fixing `x` (a pair of
  one-sided slopes in `2^Z`) is the germ of some element of `T_c(V)` fixing `x`.
- (F5) `F` is generated by two elements, and `F_[a,b] ≅ F` by conjugation with a dyadic PL map.
- (F4) `T` acts transitively on cyclically ordered pairs of dyadic points.

**Step 1: fragmentation of T.** *Claim.* If open arcs `U_1, …, U_m` with dyadic endpoints cover `S^1`, then
`T = <T_c(U_1), …, T_c(U_m)>`.

- *Interval case.* Let `V_1, …, V_r` be open intervals with left endpoints `a_1 < … < a_r` and right endpoints
  `b_1 < … < b_r`, with `V_j ∩ V_(j+1) ≠ ∅`. Put `W = V_1 ∪ … ∪ V_r` and `N_r = <T_c(V_1), …, T_c(V_r)>`.
  We show `T_c(W) ⊆ N_r` by induction on `r`; the case `r = 1` is trivial.
  - Let `h ∈ T_c(W)` and pick a dyadic `γ ∈ V_(r−1) ∩ V_r`.
  - By (F2) and the overlaps, `N_r` is transitive on the dyadic points of `W`. So some `n ∈ N_r` has
    `n(h(γ)) = γ`.
  - By (F3), some `n' ∈ T_c(V_(r−1) ∩ V_r)` has the germ of `nh` at `γ`. Then `h' = n'^(-1) n h` is the
    identity near `γ`, and `h' ∈ T_c(W)`.
  - Split `h' = h'_L h'_R`, with `h'_L = h'` left of `γ` and `h'_R = h'` right of `γ`, each the identity
    elsewhere. Both lie in `T`, since `h'` fixes a neighbourhood of `γ`.
  - `supp(h'_R)` is a compact subset of `(γ, b_r) ⊆ V_r`. `supp(h'_L)` is a compact subset of `(a_1, γ)`, and
    `(a_1, γ) ⊆ V_1 ∪ … ∪ V_(r−1)` because `γ < b_(r−1)`. By induction `h'_L ∈ N_(r−1)`, so `h ∈ N_r`.
- *Circle case.* Let `N = <T_c(U_i)>` and `g ∈ T`, and fix a dyadic `x_0`.
  - `N` is transitive on dyadic points, by (F2) on each `U_i` and the overlaps. Choose `n_1 ∈ N` with
    `n_1 g(x_0) = x_0`.
  - By (F3) choose `n_2 ∈ T_c(U_i)`, for some `U_i ∋ x_0`, with the germ of `n_1 g` at `x_0`. Then
    `g' = n_2^(-1) n_1 g` is the identity on an arc `(x_0 − δ, x_0 + δ)`.
  - So `g' ∈ T_c(I)` for the open interval `I = S^1 ∖ [x_0 − δ/2, x_0 + δ/2]`.
  - `supp(g')` is compact and covered by components of the sets `U_i ∩ I`. A minimal subcover is a chain
    `V_1, …, V_r` as in the interval case, and `T_c(V_j) ⊆ T_c(U_i)`. So `g' ∈ N`, hence `g ∈ N`.

**Step 2: a list of fixed length.** Fix once and for all:
- two generators `x, x'` of `F_[1/8, 7/8] ≅ F` (Thompson's `F` is 2-generated). They are the identity near `0`
  and `1`, so by (F1) they lie in `[F_[0,1], F_[0,1]]`. Fix expressions of `x` and `x'` as products of
  commutators of elements of `F_[0,1]`.
- by (G), elements `c_1, …, c_L` with `G = <T, c_1, …, c_L>`, and for each `c_l` an expression as a product of
  commutators `[α, β]` with `α, β ∈ G_I`, for closed arcs `I ≠ S^1`.

Let `k` be the total number of commutators in all these expressions. Only `k` is fixed here; the arcs are
placed in Step 3, and `k` does not depend on them.

**Step 3: arcs and the rotation.** Let `K = 2k`, `n = 2^(K+1)` and `J_m = [m/n, (m+1)/n]`, indices mod `n`.
- Let `t` be the rotation by `1/(2n)` and `r = t^2`. Then `t ∈ T`, `t` has order `2n`, and `r^m(J_0) = J_m`.
- The arcs `J_m` meet only in endpoints. So elements supported in two different ones of them commute: each maps
  its arc to itself and fixes its endpoints and everything outside it.
- *Transport into `J_0`.* Let `σ` be the linear map `[0, 1] → J_0`, `x ↦ x/n`. It has slope `2^(-(K+1))`, so
  conjugation by `σ` maps `F_[0,1]` onto `F_(J_0)` and `F_[1/8,7/8]` onto `F_[a,b]`, with `a = 1/(8n)` and
  `b = 7/(8n)`.
- Each arc `I` of Step 2 lies in a closed dyadic arc `I' ≠ S^1`. By (F4) there is `h_I ∈ T` with
  `h_I(I') ⊆ (0, 1/n)`.
- Conjugating every commutator of Step 2 by `σ`, respectively by `h_I`, gives commutators `[f_(2j−1), f_(2j)]`,
  `j = 1, …, k`, of elements `f_1, …, f_K` of `G_(J_0)`, repetitions allowed. A commutator `[α, β]` supported
  in `I` becomes `[h_I α h_I^(-1), h_I β h_I^(-1)]`, with both entries supported in `h_I(I) ⊆ J_0`.
- Let `C = <[f_a, f_b] : a ≠ b>`. Then `σxσ^(-1)`, `σx'σ^(-1)` and each `h_I c h_I^(-1)` used lie in `C`.

**Step 4: T needs only t and one copy of F.** The open arcs `t^m((a, b))`, `m = 0, …, 2n−1`, cover `S^1`:
`(a, b)` has length `3/(4n)`, and `t` rotates by `1/(2n) < 3/(4n)`. By Step 1,
`T = <t^m T_c((a,b)) t^(-m)>`. Since `T_c((a,b)) ⊆ F_[a,b] = <σxσ^(-1), σx'σ^(-1)>`, this gives

```text
T = <t, σxσ^(-1), σx'σ^(-1)>.
```

**Step 5: one element carrying the list.** Let `u_a = 2^a` for `a = 1, …, K`. The differences `u_a − u_b`,
`a ≠ b`, are pairwise distinct mod `n = 2^(K+1)`:
- Every `|u_a − u_b − u_c + u_d| < 2^(K+1)`, so a congruence is an equality `2^a + 2^d = 2^b + 2^c`.
- Uniqueness of binary expansions then gives `a = c` and `b = d`. The case `a = d` would force `b = c = a`.

Put `P_m(g) = r^m g r^(-m)`, the copy of `g ∈ G_(J_0)` on `J_m`, and

```text
f = P_(u_1)(f_1) P_(u_2)(f_2) ⋯ P_(u_K)(f_K) ∈ G.
```

Then `X_a = r^(-u_a) f r^(u_a) = ∏_i P_(u_i − u_a)(f_i)`. Its factor on `J_0` is `f_a`, and its other factors
sit on the arcs with index `u_i − u_a`, `i ≠ a`. For `a ≠ b`:
- An index `u_i − u_a ≡ u_j − u_b` with `i ≠ a` would give `u_i − u_j ≡ u_a − u_b`.
- If `i = j`, this says `u_a ≡ u_b`, which is impossible.
- If `i ≠ j`, distinct differences give `i = a`, which is also impossible.

So `X_a = f_a X'_a` and `X_b = f_b X'_b`, where `X'_a` and `X'_b` are supported on arcs different from each
other's and from `J_0`. Each of them commutes with `f_a`, with `f_b` and with the other. Hence
`[X_a, X_b] = [f_a, f_b]`, and `C ≤ <t, f>`.

**Step 6: conclusion.**
- By Steps 3 and 4, `T = <t, σxσ^(-1), σx'σ^(-1)> ≤ <t, f>`. In particular each `h_I ∈ <t, f>`.
- Each `c_l` is a product of elements `h_I^(-1) y h_I` with `y ∈ C`, so `c_l ∈ <t, f>`.
- Hence `G = <T, c_1, …, c_L> = <t, f>`, and `t` has order `2n = 2^(K+2)`.

Item 2 of the target is proved there. For item 3: `T` and `TA` are finitely generated, simple and contain `T`
(BHM, arXiv:2407.03149v1, §5). Lodha's `S` is treated in `lodha-simple-group-s-is-two-generated`.
