**Conventions.**
- `W = H^m ⋊ T`, `T = T_1`, `H` the subtree tower. `(f; q)(g; s) = (f q(g); qs)`, `q(g)_i = g_(q^-1 i)`.
- `a_x = (f_x; p_x)`, with the root tuple `p in T^X`.
- A *letter* is `C r(a)^(+-1) C^-1`. **Every conjugator `C` used below has top in `<p>`.**
- A *pair* is a letter of `r` times a letter of `r^-1`.
- *Reordering.* The conjugate of a pair is a pair, and `P_1 P_2 = P_2 (P_2^-1 P_1 P_2)`. Also
  `L_1 L_2 = L_2 (L_2^-1 L_1 L_2)`. So products of pairs can be reordered into the template order, with
  the same number of pairs for each `r`, and conjugator tops stay in `<p>`.

Induct on depth. For every `W in 𝒲` and `a`, `a_x = A · P` with `A = prod_r r(a)^(n_(x,r))` and `P` a product
of at most `M` pairs of each relator. Depth `0` is trivial. The two corollaries follow from the theorem,
with the commutator width bounds of the two prerequisite nodes.

## 1. Orbits of the root tuple

- **Generation.** By (E1) of `simple-group-relator-width-in-finite-simple-targets-proof`, `<p>` is the normal
  closure in `<p>` of `{r(p)}`.
- **Everything preserves orbits.** Every letter has top `pi r(p) pi^-1` with `pi in <p>`, so every letter and
  every `a_x` preserves each `<p>`-orbit and fixes every `<p>`-fixed coordinate.
- **The two kinds of orbit.** Call an orbit *moving* if it has at least two points, and *fixed*
  otherwise.

**Fixed coordinates.** Let `i` be fixed by `<p>`.
- *Sections are homomorphisms.* Taking the section at `i` is a homomorphism on the stabilizer of `i`. So
  `r(a)_i = r(a^(i))`, and the section at `i` of a letter `C r(a) C^-1` is an `H`-conjugate of
  `r(a^(i))^(+-1)`.
- *Modifying at `i`.* Left-multiplying a conjugator by `(h at i; 1)`, `h in H`, conjugates the letter by
  `(h at i; 1)`. Since the letter fixes `i`, this changes it only at coordinate `i`, by an arbitrary
  `H`-conjugation there.
- *The pairs of Sections 2--4 are trivial at `i`.* Each is a commutator `[u, L]` or a product of two such,
  with `u` either `(1; pi)`, `pi in <p>`, or supported on moving orbits. So its section at `i` is `1`.
- *Recursion.* After the modifications, the sections at `i` of all template pairs are arbitrary pairs for
  the tuple `a^(i)` in `H`. By induction, `a^(i)_x = A(a^(i)) · P^(i)` with at most `M` pairs of each relator,
  so realize `P^(i)` there. Unused pairs take equal modifications and cancel.
- *Independence.* Modifications at different fixed coordinates affect disjoint coordinates.

## 2. Top block

The word `A^-1 x` has exponent sum `0`, so its image lies in `[Phi_m, Phi_m]`.
- **Generators.** By (E1) the relator images normally generate `Phi_m`, so `[Phi_m, Phi_m]` is generated by
  the elements `[phi, psi r^(+-1) psi^-1]`, and a shortest product uses at most `|Phi_m|` of them.
- **Specialization.** Map `Phi_m -> <p>`, `x -> p_x`. This is possible because `<p> <= Sym(m)` lies in the
  variety. So `y_0 = A(p)^-1 p_x` is a product of at most `|Phi_m|` pairs with root conjugators in `<p>`.
- **The block.** Lift the conjugators as `(1; pi)`. The top block `B_top` is these pairs. The base `e` of
  `(A B_top)^-1 a_x` lies in `H^m`.

## 3. Dipole block

For a moving orbit `O`, put `Psi_O(f; q) = prod_(i in O) f_i mod [H, H]` on the stabilizer of `O`.
- **A homomorphism.** It is a homomorphism there, since the order of the product does not matter
  modulo `[H, H]`.
- **`e` has zero sum on `O`.** `A` and `a_x` agree under `Psi_O`, because `Psi_O ∘ a` is a homomorphism
  `F -> H^ab` and exponent sums match. The pairs of Section 2 are commutators in the stabilizer. So
  `Psi_O(e) = 0`.

**Dipole lemma** (`simple-nonsofic-width-over-bounded-base-symmetric-wreath-proof`). If a letter has value
`(g; sigma)` with `sigma(i) != i`, then `[(k at i; 1), (g; sigma)]` equals `k` at `i`, `g_(sigma i) k^-1 g_(sigma i)^-1` at
`sigma(i)`, and `1` elsewhere. It is a pair, and its conjugator tops lie in `<p>`.

- **Edges.** The graph with edges `{i, sigma(i)}`, over the tops `sigma = pi r(p) pi^-1`, `pi in <p>`, `r in R`, has
  components the orbits of the group these tops generate, which is `<p>`. So each moving `O` has a
  spanning tree with `|O| - 1` edges, each realized by a letter moving one endpoint to the other.
- **Solving.** One dipole per edge, at most `m - 1` in total. Strip leaves as in
  `relator-width-bounded-over-simple-iterated-wreath-products-proof`, Section 2. This solves every coordinate
  of `O` except a root `i_O`, where a unique defect `z_O in H` remains.
- **The defect is a commutator product.** Dipoles have `Psi_O = 0`, so `z_O in [H, H]`.

## 4. Defect block

For each moving `O`, write `z_O = prod_(t <= c) [k_t, l_t]` in `H`, using `H in 𝒲`.
- Choose a letter `L` whose top moves `i_O`, and put `u = [(k at i_O; 1), L] = (k at i_O, k' at sigma(i_O); 1)`.
- Then `[u, (l at i_O; 1)] = ([k, l] at i_O; 1)`. It equals `u · ((l at i_O) u^-1 (l at i_O)^-1)`, two pairs.
- So the defects cost at most `2cm` pairs.

## 5. Assembly

- **Moving coordinates.** `a_x = A · B_top · (dipoles) · (defect commutators)`.
- **Fixed coordinates.** Section 1 realizes the recursive decomposition inside the same pairs, without
  changing any other coordinate.
- **Counts.** Reordering into template order, each relator uses at most `|Phi_m| + (m - 1) + 2cm = M` pairs.
