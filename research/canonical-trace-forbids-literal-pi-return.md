ESTABLISHED.  Let `K` be a finite subgroup of a group `Gamma`, let
`y in Gamma`, and let `U_n` be canonical-trace matrix microstates on the
finite word window containing `K`, `y`, and all products `k^(-1)y`.
Write

```text
B_n=span_C { k(U_n) : k in K } subset L^2(M_(d_n),tr).      (CTL1)
```

Then

```text
dist_2(y(U_n),B_n) -> 0    if y in K,
dist_2(y(U_n),B_n) -> 1    if y notin K.                   (CTL2)
```

Indeed the Gram matrix of the packet words converges to the identity, while

```text
<k(U_n),y(U_n)> -> tau_Gamma(k^(-1)y)
```

is zero for every `k in K` exactly when `y notin K`.  The same conclusion
holds after exactifying the fixed finite packet: the rounded packet algebra
is `o(1)` from its word span.

There is a central-phase version.  If `J in K` is central of order `p`, then
on a nonzero spectral phase `zeta`, compress by

```text
P_zeta=(1/p) sum_(c=0)^(p-1) zeta^(-c) J^c.               (CTL3)
```

For `y` commuting with `J`, the compressed overlap with `K` is a finite sum
of canonical moments of `J^c k^(-1)y`; it vanishes unless `y in K` (the
central powers already belong to `K`).  Thus an outside target Pauli word
stays asymptotically orthogonal to the literal source factor even on the
marked phase sector.

Apply this to the source depth-zero Heisenberg packet in the affine-Leavitt
route.  A full depth-one two-qudit target tuple cannot consist entirely of
source-packet elements.  Therefore canonical microstates cannot place all
of its group words near the algebra generated by the literal source packet.
The source algebra in any successful application of the trace-square floor
`(FWC6)` must instead be a **matrix-coordinate recovered reservoir** (for
example a returned multiplicity corner), not the span of a fixed finite
subgroup's words.  Merely adding finitely many word equalities or normalizer
tests cannot supply the PI return.
