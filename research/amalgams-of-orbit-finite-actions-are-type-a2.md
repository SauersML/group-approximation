Type `[A_2]` is as in Fournier-Facio–Wu–Zaremsky (arXiv:2603.24687): the group is
finitely presented, point stabilizers are finitely generated, and there are finitely many
orbits on `S × S`. Faithfulness is not required. *Relative PBH* (their Conjecture 1.8) for
a group `D` means that `D` embeds in a type `[A_2]` actor `P ↷ S` with `D ∩ ker(P ↷ S) = 1`.

## 1. The gluing lemma

**Setting.** `Γ` and `D` are groups acting on one set `S`. `C` is a subgroup of both, and
the two actions restrict to the same action of `C`. Let `P = Γ *_C D`, which acts on `S` by
the universal property. Assume:
- (a) `Γ` and `D` are finitely presented, and `C` is finitely generated;
- (b) `C` has finitely many orbits on `S`;
- (c) for every `s`, the stabilizers `Γ_s`, `D_s` and `C_s` are finitely generated;
- (d) `Γ` has finitely many orbits on `S × S`.

**Theorem.** `P ↷ S` is of type `[A_2]`. Hence `D` satisfies relative PBH as soon as
`D ↷ S` is faithful. So does `Γ`, if it acts faithfully.

**Proof.**
- **Presentation.** `P` is finitely presented by (a).
- **Pair orbits.** `P ⊇ Γ`, so every `P`-orbit on `S × S` is a union of `Γ`-orbits. By (d)
  there are finitely many.
- **Stabilizers.** Fix `s` and put `H = P_s`. By Bass–Serre theory `H` is `π_1` of the graph
  of groups `H\\T`, where `T` is the Bass–Serre tree of `P`.
  - Vertices of type `Γ` are the double cosets `H\P/Γ`.
  - `HpΓ ↦ Γ·p^{-1}s` is a bijection onto the `Γ`-orbits in `P·s`.
    - It is well defined, since `(hpγ)^{-1}s = γ^{-1}p^{-1}s`.
    - It is injective, since `q^{-1}s = γ^{-1}p^{-1}s` forces `pγq^{-1} ∈ H`.
  - Likewise `D`-vertices match `D`-orbits and edges match `C`-orbits in `P·s`. So the
    quotient graph is finite by (b).
  - The vertex group at `HpΓ` is `p Γ_{p^{-1}s} p^{-1}`, finitely generated by (c), and
    similarly for `D`-vertices and edges.
  - The fundamental group of a finite graph of finitely generated groups is finitely
    generated.
- **Sharpness.** `D ∩ ker(P ↷ S) = ker(D ↷ S)`. ∎

**The kernel is free.** Let `K = ker(P ↷ S)`, and suppose `Γ` and `D` both act faithfully.
Then `K` meets every vertex stabilizer of `T` trivially, so `K` acts freely on `T` and is a
free group. PBH for the image `P/K = ⟨Γ, D⟩ ≤ Sym(S)` holds exactly when `K` is finitely
normally generated (FFWZ Question 5.9, kernel removal). Then `P/K` is finitely presented,
faithful, and keeps finite pair orbits and finitely generated stabilizers.

**When the kernel is nontrivial.** Take `a, b ∈ Γ` and `d ∈ D` with `a, a^{-1}ba ∉ C`,
`d ∉ C`, and `d(supp a)` disjoint from `supp b ∪ a^{-1}(supp b)`. Then
`[[d,a],b] = [a^{-1},b]` holds in `Sym(S)`. In `P` the corresponding word is
`d·a·d^{-1}·(a^{-1}ba)·d·a^{-1}·d^{-1}·(a^{-1}b^{-1}a)`, an alternating product of letters
outside `C`, so it is nontrivial by the normal form theorem. This is the amalgam version of
`v-and-a-homeomorphism-never-generate-a-free-product`. It shows `K ≠ 1` whenever `Γ` is
locally moving enough.

## 2. Regular ports

**Corollary (regular holomorphs).** Let `Γ ↷ S` be of type `[A_2]`, and let `C ≤ Γ` be finitely
presented and act on `S` freely and transitively, so `S = C·s_0 ≅ C`. Let `Λ ≤ Aut(C)` be
finitely presented, acting on `S` by `λ(c·s_0) = λ(c)·s_0`.
- **Conclusion.** `D = C ⋊ Λ` satisfies relative PBH, through `Γ *_C D ↷ S`.
- **Stabilizer check.** `D_{s_0} = Λ` and `C_{s_0} = 1`, both finitely generated, and `D` acts
  faithfully (the holomorph acts faithfully on `C`).

**Recovering the outer-extension theorem.** Let `S_0` be finitely presented, simple and MIF,
with `n ≥ 2`. Let `Γ = Aut_{S_0}(S_0 * F_n)` act on `X = Hom_{S_0}(S_0 * F_n, S_0) ≅ S_0^n`,
which is type (A) by BFFHZ Theorem E.
- **A regular subgroup.** `h ↦ α_h`, with `α_h(x_i) = x_i h_i`, is an injective homomorphism
  `S_0^n → Γ`, since `α_h α_k (x_i) = x_i h_i k_i`. It acts by `φ(x_i) ↦ φ(x_i)h_i^{-1}`,
  which is free and transitive.
- **What the corollary gives.** Relative PBH for `S_0^n ⋊ Λ`, for every finitely presented
  `Λ ≤ Aut(S_0) ≀ Sym(n)`.
- **What is known already.** `outer-extensions-of-fp-simple-mif-groups-satisfy-pbh` proves
  PBH, which is stronger. There the automorphisms also normalize `Γ`, and `K` is normally
  generated by the finitely many relators `λγλ^{-1} = β_λ(γ)`, for `γ` a generator of `Γ`.

**Corollary (exact factorizations).** Let `Λ` be finitely presented with an exact factorization
`Λ = C·H`: `H` is finitely generated and core-free, `C` is finitely presented, and
`C ∩ gHg^{-1} = 1` for all `g`. Suppose `C` acts regularly on a type `[A_2]` set `S` of some
`Γ`. Then `Λ` satisfies relative PBH.
- **Why.** `C` acts on `Λ/H` freely and transitively. Identify `Λ/H` with `S` as `C`-sets and
  transport the `Λ`-action.
- **Checks.**
  - The stabilizer of the base coset is `H`, which is finitely generated.
  - `Λ` acts faithfully because `H` is core-free.
  - The gluing lemma applies to `Γ *_C Λ`.
- **Special cases.** Semidirect products `C ⋊ H` recover the holomorph corollary.
  Zappa–Szép products `C ⋈ H`, where neither factor is normal, are new shapes.

**New instances need new regular ports.** A fresh finitely presented `C` acting regularly on
a type (A) orbit, with a rich `Aut(C)`, would export all of `Aut(C)` into relative PBH.
- **Open.** Does `V`, `2V`, or any fp full group, contain a finitely generated subgroup acting
  regularly, or freely with finitely many orbits, on a type (A) orbit?
- **Calibration.** The bit-toggle group `⊕_N Z/2 ≤ V` acts regularly on the eventually-zero
  sequences, but it is not finitely generated. The lamplighter `Z/2 ≀ Z ≤ 2V` acts
  transitively on the finitely supported configurations, but with stabilizer `Z`.

## Lesson for general BH

Pair-orbit finiteness can be borrowed from a type (A) actor through a small orbit-finite
"port" `C`, and every finiteness hypothesis then passes to the amalgam. The only price is the
free kernel of `Γ *_C D ↷ S`. So relative PBH is a local property, while PBH is the global
statement that this free kernel is finitely normally generated.
- **The normalizing case.** When the gluing automorphisms normalize all of `Γ`, the kernel is
  generated by the finitely many normalization relators. That is why the outer-extension
  theorem works.
- **Where the gap sits.** Any general positive answer to FFWZ Question 5.9 (kernel removal)
  turns this lemma into a PBH constructor. It would put `C ⋊ Aut(C)`-type groups into the class
  for every regular port.
- **Conditional reduction.** With kernel removal, PBH for `Λ` follows from one exact
  factorization `Λ = C·H` (core-free `H`) whose factor `C` has a regular port.
