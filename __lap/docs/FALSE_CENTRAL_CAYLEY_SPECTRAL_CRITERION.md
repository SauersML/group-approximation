# The central FALSE criterion as a finite Cayley spectral ratio

## Setup

Let

\[
P=\langle s_1,\ldots ,s_d\mid r_1,\ldots ,r_\ell\rangle=F/N
\]

and let \(w\) represent a nonidentity element of
\(\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(P)\).  For a finite quotient
\(P\twoheadrightarrow Q\), put \(L=\ker(F\to Q)\) and

\[
C_Q=L/[F,L].
\]

Let \(A_Q\leq C_Q\) be generated by the relator classes
\(a_1,\ldots ,a_\ell\), and let \(b_Q\) be the class of \(w\).  Radicality
implies \(b_Q\in A_Q\).  Passing to a finite quotient of \(A_Q\), when
necessary, gives a finite abelian group \(A\), relator labels
\(a_1,\ldots ,a_\ell\in A\), and a marked label \(b\in A\).

## Two translation Laplacians

Let \(T_a\) denote translation by \(a\) on \(\ell^2(A)\).  Define

\[
\Delta_R
:=
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(I-T_{a_j})^*(I-T_{a_j})
\]

and

\[
\Delta_b:=(I-T_b)^*(I-T_b).
\]

Since the \(a_j\) generate \(A\), the kernel of \(\Delta_R\) consists of
the constants.  Hence \(\ker\Delta_R\subseteq\ker\Delta_b\).

### Theorem 1 (exact spectral ratio)

The least constant \(C(A,b)\) for which

\[
\Delta_b\leq C(A,b)^2\Delta_R
\]

is

\[
\boxed{
C(A,b)^2
=
\max_{\chi\in\widehat A\,:\,R(\chi)>0}
\frac{|\chi(b)-1|^2}
{\sum_j|\chi(a_j)-1|^2}.
}
\]

#### Proof

The Fourier characters form an orthonormal basis of \(\ell^2(A)\).  On the
line spanned by \(\chi\), the two operators have eigenvalues

\[
\Delta_R:\quad
R(\chi)=\sum_j|\chi(a_j)-1|^2
\]

and

\[
\Delta_b:\quad
W(\chi)=|\chi(b)-1|^2.
\]

The operator inequality holds if and only if
\(W(\chi)\leq C^2R(\chi)\) on every Fourier line.  Taking the largest ratio
proves the formula. \(\square\)

Thus the central character invariant is exactly the operator norm

\[
\left\|\Delta_b^{1/2}\Delta_R^{-1/2}\right\|
\]

on the orthogonal complement of the constants.  It is a maximum spectral
ratio, not an average such as effective resistance.

## Consequence for FALSE

### Theorem 2 (spectral divergence criterion)

Suppose there are finite quotients \(P\twoheadrightarrow Q_n\) and finite
quotients \(A_n\) of their integral relation groups \(A_{Q_n}\) such that
the images of \(b_{Q_n}\) satisfy

\[
C(A_n,b_n)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Then there exists a hyperlinear nonsofic group.

#### Proof

Choose a maximizing character \(\chi_n\).  The exact spectral formula gives

\[
\frac{\sum_j|\chi_n(a_{j,n})-1|^2}
{|\chi_n(b_n)-1|^2}
=C(A_n,b_n)^{-2}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Powering \(\chi_n\) makes its marked value have chordal distance at least
\(\sqrt2\) from \(1\), while the total relator energy remains at most
\(\pi^2C(A_n,b_n)^{-2}\).  The canonical finite central cover and its
induced projective-regular representation then give Hilbert--Schmidt
asymptotic representations retaining \(w\).  Their tracial-ultraproduct
image is hyperlinear and cannot be sofic because \(w\) lies in the sofic
radical of \(P\). \(\square\)

This criterion is equivalent to divergence of the canonical central
character constant.  It is not asserted to capture noncentral matrix-defect
constructions.

## Exact cyclic formula

Suppose

\[
A=\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z,
\qquad
a_j=c_j\pmod M,
\qquad
b=d\pmod M.
\]

Writing \(\chi_t(x)=e^{2\pi itx/M}\), one obtains

\[
\boxed{
C(A,b)^2
=
\max_{t\in\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z}
\frac{\sin^2(\pi dt/M)}
{\sum_j\sin^2(\pi c_jt/M)},
}
\]

with terms having zero denominator omitted.  This is the exact one-cycle
Fourier calculation.  There are no matrix variables and no nonlinear
optimization: only the residue classes \(c_j\) of the fixed relators and the
marked residue \(d\) remain to be identified.

For the tower \(M=p^k\), FALSE follows if one can choose frequencies \(t_k\)
such that

\[
\sum_j\sin^2(\pi c_{j,k}t_k/p^k)\longrightarrow0
\]

while

\[
\left|\sin(\pi d_kt_k/p^k)\right|
\]

stays bounded below.

## Relation to integral filling

Let

\[
\delta_A(b)=min\left\{\|v\|_2:
b=\sum_jv_ja_j, v\in\mathbb Z^\ell\right\}.
\]

Telescoping gives

\[
C(A,b)\leq\delta_A(b).
\]

Thus spectral divergence requires unbounded integral filling distortion, but
for a fixed number of generators the converse holds uniformly at the level
of family divergence. The fixed-rank character compactness theorem proves

\[
\sup_i C(A_i,b_i)=\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sup_i\delta_{A_i}(b_i)=\infty
\]

for every family with a fixed number of ordered generators. Its proof uses
Hausdorff compactness of character graphs in a fixed torus and exactness of
Pontryagin duality. See `FALSE_FIXED_RANK_CHARACTER_COMPACTNESS.md`.

For a fixed individual pair \((A,b)\), the numerical gap between
\(C(A,b)\) and \(\delta_A(b)\) is still governed by paired
discriminant--Voronoi geometry. The compactness theorem is qualitative and
does not give an effective bound.

For the fold-aligned Baumslag--Solitar construction, the remaining symbolic
task would have been:

1. realize the integral transgression generator in a finite quotient of
   \(A_Q=L/[F,L]\);
2. write the fixed presentation relators as residues \(c_{j,k}\);
3. evaluate the displayed sine ratio along \(M=p^k\).

The already-proved order formula for the marked class supplies item 1 only
partially. It does not determine the relator residues required in item 2.
