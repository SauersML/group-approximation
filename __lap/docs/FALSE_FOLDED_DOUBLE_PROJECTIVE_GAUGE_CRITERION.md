# Exact projective-gauge criterion for a folded double

## Setup

**Closure for the current Kun--Thom fold.**  The criterion below is valid,
but its same-projective-model ansatz cannot retain the present marked word
`h=t^(-1) gamma t` with `gamma in Gamma`.  Exponent sum gives
`e(h)=e(gamma)`, so the displayed marked defect is exactly the scalar-gauge
defect of an amalgamated edge word and tends to zero with the edge defects.
See `FALSE_FOLDED_DOUBLE_SAME_MODEL_NO_GO.md`.  A live construction must use
distinct relative Schur classes on the two vertices, not one common class
with two scalar gauges.

Let

\[
G=\langle x_1,\ldots ,x_d\mid r_1,\ldots ,r_\ell\rangle
\]

be finitely presented.  Let \(\Gamma\leq G\) be generated by the values of
words

\[
u_1,\ldots ,u_s\in F_d.
\]

The corresponding double has presentation

\[
D=G*_\Gamma G
=
\left\langle
x^{(1)}_1,\ldots ,x^{(1)}_d,
x^{(2)}_1,\ldots ,x^{(2)}_d
\ \middle|\
r_j^{(i)},
u_a^{(2)}(u_a^{(1)})^{-1}
\right\rangle.
\]

Fix a word \(h\in F_d\) and the fold word

\[
w=h^{(2)}(h^{(1)})^{-1}.
\]

Assume that \(w\neq1\) and \(w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(D)\), as in
the Kun--Thom construction.

For a word \(v\in F_d\), let

\[
e(v)=(e_1(v),\ldots ,e_d(v))\in\mathbb Z^d
\]

be its exponent-sum vector.

## A finite projective vertex model

Let \(\pi:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\) be a finite quotient and let

\[
1\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow1
\]

be a finite central extension.  Fix a character \(\chi:Z\to\mathbb T\),
extend it if necessary to the full central kernel, and take the induced
projective-regular representation of \(Q\).  Choose unitary lifts

\[
U_1,\ldots ,U_d
\]

of \(\pi(x_1),\ldots ,\pi(x_d)\).

For each relator, evaluation is scalar:

\[
r_j(U_1,\ldots ,U_d)=\zeta_j I,
\qquad \zeta_j\in\mathbb T.
\]

If \(t=(t_1,\ldots ,t_d)\in\mathbb T^d\), define the gauge-changed lifts

\[
U_a^{,t}:=t_aU_a.
\]

Then every word satisfies

\[
v(U^t)=t^{e(v)}v(U),
\qquad
t^n:=\prod_at_a^{n_a}.
\]

In particular,

\[
r_j(U^t)=\zeta_jt^{e(r_j)}I.
\]

## Exact two-gauge formula

Use \(U^{t_1}\) for the first vertex copy and \(U^{t_2}\) for the second.
Put

\[
q:=t_2t_1^{-1}\in\mathbb T^d.
\]

### Theorem 1 (all defects are scalar and explicit)

The normalized Hilbert--Schmidt defects of the defining relators of \(D\)
are exactly:

\[
\|r_j(U^{t_i})-I\|_{2,\rm norm}
=|\zeta_jt_i^{e(r_j)}-1|,
\]

and

\[
\left\|
u_a(U^{t_2})u_a(U^{t_1})^{-1}-I
\right\|_{2,\rm norm}
=|q^{e(u_a)}-1|.
\]

The marked displacement is

\[
\boxed{
\left\|
h(U^{t_2})h(U^{t_1})^{-1}-I
\right\|_{2,\rm norm}
=|q^{e(h)}-1|.
}
\]

#### Proof

The relator formula was established above.  For an arbitrary word \(v\),

\[
v(U^{t_2})v(U^{t_1})^{-1}
=t_2^{e(v)}t_1^{-e(v)}v(U)v(U)^{-1}
=q^{e(v)}I.
\]

Apply this to each \(u_a\) and to \(h\).  Scalar matrices have normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm equal to the modulus of their scalar. \(\square\)

The crucial cancellation is exact: the reference projective phase of
\(v(U)\) disappears from the ratio of the two vertex evaluations.  No
operator-valued covariance estimate remains.

## Completion criterion

Define the paired defect energy

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_Q(t_1,t_2)^2
:={}&
\sum_{i=1}^2\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}
|\zeta_jt_i^{e(r_j)}-1|^2\\
&+
\sum_{a=1}^{s}|(t_2t_1^{-1})^{e(u_a)}-1|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

and the marked energy

\[
\mathcal W_Q(t_1,t_2)
:=|(t_2t_1^{-1})^{e(h)}-1|.
\]

### Theorem 2 (folded projective-gauge criterion)

Suppose there are finite quotients \(Q_k\), finite central extensions and
characters as above, and gauges \(t_{1,k},t_{2,k}\in\mathbb T^d\) such that

\[
\mathcal E_{Q_k}(t_{1,k},t_{2,k})\longrightarrow0
\]

while

\[
\liminf_k\mathcal W_{Q_k}(t_{1,k},t_{2,k})>0.
\]

Then there exists a hyperlinear nonsofic group.

#### Proof

Theorem 1 gives unitary assignments to the fixed presentation generators of
\(D\) for which every defining relator tends to the identity in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm while \(w\) stays separated.  They induce a
homomorphism

\[
D\longrightarrow\prod_{k\to\omega}U(n_k)
\]

into a tracial matrix ultraproduct which retains \(w\).  Its image is
hyperlinear.  Every homomorphism from \(D\) to a sofic group kills
\(\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(D)\), so the image cannot be sofic. \(\square\)

## The nonlinear discriminant interface

Let \(R\) be the \(\ell\times d\) exponent-sum matrix with rows \(e(r_j)\),
let \(U\) be the \(s\times d\) matrix with rows \(e(u_a)\), and let
\(v=e(h)\).

Writing \(t_i=e^{2\pi ix_i}\) and \(\zeta=e^{2\pi iy}\), the defect energy
is the circle-valued least-squares problem

\[
d_{\mathbb T^\ell}(y+Rx_1,0)^2
+d_{\mathbb T^\ell}(y+Rx_2,0)^2
+d_{\mathbb T^s}(U(x_2-x_1),0)^2,
\]

while the witness is

\[
d_{\mathbb T}(v\cdot(x_2-x_1),0).
\]

Thus the exact integral-carry/discriminant formulas developed on the TRUE
side apply verbatim to the final FALSE gauge problem.  The curvature phase
vector \(y\) is the input discriminant class; two inequivalent near-optimal
integral repairs produce the fold witness.

The mechanism is necessarily nonlinear.  Over the real tangent space,
normal-closure constraints force the witness direction to be controlled by
the relator and amalgam directions.  Separation can occur only through
distinct integral lifts/discriminant classes.
