# Gibbs localization of a central radical witness

Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Result

This note gives a logarithmic-dimension norm-to-trace bridge for a central
element.  It is strictly stronger, in this setting, than the
square-root-dimension fermionic criterion in
`FALSE_FERMIONIC_AMPLIFICATION.md`.

Let `Rad_sof(H)` be the intersection of the kernels of all homomorphisms
from `H` to sofic groups.  Enumerate a countable group as

`H={g_1,g_2,...}`.                                              `(GCT1)`

**Theorem 1 (Gibbs central-radical criterion).**  Suppose

`1!=z in Z(H) intersection Rad_sof(H)`                           `(GCT2)`

and there are dimensions `d_n` and operator-norm asymptotic
representations

`U_n:H->U(d_n)`.                                                `(GCT3)`

Put `Z_n=U_n(z)` and

`delta_n=max_(1<=j<=n)||Z_n U_n(g_j)-U_n(g_j) Z_n||_op`.        `(GCT4)`

Assume that, after passage to a subsequence, for some `c>0`,

`||Z_n-1||_op>=c`,                                             `(GCT5)`

and

`delta_n(1+log d_n)->0`.                                       `(GCT6)`

Then there is a hyperlinear nonsofic group.  Equivalently, the conclusion
of the main FALSE problem follows from `(GCT2)--(GCT6)`.

Only the commutation defects involving the distinguished central element
need the logarithmic rate.  All other multiplication defects in `(GCT3)`
may tend to zero arbitrarily slowly.

For a finitely generated group, if `epsilon_n` bounds the multiplication
defects on an increasing family containing the pairs `(z,g_j)` and
`(g_j,z)`, then `delta_n<=C epsilon_n` for a universal word-telescoping
constant.  Hence the simpler sufficient condition is

`epsilon_n(1+log d_n)->0`.                                    `(GCT7)`

## 2. A weighted Folner lemma

Write `Tr` for the unnormalized matrix trace and
`||a||_1=Tr(|a|)`.

**Lemma 2 (weighted matrix states give an amenable trace).**  Let `H` be
countable, let `(U_n)` be as in `(GCT3)`, and let `D_n in M_(d_n)` be
density matrices:

`D_n>=0`, `Tr(D_n)=1`.                                        `(GCT8)`

Suppose, for every fixed `g in H`,

`||U_n(g)D_n-D_nU_n(g)||_1->0`.                               `(GCT9)`

Then every pointwise ultralimit

`chi(g)=lim_omega Tr(D_n U_n(g))`                              `(GCT10)`

is an amenable tracial character of `H`.  Consequently its GNS group image
is hyperlinear.

**Proof.**  Let `F` be the free group on symbols `x_g`, `g in H`.  For each
`n`, the assignment

`x_g |-> U_n(g)`                                               `(GCT11)`

extends to an honest finite-dimensional representation `pi_n:F->U(d_n)`.
Operator-norm asymptotic multiplicativity says that, if two free words have
the same image in `H`, their `pi_n`-images converge to one another in
operator norm.  Thus the pointwise limit in `(GCT10)` is well defined on
`H`, and positivity follows from positivity of the finite-dimensional
matrix states.

For a fixed free word `w`, `(GCT9)` and a finite trace-norm telescoping sum
give

`||pi_n(w)D_n-D_n pi_n(w)||_1->0`.                            `(GCT12)`

Consequently `(GCT10)` is conjugation invariant, hence tracial.

For amenability, put `K=directSum_n C^(d_n)` and
`pi=directSum_n pi_n`.  If `P_n` is the projection onto the `n`th summand,
define states on `B(K)` by

`Omega_n(T)=Tr(D_n P_n T P_n)`.                               `(GCT13)`

Take a weak-star ultralimit `Omega`.  Equations `(GCT9)` and `(GCT12)`
give

`Omega(pi(w)T)=Omega(T pi(w))`, `w in F`, `T in B(K)`.         `(GCT14)`

The restriction of `Omega` to `C^*(pi(F))` is the character `(GCT10)`.
Thus it is a hypertrace.  By the standard hypertrace characterization of
amenable traces, the corresponding trace on `C^*(F)` is amenable.

Pull this amenable trace back from the concrete algebra `C^*(pi(F))` to
`C^*(F)`.  The matrix characterization of amenable traces gives a
trace-preserving star-homomorphism

`Theta:C^*(F)->product_omega(M_(k_m),tr_(k_m))`               `(GCT14a)`

whose trace is `chi` (equivalently, it is obtained from ucp maps which are
asymptotically multiplicative in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm).

The trace-kernel contains every relator `x_g x_h x_(gh)^(-1)`, because its
original matrix image tends to `1` in operator norm.  Since `Theta` is
trace preserving, it kills the closed ideal generated by these relators in
the tracial ultraproduct.  Thus `(GCT14a)` factors at the group level through
`H`.  Its image is hyperlinear.  Notice that this argument does **not** use
the generally delicate assertion that amenable traces descend through
arbitrary C-star quotients.  End proof.

The lemma is useful because the coordinate states need not be normalized
matrix traces.  Trace-norm asymptotic invariance is exactly what converts
the weighted states into a hypertrace; no unjustified claim that an
arbitrary weighted matrix ultraproduct is tracial is being made.

## 3. Gibbs concentration and almost invariance

For a unitary `Z`, put

`h=(Z-1)^*(Z-1)=2-Z-Z^*`, `0<=h<=4`.                         `(GCT15)`

For `beta>0`, define its Gibbs density

`D_(beta,h)=exp(beta h)/Tr(exp(beta h))`.                    `(GCT16)`

Two elementary estimates drive the proof.

**Lemma 3 (entropy concentration).**  If `h in M_d` is positive, then

`Tr(D_(beta,h) h)>=||h||_op-(log d)/beta`.                   `(GCT17)`

**Proof.**  Let `D=D_(beta,h)` and let
`S(D)=-Tr(D log D)` be its von Neumann entropy.  Since

`log Tr(exp(beta h))=beta Tr(Dh)+S(D)`                       `(GCT18)`

and `S(D)<=log d`, while

`log Tr(exp(beta h))>=beta||h||_op`,                         `(GCT19)`

rearrangement gives `(GCT17)`.  End proof.

There is a sharper form which measures the actual top spectral
multiplicity.  Put `M=||h||_op`, fix `eta>0`, and let

`r=rank(1_([M-eta,M])(h))`.                                 `(GCT17a)`

Then

`Tr(D_(beta,h)h)>=M-eta-log(d/r)/beta`.                     `(GCT17b)`

Indeed the top band gives

`log Tr(exp(beta h))>=log r+beta(M-eta)`,                   `(GCT17c)`

and combining this with `(GCT18)` and `S(D)<=log d` proves
`(GCT17b)`.

**Lemma 4 (Gibbs transport).**  If `U` is unitary and
`D=D_(beta,h)`, then

`||UDU^*-D||_1^2<=2 beta ||UhU^*-h||_op`.                   `(GCT20)`

**Proof.**  Put `D'=UDU^*`.  The partition functions of `h` and `UhU^*`
are equal.  Therefore the quantum relative entropy satisfies

`Ent(D'|D)=beta Tr(D'(UhU^*-h))`
`         <=beta||UhU^*-h||_op`.                            `(GCT21)`

Pinsker's inequality

`||D'-D||_1^2<=2 Ent(D'|D)`                                 `(GCT22)`

proves `(GCT20)`.  End proof.

For `h_n=(Z_n-1)^*(Z_n-1)`, `(GCT4)` gives, for `j<=n`,

`||U_n(g_j)h_nU_n(g_j)^*-h_n||_op<=2 delta_n`.              `(GCT23)`

## 4. Proof of Theorem 1

Put `ell_n=1+log d_n`.  Condition `(GCT6)` allows a choice
`beta_n>0` such that

`beta_n delta_n->0`, `ell_n/beta_n->0`.                     `(GCT24)`

For example, when `delta_n>0`, take

`beta_n=sqrt(ell_n/delta_n)`;                                `(GCT25)`

zero values of `delta_n` can be handled by any sufficiently rapidly growing
`beta_n`.

Set

`h_n=(Z_n-1)^*(Z_n-1)`,
`D_n=exp(beta_n h_n)/Tr(exp(beta_n h_n))`.                  `(GCT26)`

Equations `(GCT20)`, `(GCT23)`, and `(GCT24)` imply, for every fixed
`g_j`,

`||U_n(g_j)D_nU_n(g_j)^*-D_n||_1`
` <=2 sqrt(beta_n delta_n)->0.                              `(GCT27)`

Lemma 2 therefore gives an amenable tracial character `chi` of `H` and a
hyperlinear GNS group image `L`.

The character retains `z`.  Indeed `(GCT5)` gives

`||h_n||_op=||Z_n-1||_op^2>=c^2`,                          `(GCT28)`

and Lemma 3 gives

`Tr(D_n h_n)>=c^2-(log d_n)/beta_n -> c^2`.                `(GCT29)`

Consequently, in the GNS representation associated to `chi`,

`||(u_z-1)xi_chi||^2`
` =chi((u_z-1)^*(u_z-1))>=c^2>0.                           `(GCT30)`

Thus the quotient map `H->L` does not kill `z`.  If `L` were sofic, that
map would kill every element of `Rad_sof(H)`, contradicting `(GCT2)` and
`(GCT30)`.  Hence `L` is hyperlinear and nonsofic.  End proof.

The same proof gives the following strictly sharper statement.

**Theorem 1A (spectral-entropy version).**  Under `(GCT2)--(GCT5)`, put

`M_n=||(Z_n-1)^*(Z_n-1)||_op`,                              `(GCT30a)`

fix `0<eta<c^2`, and put

`r_n=rank(1_([M_n-eta,M_n])((Z_n-1)^*(Z_n-1)))`,
`L_n=1+log(d_n/r_n)`.                                       `(GCT30b)`

It is enough to assume

`delta_n L_n->0`.                                           `(GCT30c)`

**Proof.**  Choose `beta_n` with

`beta_n delta_n->0`, `L_n/beta_n->0`,                       `(GCT30d)`

and use `(GCT17b)` in place of `(GCT17)`.  The limiting weighted energy is
at least `c^2-eta>0`; the remainder of the proof is unchanged.  End proof.

Since `r_n>=1`, one has `L_n<=1+log d_n`, so Theorem 1 is the worst-case
rank-one corollary.  If the top spectral band has a fixed positive relative
rank, `L_n` stays bounded and **no quantitative rate beyond centrality
convergence is needed**.

## 5. Exact scope and sharpness of the method

The theorem yields several useful special cases.

1. If the distinguished central radical unitary is represented by matrices
   which commute **exactly** with every fixed group generator, no
   dimension/error rate is needed.
2. A central-radical weak-MF model with centrality defect
   `o(1/log d_n)` solves the main problem.
3. The conclusion uses only one tracial image retaining `z`; it does not
   claim that the whole group `H` is hyperlinear.

The logarithmic cost is intrinsic to zero-temperature Gibbs localization.
For the rank-one spike

`h=diag(a,0,...,0)`, `a>0`,                                `(GCT31)`

one has exactly

`Tr(D_(beta,h)h)=a exp(beta a)/(exp(beta a)+d-1).           `(GCT32)`

Keeping `(GCT32)` bounded away from zero requires
`beta a>=log d-O(1)`.  Thus the entropy term in `(GCT17)` cannot be removed
for this localization scheme.

This calibration fits the previously proved regular finite-quotient
barrier: the natural exact-factor models of the Kun--Thom witness satisfy a
lower bound of order `1/log d` on the relevant commutation defect.  They sit
exactly at, rather than below, the Gibbs threshold.  A successful model
must again be genuinely nonliftable, but the required profile is now
logarithmic rather than square-root dimensional.

## 6. Application target

The free central radical jet in `FALSE_FREE_CENTRAL_RADICAL_JET.md` produces
an explicit finitely generated (and, from finitely presented input,
finitely presented) group

`J(G,s)=<G,t | [s,t] central>`                              `(GCT33)`

with an infinite-order element

`1!=z=[s,t] in Z(J(G,s)) intersection Rad_sof(J(G,s))`.     `(GCT34)`

Therefore any operator-norm model of this jet satisfying `(GCT6)` proves
FALSE.  What remains open is weak MF of the noncentral shear HNN extension,
or a direct construction of such logarithmic-profile models.  Shulman's
central-HNN theorem does not apply to `(GCT33)`, because its edge map is the
nontrivial unipotent shear `s |-> zs`.
