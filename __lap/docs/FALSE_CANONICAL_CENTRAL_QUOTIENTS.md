# Canonical finite central quotients of the relation-centralized presentation

## Purpose

Let

\[
P=\langle s_1,\ldots ,s_d\mid r_1,\ldots ,r_\ell\rangle
=F/N
\]

be finitely presented, and let the image of a word \(w\in F\) be a
nonidentity element of \(\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(P)\).  The universal
relation-centralized group is

\[
\widetilde P:=F/[F,N].
\]

Its central subgroup

\[
A:=N/[F,N]
\]

is generated, as an abelian group, by the images of the finitely many
presentation relators.  The point of this note is that every finite quotient
of \(\widetilde P\) is dominated by an entirely canonical finite central
extension attached to a finite quotient of \(P\).  Consequently the central
separation ratio can be searched using ordinary finite quotients and a finite
abelian relation module.

The fixed-rank character compactness theorem now makes the final criterion
purely integral: because the number of presentation relators is fixed, the
canonical central route proves FALSE if and only if the integral coefficient
distortions \(\delta_Q(w)\) are unbounded. See
`FALSE_FIXED_RANK_CHARACTER_COMPACTNESS.md`.

**Closure update for the explicit symmetric double.** Its full finite
residual \(R\) satisfies \(R=[D,R]\). This produces one fixed relator
coefficient vector in every canonical module, so

\[
\sup_Q\delta_Q(w)<\infty
\]

for every radical word \(w\in R\). Hence the criterion below is exact but
cannot prove FALSE for the current Kun--Thom double. See
`FALSE_FINITE_RESIDUAL_COMMUTATOR_NO_GO.md`.

## The canonical cover

Fix a finite quotient

\[
\pi:P\twoheadrightarrow Q
\]

and also write \(\pi:F\twoheadrightarrow Q\).  Put

\[
L:=\ker(F\to Q).
\]

Thus \(N\leq L\).  For an integer \(m\geq2\), define

\[
J_{Q,m}:=[F,L]L^m,
\qquad
E_{Q,m}:=F/J_{Q,m},
\qquad
C_{Q,m}:=L/J_{Q,m}.
\]

Here \(L^m\) denotes the subgroup generated by the \(m\)-th powers of
elements of \(L\).

### Theorem 1 (canonical finite central cover)

There is an exact sequence

\[
1\longrightarrow C_{Q,m}\longrightarrow E_{Q,m}
\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow1
\]

with the following properties.

1. \(C_{Q,m}\) is a finite central abelian group of exponent dividing \(m\).
2. \(E_{Q,m}\) is finite.
3. The quotient map \(F\to E_{Q,m}\) factors through \(\widetilde P\).
4. If \(a_j\in C_{Q,m}\) is the image of \(r_j\), and \(b\in C_{Q,m}\)
   is the image of \(w\), then
   \[
   b\in A_{Q,m}:=\langle a_1,\ldots ,a_\ell\rangle.
   \]

#### Proof

The subgroup \([F,L]L^m\) is normal in \(F\) and is contained in \(L\).
Killing \([F,L]\) makes the image of \(L\) central, and killing \(L^m\)
makes that image have exponent dividing \(m\).  Hence \(C_{Q,m}\) is central
abelian of exponent dividing \(m\).

Since \(Q\) is finite, \(L\) has finite index in the finitely generated free
group \(F\), so \(L\) is finitely generated.  Its quotient \(C_{Q,m}\) is
therefore a finitely generated abelian group of finite exponent, hence is
finite.  The displayed extension then shows that \(E_{Q,m}\) is finite.

Because \(N\leq L\), one has

\[
[F,N]\leq[F,L]\leq J_{Q,m},
\]

which proves the factorization through \(\widetilde P\).

Finally, \(E_{Q,m}/A_{Q,m}\) is a finite quotient of
\(\widetilde P/A=P\).  The image of \(w\) in every finite quotient of \(P\)
is trivial because \(\bar w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(P)\).  Therefore
the image \(b\) of \(w\) in \(E_{Q,m}\) lies in \(A_{Q,m}\).  \(\square\)

## Cofinality

The preceding construction is not merely a convenient source of finite
quotients.  It dominates every finite quotient of \(\widetilde P\).

### Theorem 2 (cofinality among finite quotients)

Let

\[
\varphi:\widetilde P\twoheadrightarrow E
\]

be a finite quotient.  Let \(A_E=\varphi(A)\), set \(Q=E/A_E\), and let
\(L\) be the kernel of the induced map \(F\to Q\).  If \(m\) is any multiple
of the exponent of \(A_E\), then \(\varphi\) factors as

\[
\widetilde P\longrightarrow E_{Q,m}\longrightarrow E.
\]

#### Proof

The image in \(E\) of every element of \(L\) lies in the central group
\(A_E\).  Hence \([F,L]\) is killed by \(F\to E\).  The choice of \(m\)
also makes \(L^m\) map trivially.  Thus \([F,L]L^m\) is contained in the
kernel of \(F\to E\), which is exactly the asserted factorization. \(\square\)

Consequently, all finite central-label tests on \(\widetilde P\) can be
performed on the modules \(C_{Q,m}\).

## The projective regular sector

The canonical cover produces the required matrices without any additional
extension theorem.

### Proposition 3 (central character to matrices)

Let \(\chi:A_{Q,m}\to\mathbb T\) be a character.  Then there is a unitary
representation

\[
\rho_\chi:E_{Q,m}\longrightarrow U(V_\chi)
\]

with \(\dim V_\chi\leq |Q|\,|C_{Q,m}/A_{Q,m}|\), such that

\[
\rho_\chi(a)=\chi(a)I
\qquad(a\in A_{Q,m}).
\]

In particular,

\[
\rho_\chi(r_j)=\chi(a_j)I,
\qquad
\rho_\chi(w)=\chi(b)I.
\]

#### Proof

Since \(\mathbb T\) is divisible, \(\chi\) extends to a character
\(\widetilde\chi:C_{Q,m}\to\mathbb T\).  Take

\[
\rho_\chi=operatorname{Ind}_{C_{Q,m}}^{E_{Q,m}}
(\widetilde\chi).
\]

The subgroup \(C_{Q,m}\) is central, so every \(c\in C_{Q,m}\) acts in the
induced representation by the scalar \(\widetilde\chi(c)\).  The induced
space has dimension \([E_{Q,m}:C_{Q,m}]=|Q|\).  Thus the stated, slightly
weaker, dimension bound holds, and restriction to \(A_{Q,m}\) gives the
displayed formula. \(\square\)

Equivalently, after choosing a section \(Q\to E_{Q,m}\), this is the twisted
left-regular projective representation of \(Q\) associated to the pushout of
the central extension along \(\widetilde\chi\).  The presentation relators
are precisely its scalar multiplier defects.

## The finite relation-module ratio

For a character \(\chi:A_{Q,m}\to\mathbb T\), define

\[
R_{Q,m}(\chi)
:=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}|\chi(a_j)-1|^2,
\qquad
W_{Q,m}(\chi):=|\chi(b)-1|^2.
\]

Characters of \(A_{Q,m}\) extend to \(C_{Q,m}\), since \(\mathbb T\) is a
divisible abelian group, although the extension is not needed for the scalar
ratio itself.

### Theorem 4 (finite central ratio criterion)

If there are finite quotients \(Q_n\) of \(P\), integers \(m_n\geq2\), and
characters \(\chi_n\in\widehat{A_{Q_n,m_n}}\) such that

\[
W_{Q_n,m_n}(\chi_n)>0
\]

and

\[
\frac{R_{Q_n,m_n}(\chi_n)}
{W_{Q_n,m_n}(\chi_n)}\longrightarrow0,
\]

then there exists a hyperlinear nonsofic group.

#### Proof

Write \(\chi_n(b)=e^{i\theta_n}\) with
\(0<|\theta_n|\leq\pi\).  Choose an integer \(k_n\) so that

\[
|\chi_n(b)^{k_n}-1|\geq\sqrt2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
k_n\leq\frac{\pi}{|\chi_n(b)-1|}.
\]

For example, take \(k_n=1\) when \(|\theta_n|\geq\pi/2\), and otherwise
take \(k_n=\lceil\pi/(2|\theta_n|)\rceil\).  The elementary estimate

\[
|z^k-1|\leq k|z-1|
\]

gives

\[
\sum_j|\chi_n(a_j)^{k_n}-1|^2
\leq
\pi^2\frac{R_{Q_n,m_n}(\chi_n)}
{W_{Q_n,m_n}(\chi_n)}longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus the powered characters make all fixed relators asymptotically trivial
while keeping the marked radical word macroscopically visible.  Apply
Proposition 3 to the powered characters.  Sending each presentation generator
to its image under \(\rho_{\chi_n^{k_n}}\) gives a sequence of unitary models
whose relator defects tend to zero in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, while
the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt displacement of \(w\) is at least \(\sqrt2\).
The induced homomorphism of \(P\) into the tracial matrix ultraproduct
therefore retains \(w\).  Its image is hyperlinear.  Since \(w\) is in the
sofic radical of \(P\), every homomorphism from \(P\) to a sofic group kills
\(w\); hence this hyperlinear image is not sofic. \(\square\)

## Spectral mixtures give no extra freedom

The scalar character criterion already captures arbitrary finite-dimensional
representations of the central relation group.

Let \(\rho:A_{Q,m}\to U(V)\) be finite dimensional.  Because
\(A_{Q,m}\) is finite abelian, \(V\) decomposes into character spaces.  If
\(\mu_\rho\) is the normalized multiplicity measure on
\(\widehat{A_{Q,m}}\), then

\[
\sum_j\|\rho(a_j)-I\|_{2,\mathrm{norm}}^2
=\int R_{Q,m}(\chi)\,d\mu_\rho(\chi)
\]

and

\[
\|\rho(b)-I\|_{2,\mathrm{norm}}^2
=\int W_{Q,m}(\chi)\,d\mu_\rho(\chi).
\]

### Proposition 5 (Dirac extraction)

If the second integral is positive, there is a character \(\chi\) with
\(W_{Q,m}(\chi)>0\) and

\[
\frac{R_{Q,m}(\chi)}{W_{Q,m}(\chi)}
\leq
\frac{\int R_{Q,m}\,d\mu_\rho}
{\int W_{Q,m}\,d\mu_\rho}.
\]

#### Proof

On the set where \(W>0\), the quotient of the two integrals is a
\(W\,d\mu_\rho\)-weighted average of \(R/W\), with an additional
nonnegative contribution from the set where \(W=0\).  Some point in the
support must therefore have ratio no larger than the displayed average.
\(\square\)

It follows that direct sums, character mixtures, and central spectral
measures cannot beat the best scalar character.  If a finite-dimensional
central spectral construction has vanishing relator energy and positive
marked energy, Proposition 5 extracts scalar characters satisfying Theorem 4.

## Homological form

Before reduction modulo \(m\), the central kernel is

\[
C_Q:=L/[F,L]=H_1(L;\mathbb Z)_Q.
\]

The five-term exact sequence for \(1\to L\to F\to Q\to1\), together with
\(H_2(F;\mathbb Z)=0\), gives

\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(Q;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow C_Q
\longrightarrow H_1(F;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H_1(Q;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Moreover,

\[
C_{Q,m}\cong C_Q/mC_Q.
\]

Let \(A_Q\leq C_Q\) be the subgroup generated by the integral relator
classes \(a_1,\ldots ,a_\ell\), and let \(b_Q\in C_Q\) be the class of
\(w\).

### Theorem 6 (integral radical containment)

For every finite quotient \(P\twoheadrightarrow Q\), one has

\[
b_Q\in A_Q.
\]

#### Proof

Let \(K=\ker(P\to Q)=L/N\).  There is a canonical identification

\[
C_Q/A_Q
=L/(N[F,L])
\cong K/[P,K].
\]

This is a finitely generated abelian group, central in
\(P/[P,K]\), whose quotient is the finite group \(Q\).  If the image of
\(b_Q\) in \(C_Q/A_Q\) were nonzero, residual finiteness of finitely
generated abelian groups would give a finite quotient of \(C_Q/A_Q\) in
which that image remained nonzero.  Quotienting \(P/[P,K]\) by the
corresponding finite-index central subgroup would then give a finite quotient
of \(P\) separating \(w\).  This contradicts
\(w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(P)\). \(\square\)

Define the integral coefficient distortion

\[
\delta_Q(w):=min\left\{
\|v\|_2:
v=(v_1,\ldots ,v_\ell)\in\mathbb Z^\ell,
\quad b_Q=\sum_jv_ja_j
\right\}.
\]

For any character \(\chi:A_Q\to\mathbb T\), telescoping gives

\[
|\chi(b_Q)-1|
\leq
\sum_j|v_j|\,|\chi(a_j)-1|
\leq
\|v\|_2
\left(\sum_j|\chi(a_j)-1|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Consequently the optimal marked/relator phase ratio for the quotient \(Q\)
is at most \(\delta_Q(w)\).  In particular:

### Corollary 7 (unbounded distortion is necessary and sufficient)

The canonical central-quotient route proves FALSE if and only if

\[
\sup_{P\twoheadrightarrow Q\text{ finite}}\delta_Q(w)=\infty.
\]

The necessary direction follows immediately from the displayed character
estimate. The sufficient direction is the fixed-rank character compactness
theorem: for fixed \(\ell\), a uniform bound on all character ratios forces a
uniform bound on the shortest integral coefficient vectors. Thus a bounded
integral expression of the radical collision loop in terms of the fixed
presentation relators rules out this entire route, while unbounded integral
distortion completes it.

There is also an exact quotient-level nonlinear invariant.  Set

\[
\Lambda_Q(w):=sup_{\chi\in\widehat{A_Q}}
\frac{|\chi(b_Q)-1|}
{\left(\sum_j|\chi(a_j)-1|^2\right)^{1/2}},
\]

where points with zero denominator are omitted.  Theorem 6 ensures that a
zero denominator also forces zero numerator.  Finite-order characters are
dense in \(\widehat{A_Q}\), and every finite-order character factors through
\(A_Q/mA_Q\) for some \(m\).  It follows that

\[
\sup_{Q,m,\chi}
\frac{W_{Q,m}(\chi)^{1/2}}{R_{Q,m}(\chi)^{1/2}}
=
\sup_Q\Lambda_Q(w).
\]

Hence the canonical central route is completely characterized by

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{P\twoheadrightarrow Q\text{ finite}}\Lambda_Q(w)=\infty.
}
\]

The elementary estimate above says \(\Lambda_Q(w)\leq\delta_Q(w)\). The
fixed-rank character compactness theorem proves the exact family-level
equivalence

\[
\sup_Q\Lambda_Q(w)=\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sup_Q\delta_Q(w)=\infty.
\]

For a single quotient, the numerical gap between these quantities remains a
finite nonlinear discriminant/Voronoi problem for the homomorphism
\(\mathbb Z^\ell\to A_Q\). That fixed-instance geometry is not needed to
decide divergence over a family.

Thus the remaining FALSE calculation is integral finite-quotient homology:
prove that the shortest coefficient vector expressing the marked class
\([w]\) in terms of the finitely many relator classes \([r_j]\) is unbounded
along some finite quotient family. The compactness theorem then supplies the
required character ratios automatically.

This also clarifies what the existing \(p^k\)-cycle amplifier does and does
not prove. Large order of the marked class is not by itself enough. For the
explicit symmetric double, the finite-residual commutator theorem now proves
that the needed dual separation is uniformly bounded. The invariant is

\[
\frac{W_{Q,m}(\chi)}{R_{Q,m}(\chi)}.
\]

Thus identifying the local Baumslag--Solitar transgression generator inside
these canonical modules cannot finish the proof. A successful continuation
must use a genuinely noncentral kernel-carry model or a different radical
candidate whose finite residual has nonzero coinvariants.
