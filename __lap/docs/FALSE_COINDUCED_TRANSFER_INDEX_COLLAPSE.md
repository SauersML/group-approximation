# Exact transfer-index loss in a coinduced Frobenius bundle

Date: 2026-08-12

## 1. Outcome

There is a canonical way to place a global finite module into the
coinduced bundle of its restriction to a stabilizer. It is split by the
usual transfer, but the composite is not the identity: it is multiplication
by the orbit index. The same index multiplies the Frobenius pairing.

Consequently, if a local Weyl phase has order `p^n`, exact transitive
coinduction over `K/H` reduces its order to

`p^(max(n-v_p([K:H]),0)).`                              `(CTI1)`

This gives a sharp arithmetic explanation for why the natural exact
congruence-bundle splice loses the clock. For

`K_e=SL_d(Z/p^e Z)`

and the rank-two subgroup `H_e` generated by the two commuting elementary
transvections used in the external `<t,s>~=Z^2` chart,

`v_p([K_e:H_e])`
` =(d^2-3)e-(d-1)(d+2)/2.`                             `(CTI2)`

If the second compressor has the compulsory projective order
`p^(n-1)` from the long-chain monodromy theorem, then `e>=n-1`. For every
`d>=3`, `(CTI2)` eventually exceeds `n`; hence the order in `(CTI1)` is
one and the primitive clock phase is completely erased.

The conclusion is a no-go only for an exact transitive coinduced bundle
with its invariant block pairing. It does not apply to the root-dependent
amenable-stabilizer orbit charts in
`FALSE_AMENABLE_STABILIZER_ORBIT_CHART_INDUCTION.md`. Those charts are
therefore not merely convenient: they are the precise way to avoid the
transfer-index collapse.

## 2. Canonical coinduction and transfer

Let `K` be a finite group, `H<=K`, and let `R` be a commutative ring. Let
`W` be a left `R[K]`-module. Use the equivariant-function model

`C=Coind_H^K Res_H^K W`
` ={f:K->W : f(kh)=h^(-1)f(k)}`.                      `(CTI3)`

The left `K`-action is

`(g f)(k)=f(g^(-1)k).`                                 `(CTI4)`

There are canonical `R[K]`-module maps

`J:W->C`,       `J(v)(k)=k^(-1)v`,                    `(CTI5)`

and

`T:C->W`,       `T(f)=sum_(kH in K/H) k f(k).`         `(CTI6)`

The summand in `(CTI6)` is independent of the representative: replacing
`k` by `kh` replaces `f(k)` by `h^(-1)f(k)`. Equivariance of both maps is
immediate from `(CTI4)`.

**Theorem 1 (transfer-index identity).** If `m=[K:H]`, then

`T J=m id_W.`                                           `(CTI7)`

In particular, if `m` is a unit in `R`, then `J(W)` is a canonically
split `R[K]`-submodule of `C`, with retraction `m^(-1)T`.

**Proof.** For every coset representative `k`,

`k J(v)(k)=k(k^(-1)v)=v`.

There are `m` cosets, so summing gives `(CTI7)`. End proof.

This statement requires the local fiber in `(CTI3)` to be the restriction
of an already existing global `K`-module. It does not manufacture the
missing global module from an arbitrary `H`-module. That distinction is
essential in the current FALSE problem.

## 3. Exact scaling of the Frobenius pairing

Suppose that `W` has a `K`-invariant bilinear pairing

`b:W times W->D`,                                      `(CTI8)`

where `D` is an `R`-module. Define the block pairing on `C` by

`B(f,g)=sum_(kH in K/H)b(f(k),g(k)).`                  `(CTI9)`

It is independent of representatives and `K`-invariant.

**Theorem 2 (pairing-index identity).** For all `v,w in W`,

`B(Jv,Jw)=m b(v,w),`                                   `(CTI10)`

and, more generally,

`B(Jv,f)=b(v,Tf).`                                     `(CTI11)`

**Proof.** Invariance gives

`b(k^(-1)v,k^(-1)w)=b(v,w)`

and

`b(k^(-1)v,f(k))=b(v,kf(k)).`

Sum these identities over `K/H`. End proof.

There is no repair by assigning different scalar weights to the cosets.
Indeed, a weighted block form

`B_a(f,g)=sum_x a_x b(f(x),g(x))`                     `(CTI12)`

is `K`-invariant only if `a_(kx)=a_x` for all `k,x`. Transitivity of
`K` on `K/H` forces all `a_x` to be equal. Thus every invariant scalar
block weighting retains the factor `m` in `(CTI10)`, up to one common
scalar.

## 4. The exact `p`-primary loss

Take

`D=(1/p^n Z)/Z`

and suppose `b(v,w)` has exact additive order `p^n`. Write

`a=min(v_p(m),n)`.

Equation `(CTI10)` says that `B(Jv,Jw)` has exact order

`p^(n-a).`                                              `(CTI13)`

Equivalently, over `R=Z/p^n Z`, multiplication by `m` on a perfect
pairing has kernel `p^(n-a)W` when `W` is free. Therefore an exact
coinduced Weyl commutator formed from the diagonal global copy has only
`n-a` levels of `p`-primary clock depth left.

This is a quantitative criterion, not merely a unit/nonunit dichotomy:

* `v_p(m)=0` preserves the full clock;
* `0<v_p(m)<n` shortens it by exactly `v_p(m)` levels;
* `v_p(m)>=n` makes the diagonal commutator phase trivial.

## 5. Natural congruence quotients

Assume `d>=3` and let

`K_e=SL_d(Z/p^e Z).`                                   `(CTI14)`

For the two independent commuting elementary transvections in the current
external chart, their images generate

`H_e~=(Z/p^e Z)^2`,                                    `(CTI15)`

so `|H_e|=p^(2e)`. The standard order formula gives

`v_p(|K_e|)=(d^2-1)(e-1)+d(d-1)/2.`                   `(CTI16)`

Subtracting `2e` proves `(CTI2)`:

`v_p([K_e:H_e])`
` =(d^2-1)(e-1)+d(d-1)/2-2e`
` =(d^2-3)e-(d-1)(d+2)/2.`                            `(CTI17)`

The long-chain monodromy calculation proves that the gauged compressor has
projective order divisible by `p^(n-1)`. If an exact congruence orbit is
to carry that compressor, its transvection order must be divisible by
`p^(n-1)`, forcing

`e>=n-1.`                                              `(CTI18)`

Combining `(CTI17)--(CTI18)`,

`v_p([K_e:H_e])`
` >=(d^2-3)(n-1)-(d-1)(d+2)/2.`                       `(CTI19)`

For `d=3` the right side is `6n-11`, already at least `n` for `n>=3`.
For larger `d` the slope is even greater. Hence the paired phase of depth
`p^n` becomes trivial in the canonical diagonal sector of every sufficiently
deep exact congruence coinduction.

## 6. Consequence for the live construction

The calculation separates three notions which had been conflated:

1. **Exact global transversal/coinduction.** It repeats a local Frobenius
   phase over one transitive orbit and necessarily incurs the index factor
   `(CTI10)`. Natural deep congruence orbits kill the clock by `(CTI19)`.
2. **An arbitrary local stabilizer fiber.** The map `(CTI5)` is not
   available unless that fiber already extends to a global `K`-module.
   Thus transfer does not solve the chart-functorial extension problem.
3. **Root-dependent sofic orbit charts.** These have no single global
   orbit index entering their normalized block pairing. The no-dilution
   estimate `(ASI12)--(ASI16)` applies instead, so a local fixed gap can
   survive on density `1-o(1)` of the roots.

Accordingly, another exact congruence principal bundle cannot finish the
FALSE construction. The remaining viable theorem is genuinely local:
construct the paired Frobenius/Steinberg fiber maps functorially on the
amenable-stabilizer charts, allowing the exceptional set to depend on the
tested window. The transfer-index formula explains arithmetically why
that local formulation is necessary.
