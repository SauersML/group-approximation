\title{A sofic infinite simple group with Kazhdan's property (T)}
\author{\paperauthor}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 20E32; Secondary 20F65, 22D10, 37B10, 16S35, 20H25}
\keywords{sofic groups, hyperlinear groups, property (T), simple groups, LEF groups, minimal subshifts, elementary groups over rings}

\begin{abstract}
\addtolength{\leftskip}{1.5pc}\addtolength{\rightskip}{1.5pc}
Let $X$ be an infinite minimal subshift over a finite alphabet, let $q$
be a prime power, and let $R=\LC(X,\F_q)\rtimes\Z$ be the algebraic
crossed product of the locally constant functions on $X$ by the shift.
The elementary group $\EL_3(R)$ modulo its finite center is an infinite
finitely generated simple group with Kazhdan's property~\textup{(T)}
that is locally embeddable into finite groups, so it is sofic and
hyperlinear.  Brown asked in 2001 and Ozawa in 2003 whether an infinite
simple group with property~\textup{(T)} can be hyperlinear, and Pestov
printed the question, with the sofic form added, as Open question~9.1 of
his guide to hyperlinear and sofic groups.  Sturmian subshifts give
continuum many pairwise non-isomorphic such groups, one for every Turing
degree of the word problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A group $G$ is \emph{sofic}~\cite{Gromov99,Weiss} if for every finite
$F\subseteq G$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there are a finite set $Y$ and a map
$\sigma\colon F\to\operatorname{Sym}(Y)$ such that
$\sigma(g)\sigma(h)$ and $\sigma(gh)$ differ on at most $\varepsilon|Y|$
points whenever $g,h,gh\in F$, and $\sigma(g)$ moves at least
$(1-\varepsilon)|Y|$ points for $g\ne1$ in $F$.  It is
\emph{hyperlinear} if the same holds with unitary groups $\U(n)$ and the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance in place of symmetric groups and the
normalized Hamming distance.  Sofic groups are
hyperlinear~\cite{ElekSzabo}.  A group is \emph{locally embeddable into
finite groups}, or \emph{LEF}~\cite{VershikGordon}, if for every finite
$F\subseteq G$ there are a finite group $Q$ and an injective map
$\phi\colon F\to Q$ with $\phi(gh)=\phi(g)\phi(h)$ whenever
$g,h,gh\in F$.  LEF groups are sofic, by the regular representations of
the finite groups $Q$.  Pestov's guide~\cite{Pestov} surveys these
notions.

In 2001 Brown asked whether an infinite simple discrete group with
Kazhdan's property~(T) can embed into the unitary group of an
$R^\omega$-embeddable McDuff factor~\cite[Section~11, Question~7]{Brown01},
which for a countable group amounts to hyperlinearity.  Ozawa asked it
in that form: ``It is unknown whether there exists a simple
property~(T) group $\Gamma$ which is hyperlinear''~\cite[p.~527]{Ozawa}.
Pestov printed the question, citing Ozawa and adding the sofic form, as
Open question~9.1 of~\cite{Pestov}.  The interest comes from Kirchberg's theorem that a group with
property~(T) and the factorization property is residually
finite~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Kirchberg}.  An infinite simple group is not
residually finite, so an infinite simple Kazhdan group has no
factorization property, and if it is hyperlinear, then its full group
$C^*$-algebra fails the local lifting property~\cite[p.~527]{Ozawa}.
Infinite simple Kazhdan groups arise as quotients of hyperbolic
groups~\cite{Gromov} and as the Kac--Moody lattices of Caprace and
R\'emy~\cite{CapraceRemy}; none of them is known to be hyperlinear.  The
Kac--Moody lattices are finitely presented, and a finitely presented LEF
group is residually finite, so no infinite finitely presented simple
group is LEF\@.  Thom constructed a finitely generated LEF group with
property~(T) that is not residually finite~\cite[Theorem~1.4]{Thom}; it
is not simple.

The answer to the question is yes, in both forms.

\begin{mainthm}\label{thm:main}
Let $A$ be a finite alphabet, let $X\subseteq A^{\Z}$ be an infinite
minimal subshift, let $q$ be a prime power, and let
$R=\LC(X,\F_q)\rtimes\Z$ be the algebraic crossed product of the ring
of locally constant functions $X\to\F_q$ by the shift.  Put
$G=\EL_3(R)$ and $S=G/Z(G)$.  Then $Z(G)$ is finite, and $S$ is a
finitely generated infinite simple group with Kazhdan's
property~\textup{(T)} that is locally embeddable into finite groups.
In particular $S$ is sofic and hyperlinear.
\end{mainthm}

The ring $R$ is finitely generated, so $G$ has property~(T) by the
theorem of Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain on elementary groups over finitely
generated rings~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.  Local embeddability comes from
periodic approximation of the subshift, the mechanism behind the theorem
of Grigorchuk and Medynets that topological full groups of Cantor
minimal systems are LEF~\cite[Theorem~2.6]{GrigorchukMedynets}, applied
here to the ring: a long return word of $X$ gives a unital ring
homomorphism from a large finite subset of $R$ into a matrix algebra
$M_N(\F_q)$, and then $G$ embeds into an ultraproduct of the finite
groups $\GL_{3N}(\F_q)$.  Simplicity rests on the simplicity of $R$ and
on one commutator: a noncentral element of a normal subgroup of $G$ is
pushed into a matrix ring supported on a tower of small cylinders, where
it yields a nonzero elementary matrix in the normal subgroup, and
simplicity of $R$ spreads that matrix to all of $G$.  The extraction of
an elementary matrix follows Stepanov's description of the normal
subgroups of linear groups over rings~\cite{Stepanov}; the tower supplies
the noncentral element his method needs.

The construction is concrete.  For the Fibonacci subshift over a prime
field $\F_p$, the group $G$ is generated by the $24$ matrices
$e_{ij}(1)$, $e_{ij}(u^{\pm1})$ and $e_{ij}(\chi_{\{x_0=a\}})$, and its
first finite model is $\SL_{15}(\F_p)$, from the return word $abaab$
(Remark~\ref{rem:fibonacci}).  The argument for the binary Toeplitz
subshift over $\F_2$ has been formalized in Lean~4 in the repository
accompanying this paper.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SauersML/group-approximation}}

The construction depends on the subshift, and the dependence is visible
in the word problem.

\begin{mainthm}\label{thm:degrees}
Let $S_X$ be the group of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:main}}.  The word
problem of $S_X$ has the same Turing degree as the language of $X$.  For the Sturmian subshift
$X_\alpha$ of an irrational slope $\alpha$, that degree is the degree of
$\alpha$.  So for every prime power $q$ and every Turing degree $d$
there is an infinite finitely generated simple group with
property~\textup{(T)} that is LEF, hence sofic and hyperlinear, and
whose word problem has degree $d$.  In particular there are continuum
many pairwise non-isomorphic such groups.
\end{mainthm}

The groups also have the following properties (Section~\ref{sec:consequences}).
They are not finitely presented and have no nontrivial finite quotient;
they have no factorization property, and their full $C^*$-algebras fail
the local lifting property; they are MF groups that are not weakly
quasidiagonal, contrary to a sentence in the introduction
of~\cite{Dadarlat}; and the finite simple groups $\PSL_{3N}(\F_q)$,
marked by the images of the generators of $G$, converge to $S$ in the
space of marked groups, while forming a family of expanders.

The ring $R$ appears in~\cite{NonMF}, where the crossed product of a
zero-dimensional system by $\Z$ is shown to be LEF exactly when every
point is chain recurrent, and where elementary groups over certain rings with a proper
one-sided inverse, such as Leavitt algebras, are shown to have no
nontrivial homomorphism to an MF group.  A minimal subshift is chain recurrent, so the LEF property
of $R$ is a case of that theorem; the proof below is included because
its finite models are needed for the marked convergence and for
Theorem~\ref{thm:degrees}.

\subsection*{Notation}
$A$ is a finite alphabet and $T$ is the shift on $A^{\Z}$,
$(Tx)_n=x_{n+1}$.  A subshift is a closed $T$-invariant subset
$X\subseteq A^{\Z}$; it is minimal if every orbit is dense.  $L_m(X)$ is
the set of words of length $m$ occurring in points of $X$, and
$L(X)=\bigcup_m L_m(X)$ is the language of $X$.  For a word $v$ of odd
length $2k+1$ the cylinder of radius $k$ is
$C(v)=\{x\in X: x_{[-k,k]}=v\}$.  Every clopen subset of $X$ is a
finite union of cylinders of any sufficiently large radius.

$\LC(X,\F_q)$ is the commutative ring of locally constant functions
$X\to\F_q$, and $\alpha(f)=f\circ T^{-1}$.  The crossed product
$R=\LC(X,\F_q)\rtimes\Z$ consists of the finite sums $\sum_j f_ju^j$,
with $(fu^i)(gu^j)=f\,\alpha^i(g)\,u^{i+j}$; the normal form is unique.
For a clopen $W$ we write $\chi_W$ for its indicator, and then
$u\chi_Wu^{-1}=\chi_{TW}$.  An element $\sum_jf_ju^j$ has
\emph{propagation} at most $w$ if $f_j=0$ for $|j|>w$.  A function
$f\in\LC(X,\F_q)$ has \emph{radius} at most $\rho$ if
$f(x)=\Phi(x_{[-\rho,\rho]})$ for some $\Phi\colon A^{2\rho+1}\to\F_q$.

For $i\ne j$ and $r\in R$, $e_{ij}(r)=I+rE_{ij}\in\GL_3(R)$, and
$G=\EL_3(R)$ is the group they generate.  With $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:steinberg}
e_{ij}(r)e_{ij}(s)=e_{ij}(r+s),\qquad
[e_{ik}(r),e_{kj}(s)]=e_{ij}(rs)\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).
\end{equation}
So if $R$ is generated as a ring by a finite set $\Sigma$, then $G$ is
generated by the matrices $e_{ij}(s)$ with $s\in\Sigma\cup\{1\}$.

\section{The ring}\label{sec:ring}

Throughout, $X$ is an infinite minimal subshift.  It has no periodic
point, since a finite orbit would be a proper closed invariant subset.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:subshift}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(U)}] For every $m$ there is $\ell(m)$ such that every
word of $L_{\ell(m)}(X)$ contains every word of $L_m(X)$.
\item[\textup{(P)}] For every $L$ there is $k_L$ such that every
cylinder $C$ of radius $k\ge k_L$ satisfies $T^jC\cap C=\emptyset$ for
$0<|j|\le L$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(U) is uniform recurrence.  For $v\in L_m(X)$ the open sets
$T^{-n}\{x: x_{[0,m)}=v\}$, $n\ge0$, cover $X$ by minimality, and
finitely many, with $n\le n_v$, suffice by compactness.  So every $x\in X$
has an occurrence of $v$ starting at some $n\le n_v$, and
$\ell(m)=m+\max_vn_v$ works.

(P) If $T^jC(v)\cap C(v)\ne\emptyset$, then $v$ occurs twice in some
point at distance $|j|$, so $v$ has period $|j|$.  If (P) failed for
some $L$, one $j$ with $0<|j|\le L$ would be a period of words
$v_k\in L_{2k+1}(X)$ for infinitely many $k$.  A limit point of points
$x_k\in C(v_k)$ is then $j$-periodic, which is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:generation}
$R$ is generated as a ring by $u$, $u^{-1}$, the letter indicators
$\chi_a=\chi_{\{x_0=a\}}$ for $a\in A$, and one element $\lambda$ with
$\F_p[\lambda]=\F_q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$u^{-t}\chi_au^{t}=\chi_a\circ T^{t}=\chi_{\{x_t=a\}}$.  Products of
these are the indicators of all cylinders, their $\Z$-linear
combinations are $\LC(X,\F_p)$, and $\lambda$ extends the scalars to
$\F_q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:simple-ring}
$R$ is a simple ring, and its center is $\F_q$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $J\ne0$ be a two-sided ideal and choose
$0\ne P=\sum_{j\in F}f_ju^j\in J$ with $F$ finite.  After multiplying
by a power of $u$ we may assume $f_0(x)=c\ne0$ for some $x\in X$.  Since
$T^jx\ne x$ for $0\ne j\in F$, there is a clopen $W\ni x$ with
$f_0|_W=c$ and $T^jW\cap W=\emptyset$ for $0\ne j\in F$.  Then
\[
\chi_WP\chi_W=\sum_jf_j\,\chi_W\chi_{T^jW}\,u^j=c\,\chi_W\in J.
\]
The set $\bigcup_nT^nW$ is open, invariant and nonempty, so it is $X$,
and by compactness $X=T^{i_1}W\cup\dots\cup T^{i_s}W$.  Each
$\chi_{T^iW}=u^i\chi_Wu^{-i}$ lies in $J$, and
$1=1-\prod_t(1-\chi_{T^{i_t}W})$ expands into terms each containing a
factor in $J$.  So $J=R$.

Let $c=\sum_jf_ju^j$ be central.  For every clopen $W$,
$c\chi_W=\chi_Wc$ gives $f_j(\chi_{T^jW}-\chi_W)=0$ for all $j$.  For
$j\ne0$ and $x\in X$, choose $W\ni x$ with $T^{-j}x\notin W$; then the
bracket equals $-1$ at $x$, so $f_j(x)=0$.  From $cu=uc$ we get
$f_0\circ T=f_0$, and a continuous invariant function on a minimal
system is constant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Towers}\label{subsec:towers}
Let $U$ be clopen with $T^jU\cap U=\emptyset$ for $0<|j|\le2m$.  The
levels $T^aU$, $|a|\le m$, are pairwise disjoint.  For clopen
$W\subseteq U$ and $|a|,|b|\le m$ put
\[
E_{ab}(W)=\chi_{T^aW}\,u^{a-b}=u^{a-b}\,\chi_{T^bW}.
\]
Then $E_{ab}(W)E_{cd}(W')=\chi_{T^aW\cap T^{a-b+c}W'}\,u^{a-b+c-d}$,
and for $b\ne c$ the levels $T^aU$ and $T^{a-b+c}U$ are disjoint, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-units}
E_{ab}(W)\,E_{cd}(W')=\delta_{bc}\,E_{ad}(W\cap W').
\end{equation}
The linear span $B_m(U)$ of these elements is a subring of $R$ with unit
$\chi_V$, where $V=\bigsqcup_{|a|\le m}T^aU$, and
\[
(F_{ab})_{a,b}\longmapsto\sum_{a,b}(F_{ab}\circ T^{-a})\,\chi_{T^aU}\,u^{a-b}
\]
is a ring isomorphism $M_{2m+1}(\LC(U,\F_q))\to B_m(U)$; it is injective
because distinct levels have disjoint supports.  For clopen
$W\subseteq U$ and $m'\le m$ put $V_{m'}(W)=\bigsqcup_{|a|\le m'}T^aW$.
By~\eqref{eq:matrix-units}, $\chi_{V_{m'}(W)}$ commutes with
$B_{m'}(U)$ and with $\LC(X,\F_q)$, and under the isomorphism it is the
central idempotent $\chi_W\cdot I$.

\begin{lemma}[absorption]\label{lem:absorption}
Suppose $T^jU\cap U=\emptyset$ for $0<|j|\le2(m+w)$.  If $r,r'\in R$
have propagation at most $w$ and $b\in B_m(U)$, then
$rbr'\in B_{m+w}(U)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By linearity take $r=fu^i$, $b=E_{ab}(W)$ and $r'=f'u^j$ with
$|i|,|j|\le w$.  Then
$rbr'=f\,\chi_{T^{a+i}W}\,\alpha^{i+a-b}(f')\,u^{i+a-b+j}$.  Split $W$
into clopen pieces $W_k$ such that $f\,\alpha^{i+a-b}(f')$ is constant,
equal to $c_k$, on $T^{a+i}W_k$.  Then
$rbr'=\sum_kc_k\,E_{a+i,\,b-j}(W_k)$ with $|a+i|,|b-j|\le m+w$.
\end{proof}

\section{Local embeddability}\label{sec:lef}

\subsection{Periodic models}
Fix $k\ge1$.  By (U) choose a point $x\in X$, a word $v\in L_{2k}(X)$
and two occurrences of $v$ in $x$ at positions $p<p'$ with
$N=p'-p\ge\max(2k+1,\ell(2k+1))$.  Put $w=x_{[p,p')}$, and let $y$ be
the $N$-periodic sequence with $y_{[0,N)}=w$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:windows}
$L_{2k+1}(y)=L_{2k+1}(X)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A window of $y$ of length at most $2k+1\le N$ contains at most one seam,
that is, at most one pair of consecutive positions $(nN-1,nN)$.  A
window without a seam is a subword of $w$, hence of $x$.  A window with
a seam reads $\operatorname{suffix}_s(w)\operatorname{prefix}_t(w)$
with $s,t\ge1$ and $s+t\le2k+1$, so $t\le2k$.  Since $v$ is a prefix of
$w$ and $x_{[p',p'+2k)}=v$, the prefix is $x_{[p',p'+t)}$, while the
suffix is $x_{[p'-s,p')}$; so the window is
$x_{[p'-s,p'+t)}\in L(X)$.  Conversely $|w|\ge\ell(2k+1)$, so every word
of $L_{2k+1}(X)$ occurs in $w$.
\end{proof}

Let $P$ be the cyclic shift $\delta_n\mapsto\delta_{n+1}$ on
$\F_q^{\Z/N}$.  For $f\in\LC(X,\F_q)$ of radius $\rho\le k$, represented
by $\Phi$, put
\[
D_k(f)=\diag\bigl(\Phi(y_{[n-\rho,n+\rho]})\bigr)_{n\in\Z/N}.
\]
This is well defined: if $\Phi$ and $\Phi'$ of radii $\rho,\rho'\le k$
both represent $f$, then with $K=\max(\rho,\rho')$ the window
$y_{[n-K,n+K]}$ lies in $L(X)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:windows}, so it equals
$x_{[-K,K]}$ for some $x\in X$, and both formulas give $f(x)$.  Since
$D_k$ is evaluation at points, it is a ring homomorphism on functions of
radius at most $k$.  Put
\[
\psi_k\Bigl(\sum_jf_ju^j\Bigr)=\sum_jD_k(f_j)P^j
\]
whenever each $f_j$ has radius at most $k$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:model}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $g$ has radius $\rho$ and $\rho+|i|\le k$, then
$P^iD_k(g)P^{-i}=D_k(\alpha^i(g))$.
\item $\psi_k$ is additive and unital.  If every coefficient radius
$\rho$ and every exponent $i$ occurring in $r$ and in $r'$ satisfy
$\rho+|i|\le k$, then $\psi_k(rr')=\psi_k(r)\psi_k(r')$.
\item If $r\ne0$, then $\psi_k(r)\ne0$ for all sufficiently large $k$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) $P^iD_k(g)P^{-i}\delta_n=\hat g(n-i)\delta_n$ with
$\hat g(n)=\Phi_g(y_{[n-\rho,n+\rho]})$.  Also
$\alpha^i(g)(x)=g(T^{-i}x)=\Phi_g(x_{[-\rho-i,\rho-i]})$ has radius at
most $\rho+|i|$, and at position $n$ it reads $\hat g(n-i)$.

(2) By (1),
$\psi_k((fu^i)(gu^j))=D_k(f)D_k(\alpha^ig)P^{i+j}=D_k(f)P^iD_k(g)P^j$;
extend bilinearly.

(3) Let $f_j\ne0$, nonzero on some word of $L_{2\rho+1}(X)$.  For
$k\ge\rho$ that word occurs in $y$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:windows}, so
$D_k(f_j)\ne0$.  The term $D_k(f_j)P^j$ is supported on the entries
$(n+j,n)$.  Once $N>2\max|j|$, distinct exponents occupy disjoint
entries, so there is no cancellation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An embedding into an ultraproduct of matrix algebras}
Choose the data above for every $k$, with sizes $N_k$, and fix a
nonprincipal ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\N$.  For $r\in R$ put
$\Psi(r)=[\psi_k(r)]_\omega\in\prod_\omega M_{N_k}(\F_q)$, with
arbitrary values at the finitely many $k$ where $\psi_k(r)$ is not
defined.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ultraproduct}
$\Psi$ is an injective unital ring homomorphism with
$\Psi(\lambda)=\lambda I$ for $\lambda\in\F_q$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each identity $\Psi(r+r')=\Psi(r)+\Psi(r')$ and
$\Psi(rr')=\Psi(r)\Psi(r')$ holds for all large $k$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:model}(2), hence $\omega$-almost everywhere.  Injectivity
is Lemma~\ref{lem:model}(3), and constants have radius $0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{$G$ and $S$ are LEF}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ultraproduct-lef}
Every subgroup of an algebraic ultraproduct $\prod_\omega Q_k$ of finite
groups is LEF\@.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose representatives for the elements of a finite subset $F$.  The
finitely many identities $gh=g'$ and inequalities $g\ne g'$ among
elements of $F$ each hold on an $\omega$-large set of indices.  The
intersection of these sets is nonempty, and any $k$ in it gives
$\phi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lef}
$G$ and $S=G/Z(G)$ are LEF, hence sofic and hyperlinear.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Psi$ induces an injective ring homomorphism
$M_3(R)\to\prod_\omega M_{3N_k}(\F_q)$.  The units of the target form
$\prod_\omega\GL_{3N_k}(\F_q)$, so $G\le\GL_3(R)$ embeds in an
ultraproduct of finite groups and is LEF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ultraproduct-lef}.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:center} below, $Z(G)=\Lambda I_3$ with
$\Lambda=\{\lambda\in\F_q^\times:\lambda I_3\in G\}$.  Let $C=\Lambda I$,
a finite central subgroup of each $Q_k=\GL_{3N_k}(\F_q)$.  The map
$\prod_\omega Q_k\to\prod_\omega(Q_k/C)$ is onto, and its kernel
consists of the classes $[a_k]$ with $a_k\in C$ for $\omega$-almost
every $k$; since $C$ is finite, such a class is $\omega$-almost
everywhere constant, equal to some $[\lambda I]=\Psi(\lambda I_3)$.  If
$\Psi(g)=\Psi(\lambda I_3)$ for $g\in G$, then $g=\lambda I_3\in Z(G)$
by injectivity.  So $S$ embeds in $\prod_\omega(Q_k/C)$ and is LEF\@.

For a finite $F\subseteq S$, apply the LEF property to $F\cup F\cdot F$
and compose with the left regular action of $Q$ on itself.  This gives
permutations $\sigma(g)$ of the finite set $Q$ that are exactly
multiplicative on $F$, and $\sigma(g)$ has no fixed point for $g\ne1$.
So $S$ is sofic.  The permutation matrices are unitaries with
$\|\sigma(g)-I\|_2=\sqrt2$ in the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, so
$S$ is hyperlinear.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite simple approximants}\label{subsec:approximants}
Replace $w$ by its primitive root $z$, so that $w=z^e$ with $z$ not a
proper power.  This leaves $y$ unchanged, so
Lemmas~\ref{lem:windows} and~\ref{lem:model} hold with $N_k=|z|$.  An
aperiodic sequence has at least $m+1$ words of length $m$ (Morse and
Hedlund~\cite{MorseHedlund}), and a cyclic word of length $N$ has at
most $N$ cyclic windows, so $N_k\ge2k+2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:windows}.

The images of the generators $u^{\pm1}$, $\chi_a$ and $\lambda$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:generation} are $P^{\pm1}$, the diagonal matrices $D_a$
of the positions of $a$ in $z$, and $\lambda I$.  Since $P^iD_aP^{-i}$
is the indicator of the positions $n$ with $y_{n-i}=a$, and the
rotations of a primitive word are distinct, these indicators and their
complements separate positions.  So every diagonal matrix unit, and
with $P$ every matrix unit, lies in the ring generated by the images:
they generate $M_{N_k}(\F_q)$.  The generators $e_{ij}(s)$ of $G$,
$s\in\{1,u^{\pm1},\chi_a,\lambda\}$, are sent to generators of
$\EL_3(M_{N_k}(\F_q))=\SL_{3N_k}(\F_q)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:marked}
With these markings, $\SL_{3N_k}(\F_q)\to G$ and
$\PSL_{3N_k}(\F_q)\to S$ in the space of marked groups.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A word $\mathsf w$ of length $n$ in the generators has a value
$g_{\mathsf w}\in G$ whose coefficient radii and exponents are $O(n)$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:model}(2) its value in $\SL_{3N_k}(\F_q)$ is
$\psi_k(g_{\mathsf w})$ for $k\gg n$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:model}(3),
$\mathsf w=1$ in $G$ if and only if $\mathsf w=1$ in $\SL_{3N_k}(\F_q)$ for
all large $k$.  If $g_{\mathsf w}=\lambda I_3$, then
$\psi_k(g_{\mathsf w})=\lambda I$ is scalar.  Conversely, if
$\psi_k(g_{\mathsf w})$ is scalar for infinitely many $k$, then some
$\mu\in\F_q^\times$ occurs infinitely often, so
$\psi_k(g_{\mathsf w}-\mu I_3)=0$ infinitely often, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:model}(3) gives $g_{\mathsf w}=\mu I_3\in Z(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the Fibonacci subshift]\label{rem:fibonacci}
Let $\sigma$ be the substitution $a\mapsto ab$, $b\mapsto a$, with fixed
point $\varphi=abaababaabaab\cdots$, and let $X$ be the set of
$x\in\{a,b\}^{\Z}$ all of whose finite subwords occur in $\varphi$.  It
is minimal because $\sigma$ is primitive, and aperiodic because it is
Sturmian: $|L_m(X)|=m+1$.  With $\pi=\chi_a$, so that $1-\pi=\chi_b$, the
ring $R$ over a prime field $\F_p$ is generated by $u$, $u^{-1}$ and
$\pi$, and $G$ by the $24$ matrices $e_{ij}(1)$, $e_{ij}(u^{\pm1})$,
$e_{ij}(\pi)$.  The relation $(1-\pi)\,u(1-\pi)u^{-1}=0$ says that $bb$ never
occurs.  The return word $z=abaab$ has $N=5$, and its cyclic windows of
length $3$ are $aba$, $baa$, $aab$, $aba$, $bab$, exactly
$L_3(X)=\{aab,aba,baa,bab\}$.  Sending $u\mapsto P$ and
$\pi\mapsto D=\diag(1,0,1,1,0)$ gives the first finite model: the $24$
generators go to $I_{15}+E_{ij}\otimes P^{\pm1}$,
$I_{15}+E_{ij}\otimes D$ and $I_{15}+E_{ij}\otimes I_5$, which generate
$\SL_{15}(\F_p)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Property (T)}\label{sec:kazhdan}

Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain proved that for every finitely generated
associative ring $R$ with $1$ and every $n\ge3$, the group $\EL_n(R)$
has Kazhdan's property~(T)~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.  Before their
theorem, property~(T) for $\EL_n(R)$ was known only for commutative
rings or under stable range conditions.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:generation}
our $R$ is finitely generated, so $G=\EL_3(R)$ has property~(T), and so
does its quotient $S$.

\section{Normal subgroups}\label{sec:normal}

\subsection{The level ideal}
For a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ put
\[
I(N)=\{a\in R: e_{12}(a)\in N\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:level}
$I(N)$ is a two-sided ideal of $R$.  If $I(N)\ne0$, then $N=G$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$I(N)$ is an additive subgroup by~\eqref{eq:steinberg}.  The Weyl
elements
\[
w_{ij}=e_{ij}(1)\,e_{ji}(-1)\,e_{ij}(1)\in G
\]
are monomial matrices, and conjugation by them takes each $e_{kl}(a)$
to some $e_{k'l'}(\pm a)$, reaching every pair $k'\ne l'$.  So
$e_{kl}(a)\in N$ for one pair $k\ne l$ if and only if for all pairs.  Commutators of elements
of $N$ with elements of $G$ lie in $N$, and
\[
[e_{12}(a),e_{23}(b)]=e_{13}(ab),\qquad [e_{31}(b),e_{12}(a)]=e_{32}(ba),
\]
so $ab,ba\in I(N)$ for $a\in I(N)$ and $b\in R$.  If $I(N)\ne0$, then $I(N)=R$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:simple-ring}, every $e_{ij}(r)$ lies in $N$, and
$N=G$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The center}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:center}
$Z(G)=\Lambda I_3$ with $\Lambda=\{\lambda\in\F_q^\times:\lambda I_3\in G\}$,
a finite group, and $S=G/Z(G)$ is infinite.  Every homomorphism from
$G$, or from $S$, to a finite group is trivial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $g$ commutes with every $e_{ij}(1)$, then $gE_{ij}=E_{ij}g$ for all
$i\ne j$, so $g=cI_3$ with $c\in R$.  Commuting with every $e_{12}(r)$
forces $c\in Z(R)^\times=\F_q^\times$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:simple-ring}.  Scalars are central, so
$Z(G)=\Lambda I_3$.  The subgroup $e_{12}(\LC(X,\F_q))$ is infinite and
meets $Z(G)$ trivially, so $S$ is infinite.  If $N$ is a normal subgroup
of finite index in $G$, then $I(N)$ has finite index in the infinite
group $(R,+)$, so $I(N)\ne0$ and $N=G$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:level}.  The
same holds for $S$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Root detection}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:root}
If $N\trianglelefteq G$ contains an element $g\notin Z(G)$, then
$I(N)\ne0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{A localized root that does not commute with $g$.}  Let $w$ bound
the propagation of all entries of $g$ and $g^{-1}$, and put $L=2w+3$.
Let $\Sigma_L$ be the set of elements $\lambda\chi_C$,
$\lambda\chi_Cu$ and $\lambda\chi_Cu^{-1}$ with $\lambda\in\F_q$ and
$C$ a cylinder of radius at least $k_L$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:subshift}(P).
The set $C_g=\{s\in R: g\text{ commutes with }e_{ij}(s)\text{ for all }i\ne j\}$
is closed under sums and negatives by~\eqref{eq:steinberg}, and under
products since $e_{ij}(st)=[e_{ik}(s),e_{kj}(t)]$.  The additive span of
$\Sigma_L$ contains $1=\sum_C\chi_C$, $u^{\pm1}=\sum_C\chi_Cu^{\pm1}$,
every $\lambda$ and every letter indicator.  If $\Sigma_L\subseteq C_g$,
then $C_g=R$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:generation} and $g$ is central.  So some
$h=e_{ij}(s)$ with $s\in\Sigma_L$ does not commute with $g$.  Moreover
$s\in B_1(U)$ for some $U\in\{C,T^{-1}C,TC\}$, since
$\chi_C=E_{00}(C)$, $\chi_Cu=E_{10}(T^{-1}C)$ and
$\chi_Cu^{-1}=E_{-1,0}(TC)$, and every translate of $C$ has return
times exceeding $L$.

\emph{Localization.}  Put $k=[g,h]\in N$, so $k\ne1$, and $m'=w+1$.
The entries of $ghg^{-1}-I$ are $g_{pi}\,s\,(g^{-1})_{jq}$, which lie in
$B_{m'}(U)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:absorption}, since $2(1+w)<L$.  Also
$h^{-1}-I=-sE_{ij}$.  With $Y=ghg^{-1}-I$ and $Y'=h^{-1}-I$,
$k-I=Y+Y'+YY'\in M_3(B_{m'}(U))$, and likewise $k^{-1}-I$.  Let
$e=\chi_V$ be the unit of $B_{m'}(U)$ and put
$Q_U=(1-e)I_3+M_3(B_{m'}(U))$.  It is closed under products, because
$(1-e)b=b(1-e)=0$ for $b\in B_{m'}(U)$, and it contains $k$ and
$k^{-1}$.  Through
\[
M_3(B_{m'}(U))\cong M_3\bigl(M_{2m'+1}(\LC(U,\F_q))\bigr)\cong\LC(U,M_d(\F_q)),
\qquad d=3(2m'+1)\ge9,
\]
multiplication in $Q_U$ is pointwise in $x\in U$, and $k$ corresponds
to a locally constant $\kappa\colon U\to\GL_d(\F_q)$ with
$\kappa\not\equiv I$.

\emph{Case 1: some value $\kappa(x_0)$ is not scalar.}  Choose a clopen
$W\ni x_0$ in $U$ with $\kappa|_W=\kappa_0$.  For
$\varepsilon\in\GL_d(\F_q)$ let $\iota_W(\varepsilon)\in Q_U$ be the
element corresponding to the function equal to $\varepsilon$ on $W$ and
to $I$ on $U\setminus W$; explicitly
$\iota_W(\varepsilon)=(1-\chi_{V_{m'}(W)})I_3+\sum\varepsilon_{(p,a),(p',b)}\,E_{ab}(W)\,E_{pp'}$,
the sum over $p,p'\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $|a|,|b|\le m'$.  Then
$\iota_W$ is a homomorphism $\GL_d(\F_q)\to\GL_3(R)$, and
$\iota_W(\SL_d(\F_q))\le G$: the group $\SL_d(\F_q)$ is generated by the
transvections $t=I+c\,e_{(p,a),(p',b)}$, and
\begin{itemize}
\item if $p\ne p'$, then $\iota_W(t)=e_{pp'}(cE_{ab}(W))\in G$;
\item if $p=p'$, then $a\ne b$; put $x=cE_{ab}(W)$ and $y=E_{bb}(W)$, so
that $xy=x$, $yx=0$ and $x^2=0$.  For $p''\ne p$ the matrices
$M=xE_{pp''}$ and $M'=yE_{p''p}$ satisfy $M^2=M'^2=M'M=0$ and
$MM'=xE_{pp}$, so
$[e_{pp''}(x),e_{p''p}(y)]=(I+M)(I+M')(I-M)(I-M')=I+xE_{pp}=\iota_W(t)$.
\end{itemize}
Multiplication in $Q_U$ is pointwise, so
$[k,\iota_W(\varepsilon)]=\iota_W([\kappa_0,\varepsilon])$.  The group
$N_W=\{\varepsilon\in\SL_d(\F_q):\iota_W(\varepsilon)\in N\}$ is normal
in $\SL_d(\F_q)$ and contains every $[\kappa_0,\varepsilon]$.  If $N_W$
were central, then $\varepsilon\mapsto[\kappa_0,\varepsilon]$ would be a
homomorphism into the abelian group $Z(\SL_d(\F_q))$, because
$[\kappa_0,\varepsilon\delta]=[\kappa_0,\varepsilon]\,\varepsilon[\kappa_0,\delta]\varepsilon^{-1}=[\kappa_0,\varepsilon][\kappa_0,\delta]$
when $[\kappa_0,\delta]$ is scalar.  Since $\SL_d(\F_q)$ is perfect for
$d\ge3$, the homomorphism is trivial, so $\kappa_0$ commutes with every
transvection and is scalar, a contradiction.  So $N_W$ is a noncentral
normal subgroup; as $\PSL_d(\F_q)$ is simple, $N_WZ=\SL_d(\F_q)$, the
quotient $\SL_d(\F_q)/N_W$ is abelian, and perfectness gives
$N_W=\SL_d(\F_q)$.  In particular
$e_{12}(\chi_W)=\iota_W(I+e_{(1,0),(2,0)})\in N$ with $\chi_W\ne0$.

\emph{Case 2: $\kappa=\lambda I_d$ with $\lambda\colon U\to\F_q^\times$
locally constant and $\lambda\not\equiv1$.}  Then $k=cI_3$ with
$c\in\LC(X,\F_q)^\times$ equal to $\lambda\circ T^{-a}$ on $T^aU$ for
$|a|\le m'$ and to $1$ off $V$.  Choose a clopen $W\subseteq U$ with
$\lambda|_W=\lambda_0\ne1$ and put
$r=\chi_{T^{m'+1}W}\,u=u\,\chi_{T^{m'}W}\ne0$.  The set $T^{m'+1}W$ is
disjoint from $V$, because $m'+1-a\in[1,2m'+1]=[1,L]$ for $|a|\le m'$;
so $c=1$ there, while $\alpha(c^{-1})=c^{-1}\circ T^{-1}=\lambda_0^{-1}$
there, since $T^{-1}$ maps $T^{m'+1}W$ onto level $m'$ of the tower over
$W$.  So $crc^{-1}=c\,\chi_{T^{m'+1}W}\,\alpha(c^{-1})\,u=\lambda_0^{-1}r$,
and
\[
[k,e_{12}(r)]=e_{12}(crc^{-1})\,e_{12}(-r)=e_{12}\bigl((\lambda_0^{-1}-1)r\bigr)\in N,
\qquad(\lambda_0^{-1}-1)r\ne0.
\]
In both cases $I(N)\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:simple}
Every normal subgroup of $G$ is contained in $Z(G)$ or equal to $G$.
So $S=G/Z(G)$ is simple.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:root} and Lemma~\ref{lem:level}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:main}}]
$G$ is finitely generated by Lemma~\ref{lem:generation}
and~\eqref{eq:steinberg}.  $Z(G)$ is finite and $S$ is infinite by
Proposition~\ref{prop:center}; $S$ is simple by
Corollary~\ref{cor:simple}; $S$ has property~(T) by
Section~\ref{sec:kazhdan}; and $S$ is LEF, sofic and hyperlinear by
Proposition~\ref{prop:lef}.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences}\label{sec:consequences}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:consequences}
Let $G$ and $S$ be as in Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:main}}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is not finitely presented, and neither is $G$.
\item $S$ does not have the factorization property, and $C^*(S)$ does
not have the local lifting property.
\item $G$ and $S$ are MF groups that are not weakly quasidiagonal.
\item The marked finite simple groups $\PSL_{3N_k}(\F_q)$ of
Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:marked}} converge to $S$, and their
Cayley graphs form a family of expanders.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(1) A finitely presented LEF group is residually finite: the finitely
many relators already hold in the finite model of a large ball, which
then gives a homomorphism to a finite group injective on that ball.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:center}, $G$ and $S$ have no nontrivial finite
quotient.

(2) A group with property~(T) and the factorization property is
residually finite~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Kirchberg}, and $S$ is not
residually finite.  Ozawa shows that if an infinite simple group with
property~(T) is hyperlinear, then its full $C^*$-algebra fails the
local lifting property~\cite[p.~527]{Ozawa}.

(3) A group is MF~\cite{CDE} if it has a separating sequence of
asymptotically multiplicative maps to unitary groups, and weakly
quasidiagonal~\cite{Dadarlat} if the maps can be taken unital
completely positive on $C^*(G)$.  Exactly multiplicative permutation
models exist by the LEF property, and their permutation matrices
satisfy $\|\sigma(g)-I\|\ge\sqrt2$ for $g\ne1$, so $G$ and $S$ are MF\@.
By a theorem of Ozawa and Thom, an infinite weakly quasidiagonal group
with property~(T) has an infinite residually finite
quotient~\cite[Proposition~3.19]{Dadarlat}.  Every quotient of $S$ is
$S$ or trivial, and every finite quotient of $G$ is trivial, so neither
group is weakly quasidiagonal.  The introduction of~\cite{Dadarlat}
states that MF groups are weakly quasidiagonal; the implication that
follows from the definitions is the converse, by polar decomposition.

(4) Convergence is Proposition~\ref{prop:marked}.  The images of
$u^{\pm1}$, $\chi_a$ and $\lambda$ generate $M_{N_k}(\F_q)$, so each
$\SL_{3N_k}(\F_q)$ with its marking is a quotient of
$\EL_3(\F_q\langle t_1,\dots,t_r\rangle)$ marked by the corresponding
elementary matrices, and that group has property~(T)
by~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.  Cayley graphs of quotients of a Kazhdan
group with respect to the images of a fixed generating set form a family
of expanders.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds, with the same proof and for every rank
$n\ge3$, over any ring $R$ that is finitely generated, simple with
center $\F_q$, embedded unitally in an ultraproduct of matrix algebras
over $\F_q$, and equipped with tower subrings as in
Section~\ref{subsec:towers} satisfying the absorption lemma.
\end{remark}

\section{Word problems}\label{sec:degrees}

Fix $A$ and $q$.  For an infinite minimal subshift $X\subseteq A^{\Z}$
write $R_X$, $G_X$ and $S_X$ for the ring and the groups of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and let $\Sigma_0$ be the generating set
$\{e_{ij}(s): i\ne j,\ s\in\{1,u,u^{-1},\chi_a\ (a\in A),\lambda\}\}$
of $G_X$; its image generates $S_X$.  The word problem $\mathrm{WP}(S_X)$
is the set of words in $\Sigma_0^{\pm1}$ that equal $1$ in $S_X$.  Its
Turing degree does not depend on the finite generating set, so it is an
isomorphism invariant of $S_X$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:degree}
$\mathrm{WP}(S_X)$ and $L(X)$ have the same Turing degree.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$L(X)$ is computable from $\mathrm{WP}(S_X)$.}  For a word
$v=v_0\cdots v_{n-1}\in A^n$,
\[
\chi_{[v]}=\prod_{t=0}^{n-1}u^{-t}\chi_{v_t}u^{t}
\]
is the indicator of the cylinder $\{x: x_{[0,n)}=v\}$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:generation}.  By~\eqref{eq:steinberg}, for any product
$s_1\cdots s_r$ of elements of $\{u,u^{-1},\chi_a\}$ there is a nested
commutator word in $\Sigma_0$, computable from $s_1,\dots,s_r$, whose
value is $e_{12}(s_1\cdots s_r)$: use $e_{ik}(st)=[e_{ij}(s),e_{jk}(t)]$
with a third index $j$ at each step.  Then a group word $W_v$ with value
$e_{12}(\chi_{[v]})$ is computable from $v$.  An element $e_{12}(f)$ is
central in $G_X$ if and only if $f=0$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:center}.
So $W_v=1$ in $S_X$ if and only if the cylinder of $v$ is empty, that is,
if and only if $v\notin L(X)$.

\emph{$\mathrm{WP}(S_X)$ is computable from $L(X)$.}  Represent an
element of $R_X$ as a finite formal sum $\sum_jf_ju^j$, each $f_j$
given by a table $\Phi_j\colon A^{2\rho+1}\to\F_q$ for a common radius
$\rho$.  Sums and products of such representations are computable, by
the rule $(fu^i)(gu^j)=f\,\alpha^i(g)\,u^{i+j}$ and shifting of windows.
Two representations define the same element of $R_X$ if and only if the
tables of each coefficient agree on $L_{2\rho+1}(X)$, which is decided
with the oracle $L(X)$.  Given a word in $\Sigma_0^{\pm1}$, compute its
value $g\in M_3(R_X)$ in this representation.  Since $g\in G_X$, it is
trivial in $S_X$ if and only if $g=\mu I_3$ for some
$\mu\in\F_q^\times$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:center}; that is finitely
many equality tests.
\end{proof}

For an irrational $\alpha\in(0,1)$, the Sturmian subshift
$X_\alpha\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\Z}$ is the closure of the codings
$n\mapsto\lfloor(n+1)\alpha+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor n\alpha+\theta\rfloor$,
$\theta\in[0,1)$, of the rotation by $\alpha$.  It is infinite and
minimal, and $|L_m(X_\alpha)|=m+1$ for every $m$
(Morse and Hedlund~\cite{MorseHedlund40}).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sturmian}
$L(X_\alpha)$ and $\alpha$ have the same Turing degree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A word $v\in\{0,1\}^m$ lies in $L_m(X_\alpha)$ if and only if the
intersection of the arcs $\{\theta: \lfloor\theta+(t+1)\alpha\rfloor-\lfloor\theta+t\alpha\rfloor=v_t\}$,
$0\le t<m$, of the circle is nonempty.  Each arc has endpoints in
$\{-t\alpha\bmod1: 0\le t\le m\}$, and these points are distinct because
$\alpha$ is irrational; so nonemptiness is decided by the cyclic order
of finitely many points $-t\alpha\bmod1$, which is computable from
$\alpha$ to sufficient precision.  Conversely, every word of
$L_m(X_\alpha)$ has $\lfloor m\alpha\rfloor$ or
$\lfloor m\alpha\rfloor+1$ letters equal to $1$, and both values occur,
so $\lfloor m\alpha\rfloor$ is computable from $L(X_\alpha)$ for every
$m$, and with it $\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:degrees}}]
The first two sentences are Proposition~\ref{prop:degree} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sturmian}.  Let $d$ be a Turing degree and
$B\subseteq\N$ a set of degree $d$.  If $d$ is the computable degree,
take $\alpha=\sqrt2-1$.  Otherwise let $\alpha$ have the binary
expansion $0.1\,b_0\,1\,b_1\,1\,b_2\cdots$, where $b_n=1$ if $n\in B$
and $b_n=0$ otherwise.  The expansion is not eventually periodic, since
$B$ is not computable, so $\alpha$ is irrational, and $\alpha$ and $B$
are computable from each other.  Then $S_{X_\alpha}$ has the required
properties by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and its word problem has degree
$d$.  There are continuum many Turing degrees, and the degree of the
word problem is an isomorphism invariant, so the groups
$S_{X_\alpha}$ fall into continuum many isomorphism classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For computable $\alpha$, for example the Fibonacci subshift, the word
problem of $S_{X_\alpha}$ is decidable.  Whether $S_X\cong S_Y$ forces
a dynamical relation between $X$ and $Y$ is not addressed here.
\end{remark}
