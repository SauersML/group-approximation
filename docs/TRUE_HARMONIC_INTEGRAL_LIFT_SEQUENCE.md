# Harmonic discriminant repair is a norm-controlled integral lifting problem

Date: 2026-08-11

## Purpose

The paired-discriminant formula leaves two metric quantities for a harmonic
class: its cycle radius and the radius of its glued saturated-range class.
`TRUE_NONLINEAR_DISCRIMINANT_REPAIR_FORMULA.md` identifies the relevant
harmonic quotient but still writes the numerator as a closest-vector problem
in the large range lattice `L`.

This note removes that asymmetry.  The paired numerator is exactly the
additional norm paid when a harmonic dual vector is lifted to an ambient
integer carry.  All harmonic paired data occur in one short exact sequence

`0 -> L -> M_Sigma -> Q^# -> 0`.                      `(HIL1)`

Consequently the harmonic part of carry repair is equivalent to a
norm-controlled integral section of `(HIL1)`.  When harmonic rank is fixed,
the optimal nonlinear lift and the optimal linear section are equivalent up
to a constant depending only on that rank.  At `p=53` the rank is two and a
Gauss-reduced basis gives the explicit factor `4/sqrt(3)`.

This is the direct bridge from paired discriminant geometry to an integral
decoder.  It does not yet construct a uniformly bounded decoder over all
projective primes, and it does not remove the separate universal chart-
selection gate in the full hyperlinear-to-sofic problem.

## 1. Setup

Let

`Lambda=Z^m subset R^m`,                              `(HIL2)`

let `W` be a rational subspace, and put

`L=Lambda intersect W`,
`K=Lambda intersect W^perp`.                          `(HIL3)`

Let `Sigma subset K` be the spherical lattice generated by the bounded
lifted templates in a finite projective chart.  Write

`S=span_R(Sigma)`,
`H=K_R intersect S^perp`,
`Q=pi_H(K)`.                                         `(HIL4)`

Then

`Q^#=K^# intersect H`                                `(HIL5)`

by Lemma 1 of `TRUE_PROJECTIVE_HARMONIC_SYSTOLE.md`.

Define the integral annihilator of the spherical templates by

`M_Sigma={z in Lambda:<z,sigma>=0 for every sigma in Sigma}.` `(HIL6)`

Since `Sigma` spans `S`, this is equivalently the set of integer vectors
whose cycle projection is harmonic.

## 2. The harmonic lift sequence

Let `p_K:R^m->W^perp=K_R` be orthogonal projection.

**Theorem 1 (exact harmonic lift sequence).**  Projection restricts to a
surjective lattice homomorphism

`P_Sigma:M_Sigma->Q^#`, `P_Sigma(z)=p_K z`,          `(HIL7)`

with kernel `L`.  Hence

`0 -> L -> M_Sigma --P_Sigma--> Q^# -> 0`           `(HIL8)`

is a short exact sequence of free abelian groups.

**Proof.**  If `z in M_Sigma`, Proposition 1 of
`INTEGRAL_CARRY_DISCRIMINANT_DUALITY.md` gives `p_Kz in K^#`.  Moreover,
for every `sigma in Sigma`,

`<p_Kz,sigma>=<z,sigma>=0`.                          `(HIL9)`

Thus `p_Kz in H`, and `(HIL5)` gives `p_Kz in Q^#`.

Conversely, let `u in Q^# subset K^#`.  Since `p_K(Lambda)=K^#`, choose
`z in Lambda` with `p_Kz=u`.  Because `u in H`, equation `(HIL9)` vanishes
for every spherical template, so `z in M_Sigma`.  This proves surjectivity.
Finally, `p_Kz=0` exactly when `z in W`; for integral `z` this is exactly
`z in L`.  End proof.

Theorem 1 strengthens the set-theoretic pullback

`Q^#=H intersect (Lambda+W)`                          `(HIL10)`

from the nonlinear repair note: it identifies the full lattice extension
whose quotient map produces that pullback.

### Regulator balance

Put

`S_sat=Lambda intersect S`.                           `(HIL10a)`

For a Euclidean lattice `J`, write `Reg(J)` for its covolume in its real
span.

**Theorem 1A (exact regulator quotient).**  The harmonic homology and
cohomology lattices satisfy

`Reg(Q^#)=Reg(S_sat)/Reg(L)`,
`Reg(Q)=Reg(L)/Reg(S_sat)`.                            `(HIL10b)`

In particular they are reciprocal, as required by `Q^#=Q^dual`.  If

`K_H=Lambda intersect H`, `D_H=Q^#/K_H`,              `(HIL10c)`

then

`|D_H|=Reg(K_H)Reg(L)/Reg(S_sat)`.                    `(HIL10d)`

**Proof.**  We use two elementary covolume facts in the unimodular lattice
`Lambda=Z^m`.

First, if `U` is rational, the primitive orthogonal lattices
`Lambda intersect U` and `Lambda intersect U^perp` have isomorphic
discriminant groups with opposite pairings.  Hence their Gram determinants,
and therefore their regulators, are equal.

Second, suppose a lattice `M` lies in an orthogonal sum `U directSum V`, its
intersection with `U` is `J`, and orthogonal projection maps `M` onto a
lattice `T` in `V` with kernel `J`.  Lift a basis of `T` to `M` and append a
basis of `J`.  Subtracting the real `U`-components of the lifted vectors is
a determinant-one column operation.  The resulting Gram volume factors
orthogonally, so

`Reg(M)=Reg(J)Reg(T)`.                                `(HIL10e)`

Apply the first fact to `S` and the second to the exact sequence `(HIL8)`:

`Reg(S_sat)=Reg(M_Sigma)=Reg(L)Reg(Q^#)`.             `(HIL10f)`

This proves the first formula in `(HIL10b)`.  Similarly, projection
`K->Q` has kernel `S_sat`, while the orthogonal-complement identity gives
`Reg(K)=Reg(L)`.  Hence

`Reg(L)=Reg(K)=Reg(S_sat)Reg(Q)`,                     `(HIL10g)`

which proves the second formula.  Finally `K_H subset Q^#`, so its index is
the quotient of covolumes:

`[Q^#:K_H]=Reg(K_H)/Reg(Q^#)`.                       `(HIL10h)`

Substitute `(HIL10b)` to get `(HIL10d)`.  End proof.

The formula is directly computable without a harmonic basis.  Let `C_W`
and `C_S` be full-column-rank integer matrices whose columns generate
finite-index sublattices of `L` and `S_sat`, of indices `t_W` and `t_S`.
Then

`Reg(Q^#)`
` =(t_W/t_S)sqrt(det(C_S^T C_S)/det(C_W^T C_W)).`     `(HIL10i)`

The indices are the saturation indices obtained from Smith data, or
equivalently the gcds of the maximal minors.  Thus `(HIL10i)` replaces an
exact basis of the large harmonic kernel by two determinant-and-saturation
computations in the boundary and coboundary spans.

There is also a decoder obstruction depending only on this regulator.

**Corollary 1B (determinant lower bound for integral repair).**  Let
`r=rank(Q)>0`.  Every integral section `s` of `(HIL8)` satisfies

`||s||>=Reg(Q^#)^(-1/r)`
`      =(Reg(L)/Reg(S_sat))^(1/r)`.                  `(HIL10j)`

Consequently the optimal nonlinear lift distortion of `(HIL18)` below
satisfies

`kappa_Sigma>=(1/beta_r)(Reg(L)/Reg(S_sat))^(1/r)`.  `(HIL10k)`

**Proof.**  Choose a basis matrix `V` of `Q^#` and write `Z=s(V)`.  If
`lambda_1,...,lambda_r` are the squared singular values of the section,
then

`product_i lambda_i=det(Z^T Z)/det(V^T V)`.          `(HIL10l)`

The columns of `Z` are linearly independent integral vectors.  Therefore
`det(Z^T Z)` is a positive integer and is at least one, while
`det(V^T V)=Reg(Q^#)^2`.  The largest `lambda_i` is at least the geometric
mean, proving `(HIL10j)`.  Theorem 4 below gives an integral section of norm
at most `beta_r kappa_Sigma`; combine it with `(HIL10j)` to obtain
`(HIL10k)`.  End proof.

For harmonic rank two this becomes

`kappa_Sigma^2>=(3/16)Reg(L)/Reg(S_sat)`.             `(HIL10m)`

Thus a family with `Reg(L)/Reg(S_sat)->infinity` cannot have uniformly
bounded integral carry repair.  This is only a one-sided obstruction:
bounded regulator ratio does not control lattice shape or the extension
class, so it does not by itself construct a bounded section.

## 3. The numerator is the lift cost

For `u in Q^#`, define its shortest ambient integral lift norm

`lambda_Sigma(u)=min{||z||:z in M_Sigma, P_Sigma(z)=u}.` `(HIL11)`

The minimum exists because the fiber is a coset of the lattice `L`.
Let

`eta:Q^#/(K intersect H)->D_L`                        `(HIL12)`

be the injective paired-class map of Theorem 4 in
`TRUE_NONLINEAR_DISCRIMINANT_REPAIR_FORMULA.md`.

**Theorem 2 (Pythagorean paired-lift formula).**  For every `u in Q^#`,

`lambda_Sigma(u)^2`
` =||u||^2+rho_L(eta(u))^2`.                         `(HIL13)`

Here `eta(u)` means the class of `u` modulo `K intersect H`.

**Proof.**  Every lift `z` has the unique orthogonal decomposition

`z=u+a`, `a=z-u in W`.                               `(HIL14)`

Two such lifts differ by an element of `ker(P_Sigma)=L`, so their range
components form precisely the coset `eta(u)` in `L^#/L`.  Therefore the
least possible norm of `a` is `rho_L(eta(u))`.  Orthogonality of `u` and
`a` proves `(HIL13)`.  End proof.

There is also a basis-free affine integer formula.  If
`h_1,...,h_r` is a basis of `H` and the lifted templates generate `Sigma`,
then

`lambda_Sigma(u)`
` =min{||z||:z in Z^m,`
`       <z,sigma>=0 for every lifted template sigma,`
`       <z,h_j>=<u,h_j> for j=1,...,r}.`             `(HIL15)`

Indeed the spherical equations put `p_Kz` in `H`, while the `r` harmonic
equations force that projection to equal `u`.  Thus the paired numerator can
be computed without constructing either a basis of the full cycle lattice
`K` or a basis of the full saturated range lattice `L`.

An equivalent particularly compact identity is

`rho_L(eta(u))^2`
` =min_(z in M_Sigma, P_Sigma z=u)||z||^2-||u||^2`. `(HIL16)`

### The exact Moore--Penrose metric

For the optimal nonlinear phase-repair constant, the range component is
measured in the Moore--Penrose metric rather than the Euclidean metric.
Define

`rho_(A,L)(eta(u))`
` =min_(z in M_Sigma, P_Sigma z=u)`
`    ||A^dagger(z-u)||`,                              `(HIL16a)`

and the graph lift norm

`lambda_(A,Sigma)(u)`
` =min_(z in M_Sigma, P_Sigma z=u)`
`    sqrt(||u||^2+||A^dagger(z-u)||^2).`              `(HIL16b)`

The same orthogonal-fiber proof gives the exact identity

`lambda_(A,Sigma)(u)^2`
` =||u||^2+rho_(A,L)(eta(u))^2`.                     `(HIL16c)`

Thus `(HIL13)` is the Euclidean carry metric and `(HIL16c)` is the exact
ellipsoidal metric in the nonlinear repair envelope.  They are uniformly
equivalent under the real Hodge bounds because

`rho_L(eta(u))/||A||`
` <=rho_(A,L)(eta(u))`
` <=rho_L(eta(u))/sigma_+(A).`                       `(HIL16d)`

## 4. Exact harmonic distortion

Put

`K_H=K intersect H`, `D_H=Q^#/K_H`,                  `(HIL17)`

and for `c in D_H` let

`rho_H(c)=min{||u||:u in Q^#, u+K_H=c}`.             `(HIL18)`

Define the harmonic integral-lift distortion

`kappa_Sigma=sup_(0!=u in Q^#)`
`              lambda_Sigma(u)/||u||`.               `(HIL19)`

**Theorem 3 (finite harmonic discriminant formula).**

`kappa_Sigma^2`
` =max(1,max_(0!=c in D_H)`
`   (1+rho_L(eta(c))^2/rho_H(c)^2)).`                `(HIL20)`

**Proof.**  By `(HIL13)`, the range term depends only on the class of `u`
in `D_H`.  Within a fixed nonzero class, the quotient in `(HIL19)` is

`sqrt(1+rho_L(eta(c))^2/||u||^2)`,                   `(HIL21)`

which is largest at a shortest representative.  The zero class contributes
the value one because `eta(0)=0`.  End proof.

Let `B_0` be the maximum spherical-template norm.  The localization theorem
from the nonlinear repair note says that every full discriminant class with
cycle radius below `1/B_0` is harmonic, and its full cycle radius is
`rho_H`.  Therefore the complete contribution of all sub-threshold classes
to `Theta` is exactly

`max(1,max_(0!=c in D_H, rho_H(c)<1/B_0)`
`  lambda_Sigma(u_c)/||u_c||)`,                      `(HIL22)`

where `u_c` is any shortest representative.  The formerly separate
numerator and denominator are one integral-lift condition number.

## 5. Linear integral decoders

Because `Q^#` is free abelian, `(HIL8)` always admits a `Z`-linear section

`s:Q^#->M_Sigma`, `P_Sigma compose s=id`.            `(HIL23)`

Extend `s` linearly to the real spans and use its Euclidean operator norm.
Every such section satisfies

`kappa_Sigma<=||s||`,                                `(HIL24)`

since `s(u)` is one competitor in `(HIL11)`.  Thus a uniformly bounded
integral section is a complete uniform decoder for the harmonic paired
radii.

The converse is true up to a rank-only constant.  We use the standard
lattice-basis fact that for every rank `r` Euclidean lattice `J`, there is a
basis `v_1,...,v_r` with dual basis `v_1^*,...,v_r^*` satisfying

`sum_i ||v_i||||v_i^*||<=beta_r`,                    `(HIL25)`

where `beta_r<infinity` depends only on `r`.  For example an LLL-reduced
basis gives such a constant.  In rank two, Gauss reduction gives

`beta_2=4/sqrt(3)`.                                  `(HIL26)`

**Theorem 4 (nonlinear lifts versus linear sections).**  If `rank(Q)=r`,
then there is a `Z`-linear section `s` of `(HIL8)` satisfying

`kappa_Sigma<=||s||<=beta_r kappa_Sigma`.            `(HIL27)`

In rank two one may take

`||s||<=(4/sqrt(3))kappa_Sigma`.                     `(HIL28)`

**Proof.**  Choose a basis satisfying `(HIL25)`.  For each `v_i`, choose a
shortest lift `z_i` in `(HIL11)`, and define the `Z`-linear map by
`s(v_i)=z_i`.  It is a section.  If `u=sum_i x_i v_i`, then

`|x_i|=|<v_i^*,u>|<=||v_i^*||||u||`,                 `(HIL29)`

and

`||s(u)||`
` <=sum_i |x_i| ||z_i||`
` <=kappa_Sigma sum_i ||v_i^*||||v_i|| ||u||`
` <=beta_r kappa_Sigma ||u||`.                       `(HIL30)`

This proves the upper bound; `(HIL24)` proves the lower bound.

For `(HIL26)`, choose a Gauss-reduced basis with
`||v_1||<=||v_2||` and
`|<v_1,v_2>|<=||v_1||^2/2`.  The angle between the basis vectors has sine at
least `sqrt(3)/2`, so

`||v_i||||v_i^*||<=2/sqrt(3)`                        `(HIL31)`

for both indices.  Summing proves `(HIL26)`.  End proof.

Theorem 4 matters because a nonlinear closest-vector decoder is hard to
transport across charts, while a section is an explicit integer matrix.
For bounded harmonic rank, no qualitative strength is lost by searching for
the linear object.

There is an identical ellipsoidal statement.  For a section `s`, define its
decoded graph map

`s_A(u)=(u,A^dagger(s(u)-u))`
`       in H directSum ker(A)^perp`.                 `(HIL31a)`

If

`kappa_(A,Sigma)=sup_(0!=u in Q^#)`
`  lambda_(A,Sigma)(u)/||u||`,                       `(HIL31b)`

then the same reduced-basis proof, choosing the basis lifts in the graph
norm `(HIL16b)`, gives

`kappa_(A,Sigma)<=||s_A||<=beta_r kappa_(A,Sigma)`  `(HIL31c)`

for some integral section `s`.  Moreover `(HIL16d)` gives

`1+(kappa_Sigma^2-1)/||A||^2`
` <=kappa_(A,Sigma)^2`
` <=1+(kappa_Sigma^2-1)/sigma_+(A)^2`.               `(HIL31d)`

Thus the integral-section criterion applies directly to the exact nonlinear
repair norm; passing through `Theta` is optional once `s_A` is known.

## 6. The exact rank-two certificate at `p=53`

At the first cuspidal projective level,

`dim_Q H_2(H_53,Q)=2`.                               `(HIL32)`

The compact non-free cellular model now computes this rank-two lattice
exactly.  In its `328` free signed-coinvariant coordinates,

`Q^#/(K intersect H) ~= Z/qZ`,                       `(HIL32a)`

with the `83`-digit integer `q` in
`TRUE_P53_CELLULAR_HARMONIC_DISCRIMINANT.md`.  Its shortest nonintegral
dual vector has exact squared norm `a/q=0.0713191090...`, lies below the
spherical threshold `1/12`.  Exact saturated-range reduction and exhaustive
support certification give its minimum ambient integral lift norm:

`lambda(u)^2=4`,
`lambda(u)/||u||=2sqrt(q/a)=7.4890553601...`.         `(HIL32b)`

The four-term lift is exact, and exhaustive cycle-pairing profiles rule out
every integral lift of squared norm below four.  Thus this particular
discriminant class is completely solved, not merely bounded.  A controlled
lift of the second Gauss-reduced basis vector has now also been found, so the
full rank-two linear section is explicit.
The two-primary signed-coinvariant correction and the norm of the comparison
to the free HAP carry metric remain to be certified before `(HIL32b)` can be
read as a bound in the original chart.

Let the columns of `V` be an exact `Z`-basis of `Q^#`, and choose integral
lifts `z_1,z_2 in M_Sigma` with

`P_Sigma z_i=V_i`.                                   `(HIL33)`

Put

`G_H=V^T V`, `G_Z=(z_1,z_2)^T(z_1,z_2)`.            `(HIL34)`

The squared operator norm of the resulting section is the largest
generalized eigenvalue

`||s||^2=lambda_max(G_H^(-1)G_Z).`                   `(HIL35)`

Since both matrices are `2 x 2` rational matrices, `(HIL35)` is an exact
quadratic algebraic number.  It immediately certifies

`rho_L(eta(c))^2`
` <=(||s||^2-1)rho_H(c)^2`                           `(HIL36)`

for every harmonic class `c`, without enumerating `D_H`.

For the certified level-`53` lifts, their supports are disjoint and

`G_Z=[[4,0],[0,16]].`                               `(HIL35c)`

The exact generalized characteristic polynomial is recorded in
`TRUE_P53_CELLULAR_HARMONIC_DISCRIMINANT.md`; its largest root is

`56.10275777902312...`,                              `(HIL35d)`

so this integral section has norm

`||s||=7.49017741972933...`.                         `(HIL35e)`

Together with the exact shortest-class lower bound `(HIL32b)`, this gives

`7.489055360091924...<=kappa_Sigma`
` <=7.49017741972933...`.                            `(HIL35f)`

Thus the compact free harmonic lift problem at the first cuspidal level is
fully bounded.  The second lift need not be a shortest representative for
this conclusion; exact pairings make it a valid integral section, while the
first class supplies the matching lower bound to within `0.015%`.

The same construction at the next cuspidal level supplies a second family
datum.  At `p=61`, the reduced harmonic rank is again two, exact lifts have
Gram matrix

`[[17,-3],[-3,10]]`,

and the resulting section norm is `11.793652354043665...`.  The shortest
dual vector has norm `0.3532226522900641...`, so it is farther from zero
than at `p=53`.  This replication rules out immediate denominator collapse
but does not prove a uniform bound; see
`TRUE_P61_CELLULAR_HARMONIC_SECTION.md`.

Before recovering `V`, Theorem 1A already gives the exact harmonic
regulator from the two cellular spans.  If `C_W,C_S` are independent
integer coboundary and boundary spanning matrices at level `53`, with
saturation indices `t_W,t_S`, then

`Reg(Q^#)`
` =(t_W/t_S)sqrt(det(C_S^T C_S)/det(C_W^T C_W))`,     `(HIL35a)`

and every integral decoder obeys

`kappa_Sigma^2`
` >=(3/16)(t_S/t_W)`
`      sqrt(det(C_W^T C_W)/det(C_S^T C_S)).`          `(HIL35b)`

Thus the regulator and a rigorous decoder lower bound can be computed by
modular determinant and Smith-index methods without lifting the numerical
harmonic vectors.  The exact plane is still needed for the dual systole,
lattice shape, and the matching upper bound.

For the exact nonlinear metric, put `a_i=z_i-V_i` and replace `G_Z` by

`G_(Z,A)=G_H+`
`  (A^dagger(a_1,a_2))^T A^dagger(a_1,a_2).`         `(HIL36a)`

Then `lambda_max(G_H^(-1)G_(Z,A))` is `||s_A||^2` and bounds every
ellipsoidal paired radius simultaneously.

Equations `(HIL15)` and `(HIL33)` give the efficient exact workflow:

1. compute the independent boundary/coboundary determinants and saturation
   indices in `(HIL35a)`;
2. recover an exact rational basis of the two-dimensional Hodge kernel;
3. compute `Q^#` from the rational pullback `(HIL10)` and verify its
   regulator against `(HIL35a)`;
4. Gauss-reduce its two-column basis;
5. solve two affine integral lift problems `(HIL15)`;
6. evaluate the `2 x 2` generalized eigenvalue `(HIL35)`.

The observed 64.15 percent leverage concentration on the five-term cell
orbit can be used in steps 1 and 4 to eliminate the other nine cell orbits
by sparse exact equations.  Neither a rank-20,000 cycle basis nor a complete
discriminant enumeration is needed.

## 7. Family-level TRUE criterion and remaining gate

For a family of projective charts, suppose:

1. the real Hodge bounds keep `sigma_+(A)` uniformly positive and `||A||`
   uniformly bounded;
2. the nonsmall/spherical discriminant sector has a uniformly bounded
   integral decoder;
3. the harmonic lift sequences `(HIL8)` admit sections of uniformly bounded
   operator norm.

Then Theorems 3--5 of the nonlinear repair note and `(HIL24)` bound the full
carry constants uniformly.  The multiplicative comparison there gives
uniform nonlinear circle repair on the selected charts.

Condition 3 is now a concrete matrix statement, not an unspecified paired
CVP.  At fixed harmonic rank it is also necessary up to the rank constant
`beta_r`.  The live arithmetic task is to construct these sections uniformly
from the local spherical-template presentation, or to prove their norms
diverge.

`TRUE_CHAIN_COMPARISON_DECODER_TRANSFER.md` shows that this section problem
is invariant, up to an explicit multiplicative constant, under bounded
integral chain comparison.  If integral cochain maps in both directions
induce inverse maps on the harmonic lattice, then the optimal nonlinear
lift distortion and least section norm change by at most
`||f^2|| ||g^2||`.  Thus a local compact cellular model may be used in place
of the large HAP complex without first reconstructing a rational harmonic
basis.

Even a successful selected `SL_3(Z)` theorem would not alone prove that
every hyperlinear group is sofic.  As recorded in
`TRUE_SELECTED_CHART_QUANTIFIER_BOUNDARY.md`, the universal proof must still
show that arbitrary hyperlinear microstates can be routed through a family
with this decoder, or establish the equivalent universal radical domination
inequality.  The present theorem advances the integral layer without
silently replacing that final quantifier.
