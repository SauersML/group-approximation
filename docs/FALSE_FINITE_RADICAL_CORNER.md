# Finite-radical corner extraction

Date: 2026-08-11

This note records a norm-to-trace theorem which is useful independently of
the present candidates.  It is a special, completely explicit case of the
property-`(T)` radical-corner mechanism in `docs/NOTEPAD.md`.  It also
separates that mechanism from Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky, Proposition 1.6.

No claim is made here that the cyclic-shear group is MF, and therefore no
claim is made that the main hyperlinear-versus-sofic problem has been solved.

## 1. Statement

Write

`Rad_sof(E)=intersection_(phi:E->S, S sofic) ker(phi)`.

Here **MF** means group-theoretic operator-norm MF: there is an injective
homomorphism

`rho:E -> U(Q_norm)`,
`Q_norm=product_n M_(d_n)/directSum_n M_(d_n)`,          `(FRC1)`

where `directSum` is the operator-norm `c_0` ideal.

**Theorem (finite-radical corner extraction).**  Let `E` be a countable MF
group.  If

`1 != F normal E`, `F` finite, `F <= Rad_sof(E)`,       `(FRC2)`

then `E` has a hyperlinear nonsofic quotient.  In particular, the existence
of such an `E` answers negatively the question whether every hyperlinear
group is sofic.

The theorem requires no hyperlinearity assumption on `E/F` and no lower
bound on the relative rank of a spectral sector in the original MF
matrices.

## 2. Proof

In `C*_max(E)` put

`p_F=|F|^(-1) sum_(f in F) u_f`, `q_F=1-p_F`.          `(FRC3)`

The element `p_F` is the orthogonal projection onto the `F`-fixed vectors in
every unitary representation.  Normality of `F` makes it central: conjugation
by an element of `E` merely permutes the terms in `(FRC3)`.

Extend `(FRC1)` to a unital star-homomorphism on `C*_max(E)` and write

`p=rho(p_F)`, `q=rho(q_F)`.                            `(FRC4)`

Then `q` is a central projection in the C-star algebra generated by
`rho(E)`.  It is nonzero.  Indeed, if `q=0`, then `p=1`; since
`u_f p_F=p_F` for every `f in F`, one would have `rho(f)=1` for every
`f in F`, contradicting injectivity and `(FRC2)`.

Lift `q` to matrix projections `q_n`, restrict to an infinite subsequence
on which they are nonzero, and compress unitary lifts of `rho(g)` to the
`q_n`-corners.  Centrality modulo the norm-`c_0` ideal makes these
compressions asymptotically unitary and pointwise operator-norm
multiplicative.  Polar correction therefore gives maps

`v_n:E -> U(q_n M_(d_n) q_n)`                         `(FRC5)`

whose operator-norm multiplicative defects tend to zero on every fixed
pair.  If the corner ranks do not tend to infinity, amplify the corners by
matrix identities; this changes neither defects nor normalized traces.

The finite average supplies the trace separation after the corner is
renormalized.  In `C*_max(E)`, using that `F=F^(-1)`,

`Delta_F=sum_(f in F)(u_f-1)^*(u_f-1)`
`       =2|F|(1-p_F)=2|F|q_F`.                         `(FRC6)`

Consequently, with normalized traces on the nonzero `q_n`-corners,

`sum_(f in F) ||v_n(f)-1||_(2,q_n)^2 -> 2|F|`.        `(FRC7)`

The norm-null compression and polar-correction errors disappear in `(FRC7)`.
Since `F` is finite, after passing to a subsequence there is one fixed
`f_0 in F` such that

`liminf_n ||v_n(f_0)-1||_(2,q_n)>0`.                  `(FRC8)`

Choose a free ultrafilter on this subsequence.  Equation `(FRC5)` defines a
homomorphism into a tracial matrix ultraproduct.  Let `L` be its image.
Then `L` is hyperlinear, while `(FRC8)` says that the quotient map

`E -> L`                                               `(FRC9)`

does not kill `f_0`.  If `L` were sofic, `(FRC9)` would be a homomorphism
from `E` to a sofic group which retains an element of `Rad_sof(E)`, contrary
to `(FRC2)`.  Thus `L` is hyperlinear and nonsofic.  End proof.

## 3. Relation to the BDL finite-normal theorem

Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky, *On L1-approximation of groups*,
[arXiv:2508.17392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17392), Proposition 1.6,
proves

`F finite normal E`, `E/F hyperlinear`, `E MF`
`                    ==> E hyperlinear`.              `(FRC10)`

Their quotient hypothesis is needed to separate elements outside `F` and
obtain hyperlinearity of the **whole** group `E`.

The theorem above asks for a different conclusion.  When
`F <= Rad_sof(E)`, it is enough to obtain one hyperlinear **image** retaining
one element of `F`.  The renormalized complementary fixed-space corner does
that without knowing anything about `E/F`.  Thus `(FRC10)` and finite-radical
corner extraction are complementary:

* BDL gives the stronger conclusion `E` hyperlinear, under quotient
  hyperlinearity;
* `(FRC1)--(FRC9)` gives exactly the FALSE-side quotient needed here, under
  the radical hypothesis and without quotient hyperlinearity.

For the cyclic shear

`1 -> <z> ~= C_p -> J -> K -> 1`,
`1 != z in Rad_sof(J)`,                                  `(FRC11)`

this means that proving **only** operator-norm MF of `J` would finish the
main problem.  BDL's extra hypothesis that `K` is hyperlinear is unnecessary
for that finish.  What remains open is precisely MF of `J`, equivalently the
nonzero projective sector in `docs/FALSE_FINITE_KERNEL_AUDIT.md`; the corner
argument does not construct that sector.

## 4. Structural calibration

Any finite normal subgroup `F` satisfying `(FRC2)` is necessarily abelian.
Indeed conjugation gives a homomorphism `E->Aut(F)` to a finite, hence sofic,
group.  It kills `Rad_sof(E)`, so its restriction to `F` is trivial; but that
restriction is the inner-automorphism action of `F` on itself.

Moreover the extension `1->F->E->E/F->1` cannot split.  If
`E=F semidirect_alpha Q`, then

`(f,q) |-> (f,alpha(q))`

maps `E` to the finite group `F semidirect alpha(Q)` and is injective on
`F`, contradicting `F<=Rad_sof(E)`.  In the prime binary case, every normal
involution is central because `Aut(C_2)=1`.

Thus the cyclic shear has the correct unavoidable algebraic shape: a finite
abelian, nonsplit radical kernel.  The unresolved projective sector is not
an artifact of a poor presentation; it is the analytic content forced by
that nonsplitting.
