# The coinduced parity model for the one-involution sector

Date: 2026-08-11

The one-involution criterion in `FALSE_MACKEY_SPLIT_COCYCLE.md` is
algebraically consistent in a particularly canonical way.  The parity action
of the cyclic subgroup generated by the radical word coinduces to a compact
probability action of the whole group.  Its crossed product realizes the
antipodal involution and all commuting translates exactly.

This does **not** prove Connes embeddability or the existence of the required
matrix models.  The crossed product contains the original group von Neumann
algebra, so proving it Connes embeddable is already at least as hard as making
the radical word visible in a tracial matrix ultraproduct.  The point of the
construction is to identify one explicit finite-trace algebra, rather than an
unspecified infinite-dimensional representation, at which the remaining
analytic question can be aimed.

## 1. Signed coinduction

Let `G` be countable, let `s in G` have infinite order, and put

`C=<s> isomorphic Z`, `Y=G/C`.                              `(CPM1)`

Choose a section `tau:Y->G` with `tau(C)=1`.  Its Schreier cocycle is

`kappa(g,y)=tau(gy)^(-1) g tau(y) in C`.                  `(CPM2)`

Write

`chi(s^m)=(-1)^m`.                                        `(CPM3)`

On the compact probability space

`X={-1,1}^Y`                                              `(CPM4)`

with product Haar measure, define

`(g dot x)_(gy)=chi(kappa(g,y)) x_y`.                     `(CPM5)`

The Schreier identity

`kappa(gh,y)=kappa(g,hy)kappa(h,y)`                       `(CPM6)`

shows that `(CPM5)` is an action.  Every `g` acts by a coordinate
permutation followed by coordinatewise sign changes, so it preserves product
measure.

Let `o=C in Y`.  Since `kappa(s,o)=s`, one has

`(s dot x)_o=-x_o`.                                       `(CPM7)`

Thus this is precisely the coinduction from `C` to `G` of the two-point
probability action on which `s` acts by the flip.

## 2. The exact antipodal model

Form the finite tracial crossed product

`M=L_infinity(X) crossed G`,                              `(CPM8)`

and denote its canonical group unitaries by `u_g`.  Put

`T(x)=x_o`.                                               `(CPM9)`

Then `T=T^*=T^(-1)`.  With either standard crossed-product convention,
equation `(CPM7)` gives

`u_s T u_s^*=-T`.                                        `(CPM10)`

Moreover

`[u_g T u_g^*,u_h T u_h^*]=0`                            `(CPM11)`

for all `g,h in G`, because all the displayed operators belong to the
abelian algebra `L_infinity(X)`.

**Theorem 1 (exact coinduced parity model).**  Equations
`(CPM10)--(CPM11)` give an exact infinite-dimensional solution of the
one-involution relations for every infinite-order element `s` of every
countable group `G`.

The conjugates of `T` generate `L_infinity(X)`: up to the scalar sign in
`(CPM5)`, the conjugate indexed by `g` is the coordinate function at `gC`,
and these coordinate functions generate the product sigma-algebra.  Hence

`M=W^*(u_g (g in G),T)`.                                 `(CPM12)`

In particular, the exact model is not obtained by hiding the involution in
an irrelevant direct summand.

## 3. Relation to the universal central extension

Use the section and cocycle from `(CPM2)`.  Set

`T_y=u_(tau(y)) T u_(tau(y))^*`.                         `(CPM13)`

Equations `(CPM2)--(CPM3)` and `(CPM10)` give the exact signed covariance

`u_g T_y u_g^*=chi(kappa(g,y)) T_(gy)`.                `(CPM14)`

The `T_y` commute by `(CPM11)`.  Therefore, for a finite lamp
`b in directSum_Y C_2`, the operators

`W(b,g)=product_y T_y^(b_y) u_g`                       `(CPM15)`

have exactly the multiplier `(MSC20am)` of
`FALSE_MACKEY_SPLIT_COCYCLE.md`.  Lifting `(CPM15)` through the central
extension `E_univ->Q` gives an exact unitary representation in `M` with

`z_univ |-> -1`.                                       `(CPM16)`

Thus the negative central sector is present in a finite tracial von Neumann
algebra before any approximation is attempted.  The missing issue is solely
whether it can be realized in a Connes-embeddable finite algebra (or directly
by normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt matrix microstates).

## 4. Exact analytic boundary

The canonical trace on `(CPM8)` satisfies

`tau(f u_g)=0` for `g!=1`, `tau(f)=integral_X f`.       `(CPM17)`

Consequently `L(G)` embeds trace-preservingly in `M`, and

`||u_s-1||_2=sqrt(2)`.                                  `(CPM18)`

If `1!=s in Rad_sof(G)` and `M` were Connes embeddable, the restriction of
an embedding of `M` to the canonical copy of `G` would produce a hyperlinear
image retaining `s`.  That image could not be sofic.  Hence:

**Corollary 2.**  Connes embeddability of the explicit coinduced crossed
product `(CPM8)` answers negatively the question whether every hyperlinear
group is sofic.

There is an operator-norm version which is sometimes more convenient.  Let

`A_(G,s)=C^*(C(X),u_g:g in G) subset M`.               `(CPM19)`

If `A_(G,s)` is MF, apply any norm-corona embedding and normalized matrix
ultratraces to its defining relations.  The antipodal identity `(CPM10)`
forces the image of `s` to have trace tending to zero, by the estimate in
`FALSE_ONE_INVOLUTION_INSTABILITY.md`; thus it survives in the tracial
ultraproduct.  Therefore:

**Corollary 3 (single concrete MF target).**  If

`1!=s in Rad_sof(G)` and `A_(G,s)` is MF,                `(CPM20)`

then a hyperlinear nonsofic group exists.

For the explicit FALSE candidate, take the banked weak-MF symmetric double
`H`, a nontrivial infinite-order `r_0 in Rad_sof(H)`, and either

`(G,s)=(H,r_0)`

or the free-stabilized pair used in `FALSE_MACKEY_SPLIT_COCYCLE.md`.  The
first choice is analytically smaller.  The free stabilization is useful for
the algebraic central-extension presentation, but it supplies no additional
trace mass.

## 5. What the construction does and does not remove

The model proves that there is no algebraic, parity, or invariant-measure
obstruction to the one-involution relations.  In particular one should not
try to manufacture the infinite-dimensional negative sector again by
quasi-regular coloring or Mackey decomposition: `(CPM4)--(CPM16)` already
gives it canonically.

It does not provide finite-dimensional approximations.  Indeed `(CPM12)`
contains the canonical group unitaries, and the trace-preserving inclusion

`L(G) subset M`                                           `(CPM21)`

shows that Connes embeddability of the whole crossed product implies
hyperlinearity of `G`.  Also, realizing the coordinate algebra in matrices
requires the approximating group unitaries to asymptotically normalize one
common abelian algebra.  That is the same common-Cartan/phase-completion
boundary appearing on the TRUE side, specialized to one signed coordinate
orbit.  The construction localizes the gate; it does not evade it.

## 6. For the symmetric double, both vertex extensions are residually finite

The universal central extension becomes especially concrete before the free
stabilization used in the Mackey note.  Let

`D=G_0 *_Gamma G_0`                                      `(CPM22)`

be the binary Kun--Thom symmetric double.  The two copies of `G_0` are
residually finite.  Choose an infinite-order hyperbolic element

`1!=s in Rad_sof(D)`                                      `(CPM23)`

from the complete radical calculation, and define

`R=F_2[D]`, `I=ker(epsilon:R->F_2)`,
`A_s=R/I(1+s)`, `E_s=A_s semidirect D`.                `(CPM24)`

The proof of Theorem 6 in `FALSE_MACKEY_SPLIT_COCYCLE.md` applies without
the free-stabilized notation.  If `v` is the class of `1` and

`z=(1+s)v`,                                              `(CPM25)`

then

`0 -> F_2 z -> A_s -> F_2[D/<s>] ->0`                  `(CPM26)`

is exact, `z` is `D`-fixed, and

`1!=z=[v,s] in Rad_sof(E_s)`.                           `(CPM27)`

In particular `E_s` is nonsofic and the finite-radical corner theorem would
finish the FALSE problem as soon as `E_s` were known to be operator MF.

There is a sharper vertex calculation.  Put `Y=D/<s>`.  Either vertex copy
`G_i` acts freely on `Y`.  Indeed, a stabilizer element belongs to

`G_i intersect d<s>d^(-1)`.                            `(CPM28)`

Every element of `G_i` is elliptic in the Bass--Serre tree of `(CPM22)`,
whereas every nonidentity element of `d<s>d^(-1)` is hyperbolic.  Therefore
the intersection in `(CPM28)` is trivial.

Use the basis from Theorem 7 of the Mackey note,

`{z} union {e_y:y in Y}`,

for which the action has the form

`g e_y=e_(gy)+epsilon(g,y)z`, `epsilon(g,y) in F_2`. `(CPM29)`

On every free `G_i`-orbit choose a basepoint `y_0`.  Each point is uniquely
`g y_0`; replace

`e_(g y_0)` by `f_(g y_0)=g e_(y_0)`.                 `(CPM30)`

This is a triangular change of basis by multiples of `z`, and now

`h f_y=f_(hy)` for `h in G_i`.                         `(CPM31)`

Consequently, as an `F_2[G_i]`-module,

`A_s = F_2 z directSum F_2[Y]`,                       `(CPM32)`

where `Y` is a disjoint union of regular `G_i`-sets.  Hence the vertex
preimage

`J_i=A_s semidirect G_i`                               `(CPM33)`

is isomorphic to

`C_2 times ((directSum_Y C_2) semidirect G_i)`,        `(CPM34)`

a direct product with a possibly many-orbit regular binary wreath product.

**Lemma 4.**  If a group `K` is residually finite and `Y` is a disjoint
union of regular `K`-sets, then

`(directSum_Y C_2) semidirect K`                       `(CPM35)`

is residually finite.

**Proof.**  An element with nonidentity `K`-coordinate survives in a finite
quotient of `K`.  For a nonzero finite lamp `b`, choose one nonzero
coordinate in one regular orbit.  Residual finiteness supplies a finite
quotient `K->Q` separating that coordinate from the finitely many other
coordinates of `b` in the same orbit.  Project away all other orbit labels
and push the selected lamp function forward to `Q`.  Its chosen coefficient
does not cancel, so the element survives in the finite group

`(directSum_Q C_2) semidirect Q`.

This separates every nonidentity element.  End proof.

Equations `(CPM34)--(CPM35)` prove that both `J_1` and `J_2` are residually
finite.  Finally semidirect products distribute over this amalgam:

`E_s = J_1 *_(A_s semidirect Gamma) J_2`.              `(CPM36)`

Thus the explicit central-radical candidate is an amalgam of **two
residually finite groups**.  All failure of finite approximation is in the
global edge carry `(CPM26)`, not in either vertex.

This still does not make `(CPM36)` MF.  Shulman's automatic theorem applies
to an untwisted symmetric double.  After identifying the two vertex groups
with one another by the flip of `(CPM22)`, the edge identification acts
nontrivially on `A_s`; that twist is exactly the nonsplit central line in
`(CPM26)`.  Her general amalgamation criterion therefore leaves one precise
compatibility question: construct norm-corona embeddings of the two
residually finite vertex groups which agree on
`A_s semidirect Gamma` and retain `z`.  Every compatible exact finite
quotient kills `z` by `(CPM27)`, so a successful model must again use a
genuinely asymptotic carry sector.
