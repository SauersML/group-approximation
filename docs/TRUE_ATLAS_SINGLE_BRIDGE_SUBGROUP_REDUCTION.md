# The atlas single-bridge question is a subgroup-intersection problem

Date: 2026-08-13

## 1. Exact reformulation

Put the two binary-Leavitt `A8` charts in the fixed classical alignment and
write

```text
A=A8_chart1,
B=A8_chart2,
H=B intersect aligned GL_3(2).                         (SBR1)
```

A relation word with exactly one transverse second-chart syllable has the
form

```text
x b y=1,       x,y in <A,H>,       b in B minus H.     (SBR2)
```

Such a relation exists if and only if

```text
<A,H> intersect B  is strictly larger than H.          (SBR3)
```

Indeed `(SBR2)` gives `b=x^(-1)y^(-1) in <A,H>`.  Conversely, if
`b in <A,H> intersect (B minus H)`, any word in the generators of `A` and
`H` representing `b^(-1)`, followed by the one second-chart syllable `b`,
is a relation of the required form.

This is stronger than searching collision-tree spanning words: it asks
directly whether the subgroup generated by the first chart and the aligned
rank-three part of the second chart ever reaches a transverse element of the
second chart.

## 2. Exact finite search

`experiments/atlas_single_bridge_subgroup_search.py` performs a breadth-first
search in `<A,H>` using the six standard generators of `A` and four
transvections generating `H`.  Leavitt elements are represented exactly over
`F_2`; no floating-point arithmetic is used.

For each homogeneous gauge degree the script expands terms to one common
right depth and repeatedly removes a trailing tensor-identity factor.  This
gives a canonical sparse representative in the direct system

```text
M_(2^(n+d),2^n)(F_2) -> M_(2^(n+d+1),2^(n+1))(F_2),
x |-> x tensor I_2.                                  (SBR4)
```

As an independent validation, this normal form was compared with the older
fixed-depth equality partition on the entire radius-five two-chart ball:

```text
states                              109399
reference image classes              36971
canonical image classes              36971
maximum classes in either fiber           1.           (SBR5)
```

Thus the two equality tests induce exactly the same partition on all tested
states.  The archived validation artifact is
`experiments/atlas-single-bridge-normal-form-validation-r5.json`.

The corrected search completed radius seven in `<A,H>`:

```text
sphere sizes  1, 10, 68, 425, 2580, 15198, 87646, 499094
total states  605022
elements of B outside H tested as targets  20160-168
intersection hit outside H  none.                    (SBR6)
```

Here `|B|=|A8|=20160` and `|H|=168`.  The complete result is stored in
`experiments/atlas-single-bridge-subgroup-r7.json`.  It was computed on MSI
with one CPU core; peak resident memory was about `2.9 GiB`.

## 3. An explicit bridge outside the searched ball

The search proves that no word of length at most seven in the chosen
generators of `<A,H>` represents a transverse second-chart element.  There
is nevertheless a short conceptual construction outside that ball.

In root notation, the first chart contains

```text
x_23(1), x_32(1), x_12(e), x_12(f), x_21(E), x_21(F), (SBR7)
```

while the aligned subgroup `H` of the second chart contains

```text
x_13(1), x_31(1).                                      (SBR8)
```

The Steinberg commutator identities give

```text
x_12(1)=[x_13(1),x_32(1)],
x_21(1)=[x_23(1),x_31(1)].                             (SBR9)
```

Hence `<A,H>` contains the full scalar `SL_3(F_2)`.  Put

```text
w_12=x_12(1)x_21(1)x_12(1).                           (SBR10)
```

This is the scalar Weyl element interchanging coordinates `1` and `2`.
Characteristic two removes the usual minus sign, so

```text
w_12 x_12(f) w_12^(-1)=x_21(f).                       (SBR11)
```

The left root in `(SBR11)` lies in the first chart and the right root is a
transverse element of the second chart.  Expanding `(SBR9)--(SBR11)` in the
chosen chart generators gives a reduced length-19 relation with exactly one
transverse second-chart syllable.  The exact replay is archived in
`experiments/atlas-explicit-single-bridge.json`, generated by
`experiments/atlas_explicit_single_bridge.py`.

More generally scalar permutation matrices transport each of `e,f,E,F` to
every ordered root.  The roots in `(SBR7)`, together with the scalar group,
therefore contain all thirty atlas root generators.  Consequently

```text
<A,H>=Q,       and in particular <A,H> intersect B=B. (SBR12)
```

Thus `(SBR3)` is not merely strict: its left side is the entire second
chart.  The radius-seven computation remains useful as a shortest-word
bound, but it is not an all-radius obstruction.

The core Weyl transport identity is formalized over every characteristic-two
ring in `GroupApproximation/Leavitt/ElementaryWeylBridge.lean`.

## 4. Revised hyperlinear endpoint

After finite-coordinate exactification of the two chart factors, write the
second chart as `U rho(B) U^*` relative to the first.  If the boundary packet
forces `U` to commute approximately with `rho(H)`, then `(SBR11)` forces it
to commute approximately with one transverse root.  Since `H` and that root
generate `B`, finite-group stability upgrades this to approximate full
`A8` alignment.

The already archived interior word 19243 is a Leavitt relation but has
normalized-HS defect `sqrt(2)` in the fully aligned classical chart.  Thus
the remaining atlas implication has the exact form

```text
finite-coordinate H-alignment
  -> full A8 alignment by the length-19 bridge
  -> contradiction from word 19243.                  (SBR13)
```

The new bridge removes the need to extract a quadratic Pauli block *after*
`H`-alignment.  It does not prove `H`-alignment itself.  That is still the
matrix-specific commutant/normalizer recovery gate: the left regular
representation of `Q` realizes the relations exactly while its two copies
of `H` remain genuinely distinct.

There is a completely explicit dimension-free estimate behind `(SBR13)`.
Let `rho` be a regular multiple of the first `A8` chart and, after absorbing
the fixed classical alignment, write the second chart as

```text
g |-> U rho(g) U*.                                    (SBR14)
```

Use the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm and put

```text
delta_H = max_(h in H) ||U rho(h)-rho(h)U||_2,
epsilon_br = defect of the length-19 bridge relation. (SBR15)
```

Nine of the ten second-chart syllables in the bridge lie in `H`; the last
one is the transverse root `b=x_21(f)`.  Replacing those nine syllables one
at a time and using bi-invariance and the triangle inequality gives

```text
||U rho(b)-rho(b)U||_2 <= epsilon_br+9 delta_H.        (SBR16)
```

Let `D` be the diameter of `A8` for the generating set
`H union {b,b^-1}`.  The equality `<H,b>=A8` makes `D` finite, and the crude
spanning-tree estimate gives `D<=|A8|-1=20159`.  Telescoping a shortest word
for each `g` gives

```text
max_(g in A8) ||U rho(g)-rho(g)U||_2
  <= D (epsilon_br+9 delta_H).                         (SBR17)
```

Collision word 19243 has length eight, so replacing all its second-chart
syllables costs at most `8D(epsilon_br+9delta_H)`.  Passing to its commutator
with the fixed first-chart transvection costs at most twice that amount.  If
`epsilon_int` is the defect of this centrality relator, its classically
aligned value is a nonidentity group element in a regular `A8`
representation and hence has normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance `sqrt(2)`
from the identity.  Therefore every such finite-coordinate model obeys

```text
sqrt(2) <= epsilon_int
           + 16 D (epsilon_br+9 delta_H).              (SBR18)
```

Consequently an `o(1)` atlas microstate sequence cannot have
`delta_H=o(1)`.  No qualitative stability theorem is needed after the
`H`-commutant has been recovered: `(SBR18)` is the whole remaining
contradiction.  Conversely, `(SBR18)` does not estimate `delta_H` from the
atlas relators.  Supplying that estimate is exactly the unresolved
matrix-coordinate recovery theorem, and none of the signed derivative
certificates does so: they linearize at the already aligned fold, while the
exact Leavitt model is a nonlinear branch.

## 5. Search limitation

Radius eight already grows past several million candidate transitions, so
continuing blind breadth-first enumeration has poor expected value.  The
algebraic identity `(SBR11)` settles existence without it.  Future search is
useful only for minimizing the length-19 certificate or for finding a bridge
whose boundary-alignment hypotheses are weaker.
