# The complete sofic radical of the augmentation quotient

This note continues the calculation of the Kun--Thom symmetric double

\[
 D=G*_{\Gamma}G.
\]

Let \(r:D\to G\) be the fold map, \(F=\ker r\), and put

\[
 A=F_{\mathrm{ab}}\cong I_{G/\Gamma}
   =\ker\bigl(\mathbb Z[G/\Gamma]\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb Z\bigr).
\]

The quotient

\[
 P:=D/[F,F]\cong A\rtimes G                              \tag{1}
\]

is nonsofic.  We compute its entire sofic radical.

For the explicit Laurent Kun--Thom pair, write

\[
 N=\langle\!\langle\Gamma\rangle\!\rangle_G
   =\operatorname{EL}_r\bigl(
       \mathbb F_2[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_d^{\pm1}]
     \bigr)
 \triangleleft G,
 \qquad
 Q=G/N\cong\operatorname{SL}_d(\mathbb Z).
\]

The projection of transitive \(G\)-sets

\[
 p:G/\Gamma\longrightarrow G/N
\]

induces a surjective \(\mathbb ZG\)-module map

\[
 p_*:I_{G/\Gamma}\longrightarrow I_Q.                  \tag{2}
\]

Put \(M=\ker p_*\).

**Theorem.**

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(P)=M.
 }                                                       \tag{3}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 P/M\cong I_Q\rtimes G                                  \tag{4}
\]

is residually finite.

**Proof.**  The kernel of the map
\(\mathbb Z[G/\Gamma]\to\mathbb Z[Q]\) is generated by the differences

\[
 \delta_{gn\Gamma}-\delta_{g\Gamma}
 =g(\delta_{n\Gamma}-\delta_\Gamma),
 \qquad g\in G,\ n\in N.                               \tag{5}
\]

Every such difference has augmentation zero, so the same family generates
\(M\) inside \(I_{G/\Gamma}\).

Let \(j:D\to P\) be the quotient map.  Under the Bass--Serre homology
identification,

\[
 j\bigl(i_2(n)i_1(n)^{-1}\bigr)
 =\delta_{n\Gamma}-\delta_\Gamma.                       \tag{6}
\]

The complete-radical theorem for the double says

\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(D)
 =\left\langle\!\left\langle
    i_2(n)i_1(n)^{-1}:n\in N
  \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_D.                    \tag{7}
\]

Equations (5)--(7) show that

\[
 j(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(D))=M.
\]

If \(\phi:P\to S\) has sofic target, then
\(\phi\circ j:D\to S\) kills the left side of (7), and hence \(\phi\)
kills \(M\).  Thus

\[
 M\subseteq\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(P).        \tag{8}
\]

It remains to produce a residually finite quotient with kernel \(M\).
The action of \(G\) on \(I_Q\) factors through the residually finite group
\(Q\).  Therefore the map

\[
 I_Q\rtimes G\longrightarrow
 G\times(I_Q\rtimes Q),
 \qquad
 (a,g)\longmapsto\bigl(g,(a,gN)\bigr),                 \tag{9}
\]

is an injective homomorphism.  The group \(I_Q\rtimes Q\) is a subgroup of
the restricted wreath product \(\mathbb Z\wr Q\).  By Gruenberg's residual
finiteness theorem for wreath products, \(\mathbb Z\wr Q\) is residually
finite because \(\mathbb Z\) is abelian and \(Q\) is residually finite.
Hence the target of (9), and therefore \(P/M\), is residually finite.

The quotient map \(P\to P/M\) now gives the reverse inclusion in (8),
proving (3). \(\square\)

## Consequence for hyperlinear versus sofic

The obstruction in the abelian-by-residually-finite group \(P\) is not an
unspecified failure of approximation.  It is exactly the free abelian
module

\[
 M=\ker\bigl(I_{G/\Gamma}\to I_{G/N}\bigr),             \tag{10}
\]

generated by the Laurent-versus-polynomial differences
\(\delta_{n\Gamma}-\delta_\Gamma\), \(n\in N\).

Consequently, any Connes-embeddable representation of \(P\) that is
nontrivial on one element of \(M\) has a hyperlinear nonsofic image.  The
remaining negative-side problem is therefore a fully linear trace-separation
problem: retain one nonzero vector in (10) in a tracial matrix ultraproduct.
