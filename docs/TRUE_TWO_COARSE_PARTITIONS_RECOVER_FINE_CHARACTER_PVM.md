# Two jointly separating coarse partitions recover the fine character PVM

Date: 2026-08-14

## 1. Outcome

The proper-extension fine-fiber gate has a dimension-free two-partition
endpoint. Let `(Q_x)_(x in X)` be a finite PVM and let

```text
pi_0:X->Y_0,             pi_1:X->Y_1                (TCP1)
```

be jointly injective. Define the two coarse PVMs

```text
R_i(y)=sum_(pi_i(x)=y) Q_x.                          (TCP2)
```

For every self-adjoint matrix `T`, put

```text
D_Q(T)=sum_x ||[T,Q_x]||_2^2,
D_i(T)=sum_y ||[T,R_i(y)]||_2^2.                    (TCP3)
```

Then

```text
boxed: D_Q(T) <= D_0(T)+D_1(T).                     (TCP4)
```

There is no factor depending on the number or size of the character fibers.
Thus two vanishing coarse commutator energies imply vanishing fine-character
variance directly.

## 2. Exact recovery of the fine atoms

All coarse projections are sums of the same fine PVM, so the two coarse
families commute. Joint injectivity gives

```text
Q_x=R_0(pi_0(x)) R_1(pi_1(x)).                      (TCP5)
```

Indeed, the product on the right is the sum of `Q_z` over the simultaneous
fiber

```text
{z: pi_0(z)=pi_0(x), pi_1(z)=pi_1(x)},              (TCP6)
```

which contains only `x`. Hence the algebra generated by the two coarse PVMs
is exactly the fine diagonal algebra generated by `(Q_x)`.

Let

```text
E_Q(T)=sum_x Q_x T Q_x,
E_i(T)=sum_y R_i(y) T R_i(y).                        (TCP7)
```

These are Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal projections onto the corresponding
block-diagonal operator spaces. Equation `(TCP5)` gives

```text
E_Q=E_0 E_1=E_1 E_0.                                (TCP8)
```

## 3. Constant-one energy estimate

Decompose

```text
T-E_Q(T)
 =[T-E_0(T)] + E_0[T-E_1(T)].                       (TCP9)
```

The first summand is orthogonal to the range of `E_0`, while the second lies
in that range. Therefore

```text
||T-E_Q(T)||_2^2
 =||T-E_0(T)||_2^2
  +||E_0(T-E_1(T))||_2^2
 <=||T-E_0(T)||_2^2+||T-E_1(T)||_2^2.              (TCP10)
```

For a PVM `(S_j)` and self-adjoint `T`, direct block expansion gives

```text
sum_j ||[T,S_j]||_2^2
 =2||T-sum_j S_j T S_j||_2^2.                       (TCP11)
```

Substituting `(TCP11)` into `(TCP10)` proves `(TCP4)`.

```text

```

Consequently the additive coefficient module splits as

```text

```

The inverse sends `a` to `(t_0a,t_1a)`, since

```text
a=s_0(t_0a)+s_1(t_1a),                              (TCP14)
```

and the cross relations in `(TCP12)` prove uniqueness. A character of a
finite target coefficient window is therefore uniquely determined by its
restrictions to the two branch images. In the notation above, the two
restriction maps are jointly injective; in a branch-compatible window they
are jointly bijective onto their image.

Hence the fine target-character PVM is generated exactly by the two branch
restriction PVMs, and `(TCP4)` becomes

```text
fine extension-character energy
 <= branch-0 coarse energy + branch-1 coarse energy. (TCP15)
```
