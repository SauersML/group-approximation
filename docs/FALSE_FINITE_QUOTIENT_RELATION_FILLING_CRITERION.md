## 1. Outcome

identifies `M_Q tensor R` with `H_1(F;R)=R^d`; relator and marked classes map

is finite for a sofic-radical word.  See

The cocycle search in
`FALSE_CENTRAL_RELATOR_COCYCLE_LINEARIZATION.md` has a smaller intrinsic
form.  Let

`P=<s_1,...,s_d | r_1,...,r_ell>`                    `(FRF1)`

and let `w` be a nonidentity element of `Rad_sof(P)`.  For a finite quotient

`sigma:F_d->Q`                                        `(FRF2)`

which factors through `P`, put

`R_Q=ker(sigma)`,
`M_Q=R_Q/[F_d,R_Q]`.                                  `(FRF3)`

The classes of the defining relators and of the marked word lie in `M_Q`.
Moreover,

`[w] in <[r_1],...,[r_ell]>_Z <=M_Q`.                `(FRF4)`

Define the real marked filling norm

`D_Q(w;R)`
` =min {sum_j |c_j| :`
`          [w]=sum_j c_j[r_j] in M_Q tensor R}.`      `(FRF5)`

If there is a sequence of finite quotients `Q_n` for which

`D_(Q_n)(w;R)->infinity`,                             `(FRF6)`

then a hyperlinear nonsofic group exists.

The proof is constructive.  The dual linear program produces rational
characters of `M_(Q_n)`.  Their associated finite cyclic central extensions
make the marked word act as `-1`, while every defining relator has phase
error at most

`pi/D_(Q_n)(w;R)`.                                   `(FRF7)`

Thus the decisive finite computation is a marked `l_1` filling problem in
a finite-quotient relation module, not a unitary optimization and not a
full `H^2` enumeration.

## 2. Lifted central extensions are relation-module characters

Let `F=F_d` and write `A=Z/m`.  A lift-equipped central extension consists
of

`1->A->E->Q->1`                                      `(FRF8)`

together with selected lifts `u_i in E` of `sigma(s_i)`.

The lifts define a homomorphism

`u:F->E`.                                             `(FRF9)`

For `x in R_Q`, its image lies in the central subgroup `A`.  Hence

`chi_u:R_Q->A`, `chi_u(x)=u(x)`                       `(FRF10)`

is a homomorphism invariant under conjugation by `F`.  It therefore factors
through

`chi_u:M_Q->A`.                                       `(FRF11)`

Conversely, every character `(FRF11)` gives a finite central extension

`E_chi=(A times F)/N_chi`,                            `(FRF12)`

where `N_chi` is the normal graph

`N_chi={(-chi(x),x):x in R_Q}`.                       `(FRF13)`

Conjugation invariance makes `(FRF13)` normal.  The image of `A times {1}`
is still isomorphic to `A`, the quotient is `Q`, and the images of
`(0,s_i)` are the selected lifts.  The two constructions are inverse.

This proves:

**Theorem 1 (relation-module parametrization).**  Lift-equipped central
extensions of the fixed quotient `(FRF2)` by `Z/m` are naturally equivalent
to

`Hom(M_Q,Z/m)`.                                       `(FRF14)`

Under `(FRF14)`, the central exponents of the defining relators and the
marked word are

`chi([r_1]),...,chi([r_ell]),chi([w])`.               `(FRF15)`

The cocycle and generator-phase coordinates in `(CRC1)` are merely one
coordinate presentation of `(FRF14)`.

## 3. Why the marked class is integrally fillable

Let

`L_Q=<[r_1],...,[r_ell]>_Z <=M_Q`.                   `(FRF16)`

Suppose `[w]` were not in `L_Q`.  The finitely generated abelian group
`M_Q/L_Q` would contain a nonzero class represented by `[w]`.  Finitely
generated abelian groups are residually finite, so some homomorphism

`barChi:M_Q/L_Q->Z/m`                                 `(FRF17)`

would detect it.  Pull back `(FRF17)` to `M_Q` and use Theorem 1.  Every
defining relator then evaluates to one in the finite group `E_chi`, while
`w` does not.  The selected lifts would define a homomorphism

`P->E_chi`                                            `(FRF18)`

to a finite group retaining `w`.  This contradicts
`w in Rad_sof(P)`.  Therefore `(FRF4)` holds.

This argument strengthens `(CRS18)`: for each fixed finite quotient, the
marked relation class is an actual integral linear combination of the
defining-relator classes, not merely a member of the cyclic subgroup
generated by their evaluated curvatures.

Define also the integral filling norm

`C_Q(w;R)=min {sum_j |c_j| :`
`                 [w]=sum_j c_j[r_j] in M_Q}.`        `(FRF19)`

It is finite by `(FRF4)`, and

`D_Q(w;R)<=C_Q(w;R)`.                                `(FRF20)`

For every finite character `chi:M_Q->Z/m`, circular distance gives the
universal bound

`|chi([w])|_m`
` <=C_Q(w;R) max_j |chi([r_j])|_m`.                  `(FRF21)`

Hence any central-relator certificate with a fixed positive witness phase
requires `C_Q->infinity`.  A bounded family of integral fillings is a dual
no-go certificate.

## 4. LP duality constructs the separating characters

Work in the finite-dimensional real vector space

`V_Q=M_Q tensor R`.                                   `(FRF22)`

Let `T:R^ell->V_Q` send the `j`-th standard basis vector to `[r_j]`.
Equation `(FRF5)` is the quotient `l_1` norm of `[w]` under `T`.
Finite-dimensional linear-programming duality gives

`D_Q(w;R)`
` =max {lambda([w]) : lambda in V_Q^*,`
`                       |lambda([r_j])|<=1 for all j}.` `(FRF23)`

All data are rational, so a maximizing `lambda` may be chosen rational.
Orient it so

`lambda([w])=D_Q(w;R)`.                              `(FRF24)`

Assume `D_Q>0` and define the rational circle character

`theta_Q(x)=lambda(x)/(2D_Q) mod Z`.                 `(FRF25)`

After clearing denominators, `(FRF25)` is a character

`theta_Q:M_Q->Z/m_Q`                                 `(FRF26)`

for some finite `m_Q`.  Equations `(FRF23)--(FRF25)` give

`theta_Q([w])=1/2 mod Z`,                             `(FRF27)`

`dist(theta_Q([r_j]),Z)<=1/(2D_Q)`.                  `(FRF28)`

Use Theorem 1 to build the finite central extension and use its primitive
central-character representation as in
`FALSE_CENTRAL_RELATOR_SEPARATION_CRITERION.md`.  The marked word acts as
`-1`, so its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance from one is exactly two.
For every relator,

`|exp(2 pi i theta_Q([r_j]))-1|`
` <=2 pi dist(theta_Q([r_j]),Z)`
` <=pi/D_Q`.                                         `(FRF29)`

If `(FRF6)` holds, `(FRF29)` tends to zero.  The central-relator theorem
then produces a hyperlinear image which retains `w` and cannot be sofic.
This proves the outcome.

The free-part criterion does not cover every possible cyclic certificate.
A character may use torsion in `M_Q` which disappears in `(FRF22)`.  Thus
bounded `D_Q` does not by itself rule out the torsion mechanism.  The exact
modular image `(CRC19)--(CRC21)` remains the complete test; `(FRF5)` is a
particularly cheap and strong sufficient test.

## 5. Sparse exact computation of `M_Q`

The relation module can be computed without a `|Q|^3` cocycle matrix.
Choose a Schreier transversal for `R_Q` in `F`.  Reidemeister--Schreier
gives a free basis of `R_Q` with

`1+|Q|(d-1)`                                          `(FRF30)`

generators.  Abelianize, then impose the conjugation coinvariant relations

`q.x-x=0`                                             `(FRF31)`

for a generating set of `Q`.  Schreier rewriting gives the coordinate
vectors of `r_1,...,r_ell,w`.  Hermite or Smith reduction of `(FRF31)`
produces a certified presentation of `M_Q` and its free quotient.

The real criterion is then the small rational LP `(FRF5)` or its dual
`(FRF23)`.  A successful stage should emit:

1. the finite quotient multiplication/presentation and generator labels;
2. the Schreier transversal and basis;
3. the integer coinvariant relation matrix;
4. the reduced coordinates of `[r_j]` and `[w]`;
5. a rational dual vector `lambda` verifying `(FRF23)`; and
6. the exact value or certified lower bound for `D_Q`.

The dual vector itself is already the construction: `(FRF25)--(FRF29)`
turn it into the finite cyclic extension and the required matrix
microstate.  There is no subsequent nonlinear optimization.
