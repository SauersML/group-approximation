# Ash--Rudolph unimodular symbols supply local templates, not bounded absolute packets

Date: 2026-08-11

## Outcome

The Ash--Rudolph modular-symbol theorem is very close in form to the primal
packet target, but it does not by itself prove it.

For a finite-index subgroup `Gamma<SL_n(Z)`, modular symbols in top
cohomological degree are generated by **unimodular symbols**.  A unimodular
symbol is represented by an integral `n`-tuple of determinant `+-1`, hence by
one `SL_n(Z)` translate of a fixed geometric template.  Modulo `Gamma`, there
are only finitely many such symbols.  The Ash--Rudolph continued-fraction
algorithm writes every modular symbol as an integral sum of these templates.

This gives exactly the local bounded-complexity objects suggested by the
level-`121` packet calculation.  The gap is that these symbols live naturally
in the relative/Borel--Serre modular-symbol group.  The projected lattice
`Q_X` in the harmonic systole problem comes from **absolute integral cycles**.
Absolute classes map into the relative group as the kernel of a boundary-at-
infinity map.  Short generators of the ambient relative group do not imply
short generators of that integral kernel.

There is also a second, previously suppressed distinction: the map from
absolute to relative homology need not be injective. Its kernel is supplied
by Borel--Serre boundary homology. At level `121`, the exact
seven-dimensional absolute `H_2` has a five-dimensional boundary kernel and
only a two-dimensional cuspidal image. Thus relative modular symbols cannot
see five of the seven harmonic directions. See
`TRUE_ABSOLUTE_H2_INTERIOR_BOUNDARY_SPLIT.md`.

Thus Ash--Rudolph reduces the **interior part** of the family theorem to a
sharper combinatorial
statement:

> Prove that the kernel of the signed boundary incidence on unimodular
> projective symbols is real-spanned by integral combinations of uniformly
> bounded cellular norm.

For the stronger circle-repair theorem, those combinations must also admit
compatible bounded integral ambient lifts.  This is a genuine arithmetic
closing theorem; it is not contained in modular-symbol reduction.
The full absolute theorem separately needs bounded Borel--Serre boundary
packets and bounded integral/metric gluing between the boundary and cuspidal
filtrands.

## 1. What the modular-symbol theorem says

Let `X=SO(n)\SL_n(R)` and let `Gamma<SL_n(Z)` have finite index.  A basic
modular symbol is represented by

```text
[v_1,...,v_n],                                      (ARS1)
```

where the `v_i` are nonzero rational vectors, subject to alternating,
scaling, degeneracy, and cocycle relations.  After choosing primitive
integral representatives, put

```text
||[v_1,...,v_n]||_det=|det(v_1,...,v_n)|.            (ARS2)
```

The symbol is unimodular when this determinant is one.

Ash--Rudolph prove that the map from unimodular symbols onto top-degree
cohomology is surjective.  Their reduction step chooses an integral reducing
point `w` and applies the cocycle relation so that a determinant-`D` symbol is
replaced by symbols of strictly smaller determinant.  Iteration terminates at
unimodular symbols.

For `n=3`, every unimodular triple is one `SL_3(Z)` translate of the standard
triple.  Therefore, after specializing to a finite projective quotient, each
unimodular symbol has a level-independent geometric description.  Under any
fixed cellular/resolution comparison, its chain norm is bounded independently
of the projective level.

This is a real positive input: one does not need to invent candidate local
packets.  Unimodular symbols are the canonical candidates.

## 2. The absolute-closing gap

Let `Y_Gamma` be the locally symmetric quotient and `bar Y_Gamma` its
Borel--Serre compactification.  The relevant long exact sequence contains

```text
H_2(bar Y_Gamma;Z) --j-->
H_2(bar Y_Gamma,partial bar Y_Gamma;Z) --partial-->
H_1(partial bar Y_Gamma;Z).                          (ARS3)
```

Hence

```text
im(j)=ker(partial).                                  (ARS4)
```

The modular-symbol theorem provides simple generators for the middle
relative object (or, in the dual convention, its top-degree cohomology).
The absolute primal packets must lie in `(ARS4)`.  They are integral
combinations whose boundary faces cancel.

This distinction is also visible in the projective function model.  The
Ash--Grayson--Green relations on functions on `P^2(F_p)` give a compact dual
description of the cuspidal plane, but transporting that plane to the
absolute HAP/cellular cycle lattice requires the explicit comparison and
boundary-closing map identified in
`TRUE_P53_CUSPIDAL_FUNCTION_LATTICE.md`.

## 3. Why local ambient generators are not enough

There is an elementary sharp counterexample to the missing inference.  Let

```text
R_m=Z^m
```

with its orthonormal unit basis, and define

```text
beta_m:R_m->Z^(m-1),
beta_m(x_1,...,x_m)=(x_1-x_2,...,x_(m-1)-x_m).       (ARS5)
```

Every ambient generator has norm one.  Every row of `beta_m` has exactly two
nonzero coefficients, both of absolute value one, and every column occurs in
at most two rows.  Nevertheless,

```text
ker(beta_m)=Z(1,1,...,1),                            (ARS6)
lambda_1(ker(beta_m))=sqrt(m).                       (ARS7)
```

Thus even bounded coefficients, bounded row support, bounded column degree,
and unit ambient packets do not imply bounded integral kernel packets.
Global boundary cancellation may force a long circuit.

The example proves that the Ash--Rudolph theorem cannot close the family
argument through a purely formal locality claim.  Some additional feature of
the projective symbol incidence must be used.

## 4. The exact theorem-shaped target

Let `R_X` be the integral module generated by unimodular projective symbols,
let

```text
beta_X:R_X->E_X                                      (ARS8)
```

be the signed boundary-at-infinity/cellular closing map, and let `K_X` be the
absolute cuspidal image. The interior part of the denominator theorem would
follow from:

```text
K_X tensor R is spanned by k in K_X with ||k||<=A,   (ARS9)
```

for one level-independent `A`.  Indeed, after the fixed comparison with the
selected projective cellular resolution, `(ARS9)` is exactly the bounded
spanning-packet hypothesis of
`TRUE_HARMONIC_SUCCESSIVE_MINIMUM_PACKET_SCREEN.md`.

The stronger integral-section theorem asks for compatible lifts of enough
dual kernel packets with bounded synthesis/Gram norm.  In this language, the
remaining nonlinear arithmetic is not reduction to unimodular symbols; it is
bounded integral solution of the equations imposing `(ARS8)`.

Three possible structural inputs could prove `(ARS9)`:

1. an explicit bounded family of local circuits spanning `ker(beta_X)`;
2. a bounded chain contraction for the signed boundary-symbol complex that
   preserves integrality up to uniformly bounded primary corrections;
3. a decomposition of the kernel into old isometric packets and new local
   packets in projective towers.

Real expansion alone solves the corresponding real correction problem but
does not bound the integral kernel, exactly as the circle-carry analysis
predicts.

For the full absolute `H_2`, `(ARS9)` must be supplemented by a bounded packet
theorem for

```text
B_X=coker(H^2(Ybar_X;Q)->H^2(partial Ybar_X;Q)),               (ARS10)
```

and by bounded integral/metric control of the extension

```text
0 -> B_X -> H_2(X;Q) -> H_cusp^3(X;Q) -> 0.                  (ARS11)
```

Neither follows formally from unimodular-symbol generation.

## 5. Relation to the exact finite screen

The determinant-one Gram--LLL certificates in
`TRUE_PROJECTIVE_PRIMAL_PACKET_FAMILY_SCREEN.md` show that `(ARS9)` succeeds
at every computed nonzero-rank chart except the sufficient-test exception
`53`.  At level `121`, seven new directions admit one packet synthesis Gram
matrix of maximum absolute row sum `8.7642<12`.

The seven directions are now structurally classified: exactly two map to
cuspidal degree-three cohomology and five lie in the boundary kernel of the
absolute-to-relative map. Hence a unimodular-symbol support computation can
explain at most the two-dimensional image; the five-dimensional kernel needs
boundary-stratum packets.

The modular-symbol theorem now explains where a family proof should search:
among bounded combinations of unimodular symbols whose boundary faces cancel.
It also explains why the finite result is not yet formal.  The reduced Gram
basis certifies the existence of short projected packets, but the current
compact export does not identify their unimodular-symbol support or prove a
uniform closing rule.
