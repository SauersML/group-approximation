# The perfect index-two core of the Kun--Thom wreath product

Let \(G\curvearrowright X\) be transitive and put

\[
 A=\bigoplus_X\mathbb F_2,
 \qquad W=A\rtimes G.
\]

For the explicit Kun--Thom example, \(X=G/\Gamma\), the group \(G\) is
perfect, and \(W\) is nonsofic.  Let

\[
 I_X=\ker\left(\varepsilon:A\to\mathbb F_2\right)
\]

be the mod-two augmentation module, and set

\[
 P_2=I_X\rtimes G.                                     \tag{1}
\]

The group \(P_2\) was previously obtained by abelianizing the fold kernel
of the Kun--Thom symmetric double and reducing modulo two.  It is in fact an
intrinsic subgroup of the original commuting wreath product.

## 1. The derived subgroup

**Theorem 1.**  If \(G\) is perfect and acts transitively on \(X\), then

\[
 \boxed{[W,W]=P_2.}                                    \tag{2}
\]

Consequently \([W:P_2]=2\).

**Proof.**  Since \(A\) is abelian,

\[
 [W,W]=[A,G]\rtimes[G,G].                              \tag{3}
\]

The second factor is \(G\).  The subgroup \([A,G]\) is spanned over
\(\mathbb F_2\) by

\[
 g\delta_x-\delta_x=\delta_{gx}+\delta_x.              \tag{4}
\]

Every vector in (4) has even augmentation, so \([A,G]\leq I_X\).  Conversely,
fix \(o\in X\).  Transitivity gives, for every \(x\in X\), an element
\(g_x\in G\) with \(g_xo=x\), and then

\[
 \delta_x+\delta_o=g_x\delta_o-delta_o\in[A,G].       \tag{5}
\]

The vectors in (5) generate \(I_X\).  Thus \([A,G]=I_X\), and (3) proves
(2).  The quotient \(A/I_X\) has order two. \(\square\)

## 2. Perfectness of the core

The perfectness of \(P_2\) is slightly subtler: it requires vanishing of the
coinvariants of \(I_X\), not merely transitivity of \(X\).

**Theorem 2.**  If \(G\) is perfect and acts transitively on \(X\), then

\[
 \boxed{[P_2,P_2]=P_2.}                                \tag{6}
\]

**Proof.**  Again,

\[
 [P_2,P_2]=[I_X,G]\rtimes G.                           \tag{7}
\]

It remains to show \((I_X)_G=0\).  Apply group homology to

\[
 0\longrightarrow I_X\longrightarrow
 \mathbb F_2[X]\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb F_2
 \longrightarrow0.                                    \tag{8}
\]

The relevant part of the long exact sequence is

\[
 H_1(G,\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow (I_X)_G
 \longrightarrow \mathbb F_2[X]_G
 \xrightarrow{\varepsilon_G}(\mathbb F_2)_G
 \longrightarrow0.                                    \tag{9}
\]

Since \(G\) is perfect,

\[
 H_1(G,\mathbb F_2)=G_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb F_2=0.
\]

Transitivity identifies both modules on the right of (9) with
\(\mathbb F_2\), and \(\varepsilon_G\) is the identity under these
identifications.  Exactness forces \((I_X)_G=0\), or equivalently
\([I_X,G]=I_X\).  Equation (7) proves (6). \(\square\)

For the binary Laurent Kun--Thom group,

\[
 G=\operatorname{EL}_r(R)\rtimes\operatorname{SL}_d(\mathbb Z),
 \qquad r,d\geq3,
\]

both factors are perfect.  The elementary factor is perfect by

\[
 e_{ij}(a)=[e_{ik}(a),e_{kj}(1)]
\]

for distinct \(i,j,k\); \(\operatorname{SL}_d(\mathbb Z)) is generated by
elementary matrices and the same commutator identity applies.  Hence the
theorems apply to this explicit pair.

## 3. Approximation consequences

The previous radical calculation proves that \(P_2\) is nonsofic.  Since it
is a subgroup of \(W\), hyperlinearity of \(W\) implies hyperlinearity of
\(P_2\).  Conversely, (2) gives an extension

\[
 1\longrightarrow P_2\longrightarrow W
 \longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\longrightarrow1. \tag{10}
\]

Hyperlinear groups are closed under extensions with amenable quotient, so

\[
 \boxed{W\text{ is hyperlinear}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        P_2\text{ is hyperlinear}.}                    \tag{11}
\]

Thus the main FALSE-side problem is unchanged after passing to the perfect
index-two subgroup.  In particular a solution may be sought entirely inside
the finitely generated perfect nonsofic group \(P_2\).

## 4. A canonical central-cover endpoint

Because \(P_2\) is perfect, it has a universal central extension

\[
 1\longrightarrow H_2(P_2,\mathbb Z)longrightarrow
 \widetilde P_2\longrightarrow P_2\longrightarrow1.   \tag{12}
\]

Central quotients of hyperlinear groups are hyperlinear.  Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \widetilde P_2\text{ hyperlinear}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 P_2\text{ hyperlinear and nonsofic}.
 }                                                       \tag{13}
\]

This turns the earlier projective/Clifford idea into a canonical question:
one may construct microstates for the universal central cover rather than
guessing an auxiliary central extension.  The central kernel is allowed to
survive in the microstates; quotienting it at the end preserves
hyperlinearity.  What (13) does not provide is hyperlinearity of the cover;
that remains the substantive projective model-production problem.
