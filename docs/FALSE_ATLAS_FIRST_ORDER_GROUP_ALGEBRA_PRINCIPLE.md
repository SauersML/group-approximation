# First-order atlas obstructions in the group algebra

Date: 2026-08-12

## General principle

Let (k) be a field, let (G) be a finite group, and let
(sigma:G\to\operatorname{GL}(W)) be a finite-dimensional representation.
Fix (h\in G), and map the two factors of (G*G) to (G) by

\[
 g\longmapsto g,
 \qquad
 g\longmapsto hgh^{-1}.
\]

Suppose a word (w=y_1\cdots y_m\in G*G) maps to (1). Write (p_t\in G)
for its order-zero prefix through (y_t), with (p_0=p_m=1), and define

\[
 d_w=\sum_{t:\,y_t\text{ is in chart }2}(p_{t-1}-p_t)\in k[G]. \tag{GA1}
\]

On (W\otimes_k k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)), deform only the second
chart:

\[
 \rho_{1,X}(g)=\sigma(g),\qquad
 \rho_{2,X}(g)=(1+\varepsilon X)\sigma(hgh^{-1})
                     (1-\varepsilon X).              \tag{GA2}
\]

Let (k[G]) act on \(\operatorname{End}_k(W)\) by conjugation. Direct
differentiation gives the representation-independent identity

\[
 \rho_X(w)=1+\varepsilon(d_w\cdot X).                 \tag{GA3}
\]

Let (mathcal Z) be words required to remain trivial, let (mathcal S)
be survivor words required to commute with the first chart, and let (T)
generate (G). Put

\[
 I=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}k[G]d_z+
   \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\sum_{g\in T}k[G](g-1)d_s.  \tag{GA4}
\]

Then the following implication is functorial in (W):

\[
 d_q\in I
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \left.
 \begin{array}{l}
   \rho_X(z)=1\quad(z\in\mathcal Z),\\
   [\rho_X(s),\sigma(G)]=1\quad(s\in\mathcal S)
 \end{array}
 \right\}
 \Longrightarrow \rho_X(q)=1.                       \tag{GA5}
\]

Indeed, the hypotheses on the right say that every generator of (I)
annihilates (X); left-ideal membership then makes (d_q) annihilate (X),
and `(GA3)` finishes the proof. A certificate in (k[G]) therefore rules
out the proposed first-order phase simultaneously in every representation,
without classifying modules.

In characteristic two, subtraction in `(GA1)` becomes addition. The exact
552-step certificate in
`FALSE_ATLAS_UNIVERSAL_MODULAR_FIRST_ORDER_NO_GO.md` proves `(GA5)` for the
Leavitt atlas over \(\mathbb F_2\), including all nonsemisimple modules and
off-diagonal blocks.

## Characteristic-zero Leavitt-atlas theorem

Now take

\[
 G=A_8\cong GL_4(\mathbb F_2),
 \qquad
 \mathcal S=\{s_{11},s_{30},s_{44},s_{55}\},
\]

use the unique classical inner alignment, and let \(\mathcal Z_5\) be the 24
cyclic representatives of the 178 perfect-overlap-certified zero relations.
For the signed derivatives `(GA1)`, the exact computation proves

\[
 d_{11}\in
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_5}\mathbb Q[A_8]d_z+
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\sum_{g\in T}
       \mathbb Q[A_8](g-1)d_s.                       \tag{GA6}
\]

Consequently, for **every** finite-dimensional complex representation of
(A_8), every inner-aligned square-zero deformation which kills the 24 zero
relations and makes all four survivors commute with the first full chart
also kills the marked (s_{11}) phase. The conclusion allows arbitrary
dimensions, irreducible types, multiplicities, direct sums, and
off-diagonal couplings.

### Why the finite computation proves `(GA6)`

For a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra (A), a target (t\in A) lies
in the left ideal generated by (a_1,\ldots,a_r) exactly when, in every
simple left (A)-module (V),

\[
 \bigcap_i\ker(a_i|_V)\subseteq\ker(t|_V).            \tag{GA7}
\]

The forward implication is immediate. Conversely, if (t\notin Aa_1+
\cdots+Aa_r), the cyclic vector (1+I\) in the semisimple module (A/I)
has a simple component killed by every (a_i) but not by (t).

The computation uses exact rational seminormal matrices. After scalar
extension to \(\mathbb C\), restricting one (S_8) Specht module from each
conjugate-partition pair covers all (A_8)-irreducibles. There are twelve
selected partitions. Ten give one irreducible restriction each, while the
two self-conjugate partitions

\[
 (4,2,1,1),\qquad(3,3,2)
\]

split into two irreducibles each. Thus all fourteen complex irreducible
sectors are covered. In every restriction, adjoining the rows of (d_{11})
to the constraint rows raises the exact rational rank by zero; the same is
true after scalar extension, and on a split restriction it is equivalent to
the assertion on each constituent separately. Thus `(GA7)` gives membership
in the corresponding left ideal of \(\mathbb C[A_8]\). Since membership is a
linear system with rational coefficients, solvability over \(\mathbb C\) is
equivalent to solvability over \(\mathbb Q\), proving `(GA6)`.

## Exact scope

The two universal certificates close characteristic two and characteristic
zero for the following common family:

1. square-zero/first-order deformation;
2. the fixed classical inner order-zero alignment;
3. both full charts carried by one common (A_8)-module; and
4. the 24 certified zeros plus commutant-valued centrality of all four
   survivors.

They do **not** prove unitary local rigidity, rule out singular or nonlinear
branches, cover every odd modular characteristic, classify nonclassical
order-zero alignments, prove the Leavitt witness nonhyperlinear, or decide
hyperlinear versus sofic. A surviving FALSE construction must now use at
least one genuinely different ingredient: nonlinear/higher-order geometry,
a different order-zero holonomy, a non-common-chart representation, or a
modular characteristic outside the two universal theorems.
