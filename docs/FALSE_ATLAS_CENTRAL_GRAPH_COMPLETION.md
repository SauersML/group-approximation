## 2. A general graph-of-groups completion lemma

We isolate the elementary mechanism.

**Lemma 1 (central full-chart completion).**  Let `H` be a finite group with
a central element `z` of order `m`.  For `i=1,...,k`, let `S_i<H` be finite
subgroups satisfying

```text
S_i intersect <z>={1},                                 (CGC7)
```

and suppose `S_i` embeds in a finite group `K_i`.  Then the iterated
amalgam

```text
G=(K_1 x <z>) *_(S_1 x <z>) H
                  *_(S_2 x <z>) (K_2 x <z>)
                  * ... *_(S_k x <z>) (K_k x <z>)      (CGC8)
```

is a finitely generated virtually free group.  All vertex maps are
injective, the displayed copies of `z` agree, and their common image is
central in `G`.

There is a finite quotient `q:G->F` which is injective on every vertex
group.  In particular, `q(z)` still has order `m`, every `K_i` embeds in
`F`, and `q(z)` is central in `F`.

### Proof

Condition `(CGC7)` identifies the subgroup generated by `S_i` and `z` in
`H` with the direct product `S_i x <z>`.  Use this as one edge map and the
given embedding `S_i->K_i`, times the identity on `<z>`, as the other edge
map.  The normal-form theorem for graphs of groups makes every vertex map
in `(CGC8)` injective.  The element `z` is central in each vertex group and
the edge maps identify all its copies, so it is central in the fundamental
group `G`.

A fundamental group of a finite graph of finite groups is finitely
generated and virtually free.  In particular, `G` is residually finite.
The union of the nonidentity elements of its finitely many vertex groups is
finite.  For each such element choose a finite quotient in which it
survives, and take the product of those quotients.  The resulting finite
quotient `q:G->F` is injective on every vertex group.  Centrality and the
order of `z` are preserved.  End proof.

The lemma also preserves any word identity which is visible in `H`.  More
precisely, map the free product `K_1*...*K_k` to `G` through the outer
vertex groups.  A word whose letters all lie in the specified subgroups
`S_i` evaluates exactly as it does under the maps `S_i->H`, because the
edge relations identify those elements.  Passing to `F` preserves that
identity.

## 4. Canonical negative regular sector

Let `lambda_F` be the left regular representation of the finite group `F`
and put

```text
p_-=(1-lambda_F(z_F))/2.                               (CGC13)
```

Centrality of `z_F` makes `p_-` an invariant projection, and its range has
dimension `|F|/2`.  On this range, `z_F` acts as `-I`, proving `(CGC6)` for
the phase word.

For either embedded chart `K_i=A_8`, the restriction to `p_- l2(F)` has

```text

  =(Tr(lambda_F(g))-Tr(lambda_F(z_F g)))/2.            (CGC14)
```

If `g!=1` lies in `K_i`, then both `g` and `z_F g` are nonidentity: the
subgroup `K_i x <z_F>` embeds in `F`.  Both traces on the right of
`(CGC14)` are therefore zero.  At the identity the character has value
`|F|/2`.  Hence

```text

   ~= lambda_(A_8)^( directSum |F|/(2|A_8|) ).          (CGC15)
```

This proves that the two chart representations are not merely honest
extensions of the local `S_4` blocks: they have exactly the regular
multiplicity profile required in the canonical one-unitary criterion.

## 5. Exact boundary

The group `F` above is not asserted to kill the other twenty
perfect-overlap-certified zero classes.  If it did, it would strengthen the
known phase-preserving finite quotient by making the phase central, and
that is precisely the next finite relation question.

What the theorem removes is a collection of artificial side constraints.
Future work on those twenty classes may start with an exact finite-image
model having:

* both complete `A_8` charts embedded;
* regular chart multiplicities;
* the four previously stubborn relations equal to one;

so that the other certified relations tend to one without diluting the

through the cross-chart gluing.
