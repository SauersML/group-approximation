\title{A simple unital C*-algebra that is not $K_1$-injective}
\author{\paperauthor}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We answer Problem LIX of Schafhauser--Tikuisis--White negatively: there is a
unital simple separable C*-algebra $A$ and a unitary $v \in \U(A)$ with
$v \notin \Uz(A)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(v,1) \in \Uz(M_2(A))$, so the
canonical map $\U(A)/\Uz(A) \to K_1(A)$ is not injective.  The algebra is an
inductive limit of homogeneous algebras over products of a sphere with complex
projective spaces, and the obstruction that makes $v$ nontrivial is a
statement about vector bundles which we prove mod~$2$, through Wu classes,
Steenrod squares and a Thom--Gysin argument.  The whole proof is formalised in
Lean~4; we cite the formal carriers rather than reproving what they carry.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{The problem and the answer}
\label{sec:intro}

A unital C*-algebra $A$ is \emph{$K_1$-injective} when the canonical map
\[
  \kappa_A \colon \U(A)/\Uz(A) \longrightarrow K_1(A)
\]
is injective, where $\Uz(A)$ is the connected component of the identity in the
unitary group.  Injectivity of $\kappa_A$ says that a unitary which becomes
trivial after stabilisation was already trivial before it: if
$\operatorname{diag}(v,1,\dots,1)$ can be connected to the identity in some
matrix amplification, then $v$ can be connected to the identity in $A$ itself.
Schafhauser, Tikuisis and White ask, as Problem LIX of their list, whether
every unital simple C*-algebra is $K_1$-injective.  The answer is no.

\begin{mainthm}
\label{thm:main}
There is a unital simple separable C*-algebra $A$ that is not
$K_1$-injective.  Explicitly, there is a unitary $v \in \U(A)$ with
$v \notin \Uz(A)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(v,1) \in \Uz(M_2(A))$.
\leanverified{thm:main}{GroupApproximation.NinetyNineProblems.not\_problemLIX}
\end{mainthm}

Simplicity is meant in the C*-sense that the problem asks for: every closed
two-sided ideal of $A$ is $0$ or $A$.  For a unital C*-algebra this agrees with
simplicity as a ring, but the endpoint is stated in the form that was asked.

\subsection{What is proved, and where}

The result is a theorem of Lean~4, unconditionally: it depends on no hypothesis
and on no axiom beyond the three that Lean's own logic supplies, namely
\textsf{propext}, \textsf{Classical.choice} and \textsf{Quot.sound}.  The
statement quantified over is
\[
  \textsf{ProblemLIX} \;:=\;
  \forall A,\ \textsf{Nontrivial}\ A \to \textsf{IsSimpleCStar}\ A \to
  \textsf{K1Inj}\ A ,
\]
with $\textsf{K1Inj}\ A$ the injectivity of $\kappa_A$ and
$\textsf{IsSimpleCStar}\ A$ the condition that every closed two-sided ideal is
$\bot$ or $\top$.  Its negation is
\textsf{NinetyNineProblems.\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak problemLIX}, and the
separable strengthening of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
\textsf{NinetyNineProblems.\allowbreak exists\_\allowbreak separable\_%
\allowbreak simple\_\allowbreak unital\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak k1Inj},
\[
  \exists\, A,\ \textsf{SeparableSpace}\ A \wedge \textsf{Nontrivial}\ A
  \wedge \textsf{IsSimpleCStar}\ A \wedge \neg\, \textsf{K1Inj}\ A .
\]
\leanverified{thm:main-separable}{GroupApproximation.NinetyNineProblems.exists\_separable\_simple\_unital\_not\_k1Inj}
Both are proved from \textsf{CharClass.lemmaTwoHolds}, the statement of
Lemma~\ref{lem:two} at every stage of the tower, and both are hypothesis-free.

This note states the mathematics and cites the formal carriers.  It does not
reproduce the proofs those carriers contain, and where the argument formalised
differs from the argument one might expect, we say so: the divergences are
collected in Section~\ref{sec:divergences} and the most important of them,
concerning Lemma~\ref{lem:two}, is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:modtwo}.

\subsection{The shape of the construction}

Everything exists to produce one unitary in one algebra.  The algebra is an
inductive limit $A = \varinjlim A_i$ of homogeneous C*-algebras, each a corner
of a matrix algebra over a compact space, with the spaces growing by one
complex projective factor at each stage.  The unitary $v$ is the image in the
limit of a single explicit unitary $u$ over a four-sphere, and the two halves
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are proved by quite different means:

\begin{itemize}
\item $\operatorname{diag}(v,1) \in \Uz(M_2(A))$ is nearly free.  The
  generator $u$ takes values in $\U(2)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(u,1)$
  contracts already inside $\U(3)$, before any limit is taken; the contraction
  is transported along the inclusion of the first stage.
\item $v \notin \Uz(A)$ is the whole content.  A null-homotopy of $v$ in the
  limit would produce one at a finite stage, and a null-homotopy at a finite
  stage would make two explicit vector bundles isomorphic.  That they are not
  isomorphic is Lemma~\ref{lem:two}, and it is where all of the topology sits.
\end{itemize}

\section{The algebra}
\label{sec:algebra}

\subsection{The tower}

Write $\CP^d$ for the space of rank-one self-adjoint idempotents in
$M_{d+1}(\C)$, and let $S^4$ and $S^5$ be the four- and five-spheres, the
latter realised as the unit vectors of $\C^3$.  A complex vector bundle over a
compact Hausdorff space $X$ is taken throughout to be a continuous
projection-valued map, that is, a projection in $M_K(C(X))$; an isomorphism of
bundles is a Murray--von Neumann equivalence of projections, and the algebra of
sections of the endomorphism bundle of $p$ is the corner $p M_K(C(X)) p$.  No
bundle library and no Serre--Swan theorem is used, and the model is the one the
formalisation works in.

Set $d_i = 2^{\,i+1}$, and at stage $j$
\[
  Y_j \;=\; \prod_{i<j} \CP^{d_i},
  \qquad
  X_j \;=\; S^4 \times Y_j,
  \qquad
  H_j \;=\; \bigoplus_{i<j} L_i^{\oplus d_i},
\]
where $L_i$ is the tautological line bundle of the $i$-th projective factor,
pulled back to $Y_j$.  Put $E_j = \mathbf 1^2 \oplus H_j$ and
\[
  A_j \;=\; E_j \, M(C(X_j)) \, E_j .
\]
The dimensions $d_i$ are even, which is the hypothesis Step~D of
Lemma~\ref{lem:two} runs on, and they are positive; both are recorded formally
as \textsf{GroupApproximation.LIX.even\_lixDD} and
\textsf{GroupApproximation.LIX.lixDD\_pos}.

Two arithmetic checks are worth having in view, because they say why the
construction is tight rather than merely available.  Since $L_i$ has rank one
and appears with multiplicity $d_i$,
\[
  \rank E_j \;=\; 2 + \sum_{i<j} d_i \;=\; 2^{\,j+1},
  \qquad
  \dim X_j \;=\; 4 + 2\sum_{i<j} d_i \;=\; 2^{\,j+2} ,
\]
so the rank of the bundle and the dimension of the base grow together, the
second always exactly twice the first.

The same balance, for a different pair of objects, is what makes
Section~\ref{sec:modtwo} work.  There the space is not $X_j$ but
$N = S^1 \times S^5 \times \prod_i \CP^{d_i}$ and the bundle is not $E_j$ but
$V$ of rank $r = 3+m$, and one has $\dim N = 6 + 2m = 2r$ again.  The two
computations are parallel instances rather than the same one -- the $S^4$ of
the tower is replaced by the $S^1 \times S^5$ of the mapping torus, and the
rank goes up by one -- but in both the top Chern class of a rank-$r$ bundle
lands in the top cohomology of a $2r$-dimensional space, where there is room
for it to be nonzero and nothing above it to obstruct.

\subsection{The connecting maps and the limit}

The connecting map $\varphi_j \colon A_j \to A_{j+1}$ is a twisted point
evaluation,
\[
  \varphi_j(a)(x,z) \;=\; a(x) \,\oplus\, \bigl(a(x_j) \otimes 1_{L_{j+1},\,z}\bigr),
\]
where $x_j \in X_j$ is a fixed base point chosen so that every tail of the
sequence of its images is dense in the corresponding stage.  Each $\varphi_j$
is an injective unital $*$-homomorphism, and $A$ is the inductive limit, with
injective unital maps $\iota_j \colon A_j \to A$ whose images have dense union.

Simplicity comes from the choice of base point and nothing else.  For
$0 \neq a \geq 0$ in $A_k$ one picks $i \geq k$ with the image of $x_i$ inside
the open set where $a$ does not vanish; then the point-evaluation summand of
$\varphi_{k,i+1}(a)$ is nonzero at every point of $X_{i+1}$, and a section that
is nonzero in every fibre generates the whole corner, the fibres being full
matrix algebras.  So every nonzero closed two-sided ideal of the limit is
everything.

\subsection{The generator}

Over $S^5$, realised as the unit vectors $x$ of $\C^3$, the projection
$F = \mathbf 1_3 - x x^{*}$ has rank two.  Two Householder reflections through
the normalised midpoint of $p$ and $x$ give an explicit continuous frame
$\sigma_p$ on the complement of the antipode of $p$, with
$\sigma_p(x) e_3 = x$, and the two frames obtained from the two poles differ,
on the equatorial $S^4$, by a unitary
\[
  u \colon S^4 \longrightarrow \U(2), \qquad u(x) = \sigma_+(x)^{*}\sigma_-(x) .
\]
This $u$ is the generator.  It is the clutching function of $F$ over $S^5$, it
takes values in the corner addressed by $\mathbf 1_3 - e_3 e_3^{*}$, and
$\operatorname{diag}(u,1)$ is null-homotopic in $\U(3)$ by construction.  The
witness of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is $v = \iota_0(u \oplus 1_{H_0})$.

\section{Corollary 4, and why there is no Lemma 6}
\label{sec:cor4}

The unitary that has to be shown nontrivial at stage $j$ is not $u$ itself but
the image of $u$ under the tower's connecting maps.  In the source manuscript
this is handled by a separate lemma comparing that image with $u \oplus 1$ up
to homotopy.  Here it is an equality and the lemma is not needed.

Because the block that $\varphi_j$ adds is constant in the $S^4$ coordinate --
it is a point evaluation at the fixed $x_j$, tensored with a line bundle over
the new projective factor -- the climb of $u$ through the tower satisfies
\[
  \textsf{climb}_j(u) \;=\; u \oplus c_j
\]
on the nose, for a unitary $c_j$ of $H_j$ over $Y_j$ alone.  The
generalisation of Corollary~4 that one then needs is that $u \oplus c \notin
\Uz(A_j)$ for \emph{every} unitary $c$ of $H_j$ over $Y_j$, and that is the
same hemisphere gluing argument with $c$ carried on the northern side.

So no homotopy is required at this step.  The consequence is worth recording,
because it removes a dependency rather than merely shortening an argument: the
homotopy route would have needed path-connectedness of the unitary group of
$M_r(\C)$, which is not available in the ambient library at the pinned
revision.  The load-bearing constraint is that the connecting map must be
evaluated at the fixed stage point and never reindexed through anything
depending on the $S^4$ coordinate.

\section{The obstruction}
\label{sec:lemma2}

Everything above reduces Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to a statement about vector
bundles, with no C*-algebra in it.

\begin{lemma}[Lemma 2]
\label{lem:two}
Let $d_0,\dots,d_{l-1}$ be even and positive and let
\[
  M \;=\; S^5 \times \prod_{i<l} \CP^{d_i},
  \qquad m = \sum_{i<l} d_i .
\]
Over $M$ the projections $F \oplus H$ and $\mathbf 1^2 \oplus H$, both of
pointwise rank $2+m$, are not Murray--von Neumann equivalent as continuous
fields.
\leanverified{lem:two}{GroupApproximation.LIX.LemmaTwoHolds}
\end{lemma}

Two features of the statement deserve emphasis.  First, the two projections
have the same pointwise rank, so no trace or dimension count can separate them;
this is the genuinely hard statement and it is the only input the C*-side
takes.  Second, the ambient bundle in which both sit is
$V = \mathbf 1^3 \oplus H$, of rank $3+m$ and not $2+m$ -- a one-character
difference from the rank of the two projections, and one that has to be kept
straight throughout.  Formally the lemma is stated inside the single algebra
over the index set of $V$, as the failure of
\textsf{ContinuousMvNEquiv} between
\[
  \textsf{FHmat} = V - s s^{*}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \textsf{EHmat} = V - e e^{*},
\]
where $e$ and $s$ are the unit sections corresponding to $e_3$ and to $x$.
Writing it this way, rather than as a pair of block matrices over different
index sets, is what makes it type-correct: Murray--von Neumann equivalence
needs both elements in one algebra.

\section{The mod-2 proof of Lemma 2}
\label{sec:modtwo}

\subsection{What is proved here, and what is not}
\label{sec:modtwo-divergence}

A reader who knows the source argument should be warned at once.  Lemma
\ref{lem:two} is proved there by an integral computation, with Chern classes
and a Künneth decomposition over $\mathbb Z$.  \emph{That is not the argument
formalised, and it is not the argument below.}  What is proved here is a mod-2
statement throughout: the characteristic classes are the mod-2 Chern classes
$\gamma_i$, the cohomology is singular cohomology with $\F_2$ coefficients, and
the step that kills the even side runs on the Wu formula and the instability of
Steenrod squares, neither of which has an integral counterpart in this shape.
A reader who goes looking for the integral computation in the formal
development will not find it.

The re-cutting was not a matter of taste.  Working mod~2 removes every sign and
every torsion consideration from the obstruction, and it replaces the Künneth
theorem over $\mathbb Z$ -- which the formalisation does not have, since it
would need Eilenberg--Zilber -- with a decomposition over a field.  It also
makes the evenness of the dimensions $d_i$ do visible work, through the
Frobenius endomorphism of an $\F_2$-algebra, where in the integral argument
that hypothesis enters less directly.

\subsection{The setting}

Fix even positive $d_0,\dots,d_{l-1}$, put $m = \sum_i d_i$ and $r = 3+m$, and
let $M = S^5 \times \prod_i \CP^{d_i}$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:two}.  The ambient
bundle is $V = \mathbf 1^3 \oplus H$, of rank $r$.  Everything happens over
\[
  N \;=\; S^1 \times M ,
\]
a closed manifold of dimension $1 + 5 + 2m = 2r$.  So the top mod-2 cohomology
$H^{2r}(N;\F_2)$ is the one place where a rank-$r$ bundle over $N$ can have a
top Chern class at all, and it is one-dimensional over $\F_2$.  The proof
consists of computing $\gamma_r$ of one particular bundle over $N$ in two ways
and getting different answers.

\subsection{Step A: from an equivalence to a unitary}

Suppose the two projections of Lemma~\ref{lem:two} were equivalent.  Both are
written inside the single algebra of $V$, as $V - s s^{*}$ and $V - e e^{*}$
for the unit sections $s$ and $e$ corresponding to $x \in S^5$ and to the
constant $e_3$.  An equivalence between them extends, by sending $e$ to $s$, to
a unitary $g$ of the corner cut out by $V$ with $g e = s$.  This is pure
algebra and it is what turns a statement about two subprojections into a
statement about one unitary of the ambient bundle.  Formally the conclusion is
the predicate \textsf{HasStepAUnitary}: a continuous $G$ on the base, unitary
in the corner of $V$ at each point, carrying the one section to the other.

\subsection{Step B: the mapping torus}

From $g$ one builds a rank-$r$ bundle $W_g$ over $N = S^1 \times M$ which
restricts to $V$ on a slice $\{\ast\} \times M$.  The construction is a
clutching over the two-point equator of $S^1$: the identity at one seam and $g$
at the other.

The obvious alternative does not work, and it is worth saying why, because it
is the natural first attempt.  One would like to run the Whitehead path from
the identity to $g$ around the circle.  That model does not descend to $S^1$:
run over the whole circle the rotation returns to the identity, and the bundle
it produces is trivial, so it carries no obstruction at all.  The clutched
model is used instead, and it has the further advantage that the restriction to
the northern slice is $V \oplus 0$ \emph{on the nose} rather than up to
equivalence, so no Murray--von Neumann transport is needed later.

\subsection{Step C: the odd side}

The manuscript's section of $V$, transported into $W_g$, has exactly one zero
$z$, and near $z$ it is a local homeomorphism onto a neighbourhood of the
origin in $\C^r$ -- the derivative there is an isomorphism, and the inverse
function theorem is applied on an explicit chart.  From this,
\[
  \gamma_r(W_g) \;=\; j^{*}\bigl(\sigma^{*} U\bigr) \;\neq\; 0
  \qquad\text{in } H^{2r}(N;\F_2),
\]
where $U$ is the Thom class of the bundle pair, $\sigma^{*}U$ its pullback
along the section, which lies in the relative group $H^{2r}(N, N \setminus z)$
and is the local generator there, and $j^{*}$ the map to the absolute group.
That $j^{*}$ is an isomorphism is a computation about the punctured space:
$H^{2r}(N \setminus z;\F_2) = 0$, by a Mayer--Vietoris recursion over the
product structure of $N$.  No degree computation is used anywhere; the local
generator is identified by excision to a chart together with a homeomorphism.

The three named residues of this step are the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:residues}.

\subsection{Step D: the even side}

Step D is a statement about \emph{every} rank-$r$ bundle over $N$ that
restricts to $V$ on the slice, with no reference to $g$:
\[
  \gamma_r(W) \;=\; 0 .
\]
Over $\F_2$ the cohomology of $N$ decomposes as
$H^{*}(N;\F_2) = \Lambda(t) \otimes \Lambda(x) \otimes H^{*}(Y;\F_2)$ with $t$
the circle class, $x$ the class of $S^5$ and $Y$ the projective part, so one
may write $\gamma(W) = \gamma(p^{*}V)\,(1 + z b)$ with $z = t x$ and
$b \in H^{*}(Y;\F_2)$; the slice condition is what forces this shape.  Three
things then finish it.

First, $\gamma(V) = \prod_i (1+h_i)^{d_i}$ is supported in degrees divisible by
four, because each $d_i$ is even and squaring is additive over $\F_2$.  This is
the only place the evenness hypothesis is used, and it is used as the Frobenius
endomorphism rather than as a parity count.

Second, the Wu relation
\[
  \Sq^{2i}\gamma_{i+1} \;=\; \sum_{s=0}^{i} \gamma_{i-s}\,\gamma_{i+1+s},
\]
read on the $z$-part and combined with the instability of Steenrod squares --
$\Sq^{2k}$ vanishes on classes of degree below $2k$ -- kills every
even-indexed coefficient $b_n$.

Third, assembling, $\gamma_r(W) = \sum_k A_{m-k}\, b_k$ with every surviving
term zero.

\subsection{The contradiction}

Steps C and D are about the same bundle and give opposite answers, so no such
$g$ exists, and by Step A no Murray--von Neumann equivalence exists either.
Formally the contradiction is a single schema: given a top-class functional,
a Step C that makes it nonzero for every admissible $G$ and a Step D that makes
it zero for every admissible $G$, together with Step A to produce a $G$ from an
equivalence, one concludes the failure of \textsf{ContinuousMvNEquiv}.
\leanverified{lem:two}{GroupApproximation.CharClass.lemmaTwo\_of\_stepC\_stepD}
The two sides are genuinely independent: Step D quantifies over all bundles
with the right restriction and never sees $g$, while Step C is about the one
bundle $g$ builds.

\section{Step C's odd side and the three residues}
\label{sec:residues}

\subsection{The relative Euler class survives restriction}
\label{sec:hres}

Step~C produces a section of the mapping-torus bundle with exactly one zero,
and identifies the top mod-2 Chern class with the image of the Thom class under
restriction to that zero.  One of the maps in that identification is the
restriction
\[
  \textsf{lixRes} \colon
  H^{2r}(E,\,E \setminus 0;\F_2) \longrightarrow
  H^{2r}\bigl(E|_B,\,E|_B \setminus 0;\F_2\bigr)
\]
of the bundle pair to the part lying over a trivialising neighbourhood $B$ of
the section's zero, and what Step~C needs of it is injectivity.

The source of that map is a line.  The degreewise Thom algebra identifies the
relative group of the bundle pair with $H^0$ of the base, and the base
$S^1 \times S^5 \times \prod_i \CP^{d_i}$ is path-connected, so that group is
one-dimensional over $\F_2$, with the Thom class as generator.  A linear map
out of a line is injective exactly when it does not kill the generator, so the
whole of the required injectivity is the single nonvanishing
\[
  \textsf{lixRes}\,(\textsf{lixThomClassTerm}) \;\neq\; 0 .
\]
Both directions of that reduction are formal, so nothing is lost in it.

\subsection*{The neighbourhood cannot be computed in}

It is tempting to finish by transporting both sides through Thom isomorphisms
and reading the map as the restriction of $H^0$ from the base to $B$, which is
injective because the base is connected.  That is a correct description of the
mathematics and it is not a route that exists here.  The Thom isomorphism in
question is the coordinate map that names the Thom class, and it is built from
a bridge which requires a \emph{compact Hausdorff} base; $B$ is an open ball
and is neither.  There is no Thom isomorphism over $B$ to transport anything
through.  The same obstruction appears from the other side in the statement
that over a contractible base only the top Leray--Hirsch column survives at the
top degree: that statement is true, but applying it to a restricted class needs
a Leray--Hirsch presentation over the neighbourhood, and a ball has none.

Connectedness of the base is still used, and it is what makes the source a
line; it does not give the nonvanishing.

\subsection*{The fibre over the zero}

So the class is followed one step further, to the fibre over the section's zero
$z$.  The set $\{z\}$ is a single point of the base: compact, Hausdorff and
nonempty for free, and over a one-point base the Leray--Hirsch presentation
exists with nothing added and the coordinates are computable.

Because $z$ lies in $B$, restricting to the fibre factors through restricting
to $B$.  The direction in which that factorisation is used is the whole
argument and is easy to state backwards, so we state it forwards: a class
killed by $\textsf{lixRes}$ is killed over the point, and therefore surviving
over the point \emph{suffices}.  It is not claimed that the two restrictions
have the same kernel, that the restriction to the fibre is injective, or that
it detects every class.

Over the point the computation is a coordinate computation and nothing else.
The Leray--Hirsch coordinates of a class restrict coordinatewise; the top
coordinate of the Thom class is the unit of $H^0$ of the base; and the unit
restricts to the unit, which is nonzero because a point is nonempty.  So the
restricted class is nonzero, and $\textsf{lixRes}$ spares the generator.
\leanverified{sec:hres}{GroupApproximation.CharClass.injective\_lixRes}

Only linearity of the coordinate map is used, never its injectivity over the
point, which is why the detour costs one lemma rather than a second copy of the
Thom machinery.  No positivity of the $d_i$ enters this argument, no projective
factor is peeled and nothing inducts over the index family, so the empty family
$l = 0$ -- the first stage of the tower, where the base is $S^1 \times S^5$ and
any hypothesis of the form $0 < d_i$ is vacuous -- is not a special case.

\subsection{The Gysin identity}
\label{sec:hclass}

The second residue identifies the image of the Thom class in the absolute
cohomology of the total space with the pullback of the top mod-2 Chern class:
\[
  j_E(U) \;=\; \pi^{*}\gamma_r .
\]

The obstacle is that the Thom class is \emph{named} on the vector-bundle pair
while its Leray--Hirsch coordinates are \emph{read} on the projectivisation
pair, so the two relative-to-absolute maps have to be compared before anything
can be computed.  They differ by restriction to the affine chart.  On the
projectivisation the image of the Thom class is by construction the
Leray--Hirsch combination $\sum_i \pi^{*}(a_i) \smile \xi^{i}$ of its
coordinates, and those coordinates are known: the top one is the unit and the
$i$-th is the Chern coefficient $\gamma_{r-i}$.  Restricting to the chart kills
$\xi$, so every column except $i = 0$ dies and the surviving one is
$\pi^{*}(1 \smile \gamma_r) = \pi^{*}\gamma_r$.
\leanverified{sec:hclass}{GroupApproximation.CharClass.lixHclass}

The chart here \emph{is} the total space: the map in question includes $E$ into
$P(E \oplus \mathbf 1)$ as the affine chart, so restricting to the chart and
restricting to $E$ are one operation, and the argument has returned to where it
started.

It is worth saying that the identity is not a formal consequence of naturality
and splitting alone.  For arbitrary maps satisfying only those two conditions
it is false, so it has to be proved at the concrete $j_E$ and $\pi^{*}$ rather
than at a general pair, and a reader who tries to state this subsection
abstractly will lose time discovering that.

Three things this does not say, all three worth stating because the argument
invites the opposite reading.  It proves nothing about the \emph{section}: the
class compared here is the one the projection pulls back, not the one the
section detects.  It does not on its own make $\gamma_r$ nonzero -- that is the
consumer's conclusion, from this identity together with
Section~\ref{sec:hres}, and not from this identity alone.  And it says nothing
about $\xi$ away from the chart: on the complement of the zero section $\xi$
does not vanish, which is where the Gysin sequence lives, so the slogan above
must not be read as making $\xi$ zero -- that would trivialise the whole
argument.  In the other direction, the identity holds for \emph{every}
continuous corner-unitary $G$ and not only for the clutching one, so nothing in
it is special to the construction.

\subsection{The compatibility square}
\label{sec:hsq}

The third residue is the square saying that sectioning and then charting agrees
with restricting and then trivialising.  Both outer legs are relative
pullbacks, but the charting side contains an \emph{inverse} excision, so the
square cannot be checked pointwise from end to end.  Composing that excision's
forward map onto both sides removes the inverse.  One then restricts to a
smaller neighbourhood of the zero, and that restriction does two things: it
brings the two legs, which reach the local model through \emph{different}
neighbourhoods of the zero, onto a common one; and it puts the comparison
inside the region where the chart genuinely reads the section, which its clamps
guarantee only near the zero.  Only injectivity of that restriction is used,
never that it is an isomorphism.  What remains are two pullbacks along explicit
maps of pairs: on the left, a point goes to the section at its chart image; on
the right, to the point of the fibre over the section's zero with the given
frame coordinate.

The two are joined by a homotopy through maps of pairs, whose far end is the
right leg precomposed with a linear automorphism, and the homotopy is cut at
either end because the relative group in question is a line over $\F_2$; the
automorphism's own pullback is the identity for the same reason.  That cut is
not a shortcut through relative homotopy invariance -- it is a substitute for
it.  This development has no prism operator, so relative homotopy invariance is
not available as a tool at all, and cutting a homotopy over a line is what
takes its place.  The mod-2 coefficients are load-bearing here: over any other
field two isomorphisms between lines differ by a scalar and the argument fails.
\leanverified{sec:hsq}{GroupApproximation.CharClass.lixHsq}

What keeps this honest is that the two legs are shown \emph{equal}, not both
nonzero.  Equality would also follow from both being nonzero, since both are
maps between $\F_2$-lines, and that route exists; it was not taken.  On the
right, ``nonzero'' is exactly the conclusion of Section~\ref{sec:hres}, a map
out of a line being nonzero precisely when it is injective.  On the left,
``nonzero'' is not Step~C's conclusion -- that is the nonvanishing of
$\gamma_r$ -- but the injectivity hypothesis which Step~C's odd side
\emph{consumes}, which is to say the very thing this square together with
Section~\ref{sec:hres} exists to produce.  So an argument establishing the two
legs separately would have had to supply the reduction's own output by
independent means: not a circular proof, but a reduction assuming what it was
introduced to give.

Four things not to infer.  The section is not linear in the base point; what is
linear is the reconstruction in the chart's \emph{value} at a fixed point, and
the far end of the homotopy, where the base is pinned at the zero.  The chart
itself is the local homeomorphism supplied by the inverse function theorem, and
only its derivative at the zero is linear.  Nondegeneracy of the section's zero
is not proved here; it is paid for once, upstream, by the product chart being a
partial homeomorphism.  Nothing here is naturality of excision, which is not
proved anywhere in the development -- the design avoids crossing the inverse
excision by composing it away instead.

\subsection{What was checked, and in what order}

The claim that these declarations prove what they say rests on four conditions,
and they are ordered rather than independent -- a point worth stating because
the natural reading of them as a checklist is wrong.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The build succeeds.  Nothing below means anything without this.  A failed
  build still emits an axiom report, assembled from whatever modules compiled
  before the failure, and that report can be entirely clean while proving
  nothing about the module one is interested in.
\item No declaration in the build depends on \textsf{sorryAx}.  Lean closes an
  unsolved goal with that axiom and still writes an object file, and a file
  can contain no literal \textsf{sorry} token while its theorems depend on it,
  so a textual scan for the token does not answer this question.
\item The axiom report is non-empty.  A count of zero means no axiom line was
  emitted at all, which is indistinguishable from cleanliness if one only asks
  whether any line is bad.
\item The declaration one cares about appears in that report \emph{by name},
  with its own axiom list.
\end{enumerate}

For the two endpoint statements, condition~(4) reads
\[
  [\textsf{propext},\ \textsf{Classical.choice},\ \textsf{Quot.sound}] ,
\]
the three axioms of Lean's own logic and nothing else.  This was read three
times independently, on two different clones, by three different readers, each
from a forced fresh compile with build artifacts cleared rather than from a
replay, and from a clean export of the commit rather than from a working tree.
All three readings were taken from the raw build log, not from any tool's
summary of it.  They agree.

That last point is provenance rather than pedantry, and it tells a later reader
which artefact to re-derive the claim from.  A summary that truncates its own
report is indistinguishable from a complete one: in the run behind this
section, the summarising tool omitted precisely the two endpoint declarations
while reporting a plausible and truthful count of the lines it had gathered.
The failure that invites is not a reader who sees nothing and stops.  It is a
reader who sees nothing for the endpoint, concludes that the build did not
reach it, and then finds one row above it the clean-looking axiom line of
\textsf{not\_\allowbreak problemLIX\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak lemmaTwo} --
a different theorem, and a conditional one -- and cites that instead.  A
truncated report does not produce an obvious absence; it produces a plausible
wrong answer.

\section{Divergences and non-claims}
\label{sec:divergences}

We list the places where this account or the formal development departs from
what a reader may expect, and the things that are not claimed.

\emph{The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:two} is not the source's.}  The source argues
integrally, with Chern classes and a Künneth decomposition over $\mathbb Z$;
what is formalised is a mod-2 argument through Wu classes and Steenrod squares.
Section~\ref{sec:modtwo-divergence} states this at length.  It is the single
most important divergence, because both arguments prove the same statement and
only one of them is present.

\emph{Bundles are projections.}  A vector bundle is a projection-valued map
throughout, an isomorphism of bundles is a Murray--von Neumann equivalence, and
no Serre--Swan theorem is invoked.  This is a modelling choice, and statements
about ``vector bundles'' below should be read in it.

\emph{Lemma 6 of the source is absent.}  It is not that it was proved
differently; it is not needed, for the reason in Section~\ref{sec:cor4}.

\emph{$K_1(A) = 0$ is not claimed.}  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} says the map
$\kappa_A$ is not injective.  It says nothing about what $K_1(A)$ is.

\emph{No claim about the order of the kernel.}  In particular it is not claimed
that $\ker \kappa_A$ has order exactly two, and no such statement is
formalised.

\emph{Section~\ref{sec:hres} claims one implication, not an equivalence.}  It
is shown that a class killed by the restriction to the trivialising
neighbourhood is killed over the fibre at the section's zero, so that surviving
over the fibre suffices.  It is not shown that the two restrictions have the
same kernel, that the restriction to the fibre is injective, or that it detects
every class.

\emph{Nothing is computed over the trivialising neighbourhood.}  There is no
Thom isomorphism over it in the development, and none is used or claimed; see
Section~\ref{sec:hres}.

\emph{Relative homotopy invariance is not available.}  The development has no
prism operator, so the tool does not exist in it; where an argument appears to
use it, what is used instead is the cut of a homotopy over an $\F_2$-line
described in Section~\ref{sec:hsq}.  Nor is naturality of excision proved
anywhere, and nothing relies on it.

\emph{The mod-2 coefficients are not a convenience.}  Besides
Section~\ref{sec:modtwo}, where they replace an integral Künneth theorem the
development does not have, they are load-bearing in
Section~\ref{sec:hsq}: over a field other than $\F_2$ two isomorphisms between
lines differ by a scalar and that argument fails.
