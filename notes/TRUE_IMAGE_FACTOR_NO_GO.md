# Exact unitary images retain the wrong relations

## Purpose

The normal-Dirac transfer cannot be proved by replacing each exact
finite-dimensional representation with a finite action of its exact image.
This fails even for a fixed two-generator word table converging to the regular
character of the finite group `C_2`.

The point is different from the changing-word obstruction `(NIR8)-(NIR10)`.
Here the source words are always the same two generators. What changes is an
accidental relation inside the matrix image. A valid finite-action rounding
must be allowed to forget that relation.

## The example

Let

`F=<a,b>`

be free, and let

`phi:F->C_2`, `phi(a)=0`, `phi(b)=1`, `N=ker(phi)`.       `(IFR1)`

Thus

`1_N(w)=1` iff the exponent sum of `b` in `w` is even.    `(IFR2)`

For `n>=1`, put

`zeta_n=exp(2 pi i/(4n))`

and define an exact two-dimensional unitary representation by

`pi_n(a)=diag(1,zeta_n)`,
`pi_n(b)=diag(1,-1)=pi_n(a)^(2n)`.                       `(IFR3)`

Its image is the finite cyclic group generated by `pi_n(a)`. If `A(w)` and
`B(w)` are the exponent sums of `a` and `b` in a word `w`, then

`tr_2(pi_n(w))=(1+zeta_n^(A(w))(-1)^(B(w)))/2`.          `(IFR4)`

For each fixed word, `zeta_n^(A(w))->1`. Hence

`tr_2(pi_n(w))->(1+(-1)^(B(w)))/2=1_N(w)`.              `(IFR5)`

So `(pi_n)` is a sequence of honest finite-image unitary characters
converging pointwise to the normal regular `0/1` character of `F/N=C_2`.
If one requires matrix dimensions to tend to infinity, replace `pi_n` by any
growing direct-sum repetition; its normalized character and image relations
are unchanged.

## Image-factor rounding is impossible

**Theorem.** There are no finite `F`-sets `X_n` whose actions factor through
`pi_n(F)` and whose fixed-point characters converge to `1_N`, even only on
the fixed test `{a,b}`.

**Proof.** Write `sigma_n(a),sigma_n(b)` for the two permutations. Factoring
through `pi_n(F)` preserves `(IFR3)`, so

`sigma_n(b)=sigma_n(a)^(2n)`.                            `(IFR6)`

For every permutation `s` and positive integer `k`,

`supp(s^k) subset supp(s)`,                              `(IFR7)`

because a point fixed by `s` is fixed by every power. Therefore

`d_Ham(sigma_n(b),1)<=d_Ham(sigma_n(a),1)`,

or equivalently

`chi_(X_n)(b)>=chi_(X_n)(a)`.                            `(IFR8)`

Convergence to `1_N` would require the right side to tend to
`1_N(a)=1` and the left side to tend to `1_N(b)=0`, contradicting `(IFR8)`.
End proof.

The same proof allows an `o(1)` error in `(IFR6)`: the triangle inequality and
`(IFR7)` still give

`d_Ham(sigma_n(b),1)<=d_Ham(sigma_n(a),1)+o(1)`.

## Consequences for the live route

This rules out all of the following as universal normal-Dirac transfers:

1. take a finite quotient of the exact image and use its regular action;
2. choose subgroups of the exact image and use coset actions or their disjoint
   unions;
3. projectivize the matrix image, invoke residual finiteness of the resulting
   finitely generated linear group, and pull back a finite action;
4. convert a finite-image unitary character to a permutation character while
   requiring the permutation representation to remain a representation of the
   same finite image.

Indeed each construction retains `(IFR6)`. Projectivization does not help:
the projective order of `diag(1,zeta_n)` is still `4n`, and the relation with
`b` remains.

The target quotient `F/N=C_2` is of course sofic. Its correct finite action
sets `a` to the identity and `b` to an involution, thereby discarding the
changing accidental relation `b=a^(2n)`. Thus the missing transfer must be
table-dependent and nonfunctorial in the exact matrix image. It has to use
the limiting normal law, not the algebraic relations holding at an individual
matrix level.
