## Setup

The target asks that every finitely generated group `G` with solvable word problem
embed in an extension `1 -> S -> I -> Q -> 1`, where:
- `S` is finitely presented, simple and nonabelian;
- `Q` is finitely presented;
- `Q -> Out(S)` is injective.

Two facts were already on main:
- Boone--Higman implies the target, by taking `Q = 1`.
- The word problem of `I` is kernel-bounded (Attempt 1). So the kernels must be
  unboundedly complex.

The question for this session was whether outer automorphisms can do anything that
permutational Boone--Higman (PBH, the class `B_A`) cannot.

**Theorem.** Let `S` be finitely presented, simple and mixed identity free (MIF), with
`Inn(S) <= B <= Aut(S)` and `B/Inn(S)` finitely presented, and let `n >= 2`. Enlarge
the BFFHZ actor `A = Aut_S(S * F_n)` to `Γ_B = A ⋊ B`, acting on
`X = Hom_S(S * F_n, S)` by `γ·f = γ|_S ∘ f ∘ γ^-1`. Then:
- the kernel is exactly the inner copy `{iota_s}` of `S`;
- the quotient `Λ_B` is a type (A) actor containing `B`.

So every outer extension of a finitely presented simple MIF group by a finitely
presented group lies in `B_A`.

**The new lemma (W).** In an MIF group, a one-variable word map with constants that
is an endomorphism is trivial or `x -> a x a^-1`. The proof applies MIF to
`u(xy) u(y)^-1 u(x)^-1` and then compares normal forms in `S * <x> * <y>`. MIF enters
only here and in BFFHZ Lemma 2.2.

**Consequences.**
- For a finitely generated `G`, `G ∈ B_A` iff `G` embeds in an MIF-kernel outer
  extension. The reverse direction uses BFFHZ Theorem C.
- A witness beyond PBH needs an infinite, non-MIF kernel. If the kernel is already in
  `B_A`, it also needs an infinite `Q`.
- **Corollary 6''.** Suppose the conjugation action of `I` extends to a finitely
  presented simple MIF overgroup `M` of `S`, and the outer image in `Out(M)` sits in
  a finitely presented subgroup. Then `I ∈ B_A`.
- The standard highly transitive kernels contribute nothing new: `V`, the Röver--
  Nekrashevych commutator subgroups, the twisted Brin--Thompson groups and the
  Burger--Mozes groups (BFFHZ Remark 3.5).

Outcome recorded as an **obstruction**: the MIF part of the target collapses to PBH.

## Approaches and where each dies

1. **MIF kernels.** These collapse to PBH (theorem above). They cannot give a witness
   beyond PBH.

2. **Same actor, non-MIF kernel** (artifact §8, Proposition 9). Everything survives
   except finite normal generation of the kernel `K`:
   - `Γ_B` is finitely presented by BFFHZ Proposition 1.1;
   - stabilizers are finitely generated by BFFHZ Proposition 2.6 and Lemma 2.1;
   - the action is highly transitive by BFFHZ Proposition 2.3.

   `K` is finitely normally generated provided both:
   - (a) BFFHZ Question 3.2 holds for `S`;
   - (b) the word automorphisms `W = ρ(K)` are finitely normally generated modulo
     `Inn(S)`.

   *Dies at (a).* It is open for every non-MIF finitely presented simple group, and
   BFFHZ say it is unclear "even for example for Thompson's group T". Also, when (a)
   holds, `S` is already in `B_A`, so only infinite `Q` could add anything. In the FFWZ
   language this is removability of the kernel of a type [A_2] action
   (`a2-kernel-removal-iff-image-satisfies-pbh`), with no new leverage.

3. **Replace the target set by `Hom_S(S * F_n, M)` for an MIF overgroup `M`.**
   Faithfulness would come from MIF of `M`. This is a heuristic, not written as a
   claim. It dies twice:
   - The transitivity trick needs `f'(x_j) f(x_j)^-1 ∈ S`. The subgroup
     `<S, f(x_1), ..., f(x_n)> <= M` is invariant under `A`, so there is no reason for
     finitely many orbits.
   - `Aut(S)` has no action on `M` unless automorphisms extend.

   When they do extend, Corollary 6'' applies directly and again gives only PBH.

4. **Complexity.** This dies by `outer-extension-word-problem-is-kernel-bounded`
   (target Attempt 1). Unboundedly complex kernels are needed, so the target has no
   complexity advantage over BH.

5. **Target implies BH, or target implies PBH.** No reduction was found.
   - For a witness `I` with a non-MIF kernel, embedding `I` in a finitely presented
     simple group is BH for the finitely presented decidable group `I`. That is the
     conjecture itself.
   - Corollary 6'' shows what would suffice: extend the automorphisms to an MIF
     overgroup. No general extension mechanism is known.

6. **Candidate non-MIF test family: Thompson-like circle groups `T_{n,r}`.**
   *Unverified heuristic.* Olukoya (arXiv:1908.03816v1; abstract only) states that
   `Out(T_{n,r})` is infinite and contains a copy of Thompson's `F` for `n > 2`, and that
   `Aut(T_{n,r})` is characterised "as a subgroup of Aut G_{n,r}".

   If the commutator subgroup of `G_{n,r}` is a finitely presented simple highly
   transitive, hence MIF, overgroup to which these automorphisms extend, then
   Corollary 6'' puts the `F`-extensions in `B_A`. Their outer images are quotients of
   finite-by-`F` groups, which are finitely presented. This needs the group `G_{n,r}`
   to have finite abelianisation and automorphisms that act spatially (Rubin).

   None of these facts was re-read here. So Thompson-like circle groups are probably
   not a source of witnesses beyond PBH.

## Weakest points

- Lemma (W), Step 2. This is an elementary but syllable-level normal-form argument.
- Reliance on four separate clauses of BFFHZ Theorem E: finite presentation of `A`,
  faithfulness, finitely generated stabilizers and pair orbits. They are imported via
  `relative-automorphism-of-fp-simple-mif-group-has-type-a-action`. The internal BFFHZ
  proofs, including Carette's presentation, were not re-derived.
