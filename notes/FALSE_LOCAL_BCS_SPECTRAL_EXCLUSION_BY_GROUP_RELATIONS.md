# Group relations cannot locally exclude a character of a finite subgroup

Date: 2026-08-13

## 1. Outcome

A local gadget cannot translate an arbitrary Boolean constraint into group
relations by embedding its commuting involutions as a finite subgroup and
trying to delete the forbidden joint eigenspaces. The regular representation
of the resulting group restores every character of that finite subgroup.

This is an exact obstruction, not a limitation of a particular controlled
Pauli construction. It explains the special role of linear constraint
systems: an affine constraint can be imposed by collapsing a group word,
whereas deleting one point from a non-affine joint spectrum cannot.

## 2. Regular-restriction theorem

**Theorem 1.** Let `A<G` be a finite subgroup. The restriction of the left
regular representation of `G` to `A` is a Hilbert direct sum of copies of the
left regular representation of `A`:

```text
lambda_G|A ~= directSum_(A\G) lambda_A.                (BSE1)
```

Consequently every irreducible representation of `A` occurs in
`lambda_G|A`. If `A` is abelian and `chi` is a character, then

```text
p_chi=|A|^(-1) sum_(a in A) conjugate(chi(a)) lambda_G(a) (BSE2)
```

is a nonzero projection in `L(G)` with

```text
tau_G(p_chi)=1/|A|.                                    (BSE3)
```

**Proof.** Partition `G` into right `A`-cosets. For every coset `Ax`, the
subspace `ell^2(Ax)` is invariant under left multiplication by `A`, and the
map `delta_a |-> delta_(ax)` identifies it with `ell^2(A)`. Their orthogonal
sum is `ell^2(G)`, proving `(BSE1)`. Formula `(BSE2)` is the usual minimal
central projection of `C[A]` for `chi`; `(BSE1)` makes it nonzero. Only the
coefficient of the identity contributes to the group trace, giving
`(BSE3)`. End proof.

## 3. Commuting involutions

Suppose group elements `x_1,...,x_k` generate an embedded copy

```text
A ~= C_2^k.                                             (BSE4)
```

For every assignment `epsilon in {+-1}^k`, the joint spectral projection

```text
p_epsilon=2^(-k) product_i (1+epsilon_i lambda_G(x_i)) (BSE5)
```

is nonzero and has trace `2^(-k)`. Thus no additional group generators and
relations can force one chosen `p_epsilon` to vanish in **all** unitary
representations while retaining `(BSE4)`: the left regular representation of
the presented group is already a counterexample.

The only way a group presentation can remove `(BSE5)` universally is to
change the abstract subgroup generated by the `x_i`, so that the assignment
is no longer a character. Relations among commuting involutions do this by
linear equations over `F_2`. Their allowed character set is therefore an
affine subspace, exactly the LCS situation.

## 4. Central-character corners do not evade the obstruction

Let `z` be a central element of finite order and suppose `z in A`. For a
central character `omega` of `<z>`, put

```text
p_omega=|z|^(-1) sum_j conjugate(omega(z^j))lambda_G(z^j). (BSE6)
```

Every character `chi` of `A` with `chi|<z>=omega` still occurs in the
corner `p_omega L(G)`. Indeed `p_chi<=p_omega` and `(BSE3)` remains positive.
Selecting the usual `J=-1` solution-group corner therefore does not permit a
local nonlinear spectral exclusion either.

## 5. Consequence for perfect-completeness reductions

A BCS context consists of commuting involutions whose joint spectrum is
required to lie in an arbitrary Boolean relation `R`. If `R` is not affine,
this condition cannot be imposed universally by group relations on the
context subgroup. Auxiliary finite-group or Clifford gadgets do not help:
once the context subgroup embeds, `(BSE1)` restores every one of its
characters in the ambient regular representation.

This does not prove that a particular non-CE BCS algebra cannot occur as a
subalgebra of a non-CE group factor. It proves that a compiler cannot obtain
such an embedding from independent local spectral-exclusion gadgets. A
successful groupification must use a global phenomenon—stability, a radical,
or a nonlocal coupling of contexts. That is precisely the missing content in
the perfect-completeness LCS endpoint.
