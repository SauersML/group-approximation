# Augmentation-homology localization for the explicit radical double

Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Outcome

For the explicit finitely presented Kun--Thom radical double from
`FALSE_EXPLICIT_FP_RADICAL_DOUBLE.md`, the relative transferred-filling
class of `FALSE_TRANSFERRED_FILLING_PAIRING_FORMULA.md` cannot occupy the
large free Bass--Serre part of augmentation homology.

Let

`D=G *_Gamma G`,  `G=E semidirect Q`,  `L=Q*Q`,                  `(RDA1)`

and let `D->L` kill `E` in each vertex.  Its kernel is perfect.  Hence, for
every finite quotient `T` of `L` and its augmentation module `I_T`,

`H_1(D,I_T) ~= H_1(L,I_T)`.                                      `(RDA2)`

The free-product Mayer--Vietoris sequence splits the target into a vertex
part and a free overlap lattice.  Naturality in degree two forces the
relative radical filling class into the vertex part

`H_1(Q,I_T) directSum H_1(Q,I_T)`.                               `(RDA3)`

Thus the live marked torsion is controlled by finite-index stabilizer
homology of `Q`, not by the free product direction.  Shapiro's lemma gives
an exact sequence involving the stabilizers' `H_2`; this is the precise
bridge to the projective-chart computations at levels `3,5,7,53`.

## 2. The quotient kernel is perfect

Retain the stable-range construction

`Gamma=EL_r(R_+) <= E=EL_r(R)`,                                  `(RDA4)`

from `FALSE_EXPLICIT_FP_RADICAL_DOUBLE.md`.  Both `Gamma` and `E` are
perfect: the elementary commutator identity expresses every elementary
generator as a commutator once `r>=3`.

Let

`pi:D=G *_Gamma G -> Q*Q=L`                                      `(RDA5)`

be induced by the two quotient maps `G->G/E=Q`, and put `N=ker(pi)`.

**Proposition 1.**  The group `N` is perfect.

**Proof.**  Let `mathcal T` be the Bass--Serre tree of `D`.  Since `N` is
normal, the quotient graph `N\mathcal T` has

* vertex sets `L/Q` for the two vertex types; and
* edge set `L`, because the amalgamated edge group `Gamma` maps trivially
  to `L`.

This is exactly the Bass--Serre tree of the free product `L=Q*Q`, hence is
a tree.  The vertex stabilizers for the action of `N` are conjugates of
`E`, and the edge stabilizers are conjugates of `Gamma`.  Therefore `N` is
the fundamental group of a tree of groups whose vertex and edge groups are
perfect.  Abelianization of a tree of groups is generated by the vertex
abelianizations, with no graph-cycle generators.  All those
abelianizations vanish, so `N_ab=0`.  End proof.

## 3. Descent of augmentation homology

Let `M` be any `ZL`-module, regarded as a `ZD`-module through `pi`.  The
kernel `N` acts trivially on `M`.  The Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre homology
spectral sequence has

`E^2_(p,q)=H_p(L,H_q(N,M))`.                                     `(RDA6)`

Since `N` is perfect,

`H_1(N,M)=N_ab tensor M=0`,  `H_0(N,M)=M`.                       `(RDA7)`

Only `E^2_(1,0)=H_1(L,M)` contributes in total degree one.

**Theorem 2 (degree-one descent).**  Inflation along `pi` induces a natural
isomorphism

`H_1(D,M) ~= H_1(L,M)`.                                          `(RDA8)`

In particular `(RDA8)` applies to the augmentation lattice

`I_T=ker(Z[T]->Z)`                                                `(RDA9)`

of every finite quotient `L->T`.

## 4. The free-product overlap lattice

Apply the group-homology Mayer--Vietoris sequence to `L=Q_1*Q_2`.  Since
the edge group is trivial, `H_1(1,M)=0` and `H_0(1,M)=M`.  There is a
natural short exact sequence

`0 -> H_1(Q_1,M) directSum H_1(Q_2,M)`
`  -> H_1(L,M)`
`  -> B_M -> 0`,                                                  `(RDA10)`

where

`B_M=ker(M -> M_(Q_1) directSum M_(Q_2))`                        `(RDA11)`

and `M_(Q_i)` denotes coinvariants.  For `M=I_T`, the group `B_M` is a
free abelian group because it is a subgroup of the free abelian lattice
`I_T`.  Thus it is the only possible rational, unbounded-exponent part of
`H_1(L,I_T)` not already present in the two vertex groups.

The short exact sequence splits noncanonically as abelian groups, since
`B_M` is free, but no splitting is needed below.

## 5. The marked filling class has zero overlap component

Let `P_D` be the fixed finite presentation complex, let `X` be the regular
cover induced by a finite quotient `L->T`, and set

`M_X=S(H_2(P_X,Z)) <= H_2(P_D,Z)`.                               `(RDA12)`

The spherical subgroup of `H_2(P_D,Z)` lies in `M_X`, as proved in
`(ICD80)`.  Hence the transfer quotient, and in particular the relative
filling class

`kappa_(w,X) in H_2(P_D,Z)/M_X`,                                 `(RDA13)`

maps through group homology `H_2(D,Z)`.

The coefficient sequence

`0 -> I_T -> Z[T] -> Z -> 0`                                     `(RDA14)`

has connecting map

`delta_D:H_2(D,Z)->H_1(D,I_T)`.                                 `(RDA15)`

Under the injection of the transfer quotient into augmentation homology,
`kappa_(w,X)` is represented by `delta_D` of its group-homology lift.
This is the homological form of `(TFP15)`.

The quotient map `pi:D->L` and `(RDA14)` give a natural commutative square

`H_2(D,Z)  --delta_D--> H_1(D,I_T)`
`   | pi_*                    | ~=`
`   v                         v`
`H_2(L,Z)  --delta_L--> H_1(L,I_T)`.                             `(RDA16)`

For a free product,

`H_2(L,Z) ~= H_2(Q_1,Z) directSum H_2(Q_2,Z)`.                   `(RDA17)`

Naturality of the connecting map with respect to each vertex inclusion
puts `delta_L(H_2(Q_i,Z))` inside the image of `H_1(Q_i,I_T)` in
`H_1(L,I_T)`.

**Theorem 3 (vertex localization of the radical filling class).**  Under
the isomorphism `(RDA8)`, the augmentation-homology image of
`kappa_(w,X)` lies in

`V_T=im(H_1(Q_1,I_T) directSum H_1(Q_2,I_T)
        -> H_1(L,I_T))`.                                         `(RDA18)`

Equivalently, its image in the free overlap lattice `B_(I_T)` of `(RDA10)`
is zero.

**Proof.**  Apply `(RDA16)` to a group-homology lift of `(RDA13)`, use the
decomposition `(RDA17)`, and then use vertex naturality of `delta_L`.  End
proof.

This rules out the most obvious source of denominator growth: the radical
word cannot use the free Bass--Serre lattice created by `Q*Q`.  The stronger
chain-level argument in `FALSE_FIXED_KERNEL_COVER_NO_GO.md` in fact
shows that the particular marked class is zero, because the quotient kernel
is perfect.  Thus the vertex localization below describes the ambient
sector but the explicit radical word occupies the zero class in it.

## 6. Shapiro reduction to stabilizer homology

Restrict the finite `L`-set `T` to one vertex group `Q`.  Choose orbit
representatives `o`, with stabilizers `H_o<=Q`.  Then

`Z[T] ~= directSum_o Z[Q/H_o]`                                   `(RDA19)`

as `ZQ`-modules.  Shapiro's lemma and the long exact sequence of

`0 -> I_T -> Z[T] -> Z -> 0`                                     `(RDA20)`

give the exact segment

`directSum_o H_2(H_o,Z) -> H_2(Q,Z) -> H_1(Q,I_T)`
` -> directSum_o H_1(H_o,Z) -> H_1(Q,Z)`.                        `(RDA21)`

This is a fixed and computable arithmetic description of the vertex group
in `(RDA18)`.  In particular:

* rational surjectivity of the first arrow together with rational
  injectivity of the last arrow makes `H_1(Q,I_T)` finite;
* its torsion exponent is controlled by the integral cokernel and kernel in
  `(RDA21)`; and
* projective stabilizer calculations of `H_2(H_o,Z)` feed directly into
  the marked radical-double obstruction.

For the chosen arithmetic group `Q=SL_d(Z)`, the vertex group is always
finite.  Indeed `I_T tensor R` is a finite-dimensional orthogonal
representation, property `(T)` gives vanishing of degree-one real
cohomology, and finite-dimensional duality gives

`H_1(Q,I_T) tensor R=0`.                                         `(RDA21a)`

The integral homology group is finitely generated, so it is finite.  What
is not supplied by property `(T)` is a bound on its torsion exponent as
`T` varies.

For the projective charts already computed, the exact facts

`H_2(H_3,Z)=C_2^3`,  `H_2(H_5,Z)=C_2^3`,
`H_2(H_7,Z)=C_2^4`,                                              `(RDA22)`

and the rank-two rational sector at level `53` are therefore not merely
Hodge diagnostics.  They are inputs to the exact vertex sequence `(RDA21)`
that contains the only possible augmentation-homology location of the
relative radical filling class.

## 7. Consequences for TRUE and FALSE

For the explicit word and cover family, the later fixed-kernel no-go is
decisive: `kappa_(w,X)=0`, so none of the alternatives below is realized.
They remain useful for variants whose marked word does not lie in the
commutator subgroup of one fixed quotient kernel.

Let `E_T=exp(V_T)` when `V_T` is finite.  Theorem 3 implies

`E_T kappa_(w,X)=0`                                               `(RDA23)`

in the transfer quotient as far as modular holonomy can detect it.  Hence:

1. if the exponents `E_T` are uniformly bounded and one marked pairing
   remains nonzero at unbounded transferable moduli, Theorem 3 of
   `FALSE_TRANSFERRED_FILLING_PAIRING_FORMULA.md` proves FALSE;
2. if the exponent of the entire transfer quotient is uniformly bounded,
   `(TFP40)` instead bounds every fixed-cycle modulus and closes this route;
3. to prove TRUE for the explicit tower, it is enough to show that the
   vertex-localized class in `(RDA18)` is eventually invisible under every
   transferable Gram pairing; and
4. to prove FALSE, it is enough to retain one nonzero vertex-localized
   pairing while the ambient transfer quotient permits unbounded moduli.

The remaining finite calculation is now sharply localized: compute the two
maps adjacent to `H_1(Q,I_T)` in `(RDA21)`, then locate the image of the
fixed radical filling class.  The free-product overlap lattice and the
perfect Kun--Thom kernel play no further role in the marked numerator.
