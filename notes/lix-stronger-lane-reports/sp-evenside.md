### `GroupApproximation/CharClass/ParityPData.lean` (~470 lines)

The uniform theorem of sp-design §3.2, over an abstract pair of commutative rings.  No
cohomology, no `Y`, no rank, no stage.

* `ParityP.aProd`, `ParityP.bPart`, `ParityP.gammaProd` — the two Künneth components of a
  product `∏_{α ∈ L} γ_α` over a list of indices, and the product itself.
* `ParityP.IsDecomposable γ N` — **the decomposable part, as a property**: the additive
  subgroup of `H` generated by the products of *at least two* Chern classes of positive
  index whose indices sum to `N`.  This is the whole of what the argument needs to know
  about the universal Wu polynomial `E_{i+1}`, i.e. sp-design §3.3's L4a: `E_{i+1}` is
  `e_{ip+1}` with coefficient `1` plus such products.  The universal polynomials
  themselves are never written down, and no closed form is assumed (there is none at odd
  `p` in the two-factor shape `WuSymmetric.lean` enjoys at `p = 2`).
* `ParityP.bPart_eq_zero` — **L5**: the `z`-part of a decomposable monomial vanishes once
  every `b` at an earlier index in the same residue class does.  This is the one real
  induction in the file: strip factors off the list, carrying a counter for the multiples
  of `p` already removed, and use `a_q = 0` for `p ∤ q` to kill every branch in which the
  surviving `b`-index is not `≡ N (mod p)` and at most `N − p`.
* `ParityPData p R H` — the hypotheses as a structure, 17 fields, each with a docstring
  naming who discharges it.
* `ParityPData.b_step` — the inductive step, the `z`-component of the Wu relation at index
  `i`.
* `ParityPData.b_eq_zero` — **`b_{ip+1} = 0` for every `i`**; `b_eq_zero_of_mod` restates
  it as `N % p = 1`.  At `p = 2` this is `ParityData.b_odd_eq_zero`.
* `ParityPData.gamma_eq_zero`, `ParityPData.gamma_rank_eq_zero` — `γ_r(W) = 0` for
  `r ≡ 1 (mod p)` with `a_r = 0`, and the tower form `p ∣ n`, `p ∣ m` ⟹ `γ_{n+1+m}(W) = 0`.
  At `p = 2`, `n = 2` this is `ParityData.gamma_top_eq_zero` (`m` even, `r = m + 3`).
* `ParityP.pR_b_succ_of_instability`, `ParityP.pH_z_mul_of_cartan` — the two derivation
  helpers, so an instantiator who has instability in its general form and the Cartan
  formula discharges those two fields in one line each.

**Three things the theorem does not ask for**, each checked against the proof:

| not asked | why |
|---|---|
| `(p : R) = 0` | `ParityEven.lean` needs `2 = 0` to cancel `u + u`; this proof subtracts instead. The theorem is characteristic-free — the prime enters only through the arithmetic of the indices. |
| `p.Prime` | only `2 ≤ p` is used, once, to turn `c·p ≤ i·p + 1` into `c ≤ i`. |
| instability in general | only `P^i(b_{i+1}) = 0` is a field. The general form is `ParityP.Instability`, and the helper derives the field from it as soon as the weight of `z` is `≥ 2` (`n ≥ 1`). |

Likewise the Cartan formula is not a field: only `P^i(z·ι c) = z·ι(P^i c)` is, and `t`,
`x` never appear separately — only `z = t x`, so `H` may be taken commutative at odd `p`
without assuming graded commutativity anywhere.

### `GroupApproximation/CharClass/ParityPSlice.lean` (~110 lines)

Hypothesis **(A)** from the tower, and the top index.  This is sp-design §3.3's L1 + L3a.

* `ParityP.coeff_pow_expChar_eq_zero` — in characteristic `p` the coefficients of a `p`-th
  power are supported in indices divisible by `p`, via `Polynomial.map_frobenius_expand`
  and `Polynomial.coeff_expand`.  This replaces `ParityEvenSlice.lean`'s mod-2
  antidiagonal argument.
* `ParityP.sliceClass_eq_pow`, `ParityP.sliceClass_coeff_eq_zero_of_not_dvd` — the slice
  class `∏_j (1 + h_j)^{d_j}` is a `p`-th power when `p ∣ d_j` for every `j`, hence
  discharges the field `a_eq_zero`.
* `ParityPData.gamma_top_eq_zero_of_slice` — the tower-facing endpoint, the general-`p`
  replacement for `ParityData.gamma_top_eq_zero_of_slice`.  It reuses
  `ParityEvenSlice.sliceClass` and `sliceClass_coeff_eq_zero_of_lt` unchanged; those are
  already characteristic-free.

Only this file needs a characteristic hypothesis, and it takes the weakest one that names
the Frobenius: `[ExpChar R p]`.

### Why it is uniform in the stage

Nothing in either file mentions the stage, `m`, the number of projective factors, or `Y`.
The two arithmetic inputs are `p ∣ d_j` for every `j` and `p ∣ n + m`, and for the tower
`d_j = n · 2^j` (so `m = n(2^s − 1)`, `n + m = n · 2^s`) both follow from `p ∣ n` alone at
every stage.  A stage-by-stage proof was explicitly not acceptable; this one is
stage-free by construction rather than by a uniformity argument bolted on afterwards.

## TRAPS

* **`-DwarningAsError=true` on the `GroupApproximation` lib** (lakefile.toml, line 13).
  Any linter warning is a hard error, so an unused case binder from
  `induction … with | add u v hu hv ihu ihv` is fatal even though the proof is correct.
  The two non-recursive hypotheses of an `add`/`neg` closure constructor are exactly the
  ones a closure induction never uses; they must be written `_hu`, `_hv`.  Cost me nothing
  only because I checked the lakefile before the first probe.
* **`omega` abstracts `i * p` and `c * p` as unrelated atoms**, which is what makes the
  index arithmetic here workable at all — but `(i' + c) * p` is a *third* atom, so every
  step that needs distributivity has to `rw [add_mul]` first and only then call `omega`.
  Every `omega` in `ParityPData.lean` is preceded by either an explicit `add_mul` rewrite
  or an explicit `Nat.mul_le_mul` step for exactly this reason.
* **A closure predicate beats a `Finset`/`Multiset` of monomials.**  Writing the
  decomposable part of the Wu relation as an explicit indexed sum forces a coefficient
  function and a `Multiset.map`/`sum` bookkeeping layer; writing it as a four-constructor
  inductive `Prop` closed under `+` and `-` makes the consuming lemma a four-case
  `induction` with no big-operator API at all, and is strictly more general (any integral
  combination, not just a chosen enumeration).
* **`Polynomial.coeff_expand` has `R` implicit but `p` explicit**, and
  `Polynomial.map_frobenius_expand` has `p` explicit and the polynomial after it
  (`Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Expand.lean`: `variable (R : Type u) … (p q : ℕ)` at line 37
  is undone for `R` by `variable {R}` at line 46).  Reading the `variable` block alone
  gives the wrong arity for both.
