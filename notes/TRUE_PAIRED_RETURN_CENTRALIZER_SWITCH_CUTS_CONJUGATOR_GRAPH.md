# A paired-return centralizer switch cuts out the conjugator graph

## Status

This is a Cairn-only construction of the witness required by
`Higman.Star.ConjugatorGraph.graph_benign` in
`GroupApproximation/Higman/TransportStar.lean`.

The construction gives, explicitly,

* a finitely presented torsion-free group \(E\);
* an embedding \(P=F_3\times F_3\hookrightarrow E\);
* a five-generated subgroup \(L\leq E\);
* the exact intersection

\[
  \boxed{L\cap P=\Gamma=\texttt{Higman.Star.graphSub}.}
  \tag{0.1}
\]

Thus it gives the mathematical data of a
`TorsionFreeBenignWitness Star.graphSub`, hence of
`BenignTF Star.graphSub`, and therefore of the sole field
`ConjugatorGraph.graph_benign`.  The construction is not yet Lean code; no
Lean, build, MSI, or literature input was used.

The switch is unexpectedly central.  The obstruction in
`notes/FALSE_FINITE_CENTRAL_HNN_HIDDEN_LEGS_RETURN_ONLY_EDGE_JOIN.md`
applies to cutters generated by hidden conjugates of the **whole base**.  The
cutter below instead uses two proper graph lifts over a free stable-letter
group.  Their stable components cancel only for matched input words.  Their
common-edge intersection is infinite even though the centralized edge itself
is four-generated.

The proof has three exact intersection computations:

\[
  Q\cap M=\Delta,
  \qquad
  \Gamma\cap M=\Delta,
  \qquad
  \langle\Gamma,Q\rangle\cap P=\Gamma.
  \tag{0.2}
\]

The first is a free-product fold-kernel calculation, the second uses the first
coordinate quotient killing \(a\), and the third is the amalgam normal form.
All notation and repository dependencies are listed explicitly below.

## 1. The base and the target

Use the notation of `GroupApproximation/Higman/ConjugateBasis.lean` and
`GroupApproximation/Higman/TransportStar.lean`:

\[
  F_3=F(a,b,c),
  \qquad
  K=\langle b,c\rangle\leq F_3,
  \qquad
  P=F_3\times F_3.
\]

We identify \(K\) with the free group on \(b,c\).  This identification is
internal to the displayed free group: the map from the free group on two
letters to \(F_3\), sending its letters to \(b,c\), has a left inverse obtained
by killing \(a\), and its image is the defining closure
\(K=\langle b,c\rangle\).

For \(h\in K\), put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 x&=(a,1),\\
 \alpha(h)&=(h,1),\\
 r(h)&=(h,h),\\
 \gamma_h&=\alpha(h)^{-1}x r(h)=(h^{-1}ah,h).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Then

\[
  \Gamma=\langle\gamma_h:h\in K\rangle
\tag{1.2}
\]

is exactly `Star.graphSub` by `Star.graphSub_eq_closure`.
The theorem `Star.graphHom_injective` says that the map

\[
  g:\mathbb F(\{e_h:h\in K\})\longrightarrow P,
  \qquad e_h\longmapsto\gamma_h,
\tag{1.3}
\]

is injective.  We use this only to recognize explicitly written words in the
\(\gamma_h\)'s; no unproved basis assertion is needed.

Define a second homomorphism \(\beta:K\to P\) by

\[
  \beta(h)=x r(h)x^{-1}=(a h a^{-1},h),
  \tag{1.4}
\]

where the first `h` in the last display is the element \(h\in K\): explicitly
\(\beta(h)=(a h a^{-1},h)\).  Both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are homomorphisms.
Let

\[
  M=\langle\alpha(K),\beta(K)\rangle\leq P.
  \tag{1.5}
\]

Since \(K=\langle b,c\rangle\), the subgroup \(M\) is generated by the four
elements

\[
  \alpha(b),\ \alpha(c),\ \beta(b),\ \beta(c).
  \tag{1.6}
\]

In particular \(M\) is finitely generated.

Finally put

\[
  \delta_h=\alpha(h)^{-1}\beta(h)=\gamma_hx^{-1}.
  \tag{1.7}
\]

The last equality is literal:

\[
 \alpha(h)^{-1}\beta(h)
 =\alpha(h)^{-1}x r(h)x^{-1}
 =\gamma_hx^{-1}.
\]

## 2. The ambient group in its two equivalent forms

Let \(U=F(u_b,u_c)\) be the free group on two letters.  For
\(h\in K=F(b,c)\), write \(U_h\in U\) for the result of replacing \(b,c\) by
\(u_b,u_c\) in the reduced word for \(h\).  Thus

\[
  h\longmapsto U_h
\tag{2.1}
\]

is an isomorphism \(K\cong U\).

### 2.1 The iterated-HNN form

Starting from \(E_0=P\), adjoin \(u_b\) centralizing \(M\), then adjoin
\(u_c\) centralizing the transported copy of \(M\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_1&=\langle P,u_b\mid [u_b,m]=1\ (m\in M)\rangle,\\
 E=E_2&=\langle E_1,u_c\mid [u_c,m]=1\ (m\in M)\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is an iterated use of `Higman.CentHNN` from
`GroupApproximation/Higman/HNNCentralizer.lean`.  The canonical maps

\[
  P\hookrightarrow E_1\hookrightarrow E
\tag{2.3}
\]

are injective by `HNNExtension.of_injective`; henceforth we identify \(P\)
with its image in \(E\).

The subgroup generated by \(M,u_b,u_c\) is

\[
  C=\langle M,u_b,u_c\rangle\cong M\times U.
  \tag{2.4}
\]

Surjectivity of the displayed map is the definition of \(C\).  Injectivity is
the HNN normal form: a nontrivial reduced word in \(u_b,u_c\), with its
\(M\)-coefficient collected to one side, is still a nonempty reduced stable
word and cannot be trivial.  Equivalently, it follows from the amalgam model
below and `Amalgam.of_injective_push`.

### 2.2 The amalgam form

Embed \(M\) in \(P\) by inclusion and in \(C=M\times U\) by
\(m\mapsto(m,1)\).  Then

\[
  \boxed{E\cong P*_M C.}
  \tag{2.5}
\]

Indeed, both sides have the same presentation: start with \(P\), freely add
\(u_b,u_c\), and impose exactly that each new letter commute with every
element of \(M\), with no relation between \(u_b\) and \(u_c\).  Maps in both
directions are obtained from the HNN and pushout universal properties, and
they are inverse because they agree on \(P,u_b,u_c\).

The repository supplies the normal form on the right side through
`Monoid.PushoutI`; `Higman.Amalgam.pushEquiv` identifies the presented amalgam
with that normal-form model, and `Higman.Amalgam.of_injective_push` gives the
factor embeddings.  We use (2.2) for finite presentation and torsion, and
(2.5) for the last intersection descent.

## 3. The paired switch and its five generators

Inside \(E\), define

\[
  v_h=x^{-1}U_hx.
  \tag{3.1}
\]

Since every \(U_h\) commutes with \(M\), equation (1.4) gives

\[
  [v_h,r(k)]=1
  \qquad(h,k\in K):
  \tag{3.2}
\]

conjugating this commutator by \(x\) turns it into
\([U_h,\beta(k)]=1\).

Define the two lifted paths

\[
  p_h=\alpha(h)U_h,
  \qquad
  q_h=r(h)v_h.
  \tag{3.3}
\]

Because \(U(K)\) commutes with \(\alpha(K)\leq M\), the map
\(h\mapsto p_h\) is a homomorphism.  Because (3.2) holds, the map
\(h\mapsto q_h\) is also a homomorphism.  Consequently every \(p_h\) is a word
in \(p_b,p_c\), and every \(q_h\) is the same word in \(q_b,q_c\).

Set

\[
  L=\langle x,p_b,p_c,q_b,q_c\rangle\leq E.
  \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus \(L\) is generated by five explicit elements.

### Proposition 3.1 — every matched path returns the desired value

For every \(h\in K\),

\[
  \boxed{p_h^{-1}xq_h=\gamma_h.}
  \tag{3.5}
\]

### Proof

Using (3.1)--(3.3), commutation of \(U_h\) with \(\alpha(h),\beta(h)\), and
\(\beta(h)=xr(h)x^{-1}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_h^{-1}xq_h
 &=U_h^{-1}\alpha(h)^{-1}x r(h)x^{-1}U_hx\\
 &=U_h^{-1}\alpha(h)^{-1}\beta(h)U_hx\\
 &=\alpha(h)^{-1}\beta(h)x\\
 &=\gamma_h.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore \(\gamma_h\in L\cap P\) for every \(h\), and

\[
  \Gamma\leq L\cap P.
  \tag{3.6}
\]

The stable word \(U_h\) is the synchronization token.  A left path and a
right path with the same input word cancel through \(x\); two different input
words have different free \(U\)-components and do not cancel.

## 4. The transition subgroup and its exact edge intersection

Conjugate the right lift by the cutter element \(x\):

\[
  xq_hx^{-1}=U_h\beta(h)\in C=M\times U.
  \tag{4.1}
\]

The left lift is already in \(C\):

\[
  p_h=U_h\alpha(h).
  \tag{4.2}
\]

Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat A&=\{U_h\alpha(h):h\in K\}\leq C,\\
 \widehat B&=\{U_h\beta(h):h\in K\}\leq C,\\
 Q&=\langle\widehat A,\widehat B\rangle\leq C.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Both displayed sets are subgroups, since they are graphs of homomorphisms over
the common free factor \(U\).  From (3.4), (4.1), and (4.2),

\[
  Q\leq L,
  \qquad
  L=\langle x,Q\rangle.
  \tag{4.4}
\]

Let

\[
  \Delta=\left\langle\!\left\langle
     \delta_b,\delta_c
  \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_M
\tag{4.5}
\]

be the normal closure in \(M\).

This is deliberately a **normal closure in \(M\)**, not the ordinary
two-generated subgroup \(\langle\delta_b,\delta_c\rangle\).  Lemma 5.3 will
prove the sharper identification

\[
  \Delta
   =\left\langle\!\left\langle\delta_b,\delta_c
     \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_M
   =\langle\delta_h:h\in K\rangle.
  \tag{4.5a}
\]

The equality is supplied by all \(M\)-conjugates and the Cayley-edge
telescoping, not by \(\delta_b,\delta_c\) alone.

### Theorem 4.1 — transition matching returns exactly \(\Delta\)

Under the identification \(C=M\times U\),

\[
  \boxed{Q\cap M=\Delta.}
  \tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Let \(K_0*K_1\) be the free product of two copies of \(K\).  Define

\[
 \Theta:K_0*K_1\longrightarrow M\times U
\tag{4.7}
\]

by

\[
 h\in K_0\longmapsto(\alpha(h),U_h),
 \qquad
 h\in K_1\longmapsto(\beta(h),U_h).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Its range is \(Q\).  Projection to \(U\cong K\) is the fold map

\[
  \rho:K_0*K_1\longrightarrow K
\tag{4.9}
\]

which is the identity on both free factors.

Because \(K=F(b,c)\), the kernel of \(\rho\) is the normal closure of the two
elements

\[
  b_0^{-1}b_1,
  \qquad
  c_0^{-1}c_1.
  \tag{4.10}
\]

This is a direct presentation calculation: quotienting \(K_0*K_1\) by those
two relations identifies the two free bases, so the quotient is \(K\), and
the quotient map is \(\rho\).

The \(M\)-components of the two elements in (4.10) are

\[
  \alpha(b)^{-1}\beta(b)=\delta_b,
  \qquad
  \alpha(c)^{-1}\beta(c)=\delta_c.
  \tag{4.11}
\]

Conjugation by the two factor images in (4.8) acts on an element of \(M\) by
conjugation with \(\alpha(K)\) or \(\beta(K)\), because the \(U\)-component
commutes with \(M\).  These two subgroups generate \(M\).  Hence the
\(M\)-components of all elements of \(\ker\rho\) form exactly the
\(M\)-normal closure of \(\delta_b,\delta_c\), namely \(\Delta\).

An element of \(Q=\operatorname{range}\Theta\) lies in the factor
\(M=M\times1\) exactly when its \(U\)-component is trivial, equivalently when
one (hence every) representing word lies in \(\ker\rho\).  Therefore
\(Q\cap M=\Delta\). \(\square\)

This proof also shows why the old split-path leakage is absent.  The stable
projection remembers the entire reduced input word in the free group \(U\),
not only an exponent sum or a finite state.  A mismatch cannot enter \(M\).

## 5. The same \(\Delta\) is exactly \(\Gamma\cap M\)

Let

\[
  \pi:F_3\longrightarrow K
\tag{5.1}
\]

be the retraction killing \(a\) and fixing \(b,c\).  On \(M\), define

\[
  \psi=\pi\circ\operatorname{fst}:M\longrightarrow K.
  \tag{5.2}
\]

Then

\[
  \psi(\alpha(h))=h=\psi(\beta(h)).
  \tag{5.3}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — the fold kernel inside \(M\)

\[
  \boxed{\ker\psi=\Delta.}
  \tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

The homomorphism

\[
  \mu:K_0*K_1\longrightarrow M
\tag{5.5}
\]

induced by \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) is surjective by the definition of \(M\),
and \(\psi\mu=\rho\).  If \(m\in\ker\psi\), choose \(w\) with
\(\mu(w)=m\).  Then \(\rho(w)=\psi(m)=1\), so (4.10)--(4.11) put
\(m\in\Delta\).  Conversely (5.3) makes both \(\delta_b\) and \(\delta_c\)
lie in \(\ker\psi\), and the kernel is normal in \(M\).  Thus
\(\Delta\leq\ker\psi\). \(\square\)

It remains to prove that \(\Delta\) really lies in the graph.  This is where
the free-basis theorem `Star.graphHom_injective` makes the check exact.

### Lemma 5.2 — Cayley-edge conjugates are graph words

For \(h,k\in K\),

\[
  \alpha(k)\delta_h\alpha(k)^{-1}
   =g\bigl(e_{h k^{-1}}e_{k^{-1}}^{-1}\bigr)\in\Gamma.
  \tag{5.6}
\]

### Proof

The second coordinate of the left side is \(h\).  Its first coordinate is

\[
 k(h^{-1}ah\,a^{-1})k^{-1}
 =(h k^{-1})^{-1}a(h k^{-1})
  \bigl((k^{-1})^{-1}a(k^{-1})\bigr)^{-1}.
\]

This is exactly the first coordinate of the graph word on the right.  Its
second coordinate is

\[
  (h k^{-1})(k^{-1})^{-1}=h.
\]

Thus both coordinates agree. \(\square\)

### Lemma 5.3 — the normal closure is exactly the full difference subgroup

\[
  \boxed{\Delta=\langle\delta_h:h\in K\rangle\leq\Gamma.}
  \tag{5.7}
\]

### Proof

Let \(\Delta_0\) be the subgroup generated by the elements in (5.6) with
\(h\in\{b,c\}\) and arbitrary \(k\in K\).  Lemma 5.2 gives
\(\Delta_0\leq\Gamma\), and \(\Delta_0\) is normalized by \(\alpha(K)\) by
construction.

These generators are the oriented Cayley-edge differences

\[
  g(e_{s q}e_q^{-1}),
  \qquad s\in\{b,c\},\ q\in K,
  \tag{5.8}
\]

after setting \(q=k^{-1}\).  Their inverses supply the edges labelled
\(b^{-1},c^{-1}\).  A path in the left Cayley graph from \(1\) to any
\(h\in K\) telescopes:

\[
 (e_{h_n}e_{h_{n-1}}^{-1})
 (e_{h_{n-1}}e_{h_{n-2}}^{-1})\cdots
 (e_{h_1}e_1^{-1})=e_h e_1^{-1}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
  \delta_h=g(e_he_1^{-1})\in\Delta_0
  \qquad(h\in K).
  \tag{5.10}
\]

Conversely, every Cayley-edge generator (5.8) is

\[
  g(e_{s q}e_q^{-1})=\delta_{s q}\delta_q^{-1},
  \tag{5.10a}
\]

so \(\Delta_0\leq\langle\delta_h:h\in K\rangle\).  Together with (5.10),
this gives

\[
  \Delta_0=\langle\delta_h:h\in K\rangle.
  \tag{5.10b}
\]

In particular \(\delta_k\in\Delta_0\), and

\[
  \beta(k)=\alpha(k)\delta_k.
  \tag{5.11}
\]

Since \(\Delta_0\) is normalized by \(\alpha(K)\) and every
\(\delta_k\in\Delta_0\), equation (5.11) shows that it is also normalized by
\(\beta(K)\).  Hence it is normal in
\(M=\langle\alpha(K),\beta(K)\rangle\).  It contains \(\delta_b,\delta_c\),
so the minimality of their \(M\)-normal closure gives
\(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\).  Conversely \(\Delta_0\leq\Delta\), since every
generator (5.6) used to define \(\Delta_0\) is an \(M\)-conjugate of
\(\delta_b\) or \(\delta_c\).  Thus
\(\Delta=\Delta_0\leq\Gamma\). \(\square\)

### Theorem 5.4 — exact graph-edge intersection

\[
  \boxed{\Gamma\cap M=\Delta.}
  \tag{5.12}
\]

### Proof

Lemma 5.3 gives \(\Delta\leq\Gamma\cap M\).  Conversely, every first
coordinate of an element of \(\Gamma\) is a product of conjugates
\(h^{-1}ah\), so \(\pi\) kills it.  Hence

\[
  \Gamma\cap M\leq\ker\psi=\Delta
\]

by Lemma 5.1. \(\square\)

Combining Theorems 4.1 and 5.4 gives the matched crossing identity

\[
  \boxed{Q\cap M=\Gamma\cap M=\Delta.}
  \tag{5.13}
\]

## 6. The final amalgam descent

We use the following elementary consequence of the repository's amalgam
normal form.

### Lemma 6.1 — matched subgroups in an amalgam

Let \(A*_M B\) be an amalgam with injective factor maps, and let
\(Z\leq A\), \(Q\leq B\).  If

\[
  Z\cap M=Q\cap M=\Delta,
  \tag{6.1}
\]

then

\[
  \boxed{\langle Z,Q\rangle\cap A=Z.}
  \tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Write an element of \(\langle Z,Q\rangle\) as an alternating word in
\(Z\)- and \(Q\)-syllables, combining adjacent syllables from the same factor.
If a \(Q\)-syllable belongs to \(M\), then it belongs to
\(Q\cap M=\Delta\leq Z\), so it can be absorbed into its neighboring
\(Z\)-syllables (or into the single adjacent one at an endpoint).  If a
\(Z\)-syllable adjacent to a \(Q\)-syllable belongs to \(M\), then it belongs
to \(Z\cap M=\Delta\leq Q\), so it is absorbed into that \(Q\)-syllable; in
the internal case the two neighboring \(Q\)-syllables merge.  Either operation
strictly shortens the word.

After all such shortenings, either no \(Q\)-syllable remains, in which case the
element lies in \(Z\), or the alternating word is reduced and has a letter from
the \(B\)-factor outside \(M\).  The latter word cannot lie in the \(A\)-factor:
if it equalled \(a\in A\), append the \(A\)-letter \(a^{-1}\), absorb any
resulting endpoint pinch, and obtain a nonempty reduced word representing the
identity.  This contradicts
`Monoid.PushoutI.Reduced.eq_empty_of_mem_range`, the normal-form theorem used
throughout `Higman/AmalgamPushout.lean` and
`Higman/AmalgamTorsion.lean`.  Therefore every element of
\(\langle Z,Q\rangle\cap A\) lies in \(Z\).  The reverse inclusion is
immediate. \(\square\)

Apply Lemma 6.1 to the amalgam (2.5) with

\[
  A=P,
  \qquad B=C,
  \qquad Z=\Gamma.
\]

Equation (5.13) supplies its hypothesis.  Therefore

\[
  \langle\Gamma,Q\rangle\cap P=\Gamma.
  \tag{6.3}
\]

By (3.6), \(\Gamma\leq L\), and by (4.4), \(L=\langle x,Q\rangle\) with
\(x=\gamma_1\in\Gamma\).  Hence

\[
  L=\langle\Gamma,Q\rangle.
  \tag{6.4}
\]

Equations (6.3)--(6.4) prove the promised exact intersection

\[
  \boxed{L\cap P=\Gamma.}
  \tag{6.5}
\]

## 7. The finiteness and torsion checks

All clauses of `TorsionFreeBenignWitness Star.graphSub` are now explicit.

### 7.1 The base is finitely presented and torsion-free

The group \(F_3\) is a finite-rank free group, hence finitely presented in the
repository's group-presentation framework, and
the repository instance `ProductFinitePresentation.instProd` gives finite
presentation of

\[
  P=F_3\times F_3.
\]

Free groups are power-torsion-free, and `IsPowerTorsionFree.prod` gives the
same property for \(P\).

### 7.2 The edge is finitely generated

Equation (1.6) is a four-element generating set for \(M\).  Thus every use of
the HNN finite-presentation theorem has the required `Group.FG` instance.

### 7.3 The overgroup is finitely presented

Apply
`HNNFinitePresentation.isFinitelyPresented_hnnExtension` first to the HNN
extension adjoining \(u_b\) along \(M\), then to the extension adjoining
\(u_c\) along the transported copy of \(M\).  It follows that \(E\) in (2.2)
is finitely presented.

### 7.4 The overgroup is torsion-free

Apply `HNNBritton.isPowerTorsionFree_hnn` at the same two stages, starting from
the power-torsion-free group \(P\).  Hence \(E\) is power-torsion-free.

### 7.5 The embedding is injective

The embedding \(P\hookrightarrow E\) is the composite of the two canonical
HNN base maps, each injective by `HNNExtension.of_injective`.

### 7.6 The cutter is finitely generated

Equation (3.4) is the explicit five-element generating set

\[
  x,p_b,p_c,q_b,q_c
\]

for \(L\).  Thus `L.FG` follows directly from `Subgroup.fg_iff`.

### 7.7 The comap is the target

Equation (6.5), together with injectivity of the embedding, is exactly

\[
  L.\mathrm{comap}(P\hookrightarrow E)=\texttt{Star.graphSub}.
\]

Consequently the data define a `BenignWitness Star.graphSub`; the torsion
calculation upgrades it to a `TorsionFreeBenignWitness Star.graphSub`.  In the
repository's proposition wrappers this is

\[
  \boxed{\texttt{BenignTF Star.graphSub}.}
  \tag{7.1}
\]

That proposition is precisely the field
`Higman.Star.ConjugatorGraph.graph_benign`.  Feeding it to
`Star.unConjugation_of_graphBenign` supplies
`Higman.Transport.UnConjugation`, the remaining Section 5 interface.

## 8. Why the switch evades the proved obstructions

The construction does not expose \(\alpha(b),\alpha(c),r(b),r(c)\) as cutter
elements.  It exposes only their stable lifts

\[
  \alpha(s)u_s,
  \qquad
  r(s)x^{-1}u_sx.
\]

The stable projection to the free group \(U\) prevents mismatched left and
right paths from returning to the edge.  When the paths match, their quotient
is \(\delta_h\), which is not leakage: Theorems 4.1 and 5.4 show that the full
matched return subgroup is exactly the part of the target graph already lying
in the edge.  Lemma 6.1 then prevents any additional return to \(P\).

Accordingly:

* the split-axis identity does not apply, because neither transition leg lies
  in \(L\cap P\);
* the hidden-whole-base HNN obstruction does not apply, because the cutter
  uses the proper graph subgroups \(\widehat A,\widehat B\leq M\times U\), not
  conjugates of \(P\);
* no infinite subgroup is used as an HNN edge: \(M\) itself is four-generated;
* no homomorphism un-conjugates inside \(F_3\): the nonhomomorphic value is
  carried by the paired subgroup intersection \(Q\cap M\), not by a map out of
  \(F_3\).

The decisive resource is therefore not a noncentral edge isomorphism.  It is a
free stable-word synchronization channel combined with two proper graph lifts
over the same channel.  Two central finite-rank HNN letters suffice.
