# An amenable relative core cannot carry the compressor wall

Date: 2026-08-12

## 1. Outcome

Let \(\Gamma<G\) be countable, assume that \(\Gamma\) has property
\((T)\), and fix

\[
 t\Gamma t^{-1}\leq \Gamma,
 \qquad h=t^{-1}\gamma t,\quad \gamma\in\Gamma.
 \tag{ARC1}
\]

Let \((M,\tau)\) be a finite tracial von Neumann algebra and let
\(\pi:G\to U(M)\) be a homomorphism.  Put

\[
 N=\pi(\Gamma)''.
 \tag{ARC2}
\]

The following tempting route to the relative-commutant endpoint is
impossible.

**Theorem 1 (amenable-core no-go).**  If \(N\) is amenable, then

\[
 \pi(t)N\pi(t)^*=N,
 \qquad \pi(h)\in N,
 \qquad
 \pi(\Gamma)'\cap M\subseteq \pi(h)'\cap M.
 \tag{ARC3}
\]

Thus one cannot first make the relative algebra amenable and then use
Connes-embeddable amalgamation over that algebra to adjoin the missing
commutant unitary.  Any successful wall must retain a nonamenable relative
core or arise only in an extrinsic relative commutant.

The point not covered by the earlier almost-periodic no-go is the possible
diffuse center of an amenable type-I representation.  Property \((T)\)
removes that possibility: amenable factorial traces are finite-dimensional,
and finite-dimensional irreducible representations are locally rigid, so
the center of `(ARC2)` is atomic.  Trace preservation then prevents the
compressor from acting as a proper unilateral shift on its atoms.

## 2. Amenable factorial fibers are finite-dimensional

We use two standard consequences of property \((T)\).

**Lemma 2.**  Let \(\sigma:\Gamma\to U(P)\) generate an amenable finite
factor \(P\).  Then \(P\) is finite-dimensional.

**Proof.**  Amenability of \(P\) implies that the conjugation/coarse
representation associated with \(\sigma\) has almost invariant unit
vectors.  Property \((T)\) supplies a nonzero invariant vector.  Equivalently,
the corresponding factorial representation contains a nonzero
finite-dimensional subrepresentation.  The projection onto that subspace is
a nonzero finite-rank projection in the commutant.  A finite factor whose
commutant contains such a projection is type I finite, hence is a matrix
algebra.  End proof.

**Lemma 3.**  For every fixed \(d\), property \((T)\) gives only finitely
many equivalence classes of irreducible \(d\)-dimensional unitary
representations of \(\Gamma\).

**Proof.**  Fix a finite Kazhdan set.  If two irreducible
\(d\)-dimensional representations are sufficiently close on that set after
choosing bases, the identity operator is almost invariant in their
left--right representation on \(M_d(\mathbf C)\).  Property \((T)\) gives a
nonzero exact intertwiner, and irreducibility makes it a unitary
equivalence.  Thus every point of the compact quotient

\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,U(d))/U(d)
\]

is isolated, with a uniform Kazhdan neighborhood.  Compactness gives
finiteness.  End proof.

Decompose the amenable algebra \(N\) centrally.  Amenability passes to
almost every factorial fiber, so Lemma 2 makes every fiber a matrix
algebra.  The representation of \(\Gamma\) in a fiber is irreducible as an
algebra representation.  Lemma 3, followed by the countable union over
\(d\), shows that only countably many factorial characters occur.

The central decomposition of the algebra generated by \(\pi(\Gamma)\)
does not retain a separate diffuse multiplicity coordinate on which all
group generators have the same factorial character.  Consequently

\[
 N\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{d_i}(\mathbf C),
 \qquad I\text{ countable},
 \tag{ARC4}
\]

with trace

\[
 \tau|_N=\sum_{i\in I}a_i\operatorname{tr}_{d_i},
 \qquad a_i>0,\quad \sum_i a_i=1.
 \tag{ARC5}
\]

In particular the amenable relative core is atomic type I, not merely
hyperfinite.

## 3. Trace-preserving endomorphisms of the atomic core are onto

We record the elementary finite-measure fact needed for the compressor.

**Lemma 4.**  Let \(N\) have the form `(ARC4)--(ARC5)`, and let
\(\alpha:N\to N\) be a normal unital injective trace-preserving
star-homomorphism.  Then \(\alpha\) is onto.

**Proof.**  A coordinate representation

\[
 \alpha_j:N\longrightarrow M_{d_j}(\mathbf C)
\]

need not initially select only one input factor: it may be a finite direct
sum.  Write \(m_{ji}\) for the multiplicity with which the defining
representation of \(M_{d_i}(\mathbf C)\) occurs in \(\alpha_j\).  Unitality
and trace preservation give, respectively,

\[
 d_j=\sum_i m_{ji}d_i,
 \qquad
 a_i=\sum_j a_jm_{ji}\frac{d_i}{d_j}.
 \tag{ARC6}
\]

Define

\[
 p_{ij}=\frac{a_jm_{ji}d_i}{a_i d_j}.
 \tag{ARC7}
\]

The second identity in `(ARC6)` says that \(p\) is a Markov kernel.  The
first says that \((a_i)_i\) is stationary:

\[
 \sum_i a_i p_{ij}=a_j.
 \tag{ARC8}
\]

Moreover, \(p_{ij}>0\) implies \(d_j\geq d_i\), since a copy of
\(M_{d_i}(\mathbf C)\) occurs inside the \(d_j\)-dimensional coordinate.
Thus the integer-valued matrix size is nondecreasing along this stationary
Markov chain.  Stationarity forces it to be constant almost surely.  One
quick proof is to apply stationarity to every sublevel set
\(\{i:d_i\leq r\}\): a nondecreasing stationary process cannot cross any
integer threshold with positive probability.  Hence

\[
 p_{ij}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_i=d_j.
 \tag{ARC9}
\]

The dimension identity in `(ARC6)` now shows that every output coordinate
contains exactly one input coordinate, with multiplicity one.  We therefore
obtain a map \(f:I\to I\) satisfying

\[
 a_i=\sum_{j:f(j)=i}a_j.
 \tag{ARC10}
\]

The atomic probability measure \(\sum_i a_i\delta_i\) is invariant under
this deterministic map.  Every positive-mass atom is recurrent and hence
periodic.  Equation `(ARC10)` leaves no positive-mass branch feeding a
periodic orbit.  Thus \(f\) is a permutation of \(I\), decomposed into
finite cycles.  Every coordinate map is an automorphism, and therefore
\(\alpha(N)=N\).  End proof.

## 4. Proof of Theorem 1

Put \(u=\pi(t)\).  The compression relation in `(ARC1)` gives

\[
 \alpha=\operatorname{Ad}(u)|_N:N\longrightarrow N.
 \tag{ARC11}
\]

This is a normal unital injective trace-preserving star-homomorphism.  By
Section 2, \(N\) is atomic type I, and Lemma 4 makes `(ARC11)` onto.  Thus

\[
 uNu^*=N=u^*Nu.
 \tag{ARC12}
\]

Since \(\pi(\gamma)\in N\),

\[
 \pi(h)=u^*\pi(\gamma)u\in N.
 \tag{ARC13}
\]

Every member of \(\pi(\Gamma)'\cap M=N'\cap M\) commutes with `(ARC13)`,
which proves `(ARC3)`.  End proof.

## 5. Why the discarded shortcut was otherwise valid

The no-go is sharp enough to close a real proposed construction.  Suppose
one had a Connes-embeddable \(M\) and a representation \(\pi\) for which
\(N=\pi(\Gamma)''\) were amenable and \(x=\pi(h)\notin N\).  Form

\[
 \mathcal A=M*_N\bigl(N\mathbin{\overline\otimes}M_2(\mathbf C)\bigr)
 \tag{ARC14}
\]

with the trace-preserving expectations, and let

\[
 v=1\otimes
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
 \tag{ARC15}
\]

in the second factor.  Then \(v\in N'\cap\mathcal A\).  If
\(x_0=x-E_N(x)\), amalgamated freeness gives the exact formula

\[
 \|[v,x]\|_2^2=2\|x_0\|_2^2.
 \tag{ARC16}
\]

Indeed the mixed alternating trace
\(\tau(x_0^*vx_0v)\) vanishes.  Brown--Dykema--Jung, Corollary 4.5 of
*Free Entropy Dimension in Amalgamated Free Products*
([arXiv:math/0609080](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609080)), makes `(ARC14)`
Connes embeddable when its two factors have separable predual and are
Connes embeddable.  Thus `(ARC14)--(ARC16)` would have completed the
relative-bicommutant endpoint immediately.

Theorem 1 shows exactly why this cannot occur for a Kazhdan compressor:
amenability of \(N\) forces \(x\in N\), making the right side of `(ARC16)`
zero.

## 6. Surviving target

For the framed Kun--Thom pair, every successful representation must satisfy

\[
 \pi(\Gamma)''\text{ nonamenable}
 \tag{ARC17}
\]

and nevertheless have a strict extrinsic commutant wall

\[
 \pi(\Gamma)'\cap M\not\subseteq\pi(h)'\cap M.
 \tag{ARC18}
\]

This rules out not only finite and almost-periodic cores, but also arbitrary
amenable direct-integral cores and the otherwise automatic hyperfinite-
amalgamation construction.  The one-number escape criterion remains live
precisely because its finite \(\Gamma\)-algebras may converge to a
nonamenable core while retaining nonliftable asymptotic commutant mass.
