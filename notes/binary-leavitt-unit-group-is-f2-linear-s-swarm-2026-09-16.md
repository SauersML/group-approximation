## What was proven

**Result.** `Gamma_b(A)` is sofic for every cylinder `A`.
- Claim: `research/right-child-exchange-group-is-sofic.md`.
- Route: `research/right-child-exchange-group-sofic-proof.md`, requiring three established claims:
  `depth-monotone-leavitt-subalgebras-are-stably-finite`, `sofic-kernel-amenable-quotient-permanence` and
  `sofic-subgroups-carry-independent-cylinder-defects`.
- Artifact: `research/artifacts/right-child-exchange-group-soficity-2026-09-16.md`.

**Proof in five lines.**
1. `N_b = <b^j s b^-j : j in Z, s in {x, y, x1, y1}>` is normal with cyclic quotient. Finite subsets lie in conjugates of
   `Lambda_n = <b^-j s b^j : 0 <= j <= n>`.
2. In the frame `(0,1)`, `b = diag(1, beta)` with `beta = [[s_0, s_1t_1],[0,t_0]]`, and for `g = [[1,m],[0,d]]`,
   `beta^-1 g beta = [[1, t_0 m t_0],[0, s_0 d t_0 + s_1t_1 + s_1t_1 m t_0]]`. Closed forms follow by induction.
3. Grade `R` by `Z^2` (`deg s_0 = (1,0)`, `deg s_1 = (0,1)`). All generators of `Lambda_n` lie in `R^ω_≤` for
   `ω = (1, n)`. The weight is sharp, and `b` lies in no one-sided weighted part.
4. So `Lambda_n` is residually finite by the depth-monotone theorem. Hence `N_b` is locally residually finite, hence
   sofic, and `Gamma_b` is sofic-by-cyclic.
5. `Gamma_b(A) × iota_B(D_8)` is sofic, so it carries a fixed-point-free model with `rk(D_A D_B) = (3/8)^2`, that is
   `theta = 1`.

**General form (weight drift).** Let `G = <S, b>` over a finite field with `S` symmetric. If every finite window of
`b`-conjugates of `S` lies in one weighted subalgebra `R^(ω_n)_≤`, or in one `R^(ω_n)_≥`, then `G` is sofic.

**Consequences.**
- The "free subgroup keeps `Gamma_b` a live candidate" sentence of the 2026-09-12 artifact is superseded: soficity is
  all that matters, and it holds.
- `<x, y, u, beta> ≅ <y, x1, y1, b>` is sofic too.
- Remaining candidates for a relation-only certificate: `EL_3(J)` (`binary-jacobson-el3-is-sofic`, open), `R^x` itself,
  or other nonsofic subgroups containing two disjoint frames that are not covered by one drifting conjugator.

## Approaches, and exactly where each dies

1. **Amenability of `Gamma' = <x, y, u, beta>` (reduction proved, decision not reached).**
   - **The reduction.** Let `H_0 = <phi^j(g) : j >= 0, g in {u, x, y}>` with `phi(g) = beta^-1 g beta`. Then
     `phi(H_0) ⊆ H_0`, and `K = ∪_j beta^j H_0 beta^-j` is an ascending union, normal in `Gamma'`, with cyclic quotient.
     So:
     - `Gamma'` is amenable iff `H_0` is, and `F_2 <= Gamma'` iff `F_2 <= H_0`.
     - Every element of `H_0` has the form `[[1,m],[0,d]]`. The corner map `d` is a homomorphism with abelian kernel of
       exponent 2, and a free subgroup meets an abelian normal subgroup trivially. So both questions reduce to
       `D_0 = d(H_0)`.
   - **Generators of `D_0`.** Call `j` the level of a point `0^j 1 y`.
     - `a_k = d(phi^k y) = 1 + S[0^(k+1)]T[0^k 1]` adds the level-`k` point to the merged point `0^(k+1) y`. It has
       degree zero, so it lies in the locally matricial `∪ M_(2^n)(F_2)`.
     - `b_k = d(phi^k u) = 1 + Σ_(i<k) S[0^(k-1-i)1]T[0^(k+i)1]` adds level `j in [k, 2k-1]` to level `2k-1-j`.
     - `c_k = d(phi^k x) = 1 + Σ_(i<k) S[0^(k-1-i)1]T[0^(k+1+i)1]` adds level `j in [k+1, 2k]` to level `2k-j`.
       This corrects the level ranges stated earlier in this lane.
     - `B = <b_k, c_k>` is finitary unitriangular in the level basis, hence locally finite. `A = <a_k>` is locally
       finite.
   - **Where it dies.** `D_0` is generated by two locally finite subgroups, and nothing found forces amenability or
     produces a free pair.
   - **Numerics.** `pairfree.py` computes sphere sizes, with `G = u u_2` and `u_2 = beta y beta^-1`. `<G, beta>` matches
     the free sphere counts `4·3^(r-1)` through `r = 8`, and `<G, beta G beta^-1>` through `r = 7`. This is evidence
     only. `growth.py`, `relsearch.py`, `vpart.py`, `dpart.py`, `dyn.py` and `check_gens.py` were exploratory (growth,
     short relations, Thompson-`V` elements in balls, corner parts, vector dynamics of `G`).
2. **Leading-term certificates and vector ping-pong for freeness.** Tried: lowest-degree parts as in the `D_inf` proof,
   and ping-pong on basis vectors. Neither produced a certificate, and no obstruction to either is claimed.
3. **Orbit BFS on vectors (`orbits.py`).** Dies: the vector sets reached from `δ_z` grow too fast to decide finiteness.
   It was replaced by support closure on point sets (`support_closure.py`), which does terminate. For `n <= 4` and 24
   test points each, the closure is finite whenever the tail has a `0`-run of length at least `2n` after a `1`. This is a
   cross-check of local finiteness of `Lambda_n` on points, not used in the proof.
4. **The pivot, which works.** Soficity of `Gamma_b` needs neither amenability nor residual finiteness of `Gamma_b`,
   only local residual finiteness of `N_b`. The frame closed forms put each window `Lambda_n` into one depth-monotone
   weighted unit group (`check_weights.py`: all generators for `n <= 5`, sharpness for `n = 2..5`).

## What remains open

- The hole itself: `theta < 1` has no candidate subgroup left among those recorded, except `EL_3(J)` and `R^x`.
- Amenability of, and free subgroups in, `<x, y, u, beta>`, equivalently of `D_0 = <a_k, b_k, c_k>`.
- Residual finiteness and LEF of `Gamma_b`. No single weighted unit group contains `N_b`.
- Whether weight drift extends to groups needing several conjugating elements.
