## Setup

Problem 2.16 (Zaremsky's problem list, as recorded in the repo root node) asks
whether, for a cloning system on finite groups `G_n`, the Thompson-like group
`T(G_*)` is co-context-free and whether it embeds in `V`. Lehnert's conjecture
(current form due to Bleak–Matucci–Neunhöffer) says `V` is a universal coCF group.
So an embedding in `V` settles coCF for finitely generated subgroups, and a coCF
`T(G_*)` that does not embed in `V` would be a counterexample.

## 1. Status update (literature gate)

- **BDPR, arXiv:2608.02111v1** (C. Bodart, D. D'Angeli, D. Perego, E. Rodaro,
  submitted 3 Aug 2026; PDF read 2026-09-16).
  - Theorem A (p. 2): a finitely generated group embeds in `V` iff it is CF-TR, iff
    it admits a faithful context-free action (Defs 1.5–1.8, pp. 6–7; Theorem 2.9,
    p. 17).
  - Theorem B / Theorem 3.2 (pp. 2, 19): `V_(H,θ)` embeds in `V` for every finite
    `H` and every `θ ∈ End(H)`. Theorem B also covers FSS groups (Theorem 3.5).
  - Their own wording (p. 3): this "makes significant progress towards answering
    Question 2.16 of Zaremsky". They do not claim to resolve it.
- **Jaspars, arXiv:2608.01168v2** (v1 2 Aug 2026, v2 17 Aug 2026; abstract fetched
  2026-09-16) gives an independent characterisation: a group embeds in `V` iff it
  is the transition group of a finite union of context-free automata. BDPR's
  "Important remark" (p. 4) records the two proofs as independent.
- arXiv API keyword searches (cloning system / Lehnert / co-context-free / Thompson
  V, 2024–2026, run 2026-09-16) found no paper claiming to resolve Problem 2.16 in
  general.

**Theorem.** Let `G` be finite and `φ0, φ1 ∈ End(G)` with
`ker φ0 ∩ ker φ1 = 1`. Then:

- the pure direct-power cloning system `G_n = G^n`, whose cloning at leaf `k` is
  `g_k ↦ (φ0 g_k, φ1 g_k)`, is a cloning system on finite groups;
- its group `T(G_*)` and the V-type group `𝒱_(G,φ0,φ1)` (labelled triple lists)
  both embed in `V`.

For `φ0 = id` this is BDPR Theorem 3.2.

Proof idea:

- `𝒱` acts on `⊔_{p∈P} Ω_p`, where `Ω_p` is `G × {0,1}^*` modulo
  `(h,x) ~ (φ_p h, xp)`. The action is by `[h,x]·{(a_i,b_i,h_i)} = [φ_p^m(h)·φ_u(h_i), b_i u]`
  whenever `x p^m = a_i u`.
- `P` has one word per idempotent of the semigroup `S = ⟨φ0, φ1⟩`.
- Faithfulness reduces to `M = 1`, where `M = ⋂ ker(t∘s)` over `t` idempotent and
  `s ∈ S ∪ {id}`. On `M` the semigroup has the zero as its only idempotent, so it is
  nilpotent, and iterated cloning is injective.
- Each `Ω_p` is one orbit. `𝒱` is generated by `V` and single-leaf label elements.
- The loop language at `[e,""]_p` is recognised by a PDA. The PDA keeps `h` in its
  state and keeps `x` on the stack, with each letter decorated by `φ` of the suffix
  below it.

**Computation.**
`experiments/zaremsky-2-16-lehnert-conjecture-2026-09-16/twisted_power_cf_action.py`
(about 5 minutes, output `ALL CHECKS PASSED`) checked:

- `M = 1` exhaustively for all 234677 admissible pairs over
  `Z/2, Z/3, Z/4, (Z/2)^2, (Z/2)^3, S_3`;
- refinement invariance, the homomorphism property, faithfulness on short points,
  and PDA simulation against direct action, on random data for sampled pairs.

A first run failed at `(Z/2)^3` only because the test harness looked for idempotent
words of length at most 8. The script now finds them by BFS over `S`. This was a bug
in the harness, not a failure of the mathematics.

## 3. Approaches and where each dies

1. **Direct recoding of `V_(G,θ)` into `V` via period growth or explicit Cantor
   actions.** Tried before reading BDPR in full. Explicit actions on the Cantor set
   are obstructed by the twist: labels pushed down a path `u` become `φ_u(h)`, which
   has no limit along infinite words. Superseded by the germ-at-periodic-points
   action (points `x p^∞`), which is BDPR's device for `p = "0"` and is generalised
   here.
2. **Using only `p = "0"` (BDPR's dyadic points) for general `φ0`.** This fails
   faithfulness when `φ0` is not injective. Example: `G = (Z/2)^2` with coordinate
   projections. A label `h ∈ ker φ0` at a leaf is invisible at every `v0^∞` for
   `v` of length ≥ 1, and `e_"0" = φ0` kills it at `v = ""` too. Fix: one period per
   idempotent of `S` together with Lemma 3.1 (`M = 1`).
3. **Using only the F-type group.** It is not transitive on `Ω_p` (for `p = "0"` it
   fixes the `x`-part `0^∞`), so orbits are harder to control. Fix: prove the result
   for `𝒱`, then restrict to `T(G_*) ≤ 𝒱`.
4. **General (non-local) cloning systems.** Examples are the Witzel–Zaremsky
   upper-triangular matrix groups `B_n(R)` and `U_n(F_2)`, where cloning a matrix
   duplicates a row and a column. The method dies at Section 2 of the artifact.
   Group elements are not labels attached to single leaves, and there is no evident
   analogue of `Ω_p` with bounded stack decorations: the cloned entries depend on
   whole rows. I found no known coCF proof and no obstruction for these systems. BDPR
   Theorem C-type obstructions do not apply to groups containing `F`, and every
   `T(G_*)` contains `F`. **This is the natural next hole:** is `T(B_*(F_q))`
   (upper-triangular, finite field) coCF, and does it embed in `V`?
5. **Negative direction (`some-finite-cloning-system-group-does-not-embed-in-v`).**
   The standing candidates `V_(G,θ)` are eliminated. Known obstructions to embedding
   in `V` (torsion subgroups finite, period growth, BDPR's CF-TR obstructions) were
   not found to apply to any explicit cloning system group on finite groups.
