# Swarm attack on `hyperlinear-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-finite` (2026-09-16)

Target: every finitely generated hyperlinear group of finite exponent is
finite. The target stays OPEN. This session gives one established reduction
that links the target to the corona half of the program, and records several
approaches that died.

## 1. Setup and what the graph already had

- **Target.** OPEN, attempts 1 to 6.
- **Established equivalence** (`hyperlinear-bounded-exponent-trace-rigidity-iff-finiteness`):
  the target for `(m,N)` holds iff every limit trace of approximate exponent-N
  unitary models factors through a finite quotient of `F_m`.
- **Operator-norm side.** `operator-norm-restricted-burnside` (OPEN) is
  equivalent, per `(m,N)`, to `matrix-restricted-burnside-gap` (OPEN), by the
  established `corona-restricted-burnside-equals-power-law-moduli`. It feeds
  `infinite-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-non-mf` and
  `osin-periodic-quotient-is-non-mf`.
- **The remark.** The target's frontmatter says the Hilbert--Schmidt (HS)
  form and the operator-norm MF form of the Burnside finiteness principle
  imply neither the other "on its face".
- **Two established nodes used here.**
  - `bounded-exponent-fg-group-residual-has-finite-index`: the finite residual
    `K` of a finitely generated exponent-N group has finite index and no
    proper finite-index subgroup.
  - `gapped-normal-generators-give-a-hyperlinear-quotient`: a nontrivial
    corona homomorphism of a finitely generated group, normally generated by
    finitely many finite-order elements, gives a nontrivial hyperlinear
    quotient in which some generator survives with a quantitative
    Hilbert--Schmidt defect.

## 2. External gate (literature status, checked 2026-09-16)

No primary source from 2024 to 2026 settles the target. None shows `B(m,N)`
to be hyperlinear or sofic, and none constructs an infinite finitely generated
hyperlinear, sofic, amenable or LEF group of bounded exponent.

- **Pestov, *Hyperlinear and sofic groups: a brief guide*,**
  arXiv:0804.3968v8. Open question 9.4 (Weiss), "Is the free Burnside group
  of a finite exponent n sofic?" (text as pinned in the target node). No
  later answer was found in the sources checked here.
- **Donoso-Echenique and Silva, *Free Burnside groups of large odd exponent
  have cost 1*,** arXiv:2608.20472v1 (2026-08-20), abstract only. For large
  odd `n` (per the abstract, `n >= 665` when `m >= 3` and `n >= 1003` when
  `m = 2`) it proves cost 1, hence `beta_1^(2)(B(m,n)) = 0`, which gives no
  hyperlinearity obstruction; see 4.3 below.
- **Fournier-Facio, *A torsion-free non-sofic group*,** arXiv:2608.02025v2
  (2026-08-14), source read. It builds on the OpenAI non-sofic Leavitt unit
  group (announced 2026-08-01) through a Kun--Thom type criterion. That
  criterion concerns soficity and property (T) subgroups, not bounded
  exponent or hyperlinearity.
- **Ersoy, *On minimal non-sofic and ω-non-sofic groups*,**
  arXiv:2604.19174v4 (2026-06-08), source read. It is structural, conditional
  on existence, and does not touch the target.
- **Goffer, Greenfeld and Olshanskii, *Asymptotic Burnside laws*,**
  arXiv:2409.09630v2 (revised 2026-04-28), source read. Its groups satisfy
  `x^n = 1` only with limit probability 1, not as a law, so they are not
  bounded-exponent test objects.
- **arXiv API keyword searches** (2026-09-16) for Burnside with hyperlinear,
  sofic or "matricial field", "MF group" with torsion, and "torsion groups"
  with hyperlinear. Only older, unrelated hits (for example 1909.07800).
- **Not checked:** MathOverflow, zbMATH, MathSciNet.

## 3. What was proven (new graph nodes)

**Claim `bounded-exponent-corona-image-gives-hyperlinear-quotient`
(ESTABLISHED through its `-proof` route).**

Setup: `G` is `m`-generated of exponent dividing `N`, `K = Res_fin(G)`,
`c(m,N) = |R(m,N)|(m-1)+1`, and `rho : G -> U(Q_d)` is any corona
homomorphism. Exactly one of the following holds.

1. `rho(K) = 1`, and `rho` factors through the finite group `G/K`, a quotient
   of `R(m,N)`.
2. `rho(G)` is infinite, and `K` has a quotient `H` that is infinite,
   hyperlinear, of exponent dividing `N`, generated by at most `c(m,N)`
   elements, perfect, and without proper finite-index subgroups. `H` also
   has a generator with limit trace `Re tau(a) <= 1 - eta_N^2/(16 c(m,N))`.

The proof takes four steps.

- **Finite index.** `K` has finite index and no proper finite-index subgroup.
  Since `G/K` is a quotient of `R(m,N)`, `K` has at most `c(m,N)` generators,
  by Schreier.
- **Dichotomy.** A nontrivial quotient of `K` is infinite.
- **Reblocking.** Apply the gapped-normal-generators node to `K`, using its
  own finite generating set as normal generators with trivial conjugators, so
  `L = 2m'`. Bounded exponent supplies `(GNG0)` with `delta = eta_N`.
- **Inheritance.** The hyperlinear quotient `H` inherits "no proper
  finite-index subgroup" from `K`, so it is infinite.

**Route `corona-burnside-from-hyperlinear-burnside-finiteness`.** The target
implies `operator-norm-restricted-burnside`. With the existing routes it
therefore implies `infinite-fg-bounded-exponent-groups-are-non-mf` and
`osin-periodic-quotient-is-non-mf`, and through the established equivalence
also `matrix-restricted-burnside-gap` (stated in prose; no reverse route is
added, following `corona-burnside-from-power-law-moduli`).

- **Per parameter.** The target for exponent `N` and `c(m,N)` generators
  gives corona Burnside for `(m,N)`.
- **Where the HS hypothesis is spent.** Only on groups with no finite
  quotients.
- **Converse.** Not available. HS models do not become operator-norm models
  (attempt 3 of `operator-norm-restricted-burnside`).

**Further unconditional consequences** (clause 5 of the claim):

- `Rad_MF(G) <= Res_fin(G)` always.
- `Rad_MF(G) = Res_fin(G)` unless the finite residual has an infinite
  hyperlinear quotient.
- For infinite simple `G` of bounded exponent, any nontrivial corona
  homomorphism makes `G` hyperlinear. This agrees with
  `simple-torsion-groups-have-a-macroscopic-mf-dichotomy`.

**Where this leaves the target.** The HS statement implies the corona
statement, and the corona statement is equivalent (per `(m,N)`) to the matrix
gap. A counterexample to the corona statement would produce a counterexample
to the target of a special form: a perfect group with no finite quotients.

## 4. Approaches tried and where each dies

### 4.1 Reduction to prime exponent

Try to deduce the target at exponent `N = pM` from prime exponents.

- **Obstruction.** The verbal subgroup `G^M` is generated by elements of
  order dividing `p`, but products of such elements can have any order
  dividing `N`. So `G^M` does not have exponent `p`.
- **Where it dies.** Being generated by elements of order `p` is far weaker
  than having exponent `p`. `Z/p * Z/p` is infinite, hyperlinear and
  generated by order-`p` elements. Infinite groups have no Sylow or
  composition-series substitute that would give prime-exponent layers.

### 4.2 Reduction to two generators

- **Where it dies.** One would need an embedding of an `m`-generated
  hyperlinear exponent-`N` group into a 2-generated group that is both
  hyperlinear and of exponent `N`.
- **Known embeddings fail.** HNN and wreath-product embeddings introduce
  elements of infinite order. Embeddings of `B(infinity,n)` into `B(2,n)` for
  large odd `n` (Shirvanyan, recalled from memory, unverified, not fetched)
  concern free Burnside groups. Hyperlinearity of the ambient group is exactly
  the unknown.
- **Not needed.** The target quantifies over all `m`, and the per-parameter
  reduction in section 3 moves `m` to `c(m,N)` anyway.

### 4.3 Free entropy and L2-Betti numbers

- **Idea.** A finitely generated hyperlinear group satisfies the upper bound
  `delta_0 <= beta_1^(2) - beta_0^(2) + 1` on microstates free entropy
  dimension (Connes--Shlyakhtenko type inequality, recalled, not
  re-fetched). One could try to contradict it for infinite bounded-exponent
  groups.
- **Where it dies.** The inequality is an upper bound. With
  `beta_1^(2)(B(m,n)) = 0` (arXiv:2608.20472), it only says `delta_0 <= 1`,
  which is consistent with hyperlinearity. Bounded exponent gives no lower
  bound on `delta_0` beyond what finite subgroups give.

### 4.4 Amenable radical and trace concentration

- **Idea.** Push the HS trace of a hyperlinear model onto the amenable
  radical, in the style of BKKO, and exploit local finiteness there.
- **First failure.** BKKO concerns traces on `C*_r(G)`, and a hyperlinear
  trace is a trace on `C*(G)` that need not factor through `C*_r(G)`.
- **Circularity.** Amenable groups are hyperlinear, so "finitely generated
  amenable bounded-exponent groups are finite" is a special case of the
  target, and it is not known independently. Følner sets do not supply local
  finiteness.
- **Subsumed.** The reduced-MF radical nodes already record the C*-reduced
  version: `reduced-mf-hyperlinear-radical-in-amenable-radical` and
  `nonsofic-reduced-mf-group-finite-amenable-radical`.
- **Where it dies.** No bridge exists from a non-reduced trace to the
  amenable radical, and the amenable case is itself open.

### 4.5 HS analogue of Kun--Thom stability

- **Idea.** Round approximate exponent-N models to exact representations of
  finite groups.
- **Where it dies.** At the same place as attempt 1 of the target node.
  Rounding one unitary to `N`th roots of unity is free, but approximate power
  laws on all words do not force the model near any exact representation,
  and there is no HS stability of the exponent law. At the trace level this
  is precisely the trace-rigidity equivalent of the target, so there is no
  gain.

### 4.6 Universal hyperlinear quotient

- **Content.** `B(m,N)/Rad_HL(B(m,N))` is hyperlinear, because countably many
  tracial ultraproducts separate points and their weighted product embeds.
  It surjects onto `R(m,N)`. The target for `(m,N)` is equivalent to its
  being finite, and then it equals `R(m,N)`.
- **Where it dies.** It is a reformulation, already implicit in the
  trace-rigidity node. Not added.

### 4.7 A local HS restricted Burnside principle (HLRB)

- **Statement.** HS models satisfying `w^N ~ 1` for words `w` in a ball of
  radius `R'` are HS-close to the relators of `R(m,N)`. This is strictly
  stronger than the target.
- **Plausibly false at small radii.** The partial Burnside group
  `Pi_R(m,N) = F_m / <<w^N : |w| <= R>>` should satisfy `C'(1/6)` once `N` is
  large compared with `R` (roughly `N > 6(R-1)`, by a Fine--Wilf bound on
  pieces; this was checked by hand, not formally). It would then be
  cubulated (Wise) and virtually special (Agol), hence residually finite. Its
  finite quotients would satisfy the exponent law on the `R`-ball without
  satisfying the `R(m,N)` relators.
- **Unverified input.** That the torsion `C'(1/6)` case of Wise's
  cubulation applies as needed was not checked.
- **Already known failure at small radii (referee addition).** Independently
  of the unverified cubulation argument, the established node
  `partial-burnside-presentations-are-golod-shafarevich` already gives
  unbounded finite `p`-groups satisfying the law on words of length `<= R`
  whenever a prime power `q | N` has `q >= 4R + 5` (attempt 6 of the target).
- **Where it dies.** HLRB is not necessary for the target and is plausibly
  false. Not added.

### 4.8 Finite-residual shape of a minimal counterexample

- **Content.** Any counterexample contains a finite-index, perfect,
  hyperlinear subgroup with no finite quotients and an infinite simple
  quotient.
- **Where it dies.** It is already in
  `bounded-exponent-fg-group-residual-has-finite-index`, together with the
  trivial remark that subgroups of hyperlinear groups are hyperlinear. The
  simple quotient need not be hyperlinear, so one cannot pass to simple
  counterexamples. That is where "infinite simple counterexample" dies.

### 4.9 Reduced C*-algebra non-MF

- **Idea.** Prove `C*_r(B(m,N))` is not MF.
- **Where it dies.** It is subsumed by the operator-norm corollary in
  section 3 and by the reduced-MF radical nodes. It gives nothing for the
  HS target.

## 5. What remains open

- **The target itself.** Every route into it still needs HS stability or
  trace rigidity of the exponent law, and no mechanism for either is known.
- **The converse, corona Burnside implies HS Burnside.** It would need a
  way to upgrade HS models of perfect bounded-exponent groups with no finite
  quotients to operator-norm models. No such mechanism is known.
- **Faithfulness.** Whether `H` in clause 2 can be taken to be `K` itself.
  Faithful reblocking fails in general; see the "What stays open" paragraph
  of the gapped-normal-generators node.
