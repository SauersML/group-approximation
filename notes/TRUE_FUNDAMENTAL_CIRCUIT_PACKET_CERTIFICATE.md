# Bounded fundamental circuits certify the projective harmonic denominator

Date: 2026-08-12

## Outcome

The Ash--Rudolph reduction leaves an integral closing map

```text
beta_X : R_X -> E_X
```

on a module generated by uniformly short unimodular symbols.  Locality of
`beta_X` does not imply that its kernel has short generators.  This note
isolates a finite, exact condition which does imply the required harmonic
denominator bound.

Choose a maximal rationally independent set `F` of columns of an integral
matrix `beta`.  Every nonpivot column `e` has one primitive integral
dependence

```text
a_e beta(e) = sum_(f in F) b_(e,f) beta(f),
a_e>0,
gcd(a_e,(b_(e,f))_f)=1.                            (FCP1)
```

Its fundamental circuit is

```text
c_e = a_e e - sum_(f in F) b_(e,f) f.              (FCP2)
```

The circuits `c_e` are an integral, real-spanning packet for `ker(beta)`.
Consequently, if

```text
sup_e (a_e^2 + sum_f b_(e,f)^2) <= A^2             (FCP3)
```

and the fixed integral comparison from closed symbols to absolute cellular
cycles has operator norm at most `C`, then the projected harmonic cycle
lattice `Q_X` satisfies

```text
lambda_1(Q_X^#) >= 1/(CA).                          (FCP4)
```

No primitivity, bounded overlap, or bounded harmonic rank is required for
`(FCP4)`.  If every `a_e=1`, the same circuits are in fact a `Z`-basis of the
closing kernel.  In general their failure to generate integrally is killed
by `lcm_e(a_e)`.  Thus the coefficients `a_e` isolate the primary saturation
obstruction at this interface.

This turns the qualitative phrase "find bounded local circuits" into a
checkable family theorem: exhibit pivot columns with uniformly bounded
primitive fundamental-dependence height.  It does not yet prove that the
projective family has such pivots, nor does it provide the stronger bounded
integral lift section needed for the complete circle-repair theorem.

## 1. Integral fundamental circuits

Let

```text
R = Z^I
```

have its orthonormal coordinate metric, let `E` be a finite-rank free
abelian group, and let

```text
beta:R->E                                             (FCP5)
```

be integral.  Write `r=rank_Q(beta)`.  Choose `F subset I`, `|F|=r`, such
that the columns `beta(f)`, `f in F`, are independent over `Q` and span the
rational column space.

For each `e in I\F`, there is a unique rational relation expressing
`beta(e)` through the pivot columns.  Clear denominators and divide by the
common gcd to obtain the unique primitive relation `(FCP1)`.  Define `c_e`
by `(FCP2)` and put

```text
h_F(beta)=max_(e in I\F) ||c_e||
         =max_(e in I\F)
            sqrt(a_e^2+sum_(f in F)b_(e,f)^2).       (FCP6)
```

The empty maximum is zero.

**Theorem 1 (fundamental-circuit packet).**  The family

```text
{c_e:e in I\F}                                      (FCP7)
```

is linearly independent over `Q` and spans

```text
ker(beta_Q).                                         (FCP8)
```

In particular it is an integral real-spanning packet of norm at most
`h_F(beta)`.

**Proof.**  Relation `(FCP1)` says exactly that `beta(c_e)=0`.  In the
nonpivot coordinates, `c_e` has value `a_e` at `e` and zero at every other
nonpivot coordinate.  Hence the circuits are independent.  Their number is

```text
|I|-r=dim_Q ker(beta_Q),                             (FCP9)
```

so they form a rational basis of the kernel.  Their norms are `(FCP6)`.
End proof.

The rational statement is enough for the harmonic systole.  The integral
defect is also explicit.

**Proposition 2 (primary saturation bound).**  Let `C_F` be the lattice
generated by the circuits `(FCP7)` inside `ker(beta)`.  If

```text
A_F=lcm_(e in I\F) a_e,                             (FCP10)
```

with the empty lcm defined to be one,

then

```text
A_F ker(beta) subset C_F.                           (FCP11)
```

Thus `ker(beta)/C_F` is finite of exponent dividing `A_F`.  If every
`a_e=1`, then

```text
C_F=ker(beta),                                      (FCP12)
```

and the fundamental circuits are a `Z`-basis.

**Proof.**  Take `x in ker(beta)` and set

```text
y=A_F x-sum_(e in I\F)(A_F x_e/a_e)c_e.             (FCP13)
```

This is an integral kernel vector and all its nonpivot coordinates vanish.
It is therefore supported on `F`.  The pivot columns are rationally
independent, so `y=0`.  This proves `(FCP11)`.  When every `a_e=1`, take
`A_F=1`.  End proof.

The primitive relations, their heights, and `A_F` are exact finite data.
They can be obtained by rational column elimination or by the maximal minors
in Cramer's rule.  No lattice reduction or shortest-vector oracle is needed
to verify a proposed certificate.

## 2. Transfer to harmonic packets

Let `K` be an integral cycle lattice in a Euclidean space, let `H` be its
relevant harmonic real subspace, and let `P_H` be orthogonal projection.
Put

```text
Q=P_H(K),
Q^#={u in H:<u,q> in Z for every q in Q}.            (FCP14)
```

Assume, as in the rational harmonic setup used here, that `Q` is a full
lattice in `H`.

Suppose an integral map

```text
T:ker(beta)->K                                       (FCP15)
```

has real operator norm at most `C` and satisfies

```text
P_H T(ker(beta_R))=H.                                (FCP16)
```

For a chain-level boundary-symbol model, `T` is the bounded comparison from
closed symbol combinations to absolute cellular cycles.  If `beta` is only
the boundary map on homology, existence of a bounded `T` is an additional
metric lifting hypothesis and must not be inferred from exactness alone.

**Theorem 3 (circuit-to-systole certificate).**  Under `(FCP15)--(FCP16)`,

```text
lambda_1(Q^#) >= 1/(C h_F(beta))                    (FCP17)
```

whenever `H` is nonzero.

**Proof.**  By Theorem 1, the vectors `c_e` span `ker(beta_R)`.  Hence

```text
q_e=P_H T(c_e) in Q                                  (FCP18)
```

span `H`.  Projection is contractive, so

```text
||q_e||<=C||c_e||<=C h_F(beta).                      (FCP19)
```

Let `0!=u in Q^#`.  Since the `q_e` span `H`, some pairing `<u,q_e>` is
nonzero.  It is an integer by `(FCP14)`, and therefore

```text
1<=|<u,q_e>|
 <=||u|| ||q_e||
 <=C h_F(beta)||u||.                                (FCP20)
```

Taking the minimum over nonzero `u` proves `(FCP17)`.  End proof.

This is the bounded-spanning-packet theorem of
`TRUE_HARMONIC_SUCCESSIVE_MINIMUM_PACKET_SCREEN.md` with an explicit finite
certificate for the packets.

## 3. Useful sufficient forms

### Unimodular bounded circuits

If `a_e=1`, every fundamental relation is already saturated.  If in
addition it has at most `s` nonzero coefficients and every coefficient has
absolute value at most `b`, then

```text
h_F(beta)<=b sqrt(s).                               (FCP21)
```

Thus a totally unimodular boundary matrix is useful only together with a
uniform support bound on the chosen fundamental circuits.  Total
unimodularity by itself permits circuits of unbounded length.

### Graph calibration

For an oriented graph boundary matrix, a pivot forest gives the familiar
fundamental cycles.  Their coefficients are `+-1`, and `(FCP6)` is the
square root of the largest fundamental-cycle length.  A uniformly bounded
fundamental-cycle basis therefore proves `(FCP17)`.

The locality counterexample in
`TRUE_ASH_RUDOLPH_PACKET_SCOPE_AUDIT.md` is detected sharply.  For

```text
beta_m(x_1,...,x_m)=(x_1-x_2,...,x_(m-1)-x_m),       (FCP22)
```

every pivot choice has one primitive circuit

```text
(1,1,...,1),                                        (FCP23)
```

so

```text
h_F(beta_m)=sqrt(m).                                 (FCP24)
```

The matrix has bounded row support and bounded column degree, but fails the
actual circuit-height condition by exactly the observed factor.

## 4. Exact projective target

For the cuspidal image in a projective chart `X`, take:

```text
R_X     = integral module on unimodular symbols,
beta_X  = signed boundary-at-infinity closing map,
K_X     = absolute integral cuspidal cycle lattice.  (FCP25)
```

The interior harmonic denominator theorem follows if one constructs, with
constants independent of `X`,

1. a pivot set `F_X` satisfying

   ```text
   h_(F_X)(beta_X)<=A;                               (FCP26)
   ```

2. an integral absolute realization `T_X` satisfying

   ```text
   ||T_X||<=C,
   P_(H_X)T_X(ker beta_X)=H_X.                       (FCP27)
   ```

Then Theorem 3 gives

```text
inf_X lambda_1(Q_X^#)>=1/(CA).                      (FCP28)
```

The five-dimensional boundary kernel observed at level `121` requires an
analogous circuit certificate in the Borel--Serre boundary complex.  The
two packet families can then be combined only after controlling the metric
extension between boundary and cuspidal filtrands.

Condition `(FCP26)` is stronger than the existence of arbitrary bounded
kernel packets, but it is much more concrete: one proposed pivot list and
one primitive relation per nonpivot column are a complete exact
certificate.  The level-`121` Gram result strongly suggests such packets
exist, but the current export does not identify them in unimodular-symbol
coordinates.

## 5. What the four supplied papers do and do not provide

The source-level audit confirms the following division.

1. Becker--Lubotzky, *Group stability and Property (T)*,
   [arXiv:1809.00632](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00632), proves that infinite
   hyperlinear or sofic property-`(T)` groups are not HS- or
   permutation-stable in the ordinary sense.  It rules out using property
   `(T)` alone as the missing integral circuit theorem.

2. Dinur--Meshulam, *Near Coverings and Cosystolic Expansion*,
   [arXiv:1909.08507](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08507), proves

   ```text
   h_1(A_3(F_q);G)>=1/9                              (FCP29)
   ```

   for every finite coefficient group `G`, and hence a dimension-free
   Hamming correction of near covers.  This repairs a supplied nonabelian
   `1`-cochain.  It does not construct integral circuits for `(FCP25)` or a
   bounded absolute realization `(FCP27)`.

3. Kaufman--Oppenheim--Weinberger, *Coboundary expansion of coset
   complexes*, [arXiv:2411.02819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02819), proves
   uniform nonabelian cosystolic expansion for their coset-complex family
   and coboundary expansion for each fixed finite coefficient group once
   the residue characteristic is larger than its order.  Its coefficients,
   complexes, and support metric are not the growing integral
   Borel--Serre kernel `(FCP25)`; it does not imply `(FCP26)`.

4. Sinclair--Smith, *Unitary perturbations of masas in type II_1 factors*,
   [arXiv:math/0111330](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0111330), corrects a
   unitary which already nearly normalizes a supplied masa.  It does not
   construct a common masa and has no integral-cycle content.  Its exact
   role remains the Cartan-to-monomial interface audited in
   `TRUE_SINCLAIR_SMITH_MASA_PERTURBATION_AUDIT.md`.

Thus none of the four papers fills `(FCP26)--(FCP27)`.  The positive
expansion results remain useful after the arithmetic closing data have been
encoded, but they do not manufacture that data.

## 6. Scope

Theorem 3 proves the denominator/systole half only.  The fundamental
circuits may span a proper finite-index sublattice, and even a saturated
bounded circuit basis may have unbounded synthesis norm through growing
overlap.  Therefore the theorem does not construct a uniformly bounded
section of

```text
0 -> L_X -> M_X -> Q_X^# -> 0.                     (FCP30)
```

That section is still needed for full nonlinear circle repair.  The exact
next family task is now split cleanly:

```text
bounded primitive fundamental-circuit height
   + bounded absolute realization
      => harmonic denominator solved;               (FCP31)

bounded compatible synthesis/lift section
      => harmonic numerator solved.                 (FCP32)
```

The level-`121` result is direct evidence for `(FCP31)`, not yet a proof of
either family statement.
