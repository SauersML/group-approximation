# Fixed-rank character compactness

## Purpose

The canonical central-quotient route associates to each finite quotient an
abelian group generated by the fixed presentation relators and a marked
element represented by the radical word. Two quantities then arise:

- the shortest integral coefficient vector representing the marked element;
- the largest ratio between the marked phase and the combined generator
  phases over all characters.

The elementary inequality goes from the character ratio to the integral
coefficient norm. This note proves that, when the number of generators is
fixed, the converse holds uniformly at the level of boundedness. Thus no
family-dependent discriminant estimate is needed merely to detect
divergence.

## Definitions

Let \(A\) be a finitely generated abelian group with an ordered generating
tuple

\[
a=(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell).
\]

For \(b\in A\), define

\[
\delta_A(b):=
\min\left\{
\|v\|_2:
v\in\mathbb Z^\ell,
\quad b=\sum_{j=1}^\ell v_ja_j
\right\}.
\]

Also define

\[
\Lambda_A(b):=
\sup_{\chi\in\widehat A}
\frac{|\chi(b)-1|}
{\left(\sum_{j=1}^\ell|\chi(a_j)-1|^2\right)^{1/2}},
\]

where characters with zero denominator are omitted. Because the \(a_j\)
generate \(A\), the only character with zero denominator is the trivial
character, and its numerator is also zero.

## The fixed-rank compactness theorem

### Theorem 1

For every integer \(\ell\geq1\) and every \(B<\infty\), there is a finite
constant \(D_\ell(B)\) such that, for every finitely generated abelian group
\(A\), every ordered generating \(\ell\)-tuple \(a\), and every \(b\in A\),

\[
\Lambda_A(b)\leq B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta_A(b)\leq D_\ell(B).
\]

The theorem is qualitative: it proves the existence of a uniform bound but
does not presently give an effective formula for \(D_\ell(B)\).

### Proof

Write

\[
A=\mathbb Z^\ell/K
\]

so that \(a_j\) is the image of the \(j\)-th standard basis vector. Choose
\(v\in\mathbb Z^\ell\) mapping to \(b\). Under the standard pairing between
\(\mathbb Z^\ell\) and \(\mathbb T^\ell\), the dual group is the closed
subgroup

\[
H=K^\perp\leq\mathbb T^\ell,
\]

and evaluation at \(b\) is the character

\[
\chi_v:H\to\mathbb T,
\qquad
\chi_v(z)=z^v.
\]

Equip \(\mathbb T^\ell\) with the chordal metric

\[
d_\ell(z,z')=
\left(\sum_{j=1}^\ell|z_j-z'_j|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Both this metric and the chordal metric on \(\mathbb T\) are invariant under
translation. Therefore \(\Lambda_A(b)\leq B\) is exactly the assertion that
\(\chi_v\) is \(B\)-Lipschitz:

\[
|\chi_v(z)-\chi_v(z')|
\leq B\,d_\ell(z,z')
\qquad(z,z'\in H).
\]

Suppose the theorem were false. There would be subgroups
\(K_n\leq\mathbb Z^\ell\) and shortest representatives
\(v_n\in\mathbb Z^\ell\) such that

\[
\operatorname{dist}(v_n,K_n)=\|v_n\|_2\longrightarrow\infty,
\]

while the characters

\[
\chi_n:K_n^\perp\to\mathbb T,
\qquad
\chi_n(z)=z^{v_n},
\]

are all \(B\)-Lipschitz.

Let

\[
\Gamma_n=
\{(z,\chi_n(z)):z\in K_n^\perp\}
\leq\mathbb T^{\ell+1}
\]

be their graphs. The hyperspace of closed subsets of the compact metric
space \(\mathbb T^{\ell+1}\) is compact. After passing to a subsequence,
\(\Gamma_n\) converges in Hausdorff distance to a closed subgroup
\(\Gamma\leq\mathbb T^{\ell+1}\).

Let \(H\leq\mathbb T^\ell\) be the projection of \(\Gamma\) onto the first
\(\ell\) coordinates. The projection \(\Gamma\to H\) is injective. Indeed,
if \((z,u),(z,u')\in\Gamma\), approximate these points by
\((z_n,u_n),(z'_n,u'_n)\in\Gamma_n\). The common Lipschitz bound gives

\[
|u_n-u'_n|\leq B\,d_\ell(z_n,z'_n)\longrightarrow0,
\]

so \(u=u'\). Hence \(\Gamma\) is the graph of a continuous, in fact
\(B\)-Lipschitz, character

\[
\chi:H\to\mathbb T.
\]

Every continuous character on a closed subgroup of a torus extends to a
character of the torus. Equivalently, exactness of Pontryagin duality for
\(H\hookrightarrow\mathbb T^\ell\) makes the restriction map

\[
\widehat{\mathbb T^\ell}=\mathbb Z^\ell
\longrightarrow\widehat H
\]

surjective. Thus there is a fixed \(v\in\mathbb Z^\ell\) such that

\[
\chi(z)=z^v
\qquad(z\in H).
\]

Hausdorff convergence of the graphs now implies

\[
\sup_{z\in K_n^\perp}|\chi_n(z)-z^v|\longrightarrow0.
\]

For completeness, if this uniform convergence failed, choose points \(z_n\)
witnessing a fixed positive failure. A convergent subsequence of the graph
points \((z_n,\chi_n(z_n))\) would limit to \((z,\chi(z))\in\Gamma\), while
continuity gives \(z_n^v\to z^v=\chi(z)\), a contradiction.

Consequently the character

\[
\eta_n(z):=\chi_n(z)z^{-v}
\]

on \(K_n^\perp\) converges uniformly to \(1\). The image of a character is a
compact subgroup of \(\mathbb T\). Every nontrivial compact subgroup of
\(\mathbb T\) contains a point whose chordal distance from \(1\) is at least
\(\sqrt3\): this is immediate for \(\mathbb T\), and for a finite cyclic
subgroup the minimum of the maximal distance occurs at order three. Hence
\(\eta_n\) is trivial for all sufficiently large \(n\).

It follows that

\[
v_n-v\in(K_n^\perp)^\perp=K_n.
\]

Therefore

\[
\delta_{\mathbb Z^\ell/K_n}(v_n+K_n)
\leq\|v\|_2
\]

eventually, contradicting the choice of \(v_n\). This proves the theorem.

## Exact divergence equivalence

### Corollary 2

For every family \((A_i,a_i,b_i)\) with a fixed number \(\ell\) of ordered
generators,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_i\delta_{A_i}(b_i)=\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sup_i\Lambda_{A_i}(b_i)=\infty.
}
\]

#### Proof

If \(b=\sum_jv_ja_j\), telescoping and Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
|\chi(b)-1|
\leq\sum_j|v_j|\,|\chi(a_j)-1|
\leq\|v\|_2
\left(\sum_j|\chi(a_j)-1|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Taking the shortest \(v\) gives \(\Lambda_A(b)\leq\delta_A(b)\). This proves
one implication. The other is the contrapositive of Theorem 1. \(\square\)

## Application to canonical central quotients

Let

\[
P=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_d\mid r_1,\ldots,r_\ell\rangle
\]

be fixed and let \(w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(P)\). For a finite
quotient \(P\twoheadrightarrow Q\), the canonical relation module supplies
the abelian group \(A_Q\), its fixed \(\ell\)-tuple of relator classes, and
the marked class \(b_Q=[w]\). The integral radical-containment theorem gives
\(b_Q\in A_Q\), so all the preceding definitions apply.

The canonical central-cover criterion proves FALSE exactly when

\[
\sup_Q\Lambda_Q(w)=\infty.
\]

Corollary 2 therefore upgrades that criterion to

\[
\boxed{
\text{the canonical central-quotient route proves FALSE}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sup_Q\delta_Q(w)=\infty.
}
\]

Finite-order characters are dense in the dual of every finitely generated
abelian group. Thus character ratios approaching the supremum can be chosen
to factor through finite central covers, exactly as required by the matrix
construction.

## Scope and remaining gate

**Closure update.** The explicit Kun--Thom radical word does not have
unbounded \(\delta_Q(w)\). In fact the full finite residual \(R\) of the
symmetric double satisfies \(R=[D,R]\), which supplies one fixed relator
coefficient vector in every canonical module. Thus all \(\delta_Q(w)\) and
all \(\Lambda_Q(w)\) are uniformly bounded for every \(w\in R\). See
`FALSE_FINITE_RESIDUAL_COMMUTATOR_NO_GO.md`.

It is also important not to confuse \(\delta_Q(w)\) with an integral filling
norm in a full finite-cover cellular complex. The latter normally has
\(\ell|Q|\) relator coordinates, whereas \(A_Q\) is generated by the fixed
\(\ell\) coinvariant relator classes. Large cellular filling norm can
collapse after passage to coinvariants. A proof of FALSE must establish
unbounded distortion in the fixed-rank canonical module itself, or prove a
comparison theorem preventing that collapse for the chosen quotient tower.
For the current double, the stronger commutator theorem proves that collapse
does occur.
