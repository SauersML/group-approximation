# Index-three Iwahori restriction has uniformly bounded branching

Date: 2026-08-14

## 1. Outcome

Let

```text
K=SL_2(Z),                 B=B_+ or B_-,
[K:B]=3.                                               (ITB1)
```

The action of `K` on `K/B` factors through the natural three-point action
of `SL_2(F_2)=S_3` and is two-transitive.

This note proves two dimension-free representation-theoretic facts.

1. If `pi` is an irreducible `d`-dimensional unitary representation of
   `K`, then

   ```text
   pi|B = directSum_a theta_a tensor 1_(m_a)           (ITB2)
   ```

   satisfies

   ```text
   dim(theta_a)>=d/3             for every a,
   m_a=1                         for every a,
   number of constituents<=3.                         (ITB3)
   ```

2. If `theta` is an irreducible finite-dimensional unitary representation
   of `B`, then

   ```text
   Ind_B^K(theta)                                     (ITB4)
   ```

   is a multiplicity-free direct sum of at most three irreducible
   `K`-representations.  Every constituent has dimension between
   `dim(theta)` and `3 dim(theta)`.

Thus the high-dimensional escape left by the Iwahori matching reductions
cannot fragment one modular irreducible into an unbounded number of small
edge types or hide multiplicity inside a repeated edge type.  The
restriction/induction incidence graph is simple, has degree at most three
in both directions, and dimensions across every edge differ by at most a
factor of three.

This does not yet rule out the full-rank low-energy outlier.  A single pair
of growing Iwahori irreducibles can still have a one-dimensional space of
near-intertwiners generated by a matrix close to a unitary.  The remaining
problem is consequently a bounded-branching near-equivalence problem, not
an unbounded restriction-multiplicity problem.

## 2. The three-coset conditional expectation

Let `pi:K->U(V)` be irreducible and put `d=dim(V)`.  Choose left-coset
representatives

```text
r_1=1,r_2,r_3                                      (ITB5)
```

for `K/B`.  For `x in pi(B)'`, define

```text
E(x)=(1/3)sum_(i=1)^3 pi(r_i)xpi(r_i)*.                (ITB6)
```

Right multiplication of a representative by an element of `B` does not
change its summand.  Left multiplication by `K` permutes the cosets, so
`E(x)` commutes with `pi(K)`.  Irreducibility and trace preservation give

```text
E(x)=tr_d(x)1.                                        (ITB7)
```

For positive `x`, the identity-coset term in `(ITB6)` gives the exact
Pimsner--Popa inequality

```text
tr_d(x)1=E(x)>=(1/3)x.                                (ITB8)
```

No property `(T)`, approximation theorem, or limiting argument enters this
calculation.

## 3. Restriction theorem

Because the representation is unitary, its restriction is completely
reducible.  Write it as in `(ITB2)`, with the `theta_a` pairwise
inequivalent and irreducible.  Then

```text
pi(B)' = directSum_a 1_(dim theta_a) tensor M_(m_a).  (ITB9)
```

Fix a rank-one projection `f` in one multiplicity factor `M_(m_a)` and put

```text
e_a=1_(theta_a) tensor f.                              (ITB10)
```

It is a projection in `pi(B)'` whose normalized matrix trace is

```text
tr_d(e_a)=dim(theta_a)/d.                              (ITB11)
```

Apply `(ITB8)` to `e_a` and restrict the operator inequality to its range:

```text
dim(theta_a)/d >=1/3.                                 (ITB12)
```

This proves the first assertion of `(ITB3)`.  The dimension identity

```text
d=sum_a m_a dim(theta_a)                              (ITB13)
```

and `(ITB12)` now give

```text
d>=(d/3)sum_a m_a,                                    (ITB14)
```

which first proves `sum_a m_a<=3`.

Section 5 below proves that `Ind_B^K(theta_a)` is multiplicity-free.
Frobenius reciprocity then gives

```text
m_a=dim Hom_B(theta_a,pi|B)
   =dim Hom_K(Ind_B^K(theta_a),pi)<=1.                 (ITB14a)
```

Thus every nonzero `m_a` is one, and there are at most three distinct
constituents.

**Theorem 1 (bounded Iwahori restriction).**  Equations
`(ITB2)--(ITB3)` hold for every finite-dimensional irreducible unitary
representation of `K`.

In particular, restriction cannot hide any repeated multiplicity space
inside one irreducible modular block.

## 4. The index-two intersection

Two-transitivity of the action on `K/B` implies that `B` has two orbits on
`K/B`: the base point and the other two points.  Hence there are exactly two
double cosets

```text
B\K/B={B,BgB}                                         (ITB15)
```

for any `g notin B`.  The stabilizer in `B` of `gB` is

```text
C=B intersection gBg^(-1),          [B:C]=2.          (ITB16)
```

We use the following elementary index-two form of Clifford theory.

**Lemma 2 (index-two restriction).**  If `C` is an index-two subgroup of a
group `H` and `eta` is a finite-dimensional irreducible unitary
representation of `H`, then `eta|C` is either irreducible or is the direct
sum of two inequivalent irreducible representations.  In particular it is
multiplicity-free and has at most two constituents.

**Proof.**  The subgroup `C` is normal.  Choose an irreducible
`C`-subrepresentation `xi` of `eta|C`.  Its conjugate by an element of
`H\C` is the only other possible orbit type, and the two orbit spaces span
an `H`-invariant subspace, hence all of `eta`.

If the orbit has two elements, irreducibility forces exactly one copy of
each.  If `xi` is invariant up to equivalence, the multiplicity space
carries an irreducible projective representation of `H/C=C_2`.  Every
complex projective representation of a cyclic group is equivalent to an
ordinary representation, and its irreducibles are one-dimensional.  Thus
the multiplicity is one in this case as well.  This proves the lemma.

## 5. Induction theorem

Let `theta` be an irreducible representation of `B`.  Mackey's intertwining
formula and `(ITB15)` give

```text
End_K(Ind_B^K theta)
  = End_B(theta)
    directSum Hom_C(theta|C,(theta^g)|C)               (ITB17)
```

as vector spaces, with the harmless conventional choice of conjugation in
the second term.  The first summand has dimension one.

Both representations in the second `Hom` space are restrictions to the
same index-two group `C` of irreducible representations of the adjacent
index-two overgroups.  By Lemma 2, each is a multiplicity-free sum of at
most two irreducibles.  Therefore

```text
dim Hom_C(theta|C,(theta^g)|C)<=2.                     (ITB18)
```

Equations `(ITB17)--(ITB18)` show

```text
dim End_K(Ind_B^K theta)<=3.                           (ITB19)
```

If

```text
Ind_B^K theta = directSum_j pi_j tensor 1_(n_j),       (ITB20)
```

then the left side of `(ITB19)` is `sum_j n_j^2`.
Consequently every `n_j=1` and there are at most three summands.  This
proves multiplicity-freeness and the cardinality claim in Outcome 2.

Frobenius reciprocity says that `theta` occurs in `pi_j|B` whenever `pi_j`
occurs in `(ITB20)`.  Thus

```text
dim(theta)<=dim(pi_j).                                 (ITB21)
```

The opposite inequality

```text
dim(pi_j)<=3 dim(theta)                                (ITB22)
```

follows either from Theorem 1 or from the total dimension
`dim(Ind theta)=3dim(theta)`.

**Theorem 3 (bounded Iwahori induction).**  The decomposition `(ITB20)` is
multiplicity-free, has at most three summands, and satisfies
`(ITB21)--(ITB22)`.

## 6. Consequence for a putative negative matching sequence

Decompose the two exact modular-vertex representations in a central-regular
negative sequence into irreducibles.  The dimension-tight theorem in
`TRUE_IWAHORI_MATCHING_FIXED_TYPE_REDUCTION.md` says that positive normalized
mass must escape through modular irreducibles of unbounded dimension on
both vertices.  Theorem 1 now says that each such block restricts through
at most three pairwise inequivalent Iwahori constituents, all of comparable
dimension.

Conversely, Theorem 3 says that any one of those Iwahori constituents can
belong to at most three inequivalent modular induction branches on either
side.  Hence each individual restriction/induction incidence graph has
uniformly bounded degree, with dimensions comparable within a factor of
three.

Combine this with
`TRUE_IWAHORI_CENTRAL_REGULAR_OUTLIER_LOCALIZATION.md`.  Its low-energy
mixed space decomposes over pairs of Iwahori irreducibles.  The present
theorems show that the physical identity mass cannot evade analysis by
splitting any one growing vertex block among more and more tiny edge
constituents.  If a positive portion of the identity mass can be localized
to one cross-pair of constituents, that pair necessarily has the form

```text
theta_n,eta_n irreducible representations of B,
dim(theta_n) comparable to dim(eta_n) ->infinity,
theta_n and eta_n extend through opposite K vertices,
the mixed gap on Hom(eta_n,theta_n) tends to zero along
a near-full-rank, near-unitary direction.              (ITB23)
```

The word "near" in `(ITB23)` cannot yet be removed.  Nor has localization to
one positive-mass cross-pair been proved: the two vertex decompositions have
independent relative position, so the identity matrix may distribute its
mass over an unbounded number of pairs even though each restriction map has
degree at most three.  The group
`B/<z> ~= Z*C_2` has continuous high-dimensional representation families,
so bounded branching alone does not give a uniform spectral separation
between inequivalent `B`-irreducibles.  A completion must use the opposite
modular extension conditions both to control this cross-pair mass transport
and to prove that pairs as in `(ITB23)` cannot approach without approaching
the actual intersection of the two extension loci.

The exact remaining gate is therefore a dimension-free bounded-branching
transport/transversality theorem for growing Iwahori irreducibles.  It has
two parts: localize all but `o(d)` physical mass into correctable cross-pairs,
then correct each near pair to the intersection of the two modular extension
loci.  This is strictly smaller than arbitrary commutant recovery and
strictly smaller than matching unrestricted representation semirings, but
neither part is asserted here.
