# MF reflection and matrix-corona support

**Date:** 2026-08-24

## 1. The reflection

For a countable group `G`, put

```text
R_MF(G)=G/Rad_MF(G).
```

The quotient is MF, and every homomorphism from `G` to a countable MF group
factors uniquely through it.  Thus `R_MF` is a left adjoint to the inclusion
of countable MF groups into countable groups, with a surjective unit.

This gives the construction calculus

```text
MF limit     = ordinary group limit, when the result is countable,
MF colimit   = R_MF(ordinary group colimit).
```

The limit statement follows because a nonidentity point of a group limit is
detected in one MF coordinate.  The colimit statement is the adjunction.

Hence finite products, equalizers, pullbacks, and countable inverse limits
with countable underlying group stay MF.  Free products, amalgams, HNN
extensions, quotients, and arbitrary countable presentations are handled by
forming the ordinary group and taking one final radical quotient.

## 2. MF-equivalences

A map `f:G->H` is an MF-equivalence when `R_MF(f)` is an isomorphism.  This is
equivalent to

```text
Hom(H,M) ~= Hom(G,M)
```

naturally for every countable MF target `M`.

MF-equivalences satisfy two-out-of-three, are closed under retracts, and are
stable under every countable colimit.  A surjection is an MF-equivalence
exactly when its kernel lies in the MF radical of its source.

If `A` has full MF radical, `A->1` is an MF-equivalence.  Pushing this map out
along any `A->G` proves that

```text
G -> G/normalClosure(image(A))
```

is an MF-equivalence.  Because the class is colimit-stable, this remains true
inside arbitrary subsequent amalgams, HNN extensions, directed unions, and
presentation gluings.

## 3. The intrinsic topology

Give `G` the topology generated at the identity by kernels of maps to
countable MF groups.  The closure of the identity is exactly `Rad_MF(G)`, so
`G->R_MF(G)` is its Hausdorffization.  The topology is Hausdorff exactly when
`G` is MF.

After imposing a normal subgroup `N`, the preimage of the identity closure in
`G/N` is exactly `mfroot_G(N)`.  Thus MF-semantic consequence is topological
indistinguishability in the intrinsic residual topology of the presented
quotient.

## 4. Projection ideals in a matrix corona

For

```text
Q_d=(prod_n M_(d_n)(C))/(directSum_n M_(d_n)(C)),
```

let projections `p,q` have coordinate ranks `r_n,s_n`.  Then

```text
q in Ideal(p) iff s_n<=m r_n eventually for some fixed m.
```

In particular `p` is full exactly when `d_n<=m r_n` eventually.  Its corner
is the matrix corona with coordinate sizes `r_n` on the support of `p`.

Rank-one projections supported on subsets `S subset N` give

```text
Ideal(e_S)<=Ideal(e_T) iff S subset T modulo a finite set.
```

Thus every infinite matrix corona contains a copy of `P(N)/Fin` in its ideal
lattice and has continuum many pairwise incomparable projection-generated
ideals.

## 5. Ideal-valued support of a representation

For `rho:G->U(Q_d)`, define

```text
Supp_rho(g)=Ideal(rho(g)-1).
```

Then

```text
Supp(gh)<=Supp(g) join Supp(h),
Supp([g,h])<=Supp(g) meet Supp(h),
Supp(hgh^(-1))=Supp(g).
```

For an ideal `J`, the elements with support contained in `J` form exactly the
kernel of the representation in `Q_d/J`.  Finite-order elements have support
generated by their nontrivial spectral projection, so their support is
classified by the eventual growth class of its coordinate ranks.

This replaces a yes/no visibility mark by a continuum-valued ideal geometry.

The calculus is sharp already in the constant `2 x 2` corona.  If `X` and
`Z` are Pauli involutions localized on coordinate sets `S` and `T`, then

```text
Supp(X_S Z_T)=J_(S union T),
Supp([X_S,Z_T])=J_(S intersection T).
```

Thus multiplication realizes union and commutators realize intersection.
Support sets modulo finite error also give continuum many conjugacy classes
of involutions.  By allocating a disjoint infinite coordinate set to every
nonedge, every countable graph becomes the exact commutation graph of a
family of nonidentity involutions in this single corona.

The pairwise support blocks may be chosen disjoint.  Quotienting by the ideal
supported on any selected union of blocks kills exactly those commutators.
For a graph with infinitely many nonedges, the quotient lattice therefore
contains the full power-set lattice of its nonedges, and one fixed family of
involutions realizes every graph completion across those quotients.

## 6. Boolean towers

Let `H=EL_12(L_(F_2)(1,2))`, the explicit infinite simple group with full MF
radical.  For a countable set `I`,

```text
K_I=directSum_(i in I)H
```

has full MF radical.  Every normal subgroup is uniquely `K_S` for a subset
`S subset I`, so its normal-subgroup lattice is `P(I)`.  Every normal subgroup
and every nontrivial quotient again has full MF radical.

For finite `I`, the groups `H^n` are pairwise nonisomorphic and have exactly
`n` minimal normal subgroups.  For every MF group `Q`, the groups `H^n x Q`
all have reflection `Q`, while their characteristic radicals retain these
different Boolean lengths.

## 7. Finitely presented Grushko towers

Let `B` be the finitely presented Kazhdan Hilbert-hotel group with full MF
radical.  Its free powers

```text
A_n=B * ... * B
```

are finitely presented full-radical groups.  Property `FA` makes `B` freely
indecomposable, and Grushko uniqueness distinguishes the `A_n`.

For every finitely presented MF group `Q`,

```text
E_(n,Q)=A_n x Q
```

is finitely presented and non-MF, has radical `A_n`, and has the same maps to
every MF target as `Q`.  The same is true for compact targets and
finite-dimensional linear targets over every field.  The groups `E_(n,Q)`
are pairwise nonisomorphic because their characteristic radicals have
different Grushko lengths.

The reflection `Q` therefore has infinitely many finitely presented algebraic
realizations with identical MF-visible representation theory and sharply
different invisible geometry.

## 8. Regular star kernels

Fix a perfect group `B` with full MF radical and a normal generator `d`, and
write `D=<d>`.  The cyclic-edge realization

```text
C_d(Q)=B *_D (Q x D)
```

has a split quotient `pi_Q:C_d(Q)->Q`.  Its hidden kernel has a complete
Bass--Serre description.  If `B_q=qBq^(-1)`, then

```text
ker(pi_Q) ~= *_(D,q in Q) B_q,
C_d(Q) ~= ker(pi_Q) semidirectProduct Q.
```

The first group is the star amalgam of one copy of `B` for every element of
`Q`, with all copies of `D` identified pointwise.  The quotient group acts by
left translation on the index set and fixes `D`.  Thus the group law of `Q`
is encoded exactly by the permutation action on a regular family of
MF-invisible vertex groups.

The kernel is perfect and has full MF radical.  Perfectness holds because it
is generated by perfect subgroups `B_q`; full invisibility holds because
every map to an MF target is trivial on each `B_q`.  Hence every countable
group occurs as the quotient of a split extension by an explicitly described
perfect MF black hole.

## 9. Functorial camouflage

The preceding construction is functorial.  A homomorphism `f:Q->R` induces
the unique map `C_d(f)` which is the identity on `B` and sends

```text
(q,x) |-> (f(q),x)
```

on the other vertex group.  This gives natural transformations

```text
pi:C_d => Id,                 s:Id => C_d,
pi after s = id.
```

The functor is faithful and preserves injections and surjections.  For an
injection, the regular-star normal form embeds the vertex groups indexed by
`Q` into the distinct vertex groups indexed by `f(Q)`.  For a surjection, the
two vertex images generate the target amalgam.

Every component `pi_Q` is at once a split epimorphism with perfect
full-radical kernel, an MF-equivalence, and an order isomorphism on complete
lattices of MF-closed normal subgroups.  Thus MF-invisible thickening is not
merely an existence construction: it is a faithful algebraic operation on
the entire category of countable groups.

## 10. One universal countable kernel

The star kernel depends only on the cardinality of the underlying set of
`Q`, not on its multiplication.  For a nonempty set `I`, write

```text
K_I=*_(D,i in I) B_i.
```

Because the same element `d` lies in every vertex group and normally
generates each copy of `B`, it normally generates the entire group `K_I`.
Thus `K_I` is simultaneously perfect, singly normally generated, and
full-MF-radical.

The group `K_I` is finitely generated exactly when `I` is finite.  A finite
set of reduced amalgam words involves only finitely many vertex factors and
therefore cannot generate an element of a new factor `B_i\D`.  Conversely,
finitely many finitely generated vertex groups generate a finite star.

It follows that one fixed group

```text
K_infinity=*_(D,n in N) B_n
```

serves as the kernel for every countably infinite quotient.  Different
groups `Q` supply different left-regular actions on the same abstract group:

```text
C_d(Q) ~= K_infinity semidirectProduct Q.
```

When `Q` is MF, `K_infinity` is the exact MF radical.  When `Q` is also
finitely generated, the ambient split extension is finitely generated even
though its perfect exact radical is not.  Taking `Q=Z` gives one finitely
generated group whose infinitely generated radical is normally generated by
the single word `d`, and whose quotient becomes MF exactly when that word is
killed.

## 11. Exact compression plus local stability

For `L<=G`, let `D(G,L)` be the intrinsic compression--centralizer defect.
Exact finite-dimensional commutant rigidity gives

```text
D(G,L)<=Rad_fd(G).
```

If `G` is point-norm matricially stable, then

```text
Rad_fd(G)=Rad_MF(G),
```

and therefore

```text
D(G,L)<=Rad_MF(G).
```

Thus a nontrivial compression defect obstructs MF, and a normally generating
compression defect gives full MF radical.

The global stability hypothesis can be localized.  For a fixed `w in G`, it
is enough that every operator-norm asymptotic homomorphism `phi_n` admit
genuine homomorphisms `pi_n` satisfying only

```text
||phi_n(w)-pi_n(w)|| -> 0.
```

If every exact finite-dimensional representation kills `w`, this one-value
correction already puts `w` in `Rad_MF(G)`.

## 12. One-row operator-norm collapse

Let `e,f` be finite-dimensional projections with `rank(e)>rank(f)`.  For
every unitary `J`, rank-nullity applied to `fJ:eH->fH` gives

```text
||(1-f)Je||_op=1.
```

In particular, if exact `M_p` and `M_(p^2)` packets share a nonzero carrier
`P`, their minimal projections have ranks `rank(P)/p` and `rank(P)/(p^2)`.
Any return from the first to the second therefore has wrong-target leakage
exactly one.  No lower bound on the carrier's relative ambient rank is
needed.

More precisely, the singular value `1` occurs with multiplicity at least
`rank(e)-rank(f)`.  Hence every Schatten norm sees the same deficit:

```text
Tr(|(1-f)Je|^p) >= rank(e)-rank(f).
```

If the return is only an approximate isometry with
`||J^*J-1||<=eps`, the first `rank(e)-rank(f)` singular values remain at
least `sqrt(1-eps)`.  Thus the obstruction survives polar correction and
fixed-packet perturbation quantitatively.

This yields a finite-coordinate MF criterion.  Suppose survival of a marked
word in increasingly accurate matrix models produces, on one nonzero common
carrier, exact coarse and fine packets and a fixed return word `J` satisfying

```text
||(1-f)Je||_op <= omega(relator defect),
omega(t)->0.
```

The rank identity contradicts the vanishing modulus, so the mark lies in the
MF radical.  Normal generation then gives full MF radical.

For the binary Leavitt matrix chart, the smallest remaining instance is the
first prefix refinement.  With

```text
x=s_00 t_0,       y=s_0 t_00,
yx=e_00,          xy=f_00,00,
```

the target is to authenticate the fixed crossed-prefix return word on one
finite multiplicity atom so that

```text
||(1-f_00,00)J_x e_00||_op=o(1).
```

Once this one-row statement is proved, the exact rank floor kills the marked
complementary root, and the established elementary commutator saturation
propagates the kill to the whole group.

## 13. Graphs of full-radical groups

The class of countable groups with full MF radical is closed under quotients,
extensions, and countable colimits.  The proofs are direct: every map to an
MF target is trivial on each input group, and the input images generate the
relevant quotient, extension, or colimit.

Let `G(Y)` be a connected countable graph of groups whose vertex groups all
have full MF radical.  Killing the vertex groups gives

```text
pi_1(G(Y)) -> pi_1(Y),
```

and every map to an MF group factors uniquely through this quotient.  Since
`pi_1(Y)` is free and therefore MF,

```text
Rad_MF(pi_1(G(Y)))
  =normalClosure(all vertex groups),
R_MF(pi_1(G(Y)))=pi_1(Y).
```

Thus a tree of full-radical groups again has full MF radical, while each
independent graph cycle contributes exactly one free MF-visible generator.
Finite graphs with finitely presented vertex groups and finitely generated
edge groups give finitely presented realizations of these free reflections.

## 14. Principal closure over quotient-MF groups

Suppose every quotient of `Q` is MF.  The closure transplantation formula
then becomes

```text
cl_MF^(C_d(Q))(N)=N normalClosure(d)
```

for every normal subgroup `N`.  Consequently

```text
C_d(Q)/N is MF  iff  d in N.
```

The MF-closed normal subgroups are precisely those containing the hidden
kernel, and their complete lattice is canonically the normal-subgroup lattice
of `Q`.  Every amenable `Q` has this property because all of its quotients
are amenable and MF.  Thus one fixed word classifies MF throughout the full
quotient lattice for arbitrary amenable visible quotients.

## 15. Arbitrary permutation camouflage

For a countable `Q`-set `X`, let

```text
K_X=*_(D,x in X)B_x,
W(Q,X)=K_X semidirectProduct Q,
```

where `Q` permutes the vertex copies and fixes their common subgroup `D`.
Every map from `W(Q,X)` to an MF group kills `K_X` and factors uniquely
through `Q`.  Hence its MF radical is the preimage of `Rad_MF(Q)`.

The same argument works after imposing arbitrary relations.  For every
normal subgroup `N normal W(Q,X)`,

```text
cl_MF^(W(Q,X))(N)=pi^(-1)(cl_MF^Q(pi(N))).
```

If every quotient of `Q` is MF, this reduces to

```text
cl_MF^(W(Q,X))(N)=N K_X=N normalClosure(d),
W(Q,X)/N is MF  iff  d in N.
```

When `X` is countably infinite, the abstract kernel is always the same group
`K_infinity`; only the permutation action changes.  It is perfect, has full
MF radical, and is normally generated by `d`.  If `B` and `Q` are finitely
generated, the semidirect product is finitely generated exactly when the
action has finitely many orbits.  Thus one fixed invisible kernel realizes
every countable permutation dynamics while preserving exactly the prescribed
MF-visible quotient.

Equivariant maps of actions become MF equivalences.  Every `Q`-map
`p:X->Y` gives, by factor relabeling,

```text
W(Q,X) -> W(Q,Y).
```

The assignment is a faithful functor, and the group map is injective exactly
when `p` is and surjective exactly when `p` is.  Its kernel acts freely on the
star-amalgam tree because the map is injective on every factor.  Both
outgoing Hom functors into an MF group identify with `Hom(Q,M)`, so every
induced map is an exact MF-equivalence.  MF reflection therefore collapses
this entire copy of the category of countable `Q`-sets to the constant object
`Q`.  In fact every noninjective map has kernel `F_infinity`, even if it
identifies only one pair of points.  A full-radical group has no proper
finite-index subgroup, so the kernel of the basic two-factor fold
`B *_D B -> B` is already the fundamental group of two vertices joined by
countably many edges.  Every other noninjective fold kernel retracts onto
this `F_infinity`.

For a finitely presented seed and finitely presented `Q`, the transitive
model `W(Q,Q/S)` is finitely presented whenever `S` is finitely generated.
Taking `Q=F(a,b)` and `S_n=<a^(2^n)>` yields an infinite tower

```text
... -> W(Q,Q/S_(n+1)) -> W(Q,Q/S_n) -> ...
```

of finitely presented non-MF groups.  Every bonding kernel is `F_infinity`,
every universal MF quotient is `F(a,b)`, and every bonding map is invisible
to the complete representation functor into MF targets.

## 16. Universal outer symmetry of one hidden kernel

Let `X` be countably infinite.  Every permutation of `X` acts on

```text
K_infinity=*_(D,x in X)B_x
```

by permuting the factors and fixing `D`.  Distinct factors are not conjugate
inside `K_infinity`: the amalgam normal-form theorem distinguishes their
vertex types.  Therefore a nonidentity factor permutation cannot be inner,
and

```text
Sym(X) -> Out(K_infinity)
```

is injective.  The kernel `K_infinity` is centerless because a central
element must lie in every factor and hence in `D`, while `B` is nonabelian
simple and centerless.

The construction extends from permutations to arbitrary self-maps.  A map
`f:X->X` induces an endomorphism `Phi_f` by sending `B_x` identically onto
`B_(f(x))`.  Normal form gives the exact dictionary

```text
Phi_f injective   iff f injective,
Phi_f surjective  iff f surjective,
Phi_f invertible  iff f bijective.
```

Moreover, distinct self-maps induce distinct endomorphisms.  Hence the full
transformation monoid of `X` embeds in `End(K_infinity)`.  Every countable
monoid embeds in a countable full transformation monoid by its left regular
action, so `End(K_infinity)` contains every countable monoid.  An injective
non-surjective shift and a surjective non-injective fold show that
`K_infinity` is respectively non-co-Hopfian and non-Hopfian.

There is also an exact Hilbert-hotel identity.  For every nonempty countable
set `I`,

```text
*_(D,i in I) K_infinity^(i) ~= K_infinity.
```

Indeed, expanding each copy into its countable family of `B`-factors and
flattening the indices replaces `I x N` by `N`.  Thus `K_infinity` absorbs
any countable number of copies of itself over the single cyclic subgroup
generated by `d`.

Partitions compute the kernels of the factor folds exactly.  For an
equivalence relation `E` on `X`, let `N_E` be normally generated by the words
`b_x b_y^(-1)` with `x E y` and `b in B`.  Then

```text
K_X/N_E ~= K_(X/E).
```

For a self-map `f`, `ker(Phi_f)=N_(ker f)` and its image is the subamalgam
on `f(X)`.  Consequently the partition lattice of `X` embeds
order-preservingly into the normal-subgroup lattice of `K_X`, with joins
sent to normal-subgroup joins.  There are continuum many partitions of `N`
with countably infinite quotient.  Each yields a distinct normal subgroup
`N_E` for which

```text
K_infinity/N_E ~= K_infinity.
```

Every such quotient splits because a quotient map of countable sets has a
section and factor relabeling is functorial.  Thus the same full-radical group
has continuum many distinct split self-quotient kernels.

Every nonzero partition kernel is `F_infinity`.  Indeed, the quotient map is
injective on each amalgam factor, so its kernel is free, while any one
identified pair supplies a retraction onto the two-factor fold kernel
`F_infinity`.  Hence

```text
K_infinity ~= F_infinity semidirectProduct K_infinity
```

in continuum many distinct ways.  The normal subgroup `F_infinity` is MF
with trivial MF radical, while the ambient group has full MF radical.  Thus
MF-radical heredity fails even for normal subgroups inside this one fixed
group.

Every countable group embeds in `Sym(X)`.  Thus one fixed countable
centerless perfect group, normally generated by one involution and having
full MF radical, has an outer automorphism group containing every countable
group.  Each such permutation action lifts to `Aut(K_infinity)`, and its
semidirect product retains the exact radical and semantic-closure formulas
of Section 15.

For every infinite set `S` of primes, the amenable MF group

```text
Q_S=directSum_(p in S) C_p
```

has a different set of element orders.  The corresponding split extensions
`K_infinity semidirectProduct Q_S` are therefore continuum many pairwise
nonisomorphic countable groups with the same characteristic MF radical
`K_infinity` and with amenable abelian universal MF quotients.

This universal symmetry cannot occur for a finitely generated countable
kernel: an endomorphism of a finitely generated group is determined by the
images of finitely many generators, so its endomorphism monoid is countable,
whereas the full transformation monoid of `N` is uncountable.

## 17. One universal presentation compiler at each generator rank

Let `F_X` be the free group on a nonempty finite or countable set `X`, and
fix the single group

```text
U_X=C_d(F_X).
```

If `R normal F_X` and `Q=F_X/R`, impose the elements of `R` only in the
canonical visible copy of `F_X`.  The resulting quotient is exactly

```text
U_X/normalClosure(R) ~= C_d(Q).
```

The hidden factor and the word `d` are unchanged.  Imposing the one further
relation `d=1` gives `Q`.  If `Q` is MF, the intermediate group is non-MF,
its radical is `normalClosure(d)`, and every map from it to an MF target
factors uniquely through `Q`.

Thus one fixed source `U_n` compiles every `n`-generated MF group.  When the
seed and `Q` are finitely presented, the compiler and the intermediate group
are finitely presented: one merely adds the finite list of visible relators.
The countable source `U_infinity` similarly compiles every countable MF
group.

The map

```text
R |-> normalClosure_(U_X)(R)
```

embeds the complete lattice of normal subgroups of `F_X` into that of
`U_X` and preserves arbitrary joins.  Its intersection with the visible
free factor recovers `R` exactly.  Above the fixed radical
`normalClosure(d)`, the ordinary correspondence theorem identifies the
entire quotient interval with the normal-subgroup lattice of `F_X`; the MF
members of that interval are precisely the MF groups generated by `X`.

The compiler quotient has an exact split kernel.  For `R!=1`, the regular
star indexed by `F_X` folds noninjectively onto the star indexed by `Q`, so
its kernel is `F_infinity`.  The visible section contributes `R`, giving

```text
ker(U_X->C_d(Q)) ~= F_infinity semidirectProduct R.
```

Thus even one nontrivial visible relation creates a canonical infinite-rank
free folding layer, while the final word `d` controls all MF invisibility.

## 18. Black-hole attachments as semantic relation cells

Let `C<=B`, embed `C` in a countable group `G`, and form

```text
A=B *_C G.
```

Every map from `A` to an MF group is trivial on `B`, hence kills the embedded
copy of `C` in `G`.  Conversely, every map from

```text
barG=G/normalClosure_G(C)
```

extends uniquely across `A` by using the trivial map on `B`.  Therefore

```text
Hom(A,M) ~= Hom(barG,M),
R_MF(A) ~= R_MF(barG)
```

naturally for every MF target `M`.  If `barG` is MF, the radical of `A` is
the normal closure of the attached `B`.  When `C=<c>` and `c` normally
generates `B`, this radical is generated by the single word `c`, which
remains nonidentity by amalgam normal form.

The same proof works for any finite or countable family of attachments to
one visible group: the MF reflection is obtained by killing exactly the
normal closure of all attached subgroups.  Finite attachments preserve
finite presentation when the vertex groups are finitely presented and the
attaching subgroups are finitely generated.  Hence one fixed black-hole
piece is an exact semantic relation cell: attaching it forces a chosen
subgroup to vanish in every MF target while retaining it in the group.
