# A single Laurent root normally generates the augmentation sofic radical

Retain the explicit binary Kun--Thom pair

\[
 R_+=\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_d],\qquad
 R=\mathbb F_2[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_d^{\pm1}],
\]

\[
 \Gamma=\operatorname{EL}_r(R_+),\qquad
 N=\operatorname{EL}_r(R),\qquad
 G=N\rtimes\operatorname{SL}_d(\mathbb Z),
\]

where \(r,d\geq3\).  Let

\[
 X=G/\Gamma,\qquad Y=G/N,
\]

and let

\[
 p_*:I_X\longrightarrow I_Y
\]

be the map between augmentation modules induced by \(p:X\to Y\).  Put

\[
 P=I_X\rtimes G,\qquad M=\ker p_*.
\]

The complete-radical calculation in
`AUGMENTATION_SOFIC_RADICAL.md` gives

\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(P)=M.                 \tag{1}
\]

This note proves that the whole radical in (1) is normally generated by one
explicit element.

## 1. An orbital-module lemma

Let \(\Gamma\leq G\), let \(h\in G\), and set

\[
 H=\langle\Gamma,h\rangle.
\]

Write \(X=G/\Gamma\), and define

\[
 v_h=\delta_{h\Gamma}-\delta_\Gamma\in I_X.
\]

**Lemma 1.**  The cyclic \(\mathbb ZG\)-submodule generated by \(v_h\) is

\[
 \mathbb ZGv_h
 =\ker\bigl(\mathbb Z[G/\Gamma]\longrightarrow
                    \mathbb Z[G/H]\bigr).              \tag{2}
\]

In particular the right side lies in \(I_X\).

**Proof.**  Give \(G/\Gamma\) the graph having, for every \(g\in G\), an
edge

\[
 g\Gamma\longleftrightarrow gh\Gamma.
\]

The edge differences are precisely

\[
 g v_h=\delta_{gh\Gamma}-\delta_{g\Gamma}.
\]

The connected component of \(g\Gamma\) is \(gH/\Gamma\): an edge path
multiplies on the right by letters in \(\Gamma\cup\{h,h^{-1}\}\), and every
word in those letters gives such a path.  Hence the components are indexed
by \(G/H\).

For any graph, the kernel of the map from the free abelian group on its
vertices to the free abelian group on its connected components is generated
by the differences across its edges.  Applying this observation to the
orbital graph proves (2).  Since every edge difference has augmentation
zero, the kernel is contained in \(I_X\). \(\square\)

## 2. One Laurent root generates the Laurent elementary group over the
polynomial subgroup

Put

\[
 \mu=(x_1x_2\cdots x_d)^{-1}\in R,
 \qquad h=e_{12}(\mu)\in N.                            \tag{3}
\]

The element \(h\) does not belong to \(\Gamma\), since its \((1,2)\)-entry
is not in \(R_+\).

**Lemma 2.**

\[
 \langle\Gamma,h\rangle=N.                            \tag{4}
\]

**Proof.**  Constant Weyl matrices belong to
\(\operatorname{EL}_r(\mathbb F_2)\leq\Gamma\).  Since the characteristic
is two, their conjugation moves \(e_{12}(\mu)\) to \(e_{ij}(\mu)\) for every
ordered pair \(i\ne j\), without a sign issue.

Fix \(1\leq \ell\leq d\), and put

\[
 b_\ell=\prod_{s\ne\ell}x_s\in R_+.
\]

For distinct root indices \(i,j,k\), the elementary commutator relation

\[
 [e_{ij}(a),e_{jk}(b)]=e_{ik}(ab)                     \tag{5}
\]

gives

\[
 [e_{ij}(\mu),e_{jk}(b_\ell)]
   =e_{ik}(x_\ell^{-1}).                               \tag{6}
\]

Thus \(\langle\Gamma,h\rangle\) contains every root element with coefficient
\(x_\ell^{-1}\), in every root position.

Relation (5), together with conjugation by constant Weyl matrices, lets us
multiply any two already available coefficients while retaining an arbitrary
root position.  Starting with the polynomial coefficients in \(R_+\) and
the coefficients \(x_1^{-1},\ldots,x_d^{-1}\) from (6), we therefore obtain

\[
 e_{ij}(x_1^{n_1}\cdots x_d^{n_d})
 \quad(n_1,\ldots,n_d\in\mathbb Z)                    \tag{7}
\]

for every \(i\ne j\).  Finally,

\[
 e_{ij}(a)e_{ij}(b)=e_{ij}(a+b)                        \tag{8}
\]

gives \(e_{ij}(f)\) for every Laurent polynomial \(f\in R\).  These root
elements generate \(\operatorname{EL}_r(R)=N\), proving (4). \(\square\)

## 3. The singly normally generated radical

Let

\[
 v=\delta_{h\Gamma}-\delta_\Gamma\in I_X\leq P,       \tag{9}
\]

with \(h\) as in (3).

**Theorem 3.**

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(P)
   =M
   =\mathbb ZGv
   =\langle\!\langle v\rangle\!\rangle_P.
 }                                                       \tag{10}
\]

**Proof.**  Lemmas 1 and 2 give

\[
 \mathbb ZGv
 =\ker\bigl(\mathbb Z[G/\Gamma]\to\mathbb Z[G/N]\bigr)
 =M.                                                     \tag{11}
\]

Because \(I_X\) is an abelian normal subgroup of \(P=I_X\rtimes G\), the
normal closure of \(v\) in \(P\) is exactly its \(\mathbb ZG\)-span.  Now
combine (11) with (1). \(\square\)

Consequently, for every homomorphism \(\phi:P\to S\),

\[
 \phi(v)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \phi(M)=1.      \tag{12}
\]

In particular every homomorphism from \(P\) to a sofic group kills the one
fixed element \(v\), while any Connes-embeddable representation satisfying
\(\phi(v)\ne1\) has a hyperlinear nonsofic image.  The FALSE-side model
production problem for this quotient is therefore a single explicit
trace-separation problem, not a search over the whole radical.

## 4. Bounded-exponent variants

For \(m\geq2\), put

\[
 P_m=(I_X/mI_X)\rtimes G,
\]

and let \(M_m\) be the image of \(M\) in \(I_X/mI_X\).  The vector \(v\)
remains nonzero modulo \(m\), because it is the difference of two distinct
basis vectors.

**Corollary 4.**

\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(P_m)
 =M_m
 =(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)G\,\bar v
 =\langle\!\langle\bar v\rangle\!\rangle_{P_m}.        \tag{13}
\]

**Proof.**  Precomposing a homomorphism \(P_m\to S\) to a sofic group with
\(P\to P_m\) shows that it kills \(M_m\).  Conversely,

\[
 P_m/M_m\cong (I_{G/N}/mI_{G/N})\rtimes G
\]

embeds in

\[
 G\times\bigl((I_{G/N}/mI_{G/N})\rtimes G/N\bigr).
\]

The second factor is a subgroup of
\((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\wr(G/N)\), which is residually finite because its
base is finite and \(G/N\) is residually finite.  Thus the displayed quotient
is residually finite and supplies the reverse radical inclusion.  Cyclicity
follows by reducing (11) modulo \(m\). \(\square\)

## 5. The relative-Kazhdan shortcut is unavailable

The cyclicity in (10) might suggest proving relative property \((T)\) for
the pair \((P,M)\), cutting a weak-MF model by the relative Kazhdan
projection, and thereby forcing a tracial corner to see \(v\).  The premise
is false.

**Proposition 5.**  Neither \((P,M)\) nor \((P_m,M_m)\), for \(m\geq2\),
has relative property \((T)\).

**Proof.**  We give the finite-exponent argument; the integral argument is
the same with circle-valued random variables concentrated near \(1\).
Characters of \(I_X/mI_X\) are restrictions of configurations in
\((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^X\).  For \(0<t<1\), choose independently at every
coordinate the value \(0\) with probability \(1-t\) and the value \(1\)
with probability \(t\).  The resulting product measure is invariant under
every permutation of \(X\), hence under \(G\).  Restricting the random
character to \(M_m\) gives a \(G\)-invariant probability measure
\(\nu_t\) on \(\widehat{M_m}\), and \(\nu_t\to\delta_1\) weakly as
\(t\to0\).

The restriction is the trivial character exactly when the random
configuration is constant on every fiber of \(X\to G/N\).  Each fiber is a
translate of \(N/\Gamma\), which is infinite: the cosets
\(e_{12}(x_1^{-k})\Gamma\), \(k\geq1\), are distinct, since the difference
of two distinct negative powers is not in \(R_+\).  An iid nonconstant Bernoulli
configuration is constant on an infinite fiber with probability zero.
Consequently

\[
 \nu_t(\{1\})=0\qquad(0<t<1).                          \tag{14}
\]

The standard dual criterion for relative property \((T)\) of an abelian
normal subgroup says that (14), together with
\(\nu_t\to\delta_1\), is precisely an obstruction to relative property
\((T)\).  This proves the assertion for \((P_m,M_m)\).

For \(M\), replace the Bernoulli values \(0,1\) by iid circle variables whose
laws are non-atomic and converge to the point mass at \(1\).  Restriction to
\(M\) is again almost surely nontrivial and its laws converge to the trivial
character. \(\square\)

Thus property \((T)\) of the ambient Kun--Thom groups cannot by itself turn
operator-norm visibility of \(v\) into positive normalized trace.  Any
successful model must use genuinely nonliftable trace geometry rather than a
relative Kazhdan projection.
