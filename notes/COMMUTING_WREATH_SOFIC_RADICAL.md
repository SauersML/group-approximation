# The complete sofic radical of the Kun--Thom commuting wreath product

Let \(\Gamma<G\) be one of the explicit Kun--Thom infranormal Kazhdan
pairs, and write

\[
 N=\langle\!\langle\Gamma\rangle\!\rangle_G.
\]

Put

\[
 X=G/\Gamma,\qquad Y=G/N,
\]

and let

\[
 A_X=\bigoplus_X\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,qquad
 A_Y=\bigoplus_Y\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

The map \(p:X\to Y\) induces the fiber-sum homomorphism

\[
 p_*:A_X\longrightarrow A_Y,qquad
 p_*(a_x)=a_{p(x)}.                                    \tag{1}
\]

Consider the Kun--Thom group

\[
 W=A_X\rtimes G.                                       \tag{2}
\]

Kun and Thom prove that \(W\) is nonsofic.  We determine exactly which
elements every homomorphism from \(W\) to a sofic group must kill.

## The complete radical

Let

\[
 K=\ker p_*\leq A_X.                                   \tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, \(K\) is generated by the coordinate differences

\[
 a_{gn\Gamma}a_{g\Gamma},qquad g\in G,\ n\in N.       \tag{4}
\]

**Theorem 1.**

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(W)=K.}       \tag{5}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 W/K\cong A_Y\rtimes G                                \tag{6}
\]

is residually finite.

**Proof.**  Let \(\phi:W\to S\) be a homomorphism to a sofic group.  Let
\(r:W\to G\) be the retraction which kills \(A_X\), and replace \(\phi\) by

\[
 \Phi=(\phi,r):W\longrightarrow S\times G.             \tag{7}
\]

The target is sofic and \(\Phi|_G\) is injective.  After embedding the image
of \(\Phi\) into a symmetric-group ultraproduct, put

\[
 q=\Phi(a_\Gamma).
\]

The element \(q\) centralizes \(\Phi(\Gamma)\).  Kun--Thom centralizer
normalization says that the full permutation-ultraproduct centralizer of
\(\Phi(\Gamma)\) is normalized by \(\Phi(G)\).  Consequently

\[
 \Phi(a_{g\Gamma})=\Phi(g)q\Phi(g)^{-1}
 \in C(\Phi(\Gamma))                                   \tag{8}
\]

for every \(g\in G\).  Hence, for \(\gamma\in\Gamma\),

\[
 \Phi(a_{\gamma g\Gamma})
 =\Phi(\gamma)\Phi(a_{g\Gamma})\Phi(\gamma)^{-1}
 =\Phi(a_{g\Gamma}).                                   \tag{9}
\]

The kernel of \(\Phi\) is normal in \(W\).  Therefore it contains all
\(G\)-translates of the differences in (9).  The equivalence relation on
\(X\) generated by these translated edges is the orbit relation of the
normal closure \(N\).  Its classes are exactly the fibers of
\(G/\Gamma\to G/N\).  Edge differences generate the kernel of the fiber-sum
map (1), so

\[
 K\leq\ker\Phi\leq\ker\phi.                            \tag{10}
\]

Thus \(K\subseteq\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(W)\).

For the reverse inclusion, (1) gives (6).  The map

\[
 A_Y\rtimes G\longrightarrow
 G\times(A_Y\rtimes G/N),qquad
 (a,g)\longmapsto(g,(a,gN)),                           \tag{11}
\]

is injective.  The second factor is the restricted wreath product

\[
 (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)\wr(G/N).
\]

The group \(G/N\) is residually finite in the explicit Kun--Thom example,
so this finite-base wreath product is residually finite.  Hence the target
of (11), and therefore \(W/K\), is residually finite.  The quotient map to
this sofic group shows that no element outside \(K\) lies in the sofic
radical.  This proves (5). \(\square\)

## One explicit normal generator

Now specialize to the binary Laurent pair

\[
 \Gamma=\operatorname{EL}_r(\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_d]),
\]

\[
 N=\operatorname{EL}_r(
       \mathbb F_2[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_d^{\pm1}]),
 \qquad r,d\geq3.
\]

Set

\[
 \mu=(x_1\cdots x_d)^{-1},\qquad
 h=e_{12}(\mu),\qquad
 w=a_{h\Gamma}a_\Gamma.                               \tag{12}
\]

The root-generation calculation in
`CYCLIC_AUGMENTATION_SOFIC_RADICAL.md` proves

\[
 \langle\Gamma,h\rangle=N.                            \tag{13}
\]

The orbital graph on \(G/\Gamma\) with edges
\(g\Gamma\leftrightarrow gh\Gamma\) has components \(G/N\).  Its edge
differences are the conjugates of \(w\).  Therefore Theorem 1 strengthens
to the following statement.

**Corollary 2.**

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{sof}}(W)
 =\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle_W.
 }                                                       \tag{14}
\]

Thus every homomorphism from \(W\) to a sofic group kills the one fixed
involution \(w\), and it kills no additional universally forced elements
beyond the normal closure of \(w\).

## Hyperlinear consequence

Let \(\rho:W\to U(\mathcal M)\) be a homomorphism into a
Connes-embeddable finite tracial von Neumann algebra.  If

\[
 \rho(w)\ne1,                                           \tag{15}
\]

then \(\rho(W)\) is hyperlinear and nonsofic.  Indeed it is hyperlinear as a
subgroup of \(U(\mathcal M)\); if it were sofic, the quotient map
\(W\to\rho(W)\) would have to kill (14), contradicting (15).

Because \(w^2=1\), condition (15) is equivalent, after replacing the trace
by its restriction to the generated von Neumann algebra, to positivity of
the single projection

\[
 e_w=\frac{1-\rho(w)}2.                                 \tag{16}
\]

The FALSE-side problem for the original Kun--Thom example is therefore
exactly a one-projection Connes-embeddability problem.
