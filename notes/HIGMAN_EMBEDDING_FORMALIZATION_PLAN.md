# Formalizing Higman's embedding theorem: what it would take

Written 2026-08-19, while trying to close Chiodo Theorem 3.10 / Belegradek
Theorem A.1 (*there is a finitely presented torsion-free group containing a
copy of every finitely presented torsion-free group*) **unconditionally**.

> **Read `GroupApproximation/Higman/Program.lean` first.**  That module is the
> authoritative, Lean-level itemization of what Chiodo's Theorem 2.2 costs,
> and it is sharper than this note in two places: it reduces the *join* of
> benign subgroups to a single statement, `Higman.PinchProperty`, and it
> splits recursive presentability of the absorber into (B1) the direct sum and
> (B2) the effective torsion-free radical.  This note is kept for what it adds
> and `Program.lean` does not carry: the inventory of Mathlib primitives
> verified against the pin, the comparison of the two classical routes, the
> list of routes that were tried and rejected, and the cost estimate.

The conclusion of that attempt is recorded first because it determines
everything else: the statement is now reduced, in Lean and with no
hypotheses, to exactly one thing, and that thing is Higman's embedding
theorem.  This note says what formalizing it would cost, with the primitives
named, so the decision to start is an informed one.

## 1.  Where the reduction stands

`GroupApproximation/Sofic/ChiodoBelegradekTheorem.lean` states the sentence as
one closed formula, `ChiodoBelegradek.Statement`, and proves:

* `statementCountable` --- the same sentence with *finitely presented*
  weakened to *countable* --- **unconditionally**, with
  `Monsters.ChiodoFreeAbsorber = (⨿_c G_c)^tf` as the witness;
* `statementCountable_of_statement` --- the weakening is exactly that one
  clause and nothing else;
* four producers of `Statement`, the sharpest being
  `statement_of_embedding_chiodoFreeAbsorber`: **a finitely presented
  torsion-free group with an injection of `ChiodoFreeAbsorber` into it gives
  the theorem outright.**

So the whole remaining distance is: *the absorber, which is countable,
torsion-free and recursively presented, embeds in a finitely presented
torsion-free group.*  That is Chiodo Theorem 2.2, which is Higman's embedding
theorem with the torsion-order clause added.

## 2.  Why there is no cheaper route

Any such `U` contains every finitely presented torsion-free group, among them
ones with unsolvable word problem, and a finitely presented group whose word
problem were solvable would give solvable word problem to every subgroup
generated by an explicitly listed finite set of its elements.  So producing
`U` proves Novikov--Boone as a corollary, and in fact more: Higman embedding
is strictly stronger, as `Computability/RabinConstruction.lean` already
records.  There is no soft, counting, or compactness argument: a group
containing all recursively presented data must simulate a machine.

Routes considered and rejected during the attempt, recorded so they are not
re-tried:

* *Take a known finitely presented torsion-free group.*  Thompson's `F` has no
  free subgroup of rank two; Higman's four-generator group is not universal;
  no named group is known to be universal except by this theorem.
* *Direct limits / ascending unions.*  Never finitely presented.
* *Small cancellation.*  Produces finitely presented groups only from finite
  input; the universal input is infinite.
* *Plain Higman plus a torsion fix afterwards.*  The torsion-free quotient of
  a finitely presented group need not be finitely presented, and the absorber
  need not survive it.  The torsion clause has to come from the construction,
  which is why Chiodo states 2.2 with `Tord(P⁻) = Tord(T(P))`.

## 3.  What Mathlib supplies (verified against the pin, v4.32.2 /
`905b95818eb32af7874a58b427f50c1711a5e96c`)

* `Mathlib.GroupTheory.HNNExtension` --- HNN extensions, `of`, `t`, `lift`,
  `hom_ext`, transversals, `NormalWord`, and **Britton's Lemma** as
  `HNNExtension.ReducedWord.toList_eq_nil_of_mem_of_range`.
* `Mathlib.GroupTheory.PushoutI` --- amalgamated free products, `of`, `base`,
  `lift`, `NormalWord`, `of_injective`, `base_injective`,
  `Reduced.eq_empty_of_mem_range`, and `inf_of_range_eq_base_range`
  (the intersection of two factors is the base).
* `Mathlib.GroupTheory.CoprodI` --- indexed free products, `Word` normal form,
  `NeWord`, and the ping-pong lemma `lift_injective_of_ping_pong` (plus
  `FreeGroup.injective_lift_of_ping_pong`).
* `Mathlib.GroupTheory.FinitelyPresentedGroup` --- `Group.IsFinitelyPresented`,
  `of_surjective`, `quotient`, `equiv`, `exists_mulEquiv_presentedGroup`,
  and the instances for free groups, finite groups, `PresentedGroup` on a
  finite relator set, and binary free products.
* `Mathlib.GroupTheory.PresentedGroup` --- `toGroup`, `map`, `ext`, and the
  binary `coprodPresentations`.
* `Mathlib.GroupTheory.FreeGroup.NielsenSchreier`.

Not in Mathlib, and needed: the indexed version of `coprodPresentations`
(**now supplied** by `Algebra/CoprodIPresentation.lean`); finite presentation
of an amalgam of finitely presented groups over a finitely generated
subgroup; anything about benign subgroups.

## 4.  What this repository supplies

* `Algebra/HNNFinitePresentation.lean` --- an HNN extension of a finitely
  presented group over a finitely generated associated subgroup is finitely
  presented.  This is the HNN half of the permanence the benign calculus
  needs; the amalgam half does not exist yet and should be written against
  `Monoid.PushoutI` in the same style.
* `Algebra/HNNTorsionFree.lean`, `HNNRetraction.lean`, `HNNCongr.lean`,
  `HNNPresentation.lean`, `HNNTrivialAssociated.lean` --- torsion-freeness of
  HNN extensions, retractions, functoriality.
* `Computability/` (~20k lines) --- Post machines, modular machines,
  `QuadMachine`, the semigroup word problem, the **Boone group** as an HNN
  tower with its Britton-style "goodness" analysis, Novikov--Boone,
  Adian--Rabin, Rabin's construction, and a primitive-recursion layer
  (`FreeGroupCheckerPrimrec`, `PresentationCodes`, `RawTransformPrimrec`).

The Boone development is the single most relevant asset: it is a worked
example, in this repository's own idiom, of *a group built from a machine
whose Britton analysis is carried out in full*.  Higman needs the same kind of
analysis, done once more and in a uniform family.

## 5.  Two routes

**(A) Higman/Rotman, via benign subgroups.**  `H ≤ F` is *benign* if some
finitely presented `K ⊇ F` has a finitely generated `L` with `H = F ∩ L`.
Steps: the closure calculus (finitely generated subgroups are benign; benign
is preserved by enlarging `F`, by joins, by intersections, by images and
preimages along maps of free groups); **Higman's Lemma**, that a subgroup of
`F(a,b)` generated by `{b⁻ⁿ a bⁿ : n ∈ A}` for `A` recursively enumerable is
benign; and the rope trick assembling the theorem.  The calculus is where the
amalgam and HNN permanence lemmas are consumed, and Higman's Lemma is where a
machine is simulated.

**(B) Aanderaa--Cohen, via modular machines.**  Build `G(M)` for a modular
machine `M`, prove the configuration-to-group-element dictionary by Britton,
and derive the embedding.  This reuses `Computability/ModularMachine*.lean`
and the Boone idiom directly, and it avoids the benign calculus.

**Recommendation: (A), and the `Higman/` lane has taken it.**  Route (A) is
what `GroupApproximation/Higman/` implements, and it has got further than this
note originally assumed: intersections, kernels, preimages and finite
intersections of benign subgroups are proved, the amalgam is identified with
Mathlib's `PushoutI` so the normal form is available, and the join is reduced
to `Higman.PinchProperty`.  Route (B)'s machine content would still follow the
Boone modules, because that is the only part of this repository that has ever
survived a Britton argument end to end --- and `PinchProperty` is itself a
Britton induction, so the Boone idiom applies to it too.

## 6.  Module order, with the safe part first

Safe, mechanical, no machine content --- each of these is a self-contained
Mathlib-style theorem:

1. **DONE.**  `Algebra/CoprodIFinitePresentation.lean` --- a free product of
   finitely many finitely presented groups is finitely presented (the indexed
   case; Mathlib has only the binary one), proved through
   `Algebra/CoprodIPresentation.lean`.  Then
   `Algebra/PushoutIFinitePresentation.lean` --- a pushout of finitely many
   finitely presented groups along a finitely generated group is finitely
   presented, hence so is an amalgam `G₁ *_H G₂` over a finitely generated
   `H`.  Both mirror `Algebra/HNNFinitePresentation.lean`: ambient free
   product, relators `{of i (φ i s) · (of i₀ (φ i₀ s))⁻¹ : s ∈ S}` for `S` a
   finite generating set of the base, then
   `Group.IsFinitelyPresented.quotient`, with an equalizer argument
   (`MonoidHom.eq_of_eqOn_dense`) to get from `S` to all of `H`.
2. `Algebra/PushoutITorsionFree.lean` --- an amalgam of torsion-free groups is
   torsion-free.  From `PushoutI.NormalWord`: a cyclically reduced word of
   length `≥ 1` has powers of unbounded length.  (Mathlib does **not** have
   this; it is the Kurosh-type fact the absorber deliberately avoided
   needing.)
3. **DONE, in the `Higman/` lane, not here.**  `Higman/Benign.lean` has the
   definition (`BenignWitness`, bundled), the base case, intersections and
   kernels; `Higman/BenignClosure.lean` has preimages and finite
   intersections; `Higman/AmalgamPresentation.lean` and
   `Higman/AmalgamPushout.lean` have the binary amalgam and its finite
   presentation.  An earlier draft of this step lived in
   `Algebra/BenignSubgroup.lean` and `Algebra/BinaryAmalgam.lean`; both were
   **deleted** as duplicates once the `Higman/` lane was found, and the one
   lemma of theirs that the `Higman/` lane did not have was rewritten against
   `BenignWitness` as `Higman/BenignAmbient.lean`.
4. The rest of the calculus: `Benign.mapEmb` --- the image half of Lemma 3.3,
   **done** in `Higman/BenignAmbient.lean`, the first closure lemma that needs
   an amalgam rather than a direct product; then the join, which is
   `Higman.PinchProperty`; then Higman's operations on subsets.

Machine content, in Boone's idiom, only after the above:

5. The uniform machine-to-group family and its Britton dictionary.
6. Higman's Lemma for recursively enumerable exponent sets.
7. The embedding, then Chiodo's torsion clause: torsion in an HNN extension is
   conjugate into the base and torsion in an amalgam into a factor, so a tower
   built over a torsion-free base is torsion-free.  Step 2 and
   `Algebra/HNNTorsionFree.lean` are exactly these two facts.
8. `Monsters.ChiodoFreeAbsorber` is recursively presented (Chiodo Prop 3.8).
   This needs a notion of recursive presentation for countably generated
   groups, which the repository does not have.  The generating type is
   `Monsters.CodedGenerator = Σ c, Fin c.1` and the relator set is
   `Monsters.codedCoprodRels`, which is decidable; the enlargement to the
   torsion-free quotient's relators is the recursively enumerable part.
9. Discharge `ChiodoHost.TorsionFreeHigmanHull ChiodoFreeAbsorber`, then
   `ChiodoBelegradek.statement_of_freeHull` closes the theorem.

## 7.  Honest cost

Steps 1--4 are ordinary Mathlib-style work.  Steps 5--7 are the theorem, and
they have never been formalized in any proof assistant --- not Lean, not Coq,
not Isabelle.  The comparable landmark in this repository is Novikov--Boone,
which Higman embedding strictly contains.  Plan in months and in compiler
iterations, not in sessions, and do not accept a blind draft of steps 5--7:
Britton arguments are exactly the kind that look right and are not.
