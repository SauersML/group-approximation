## 2. Structure of the commuting-lamp quotient

**Proposition 2.1.**  `W/<zeta> = F_2[X] semidirect V`, the permutational
wreath product `F_2 wr_{V/Gbar} V`.

*Proof.*  `zeta` is central in `W` and lies in `ClLamp(X)`, so
`W/<zeta> = (ClLamp(X)/<zeta>) semidirect V`.  In `ClLamp(X)/<zeta>` the
images `cbar_x` satisfy `cbar_x^2 = 1` and `cbar_x cbar_y = cbar_y cbar_x`,
and the presentation of `ClLamp(X)` becomes the presentation of the
elementary abelian `2`-group on the basis `X`; the sites remain distinct
because the finite Clifford subgroups have order `2^{|F|+1}` on a finite site
set `F`, so their images have order `2^{|F|}`.  Hence
`ClLamp(X)/<zeta> = F_2[X]` with the permutation action of `V`. []

**Proposition 2.2 (coordinates).**  As a `V`-set,
```
X  =  Z  x  (Z[1/2]/Z)^3,
```
with `Torus := (Z[1/2]/Z)^3` and

* `tau . (n,y) = (n+1, y)`,
* `(v,A) . (n,y) = (n, 2^{-n} v + A y)` for `(v,A) in T = Z[1/2]^3 sd SL_3(Z)`.

Under this identification `o = (0,0)`, `tau o = (1,0)`,
`v_1 tau o = (1, e_1/2)`, and `level(n,y) = n`.

*Proof.*  `level : X -> Z` is onto with fibre over `n` the `T`-orbit
`T tau^n Gbar / Gbar = T / (tau^n Gbar tau^{-n}) = T / Gbar_{-n}`.  Since
`Gbar_{-n} = 2^n Z^3 sd SL_3(Z)` and `SL_3(Z)` preserves `2^n Z^3`, the map
`(v,A) Gbar_{-n} |-> v + 2^n Z^3` is a bijection
`T/Gbar_{-n} -> Z[1/2]^3 / 2^n Z^3`, equivariant for
`(v,A).(w + 2^nZ^3) = v + Aw + 2^nZ^3`.  Rescaling by `y = 2^{-n} w`
identifies `Z[1/2]^3/2^nZ^3` with `Torus` and turns the action into
`y |-> 2^{-n}v + Ay`.  Finally `tau` sends the class of `w` at level `n` to
the class of `abar(w) = 2w` at level `n+1`, which in `y`-coordinates is
`2^{-(n+1)}(2w) = y`.  The three displayed points are immediate, and
`e_1/2 != 0` in `Torus`. []

So `W/<zeta>` is a lamplighter with `F_2` lamps over a two-coordinate site
set: a `Z` of telescope levels, and inside each level a copy of the
`2`-Prufer torus `(Z(2^infty))^3` on which the affine group acts, the
translation part acting `2^n`-times finer at level `n`.

**Corollary 2.3.**  `E/<w>` surjects onto `W/<zeta>` with kernel the image of
`K = ker(E -> W)`, and `w` is not in `K`.  Both quotients contain the image
`ubar` of `u`, and `ubar != 1` in `W/<zeta>` (`commuting-lamp-defect-survives`:
`ubar = cbar_{(1,0)} + cbar_{(1,e_1/2)} != 0`).  In particular
`u` is not in `{1,w}`.

## 3. Every exact finite model collapses: the quotients are not LEF

**Theorem 3.1.**  None of `E`, `W`, `E/<w>`, `W/<zeta>` is locally embeddable
into finite groups.  Consequently none of them is residually finite.

*Proof.*  Let `G` be any group carrying elements `s_1,...,s_6` (images of the
base generators), `tau`, `c` such that the relations
```
tau s_i tau^{-1} = abar(s_i)   (i = 1..6),      [c, s_i] = 1   (i = 1..6)
```
hold, and such that `u = [tau c tau^{-1}, s_1] != 1`.  All four groups
qualify: the relations are among the defining relators of `E` and survive in
every quotient, and `u != 1` holds in `E` and `W` because `u^2 = w != 1`, and
in `E/<w>`, `W/<zeta>` by Corollary 2.3.

Suppose `G` is LEF.  Let `F` be a finite subset of `G` containing `1`, `u`,
`c`, `tau`, `tau^{-1}`, `tau c tau^{-1}`, the `s_i`, and every prefix of the
six words `abar(s_i)` and of the commutator `[tau c tau^{-1}, s_1]`.  Choose a
finite group `H` and a map `phi : F -> H` that is injective and satisfies
`phi(xy) = phi(x)phi(y)` whenever `x, y, xy in F`.

Put `Gamma^ = <phi(s_1),...,phi(s_6)> <= H`.  Multiplicativity on `F`
transports the six compression relations verbatim:
`phi(tau) phi(s_i) phi(tau)^{-1} = phi(abar(s_i))`, and `phi(abar(s_i))` is the
corresponding word in the `phi(s_j)`, hence lies in `Gamma^`.  Therefore
```
phi(tau) Gamma^ phi(tau)^{-1} <= Gamma^ .
```
Conjugation is an automorphism of the *finite* group `H`, so the left side has
the same cardinality as `Gamma^`, and the inclusion is an equality.  (This is
`finite-quotient-blindness` in its local form, and an instance of
`invariant-size-collapse` with `Nat.card` as the invariant size.)

The centralizer relations give `phi(c) in C_H(Gamma^)`, hence
```
phi(tau c tau^{-1}) = phi(tau) phi(c) phi(tau)^{-1}
   in  C_H(phi(tau) Gamma^ phi(tau)^{-1}) = C_H(Gamma^).
```
Since `phi(s_1) in Gamma^`, the two elements commute, so
`phi(u) = [phi(tau c tau^{-1}), phi(s_1)] = 1 = phi(1)`, contradicting
injectivity of `phi` on `F`.  Residual finiteness implies LEF, so it fails
too. []

Theorem 3.1 closes the whole exact-model family of routes for both open MF
claims: residual finiteness, LEF, local embeddability into any class of
groups carrying a conjugation-invariant strictly monotone size, and every MF
proof factoring through `isLEF_of_residuallyFinite`, `isLEF_of_locallyFinite`,
or `isWeakMF_of_isLEF`.  It is the (T)-side analogue of
`CyclicBaseLEFObstruction`, and the proof is shorter: no exponent arithmetic
is needed, only that a conjugation-compressed subgroup of a finite group is
uncompressed.

## 4. The exact finite residual of `W/<zeta>`

Write `Wbar = W/<zeta> = L semidirect V`, `L = F_2[X]`, and
```
L_0 = ker( L -> sum_{n in Z} F_2 ),   e_x |-> e_{level(x)} ,
```
the configurations having an even number of lamps on each telescope level.
`L_0` is spanned by the pairs `e_x + e_{x'}` with `level(x) = level(x')`, and
it is `V`-invariant.  Note `ubar in L_0`.

**Theorem 4.1.**  The finite residual of `Wbar` — the intersection of all
finite-index normal subgroups — is exactly `L_0`.

*Proof.*  (a) *`L_0` is killed by every finite quotient.*  Let
`phi : Wbar -> F` be a homomorphism to a finite group, `K = ker(phi) cap L`,
and `M = L/K`, a finite `F_2[V]`-module.  The action homomorphism
`V -> GL(M)` has finite image, so its kernel `N` is normal of finite index in
`V`, and `N` acts trivially on `M`.

Apply the blindness step of Theorem 3.1 to the finite group `V/N`: the image
of `Gbar` is normalized by the image of `tau`, hence
`q(Gbar) = q(Gbar_k)` for every `k in Z`, where `q : V -> V/N`; since
`T = union_k Gbar_k`, also `q(T) = q(Gbar)`, that is
```
T <= Gbar . N .
```
Let `x, x'` lie in a common level.  Levels are `T`-orbits, so `x' = h x` for
some `h in T`.  Write `x = g Gbar`, so `Stab_V(x) = g Gbar g^{-1}`.  Since `N`
is normal, conjugating the last display by `g` gives
`T = g T g^{-1} <= Stab_V(x) . N`, so `h = sigma n` with
`sigma in Stab_V(x)`, `n in N`.  In `M`, writing `m_z` for the image of `e_z`,
```
m_{x'} = h . m_x = sigma . (n . m_x) = sigma . m_x = m_{sigma x} = m_x ,
```
using that `N` acts trivially on `M`.  Hence `e_x + e_{x'} in K` for all
same-level pairs, so `L_0 <= K <= ker(phi)`.

(b) *Nothing else is killed.*  `Wbar / L_0 = (sum_{n in Z} F_2) semidirect V`
with `V` acting through `level : V -> Z`.  This group is residually finite:
an element with nontrivial `V`-part is separated because `V` is residually
finite (Malcev, `V <= GL_4(Q)` finitely generated); an element
`f != 0` of `sum_Z F_2` with support of diameter `< m` is separated by the
finite quotient `(sum_{Z/m} F_2) semidirect (V / level^{-1}(mZ))`, which
folds `Z` to `Z/m` and is finite of order `2^m m`.  Since `L_0` is the kernel
of `Wbar -> Wbar/L_0` and the latter is residually finite, the finite residual
of `Wbar` is contained in `L_0`. []

**Corollary 4.2.**  `Wbar` is not residually finite; its finite residual is a
locally finite elementary abelian `2`-group, and `Wbar/L_0` is residually
finite, hence LEF, hence MF.  Every "detect the surviving defect by finite
quotients" route is dead in the strongest sense: the defect `ubar` lies in
`L_0`, and *all* of `L_0` is invisible to finite quotients.

## 5. The telescope base is LEF, and MF fails for `Z`-extensions of LEF groups

**Theorem 5.1.**  `ClLamp(X) semidirect T` and `F_2[X] semidirect T` are LEF.

*Proof.*  Fix `k` and consider `ClLamp(X) semidirect Gbar_k`.  `Gbar_k` has
finite orbits on `X` (`finite-telescope-level-coset-orbits`; concretely, in
the coordinates of Proposition 2.2 the `Gbar_k`-orbit of `(n,y)` is
`2^{-n-k}Z^3/Z^3 + SL_3(Z) y`, a finite subset of `Torus`), and `ClLamp(X)`
is locally finite, so every finite subset of `ClLamp(X)` lies in a finite
`Gbar_k`-invariant subgroup `L'` (`finite-site-orbits-invariant-clifford-kernel`).
Thus `ClLamp(X) semidirect Gbar_k` is the directed union of the subgroups
`L' semidirect Gbar_k` with `L'` finite.

Each `L' semidirect Gbar_k` is residually finite: `Aut(L')` is finite, so the
action kernel `N_0 <= Gbar_k` has finite index; given a finite set of
nontrivial elements, choose `N <= N_0` normal of finite index in `Gbar_k`
avoiding their `Gbar_k`-parts (possible since `Gbar_k` is finitely generated
linear, hence residually finite), and map onto the finite group
`L' semidirect (Gbar_k / N)`.

Residually finite implies LEF, LEF passes to directed unions, and
`ClLamp(X) semidirect T = union_k (ClLamp(X) semidirect Gbar_k)` is again a
directed union.  The same argument verbatim, with `F_2[X]` in place of
`ClLamp(X)`, gives the second statement. []

**Corollary 5.2.**  `ClLamp(X) semidirect T` and `F_2[X] semidirect T` are
sofic and MF (`isWeakMF_of_isLEF`, `IsWeakMF.isOperatorMF`), and
```
W = (ClLamp(X) semidirect T) semidirect Z ,
Wbar = (F_2[X] semidirect T) semidirect Z ,
```
both `Z`-extensions of LEF groups by the manuscript's reassociation
`ClLamp(X) sd (T sd Z) = (ClLamp(X) sd T) sd Z`.

**Theorem 5.3 (sharp failure of MF permanence).**  MF is not closed under
extensions with kernel LEF and quotient `Z`.  Explicitly,
`1 -> ClLamp(X) sd T -> W -> Z -> 1` has LEF (hence sofic, hence MF) kernel,
amenable quotient, and non-MF middle group.

*Proof.*  Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.2, and the established
`sofic-non-mf-witness` (`W` is not MF). []

This sharpens `mf-extension-nonclosure` (which used the locally finite kernel
`ClLamp(X)` and the residually finite quotient `V`) and is the reason no
permanence theorem can settle the two open claims: the operator-norm analogue
of the Elek--Szabo theorem — soficity is preserved by extensions with
amenable quotient — is *false*, and `W` is the counterexample.  Both `W` and
`Wbar` are `Z`-extensions of LEF groups; the first is not MF; so the MF
question for the second cannot be answered by any argument that does not see
the central sign.

## 6. The corona Hilbert--Schmidt radical of `Wbar` is exactly the finite residual

Let `Theta : Wbar -> U(Q)` be a corona representation and let
`Theta_2 : Wbar -> U(M^omega)` be the induced tracial representation into the
tracial ultraproduct of the coordinate matrix algebras: choose unitary lifts
`U_{g,n}`, note that two lifts differ by an element of `sum_n M_{d_n}`, whose
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm tends to `0` because it is dominated by the
operator norm; so `Theta_2` is well defined, and it is a homomorphism.

**Theorem 6.1.**  For every corona representation `Theta` of `Wbar`,
`L_0 <= ker Theta_2`.  Conversely every element outside `L_0` is detected by
some `Theta_2`.  Hence
```
{ g in Wbar : Theta_2(g) = 1 for every corona representation Theta } = L_0 ,
```
the finite residual of Theorem 4.1.

*Proof.*  Run the manuscript's transport inside `Wbar`.  Let `B` be the norm
corona of the coordinate Hilbert--Schmidt spaces `K_n = L^2(M_{d_n}, tr)`, let
`beta : Wbar -> U(B)` be the adjoint representation `beta_n(g)x = U_{g,n} x
U_{g,n}^*` (a genuine homomorphism, because operator-norm multiplicative
defects give operator-norm defects of the adjoint maps), and let
`K_omega` be the Hilbert-space ultraproduct.  `Gbar <= V <= Wbar` has property
(T); let `P` be the image of its Kazhdan projection, i.e. the projection onto
the `Gbar`-fixed vectors of `K_omega`.

`abar(Gbar) = tau Gbar tau^{-1} <= Gbar` gives `P <= beta(tau) P beta(tau)^*`,
and `B` is stably finite, so the two Murray--von Neumann equivalent
projections are equal:
```
P = beta(tau) P beta(tau)^* .                                     (6.1)
```
This is `kazhdan-asymptotic-commutant-transport`.  Conjugating (6.1) by
`beta(tau)^{\pm 1}` repeatedly gives `Fix(Gbar_k) = Fix(Gbar)` in `K_omega`
for *every* `k in Z`, not only for `k >= 0`; since `T = union_k Gbar_k`, a
vector fixed by all `Gbar_k` is `T`-fixed, so
```
Fix(Gbar) = Fix(T) .                                              (6.2)
```
The lamp `c_o` commutes with `Gbar = Stab_V(o)` in `Wbar`, so the vector
`xi_o` represented by the lifts of `Theta(c_o)` is `Gbar`-fixed, hence
`T`-fixed by (6.2).  For `g in V` the vector represented by the lifts of
`Theta(c_{go}) = Theta(g c_o g^{-1})` is `beta(g) xi_o`, which is
`g T g^{-1} = T`-fixed because `T` is normal in `V`.  Therefore, for every
site `x` and every `h in T`,
```
|| Theta(c_{hx}) - Theta(c_x) ||_{2,omega} = 0 ,
```
and multiplying by the self-adjoint unitary `Theta(c_x)`, which preserves the
Hilbert--Schmidt norm,
```
|| Theta(c_{hx} + c_x) - 1 ||_{2,omega} = 0 .
```
The `T`-orbit of `x` is its whole level, so every same-level pair lies in
`ker Theta_2`; these pairs generate `L_0`, and `ker Theta_2` is a subgroup.

For the converse, let `g` be outside `L_0`.  By Theorem 4.1 there is a finite
quotient `pi : Wbar -> F` with `pi(g) != 1`.  Compose with the left regular
permutation representation of `F`: it is an honest corona representation
(constant sequence), and for `pi(g) != 1` every basis vector is moved, so the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance of the permutation matrix from `1` is
`sqrt 2`. []

**Corollary 6.2.**  `Rad_MF(Wbar)` is a `V`-submodule of `L_0`, and `Wbar` is
MF if and only if `Rad_MF(Wbar) = 0`.  Any corona representation detecting an
element `g in L_0` must send it to a symmetry `1 - 2p` where `p != 0` is a
projection of *vanishing normalized rank*: `rank(p_n)/d_n -> 0`.

This is the precise shape of the missing model.  It is not by itself an
obstruction: by `hs-invisibility-inflation-no-go` a corona model may be
inflated by a trivial block until every element is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible
without changing any operator norm.  What Theorem 6.1 says is that for `Wbar`
the inflation is not optional — the invisible set is forced, and it is
precisely the finite residual.

## 7. Where the two open claims now stand

### 7.1 They are two faces of one question

Let `u = [d, v_1]` as above.

1. `commuting-lamp-quotient-mf` (`Wbar` is MF) implies
   `unsquared-defect-mf-visible` (`u` is not in `Rad_MF(E)`): compose
   `E -> W -> Wbar` with a faithful corona representation and use
   `commuting-lamp-defect-survives`.  This is the established route
   `commuting-lamp-mf-detects-defect`.
2. `literal-mark-quotient-mf` (`E/<w>` is MF) *also* implies
   `unsquared-defect-mf-visible`, because `u` is not in `<w> = {1,w}` by
   Corollary 2.3, so a faithful corona representation of `E/<w>` pulled back
   to `E` detects `u`.  This is the new route
   `literal-mark-quotient-detects-defect`.
3. Consequently a single negative answer refutes both: if
   `u in Rad_MF(E)`, then no corona representation of `E/<w>` or of `Wbar`
   is injective, since both groups retain a nontrivial image of `u`.

So the two assigned frontier holes are not independent.  They differ only in
what *else* has to be detected.  By the concurrent block-amalgam analysis
(`literal-lamp-kernel-clifford-block-amalgam`, established the same day),
```
E/<w>  =  ( *_{i in I} (Z/2)^8 )  semidirect V ,
```
a free product of elementary abelian blocks indexed by a block set `I` that
is `V`-isomorphic to `X`; `Wbar` is obtained from it by making all same-level
sites commute, not only those in a common block.  The two groups therefore
have the same site combinatorics and the same compression data, but the
literal one has a lamp kernel that is not locally finite: two same-level
lamps in different blocks generate an infinite dihedral group.  Every
statement of Sections 3 and 4 that uses only the relations transfers
verbatim to `E/<w>`; Theorem 4.1's identification of the finite residual with
a locally finite elementary abelian group does not.

`Wbar` is therefore the correct place to work, and by Corollary 6.2 its MF
radical is a `V`-submodule of `L_0`.  The submodule lattice is visible: `L_0`
is the increasing union of the submodules generated by same-level pairs whose
difference in `Torus` has order at most `2^k`, and the normal closure of
`ubar` is the first of them (differences of order `2`).

### 7.2 The one hinge: does the operator-norm relative commutant grow?

Let `Theta : Wbar -> U(Q)` be a corona representation and
```
C = Theta(Gbar)' cap Q ,      Ad_tau(C) = Theta(tau) C Theta(tau)^*
                                        = Theta(abar(Gbar))' cap Q .
```
Because `abar(Gbar) <= Gbar`, always `C <= Ad_tau(C)`.  And
`Theta(c_{tau o}) = Ad_tau(Theta(c_o)) in Ad_tau(C)`.  Hence:

> If `Ad_tau(C) = C` for every corona representation of `Wbar`, then
> `Theta(c_{tau o})` commutes with `Theta(v_1)`, so `Theta(ubar) = 1` for
> every `Theta`; `Wbar` is not MF, `u in Rad_MF(E)`, `E/<w>` is not MF, and
> `unsquared-defect-mf-visible` is false.
>
> Conversely, proving either open claim requires exhibiting a corona
> representation whose relative commutant *strictly grows* under the
> compression.

This is the exact operator-norm analogue of the two collapses already known:

| ambient category | invariant size | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| finite groups | `Nat.card` | collapse (Theorem 3.1) |
| finite-dimensional representations | `finrank` of the commutant | collapse (`commutant-no-growth`) |
| Hilbert--Schmidt ultraproduct | MvN class of the Kazhdan projection | collapse (Theorem 6.1) |
| norm matrix corona `Q`, operator norm | none known | **open** |

The first three are instances of `invariant-size-collapse`.  The fourth is
the whole content of both assigned claims, and it is stated as the new claim
`corona-commutant-no-growth`.

Two remarks on why the fourth line is genuinely different, both of which
block the obvious attempts.

*Nesting plus inner isomorphism is not equality in `Q`.*  `C <= Ad_tau(C)`
with `Ad_tau` inner does force equality in every category with a
conjugation-invariant strictly monotone size, but `Q` has none: it is stably
finite yet has no trace, and the transport argument recovers a size only
after passing to the adjoint corona `B`, where the norm in play is
Hilbert--Schmidt.  That passage is exactly what makes Theorem 6.1 a
`2`-norm statement.

*Cornering is unavailable.*  The mechanism that kills `w` cuts by the
projection `(1 - Theta(w))/2`, which is legitimate because `w` is central in
the whole group.  For `ubar` the centralizer is
`C_{Wbar}(ubar) = L semidirect V_ubar` where `V_ubar` is the setwise
stabilizer of `{(1,0),(1,e_1/2)}` in `V`.  Any element of `V` with nonzero
`level` moves that pair to another level, so `V_ubar <= T`; and the setwise
stabilizer inside `T` of a pair of level-`1` sites is contained in `Gbar`
(explicitly `V_ubar = (Z e_1 + 2Z^3) sd { A : A e_1 = e_1 mod 2 }`, of index
`28` in `Gbar`).  In particular `tau` is not in `C_{Wbar}(ubar)`: the corner
one is allowed to cut is a corner of a group that no longer contains the
compressor, so the transport argument cannot be re-run inside it.  Moreover
`C_{Wbar}(ubar)` is a subgroup of `L sd Gbar`, which is LEF by Theorem 5.1,
so it *does* admit corona representations sending `ubar` to `-1`: the corner
route yields no contradiction, and it will not refute the claims.

### 7.3 What a positive model has to do, and the one structural hint

Combining Sections 4--6, a proof of `commuting-lamp-quotient-mf` must produce
operator-norm microstates in which:

* the six base generators and `tau` satisfy the compression relations up to
  operator-norm `o(1)` while the represented `Gbar` is *not* normalized by the
  represented `tau` — no exact finite model does this (Theorem 3.1);
* the lamps at two distinct sites of the same level are separated in operator
  norm on a corner of vanishing normalized rank (Corollary 6.2).

The one structural hint that the answer may still be positive: the difference
between `W` and `Wbar` is exactly the vanishing of the central sign, and the
sign is precisely the obstruction to closing the level tower.  In the
coordinates of Proposition 2.2 a finite model must truncate the level
interval and re-glue it; the `8`-fold expansion of the level fibres means the
seam is not a permutation identification, and in `W` the failure of the seam
is measured by `zeta`.  In `Wbar` that measurement is trivial, which is what
makes an approximate seam conceivable, whereas Theorem 5.3 shows that no
general permanence theorem can supply it.  This is the same shape as the
cyclic calibration, where the analogous quotient is neither residually finite
nor LEF and is nevertheless MF — there by amenability plus
Tikuisis--White--Winter, a route unavailable here because `Gbar` has (T).

## 9. The same two theorems for the literal marked quotient

`cairn-literal` computed `E` completely
(`notes/LITERAL_GROUP_BLOCK_AMALGAM_STRUCTURE_2026-08-14.md`):
`E = N_E semidirect V` with `N_E` the free product of order-`512` blocks
`ClLamp(8)` amalgamated over `<w>`, the block set `V`-isomorphic to `X`, so

```text
E/<w> = ( *_{i in I} (Z/2)^8 ) semidirect V .
```

Both Sections 4 and 6 were written for `Wbar`, but neither proof uses
commutativity of the lamp kernel.  State them once for both.

Let `Qbar` be either `Wbar` or `E/<w>`, let `c_x` (`x in X`) be its lamp
involutions, and let
```
R = normal closure of { c_x c_{x'} : level(x) = level(x') } .
```

**Theorem 9.1.**  In every homomorphism `phi` from `Qbar` to a finite group,
all lamps of a common level have the same image; hence `R <= ker phi`.

*Proof.*  Blindness in the finite group `phi(V)` gives
`phi(tau) phi(Gbar) phi(tau)^{-1} = phi(abar(Gbar)) <= phi(Gbar)`, hence
equality by cardinality, hence `phi(Gbar_k) = phi(Gbar)` for every `k in Z`
and `phi(T) = phi(Gbar)`.  So each `h in T` is `gamma k` with
`gamma in Gbar` and `k in ker phi`.  Fix `x = g Gbar`; conjugating the
inclusion `T <= Gbar . ker phi` by `g` and using normality of `ker phi`,
`T <= Stab_V(x) . ker phi`, so `h = sigma k` with `sigma in Stab_V(x)`.
Then `phi(c_{hx}) = phi(sigma) phi(k c_x k^{-1}) phi(sigma)^{-1}
= phi(c_{sigma x}) = phi(c_x)`.  Levels are `T`-orbits, so this covers every
same-level pair. []

**Theorem 9.2.**  `Qbar/R` is residually finite, and equals
`(sum_{n in Z} F_2) semidirect V` for `Wbar` and
`( *_{n in Z} Z/2 ) semidirect V` for `E/<w>`, with `V` acting through
`level : V -> Z`.  Hence `R` is exactly the finite residual of `Qbar`.

*Proof.*  Collapsing each level to a single lamp `xi_n` turns a block
`(Z/2)^8` into `<xi_n>` and amalgamates all blocks of a level, so the
level-collapse quotient of the free product is `*_{n in Z} Z/2`, and of the
elementary abelian lamp group is `sum_{n in Z} F_2`.  `T` preserves levels
and therefore acts trivially on the `xi_n`; `tau` shifts them.

Residual finiteness: an element with nontrivial `V`-part is separated by
Malcev.  For an element of the lamp part, take a reduced word in the `xi_n`
using levels in a window of width `< m` and fold `Z` to `Z/m`; adjacent
letters keep distinct level labels, so the folded word stays reduced and
stays nontrivial.  The folded group is
`(sum_{Z/m} F_2) semidirect Z/m`, which is finite, respectively
`( *_{Z/m} Z/2 ) semidirect Z/m`, which is finitely generated virtually free
and hence residually finite; compose with one more finite quotient in the
second case. []

**Theorem 9.3.**  For every corona representation `Theta` of `Qbar`, the
induced tracial representation kills `R`, and every element outside `R` is
detected by some corona representation.  So for both marked quotients the
corona Hilbert--Schmidt radical is exactly the finite residual.

*Proof.*  Section 6 verbatim: transport gives `Fix(Gbar) = Fix(T)` in
`K_omega`; the lamp `c_o` commutes with `Gbar = Stab_V(o)`, so its vector is
`T`-fixed, and translating by `beta(g)` with `T` normal in `V` makes every
lamp vector `T`-fixed; `T`-orbits are levels; `ker Theta_2` is normal and
therefore contains `R`.  The converse is Theorem 9.2 plus the regular
permutation representation of a separating finite quotient. []

The asymmetry between the two groups is exactly this: for `Wbar` the finite
residual `L_0` is a locally finite elementary abelian `2`-group, whereas for
`E/<w>` it contains infinite-order elements — two same-level lamps in
different blocks generate an infinite dihedral group, and their product lies
in `R`.  The level-collapse quotients differ correspondingly, one being a
lamplighter and the other a free lamplighter.

## 10. The central-extension criterion, and what it does not say

`notes/NOTEPAD.md:39199-39232` records, for a different candidate and never
applied to these two holes, the following criterion.  Let
`1 -> C_2 -> E -> K -> 1` be central with `K` MF, write a section cocycle
`s(k)s(l) = omega(k,l)s(kl)`, and let `chi` be the nontrivial character of
`C_2`.  Then `E` is MF if and only if there are maps `v_n : K -> U(d_n)` with
`||v_n(k)v_n(l) - chi(omega(k,l)) v_n(kl)||_op -> 0` for every fixed pair.

Both directions check out.  For `1 => 2`, an injective corona representation
of `E` has `Theta(z) != 1`, so the spectral projection of `Theta(z)` at `-1`
is a nonzero projection commuting with `Theta(E)`; cutting by it gives a
corona representation with `Theta(z) = -1`, whose values on a section are the
required `v_n`.  For `2 => 1`, set
`V_n(s(k)c) = chi(c) v_n(k) (+) pi_n(k)` for a faithful MF model `pi` of `K`;
block-diagonal multiplicativity is the cocycle identity, the second block
separates elements with nontrivial image in `K`, and the first separates the
central generator.

Applied to `(E, <w>)` or to `(W, <zeta>)`, condition 2 says exactly that some
corona representation of the total group sends the central involution to
`-1`.  Theorem A says none does.  So the criterion here is a restatement of
Theorem A, and it is consistent with either answer for the quotient: it
constrains the twisted sector only, and MF of `K` is a hypothesis of the
useful direction rather than a conclusion.  In particular it is **not** an
obstruction to either open claim, and it should not be read as one.

What it does add is a clean way to say what Theorem A means: the failure of
MF for `E` is precisely the emptiness of the `chi`-twisted sector over
`E/<w>`.  Whether the untwisted sector is full — that is, whether `E/<w>` is
MF — is untouched by it.

## 11. Compressor-free form of the fork

`relative-wall-commutant-growth` (cairn-wall,
`notes/TRUE_RELATIVE_WALL_IS_COMMUTANT_GROWTH.md`, Lemma 1) proves for an
arbitrary homomorphism into an arbitrary group that `Ad pi(s)` carries
`C_U(pi(Gamma))` *onto* `C_U(pi(s Gamma s^{-1}))`.  With `U = U(Q)` this
applies verbatim to corona representations, and gives a compressor-free form
of Section 7.2.

**Proposition 11.1.**  Let `Theta` be a corona representation of `Wbar` (or
of `E`, or `E/<w>`) and `C = Theta(Gbar)' cap Q`.  Then no growth at `tau` —
`C = Ad Theta(tau)(C)` — holds if and only if `C` is invariant under
`Ad Theta(V)`.

*Proof.*  The set of `g` with `Ad Theta(g)(C) = C` is a subgroup.  It
contains `Gbar`, since `gamma Gbar gamma^{-1} = Gbar`.  If it contains `tau`
it contains `<Gbar, tau> = V`.  Conversely invariance under `Ad Theta(V)`
includes `tau`. []

So the fork can be stated without any choice of compressor: *is the relative
commutant of the represented Kazhdan subgroup normalized by the represented
telescope group?*  Two calibration points from the same source: the
Pimsner--Popa index is a conjugation-invariant strictly monotone size in the
tracial branch, and `Q` carries no trace, so that invariant does not transfer
here; and the analogous inclusion is genuinely proper in a metric
ultraproduct of finite groups (`kun-thom-clifford-cover-weakly-sofic`), so no
purely group-theoretic no-go can be valid — any proof must use something
specific to the operator-norm corona.

## 13. The fork is a necessity, not only a sufficiency

Section 7.2 showed that no growth of the operator-norm relative commutant
would refute both open claims.  The converse implication is elementary and
needs neither transport nor property (T).

**Theorem 13.1.**  Let `Theta` be an injective corona representation of
`Wbar` or of `E/<w>` and `C = Theta(Gbar)' cap Q`.  Then
`C` is *strictly* contained in `Ad Theta(tau)(C) = Theta(abar(Gbar))' cap Q`.

*Proof.*  `Stab_V(tau o) = tau Gbar tau^{-1} = abar(Gbar)`, and a lamp
commutes with every group element fixing its site, so
`Theta(c_{tau o}) in Theta(abar(Gbar))' cap Q = Ad Theta(tau)(C)`.  Were it
also in `C` it would commute with `Theta(v_1)`, forcing
`Theta(u) = Theta(c_{tau o} c_{v_1 tau o}) = 1`, contradicting injectivity
since `u` survives in both quotients. []

So growth is necessary for either claim and its absence is fatal to both:
`corona-commutant-no-growth` is an exact hinge, and the growth, if it exists,
is realized by a lamp rather than by an exotic operator.

**Transfer to the Kun--Thom wreath.**  Nothing in Section 7.2 or in Theorem
13.1 uses the affine base.  For any pair `Gamma <= G` with `Gamma` Kazhdan
and `t Gamma t^{-1} < Gamma` proper, the commuting-lamp wreath
`(sum_{G/Gamma} C_2) semidirect G` contains the same configuration, so
`corona-commutant-no-growth` also decides `kun-thom-wreath-weak-mf`
negatively, and MF of that wreath likewise forces growth.  One theorem
settles three open questions.

## 14. A second route to the literal quotient: Shulman's amalgam criterion

Shulman, arXiv:2603.13564v2, **Theorem 20** (read from the PDF this session,
transcribed in `research/artifacts/shulman-2603-13564-verified.md`): for
separable `A, B, C` with inclusions into `A` and `B`, the amalgam `A *_C B`
is MF if and only if there are embeddings of `A` and of `B` into one norm
matrix corona that agree on `C`.  Section 2.1 of the same paper identifies
`C*(G_1 *_H G_2)` with `C*(G_1) *_{C*(H)} C*(G_2)`.

With cairn-literal's presentation `E/<w> = V *_{B_1} ((Z/2)^8 sd B_1)` this
gives the first non-exact-model route to `literal-mark-quotient-mf`: produce
corona embeddings of `C*(V)` and of `C*((Z/2)^8 sd B_1)` agreeing on
`C*(B_1)`.  Two observations.

*It is the same hinge again.*  Compatibility makes the eight lamps of a block
distinct commuting symmetries in the relative commutant of the site
stabilizer `B` that are permuted transitively by the represented `B_1`; such
a symmetry lies in `C(B) \ C(B_1)`, which is exactly commutant growth at the
compressor.  Two independent routes — Kazhdan transport and Shulman
compatibility — arrive at the same operator-algebraic question.

*It costs more than the group claim needs.*  Theorem 20 concludes MF of the
full group C*-algebra, and requires both vertex algebras to be MF; both
contain `C*(Z^3 sd SL_3(Z))`, the full C*-algebra of a property-(T) group.
Shulman's own introduction notes how few non-amenable groups are known to
have MF full C*-algebra.

*Priority datum.*  That same introduction states, in March 2026: "There are
no examples of groups whose full or reduced C*-algebra is not MF."  Theorem A
of this repository supplies exactly such an example.  Flagged for the credit
and priority audit; not acted on here.

## 15. Working the hinge: two attacks, one closed

### 15.1 Uniform stability cannot reach a corona representation

Glebsky--Lubotzky--Monod--Rangarajan (arXiv:2301.00476, abstract recorded in
`research/artifacts/glmr-2301-00476-verified.md`) prove *uniform* stability
for lattices in higher-rank semisimple groups.  Uniform stability hypothesizes
that the multiplicative defect is small as a supremum over *all* pairs of
group elements.

A corona representation of an infinite group does not supply that.  Choosing
unitary lifts `U_{gamma,n}`, each fixed pair has defect tending to `0`,
because the difference lies in the ideal; but the coordinate supremum

```text
D_n = sup_{gamma, gamma'} || U_{gamma gamma',n} - U_{gamma,n} U_{gamma',n} ||
```

need not tend to `0`.  All one gets is multiplicativity on balls `B_{R_n}` of
slowly growing radius.  Re-choosing lifts changes each coordinate by an ideal
element, which is a pointwise correction and leaves the supremum over the
pairs outside `B_{R_n}` untouched; truncating the map off `B_{R_n}` creates a
defect of full size at the boundary.

So the uniform-stability attack fails before the group class matters, and in
particular the unresolved question of whether those theorems cover
`Z^3 sd SL_3(Z)` — a lattice in a *non*-semisimple group — never has to be
settled.  This is `corona-defect-is-not-uniform`, and it kills the route
`corona-commutant-no-growth-via-uniform-stability`.

What survives is the same reduction with **matricial stability** (Dadarlat,
Adv. Math. 2021, already cited by the manuscript) in place of uniform
stability: that notion is stated for lifting a homomorphism into the matrix
corona, which is exactly the hypothesis at hand.  Whether the affine Kazhdan
group is matricially stable in the operator norm is open, and is the live
version of this attack.

### 15.2 Averaging cannot reach the operator norm at all

The other reflex — use property (T) to average an almost-commuting element
onto the commutant — is not merely difficult here; it is unavailable, and for
a reason worth stating once.

**Proposition 15.1.**  For an infinite property-(T) group, the Kazhdan
projection `p` is not in the image of `l^1(Gamma)` in `C*_max(Gamma)`, and the
random-walk averages `mu^{*n} -> p` are not `l^1`-Cauchy.

*Proof.*  `lambda(a) delta_e = a`, so the regular representation is injective
on `l^1(Gamma)`.  It factors through `C*_max`.  An infinite group has no
invariant `l^2` vectors, so `lambda(p) = 0`, while `epsilon(p) = 1`.  If
`p = ` image of `a in l^1` then `lambda(a) = 0`, so `a = 0`, so `p = 0` —
contradiction.  Since `|| . ||_{C*_max} <= || . ||_{l^1}`, an `l^1`-Cauchy
sequence would have an `l^1` limit mapping onto the `C*_max` limit `p`, which
is now excluded. []

An isometric action of `Gamma` on a Banach space extends to `l^1(Gamma)` and,
without a Hilbertian structure, to nothing larger.  `Ad Theta` on `Q` is
isometric and `Q` is not Hilbertian, so `Ad Theta(p)` is undefined and
`Ad Theta(mu^{*n})` need not converge: the fixed-point projection onto the
relative commutant cannot be manufactured by averaging in the operator norm.

This is the structural reason the transport argument moves to the adjoint
corona on Hilbert--Schmidt spaces before invoking `p`, and hence the reason
every property-(T) argument in this cluster terminates in the `2`-norm —
Theorem 6.1 included.  It also answers, on the corona side, the question of
what separates `Q` from a metric ultraproduct of finite groups: the latter
carries the Hilbertian coefficient structure in which `p` acts.

Scope: this closes averaging, not the hinge.  K-theoretic, saturation and
stability arguments do not use `p` and are untouched.
