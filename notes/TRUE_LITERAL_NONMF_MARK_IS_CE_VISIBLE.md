# The literal non-MF mark is visible in sofic and CE representations

Date: 2026-08-13

## 1. Outcome

Let

```text
Gamma=Z^3 semidirect SL_3(Z),
beta(v,A)=(2v,A),
a=(e_1,1) notin beta(Gamma),
```

and let `E` be the literal finitely presented group from
`non_mf_groups_exist.tex`.  Thus `E` has a stable letter `t`, an involution
`c` centralizing `Gamma`, and the additional relations saying that

```text
w=[t c t^(-1), a t c t^(-1) a^(-1)]
```

is a central involution.

The operator-norm theorem proves

```text
w in Rad_MF(E).                                         (LCV1)
```

The tracial conclusion is the opposite.

**Theorem 1 (exact sofic and CE visibility).** There are a countable sofic
group `S`, a Connes-embeddable finite tracial von Neumann algebra `M`, and
homomorphisms

```text
rho:E->S,
pi:E->U(M)
```

such that

```text
rho(w)!=1,
pi(w)=-1.                                               (LCV2)
```

Consequently

```text
w notin Rad_hyp(E),
w notin Rad_sof(E),
Rad_MF(E) notsubseteq Rad_hyp(E),
Rad_MF(E) notsubseteq Rad_sof(E).                       (LCV3)
```

This does not prove that `E` is hyperlinear: `pi` need not be faithful.
It instead proves that this literal word is not the sofic-radical half of a
collision.  A different sofic-radical word that survives a hyperlinear
representation is still required to solve the hyperlinear-versus-sofic
problem.

## 2. The finite coset tower

For `n>=1`, put

```text
X_n=Gamma/beta^n(Gamma),
X=disjointUnion_(n>=1) X_n.                             (LCV4)
```

Every `X_n` is finite, of cardinality `8^n`.  Define

```text
F(g beta^n(Gamma))=beta(g) beta^(n+1)(Gamma).           (LCV5)
```

Injectivity of `beta` makes `F` a well-defined injection, and

```text
F(delta x)=beta(delta)F(x).                            (LCV6)
```

Let `A_Cl` be the tracial complex CAR/Clifford algebra generated by
self-adjoint Majoranas `C_x`, `x in X`, satisfying

```text
C_x^2=1,
C_x C_y=-C_y C_x       (x!=y).                         (LCV7)
```

The permutation action of `Gamma` on `X` acts by trace-preserving
Bogoliubov automorphisms.  The injection `F` defines a trace-preserving
unital embedding

```text
alpha_A(C_x)=C_(F(x)).                                 (LCV8)
```

By `(LCV6)`, this extends to a trace-preserving injective endomorphism of

```text
N=A_Cl crossed Gamma,
alpha(u_delta)=u_(beta(delta)).                        (LCV9)
```

## 3. Why the core is Connes embeddable

Let `Y_m=X_1 disjointUnion ... disjointUnion X_m`, let `A_m` be the
finite-dimensional Clifford algebra generated by the coordinates in
`Y_m`, and put

```text
N_m=A_m crossed Gamma.                                 (LCV10)
```

The action of `Gamma` on `Y_m` has finite image.  Its kernel `Lambda_m`
has finite index in `Gamma` and acts trivially on `A_m`.  Hence `N_m` is a
finite twisted crossed product of

```text
A_m tensor L(Lambda_m).                                (LCV11)
```

The group `Lambda_m` is residually finite, because it is a subgroup of
the residually finite linear group `Gamma`; therefore `L(Lambda_m)` is
Connes embeddable.  Finite-dimensional tensor products and finite twisted
crossed products preserve Connes embeddability, so every `N_m` is Connes
embeddable.

The algebras `N_m` increase trace preservingly and their union is weakly
dense in `N`.  Connes embeddability is preserved under such increasing
tracial unions.  Thus

```text
N is Connes embeddable.                                (LCV12)
```

There is a stronger group-level statement.  Put

```text
K_m=ClLamp(Y_m) semidirect Gamma.                      (LCV12a)
```

The Clifford lamp group on `Y_m` is finite, so `Gamma` has finite index in
`K_m`.  Since `Gamma` is residually finite and therefore sofic, every
`K_m` is sofic.  These groups increase with union

```text
K=ClLamp(X) semidirect Gamma.                          (LCV12b)
```

Hence `K` is sofic.  The map `F` and the endomorphism `beta` define an
injective endomorphism of `K`.  Its automorphic direct limit `K_tilde` is
sofic because soficity is preserved under directed unions.  Finally put

```text
S=K_tilde semidirect Z.                                (LCV12c)
```

The quotient in `(LCV12c)` is amenable.  Soficity is preserved under
extensions of a sofic normal subgroup by an amenable quotient, so `S` is
sofic.  This uses the valid sofic-by-amenable closure direction; it makes no
claim about arbitrary amenable-by-sofic groups.

This finite-stage argument is the feature absent from the full
Kun--Thom coset action.  One must not replace it by the false general
claim that an amenable-by-sofic group is automatically sofic or
hyperlinear.

### The dilation is the original Clifford witness

The finite-tower construction also proves more than the existence of some
sofic detector: its final group `S` is canonically the Clifford witness group
from the non-MF manuscript.

Let `X_tilde` be the site set in the automorphic dilation.  It is the union
of the backward translates `T^(-m)X`, `m>=0`.  If `x` lies in `X_n`, define

```text
level(T^(-m)x)=n-m.                                   (LCV12d)
```

This is well-defined because replacing `T^(-m)x` by
`T^(-(m+1))F(x)` raises both indices by one.  The base group preserves this
level and `T` raises it by one.

Put `x_0=beta(Gamma) in X_1` and `y=T^(-1)x_0`, so `level(y)=0`.  The group
generated by the automorphic dilation of `Gamma` and `T` is the mapping
telescope group `V` of the manuscript.  It acts transitively on `X_tilde`:
`T^n y` is the root of `X_n`, `Gamma` is transitive on every `X_n`, and
backward translates are reached by elements of `V`.

The stabilizer of `y` is exactly `Gamma`.  The level function first forces a
stabilizer element to have `T`-height zero.  Write such an element as

```text
v=T^(-m) gamma T^m.
```

After translating the equation `v y=y` forward by `T^(m+1)`, one finds that
`beta(gamma)` fixes the root of `X_(m+1)`.  Hence
`beta(gamma) in beta^(m+1)(Gamma)`, so `gamma in beta^m(Gamma)` and the
displayed element `v` already lies in `Gamma`.  Conversely `Gamma` fixes
`y` by covariance.  Therefore

```text
X_tilde is V-equivariantly isomorphic to V/Gamma.      (LCV12e)
```

Automorphic dilation commutes here with the displayed semidirect-product
construction, so `(LCV12e)` gives

```text
S is isomorphic to ClLamp(V/Gamma) semidirect V = W.   (LCV12f)
```

Thus the original witness `W` is indeed sofic.  The proof is the finite-stage
coset-tower proof above, not the invalid inference from the bare exact sequence
with amenable kernel and sofic quotient.  Combined with the MF-radical sign,
this yields the stronger structural statement

```text
W is finitely generated, locally-finite-by-residually-finite,
sofic, and non-MF.                                    (LCV12g)
```

## 4. Automorphic dilation and the literal presentation

Let `(N_tilde,alpha_tilde)` be the minimal automorphic dilation of
`(N,alpha)`.  It is the increasing tracial union of copies of `N`, hence is
Connes embeddable.  Form

```text
M=N_tilde crossed_(alpha_tilde) Z                      (LCV13)
```

and let `U` be the implementing unitary, with

```text
U x U^*=alpha_tilde(x).                                (LCV14)
```

Følner compression for `Z`, followed by matrix microstates for
`N_tilde`, proves that `(LCV13)` is Connes embeddable.

Let

```text
x_0=beta(Gamma) in X_1,
a_0=C_(x_0),
C=U^* a_0 U.                                           (LCV15)
```

Define

```text
pi(delta)=u_delta       (delta in Gamma),
pi(t)=U,
pi(c)=C.                                                (LCV16)
```

The HNN relations follow from `(LCV9)` and `(LCV14)`.  Moreover, for
`delta in Gamma`,

```text
U[C,u_delta]U^*=[a_0,u_(beta(delta))]=0,               (LCV17)
```

because `beta(Gamma)` fixes `x_0`.  Hence `C` is an involution centralizing
the represented copy of `Gamma`, as required by the literal presentation.

Finally,

```text
pi(t c t^(-1))=a_0,
pi(a t c t^(-1) a^(-1))=C_(a x_0).                    (LCV18)
```

The assumption `a notin beta(Gamma)` says exactly that `a x_0!=x_0`.
The two Majoranas in `(LCV18)` therefore anticommute, and their group
commutator is the scalar `-1`.  Thus `pi(w)=-1`.  This scalar is a central
involution, so every extra defining relation involving `w` is also
satisfied.  Theorem 1 follows.

To obtain the radical statement without treating `U(M)` itself as a
countable group, take the countable image `Q=pi(E)`.  Connes embeddability
of `M` embeds `Q` faithfully in a tracial matrix ultraproduct, so `Q` is
hyperlinear.  The quotient map `E->Q` detects `w`, proving the hyperlinear
part of `(LCV3)`.

The same calculation is already group valued.  Let `z` be the central
Clifford sign in `K_tilde`, let `T` generate the final `Z` in `(LCV12c)`,
and put

```text
rho(delta)=delta,
rho(t)=T,
rho(c)=T^(-1)c_(x_0)T.                                (LCV18a)
```

Every literal defining relation holds and `rho(w)=z!=1`.  Since `S` is
sofic, this proves `w notin Rad_sof(E)` directly.

The scalar CE representation can alternatively be obtained from the
negative central corner of this group model.  In `L(S)` put

```text
p=(1-u_z)/2.
```

Soficity of `S` makes `L(S)` and `pL(S)` Connes embeddable.  The homomorphism
`h |-> p u_(rho(h))` sends `w` to `-p`, the scalar `-1` in the unital
corner.

There is also a group-class separation.  The range `Q=rho(E)` is generated
by the eight literal generators and is a subgroup of the sofic group `S`,
so it is finitely generated and sofic.  Functoriality of the MF radical
sends `(LCV1)` to

```text
1!=rho(w) in Rad_MF(Q).
```

Hence `Q` is an explicit finitely generated sofic group which is not MF in
the operator-norm matrix-corona sense.

In fact `(LCV12f)` and transitivity show that this range is all of `S`: it
contains `Gamma` and `T`, and it contains the lamp at `y`; conjugation by
`V` supplies every lamp site.  Hence `Q=S=W`, recovering `(LCV12g)` directly.

There is a simple-envelope strengthening.  Elek--Szabó prove that every
countable sofic group embeds in a countable simple sofic group.  Embed `Q`
in such a group `S_sof`.  Portability sends the displayed nontrivial
MF-radical element into `Rad_MF(S_sof)`.  Since this radical is normal and
`S_sof` is simple,

```text
Rad_MF(S_sof)=S_sof.
```

Thus there is a countable simple sofic (hence hyperlinear) group every one
of whose homomorphisms to an operator-MF group is trivial.  This strengthens
the operator-norm/tracial separation but cannot supply a hyperlinear
nonsofic group, because `S_sof` is itself sofic.

## 5. Consequence for the spin-block proposal

On the `w=-1` sector, the eight conjugates indexed by
`Gamma/beta(Gamma)` do generate an exact copy of the complex Clifford
algebra `Cl_8(C)=M_16(C)`.  That finite spin block is therefore a correct
structural extraction, but it cannot lead to a contradiction: the model
above realizes it exactly in a Connes-embeddable algebra.

Any claimed coordinate-commutant theorem which, together with that spin
block, forces `w=1` is false as stated.  The missing compatibility cannot
be supplied merely by positivity of the `w=-1` corner or by the fixed size
of the spin factor.  In the coefficient directions, approximate group
relations still need not recover exact coordinate commutants with the
nesting required by finite-dimensional dimension counting.

## 6. The surviving collision target

For a countable group `H`, define

```text
Rad_sof(H)=intersection_(f:H->S, S sofic) ker(f),
Rad_hyp(H)=intersection_(f:H->Q, Q hyperlinear) ker(f). (LCV19)
```

Since every sofic group is hyperlinear,

```text
Rad_hyp(H) subseteq Rad_sof(H).                         (LCV20)
```

If one finds

```text
x in Rad_sof(H) minus Rad_hyp(H),                      (LCV21)
```

choose a hyperlinear detector `rho:H->Q` with `rho(x)!=1` and replace `Q`
by `rho(H)`.  The image is hyperlinear.  If it were sofic, the quotient map
from `H` to that image would kill `x`, contradicting detection.  Thus
`(LCV21)` is exactly a hyperlinear nonsofic certificate.

The literal group `E` proves the different separation `(LCV3)`: its mark is
visible even in a sofic target.  The
Kun--Thom groups supply explicit nontrivial elements of `Rad_sof`.  The
open task is still to make one of those Kun--Thom elements survive a CE
representation, for example through the one-orbit parity corner in
`FALSE_ONE_VERTEX_GROUP_CORNER_AND_COVER.md`.
