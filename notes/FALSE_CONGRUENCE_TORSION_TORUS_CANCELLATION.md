# Congruence torsion exists cyclotomically but cancels through root tori

Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Outcome

The torsion-free calculation for the isolated radical augmentation module in
`FALSE_PRIME_TWO_BOCKSTEIN_IDENTIFICATION.md` does **not** extend to the
whole fixed-prime intermediate group.  There is an explicit large family of
additional primary classes.

Fix a prime `p`, put

`R=Z[x_1^(+-1),...,x_d^(+-1)]`,
`E=EL_r(R)`,
`E_p=ker(E->EL_r(R/pR))`,                                `(CTC1)`

and let

`h=e_12(x_1^(-1))`, `B_p=<E_p,h>`.                      `(CTC2)`

Then `B_p/E_p~=C_p`.  For every pair of distinct indices
`i,j in {3,...,r}` and every nonzero `f_bar in R/pR`, the relative root

`k_(ij,f)=e_ij(pf) in E_p`                              `(CTC3)`

defines:

1. a nonzero order-`p` class in `H_1(B_p,Z)`;
2. a nonzero class killed by `varpi=1-zeta_p` in
   `H_1(B_p,Z[zeta_p]_chi)`.

Thus the complementary cyclotomic sector really does contain
`varpi`-torsion.  It cannot be eliminated by extending the isolated
augmentation-ideal calculation.

However, this entire explicit family contributes **zero** to the
simultaneous Bockstein quotient

`H_2(B_p,F_p)/(ker(beta_1)+ker(beta_chi))`.             `(CTC4)`

Indeed `h` and every root in `(CTC3)` commute.  Their two-torus fundamental
class lies in `ker(beta_1)` and its cyclotomic Bockstein is precisely the
class in item 2, up to a unit.  Consequently all lower-right congruence
torsion lies in

`beta_chi(ker(beta_1))`.                               `(CTC5)`

The remaining FALSE gate is therefore not mere existence of primary
torsion.  It is a genuinely coupled class not generated by these commuting
root tori, or a relation-module/transgression class outside their span.

## 2. The first congruence logarithm

Every `g in E_p` has the form

`g=1+pA`, `A in M_r(R)`.                               `(CTC6)`

Define

`theta_p(g)=A mod p in sl_r(R/pR)`.                    `(CTC7)`

**Lemma 1 (first congruence quotient).**  Formula `(CTC7)` is a group
homomorphism from `E_p` to the additive group `sl_r(R/pR)`.  It is
equivariant for conjugation by `B_p`, where the target carries conjugation
by the reduction of `B_p`.

**Proof.**  Modulo `p^2`,

`(1+pA)(1+pC)=1+p(A+C)`,                               `(CTC8)`

so `(CTC7)` is additive.  Since every elementary matrix has determinant
one,

`1=det(1+pA)=1+p tr(A) mod p^2`,                       `(CTC9)`

and therefore `tr(A)=0 mod p`.  Conjugation before taking `(CTC7)` becomes
conjugation by the reduced matrix.  End proof.

In particular,

`theta_p(e_ij(pf))=f_bar E_ij`.                        `(CTC10)`

This will certify all nonvanishing assertions below; no presentation of
the full congruence subgroup is needed.

## 3. A large fixed primary subspace in `E_p^ab`

Let `W_p` be the `F_p`-space

`W_p=directSum_(3<=i!=j<=r) (R/pR) E_ij`.              `(CTC11)`

For `f_bar in R/pR`, choose any lift `f in R` and send

`f_bar E_ij |-> [e_ij(pf)] in (E_p)_ab`.              `(CTC12)`

**Lemma 2 (lower-right primary roots).**  Formula `(CTC12)` is a
well-defined injection of additive groups

`W_p -> (E_p)_ab[p]`.                                  `(CTC13)`

Moreover conjugation by `h` fixes its image pointwise.

**Proof.**  Choose an index `l` different from `i,j`; the stable-rank
construction has more than enough indices.  The elementary commutator
identity gives

`[e_(i l)(pf),e_(l j)(p)]=e_ij(p^2 f)`.                `(CTC14)`

On the other hand

`e_ij(pf)^p=e_ij(p^2f)`.                               `(CTC15)`

Thus every class in `(CTC12)` is killed by `p`.  Changing `f` by a
multiple of `p` changes the root element by an element of the form
`e_ij(p^2g)`, hence by a commutator, so `(CTC12)` is well-defined and
`F_p`-linear.  Its composition with `theta_p` is the inclusion `(CTC11)`;
it is therefore injective.

The roots `e_12(x_1^(-1))` and `e_ij(pf)` commute when `i,j>=3`, so
conjugation by `h` fixes every class in `(CTC13)`.  End proof.

The lower-right projection

`pr_lr:sl_r(R/pR)->W_p`                                `(CTC16)`

is unchanged by conjugation with `e_12(x_1^(-1))`: that conjugation can
alter only entries meeting row or column `1,2`.  Hence

`pr_lr theta_p((h-1)a)=0`                              `(CTC17)`

for every `a in (E_p)_ab`.  It follows that `(CTC13)` remains injective
after taking `h`-coinvariants.

## 4. Ordinary primary torsion in `H_1(B_p,Z)`

The extension

`1->E_p->B_p->C_p->1`                                 `(CTC18)`

has ordinary homology five-term sequence

`H_2(C_p,Z)->((E_p)_ab)_(C_p)`
`  ->H_1(B_p,Z)->H_1(C_p,Z)->0`.                       `(CTC19)`

Since `H_2(C_p,Z)=0`, the middle left arrow is injective.  Lemma 2 and
`(CTC17)` therefore prove:

**Theorem 3 (ordinary congruence torsion).**  There is a natural injection

`W_p -> H_1(B_p,Z)[p]`.                                `(CTC20)`

In particular the ordinary primary group in `(BTI27)` is not zero for the
full intermediate group.

## 5. Cyclotomic primary torsion and the extension transgression

Let `O_p=Z[zeta_p]` and let `O_chi` be the local system on which the image
of `h` acts by `zeta_p`.  For this nontrivial character,

`H_1(C_p,O_chi)=0`,
`H_2(C_p,O_chi)=O_p/(zeta_p-1)O_p~=F_p`.               `(CTC21)`

The five-term sequence for `(CTC18)` becomes

`F_p --d_2--> ((E_p)_ab tensor O_p)_(C_p,chi)`
`  ->H_1(B_p,O_chi)->0`.                               `(CTC22)`

The transgression of the standard generator is represented by the
extension element

`h^p=e_12(p x_1^(-1))`.                               `(CTC23)`

Under `theta_p`, this is `x_1^(-1)E_12`; its lower-right projection is
zero.

Define

`lambda:((E_p)_ab tensor O_p)_(C_p,chi)->W_p`          `(CTC24)`

by applying `pr_lr theta_p` and reducing the scalar in `O_p` modulo
`varpi`.  This is well-defined: modulo `varpi`, `zeta_p=1`, and `(CTC17)`
annihilates the twisted coinvariant relation.  Equations `(CTC23)--(CTC24)`
show that `lambda d_2=0`, so `lambda` descends through `(CTC22)`.

For a class from `(CTC12)`, the twisted coinvariant relation is

`(zeta_p-1)[e_ij(pf)]=0`,                              `(CTC25)`

because `h` fixes that root.  Its image under `lambda` is `f_bar E_ij`.
We have proved:

**Theorem 4 (cyclotomic congruence torsion).**  There is a split injection
of additive groups

`W_p -> H_1(B_p,O_chi)[varpi]`.                        `(CTC26)`

In particular, the additional relation-module/base-group sector mentioned
after `(BTI32)` is genuinely nonzero and is already infinite-dimensional
over `F_p` when the Laurent ring is used.

The word "split" in `(CTC26)` refers only to the displayed additive
`F_p`-subspace, split by `(CTC24)`.  No decomposition of the entire homology
group is asserted.

## 6. The commuting-torus Bockstein calculation

The simultaneous quotient `(CTC4)` is subtler than the two target torsion
groups.  Fix one nonzero root `k=k_(ij,f)` from `(CTC3)`.  The elements
`h,k` commute and generate a copy of `Z^2`: their nonzero entries occupy
disjoint matrix coordinates, so no nontrivial powers can cancel.

Let

`alpha_k in H_2(B_p,F_p)`                              `(CTC27)`

be the image of the mod-`p` fundamental class of this two-torus.

**Lemma 5 (root-torus Bocksteins).**  For the ordinary and cyclotomic
Bocksteins in `(BTI24)`,

`beta_1(alpha_k)=0`,
`beta_chi(alpha_k)=u [k]`,                             `(CTC28)`

where `u` is a unit of `O_p` modulo `varpi`.

**Proof.**  The group map `Z^2=<h,k>->B_p` carries the integral torus
fundamental class to an integral class whose reduction is `alpha_k`.
Hence the ordinary Bockstein vanishes.

For the cyclotomic calculation, use the standard one-vertex cellular
complex of the torus.  On coefficients `O_chi`, the generator `h` acts by
`zeta_p` and `k` acts trivially.  The boundary of the lifted two-cell is,
up to the orientation convention,

`(zeta_p-1)e_k-(1-1)e_h=(zeta_p-1)e_k`.                `(CTC29)`

Dividing by `varpi=1-zeta_p` in the coefficient Bockstein gives a unit
multiple of the one-cycle represented by `k`.  Naturality under
`Z^2->B_p` proves `(CTC28)`.  End proof.

Combining Lemma 5 with Theorem 4 gives the promised cancellation.

**Theorem 6 (the fixed congruence torsion is simultaneously harmless).**
The whole explicit subspace `(CTC26)` satisfies

`W_p <= beta_chi(ker(beta_1))`.                        `(CTC30)`

Consequently no class whose cyclotomic obstruction is contained in this
lower-right root subspace survives the simultaneous quotient `(CTC4)`.

**Proof.**  The classes `[k_(ij,f)]` span `(CTC26)`.  Lemma 5 supplies for
each generator a class `alpha_k in ker(beta_1)` whose cyclotomic
Bockstein is that generator up to a nonzero scalar.  Linearity gives
`(CTC30)`.  If `alpha` has cyclotomic Bockstein in this subspace, subtract
a corresponding linear combination of the `alpha_k`.  The difference has
zero cyclotomic Bockstein, while the subtracted class belongs to
`ker(beta_1)`.  Thus `alpha` lies in
`ker(beta_1)+ker(beta_chi)`.  End proof.

The same cancellation holds in the cellular complex used for the filling
problem.  For a connected presentation complex with fundamental group
`B_p` (or a larger group containing this torus), the natural map from
cellular `H_2` onto group `H_2` is surjective by the Hopf exact sequence.
Lift `alpha_k` through that map.  Degree-one cellular and group homology
agree with both local systems, and coefficient Bocksteins are natural.
Thus `(CTC28)--(CTC30)` apply to the finite-support cellular Bockstein
quotient, not only to an abstract classifying space.

## 7. Exact remaining gate

This calculation resolves both tempting shortcuts.

1. The full cyclotomic `H_1` is not primary-torsion-free; Theorem 4 gives
   explicit counterexamples.
2. The mere presence of simultaneous ordinary and cyclotomic target
   torsion does not produce a gluing obstruction; Theorem 6 cancels the
   largest obvious common family.

What remains is the quotient of the cyclotomic Bockstein image by the
root-torus subspace `(CTC30)`, together with the relation-module part of
`H_1` and the extension transgressions.  A surviving FALSE class must have
one of the following genuinely coupled forms:

* a primary class not represented by an `h`-fixed commuting root;
* a difference between multiple fixed double-coset orbits in the
  Bass--Serre relation module; or
* a derived transgression coupling those two sectors.

This is a strict reduction of the local prime computation.  Lower-right
first-congruence classes should be removed before any derived matrix or
finite-support Bockstein search; otherwise the computation will report a
large but automatically canceling primary block.
