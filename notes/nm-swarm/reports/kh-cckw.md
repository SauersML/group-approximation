## CCKW Theorem 3.1(ii), cyclic case — proof as a lemma list (before authoring)
Route chosen: combinatorial nonpositive curvature on the coset complex (no metric CAT(0) geometry), in the
equilateral (6,3) regime, which needs link girth ≥ 6 at EVERY vertex type (true: 6, 8, 8). Vertex groups
P₁ = ⟨a,b⟩, P₂ = ⟨c,b⟩, P₃ = ⟨c,a⟩; edge groups ⟨a⟩ = P₁∩P₃, ⟨b⟩ = P₁∩P₂, ⟨c⟩ = P₂∩P₃; face group 1.

- **T0 (vertex groups embed, intersections are the edge groups).** From ψ: `ψ` injective on each Pᵢ (module 2
  bounds + normal forms, done above) and, by a finite check in GL₄(F₇) (done in the model test; Lean: normal-form
  readouts), ψ(Pᵢ) ∩ ψ(Pⱼ) = ψ(edge group) ⇒ Pᵢ ∩ Pⱼ = edge group in GHB(7).
- **T1 (coset complex X).** Vertices: cosets gPᵢ (typed by i); edges: cosets of edge groups; triangles: elements g
  (vertices gP₁, gP₂, gP₃). Simplicial and flag-free-of-extra-simplices by T0. G acts type-preservingly;
  stab(gPᵢ) = gPᵢg⁻¹.
- **T2 (X connected and simply connected; Tits' lemma).** GHB(7) is generated by the Pᵢ and its presentation is
  the colimit of the triangle of groups (generators a,b,c; every relator lives in one vertex group), so every
  closed edge path in X is null-homotopic by triangle moves. Combinatorial form: an edge loop based at the base
  triangle spells a word in the Pᵢ; it closes iff the word is trivial in G; G = colimit ⇒ the word reduces to the
  empty word by vertex-group relations ⇒ the loop contracts. (Abels–Holz 1993, Thm 2.4, in the triangle case.)
- **T3 (links have girth ≥ 6).** lk(gPᵢ) ≅ coset graph Cos(Pᵢ; edge groups): bipartite, edges = elements of Pᵢ.
  A cycle of length 2m ⇔ an alternating relation x₁y₁⋯xₘyₘ = 1 with all factors nontrivial. Girth ≥ 6 ⇔ no such
  relation for m ≤ 2 — a finite certificate (model test: true; P₂, P₃ even girth ≥ 8). Lean: via ψ-injectivity,
  `decide +kernel` over the normal-form exponents (7⁴·36 products at most).
- **T4 (locally 6-large, 2-dimensional ⇒ projection lemma).** For a simply connected triangle complex all of whose
  vertex links have girth ≥ 6: for every vertex o and n ≥ 1, and every simplex τ ⊆ S_n(o) (sphere in X¹), the set
  of vertices of B_{n−1}(o) adjacent to all of τ spans a NONEMPTY simplex (Januszkiewicz–Świątkowski 2006,
  Lemma 7.7/Cor 7.5 in dim 2; Chepoi 2000: 1-skeleta are bridged graphs). Proof by induction on n with T2:
  a failure gives a short cycle in a link (contradicting T3) or a non-contractible loop (contradicting T2), via
  minimal disc diagrams (triangulated discs mapping to X) and the combinatorial Gauss–Bonnet
  Σ_v (6 − #corners(v)) · ... with interior vertices of degree ≥ 6 ⇒ boundary curvature ≥ 6 (Lyndon–Schupp V.3
  style, (3,6)-case). Consequences: balls B_n(σ) are convex (geodesically closed), X¹ is bridged.
- **T5 (a 4-clique cannot exist).** X is 2-dimensional and flag (T0 + T3: a 3-cycle in X¹ bounds a triangle of
  X), so X¹ has no K₄.
- **T6 (fixed simplex for a finite cyclic group).** Let F = ⟨g⟩ finite, O = F·v a finite orbit. Let r be minimal
  with C := ∩_{o∈O} B_r(o) ≠ ∅; C is convex (T4), F-invariant, finite. If diam C ≥ 2 then for x,y ∈ C at distance
  2 with midpoint z, bridgedness (T4) gives d(o,z) ≤ r − 1 for every o with d(o,x) = d(o,y) = r ... — the standard
  "centre of a finite set in a bridged graph is a clique" (Chepoi–Osajda 2015, Thm C / Polat 1993; in dim 2 the
  argument is short): minimality of r and convexity force diam C ≤ 1, so C spans a simplex σ (T5: at most a
  triangle). F permutes the vertices of σ preserving types, hence fixes σ's vertices pointwise.
- **T7 (conclusion).** g fixes a vertex hPᵢ ⇒ g ∈ hPᵢh⁻¹ ⇒ h⁻¹gh ∈ Pᵢ. This is
  `CCKWFiniteOrderConjugateIntoVertex` (with x := h).
