# Swarm attack on `thompson-f-is-amenable` (2026-09-16)

Agent: `swarm-thompson-f-is-amenable`. Hole locked and worked:
`thompson-f-every-twisted-binomial-is-ore-with-every-element` (OPEN).

## 1. Setup

- `R = K[F]`, a domain. `F` is amenable iff `R` is Ore (`thompson-f-amenable-iff-group-ring-is-ore`).
- The open hole asks for property O of every pair `(g, lambda)`: `(1 + lambda g) R ∩ b R ≠ 0`
  for all nonzero `b`.
- Established before today:
  - one-bump `g` against every `b`;
  - multi-bump `g` against `b` in stabilizer-type subgroup rings;
  - `lambda = -1` against binomial `b`.
- The binomial-pairs scope says twisted pairs follow from item 1 only when the twist extends
  to a character of `<a, c>`, and "are not claimed in general".

## 3. What was proven

New node: `research/thompson-f-twisted-binomial-pairs-have-common-multiples.md`, with route `-proof`.
The route requires only `thompson-f-binomial-pairs-have-common-multiples`, which is established.

1. **Twisted pairs.** For all `a, c ≠ 1` and `lambda, mu` in `K^x`,
   `(1 + lambda a) R ∩ (1 + mu c) R ≠ 0`, and the same for left ideals.
   - Let `Gamma ⊆ <a, c> × K^x` be generated by `(a, -lambda)` and `(c, -mu)`.
   - *Case A: `Gamma` is a graph.* Then `chi(a) = -lambda`, `chi(c) = -mu` is a character of
     `H = <a, c>`. Twist `h -> chi(h) h` on `K[H]` and use the untwisted item 1 (Fox gradient
     plus Brin--Squier), restricted to `K[H]`.
   - *Case B: `Gamma` contains `(1, kappa)` with `kappa ≠ 1`.* Then `rho(h, k) = k h` has
     `rho(gamma) - 1` in `I = xR + yR` for every `gamma`, so `kappa - 1` is in `I` and `I = R`.
     If `xR ∩ yR = 0`, then `R = xR ⊕ yR`. Writing `1 = e + f` and `x = e x + f x` forces
     `f x = 0`, so `f = 0` and `yR = 0`, a contradiction.
2. **Corollary.** Every `1 + lambda g` with `g ≠ 1` meets `b R` whenever `|supp b| <= 2`.
   Equivalently, any two elements with at most two terms each have a common right multiple.
   - This extends the "established cases" list of the hole from `lambda = -1` to every `lambda`,
     and to every `g`, multi-bump included.
   - A non-Ore witness must have a side with `>= 3` terms.
3. **Germ twist (item 4 of the node).** If `e_0(g) ≠ 0` or `e_1(g) ≠ 0`, twisting by
   `chi = gamma^(p e_0 + q e_1)`, where `gamma^d = -1/lambda` and `d = gcd`, converts
   `1 + lambda g` to `1 - g`. This works over `L = K(gamma)` and descends to `K` coordinatewise
   in a `K`-basis of `L`.
   - So for `g ∉ F'`, the hole for `g` over all fields is equivalent to its `lambda = -1` case.
   - The twist can only matter for `g` in `F'`.
   - This does not settle the `lambda = -1` multi-bump case, which is still open against
     three-term `b`.

Honest size of the contribution: item 1, Case B, is a standard ring-theoretic observation.
The gain for the graph is that twisted pairs, and hence binomial `b`, are now settled for every
twist and every `g`. The hard core, three or more conjugates of a multi-bump `g`, is untouched.

## 4. Approaches tried and where each dies

- **Comaximality for three conjugates (dies).**
  - *Setup.* Property O against `b = sum_(i<=3) beta_i t_i` needs
    `∩_i (1 + lambda c_i) R ≠ 0` with `c_i = t_i^-1 g t_i`.
  - *What the domain property gives.* A surjection `R → ⊕_i R/x_i R` gives `∩_i x_i R ≠ 0`.
    Otherwise `R ≅ ⊕_i R/x_i R` with three nonzero summands, because non-units generate proper
    ideals. That yields nontrivial idempotents in `End(R_R) = R`. So Chinese-remainder
    surjectivity would suffice. So would `(x_1 R ∩ x_2 R) + x_3 R = R`, by item 3 applied to
    the nonzero right ideal `x_1 R ∩ x_2 R`.
  - *Why it fails.* Comaximality of `x_1 R + x_2 R + x_3 R` only gives a rank-2 syzygy module, not a
    common multiple. For one-sided ideals, pairwise comaximality does not imply
    `x_1 R + (x_2 R ∩ x_3 R) = R`; the commutative proof multiplies ideals on the wrong side.
    And when `c_i -> -lambda` extends to a character of `<c_1, c_2, c_3>`, which happens for
    instance whenever the `c_i` are conjugate inside that subgroup, the ideals are not
    comaximal at all.
- **Germ twist inside `F'` (dies).**
  - *Setup.* No character of `F` is nontrivial on `F'`. Characters of `H = <c_t : t in T>`
    exist, for instance germ exponents at `inf supp H` and `sup supp H`.
  - *Why it fails.* They must take the same value `-lambda` on every conjugate at once. If such a
    character exists, the twist only reduces to `lambda = -1`, which is equally open for three or
    more conjugates. If it does not, we are back in the comaximal case, which suffices only for
    two conjugates.
- **Twisting is invisible to counting certificates (observation).**
  - The coset test puts one linear condition on each coset `<c> h` met by `supp v`, whatever
    `mu = -1/lambda` is. So Guba-style counts and the coset-count certificate of the hole are
    uniform in `lambda`.
  - The twist parameter only matters for algebraic certificates such as Fox gradients. That is
    why pairs could be settled for every twist by algebra alone.
- **Amenable subgroups of conjugates (true, useless for the core).**
  - *Bound.* For finite `S` and `c` of infinite order, the cosets `<c> h` met by `S` number at
    most `|cS \ S|`: the top element `s` of each segment has `cs ∉ S`.
  - *Consequence.* If `<c_t : t in supp b>` is amenable, a Følner set with
    `sum_t |c_t S \ S| < |S|` gives a nonzero `v` in every `(1 + lambda c_t) R`.
  - *Why it is useless.* For multi-bump `g` and generic `b`, the conjugates generate subgroups
    containing copies of `F`, so this is circular.
- **Division-ring coefficients (dies).**
  - *Setup.* With `F × F ⊆ F` and `K[F × F] ⊆ D_2[F_1]`, where `D_2` is the Malcev--Neumann
    division ring of the second factor, the one-bump count runs over `D_2`. It works with
    non-central `mu`, since `g` commutes with `D_2` and `g^k - mu^k = (g - mu)(...)`.
  - *Why it fails.* Descent back to `K[F × F]` needs clearing denominators in `D_2` over
    `K[F_2]`, which is the Ore condition for `F_2`. This is circular. It is a sharper form of
    the hole's "other bumps as coefficients" attempt.
- **Degree growth under `g^k` (dies).**
  - *Setup.* Monomials of `b` that move an interior bump endpoint are not pushed into an affine
    end by conjugation with `g^k`. Their positive normal forms grow in degree linearly in `k`.
  - *Why it fails.* In the window count the target dimension is about `4^(D_k+1)/3 · |A|` with
    `D_k` linear in `k`, against `(N + 1) |A|` unknowns. Exponential beats linear, so the count
    fails. This confirms the "global count" attempt in the hole.
- **Rank functions (not closed).**
  - *Setup.* Property O fails for `(g, lambda, b)` iff `r -> b̄ r` embeds `R` into
    `M = R/(1 + lambda g) R`. When `b̄` generates `M`, the domain property forbids that. Then
    `bR + (1 + lambda g) R = R`, which is item 3 of the new node.
  - *Why it is not closed.* In general one would need a rank on right modules that is monotone on
    submodules and vanishes on `M`. For the Malcev--Neumann envelope that is flatness over `R`,
    which I could not establish. It is the Ore question in another form.
- **Caveat on the hole's "Opposite orientations" attempt.** It says to "factor out powers of
  `g_1` and `g_2` to kill the germs at `p` and `q`".
  - *The gap.* Modulo `J = (1 + lambda g) R`, only left powers of `g` give scalar congruences.
    The hole's own "shifting with the centralizer" bullet shows that `g_1` alone permutes
    cosets.
  - *What powers of `g` can do.* When `p = 0` and `q = 1`, multiplying a monomial `f` by `g^k`
    shifts `(e_0(f), e_1(f))` by `k (e_0(g), e_1(g))`. That normalizes one coordinate, not both,
    unless the vectors are proportional. For interior `p` or `q`, monomials of `b` do not fix
    those points, so their germs there are undefined.
  - *Consequence.* That partial reduction needs a different justification, for example
    restricting to monomials that fix `p` and `q`, which is the stabilizer case already
    established. I did not edit the node.
