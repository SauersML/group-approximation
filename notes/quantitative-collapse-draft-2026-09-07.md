trivial" is equivalent to a single linear inequality on unitary matrices of every size, with a
FIXED finite set of relations and a constant independent of the dimension.  Two consequences
follow with no further input: a finitely presented cover with the same property on the same
generators, and openness of the property in the space of marked groups.  Items 3 and 4 of the
forwarded note (Hull common quotients with prescribed finite data; `C^*_r(Q)` simple with unique
trace and stable rank one) rest on Hull's theorem and on Dahmani--Guirardel--Osin and
Gerasimova--Osin respectively, i.e. on the same literature footing as Theorem 5; they are drafted
as a remark after Theorem 5 and are not part of the Lean campaign.

```tex
\subsection{A dimension-independent collapse inequality}
\label{sec:collapse}

For a group $G$ with generators $g_1,\dots,g_m$, a \emph{relation} is a
word $r$ in the free group $F_m$ with $r(g_1,\dots,g_m)=1$.  For
unitaries $U_1,\dots,U_m\in\U(d)$ write $r(U)=r(U_1,\dots,U_m)$.  Every
group in this paper satisfies the following inequality, which says
more than that the group is not MF: finitely many equations in the
$U_i$, with a fixed constant, control the whole tuple in every matrix
dimension at once.

\begin{proposition}[linear collapse]\label{prop:collapse}
Let $G$ be generated by $g_1,\dots,g_m$, and suppose that every
homomorphism from $G$ to an MF group is trivial.  Then there are
finitely many relations $r_1,\dots,r_s$ of $G$ and a constant $C>0$
such that for every $d\ge1$ and all $U_1,\dots,U_m\in\U(d)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
 \max_i\opnorm{U_i-1}\le C\max_j\opnorm{r_j(U)-1}.
\end{equation}
Conversely, if~\eqref{eq:collapse} holds for some finite set of
relations of $G$ and some $C$, then every homomorphism from $G$ to an
MF group is trivial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The group $G$ is perfect: otherwise its abelianization is a nontrivial
finitely generated abelian group, which has a nontrivial finite
quotient, and finite groups are MF\@.  So there are words
$a_{ik},b_{ik}\in F_m$ with
$g_i=\prod_{k=1}^{q_i}[a_{ik}(g),b_{ik}(g)]$, and
$w_i=x_i^{-1}\prod_k[a_{ik},b_{ik}]$ is a relation of $G$ for each $i$.

Put $D(U)=\max_i\opnorm{U_i-1}$.  For unitaries,
$\opnorm{AB-1}\le\opnorm{A-1}+\opnorm{B-1}$ and
$\opnorm{A^{-1}-1}=\opnorm{A-1}$, so $\opnorm{a(U)-1}\le|a|\,D(U)$ for
every word $a$ of length $|a|$.  Also
$[A,B]-1=(AB-BA)B^{-1}A^{-1}$ and $AB-BA=(A-1)(B-1)-(B-1)(A-1)$, so
$\opnorm{[A,B]-1}\le2\opnorm{A-1}\opnorm{B-1}$.  With
$B=2\max_i\sum_k|a_{ik}||b_{ik}|$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse-local}
 D(U)\le\max_i\opnorm{w_i(U)-1}+B\,D(U)^2 .
\end{equation}

Suppose that no finite set of relations and no constant
satisfy~\eqref{eq:collapse}.  Enumerate the relations of $G$ as an
increasing sequence of finite sets $\mathcal R_1\subseteq\mathcal
R_2\subseteq\cdots$ whose union is all of them, with
$w_1,\dots,w_m\in\mathcal R_1$.  For each $n$ there are $d_n$ and
$U^{(n)}\in\U(d_n)^m$ with $D_n>n\delta_n$, where $D_n=D(U^{(n)})$ and
$\delta_n=\max_{r\in\mathcal R_n}\opnorm{r(U^{(n)})-1}$.  Since
$D_n\le2$, $\delta_n\to0$, so every relation of $G$ has defect tending
to zero along the sequence, and $g_i\mapsto(U^{(n)}_i)_n$ defines a
corona homomorphism of $G$; after padding each $U^{(n)}$ by an identity
block, which changes neither $D_n$ nor $\delta_n$, the sizes may be
taken strictly increasing.  Its image is an MF group, so the
homomorphism is trivial and $D_n\to0$.  But~\eqref{eq:collapse-local}
and $\delta_n<D_n/n$ give $1<1/n+BD_n$ for every $n$, which is
impossible.

For the converse, let $\varphi\colon G\to M$ be a homomorphism to an MF
group and $M\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})$ a corona embedding.  Lift
the images of $g_1,\dots,g_m$ to unitaries $U^{(n)}\in\U(d_n)^m$.  Each
$r_j(U^{(n)})$ tends to $1$, so by~\eqref{eq:collapse} each $U^{(n)}_i$
tends to $1$, and $\varphi(g_i)$ is trivial in the corona.  So
$\varphi$ is trivial.
\end{proof}

The proof produces the relations and the constant by compactness; it
does not compute them.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:collapse-fp}
Let $G$ and $r_1,\dots,r_s$ be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse},
and put $P=\langle x_1,\dots,x_m\mid r_1,\dots,r_s\rangle$.  Then $P$
maps onto $G$, and every homomorphism from $P$ to an MF group is
trivial.  In particular, every finitely generated group with this
property is a quotient of a finitely presented group with the same
property and the same number of generators, and the property is open
in the space of marked groups on $m$ generators.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The $r_j$ are relations of $G$, so $P$ maps onto $G$, and they are
relations of $P$, so~\eqref{eq:collapse} holds for $P$ with the same
$C$; the converse in Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} applies.  Any
marked group in which $r_1,\dots,r_s$ hold is a quotient of $P$, which
gives openness.
\end{proof}

Applied to the two-generated group $Q$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:torsion-free}, the proposition gives finitely many
equations in two unitary matrices $U,V$ with
$\max\{\opnorm{U-1},\opnorm{V-1}\}\le C\max_j\opnorm{r_j(U,V)-1}$ in
every dimension, while the left regular representation of $Q$ solves
the same equations exactly with $U,V\ne1$.
```

```tex
\begin{remark}
Two further properties of $Q$ come from the literature.  First, $Q$ can
be fed into Hull's common-quotient construction: for every finitely
presented torsion-free acylindrically hyperbolic group $G$ and finite
$\Omega\subseteq G$ there is a quotient $G\to P$ injective on $\Omega$
with $P$ two-generated, finitely presented, torsion-free, acylindrically
hyperbolic, with property~\textup{(T)}, and with no nontrivial
homomorphism to an MF group~\cite[Theorem~7.1 and
Corollary~7.4]{Hull}; for $G=F_2$ this gives such groups of arbitrarily
large girth in the inherited marking.  Second, $C^*_{\mathrm r}(Q)$ is
simple with unique tracial state, because $Q$ is acylindrically
hyperbolic with trivial finite radical~\cite[Theorem~2.35]{DGO}, and
has stable rank one~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{GerasimovaOsin}; so it is a
separable simple unital $C^*$-algebra with unique trace and stable rank
one, generated by two unitaries, that is not MF\@.
\end{remark}
```
