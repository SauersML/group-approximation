# The full certified atlas package has an exact finite central phase

Date: 2026-08-11

## Result

Let

\[
P=A_8*A_8
\]

be the two-chart atlas group.  Let \(\mathcal Z_5\) be the 24 cyclic-class
representatives of the 178 radius-five relations already proved trivial by
perfect-overlap certificates, and let \(r=r_{11}\) be phase class `11`.

**Theorem.** There is a homomorphism

\[
 \rho:P\longrightarrow H,
 \qquad H\cong (C_2)^7\rtimes A_8,
 \qquad |H|=2^7|A_8|=2{,}580{,}480,              \tag{DC1}
\]

such that

\[
 \rho(w)=1\quad(w\in\mathcal Z_5),
 \qquad
 \rho(r)=z\ne1,
 \qquad
 z\in Z(H),\quad z^2=1.                          \tag{DC2}
\]

Moreover \(Z(H)=\langle z\rangle\cong C_2\).  Consequently

\[
 r\notin
 \left\langle\!\left\langle
       \mathcal Z_5,\ [r,P]
 \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_P.               \tag{DC3}
\]

Thus phase class `11` survives after imposing **both** every currently
certified radius-five zero relation and full centrality of the phase.  This
closes the exact finite-central-target gate left open in
`FALSE_ATLAS_CERTIFIED_A8_QUOTIENT.md`.

The same finite group gives an honest complex unitary model with canonical
regular chart marginals.  There are representations

\[
 \pi_1,\pi_2:A_8\longrightarrow U(1{,}290{,}240)
\]

whose restrictions are each \(64\) copies of the regular representation of
\(A_8\), for which all 24 classes act as \(I\) and

\[
 \pi(r)=-I.                                      \tag{DC4}
\]

In particular the phase is scalar and occupies all of the dimension; this
is not merely an operator-norm-visible small corner.

## Dual-number construction

Put

\[
 D=\mathbb F _2[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]

Fix an isomorphism from the atlas copy
\(GL_4(\mathbb F _2)\cong A_8\) to the natural degree-eight permutation
group, and write

\[
 \sigma:A_8\hookrightarrow GL_8(D)
\]

for its permutation-matrix representation.  Use the inner alignment

```text
h = 00000100000100000100000000000001
```

in row-major \(\mathbb F _2\) encoding.  In the chosen eight-point action,

```text
sigma(h) = (0 2)(1 7)(3 5)(4 6)
```

in zero-based notation.  Finally set

\[
 X=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0,0,0,0,1,0),
 \qquad
 U=(I+\varepsilon X)\sigma(h).                    \tag{DC5}
\]

Define the two chart maps by

\[
 \rho_1(g)=\sigma(g),
 \qquad
 \rho_2(g)=U\sigma(g)U^{-1}.                     \tag{DC6}
\]

These are honest group homomorphisms, not approximate representations.
Since the characteristic is two and \(\varepsilon^2=0\),

\[
 (I+\varepsilon X)^{-1}=I+\varepsilon X.          \tag{DC7}
\]

Writing a dual-number matrix as \(A_0+\varepsilon A_1\), multiplication is
the exact binary rule

\[
 (A_0,A_1)(B_0,B_1)
 =\bigl(A_0B_0,\ A_0B_1+A_1B_0\bigr).            \tag{DC8}
\]

Direct evaluation of the original atlas words with `(DC8)` gives

\[
 \rho(w)=(I,0) \quad(w\in\mathcal Z_5),
 \qquad
 \rho(r)=(I,I)=(1+\varepsilon)I.                 \tag{DC9}

The second equality immediately proves that the phase is nonidentity,

Every one of the 178 certified relations is a cyclic conjugate or inverse

## Identification of the finite image

Reduction modulo \(\varepsilon\) sends both charts onto the same natural
copy of \(A_8\).  Since \(\sigma(h)\) already belongs to that copy, the
kernel generated by the discrepancy between the two charts consists of

\[
 I+\varepsilon\bigl(X+gXg^{-1}\bigr),
 \qquad g\in A_8.                                \tag{DC10}
\]

The diagonal of \(X\) is the characteristic function of a two-element
subset of the eight points.  The group \(A_8\) is two-transitive, so the
span of `(DC10)` contains the characteristic function of every two-element
subset difference.  These span exactly the even-weight subspace

\[
 E=\{v\in\mathbb F _2^8:\textstyle\sum_i v_i=0\},
 \qquad \dim E=7.                                \tag{DC11}
\]

Conversely every generator in `(DC10)` lies in this subspace.  Therefore
the image is exactly

\[
 H=(I+\varepsilon\operatorname{Diag}(E))\rtimes A_8
   \cong(C_2)^7\rtimes A_8.                      \tag{DC12}
\]

The fixed vectors of the natural \(A_8\)-action on \(E\) form the scalar
line \(\langle(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)\rangle\).  Since \(A_8\) has trivial
center, `(DC12)` has center precisely

\[
 Z(H)=\{I,(1+\varepsilon)I\}.                    \tag{DC13}
\]

This also identifies the atlas phase with the unique nonidentity central
element of the finite image.

## Regular-marginal unitary corollary

Let \(\lambda_H\) be the complex left-regular representation of \(H\), and
let \(V_-\) be the \((-1)\)-eigenspace of the central involution \(z\).
Then

\[
 \dim V_-=|H|/2=1{,}290{,}240.                   \tag{DC14}
\]

For either embedded chart \(A_8\le H\), the subgroup
\(A_8\times\langle z\rangle\) has index

\[
 [H:A_8\times\langle z\rangle]=64.
\]

Restricting \(V_-\) to that chart therefore gives

\[
 V_-\!\downarrow_{A_8}\cong64\lambda_{A_8}.     \tag{DC15}
\]

On this space \(z\) acts as \(-I\), proving `(DC4)`.  Thus the exact
finite quotient automatically upgrades to a balanced full-chart unitary
model with a trace-visible phase.

## Consequence and exact scope

Suppose `(DC3)` were false.  Any homomorphism killing \(\mathcal Z_5\) and
centralizing \(r\) would then kill \(r\).  Equations `(DC2)` contradict
this, proving the nonmembership.

This is a strict advance over the previous outer \(A_8\) quotient: there
the phase survived but was noncentral, while here it is the unique central
involution of an explicit finite image.  It also subsumes the earlier
four-relation order-2688 central block by treating all 24 certified
classes simultaneously.

It does **not** yet prove that \(r\ne0\) in the full relation module
\(R/[P,R]\).  Relations outside the currently certified radius-five
package may still kill the phase.  Nor does the characteristic-two matrix
construction itself prove hyperlinearity or nonsoficity.  Its exact content
is that the entire known local relation package, even after centralization,
cannot kill class `11`; the next obstruction must use genuinely new
higher-radius/full-Leavitt relations.
