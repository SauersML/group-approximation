# Matrix exponents give a free marked compressor orbit

Date: 2026-08-12

## 1. Outcome

The standard Laurent Kun--Thom pair uses the exponent lattice
(\mathbb Z^d).  Every nonzero exponent vector has a large parabolic
stabilizer in (\operatorname {SL}_d(\mathbb Z)), and that stabilizer enters
the unresolved Fourier-fiber globalization.

There is a simple amplification which preserves the entire Kazhdan,
residual-finiteness, compression, and positive-core structure while making
the **marked external orbit free**.

Let (k=\mathbb F_q), let (d,r\geq3), and put

\[
V=M_d(\mathbb Z),\qquad C=M_d(\mathbb N).
\tag{FMO1}
\]

Write (y^A) for the group-algebra monomial associated with (A\in V),
and define

\[
R_+=k[C]\cong k[y_{ij}:1\leq i,j\leq d],
\qquad
R=k[V]\cong k[y_{ij}^{\pm1}:1\leq i,j\leq d].
\tag{FMO2}
\]

Let (L=\operatorname {SL}_d(\mathbb Z)) act on (V) by left
multiplication and hence on (R) by

\[
\alpha_\ell(y^A)=y^{\ell A}.
\tag{FMO3}
\]

Set

\[
\Gamma=E_r(R_+),
\qquad
G=E_r(R)\rtimes L.
\tag{FMO4}
\]

Then `(FMO4)` is again a residually finite Kazhdan nonnormal infranormal
pair.  Its positive compression semigroup contains

\[
L_+=L\cap M_d(\mathbb N).
\tag{FMO5}
\]

Its positive Bernoulli core is sofic by the same polynomial-submonoid
argument as in `FALSE_POSITIVE_CORE_IS_SOFIC.md`.

Now take

\[
t=I_d+E_{12}\in L_+,
\qquad
\gamma=e_{ab}(y^{I_d})\in\Gamma\quad(a\neq b),
\qquad
h=t^{-1}\gamma t.
\tag{FMO6}
\]

The coefficient exponent of (h) is

\[
t^{-1}I_d=I_d-E_{12}\notin C,
\tag{FMO7}
\]

so (h\notin\Gamma), while (t h t^{-1}=\gamma\in\Gamma).  Moreover

\[
\operatorname {Stab}_L(I_d)=\{1\},
\tag{FMO8}
\]

and hence the external orbit of the marked coefficient is the regular
(L)-set.  The whole forward compressor ray is positive:

\[
t^nI_d=I_d+nE_{12}\in C\qquad(n\geq0),
\tag{FMO9}
\]

whereas the first inverse step is outside (C).

Thus the one-cut geometry survives, but the marked orbit has no stabilizer
cocycle or stabilizer intertwiner to solve.  This removes one genuine
globalization obstruction from the explicit pair.

It does not by itself prove FALSE.  The positive polynomial generators
still have rank-one exponent matrices and their covariance relations must
be realized.  More importantly, an honest finite exponent quotient still
absorbs the predecessor into the positive monoid.  The remaining problem is
now separated cleanly from the marked orbit: build the nonliftable paired
Fourier functor on regular external charts while respecting the finite
positive ring window.

## 2. The coefficient rings and the positive action

The additive monoid (C) is freely generated by the (d^2) standard
matrix units.  Hence `(FMO2)` is literally a polynomial/Laurent polynomial
pair in (d^2) variables.

For every (ell\in L_+), left multiplication sends a nonnegative matrix
to a nonnegative matrix.  Therefore

\[
\alpha_\ell(R_+)\subseteq R_+,
\tag{FMO10}
\]

and the external element (ell) compresses (Gamma).  The product rule
shows that

\[
T=\Gamma L_+
\tag{FMO11}
\]

is a subsemigroup of the compression semigroup (P_\Gamma).

Positive elementary transvections belong to (L_+) and generate (L) as
a group.  It remains to check that the conjugates of (R_+) generate the
whole Laurent coefficient ring.  For every standard matrix unit (E_{ij}),
the monomial exponent (E_{ij}) lies in (C).  A determinant-one diagonal
sign change which flips row (i) and one other row sends (E_{ij}) to
(-E_{ij}).  Consequently the monoid generated by

\[
\bigcup_{\ell\in L}\ell C
\tag{FMO12}
\]

contains both signs of every standard basis element of (V), and is all of
(V).  The (L)-conjugates of (R_+) therefore generate (R) as a ring.
Elementary root generators then show that (T) generates (G) as a group.
This is precisely infranormality.

Nonnormality follows already from `(FMO6)--(FMO7)`.

## 3. Property (T) and residual finiteness

Both rings in `(FMO2)` are finitely generated commutative rings.  The
universal-lattice property-`(T)` theorem gives property `(T)` to
(E_r(R_+)) and (E_r(R)) for (r\geq3).  The quotient group (L) also
has property `(T)` for (d\geq3), and property `(T)` is closed under group
extensions.  Hence both (Gamma) and (G) are Kazhdan.

Residual finiteness is explicit.  For a finite test set, reduce the
coefficient field if necessary, reduce (V) modulo (mV), and reduce (L)
modulo the same (m).  This gives the finite equivariant coefficient ring

\[
k[M_d(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)]
\tag{FMO13}
\]

and a finite semidirect-product matrix quotient.  Choosing (m) larger
than all coordinate differences in the finite monomial support separates
that support, and the ordinary congruence quotients separate the external
matrices.  Thus (G), and hence (Gamma), is residually finite.

## 4. The positive core remains sofic

Fix (ell\in L_+).  The stabilizer in (Gamma) of the positive coset
(ell\Gamma) is

\[
H_\ell=\ell\Gamma\ell^{-1}=E_r(k[\ell C]).
\tag{FMO14}
\]

The monoid (ell C) is freely generated by the (d^2) matrices
(ell E_{ij}): invertibility of (ell) over (mathbb Z) makes them
linearly independent and preserves all additive relations.  Hence

\[
k[\ell C]\cong k[z_{ij}:1\leq i,j\leq d]
\tag{FMO15}
\]

is again a polynomial ring.  Suslin stability identifies the elementary
and special linear groups over both polynomial rings.

If (g\in\Gamma\setminus H_\ell), some matrix entry of (g) contains a
monomial exponent outside (ell C).  Truncating all monomials above the
maximum degree occurring in that entry gives a finite ring quotient which
preserves the offending monomial and separates (g) from (H_\ell).
Thus every positive stabilizer is congruence separable.

Exactly as in `FALSE_POSITIVE_CORE_IS_SOFIC.md`, the positive action

\[
\Gamma\curvearrowright T\Gamma/\Gamma
\tag{FMO16}
\]

is sofic, and so is its generalized binary wreath product.  The common
positive Bernoulli crossed product is therefore Connes embeddable.

## 5. Why the marked orbit is genuinely simpler

For every full-rank exponent (A\in M_d(\mathbb Z)),

\[
\ell A=A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ell=I_d.
\tag{FMO17}
\]

Thus every full-rank exponent has a free (L)-orbit.  The choice (A=I_d)
has the additional positive-ray property `(FMO9)`.  The old standard-vector
choice had a parabolic stabilizer and required a stabilizer-covariant fiber
functor.  Here the stabilizer equation is empty.

At a finite external window, the marked labels are indexed directly by a
finite ball in (L), with no quotient by a subgroup and no ambiguity in a
Schreier section.  Therefore the relative-holonomy table on the marked
orbit is a free vertex potential: edge transitions are automatically

\[
\zeta_1(s,x)=t(sx)\zeta_0(s,x)t(x)^*.
\tag{FMO18}
\]

All cycle identities in the external presentation follow formally from
`(FMO18)`.  What remains nontrivial is not marked-orbit holonomy but the
compatibility of the same matrices with the positive coefficient-ring
relations and the base elementary group.

There is an exact finite version of the same observation.  Let

\[
L_m=\operatorname {im}\bigl(L\longrightarrow
\operatorname {SL}_d(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\tag{FMO19}
\]

Under the left action on (M_d(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)), the orbit of (I_d)
is

\[
L_m I_d=L_m,
\tag{FMO20}
\]

and its stabilizer is trivial.  Thus this finite orbit is the regular
(L_m)-set, not merely a locally free Schreier chart.  The free-table theorem
of `FALSE_REGULAR_BUNDLE_FREE_TABLE_AND_KERNEL_GATE.md` applies to the
marked orbit without a kernel-valued stabilizer carry.

This does **not** solve the finite model.  In the same quotient,

\[
I_d-E_{12}\equiv I_d+(m-1)E_{12}\pmod m,
\tag{FMO21}
\]

and the right side lies in the image of (C).  Hence the marked Laurent
coefficient is already in the finite positive image.  Every unitary which
commutes with the positive image also commutes with the marked image.  The
regular-orbit gain and the finite-ring absorption theorem coexist: the
former removes the stabilizer equation, while the latter forces the final
model to be genuinely nonliftable rather than an honest congruence model.

This is the correct next place to reuse the paired Fourier cell.  The local
wall and its external transport no longer require an amenable-stabilizer
induction.  A successful construction must only make the regular-orbit
fiber assignment into an asymptotic representation of the full positive
ring window.  Conversely, any obstruction now found at that step is a ring
or Kazhdan covariance obstruction, not a stabilizer artifact.
