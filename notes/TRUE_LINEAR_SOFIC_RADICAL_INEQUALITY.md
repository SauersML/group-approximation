# Sofic-radical membership is equivalent to a linear finite-action inequality

Date: 2026-08-11

## Purpose

The one-witness reduction in `TRUE_ONE_WITNESS_TRANSITIVE_LOSS.md` produces
a positive weighted separator if the normal-Dirac transfer fails.  In the
finite-presentation formulation, the outside word belongs to the full sofic
radical.  Cartesian powers of finite actions give a stronger conclusion:
sofic-radical membership is equivalent to one global **linear** inequality
between the motion of the radical word and the total relator defect.

This eliminates the arbitrary minimax weights.  A hypothetical hyperlinear
nonsofic group already yields a finite presentation whose finite-action
characters satisfy the linear inequality, while finite-dimensional unitary
characters violate it asymptotically by the maximum possible amount.

## Setup

Let `F` be a finitely generated free group, let `R subset F` be finite, and
put

`P=F/normal_closure_F(R)`.                              `(LSR1)`

Fix `w in F`.  For a nonempty finite `F`-action `rho`, write

`d_rho(g)=1-chi_rho(g)`                                `(LSR2)`

for the moved-point fraction of `g`, and define

`Def_R(rho)=sum_(r in R)d_rho(r)`.                     `(LSR3)`

Repeated relators may be retained in `(LSR3)` if `R` is regarded as a
finite multiset; nothing below changes.

## Qualitative radical criterion

**Lemma 1.**  The image of `w` belongs to `Rad_sof(P)` if and only if every
sequence of nonempty finite `F`-actions `(rho_n)` satisfies

`Def_R(rho_n)->0  ==>  d_(rho_n)(w)->0`.               `(LSR4)`

**Proof.**  Suppose first that the implication fails.  After passing to a
subsequence there is `eta>0` such that

`Def_R(rho_n)->0`, `d_(rho_n)(w)>=eta`.                `(LSR5)`

Choose a nonprincipal ultrafilter and send each free generator to its
sequence of permutations in the metric ultraproduct.  Every relator in `R`
has zero ultraproduct Hamming length, so the homomorphism from `F` factors
through `P`.  The image is a subgroup of a metric ultraproduct of finite
symmetric groups and is therefore sofic.  The second part of `(LSR5)` says
that the image of `w` is nonidentity.  Hence `w` is not in `Rad_sof(P)`.

Conversely, if `w` is not in `Rad_sof(P)`, there is a homomorphism

`phi:P->S`, `S` sofic, `phi(w)!=1`.                    `(LSR6)`

Restrict to the finitely generated subgroup of `S` generated by the image
of the chosen free generators and take a faithful sofic approximation of
that subgroup.  Pulling its generator permutations back to `F` gives
finite actions whose relator defects tend to zero, while the moved fraction
of `w` stays bounded away from zero (and may be made to tend to one by slow
Cartesian powering).  Thus `(LSR4)` fails.  End proof.

## Cartesian powers force a linear modulus

For the diagonal action on `X^m`, fixed-point fractions multiply:

`chi_(rho^(times m))(g)=chi_rho(g)^m`.                 `(LSR7)`

Consequently, for `0<=a<=1`,

`1-(1-a)^m<=m a`.                                     `(LSR8)`

**Theorem 2 (linear sofic-radical inequality).**  The following are
equivalent.

1. The image of `w` lies in `Rad_sof(P)`.
2. There exists a finite constant `C>=0` such that every nonempty finite
   `F`-action satisfies

   `d_rho(w)<=C Def_R(rho)`.                           `(LSR9)`

3. There exists such a constant `C` for every transitive finite action
   `F/H`, where `H<=F` has finite index.

**Proof.**  Assertion 2 implies the qualitative criterion `(LSR4)`, so
Lemma 1 gives `2=>1`.  The implication `2=>3` is immediate.

For `3=>2`, decompose an arbitrary finite action into transitive orbits.
Both `d_rho(w)` and `Def_R(rho)` are affine, with weights equal to the
relative orbit sizes.  Summing the transitive inequalities gives `(LSR9)`
for the whole action.

It remains to prove `1=>2`.  By Lemma 1 it suffices to show that failure of
every linear bound contradicts `(LSR4)`.  If no `C` works, choose finite
actions `rho_n` and put

`a_n=Def_R(rho_n)`, `b_n=d_(rho_n)(w)>n a_n`.          `(LSR10)`

The strict inequality ensures `b_n>0`, and `a_n<=1/n` because `b_n<=1`.
Thus `a_n->0`; Lemma 1 then gives `b_n->0`.

Set

`m_n=ceil(1/b_n)`                                     `(LSR11)`

and let `sigma_n=rho_n^(times m_n)`.  Equations `(LSR7)--(LSR8)` give

`Def_R(sigma_n)<=m_n a_n`
` <=a_n/b_n+a_n->0`.                                  `(LSR12)`

On the other hand,

`d_(sigma_n)(w)=1-(1-b_n)^(m_n)`
` >=1-(1-b_n)^(1/b_n)>=1-e^(-1)`.                     `(LSR13)`

The last inequality is `log(1-b)<=-b`.  Equations `(LSR12)--(LSR13)`
contradict `(LSR4)`.  Hence a finite `C` exists, proving `1=>2` and the
theorem.  End proof.

## Finite-index-subgroup form

For a transitive action `F/H`, equation `(LSR9)` is exactly

`Prob_(xH in F/H)[x^(-1)wx notin H]`
` <=C sum_(r in R) Prob_(xH in F/H)[x^(-1)rx notin H]`. `(LSR14)`

Thus radical membership is equivalent to a uniform linear implication on
all finite-index subgroups, averaged over their conjugates.  No disjoint
union, real separator weights, or multiple outside witnesses are needed.

The optimal constant has the exact variational formula

`C_(P,w;R)=sup_(rho:Def_R(rho)>0) d_rho(w)/Def_R(rho)`
` =sup_([F:H]<infinity, Def_R(F/H)>0)`
`      d_(F/H)(w)/Def_R(F/H)`.                         `(LSR15)`

Indeed orbit decomposition writes the numerator and denominator as the
same convex combinations of the orbit numerators and denominators.  Their
ratio is a weighted average of the orbit ratios, with weights proportional
to the orbit denominator.  An orbit with zero denominator also has zero
numerator by Theorem 2 and contributes nothing.  Thus the supremum is
already transitive, and Theorem 2 says precisely that `(LSR15)` is finite.

Rearranging `(LSR9)` gives the positive loss inequality

`C sum_(r in R)(1-chi_rho(r))+chi_rho(w)>=1`.          `(LSR16)`

After division by `C|R|+1` (with the evident interpretation when `C=0`),
this is a positive normalized certificate of the form `(PLC5)` in
`NOTEPAD.md`, with equal weight on every listed relator.  The constant is
not asserted to be computable from the presentation.

## Exact consequence for the hyperlinear--sofic problem

Combine Theorem 2 with the finite-presentation localization
`(FPR1)--(FPR12)` in `NOTEPAD.md`.

**Corollary 3.**  A hyperlinear nonsofic group exists if and only if there
are `F,R,P,w` as in `(LSR1)` and normalized finite-dimensional characters
`psi_n:F->C`, real and nonnegative, such that

`psi_n(r)->1` for every `r in R`,
`psi_n(w)->0`,                                         `(LSR17)`

while the finite-action inequality `(LSR9)` holds for some finite `C`.

**Proof.**  From a counterexample, finite-presentation localization gives
`w in Rad_sof(P)` together with a hyperlinear quotient in which `w`
survives.  Conjugate doubling its matrix characters gives `(LSR17)`, and
Theorem 2 gives `(LSR9)`.  Conversely, `(LSR17)` produces a hyperlinear
quotient of `P` retaining `w`, whereas `(LSR9)` says `w in Rad_sof(P)`.
That quotient cannot be sofic.  End proof.

For the matrix characters in `(LSR17)`, the direct unitary analogue of the
left side of `(LSR9)` has

`1-psi_n(w)->1`,
`sum_(r in R)(1-psi_n(r))->0`.                         `(LSR18)`

Hence no finite constant satisfies the unitary version.  The remaining TRUE
theorem is now particularly sharp: prove that a normal `0/1` limit of
finite-dimensional characters cannot violate a linear inequality which is
valid on every finite quasiregular character.  Theorem 2 proves the linear
classical modulus; it does not yet transfer that inequality to matrix
characters.
