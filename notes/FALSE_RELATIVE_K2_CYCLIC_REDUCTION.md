# Relative K2 reduces the stabilizer-paired quotient to one cyclic direction

Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Outcome

After the polynomial-stabilizer relations are imposed, the apparently
infinite paired Bockstein problem for the Laurent congruence intermediate
group has at most one dimension.  At prime two even that last dimension
vanishes.  All directions coming from the
congruence kernel are generated, up to prime-to-`p` defect, by relative
elementary roots.  Their ordinary Bockstein classes have the explicit
stabilizer lifts constructed in
`FALSE_CYCLIC_ROOT_POWER_STABILIZER_NO_GO.md`.

At odd primes, the only possible residual direction is the cyclic quotient
generated by the Laurent element `h`.  Thus the local problem there is no
longer to compute a large congruence homology group.  It is the single
question whether the ordinary `h`-torsion class belongs to

`beta_1(ker(beta_chi))`                                `(RK1)`

after relative-root stabilizer classes are removed.

The prime-two sign case is settled completely in Section 7.

## 2. An abstract paired-quotient formula

Let `G` be a group, let `p` be prime, and let

`beta_1:H_2(G,F_p)->H_1(G,Z)`,
`beta_chi:H_2(G,F_p)->H_1(G,O_chi)`                   `(RK2)`

be the ordinary and cyclotomic coefficient Bocksteins.  Let `J` be any
subspace of `H_2(G,F_p)`; below it will be the sum of the images of all
polynomial double-coset stabilizers.

Because the coefficient sequence is exact,

`im(beta_1)=H_1(G,Z)[p]`.                             `(RK3)`

The first isomorphism theorem gives the exact linear identity

`H_2(G,F_p)/(ker(beta_1)+ker(beta_chi)+J)`
` ~= H_1(G,Z)[p] /`
`    (beta_1(ker(beta_chi))+beta_1(J))`.              `(RK4)`

Indeed, first quotient by `ker(beta_1)` and identify the result with
`im(beta_1)`; the images of the other two summands are exactly the
denominator on the right.  Formula `(RK4)` is important: `beta_1` does not
itself descend through `ker(beta_chi)`, but its image quotient does.

## 3. Relative roots lie in the stabilizer-Bockstein image

Use the stable elementary group for the moment.  Put

`R=Z[x_1^(+-1),...,x_d^(+-1)]`,
`R_+=Z[x_1,...,x_d]`,
`E=E(R)`, `Gamma=E(R_+)`,                             `(RK5)`

and

`K_p=ker(E(R)->E(R/pR))`.                             `(RK6)`

Let `N_p=E(R,pR)` be the normal closure in `E(R)` of the root elements

`e_ij(pa)`, `a in R`.                                 `(RK7)`

Let `B_p=<K_p,h>`, where `h=e_12(x_1^(-1))`; then
`B_p/K_p~=C_p`.  For `d in E`, write

`S_d=B_p intersect d Gamma d^(-1)`,
`J_p=sum_d im(H_2(S_d,F_p)->H_2(B_p,F_p))`.           `(RK8)`

**Lemma 1.**  The image of `N_p` in `H_1(B_p,Z)` is contained in

`beta_1(J_p)`.                                        `(RK9)`

**Proof.**  The group `N_p` is generated by conjugates

`z=g e_ij(pa) g^(-1)`, `g in E`.                      `(RK10)`

In the abelianization, split `a` into its finitely many Laurent monomials.
It is therefore enough to treat

`r=e_ij(p c x^u)`                                     `(RK11)`

for an integer `c` and an exponent vector `u`.  Choose a third index
`ell`.  Then

`r^p=e_ij(p^2 c x^u)`
`   =[e_iell(p c x^u),e_ellj(p)]`.                    `(RK12)`

Both commutator factors lie in `K_p`.  The directed root pattern
`i->ell->j` is acyclic, so a determinant-one Laurent diagonal (using one
unused coordinate) simultaneously conjugates `r` and both factors in
`(RK12)` into `Gamma`.  The cyclic-plus-commutator bar cycle `(CRP12)`
therefore belongs to one `S_d` and has ordinary Bockstein `[r]`.

Conjugate the entire cycle by `g`.  Normality of `K_p` keeps all its terms
in `B_p`, while its stabilizer becomes the conjugate corresponding to
`g d`.  Its Bockstein is `[z]`.  These classes generate the image of
`N_p`, proving `(RK9)`.  End proof.

Combining `(RK4)` and `(RK9)` shows that the stabilizer-paired quotient is
a quotient of

`H_1(B_p,Z)[p] / im(N_p)`.                            `(RK13)`

## 4. The congruence defect is a K2 cokernel

Let `S=R/pR`.  The Steinberg extensions form a commutative diagram

`K_2(R) -> St(R) -> E(R) -> 1`
`  |          |        |`
`K_2(S) -> St(S) -> E(S) -> 1`.                       `(RK14)`

The middle map is onto because every root over `S` lifts to a root over
`R`.  Its kernel is normally generated by roots with coefficients in
`pR`; its image in `E(R)` is `N_p`.  Applying the snake argument to
`(RK14)` gives

`K_p/N_p ~= coker(K_2(R)->K_2(S))`.                   `(RK15)`

For completeness, the map from left to right in `(RK15)` can be described
directly.  Lift an element of `K_p` to `St(R)`.  Its image in `St(S)` lies
in the central subgroup `K_2(S)`.  Changing the lift changes this element
by the image of `K_2(R)`.  The kernel consists exactly of lifts in the
relative Steinberg kernel, whose elementary images form `N_p`; every
element of `K_2(S)` has a lift because `St(R)->St(S)` is onto.

## 5. Exact Laurent K2 calculation

Both `Z` and `F_p` are regular.  Iterating the fundamental theorem for
Laurent polynomial rings gives, naturally in the base ring,

`K_2(A[Z^d])`
` ~= K_2(A) directSum K_1(A)^d`
`    directSum K_0(A)^(binom(d,2))`.                  `(RK16)`

The needed base groups and reduction maps are

`K_2(Z)=C_2`, `K_2(F_p)=0`,
`K_1(Z)={+-1}`, `K_1(F_p)=F_p^x`,
`K_0(Z)=Z -> K_0(F_p)=Z` is an isomorphism.           `(RK17)`

Therefore

`coker(K_2(R)->K_2(S))`
` ~= (F_p^x/{+-1})^d`                                 `(RK18)`

for odd `p`, while it is zero for `p=2`.  In particular,

`K_p/N_p has no p-torsion`.                           `(RK19)`

Formula `(RK16)` is the regular Bass--Heller--Swan decomposition; the Nil
and negative-`K` terms vanish.  The equality `K_2(F_p)=0` is the even-degree
part of Quillen's finite-field calculation.  Thus `(RK18)` is an exact
calculation, not a finiteness heuristic.

The argument is written stably.  It applies verbatim in any finite rank in
which the Steinberg `K_2` and elementary groups have stabilized; the rank
in the Laurent construction may be enlarged once and for all.

## 6. Reduction to the h direction

The quotient `B_p/N_p` fits into

`1->K_p/N_p->B_p/N_p->C_p->1`.                       `(RK20)`

The kernel has order prime to `p` by `(RK18)`.  Hence the `p`-primary part
of the abelianization of `B_p/N_p` has dimension at most one, and any such
class is supported on the image of `h`.  From `(RK4)`, `(RK9)`, and
`(RK19)` we obtain:

**Theorem 2 (cyclic reduction).**  The quotient

`H_2(B_p,F_p) /`
` (ker(beta_1)+ker(beta_chi)+J_p)`                    `(RK21)`

has `F_p`-dimension at most one.  If it is nonzero, its ordinary
Bockstein projects nontrivially to the `C_p` quotient generated by `h`.

At `p=2` there is no relative `K_2` defect at all; before the calculation
below, the only possible class is the sign-cyclic `h` direction.

## 7. The prime-two cyclic direction also vanishes

Now take `p=2`, so `O_chi=Z_-`.  Put

`c=diag(-1,1,-1,1,1,...)`.                            `(RK22)`

There are two entries equal to `-1`, so `c` has determinant one.  It is a
constant elementary matrix in stable rank; hence

`c in B_2 intersect Gamma`.                           `(RK23)`

It is congruent to the identity modulo two.  Conjugation on the `(1,2)`
root gives

`c h c^(-1)=h^(-1)`.                                  `(RK24)`

Set `d=c h`.  Then

`c^2=d^2=1`, `h=c d`.                                 `(RK25)`

The subgroup `<c,h>` is the infinite dihedral group, written as the free
product of the reflection subgroups `<c>` and `<d>`.  The sign character
is

`chi(c)=+1`, `chi(d)=-1`.                             `(RK26)`

Consider the two mod-two square cycles

`u_c=[c|c]`, `u_d=[d|d]`.                             `(RK27)`

Their Bocksteins are

`beta_1(u_c)=[c]`, `beta_chi(u_c)=[c]`,
`beta_1(u_d)=[d]`, `beta_chi(u_d)=0`.                 `(RK28)`

The last equality is already true on integral chains:

`partial_chi[d|d]=-[d]+[d]=0`.                        `(RK29)`

Thus `u_d in ker(beta_chi)`.  On the other hand, `u_c` belongs to the
identity-stabilizer image in `J_2` by `(RK23)`.  In ordinary first homology,
`(RK25)` gives

`[h]=[c]+[d]`
`    =beta_1(u_c)+beta_1(u_d)`
`    in beta_1(J_2)+beta_1(ker(beta_chi)).`            `(RK30)`

Theorem 2 says that the class of `[h]` spans the only possible quotient in
`(RK21)`.  Equation `(RK30)` kills it.  We have proved:

**Theorem 3 (complete prime-two base-row vanishing).**  In stable rank,

`H_2(B_2,F_2) /`
` (ker(beta_+)+ker(beta_-)+J_2)=0`.                    `(RK31)`

This includes all congruence directions, not only the displayed dihedral
Jordan blocks or cyclic root powers.

## 8. The exact remaining odd-prime question

The one-block dihedral table gives strong evidence that the last direction
also vanishes: its pure cyclic square class has nonzero ordinary Bockstein
and zero sign Bockstein.  What remains to prove in the full group is the
odd-prime global version of that statement:

`the h-primary class belongs to`
` beta_1(ker(beta_chi))+beta_1(J_p).`                  `(RK32)`

If `(RK32)` holds, `(RK4)` proves complete vanishing of the base-row
paired obstruction after polynomial stabilizers.  If it fails, Theorem 2
has isolated a unique candidate rather than an infinite family.  Its
failure must come from a derived correlation between the cyclic quotient
and the relative elementary kernel; no Laurent root, first-order Jordan
block, deeper cyclic root power, or relative `K_2` class can supply another
direction.
