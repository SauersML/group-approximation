# An integral cyclic radical module and its small-phase endpoint

Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Purpose

The parity module in `FALSE_MACKEY_SPLIT_COCYCLE.md` has a fixed central
involution.  This note gives an integral analogue with an infinite cyclic
central radical.  Its central character can therefore be taken to be
`exp(i epsilon)` with `epsilon -> 0`, and a tensor power can accumulate that
small phase to a fixed nontrivial scalar.

The construction is canonical and purely algebraic.  It does **not** by
itself construct the required matrix maps.  Its gain over the parity model is
an exact first-order target: defects which are `o(epsilon)` suffice, rather
than a fixed antipodal sector.

## 2. The universal integral module

Let `G` be a countable group and let `s in G` have infinite order.  Put

`R=Z[G]`, `I=ker(augmentation:R->Z)`,

and regard `R` as a left `Z[G]`-module.  Define

`A_Z=R/I(s-1)`,                                           `(ICR1)`

where `I(s-1)={q(s-1):q in I}`.  Let `v` be the class of `1` and put

`z=(s-1)v`.                                               `(ICR2)`

**Theorem 1 (universal integral cyclic module).**  There is an exact
nonsplit sequence of `Z[G]`-modules

`0 -> Z z -> A_Z -> Z[G/<s>] -> 0`.                      `(ICR3)`

The element `z` has infinite order and is fixed by `G`.

**Proof.**  Right multiplication

`mu:R->R(s-1)`, `q |-> q(s-1)`,                          `(ICR4)`

is injective.  To see this, decompose a finitely supported coefficient
function on `G` over the right cosets of `<s>`.  On each coset, `(ICR4)` is
multiplication by `t-1` on a finitely supported Laurent polynomial.  The
coefficient recursion for `q(t-1)=0` makes all coefficients on that coset
equal, and finite support makes them zero.

Consequently

`R(s-1)/I(s-1) ~= R/I ~= Z`.                             `(ICR5)`

Its generator is the class of `s-1`, namely `z`.  Quotienting `(ICR1)` by
this subgroup gives

`A_Z/Zz ~= R/R(s-1) ~= Z[G/<s>]`,                       `(ICR6)`

because `R/R(s-1)` identifies `g` with `gs`.  This proves exactness and the
infinite order of `z`.  For every `g in G`,

`(g-1)z=(g-1)(s-1)v=0`                                  `(ICR7)`

by the definition of `(ICR1)`, so `z` is fixed.

It remains to prove nonsplitting.  Every `G`-equivariant homomorphism
`F:R->Z`, with trivial action on the target, has the form

`F(q)=c augmentation(q)`                                `(ICR8)`

for `c=F(1)`.  Therefore every `G`-equivariant map `A_Z->Z` sends `z` to
zero.  A splitting of `(ICR3)` would give a `G`-equivariant retraction
`A_Z->Zz ~= Z` which sends `z` to `1`, contradicting `(ICR8)`.  End proof.

The same proof works over any nonzero commutative coefficient ring `k`, with
`k[G]/I(s-1)` in place of `(ICR1)`.  The integral case is singled out because
its central character has a continuous small-phase parameter.

There is a direct group-theoretic description.  Let

`W=Z wr G=Z^(G) semidirect G`,                          `(ICR8a)`

let `t_0` be the lamp at the identity, and put

`z_0=[s,t_0]`.                                         `(ICR8b)`

**Corollary 1A (minimal centralization quotient).**  The group `(ICR9)`
below is canonically

`E_Z ~= W / normalClosure_W({[g,z_0]:g in G})`.        `(ICR8c)`

Equivalently, `E_Z` is the universal quotient of the integral restricted
wreath product in which `[s,t_0]` is central.

**Proof.**  The lamp module of `W` is the left regular module `R`, with
`t_0` corresponding to `1`.  The element `z_0` corresponds, up to the fixed
commutator convention, to `(s-1)`.  Conjugating it by `g` and dividing by
it adds the module relation

`(g-1)(s-1)=0`.                                        `(ICR8d)`

The `G`-submodule generated by all relations `(ICR8d)` is exactly
`I(s-1)`, because the augmentation ideal is generated by the elements
`g-1`.  Quotienting the lamp module therefore gives `(ICR1)`, and no other
relations are introduced.  End proof.

## 3. A central infinite-order element in the sofic radical

Form

`E_Z=A_Z semidirect G`.                                  `(ICR9)`

Write `t` for the element of the additive normal subgroup defined by `v`.
With the commutator convention `[x,y]=xyx^(-1)y^(-1)`, one has

`z=[s,t]`.                                               `(ICR10)`

It is central by `(ICR7)`.

**Theorem 2 (integral radical lift).**  If

`s in Rad_sof(G)`,                                      `(ICR11)`

then

`1 != z in Rad_sof(E_Z)`,                               `(ICR12)`

and `z` has infinite order.  If `G` is finitely generated, then so is
`E_Z`.

**Proof.**  Let `Phi:E_Z->S` be a homomorphism to a sofic group.  Its
restriction to `G` kills `s` by `(ICR11)`.  Equation `(ICR10)` then gives
`Phi(z)=1`, proving `(ICR12)`.  Nontriviality and infinite order are Theorem
1.  Finally `A_Z` is cyclic as a `Z[G]`-module, generated by `v`, so a finite
generating set of `G`, together with `t`, generates `E_Z`.  End proof.

Thus every nonsoficity witness of infinite order canonically yields a
finitely generated nonsofic group with an infinite cyclic central subgroup
inside its full sofic radical.  This conclusion uses no approximation
property of `G`.

**Corollary 2A (explicit application).**  Let

`D=G_0 *_Gamma G_0`

be the banked Kun--Thom symmetric double, and choose the primitive fold word

`s=i_2(h)i_1(h)^(-1)`

from `FALSE_COINDUCED_PARITY_MODEL.md`.  That word is cyclically reduced of
length two, hence has infinite order, and belongs to `Rad_sof(D)`.  Applying
`(ICR1)--(ICR9)` gives an explicit finitely generated nonsofic group with a
central infinite cyclic subgroup contained in its sofic radical.

## 4. The integer Schreier multiplier

Put

`Y=G/<s>`                                               `(ICR13)`

and choose a section `tau:Y->G` with `tau(<s>)=1`.  Define the integer
Schreier cocycle by

`g tau(y)=tau(gy)s^(kappa(g,y))`.                       `(ICR14)`

Set

`e_y=tau(y)v in A_Z`.                                   `(ICR15)`

**Theorem 3 (Schreier basis and carry).**  The set

`{z} union {e_y:y in Y}`                                `(ICR16)`

is a `Z`-basis of `A_Z`, and

`g e_y=e_(gy)+kappa(g,y)z`.                             `(ICR17)`

Consequently, for

`Q_Z=Z^(Y) semidirect G=E_Z/<z>`,                       `(ICR18)`

the section which lifts the lamp at `y` to `e_y` has central multiplier

`omega((b,g),(c,h))=z^(L_g(c))`,                        `(ICR19)`

where

`L_g(c)=sum_(y in Y)c_y kappa(g,y) in Z`.               `(ICR20)`

**Proof.**  The quotient map in `(ICR3)` sends `e_y` to the standard basis
vector `delta_y`.  The only remaining kernel coordinate is the infinite
cyclic group generated by `z`, so `(ICR16)` is a basis.

Since `sv=v+z` and `z` is fixed, induction in both directions gives

`s^n v=v+nz` for every `n in Z`.                        `(ICR21)`

Equations `(ICR14)--(ICR15)` now give `(ICR17)`.  For a finite lamp `c`,
write `c_tilde=sum_y c_y e_y`.  Then `(ICR17)` gives

`g c_tilde=(g c)_tilde+L_g(c)z`.                        `(ICR22)`

Multiplication in the semidirect product proves `(ICR19)`.  End proof.

For a central character `z |-> exp(i theta)`, `(ICR19)` becomes the scalar
multiplier

`omega_theta((b,g),(c,h))=exp(i theta L_g(c))`.         `(ICR23)`

This is the continuously tunable version of the parity multiplier in
`FALSE_MACKEY_SPLIT_COCYCLE.md`.

## 5. One-unitary criterion

The multiplier `(ICR23)` again has a one-coordinate formulation.  All norms
below are normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norms.

**Theorem 4 (small-phase one-unitary criterion).**  Let `epsilon_n>0` tend
to zero.  Suppose there are dimensions `d_n`, maps

`U_n:G->U(d_n)`                                         `(ICR24)`

and unitaries `T_n in U(d_n)` such that, for every fixed displayed group
variable,

`||U_n(g)U_n(h)-U_n(gh)||_2=o(epsilon_n)`,              `(ICR25)`

`||U_n(s)T_nU_n(s)^*-exp(i epsilon_n)T_n||_2`
`  =o(epsilon_n)`,                                      `(ICR26)`

and

`||[U_n(g)T_nU_n(g)^*,U_n(h)T_nU_n(h)^*]||_2`
`  =o(epsilon_n)`.                                      `(ICR27)`

Then there is a hyperlinear nonsofic group.

**Proof.**  For the section in Theorem 3, define

`T_(n,y)=U_n(tau(y))T_nU_n(tau(y))^*`.                 `(ICR28)`

For every fixed integer `k`, `(ICR26)` gives

`U_n(s)^kT_nU_n(s)^(-k)`
`  =exp(i k epsilon_n)T_n+o(epsilon_n)`                `(ICR29)`

in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  The error remains
`o(epsilon_n)` because `k` is fixed.  Combining `(ICR14)`, `(ICR25)`, and
`(ICR29)` yields

`U_n(g)T_(n,y)U_n(g)^*`
` =exp(i epsilon_n kappa(g,y))T_(n,gy)+o(epsilon_n)`.
                                                               `(ICR30)`

For a finite lamp `b`, put

`W_n(b,g)=product_y T_(n,y)^(b_y) U_n(g)`.             `(ICR31)`

Negative lamp powers cause no problem because all factors are unitary.
For each fixed pair of finite lamps, only finitely many commutations are
used, so `(ICR27)` and `(ICR30)` show that `(ICR31)` has multiplier
`(ICR23)` with defect `o(epsilon_n)`.

Lift `(ICR31)` through `E_Z->Q_Z` and send `z` to
`exp(i epsilon_n)`.  This gives maps

`Phi_n:E_Z->U(d_n)`                                     `(ICR32)`

whose pointwise multiplicative defects are `o(epsilon_n)` and for which

`Phi_n(z)=exp(i epsilon_n)1`.                           `(ICR33)`

Fix `theta in (0,2 pi)` and put

`m_n=floor(theta/epsilon_n)`,
`Psi_n(x)=Phi_n(x)^(tensor m_n)`.                       `(ICR34)`

The normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt tensor telescoping inequality gives

`||Psi_n(x)Psi_n(y)-Psi_n(xy)||_2`
` <=m_n o(epsilon_n)=o(1)`.                            `(ICR35)`

Moreover

`Psi_n(z)=exp(i m_n epsilon_n)1 -> exp(i theta)1`.     `(ICR36)`

Thus `(ICR34)` induces a homomorphism from `E_Z` to a tracial matrix
ultraproduct whose image `L` is hyperlinear and retains `z`.  If `L` were
sofic, the quotient homomorphism `E_Z->L` would kill `z` by Theorem 2,
contradicting `(ICR36)`.  Hence `L` is hyperlinear and nonsofic.  End proof.

No injectivity or separation hypothesis on the maps `(ICR24)` is required.
The only strict datum is the first-order central curvature `(ICR26)`.

## 6. Exact infinite-dimensional model and remaining gate

For every real `theta`, coinduce the rotation action of `<s>` on the circle

`s dot w=exp(i theta)w`                                 `(ICR37)`

to `G`.  On the compact Haar probability space

`X_theta=T^Y`,                                         `(ICR38)`

the resulting action is

`(g dot x)_(gy)=exp(i theta kappa(g,y))x_y`.           `(ICR39)`

In

`L_infinity(X_theta) crossed G`,                       `(ICR40)`

let `T(x)=x_(<s>)`.  The canonical group unitaries and `T` satisfy

`u_s T u_s^*=exp(i theta)T`,                           `(ICR41)`

and all conjugates `u_gTu_g^*` commute.  Hence `(ICR40)` realizes the
multiplier `(ICR23)` exactly.

The construction therefore has no algebraic or invariant-measure gap.  The
remaining question is finite-dimensional and quantitative:

> Can the exact family `(ICR40)` be approximated at phases
> `epsilon_n -> 0` with every fixed relation defect `o(epsilon_n)`?

An `O(epsilon_n^2)` construction would solve the main FALSE problem by
Theorem 4.  An `Omega(epsilon_n)` floor would close this small-phase route in
the same way that the fixed-sign matching theorem closes exact RF vertex
gluing.  Ordinary finite equivariant quotients give the latter, linear
floor: they kill `s`, so `(ICR26)` has defect
`|1-exp(i epsilon_n)|=epsilon_n+O(epsilon_n^3)`.

This endpoint is related to, but algebraically smaller than, the doubled
integral Heisenberg cover in the shared notebook.  It needs one cyclic lamp
coordinate and one integer Schreier carry rather than a symplectic pair.  It
does not evade the non-Cartan approximation problem; it isolates its
first-order derivative in the smallest available central-radical module.

## 7. Honest Kazhdan vertex models have a linear phase floor

For the Kun--Thom symmetric-double realization, the required `o(epsilon)`
data cannot be obtained by gluing honest finite-dimensional representations
of the two residually finite vertices.  The fixed-sign estimate in
`FALSE_RF_VERTEX_HS_MATCHING_NO_GO.md` has the following small-phase form.

Let `Gamma<G_0` have Kazhdan constant `kappa` for a finite set `S`, let

`h=t^(-1) gamma t notin Gamma`,

and let

`alpha_i:G_0->U(d)`, `i=1,2`,                          `(ICR42)`

be honest finite-dimensional representations.  Put

`S_h=alpha_2(h)alpha_1(h)^(-1)`,
`delta=max_(a in S)||alpha_1(a)-alpha_2(a)||_2`.        `(ICR43)`

The finite-representation matching theorem gives

`||S_h-1||_2<=(2/kappa)delta`.                          `(ICR44)`

**Proposition 5 (linear central-curvature floor).**  For every unitary `V`,
every real `epsilon`, and

`eta=||exp(i epsilon)1-[V,S_h]||_2`,                   `(ICR45)`

one has

`|exp(i epsilon)-1|<=eta+(4/kappa)delta`.              `(ICR46)`

Consequently, if `epsilon_n->0`, it is impossible to have simultaneously

`eta_n=o(epsilon_n)`, `delta_n=o(epsilon_n)`            `(ICR47)`

using honest vertex representations.

**Proof.**  For arbitrary unitaries `V,S`,

`||[V,S]-1||_2<=2||S-1||_2`.                           `(ICR48)`

The triangle inequality, `(ICR44)`, and `(ICR48)` give `(ICR46)`.  Since
`|exp(i epsilon)-1|/|epsilon|->1`, equations `(ICR46)--(ICR47)` are
incompatible.  End proof.

Thus a cost-one or forest repair of the circle phase on the common
`Gamma`-orbit, by itself, cannot close Theorem 4.  Such a repair treats the
edge relation.  Proposition 5 shows that attaching it to honest vertex
representations leaves a first-order Kazhdan mismatch.  Any successful
small-phase construction must already be genuinely nonliftable in at least
one vertex sector, with a first-order term which cancels that mismatch and a
remaining curvature of order `o(epsilon)`.
