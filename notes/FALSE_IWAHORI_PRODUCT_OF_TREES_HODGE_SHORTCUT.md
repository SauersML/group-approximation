# The Iwahori complex is not a product-of-trees quotient

Date: 2026-08-14

## 1. Outcome

The cosystolic-angle gate in
`TRUE_IWAHORI_FACTOR_GATE_IS_COSYSTOLIC_ANGLE.md` cannot be closed by
treating

```text
G=SL_2(Z[1/2])=SL_2(Z) *_(B_+ ~= B_-) SL_2(Z)          (PTH1)
```

as a cocompact lattice in a product of locally finite trees and then
applying the ordinary cubical Hodge theory of such a quotient.  The group
`G` contains an embedded `BS(1,4)` whose cyclic base subgroup is
exponentially distorted.  Hence `G` is not a CAT(0) group.  In particular,
it cannot act properly and cocompactly on a finite product of locally finite
trees.

This does **not** invalidate the finite two-dimensional classifying complex
used for the Euler-characteristic and Lueck-approximation arguments.  It
identifies its geometry more accurately: the Bass--Serre direction carries
fourth-power holonomy.  Its universal cover is a warped tree of vertex
trees, not a direct product with uniformly isometric attaching maps.

The remaining spectral target is therefore a cosystolic estimate for this
`BS(1,4)` mapping-telescope geometry.  A Ramanujan or upper-Hodge theorem
for ordinary product-of-trees quotients does not supply that estimate.

## 2. An explicit embedded `BS(1,4)`

For `r in Z[1/4]`, put

```text
U(r)=[[1,r],[0,1]],
C   =[[2,0],[0,1/2]].                                  (PTH2)
```

Both matrices lie in `G`, and direct multiplication gives

```text
C U(r) C^(-1)=U(4r).                                   (PTH3)
```

Let `X=U(1)`.  Equation `(PTH3)` gives a homomorphism

```text
BS(1,4)=<c,x | c x c^(-1)=x^4>
        -> G,
c |-> C,
x |-> X.                                               (PTH4)
```

It is injective.  Indeed, the subgroup generated by `C` and `X` consists of
the matrices

```text
U(r) C^k,             r in Z[1/4], k in Z.             (PTH5)
```

The multiplication rule is the semidirect-product rule for
`Z[1/4] semidirect Z`, with the generator of `Z` acting by multiplication by
`4`.  Moreover, the diagonal entries of `U(r)C^k` recover `k`, and then its
upper-right entry recovers `r`.  Thus `(PTH5)` is a unique normal form, and
the image in `(PTH4)` is exactly

```text
Z[1/4] semidirect_(times 4) Z ~= BS(1,4).              (PTH6)
```

This is the same fourth-power dynamical subgroup isolated in
`TRUE_IWAHORI_MATCHING_REDUCES_TO_FACTORIZED_BS14_REPAIR.md`; it is not an
auxiliary obstruction introduced by the present argument.

## 3. The base cyclic group is exponentially distorted

For every `n>=0`, `(PTH3)` gives

```text
X^(4^n)=C^n X C^(-n).                                  (PTH7)
```

Therefore, in the word metric of `G` associated to any finite generating
set containing `C` and `X`,

```text
|X^(4^n)|_G<=2n+1.                                    (PTH8)
```

Its intrinsic word length in the infinite cyclic group `<X>` is `4^n`.
Hence `<X>` is exponentially distorted in `G`.

The conclusion is independent of the selected finite generating set,
because word metrics from two finite generating sets are bi-Lipschitz up to
a fixed multiplicative constant.

## 4. Why this forbids a geometric CAT(0) action

Suppose a finitely generated group `Lambda` acts properly and cocompactly by
isometries on a proper CAT(0) space `Y`.  The orbit map is a quasi-isometry.
Every element of `Lambda` acts semisimply on `Y`.  An infinite-order element
cannot be elliptic: properness makes point stabilizers finite.  It is
therefore hyperbolic and has positive translation length

```text
ell(g)=inf_y d(y,gy)>0.                                (PTH9)
```

Consequently

```text
d(y,g^m y)>=m ell(g),                                  (PTH10)
```

and the quasi-isometric orbit map gives a linear lower bound on
`|g^m|_Lambda`.  Every infinite cyclic subgroup of a CAT(0) group is thus
undistorted.

Applying this to `X in G` contradicts `(PTH8)`.  We obtain

```text
G is not a CAT(0) group.                               (PTH11)
```

A finite product of locally finite trees, equipped with its standard
piecewise Euclidean metric, is a proper CAT(0) space.  Hence `(PTH11)` rules
out a proper cocompact action of `G` on any such product.

## 5. What survives from the Hodge strategy

Jordan--Livne develop commuting partial Laplacians and Ramanujan local
systems for finite cubical complexes uniformized by products of regular
trees.  Those hypotheses encode isometric, nonexpanding transport in each
tree direction.  They do not hold for `(PTH1)`: traversing the Bass--Serre
edge conjugates `U(r)` to `U(4r)`.

The graph-of-spaces model for a torsion-free finite-index subgroup of `G`
is still finite and two-dimensional.  It remains sufficient for

- the Euler-characteristic calculation;
- the positive second `L^2`-Betti number;
- Lueck approximation along residual finite towers; and
- the Mayer--Vietoris identification of the factor gate.

What it does not provide is a regular cubical product decomposition.  The
correct analytic complex must retain the expanding transport

```text
r |-> 4r                                                (PTH12)
```

and its inverse contraction.  This suggests a weighted or anisotropic
upper Laplacian on a `BS(1,4)` mapping telescope.  The inverse factor `1/4`
is compatible with the odd-spectral-shadow phenomenon already isolated in
the finite-dimensional model, but no uniform cosystolic estimate is proved
here.

Accordingly, the live endpoint is now narrower:

```text
prove a harmonic-orthogonality and positive-singular-gap theorem
for the warped Iwahori/BS(1,4) telescope, not for an ordinary
product-of-trees cube complex.                         (PTH13)
```
