# Projective carry denominator collapse is exactly a harmonic dual-systole problem

Date: 2026-08-11

## Purpose

The projective stabilizer calculations at `p=3,5,7` give

`H_2(H_3,Z) ~= H_2(H_5,Z) ~= C_2^3`,
`H_2(H_7,Z) ~= C_2^4`,
`H_2(H_p,R)=0` for `p=3,5,7`,                         `(PHS1)`

while cuspidal cohomology proves that `H_2(H_p,R)` is nonzero at other
prime levels.  This note isolates exactly what the latter fact can and
cannot do to the carry constant.  The relevant invariant is not the
dimension or torsion of stabilizer homology by itself.  It is the first
systole of the dual of the integral homology lattice equipped with the
quotient Euclidean metric inherited from the presentation complex.

## Lattices in one finite chart

Use the notation of `INTEGRAL_CARRY_DISCRIMINANT_DUALITY.md`.  In one
finite projective chart let

`K=ker(d1^T) intersect Z^cells`                        `(PHS2)`

be the primitive integral cycle lattice.  Let `Sigma subset K` be the
spherical subgroup generated by the uniformly bounded lifted spherical
templates, and put

`S=span_R(Sigma)`, `H=K_R intersect S^perp`.           `(PHS3)`

Write `pi_H:K_R->H` for orthogonal projection and define

`Q=pi_H(K) subset H`.                                 `(PHS4)`

The subspace `S` is rational, so `Q` is a Euclidean lattice.  Its abstract
free abelian group is naturally

`Q ~= K/(K intersect S)`
`  ~= H_2(H_p,Z)/torsion`,                            `(PHS5)`

where the second isomorphism is the cover Hopf quotient.  Equation `(PHS4)`
is important: it equips the free stabilizer homology with the quotient norm
actually seen by the carry problem, rather than an arbitrary word or
cellular norm.

Let

`Q^#={u in H:<u,Q> subset Z}`                         `(PHS6)`

and define the dangerous harmonic dual systole

`delta_H=min({||u||:u in Q^# minus K})`,              `(PHS7)`

with `delta_H=+infinity` if the displayed set is empty.  In `(PHS7)`, `K`
means its given embedding in the ambient real cycle space; thus a vector of
`Q^#` which is already an integral cycle represents the zero discriminant
class and is harmless.

**Lemma 1 (dual identification).**

`Q^#=K^# intersect H`.                                `(PHS8)`

**Proof.**  For `u in H` and `k in K`, orthogonality to `S` gives

`<u,k>=<u,pi_H k>`.                                   `(PHS9)`

Thus `<u,K> subset Z` if and only if `<u,Q> subset Z`, which is exactly
`(PHS8)`.  End proof.

## Exact localization theorem

Let `B` be the maximum norm of the finite spherical templates, as in
Proposition 12 of `INTEGRAL_CARRY_DISCRIMINANT_DUALITY.md`.  For a nonzero
class `c in D_K=K^#/K`, let `rho_K(c)` be its shortest-coset radius.

**Theorem 2 (harmonic systole criterion).**  Every nonzero discriminant
class satisfies

`rho_K(c)>=min(1/B,delta_H)`.                          `(PHS10)`

Moreover, along any family of projective charts,

`inf_(0!=c in D_K) rho_K(c) -> 0`

if and only if

`delta_H -> 0`                                       `(PHS11)`

along a subsequence.  Whenever `(PHS11)` occurs, the carry constants obey

`Theta_p >= 1/delta_H -> infinity`.                  `(PHS12)`

**Proof.**  Choose a shortest representative `u in K^#` of a nonzero class
`c`.  If `||u||>=1/B`, the first term on the right of `(PHS10)` applies.
If `||u||<1/B`, Proposition 12 forces `u` to be orthogonal to `Sigma`, hence
`u in H`.  Lemma 1 gives `u in Q^#`; and `u notin K` because `c` is
nonzero.  Therefore `||u||>=delta_H`, proving `(PHS10)`.

If discriminant radii tend to zero, eventually they are less than `1/B`,
so the same argument produces vectors counted by `(PHS7)` and forces
`delta_H` to zero.  Conversely, any `u in Q^# minus K` belongs to `K^#` by
Lemma 1 and represents a nonzero discriminant class of radius at most
`||u||`.  This proves `(PHS11)`.  Finally, Proposition 1 of the discriminant
note supplies an integer carry whose cycle projection is `u`; its distance
from the saturated range lattice is at least one.  Dividing by `||u||`
gives `(PHS12)`.  End proof.

When `H_2(H_p,R)=0`, the lattice `Q` has rank zero and
`delta_H=+infinity`, recovering the `p=3,5,7` denominator bound.  At a
cuspidal level such as `p=53`, nonzero real homology merely creates a
positive-rank harmonic sector.  By itself it does not say whether `(PHS7)`
contains a nonintegral vector or whether `delta_H` is small.

The compact non-free cellular computation at `p=53` now answers the first
of those two questions in its own free signed-coinvariant lattice: the
harmonic quotient is cyclic of order `q` and

`delta_H^2=a/q=0.0713191090...`,
`delta_H=0.2670563781...`.                            `(PHS12a)`

Thus a genuine nonintegral class lies below the spherical threshold.  Its
minimum ambient integral lift has squared norm exactly four, so its exact
paired contribution is

`2sqrt(q/a)=7.4890553601...`.                         `(PHS12b)`

Hence `Theta>=7.4890553601...` in that compact metric.  This is one
fixed-level certificate, not a family-level collapse.  Transferring it to
the original HAP carry metric still requires the bounded integral comparison
and the two-primary stabilizer correction described in
`TRUE_P53_CELLULAR_HARMONIC_DISCRIMINANT.md`.

The harmonic numerator is also bounded in the other direction.  Exact
integral lifts of a Gauss-reduced basis have lift Gram matrix
`diag(4,16)`, and the largest generalized squared singular value against
the harmonic Gram matrix is `56.10275777902312...`.  Thus the compact
harmonic decoder has a section of norm

`7.49017741972933...`,                                      `(PHS12c)`

and its optimal section constant satisfies the near-sharp bracket

`7.489055360091924...<=kappa_Sigma`
` <=7.49017741972933...`.                                  `(PHS12d)`

This upper bound is for the free harmonic sector.  It does not yet include
the bounded spherical templates, the omitted two-primary stabilizer
coordinates, or the norm loss in comparison with the free HAP carry metric.
Those qualifications are essential before reading `(PHS12d)` as an upper
bound for the original full-chart constant.

At the next cuspidal prime, `p=61`, the compact harmonic rank is again two,
but

`delta_H=0.3532226522900641...`.                              `(PHS12e)`

This is larger than the level-`53` systole and lies above its own spherical
threshold `1/sqrt(28)`.  Exact integral lifts give a harmonic section of
norm `11.793652354043665...`.  Hence the second cusp exhibits neither
harmonic denominator collapse nor a huge lift forced by its `114`-digit
cyclic discriminant.  The constant is larger than at `p=53`, so these two
points are evidence, not a family bound.

## A regulator criterion for divergence

Suppose `Q` has rank `r>0`, and let `Reg(Q)` denote its Euclidean
covolume in `H`.  Let `v_r` be the volume of the unit ball in `R^r`.
Since `Reg(Q^#)=Reg(Q)^(-1)`, Minkowski's convex body theorem gives

`lambda_1(Q^#)<=2(v_r Reg(Q))^(-1/r)`.                `(PHS13)`

If the right side is less than one, its realizing nonzero vector cannot be
a nonzero element of the ambient integer lattice `K`, because every such
vector has Euclidean norm at least one.  Hence it is counted by `(PHS7)`.

**Corollary 3 (regulator-growth obstruction).**  Along any sequence with
positive harmonic rank, if

`2(v_r Reg(Q))^(-1/r) -> 0`,                          `(PHS14)`

then `delta_H->0` and `Theta_p->infinity`.  Equivalently, using
`v_r^(-1/r) asymp sqrt(r)`, it is sufficient that

`Reg(Q)^(1/r)/sqrt(r) -> infinity`.                  `(PHS15)`

**Proof.**  Apply `(PHS13)`.  Once the upper bound is less than one, the
short vector lies in `Q^# minus K`; then Theorem 2 applies.  End proof.

This is a one-sided criterion.  Failure of `(PHS14)` does not prove bounded
carry transversality, and a uniform positive lower bound on `delta_H`
controls only the denominator in the exact discriminant formula

`Theta^2=max_(0!=c)(1+rho_L(gamma c)^2/rho_K(c)^2)`. `(PHS16)`

The glued range radii `rho_L(gamma c)` remain the independent numerator
problem.

## Consequences for the next exact computations

The exact groups `C_2^3,C_2^3,C_2^4` at `p=3,5,7` do not expose the harmonic
metric because their free quotients are zero.  Their changing two-torsion
is useful validation that the computation is detecting real arithmetic,
but it cannot test Theorem 2.  The first literature-certified prime with
nonzero cuspidal degree-three cohomology is `p=53`, hence it is the first
decisive projective chart for this purpose.

For `p=53`, an exact run should export more than the abstract group:

1. an integral basis of `K` and generators of `Sigma`;
2. the Gram matrix of `Q=pi_H(K)` (rational coordinates suffice);
3. `r`, `Reg(Q)`, and the shortest vectors of `Q^#`;
4. whether the shortest dual vector is nonintegral in the ambient cell
   lattice;
5. the paired glued class in `L^#/L`, since even a safe harmonic systole
   does not bound the numerator in `(PHS16)`.

This replaces the false target “prove `H_2(H_p,Z)=C_2^3` uniformly” by two
precise metric targets: bound the harmonic dual systole from below and
bound the paired range/cycle discriminant-radius ratio from above.

The exact coupling of these two targets is now given in
`TRUE_NONLINEAR_DISCRIMINANT_REPAIR_FORMULA.md`.  In particular,
`Q^#=H intersect (Z^m+W)`, the quotient
`Q^#/(H intersect Z^m)` injects into the paired range discriminant, and all
classes below the spherical threshold are computed by this harmonic
quotient.  At `p=53` this replaces a full integral cycle-kernel computation

The paired numerator has now also been absorbed into the harmonic lattice
