# Exact relative absorption does not rule out an asymptotic branch cut

Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Correction and outcome

The exact relative calculation in
`FALSE_NATURAL_FINITE_CLOCK_RELATIVE_ABSORPTION.md` is valid: in every
natural finite coefficient-ring image, the positive subgroup's homology
surjects onto the full clock coordinate

\[
C_k\cong \mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z.
\]

What does **not** follow is that this tower is sterile for the asymptotic
phase-splitting criterion.  Exact relative splitting asks a character to be
identically one on the whole positive finite image.  Hyperlinear gluing asks
only that its defect tend to zero on each fixed positive word.  The element
of the positive finite image which equals the Laurent fold may have word
length tending to infinity, and a microscopic phase on the fixed local
coordinates can accumulate to a macroscopic phase on that collision.

The finite clock is exactly at this scale.  Thus the natural tower remains a
candidate for an **asymptotic branch-cut** construction, although it cannot
give an exactly split relative Schur class.

## 2. The elementary separation

Let

\[
C_k=\langle \kappa_k\rangle\cong \mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\chi_k(\kappa_k)=\exp(2\pi i/p^k).
\]

The subgroup generated by \(\kappa_k\) is all of \(C_k\), so quotienting by
that subgroup kills the clock class exactly.  Nevertheless

\[
|\chi_k(\kappa_k)-1|=O(p^{-k}),
\]

while for the order-\(p\) Bockstein shadow

\[
b_k=p^{k-1}\kappa_k
\]

one has

\[
|\chi_k(b_k)-1|=|\exp(2\pi i/p)-1|>0.
\]

Hence exact generation of the clock group by positive-side classes is
compatible with vanishing phase on every bounded clock coordinate and a
fixed phase on a growing positive combination.

More generally, suppose the finitely many tested vertex and edge relators
pair with \(\chi_k\) through centered integers

\[
m_{1,k},\ldots ,m_{r,k}\in[-p^k/2,p^k/2].
\]

If

\[
\sum_{j=1}^r m_{j,k}^2=o(p^{2k}),
\]

then all their squared chordal defects tend to zero, because

\[
\sum_j|\exp(2\pi i m_{j,k}/p^k)-1|^2
\leq \frac{4\pi^2}{p^{2k}}\sum_jm_{j,k}^2.
\]

At the same time, a marked coordinate equal to a \(p\)-adic unit times
\(p^{k-1}\) retains a nontrivial \(p\)-th-root phase.  No contradiction is
created by the fact that the local coordinates generate \(C_k\) as an
abstract group: the coefficient needed to reach the marked element grows
with \(k\).

## 3. Application to the natural finite clock

For the metacyclic clock module, the proved map

\[
H_1(\langle h\rangle,J_k)\longrightarrow H_1(B_k,J_k)
\longrightarrow C_k
\]

is the quotient

\[
J_k\longrightarrow J_k/(c-(1+p))J_k\cong C_k
\]

and is surjective.  This proves failure of exact relative splitting.  It
does not determine the centered clock coordinates of the **fixed**
presentation relators after Shapiro induction and a choice of Schreier
gauge.

Those coordinates are explicit.  First rescale the local crossed
homomorphism \(z_k\) from `FALSE_MIXED_LHS_COCYCLE_FORMULA.md` by the inverse
unit that sends the distinguished augmentation coordinate to
`1 mod p^k`.  For the resulting \(z'_k\), a Schreier transition

\[
\beta_\tau(x,s)=h^{a_\tau(x,s)}c^{t_\tau(x,s)}
\]

contributes the phase

\[
\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i a_\tau(x,s)}{p^k}\right)
\]

on the distinguished augmentation vector.  Exact absorption says that the
collection of all positive transitions generates the clock coordinate.  The
asymptotic question is instead whether one can choose the transversal (or,
equivalently, the scalar gauge) so that the transitions attached to every
fixed presentation coordinate have centered exponents \(o(p^k)\), while the
growing collision representing the fold has exponent a unit times
\(p^{k-1}\).

This normalization is essential.  A modular inverse such as
`q^(-1) mod p^k` need not have a bounded standard representative, so the raw
unscaled character does not itself justify a small-chord estimate.

This is precisely the long-relator phase localization allowed by the
phase-splitting criterion: local curvature tends to one, but the branch cut
has nontrivial holonomy.

## 4. Correct finite computation

Do not compute larger Schur multipliers merely to ask whether the natural
relative quotient is nonzero; it is zero by theorem.  Compute the **metric
restriction profile** instead:

1. construct the explicit Shapiro cocycle induced from \(z_k\);
2. evaluate its centered clock exponent on the fixed covariance and edge
   relators;
3. minimize their quadratic chordal energy over Schreier transversals, or
   over the equivalent finite scalar gauge variables;
4. evaluate the same gauge on the growing positive word whose finite image
   equals the marked Laurent element; and
5. test the little-oh bound above together with the order-\(p\) marked
   phase.

For an orbit-sum model, step 3 is the normalized average of the squared
transition phases.  For a single central-character sector, it is the finite
list of scalar relator pairings.  These are different optimization problems
and should not be conflated.

A positive metric profile, together with the already proved Bockstein
transport and the phase-splitting endpoint, proves FALSE.  A dimension-free
lower bound would close the natural tower for the correct reason: quantitative
relative cocycle stability, not exact relative absorption.

## 5. Scope

This note does not prove that the metric profile tends to zero.  It corrects
the inference from an exact relative quotient to an asymptotic
Hilbert--Schmidt obstruction.  The finite ring tower is excluded only from
the exact-splitting route; its growing-word branch-cut route remains open and
is now an explicit modular gauge computation.

The first exact HAP restriction-map calculation is recorded in
`experiments/split_clock_relative_h2_k1_results.md`.  At `p=3`, `k=1`, the
ambient multiplier is `C_3 directSum C_3 directSum C_9`, while the natural
translation-positive subgroup has image `C_3 directSum C_9` and cokernel
`C_3`.  This independently verifies exact absorption of a growing-order
direction without supplying a quantitative fixed-window obstruction.
