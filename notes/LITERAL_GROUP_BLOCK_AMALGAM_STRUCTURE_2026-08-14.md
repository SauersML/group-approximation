## 2.  The presentation without the centrality relators

Let

    Ê = ⟨v₁,v₂,v₃,x,y,z,t,c | R_base, R_stable, c², R_lamp⟩

be `E` with the eight relators `R_central` deleted, so that `E = Ê / K` with

    K = ⟨⟨ [w,g] : g ∈ {v₁,v₂,v₃,x,y,z,t,c} ⟩⟩_Ê .          (2.1)

**Lemma 2.1.**  `Ê ≅ V ∗_B (B × ℤ/2)`, where the second factor is generated by
`B` and the involution `c`.

*Proof.*  The subpresentation on `v₁,v₂,v₃,x,y,z,t` with relators
`R_base ∪ R_stable` is the standard ascending HNN presentation
`⟨B, t | t g t⁻¹ = α(g), g ∈ gens(B)⟩`; the conjugation relations hold for the
six generators, hence for all of `B`.  By (I1)--(I4a) this group is `V`, and
`B ≤ V`.  Adjoining one generator `c` with the relators `c²` and `[c,g]`,
`g ∈ gens(B)`, is by definition the presentation of the pushout of
`V ← B → B × ℤ/2`. ∎

Since `[c, B] = 1` and `c² = 1`, the assignment `c ↦ 1`, `V ↦ V` respects all
relators, so there is a retraction `π : Ê → V` splitting the inclusion of `V`.
Put `N = ker π = ⟨⟨c⟩⟩_Ê`, so `Ê = N ⋊ V`.

**Lemma 2.2.**  Let `X = V/B` and let `o = B ∈ X`.  For `g ∈ V` put
`c_{gB} := g c g⁻¹` (well defined because `B` centralises `c`).  Then

    N = ∗_{ξ ∈ X} ⟨c_ξ⟩ ≅ ∗_X ℤ/2 ,

and `V` acts on `N` by permuting the factors along its action on `X`.

*Proof.*  `Ê` acts on the Bass--Serre tree `𝒯` of Lemma 2.1 with vertex
stabilisers the conjugates of `V` and of `B × ℤ/2`, and edge stabilisers the
conjugates of `B`.  As `N ⊴ Ê`, `N ∩ gVg⁻¹ = g(N ∩ V)g⁻¹ = 1` and
`N ∩ g(B × ℤ/2)g⁻¹ = g⟨c⟩g⁻¹` of order 2, while `N ∩ gBg⁻¹ = 1`.  Thus `N`
acts on `𝒯` with trivial edge stabilisers, one orbit of `V`-type vertices
(`Ê = NV`), and `N\Ê/(B×ℤ/2) = V/B = X` orbits of the other type, likewise
`X` orbits of edges.  The quotient graph is a star with `1 + |X|` vertices and
`|X|` edges, hence a tree, so by (I4d) `N` is the free product of the vertex
groups `g⟨c⟩g⁻¹ ≅ ℤ/2`, `gB ∈ X`, with no free part. ∎

In this notation `d := tct⁻¹ = c_{τo}` and `v₁dv₁⁻¹ = c_{v₁τo}`, so

    w = [c_a , c_b] ,     a = τo ,  b = v₁τo .              (2.3)

`a ≠ b` is exactly the last assertion of `lem:linear` (I2), as the manuscript
notes after that lemma.

## 3.  Imposing centrality: `E = N_E ⋊ V`

Because `π(c) = 1` we have `π(w) = 1`, so `π(K) = 1` and `π` descends to a
retraction `E → V` splitting `V ≤ E`.  Also `w ∈ N` and `N ⊴ Ê`, so `K ≤ N`.
Writing `N_E := N/K = ker(E → V)`:

    E = N_E ⋊ V ,   K ≤ N = ∗_X ℤ/2 .                       (3.1)

**Lemma 3.1.**  `N_E` is the quotient of `N = ∗_X ℤ/2` by the smallest normal
subgroup making the element `w = [c_a,c_b]` central in `N` and fixed by `V`.
Equivalently, `N_E` is the universal group generated by involutions
`(c_ξ)_{ξ∈X}` subject to

    [c_{ga}, c_{gb}] = ζ   (g ∈ V),    ζ central,           (3.2)

where `ζ` denotes the common value, i.e. the image of `w`.

*Proof.*  `[w, gh] = [w,g] · g[w,h]g⁻¹`, so the normal closure of the eight
displayed commutators equals `⟨⟨[w,h] : h ∈ Ê⟩⟩`; thus `E = Ê/K` is the
largest quotient of `Ê` in which `w` is central.  Splitting `h = pg`
(`p ∈ N`, `g ∈ V`) gives generators `[w,p]` (`p ∈ N`) and
`w · ({}^g w)⁻¹` (`g ∈ V`) for `K` as a `V`-invariant normal subgroup of `N`.
The first family says `w` is central in `N_E`, the second that all the
conjugates `{}^g w = [c_{ga}, c_{gb}]` are equal to `w`.  Finally `ζ² = 1` is
a consequence: `c_a [c_a,c_b] c_a⁻¹ = [c_b,c_a] = [c_a,c_b]⁻¹`, which equals
`[c_a,c_b]` by centrality. ∎

So `N_E` is the "Clifford group of a graph": let `𝒢` be the graph on the
vertex set `X` whose edges are the `V`-orbit of the unordered pair `{a,b}`.
Then

    N_E ≅ C(𝒢) := ⟨ (c_ξ)_{ξ∈X}, ζ | c_ξ² = ζ² = 1, ζ central,
                     [c_ξ,c_η] = ζ  for {ξ,η} ∈ E(𝒢) ⟩ ,     (3.3)

and the map `E → W` of `prop:witness` restricts on `N_E` to the map
`C(𝒢) → C(complete graph on X) = ClLamp(X)` fixing every `c_ξ` and `ζ`.
(That `φ(c_ξ) = c_{φ(g)o}` for `ξ = gB` is immediate from `φ(c) = c_o`, and
`φ` is onto because `V` is transitive on `X` and `ζ = φ(w)`; this is the
surjectivity argument of the manuscript's proof of Theorem E.)

## 4.  The site set and the orbital graph

**Proposition 4.1.**  As a `V`-set,

    X = V/B ≅ ⨆_{n ∈ ℤ} ℤ[1/2]³ / 2ⁿℤ³ ,

the class of `t τⁿ B` (`t = (u,A) ∈ T`) being `(n, [u] mod 2ⁿℤ³)`.  The action
of `s τ^m` (`s = (u_s,A_s)`) sends `(n,[u])` to `(n+m, [u_s + A_s 2^m u])`.
In these coordinates `o = (0,[0])`, `a = τo = (1,[0])` and
`b = v₁τo = (1,[e₁])`, and `a ≠ b` because `e₁ ∉ 2ℤ³`.

*Proof.*  Every `v ∈ V` is uniquely `tτⁿ` (I3).  Projecting `V → V/T ≅ ℤ`
shows `tτⁿB = t'τ^{n'}B` forces `n = n'`, and then
`t⁻¹t' ∈ τⁿBτ⁻ⁿ = B_n := 2ⁿℤ³ ⋊ SL₃(ℤ)`.  Since `SL₃(ℤ) ≤ B_n` and
`A(2ⁿℤ³) = 2ⁿℤ³` for `A ∈ SL₃(ℤ)`, the map `(u,A)B_n ↦ [u] mod 2ⁿℤ³` is a
bijection `T/B_n → ℤ[1/2]³/2ⁿℤ³`.  The action formula is
`sτ^m · tτⁿB = s(τ^m t τ^{-m}) τ^{m+n}B` with `τ^m(u,A)τ^{-m} = (2^m u, A)`. ∎

In particular every level `ℤ[1/2]³/2ⁿℤ³ ≅ (ℤ(2^∞))³` is countably infinite,
so `X` is countably infinite.

**Proposition 4.2.**  The orbital graph `𝒢` of §3 is the disjoint union of
countably many copies of the complete graph `K₈`.  Explicitly, two sites are
adjacent iff they are distinct, lie in the same level `n`, and differ by an
element of `2^{n-1}ℤ³/2ⁿℤ³ ≅ (ℤ/2)³`.  Consequently `𝒢` is 7-regular, its
connected components ("blocks") are the cosets of `2^{n-1}ℤ³/2ⁿℤ³` inside
`ℤ[1/2]³/2ⁿℤ³`, and the block set `I` is `V`-isomorphic to `X` itself, a
block at level `n` corresponding to a site at level `n-1`.

*Proof.*  Apply Proposition 4.1 to `(a,b) = ((1,[0]),(1,[e₁]))`.  The image of
this ordered pair under `sτ^m` is `((n,[u_s]), (n,[u_s + 2^{m}A_s e₁]))` with
`n = 1+m`.  `[audit-fix 2026-08-15]` The original argument here let `A_s` run
over `SL₃(ℤ)` (all primitive vectors), which invokes the conditional (I1).
CRW-free replacement, verified by direct finite computation with the three
displayed `lem:linear` matrices reduced mod 2: the orbit of `ē₁` under
`⟨x̄,ȳ,z̄⟩ ≤ GL₃(𝔽₂)` is all seven nonzero classes of `(ℤ/2)³`

`e₂` and `(1,0,1)`).  Since the linear parts of the level stabiliser realise
this orbit and `2^{n-1}v mod 2ⁿℤ³` depends only on `v mod 2`, the neighbour

neighbours of `(n,[u])` are the seven sites `(n,[u + 2^{n-1}ε])`, `ε ≠ 0`;

## 5.  The block amalgam

**Theorem 5.1 (lamp group of `E`).**  Let `P_i ≤ N_E` be the subgroup
generated by the eight `c_ξ` with `ξ` in the block `i ∈ I`.  Then
`P_i ≅ ClLamp(8)`, of order `2⁹ = 512`, all `P_i` share the central involution
`ζ = w`, and

    N_E ≅ ∗_{⟨ζ⟩, i ∈ I} P_i ,

the free product of countably many copies of `ClLamp(8)` amalgamated over
their common central subgroup `⟨ζ⟩ ≅ ℤ/2`.  Moreover `ζ ≠ 1`.

*Proof.*  By (3.3) and Proposition 4.2, `N_E` is presented by: involutions
`c_ξ`, a central involution `ζ`, and the relations `[c_ξ,c_η] = ζ` for
distinct `ξ,η` in a common block.  Group the generators by blocks.  The
presentation of the amalgam `∗_{⟨ζ⟩} P_i` (a common central `ℤ/2` in each
factor) is the union of the factor presentations together with the
identification of the copies of `⟨ζ⟩`; that is literally the displayed
presentation, since "`ζ` central in each factor" plus "the factors generate"
is "`ζ` central".  Hence `N_E ≅ ∗_{⟨ζ⟩} ClLamp(8)^{(i)}` provided each factor
really is `ClLamp(8)` and `ζ ≠ 1`; both follow from the surjection
`N_E → ClLamp(X)` of §3, which maps `P_i` onto the Clifford lamp group of the
corresponding eight sites — of order `2⁹` by (I5) — and `ζ` to `−1 ≠ 1`.  A
surjection of a group of order at most `2⁹` onto a group of order `2⁹` is an
isomorphism. ∎

**Theorem 5.2 (the group `E` as an amalgam).**  With `B₁ = τ⁻¹Bτ` the
stabiliser of the block of `c`,

    E ≅ ( V × ⟨w⟩ ) ∗_{ B₁ × ⟨w⟩ } ( ClLamp(8) ⋊ B₁ ) ,

where `B₁` acts on the eight Clifford sites through `AGL₃(𝔽₂)` and fixes `ζ`.
Both vertex groups are residually finite; `E` itself is not
`[audit-fix 2026-08-15: cite `cor:notRFD` — `w ≠ 1` dies in every finite
quotient — rather than Theorem A, whose application to the literal
presentation sits on the conditional surface]`.

*Proof.*  `E = N_E ⋊ V` acts on the Bass--Serre tree `𝒯'` of the star-shaped
graph of groups of Theorem 5.1 (central vertex group `⟨ζ⟩`, leaf vertex groups
`P_i`, all edge groups `⟨ζ⟩`): `N_E` acts as usual, and `g ∈ V` acts by
`n⟨ζ⟩ ↦ (gng⁻¹)⟨ζ⟩` and `nP_i ↦ (gng⁻¹)P_{g·i}`, which is well defined
because `gζg⁻¹ = ζ` (centrality of `w` in `E`) and `gP_ig⁻¹ = P_{g·i}`.  The
stabiliser of the base central vertex is `⟨ζ⟩ ⋊ V = ⟨w⟩ × V`; the stabiliser
of the leaf `P_{i₀}` (`i₀` = block of `c`) is `P_{i₀} ⋊ Stab_V(i₀) =
ClLamp(8) ⋊ B₁`; the stabiliser of the edge between them is `⟨w⟩ × B₁`.  `V`
is transitive on `I` (Proposition 4.2) and `N_E` is transitive on each coset
family, so the quotient graph is a single edge; (I4d) gives the displayed
amalgam.  For residual finiteness of the vertex groups: `V` is linear over `ℚ`
(I3) and `ℤ/2` is finite; `ClLamp(8) ⋊ B₁` has the finite-index subgroup
`ClLamp(8) × B₂`, where `B₂ = ker(B₁ → AGL₃(𝔽₂))`, which is residually finite,
and residual finiteness passes to finite-index overgroups. ∎

Killing the centre of the blocks gives the companion statement used in §9:

    E/⟨w⟩ ≅ ( ∗_{i ∈ I} (ℤ/2)⁸ ) ⋊ V ≅ V ∗_{B₁} ( (ℤ/2)⁸ ⋊ B₁ ) ,   (5.3)

because `(∗_{⟨ζ⟩} P_i)/⟨ζ⟩ = ∗_i (P_i/⟨ζ⟩)` and `ClLamp(8)/⟨ζ⟩ ≅ (ℤ/2)⁸`.

## 6.  The kernel of `E → W` is free

**Theorem 6.1.**  `ker(E → W)` is a free group of countably infinite rank.  In
particular it contains nonabelian free subgroups and is **not amenable**.

*Proof.*  Write `φ : E → W` for the witness map.  On the quotient
`E/N_E = V → W/ClLamp(X) = V` the induced map is the identity (it sends the
six base generators to `j(ḡ)` and `t` to `τ`, which is the identification of
`B ∗_α` with `Γ̄ ∗_{ᾱ}` from (I1)--(I3)), and `φ(N_E) = ClLamp(X)`.  Hence

    ker φ = ker( N_E → ClLamp(X) ) .

By Theorem 5.1, `N_E = ∗_{⟨ζ⟩} P_i` acts on the associated Bass--Serre tree
with vertex stabilisers the conjugates of the `P_i` and of `⟨ζ⟩`.  Each `P_i`
maps injectively into `ClLamp(X)` (Theorem 5.1) and `ζ ↦ −1 ≠ 1`, so, `ker φ`
being normal,

    ker φ ∩ g P_i g⁻¹ = g (ker φ ∩ P_i) g⁻¹ = 1 ,
    ker φ ∩ g ⟨ζ⟩ g⁻¹ = 1 .

Thus `ker φ` acts freely on a tree and is free by (I4c).

Nontriviality and non-amenability: pick sites `ξ, η, θ` in three distinct
blocks.  Elements of distinct factors that avoid the amalgamated subgroup
generate their free product (I4b), so `⟨c_ξ,c_η,c_θ⟩ ≅ ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/2`, whose
kernel of the map to `ℤ/2` is free of rank 2 (Kurosh, or Euler
characteristic).  `ClLamp(X)` is locally finite (I5), so the image of this
`F₂` is finite, whence `F₂ ∩ ker φ` has finite index in `F₂` and is again
nonabelian free (Nielsen--Schreier).  A group containing `F₂` is not amenable.

Infinite rank: a finitely generated subgroup of `∗_{⟨ζ⟩}P_i` involves only
finitely many factors, say those indexed by `J ⊆ I` finite, and hence lies in
`∗_{⟨ζ⟩, i∈J}P_i`.  But for `ξ` in a block of `J` and `θ` in a block outside
`J`, the element `[c_ξ,c_θ]·ζ` lies in `ker φ` (in `ClLamp(X)` all distinct
sites anticommute, so `[c_ξ,c_θ] ↦ ζ ↦ ζ²=1`) and is a length-4 alternating
word times a central element, hence `≠ 1` by (I4b) and not in
`∗_{⟨ζ⟩,i∈J}P_i`.  So `ker φ` is not finitely generated, and a free group that
is not finitely generated has countably infinite rank here (the ambient group
is countable). ∎

**Corollary 6.2.**  The Cairn claim `literal-witness-kernel-amenable` is false,
and the route `literal-sofic-via-amenable-kernel` cannot establish
`literal-group-sofic`.

Two independent reasons that route is dead, in fact.  Besides Theorem 6.1, its
"why sufficient" paragraph invokes extension permanence in the direction
*amenable kernel + sofic quotient ⟹ sofic*.  The Elek--Szabó theorem
(`arXiv:math/0305352`) is the opposite direction — **sofic** kernel and
**amenable** quotient, proved through the Kaloujnine--Krasner embedding
`G ↪ N ≀ Q` — and the amenable-kernel direction is an instance of the open
extension problem for sofic groups (the Kaloujnine--Krasner argument only
gives `N^Q` sofic, so it reduces to sofic-by-sofic).  Section 9 shows this is
not a technicality.

## 7.  Normal form and the word problem

amalgamated normal form `ζ^ε q₁ ⋯ q_k` with `ε ∈ {0,1}`,
`q_j ∈ P_{i_j} \ ⟨ζ⟩`, `i_j ≠ i_{j+1}` — `[audit-fix 2026-08-15]` uniqueness
of the `q_j` holds only *relative to fixed transversals* of `⟨ζ⟩` in each
`P_i` (as stated it fails: `(ζq₁)(ζq₂) = q₁q₂` with all four letters outside
`⟨ζ⟩`); the sequence of blocks `i₁,…,i_k` and the length `k` are absolutely
unique.  The word problem for the literal forty-one-relator presentation is
solvable.

*Proof.*  Uniqueness of `p·g` is (3.1); uniqueness of the amalgam normal form
up to the standard `⟨ζ⟩`-bookkeeping is (I4b) together with Theorem 5.1 (the
central `ζ` may be pushed to the front, and each `q_j` is then well defined
modulo nothing, since `⟨ζ⟩` has been factored out on the left).

Algorithm.  Given a word `ω` in `v₁^{±},…,c^{±}`, scan from the left, keeping a
running element `g ∈ V` (a `4×4` rational matrix together with the `τ`-exponent
`n`, since `V = T ⋊ ⟨τ⟩` and `det` records `n`) and a list of Clifford letters.
On reading a `V`-generator, multiply into `g`.  On reading `c`, emit the letter
`c_ξ` with `ξ = g·o`, computed in the coordinates of Proposition 4.1 as
`(n, [u] mod 2ⁿℤ³)`, and continue.  This terminates and computes the
decomposition `ω = p·g` of the previous paragraph, with `p` a word in the
`c_ξ`.  Site equality and block equality are decidable: two sites are equal iff
their levels agree and their translation parts differ by an element of
`2ⁿℤ³`, and they are in one block iff the difference lies in `2^{n-1}ℤ³` —
both are finite rational arithmetic tests.  Now reduce `p`: repeatedly replace
a maximal run of consecutive letters lying in one block by their product,
computed inside the explicit finite group `ClLamp(8)` (order 512); a product
falling in `⟨ζ⟩` is deleted after moving `ζ` to the front (it is central), which
strictly shortens the word and may merge two neighbouring runs.  The process
terminates and outputs the normal form.  Finally `ω = 1` in `E` iff `g = 1` in
`V` (decidable: a rational matrix identity plus `n = 0`) and the normal form of
`p` is empty with `ε = 0`. ∎

Note the resulting contrast, which is not a contradiction: `E` has solvable
word problem while being non-residually-finite and non-MF.  The obstruction of
Theorem A is analytic, not algorithmic.

## 9.  What this does to the soficity question

`literal-group-sofic` stays OPEN, but its landscape changes.

**(a) The amenable-kernel route is gone** (Corollary 6.2).  What survives of
the extension `1 → ker φ → E → W → 1` is that `E` is *free-by-sofic*, an
instance of the open extension problem for sofic groups.

**(b) `E` is a Deligne-type central extension.**  By (5.3), `E` is a central
extension

    1 → ℤ/2 = ⟨w⟩ → E → E/⟨w⟩ → 1

of a finitely presented group, and `w` dies in every finite quotient of `E`
(a finite quotient gives an exact finite-dimensional unitary representation,
and `literal-central-mark-corona-invisible` kills `w` in all of those; this is
`cor:notRFD` of the manuscript).  That is exactly the configuration of the
theorem of Gohla and Thom, as stated in `arXiv:2410.02913`, Theorem 1.5: *if a
finitely presented group `Γ` is `ρ`-stable (flexibly stable in permutations:
every `ε`-almost action on `Ω` is `ρ(ε)`-close to a genuine action after
enlarging `Ω` to a finite superset) and `0 → ℤ/2 → Γ̃ → Γ → 1` is a central
extension whose central element is trivial in every finite quotient of `Γ̃`,
then `Γ̃` is not sofic.*  (Statement read from `arXiv:2410.02913`; the primary
Gohla--Thom source was not fetched, so this citation should be re-verified
against the primary paper before it carries weight.)

Consequently:

    E/⟨w⟩ flexibly permutation-stable   ⟹   E is NOT sofic.

So the literal group is a candidate *finitely presented non-sofic group*, and
soficity of `E` is equivalent to an approximate-lifting question for the class
of the central extension (5.3) rather than to any permanence property.

**(c) The Kun--Thom boundary.**  `Ê = V ∗_B (B × ℤ/2)` is precisely the
"free lamp" shape `G ∗_Γ (Γ × K)` of the Kun--Thom non-soficity mechanism
(`arXiv:2608.06222`, Theorem 4.1: for `Γ` infranormal in `G` with both Kazhdan,
the permutation-ultraproduct centraliser of `σ(Γ)` is normalised by `σ(G)`).
Here `Γ = B` is Kazhdan and infranormal in `G = V` — `τ` compresses `B`, and
`B` together with the compressors generates `V` — and the element that
mechanism kills is `λ = [tct⁻¹, v₁]`, which is exactly the unsquared defect
`u` of the manuscript, with `w = u²` by `lem:square`.  The single hypothesis
that fails is that `V` is **not** Kazhdan: `V ↠ ℤ` via the `τ`-exponent.  This
is the precise reason the manuscript's construction can produce a *sofic*
non-MF witness at all, and it is the precise gap that a would-be proof of
non-soficity of `E` has to fill by other means — for instance by (b).

**(d) A cheap corollary in the neighbouring cluster.**  If `E/⟨w⟩` is
residually finite then it is MF (`lem:rfmf`), which is precisely the open
claim `literal-mark-quotient-mf` and therefore gives `Rad_MF(E) = {1,w}`
(`literal-mf-radical-exact`) through the existing route.  This does *not* give
soficity of `E` — the missing permanence in Corollary 6.2 is exactly what
Gohla--Thom says may genuinely fail.  Note the natural residual-finiteness
strategy for `E/⟨w⟩` meets a concrete obstacle: in every finite quotient of `V`
all telescope levels have the same image (if `π(B) ⊆ π(τ⁻¹Bτ)` with both
finite of the same cardinality, they are equal), so `B` is not separable in
`V` and the site set collapses in every `V`-equivariant finite model.
