## Setup

`AMENABLE_fp` is `Pi^0_2` but not `Pi^0_1`, by (AF1) of
`amenability-of-finite-presentations-arithmetical-position`. The root asks for
a computable reduction from `TOT` whose positive branch is amenable.

The recorded no-go theorems close:

- finite equation packets and finitely generated centralizer switches
  (`finitely-generated-centralizer-switches-are-sigma1`);
- genuine HNN switches over amenable bases;
- finite graphs of amenable groups
  (`finite-graphs-of-amenable-groups-have-sigma1-locus`);
- finite `L`-presentations over amenable bases.

Their common feature: the amenable locus is witnessed by finitely many
derivations.

The target of this lane was a switch whose amenable event involves infinitely
many independent memberships, while the presentation stays finite.

## What was established

1. **Commuting-conjugate switch** (`commuting-conjugate-switch-amenability-criterion`,
   proved by `commuting-conjugate-switch-criterion-proof`). For `C = <S_C> <= K` and
   finite `U`, put

   ```text
   Q(K,C,U) = <K, b | [b,c] (c in S_C), [b, u b u^-1] (u in U)>.
   ```

   - `Q ~= A(Gamma) x| K`, where `Gamma` is the `K`-invariant graph on `K/C`
     spanned by the pairs `{C, uC}`.
   - `C`'s neighbours are exactly `(CUC cup CU^-1C)/C`.
   - `Q` is amenable iff `K` is amenable and `K = D := C cup CUC cup CU^-1C`. The
     positive branch is `Z wr_(K/C) K`. Otherwise `b` and `kbk^-1` (`k notin D`)
     generate `F_2`, by retracting the RAAG onto a non-edge.
   - The proof is elementary: von Dyck plus semidirect products, with no Bass–Serre
     theory.
   - `U` empty is the recorded centralizer switch. With `K = Z`, `C = 1` and `U`
     infinite, it is the shift-RAAG family.
2. **Finite-rank screen** (`finite-bi-index-forces-finite-index-in-metabelian-groups`,
   proved by `finite-bi-index-metabelian-proof`). A transitive finite-rank `K`-set
   is finite when:
   - `K` is virtually polycyclic, by induction on derived length with the content
     invariant on lattices; or
   - `K` is finitely generated abelian-by-virtually-abelian. Here the orbit module
     has finitely many cyclic submodules, hence finite length. Its simple factors
     are fields with finitely generated unit group, which are finite by a
     unique-factorization argument.

   The draft first used Zariski's lemma. It was replaced by the elementary
   Lemma 6.5, so the route cites no textbook theorem beyond unique factorization
   and subgroups of finitely generated abelian groups.

   **Consequence.** For such ambients `{e : K_e = D_e}` is `Sigma^0_1`, certified
   by a finite coset table. So the switch never reduces `TOT` through polycyclic
   or finitely generated metabelian ambients. This includes the finitely presented
   metabelian groups.
3. **Reduction** (`amenable-fp-pi2-complete-from-coset-cover-hardness`). The root
   follows from the new OPEN hole `amenable-fp-double-coset-cover-is-pi2-hard`: a
   computable family of amenable finite presentations with finite word sets
   `S_e`, `U_e` such that `e in TOT` iff `K_e = D_e`. This is the first live route
   into the root.

## Structural remarks (not promoted to nodes)

- **Suborbits must be infinite.** Let `K` be finitely generated by `s_1..s_n` and
  `C` commensurated, meaning `|CuC/C|` is finite for all `u`.
  - The union of the orbital graphs `{kC, k s_i C}` is `K`-invariant and locally
    finite.
  - It is connected: `C -- s_1 C -- s_1 s_2 C -- ...`.
  - Its graph distance is `K`-invariant and unbounded on infinite `X = K/C`, so
    the rank is infinite.

  So on the total branch `C_e` is not commensurated: some suborbit is infinite.
  - This kills any design that reads the switch off an isometric action on a
    locally finite tree or graph, such as Bass–Serre trees of `BS(1,n)`-like
    ambients or Schreier-type geometric models: distances give infinitely many
    orbitals.
- **Two-transitive ambients reduce to stabilizer generation.** Suppose
  `K` acts 2-transitively on `X`, `P = Stab(x)`, `C <= P` and `gx != x`. Then
  `D(C,{g}) cap P = C`: if `c g c' in P` then `g c' x = x`, so `gx = x`.
  - Hence `K = D(C,{g})` iff `C = P`. The hole then asks for a family where
    "`<S_e>` is the whole point stabilizer" is `Pi^0_2`-hard.
  - For a fixed ambient with explicitly known finitely generated stabilizers,
    such as Houghton groups, this is again `Sigma^0_1`. Only varying ambients,
    whose stabilizer generators are not known in advance, can help.
- **Lamp choice.** With `C_2` lamps (`b^2 = 1`) the same presentation gives a
  right-angled Coxeter extension. A single non-edge only retracts onto
  `C_2 * C_2 = D_infinity`, which is amenable. Free subgroups need three
  pairwise non-adjacent vertices, which ties the criterion to triangles in the
  complement graph rather than to the cover set. The `Z` lamp gives the clean
  dichotomy, so it was kept.

## Approaches and where each dies

1. **Polycyclic and metabelian ambients**, including the finitely presented
   metabelian groups with the directional lamplighter interface. *Dead*:
   finite bi-index forces finite index, and the locus becomes `Sigma^0_1`.
2. **Houghton groups `H_n`, `n >= 3`.** These are elementary amenable, and the
   natural action has rank two with stabilizer `~= H_n`.
   `Q(H_n, Stab(x), {g}) = Z wr_X H_n` is a finitely presented amenable positive
   instance, where finite presentation is from Brown 1987, **unverified**.
   *Dies for encoding*: the group, its rank and its stabilizer generators are
   fixed, and the word problem is solvable.
3. **Wreath lift `K = M wr_X H`** of a machine group `M` over a highly transitive
   `H`, acting on `X x M/P`. The rank is `1 + |P\M/P|`. *Transfers nothing*: the
   cover problem returns to `M`.
4. **KMS machine groups `G(M)` in `A_p^2 A`.**
   - An infinite finite-rank transitive action reduces to an infinite simple
     `F_p[R]`-module with finitely many `R`-orbits, for some finitely generated
     metabelian `R`. The orbit module has finite length by the unit trick, which
     holds in any group ring.
   - *Open*. The field argument needs commutativity.
   - Tried and failed: small semilinear models, which all have infinitely many
     orbits.
     - An infinite field of characteristic `p` algebraic over `F_p`, with a
       finitely generated `R <= F^x x| <Frobenius>`: the multiplicative part lies
       in a finite subfield, so orbits are finite.
     - The shift model `Z wr Z` acting on `F_p[t_i^(+-1) : i in Z]`, by `t_0` and
       the index shift: the number of monomials in the support is invariant, and
       this module is not simple anyway.
   - *Induced modules die by support size.* Take `R = B x| <t>` with `B` abelian,
     for instance `R = Z wr Z` with `B = Z^(Z)`. Let `W` be a finite field `F_q`
     on which `B` acts by a character `chi : B -> F_q^x` whose values generate
     `F_q`. Suppose the conjugate modules `W_i`, where `B` acts by
     `chi(t^i . t^-i)`, are pairwise non-isomorphic, that is, not Galois
     conjugate. For `Z wr Z`, take a non-periodic sequence of values
     `chi(e_i)`. Then `Ind_B^R W = (+)_(i in Z) t^i W` is simple and infinite.
     - Simplicity: a nonzero vector lies in a finite sum of pairwise
       non-isomorphic simple `F_p[B]`-modules. So its `F_p[B]`-span contains
       every component in its support, and `t`-translates give everything.
     - Orbits: the number of nonzero components is `R`-invariant, so there are
       infinitely many orbits.
     - The same invariant applies whenever the `B`-isotypic decomposition of a
       simple module has infinitely many components. The remaining case is a
       simple module whose restriction to `B` has no simple submodule, or only
       finitely many isotypic components.
   - *Refinement in the KMS class itself (proved, artifact Section 5.1).* In
     `A_p^2 A`, the group `R` has an elementary abelian normal `p`-subgroup `B`
     with `R/B` abelian. Then:
     - the infinite simple factor `V` must satisfy `V^B = 0`, since otherwise
       `B` acts trivially and the field argument applies;
     - some `B`-orbit on `V` is infinite, by the fixed-point count for finite
       `p`-groups;
     - the proper stabilizers `B_v` lie in finitely many `R`-conjugacy classes,
       and their conjugates swallow arbitrarily large finite subgroups of `B`.

     Because the only simple `F_p[B]`-module is trivial, the isotypic-support
     invariant above is useless in this class. The test case is, for instance,
     `R = C_p wr Z`: is there an infinite simple `F_p[R]`-module with no
     base-fixed vector and finitely many orbits? The constraints allow half-line
     stabilizers such as `(+)_(i >= 0) C_p`, so they give no contradiction.
   - *Local-field model: tried, dies on a metric invariant.* Let
     `V = C_c^infty(F_p((t)), F_p)`, with `C_p wr Z = F_p[t^(+-1)] x| <t>`
     acting by `x -> t^j x + b`.
     - `V` is simple. Every nonzero ideal of `F_p[E]`, for a finite subgroup `E`
       of `F_p((t))/t^n O`, contains the norm `N_E`. Norms of `t^m O/t^n O`
       are ball indicators, and scaling plus dense translations give all
       balls.
     - `V^B = 0` by density, and the stabilizer of `1_O` is `F_p[t]`, a half-line.
     - It has infinitely many orbits. For supports, diameter divided by the
       largest ball radius is a similarity invariant, and it equals `p^k` on
       `1_O + 1_(t^-k + O)` for `k >= 2`. Measure is not invariant here,
       because `t` scales it.
     - Any group of similarities of an ultrametric space keeps this invariant.
       Diagonal models on `F_p((t)) x F_p((t^-1))` preserve product Haar
       measure instead. So an example would need an action that is not by
       similarities.

     Full proof in artifact Section 5.1.
   - There are no examples and no proof. The orbit-finiteness condition on a
     simple module over a non-commutative group ring is the open part.
5. **Thompson's group `F`** on dyadic points has rank three. It is unusable while
   its amenability is open, and it is a single group.
6. **Topological full groups of minimal subshifts** are amenable, with rich
   actions. They were not pursued: no finitely presented example with a
   computation-controlled rank came to mind, and the finite-presentation status
   was not checked.

## What remains open

- `amenable-fp-double-coset-cover-is-pi2-hard`. The ambients must be finitely
  presented amenable groups, neither virtually polycyclic nor
  abelian-by-virtually-abelian, with a finitely generated subgroup of infinite
  index, finite bi-index and infinite suborbits. Their rank must be controlled by
  a computation.
- The class-three test: does some finitely generated metabelian `R` have an
  infinite simple `F_p[R]`-module with finitely many `R`-orbits? For the KMS class
  it suffices to ask this for `R` in `A_p A` and modules with no nonzero
  fixed vector of the base `B`, for example `R = C_p wr Z`. A negative answer
  screens out the KMS machine groups. A positive answer is a first candidate
  ambient.
