# Invariant-corner visibility audit — 2026-08-30

## Verdict

The universal claim `mf-invariant-corner-visibility` cannot currently be
promoted to an established node, and no counterexample can honestly be
recorded.  A proof would establish the open root `mf-implies-hyperlinear` by
the already complete route
`mf-implies-hyperlinear-via-invariant-corner`.  A counterexample would
separate weak group MF from elementwise trace-controlled operator MF and
invalidate the two routes which require the corner claim; it would *not* by
itself refute `mf-implies-hyperlinear`, because the latter only asks for
Hilbert--Schmidt models.  The repository contains neither a universal proof
nor such a separating pair.

This pass does close the largest elementary elementwise regime left implicit
in the target: finite-dimensional visibility always gives the desired gap,
with `P=1`.  The proof is canonicalized as
`mf-fd-visible-elements-have-global-trace-gaps`.  Thus a counterexample must
be a pair

```text
(G,g),  G MF,  1 != g in Rad_fd(G) \ Rad_MF(G).
```

The target is therefore only genuinely open on the strict radical-difference
zone where exact matrices kill the mark but asymptotic operator-norm matrices
do not.

## The balanced-separator construction

Let `phi_n:G->U(d_n)` be a faithful MF model and let
`rho:G->U(m)` be exact with `rho(g)!=I`.  The table

```text
phi_n^(direct sum m) direct sum rho^(direct sum d_n)
```

has two equal-dimensional halves.  Its defect is that of `phi_n`, its
operator separation dominates that of `phi_n`, and its real trace at `g` is
at most

```text
1-(1-Re tr_m(rho(g)))/2 < 1.
```

No corner extraction, density estimate, or defect/rank rate is involved.
This also explains why the finite-dimensional residual, rather than the
finite residual, is the exact first obstruction.

## Why the four evident generalizations stop

### 1. Spectral projection of the marked unitary

For a faithful corona image `u(g)!=1`, functional calculus gives a nonzero
spectral projection for a part of `u(g)` away from one whenever the relevant
spectral cut is available.  It commutes with `u(g)`, but there is no reason it
commutes with `u(a)` for the other group elements.  Taking its `G`-orbit does
not repair this: a finite orbit sum need not be a projection, and an infinite
support supremum belongs naturally to a bidual rather than to the norm
matrix corona.

### 2. The invariant hull of one outlier vector

At coordinate level an operator-norm witness may live on one eigenvector.
The subspace generated from that vector by the coordinate group tables can
be the whole `d_n`-dimensional space.  Even for exact irreducible matrix
representations, the smallest invariant subspace containing a nonzero
vector is the whole space.  Hence invariant hull formation can dilute a
rank-one trace gap back to order `1/d_n`; it supplies no dimension-free
constant.

### 3. Tensoring until the trace drops

If the initial trace gap is `kappa_n`, a tensor exponent of order
`1/kappa_n` can make the trace macroscopic after identity padding removes
scalar phase.  But the operator-norm multiplication defect grows linearly in
the exponent.  The needed condition is exactly a defect-versus-gap rate of
the kind isolated in `mf-trace-gap-defect-ratio-models`.  Weak MF supplies no
such rate, and identity-block padding can make `kappa_n` decay arbitrarily
faster without changing any operator-norm data.

### 4. Central support in a tracial completion

The support of the ideal generated by `u(g)-1` becomes a central projection
in a von Neumann envelope, but the target needs a projection in the norm
corona itself whose intrinsic corner traces are represented by finite
matrices.  These are different assertions.  Rank-one projection sequences
in dimensions tending to infinity are the basic warning: they are nonzero
in the norm corona while every ambient normalized matrix trace assigns them
mass tending to zero.  Passing to a bidual support does not manufacture the
required norm-corona corner or a dimension-free coordinate trace gap.

## Exact remaining target

After the new finite-dimensional separator theorem and the established
finite-normal/finite-central-phase cases, a useful minimal attack instance
must have all of the following features:

1. `G` is weak MF;
2. `1!=g` is in `Rad_fd(G)`;
3. `g` is not in `Rad_MF(G)` (automatic from weak MF of `G`);
4. `g` is not covered by a finite-normal or finite-central-phase corner;
5. one must either select MF tables with a defect/trace rate or construct an
   unbounded-rank invariant corner directly.

This boundary is strict and proof-relevant.  It rules out any further attack
that merely adjoins an exact finite-dimensional detector, takes a spectral
cut of the marked unitary, or reuses an arbitrarily padded MF realization.
