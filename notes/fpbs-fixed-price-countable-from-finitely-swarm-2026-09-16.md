# Countable fixed-price passage: amalgamation descent and what it leaves (September 16, 2026)

Target: `fpbs-fixed-price-countable-from-finitely-generated` (OPEN, and it
stays OPEN).

> Assume every infinite finitely generated group has fixed price (call this
> **H**). Prove that every countably infinite group has fixed price.

Main artifact written in this session:
`research/artifacts/fpbs-amalgam-descent-amenably-generated-2026-09-16.md`.
It builds on the existing passage artifact
`research/artifacts/fpbs/docs/fixed-price-countable-passage.md` (Theorems B
and G, Proposition F, examples T4 and T5), and its conventions are kept.

**Notation.**
- Actions are essentially free p.m.p. actions ("free actions").
- `C(Gamma)` is the infimum of the costs of free actions of `Gamma`.
- `s_Gamma` is the Bernoulli shift.
- **AG** is the class of countable groups generated by finitely many amenable
  subgroups, equivalently by finitely many amenable subgroups plus a finite
  set.

**Theorem 1 (unconditional).** Let `A` be an infinite amenable common subgroup
of countable groups `Gamma_1` and `Gamma_2`, and suppose every free action of
each `Gamma_i` has finite cost. If `G = Gamma_1 *_A Gamma_2` has fixed price,
then both `Gamma_i` have fixed price, the restriction of every free action of
`G` to `Gamma_i` has cost `C(Gamma_i)`, and `c(G) = C(Gamma_1) + C(Gamma_2) - 1`.

How it is proved:
1. For every free action `b` of `G`, Gaboriau's amalgamation formula (lecture
   notes [GL] Thm 2.27, from Gaboriau 2000, Theoreme IV.15) gives
   `C(b) = C(b|Gamma_1) + C(b|Gamma_2) - 1`. The subtracted term is 1 because
   `E_{b|A}` is hyperfinite with infinite orbits.
2. Co-induction gives free actions of `G` with both restrictions near their
   infima. With fixed price of `G`, this pins down `c(G)`.
3. Since each restriction is at least its infimum, both restrictions of every
   free `b` are exactly `C(Gamma_i)`. Taking `b = s_G` gives
   `C(s_{Gamma_i}) = C(Gamma_i)`.
4. Abert-Weiss and Tucker-Drob (Cor 6.20, Def 6.6, Cor 6.8(1)) then bound the
   cost of every free action of `Gamma_i` by `C(s_{Gamma_i})`.

**Theorem 2' (unconditional tower).** Let `Gamma = <A_1, ..., A_m, S>` with
every `A_i` infinite amenable and `S` finite. Embed each `A_i` in a finitely
generated `B_i` (Schupp), and put `G_i = G_(i-1) *_(A_i) B_i` with
`G_0 = Gamma`. Then `G_m` is finitely generated, every factor in the tower
has only finite-cost free actions (Corollary 1.1'), and fixed price of `G_m`
alone gives fixed price of `Gamma` by downward induction with Theorem 1.

**Theorem 2 (under H).** Every countably infinite group in AG has fixed price.

**What this adds to the passage artifact.**
- Its §6 notes that shape (A) of Theorem G ("no fixed pseudocost") cannot be
  excluded by an argument that passes only through subgroups of `Gamma`.
- Theorem 2 applies H at a finitely generated **supergroup**, and the
  amalgamation formula converts fixed price of the supergroup into
  `C(s_Gamma) = C(Gamma)`.
- Under H, a counterexample must lie outside AG. AG contains all finitely
  generated groups, all countable amenable groups, and non-finitely-generated,
  non-locally-finite groups such as `Q * Z` and `SL(2,Q)`.

**Proposition 4 (limit of the method).** If `Gamma_1 *_A Gamma_2` is finitely
generated, then `Gamma_1 = <A, S_1>` for a finite `S_1`. The proof uses a
minimal expression in letters from `<A, S_1>` and `<A, S_2>`, together with
the normal form theorem. So a single descent step from a finitely generated
group over an amenable subgroup reaches exactly AG.

**Test group outside AG (artifact §5.1).** `Gamma_mal` is the direct limit of
`F_2 -> F_2 -> ...` along `phi(a) = a`, `phi(b) = b a b^-2`.
- `phi(F_2)` is a proper malnormal rank-2 subgroup. The proof is a Stallings
  folding plus a pullback-graph criterion, proved in Lemma 5.2 from standard
  folding facts, and a certificate script checks it.
- So `Gamma_mal` is locally free and not finitely generated, the stage
  subgroups are malnormal, and all amenable subgroups are cyclic. Hence
  `Gamma_mal` is not in AG.
- It has no normal, commensurated or chain-base finitely generated infinite
  subgroup, so Proposition F does not apply.
- `c*(Gamma_mal) = 2`.
- H holds unconditionally for its finitely generated subgroups, since they
  are free. So any argument that uses H only on subgroups of `Gamma_mal`
  would prove outright that `Gamma_mal` has fixed price. An argument using H
  on a finitely generated supergroup, as Theorem 2 does, is not limited in
  this way. The implication "H implies fixed price of `Gamma_mal`" is
  undecided here.

## 2. Approaches and where each dies

### 2.1 Exhaustion by finitely generated subgroups

This is already recorded on the target node. Under H each stage has a fixed
price, and pseudocost satisfies `PC(a) <= liminf c(Delta_n)`. That is only an
upper bound. The passage artifact's Corollary C sandwiches
`C(Gamma) <= PC(a) <= PC(s_Gamma) <= c*`, but it cannot close the gap.

Pseudocost is an infimum over arbitrary intermediate relations, not over
subgroup orbit relations, and H says nothing about those. The target node
also has an example where the stage prices diverge while the union has price
one (Gaboriau's lectures, Exercise 2.60(1)).

**Dies at:** H fixes the costs of the subgroup relations `E_{a|Delta}`. It does
not fix the cost of completing a near-optimal graphing of `E_{a|Delta_n}` into
one of `E_{a|Delta_(n+1)}`. No general formula computes that increment.

### 2.2 Restriction rigidity from an arbitrary finitely generated host

By the Schupp embedding claim, `Gamma` embeds in a finitely generated `G`, and
H gives fixed price of `G`. The statement needed is: for every free action `b`
of `G`, `C(b|Gamma) = C(Gamma)`. Taking `b = s_G` gives
`C(s_Gamma) = C(Gamma)`, and Theorem B of the passage artifact then gives
fixed price, provided every free action of `Gamma` has finite cost.

**Dies at:** for a general embedding, no formula relates `C(b)` to `C(b|Gamma)`.
Fixed price of `G` constrains only the total. The one mechanism found that
turns constancy of the total into constancy of the restrictions is additivity:
the amalgamation formula over a hyperfinite subrelation, where the total is a
sum of two restriction costs, each bounded below by its infimum (Theorem 1,
Step 3). This is what forces the method onto amalgams.

### 2.3 Amalgamation descent over amenable subgroups

This works, and gives Theorems 1, 2' and 2.

**Dies at:** Proposition 4. A group that is an amalgam factor over an amenable
subgroup of a finitely generated group already lies in AG.

**Not analyzed, and possible ways further:**
- towers in which only the top group is finitely generated. Proposition 4
  does not propagate downward, because an amenable subgroup of an amalgam
  need not be conjugate into a factor;
- HNN extensions and finite graphs of groups over amenable edge groups. [GL]
  Cor 2.32 gives the HNN cost formula, and analogues of Theorem 1 and
  Proposition 4 are expected but not checked;
- descent through supergroups in which `Gamma` is normal.

### 2.4 Nonamenable amalgams

The amalgamation formula needs the amalgamated relation hyperfinite. If a free
action of `A` has hyperfinite orbit relation, then `A` is amenable ([GL]
Remark 1.12). So for nonamenable `A` the formula does not apply to the
decomposition of a free action. This rules out the obvious host for
`Gamma_mal`.

### 2.5 The obvious host for `Gamma_mal`

`M = <a, b, t | t a t^-1 = a, t b t^-1 = b a b^-2>` is finitely generated and
presumably has `Gamma_mal` as the kernel of `M -> Z`. This was not
re-derived for this `phi`.

**Dies at:** `Gamma_mal` is normal of infinite index there, not a factor, and
`M` is an HNN extension over the nonamenable `phi(F_2)`. So neither Theorem 1
nor [GL] Cor 2.32 applies.

### 2.6 l^2 lower bounds

Gaboriau's inequality `C(Gamma) >= 1 + beta_1^(2)(Gamma)` is a lower bound
that does not depend on the action. It can decide fixed price only when it
matches an upper bound for every free action.

For `Gamma_mal`, `c* = 2`. If `beta_1^(2)(Gamma_mal) = 1`, a failure of fixed
price would have to be a free action of infinite cost (shape (B) of Theorem
G). But the Fox-matrix computation in the style of passage-artifact T5 has a
trinomial entry `1 - b a b^-1 - b a b^-2`, and injectivity would need a
zero-divisor input (for example Linnell's theorem) that was not consulted.

Artifact §5.1 shows that this `phi`-shape cannot avoid the trinomial. No
proper malnormal `<a, v>` has `v` with exactly two `b`-letters, and an
explicit conjugation witness proves it.

**Dies at:** the zero-divisor input was not consulted.

## 3. Where a referee should push

1. **Hypotheses of [GL] Thm 2.27.** The definition of `R_1 *_{R_3} R_2` in
   Gaboriau, Invent. Math. 139 (2000), was not re-read.
   - Theorem 1 uses the formula through the lecture notes' statement and the
     sentence before it: a free action of `Gamma_1 *_{Gamma_3} Gamma_2`
     induces the relation amalgam.
   - Artifact Lemma 1.5 checks the natural freeness conditions directly:
     intersection, generation, and no closed alternating chain.
   - The formula also needs finite cost of the factors, which the claim
     assumes. Footnote 17 of [GL] says this "can be removed", but that is not
     used.
2. **Tucker-Drob.** Cor 6.20, Def 6.6 and Cor 6.8(1) are used as tabulated in
   §1 of the passage artifact, which records a full-text reading on September
   12, 2026. They were not re-read in this session.
3. **Schupp embedding.** Only "every countable group embeds in a finitely
   generated group" is consumed. The graph claim
   `countable-group-embeds-in-two-generator-simple-group` is established by a
   citation route whose own node says the source was not re-read. The weaker
   statement used here is the classical Higman-Neumann-Neumann embedding, but
   that was not re-checked either.
4. **Standard folding facts.** The Stallings facts (S1)-(S5) behind §5.1 were
   not re-read. §5.1 feeds no graph node.
