# Finite-residual commutators close the canonical central route

## Outcome

Let \(P\) be finitely presented, let

\[
R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)=
\bigcap_{\phi:P\to Q,\ Q\ {\rm finite}}\ker\phi
\]

be its finite residual, and let \(w\in R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)\). If

\[
w\in[P,R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)],
\]

then one fixed integral coefficient vector expresses \(w\) in terms of the
presentation relators in every canonical finite-quotient relation module.
Consequently all canonical character ratios are uniformly bounded.

For the explicit Kun--Thom symmetric double

\[
D=G*_\Gamma G,
\]

the complete finite residual \(R\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{R=[D,R].}
\]

It follows that the canonical central-quotient route is sterile for
**every** radical word of this double, including the fold-aligned
Baumslag--Solitar word. The local \(p^k\) clock amplifier therefore cannot
finish FALSE through a finite central-relator quotient of the fixed
presentation.

## 1. A general fixed-coefficient criterion

Fix a finite presentation

\[
P=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_d\mid r_1,\ldots,r_\ell\rangle=F/N.
\]

For a finite quotient \(P\twoheadrightarrow Q\), let

\[
L_Q=\ker(F\to Q),
\qquad
C_Q=L_Q/[F,L_Q].
\]

Every element of \(R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)\), and every defining relator, belongs
to \(L_Q\).

### Theorem 1

If \(w\in[P,R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)]\), then there is one vector
\(v=(v_1,\ldots,v_\ell)\in\mathbb Z^\ell\), independent of \(Q\), such that

\[
[w]=\sum_{j=1}^\ell v_j[r_j]
\qquad\text{in }C_Q
\]

for every finite quotient \(P\twoheadrightarrow Q\). In particular,

\[
\sup_Q\delta_Q(w)\leq\|v\|_2<\infty.
\]

#### Proof

Let \(\widetilde R\) be the inverse image of \(R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)\) in
\(F\), and let \(W\in F\) be the fixed word representing \(w\). The
hypothesis \(w\in[P,R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)]\) gives an element

\[
c\in[F,\widetilde R]
\]

with the same image as \(W\) in \(P\). Hence

\[
Wc^{-1}\in N.
\]

Because \(N\leq\widetilde R\), one has

\[
[F,N]\leq[F,\widetilde R].
\]

The abelian group \(N/[F,N]\) is generated by the images of the finitely
many defining relators: every element of \(N\) is a product of conjugates of
the \(r_j^{\pm1}\), and conjugation disappears modulo \([F,N]\). Therefore
there is a fixed vector \(v\in\mathbb Z^\ell\) such that

\[
W\left(\prod_jr_j^{v_j}\right)^{-1}
\in[F,\widetilde R].
\]

For every finite quotient \(Q\), the finite residual is killed, so

\[
\widetilde R\leq L_Q.
\]

Thus

\[
[F,\widetilde R]\leq[F,L_Q],
\]

and the preceding identity becomes

\[
[W]=\sum_jv_j[r_j]
\qquad\text{in }L_Q/[F,L_Q].
\]

The same \(v\) works for every \(Q\), proving the theorem. \(\square\)

### Corollary 2

Under the hypothesis of Theorem 1,

\[
\Lambda_Q(w)\leq\delta_Q(w)\leq\|v\|_2
\qquad\text{for every finite }Q.
\]

Hence neither the canonical central covers nor any finite central-relator
models of the presentation can prove FALSE.

#### Proof

The first inequality is telescoping and Cauchy--Schwarz. The second is
Theorem 1. The canonical-cover cofinality theorem says that every finite
quotient of the relation-centralized presentation factors through one of
these covers, while the universality theorem says that every finite
central-relator model is such a quotient. \(\square\)

## 2. The radical module of the symmetric double

Use the explicit binary Laurent Kun--Thom pair

\[
\Gamma<N<G
\]

and put

\[
D=G*_\Gamma G,
\qquad
D_N=G*_N G.
\]

Let

\[
q:D\to D_N,
\qquad
R=\ker q.
\]

The complete-radical calculation and residual finiteness of \(D_N\) give

\[
R=\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm sof}(D)
 =R_{\mathrm{fin}}(D).
\]

Indeed, every finite group is sofic, so the sofic radical is contained in
the finite residual; conversely the residually finite quotient \(D/R=D_N\)
separates every element outside \(R\).

The abelianized-radical calculation gives a natural
\(\mathbb ZD_N\)-module isomorphism

\[
R_{\rm ab}=R/[R,R]
\cong
A:=
\ker\left(
\mathbb Z[D_N/\Gamma]\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z[D_N/N]
\right).
\]

The map of permutation modules is induced by
\(d\Gamma\mapsto dN\).

## 3. Vanishing of the radical coinvariants

### Theorem 3

\[
A_{D_N}=0.
\]

#### Proof

Apply \(H_*(D_N,-)\) to the short exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow A
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[D_N/\Gamma]
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[D_N/N]
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The relevant part of the long exact sequence is

\[
H_1(D_N,\mathbb Z[D_N/\Gamma])
\longrightarrow
H_1(D_N,\mathbb Z[D_N/N])
\longrightarrow
A_{D_N}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z[D_N/\Gamma]_{D_N}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z[D_N/N]_{D_N}.
\]

Shapiro's lemma identifies the first two terms with

\[
H_1(\Gamma,\mathbb Z)=\Gamma_{\rm ab},
\qquad
H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=N_{\rm ab}.
\]

Both \(\Gamma\) and \(N\) are stable elementary groups and are perfect, so
these groups vanish. Both permutation actions are transitive, hence both
coinvariant modules on the right are canonically \(\mathbb Z\). The induced
map is the identity. Exactness therefore forces

\[
A_{D_N}=0.
\]

\(\square\)

### Corollary 4

\[
\boxed{R=[D,R].}
\]

#### Proof

Conjugation by \(D\) on \(R_{\rm ab}\) factors through \(D/R=D_N\), and

\[
R/[D,R]
\cong
(R/[R,R])_{D_N}
\cong A_{D_N}.
\]

Theorem 3 makes the last group zero. \(\square\)

## 4. Closure of the canonical FALSE route

### Theorem 5

For any finite presentation of the explicit symmetric double \(D\), and
for every \(w\in R\), there is a fixed constant \(B_w<\infty\) such that

\[
\delta_Q(w)\leq B_w,
\qquad
\Lambda_Q(w)\leq B_w
\]

for every finite quotient \(D\twoheadrightarrow Q\).

In particular, the universal centralized-presentation ratio is finite for
every radical word:

\[
C_{\rm cent}(D,w)<\infty.
\]

#### Proof

Corollary 4 gives \(w\in[D,R]\), and \(R=R_{\mathrm{fin}}(D)\). Apply
Theorem 1 and then Corollary 2. \(\square\)

The statement applies in particular to

\[
w=i_2(h)i_1(h)^{-1},
\]

where \(h\in N\setminus\Gamma\) is the fold-aligned
Baumslag--Solitar element. Its local integral cycle may have exact
\(p^k\)-primary order, but no global canonical central character can make
the fixed relator phases vanish while retaining \(w\): the fixed vector
from Theorem 1 bounds the marked phase by the combined relator phase on
every finite quotient.

## 5. Consequence for the remaining search

This no-go is specific to the scalar or multi-central relator route. It does
not rule out genuinely noncentral approximate models, such as the surviving
base-dependent Fourier/Frobenius kernel-carry system.

It also gives a design constraint for a replacement radical candidate. A
candidate intended for the canonical central strategy must have a marked
finite-residual class that survives

\[
R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)/[P,R_{\mathrm{fin}}(P)].
\]

The explicit symmetric double fails this test maximally: that entire
quotient is zero.

There is now an exact refinement of this necessary condition. The note
FALSE_DIRECTED_COEFFICIENT_COMPACTNESS.md constructs a canonical central
envelope \(E=F/[F,\widetilde R]\) and proves that bounded coefficient
distortion is equivalent to absorption by \(R_{\rm fin}(E)\). Thus the
actual obstruction is a quotient \(\Omega\) of
\(R_{\rm fin}(P)/[P,R_{\rm fin}(P)]\). If \(E\) is residually finite then
the two groups agree; in general nonzero ordinary coinvariants alone do not
yet suffice.
