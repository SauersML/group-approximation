# Balanced radical implantation is Connes-embeddably visible

Date: 2026-08-13

## Outcome

The scalar Clifford representation does not itself extend across a perfect
radical-implantation amalgam, but a balanced two-sector version does.  This
gives a finite simple *packet* separating the MF and hyperlinear radicals.

Let `E` be the literal non-MF group with central involution `w`, and let
`A` be a finite perfect group normally generated by an involution `a`.  Form

```text
H_A = E *_(<w>=<a>) A.
```

The existing radical-implantation argument gives

```text
i(A) <= Rad_MF(H_A),                                (BRI1)
```

where `i:A->H_A` is the Bass--Serre vertex embedding.  On the other hand,
there is one homomorphism from `H_A` to a hyperlinear group which is faithful
on the whole copy of `A`.  Consequently

```text
i^(-1)(Rad_hyp(H_A)) = {1}.                         (BRI2)
```

For `A=PSL(2,13)`, every nonidentity element of the credited arithmetic
simple group is therefore MF-invisible in `H_A` and simultaneously visible
under one common hyperlinear detector.

## 1. Balancing the Clifford sign

The coset-tower construction supplies a homomorphism

```text
pi_- : E -> U(M_-)
```

into a Connes-embeddable finite von Neumann algebra with `pi_-(w)=-1`.
Take its direct sum with the trivial representation, with trace weight `1/2`
on each summand:

```text
M_E = M_- direct_sum C,
pi_E = pi_- direct_sum 1.
```

Then `W=pi_E(w)` is a non-scalar involution whose two spectral projections
both have trace `1/2`.  Thus

```text
D_E = W*(W) = C^2
```

with the balanced trace.

Let `lambda_A` be the left regular representation of `A`.  Since `a` is a
nonidentity involution, left multiplication by `a` has no fixed points and
is a product of two-cycles.  Hence `lambda_A(a)` also has `+1` and `-1`
spectral projections of trace `1/2`.  The two copies of balanced `C^2` admit
a trace-preserving identification carrying

```text
W <-> lambda_A(a).                                  (BRI3)
```

This is why the scalar no-go in
`FALSE_PERFECT_RADICAL_IMPLANTATION_CANNOT_CARRY_SCALAR_PHASE.md` is not the
end of the construction: the balancing makes the common involution central
only relative to the `E` vertex, not in the whole target.

## 2. The Connes-embeddable amalgam

Form the reduced tracial von Neumann amalgam

```text
M = M_E *_(C^2) M_|A|(C).                           (BRI4)
```

Both vertex algebras are Connes embeddable, and the common algebra `C^2` is
finite-dimensional, hence hyperfinite.  Brown--Dykema--Jung, Corollary 4.5,
proves that a tracial amalgamated free product of separable
`R^omega`-embeddable von Neumann algebras over a hyperfinite algebra is again
`R^omega`-embeddable:

[`Free Entropy Dimension in Amalgamated Free Products`](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609080).

The canonical vertex embeddings in `(BRI4)` are faithful and agree on the
identified `C^2`.  The group-amalgam universal property therefore gives

```text
pi : H_A -> U(M),
```

whose restriction to `A` is the faithful left regular representation.  If
`Q=pi(H_A)`, then `Q` is hyperlinear and finitely generated, and `pi|_A` is
injective.  This proves `(BRI2)`.  The abstract residual step is
kernel-checked in `Sofic/RadicalSeparation.lean` as

```text
comap_hyperlinearResidual_eq_bot_of_detector_injective.
```

Moreover `Q` is not MF: otherwise the quotient homomorphism `H_A->Q` would
kill `(BRI1)`, contradicting faithfulness on `A`.  Thus the construction also
gives a finitely generated hyperlinear non-MF image containing the prescribed
finite simple group.

## 3. Arithmetic specialization and provenance

Take `A=PSL(2,13)` and let `a` be any involution.  The exact arithmetic and
finite-group certificates are credited to
[`eekarabiyik/SL2F13`](https://github.com/eekarabiyik/SL2F13/tree/main), as
audited in `SL2F13_RADICAL_APPLICATION_AUDIT.md`.  Combining that certificate
with `(BRI1)--(BRI4)` yields

```text
PSL(2,13) <= Rad_MF(H_13),
i^(-1)(Rad_hyp(H_13)) = {1}.                         (BRI5)
```

So the same explicit inverse-Galois-labelled simple subgroup is invisible to
all MF targets yet faithfully visible in one hyperlinear target.

## 4. Relevance to universal hyperlinearity

This is not a nonhyperlinear group.  It amplifies the already-known
MF-versus-hyperlinear separation from one central sign to an entire finite
simple subgroup.  It also shows precisely what balancing can repair: scalar
phase compatibility over a finite edge.

It does **not** repair the decisive radical collision.  Replacing `Rad_MF`
in `(BRI1)` by `Rad_sof` would already require a hyperlinear detector of the
sofic-radical involution on the second vertex, which is the open problem in
another form.  The active nonhyperlinear endpoint therefore remains the
paired coefficient-radical extraction/finite-multiplicity gate.
