## The strengthened theorem

Let R be a countable unital ring with s, t such that ts = 1 and R(1−st)R = R.  Then every
homomorphism from EL_n(R) to an MF group is trivial for every n ≥ 2 (Theorem B currently says
n ≥ 4).  For nonzero R the hypothesis is equivalent to a properly infinite unit: v_0, v_1, w_0,
w_1 with w_i v_j = δ_ij (PI); no relation v_0w_0 + v_1w_1 = 1 is needed.

## 1. Fullness gives two disjoint copies of R

e = 1 − st, Σ_{j<m} a_j e b_j = 1.  Put v_0 = s^m, w_0 = t^m, v_1 = Σ_j s^j e b_j,
w_1 = Σ_j a_j e t^j.  Since es = te = 0 and ts = 1, e t^i s^j e = δ_ij e for i, j < m
(for j > i the middle is a positive power of s, killed by e on the left; for i > j a positive
power of t, killed by e on the right).  So w_0v_0 = 1, w_1v_1 = Σ a_j e b_j = 1,
w_0v_1 = Σ t^{m−j} e b_j = 0, w_1v_0 = Σ a_j e s^{m−j} = 0.  Conversely (PI) gives s = v_0,
t = w_0 and the single witness w_1(1 − v_0w_0)v_1 = 1.

## 2. Rank-two normal generation

Lemma.  wv = 1, ba = 1, bv = 0.  Then D = diag(1+vb, 1) = [e_12(v), e_21(b)] ∈ EL_2(R)
normally generates EL_2(R); every elementary generator is a product of at most four conjugates
of D^{±1}.  Proof: (3) the commutator computes to diag(1+vb,1) using only bv = 0;
(4) [D, e_12(ar)] = e_12((1+vb)ar − ar) = e_12(vr) since ba = 1, so e_12(vR) ⊆ N;
(5) f = 1 − vw is an idempotent with fv = wf = 0, g = vfw is an orthogonal idempotent,
z = vf + fw + 1 − f − g satisfies z² = 1 and zf = vf; (6) diag(z, z⁻¹) =
e_12(z)e_21(−z⁻¹)e_12(z)·e_12(−1)e_21(1)e_12(−1), so h = diag(z,z) ∈ EL_2(R);
(7) h e_12(fr) h⁻¹ = e_12(zfrz) = e_12(v(frz)) ∈ N; then e_12(r) = e_12(vwr)e_12(fr) ∈ N, and
the signed permutation e_12(1)e_21(−1)e_12(1) conjugates upper roots to lower roots.  No
property (T) is used; EL_2(R) is perfect under (PI).

## 3. Higher rank inside rank two

Under (PI) choose S_1..S_m, T_1..T_m with T_iS_j = δ_ij (distinct words of a common length in
the v's, reversed words in the w's; m = 4 from the length-two words).  p = Σ S_iT_i.
j(A) = 1 − p + Σ_{ij} S_i A_ij T_j is an injective homomorphism GL_m(R) → R^× ((1−p)S_i = 0,
T_j(1−p) = 0, T_i j(A) S_j = A_ij).  Ψ(A) = diag(j(A), 1); for i ≠ j,
Ψ(e_ij(r)) = diag(1 + S_i r T_j, 1) = [e_12(S_i r), e_21(T_j)] ∈ EL_2(R) (only T_jS_i = 0 is
used).  Ψ(e_12(1)) = diag(1 + S_1T_2, 1) normally generates EL_2(R) by §2 with
v = S_1, w = T_1, a = S_2, b = T_2.  Rank-descent principle: EL_2(R) is normally generated by an
embedded copy of EL_m(R) for every m ≥ 2; so triviality of all homomorphisms from some EL_m(R)
into a class of groups gives it for every EL_n(R), n ≥ 2.

## 4. The theorem at n ≥ 2

ρ : EL_2(R) → M with M MF.  ρ∘Ψ : EL_4(R) → M is trivial by the rank-four theorem, so
ker ρ ∋ Ψ(e_12(1)), whose normal closure is EL_2(R).  For n ≥ 2 every root of EL_n(R) lies in an
embedded EL_2(R).

## 5. One finitely generated source

With S the unital subring generated by v_0, v_1, w_0, w_1 (full with the single witness
w_1(1−v_0w_0)v_1 = 1), the rank-four theorem for S plus Ψ : EL_4(S) → EL_2(R) already gives
the theorem for R, using conjugators from EL_2(R).

## 6. Consequences

For R = L_{F_2}(1,2): x ↦ (t_i x s_j) is a ring isomorphism R ≅ M_2(R), so
EL_2(R) ≅ EL_2(M_2(R)) = EL_4(R) (cross-block roots are block-elementary; a within-block root
is a Steinberg commutator of two cross-block roots through the other block).  So the headline
transfers to EL_2 without a rank-two (T) or normal-structure citation.  For every nonzero ring
under the theorem, j embeds the non-MF group EL_4(R) into R^×, so R^× and every GL_n(R), n ≥ 1,
are not MF (non-MF, not full collapse).  C*_r(EL_n(R)) separable, stably finite, not MF for
n ≥ 2; C*_max(EL_n(R)) contains a proper isometry via the embedded strict compression.
Quantitatively, ℓ(ρ(e_ij(r))) ≤ 4 ℓ(ρ(D)) for any conjugation-invariant subadditive length.
