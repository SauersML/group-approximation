# A monomial frame makes the marked Kun--Thom orbit amenable

Date: 2026-08-12

## 1. Outcome

Replace the standard exponent lattice `Z^3` in the square-zero Kun--Thom
pair by the ordered-two-frame lattice

`Lambda=M_(3 times 2)(Z)~=Z^6`,                       `(AMF1)`

with `SL_3(Z)` acting by left multiplication. There is a positive exponent
`u in N^6` whose strict predecessor `v=t^(-1)u` has a negative coordinate
and whose stabilizer is exactly `Z^2`.

This produces a new explicit pair `Gamma<G` with all the established
Kazhdan, residual-finiteness, infranormality, Baumslag--Solitar Bockstein,
and sofic-radical properties. In addition, the actual marked tangent orbit
has amenable stabilizer and its monomials span the literal permutation
module

`A[L/H_*]`, `H_*~=Z^2`.                                `(AMF2)`

Unlike a finite-dimensional coefficient tag, the monomial frame has no
short linear recurrence: distinct orbit points are linearly independent
Laurent monomials. Thus it simultaneously removes the nonamenable
stabilizer and preserves the genuine long branch cut.

The remaining FALSE gate is extension from this exact marked permutation
submodule to a normalized-Hilbert--Schmidt almost-representation of the full
elementary semidirect product. Exact finite Laurent quotients still absorb
the marked class by the Kazhdan cyclic-seed theorem, so the extension must
use the root-dependent orbit charts rather than one global congruence
quotient.

## 2. The exponent representation and its positive cone

Fix an odd prime `p`, put

`q=p+1`, `A=Z[1/q]`,                                   `(AMF3)`

and form

`C=A directSum A^3`, `(A^3)^2=0`,                      `(AMF4)`

with `Q=SL_3(A)` acting standardly on the square-zero summand.

Let

`L=SL_3(Z)`                                           `(AMF5)`

act on `Lambda` in `(AMF1)` by applying the same matrix to both columns.
Write `Lambda_+=N^6` in the standard matrix coordinates, and use group-ring
notation

`R_+=C[Lambda_+]~=C[y_11,y_21,y_31,y_12,y_22,y_32]`,

`R=C[Lambda]`
` ~=C[y_11^(+-1),...,y_32^(+-1)].`                    `(AMF6)`

Every nonnegative elementary transvection `I+e_ij` has a nonnegative
matrix on both copies of `Z^3`, so it sends `Lambda_+` into itself and
compresses `R_+`.

For even `r>=4`, set

`Gamma=E_r(R_+) semidirect Q`,

`G=E_r(R) semidirect (Q times L).`                     `(AMF7)`

## 3. The global pair

**Theorem 1.** The groups in `(AMF7)` are residually finite Kazhdan groups.
The subgroup `Gamma<G` is infranormal and nonnormal, and

`normalClosure_G(Gamma)=E_r(R) semidirect Q=ker(G->L).` `(AMF8)`

**Proof.** Both rings in `(AMF6)` are finitely generated commutative unital
rings, so the universal-lattice property-`(T)` theorem applies to their
elementary groups. The groups `Q` and `L` have property `(T)`, and extension
stability gives property `(T)` for `(AMF7)`.

The nonnegative elementary transvections belong to the compression
semigroup and generate `L` as a group. It remains only to check that the
`L`-conjugates of the positive coefficient ring generate the full Laurent
ring. Every positive coordinate monomial belongs to `R_+`. For each row
coordinate `i`, a determinant-one diagonal sign matrix sends `e_i` to
`-e_i`; applied to a monomial supported in either column, it produces the
inverse coordinate monomial. Hence the ring generated by the conjugates of
`R_+` contains all twelve positive and inverse Laurent generators and is
`R`. Elementary additivity and

`[e_ij(a),e_jk(b)]=e_ik(ab)`                           `(AMF9)`

then show that the conjugate root groups generate `E_r(R)`. This proves
the compression-semigroup assertion, infranormality, and `(AMF8)`.

Nonnormality follows from the explicit strict predecessor in Section 4.

For residual finiteness, reduce `A` modulo a prime not dividing `q`, reduce
`Lambda` modulo `m Lambda`, and use

`C_ell[Lambda/m Lambda]`                               `(AMF10)`

with the compatible finite images of `Q` and `L`. Varying the prime and
`m` separates every Laurent matrix entry and every external component.
End proof.

## 4. A positive frame with a strict amenable predecessor

Use

`t=I+e_12`, `s=I+e_13 in L`.                           `(AMF11)`

Regard an element of `Lambda` as an ordered pair of columns and put

`v=(e_1,e_2-e_1)`,

`u=t v=(e_1,e_2).`                                     `(AMF12)`

Then

`u in Lambda_+`, `v notin Lambda_+`,

`t^(-1)u=v`, `s v=v`.                                  `(AMF13)`

Let `a=e_1` in the square-zero coefficient `A^3`, let

`P=diag(1,-1,1,-1,...,1,-1)`,                          `(AMF14)`

and define

`gamma=1+a y^u P in Gamma`,

`h=t^(-1)gamma t=1+a y^v P in G\Gamma`.               `(AMF15)`

Membership in the elementary groups follows from the usual square-zero
two-by-two diagonal factorization. The negative `(row 1,column 2)` entry of
`v` proves strictness.

Put

`c_0=diag(q,1,q^(-1)) in Q`, `c=(c_0,s)`.              `(AMF16)`

The element `c_0` scales `a` by `q`, while `s` fixes `v`. Therefore

`c h c^(-1)=h^q`.                                      `(AMF17)`

As before, the external projection of `c` and the square-zero powers of
`h` prove

`B=<h,c>~=BS(1,q).`                                    `(AMF18)`

## 5. Exact amenable stabilizer and permutation orbit

Let

`H_*=Stab_L(v).`                                       `(AMF19)`

Fixing the first column `e_1` and the second column `e_2-e_1` is equivalent
to fixing `e_1,e_2`. Hence

`H_*={I+a e_13+b e_23:a,b in Z}~=Z^2.`                `(AMF20)`

In particular, `H_*` is amenable and contains `s`.

Laurent monomials are a basis of `C[Lambda]`. Therefore

`gH_* |-> y^(gv)`                                      `(AMF21)`

extends to an injective `A[L]`-module map

`A[L/H_*] -> A[Lambda].`                               `(AMF22)`

Its image is exactly the `A`-linear span of the marked monomial orbit.
Thus the marked orbit is not merely parametrized by an amenable homogeneous
space: it is an actual permutation submodule of the tangent coefficients.

Since `L` is residually finite and `H_*` is amenable,

`L curvearrowright L/H_*` is a sofic action.             `(AMF23)`

The finite root-dependent charts from the amenable-stabilizer theorem now
apply directly to `(AMF21)`.

## 6. Paired cycle and radical word

The normalized bar cycle

`Z_p=sum_(j=1)^p [h^j|h]`
`    -[c|h]-[ch|c^(-1)]+[c^(-1)|c]`                  `(AMF24)`

has

`partial Z_p=p[h]`,

`partial_chi Z_p=(1-zeta_p)[c^(-1)]`,                 `(AMF25)`

for `chi(h)=zeta_p`, `chi(c)=1`. The Fox calculation for `BS(1,q)` again
makes both Bocksteins injective.

Every conjugate of `Gamma` has trivial external projection. An element of
`B` with stable-letter exponent `m` projects to `s^m`, so

`B cap g Gamma g^(-1)<=ker(B->Z)`                     `(AMF26)`

for every `g`. The cyclotomic stabilizer argument therefore proves that
`[Z_p]` survives every double-coset stabilizer exactly as in
`FALSE_RADICAL_BS_PAIRED_KUN_THOM_DOUBLE.md`.

For

`D=G *_Gamma G`, `w=i_2(h)i_1(h)^(-1)`,               `(AMF27)`

equation `(AMF8)`, the Kun--Thom radical theorem, and amalgam normal form
give

`1!=w in Rad_sof(D).`                                  `(AMF28)`

## 7. Why this is better than a finite coefficient tag

The finite coefficient-frame construction in
`FALSE_AMENABLE_FRAME_TAGGED_KUN_THOM_PAIR.md` already cuts the marked
stabilizer down to `Z^2`, but its tag satisfies the fixed recurrence

`t^(-1)u_0=(2-t)u_0`.                                  `(AMF29)`

It therefore does not create a new branch-cut direction.

Here the tag is the exponent itself. Distinct cosets in `L/H_*` give
distinct basis monomials by `(AMF21)`. In particular there is no nonzero
finite relation

`sum_j a_j y^(g_jv)=0`                                 `(AMF30)`

among distinct orbit points. The only collision appears after passing to a
finite exponent quotient, where it occurs at a depth that may tend to
infinity. This is precisely the high-order regime in which the long paired
Fourier chain operates.

## 8. Remaining analytic lemma

The marked orbit now satisfies the exact hypotheses needed by the
amenable-stabilizer chart theorem and the no-dilution estimate:

1. its local stabilizer is `Z^2`;
2. its orbit labels are linearly independent before finite truncation;
3. the paired Fourier block has exact local marked energy two; and
4. bad chart roots may have density tending to zero without diluting that
   energy.

What remains is a relative finite-window extension theorem:

> Extend the paired map on the permutation tangent submodule `(AMF22)` to
> the fixed finite window of `E_r(R) semidirect Q`, with normalized-HS
> multiplicativity and covariance defects tending to zero, while the two
> extensions agree on the positive subring window.

The extension cannot factor through one honest finite Laurent ring: the
natural finite-image absorption and Kazhdan cyclic-seed theorems would then
kill the fold. It must retain the root-dependent orbit charts through the
elementary Steinberg relations. Proving this one extension theorem, followed
by orbit-chart induction, gives the compatible pair with marked squared gap
`2-o(1)` and therefore proves FALSE.
