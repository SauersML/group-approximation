# Every bounded multiple of the canonical discriminant class escapes

Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Outcome

Let finite presentation covers converge locally to an infinite cover in
which a fixed lifted word has infinite order in integral first homology.
Suppose that the minimum real fillings of the word in the finite covers
remain bounded.  The integral obstruction is then represented by the
canonical cycle-discriminant classes `c_n`.

The usual filling dichotomy only states that

`rho(c_n)->infinity`.                                      `(DME1)`

There is a stronger conclusion:

`min_(1<=k<=K) rho(k c_n)->infinity`                       `(DME2)`

for every fixed `K`.  In particular,

`ord(c_n)->infinity`.                                      `(DME3)`

Thus the bounded-real branch cannot be caused by a fixed-order torsion
class translated farther out in a growing lattice.  Every bounded nonzero
multiple of the marked class escapes simultaneously.  Any phase mechanism
which stays on the cyclic direction generated by `c_n` must therefore use
genuinely growing order.

For the fixed-prime Laurent tower this applies in both the absolute and
constant-stabilizer intermediate covers.  It does not by itself prove
FALSE: a small class outside `<c_n>` could still detect `c_n` through the
discriminant pairing.

## 2. Setup

Let `Y_n` be finite covers of one finite presentation complex which converge
locally, at compatible base vertices, to a cover `Y_infinity`.  Let

`b_n in C_1(Y_n,Z)`, `b_infinity in C_1(Y_infinity,Z)` `(DME4)`

be the compatible lifts of one fixed word.  Assume that `b_n` is an
integral boundary for every `n`, while

`[b_infinity]` has infinite order in `H_1(Y_infinity,Z)`. `(DME5)`

Write

`q_n in ker(partial_(2,n))^perp`                       `(DME6)`

for the unique minimum real filling of `b_n`.  If `z_n` is any integral
filling, the orthogonal decomposition

`z_n=q_n+u_n`                                         `(DME7)`

defines the canonical class

`c_n=u_n+K_n in K_n^#/K_n`,
`K_n=ker(partial_(2,n)) intersect C_2(Y_n,Z)`.         `(DME8)`

The class is independent of `z_n`.  For every integer `k`, the canonical
real filling and discriminant class of `k b_n` are respectively

`k q_n` and `k c_n`.                                   `(DME9)`

Indeed the real filling equation is linear and its minimum solution is
unique, while multiplying `(DME7)` by `k` gives the integral gluing class.

## 3. Simultaneous multiple escape

**Theorem 1 (bounded-multiple escape).**  Assume `(DME4)--(DME5)` and

`sup_n ||q_n||_2 < infinity`.                          `(DME10)`

Then `(DME2)` holds for every positive integer `K`.

**Proof.**  Suppose otherwise.  There are a fixed `K`, a constant `C`, a
subsequence, and integers

`1<=k_n<=K`                                            `(DME11)`

such that

`rho_(K_n)(k_n c_n)<=C`.                              `(DME12)`

After passing to a further subsequence, the finite-valued sequence `k_n`
is one constant `k` with `1<=k<=K`.

The exact discriminant Pythagoras identity, applied to the loop `k b_n`,
is

`Fill_(Z,2)(k b_n)^2`
` =k^2||q_n||_2^2+rho_(K_n)(k c_n)^2`.                `(DME13)`

Equations `(DME10)` and `(DME12)` therefore give integral fillings of
`k b_n` with one uniform `ell^2` norm bound.

An integral chain of bounded `ell^2` norm has uniformly bounded support:
every nonzero coefficient contributes at least one to its squared norm.
Delete closed support components which do not meet the fixed loop.  The
remaining support then lies in one fixed combinatorial neighborhood of the
loop.  Local convergence and a subsequence give a single finite integral
two-chain in `Y_infinity` whose boundary is `k b_infinity`.

Hence `k[b_infinity]=0` in `H_1(Y_infinity,Z)`, contrary to `(DME5)`.
This proves `(DME2)`.  End proof.

**Corollary 2 (order escape).**  Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1,
`(DME3)` holds.

**Proof.**  If `ord(c_n)<=K` along a subsequence, choose
`1<=k_n<=K` with `k_n c_n=0`.  Then the left side of `(DME2)` is zero on
that subsequence, a contradiction.  End proof.

The same proof gives the following useful uniform form.  For every finite
set `A subset Z minus {0}`,

`min_(k in A) rho_(K_n)(k c_n)->infinity`.             `(DME14)`

No divisibility or primary decomposition of the discriminant groups is
used.

## 4. Application to the fixed-prime Laurent tower

Use the notation of `FALSE_FIXED_PRIME_CONSTANT_STABILIZER_TOWER.md`:

`K_n=pi^(-1)(L_n)`, `H_n=pi^(-1)(J L_n)`,             `(DME15)`

with limiting subgroups

`pi^(-1)(E(p))`, `pi^(-1)(J E(p))`.                  `(DME16)`

The marked word has infinite order in the abelianization of both limiting
groups.  In the bounded-real branch of Theorem 6 of that note, Theorem 1
therefore applies twice.  If `c_(K,n)` and `c_(H,n)` are the two canonical
classes, then for every fixed `K`,

`min_(1<=k<=K)rho(k c_(K,n))->infinity`,
`min_(1<=k<=K)rho(k c_(H,n))->infinity`.              `(DME17)`

In particular both orders tend to infinity, even though the transfer degree

`|J/(J intersect L_n)|=|SL_3(F_p)|`                  `(DME18)`

is fixed.  Thus the remaining arithmetic obstruction is not finite central
torsion coming from the constant Kazhdan block.  It is an unbounded-order
class generated by the Laurent-depth direction.

## 5. Scope

The theorem removes one possible false shortcut.  Divergence of
`rho(c_n)` cannot be converted to marked visibility merely by passing to a
fixed multiple or a fixed cyclotomic coefficient system: every such
multiple is itself far from the integral cycle lattice.

It also identifies the live possibility precisely.  A successful FALSE
phase in the bounded-real branch must either

1. use multiples whose size tends to infinity with `n`; or
2. use a different small discriminant class `d_n` with nonzero pairing
   against `c_n` and a sufficiently dense marked orbit.

Theorem 1 makes no assertion that such `d_n` exist.  That is the remaining
marked nonisotropy/incidence problem.
