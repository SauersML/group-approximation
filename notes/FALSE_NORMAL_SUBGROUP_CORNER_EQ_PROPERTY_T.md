# A normal-subgroup fixed-vector corner exists exactly for a Kazhdan subgroup

Date: 2026-08-12

## 1. Statement

Let `N normal H` be discrete groups and let

`A=C*_max(H)`,
`I_N=ker(C*_max(H)->C*_max(H/N))`.                    `(NSC1)`

Equivalently, `I_N` is the closed two-sided ideal generated by
`{u_n-1:n in N}`.

**Theorem 1 (normal-subgroup corner criterion).**  The following are
equivalent.

1. `N` has property `(T)`.
2. `I_N` is a unital C-star algebra.
3. There is a central projection `q_N in C*_max(H)` such that in every
   unitary representation `pi` of `H`, `pi(1-q_N)` is the orthogonal
   projection onto the `N`-fixed vectors.

When these conditions hold,

`I_N=q_N A=A q_N`,                                      `(NSC2)`

and `1-q_N` is the image in `C*_max(H)` of the Kazhdan projection of `N`.

The point is that relative property `(T)` for the pair `(H,N)` is not enough.
For example, `(SL_2(Z) semidirect Z^2,Z^2)` has relative property `(T)`, but
its normal-subgroup ideal is nonunital because `Z^2` does not have property
`(T)`.

## 2. Property `(T)` gives the corner

Assume `N` has property `(T)`.  The canonical map

`C*_max(N)->C*_max(H)`                                  `(NSC3)`

is injective.  Indeed every unitary representation of `N` occurs inside the
restriction to `N` of its induced representation to `H`; because `N` is
normal, the fiber over the identity coset is `N`-invariant and carries the
original representation.

Let `p_N in C*_max(N)` be the Kazhdan projection.  Its image under `(NSC3)`
is central in `A`.  To see this, conjugation by `h in H` preserves `N` and
carries `p_N` to a projection which, in every representation, again projects
onto the `N`-fixed vectors.  The universal characterization of the Kazhdan
projection therefore gives

`u_h p_N u_h^*=p_N`.                                   `(NSC4)`

Put `q_N=1-p_N`.  The quotient in `(NSC1)` sends `p_N` to one, so `q_N in
I_N`.  Conversely

`(u_n-1)p_N=0`, `n in N`,                              `(NSC5)`

and hence every generator of `I_N` belongs to `Aq_N`.  Thus `(NSC2)` holds,
and `q_N` is the unit of `I_N`.

In any representation `pi`, `pi(p_N)` is the projection onto the `N`-fixed
vectors by definition.  This proves all three assertions in this direction.

## 3. A unital ideal forces property `(T)` of `N`

Assume `I_N` has a unit `q`.  The unit of a closed two-sided ideal in a
unital C-star algebra is a central projection.  Put `p=1-q`.

For every representation `pi` of `H`, `pi(p)` is exactly the projection onto
the `N`-fixed vectors.  Indeed `(u_n-1)p=0`, so its range is fixed by `N`.
Conversely an `N`-fixed vector is annihilated by every generator of `I_N`,
hence by `q`, and is therefore fixed by `p`.

Suppose toward a contradiction that `N` does not have property `(T)`.  There
is a unitary representation

`sigma:N->U(K)`                                         `(NSC6)`

with almost invariant unit vectors and no nonzero invariant vector.  Form

`pi=Ind_N^H(sigma)`.                                    `(NSC7)`

Because `N` is normal, the restriction of `(NSC7)` to `N` is a direct sum,
over `H/N`, of conjugates of `sigma`.  None has an invariant vector, so

`pi(p)=0`, `pi(q)=1`.                                   `(NSC8)`

On the other hand, since `q in I_N`, for any `eta>0` it can be approximated
in norm by

`y=sum_(j=1)^m a_j(u_(n_j)-1)b_j`,                     `(NSC9)`

where `n_j in N` and, after a second approximation, `a_j,b_j` are finite
linear combinations of group unitaries.

Realize the induced representation on the square-summable bundle over
`H/N`.  Let `xi` be a unit vector supported on the identity coset whose
fiber value is an almost invariant vector for `sigma`.  The finite list of
coefficients in `(NSC9)` produces only finitely many cosets and only finitely
many conjugates in `N`.  Choose the fiber vector sufficiently invariant
under that finite subset of `N`.  Then

`||pi((u_(n_j)-1)b_j)xi||`                             `(NSC10)`

is as small as desired for every `j`, and consequently

`||pi(y)xi||<eta`.                                     `(NSC11)`

Choose the approximation in `(NSC9)` with `||q-y||<1/4` and then choose
`xi` so that `(NSC11)` is less than `1/4`.  Equations `(NSC8)` and `(NSC11)`
give

`1=||pi(q)xi||<1/2`,                                   `(NSC12)`

a contradiction.  Therefore `N` has property `(T)`.

This proves Theorem 1.

## 4. Consequence for the Kun--Thom lamp groups

For an infinite set `X` and a nontrivial finite group `K`, neither

`B_sum=directSum_X K` nor `B_free=freeProduct_(x in X) K_x`               `(NSC13)`

has property `(T)`.

The first group is infinite amenable, and an amenable property-`(T)` group is
finite.  The second has the Haagerup property through its proper action on
the Bass--Serre tree; an infinite group with both the Haagerup property and
property `(T)` would be finite.  Hence Theorem 1 gives

`ker(C*_max(B_sum semidirect G)->C*_max(G))` is nonunital`,
`ker(C*_max(B_free semidirect G)->C*_max(G)) is nonunital`.               `(NSC14)`

Thus there is no norm-continuous central projection which cuts all
representations into the lamp-fixed and lamp-nonfixed sectors.  This remains
true without asking whether either pair has relative property `(T)`.

Neuhauser's theorem supplies the stronger fact that the pairs themselves do
not have relative property `(T)` in the commuting-lamp case, and pullback
gives the free-lamp case; see
`FALSE_LAMP_RELATIVE_T_SOURCE_AUDIT.md`.  Theorem 1 explains the exact
C-star-algebraic threshold relevant to trace renormalization and corrects the
tempting but invalid inference

`relative (T) of (H,N) => a projection onto N-fixed vectors in C*_max(H)`.

The only subgroup-fixed Kazhdan corner available in the current construction
comes from a subgroup which itself has property `(T)`, such as the arithmetic
vertex `Gamma`.  The infinite lamp kernel cannot provide a second corner.
