Finding groups which are not G -approximated is notoriously hard. One of the motivations
to study stability comes from the following observation (see [14,2] for GHamm , but valid
in all the cases listed above).

Proposition 1.1. Let G be a class as above, where Gn are linear groups, and Γ a finitely
generated group. If Γ is G -approximated and G -stable, then it is residually finite.

   This implies that if one found a finitely generated group Γ which is G -stable and
not residually finite, then it is not G -approximated. This line of proof was implemented
ﬁrst in [9] to give examples of non-Frobenius approximated groups, i.e. groups which
are not G2 -approximated. Following this breakthrough, Thom asked in his ICM address
[20, Question 3.11] about all 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞. In response, Lubotzky and Oppenheim [17]
solved almost all cases and gave non-Gp -approximated groups for 1 < p < ∞. These
                      B. Bachner et al. / Journal of Algebra 702 (2026) 235--243                     237



examples are the so called ``ℓ-adic Deligne central extensions'', see [9, Section 5.2] for
their construction. There are thus four cases left to handle: p = 1, the Hamming distance
on permutation groups, the HS-norm and the operator norm.
   We prove that ℓ-adic Deligne central extensions are not G1 -approximated, solving the
case p = 1 in Thom’s question. Interestingly, our method is diﬀerent from the previous
works -- using a short argument, we manage to establish that these groups are not
G1 -approximated, without the need to show that they are G1 -stable. The question of
G1 -stability for these groups remains open.
   With that said, this is only modest progress as the last three cases are the holy-grail
of the subject: Groups which are GHamm -approximated are called sofic, the ones which
are GHS -approximated are called hyperlinear or Connes embeddable, and the ones which
are Gop -approximated are called MF.1 Whether all groups are sofic, hyperlinear or MF
are three major open problems in group theory. Given the previous works, it is natural
to study whether the ℓ-adic Deligne central extensions are the much desired concrete
counter examples to these three problems. The reader is referred to [7,8,15,6] for eﬀorts
to prove they are not sofic, and to [12] for eﬀorts to prove that real Deligne central
extensions are not hyperlinear.
   The novelty in our approach is the use of at least two diﬀerent norms on U(n) to
deduce inapproximability. Usually, when two diﬀerent bi-invariant metrics dn ≤ d′n are
given on Gn , it is not clear that {Gn , d′n }-approximation (respectively, {Gn , d′n }-stability)
implies {Gn , dn }-approximation (respectively, {Gn , dn }-stability), nor vice versa.
   Denote by kerRF (Γ) the kernel of the map from Γ to its profinite completion Γ.          ˆ︁ In
other words, kerRF (Γ) consists of elements in Γ that are trivial in all finite quotients.

Deﬁnition 1.2. A finitely generated group Γ is said to be of Deligne-type if there is a
central element J ∈ Γ of order 2 such that J ∈ kerRF (Γ).

   See [11] and [9, Section 5] for examples, and many more in [19,16]. Our main theorem
is a version of Proposition 1.1 which deals with two diﬀerent Schatten norms, that is
applicable to groups of Deligne-type. This refines the previously taken route of proving
stability in order to deduce inapproximability.

Theorem 1.3. Let Γ be a group of Deligne-type, and let 1 ≤ p ≤ q ≤ ∞. If Γ is Gq ­
stable, then it is not Gp -approximated. In particular, if Γ is Frobenius stable, then it is
not Gp -approximated for any 1 ≤ p ≤ 2.

   As a consequence of the construction of Deligne-type groups which are Frobenius
stable obtained in [9, Proposition 5.6 and Theorem 5.1], we have:

Corollary 1.4. There exist finitely presented groups which are not G1 -approximated.

nomena for approximability, which is of independent interest.

Proposition 1.6. Let Γ be a finitely generated group with a finite normal subgroup N such
that Γ/N is hyperlinear. If Γ is MF, then it is hyperlinear.

Proposition 1.7. Let 1 ≤ p ≤ q ≤ ∞. Let Γ be a finitely generated group with a finite
normal subgroup N such that Γ/N is Gq -approximated. If Γ is Gp -approximated, then it
is Gq -approximated.

  Let us end with suggesting the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.8. Let Γ be a finitely generated group such that H 2 (Γ, V ) = 0 for every
unitary representation of Γ on a Hilbert space V , then Γ is operator-HS-stable.

Conjecture 1.8 is true, these groups are not MF.
