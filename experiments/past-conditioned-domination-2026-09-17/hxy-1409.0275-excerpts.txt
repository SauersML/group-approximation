--- Introduction, p. 2 (definition of algebraic past) ---
G is said to be left-orderable if there exists a linear ordering in G which is invariant under
left translations. The group G is left-orderable if and only if it contains a subset Φ, called an
algebraic past of G ([32]), with the following properties:
(1) Φ ∩ Φ −1 = ∅,
(2) Φ ∪ Φ −1 ∪ {eG} = G,
(3) Φ · Φ ⊆ Φ .
Indeed, with respect to the algebraic past Φ, one obtains the desired linear ordering on G as
follows: g1 is less than g2 (write g1 <Φ g2), if g−1
2 g1 ∈ Φ.

algebraic past Φ , (X, G) be a G-system, µ ∈ M(X, G), and A be a G-invariant sub- σ-algebra
of Bµ
X. Then for any α, β ∈ P µ
X ,
hµ(G, α ∨ β|A) = hµ(G, β|A) + Hµ(α|βG ∨ αΦ ∨ A),
where βG = ⋁
g∈G gβ and αΦ = ⋁
g∈Φ gα. In particular, hµ(G, α|A) = Hµ(α|αΦ ∨ A).
