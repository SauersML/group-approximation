\title{An explicit finitely presented non-MF group}

\begin{abstract}
A countable discrete group is MF if it embeds into the unitary group of a
norm quotient $\prod_n M_{d_n}(\C)/\bigoplus_n M_{d_n}(\C)$ of matrix
algebras.  Whether every group is MF has been a longstanding open
question; it is the operator-norm case of the family of metric
approximation problems whose sofic (rank-metric) case was solved only
recently, and whose unnormalized Frobenius case was solved by De
Chiffre, Glebsky, Lubotzky and Thom.  We construct an explicit finitely
presented group $E$, together with a designated central involution
$w\in E$, such that $w\neq 1$ but \emph{every} homomorphism from $E$ to
the unitary group of \emph{every} norm quotient of matrix algebras kills
$w$.  Consequently $E$ is not MF, and neither its full nor its reduced
group $C^*$-algebra is an MF algebra.  The group is built from
$\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$, one HNN letter implementing the proper
endomorphism $(v,A)\mapsto(2v,A)$, and one additional involution; the
proof combines the Kazhdan projection of $\Gamma$ with stable finiteness
of matrix coronas.  The finite-dimensional case of the kill theorem is
elementary and uses neither property (T) nor unitarity; we also show,
via quasidiagonality of amenable groups, that the property (T)
hypothesis cannot be dropped.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $(d_n)_{n\in\N}$ be a sequence of positive integers and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corona}
  \mathcal Q\;=\;\prod_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)\Big/\bigoplus_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)
\end{equation}
be the associated \emph{norm matrix corona}: bounded sequences of
matrices modulo sequences converging to $0$ in operator norm.  Following
Blackadar--Kirchberg \cite{BK}, a separable $C^*$-algebra is \emph{MF}
if it embeds into some algebra of the form \eqref{eq:corona}; following
the group-theoretic usage (see, e.g., \cite{Shulman}), a countable
discrete group $E$ is \emph{MF} if there is an \emph{injective} group
homomorphism
\[
  E\;\longrightarrow\;\U(\mathcal Q)
\]
for some choice of $(d_n)$.  Equivalently, $E$ admits finite-dimensional
approximate unitary representations that are approximately injective,
with all defects measured in operator norm.  Residually finite groups
are MF, amenable groups are MF as a consequence of the
Tikuisis--White--Winter theorem \cite{TWW}, free products of amenable
groups are MF, and a substantial permanence theory exists
\cite{Shulman}.  Whether \emph{every} countable group is MF has remained
open; it is the operator-norm member of the family of approximation
problems surveyed in \cite{DGLT,BDL}, whose unnormalized-Frobenius case
was solved negatively in \cite{DGLT} and whose sofic case was solved
negatively by Kun and Thom \cite{KT}.  The operator-norm case resisted
both methods: the cohomological argument of \cite{DGLT} needs a
submultiplicative norm interacting with a trace, and rank-metric
arguments are blind to operator-norm defects.

This paper answers the question negatively, with an explicit finitely
presented example.

\begin{mainthm}[non-MF group]\label{thm:A}
Let $\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$, let
$\alpha\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma$ be the injective endomorphism
$\alpha(v,A)=(2v,A)$, and let $a=(e_1,1)\in\Gamma$, which does not lie
in $\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Let $E$ be the finitely presented group obtained
from $\Gamma$ by adjoining a stable letter $t$ with
$t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma$, an involution $c$
commuting with $\Gamma$, and by imposing that
\[
  w\;:=\;\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a\,(tct^{-1})\,a^{-1}\bigr]
\]
is a central involution (Definition~\ref{def:E}).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $w\neq 1$ in $E$;
  \item for every sequence $(d_n)$ and every group homomorphism
    $\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ into the corresponding norm matrix
    corona \eqref{eq:corona}, one has $\Theta(w)=1$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $E$ is not MF, and neither $\Cmax(E)$ nor $\Cred(E)$ is an
MF $C^*$-algebra.
\end{mainthm}

The mechanism is a marked-word conversion of a $C^*$-algebraic
non-finiteness phenomenon into a group-level kernel theorem.  It has
five steps, each elementary or classical in isolation.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item\emph{Central corner.}  If $\Theta(w)\neq 1$, then
    $e=\tfrac12(1-\Theta(w))$ is a nonzero projection commuting with the
    image of $\Theta$; cutting by a lift of $e$ replaces $\mathcal Q$ by
    another norm matrix corona on which $\Theta(w)=-1$
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:corner}).  This cut eliminates all relative-rank
    (``rank blindness'') phenomena: on the corner, the deviation of $w$
    from $1$ has full normalized size.
  \item\emph{Conjugation representation.}  Unitary lifts $U_{g,n}$ of
    the corner representation conjugate the corner coordinates
    $M_{r_n}(\C)$, and operator-norm multiplicativity of $\Theta$ makes
    $g\mapsto (\Ad U_{g,n})_n$ an \emph{exact} homomorphism $\beta$ into
    the norm corona of the algebras $B(L^2(M_{r_n}))$
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:beta}).
  \item\emph{Kazhdan projection.}  $\Gamma$ has property (T), so its
    maximal group $C^*$-algebra contains the Kazhdan projection
    $p_\Gamma$, whose image under every representation is the projection
    onto the invariant vectors.  The HNN relation
    $t\Gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\Gamma)\subseteq\Gamma$ forces, in
    \emph{every} $C^*$-representation, the order relation
    $P\le\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$ between the images of $p_\Gamma$ and its
    $t$-conjugate (Lemma~\ref{lem:order}).
  \item\emph{Stable finiteness.}  A norm corona of matrix algebras is
    finite: isometries are unitaries (Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}).  Hence
    the two unitarily equivalent projections in step (3) must be equal:
    $\beta(t)$ \emph{preserves} the $\Gamma$-fixed space.
  \item\emph{The marked word.}  The lamp $c$ commutes with $\Gamma$, so
    the matrix vector representing $\Theta(c)$ is $\Gamma$-fixed; by
    step (4) so is the vector representing $\Theta(tct^{-1})$; hence
    $a\in\Gamma$ asymptotically fixes it, and the commutator word $w$
    is forced to $1$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm --- while the
    corner normalization of step (1) says it equals $-1$ in operator
    norm.  The two conclusions contradict each other
    (Section~\ref{sec:proofA}).
\end{enumerate}

Nontriviality of $w$ is witnessed by a completely explicit
representation of $E$ into a semidirect product of a Clifford group by a
linear group over $\Z[1/2]$ (Section~\ref{sec:witness}); no
combinatorial theory of HNN extensions is needed.

Two companion results delimit the mechanism precisely.  First, in
finite dimensions the kill theorem is elementary and needs neither
property (T), nor unitarity, nor centrality of $w$:

\begin{mainthm}[finite-dimensional sterility]\label{thm:B}
Let $k$ be a field, $V$ a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, $H$ a
group, $\Gamma\le H$ a subgroup, and $t,c\in H$, $a\in\Gamma$ elements
with $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ and $c\gamma=\gamma c$ for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$.  Then every homomorphism $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$
satisfies
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)\;=\;1 .
\]
\end{mainthm}

Applied to $H=E$, Theorem~\ref{thm:B} shows that \emph{every}
finite-dimensional representation of $E$ over \emph{every} field kills
$w$: the group $E$ is not residually finite-dimensional, no finite
quotient can witness $w\neq 1$, and the infinite Clifford witness of
Section~\ref{sec:witness} is not an artifact of the proof but a
necessity.  Property (T) enters the corona case for exactly one reason:
it makes ``the projection onto the $\Gamma$-fixed space'' an element of
the corona, uniformly across all coordinates at once.

Second, the property (T) hypothesis cannot be dropped:

\begin{mainthm}[necessity of property (T)]\label{thm:C}
Let $G_0=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)=\langle\, \gamma_0,t\mid
t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2\,\rangle$, let $\Gamma_0=\langle
\gamma_0\rangle\cong\Z$, let $X_0=G_0/\Gamma_0$, and let
$E_0=C(X_0)\rtimes G_0$ be the corresponding Clifford extension
(Construction~\ref{con:clifford}), with central sign $z_0$.  Then
$z_0\neq1$, the group $E_0$ is amenable, and $E_0$ \emph{is} MF: it
embeds into a norm matrix corona, with $z_0$ surviving.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, every finite-dimensional representation of $E_0$
nevertheless kills $z_0$; thus $E_0$ is an amenable MF group that is not
residually finite-dimensional.
\end{mainthm}

Theorem~\ref{thm:C} is the exact analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with
the Kazhdan group $\Gamma$ replaced by $\Z$: every ingredient of the
construction survives except the Kazhdan projection, and the conclusion
fails.  The obstruction of this paper is therefore genuinely a property
(T) phenomenon, not an artifact of lamps, signs, or HNN compression.

\subsection*{What this paper does not prove}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} does \emph{not} show that $E$ fails the
Connes embedding property or hyperlinearity, and it does not touch the
question whether hyperlinear groups are sofic.  The finiteness step (4)
is special to operator-norm coronas: in a tracial (Hilbert--Schmidt)
matrix ultraproduct the conjugation representation acts on the infinite
Hilbert space $L^2$ of the ultraproduct, the relevant fixed-space
projections live in a properly infinite algebra, and a projection there
can be properly contained in a unitarily equivalent one --- the
unilateral-shift phenomenon.  Section~\ref{sec:limits} makes this
precise.  The same distinction explains why the group $E/\langle
w\rangle$ and the ambient groups $G$ remain untouched by our argument.

\subsection*{External inputs}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} consumes exactly two classical facts
beyond elementary linear algebra and $C^*$-basics: property (T) of
$\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ (Fact~\ref{fact:T}) and the existence and
defining property of the Kazhdan projection (Fact~\ref{fact:kazhdan}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:C} additionally uses quasidiagonality of amenable
group $C^*$-algebras \cite{TWW} (Fact~\ref{fact:tww}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} consumes nothing.

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}
That $\Cmax$ of a suitable group can fail to be stably finite --- so
that $\Cmax$ itself is not MF --- is a known consequence of Kazhdan
projections and compression, in the spirit of the non-quasidiagonality
results of \cite{CDE}; but non-MF-ness of one group $C^*$-algebra does
not obstruct the \emph{group} from embedding into a corona, since group
MF-ness only requires some quotient of $\Cmax$ to be MF.  The new
ingredient here is the marked central involution $w$: the additional
generator $c$ converts the compression phenomenon into a specific
nontrivial group element that every corona representation must kill.
For the necessity theorem we use \cite{TWW}; for context on the
approximation landscape see \cite{DGLT,BDL,KT,Shulman}.

\section{Notation and external inputs}\label{sec:prelim}

All groups are discrete and countable.  For a unital $C^*$-algebra $A$
we write $\U(A)$ for its unitary group.  On $M_r(\C)$ we use the
normalized trace $\tr_r$, the operator norm $\opnorm{\cdot}$, and the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
$\hsnorm{x}=\tr_r(x^*x)^{1/2}$.  We will use three elementary facts
without further comment: $\hsnorm{x}\le\opnorm{x}$;
$|\tr_r(x)|\le\opnorm{x}$; and $\hsnorm{uxv}=\hsnorm{x}$ for unitaries
$u,v$.

Given a sequence $(d_n)_{n\in J}$ of positive integers indexed by an
infinite set $J\subseteq\N$, we write
\[
  \mathcal Q\bigl((d_n)_{n\in J}\bigr)
  \;=\;\prod_{n\in J} M_{d_n}(\C)\Big/\bigoplus_{n\in J} M_{d_n}(\C),
\]
the quotient of bounded sequences by sequences with
$\opnorm{x_n}\to0$.  We call any such algebra a \emph{norm matrix
corona}.  A \emph{corona representation} of a group $E$ is a group
homomorphism $E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ for some norm matrix corona
$\mathcal Q$; the group $E$ is \emph{MF} if it admits an injective
corona representation.

For a group $H$, $\Cmax(H)$ denotes the maximal group $C^*$-algebra and
$u_h\in\Cmax(H)$ the canonical unitaries.  Every unitary representation
$\pi$ of $H$ on a Hilbert space extends uniquely to a
$*$-representation of $\Cmax(H)$, still denoted $\pi$.  Every group
homomorphism $\varphi\colon H_1\to H_2$ induces a unital
$*$-homomorphism $\Cmax(H_1)\to\Cmax(H_2)$, $u_h\mapsto
u_{\varphi(h)}$.

We use the commutator convention $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$.

The following two classical facts are the only external inputs to
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

\begin{fact}[{property (T); \cite[Sections 1.4--1.5 and 4.2]{BHV}}]\label{fact:T}
$\SL_3(\Z)$ has property (T), the pair
$\bigl(\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z),\,\Z^3\bigr)$ has relative property (T),
and consequently $\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ has property (T).
$\Gamma$ is finitely presented.
\end{fact}

\begin{fact}[{Kazhdan projection; \cite{AW}, \cite[Chapter 6]{BHV}}]\label{fact:kazhdan}
Let $\Gamma$ be a group with property (T).  There is a projection
$p_\Gamma\in\Cmax(\Gamma)$ such that for every unitary representation
$\pi$ of $\Gamma$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, the operator
$\pi(p_\Gamma)$ is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
$\Fix(\pi)=\{\xi\in\mathcal H:\pi(\gamma)\xi=\xi
\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\}$.  Moreover $u_\gamma
p_\Gamma=p_\Gamma$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$.
\end{fact}

The last identity follows from the defining property: in every
representation, $\pi(u_\gamma p_\Gamma)$ and $\pi(p_\Gamma)$ agree, and
$\Cmax(\Gamma)$ has a faithful representation.

The only additional input, used exclusively in
Section~\ref{sec:necessity}, is:

\begin{fact}[{\cite[Corollary B]{TWW}; see also \cite[Chapter 7]{BO}}]\label{fact:tww}
The reduced group $C^*$-algebra of a countable amenable group is
quasidiagonal.  Every separable quasidiagonal $C^*$-algebra embeds into
a norm matrix corona; in particular every countable amenable group is
MF.
\end{fact}

\section{The group}\label{sec:group}

Throughout, $\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ with multiplication
$(v,A)(w,B)=(v+Aw,\,AB)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:alpha}
The map $\alpha(v,A)=(2v,A)$ is an injective endomorphism of $\Gamma$
with image $2\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ of index $8$, and
$a:=(e_1,1)\notin\alpha(\Gamma)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We compute
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \alpha\bigl((v,A)(w,B)\bigr)&=\alpha(v+Aw,\,AB)=(2v+2Aw,\,AB)\\
  &=(2v,A)(2w,B)=\alpha(v,A)\,\alpha(w,B),
\end{aligned}
\]
so $\alpha$ is a homomorphism,
clearly injective, with image $2\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$; the index equals
$[\Z^3:2\Z^3]=8$.  If $(e_1,1)=(2v,A)$ then $A=1$ and $e_1=2v$, which is
impossible in $\Z^3$.
\end{proof}

Let $G$ denote the ascending HNN extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
  G\;=\;\bigl\langle\,\Gamma,\;t\;\big|\;
  t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)\ (\gamma\in\Gamma)\,\bigr\rangle .
\end{equation}
We will use $G$ only as an auxiliary group receiving no injectivity
claims; every statement we need about it is either a presentation
identity or is verified inside an explicit linear quotient
(Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}).

\begin{definition}\label{def:E}
Fix a finite presentation $\Gamma=\langle S\mid R\rangle$ and, for each
$s\in S$, a word $\alpha(s)$ in $S$ representing the image of $s$ under
$\alpha$.  Let $a\in\Gamma$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:alpha}, written as a
word in $S$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
  E=\Bigl\langle\, S,\,t,\,c \;\Big|\;
  \begin{aligned}
   & R,\quad tst^{-1}=\alpha(s)\ (s\in S),\quad c^2=1,\quad [c,s]=1\ (s\in S),\\
   & w^2=1,\quad [w,s]=[w,t]=[w,c]=1\ (s\in S)
  \end{aligned}
  \,\Bigr\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $w$ abbreviates the word
$\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\,a\,(tct^{-1})\,a^{-1}\bigr]$.
\end{definition}

Since $S$ and $R$ are finite, $E$ is finitely presented.  The
presentation identities give a canonical homomorphism $G\to E$ and a
canonical (not a priori injective) homomorphism $\Gamma\to E$; we write
$\gamma$, $t$, $c$ for the images in $E$.  In $E$ the element $w$ is a
central involution by fiat, and the relations
$t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$, $[c,\gamma]=1$ hold for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$ because they hold on generators.

\section{The witness: \texorpdfstring{$w\neq 1$}{w is nontrivial}}\label{sec:witness}

\begin{construction}[Clifford lamp groups]\label{con:clifford}
Let $X$ be a set and let $\Cl(X)$ denote the real Clifford algebra of
the free vector space $\R^{(X)}$ with the quadratic form for which the
standard basis $(\varepsilon_x)_{x\in X}$ is orthonormal.  Thus
$\Cl(X)$ is a unital associative $\R$-algebra containing elements
$c_x:=\varepsilon_x$ with
\[
  c_x^2=1,\qquad c_xc_y=-\,c_yc_x\quad(x\neq y).
\]
The algebra $\Cl(X)$ is nonzero (for finite $X$ it has dimension
$2^{|X|}$, and in general it is the directed union of the
$\Cl(F)$ over finite $F\subseteq X$), so $-1\neq 1$ in $\Cl(X)$.  Let
$C(X)\le\Cl(X)^{\times}$ be the subgroup generated by
$\{\pm1\}\cup\{c_x:x\in X\}$ and write $z=-1\in C(X)$, a central
involution.  Every permutation of $X$ induces an isometry of the
quadratic space, hence an algebra automorphism of $\Cl(X)$ mapping
$c_x\mapsto c_{\sigma(x)}$ and fixing $z$; this gives an action of any
group acting on $X$ by automorphisms of $C(X)$, and a semidirect
product $C(X)\rtimes G$ for any $G$-set $X$.
\end{construction}

\begin{lemma}[linear model]\label{lem:linear}
Embed $\Gamma\hookrightarrow\GL_4(\Z)$ by
$(v,A)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}A&v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and let
$D=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)\in\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$.  Then
$D\gamma D^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$, so
$\bar G:=\langle\Gamma,D\rangle\le\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$ is a quotient of $G$
via $t\mapsto D$.  In the $\bar G$-set $\bar X:=\bar G/\Gamma$ the
cosets $D\Gamma$ and $aD\Gamma$ are distinct.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$D\begin{pmatrix}A&v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}D^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}A&2v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, which is the matrix of
$\alpha(v,A)$; so the assignment $\gamma\mapsto\gamma$, $t\mapsto D$
respects the relations \eqref{eq:G}.  If $aD\Gamma=D\Gamma$ then
$D^{-1}aD\in\Gamma$ inside $\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$.  But
$D^{-1}aD=\begin{pmatrix}1&\tfrac12 e_1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has a
non-integral entry, so it does not lie in the integral group $\Gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:witness}
$w\neq1$ in $E$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\bar X=\bar G/\Gamma$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} and form
$W:=C(\bar X)\rtimes\bar G$ as in Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.
Define a map on the generators of \eqref{eq:E} by
\[
  s\mapsto s\in\bar G\quad(s\in S),\qquad t\mapsto D\in\bar G,\qquad
  c\mapsto c_{o},\quad o:=\Gamma\in\bar X .
\]
We check the relations.  The relations $R$ and
$tst^{-1}=\alpha(s)$ hold in $\bar G$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}.  We
have $c_o^2=1$.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we have $\gamma o=o$, so
$\gamma c_o\gamma^{-1}=c_{\gamma o}=c_o$, giving $[c,s]=1$ for $s\in S$.
Under this assignment,
\[
  tct^{-1}\mapsto c_{D\Gamma},\qquad
  a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\mapsto c_{aD\Gamma},
\]
and the two sites are distinct by Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}, so
\[
  w\mapsto[c_{D\Gamma}\,,\,c_{aD\Gamma}]
  = c_{D\Gamma}c_{aD\Gamma}c_{D\Gamma}^{-1}c_{aD\Gamma}^{-1}
  = -1 = z .
\]
Since $z$ is a central involution of $W$, the remaining relations
$w^2=1$ and $[w,s]=[w,t]=[w,c]=1$ are also satisfied.  Hence the
assignment extends to a group homomorphism $E\to W$ sending $w$ to
$z\neq1$, and therefore $w\neq1$ in $E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The witness uses only matrices over $\Z[1/2]$ and finite pieces of a
Clifford algebra; no normal-form theory for HNN extensions enters.  In
particular the consistency of the presentation \eqref{eq:E} is verified
by an explicit linear-algebraic model.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite-dimensional sterility}\label{sec:findim}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.  Everything happens inside the
finite-dimensional algebra $\End_k(V)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
Let $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$ be a homomorphism and let
\[
  C\;=\;\{\,x\in\End_k(V)\;:\;\pi(\gamma)\,x=x\,\pi(\gamma)
  \text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\,\}
\]
be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$, a linear subspace of $\End_k(V)$.
Let $u=\pi(t)$ and consider the linear automorphism
$\Phi(x)=uxu^{-1}$ of $\End_k(V)$.

We claim $C\subseteq\Phi(C)$.  Let $x\in C$ and set $y=u^{-1}xu$, so
that $x=\Phi(y)$.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we have
$t\gamma t^{-1}\in\Gamma$, hence $\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})$ commutes with
$x$, and therefore
\[
  \pi(\gamma)\,y
  = u^{-1}\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,x\,u
  = u^{-1}\,x\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,u
  = y\,\pi(\gamma).
\]
Thus $y\in C$, proving the claim.  Since $\Phi$ is a linear
isomorphism, $\dim_k\Phi(C)=\dim_kC<\infty$, and the inclusion
$C\subseteq\Phi(C)$ forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinning}
  C\;=\;\Phi(C)\;=\;uCu^{-1}.
\end{equation}

Now $\pi(c)\in C$ because $c$ centralizes $\Gamma$.  By
\eqref{eq:pinning}, $\pi(tct^{-1})=u\,\pi(c)\,u^{-1}\in C$.  Since
$a\in\Gamma$, the element $\pi(tct^{-1})$ commutes with $\pi(a)$, so
$\pi\bigl(a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr)=\pi(tct^{-1})$, and therefore
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)
  =\bigl[\,\pi(tct^{-1}),\;\pi(tct^{-1})\bigr]=1. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:notRFD}
The group $E$ of Definition~\ref{def:E} admits no injective
homomorphism into $\GL(V)$ for any finite-dimensional vector space $V$
over any field.  In particular $E$ is not residually
finite-dimensional, not maximally almost periodic, and not residually
finite, and no finite quotient of $E$ can witness $w\neq1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with $H=E$ and the images of $\Gamma$, $t$,
$c$, $a$ in $E$; the hypotheses hold by the defining relations
\eqref{eq:E}.  Every finite-dimensional representation kills $w$, and
$w\neq1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  A finite quotient is a
finite-dimensional representation (via any faithful permutation
representation), so it also kills $w$.
\end{proof}

\section{Norm matrix coronas}\label{sec:corona}

Throughout this section $\mathcal Q=\mathcal Q((d_n)_{n\in J})$ is a
norm matrix corona, $q\colon\prod_{n\in J}M_{d_n}(\C)\to\mathcal Q$ the
quotient map.

\begin{lemma}[lifting]\label{lem:lift}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every projection $e\in\mathcal Q$ lifts to a sequence
    $(e_n)_{n\in J}$ of projections, $e_n\in M_{d_n}(\C)$.
  \item Every unitary $u\in\mathcal Q$ lifts to a sequence of unitaries.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Lift $e$ to a bounded sequence $(x_n)$; replacing $x_n$ by
$\tfrac12(x_n+x_n^*)$ and then by the functional-calculus cutoff
$\min(1,\max(0,x_n))$, we may assume $0\le x_n\le1$.  Since $q((x_n))$
is a projection, $\opnorm{x_n^2-x_n}\to0$, so the spectrum of $x_n$ is
eventually contained in $[0,\tfrac13)\cup(\tfrac23,1]$; for such $n$
put $e_n=\chi_{(\frac12,\,\infty)}(x_n)$, a projection with
$\opnorm{e_n-x_n}\to0$, and set $e_n=0$ for the finitely many remaining
$n$.  Then $(e_n)$ lifts $e$.

(2) Lift $u$ to a bounded sequence $(x_n)$ with $\opnorm{x_n}\le 1$.
Since $q((x_n))$ is unitary, $\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$, so $x_n$ is
eventually invertible; for such $n$ put $u_n=x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$, a
unitary with $\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\to0$; set $u_n=1$ for the remaining $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[finiteness]\label{lem:finite}
Let $\mathcal Q$ be a norm matrix corona.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every isometry in $\mathcal Q$ is a unitary; the same holds in
    every matrix amplification $M_k(\mathcal Q)$.
  \item If $p,q\in\mathcal Q$ are projections with $p\le q$ and
    $v^*v=q$, $vv^*=p$ for some $v\in\mathcal Q$, then $p=q$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Let $v\in\mathcal Q$ with $v^*v=1$ and lift $v$ to $(x_n)$ with
$\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$.  Eventually $x_n$ is invertible (a
left-invertible square matrix is invertible), and the unitary
$u_n=x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$ satisfies $\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\to0$; hence
$v=q((u_n))$ is unitary.  For $M_k(\mathcal Q)$ note
$M_k(\mathcal Q)\cong\mathcal Q((k\,d_n)_{n\in J})$, since matrix
amplification commutes with products and with the ideal of null
sequences.

(2) As in any $C^*$-algebra, $v^*v=q$ gives $v=vq$ (because
$\opnorm{v(1-q)}^2=\opnorm{(1-q)v^*v(1-q)}=\opnorm{(1-q)q(1-q)}=0$) and
$vv^*=p$ gives $v=pv$ (because
$\opnorm{(1-p)v}^2=\opnorm{(1-p)vv^*(1-p)}=0$).  From $p\le q$ we get
$qv=q(pv)=pv=v$, hence $(1-q)v=0$ and, taking adjoints,
$v^*(1-q)=0$ and $(1-q)v^*=\bigl(v(1-q)\bigr)^*=0$.  Then
$s:=v+(1-q)$ satisfies
\[
  s^*s=v^*v+v^*(1-q)+(1-q)v+(1-q)=q+0+0+(1-q)=1,
\]
so $s$ is an isometry, and
\[
  ss^*=vv^*+v(1-q)+(1-q)v^*+(1-q)=p+0+0+(1-q)=p+(1-q).
\]
By part (1), $ss^*=1$, i.e.\ $p+(1-q)=1$, i.e.\ $p=q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[central corners]\label{lem:corner}
Let $\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a corona representation and
suppose $\Theta(w)\neq1$.  Then there are an infinite set
$J'\subseteq J$, positive integers $(r_n)_{n\in J'}$ with $r_n\le
d_n$, and a corona representation
\[
  \Theta_-\colon E\longrightarrow
  \U\bigl(\mathcal Q'\bigr),\qquad
  \mathcal Q'=\mathcal Q\bigl((r_n)_{n\in J'}\bigr),
\]
with $\Theta_-(w)=-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $w^2=1$ and $w$ is central in $E$, the element $\Theta(w)$ is a
self-adjoint unitary commuting with $\Theta(E)$, and
$e:=\tfrac12(1-\Theta(w))$ is a projection commuting with $\Theta(E)$,
nonzero because $\Theta(w)\neq1$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}(1) lift $e$
to projections $(e_n)$; let $J'=\{n\in J: e_n\neq0\}$ and
$r_n=\operatorname{rank}e_n$ for $n\in J'$.  The set $J'$ is infinite,
since otherwise $(e_n)$ would be a null sequence and $e=0$.

Consider the map
\[
  \Psi\colon \prod_{n\in J'} e_nM_{d_n}(\C)e_n
  \longrightarrow e\,\mathcal Q\,e,\qquad
  (x_n)_{n\in J'}\longmapsto q\bigl((\tilde x_n)_{n\in J}\bigr),
\]
where $\tilde x_n=x_n$ for $n\in J'$ and $\tilde x_n=0$ otherwise.
$\Psi$ is a $*$-homomorphism onto $e\mathcal Qe$: surjectivity holds
because any $y\in e\mathcal Qe$ lifts to $(y_n)$ with
$\opnorm{e_ny_ne_n-y_n}\to0$, and $e_ny_ne_n=0$ off $J'$.  Its kernel
is exactly the sequences with $\opnorm{x_n}\to0$.  Choosing unitary
identifications $e_n\C^{d_n}\cong\C^{r_n}$ identifies
$e_nM_{d_n}(\C)e_n$ with $M_{r_n}(\C)$ compatibly with adjoints and
norms, so
\[
  e\,\mathcal Q\,e\;\cong\;\mathcal Q\bigl((r_n)_{n\in J'}\bigr)
  =\mathcal Q' ,
\]
a unital isomorphism carrying $e$ to $1$.  Finally, since $e$ commutes
with $\Theta(E)$, the map $\Theta_-(g):=\Theta(g)e$ is a unital group
homomorphism $E\to\U(e\mathcal Qe)=\U(\mathcal Q')$, and
$\Theta_-(w)=\Theta(w)e=(1-2e)e=-e=-1$ in $\mathcal Q'$.
\end{proof}

\section{The conjugation representation and the Kazhdan projection}\label{sec:conj}

Fix now a corona representation $\Theta_-\colon
E\to\U(\mathcal Q')$ with $\Theta_-(w)=-1$, where
$\mathcal Q'=\mathcal Q((r_n)_{n\in J'})$ as produced by
Lemma~\ref{lem:corner}.  Using Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}(2), choose for
every $g\in E$ a sequence of unitaries
$U_{g,n}\in M_{r_n}(\C)$ lifting $\Theta_-(g)$, with $U_{1,n}=1$.

Let $K_n=L^2\bigl(M_{r_n}(\C),\tr_{r_n}\bigr)$, a Hilbert space of
dimension $r_n^2$ whose bounded operators $B(K_n)$ form the matrix
algebra $M_{r_n^2}(\C)$, and let
\[
  \mathcal B\;=\;\prod_{n\in J'}B(K_n)\Big/\bigoplus_{n\in J'}B(K_n),
\]
again a norm matrix corona.  For a unitary $U\in M_{r_n}(\C)$ let
$\Ad U\in\U(B(K_n))$ denote $x\mapsto UxU^*$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:beta}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For unitaries $U,V\in M_r(\C)$ and every $x\in M_r(\C)$,
    \[
      \opnorm{UxU^*-VxV^*}\le2\opnorm{U-V}\,\opnorm{x},
    \]
    hence $\opnorm{\Ad U-\Ad V}\le2\opnorm{U-V}$ as operators on
    $B(K_n)$.
  \item The class $\beta(g)$ of the sequence $(\Ad U_{g,n})_{n\in J'}$
    in $\mathcal B$ defines a group homomorphism
    $\beta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal B)$, independent of the choice of
    unitary lifts.
  \item $\beta$ extends to a unital $*$-homomorphism
    $\tilde\beta\colon\Cmax(E)\to\mathcal B$ with
    $\tilde\beta(u_g)=\beta(g)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) $UxU^*-VxV^*=(U-V)xU^*+Vx(U-V)^*$; take norms.

(2) If $(U_{g,n})$ and $(U'_{g,n})$ both lift $\Theta_-(g)$ then
$\opnorm{U_{g,n}-U'_{g,n}}\to0$, so by (1) the two sequences of adjoint
maps agree in $\mathcal B$; this is independence of lifts.  For
multiplicativity, $U_{g,n}U_{h,n}$ and $U_{gh,n}$ both lift
$\Theta_-(gh)$, so $\opnorm{U_{g,n}U_{h,n}-U_{gh,n}}\to0$ and, by (1)
again, $\beta(g)\beta(h)=\beta(gh)$.  Finally $\beta(1)=1$ since
$U_{1,n}=1$, so each $\beta(g)$ is a unitary with inverse
$\beta(g^{-1})$.

(3) Represent $\mathcal B$ faithfully and unitally on a Hilbert space
$\mathcal H_{\mathcal B}$.  Then $g\mapsto\beta(g)$ is a unitary
representation of $E$ on $\mathcal H_{\mathcal B}$, which extends to a
$*$-representation $\tilde\beta$ of $\Cmax(E)$ on
$\mathcal H_{\mathcal B}$.  For $x\in\Cmax(E)$ the operator
$\tilde\beta(x)$ lies in the norm closure of the linear span of
$\{\beta(g):g\in E\}$, which is contained in the $C^*$-subalgebra
$\mathcal B$.
\end{proof}

Recall the auxiliary HNN extension $G$ of \eqref{eq:G} and the
canonical homomorphisms $\Gamma\to G\to E$.  Composing,
$\tilde\beta$ restricts to unital $*$-homomorphisms of
$\Cmax(G)$ and $\Cmax(\Gamma)$ into $\mathcal B$; we denote all of them
$\tilde\beta$, and we write
\[
  P\;:=\;\tilde\beta(p_\Gamma)\;\in\;\mathcal B ,
\]
a projection.

\begin{lemma}[order identity]\label{lem:order}
Let $\iota\colon\Cmax(\Gamma)\to\Cmax(G)$ be induced by
$\Gamma\to G$, and write $\bar p=\iota(p_\Gamma)$.  Then, in
$\Cmax(G)$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $u_\gamma\,\bar p=\bar p$ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$;
  \item $\bar p\,\bigl(u_t\,\bar p\,u_t^*\bigr)=\bar p$; that is,
    $\bar p\le u_t\bar pu_t^*$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, in $\mathcal B$: $\beta(\gamma)P=P$ for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$, and $P\le\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both identities may be checked in an arbitrary unitary representation
$\pi$ of $G$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, since $\Cmax(G)$ admits a
faithful one.  The composition $\pi\circ\iota$ is the integrated form
of $\pi|_\Gamma$ (both are $*$-homomorphisms extending the same unitary
representation of $\Gamma$), so by Fact~\ref{fact:kazhdan},
$\pi(\bar p)$ is the orthogonal projection onto
$\Fix(\pi|_\Gamma)$, and (1) holds.

For (2): $\pi(u_t\bar pu_t^*)=\pi(t)\,\pi(\bar p)\,\pi(t)^*$ is the
orthogonal projection onto $\pi(t)\Fix(\pi|_\Gamma)$.  We claim
$\Fix(\pi|_\Gamma)\subseteq\pi(t)\Fix(\pi|_\Gamma)$.  Let
$\xi\in\Fix(\pi|_\Gamma)$ and put $\eta=\pi(t)^{-1}\xi$.  For
$\gamma\in\Gamma$, using $t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)\in\Gamma$,
\[
  \pi(\gamma)\eta=\pi(t)^{-1}\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\xi
  =\pi(t)^{-1}\pi(\alpha(\gamma))\xi=\pi(t)^{-1}\xi=\eta ,
\]
so $\eta\in\Fix(\pi|_\Gamma)$ and $\xi=\pi(t)\eta$.  The claimed
inclusion of ranges gives
$\pi(\bar p)\le\pi(u_t\bar pu_t^*)$, i.e.\ (2) in the representation
$\pi$; as $\pi$ was arbitrary and some $\pi$ is faithful, (2) holds in
$\Cmax(G)$.  The final statement follows by applying the
$*$-homomorphism $\tilde\beta$ (through $\Cmax(G)$).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[compression pinning]\label{lem:pinning}
$P=\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$ in $\mathcal B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $Q_0=\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$ and $v=\beta(t)P$.  Then
$v^*v=P\beta(t)^*\beta(t)P=P$ and $vv^*=Q_0$, and $P\le Q_0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:order}.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}(2) with
$p:=P$, $q:=Q_0$, and the element $v^*$, which satisfies
$(v^*)^*(v^*)=vv^*=Q_0$ and $(v^*)(v^*)^*=v^*v=P$; it yields $P=Q_0$.
\end{proof}

Finally we make the vector-level statements literal.  Fix a free
ultrafilter $\omega$ on the infinite index set $J'$, and let
\[
  K_\omega\;=\;\Bigl\{(\xi_n)_{n\in J'}:\ \xi_n\in K_n,\
  \sup_n\lVert\xi_n\rVert<\infty\Bigr\}\Big/
  \bigl\{(\xi_n):\ \lim_{n\to\omega}\lVert\xi_n\rVert=0\bigr\},
\]
the Hilbert-space ultraproduct with inner product
$\langle[\xi],[\eta]\rangle=\lim_{n\to\omega}\langle\xi_n,\eta_n\rangle$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sigma}
For a bounded sequence $(T_n)_{n\in J'}$ of operators with class
$[T]\in\mathcal B$, the assignment
$\sigma([T])\,[\,(\xi_n)\,]=[\,(T_n\xi_n)\,]$ is a well-defined unital
$*$-homomorphism $\sigma\colon\mathcal B\to B(K_\omega)$.  Moreover
$\sigma(P)$ is the orthogonal projection onto
\[
  \Fix_\omega\;=\;\bigl\{\zeta\in K_\omega:\
  \sigma(\beta(\gamma))\zeta=\zeta\ \text{for all}\
  \gamma\in\Gamma\bigr\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $(T_n)$ is a null sequence in operator norm then
$\lVert T_n\xi_n\rVert\le\opnorm{T_n}\sup\lVert\xi_n\rVert\to0$, in
particular along $\omega$; so $\sigma$ is well defined on
$\mathcal B$, and it is clearly a unital $*$-homomorphism.  The
composition $\sigma\circ\tilde\beta\colon\Cmax(\Gamma)\to B(K_\omega)$
is a $*$-representation on a Hilbert space extending the unitary
representation $\gamma\mapsto\sigma(\beta(\gamma))$; by uniqueness of
integrated forms and Fact~\ref{fact:kazhdan},
$\sigma(P)=\sigma(\tilde\beta(p_\Gamma))$ is the orthogonal projection
onto the fixed space of that representation, which is $\Fix_\omega$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:A}}\label{sec:proofA}

Part (1) is Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  For part (2), let
$\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a corona representation and
suppose, for contradiction, that $\Theta(w)\neq1$.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} to obtain $\Theta_-$ with $\Theta_-(w)=-1$ on
the corner corona $\mathcal Q'$, and adopt all notation of
Section~\ref{sec:conj}: the unitary lifts $U_{g,n}$, the corona
$\mathcal B$, the homomorphism $\beta$, the projection $P$, the
ultraproduct $K_\omega$ and the representation $\sigma$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: the lamp vector is fixed.}
Each $U_{c,n}$ is a unitary matrix, hence a vector in $K_n$ of norm
$\hsnorm{U_{c,n}}=1$; let $\xi_c=[(U_{c,n})]\in K_\omega$, a unit
vector.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we have $\gamma c\gamma^{-1}=c$ in $E$,
so $U_{\gamma,n}U_{c,n}U_{\gamma,n}^*$ and $U_{c,n}$ both lift
$\Theta_-(c)$, whence
$\opnorm{U_{\gamma,n}U_{c,n}U_{\gamma,n}^*-U_{c,n}}\to0$ and a fortiori
the $K_n$-norms tend to $0$.  Therefore
$\sigma(\beta(\gamma))\xi_c=\xi_c$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$, i.e.\
$\xi_c\in\Fix_\omega$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma},
$\sigma(P)\xi_c=\xi_c$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: the translated lamp vector is fixed.}
Let $\xi_d:=\sigma(\beta(t))\xi_c=[(U_{t,n}U_{c,n}U_{t,n}^*)]$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:pinning} and Step 1,
\[
  \sigma(P)\,\xi_d
  =\sigma\bigl(\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*\bigr)\,\sigma(\beta(t))\,\xi_c
  =\sigma(\beta(t))\,\sigma(P)\,\xi_c
  =\xi_d ,
\]
so $\xi_d\in\Fix_\omega$.  In particular, since $a\in\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:afix}
  \lim_{n\to\omega}\ \hsnorm{\,U_{a,n}D_nU_{a,n}^*-D_n\,}=0,
  \qquad D_n:=U_{t,n}U_{c,n}U_{t,n}^* .
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 3: the commutator word collapses in
Hilbert--Schmidt norm.}
Put $E_n:=U_{a,n}D_nU_{a,n}^*$ and
$W_n:=D_nE_nD_n^{-1}E_n^{-1}$, unitaries in $M_{r_n}(\C)$.  For
unitaries $D,E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \hsnorm{DED^{-1}E^{-1}-1}&=\hsnorm{DE-ED}\\
  &\le\hsnorm{(D-E)E}+\hsnorm{E(E-D)}=2\hsnorm{D-E},
\end{aligned}
\]
using unitary invariance of $\hsnorm\cdot$.  With \eqref{eq:afix},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
  \lim_{n\to\omega}\ \hsnorm{\,W_n-1\,}\;=\;0 .
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 4: the corner normalization forbids the
collapse.}
The finite product $W_n$ of the lifts
$U_{t,n},U_{c,n},U_{a,n}$ and their adjoints is an operator-norm lift
of
\[
  \Theta_-\bigl(\,(tct^{-1})\,\cdot\,a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\,\cdot\,
  (tct^{-1})^{-1}\,\cdot\,\bigl(a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}\,\bigr)
  \;=\;\Theta_-(w)\;=\;-1 ,
\]
so $\opnorm{W_n+1}\to0$ as $n\to\infty$ in $J'$, hence also along
$\omega$.  Therefore
\[
  \hsnorm{W_n-1}\;\ge\;\hsnorm{(-1)-1}-\hsnorm{W_n-(-1)}
  \;\ge\;2-\opnorm{W_n+1}\;\xrightarrow[n\to\omega]{}\;2 ,
\]
contradicting \eqref{eq:collapse}.  This contradiction shows
$\Theta(w)=1$, proving part (2).

\medskip

For the final assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}: an MF embedding of
$E$ would be an injective corona representation, which is impossible
because every corona representation sends the nontrivial element $w$
to $1$.  If $\Cred(E)$ were an MF algebra, a unital $C^*$-embedding
$\Cred(E)\hookrightarrow\mathcal Q$ composed with the canonical map
$E\to\U(\Cred(E))$, which is injective on $E$ because the left regular
representation is faithful as a group representation, would exhibit an
injective corona representation of $E$; the same argument applies to
$\Cmax(E)$ via the universal representation.  \qed

\begin{remark}\label{rem:radical}
The proof shows more than non-injectivity of each individual
$\Theta$: the fixed element $w$ lies in the kernel of \emph{every}
corona representation of $E$, so every corona representation factors
through $E/\langle w\rangle$ (note $\langle w\rangle=\{1,w\}$ is
central, hence normal).  In the language of approximation radicals, $w$
is a nontrivial element of the ``MF radical'' of $E$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
No lower bound on the corner ranks $r_n$, or on their density in
$d_n$, is used anywhere: Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} renormalizes the trace
so that all subsequent estimates are relative to the corner.  This is
what makes the operator-norm setting immune to the rank-blindness that
blocks the same argument in Hilbert--Schmidt metrics
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{remark}

\section{Necessity of property (T)}\label{sec:necessity}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:C}.  Recall the setting: $G_0=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)$
with base $\Gamma_0=\langle\gamma_0\rangle\cong\Z$ and stable letter
$t$ satisfying $t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2$; $X_0=G_0/\Gamma_0$;
$E_0=C(X_0)\rtimes G_0$ with central sign $z_0=-1\in C(X_0)$ as in
Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
\emph{$z_0\neq1$:} immediate from Construction~\ref{con:clifford},
since $C(X_0)\le\Cl(X_0)^\times$ and $\Cl(X_0)\neq0$.

\emph{Amenability:} every finitely generated subgroup of $C(X_0)$ is
contained in the subgroup generated by $\pm1$ and finitely many
$c_x$, which is a finite $2$-group (it has order at most
$2^{k+1}$ for $k$ sites, since every element is $\pm$ a reduced product
of distinct generators).  Hence $C(X_0)$ is locally finite, so
amenable.  The group $G_0=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\cong\Z[1/2]\rtimes\Z$ is
metabelian, so amenable.  As an extension of the amenable $G_0$ by the
amenable $C(X_0)$, the group $E_0$ is amenable.

\emph{MF:} by Fact~\ref{fact:tww}, the amenable group $E_0$ is MF, and
an MF embedding $E_0\hookrightarrow\U(\mathcal Q)$ is injective, so the
image of $z_0$ is a nontrivial (self-adjoint) unitary in the corona:
the central sign survives.

\emph{Finite-dimensional sterility persists:} $E_0$ contains the
pattern of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with $H=E_0$, $\Gamma=\Gamma_0$,
$t=t$, $c=c_{o}$ ($o=\Gamma_0\in X_0$, fixed by $\Gamma_0$), and
$a=\gamma_0\in\Gamma_0$.  We have
$t\Gamma_0t^{-1}=\langle\gamma_0^2\rangle\subseteq\Gamma_0$ and
$[c_o,\Gamma_0]=1$.  Moreover $\gamma_0t\Gamma_0\neq t\Gamma_0$ in
$G_0$: otherwise $t^{-1}\gamma_0t\in\Gamma_0$; in the faithful affine
model $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)=\Z[1/2]\rtimes\Z$ with
$\gamma_0=(1,0)$ and $t=(0,1)$ acting by multiplication by $2$, we
compute $t^{-1}\gamma_0t=(\tfrac12,0)\notin\Gamma_0=\Z\times\{0\}$.
Hence
\[
  \bigl[\,tc_ot^{-1},\;\gamma_0(tc_ot^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}\bigr]
  =[c_{t\Gamma_0},c_{\gamma_0t\Gamma_0}]=z_0
\]
in $E_0$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:B} says every finite-dimensional
representation of $E_0$, over every field, kills $z_0$.  Since
$z_0\neq1$, the group $E_0$ is not residually finite-dimensional.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:C} calibrates Theorem~\ref{thm:A} exactly.  The two
constructions differ in a single hypothesis --- property (T) of the
base --- and the conclusion flips.  In particular no proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} can avoid using property (T) (or some substitute
producing the fixed-space projection inside the corona), and the
elementary Theorem~\ref{thm:B} is genuinely weaker than
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}: MF approximations of $E_0$ exist even though
finite-dimensional representations all kill the sign.
\end{remark}

\section{Limitations: Hilbert--Schmidt metrics and hyperlinearity}\label{sec:limits}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} does not extend to tracial
ultraproducts, for a structural reason worth recording.

Suppose one replaces the norm corona $\mathcal Q$ by a tracial
ultraproduct $\mathcal M=\prod^\omega(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$, with
defects measured in $\hsnorm\cdot$ rather than operator norm, and asks
whether a hyperlinear-style embedding of $E$ must kill $w$.  Two steps
break.

First, Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} fails: Hilbert--Schmidt-multiplicative
lifts need not be operator-norm multiplicative, so the adjoint maps
$\Ad U_{g,n}$ need not assemble into a homomorphism into any norm
corona.  One can instead pass to the conjugation representation on
$L^2(\mathcal M)$, which is an honest representation of $E$.

Second, and decisively, Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} is then unavailable:
$B(L^2(\mathcal M))$ is properly infinite, and the Kazhdan projections
onto the fixed spaces of $\Gamma$ and of $t\Gamma t^{-1}$, though
unitarily equivalent via the shift and nested by the same argument as
in Lemma~\ref{lem:order}, may satisfy a \emph{proper} inclusion
$P<\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$: exactly the unilateral-shift phenomenon.  The
normalized traces of the two fixed-space projections agree, so the
discrepancy is a trace-zero corner, which can moreover be diluted
arbitrarily by tensoring a hypothetical microstate with an auxiliary
regular representation; no normalized-rank argument can force it to be
visible.  In particular the present paper proves nothing about
hyperlinearity or Connes embeddability of $E$, and nothing about the
sofic-versus-hyperlinear problem.

The precise dividing line is thus: \emph{operator-norm matrix models
form a stably finite world, in which property (T) compression is
rigid; tracial matrix models form a properly infinite world at the
level of their conjugation actions, in which the same compression can
hide in a trace-zero corner.}
