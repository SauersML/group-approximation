\title[A finitely presented group that is not MF]{A finitely presented group that is not MF}
\author{\paperauthor}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 46L05; Secondary 20F05, 22D25, 20E26, 68V20}
\keywords{MF groups, matrix coronas, property (T), Kazhdan transport,
Clifford groups}

\begin{abstract}
A countable discrete group is MF if it admits unitary matrix models that
are asymptotically multiplicative in operator norm and separate every
nontrivial element from the identity; equivalently, if it embeds in the
unitary group of a quotient
$\prod_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)/\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)$.  We construct
an explicit finitely presented group $E$ that is not MF, refuting the
conjecture that every countable group is MF.  The reduced group
$C^*$-algebra of $E$ is a separable, stably finite $C^*$-algebra that is
not MF.

The mechanism is a rigidity theorem for property-\textup{(T)} subgroups:
if $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$, then in operator-norm matrix models
conjugation by $t$ preserves the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant
of $\Gamma$.  In $E$ this forces a commutator $u$ to converge to $1$ in
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, while an explicit Clifford-group
quotient makes the central involution $w=u^2$ equal to $-1$ on a nonzero
spectral corner --- a contradiction, so every matrix model of $E$
collapses $w$.  More generally, every finitely presented
property-\textup{(T)} group with a proper injective self-embedding
produces a finitely presented non-MF group; the group $E$, with eight
generators and forty-one relators, is the instance built on
$\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$.

The homomorphism proving $w\ne1$ maps $E$ onto a second group $W$, a
Clifford extension of a linear group.  We prove that $W$ is finitely
generated, sofic --- hence hyperlinear --- and not MF; thus neither
soficity nor hyperlinearity implies the MF property.  We do not determine here whether $E$
itself is sofic or hyperlinear.  MF groups are closed under
neither quotients nor extensions, and the reduced group $C^*$-algebra of
$W$ is in addition exact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Can every countable discrete group be realized by matrices, if
multiplication is only required to hold up to operator-norm errors that
vanish along a sequence of finite dimensions?  Shulman calls the positive
assertion the \emph{MF conjecture} \cite{Shulman}, and no group was known
to fail it \cite{Schafhauser,Shulman}.  Residually finite groups satisfy
it through their finite quotients, as do LEF groups \cite{CDE} and, by
quasidiagonality \cite{TWW}, all amenable groups; recent work establishes
it for many amalgamated free products \cite{Schafhauser,Shulman,GKEMP}.
The corresponding $C^*$-algebra question is older: Blackadar and
Kirchberg, who introduced MF algebras, remarked that no stably finite
separable $C^*$-algebra was known not to be MF \cite{BK,CDE}, and their
natural candidate $\Cred(F_2)$ was in fact proved MF by Haagerup and
Thorbj{\o}rnsen \cite{HT}.  The negative solution of the Connes embedding
problem subsequently supplied abstract stably finite non-MF algebras
\cite{MIPRE,FGH}.  No countable group was known whose reduced or maximal group
$C^*$-algebra fails to be MF \cite{Shulman}.

These questions sit at the endpoint of a program of finitary
approximation.  In his 2018 ICM address, Thom asked for which Schatten
$p$-norms there exist non-approximable groups
\cite[Question~3.11]{Th}: the Frobenius case $p=2$ was settled by
De~Chiffre--Glebsky--Lubotzky--Thom \cite{DGLT}, the cases $1<p<\infty$
by Lubotzky--Oppenheim \cite{LuO}, and the case $p=1$ by
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky \cite{BDL}, who describe the three remaining
metrics --- the Hamming metric (soficity), the Hilbert--Schmidt norm
(hyperlinearity), and the operator norm (MF) --- as ``the holy-grail of
the subject.''  The Hamming case was disproved in 2026: the first
nonsofic group was constructed by OpenAI \cite{OAI}, Fournier-Facio then
gave a torsion-free example \cite{FFF}, and Kun--Thom developed further
wreath-product examples \cite{KT}.  One-sided conjugation of a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup into itself,
the mechanism driving those arguments, is also the conceptual ancestor of the present
one.  Soficity concerns permutations in the Hamming metric, while MF concerns
unitaries in operator norm, so there is no direct metric comparison between
the two approximation properties.  The operator-norm setting
requires a different rigidity theorem, proved here by transporting
finite-stage spectral corners of the adjoint actions through an
equal-rank projection flip.  This paper settles the
operator-norm case by one explicit finitely presented group; it also
proves that soficity does not imply MF and produces an explicit reduced
group $C^*$-algebra that is stably finite but not MF.  Of the three
problems, only the Hilbert--Schmidt case --- hyperlinearity --- remains
open.

In the language of approximate representations, a countable discrete
group $G$ is \emph{MF} if, for every finite subset $F\subseteq G$ and
every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a unitary matrix model
$V\colon G\to\U(r)$ that is multiplicative within $\varepsilon$ in
operator norm on $F$ and separates distinct elements of $F$ by
operator-norm distance at least $1$.  Equivalently --- this is the
formulation in which the notion was introduced by
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt and is used by Shulman and
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky \cite{CDE,Shulman,BDL} --- let $(d_n)_{n\in\N}$
be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corona}
  \mathcal Q\;=\;\prod_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)\Big/\bigoplus_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)
\end{equation}
be the associated matrix quotient --- a corona algebra, here called the
\emph{norm matrix corona}: bounded sequences of
matrices modulo sequences converging to $0$ in operator norm.  Following
Blackadar--Kirchberg \cite{BK}, a separable $C^*$-algebra is \emph{MF}
if it embeds into some algebra of the form \eqref{eq:corona}; the group
$G$ is MF if there is an injective homomorphism
\[
  G\longrightarrow\U(\mathcal Q).
\]
The two formulations are equivalent
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}).
We shall use the equivalent coordinate model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary-corona}
 \Ucor((d_n))
 :=\prod_n\U(M_{d_n}(\C))\Big/
 \bigl\{(u_n):\opnorm{u_n-1}\longrightarrow0\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is normal, and polar correction gives a canonical
isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary-corona-iso}
 \kappa_{(d_n)}\colon \Ucor((d_n))\ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\
 \U(\mathcal Q),
\end{equation}
proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}.  Thus one may equivalently embed
$G$ into $\Ucor((d_n))$.
\begin{proposition}[equivalent MF formulations]
\label{prop:mf-equivalences}
For every countable group, the $C^*$-corona definition above is
equivalent to the unitary-sequence definition.  The latter is equivalent to
operator-norm local models with separation constant $1$, and allowing
arbitrary positive dimensions is equivalent to requiring the dimensions to
be strictly increasing.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMFDefinitionEquivalences}
\end{proposition}

The proof --- an explicit tensor-power amplification in one direction,
a weak-MF adapter in the other, and a cumulative-block dimension
normalization --- is deferred, together with the precise formulation of
the operator-norm local models, to Appendix~\ref{app:equivalences}.

Hereafter ``MF'' means this property: the operator-norm notion of
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt, in the form used by Shulman, with no
additional requirement that matrix models recover the regular
representation, its norms, or its trace.  Stronger conventions exist and
should not be confused with it: in \cite{GKEMP} the approximating maps
must additionally recover both the
canonical trace and the left-regular norm on every group-ring element,
and the purely matricial field (PMF) convention of
\cite[Definition~1.2]{MdlS} requires finite-dimensional
representations whose group-ring norms converge to the regular norms
(trace convergence is included in some related conventions but not in
that definition).  Every assertion below uses only the MF property fixed
here.

Throughout, $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ for group elements, while for matrices
$[x,y]=xy-yx$; the arguments always make the reading unambiguous.

\subsection*{The result and the mechanism}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} produces an explicit group $E$ on eight generators
$v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c$ with forty-one relators, containing a designated
central involution $w$, such that $w\ne1$ and yet every homomorphism
from $E$ to the unitary group of a norm matrix corona sends $w$ to $1$.
In particular no such homomorphism is injective, and $E$ is not MF.  The
proof has four steps, each quantitative at a fixed matrix dimension.
\begin{enumerate}
\item An operator-norm approximate representation acts by conjugation on
the Hilbert--Schmidt space of each matrix block, and operator-norm
control keeps this adjoint action approximately multiplicative.
\item The subgroup of $E$ generated by the images of
$v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z$ is a quotient of a group $\mathcal B$ with
property~\textup{(T)}, proved intrinsically from the displayed
presentation.  At each dimension, property~\textup{(T)} provides a
spectral corner --- a finite-stage Kazhdan projection --- spanned by the
vectors almost fixed by the adjoint action of that subgroup.
\item The defining relations give
$t\,\iota(\mathcal B)\,t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\mathcal B)$, hence a
one-sided containment between the corner and its conjugate by $t$.  The
two projections have equal rank, so an equal-rank flip upgrades the
containment to equality: conjugation by $t$ preserves the
Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of the base.  (In ultraproduct
language: the fixed-space projection satisfies $P\le VPV^*$, and
finiteness of the ambient $C^*$-ultraproduct --- finite in the
$C^*$-sense of having no proper isometries --- forces equality.)
\item The element $c$ commutes with the base image, so its conjugate
$d=tct^{-1}$ asymptotically commutes with it as well, and the commutator
$u=[d,v_1]$ converges to $1$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  If
some model detected the central involution $w=u^2$, cutting to the
$-1$-spectral corner of $w$ --- the corner of $q=(1-\Theta(w))/2$ ---
would give a model in which $w=-1$ exactly, at normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt distance $2$ from $1$; but $w\ne1$ in $E$, witnessed by
an explicit Clifford-group quotient in which $d$ and $v_1dv_1^{-1}$
become anticommuting involutions and $w$ becomes the central sign.
\end{enumerate}
\[
 \boxed{\text{rigidity: }u\sim_2 1}
 \qquad\text{versus}\qquad
 \boxed{\text{Clifford model: }w=u^2=-1.}
\]

Two groups play distinct roles throughout, and the distinction matters.
The finitely presented group $E$ is the headline example: it is not MF,
and we do not determine whether $E$ itself is sofic or hyperlinear
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).  The homomorphism proving $w\ne1$ maps $E$
onto a second, finitely generated group $W$, a Clifford extension of a
linear group, and Theorem~\ref{thm:E} proves that $W$ is sofic --- hence
hyperlinear --- and not MF.  Soficity is established for $W$, not for
$E$.  On the algebra side, the reduced group $C^*$-algebra of $E$ is the
stably finite non-MF example (Theorem~\ref{thm:D}); the reduced algebra
of $W$ is in addition exact.

\subsection*{Operator norm versus tracial approximation}
The argument is specific to operator norm: the spectral-corner and
leakage estimates of the transport theorem require the adjoint actions to
be controlled in operator norm on the Hilbert--Schmidt spaces.
Hilbert--Schmidt-small defects alone do not provide
that operator control, so the proof does not give a hyperlinear obstruction.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} has no external group-theoretic inputs:

The classical identification of the base with

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}
The closest conceptual analogue is the recent work on nonsofic groups.
One-sided conjugation of a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup and propagation
from its centralizer appear in \cite{OAI,KT}, building on rigidity results of
Kun and Kun--Thom \cite{Kun16,KT19}; related centralizer rigidity for sofic
embeddings is proved in \cite{AlekseevThom}.  Those results concern Hamming or
tracial approximations.  Here the adjoint actions stay on finite-stage
Hilbert--Schmidt spaces, the Kazhdan projection is replaced by explicit
spectral corners, and an equal-rank projection flip prevents a one-sided
compression from enlarging the corner.  The complementary finite-group corner then converts
that rigidity into a fixed nontrivial element sent to the identity by every
corona representation.  The Clifford central extension supplies the element
whose survival is proved independently.
The results of \cite{OAI,KT} provide context only: no theorem in this paper
uses either one as an input.

Slofstra's constructions supply close precedents for the Clifford group,
distinguished central involution, shift, and HNN doubling map
\cite[Section~2 and Remark~3.3]{SlofstraProfile}.  Negative-eigenspace
compression appears in Slofstra--Vidick
\cite[proof of Proposition~2.7]{SlofstraVidick}, and
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky develop operator-norm rounding, compression, and
polar-correction procedures in detail
\cite[Lemmas~2.2--2.3, Proposition~2.4, and the proof of
Proposition~1.5]{BDL}.  That local procedure enters the finite-normal corner
reduction below; the transport step itself is the finite-stage
spectral-corner argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}.

The analytic passage from operator norm to the Hilbert--Schmidt conjugation
representation belongs to the $\pi\otimes\bar\pi$ framework of Bekka and
Bekka--Valette \cite{BekkaAmenable,BekkaValette}.  Dadarlat uses normalized
finite-rank projections as almost invariant Hilbert--Schmidt vectors in an
operator-norm approximation argument
\cite[Lemma~3.18 and Proposition~3.19]{Dadarlat}.  These are the direct
analytic precedents for the conjugation step used here.

Slofstra's quotients by elements invisible to exact or approximate
finite-dimensional representations precede the MF-radical language used
below \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}.  Fritz's marked
$\mathrm{BS}(2,3)$ example provides additional context for the cyclic
comparison \cite{Fritz,SlofstraVidick}.

Central extensions and rigidity of approximate representations also drive
the Frobenius obstruction of De Chiffre--Glebsky--Lubotzky--Thom
\cite{DGLT}.  In the proof of Proposition~1.6,
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky treat the finite-central case by a
character-isotypic corner, and reduce the general finite-normal case to it by
passing to the finite-index centralizer \cite{BDL}.  The finite-normal
criterion here combines the corresponding coordinate-corner construction
with the one-sided Kazhdan conjugation and does not assume that the quotient is
hyperlinear.  Related finite-target approximation and extension results for
weak soficity appear in
\cite{GR,Gl19}.

Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt provide the group-MF framework used here and,
through Abels' group, an amenable MF group that is not residually finite
\cite[Section~2.14 and Corollary~2.18]{CDE}.  The cyclic comparison addresses
the narrower question of what happens to the marked conjugation
configuration when
property~\textup{(T)} is removed.

Finally, failure of the MF property for a group $C^*$-algebra does not by
itself prevent the group from embedding into a corona: the group property
requires only a faithful canonical image in some MF quotient of $\Cmax(E)$.
The result here produces a specific nontrivial element in the kernel of every
such representation.  For broader context see
\cite{DGLT,BDL,OAI,KT,Shulman,Dadarlat,SlofstraCorrelations,SlofstraProfile,SlofstraVidick}.

\section{Notation and corona lemmas}\label{sec:prelim}

All groups are discrete and countable.  Property~\textup{(T)} is the
textbook complex-unitary property.  A $C^*$-homomorphism is unital only when
this is stated.  In particular, an MF embedding may be nonunital, as is
customary for embedding a unital algebra as a $C^*$-subalgebra.  If
$e\colon A\to B$ is an injective, possibly nonunital $*$-homomorphism between
unital $C^*$-algebras, then $u\mapsto e(u)+(1-e(1))$ is an injective
homomorphism $\U(A)\to\U(B)$; this complement correction is used below.  For
a unital $C^*$-algebra $A$
we write $\U(A)$ for its unitary group.  On $M_r(\C)$ we use the
normalized trace $\tr_r$, the operator norm $\opnorm{\cdot}$, and the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
$\hsnorm{x}=\tr_r(x^*x)^{1/2}$; for coordinate sequences we write
$a\sim_2 b$ when $\hsnorm{a_n-b_n}\to0$.  We will use three elementary facts
without further comment: $\hsnorm{x}\le\opnorm{x}$;
$|\tr_r(x)|\le\opnorm{x}$; and $\hsnorm{uxv}=\hsnorm{x}$ for unitaries
$u,v$.  For a nonempty finite set $Y$, write
$M_Y(\C)=\C^{Y\times Y}$ for the corresponding indexed matrix algebra.

Given a sequence $(d_n)_{n\in\N}$ of positive integers, we write
\[
  \mathcal Q\bigl((d_n)_{n\in\N}\bigr)
  \;=\;\prod_{n\in\N}^{\ell^\infty} M_{d_n}(\C)
  \Big/\bigoplus_{n\in\N}^{c_0} M_{d_n}(\C),
\]
the quotient of the bounded $C^*$-product by the ideal of sequences with
$\opnorm{x_n}\to0$.  We call any such algebra a \emph{norm matrix
corona}.  We also write
\[
 \Ucor((d_n)_{n\in\N})
 =\prod_{n\in\N}\U(M_{d_n}(\C))\Big/
 \bigl\{(u_n):\opnorm{u_n-1}\longrightarrow0\bigr\}.
\]
Write $q\colon\prod_{n\in\N}^{\ell^\infty}M_{d_n}(\C)\to\mathcal Q$
for the quotient map of a norm matrix corona.

The following lifting facts are standard perturbation theory; see
Loring \cite{Loring} for a systematic treatment.

\begin{lemma}[lifting]\label{lem:lift}
Every unitary $u\in\mathcal Q$ lifts to a sequence of unitaries.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUnitaryLifting}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lift $u$ to an arbitrary bounded sequence $(x_n)$.  Since
$q((x_n))$ is unitary, the one-sided Gram defect satisfies
$\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$; no condition on $x_nx_n^*$ is needed.  Once
$\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\le\tfrac12$, the square Gram matrix $x_n^*x_n$ is
invertible, hence so is $x_n$, and the polar correction
$u_n=x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$ is unitary; continuous functional calculus
gives the quantitative estimate
\[
 \opnorm{u_n-x_n}
 \le 2\opnorm{x_n}\,\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}.
\]
The sequence $(x_n)$ is bounded, so the right-hand side tends to $0$.
Setting $u_n=1$ at the finitely many remaining indices therefore gives a
unitary sequence with $\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[unitaries in the quotient]\label{lem:unitarycorona}
For every sequence $(d_n)$, the map
\[
 \kappa_{(d_n)}\colon \Ucor((d_n))\longrightarrow\U(\mathcal Q),
 \qquad [(u_n)]\longmapsto q((u_n)),
\]
is a canonical group isomorphism.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUnitaryCoronaEquivalence}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map is well defined, and its kernel consists exactly of the unitary
sequences satisfying $\opnorm{u_n-1}\to0$, so it is injective.  It is
surjective by Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}: every unitary in $\mathcal Q$ has a
unitary lift $(u_n)$, whose class in \eqref{eq:unitary-corona} maps to it.
\end{proof}

A \emph{corona representation} of a group $H$ is a homomorphism
$H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$, and it \emph{detects} $g\in H$ --- separates $g$
from the identity --- when it does not send $g$ to $1$.  An
\emph{operator-norm asymptotic representation} of $H$ is a sequence of
maps $U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ such that
$\opnorm{U_n(g)U_n(h)-U_n(gh)}\to0$ for all $g,h\in H$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} canonically identifies a corona
representation with a homomorphism into $\Ucor((d_n))$, which we call
its coordinate model.  A countable group is \emph{MF} if it admits an
injective corona representation for a strictly increasing dimension
sequence; by the dimension-amplification equivalence, allowing an
arbitrary positive dimension sequence gives the same class of groups.
No condition is imposed on an asymptotic representation at the identity:
taking $g=h=1$ gives $\opnorm{U_n(1)-1}\to0$ automatically, since
$U_n(1)$ is unitary.  Coordinate unitary lifts of a corona
representation of a countable group form exactly such a sequence.

The maximal group $C^*$-algebra $\Cmax(H)$ is used only for the final
assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}; the construction and its universal
property, stated universe-relatively as the type-theoretic foundations of
the accompanying formal development require, are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:maxcstar}.

\section{One-sided Kazhdan transport}\label{sec:transport}

The rigidity mechanism is best remembered before the construction.  The
same elementary principle appears in three finite worlds:
\[
 \begin{array}{c@{\quad}c@{\quad}c}
 X\subseteq Y,\ |X|=|Y| & V\subseteq W,\ \dim V=\dim W
   & \substack{p\le q,\ p\sim q\\
       \text{in a finite }C^*\text{-algebra}}\\[2mm]
 \Downarrow & \Downarrow & \Downarrow\\[-1mm]
 X=Y & V=W & p=q.
 \end{array}
\]
In the right-hand column, $p\sim q$ is Murray--von Neumann equivalence
and \emph{finite} is meant in the $C^*$-sense: no proper isometries.
Finite-dimensional representations use the middle implication directly.
For matrix models, property~\textup{(T)} supplies at every coordinate a
spectral corner --- a finite-stage Kazhdan projection --- and the same
middle implication, in the form of an equal-rank projection flip, turns
the one-sided compression of that corner into a two-sided symmetry.  The
right-hand implication is the classical ultraproduct shadow of this step
(Remark~\ref{rem:ultraproduct}).  This yields the reusable theorem behind
every result below.

Recall from Section~\ref{sec:prelim} the notions of corona
representation, detection, and operator-norm asymptotic representation,
and the notation $a_n\sim_2 b_n$ for
$\hsnorm{a_n-b_n}\to0$ in the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.

\begin{theorem}[Kazhdan transport of Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutants]
\label{thm:kazhdan-transport}
Let $\Gamma$ and $H$ be groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let $\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism, let
$s\in H$, and let $(d_n)$ be a sequence of positive integers.  Suppose
\[
 s\iota(\Gamma)s^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma).
\]
Let $U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic representation.
If $(x_n)$ is uniformly operator-norm bounded and asymptotically
centralizes $\iota(\Gamma)$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm,
\[
 \hsnorm{x_nU_n(\iota(\gamma))-U_n(\iota(\gamma))x_n}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma),
\]
then the conjugated sequence does as well:
\[
\hsnorm{[U_n(s)x_nU_n(s)^*,U_n(\iota(\gamma))]}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.manuscriptKazhdanTransport}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof is quantitative at each single coordinate $n$; no ultrafilter
or subsequence is needed.  Property~\textup{(T)} provides a finite
symmetric generating set $S\subseteq\Gamma$ with $1\in S$ and a Kazhdan
constant $\kappa\in(0,1]$ for the pair $(S,\kappa)$.

\emph{The adjoint model.}  Regard $M_{d_n}(\C)$ as the Hilbert space
$L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$ and let each group element act by
conjugation, $\Ad U_n(g)\,\xi=U_n(g)\,\xi\,U_n(g)^*$.  Operator-norm
almost multiplicativity of $U_n$ makes $g\mapsto\Ad U_n(g)$ an
operator-norm asymptotic representation on these Hilbert spaces; this
conjugation maneuver is the finite-stage form of the
$\pi\otimes\bar\pi$ construction.

\emph{The spectral corner.}  Let $H_n$ be the Hermitian part of the
average $\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s'\in S}\Ad U_n(\iota(s'))$, fix the threshold
\[
 \theta=\tfrac12\Bigl(\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\kappa^2}{4|S|}\Bigr)+1\Bigr)<1,
\]
and let $P_n$ be the spectral projection of $H_n$ for the part of the
spectrum above $\theta$.  Two spectral estimates tie $P_n$ to the group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Displacement.}  For every $\gamma\in\Gamma$,
$\opnorm{(\Ad U_n(\iota(\gamma))-1)\,P_n}\to0$: a vector whose average
$S$-energy exceeds $\theta$ is almost fixed by each generator, because
$(S,\kappa)$ is a Kazhdan pair and $\theta$ exceeds
$1-\kappa^2/(4|S|)$, hence almost fixed by every word in $S$.  The
corner $P_n$ is the finite-stage substitute for the Kazhdan projection.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Capture.}  Write $\xi_n\in L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$
for the vector $x_n$.  The commutator hypothesis says each
$\Ad U_n(\iota(s'))$ almost fixes $\xi_n$, so the residual
$\xi_n-H_n\xi_n$ is Hilbert--Schmidt null; a Cauchy--Schwarz energy
estimate then bounds the mass of $(1-P_n)\xi_n$ by the residual and the
uniform bound on $\opnorm{x_n}$, so $\xi_n$ is asymptotically captured
by the corner.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Leakage by the equal-rank flip.}  Put
$T_n=\Ad U_n(s)$ and $Q_n=T_nP_nT_n^*$, a projection of the same rank as
$P_n$.  The conjugation relation enters exactly once: for
$\gamma\in\Gamma$ the element $s^{-1}$ pulls the almost-fixedness of a
corner vector under $\iota(\Gamma)\supseteq s\iota(\Gamma)s^{-1}$ back to
almost-fixedness under each $\iota(s')$, $s'\in S$; by the capture
estimate the pulled-back vector is again almost in the corner.  Thus the
corner is almost contained in its conjugate,
$\opnorm{(1-Q_n)P_n}\to0$ --- the finite-stage form of the one-sided
inclusion $P\le VPV^*$.  Since $\operatorname{rank}P_n=
\operatorname{rank}Q_n$, the equal-rank flip --- for projections $p,q$ of
equal finite rank, $\opnorm{(1-q)p}\le\varepsilon<1$ implies
$\opnorm{(1-p)q}\le\varepsilon/\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}$ --- reverses the
containment:
\[
 \boxed{\opnorm{(1-P_n)\,Q_n}\longrightarrow0.}
\]
This is the middle implication of the display at the head of the section,
and it is the finite-dimensional substitute for finiteness of a norm
ultraproduct: a one-sided compression cannot enlarge the corner.

\emph{Assembly.}  Fix $\gamma$ and $\varepsilon>0$, let $M$ bound
$\opnorm{x_n}$, and put $q=\min(1,\varepsilon/(40(M^2+1)))$.  For all
large $n$: capture places $\xi_n$ within $q$-mass of $P_n\xi_n$; hence
$T_n\xi_n$ lies close to the range of $Q_n$; leakage moves it into the
range of $P_n$; and displacement makes $\Ad U_n(\iota(\gamma))$ almost
fix it.  Bookkeeping through these three exchanges bounds the squared
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt displacement of $T_n\xi_n$ by
$18q^2M^2+16q\le\varepsilon$.  Since $T_n\xi_n$ is the vector of
$U_n(s)x_nU_n(s)^*$, this proves the asserted convergence --- with an
explicit rate in terms of $\kappa$, $|S|$, $M$, and the multiplicative
defects of $U_n$ on the finitely many group elements involved.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the ultraproduct view]\label{rem:ultraproduct}
The classical one-paragraph account of the same mechanism, recorded as
intuition only --- it is not the proof above and not the formalized
route.  Pass to a subsequence and a free ultrafilter $\omega$, form the
Hilbert ultraproduct $K_\omega$ of the spaces
$L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$ and the norm ultraproduct
$B_\omega=\prod_\omega B(K_n)$.  The adjoint actions define an honest
representation $\pi$ of $H$ on $K_\omega$, and $B_\omega$ is finite,
since polar correction turns any isometry into a unitary along $\omega$.
For $P$ the image of the Kazhdan projection \cite{BHV} under
$\pi\circ\iota$ and $V=\pi(s)$, the one-sided compression gives
$P\le VPV^*$, and finiteness forces $P=VPV^*$: writing $Q=VPV^*$ and
$r=V^*Q$, one has $r^*r=Q$ and $rr^*=P\le Q$, so $\sigma=r+(1-Q)$ is an
isometry with $\sigma\sigma^*=P+(1-Q)$, and finiteness makes $\sigma$
unitary.  The finite-stage proof replaces the ultrafilter by the explicit
threshold $\theta$ and replaces finiteness of $B_\omega$ by the
equal-rank flip.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The negative-corner picture.}
The mechanism is easiest to see one level up, and this picture is the one
to keep in mind.  Suppose a corona representation $\Theta$ of $H$ detects
a central involution $\varepsilon=u^2$, where $u=[tct^{-1},\iota(a)]$ is
a conjugation defect (data as in Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}
below).  The nonzero projection
$q=(1-\Theta(\varepsilon))/2$, central relative to $\Theta(H)$, cuts
$\Theta$ to a corner representation with $\Theta_q(\varepsilon)=-q$
exactly; a spectral cut at $1/2$ of a self-adjoint lift of $q$ produces
coordinate projections whose nonzero ranks identify the corner
$q\mathcal Qq$ with a positive-rank norm matrix corona.  On that corner,
$c$ lies in the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of
$\iota(\Gamma)$, transport
(Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}) puts $d=tct^{-1}$ there as well,
so $u\sim_21$ and hence $\varepsilon=u^2\sim_21$ --- contradicting
$\varepsilon=-1$ on the corner, whose normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
distance from $1$ is $2$.  The formal development records this
contradiction as a stand-alone theorem about marked operator-norm
asymptotic representations; the printed proofs route instead through the
finite-normal criterion of Section~\ref{sec:conj}, whose coordinatewise
Reynolds corner makes the corner identification sketched here rigorous
without ever forming $q\mathcal Qq$ abstractly.

The entire non-MF argument is the following consequence of the transport
theorem, applied to the two-element central subgroup
$\{1,\varepsilon\}$.

\begin{theorem}[central-sign criterion]\label{thm:sign-criterion}
Let $H$ and $\Gamma$ be countable groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism, and suppose
\[
 t\iota(\Gamma)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma),
 \qquad [c,\iota(\Gamma)]=1.
\]
Put $d=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[d,\iota(a)]$ for some $a\in\Gamma$.
If
\[
 \varepsilon=u^2\ne1,\qquad \varepsilon^2=1,\qquad \varepsilon\in Z(H),
\]
then every homomorphism from $H$ to a norm matrix corona kills
$\varepsilon$, and $H$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanSignCriterion}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.manuscriptCentralSignCriterion}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The data form a Kazhdan conjugation datum in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:pattern}: $\Gamma$ has property~\textup{(T)},
$t$ compresses $\iota(\Gamma)$ into itself, and $c$ centralizes it.  Its
defect subgroup $N_{\mathrm{comp}}$ --- the normal closure of
the commutators $[tct^{-1},\iota(\gamma)]$ --- contains
$u=[d,\iota(a)]$, hence also $\varepsilon=u^2$.  Since $\varepsilon$ is a
central involution, $F=\{1,\varepsilon\}$ is a finite normal subgroup of
$H$ contained in $N_{\mathrm{comp}}$.  The finite-normal obstruction
criterion --- Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, proved in
Section~\ref{sec:conj} from the transport theorem alone, with no result
in between used in its proof --- therefore kills $\varepsilon$ under
every corona representation.  Since $\varepsilon\ne1$, no corona
representation of $H$ is injective, and $H$ is not MF.
\end{proof}

The negative-corner picture is the intuition; the finite-normal criterion
is the engine.

\section{The Kazhdan--Clifford construction}\label{sec:construction}

Given $\Gamma,\alpha,a$ as in the theorem below, the construction is most
readable with one redundant generator $\varepsilon$: declare it to be a
central involution and impose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conceptual-sign}
 \boxed{\varepsilon=u^2,\qquad u=[tct^{-1},a].}
\end{equation}
This is the conceptual presentation: rigidity forces $u\sim_2 1$, whereas
the Clifford model makes $u^2=-1$.  The theorem below uses the
Tietze-eliminated version.  Since $d=tct^{-1}$ is an involution,
$u^2=[d,ada^{-1}]$; conversely, centrality of $u^2$ forces $(u^2)^2=1$.
Thus the redundant sign costs one generator and saves the reader the only
opaque word in the construction.

\begin{theorem}[Kazhdan--Clifford construction]
\label{thm:kazhdan-clifford}
Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely presented group with property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\alpha\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Gamma$ be an injective endomorphism, and
choose $a\in\Gamma\setminus\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Adjoin elements $t,c$ and impose
\[
 t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma),\qquad c^2=1,\qquad [c,\gamma]=1
 \quad(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]
Put
\[
 d=tct^{-1},\qquad u=[d,a],\qquad w=u^2=[d,ada^{-1}],
\]
and impose centrality of $w$.
More explicitly, for a finite generating set $S_\Gamma$ it is enough to
impose the conjugation and commutation relations for $s\in S_\Gamma$, together with
\[
 [w,s]=1\quad(s\in S_\Gamma),\qquad [w,t]=[w,c]=1.
\]
Thus $w$ commutes with a generating set of the entire resulting group.  The
group $E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is finitely presented,
the canonical map $\Gamma\to E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is injective, and $w$ is a
nontrivial central involution.  Every homomorphism
\[
 E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)\longrightarrow \U(\mathcal Q)
\]
to the unitary group of every norm matrix corona kills $w$.  Consequently
$E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanCliffordConstruction}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCliffordConstruction.kazhdanCliffordConstruction}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite presentability follows from a relative finite presentation: start with
the free product of $\Gamma$ and the free group on $t,c$, and quotient by the
finitely many displayed relations for a finite generating set of $\Gamma$.

The relation $w^2=1$ is redundant.  Put $b=ada^{-1}$.  Both $d$ and $b$ are
involutions, $w=[d,b]=(db)^2$, and direct cancellation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:involution-reversal}
 dwd^{-1}=w^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Centrality also gives $dwd^{-1}=w$, whence $w=w^{-1}$ and $w^2=1$.

For nontriviality, realize the ascending HNN extension
\[
 G=\langle\Gamma,t\mid t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)\rangle
\]
as a mapping telescope.  Let $\Gamma_\infty$ be the direct limit of the
system $\Gamma\xrightarrow{\alpha}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\alpha}\cdots$;
injectivity of $\alpha$ passes to the limit, so the level-zero map embeds
$\Gamma$ in $\Gamma_\infty$.  Applying $\alpha$ levelwise induces an
\emph{automorphism} $\sigma$ of $\Gamma_\infty$: it is injective, and it
is surjective because an element at level $k$ equals $\sigma$ of the same
element at level $k+1$.  Put $G=\Gamma_\infty\rtimes_\sigma\Z$, with
stable letter $t$ implementing $\sigma$; then $t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$
for $\gamma\in\Gamma$, and we write $X=G/\Gamma$ for the coset space of
the level-zero copy.  Let $G$ permute the Clifford generators
$(e_x)_{x\in X}$ and map $c$ to $e_\Gamma$.  Then
\[
 d\longmapsto e_{t\Gamma},\qquad
 ada^{-1}\longmapsto e_{at\Gamma}.
\]
The two cosets are distinct: equality would give
$t^{-1}at\in\Gamma$, equivalently $a\in\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Hence the two
Clifford generators anticommute and
\[
 w\longmapsto(e_{t\Gamma}e_{at\Gamma})^2=-1.
\]
The Clifford sign is central, so the map respects every defining relation.
It follows simultaneously that $w\ne1$ and that the canonical map of
$\Gamma$ is injective.

Finally, $w=u^2$ is a nontrivial central involution.  The central-sign
criterion therefore says that every corona representation kills $w$.
Since $w\ne1$, none is faithful.
\end{proof}

The sign argument has a useful Clifford-free strengthening.  For a subgroup
$L\le H$, let $G_{\rm comp}(L)$ be generated by the elements $s$ satisfying
$sLs^{-1}\subseteq L$ --- the \emph{one-sided conjugators} of $L$ --- and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsicdefect}
 \mathfrak D(H,L)=
 \left\langle\!\left\langle
 [gzg^{-1},\ell]:g\in G_{\rm comp}(L),\ z\in C_H(L),\ \ell\in L
 \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_H.
\end{equation}

Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-radical} in
Section~\ref{sec:conj} upgrades the sign criterion to this intrinsic
defect: in the presence of a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup
$L=\iota(\Gamma)$, every finite normal subgroup of $H$ contained in
$\mathfrak D(H,L)$ is killed by every corona representation of $H$.

The same element $a$ drives both halves of the proof.  Analytically,
$a\in\Gamma$, so fixed-space transport forces the displaced lamp to commute
with its image in finite-dimensional norm models.  Geometrically,
$a\notin\alpha(\Gamma)$, so $a$ moves that lamp to a different HNN coset,
where Clifford anticommutation produces $-1$.  Thus $a$ lives on the boundary
of the self-embedding: rigidity cannot detect that boundary, while the
Clifford representation detects it by a sign.

The following theorem is the fully explicit instance, developed in
Sections~\ref{sec:group}--\ref{sec:proofA}.

\begin{mainthm}[explicit Kazhdan--Clifford group]\label{thm:A}
Let $E$ be the group on the eight generators
$v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c$ with the forty-one relators displayed in
Definition~\ref{def:E}.  Put
\[
 w=[tct^{-1},\,v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}].
\]
The defining relations make $w$ central; together with $c^2=1$, centrality
implies $w^2=1$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $w\neq 1$ in $E$, so $w$ is a central involution;
  \item for every sequence $(d_n)$ of positive integers and every group homomorphism
    $\Theta\colon E\to\Ucor((d_n))$ into the corresponding unitary norm
    corona \eqref{eq:unitary-corona}, one has $\Theta(w)=1$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $E$ is not MF, and neither $\Cmax(E)$ nor $\Cred(E)$ is an
MF $C^*$-algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremA}
\end{mainthm}

For the group-approximation reader, the obstruction has an equivalent
finite, dimension-free form in the usual local-model language, with no
corona or ultrafilter mentioned:

\begin{corollary}[uniform finite operator-norm obstruction]\label{cor:uniform}
There are a number $\delta>0$ and a finite set $F_0\subset E$ such that,
in every matrix dimension, any unitary-valued map $\phi$ satisfying
\[
 \opnorm{\phi(gh)-\phi(g)\phi(h)}\leq\delta
 \qquad(g,h\in F_0)
\]
also satisfies $\opnorm{\phi(w)-1}<1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralUniformObstruction}{GroupApproximation.LiteralUniformObstruction.literal_uniform_operatorNorm_obstruction}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If no such pair $(\delta,F_0)$ existed, then choosing, for each $n$, a
model violating the conclusion for $\delta=1/n$ and the $n$th set of an
exhaustion of $E$ would produce an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $E$ whose values at $w$ stay at distance at least $1$
from the identity; its class in the unitary-sequence corona would be a
corona representation detecting $w$, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(2).  This compactness argument computes no explicit
$\delta$ or $F_0$; obtaining an effective modulus directly from the
rational spectral certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:group} remains a sharper quantitative problem.
\end{proof}

The exponent $2$ is not special.  For $m\ge2$, let $E_m$ be the same
eight-generator presentation, replacing the three relations
$tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^2$ by $tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^m$ and leaving every other
relator unchanged; thus $E_2=E$.  Write $w_m$ for the image of the same
marked word $w$.

\begin{corollary}[the scaling family]\label{cor:scaling-family}
For every natural number $m\ge2$, the group $E_m$ is finitely presented and
$w_m$ is a nontrivial central involution killed by every homomorphism from
$E_m$ to every unitary norm matrix corona.  Consequently $E_m$ is not MF,
and neither $\Cmax(E_m)$ nor $\Cred(E_m)$ is an MF $C^*$-algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/ScalingFamilyEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.ScalingFamilyEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremFamily}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Replace the affine dilation $\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)$ by
$\operatorname{diag}(m,m,m,1)$ (both acting as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}).  It conjugates each integral translation
$v_i$ to $v_i^m$ and fixes the three linear generators.  Its inverse sends
$v_1$ to translation by $e_1/m$, which is not integral; hence the two
Clifford sites remain distinct and $w_m$ maps to $-1$.  The Kazhdan base,
the one-sided conjugation argument, and the central-sign criterion are
independent of $m$.
\end{proof}

For the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, put $d=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[d,v_1]$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:square} gives the useful identity $w=u^2$.
The proof has two opposed halves, both centered on $u$:
\[
 \boxed{\text{matrix-corona rigidity: }u\sim_2 1}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \boxed{\text{Clifford model: }u^2=-1.}
\]
For a corona representation that detects $w$, cut to the negative central
corner of $w$.  Kazhdan transport gives $u\sim_2 1$, hence $w=u^2\sim_2 1$;
the corner identity gives $w=-1$.  This contradiction is the picture
behind Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, and it is independently formalized as a
stand-alone theorem about marked asymptotic representations.
Section~\ref{sec:proofA} deduces the theorem from
the sign criterion and the witness, and Section~\ref{sec:conj} proves the
general finite-normal criterion that powers the sign criterion.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.negativeCorner_kazhdanTransport_contradiction}

Nontriviality of $w$ is witnessed by a completely explicit
representation of $E$ into a semidirect product of a Clifford group by a
mapping-telescope group built from the exact rational affine matrix model
(Section~\ref{sec:witness}); no
combinatorial theory of HNN extensions is needed.

\subsection*{Generalizations and consequences}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is an instance of a general finite-normal obstruction
(Definition~\ref{def:pattern}).  A Kazhdan conjugation datum consists of a
property~\textup{(T)} group $\Lambda$, a homomorphism
$\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$, and elements $t,c\in H$ such that
$t\iota(\Lambda)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Lambda)$ and
$[c,\iota(\Lambda)]=1$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} sends every finite
normal subgroup contained in the resulting defect subgroup to
the identity under every corona representation.
For $E$, the relevant subgroup is $\{1,w\}$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:nofaithful}
records the resulting obstruction for $C^*$-subalgebras of norm matrix coronas,
while
Theorem~\ref{thm:C} shows that finite-dimensional collapse by itself does
not decide whether the marked word is trivial.  The same instance yields a
finite universal Horn sentence valid in every MF group and falsified by
$E$, hence a nonempty clopen set of non-MF marked groups
(Proposition~\ref{prop:horn} and Corollary~\ref{cor:cylinder}), and the
failures of quotient and extension permanence
(Corollary~\ref{cor:quotclosure} and Section~\ref{sec:consequences}).

The companion theorems are stated in their home sections:
Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, the exact finite-dimensional analogue, opens
Section~\ref{sec:findim}; Theorem~\ref{thm:C}, the cyclic comparison,
opens Section~\ref{sec:necessity}; and Theorems~\ref{thm:D}
and~\ref{thm:E}, the reduced-algebra and sofic--hyperlinear
separations, open the corresponding subsections of
Section~\ref{sec:consequences}.

\section{The explicit presentation and its property-(T) base}\label{sec:group}

We work throughout with the groups defined by the relators below.  The
proof of property~\textup{(T)} is intrinsic to these presentations: an
exact rational spectral certificate for an auxiliary Steinberg-type
presentation, a replayed surjection onto the linear presentation, and a
fixed-point bridge from the linear subgroup to the full base.  No
identification with a matrix group enters the proof, and every later
relation involving the base's image in $E$ is used directly from
the displayed presentation.
First define the \emph{linear presentation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sl3presentation}
 \mathcal R=\left\langle x,y,z\ \middle|\
 \begin{gathered}
 x^3=y^3=z^2=(xz)^3=(yz)^3=1,\\
 (x^{-1}zxy)^2=(y^{-1}zyx)^2=(xy)^6=1
 \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
and the \emph{base}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-presentation}
\mathcal B=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\ \middle|\
\begin{gathered}
\text{the eight relations in \eqref{eq:sl3presentation}},\\
[v_1,v_2]=[v_1,v_3]=[v_2,v_3]=1,\\
xv_1x^{-1}=v_3,\ xv_2x^{-1}=v_1,\
xv_3x^{-1}=v_2,\\
yv_1y^{-1}=v_1,\ yv_2y^{-1}=v_2^{-1}v_3,\
yv_3y^{-1}=v_1v_2^{-1},\\
zv_1z^{-1}=v_2v_3^{-1},\ zv_2z^{-1}=v_1v_3^{-1},\
zv_3z^{-1}=v_3^{-1}
\end{gathered}\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Write $\iota\colon\mathcal B\to E$ for the homomorphism induced by the first
six generators of the presentation below.  This map is not assumed to be
injective.

\begin{definition}\label{def:E}
Use the six base generators and the relations displayed in
\eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
  E=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\ \middle|\
  \begin{gathered}
   \text{all relations in \eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}},\\
   tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^2\ (1\le i\le3),\\
   txt^{-1}=x,\quad tyt^{-1}=y,\quad tzt^{-1}=z,\\
   c^2=1,\\
   [c,g]=1\quad(g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
   [w,g]=1\quad(g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\})
  \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $w$ abbreviates the word
$\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\,v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}\bigr]$.
This is an explicit finite presentation with eight generators and
forty-one relators: the twenty base relators, six stable-letter
relators, $c^2$, the six relations making $c$ commute with the base
generators, and eight centrality relators.  Its defining
relations make $w$ central, and centrality together with $c^2=1$ implies
$w^2=1$ by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}; the relations on the six base
generators also give
\[
 t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\mathcal B),
 \qquad [c,\iota(\mathcal B)]=1.
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFPresentation}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFPresentation.manuscriptLiteralPresentation}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Six generators suffice.}
The group $E$ is generated by the six elements $v_1,x,y,z,t,c$; in
particular its minimal number of generators is at most six.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSixGenerator}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSixGenerator.literal_sixGenerated_finitelyPresented_nonMF}
There is also a Tietze-equivalent presentation on these six generators with
thirty-two relators; the routine calculation is printed for orientation,
and the formal development uses only the six-generation bound above.
In \eqref{eq:E}, eliminate $v_3$ and $v_2$ by the Tietze substitutions
\[
 v_3=xv_1x^{-1},\qquad v_2=x^2v_1x^{-2}.
\]
Delete the two relations used as definitions.  After substitution, the
remaining $x$-action relation follows from $x^3=1$.  The two stable-letter
relations for $v_2,v_3$ follow from the one for $v_1$ and $[t,x]=1$; the two
$c$-commutation relations and two centrality relations for $v_2,v_3$
similarly follow
from those for $v_1$ and $x$.  These six redundant relators may therefore be
deleted.  Starting from forty-one relators, the two Tietze definitions, the
remaining $x$-action relation, and those six consequences leave thirty-two,
on the generators $v_1,x,y,z,t,c$.

Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} below proves that $w\neq1$.

\subsection{Property~\textup{(T)} of the base}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:literal-base-T}
The twenty-relator group $\mathcal B$ has property~\textup{(T)}, in both
the real-orthogonal and complex-unitary formulations.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralBaseP13PropertyTBridge}{GroupApproximation.LiteralBaseP13PropertyTBridge.manuscriptBaseHasKazhdanPropertyT}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof is intrinsic: it imports no presentation theorem and no
classical property~\textup{(T)} statement, and it never identifies
$\mathcal B$ with a matrix group.  It has three steps.

\emph{Step 1: a certified Steinberg-type presentation.}  Let $P_{13}$ be
the group on the six generators
$e_{12},e_{13},e_{21},e_{23},e_{31},e_{32}$ with the thirteen relators
\[
\begin{gathered}
 [e_{12},e_{23}]=e_{13},\qquad [e_{13},e_{32}]=e_{12},\qquad
 [e_{21},e_{13}]=e_{23},\\
 [e_{23},e_{31}]=e_{21},\qquad [e_{31},e_{12}]=e_{32},\qquad
 [e_{32},e_{21}]=e_{31},\\
 [e_{12},e_{13}]=[e_{12},e_{32}]=[e_{13},e_{23}]
 =[e_{21},e_{23}]=[e_{21},e_{31}]=[e_{31},e_{32}]=1,\\
 (e_{12}\,e_{21}^{-1}\,e_{12})^{4}=1,
\end{gathered}
\]
the elementary-matrix relations in rank three.  An exact sum-of-squares
certificate with integer matrix data, verified by denominator-free
rational arithmetic, proves the degree-one Hodge estimate for $P_{13}$
with spectral gap $\tfrac1{500}$; testing it on coboundaries gives, in
every orthogonal representation, the quadratic gap
\[
 \lVert\Delta\xi\rVert^2\ \ge\ \tfrac1{500}\,\langle\Delta\xi,\xi\rangle
\]
for the six-generator Laplacian $\Delta$.  The gap places the spectrum of
$\Delta$ in $\{0\}\cup[\tfrac1{500},\infty)$, and the kernel of $\Delta$
is exactly the space of invariant vectors; a unit vector almost fixed by
the six generators has small Laplacian energy and is therefore close to
an invariant vector.  This yields a Kazhdan pair: $P_{13}$ has
property~\textup{(T)}.  Certificates of this kind go back to Ozawa's
algebraic characterization of property~\textup{(T)} \cite{Ozawa} and its
computer-assisted implementations for $\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$
\cite{NetzerThom}; the certificate used here is exact and is replayed in
full inside the formal development.

\emph{Step 2: the replayed surjection onto the linear presentation.}
Map the six generators to the words
\[
\begin{gathered}
 e_{12}\mapsto y^{-1}xzy^{-1}x^{-1},\quad
 e_{13}\mapsto xzy^{-1}x^{-1}y,\quad
 e_{21}\mapsto y^{-1}xyxz,\\
 e_{23}\mapsto x^{-1}y^{-1}xzy^{-1},\quad
 e_{31}\mapsto xy^{-1}xzy^{-1}x,\quad
 e_{32}\mapsto x^{-1}zy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-1}
\end{gathered}
\]
in the linear presentation $\mathcal R$ of \eqref{eq:sl3presentation}.
A word-by-word replay inside $\mathcal R$, using only its eight displayed
relators, checks that all thirteen relators of $P_{13}$ map to the
identity, so the assignment defines a homomorphism
$P_{13}\to\mathcal R$.  The same replay exhibits explicit words in the
six images whose values are $z$ and $xy$, and a further replay shows that
$z$ and $xy$ generate $\mathcal R$; hence the homomorphism is surjective.
Property~\textup{(T)} passes to quotients, so $\mathcal R$ has
property~\textup{(T)}.

\emph{Step 3: the fixed-point bridge to the base.}  The generators
$x,y,z$ of $\mathcal B$ satisfy the relators of $\mathcal R$, so
$\mathcal R$ maps to the linear subgroup of $\mathcal B$ they generate,
and Step~2
gives a finite set $S$ of words in $x,y,z$ and a $\kappa>0$ forming a
Kazhdan pair for it.  Let $\rho$ be an orthogonal representation of
$\mathcal B$ with a unit vector $\xi$ displaced by less than $\kappa/64$ by
every element of the finite control set consisting of $S$ together with
the translations $v_2,v_3$.  Three estimates produce an invariant vector
near $\xi$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Projecting $\xi$ to the subspace of vectors fixed by the linear
subgroup gives such a fixed vector $p$ with $\lVert p-\xi\rVert<1/64$, by the
moving-projection estimate for the Kazhdan pair.
\item Translations move $p$ by a bound independent of the lattice
vector.  Three explicit words in $x,y,z$ act on the
translation lattice as elementary transvections, and consequently every
lattice element is a product of two linear-subgroup conjugates of basis
translations --- a two-conjugate normal form proved inside the
presentation.  A conjugate $rvr^{-1}$ with $r$ in the linear subgroup
displaces the fixed vector $p$ exactly as much as $v$ does, and each control
translation displaces $p$ by less than $3/64$ (transferring its bound at
$\xi$ across $\lVert p-\xi\rVert$).  Hence every translation displaces $p$
by at most $1/8$.
\item The orbit of $p$ under the translation subgroup is bounded, so the
Hilbert-space circumcenter argument applies: the projection $\eta$ of $p$
to the translation-fixed subspace satisfies $\lVert \eta-p\rVert\le1/8$.
The linear subgroup normalizes the translation subgroup and fixes $p$,
so $\eta$ is fixed by it as well; translations and the linear generators
generate $\mathcal B$,
so $\eta$ is $\mathcal B$-invariant, and
$\lVert \eta-\xi\rVert\le 1/8+1/64<1$ keeps $\eta$ nonzero.
\end{enumerate}
Thus almost-invariant vectors produce invariant vectors and $\mathcal B$
has property~\textup{(T)} in the real-orthogonal formulation;
realification and complexification identify it with the complex-unitary
formulation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the classical identification]\label{rem:classical-base}
Classically, the eight relations \eqref{eq:sl3presentation} present
$\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$ \cite[Theorem~2]{CRW} --- the matrix-labelled form of
the generators in Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} is also recorded in \cite{CLV}
--- the base $\mathcal B$ is then the affine group
$\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$, and property~\textup{(T)} of that
affine group is classical \cite[Example~1.7.4(i)]{BHV}.  This
identification is quoted for orientation only: the proof above does not
use it, and no statement in this paper depends on it.
\end{remark}

\section{The witness: \texorpdfstring{$w\neq 1$}{w is nontrivial}}\label{sec:witness}

Clifford signs, shifts, and HNN doubling have appeared together before in
approximate-representation theory, notably in Slofstra's hyperlinear-profile
construction \cite[Section~2]{SlofstraProfile}.  The construction below uses
the Clifford lamp only as an explicit algebraic witness.  It uses the
displayed quotient of the base and is independent of the classical
identification recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:classical-base}.

\begin{construction}[Clifford lamp groups]\label{con:clifford}
For a set $X$, use the real Clifford-algebra convention generated by
$(c_x)_{x\in X}$ with $c_x^2=1$ and $c_xc_y=-c_yc_x$ for $x\ne y$, and let
$\ClLamp(X)$ be the subgroup of units generated by $-1$ and the $c_x$.
Write $\zeta=-1$.  In this concrete group,
\[
 \zeta^2=c_x^2=1,\qquad c_xc_y=\zeta c_yc_x\quad(x\ne y).
\]
The sign $\zeta$ is nontrivial.  Thus $\ClLamp(X)$ is a central
extension, by the sign $\zeta$, of the elementary abelian $2$-group on
the sites $X$: distinct site involutions commute only up to $\zeta$,
unlike the commuting lamps of a classical lamplighter.  Every
permutation of $X$ acts on this
group by $c_x\mapsto c_{\sigma(x)}$ and fixes $\zeta$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptCliffordConstruction}
\end{construction}

\begin{lemma}[linear model]\label{lem:linear}
For $M\in\GL_3(\mathbb Q)$ and $u\in\mathbb Q^3$, write
\[
 A(M,u)=\begin{pmatrix}M&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Assign $v_i\mapsto A(I_3,e_i)$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},0\right),
&y&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&-1&-1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},0\right),\\[3pt]
 z&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\-1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix},0\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let $\bar\Gamma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ be the subgroup generated by these six
matrices, and put
$D=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)$.  Direct matrix computation proves that
the six matrices satisfy all twenty relators of $\mathcal B$ and that
\[
 \bar\alpha(u)=DuD^{-1}
\]
is an injective endomorphism of $\bar\Gamma$ which squares the three
translation generators and fixes $x,y,z$.  Moreover the matrix assigned to
$v_1$ does not lie in $\operatorname{range}(\bar\alpha)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptLinearModel}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The twenty relator checks reduce to sixteen rational identities for each
matrix equation.  Conjugation by $D$ gives the six stable-letter equations
and is injective because it is conjugation by an invertible matrix.  Every
displayed generator and its explicitly computed inverse has integral entries;
equivalently, all six generators lie in $\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$.  Hence
$\bar\Gamma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$.  However $D^{-1}\bar v_1D$ has entry $1/2$
in position $(1,4)$, so it is not in $\bar\Gamma$.  This proves the last
assertion.
\end{proof}

Form the direct limit $T=\varinjlim(\bar\Gamma,\bar\alpha)$ --- the
algebraic mapping telescope of $\bar\alpha$ --- extend it by its shift
$\tau$, and call the resulting group $V=T\rtimes\mathbb Z$.  Let
$j\colon\bar\Gamma\hookrightarrow V$ be its level-zero map and put
$X=V/j(\bar\Gamma)$, with root $o=j(\bar\Gamma)$.  The final assertion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} is exactly the statement that
\[
 \tau o\ne j(\bar v_1)\tau o.
\]
Thus the Clifford generators at these two sites anticommute.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:witness}
$w\neq1$ in $E$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFLinearWitness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFLinearWitness.literal_mark_ne_one}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Form $W:=\ClLamp(X)\rtimes V$ as in Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.
Define a map on the generators of \eqref{eq:E} by
\[
\begin{gathered}
  g\mapsto j(\bar g)\in V\quad
  (g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
  t\mapsto\tau,\qquad c\mapsto c_o .
\end{gathered}
\]
The exact checks in Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} give the twenty base and six
stable-letter relations.  The level-zero subgroup fixes $o$, so $c_o$
commutes with every base generator, and $c_o^2=1$.
Under this assignment,
\[
  tct^{-1}\mapsto c_{\tau o},\qquad
  v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}\mapsto c_{j(\bar v_1)\tau o},
\]
and the two sites are distinct by Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}, so
\[
  w\mapsto[c_{\tau o},c_{j(\bar v_1)\tau o}]=-1=\zeta .
\]
Since $\zeta$ is central in $W$, the remaining relations $[w,g]=1$ for
$g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\}$ are also satisfied.  Hence the
assignment extends to a group homomorphism $E\to W$ sending $w$ to
$\zeta\neq1$, and therefore $w\neq1$ in $E$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the witness.}
The target uses the infinite group $\ClLamp(X)$ when $X$ is
infinite, but each group element involves only finitely many Clifford sites;
the remaining data are rational matrices.  No normal-form theorem for an HNN
extension enters: the mapping telescope supplies the required group and the
range characterization separates the two sites.

\section{The defect square and the proof of Theorem A}\label{sec:proofA}

\begin{lemma}[the marked word is a defect square]\label{lem:square}
Let $H_1$ be a group and $d,a_1\in H_1$ with $d^2=1$.  Then
\[
  \bigl[\,d,\;a_1\,d\,a_1^{-1}\bigr]\;=\;[\,d,a_1\,]^{\,2} .
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/CompressionDefectSquare}{GroupApproximation.commutator_conjugate_eq_commutator_sq_of_sq_eq_one}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $b=a_1da_1^{-1}$, so $b^2=a_1d^2a_1^{-1}=1$; thus $b^{-1}=b$ and
$d^{-1}=d$.  Then $[d,a_1]=da_1d^{-1}a_1^{-1}=d\,(a_1da_1^{-1})=db$,
and
\[
  [d,a_1]^2=(db)^2=db\,db=d\,b\,d^{-1}b^{-1}=[d,b] . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Part (1) is Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  For part (2), let
$\Theta\colon E\to\Ucor((d_n))$ be given and pass to the corona
representation $\kappa_{(d_n)}\circ\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}.  Apply the central-sign criterion
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}) with $\Gamma=\mathcal B$, the canonical
homomorphism $\iota$, the elements $t,c\in E$, and $a=v_1$.  Its
hypotheses are exactly the displayed relators:
Proposition~\ref{prop:literal-base-T} gives property~\textup{(T)} of
$\mathcal B$; the six stable-letter relators give
$t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\mathcal B)$; and the six
$c$-commutation relators give $[c,\iota(\mathcal B)]=1$.  No injectivity of $\iota$ is
required.  In $E$ we have $d^2=(tct^{-1})^2=tc^2t^{-1}=1$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:square} with $a_1=\iota(v_1)$ identifies the marked word as
the square of the conjugation defect $u=[d,\iota(v_1)]$:
\[
  w=\bigl[\,d,\;\iota(v_1)\,d\,\iota(v_1)^{-1}\bigr]
  =[\,d,\iota(v_1)\,]^{\,2}=u^2 .
\]
Centrality of $w$ is a defining relator; together with $c^2=1$ it forces
$w^2=1$ by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}; and $w\ne1$ by part (1).  The
criterion therefore kills $w$ in every corona representation of $E$.
Since $\kappa_{(d_n)}$ is injective, $\Theta(w)=1$.  This proves part (2).

\medskip

For the final assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}: an MF embedding of
$E$ would be an injective corona representation, which is impossible
because every corona representation sends the nontrivial element $w$
to $1$.  If $\Cred(E)$ were an MF algebra, an injective, possibly nonunital
$*$-homomorphism $e\colon\Cred(E)\to\mathcal Q$ would give the injective
unitary homomorphism $u\mapsto e(u)+(1-e(1))$.  Composing it with the
canonical map $E\to\U(\Cred(E))$, and then with
$\kappa_{(d_n)}^{-1}$, would exhibit an injective corona representation of
$E$: the map $g\mapsto\lambda_g$ is injective.  The canonical map
$E\to\U(\Cmax(E))$ is injective as well, because the left regular
representation factors through $\Cmax(E)$ and sends $u_g$ to $\lambda_g$.
Thus an MF embedding of $\Cmax(E)$ would again produce an
injective corona representation of $E$ after the same
$\kappa_{(d_n)}^{-1}$ conversion.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The marked radical element.}
The proof shows more than non-injectivity of each individual
$\Theta$: the fixed element $w$ lies in the kernel of \emph{every}
corona representation of $E$, so every corona representation factors
through $E/\langle w\rangle$ (note $\langle w\rangle=\{1,w\}$ is
central, hence normal).  In the language of approximation radicals, $w$
is a nontrivial element of the ``MF radical'' of $E$.
This terminology is meant in analogy with Slofstra's earlier quotients
$G^{\mathrm{fin}}$ and $G^{\mathrm{fa}}$, obtained by killing elements
invisible to exact and approximate finite-dimensional representations,
respectively \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} the same holds in every countable
group with a Kazhdan conjugation datum: every finite normal subgroup of
its defect subgroup lies in the MF radical.
Section~\ref{sec:consequences} develops the radical systematically;
we do not know whether $E/\langle w\rangle$ is MF, i.e.\ whether the
MF radical of $E$ is exactly $\{1,w\}$.

\paragraph{No density hypothesis.}
No lower bound on the corner ranks $r_n$, or on their density in
$d_n$, is used anywhere: the coordinate Reynolds corner in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} discards zero-rank coordinates and equips
each retained corner block with its own
normalized trace.  Thus all subsequent
Hilbert--Schmidt estimates are relative to that block.  This is
what makes the operator-norm setting insensitive to small relative ranks,
which block the same argument in Hilbert--Schmidt metrics
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{The finite-dimensional obstruction}\label{sec:findim}

In finite dimensions the obstruction is elementary and needs neither
property (T), nor unitarity, nor centrality of $w$; it kills the entire
intrinsic defect \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}:

\begin{mainthm}[the exact finite-dimensional analogue]\label{thm:B}
Let $k$ be a field, $V$ a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, $H$ a
group, $\Gamma\le H$ a subgroup, and $t,c\in H$, $a\in\Gamma$ elements
with $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ and $c\gamma=\gamma c$ for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$.  Then every homomorphism $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$
satisfies
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)\;=\;1 .
\]
More generally, every such $\pi$ sends the entire subgroup
$\mathfrak D(H,\Gamma)$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptTheoremB}
\end{mainthm}

Applied to $H=E$, Theorem~\ref{thm:B} shows that \emph{every}
finite-dimensional representation of $E$ over \emph{every} field sends
$w$ to the identity: finite-dimensional linear representations do not separate the points
of $E$, no finite
quotient can witness $w\neq 1$, and the infinite Clifford witness of
Section~\ref{sec:witness} cannot be replaced by a finite-dimensional one.
In the terminology of \cite[Definition~1.2]{BDL}, the central involution
$w$, invisible in every finite quotient, makes $E$ a group of
\emph{Deligne type}; such groups were proposed there as the natural
candidate counterexamples for all three remaining approximation problems,
and $E$ realizes that proposal at the operator-norm endpoint.
Property~\textup{(T)} enters the corona argument through a finite-stage
spectral corner for the adjoint action at each coordinate, and the
equal-rank projection flip turns the one-sided compression of that
corner into equality.

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.  Everything happens inside the
finite-dimensional algebra $\End_k(V)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
Let $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$ be a homomorphism and let
\[
  C\;=\;\{\,x\in\End_k(V)\;:\;\pi(\gamma)\,x=x\,\pi(\gamma)
  \text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\,\}
\]
be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$, a linear subspace of $\End_k(V)$.
Let $u=\pi(t)$ and consider the linear automorphism
$\Phi(x)=uxu^{-1}$ of $\End_k(V)$.

We claim $C\subseteq\Phi(C)$.  Let $x\in C$ and set $y=u^{-1}xu$, so
that $x=\Phi(y)$.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we have
$t\gamma t^{-1}\in\Gamma$, hence $\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})$ commutes with
$x$, and therefore
\[
  \pi(\gamma)\,y
  = u^{-1}\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,x\,u
  = u^{-1}\,x\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,u
  = y\,\pi(\gamma).
\]
Thus $y\in C$, proving the claim.  Since $\Phi$ is a linear
isomorphism, $\dim_k\Phi(C)=\dim_kC<\infty$, and the inclusion
$C\subseteq\Phi(C)$ forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinning}
  C\;=\;\Phi(C)\;=\;uCu^{-1}.
\end{equation}

Now $\pi(c)\in C$ because $c$ centralizes $\Gamma$.  By
\eqref{eq:pinning}, $\pi(tct^{-1})=u\,\pi(c)\,u^{-1}\in C$.  Since
$a\in\Gamma$, the element $\pi(tct^{-1})$ commutes with $\pi(a)$, so
$\pi\bigl(a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr)=\pi(tct^{-1})$, and therefore
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)
  =\bigl[\,\pi(tct^{-1}),\;\pi(tct^{-1})\bigr]=1. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The same calculation proves the final, subgroup-valued assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.  The elements of $H$ whose conjugation action
preserves $C$ in both directions form a subgroup.  The argument above
puts every one-sided conjugator in that subgroup, and therefore puts all
of $G_{\rm comp}(\Gamma)$ in it.  Thus, for $g\in
G_{\rm comp}(\Gamma)$ and $z\in C_H(\Gamma)$, the operator
$\pi(gzg^{-1})$ still belongs to $C$ and commutes with every
$\pi(\ell)$, $\ell\in\Gamma$.  Hence $\pi$ kills all the generators
in \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}, and normality of its kernel kills their
normal closure.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:notRFD}
The group $E$ of Definition~\ref{def:E} admits no injective homomorphism
into $\GL(V)$ for any finite-dimensional vector space $V$ over any field.
More strongly, every finite-dimensional linear representation maps $w$ to
the identity, and every homomorphism to a finite group maps $w$ to the
identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralFiniteDimensionalObstruction}{GroupApproximation.LiteralFiniteDimensionalObstruction.manuscriptFiniteDimensionalConsequences}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with $H=E$, the image of $\mathcal B$, and the
elements $t,c,v_1$; the hypotheses hold by the defining relations
\eqref{eq:E}.  Every finite-dimensional representation kills $w$, and
$w\neq1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  For a finite target, compose
with its faithful left regular representation on the group algebra over
$\mathbb Q$: the composite is a finite-dimensional linear representation,
so it kills $w$, and evaluating the resulting identity matrix at the basis
vector of the identity element returns the image of $w$ in the finite
group.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the corona argument is different.}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} used only that a finite-dimensional
subspace cannot be properly contained in an isomorphic image of itself.
An MF model is only asymptotically multiplicative, so its coordinate fixed
spaces are not literal invariant subspaces.  Operator-norm control keeps
the coordinate adjoint actions almost multiplicative on the
Hilbert--Schmidt spaces; property~(T) then supplies the spectral corner
playing the role of an approximate fixed space, and the equal-rank flip
turns the one-sided compression of that corner into equality --- the
quantitative shadow of the exact finite-dimensional argument above.

\section{The finite-normal obstruction}\label{sec:conj}

This section proves the general finite-normal obstruction that powers
the central-sign criterion and hence Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.  Its proof
uses the transport theorem and a coordinatewise operator-norm corner,
and nothing after Section~\ref{sec:transport}, so the forward reference
from the sign criterion is not circular.  We fix the data of the
criterion.

\begin{definition}[Kazhdan conjugation datum]\label{def:pattern}
Let $H$ be a countable group.  A \emph{Kazhdan conjugation datum} in $H$
is a tuple $(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, where $\Lambda$ is a countable
group with property (T), $\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ is a group
homomorphism --- \emph{not} assumed injective --- and $t,c\in H$,
subject to
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(M1)] $t\,\iota(\lambda)\,t^{-1}\in\iota(\Lambda)$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$;
  \item[(M2)] $c\,\iota(\lambda)=\iota(\lambda)\,c$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
Write $d:=tct^{-1}$.  The \emph{defect subgroup} of the
datum is the normal closure
\[
  N_{\mathrm{comp}}
  \;=\;\bigl\langle\!\bigl\langle\,
  [\,d,\iota(\lambda)\,]\;:\;\lambda\in\Lambda
  \,\bigr\rangle\!\bigr\rangle_H\;\trianglelefteq\;H .
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMarkedKazhdanPattern}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[finite-normal obstruction criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $H$ be a countable group with a Kazhdan conjugation datum
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, and let $F\trianglelefteq H$ be a finite
normal subgroup with $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$.  Then every
corona representation $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies
$\Theta(f)=1$ for all $f\in F$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptFiniteNormalObstructionCriterion}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The argument has two independent halves.  The first, which uses the
Kazhdan datum, is a kill theorem in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm;
the second, which uses only finiteness and normality of $F$, converts
that tracial invisibility into an exact operator-norm contradiction on a
coordinate corner.

\emph{Half one: the defect dies tracially.}  Let $(U_{g,n})$ be
coordinate unitary lifts of any operator-norm almost representation of
$H$ (in particular, of a corona representation), and put
\[
  K_2=\{g\in H:\hsnorm{U_{g,n}-1}\longrightarrow0\}.
\]
Unitary invariance and asymptotic multiplicativity make
$K_2\trianglelefteq H$.  Since $c$ centralizes $\iota(\Lambda)$, its
lifts belong to the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of
$\iota(\Lambda)$; applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} to $x_n=U_{c,n}$ puts the lifts of
$d=tct^{-1}$ there as well, and therefore
$[d,\iota(\lambda)]\in K_2$ for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$.  Hence
$N_{\mathrm{comp}}\subseteq K_2$, and so $F\subseteq K_2$: every element
of the defect subgroup is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in
\emph{every} operator-norm almost representation of $H$.

\emph{Half two: the coordinate Reynolds corner.}  Suppose a corona
representation $\Theta$ is nontrivial on $F$; choose coordinate unitary
lifts $(V_{g,n})$ of $\Theta$ and form the coordinate Reynolds averages
\[
 p_n=\frac1{|F|}\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}.
\]
Because the lifts are asymptotically multiplicative and $F$ is finite,
$(p_n)$ is asymptotically self-adjoint and idempotent, and normality of
$F$ makes it asymptotically central for all of $\Theta(H)$.  Spectral
rounding at $1/2$ of the Hermitian part of $p_n$ produces genuine
coordinate projections; let $q_n$ be their complements.  Nontriviality of
$\Theta$ on $F$ forces $q_n\ne0$ along infinitely many coordinates ---
otherwise the averages converge to $1$ and $\Theta|_F$ is trivial.
Retain exactly those coordinates, relabel them by $\N$, compress every
lift to the corner $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\cong M_{r_n}(\C)$ with
$r_n=\operatorname{rank}q_n>0$, and polar-correct the compressed
matrices.  The result is an operator-norm almost representation
$(W_{g,n})$ of $H$ on the nonzero corner blocks; since the corner is the
complement of the rounded average, the finite average vanishes there:
\[
 \Bigl\lVert\sum_{f\in F}W_{f,n}\Bigr\rVert\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in operator norm.}
 \tag{\ensuremath{\ast}}
\]

\emph{The contradiction.}  Half one applies to the corner almost
representation $(W_{g,n})$ itself: every $f\in F$ satisfies
$\hsnorm{W_{f,n}-1}\to0$.  Since $F$ is finite, a diagonal selection of
coordinates makes this uniform over $F$: passing to stages at which all
$|F|$ distances are below $1/(n+1)$ gives
\[
 \sum_{f\in F}W_{f,n}\longrightarrow |F|1
 \quad\text{in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm},
\]
each block carrying its own normalized trace.  This contradicts
\((\ast)\), because operator-norm convergence dominates normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt convergence.  Hence no corona representation is
nontrivial on $F$.
\end{proof}

The proof of half two used only one property of $N_{\mathrm{comp}}$: every
element is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in every operator-norm almost
representation of $H$.  Any normal subgroup with that tracial-kill
property therefore obstructs its finite normal subgroups in exactly the
same way; the intrinsic-defect theorem below exploits this
factorization.

Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(2), proved in Section~\ref{sec:proofA} from the
sign criterion, is also the instance \(H=E\), \(\Lambda=\mathcal B\),
\(\iota\) the canonical homomorphism, and \(F=\{1,w\}\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}; the membership \(w\in N_{\mathrm{comp}}\)
is the identity \(w=[d,\iota(v_1)]^{\,2}\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:square}.
The transport theorem is quantitative at each finite stage, so it
supplies directly the ordinary sequential convergence needed above.

\subsection{The intrinsic defect}

The transport estimate localizes at a single element $t$, but nothing
in it is specific to $t$.  Recall from \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect} the
subgroup $G_{\rm comp}(L)$ generated by all one-sided conjugators of a
subgroup $L\le H$ and the intrinsic defect
$\mathfrak D(H,L)$.

\begin{corollary}[transport along all one-sided conjugators]\label{cor:generaltransport}
Let $(r_n)$ and $V_{g,n}\in\U(r_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $H$, let $\Lambda$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ be a homomorphism, and put $L=\iota(\Lambda)$.
Suppose that $(x_n\in M_{r_n}(\C))$ is uniformly bounded in operator norm
and belongs to the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of $L$:
\[
 \hsnorm{x_n-V_{\iota(\lambda),n}x_nV_{\iota(\lambda),n}^*}
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
Then every $g\in G_{\rm comp}(L)$ acts in both directions on that
asymptotic commutant.  Explicitly, both
\[
 V_{g,n}x_nV_{g,n}^*
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 V_{g,n}^*x_nV_{g,n}
\]
satisfy the preceding convergence for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$.
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.compressionGroup_transport_both}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a one-sided conjugator $s$, Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} gives
forward transport.  Reverse transport is the same assembly run with the
adjoint unitary: the equal-rank flip in that proof reverses the
one-sided containment of the spectral corners, so conjugating the leakage
estimate by the implementing unitary bounds the leakage for
$\Ad U_n(s)^*$ as well, and the displacement and capture steps are
unchanged.  The elements acting in both
directions form a subgroup of $H$, and hence contain the subgroup generated
by all one-sided conjugators, namely $G_{\rm comp}(L)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MF-invisibility of the intrinsic defect]
\label{thm:compression-radical}
Let $H$ and $\Gamma$ be countable groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism, and put
$L=\iota(\Gamma)$.  If $F\trianglelefteq H$ is finite and
$F\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, then every homomorphism from $H$ to every norm
matrix corona kills $F$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.manuscriptCompressionRadical}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the factorization recorded after Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, it
suffices to prove the tracial half for the larger subgroup: every element
of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in every operator-norm
almost representation of $H$.  So let $(U_{h,n})$ be coordinate lifts of
such a representation and put
$K_2=\{h:\hsnorm{U_{h,n}-1}\to0\}$, a normal subgroup of $H$.
If $z\in C_H(L)$, then $(U_{z,n})$ lies in the asymptotic commutant, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:generaltransport} puts $(U_{gzg^{-1},n})$ there for every
$g\in G_{\rm comp}(L)$; hence each commutator $[gzg^{-1},\ell]$,
$\ell\in L$, belongs to $K_2$.  Thus every displayed generator of
$\mathfrak D(H,L)$ belongs to $K_2$, and normality of $K_2$ absorbs the
normal closure, so $\mathfrak D(H,L)\le K_2$.  The coordinate Reynolds
corner and the finite-sum contradiction of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}
apply verbatim to any finite normal $F\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$.
\end{proof}


\section{The cyclic comparison}\label{sec:necessity}

The elementary finite-dimensional calculation of
Section~\ref{sec:findim} does not by itself determine
whether its marked word is trivial:

\begin{mainthm}[the cyclic comparison]\label{thm:C}
Define the marked presentation
\[
 E_{\mathrm{BS}}=\left\langle\gamma_0,t,c\ \middle|\
 \begin{aligned}
  &t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2,\quad c^2=1,\quad[c,\gamma_0]=1,\\
  &w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1,\quad
    [w_{\mathrm{BS}},\gamma_0]=[w_{\mathrm{BS}},t]
      =[w_{\mathrm{BS}},c]=1
 \end{aligned}\right\rangle,
\]
where
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}=[tct^{-1},\gamma_0(tct^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}]$.
Then $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ is finitely presented and $w_{\mathrm{BS}}\ne1$.
There is an explicit surjective homomorphism from $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ onto its
realized Clifford quotient that maps $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to the nontrivial
central sign.  Nevertheless every finite-dimensional representation of
$E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ over every field maps $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Monsters/LiteralCyclicCalibration}{GroupApproximation.LiteralCyclicCalibration.manuscriptCyclicCalibration}
\end{mainthm}

The quotient statement makes the comparison at the level actually used by
the proof: exact finite-dimensional invisibility does not by itself force
triviality of the marked word.  The MF conclusion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is the separate uniform assertion supplied by the
fixed-space argument.


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
As with $w$, the relator $w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1$ is redundant
--- $tct^{-1}$ and $\gamma_0(tct^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}$ are involutions, so
centrality forces it by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal} --- and is retained only to display the
marked pattern.  Deleting it gives a six-relator presentation of the same
group.
The stable relation gives
\(t\langle\gamma_0\rangle t^{-1}\subseteq
\langle\gamma_0\rangle\), and \(c\) centralizes this cyclic base.
The same commutant argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:B} therefore maps
\(w_{\mathrm{BS}}\) to the identity in every finite-dimensional
representation over every field.

For nontriviality, use the affine realization of
\(\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\) and its coset action.  The root site is fixed by the
cyclic base, while the two sites represented by \(t\) and \(\gamma_0t\)
are distinct.  Attach Clifford generators to these sites and map \(c\) to
the root lamp.  All seven relators hold, and
\[
 w_{\mathrm{BS}}\longmapsto
 [c_{t\langle\gamma_0\rangle},
  c_{\gamma_0t\langle\gamma_0\rangle}]
 =-1.
\]
Thus \(w_{\mathrm{BS}}\ne1\).  Restricting the target to the subgroup
generated by the three displayed images gives the asserted surjective
realized quotient.  This comparison isolates the precise limitation of the
finite-dimensional argument: exact finite-dimensional invisibility does not
itself imply that the marked word is trivial.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Sharpness of the Kazhdan hypothesis.}
The realized Clifford quotient in Theorem~\ref{thm:C} is amenable, hence MF,
and its quotient map detects $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$: some corona
representation of $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ separates the marked word from the
identity, so property~\textup{(T)} cannot be omitted from the
central-sign criterion.  (In the radical language of
Section~\ref{sec:consequences}:
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}\notin\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E_{\mathrm{BS}})$.)
The affine group $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\cong\mathbb Z[1/2]\rtimes\mathbb Z$ is
solvable.  The Clifford lamp group on its coset space is locally finite, so
their semidirect product is amenable.  The realized quotient is a subgroup
of this amenable group and is therefore amenable, hence MF by \cite{TWW}.
Its marked word is the nontrivial Clifford sign by the calculation above.
The amenable-implies-MF step is a literature input, outside the formal
development; the non-LEF statement below is formalized.

This MF detector is necessarily approximate: neither the group
$E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ nor its realized Clifford quotient is LEF, and the latter is not
residually finite.  Indeed, in every finite group the doubling relation makes
the image of $\langle\gamma_0^2\rangle$ equal to the image of
$\langle\gamma_0\rangle$; the lamp therefore commutes with the required
``halved'' element and the marked word collapses.
\leanverified{Monsters/CyclicBaseLEFObstruction}{GroupApproximation.CyclicBaseLEFObstruction.cyclicBase_exactModel_package}

\section{Consequences}\label{sec:consequences}

We record the universal MF radical, the reduced-group-$C^*$ consequence, a
sofic non-MF detector, and failures of quotient and extension permanence.
They all come from the same marked element.

\subsection{The MF radical}

\begin{definition}\label{def:radical}
For a countable group $G_1$, the \emph{MF radical} is
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)
  \;=\;\bigcap\,\bigl\{\ker\Theta:\ \Theta\ \text{a corona
  representation of }G_1\bigr\},
\]
a normal subgroup of $G_1$.  This is the operator-norm analogue of
the invisible subgroups of \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}
and of the metric-ultraproduct approximation framework of \cite{NST}.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMFRadical}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[functoriality]\label{lem:portable}
For every homomorphism $\varphi\colon G_1\to G_2$ of countable
groups,
\[
  \varphi\bigl(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)\bigr)
  \;\subseteq\;\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2):
\]
the MF radical is functorial.  In particular, if
$\varphi\colon E\to G_2$ satisfies $\varphi(w)\neq1$, then $G_2$ is
not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptRadicalPortability}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $x\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$ and $\Theta$ is a
corona representation of $G_2$, then $\Theta\circ\varphi$ is a corona
representation of $G_1$, so $\Theta(\varphi(x))=1$.  For the second
statement: $w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, so
$1\neq\varphi(w)\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2)$, and an
injective corona representation of $G_2$ would kill $\varphi(w)$, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the universal MF quotient]\label{prop:univquot}
Let $G_1$ be a countable group and
$R=\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the kernel of a single corona representation of $G_1$.
\item $G_1/R$ is MF.
\item Every homomorphism from $G_1$ to an MF group factors through
$G_1/R$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $G_1$ is MF if and only if $R=\{1\}$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUniversalMFQuotient}
\end{proposition}

The statement is presumably folklore once the radical is defined; the
proof is included for completeness, with no claim of novelty.

\begin{proof}
(3) If $\varphi\colon G_1\to M$ with $M$ MF and
$j\colon M\to\Ucor((d_n))$ is an injective corona representation,
then $j\circ\varphi$ is a corona representation of $G_1$, so
$j(\varphi(R))=1$ and, by injectivity of $j$, $\varphi(R)=1$.

(1) and (2).  Work on $Q=G_1/R$ from the outset.  If $1\ne q\in Q$ and
$g\in G_1$ represents $q$, then $g\notin R$.  Hence some corona
representation of $G_1$ detects $g$.  Every corona representation kills
$R$ by definition, so this representation descends to one of $Q$ that
detects $q$.  Thus the intersection of the kernels of all corona
representations of the countable group $Q$ is trivial.

Enumerate the nonidentity elements of $Q$ and choose a detecting corona
representation for each; pass to coordinate unitary lifts, so that each
detector becomes a sequential almost representation with its marked
element separated from $1$.  Diagonalize at the coordinate level: at
stage $k$, select in each of the first $k$ detectors a coordinate at
which multiplicativity holds within $1/k$ on the first $k$ group elements
while the marked separation persists, and take the block sum of the $k$
selected matrices.  Operator norms of block sums are maxima, so the
resulting single sequence is an almost representation detecting every
nonidentity element; its class in the unitary-sequence quotient, composed
with the polar-correction isomorphism of Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona},
is a faithful corona representation of $Q$.  This
proves (2).  Composing it with $G_1\twoheadrightarrow Q$ gives a single
corona representation of $G_1$ with kernel exactly $R$, proving (1).
For the final equivalence:
if $G_1$ is MF, an injective corona representation forces
$R=\{1\}$; if $R=\{1\}$, then (2) says $G_1=G_1/R$ is MF.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[exact radical from a candidate quotient]\label{cor:exactradical}
Let $N\trianglelefteq G_1$ satisfy
$N\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.  Every corona
representation of $G_1$ factors uniquely through $G_1/N$.  If in
addition $G_1/N$ is MF, then
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)=N.
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptExactRadicalFromCandidateQuotient}
\end{corollary}

Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}, this says that proving a
concrete defect subgroup invisible and its quotient MF computes both the
entire MF radical and the universal MF quotient.

\begin{proof}
The containment $N\le\ker\Theta$ gives the unique quotient
factorization for every corona representation $\Theta$.  For the
reverse containment in the displayed equality, compose the quotient
map $G_1\to G_1/N$ with an injective corona representation of $G_1/N$:
its kernel is exactly $N$, while the MF radical lies in the kernel of
every corona representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nofaithful}
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra admitting an injective, possibly
nonunital, $*$-homomorphism into a norm matrix corona.  Then the group $E$
admits no injective homomorphism into $\U(A)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFConsequences.literal_no_faithful_corona_subalgebra_target}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $j\colon A\to Q$ be the possibly nonunital inclusion into the ambient
corona and let $\phi\colon E\to\U(A)$.  Complement correction gives
\[
   \widehat j(u)=j(u)+1-j(1_A)\in\U(Q).
\]
This is an injective homomorphism on \(\U(A)\), so
\(\widehat j\circ\phi\) is a corona representation of \(E\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(2) sends \(w\) to the identity there; injectivity of
\(\widehat j\) gives \(\phi(w)=1\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} has
\(w\neq1\), so \(\phi\) cannot be injective.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite universal Horn obstruction}

Theorem~\ref{thm:A} can be read as one first-order fact about all MF
groups at once: a finite quasi-identity, that is, a single universal
implication between finitely many equations (a universal Horn
sentence).  Write $R$ for the forty-one relators of \eqref{eq:E},
regarded as words in eight variables, and $w$ for the marked word in those
variables.

\begin{proposition}[finite quasi-identity]\label{prop:horn}
Every MF group $G$ satisfies the finite universal Horn sentence
\[
 \forall x_1,\dots,x_8\colon\quad
 \bigwedge_{r\in R}\,r(x_1,\dots,x_8)=1
 \;\Longrightarrow\; w(x_1,\dots,x_8)=1 ,
\]
while the canonical generating tuple of $E$ satisfies every relator in $R$
and has $w\ne1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralUniversalHorn}{GroupApproximation.LiteralUniversalHorn.manuscriptLiteralUniversalHorn}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A tuple satisfying the relators induces a homomorphism $E\to G$ sending
the marked word of $E$ to $w(x_1,\dots,x_8)$.  If $G$ is MF, then
Lemma~\ref{lem:portable} and Theorem~\ref{thm:A} make that image trivial.
The canonical tuple of $E$ satisfies $R$ by definition, and its marked
word is nontrivial by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[a basic clopen set of non-MF marked groups]\label{cor:cylinder}
In the space of eight-generator marked groups --- quotients of the free
group $F_8$, topologized so that a basic clopen set fixes the truth of
finitely many equations among words in the generators --- the marked groups
satisfying all relators of $R$ and $w\ne1$ form a nonempty clopen set
consisting entirely of non-MF groups.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralMarkedCylinder}{GroupApproximation.LiteralMarkedCylinder.literal_nonempty_clopen_nonMF_cylinder}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Both conditions constrain finitely many values of the marked
multiplication, so the set is clopen; it contains the marked group $E$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(1), and Proposition~\ref{prop:horn} excludes every MF
point.
\end{proof}

Thus the obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is finite, local, and
locally constant on a basic clopen neighborhood in the marked-group
topology.

\subsection{Permanence and its failure}

\begin{lemma}[subgroups]\label{lem:subgroupMF}
Every subgroup of an MF group is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.IsOperatorMF.subgroup}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Restrict an injective corona representation to the subgroup: injectivity
and the homomorphism law are preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[residually finite groups]\label{lem:rfmf}
Every countable residually finite group is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_of_residuallyFinite}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Residual finiteness makes the group LEF: for each finite test set $F$
there are a finite quotient injective on $F$ and, composing with its left
regular permutation action, an \emph{exact} finite local model --- every
product of elements of $F$ is represented with zero multiplicative
defect.  Distinct elements of $F$ map to distinct permutations; at a
point where the two permutations disagree, the difference of the two
permutation matrices has a column of norm at least $1$, so the model
separates distinct elements of $F$ by operator norm at least $1$.  These
exact permutation models are operator-norm local models with separation
constant $1$, so the group is MF by the local-to-corona direction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[locally finite groups]\label{lem:lfmf}
Every countable locally finite group is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/LocallyFiniteMF}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_of_locallyFinite}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof is local: no exhaustion, direct limit, or diagonal argument is
needed.  Given a finite test set $F$, local finiteness supplies one
finite subgroup $K$ containing it.  The left regular permutation action
of $K$, applied to a retraction of the group onto $K$ that is only
required to restrict to the identity map on the test set, is an exact
finite local model on $F$, separating distinct elements by operator norm
at least $1$ exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:rfmf}.  Thus the group is LEF,
and the same local-to-corona direction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences} makes it MF.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[quotients]\label{cor:quotclosure}
MF groups are not closed under quotients: the free group
\(F_8\) is MF, and the explicit group \(E\) is its quotient
but is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralMFQuotientControls}{GroupApproximation.LiteralMFQuotientControls.manuscriptQuotientNonclosure}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Map the eight free generators onto the eight displayed generators of \(E\).
The resulting homomorphism is surjective because those generators define the
displayed presentation.  The group \(F_8\) is residually finite, hence MF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rfmf}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} shows that \(E\) is not MF.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The reduced group algebra of \texorpdfstring{$E$}{E}}

Every MF algebra is stably finite
\cite{BK}, so Theorem~\ref{thm:D} exhibits a separable stably finite
$C^*$-algebra that is not MF.  Abstract examples follow from the failure
of the Connes embedding problem \cite{FGH}; the point here is
concreteness: the algebra is the reduced group $C^*$-algebra of an
explicit finitely presented group.  Even for
$\Cred(\SL_3(\mathbb Z))$ and $\Cred(\SL_4(\mathbb Z))$ the MF property is
undecided; only the stronger PMF property is known to fail, for
$\SL_4(\mathbb Z)$ \cite{MdlS}.  Our example has the following properties:

\begin{mainthm}[the reduced group algebra of $E$]\label{thm:D}
$\Cred(E)$ is a unital separable $C^*$-algebra which carries a
faithful tracial state --- and is therefore stably finite --- but is
not an MF algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremD}
\end{mainthm}

The canonical homomorphism $\mathcal B\to E$ is injective: composing it
with the homomorphism $E\to W$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} gives
$g\mapsto j(\bar g)$, and $\mathcal B\to\bar\Gamma$ is injective by
Proposition~\ref{prop:literal-base-T} together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}.  Since the infinite Kazhdan group $\mathcal B$ is
nonamenable, so is $E$.  Hence $\Cred(E)$ is not nuclear
\cite{Lance}.  The nuclear form of the
Blackadar--Kirchberg problem --- whether every stably finite separable
nuclear $C^*$-algebra is quasidiagonal --- is therefore untouched by
Theorem~\ref{thm:D}.  The two classical facts used in this paragraph ---
nonamenability of infinite Kazhdan groups and nonnuclearity of the
reduced algebra of a nonamenable group --- are literature results quoted
for orientation only; they are outside the formal development, and no
theorem below depends on them.


\begin{lemma}\label{lem:faithfultrace}
Let $G_1$ be a countable discrete group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The canonical tracial state $\tau$ on $\Cred(G_1)$ is
faithful.
\item Every unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ with a faithful tracial state
is stably finite: for every nonempty finite index set $I$, every isometry
in $M_I(A)$ is a unitary.  In particular this holds in $M_k(A)$ for every
$k\ge1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptFaithfulTraceAndStableFiniteness}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Realize $\Cred(G_1)\subseteq B(\ell^2G_1)$ as the norm closure
of the span of the left translation unitaries $\lambda_g$, and let
$\rho_g$ be the right translation unitaries,
$(\rho_g\xi)(h)=\xi(hg)$; each $\rho_g$ commutes with each
$\lambda_{g'}$, hence with every element of $\Cred(G_1)$.  The
canonical trace is $\tau(x)=\langle x\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle$.  If
$\tau(x^*x)=0$ then $\lVert x\delta_e\rVert=0$, so $x\delta_e=0$,
and then
$x\delta_g=x\rho_{g^{-1}}\delta_e=\rho_{g^{-1}}x\delta_e=0$ for
every $g\in G_1$.  The vectors $\delta_g$ are total in
$\ell^2G_1$, so $x=0$.

(2) On $M_k(A)$ the functional
$\tau_k\bigl([x_{ij}]\bigr)=\tfrac1k\sum_i\tau(x_{ii})$ is a state
with the trace property
$\tau_k(xy)=\tfrac1k\sum_{i,j}\tau(x_{ij}y_{ji})=\tau_k(yx)$, and it
is faithful: the $i$-th diagonal entry of $x^*x$ is
$\sum_jx_{ji}^*x_{ji}$, so $\tau_k(x^*x)=0$ forces
$\tau(x_{ji}^*x_{ji})=0$, hence $x_{ji}=0$, for all $i,j$.  If
$v\in M_k(A)$ satisfies $v^*v=1$, then $y:=1-vv^*$ is a projection
with
$\tau_k(y)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(vv^*)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(v^*v)=0$; since
$y=y^*y$, faithfulness gives $y=0$, i.e.\ $vv^*=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:D}]
$\Cred(E)$ is unital and separable since $E$ is countable; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace} it carries a faithful tracial state
and is stably finite.  It is not an MF algebra by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A sofic non-MF group}

The witness that proves $w\ne1$ carries the final separation:

\begin{mainthm}[sofic and hyperlinear groups that are not MF]\label{thm:E}
The Clifford witness $W=\ClLamp(X)\rtimes V$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} is a finitely generated sofic group that
is not MF; since sofic groups are hyperlinear, $W$ is likewise a
hyperlinear group that is not MF.  In particular, neither soficity nor
hyperlinearity implies the MF property.
\leanverified{Endpoint/NonMFImpact}{GroupApproximation.NonMFImpact.witness_sofic_hyperlinear_nonMF}
\end{mainthm}


The Clifford witness has substantially more structure than was needed to
prove $w\ne1$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}]
Write $T=\varinjlim(\bar\Gamma,\bar\alpha)$, so
$V=T\rtimes\mathbb Z$, and let $B\le V$ be the level-zero copy of
$\bar\Gamma$.  The image of $\bar\alpha$ has finite index (at most eight,
by the parity classes of the translation lattice).  Thus every telescope
level $\bar\Gamma_n\le T$ is commensurable with $B$.  The stable letter
conjugates $B$ onto $\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$, so all of $V$ commensurates
$B$.  Orbit--stabilizer now shows that every $\bar\Gamma_n$-orbit in
$X=V/B$ is finite.

Consider a finite subset of $\ClLamp(X)\rtimes T$.  Its $T$-coordinates
lie in one level $\bar\Gamma_n$.  Each of its lamp coordinates has finite
$\bar\Gamma_n$-orbit: this holds for the central sign and for each site
lamp, hence for every Clifford word.  The finitely many resulting orbits
generate a finite $\bar\Gamma_n$-invariant subgroup $L\le\ClLamp(X)$,
because $\ClLamp(X)$ is locally finite.  Our original finite set therefore
lies in $L\rtimes\bar\Gamma_n$, which is sofic (finite-by-sofic).  Soficity
is local over finite subsets, so $\ClLamp(X)\rtimes T$ is sofic.

Reassociating the semidirect products gives
\[
 W=\ClLamp(X)\rtimes(T\rtimes\mathbb Z)
   \cong (\ClLamp(X)\rtimes T)\rtimes\mathbb Z.
\]
The final group is sofic because a split extension of a sofic group by
$\Z$ is sofic: truncate the integer levels to $\Z/N$, let the shift act
cyclically, and induce the kernel's sofic approximations over the level
set; the cocycle identity that makes the finite-quotient case exact now
fails only on the wrap-around boundary, a fraction of at most $2|k|/N$
of the levels for a window shifted by $k$, which vanishes as $N$ grows.
This is the special case of the Elek--Szab\'o amenable-extension theorem
\cite{ElekSzabo} that the argument needs, proved directly.  Here
$\bar\Gamma$ is sofic because its six generators are casts of integral
matrices: the integral subgroup is residually finite through its
congruence quotients, and casting is an isomorphism onto $\bar\Gamma$.

The map $E\to W$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} is surjective: its image
contains $V$, the root lamp $c_o$, and hence every lamp by transitivity of
the $V$-action on $X$.  Since $E$ is generated by
  $v_1,x,y,z,t,c$ (by the Tietze calculation above), their six
images generate $W$.  The map sends
$w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ to the
nontrivial sign $\zeta$; portability (Lemma~\ref{lem:portable}) makes
$\zeta\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(W)$.  Hence $W$ is not MF.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Failure of extension permanence.}
MF groups are not closed under extensions.  More precisely, $W$ fits into
an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow\ClLamp(X)\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow V
 \longrightarrow1
\]
whose kernel and quotient are MF, while $W$ is not.
The concrete lamp kernel is locally finite, LEF, sofic, and MF, while the
total witness is finitely generated and non-MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralWitnessConsequences}{GroupApproximation.LiteralWitnessConsequences.literalWitness_locallyFiniteKernel_nonMF}

\begin{proof}
The Clifford lamp group is locally finite, hence MF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:lfmf}.  The group $V$ identifies with the subgroup of
$\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ generated by $\bar\Gamma$ and $D$: the telescope
levels are $D^{-n}\bar\Gamma D^{n}$, and the determinant records the
$\mathbb Z$-coordinate.  It is residually finite: at every odd modulus
the determinant $8$ of the dilation is invertible, so the congruence
reduction of the integral affine model descends to conjugation by the
dilation and extends over the telescope and its shift, separating any
nontrivial element --- the conclusion Mal'cev's theorem \cite{Malcev}
gives for every finitely generated linear group, obtained here by the
direct congruence argument.  Thus $V$ is MF by Lemma~\ref{lem:rfmf},
whereas $W$ is not MF by the preceding construction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{An exact stably finite non-MF algebra.}
The same witness gives a concrete separable exact example:
$\Cred(W)$ is exact, has a faithful tracial state, is stably finite, and is
not MF.  Indeed, the locally finite lamp kernel is amenable and hence exact,
while the linear group $V\leq\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ is exact
\cite{GHW}.  Exact groups are closed under extensions
\cite{KWPermanence}, so $W$ is exact; for discrete groups this is equivalent
to exactness of the reduced group algebra \cite{KWExact}.  The canonical trace
and stable finiteness follow as in Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace}.  Finally, an
MF embedding of $\Cred(W)$ would restrict to a faithful norm-corona model of
the canonical group unitaries, contradicting that $W$ is not MF.  All parts
of this paragraph except exactness are also proved by a closed Lean endpoint;
the present formal library has no definition of exact $C^*$-algebras.

\paragraph{A simple sofic envelope.}
There is a countable simple sofic group $S_{\mathrm{simp}}$ with
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(S_{\mathrm{simp}})=S_{\mathrm{simp}}.
\]
Equivalently, every homomorphism from $S_{\mathrm{simp}}$ to an MF group is
trivial.

\begin{proof}
By Elek--Szab\'o, every countable sofic group embeds in a countable simple
sofic group \cite[Theorem~1]{ElekSzaboHyper}.  Embed $W$ in such a group.  Portability
sends its nontrivial radical element $\zeta$ to a nontrivial element of the
MF radical of the simple group.  That radical is normal, so it is the whole
group.  Every homomorphism to an MF group kills the MF radical.  (The
embedding theorem is a literature input; this paragraph is outside the
formal development.)
\end{proof}


\section{The operator-norm boundary}\label{sec:limits}

The proof is genuinely operator-norm in its present form.  If multiplication
is controlled only in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, the induced adjoint
operators on the Hilbert--Schmidt spaces need not be close in operator norm.
The spectral-corner, displacement, and leakage estimates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} require exactly that operator-norm
control, so normalized Hilbert--Schmidt control does not supply the
spectral-subspace transport used there.

This identifies a boundary of the method, not a tracial nonapproximation
theorem for $E$.  Alekseev--Thom ask whether commutants arising in sofic
approximations of Kazhdan groups can be recovered from finite-dimensional
coordinate subalgebras \cite[Open Problem~6.2]{AlekseevThom}.  Such a
coordinate description is separate from the operator-norm theorem proved
here.

\subsection*{Questions}
The finite-normal mechanism has a precise torsion limitation: every finite
subgroup of a torsion-free group is trivial.
\leanverified{Sofic/TorsionFreeFiniteNormalLimit}{GroupApproximation.finiteSubgroup_eq_bot_of_isMulTorsionFree}
Consequently a torsion-free example would require a different final
obstruction, even if its transport step were similar.  Fournier-Facio
removed the analogous torsion obstacle for nonsoficity by a small-cancellation
construction \cite{FFF}.  That strategy does not transfer directly here:
the final operator-norm contradiction uses a nontrivial finite normal
subgroup, which a torsion-free group cannot contain.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is $E/\langle w\rangle$ MF?  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:exactradical}, a positive answer would compute
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)=\{1,w\}$ exactly.  There is a concrete
intermediate test.  Theorem~\ref{thm:B} kills
$u=[tct^{-1},v_1]$ in every finite-dimensional representation, whereas
the image of $u$ survives after quotienting the Clifford witness $W$ by its
central sign $\zeta$: it becomes the product of the two distinct lamp basis
elements at $\tau o$ and $j(\bar v_1)\tau o$.  Thus proving
$W/\langle\zeta\rangle$ MF would already
show $u\notin\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$; any such detecting models
must be genuinely non-finite-quotient in flavor.
\item Is there a torsion-free finitely presented non-MF group?  The
mechanism here is torsion-critical: the obstruction lives in a finite
normal subgroup.
\item Is $E$ sofic, or hyperlinear?  The adjoint spectral-corner
estimates require operator-norm multiplicative control, and neither
property is decided by the results above.
\item Does MF imply hyperlinearity?  A faithful embedding into a norm
matrix corona need not make any coordinate trace faithful, so the defining
MF representation does not immediately supply a hyperlinear model.  This
question concerns the CDE norm-corona convention fixed above; for stronger
trace-controlled MF conventions the implication is built into the definition.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proof of the MF equivalences}\label{app:equivalences}

An \emph{operator-norm local model} for a countable group $G$, a finite
set $F\subseteq G$, a separation constant $\delta>0$, and a tolerance
$\varepsilon>0$ is a map $V\colon E\to\U(r)$, for some positive
dimension $r$, such that
\begin{gather*}
 \opnorm{V(g)V(h)-V(gh)}\le\varepsilon\quad(g,h\in F),\\
 \opnorm{V(g)-V(h)}\ge\delta\quad(g\ne h\in F).
\end{gather*}
The local formulation in Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences} asks for
such a model with $\delta=1$ for every finite $F$ and every
$\varepsilon>0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} identifies the unitary-sequence quotient with
the unitary group of the $C^*$-quotient.  Replacing an arbitrary
finite coordinate set by a standard set of the same cardinality merely
conjugates that coordinate by a permutation unitary.  This proves the first
equivalence once the dimension convention is normalized.

Suppose first that
$\rho\colon G\to\Ucor((d_n))$ is injective, and choose unitary lifts
$u_n(g)$ of $\rho(g)$.  Fix a finite set $F\subseteq G$ and $\varepsilon>0$.
For each ordered pair $g\ne h$ in $F$, injectivity says that
$(u_n(g)^{-1}u_n(h))_n$ does not converge to the identity in operator norm.
Hence there are $\delta_{g,h}>0$ and arbitrarily large coordinates at which
\[
 \lVert u_n(g)-u_n(h)\rVert\ge\delta_{g,h}.
\]
Choose an integer $N_{g,h}$ with $8<N_{g,h}\,\delta_{g,h}^2$.  An explicit
tensor-power estimate then applies: whenever two unitaries are
$\delta_{g,h}$-separated, some tensor power of exponent $p\le N_{g,h}$
separates them by more than $1$ --- amplifying a spectral gap of the
relative unitary until it crosses the unit circle takes at most
$N_{g,h}$ steps once $8<N_{g,h}\delta_{g,h}^2$.
The quotient homomorphism law makes all multiplicative defects for pairs in
$F$ arbitrarily small at every sufficiently large coordinate.  Choose, for
each $g\ne h$, a coordinate with the displayed separation and with every
required defect at most $\varepsilon/N_{g,h}$; then the $p$th tensor power
separates the designated pair
by more than $1$, while the tensor-product telescoping identity bounds each
new defect by $p\,\varepsilon/N_{g,h}\le\varepsilon$.  Finally take the block
sum of these finitely many pair-specific models.  Operator norms of block
diagonal matrices are maxima, so every pair in $F$ is separated by at least
$1$ and every required product has defect at most $\varepsilon$.  This gives
the normalized local model.

Conversely, local models with separation constant $1$ for all finite sets
and tolerances make the group \emph{weakly} MF: choosing models along an
exhaustion with errors tending to zero, each fixed pair $g,h$ is
eventually represented multiplicatively and each fixed pair $g\ne h$
eventually separated by $1$, so the classes define an injective
homomorphism into the unitary-sequence quotient with no condition on the
dimension sequence.  Weak MF in turn makes the norm-MF radical of
Definition~\ref{def:radical} trivial, and the block-sum diagonalization
in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}(2) then assembles the
detecting sequences into a single faithful corona representation.  This
is the dependency route: the weak-to-corona upgrade is the
universal-quotient construction, not a separate exhaustion.

It remains only to normalize dimensions.  Given positive dimensions
$d_n$, replace the $n$th coordinate model by the cumulative block sum on
$X_0\oplus\cdots\oplus X_n$, where $X_k$ is the $k$th original model
space: embed the $n$th original model in the newest block and put
identity blocks in all earlier positions.  The dimensions
$D_n=d_0+\cdots+d_n$ increase strictly.  Products, multiplicative
defects, and pairwise operator-norm distances are unchanged, because
block-diagonal operator norms are maxima and every earlier block is the
same identity for all group elements; in particular no approximation
error from earlier coordinates is carried forward.  Thus the construction
preserves and reflects the quotient relation and injectivity.
Forgetting strict increase proves the converse.
\end{proof}

\section{The maximal group \texorpdfstring{$C^*$}{C*}-algebra}\label{app:maxcstar}

For an ordinary reader, $\Cmax(H)$ is the usual maximal group
$C^*$-algebra and this appendix says nothing surprising.  The formal
development, working in type-theoretic foundations, constructs it
relative to a set-theoretic universe, and the printed statement records
that form exactly.  Fix a set-theoretic universe $\mathfrak U$.  For a
$\mathfrak U$-small
group $H$, define $C^{*}_{\max,\mathfrak U}(H)$ to be the closed
$*$-subalgebra generated by the diagonal copy of $H$ in the bounded product
of all its nontrivial unitary representations on $\mathfrak U$-small unital
$C^*$-algebras.  This algebra lives in the next universe.  We suppress the
subscript $\mathfrak U$ and write $u_h$ for the canonical unitary
associated with $h\in H$.

\begin{proposition}[universe-relative maximal group algebra]
\label{prop:maximal-cstar}
The canonical map $h\mapsto u_h$ is injective.  For every
$\mathfrak U$-small unital $C^*$-algebra $B$ and every homomorphism
$\rho\colon H\to\U(B)$, there is a unique unital $*$-homomorphism
$C^{*}_{\max,\mathfrak U}(H)\to B$ mapping $u_h$ to $\rho(h)$ for
every $h\in H$.
\leanverified{Analysis/MaximalGroupCStar}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptUniverseRelativeMaximalGroupCStar}
\end{proposition}

For a nontrivial target, projection to its coordinate gives the extension;
for a trivial target, the map is unique.  Density of the generated
$*$-subalgebra gives uniqueness, while the left-regular coordinate proves
injectivity.
