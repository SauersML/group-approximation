\title{An explicit finitely presented non-MF group}
\author{\paperauthor}

\begin{abstract}
A countable discrete group is MF in the Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt--%
Shulman sense if it embeds into the unitary group of a norm matrix corona
$\prod_n M_{d_n}(\C)/\bigoplus_n M_{d_n}(\C)$.  As of August 2026, whether
every group is MF remained open.  The precise group notion dates to
2012--2013 and has an operator-algebraic lineage going back to the
Blackadar--Kirchberg MF program of 1997.  It is one of a family of metric
approximation problems whose
normalized-Hamming (sofic) case was solved recently by OpenAI and
subsequently generalized by Kun and Thom, and whose unnormalized
Frobenius case was solved by De Chiffre, Glebsky, Lubotzky and Thom.  We
construct an explicit finitely
presented group $E$, together with a designated central involution
$w\in E$, such that $w\neq 1$ but \emph{every} homomorphism from $E$ to
the unitary group of every norm matrix corona of the displayed form kills
$w$.  Consequently $E$ is not MF, and neither its full nor its reduced
group $C^*$-algebra is an MF algebra.  The group is built from
$\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$, one HNN letter implementing the proper
endomorphism $(v,A)\mapsto(2v,A)$, and one additional involution; the
proof combines the Kazhdan projection of $\Gamma$ with stable finiteness
of matrix coronas.  The finite-dimensional case of the kill theorem is
elementary and uses neither property (T) nor unitarity; we also show,
via quasidiagonality of amenable groups, that the same marked presentation
with a cyclic base can have a corona representation in which the marked
involution survives.  The kill theorem is proved as a general
finite-normal criterion: if a countable group contains the image of a
property (T) group, compressed by a letter $t$ and centralized by a
letter $c$, then every finite normal subgroup contained in the
compression-defect subgroup --- the normal closure of the commutators
of $tct^{-1}$ with that image --- is killed by every homomorphism into
the unitary group of every $C^*$-subalgebra of a norm matrix corona.
More generally, finiteness can be replaced by property~(T): every normal
property~(T) subgroup contained in the compression defect lies in the MF
radical, and may be infinite, noncentral, and torsion-free.
Among the consequences: $\Cred(E)$ is a unital separable $C^*$-algebra
with a faithful tracial state --- hence stably finite --- that is not
an MF algebra, giving an explicit reduced-group-$C^*$-algebra realization
of the already known failure of stable finiteness to imply the MF property;
MF groups are closed under neither quotients
nor split extensions; every countable group has a largest MF quotient,
by its MF radical, and the radical element $w$ is portable: any group
receiving $E$ with $w$ surviving is non-MF; for every $m\ge2$ a scaled
variant $E_m$ ($E=E_2$) is a finitely presented non-MF group, and
there exist $2^{\aleph_0}$ pairwise non-isomorphic finitely generated
non-MF groups; the MF locus is closed in every fixed-rank marked-group
space, equivalently non-MF-ness is open; and the kill theorem holds in a
uniform quantitative form over all matrix sizes at once.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $(d_n)_{n\in\N}$ be a sequence of positive integers and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corona}
  \mathcal Q\;=\;\prod_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)\Big/\bigoplus_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)
\end{equation}
be the associated \emph{norm matrix corona}: bounded sequences of
matrices modulo sequences converging to $0$ in operator norm.  Following
Blackadar--Kirchberg \cite{BK}, a separable $C^*$-algebra is \emph{MF}
if it embeds into some algebra of the form \eqref{eq:corona}.  We use
\emph{MF group} exclusively in the sense introduced by
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt and used by Shulman \cite{CDE,Shulman}: a
countable discrete group $E$ is MF if there is an \emph{injective} group
homomorphism
\[
  E\;\longrightarrow\;\U(\mathcal Q)
\]
for some choice of $(d_n)$.  Equivalently, $E$ admits finite-dimensional
approximate unitary representations that are approximately injective,
with all defects measured in operator norm.
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt take the dimensions to be increasing.  Allowing
an arbitrary positive sequence, as we do, is equivalent: replace its $n$th
term by the cumulative block sum through stage $n$ and place the old unitary
in the newest block.  The operator norm of every difference is unchanged.
The formal companion proves this normalization at the level of the
unitary-sequence corona.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFIncreasingDimensions}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMFIncreasing_iff}
This qualification matters:
Shulman notes that a strictly stronger group property has also been called
MF \cite[Definition~3 and footnote~1]{Shulman}: \emph{purely} MF requires
the approximating maps to be genuine finite-dimensional representations.
The two properties must not be conflated when comparing with the
literature: Magee--de la Salle proved that $\SL_4(\Z)$ is not purely MF
\cite{MdlS}, while $\SL_4(\Z)$ is residually finite and hence MF in the
sense used here.  Residually finite groups
are MF, amenable groups are MF as a consequence of the
Tikuisis--White--Winter theorem \cite{TWW}, free products of amenable
groups are MF, and a substantial permanence theory exists
\cite{Shulman}.

The age of the problem has two layers.  Blackadar and Kirchberg introduced
MF $C^*$-algebras in 1997 \cite{BK}; the broader stably-finite-to-MF
embedding question attributed to Kirchberg implies a positive answer to
the corresponding operator-norm approximation problem for groups
\cite[Introduction]{DGLT}.  The precise group property used here was
introduced as Definition~2.7 by Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt in a preprint
first posted on October~15, 2012 and published in 2013 \cite{CDE}.  Their
paper introduces the definition but does not formulate the universal group
question in those words, so we distinguish the 1997 problem lineage from
the 2012--2013 definition.  At the date of the present result, these were
respectively about twenty-nine years and nearly fourteen years old.  As
late as May 2026, Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky described whether every group is
MF as a longstanding open problem \cite[pp.~235--238]{BDL}.  It is the
operator-norm member of the family of
approximation problems surveyed in \cite{DGLT,BDL}, whose
unnormalized-Frobenius case
was solved negatively in \cite{DGLT}.  Building on rigidity theorems for
sofic approximations of Kazhdan groups due to Kun \cite{Kun16} and
Kun--Thom \cite{KT19}, OpenAI constructed the first nonsofic group
\cite{OAI}; Kun and Thom then extracted and generalized the mechanism,
obtaining nonsofic generalized wreath products \cite{KT}, and
Fournier-Facio exhibited finitely presented torsion-free examples
\cite{FF}.  In the sofic problem the metric is normalized Hamming distance
on permutation groups, not rank distance; the three metric conventions are
stated together in \cite[Introduction]{Shulman}.  Neither result supplies an
operator-norm conclusion: \cite{DGLT} treats the unnormalized Frobenius norm,
while permutation/Hamming-metric arguments do not directly control
operator-norm defects.

Against this August 2026 literature record, the theorem below answers the
question negatively with an explicit finitely presented example.

\begin{mainthm}[non-MF group]\label{thm:A}
Let $\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$, let
$\alpha\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma$ be the injective endomorphism
$\alpha(v,A)=(2v,A)$, and let $a=(e_1,1)\in\Gamma$, which does not lie
in $\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Let $E$ be the finitely presented group obtained
from $\Gamma$ by adjoining a stable letter $t$ with
$t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma$, an involution $c$
commuting with $\Gamma$, and by imposing that
\[
  w\;:=\;\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a\,(tct^{-1})\,a^{-1}\bigr]
\]
is central and satisfies $w^2=1$ (Definition~\ref{def:E}).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $w\neq 1$ in $E$, so $w$ is a central involution;
  \item for every sequence $(d_n)$ and every group homomorphism
    $\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ into the corresponding norm matrix
    corona \eqref{eq:corona}, one has $\Theta(w)=1$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $E$ is not MF, and neither $\Cmax(E)$ nor $\Cred(E)$ is an
MF $C^*$-algebra.
\end{mainthm}

A completely explicit presentation of $E$ --- eight generators, with
every relation displayed --- appears as \eqref{eq:E} in
Definition~\ref{def:E}.

The proof turns a $C^*$-algebraic finiteness argument into a group-level
kernel theorem.  For orientation, it can be organized into the following
five steps.  The individual ingredients and the surrounding
compression/lamp architecture have substantial antecedents.  What is new
here is the specific stable-finite Kazhdan pinning argument in a norm matrix
corona and its conversion into a universal marked kernel.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item\emph{Central corner.}  If $\Theta(w)\neq 1$, then
    $e=\tfrac12(1-\Theta(w))$ is a nonzero projection commuting with the
    image of $\Theta$; cutting by a lift of $e$ replaces $\mathcal Q$ by
    another norm matrix corona on which $\Theta(w)=-1$
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:corner}).  This cut eliminates small-relative-rank
    phenomena: on the corner, the deviation of $w$
    from $1$ has full normalized size.  This is a norm-corona adaptation
    of the negative-eigenspace compression for a central involution in
    the proof of \cite[Proposition~2.7]{SlofstraVidick}; the distinction
    is that no positive relative-rank estimate is needed here.
  \item\emph{Conjugation representation.}  Unitary lifts $U_{g,n}$ of
    the corner representation conjugate the corner coordinates
    $M_{r_n}(\C)$, and exact multiplicativity of $\Theta$ in the quotient
    makes arbitrary coordinate lifts asymptotically multiplicative in
    operator norm.  Thus
    $g\mapsto (\Ad U_{g,n})_n$ defines an \emph{exact} homomorphism $\beta$ into
    the norm corona of the algebras $B(L^2(M_{r_n}))$
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:beta}).
  \item\emph{Kazhdan projection.}  $\Gamma$ has property (T), so its
    maximal group $C^*$-algebra contains the Kazhdan projection
    $p_\Gamma$, whose image under every representation is the projection
    onto the invariant vectors.  The HNN relation
    $t\Gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\Gamma)\subseteq\Gamma$ forces, in
    \emph{every} $C^*$-representation, the order relation
    $P\le\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$ between the images of $p_\Gamma$ and its
    $t$-conjugate (Lemma~\ref{lem:order}).
  \item\emph{Stable finiteness.}  A norm matrix corona is stably finite:
    isometries in every matrix amplification are unitaries
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}).  Hence
    the two unitarily equivalent projections in step (3) must be equal:
    $\beta(t)$ \emph{preserves} the $\Gamma$-fixed space.
  \item\emph{The marked word.}  The involution $c$ commutes with $\Gamma$, so
    the matrix vector representing $\Theta(c)$ is $\Gamma$-fixed; by
    step (4) so is the vector representing $\Theta(tct^{-1})$; hence
    $a\in\Gamma$ asymptotically fixes it, and the commutator word $w$
    is forced to $1$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm along the chosen
    ultrafilter --- while the
    corner normalization of step (1) says it equals $-1$ in operator
    norm.  The two conclusions contradict each other
    (Section~\ref{sec:proofA}).
\end{enumerate}

The upper lane is purely group-theoretic; the lower lane is the
finite-matrix rigidity input.  Their common output is incompatible with the
Reynolds corner.

Nontriviality of $w$ is witnessed by a completely explicit
representation of $E$ into a semidirect product of a Clifford group by a
linear group over $\Z[1/2]$ (Section~\ref{sec:witness}); no
combinatorial theory of HNN extensions is needed.

Two companion results clarify the mechanism.  First, in
finite dimensions the kill theorem is elementary and needs neither
property (T), nor unitarity, nor centrality of $w$:

For a subgroup $L\le H$, put
\[
 \mathcal C(L)=\{s\in H:sLs^{-1}\subseteq L\},\qquad
 G_{\rm comp}(L)=\langle\mathcal C(L)\rangle,
\]
and define the intrinsic \emph{compression--centralizer defect}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsicdefect}
 \mathfrak D(H,L)=
 \left\langle\!\left\langle
 [gzg^{-1},\ell]:g\in G_{\rm comp}(L),\ z\in C_H(L),\ \ell\in L
 \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_H .
\end{equation}

\begin{mainthm}[finite-dimensional obstruction]\label{thm:B}
Let $k$ be a field, $V$ a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, $H$ a
group, $\Gamma\le H$ a subgroup, and $t,c\in H$, $a\in\Gamma$ elements
with $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ and $c\gamma=\gamma c$ for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$.  Then every homomorphism $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$
satisfies
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)\;=\;1 .
\]
More generally, every such $\pi$ kills the entire subgroup
$\mathfrak D(H,\Gamma)$.
\leanverified{Criterion/FiniteDimensionalKill}{GroupApproximation.map_marked_commutator_eq_one_units}
\leanverified{Criterion/CompressionCentralizerDefect}{GroupApproximation.compressionCentralizerDefect_le_ker}
\end{mainthm}

Applied to $H=E$, Theorem~\ref{thm:B} shows that \emph{every}
finite-dimensional representation of $E$ over \emph{every} field kills
$w$: finite-dimensional linear representations do not separate the points
of $E$, no finite
quotient can witness $w\neq 1$, and the infinite Clifford witness of
Section~\ref{sec:witness} cannot be replaced by a finite-dimensional one.
Property (T) enters the corona argument for one reason:
it makes ``the projection onto the $\Gamma$-fixed space'' an element of
the corona, uniformly across all coordinates at once.

Second, the fixed-space rigidity in that argument cannot simply be omitted:

\begin{mainthm}[cyclic-base comparison]\label{thm:C}
Let $G_0=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)=\langle\, \gamma_0,t\mid
t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2\,\rangle$, let $\Gamma_0=\langle
\gamma_0\rangle\cong\Z$, let $X_0=G_0/\Gamma_0$, and let
$E_0=\ClLamp(X_0)\rtimes G_0$ be the corresponding Clifford extension
(Construction~\ref{con:clifford}), with central sign $z_0$.  Also define
the literal marked-presentation analogue
\[
 E_{\mathrm{BS}}=\left\langle\gamma_0,t,c\ \middle|\
 \begin{aligned}
  &t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2,\quad c^2=1,\quad[c,\gamma_0]=1,\\
  &w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1,\quad
    [w_{\mathrm{BS}},\gamma_0]=[w_{\mathrm{BS}},t]
      =[w_{\mathrm{BS}},c]=1
 \end{aligned}\right\rangle,
\]
where
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}=[tct^{-1},\gamma_0(tct^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}]$.
Then $E_0$ is an amenable MF group and $z_0\neq1$.  There is a quotient
homomorphism $E_{\mathrm{BS}}\twoheadrightarrow E_0$ sending
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to $z_0$; consequently the literal analogous presentation
has a corona representation in which its marked involution survives.
Nevertheless, every finite-dimensional representation of either group
kills the corresponding marked element.
\end{mainthm}

The quotient statement makes the comparison at the level actually used by
the proof: the marked-word relations and witness mechanism survive when the
Kazhdan base is replaced by $\Z$, but the universal corona-kill conclusion
does not.  Thus property~(T), or a substitute furnishing a uniform
fixed-space projection, is essential to this argument; this is not a claim
that every possible proof of the concrete Theorem~\ref{thm:A} must invoke
property~(T).

\subsection*{Generalizations and consequences}
The corona half of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is proved once, in the
following generality (Definition~\ref{def:pattern}).  A \emph{marked
Kazhdan pattern} in a countable group $H$ is a tuple
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$ consisting of a countable group $\Lambda$ with
property (T), a homomorphism $\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ ---
injectivity is \emph{not} assumed --- and elements $t,c\in H$ with
$t\,\iota(\Lambda)\,t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Lambda)$ and
$[c,\iota(\Lambda)]=1$.  Writing $d=tct^{-1}$, the
\emph{compression-defect subgroup} of the pattern is the normal
closure
\[
  N_{\mathrm{comp}}
  \;=\;\bigl\langle\!\bigl\langle\,[\,d,\iota(\lambda)\,]\;:\;
  \lambda\in\Lambda\,\bigr\rangle\!\bigr\rangle_H .
\]
The \emph{finite-normal obstruction criterion}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}) states: every finite normal subgroup of
$H$ contained in $N_{\mathrm{comp}}$ is killed by every corona
representation of $H$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:normalkazhdan} replaces the
finite subgroup by an arbitrary normal property~(T) subgroup and places
that entire subgroup in the MF radical.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(2) is the instance $H=E$
with $F=\{1,w\}$: since $c^2=1$ forces $d^2=1$, the marked word
satisfies the identity $w=[\,d,a\,]^2\in N_{\mathrm{comp}}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:square}).  The corner cut that starts the proof is the
finite-group averaging (Reynolds) projection
$\smash{|F|^{-1}\sum_{f\in F}\Theta(f)}$, whose
complement for $F=\{1,w\}$ is exactly the central corner of step (1)
above.  Corollary~\ref{cor:subalg} upgrades the target from coronas to
arbitrary $C^*$-subalgebras of coronas, so that, for instance, every
homomorphism from $H$ into the unitary group of a unital separable
quasidiagonal $C^*$-algebra, or of $\Cred(F_k)$ for a free group
$F_k$ \cite{HT}, kills such an $F$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:C} calibrates
the hypotheses: for the same pattern over the base $\Z$, the
conclusion fails.

Section~\ref{sec:consequences} records consequences in four
directions.

\emph{(i) $C^*$-algebras.}  Blackadar and Kirchberg proved that MF
algebras are stably finite and asked about the converse: whether
every stably finite separable $C^*$-algebra is MF \cite{BK}, a
question sometimes described --- in Kirchberg's stronger formulation,
a conjecture --- as a $C^*$-analogue of the Connes embedding problem
(see, e.g., the introduction of \cite{Rainone}).  The converse was
already known to fail after the negative solution of the Connes embedding
problem from $\mathrm{MIP}^*=\mathrm{RE}$ \cite{JNVWY}: Goldbring gives
the direct deduction by passing from a non-$\mathcal R^\omega$-embeddable
$\mathrm{II}_1$ factor to a separable stably finite non-MF
$C^*$-algebra \cite[Corollary~5.2]{GoldbringMF}.  The new point here is
that the counterexample is the reduced group $C^*$-algebra of the explicit
finitely presented group constructed above.  Indeed, as recently as March
2026 no full or reduced group $C^*$-algebra was known not to be MF
\cite[Introduction]{Shulman}:

\begin{mainthm}[a stably finite non-MF reduced group algebra]\label{thm:D}
$\Cred(E)$ is a unital separable $C^*$-algebra which carries a
faithful tracial state --- and is therefore stably finite --- but is
not an MF algebra.

Here $\Cred(E)$ is not nuclear ($E$ contains an isomorphic copy of
$\Gamma$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:iotainj}, and $\Gamma$ is not
amenable), so the quasidiagonality problems for \emph{nuclear} stably
finite algebras around \cite{BK,TWW} are untouched
(Remark~\ref{rem:nonnuclear}).  In the opposite direction, for the
ambient HNN extension $G$ of \eqref{eq:G} the algebra $\Cmax(G)$
contains an explicit proper isometry --- so it is not finite, in
particular not MF --- although the group $G$ itself is MF, being
residually finite by the Borisov--Sapir mapping-torus theorem
\cite{BoS} (Proposition~\ref{prop:ambient}).

\emph{(ii) Permanence.}  MF groups are closed under neither quotients
nor split extensions: $E$ is a quotient of the MF group $F_8$, and
the witness group $W=\ClLamp(\bar X)\rtimes\bar G$ of
Section~\ref{sec:witness} is a finitely generated non-MF group that
is a split extension of the residually finite linear group $\bar G$
by the locally finite group $\ClLamp(\bar X)$
(Corollaries~\ref{cor:quotclosure} and~\ref{cor:extclosure}); both
statements contrast with the positive permanence theory of
\cite{Shulman}.

\emph{(iii) Structure.}  The \emph{MF radical} of a countable group
--- the intersection of the kernels of its corona representations,
cf.\ the finite and finite-approximate quotients of
\cite{SlofstraCorrelations} --- is functorial under arbitrary homomorphisms, and the
quotient by it is the largest MF quotient, realized by a single
corona representation (Lemma~\ref{lem:portable} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}; the structural statement is
presumably folklore, and we claim no novelty for it).  Portability of
the radical element $w$ has a striking consequence: embedding $E$
into a $2$-generator simple group $S$, which is possible by
Goryushkin's theorem \cite{Go}, gives a $2$-generator simple group
all of whose homomorphisms into MF groups are trivial
(Corollary~\ref{cor:simple}).

\emph{(iv) Multiplicity and uniformity.}  For every $m\ge2$ the
construction with the endomorphism $(v,A)\mapsto(mv,A)$ yields a
finitely presented non-MF group $E_m$ with $E=E_2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:family}); the groups $E\times\Z^k$, $k\in\N$, are
pairwise non-isomorphic finitely presented non-MF groups, and there
exist $2^{\aleph_0}$ pairwise non-isomorphic finitely generated
non-MF groups (Corollary~\ref{cor:continuum}).  Finally, the kill
theorem is uniform over all matrix sizes at once
(Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}): for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are
$\delta>0$ and a finite $F_0\subseteq E$ such that every map
$\varphi\colon E\to\U(M_r(\C))$, for any $r$, with
$\opnorm{\varphi(g)\varphi(h)-\varphi(gh)}\le\delta$ for all
$g,h\in F_0$, satisfies $\opnorm{\varphi(w)-1}<\varepsilon$; the
proof is a compactness reduction to Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} and
yields no explicit pair $(\delta,F_0)$.

\subsection*{What this paper does not prove}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} does \emph{not} show that $E$ is
nonhyperlinear; equivalently, it does not show that the canonical group
trace, or the associated group von Neumann algebra, fails the relevant
Connes-embedding property.  It also does not touch the
question whether hyperlinear groups are sofic.  The finiteness step (4)
is special to operator-norm coronas: in a tracial (Hilbert--Schmidt)
matrix ultraproduct the available conjugation representation acts on
$L^2$ of the ultraproduct.  When that Hilbert space is infinite-dimensional,
its full operator algebra is properly infinite, so the argument no longer
forces nested unitarily equivalent fixed-space projections to be equal.
Section~\ref{sec:limits} makes this
precise.  The same distinction explains why the group $E/\langle
w\rangle$ remains untouched by our argument; the ambient group $G$ is
in fact MF (Proposition~\ref{prop:ambient}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:D}
likewise concerns only the operator-norm side: it does not bear on
quasidiagonality of nuclear stably finite algebras, nor on tracial
(Connes-embedding) approximation questions.

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}
The closest conceptual analogue is the recent work on nonsofic groups.
The group-level skeleton in OpenAI's Proposition~2.3 consists of a
property-(T) subgroup $\Gamma$, one-sided containments
$t_i\Gamma t_i^{-1}\leq\Gamma$, and a subgroup centralizing $\Gamma$ that
is moved by a compressor \cite[Proposition~2.3]{OAI}.  Contemporaneous work
of Kun--Thom isolates this mechanism through the compression semigroup
$P_\Gamma=\{g:g\Gamma g^{-1}\leq\Gamma\}$, infranormality, normalization
of centralizers, invariance of fixed algebras, and a sofic Mautner envelope
\cite[Theorems~A, B and~C]{KT}.
The property-(T) rigidity engine behind these developments ---
almost-invariant structure for expander sofic approximations of Kazhdan
groups, upgraded and propagated --- originates in Kun's expander
decomposition \cite{Kun16} and in Kun--Thom \cite{KT19}.
Contemporaneously with \cite{OAI,KT}, and completed beforehand (see the
dated footnote in \cite{AlekseevThom}), Alekseev--Thom proved that the
centralizer of any sofic embedding of a Kazhdan group is a metric
ultraproduct of permutation groups, with LEF and residual-finiteness
consequences \cite[Theorem~A]{AlekseevThom}.  All of these results live
on the Hamming/tracial side: they extract structural information about
the approximations themselves.  Lemmas~\ref{lem:beta},
\ref{lem:order} and~\ref{lem:pinning} below instead run the conjugation
representation of operator-norm lifts and extract an exact equality of
Kazhdan projections from stable finiteness; the output is not structure
of an approximation but a universal marked kernel element.
It also records the finite analogue: a
one-sided inclusion $t\Gamma t^{-1}\le\Gamma$ becomes an equality in every
finite quotient.
We therefore make no novelty claim for the one-sided-compression/lamp or
Mautner-type fixed-space-propagation paradigms.
In the reverse direction the divergence is equally sharp: the lamp groups
of \cite{KT} are plain abelian sums $\bigoplus_{G/\Gamma}\Z/2$, with no
central extension, no distinguished sign, and no marked element, and the
OpenAI construction uses a commuting Thompson-type witness subgroup with
no Clifford structure.  The marked-central-kernel device and the Clifford
sign witness of the present paper are not adaptations of either.

The base group itself also has a lineage.  A finitely presented Kazhdan
group carrying a proper ``scaling'' endomorphism of an abelian normal
subgroup belongs to the Cornulier--Abels lineage \cite{Cornulier,Abels}:
Thom followed this pattern, with the shift on a central $\mathbb F_p[t,t^{-1}]$-
(or $\Z[1/p]$-)line inside a Kazhdan matrix group, to produce hyperlinear
Kazhdan groups that are not residually finite \cite[Section~2]{Thom10}.
Our $\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ with $\alpha(v,A)=(2v,A)$ is the
simplest integral analogue of this scaling pattern
(Remark~\ref{rem:whythisbase}).  The approximation conclusions in
\cite{Thom10} are sofic or hyperlinear and use Hamming or tracial
Hilbert--Schmidt models, not operator-norm coronas.  Here the
replacement for finite cardinality is the implication
\[
  P\leq\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  P=\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*,
\]
because the conjugation representation lands in a stably finite norm
matrix corona.  The central corner then converts this equality into a fixed
nontrivial element killed by every corona representation.

The Clifford and marked-sign sides also have close antecedents.  Slofstra
uses finite and infinite-rank Clifford groups with a distinguished central
involution, a shift semidirect product, and an HNN extension implementing a
doubling map in an approximate-representation problem; he also explicitly
records an operator-norm variant \cite[Section~2 and Remark~3.3]{SlofstraProfile}.
The placement and purpose of those pieces differ here: the proper
compression is in the Kazhdan base, the Clifford group supplies an external
nontriviality witness, and the conclusion is a universal kernel rather than
a dimension lower bound.  Slofstra--Vidick's negative-eigenspace compression
for an approximate central involution is the local predecessor of
Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} \cite[proof of Proposition~2.7]{SlofstraVidick}.  Their
Hilbert--Schmidt argument needs quantitative negative-eigenspace dimension.
In the operator-norm setting, Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky subsequently carried
out the dimension-changing corner pipeline explicitly.  Their
Lemmas~2.2--2.3 round an approximate involution and polar-correct an
almost-unitary compression, Proposition~2.4 restricts an asymptotic
representation to a nonzero almost-invariant corner, and Proposition~1.5
applies these steps to a central Deligne involution before invoking
operator--Hilbert--Schmidt stability
\cite[Lemmas~2.2--2.3 and Propositions~1.5 and~2.4]{BDL}.  Thus changing the
matrix size to the negative eigenspace, without a positive relative-rank
hypothesis, is prior art.  Our argument uses the same local corner technology
but replaces operator--Hilbert--Schmidt stability and Mal'cev's theorem by
Kazhdan adjoint pinning and stable finiteness, producing an unconditional
universal marked kernel.

There is an older analytic ancestor as well.  At the foundational level,
the conjugation action on Hilbert--Schmidt operators is the
$\pi\otimes\bar\pi$ model central to Bekka's theory of amenable unitary
representations \cite{BekkaAmenable}; Bekka--Valette connect this model
with property~(T) and the appearance of finite-dimensional
subrepresentations \cite[Theorem~1]{BekkaValette}.  More directly for the
operator-norm approximation setting, Dadarlat's Lemma~3.18 shows
that operator-norm almost-commuting finite-rank projections force weak
containment of the trivial representation in $\pi\otimes\bar\pi$; its proof
turns the normalized projections into almost invariant normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt vectors for the conjugation representation
\cite[Lemma~3.18 and its proof]{Dadarlat}.  Proposition~3.19, labeled Ozawa--Thom and
described there as essentially pointed out by Thom on MathOverflow, then
uses property~(T) to derive an infinite residually finite quotient
\cite[Proposition~3.19]{Dadarlat}.  These results do not produce a universal
marked kernel, but they are direct prior art for the operator-norm-to-Hilbert--
Schmidt conjugation maneuver used here.

The broad exact-versus-asymptotic marked-element phenomenon is also older.
Slofstra defines the quotients $G^{\mathrm{fin}}$ and $G^{\mathrm{fa}}$ by
elements invisible, respectively, to finite-dimensional representations and
to finite-dimensional approximate representations \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}.
This is the natural precedent for the informal ``MF radical'' language below
and for the philosophy of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:C} is not a
claim to originate that separation; it calibrates the same marked
compression presentation used in Theorem~\ref{thm:A} in the operator-norm/MF
setting.  Slofstra--Vidick also recall Fritz's earlier marked
$\mathrm{BS}(2,3)$ hyperlinear-profile example \cite{Fritz}, restated as
\cite[Example~3.1]{SlofstraVidick}, providing further Baumslag--Solitar
context for the cyclic comparison.

De Chiffre--Glebsky--Lubotzky--Thom combine cohomological stability with
non-residually-finite central extensions to obstruct Frobenius approximation
\cite[Introduction]{DGLT}.  Their norm, rigidity input, and conclusion differ
from ours, but their strategy is an important predecessor for the broader
architecture ``designated central obstruction plus rigidity of approximate
representations.''  At the operator/Hilbert--Schmidt boundary,
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky use a finite normal subgroup as a bridge between
the two approximation notions: if $N\trianglelefteq\Lambda$ is finite,
$\Lambda/N$ is hyperlinear, and $\Lambda$ is MF, then $\Lambda$ is
hyperlinear \cite[Proposition~1.6]{BDL}.  That result does not kill $N$;
its proof repairs the restriction to $N$ and passes to a nonzero
character-isotypic corner before applying Proposition~2.4
\cite[proof of Proposition~1.6]{BDL}.  This is direct local prior art for the
finite-normal corner.  Our argument combines the corner with the one-sided
Kazhdan compressor and stable finiteness to force universal annihilation of
the finite subgroup, without assuming hyperlinearity of the quotient.
Reformulations of (weak) soficity through equations over groups and
extensions involving residually finite groups, locating potential
approximation obstructions in finite (central) extensions, appear in
Glebsky--Rivera and Glebsky \cite{GR,Gl19}.  The obstruction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} likewise lives in a finite central extension, though
here the quotient $E/\langle w\rangle$ is not claimed to be residually
finite.

The Kazhdan-projection compression observation needed here is proved
directly in Lemmas~\ref{lem:order} and~\ref{lem:pinning}; we do not
attribute that observation to Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt.  Their work is
used for the group-MF framework and for comparison examples.  In
particular, their treatment of Abels' finitely presented solvable group
already supplies an amenable MF group that is not residually finite
\cite[Section~2.14 and Corollary~2.18]{CDE}.  Since a finitely generated
maximally almost periodic group is residually finite by Mal'cev's theorem,
the broad phenomenon ``finitely presented amenable MF but non-MAP'' is
therefore not new.  Theorem~\ref{thm:C} instead gives a calibrated statement
about the same marked-sign compression pattern used in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

Finally, non-MF-ness of one group $C^*$-algebra alone does not obstruct the
\emph{group} from embedding into a corona, since group MF-ness only requires
an MF quotient of $\Cmax(E)$ in which the canonical image of $E$ remains
faithful.  The contribution asserted here is
    narrower: stable-finite Kazhdan fixed-space rigidity in a norm matrix corona, together
with the central-corner/marked-word device producing a specific nontrivial
element in the kernel of every such representation.  For context on the
approximation landscape see
\cite{DGLT,BDL,OAI,KT,Shulman,Dadarlat,SlofstraCorrelations,SlofstraProfile,SlofstraVidick}.

\section{Notation and external inputs}\label{sec:prelim}

All groups are discrete and countable.  All $C^*$-homomorphisms and
$C^*$-algebra embeddings are understood to be unital unless explicitly
stated otherwise.  For a unital $C^*$-algebra $A$
we write $\U(A)$ for its unitary group.  On $M_r(\C)$ we use the
normalized trace $\tr_r$, the operator norm $\opnorm{\cdot}$, and the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
$\hsnorm{x}=\tr_r(x^*x)^{1/2}$.  We will use three elementary facts
without further comment: $\hsnorm{x}\le\opnorm{x}$;
$|\tr_r(x)|\le\opnorm{x}$; and $\hsnorm{uxv}=\hsnorm{x}$ for unitaries
$u,v$.

Given a sequence $(d_n)_{n\in J}$ of positive integers indexed by an
infinite set $J\subseteq\N$, we write
\[
  \mathcal Q\bigl((d_n)_{n\in J}\bigr)
  \;=\;\prod_{n\in J}^{\ell^\infty} M_{d_n}(\C)
  \Big/\bigoplus_{n\in J}^{c_0} M_{d_n}(\C),
\]
the quotient of the bounded $C^*$-product by the ideal of sequences with
$\opnorm{x_n}\to0$.  We call any such algebra a \emph{norm matrix
corona}.  A \emph{corona representation} of a group $E$ is a group
homomorphism $E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ for some norm matrix corona
$\mathcal Q$; the group $E$ is \emph{MF} if it admits an injective
corona representation.

For a group $H$, $\Cmax(H)$ denotes the maximal group $C^*$-algebra and
$u_h\in\Cmax(H)$ the canonical unitaries.  Every unitary representation
$\pi$ of $H$ on a Hilbert space extends uniquely to a
$*$-representation of $\Cmax(H)$, still denoted $\pi$.  Every group
homomorphism $\varphi\colon H_1\to H_2$ induces a unital
$*$-homomorphism $\Cmax(H_1)\to\Cmax(H_2)$, $u_h\mapsto
u_{\varphi(h)}$.

We use the commutator convention $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$.

The following two classical facts are the only non-elementary external
inputs to the analytic kernel argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

\begin{fact}[{property (T); \cite[Example~1.7.4(i)]{BHV}}]\label{fact:T}
$\SL_3(\Z)$ and $\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ have property (T)
\cite[Example~1.7.4(i), via Corollary~1.4.16 and Theorem~1.7.1]{BHV};
an explicit-constant proof for $\SL_n(\Z)$, $n\ge3$, is
\cite[Theorem~4.2.5]{BHV}.  The group $\Gamma$ is finitely presented ---
not as a consequence of property (T), which does not imply finite
presentability \cite[Section~3.4]{BHV}, but by the explicit presentation
\eqref{eq:gamma-presentation} below, built on \cite[Theorem~2]{CRW}.
\end{fact}

\begin{fact}[{Kazhdan projection; \cite{AW,Valette}}]\label{fact:kazhdan}
Let $\Gamma$ be a group with property (T).  There is a projection
$p_\Gamma\in\Cmax(\Gamma)$ such that for every unitary representation
$\pi$ of $\Gamma$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, the operator
$\pi(p_\Gamma)$ is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
$\Fix(\pi)=\{\xi\in\mathcal H:\pi(\gamma)\xi=\xi
\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\}$.  Moreover $u_\gamma
p_\Gamma=p_\Gamma u_\gamma=p_\Gamma$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$.
\end{fact}

The identities follow from the defining property: the left identity holds in
every representation, and the right identity follows by taking adjoints (or
replacing $\gamma$ by $\gamma^{-1}$); $\Cmax(\Gamma)$ has a faithful
representation.

The only additional input, used exclusively in
Section~\ref{sec:necessity}, is:

\begin{fact}[{\cite[Corollary C]{TWW}}]\label{fact:tww}
The reduced group $C^*$-algebra of a countable amenable group is
quasidiagonal.
\end{fact}

\begin{fact}[{quasidiagonal embedding; \cite[Theorem~3.2.2]{BK}, see also \cite[Chapter 7]{BO}}]
\label{fact:qdcorona}
Every separable unital quasidiagonal $C^*$-algebra embeds unitally into a
norm matrix corona.  Consequently every countable amenable group is MF.
\end{fact}

\section{The group}\label{sec:group}

Throughout, $\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ with multiplication
$(v,A)(w,B)=(v+Aw,\,AB)$.

We first record concrete presentation data.  By \cite[Theorem~2]{CRW},
$\SL_3(\Z)$ has the presentation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sl3presentation}
 \left\langle x,y,z\ \middle|\
 \begin{gathered}
 x^3=y^3=z^2=(xz)^3=(yz)^3=1,\\
 (x^{-1}zxy)^2=(y^{-1}zyx)^2=(xy)^6=1
 \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
where the generators are realized by the matrices
\[
x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
y=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&-1&-1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
z=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\-1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
(The matrix realization is assembled from the data of
\cite[Sections~1, 2 and~4]{CRW}, which set $x=h$, $y=aha$, $z=p$ in the
notation there; it is not displayed as a single statement in \cite{CRW}.
We have verified directly that these three matrices satisfy the eight
relations and generate $\SL_3(\Z)$, which suffices since the finitely
generated linear group $\SL_3(\Z)$ is Hopfian \cite{Ma}.)
Let $v_i=(e_i,1)$ for $1\le i\le3$.  Reading off the columns of these
matrices gives the following complete finite presentation of the semidirect
product:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-presentation}
\Gamma=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\ \middle|\
\begin{gathered}
\text{the eight relations in \eqref{eq:sl3presentation}},\\
[v_1,v_2]=[v_1,v_3]=[v_2,v_3]=1,\\
xv_1x^{-1}=v_3,\ xv_2x^{-1}=v_1,\
xv_3x^{-1}=v_2,\\
yv_1y^{-1}=v_1,\ yv_2y^{-1}=v_2^{-1}v_3,\
yv_3y^{-1}=v_1v_2^{-1},\\
zv_1z^{-1}=v_2v_3^{-1},\ zv_2z^{-1}=v_1v_3^{-1},\
zv_3z^{-1}=v_3^{-1}
\end{gathered}\right\rangle.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:alpha}
The map $\alpha(v,A)=(2v,A)$ is an injective endomorphism of $\Gamma$
with image $2\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ of index $8$, and
$a:=(e_1,1)\notin\alpha(\Gamma)$.
\leanverified{Monsters/AffineSL3Doubling}{GroupApproximation.AffineSL3Doubling.doubling_package}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We compute
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \alpha\bigl((v,A)(w,B)\bigr)&=\alpha(v+Aw,\,AB)=(2v+2Aw,\,AB)\\
  &=(2v,A)(2w,B)=\alpha(v,A)\,\alpha(w,B),
\end{aligned}
\]
so $\alpha$ is a homomorphism,
clearly injective, with image $2\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$; the index equals
$[\Z^3:2\Z^3]=8$.  If $(e_1,1)=(2v,A)$ then $A=1$ and $e_1=2v$, which is
impossible in $\Z^3$.
\end{proof}

Let $G$ denote the ascending HNN extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
  G\;=\;\bigl\langle\,\Gamma,\;t\;\big|\;
  t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)\ (\gamma\in\Gamma)\,\bigr\rangle .
\end{equation}
We will use $G$ only as an auxiliary group receiving no injectivity
claims; every statement we need about it is either a presentation
identity or is verified inside an explicit linear quotient
(Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}).

\begin{definition}\label{def:E}
Use the six base generators and the relations displayed in
\eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}, and put $a=v_1$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
  E=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\ \middle|\
  \begin{gathered}
   \text{all relations in \eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}},\\
   tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^2\ (1\le i\le3),\\
   txt^{-1}=x,\quad tyt^{-1}=y,\quad tzt^{-1}=z,\\
   c^2=1,\qquad w^2=1,\\
   [c,g]=1\quad(g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
   [w,g]=1\quad(g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\})
  \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $w$ abbreviates the word
$\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\,v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}\bigr]$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFPresentation}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFPresentation.literal_algebraic_package}
\end{definition}

Thus \eqref{eq:E} is an explicit presentation with eight generators and a
fully displayed finite list of relators.  The
presentation identities give a canonical homomorphism $G\to E$ and a
canonical (not a priori injective) homomorphism $\Gamma\to E$; we write
$\gamma$, $t$, $c$ for the images in $E$.  In $E$ the defining relations
make $w$ central and impose $w^2=1$; Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} below
proves that $w\neq1$.  The relations
$t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$, $[c,\gamma]=1$ hold for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$ because they hold on generators.
Figure~\ref{fig:assembly} summarizes the three-stage assembly and the
fate of the marked word.

\begin{remark}[presentation robustness]\label{rem:anypresentation}
Nothing in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} depends on the particular
presentation \eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}: for \emph{any} finite
presentation of $\Gamma$, together with words representing the images of
the generators under $\alpha$ and a word representing $a$, the analogue
of \eqref{eq:E} satisfies Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with the same proof, since
the argument consumes the relations only through the canonical
homomorphisms $\Gamma\to E$ and $G\to E$.  The role of
\cite[Theorem~2]{CRW} is therefore confined to certifying that
\eqref{eq:sl3presentation} is complete as a presentation of
$\SL_3(\Z)$.  The two remaining ingredients of the explicit display are
verified independently of \cite{CRW}: the eight relations hold by direct
integer computation, and the displayed matrices generate $\SL_3(\Z)$
because explicit words in them realize every elementary transvection ---
for instance $y^{-1}xzy^{-1}x^{-1}$ is the matrix $1+E_{12}$, and the
elementary matrices classically generate $\SL_3(\Z)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[nontriviality of the marked image forces proper infinite
compression]\label{rem:properness}
Let $\rho$ be any homomorphism of $E$ to a group, and write
$\Gamma'=\rho(\Gamma)$.  If $\rho(t)\Gamma'\rho(t)^{-1}=\Gamma'$
--- in particular whenever $\Gamma'$ is finite, since a one-sided
inclusion of conjugate finite subgroups is an equality --- then
$a':=\rho(t)^{-1}\rho(a)\rho(t)\in\Gamma'$, so
\[
  \rho\bigl(a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr)
  =\rho(t)\,a'\rho(c)a'^{-1}\,\rho(t)^{-1}
  =\rho(tct^{-1}),
\]
because $\rho(c)$ commutes with $\Gamma'$; hence $\rho(w)=1$.
Consequently any homomorphism with $\rho(w)\neq1$ compresses $\Gamma'$
properly, and $\Gamma'$ is then infinite.  This shows structurally why
the pattern cannot collide with the standard certificates of MF-ness: an
infinite property-(T) image rules out amenable targets, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} rules out linear and residually finite ones.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[why this base]\label{rem:whythisbase}
A base for Definition~\ref{def:E} must be a finitely presented Kazhdan
group with a proper self-embedding.  Infinite Kazhdan groups are
typically co-Hopfian --- lattices in higher-rank semisimple groups by
Prasad \cite{Prasad}, torsion-free hyperbolic groups by Sela
\cite{Sela} --- so such bases are scarce.  In
$\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$ the abelian factor exists precisely to
break co-Hopficity through the doubling endomorphism, while relative
property (T) of the pair $(\Gamma,\Z^3)$ restores property (T) for the
whole group.
\end{remark}

\section{The witness: \texorpdfstring{$w\neq 1$}{w is nontrivial}}\label{sec:witness}

Clifford signs, shifts, and HNN doubling have appeared together before in
approximate-representation theory, notably in Slofstra's hyperlinear-profile
construction \cite[Section~2]{SlofstraProfile}.  The construction below uses
the Clifford lamp only as an explicit algebraic witness: the one-sided HNN
compression relevant to the obstruction remains in the separate Kazhdan
base $\Gamma$.

\begin{construction}[Clifford groups]\label{con:clifford}
Let $X$ be a set.  Define $\Cl(X)$ to be the quotient of the free unital
associative real algebra on symbols $(c_x)_{x\in X}$ by the relations
\[
  c_x^2=1,\qquad c_xc_y=-\,c_yc_x\quad(x\neq y).
\]
For completeness, here is a concrete PBW model.  Choose a total order on
$X$, and let $A_X$ be the real vector space with basis $(e_F)$ indexed by
the finite subsets $F\subseteq X$.  Put
\[
 e_F e_G=(-1)^{\nu(F,G)}e_{F\mathbin{\triangle}G},\qquad
 \nu(F,G)=\#\{(x,y)\in F\times G:y<x\}.
\]
Modulo two, $\nu$ is additive in each variable with respect to symmetric
difference, so this multiplication is associative and $e_\varnothing$ is
the identity.  The elements $e_{\{x\}}$ square to $1$, and for $x\neq y$
exactly one of $\nu(\{x\},\{y\})$ and $\nu(\{y\},\{x\})$ is one; hence they
anticommute.  The defining relations therefore give a homomorphism
$\Cl(X)\to A_X$ sending $c_x$ to $e_{\{x\}}$.  Conversely the relations
reduce every word to an ordered square-free monomial, whose image is
$\pm e_F$.  Thus these monomials are a basis, $\Cl(X)\neq0$, and
$-1\neq1$ without invoking a normal-form theorem.

Let $\ClLamp(X)\le\Cl(X)^{\times}$ be the subgroup generated by
$\{\pm1\}\cup\{c_x:x\in X\}$ and write $z=-1\in\ClLamp(X)$, a central
involution.  Every permutation of $X$ induces an algebra automorphism of
$\Cl(X)$ mapping
$c_x\mapsto c_{\sigma(x)}$ and fixing $z$; this gives an action of any
group acting on $X$ by automorphisms of $\ClLamp(X)$, and a semidirect
product $\ClLamp(X)\rtimes G$ for any $G$-set $X$.
The group $\ClLamp(X)$ is the central sign extension of
$\bigoplus_X\Z/2$ defined by the ordered-inversion cocycle above; its
commutator pairing is
$(-1)^{|F||G|-|F\cap G|}$.  It is a Clifford $2$-group of
possibly infinite rank, familiar as the multiplicative group generated by
Clifford (Majorana) generators in the CAR algebra (see, e.g., \cite{BR2}).
The same finite- and infinite-rank Clifford groups with their distinguished
central sign appear explicitly in \cite[Section~2]{SlofstraProfile}; for a modern toolbox of
property~(T) groups with wreath-like lamp structure see \cite{CIOS}.
\leanverified{Monsters/CliffordAlgebraLamp}{GroupApproximation.CliffordAlgebraLamp.cliffordLamp_group_package}
\leanverified{Monsters/CliffordAlgebraLamp}{GroupApproximation.CliffordAlgebraLamp.cliffordLamp_permutation_package}
\end{construction}

\begin{lemma}[linear model]\label{lem:linear}
Embed $\Gamma\hookrightarrow\GL_4(\Z)$ by
$(v,A)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}A&v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and let
$D=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)\in\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$.  Then
$D\gamma D^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$, so
$\bar G:=\langle\Gamma,D\rangle\le\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$ is a quotient of $G$
via $t\mapsto D$.  In the $\bar G$-set $\bar X:=\bar G/\Gamma$ the
cosets $D\Gamma$ and $aD\Gamma$ are distinct.
\leanverified{Monsters/ExplicitLinearModel}{GroupApproximation.ExplicitLinearModel.doubling_linear_model_package}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$D\begin{pmatrix}A&v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}D^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}A&2v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, which is the matrix of
$\alpha(v,A)$; so the assignment $\gamma\mapsto\gamma$, $t\mapsto D$
respects the relations \eqref{eq:G}.  If $aD\Gamma=D\Gamma$ then
$D^{-1}aD\in\Gamma$ inside $\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$.  But
$D^{-1}aD=\begin{pmatrix}1&\tfrac12 e_1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has a
non-integral entry, so it does not lie in the integral group $\Gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:witness}
$w\neq1$ in $E$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFLinearWitness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFLinearWitness.literal_mark_ne_one}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\bar X=\bar G/\Gamma$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} and form
$W:=\ClLamp(\bar X)\rtimes\bar G$ as in Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.
Define a map on the generators of \eqref{eq:E} by
\[
\begin{gathered}
  g\mapsto g\in\bar G\quad
  (g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
  t\mapsto D\in\bar G,\qquad
  c\mapsto c_{o},\quad o:=\Gamma\in\bar X .
\end{gathered}
\]
We check the relations.  The base relations in
\eqref{eq:gamma-presentation} and
$tgt^{-1}=\alpha(g)$ for the six base generators hold in $\bar G$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}.  We
have $c_o^2=1$.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we have $\gamma o=o$, so
$\gamma c_o\gamma^{-1}=c_{\gamma o}=c_o$, giving the six commutation
relations for $c$.
Under this assignment,
\[
  tct^{-1}\mapsto c_{D\Gamma},\qquad
  a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\mapsto c_{aD\Gamma},
\]
and the two sites are distinct by Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}, so
\[
  w\mapsto[c_{D\Gamma}\,,\,c_{aD\Gamma}]
  = c_{D\Gamma}c_{aD\Gamma}c_{D\Gamma}^{-1}c_{aD\Gamma}^{-1}
  = -1 = z .
\]
Since $z$ is a central involution of $W$, the remaining relations
$w^2=1$ and $[w,s]=[w,t]=[w,c]=1$ are also satisfied.  Hence the
assignment extends to a group homomorphism $E\to W$ sending $w$ to
$z\neq1$, and therefore $w\neq1$ in $E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The target uses the infinite group $\ClLamp(\bar X)$ when $\bar X$ is

the remaining data are matrices over $\Z[1/2]$.  No normal-form theory for
HNN extensions enters.  In
particular the consistency of the presentation \eqref{eq:E} is verified
by an explicit linear-algebraic model.
\end{remark}

\section{The finite-dimensional obstruction}\label{sec:findim}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.  Everything happens inside the
finite-dimensional algebra $\End_k(V)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
Let $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$ be a homomorphism and let
\[
  C\;=\;\{\,x\in\End_k(V)\;:\;\pi(\gamma)\,x=x\,\pi(\gamma)
  \text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\,\}
\]
be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$, a linear subspace of $\End_k(V)$.
Let $u=\pi(t)$ and consider the linear automorphism
$\Phi(x)=uxu^{-1}$ of $\End_k(V)$.

We claim $C\subseteq\Phi(C)$.  Let $x\in C$ and set $y=u^{-1}xu$, so
that $x=\Phi(y)$.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we have
$t\gamma t^{-1}\in\Gamma$, hence $\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})$ commutes with
$x$, and therefore
\[
  \pi(\gamma)\,y
  = u^{-1}\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,x\,u
  = u^{-1}\,x\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,u
  = y\,\pi(\gamma).
\]
Thus $y\in C$, proving the claim.  Since $\Phi$ is a linear
isomorphism, $\dim_k\Phi(C)=\dim_kC<\infty$, and the inclusion
$C\subseteq\Phi(C)$ forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinning}
  C\;=\;\Phi(C)\;=\;uCu^{-1}.
\end{equation}

Now $\pi(c)\in C$ because $c$ centralizes $\Gamma$.  By
\eqref{eq:pinning}, $\pi(tct^{-1})=u\,\pi(c)\,u^{-1}\in C$.  Since
$a\in\Gamma$, the element $\pi(tct^{-1})$ commutes with $\pi(a)$, so
$\pi\bigl(a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr)=\pi(tct^{-1})$, and therefore
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)
  =\bigl[\,\pi(tct^{-1}),\;\pi(tct^{-1})\bigr]=1. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The same calculation proves the final, subgroup-valued assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.  The elements of $H$ whose conjugation action
preserves $C$ in both directions form a subgroup.  The argument above
puts every one-sided compressor in that subgroup, and therefore puts all
of $G_{\rm comp}(\Gamma)$ in it.  Thus, for $g\in
G_{\rm comp}(\Gamma)$ and $z\in C_H(\Gamma)$, the operator
$\pi(gzg^{-1})$ still belongs to $C$ and commutes with every
$\pi(\ell)$, $\ell\in\Gamma$.  Hence $\pi$ kills all the generators
in \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}, and normality of its kernel kills their
normal closure.

\section{Norm matrix coronas}\label{sec:corona}

Throughout this section $\mathcal Q=\mathcal Q((d_n)_{n\in J})$ is a
norm matrix corona, $q\colon\prod_{n\in J}M_{d_n}(\C)\to\mathcal Q$ the
quotient map.

The following lifting facts are standard perturbation theory; see
Loring \cite{Loring} for a systematic treatment.

\begin{lemma}[lifting]\label{lem:lift}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every projection $e\in\mathcal Q$ lifts to a sequence
    $(e_n)_{n\in J}$ of projections, $e_n\in M_{d_n}(\C)$.
  \item Every unitary $u\in\mathcal Q$ lifts to a sequence of unitaries.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Lift $e$ to a bounded sequence $(x_n)$; replacing $x_n$ by
$\tfrac12(x_n+x_n^*)$ and then by the functional-calculus cutoff
$f(x_n)$, where $f(t)=\min(1,\max(0,t))$, we may assume
$0\le x_n\le1$.  These replacements preserve the quotient class because
functional calculus commutes with the quotient map and
$f(e)=e$.  Since $q((x_n))$
is a projection, $\opnorm{x_n^2-x_n}\to0$, so the spectrum of $x_n$ is
eventually contained in $[0,\tfrac13)\cup(\tfrac23,1]$; for such $n$
put $e_n=\chi_{(\frac12,\,\infty)}(x_n)$, a projection with
$\opnorm{e_n-x_n}\to0$, and set $e_n=0$ for the finitely many remaining
$n$.  Then $(e_n)$ lifts $e$.

(2) Lift $u$ to an arbitrary bounded sequence $(x_n)$.
Since $q((x_n))$ is unitary, $\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$, so $x_n$ is
eventually invertible; for such $n$ put $u_n=x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$, a
unitary with $\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\to0$; set $u_n=1$ for the remaining $n$.
\end{proof}

Stable finiteness of norm matrix coronas is standard and goes back to
Blackadar--Kirchberg \cite{BK}; we include the short proofs to keep the
external surface explicit.

\begin{lemma}[stable finiteness]\label{lem:finite}
Let $\mathcal Q$ be a norm matrix corona.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every isometry in $\mathcal Q$ is a unitary; the same holds in
    every matrix amplification $M_k(\mathcal Q)$.
  \item If $p,q\in\mathcal Q$ are projections with $p\le q$ and
    $v^*v=q$, $vv^*=p$ for some $v\in\mathcal Q$, then $p=q$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Let $v\in\mathcal Q$ with $v^*v=1$ and lift $v$ to $(x_n)$ with
$\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$.  Eventually $x_n$ is invertible (a
left-invertible square matrix is invertible), and the unitary
$u_n=x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$ satisfies $\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\to0$; hence
$v=q((u_n))$ is unitary.  For $M_k(\mathcal Q)$ note
$M_k(\mathcal Q)\cong\mathcal Q((k\,d_n)_{n\in J})$, since matrix
amplification commutes with products and with the ideal of null
sequences.

(2) This is the standard fact that in a finite algebra a projection is
not equivalent to a proper subprojection; we include the short
verification for completeness.  As in any $C^*$-algebra, $v^*v=q$ gives
$v=vq$ (because
$\opnorm{v(1-q)}^2=\opnorm{(1-q)v^*v(1-q)}=\opnorm{(1-q)q(1-q)}=0$) and
$vv^*=p$ gives $v=pv$ (because
$\opnorm{(1-p)v}^2=\opnorm{(1-p)vv^*(1-p)}=0$).  From $p\le q$ we get
$qv=q(pv)=pv=v$, hence $(1-q)v=0$ and, taking adjoints,
$v^*(1-q)=0$ and $(1-q)v^*=\bigl(v(1-q)\bigr)^*=0$.  Then
$s:=v+(1-q)$ satisfies
\[
  s^*s=v^*v+v^*(1-q)+(1-q)v+(1-q)=q+0+0+(1-q)=1,
\]
so $s$ is an isometry, and
\[
  ss^*=vv^*+v(1-q)+(1-q)v^*+(1-q)=p+0+0+(1-q)=p+(1-q).
\]
By part (1), $ss^*=1$, i.e.\ $p+(1-q)=1$, i.e.\ $p=q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[corner coronas]\label{lem:cornercorona}
Let $e\in\mathcal Q$ be a nonzero projection.  Then there are an
infinite set $J'\subseteq J$ and positive integers $(r_n)_{n\in J'}$
with $r_n\le d_n$ such that $e\mathcal Qe$ is unitally
$*$-isomorphic to the norm matrix corona
$\mathcal Q'=\mathcal Q\bigl((r_n)_{n\in J'}\bigr)$, by an
isomorphism carrying $e$ to $1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}(1) lift $e$
to projections $(e_n)$; let $J'=\{n\in J: e_n\neq0\}$ and
$r_n=\operatorname{rank}e_n$ for $n\in J'$.  The set $J'$ is infinite,
since otherwise $(e_n)$ would be a null sequence and $e=0$.

Consider the map
\[
  \Psi\colon \prod_{n\in J'} e_nM_{d_n}(\C)e_n
  \longrightarrow e\,\mathcal Q\,e,\qquad
  (x_n)_{n\in J'}\longmapsto q\bigl((\tilde x_n)_{n\in J}\bigr),
\]
where $\tilde x_n=x_n$ for $n\in J'$ and $\tilde x_n=0$ otherwise.
$\Psi$ is a $*$-homomorphism onto $e\mathcal Qe$: surjectivity holds
because any $y\in e\mathcal Qe$ lifts to $(y_n)$ with
$\opnorm{e_ny_ne_n-y_n}\to0$, and $e_ny_ne_n=0$ off $J'$.  Its kernel
is exactly the sequences with $\opnorm{x_n}\to0$.  Choosing unitary
identifications $e_n\C^{d_n}\cong\C^{r_n}$ identifies
$e_nM_{d_n}(\C)e_n$ with $M_{r_n}(\C)$ compatibly with adjoints and
norms, so
\[
  e\,\mathcal Q\,e\;\cong\;\mathcal Q\bigl((r_n)_{n\in J'}\bigr)
  =\mathcal Q' ,
\]
a unital isomorphism carrying $e$ to $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[finite-normal corners]\label{lem:corner}
Let $H$ be a group, $F\trianglelefteq H$ a finite normal subgroup,
and $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ a corona representation whose
restriction to $F$ is nontrivial.  Then there are an infinite set
$J'\subseteq J$, positive integers $(r_n)_{n\in J'}$ with
$r_n\le d_n$, and a corona representation
\[
  \Theta_-\colon H\longrightarrow
  \U\bigl(\mathcal Q'\bigr),\qquad
  \mathcal Q'=\mathcal Q\bigl((r_n)_{n\in J'}\bigr),
\]
with
\[
  \sum_{f\in F}\Theta_-(f)\;=\;0 .
\]
In the case $F=\{1,w\}$ with $w$ a central involution and
$\Theta(w)\neq1$, the corner projection is
$e=\tfrac12(1-\Theta(w))$ and the conclusion reads
$\Theta_-(w)=-1$.
\end{lemma}

In the involution case, this is the norm-corona counterpart of
Slofstra--Vidick's compression to
the $(-1)$-eigenspace of a repaired approximate central involution
\cite[proof of Proposition~2.7]{SlofstraVidick}.  In their normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt setting a quantitative separation supplies a positive
relative dimension.  Here nonzeroness of the corona projection is enough:
passing to its corner changes the ambient matrix sequence and renormalizes
all later traces there.  The operator-norm involution version of this
corner-and-polar-correction pipeline appears in
\cite[Proposition~1.5]{BDL}, with rounding and compression inputs in
\cite[Lemma~2.2 and Proposition~2.4]{BDL}; the character-isotypic
finite-normal variant appears in the proof of
\cite[Proposition~1.6]{BDL}.  For general $F$ the corner projection is the
complement of the standard finite-group Reynolds projection of the finite
normal subgroup.

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
  p_F\;=\;\frac1{|F|}\sum_{f\in F}\Theta(f) .
\]
Then $p_F^*=p_F$, because $\Theta(F)$ is closed under adjoints ($F$ is
closed under inverses), and $p_F^2=p_F$: for each $f_0\in F$, left
translation by $f_0$ permutes $F$, so $\Theta(f_0)\,p_F=p_F$, and
averaging over $f_0$ gives $p_F^2=p_F$.  Thus $p_F$ is a projection.
For $g\in H$, conjugation by $g$ permutes the normal subgroup $F$, so
$\Theta(g)\,p_F\,\Theta(g)^{-1}=p_F$: the projection $p_F$ commutes
with $\Theta(H)$.  If $p_F=1$ then, for every $f_0\in F$,
$\Theta(f_0)=\Theta(f_0)\,p_F=p_F=1$, contradicting nontriviality of
$\Theta|_F$; so $e:=1-p_F$ is a nonzero projection commuting with
$\Theta(H)$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:cornercorona}, $e\mathcal Qe\cong\mathcal Q'$ by a
unital $*$-isomorphism carrying $e$ to $1$.  Since $e$ commutes with
$\Theta(H)$, the map $\Theta_-(g):=\Theta(g)e$ is a unital group
homomorphism $H\to\U(e\mathcal Qe)=\U(\mathcal Q')$, and
\[
  \sum_{f\in F}\Theta_-(f)
  =\Bigl(\sum_{f\in F}\Theta(f)\Bigr)e
  =|F|\,p_F\,(1-p_F)=0 .
\]
For $F=\{1,w\}$ with $w$ central of order two,
$p_F=\tfrac12(1+\Theta(w))$, so $e=\tfrac12(1-\Theta(w))$ and
$\Theta_-(w)=\Theta(w)e=(1-2e)e=-e=-1$ in $\mathcal Q'$.
\end{proof}

\section{The conjugation representation and the Kazhdan projection}\label{sec:conj}

The corona half of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} will be deduced from a general
criterion, whose data we now fix.

\begin{definition}[marked Kazhdan pattern]\label{def:pattern}
Let $H$ be a countable group.  A \emph{marked Kazhdan pattern} in $H$
is a tuple $(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, where $\Lambda$ is a countable
group with property (T), $\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ is a group
homomorphism --- \emph{not} assumed injective --- and $t,c\in H$,
subject to
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(M1)] $t\,\iota(\lambda)\,t^{-1}\in\iota(\Lambda)$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$;
  \item[(M2)] $c\,\iota(\lambda)=\iota(\lambda)\,c$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
Write $d:=tct^{-1}$.  The \emph{compression-defect subgroup} of the
pattern is the normal closure
\[
  N_{\mathrm{comp}}
  \;=\;\bigl\langle\!\bigl\langle\,
  [\,d,\iota(\lambda)\,]\;:\;\lambda\in\Lambda
  \,\bigr\rangle\!\bigr\rangle_H\;\trianglelefteq\;H .
\]
There are two canonical enlargements.  The integer-orbit defect is
\[
 N_{\rm orb}=\left\langle\!\left\langle
 [t^nct^{-n},\iota(\lambda)]:n\in\Z,\ \lambda\in\Lambda
 \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_H,
\]
and, for $L=\iota(\Lambda)$, the intrinsic defect is
$\mathfrak D(H,L)$ from \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}.  Since $t$ is a
one-sided compressor and $G_{\rm comp}(L)$ is a group, all powers
$t^n$, including negative powers, lie in $G_{\rm comp}(L)$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defectinclusions}
 N_{\rm comp}\ \le\ N_{\rm orb}\ \le\ \mathfrak D(H,L).
\end{equation}
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanCompressionCore}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore}
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanCompressionCore}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.defectNormal}
\leanverified{Sofic/IntrinsicCompressionDefect}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.defectNormal_le_orbitDefectNormal}
\leanverified{Sofic/IntrinsicCompressionDefect}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.orbitDefectNormal_le_compressionCentralizerDefect}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[finite-normal obstruction criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $H$ be a countable group with a marked Kazhdan pattern
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, and let $F\trianglelefteq H$ be a finite
normal subgroup with $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$.  Then every
corona representation $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies
$\Theta(f)=1$ for all $f\in F$.
\leanverified{Sofic/FiniteNormalCoronaObstruction}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.finiteNormal_le_normMatrixCoronaKernel}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[normal-Kazhdan radical criterion]\label{thm:normalkazhdan}
Let $H$ be countable with a marked Kazhdan pattern, and let
$K\trianglelefteq H$ have property~(T).  If $K\le N_{\rm comp}$, then
\[
  K\le \operatorname{Rad}_{\rm MF}(H).
\]
Thus every norm-matrix-corona representation of $H$ kills $K$.  In
particular, if $K\neq1$, then $H$ is non-MF.  The obstruction subgroup
need not be finite, central, or torsion.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanMFRadical}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.normalKazhdan_le_normMFResidual}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a homomorphism $\Theta$ from $H$ to an operator-norm matrix
ultraproduct, and suppose that $\Theta(k)\neq1$ for some $k\in K$.
Replace the target by the countable range $R=\Theta(H)$.  The subgroup
$K'=\Theta(K)\le R$ is nontrivial and normal, has property~(T), and is
contained in the compression defect of the transported pattern.

The inclusion of $R$ into the original ultraproduct is injective, so
the universal MF radical of $R$ is trivial.  The universal-quotient
diagonalization therefore gives a faithful representation of $R$ in
one cofinite norm matrix corona.  Finally, bounded tensor powers,
taken separately for the finitely many ordered pairs in a local test
set and then combined by block sum, normalize all pairwise separation
constants to $1$.  Hence $R$ has a uniformly separated operator-norm
matrix approximation.

Apply the normal property~(T) corner construction to $K'$.  It produces
from this ambient matrix model a tracial matrix-ultraproduct
representation in which some element of $K'$ survives.  Applied to the
same corner model, the compressor argument makes every generator
$[tct^{-1},\iota(\lambda)]$ trivial, hence kills its normal closure and
all of $K'$, a contradiction.  This is the second Kazhdan projection
hidden in the finite-normal argument: it replaces the finite Reynolds
average.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Kazhdan part and exact radical]\label{cor:kazhdanpart}
Let $\mathcal D_T$ be the subgroup generated by all normal property~(T)
subgroups of $H$ contained in $N_{\rm comp}$.  Then
$\mathcal D_T\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm MF}(H)$.  If
$N_{\rm comp}$ itself has property~(T), every corona representation
factors uniquely through $H/N_{\rm comp}$; and if this quotient is MF,
then
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\rm MF}(H)=N_{\rm comp}.
\]

\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanMFRadical}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.existsUnique_defectNormal_factorization_to_normMatrixCorona}
\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanMFRadical}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.normMFResidual_eq_defectNormal}

\begin{theorem}[intrinsic normal-Kazhdan criterion]
\label{thm:intrinsicnormalkazhdan}
Let $L=\iota(\Lambda)\le H$ and let $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ be the
compression--centralizer defect from \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}.  If
$K\trianglelefteq H$ has property~\textup{(T)} and
$K\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, then
\[
 K\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm MF}(H).
\]
Consequently, if $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ itself has property~\textup{(T)},
every corona representation factors through
$H/\mathfrak D(H,L)$; if that quotient is MF, then
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\rm MF}(H)=\mathfrak D(H,L).
\]
The same containment holds for the subgroup generated by all normal
property~\textup{(T)} subgroups contained in $\mathfrak D(H,L)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the conjugation representation associated to a corona model, let $P$
be the Kazhdan projection onto the $L$-fixed vectors.  For every one-sided
compressor $s\in\mathcal C(L)$ the fixed-vector calculation gives
$P\le\beta(s)P\beta(s)^*$.  Stable finiteness upgrades this to equality,
so $P$ commutes with $\beta(s)$.  It therefore commutes with
$\beta(g)$ for every $g\in G_{\rm comp}(L)$, including inverses and
arbitrary finite products.
\leanverified{Kazhdan/FixedSpaceStabilizer}{GroupApproximation.FixedSpaceStabilizer.compressionGroup_le_stabilizer}

If $z\in C_H(L)$, then the vector represented by $\Theta(z)$ is
$L$-fixed.  Hence the vector represented by
$\Theta(gzg^{-1})$ is again $L$-fixed for every
$g\in G_{\rm comp}(L)$.  It follows that every generator
$[gzg^{-1},\ell]$ of \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}, and therefore its
normal closure, is trivial in the associated tracial matrix
ultraproduct.
\leanverified{Kazhdan/FixedSpaceDefect}{GroupApproximation.FixedSpaceDefect.compressionCentralizerDefect_le_ker}
The second Kazhdan-corner argument from
Theorem~\ref{thm:normalkazhdan}, now with
$K\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, gives the radical containment.  The
factorization and equality statements follow from the universal property
of the quotient and Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[faithfully tracial targets]\label{thm:tracialtarget}
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra carrying a faithful tracial state
$\tau$, and suppose that $A\otimes_{\max}A^{\rm op}$ is stably finite.
For every homomorphism $\Theta\colon H\to\U(A)$,
\[
 \Theta\bigl(\mathfrak D(H,L)\bigr)=1.
\]
In particular the conclusion holds when $A$ is nuclear and faithfully
tracial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider the exact representation
\[
 \beta(g)=\Theta(g)\otimes\Theta(g)^{*,{\rm op}}
 \quad\hbox{in }\U(A\otimes_{\max}A^{\rm op}).
\]
On $L^2(A,\tau)$ its left--right action is conjugation by $\Theta(g)$.
The Kazhdan projection for $L$ therefore projects onto the fixed vectors
of this conjugation action.  The one-sided compressor calculation gives
the same projection order as above, and stable finiteness of the maximal
tensor product turns it into equality.  Thus the fixed space is invariant
under $G_{\rm comp}(L)$.  Since $\Theta(z)$ is fixed whenever
$z\in C_H(L)$, so is $\Theta(gzg^{-1})$ for every
$g\in G_{\rm comp}(L)$.  It commutes in $L^2(A,\tau)$ with every
$\Theta(\ell)$, $\ell\in L$; faithfulness of $\tau$ promotes this
$L^2$ equality to equality in $A$.  Hence all generators of
$\mathfrak D(H,L)$ vanish under $\Theta$.

If $A$ is nuclear, maximal and minimal tensor products agree.  The
product trace on $A\otimes_{\min}A^{\rm op}$ is faithful, so this tensor
product is stably finite.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[higher Kazhdan sectors]\label{rem:highersectors}
The preceding arguments use the isolated trivial representation of
$\Lambda$.  A plausible higher-sector extension replaces it by a
finite-dimensional representation $\rho$ with
$\rho\circ\alpha\simeq\rho$, where $\alpha$ is the compression
endomorphism.  A tuple of lamp vectors transforming as $\rho$ should be
pinned by the corresponding isotypic Kazhdan projection, producing
vanishing covariance defects rather than commutators.  Brown's
finite-dimensional-sector Kazhdan projections \cite{BrownKazhdan} suggest this formulation,
but we do not claim the extension as a theorem here: the covariance data
and the passage from a one-sided sector inclusion to its precise defect
relations still have to be written out.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:criterioncontext}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(2) is the instance $H=E$, $\Lambda=\Gamma$,
$\iota$ the canonical homomorphism, and $F=\{1,w\}$; the membership
$w\in N_{\mathrm{comp}}$ is the identity $w=[d,\iota(a)]^2$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:square} below.  The property (T) hypothesis cannot
simply be deleted: Theorem~\ref{thm:C} realizes the pattern over the

As with Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, this is not a claim that every possible
proof must proceed through property (T); a substitute furnishing the
uniform fixed-space projection is what the argument consumes.
\end{remark}

For the remainder of this section and in Section~\ref{sec:proofA},
fix a countable group $H$ with a marked Kazhdan pattern
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$ and a finite normal subgroup
$F\trianglelefteq H$ with $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$, and suppose
given a corona representation $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ whose
restriction to $F$ is nontrivial; the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} will derive a contradiction.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} there is a corona representation
$\Theta_-\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q')$,
$\mathcal Q'=\mathcal Q((r_n)_{n\in J'})$, with
$\sum_{f\in F}\Theta_-(f)=0$.  Using Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}(2), choose
for every $g\in H$ a sequence of unitaries
$U_{g,n}\in M_{r_n}(\C)$ lifting $\Theta_-(g)$, with $U_{1,n}=1$.

Let $K_n=L^2\bigl(M_{r_n}(\C),\tr_{r_n}\bigr)$, a Hilbert space of
dimension $r_n^2$ whose bounded operators $B(K_n)$ form the matrix
algebra $M_{r_n^2}(\C)$, and let
\[
  \mathcal B\;=\;\prod_{n\in J'}B(K_n)\Big/\bigoplus_{n\in J'}B(K_n),
\]
again a norm matrix corona.  For a unitary $U\in M_{r_n}(\C)$ let
$\Ad U\in\U(B(K_n))$ denote $x\mapsto UxU^*$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:beta}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For unitaries $U,V\in M_r(\C)$ and every $x\in M_r(\C)$,
    \[
      \hsnorm{UxU^*-VxV^*}\le2\opnorm{U-V}\,\hsnorm{x},
    \]
    hence $\opnorm{\Ad U-\Ad V}\le2\opnorm{U-V}$ as operators on the
    Hilbert space $K_n$ (and therefore as elements of $B(K_n)$).
  \item The class $\beta(g)$ of the sequence $(\Ad U_{g,n})_{n\in J'}$
    in $\mathcal B$ defines a group homomorphism
    $\beta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal B)$, independent of the choice of
    unitary lifts.
  \item $\beta$ extends to a unital $*$-homomorphism
    $\tilde\beta\colon\Cmax(H)\to\mathcal B$ with
    $\tilde\beta(u_g)=\beta(g)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Since
$UxU^*-VxV^*=(U-V)xU^*+Vx(U-V)^*$, the ideal property of the normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \hsnorm{UxU^*-VxV^*}
 &\le \hsnorm{(U-V)xU^*}+\hsnorm{Vx(U-V)^*}\\
 &\le 2\opnorm{U-V}\,\hsnorm{x}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over $\hsnorm{x}=1$ proves the asserted operator-norm
bound on $K_n$.

(2) If $(U_{g,n})$ and $(U'_{g,n})$ both lift $\Theta_-(g)$ then
$\opnorm{U_{g,n}-U'_{g,n}}\to0$, so by (1) the two sequences of adjoint
maps agree in $\mathcal B$; this is independence of lifts.  For
multiplicativity, $U_{g,n}U_{h,n}$ and $U_{gh,n}$ both lift
$\Theta_-(gh)$, so $\opnorm{U_{g,n}U_{h,n}-U_{gh,n}}\to0$ and, by (1)
again, $\beta(g)\beta(h)=\beta(gh)$.  Finally $\beta(1)=1$ since
$U_{1,n}=1$, so each $\beta(g)$ is a unitary with inverse
$\beta(g^{-1})$.

(3) This is precisely the universal property of the maximal group
$C^*$-algebra: the homomorphism $\beta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal B)$ integrates
uniquely to a unital $*$-homomorphism
$\tilde\beta\colon\Cmax(H)\to\mathcal B$ satisfying
$\tilde\beta(u_g)=\beta(g)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[order identity]\label{lem:order}
Let $H_1$ be a countable group, $\Lambda_1$ a countable group with
property (T), $\iota_1\colon\Lambda_1\to H_1$ a homomorphism, and
$t_1\in H_1$ an element with
$t_1\,\iota_1(\lambda)\,t_1^{-1}\in\iota_1(\Lambda_1)$ for every
$\lambda\in\Lambda_1$; write
$\bar p=(\iota_1)_*(p_{\Lambda_1})\in\Cmax(H_1)$.  Then, in
$\Cmax(H_1)$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $u_{\iota_1(\lambda)}\,\bar p=\bar p$ for all
    $\lambda\in\Lambda_1$;
  \item $\bar p\,\bigl(u_{t_1}\,\bar p\,u_{t_1}^*\bigr)=\bar p$; that
    is, $\bar p\le u_{t_1}\bar pu_{t_1}^*$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, for the standing pattern, in $\mathcal B$:
$\beta(\iota(\lambda))P=P$ for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$, and
$P\le\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) is the image under $(\iota_1)_*$ of the identity
$u_\lambda\,p_{\Lambda_1}=p_{\Lambda_1}$ of Fact~\ref{fact:kazhdan},
since $(\iota_1)_*(u_\lambda)=u_{\iota_1(\lambda)}$.

For (2), both sides may be evaluated in an arbitrary unitary
representation $\pi$ of $H_1$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, since
$\Cmax(H_1)$ admits a faithful one.  The composition
$\pi\circ(\iota_1)_*$ is the integrated form of the unitary
representation $\pi\circ\iota_1$ of $\Lambda_1$ (both are
$*$-homomorphisms extending the same unitary representation), so by
Fact~\ref{fact:kazhdan}, $\pi(\bar p)$ is the orthogonal projection
onto
\[
  \Fix(\pi\circ\iota_1)=\{\xi\in\mathcal H:
  \pi(\iota_1(\lambda))\xi=\xi\ \text{for all }\lambda\in\Lambda_1\},
\]
and, since
$\pi(u_{t_1}\bar pu_{t_1}^*)=\pi(t_1)\,\pi(\bar p)\,\pi(t_1)^*$, the
left-hand side is the orthogonal projection onto
$\pi(t_1)\Fix(\pi\circ\iota_1)$.  We claim
$\Fix(\pi\circ\iota_1)\subseteq\pi(t_1)\Fix(\pi\circ\iota_1)$.  Let
$\xi\in\Fix(\pi\circ\iota_1)$ and put $\eta=\pi(t_1)^{-1}\xi$.  For
$\lambda\in\Lambda_1$ choose $\lambda'\in\Lambda_1$ with
$t_1\,\iota_1(\lambda)\,t_1^{-1}=\iota_1(\lambda')$; then
\[
  \pi(\iota_1(\lambda))\eta
  =\pi(t_1)^{-1}\pi(t_1\,\iota_1(\lambda)\,t_1^{-1})\xi
  =\pi(t_1)^{-1}\pi(\iota_1(\lambda'))\xi=\pi(t_1)^{-1}\xi=\eta ,
\]
so $\eta\in\Fix(\pi\circ\iota_1)$ and $\xi=\pi(t_1)\eta$.  The
inclusion of ranges gives
$\pi(\bar p)\le\pi(u_{t_1}\bar pu_{t_1}^*)$, i.e.\ (2) in the
representation $\pi$; as $\pi$ was arbitrary and some $\pi$ is
faithful, (2) holds in $\Cmax(H_1)$.  The final statement follows by
applying the $*$-homomorphism $\tilde\beta$ to the instance
$(H_1,\Lambda_1,\iota_1,t_1)=(H,\Lambda,\iota,t)$, for which (M1) is
the required one-sided containment.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[compression equality]\label{lem:pinning}
$P=\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*$ in $\mathcal B$.

\begin{proof}

We now pass from the corona projection $P$ to its literal fixed-space action
on a Hilbert-space ultraproduct.  Fix a free
ultrafilter $\omega$ on the infinite index set $J'$, and let
\[
  K_\omega\;=\;\Bigl\{(\xi_n)_{n\in J'}:\ \xi_n\in K_n,\
  \sup_n\lVert\xi_n\rVert<\infty\Bigr\}\Big/
  \bigl\{(\xi_n):\ \lim_{n\to\omega}\lVert\xi_n\rVert=0\bigr\},
\]
the Hilbert-space ultraproduct with inner product
$\langle[\xi],[\eta]\rangle=\lim_{n\to\omega}\langle\xi_n,\eta_n\rangle$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sigma}
For a bounded sequence $(T_n)_{n\in J'}$ of operators with class
$[T]\in\mathcal B$, the assignment
$\sigma([T])\,[\,(\xi_n)\,]=[\,(T_n\xi_n)\,]$ is a well-defined unital
$*$-homomorphism $\sigma\colon\mathcal B\to B(K_\omega)$.  Moreover
$\sigma(P)$ is the orthogonal projection onto
\[
  \Fix_\omega\;=\;\bigl\{\zeta\in K_\omega:\
  \sigma(\beta(\iota(\lambda)))\zeta=\zeta\ \text{for all}\
  \lambda\in\Lambda\bigr\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $(T_n)$ is a null sequence in operator norm, then
\[
  \lVert T_n\xi_n\rVert
  \le \opnorm{T_n}\sup_n\lVert\xi_n\rVert\longrightarrow0 .
\]
In particular this holds along $\omega$, so $\sigma$ is well defined on
$\mathcal B$, and it is clearly a unital $*$-homomorphism.  The map
\[
  \sigma\circ\tilde\beta\circ\iota_*\colon
  \Cmax(\Lambda)\longrightarrow B(K_\omega)
\]
is a $*$-representation on a Hilbert space extending the unitary representation
$\lambda\mapsto\sigma(\beta(\iota(\lambda)))$; by uniqueness of
integrated forms and Fact~\ref{fact:kazhdan},
\[
  \sigma(P)=\sigma\bigl(\tilde\beta(\iota_*(p_\Lambda))\bigr)
\]
is the orthogonal projection onto the fixed space of that
representation, which is $\Fix_\omega$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the criterion and of Theorem \ref{thm:A}}\label{sec:proofA}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}]
Suppose, for contradiction, that some corona representation
$\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ is nontrivial on $F$, and adopt all
notation fixed in Section~\ref{sec:conj}: the corner representation
$\Theta_-$ with $\sum_{f\in F}\Theta_-(f)=0$, the unitary lifts
$U_{g,n}$, the corona $\mathcal B$, the homomorphism $\beta$, the
projection $P$, the ultraproduct $K_\omega$ and the representation
$\sigma$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: the lamp vector is fixed.}
Each $U_{c,n}$ is a unitary matrix, hence a vector in $K_n$ of norm
$\hsnorm{U_{c,n}}=1$; let $\xi_c=[(U_{c,n})]\in K_\omega$, a unit
vector.  For $\lambda\in\Lambda$ we have
$\iota(\lambda)\,c\,\iota(\lambda)^{-1}=c$ in $H$ by (M2), so
$U_{\iota(\lambda),n}U_{c,n}U_{\iota(\lambda),n}^*$ and $U_{c,n}$
both lift $\Theta_-(c)$, whence
$\opnorm{U_{\iota(\lambda),n}U_{c,n}U_{\iota(\lambda),n}^*-U_{c,n}}\to0$
and a fortiori the $K_n$-norms tend to $0$.  Therefore
$\sigma(\beta(\iota(\lambda)))\xi_c=\xi_c$ for every
$\lambda\in\Lambda$, i.e.\ $\xi_c\in\Fix_\omega$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma}, $\sigma(P)\xi_c=\xi_c$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: the translated lamp vector is fixed.}
Let $\xi_d:=\sigma(\beta(t))\xi_c=[(D_n)]$, where
$D_n:=U_{t,n}U_{c,n}U_{t,n}^*$, an operator-norm lift of
$\Theta_-(d)$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:pinning} and Step 1,
\[
  \sigma(P)\,\xi_d
  =\sigma\bigl(\beta(t)P\beta(t)^*\bigr)\,\sigma(\beta(t))\,\xi_c
  =\sigma(\beta(t))\,\sigma(P)\,\xi_c
  =\xi_d ,
\]
so $\xi_d\in\Fix_\omega$: for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:afix}
  \lim_{n\to\omega}\
  \hsnorm{\,U_{\iota(\lambda),n}D_nU_{\iota(\lambda),n}^*-D_n\,}=0 .
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 3: every defect commutator collapses in
Hilbert--Schmidt norm.}
For $\lambda\in\Lambda$ put
\[
  X_{\lambda,n}
  :=D_n\,U_{\iota(\lambda),n}\,D_n^{-1}\,U_{\iota(\lambda),n}^{-1},
\]
a unitary in $M_{r_n}(\C)$ and an operator-norm lift of
$\Theta_-\bigl([\,d,\iota(\lambda)\,]\bigr)$.  Two applications of
unitary invariance of $\hsnorm\cdot$ give
\[
  \hsnorm{X_{\lambda,n}-1}
  =\hsnorm{D_nU_{\iota(\lambda),n}-U_{\iota(\lambda),n}D_n}
  =\hsnorm{\,D_n-U_{\iota(\lambda),n}D_nU_{\iota(\lambda),n}^{-1}\,},
\]
so by \eqref{eq:afix},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
  \lim_{n\to\omega}\ \hsnorm{\,X_{\lambda,n}-1\,}\;=\;0
  \qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in\Lambda .
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 4: the collapse propagates to the
compression-defect subgroup.}
Let $f\in F$.  Since $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$, there are
finitely many (possibly zero) elements $h_1,\dots,h_L\in H$,
$\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_L\in\Lambda$ and signs
$\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_L\in\{\pm1\}$ with
\[
  f\;=\;\prod_{i=1}^{L}
  h_i\,[\,d,\iota(\lambda_i)\,]^{\varepsilon_i}\,h_i^{-1} .
\]
Then
\[
  V_{f,n}\;:=\;\prod_{i=1}^{L}
  U_{h_i,n}\,X_{\lambda_i,n}^{\varepsilon_i}\,U_{h_i,n}^{*}
\]
is an operator-norm lift of $\Theta_-(f)$, and the telescoping
estimate $\hsnorm{AB-1}\le\hsnorm{A-1}+\hsnorm{B-1}$ for unitaries
$A,B$, together with unitary invariance
($\hsnorm{UXU^*-1}=\hsnorm{X-1}$ and
$\hsnorm{X^{-1}-1}=\hsnorm{X-1}$), gives
\[
  \hsnorm{V_{f,n}-1}\;\le\;\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hsnorm{X_{\lambda_i,n}-1}
  \;\xrightarrow[n\to\omega]{}\;0
\]
by \eqref{eq:collapse}.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 5: the corner average forbids the
collapse.}
Each $V_{f,n}$ lifts $\Theta_-(f)$, so the finite sum
$\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}$ is an operator-norm lift of
$\sum_{f\in F}\Theta_-(f)=0$:
\[
  \opnorm{\,\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}\,}\;\longrightarrow\;0
  \qquad(n\to\infty\ \text{in}\ J'),
\]
hence also along $\omega$.  On the other hand, by Step 4,
\[
  \hsnorm{\,\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}\;-\;|F|\,}
  \;\le\;\sum_{f\in F}\hsnorm{V_{f,n}-1}
  \;\xrightarrow[n\to\omega]{}\;0 ,
\]
so, using $\hsnorm{x}\le\opnorm{x}$,
\[
  |F|\;=\;\hsnorm{\,|F|\cdot1\,}
  \;\le\;\hsnorm{\,\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}-|F|\,}
  +\opnorm{\,\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}\,}
  \;\xrightarrow[n\to\omega]{}\;0 ,
\]
which is absurd since $|F|\ge1$.  This contradiction proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the marked word is a defect square]\label{lem:square}
Let $H_1$ be a group and $d,a_1\in H_1$ with $d^2=1$.  Then
\[
  \bigl[\,d,\;a_1\,d\,a_1^{-1}\bigr]\;=\;[\,d,a_1\,]^{\,2} .
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/CompressionDefectSquare}{GroupApproximation.commutator_conjugate_eq_commutator_sq_of_sq_eq_one}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $b=a_1da_1^{-1}$, so $b^2=a_1d^2a_1^{-1}=1$; thus $b^{-1}=b$ and
$d^{-1}=d$.  Then $[d,a_1]=da_1d^{-1}a_1^{-1}=d\,(a_1da_1^{-1})=db$,
and
\[
  [d,a_1]^2=(db)^2=db\,db=d\,b\,d^{-1}b^{-1}=[d,b] . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Part (1) is Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  For part (2), let
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to E$ be the canonical homomorphism and
$a=v_1=(e_1,1)$.  The tuple $(\Gamma,\iota,t,c)$ is a marked Kazhdan
pattern in $E$: $\Gamma$ has property (T) by Fact~\ref{fact:T}; (M1)
holds because $t\,\iota(\gamma)\,t^{-1}=\iota(\alpha(\gamma))$ for
every $\gamma\in\Gamma$ (both sides are homomorphisms in $\gamma$,
and \eqref{eq:E} gives the identity on the six base generators);
(M2) holds because $[c,g]=1$ in \eqref{eq:E} for the six base
generators $g$.  In $E$ we have $d^2=(tct^{-1})^2=tc^2t^{-1}=1$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:square} with $a_1=\iota(a)$ gives
\[
  w=\bigl[\,d,\;\iota(a)\,d\,\iota(a)^{-1}\bigr]
  =[\,d,\iota(a)\,]^{\,2}\;\in\;N_{\mathrm{comp}} ,
\]
since $[\,d,\iota(a)\,]$ is one of the defining generators of
$N_{\mathrm{comp}}$.  The subgroup $F=\{1,w\}=\langle w\rangle$ is
finite and normal: centrality and $w^2=1$ are defining relations, while
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} gives $w\neq1$.  Moreover
$F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}
therefore gives $\Theta(w)=1$ for every corona representation
$\Theta$ of $E$, which is part (2).

\medskip

For the final assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}: an MF embedding of
$E$ would be an injective corona representation, which is impossible
because every corona representation sends the nontrivial element $w$
to $1$.  If $\Cred(E)$ were an MF algebra, a unital $C^*$-embedding
$\Cred(E)\hookrightarrow\mathcal Q$ composed with the canonical map
  $E\to\U(\Cred(E))$, would exhibit an injective corona representation of
  $E$: the map $g\mapsto\lambda_g$ is injective, although the quotient map
  $\Cmax(E)\to\Cred(E)$ need not be faithful.  The canonical map
  $E\to\U(\Cmax(E))$ is also injective, since its composition with the
  regular representation is $g\mapsto\lambda_g$; this treats $\Cmax(E)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:radical}
The proof shows more than non-injectivity of each individual
$\Theta$: the fixed element $w$ lies in the kernel of \emph{every}
corona representation of $E$, so every corona representation factors
through $E/\langle w\rangle$ (note $\langle w\rangle=\{1,w\}$ is
central, hence normal).  In the language of approximation radicals, $w$
is a nontrivial element of the ``MF radical'' of $E$.
This terminology is meant in analogy with Slofstra's earlier quotients
$G^{\mathrm{fin}}$ and $G^{\mathrm{fa}}$, obtained by killing elements
invisible to exact and approximate finite-dimensional representations,
respectively \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} the same holds in every countable
group with a marked Kazhdan pattern: every finite normal subgroup of
the compression-defect subgroup lies in the MF radical.
Section~\ref{sec:consequences} develops the radical systematically;
we do not know whether $E/\langle w\rangle$ is MF, i.e.\ whether the
MF radical of $E$ is exactly $\{1,w\}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
No lower bound on the corner ranks $r_n$, or on their density in
$d_n$, is used anywhere: Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} renormalizes the trace
so that all subsequent estimates are relative to the corner.  This is
what makes the operator-norm setting insensitive to small relative ranks,
which block the same argument in Hilbert--Schmidt metrics
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The MF radical}

\begin{definition}\label{def:radical}
For a countable group $G_1$, the \emph{MF radical} is
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)
  \;=\;\bigcap\,\bigl\{\ker\Theta:\ \Theta\ \text{a corona
  representation of }G_1\bigr\},
\]
a normal subgroup of $G_1$.  This is the operator-norm analogue of
the invisible subgroups of \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}
and of the approximation kernels studied in \cite{NST}.
The formal companion defines the corresponding intersection over
unitary-sequence corona presentations.  Its predicate
\texttt{NormMFInvisible} is a different, a priori stronger formulation: it
quantifies over every operator-norm matrix ultraproduct and every ultrafilter,
including principal ultrafilters.  These notions are not definitionally
identified.  For countable groups the companion proves the bridge theorem
equating the resulting subgroups and separately characterizes operator-MF
groups by triviality of that residual.
Passing from that presentation to the displayed C-star quotient uses the
polar-correction identification recorded above, which is not itself a Lean
declaration.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFCoronaRadical}{GroupApproximation.coronaMFResidual_eq_normMFResidual}
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFCoronaRadical}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_iff_coronaMFResidual_eq_bot}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[portability]\label{lem:portable}
For every homomorphism $\varphi\colon G_1\to G_2$ of countable
groups,
\[
  \varphi\bigl(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)\bigr)
  \;\subseteq\;\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2):
\]
the MF radical is functorial.  In particular, if
$\varphi\colon E\to G_2$ satisfies $\varphi(w)\neq1$, then $G_2$ is
not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFCoronaRadical}{GroupApproximation.map_coronaMFResidual_le}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $x\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$ and $\Theta$ is a
corona representation of $G_2$, then $\Theta\circ\varphi$ is a corona
representation of $G_1$, so $\Theta(\varphi(x))=1$.  For the second
statement: $w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, so
$1\neq\varphi(w)\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2)$, and an
injective corona representation of $G_2$ would kill $\varphi(w)$, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the largest MF quotient]\label{prop:univquot}
Let $G_1$ be a countable group and
$R=\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the kernel of a single corona representation of $G_1$.
\item $G_1/R$ is MF.
\item Every homomorphism from $G_1$ to an MF group factors through
$G_1/R$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $G_1$ is MF if and only if $R=\{1\}$.  The statement is
presumably folklore once the radical is defined; we include a proof
for completeness and claim no novelty for it.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFCoronaRadical}{GroupApproximation.exists_normMatrixCoronaRepresentation_ker_eq_coronaMFResidual}
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFCoronaRadical}{GroupApproximation.coronaMFQuotient_isOperatorMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFCoronaRadical}{GroupApproximation.existsUnique_coronaMFQuotient_factorization_to_isOperatorMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFCoronaRadical}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_iff_coronaMFResidual_eq_bot}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(3) If $\varphi\colon G_1\to M$ with $M$ MF and
$j\colon M\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ is an injective corona representation,
then $j\circ\varphi$ is a corona representation of $G_1$, so
$j(\varphi(R))=1$ and, by injectivity of $j$, $\varphi(R)=1$.

(1) and (2).  If $R=G_1$, the trivial representation has kernel $R$
and $G_1/R$ is trivial, hence MF; so assume $R\neq G_1$ and let
$(g_i)_{i\ge1}$ list, possibly with repetitions, elements of
$G_1\setminus R$ meeting every nontrivial coset of $R$.  For each $i$
choose a corona representation
$\Theta_i\colon G_1\to\U(\mathcal Q_i)$,
$\mathcal Q_i=\mathcal Q\bigl((d^{(i)}_n)_{n\in J_i}\bigr)$, with
$\Theta_i(g_i)\neq1$; put
$\varepsilon_i=\tfrac12\opnorm{\Theta_i(g_i)-1}>0$, and choose
unitary lifts $\bigl(U^{(i)}_{g,n}\bigr)_{n\in J_i}$ of $\Theta_i(g)$
for every $g\in G_1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}), with $U^{(i)}_{1,n}=1$.
Recall that the quotient norm in a corona is the limit superior of
the coordinate norms: subtracting a null sequence does not change the
limit superior, and truncating finitely many coordinates realizes it.

Enumerate $G_1=\{h_1,h_2,\dots\}$ and fix a stage $k\ge1$.  For each
$i\le k$: the finitely many products
$U^{(i)}_{g,n}U^{(i)}_{h,n}$ and $U^{(i)}_{gh,n}$ with
$g,h\in\{h_1,\dots,h_k\}$ lift the same corona elements, so there is
$n_0$ with all the corresponding defects at most $1/k$ for
$n\ge n_0$; and since
$\limsup_{n\in J_i}\opnorm{U^{(i)}_{g_i,n}-1}=2\varepsilon_i$, there
are infinitely many $n\in J_i$ with
$\opnorm{U^{(i)}_{g_i,n}-1}\ge\varepsilon_i$.  Choose
$n_i(k)\in J_i$ satisfying both conditions and set
\[
  \Phi_k(g)\;=\;\bigoplus_{i\le k}U^{(i)}_{g,\,n_i(k)}
  \;\in\;\U\bigl(M_{D_k}(\C)\bigr),\qquad
  D_k=\sum_{i\le k}d^{(i)}_{n_i(k)} .
\]
Define $\Theta_\infty(g)$ as the class of $(\Phi_k(g))_{k\in\N}$ in
$\mathcal Q\bigl((D_k)_{k\in\N}\bigr)$.  For fixed $g,h\in G_1$, both
lie in $\{h_1,\dots,h_k\}$ for all large $k$, and the operator norm
of a block sum is the maximum of the block norms, so
$\opnorm{\Phi_k(g)\Phi_k(h)-\Phi_k(gh)}\le1/k\to0$; together with
$\Phi_k(1)=1$ this makes $\Theta_\infty$ a corona representation of
$G_1$.  Consequently $\Theta_\infty(R)=1$.  Conversely, if
$g\notin R$, pick $i$ with $g\in g_iR$; then
$\Theta_\infty(g)=\Theta_\infty(g_i)$, and for every $k\ge i$,
$\opnorm{\Phi_k(g_i)-1}\ge\opnorm{U^{(i)}_{g_i,n_i(k)}-1}
\ge\varepsilon_i$, so $\opnorm{\Theta_\infty(g_i)-1}\ge\varepsilon_i$
and $\Theta_\infty(g)\neq1$.  Hence $\ker\Theta_\infty=R$, proving
(1), and $\Theta_\infty$ descends to an injective corona
representation of $G_1/R$, proving (2).  For the final equivalence:
if $G_1$ is MF, an injective corona representation forces
$R=\{1\}$; if $R=\{1\}$, then (2) says $G_1=G_1/R$ is MF.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[exact radical from a candidate quotient]\label{cor:exactradical}
Let $N\trianglelefteq G_1$ satisfy
$N\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.  Every corona
representation of $G_1$ factors uniquely through $G_1/N$.  If in
addition $G_1/N$ is MF, then
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)=N.
\]
Thus proving that a concrete defect subgroup is invisible and that its
quotient is MF computes both the entire MF radical and the largest MF
quotient.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFResidualExactQuotient}{GroupApproximation.existsUnique_quotient_factorization_to_normMatrixCorona}
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFResidualExactQuotient}{GroupApproximation.coronaMFResidual_eq_of_le_and_quotient_isOperatorMF}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The containment $N\le\ker\Theta$ gives the unique quotient
factorization for every corona representation $\Theta$.  For the
reverse containment in the displayed equality, compose the quotient
map $G_1\to G_1/N$ with an injective corona representation of $G_1/N$:
its kernel is exactly $N$, while the MF radical lies in the kernel of
every corona representation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Targets beyond coronas}

\begin{corollary}[subalgebras of coronas]\label{cor:subalg}
Let $H$, $(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$ and $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$ be
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.  Let $A$ be a nonzero
$C^*$-subalgebra of a norm matrix corona $\mathcal Q$ whose unit
$p:=1_A$ is not necessarily the unit of $\mathcal Q$, and let
$\Theta\colon H\to\U(A)$ be a group homomorphism.  Then
$\Theta(f)=1_A$ for every $f\in F$.  The same conclusion holds when
$A$ is a unital separable MF algebra --- for instance a unital
separable quasidiagonal algebra (Fact~\ref{fact:qdcorona}), in
particular an AF algebra, or $\Cred(F_k)$ for a free group $F_k$
\cite[Corollary~8.4]{HT}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every unitary $u$ of $A$ satisfies $u=pup$ and $u^*u=uu^*=p$, so
$\U(A)\subseteq\U(p\mathcal Qp)$, and $p\neq0$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:cornercorona} there is a unital $*$-isomorphism of
$p\mathcal Qp$ onto a norm matrix corona carrying $p$ to the unit.
Composing, $\Theta$ becomes a corona representation of $H$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} gives $\Theta(f)=1_A$ for $f\in F$.  If
$A$ is a unital separable MF algebra, fix a $*$-monomorphism
$\psi\colon A\to\mathcal Q_1$ into a norm matrix corona, unital or
not \cite{BK}; then $\psi(A)$ is a $C^*$-subalgebra of
$\mathcal Q_1$ with unit $\psi(1_A)$, and applying the first part to
$\psi\circ\Theta$ gives $\psi(\Theta(f))=\psi(1_A)$, whence
$\Theta(f)=1_A$ by injectivity.  Unital separable quasidiagonal
algebras embed unitally into coronas by Fact~\ref{fact:qdcorona}, AF
algebras are quasidiagonal, and $\Cred(F_k)$ is MF for every
$k\in\N\cup\{\infty\}$ by \cite[Corollary~8.4]{HT}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nofaithful}
The group $E$ admits no injective homomorphism into the unitary
group of any $C^*$-subalgebra of any norm matrix corona --- in
particular, none into $\U(A)$ for $A$ a unital separable MF algebra,
a unital separable quasidiagonal algebra, or $\Cred(F_k)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, the pattern
$(\Gamma,\iota,t,c)$ and $F=\{1,w\}$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Corollary~\ref{cor:subalg} in $H=E$; hence any such homomorphism
kills $w$, while $w\neq1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stable finiteness does not imply MF}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:faithfultrace}
Let $G_1$ be a countable discrete group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The canonical tracial state $\tau$ on $\Cred(G_1)$ is
faithful.
\item Every unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ with a faithful tracial state
is stably finite: for every $k\ge1$, every isometry in $M_k(A)$ is a
unitary.
\leanverified{Analysis/FaithfulTracialMatrix}{GroupApproximation.FaithfulTracialState.matrix_mul_star_eq_one_of_star_mul_eq_one}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Realize $\Cred(G_1)\subseteq B(\ell^2G_1)$ as the norm closure
of the span of the left translation unitaries $\lambda_g$, and let
$\rho_g$ be the right translation unitaries,
$(\rho_g\xi)(h)=\xi(hg)$; each $\rho_g$ commutes with each
$\lambda_{g'}$, hence with every element of $\Cred(G_1)$.  The
canonical trace is $\tau(x)=\langle x\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle$.  If
$\tau(x^*x)=0$ then $\lVert x\delta_e\rVert=0$, so $x\delta_e=0$,
and then
$x\delta_g=x\rho_{g^{-1}}\delta_e=\rho_{g^{-1}}x\delta_e=0$ for
every $g\in G_1$.  The vectors $\delta_g$ are total in
$\ell^2G_1$, so $x=0$.

(2) On $M_k(A)$ the functional
$\tau_k\bigl([x_{ij}]\bigr)=\tfrac1k\sum_i\tau(x_{ii})$ is a state
with the trace property
$\tau_k(xy)=\tfrac1k\sum_{i,j}\tau(x_{ij}y_{ji})=\tau_k(yx)$, and it
is faithful: the $i$-th diagonal entry of $x^*x$ is
$\sum_jx_{ji}^*x_{ji}$, so $\tau_k(x^*x)=0$ forces
$\tau(x_{ji}^*x_{ji})=0$, hence $x_{ji}=0$, for all $i,j$.  If
$v\in M_k(A)$ satisfies $v^*v=1$, then $y:=1-vv^*$ is a projection
with
$\tau_k(y)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(vv^*)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(v^*v)=0$; since
$y=y^*y$, faithfulness gives $y=0$, i.e.\ $vv^*=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:D}]
$\Cred(E)$ is unital and separable since $E$ is countable; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace} it carries a faithful tracial state
and is stably finite.  It is not an MF algebra by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:nonnuclear}
Blackadar and Kirchberg proved that MF algebras are stably finite
\cite{BK}.  The general converse was already refuted as a consequence of
the negative solution to the Connes embedding problem
\cite[Corollary~5.2]{GoldbringMF}; Theorem~\ref{thm:D} instead realizes
that failure as the reduced group $C^*$-algebra of an explicit finitely
presented group, with a faithful trace.  The example is not nuclear:
$\Gamma$ embeds into $E$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:iotainj}); the infinite group $\Gamma$ has
property (T) and is therefore not amenable, since amenable groups
with property (T) are compact, hence finite when discrete
\cite[Theorem~1.1.6]{BHV}; and the reduced $C^*$-algebra of a
nonamenable group is not nuclear \cite[Theorem~2.6.8]{BO}.  The
nuclear territory is untouched: whether every stably finite
separable \emph{nuclear} $C^*$-algebra is quasidiagonal remains
open, and by \cite{TWW} every faithful trace on a separable nuclear
$C^*$-algebra satisfying the UCT is quasidiagonal.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The ambient HNN extension}

\begin{lemma}[residually finite groups are MF]\label{lem:rfmf}
Every countable residually finite group is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_of_residuallyFinite}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $G_1$ be countable and residually finite; if $G_1$ is trivial
the statement is trivially true, so enumerate its nontrivial elements
$x_1,x_2,\dots$ (with repetitions if $G_1$ is finite).  For each $k$
choose a finite-index normal subgroup $N_k\trianglelefteq G_1$ with
$x_k\notin N_k$, put $M_k=N_1\cap\dots\cap N_k$, a finite-index
normal subgroup, and let $\varphi_k\colon G_1\to G_1/M_k$ be the
quotient map onto the finite group $G_1/M_k$.  Let $\pi_k$ be the
left regular representation of $G_1/M_k$ composed with $\varphi_k$,
a finite-dimensional unitary representation of $G_1$.  Define
$\Theta(g)$ as the class of $(\pi_k(g))_k$ in
$\mathcal Q\bigl((|G_1/M_k|)_k\bigr)$; each $\pi_k$ is an exact
representation, so $\Theta$ is a corona representation.  For
$g=x_i$ and $k\ge i$ the element $\varphi_k(x_i)$ is nontrivial, so
the permutation matrix $\pi_k(x_i)$ moves some standard basis
vector $\delta$, giving
$\lVert(\pi_k(x_i)-1)\delta\rVert=\sqrt2$ and
$\opnorm{\pi_k(x_i)-1}\ge\sqrt2$.  Hence
$\opnorm{\Theta(x_i)-1}\ge\sqrt2$ and $\Theta$ is injective.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the ambient group]\label{prop:ambient}
Let $G$ be the ascending HNN extension \eqref{eq:G}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Cmax(G)$ contains an explicitly described nonunitary
isometry.  In particular $\Cmax(G)$ is not finite, a fortiori not
stably finite, and not an MF algebra.
\item $G$ is residually finite, hence MF.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Write $\iota_G\colon\Gamma\to G$ for the canonical homomorphism
and $\bar p=(\iota_G)_*(p_\Gamma)\in\Cmax(G)$ for the image of the
Kazhdan projection.  The data $(G,\Gamma,\iota_G,t)$ satisfy the
hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:order}, because
$t\,\iota_G(\gamma)\,t^{-1}=\iota_G(\alpha(\gamma))$; hence
$\bar p\le Q_1:=u_t\,\bar p\,u_t^*$.  Put
\[
  s\;=\;\bar p\,u_t^*\;+\;(1-Q_1) .
\]
From $\bar p\le Q_1$ we get $\bar pQ_1=Q_1\bar p=\bar p$, hence
$(1-Q_1)\bar p=0$ and $\bar p(1-Q_1)=0$; also
$Q_1u_t\bar p=u_t\bar p\,u_t^*u_t\,\bar p=u_t\bar p$, so
$(1-Q_1)u_t\bar p=0$ and, taking adjoints,
$\bar pu_t^*(1-Q_1)=0$.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
  s^*s&=u_t\bar p\,\bar pu_t^*+u_t\bar p(1-Q_1)+(1-Q_1)\bar pu_t^*
  +(1-Q_1)\\
  &=Q_1+(1-Q_1)=1
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
  ss^*&=\bar pu_t^*u_t\bar p+\bar pu_t^*(1-Q_1)+(1-Q_1)u_t\bar p
  +(1-Q_1)\\
  &=\bar p+(1-Q_1)=1-(Q_1-\bar p):
\end{aligned}
\]
$s$ is an isometry with defect $1-ss^*=Q_1-\bar p$.
Thus $s^*s=1$ makes $s$ left-invertible, with left inverse $s^*$,
whereas $ss^*\neq1$ makes $s$ a nonunit.  Equivalently, with
$a=s^*$ and $b=s$ one has $ab=1$ but $ba\neq1$, so
$\Cmax(G)$ is not directly finite.  The purely algebraic
projection-compression implication is checked in Lean; the formal companion
does not model $\Cmax(G)$ itself.
\leanverified{Analysis/ProperIsometryFromCompression}{GroupApproximation.ProperProjectionCompression.star_isometry_mul_eq_one_and_reverse_ne}

It remains to check $Q_1\neq\bar p$.  Let
$H_0=t\,\iota_G(\Gamma)\,t^{-1}\le G$ and let $\pi$ be the
quasi-regular representation of $G$ on $\ell^2(G/H_0)$, with
basepoint $b=H_0\in G/H_0$.  As in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:order}, $\pi(\bar p)$ is the orthogonal projection
onto the $\iota_G(\Gamma)$-invariant vectors, and
$\pi(Q_1)=\pi(t)\pi(\bar p)\pi(t)^*$.  The vector
$\pi(t)^{-1}\delta_b=\delta_{t^{-1}H_0}$ is
$\iota_G(\Gamma)$-invariant: for $\gamma\in\Gamma$,
\[
  \iota_G(\gamma)\,t^{-1}H_0
  =t^{-1}\bigl(t\,\iota_G(\gamma)\,t^{-1}\bigr)H_0=t^{-1}H_0 ,
\]
since $t\,\iota_G(\gamma)\,t^{-1}\in H_0$; hence
$\pi(Q_1)\delta_b=\delta_b$.  On the other hand
$\pi(\iota_G(a))\delta_b=\delta_{aH_0}\neq\delta_b$: if $aH_0=H_0$
then $a\in H_0$, and applying the homomorphism $G\to\bar G$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}, which restricts to the identity on $\Gamma$
and sends $t$ to $D$, would give
$a\in D\Gamma D^{-1}=\alpha(\Gamma)$ inside $\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$,
contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:alpha}.  So $\delta_b$ is not
$\iota_G(\Gamma)$-invariant, i.e.\
$\pi(\bar p)\delta_b\neq\delta_b$ while $\pi(Q_1)\delta_b=\delta_b$;
hence $Q_1\neq\bar p$ and $s$ is not a unitary.

If $\Cmax(G)$ embedded unitally into a norm matrix corona, the image
of $s$ would be an isometry there, hence a unitary by
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}(1), and injectivity would force $ss^*=1$, a
contradiction; so $\Cmax(G)$ is not an MF algebra, and the isometry
$s$ itself witnesses the failure of finiteness.

(2) $G$ is the mapping torus of the injective endomorphism $\alpha$
of the finitely generated linear group $\Gamma\le\GL_4(\Z)$, so $G$
is residually finite by Borisov--Sapir \cite[Theorem~1.6]{BoS}; MF-ness
follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:rfmf}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ambient} separates group-level and
$C^*$-level MF-ness in the direction opposite to
Theorem~\ref{thm:D}: $G$ is an MF group whose maximal group
$C^*$-algebra is not even finite, while $E$ is a non-MF group whose
reduced group $C^*$-algebra is stably finite.  The one-way
implication --- if $\Cmax$ or $\Cred$ of a group is an MF algebra
then the group is MF --- is recorded in \cite{Shulman}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Failure of permanence properties}

\begin{corollary}[quotients]\label{cor:quotclosure}
MF groups are not closed under quotients: the free group $F_8$ is
MF, and $E$ is a quotient of $F_8$.
\ifanonymous\else
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralKazhdanCompression}{GroupApproximation.LiteralKazhdanCompression.literal_free_source_isOperatorMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralKazhdanCompression}{GroupApproximation.LiteralKazhdanCompression.literal_quotientMap_surjective}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralKazhdanCompression}{GroupApproximation.LiteralKazhdanCompression.literal_operatorMF_not_closed_under_quotient_of_hasKazhdanPropertyT}
\fi
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$E$ is generated by eight elements \eqref{eq:E}, hence is a quotient
of $F_8$.  Free groups are residually finite: $F_8$ embeds into
$F_2$ (a subgroup of index $7$ of $F_2$ is free of rank $8$ by
the classical Nielsen--Schreier theorem), $F_2$ embeds into $\SL_2(\Z)$
by Sanov's classical
ping-pong pair
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
residual finiteness passes to subgroups, and $\GL_2(\Z)$ is
residually finite by reduction modulo primes (a nontrivial integer
matrix stays nontrivial modulo any prime exceeding the absolute
values of the entries of its difference from the identity).  So
$F_8$ is MF by Lemma~\ref{lem:rfmf}, while $E$ is not MF by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the witness group is non-MF]\label{prop:W}
Let $W=\ClLamp(\bar X)\rtimes\bar G$ be the witness group of
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  Then $W$ is finitely generated, the
homomorphism $E\to W$ of that proof is surjective, and the central
sign $z=-1\in\ClLamp(\bar X)$ satisfies
$z\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(W)\setminus\{1\}$.  In
particular $W$ is not MF.
\ifanonymous\else
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFLinearWitness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFLinearWitness.witnessHom_mark}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFLinearWitness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFLinearWitness.witnessHom_surjective}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFLinearWitness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFLinearWitness.witnessGroup_finitelyGenerated}

\fi
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The image of the homomorphism $E\to W$ contains the six base
generators of $\Gamma$, hence $\Gamma$; it contains $D$; so it
contains $\bar G=\langle\Gamma,D\rangle$.  It contains $c_o$, hence
$c_{g\Gamma}=g\,c_o\,g^{-1}$ for every $g\in\bar G$, which is all of
$\{c_x:x\in\bar X\}$ since $\bar G$ acts transitively on
$\bar X=\bar G/\Gamma$.  Finally
$(c_{D\Gamma}c_{aD\Gamma})^2=-c_{D\Gamma}^2c_{aD\Gamma}^2=-1=z$
because the two sites are distinct (Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}), so the
image contains $z$ and therefore all of
$\ClLamp(\bar X)=\langle\pm1,\,c_x:x\in\bar X\rangle$: the map is
surjective, and $W$ is generated by the images of the eight
generators of $E$.  The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}
sends $w$ to $z$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:portable} gives
$z\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(W)$, and $z\neq1$ in $W$ by
Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.  A group whose MF radical is
nontrivial admits no injective corona representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[extensions]\label{cor:extclosure}
MF groups are not closed under extensions, even split ones: in the
split exact sequence
\[
  1\longrightarrow\ClLamp(\bar X)\longrightarrow W
  \longrightarrow\bar G\longrightarrow1,
\]
the kernel is locally finite, hence amenable and MF; the quotient
$\bar G\le\GL_4(\Z[1/2])$ is a finitely generated linear group,
hence residually finite by Mal'cev's theorem \cite{Ma} and MF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rfmf}; and $W$ is not MF
(Proposition~\ref{prop:W}).  In particular there is a finitely
generated non-MF group that is (locally finite)-by-(residually
finite).  This contrasts with the positive permanence theory for MF
groups in \cite{Shulman}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Exactness and splitting hold by construction of the semidirect
product.  Every finite subset of $\ClLamp(\bar X)$ lies in the
subgroup generated by $\pm1$ and finitely many $c_x$, a finite
$2$-group, as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}; so
$\ClLamp(\bar X)$ is locally finite, hence amenable, hence MF by
Facts~\ref{fact:tww} and~\ref{fact:qdcorona}.  The remaining
assertions were proved above.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[a simple envelope]\label{cor:simple}
There exists a $2$-generator simple group $S$ with
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(S)=S$: every corona representation
of $S$, and every homomorphism from $S$ into any MF group, is
trivial.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Goryushkin's theorem \cite{Go}, the countable group $E$ embeds
into a $2$-generator simple group $S$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:portable}
applied to the embedding, the image of $w$ is a nontrivial element
of $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(S)$.  The radical is a normal
subgroup of the simple group $S$, so
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(S)=S$, i.e.\ every corona
representation of $S$ is trivial.  If $\varphi\colon S\to M$ is a
homomorphism to an MF group and $j$ an injective corona
representation of $M$, then $j\circ\varphi$ is a corona
representation, hence trivial, so $\varphi$ is trivial.  The
simple-envelope theorem is entirely Goryushkin's; what is added here
is the nontrivial portable radical element.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finitely presented Kazhdan envelopes}

We use the following recent external embedding theorem.  Chatterji and
Kassabov prove that, given a finitely generated recursively presented
group $H$ and $1\neq h\in H$, there is an embedding
$j\colon H\hookrightarrow\Lambda$ into a finitely presented
property~(T) group $\Lambda$ which is normally generated by $j(h)$
\cite[Theorem~3]{ChatterjiKassabov}.  The arXiv record
\arxiv{2601.22907v1} was submitted on January~30, 2026; the manuscript
itself is dated August~11, 2026.  We use only that theorem and make no
claim that its construction is part of the present work.

\begin{theorem}[a finitely presented Kazhdan group with full MF radical]
\label{thm:kazhdanfullradical}
There exist a finitely presented property~(T) group $\Lambda$ and an
embedding $j\colon E\hookrightarrow\Lambda$ such that
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(\Lambda)=\Lambda .
\]
Every corona representation of $\Lambda$, and every homomorphism from
$\Lambda$ to an MF group, is trivial.  Moreover every finite-dimensional
linear representation of $\Lambda$, over every field, is trivial, and
$\Lambda$ is perfect.
\ifanonymous\else
\leanconditional{Sofic/NormallyGeneratedMFObstruction}{GroupApproximation.NormallyGeneratedKazhdanEnvelopeData.envelope_normMFResidual_eq_top}
\leanconditional{Sofic/NormallyGeneratedMFObstruction}{GroupApproximation.NormallyGeneratedKazhdanEnvelopeData.hom_eq_one_to_isOperatorMF}
\leanconditional{Sofic/NormallyGeneratedMFObstruction}{GroupApproximation.NormallyGeneratedKazhdanEnvelopeData.allFiniteDimensionalRepresentationsTrivial}
\leanconditional{Sofic/NormallyGeneratedMFObstruction}{GroupApproximation.NormallyGeneratedKazhdanEnvelopeData.envelopeIsPerfect_of_eq_commutator}
\fi
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cite[Theorem~3]{ChatterjiKassabov} to $(E,w)$.  Portability gives
$j(w)\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(\Lambda)$.  The radical is
normal, whereas $j(w)$ normally generates $\Lambda$, so the radical is
all of $\Lambda$.  The statements about corona representations and maps
to MF groups follow from Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}.

Let $\pi\colon\Lambda\to\GL(V)$ with $V$ finite-dimensional over an
arbitrary field.  Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, applied to $\pi\circ j$, gives
$\pi(j(w))=1$; normal generation gives $\pi=1$.  Finally $w$ is a
commutator, hence $j(w)$ lies in the commutator subgroup of $\Lambda$.
That normal subgroup contains a normal generator, so it is all of
$\Lambda$ and $\Lambda$ is perfect.
\end{proof}

In particular $\Lambda$ has no nontrivial finite or residually finite
quotient.  It also has no nontrivial amenable quotient and no nontrivial
finitely generated linear quotient: quotients of the finitely generated
group $\Lambda$ are countable, amenable groups are MF by
Facts~\ref{fact:tww} and~\ref{fact:qdcorona}, and finitely generated
linear groups are residually finite by Mal'cev's theorem \cite{Ma}.
Thus $\Lambda$ is minimally almost periodic.  By the classical
Peter--Weyl theorem \cite{PeterWeyl}, its Bohr compactification is
trivial; this last Peter--Weyl implication is not part of the formal
companion.

\begin{theorem}[a universal Kazhdan group with full MF radical]
\label{thm:universalkazhdanradical}
There exists a finitely presented property~(T) group
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{univ}}$ which contains every finitely presented group
as a subgroup and satisfies
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{univ}})
  =\Lambda_{\mathrm{univ}}.
\]
Every homomorphism from $\Lambda_{\mathrm{univ}}$ to an MF group is
trivial.
\ifanonymous\else
\leanconditional{Sofic/UniversalKazhdanMFObstruction}{GroupApproximation.UniversalNormallyGeneratedKazhdanEnvelopeData.consequence_package}
\fi
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Enumerate all finite group presentations and form the free product of the
groups they present.  The Higman--Neumann--Neumann and Higman embedding
theorems \cite{HNN,Higman} embed this recursively presented countable
group into a finitely presented group $U$.  Thus $U$ contains every
finitely presented group.  Choose $i\colon E\hookrightarrow U$.
Portability and injectivity give
$1\neq i(w)\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(U)$.  Apply
\cite[Theorem~3]{ChatterjiKassabov} to $(U,i(w))$.  Its image in the
resulting finitely presented property~(T) envelope normally generates,
so portability forces the final MF radical to be the entire group.
Universality survives the embedding of $U$, and the last assertion follows
from Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}.
\end{proof}

The directionality is worth emphasizing:
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{univ}}$ contains every finite group and every finitely
presented residually finite, amenable, or linear group, while admitting no
nontrivial quotient in any of those MF classes.

\subsection{The scaling family}

For an integer $m\ge2$ let $E_m$ be the group with the presentation
\eqref{eq:E}, except that the three relations
$tv_it^{-1}=v_i^2$ are replaced by
\[
  tv_it^{-1}=v_i^m\qquad(1\le i\le3);
\]
thus $E=E_2$, while the marked word
$w_m=[\,tct^{-1},\,v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}]$ is unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[scaling family]\label{thm:family}
For every $m\ge2$: the group $E_m$ is finitely presented, $w_m$ is a
nontrivial central involution in $E_m$, and every corona
representation of $E_m$ kills $w_m$.  Consequently $E_m$ is not MF,
neither $\Cmax(E_m)$ nor $\Cred(E_m)$ is an MF algebra, and
$\Cred(E_m)$ is stably finite but not MF.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite presentation is clear from the displayed presentation.

\emph{The pattern.}  Define $\alpha_m\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma$ by
$\alpha_m(v,A)=(mv,A)$.  The computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:alpha}
applies with $2$ replaced by $m$: $\alpha_m$ is an injective
endomorphism with image $m\Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\Z)$, and
$a=(e_1,1)\notin\alpha_m(\Gamma)$ because $e_1\notin m\Z^3$.  On the
six base generators $\alpha_m$ fixes $x,y,z$ and sends $v_i$ to
$v_i^m$, so the relations of $E_m$ assert
$t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha_m(\gamma)$ on generators; hence, with
$\iota_m\colon\Gamma\to E_m$ the canonical homomorphism,
$t\,\iota_m(\gamma)\,t^{-1}=\iota_m(\alpha_m(\gamma))$ for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$, and $c$ commutes with $\iota_m(\Gamma)$: the
tuple $(\Gamma,\iota_m,t,c)$ is a marked Kazhdan pattern in $E_m$
(Fact~\ref{fact:T}).
\leanverified{Monsters/AffineSL3Scaling}{GroupApproximation.AffineSL3Scaling.scaling_package}
As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A},
$d^2=tc^2t^{-1}=1$ in $E_m$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:square} gives
$w_m=[\,d,\iota_m(a)\,]^2\in N_{\mathrm{comp}}$; the subgroup
$\{1,w_m\}$ is finite and normal, so Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}
kills $w_m$ in every corona representation.

\emph{Nontriviality.}  Let
$D_m=\operatorname{diag}(m,m,m,1)\in\GL_4(\Z[1/m])$.  As in
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear},
$D_m\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A&v\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
D_m^{-1}
=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A&mv\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is the
matrix of $\alpha_m(v,A)$, and
$D_m^{-1}aD_m=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\frac1me_1\\0&1
\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\notin\Gamma$, so in
$\bar G_m:=\langle\Gamma,D_m\rangle\le\GL_4(\Z[1/m])$ the cosets
$D_m\Gamma$ and $aD_m\Gamma$ of $\bar X_m:=\bar G_m/\Gamma$ are
distinct.  The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} applies with
$(\bar G,\bar X,D)$ replaced by $(\bar G_m,\bar X_m,D_m)$ --- its
only inputs were the conjugation identity, the fixing of the base
coset by $\Gamma$, and the coset separation --- and sends $w_m$ to
$[c_{D_m\Gamma},c_{aD_m\Gamma}]=-1\neq1$ in
$\ClLamp(\bar X_m)\rtimes\bar G_m$.

The final assertions follow exactly as in the proofs of
Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:D}, with $E_m$ in place of $E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:familyscope}
We do not claim the groups $E_m$ are pairwise non-isomorphic;
multiplicity is addressed in Corollary~\ref{cor:continuum}.  For
every $d\ge3$ the same construction runs with
$\Z^d\rtimes\SL_d(\Z)$ in place of $\Gamma$ --- property (T) holds
by the same references \cite[Sections 1.4--1.5 and 4.2]{BHV} ---
once a finite presentation of the base is fixed; we confine the
formal statement to $d=3$, where the presentation
\eqref{eq:gamma-presentation} is completely explicit.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Multiplicity}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:subgroupMF}
Every subgroup of an MF group is MF; equivalently, a group
containing a non-MF subgroup is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.IsOperatorMF.subgroup}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The restriction of an injective corona representation to a subgroup
is an injective corona representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[multiplicity]\label{cor:continuum}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item The groups $E\times\Z^k$, $k\in\N$, are pairwise
non-isomorphic, finitely presented, and non-MF.
\item There exist $2^{\aleph_0}$ pairwise non-isomorphic finitely
generated non-MF groups.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every group in sight contains an isomorphic copy of $E$, hence is
non-MF by Theorem~\ref{thm:A} and Lemma~\ref{lem:subgroupMF}.

(1) $E\times\Z^k$ is finitely presented because $E$ is.  Its
abelianization is $E^{\mathrm{ab}}\times\Z^k$; since $E$ is finitely
generated, $E^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is a finitely generated abelian group,
of some torsion-free rank $r_0<\infty$, so the abelianization of
$E\times\Z^k$ has torsion-free rank $r_0+k$, an isomorphism
invariant that separates distinct $k$.

(2) By a classical construction of B.\,H.~Neumann \cite{Neumann}
there is a family $(N_i)_{i\in I}$ with $|I|=2^{\aleph_0}$ of
pairwise non-isomorphic $2$-generator groups.  Put
$G_i=E\times N_i$, a finitely generated non-MF group.  For a fixed
countable group $G_1$, the set $\{i\in I:G_i\cong G_1\}$ is
countable: a countable group has only countably many finitely
generated subgroups (each is determined by a finite generating
subset), hence countably many isomorphism classes of such; if
$G_i\cong G_1$ then $N_i$ embeds into $G_1$ as $\{1\}\times N_i$;
and the $N_i$ are pairwise non-isomorphic, so
$i\mapsto[N_i]$ is injective on the set in question.  A map from a
set of cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$ with countable fibres has image of
cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$; apply this to $i\mapsto[G_i]$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Algorithmic unrecognizability}

We use the Adian--Rabin theorem in its standard Markov-property form
\cite{Adian58,Rabin}: if an isomorphism-invariant property has a finitely
presented positive example and a finitely presented forbidden group which
cannot embed in any finitely presented group having the property, then the
property is undecidable from finite presentations.

\begin{theorem}[MF recognition is undecidable]\label{thm:mfrecognition}
There is no algorithm which, given a finite group presentation, decides
whether the presented group is MF.
\ifanonymous\else
\leanconditional{Computability/MarkovMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.MarkovMFConsequences.operatorMF_recognition_undecidable}
\fi
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Being MF is inherited by subgroups (Lemma~\ref{lem:subgroupMF}).  The
trivial group is a finitely presented positive example, while the finitely
presented non-MF group $E$ cannot embed in any MF group.  Thus MF is a
Markov property, and Adian--Rabin applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[negative instances are not recursively enumerable]
\label{thm:nonmfre}
For any standard effective coding of finite group presentations, the set
of codes presenting non-MF groups is not recursively enumerable.
Consequently there is no sound and complete recursively verifiable system
of finite certificates for non-MF-ness of finitely presented groups.
\ifanonymous\else
\leanconditional{Computability/MarkovMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.MarkovMFConsequences.operatorMF_negative_side_not_re}
\fi
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a finitely presented group $N$ with undecidable word problem.  The
effective test-group construction in the proof of Adian--Rabin associates
to every word $u$ a finite presentation $P_u$ such that $u=1$ in $N$
implies $G(P_u)=1$, whereas $u\neq1$ implies that $E$ embeds in $G(P_u)$.
Hence $G(P_u)$ is MF exactly when $u=1$.  Trivial words are recursively
enumerable.  If non-MF finite presentations were also recursively
enumerable, pulling that enumeration back along $u\mapsto P_u$ would
enumerate the nontrivial words of $N$; running both enumerations in parallel
would decide its word problem, a contradiction.  The certificate statement
follows because the objects accepted by a recursive finite verifier form a
recursively enumerable set.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$C^*$-recognition problems]\label{cor:Cstarrecognition}
There is no algorithm which, from a finite presentation of a group $H$,
decides any of the following five properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Cred(H)$ is MF;
\item $\Cmax(H)$ is MF;
\item $\Cmax(H)$ is finite;
\item $\Cmax(H)$ is stably finite;
\item $\Cmax(H)$ is directly finite.
\end{enumerate}
For each property, the set of finite presentations on which it fails is
not recursively enumerable.
\ifanonymous\else
\leanconditional{Computability/CStarRecognitionConsequences}{GroupApproximation.CStarRecognitionConsequences.all_groupCStar_recognition_undecidable}
\leanconditional{Computability/CStarRecognitionConsequences}{GroupApproximation.CStarRecognitionConsequences.all_groupCStar_negative_sides_not_re}
\fi
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $H\le K$, the restriction of the left regular representation of $K$
to $H$ is a direct sum of copies of the left regular representation of
$H$, so $\Cred(H)\hookrightarrow\Cred(K)$.  The analogous maximal
embedding was recalled in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:universalCstarcontrast}.  MF-ness, finiteness, stable
finiteness, and direct finiteness pass to unital $C^*$-subalgebras.
Thus each property is subgroup-hereditary at the group level.

For the first two properties, the forbidden finitely presented group is
$E$.  For the last three it is the finitely presented group $G$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:ambient}, whose maximal algebra contains a proper
isometry.  The trivial group is a positive example in every case, so the
Adian--Rabin theorem gives undecidability.  Applying its effective
test-group construction to the respective forbidden group gives the same
pullback argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:nonmfre}, hence failure of
recursive enumerability on the negative side.
\end{proof}

The formal companion kernel-checks these computability-theoretic pullbacks
from explicitly supplied effective Adian--Rabin transformations and their
correctness equivalences.  It does not claim to formalize the historical
test-group construction itself.

\subsection{Uniform invisibility}

\begin{theorem}[uniform invisibility]\label{thm:uniform}
Let $H$, $(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$ and $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$ be
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, and fix $f_0\in F$ --- for
instance $H=E$ and $f_0=w$.  For every $\varepsilon>0$ there exist
$\delta>0$ and a finite subset $F_0\subseteq H$ such that for every
$r\ge1$ and every map $\varphi\colon H\to\U(M_r(\C))$ with
\[
  \opnorm{\varphi(g)\varphi(h)-\varphi(gh)}\;\le\;\delta
  \qquad\text{for all }g,h\in F_0,
\]
one has $\opnorm{\varphi(f_0)-1}<\varepsilon$.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormMFPrintedConsequences}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.finiteNormal_uniform_invisibility}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the statement fails for some $\varepsilon_0>0$.  Fix an
enumeration $H=\{h_1,h_2,\dots\}$ (with repetitions if $H$ is finite)
and put $F_k=\{h_1,\dots,h_k\}$.
For each $k\ge1$, the failure at $(\delta,F_0)=(1/k,F_k)$ produces
$r_k\ge1$ and $\varphi_k\colon H\to\U(M_{r_k}(\C))$ with
$\opnorm{\varphi_k(g)\varphi_k(h)-\varphi_k(gh)}\le1/k$ for all
$g,h\in F_k$, but $\opnorm{\varphi_k(f_0)-1}\ge\varepsilon_0$.
Define $\Theta(g)$ as the class of the sequence
$(\varphi_k(g))_{k\in\N}$ in $\mathcal Q\bigl((r_k)_{k\in\N}\bigr)$,
a unitary element.  For fixed $g,h\in H$ we have $g,h\in F_k$ for
all large $k$, so the displayed defect tends to $0$ and
$\Theta(g)\Theta(h)=\Theta(gh)$.  Taking $g=h=1$ gives
$\opnorm{\varphi_k(1)^2-\varphi_k(1)}\le1/k$ for large $k$, hence
$\opnorm{\varphi_k(1)-1}\le1/k$ after multiplying by the unitary
$\varphi_k(1)^*$; so $\Theta(1)=1$, and then
$\Theta(g)\Theta(g^{-1})=\Theta(1)=1$ for every $g$.  Thus $\Theta$
is a corona representation of $H$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}
gives $\Theta(f_0)=1$, i.e.\ $\opnorm{\varphi_k(f_0)-1}\to0$ ---
contradicting $\opnorm{\varphi_k(f_0)-1}\ge\varepsilon_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:effective}
Conversely, the uniform statement applied to unitary lifts recovers
the kill part of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, so the two are
equivalent.  The compactness proof yields no explicit
$(\delta,F_0)$, and none is claimed; an effective version would
require tracking the Kazhdan constant of $\Lambda$ through
Sections~\ref{sec:conj}--\ref{sec:proofA}, which we do not pursue.
\end{remark}

The closedness assertion below is a standard consequence of Dadarlat's
finite-local characterization of MF groups and his observation that groups
locally embeddable in MF groups are MF
\cite[Proposition~3.2 and Remark~3.3(i)]{Dadarlat}, together with the standard
fact that a marked limit locally embeds in its approximating groups; for a
modern formulation see \cite[Theorem~2.16]{FFGS}.  We claim no novelty for
that implication.  We record it because the word-ball certificate is useful
here and because the full topological statement is machine-checked below.

\begin{theorem}[marked-space closedness and the literal cylinder]
\label{thm:markedclosed}
For every $k$, the MF locus in the space $\mathcal G_k$ of
$k$-marked groups is closed; equivalently, the non-MF locus is open.
More precisely, every non-MF marked group has a finite reduced-word ball
whose relation pattern defines a neighborhood consisting entirely of
non-MF groups.

For the canonical eight-generator marking of $E$, there is the explicit
clopen neighborhood
\[
 \mathcal O_E=\{(H;h_1,\ldots,h_8):
 r(h_1,\ldots,h_8)=1\ (r\in R),\quad
 w(h_1,\ldots,h_8)\neq1\}.
\]
Every group in $\mathcal O_E$ is non-MF.  With the literal relator set of
Definition~\ref{def:E}, membership is determined by the relation pattern
on reduced words of length at most $34$.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFLocalNormalization}{GroupApproximation.OperatorMFLocalNormalization.isOperatorMF_iff_isNormApproximable_one}
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.isClosed_operatorMFLocus}
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.exists_wordBall_cylinder_subset_compl_operatorMFLocus}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralPresentationRadius}{GroupApproximation.LiteralPresentationRadius.relators_or_markedWord_length_le_34}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The tensor-power separation amplifier gives the following normalized local
criterion: a countable group is MF exactly when, for every finite subset
and every positive error, it has an operator-norm unitary model whose
distinct elements are separated by at least $1$.

Suppose $(G_n,S_n)\to(G,S)$ and every $G_n$ is MF.  Given a finite
$F\subseteq G$, choose words in the free marked generators representing
every element of $F\cup F^2$.  On a sufficiently small cylinder, all
equalities and inequalities among these finitely many words agree with
those in $G$.  A normalized local model in one such $G_n$ therefore pulls
back, word by word, to a local model of $G$ with the same multiplicative
error and separation.  The criterion makes $G$ MF.  This proves
closedness; recording the finitely many control words inside one reduced
word ball gives the stronger finite-radius statement.

For the literal neighborhood, the relators give a surjection
$E\twoheadrightarrow H$.  If the marked word survives in $H$, portability
of $w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm MF}(E)$ puts its nontrivial image in
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm MF}(H)$, so $H$ is non-MF.  Equality or
inequality of each fixed word is clopen, and there are finitely many
relators.  Direct reduction of the displayed words gives length at most
$16$ for $w$ and at most $34$ for every relator (the longest are the
centrality relators $[w,g]$), proving the asserted radius.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[open questions]\label{rem:openq}
Two questions remain adjacent to this section.  (i) Is $E/\langle
w\rangle$ MF, i.e.\ is the MF radical of $E$ exactly $\{1,w\}$?
(ii) Does a \emph{torsion-free} non-MF group exist?  Theorem~\ref{thm:normalkazhdan}
removes the former conceptual obstruction: it would suffice to construct
a torsion-free finitely presented $H$ with a nontrivial normal
property~(T) subgroup $K\le N_{\rm comp}$ (or, after the corresponding
whole-defect upgrade, $K\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$).  A plausible route is to
combine a torsion-free Rips construction, which supplies the normal
Kazhdan kernel, with the one-sided-compressor geometry in
Fournier-Facio's torsion-free nonsofic construction \cite{FF}; we do not
carry out that construction here.
\end{remark}

\section{The cyclic-base comparison}\label{sec:necessity}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:C}.  The broader phenomenon that a marked central
involution can be invisible to every exact finite-dimensional representation
but visible to approximate representations is central to Slofstra's work
\cite{SlofstraCorrelations}; the point here is its occurrence in the same
operator-norm marked-compression pattern as Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.  Recall the
setting: $G_0=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)$
with base $\Gamma_0=\langle\gamma_0\rangle\cong\Z$ and stable letter
$t$ satisfying $t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2$; $X_0=G_0/\Gamma_0$;
$E_0=\ClLamp(X_0)\rtimes G_0$ with central sign
$z_0=-1\in\ClLamp(X_0)$ as in
Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.  Figure~\ref{fig:cyclic-comparison}
summarizes the comparison.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
\emph{$z_0\neq1$:} immediate from Construction~\ref{con:clifford},
since $\ClLamp(X_0)\le\Cl(X_0)^\times$ and $\Cl(X_0)\neq0$.

\emph{Amenability:} every finitely generated subgroup of $\ClLamp(X_0)$ is
contained in the subgroup generated by $\pm1$ and finitely many
$c_x$, which is a finite $2$-group (it has order at most
$2^{k+1}$ for $k$ sites, since every element is $\pm$ a reduced product
of distinct generators).  Hence $\ClLamp(X_0)$ is locally finite, so
amenable.  The group $G_0=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\cong\Z[1/2]\rtimes\Z$ is
metabelian, so amenable.  As an extension of the amenable $G_0$ by the
amenable $\ClLamp(X_0)$, the group $E_0$ is amenable.

\emph{MF:} by Facts~\ref{fact:tww} and~\ref{fact:qdcorona}, the amenable
group $E_0$ is MF, and
an MF embedding $E_0\hookrightarrow\U(\mathcal Q)$ is injective, so the
image of $z_0$ is a nontrivial (self-adjoint) unitary in the corona:
the central sign survives.

\emph{The quotient from the literal presentation:} assign
$\gamma_0\mapsto\gamma_0$, $t\mapsto t$, and $c\mapsto c_o$, where
$o=\Gamma_0\in X_0$.  The point $o$ is fixed by $\Gamma_0$, so
$[c_o,\gamma_0]=1$.  Also
$t\Gamma_0t^{-1}=\langle\gamma_0^2\rangle\subseteq\Gamma_0$.
Moreover $\gamma_0t\Gamma_0\neq t\Gamma_0$ in
$G_0$: otherwise $t^{-1}\gamma_0t\in\Gamma_0$; in the faithful affine
model $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)=\Z[1/2]\rtimes\Z$ with
$\gamma_0=(1,0)$ and $t=(0,1)$ acting by multiplication by $2$, we
compute $t^{-1}\gamma_0t=(\tfrac12,0)\notin\Gamma_0=\Z\times\{0\}$.
Consequently
\[
 w_{\mathrm{BS}}\longmapsto
 [c_{t\Gamma_0},c_{\gamma_0t\Gamma_0}]=z_0.
\]
The image is central of order two, so every relator of
$E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ is respected.  The resulting homomorphism is surjective:
$\gamma_0,t$ generate $G_0$, the conjugates of $c_o$ give all $c_x$ because
$G_0$ acts transitively on $X_0$, and their commutators contain $z_0$.
Composing this quotient with the MF embedding of $E_0$ gives the asserted
corona representation of $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$.

\emph{The finite-dimensional obstruction persists:} $E_0$ contains the
pattern of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with $H=E_0$, $\Gamma=\Gamma_0$,
$t=t$, $c=c_{o}$ ($o=\Gamma_0\in X_0$, fixed by $\Gamma_0$), and
$a=\gamma_0\in\Gamma_0$.  We have
$t\Gamma_0t^{-1}=\langle\gamma_0^2\rangle\subseteq\Gamma_0$ and
$[c_o,\Gamma_0]=1$.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:B} says every
finite-dimensional representation of $E_0$, over every field, kills $z_0$.
Applying the same theorem directly to the displayed presentation of
$E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ shows that every finite-dimensional representation kills
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:C} shows that the argument cannot be generalized merely by
deleting its fixed-space rigidity input.  The elementary
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} is genuinely weaker than the corona conclusion:
$E_0$ has MF approximations even though its finite-dimensional
representations all kill the sign.
\end{remark}

\section{Limitations: Hilbert--Schmidt metrics and hyperlinearity}\label{sec:limits}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} does not extend to tracial
ultraproducts, for a structural reason worth recording.

Suppose one replaces the norm corona $\mathcal Q$ by a tracial
ultraproduct $\mathcal M=\prod^\omega(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$, with
defects measured in $\hsnorm\cdot$ rather than operator norm, and asks
whether a hyperlinear-style embedding of $E$ must kill $w$.  Two steps
break.

First, Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} fails: Hilbert--Schmidt-multiplicative
lifts need not be operator-norm multiplicative, so the adjoint maps
$\Ad U_{g,n}$ need not assemble into a homomorphism into any norm
corona.  One can instead pass to the conjugation representation on
$L^2(\mathcal M)$, which is an honest representation of $E$.

The tracial ultraproduct $\mathcal M$ itself is a finite von Neumann algebra.
Second, and decisively, Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} is unavailable after the
argument passes to its conjugation action.  The available ambient algebra is
$B(L^2(\mathcal M))$; when $L^2(\mathcal M)$ is infinite-dimensional this
algebra is properly infinite and has no normalized finite trace with which
to compare the fixed-space projections.  Thus the inclusion supplied by the
analogue of Lemma~\ref{lem:order} is not forced to be equality.  Proper
inclusion between nested unitarily equivalent projections is possible in
such an ambient algebra (the unilateral-shift phenomenon), but we do not
claim that it occurs for a tracial representation of the present group.
In particular the present paper proves nothing about
hyperlinearity or Connes embeddability of $E$, and nothing about the
sofic-versus-hyperlinear problem.

The dividing line in the present argument is thus: \emph{operator-norm matrix
models form a stably finite world, in which property (T) compression is
rigid; for tracial matrix models the available ambient algebra of the
conjugation action can be properly infinite, so the same projection
comparison need not collapse to equality.}

This failure is closely related to a current structural question rather than
to a known impossibility theorem.  Alekseev--Thom ask whether, for a Kazhdan
representation into a tracial ultraproduct of matrices, its commutant can be
recovered as an ultraproduct of finite-dimensional subalgebras, possibly as
coordinate centralizers of suitable lifts \cite[Open Problem~6.2]{AlekseevThom}.
Such a coordinate model would provide finite-level structure absent from the
argument above, but their question is open and does not restore the present
stable-finiteness proof in the tracial setting.
