\title{Limits of Matrix Approximation of Groups in Operator Norm}
\author{\paperauthor}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 46L05; Secondary 20C07, 16S50, 20G35}
\keywords{MF groups, norm matrix coronas, MF radicals, property~(T), asymptotic representations, Leavitt algebra}

\begin{abstract}
We show that not every countable group is MF, where MF means admitting
finite-dimensional unitary models that are asymptotically multiplicative
and asymptotically separate nonidentity elements in operator norm.  More
precisely, the simple property-\textup{(T)} group
$H=\EL_{12}(L_{\F_2}(1,2))$ has trivial image under every homomorphism to
an MF group; consequently its reduced group $C^*$-algebra is a separable
stably finite $C^*$-algebra that is not MF\@.  The obstruction comes from a
one-sided compression phenomenon for Kazhdan subgroups in norm matrix
coronas.  In the opposite direction, we prove an MF permanence theorem for
HNN extensions whose edge isomorphism is implemented by a unitary of a
norm matrix corona.  Combining the two mechanisms, we show that
recognizing MF groups from finite presentations is $\Pi^0_2$-complete.
\end{abstract}

Not every countable group is MF.

 H=\EL_{12}(L_{\F_2}(1,2)).

The group $H$ is countable, nontrivial, simple, and has
property~\textup{(T)}, but every homomorphism from $H$ to an MF group is
trivial.  In particular, $H$ is not MF.
Its reduced group $C^*$-algebra
$C^*_{\mathrm r}(H)$ is separable and stably finite, but is not MF.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/HeadlineTheorem}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptBinaryLeavittHeadline}
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/ReducedCStarConsequence}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptReducedCStarConsequence}

More precisely, a countable group $G$ is \emph{MF} if there are positive
integers $d_n$ and maps $V_n\colon G\to\U(d_n)$, with $V_n(1)=1$, such that
\[
 \opnorm{V_n(gh)-V_n(g)V_n(h)}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(g,h\in G),
\]
and
\[
 \limsup_n\opnorm{V_n(g)-1}>0
 \qquad(g\in G\setminus\{1\}).
\]
This is the operator norm notion of MF group introduced by
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt~\cite{CDE}.

Equivalently, $G$ embeds in the unitary group of the norm matrix corona
associated to the sequence $\mathbf d=(d_n)$,
%<webmath object="Qd" when="\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}" title="the matrix sequence world used to test MF">
% Read \(\mathbf d=(d_1,d_2,d_3,\ldots)\) as a [[sequence]] of positive whole
% numbers: \(d_n\) is the number of rows and columns in the nth [[matrix]].
% The [[calligraphic letter|calligraphic]] Q is simply the name of the object
% being defined.  On the
% right of the equals sign, \(\prod_n\) means an [[infinite product]] of matrix
% choices and \(\bigoplus_n\) means the [[direct sum]] of choices that shrink
% to zero.  The slash forms a [[quotient]], so two [[bounded sequence|bounded
% sequences]] count as equal when the [[operator norm]] of their difference
% has [[limit]] zero.  A [[group]] has an [[embedding]] here when it has a
% [[faithful]] [[representation]] by these matrix sequences.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="Mdn" when="M_{d_n}(\C)" title="the square matrices used at stage n">
% \(M_{d_n}(\C)\) is the [[set]] of all square [[matrix|matrices]] with
% \(d_n\) rows and \(d_n\) columns and with [[complex number|complex number]]
% entries.  The subscript n is an [[index]] selecting one stage.  The whole
% number \(d_n\) may grow as n grows, so later stages may use larger matrices.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="n∏" when="\prod_nM_{d_n}(\C)" title="one bounded matrix sequence across all finite models">
% The large product sign \(\prod_n\) means: choose one [[matrix]] from every
% indexed matrix set \(M_{d_n}(\C)\).  One choice from each stage makes a
% [[sequence]].  Only [[bounded sequence|bounded sequences]] are allowed here:
% one fixed number must be at least as large as every matrix's [[operator
% norm]].  Two sequences are added or multiplied position by position.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="n⨁" when="\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C)" title="the matrix sequences that shrink to zero">
% The large circled plus sign \(\bigoplus_n\) names the [[direct sum]].  It
% contains exactly the matrix [[sequence|sequences]] whose [[operator norm|operator
% norms]] have [[limit]] zero.  Placing it after the slash means forming a
% [[quotient]]: any error sequence that shrinks to zero is treated as exactly
% zero in the [[norm matrix corona]].
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="C" when="M_{d_n}(\C)" title="the number system used for matrix entries">
% The [[blackboard-bold letter|blackboard bold]] C, \(\C\), means the
% [[complex number|complex numbers]].
% They include ordinary real numbers and numbers involving \(i\), where
% \(i^2=-1\).  They are the entries used in the paper's [[unitary matrix|unitary
% matrices]] and the coordinates in its [[vector space|vector spaces]].
%</webmath>
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}
 =\prod_nM_{d_n}(\C)\big/\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C).
\]
Here
%<webmath object="n⨁" when="\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C)" title="the matrix sequences that fade to zero">
% The left side is a short name for the [[set]] described on the right.  A
% member is a [[sequence]] \((x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots)\) in which \(x_n\) is a
% \(d_n\)-by-\(d_n\) matrix of [[complex number|complex numbers]].  The final
% condition, \(\lVert x_n\rVert\longrightarrow0\), says that the matrices
% become arbitrarily small in [[operator norm]] as n grows.  They need not ever
% equal the zero matrix; their effect only has to approach zero.  The [[direct
% sum]] is exactly the collection of these fading sequences.  In the [[norm
% matrix corona]], the [[quotient]] ignores such a sequence, so two bounded
% matrix sequences count as the same when the operator norm of their difference
% approaches zero.
%</webmath>
\[
 \bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C)
 =\{(x_n)\in\prod_nM_{d_n}(\C):\opnorm{x_n}\longrightarrow0\}
\]
is the $c_0$-direct sum.

A separable $C^*$-algebra is called MF if it embeds as a $C^*$-subalgebra of
a norm matrix corona.  For every countable group $G$, the reduced group
algebra $C^*_{\mathrm r}(G)$ is separable and has a faithful canonical trace,
hence is stably finite.  Suppose that $e\colon C^*_{\mathrm r}(G)\to\mathcal
Q_{\mathbf d}$ is an MF embedding and put $p=e(1)$.  Then
$g\mapsto e(\lambda_g)+(1-p)$ is an injective homomorphism from $G$ into
$\mathcal U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})$, so $G$ is MF.  Therefore a non-MF group
automatically gives a separable stably finite reduced group $C^*$-algebra that
is not MF.

For a countable group $G$, define its MF radical by
%<webmath object="RadMF" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)" title="the elements of G that every allowed matrix model erases">
% \(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)\), read “the MF radical of G,” is a [[normal
% subgroup]] of the [[group]] G.  It contains exactly the elements that every
% [[homomorphism]] from G into every [[norm matrix corona]] sends to the
% [[identity]].  In plainer words, these are the group moves that no allowed
% [[finite-matrix model|finite matrix experiment]] can distinguish from doing
% nothing.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="d⋂" when="\bigcap_{\mathbf d}" title="requiring the condition for every choice of matrix sizes">
% The symbol \(\bigcap\) means [[intersection]].  The bold d underneath it
% runs through every [[sequence]] \(\mathbf d=(d_1,d_2,\ldots)\) of matrix
% sizes.  An element lies in this intersection only if the stated condition
% holds for every possible choice of finite matrix sizes.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="π:G→U(Qd)⋂" when="\bigcap_{\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})}" title="checking every allowed map from G">
% After a matrix size [[sequence]] is chosen, the [[Greek letter]] \(\pi\),
% read “pi,” names a [[homomorphism]].  The notation
% \(\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})\)
% says that its inputs come from the [[group]] G and its outputs are [[unitary
% matrix|unitary elements]] of the [[norm matrix corona]].  The second
% \(\bigcap\) is another [[intersection]], so the condition must hold for every
% such map \(\pi\).
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="ker" when="\bigcap_{\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})}\ker\pi" title="the elements that one map erases">
% \(\ker\pi\) means the [[kernel]] of the map \(\pi\): all elements of G that
% \(\pi\) sends to the [[identity]].  The complete formula takes an
% [[intersection]] over every map \(\pi\) and every matrix size [[sequence]].
% What remains is exactly the [[MF radical]].
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)
 =\bigcap_{\mathbf d}
   \bigcap_{\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})}\ker\pi.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-residual-calculus} shows that $G$ is MF
precisely when $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ is trivial.  If
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=G$, every homomorphism from $G$ to an MF group is
trivial.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrintedCriterion}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptFullRadicalKillsMFTargets}

For a subgroup $L\le G$, put
%<webmath object="CompG" when="\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)" title="the elements of G that carry L back inside L">
% \(\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)\) is the name of a [[set]] of elements u inside
% the [[group]] G.  The braces use [[set-builder notation]]: u is included when
% \(uLu^{-1}\le L\).  Here \(u^{-1}\) is the [[inverse]] of u,
% \(uLu^{-1}\) is the result of [[conjugation|conjugating]] every element of L,
% and \(\le\) means “is a [[subgroup]] of.”  The result may be smaller than L;
% equality is not required.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="L" when="uLu^{-1}\le L" title="the collection L carried back inside itself">
% L is a [[subgroup]] of G.  The symbol \(\le\) means subgroup containment,
% not numerical comparison.  Thus \(uLu^{-1}\le L\) says that
% \(u\ell u^{-1}\) belongs to L for every element \(\ell\) in L.  The exponent
% \(-1\) means [[inverse]], and the product of three factors is [[conjugation]].
% The paper later uses the rigidity property called [[property (T)]] for L.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}_G(L)=\{u\in G:uLu^{-1}\le L\}
\]
and define its \emph{compression--centralizer defect} by
%<webmath object="DG" when="\mathfrak D_G(L)" title="everything forced by the listed failures to commute">
% The [[fraktur letter|ornate letter]] \(\mathfrak D_G(L)\) names the [[normal
% closure]] in G of the listed [[commutator|commutators]].  Inside the braces,
% the colon means
% “subject to”: u lies in \(\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)\), c lies in the
% [[centralizer]] \(C_G(L)\), and \(\ell\) lies in L.  The bracket
% \([ucu^{-1},\ell]\) abbreviates
% \(ucu^{-1}\ell u c^{-1}u^{-1}\ell^{-1}\).  The double angle brackets mean
% “take the smallest [[normal subgroup]] of G containing every such result.”
% The theorem shows that certain [[rigidity|rigid]] subgroups inside it are
% erased by every map to an [[MF group]].
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsic-defect}
 \mathfrak D_G(L)
 =\normal{
   [ucu^{-1},\ell]:
   u\in\operatorname{Comp}_G(L),\ c\in C_G(L),\ \ell\in L
 }_G.
\end{equation}
\begin{mainthm}[one-sided compression criterion]
\label{thm:compression-criterion}
Let $G$ be countable and let $L\le G$ have property~\textup{(T)}.  If
$K\trianglelefteq G$ has property~\textup{(T)} and
$K\le\mathfrak D_G(L)$, then
%<webmath object="K" when="K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)" title="the subgroup K that every allowed matrix model erases">
% K is the [[normal subgroup]] assumed in the theorem, and it has [[property
% (T)]].  The symbol \(\le\) means that K is contained as a subgroup in the
% [[MF radical]] of G.  Equivalently, every [[homomorphism]] from G into every
% [[norm matrix corona]] sends every element of K to the [[identity]].
%</webmath>
\[
 K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G).
\]
In particular, a nontrivial such $K$ makes $G$ non-MF.  If $G$ has
property~\textup{(T)} and $\mathfrak D_G(L)=G$, then
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=G$.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrintedCriterion}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptOneSidedCompressionCriterion}
\end{mainthm}

We sketch the argument.  Corollary~\ref{cor:defect-hs} gives
$\mathfrak D_G(L)\le R_{\infty\to2}(G)$.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrintedDefectShadow}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptPrintedDefectShadowInclusion}
That is, every operator norm asymptotic representation of $G$ is
asymptotically trivial on $\mathfrak D_G(L)$ in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  If a corona homomorphism were nontrivial on $K$,
the Kazhdan projection of $K$ would cut out a $G$-invariant corner on
which $K$ has no nonzero fixed vectors
(Lemma~\ref{lem:central-corona-corner}); by the Kazhdan inequality some
$s_0\in K$ would then stay bounded away from the identity in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm along a subsequence, contradicting
$s_0\in\mathfrak D_G(L)$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:commutant} gives a separate finite-dimensional vanishing
result: every finite-dimensional linear representation of $G$ over any field
kills $\mathfrak D_G(L)$.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrintedDefect}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptPrintedDefectFiniteDimensionalSterility}

We next apply Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion} to the binary Leavitt
construction that produces the group $H$ announced above.

% \(L_{\F_2}(1,2)\) names a particular [[Leavitt algebra]].  The subscript
% \(\F_2\) names its [[field]] with two numbers, and \((1,2)\) records that one
% [[free module]] can behave like two copies of itself.  The algebra is
% [[generated by relations]] from four elements \(s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1\).  Their
% subscripts are [[index|indices]], not powers.  The equations below say that
% the t elements recover inputs placed by the s elements.  The proof uses this
% rule for copying itself to construct the [[matrix]] \(\tau\).
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="F2" when="L_{\F_2}(1,2)" title="the number system containing only 0 and 1">
% \(\F_2\), read “F two,” uses a [[blackboard-bold letter|blackboard bold
% letter]] F and is the
% [[field]] containing only 0 and 1.  Its
% arithmetic has \(1+1=0\), so its only nonzero [[scalar]] is 1.  The subscript
% 2 records the number of elements in the field.  Later this prevents a
% [[scalar]] multiple of the [[identity matrix]] from hiding in the
% [[center]] of H, which helps prove that H is a [[simple group]].
%</webmath>
Let $L_{\F_2}(1,2)$ denote the binary Leavitt algebra.  It is the unital
$\F_2$-algebra generated by $s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1$ subject to
%<webmath object="ti" when="t_i s_j=\delta_{ij}" title="the two rules that make one copy behave like two">
% The letters \(s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1\) are four named elements of the [[Leavitt
% algebra]], and their subscripts 0 and 1 are [[index|indices]], not powers.
% Writing two letters side by side means multiply them in that order.  In the
% first equation, i and j can each be 0 or 1, and the [[Kronecker delta]]
% \(\delta_{ij}\) is 1 when \(i=j\) and 0 otherwise.  Thus \(t_i\) reverses
% the matching \(s_i\) and cancels the other s.  The comma separates the two
% rules.  The second equation says the two pieces \(s_0t_0\) and \(s_1t_1\)
% add up to the [[identity]] 1.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="δij" when="t_i s_j=\delta_{ij}" title="a symbol that chooses 1 when i equals j and 0 otherwise">
% The [[Greek letter]] \(\delta\), read “delta,” is the [[Kronecker delta]].  The
% subscripts i and j are [[index|indices]].  The expression \(\delta_{ij}\)
% equals 1 when i and j are the same index and equals 0 when they are different.
% Consequently, the equation \(t_i s_j=\delta_{ij}\) is a short way to state
% four equations: \(t_0s_0=1\), \(t_1s_1=1\), \(t_0s_1=0\), and
% \(t_1s_0=0\).
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leavitt}
 t_i s_j=\delta_{ij},
 \qquad s_0t_0+s_1t_1=1.
\end{equation}
%<webmath object="eij" when="e_{ij}(a)" title="the identity matrix with one extra entry a">
% \(e_{ij}(a)=1+aE_{ij}\) is an [[elementary matrix]].  The letters i and j
% are [[index|indices]] naming different rows and columns.  Here 1 means the
% [[identity matrix]], while \(E_{ij}\) has a single 1 in row i, column j and
% zeros elsewhere.  Multiplying by the [[ring]] element a replaces that one
% entry by a.  Thus \(e_{ij}(a)\) is the identity matrix with the extra entry
% a in row i, column j.  Products and [[commutator|commutators]] of these
% matrices reproduce addition and multiplication in R.
%</webmath>
For a unital ring $R$, write $e_{ij}(a)=1+aE_{ij}$ and let
$\EL_n(R)$ be the subgroup of $\GL_n(R)$ generated by the elements
$e_{ij}(a)$, $i\ne j$.

\begin{mainthm}[the binary Leavitt group]\label{thm:headline}
%<webmath object="EL12" when="H=\EL_{12}(R)" title="the group H made from 12-by-12 matrices">
% The equation \(H=\EL_{12}(R)\) gives the [[group]] a short name, H.  The
% notation \(\EL_{12}(R)\) means the [[elementary group]] made from 12-by-12
% [[elementary matrix|elementary matrices]] whose entries lie in the [[ring]]
% R.  Here R is the binary [[Leavitt algebra]].  The theorem says H contains
% something besides the [[identity]], is a [[simple group]], has [[property
% (T)]], and every [[homomorphism]] from H to an [[MF group]] is the [[trivial
% homomorphism]].
%</webmath>
Put $R=L_{\F_2}(1,2)$ and $H=\EL_{12}(R)$.  Then $H$ is countable,
nontrivial, simple, has property~\textup{(T)}, and
%<webmath object="RadMF" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)" title="why every allowed map erases all of H">
% \(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)\) is the [[MF radical]] of the [[group]] H.  The
% equals sign says this [[normal subgroup]] is all of H, not merely part of it.
% Therefore every element of H is sent to the [[identity]] by every
% [[homomorphism]] into a [[norm matrix corona]].  Every such map is the
% [[trivial homomorphism]], so H cannot be an [[MF group]].
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H.
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/ReducedCStarConsequence}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptPrintedHeadline}
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/HeadlineTheorem}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptBinaryLeavittHeadline}
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/ReducedCStarConsequence}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptReducedCStarConsequence}
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/ReducedCStarConsequence}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptNotEverySeparableStablyFiniteCStarAlgebraIsMF}
Equivalently, every homomorphism from $H$ to an MF group is trivial.
In particular, $H$ is non-MF.
Its reduced group $C^*$-algebra
$C^*_{\mathrm r}(H)$ is separable and stably finite, but is not MF.
\end{mainthm}

%<webmath object="τ" when="\tau L\tau^{-1}\le L" title="the matrix tau that carries L back inside L">
% The [[Greek letter]] \(\tau\), read “tau,” names a particular [[invertible]]
% 12-by-12 [[matrix]] constructed later.  The [[superscript]] \(-1\) means
% [[inverse]].  The product \(\tau L\tau^{-1}\) means apply [[conjugation]] by
% tau to every element of the [[subgroup]] L.  The symbol \(\le\) says every
% result still lies in L, although not every element of L must be reached.
% This containment in only one direction is what the [[property (T)]] argument
% needs.
%</webmath>

We also determine the arithmetical complexity of MF recognition for
finite presentations, in the Kleene--Mostowski hierarchy whose levels
$\Sigma^0_n$ and $\Pi^0_n$ count alternations of quantifiers over a
decidable relation.

\begin{mainthm}[recognizing MF groups]\label{thm:recognition}
Fix an effective coding of finite presentations of groups by natural
numbers.  The set of codes of finite presentations of MF groups is
$\Pi^0_2$-complete, and the set of codes of finite presentations of non-MF
groups is $\Sigma^0_2$-complete.  In particular, neither set is
recursively enumerable, and no algorithm decides from a finite presentation
whether the presented group is MF\@.  More precisely, there is a computable
map $e\mapsto\widehat R_e$ into finite presentations such that the group
presented by $\widehat R_e$ is MF if and only if the $e$-th partial
computable function has infinite domain.
\end{mainthm}

Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-upper-bound} gives a $\Pi^0_2$ description of
the MF presentations: a presentation is MF exactly when finite
certificates exist at every scale (Section~\ref{sec:local}).  For
hardness we reduce the $\Pi^0_2$-complete set of programs with infinite
domain to MF recognition: from $e$ we construct a finite presentation
$\widehat R_e$ (Section~\ref{sec:compiler}).  If the domain is finite,
then $H\le\widehat R_e$, so $\widehat R_e$ is not MF\@.  If the domain is
infinite, then $\widehat R_e$ satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hnn-permanence}, an MF permanence theorem for HNN
extensions whose edge isomorphism is implemented by a unitary of a norm
matrix corona, and is MF (Sections~\ref{sec:hnn} and~\ref{sec:positive}).
The MF property is closed in the space of marked groups
(Lemma~\ref{lem:marked-closed}), so every finitely generated group that is
not MF is a quotient of a finitely presented one;
Theorem~\ref{thm:recognition} adds the uniform family and the exact level.
Section~\ref{sec:prescribed-quotients}, which the proofs of
Theorems~\ref{thm:headline} and~\ref{thm:recognition} do not use, proves
that $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=f^{-1}(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q))$ for every
surjection $f\colon G\to Q$ with $\ker f\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$, and
that every countable MF group $Q$ is the maximal MF quotient
$W_Q/\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)$ of a non-MF group $W_Q$.

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}

Blackadar and Kirchberg introduced the notions of MF and NF algebras in a
1994 Oberwolfach abstract~\cite{BK94}.  They wrote that the classes of NF,
strong NF, and separable nuclear stably finite $C^*$-algebras might coincide.
Their 1997 paper developed these notions and proved that a separable
$C^*$-algebra is NF if and only if it is nuclear and MF~\cite{BK}.  It also
asked whether every separable nuclear stably finite $C^*$-algebra is
quasidiagonal.  This was a question about nuclear $C^*$-algebras, not about
arbitrary groups.

The group property used here was introduced by
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt~\cite{CDE}.  If every separable stably finite $C^*$-algebra were MF,
then every countable group would be MF because its reduced group
$C^*$-algebra is separable and stably finite.  Later approximation papers
described the resulting operator norm group problem as related to
Kirchberg's question~\cite{LubotzkyOppenheim,Thom}.  The negative solution of
the Connes embedding problem~\cite{MIPRE} yields separable stably finite
$C^*$-algebras that are not MF, as Goldbring and Hart
observe~\cite[Proposition~6.1 and Remark~6.2]{GoldbringHart}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} gives such an example among
reduced group $C^*$-algebras.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/ReducedCStarConsequence}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptSeparableStablyFiniteNonMFCStarAlgebraExists}
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/CountableNonMF}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptNotEveryCountableGroupIsMF}
Korchagin subsequently developed
permanence and local approximation results for MF groups~\cite{Korchagin}.
Shulman proves that $A*_C A$ is MF whenever $A$ is a separable MF
$C^*$-algebra and $C\subseteq A$ is a
$C^*$-subalgebra~\cite[Theorem~10]{Shulman}.  She also proves that the full
group $C^*$-algebras of amalgamated free products of amenable groups and of
certain semidirect products are MF~\cite{Shulman}.
Nonapproximability was known for the unnormalized Schatten $p$-norms with
$1<p<\infty$~\cite{DGLT,LubotzkyOppenheim}.
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky later proved nonapproximability for $p=1$~\cite{BDL}.
The operator norm case $p=\infty$ remained open.  They also identify stability
from operator norm to normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm as a possible route to
a non-MF group~\cite[Proposition~1.5]{BDL}.  Here
operator norm control implies that coordinatewise conjugation defines a
representation in a second norm matrix corona.  The Kazhdan projection of
this conjugation representation makes
the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant invariant under the one-sided
compressor.  Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan} then proves that a normal
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup
contained in the defect lies in the MF radical.

Leavitt introduced the algebras $L_K(1,n)$ as examples of rings with
nonunique module rank~\cite{Leavitt}.  Their defining relations supply the
internal compression used here.  The binary Leavitt algebra is purely
infinite simple~\cite{AbramsAranda}, hence an exchange ring~\cite{AraExchange},
and its center is the base field~\cite{ArandaCrow}.  Preusser classified the
subgroups of $\GL_n$ over an exchange ring that are normalized by
$\EL_n$~\cite{Preusser}.  His sandwich theorem, together with the simplicity
of the binary Leavitt algebra and the equality $Z(R)=\F_2$, implies that
$\EL_{12}(R)$ is simple (Proposition~\ref{prop:simple}).
Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain give property~\textup{(T)} for
$\EL_n(R)$ whenever $n\ge3$ and $R$ is a finitely generated unital
associative ring~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.
Related rigidity results for metric approximations of Kazhdan groups appear
in~\cite{KT19,AlekseevThom,Dadarlat}.

Decision problems for finitely presented groups go back to Dehn, and the
theorem of Adian and Rabin~\cite{Adian,Rabin} shows that no Markov
property can be recognized from a finite presentation.  Once a finitely
presented non-MF group exists, the MF property is Markov, and Adian--Rabin
gives undecidability; Theorem~\ref{thm:recognition} gives the exact level.
Torsion-freeness is the only other property of finitely presented groups
whose recognition problem was known to be $\Pi^0_2$-complete, by
Lempp~\cite{Lempp}.  For recursively presented groups,
Bilanovic--Chubb--Roven proved that every Markov property is
$\Pi^0_2$-hard to recognize~\cite{BCR}; their construction is the first
step of Section~\ref{sec:compiler}.  The passage from recursive to finite
presentations is where the operator algebra enters: a generic Higman
embedding does not preserve the MF property, and the HNN extensions of
Section~\ref{sec:compiler} are chosen so that
Theorem~\ref{thm:hnn-permanence} applies to them.

\section{One-sided compression and the MF radical}\label{sec:compression}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion}.  Let
$uLu^{-1}\le L$.  In a finite-dimensional representation $\rho$,
conjugation by $\rho(u)$ maps the commutant of $\rho(L)$ into itself; the
map is injective, hence surjective, so the commutant is invariant
(Theorem~\ref{thm:commutant}).  In a norm matrix corona the same
conclusion is obtained for the Kazhdan projection of a
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup: conjugation moves the projection below
itself, and stable finiteness of the corona forces equality
(Section~\ref{sec:transport}).  A normal property-\textup{(T)} subgroup
contained in the compression defect then lies in the MF radical
(Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan}).

\subsection{The MF radical}

For $N\trianglelefteq G$ define its \emph{MF kernel closure}
by
%<webmath object="whole" when="\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)" title="how to read the definition of MF kernel closure">
% The whole equation says that the [[MF closure|MF kernel closure]] of N inside G
% consists of the elements that every [[homomorphism]] f must send to the
% [[identity]] whenever f maps G to an [[MF group]] M and already sends every
% element of N to the identity.  The large [[intersection]] means an element
% must lie in every eligible [[kernel]].  The condition \(N\le\ker f\) states
% that f erases N.  Thus the right side collects N together with anything
% else that all MF models are forced to erase after N has been erased.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)
 =\bigcap\{\ker f:N\le\ker f,\ f\colon G\to M,\ M\text{ MF}\}.
\]
The image of a corona homomorphism from $G$ is countable and is itself MF;
conversely, every MF target embeds in a norm matrix corona.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrintedDefinitions}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptCoronaImagesAndTargets}
Thus
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(1)$, and
$G/N$ is MF precisely when
$\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)=N$.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/LiteralMFClosure}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptMFResidualCalculusLiteral}
This is a closure operation on the normal subgroups of one fixed group; it is
unrelated to topological closure of classes of marked groups.

\begin{proposition}[basic properties of the MF radical]\label{prop:mf-residual-calculus}
For every countable group $G$, the subgroup $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ is fully
invariant, the quotient $G/\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ is MF, and
%<webmath object="whole" when="G/N\text{ is MF}" title="when dividing G by N produces an MF group">
% Read the whole display as an “if and only if.”  The [[quotient group]]
% \(G/N\) is MF exactly when the MF kernel closure of N equals N itself.
% The double arrow says each statement implies the other.  The condition in
% parentheses says N is a [[normal subgroup]] of G, which is required for
% \(G/N\) to be a group.  In plain language, finite matrix approximations can
% distinguish every nonidentity element of the quotient precisely when the
% requirement to erase N does not force them to erase any additional element
% of G.
%</webmath>
\[
 G/N\text{ is MF}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)=N
 \qquad(N\trianglelefteq G).
\]
In particular, $G$ is MF if and only if its MF radical is trivial.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/LiteralMFClosure}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptMFResidualCalculusLiteral}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An intersection of kernels is normal.  If $\alpha$ is an endomorphism of
$G$, $x\in\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$, and $\pi$ is a corona homomorphism from $G$,
then $\pi\circ\alpha$ kills $x$.  Hence $\pi(\alpha(x))=1$ for every $\pi$,
which proves full invariance.

Put $R=\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$.  If $G/R$ is trivial, there is nothing to
prove.  If $x\in G/R$ is nontrivial and $g\in G$ maps to $x$, then
$g\notin R$.  Some homomorphism from $G$ to an MF group therefore separates
$g$ from the identity.  It kills $R$ and descends to a homomorphism from $G/R$
that separates $x$.  Thus $G/R$ is residually MF, and hence MF by
Korchagin~\cite[Proposition~6]{Korchagin}.

For $N\trianglelefteq G$, homomorphisms from $G/N$ to MF groups correspond
to homomorphisms from $G$ to MF groups that kill $N$.  It follows directly
that
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G/N)
 =\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)/N.
\]
Since a countable group is MF exactly when its MF radical is trivial, this
identity gives the asserted closure criterion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite dimension}

\begin{theorem}[finite-dimensional commutant rigidity]\label{thm:commutant}
Let $G$ be a group, let $L\le G$, and suppose $u\in G$ satisfies
$uLu^{-1}\le L$.  If $k$ is a field, $V$ is a finite-dimensional
$k$-vector space, and $\rho\colon G\to\GL(V)$ is a homomorphism, then
%<webmath object="whole" when="\rho(u)\rho(L)'\rho(u)^{-1}=\rho(L)'" title="conjugating the commutant gives the same commutant back">
% The prime in \(\rho(L)'\) means the [[commutant]]: the [[set]] of linear
% operators that commute with every matrix \(\rho(\ell)\) for \(\ell\in L\).
% Placing \(\rho(u)\) on the left and its [[inverse]] on the right applies
% [[conjugation]] to each operator in that set.  The equality says this
% conjugation rearranges the commutant without losing or adding any operators.
% The hypothesis gives only the one-way containment \(uLu^{-1}\le L\);
% finite dimensionality lets the proof strengthen the corresponding
% containment of commutants to equality.
%</webmath>
\[
 \rho(u)\rho(L)'\rho(u)^{-1}=\rho(L)',
\]
where $\rho(L)'$ is the commutant of $\rho(L)$ in $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$.
Consequently, if $c\in C_G(L)$, then
%<webmath object="whole" when="\rho([ucu^{-1},\ell])=1" title="the representation makes each listed commutator trivial">
% For each element \(\ell\) of L, first form the [[commutator]] of
% \(ucu^{-1}\) and \(\ell\), and then apply the [[representation]] \(\rho\).
% The result is the [[identity matrix]] 1.  Equivalently, the represented
% matrices \(\rho(ucu^{-1})\) and \(\rho(\ell)\) commute.  The assumption
% \(c\in C_G(L)\) says c originally commutes with L; the preceding equality
% of commutants shows that conjugating c by u preserves that commuting
% behavior after any finite-dimensional representation.
%</webmath>
\[
 \rho([ucu^{-1},\ell])=1
 \qquad(\ell\in L).
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/FiniteDimensionalCommutant}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptFiniteDimensionalCommutantRigidity}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $C=\rho(L)'$.  If $x\in C$, $h\in L$, and
$h'=uhu^{-1}\in L$, then
%<webmath object="whole" when="\rho(h)\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(u)" title="the calculation showing the conjugated operator still commutes with L">
% The three lines track one matrix product while moving x past
% \(\rho(h')\).  The first equality rewrites h using \(h'=uhu^{-1}\).  The
% second uses \(x\in C=\rho(L)'\), which says x commutes with \(\rho(h')\).
% The third rewrites back to \(\rho(h)\).  The first and last lines differ
% only in whether \(\rho(h)\) appears before or after
% \(\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(u)\), so that conjugated operator commutes with
% \(\rho(h)\).  Since h was arbitrary in L, it belongs to the [[commutant]].
%</webmath>
\begin{align*}
 \rho(h)\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(u)
 &=\rho(u)^{-1}\rho(h')x\rho(u)\\
 &=\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(h')\rho(u)\\
 &=\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(u)\rho(h).
\end{align*}
Thus $\rho(u)^{-1}C\rho(u)\subseteq C$.  Conjugation by
$\rho(u)^{-1}$ is injective, and $C$ is finite-dimensional.  Its restriction
to $C$ is therefore surjective, so the inclusion is equality.  Conjugating
by $\rho(u)$ gives $\rho(u)C\rho(u)^{-1}=C$.

If $c\in C_G(L)$, then $\rho(c)\in C$, hence
$\rho(ucu^{-1})\in C$.  Therefore
$\rho([ucu^{-1},\ell])=1$ for every $\ell\in L$.
\end{proof}

The finite-dimensionality hypothesis cannot be dropped: an injective
linear self-map of an infinite-dimensional commutant need not be
surjective.

\subsection{Kazhdan transport in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm}
\label{sec:transport}

For $a\in M_d(\C)$ write
%<webmath object="whole" when="\hsnorm{a}=\tr_d(a^*a)^{1/2}" title="how normalized Hilbert--Schmidt size and normalized trace are defined">
% The first equality defines the [[normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm]] of a.
% Form \(a^*a\), where the star means [[adjoint|conjugate transpose]], take its
% normalized [[trace]], and then take the nonnegative square root indicated
% by the exponent \(1/2\).  The second equality defines \(\tr_d\): take the
% ordinary trace, which sums the diagonal entries, and divide by the matrix
% size d.  This normalization makes the identity matrix have norm 1 and lets
% matrices of different sizes be compared on the same scale.
%</webmath>
\[
 \hsnorm{a}=\tr_d(a^*a)^{1/2},
 \qquad \tr_d=\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}.
\]
An \emph{operator norm asymptotic representation} of a countable group $G$
is a sequence of maps $V_n\colon G\to\U(d_n)$ such that $V_n(1)=1$ and
%<webmath object="whole" when="\opnorm{V_n(gh)-V_n(g)V_n(h)}\longrightarrow0" title="the multiplication errors shrink to zero">
% For fixed group elements g and h, compare \(V_n(gh)\), assigned after
% multiplying in G, with \(V_n(g)V_n(h)\), obtained by multiplying the two
% assigned matrices.  Their difference is the [[multiplication error]], and
% the double bars measure its [[operator norm]].  The arrow to zero says that
% this error becomes as small as desired when n is sufficiently large.  The
% statement must hold for every pair g and h, although the stage at which a
% requested accuracy is reached may depend on that pair.
%</webmath>
\[
 \opnorm{V_n(gh)-V_n(g)V_n(h)}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(g,h\in G).
\]
For such a sequence put
%<webmath object="whole" when="K_2(V)=\{g\in G" title="the elements that become invisible in Hilbert--Schmidt norm">
% The braces define a [[set]] called \(K_2(V)\).  An element g of G belongs to
% it when \(V_n(g)\) approaches the [[identity matrix]] 1 in normalized
% [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm]] as n grows.  Thus this particular asymptotic
% representation makes g increasingly hard to distinguish from the identity
% by that norm.  The subscript 2 refers to the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, which is
% the Schatten 2-norm.  This is weaker than convergence in operator norm
% because a small fraction of matrix directions may still move greatly.
%</webmath>
\[
 K_2(V)=\{g\in G:\hsnorm{V_n(g)-1}\longrightarrow0\}.
\]
The operator norm defects also tend to zero in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm, so $K_2(V)$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.  Define
%<webmath object="whole" when="R_{\infty\to2}(G)=\bigcap_VK_2(V)" title="the elements every operator-norm model loses in Hilbert--Schmidt norm">
% The symbol \(R_{\infty\to2}(G)\) names the [[universal Hilbert–Schmidt
% kernel]].  The large [[intersection]] runs over every operator norm
% [[asymptotic representation]] V of G.  Membership requires an element to
% lie in every \(K_2(V)\), so every such model sends it toward the identity in
% normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm.  The label \(\infty\to2\) records the two
% norms: the approximations are controlled in operator norm, also called the
% infinity norm, while invisibility is measured in the Schatten 2-norm.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shadow-residual}
 R_{\infty\to2}(G)=\bigcap_V K_2(V),
\end{equation}
where $V$ ranges over all operator norm asymptotic representations of $G$.

For $L\le G$, let
%<webmath object="whole" when="\mathcal C_2(V,L)=" title="the bounded sequences that almost commute with V of L">
% The braces contain matrix [[sequence|sequences]] \((x_n)\) satisfying two
% conditions.  First, one finite bound works for every operator norm
% \(\lVert x_n\rVert\).  Second, for each \(\ell\in L\), the [[commutator]]
% \([V_n(\ell),x_n]\) has normalized [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm]] tending to zero.
% Therefore the matrices need not commute exactly at any finite stage, but
% their failure to commute becomes small in the normalized 2-norm, and the
% matrices \(x_n\) themselves are not allowed to grow without bound.  The
% left side names this [[asymptotic commutant]].
%</webmath>
\[
 \mathcal C_2(V,L)
 =\left\{(x_n)\ \middle|\
   \begin{aligned}
    &\sup_n\opnorm{x_n}<\infty,\\
    &\hsnorm{[V_n(\ell),x_n]}\longrightarrow0 &&(\ell\in L)
   \end{aligned}\right\}
\]
be the bounded Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of $V(L)$.  Coordinatewise
conjugation by $V_n(g)$ defines a bijection, denoted $\operatorname{Ad}(V(g))$,
of the bounded matrix sequences.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:stable-finite}
For every sequence of positive integers $(m_n)$, the norm matrix corona
%<webmath object="whole" when="\prod_nM_{m_n}(\C)\big/\bigoplus_nM_{m_n}(\C)" title="the norm matrix corona whose stable finiteness is used here">
% The numerator consists of bounded [[sequence|sequences]] of square complex
% matrices, with an \(m_n\)-by-\(m_n\) matrix at stage n.  The denominator
% consists of sequences whose [[operator norm]] tends to zero.  Forming the
% [[quotient]] means two bounded sequences represent the same [[corona class]]
% when the norm of their difference tends to zero.  The lemma asserts that
% this algebra is [[stably finite]], a cancellation property later used to
% turn containment between two equivalent [[projection|projections]] into
% equality.
%</webmath>
\[
 \prod_nM_{m_n}(\C)\big/\bigoplus_nM_{m_n}(\C)
\]
is stably finite.  Consequently, unitarily equivalent projections in a norm
matrix corona are equal whenever one is dominated by the other.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/StableFiniteness}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptNormMatrixCoronaStableFinite}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $v^*v=1$ in the corona, and let $(x_n)$ be a bounded lift of
$v$.  Then $x_n^*x_n\to1$ in norm.  For all sufficiently large $n$, the matrix
$x_n$ is invertible, and the unitary $x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$ differs from
$x_n$ by $o(1)$.  Thus $v$ is represented by unitaries and $vv^*=1$.  The
same argument applies to every matrix amplification of the corona.  If $A$
is a stably finite unital $C^*$-algebra, equivalent projections $p\le q$ satisfy
$p=q$.  Indeed, if $w^*w=q$ and $ww^*=p$, then $w\in qAq$ is an isometry in
the corner.  The corner is finite because $w+(1-q)$ is an isometry in $A$.
Consequently $ww^*=q$, and hence $p=q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[one-sided order for the Kazhdan projection]
\label{lem:kazhdan-projection-order}
Let $L$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let $B$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra, and
let $\pi\colon L\to\U(B)$ be a homomorphism.  Denote by
$P\in B$ the image of the Kazhdan projection under the extension
$C^*_{\max}(L)\to B$.  If $U\in\U(B)$ satisfies
%<webmath object="whole" when="U\pi(L)U^*\subseteq\pi(L)" title="U carries the represented subgroup back inside itself">
% The notation \(\pi(L)\) means all unitary elements obtained by applying
% \(\pi\) to elements of L.  For each one, place U on the left and \(U^*\) on
% the right; because U is unitary, \(U^*\) is its inverse, so this performs
% [[conjugation]].  The subset sign says every resulting element still lies
% in \(\pi(L)\), although the conjugation need not reach every element of
% \(\pi(L)\).  This is the represented version of the one-sided containment
% \(uLu^{-1}\le L\).
%</webmath>
\[
 U\pi(L)U^*\subseteq\pi(L),
\]
then
%<webmath object="whole" when="U^*PU\le P" title="the moved fixed-vector projection lies inside the original one">
% The [[projection]] P selects the vectors fixed by the represented action of
% L.  The product \(U^*PU\) is P moved by [[conjugation]] with the unitary U.
% For projections, the sign \(\le\) means that the [[subspace]] selected by
% the projection on the left lies inside the subspace selected by P; it is
% an order relation on projections.  Thus every vector selected after this
% move was already fixed by L.  The stable-finiteness argument later upgrades
% this one-way containment to equality in the norm matrix corona.
%</webmath>
\[
 U^*PU\le P.
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/KazhdanProjectionOrderLiteral}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptLiteralMaximalCStarKazhdanProjectionOrder}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Represent $B$ faithfully and nondegenerately on a Hilbert space
$\mathcal H$.  The represented projection $P$ is the orthogonal projection
onto $\operatorname{Fix}\pi(L)$.  If $\xi$ is $L$-fixed and $\ell\in L$,
then
%<webmath object="whole" when="\pi(\ell)U^*\xi" title="why U star of a fixed vector is still fixed">
% The calculation checks whether \(U^*\xi\) is fixed by L.  The first line
% applies \(\pi(\ell)\) to that vector.  The first equality inserts
% \(U^*U=1\) and groups the factors so that \(U\pi(\ell)U^*\) appears.  That
% operator belongs to \(\pi(L)\), and \(\xi\) is fixed by every operator in
% \(\pi(L)\), so it can be removed when acting on \(\xi\).  The final result
% is \(U^*\xi\), exactly the equation required for an L-fixed vector.
%</webmath>
\[
 \pi(\ell)U^*\xi
 =U^*\bigl(U\pi(\ell)U^*\bigr)\xi
 =U^*\xi,
\]
because $U\pi(\ell)U^*$ belongs to $\pi(L)$.  Hence
$U^*\operatorname{Fix}\pi(L)\subseteq\operatorname{Fix}\pi(L)$, so the
range projection $U^*PU$ is dominated by $P$ in $B(\mathcal H)$.  Faithfulness
of the representation gives the same projection inequality in $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[one-sided Kazhdan transport]\label{thm:transport}
Let $L\le G$ have property~\textup{(T)}, and let $u\in G$ satisfy
$uLu^{-1}\le L$.  Let $(V_n)$ be an operator norm asymptotic
representation of $G$.  Then
%<webmath object="whole" when="\operatorname{Ad}(V(u))\bigl(\mathcal C_2(V,L)\bigr)" title="conjugation by V of u preserves the entire asymptotic commutant">
% The notation \(\operatorname{Ad}(V(u))\) means conjugate each matrix
% sequence by the matrices assigned to u.  Its input here is the full
% [[asymptotic commutant]] \(\mathcal C_2(V,L)\): bounded sequences that
% almost commute with the represented subgroup L in normalized
% [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm]].  The equality says conjugation carries this set
% onto itself.  Both directions matter: every eligible sequence remains
% eligible after conjugation, and every eligible sequence is the conjugate of
% another eligible sequence.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}(V(u))\bigl(\mathcal C_2(V,L)\bigr)
 =\mathcal C_2(V,L).
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/TransportCommutantEquality}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptOneSidedKazhdanTransportCommutantEquality}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x=(x_n)\in\mathcal C_2(V,L)$ and let $\omega$ be a free ultrafilter.
Regard $M_{d_n}(\C)$ as a Hilbert
space with its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt inner product, and form the
ultraproduct of Hilbert spaces $\mathcal K_\omega$.  Conjugation by the $V_n(g)$
defines a unitary representation $\sigma$ of $G$ on $\mathcal K_\omega$.
Indeed, for unitaries $A,B$ one has
%<webmath object="whole" when="\opnorm{\operatorname{Ad}(A)-\operatorname{Ad}(B)}" title="close unitaries give close conjugation operators">
% The left side measures the operator-norm distance between two operations on
% matrices: conjugation by A and conjugation by B.  The right side is twice
% the operator-norm distance between A and B themselves.  The inequality says
% that changing the conjugating unitary by an error of size \(\varepsilon\)
% changes the entire conjugation operation by at most \(2\varepsilon\).  This
% bound transfers the multiplication errors of the maps \(V_n\) to the
% corresponding conjugation actions.
%</webmath>
\[
 \opnorm{\operatorname{Ad}(A)-\operatorname{Ad}(B)}
 \le2\opnorm{A-B},
\]
so the operator norm multiplicative defects of $(V_n)$ also make the
conjugation actions multiplicative modulo $c_0$.  The class
$\xi=[x_n]_\omega$ is fixed by $\sigma(L)$.

For $g\in G$, let $\widetilde\sigma(g)$ be the class of the coordinate
operators $\operatorname{Ad}(V_n(g))$ in the norm matrix corona
%<webmath object="whole" when="\mathcal B=\prod_nB(M_{d_n}(\C))" title="the corona of operators acting on the matrix spaces">
% The symbol \(\mathcal B\) names a [[norm matrix corona]].  At stage n,
% \(B(M_{d_n}(\mathbb C))\) is the set of all linear operators acting on the
% vector space of \(d_n\)-by-\(d_n\) complex matrices.  The product contains
% bounded sequences of such operators.  The direct sum contains the sequences
% whose operator norms tend to zero.  Taking the [[quotient]] treats two
% bounded operator sequences as equal when their difference tends to zero in
% operator norm.
%</webmath>
\[
 \mathcal B=\prod_nB(M_{d_n}(\C))\big/\bigoplus_nB(M_{d_n}(\C)).
\]
After choosing matrix units on each $M_{d_n}(\C)$, this is a norm matrix
corona with coordinate sizes $d_n^2$.
The preceding estimate shows directly that
$g\mapsto\widetilde\sigma(g)$ is an exact homomorphism
$G\to\U(\mathcal B)$.  Its restriction to $L$ therefore extends to a
$*$-homomorphism $C^*_{\max}(L)\to\mathcal B$.  Let $P\in\mathcal B$ be the
image of the Kazhdan projection~\cite{AkemannWalter}.  Under the natural
representation of $\mathcal B$ on $\mathcal K_\omega$, its range is
$\operatorname{Fix}\sigma(L)$.  This representation of $\mathcal B$ need
not be faithful; it is used only after the following projection identity has
been proved inside $\mathcal B$.  Since
$uLu^{-1}\le L$, the unitary $U=\widetilde\sigma(u)$ satisfies the
hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:kazhdan-projection-order}, and hence
$U^*PU\le P$ inside $\mathcal B$.
The two projections are unitarily equivalent.  Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-finite}
therefore gives $U^*PU=P$, so $U$ commutes with $P$.  Hence both $U\xi$ and
$U^*\xi$ are fixed by $\sigma(L)$.  Since $\omega$ was arbitrary, the
corresponding Hilbert--Schmidt commutators converge to zero along the full
sequence.
Thus $\operatorname{Ad}(V(u))$ and its inverse both preserve
$\mathcal C_2(V,L)$, which proves the equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:defect-hs}
Let $L\le G$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let $uLu^{-1}\le L$, and let
$c\in C_G(L)$.  Then
%<webmath object="whole" when="[ucu^{-1},\ell]\in R_{\infty\to2}(G)" title="the compression commutator is invisible in every Hilbert--Schmidt shadow">
% For each \(\ell\in L\), form the [[commutator]] of \(ucu^{-1}\) and
% \(\ell\).  Membership in \(R_{\infty\to2}(G)\) says that every operator-norm
% [[asymptotic representation]] sends this group element toward the identity
% in normalized [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm]].  The conclusion holds because c
% commutes with L before conjugation and the transport theorem says
% conjugation by the one-sided compressor u preserves the Hilbert–Schmidt
% asymptotic commutant.
%</webmath>
\[
 [ucu^{-1},\ell]\in R_{\infty\to2}(G)
 \qquad(\ell\in L).
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/DefectHS}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptCompressionDefectHSInvisible}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $(V_n)$ be an operator norm asymptotic representation.  If
$c\in C_G(L)$, then $(V_n(c))\in\mathcal C_2(V,L)$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}, the same is true of
$(V_n(u)V_n(c)V_n(u)^*)$.  Asymptotic multiplicativity identifies this
sequence in operator norm with $(V_n(ucu^{-1}))$; explicitly,
%<webmath object="whole" when="\opnorm{V_n(u)V_n(c)V_n(u)^*-V_n(ucu^{-1})}" title="the matrix product approximates the matrix assigned to the group product">
% The first matrix product uses the separate assignments to u, c, and
% \(u^{-1}\); because \(V_n(u)^*\) is the inverse of the unitary \(V_n(u)\),
% it represents the conjugation pattern.  The second matrix is assigned
% directly to the group element \(ucu^{-1}\).  The expression inside the
% norm is their [[multiplication error]].  The arrow to zero says the two
% descriptions become arbitrarily close in [[operator norm]] as n grows,
% which follows by applying asymptotic multiplicativity a fixed number of
% times.
%</webmath>
\[
 \opnorm{V_n(u)V_n(c)V_n(u)^*-V_n(ucu^{-1})}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore
%<webmath object="whole" when="\hsnorm{V_n([ucu^{-1},\ell])-1}" title="the represented commutator approaches the identity in Hilbert--Schmidt norm">
% The matrix \(V_n([ucu^{-1},\ell])\) is the nth assignment to the displayed
% group [[commutator]].  Subtracting the identity matrix 1 measures how far
% that assignment is from being trivial.  The double bars use normalized
% [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm]], and the arrow to zero says this distance becomes
% arbitrarily small for every fixed \(\ell\in L\).  Since the asymptotic
% representation was arbitrary, this convergence proves that each
% commutator belongs to the universal infinity-to-two kernel.
%</webmath>
\[
 \hsnorm{V_n([ucu^{-1},\ell])-1}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\ell\in L).
\]
Intersecting over $(V_n)$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normal Kazhdan subgroups and the MF radical}

Let $K\trianglelefteq G$ have property~\textup{(T)}, and let $p$ be the
image of its Kazhdan projection under a corona representation of $G$.
Normality of $K$ implies that $p$ commutes with the image of $G$.  Hence
$q=1-p$ also commutes with that image, and in every nondegenerate
representation of the corner $q\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}q$ the induced
representation of $K$ has no nonzero fixed vectors.

\begin{lemma}[central corona corners]\label{lem:central-corona-corner}
Let \(\rho\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})\) be a homomorphism from a
countable group, and let \(q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}\) be a nonzero
projection commuting with \(\rho(G)\).  Then, after passing to an infinite
coordinate subsequence, there are nonzero projections
\(q_n\in M_{d_n}(\C)\), integers \(r_n=\operatorname{rank}(q_n)\), unitary
identifications \(J_n\colon\C^{r_n}\to q_n\C^{d_n}\), and an operator norm
asymptotic representation
%<webmath object="whole" when="W_n\colon G\longrightarrow\U(r_n)" title="the compressed matrices form a standard asymptotic representation">
% For each stage n, the map \(W_n\) assigns every element of G a unitary
% matrix on a space whose dimension is the rank of the [[projection]] \(q_n\).
% The unitary identification \(J_n\) carries this standard matrix space onto
% the range of \(q_n\).  Conjugating by \(J_n\) therefore places \(W_n(g)\)
% inside the [[corner]] \(q_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)q_n\).  The multiplication
% errors of \((W_n)\) tend to zero in operator norm.
%</webmath>
\[
 W_n\colon G\longrightarrow\U(r_n)
\]
with \(W_n(1)=I_{r_n}\).  In the corona over the retained coordinates, the
class of \((J_nW_n(g)J_n^*)\) is the coordinate restriction of
\(q\rho(g)\).
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/CentralCoronaCorner}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptCentralCoronaCorner}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lift \(q\) to projections \(q_n\), and lift each \(\rho(g)\) to unitaries
\(U_n(g)\), choosing \(U_n(1)=I_{d_n}\).  The first lift is obtained by
spectral rounding; the second is obtained by polar correction, since the two
unitarity defects of any lift converge to zero in operator norm.  The
commutation relation in the corona gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\opnorm{q_nU_n(g)-U_n(g)q_n}" title="the lifted projection and lifted representation almost commute">
% The two products differ only in the order of \(q_n\) and \(U_n(g)\).
% Their difference is therefore the [[commutator]] of those matrices, written
% without bracket notation.  The operator norm tending to zero says that the
% two orders become arbitrarily close at late stages.  This follows because q
% commutes exactly with \(\rho(g)\) in the quotient corona: any failure of
% their chosen lifts to commute represents the zero corona class and hence
% must vanish in operator norm.
%</webmath>
\[
 \opnorm{q_nU_n(g)-U_n(g)q_n}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(g\in G).
\]
Consequently \(q_nU_n(g)q_n\), viewed on \(q_n\C^{d_n}\), has both unitarity
defects converging to zero.  For each fixed \(g\), let
\(\widetilde W_n(g)\) be its polar correction whenever both defects are at
most \(1/2\), and set \(\widetilde W_n(g)=q_n\) at the finitely many earlier
stages.  Then \(\widetilde W_n(g)\) is unitary in the corner and satisfies
%<webmath object="whole" when="\opnorm{\widetilde W_n(g)-q_nU_n(g)q_n}" title="polar correction changes the compressed matrix by a vanishing amount">
% The matrix \(q_nU_n(g)q_n\) is the direct compression of the lifted unitary
% to the range of \(q_n\), but at a finite stage it may not be exactly
% unitary on that range.  [[polar correction|Polar correction]] replaces it by the exactly
% unitary matrix \(\widetilde W_n(g)\).  The displayed operator norm is the size of the
% change made by that correction.  Its convergence to zero says the exact
% unitary replacement represents the same [[corona class]] as the original
% compressed sequence.
%</webmath>
\[
 \opnorm{\widetilde W_n(g)-q_nU_n(g)q_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For \(g=1\), the compression is already the identity \(q_n\) of the corner,
so take \(\widetilde W_n(1)=q_n\).  Since \(q\ne0\), infinitely many
\(q_n\) are nonzero.  Retain and relabel those coordinates.  Put
\(r_n=\operatorname{rank}(q_n)\), choose a unitary
\(J_n\colon\C^{r_n}\to q_n\C^{d_n}\), and define
\[
 W_n(g)=J_n^*\widetilde W_n(g)J_n\in\U(r_n).
\]
Conjugation by \(J_n\) preserves operator norms and multiplicative defects.
Thus \((W_n)\) is an operator norm asymptotic representation with
\(W_n(1)=I_{r_n}\).  The displayed estimate identifies the class of
\((J_nW_n(g)J_n^*)\) with the coordinate restriction of \(q\rho(g)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[normal Kazhdan radical theorem]\label{thm:normal-kazhdan}
Let \(G\) be countable and let \(K\trianglelefteq G\) have
property~\textup{(T)} with \(K\le R_{\infty\to2}(G)\).  Then
%<webmath object="whole" when="K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)" title="the normal property-(T) subgroup is erased by every MF model">
% The sign \(\le\) states that the subgroup K is contained in the [[MF
% radical]] of G.  By definition, this means every [[homomorphism]] from G to
% every [[norm matrix corona]] sends each element of K to the identity.  The
% hypotheses supply two ingredients: K has [[property (T)]], and K lies in
% the universal infinity-to-two kernel.  The proof uses property (T) to turn
% Hilbert–Schmidt invisibility into total invisibility in every operator-norm
% matrix model.
%</webmath>
\[
 K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G).
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/NormalKazhdan}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptNormalKazhdanRadical}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that a homomorphism
\(\Theta\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})\) is nontrivial on \(K\).
Replace \(G\) by \(\Theta(G)\), replace \(K\) by \(\Theta(K)\), and denote
the resulting inclusion into the corona again by \(\Theta\).  The new \(K\)
is a nontrivial normal property-\textup{(T)} subgroup, since
property~\textup{(T)} passes to quotients.  Every one of its
elements remains in the universal Hilbert--Schmidt kernel, because any
operator norm asymptotic representation of \(\Theta(G)\), precomposed with
the quotient map from the original group, is such a representation of the
original group.

Let \(p_K\in C^*_{\max}(K)\) be the Kazhdan projection and let \(p\) be its
image in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}\).  Under any faithful representation of the
corona, \(p\) is the projection onto the \(K\)-fixed vectors.
Normality of \(K\) gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\Theta(g)p\Theta(g)^*=p" title="normality makes the Kazhdan projection commute with the whole group">
% The map \(\Theta\) represents G in the norm matrix corona, and p projects
% onto the vectors fixed by K.  Conjugating p by the unitary \(\Theta(g)\)
% gives the projection onto the moved fixed-vector space.  Because K is a
% [[normal subgroup]], conjugation by g rearranges K itself, so the moved
% K-fixed space is the original K-fixed space.  The equality of projections
% states this invariance.  Equivalently, p commutes with every
% \(\Theta(g)\).
%</webmath>
\[
 \Theta(g)p\Theta(g)^*=p
 \qquad(g\in G).
\]
Indeed, in a faithful representation of the corona the two projections have
ranges \(\Theta(g)\operatorname{Fix}\Theta(K)\) and
\(\operatorname{Fix}\Theta(K)\); these agree because \(gKg^{-1}=K\).
Put \(q=1-p\).  The projection \(q\) is nonzero: if \(p=1\), then every vector
is fixed by \(K\), so every element of \(K\) has trivial corona image.
Lemma~\ref{lem:central-corona-corner}
gives positive integers \(r_n\) and an operator norm asymptotic representation
\(W_n\colon G\to\U(r_n)\).  Let
\(\widehat\Theta\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf r})\) be its corona
homomorphism.  The coordinatewise corner identifications carry
\(\widehat\Theta\) to the coordinate restriction of
\(g\mapsto q\Theta(g)\).

Choose a finite Kazhdan set \(S\subseteq K\) and a Kazhdan
constant \(\kappa>0\).  The Kazhdan projection of \(K\) under
\(\widehat\Theta\) is zero.  Hence the positive element
%<webmath object="whole" when="b=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}" title="b averages the squared movement caused by the Kazhdan set">
% For each element s in the finite [[Kazhdan set]] S, the operator
% \(\widehat\Theta(s)-1\) measures how far the compressed representation of s
% is from the identity.  Multiplying its [[adjoint]] by itself produces
% a positive operator measuring squared movement.  The summation adds these
% operators for every s in S, and division by \(|S|\), the number of elements
% in S, takes their average.  The result b is therefore the average squared
% displacement operator for the chosen Kazhdan set.
%</webmath>
\[
 b=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}
       (\widehat\Theta(s)-1)^*(\widehat\Theta(s)-1)
\]
satisfies
%<webmath object="whole" when="b\ge\frac{\kappa^2}{|S|}\,1" title="the average movement has a uniform positive lower bound">
% The inequality compares positive operators in the relabelled matrix corona.
% The right side is the identity multiplied by the positive number
% \(\kappa^2/|S|\), where \(\kappa\) is the [[Kazhdan constant]] and \(|S|\)
% is the size of the finite Kazhdan set.  Saying b is at least this operator
% means every vector is moved, on average, by a squared amount
% bounded below by that constant times its squared length.  This lower bound
% follows because the representation contains no nonzero K-fixed vector.
%</webmath>
\[
 b\ge\frac{\kappa^2}{|S|}\,1.
\]
Represent \(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf r}\) faithfully.  Because the image of the
Kazhdan projection is zero, \(\widehat\Theta|_K\) has no nonzero fixed
vectors.  The defining Kazhdan inequality therefore gives this operator
inequality.

The coordinate elements
%<webmath object="whole" when="b_n=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}(W_n(s)-I_{r_n})^*" title="b sub n is the finite-stage version of the average movement operator">
% This repeats the definition of b at matrix stage n.  The identity is
% now \(I_{r_n}\), and \(W_n(s)\) is the exactly unitary compressed matrix
% assigned to s.  For each s, the product
% \((W_n(s)-I_{r_n})^*(W_n(s)-I_{r_n})\) is a positive matrix measuring squared
% displacement.  Summing over the finite set S and dividing by its size gives
% the average \(b_n\).  The sequence \((b_n)\) represents b in the quotient
% corona because the \(W_n(s)\) represent the compressed action.
%</webmath>
\[
 b_n=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}
       (W_n(s)-I_{r_n})^*(W_n(s)-I_{r_n})
\]
represent \(b\).  Taking the normalized trace on \(M_{r_n}(\C)\) yields
%<webmath object="whole" when="\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\hsnorm{W_n(s)-I_{r_n}}^{\,2}" title="the average squared Hilbert--Schmidt movement stays positive">
% Each squared norm on the left is the normalized [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm]] of
% \(W_n(s)-I_{r_n}\), squared.  Averaging over s gives the normalized trace of
% \(b_n\).  The right side is the fixed positive number
% \(\kappa^2/|S|\) minus an error denoted \(o(1)\), meaning a numerical
% sequence that tends to zero.  Thus the average squared distance from the
% corner identity cannot tend to zero.  Consequently, after choosing a
% subsequence, at least one fixed element of S remains a definite distance
% from the identity.
%</webmath>
\[
 \frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\hsnorm{W_n(s)-I_{r_n}}^{\,2}
 \ge \frac{\kappa^2}{|S|}-o(1).
\]
The order inequality for $b$ says that the negative part of
$b_n-(\kappa^2/|S|)I_{r_n}$ converges to zero in operator norm.  Taking normalized
traces gives the displayed inequality.
After passing to a subsequence, one fixed \(s_0\in S\) therefore stays a
positive Hilbert--Schmidt distance from \(I_{r_n}\).  This
contradicts \(s_0\in K\le R_{\infty\to2}(G)\), established after passing to
\(\Theta(G)\).  Thus every corona homomorphism kills \(K\), as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion}]
For every $u\in\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)$, $c\in C_G(L)$, and $\ell\in L$,
Corollary~\ref{cor:defect-hs} gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="[ucu^{-1},\ell]\in R_{\infty\to2}(G)" title="each generator of the defect is universally Hilbert--Schmidt trivial">
% The element inside brackets is one of the [[commutator|commutators]] used to
% generate \(\mathfrak D_G(L)\).  The membership sign says this element lies
% in the universal infinity-to-two kernel: every operator-norm asymptotic
% representation sends it toward the identity in normalized
% [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm]].  The statement applies to every permitted u, c,
% and \(\ell\).  It is exactly the conclusion of the preceding corollary,
% now applied simultaneously to all generators of the defect subgroup.
%</webmath>
\[
 [ucu^{-1},\ell]\in R_{\infty\to2}(G).
\]
Normality of \(R_{\infty\to2}(G)\) therefore gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\mathfrak D_G(L)\le R_{\infty\to2}(G)" title="the entire defect subgroup is universally Hilbert--Schmidt trivial">
% The left side is the [[normal closure]] of all compression commutators from
% the preceding display.  Every one of those generators lies in
% \(R_{\infty\to2}(G)\), and that kernel is a [[normal subgroup]], so it also
% contains all products, inverses, and conjugates used to form their normal
% closure.  The containment therefore says every element of the full defect
% subgroup is sent toward the identity in normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm by
% every operator-norm asymptotic representation.
%</webmath>
\[
 \mathfrak D_G(L)\le R_{\infty\to2}(G).
\]
Since \(K\le\mathfrak D_G(L)\), Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan} now gives
\(K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)\).  Taking a nontrivial $K$ gives the non-MF
conclusion.  Taking $K=G=\mathfrak D_G(L)$ gives
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=G$.

For a finite-dimensional linear representation over an arbitrary field,
Theorem~\ref{thm:commutant} kills every generator of
\(\mathfrak D_G(L)\).  Its kernel is normal, so it contains
\(\mathfrak D_G(L)\).
\end{proof}

\section{The binary Leavitt group}\label{sec:leavitt}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}.  The defining relation
$R\cong R^2$ of the binary Leavitt algebra supplies the one-sided
self-compression required by Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion};
simplicity of $\EL_{12}(R)$ then turns a single invisible commutator into
a full MF radical.

\subsection{The self-compression}

Throughout this section $R=L_{\F_2}(1,2)$ and
%<webmath object="whole" when="p=s_0t_0" title="the two complementary Leavitt pieces are named p and q">
% This display introduces abbreviations.  The element p is the product
% \(s_0t_0\), and q is the product \(s_1t_1\), inside the binary [[Leavitt
% algebra]] R.  The subscripts 0 and 1 are [[index|indices]], not powers.
% Writing the letters next to one another means multiplication in the given
% order.  The defining Leavitt relation says these two elements add to the
% identity, so they behave as complementary idempotent pieces used by the
% compression construction.
%</webmath>
\[
 p=s_0t_0,\qquad q=s_1t_1.
\]
The relations \eqref{eq:leavitt} give
%<webmath object="whole" when="p+q=1" title="p and q split the identity, and q contains a copy of the identity">
% The first equation says the two Leavitt elements p and q add to the
% [[identity]] 1.  The second says that multiplying q on the left by \(t_1\)
% and on the right by \(s_1\) gives 1.  Using \(q=s_1t_1\), it becomes
% \(t_1s_1t_1s_1=1\), which follows from \(t_1s_1=1\).  In particular q
% cannot be zero.  The two facts let q provide the unchanged part of the
% matrix compression while the p part carries the compressed copy.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pq}
 p+q=1,\qquad t_1qs_1=1.
\end{equation}
In particular $q\ne0$.

We use indices $0,\ldots,11$ for $12\times12$ matrices.  We identify
$\GL_3(R)$ with the subgroup of $\GL_{12}(R)$ consisting of the matrices
$\operatorname{diag}(A,I_9)$.  Define a map
$\Psi\colon M_3(R)\to M_3(R)$ by
%<webmath object="whole" when="\Psi(A)=qI_3+s_0A t_0" title="Psi compresses A into one Leavitt piece and fills the other with identity">
% The Greek letter \(\Psi\), read “psi,” names a map from 3-by-3 matrices to
% 3-by-3 matrices.  The term \(s_0At_0\) multiplies every entry of A by
% \(s_0\) on the left and \(t_0\) on the right, placing a copy of A in the p
% part of the Leavitt algebra.  The term \(qI_3\) places q on the three
% diagonal positions and zero elsewhere.  Adding it supplies the unchanged q
% part, making the map send the identity matrix to the identity matrix.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-compression}
 \Psi(A)=qI_3+s_0A t_0.
\end{equation}
Here $s_0At_0$ denotes the matrix $(s_0a_{ij}t_0)_{ij}$.  The relations
$q^2=q$, $qs_0=t_0q=0$, and $t_0s_0=1$, all consequences of
\eqref{eq:leavitt}, show that $\Psi$ is multiplicative.  Moreover,
\[
 \Psi(I_3)=qI_3+s_0I_3t_0=qI_3+pI_3=(p+q)I_3=I_3,
\]
so $\Psi$ preserves the identity.  Finally, $t_0\Psi(A)s_0=A$, and hence
$\Psi$ is injective.  These identities show that $\Psi$ is injective, multiplicative, and
identity preserving, so its restriction to $\GL_3(R)$ is an injective
group homomorphism $\GL_3(R)\to\GL_3(R)$.

To realize $\Psi$ by conjugation on the embedded copy of $\GL_3(R)$,
define the following $6\times6$ block matrices:
%<webmath object="whole" when="X=\begin{pmatrix}s_0I_3" title="the block matrices X and Y that implement the compression">
% X and Y are 6-by-6 [[block matrix|block matrices]], written as 2-by-2 arrays
% of 3-by-3 blocks.  They are not arbitrary.  The entries involving
% \(s_0\) and \(t_0\) place A in the compressed p part, while the entries
% involving \(s_1\) and \(t_1\) supply the complementary q part.  The
% Leavitt relations make X and Y mutual inverses.  More importantly,
% multiplying \(X\operatorname{diag}(A,I_3)Y\) produces
% \(\operatorname{diag}(\Psi(A),I_3)\).  Thus X is a change of coordinates
% that performs the compression \(A\mapsto\Psi(A)\).
%</webmath>
\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}s_0I_3&s_1t_0I_3\\0&t_1I_3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}t_0I_3&0\\s_0t_1I_3&s_1I_3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A block multiplication using \eqref{eq:leavitt} gives $XY=YX=I_6$, so
$Y=X^{-1}$.  Put
%<webmath object="whole" when="\tau=\operatorname{diag}(X,Y)" title="tau places X and its inverse on the block diagonal">
% The matrix \(\tau\) puts the 6-by-6 change-of-coordinates matrix X and its
% inverse Y on two diagonal blocks.  The doubling is deliberate: X by itself
% need not belong to an [[elementary group]], but the standard Whitehead
% construction makes \(\operatorname{diag}(X,X^{-1})\) a product of
% [[elementary matrix|elementary matrices]].  Thus the extra six coordinates
% turn the desired change of coordinates into an allowed element of
% \(\operatorname{EL}_{12}(R)\).
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 \tau=\operatorname{diag}(X,Y)\in\GL_{12}(R).
\end{equation}

A direct block multiplication gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\tau\,\operatorname{diag}(A,I_9)\,\tau^{-1}" title="tau turns A into its compressed copy Psi(A)">
% The middle matrix places A in the first 3-by-3 block and acts as the
% [[identity matrix]] on the other nine coordinates.  Conjugating it by tau
% applies X and its inverse to the first six coordinates.  Direct block
% multiplication gives \(qI_3+s_0At_0=\Psi(A)\) in the first block and
% leaves the other blocks equal to the identity.  This is the central payoff
% of the construction: the abstract compression map Psi is now performed by
% conjugation inside a matrix group.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner-conjugation}
 \tau\,\operatorname{diag}(A,I_9)\,\tau^{-1}
 =\operatorname{diag}(\Psi(A),I_9).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tau-elementary}
The matrix $\tau$ defined in \eqref{eq:tau} belongs to $\EL_{12}(R)$.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/RankTwelveConfiguration}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.RankTwelveEndpoint.printedTauGL_mem}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $I=I_6$.  The Whitehead factorization gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\operatorname{diag}(X,X^{-1})" title="the block diagonal matrix is factored into six elementary block matrices">
% This display is a membership certificate, not another compression formula.
% Its left side is tau because \(Y=X^{-1}\).  Every factor on the right has
% identity blocks on the diagonal and a single off-diagonal block.  Such a
% [[block-unipotent]] matrix is a product of ordinary [[elementary
% matrix|elementary matrices]], one for each entry of that off-diagonal
% block.  Therefore the six factors prove that tau is built entirely from
% elementary matrices and hence belongs to \(\operatorname{EL}_{12}(R)\).
%</webmath>
\begin{align}\label{eq:whitehead-factorization}
 \operatorname{diag}(X,X^{-1})
 &=\begin{pmatrix}I&X\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\-X^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&X\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\I&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\I&I\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
If $B=(b_{rs})_{r,s=0}^5\in M_6(R)$, then
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}I&B\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
   =\prod_{r,s=0}^5e_{r,6+s}(b_{rs}),
 \qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\B&I\end{pmatrix}
   =\prod_{r,s=0}^5e_{6+r,s}(b_{rs}).
\]
In either product, distinct off-diagonal matrix units have product zero, so
no cross-terms occur.  Every factor on the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:whitehead-factorization} therefore belongs to $\EL_{12}(R)$.
Since $Y=X^{-1}$, its left-hand side is $\tau$, and the result follows.
\end{proof}

Set $H=\EL_{12}(R)$, and let
%<webmath object="EL3" when="L=\EL_3(R)" title="the 3-by-3 matrix collection L">
% This equation defines L to be \(\EL_3(R)\), the [[elementary group]] made
% from 3-by-3 [[elementary matrix|elementary matrices]] over the [[ring]] R.
% It is embedded in H by placing a 3-by-3 matrix in the upper-left block and
% the 9-by-9 identity in the complementary block.  The symbol \(\le\) means
% that the resulting image is a [[subgroup]] of H.
%</webmath>
$L=\EL_3(R)\le H$ denote the image of the embedding
$A\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(A,I_9)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:leavitt-compression}
The groups $H$ and $L$ have property~\textup{(T)}, and $\tau\in H$ satisfies
%<webmath object="whole" when="\tau L\tau^{-1}\le L" title="tau sends every element of L back inside L">
% The product \(\tau L\tau^{-1}\) means conjugate every element of the
% subgroup L by tau.  The containment sign says every result still lies in L;
% equality is not claimed.  It follows because L is generated by the
% elementary matrices \(e_{ij}(a)\), and the preceding calculation sends
% each generator to \(e_{ij}(s_0at_0)\), another elementary matrix in L.
% This one-way self-compression is the hypothesis needed by the property-(T)
% transport argument.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compresses-L}
 \tau L\tau^{-1}\le L.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The ring $R$ is generated by $s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1$ as a unital ring.  The theorem
of Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain therefore gives property~\textup{(T)} for
$\EL_{12}(R)$ and $\EL_3(R)$~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.  Lemma
\ref{lem:tau-elementary} gives $\tau\in H$.

For $i\ne j$ and $a\in R$, equations \eqref{eq:corner-conjugation} and
\eqref{eq:matrix-compression} give
\[
 \tau e_{ij}(a)\tau^{-1}=e_{ij}(s_0at_0)\in L,
\]
where the elementary matrices are viewed in the embedded copy of $L$.
These matrices generate $L$, so \eqref{eq:compresses-L} follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Simplicity and the full MF radical}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:simple}
The group $H=\EL_{12}(R)$ is nontrivial and simple.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/RankTwelveSimplicity}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.RankTwelveEndpoint.manuscriptPropositionSimple}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The ring $R$ is purely infinite simple~\cite{AbramsAranda}, and therefore is
an exchange ring~\cite{AraExchange}.  Let $N\trianglelefteq H$.  Since
$H=\EL_{12}(R)$, the subgroup $N\le\GL_{12}(R)$ is normalized by
$\EL_{12}(R)$.  Preusser's sandwich theorem~\cite[Theorem~3]{Preusser}
provides an ideal $I\trianglelefteq R$ such that
\[
 \EL_{12}(R,I)\le N\le C_{12}(R,I).
\]
Simplicity of $R$ gives $I=0$ or $I=R$.  If $I=R$, then
$\EL_{12}(R,I)=H$, so $N=H$.

Suppose that $I=0$.  Then $N\le C_{12}(R,0)=Z(\GL_{12}(R))$.  A matrix that
commutes with every $e_{ij}(1)$ is a scalar matrix $\lambda I_{12}$.
Commutation with $e_{ij}(a)$ for arbitrary $a\in R$ then gives
$\lambda a=a\lambda$, so $\lambda\in Z(R)^\times$.  Since
$Z(R)=\F_2$~\cite[Corollary~4.3]{ArandaCrow}, we have $\lambda=1$ and
$N=1$.  Finally, $e_{01}(1)\ne1$, so $H$ is nontrivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:defect}
Let
%<webmath object="whole" when="c=e_{34}(1)" title="the concrete elements c, ell, and d used in the defect">
% This display defines three elements of H.  The elementary matrix c has its
% extra 1 in row 3, column 4, while \(\ell\) has its extra 1 in row 1,
% column 2.  Their separated positions make c commute with the upper-left
% 3-by-3 subgroup L.  The element d is the [[commutator]] of \(\tau
% c\tau^{-1}\) with \(\ell\).  Thus d is exactly one of the generators
% allowed in the compression-centralizer defect associated with L.
%</webmath>
\[
 c=e_{34}(1),\qquad \ell=e_{12}(1),\qquad
 d=[\tau c\tau^{-1},\ell]
\]
in $H$.  Then
%<webmath object="d" when="d=e_{02}(q)" title="three facts about c, d, and H">
% The three statements are separated by commas.  First,
% \(c\in C_H(L)\) says c belongs to the [[centralizer]] of L in H.  Second,
% \(d=e_{02}(q)\ne1\) says the [[commutator]] d is an [[elementary matrix]]
% with q in row 0, column 2 and is not the [[identity]].  Third,
% \(\normal d_H=H\) says the [[normal closure]] of d is the whole [[group]] H.
% The explicit formula shows that d is nontrivial, and simplicity of H then
% proves the last statement.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="e02" when="d=e_{02}(q)" title="the identity matrix with q added in row 0, column 2">
% \(e_{02}(q)\) is an [[elementary matrix]]: the 12-by-12 [[identity matrix]]
% with one additional entry q in row 0, column 2.  The subscript 02 gives those
% two [[index|indices]].  Because \(q\ne0\), the extra entry is nonzero and the
% matrix is not the identity.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="q" when="d=e_{02}(q)" title="why the extra matrix entry q is not zero">
% The letter q abbreviates the [[Leavitt algebra]] product \(s_1t_1\).  If q
% were zero, multiplying it on the left by \(t_1\) and on the right by \(s_1\)
% would still give zero.  But the defining equations give \(t_1qs_1=1\), so
% q cannot be zero.  Its [[nonzero]] [[off-diagonal|position away from the main
% diagonal]] makes d different from the [[identity matrix]].
%</webmath>
\[
 c\in C_H(L),\qquad d=e_{02}(q)\ne1,
 \qquad \normal d_H=H.
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/RankTwelveConfiguration}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.RankTwelveEndpoint.manuscriptPropositionDefect}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $i,j\in\{0,1,2\}$ with $i\ne j$ and $a\in R$, we have $j\ne3$ and
$i\ne4$.  Hence the Steinberg relations give
\[
 [e_{ij}(a),e_{34}(1)]=1.
\]
These matrices generate $L$, so $c=e_{34}(1)$ belongs to $C_H(L)$.

Direct multiplication of the matrices in \eqref{eq:tau} gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\tau c\tau^{-1}=e_{01}(q)e_{34}(1)" title="conjugating c by tau produces a second elementary transvection">
% The left side conjugates the elementary matrix c by the explicit matrix
% tau.  Direct block multiplication yields the product of two elementary
% matrices on the right.  The factor \(e_{01}(q)\) has the Leavitt element q
% in row 0, column 1; the factor \(e_{34}(1)\) is the original c.  These two
% factors commute because their row and column positions do not meet.  In the
% next commutator with \(e_{12}(1)\), the second factor disappears and the
% first produces \(e_{02}(q)\).
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moved-mark}
 \tau c\tau^{-1}=e_{01}(q)e_{34}(1).
\end{equation}
The second factor commutes with $\ell=e_{12}(1)$.  The identity
$[e_{ij}(a),e_{jk}(b)]=e_{ik}(ab)$ therefore gives $d=e_{02}(q)$, which is
nontrivial because $q\ne0$.  Its normal closure is a nontrivial normal
subgroup of $H$, and hence is all of $H$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:simple}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}]
The ring $R$ is a finitely generated $\F_2$-algebra, so $H$ is countable,
and $H$ has property~\textup{(T)} by the Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain theorem
for elementary groups over finitely generated rings~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.

The containment \eqref{eq:compresses-L}, the centrality of $c$ relative to
$L$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:defect} show that
$d\in\mathfrak D_H(L)$.  The subgroup $\mathfrak D_H(L)$ is normal and
Proposition~\ref{prop:defect} gives $\normal d_H=H$, so
$\mathfrak D_H(L)=H$.  The group $H$ has property~\textup{(T)}, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion}, applied with $K=H$, gives
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:simple} shows that $H$ is nontrivial and simple.

If $M$ is MF and $f\colon H\to M$ is a homomorphism, compose $f$ with a
faithful corona embedding of $M$.  Since
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H$, this composition and hence $f$ are trivial.  If
$H$ were MF, its identity homomorphism would contradict this conclusion.

Finally, $C^*_{\mathrm r}(H)$ is separable because $H$ is countable.  Its
canonical trace is faithful, so it is stably finite.  If an MF embedding
$e\colon C^*_{\mathrm r}(H)\to\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}$ existed and $p=e(1)$,
then $h\mapsto e(\lambda_h)+(1-p)$ would embed $H$ in
$\mathcal U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})$.  This contradicts the fact that $H$ is
not MF.
\end{proof}

\section{Operator norm models on finite sets}\label{sec:local}

The MF property is defined through sequences of models on all of $G$,
but it is determined by finite data with a fixed separation constant.
This section proves that reformulation and derives the upper bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:recognition} from it.

\begin{lemma}[locality with fixed separation]\label{lem:local-models}
A countable group $G$ is MF if and only if for every finite set
$F\subseteq G$ containing $1$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there are $d\ge1$
and a map $V\colon F\to\U(d)$ with $V(1)=1$ such that
\begin{gather*}
 \opnorm{V(gh)-V(g)V(h)}\le\varepsilon\quad\text{whenever }g,h,gh\in F,\\
 \opnorm{V(g)-1}\ge1\quad\text{for }g\in F\setminus\{1\}.
\end{gather*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that every finite $F\ni1$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ admit
such a map.  Enumerate $G=\{g_0=1,g_1,g_2,\dots\}$, let
$F_n=\{g_ig_j:i,j\le n\}\cup\{g_i:i\le n\}$, let $V_n\colon F_n\to\U(d_n)$
be a map for $(F_n,1/n)$, and extend it to $G$ by $V_n(g)=1$ for
$g\notin F_n$.  For $g,h\in G$ the elements $g$, $h$, $gh$ lie in $F_n$
for all large $n$, so $\opnorm{V_n(gh)-V_n(g)V_n(h)}\le1/n\to0$; and for
$g\ne1$ we have $\opnorm{V_n(g)-1}\ge1$ for all large $n$.  Hence $G$ is
MF.

Conversely, let $V_n\colon G\to\U(d_n)$ be MF models: $V_n(1)=1$, the
multiplication defects tend to zero, and
$c_g=\limsup_n\opnorm{V_n(g)-1}>0$ for every $g\ne1$.  The separation
constants $c_g$ may be arbitrarily small, and we first amplify them.  For
a unitary $u$ and $k\ge1$ the tensor power $u^{\otimes k}$ is a unitary of
dimension $d^k$, $(uv)^{\otimes k}=u^{\otimes k}v^{\otimes k}$, and
telescoping gives $\opnorm{u^{\otimes k}-v^{\otimes k}}\le k\opnorm{u-v}$.
If $\opnorm{u-1}=c>0$, write $c=2\sin(\theta/2)$ with $0<\theta\le\pi$;
then $e^{i\theta}$ or $e^{-i\theta}$ is an eigenvalue of $u$, so
$e^{ik\theta}$ or $e^{-ik\theta}$ is an eigenvalue of $u^{\otimes k}$, and
$\opnorm{u^{\otimes k}-1}\ge\lvert e^{ik\theta}-1\rvert=2\lvert\sin(k\theta/2)\rvert$.
If $\theta\ge\pi/2$ take $k=1$; otherwise the first $k$ with
$k\theta\ge\pi/2$ satisfies $k\theta<\pi/2+\theta<\pi$.  In both cases
$k\theta\in[\pi/2,3\pi/2]$ modulo $2\pi$, so
$\opnorm{u^{\otimes k}-1}\ge\sqrt2$.  Write $k(c)$ for this exponent; it
depends only on $c$.

Now fix a finite $F\ni1$ and $\varepsilon>0$, let
$K=\max_{g\in F\setminus\{1\}}k(c_g)$, and put
\[
 W_n(g)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}V_n(g)^{\otimes k}\in\U(D_n),
 \qquad D_n=\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_n^{k}.
\]
Then $W_n(1)=1$ and
$\opnorm{W_n(gh)-W_n(g)W_n(h)}\le K\opnorm{V_n(gh)-V_n(g)V_n(h)}$, so the
defects of $W_n$ tend to zero.  For $g\in F\setminus\{1\}$ choose a
subsequence along which $\opnorm{V_n(g)-1}\to c_g$ and eigenvalues
$\lambda_n$ of $V_n(g)$ with $\lvert\lambda_n-1\rvert\to c_g$; passing to
a further subsequence, $\lambda_n\to\lambda$ with $\lvert\lambda-1\rvert=c_g$,
and then $\lambda_n^{k}\to\lambda^{k}$ with $\lvert\lambda^{k}-1\rvert\ge\sqrt2$
for $k=k(c_g)$.  Since $\lambda_n^{k}$ is an eigenvalue of
$V_n(g)^{\otimes k}$, it follows that
$\limsup_n\opnorm{W_n(g)-1}\ge\sqrt2>1$.  Choose $n_0$ such that for
$n\ge n_0$ every defect of $W_n$ on $F$ is at most $\varepsilon$, and for
each $g\in F\setminus\{1\}$ choose $m_g\ge n_0$ with
$\opnorm{W_{m_g}(g)-1}\ge1$.  Let $V$ be the restriction to $F$ of
$\bigoplus_{g\in F\setminus\{1\}}W_{m_g}$ (and of $W_{n_0}$ if
$F=\{1\}$).  Then $V(1)=1$, the defect of $V$ on $F$ is the maximum of
the defects of its blocks, hence at most $\varepsilon$, and for
$g\in F\setminus\{1\}$ the block $m_g$ gives $\opnorm{V(g)-1}\ge1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[marked closedness]\label{lem:marked-closed}
The class of MF groups is closed in the space of marked groups.  In
particular, every finitely generated group that is not MF is a quotient
of a finitely presented group that is not MF\@.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An $(F,\varepsilon)$-model in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:local-models}
depends only on the multiplication table of $F$, that is, on which
products $gh$ with $g,h\in F$ lie in $F$ and which elements of $F$ are
equal.  If $G=\langle X\mid N\rangle$ is finitely generated and not MF,
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-models} gives a finite $F\ni1$ and $\varepsilon>0$
with no $(F,\varepsilon)$-model.  Represent the elements of $F$ by words
in $X$; the finitely many equalities among the words and their pairwise
products that hold in $G$ are consequences of finitely many relators
$N_0\subseteq N$, and the inequalities hold in every marked group
mapping onto $G$.  Hence every marked group that agrees with $G$ on the
finitely many words involved carries a table isomorphic to that of $F$,
and so is not MF; this is closedness.  Applied to $\langle X\mid N_0\rangle$,
which maps onto $G$ and carries the same table, it gives the finitely
presented quotient.
\end{proof}

We use the Kleene--Mostowski arithmetical hierarchy in its standard
form~\cite{Soare}: a set $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ is $\Sigma^0_1$ if
$A=\{x:\exists y\,R(x,y)\}$ for a decidable relation $R$, it is
$\Pi^0_1$ if its complement is $\Sigma^0_1$, and $\Sigma^0_2$ and
$\Pi^0_2$ are the classes $\{x:\exists y\,\forall z\,R(x,y,z)\}$ and
$\{x:\forall y\,\exists z\,R(x,y,z)\}$ with $R$ decidable.  Fix an
effective coding of finite presentations by natural numbers, under which a
code determines computably its number of generators and its finite list of
relator words, and write $G_P$ for the group presented by the code $P$.
Let $\mathrm{MF}_{\mathrm{fp}}$ be the set of codes $P$ such that $G_P$ is
MF, and $\mathrm{NONMF}_{\mathrm{fp}}$ its complement.

\begin{proposition}[finite certificates]\label{prop:mf-upper-bound}
There is a decidable relation $C(P,n,c)$ on triples of natural numbers
such that a code $P$ lies in $\mathrm{MF}_{\mathrm{fp}}$ if and only if
for every $n$ there is $c$ with $C(P,n,c)$.  Consequently
$\mathrm{MF}_{\mathrm{fp}}\in\Pi^0_2$ and
$\mathrm{NONMF}_{\mathrm{fp}}\in\Sigma^0_2$.
\leanverified{Computability/MFRecognitionPi02}{GroupApproximation.MFRecognitionPi02.operatorMFCode_pi02}
\leanverified{Computability/MFRecognitionPi02}{GroupApproximation.MFRecognitionPi02.nonOperatorMFCode_sigma02}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $P=\langle x_1,\dots,x_k\mid r_1,\dots,r_m\rangle$.  A
\emph{certificate at scale $n$} is a triple $c=(d,\ell,\pi)$ consisting of
a dimension $d\ge1$, a labelling $\ell$ of every reduced word of length at
most $n$ in $x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_k^{\pm1}$ by one of the symbols
$\mathsf T$ and $\mathsf S$, and, for every word $w$ labelled $\mathsf T$,
an expression of $w$ as a product of conjugates of the relators and their
inverses that freely reduces to $w$.  Such an expression is checkable, and
its existence forces $w=1$ in $G_P$.  Let $\Phi(P,n,c)$ be the statement
that there are $U_1,\dots,U_k\in\U(d)$ with
$\opnorm{r_i(U)-1}\le2^{-n}$ for $i\le m$ and $\opnorm{w(U)-1}\ge1/4$ for
every word $w$ labelled $\mathsf S$, where $w(U)$ is the evaluation of $w$
at $U_1,\dots,U_k$ with $x_j^{-1}\mapsto U_j^{*}$.  Writing
$U_j=A_j+iB_j$ with real matrices, unitarity is a system of polynomial
equations, and a bound $\opnorm{X}\le t$ or $\opnorm{X}\ge t$ on a matrix
whose entries are polynomials in the variables is a semialgebraic
condition, since $\opnorm{X}\le t$ holds precisely when $t^2-X^*X$ is
positive semidefinite.  Thus $\Phi(P,n,c)$ is a sentence of the
first-order theory of the ordered field of real numbers, and its truth is
decidable by Tarski's theorem~\cite{Tarski}.  Let $C(P,n,c)$ hold when $c$
is a well-formed certificate at scale $n$ whose $\mathsf T$-expressions
check and $\Phi(P,n,c)$ holds.  Then $C$ is decidable.

Suppose that $G_P$ is MF.  Fix $n$, let $L=\max(n,\lvert r_1\rvert,\dots,\lvert r_m\rvert)$,
let $F$ be the set of images in $G_P$ of all words of length at most $L$,
together with their inverses, and let $\varepsilon=2^{-n}/(4L)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-models} gives $V\colon F\to\U(d)$ with $V(1)=1$,
defect at most $\varepsilon$ on $F$, and $\opnorm{V(g)-1}\ge1$ for
$g\in F\setminus\{1\}$.  Put $U_j=V(\bar x_j)$, where $\bar w$ denotes
the image of a word $w$ in $G_P$.  For $g\in F$ we have $g^{-1}\in F$ and
$\opnorm{V(g)V(g^{-1})-1}\le\varepsilon$, so
$\opnorm{V(g^{-1})-V(g)^*}\le\varepsilon$.  For a word $w=y_1\cdots y_\ell$
of length $\ell\le L$, all of whose prefixes have images in $F$, induction
on $\ell$ gives $\opnorm{w(U)-V(\bar w)}\le2\ell\varepsilon$.  In
particular $\opnorm{r_i(U)-1}\le2L\varepsilon\le2^{-n}$.  Label a word $w$
of length at most $n$ by $\mathsf T$ if $\bar w=1$, with a normal-closure
expression as witness, and by $\mathsf S$ otherwise; for the latter,
$\opnorm{w(U)-1}\ge\opnorm{V(\bar w)-1}-2n\varepsilon\ge1-\tfrac12\ge\tfrac14$.
So $C(P,n,c)$ holds for this certificate.

Conversely, suppose that for every $n$ some certificate $c_n=(d_n,\ell_n,\pi_n)$
satisfies $C(P,n,c_n)$, and choose $U^{(n)}\in\U(d_n)^k$ witnessing
$\Phi(P,n,c_n)$.  For each $g\in G_P$ fix a reduced word $w_g$ representing $g$,
with $w_1$ the empty word, and put $V_n(g)=w_g(U^{(n)})$.  Then
$V_n(1)=1$.  For $g,h\in G_P$ the word $t=w_gw_hw_{gh}^{-1}$ is trivial in
$G_P$, so it is freely equal to a product of $A$ conjugates of relators and
their inverses, for some $A$ depending on $g$ and $h$ but not on $n$.
Since evaluation is a homomorphism on the free group, since the operator
norm is unitarily invariant, and since
$\opnorm{xy-1}\le\opnorm{x-1}+\opnorm{y-1}$ for unitaries $x,y$,
\[
 \opnorm{V_n(g)V_n(h)-V_n(gh)}
 =\opnorm{t(U^{(n)})-1}
 \le A\,2^{-n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For $g\ne1$ the word $w_g$ is nontrivial in $G_P$, so for
$n\ge\lvert w_g\rvert$ the certificate $c_n$ cannot label it $\mathsf T$,
hence labels it $\mathsf S$, and $\opnorm{V_n(g)-1}\ge1/4$.  Thus
$\limsup_n\opnorm{V_n(g)-1}\ge1/4>0$, and $G_P$ is MF.

The displayed equivalence exhibits $\mathrm{MF}_{\mathrm{fp}}$ as
$\{P:\forall n\,\exists c\,C(P,n,c)\}$, a $\Pi^0_2$ set, and its
complement as a $\Sigma^0_2$ set.
\end{proof}

\section{HNN extensions with a corona conjugator}\label{sec:hnn}

The preceding sections turned a one-sided conjugation inside a norm
matrix corona into an obstruction to operator norm approximation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:recognition} also needs the complementary positive
principle: when the conjugation is implemented by a unitary of the corona,
an HNN extension remains MF\@.  The central case $\theta=\mathrm{id}$ is
close to Shulman's permanence theorem for central HNN
extensions~\cite{Shulman}; the form needed below carries a tracial state
that vanishes off the identity, which is what makes the group inject, and
allows an edge isomorphism implemented by a corona unitary.  Its
hypothesis is accordingly stronger than the MF property of the base.

A countable group $G$ \emph{admits a tracial MF realization}
$(A,\rho,\tau)$ if $A$ is a separable unital MF $C^*$-algebra,
$\rho\colon G\to\U(A)$ is a homomorphism, and $\tau$ is a tracial state on
$A$ with $\tau(\rho(g))=0$ for every $g\ne1$.  Since $\tau(1)=1$, such a
group embeds in $\U(A)$, so it is MF\@.
Padding blocks with zeros embeds every norm matrix corona
$\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}$ isometrically into $\prod_nM_n(\C)/\bigoplus_nM_n(\C)$,
so the MF algebras of the introduction are exactly the separable
$C^*$-algebras that embed in the latter corona, as in~\cite{BK,Shulman}.

\begin{lemma}[residually finite groups]\label{lem:rf-regular}
Every countable residually finite group admits a tracial MF realization.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $G$ be countable and residually finite, and choose finite-index normal
subgroups $N_1\ge N_2\ge\cdots$ with $\bigcap_kN_k=\{1\}$; this is possible
because $G$ is countable.  Let $d_k=[G:N_k]$ and let
$\lambda_k\colon G\to\U(d_k)$ be the left regular representation of
$G/N_k$.  In the corona $\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}$ put
$\rho(g)=[(\lambda_k(g))_k]$; then $\rho$ is a homomorphism into the
unitary group.  Fix a free ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\mathbb N$ and set
$\tau([(x_k)_k])=\lim_\omega\operatorname{tr}_{d_k}(x_k)$, where
$\operatorname{tr}_{d_k}$ is the normalized trace on $M_{d_k}(\C)$; this is
well defined because $\lvert\operatorname{tr}_{d_k}(x_k)\rvert\le\opnorm{x_k}$,
and it is a tracial state on $\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}$.  If $g\ne1$, then
$g\notin N_k$ for all large $k$, so $\operatorname{tr}_{d_k}(\lambda_k(g))=0$
for all large $k$ and $\tau(\rho(g))=0$.  Let $A$ be the $C^*$-algebra
generated by $\rho(G)$ and the unit of $\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}$; it is
separable, unital, and MF, and $(A,\rho,\tau|_A)$ is a tracial MF
realization of $G$.
\end{proof}

Let $G$ be a countable group, let $S\le G$ be a subgroup, and let
$\theta\colon S\to G$ be an injective homomorphism.  The HNN extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hnn}
 R=\langle G,t\mid tst^{-1}=\theta(s)\ (s\in S)\rangle
\end{equation}
contains $G$ by Britton's lemma~\cite{Britton}.

\begin{theorem}[HNN permanence with a corona conjugator]\label{thm:hnn-permanence}
In the situation of~\eqref{eq:hnn}, suppose that $G$ admits a tracial
MF realization $(A,\rho,\tau)$, and that for some norm matrix corona
$\mathcal Q$ there are an injective $*$-homomorphism
$\iota\colon A\to\mathcal Q$ and a unitary $W\in\mathcal Q$ with
\[
 W\,\iota\rho(s)\,W^*=\iota\rho(\theta(s))
 \qquad(s\in S).
\]
Then $R$ admits a tracial MF realization.  In particular, $R$ is MF\@.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $D=C^*(\iota\rho(G))\subseteq\mathcal Q$, a separable
$C^*$-algebra with unit $p=\iota(1)$, and let
$B_0=C^*(\iota\rho(S))$ and $B_1=C^*(\iota\rho(\theta(S)))$, both
containing $p$.  Conjugation by $W$ restricts to a $*$-isomorphism
$\Theta\colon B_0\to B_1$ carrying $\iota\rho(s)$ to $\iota\rho(\theta(s))$.
Let $U$ be the quotient of the full unital free product $D*C(\mathbb T)$ by
the closed ideal generated by the elements $ubu^*-\Theta(b)$, $b\in B_0$,
where $u$ is the canonical unitary generator of $C(\mathbb T)$.  We write
$d$ and $u$ for the images of $d\in D$ and $u$ in $U$.  By construction,
$U$ has the following universal property: whenever $\pi\colon D\to E$ is a
unital $*$-homomorphism into a unital $C^*$-algebra and $v\in\U(E)$
satisfies $v\pi(b)v^*=\pi(\Theta(b))$ for $b\in B_0$, there is a unique
$*$-homomorphism $U\to E$ extending $\pi$ and sending $u$ to $v$.  We
first embed $U$ in an amalgamated free product, which Shulman's criterion
shows to be MF, and then construct the required tracial state on the copy
of $R$ inside $U$.

\emph{Step 1: $U$ embeds in an amalgamated free product.}  This step is a
self-contained form of a corner argument of Ueda~\cite{Ueda}.  Let
$C=B_0\oplus B_1$, $A_1=M_2(D)$, and $A_2=M_2(B_0)$, with the unital
inclusions
\[
 \iota_A(c_0,c_1)=\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}\in A_1,
 \qquad
 \iota_B(c_0,c_1)=\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&\Theta^{-1}(c_1)\end{pmatrix}\in A_2,
\]
and let $P=A_1*_CA_2$ be the full amalgamated free product, in which
$A_1$ and $A_2$ embed~\cite[Section~2.1]{Shulman}.  Write $e_{ij}$ and
$f_{ij}$ for the matrix units of $A_1$ and $A_2$.  Since
$\iota_A(p,0)=\iota_B(p,0)$ and $\iota_A(0,p)=\iota_B(0,p)$, we have
$e_{11}=f_{11}=:e$ and $e_{22}=f_{22}$ in $P$.  Define
$\pi_D\colon D\to ePe$ by $\pi_D(d)=\operatorname{diag}(d,0)$, a unital
$*$-homomorphism into the corner, and put $w=e_{12}f_{21}\in ePe$.  Then
$ww^*=e_{12}f_{22}e_{21}=e_{12}e_{22}e_{21}=e$ and
$w^*w=f_{12}e_{22}f_{21}=f_{12}f_{22}f_{21}=e$, so $w$ is a unitary of
$ePe$.  For $b\in B_0$ the element $\operatorname{diag}(b,0)$ is
$\iota_A(b,0)=\iota_B(b,0)$, so
\begin{align*}
 w\pi_D(b)w^*
 &=e_{12}\bigl(f_{21}\operatorname{diag}(b,0)f_{12}\bigr)e_{21}
 =e_{12}\,\iota_B(0,\Theta(b))\,e_{21}\\
 &=e_{12}\,\iota_A(0,\Theta(b))\,e_{21}
 =\pi_D(\Theta(b)),
\end{align*}
where the middle equality uses
$\iota_B(0,\Theta(b))=\operatorname{diag}(0,b)$ in $A_2$.  The universal
property of $U$ gives a $*$-homomorphism $\Phi\colon U\to ePe\subseteq P$
with $\Phi|_D=\pi_D$ and $\Phi(u)=w$.  We prove that $\Phi$ is injective
by constructing $\Psi\colon P\to M_2(U)$ with
$\Psi\Phi(x)=\operatorname{diag}(x,0)$.  Let $\Psi_1\colon A_1\to M_2(U)$ be the entrywise inclusion
$D\subseteq U$, and let $\Psi_2\colon A_2\to M_2(U)$ be the entrywise
inclusion $B_0\subseteq U$ followed by conjugation by
$\operatorname{diag}(1,u)$.  On $C$ we have
$\Psi_1(\iota_A(c_0,c_1))=\operatorname{diag}(c_0,c_1)$ and
$\Psi_2(\iota_B(c_0,c_1))=\operatorname{diag}(c_0,u\Theta^{-1}(c_1)u^*)
=\operatorname{diag}(c_0,c_1)$, so the pair induces a $*$-homomorphism
$\Psi\colon P\to M_2(U)$.  On generators,
$\Psi\Phi(d)=\operatorname{diag}(d,0)$ and
$\Psi\Phi(u)=\Psi_1(e_{12})\Psi_2(f_{21})=\operatorname{diag}(u,0)$.
Hence $\Psi\Phi$ is the injective $*$-homomorphism $x\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(x,0)$
of $U$ into $M_2(U)$, and $\Phi$ is injective.

\emph{Step 2: $P$ is MF.}  By Shulman's criterion~\cite[Theorem~16]{Shulman},
the full amalgamated free product $A_1*_CA_2$ of separable $C^*$-algebras
is MF as soon as there are injective $*$-homomorphisms $\varphi_A$ and
$\varphi_B$ of $A_1$ and $A_2$ into one norm matrix corona with
$\varphi_A\circ\iota_A=\varphi_B\circ\iota_B$.  Identify
$M_2(\mathcal Q)$ with the norm matrix corona of the doubled dimension
sequence, and let $\varphi_A\colon M_2(D)\to M_2(\mathcal Q)$ be the
entrywise inclusion and
$\varphi_B\colon M_2(B_0)\to M_2(\mathcal Q)$ the entrywise inclusion
followed by conjugation by $\operatorname{diag}(1,W)$.  Both are injective,
and on $C$,
\[
 \varphi_B(\iota_B(c_0,c_1))
 =\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&W\Theta^{-1}(c_1)W^*\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}
 =\varphi_A(\iota_A(c_0,c_1)),
\]
because $\Theta$ is conjugation by $W$.  Hence $P$ is MF, and so are its
$C^*$-subalgebras $ePe$ and $\Phi(U)\cong U$.

\emph{Step 3: the tracial state.}  The functional
$T=\tau\circ\iota^{-1}$ is a tracial state on $\iota(A)\supseteq D$ with
$T(\iota\rho(g))=0$ for $g\ne1$.  In the GNS representation of $D$ for
$T$, the vectors $\xi_g=\iota\rho(g)\xi_T$, $g\in G$, are orthonormal,
because $\langle\xi_g,\xi_h\rangle=T(\iota\rho(h^{-1}g))$.  Their closed
span is invariant under $D$, and on it $\iota\rho(g)$ acts as the left
regular representation $\lambda_G(g)$ of $G$.  Thus there is a
$*$-homomorphism $\pi\colon D\to C^*_{\mathrm r}(G)$ with
$\pi(\iota\rho(g))=\lambda_G(g)$.  Since $G\le R$, the space
$\ell^2(R)$ is a direct sum of copies of $\ell^2(G)$ indexed by the right
cosets of $G$, so $\lambda_R|_G$ is a multiple of $\lambda_G$ and the
assignment $\lambda_G(g)\mapsto\lambda_R(g)$ extends to a
$*$-homomorphism $C^*_{\mathrm r}(G)\to C^*_{\mathrm r}(R)$.  Composing, we
obtain $\sigma_0\colon D\to C^*_{\mathrm r}(R)$ with
$\sigma_0(\iota\rho(g))=\lambda_R(g)$.  The pair $(\sigma_0,\lambda_R(t))$
satisfies the covariance relation
$\lambda_R(t)\sigma_0(b)\lambda_R(t)^*=\sigma_0(\Theta(b))$ for
$b\in B_0$: on the generators $\iota\rho(s)$ both sides equal
$\lambda_R(\theta(s))$, because $tst^{-1}=\theta(s)$ in $R$, and two
$*$-homomorphisms that agree on generators agree on $B_0$.  The universal
property of $U$ gives $\sigma\colon U\to C^*_{\mathrm r}(R)$ with
$\sigma(\iota\rho(g))=\lambda_R(g)$ and $\sigma(u)=\lambda_R(t)$.

Define $j\colon R\to\U(U)$ by $j(g)=\iota\rho(g)$ for $g\in G$ and
$j(t)=u$.  The defining relations of~\eqref{eq:hnn} hold in $U$, since
$u\,\iota\rho(s)\,u^*=\Theta(\iota\rho(s))=\iota\rho(\theta(s))$, so $j$
is a homomorphism, and $\sigma\circ j=\lambda_R$ is injective, so $j$ is
injective.  Let $A'=C^*(j(R))\subseteq U$, a separable unital MF
$C^*$-algebra, and let $\tau'=\tau_R\circ\sigma|_{A'}$, where $\tau_R$ is
the canonical trace $x\mapsto\langle x\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle$ of
$C^*_{\mathrm r}(R)$.  Then $\tau'$ is a tracial state on $A'$ with
$\tau'(j(r))=\tau_R(\lambda_R(r))=0$ for $r\ne1$.  Hence $(A',j,\tau')$
is a tracial MF realization of $R$.
\end{proof}

Injectivity of $j$ follows from $\sigma\circ j=\lambda_R$, not from the
covariant pair $(\iota\rho,W)$, which need not be faithful; in the
applications below it is not.

\begin{corollary}[central HNN extensions]\label{cor:central-hnn}
If $G$ admits a tracial MF realization and $S\le G$ is any subgroup, then
$\langle G,t\mid[t,s]=1\ (s\in S)\rangle$ admits a tracial MF realization.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:hnn-permanence} with $\theta$ the inclusion of
$S$, with $\iota$ any injective $*$-homomorphism of $A$ into a norm matrix
corona, and with $W=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{The complexity of recognizing MF groups}\label{sec:compiler}

Write $W_e$ for the domain of the $e$-th partial computable function and
\[
 \mathrm{INF}=\{e:W_e\text{ is infinite}\},
 \qquad
 \mathrm{FIN}=\{e:W_e\text{ is finite}\}.
\]
The set $\mathrm{INF}$ is $\Pi^0_2$-complete and $\mathrm{FIN}$ is
$\Sigma^0_2$-complete~\cite[Chapter~IV]{Soare}.  We now combine the obstruction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} with the permanence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hnn-permanence}: this section constructs a computable
map $e\mapsto\widehat R_e$ into finite presentations such that the
presented group contains $H$ when $e\in\mathrm{FIN}$ and falls under
Theorem~\ref{thm:hnn-permanence} when $e\in\mathrm{INF}$, so that it is
MF if and only if $e\in\mathrm{INF}$.  The construction of
Bilanovic--Chubb--Roven gives a recursively presented group
$C_e$ that is trivial when $e\in\mathrm{INF}$ and contains $H$ when
$e\in\mathrm{FIN}$.  We embed $C_e$ in a three-generated recursively
presented group $Q_e=F/N_e$, use a Mikhailova subgroup to detect $N_e$
inside a finitely presented group, and apply two HNN extensions, in the
manner of Higman's rope trick~\cite{Higman}, to obtain $\widehat R_e$.
This section proves that $\widehat R_e$ is not MF when
$e\in\mathrm{FIN}$; Section~\ref{sec:positive} proves that it is MF when
$e\in\mathrm{INF}$.

\subsection*{A recursive presentation of $H$}

\begin{lemma}[a recursive presentation of $H$]\label{lem:seed}
The group $H=\EL_{12}(R)$ is finitely generated and has decidable word
problem.  In particular, it has a recursive presentation on finitely many
generators.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $Y$ be the set of elements $e_{ij}(a)$ with $i\ne j$ and
$a\in\{1,s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1\}$.  Since $e_{ij}(a+b)=e_{ij}(a)e_{ij}(b)$ and
$[e_{ik}(a),e_{kj}(b)]=e_{ij}(ab)$ for distinct $i,j,k$, and since every
element of $R$ is a sum of monomials in the generators $s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1$,
induction on the length of a monomial shows that every $e_{ij}(a)$ lies in
the subgroup generated by $Y$; there is always an index $k\notin\{i,j\}$
because $12\ge3$.  Thus $H=\langle Y\rangle$.  The monomials in
$s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1$ that contain no subword $t_is_j$ and no subword
$s_1t_1$ form the standard reduced $\F_2$-basis of $R$~\cite{Leavitt}.
Products of reduced monomials are computed by the defining reductions, so
equality in $R$ is decidable.  A word in $Y^{\pm1}$ is therefore evaluated to an explicit
$12\times12$ matrix over $R$, and it represents $1$ in $H$ exactly when
that matrix is the identity.  The set of words representing $1$ is
decidable, hence recursively enumerable, and $\langle Y\mid\text{that set}\rangle$
is a recursive presentation of $H$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The group $C_e$}

\begin{lemma}[the group $C_e$]\label{lem:switch}
There is an algorithm that computes from $e$ a recursive presentation, on
a computable set of generators, of a group $C_e$ such that $C_e$ is
trivial if $e\in\mathrm{INF}$ and $H$ embeds in $C_e$ if
$e\in\mathrm{FIN}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the construction of Bilanovic--Chubb--Roven~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{BCR}
to the trivial group and $H$.
Let $\langle Y\mid T\rangle$ be the recursive presentation of $H$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:seed}.  Take generators $y_{i,\ell}$ for $i\ge1$ and
$\ell\in Y$, one copy $Y_i$ of $Y$ for each $i$, and as relators all
words of $T$ rewritten in each copy $Y_i$, together with every generator
$y_{i,\ell}$ for which $i\le\lvert W_e\rvert$.  The last family is
recursively enumerable uniformly in $e$: enumerate $W_e$, and whenever its
$i$-th element appears, enumerate all of $Y_i$.  The group presented is
the free product of the copies $H_i\cong H$, $i\ge1$, with the first
$\lvert W_e\rvert$ copies killed.  If $W_e$ is infinite every copy is
killed and $C_e$ is trivial; if $W_e$ is finite, $C_e$ is the free product
of the remaining copies and contains $H$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{A three-generator embedding}

Let $F=F(x,y,t)$ be the free group on $x,y,t$, and let
$y_i=x^iyx^{-i}$ for $i\in\mathbb Z$.  For a countable group $C$ with a
generating sequence $(c_i)_{i\ge1}$, put $c_i=1$ for $i\le0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge}
 B(C)=\bigl\langle C*F(x,y),\ t\ \bigm|\ ty_it^{-1}=c_iy_i\ (i\in\mathbb Z)\bigr\rangle.
\end{equation}
Let $H_0=B(1)=\langle x,y,t\mid[t,y_i]=1\ (i\in\mathbb Z)\rangle$, let
$q_+\colon F\to H_0$ be the quotient map, and let $N_+=\ker q_+$.

\begin{lemma}[the three-generator embedding]\label{lem:bridge}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B(C)$ is an HNN extension of $C*F(x,y)$, so $C$ embeds in $B(C)$;
it is generated by $x,y,t$; and a recursive presentation of $B(C)$ on
$x,y,t$ is computable from a recursive presentation of $C$.  We write
$B(C)=F/N_C$ and $q_C\colon F\to B(C)$ for the quotient map.
\item Killing $C$ induces a surjection $B(C)\to H_0$; hence
$N_C\le N_+$, with equality when $C$ is trivial.
\item The assignment $x\mapsto(x_1,x_2)$, $y\mapsto(y,1)$, $t\mapsto(1,t)$
defines an injective homomorphism $j\colon H_0\to P=F(x_1,y)\times F(x_2,t)$.
In particular, $H_0$ is residually finite.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) The elements $y_i$, $i\in\mathbb Z$, freely generate the normal
closure of $y$ in $F(x,y)$~\cite[Chapter~I]{LyndonSchupp}.  The retraction
$C*F(x,y)\to F(x,y)$ killing $C$ sends $c_iy_i$ to $y_i$, so a nontrivial
reduced word in the $c_iy_i$ maps to a nontrivial word in the $y_i$; hence
the $c_iy_i$ freely generate a free subgroup, and $y_i\mapsto c_iy_i$ is an
isomorphism between two free subgroups of the base.  Thus~\eqref{eq:bridge}
is an HNN extension, and the base, hence $C$, embeds by Britton's lemma.
The relation $c_i=ty_it^{-1}y_i^{-1}$ shows that $x,y,t$ generate $B(C)$,
and substituting this expression for $c_i$ into the relators of $C$ and
into~\eqref{eq:bridge} produces a recursive presentation on $x,y,t$,
uniformly in the presentation of $C$.

(2) The relators of~\eqref{eq:bridge} map to the relators $[t,y_i]$ of
$H_0$ when every $c_i$ is sent to $1$, and the map is the identity on
$x,y,t$.

(3) The relators $[t,y_i]$ are sent to $[(1,t),(x_1^iyx_1^{-i},1)]=1$, so
$j$ is a homomorphism.  Let $N_0$ be the normal closure of $\{y,t\}$ in
$H_0$.  Then $H_0/N_0=\langle x\rangle$ is infinite cyclic and the
extension splits, so $H_0=N_0\rtimes\langle x\rangle$.  Rewriting the
presentation of $H_0$ over the transversal $\{x^n\}$ by the
Reidemeister--Schreier method~\cite[Chapter~II]{LyndonSchupp} presents
$N_0$ on the generators $y_n=x^nyx^{-n}$ and $t_m=x^mtx^{-m}$, $n,m\in\mathbb Z$,
with relators the conjugates $x^m[t,y_n]x^{-m}=[t_m,y_{n+m}]$, that is,
all commutators $[t_m,y_n]$.  Hence $N_0=F(y_n:n\in\mathbb Z)\times F(t_m:m\in\mathbb Z)$.
Now $j(y_n)=(x_1^nyx_1^{-n},1)$ and $j(t_m)=(1,x_2^mtx_2^{-m})$, and the
elements $x_1^nyx_1^{-n}$ freely generate the normal closure of $y$ in
$F(x_1,y)$, and likewise for $t$; so $j$ restricted to $N_0$ is an
isomorphism onto the product of these two normal closures.  Finally
$j(x)=(x_1,x_2)$ has infinite order and $j(x)^k\notin j(N_0)$ for $k\ne0$,
because the exponent sum of $x_1$ in $j(x^kn)$ is $k$ for $n\in N_0$.
Hence $j$ is injective on $N_0\rtimes\langle x\rangle$.  Free groups are
residually finite and residual finiteness passes to direct products and
subgroups, so $H_0$ is residually finite.
\end{proof}

For $e\in\mathbb N$ let $Q_e=B(C_e)=F/N_e$ with $C_e$ the group of
Lemma~\ref{lem:switch}, and write $q_e\colon F\to Q_e$.  By
Lemmas~\ref{lem:switch} and~\ref{lem:bridge}, a recursive presentation of
$Q_e$ on $x,y,t$ is computable from $e$; $N_e\le N_+$; if $e\in\mathrm{INF}$
then $Q_e=H_0$, $N_e=N_+$ and $q_e=q_+$; and if $e\in\mathrm{FIN}$ then
$H\le C_e\le Q_e$.

\subsection*{Detecting $N_e$ inside a finitely presented group}

\begin{lemma}[Higman embedding and the Mikhailova subgroup]\label{lem:mikhailova}
There is an algorithm that computes from $e$ a finite presentation
$\langle X_e\mid R_e\rangle$ of a group $\widehat H_e$, words
$w_x,w_y,w_t\in F(X_e)$, and a finite generating set of a subgroup
$M_e\le F(X_e)\times F(X_e)$, such that $x\mapsto w_x$, $y\mapsto w_y$,
$t\mapsto w_t$ induces an embedding $Q_e\to\widehat H_e$, and
$M_e=\{(u,v):u=v\text{ in }\widehat H_e\}$.  Consequently, with
\[
 K^0_e=F\times F(X_e)\times F(X_e),
 \qquad
 i_e(f)=(f,w_f,1),
 \qquad
 L^0_e=F\times M_e,
\]
where $w_f$ is the image of $f$ under $x\mapsto w_x$, $y\mapsto w_y$,
$t\mapsto w_t$, the map $i_e\colon F\to K^0_e$ is injective and
$i_e(f)\in L^0_e$ if and only if $f\in N_e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Higman's embedding theorem~\cite{Higman} embeds every finitely generated
recursively presented group in a finitely presented group, and its proof
is effective; an explicit algorithm producing the finite presentation and
the embedding words from a recursive presentation is given by
Mikaelian~\cite{Mikaelian}.  Apply it to the presentation of $Q_e$.
Following Mikhailova~\cite{Mikhailova}, let $M_e$ be the subgroup of
$F(X_e)\times F(X_e)$ generated by the pairs $(z,z)$, $z\in X_e$, and
$(r,1)$, $r\in R_e$.  If $(u,v)\in M_e$ then $u=v$ in $\widehat H_e$,
since this holds for the generators.  Conversely, if $u=v$ in
$\widehat H_e$, then $uv^{-1}$ lies in the normal closure of $R_e$, so it
is a product of conjugates $gr^{\pm1}g^{-1}$ with $r\in R_e$, and
$(gr^{\pm1}g^{-1},1)=(g,g)(r^{\pm1},1)(g,g)^{-1}\in M_e$; hence
$(u,v)=(uv^{-1},1)(v,v)\in M_e$.  The first coordinate makes $i_e$
injective, and $(f,w_f,1)\in F\times M_e$ if and only if $w_f=1$ in
$\widehat H_e$, if and only if $q_e(f)=1$ because the embedding is
injective, if and only if $f\in N_e$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The two HNN extensions}

Let $P=F(x_1,y)\times F(x_2,t)$ and $j\colon H_0\to P$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}.  Define the graph witness
\[
 K^{\mathrm g}=F\times P,
 \qquad
 L^{\mathrm g}=\{(f,\,jq_+(f)):f\in F\},
\]
and the product witness
\[
 K_e=K^0_e\times K^{\mathrm g},
 \qquad
 L_e=L^0_e\times L^{\mathrm g},
 \qquad
 i\colon F\to K_e,\quad i(f)=\bigl(i_e(f),(f,1)\bigr).
\]
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-rope}
 \Gamma_e=\bigl\langle K_e,\ v\ \bigm|\ [v,\ell]=1\ (\ell\in L_e)\bigr\rangle,
 \qquad
 S_e=\bigl\langle i(F),\ v\,i(F)\,v^{-1}\bigr\rangle\le\Gamma_e.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[the first HNN extension]\label{lem:central-rope}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K_e$ is a direct product of free groups of finite rank, hence
finitely presented and residually finite; $L_e$ is finitely generated;
$i$ is injective; and $i(F)\cap L_e=i(N_e)$.
\item $\Gamma_e$ is finitely presented, and a finite presentation is
computable from $e$.  The natural map from the amalgamated free product
$F*_{N_e}F$, in which $n\in N_e$ in the first copy is identified with
$n$ in the second copy, onto $S_e$, sending the first copy to $i(F)$ and
the second to $v\,i(F)\,v^{-1}$, is an isomorphism.
\item There is a homomorphism $\tau_e\colon S_e\to Q_e$ with
$\tau_e(i(f))=q_e(f)$ and $\tau_e(v\,i(f)\,v^{-1})=1$ for $f\in F$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) $K_e=F\times F(X_e)\times F(X_e)\times F\times P$ is a product of six
free groups of finite rank, so it is finitely presented, and residually
finite because free groups are.  The subgroup $L_e$ is generated by the
basis of the first factor, the generators of $M_e$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:mikhailova}, and the elements $(a,jq_+(a))$ for
$a\in\{x,y,t\}$, so it is finitely generated.  Injectivity of $i$ is clear
from the first coordinate.  An element $i(f)$ lies in $L_e$ if and only if
$i_e(f)\in L^0_e$ and $(f,1)\in L^{\mathrm g}$; the first holds if and only
if $f\in N_e$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:mikhailova}, and the second if and only if
$jq_+(f)=1$, that is, $f\in N_+$.  Since $N_e\le N_+$, the intersection is
$i(N_e)$.

(2) The presentation~\eqref{eq:central-rope} is finite once $[v,\ell]=1$ is
imposed only for the finitely many generators $\ell$ of $L_e$, and it is
computable from $e$ because $K_e$, $L_e$ and $i$ are.  It is an HNN
extension of $K_e$ with both edge groups equal to $L_e$ and the identity
as edge isomorphism.  The map from $F*_{N_e}F$ to $S_e$ is well defined
because $i(n)\in L_e$ commutes with $v$ for $n\in N_e$.  A reduced word of
the amalgamated free product alternates between elements of the two
copies of $F$ lying outside $N_e$; its image is a word
$i(f_0)\,v\,i(f_1)\,v^{-1}\,i(f_2)\cdots$ in which every letter between
consecutive occurrences of $v^{\pm1}$ is some $i(f_k)$ with
$f_k\notin N_e$, hence $i(f_k)\notin L_e$ by (1).  Such a word contains no
pinch $v^{\pm1}\ell v^{\mp1}$ with $\ell\in L_e$, so it is nontrivial in
$\Gamma_e$ by Britton's lemma.

(3) Define $\tau_e$ on $F*_{N_e}F$ by $q_e$ on the first copy and by the
trivial map on the second; both kill $N_e$, so the definition is
consistent on the amalgamated subgroup, and (2) transports it to $S_e$.
\end{proof}

The second HNN extension is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted-rope}
 R_e=\bigl\langle\Gamma_e\times Q_e,\ u\ \bigm|\ u(s,1)u^{-1}=(s,\tau_e(s))\ (s\in S_e)\bigr\rangle,
\end{equation}
whose edge maps $s\mapsto(s,1)$ and $s\mapsto(s,\tau_e(s))$ are injective
homomorphisms of $S_e$ into $\Gamma_e\times Q_e$; so
$\Gamma_e\times Q_e$ embeds in $R_e$ by Britton's lemma.  The
presentation~\eqref{eq:twisted-rope} lists the recursively enumerable
relators of $Q_e$.  Its finite replacement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-rope}
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat R_e=\Bigl\langle\Gamma_e\times F,\ u\ \Bigm|\ 
 &u\bigl(i(a),1\bigr)u^{-1}=\bigl(i(a),a\bigr),\\
 &u\bigl(v\,i(a)\,v^{-1},1\bigr)u^{-1}=\bigl(v\,i(a)\,v^{-1},1\bigr)
 \quad(a\in\{x,y,t\})\Bigr\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma_e\times F$ is equipped with the finite presentation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:central-rope}(2), the basis $x,y,t$ of $F$, and the
commutation relators between the two factors.

\begin{lemma}[the finite presentation]\label{lem:finite-rope}
The groups $\widehat R_e$ and $R_e$ are isomorphic by the map that is the
identity on $\Gamma_e$ and on $u$ and sends $F$ onto $Q_e$ by $q_e$.  A
code of the finite presentation~\eqref{eq:finite-rope} is computable
from $e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both maps $f\mapsto u(i(f),1)u^{-1}$ and $f\mapsto(i(f),f)$ are
homomorphisms $F\to\widehat R_e$, and they agree on the basis, so
$u(i(f),1)u^{-1}=(i(f),f)$ for all $f\in F$; likewise
$u(v\,i(f)\,v^{-1},1)u^{-1}=(v\,i(f)\,v^{-1},1)$ for all $f$.  If
$n\in N_e$, then $i(n)\in L_e$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:central-rope}(1), so
$v\,i(n)\,v^{-1}=i(n)$ in $\Gamma_e$, and comparing the two relations
gives $(i(n),n)=(i(n),1)$, that is, $(1,n)=1$ in $\widehat R_e$.  Hence
the factor $F$ of $\widehat R_e$ factors through $Q_e=F/N_e$, and the
relations of~\eqref{eq:finite-rope} become $u(s,1)u^{-1}=(s,\tau_e(s))$
for $s$ in the generating set $\{i(a),\,v\,i(a)\,v^{-1}:a\in\{x,y,t\}\}$
of $S_e$; both sides define homomorphisms on $S_e$, so the relations hold
for all $s\in S_e$.  This defines a homomorphism $R_e\to\widehat R_e$; conversely, the
relations of~\eqref{eq:finite-rope} hold in $R_e$ after $F\to Q_e$,
because $\tau_e(i(a))=q_e(a)$ and $\tau_e(v\,i(a)\,v^{-1})=1$.  The two
homomorphisms are mutually inverse on generators.  Computability of the
code follows from Lemmas~\ref{lem:mikhailova} and~\ref{lem:central-rope}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the case $e\in\mathrm{FIN}$]\label{lem:negative-branch}
If $e\in\mathrm{FIN}$, then $H$ embeds in $\widehat R_e$, and
$\widehat R_e$ is not MF\@.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $e\in\mathrm{FIN}$ we have $H\le C_e\le Q_e\le\Gamma_e\times Q_e\le R_e\cong\widehat R_e$
by Lemmas~\ref{lem:switch}, \ref{lem:bridge}(1), \ref{lem:finite-rope} and
Britton's lemma for~\eqref{eq:twisted-rope}.  Restricting MF models to a
subgroup shows that subgroups of MF groups are MF, and $H$ is not MF by
Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}.
\end{proof}

\section{The case $e\in\mathrm{INF}$}\label{sec:positive}

We now prove that $\widehat R_e$ is MF for $e\in\mathrm{INF}$ and deduce
Theorem~\ref{thm:recognition}.  The tracial MF realization of $R_e$ is
constructed inside a norm reduced product of MF algebras, so we first
record that separable subalgebras of such products are MF\@.  For $C^*$-algebras $B_n$, $n\ge1$, write
$\prod_nB_n/\bigoplus_nB_n$ for the quotient of the bounded sequences by
the sequences with $\opnorm{x_n}\to0$; its norm is $\limsup_n\opnorm{x_n}$.

\begin{lemma}[reduced products of MF algebras]\label{lem:reduced-products}
If every $B_n$ is MF, then every separable $C^*$-subalgebra of
$\prod_nB_n/\bigoplus_nB_n$ is MF\@.  Moreover, $M_k(B)$ is MF whenever
$B$ is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The second assertion holds because $M_k$ of a norm matrix corona is the
norm matrix corona of the dimension sequence multiplied by $k$.  For the
first, let $A$ be a separable $C^*$-subalgebra, and choose a sequence
$(a_i)_{i\ge1}$ dense in $A$, with lifts $(a_i^{(n)})_n$.  Choose for each
$n$ an injective $*$-homomorphism $\eta_n$ of $B_n$ into a norm matrix
corona, and lifts $(a_i^{(n,k)})_k$ of $\eta_n(a_i^{(n)})$ with
$\sup_k\opnorm{a_i^{(n,k)}}\le\opnorm{a_i^{(n)}}+1$.  Enumerate the
noncommutative $*$-polynomials with coefficients in $\mathbb Q(i)$ as
$p_1,p_2,\dots$, and let $\mathcal P_n$ be the set of those among
$p_1,\dots,p_n$ that involve only $a_1,\dots,a_n$, choosing $p_1=0$ so that
$\mathcal P_n$ is nonempty.  For $p\in\mathcal P_n$
the number $\opnorm{p(a^{(n)})}$ equals $\limsup_k\opnorm{p(a^{(n,k)})}$,
since $\eta_n$ is isometric.  Hence there is $K_n$ such that
$\opnorm{p(a^{(n,k)})}\le\opnorm{p(a^{(n)})}+1/n$ for all $k\ge K_n$ and
$p\in\mathcal P_n$, and for each $p\in\mathcal P_n$ there is $k_p\ge K_n$
with $\opnorm{p(a^{(n,k_p)})}\ge\opnorm{p(a^{(n)})}-1/n$.  Put
\[
 b_i^{(n)}=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P_n}a_i^{(n,k_p)},
\]
a block diagonal matrix.  For $p\in\mathcal P_n$ the norm of
$p(b^{(n)})$ is the maximum of $\opnorm{p(a^{(n,k_{p'})})}$ over
$p'\in\mathcal P_n$, which lies between $\opnorm{p(a^{(n)})}-1/n$ and
$\opnorm{p(a^{(n)})}+1/n$.  Every polynomial belongs to $\mathcal P_n$ for
all large $n$, so
$\limsup_n\opnorm{p(b^{(n)})}=\limsup_n\opnorm{p(a^{(n)})}=\opnorm{p(a)}$
for every $p$.  Therefore $a_i\mapsto[(b_i^{(n)})_n]$ is well defined and
isometric on the $*$-algebra over $\mathbb Q(i)$ generated by the $a_i$,
which is dense in $A$, and it extends to an isometric $*$-homomorphism of
$A$ into a norm matrix corona.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[tensor synchronization]\label{lem:tensor-sync}
Let $\Gamma$ be a countable group with a tracial MF realization
$(A_1,\rho_1,\tau_1)$, let $Q$ be a countable group with homomorphisms
$\beta_n\colon Q\to B_n$ to finite groups such that every $q\ne1$
satisfies $\beta_n(q)\ne1$ for all large $n$, let $S\le\Gamma$, and let
$\tau\colon S\to Q$ be a homomorphism.  Suppose that for every $n$ there
is a homomorphism $\lambda_n\colon\Gamma\to G_n$ to a finite group with
\[
 \ker(\lambda_n|_S)\le\ker(\beta_n\circ\tau).
\]
Then there are a separable unital MF $C^*$-algebra $A$, a homomorphism
$V\colon\Gamma\times Q\to\U(A)$, a tracial state $T$ on $A$ with
$T(V(g,q))=0$ for $(g,q)\ne(1,1)$, and a unitary $W\in A$ with
$WV(s,1)W^*=V(s,\tau(s))$ for all $s\in S$.  Consequently
\[
 \bigl\langle\Gamma\times Q,\ u\ \bigm|\ u(s,1)u^{-1}=(s,\tau(s))\ (s\in S)\bigr\rangle
\]
admits a tracial MF realization, and in particular is MF\@.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $E_n$ be the image of $(\lambda_n,\beta_n)\colon\Gamma\times Q\to G_n\times B_n$,
a finite group of order $k_n$, and let $L_n$ be its left regular
representation on $\ell^2(E_n)$.  Put $B'_n=A_1\otimes M_{k_n}(\C)$,
which is MF by Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-products}, let
$E=\prod_nB'_n/\bigoplus_nB'_n$, and define
\[
 V(g,q)=\bigl[\bigl(\rho_1(g)\otimes L_n(\lambda_n(g),\beta_n(q))\bigr)_n\bigr],
 \qquad
 T\bigl([(x_n)_n]\bigr)=\lim_\omega(\tau_1\otimes\operatorname{tr}_{k_n})(x_n),
\]
for a free ultrafilter $\omega$.  Each coordinate of $V$ is a homomorphism
into a unitary group, so $V$ is a homomorphism, and $T$ is a tracial state
on $E$ because $\lvert(\tau_1\otimes\operatorname{tr}_{k_n})(x_n)\rvert\le\opnorm{x_n}$.
If $g\ne1$ then $\tau_1(\rho_1(g))=0$, so $T(V(g,q))=0$; if $g=1$ and
$q\ne1$ then $\beta_n(q)\ne1$ for all large $n$, the regular
representation of a finite group has trace zero off the identity, and
again $T(V(1,q))=0$.

The two homomorphisms $s\mapsto(\lambda_n(s),1)$ and
$s\mapsto(\lambda_n(s),\beta_n\tau(s))$ from $S$ to $E_n$ have the same
kernel, namely $\ker(\lambda_n|_S)$, by the hypothesis.  Their images are
therefore isomorphic subgroups of $E_n$ of the same order, isomorphic via
$(\lambda_n(s),1)\mapsto(\lambda_n(s),\beta_n\tau(s))$.  The restriction
of $L_n$ to a subgroup of $E_n$ is unitarily equivalent to the direct sum
of as many copies of the left regular representation of that subgroup as
it has right cosets, so there is a unitary $W_n\in M_{k_n}(\C)$ with
$W_nL_n(\lambda_n(s),1)W_n^*=L_n(\lambda_n(s),\beta_n\tau(s))$ for all
$s\in S$.  Put $W=[(1\otimes W_n)_n]\in E$; then $WV(s,1)W^*=V(s,\tau(s))$
for $s\in S$.  Let $A$ be the $C^*$-algebra generated by $V(\Gamma\times Q)$
and $W$; it is separable and unital, and MF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-products}.  The triple $(A,V,T|_A)$ is a tracial
MF realization of $\Gamma\times Q$.  For the last assertion, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:hnn-permanence} to this realization.  Let $\iota$ be an
injective $*$-homomorphism of $A$ into a norm matrix corona and put
$p=\iota(1)$.  The element $\widetilde W=\iota(W)+(1-p)$ is a unitary of the
full corona and implements the required covariance on $\iota(A)$, so it is
the required conjugator.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the case $e\in\mathrm{INF}$]\label{lem:positive-branch}
If $e\in\mathrm{INF}$, then $\widehat R_e$ admits a tracial MF
realization; in particular, it is MF\@.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $e\in\mathrm{INF}$, so that $Q_e=H_0$, $N_e=N_+$, $q_e=q_+$, and
$\tau_e(i(f))=q_+(f)$, $\tau_e(v\,i(f)\,v^{-1})=1$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-rope} it suffices to show that $R_e$ admits a
tracial MF realization, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-sync} applied to $\Gamma=\Gamma_e$,
$Q=H_0$, $S=S_e$ and $\tau=\tau_e$, it suffices to produce the data of
that lemma.

The group $K_e$ is residually finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:central-rope}(1),
hence admits a tracial MF realization by Lemma~\ref{lem:rf-regular},
and $\Gamma_e$ is the central HNN extension of $K_e$ over $L_e$, hence
admits one by Corollary~\ref{cor:central-hnn}.

The group $P=F(x_1,y)\times F(x_2,t)$ is residually finite; choose
finite-index normal subgroups $P_1\ge P_2\ge\cdots$ of $P$ with trivial
intersection, let $r_n\colon P\to C_n=P/P_n$, and put
$\beta_n=r_n\circ j\colon H_0\to C_n$ with $j$ the embedding of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}(3).  Since $j$ is injective and the $P_n$ decrease
to the trivial group, every $h\ne1$ has $\beta_n(h)\ne1$ for all large $n$.

Let $G_n=(C_n\times C_n)\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle$, where $\sigma$ of
order two exchanges the coordinates.  Define $\lambda_n$ on
$K_e=K^0_e\times(F\times P)$ by killing $K^0_e$ and sending $(f,p)$ to
$(r_njq_+(f),\,r_n(p))$, and put $\lambda_n(v)=\sigma$.  This respects the
relations of~\eqref{eq:central-rope}: the factor $L^0_e$ of $L_e$ lies in
$K^0_e$ and is killed, and an element $(f,jq_+(f))$ of $L^{\mathrm g}$ is
sent to $(r_njq_+(f),r_njq_+(f))$, which lies on the diagonal and commutes
with $\sigma$.  Hence $\lambda_n\colon\Gamma_e\to G_n$ is a homomorphism,
and on the generators of $S_e$,
\[
 \lambda_n(i(f))=(r_njq_+(f),1),
 \qquad
 \lambda_n(v\,i(f)\,v^{-1})=(1,r_njq_+(f)).
\]
Let $\pi_0,\pi_1\colon S_e\to H_0$ be the homomorphisms that are $q_+$ on
one copy of $F$ in $S_e\cong F*_{N_+}F$ and trivial on the other, as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:central-rope}(3); thus $\pi_0=\tau_e$.  The displayed
formulas show that $\lambda_n|_{S_e}=(r_nj\pi_0,\,r_nj\pi_1)$, both sides
being homomorphisms that agree on generators.  Therefore
\[
 \ker(\lambda_n|_{S_e})=\ker(r_nj\pi_0)\cap\ker(r_nj\pi_1)\le\ker(\beta_n\circ\tau_e),
\]
which is the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-sync}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:recognition}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-rope} the map $e\mapsto\widehat R_e$ is
computable, by Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-branch} the presented group is MF
for $e\in\mathrm{INF}$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:negative-branch} it is not MF
for $e\in\mathrm{FIN}$.  Hence $\mathrm{INF}$ reduces to
$\mathrm{MF}_{\mathrm{fp}}$ and $\mathrm{FIN}$ reduces to
$\mathrm{NONMF}_{\mathrm{fp}}$ under computable many-one reductions.  Since
$\mathrm{INF}$ is $\Pi^0_2$-complete and $\mathrm{FIN}$ is
$\Sigma^0_2$-complete, and since
$\mathrm{MF}_{\mathrm{fp}}\in\Pi^0_2$ and
$\mathrm{NONMF}_{\mathrm{fp}}\in\Sigma^0_2$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-upper-bound}, the two sets are complete at their
levels.  A $\Pi^0_2$-complete set is not $\Sigma^0_2$ and a
$\Sigma^0_2$-complete set is not $\Pi^0_2$, so neither set is recursively
enumerable, and in particular neither is decidable.
\end{proof}

The homomorphism $V$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-sync} does not extend
faithfully to $R_e$ in the corona: the finite maps $\lambda_n$ are trivial
on the Mikhailova factor, so $W$ commutes with $V(\gamma,1)$ for every
$\gamma\in K^0_e$, although $u$ does not commute with $(\gamma,1)$ in
$R_e$.  Injectivity is obtained in Theorem~\ref{thm:hnn-permanence} from
the universal algebra.  Three questions remain open.  It is not known
whether the models of $\widehat R_e$ can be chosen to converge to
$C^*_{\mathrm r}(\widehat R_e)$ in operator norm, so that the reduced
$C^*$-algebra itself is MF; the argument produces a tracial state on an
abstract MF algebra, not strong convergence.  It is not known whether
$\widehat R_e$ is hyperlinear for $e\in\mathrm{INF}$, which would place
the recognition problem for hyperlinearity at the same level.  Finally,
it is not known whether an analogous family of finitely presented groups
exists for soficity; the construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-sync} has
no counterpart for permutation models.



\section{Pullback formulas for MF radicals}\label{sec:prescribed-quotients}

This section is not used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:recognition}.
It proves that the MF radical pulls back along surjections whose kernel
lies in the radical, and it realizes every countable MF group as the
maximal MF quotient of a non-MF group.

\begin{theorem}[prescribed maximal MF quotients]\label{thm:prescribed-quotients}
Let $B$ be a countable group with $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$, and let
$1\ne d\in B$ normally generate $B$.  Put $A=\langle d\rangle$ and, for a
countable group $Q$, define

% records which chosen group was used; it is not multiplication.  The star

% new equations are those identifying the two copies of A.

%<webmath object="∗A" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the operation that joins the two groups along A">

% A.”  It forms an [[amalgamated free product]] by taking the two groups on

% A generated by d.
%</webmath>
\[
 W_Q=B*_A(Q\times A).
\]
Then the map $\pi_Q\colon W_Q\to Q$ which kills $B$ and projects the second
vertex group onto $Q$ is a split epimorphism, and
%<webmath object="RadMF" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)" title="the elements of W_Q that every allowed matrix model erases">

\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)
 =\pi_Q^{-1}\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr).
\]
The group $W_Q$ is non-MF.  If $Q$ is MF, then
%<webmath object="whole" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)=\ker\pi_Q" title="three descriptions of the part of W sub Q erased by matrices">
% This chain says that three [[normal subgroup|normal subgroups]] are equal.
% The [[MF radical]] of \(W_Q\) contains the elements every MF model sends to
% the identity.  The [[kernel]] of \(\pi_Q\) contains the elements lost when
% \(W_Q\) is mapped onto Q.  The double angle brackets give the [[normal
% closure]] of d in \(W_Q\).  Because Q is assumed to be MF, its own MF
% radical is trivial, so the elements forced to disappear are exactly this
% kernel, which is also exactly everything normally generated by d.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)=\ker\pi_Q=\normal{d}_{W_Q}.
\]
Moreover, precomposition with $\pi_Q$ induces a bijection
%<webmath object="whole" when="\operatorname{Hom}(Q,M)" title="why maps out of W sub Q are the same as maps out of Q">
% The left [[set]] contains all [[homomorphism|homomorphisms]] from Q to the MF
% group M; the right set contains all homomorphisms from \(W_Q\) to M.  The
% arrow sends \(h\colon Q\to M\) to the [[composition]] \(h\circ\pi_Q\): first
% map \(W_Q\) onto Q, then apply h.  Calling this arrow a [[bijection]] means
% every homomorphism from \(W_Q\) to an MF target is obtained in exactly one
% such way.  MF targets therefore retain the Q information and necessarily
% erase the attached B information.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(Q,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(W_Q,M)
\]
for every MF group $M$.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrescribedQuotients}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptPrescribedMFQuotients}
\end{theorem}

We begin with the pullback of the radical along a surjection whose kernel
lies in the radical.

\begin{proposition}[full-kernel pullback]\label{prop:full-kernel-pullback}
Let $f\colon G\to Q$ be a surjective homomorphism of countable groups.  If
$\ker f\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$, then
%<webmath object="pullback-radical" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=f^{-1}\!\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr)" title="how the erased elements of G are read from Q">
% The [[homomorphism]] \(f\colon G\to Q\) is [[surjective]], and its [[kernel]]
% is already inside the [[MF radical]] of G.  Therefore every allowed
% finite matrix [[representation]] of G ignores the kernel and [[factors
% through]] Q.  On the right, \(f^{-1}\) means [[preimage]].  The equality says
% that an element of G is erased by every representation exactly when its
% [[image]] in Q is erased by every representation of Q.  This is a [[pullback]]
% formula.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)
 =f^{-1}\!\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr).
\]
In particular, the conclusion holds whenever
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(\ker f)=\ker f$.
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrintedFullKernelPullback}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptPrintedFullKernelPullbackParagraph}
More generally, for every normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$,
%<webmath object="closure-pullback-general" when="\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)=f^{-1}\!\left(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^Q(f(N))\right)" title="how relations forced in G are read from Q">
% \(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)\) is an MF [[closure operation]] on
% the [[normal subgroup]] N inside G: it adds every element that all
% [[homomorphism|homomorphisms]] to [[MF group|MF groups]] are forced to erase
% once they erase N.  The superscript G names the surrounding group and the
% subscript MF names the condition.  The expression \(f(N)\) is the [[image]]
% of N in Q, while \(f^{-1}\) takes a [[preimage]].  The equality says that the
% forced elements in G are exactly the pullback of the forced elements in Q.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)
 =f^{-1}\!\left(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^Q(f(N))\right).
\]
Consequently,
%<webmath object="quotient-correspondence-general" when="G/N\text{ is MF}\Longleftrightarrow\ker f\le N\text{ and }Q/f(N)\text{ is MF}" title="exactly when matrices can model G divided by N without losing information">
% The slash in \(G/N\) forms a [[quotient group]] by treating every element of
% the [[normal subgroup]] N as the [[identity]].  The double arrow
% \(\Longleftrightarrow\) means “if and only if,” so the two sides are exactly
% equivalent.  The condition \(\ker f\le N\) says N contains the [[kernel]] of
% f.  The expression \(Q/f(N)\) is another quotient group, using the [[image]]
% of N.  Thus \(G/N\) is an [[MF group]] exactly when N removes everything f
% forgets and the remaining quotient of Q is MF.
%</webmath>
\[
 G/N\text{ is MF}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ker f\le N\ \text{ and }\ Q/f(N)\text{ is MF}.
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/PrintedFullKernelPullback}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptPrintedFullKernelPullbackParagraph}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every corona homomorphism from $G$ kills $\ker f$ and therefore factors
uniquely through $f$.  Conversely, composing a corona homomorphism from $Q$
with $f$ gives one of $G$.  Intersecting these two corresponding families of
kernels gives the displayed MF radical pullback.  For the stated sufficient
condition,
let \(\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})\) be any corona homomorphism.
Its restriction to \(\ker f\) is trivial because
\(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(\ker f)=\ker f\).  Hence
\(\ker f\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)\).

The same factorization, restricted to homomorphisms that kill $N$, gives the
closure formula.  If this closure equals $N$, then it contains $\ker f$.
Mapping the equality onto $Q$ shows that
$\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^Q(f(N))=f(N)$.  Conversely, these two
conditions give
%<webmath object="whole" when="\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)=f^{-1}(f(N))=N" title="the two closure conditions force the MF closure to be exactly N">
% Start with N and take its image \(f(N)\) in Q.  The hypothesis says that
% \(f(N)\) is already [[MF closure|MF-closed]] in Q, so pulling that closure
% back gives \(f^{-1}(f(N))\).  Because N contains the [[kernel]] of f, this
% [[preimage]] is exactly N rather than a larger subgroup.  The chain of two
% equals signs therefore proves that applying MF closure inside G adds no new
% elements to N.  By the earlier criterion, this is equivalent to the
% [[quotient group]] \(G/N\) being an [[MF group]].
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)
 =f^{-1}(f(N))=N.
\]
The final equivalence now follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-residual-calculus}, applied in $G$ and $Q$.
\end{proof}

Thus image under $f$ and inverse image under $f$ give mutually inverse
correspondences, preserving inclusion between the normal subgroups with MF
quotient in $G$ and in $Q$.  The map induced by $f$ on the maximal MF
quotients is an isomorphism:
%<webmath object="visible-quotient-iso" when="G/\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)\cong Q/\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)" title="the largest detectable parts of G and Q have the same structure">
% Each slash forms a [[quotient group]] by removing the corresponding [[MF
% radical]].  The symbol \(\cong\) means there is an [[isomorphism]]: a
% reversible pairing that preserves multiplication between the two quotients.
% Because
% the [[kernel]] of f is already invisible to MF, every finite matrix
% [[representation]] of G [[factors through]] f.  After the invisible elements
% are removed, G and Q therefore retain exactly the same group structure.
%</webmath>
\[
 G/\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)
 \cong Q/\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q).
\]
These correspondences respect composition of surjective homomorphisms whose
kernels lie in the MF radical.

Let $B$ be a countable group with $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$, and let
$1\ne d\in B$ normally generate $B$.  Put $A=\langle d\rangle$.  For a
countable group $Q$, define
%<webmath object="WQ" when="W_Q=B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the group formed by attaching B to Q times A">
% The equation defines a new [[group]] called \(W_Q\); the subscript Q records
% which chosen group Q was used.  The symbol \(\times\) forms the [[direct
% product]] \(Q\times A\), whose elements are pairs \((q,a)\).  The starred
% symbol \(*_A\) forms an [[amalgamated free product]], joining B to
% \(Q\times A\) while identifying their copies of the [[subgroup]] A.  Every
% [[homomorphism]] from B to an [[MF group]] is the [[trivial homomorphism]], so
% an MF target receives only the information coming from Q.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="∗A" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the operation that joins the two groups along A">
% The starred symbol with subscript A, \(*_A\), means [[amalgamated free
% product]].  Start with the [[group]] B and the [[direct product]]
% \(Q\times A\), identify the copy of the [[subgroup]] A in each, and impose no
% additional equations connecting the two sides.  The subscript A records the
% shared subgroup used for the attachment.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="B" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the attached group B that allowed matrix models cannot detect">
% B is the attached [[group]].  Its [[MF radical]] equals all of B, meaning
% every [[homomorphism]] from B to any [[MF group]] is the [[trivial
% homomorphism]].  Inside \(W_Q\), B supplies elements that finite matrix
% [[representation|representations]] cannot detect.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="Q" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the chosen group Q that remains detectable">
% Q is any chosen [[countable]] [[group]], meaning its elements can be put in a
% list.  The
% construction builds \(W_Q\) around this Q.  The [[homomorphism]] \(\pi_Q\)
% recovers Q, and every homomorphism from \(W_Q\) to an [[MF group]] [[factors
% through]] \(\pi_Q\).  Thus the MF target receives exactly the information
% from Q.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="A" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the part A used to attach the two groups">
% A is the [[cyclic subgroup]] made from d and its [[inverse]].  The construction
% identifies this copy of A inside B with the second coordinate of the [[direct
% product]] \(Q\times A\).  Treating d as the [[identity]] collapses A; because
% the [[normal closure]] of d is B, it collapses B as well and leaves Q.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amalgam}
 W_Q=B*_A(Q\times A),
\end{equation}
where $A\to Q\times A$ is the inclusion into the second factor.  The trivial
map $B\to Q$ and the projection $Q\times A\to Q$ agree on $A$.  They
therefore define an epimorphism
%<webmath object="πQ" when="\pi_Q\colon W_Q\longrightarrow Q" title="the map that discards B and A and keeps Q">
% The [[Greek letter]] \(\pi\), read “pi,” names a [[homomorphism]], and the
% [[subscript]] Q records its destination.  The colon and arrow say its inputs are
% in \(W_Q\) and its outputs are in Q.  It sends every element of B to the
% [[identity]] and sends each ordered pair \((q,a)\) in the [[direct product]]
% \(Q\times A\) to q.  Every q is reached, so the map is [[surjective]].  Its
% [[kernel]] is the [[normal closure]] of d.
%</webmath>
\[
 \pi_Q\colon W_Q\longrightarrow Q.
\]
It is split by the inclusion $Q\to Q\times A\to W_Q$.  The normal-form
theorem for amalgamated free products shows that both vertex maps in
\eqref{eq:amalgam} are injective; in particular, $d\ne1$ in $W_Q$.

\begin{proposition}[universal factorization]\label{prop:universal-factorization}
Let $T$ be a group such that every homomorphism $B\to T$ is trivial.  Then
precomposition with $\pi_Q$ is a bijection
%<webmath object="Hom" when="\operatorname{Hom}(Q,T)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(W_Q,T)" title="maps into T can be described using Q alone">
% \(\operatorname{Hom}(Q,T)\) is the [[set]] of all [[homomorphism|homomorphisms]]
% from the [[group]] Q to the group T.  The arrow sends such a map h to the
% map in two stages \(h\circ\pi_Q\) from \(W_Q\) to T; this is called
% [[precomposition]] with \(\pi_Q\).  The statement says this arrow is a
% [[bijection]]: every map on either side corresponds to exactly one map on the
% other side.  Consequently, T receives no information from \(W_Q\) beyond Q.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(Q,T)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(W_Q,T).
\]
Furthermore,
%<webmath object="ker" when="\ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}" title="the elements discarded by the map to Q">
% \(\ker\pi_Q\) is the [[kernel]] of \(\pi_Q\): exactly the elements of
% \(W_Q\) sent to the [[identity]] in Q.  The double angle brackets
% \(\normal d_{W_Q}\) mean the [[normal closure]] of d inside \(W_Q\).  The
% equals sign says these are the same [[normal subgroup]]: erasing d erases
% exactly what the map \(\pi_Q\) erases, no more and no less.
%</webmath>
\[
 \ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}.
\]
\leanverified{Manuscript/OneSidedMFRadical/UniversalFactorization}{GroupApproximation.Manuscript.OneSidedMFRadical.manuscriptUniversalFactorization}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $f\colon W_Q\to T$ be a homomorphism.  Its restriction to $B$ is
trivial, so its restriction to the amalgamated subgroup $A$ is trivial.  For
$(q,a)\in Q\times A$, we then have
%<webmath object="whole" when="f(q,a)=f(q,1)f(1,a)=f(q,1)" title="the map f ignores the A-coordinate of each pair">
% The pair \((q,a)\) in the [[direct product]] \(Q\times A\) can be factored
% as \((q,1)(1,a)\): one factor carries only the Q-coordinate and the other
% carries only the A-coordinate.  Because f is a [[homomorphism]], it turns
% that product into \(f(q,1)f(1,a)\).  The restriction of f to A is trivial,
% so \(f(1,a)\) is the [[identity]] and disappears from the product.  Thus the
% value of f depends only on q.  This is exactly why f [[factors through]] the
% projection from \(Q\times A\) onto Q.
%</webmath>
\[
 f(q,a)=f(q,1)f(1,a)=f(q,1).
\]
Thus the restriction of $f$ to $Q\times A$ factors uniquely through the
projection onto $Q$.  The universal property of the amalgam shows that $f$
factors through $\pi_Q$.  The factorization is unique because $\pi_Q$ is
surjective.

The element $d$ belongs to $\ker\pi_Q$.  Conversely, quotienting $W_Q$ by
$\normal d_{W_Q}$ kills $B$, since $\normal d_B=B$, and kills the $A$-factor
of $Q\times A$.  The resulting quotient is $Q$, with quotient map
induced by $\pi_Q$.  Hence $\ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:prescribed-quotients}]
Every homomorphism from $B$ to an MF group is trivial: compose it with a
faithful corona embedding of the target and use
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-factorization}
therefore gives the asserted bijection for every MF group $M$.  In
addition, the definition of $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$ says directly that
every homomorphism from $B$ to $\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})$ is trivial.
Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-factorization} with
$T=\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})$ shows that every corona homomorphism from
$W_Q$ factors uniquely through $\pi_Q$.  Intersecting their kernels gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)=\pi_Q^{-1}" title="the MF radical of W sub Q is pulled back from Q">
% Every homomorphism from \(W_Q\) to a [[norm matrix corona]] factors uniquely
% through the map \(\pi_Q\colon W_Q\to Q\).  Therefore an element of \(W_Q\)
% is erased by every such homomorphism exactly when its image in Q lies in the
% [[MF radical]] of Q.  The notation \(\pi_Q^{-1}\) means [[preimage]], not an
% inverse function: it collects all elements whose image has the stated
% property.  The equality says that the complete invisible part of \(W_Q\) is
% precisely the pullback of the invisible part of Q.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)
 =\pi_Q^{-1}\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr).
\]
The kernel of $\pi_Q$ belongs to this radical, and it contains the
nonidentity element $d$.  Thus $W_Q$ is non-MF.  If $Q$ is MF, its MF radical
is trivial, and the displayed equality becomes
%<webmath object="whole" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)=\ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}" title="when Q is MF, the invisible part is exactly the normal closure of d">
% If Q is an [[MF group]], its [[MF radical]] contains only the identity.
% Pulling that one-element subgroup back through \(\pi_Q\) gives the
% [[kernel]] of \(\pi_Q\), namely all elements of \(W_Q\) sent to the identity
% in Q.  The preceding proposition identifies this kernel with the [[normal
% closure]] of d in \(W_Q\).  Thus all three expressions name the same
% [[normal subgroup]].  In particular d is nontrivial but invisible to every
% allowed matrix model, so \(W_Q\) is not MF.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)=\ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}.
\]

\end{proof}

If $N\trianglelefteq W_Q$, the same factorization gives
%<webmath object="whole" when="\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^{W_Q}(N)=\pi_Q^{-1}" title="MF closure in W sub Q is the pullback of MF closure in Q">
% First send the [[normal subgroup]] N into Q, obtaining \(\pi_Q(N)\).  Take
% the [[MF closure]] of that image inside Q, which adds every element that all
% MF-target maps must erase once they erase \(\pi_Q(N)\).  Finally take the
% [[preimage]] under \(\pi_Q\), bringing the result back to \(W_Q\).  The
% equality says this three-step procedure gives exactly the MF closure of N
% computed directly inside \(W_Q\).  It works because every relevant
% [[homomorphism]] from \(W_Q\) factors uniquely through Q.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure-pullback}
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^{W_Q}(N)
 =\pi_Q^{-1}\!\left(
   \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^Q(\pi_Q(N))
  \right).
\end{equation}
Indeed, a homomorphism from $W_Q$ to an MF group kills $N$ exactly when its
unique factor through $Q$ kills $\pi_Q(N)$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-residual-calculus}
and \eqref{eq:closure-pullback} give
%<webmath object="whole" when="W_Q/N\text{ is MF}" title="the two exact conditions for the quotient W sub Q over N to be MF">
% The double arrow means the statement on the left holds exactly when both
% conditions on the right hold.  First, N must contain the [[kernel]] of
% \(\pi_Q\); otherwise the quotient would retain a nontrivial element that all
% MF models erase.  Second, after mapping N into Q, the [[quotient group]]
% \(Q/\pi_Q(N)\) must itself be an [[MF group]].  Together these conditions
% say that \(W_Q/N\) has discarded the entire invisible attached part and that
% the remaining quotient inherited from Q admits the required finite-matrix
% approximations.
%</webmath>
\[
 W_Q/N\text{ is MF}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ker\pi_Q\le N
 \text{ and }
 Q/\pi_Q(N)\text{ is MF}.
\]
Here, if $\ker\pi_Q\le N$, then
$N=\pi_Q^{-1}(\pi_Q(N))$.

Taking $Q=\mathbb Z$ makes the quotient criterion especially concrete:
for every normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq W_{\mathbb Z}$,
%<webmath object="whole" when="W_{\mathbb Z}/N\text{ is MF}" title="for this concrete group, the quotient is MF exactly when N contains d">
% Here \(\mathbb Z\) is the [[group of integers]] under addition, and every
% quotient of it is MF.  The general two-condition criterion therefore loses
% its second condition.  What remains is that N must contain the element d,
% or equivalently contain its [[normal closure]], which is the kernel of the
% map from \(W_{\mathbb Z}\) to \(\mathbb Z\).  The double arrow means both
% directions hold: containing d is sufficient for the [[quotient group]] to
% be an [[MF group]], and any MF quotient must contain d.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-word-quotient-test}
 W_{\mathbb Z}/N\text{ is MF}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d\in N.
\end{equation}
Indeed, every quotient of $\mathbb Z$ is MF, while
$\ker\pi_{\mathbb Z}=\normal d_{W_{\mathbb Z}}$.
