\title[A finitely presented group that is not MF]{A finitely presented group that is not MF}
\author{\paperauthor}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 46L05; Secondary 20F05, 22D25, 20E26, 68V20}
\keywords{MF groups, matrix coronas, property (T), Kazhdan transport,
Clifford groups}

\begin{abstract}
A countable discrete group is MF if it embeds in the unitary group of a
quotient $\prod_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)/\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)$;
equivalently, if it admits unitary matrix models that are asymptotically
multiplicative in operator norm and separate every nontrivial element
from the identity.  We construct a finitely presented group $E$, on
eight generators and forty-one relators, that is not MF\@.  This refutes
the conjecture that every countable group is MF.

In every finite quotient of a group, a subgroup and a one-sided
conjugate $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ of it have equal images: one
image contains the other, and conjugacy gives them the same
cardinality.  We prove the corresponding statement for matrix coronas,
with finiteness of a norm ultraproduct in place of the cardinality
count.

Let $\Gamma\le H$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$, let $c$ centralize $\Gamma$, and put
$d=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[d,a]$ for $a\in\Gamma$.  We prove that in every
operator-norm matrix model of $H$ conjugation by $t$ preserves the
Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of $\Gamma$; hence
$\hsnorm{u-1}\to0$.  In $E$ the element $w=u^2$ is central and
satisfies $w^2=1$, and a Clifford-group quotient of $E$ sends it to
$-1$; so $w\ne1$, while every matrix model sends $w$ to $1$.  The construction takes as
input any finitely presented property-\textup{(T)} group with a proper
injective self-embedding; $E$ is the instance built on
$\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$.

The reduced group $C^*$-algebra $\Cred(E)$ is separable and carries a
faithful tracial state, hence is stably finite, but is not MF\@.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}


Can every countable discrete group be realized by matrices, if
multiplication is only required to hold up to operator-norm errors that
vanish along a sequence of finite dimensions?  Shulman calls the positive
assertion the \emph{MF conjecture} \cite{Shulman}, and no group was known
to fail it \cite{Schafhauser,Shulman}.  We prove that the answer is no:
there is an explicit finitely presented group, on eight generators and
forty-one relators, that is not MF\@.  Residually finite groups satisfy
the conjecture through their finite quotients, as do LEF groups \cite{CDE} and, by
quasidiagonality \cite{TWW}, all amenable groups.  Recent work
establishes it for many amalgamated free products
\cite{Schafhauser,Shulman,GKEMP}.
The corresponding $C^*$-algebra question is older.  Blackadar and
Kirchberg, who introduced MF algebras, remarked that no stably finite
separable $C^*$-algebra was known not to be MF \cite{BK,CDE}.  Their
natural candidate was $\Cred(F_2)$, which Haagerup and
Thorbj{\o}rnsen later proved to be MF \cite{HT}.  The negative solution of the Connes embedding
problem subsequently supplied abstract stably finite non-MF algebras
\cite{MIPRE,FGH}.  No countable group was known whose reduced or maximal group
$C^*$-algebra fails to be MF \cite{Shulman}.

In his 2018 ICM address, Thom asked for which Schatten
$p$-norms there exist non-approximable groups \cite[Question~3.11]{Th}.
The case $p=2$ was settled by De~Chiffre--Glebsky--Lubotzky--Thom
\cite{DGLT}, the cases $1<p<\infty$ by Lubotzky--Oppenheim \cite{LuO},
and the case $p=1$ by Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky \cite{BDL}.  That left
the Hamming metric (soficity), the Hilbert--Schmidt norm
(hyperlinearity), and the operator norm (MF)\@.  The Hamming case was disproved in 2026:
the first nonsofic group was constructed by OpenAI \cite{OAI},
Fournier-Facio then gave a torsion-free example \cite{FFF}, and
Kun--Thom developed further wreath-product examples \cite{KT}.
Our construction begins, as those arguments do, with a relation
$t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ for a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup
$\Gamma$.  Soficity concerns permutations in the Hamming metric and MF
concerns unitaries in operator norm, so the rigidity input is a
different one: we prove that conjugation by $t$ preserves the
Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of $\Gamma$ in every
operator-norm matrix model.  Of the three problems, only hyperlinearity
remains open.
In the language of approximate representations, a countable discrete
group $G$ is \emph{MF} if, for every finite subset $F\subseteq G$ and
every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a unitary matrix model
$V\colon G\to\U(r)$ that is multiplicative within $\varepsilon$ in
operator norm on $F$ and separates distinct elements of $F$ by
operator-norm distance at least $1$.  There is an equivalent corona
formulation, in which the notion was introduced by
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt and is used by Shulman and
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky \cite{CDE,Shulman,BDL}.  Let $(d_n)_{n\in\N}$
be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corona}
  \mathcal Q\;=\;\prod_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)\Big/\bigoplus_{n} M_{d_n}(\C)
\end{equation}
be the associated matrix quotient: bounded sequences of matrices
modulo sequences converging to $0$ in operator norm.  We call any such
corona algebra a \emph{norm matrix corona}.  Following
Blackadar--Kirchberg \cite{BK}, a separable $C^*$-algebra is \emph{MF}
if it embeds into some algebra of the form \eqref{eq:corona}; the group
$G$ is MF if there is an injective homomorphism
$G\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.
The two formulations are equivalent
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}).
We shall use the equivalent coordinate model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary-corona}
 \Ucor((d_n))
 :=\prod_n\U(M_{d_n}(\C))\Big/
 \bigl\{(u_n):\opnorm{u_n-1}\longrightarrow0\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is normal, and polar correction gives a canonical
isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary-corona-iso}
 \kappa_{(d_n)}\colon \Ucor((d_n))\ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\
 \U(\mathcal Q),
\end{equation}
proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}.  Thus one may equivalently embed
$G$ into $\Ucor((d_n))$.
\begin{proposition}[equivalent MF formulations]
\label{prop:mf-equivalences}
For every countable group, the $C^*$-corona definition above is
equivalent to the unitary-sequence definition.  The latter is equivalent to
operator-norm local models with separation constant $1$, and allowing
arbitrary positive dimensions is equivalent to requiring the dimensions to
be strictly increasing.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMFDefinitionEquivalences}
\end{proposition}

Section~\ref{app:equivalences} defines the operator-norm local models
and proves the proposition.

Hereafter ``MF'' means this property: the operator-norm notion of
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt, in the form used by Shulman.  Stronger
conventions exist.
In \cite{GKEMP} the approximating maps must also recover the canonical
trace and the left-regular norm on every group-ring element.  The
purely matricial field (PMF) convention of \cite[Definition~1.2]{MdlS}
requires finite-dimensional representations whose group-ring norms
converge to the regular norms.

Throughout, $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ denotes the commutator of two group
elements.  The additive commutator of two operators is written
$[x,y]_{-}=xy-yx$, so that the two brackets are never confused where a
group element and its matrix image occur together.
\subsection*{The result}

\begin{mainthm}[explicit Kazhdan--Clifford group]\label{thm:A}
Let $E$ be the group on the eight generators
$v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c$ with the forty-one relators displayed in
Definition~\ref{def:E}, and put
\[
 w=[tct^{-1},\,v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}].
\]
Then $w\ne1$, while $\Theta(w)=1$ for every sequence $(d_n)$ of
positive integers and every homomorphism
$\Theta\colon E\to\Ucor((d_n))$ into the corresponding unitary norm
corona \eqref{eq:unitary-corona}.  In particular $E$ is not MF, and
neither $\Cmax(E)$ nor $\Cred(E)$ is an MF $C^*$-algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremA}
\end{mainthm}

\noindent
The defining relations make $w$ central, and centrality together with
$c^2=1$ gives $w^2=1$, so $w$ is a central involution.  The group is
written out in Definition~\ref{def:E}; Section~\ref{sec:example} proves
the theorem.

\subsection*{The shape of the argument}
Two independent implications meet at the single element $w$.  Write
$\sim_2$ for convergence in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, $\Gamma$
for the property-\textup{(T)} base with its embedding
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to E$, and $d=tct^{-1}$; then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 t\,\iota(\Gamma)\,t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma)
 \ &\Longrightarrow\ \text{Kazhdan transport}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ [\,d,\iota(a)\,]\sim_2 1\\
 &\Longrightarrow\ \text{negative central-sign corner}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \Theta(w)=1\ \text{ for every corona }\Theta,\\[4pt]
 \text{Clifford lamp quotient (Construction~\ref{con:clifford})}
 \ &\Longrightarrow\ w\mapsto\zeta=-1
 \ \Longrightarrow\ w\neq1 .
\end{aligned}
\]
The first line is analytic and the second is algebraic; they share no
ingredient, and their conjunction is the whole of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
Everything after it in this paper either certifies the explicit finitely
presented input, generalizes the obstruction, or draws consequences.

Three hypotheses do three separate jobs, and it is worth saying which is
which before the details.  Property~\textup{(T)} enters \emph{only} to
produce and control the projection onto the fixed space of the adjoint
action; finiteness of the norm ultraproduct is what reverses the
one-sided inclusion $P\le VPV^*$ into an equality; and the Clifford
quotient is what makes $w$ nontrivial.  A reader interested in the
spectral-gap step can therefore inspect
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} without first reading the
forty-one relators, and a reader interested in the group construction
can go straight to Section~\ref{sec:group}.

\subsection*{The mechanism}
Let $\pi$ be a homomorphism to a finite group and let
$t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$.  Then
$\pi(t\Gamma t^{-1})=\pi(\Gamma)$: the left side is a conjugate of the
right side contained in it, and the two are finite of the same
cardinality.  So an element $c$ centralizing $\Gamma$ has
$\pi(tct^{-1})$ centralizing $\pi(\Gamma)$, and every commutator
$[\,tct^{-1},a\,]$ with $a\in\Gamma$ lies in $\ker\pi$.

Every finite-dimensional representation $\pi$ of the ambient group over
every field gives the same conclusion, with the commutant in place of
the subgroup.  Let $C$ be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$ in
$\End_k(V)$.  One-sided conjugation gives
$C\subseteq\pi(t)\,C\,\pi(t)^{-1}$, the two sides have equal finite
dimension, and therefore
\[
 C=\pi(t)\,C\,\pi(t)^{-1}
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:B} and Remark~\ref{rem:invariantsize}).  Since
$\pi(c)\in C$, also $\pi(tct^{-1})\in C$, so it commutes with every
$\pi(a)$, $a\in\Gamma$.  The rest of the paper carries out this
commutant argument in a matrix corona: the commutant becomes a
Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant, equal dimension becomes
finiteness of a norm ultraproduct, and property~\textup{(T)} gives the
projection that finiteness is applied to.

The operator norm controls the approximations and defines the corona.
We use the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm to regard $M_d(\C)$ as the
Hilbert space $L^2(M_d(\C),\tr_d)$; conjugation by a unitary then acts
unitarily on it, so property~\textup{(T)} applies.  The passage from
the first norm to the second is dimension-free: for unitaries
$u_1,u_2$,
\[
 \bigl\lVert\Ad u_1-\Ad u_2\bigr\rVert_{B(L^2(M_d(\C),\tr_d))}
 \;\le\;2\,\opnorm{u_1-u_2},
\]
so operator-norm almost multiplicativity gives an almost representation
on the Hilbert--Schmidt spaces.  The matrix models and the separation
of group elements are defined by the operator norm alone.

Write $\sim_2$ for convergence in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.

Let $\Gamma$ be a property-\textup{(T)} group with a homomorphism
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$, and let $t,c\in H$ satisfy
\[
 t\,\iota(\Gamma)\,t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma),\qquad
 [c,\iota(\Gamma)]=1 .
\]
An operator-norm almost representation of $H$ acts by conjugation on
the Hilbert--Schmidt space of each matrix block, almost
multiplicatively by the estimate above; let $\pi$ be the resulting
representation of $H$ in the norm ultraproduct $B_\omega$ of the
blocks, acting on the Hilbert-space ultraproduct $K_\omega$.  The
Kazhdan gap isolates the eigenvalue $1$ of the Hermitian average of the
$\pi(\iota(s))$ over a Kazhdan generating set, so functional calculus
gives the orthogonal projection $P\in B_\omega$ onto the
$\iota(\Gamma)$-fixed subspace of $K_\omega$.  Put $V=\pi(t)$.  The
inclusion $t\iota(\Gamma)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma)$ gives
$P\le VPV^*$; the two projections are Murray--von Neumann equivalent,
and $B_\omega$ contains no proper isometry, so
\[
 P=VPV^* .
\]
Hence conjugation by $t$ preserves the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic
commutant of $\iota(\Gamma)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}).

Put $d=tct^{-1}$ and, for $a\in\Gamma$,
\[
 u=[\,d,\iota(a)\,].
\]
Since $c$ commutes with $\iota(\Gamma)$, the lifts of $c$ lie in that
asymptotic commutant, and the theorem puts the lifts of $d$ there as
well; hence
\[
 u\sim_2 1
\]
in every operator-norm matrix model of $H$.

Suppose now that $\varepsilon=u^2$ is central with $\varepsilon^2=1$,
and let $\Theta$ be a corona representation with
$\Theta(\varepsilon)\ne1$.  Then $\Theta(\varepsilon)$ is a
self-adjoint unitary commuting with $\Theta(H)$, so
\[
 q=\tfrac12\bigl(1-\Theta(\varepsilon)\bigr)
\]
is a nonzero projection commuting with $\Theta(H)$.  Lifting $\Theta$
to coordinates and compressing each block to $q$ gives a second
operator-norm matrix model of $H$, whose blocks have their own
normalized traces, in which $\varepsilon=-1$.  On that model
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} gives $u^2\sim_2 1$, while
$\hsnorm{-1-1}=2$.  Hence $\Theta(\varepsilon)=1$ for every corona
representation $\Theta$, and if $\varepsilon\ne1$ then $H$ is not MF
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}).

For $\varepsilon\ne1$, adjoin $t$ and $c$ to a finitely presented
Kazhdan group $\Gamma$ that is not co-Hopfian, along a proper injective
endomorphism $\alpha$, and take $a\in\Gamma\setminus\alpha(\Gamma)$.  In
the mapping telescope of $\alpha$ the cosets $t\Gamma$ and $at\Gamma$
are distinct, since $t^{-1}at\in\Gamma$ would put $a$ in
$\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Send those two cosets to distinct generators of a
Clifford group: distinct generators anticommute, so $u$ becomes a
product of two anticommuting involutions and $w=u^2=-1$ in that
quotient (Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford}).

Taking
\[
 \Gamma=\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z),\qquad
 \alpha(v,A)=(2v,A),\qquad a=(e_1,I)
\]
gives the group $E$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

Only the subgroup $\iota(\Gamma)$ is assumed to have
property~\textup{(T)}.  The group $E$ surjects onto $\mathbb Z$, by
sending $t$ to $1$ and every other generator to $0$, so $E$ itself does
not have property~\textup{(T)}.

Three further results are proved below.  The quotient
$E/\langle w\rangle$ is again not MF: the image of $u$ is
nontrivial there and lies in the kernel of every corona representation,
so $\{1,w\}$ is a proper subgroup of the MF radical of $E$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:signfree}).  The reduced group $C^*$-algebra
$\Cred(E)$ is separable and stably finite but not MF
(Theorem~\ref{thm:D}).  The Clifford quotient $W$ used to prove
$w\ne1$ is finitely generated, sofic, and not MF, so neither soficity
nor hyperlinearity implies the MF property (Theorem~\ref{thm:E});
soficity of $E$ itself is not decided by this method
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

A Lean~4 formalization accompanies the paper.  It proves the main
statements, the literal presentation, and the finite-stage form of the
transport theorem; the repository metadata records which declaration
corresponds to each numbered statement.

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}
The closest analogue is the recent work on nonsofic groups.  One-sided
conjugation of a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup, with propagation from
its centralizer, appears in \cite{OAI,KT}, building on rigidity results
of Kun and Kun--Thom \cite{Kun16,KT19}; Alekseev--Thom prove a related
centralizer rigidity for sofic embeddings \cite{AlekseevThom}.  Those
results concern Hamming or tracial approximations.  Here the adjoint
actions stay on the Hilbert--Schmidt spaces of the matrix blocks, and
$C^*$-finiteness of the norm ultraproduct forbids a proper one-sided
compression of the Kazhdan fixed space; the quantitative form is the
equal-rank reversal estimate of Section~\ref{app:finite-stage}.

Slofstra's hyperlinear-profile construction uses a Clifford group, a
distinguished central involution, a shift, and an HNN doubling map
\cite[Section~2 and Remark~3.3]{SlofstraProfile}, and his quotients by
the elements invisible to exact or approximate finite-dimensional
representations precede the MF radical used below
\cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}.  Negative-eigenspace
compression appears in Slofstra--Vidick
\cite[proof of Proposition~2.7]{SlofstraVidick}, and Fritz's marked
$\mathrm{BS}(2,3)$ example is the context for the cyclic comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:necessity} \cite{Fritz,SlofstraVidick}.
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky develop the operator-norm rounding,
compression, and polar-correction procedures used in the finite-normal
corner reduction \cite[Lemmas~2.2--2.3, Proposition~2.4, and the proof
of Proposition~1.5]{BDL}; in the proof of their Proposition~1.6 they
treat the finite-central case by a character-isotypic corner and reduce
the general finite-normal case to it through the finite-index
centralizer.  Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} below combines that
coordinate corner with one-sided Kazhdan conjugation, and its
hypotheses constrain only the subgroup, not the quotient.  Central
extensions and rigidity of approximate representations also drive the
Frobenius obstruction of De~Chiffre--Glebsky--Lubotzky--Thom
\cite{DGLT}; finite-target approximation and extension results for weak
soficity appear in \cite{GR,Gl19}.

The passage from operator norm to the Hilbert--Schmidt conjugation
representation belongs to the $\pi\otimes\bar\pi$ framework of Bekka
and Bekka--Valette \cite{BekkaAmenable,BekkaValette}, and Dadarlat uses
normalized finite-rank projections as almost invariant
Hilbert--Schmidt vectors in an operator-norm approximation argument
\cite[Lemma~3.18 and Proposition~3.19]{Dadarlat}.  The group-MF
framework is that of Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt, who also give,
through Abels' group, an amenable MF group that is not residually
finite \cite[Section~2.14 and Corollary~2.18]{CDE}.

The group property is weaker than the algebra property: it asks only for
a faithful canonical image in some MF quotient of $\Cmax(E)$, so failure
of MF for a group $C^*$-algebra leaves open whether the group embeds in
a corona.  For $E$ both fail, and for the same reason.

\section{Norm matrix coronas}\label{sec:prelim}

All groups are discrete and countable.  Property~\textup{(T)} is the
standard formulation for unitary representations on complex Hilbert
spaces.  A $C^*$-homomorphism is unital only when
this is stated.  In particular, an MF embedding may be nonunital, as is
customary for embedding a unital algebra as a $C^*$-subalgebra.  If
$e\colon A\to B$ is an injective, possibly nonunital $*$-homomorphism between
unital $C^*$-algebras, then $u\mapsto e(u)+(1-e(1))$ is an injective
homomorphism $\U(A)\to\U(B)$; this complement correction is used below.  For
a unital $C^*$-algebra $A$
we write $\U(A)$ for its unitary group.  On $M_r(\C)$ we use the
normalized trace $\tr_r$, the operator norm $\opnorm{\cdot}$, and the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
$\hsnorm{x}=\tr_r(x^*x)^{1/2}$.  For coordinate sequences we write
$a\sim_2 b$ when $\hsnorm{a_n-b_n}\to0$.  We use without comment:
$\hsnorm{x}\le\opnorm{x}$;
$|\tr_r(x)|\le\opnorm{x}$; and $\hsnorm{uxv}=\hsnorm{x}$ for unitaries
$u,v$.  For a nonempty finite set $Y$, write
$M_Y(\C)=\C^{Y\times Y}$ for the corresponding indexed matrix algebra.

Given a sequence $(d_n)_{n\in\N}$ of positive integers, we write
\[
  \mathcal Q\bigl((d_n)_{n\in\N}\bigr)
  \;=\;\prod_{n\in\N}^{\ell^\infty} M_{d_n}(\C)
  \Big/\bigoplus_{n\in\N}^{c_0} M_{d_n}(\C),
\]
the quotient of the bounded $C^*$-product by the ideal of sequences with
$\opnorm{x_n}\to0$.  We call any such algebra a \emph{norm matrix
corona}.  We also write
\[
 \Ucor((d_n)_{n\in\N})
 =\prod_{n\in\N}\U(M_{d_n}(\C))\Big/
 \bigl\{(u_n):\opnorm{u_n-1}\longrightarrow0\bigr\}.
\]
Write $q\colon\prod_{n\in\N}^{\ell^\infty}M_{d_n}(\C)\to\mathcal Q$
for the quotient map of a norm matrix corona.

The following lifting facts are standard perturbation theory; see
Loring \cite{Loring} for a systematic treatment.

\begin{lemma}[lifting]\label{lem:lift}
Every unitary $u\in\mathcal Q$ lifts to a sequence of unitaries.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUnitaryLifting}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lift $u$ to a bounded sequence $(x_n)$; unitarity of $q((x_n))$ gives
$\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$.  Once $\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\le\tfrac12$,
the matrix $x_n$ is invertible and the polar correction
$u_n=x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$ is unitary with
$\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\le2\opnorm{x_n}\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$ by
continuous functional calculus; set $u_n=1$ at the finitely many
remaining indices.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[unitaries in the quotient]\label{lem:unitarycorona}
For every sequence $(d_n)$, the map
\[
 \kappa_{(d_n)}\colon \Ucor((d_n))\longrightarrow\U(\mathcal Q),
 \qquad [(u_n)]\longmapsto q((u_n)),
\]
is a canonical group isomorphism.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUnitaryCoronaEquivalence}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The kernel consists exactly of the unitary sequences with
$\opnorm{u_n-1}\to0$, giving injectivity; surjectivity is
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}.
\end{proof}

A \emph{corona representation} of a group $H$ is a homomorphism
$H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.  An
\emph{operator-norm asymptotic representation} of $H$ is a sequence of
maps $U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ such that
$\opnorm{U_n(g)U_n(h)-U_n(gh)}\to0$ for all $g,h\in H$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} canonically identifies a corona
representation with a homomorphism into $\Ucor((d_n))$, which we call
its coordinate model.  A countable group is \emph{MF} if it admits an
injective corona representation for a strictly increasing dimension
sequence; by the dimension-amplification equivalence, allowing an
arbitrary positive dimension sequence gives the same class of groups.
Taking $g=h=1$ gives $\opnorm{U_n(1)-1}\to0$ automatically, since
$U_n(1)$ is unitary.  Coordinate unitary lifts of a corona
representation of a countable group form exactly such a sequence.

Section~\ref{app:maxcstar} constructs the maximal group $C^*$-algebra
$\Cmax(H)$ and proves its universal property.

\section{One-sided Kazhdan transport}\label{sec:transport}

Three finite statements of one principle:
\[
 \begin{array}{c@{\quad}c@{\quad}c}
 X\subseteq Y,\ |X|=|Y| & V\subseteq W,\ \dim V=\dim W
   & \substack{p\le q,\ p\sim q\\
       \text{in a finite }C^*\text{-algebra}}\\[2mm]
 \Downarrow & \Downarrow & \Downarrow\\[-1mm]
 X=Y & V=W & p=q.
 \end{array}
\]
In the right-hand column, $p\sim q$ is Murray--von Neumann equivalence
and \emph{finite} is meant in the $C^*$-sense: no proper isometries.
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} uses the middle implication, applied to the
commutant $C$ of the base inside a finite-dimensional endomorphism
algebra: the one-sided conjugation carries $C$ into a conjugate copy of
itself, of the same finite dimension, so the two coincide.  The proof below uses
the right-hand implication, applied to the projection $P$ onto the
Kazhdan fixed space inside a norm ultraproduct: the one-sided
conjugation gives $P\le VPV^*$, the two projections are Murray--von
Neumann equivalent, and the ultraproduct has no proper isometry, so
$P=VPV^*$.

\begin{theorem}[Kazhdan transport of Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutants]
\label{thm:kazhdan-transport}
Let $\Gamma$ and $H$ be groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let $\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism, let
$s\in H$, and let $(d_n)$ be a sequence of positive integers.  Suppose
\[
 s\iota(\Gamma)s^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma).
\]
Let $U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic representation.
If $(x_n)$ is uniformly operator-norm bounded and asymptotically
centralizes $\iota(\Gamma)$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm,
\[
 \hsnorm{x_nU_n(\iota(\gamma))-U_n(\iota(\gamma))x_n}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma),
\]
then the conjugated sequence does as well:
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.manuscriptKazhdanTransport}
\[
\hsnorm{[U_n(s)x_nU_n(s)^*,U_n(\iota(\gamma))]_{-}}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the conclusion fails: there are $\gamma_0\in\Gamma$,
$\delta>0$, and an infinite set $I\subseteq\N$ with
$\hsnorm{[U_n(s)x_nU_n(s)^*,U_n(\iota(\gamma_0))]_{-}}\ge\delta$ for
$n\in I$.  Fix a free ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\N$ with $I\in\omega$.

\emph{The adjoint model.}  Regard $M_{d_n}(\C)$ as the Hilbert space
$K_n=L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$ and let each group element act on it
by conjugation, $\Ad U_n(g)\,\xi=U_n(g)\,\xi\,U_n(g)^*$.
Operator-norm almost multiplicativity of $U_n$ makes
$g\mapsto\Ad U_n(g)$ an operator-norm asymptotic representation on
these Hilbert spaces; this is the finite-stage form of the
$\pi\otimes\bar\pi$ construction.

\emph{The ultraproduct.}  Let $K_\omega$ be the Hilbert-space
ultraproduct of the $K_n$ along $\omega$ and let
$B_\omega=\prod_\omega B(K_n)$ be the norm ultraproduct, acting on
$K_\omega$ by $[A_n]_\omega[\xi_n]_\omega=[A_n\xi_n]_\omega$; write
$[\,\cdot\,]_\omega$ for classes in either.  This action is faithful:
if $A=[A_n]_\omega\neq0$ then $\lim_\omega\opnorm{A_n}=\delta>0$, and
unit vectors $\xi_n$ with
$\lVert A_n\xi_n\rVert\ge\opnorm{A_n}-\tfrac1n$ give
$\lVert A[\xi_n]_\omega\rVert=\lim_\omega\lVert A_n\xi_n\rVert=\delta$.
An inclusion of ranges in $K_\omega$ may therefore be read back as an
inequality of projections in $B_\omega$: if
$\operatorname{ran}P\subseteq\operatorname{ran}Q$ for projections
$P,Q\in B_\omega$, then $QP$ and $P$ act identically on $K_\omega$,
so $QP=P$, that is, $P\le Q$.
Asymptotic multiplicativity makes
\[
 \pi\colon H\longrightarrow\U(B_\omega),\qquad
 \pi(g)=[\Ad U_n(g)]_\omega,
\]
a homomorphism.  The algebra $B_\omega$ is \emph{finite}.  Indeed,
suppose $\sigma=[\sigma_n]_\omega$ satisfies $\sigma^*\sigma=1$.  Then
$\opnorm{\sigma_n^*\sigma_n-1}\to0$ along $\omega$, and each
$\sigma_n$ acts on a finite-dimensional space.  Polar correction
therefore replaces $\sigma_n$ by a unitary $w_n$ with
$\opnorm{\sigma_n-w_n}\to0$.  Hence $\sigma=[w_n]_\omega$ is unitary.

\emph{The projection onto the fixed subspace.}
Property~\textup{(T)} provides a finite
symmetric generating set $S\subseteq\Gamma$ with $1\in S$ and a Kazhdan
constant $\kappa\in(0,1]$ for the pair $(S,\kappa)$.  Let $h$ be the
Hermitian part of $\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s'\in S}\pi(\iota(s'))$ and let
$\mathrm{Fix}\subseteq K_\omega$ be the subspace of vectors fixed by
every $\pi(\iota(\gamma))$.  Both $\mathrm{Fix}$ and its
orthocomplement are invariant under each unitary $\pi(\iota(s'))$,
hence under $h$.  On $\mathrm{Fix}$, $h=1$.  The restriction of
$\pi\circ\iota$ to $\mathrm{Fix}^\perp$ has no nonzero invariant
vector, so a unit vector $\xi\in\mathrm{Fix}^\perp$ satisfies
$\lVert\pi(\iota(s'))\xi-\xi\rVert\ge\kappa$ for some generator.  For
that generator
$\operatorname{Re}\langle\pi(\iota(s'))\xi,\xi\rangle\le1-\kappa^2/2$,
while every other term is at most $1$.  Averaging gives
$\langle h\xi,\xi\rangle\le1-\kappa^2/(2|S|)$.  Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{sp}(h)\subseteq
 \Bigl[-1,\,1-\tfrac{\kappa^2}{2|S|}\Bigr]\cup\{1\},
\]
and the spectral projection $P$ of $h$ at the isolated point $1$
belongs to $B_\omega$ by continuous functional calculus and projects
onto $\mathrm{Fix}$.

\emph{One-sided compression and finiteness.}  Let $V=\pi(s)$ and
$Q=VPV^*$.  Since $s\iota(\Gamma)s^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma)$, every
vector of $\mathrm{Fix}$ is fixed by each
$\pi(s\iota(\gamma)s^{-1})$.  On the other hand, $\eta$ is fixed by
all $\pi(s\iota(\gamma)s^{-1})$ exactly when
$V^*\eta\in\mathrm{Fix}$.  Hence
$\operatorname{ran}P=\mathrm{Fix}\subseteq V\,\mathrm{Fix}
=\operatorname{ran}Q$, that is, $P\le Q$.
The element $r=V^*Q$ satisfies $r^*r=Q$ and $rr^*=V^*QV=P$.  Since
$r^*r=Q$, we have $r=rQ$.  Since
$\operatorname{ran}r=\operatorname{ran}P\subseteq\operatorname{ran}Q$,
we also have $r=Qr$.  Hence the cross terms in $\sigma^*\sigma$ vanish
for $\sigma=r+(1-Q)$, and $\sigma^*\sigma=Q+(1-Q)=1$: $\sigma$ is an
isometry.  Finiteness of $B_\omega$ makes
$\sigma$ unitary, and $\sigma\sigma^*=P+(1-Q)$ forces $Q=P$.

\emph{Conclusion.}  Let $\xi=[\xi_n]_\omega\in K_\omega$, where
$\xi_n\in K_n$ is the vector of $x_n$; the uniform operator-norm bound
makes $\xi$ well defined.  By unitary invariance of the normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm, the commutator hypothesis says exactly that each
$\pi(\iota(\gamma))$ fixes $\xi$.  Thus $\xi\in\mathrm{Fix}$.  Since $Q=P$, we obtain
$V\xi\in V\,\mathrm{Fix}=\operatorname{ran}Q=\operatorname{ran}P
=\mathrm{Fix}$.
The vector of $U_n(s)x_nU_n(s)^*$ is $\Ad U_n(s)\,\xi_n$, so, again by
unitary invariance,
\[
 \hsnorm{[U_n(s)x_nU_n(s)^*,U_n(\iota(\gamma))]_{-}}
 =\bigl\lVert(\Ad U_n(\iota(\gamma))-1)\,\Ad U_n(s)\,\xi_n\bigr\rVert
 \longrightarrow0
\]
along $\omega$, for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$.  Taking $\gamma=\gamma_0$
contradicts $I\in\omega$.  Since the failing subsequence was
arbitrary, the full sequence converges.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the finite-stage proof]\label{rem:finite-stage}
The proof above is qualitative, since it passes to an ultrafilter.
Section~\ref{app:finite-stage} proves the theorem a second time,
quantitatively at each single coordinate $n$: an explicit almost-fixed
spectral subspace of the averaged adjoint action replaces the
projection $P$, and the equal-rank reversal estimate replaces
finiteness of $B_\omega$.  The rate there is explicit in $\kappa$,
$|S|$, the uniform bound on $(x_n)$, and the multiplicative defects of
$U_n$, once the robust spectral-gap input stated there is granted at
each stage.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The finite-dimensional case}
Let $k$ be a field, let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space,
let $\pi\colon H\to\GL_k(V)$ be a representation, and let
\[
 C=\{x\in\End_k(V):\pi(\gamma)x=x\pi(\gamma)\ \text{for all}\
 \gamma\in\Gamma\}
\]
be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$.  Write $\Phi(x)=\pi(t)\,x\,\pi(t)^{-1}$
for conjugation by $\pi(t)$, a linear automorphism of $\End_k(V)$.  The
one-sided relation $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ gives
$C\subseteq\Phi(C)$, and the two subspaces then have the same finite
dimension, so
\[
 C\subseteq\Phi(C),\quad \dim_kC=\dim_k\Phi(C)
 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad C=\Phi(C).
\]
Since $\pi(c)\in C$, the operator $\pi(tct^{-1})=\Phi(\pi(c))$ again
lies in $C$, so it commutes with $\pi(a)$ for every $a\in\Gamma$ and
\[
 \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\,a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)=1 .
\]
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.  The argument is algebraic; it uses no
property~\textup{(T)} and no hypothesis on the characteristic.  Applied
to $E$ it shows that every finite-dimensional linear representation
over every field, and hence every homomorphism to a finite group, sends
the marked word to the identity (Corollary~\ref{cor:notRFD}).

Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} is the approximate analogue of
this argument.  An MF model is only asymptotically multiplicative, so
its coordinate fixed spaces are not invariant subspaces; operator-norm
control keeps the adjoint actions almost multiplicative on the
Hilbert--Schmidt spaces, an almost-fixed spectral subspace of a Kazhdan
average takes the place of $C$, and the equal-rank reversal estimate of
Section~\ref{app:finite-stage} takes the place of equal dimension.
Property~\textup{(T)} is what extends the conclusion from exact
finite-dimensional representations to approximate operator-norm models.
The cyclic comparison of Section~\ref{sec:necessity} shows that it is
needed: there is a marked group whose word is trivial in every
finite-dimensional representation and nontrivial in an MF model.

\section{The central-sign obstruction}\label{sec:sign}

Let $\varepsilon$ be a central involution of $H$ and let $\Theta$ be a
corona representation with $\Theta(\varepsilon)\ne1$.  Then
$\Theta(\varepsilon)$ is a self-adjoint unitary commuting with
$\Theta(H)$, the projection $\tfrac12(1-\Theta(\varepsilon))$ is
nonzero, and compressing to it gives a matrix model of $H$ in which
$\varepsilon$ equals $-1$ and each block has its own normalized trace.

The corner is what turns the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} into an obstruction.  That theorem
bounds a commutator in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, and padding a
matrix model with an identity block leaves every operator-norm quantity
unchanged while sending all normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distances to
zero (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).  Compressing to the corner changes the
Hilbert--Schmidt scale and not the approximation norm: the operator norm
on $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n$ is the restriction of the operator norm, while
the trace is renormalized on the corner, so
$\hsnorm{1-(-1)}=2$ there for the corner's own normalized trace,
however small the ratio $r_n/d_n$ becomes.

\begin{theorem}[central-sign criterion]\label{thm:sign-criterion}
Let $H$ and $\Gamma$ be countable groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism, and suppose
\[
 t\iota(\Gamma)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma),
 \qquad [c,\iota(\Gamma)]=1.
\]
Put $d=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[d,\iota(a)]$ for some $a\in\Gamma$.
Assume that $\varepsilon:=u^2$ is a nontrivial central involution of
$H$.  Then every homomorphism from $H$ to a norm matrix corona maps
$\varepsilon$ to the identity, and $H$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanSignCriterion}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCompressionCore.manuscriptCentralSignCriterion}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a corona representation and
suppose $\Theta(\varepsilon)\ne1$.

\emph{The negative corner.}  Choose coordinate unitary lifts
$(V_{g,n})$ of $\Theta$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}) and put
the averaging operator
$p_n=\tfrac12(1+V_{\varepsilon,n})$ of the
two-element subgroup $F=\{1,\varepsilon\}$.  Because the lifts are
asymptotically multiplicative and $\varepsilon^2=1$, the Hermitian
part of $p_n$ is asymptotically idempotent, so its spectrum clusters
at $\{0,1\}$ and rounding at $\tfrac12$ by continuous functional
calculus produces genuine projections at operator-norm distance $o(1)$
from $p_n$; centrality of $\varepsilon$ makes them asymptotically
central for all of $\Theta(H)$.  Let $q_n$ be their complements.  If
$q_n$ vanished for all large $n$ the averages would converge to $1$ and
$\Theta(\varepsilon)$ would be $1$; so $q_n\ne0$ along infinitely
many coordinates, which we retain and relabel by $\N$.  Compressing
every lift to $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\cong M_{r_n}(\C)$ and polar-correcting
the compressions --- each is almost unitary in the corner, by
asymptotic centrality --- gives an operator-norm asymptotic
representation $(W_{g,n})$ of $H$ on nonzero blocks with
\[
 \opnorm{W_{\varepsilon,n}+1}\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Equip each corner block with its own normalized trace $\tr_{r_n}$; all
estimates below are relative to $r_n$ and independent of the ratio
$r_n/d_n$.

\emph{Transport on the corner.}  Since $c$ centralizes
$\iota(\Gamma)$, the sequence $(W_{c,n})$ centralizes
$(W_{\iota(\gamma),n})$ asymptotically in operator norm, hence in
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  The compression relation
$t\iota(\Gamma)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\Gamma)$ and
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}, applied to $x_n=W_{c,n}$, put the
conjugated sequence $(W_{d,n})$ in the same asymptotic commutant.
Therefore $\hsnorm{W_{u,n}-1}\to0$, and squaring gives
$\hsnorm{W_{u,n}^2-1}\to0$.

\emph{The contradiction.}  The relation $\varepsilon=u^2$ gives
$\opnorm{W_{u,n}^2-W_{\varepsilon,n}}\to0$, while
$W_{\varepsilon,n}\to-1$ in operator norm and therefore also in
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Hence $\hsnorm{W_{u,n}^2+1}\to0$
and $\hsnorm{W_{u,n}^2-1}\to0$, so $\hsnorm{2\cdot1}\to0$, which is
false.  Thus $\Theta(\varepsilon)=1$.  Since $\varepsilon\ne1$, no
corona representation of $H$ is injective, and $H$ is not MF.
\end{proof}

Finiteness of $F=\{1,\varepsilon\}$ entered only through the averaging
operator $p_n$.  Section~\ref{sec:conj} proves the same conclusion for an
arbitrary finite normal subgroup of the compression defect
(Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}), and then for an arbitrary normal
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup of it (Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan}),
where a spectral projection of a Kazhdan average takes the place of the
negative corner.

\section{The Kazhdan--Clifford construction}\label{sec:construction}

Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely presented Kazhdan group, let $\alpha$ be a
proper injective endomorphism of it, and let
$a\in\Gamma\setminus\alpha(\Gamma)$.  These data give both the
configuration of Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion} and a nontrivial
sign.

With $d=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[d,a]$, put
\[
 w=u^2 .
\]
Since $d$ is an involution, $w=[\,d,ada^{-1}]$, a word in the
generators, so centrality of $w$ is a relation among them; centrality
then forces $w^2=1$ by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal} below.

\begin{theorem}[Kazhdan--Clifford construction]
\label{thm:kazhdan-clifford}
Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely presented group with property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\alpha\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Gamma$ be an injective endomorphism, and
choose $a\in\Gamma\setminus\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Adjoin elements $t,c$ and impose
\[
 t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma),\qquad c^2=1,\qquad [c,\gamma]=1
 \quad(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]
Put
\[
 d=tct^{-1},\qquad u=[d,a],\qquad w=u^2=[d,ada^{-1}],
\]
and impose centrality of $w$.
More explicitly, for a finite generating set $S_\Gamma$ it is enough to
impose the conjugation and commutation relations for $s\in S_\Gamma$, together with
\[
 [w,s]=1\quad(s\in S_\Gamma),\qquad [w,t]=[w,c]=1.
\]
Thus $w$ commutes with a generating set of the entire resulting group.  The
group $E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is finitely presented,
the canonical map $\Gamma\to E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is injective, and $w$ is a
nontrivial central involution.  Every homomorphism
\[
 E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)\longrightarrow \U(\mathcal Q)
\]
to the unitary group of every norm matrix corona maps $w$ to $1$.  Consequently
$E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanCliffordConstruction}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCliffordConstruction.kazhdanCliffordConstruction}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite presentability follows from a relative finite presentation: start with
the free product of $\Gamma$ and the free group on $t,c$, and quotient by the
finitely many displayed relations for a finite generating set of $\Gamma$.

The relation $w^2=1$ is redundant.  Put $b=ada^{-1}$.  Both $d$ and $b$ are
involutions, $w=[d,b]=(db)^2$, and direct cancellation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:involution-reversal}
 dwd^{-1}=w^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Centrality also gives $dwd^{-1}=w$, whence $w=w^{-1}$ and $w^2=1$.

For nontriviality, realize the ascending HNN extension
\[
 G=\langle\Gamma,t\mid t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)\rangle
\]
as a mapping telescope.  Let $\Gamma_\infty$ be the direct limit of the
system $\Gamma\xrightarrow{\alpha}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\alpha}\cdots$;
injectivity of $\alpha$ passes to the limit, so the level-zero map embeds
$\Gamma$ in $\Gamma_\infty$.  Applying $\alpha$ levelwise induces an
injective endomorphism $\sigma$ of $\Gamma_\infty$.  It is surjective,
hence an automorphism, because an element at level $k$ equals $\sigma$
of the same element at level $k+1$.  Put
$G=\Gamma_\infty\rtimes_\sigma\Z$, with stable letter $t$ implementing
$\sigma$; then $t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma$.
Write $X=G/\Gamma$ for the coset space of the level-zero copy.  Let
$G$ permute the Clifford generators $(e_x)_{x\in X}$ and map $c$ to
$e_\Gamma$.  Then
\[
 d\longmapsto e_{t\Gamma},\qquad
 ada^{-1}\longmapsto e_{at\Gamma}.
\]
The two cosets are distinct: equality would give
$t^{-1}at\in\Gamma$, equivalently $a\in\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Hence the two
Clifford generators anticommute and
\[
 w\longmapsto(e_{t\Gamma}e_{at\Gamma})^2=-1.
\]
The Clifford sign is central, so the map respects every defining relation.
It follows simultaneously that $w\ne1$ and that the canonical map of
$\Gamma$ is injective.

Finally, $w=u^2$ is a nontrivial central involution.  By the
central-sign criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}), every
corona representation maps $w$ to the identity.  Since $w\ne1$, none
is faithful.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a small-cancellation realization]
\label{rem:ff-realization}
Fournier-Facio has pointed out (personal communication, 2026) that the
affine base of Sections~\ref{sec:group}--\ref{sec:witness} can be
replaced by a small-cancellation construction as in \cite{FFF}.  His
finitely presented torsion-free property-\textup{(T)} group $P$
contains a copy of every finitely presented torsion-free group, in
particular a direct product $P_1\times P_2$ with $P_i\cong P$.  Then
$\alpha\colon P\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}P_1\le P$, together with any
$a\in P_2\setminus\{1\}$, satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford}.  The group $E(P,\alpha,a)$
contains involutions whatever the base.  The affine base is used for

Theorems~\ref{thm:D} and~\ref{thm:E}.
\end{remark}

\section{A finitely presented non-MF group}\label{sec:example}

Specialize Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-data}
 \Gamma=\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z),\qquad
 \alpha(v,A)=(2v,A),\qquad a=(e_1,I).
\end{equation}
The affine group $\Gamma$ is finitely presented and has
property~\textup{(T)} \cite[Example~1.7.4(i)]{BHV};
Section~\ref{sec:group} also proves this from the presentation used
below, by an exact rational sum-of-squares certificate.  The
endomorphism $\alpha$ is injective because
doubling is injective on $\mathbb Z^3$, it is proper because
$e_1\notin2\mathbb Z^3$, and $a\notin\alpha(\Gamma)$ for the same
reason.  Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} therefore applies, and
relative to $\Gamma$ the resulting group has the finite presentation
\[
 E=\left\langle\,\Gamma,t,c \ \middle|\
 \begin{gathered}
  t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma),\quad
  [c,\gamma]=1\quad(\gamma\in S_\Gamma),\\
  c^2=1,\qquad [w,g]=1
 \end{gathered}\,\right\rangle,
\]
where $S_\Gamma$ is a finite generating set of $\Gamma$, $g$ runs over
the generators, and $w=[\,tct^{-1},\,a\,(tct^{-1})\,a^{-1}]$.
Expanding a standard presentation of the affine group in the
generators $v_1,v_2,v_3$ for the translations and $x,y,z$ for the
linear part turns this into the eight-generator, forty-one-relator
presentation written out in Definition~\ref{def:E}; a Tietze
optimization removes two generators and nine relators
(Section~\ref{sec:group}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
That $w\ne1$ is Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.

Let $\Theta\colon E\to\Ucor((d_n))$ be given and pass to
the corona representation $\kappa_{(d_n)}\circ\Theta\colon
E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}.  Apply the
central-sign criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}) with
$\Gamma=\mathcal B$, the canonical homomorphism $\iota$, the elements
$t,c\in E$, and $a=v_1$.  Its hypotheses are the displayed relators:
Proposition~\ref{prop:literal-base-T} gives property~\textup{(T)} of
$\mathcal B$; the six stable-letter relators give
$t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\mathcal B)$; and the six
$c$-commutation relators give $[c,\iota(\mathcal B)]=1$.  In $E$ we
have $d^2=(tct^{-1})^2=tc^2t^{-1}=1$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:square} with
$a_1=\iota(v_1)$ gives
\[
  w=\bigl[\,d,\;\iota(v_1)\,d\,\iota(v_1)^{-1}\bigr]
  =[\,d,\iota(v_1)\,]^{\,2}=u^2 ,
  \qquad u=[\,d,\iota(v_1)\,].
\]
Centrality of $w$ is a defining relator; with $c^2=1$ it gives $w^2=1$
by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}; and $w\ne1$ as above.  Hence
$(\kappa_{(d_n)}\circ\Theta)(w)=1$, and $\Theta(w)=1$ because
$\kappa_{(d_n)}$ is injective.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.negativeCorner_kazhdanTransport_contradiction}

An MF embedding of $E$ would be an injective corona representation, and
every corona representation maps the nontrivial element $w$ to $1$; so
$E$ is not MF\@.  Suppose $\Cred(E)$ were an MF algebra, with an
injective, possibly nonunital $*$-homomorphism
$e\colon\Cred(E)\to\mathcal Q$.  Complement correction gives the
injective unitary homomorphism $u\mapsto e(u)+(1-e(1))$; composing it
with the canonical map $E\to\U(\Cred(E))$, which is injective because
$g\mapsto\lambda_g$ is, and then with $\kappa_{(d_n)}^{-1}$, exhibits
an injective corona representation of $E$.  The canonical map
$E\to\U(\Cmax(E))$ is injective as well, since the left regular
representation factors through $\Cmax(E)$ and sends $u_g$ to
$\lambda_g$, so an MF embedding of $\Cmax(E)$ produces an injective
corona representation of $E$ after the same conversion.
\end{proof}

The two inputs of this proof are proved from the displayed relators:
property~\textup{(T)} of $\mathcal B$ in Section~\ref{sec:group}, and
the Clifford model witnessing $w\ne1$ in Section~\ref{sec:witness}.

The obstruction already appears at a finite tolerance, on a finite set
of group elements, in every dimension.

\begin{corollary}[uniform finite operator-norm obstruction]\label{cor:uniform}
There exist $\delta>0$ and a finite set $F_0\subset E$ such that,
in every matrix dimension, any unitary-valued map $\phi$ satisfying
\[
 \opnorm{\phi(gh)-\phi(g)\phi(h)}\leq\delta
 \qquad(g,h\in F_0)
\]
also satisfies $\opnorm{\phi(w)-1}<1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralUniformObstruction}{GroupApproximation.LiteralUniformObstruction.literal_uniform_operatorNorm_obstruction}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose no such pair $(\delta,F_0)$ existed.  For each $n$, choose a
model violating the conclusion for $\delta=1/n$ and the $n$th set of an
exhaustion of $E$.  These models form an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $E$ whose values at $w$ stay at distance at least $1$
from the identity.  Its class in the unitary-sequence corona is a
corona representation $\Theta$ with $\Theta(w)\ne1$, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.  The compactness argument is not quantitative;
determining an effective modulus from the rational spectral
certificate of Section~\ref{sec:group} remains open.
\end{proof}

\section{The position of the example}\label{sec:position}

Throughout, $d=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[d,\iota(v_1)]$, so that $w=u^2$.
Write $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ for the \emph{MF radical}
of a countable group $G$: the intersection of the kernels of all its
corona representations (Definition~\ref{def:radical}).  Thus $G$ is MF
exactly when $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=1$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:A} says
$w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ with $w\ne1$.

\subsection*{The quotient by the sign}

\begin{mainthm}[the sign-free quotient of $E$]\label{thm:signfree}
The quotient $E/\langle w\rangle$ is not MF\@.  The image of
$[\iota(v_1),d]$, the inverse of the marked commutator $u$, is
nontrivial there and lies in
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E/\langle w\rangle)$; consequently
$\{1,w\}$ is a proper subgroup of
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSignFreeQuotient}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSignFreeQuotient.signFree_collapse}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSignFreeQuotient}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSignFreeQuotient.signFreeQuotient_not_isCDEOperatorMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSignFreeQuotient}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSignFreeQuotient.commutator_not_zpow_mark}
\end{mainthm}

The proof is in Section~\ref{subsec:collapse}.  It uses a second
consequence of one-sided compression, proved there: if an involution
is centralized by the compressed copy of a Kazhdan subgroup, and its
conjugates under that subgroup commute with one another, then every
corona representation identifies the entire conjugation orbit
(Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}).  In $E$ those conjugates of $d$ commute
only up to $w$, so in $E/\langle w\rangle$ the element $d$ satisfies
both hypotheses, and every corona representation of $E/\langle
w\rangle$ maps $u$ to the identity.  Thus $E/\langle w\rangle$ is a
second finitely presented non-MF group, and Theorem~\ref{thm:A} does
not determine $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection*{A stably finite non-MF group algebra}
Every MF algebra is stably finite \cite{BK}; the converse fails.  Here
stable finiteness comes from the faithful canonical trace
(Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace}), independently of the MF question.
Abstract examples follow from the failure of the Connes embedding
problem \cite{FGH}; this one is explicit.  For
$\Cred(\SL_3(\mathbb Z))$ and $\Cred(\SL_4(\mathbb Z))$ the MF
property is undecided; only the stronger PMF property is known to
fail, for $\SL_4(\mathbb Z)$ \cite{MdlS}.

\begin{mainthm}[the reduced group algebra of $E$]\label{thm:D}
$\Cred(E)$ is a unital separable $C^*$-algebra which has a
faithful tracial state --- and is therefore stably finite --- but is
not an MF algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremD}
\end{mainthm}

\begin{proof}
$\Cred(E)$ is unital and separable because $E$ is countable, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace} the canonical trace is faithful and the
algebra is therefore stably finite.  It is not an MF algebra by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

The algebra is not nuclear: the canonical map
$\mathcal B\to E$ is injective (Section~\ref{sec:witness}), so $E$
contains an infinite Kazhdan group and is nonamenable, whence
$\Cred(E)$ is not nuclear \cite{Lance}.  The nuclear form of the
Blackadar--Kirchberg problem --- whether every stably finite separable
nuclear $C^*$-algebra is quasidiagonal --- therefore remains open.
\leanverified{Analysis/NuclearityAmenability}{GroupApproximation.NuclearityAmenability.not_isAmenable_of_base_embeds}

\subsection*{A sofic example}
Let $X=G/\Gamma$ be the coset space of the mapping telescope
$G=\Gamma_\infty\rtimes\Z$ used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford}, at the affine data
\eqref{eq:affine-data}, and let
\[
 W=\ClLamp(X)\rtimes G
\]
be the Clifford lamp group over it.  This is the target of the
homomorphism used in that proof to show $w\ne1$;
Section~\ref{sec:witness} realizes it over $\mathbb Q$, writing $V$
for $G$ in the explicit rational model.

\begin{mainthm}[a sofic non-MF group]\label{thm:E}
The Clifford witness $W=\ClLamp(X)\rtimes V$ of
Section~\ref{sec:witness} is a finitely generated sofic group that
is not MF; since sofic groups are hyperlinear, $W$ is likewise a
hyperlinear group that is not MF\@.  In particular, neither soficity nor
hyperlinearity implies the MF property.
\leanverified{Endpoint/NonMFImpact}{GroupApproximation.NonMFImpact.witness_sofic_hyperlinear_nonMF}
\end{mainthm}

The proof is in Section~\ref{sec:consequences}.  Soficity comes from
the finite-index image of the doubling map: every telescope level then
has finite orbits on $X$, so every finite subset of
$\ClLamp(X)\rtimes\Gamma_\infty$ lies in a finite-by-sofic subgroup,
and the remaining $\Z$-extension is sofic by Elek--Szab\'o
\cite{ElekSzabo}.  Failure of MF follows from functoriality of the
radical, since the surjection $E\to W$ sends $w$ to the nontrivial
Clifford sign (Lemma~\ref{lem:portable}).  The same surjection shows that MF groups
are not closed under quotients, and the extension
$1\to\ClLamp(X)\to W\to V\to1$, whose kernel is locally finite and
whose quotient is residually finite, shows that they are not closed
under extensions either (Section~\ref{sec:consequences}).

The soficity of $E$ itself remains open: $W$ is a proper quotient of
$E$, with kernel a free group of infinite rank.
Section~\ref{sec:limits} explains why the operator-norm method decides
neither soficity nor hyperlinearity for either group.

\section{The MF equivalences}\label{app:equivalences}

An \emph{operator-norm local model} for a countable group $G$, a finite
set $F\subseteq G$, a separation constant $\delta>0$, and a tolerance
$\varepsilon>0$ is a map $V\colon G\to\U(r)$, for some positive
dimension $r$, such that
\begin{gather*}
 \opnorm{V(g)V(h)-V(gh)}\le\varepsilon\quad(g,h\in F),\\
 \opnorm{V(g)-V(h)}\ge\delta\quad(g\ne h\in F).
\end{gather*}
The local formulation in Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences} asks for
such a model with $\delta=1$ for every finite $F$ and every
$\varepsilon>0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} identifies the unitary-sequence quotient with
the unitary group of the $C^*$-quotient.  Replacing an arbitrary
finite coordinate set by a standard set of the same cardinality merely
conjugates that coordinate by a permutation unitary.  This proves the first
equivalence once the dimension convention is normalized.

\emph{Dimension normalization.}  Given positive dimensions $d_n$,
replace the $n$th coordinate model by
\[
 \widetilde V_n(g)=I_{X_0}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{X_{n-1}}\oplus V_n(g)
\]
on $X_0\oplus\cdots\oplus X_n$, of strictly increasing dimension
$D_n=d_0+\cdots+d_n$.  Products, multiplicative defects, and pairwise
operator-norm distances are unchanged, because block-diagonal operator
norms are maxima and every earlier block is the same identity for all
group elements.  The construction therefore preserves and reflects
the quotient relation and injectivity, and forgetting strict increase
proves the converse.  The universal-quotient construction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot} uses exactly this step to meet the
strictly-increasing dimension convention; nothing below depends on
that proposition.

Suppose first that
$\rho\colon G\to\Ucor((d_n))$ is injective, and choose unitary lifts
$u_n(g)$ of $\rho(g)$.  Fix a finite set $F\subseteq G$ and $\varepsilon>0$.
For each ordered pair $g\ne h$ in $F$, injectivity says that
$(u_n(g)^{-1}u_n(h))_n$ does not converge to the identity in operator norm.
Hence there are $\delta_{g,h}>0$ and arbitrarily large coordinates at which
\[
 \lVert u_n(g)-u_n(h)\rVert\ge\delta_{g,h}.
\]
Choose an integer $N_{g,h}$ with $8<N_{g,h}\,\delta_{g,h}^2$.  An explicit
tensor-power estimate then applies: whenever two unitaries are
$\delta_{g,h}$-separated, some tensor power of exponent $p\le N_{g,h}$
separates them by more than $1$ --- amplifying a spectral gap of the
relative unitary until it crosses the unit circle takes at most
$N_{g,h}$ steps once $8<N_{g,h}\delta_{g,h}^2$.
The quotient homomorphism law makes all multiplicative defects for pairs in
$F$ arbitrarily small at every sufficiently large coordinate.  Choose, for
each $g\ne h$, a coordinate with the displayed separation and with every
required defect at most $\varepsilon/N_{g,h}$.  Then the $p$th tensor
power separates the designated pair by more than $1$, while the
tensor-product telescoping identity bounds each new defect by
$p\,\varepsilon/N_{g,h}\le\varepsilon$.  Finally take the block
sum of these finitely many pair-specific models.  Operator norms of block
diagonal matrices are maxima, so every pair in $F$ is separated by at least
$1$ and every required product has defect at most $\varepsilon$.  This gives
the normalized local model.

Conversely, local models with separation constant $1$ for all finite
sets and tolerances make the group \emph{weakly} MF\@.  Choose models
along an exhaustion with errors tending to zero.  Then each fixed pair
$g,h$ is eventually represented multiplicatively, and each fixed pair
$g\ne h$ is eventually separated by $1$.  Hence the classes define an
injective homomorphism into the unitary-sequence quotient, with no
condition on the dimension sequence.  Weak MF in turn makes the norm-MF radical of
Definition~\ref{def:radical} trivial, and the block-sum diagonalization
in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}(2) then assembles the
separating sequences into a single faithful corona representation.  This
weak-to-corona implication is thus obtained from the
universal-quotient construction --- whose dimension bookkeeping is the
normalization step above --- not from a separate exhaustion.

\end{proof}
\section{The explicit presentation and its property-(T) base}\label{sec:group}

We work throughout with the groups defined by the relators below.  The
base is the affine group $\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$, a
familiar Kazhdan group (Remark~\ref{rem:classical-base}), and
property~\textup{(T)} for it is classical
\cite[Example~1.7.4(i)]{BHV}.  The proof given here works from the
displayed presentations, in three steps: an exact rational spectral
certificate for an auxiliary Steinberg-type presentation, an explicit
surjection onto the linear presentation, and a fixed-point bridge from
the linear subgroup to the full base.
First define the \emph{linear presentation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sl3presentation}
 \mathcal R=\left\langle x,y,z\ \middle|\
 \begin{gathered}
 x^3=y^3=z^2=(xz)^3=(yz)^3=1,\\
 (x^{-1}zxy)^2=(y^{-1}zyx)^2=(xy)^6=1
 \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
and the \emph{base}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-presentation}
\mathcal B=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\ \middle|\
\begin{gathered}
\text{the eight relations in \eqref{eq:sl3presentation}},\\
[v_1,v_2]=[v_1,v_3]=[v_2,v_3]=1,\\
xv_1x^{-1}=v_3,\ xv_2x^{-1}=v_1,\
xv_3x^{-1}=v_2,\\
yv_1y^{-1}=v_1,\ yv_2y^{-1}=v_2^{-1}v_3,\
yv_3y^{-1}=v_1v_2^{-1},\\
zv_1z^{-1}=v_2v_3^{-1},\ zv_2z^{-1}=v_1v_3^{-1},\
zv_3z^{-1}=v_3^{-1}
\end{gathered}\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Write $\iota\colon\mathcal B\to E$ for the homomorphism induced by the first
six generators of the presentation below.  This map is not assumed to be
injective.

\begin{definition}\label{def:E}
Use the six base generators and the relations displayed in
\eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
  E=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\ \middle|\
  \begin{gathered}
   \text{all relations in \eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}},\\
   tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^2\ (1\le i\le3),\\
   txt^{-1}=x,\quad tyt^{-1}=y,\quad tzt^{-1}=z,\\
   c^2=1,\\
   [c,g]=1\quad(g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
   [w,g]=1\quad(g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\})
  \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $w$ abbreviates the word
$\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\,v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}\bigr]$.
This is an explicit finite presentation with eight generators and
forty-one relators: the twenty base relators, six stable-letter
relators, $c^2$, the six relations making $c$ commute with the base
generators, and eight centrality relators.  Its defining
relations make $w$ central, and centrality together with $c^2=1$ implies
$w^2=1$ by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}.  The relations on the six base
generators also give
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFPresentation}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFPresentation.manuscriptLiteralPresentation}
\[
 t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\mathcal B),
 \qquad [c,\iota(\mathcal B)]=1.
\]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Six generators suffice.}
The eight-generator presentation is used because it displays the
structure used in the proof: the three commuting translations, the
linear generators acting on them, the stable letter $t$, and $c$.
The group $E$ is nevertheless generated by the six elements
$v_1,x,y,z,t,c$.
There is also a Tietze-equivalent presentation on these six generators

\subsection{Property~\textup{(T)} of the base}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:literal-base-T}
The twenty-relator group $\mathcal B$ has property~\textup{(T)}, in both
the real-orthogonal and complex-unitary formulations.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralBaseP13PropertyTBridge}{GroupApproximation.LiteralBaseP13PropertyTBridge.manuscriptBaseHasKazhdanPropertyT}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: a certified Steinberg-type presentation.}  Let $P_{13}$ be
the group on the six generators
$e_{12},e_{13},e_{21},e_{23},e_{31},e_{32}$ with the thirteen relators
\[
\begin{gathered}
 [e_{12},e_{23}]=e_{13},\qquad [e_{13},e_{32}]=e_{12},\qquad
 [e_{21},e_{13}]=e_{23},\\
 [e_{23},e_{31}]=e_{21},\qquad [e_{31},e_{12}]=e_{32},\qquad
 [e_{32},e_{21}]=e_{31},\\
 [e_{12},e_{13}]=[e_{12},e_{32}]=[e_{13},e_{23}]
 =[e_{21},e_{23}]=[e_{21},e_{31}]=[e_{31},e_{32}]=1,\\
 (e_{12}\,e_{21}^{-1}\,e_{12})^{4}=1,
\end{gathered}
\]
the elementary-matrix relations in rank three.  An exact sum-of-squares
certificate with integer matrix data, verified by denominator-free
rational arithmetic, proves the degree-one Hodge estimate for $P_{13}$
with gap $\tfrac1{500}$.  Testing it on coboundaries gives, in
every orthogonal representation, the quadratic gap
\[
 \lVert\Delta\xi\rVert^2\ \ge\ \tfrac1{500}\,\langle\Delta\xi,\xi\rangle
\]
for the six-generator Laplacian $\Delta$.  The gap places the spectrum
of $\Delta$ in $\{0\}\cup[\tfrac1{500},\infty)$.  The kernel of
$\Delta$ is exactly the space of invariant vectors.  The localization
makes $0$ an isolated point of the spectrum but does not by itself
exclude it, so one more step is needed: in a representation with no
nonzero invariant vector the kernel is trivial, and $\Delta$ is then
bounded below.  Hence a unit
vector almost fixed by the six generators has small Laplacian energy
and is close to an invariant vector.  This yields a Kazhdan pair: $P_{13}$ has
property~\textup{(T)}.  Certificates of this kind go back to Ozawa's
algebraic characterization of property~\textup{(T)} \cite{Ozawa} and its
computer-assisted implementations for $\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$
\cite{NetzerThom}.

\emph{Step 2: the explicit surjection onto the linear presentation.}
Map the six generators to the words
\[
\begin{gathered}
 e_{12}\mapsto y^{-1}xzy^{-1}x^{-1},\quad
 e_{13}\mapsto xzy^{-1}x^{-1}y,\quad
 e_{21}\mapsto y^{-1}xyxz,\\
 e_{23}\mapsto x^{-1}y^{-1}xzy^{-1},\quad
 e_{31}\mapsto xy^{-1}xzy^{-1}x,\quad
 e_{32}\mapsto x^{-1}zy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-1}
\end{gathered}
\]
in the linear presentation $\mathcal R$ of \eqref{eq:sl3presentation}.
A word-by-word verification inside $\mathcal R$, using only its eight
displayed relators, checks that all thirteen relators of $P_{13}$ map to
the identity, so the assignment defines a homomorphism
$P_{13}\to\mathcal R$.  The same computation exhibits explicit words in
the six images whose values are $z$ and $xy$.  A further computation
shows that $z$ and $xy$ generate $\mathcal R$.  Hence the homomorphism
is surjective.
Property~\textup{(T)} passes to quotients, so $\mathcal R$ has
property~\textup{(T)}.

\emph{Step 3: the fixed-point bridge to the base.}  The generators
$x,y,z$ of $\mathcal B$ satisfy the relators of $\mathcal R$, so
$\mathcal R$ maps to the linear subgroup of $\mathcal B$ they generate,
and Step~2
gives a finite set $S$ of words in $x,y,z$ and a $\kappa>0$ forming a
Kazhdan pair for it.  Let $\rho$ be an orthogonal representation of
$\mathcal B$ with a unit vector $\xi$ displaced by less than $\kappa/64$ by
every element of the finite control set consisting of $S$ together
with the translations $v_2,v_3$.  The third basis translation needs no
separate control: $v_1=xv_2x^{-1}$ by the displayed $x$-action, and
conjugation by the fixed unitary of $x$ preserves displacements of
vectors fixed by the linear subgroup.  Three estimates produce an
invariant vector near $\xi$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Projecting $\xi$ to the subspace of vectors fixed by the linear
subgroup gives such a fixed vector $p$ with $\lVert p-\xi\rVert<1/64$, by the
moving-projection estimate for the Kazhdan pair.
\item Translations move $p$ by a bound independent of the lattice
vector.  Three explicit words in $x,y,z$ act on the translation
lattice as elementary transvections.  Consequently every lattice
element is a product of two linear-subgroup conjugates of basis
translations --- a two-conjugate normal form proved inside the
presentation.  A conjugate $rvr^{-1}$ with $r$ in the linear subgroup
displaces the fixed vector $p$ exactly as much as $v$ does.  Each
control translation displaces $p$ by less than $3/64$, because its
displacement bound at $\xi$ transfers across $\lVert p-\xi\rVert$.
Hence every translation displaces $p$ by at most $1/8$.
\item The orbit of $p$ under the translation subgroup is bounded, so
the Hilbert-space circumcenter argument gives a translation-fixed
vector $q$ with $\lVert q-p\rVert\le1/8$; the orthogonal projection
$\eta$ of $p$ to the translation-fixed subspace is the nearest fixed
vector, so $\lVert\eta-p\rVert\le\lVert q-p\rVert\le1/8$.
The linear subgroup normalizes the translation subgroup and fixes $p$,
so $\eta$ is fixed by it as well.  Translations and the linear
generators generate $\mathcal B$, so $\eta$ is $\mathcal B$-invariant.
Finally, $\lVert \eta-\xi\rVert\le 1/8+1/64<1$ keeps $\eta$ nonzero.
\end{enumerate}
Thus almost-invariant vectors produce invariant vectors and $\mathcal B$
has property~\textup{(T)} in the real-orthogonal formulation;
realification and complexification identify it with the complex-unitary
formulation.
\end{proof}

The presentation $P_{13}$ used in Step~1 is a presentation of
$\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$.

\begin{theorem}[completeness of the thirteen-relator presentation]
\label{thm:p13-complete}
The canonical homomorphism $P_{13}\to\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$, sending each
generator $e_{ij}$ to the corresponding elementary matrix, is an
isomorphism.
\leanverified{Monsters/P13DescentMaster}{GroupApproximation.P13DescentMaster.p13CompletenessEquiv}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\pi\colon P_{13}\to\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$ for the map in question, and
\[
 w_{12}=e_{12}e_{21}^{-1}e_{12},\qquad
 w_{13}=e_{13}e_{31}^{-1}e_{13},\qquad
 w_{23}=e_{23}e_{32}^{-1}e_{23}
\]
for the three signed swaps; $w_{12}$ is the Weyl element of the
upper-left $2\times2$ block, and the thirteenth relator is
$w_{12}^4=1$.  The letter $w$ is reserved for the marked word of
\eqref{eq:E} and is not used in this proof.  Let $e_3$ be
the third basis column of $\mathbb Z^3$, let
$\lVert\cdot\rVert_1$ be the $\ell^1$ norm on $\mathbb Z^3$, and put
\[
 H_{\mathrm{par}}=\langle e_{12},e_{21},e_{31},e_{32}\rangle\le P_{13},
\]
the subgroup on the four root letters whose matrices fix $e_3$.

\emph{Surjectivity.}  This is Euclidean elimination.  Division with
remainder in the first column of $A\in\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$ is a product of
integer powers of elementary matrices, and iterating it on columns and
then on rows reduces $A$ to a diagonal sign matrix.  Each such sign
matrix is the square of a signed swap, and each signed swap is the
displayed three-transvection word.

\emph{Injectivity: a normal form.}  Write an element of $P_{13}$ as a
word $W=\ell_k\cdots\ell_1$ in the letters $e_i^{\pm1}$.  Given a
column $b\in\mathbb Z^3$, the \emph{trajectory} of $W$ over $b$ is the
sequence $b,\ \pi(\ell_1)b,\ \pi(\ell_2\ell_1)b,\ \dots,\ \pi(W)b$, and
$W$ is \emph{norm-monotone} over $b$ when the $\ell^1$ norms along that
sequence do not decrease.  Using only the thirteen relators, every $W$
can be rewritten as
\[
 W=G\,m,\qquad m\in\langle w_{13},w_{23}\rangle,\qquad
 G\ \text{norm-monotone over}\ b:=\pi(m)e_3 .
\]
The rewriting is a descent, in the lexicographic order on
$\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$, of the pair consisting of the largest
height at which monotonicity fails and the position of the topmost such
failure.  One step splits $W$ at that position and rewrites the
offending pair of adjacent letters; the thirty-six ordered pairs of
root families fall into four classes --- cancelling, commuting,
Steinberg, and braid --- each closed by one of the displayed relators,
and each strictly decreasing the pair.

\emph{Injectivity: a kernel word has constant trajectory.}
Suppose $\pi(W)=1$.
The subgroup $\langle w_{13},w_{23}\rangle$ maps into the signed
permutation matrices, so $b=\pi(m)e_3$ is a signed basis column and
$\lVert b\rVert_1=1$.  The trajectory of $G$ over $b$ starts at $b$ and
ends at $\pi(G)b=\pi(m)^{-1}b=e_3$, again of norm one, and it is
nondecreasing; hence it is constant.  A root letter that preserves the
$\ell^1$ norm of a signed basis column fixes that column, so every
letter of $G$ fixes $b$ --- and, taking the whole word,
$\pi(m)e_3=\pi(G)b=e_3$, so $\pi(m)$ fixes $e_3$.

\emph{Injectivity: into the parabolic.}  Conjugation by $w_{13}$ and by
$w_{23}$ permutes the twelve letters $e_i^{\pm1}$, by two explicit
tables derived from the relators.  Applying them letter by letter turns
$m^{-1}Gm$ into a word in root letters, each of which fixes
$\pi(m)^{-1}b=e_3$.  The root letters whose matrices fix $e_3$ are
exactly $e_{12}^{\pm1},e_{21}^{\pm1},e_{31}^{\pm1},e_{32}^{\pm1}$, so
$m^{-1}Gm\in H_{\mathrm{par}}$.

\emph{Injectivity: the subgroup $\langle w_{13},w_{23}\rangle$.}  The group
$\langle w_{13},w_{23}\rangle$ acts on the six signed basis columns,
and the orbit of $e_3$ is all six of them, with coset representatives
\[
 1,\quad w_{13},\quad w_{23},\quad w_{13}^2,\quad w_{13}^3,\quad
 w_{23}w_{13}^2
\]
carrying $e_3$ to $e_3,e_1,e_2,-e_3,-e_1,-e_2$ respectively.  Twelve
Schreier relations, one for each generator and coset, have the form
$w_\bullet\,r_j=r_{j'}\,w^{k}$: every Schreier element is a power of
the block Weyl element $w_{12}$.  The identity that closes the table is
$w_{23}^2=w_{12}^2w_{13}^2$, the residue of the classical computation
$\{-1,-1\}=w_{12}^4$ in $K_2(3,\mathbb Z)$, and it is available exactly
because the thirteenth relator gives $w_{12}^4=1$.  Hence every element
of $\langle w_{13},w_{23}\rangle$ is $r_jw_{12}^k$; if in addition its
matrix fixes $e_3$, then $r_je_3=e_3$ forces $j=0$, leaving
$m=w_{12}^k\in
H_{\mathrm{par}}$.  With the previous step,
\[
 W=m\cdot\bigl(m^{-1}Gm\bigr)\in H_{\mathrm{par}} .
\]

\emph{Injectivity: separation inside the parabolic.}  Let
$N=\langle e_{31},e_{32}\rangle$ and $\Lambda=\langle
e_{12},e_{21}\rangle$.  The Steinberg relations make $N$ abelian and
normalized by $\Lambda$, and every element of $H_{\mathrm{par}}$ is
$e_{31}^{a}e_{32}^{b}\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Lambda$.  On $\Lambda$ the
displayed relators reduce to the braid presentation
$\langle p,q\mid pqp=qpq,\ (pqp)^4=1\rangle$, which maps isomorphically
onto $\SL_2(\mathbb Z)$: it surjects onto
$\mathbb Z/2\ast\mathbb Z/3$, that quotient embeds by the M\"obius
ping-pong argument, and the residual central element is trivial by
$(pqp)^4=1$.  So $\pi$ is injective on $\Lambda$.  Now let
$W=e_{31}^{a}e_{32}^{b}\gamma\in H_{\mathrm{par}}$ have $\pi(W)=1$.
The upper-left $2\times2$ block of $\pi(W)$ is the matrix of $\gamma$,
so $\gamma=1$; the bottom row of $\pi(W)$ is then $(a,b,1)$, so
$a=b=0$.  Hence $W=1$, and $\ker\pi$ is trivial.
\end{proof}

The separation step is applied only after the factor $m$ has itself
been placed in $H_{\mathrm{par}}$ and the two factors recombined:
applied directly to $m^{-1}Gm$ it would meet $\pi(m^{-1}Gm)=\pi(m)^{-1}$
rather than $1$.

\begin{remark}[the classical identification]\label{rem:classical-base}
The eight relations \eqref{eq:sl3presentation} present
$\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$: Theorem~\ref{thm:p13-complete} composed with the
surjection of Step 2 identifies $\mathcal R$ with $\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$,
and the base $\mathcal B$ is then the affine group
$\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z)$ --- the surjection
$\mathcal B\to\bar\Gamma$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} onto the matrix
realization is an isomorphism.
\leanverified{Monsters/LiteralBaseCompleteness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralBaseCompleteness.baseAffineEquiv}
The presentations go back to \cite[Theorem~2]{CRW}, with the
matrix-labelled generators also recorded in \cite{CLV}, and
property~\textup{(T)} of the affine group is classical
\cite[Example~1.7.4(i)]{BHV}.
\end{remark}
\section{The witness: \texorpdfstring{$w\neq 1$}{w is nontrivial}}\label{sec:witness}

Clifford signs, shifts, and HNN doubling have appeared together before in
approximate-representation theory, notably in Slofstra's hyperlinear-profile
construction \cite[Section~2]{SlofstraProfile}.  Below we define a
homomorphism from $E$ to a Clifford lamp group under which
$w\mapsto-1$.

\begin{construction}[Clifford lamp groups]\label{con:clifford}
For a set $X$, use the real Clifford-algebra convention generated by
$(c_x)_{x\in X}$ with $c_x^2=1$ and $c_xc_y=-c_yc_x$ for $x\ne y$, and let
$\ClLamp(X)$ be the subgroup of units generated by $-1$ and the $c_x$.
Write $\zeta=-1$.  In this concrete group,
\[
 \zeta^2=c_x^2=1,\qquad c_xc_y=\zeta c_yc_x\quad(x\ne y).
\]
The sign $\zeta$ is nontrivial.  Thus $\ClLamp(X)$ is a central
extension, by the sign $\zeta$, of the elementary abelian $2$-group on
the sites $X$: distinct site involutions commute only up to $\zeta$,
unlike the commuting lamps of a classical lamplighter.  Every
permutation of $X$ acts on this
group by $c_x\mapsto c_{\sigma(x)}$ and fixes $\zeta$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptCliffordConstruction}
\end{construction}

\begin{lemma}[linear model]\label{lem:linear}
For $M\in\GL_3(\mathbb Q)$ and $u\in\mathbb Q^3$, write
\[
 A(M,u)=\begin{pmatrix}M&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Assign $v_i\mapsto A(I_3,e_i)$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},0\right),
&y&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&-1&-1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},0\right),\\[3pt]
 z&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\-1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix},0\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let $\bar\Gamma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ be the subgroup generated by these six
matrices, and put
$D=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)$.  Direct matrix computation proves that
the six matrices satisfy all twenty relators of $\mathcal B$ and that
\[
 \bar\alpha(u)=DuD^{-1}
\]
is an injective endomorphism of $\bar\Gamma$ which squares the three
translation generators and fixes $x,y,z$.  Moreover the matrix assigned to
$v_1$ does not lie in $\operatorname{range}(\bar\alpha)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptLinearModel}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The twenty relator checks reduce to sixteen rational identities for each
matrix equation.  Conjugation by $D$ gives the six stable-letter equations
and is injective because it is conjugation by an invertible matrix.  Every
displayed generator and its explicitly computed inverse has integral entries;
equivalently, all six generators lie in $\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$.  Hence
$\bar\Gamma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$.  However $D^{-1}\bar v_1D$ has entry $1/2$
in position $(1,4)$, so it is not in $\bar\Gamma$.  This proves the last
assertion.
\end{proof}

Form the direct limit $T=\varinjlim(\bar\Gamma,\bar\alpha)$, the
algebraic mapping telescope of $\bar\alpha$, extend it by its shift
$\tau$, and call the resulting group $V=T\rtimes\mathbb Z$.  Let
$j\colon\bar\Gamma\hookrightarrow V$ be the level-zero map.  Put
$X=V/j(\bar\Gamma)$, with root $o=j(\bar\Gamma)$.  The final assertion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} states that $\tau o\ne j(\bar v_1)\tau o$;
the Clifford generators at these two sites therefore anticommute.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:witness}
$w\neq1$ in $E$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFLinearWitness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFLinearWitness.literal_mark_ne_one}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Form $W:=\ClLamp(X)\rtimes V$ as in Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.
Define a map on the generators of \eqref{eq:E} by
\[
\begin{gathered}
  g\mapsto j(\bar g)\in V\quad
  (g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
  t\mapsto\tau,\qquad c\mapsto c_o .
\end{gathered}
\]
The exact checks in Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} give the twenty base and six
stable-letter relations.  The level-zero subgroup fixes $o$, so $c_o$
commutes with every base generator, and $c_o^2=1$.
Under this assignment,
\[
  tct^{-1}\mapsto c_{\tau o},\qquad
  v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}\mapsto c_{j(\bar v_1)\tau o},
\]
and the two sites are distinct by Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}, so
\[
  w\mapsto[c_{\tau o},c_{j(\bar v_1)\tau o}]=-1=\zeta .
\]
Since $\zeta$ is central in $W$, the remaining relations $[w,g]=1$ for
$g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\}$ are also satisfied.  Hence the
assignment extends to a group homomorphism $E\to W$ sending $w$ to
$\zeta\neq1$, and therefore $w\neq1$ in $E$.
\end{proof}

When $X$ is infinite so is $\ClLamp(X)$, but each of its elements
involves only finitely many Clifford sites, and the remaining data of
the target are rational matrices.  The two sites are separated by the
range characterization of Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}: $D^{-1}\bar v_1D$ has
the entry $1/2$ in position $(1,4)$ and therefore does not lie in
$\bar\Gamma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$.
\section{The defect square}\label{sec:proofA}

\begin{lemma}[the marked word is a defect square]\label{lem:square}
Let $H_1$ be a group and $d,a_1\in H_1$ with $d^2=1$.  Then
\leanverified{Sofic/CompressionDefectSquare}{GroupApproximation.commutator_conjugate_eq_commutator_sq_of_sq_eq_one}
\[
  \bigl[\,d,\;a_1\,d\,a_1^{-1}\bigr]\;=\;[\,d,a_1\,]^{\,2} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $b=a_1da_1^{-1}$, so $b^2=a_1d^2a_1^{-1}=1$; thus $b^{-1}=b$ and
$d^{-1}=d$.  Then $[d,a_1]=da_1d^{-1}a_1^{-1}=d\,(a_1da_1^{-1})=db$,
and
\[
  [d,a_1]^2=(db)^2=db\,db=d\,b\,d^{-1}b^{-1}=[d,b] . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The marked radical element.}
Since every corona representation of $E$ maps $w$ to the identity, each
one factors through $E/\langle w\rangle$, where
$\langle w\rangle=\{1,w\}$ is central and hence normal; that is, $w$ is
a nontrivial element of the MF radical of $E$.  The terminology follows
Slofstra's quotients $G^{\mathrm{fin}}$ and $G^{\mathrm{fa}}$, obtained
by quotienting out the elements invisible to exact and to approximate
finite-dimensional representations
\cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} extends the conclusion to every countable
group with a Kazhdan conjugation datum: every finite normal subgroup of
the compression defect lies in the MF radical.  Its proof gives
$\hsnorm{U_{u,n}-1}\to0$ for the commutator
$u=[d,\iota(v_1)]$ in every operator-norm almost representation of $E$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:signfree} places $u$ itself in the radical:
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ properly contains $\{1,w\}$.

\paragraph{Corner ranks.}
The coordinate averaging corner in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} discards the zero-rank coordinates and
equips each retained corner block $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n$ with its own
normalized trace, so every Hilbert--Schmidt estimate there is relative
to $r_n=\operatorname{rank}q_n$ and independent of the ratio $r_n/d_n$.
A small relative rank therefore costs nothing in the operator-norm
setting, while it blocks the same argument in Hilbert--Schmidt metrics
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
\section{The finite-stage transport proof}\label{app:finite-stage}

This appendix proves Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} a second
time, quantitatively at each single coordinate $n$: every estimate
below is an explicit inequality in $\kappa$, $|S|$, the uniform bound
on $(x_n)$, and the multiplicative defects of $U_n$, once the robust
spectral-gap input of~(i) is granted at each stage.  That input is the
one place where a limit still enters, as
Remark~\ref{rem:finite-stage} says: property~\textup{(T)} constrains
exact unitary representations, while at a finite stage the matrix
models only generate a representation of the free group on $S$, so no
threshold in $\kappa$, $|S|$ and the defects on a fixed finite set can
supply it.  Making that step effective as well would need a
sum-of-squares certificate for property~\textup{(T)} in the sense of
\cite{Ozawa}.  Corollary~\ref{cor:generaltransport} and
Section~\ref{sec:limits} refer to this proof.

\begin{proof}[Finite-stage proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}]
Property~\textup{(T)} provides a finite symmetric generating set
$S\subseteq\Gamma$ with $1\in S$ and a Kazhdan constant
$\kappa\in(0,1]$ for the pair $(S,\kappa)$.  The adjoint model is that
of the printed proof: each group element acts by conjugation,
$\Ad U_n(g)\,\xi=U_n(g)\,\xi\,U_n(g)^*$, on
$L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$, and operator-norm almost multiplicativity
of $U_n$ makes $g\mapsto\Ad U_n(g)$ an operator-norm asymptotic
representation on these Hilbert spaces.

\emph{The almost-fixed spectral subspace.}  Let $H_n$ be the Hermitian part of the
average $\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s'\in S}\Ad U_n(\iota(s'))$, fix the threshold
\[
 \theta=\tfrac12\Bigl(\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\kappa^2}{4|S|}\Bigr)+1\Bigr)<1,
\]
and let $P_n$ be the spectral projection of $H_n$ for the part of the
spectrum above $\theta$.  Two spectral estimates tie $P_n$ to the group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Displacement.}  For every $\gamma\in\Gamma$,
$\opnorm{(\Ad U_n(\iota(\gamma))-1)\,P_n}\to0$.  The mechanism is a
robust spectral gap: for every pair $a,b$ with
$1-\kappa^2/(4|S|)<a$ and $b<1$, once the multiplicative defects of
$U_n$ on the finitely many group elements involved are small enough,
no eigenvalue of $H_n$ lies in $[a,b]$
--- an eigenvector with intermediate eigenvalue would be a unit vector
that the Kazhdan pair forbids: too fixed to be moved by
$\kappa$, too moved to be fixed.
\leanverified{Sofic/WeakMFVectorGNS}{GroupApproximation.WeakMFVectorGNS.hermitianAverage_eventually_no_intermediate_eigenvalues}
Hence the spectrum of $H_n$ above $\theta$ is eventually within any
prescribed $\delta$ of $1$, so a unit vector $\eta\in\operatorname{ran}P_n$
has $\langle(1-H_n)\eta,\eta\rangle\le\delta$; since $1-H_n$ is the
average of the positive operators $1-\operatorname{Re}\Ad U_n(\iota(s'))$,
each generator satisfies
$\tfrac1{2}\hsnorm{(\Ad U_n(\iota(s'))-1)\eta}^2\le|S|\,\delta$, and a word
in $S$ of length $\ell$ moves $\eta$ by at most $\ell\sqrt{2|S|\delta}$
plus the accumulated multiplicative defects.  The projection $P_n$ is
the finite-stage substitute for the projection onto the fixed subspace.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Spectral concentration.}  Write $\xi_n\in L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$
for the vector $x_n$.  The commutator hypothesis says each
$\Ad U_n(\iota(s'))$ almost fixes $\xi_n$, so the residual
$(1-H_n)\xi_n$ is Hilbert--Schmidt null.  On
$\operatorname{ran}(1-P_n)$ the operator $1-H_n$ is bounded below by
$1-\theta$, and it commutes with $P_n$, so every vector $\eta$
satisfies
\[
 \hsnorm{(1-P_n)\eta}\;\le\;(1-\theta)^{-1}\,\hsnorm{(1-H_n)\eta}.
\]
Applied to $\eta=\xi_n$, this shows $\xi_n$ is asymptotically
concentrated in $\operatorname{ran}P_n$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Reversing the one-sided projection estimate.}  Put
$T_n=\Ad U_n(s)$ and $Q_n=T_nP_nT_n^*$, a projection of the same rank as
$P_n$.  The conjugation relation enters exactly once: for
$\gamma\in\Gamma$ the element $s^{-1}$ pulls the almost-fixedness of a
vector of $\operatorname{ran}P_n$ under
$\iota(\Gamma)\supseteq s\iota(\Gamma)s^{-1}$ back to
almost-fixedness under each $\iota(s')$, $s'\in S$.  By the
concentration estimate, the pulled-back vector is again almost in the
subspace.  Thus
$\operatorname{ran}P_n$ is almost contained in its conjugate,
$\opnorm{(1-Q_n)P_n}\to0$ --- the finite-stage form of the one-sided
inclusion $P\le VPV^*$.  Since $\operatorname{rank}P_n=
\operatorname{rank}Q_n$, the equal-rank reversal estimate --- for projections $p,q$ of
equal finite rank, $\opnorm{(1-q)p}\le\varepsilon<1$ implies
$\opnorm{(1-p)q}\le\varepsilon/\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}$ --- reverses the
containment:
\[
 \opnorm{(1-P_n)\,Q_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is the middle implication of the display at the head of
Section~\ref{sec:transport}, and it is the finite-dimensional
substitute for finiteness of a norm ultraproduct: a one-sided
compression cannot enlarge the subspace.

\emph{Combining the estimates.}  Fix $\gamma$ and $\varepsilon>0$, let $M\ge1$ bound
$\opnorm{x_n}$, and put $q=\min(1,\varepsilon/(40(M^2+1)))$.  For all
large $n$ the three estimates hold at tolerance $q$:
\[
 \hsnorm{(1-P_n)\xi_n}^2\le q,\qquad
 \opnorm{(1-P_n)Q_n}\le q,\qquad
 \opnorm{(A_n-1)P_n}\le q,
\]
where $A_n=\Ad U_n(\iota(\gamma))$.  Decompose the transported vector
along $P_n$ and $Q_n$:
\[
 T_n\xi_n\;=\;P_nQ_n\,T_n\xi_n\;+\;(1-P_n)Q_n\,T_n\xi_n
 \;+\;(1-Q_n)\,T_n\xi_n.
\]
Since $Q_n=T_nP_nT_n^*$ and $T_n$ is conjugation by a unitary,
\[
 \hsnorm{(1-Q_n)T_n\xi_n}=\hsnorm{T_n(1-P_n)\xi_n}
 =\hsnorm{(1-P_n)\xi_n}\le q^{1/2};
\]
the middle term obeys
$\hsnorm{(1-P_n)Q_n\,T_n\xi_n}\le\opnorm{(1-P_n)Q_n}\hsnorm{\xi_n}
\le qM$, and the displacement estimate moves the first term by at most
$q\hsnorm{\xi_n}\le qM$.  Applying $A_n-1$, whose operator norm is at
most $2$, to the last two terms and using the displacement bound on
the first gives
\[
 \hsnorm{(A_n-1)\,T_n\xi_n}^2
 \;\le\;\bigl(qM+2qM+2q^{1/2}\bigr)^2
 \;\le\;18q^2M^2+16q\;\le\;\varepsilon.
\]
Since $T_n\xi_n$ is the vector of $U_n(s)x_nU_n(s)^*$, this proves the
asserted convergence --- with an explicit rate in terms of $\kappa$,
$|S|$, $M$, and the multiplicative defects of $U_n$ on the finitely many
group elements involved.
\end{proof}

\section{Transport at every scale}\label{supp:transport-variants}

The transport theorem extends to arbitrary weights $w_n$ in place of
the dimension; the normalization $w_n=d_n$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} is one special case.

\begin{theorem}[transport at every scale, and of intertwiners]
\label{thm:transport-variants}
Let $\Gamma$, $H$, $\iota$, and $s$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $(U_n)$ be as there, let $(w_n)$ be nonnegative weights,
and suppose there is $C$ with
$\operatorname{Tr}(x_n^*x_n)\le C\,w_n$ for all $n$, and that for
every $\gamma\in\Gamma$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\,\bigl|x_n-U_n(\iota(\gamma))\,x_n\,
   U_n(\iota(\gamma))^*\bigr|^2\le\varepsilon\,w_n.
\]
Then $U_n(s)\,x_nU_n(s)^*$ and $U_n(s)^*\,x_nU_n(s)$ satisfy both
conditions as well.
\item Let $U^1_n\colon H\to\U(d^1_n)$ and
$U^2_n\colon H\to\U(d^2_n)$ be operator-norm asymptotic
representations and let $(x_n)$ be uniformly operator-norm bounded
$d^1_n\times d^2_n$ matrices with
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Tr}\,\bigl|x_n-U^1_n(\iota(\gamma))\,x_n\,
   U^2_n(\iota(\gamma))^*\bigr|^2}{d^1_n+d^2_n}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]
Then $U^1_n(s)\,x_n\,U^2_n(s)^*$ satisfies the same convergence for
every $\gamma\in\Gamma$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ScaledKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.ScaledKazhdanTransport.scaled_transport_both}
\leanverified{Sofic/IntertwinerKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.IntertwinerKazhdanTransport.manuscriptIntertwinerTransport}
\leanverified{Sofic/IntertwinerKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.IntertwinerKazhdanTransport.scaled_intertwiner_transport}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1)  Give each coordinate space with $w_n>0$ the inner product
$\langle x,y\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(y^*x)/w_n$; an index with
$w_n=0$ forces $x_n=0$.  Conjugation by a unitary is unitary for every
weight, and both hypotheses and both conclusions concern only the
renormalized norms, so the ultraproduct proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} applies verbatim: the mass bound
defines the ultraproduct vector, the adjoint actions remain
operator-norm almost multiplicative, and finiteness reverses the
one-sided compression in both directions.

(2)  The block sum $W_n=U^1_n\oplus U^2_n$ is an operator-norm
asymptotic representation, since block-diagonal operator norms are
maxima.  The off-diagonal embedding of $x_n$ has operator norm
$\opnorm{x_n}$, its commutator with $W_n(\iota(\gamma))$ is the
off-diagonal embedding of the intertwining defect --- whose normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm on the block space has the denominator
$d^1_n+d^2_n$ --- and conjugation by $W_n(s)$ acts on the embedded
corner by $x_n\mapsto U^1_n(s)\,x_n\,U^2_n(s)^*$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} applied to $W_n$ transports the
embedded sequence, and the same reduction through (1) gives the
intertwiner transport at every weight.
\end{proof}

The dimension weight $w_n=d_n$ recovers
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} with the uniform operator-norm
bound relaxed to mass boundedness; the collapse proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} uses a rank weight, which normalizes a
defect at its own scale; and the adjoint sector of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} is the diagonal case of (2).

Invariant tensors of type $(p,q)$ may be identified with
Hilbert--Schmidt intertwiners from the $q$-fold tensor power to the
$p$-fold tensor power.  Tensor powers of unitaries are exactly
multiplicative and satisfy
$\opnorm{A^{\otimes p}-B^{\otimes p}}\le p\,\opnorm{A-B}$, so tensor
powers of an operator-norm asymptotic representation are again
operator-norm asymptotic representations, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport-variants}(2) applies to every pair of them:
one-sided compression of a Kazhdan image creates no asymptotic invariant
tensors of any fixed type, at any weight.
\leanverified{Sofic/TensorPowerTransport}{GroupApproximation.TensorPowerTransport.manuscriptFixedTensorTransport}
\section{The compression defect and its obstruction theorems}\label{sec:conj}

\begin{definition}[Kazhdan conjugation datum]\label{def:pattern}
Let $H$ be a countable group.  A \emph{Kazhdan conjugation datum} in $H$
is a tuple $(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, where $\Lambda$ is a countable
group with property (T), $\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ is a group
homomorphism, not assumed injective, and $t,c\in H$,
subject to
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(M1)] $t\,\iota(\lambda)\,t^{-1}\in\iota(\Lambda)$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$;
  \item[(M2)] $c\,\iota(\lambda)=\iota(\lambda)\,c$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
Write $d:=tct^{-1}$.  The \emph{compression defect} of the
datum is the normal closure
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMarkedKazhdanPattern}
\[
  N_{\mathrm{comp}}
  \;=\;\bigl\langle\!\bigl\langle\,
  [\,d,\iota(\lambda)\,]\;:\;\lambda\in\Lambda
  \,\bigr\rangle\!\bigr\rangle_H\;\trianglelefteq\;H .
\]
\end{definition}

Both conditions are relations among generators, so a datum can be read
off a presentation.  The relations force $c$ to commute with
$\iota(\Lambda)$ and force $d$ to commute with the compressed copy
$t\iota(\Lambda)t^{-1}$, but they do not constrain the commutators
$[d,\iota(\lambda)]$; the compression defect $N_{\mathrm{comp}}$ is
their normal closure.
The theorems of this section show that every finite normal subgroup of
$H$ contained in $N_{\mathrm{comp}}$, and more generally every normal
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup contained in it, lies in the kernel of
every corona representation of $H$.

\begin{definition}[Hilbert--Schmidt invisibility]\label{def:invisible}
An element $g$ of a countable group $H$ is \emph{Hilbert--Schmidt
invisible} if, for every operator-norm asymptotic representation of
$H$ and every ultrafilter refining the cofinite filter, the induced
homomorphism into the tracial ultraproduct of the matrix blocks maps
$g$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptHSInvisibleCharacterization}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[finite-normal obstruction criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $H$ be a countable group with a Kazhdan conjugation datum
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, and let $F\trianglelefteq H$ be a finite
normal subgroup with $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$.  Then every
corona representation $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies
$\Theta(f)=1$ for all $f\in F$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptFiniteNormalObstructionCriterion}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Tracial vanishing of the defect.}  Let $(U_{g,n})$ be
coordinate unitary lifts of any operator-norm almost representation of
$H$ (in particular, of a corona representation), and put
\[
  K_2=\{g\in H:\hsnorm{U_{g,n}-1}\longrightarrow0\}.
\]
Unitary invariance and asymptotic multiplicativity make
$K_2\trianglelefteq H$.  Since $c$ centralizes $\iota(\Lambda)$, its
lifts belong to the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of
$\iota(\Lambda)$.  Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} to
$x_n=U_{c,n}$ puts the lifts of $d=tct^{-1}$ there as well.  Therefore
$[d,\iota(\lambda)]\in K_2$ for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$; since
$K_2$ is normal, it contains the normal closure of these commutators:
$N_{\mathrm{comp}}\subseteq K_2$, and so $F\subseteq K_2$.
Convergence of the full sequence gives triviality along every
ultrafilter refining the cofinite filter, so every element of
$N_{\mathrm{comp}}$ is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible
(Definition~\ref{def:invisible}).

\emph{The coordinate averaging corner.}  Suppose a corona
representation $\Theta$ is nontrivial on $F$; choose coordinate unitary
lifts $(V_{g,n})$ of $\Theta$ and form the coordinate averages
\[
 p_n=\frac1{|F|}\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}.
\]
Because the lifts are asymptotically multiplicative and $F$ is finite,
$(p_n)$ is asymptotically self-adjoint and idempotent, and normality of
$F$ makes it asymptotically central for all of $\Theta(H)$.  The
Hermitian part of $p_n$ is asymptotically idempotent, so its spectrum
clusters at $\{0,1\}$ and rounding at $1/2$ by continuous functional
calculus yields genuine projections at operator-norm distance $o(1)$
from $p_n$; the rounded projections inherit the asymptotic centrality.
Let $q_n$ be their complements.  Nontriviality of
$\Theta$ on $F$ forces $q_n\ne0$ along infinitely many coordinates ---
otherwise the averages converge to $1$ and $\Theta|_F$ is trivial.
Retain exactly those coordinates and relabel them by $\N$.  Compress
every lift to the corner $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\cong M_{r_n}(\C)$ with
$r_n=\operatorname{rank}q_n>0$, and polar-correct the compressed
matrices: asymptotic centrality makes each compression
$q_nV_{g,n}q_n$ almost unitary in the corner, so its polar unitary is
at operator-norm distance $o(1)$ and almost-multiplicativity is preserved by
the correction.  The result is an operator-norm almost representation
$(W_{g,n})$ of $H$ on the nonzero corner blocks.  Since the corner is
the complement of the rounded average, the finite average vanishes
there:
\[
 \Bigl\lVert\sum_{f\in F}W_{f,n}\Bigr\rVert\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in operator norm.}
 \tag{\ensuremath{\ast}}
\]

\emph{The contradiction.}  The tracial vanishing applies to the corner
almost representation $(W_{g,n})$ itself: every $f\in F$ satisfies
$\hsnorm{W_{f,n}-1}\to0$.  Since $F$ is finite, the maximum of these
$|F|$ null sequences is itself null, so
\[
 \sum_{f\in F}W_{f,n}\longrightarrow |F|1
 \quad\text{in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm},
\]
each block with its own normalized trace.  This contradicts
\((\ast)\), because operator-norm convergence dominates normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt convergence.  Hence no corona representation is
nontrivial on $F$.
\end{proof}

The averaging-corner argument used only one property of $N_{\mathrm{comp}}$: every
element is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in every operator-norm almost
representation of $H$.  Any normal subgroup with that tracial-vanishing
property therefore obstructs its finite normal subgroups in exactly the
same way; Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-radical} exploits this.

The central-sign criterion of the main text
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}) is the case $F=\{1,\varepsilon\}$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}: there the average is
$p_n=\tfrac12(1+V_{\varepsilon,n})$, its complementary corner is the negative
spectral corner of the sign, and compression to that corner gives a model in
which $\varepsilon=-1$ exactly.  Nothing else in the two-element case is
special, and the general statement has no centrality hypothesis.

\subsection{The intrinsic defect}

The transport estimate applies to every one-sided conjugator of a
subgroup, not only to the element $t$.  For a subgroup
$L\le H$, let $G_{\rm comp}(L)$ be generated by the elements $s$ satisfying
$sLs^{-1}\subseteq L$ --- the \emph{one-sided conjugators} of $L$ --- and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsicdefect}
 \mathfrak D(H,L)=
 \left\langle\!\left\langle
 [gzg^{-1},\ell]:g\in G_{\rm comp}(L),\ z\in C_H(L),\ \ell\in L
 \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_H,
\end{equation}
the \emph{intrinsic defect} of $L$ in $H$.  It depends only on the
pair $(H,L)$, not on a choice of datum.

\begin{corollary}[transport along all one-sided conjugators]\label{cor:generaltransport}
Let $(r_n)$ and $V_{g,n}\in\U(r_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $H$, let $\Lambda$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ be a homomorphism, and put $L=\iota(\Lambda)$.
Suppose that $(x_n\in M_{r_n}(\C))$ is uniformly bounded in operator norm
and belongs to the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of $L$:
\[
 \hsnorm{x_n-V_{\iota(\lambda),n}x_nV_{\iota(\lambda),n}^*}
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
Then every $g\in G_{\rm comp}(L)$ acts in both directions on that
asymptotic commutant.  Explicitly, both
\[
 V_{g,n}x_nV_{g,n}^*
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 V_{g,n}^*x_nV_{g,n}
\]
satisfy the preceding convergence for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$.
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.compressionGroup_transport_both}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a one-sided conjugator $s$, Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} gives
forward transport.  Reverse transport follows from the same proof: the
equality $P=VPV^*$ obtained there gives $V^*PV=P$ as well, so the fixed
subspace is preserved by conjugation in both directions.  In the
finite-stage proof of Section~\ref{app:finite-stage}, the equal-rank
reversal estimate reverses the one-sided containment of the spectral
subspaces; the same argument then runs with the adjoint unitary, with
the displacement and concentration steps unchanged.  The elements acting in both
directions form a subgroup of $H$, and hence contain the subgroup generated
by all one-sided conjugators, namely $G_{\rm comp}(L)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the intrinsic defect and the MF radical]
\label{thm:compression-radical}
Let $H$ and $\Gamma$ be countable groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism, and put
$L=\iota(\Gamma)$.  If $F\trianglelefteq H$ is finite and
$F\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, then every homomorphism from $H$ to every norm
matrix corona maps every element of $F$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.manuscriptCompressionRadical}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $(U_{h,n})$ be coordinate lifts of an operator-norm almost
representation of $H$ and put
$K_2=\{h:\hsnorm{U_{h,n}-1}\to0\}$, a normal subgroup of $H$.
If $z\in C_H(L)$, then $(U_{z,n})$ lies in the asymptotic commutant of
$L$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:generaltransport} places
$(U_{gzg^{-1},n})$ there for every $g\in G_{\rm comp}(L)$.  Hence each
generator $[gzg^{-1},\ell]$, $\ell\in L$, displayed in
\eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect} belongs to $K_2$; since
$K_2\trianglelefteq H$, it contains their normal closure:
$\mathfrak D(H,L)\le K_2$.  Every element of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ is
therefore Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in every operator-norm almost
representation of $H$, and the coordinate averaging corner of
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} applies verbatim to any finite normal
$F\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The normal-Kazhdan form of the obstruction}

Finiteness of $F$ enters Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} only through the
averaging corner.  A finite group has property~\textup{(T)}, and
averaging over it is the projection onto its fixed space; for an arbitrary normal
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup, the spectral projection of a Kazhdan
average takes its place.  The resulting form of the obstruction has no
finiteness, centrality, or torsion hypothesis; it is used in
Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\begin{theorem}[normal-Kazhdan obstruction]\label{thm:normal-kazhdan}
Let $H$ be a countable group with a Kazhdan conjugation datum
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, and let $K\trianglelefteq H$ be a normal
subgroup with property~\textup{(T)} such that
$K\subseteq N_{\mathrm{comp}}$.  Then every corona representation
$\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies $\Theta(k)=1$ for all
$k\in K$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptNormalKazhdanObstruction}
\end{theorem}

Taking $K=F$ finite recovers Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, since finite
groups have property~\textup{(T)}: the whole group with tolerance $1$
is a Kazhdan pair, by orbit averaging.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanMFRadical}{GroupApproximation.hasKazhdanPropertyT_of_finite}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan}, $K$ may be infinite, noncentral,
and torsion-free.

\begin{proof}
The containment $N_{\mathrm{comp}}\subseteq K_2$ from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} uses no finiteness, so every element of
$K$ is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in every operator-norm almost
representation of $H$.  It remains to replace the averaging corner by
a corner adapted to $K$; the spectral projection of a Kazhdan average
provides it.

Suppose a corona representation $\Theta$ is nontrivial on $K$.  Its
image $\bar H=\Theta(H)$ is countable, hence MF, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}, applied along an exhaustion of
$\bar H$, gives an operator-norm asymptotic representation
$(V_{g,n})$ in which every fixed nontrivial element eventually stays
at operator-norm distance at least $1$ from the identity.  These
models bound only operator-norm distances; at growing dimension they
give no lower bound on normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distances, and the
corner constructed below provides that lower bound.  The datum and the
subgroup pass to their $\Theta$-images with normality and
property~\textup{(T)} preserved, and $\bar K=\Theta(K)$ is nontrivial
and lies in the compression defect of the image datum; it suffices to
derive a contradiction for $\bar H$.

\emph{The moving spectral subspace.}  Fix a finite symmetric
generating Kazhdan set $S\subseteq\bar K$ with constant
$\varepsilon_0$, at each coordinate let $h_n$ be the Hermitian part of
the average of the models of $S$, and cut the spectrum of $h_n$ at
$\theta=1-\varepsilon_0^2/(4|S|)$.  Let $q_n$ be the spectral
projection of $h_n$ for $[-1,\theta]$: the \emph{moving spectral
subspace}.  On
its complement --- the top spectral subspace --- every model of every
element of $\bar K$ is close to the identity in operator norm, by the
Kazhdan-pair displacement estimate.  If $q_n=0$ for all large $n$,
every element of $\bar K$ would satisfy $\opnorm{V_{k,n}-1}\to0$,
contradicting the operator-norm separation of any fixed nontrivial
element of $\bar K$.  So the moving subspace is nonzero along an
infinite set of coordinates, which we retain.

\emph{The tracial deficit on the corner.}  Equip each retained corner
block $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n$ with its renormalized trace.  By the
definition of the cut, $q_nh_nq_n\le\theta q_n$, so the average over
$S$ of the real parts of the renormalized traces of the compressed
models is at most $\theta$.  Hence at every retained coordinate some
generator in $S$ has compressed model with real renormalized trace at
most $\theta$, and therefore normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance at
least $\sqrt{2(1-\theta)}-o(1)$ from the corner identity after polar
correction; passing to a subsequence fixes a single such generator
$s_0$.

\emph{Compression.}  Normality makes the cut asymptotically central.
Conjugating by an ambient model $V_{g,n}$ carries $h_n$ to the
Hermitian average over the conjugate generating set $gSg^{-1}$ of
$\bar K$, which has the same Kazhdan constant.  The two estimates of
Section~\ref{app:finite-stage} now apply to either average
symmetrically: a vector in the top spectral subspace of one average is
almost fixed by each generator of the other set (displacement,
transported across the finitely many conjugations by
almost-multiplicativity), hence almost lies in the top spectral
subspace of the other average (the concentration inequality).
Two-sided almost-containment of the two subspaces forces
$\opnorm{q_n-V_{g,n}q_nV_{g,n}^{*}}\to0$, that is,
$\opnorm{[q_n,V_{g,n}]_{-}}\to0$ for every $g\in\bar H$.  Compressing
every model to the moving corner and polar-correcting yields an
operator-norm asymptotic representation of all of $\bar H$ on nonzero
blocks, in which $s_0$ keeps a definite normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
distance from the identity.

\emph{The contradiction.}  The tracial vanishing applies verbatim to the
corner representation: $\bar K$ lies in the compression defect of the
image datum, so every element of $\bar K$ --- in particular $s_0$ ---
is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible on the corner, contradicting the
tracial deficit of $s_0$.  The coordinates are scheduled as in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.
\end{proof}

The proofs above use the conjugation datum through one property only:
every element of the compression defect is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in
every operator-norm almost representation.  That property alone
suffices.

\begin{theorem}[abstract invisibility obstruction]\label{thm:abstract-nk}
Let $H$ be a countable group and let $D\le H$ be a subgroup every
element of which is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible
(Definition~\ref{def:invisible}).  Let $K\trianglelefteq H$ be a normal subgroup with
property~\textup{(T)} such that $K\le D$.  Then every corona
representation $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies
$\Theta(k)=1$ for all $k\in K$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptAbstractNormalKazhdanObstruction}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan} used its datum only to
make every element of the compression defect Hilbert--Schmidt invisible
in every operator-norm almost representation; here that invisibility
is the hypothesis.  Suppose a corona representation $\Theta$ is
nontrivial on $K$.  As there, the image $\bar H=\Theta(H)$ is
countable and MF, and Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences} provides
an operator-norm asymptotic representation of $\bar H$ that separates
every fixed nontrivial element.  Every operator-norm almost
representation of $\bar H$ composes with $\Theta$ to one of $H$, so
every element of $\Theta(D)$ inherits the invisibility hypothesis,
while $\bar K=\Theta(K)\le\Theta(D)$ is normal, Kazhdan, and
nontrivial.  The moving spectral subspace, the tracial deficit, and
the compression step of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan} apply verbatim to $\bar K$, and the
contradiction step uses the inherited invisibility in place of the
defect containment.
\end{proof}

Applied to the intrinsic defect, Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-nk} gives
the normal-Kazhdan companion of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-radical}:

\begin{corollary}[normal-Kazhdan form of the intrinsic radical]\label{cor:intrinsic-nk}
Let $H$ and $\Gamma$ be countable groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let $\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism,
and put $L=\iota(\Gamma)$.  If $K\trianglelefteq H$ is a normal
subgroup with property~\textup{(T)} and $K\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, then
every homomorphism from $H$ to every norm matrix corona maps every
element of $K$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptIntrinsicNormalKazhdanRadical}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-radical} shows that every
element of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ is Hilbert--Schmidt invisible in every
operator-norm almost representation of $H$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-nk} with $D=\mathfrak D(H,L)$ concludes.
\end{proof}

The subgroup generated by \emph{all} normal property-\textup{(T)}
subgroups of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ therefore also lies in the kernel of
every corona representation.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptIntrinsicNormalKazhdanPart}
\section{The finite-dimensional obstruction}\label{sec:findim}

The finite-dimensional analogue of the obstruction is elementary: it
needs finite dimensionality and the one-sided conjugation and
centralizer relations alone, and its conclusion covers the entire
intrinsic defect \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}:

\begin{theorem}[the exact finite-dimensional analogue]\label{thm:B}
Let $k$ be a field, $V$ a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, $H$ a
group, $\Gamma\le H$ a subgroup, and $t,c\in H$, $a\in\Gamma$ elements
with $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ and $c\gamma=\gamma c$ for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$.  Then every homomorphism $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$
satisfies
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)\;=\;1 .
\]
More generally, every such $\pi$ sends the entire subgroup
$\mathfrak D(H,\Gamma)$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptTheoremB}
\end{theorem}

Applied to $H=E$, Theorem~\ref{thm:B} shows that \emph{every}
finite-dimensional representation of $E$ over \emph{every} field sends
$w$ to the identity.  Consequently finite-dimensional linear
representations do not separate the points of $E$, no finite quotient
can witness $w\neq 1$, and the infinite Clifford witness of
Section~\ref{sec:witness} cannot be replaced by a finite-dimensional
one.  In the terminology of \cite[Definition~1.2]{BDL}, the central
involution $w$, invisible in every finite quotient, makes $E$ a group
of \emph{Deligne type}.  Such groups were proposed there as the
natural candidate counterexamples for all three remaining
approximation problems, and $E$ realizes that proposal at the
operator-norm endpoint.
Property~\textup{(T)} enters the corona argument through the Kazhdan
fixed-space projection of the adjoint actions --- in quantitative form,
a finite-stage spectral subspace at each coordinate, where the
equal-rank reversal estimate of Section~\ref{app:finite-stage} plays
the role that equal dimension plays in the exact argument below.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
Let $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$ be a homomorphism and let
\[
  C\;=\;\{\,x\in\End_k(V)\;:\;\pi(\gamma)\,x=x\,\pi(\gamma)
  \text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\,\}
\]
be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$, a linear subspace of $\End_k(V)$.
Let $u=\pi(t)$ and consider the linear automorphism
$\Phi(x)=uxu^{-1}$ of $\End_k(V)$.

We claim $C\subseteq\Phi(C)$.  Let $x\in C$ and set $y=u^{-1}xu$, so
that $x=\Phi(y)$.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we have
$t\gamma t^{-1}\in\Gamma$, hence $\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})$ commutes with
$x$, and therefore
\[
  \pi(\gamma)\,y
  = u^{-1}\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,x\,u
  = u^{-1}\,x\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,u
  = y\,\pi(\gamma).
\]
Thus $y\in C$, proving the claim.  Since $\Phi$ is a linear
isomorphism, $\dim_k\Phi(C)=\dim_kC<\infty$, and the inclusion
$C\subseteq\Phi(C)$ forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinning}
  C\;=\;\Phi(C)\;=\;uCu^{-1}.
\end{equation}

Now $\pi(c)\in C$ because $c$ centralizes $\Gamma$.  By
\eqref{eq:pinning}, $\pi(tct^{-1})=u\,\pi(c)\,u^{-1}\in C$.  Since
$a\in\Gamma$, the element $\pi(tct^{-1})$ commutes with $\pi(a)$, so
$\pi\bigl(a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr)=\pi(tct^{-1})$, and therefore
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)
  =\bigl[\,\pi(tct^{-1}),\;\pi(tct^{-1})\bigr]=1. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The same calculation proves the final, subgroup-valued assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.  The elements of $H$ whose conjugation action
preserves $C$ in both directions form a subgroup.  The argument above
puts every one-sided conjugator in that subgroup, and therefore puts all
of $G_{\rm comp}(\Gamma)$ in it.  Thus, for $g\in
G_{\rm comp}(\Gamma)$ and $z\in C_H(\Gamma)$, the operator
$\pi(gzg^{-1})$ still belongs to $C$ and commutes with every
$\pi(\ell)$, $\ell\in\Gamma$.  Hence every displayed generator in
\eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect} lies in $\ker\pi$; since
$\ker\pi\trianglelefteq H$, the whole normal closure
$\mathfrak D(H,\Gamma)$ does as well.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:notRFD}
The group $E$ of Definition~\ref{def:E} admits no injective homomorphism
into $\GL(V)$ for any finite-dimensional vector space $V$ over any field.
More strongly, every finite-dimensional linear representation maps $w$ to
the identity, and every homomorphism to a finite group maps $w$ to the
identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralFiniteDimensionalObstruction}{GroupApproximation.LiteralFiniteDimensionalObstruction.manuscriptFiniteDimensionalConsequences}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with $H=E$, the image of $\mathcal B$, and the
elements $t,c,v_1$; the hypotheses hold by the defining relations
\eqref{eq:E}.  Every finite-dimensional representation maps $w$ to the
identity, and
$w\neq1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  For a finite target, compose
with its faithful left regular representation on the group algebra over
$\mathbb Q$.  The composite is a finite-dimensional linear
representation, so it maps $w$ to the identity.  Evaluating the resulting identity
matrix at the basis vector of the identity element returns the image
of $w$ in the finite group.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the invariant-size principle]\label{rem:invariantsize}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} used exactly one feature of finite
dimensionality: an inclusion $C_1\subseteq C_2$ of subspaces with
$\dim_kC_1=\dim_kC_2<\infty$ is an equality.  In abstract form: suppose a
conjugation-invariant size
$\sigma$ with values in $\mathbb N$ is defined on the subgroups of a
group $Q$ and satisfies the separation property that $K\le L$ with
$\sigma(L)\le\sigma(K)$ forces $K=L$.  If $gKg^{-1}\subseteq K$, then
$gKg^{-1}$ and $K$ have equal size, so the inclusion is an equality:
such a target admits no strict one-sided compression.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptInvariantSizePrinciple}
Cardinality on the subgroups of a finite group qualifies: in a finite
group $gKg^{-1}\subseteq K$ already gives $gKg^{-1}=K$, so a strict
one-sided compression has no image in any finite quotient.  The
finite-dimensional case is not the same principle applied to
subgroups.  The natural
candidate size there, the dimension of the fixed space of the adjoint
action, is $\dim_k\End_H(V)$, the dimension of the commutant; it is
order-reversing rather than order-preserving in $H$, and it does not
separate nested subgroups --- $A_4<S_4$ in the standard
three-dimensional representation have the same commutant $k\cdot1$.
What the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} uses is the principle one level
down, on the commutant itself: the inclusion $C\subseteq uCu^{-1}$
between subspaces of equal finite dimension is an equality
\eqref{eq:pinning}.  Pinning the commutant, not the subgroup, is what
makes the defect trivial.  The corona argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}
is the analytic counterpart of this principle at infinite dimension.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[chain conditions suffice]\label{rem:chaincondition}
The separation property of the size can be dropped, at the price of
concluding equality of values rather than of subgroups.  An order isomorphism of a
poset with no infinite strictly ascending chain fixes every point that
it moves weakly upward, and dually with descending chains; consequently
a conjugation-equivariant monotone invariant with chain-condition values
takes the same value on $\Gamma$ and on $t\Gamma t^{-1}$.  In
particular, for every finite-dimensional representation $\sigma$ of $H$
over every field, the fixed subspaces of the subgroup and of its
one-sided conjugate coincide.
\leanverified{Criterion/ChainConditionCompression}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptChainConditionRigidity}
Applied to the representations induced on tensor constructions ---
$V^{\otimes p}\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes q}$ with the contragredient
action, finite direct sums, $\operatorname{Hom}$-spaces ---
compression creates no invariant tensors of any finite type; the
commutant identity \eqref{eq:pinning} is the case
$\End(V)\cong V\otimes V^{*}$.
\leanverified{Criterion/TensorInvariantRigidity}{GroupApproximation.TensorInvariantRigidity.manuscriptTensorInvariantRigidity}
Taking the invariant to be the
Zariski closure of the image in $\GL(V)$, whose closed subgroups
satisfy the descending chain condition, gives
$\overline{\sigma(t\Gamma t^{-1})}^{\,Z}=\overline{\sigma(\Gamma)}^{\,Z}$:
an exact finite-dimensional representation assigns the compressed
subgroup and the subgroup the same algebraic envelope.  Only the chain
condition is used: whenever the closed subgroups are well-founded
under $\supseteq$ and the envelope is monotone and conjugation-stable,
a one-sided compression leaves the envelope fixed.
\leanverified{Criterion/ClosedEnvelopeCompression}{GroupApproximation.ClosedEnvelopeCompression.manuscriptEnvelopeCompressionBlind}
The chain condition itself, for Zariski-closed subgroups of $\GL(V)$ over
an arbitrary field, is proved here from the Hilbert basis theorem rather
than quoted, and with it the displayed identity in the form printed above.
\leanverified{Algebra/ZariskiDescendingChain}{GroupApproximation.ZariskiClosedSubgroup.manuscriptZariskiClosureOfRepresentation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport-variants}(2)
is the asymptotic counterpart at fixed tensor type.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Why the corona argument is different.}
An MF model is only asymptotically multiplicative, so its coordinate fixed
spaces are not literal invariant subspaces.  Operator-norm control keeps
the coordinate adjoint actions almost multiplicative on the
Hilbert--Schmidt spaces.  The spectral gap of a Kazhdan pair then
produces an almost-fixed spectral subspace.  The equal-rank reversal estimate of
Section~\ref{app:finite-stage} turns its one-sided compression into
equality.  These are the quantitative counterparts of the exact
finite-dimensional steps above.
\section{The cyclic comparison}\label{sec:necessity}

The finite-dimensional calculation of
Section~\ref{sec:findim} does not by itself determine
whether its marked word is trivial:

\begin{theorem}[the cyclic comparison]\label{thm:C}
Define the marked presentation
\[
 E_{\mathrm{BS}}=\left\langle\gamma_0,t,c\ \middle|\
 \begin{aligned}
  &t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2,\quad c^2=1,\quad[c,\gamma_0]=1,\\
  &w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1,\quad
    [w_{\mathrm{BS}},\gamma_0]=[w_{\mathrm{BS}},t]
      =[w_{\mathrm{BS}},c]=1
 \end{aligned}\right\rangle,
\]
where
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}=[tct^{-1},\gamma_0(tct^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}]$.
Then $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ is finitely presented and $w_{\mathrm{BS}}\ne1$.
There is an explicit surjective homomorphism from $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ onto its
realized Clifford quotient that maps $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to the nontrivial
central sign.  Nevertheless every finite-dimensional representation of
$E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ over every field maps $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Monsters/LiteralCyclicCalibration}{GroupApproximation.LiteralCyclicCalibration.manuscriptCyclicCalibration}
\end{theorem}

Thus exact finite-dimensional invisibility does not by itself force
triviality of the marked word.  The MF conclusion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is the separate uniform assertion proved by the
fixed-space argument.


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
As with $w$, the relator $w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1$ is redundant: $tct^{-1}$
and $\gamma_0(tct^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}$ are involutions, so centrality
forces it by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}.  It is retained only to
display the marked pattern.  Deleting it gives a six-relator
presentation of the same group.
The stable relation gives
\(t\langle\gamma_0\rangle t^{-1}\subseteq
\langle\gamma_0\rangle\), and \(c\) centralizes this cyclic base.
The same commutant argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:B} therefore maps
\(w_{\mathrm{BS}}\) to the identity in every finite-dimensional
representation over every field.

For nontriviality, use the affine realization of
\(\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\) and its coset action.  The root site is fixed by the
cyclic base, while the two sites represented by \(t\) and \(\gamma_0t\)
are distinct.  Attach Clifford generators to these sites and map \(c\) to
the root lamp.  All seven relators hold, and
\[
 w_{\mathrm{BS}}\longmapsto
 [c_{t\langle\gamma_0\rangle},
  c_{\gamma_0t\langle\gamma_0\rangle}]
 =-1.
\]
Thus \(w_{\mathrm{BS}}\ne1\).  Restricting the target to the subgroup
generated by the three displayed images gives the asserted surjective
realized quotient.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Sharpness of the Kazhdan hypothesis.}
The realized Clifford quotient in Theorem~\ref{thm:C} is amenable,
hence MF, and its quotient map sends $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to a nontrivial
element.  Composing with a faithful corona representation of that
quotient gives a corona representation $\Theta$ of $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$
with $\Theta(w_{\mathrm{BS}})\ne1$, so property~\textup{(T)} cannot be
omitted from the central-sign criterion.  In the radical language of
Section~\ref{sec:consequences},
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}\notin\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E_{\mathrm{BS}})$.
The affine group $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\cong\mathbb Z[1/2]\rtimes\mathbb Z$ is
solvable.  The Clifford lamp group on its coset space is locally finite, so
their semidirect product is amenable.  The realized quotient is a subgroup
of this amenable group and is therefore amenable, hence MF by \cite{TWW}.
Its marked word is the nontrivial Clifford sign by the calculation above.

The corona representation just constructed is necessarily approximate:
neither the group $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ nor its realized Clifford quotient
is LEF, and the latter is not residually finite.  In every finite
group the doubling relation makes the image of
$\langle\gamma_0^2\rangle$ equal to the image of
$\langle\gamma_0\rangle$; the lamp therefore commutes with the
required ``halved'' element and the marked word maps to the identity,
so neither group is residually finite.  The same equations transport
through any exact local embedding of a finite test set containing the
defining relators and the marked word, so an exact finite local model
would already force the marked word to the identity: neither group is
LEF.

The same construction works for every exponent.  For $m\ge2$, let $E_m$ be the same

\begin{corollary}[the scaling family]\label{cor:scaling-family}
For every natural number $m\ge2$, the group $E_m$ is finitely presented and
$w_m$ is a nontrivial central involution mapped to the identity by every
homomorphism from $E_m$ to every unitary norm matrix corona.  Consequently $E_m$ is not MF,

\leanverified{Sofic/ScalingFamilyEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.ScalingFamilyEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremFamily}

\begin{proof}

\section{Consequences}\label{sec:consequences}

\subsection{The MF radical}

\begin{definition}\label{def:radical}
For a countable group $G_1$, the \emph{MF radical} is
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)
  \;=\;\bigcap\,\bigl\{\ker\Theta:\ \Theta\ \text{a corona
  representation of }G_1\bigr\},
\]
a normal subgroup of $G_1$.  The intersection does not change if the
dimension sequences are allowed to be arbitrary positive rather than
strictly increasing (Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}).  This is the
operator-norm analogue of
the invisible subgroups of \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}
and of the metric-ultraproduct approximation framework of \cite{NST}.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMFRadical}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[functoriality]\label{lem:portable}
For every homomorphism $\varphi\colon G_1\to G_2$ of countable
groups,
\[
  \varphi\bigl(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)\bigr)
  \;\subseteq\;\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2):
\]
the MF radical is functorial.  In particular, if
$\varphi\colon E\to G_2$ satisfies $\varphi(w)\neq1$, then $G_2$ is
not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptRadicalPortability}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $x\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$ and $\Theta$ is a
corona representation of $G_2$, then $\Theta\circ\varphi$ is a corona
representation of $G_1$, so $\Theta(\varphi(x))=1$.  For the second
statement, $w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, so
$1\neq\varphi(w)\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2)$, and an
injective corona representation of $G_2$ would map $\varphi(w)$ to $1$, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the universal MF quotient]\label{prop:univquot}
Let $G_1$ be a countable group and
$R=\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the kernel of a single corona representation of $G_1$.
\item $G_1/R$ is MF.
\item Every homomorphism from $G_1$ to an MF group factors through
$G_1/R$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $G_1$ is MF if and only if $R=\{1\}$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUniversalMFQuotient}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(3) If $\varphi\colon G_1\to M$ with $M$ MF and
$j\colon M\to\Ucor((d_n))$ is an injective corona representation,
then $j\circ\varphi$ is a corona representation of $G_1$, so
$j(\varphi(R))=1$ and, by injectivity of $j$, $\varphi(R)=1$.

(1) and (2).  Work on $Q=G_1/R$.  Every corona representation of
$G_1$ maps $R$ to $1$, and for each $g\notin R$ there is one with
$\Theta(g)\ne1$, which descends; so for every $\bar g\ne1$ in $Q$ there
is a corona representation $\Theta_{\bar g}$ of $Q$ with
$\Theta_{\bar g}(\bar g)\ne1$.  Enumerate the nonidentity elements and
these representations, pass to coordinate unitary lifts, and
diagonalize: at stage
$k$, select in each of the first $k$ of them a coordinate at which
multiplicativity holds within $1/k$ on the first $k$ group elements
and each of the first $k$ marked elements stays separated by its fixed
positive constant --- possible because each $\Theta_{\bar g}$ separates
its own element along infinitely many coordinates --- and take the
block sum.  Block operator norms are maxima, so the resulting sequence
is an almost representation separating every nonidentity element from
the identity; its class,
composed with the polar-correction isomorphism of
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}, is a faithful corona representation of
$Q$.  This proves (2), and composing with
$G_1\twoheadrightarrow Q$ proves (1).  Finally, if $G_1$ is MF then an
injective corona representation forces $R=\{1\}$; if $R=\{1\}$ then
(2) says $G_1$ is MF.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[exact radical from a candidate quotient]\label{cor:exactradical}
Let $N\trianglelefteq G_1$ satisfy
$N\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.  Every corona
representation of $G_1$ factors uniquely through $G_1/N$.  If in
addition $G_1/N$ is MF, then
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptExactRadicalFromCandidateQuotient}
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)=N.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The containment $N\le\ker\Theta$ gives the unique quotient
factorization for every corona representation $\Theta$.  For the
reverse containment in the displayed equality, compose the quotient
map $G_1\to G_1/N$ with an injective corona representation of $G_1/N$:
its kernel is exactly $N$, while the MF radical lies in the kernel of
every corona representation.
\end{proof}

The MF hypothesis on the quotient can be dropped entirely; what
remains is an unconditional reduction.

\begin{corollary}[radical reduction to the quotient]\label{cor:pullback}
Let $N\trianglelefteq G_1$ satisfy
$N\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$ and let
$q\colon G_1\to G_1/N$ be the quotient map.  Then
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptRadicalReductionToQuotient}
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)
 \;=\;
 q^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1/N)\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the forward containment, Lemma~\ref{lem:portable} gives
$q(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1))
\subseteq\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1/N)$.  Conversely, suppose
$q(x)\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1/N)$ and let $\Theta$ be a
corona representation of $G_1$.  Every corona representation of $G_1$
maps $N$ to $1$ by hypothesis, so $\Theta$ factors as $\bar\Theta\circ q$
with $\bar\Theta$ a corona representation of $G_1/N$; hence
$\Theta(x)=\bar\Theta(q(x))=1$.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:exactradical} is the case of trivial quotient
radical.  Corollary~\ref{cor:pullback} needs no hypothesis on the
quotient: the corona representation theory of $G_1$ \emph{is} that of
$G_1/N$, whether or not the latter is MF.

By Corollary~\ref{cor:pullback}, the passage from $N$ to the full
preimage of the quotient radical is a closure operation on the normal
subgroups of a fixed group.

\begin{proposition}[the MF closure of relations]\label{prop:mfclosure}
For a countable group $G_1$ and $N\trianglelefteq G_1$ with quotient map
$q\colon G_1\to G_1/N$, put
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)
 =q^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1/N)\bigr).
\]
An element belongs to $\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)$ exactly when
every corona representation of $G_1$ that maps $N$ to the identity maps
it to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/MFRelationClosure}{GroupApproximation.mem_actualCoronaMFClosure_iff}
The operation $N\mapsto\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)$ is extensive,
monotone, and idempotent on normal subgroups;
$G_1/\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)$ is MF; and
$\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)=N$ exactly when $G_1/N$ is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/MFRelationClosure}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptRelationClosure}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Corona representations of $G_1$ that map $N$ to $1$ correspond to corona
representations of $G_1/N$, which gives the membership
characterization; extensivity, monotonicity, and idempotence are read
off from it.  The quotient clause is Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}(2)
transported along the third isomorphism theorem, and the fixed-point
clause is the final equivalence of Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}.
\end{proof}

For a presentation $G=\langle X\mid R\rangle=F/N$, the closure of $N$
consists of the relations satisfied by every solution of $R$ in every
MF group, and the group is MF exactly when $N$ is closed.
Proposition~\ref{prop:horn} displays one finite fragment of this
closure, and the marked element of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is an element of
$\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)\setminus N$ for the forty-one
relators.

\begin{proposition}[iterated sound rules]\label{prop:sounditeration}
Consider the subgroups of $G_1$ reachable from the trivial subgroup by
two operations: from a reached \emph{normal} subgroup $N$, pass to any
subgroup of $\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)$; and from any family
of reached subgroups, pass to their join, the subgroup generated by
their union.  Every subgroup so reached lies in
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$; a reached \emph{normal}
subgroup with MF
quotient equals $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$; and if the
whole group is reached, every homomorphism from $G_1$ to a countable MF
group is trivial.
\leanverified{Sofic/MFRelationClosure}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptSoundIterationClosure}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Induct over the two operations.  If a reached normal subgroup $N$
satisfies $N\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$, then
Corollary~\ref{cor:pullback} gives
$\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}(N)=\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$,
so every subgroup chosen in the first operation is again inside the
radical --- normal or not.  For the second operation, the radical is
itself a subgroup, hence an upper bound in the subgroup lattice for any
family contained in it; so the join $\bigvee_iH_i=\langle\bigcup_iH_i\rangle$
lies in the radical too.  The union itself need not be a subgroup, and
it is the join that the calculus uses.  The final two assertions are
Corollary~\ref{cor:exactradical} and Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot};
the second of them needs $N$ normal, which is where the quotient
$G_1/N$ is formed.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm:criterion}, \ref{thm:normal-kazhdan},
and~\ref{thm:collapse} are sound rules in this sense: each places a
subgroup inside the radical of every countable group with its data,
hence inside the radical of every reached quotient.
Theorem~\ref{thm:saturation} is the case in which a single application
reaches the whole group.  A rule instance can hold at a later stage
without holding at an earlier one: modulo the earlier stage an element
may acquire finite order, an orbit may begin to commute, or a subgroup
may become normal.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nofaithful}
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra admitting an injective, possibly
nonunital, $*$-homomorphism into a norm matrix corona.  Then the group $E$
admits no injective homomorphism into $\U(A)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFConsequences.literal_no_faithful_corona_subalgebra_target}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $j\colon A\to Q$ be the possibly nonunital inclusion into the ambient
corona and let $\phi\colon E\to\U(A)$.  Complement correction gives
\[
   \widehat j(u)=j(u)+1-j(1_A)\in\U(Q).
\]
This is an injective homomorphism on \(\U(A)\), so
\(\widehat j\circ\phi\) is a corona representation of \(E\).
By Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, \((\widehat j\circ\phi)(w)=1\); injectivity
of \(\widehat j\) gives \(\phi(w)=1\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} has
\(w\neq1\), so \(\phi\) cannot be injective.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Permanence and its failure}

\begin{lemma}[permanence]\label{lem:permanence}
Let all groups be countable.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every subgroup of an MF group is MF.
\item Every residually finite group is MF.
\item Every locally finite group is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.IsOperatorMF.subgroup}
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_of_residuallyFinite}
\leanverified{Sofic/LocallyFiniteMF}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_of_locallyFinite}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1)  Restrict an injective corona representation.  (2)  For a finite
test set $F$, a finite quotient injective on $F$, composed with its
left regular permutation action, is an exact local model; distinct
permutations differ in some column of a permutation matrix, giving
operator-norm separation at least $1$, and the local-to-corona
direction of Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences} concludes.
(3)  A finite test set lies in a finite subgroup $K$; a map that is
the identity on all of $K$, composed with the left regular permutation
action of $K$, is an exact local model on the test set --- products of
test elements stay in $K$ --- separating as in (2).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[quotients]\label{cor:quotclosure}
MF groups are not closed under quotients: the free group
\(F_8\) is MF, and the explicit group \(E\) is its quotient
but is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralMFQuotientControls}{GroupApproximation.LiteralMFQuotientControls.manuscriptQuotientNonclosure}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Map the eight free generators onto the eight displayed generators of \(E\).
The resulting homomorphism is surjective because those generators define the
displayed presentation.  The group \(F_8\) is residually finite, hence MF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(2).  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} shows that \(E\) is not MF.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Matricial stability and the radical}\label{subsec:stability}

Call a countable group $G$ \emph{point-norm matricially stable} if for
every operator-norm almost representation $(V_n)$ of $G$ there are
genuine finite-dimensional unitary representations $\pi_n$ on the same
models with $\opnorm{V_n(g)-\pi_n(g)}\to0$ for every $g\in G$.
Write $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{fd}}(G)$ for the intersection of
the kernels of all finite-dimensional unitary representations of $G$.

\begin{proposition}[stability identifies the radicals]\label{prop:stabradical}
For every countable group,
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)\le\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{fd}}(G)$.
If $G$ is point-norm matricially stable, then
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{fd}}(G)$;
in particular a stable group with
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{fd}}(G)\neq1$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/MatricialStabilityRadical}{GroupApproximation.MatricialStabilityRadical.actualCoronaMFResidual_le_fdUnitaryResidual}
\leanverified{Sofic/MatricialStabilityRadical}{GroupApproximation.MatricialStabilityRadical.actualCoronaMFResidual_eq_fdUnitaryResidual}
\leanverified{Sofic/MatricialStabilityRadical}{GroupApproximation.MatricialStabilityRadical.not_isCDEOperatorMF_of_stable_of_fdResidual_ne_bot}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first inclusion needs no stability.  A finite-dimensional unitary
representation $\pi$ on $\C^{d}$ amplifies to $\pi^{\oplus n}$ on
$\C^{nd}$; the dimensions $nd$ are strictly increasing, so
$(\pi^{\oplus n})_n$ is a corona representation in the sense of
\eqref{eq:unitary-corona}.  Since
$\opnorm{\pi^{\oplus n}(x)-1}=\opnorm{\pi(x)-1}$ for every $n$, that
sequence is null exactly when $\pi(x)=1$.  Hence an MF-invisible
element is sent to the identity by every finite-dimensional
unitary representation.

For the converse inclusion, assume $G$ point-norm matricially stable,
let $x\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{fd}}(G)$, and suppose toward a
contradiction that a corona representation $\Theta$ has
$\Theta(x)\ne1$.  Choose unitary coordinate lifts $U_n(g)$ of
$\Theta(g)$.  Since $\Theta(x)-1\ne0$ in the norm quotient, after
passing to a subsequence there is $\delta>0$ with
$\opnorm{U_n(x)-1}\ge\delta$ for every $n$.  The lifts form an
operator-norm almost representation, so stability gives genuine
representations $\pi_n$ on the same blocks with
$\opnorm{U_n(g)-\pi_n(g)}\to0$ for each fixed $g$.  But
$x\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{fd}}(G)$ gives $\pi_n(x)=1$ for every
$n$, so
$\opnorm{U_n(x)-1}=\opnorm{U_n(x)-\pi_n(x)}\to0$, against the uniform
lower bound.
\end{proof}

For a stable group the corona representation theory is therefore
computed by finite-dimensional representation theory alone.  Combined
with Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, this is
a second route to non-MF groups, one that uses neither torsion nor a
marked word nor a conjugation datum: every point-norm matricially
stable group with $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{fd}}(G)\ne1$ fails to
be MF.

\subsection{Orbit collapse and defect saturation}\label{subsec:collapse}

The criteria of Section~\ref{sec:conj} bound the radical from below
by torsion or rigidity already present in the compression defect.  This
subsection gives a second application of one-sided compression, under
a commuting-orbit hypothesis in place of centrality: every corona
representation identifies an entire conjugation orbit of an
involution, so whole families of commutators --- not one marked
element --- enter the radical, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:pullback}
the radical computation reduces to the quotient by all of them.

\begin{definition}[involutive compression witness]\label{def:invwitness}
Let $H$ be a group, $L\le H$ a subgroup, and $s\in H$.  An element
$k\in H$ is an \emph{involutive compression witness} for $(L,s)$ if
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(W1)] $k^2=1$;
\item[(W2)] $s\gamma s^{-1}$ commutes with $k$ for every $\gamma\in L$;
\item[(W3)] the conjugates $\gamma_1k\gamma_1^{-1}$ and
  $\gamma_2k\gamma_2^{-1}$ commute for all $\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in L$.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{involutive collapse defect} of the pair $(L,s)$ is the
normal subgroup
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptInvolutiveCollapsePattern}
\[
  D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)
  \;=\;
  \bigl\langle\!\bigl\langle\,
  [\gamma,k]\;:\;\gamma\in L,\
  k\text{ a witness for }(L,s)
  \,\bigr\rangle\!\bigr\rangle_H .
\]
\end{definition}

In a permutational wreath product $K^{(X)}\rtimes G$ with $L\le G$
compressed by $s$ and $x_0\in X$ a point fixed by $sLs^{-1}$, every
involution of the lamp copy at $x_0$ is a witness: lamps at distinct
sites commute, and the compressed copy fixes the site.  The theorem
below then identifies the lamps along the whole $L$-orbit of $x_0$ in
every corona representation.

\begin{theorem}[involutive collapse]\label{thm:collapse}
Let $H$ be countable, let $L\le H$ be a subgroup with
property~\textup{(T)}, and let $s\in H$ satisfy
$s\gamma s^{-1}\in L$ for every $\gamma\in L$.  Then
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptInvolutiveCollapse}
\[
  D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)\;\le\;\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Fix a witness $k$ and suppose, toward a contradiction, that some corona
representation $\Theta$ has $\Theta([\gamma_0,k])\ne1$ for some
$\gamma_0\in L$.
Coordinate unitary lifts (Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}) give an
operator-norm almost representation $(U_{g,n})$ on blocks
$M_{d_n}(\C)$, and after passing to a subsequence there is $\eta>0$
with $\opnorm{U_{[\gamma_0,k],n}-1}\ge\eta$ for every $n$.

\emph{Step 1: approximation by commuting self-adjoint involutions.}  For
$\gamma\in L$ put $v_\gamma=\gamma k\gamma^{-1}$; by (W1), (W3), and
asymptotic multiplicativity their images asymptotically commute and
square to $1$.  The spectral sign of the Hermitian part of a unitary
$u$ is an exact self-adjoint involution at operator distance at most
$\opnorm{u^2-1}$ from $u$, and block compression along the finite
commutative corner structure of the previously corrected involutions
preserves exact commutation; induction over an enumeration of $L$
yields exact pairwise-commuting self-adjoint involutions $V_n(\gamma)$
with $\opnorm{V_n(\gamma)-U_{v_\gamma,n}}\to0$ and
$\opnorm{U_{g,n}V_n(\gamma)U_{g,n}^{*}-V_n(g\gamma)}\to0$ for each
fixed $g\in L$, where $g\gamma$ denotes the orbit index of
$gv_\gamma g^{-1}$.

\emph{Step 2: a rank pseudometric on the orbit.}  For commuting exact
involutions $e,f$ the self-adjoint displacement $T=e-f$ satisfies
$T^3=4T$, so $T^2/4$ is an orthogonal projection and
$\operatorname{Tr}|e-f|^2=4\operatorname{rank}((e-f)^2/4)$: rank is an
exact support count for the Hilbert--Schmidt displacement here.  Hence
$d_n(\gamma,\mu)=\operatorname{rank}(V_n(\gamma)-V_n(\mu))$ is an
integer-valued pseudometric; projections at operator distance below
$1$ have equal rank, so the covariance of Step 1 makes it eventually
translation invariant: $d_n(g\gamma,g\mu)=d_n(\gamma,\mu)$ for all
large $n$, for each fixed triple.  The rank comparison is applied to
the projections $T^2/4$, not to the involutions themselves, and that
is where the bound below $1$ is needed, since equality of ranks for
projections requires it: the displacements have norm at
most $2$, so a covariance error below $1/8$ moves the associated
projections by at most $1/4<1$.

\emph{Step 3: normalization at the generators.}  Property~\textup{(T)} makes
$L$ finitely generated; fix a Kazhdan pair $(S,\kappa)$ with $S$
finite symmetric and put $k_n=\sum_{a\in S}d_n(1,a)$.  Translation
invariance and the triangle inequality give
$d_n(1,\gamma)\le|\gamma|_S\,k_n$ eventually, for the word length
$|\cdot|_S$.  If $k_n=0$ along a subsequence, the whole orbit
collapses there to a single exact involution and
$\opnorm{U_{[\gamma_0,k],n}-1}\to0$, against the separation; so
$k_n\ge1$ for all large $n$.

\emph{Step 4: an approximate $1$-cocycle from the displacements.}  In the Euclidean
space of matrices set
$b_n(\gamma)=k_n^{-1/2}\bigl(V_n(\gamma)-V_n(1)\bigr)$.  By Steps 2
and 3, $\|b_n(\gamma)\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2\le4\,|\gamma|_S$ at every
large stage, with total generator mass
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generator-mass}
  \sum_{a\in S}\|b_n(a)\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2=4
\end{equation}
whenever $k_n\ge1$.  Conjugation by $U_{a,n}$ is a Frobenius isometry.
Fix $a,\gamma\in L$, put $\mu=a^{-1}\gamma$, and let $k_n^{-1/2}D_n$ be
the defect in the cocycle identity, so that
\[
  D_n=\bigl(V_n(\gamma)-V_n(1)\bigr)
  -U_{a,n}\bigl(V_n(\mu)-V_n(1)\bigr)U_{a,n}^{*}
  -\bigl(V_n(a)-V_n(1)\bigr).
\]
Two groupings of this one expression control the two factors of the
Hilbert--Schmidt estimate, and neither yields the other.  As written,
$D_n$ is a sum of three displacements of the commuting family of
Step~1, one of them conjugated; rank is subadditive and invariant
under unitary conjugation, so Step~2 with the bound of Step~3 gives
\[
  \operatorname{rank}D_n\le d_n(\gamma,1)+d_n(\mu,1)+d_n(a,1)
  \le\bigl(|\gamma|_S+|\mu|_S+|a|_S\bigr)k_n
\]
eventually.  Regrouped as
\[
  D_n=\bigl(V_n(\gamma)-U_{a,n}V_n(\mu)U_{a,n}^{*}\bigr)
  +\bigl(U_{a,n}V_n(1)U_{a,n}^{*}-V_n(a)\bigr),
\]
the same $D_n$ is a sum of two covariance errors of Step~1, so
$\opnorm{D_n}\to0$.  The rank bound comes from the first grouping and
the operator-norm bound from the second.  The norm bound alone would
not give the rank bound: two exact involutions at small operator
distance can differ in full rank.  Combining the two bounds through
$\operatorname{Tr}|D|^2\le\operatorname{rank}(D)\cdot\opnorm{D}^2$
bounds the normalized defect mass by
$\bigl(|\gamma|_S+|\mu|_S+|a|_S\bigr)\opnorm{D_n}^2\to0$: the defect
mass vanishes at the scale $k_n$.  All estimates below concern only
differences of the involutions.

\emph{Step 5: a bounded profile and an approximate coboundary.}  The
Gaussians $e^{-t\|\cdot\|^2}$ of the approximate cocycle are almost
invariant under the twisted action, and the spectral gap of
$(S,\kappa)$ bounds the limiting profile
$\gamma\mapsto\lim_{n\to\omega}\|b_n(\gamma)\|_{\mathrm F}^2$, the limit
along a fixed free ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\N$, by its
generator values \eqref{eq:generator-mass} --- Delorme's argument at
the level of limiting seminorms.  The limit must be taken along an
ultrafilter and not as a $\limsup$: positive definiteness of the
Gaussians is transferred from the finite stages by evaluating a finite
quadratic form with \emph{signed} coefficients, which only a linear
limit functional respects, and $\limsup$ gives the inequality in the
wrong direction.  The same linearity is what makes
\eqref{eq:generator-mass} an identity in the limit rather than an
inequality.  Since $L$ is countable one could instead pass to a
subsequence along which all the limits exist.  A circumcenter of the displacement
orbit, taken at the level of bounded sequences, then produces one
bounded sequence $(y_n)$ with
$b_n(a)\approx y_n-U_{a,n}y_nU_{a,n}^{*}$ in limiting normalized mass,
simultaneously for any prescribed finite subset of $L$.

\emph{Step 6: transport by the conjugator $s$.}  By (W2) the
models of $sas^{-1}$ and of $1$ converge to one another, which is
an operator-norm statement; Step~4 has just warned that two exact
involutions at small operator distance can nevertheless differ in full
rank, so this does not yet bound the displacement.  What closes the gap
is a rigidity property of the commuting family of Step~1: two
\emph{commuting} exact self-adjoint involutions $e,f$ at operator
distance $\opnorm{e-f}<2$ are equal.  Indeed $T=e-f$ is self-adjoint
with $T^3=4T$, so $T^2/4$ is an orthogonal projection, whose norm is
therefore $0$ or $1$; but $\opnorm{T^2/4}=\opnorm{T}^2/4<1$, so
$T^2=0$ and hence $T=0$.  The displacement therefore vanishes exactly
on compressed elements, so including the
compressed generators $sas^{-1}$ among the prescribed elements makes
the coboundary $(y_n)$
almost fixed by them; hence $z_n=U_{s,n}^{*}y_nU_{s,n}$ is almost
fixed by every $a\in S$, because the models of $as^{-1}$ and
$s^{-1}(sas^{-1})$ agree asymptotically.  Transporting forward as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} --- compression to the
almost-fixed spectral subspace of the averaged adjoint action of $S$,
then the one-sided estimate $P\le VPV^{*}$ at the rescaled mass ---
returns $y_n$ up to vanishing mass, again almost fixed by every
$a\in S$.  Then $b_n(a)\approx y_n-U_{a,n}y_nU_{a,n}^{*}$ has small
mass for every $a\in S$ at a common large stage, contradicting
\eqref{eq:generator-mass}.  Normality of corona kernels extends the
conclusion from the commutators to all of $D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the quantitative form]\label{rem:collapse-finite-stage}
As with the transport theorem, the limits in the sketch above can be
replaced by explicit tolerances, the circumcenter by an approximate
circumcenter of a near-optimal covering sequence, and the final
contradiction assembled at one sufficiently late coordinate; we do not
print that bookkeeping here.  The commutation hypothesis (W3) is
essential: it gives the joint spectral calculus behind the rank
identity of Step 2.  Anticommuting Clifford coordinates have no joint
spectral labels; their collapse mechanism is the central sign of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.  At the corona level,
Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-collapse} below removes the restriction to
involutions, and Theorem~\ref{thm:torsion-collapse} extends the
collapse to witnesses of every finite order through their discrete
Fourier idempotents.
\end{remark}

The witness of Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} is a group element.  The same
collapse holds for arbitrary projections of the corona.

\begin{theorem}[projection collapse]\label{thm:projection-collapse}
Let $H$ be countable, let $L\le H$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$s\in H$ satisfy $s\gamma s^{-1}\in L$ for every $\gamma\in L$, and let
$\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a corona representation.  Let
$p\in\mathcal Q$ be a projection such that $p$ commutes with
$\Theta(s\gamma s^{-1})$ for every $\gamma\in L$ and the projections
$\Theta(\gamma)\,p\,\Theta(\gamma)^*$, $\gamma\in L$, commute pairwise.
Then $p$ commutes with $\Theta(L)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ProjectionCompressionCollapse}{GroupApproximation.ProjectionCompressionCollapse.corona_projection_collapse}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The symmetry $\sigma=1-2p$ is a self-adjoint unitary of $\mathcal Q$;
it is written $\sigma$ because $w$ denotes the marked word of $E$
throughout.  The
subgroup $H'\le\U(\mathcal Q)$ generated by $\Theta(H)$ and $\sigma$ is
countable.  Inside $H'$, the image $\Theta(L)$ has
property~\textup{(T)}, $\Theta(s)$ compresses it, $\sigma$ is an involution
centralizing the compressed copy, and the $\Theta(L)$-conjugates of $\sigma$
commute because the conjugated projections do.  So $\sigma$ is an involutive
compression witness in $H'$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} places
every commutator $[\Theta(\gamma),\sigma]$, $\gamma\in L$, in
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H')$.  The inclusion
$H'\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ is itself a corona representation of $H'$, so it
maps each of these commutators to the identity:
$[\Theta(\gamma),\sigma]=1$ in $\mathcal Q$, and $\sigma=1-2p$ gives
$[\Theta(\gamma),p]_{-}=0$.
\end{proof}

The witness here need not come from a group element: the projection is
an arbitrary element of the corona.  The relative commutant
$\Theta(sLs^{-1})'\cap\mathcal Q$ therefore cannot exceed
$\Theta(L)'\cap\mathcal Q$ by a projection with commuting
$L$-orbit.

Projections in hand, functional calculus removes the involutivity of
the witness altogether.

\begin{theorem}[torsion collapse]\label{thm:torsion-collapse}
Let $H$ be countable, let $L\le H$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$s\in H$ satisfy $s\gamma s^{-1}\in L$ for every $\gamma\in L$, and
let $k\in H$ have finite order.  If the compressed copy $sLs^{-1}$
centralizes $k$ and the conjugates $\gamma k\gamma^{-1}$,
$\gamma\in L$, commute pairwise, then every commutator $[\gamma,k]$,
$\gamma\in L$, is invisible in every corona representation of $H$; the
normal closure of all such commutators, over all finite-order witnesses
for $(L,s)$, lies in $\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)$, with
equality whenever the corresponding quotient of $H$ is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/TorsionSpectralCollapse}{GroupApproximation.TorsionSpectralCollapse.actualCoronaMFInvisible_of_torsionWitness}
\leanverified{Sofic/TorsionSpectralCollapse}{GroupApproximation.TorsionSpectralCollapse.torsionCollapseDefect_le_actualCoronaMFResidual_of_kazhdan}
\leanverified{Sofic/TorsionSpectralCollapse}{GroupApproximation.TorsionSpectralCollapse.actualCoronaMFResidual_eq_torsionCollapseDefect_of_kazhdan}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Theta$ be a corona representation, $m$ the order of $k$, and
$u=\Theta(k)$, a unitary with $u^{m}=1$.  Its discrete Fourier
idempotents
$p_j=m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathbf{Z}/m}\zeta^{-jt}u^{t}$, for
$\zeta=e^{2\pi i/m}$ and $j\in\mathbf{Z}/m$, are self-adjoint
idempotents of the corona.  Each $p_j$ is a polynomial in $u$, so
whatever commutes with $u$ commutes with every $p_j$: the represented
compressed copy centralizes each $p_j$, and the
$\Theta(L)$-conjugates of each $p_j$ commute pairwise because the
conjugated witnesses do.  Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-collapse}
therefore makes every $p_j$ commute with $\Theta(L)$, and Fourier
reconstruction expresses $u$ as a $\zeta$-weighted sum of the $p_j$,
so $u$ commutes with $\Theta(L)$ and
$\Theta([\gamma,k])=1$ for every $\gamma\in L$.
\end{proof}

In the quotient of the Kazhdan--Clifford group of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} by its central sign all lamps commute, and the
moved root lamp is itself an involutive witness.

\begin{theorem}[the commuting-lamp quotient]\label{thm:commutinglamp}
The quotient $W/\langle\zeta\rangle$ of the Kazhdan--Clifford group
by its central sign is sofic and is not MF\@.  More precisely, the
image of the
commutator $[v_1,tct^{-1}]$, the inverse of the marked commutator
$u$, in $W/\langle\zeta\rangle$ is nontrivial and lies in
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(W/\langle\zeta\rangle)$; that last
conclusion holds with $W$ replaced by the ambient affine--Clifford group of
any injective endomorphism $\alpha$ of a countable
property-\textup{(T)} group and any base element outside the image of
$\alpha$.
\leanverified{Sofic/CommutingLampQuotientSofic}{GroupApproximation.CommutingLampQuotientSofic.literalSignFreeQuotient_isSofic_and_not_isCDEOperatorMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/CommutingLampCollapse}{GroupApproximation.CommutingLampCollapse.commutingLampQuotient_collapse}
\leanverified{Sofic/CommutingLampCollapse}{GroupApproximation.CommutingLampCollapse.literalCommutingLampQuotient_collapse}
\leanverified{Sofic/CommutingLampCollapse}{GroupApproximation.CommutingLampCollapse.literalCommutingLampQuotient_not_isCDEOperatorMF}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $q\colon W\to W/\langle\zeta\rangle$ and
$k=q(tct^{-1})$, the moved root lamp.  Since $q(\zeta)=1$, any
two lamps commute in the quotient, so the $q(L)$-conjugation orbit of
$k$ commutes pairwise, where $L=q(\bar\Gamma)$ is the image of the
base copy; $k$ remains an involution, and the compressed copy
$q(t)Lq(t)^{-1}$ centralizes $k$ because the compressed base fixes the
moved site.  Property~\textup{(T)} passes to the image $L$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} places every commutator
$[q(\bar v_1),k]$ in the MF radical of the quotient.  The commutator
remains nontrivial in the sign quotient: in the signed finite-support model of the lamp
kernel, the commutator is the product of the two distinct lamp basis
elements at $\tau o$ and $j(\bar v_1)\tau o$, whose configuration
coordinate is nonzero, while every element of
$\langle\zeta\rangle$ has configuration coordinate zero.

The lamp kernel becomes abelian in the quotient:
$\ClLamp(X)/\langle\zeta\rangle$ is the elementary abelian group
$\bigoplus_X\Z/2$ under the same $V$-action, so
$W/\langle\zeta\rangle\cong\bigl(\bigoplus_X\Z/2\bigr)\rtimes V$.
That kernel is again locally finite with finite level orbits, so the
argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} gives soficity unchanged.
\end{proof}

Thus $W/\langle\zeta\rangle$ is a second finitely generated sofic group
that is not MF\@.  The same collapse reaches the finitely presented
group.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:signfree}]
Write $q\colon E\to E/\langle w\rangle$, $\bar d=q(d)$, and
$\bar L=q(\iota(\mathcal B))$.  The element $\bar d$ is an involution
because $c^2=1$, and $q(t)\bar Lq(t)^{-1}$ centralizes $\bar d$
because $t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1}$ commutes with $c$ in $E$.
Property~\textup{(T)} passes to $\bar L$, and $q(t)$ compresses it.
Conditions (W1) and (W2) of Definition~\ref{def:invwitness} therefore
hold, and the content is (W3).

The rotation letters commute with $d$, and $t$ carries each $v_i$ to
$v_i^2$, so $\iota(v_i^2)$ commutes with $d$ as well.  Hence
$\iota(g)\,d\,\iota(g)^{-1}$ depends only on the exponent vector of
the translation part of $g$ modulo $2$, which leaves eight conjugates,
indexed by $(\Z/2)^3$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} the
marked word is $w=[d,\iota(v_1)\,d\,\iota(v_1)^{-1}]$, the commutator
attached to the class of $v_1$.  The six rotation words
$x,x^2,z,yz,yx,y^2x$ carry that class onto the six remaining nonzero
classes, and each commutes with $d$; conjugating the displayed
identity by the corresponding $\iota(r)$ exhibits every nonzero parity
commutator as a conjugate of $w$, while the zero class contributes
$[d,d]=1$.  Centrality of $w$ puts all eight in $\langle w\rangle$,
and one further conjugation reduces an arbitrary pair of conjugates to
this case.  Thus $\bar d$ is an involutive compression witness for
$(\bar L,q(t))$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} places
$[q(\iota(v_1)),\bar d]$ in
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E/\langle w\rangle)$.

Nontriviality passes through the homomorphism $E\to W$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}: it carries $[\iota(v_1),d]$ to the
product of the lamp basis elements at the two distinct sites $\tau o$
and $j(\bar v_1)\tau o$, and carries $\langle w\rangle$ into
$\langle\zeta\rangle$, whose configuration coordinate is zero.  Hence
$[\iota(v_1),d]\notin\langle w\rangle$, its image in
$E/\langle w\rangle$ is nontrivial, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot} makes $E/\langle w\rangle$ non-MF\@.
Corollary~\ref{cor:pullback} identifies
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ with the $q$-preimage of
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E/\langle w\rangle)$, so $u$ belongs
to it.
\end{proof}

Thus $E/\langle w\rangle$ is a second finitely presented non-MF group.
\begin{corollary}[collapse computes the radical]\label{cor:collapsequot}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}, write
$q\colon H\to H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$ for the quotient map.  Then,
unconditionally,
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)
  \;=\;
  q^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}
  (H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s))\bigr),
\]
and if $H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$ is MF, then
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptCollapseRadicalReduction}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} with
Corollaries~\ref{cor:pullback} and~\ref{cor:exactradical}.
\end{proof}

In the second case $H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$ is the universal MF
quotient of $H$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}): the corona
representation theory of $H$ is computed exactly by the quotient.

\begin{theorem}[defect saturation]\label{thm:saturation}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every homomorphism $f$ from $H$ to a countable MF group maps
every element of $D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$ to the identity.
\item If $D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)=H$, then
$\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H$; every homomorphism from $H$
to a countable MF group is trivial; and if $H$ is nontrivial, then
$H$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptDefectSaturation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1)  By Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} and Lemma~\ref{lem:portable},
\[
 f(D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s))\le
 f(\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H))\le
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(f(H)).
\]
The subgroup $f(H)$ of the MF target is MF
(Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(1)), so its radical is trivial by
Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}.
(2)  The radical statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} with
$D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)=H$; triviality of every homomorphism follows
from (1); and a nontrivial group with full radical is not MF by
Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}.
\end{proof}

A group as in (2) admits no nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary
representation, no nontrivial MF quotient, and no nontrivial
homomorphism to any countable residually finite or amenable group.
Proving the analytic obstruction for a compression defect and then
arranging that the compression defect is the whole group develops a
suggestion of Fournier-Facio (personal communication, 2026; cf.\ the
Questions preamble of Section~\ref{sec:limits}).  Whether a
small-cancellation construction produces a finitely presented group
with $D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)=H$, or with a normal-Kazhdan defect equal
to the whole group, is open.


\subsection{The reduced group algebra of \texorpdfstring{$E$}{E}}

The stable finiteness in Theorem~\ref{thm:D} comes from the following
two standard facts.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:faithfultrace}
Let $G_1$ be a countable discrete group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The canonical tracial state $\tau$ on $\Cred(G_1)$ is
faithful.
\item Every unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ with a faithful tracial state
is stably finite: for every nonempty finite index set $I$, every isometry
in $M_I(A)$ is a unitary.  In particular this holds in $M_k(A)$ for every
$k\ge1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptFaithfulTraceAndStableFiniteness}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Realize $\Cred(G_1)\subseteq B(\ell^2G_1)$ as the norm closure
of the span of the left translation unitaries $\lambda_g$.  Let
$\rho_g$ be the right translation unitaries,
$(\rho_g\xi)(h)=\xi(hg)$.  Each $\rho_g$ commutes with each
$\lambda_{g'}$, hence with every element of $\Cred(G_1)$.  The
canonical trace is $\tau(x)=\langle x\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle$.  If
$\tau(x^*x)=0$ then $\lVert x\delta_e\rVert=0$, so $x\delta_e=0$,
and then
$x\delta_g=x\rho_{g^{-1}}\delta_e=\rho_{g^{-1}}x\delta_e=0$ for
every $g\in G_1$.  The vectors $\delta_g$ are total in
$\ell^2G_1$, so $x=0$.

(2) On $M_k(A)$ the functional
$\tau_k\bigl([x_{ij}]\bigr)=\tfrac1k\sum_i\tau(x_{ii})$ is a state
with the trace property
$\tau_k(xy)=\tfrac1k\sum_{i,j}\tau(x_{ij}y_{ji})=\tau_k(yx)$, and it
is faithful: the $i$-th diagonal entry of $x^*x$ is
$\sum_jx_{ji}^*x_{ji}$, so $\tau_k(x^*x)=0$ forces
$\tau(x_{ji}^*x_{ji})=0$, hence $x_{ji}=0$, for all $i,j$.  If
$v\in M_k(A)$ satisfies $v^*v=1$, then $y:=1-vv^*$ is a projection
with
$\tau_k(y)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(vv^*)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(v^*v)=0$; since
$y=y^*y$, faithfulness gives $y=0$, i.e.\ $vv^*=1$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{A sofic non-MF group}

The same quotient that proves $w\ne1$ also provides the sofic non-MF group of
Theorem~\ref{thm:E}.


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}]
Write $T=\varinjlim(\bar\Gamma,\bar\alpha)$, so
$V=T\rtimes\mathbb Z$, and let $B\le V$ be the level-zero copy of
$\bar\Gamma$.  The image of $\bar\alpha$ has finite index (at most eight,
by the parity classes of the translation lattice).  Thus every telescope
level $\bar\Gamma_n\le T$ is commensurable with $B$.  The stable letter
conjugates $B$ onto $\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$, so all of $V$ commensurates
$B$.  Orbit--stabilizer now shows that every $\bar\Gamma_n$-orbit in
$X=V/B$ is finite.

Consider a finite subset of $\ClLamp(X)\rtimes T$.  Its $T$-coordinates
lie in one level $\bar\Gamma_n$.  Each of its lamp coordinates has finite
$\bar\Gamma_n$-orbit: this holds for the central sign and for each site
lamp, hence for every Clifford word.  The finitely many resulting orbits
generate a finite $\bar\Gamma_n$-invariant subgroup $L\le\ClLamp(X)$,
because $\ClLamp(X)$ is locally finite.  Our original finite set therefore
lies in $L\rtimes\bar\Gamma_n$, which is sofic (finite-by-sofic).  Soficity
is local over finite subsets, so $\ClLamp(X)\rtimes T$ is sofic.

Reassociating the semidirect products gives
\[
 W=\ClLamp(X)\rtimes(T\rtimes\mathbb Z)
   \cong (\ClLamp(X)\rtimes T)\rtimes\mathbb Z.
\]
The final group is sofic because a split extension of a sofic group by
$\Z$ is sofic.  Truncate the integer levels to $\Z/N$ and let the
shift act cyclically.  Induce the kernel's sofic approximations over
this finite level set.  The cocycle identity that makes the
finite-quotient case exact now fails only on the wrap-around boundary.
For a window shifted by $k$, that boundary occupies at most $2|k|/N$
of the levels, which vanishes as $N$ grows.
This is the $\Z$-extension case of the Elek--Szab\'o amenable-extension
theorem \cite{ElekSzabo}.  Here
$\bar\Gamma$ is sofic: its six generators lie in $\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$,
the resulting integral subgroup is residually finite through its
congruence quotients, and it is isomorphic to $\bar\Gamma$.

The map $E\to W$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} is surjective: its image
contains $V$, the root lamp $c_o$, and hence every lamp by transitivity of
the $V$-action on $X$.  Since $E$ is generated by
  $v_1,x,y,z,t,c$ (by the Tietze calculation above), their six
images generate $W$.  The map sends
$w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ to the
nontrivial sign $\zeta$; functoriality (Lemma~\ref{lem:portable}) makes
$\zeta\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(W)$.  Hence $W$ is not MF.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Failure of extension permanence.}
MF groups are not closed under extensions.  More precisely, $W$ fits into
an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow\ClLamp(X)\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow V
 \longrightarrow1
\]
whose kernel and quotient are MF, while $W$ is not.
The concrete lamp kernel is locally finite, LEF, sofic, and MF, while the
total witness is finitely generated and non-MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralWitnessConsequences}{GroupApproximation.LiteralWitnessConsequences.literalWitness_locallyFiniteKernel_nonMF}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(3), the Clifford lamp group is locally
finite, hence MF\@.  The group $V$ identifies with the subgroup of
$\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ generated by $\bar\Gamma$ and $D$: the telescope
levels are $D^{-n}\bar\Gamma D^{n}$, and the determinant gives the
$\mathbb Z$-coordinate.  It is residually finite.  At every odd
modulus the determinant $8$ of the dilation is invertible, so the
congruence reduction of the integral affine model descends to
conjugation by the dilation and extends over the telescope and its
shift.  These reductions separate any nontrivial element; compare
Mal'cev's theorem \cite{Malcev} for finitely generated linear groups.  Thus $V$ is MF by Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(2),
whereas $W$ is not MF by the preceding construction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Extensions by $\Z$.}
Composing the two
canonical projections gives a surjection $W\to\Z$ whose kernel is
$\ClLamp(X)\rtimes T$, and this kernel is LEF, hence MF: every finite
subset lies in $L\rtimes\bar\Gamma_n$ for a finite invariant lamp
subgroup $L$ and one telescope level, and that group is residually
finite because the level acts on the finite group $L$ through a finite
quotient.  Thus $W$ is an extension of an LEF group by $\Z$ and is not
MF, while soficity is preserved by every extension with amenable
quotient, as in Elek--Szab\'o \cite{ElekSzabo}; that permanence theorem is
proved here from the invariant-mean definition of amenability, and is not
quoted.
\leanverified{Sofic/LEFMarkedCompression}{GroupApproximation.LiteralLEFExtension.literalWitness_LEFKernel_integerExtension_nonMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/SoficByAmenablePermanence}{GroupApproximation.SoficByAmenablePermanence.isSofic_of_isSofic_ker_of_isAmenable}

\paragraph{An exact stably finite non-MF algebra.}
The same witness gives a concrete separable exact example:
$\Cred(W)$ is exact, has a faithful tracial state, is stably finite, and is
not MF\@.  Indeed, the locally finite lamp kernel is amenable and hence exact,
while the linear group $V\leq\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ is exact
\cite{GHW}.  Exact groups are closed under extensions
\cite{KWPermanence}, so $W$ is exact; for discrete groups this is equivalent
to exactness of the reduced group algebra \cite{KWExact}.  The canonical trace
and stable finiteness follow as in Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace}.  Finally, an
MF embedding of $\Cred(W)$ would restrict to a faithful norm-corona model of
the canonical group unitaries, contradicting that $W$ is not MF\@.

\paragraph{A simple sofic envelope.}
There is a countable simple sofic group $S_{\mathrm{simp}}$ with
\[
 \operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(S_{\mathrm{simp}})=S_{\mathrm{simp}}.
\]
Equivalently, every homomorphism from $S_{\mathrm{simp}}$ to an MF group is
trivial.

\begin{proof}
By Elek--Szab\'o, every countable sofic group embeds in a countable simple
sofic group \cite[Theorem~1]{ElekSzaboHyper}.  Embed $W$ in such a group.  Functoriality
of the MF radical sends $\zeta$ to a nontrivial element of the
MF radical of the simple group.  That radical is normal, so it is the whole
group.  Every homomorphism to an MF group maps the MF radical to $1$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Marked-limit closure}

The explicit clopen cylinder constructed in the next subsection is an
instance of a general phenomenon: at every fixed number of marked
generators, the MF property is closed under limits of marked groups,
and its failure always has a finite certificate.

\begin{theorem}[the MF locus is closed]\label{thm:markedclosed}
Fix $k\ge1$ and consider the space of $k$-marked groups --- quotients
of the free group $F_k$, topologized so that a basic clopen set fixes
the truth of finitely many equations among words in the generators.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The marked groups with MF quotient form a closed set.
\item Equivalently, the non-MF marked groups form an open set.
\item Every non-MF $k$-marked group admits a finite-radius
certificate: there is a radius $R$ such that every $k$-marked group
whose relations agree with it on the reduced words of length at most
$R$ is non-MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.isClosed_operatorMFLocus}
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.isOpen_compl_operatorMFLocus}
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.exists_wordBall_cylinder_subset_compl_operatorMFLocus}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A failure of MF for the quotient is a failure of normalized local
approximation, and such a failure is witnessed on one finite subset of
the quotient.  Expressing the finitely many multiplications and
inequalities of that subset by words in the marked generators produces
finitely many relation constraints; every marked group agreeing on
those constraints transports the failed local-model problem verbatim,
so an entire basic clopen neighborhood fails MF\@.  Enclosing the
constraint words in a reduced-word ball gives the radius form.
\end{proof}

Thus failure of MF is always certified by one finite ball of the
marked Cayley diagram.  The next subsection computes such a
certificate explicitly for $E$.

\subsection{A finite universal Horn obstruction}

Theorem~\ref{thm:A} can be read as one first-order fact about all MF
groups at once: a finite quasi-identity, that is, a single universal
implication between finitely many equations (a universal Horn
sentence).  Write $R$ for the forty-one relators of \eqref{eq:E},
regarded as words in eight variables, and $w$ for the marked word in those
variables.

\begin{proposition}[finite quasi-identity]\label{prop:horn}
Every MF group $G$ satisfies the finite universal Horn sentence
\[
 \forall x_1,\dots,x_8\colon\quad
 \bigwedge_{r\in R}\,r(x_1,\dots,x_8)=1
 \;\Longrightarrow\; w(x_1,\dots,x_8)=1 ,
\]
while the canonical generating tuple of $E$ satisfies every relator in $R$
and has $w\ne1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralUniversalHorn}{GroupApproximation.LiteralUniversalHorn.manuscriptLiteralUniversalHorn}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A tuple satisfying the relators induces a homomorphism $E\to G$ sending
the marked word of $E$ to $w(x_1,\dots,x_8)$.  If $G$ is MF, that image
is trivial: $w\in\operatorname{Rad}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, and the radical is functorial
(Lemma~\ref{lem:portable}).
The canonical tuple of $E$ satisfies $R$ by definition, and its marked
word is nontrivial by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[a basic clopen set of non-MF marked groups]\label{cor:cylinder}
In the space of eight-generator marked groups --- quotients of the free
group $F_8$, topologized so that a basic clopen set fixes the truth of
finitely many equations among words in the generators --- the marked groups
satisfying all relators of $R$ and $w\ne1$ form a nonempty clopen set
consisting entirely of non-MF groups.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralMarkedCylinder}{GroupApproximation.LiteralMarkedCylinder.literal_nonempty_clopen_nonMF_cylinder}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Both conditions constrain finitely many values of the marked
multiplication, so the set is clopen; it contains the marked group $E$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:horn} excludes every MF
point.
\end{proof}

Thus the obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is finite, local, and
locally constant on a basic clopen neighborhood in the marked-group
topology.

\subsection{Undecidability of MF recognition}

Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(1) and Theorem~\ref{thm:A} make MF a Markov
property of finitely presented groups: the trivial group is MF, and by
subgroup heredity the finitely presented group $E$ embeds in no MF
group.  The Adian--Rabin theorem \cite{Rabin58} therefore applies.

\begin{corollary}[undecidability]\label{cor:undecidable}
There is no algorithm that decides, from a finite presentation,
whether the presented group is MF\@.  By the effective form of the
Adian--Rabin construction \cite{Rabin58}, the set of finite
presentations of non-MF groups is not even recursively enumerable.
\end{corollary}

The Adian--Rabin construction \cite{Rabin58} is quoted here and used
nowhere else.  It gives a computable map taking
an instance $(P,w)$ of the word problem of a fixed finitely presented
group with undecidable word problem to a finite presentation whose
group is trivial --- hence MF --- when $w=1$, and contains $E$ ---
hence non-MF, by subgroup heredity --- when $w\ne1$.  The instances
with $w=1$ form a recursively enumerable set which, the word problem
being undecidable, is not recursive; hence the instances with $w\ne1$
form a set that is \emph{not} recursively enumerable.  An enumeration
of the non-MF presentations would enumerate that set, making the word
problem decidable.  Only undecidability of the word problem is used
here, not its completeness under many-one reduction: the conclusion
comes from taking the preimage, under the computable Adian--Rabin
reduction, of a set that is not recursively enumerable.  The two
group-theoretic inputs to that argument are machine-checked ---
subgroup heredity of MF and the finitely presented non-MF group ---
as is the implication itself: from any computable
Adian--Rabin reduction, undecidability and the failure of recursive
enumerability on the negative side follow formally.
\leanverified{Computability/MarkovMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.MarkovMFConsequences.operatorMF_subgroup_hereditary}
\leanverified{Computability/MarkovMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.MarkovMFConsequences.exists_finitelyPresented_nonOperatorMF}
\leanverified{Computability/MarkovMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.MarkovMFConsequences.operatorMF_recognition_undecidable}
Theorem~\ref{thm:markedclosed} certifies single non-MF groups by
finite balls; the corollary says no algorithm classifies the positive
and negative cases from presentations.

The phenomenon also has infinite multiplicity, in two senses that need
no new analysis.  The groups $E\times\mathbb Z^{k}$ for $k\ge0$ are
finitely presented, pairwise nonisomorphic --- their abelianizations
have distinct torsion-free ranks, equivalently distinct numbers of
homomorphisms to $\mathbb Z/2$ --- and non-MF, each containing $E$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(1)).
\leanverified{Sofic/ProductMultiplicity}{GroupApproximation.ProductMultiplicity.manuscriptInfiniteMultiplicity}
Beyond finite presentation, pairing $E$
with a continuum of pairwise nonisomorphic finitely generated
groups yields $2^{\aleph_0}$ pairwise
nonisomorphic finitely generated non-MF groups: each product
$E\times N$ is non-MF, and a fixed countable group $X$ satisfies
$X\cong E\times N$ for at most countably many of the continuum many
isomorphism types $N$ --- each such $N$ embeds in $X$ as a finitely
generated subgroup, a countable group has only countably many finitely
generated subgroups, and the types $N$ are pairwise nonisomorphic.  A
map with countable fibres from a continuum-sized family has
continuum-sized image.  The counting step is needed because the
products can fail to be pairwise nonisomorphic even when the factors
are.  The deduction uses only that the $N$ are finitely generated, and at
that strength the input is no longer quoted: a continuum of pairwise
nonisomorphic finitely generated groups is constructed below, as a
lamplighter over coset spaces of the free group rather than as a subdirect
product of alternating groups, so the conclusion carries no literature
input.  Neumann's sharper two-generator form is quoted, not reproved
\cite{Neumann37}, and is not needed here.
\leanverified{Sofic/ContinuumMultiplicity}{GroupApproximation.ContinuumMultiplicity.manuscriptContinuumMultiplicity}
\leanverified{Monsters/NeumannContinuum}{GroupApproximation.NeumannContinuum.manuscriptContinuumMultiplicityUnconditional}

\section{Limitations of the operator-norm method}\label{sec:limits}

The proof is specific to the operator norm.  If multiplication
is controlled only in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, the induced adjoint
operators on the Hilbert--Schmidt spaces need not be close in operator norm.
Both transport proofs require exactly that operator-norm control: the
ultraproduct proof needs it to obtain an honest representation on the
ultraproduct, and the finite-stage proof of
Section~\ref{app:finite-stage} needs it for the spectral estimates.
Normalized Hilbert--Schmidt control therefore does not give the
transport used here.  The failure is exact and dimension-free: against
the operator-norm estimate $\opnorm{\Ad(U)-\Ad(V)}\le2\opnorm{U-V}$,
there are unitaries of arbitrarily small normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
distance whose adjoints stay $2$ apart in operator norm --- the same
constant, in the opposite direction.
\leanverified{Sofic/HilbertSchmidtAdjointGap}{GroupApproximation.HilbertSchmidtAdjointGap.exists_hsClose_adjointFar}

Conversely, amplification by an identity block separates the two
normalizations exactly.  Padding a model with an identity
block preserves every operator-norm quantity --- the multiplicative
defects and the pairwise separations are unchanged --- while the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distances decay with the padding ratio:
below any prescribed bound, every operator-norm local model can be
replaced by one all of whose pairwise Hilbert--Schmidt distances are
smaller than the bound.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormTraceGap}{GroupApproximation.NormModel.exists_hs_collapse}
Hilbert--Schmidt invisibility of an element alone therefore can never
prove a group non-MF: an obstruction must produce an invariant
substructure with its own scale, renormalized on its own range so that
the normalization is unchanged by amplification, rather than reading a
normalized trace off the ambient blocks.  Macroscopic relative rank is
\emph{not} what is required, and none of the constructions here has
it: padding leaves the averaging corner $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n$ untouched
while $d_n$ grows, so its relative rank $r_n/d_n$ may tend to zero,
and the argument runs on the corner's own normalized trace.  The
moving spectral subspace and the rank-calibrated collapse renormalize
on their own range and on the displacement scale $k_n$ in the same
way.

This is a limitation of the method, not a tracial nonapproximation
theorem for $E$.  Alekseev--Thom ask whether commutants arising in sofic
approximations of Kazhdan groups can be recovered from finite-dimensional
coordinate subalgebras \cite[Open Problem~6.2]{AlekseevThom}.  Such a
coordinate description is separate from the operator-norm theorem proved
here.

Finite dimensionality of the coordinates is essential in a second,
sharper sense: it cannot be replaced by stable finiteness of the
coordinate algebras together with faithful traces.  In a general
faithfully traced unital $C^*$-algebra the transport argument would
act on the trace Hilbert space by the left and right regular actions,
and the von Neumann algebra these two actions generate can fail every
finiteness property --- for an infinite-dimensional
$\mathrm{II}_1$ factor in standard form it is all bounded operators
on the trace space.  Constant coordinates $A_n=\Cred(E)$ give a
counterexample to any such generalization: the left regular representation embeds
$E$ exactly and injectively into the corresponding corona, the
element $c$ centralizes the Kazhdan base there, and conjugation by
$t$ produces $tct^{-1}$, whose commutator with the base is the element
$u$ with $u^2=w\ne1$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.  A faithfully-traced
coordinate transport theorem would make that commutator trivial in the
corona; no such theorem can hold.
\leanverified{Sofic/FaithfullyTracedCoordinateNoGo}{GroupApproximation.FaithfullyTracedCoordinates.manuscriptFaithfullyTracedCoordinateNoGo}
The refuted statement is the
transport theorem of Section~\ref{sec:transport} with its matrix
coordinates replaced by finite faithfully traced $C^*$-coordinates and
its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm by the trace $2$-norm, every other
hypothesis copied verbatim.

remains for a torsion-free example is group-theoretic: a torsion-free

\item Neither soficity nor hyperlinearity of $E$ is decided by the

\item Does MF imply hyperlinearity?  A faithful embedding into a norm

\item By Proposition~\ref{prop:sounditeration}, every family of

\section{The maximal group \texorpdfstring{$C^*$}{C*}-algebra}\label{app:maxcstar}

The maximal group $C^*$-algebra completes the group ring over all
unitary representations at once, and ``all'' is a class rather than a
set.  The construction below is relative to a fixed set-theoretic
universe $\mathfrak U$, which makes the indexing family a set; the
result is the usual maximal group $C^*$-algebra.  For a
$\mathfrak U$-small
group $H$, define $C^{*}_{\max,\mathfrak U}(H)$ to be the closed
$*$-subalgebra generated by the diagonal copy of $H$ in the bounded product
of all its nontrivial unitary representations on $\mathfrak U$-small unital
$C^*$-algebras.  We suppress the
subscript $\mathfrak U$ and write $u_h$ for the canonical unitary
associated with $h\in H$.

\begin{proposition}[universe-relative maximal group algebra]
\label{prop:maximal-cstar}
The canonical map $h\mapsto u_h$ is injective.  For every
$\mathfrak U$-small unital $C^*$-algebra $B$ and every homomorphism
$\rho\colon H\to\U(B)$, there is a unique unital $*$-homomorphism
$C^{*}_{\max,\mathfrak U}(H)\to B$ mapping $u_h$ to $\rho(h)$ for
every $h\in H$.
\leanverified{Analysis/MaximalGroupCStar}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptUniverseRelativeMaximalGroupCStar}
\end{proposition}

For a nontrivial target, projection to its coordinate gives the extension;
for a trivial target, the map is unique.  Density of the generated
$*$-subalgebra gives uniqueness, while the left-regular coordinate proves
injectivity.

The maximal and reduced algebras separate already at the level of
finiteness, by an algebraic mechanism:

\begin{proposition}[proper compression forbids finiteness]\label{prop:proper-isometry}
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra (a unital star ring suffices)
containing a projection $p$ and a unitary $u$ such that the conjugate
$upu^{*}$ satisfies $p\,(upu^{*})=(upu^{*})\,p=p$, equivalently
$p\le upu^{*}$, and $upu^{*}\ne p$.  Then $A$
contains an isometry that is not a unitary; consequently $A$ is not
stably finite and has no faithful tracial state.
\leanverified{Analysis/ProperIsometryFromCompression}{GroupApproximation.ProperProjectionCompression.isometry_not_isUnit}
\leanverified{Analysis/ProperIsometryFromCompression}{GroupApproximation.ProperProjectionCompression.not_isStablyFiniteRing}
\leanverified{Analysis/ProperIsometryFromCompression}{GroupApproximation.ProperProjectionCompression.no_faithfulTracialState}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[the maximal algebra under strict Kazhdan compression]
\label{rem:maxinfinite}
For a group with a strict Kazhdan compression
$t\Gamma t^{-1}\subsetneq\Gamma$ --- the base of $E$ is the running
example --- the spectral projection of the averaged generator of the
Kazhdan pair, built as in Section~\ref{sec:transport}, exists in the
maximal algebra, conjugation by $u_t$ dominates it, and the domination is
strict: at the quasi-regular coordinate $\ell^2(G/t\Gamma t^{-1})$ the
point mass at the base coset is fixed by the compressed subgroup and
moved by every element outside it, so the two projections differ
there.
\leanverified{Analysis/QuasiRegularWitness}{GroupApproximation.QuasiRegularWitness.baseVector_apply_base}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-isometry} the maximal algebra of such
a group is then not stably finite, has no faithful trace, and in
particular is neither residually finite-dimensional nor MF\@.  The
assembly runs through the spectral projection, in the maximal algebra,
of the averaged generator built from the Kazhdan pair: the spectral
gap places the projection, the quasi-regular coordinate separates it
from its conjugate, and Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-isometry}
applies verbatim.  The contrast with Theorem~\ref{thm:D} is the point of
this remark: for the reduced algebra of $E$ the canonical trace is
faithful and the algebra is stably finite, so its failure of MF is not
a failure of finiteness; for the maximal algebra, infiniteness is
forced by the strict compression before any MF considerations arise.
\end{remark}
