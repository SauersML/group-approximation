\title{One-Sided Kazhdan Compression and the Failure of MF Approximation}
\author{\paperauthor}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 46L05; Secondary 20C07, 16S50, 20G35}
\keywords{MF groups, norm matrix coronas, MF radicals, property~(T), asymptotic representations, Leavitt algebra}

\begin{abstract}
Not every countable group is MF in the operator-norm approximation sense.
We identify an obstruction arising when conjugation sends a
property~\textup{(T)} subgroup into itself.  The resulting normal subgroup is
invisible to finite-dimensional representations, and every normal
property~\textup{(T)} subgroup that it contains is invisible to MF
representations.  When these subgroups normally generate the group, every MF
representation is trivial.  Applying this criterion gives a simple
property~\textup{(T)} group that is not MF.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A countable group is \emph{MF} if it embeds in the unitary group of a norm
matrix corona
%<webmath object="Qd" when="\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}" title="the norm matrix corona used in the definition of MF">
% \(\mathbf d=(d_1,d_2,d_3,\ldots)\) records the matrix size used at each
% stage of the approximation.
% The quotient contains bounded sequences of matrices of those sizes.  It
% treats two sequences as the same when their [[operator norm|operator-norm]] difference tends
% to zero.  Embedding a [[group]] here means representing every group element by
% such a matrix sequence without losing the identity of any element.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="Mdn" when="M_{d_n}(\C)" title="the matrices in the nth finite model">
% This is the algebra of all \(d_n\)-by-\(d_n\) matrices with complex entries.
% The integer \(d_n\) may increase with \(n\), so later models may use larger
% matrices.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="∏n" when="\prod_nM_{d_n}(\C)" title="one bounded matrix sequence across all finite models">
% An element of this product chooses one matrix from each \(M_{d_n}(\C)\),
% with a single bound on all their operator norms.  Addition and multiplication
% are performed coordinate by coordinate.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="⨁n" when="\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C)" title="the matrix sequences whose size tends to zero">
% This direct sum contains exactly the sequences whose operator norms tend to
% zero.  Dividing by it makes every uniformly disappearing error equal to zero
% in the norm matrix corona.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="C" when="M_{d_n}(\C)" title="complex entries in the finite matrices">
% \(\C\) denotes the complex numbers, which include both real numbers and
% imaginary numbers.  Unitary matrices naturally act on vector spaces over
% \(\C\).
%</webmath>
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}
 =\prod_nM_{d_n}(\C)\big/\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C).
\]
Here
%<webmath object="⨁n" when="\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C)" title="the sequences discarded by the quotient">
% The displayed set says exactly what belongs to this direct sum: a matrix
% sequence \((x_n)\) is discarded when the operator norm of \(x_n\) tends to
% zero.
%</webmath>
\[
 \bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C)
 =\{(x_n)\in\prod_nM_{d_n}(\C):\opnorm{x_n}\longrightarrow0\}
\]
is the $c_0$-direct sum.  Equivalently, an MF group admits
finite-dimensional unitary models whose multiplicative defects tend to zero
in operator norm and which asymptotically separate its nonidentity elements:
for each $g\ne1$, the quantity
$\limsup_n\opnorm{V_n(g)-1}$ is positive, with a lower bound that may depend
on $g$.
Throughout, \emph{MF group} means this operator-norm notion of
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt and Korchagin~\cite{CDE,Korchagin}.

For a countable group $G$, define its MF radical by
%<webmath object="RadMF" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)" title="the elements of G invisible to every finite-matrix limit">
% This is a [[subgroup]] of G.  An element belongs to it when every [[homomorphism]]
% from G into every [[norm matrix corona]] sends that element to the [[identity]].
% Thus the MF radical records precisely what operator-norm finite-matrix
% representations can never distinguish from doing nothing.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="⋂d" when="\bigcap_{\mathbf d}" title="requiring the condition for every choice of matrix sizes">
% The bold d runs through all sequences d_1,d_2,....  An element survives this
% intersection only when no possible choice of finite matrix sizes can detect
% it.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="⋂π:G→U(Qd)" when="\bigcap_{\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})}" title="requiring the condition for every representation of G in that corona">
% For the chosen sizes, pi runs through all multiplication-preserving maps from
% G to the unitary elements of the norm matrix corona.  The intersection keeps
% only the group elements erased by every one of those maps.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="ker" when="\bigcap_{\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})}\ker\pi" title="the elements that one representation sends to the identity">
% The [[kernel]] of pi is the set of elements of G represented by the [[identity]].
% Intersecting these kernels over every pi and every matrix-size sequence gives
% the MF radical.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)
 =\bigcap_{\mathbf d}
   \bigcap_{\pi\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})}\ker\pi.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-residual-calculus} below proves that $G$ is MF
precisely when $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ is trivial.  If
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=G$, every homomorphism from $G$ to an MF group is
trivial.

More generally, for $N\trianglelefteq G$ define its \emph{MF-kernel closure}
by
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)
 =\bigcap\{\ker f:N\le\ker f,\ f\colon G\to M,\ M\text{ MF}\}.
\]
The image of a corona homomorphism from $G$ is countable and is itself MF;
conversely, every MF target embeds in a norm matrix corona.  Thus
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(1)$, and
$G/N$ is MF precisely when
$\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)=N$.
This is a closure operation on the normal subgroups of one fixed group; it is
unrelated to topological closure of classes of marked groups.

\begin{proposition}[basic properties of the MF radical]\label{prop:mf-residual-calculus}
For every countable group $G$, the subgroup $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ is fully
invariant, the quotient $G/\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ is MF, and
\[
 G/N\text{ is MF}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^G(N)=N
 \qquad(N\trianglelefteq G).
\]
In particular, $G$ is MF if and only if its MF radical is trivial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An intersection of kernels is normal.  If $\alpha$ is an endomorphism of
$G$, $x\in\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$, and $\pi$ is a corona homomorphism of $G$,
then $\pi\circ\alpha$ kills $x$.  Hence $\pi(\alpha(x))=1$ for every $\pi$,
which proves full invariance.

Put $R=\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$ and enumerate the nonidentity elements of
$G/R$ as $(x_j)_{j\ge1}$, and enumerate the pairs in $(G/R)^2$.  For each
$x_j$, the definition of $R$ supplies a corona homomorphism whose value at
$x_j$ has positive distance from the identity.  Every such homomorphism
kills $R$ and therefore descends to $G/R$.  Choose coordinate unitary lifts,
using polar correction as in Lemma~\ref{lem:central-corona-corner}.  At stage
$n$, take a direct
sum of one coordinate from each of the first $n$ models.  Choose each
coordinate far enough out that the first $n$ multiplication defects are at
most $1/n$ and the designated value at $x_j$ retains at least half of its
corona-norm separation.  Such coordinates exist arbitrarily far out: the
defects converge to zero, while the quotient norm is a limsup.  The resulting
operator-norm asymptotic representation detects every $x_j$, so its corona
homomorphism is faithful on $G/R$.  Thus $G/R$ is MF.

Applied to $G/N$, the same argument says that $G/N$ is MF exactly when the
intersection of the kernels of all MF-target maps killing $N$ is $N$, which
is the asserted closure criterion.
\end{proof}

For a subgroup $L\le G$, put
%<webmath object="CompG" when="\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)" title="the elements of G that send L back inside itself">
% Comp_G(L) consists of those u in G for which conjugation sends every element
% of L to another element of L.  The image uLu^{-1} may be a proper smaller
% copy of L; equality is not required.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="L" when="uLu^{-1}\le L" title="the subgroup carried into itself by conjugation">
% Here L is the subgroup to which property (T) will be applied.  The inclusion
% means that u ell u^{-1} belongs to L for every ell in L, even if conjugation
% by u does not reach every element of L.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}_G(L)=\{u\in G:uLu^{-1}\le L\}
\]
and define its \emph{compression--centralizer defect} by
%<webmath object="DG" when="\mathfrak D_G(L)" title="the normal subgroup generated by all compression commutators">
% Choose u that sends L into itself, c that commutes with every element of L,
% and ell in L.  Form [ucu^{-1},ell], namely
% ucu^{-1} ell u c^{-1}u^{-1}ell^{-1}.  D_G(L) is the smallest normal subgroup
% of G containing every element obtained this way.  The theorem below proves
% that certain rigid normal subgroups lying here vanish in every MF target.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsic-defect}
 \mathfrak D_G(L)
 =\normal{
   [ucu^{-1},\ell]:
   u\in\operatorname{Comp}_G(L),\ c\in C_G(L),\ \ell\in L
 }_G.
\end{equation}
\begin{mainthm}[one-sided compression criterion]
\label{thm:compression-criterion}
Let $G$ be countable and let $L\le G$ have property~\textup{(T)}.  If
$K\trianglelefteq G$ has property~\textup{(T)} and
$K\le\mathfrak D_G(L)$, then
%<webmath object="K" when="K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)" title="the normal property-(T) subgroup forced to disappear in every MF target">
% K is the normal subgroup assumed above to have property (T) and to lie in
% the compression defect.  This conclusion says that every [[homomorphism]] from
% G into every [[norm matrix corona]] sends every element of K to the [[identity]].
%</webmath>
\[
 K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G).
\]
In particular, a nontrivial such $K$ makes $G$ non-MF; if $G$ has
property~\textup{(T)} and $\mathfrak D_G(L)=G$, then
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=G$.
\end{mainthm}

Corollary~\ref{cor:defect-hs} puts $\mathfrak D_G(L)$ in the kernel detected
by normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  If a corona representation were
nontrivial on $K$, normality of $K$ would make its Kazhdan projection commute
with the image of $G$.  The complementary corner would then contain no
$K$-fixed vectors.  A Kazhdan inequality gives an element of $K$ whose
Hilbert--Schmidt distance from the identity is bounded below there, contrary
to the first conclusion.  Independently, Theorem~\ref{thm:commutant} shows
that every finite-dimensional linear representation over every field kills
$\mathfrak D_G(L)$.

Whether every countable group is MF was asked in the group-MF literature and
remained open~\cite{CDE,Korchagin,Thom,Shulman}.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion} to the binary Leavitt construction
below answers the question.

%<webmath object="LF2" when="L_{\F_2}(1,2)" title="the binary Leavitt algebra used to build the group">
% This [[Leavitt algebra|algebra]] is generated by four symbols s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1 subject to the
% two displayed families of equations.  Those equations let s_0 and s_1 put
% two copies of a free module inside one copy, while t_0 and t_1 recover the
% two inputs.  The proof uses this exact self-copying algebra to construct the
% matrix tau that sends the subgroup L back inside itself.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="F2" when="L_{\F_2}(1,2)" title="the two-element coefficient field">
% F_2 has only the numbers 0 and 1, and 1+1=0.  Its only nonzero scalar is 1.
% Later, that fact rules out a nonidentity scalar matrix in the center of H,
% which is needed to prove that H is simple.
%</webmath>
Let $L_{\F_2}(1,2)$ denote the binary Leavitt algebra.  It is the unital
$\F_2$-algebra generated by $s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1$ subject to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leavitt}
 t_i s_j=\delta_{ij},
 \qquad s_0t_0+s_1t_1=1.
\end{equation}
%<webmath object="eij" when="e_{ij}(a)" title="an elementary matrix carrying the coefficient a">
% The matrix e_ij(a) has 1 on every diagonal position, the ring element a in
% row i and column j, and 0 in every other off-diagonal position.  Products
% and commutators of these matrices encode addition and multiplication in R.
%</webmath>
For a unital ring $R$, write $e_{ij}(a)=1+aE_{ij}$ and let
$\EL_n(R)$ be the subgroup of $\GL_n(R)$ generated by the elements
$e_{ij}(a)$, $i\ne j$.

\begin{mainthm}[the binary Leavitt group]\label{thm:headline}
%<webmath object="EL12" when="H=\EL_{12}(R)" title="the elementary-matrix group H studied in the theorem">
% EL_12(R) is generated by 12-by-12 elementary matrices with entries from the
% binary Leavitt algebra R.  The theorem proves that this group H is nontrivial
% and simple, has property (T), and has no nontrivial homomorphism to any MF
% group.
%</webmath>
Put $R=L_{\F_2}(1,2)$ and $H=\EL_{12}(R)$.  Then $H$ is nontrivial, simple,
has property~\textup{(T)}, and
%<webmath object="RadMF" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)" title="every element of H is invisible to every MF-target representation">
% The MF radical of H consists of the elements sent to the identity by every
% homomorphism from H into every norm matrix corona.  Equality with H says
% that all elements are sent to the identity, so every such homomorphism is
% trivial and H cannot itself be MF.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H.
\]
Equivalently, every homomorphism from $H$ to an MF group is trivial.
In particular, $H$ is non-MF.
\end{mainthm}

%<webmath object="τ" when="\tau L\tau^{-1}\le L" title="the explicit matrix that sends L into a copy inside L">
% Tau is a particular invertible 12-by-12 elementary matrix constructed later.
% Conjugating every element of L by tau gives another element of L.  This is
% the one-sided inclusion used by the property-(T) argument.
%</webmath>
Inside $H$, an explicit element $\tau$ satisfies $\tau L\tau^{-1}\le L$ for
the upper-left subgroup $L\cong\EL_3(R)$.  The element $c=e_{34}(1)$
centralizes $L$, and for $\ell=e_{12}(1)$ the corresponding commutator is
%<webmath object="d" when="d=e_{02}(s_1t_1)" title="the concrete nonidentity commutator inside H">
% Here d is the result of comparing tau c tau^{-1} ell with
% ell tau c tau^{-1}.  The calculation gives an elementary matrix whose
% off-diagonal entry is s_1t_1.  The Leavitt relations show that this entry is
% nonzero, so d is not the identity.  The unit sandwich
% t_1(s_1t_1)s_1=1 and the elementary commutator relations show directly that
% d normally generates H.
%</webmath>
\[
d=e_{02}(s_1t_1).
\]
The relation $t_1(s_1t_1)s_1=1$ and the elementary commutator relations show
directly that $d$ normally generates $H$.  Since
$d\in\mathfrak D_H(L)$ and $\mathfrak D_H(L)$ is normal, it follows that
$\mathfrak D_H(L)=H$.  Therefore
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion} gives
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H$.

If $\rho\colon G\to\GL(V)$ is a homomorphism and $V$ is finite-dimensional,
conjugation by $\rho(u)$ maps the commutant of $\rho(L)$ injectively into
itself, hence bijectively.  Thus $\rho$ kills $\mathfrak D_G(L)$.
Consequently, $\mathfrak D_G(L)=1$ if $G$ has a faithful finite-dimensional
linear representation.  The same conclusion holds if $G$ is residually
finite, because every nonidentity element then survives in a finite
permutation representation.  If $G$ is amenable, its
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup $L$ is finite~\cite{BHV}; then
$uLu^{-1}=L$, so
$ucu^{-1}$ centralizes $L$ for every
$u\in\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)$, and again $\mathfrak D_G(L)=1$.

The argument is specific to operator-norm approximation.  It does not prove
that $H$ is nonsofic or nonhyperlinear: normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
approximations do not provide the operator-norm control used to construct the
conjugation representation in Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}.

Full MF radicals determine prescribed MF-visible quotients as follows.

\begin{mainthm}[prescribed MF-visible quotients]\label{thm:prescribed-quotients}
Let $B$ be a countable group with $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$, and let
$1\ne d\in B$ normally generate $B$.  Put $A=\langle d\rangle$ and, for a
countable group $Q$, define
\[
 W_Q=B*_A(Q\times A).
\]
Then the map $\pi_Q\colon W_Q\to Q$ which kills $B$ and projects the second
vertex group onto $Q$ is a split epimorphism, and
%<webmath object="RadMF" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)" title="the part of W_Q invisible to every MF-target representation">
% The formula computes exactly which elements of W_Q every norm-corona
% representation sends to the identity: they are precisely the elements whose
% image under pi_Q lies in the MF radical of Q.  If Q is MF, this reduces to
% the kernel of pi_Q.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)
 =\pi_Q^{-1}\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr).
\]
The group $W_Q$ is non-MF.  If $Q$ is MF, then
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)=\ker\pi_Q=\normal{d}_{W_Q}.
\]
Moreover, precomposition with $\pi_Q$ induces a bijection
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(Q,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(W_Q,M)
\]
for every MF group $M$.
\end{mainthm}

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}

The group-MF framework used here is developed by
Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt and Korchagin~\cite{CDE,Korchagin}.
Shulman proves MF permanence for doubles $G*_H G$ of MF groups and, separately,
MF full group $C^*$-algebra results for several classes of amalgamated and
semidirect products~\cite{Shulman}.  The operator-norm case
remained open after non-approximability had been established for finite
Schatten norms~\cite{DGLT,LubotzkyOppenheim,Thom}.
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky study an operator--Hilbert--Schmidt stability
condition as a possible source of non-MF groups~\cite{BDL}.  Here
operator-norm control implies that coordinatewise conjugation defines a
representation in a second norm matrix corona.  Its Kazhdan projection makes
the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant invariant under the one-sided
compressor.  Section~4 then proves that a normal property-\textup{(T)} subgroup
contained in the defect lies in the MF radical.

A closely related $C^*$-algebraic phenomenon appears in Tseng's work
\cite{Tseng}: a proper one-sided compression of a property-\textup{(T)}
subgroup makes its Kazhdan projection infinite in the full group
$C^*$-algebra.  That result obstructs MF-ness of the full group
$C^*$-algebra.  The present argument instead applies stable finiteness to the
corona representation formed by coordinatewise conjugation and obtains an
obstruction to the group-MF property itself.

Leavitt introduced the algebras $L_K(1,n)$ as examples of rings with
nonunique module rank~\cite{Leavitt}.  Their defining relations supply the
internal compression used here.  The binary Leavitt algebra is purely
infinite simple~\cite{AbramsAranda}, hence an exchange ring~\cite{AraExchange},
and its center is the base field~\cite{ArandaCrow}.  Preusser's work gives
general normal-subgroup theorems for linear groups over exchange
rings~\cite{Preusser}; the simplicity proof below instead extracts an
elementary root directly from a nonidentity normal element.
Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain give property~\textup{(T)} for
$\EL_n(R)$ whenever $n\ge3$ and $R$ is a finitely generated unital
associative ring~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.
Related rigidity results for metric approximations of Kazhdan groups appear
in~\cite{KT19,AlekseevThom,Dadarlat}.

\section{One-sided compression in finite dimension}

\begin{theorem}[finite-dimensional commutant rigidity]\label{thm:commutant}
Let $G$ be a group, let $L\le G$, and suppose $u\in G$ satisfies
$uLu^{-1}\le L$.  If $k$ is a field, $V$ is a finite-dimensional
$k$-vector space, and $\rho\colon G\to\GL(V)$ is a homomorphism, then
\[
 \rho(u)\rho(L)'\rho(u)^{-1}=\rho(L)',
\]
where $\rho(L)'$ is the commutant of $\rho(L)$ in $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$.
Consequently, if $c\in C_G(L)$, then
\[
 \rho([ucu^{-1},\ell])=1
 \qquad(\ell\in L).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $C=\rho(L)'$.  If $x\in C$, $h\in L$, and
$h'=uhu^{-1}\in L$, then
\begin{align*}
 \rho(h)\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(u)
 &=\rho(u)^{-1}\rho(h')x\rho(u)\\
 &=\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(h')\rho(u)\\
 &=\rho(u)^{-1}x\rho(u)\rho(h).
\end{align*}
Thus $\rho(u)^{-1}C\rho(u)\subseteq C$.  Conjugation by
$\rho(u)^{-1}$ is injective, and $C$ is finite-dimensional.  Its restriction
to $C$ is therefore surjective, so the inclusion is equality.  Conjugating
by $\rho(u)$ gives $\rho(u)C\rho(u)^{-1}=C$.

If $c\in C_G(L)$, then $\rho(c)\in C$, hence
$\rho(ucu^{-1})\in C$.  It commutes with every $\rho(\ell)$,
$\ell\in L$, which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The finite-dimensional hypothesis is essential here.  On an
infinite-dimensional space an injective endomorphism of the commutant need
not be surjective.  In Section~3 the Kazhdan projection places the analogous
endomorphism inside a norm matrix corona, where stable finiteness supplies
the missing surjectivity at the level of fixed-space projections.

\section{Kazhdan transport in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm}

For $a\in M_d(\C)$ write
\[
 \hsnorm{a}=\tr_d(a^*a)^{1/2},
 \qquad \tr_d=\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}.
\]
An \emph{operator-norm asymptotic representation} of a countable group $G$
is a sequence of maps $V_n\colon G\to\U(d_n)$ such that $V_n(1)=1$ and
\[
 \opnorm{V_n(gh)-V_n(g)V_n(h)}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(g,h\in G).
\]
For such a sequence put
\[
 K_2(V)=\{g\in G:\hsnorm{V_n(g)-1}\longrightarrow0\}.
\]
The operator-norm defects also tend to zero in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm, so $K_2(V)$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shadow-residual}
 R_{\infty\to2}(G)=\bigcap_V K_2(V),
\end{equation}
where $V$ ranges over all operator-norm asymptotic representations of $G$.
An element is universally Hilbert--Schmidt trivial precisely when it belongs
to $R_{\infty\to2}(G)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:stable-finite}
For every sequence $(m_n)$, the norm matrix corona
\[
 \prod_nM_{m_n}(\C)\big/\bigoplus_nM_{m_n}(\C)
\]
is stably finite.  Consequently, unitarily equivalent projections in a norm
matrix corona are equal whenever one is dominated by the other.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $v^*v=1$ in the corona, and let $(x_n)$ be a bounded lift of
$v$.  Then $x_n^*x_n\to1$ in norm.  For all sufficiently large $n$, the matrix
$x_n$ is invertible, and the unitary $x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$ differs from
$x_n$ by $o(1)$.  Thus $v$ is represented by unitaries and $vv^*=1$.  The
same argument applies to every matrix amplification of the corona.  If $A$
is a stably finite unital $C^*$-algebra, equivalent projections $p\le q$ satisfy
$p=q$: if $w^*w=q$ and $ww^*=p$, then $w\in qAq$ is an isometry in the
corner.  The corner is finite because $w+(1-q)$ is an isometry in $A$.
Consequently $ww^*=q$, and hence $p=q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[one-sided order for the Kazhdan projection]
\label{lem:kazhdan-projection-order}
Let $L$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let $B$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra, and
let $\pi\colon L\to\U(B)$ be a homomorphism.  Denote by
$P\in B$ the image of the Kazhdan projection under the extension
$C^*_{\max}(L)\to B$.  If $U\in\U(B)$ satisfies
\[
 U\pi(L)U^*\subseteq\pi(L),
\]
then
\[
 U^*PU\le P.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Represent $B$ faithfully and nondegenerately on a Hilbert space
$\mathcal H$.  The represented projection $P$ is the orthogonal projection
onto $\operatorname{Fix}\pi(L)$.  If $\xi$ is $L$-fixed and $\ell\in L$,
then
\[
 \pi(\ell)U^*\xi
 =U^*\bigl(U\pi(\ell)U^*\bigr)\xi
 =U^*\xi,
\]
because $U\pi(\ell)U^*$ belongs to $\pi(L)$.  Hence
$U^*\operatorname{Fix}\pi(L)\subseteq\operatorname{Fix}\pi(L)$, so the
range projection $U^*PU$ is dominated by $P$ in $B(\mathcal H)$.  Faithfulness
of the representation gives the same projection inequality in $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[one-sided Kazhdan transport]\label{thm:transport}
Let $L\le G$ have property~\textup{(T)}, and let $u\in G$ satisfy
$uLu^{-1}\le L$.  Let $(V_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $G$, and let $(x_n)$ be uniformly bounded in operator
norm.  If
\[
 \hsnorm{V_n(\ell)x_nV_n(\ell)^*-x_n}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\ell\in L),
\]
then, for every $\ell\in L$,
\begin{align*}
 \hsnorm{V_n(\ell)V_n(u)x_nV_n(u)^*V_n(\ell)^*
          -V_n(u)x_nV_n(u)^*}&\longrightarrow0,\\
 \hsnorm{V_n(\ell)V_n(u)^*x_nV_n(u)V_n(\ell)^*
          -V_n(u)^*x_nV_n(u)}&\longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\omega$ be a free ultrafilter.  Regard $M_{d_n}(\C)$ as a Hilbert
space with its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt inner product, and form the
Hilbert-space ultraproduct $\mathcal K_\omega$.  Conjugation by the $V_n(g)$
defines a unitary representation $\sigma$ of $G$ on $\mathcal K_\omega$.
Indeed, for unitaries $A,B$ one has
\[
 \opnorm{\operatorname{Ad}(A)-\operatorname{Ad}(B)}
 \le2\opnorm{A-B},
\]
so the operator-norm multiplicative defects of $(V_n)$ also make the
conjugation actions multiplicative modulo $c_0$.
The class $\xi=[x_n]_\omega$ is fixed by $\sigma(L)$.

For $g\in G$, let $\widetilde\sigma(g)$ be the class of the coordinate
operators $\operatorname{Ad}(V_n(g))$ in the norm matrix corona
\[
 \mathcal B=\prod_nB(M_{d_n}(\C))\big/\bigoplus_nB(M_{d_n}(\C)).
\]
After choosing matrix units on each $M_{d_n}(\C)$, this is a norm matrix
corona with coordinate sizes $d_n^2$.
The preceding estimate shows directly that
$g\mapsto\widetilde\sigma(g)$ is an exact homomorphism
$G\to\U(\mathcal B)$.  Its restriction to $L$ therefore extends to a
$*$-homomorphism $C^*_{\max}(L)\to\mathcal B$.  Let $P\in\mathcal B$ be the
image of the Kazhdan projection~\cite{AkemannWalter}.  Under the natural
representation of $\mathcal B$ on $\mathcal K_\omega$, its range is
$\operatorname{Fix}\sigma(L)$.  This representation of $\mathcal B$ need
not be faithful; it is used only after the following projection identity has
been proved inside $\mathcal B$.  Since
$uLu^{-1}\le L$, the unitary $U=\widetilde\sigma(u)$ satisfies the
hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:kazhdan-projection-order}, and hence
$U^*PU\le P$ inside $\mathcal B$.
The two projections are unitarily equivalent.  Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-finite}
therefore gives $U^*PU=P$.  Hence both $U$ and $U^*$ preserve
$\operatorname{Fix}\sigma(L)$, so $U\xi$ and $U^*\xi$ are fixed by
$\sigma(L)$.  The two limits in the statement therefore vanish along
$\omega$.  Since this holds for every free ultrafilter, both sequences
converge ordinarily.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:defect-hs}
Let $L\le G$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let $uLu^{-1}\le L$, and let
$c\in C_G(L)$.  Then
\[
 [ucu^{-1},\ell]\in R_{\infty\to2}(G)
 \qquad(\ell\in L).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $(V_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic representation.  If
$c\in C_G(L)$, the sequence $(V_n(c))$ asymptotically commutes with
$V_n(L)$ in operator norm and hence in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} shows that the same is true of
$(V_n(u)V_n(c)V_n(u)^*)$.  Asymptotic multiplicativity identifies this
sequence in operator norm with $(V_n(ucu^{-1}))$; explicitly,
\[
 \opnorm{V_n(u)V_n(c)V_n(u)^*-V_n(ucu^{-1})}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \hsnorm{V_n([ucu^{-1},\ell])-1}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\ell\in L).
\]
Intersecting over $(V_n)$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Normal Kazhdan subgroups and the MF radical}

Let $K\trianglelefteq G$ have property~\textup{(T)}, and let $p$ be the
image of its Kazhdan projection under a corona representation of $G$.
Normality of $K$ implies that $p$ commutes with the image of $G$.  Hence
$q=1-p$ also commutes with that image, and the corner
$q\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}q$ has no nonzero $K$-fixed vectors.

\begin{lemma}[central corona corners]\label{lem:central-corona-corner}
Let \(\rho\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})\) be a homomorphism from a
countable group, and let \(q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}\) be a nonzero
projection commuting with \(\rho(G)\).  Then, after passing to an infinite
coordinate subsequence, there are nonzero projections
\(q_n\in M_{d_n}(\C)\) and an operator-norm asymptotic representation
\[
 W_n\colon G\longrightarrow\U(q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n)
\]
whose corona class is the corner representation \(g\mapsto q\rho(g)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lift \(q\) to projections \(q_n\), and lift each \(\rho(g)\) to unitaries
\(U_n(g)\).  Projection lifting follows by spectral rounding.  Unitary
lifting follows by polar-correcting any lift, since its two unitarity defects
converge to zero in operator norm.  The commutation relation in the corona
gives
\[
 \opnorm{q_nU_n(g)-U_n(g)q_n}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(g\in G).
\]
Consequently \(q_nU_n(g)q_n\), viewed on \(q_n\C^{d_n}\), has both unitarity
defects converging to zero.  Its polar correction \(W_n(g)\) is unitary and
satisfies
\[
 \opnorm{W_n(g)-q_nU_n(g)q_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The homomorphism relation for \(\rho\) now makes \((W_n)\) an operator-norm
asymptotic representation.  Since \(q\ne0\), infinitely many \(q_n\) are
nonzero; retain those coordinates.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[normal Kazhdan radical theorem]\label{thm:normal-kazhdan}
Let \(G\) be countable, let \(D\le R_{\infty\to2}(G)\), and let
\(K\trianglelefteq G\) have property~\textup{(T)} with \(K\le D\).  Then
\[
 K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that a homomorphism
\(\Theta\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})\) is nontrivial on \(K\).
Replace \(G\) by \(\Theta(G)\), replace \(K\) by \(\Theta(K)\), and denote
the resulting inclusion into the corona again by \(\Theta\).  The new \(K\)
is a nontrivial normal property-\textup{(T)} subgroup, since
property~\textup{(T)} passes to quotients.  Every one of its
elements remains in the universal Hilbert--Schmidt kernel, because any
operator-norm asymptotic representation of \(\Theta(G)\), precomposed with
the quotient map from the original group, is such a representation of the
original group.

Let \(p_K\in C^*_{\max}(K)\) be the Kazhdan projection and let \(p\) be its
image in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}\).  Thus \(p\) is the projection onto the
\(K\)-fixed vectors in every faithful representation of the corona.
Normality of \(K\) gives
\[
 \Theta(g)p\Theta(g)^*=p
 \qquad(g\in G).
\]
Indeed, in a faithful representation of the corona the two projections have
ranges \(\Theta(g)\operatorname{Fix}\Theta(K)\) and
\(\operatorname{Fix}\Theta(K)\); these agree because \(gKg^{-1}=K\).
Put \(q=1-p\).  The projection \(q\) is nonzero, since \(p=1\) would imply
that every element of \(K\) has trivial image.  Lemma~\ref{lem:central-corona-corner}
gives an operator-norm asymptotic representation \((W_n)\) on nonzero
corners \(q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\).

Choose a finite symmetric Kazhdan set \(S\subseteq K\) and a Kazhdan
constant \(\kappa>0\).  In the corner \(q\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d}q\), the
Kazhdan projection is zero.  Hence the positive element
\[
 b=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}
       (q\Theta(s)q-q)^*(q\Theta(s)q-q)
\]
satisfies
\[
 b\ge\frac{\kappa^2}{|S|}\,q.
\]
Indeed, in every representation of the corner there are no \(K\)-fixed
vectors, so the defining Kazhdan inequality gives this operator inequality.

The coordinate elements
\[
 b_n=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}(W_n(s)-q_n)^*(W_n(s)-q_n)
\]
represent \(b\).  Taking the normalized trace on
\(q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\) yields
\[
 \frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\hsnorm{W_n(s)-q_n}^{\,2}
 \ge \frac{\kappa^2}{|S|}-o(1).
\]
Here the Hilbert--Schmidt norms use the normalized trace of the corner.  The
order inequality for $b$ says that the negative part of
$b_n-(\kappa^2/|S|)q_n$ converges to zero in operator norm, which gives the
displayed trace inequality.
After passing to a subsequence, one fixed \(s_0\in S\) therefore stays a
positive Hilbert--Schmidt distance from the corner identity.  This
contradicts \(s_0\in K\le D\le R_{\infty\to2}(G)\).  Thus every corona
homomorphism kills \(K\), as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion}]
For every $u\in\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)$, $c\in C_G(L)$, and $\ell\in L$,
Corollary~\ref{cor:defect-hs} gives
\[
 [ucu^{-1},\ell]\in R_{\infty\to2}(G).
\]
Normality of \(R_{\infty\to2}(G)\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak D_G(L)\le R_{\infty\to2}(G).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan} now gives
\(K\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)\).  The two stated consequences follow by taking
\(K\ne1\), or \(K=G=\mathfrak D_G(L)\), respectively.

For a finite-dimensional linear representation over an arbitrary field,
Theorem~\ref{thm:commutant} kills every generator of
\(\mathfrak D_G(L)\).  Its kernel is normal, so it contains
\(\mathfrak D_G(L)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[functoriality and normal generation]\label{prop:defect-saturation}
Let $L\le G$ and put $D=\mathfrak D_G(L)$.

If $f\colon G\to Q$ is a homomorphism, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-functoriality}
 f(D)\le\mathfrak D_{f(G)}(f(L)).
\end{equation}
If $S\le G$ is a nontrivial simple subgroup and $D\cap S\ne1$, then
$S\le D$.  If in addition the normal closure of $S$ in $G$ is all of $G$,
then $D=G$.  If moreover $G$ is countable and both $L$ and $G$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, then
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=G.
\]

Suppose instead that $f$ is onto, $S\le D$ is simple, $f(S)\ne1$, and
$f(S)$ normally generates $Q$.  Then
\[
 \mathfrak D_Q(f(L))=Q.
\]
If, in addition, $G$ is countable and both $L$ and $G$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, then
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)=Q.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $u\in\operatorname{Comp}_G(L)$, then
$f(u)f(L)f(u)^{-1}\le f(L)$; if $c\in C_G(L)$, then
$f(c)\in C_{f(G)}(f(L))$.  Therefore $f$ takes every defining generator of
$D$ into a defining generator of $\mathfrak D_{f(G)}(f(L))$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:defect-functoriality}.

The subgroup $D$ is normal in $G$, so $D\cap S$ is normal in $S$.
Simplicity and $D\cap S\ne1$ give $D\cap S=S$, hence $S\le D$.  Since $D$
is normal in $G$, it then contains the normal closure of $S$.  Under the
second hypothesis this is $G$.  The direct radical assertion is
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion} with $K=G$.

For the image statement, \eqref{eq:defect-functoriality} gives
$f(S)\le\mathfrak D_Q(f(L))$.  The latter subgroup is normal in $Q$, so it
contains the normal closure of $f(S)$ and is therefore all of $Q$.  Property
\textup{(T)} passes to quotients, so the final hypotheses give property
\textup{(T)} for both $f(L)$ and $Q$.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion} inside $Q$, with $K=Q$, proves the
last assertion.
\end{proof}
\section{The binary Leavitt self-compression}

Throughout this section $R=L_{\F_2}(1,2)$ and
\[
 p=s_0t_0,\qquad q=s_1t_1.
\]
The relations \eqref{eq:leavitt} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pq}
 p+q=1,\qquad t_1qs_1=1.
\end{equation}
In particular $q\ne0$.

We use indices $0,\ldots,11$ for $12\times12$ matrices and identify
$\GL_3(R)$ with the subgroup of $\GL_{12}(R)$ consisting of the matrices
$\operatorname{diag}(A,I_9)$.  On $M_3(R)$ consider the unital injective
endomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-compression}
 \Psi(A)=qI_3+s_0A t_0.
\end{equation}
Here $s_0At_0$ denotes the matrix $(s_0a_{ij}t_0)_{ij}$.  The relations
$qs_0=t_0q=0$ and $t_0s_0=1$ show that $\Psi$ is multiplicative and unital.
The identity $t_0\Psi(A)s_0=A$ proves injectivity.  The term $qI_3$ is needed
for unitality because the map $A\mapsto s_0At_0$ sends $I_3$ to $pI_3$.

Set
\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}s_0I_3&s_1t_0I_3\\0&t_1I_3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}t_0I_3&0\\s_0t_1I_3&s_1I_3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A direct calculation using \eqref{eq:leavitt} gives $XY=YX=I_6$.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 \tau=\operatorname{diag}(X,Y)\in\GL_{12}(R).
\end{equation}
Writing $I=I_6$, the Whitehead factorization is
\begin{align}\label{eq:whitehead-factorization}
 \operatorname{diag}(X,X^{-1})
 &=\begin{pmatrix}I&X\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\-X^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&X\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\I&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\I&I\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
Each block-unipotent factor is a product of elementary $12\times12$
matrices: its off-diagonal $6\times6$ block may be inserted one entry at a
time, and the corresponding matrix units have pairwise zero products.
Since $Y=X^{-1}$, \eqref{eq:whitehead-factorization} proves
$\tau\in\EL_{12}(R)$.

Let $H=\EL_{12}(R)$ and let
%<webmath object="EL3" when="L=\EL_3(R)" title="the upper-left elementary subgroup used for property (T)">
% L is the copy of the 3-by-3 elementary group occupying the first three rows
% and columns of H.  It has property (T).  The explicit matrix tau sends this
% whole [[subgroup]] into another copy contained in L.
%</webmath>
$L=\EL_3(R)\le H$ be the upper-left corner.
Both groups have property~\textup{(T)} by the theorem of Ershov and
Jaikin-Zapirain~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.
Block multiplication yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner-conjugation}
 \tau\,\operatorname{diag}(A,I_9)\,\tau^{-1}
 =\operatorname{diag}(\Psi(A),I_9).
\end{equation}
For every $i\ne j$ and $a\in R$,
$\Psi(e_{ij}(a))=e_{ij}(s_0at_0)$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compresses-L}
 \tau L\tau^{-1}\le L.
\end{equation}

The field $\F_2$ is used in the diagonal branch of the simplicity argument
below: the only central unit of $R$ is then $1$.  The dimension $12$ comes
from applying the $6\times6$ Whitehead
factorization to the $3\times3$ Kazhdan corner.  We do not claim that $12$ is
minimal.

\section{The binary Leavitt group has full MF radical}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:simple}
The group $H=\EL_{12}(R)$ is simple.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We use two concrete ring facts.  First, for every $0\ne x\in R$ there are
$a,b\in R$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-sided-sandwich}
 axb=1.
\end{equation}
This is the form of pure infiniteness needed here~\cite{AbramsAranda}.
Second, the only central unit of $R$ is $1$: indeed
$Z(R)=\F_2$ by~\cite[Corollary~4.3]{ArandaCrow} and
$\F_2^\times=\{1\}$.

We first record that a nonzero root normally generates the elementary group.
More generally, let $S$ be a unital ring, let $n\ge3$, and suppose
$a,x,b\in S$ satisfy $axb=1$.  If a normal subgroup of $\EL_n(S)$ contains
$e_{ij}(x)$, choose $k$ different from $i$ and $j$.  The elementary
commutator relations give
\[
 [e_{ij}(x),e_{jk}(b)]=e_{ik}(xb),\qquad
 [e_{ji}(a),e_{ik}(xb)]=e_{jk}(1),
\]
so the normal subgroup contains a unit root.  From any $e_{uv}(1)$ in the
normal subgroup and any third index
$w$, the relations
\[
 [e_{wu}(r),e_{uv}(1)]=e_{wv}(r),\qquad
 [e_{uv}(1),e_{vw}(r)]=e_{uw}(r)
\]
put the corresponding source and target moves, with arbitrary coefficient
$r\in S$, in the normal subgroup.  Repeating these moves, using a third
index when needed, gives every elementary generator.  Thus, by
\eqref{eq:two-sided-sandwich}, it remains to prove that every nontrivial
normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq H$ contains a nonzero elementary root.

We also need the following coefficient-separation consequence of the Leavitt
relations.  If $r,s\in R$ are nonzero, then there are $a,b\in R$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-separation}
 arb=0,\qquad bsar\ne0.
\end{equation}
Here is a direct proof.  For every $w\in R$ there are $x,y\in R$ with
$xwy=0$ and $yx\ne0$.  This is immediate with $x=y=1$ if $w=0$.
Otherwise choose $c,d$ with $cwd=1$ and put $x=t_1c$, $y=ds_0$.
Then $xwy=t_1s_0=0$.  Moreover $s_0t_1\ne0$, since
\[
 1=(t_0s_0)(t_1s_1)=t_0(s_0t_1)s_1,
\]
and $yx=ds_0t_1c=0$ would imply
\[
 s_0t_1=(cwd)(s_0t_1)(cwd)
       =(cw)\bigl((ds_0)(t_1c)\bigr)(wd)=0.
\]
To obtain \eqref{eq:coefficient-separation}, choose factorizations
\[
 c_r\,r\,d_r=1,\quad e_d\,d_r\,f_d=1,\quad
 e_s\,s\,f_s=1,\quad e_t(c_r r)f_t=1,
\]
apply the preceding construction to $w=e_tf_d$, and set
\[
 a=f_s x e_t c_r,\qquad b=d_r f_d y e_s.
\]
Then $arb=0$.  If $bsar=0$, multiplication on the left by $e_d$ and on the
right by $f_t$ gives $yx=0$, a contradiction.

Now let $N\trianglelefteq H$ be nontrivial and choose $1\ne g\in N$.
Suppose first that $g$ is diagonal.  Its inverse is
also diagonal.  If $g$ commuted with every $e_{ij}(a)$, then commuting with
the $e_{ij}(1)$ would force $g=\lambda I_{12}$, and commuting with arbitrary
$e_{ij}(a)$ would make $\lambda$ a central unit.  Hence $g=1$, a
contradiction.  Thus for some $i\ne j$ and $a\in R$, conjugation by $g$ sends
$e_{ij}(a)$ to $e_{ij}(y)$ with $y\ne a$.  Consequently
\[
 [g,e_{ij}(a)]=e_{ij}(y-a)\in N
\]
is a nonzero root.

Suppose next that $g$ is not diagonal, and choose $\ell\ne i$ with
$g_{\ell i}\ne0$.  Put $r=(g^{-1})_{\ell i}$.  If $r=0$, set
\[
 A=gE_{i\ell}g^{-1},\qquad B=E_{i\ell}.
\]
Then $AB=0$, and the double commutator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-unipotent-sparse}
 [g e_{i\ell}(1)g^{-1},e_{i\ell}(1)]=1-BA
\end{equation}
belongs to $N$.  Its defect $-BA$ is square-zero and supported in row $i$.
There is an $m$ for which
$g_{\ell i}(g^{-1})_{\ell m}\ne0$: otherwise the
$(\ell,\ell)$ entry of $g^{-1}g=1$ would give $g_{\ell i}=0$.
Thus the $(i,m)$ entry of the defect in
\eqref{eq:row-unipotent-sparse} is nonzero.

It remains to treat $r\ne0$.  Apply \eqref{eq:coefficient-separation} to
$r$ and $s=g_{\ell i}$, obtaining $a,b$ with
$arb=0$ and $bsar\ne0$.  This time put
\[
 A=g(aE_{i\ell})g^{-1},\qquad B=bE_{i\ell}.
\]
Again $AB=0$, because its potentially nonzero coefficient contains $arb$.
As the two elementary roots commute, reduction modulo $N$ shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-unipotent-dense}
 [g e_{i\ell}(a)g^{-1},e_{i\ell}(b)]=1-BA\in N.
\end{equation}
The defect is square-zero and supported in row $i$, while its $(i,i)$ entry
is $-bsar\ne0$.

Both off-diagonal cases therefore produce an element $1+v\in N$ with
$v^2=0$, supported in a single row $i$, and with some $v_{im}\ne0$.
Choose $n$ different from both $i$ and $m$ (with only $n\ne i$ needed when
$m=i$).  Direct multiplication gives the row-extraction identity
\[
 [1+v,e_{mn}(1)]=e_{in}(v_{im})\in N.
\]
This is a nonzero root, so the normal-generation argument above gives
$N=H$.  Hence every normal subgroup of $H$ is trivial or all of $H$.
Finally, $e_{01}(1)\ne1$, so $H$ is nontrivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:defect}
Let
\[
 c=e_{34}(1),\qquad \ell=e_{12}(1),\qquad
 d=[\tau c\tau^{-1},\ell]
\]
in $H$.  Then
%<webmath object="d" when="d=e_{02}(q)" title="the nonidentity element produced by the compression calculation">
% The commutator d is exactly the elementary matrix with q in row 0, column 2.
% Since q is nonzero, d is not the identity.  It lies in the compression defect,
% and the identity t_1 q s_1=1 makes the normal subgroup generated by d equal
% to H through elementary commutators.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="e02" when="d=e_{02}(q)" title="the elementary matrix with q in row 0 and column 2">
% This is the 12-by-12 identity matrix with one additional entry q in row 0,
% column 2.  It differs from the identity exactly because q is nonzero.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="q" when="d=e_{02}(q)" title="the nonzero Leavitt-algebra entry in d">
% Here q=s_1t_1.  The relation t_1 q s_1=1 proves that q cannot be zero.  Its
% occurrence off the diagonal is therefore enough to prove d is nonidentity.
%</webmath>
\[
 c\in C_H(L),\qquad d=e_{02}(q)\ne1,
 \qquad \normal d_H=H.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every elementary generator of the upper-left $3\times3$ corner has both
indices in $\{0,1,2\}$.  The elementary commutator relations show that it
commutes with $c=e_{34}(1)$, so $c\in C_H(L)$.

Direct multiplication of the matrices in \eqref{eq:tau} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moved-mark}
 \tau c\tau^{-1}=e_{01}(q)e_{34}(1).
\end{equation}
The second factor commutes with $\ell=e_{12}(1)$, and
$[e_{ij}(a),e_{jk}(b)]=e_{ik}(ab)$ gives
$d=e_{02}(q)$.  This is nontrivial because $q\ne0$.  Since
$t_1qs_1=1$, the preceding sandwiched-root argument gives
$\normal d_H=H$ directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}]
The finitely generated $\F_2$-algebra $R$ is countable, and hence so is $H$.
The ring $R$ is finitely generated, and $H$ has property~\textup{(T)} by the
Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain theorem for elementary groups over finitely generated
rings~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJZ}.

The containment \eqref{eq:compresses-L}, the centrality of $c$ relative to
$L$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:defect} show that
$d\in\mathfrak D_H(L)$.  The subgroup $\mathfrak D_H(L)$ is normal and
Proposition~\ref{prop:defect} gives $\normal d_H=H$, so
$\mathfrak D_H(L)=H$.  The group $H$ has property~\textup{(T)}, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-criterion}, applied with $K=H$, gives
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H$.
Independently of this radical calculation, Proposition~\ref{prop:simple}
shows that $H$ is nontrivial and simple.

If $M$ is MF and $f\colon H\to M$ is a homomorphism, compose $f$ with a
faithful homomorphism from $M$ to a norm matrix corona.  Since
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=H$, this composition and hence $f$ are trivial.  If
$H$ were MF, its identity homomorphism would contradict this conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section{MF-visible quotients}\label{sec:prescribed-quotients}

The MF radical is exact across every surjection whose kernel is already
MF-invisible.

\begin{proposition}[full-kernel pullback]\label{prop:full-kernel-pullback}
Let $f\colon G\to Q$ be a surjective homomorphism of countable groups.  If
$\ker f\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$, then
%<webmath object="pullback-radical" when="\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)=f^{-1}\!\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr)" title="the MF-invisible part of G is exactly the preimage of the MF-invisible part of Q">
% The assumption says that every finite-matrix-limit representation of G
% already erases the kernel of f.  Such a representation therefore descends
% uniquely to Q.  An element of G is erased by every representation precisely
% when its image in Q is erased by every representation of Q.
%</webmath>
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)
 =f^{-1}\!\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr).
\]
In particular, the conclusion holds whenever
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(\ker f)=\ker f$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every corona homomorphism from $G$ kills $\ker f$ and therefore factors
uniquely through $f$.  Conversely, composing a corona homomorphism of $Q$
with $f$ gives one of $G$.  The two families of kernels have the displayed
intersection.  For the final assertion, functoriality of the MF radical under
the inclusion $\ker f\to G$ gives
$\ker f\le\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$.
\end{proof}

Let $B$ be a countable group with $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$, and let
$1\ne d\in B$ normally generate $B$.  Put $A=\langle d\rangle$.  For a
countable group $Q$, define
%<webmath object="WQ" when="W_Q=B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the group obtained by attaching an MF-invisible part to Q">
% W_Q joins B to Q times A while identifying the copy of A in each group.
% Every homomorphism from B to an MF group is trivial, so every MF-target map
% from W_Q contains exactly the information supplied by Q and nothing extra
% from B.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="∗A" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="joining B and Q times A along their common subgroup A">
% This amalgamated free product starts with both groups and identifies their
% two copies of A.  Apart from that identification, it imposes no relations
% between elements coming from the two sides.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="B" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the attached group invisible to every MF target">
% B has full MF radical, meaning every homomorphism from B to any MF group is
% trivial.  It supplies the non-MF kernel in W_Q while remaining invisible in
% every finite-matrix-limit representation.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="Q" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the chosen group retained as the visible quotient">
% Q is an arbitrary countable group chosen before constructing W_Q.  The map
% pi_Q recovers this Q, and every homomorphism from W_Q to an MF target factors
% uniquely through it.
%</webmath>
%<webmath object="A" when="B*_A(Q\times A)" title="the cyclic subgroup used to attach B to Q times A">
% A is generated by d.  The construction identifies this subgroup inside B
% with the second coordinate of Q times A.  Making d equal to the identity
% then removes B and the A coordinate, leaving Q.
%</webmath>
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amalgam}
 W_Q=B*_A(Q\times A),
\end{equation}
where $A\to Q\times A$ is the inclusion into the second factor.  The maps
from $B$ and $Q\times A$ to $Q$ that are respectively trivial and the
projection onto $Q$ agree on $A$.  They therefore define an epimorphism
%<webmath object="πQ" when="\pi_Q\colon W_Q\longrightarrow Q" title="the map that forgets the attached B and A parts and returns Q">
% Pi_Q sends every element coming from B to the identity, sends (q,a) from
% Q times A to q, and reaches every element of Q.  Its kernel is the normal
% subgroup generated by d.
%</webmath>
\[
 \pi_Q\colon W_Q\longrightarrow Q.
\]
It is split by the inclusion $Q\to Q\times A\to W_Q$.  The normal-form
theorem for amalgamated free products shows that both vertex maps in
\eqref{eq:amalgam} are injective; in particular, $d\ne1$ in $W_Q$.

\begin{proposition}[universal factorization]\label{prop:universal-factorization}
Let $T$ be a group such that every homomorphism $B\to T$ is trivial.  Then
precomposition with $\pi_Q$ is a bijection
%<webmath object="Hom" when="\operatorname{Hom}(Q,T)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(W_Q,T)" title="the same collection of maps into T, described from Q or from W_Q">
% Hom(Q,T) is the collection of multiplication-preserving maps from Q to T.
% The bijection says that every such map gives exactly one map from W_Q to T,
% and every map from W_Q to T arises this way.  Thus T receives no information
% from W_Q beyond the quotient Q.
%</webmath>
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(Q,T)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(W_Q,T).
\]
Furthermore,
%<webmath object="ker" when="\ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}" title="all elements removed when W_Q is mapped onto Q">
% The kernel of pi_Q contains exactly the elements sent to the identity in Q.
% The equality says this kernel is the smallest normal subgroup of W_Q that
% contains d.
%</webmath>
\[
 \ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $f\colon W_Q\to T$ be a homomorphism.  Its restriction to $B$ is
trivial, so its restriction to the amalgamated subgroup $A$ is trivial.  For
$(q,a)\in Q\times A$, we then have
\[
 f(q,a)=f(q,1)f(1,a)=f(q,1).
\]
Thus the restriction of $f$ to $Q\times A$ factors uniquely through the
projection onto $Q$.  The universal property of the amalgam shows that $f$
factors through $\pi_Q$.  The factorization is unique because $\pi_Q$ is
surjective.

The element $d$ belongs to $\ker\pi_Q$.  Conversely, quotienting $W_Q$ by
$\normal d_{W_Q}$ kills $B$, since $\normal d_B=B$, and kills the second
factor $A$ in $Q\times A$.  The resulting quotient is $Q$, with quotient map
induced by $\pi_Q$.  Hence $\ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:prescribed-quotients}]
Every homomorphism from $B$ to an MF group is trivial: compose it with a
faithful corona embedding of the target and use
$\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-factorization}
therefore gives the asserted bijection for every MF group $M$.  In
addition, the definition of $\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(B)=B$ says directly that
every homomorphism from $B$ to $\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})$ is trivial.
Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-factorization} with
$T=\U(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf d})$ shows that every corona homomorphism from
$W_Q$ factors uniquely through $\pi_Q$.  Intersecting their kernels gives
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)
 =\pi_Q^{-1}\bigl(\Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(Q)\bigr).
\]
The kernel of $\pi_Q$ belongs to this radical, and it contains the
nonidentity element $d$.  Thus $W_Q$ is non-MF.  If $Q$ is MF, its MF radical
is trivial, and the displayed equality becomes
\[
 \Rad_{\mathrm{MF}}(W_Q)=\ker\pi_Q=\normal d_{W_Q}.
\]

\end{proof}

If $N\trianglelefteq W_Q$, the same factorization gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure-pullback}
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^{W_Q}(N)
 =\pi_Q^{-1}\!\left(
   \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm{MF}}^Q(\pi_Q(N))
  \right).
\end{equation}
Indeed, an MF-target homomorphism from $W_Q$ kills $N$ exactly when its
unique factor through $Q$ kills $\pi_Q(N)$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-residual-calculus}
and \eqref{eq:closure-pullback} give
\[
 W_Q/N\text{ is MF}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ker\pi_Q\le N
 \text{ and }
 Q/\pi_Q(N)\text{ is MF}.
\]
Here, if $\ker\pi_Q\le N$, then
$N=\pi_Q^{-1}(\pi_Q(N))$; this is the only additional observation needed
for the displayed equivalence.
