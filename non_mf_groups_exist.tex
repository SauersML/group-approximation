\title[Non-MF groups exist]{Non-MF groups exist}
\author{\paperauthor}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 46L05; Secondary 20F05, 22D25, 20E26}
\keywords{MF groups, approximate unitary representations, property~(T),
group $C^*$-algebras, sofic groups}

\begin{abstract}
A countable discrete group is MF if it embeds in the unitary group of a
norm matrix corona
$\mathcal Q=\prod_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)/\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)$.
We prove that non-MF groups exist, resolving in the negative the
longstanding question of whether every countable group is MF.  Our principal example is an
explicit finitely presented sofic group.

The proof follows the central-involution strategy of
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky.  The new ingredient is a one-sided Kazhdan
transport theorem: in every operator-norm asymptotic representation,
one-sided conjugation preserves the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant
of a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup.  This forces every homomorphism into
the unitary group of a norm matrix corona to map a distinguished central
involution to the identity, while an auxiliary Clifford quotient shows
that the involution is nontrivial.

We also prove a tensor-power amplification lemma for operator-norm
models.  It implies that MF is closed in the space of marked groups and
under directed colimits.  Consequently, the existence of a countable
non-MF group is equivalent to the existence of a finitely generated one
and to the existence of a finitely presented one.  Further
consequences include a separable exact
stably finite reduced group $C^*$-algebra that is not MF and a hyperlinear
trace that is not MF.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}


The \emph{MF problem} (MF = matricial field) asks whether every
countable discrete group admits finite-dimensional matrix models whose
multiplicative errors tend to zero in operator norm and which
asymptotically separate every nontrivial element from the identity
\cite{Shulman}.  The separation clause is part of the definition,
as constant identity maps are asymptotically multiplicative for every
group.  Throughout, MF for groups means the
operator-norm property of Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt \cite{CDE};
stronger conventions are discussed after
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}.  To our knowledge no
counterexample was previously known \cite{Schafhauser,Shulman}.  We
construct an explicit eight-generator, forty-one-relator group that is
not MF\@.  The presentation uses separate generators for the three
translations and the three generators of the linear action.  The Tietze
reduction in Section~\ref{sec:group} gives a six-generator,
thirty-two-relator presentation.

By tensor-power amplification (Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-amplification}), the
element-dependent separation in this definition can be made uniform.
Hence MF is closed in the space of marked groups and under directed
colimits (Theorem~\ref{thm:markedclosed}).  It follows that the existence
of a countable non-MF group is equivalent to the existence of a finitely
generated one and to the existence of a finitely presented one
(Section~\ref{sec:prelim}).

Residually finite and LEF groups are MF \cite{CDE}.  Amenable groups are
MF because their reduced group $C^*$-algebras embed into the universal UHF
algebra \cite[Theorem~B]{SchafhauserAF}, using the quasidiagonality of nuclear
$C^*$-algebras \cite{TWW}.

Several nearby notions are stronger.
Schafhauser's group models additionally approximate the left regular
representation \cite{Schafhauser}, and the strong models of
Gao--Kunnawalkam Elayavalli--Manzoor--Patchell recover the regular norm
\cite{GKEMP}.  Shulman's amalgamated-product results concern, in particular,
the MF property of full group $C^*$-algebras \cite{Shulman}.  Each of these
stronger group or $C^*$-algebraic properties implies the group property
used here.

\subsection*{The result}

\begin{mainthm}[an explicit finitely presented non-MF group]\label{thm:A}
Let $E$ be the group on the eight generators
$v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c$ with the forty-one relators displayed in
Definition~\ref{def:E}, and put
\[
 \hat c=tct^{-1},\qquad u=[\,\hat c,v_1],\qquad w=u^{2}
 =[\,\hat c,\,v_1\hat cv_1^{-1}].
\]
The element $w$ is central and satisfies $w^2=1$.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $w\ne1$ in $E$, so $w$ is a central involution;
 \item for every sequence $(d_n)$ of positive integers and every
  homomorphism $\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q((d_n)))$ into the unitary
  group of the corresponding matrix quotient, one has $\Theta(w)=1$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $E$ is not MF, and neither $\Cmax(E)$ nor $\Cred(E)$ is an
MF $C^*$-algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremA}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralTheoremAPackage}{GroupApproximation.LiteralTheoremAPackage.manuscriptTheoremA_package}
\end{mainthm}

The construction also yields a torsion-free finitely presented non-MF group
(Theorem~\ref{thm:torsionfree}).  The MF residual of the explicit group is
computed exactly in Section~\ref{sec:exact-residual}.

\subsection*{Outline of the proof}

The overarching strategy goes back to Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky
\cite[Proposition~1.5]{BDL}: obstruct MF by proving that a distinguished
central involution cannot be separated from the identity by operator-norm
asymptotic representations.  Their argument compresses to the involution's
$(-1)$-eigenspace and invokes operator--Hilbert--Schmidt stability.  The new
ingredient here is a mechanism that supplies the required
Hilbert--Schmidt rigidity directly from one-sided conjugation of a
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup, without assuming operator--Hilbert--Schmidt
stability of the ambient group.

Multiplicative defects are measured in operator norm; asymptotic-commutant
estimates are measured in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Let $L\le H$
have property~\textup{(T)}, and let $t,c\in H$ satisfy
\[
 tLt^{-1}\subseteq L,\qquad [c,L]=1 .
\]
Conjugation by the unitaries in an operator-norm asymptotic
representation $(U_n)$ of $H$ defines an asymptotic representation on
the Hilbert--Schmidt spaces $L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$.  This is where
the operator norm enters: it controls the multiplicative defects of the
conjugation actions.  Property~\textup{(T)} produces the projection onto
the $L$-invariant vectors.  The subgroup inclusion implies
that this projection is dominated by its conjugate under $t$.  The two
projections are equivalent in a finite algebra and therefore equal.
Consequently, conjugation by $t$ preserves the asymptotic commutant of
$L$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
(Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}).

Put $\hat c=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[\,\hat c,a\,]$ for some $a\in L$.
Since $c$ commutes with $L$, the sequence $(U_n(c))$ lies in that
asymptotic commutant.  Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} therefore gives
$\hsnorm{[U_n(\hat c),U_n(\ell)]_{-}}\to0$ for every
$\ell\in L$.  Consequently, in every operator-norm asymptotic
representation $(U_n)$ of $H$,
\[
 \hsnorm{U_n(u)-1}\longrightarrow0 .
\]

Suppose now that $w=u^2$ is central in $H$ with $w^2=1$, and let
$\Theta$ be a homomorphism $H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ with $\Theta(w)\ne1$.
Then $q=\tfrac12(1-\Theta(w))$ is a nonzero projection commuting with
$\Theta(H)$.  Compression to the corresponding matrix corners, equipped
with their normalized traces, gives an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $H$ in which the image of $w$ converges to $-1$ in
operator norm.
There the display above gives $\hsnorm{U_n(u)^2-1}\to0$, while
$\hsnorm{-1-1}=2$.  Hence $\Theta(w)=1$, and if $w\ne1$ then $H$ is not
MF (Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}).  Normalizing the trace on each
corner is essential.  Enlarging a model by an identity block preserves
its operator-norm defects and separations but can make its normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt distances arbitrarily small (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely presented Kazhdan group with a proper
injective endomorphism $\alpha$, let $a\in\Gamma\setminus\alpha(\Gamma)$,
and adjoin $t$ and $c$ subject to $t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)$,
$c^2=1$ and $[c,\gamma]=1$.  In the ascending HNN extension of $\Gamma$
along $\alpha$ the cosets $t\Gamma$ and $at\Gamma$ are distinct, since
$t^{-1}at\in\Gamma$ would give $a\in\alpha(\Gamma)$.  An auxiliary
Clifford quotient witnesses that $w\ne1$.
For a set $X$, the Clifford group $\Cl(X)$ is generated by a central
involution $\zeta$ together with involutions $c_x$, $x\in X$, subject
to $[c_x,c_y]=\zeta$ for $x\ne y$; every permutation of $X$ acts on it
by permuting the $c_x$ and fixing $\zeta$
(Construction~\ref{con:clifford}).  Let the ambient group act in this
way on $\Cl(X)$ for $X$ the coset space, and send $c$ to the generator
$c_x$ at the base coset.  Then $\hat c$ and $a\hat ca^{-1}$ go to the generators
at the two distinct cosets, so $u$ goes to a product of two
anticommuting involutions and $w=u^2$ to $\zeta\ne1$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford}).

Taking
\[
 \Gamma=\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z),\qquad
 \alpha(v,A)=(2v,A),\qquad a=(e_1,I)
\]
gives the group $E$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

Property~\textup{(T)} is used only for the embedded base subgroup $\Gamma$:
the group $E$ itself maps onto $\mathbb Z$ by sending $t$ to $1$ and
every other generator to $0$, and therefore does not have
property~\textup{(T)}.  An exact finite-dimensional counterpart of
the commutant argument, with equality of dimensions in place of
finiteness of the ultraproduct, is Theorem~\ref{thm:exactfd}.

The known implications among the finite approximation properties of
countable groups are
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
 \text{residually finite} & \Longrightarrow & \text{LEF}
   & \Longrightarrow & \text{MF}\\[2pt]
 & & \big\Downarrow & & \\[2pt]
 & & \text{sofic} & \Longrightarrow & \text{hyperlinear.}
\end{array}
\]
The reverse arrows from LEF to residual finiteness, from MF to LEF, and from
soficity to LEF all fail.  The finitary alternating group is locally finite,
hence LEF, but is infinite simple and therefore not residually finite.  Abels'
finitely presented group is MF but
not residually finite \cite[Section~2.14 and Corollary~2.18]{CDE}; since a
finitely presented LEF group is residually finite
\cite[Remark~2.11]{CDE}, it is not LEF.  Finally,
the group $E$ below is sofic but not LEF
(Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:Esofic}).  The
reverse of the bottom arrow,
\textup{hyperlinear}$\,\Rightarrow\,$\textup{sofic}, remains open, as does the
additional implication
\textup{MF}$\,\Rightarrow\,$\textup{hyperlinear}.  The canonical
trace on $\Cmax(E)$ is hyperlinear but not MF
(Theorem~\ref{thm:trace}).

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}

\noindent\emph{MF algebras and traces.}
The MF problem for $C^*$-algebras predates the group question.  Blackadar and
Kirchberg, who introduced MF algebras, remarked that no stably finite
separable $C^*$-algebra was known not to be MF \cite{BK,CDE}.  Their
natural candidate was $\Cred(F_2)$, which Haagerup and
Thorbj{\o}rnsen later proved to be MF \cite{HT}.  The negative solution of the Connes embedding
problem subsequently implied the existence of abstract stably finite
non-MF algebras \cite{MIPRE,FGH}.  To our knowledge no countable group
was known whose reduced or maximal group $C^*$-algebra fails to be MF
\cite{Shulman}.  The maximal-algebra obstruction applies to every countable
group with a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup properly conjugated into itself.
The corresponding Kazhdan projection is strictly dominated by its conjugate,
so the maximal group algebra contains a proper isometry and is not stably
finite, and therefore is not MF (Proposition~\ref{rem:maxinfinite}).

There is a parallel hierarchy for traces.  Brown developed tracial
analogues of finite-dimensional approximation properties
\cite{BrownMeans}, and MF traces were studied by Rainone--Schafhauser
\cite{RainoneSchafhauser}.  We use Shulman's formulation
\cite{ShulmanTraces}, in which hyperlinear and MF traces differ by
whether the algebraic defects are measured in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm or in operator norm.  Several classes are known
for which every hyperlinear trace is MF; for example, Shulman proves
this for cones \cite{ShulmanCones}.  By contrast, the non-MF traces
arising from the failure of the Connes embedding problem do not factor
through tracial ultraproducts of matrix algebras and are not
hyperlinear \cite{MIPRE,Schafhauser}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:trace}
separates the hyperlinear and MF trace classes.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Metric approximation problems.}
In his 2018 ICM address, Thom asked for which Schatten
$p$-norms there exist non-approximable groups \cite[Question~3.11]{Th}.
The case $p=2$ was settled by De~Chiffre, Glebsky, Lubotzky, and Thom
\cite{DGLT}, the cases $1<p<\infty$ by Lubotzky and Oppenheim
\cite{LuO}, and the case $p=1$ by Bachner, Dogon, and Lubotzky
\cite{BDL}.  Those
results left three approximation problems open: whether every countable
group is sofic (permutations in the Hamming metric), hyperlinear
(unitaries in the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm), or MF (unitaries in
operator norm).  The first was answered negatively in 2026: a nonsofic
group was constructed in \cite{OAI}, Fournier-Facio then gave a
torsion-free example \cite{FFF}, and Kun--Thom developed further
wreath-product examples \cite{KT}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} settles the MF
problem negatively; as of August 2026 the hyperlinear problem remains open.
The nonsofic and MF arguments both use a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup
$\Gamma$ satisfying $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$.  In the MF setting,
conjugation by $t$ preserves the Hilbert--Schmidt asymptotic commutant of
$\Gamma$ whenever the multiplicative defects converge to zero in operator
norm.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Related arguments.}
Recent work on nonsofic groups uses one-sided conjugation of a
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup together with an element centralizing that
subgroup \cite{OAI,KT}, building on rigidity results of Kun and Kun--Thom
\cite{Kun16,KT19}.  Alekseev--Thom prove a related centralizer rigidity for
sofic embeddings \cite{AlekseevThom}.  Those results concern Hamming or tracial
approximations.  In the present operator-norm setting, the group acts by
conjugation on the Hilbert--Schmidt space of each matrix block.  Finiteness
of the norm ultraproduct implies that the projection onto the
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup's fixed subspace equals its unitary conjugate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}).

Slofstra's hyperlinear-profile construction uses a Clifford group, a
distinguished central involution, a shift, and an HNN doubling map
\cite[Section~2 and Remark~3.3]{SlofstraProfile}.  He also considers the
elements sent to the identity by every exact finite-dimensional
representation and those sent to the identity by every approximate
representation \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations}.
These subgroups are the exact and approximate analogues of the MF
residual defined below.

Bekka and Bekka--Valette study property~\textup{(T)} through the
conjugation representation $\pi\otimes\bar\pi$ on Hilbert--Schmidt
space \cite{BekkaAmenable,BekkaValette}.  Dadarlat uses normalized
finite-rank projections as almost invariant Hilbert--Schmidt vectors
in an operator-norm approximation argument
\cite[Lemma~3.18 and Proposition~3.19]{Dadarlat}.  The group-MF
framework is that of Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt, who also give,
through Abels' group, an amenable MF group that is not residually
finite \cite[Section~2.14 and Corollary~2.18]{CDE}.

An MF embedding of a group $C^*$-algebra restricts to an MF embedding of
the underlying group.  The converse is open.  An injective homomorphism
$E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ need not extend to a $C^*$-algebra embedding
$\Cred(E)\hookrightarrow\mathcal Q$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} rules out both
embeddings for $E$.

\section{Matrix quotients}\label{sec:prelim}

All groups are discrete and countable.  We use the standard formulation of
property~\textup{(T)} for unitary representations on complex Hilbert
spaces.  For group elements $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$; for operators
$[x,y]_{-}=xy-yx$.  Throughout, $*$-homomorphisms are not assumed
unital unless explicitly stated; in particular, an MF embedding may
be nonunital.  For a unital $C^*$-algebra
$A$ we write $\U(A)$ for its unitary group.  On $M_r(\C)$ we use the
normalized trace $\tr_r$, the operator norm $\opnorm{\cdot}$, and the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm $\hsnorm{x}=\tr_r(x^*x)^{1/2}$.  For a
nonzero projection $q\in M_r(\C)$ we equip the corner $qM_r(\C)q$ with
its own normalized trace.  When the trace must be specified, we write
$\lVert x\rVert_{2,\tr_r}$.
The unnormalized trace is $\operatorname{Tr}=r\cdot\tr_r$ on $M_r(\C)$.
We repeatedly use the inequalities
$\hsnorm{x}\le\opnorm{x}$;
$|\tr_r(x)|\le\opnorm{x}$; and $\hsnorm{uxv}=\hsnorm{x}$ for unitaries
$u,v$.

Given a sequence $(d_n)_{n\in\N}$ of positive integers, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corona}
  \mathcal Q\bigl((d_n)_{n\in\N}\bigr)
  \;=\;\prod_{n\in\N} M_{d_n}(\C)
  \Big/\bigoplus_{n\in\N} M_{d_n}(\C),
\end{equation}
where the product is the bounded $C^*$-product and $\bigoplus_n$ is the
$c_0$-sum, the ideal of sequences with $\opnorm{x_n}\to0$; equivalently,
$\mathcal Q$ is the corona algebra of $\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\C)$.  Write
$q\colon\prod_{n}M_{d_n}(\C)\to\mathcal Q$ for the quotient map.

Following Blackadar--Kirchberg \cite{BK}, a separable $C^*$-algebra is
\emph{MF} if it embeds into an algebra of the form \eqref{eq:corona}.

\emph{Convention on the dimension sequence.}  When the dimension
sequence is not displayed, $\mathcal Q$ denotes
$\mathcal Q((d_n)_{n\in\N})$ for a sequence fixed by the surrounding
discussion.  If no sequence has been fixed, a map into $\mathcal Q$
or $\U(\mathcal Q)$ is understood to allow the sequence $(d_n)$ to
vary; likewise, a quantification over all such maps also ranges over
all dimension sequences.  Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}
shows that nothing changes if the dimensions are required to be
strictly increasing.

Three quotients of $\prod_nM_{d_n}(\C)$ appear below, differing in
which sequences they annihilate:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathcal Q$, where $\opnorm{x_n}\to0$: the norm matrix corona
 \eqref{eq:corona}, and the target of an MF embedding;
\item $B_\omega$, where $\lim_\omega\opnorm{x_n}=0$: the norm
 ultraproduct of Section~\ref{sec:transport}, a finite $C^*$-algebra,
 which contains the Kazhdan projection;
\item the tracial ultraproduct, where $\lim_\omega\hsnorm{x_n}=0$: the
 target for hyperlinearity.
\end{itemize}
The ideals defining $\mathcal Q$ and $B_\omega$ are given by
operator-norm convergence, the third by normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
convergence; Section~\ref{sec:limits} identifies the step that needs
the operator norm.

The next two lemmas are standard (cf.~\cite{BK,Loring98}); we include
the short proofs to fix the constants used later.

\begin{lemma}[lifting]\label{lem:lift}
Every unitary $u\in\mathcal Q$ lifts to a sequence of unitaries.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUnitaryLifting}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lift $u$ to a bounded sequence $(x_n)$.  Unitarity of $q((x_n))$ gives
$\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$.\leanstep{PRE.09}
Once $\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\le\tfrac12$,
the matrix $x_n$ is invertible and the polar correction
$u_n=x_n(x_n^*x_n)^{-1/2}$ is unitary with
$\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\le2\opnorm{x_n}\opnorm{x_n^*x_n-1}\to0$ by
continuous functional calculus.\leanstep{PRE.10}  Set $u_n=1$ at the finitely
many remaining indices.\leanstep{PRE.11}
\end{proof}

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary-corona}
 \mathcal N=\bigl\{(u_n)\in\textstyle\prod_n\U(d_n)
 \ :\ \opnorm{u_n-1}\longrightarrow0\bigr\},
\end{equation}
a normal subgroup of $\prod_n\U(d_n)$.

\begin{lemma}[unitaries in the quotient]\label{lem:unitarycorona}
For every sequence $(d_n)$, with $\mathcal N$ as in
\eqref{eq:unitary-corona}, the map
\[
 \kappa_{(d_n)}\colon
 \prod_{n}\U(d_n)\big/\mathcal N\longrightarrow\U(\mathcal Q),
 \qquad [(u_n)]\longmapsto q((u_n)),
\]
is a canonical group isomorphism.  Thus a homomorphism
$H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ may be represented by unitary lifts $U_n(g)$
whose multiplicative defects tend to $0$ in operator norm, with two
lift sequences identified when their quotient tends to $1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUnitaryCoronaEquivalence}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The kernel of $(u_n)\mapsto q((u_n))$ is exactly $\mathcal N$, so the
induced map is injective.\leanstep{PRE.13}
Surjectivity is Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}.\leanstep{PRE.14}
\end{proof}

An \emph{operator-norm asymptotic representation} of $H$ is a sequence of
maps $U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ such that $\opnorm{U_n(g)U_n(h)-U_n(gh)}\to0$
for all $g,h\in H$.  Taking
$g=h=1$ gives $\opnorm{U_n(1)-1}\to0$ automatically, since $U_n(1)$ is
unitary.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}, choosing unitary lifts
$U_n(g)$ of $\Theta(g)$ turns a homomorphism
$\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ into such a sequence, and every such
sequence arises this way.  A countable group is \emph{MF} if it admits
an injective homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ for some sequence of
dimensions.

For a finite set $F\subseteq H$, a separation constant $\delta>0$,
and a tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, an \emph{approximate unitary
representation} is a map $V\colon H\to\U(r)$, for some $r\ge1$, such
that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \opnorm{V(g)V(h)-V(gh)}&\le\varepsilon &&(g,h\in F),\\
  \opnorm{V(g)-V(h)}&\ge\delta &&(g\ne h\in F).
 \end{aligned}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Equivalent formulations of the MF property]
\label{prop:mf-equivalences}
For every countable group, the group-MF definition above --- an injective
homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ --- is
equivalent to the definition by sequences of unitaries.  Both are
equivalent to the existence of approximate unitary representations with
separation constant $1$, and allowing arbitrary positive dimensions is
equivalent to requiring the dimensions to be strictly increasing.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMFDefinitionEquivalences}
\end{proposition}

The nontrivial point in this equivalence is that an element-dependent
positive separation in the corona can be made uniform, by the following
operator-norm amplification.

\begin{lemma}[tensor amplification]\label{lem:tensor-amplification}
Let $u,v\in\U(d)$ and $\opnorm{u-v}\ge\delta>0$.  If
$N\in\N$ satisfies $8<N\delta^2$, then for some $1\le p\le N$,
\[
 \opnorm{u^{\otimes p}-v^{\otimes p}}\ge1.
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptTensorAmplification}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $z=u^*v$.  It has a spectral value $e^{2\pi i\alpha}$ with
$|1-e^{2\pi i\alpha}|\ge\delta$.  If
$\alpha'=\min(\{\alpha\},1-\{\alpha\})$, then
$2\sin(\pi\alpha')\ge\delta$.  Successive multiples move by
$\pm\alpha'$ modulo $1$, so some
$p\le\lceil1/(6\alpha')\rceil\le\pi/(3\delta)+1$ has
$\{p\alpha\}\in[1/6,5/6]$.  Since $e^{2\pi ip\alpha}$ belongs to the
spectrum of $z^{\otimes p}$,
\[
 \opnorm{u^{\otimes p}-v^{\otimes p}}
 \ge |1-e^{2\pi ip\alpha}|\ge1.
\]
Finally, $\delta\le2$ for two unitaries, and
$8<N\delta^2$ therefore gives $\pi/(3\delta)+1<N$.
\end{proof}

Telescoping the difference of two $p$-fold tensor products of
contractions shows that a $p$th tensor power multiplies every
multiplicative defect by at most $p$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} gives the equivalence between a homomorphism
to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ and an operator-norm asymptotic representation.
Positive dimensions can be made strictly increasing by replacing the
$n$th model $V_n$ with
\[
 I_{d_0}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{d_{n-1}}\oplus V_n.
\]
Block-diagonal operator norms are maxima, so this changes neither defects
nor separations.

Suppose $\rho\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q((d_n)))$ is injective and choose
unitary lifts $u_n(g)$.  Fix a finite $F\subseteq G$ and
$\varepsilon>0$.  For each $g\ne h$ in $F$, injectivity supplies
$\delta_{g,h}>0$ and arbitrarily large coordinates with
$\opnorm{u_n(g)-u_n(h)}\ge\delta_{g,h}$.  Choose
$N_{g,h}$ with $8<N_{g,h}\delta_{g,h}^2$, and choose such a coordinate
where every required multiplicative defect is at most
$\varepsilon/N_{g,h}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-amplification} gives a
tensor power $p\le N_{g,h}$ which separates this pair by at least $1$,
while every required defect remains at most $\varepsilon$.  The block sum
of these pair-specific models has defect at most $\varepsilon$ and
separates every distinct pair in $F$ by at least $1$.  If $|F|\le1$, the
constant one-dimensional model suffices.

Conversely, choose constant-$1$ local models along an exhaustion of $G$
with errors tending to zero.  Their classes define a homomorphism into
$\prod_n\U(r_n)/\mathcal N$; eventual separation by $1$ makes it injective.
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} identifies this quotient with
$\U(\mathcal Q((r_n)))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the MF locus is closed]\label{thm:markedclosed}
For each $k\ge1$, the MF groups form a closed subset of the space of
$k$-marked groups.  Equivalently, every non-MF $k$-marked group has a
finite-radius obstruction: all marked groups agreeing with it on a
sufficiently large word ball are non-MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.isClosed_operatorMFLocus}
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.isOpen_compl_operatorMFLocus}
\leanverified{Sofic/MarkedMFClosed}{GroupApproximation.MarkedGroupSpace.exists_wordBall_cylinder_subset_compl_operatorMFLocus}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}, failure of MF is witnessed by a
finite set and a positive tolerance for which the constant-$1$ local model
does not exist.  Choose words representing this finite set and all products
appearing in its multiplicativity conditions.  The relevant equalities and
inequalities among these words determine a basic clopen neighborhood, and
the same local obstruction holds throughout it.  A word ball containing all
of these words gives the finite-radius formulation.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Directed-colimit permanence.}
The class of countable MF groups is closed under directed colimits.

\begin{proof}
Let $G=\varinjlim_iG_i$ with every $G_i$ MF.  Given finite
$F\subseteq G$ and $\varepsilon>0$, choose a stage containing lifts of
all elements and products occurring in the local conditions on $F$, and
then a later stage at which every equality and inequality among those
finitely many elements has stabilized to its truth value in $G$.
Composing a constant-$1$ local model at that stage with the lifts gives
one with the prescribed data on $F$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences} gives that $G$ is MF.
\end{proof}

The amplification step plays the role that rank amplification plays in
the direct-limit theorem for linear sofic groups
\cite{ArzhantsevaPaunescu}.

\noindent\textbf{Finite-presentation reduction.}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a countable non-MF group exists;
\item a finitely generated non-MF group exists;
\item a finitely presented non-MF group exists.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proof}
If $G$ is non-MF, a failed local condition is witnessed on a finite set
$F\subseteq G$.  The subgroup $H=\langle F\rangle$ is non-MF, since an MF
model of $H$ on that finite data would contradict the obstruction in $G$.

Write $H=F_k/N$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:markedclosed}, a basic cylinder
around $H$, determined by finitely many words $W\subseteq F_k$, consists
entirely of non-MF groups.  Let $R_+$ be the words in $W$ which are trivial
in $H$, and put $P=F_k/\langle\!\langle R_+\rangle\!\rangle$.  Then $P$ is
finitely presented and maps onto $H$.  Every required equality holds in
$P$, while every word of $W$ nontrivial in $H$ remains nontrivial in $P$
because of the quotient map $P\twoheadrightarrow H$.  Thus $P$ lies in
the same non-MF cylinder.  The remaining implications are immediate.
\end{proof}

Two stronger conventions also occur in the literature.  The maps in
\cite{GKEMP} must recover the canonical trace and the left-regular norm of
every group-ring element.  The purely matricial field (PMF) property of
\cite[Definition~1.2]{MdlS} requires finite-dimensional representations
whose group-ring norms converge to the regular norms.

\section{One-sided conjugation in matrix models}\label{sec:transport}

One-sided conjugation has analogous consequences in finite groups,
finite-dimensional representations, and norm ultraproducts.  For a
homomorphism $\pi$ to a finite group,
$\pi(t\Gamma t^{-1})=\pi(\Gamma)$, since the left side is a conjugate
of the right side contained in it and the two are finite of the same
cardinality.  In finite dimensions, the commutant $C$ of the base
satisfies $C\subseteq\pi(t)C\pi(t)^{-1}$; equality follows because
the two spaces have the same dimension.  In a norm ultraproduct, the
fixed-space projection satisfies $P\le VPV^*$ when $L\le H$ and
$tLt^{-1}\subseteq L$, where $P$ is the projection onto the $L$-fixed
vectors and $V$ represents $t$; the two
projections are Murray--von Neumann equivalent, and finiteness of the
ultraproduct gives $P=VPV^*$.

The two lemmas of this section are standard facts about finite
$C^*$-algebras; see, e.g., \cite{Bla06}.

\begin{lemma}[comparison in a finite algebra]\label{lem:finitecompare}
Let $A$ be a finite unital $C^*$-algebra and let $p,q\in A$ be
projections with $p\le q$ and $p\sim q$.  Then $p=q$.
\leanverified{Sofic/UltraproductKazhdanTransport}{GroupApproximation.kt_10_finiteness_reverses}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $r\in A$ satisfy $r^*r=q$ and $rr^*=p$.  From $r^*r=q$ we get
$r=rq$.  Since $p\le q$ we have $qp=p$, so
\[
 pr=rr^*r=rq=r,\qquad qr=q(pr)=(qp)r=pr=r,
\]
and $r=qr$ as well.  Hence the cross terms in
$\sigma^*\sigma$ vanish for $\sigma=r+(1-q)$, and
$\sigma^*\sigma=q+(1-q)=1$, so $\sigma$ is an isometry.  Since $A$ is
finite, $\sigma$ is unitary, and $\sigma\sigma^*=p+(1-q)=1$ gives
$p=q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[finiteness of a norm quotient of matrix blocks]%
\label{lem:ultrafinite}
Let $(K_n)$ be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and let
\[
 B_{\mathrm c}=\prod_nB(K_n)\Big/\bigoplus_nB(K_n)
\]
be the quotient of the bounded family algebra by the $c_0$-sum.  Then
$B_{\mathrm c}$ is a finite $C^*$-algebra: every $\sigma\in
B_{\mathrm c}$ with $\sigma^*\sigma=1$ satisfies $\sigma\sigma^*=1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/UltraproductDedekindFinite}{GroupApproximation.kt_06_ultraproduct_finite}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lift $\sigma$ to a bounded family $(\sigma_n)$.  Then
$\opnorm{\sigma_n^*\sigma_n-1}\to0$, so for all large $n$ the Gram
defect is at most $\tfrac12$ and $\sigma_n^*\sigma_n$ is invertible;
each $\sigma_n$ acts on a finite-dimensional space, so polar correction
replaces it by a unitary $w_n=\sigma_n(\sigma_n^*\sigma_n)^{-1/2}$ with
$\opnorm{\sigma_n-w_n}\to0$.  Hence $\sigma$ is the class of $(w_n)$,
and $w_nw_n^*=1$ at every such $n$ gives $\sigma\sigma^*=1$.
\end{proof}

Let $\omega$ be a free ultrafilter on $\N$, let $K_\omega$ be the
Hilbert-space ultraproduct of the $K_n$, and let
$B_\omega=\prod_\omega B(K_n)$ be the norm ultraproduct, acting on
$K_\omega$ by $[A_n]_\omega[\xi_n]_\omega=[A_n\xi_n]_\omega$.  The
same polar-correction argument shows that $B_\omega$ is finite, with
ordinary eventual convergence replaced by convergence along $\omega$.
Its action on $K_\omega$ is faithful: if $A=[A_n]_\omega\ne0$ then
$\lim_\omega\opnorm{A_n}=\delta>0$, and unit vectors $\xi_n$ with
$\lVert A_n\xi_n\rVert\ge\opnorm{A_n}-1/n$ give
$\lVert A[\xi_n]_\omega\rVert=\delta$.  Hence for projections
$P,Q\in B_\omega$ the inclusion
$\operatorname{ran}P\subseteq\operatorname{ran}Q$ makes $QP$ and $P$
act identically on $K_\omega$, so $QP=P$, that is, $P\le Q$; the
converse is immediate.

\subsection*{The exact case}

% LABELS.  `mainthm` numbers by letter in order of appearance, so a label
% spelled `thm:X` is a promise about position that the source cannot keep.
% This one is an ordinary numbered theorem, not a lettered one.  The legacy
% aliases `thm:B`, `thm:C`, `thm:D`, `thm:E` are gone; label after the
% content, never after the expected letter.
\begin{theorem}[the finite-dimensional case]\label{thm:exactfd}
Let $k$ be a field, $V$ a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, $H$ a
group, $\Gamma\le H$ a subgroup, $t,c\in H$, and $a\in\Gamma$.  Assume
that $t\Gamma t^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma$ and $c\gamma=\gamma c$ for all
$\gamma\in\Gamma$.  Then every homomorphism $\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$
satisfies
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)\;=\;1 .
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptTheoremB}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 C=\{x\in\End_k(V):\pi(\gamma)x=x\pi(\gamma)\ \text{for all}\
 \gamma\in\Gamma\}
\]
be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$, a linear subspace of $\End_k(V)$, and
write $\Phi(x)=\pi(t)\,x\,\pi(t)^{-1}$ for conjugation by $\pi(t)$, a
linear automorphism of $\End_k(V)$.

We claim $C\subseteq\Phi(C)$.  Let $x\in C$ and put
$y=\pi(t)^{-1}x\,\pi(t)$, so that $x=\Phi(y)$.  For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ we
have $t\gamma t^{-1}\in\Gamma$, hence $\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})$ commutes
with $x$, and therefore
\[
  \pi(\gamma)\,y
  = \pi(t)^{-1}\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,x\,\pi(t)
  = \pi(t)^{-1}\,x\,\pi(t\gamma t^{-1})\,\pi(t)
  = y\,\pi(\gamma).
\]
Thus $y\in C$, proving the claim.  Since $\Phi$ is a linear isomorphism,
$\dim_k\Phi(C)=\dim_kC<\infty$, and the inclusion $C\subseteq\Phi(C)$
forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinning}
  C\;=\;\Phi(C)\;=\;\pi(t)\,C\,\pi(t)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Now $\pi(c)\in C$ because $c$ centralizes $\Gamma$, so
$\pi(tct^{-1})=\Phi(\pi(c))\in C$ by \eqref{eq:pinning}.  Since
$a\in\Gamma$, the operator $\pi(tct^{-1})$ commutes with $\pi(a)$, so
$\pi\bigl(a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr)=\pi(tct^{-1})$ and
\[
  \pi\bigl(\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\;a(tct^{-1})a^{-1}\bigr]\bigr)
  =\bigl[\,\pi(tct^{-1}),\;\pi(tct^{-1})\bigr]=1 . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

Every finite-dimensional linear representation of the group $E$ of
Definition~\ref{def:E} below, over any field, maps $w$ to the identity;
consequently every homomorphism from $E$ to
a finite group does as well (Corollary~\ref{cor:notRFD}).

In an MF model, multiplicativity is only asymptotic, so the coordinate
fixed spaces need not be invariant.  Because the multiplicative
defects tend to $0$ in operator norm, the adjoint actions on the
Hilbert--Schmidt spaces are asymptotically multiplicative, and the
corresponding Kazhdan projection belongs to a finite norm ultraproduct,
where $P\le VPV^*$ and $P\sim VPV^*$ imply $P=VPV^*$.
The property~\textup{(T)} hypothesis is
essential: the group $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:necessity} has cyclic base.  Its distinguished word is
trivial in every finite-dimensional representation over every field,
but some homomorphism $E_{\mathrm{BS}}\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps that word
to a nontrivial element.

\subsection*{The asymptotic case}

Recall that a \emph{Kazhdan pair} for a property-\textup{(T)} group $L$
is a finite set $S\subseteq L$ together with a constant $\kappa>0$ such
that, in every unitary representation $\pi$ of $L$ with no nonzero
invariant vector, every unit vector $\xi$ satisfies
$\lVert\pi(a)\xi-\xi\rVert\ge\kappa$ for some $a\in S$.
The next lemma is the spectral-gap form of the Kazhdan projection, which
goes back to Akemann and Walter \cite{AW81}; see \cite{DN19} for a
general treatment.

\begin{lemma}[Kazhdan spectral gap]\label{lem:kazhdan-gap}
Let $L$ be a group with property~\textup{(T)}, let $S\subseteq L$ be a
finite symmetric Kazhdan set containing $1$ with Kazhdan constant
$\kappa\in(0,1]$, and let $\pi$ be a unitary representation of $L$ in a
unital $C^*$-algebra.  Then the average
$h=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{a\in S}\pi(a)$ is self-adjoint and
\[
 \operatorname{sp}(h)\subseteq
 \Bigl[-1,\;1-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2|S|}\Bigr]\cup\{1\}.
\]
\leanverified{Sofic/UltraproductKazhdanProjection}{GroupApproximation.UltraproductKazhdanProjection.manuscriptKazhdanSpectralGap}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Symmetry of $S$ makes $h$ self-adjoint, and $\opnorm h\le1$ puts its
spectrum in $[-1,1]$.  Let $\mu\in\operatorname{sp}(h)$ with $\mu\ne1$.
Evaluation at $\mu$ is a state on $C^*(1,h)$; extend it to a state
$\varphi$ on the ambient $C^*$-algebra.  Let
$\sigma_\varphi$ be its GNS representation and set
$\widetilde\pi=\sigma_\varphi\circ\pi$.  Since
$\varphi((h-\mu)^2)=0$,
\[
 \lVert(\sigma_\varphi(h)-\mu)\xi_\varphi\rVert^2
 =\varphi((h-\mu)^2)=0.
\]
Thus the cyclic vector $\xi=\xi_\varphi$ is a unit vector with
$\sigma_\varphi(h)\xi=\mu\xi$.  Split
$\xi=\xi_0+\xi_1$ along the $\widetilde\pi(L)$-invariant vectors and
their orthocomplement, both invariant under $\sigma_\varphi(h)$.  On the
first summand $\sigma_\varphi(h)=1$, so $\mu\ne1$ forces $\xi_0=0$.  Now
\[
 1-\mu=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{a\in S}
  \bigl(1-\operatorname{Re}\langle\widetilde\pi(a)\xi,\xi\rangle\bigr)
 =\frac1{2|S|}\sum_{a\in S}\lVert\widetilde\pi(a)\xi-\xi\rVert^{2},
\]
and $\widetilde\pi$ has no nonzero invariant vector on the orthocomplement,
so $\lVert\widetilde\pi(a)\xi-\xi\rVert\ge\kappa$ for some $a\in S$ and
$1-\mu\ge\kappa^{2}/(2|S|)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[one-sided conjugation preserves asymptotic commutants]
\label{thm:kazhdan-transport}
Let $H$ be a group, let $L\le H$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$t\in H$, and let $(d_n)$ be a sequence of positive integers.  Suppose
\[
 tLt^{-1}\subseteq L.
\]
Let $U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic representation.
If $(x_n)$ is uniformly operator-norm bounded and asymptotically
centralizes $L$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm,
\[
 \hsnorm{[x_n,U_n(\ell)]_{-}}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\ell\in L),
\]
then the conjugated sequence does as well:
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptSubgroupSpecializations}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptKazhdanTransportSubgroup}
\[
\hsnorm{[U_n(t)x_nU_n(t)^*,U_n(\ell)]_{-}}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\ell\in L).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the conclusion fails: there are $\ell_0\in L$,
$\delta>0$, and an infinite set $I\subseteq\N$ with
$\hsnorm{[U_n(t)x_nU_n(t)^*,U_n(\ell_0)]_{-}}\ge\delta$ for
$n\in I$.  Fix a free ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\N$ with $I\in\omega$.

\emph{The adjoint model.}  Regard $M_{d_n}(\C)$ as the Hilbert space
$K_n=L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$ and let each group element act on it
by conjugation, $\Ad U_n(g)\,\xi=U_n(g)\,\xi\,U_n(g)^*$.
The operator-norm almost multiplicativity of $U_n$ implies that
$g\mapsto\Ad U_n(g)$ is an operator-norm asymptotic representation on
these Hilbert spaces.

\emph{The ultraproduct.}  Let $K_\omega$ and
$B_\omega=\prod_\omega B(K_n)$ be as in Section~\ref{sec:transport};
write $[\,\cdot\,]_\omega$ for classes in either.  The action of
$B_\omega$ on $K_\omega$ is faithful, and $B_\omega$ is finite: every
isometry in it is unitary, by the ultrafilter version of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ultrafinite}.  For projections in $B_\omega$, inclusion
of ranges in $K_\omega$ is equivalent to the projection order.
Asymptotic multiplicativity implies that
\[
 \pi\colon H\longrightarrow\U(B_\omega),\qquad
 \pi(g)=[\Ad U_n(g)]_\omega,
\]
a homomorphism.

\emph{The projection onto the fixed subspace.}  Choose a finite
symmetric Kazhdan set $S\subseteq L$ containing $1$, with Kazhdan
constant $\kappa\in(0,1]$, put $h=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s'\in S}\pi(s')$,
and let $\mathrm{Fix}\subseteq K_\omega$ be the subspace of vectors
fixed by every $\pi(\ell)$, $\ell\in L$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:kazhdan-gap},
$1$ is isolated in $\operatorname{sp}(h)$, so the spectral projection
$P$ of $h$ at $1$ belongs to $B_\omega$ by continuous functional
calculus; since $h=1$ exactly on $\mathrm{Fix}$, its range is
$\mathrm{Fix}$.

\emph{The two projections agree.}  Let $V=\pi(t)$ and $Q=VPV^*$.  Since
$tLt^{-1}\subseteq L$, every vector of
$\mathrm{Fix}$ is fixed by each $\pi(t\ell t^{-1})$, $\ell\in L$; and $\eta$
is fixed by all $\pi(t\ell t^{-1})$, $\ell\in L$, exactly when
$V^*\eta\in\mathrm{Fix}$.  Hence
$\operatorname{ran}P=\mathrm{Fix}\subseteq V\,\mathrm{Fix}
=\operatorname{ran}Q$, so $P\le Q$.  The
element $r=V^*Q$ satisfies $r^*r=Q$ and $rr^*=V^*QV=P$, so $P\sim Q$.
Since $B_\omega$ is finite, Lemma~\ref{lem:finitecompare} gives $Q=P$.

\emph{Conclusion.}  Let $\xi=[x_n]_\omega\in K_\omega$; the uniform
operator-norm bound makes this class well defined.  By unitary invariance of the normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm, the commutator hypothesis says exactly that each
$\pi(\ell)$, $\ell\in L$, fixes $\xi$.  Thus $\xi\in\mathrm{Fix}$.  Since $Q=P$, we obtain
$V\xi\in V\,\mathrm{Fix}=\operatorname{ran}Q=\operatorname{ran}P
=\mathrm{Fix}$.
The matrix $U_n(t)x_nU_n(t)^*$ is the vector $\Ad U_n(t)\,x_n$ of
$K_n$, so, again by unitary invariance,
\[
 \hsnorm{[U_n(t)x_nU_n(t)^*,U_n(\ell)]_{-}}
 =\bigl\lVert(\Ad U_n(\ell)-1)\,\Ad U_n(t)\,x_n\bigr\rVert
 \longrightarrow0
\]
along $\omega$, for every $\ell\in L$.  For $\ell=\ell_0$ this
contradicts $I\in\omega$.
\end{proof}

\section{The central involution obstruction}\label{sec:sign}

This section follows the central-involution strategy of
Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky \cite[Proposition~1.5]{BDL}.  In their setting,
operator--Hilbert--Schmidt stability replaces the compressed asymptotic
representation by nearby genuine finite-dimensional representations,
which map the involution to the identity.  Here
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} instead forces the word whose square
is the involution to be Hilbert--Schmidt close to the identity on the
compressed corner.

Let $w$ be a central involution of $H$ and let $\Theta$ be a
homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ with $\Theta(w)\ne1$.  Then
$\Theta(w)$ is a self-adjoint unitary commuting with $\Theta(H)$, and
$q=\tfrac12(1-\Theta(w))$ is a nonzero projection on whose corner
$q\mathcal Q q$ the compression of $\Theta(w)$ is $-q$ exactly.  The
argument below, and the generalizations in Section~\ref{sec:conj},
compress a matrix model of $H$ to the corners cut out by coordinate
lifts of such a projection.

\begin{lemma}[compression to an asymptotically central corner]
\label{lem:corner}
Let $H$ be a group, let $V_{g,n}\in\U(d_n)$ be an operator-norm
asymptotic representation of $H$, and let $q_n\in M_{d_n}(\C)$ be
nonzero projections with
\[
 \opnorm{[q_n,V_{g,n}]_{-}}\longrightarrow0\qquad(g\in H).
\]
Write $r_n=\operatorname{rank}q_n\ge1$ and identify
$q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\cong M_{r_n}(\C)$.  Then there are unitaries
$W_{g,n}\in\U(r_n)$ with
\[
 \opnorm{W_{g,n}-q_nV_{g,n}q_n}\longrightarrow0\qquad(g\in H),
\]
and $(W_{g,n})$ is an operator-norm asymptotic representation of $H$
on the corners.
\leanverified{Analysis/PrintedCornerCompression}{GroupApproximation.PrintedCornerCompression.manuscriptCornerAsymptoticRepresentation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $g$, abbreviate $e=q_n$, $a=V_{g,n}$ and
$\delta=\opnorm{[e,a]_{-}}$, and note that the unit of the corner is
$e$.  From $eae-ae=[e,a]_{-}e$ and
$ea^*-a^*e=-\bigl([e,a]_{-}\bigr)^*$ we get
$\opnorm{eae-ae}\le\delta$ and $\opnorm{ea^*-a^*e}\le\delta$, so
\[
 \opnorm{(eae)^*(eae)-e}
 =\opnorm{ea^*(ea-ae)e}\le\delta ,
\]
since $a^*a=1$.  Once $2\delta\le\tfrac12$ the corner element
$eae$ is invertible in $M_{r_n}(\C)$.  Its polar correction
$W_{g,n}=eae\,\bigl((eae)^*(eae)\bigr)^{-1/2}$ is unitary with
$\opnorm{W_{g,n}-eae}\le4\delta$ by the estimate in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}.  Set $W_{g,n}=1$ at the finitely many remaining
indices.  For $g,h\in H$ write $b=V_{h,n}$ and
$\varepsilon=\opnorm{ab-V_{gh,n}}$.  Then
\[
 \opnorm{(eae)(ebe)-eV_{gh,n}e}
 \le\opnorm{ea(eb-be)e}+\opnorm{e(ab-V_{gh,n})e}
 \le\opnorm{[e,b]_{-}}+\varepsilon ,
\]
which tends to $0$.  Adding the three polar corrections gives
\[
 \opnorm{W_{g,n}W_{h,n}-W_{gh,n}}\longrightarrow0 . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The rounding, compression and polar-correction estimates of
Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} follow Bachner--Dogon--Lubotzky
\cite[Lemmas~2.2--2.3, Proposition~2.4, and the proof of
Proposition~1.5]{BDL}.  Compression to a negative eigenspace appears
in Slofstra--Vidick \cite[proof of
Proposition~2.7]{SlofstraVidick}.  The finite normal case,
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, uses the character-isotypic corner of
\cite[proof of Proposition~1.6]{BDL} together with one-sided
conjugation of a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup.

\begin{theorem}[central involution obstruction]\label{thm:sign-criterion}
Let $H$ be a countable group, let $L\le H$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let $t,c\in H$, and suppose
\[
 tLt^{-1}\subseteq L,
 \qquad [c,L]=1.
\]
Put $\hat c=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[\hat c,a]$ for some $a\in L$.
If $w:=u^2$ is a nontrivial central involution of $H$, then every
homomorphism $H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps
$w$ to the identity, and $H$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptSubgroupSpecializations}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptCentralSignCriterionSubgroup}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a homomorphism and
suppose $\Theta(w)\ne1$.

\emph{The $(-1)$-spectral corner.}  Choose coordinate unitary lifts
$(V_{g,n})$ of $\Theta$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}) and put
$p_n=\tfrac12(1+V_{w,n})$, the averaging operator of the
two-element subgroup $F=\{1,w\}$.  Because the lifts are
asymptotically multiplicative and $w^2=1$, we have
$\opnorm{V_{w,n}^2-1}\to0$ and hence
$\opnorm{V_{w,n}^*-V_{w,n}}\to0$.  Set
\[
 a_n=\tfrac12(V_{w,n}+V_{w,n}^*),\qquad
 e_n=\tfrac12(1+a_n).
\]
Then $e_n=e_n^*$, $\opnorm{e_n-p_n}\to0$, and
$\opnorm{e_n^2-e_n}\to0$.  The spectrum of $e_n$ therefore concentrates
near $\{0,1\}$, and spectral rounding at $\tfrac12$ gives projections at
operator-norm distance $o(1)$ from $p_n$.  Since $w$ is
central, these projections asymptotically commute with every lift of
$\Theta(H)$.  Let $q_n$ be their complements.  If
$q_n$ vanished for all large $n$ the averages would converge to $1$ and
$\Theta(w)$ would be $1$; so $q_n\ne0$ along infinitely
many coordinates, which we retain and relabel by $\N$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} now gives an operator-norm asymptotic
representation $(W_{g,n})$ of $H$ on the corners
$q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\cong M_{r_n}(\C)$.  On those corners
$q_nV_{w,n}q_n=-q_n+o(1)$, because $q_n$ is the complement of a
projection within $o(1)$ of $\tfrac12(1+V_{w,n})$, so
\[
 \opnorm{W_{w,n}+1}\longrightarrow0 .
\]
By the standing corner-trace convention, all estimates below use
$\tr_{r_n}$ and are independent of the ratio $r_n/d_n$.

\emph{The commutant on the corner.}  Since $c$ centralizes
$L$, the sequence $(W_{c,n})$ centralizes
$(W_{\ell,n})$, $\ell\in L$, asymptotically in operator norm, hence in
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} applied to $x_n=W_{c,n}$, the
conjugated sequence $(W_{\hat c,n})$ lies in the same asymptotic commutant.
Therefore $\hsnorm{W_{u,n}-1}\to0$, and squaring gives
$\hsnorm{W_{u,n}^2-1}\to0$.

\emph{The contradiction.}  The relation $w=u^2$ gives
$\opnorm{W_{u,n}^2-W_{w,n}}\to0$, while
$W_{w,n}\to-1$ in operator norm and therefore also in
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Hence $\hsnorm{W_{u,n}^2+1}\to0$
and $\hsnorm{W_{u,n}^2-1}\to0$.  The triangle inequality would then
give $\hsnorm{2\cdot1}\to0$, contradicting $\hsnorm{2\cdot1}=2$.
Thus $\Theta(w)=1$.  Since $w\ne1$, every homomorphism
$H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ has nontrivial kernel, so $H$ is not MF.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} replaces $\{1,w\}$ by any finite normal
subgroup of the normal closure of the commutators
$[tct^{-1},\ell]$, $\ell\in L$.  Replacing the two-element
average by a Kazhdan spectral projection gives the analogous result for
normal property-\textup{(T)} subgroups, including infinite, noncentral, and
torsion-free ones (Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan}).

\section{The basic non-MF construction}\label{sec:construction}

\begin{theorem}[the basic construction]
\label{thm:kazhdan-clifford}
Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely presented group with property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\alpha\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Gamma$ be an injective endomorphism, and
choose $a\in\Gamma\setminus\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Define
$E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ by adjoining elements $t,c$ and imposing
\[
 t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma),\qquad c^2=1,\qquad [c,\gamma]=1
 \quad(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]
Put
\[
 \hat c=tct^{-1},\qquad u=[\hat c,a],\qquad w=u^2=[\hat c,a\hat ca^{-1}],
\]
and impose centrality of $w$.
More explicitly, for a finite generating set $S_\Gamma$ it is enough to
impose the conjugation and commutation relations for $s\in S_\Gamma$, together with
\[
 [w,s]=1\quad(s\in S_\Gamma),\qquad [w,t]=[w,c]=1.
\]
The group $E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is finitely presented,
the canonical map $\Gamma\to E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is injective, and $w$ is a
nontrivial central involution.  Every homomorphism
$E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps $w$ to $1$, so
$E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/KazhdanCliffordConstruction}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanCliffordConstruction.kazhdanCliffordConstruction}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite presentability follows from the relative presentation.  Start
with the free product of $\Gamma$ and the free group on $t,c$, and
quotient by the finitely many displayed relations for a finite
generating set of $\Gamma$.

The relation $w^2=1$ need not be imposed.  Put $b=a\hat ca^{-1}$.  Both $\hat c$
and $b$ are involutions, $u=[\hat c,a]=\hat cb$, and $w=u^2=(\hat cb)^2$.
Since $\hat c^2=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:involution-reversal}
 \hat c\,w\,\hat c^{-1}=(b\hat c)^2=w^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Centrality also gives $\hat c w\hat c^{-1}=w$, whence $w=w^{-1}$ and
$w^2=1$.

For nontriviality, consider the ascending HNN extension
\[
 G=\langle\Gamma,t\mid t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma)\rangle
\]
and use Britton's lemma to identify $\Gamma$ with its canonical copy in
$G$.

Let $X=G/\Gamma$ be its coset space.  The Clifford
group $\Cl(X)$ is generated by a central involution $\zeta$ and
involutions $c_x$, $x\in X$, subject to $[c_x,c_y]=\zeta$ for
$x\ne y$ (Construction~\ref{con:clifford}).  Every permutation of $X$
acts on $\Cl(X)$ by permuting the $c_x$ and fixing $\zeta$.  Let $G$ act
in this way and map
$c$ to $c_\Gamma$.  Then
\[
 \hat c\longmapsto c_{t\Gamma},\qquad
 a\hat ca^{-1}\longmapsto c_{at\Gamma}.
\]
The two cosets are distinct: equality would give
$t^{-1}at\in\Gamma$, equivalently $a\in\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Hence the two
Clifford generators anticommute and
\[
 w\longmapsto(c_{t\Gamma}c_{at\Gamma})^2=\zeta\ne1 .
\]
The sign $\zeta$ is central, so the map respects every defining relation.
It follows both that $w\ne1$ and that the canonical map of
$\Gamma$ is injective.

Thus $w=u^2$ is a nontrivial central involution, and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion} every homomorphism to
$\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps $w$ to the identity.  Since $w\ne1$, every such
homomorphism has nontrivial kernel.
\end{proof}

Finite presentability of $\Gamma$ is used only to make
$E(\Gamma,\alpha,a)$ finitely presented; for the rest, $\Gamma$ may be
any property-\textup{(T)} group with a proper injective endomorphism.

\section{The explicit affine example}\label{sec:example}

Specialize Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-data}
 \Gamma=\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb Z),\qquad
 \alpha(v,A)=(2v,A),\qquad a=(e_1,I).
\end{equation}
The affine group $\Gamma$ is finitely presented, and it has
property~\textup{(T)} because it is a lattice in
$\mathbb R^3\rtimes\SL_3(\mathbb R)$
\cite[Corollary~1.4.16 and Theorem~1.7.1]{BHV}; see also \cite{Burger}.
Moreover,
Section~\ref{sec:group} identifies it with the presentation used below.
The endomorphism $\alpha$ is injective because doubling is injective
on $\mathbb Z^3$, and it is not surjective because
$e_1\notin2\mathbb Z^3$.  The same observation gives
$a\notin\alpha(\Gamma)$.  Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford}
applies.  Relative to $\Gamma$, the resulting group has the finite
presentation
\[
 E=\left\langle\,\Gamma,t,c \ \middle|\
 \begin{gathered}
  t\gamma t^{-1}=\alpha(\gamma),\quad
  [c,\gamma]=1\quad(\gamma\in S_\Gamma),\\
  c^2=1,\qquad [w,g]=1
 \end{gathered}\,\right\rangle,
\]
where $S_\Gamma$ is a finite generating set of $\Gamma$, $g$ runs over
the generators, and $w=[\,tct^{-1},\,a\,(tct^{-1})\,a^{-1}]$.
Substituting a standard presentation of the affine group in the
generators $v_1,v_2,v_3$ for the translations and $x,y,z$ for the
linear part yields the eight-generator, forty-one-relator presentation
below.
First define the \emph{linear presentation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sl3presentation}
 \mathcal R=\left\langle x,y,z\ \middle|\
 \begin{gathered}
 x^3=y^3=z^2=(xz)^3=(yz)^3=1,\\
 (x^{-1}zxy)^2=(y^{-1}zyx)^2=(xy)^6=1
 \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
and the \emph{base}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-presentation}
\mathcal B=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\ \middle|\
\begin{gathered}
\text{the eight relations in \eqref{eq:sl3presentation}},\\
[v_1,v_2]=[v_1,v_3]=[v_2,v_3]=1,\\
xv_1x^{-1}=v_3,\ xv_2x^{-1}=v_1,\
xv_3x^{-1}=v_2,\\
yv_1y^{-1}=v_1,\ yv_2y^{-1}=v_2^{-1}v_3,\
yv_3y^{-1}=v_1v_2^{-1},\\
zv_1z^{-1}=v_2v_3^{-1},\ zv_2z^{-1}=v_1v_3^{-1},\
zv_3z^{-1}=v_3^{-1}
\end{gathered}\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Write $\iota\colon\mathcal B\to E$ for the homomorphism induced by the first
six generators of the presentation below.

\begin{definition}\label{def:E}
Use the six base generators and the relations displayed in
\eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
  E=\left\langle v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\ \middle|\
  \begin{gathered}
   \textup{(B)}\quad\text{all relations in \eqref{eq:gamma-presentation}},\\
   \textup{(T)}\quad tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^2\ (1\le i\le3),\\
   \phantom{\textup{(T)}\quad}
     txt^{-1}=x,\quad tyt^{-1}=y,\quad tzt^{-1}=z,\\
   \textup{(C)}\quad c^2=1,\\
   \phantom{\textup{(C)}\quad}
     [c,g]=1\ \ (g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
   \textup{(W)}\quad [w,g]=1\quad(g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\})
  \end{gathered}\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $w$ abbreviates the word
$\bigl[\,tct^{-1},\,v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}\bigr]$.
This is an explicit finite presentation with eight generators and
forty-one relators: twenty in family~\textup{(B)}, six in
family~\textup{(T)}, seven in family~\textup{(C)}, and eight in
family~\textup{(W)}.
Centrality of $w$ together with $c^2=1$ implies
$w^2=1$ by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}.  Families~\textup{(T)}
and~\textup{(C)} give, respectively,
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFPresentation}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFPresentation.manuscriptLiteralPresentation}
\[
 t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\mathcal B),
 \qquad [c,\iota(\mathcal B)]=1.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[the distinguished word is a square]\label{lem:square}
Let $H_1$ be a group and $x,y\in H_1$ with $x^2=1$.  Then
\leanverified{Sofic/CompressionDefectSquare}{GroupApproximation.commutator_conjugate_eq_commutator_sq_of_sq_eq_one}
\[
  \bigl[\,x,\;y\,x\,y^{-1}\bigr]\;=\;[\,x,y\,]^{\,2} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $z=yxy^{-1}$, so $z^2=yx^2y^{-1}=1$; thus $z^{-1}=z$ and
$x^{-1}=x$.  Then $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}=x\,(yxy^{-1})=xz$,
and
\[
  [x,y]^2=(xz)^2=xz\,xz=x\,z\,x^{-1}z^{-1}=[x,z] . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
The group $E$ is the specialization of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} to the data
\eqref{eq:affine-data}, and $w\ne1$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.

Let $(d_n)$ be given, write $\mathcal Q=\mathcal Q((d_n))$, and let
$\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a homomorphism.  Apply the central
involution obstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}) with
$L=\iota(\mathcal B)\le E$, the elements $t,c\in E$, and
$a=\iota(v_1)$.  The basic construction identifies $\mathcal B$ with
$L$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:literal-base-T} gives
property~\textup{(T)} of $L$, while families~\textup{(T)}
and~\textup{(C)} give
$t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1}\subseteq\iota(\mathcal B)$ and
$[c,\iota(\mathcal B)]=1$, respectively.  In $E$ we
have $\hat c^2=(tct^{-1})^2=tc^2t^{-1}=1$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:square}
with $x=\hat c$ and $y=\iota(v_1)$ gives
\[
  w=\bigl[\,\hat c,\;\iota(v_1)\,\hat c\,\iota(v_1)^{-1}\bigr]
  =[\,\hat c,\iota(v_1)\,]^{\,2}=u^2 ,
  \qquad u=[\,\hat c,\iota(v_1)\,].
\]
The defining relations make $w$ central, and $c^2=1$ then implies
$w^2=1$ by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}.  Hence $\Theta(w)=1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.negativeCorner_kazhdanTransport_contradiction}

Every homomorphism $E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps the nontrivial element
$w$ to $1$, so none is injective and $E$ is not MF\@.  The canonical
maps $E\to\U(\Cred(E))$ and $E\to\U(\Cmax(E))$ are injective, the
first because $g\mapsto\lambda_g$ is and the second because the left
regular representation factors through $\Cmax(E)$.  If
$e\colon A\to\mathcal Q$ is an injective, possibly nonunital,
$*$-homomorphism from a unital algebra, then $u\mapsto e(u)+1-e(1)$ is
an injective homomorphism $\U(A)\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.  Hence an MF
embedding of either $\Cred(E)$ or $\Cmax(E)$ would give an injective
homomorphism $E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[uniform finite operator-norm obstruction]\label{cor:uniform}
There exist $\delta>0$ and a finite set $F_0\subset E$ such that, for
every $d\ge1$, every map $\phi\colon E\to\U(d)$ satisfying
\[
 \opnorm{\phi(gh)-\phi(g)\phi(h)}\leq\delta
 \qquad(g,h\in F_0)
\]
satisfies $\opnorm{\phi(w)-1}<1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralUniformObstruction}{GroupApproximation.LiteralUniformObstruction.literal_uniform_operatorNorm_obstruction}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix an exhaustion $F_1\subseteq F_2\subseteq\cdots$ of $E$ by finite
sets.  If no such pair $(\delta,F_0)$ exists, then for each $n$ there
is a unitary-valued $\phi_n$ with multiplicative defect at most $1/n$
on $F_n$ and $\opnorm{\phi_n(w)-1}\ge1$.  The sequence $(\phi_n)$ is an
operator-norm asymptotic representation of $E$, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} its class is a homomorphism
$\Theta\colon E\to\U(\mathcal Q)$, and $\opnorm{\Theta(w)-1}\ge1$ gives
$\Theta(w)\ne1$, contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

Since $E$ is finitely presented, the forty-one relators of
\eqref{eq:E} serve as the test set, and the hypothesis becomes a
condition on an eight-tuple of unitaries.

\begin{corollary}[a uniform obstruction on the presentation]
\label{cor:relator-uniform}
There is a $\delta>0$ with the following property.  For every $d\ge1$
and every eight-tuple $V_1,\dots,V_8\in\U(d)$, if
\[
 \opnorm{r(V_1,\dots,V_8)-1}\le\delta
 \qquad\text{for each of the forty-one relators $r$ of \eqref{eq:E},}
\]
then $\opnorm{w(V_1,\dots,V_8)-1}<1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralRelatorObstruction}{GroupApproximation.LiteralRelatorObstruction.literal_relator_uniform_obstruction}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If no such $\delta$ exists, then for every $n$ there are a dimension
$d_n$ and a tuple $V^{(n)}$ satisfying every relator to within $1/n$
and with $\opnorm{w(V^{(n)})-1}\ge1$.  Each tuple defines a
homomorphism $\varphi_n\colon F_8\to\U(d_n)$, since $F_8$ is free, and
by Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona} the classes define a homomorphism
$\Phi\colon F_8\to\U(\mathcal Q((d_n)))$.  Each of the forty-one
relators lies in $\ker\Phi$, its images converging to $1$ in operator
norm, so $\Phi$ factors as $\Theta\circ\pi$ through the quotient map
$\pi\colon F_8\to E$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives $\Theta(w)=1$, that
is $\opnorm{w(V^{(n)})-1}\to0$, contradicting the lower bound $1$.
\end{proof}

The compactness proof is ineffective.  An explicit value of $\delta$ and an
explicit test set $F_0$ would require an effective Kazhdan constant for the
base and effective compression estimates.

\section{An auxiliary Clifford witness}\label{sec:witness}

\begin{construction}[Clifford groups]\label{con:clifford}
For a set $X$, use the real Clifford-algebra convention generated by
$(c_x)_{x\in X}$ with $c_x^2=1$ and $c_xc_y=-c_yc_x$ for $x\ne y$, and let
$\Cl(X)$ be the subgroup of units generated by $-1$ and the $c_x$.
Write $\zeta=-1$.  In this concrete group,
\[
 \zeta^2=c_x^2=1,\qquad c_xc_y=\zeta c_yc_x\quad(x\ne y).
\]
The sign $\zeta$ is nontrivial.  Thus $\Cl(X)$ is a central extension
of the elementary abelian $2$-group on $X$, with central kernel
generated by $\zeta$.  For distinct $x,y\in X$, the commutator
$[c_x,c_y]$ equals $\zeta$.  Every
permutation of $X$ acts on this
group by $c_x\mapsto c_{\sigma(x)}$ and fixes $\zeta$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptCliffordConstruction}
\end{construction}

Since $\Cl(X)/\langle\zeta\rangle\cong\bigoplus_XC_2$, every
permutation group $\Sigma\curvearrowright X$ gives
\[
 (\Cl(X)\rtimes\Sigma)/\langle\zeta\rangle
 \cong \left(\bigoplus_XC_2\right)\rtimes\Sigma.
\]
Modulo its sign, $\Cl(X)\rtimes\Sigma$ is thus an ordinary
permutational wreath product.

\begin{lemma}[linear model]\label{lem:linear}
For $M\in\GL_3(\mathbb Q)$ and $u\in\mathbb Q^3$, write
\[
 A(M,u)=\begin{pmatrix}M&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Assign $v_i\mapsto A(I_3,e_i)$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},0\right),
&y&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&-1&-1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},0\right),\\[3pt]
 z&\mapsto A\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\-1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix},0\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let $\bar\Gamma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ be the subgroup generated by these six
matrices, and put
$D=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)$.  The six matrices satisfy all twenty
relators of $\mathcal B$, and
\[
 \bar\alpha(u)=DuD^{-1}
\]
is an injective endomorphism of $\bar\Gamma$ which squares the three
translation generators and fixes $x,y,z$.  Moreover the matrix assigned to
$v_1$ does not lie in $\operatorname{range}(\bar\alpha)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptLinearModel}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each of the twenty relators reduces to an equality between explicit
$4\times4$ rational matrices.  Conjugation by $D$ gives the six stable-letter equations
and is injective because it is conjugation by an invertible matrix.  Every
displayed generator and its explicitly computed inverse has integral entries;
equivalently, all six generators lie in $\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$.  Hence
$\bar\Gamma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Z)$.  However $D^{-1}\bar v_1D$ has entry $1/2$
in position $(1,4)$, so it is not in $\bar\Gamma$.  This proves the last
assertion.
\end{proof}

Form the direct limit $T=\varinjlim(\bar\Gamma,\bar\alpha)$ and let
$\tau$ denote its shift automorphism.  Set $\Sigma=T\rtimes\mathbb Z$.
This is the group $G$ used in Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} for
the data \eqref{eq:affine-data}.  Let
$j\colon\bar\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Sigma$ be the level-zero map.  Put
$X=\Sigma/j(\bar\Gamma)$, with base point $o=j(\bar\Gamma)$.  Since
$D^{-1}\bar v_1D\notin\bar\Gamma$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}), we have
$\tau o\ne j(\bar v_1)\tau o$, and the Clifford generators at these two
points anticommute.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:witness}
$w\neq1$ in $E$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFLinearWitness}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFLinearWitness.literal_mark_ne_one}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Form $W:=\Cl(X)\rtimes\Sigma$ as in Construction~\ref{con:clifford}.
Define a map on the generators of \eqref{eq:E} by
\[
\begin{gathered}
  g\mapsto j(\bar g)\in\Sigma\quad
  (g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z\}),\\
  t\mapsto\tau,\qquad c\mapsto c_o .
\end{gathered}
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear} gives the twenty base and six stable-letter
relations.  The level-zero subgroup fixes $o$, so $c_o$
commutes with every base generator, and $c_o^2=1$.
Under this assignment,
\[
  tct^{-1}\mapsto c_{\tau o},\qquad
  v_1(tct^{-1})v_1^{-1}\mapsto c_{j(\bar v_1)\tau o},
\]
and the two points are distinct by Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}, so
\[
  w\mapsto[c_{\tau o},c_{j(\bar v_1)\tau o}]=-1=\zeta .
\]
Since $\zeta$ is central in $W$, the remaining relations $[w,g]=1$ for
$g\in\{v_1,v_2,v_3,x,y,z,t,c\}$ are also satisfied.  Hence the
assignment extends to a group homomorphism $E\to W$ sending $w$ to
$\zeta\neq1$, and therefore $w\neq1$ in $E$.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences of the example}\label{sec:position}

Throughout, $\hat c=tct^{-1}$ and $u=[\hat c,\iota(v_1)]$, so that $w=u^2$.
For a countable group $G$, write $\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G)$
for its \emph{MF residual}: the intersection of the kernels of all its
homomorphisms to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ (Definition~\ref{def:radical}).
Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot} shows that $G$ is MF exactly when this
subgroup is trivial.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:A},
$1\ne w\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$.

\subsection*{The quotient by the central involution}

\begin{mainthm}[the quotient of $E$ by $\langle w\rangle$]\label{thm:signfree}
The quotient $E/\langle w\rangle$ is not MF\@.  The image of
$[\iota(v_1),\hat c]=u^{-1}$ is nontrivial in $E/\langle w\rangle$ and lies
in $\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E/\langle w\rangle)$; consequently
$\{1,w\}$ is a proper subgroup of
$\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSignFreeQuotient}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSignFreeQuotient.signFree_collapse}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSignFreeQuotient}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSignFreeQuotient.signFreeQuotient_not_isCDEOperatorMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSignFreeQuotient}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSignFreeQuotient.commutator_not_zpow_mark}
\end{mainthm}

The MF residuals of $E$ and $E/\langle w\rangle$ are computed exactly in
Section~\ref{sec:exact-residual}.

\noindent
Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} states that if an involution commutes with the
conjugate copy of a Kazhdan subgroup and its conjugates under the
original subgroup commute pairwise, then every homomorphism to
$\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps all of those conjugates to the same element.
In $E$ the pairwise commutators of those conjugates of $\hat c$ lie in
$\langle w\rangle$, so in $E/\langle w\rangle$ the element $\hat c$
satisfies these hypotheses, and every homomorphism
$E/\langle w\rangle\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps $u$ to $1$.  Thus
$E/\langle w\rangle$ is a second finitely presented non-MF group.

\subsection*{A stably finite non-MF group algebra}
Every MF algebra is stably finite \cite{BK}; the converse fails.  The
canonical trace on $\Cred(E)$ is faithful, so $\Cred(E)$ is stably
finite (Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace}).
Abstract examples follow from the failure of the Connes embedding
problem \cite{FGH}; the reduced algebra of the finitely presented group
$E$ is an explicit one (Theorem~\ref{thm:reduced}).  For
$\Cred(\SL_3(\mathbb Z))$ and $\Cred(\SL_4(\mathbb Z))$ the MF
property is undecided; only the stronger PMF property is known to
fail, for $\SL_4(\mathbb Z)$ \cite{MdlS}.

\begin{mainthm}[the reduced group algebra of $E$]\label{thm:reduced}
The algebra $\Cred(E)$ is unital and separable, has a faithful
tracial state, and is therefore stably finite, but it is not an MF
algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremD}
\end{mainthm}

\begin{proof}
$\Cred(E)$ is unital and separable because $E$ is countable, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:faithfultrace} the canonical trace is faithful and the
algebra is therefore stably finite.  It is not an MF algebra by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

The block normal form of Section~\ref{sec:algebras-sofic} shows in
addition that $E$, and hence $\Cred(E)$, is exact.
The canonical map $\mathcal B\to E$ is injective
(Section~\ref{sec:witness}), so $E$ contains an infinite Kazhdan group
and is nonamenable.
\leanverified{Analysis/NuclearityAmenability}{GroupApproximation.NuclearityAmenability.manuscriptNotAmenableOfBaseEmbeds}
By Lance's theorem, nonamenability of a discrete group is equivalent
to nonnuclearity of its reduced group $C^*$-algebra, so
$\Cred(E)$ is not nuclear \cite{Lance}.  In the direction used here,
nuclearity is read as the completely positive approximation property:
a discrete group whose reduced algebra has that property admits an
invariant mean, and $E$ does not.
\leanverified{Analysis/LanceNuclearity}{GroupApproximation.CStarExactness.reducedGroupCStar_not_nuclear}
Since $\Cred(E)$ is nonnuclear, this example does not address the
nuclear form of the Blackadar--Kirchberg problem, whether every stably
finite separable nuclear $C^*$-algebra is quasidiagonal.

The maximal algebra behaves differently: $\Cmax(E)$ is not stably
finite and has no faithful tracial state.  This holds for every group
containing a property-\textup{(T)} subgroup properly conjugated into
itself, and comes from a proper isometry built directly from the
Kazhdan projection (Proposition~\ref{rem:maxinfinite}).

\subsection*{A sofic example}

\begin{mainthm}[the explicit group is sofic]\label{thm:Esofic}
The group $E$ of Definition~\ref{def:E} is sofic.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSoficAssembly}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSoficAssembly.markedGroup_isSofic}
\end{mainthm}

\noindent
Hence $E$ is hyperlinear.  LEF groups are MF \cite{CDE} and $E$ is not
MF (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}), so $E$ is not LEF; a residually finite group
is LEF, so $E$ is not residually finite either.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSoficAssembly}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSoficAssembly.markedGroup_finitelyPresented_hyperlinear_nonMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSoficAssembly}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSoficAssembly.markedGroup_sofic_not_isLEF_unconditional}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSoficAssembly}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSoficAssembly.markedGroup_finitelyPresented_sofic_nonMF}

Let $E_0$ be the kernel of the exponent sum in $t$.  For every finite
subset of $E_0$, the $T$-coordinates lie in a single level of the
ascending chain and the lamp coordinates involve finitely many
conjugates of $c$; the subgroup they generate embeds in a residually
finite group.  Hence $E_0$ is LEF (Section~\ref{sec:Esofic}).  Since
$E$ is a split extension of $E_0$ by $\Z$, it is sofic by the
Elek--Szab\'o amenable-extension theorem \cite{ElekSzabo}.  This
extension does not preserve LEF: LEF groups are MF \cite{CDE}, so
$E_0$ is LEF whereas $E$ is not.

MF groups are not closed under extensions or quotients: a
Clifford quotient of $E$ is a non-MF extension of an MF group by an
MF group, and the MF group $F_8$ surjects onto $E$
(Section~\ref{sec:algebras-sofic}, Corollary~\ref{cor:quotclosure}).

\subsection*{A hyperlinear non-MF trace}

We use Shulman's terminology \cite{ShulmanTraces}.  Hyperlinear and MF
traces are defined by the same pointwise bounded, trace-correct matrix
models, with the algebraic defects measured in normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt norm and operator norm, respectively.  The
approximating maps themselves need not be linear, positive, unital, or
completely positive.  Every MF trace is hyperlinear; Shulman asks
whether the converse holds.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:mftrace-group}
Let $G$ be a countable group and let $\tau_G$ be the canonical trace on
$\Cmax(G)$, determined by $\tau_G(u_g)=1$ for $g=1$ and $\tau_G(u_g)=0$
otherwise.  If $\tau_G$ is an MF trace, then $G$ is MF\@.
\leanverified{Sofic/TraceSeparationEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptMFTraceGroupBridge}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\varphi_n\colon\Cmax(G)\to M_{k_n}(\C)$ witness that $\tau_G$ is MF
and put $\mathcal Q=\prod_n M_{k_n}(\C)\big/\bigoplus_n M_{k_n}(\C)$,
the quotient of the bounded product by the operator-norm $c_0$ ideal.
Pointwise boundedness puts $(\varphi_n(a))_n$ in the bounded product for
every $a$.  The operator-norm defect conditions make
$\Phi(a)=[(\varphi_n(a))_n]$ a $*$-homomorphism
$\Phi\colon\Cmax(G)\to\mathcal Q$.  Set $p=\Phi(1)$, a projection with
$\Phi(\Cmax(G))\subseteq p\mathcal Qp$.  Then
$\Theta(g)=\Phi(u_g)+(1-p)$ defines a homomorphism
$\Theta\colon G\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.  We claim that $\Theta$ is
injective.  If $g\ne1$ and $\Theta(g)=1$, then $\Phi(u_g)=\Phi(1)$, so
$\lVert\varphi_n(u_g)-\varphi_n(1)\rVert\to0$; the normalized trace is
contractive for the operator norm, whereas trace-correctness gives
$\operatorname{tr}_{k_n}(\varphi_n(u_g))\to0$ and
$\operatorname{tr}_{k_n}(\varphi_n(1))\to1$, a contradiction.  Thus $G$
embeds in the unitary group of a norm matrix corona and is MF
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences}).
\end{proof}

\noindent
Schafhauser \cite[Definition~1.1 and Proposition~2.2]{Schafhauser}
uses a stronger notion of MF group and relates it to the canonical
trace on $\Cred(G)$.  Here we use the Carri\'on--Dadarlat--Eckhardt
notion and the canonical trace on $\Cmax(G)$, as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:mftrace-group}.

\begin{mainthm}[a hyperlinear non-MF trace]\label{thm:trace}
The canonical trace $\tau_E$ on $\Cmax(E)$ is hyperlinear but not
MF\@.  Consequently, there is a separable unital $C^*$-algebra carrying
a hyperlinear non-MF trace.  In particular, this gives a negative
answer to Shulman's question whether every hyperlinear trace is MF
\cite{ShulmanTraces}.
\leanverified{Sofic/TraceSeparationEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptTraceSeparation}
\end{mainthm}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:Esofic}, $E$ is sofic.  Let
$\sigma_n\colon E\to\operatorname{Sym}(d_n)$ be a sofic approximation
and $U_n(g)$ the associated permutation unitaries.  For a free
ultrafilter $\omega$, let
\[
 \oplus_{2,\omega}M_{d_n}(\C)
 =\bigl\{(x_n):\lim_\omega\lVert x_n\rVert_{2,\tr_{d_n}}=0\bigr\}
\]
inside the bounded product, and write
$\operatorname{tr}_\omega([(x_n)])=\lim_\omega\tr_{d_n}(x_n)$ for the
induced trace on the tracial ultraproduct.  The classes
$[(U_n(g))_n]$ define a homomorphism
\[
E\longrightarrow
\U\!\left(\prod_n M_{d_n}(\C)\big/\oplus_{2,\omega}M_{d_n}(\C)\right).
\]
Moreover, asymptotic freeness of the sofic approximation gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}_\omega([(U_n(g))_n])=\delta_{g,1},
\]
because the normalized trace of a permutation matrix is the proportion
of its fixed points.  By the universal property of $\Cmax(E)$ this
extends to a $*$-homomorphism $\pi$ with
$\tau_E=\operatorname{tr}_\omega\circ\pi$, so $\tau_E$ is hyperlinear
by Shulman's tracial-ultraproduct characterization
\cite{ShulmanTraces}.

If $\tau_E$ were an MF trace, Lemma~\ref{lem:mftrace-group} would imply
that $E$ is MF, contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:A}\@.  Separability of
$\Cmax(E)$ follows from countability of $E$.
\end{proof}

\section{Weighted asymptotic-commutant invariance}\label{supp:transport-variants}

Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} holds with an arbitrary weight
$\nu_n$ in place of the dimension.

\begin{theorem}[one-sided conjugation at an arbitrary weight]
\label{thm:transport-variants}
Let $H$, $L$, and $t$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}, let $U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ be an
operator-norm asymptotic representation, let $(\nu_n)$ be nonnegative
real weights, and let $x_n\in M_{d_n}(\C)$ be matrices for which there is
$C$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(x_n^*x_n)\le C\,\nu_n$ for all $n$ and,
for every $\ell\in L$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\,\bigl|x_n-U_n(\ell)\,x_n\,
   U_n(\ell)^*\bigr|^2\le\varepsilon\,\nu_n.
\]
Then $U_n(t)\,x_nU_n(t)^*$ and $U_n(t)^*\,x_nU_n(t)$ satisfy both
conditions as well.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptSubgroupSpecializations}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptWeightedTransportSubgroup}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equip the $n$th coordinate space with the inner product
\[
 \langle x,y\rangle_n=
 \begin{cases}
  \operatorname{Tr}(y^*x)/\nu_n,&\nu_n>0,\\
  \operatorname{Tr}(y^*x),&\nu_n=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
If $\nu_n=0$, the bound $\operatorname{Tr}(x_n^*x_n)\le C\nu_n$
forces $x_n=0$, so choosing the ordinary Hilbert--Schmidt
inner product on that coordinate does not affect the distinguished
vector.  Conjugation by a unitary is unitary for every one of these
inner products.  The bound makes $(x_n)$ a bounded ultraproduct vector,
and the adjoint actions remain operator-norm almost multiplicative
because the scalar normalization of the Hilbert norm does not enter
the operator norm.  Thus the ultraproduct proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} applies with these weighted
coordinate Hilbert spaces.  Finiteness of the norm ultraproduct again
turns $P\le VPV^{*}$ into $P=VPV^{*}$, which gives both conjugation
directions.
\end{proof}

Taking $\nu_n=k_n$, the rank of the
projection lift in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-collapse}, gives the normalization used
there.

\section{Obstructions from a one-sided conjugation datum}\label{sec:conj}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}, applied to the property-\textup{(T)}
subgroup $L=\iota(\Lambda)$, the normal closure
$N_{\mathrm{conj}}$ of the commutators $[tct^{-1},\iota(\lambda)]$ is
contained in $K_2^{\omega}(U)$ for every operator-norm asymptotic
representation $U$ of $H$.  For a finite normal subgroup of
$N_{\mathrm{conj}}$, the corner of Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} is the
complement of a projection obtained from its averaging operators
(Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}), and the central involution of
Section~\ref{sec:sign} is the case $F=\{1,w\}$.  For a normal
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup, which may be infinite, noncentral and
torsion-free, the corner is a spectral projection of a Kazhdan
average (Theorems~\ref{thm:abstract-nk}
and~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan}).  Corollary~\ref{cor:intrinsic-nk}
replaces $N_{\mathrm{conj}}$ by the subgroup
$\mathfrak D(H,L)$ of \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}, which depends on $L$
alone and not on a choice of $t$ and $c$.

\begin{definition}[Kazhdan conjugation datum]\label{def:pattern}
Let $H$ be a countable group.  A \emph{Kazhdan conjugation datum} in
$H$ is a tuple $(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, where $\Lambda$ is a countable
group with property~\textup{(T)}, $\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ is a group
homomorphism, possibly with nontrivial kernel, and $t,c\in H$,
subject to
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(M1)] $t\,\iota(\lambda)\,t^{-1}\in\iota(\Lambda)$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$;
  \item[(M2)] $c\,\iota(\lambda)=\iota(\lambda)\,c$ for every
    $\lambda\in\Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
Write $\hat c:=tct^{-1}$.  The datum determines the normal closure
\leanverified{Sofic/ConjugationDatumAnyUniverse}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMarkedKazhdanPattern_anyUniverse}
\[
  N_{\mathrm{conj}}
  \;=\;\bigl\langle\!\bigl\langle\,
  [\,\hat c,\iota(\lambda)\,]\;:\;\lambda\in\Lambda
  \,\bigr\rangle\!\bigr\rangle_H\;\trianglelefteq\;H .
\]
\end{definition}

For a finitely generated $\Lambda$, conditions (M1)--(M2) are imposed by
finitely many relations once generating sets are fixed.  Conditions
(M1)--(M2) imply that $c$ commutes with $\iota(\Lambda)$ and that $\hat c$
commutes with the subgroup $t\iota(\Lambda)t^{-1}$.

\begin{definition}[universal $2$-norm triviality]\label{def:invisible}
Let $U=(U_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic representation of a
countable group $H$ and let $\omega$ be an ultrafilter refining the
cofinite filter.  Write
\[
 K_2^{\omega}(U)\;=\;\bigl\{g\in H:\
 \textstyle\lim_\omega\hsnorm{U_n(g)-1}=0\bigr\},
\]
the kernel of the homomorphism induced by $U$ into the tracial
ultraproduct of the matrix blocks along $\omega$, and hence a normal
subgroup of $H$.  An element $g$ is \emph{universally $2$-norm trivial} if
$g\in K_2^{\omega}(U)$ for every such $U$ and every such $\omega$.
If $\hsnorm{U_n(g)-1}\to0$ along the full sequence, then
$g\in K_2^{\omega}(U)$ for every such $\omega$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptHSInvisibleCharacterization}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[finite-normal obstruction criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $H$ be a countable group with a Kazhdan conjugation datum
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, and let $F\trianglelefteq H$ be a finite
normal subgroup with $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{conj}}$.  Then every
homomorphism $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies
$\Theta(f)=1$ for all $f\in F$.
\leanverified{Sofic/FiniteNormalAnyUniverse}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptFiniteNormalObstructionCriterion_anyUniverse}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Universal $2$-norm triviality of $N_{\mathrm{conj}}$.}  Let
$U_n\colon H\to\U(d_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic representation
of $H$ (for instance, coordinate unitary lifts of a homomorphism to
$\U(\mathcal Q)$), write $U_{g,n}=U_n(g)$, and put
\[
  K_2=\{g\in H:\hsnorm{U_{g,n}-1}\longrightarrow0\}.
\]
Unitary invariance and asymptotic multiplicativity make
$K_2\trianglelefteq H$.  Put $L=\iota(\Lambda)$, which has
property~\textup{(T)} because it is a quotient of $\Lambda$.
Since $c$ centralizes $L$, the sequence $(U_{c,n})$ belongs to the
asymptotic commutant of $L$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}, applied to $x_n=U_{c,n}$, shows
that $(U_{\hat c,n})$ for $\hat c=tct^{-1}$ lies in the same asymptotic
commutant.  Therefore
$[\hat c,\iota(\lambda)]\in K_2$ for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$; since
$K_2$ is normal, it contains the normal closure of these commutators:
$N_{\mathrm{conj}}\subseteq K_2$, and so $F\subseteq K_2$.
Convergence of the full sequence gives triviality along every
ultrafilter refining the cofinite filter, so every element of
$N_{\mathrm{conj}}$ is universally $2$-norm trivial
(Definition~\ref{def:invisible}).

\emph{The coordinate averaging corner.}  Suppose a homomorphism $\Theta$ to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ is nontrivial on $F$; choose coordinate unitary
lifts $(V_{g,n})$ of $\Theta$ and form the coordinate averaging operators
\[
 p_n=\frac1{|F|}\sum_{f\in F}V_{f,n}.
\]
Because the lifts are asymptotically multiplicative and $F$ is finite,
$(p_n)$ is asymptotically self-adjoint and idempotent.  Since $F$ is
normal, $(p_n)$ asymptotically commutes with every lift of
$\Theta(H)$.  Continuous functional calculus applied to the Hermitian
parts yields genuine projections at operator-norm distance $o(1)$ from
$p_n$, and these projections are asymptotically central as well.
Let $q_n$ be their complements.  Nontriviality of
$\Theta$ on $F$ forces $q_n\ne0$ along infinitely many coordinates ---
otherwise the averages converge to $1$ and $\Theta|_F$ is trivial.
Retain exactly those coordinates and relabel them by $\N$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} gives an operator-norm asymptotic
representation $(W_{g,n})$ of $H$ on the corners
$q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\cong M_{r_n}(\C)$, $r_n=\operatorname{rank}q_n>0$.
Since $q_n$ is the complement of a projection within $o(1)$ of $p_n$,
the finite average vanishes on the corner:
\[
 \Bigl\lVert\sum_{f\in F}W_{f,n}\Bigr\rVert\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in operator norm.}
 \tag{\ensuremath{\ast}}
\]

\emph{The contradiction.}  Since $F\subseteq N_{\mathrm{conj}}$ and
$(W_{g,n})$ is itself an operator-norm asymptotic representation of
$H$, every $f\in F$ satisfies $\hsnorm{W_{f,n}-1}\to0$.  Because $F$
is finite, these convergences are uniform over $f\in F$, so
\[
 \sum_{f\in F}W_{f,n}\longrightarrow |F|1
 \quad\text{in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm},
\]
each block with its own normalized trace.  This contradicts
\((\ast)\), because operator-norm convergence dominates normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt convergence.  Hence every homomorphism
$H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ is trivial on $F$.
\end{proof}

The same conclusion holds for every finite normal subgroup $F$ contained
in a subgroup $D\le H$ whose elements lie in $K_2^{\omega}(U)$ for every
operator-norm asymptotic representation $U$ of $H$ and every $\omega$.
Finiteness of $F$ is essential: universal $2$-norm triviality alone does
not force triviality in the corona (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

The central involution obstruction
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion}) is the case $F=\{1,w\}$, where
$p_n=\tfrac12(1+V_{w,n})$; in the corona corner the image of $w$ is
$-1$ exactly, while the finite-stage compressions satisfy
$\opnorm{W_{w,n}+1}\to0$.

\subsection{An intrinsic form}

For a subgroup $L\le H$, let
\[
 P_L=\{s\in H:sLs^{-1}\subseteq L\},
\]
the \emph{compression semigroup} of $L$ in the terminology of
Kun--Thom \cite{KT}, and put $G^{+}(L)=\langle P_L\rangle$.  Then set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsicdefect}
 \mathfrak D(H,L)=
 \left\langle\!\left\langle
 [gzg^{-1},\ell]:g\in G^{+}(L),\ z\in C_H(L),\ \ell\in L
 \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_H,
\end{equation}
which depends only on the pair $(H,L)$, not on a choice of datum.

\begin{corollary}[transport by the compression semigroup]\label{cor:generaltransport}
Let $(r_n)$ and $V_{g,n}\in\U(r_n)$ be an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $H$, let $\Lambda$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Lambda\to H$ be a homomorphism, and put $L=\iota(\Lambda)$.
Suppose that $(x_n\in M_{r_n}(\C))$ is uniformly bounded in operator norm
and belongs to the asymptotic commutant in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm of $L$:
\[
 \hsnorm{x_n-V_{\iota(\lambda),n}x_nV_{\iota(\lambda),n}^*}
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
Then for every $g\in G^{+}(L)$, conjugation and inverse conjugation
both preserve that asymptotic commutant.  Explicitly, both
\[
 V_{g,n}x_nV_{g,n}^*
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 V_{g,n}^*x_nV_{g,n}
\]
satisfy the preceding convergence for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$.
\leanverified{Sofic/DefectActionAnyUniverse}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.compressionGroup_transport_both_anyUniverse}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a one-sided conjugator $s$, Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}
applied to $L=\iota(\Lambda)$ gives the conclusion for
$V_{s,n}x_nV_{s,n}^*$.  The subgroup $L$ has property~\textup{(T)}
because it is a quotient of $\Lambda$.  The equality of the
fixed-space projection with its conjugate established in that proof
also gives invariance under the inverse conjugation, hence the
conclusion for $V_{s,n}^*x_nV_{s,n}$.  The set of $g\in H$ for which
conjugation by $V_{g,n}$ and by $V_{g,n}^*$ both preserve the
asymptotic commutant is a subgroup, and it contains every one-sided
conjugator; hence it contains $G^{+}(L)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the subgroup $\mathfrak D$ and the MF residual]
\label{thm:compression-radical}
Let $H$ and $\Gamma$ be countable groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let
$\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism, and put
$L=\iota(\Gamma)$.  If $F\trianglelefteq H$ is finite and
$F\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, then every homomorphism $H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps every element of $F$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/DefectRadicalAnyUniverse}{GroupApproximation.KazhdanAsymptoticCommutant.manuscriptCompressionRadical_anyUniverse}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $(U_{h,n})$ be coordinate lifts of an operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $H$ and put
$K_2=\{h:\hsnorm{U_{h,n}-1}\to0\}$, a normal subgroup of $H$.
If $z\in C_H(L)$, then $(U_{z,n})$ lies in the asymptotic commutant of
$L$, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:generaltransport} so does
$(U_{gzg^{-1},n})$ for every $g\in G^{+}(L)$.  Hence each
generator $[gzg^{-1},\ell]$, $\ell\in L$, displayed in
\eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect} belongs to $K_2$; since
$K_2\trianglelefteq H$, it contains their normal closure:
$\mathfrak D(H,L)\le K_2$.  Every element of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ is
therefore universally $2$-norm trivial in every operator-norm asymptotic
representation of $H$.  If a homomorphism $H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ were
nontrivial on a finite normal $F\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, the
averaging-corner argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} would
contradict this universal $2$-norm triviality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The normal-Kazhdan form of the obstruction}

A finite group has property~\textup{(T)}, and its averaging operator is
the projection onto its fixed space.  The spectral projection of a
Kazhdan average replaces that operator for an arbitrary normal
property-\textup{(T)} subgroup, which may be infinite, noncentral and
torsion-free.

\begin{theorem}[obstruction from universal $2$-norm triviality]\label{thm:abstract-nk}
Let $H$ be a countable group and let $D\le H$ be a subgroup every
element of which is universally $2$-norm trivial
(Definition~\ref{def:invisible}).  Let $K\trianglelefteq H$ be a normal subgroup with
property~\textup{(T)} such that $K\le D$.  Then every homomorphism $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies
$\Theta(k)=1$ for all $k\in K$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptAbstractNormalKazhdanObstruction}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a homomorphism $\Theta$ to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ is nontrivial on
$K$.  Its image $\bar H=\Theta(H)$ is countable and, as a subgroup of
$\U(\mathcal Q)$, is MF by definition.  Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences},
applied along an exhaustion of $\bar H$, gives an operator-norm
asymptotic representation
$(V_{g,n})$ in which every fixed nontrivial element eventually stays
at operator-norm distance at least $1$ from the identity.  These
models provide operator-norm separation, but at growing dimension this
yields no lower bound on normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distances.  Every
operator-norm asymptotic representation of $\bar H$ composes with
$\Theta$ to one of $H$, so every element of $\Theta(D)$ is again
universally $2$-norm trivial, while $\bar K=\Theta(K)\le\Theta(D)$ is normal,
Kazhdan and nontrivial.  We now work in $\bar H$.

\emph{The Kazhdan corner.}  Fix a free ultrafilter $\omega$.  Let
$H_\omega$ be the Hilbert-space ultraproduct of the coordinate spaces
$\C^{d_n}$, and let $B_\omega=\prod_\omega B(\C^{d_n})$ act on
$H_\omega$ as in Section~\ref{sec:transport}.  The classes
$\pi(g)=[V_{g,n}]_\omega$ define a unitary representation of $\bar H$
on $H_\omega$.  Fix a finite symmetric generating Kazhdan set
$S\subseteq\bar K$ containing $1$, with constant $\varepsilon_0$, put
$\theta=1-\varepsilon_0^{2}/(4|S|)$, and let
\[
 h=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{a\in S}\pi(a)\in B_\omega .
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:kazhdan-gap} the spectrum of $h$ lies in
$[-1,1-\varepsilon_0^{2}/(2|S|)]\cup\{1\}$ and hence in
$[-1,\theta]\cup\{1\}$, so the spectral projection $P=\chi_{\{1\}}(h)$
belongs to $B_\omega$; its range is $\operatorname{Fix}\pi(\bar K)$,
where $h=1$.  Put $q=1-P$.

If $q=0$ then $\pi(k)=1$ for every $k\in\bar K$, against the
operator-norm separation of a fixed nontrivial element of $\bar K$.
For $g\in\bar H$ the projection $\pi(g)P\pi(g)^{*}$ has range
$\operatorname{Fix}\pi(g\bar Kg^{-1})=\operatorname{Fix}\pi(\bar K)$ by
normality, so $P$ and $q$ commute with $\pi(\bar H)$ exactly.  Lift $q$
to projections $q_n\in M_{d_n}(\C)$, nonzero along $\omega$ and with
$\opnorm{[q_n,V_{g,n}]_{-}}\to_\omega0$ for every $g\in\bar H$, and lift
$h$ to the self-adjoint coordinates
\[
 h_n=\frac1{2|S|}\sum_{a\in S}\bigl(V_{a,n}+V_{a,n}^{*}\bigr)
 \in M_{d_n}(\C),
\]
which represent $h$ because $S$ is symmetric.  The compression of $h$
to the corner satisfies $qhq\le\theta q$ in $B_\omega$, and, writing
$x_{+}$ for the positive part of a self-adjoint $x$, the positive part
is a continuous function of $x$ and the norm ultraproduct computes it
coordinatewise, so that inequality is exactly the coordinate statement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner-spectral}
 \opnorm{\bigl(q_nh_nq_n-\theta q_n\bigr)_{+}}\to_\omega0 .
\end{equation}

To obtain ordinary convergence before compressing, pass to a
subsequence.  Enumerate
$\bar H=\{g_1,g_2,\dots\}$.  For each $j$ the set
\[
 A_j=\Bigl\{n\ :\ q_n\ne0,\ \ \max_{i\le j}\opnorm{[q_n,V_{g_i,n}]_{-}}<1/j,\ \
 \opnorm{\bigl(q_nh_nq_n-\theta q_n\bigr)_{+}}<1/j\Bigr\}
\]
is again a finite intersection of sets in $\omega$ --- the last
constraint by \eqref{eq:corner-spectral} --- hence lies in $\omega$ and
in particular is infinite.  Choose $n_1<n_2<\cdots$ with $n_j\in A_j$
and relabel along $(n_j)_j$.  Then $q_n\ne0$ for every $n$.  For every
fixed $g\in\bar H$, the element $g=g_i$ occurs in the constraints
defining $A_j$ for all $j\ge i$, so
$\opnorm{[q_n,V_{g,n}]_{-}}\to0$.  The last constraint defining $A_j$
also implies that the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:corner-spectral}
tends to $0$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} now gives an
operator-norm asymptotic representation $(W_{g,n})$ of $\bar H$ on the
corners $q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n$.

\emph{A $2$-norm separation on the corner.}  Write
$r_n=\operatorname{rank}q_n$ and equip the corner
$q_nM_{d_n}(\C)q_n\cong M_{r_n}(\C)$ with its renormalized trace
$\tr_{r_n}$.  Applying $\tr_{r_n}$ to the ordinary form of
\eqref{eq:corner-spectral} gives
\[
 \frac1{|S|}\sum_{a\in S}\operatorname{Re}\tr_{r_n}\bigl(q_nV_{a,n}q_n\bigr)
 \;=\;\tr_{r_n}\bigl(q_nh_nq_n\bigr)\;\le\;\theta+o(1),
\]
and polar correction moves each $q_nV_{a,n}q_n$ by $o(1)$ in operator
norm, hence by $o(1)$ in $\tr_{r_n}$, so the same bound holds with
$W_{a,n}$ in its place: the average over $S$
of the real parts of the renormalized traces of the compressed models
is at most $\theta+o(1)$.  For each $n$, at least one generator in
$S$ therefore has compressed model with real renormalized trace at
most $\theta+o(1)$, and so normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance at
least $\sqrt{2(1-\theta)}-o(1)$ from the corner identity.  Since $S$
is finite, a subsequence realizes this at a single generator $s_0$.

\emph{The contradiction.}  The corner models form an operator-norm
asymptotic representation of $\bar H$, and $s_0$ lies in
$\bar K\le\Theta(D)$.  Hence $s_0$ is universally $2$-norm trivial in the corner
models: $\hsnorm{W_{s_0,n}-1}\to0$, contradicting the positive lower
bound above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[normal-Kazhdan obstruction]\label{thm:normal-kazhdan}
Let $H$ be a countable group with a Kazhdan conjugation datum
$(\Lambda,\iota,t,c)$, and let $K\trianglelefteq H$ be a normal
subgroup with property~\textup{(T)} such that
$K\subseteq N_{\mathrm{conj}}$.  Then every homomorphism $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies $\Theta(k)=1$ for all
$k\in K$.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanAnyUniverse}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptNormalKazhdanObstruction_anyUniverse}
\end{theorem}

Since finite groups have property~\textup{(T)},
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} is the finite case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan}.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanMFRadical}{GroupApproximation.hasKazhdanPropertyT_of_finite}

\begin{proof}
The first step of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} shows that
every element of $N_{\mathrm{conj}}$ is universally $2$-norm trivial
(Definition~\ref{def:invisible}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-nk} with
$D=N_{\mathrm{conj}}$ gives the conclusion.
\end{proof}

Every element of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ is universally $2$-norm trivial, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-nk} gives the following normal-Kazhdan
analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-radical}:

\begin{corollary}[normal-Kazhdan form of the intrinsic residual]\label{cor:intrinsic-nk}
Let $H$ and $\Gamma$ be countable groups, let $\Gamma$ have
property~\textup{(T)}, let $\iota\colon\Gamma\to H$ be a homomorphism,
and put $L=\iota(\Gamma)$.  If $K\trianglelefteq H$ is a normal
subgroup with property~\textup{(T)} and $K\le\mathfrak D(H,L)$, then
every homomorphism $H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ maps every
element of $K$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanAnyUniverse}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptIntrinsicNormalKazhdanRadical_anyUniverse}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-radical} shows that every
element of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ is universally $2$-norm trivial in every
operator-norm asymptotic representation of $H$; apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-nk} with $D=\mathfrak D(H,L)$.
\end{proof}

The subgroup generated by \emph{all} normal property-\textup{(T)}
subgroups of $\mathfrak D(H,L)$ therefore also lies in the kernel of
every homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormalKazhdanAnyUniverse}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptIntrinsicNormalKazhdanPart_anyUniverse}

\section{The finite-dimensional obstruction}\label{sec:findim}

Every finite-dimensional representation of $E$ over any field maps
$w$ to the identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:exactfd} with $H=E$).
Consequently finite-dimensional linear representations do not separate
the points of $E$, and every finite quotient maps $w$ to the
identity.  Since the
Clifford quotient of Section~\ref{sec:witness} sends $w$ to a
nontrivial element, that quotient is infinite.  The central involution
$w$ lies in the kernel of every homomorphism to a finite group, so $E$
is a group of \emph{Deligne type} in the terminology of
\cite[Definition~1.2]{BDL}.  Such groups were proposed there as the
natural candidate counterexamples for all three remaining approximation
problems, and $E$ is such a counterexample for the operator norm.

\begin{theorem}[the exact intrinsic obstruction]\label{thm:exactfd-intrinsic}
Let $k$ be a field, $V$ a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, $H$ a
group, and $\Gamma\le H$ a subgroup.  Then every homomorphism
$\pi\colon H\to\GL(V)$ is trivial on the subgroup
$\mathfrak D(H,\Gamma)$ of \eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect}.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptTheoremB}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $C$ be the commutant of $\pi(\Gamma)$.  The elements $g\in H$ for
which conjugation by $\pi(g)$ preserves $C$ in both directions form a
subgroup.  The commutant argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:exactfd} shows
that every element of the compression semigroup $P_\Gamma$ belongs to
this subgroup, hence so does all of
$G^{+}(\Gamma)=\langle P_\Gamma\rangle$.  Thus, for $g\in
G^{+}(\Gamma)$ and $z\in C_H(\Gamma)$, the operator
$\pi(gzg^{-1})$ still belongs to $C$ and commutes with every
$\pi(\ell)$, $\ell\in\Gamma$.  Hence every displayed generator in
\eqref{eq:intrinsicdefect} lies in $\ker\pi$; since
$\ker\pi\trianglelefteq H$, the whole normal closure
$\mathfrak D(H,\Gamma)$ does as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:notRFD}
Every finite-dimensional linear representation of the group $E$ of
Definition~\ref{def:E}, over any field, maps $w$ to the identity, and
every homomorphism from $E$ to a finite group does as well.  In
particular $E$ admits no injective homomorphism into $\GL(V)$ for any
finite-dimensional vector space $V$ over any field.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralFiniteDimensionalObstruction}{GroupApproximation.LiteralFiniteDimensionalObstruction.manuscriptFiniteDimensionalConsequences}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:exactfd} with $H=E$, the image of $\mathcal B$, and the
elements $t,c,v_1$; the hypotheses hold by the defining relations
\eqref{eq:E}.  Every finite-dimensional representation maps $w$ to the
identity, and
$w\neq1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  For a finite target,
compose with its faithful left regular representation over
$\mathbb Q$.  The composite is a finite-dimensional linear
representation and hence maps $w$ to the identity; faithfulness of the
left regular representation then implies that the original
homomorphism also maps $w$ to the identity.
\end{proof}

\section{The cyclic comparison}\label{sec:necessity}

Fritz's marked $\mathrm{BS}(2,3)$ example is the context for this
comparison \cite{Fritz,SlofstraVidick}.

% Not the lettered Theorem C: an ordinary numbered theorem.  Cite
% \ref{thm:cyclic}; `thm:C` is a legacy alias.  See the note at thm:exactfd.
\begin{theorem}[the cyclic comparison]\label{thm:cyclic}
Define the presentation
\[
 E_{\mathrm{BS}}=\left\langle\gamma_0,t,c\ \middle|\
 \begin{aligned}
  &t\gamma_0t^{-1}=\gamma_0^2,\quad c^2=1,\quad[c,\gamma_0]=1,\\
  &w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1,\quad
    [w_{\mathrm{BS}},\gamma_0]=[w_{\mathrm{BS}},t]
      =[w_{\mathrm{BS}},c]=1
 \end{aligned}\right\rangle,
\]
where
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}=[tct^{-1},\gamma_0(tct^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}]$.
Then $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ is finitely presented and $w_{\mathrm{BS}}\ne1$.
There is an explicit surjective homomorphism from $E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ onto
the subgroup of the Clifford semidirect product generated by the
displayed images, and it maps $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to $\zeta\ne1$.
Nevertheless every finite-dimensional representation of
$E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ over every field maps $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ to the identity.
\leanverified{Monsters/LiteralCyclicCalibration}{GroupApproximation.LiteralCyclicCalibration.manuscriptCyclicCalibration}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic}]
The relator $w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1$ is redundant: $tct^{-1}$
and $\gamma_0(tct^{-1})\gamma_0^{-1}$ are involutions, so centrality
forces it by \eqref{eq:involution-reversal}.  Deleting it gives a
six-relator presentation of the same group.
The stable relation gives
\(t\langle\gamma_0\rangle t^{-1}\subseteq
\langle\gamma_0\rangle\), and \(c\) centralizes this cyclic base.
By the commutant argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:exactfd}, every
finite-dimensional representation over every field maps
\(w_{\mathrm{BS}}\) to the identity.

For nontriviality, use the affine realization of
\(\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\) and its coset action.  The base point is fixed by the
cyclic base, while the two points represented by \(t\) and \(\gamma_0t\)
are distinct.  Attach Clifford generators to these points and map \(c\) to
the generator at the base point.  All seven relators hold, and
\[
 w_{\mathrm{BS}}\longmapsto
 [c_{t\langle\gamma_0\rangle},
  c_{\gamma_0t\langle\gamma_0\rangle}]
 =-1.
\]
Thus \(w_{\mathrm{BS}}\ne1\).  Restricting the target to the subgroup
generated by the three displayed images gives the asserted surjection
onto a Clifford quotient.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Sharpness of the Kazhdan hypothesis.}
Running the Clifford representation of the preceding proof over
finite site sets gives explicit unitary models of the seven relators
in which $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ is the scalar $-1$.  Fix $m\ge0$, put
$M=2m+3$ and $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/M}$, and take the coordinates
$\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/M$, the first for the Clifford sign and
the second for the spectrum of the base.  Let $X$ and $Z$ be the flip and
the sign on the first coordinate, put $D=2^{-1/2}(X+Z)$, a
self-adjoint unitary, and set
\[
 \gamma_0(s,k)=(-1)^{s}\zeta^{k},\qquad c=t^{-1}Dt,
\]
where $\gamma_0$ is the displayed diagonal unitary, $b_0=0$,
$b_1=m+1$, and $t$ is the pullback unitary
\[
 (t\xi)(s,k)=\xi(s,2k+b_s),
 \qquad t e_{(s,\,2k+b_s)}=e_{(s,k)}.
\]
This is well defined because $(s,k)\mapsto(s,2k+b_s)$ is a permutation
when $M$ is odd.  Here $\gamma_0$ and $t$
are monomial and $c$ is an equal-weight sum of two monomial matrices.
Since $tct^{-1}=D$ and $\gamma_0^2(s,k)=\zeta^{2k}$ is independent of
the spin coordinate, the distinguished word is
\[
 w_{\mathrm{BS}}=\bigl[\,D,\;ZDZ\,\bigr]=(D\cdot ZDZ)^2=-1 ,
\]
because $ZDZ=2^{-1/2}(Z-X)$ and $D\cdot ZDZ=\tfrac12(XZ-ZX)$ squares
to $-1$.  Being a scalar, $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ satisfies exactly the four
relators $w_{\mathrm{BS}}^2=1$ and
$[w_{\mathrm{BS}},\gamma_0]=[w_{\mathrm{BS}},t]=[w_{\mathrm{BS}},c]=1$;
with $c^2=1$ this is five of the seven.  The remaining two hold
approximately.  From $2(m+1)=M-1$ we get
$\zeta^{m+1}=-e^{-i\pi/M}$, and
\[
 \opnorm{t\gamma_0t^{-1}-\gamma_0^{2}}=\bigl|1+\zeta^{m+1}\bigr|
 =2\sin\frac{\pi}{2M},
 \qquad
 \opnorm{[c,\gamma_0]-1}\le2\bigl|1+\zeta^{m+1}\bigr| ;
\]
$D$ commutes with $\gamma_0^2$ exactly, so the lamp relator inherits
the doubling defect and nothing else.  Both are $O(1/m)$.  Assembling
the models over $m$ gives
$\Theta\colon E_{\mathrm{BS}}\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ with
$\Theta(w_{\mathrm{BS}})\ne1$, and the image of $\Theta$ is MF
because it embeds in $\U(\mathcal Q)$.  So the
property~\textup{(T)} hypothesis in the central involution
obstruction is essential: with the cyclic base
$\langle\gamma_0\rangle$ in place of a Kazhdan base, some
homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ is nontrivial on the distinguished
word.  Equivalently, in
the residual language of Section~\ref{sec:consequences},
$w_{\mathrm{BS}}\notin\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E_{\mathrm{BS}})$.
\leanverified{Sofic/CliffordBSAmenableMF}{GroupApproximation.CliffordBSAmenableMF.manuscriptSharpnessOfKazhdanHypothesis}

The realized Clifford quotient is amenable: it is a subgroup of
the semidirect product of the locally finite Clifford group on the
coset space by the solvable affine group
$\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\cong\mathbb Z[1/2]\rtimes\mathbb Z$.  It is
therefore MF because the reduced $C^*$-algebra of every countable
amenable group embeds into the universal UHF algebra
\cite[Theorem~B]{SchafhauserAF}.  The models above give
$\Theta(w_{\mathrm{BS}})\ne1$ for an MF image
$\Theta(E_{\mathrm{BS}})$.

Exact finite-dimensional representations behave differently.  In a finite image, the
doubling relation gives
$\pi(\langle\gamma_0^2\rangle)=\pi(\langle\gamma_0\rangle)$; since
$\pi(tct^{-1})$ centralizes the first subgroup, it centralizes
$\pi(\gamma_0)$, and the image of $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$ is the identity.
To transfer this obstruction to exact local embeddings, take a finite
set containing the generators together with every intermediate prefix
of every defining word and of $w_{\mathrm{BS}}$.  Any exact local
embedding of this set evaluates those words correctly and therefore
satisfies the same equations.  Hence neither
$E_{\mathrm{BS}}$ nor its Clifford quotient is LEF, and the latter is
not residually finite.
\leanverified{Monsters/CyclicBaseLEFObstruction}{GroupApproximation.CyclicBaseLEFObstruction.cyclicBase_exactModel_package}

\paragraph{The scaling family.}
For $m\ge2$, define $E_m$ by replacing the three relations
$tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^2$ in the eight-generator presentation of $E$ with
$tv_i t^{-1}=v_i^m$ and leaving every other relator unchanged.  Then
$E_2=E$.  Write $w_m$ for the image of the same
distinguished word $w$.

\begin{corollary}[the scaling family]\label{cor:scaling-family}
For every natural number $m\ge2$, the group $E_m$ is finitely presented and
$w_m$ is a nontrivial central involution mapped to the identity by every
homomorphism $E_m\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.  Consequently $E_m$ is not MF,
and neither $\Cmax(E_m)$ nor $\Cred(E_m)$ is an MF $C^*$-algebra.
\leanverified{Sofic/ScalingFamilyEndpoint}{GroupApproximation.ScalingFamilyEndpoint.manuscriptTheoremFamily}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Replace the affine dilation $\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)$ by
$\operatorname{diag}(m,m,m,1)$ (both acting as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}).  It conjugates each integral translation
$v_i$ to $v_i^m$ and fixes the three linear generators.  Conjugating
$v_1$ by the inverse dilation gives translation by $e_1/m$, which is
not integral.  Hence the two relevant cosets remain distinct, and the
Clifford representation sends $w_m$ to $-1$.  The Kazhdan and
central-involution arguments are unchanged for every $m\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\section{The MF residual}\label{sec:consequences}

\begin{definition}\label{def:radical}
For a countable group $G_1$, the \emph{MF residual} is
\[
  \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)
  \;=\;\bigcap_{(d_n)}\ \,\bigcap_{\Theta\colon G_1\to\U(\mathcal Q((d_n)))}\ker\Theta,
\]
a normal subgroup of $G_1$; the inner intersection is over all
homomorphisms and the outer one over all sequences $(d_n)$ of positive
integers, in accordance with the convention fixed after
\eqref{eq:corona}.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptMFRadical}
\end{definition}

If $G_1/N$ is MF, compose $G_1\to G_1/N$ with an injective homomorphism
$G_1/N\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.  The resulting homomorphism has kernel $N$,
so $\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)\le N$.  Thus the MF residual
is contained in every normal subgroup whose quotient is MF.
Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot} shows that its own quotient is MF.
It is the operator-norm analogue of the invisible subgroups
of \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.7]{SlofstraCorrelations} and of the
metric-ultraproduct approximation framework of \cite{NST}.

\begin{lemma}[functoriality]\label{lem:portable}
For every homomorphism $\varphi\colon G_1\to G_2$ of countable
groups,
\[
  \varphi\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)\bigr)
  \;\subseteq\;\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2).
\]
In particular, if
$\varphi\colon E\to G_2$ satisfies $\varphi(w)\neq1$, then $G_2$ is
not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptRadicalPortability}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $x\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$ and $\Theta$ is a
homomorphism $G_2\to\U(\mathcal Q)$, then $\Theta\circ\varphi$ is a homomorphism $G_1\to\U(\mathcal Q)$, so $\Theta(\varphi(x))=1$.  For
the second statement, Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives
$w\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$.  Hence
$1\neq\varphi(w)\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_2)$.  An
injective homomorphism $G_2\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ would map $\varphi(w)$
to $1$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the universal MF quotient]\label{prop:univquot}
Let $G_1$ be a countable group and
$R=\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the kernel of a single homomorphism $G_1\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.
\item $G_1/R$ is MF.
\item Every homomorphism from $G_1$ to an MF group factors through
$G_1/R$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $G_1$ is MF if and only if $R=\{1\}$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptUniversalMFQuotient}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(3) If $\varphi\colon G_1\to M$ with $M$ MF and
$j\colon M\to\U(\mathcal Q((d_n)))$ is an injective homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$,
then $j\circ\varphi$ is a homomorphism $G_1\to\U(\mathcal Q)$, so
$j(\varphi(R))=1$ and, by injectivity of $j$, $\varphi(R)=1$.

(1) and (2).  Set $Q=G_1/R$.  For every $\bar g\ne1$ in $Q$, the
definition of $R$ gives a homomorphism
$\Theta_{\bar g}\colon Q\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ with
$\Theta_{\bar g}(\bar g)\ne1$.  Enumerate the nonidentity elements
$\bar g_1,\bar g_2,\dots$ and choose coordinate unitary lifts of the
corresponding homomorphisms.  At stage $k$, choose in the $i$th lift,
for each $i\le k$, a coordinate at which multiplicativity holds within
$1/k$ on the first $k$ group elements.  Choose the same coordinate so
that the designated element $\bar g_i$ remains separated by its fixed
positive constant.  Such coordinates exist because
$\Theta_{\bar g_i}$ separates $\bar g_i$ along infinitely many
coordinates.  Take the block sum of
the $k$ chosen coordinates.  Block operator norms are maxima, so the
single block coming from $\Theta_{\bar g_i}$ already separates
$\bar g_i$, and the sum therefore separates all of
$\bar g_1,\dots,\bar g_k$ at once.  The resulting sequence is an
operator-norm asymptotic representation separating every nonidentity
element from the identity; its class, composed with the canonical
unitary-corona isomorphism $\kappa_{(d_n)}$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}, is a
faithful homomorphism $Q\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.  This proves (2), and composing with
$G_1\twoheadrightarrow Q$ proves (1).  Finally, if $G_1$ is MF then an
injective homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$ gives $R=\{1\}$; if $R=\{1\}$ then
(2) says $G_1$ is MF.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[exact residual from a candidate quotient]\label{cor:exactradical}
Let $N\trianglelefteq G_1$ satisfy
$N\le\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$.  Every homomorphism $G_1\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ factors uniquely through $G_1/N$.  If in
addition $G_1/N$ is MF, then
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptExactRadicalFromCandidateQuotient}
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)=N.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The containment $N\le\ker\Theta$ gives the unique quotient
factorization for every such $\Theta$.  For the
reverse containment in the displayed equality, compose the quotient
map $G_1\to G_1/N$ with an injective homomorphism $G_1/N\to\U(\mathcal Q)$:
its kernel is exactly $N$, while the MF residual lies in the kernel of
every homomorphism to $\U(\mathcal Q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[residual reduction to the quotient]\label{cor:pullback}
Let $N\trianglelefteq G_1$ satisfy
$N\le\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$ and let
$q\colon G_1\to G_1/N$ be the quotient map.  Then
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptRadicalReductionToQuotient}
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)
 \;=\;
 q^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1/N)\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the forward containment, Lemma~\ref{lem:portable} gives
$q(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1))
\subseteq\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1/N)$.  Conversely, suppose
$q(x)\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1/N)$ and let $\Theta$ be
a homomorphism $G_1\to\U(\mathcal Q)$.  Since
$N\le\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(G_1)$, $\Theta$ factors as
$\bar\Theta\circ q$ with $\bar\Theta$ a homomorphism
$G_1/N\to\U(\mathcal Q)$; hence $\Theta(x)=\bar\Theta(q(x))=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nofaithful}
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra admitting an injective, possibly
nonunital, $*$-homomorphism into a matrix quotient.  Then the group $E$
admits no injective homomorphism into $\U(A)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralNonMFConsequences}{GroupApproximation.LiteralNonMFConsequences.literal_no_faithful_corona_subalgebra_target}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $j\colon A\to\mathcal Q$ be the injective, possibly nonunital,
$*$-homomorphism and let $\phi\colon E\to\U(A)$.  Define
\[
   \widehat j(u)=j(u)+1-j(1_A)\in\U(\mathcal Q).
\]
Then \(\widehat j\) is an injective homomorphism on \(\U(A)\), so
\(\widehat j\circ\phi\) is a homomorphism \(E\to\U(\mathcal Q)\).
By Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, \((\widehat j\circ\phi)(w)=1\); injectivity
of \(\widehat j\) gives \(\phi(w)=1\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} has
\(w\neq1\), so \(\phi\) cannot be injective.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Permanence and its failure}

\begin{lemma}[permanence]\label{lem:permanence}
Let all groups be countable.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every subgroup of an MF group is MF.
\item Every residually finite group is MF.
\item Every locally finite group is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.IsOperatorMF.subgroup}
\leanverified{Sofic/OperatorMFPositiveControls}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_of_residuallyFinite}
\leanverified{Sofic/LocallyFiniteMF}{GroupApproximation.isOperatorMF_of_locallyFinite}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1)  Restrict an MF embedding of the ambient group to the subgroup.
(2)  For a finite test set $F$, choose a finite quotient that is injective on
$F$ and compose it with the left regular permutation action.  This gives an
exact finite-dimensional representation.  Distinct permutations differ in a
column of their permutation matrices and therefore have operator-norm distance
at least $1$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-equivalences} now applies.
(3)  A finite test set $F$ lies in a finite subgroup $K$.  The left
regular representation $\lambda_K\colon K\to\U(\C^{K})$ is a
finite-dimensional unitary representation of $K$ by permutation
matrices.  Because a local model is defined on all of $G$, set
\[
 V(g)=\lambda_K(g)\ \ (g\in K),\qquad V(g)=1\ \ (g\notin K).
\]
On the test set $F\subseteq K$, this map is exactly multiplicative,
since products of test elements lie in $K$ and $\lambda_K$ is a
homomorphism; the values outside $K$ do not enter the two conditions.
It separates as in (2).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[quotients]\label{cor:quotclosure}
MF groups are not closed under quotients: the free group
\(F_8\) is MF, and the explicit group \(E\) is its quotient
but is not MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralMFQuotientControls}{GroupApproximation.LiteralMFQuotientControls.manuscriptQuotientNonclosure}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Map the eight free generators onto the eight displayed generators of \(E\).
The resulting homomorphism is surjective because those generators define the
displayed presentation.  The group \(F_8\) is residually finite, hence MF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(2), while \(E\) is not MF by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

\section{Commuting orbits and the MF residual}\label{subsec:collapse}

In Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-criterion} the corner is the negative
spectral corner of a central involution.  Here it is an arbitrary
projection of $\mathcal Q$ whose conjugation orbit commutes.

\begin{theorem}[a commuting orbit forces commutation]\label{thm:projection-collapse}
Let $H$ be countable, let $L\le H$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let
$s\in H$ satisfy $s\gamma s^{-1}\in L$ for every $\gamma\in L$, and let
$\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a homomorphism.  Let
$p\in\mathcal Q$ be a projection such that $p$ commutes with
$\Theta(s\gamma s^{-1})$ for every $\gamma\in L$ and the projections
$\Theta(\gamma)\,p\,\Theta(\gamma)^*$, $\gamma\in L$, commute pairwise.
Then $p$ commutes with $\Theta(L)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ProjectionCompressionCollapse}{GroupApproximation.ProjectionCompressionCollapse.corona_projection_collapse}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Property~\textup{(T)} makes $L$ finitely generated; fix a finite
symmetric generating set $S=\{a_1,\dots,a_m\}$.  Write
$p_g=\Theta(g)\,p\,\Theta(g)^{*}$ and $\Delta_g=p_g-p$ for $g\in L$, and
suppose $\Delta_{g_0}\ne0$ for some $g_0\in L$.

Since $p_g$ and $p$ are commuting projections, $\Delta_g$ is self-adjoint
with $\Delta_g^{3}=\Delta_g$, so $\Delta_g^{2}$ is a projection and $\Delta_g\Delta_g^{2}=\Delta_g$;
write $q_g=\Delta_g^{2}$.  All the $p_g$ commute, hence so do all the $\Delta_g$
and all the $q_g$.  Put
\[
 q=1-\prod_{a\in S}(1-q_a),
\]
the join of the projections $q_a$, $a\in S$.  The elements $g$ with
$p_g=p$ form a subgroup of $L$, so if $q=0$, and hence $\Delta_a=0$ for
every $a\in S$, then $\Delta_g=0$ for every $g\in L$, contradicting
$\Delta_{g_0}\ne0$.  Thus $q\ne0$.

\emph{The $q$-normalized Hilbert space.}  Take a self-adjoint bounded
lift $(x_n)$ of $q$.  Since $\opnorm{x_n^{2}-x_n}\to0$, for all
sufficiently large $n$ the spectrum of $x_n$ avoids $[1/4,3/4]$.
Choose a continuous function $f\colon\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ with
$f=0$ on $(-\infty,1/4]$ and $f=1$ on $[3/4,\infty)$.  Continuous
functional calculus then gives, for all sufficiently large $n$, a
projection $Q_n=f(x_n)$ with $\opnorm{Q_n-x_n}\to0$; define $Q_n=0$
on the finitely many remaining coordinates.  Thus $(Q_n)$ is a
projection lift of $q$.

Let $J=\{n:Q_n\ne0\}$.  Because $q\ne0$, the set $J$ is infinite.
Restriction of coordinates induces a $*$-homomorphism
\[
 \rho_J\colon
 \prod_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)/\bigoplus_nM_{d_n}(\mathbb C)
 \longrightarrow
 \prod_{n\in J}M_{d_n}(\mathbb C)/\bigoplus_{n\in J}M_{d_n}(\mathbb C).
\]
Replace $\Theta$ by $\rho_J\circ\Theta$ and every element introduced
above by its image under $\rho_J$.  All projection, commutation and
cocycle identities are preserved, while the new image of $q$ is still
nonzero because its lift consists of nonzero projections and therefore
has norm $1$ at every coordinate of $J$.  Relabel $J$ by $\mathbb N$;
then, without changing notation, we have
$k_n=\operatorname{rank}Q_n\ge1$ for every $n$.

Give $M_{d_n}(\C)$ the inner product
$\langle x,y\rangle_n=\operatorname{Tr}(y^{*}x)/k_n$, write
$\lVert x\rVert_{\mathrm F}=\operatorname{Tr}(x^{*}x)^{1/2}$ for the
unnormalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, and let $K_\omega$ be the
Hilbert-space ultraproduct of these spaces along a free ultrafilter
$\omega$.

\emph{The corner-supported ideal.}  Let $\mathcal I_q$ be the
algebraic two-sided ideal generated by $q$, namely the set of finite
sums $\sum_{j\le r}x_jqy_j$ with $x_j,y_j\in\mathcal Q$.  It is
invariant under inner automorphisms.  It is not closed in norm; the
finite-sum description gives the rank bound \eqref{eq:rank-frobenius}
below.

For $z=\sum_{j\le r}x_jqy_j\in\mathcal I_q$, choose bounded coordinate
lifts $X_{j,n}$ of $x_j$ and $Y_{j,n}$ of $y_j$ and put
$Z_n=\sum_{j\le r}X_{j,n}Q_nY_{j,n}$; then
$\operatorname{rank}(Z_n)\le rk_n$.  The square of the Frobenius norm
is the sum of the squares of the singular values; at most
$\operatorname{rank}(Z_n)$ of them are nonzero and each is at most the
operator norm, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-frobenius}
 \lVert Z_n\rVert_{\mathrm F}^{2}
 \;\le\;\operatorname{rank}(Z_n)\opnorm{Z_n}^{2}
 \;\le\;r\,k_n\opnorm{Z_n}^{2}
\end{equation}
and $(Z_n)$ is a bounded sequence for the inner products
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_n$.  Its class in $K_\omega$ depends only on
$z=\sum_jx_jqy_j$: a sequence $Z_n'$ obtained from a second
decomposition, with $r'$ summands, satisfies $\opnorm{Z_n-Z_n'}\to0$
and $\operatorname{rank}(Z_n-Z_n')\le(r+r')k_n$, so
$\lVert Z_n-Z_n'\rVert_{\mathrm F}^{2}/k_n\to0$ by
\eqref{eq:rank-frobenius}.  Write $\Lambda(z)=[Z_n]_\omega$.  The map
$\Lambda\colon\mathcal I_q\to K_\omega$ is linear,
\[
 \lVert\Lambda(q)\rVert
 =\lim_\omega\Bigl(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Q_n}{k_n}\Bigr)^{1/2}=1,
\]
and $\Lambda(z)=0$ implies $\Lambda(xzy)=0$ for all
$x,y\in\mathcal Q$, since bounded lifts of $x$ and $y$ multiply
$\lVert Z_n\rVert_{\mathrm F}$ by a bounded factor.

\emph{An exact cocycle.}  For $a\in S$ the projection $q_a$ annihilates
$\prod_{b\in S}(1-q_b)$, so $q_aq=q_a$ and
$\Delta_a=\Delta_aq_a=\Delta_aq\in\mathcal I_q$.  From
$p_{gh}=\Theta(g)\,p_h\,\Theta(g)^{*}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse-cocycle}
 \Delta_{gh}=\Delta_g+\Theta(g)\,\Delta_h\,\Theta(g)^{*}\qquad(g,h\in L),
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal I_q$ is inner-invariant, so $\Delta_g\in\mathcal I_q$ for
every $g\in L$.

Choose unitary coordinate lifts $U_n(h)$ of $\Theta(h)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:unitarycorona}).  The formula
$\pi(h)=[\Ad U_n(h)]_\omega$ defines a unitary representation of $H$ on
$K_\omega$: conjugation by a unitary preserves each
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_n$, and
$\opnorm{\Ad u-\Ad v}\le2\opnorm{u-v}$.  Moreover,
$\pi(h)\Lambda(z)=\Lambda\bigl(\Theta(h)\,z\,\Theta(h)^{*}\bigr)$ on
the closed invariant subspace
$K_q=\overline{\Lambda(\mathcal I_q)}$.  By
\eqref{eq:collapse-cocycle} the map $\beta(g)=\Lambda(\Delta_g)$ satisfies
\[
 \beta(gh)=\beta(g)+\pi(g)\beta(h)\qquad(g,h\in L),
\]
a $1$-cocycle for $\pi|_L$ with values in $K_q$.

\emph{The cocycle is nonzero.}  Suppose $\beta(a)=0$ for every $a\in S$.  Put $r_1=1$ and
$r_i=\prod_{j<i}(1-q_{a_j})$ for $i\ge2$.  The products
$e_i=r_iq_{a_i}$ are projections with $e_i=r_i-r_{i+1}$, so they are
pairwise orthogonal and $\sum_ie_i=1-r_{m+1}=q$.  Each $\Delta_{a_i}$
commutes with $r_i$, so $e_i=(\Delta_{a_i}r_i)\,\Delta_{a_i}$ and
$\Lambda(e_i)=0$, whence $\Lambda(q)=0$ against
$\lVert\Lambda(q)\rVert=1$.  So $\beta$ is not identically zero.

\emph{Property~\textup{(T)}.}  By the Delorme--Guichardet theorem
\cite{Delorme,Guichardet}, $\beta$ is a coboundary: there is $y\in K_q$
with $\beta(g)=y-\pi(g)y$ for every $g\in L$.  The hypothesis on $p$
gives $\Delta_{s\gamma s^{-1}}=0$, hence
$\pi(s\gamma s^{-1})y=y$ for every $\gamma\in L$.

Normalize the Hilbert--Schmidt norms by $k_n=\operatorname{rank}Q_n$
rather than by $d_n$ and apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport-variants} with that weight.  In its proof,
$P$ is the projection onto
$\operatorname{Fix}\pi(L)$ and the conjugate
$Q=\pi(s)P\pi(s)^{*}$ onto
$\operatorname{Fix}\pi(sLs^{-1})$, with $P\le Q$ and $P\sim Q$ in
the same finite norm ultraproduct.  Lemma~\ref{lem:finitecompare}
therefore gives $P=Q$.  Hence $\pi(g)y=y$ and
$\beta(g)=0$ for every $g\in L$, contradicting the previous paragraph.
Therefore $\Delta_g=0$ for every $g\in L$ in the original corona; equivalently,
$p$ commutes with $\Theta(g)$ for every $g\in L$.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, every projection of $\Theta(sLs^{-1})'\cap\mathcal Q$
whose $\Theta(L)$-conjugates commute pairwise already lies in
$\Theta(L)'\cap\mathcal Q$.

\begin{definition}[involutive orbit witness]\label{def:invwitness}
Let $H$ be a group, $L\le H$ a subgroup, and $s\in H$.  An element
$k\in H$ is an \emph{involutive orbit witness} for $(L,s)$ if
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(W1)] $k^2=1$;
\item[(W2)] $s\gamma s^{-1}$ commutes with $k$ for every $\gamma\in L$;
\item[(W3)] the conjugates $\gamma_1k\gamma_1^{-1}$ and
  $\gamma_2k\gamma_2^{-1}$ commute for all $\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in L$.
\end{enumerate}
The pair $(L,s)$ determines the normal subgroup
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptInvolutiveCollapsePattern}
\[
  D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)
  \;=\;
  \bigl\langle\!\bigl\langle\,
  [\gamma,k]\;:\;\gamma\in L,\
  k\text{ a witness for }(L,s)
  \,\bigr\rangle\!\bigr\rangle_H .
\]
\end{definition}

In a permutational wreath product $K^{(X)}\rtimes G$ with $L\le G$
conjugated into itself by $s$ and $x_0\in X$ a point fixed by
$sLs^{-1}$, every involution in the copy of $K$ supported at $x_0$ is
a witness: distinct coordinate copies commute, and $sLs^{-1}$ fixes
$x_0$.

\begin{theorem}[the orbit of an involutive witness]\label{thm:collapse}
Let $H$ be countable, let $L\le H$ be a subgroup with
property~\textup{(T)}, and let $s\in H$ satisfy
$s\gamma s^{-1}\in L$ for every $\gamma\in L$.  Then
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptInvolutiveCollapse}
\[
  D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)\;\le\;\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ be a homomorphism and $k$ a
witness for $(L,s)$.  By (W1) the unitary $u=\Theta(k)$ is
self-adjoint, so $p=\tfrac12(1-u)$ is a projection, and
\[
 \Theta(\gamma)\,p\,\Theta(\gamma)^{*}
 =\tfrac12\bigl(1-\Theta(\gamma k\gamma^{-1})\bigr)
 \qquad(\gamma\in L).
\]
By (W2) the projection $p$ commutes with $\Theta(s\gamma s^{-1})$ for
$\gamma\in L$, and by (W3) the displayed projections commute pairwise.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-collapse}, $p$ commutes with
$\Theta(L)$; hence so does $u=1-2p$, and $\Theta([\gamma,k])=1$ for every
$\gamma\in L$.  Kernels are normal, so $\Theta$ is trivial on
$D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[finite-order witnesses]\label{rem:torsion-collapse}
Condition (W1) can be relaxed to finite order.  Let $H$ be countable,
let $L\le H$ have property~\textup{(T)}, let $s\in H$ satisfy
$s\gamma s^{-1}\in L$ for every $\gamma\in L$, and let $k\in H$ have
finite order $m$.  If $sLs^{-1}$ centralizes $k$ and the conjugates
$\gamma k\gamma^{-1}$, $\gamma\in L$, commute pairwise, then every
homomorphism $\Theta\colon H\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ satisfies
$\Theta([\gamma,k])=1$ for every $\gamma\in L$.  For
$j\in\mathbf{Z}/m$, let
\[
 p_j=\frac1m\sum_{t\in\mathbf{Z}/m}
 e^{-2\pi ijt/m}\Theta(k)^t.
\]
These are the spectral projections of $\Theta(k)$.  The hypotheses
imply that $\Theta(sLs^{-1})$ centralizes every $p_j$ and that their
$\Theta(L)$-conjugates commute pairwise.  Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-collapse}
therefore makes every $p_j$ commute with $\Theta(L)$.  Fourier
reconstruction,
$\Theta(k)=\sum_j e^{2\pi ij/m}p_j$, then shows that $\Theta(k)$ also
commutes with $\Theta(L)$.  The normal closure of all such commutators,
over all finite-order witnesses for $(L,s)$, lies in
$\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)$, with equality whenever the
corresponding quotient of $H$ is MF.
\leanverified{Sofic/TorsionSpectralCollapse}{GroupApproximation.TorsionSpectralCollapse.actualCoronaMFInvisible_of_torsionWitness}
\leanverified{Sofic/TorsionSpectralCollapse}{GroupApproximation.TorsionSpectralCollapse.torsionCollapseDefect_le_actualCoronaMFResidual_of_kazhdan}
\leanverified{Sofic/TorsionSpectralCollapse}{GroupApproximation.TorsionSpectralCollapse.actualCoronaMFResidual_eq_torsionCollapseDefect_of_kazhdan}
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:signfree}]
Write $q\colon E\to E/\langle w\rangle$, $\bar c=q(\hat c)$, and
$\bar L=q(\iota(\mathcal B))$.  The element $\bar c$ is an involution
because $c^2=1$, and $q(t)\bar Lq(t)^{-1}$ centralizes $\bar c$
because conjugating the relation $[c,\iota(\mathcal B)]=1$
of \eqref{eq:E} by $t$ gives
$[\,t\iota(\mathcal B)t^{-1},\,\hat c\,]=1$ in $E$, where $\hat c=tct^{-1}$.  The
subgroup that centralizes $\hat c$ is the conjugate one, which is why the
relation is conjugated by $t$.  Property~\textup{(T)} passes to $\bar L$,
and $q(t)$ conjugates it into itself.  Thus (W1) and (W2) of Definition~\ref{def:invwitness} hold, and only
(W3) remains.

\emph{Pairwise commutation of the orbit.}  The linear generators $x,y,z$
commute with $\hat c$, and conjugation by
$t$ sends each $v_i$ to $v_i^2$, so $\iota(v_i^2)$ commutes with $\hat c$
as well.  The conjugate $\iota(g)\,\hat c\,\iota(g)^{-1}$ therefore
depends only on the exponent vector of the translation part of $g$
modulo $2$, so there are exactly eight such conjugates, indexed by
$(\Z/2)^3$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} the
distinguished word is
$w=[\hat c,\iota(v_1)\,\hat c\,\iota(v_1)^{-1}]$, the commutator attached to
the class of $v_1$.  The linear part acts through
$\SL_3(\mathbb Z/2)$, which is transitive on the nonzero parity
classes; the seven words
$1,x,x^2,z,yx,y^2x,xyx$ in the linear generators send the class of $v_1$
to all seven nonzero classes, and each commutes with $\hat c$.
Conjugating the displayed identity by the corresponding $\iota(r)$
shows that every nonzero parity commutator is a conjugate of $w$; the
zero class gives $[\hat c,\hat c]=1$.  Since $w$ is central, all eight lie in
$\langle w\rangle$.  Conjugating the commutator of two arbitrary
conjugates by the inverse of one of them reduces it to this case.  Hence all conjugates of
$\bar c$ under $\bar L$ commute pairwise.  Thus $\bar c$ is an
involutive orbit witness for $(\bar L,q(t))$, and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}
$[q(\iota(v_1)),\bar c]\in
\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E/\langle w\rangle)$.

The homomorphism $E\to W$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} sends
$[\iota(v_1),\hat c]$ to the
product of the Clifford generators at the two distinct points $\tau o$
and $j(\bar v_1)\tau o$, and sends $\langle w\rangle$ into
$\langle\zeta\rangle$.  The product of the Clifford generators at two
distinct points does not lie in $\langle\zeta\rangle$.  Hence
$[\iota(v_1),\hat c]\notin\langle w\rangle$, and its image in
$E/\langle w\rangle$ is nontrivial.  This image lies in the MF
residual, so Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot} implies that the quotient
is non-MF.
Corollary~\ref{cor:pullback} identifies
$\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ with the $q$-preimage of
$\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E/\langle w\rangle)$, so $u$ belongs
to it.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[collapse computes the residual]\label{cor:collapsequot}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}, write
$q\colon H\to H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$ for the quotient map.  Then
\[
  \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)
  \;=\;
  q^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}
  (H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s))\bigr),
\]
and if $H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$ is MF, then
$\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(H)=D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptCollapseRadicalReduction}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} with
Corollaries~\ref{cor:pullback} and~\ref{cor:exactradical}.
\end{proof}

If $H/D_{\mathrm{coll}}(L,s)$ is MF, then it is the universal MF
quotient of $H$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:univquot}): every homomorphism
from $H$ to an MF group factors through it.

\subsection{The exact residual of $E$}\label{sec:exact-residual}
For the literal group $E$, put $d=\hat c=tct^{-1}$,
$u=[d,v_1]$, and $D=\langle\!\langle u\rangle\!\rangle_E$.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)=D=N_{\mathrm{conj}},
 \qquad
 E/D\cong \Sigma*_{\mathcal B}(\mathcal B\times C_2),
\]
and $E/D$ is MF.  Moreover, if $q\colon E\to E/\langle w\rangle$ and
$D_{\mathrm{coll}}$ is the collapse defect used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:signfree}, then
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E/\langle w\rangle)
   =D_{\mathrm{coll}}=D/\langle w\rangle,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)=q^{-1}(D_{\mathrm{coll}}).
\]

\begin{proof}
$d$ commutes with the linear part of $\mathcal B$ and with every
even translation.  Hence $[d,b]$ depends only on the translation part of
$b$ modulo $2$.  The linear part is transitive on the seven nonzero classes
of $(\mathbb Z/2)^3$, by the explicit seven-word calculation used in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:signfree}; every nontrivial $[d,b]$ is therefore a
conjugate of $u$.  This proves $D=N_{\mathrm{conj}}$.  Moreover,
$u\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:signfree}; since the residual is normal,
$D=\langle\!\langle u\rangle\!\rangle_E
\le\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$.  Universal $2$-norm triviality
alone does not imply this corona statement (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

In the quotient by $D$, the involution $d$ commutes with all of
$\mathcal B$.  Replacing $c$ by $d$, so that $c=t^{-1}dt$, makes the old
relation $[c,\mathcal B]=1$ redundant after conjugation by $t$; the
central-mark relations are redundant as well because $w=u^2$.  The remaining
presentation is precisely
\[
 \langle\mathcal B,t,d:\ tbt^{-1}=\alpha(b),\ d^2=1,
 [d,\mathcal B]=1\rangle
 \cong \Sigma*_{\mathcal B}(\mathcal B\times C_2).
\]
The group $\Sigma$ is finitely generated linear in characteristic zero and
hence residually finite \cite{Malcev}.  Put $G_0=\Sigma\times C_2$.  Choose a
separating chain of finite quotients of $G_0$, let $A$ be the $C^*$-algebra
generated by their regular representations in the norm matrix corona, and let
$\tau$ be an ultralimit of the normalized traces.  The algebra $A$ is
separable because it is generated by the countable family of group unitaries;
it is MF and
$\tau(g)=0$ for every $g\ne1$.  Put $C=C^*(\mathcal B)\subseteq A$.
Shulman's symmetric amalgam theorem \cite[Theorem~10]{Shulman} makes
the full unital amalgam $A*_{C}A$ MF.  The canonical homomorphism
$G_0*_{\mathcal B}G_0\to\U(A*_{C}A)$ is injective.  Indeed, in the GNS von
Neumann algebra of $(A,\tau)$, the trace-preserving conditional expectation
onto $C''$ sends every group unitary outside $\mathcal B$ to zero.  The
reduced amalgam trace therefore vanishes on every nontrivial Bass--Serre
reduced word.  Hence
$G_0*_{\mathcal B}G_0$ is MF.  Normal forms for amalgamated free products give
an embedding
\[
 \Sigma*_{\mathcal B}(\mathcal B\times C_2)
 \hookrightarrow G_0*_{\mathcal B}G_0,
\]
using the first vertex group for $\Sigma$ and the second for
$\mathcal B\times C_2$.  Subgroups of MF groups are MF, so $E/D$ is MF.
Corollary~\ref{cor:exactradical} now gives
$\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)=D$.  Applying
Corollary~\ref{cor:pullback} first to $\langle w\rangle\le D$ and then to
the collapse defect gives the two identities downstairs.  Explicitly,
$q(u)^{-1}=[q(\iota(v_1)),\bar c]$ is one of the generators of
$D_{\mathrm{coll}}$, so $D/\langle w\rangle\le D_{\mathrm{coll}}$; the
reverse inclusion follows from the residual calculation just proved.
\end{proof}

\section{Group algebras, soficity, and permanence}
\label{sec:algebras-sofic}

\subsection{The reduced group algebra of \texorpdfstring{$E$}{E}}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:faithfultrace}
Let $G_1$ be a countable discrete group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The canonical tracial state $\tau$ on $\Cred(G_1)$ is
faithful.
\item Every unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ with a faithful tracial state
is stably finite: for every $k\ge1$, every isometry in $M_k(A)$ is a
unitary.
\leanverified{Sofic/ManuscriptExactWrappers}{GroupApproximation.ManuscriptExactWrappers.manuscriptFaithfulTraceAndStableFiniteness}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Realize $\Cred(G_1)\subseteq B(\ell^2G_1)$ as the norm closure
of the span of the left translation unitaries $\lambda_g$.  Let
$\rho_g$ be the right translation unitaries,
$(\rho_g\xi)(h)=\xi(hg)$.  Each $\rho_g$ commutes with each
$\lambda_{g'}$, hence with every element of $\Cred(G_1)$.  The
canonical trace is $\tau(x)=\langle x\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle$.  If
$\tau(x^*x)=0$ then $\lVert x\delta_e\rVert=0$, so $x\delta_e=0$,
and then
$x\delta_g=x\rho_{g^{-1}}\delta_e=\rho_{g^{-1}}x\delta_e=0$ for
every $g\in G_1$.  The vectors $\delta_g$ are total in
$\ell^2G_1$, so $x=0$.

(2) On $M_k(A)$ the functional
$\tau_k\bigl([x_{ij}]\bigr)=\tfrac1k\sum_i\tau(x_{ii})$ is a state
with the trace property
$\tau_k(xy)=\tfrac1k\sum_{i,j}\tau(x_{ij}y_{ji})=\tau_k(yx)$.
It is faithful.  Indeed, the $i$-th diagonal entry of $x^*x$ is
$\sum_jx_{ji}^*x_{ji}$.  If $\tau_k(x^*x)=0$, then
$\tau(x_{ji}^*x_{ji})=0$ and hence $x_{ji}=0$ for all $i,j$.  If
$v\in M_k(A)$ satisfies $v^*v=1$, then $y:=1-vv^*$ is a projection
with
$\tau_k(y)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(vv^*)=\tau_k(1)-\tau_k(v^*v)=0$; since
$y=y^*y$, faithfulness gives $y=0$, i.e.\ $vv^*=1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Clifford quotient and permanence}

The map $E\to W$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} is surjective: its image
contains $\Sigma$ and the generator $c_o$ at the base point, hence
every Clifford generator by transitivity of
the $\Sigma$-action on $X$.  Since $E$ is generated by
$v_1,x,y,z,t,c$ (Section~\ref{sec:group}), their six
images generate $W$.  The map sends
$w\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ to the nontrivial sign
$\zeta$, so $\zeta\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(W)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:portable}.  Hence the finitely generated group $W$ is
not MF\@.  Neither is $\Cred(W)$: if $\Cred(W)$ embedded in a matrix
quotient, the map $u\mapsto e(u)+1-e(1)$ would give an injective
homomorphism $W\to\U(\mathcal Q)$, as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

\paragraph{Failure of extension permanence.}
MF groups are not closed under extensions.  More precisely, $W$ fits into
an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow\Cl(X)\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow\Sigma
 \longrightarrow1
\]
whose kernel is locally finite --- hence LEF, sofic and MF --- and
whose quotient is MF, while $W$ itself is finitely generated and not
MF\@.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralWitnessConsequences}{GroupApproximation.LiteralWitnessConsequences.literalWitness_locallyFiniteKernel_nonMF}

\begin{proof}
The Clifford group $\Cl(X)$ is locally finite: every finitely
generated subgroup involves only finitely many sites, hence is contained
in the corresponding finite Clifford group.  Therefore $\Cl(X)$ is MF
by Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(3).  The group $\Sigma$ identifies with
the subgroup of
$\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ generated by $\bar\Gamma$ and $D$: the levels
are $D^{-n}\bar\Gamma D^{n}$, and the determinant gives the
$\mathbb Z$-coordinate.  It is residually finite.  Modulo every odd
integer, the dilation matrix is invertible because its determinant is
$8$; hence the congruence reduction of the integral affine model is
compatible with the dilation and extends over the direct limit and
its shift.  These finite quotients separate every nontrivial element
of $\Sigma$, consistent with Mal'cev's theorem for finitely generated
linear groups \cite{Malcev}.  Thus $\Sigma$ is MF by Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(2),
whereas $W$ is not MF by the preceding construction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Extensions by $\Z$.}
The two canonical projections define a surjection $W\to\Z$ with kernel
$\Cl(X)\rtimes T$.  Every finite subset of the kernel lies in
$L\rtimes\bar\Gamma_n$ for some finite invariant subgroup
$L\le\Cl(X)$ and some level $\bar\Gamma_n$.  This semidirect product is
residually finite because the action on $L$ factors through a finite
quotient.  The kernel is therefore LEF and hence MF.  Thus $W$ is a non-MF
extension of an LEF group by $\Z$.  It is nevertheless sofic by the
Elek--Szab\'o amenable-extension theorem \cite{ElekSzabo}.
\leanverified{Sofic/LEFMarkedCompression}{GroupApproximation.LiteralLEFExtension.literalWitness_LEFKernel_integerExtension_nonMF}
\leanverified{Sofic/SoficByAmenablePermanence}{GroupApproximation.SoficByAmenablePermanence.manuscriptSoficOfSoficKerOfAmenableQuotient}

\paragraph{A simple sofic envelope.}
There is a countable simple sofic group $S_{\mathrm{simp}}$ with
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(S_{\mathrm{simp}})=S_{\mathrm{simp}}.
\]
Equivalently, every homomorphism from $S_{\mathrm{simp}}$ to an MF group is
trivial.

\begin{proof}
By Elek--Szab\'o, every countable sofic group embeds in a countable simple
sofic group \cite[Theorem~1]{ElekSzaboHyper}.  Embed $E$, which is sofic
by Theorem~\ref{thm:Esofic}, in such a group.  By functoriality of
the MF residual, the image of $w$ is a nontrivial element of the MF
residual of the simple group.  This residual is a nontrivial normal
subgroup, so simplicity makes it the whole group.  Every homomorphism to an MF group maps the MF residual to $1$.
\end{proof}
\leanverified{Sofic/SimpleSoficEnvelopeUnconditional}{GroupApproximation.SimpleSoficEnvelopeUnconditional.manuscript_simpleSoficEnvelope}

\subsection{Soficity of $E$}\label{sec:Esofic}

Deleting the letter $c$ and the families \textup{(C)} and \textup{(W)}
from \eqref{eq:E} leaves the presentation
$\langle\,\mathcal B,t\mid tut^{-1}=\bar\alpha(u)\,\rangle$ of the
ascending HNN extension of the base along the doubling endomorphism.
By Remark~\ref{rem:classical-base} the base is $\bar\Gamma$, so
\[
 E/\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\;\cong\;\Sigma
\]
for the group $\Sigma=T\rtimes\Z$ of Section~\ref{sec:witness}, with
its site set $X=\Sigma/B$, base point $o$, shift $\tau$ and level-zero
base $B=j(\bar\Gamma)$.  The levels
\[
 \bar\Gamma_n=\tau^{-n}B\tau^{n}\qquad(n\ge0)
\]
form an ascending chain of copies of $\bar\Gamma$ with union
$T$.  Put $K=\tau^{-1}B\tau$; then $B\le K$ and
$[K:B]=[\bar\Gamma:\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)]=8$.  The cosets
$\Sigma/K$ are the \emph{blocks}, and each block, as a set of sites, has
$[K:B]$ elements.

Let $\mathcal G$ be the graph on $X$ whose edges are the $\Sigma$-orbit
of the pair $\{\tau o,\,j(\bar v_1)\tau o\}$, whose entries are
distinct by Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}.  Two sites are adjacent in
$\mathcal G$ exactly when they are distinct and lie in a common block.
Adjacent sites share a block because the two sites are $gB$ and $hB$
for the representatives $g=\tau$ and $h=j(\bar v_1)\tau$, and
$g^{-1}h=\tau^{-1}j(\bar v_1)\tau\in K$ since $j(\bar v_1)\in B$ and
$K=\tau^{-1}B\tau$; hence $gK=hK$.  (Sites are cosets, so the product
and inverse have to be taken in $\Sigma$ on representatives, not on the
sites themselves.)  Conversely, the bijection
$u\,\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)\mapsto\tau^{-1}j(u)\tau o$ identifies the
block of $o$ with $\bar\Gamma/\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$ and the
generating edge with the pair
$\{\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma),\,\bar v_1\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)\}$.  Left
multiplication by $\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$ on the coset space
$\bar\Gamma/\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$ is transitive on the nontrivial
cosets, so every pair of distinct sites of a block is the image of
the generating pair.  Let $L=\langle x,y,z\rangle$ be the linear part,
so $\bar\Gamma=\Z^3\rtimes L$.  Since $\bar\alpha$ squares the three
translations and fixes $x,y,z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}), its image is
$(2\Z^3)\rtimes L$.  Reduction of the translation coordinate modulo~$2$
identifies $\bar\Gamma/\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$ with $(\Z/2)^3$.
Under that identification left multiplication by an
element of $\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$ with linear part $N$ and
(necessarily even) translation part sends a class $\bar u$ to
$\bar N\bar u$, so the action factors through the linear action of the
reduction $\bar L\le\GL_3(\mathbb F_2)$ of $L$.  The seven words
\[
 1,\quad x,\quad x^2,\quad z,\quad yx,\quad y^2x,\quad xyx
\]
send $\bar e_1$ to
$\bar e_1$, $\bar e_3$, $\bar e_2$, $(0,1,1)$, $(1,1,0)$, $(1,1,1)$,
$(1,0,1)$, the seven nonzero classes.  This proves the required
transitivity.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralAffineCosetTransitivity}{GroupApproximation.LiteralAffineCosetTransitivity.conjD_cosetTransitive}

For a graph $\mathcal G$ on $X$, let $C(\mathcal G)$ be the group
presented by generators $\zeta$ and $c_\xi$ $(\xi\in X)$ and relations
\[
 \zeta^2=c_\xi^2=1,\qquad [\zeta,c_\xi]=1,\qquad
 [c_\xi,c_\eta]=\zeta\ \ \text{for each edge }\{\xi,\eta\}
 \text{ of }\mathcal G .
\]
For the complete graph on $X$ this is $\Cl(X)$ of
Construction~\ref{con:clifford}, and adjoining the anticommutation
relations at the remaining pairs gives a surjection
$C(\mathcal G)\to\Cl(X)$.

\begin{proposition}[normal form for $E$]\label{prop:blocknormalform}
There is an isomorphism
\[
 E\;\cong\;C(\mathcal G)\rtimes\Sigma
\]
under which $\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle$ is the factor
$C(\mathcal G)$, the conjugate $gcg^{-1}$ is the lamp $c_{gB}$, and the
distinguished word $w$ is the sign $\zeta$.  Each level $\bar\Gamma_n$ has
finite orbits on $X$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralBlockNormalForm}{GroupApproximation.LiteralBlockNormalForm.markedGroupEquivModel}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralBlockNormalForm}{GroupApproximation.LiteralBlockNormalForm.lampKernelEquiv}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralBlockNormalForm}{GroupApproximation.LiteralBlockNormalForm.manuscriptSiteLampImage}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralBlockNormalForm}{GroupApproximation.LiteralBlockNormalForm.toModel_mark}

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Send the six base letters and $t$ to the corresponding generators of
$\Sigma$, and $c$ to $c_o$.  Families~\textup{(B)} and~\textup{(T)} of
\eqref{eq:E} hold in $\Sigma$ by construction.  For family~\textup{(C)}:
$c_o^2=1$, and $B$ fixes $o$, so each base generator fixes $c_o$.  For
family~\textup{(W)}: the pair $\{\tau o,\,j(\bar v_1)\tau o\}$ is an
edge, so the image of $w$ is
$[c_{\tau o},c_{j(\bar v_1)\tau o}]=\zeta$, which is central in
$C(\mathcal G)$ and fixed by $\Sigma$.  This defines
$E\to C(\mathcal G)\rtimes\Sigma$.

In the other direction, send $\zeta$ to $w$ and $c_{gB}$ to
$gcg^{-1}$.  The assignment is independent of the coset representative,
because the base centralizes $c$.  The images are involutions, and $w$
is central of order two.  Along an edge
$g\{\tau o,\,j(\bar v_1)\tau o\}$ the commutator of the two images is
$gwg^{-1}=w$, and $C(\mathcal G)$ imposes relations only along edges.
So the assignment respects the defining relations of
$C(\mathcal G)$.
Combined with the splitting $\Sigma\to E$ on the seven
remaining letters, this gives $C(\mathcal G)\rtimes\Sigma\to E$.  The
two composites fix the eight letters of $E$ and the generators
$\zeta,c_\xi$ of $C(\mathcal G)$, hence are the identity.

For the orbits, $\bar\alpha(\bar\Gamma)$ has index eight in
$\bar\Gamma$ --- the parity classes of the translation lattice --- so
$\tau$ conjugates $B$ onto a subgroup of finite index in itself.  Every
$\bar\Gamma_n$ is therefore commensurable with $B$, all of $\Sigma$
commensurates $B$, and orbit--stabilizer makes each $\bar\Gamma_n$-orbit in
$X=\Sigma/B$ finite.
\end{proof}

Composing the isomorphism with $C(\mathcal G)\to\Cl(X)$ gives the
surjection $E\to W$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}.  The group
$\Cl(X)$ is locally finite; $C(\mathcal G)$ is not, since two lamps at
sites of different blocks generate an infinite dihedral group and three
blocks give a nonabelian free subgroup.  For a finite set $S$ of sites
write
\[
 M_S=\bigl\langle\,\zeta,\;c_\xi\ (\xi\in S)\,\bigr\rangle
 \;\le\;C(\mathcal G).
\]

This distinction prevents a misleading wreath-product identification.
The auxiliary quotient $W/\langle\zeta\rangle$ is an ordinary
permutational lamp group, but
$C(\mathcal G)/\langle\zeta\rangle$ is the free product of the elementary
abelian $2$-groups attached to the blocks of $\mathcal G$.  Thus the present
normal form does not identify $E/\langle w\rangle$ with an ordinary
permutational wreath product.  It is nevertheless closely related to
central extensions of lamplighter groups \cite{FFG23,Bodart25}, to the
generalized wreath products of Kun--Thom \cite{KT}, to the sofic-action
framework of Gao, Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, and Patchell \cite{GKEP}, and
to the halo-product framework of Alekseev--Bradford
\cite{AlekseevBradford}.

\begin{lemma}[finite-site subgroups are residually finite]\label{lem:window}
For every finite set $S$ of sites the subgroup $M_S$ is residually
finite.
\leanverified{Sofic/BlockCliffordTowerSofic}{GroupApproximation.BlockCliffordTowerSofic.manuscriptResiduallyFiniteWindow}
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralSoficAssembly}{GroupApproximation.LiteralSoficAssembly.lampEquiv}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The quotient map by $\langle\zeta\rangle$ maps $C(\mathcal G)$ onto
the free product of the elementary abelian $2$-groups of the blocks,
and $M_S$ into the free product of the finitely many factors at blocks
meeting $S$.  A free
product of finite groups is residually finite.  The kernel of
$C(\mathcal G)\to C(\mathcal G)/\langle\!\langle\zeta\rangle\!\rangle$ is
the normal closure of $\zeta$, and $\zeta$ is central, so that normal
closure is $\langle\zeta\rangle$; hence the kernel of this map on $M_S$
lies in $\langle\zeta\rangle$.  The surjection
$C(\mathcal G)\to\Cl(X)$ sends $\zeta$ to the sign of $\Cl(X)$, which
is nontrivial, and $\Cl(X)$ is locally finite, so the image of the
finitely generated subgroup $M_S$ in $\Cl(X)$ is finite.  An element of $M_S$
in the kernel of the first map lies in $\langle\zeta\rangle$, and the
second map separates the two elements of that subgroup.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Esofic}]
Let $E_0$ be the kernel of the homomorphism $E\to\Z$ given by the
exponent sum in $t$.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:blocknormalform}, $E_0\cong C(\mathcal G)\rtimes
T$.

Take a finite subset of $E_0$.  Its $T$-coordinates lie in one level
$\bar\Gamma_n$, the chain being ascending, and its
$C(\mathcal G)$-coordinates involve only finitely many lamp generators
and possibly the sign, so they lie in $M_{S_0}$ for some finite set
$S_0$ of sites.  Let
$S$ be the union of the $\bar\Gamma_n$-orbits of the sites in $S_0$, a
finite set by Proposition~\ref{prop:blocknormalform}.  Then $M_S$ is
$\bar\Gamma_n$-invariant and contains $M_{S_0}$, so the finite subset lies
in $M_S\rtimes\bar\Gamma_n$.

That group is residually finite.  Indeed $M_S$ is residually finite by
Lemma~\ref{lem:window}, the level $\bar\Gamma_n\cong\bar\Gamma$ is
residually finite through the congruence quotients of its matrix model
(Lemma~\ref{lem:linear}), and the action of $\bar\Gamma_n$ on $M_S$
factors through a finite permutation group $F$ because $S$ is finite.
The kernel of $\bar\Gamma_n\to F$ has finite index, and the
corresponding finite-index subgroup of the semidirect product is
$M_S\times\ker(\bar\Gamma_n\to F)$, which is residually finite;
residual finiteness passes to a group containing a residually finite
subgroup of finite index.

Hence $E_0$ is a directed union of subgroups that embed in residually
finite groups; that is, $E_0$ is LEF and therefore sofic.  The group
$E$ is a split extension of $E_0$ by $\Z$, split by $t$, so the
Elek--Szab\'o amenable-extension theorem implies that $E$ is sofic
\cite{ElekSzabo}, and hence hyperlinear.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, $E$
is not MF\@.  LEF groups are MF \cite{CDE}, so $E$ is not LEF; a
residually finite group is LEF, so $E$ is not residually finite
either.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exactness.}
The same decomposition also proves that $E$ is exact.  The quotient
$C(\mathcal G)/\langle\zeta\rangle$ is the free product of the
elementary abelian $2$-groups attached to the blocks, each of order
$2^{8}$.  There are countably many blocks; enumerate them, and let
$Q_n$ be the free product of the factors attached to the first $n$.
Each factor is finite, hence exact, so $Q_n$ is a free product of
finitely many exact groups and is exact by Dykema's theorem
\cite{DykemaExact}.  The $Q_n$ increase with union the whole quotient.
In a discrete group every subgroup is open, so a group that is the
union of an increasing family of exact subgroups is exact
\cite[Lemma~2.5]{KWExact}, and the quotient is exact.  Since
$\langle\zeta\rangle$ is finite, extension permanence
\cite{KWPermanence} gives exactness of $C(\mathcal G)$.  The group $\Sigma\le\GL_4(\mathbb Q)$ is exact by
Guentner--Higson--Weinberger \cite{GHW}.  Finally
$E\cong C(\mathcal G)\rtimes\Sigma$ is an extension, hence is exact by
\cite{KWPermanence}.  For a discrete group, group exactness is
equivalent to exactness of its reduced group $C^*$-algebra
\cite{KWExact}; therefore $\Cred(E)$ is exact.


\section{Quasi-identities and undecidability}
\label{sec:marked-horn}

\subsection{A finite universal Horn obstruction}

The presentation of $E$ yields a finite quasi-identity ---
equivalently, a universal Horn sentence --- satisfied by every MF
group.  Write $R$ for the forty-one relators of \eqref{eq:E},
regarded as words in eight variables, and $w$ for the distinguished word in those
variables.

\begin{proposition}[finite quasi-identity]\label{prop:horn}
Every MF group $G$ satisfies the finite universal Horn sentence
\[
 \forall x_1,\dots,x_8\colon\quad
 \bigwedge_{r\in R}\,r(x_1,\dots,x_8)=1
 \;\Longrightarrow\; w(x_1,\dots,x_8)=1 ,
\]
while the canonical generating tuple of $E$ satisfies every relator in $R$
and has $w\ne1$.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralUniversalHorn}{GroupApproximation.LiteralUniversalHorn.manuscriptLiteralUniversalHorn}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A tuple satisfying the relators induces a homomorphism $E\to G$ sending
the distinguished word of $E$ to $w(x_1,\dots,x_8)$.  If $G$ is MF,
the image is trivial, because
$w\in\operatorname{Res}_{\mathrm{MF}}(E)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:A} and
the residual is functorial (Lemma~\ref{lem:portable}).
The canonical tuple of $E$ satisfies $R$ by definition, and its
distinguished word is nontrivial by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

In the space of eight-generator marked groups, the groups satisfying
all relators of $R$ and $w\ne1$ form a nonempty clopen set.  Both
conditions depend on finitely many word equalities and inequalities,
and $E$ belongs to this set.  Proposition~\ref{prop:horn} excludes every
MF group from it.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralMarkedCylinder}{GroupApproximation.LiteralMarkedCylinder.literal_nonempty_clopen_nonMF_cylinder}

\subsection{Undecidability of MF recognition}

Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(1) and Theorem~\ref{thm:A} show that MF is
a Markov property of finitely presented groups: the trivial group is
MF, while by subgroup heredity the finitely presented group $E$
embeds in no MF group.  The Adian--Rabin theorem therefore implies
that MF recognition is undecidable \cite{Rabin58}.

Fix an effective coding of finite presentations by natural numbers,
under which a code determines computably its number of generators and
its finite list of relator words, and write $G_e$ for the group
presented by the code $e$.

\begin{corollary}[undecidability of MF recognition]%
\label{cor:undecidable}
Write $W$ for the predicate on pairs consisting of a presentation code
and a word in the generators of that code, holding when the word is
trivial in the presented group.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $W$ is undecidable;
\item no algorithm decides, from a presentation code, whether the
presented group is MF;
\item $W$ is recursively enumerable, whereas its complement and the
set of presentation codes of non-MF groups are not.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\leanverified{Computability/BooneWordProblemUndecidable}{GroupApproximation.Computability.not_computablePred_wordProblemPred}
\leanverified{Computability/BooneWordProblemUndecidable}{GroupApproximation.Computability.operatorMF_recognition_not_computable}
\leanverified{Computability/WordProblemRE}{GroupApproximation.WordProblemRE.rePred_wordProblemPred}
\leanverified{Computability/WordProblemRE}{GroupApproximation.WordProblemRE.operatorMF_negative_side_not_re}
\leanverified{Computability/AdianRabinVariantTransform}{GroupApproximation.AdianRabinVariantTransform.operatorMF_recognition_undecidable_of_wordProblem}
\leanverified{Computability/AdianRabinVariantTransform}{GroupApproximation.AdianRabinVariantTransform.operatorMF_negative_side_not_re_of_wordProblem}

\begin{proof}
For the undecidability of $W$, use Boone's finitely presented group with
undecidable word problem \cite{Boone}.  A decision procedure for the uniform
predicate $W$ would decide the word problem of this one fixed presentation,
which is impossible.

For the reduction we use the effective form of the Adian--Rabin
construction, which supplies a computable map $\Delta$ from pairs (a
presentation code $e$, a word $u$ in its generators) to presentation
codes such that

\begin{itemize}
\item if $u=1$ in $G_e$, then $\Delta(e,u)$ presents an MF group: the
trivial group in the classical form, and a free group in the variant
used here, a free group being residually finite and so MF by
Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(2); and
\item if $u\ne1$ in $G_e$, then $G_e$ embeds in the group presented by
$\Delta(e,u)$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
This is Rabin's theorem \cite{Rabin58} and, in the form with the
explicit computable output presentation, Adian's \cite{Adian55}; an
English translation of Adian's papers with the construction written out
is \cite{NybergBrodda}.  Apply $\Delta$ to the free product of the
input presentation with a fixed presentation of $E$ and to the input
word.  The resulting presentation code is computable from the pair and
presents an MF group exactly when the input word is trivial.  In the
trivial case the output group is free and hence MF.  In the nontrivial
case the free product, and therefore $E$, embeds in the output group.
The output group is then non-MF by Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(1).  Composing a
decision procedure for MF recognition with this map would therefore
decide $W$, so no such procedure exists.  For the last assertion, the
positive instances of $W$ are exactly those carrying a finite
certificate --- a product of conjugates of relators freely equal to
the given word --- and whether a given certificate works is
decidable, so $W$ is recursively enumerable.  Were its complement
recursively enumerable as well, $W$ would be decidable, which it is
not.  Finally, the preimage of the set of non-MF presentation codes
under this computable map is exactly the set of pairs outside $W$, and
the preimage of a recursively enumerable set under a computable map is
recursively enumerable.
\end{proof}

\leanverified{Computability/AdianRabinVariantTransform}{GroupApproximation.AdianRabinVariantTransform.operatorMF_recognition_undecidable_of_wordProblem}

\paragraph{Multiplicity.}
There are infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic finitely presented
non-MF groups, and continuum many pairwise nonisomorphic finitely
generated non-MF groups.
The groups $E\times\mathbb Z^{k}$ for $k\ge0$ are
finitely presented, pairwise nonisomorphic, and non-MF, each containing
$E$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:permanence}(1)).  They are pairwise nonisomorphic
because their abelianizations have distinct torsion-free ranks.
\leanverified{Sofic/LiteralProductMultiplicity}{GroupApproximation.LiteralProductMultiplicity.manuscriptInfiniteMultiplicity_literal}
For finitely generated examples, choose continuum many pairwise
nonisomorphic finitely generated groups $N$, for instance Neumann's
two-generator groups \cite{Neumann37}.  Each product $E\times N$ is
non-MF\@.  A fixed countable group $X$ can be isomorphic to
$E\times N$ for at most countably many isomorphism types of $N$,
because each such $N$ embeds in $X$ as a finitely generated subgroup
and $X$ has only countably many finitely generated subgroups.  Hence
the products yield $2^{\aleph_0}$ pairwise nonisomorphic finitely
generated non-MF groups.

\section{Limitations of the operator-norm method}\label{sec:limits}

The argument requires operator-norm control.  If multiplication is
controlled only in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm, the induced adjoint
operators on the Hilbert--Schmidt spaces need not be close in operator norm.
The estimate $\opnorm{\Ad(U)-\Ad(V)}\le2\opnorm{U-V}$ has no converse:
unitaries can be arbitrarily close in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm while
their adjoint operators remain at operator-norm distance $2$.
Operator-norm control ensures that the classes
$[\Ad U_n(g)]_\omega$ are multiplicative; Hilbert--Schmidt control
alone does not provide this.
\leanverified{Sofic/HilbertSchmidtAdjointGap}{GroupApproximation.HilbertSchmidtAdjointGap.exists_hsClose_adjointFar}

Adding an identity block leaves operator-norm multiplicative defects
and separations unchanged but decreases normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
distances.  By taking the identity block sufficiently large, all
pairwise normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distances can be made smaller
than any prescribed positive bound.
\leanverified{Sofic/NormTraceGap}{GroupApproximation.NormModel.exists_hs_collapse}
Thus universal $2$-norm triviality of an element does not by itself prove a
group non-MF\@.  The central-involution argument passes to an
invariant corner and equips it with the trace normalized by its own
rank $r_n$: the $2$-norm separation on the corner is measured against
$r_n$ rather than $d_n$, so $r_n/d_n$ may tend to zero without
affecting the contradiction.
The spectral projection of Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan} is
normalized on its own range in the same way, and the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-collapse} normalizes by the rank $k_n$.

Stable finiteness and faithful traces on the coordinate algebras do
not suffice for Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}.  For a finite
factor $M$ in its standard representation on $L^2(M,\tau)$, the right
action is the commutant of the left action.  Hence the von Neumann
algebra generated by the two actions is
$(M\cup M')''=Z(M)'=B(L^2(M,\tau))$; in infinite dimension this is not
a finite $C^*$-algebra.  Constant coordinates
$A_n=\Cred(E)$ give an explicit counterexample: the left regular
representation embeds $E$ exactly and injectively into the norm
quotient
\[
 \textstyle\prod_nA_n\big/\bigoplus_nA_n ,
\]
the element $c$ centralizes the Kazhdan base there, and conjugation by
$t$ produces $tct^{-1}$, whose commutator with the base is the element
$u$ with $u^2=w\ne1$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\leanverified{Sofic/FaithfullyTracedCoordinateNoGo}{GroupApproximation.FaithfullyTracedCoordinates.manuscriptFaithfullyTracedCoordinateNoGo}
Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} fails when its matrix
coordinates are replaced by stably finite unital $C^*$-algebras
carrying faithful tracial states and its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
norm by the trace $2$-norm, with all its remaining hypotheses
unchanged.

Alekseev--Thom ask whether commutants arising in sofic approximations
of Kazhdan groups can be recovered from finite-dimensional coordinate
subalgebras \cite[Open Problem~6.2]{AlekseevThom}.  Their question
concerns Hamming and tracial models, whereas the adjoint spectral
estimates here require operator-norm multiplicative control, which
permutation and Hilbert--Schmidt models do not supply.

\paragraph{Unitary lifting over general $C^*$-algebras.}
If $u$ is unitary in
$\prod_nA_n/\bigoplus_nA_n$ for unital $C^*$-algebras $A_n$, and
$(x_n)$ is any bounded lift, then
$\opnorm{x_n^{*}x_n-1}\to0$ and $\opnorm{x_nx_n^{*}-1}\to0$.
Thus $x_n$ is invertible for all large $n$, and the polar correction
$u_n=x_n(x_n^{*}x_n)^{-1/2}$ is unitary with
$\opnorm{u_n-x_n}\to0$.  This argument uses only polar correction in the
coordinate algebras and requires neither real rank zero nor semiprojectivity
in the sense of Loring \cite{Loring98}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport}
additionally uses the Hilbert-space conjugation model and finiteness of the
norm ultraproduct.

\section{A torsion-free finitely presented non-MF group}
\label{sec:torsionfree}

\begin{theorem}[a torsion-free example]\label{thm:torsionfree}
There exists a torsion-free finitely presented non-MF group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The construction in \cite[Section~2]{FFF} starts from a torsion-free finitely presented double
HNN extension and applies Hull's common-quotient theorem to obtain a
torsion-free finitely presented acylindrically hyperbolic property-\textup{(T)}
quotient $G_0$.  In the notation of that construction, $G_0$ contains a
Kazhdan subgroup $\Gamma$, a stable letter $t_1$, and an embedded finitely
presented infinite simple torsion-free group $S'\le\Gamma$ such that
\[
 t_1\Gamma t_1^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma,
 \qquad t_1^{-1}S't_1\subseteq C_{G_0}(\Gamma).
\]
For $s\in S'$, put $z=t_1^{-1}st_1$.  Then
$z\in C_{G_0}(\Gamma)$ and $t_1\in P_\Gamma\subseteq G^+(\Gamma)$, so for
every $\ell\in\Gamma$,
\[
 [s,\ell]=[t_1zt_1^{-1},\ell]\in\mathfrak D(G_0,\Gamma).
\]
Thus $[S',S']\le\mathfrak D(G_0,\Gamma)$.  The infinite nonabelian simple
group $S'$ is perfect, and hence
$S'\le\mathfrak D(G_0,\Gamma)$.

Put $N=\langle\!\langle S'\rangle\!\rangle_{G_0}$.  This subgroup is infinite and
normal, hence s-normal.  By Osin's lemma, $N$ acts non-elementarily
\cite[Lemma~7.1]{Osin}.  The finite radical of the torsion-free group $G_0$ is
trivial, so $N$ is suitable.  Apply Theorem~7.1 of \cite{Hull} with the
elements $t_i$ equal to a finite generating set of $G_0$ and with an
injectivity radius containing a fixed nonidentity element of $S'$, and write
$q\colon G_0\twoheadrightarrow G$ for the resulting quotient.  The group $G$
is torsion-free by Theorem~7.1(e), finitely presented because the
construction adds finitely many relators, and property-\textup{(T)} because it
is a quotient of $G_0$.  Condition~(c) gives that the image of $N$ equals
$G$.  The image of $S'$ is nontrivial, and its kernel is a proper normal
subgroup of the simple group $S'$, so $q|_{S'}$ is injective.  It follows
that $q(S')$ normally generates $G$.

Put $L=q(\Gamma)$.  The relations above descend to
\[
 q(t_1)Lq(t_1)^{-1}\subseteq L,
 \qquad q(t_1)^{-1}q(S')q(t_1)\subseteq C_G(L).
\]
The same commutator calculation gives
$q(S')\le\mathfrak D(G,L)$.  Since $q(S')$ normally generates $G$ and
$\mathfrak D(G,L)$ is normal, $\mathfrak D(G,L)=G$.
Applying Corollary~\ref{cor:intrinsic-nk} with $K=G$ shows that every
homomorphism $G\to\U(\mathcal Q)$ is trivial.  The embedded copy of $S'$
makes $G$ nontrivial, so no such homomorphism is injective and $G$ is not
MF.

The normal-Kazhdan criterion is essential here because a torsion-free group
has no nontrivial finite subgroup.
\leanstep{LI.07}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a small-cancellation realization]
\label{rem:ff-realization}
The affine base of Sections~\ref{sec:group}--\ref{sec:witness} can be
replaced by the small-cancellation construction of \cite[\S2]{FFF},
which Fournier-Facio pointed out to us.  There a
universal finitely presented torsion-free group --- one containing a
copy of every finitely presented torsion-free group --- is embedded by
small cancellation in a finitely presented torsion-free
property-\textup{(T)} group $P$.  Hence that $P$
contains every finitely presented torsion-free group, in particular a
direct product $P_1\times P_2$ with $P_i\cong P$; loc.\ cit.\ states
this as $P_1\times P_2\times S\le P$ with $S$ finitely presented simple,
and the third factor is not needed here.  Choosing an
isomorphism $\alpha\colon P\to P_1\le P$ and any
$a\in P_2\setminus\{1\}$ gives the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford}.  The group $E(P,\alpha,a)$ has
torsion even though $P$ is torsion-free, since the central involution $w$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-clifford} is nontrivial.
\end{remark}

\section{Open questions}

The construction leaves the following questions open:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does MF imply hyperlinearity?  An MF embedding controls
operator norm but does not require the induced tracial model to
separate nontrivial group elements, so it does not directly produce a
hyperlinear model.  This
question concerns the CDE convention fixed above; for stronger
trace-controlled MF conventions the implication is built into the definition.
\item Does the obstruction survive when the matrix blocks are replaced
by other coordinate algebras?  The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kazhdan-transport} uses finite dimensionality of
$M_{d_n}(\C)$ for the conjugation action on
$L^2(M_{d_n}(\C),\tr_{d_n})$ and for finiteness of the norm
ultraproduct.  These conclusions need not hold for arbitrary faithfully
traced coordinate algebras.  For
which separable $C^*$-algebras $A_n$ does every homomorphism
$H\to\U\bigl(\prod_nA_n/\bigoplus_nA_n\bigr)$ still map $w$ to the
identity?
\item For which groups do the criteria of
Theorems~\ref{thm:criterion},~\ref{thm:normal-kazhdan}
and~\ref{thm:projection-collapse} compute the residual exactly?
\end{enumerate}

\section{The maximal group \texorpdfstring{$C^*$}{C*}-algebra}\label{app:maxcstar}

For a countable group $H$, the maximal group $C^*$-algebra $\Cmax(H)$ is
the completion of the group ring $\C[H]$ in the norm
\[
 \lVert x\rVert_{\max}=\sup_\pi\lVert\pi(x)\rVert,
\]
the supremum taken over unitary representations of $H$; the supremum is
finite because $\lVert\pi(\sum_h\lambda_hh)\rVert\le\sum_h|\lambda_h|$.
Write $u_h$ for the canonical unitary of $h\in H$.

\begin{proposition}[the universal property]
\label{prop:maximal-cstar}
The canonical map $h\mapsto u_h$ is injective, and for every unital
$C^*$-algebra $B$ and every homomorphism $\rho\colon H\to\U(B)$,
there is a unique unital $*$-homomorphism $\Cmax(H)\to B$ with
$u_h\mapsto\rho(h)$ for every $h\in H$.
\leanverified{Analysis/MaximalCStarAllUniverses}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptMaximalGroupCStarUniversalProperty}
\end{proposition}

Injectivity follows from the left regular representation, and
uniqueness follows from density of $\C[H]$.

\begin{proposition}[a strictly dominated projection gives a proper isometry]\label{prop:proper-isometry}
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra containing a projection $p$ and a
unitary $u$ with $p<upu^{*}$.  Then $A$
contains an isometry that is not a unitary; consequently $A$ is not
stably finite and has no faithful tracial state.
\leanverified{Analysis/ProperIsometryStrictOrder}{GroupApproximation.manuscriptProperIsometryStrictOrder}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $q=upu^{*}$, so $q$ is a projection with $pq=qp=p$, and put
$r=pu^{*}$.  Then
\[
 r^{*}r=up\,pu^{*}=upu^{*}=q,\qquad rr^{*}=pu^{*}up=p .
\]
Put $s=r+(1-q)$.  Since $p(1-q)=0$ and $(1-q)p=0$,
\[
 s^{*}s=r^{*}r+(1-q)=q+(1-q)=1,
\]
and since $pu^{*}(1-q)=pu^{*}-pu^{*}upu^{*}=pu^{*}-pu^{*}=0$ and
likewise $(1-q)up=0$,
\[
 ss^{*}=rr^{*}+(1-q)=p+(1-q)=1-(q-p).
\]
Thus $s^{*}s=1$ but $ss^{*}=1-(q-p)\ne1$, so $s$ is a nonunitary
isometry.  A unital $C^*$-algebra containing a
nonunitary isometry is not finite, hence not stably finite.  If $\tau$
were a faithful tracial state then
$\tau(1-ss^{*})=\tau(1)-\tau(s^{*}s)=0$ while $1-ss^{*}=q-p$ is a
nonzero positive element, contradicting faithfulness.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the maximal algebra under proper one-sided conjugation]
\label{rem:maxinfinite}
Let $G$ be a countable group containing a property-\textup{(T)}
subgroup $\Gamma$ and an element $t$ with
$t\Gamma t^{-1}\subsetneq\Gamma$.  Then $\Cmax(G)$ is not stably
finite, has no faithful tracial state, and is in particular neither
residually finite-dimensional nor MF\@.
\leanverified{Analysis/QuasiRegularWitness}{GroupApproximation.QuasiRegularWitness.baseVector_apply_base}
\leanverified{Analysis/StrictCompressionFromPrinted}{GroupApproximation.MaximalCStarPrintedHypotheses.manuscriptMaximalCStarRemarkFromPrintedHypotheses}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $P\in\Cmax(G)$ be the spectral projection at $1$ of
the Hermitian Kazhdan average $\tfrac1{2|S|}\sum_{s\in S}(u_s+u_s^{*})$,
which exists by Lemma~\ref{lem:kazhdan-gap}.  Then
$P\le u_tPu_t^{*}$.  This inequality is strict.  In the quasi-regular
representation on $\ell^2(G/t\Gamma t^{-1})$, the point mass at the
base coset is fixed by $t\Gamma t^{-1}$ and moved by every element of
$\Gamma\setminus t\Gamma t^{-1}$.  Thus the two fixed-space projections
are distinct in this representation.  Now apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-isometry}.
\end{proof}

The base subgroup of $E$ and the element $t$ form such a pair, so
$\Cmax(E)$ is not stably finite.  The reduced algebra behaves
differently: its canonical trace is faithful and it is stably finite,
despite being non-MF (Theorem~\ref{thm:reduced}).
