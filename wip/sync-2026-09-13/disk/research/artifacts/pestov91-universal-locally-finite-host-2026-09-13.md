# One simple Kazhdan LEF group containing every countable locally finite group

2026-09-13. A written extension of the repository's minimal-subshift answer
to Pestov 9.1. This proof is not a Lean formalization. Its simplicity,
property-(T), and LEF input is explicitly the existing subshift theorem;
the new work is the compatible embedding construction below.

## Theorem

There is a fixed infinite simple Kazhdan LEF group `S`, generated by
eighteen involutions, into which every countable locally finite group
embeds. Thus `S` is also sofic, hyperlinear, and operator MF. It contains
`Q/Z` and, for every prime `p` and positive integer `n`,
`GL_n(overline(F_p))`. It has no nontrivial finite quotient and is not
finitely presented.

The quantifiers are `exists S, forall L, exists an embedding L -> S`.
The group `S` is chosen once, independently of `L`. Merely putting every
finite group in `S` would not prove this statement.

## 1. A binary subshift with all compatible cyclic factors

Let

\[
 Y=\varprojlim_{j\ge2}\mathbb Z/j!\mathbb Z,
 \qquad T_Y(y)=y+1.
\]

This is the universal odometer. Su Gao and Ruiwen Li,
[*Subshifts of Finite Symbolic Rank*, Theorem 8.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.09352v2#S8),
prove that every infinite odometer is the maximal equicontinuous factor
of a minimal subshift of symbolic rank two. Their proof constructs the
subshift over the binary alphabet. Apply it to `Y`, obtaining an infinite
minimal binary subshift `(X,T)` and an onto equivariant map `pi:X -> Y`.
Only the existence of this factor map is used; neither maximality nor
an almost one-to-one condition is needed.

Set

\[
 R=LC(X,\mathbb F_2)\rtimes_T\mathbb Z,
 \qquad ufu^{-1}=f\circ T^{-1},\qquad S=EL_3(R).
\]

The repository theorem `simple-kazhdan-lef-group-from-minimal-subshift`
applies to every infinite minimal subshift. Its proof also shows
`Z(EL_3(R)) <= F_2^times I_3 = {I_3}`. Therefore `S` itself is infinite,
simple, Kazhdan, and LEF, without taking a quotient that might affect
the embeddings below.

## 2. Matrix algebras from the cyclic factors

For each positive integer `N`, let `pi_N:X -> Z/NZ` be the compatible
cyclic factor obtained from `pi`, and put

\[
 C_a^{(N)}=\pi_N^{-1}(a),\qquad
 E_{ab}^{(N)}=1_{C_a^{(N)}}u^{a-b}
 \quad(0\le a,b<N).
\]

The cells are nonempty, clopen, partition `X`, and satisfy
`T(C_a^(N))=C_(a+1)^(N)`, with subscripts taken modulo `N` in this last
formula. The exponent `a-b` is the actual integer difference of the
chosen representatives, not a residue class. Crossed-product
multiplication gives

\[
 E_{ab}^{(N)}E_{cd}^{(N)}
 =1_{C_a^{(N)}}1_{T^{a-b}C_c^{(N)}}u^{a-b+c-d}
 =\delta_{bc}E_{ad}^{(N)},\qquad
 \sum_a E_{aa}^{(N)}=1.                                    \tag{1}
\]

Hence there is a unital injection `i_N:M_N(F_2) -> R`. To see injectivity
directly, multiply a vanishing linear combination on the left by
`E_aa` and on the right by `E_bb`; the surviving matrix unit is nonzero.

If `M=rN`, compatibility of the factors gives the exact refinement

\[
 E_{ab}^{(N)}
 =\sum_{t=0}^{r-1} E_{a+tN,b+tN}^{(M)}.                    \tag{2}
\]

Ordering the `M` coordinates by `(t,a)`, (2) is the unital diagonal
embedding `A -> diag(A,...,A)` with `r` copies. Thus `R` contains the
unital locally matricial ring

\[
 B=\varinjlim_j M_{j!}(\mathbb F_2),
 \qquad A\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}_{j+1}(A).           \tag{3}
\]

This argument works for any zero-dimensional system with the indicated
compatible cyclic factors. Minimality and symbolic coding enter only
when passing to the finitely generated simple Kazhdan host.

## 3. The finite linear groups lie in EL_3(R)

The unital embedding `M_N(F_2) -> R` induces an injection
`GL_3(M_N(F_2)) -> GL_3(R)`. Under flattening, the source is
`GL_(3N)(F_2)=SL_(3N)(F_2)`. Its image lies in `EL_3(R)`.

Here is the elementary verification of the last point. Label flattened
coordinates by `(i,a)`, where `i in {1,2,3}` and `0<=a<N`. A transvection
from `(j,b)` to `(i,a)` with `i != j` is the image of
`e_ij(E_ab^(N))`. For two different coordinates `(i,a)` and `(i,b)`
in the same colour, choose `j != i` and any `c`. Then

\[
 [I+e_{(i,a),(j,c)},I+e_{(j,c),(i,b)}]
 =I+e_{(i,a),(i,b)}.                                     \tag{4}
\]

Both factors already lie in `EL_3(R)`. Transvections generate the finite
special linear group, proving the assertion. In characteristic two all
invertible matrices have determinant one, so there is no determinant
obstruction for the permutation representations used next.

Equations (2)--(4) put the compatible union

\[
 D=\varinjlim_j SL_{3j!}(\mathbb F_2),
 \qquad A\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}_{j+1}(A)            \tag{5}
\]

inside `S`. A fixed rearrangement of the colour and tower coordinates
identifies the indicated connecting maps with these block diagonals.

## 4. Compatible regular representations give universality

Let `L` be any countable locally finite group. Enumerating its elements
gives an increasing union of finite subgroups `L_1 <= L_2 <= ...`.
Choose strictly increasing integers `j_i >= 2` so that
`|L_i|` divides `d_i=3j_i!`. We construct compatible embeddings

\[
 \rho_i:L_i\hookrightarrow SL_{d_i}(\mathbb F_2)
\]

whose permutation actions on `d_i` points are disjoint unions of regular
`L_i`-actions. At the first stage take `d_1/|L_1|` regular copies.

Suppose `rho_i` has been constructed. The connecting map in (5) repeats
it `d_(i+1)/d_i` times, producing `d_(i+1)/|L_i|` regular `L_i`-orbits.
Take `d_(i+1)/|L_(i+1)|` copies of the regular `L_(i+1)`-action.
Restricted to `L_i`, each copy splits into `[L_(i+1):L_i]` regular
orbits, so its total number of regular `L_i`-orbits is also
`d_(i+1)/|L_i|`. There is therefore an equivariant bijection of the two
finite `L_i`-sets. Conjugate the chosen `L_(i+1)`-representation by the
corresponding permutation matrix. Its restriction now equals the
prescribed amplification of `rho_i` exactly.

This completes the induction. Faithfulness at each stage gives

\[
 L\hookrightarrow D\hookrightarrow S.                    \tag{6}
\]

In fact the same construction gives a unital algebra embedding
`F_2[L] -> M_3(B) -> M_3(R)`: each regular representation is faithful on
the whole finite group algebra, since applying its matrices to the basis
vector at the identity detects every coefficient. Compatibility on group
elements extends linearly.

The use of regular actions is essential to this proof. An arbitrary
representation of `L_i` need not extend to `L_(i+1)`, even after the
particular prescribed amplification. No extension property for arbitrary
preassigned finite subgroups of `S` is claimed.

## 5. Eighteen involutions suffice

Let `p=1_{[1]}` be the one-letter cylinder function of the binary shift.
The unital ring `R` is generated by `u,u^{-1},p`: products of conjugates
of `p` and `1-p` give cylinder functions, and their sums give every
locally constant function.

Take the eighteen elements

\[
 \{e_{ij}(u),e_{ij}(u^{-1}),e_{ij}(p):1\le i\ne j\le3\}.
\]

They are involutions in characteristic two. For each `i != j`, choose
the remaining index `k`; then
`[e_ik(u),e_kj(u^{-1})]=e_ij(1)`. Once the constant roots are available,
addition `e_ij(a+b)=e_ij(a)e_ij(b)` and
`[e_ik(a),e_kj(b)]=e_ij(ab)` generate every root coefficient in the ring.
Thus these eighteen elements generate `S`.

## 6. Consequences and boundaries

- `Q/Z` is countable and locally finite, so it embeds in this fixed `S`.
  In particular `S` contains every Prüfer group, simultaneously.
- For any prime `p` and finite `n`, `GL_n(overline(F_p))` is an increasing
  union of finite linear groups and embeds in `S`. The characteristic of
  the source need not be two: (6) uses permutation representations.
- Every finite group embeds, while every homomorphism from `S` to a finite
  group is trivial. This follows from infinite simplicity.
- `S` is not finitely presented. A finitely presented LEF group is
  residually finite, whereas an infinite simple group is not.

The construction proves universality for countable locally finite groups,
not for all countable LEF or all countable amenable groups. It preserves
LEF and does not advance the separate finitely presented simple sofic
problem. The odometer group alone has not been declared Kazhdan: the
finite-alphabet subshift extension is what makes its larger crossed-product
ring finitely generated.

## 7. Sources, novelty boundary, and validation

The new host theorem is derived from the repository's 9.1 theorem plus
the explicit matrix-unit and compatible-regular-representation arguments
above. The odometer realization is Gao--Li, Theorem 8.6; its statement
and binary construction were read in the primary source on 2026-09-13.
The property-(T) input in the base theorem remains Ershov--Jaikin-Zapirain,
not a new property-(T) proof here.

A bounded search of the current main research graph and web searches for
Kazhdan groups containing Hall's group or all countable locally finite
groups found no matching result. This is not a proof of global priority.
The regular-representation embedding argument is elementary and carries
no separate priority claim. The assertion beyond the existing 9.1 theorem
is that the same fixed simple Kazhdan LEF host can be chosen universally
for all countable locally finite inputs.
