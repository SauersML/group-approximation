### 45.1 Import and Section 0

- **Import.** Remark 3.6 of arXiv:2509.05054v2, which I did not read. `w7-radu-r4-linear` quotes it from the source:
  "the only non-trivial elements in Γ_R of length at most eight that vanish under Φ are δ⁴ and δ⁻⁴".
- **Route step 1, re-derived.**
  - `Gamma_R^ab = (Z/2)^3` is finite, so `Gamma'` has finite index and contains `Gamma_0`.
  - `Phi(Gamma')` is finitely generated and linear, so it is residually finite. For `g` in `Gamma_0` with
    `Phi(g) != 1`, a finite quotient of `Phi(Gamma')` gives a finite-index subgroup of `Gamma'`, and hence of
    `Gamma_R`, that misses `g`, which is impossible.
  - So `Gamma_0 ⊆ ker Phi`, and `Gamma_0 ∩ B_8 ⊆ {1, delta^(+-4)}`.
- **Parity.** Every relator has even length, so letter parity is a homomorphism to `Z/2`. It kills `Gamma'`, so the
  elements of `Gamma_0` have even length.

### 45.2 Lemmas 1.1 and 1.2, Theorem A and Lemma B

- **Lemma 1.1, re-derived.**
  - Square moves preserve the horizontal and vertical letter counts, and free cancellation lowers both.
  - So a word for `alpha omega` has at least `|alpha|` horizontal and `|omega|` vertical letters.
  - A word of length `|omega|` for `omega` in `V` therefore uses only `x, y, z`. It represents `omega` in the free
    product of three `C_2`, so it is the reduced word.
- **Lemma 1.2, re-derived.** Let `g != h` lie in `B_4` with `g^(-1) h` in `Gamma_0`.
  - The import forces `g^(-1) h = delta^(+-4)`, of length 8, so `|g| = |h| = 4`.
  - Geodesic words for `g^(-1)` and `h` concatenate to the reduced word `xzxzxzxz` or its inverse.
  - So `{g, h} = {delta^2, delta^(-2)}`.
- **Theorem A, re-derived.**
  - *Step 1.* `A' = u^(-1) A w^(-1)` and `B' = w B u` satisfy `B'A' = w (BA) w^(-1) = I`, and
    `A'B' = u^(-1) (AB) u`.
  - *Step 2.* `D = I - A'B'` is idempotent and supported in `B_4`.
  - *Step 3.* A finite quotient of `Gamma_R/Gamma_0` separates the images of the finite supports. There
    `M_n(K[Q])` is finite-dimensional, hence directly finite. So the coset sums of `D` vanish, over every field.
  - *Step 4.* `D = E delta^2 + E' delta^(-2)` with `E + E' = 0`.
  - *Step 5.* `D^2 = E^2 (delta^4 - 2 + delta^(-4))`, since the entries of `E` lie in `K` and commute with `delta`. Its support `{1, delta^(+-4)}` is disjoint
    from `{delta^(+-2)}`. Comparing coefficients at `delta^2` gives `E = 0`.
  - *Over a commutative ring.* The Mechanism sentence holds: `M_n(R[Q])` embeds in `M_(n|Q|)(R)`, which is directly
    finite by determinants.
- **Lemma B, re-derived.**
  - A point of `B_k` that is alone in its coset has coefficient 0, so `supp D ⊆ C_k`.
  - The coefficient of `D^2` at `g` in `C_k` sums over `g_1 g_2 = g` with `g_1, g_2` in `C_k`.
  - Product-freeness makes every such sum 0, so `D = D^2 = 0`.

### 45.3 Lemma 4.1, Lemma 4.2 and Theorem C

**Lemma 4.1, re-derived.**
- **Setup.** Write `h = g delta^4` and `g = alpha omega`, and let `j` be the length of the cancelling suffix. Then
  `|h| - |g| = 8 - 2j`.
- **Cases.** From `|g|, |h| <= 5`, `|g| + |h| >= 8` and equal parity, the pairs are `(3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (5,5)`,
  with `j = 3, 4, 5, 4`.
  - `j = 3`: `g = xzx` and `h = zxzxz`.
  - `j = 4`, `|g| = 4`: `g = zxzx = delta^(-2)` and `h = delta^2`.
  - `j = 5`: `g = xzxzx` and `h = zxz`.
  - `j = 4`, `|g| = 5`: `omega` ends in `zxzx`, and the letter before it is `y` or absent, so `g = t delta^(-2)` with
    `t` in `{a, b, c, y}`.
- **The set `F`.** `delta x = xzx`, `delta^(-3) x = zxzxz`, `delta^(-2) x = zxz` and `delta^2 x = xzxzx`.

**Pairs at length ten, re-derived.**
- Under H10, `g^(-1) h = y delta^(+-4) y` has length 10, so `|g| = |h| = 5`.
- Its reduced word `y xzxzxzxz y` splits as `(yxzxz)(xzxzy)`, and its inverse as `(yzxzx)(zxzxy)`.
- Both give the pair `{delta^2 y, delta^(-2) y}`.
- Parity excludes odd lengths, the import excludes nontrivial lengths up to seven, and Lemma 4.1 covers length eight.
  So `C_5 ⊆ S`.

**Lemma 4.2, re-derived.**
- **Step 1, twisting.**
  - The rules `za = bz`, `zb = az` and `zc = cx` follow from `delta a = b delta`, `delta c = c delta^(-1)` and
    `xc = cz`.
  - These first-level actions (`x` fixes `a, b, c`; `y` swaps `b, c`; `z` swaps `a, b`) agree with
    `radu-bmw-lattice-splits-as-amalgams-of-virtually-free-groups`.
  - `delta^(+-2)` commutes with `a` and `b`, and `delta^(+-2) c = c delta^(-+2)`.
  - `delta^i x c = c delta^(-i-1) x`, since `delta^(-i) z = delta^(-i-1) x`. On exponents this is `1 <-> -2` and
    `-3 <-> 2`.
  - The four `y` rules are checked.
- **Step 2, `Omega Omega ∩ Omega = ∅`.**
  - *`F F`.* The exponents are `E_F - E_F = {0, +-1, +-3, +-4, +-5}`, which never contain `+-2`, and all these
    products are rotations.
  - *`F R` and `R F`.* The exponents are `{0, -1, 3, -5, +-4}`, disjoint from `E_F = {1, -3, -2, 2}`, and all these
    products are reflections.
  - *`R R`.* `R R ⊆ {1, delta^(+-4)}`.
  - *With `y`.* The eight products involving `yR ∪ Ry` either have `y` strictly inside, or reduce to
    `y(rho phi)`, `(phi rho) y`, `y (rho rho') y` or `rho rho'`. None lies in `Omega`.
- **Step 3.** `Gamma_R = A V` has unique normal forms, and step 1 moves `alpha_2` to the left.

**Theorem C.** Theorem C follows from Lemma 4.1, H10, Lemma 4.2 and Lemma B.

**Section 4.3.**
- `(zx)^(-1) (xz)^3 = delta^4`, `delta^(-1) delta^(-3) = delta^(-4)` and `(delta^(-2))^(-1) delta^2 = delta^4`.
- So `delta`, `delta^2` and `delta^(+-3)` lie in `C_6`, and `delta delta = delta^2`.
