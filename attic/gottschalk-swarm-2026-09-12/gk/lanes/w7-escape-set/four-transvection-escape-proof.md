1. **Pair** (Theorem 1.1). Put `A = s_0 t_1`, `A' = s_1 t_0`, `B = s_1 t_00` and `B' = s_00 t_1`. Then
   `x = B'A' + A'A` and `y = AB + A'A`, with `yx = 1` and `xy = 1 - s_01 t_01`.
2. **Invariant.** If a conjugate of `<S4>` lay in `H^ω_≤`, the conjugate span, and with it the pair, would lie in the
   stably finite subalgebra of `depth-monotone-leavitt-subalgebras-are-stably-finite`. The `≥` side is the same.
3. **Minimality** (Remark 1.2). The weights `a = b` and `b = 2a` put each three-element subset on one side.
4. **Local finiteness** (Proposition 1.3, Corollary 1.4). Elements of the `≤` span preserve the finite-dimensional
   `F_r` for large `r`, and the cylinder functions are the union of the `F_r`. The involution `*` exchanges the two
   sides. An invertible locally finite operator has a locally finite inverse.
