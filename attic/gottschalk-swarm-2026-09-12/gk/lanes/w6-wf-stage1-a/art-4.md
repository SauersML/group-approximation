
## 8. Proposition E: finite-dimensional representations kill the lift

**Proposition E.** Let `H <= G` contain `z`, `d1` and `h`. Let `K` be a field of characteristic 3, `V != 0` a
finite-dimensional `K`-space, and `psi0 : H -> GL(V)` a homomorphism such that
- `psi0(z) = -1`, and
- `g := psi0(h)` carries the `+1`-eigenspace `V+` of `psi0(d1)` onto its `-1`-eigenspace `V-`.

Then `Y_h` has no left inverse in `P1 eps_- F_3[H] P1`.

*Proof.*
- **The representation.** Let `psi0*(x) = psi0(x^(-1))^T` be the contragredient on `V*`, and put
  `rho = psi0 ⊗ psi0 ⊗ psi0*` on `W = V ⊗ V ⊗ V*`. Then `rho(z) = (-1)^3 = -1`. So the linear extension of `rho`
  is a unital ring map `eps_- F_3[H] -> End_K(W)`, because `eps_- -> 2(1 - (-1)) = 1`.
- **The odd part.** `psi0(d1)` is an involution and `2` is invertible, so `V = V+ ⊕ V-`. Put
  `V+* = {f : f(V-) = 0}` and `V-* = {f : f(V+) = 0}`; `psi0*(d1)` acts on `V±*` by `±1`.
  - So `W` is the sum of the eight blocks `W_(abc) = V_a ⊗ V_b ⊗ V_c*`, `a, b, c in {+,-}`, and `rho(d1)` acts
    on `W_(abc)` by the sign `abc`.
  - Hence `rho(P1) = 2(1 - rho(d1))` is the projection onto `O = W_(++-) ⊕ W_(+-+) ⊕ W_(-++) ⊕ W_(---)`.
- **Compressing.** Suppose `v Y_h = P1` with `v in P1 eps_- F_3[H] P1`. Applying `rho` and restricting to `O`
  gives `rho_O(v) rho_O(h) = 1_O`. So `rho_O(h)` is injective on the finite-dimensional space `O`, hence onto.
- **A missing block.** Two block maps vanish:
  - `pi+ g i+ = 0`, because `g V+ = V-`;
  - the `V-* -> V-*` block of `psi0*(h)`, which is `f -> f ∘ g^(-1) ∘ pi-`, because `g^(-1) V- = V+`.
- **The image misses `W_(++-)`.** The component of `rho(h)` from `W_(abc)` into `W_(++-)` is the tensor product
  of the blocks `V_a -> V+`, `V_b -> V+` and `V_c* -> V-*`.
  - For `(++-)`, `(+-+)` and `(-++)`, the first or the second factor is `pi+ g i+ = 0`.
  - For `(---)`, the third factor is the `V-* -> V-*` block, which is `0`.
  So `rho_O(h) O ⊆ W_(+-+) ⊕ W_(-++) ⊕ W_(---)`. As `g` maps `V+` onto `V-`, `dim V+ = dim V- >= 1`, so
  `W_(++-) != 0`, and `rho_O(h)` is not onto. Contradiction. ∎

**Why a tensor cube.** `psi0` alone is also a unital ring map on `eps_- F_3[H]`. It sends `P1` to the
projection onto `V-`, and `Y_h` to `pi- g i-`, which is often invertible. So `psi0` sees nothing; the
obstruction appears only in `V ⊗ V ⊗ V*`.

**Corollary E.1 (chart algebras).** Let `A ⊆ R` be a unital subalgebra with `s0 in A`, and `phi : A -> M_n(K)`
a unital ring map, `n >= 1`, `K` a field of characteristic 3. Then no left inverse of `Y_h` is supported
in `GL_2(A)`.

*Proof.* Through the `{0,1}` chart, `GL_2(A) <= G` contains `z = -I`, `d1 = diag(1,-1)`, and
`h = [[0,1],[-1,s0]]` with `h^(-1) = [[s0,-1],[1,0]]`. Take `psi0 = GL_2(phi)` on `V = K^n ⊕ K^n`, so that
`V+ = K^n ⊕ 0`, `V- = 0 ⊕ K^n` and `psi0(h) = [[0,I],[-I,phi(s0)]]`, which maps `V+` onto `V-`. ∎

Instances:
1. **Polynomial and free charts.** `A = F_3[s0]` or `A = F_3<s0,s1>`, with `s_i -> 1`. The same holds for
   any residually finite-dimensional `A`.
2. **The Jacobson algebra.** `A = T = F_3<s0,t0>`, with `s0, t0 -> 1`. This covers the region of Section 6
   (`k`, `n = diag(1, 1 + e)`, tests T1 and T2), and it subsumes Claim D.3.
3. **One more letter.** `A = F_3<s0,t0,t1>`, with `s0, t0 -> 1` and `t1 -> 0`.
   - The monomials `s0^a t_nu` have no `s1 t1` junction, so they are distinct Leavitt normal-form monomials
     and linearly independent.
   - They span `A` and multiply by `t0 s0 = 1` and `t1 s0 = 0`, so
     `A = F_3<s0,t0,t1 | t0 s0 = 1, t1 s0 = 0>` and the map is well defined.

**Corollary E.2 (other charts).** Let `C` be a complete prefix code with `|C| >= 2`, with chart
`a <-> (t_u a s_w)_(u,w in C)`. In this chart `d1` is diagonal with sign `-1` exactly on words beginning
with `1`. Since `h s0 = -s1` and `h^(-1) s1 = -s0`, the chart entries satisfy

```text
t_(0u) h s_(0w) = -t_(0u) s_(1w) = 0,        t_(1u) h^(-1) s_(1w) = -t_(1u) s_(0w) = 0.
```

So if `A` contains the chart entries of `h` and `h^(-1)` and has a unital map `phi : A -> M_n(K)`, then
`psi0 = GL_C(phi)` maps `V+` into `V-` and, under the inverse, `V-` into `V+`. Hence `V+` goes onto `V-`, and
Proposition E applies to `GL_C(A)`. This covers the `GL_m` cases of Corollary D.2.

**What escapes.**
- **No finite-dimensional representation.** For a left inverse `sum lambda_g Y_g`, let `A_v` be generated by
  the chart entries of `z, d1, h` and of all `g, g^(-1)` with `g in supp lambda`. Then `H_v <= GL_2(A_v)`, and
  Corollary E.1 forces `A_v` to have no nonzero finite-dimensional representation. The same holds in every
  chart of Corollary E.2.
- **Example.** Any `A` containing `s0, t0, s1, t1`.
  - In a finite-dimensional representation, `t1 s1 = 1` makes `s1` invertible.
  - Then `t0 s1 = 0` gives `t0 = 0`, contradicting `t0 s0 = 1`.
  - The Thompson unit `g0 = [[s0, s1 t0],[0, t1]]` generates such an algebra, since
    `g0^(-1) = [[t0, 0],[s0 t1, s1]]`.
- **Consequence.** A certificate for Section 6 needs chart entries realizing two isometries with orthogonal
  ranges, as Thompson-type elements do, alongside `h`.
- **Beyond chart maps.** Proposition E needs only some finite-dimensional `psi0` on `H_v` with `z -> -1` and
  `psi0(h) V+ = V-`; chart maps are one source of such representations.
