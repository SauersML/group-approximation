## 0. Summary

* **Section 1 (established): regular models of graphs of locally finite groups.** Every countable graph of groups
  with countable locally finite vertex groups carries a characteristic-two rank model with the free profile on
  every finite subgroup of every vertex group. It is fixed-point-free when a vertex group is infinite, and it is
  a representation into the units of a weakly finite algebra. When the unit root pairs of disjoint cylinders sit
  in one vertex group, `rk(D_A) = 3/8` and `theta = 1`.
* **Section 2 (established): the mismatched factors are inert.** Both escaping configurations sit in two-vertex
  amalgams of locally finite subgroups of `R^x`:
  * the pair `a = T_(0,1)`, `b = T_(1,00)`, whose product has infinite order;
  * the `F`-generator `b' = tau sigma` of `w4-sub-adversary`, together with its whole conjugation chain
    `b'^n k_0 b'^(-n) = k_n` on nested frames.

  So no argument that uses these as factors, through products, powers or their conjugation chains, and only
  relations of such an amalgam, can force `theta < 1`, the Sylvester gate or the weakly finite gate.
* **Section 3: what these models do not impose.** An explicit commutation `[T_(01,10), T_(000,0010)] = 1` holds
  in `R^x` and fails in the amalgam that contains the pair. Fock local letters realize such commutations and fail
  partial conjugations; graph models do the reverse. A proof has to use a relation of `R^x` that no graph of
  locally finite subgroups routes, or a global input.

## 1. Regular models of graphs of locally finite groups

**Setting.** A graph of groups `Y` consists of a countable graph with oriented edges, a countable locally finite
group `L_v` for each vertex `v`, and for each edge `e : v -> w` a subgroup `K_e <= L_v` with an injective
homomorphism `phi_e : K_e -> L_w`. Fix a maximal forest `F ⊆ Y`. The fundamental group `pi_1(Y)` is generated by
the `L_v` and letters `t_e`, subject to the relations of each `L_v`, `t_e k t_e^-1 = phi_e(k)` for `k in K_e`,
and `t_e = 1` for `e in F`.

**Main case.** Each `L_v` is a countable locally finite subgroup of `R^x`, and each edge carries a unit `u_e` with
`K_e <= L_v cap u_e^-1 L_w u_e` and `phi_e(k) = u_e k u_e^-1`, where `u_e = 1` on forest edges. Then vertex
inclusions and `t_e -> u_e` define a homomorphism `psi : pi_1(Y) -> R^x`. A forest edge amalgamates `L_v` and
`L_w` along a common subgroup; a non-forest edge is a single partial conjugation.

**Theorem 1.1.** There is a homomorphism `sigma : pi_1(Y) -> M^x`, with `M = prod_U M_(m_n)(F_2)` and normalized
rank `rk`, such that:
1. `rk(sigma(alpha)) = rank(lambda_Q(alpha)) / |Q|` for every vertex `v`, finite `Q <= L_v` and `alpha in F_2[Q]`,
   where `lambda_Q` is the left regular representation;
2. `sigma` is nontrivial when some `L_v != 1`, and `Fix_r(sigma(pi_1(Y))) = 0` when some `L_v` is infinite;
3. every matrix algebra `M_k(M)` is directly finite, so `sigma` is a representation into the units of a weakly
   finite `F_2`-algebra.

*Proof.*
* **Stages.** Enumerate the vertices and edges. At stage `n` let `Y_n` be the finite subgraph on the first `n`
  vertices with the edges among them that are among the first `n` edges, and choose finite subgroups
  `H_(n,v) <= L_v`, increasing in `n` and exhausting `L_v`. For `e : v -> w` in `Y_n` put
  `K_(n,e) = {k in K_e cap H_(n,v) : phi_e(k) in H_(n,w)}`, a subgroup mapped injectively into `H_(n,w)`. Every
  finite subset of `K_e` lies in `K_(n,e)` for large `n`.
* **Free actions along the forest.** Put `N_n = prod_(v in Y_n) |H_(n,v)|` and `|Omega_n| = N_n`. In each component
  of `F cap Y_n` choose a root `v_0` and a free action of `H_(n,v_0)` on `Omega_n`. Suppose a free action of
  `H_(n,v)` is given and `e` is a forest edge from `v` to a new vertex `w`.
  * Restricted to `K_(n,e)` it is free with `N_n / |K_(n,e)|` orbits.
  * Take any free action of `H_(n,w)` on `Omega_n`, which exists because `|H_(n,w)|` divides `N_n`. Restricted
    along `phi_e` it is free on `phi_e(K_(n,e))` with the same number of orbits.
  * Two free actions of one finite group on sets of equal size are isomorphic, so there is a bijection `beta` of
    `Omega_n` with `beta(k x) = phi_e(k) beta(x)`. Transport the `H_(n,w)`-action by `beta^-1`. Now
    `lambda_(n,w)(phi_e(k)) = lambda_(n,v)(k)` for `k in K_(n,e)`. An edge oriented towards `v` uses `phi_e^-1`.

  Induction over the forest gives free actions `lambda_(n,v)` of all `H_(n,v)`, agreeing on forest edges.
* **Letters.** For a non-forest edge `e : v -> w`, `K_(n,e)` acts freely through `lambda_(n,v)` and
  `phi_e(K_(n,e))` acts freely through `lambda_(n,w)`, with the same number of orbits. Matching orbits and applying
  `phi_e` inside each gives a permutation `pi_(n,e)` with `pi lambda_(n,v)(k) pi^-1 = lambda_(n,w)(phi_e(k))`.
* **Limit.** Put `sigma(h) = [lambda_(n,v)(h)]` for `h in L_v`, `sigma(t_e) = [pi_(n,e)]` off the forest and
  `sigma(t_e) = 1` on it, using the identity at the finitely many stages where a value is undefined. A relation of
  `L_v` holds at every stage containing its letters, and an edge relation for `k in K_e` holds exactly once
  `k in K_(n,e)`. So `sigma` is a homomorphism.
* **(1).** Once `Q <= H_(n,v)`, `Omega_n` is a free `Q`-set, so `F_2[Omega_n]` is `F_2[Q]^(N_n/|Q|)` as a left
  `F_2[Q]`-module and `rank(lambda(alpha)) = (N_n/|Q|) rank(lambda_Q(alpha))`.
* **(2).** Nontriviality follows from (1). A vector fixed by `sigma(L_v)` has rank at most `1/|H_(m,v)|` for every
  `m`, by the argument of Theorem 1.1(2) of the firewall artifact, and `|H_(m,v)| -> infinity` when `L_v` is
  infinite.
* **(3).** `M_k(M) = prod_U M_(k m_n)(F_2)` with its normalized rank. If `AB = I` there, then at almost every stage
  `rank(I - A_n B_n) = o(m_n)`, and `rank(I - B_n A_n) = rank(I - A_n B_n)` in `M_(k m_n)(F_2)`. So `I - BA` has rank
  zero, hence is zero in the ultraproduct. QED

**Corollary 1.2 (independent defects).** In the main case, let `A_1, ..., A_k` be pairwise disjoint proper
cylinders whose unit root pairs `iota_(A_i)(x_12(1))`, `iota_(A_i)(x_23(1))` lie in one vertex group `L_v`. Then
`rk(sigma(N^A_12)) = 1/2`, `rk(sigma(D_A)) = 3/8` and `rk(sigma(D_(A_1) ... D_(A_k))) = (3/8)^k`. So `theta = 1`,
and `sigma(D_A) != 0` in a weakly finite algebra.

*Proof.* The configurations span `D_8 x ... x D_8 <= L_v`, and Theorem 1.1(1) gives the free profile on it. The
rank counts `4/8` and `3/8` in `F_2[D_8]` are part 3 of `locally-finite-hnn-data-allow-independent-cylinder-defects`,
and ranks of tensor products multiply. QED

**Corollary 1.3 (firewall).** Suppose an argument proves strict submultiplicativity, or that every rank function on
`F_2[R^x]` kills `D`, or that every weakly finite representation of `R^x` kills `D`. Suppose its group-theoretic
inputs are relations that hold in `pi_1(Y)` for one graph `Y` of the main case containing the configurations it
uses, and that its other inputs are rank calculus in rank ultraproducts, nontriviality and fixed-point-freeness.
Then the argument fails: `sigma` of Theorem 1.1 satisfies every input, and `sigma(D_A) != 0` with `theta = 1`.
